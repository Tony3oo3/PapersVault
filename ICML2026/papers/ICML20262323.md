## Problem

Standard reverse-KL-regularized RLVR reliably improves pass@1 on reasoning tasks but fails to yield comparable gains in pass@k, indicating that it sharpens a narrow subset of pre-existing reasoning modes rather than discovering new ones. The reverse-KL anchor stabilizes training but inherently suppresses low-density trajectories, while removing it induces reward hacking and replacing it with forward-KL allocates probability mass to reward-irrelevant regions. The paper asks whether one can retain the stabilizing benefits of reverse-KL while explicitly enabling controlled empirical support expansion.

## Input / Output

The method takes a math problem as text input along with a frozen reference LLM (e.g., Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Base) and produces a reasoning trajectory ending in a final numeric answer, formatted between <start_working_out>/<end_working_out> and <SOLUTION>/<SOLUTION> tags. The guide function q(x,y) is computed on-the-fly from the policy's own token-level entropy or surprisal during generation, requiring no extra forward passes. The pseudo-KL regularizer is approximated using the second-order unbiased estimator, making the objective compatible with standard PPO/GRPO-style policy gradient updates.

## Method

SAGE replaces the standard reverse-KL regularizer D_KL(π_θ‖π_ref) with a pseudo-KL divergence D_KL(π_θ‖q·π_ref), where q(x,y)>0 is a trajectory-dependent guide function held fixed during each policy update. The resulting stationary solution π_θ(y|x) ∝ q(x,y)·π_ref(y|x)·exp(r(x,y)/β) multiplicatively reshapes the anchor, amplifying low-density yet reward-compatible reasoning regions while preserving the stabilizing effect of reverse-KL. Three instantiations are provided: random exploration (stochastic Gaussian perturbation), token-level exploration (surprisal-weighted modulation), and branch-level exploration (entropy-thresholded rule q=1+γ(H_t−τ)⁺ that selectively amplifies only high-entropy branching decisions). The branch-level variant is shown to satisfy the sufficient condition in Theorem 4.3 for empirical support expansion, and the decomposition in Eq. (8) reveals that SAGE modifies the KL leash along q rather than globally smoothing the policy. The framework is plug-and-play, integrating into GRPO, DAPO, and BNPO without altering their optimization structure.

## Results

On the GRPO family with Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Base, GRPO+Branch (SAGE) achieves the strongest dataset-averaged pass@1 of 0.284 and pass@256 of 0.729 across AIME, AMC23, and MATH-500, outperforming vanilla GRPO (0.266 / 0.714) and all other SAGE variants. On AIME specifically, GRPO+Branch reaches 0.063 pass@1 (vs. 0.053 for GRPO and 0.033 for the base model), while on AMC23 it attains 0.465 pass@1 (vs. 0.430 for GRPO). The method also generalizes to long-reasoning models: on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B, GRPO+Branch improves average pass@1 to 0.452 while preserving pass@64 at 0.774, compared to GRPO's 0.449 / 0.771.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), GRPO+Branch wins on average pass@1 (0.284 vs. 0.266) and pass@256 (0.729 vs. 0.714), with the largest gap on AIME pass@1 (+0.010) and AMC23 pass@1 (+0.035). Against GRPO+ForwardKL (Deng et al., 2025b), SAGE achieves higher average pass@1 (0.284 vs. 0.265) and marginally higher pass@256 (0.729 vs. 0.727), while ForwardKL retains an edge on AIME pass@256 (0.433 vs. 0.427) and AMC23 pass@256 (0.975 vs. 0.965), confirming that forward-KL broadens coverage indiscriminately at the cost of off-target mass. Against BNPO without KL (Xiao et al., 2025), BNPO+Branch improves average pass@1 from 0.243 to 0.271 and pass@256 from 0.699 to 0.715, reversing the mode-collapse trade-off. Against DAPO without KL (Yu et al., 2025), DAPO+Branch lifts average pass@1 from 0.264 to 0.267 and pass@256 from 0.696 to 0.712, restoring coverage that naive dynamic sampling erodes. Overall, SAGE consistently wins or ties on both accuracy and coverage metrics across all four baseline families, with the most pronounced gains in the low-sample (pass@1) regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAGE builds directly on GRPO, DAPO, and BNPO as the underlying RLVR algorithms, modifying their reverse-KL regularization term rather than their reward or sampling structure. It departs from the dominant line of work that alleviates mode collapse by weakening or removing the KL anchor (no-KL, forward-KL, f-divergence variants) by instead *reshaping the anchor distribution itself* through a multiplicative guide function q(x,y). The theoretical grounding draws on Wu et al.'s empirical support framework and the closed-form exponential-tilting analysis from Deng et al., while the guide function instantiations leverage intrinsic signals (entropy, surprisal) identified in prior reasoning-analysis literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SAGE provides a clean, modular mechanism for the exploration-exploitation trade-off in RLVR that is orthogonal to existing techniques (advantage shaping, dynamic sampling, entropy bonuses), making it a practical plug-and-play addition to diverse RLVR pipelines. A key limitation is that SAGE redistributes probability mass only within the reference model's support, so it cannot introduce genuinely new reasoning modes absent from the base model—distillation from stronger teachers remains complementary. Open questions include whether richer, learned guide functions (e.g., from teacher models or domain knowledge) can further expand the effective support, and whether the entropy-based branch signal generalizes beyond mathematical reasoning to other structured reasoning domains.
