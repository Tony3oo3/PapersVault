## Problem

Cross-domain offline RL must leverage a large source dataset to improve policy learning in a data-scarce target domain, but dynamics mismatch makes many source transitions kinematically infeasible, causing negative transfer. Existing non-parametric geometric methods (balanced OT, k-NN) enforce a fixed transfer budget, yielding only relative relevance rankings rather than absolute availability scores, which makes performance highly sensitive to hand-tuned thresholds when the true cross-domain overlap is unknown. The paper asks whether a principled mechanism can compute sample-wise availability scores that adapt to unknown dynamics mismatch without prescribing a fixed retention ratio.

## Input / Output

The method takes two offline datasets of transition tuples (state, action, next-state concatenated into a joint vector): a typically large source dataset (e.g., ~1M D4RL transitions) and a small target dataset (e.g., 5 trajectories ≈ 5000 transitions). It produces two outputs: (1) a per-source-transition relevance weight vector computed once as a preprocessing step via block-wise Sinkhorn (batch size 10,000), and (2) a target-domain policy trained with IQL where source transitions are reweighted by the normalized relevance scores and the actor is additionally constrained by a CVAE target behavior model. The weights are absolute (not relative rankings), so no downstream hard threshold is needed.

## Method

ROOT casts source selection as a soft subset selection problem—learning a reweighted source distribution that minimizes Wasserstein distance to the target—and then introduces two relaxations to make it tractable under partial domain overlap. First, a KL ambiguity set around the empirical target marginal handles target-side uncertainty from scarce samples, replacing exact matching with a penalized divergence. Second, a source-side generalized KL penalty relaxes mass conservation, enabling a transport-or-discard mechanism where high-cost source mass is down-weighted or discarded rather than forcibly matched to the target support. The resulting unbalanced OT objective is solved with entropic regularization via a block-wise Sinkhorn algorithm for scalability. A key theoretical result (Proposition 3.2) shows the induced relevance weights decay exponentially with each source sample's minimum transport cost to the target, providing a formal outlier-suppression guarantee. Downstream, the weights modulate the IQL critic loss for source transitions, while a CVAE-constrained actor ensures output actions remain within the target behavior manifold.

## Results

On the OTDF-style kinematic shift benchmark (4 environments × 3 source qualities × 3 target qualities, 36 scenarios total), ROOT achieves a total normalized score of 2073.5, surpassing the strongest baseline DmC (1902.2) by 9.0% and the balanced-OT baseline OTDF (1547.6) by 34.0%. ROOT attains the highest score in 19 out of 36 scenarios. On the ODRL gravity/friction shift benchmark (32 settings), ROOT obtains a total score of 1477.97, outperforming MOBODY (1427.26) and REAG (986.13), with the best score in 16/32 settings and top-2 in 26/32 settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ROOT outperforms all 8 baselines in aggregate on the kinematic shift benchmark (2073.5 vs. DmC 1902.2, OTDF 1547.6, IGDF 1271.0, DARA 1219.8, SRPO 1195.7, IQL 1193.0, BOSA 513.5), winning 19/36 individual scenarios. The most pronounced advantage is over OTDF (balanced OT), which ROOT surpasses by 34.0% in total score; OTDF degrades sharply on high-quality target settings (e.g., Hopper-m→Expert: 35.0 vs. ROOT's 99.4), confirming that forced mass conservation harms performance when source-target overlap is partial. Against DmC (the strongest baseline), ROOT leads by 9.0% overall but the gap narrows on medium-quality target tasks where both methods perform similarly. On the ODRL gravity/friction benchmark, ROOT (1477.97) edges out MOBODY (1427.26) and substantially outperforms REAG (986.13), DARA (890.62), BOSA (875.88), and SRPO (647.91), achieving top-2 in 26/32 settings. Notably, ROOT's advantage is largest at moderate shift levels (e.g., shift=2.0, where it wins 7/8 settings), while at extreme shifts (0.1, 5.0) it remains competitive but occasionally trails MOBODY or REAG on individual tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

ROOT extends the non-parametric geometric approach to cross-domain offline RL data filtering, building directly on the OT-based filtering paradigm of OTDF (Lyu et al., 2025) and the KNN-based approach of DmC (Le Pham Van et al., 2025), while departing from their shared reliance on balanced transport that enforces a fixed transfer quota. The mathematical core draws on the Kantorovich formulation of unbalanced optimal transport (Chizat et al., 2018), repurposing its relaxed marginal constraints as a principled mechanism for source-sample discarding rather than forced matching. For policy extraction, ROOT uses IQL (Kostrikov et al., 2022) as a stable backbone, augmenting it with learned relevance weights and a CVAE-based target-behavior constraint.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ROOT addresses a concrete and previously unaddressed limitation of OT-based cross-domain filtering: the implicit assumption that a fixed fraction of source data must be transferred, which is ill-suited when true cross-domain overlap is unknown. The exponential-decay guarantee (Proposition 3.2) provides a formal justification for why incompatible transitions are suppressed without hand-tuned thresholds, and the empirical results across 36+32 settings demonstrate consistent gains over both parametric and non-parametric baselines. Open questions remain around the dependence on the choice of geometric cost (and representation), scalability to very high-dimensional or large-scale settings, and whether the alignment phase can be made jointly differentiable with policy learning rather than a fixed preprocessing step.
