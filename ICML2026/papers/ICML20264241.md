## Problem

The MAXECC problem seeks to color vertices of an edge-colored hypergraph to maximize the number of satisfied edges (edges whose vertices all match the edge's color). The prior best approximation for rank-r hypergraphs, from Crane et al. (ICML 2025), achieves a ratio of (2/e)^r · (r+1)^(−1), which decays exponentially in the hypergraph rank r, rendering the guarantee essentially useless for moderate r. This paper closes that gap by providing polynomial-in-r approximation guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method takes an edge-colored hypergraph H=(V,E,ℓ) with k colors, maximum hyperedge size (rank) r, and non-negative edge weights, along with an optimal LP-relaxation solution {x_v^c} where x_v^c ∈ [0,1] represents the fractional assignment of color c to vertex v. It produces a deterministic vertex coloring λ:V→[k] (one color per vertex) that satisfies a weighted fraction of edges, with the approximation ratio depending on r and k. The LP relaxation enforces the constraint that for each edge e of color c_e, x_v^{c_e} ≥ z_e for all v∈e, linking the fractional edge-satisfaction variable to vertex assignments.

## Method

The paper contributes three algorithmic improvements over Crane et al.'s randomized rounding framework. First, a refined analysis of the same two-phase algorithm (random thresholding followed by random-permutation conflict resolution) tightens the bound from (2/e)^r/(r+1) to 1/(r+1) by exploiting the convexity of 1/(1+x) more carefully. Second, a novel dependent-rounding algorithm applies a DEPROUND subroutine (which rounds two fractional color values while preserving expectations and order) in a tournament fashion over ⌈log k⌉ rounds, achieving a 1/⌈k/2⌉ approximation that is competitive when k is small relative to r. Third, a scaling modification replaces the thresholding condition α_c < x_v^c with α_c < x_v^c · e^{−wx_v^c} for w∈(2/3,1), and a key technical lemma (Theorem 5.1) proves that the resulting satisfaction probability is minimized when tentative color assignments are balanced across vertices, yielding the improved ratio (1−e^{−r})/r — approximately 0.432 for graphs (r=2), surpassing the prior best of 0.38.

## Results

The paper provides three approximation guarantees for MAXECC on rank-r hypergraphs: (1) a refined analysis of the existing randomized rounding algorithm yielding 1/(r+1), (2) a dependent-rounding algorithm yielding 1/⌈k/2⌉, and (3) a scaling-based algorithm yielding (1−e^{−r})/r. For the important special case of graphs (r=2), the scaling algorithm achieves an approximation ratio of ≈0.432, improving upon the previously best-known 0.38 for graphs and 0.180 for the general hypergraph algorithm of Crane et al. (2025). The key structural improvement is replacing the exponentially decaying bound (2/e)^r·1/(r+1) with a polynomially decaying 1/r-type bound, yielding multiplicative gains that grow with r (e.g., ~25× improvement at r=10).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the LP-rounding algorithm of Crane et al. (ICML 2025), which achieves ratio (2/e)^r·1/(r+1) for rank-r hypergraphs and a specialized 0.38 ratio for graphs. The paper's scaling algorithm dominates this baseline at every rank: for r=2 it improves 0.180→0.432 (and 0.38→0.432 for the graph-specialized case), for r=3 it improves 0.100→0.317, and for r=10 it improves 0.004→0.100. The gap widens dramatically with r because Crane et al.'s bound contains the factor (2/e)^r ≈ 0.736^r which decays exponentially, while the paper's (1−e^{−r})/r decays only as 1/r. The refined analysis (1/(r+1)) also strictly dominates Crane et al. at every r, and the dependent-rounding algorithm (1/⌈k/2⌉) provides an additional competitive guarantee when the number of colors k is small relative to r.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Crane et al. (ICML 2025), who provided the first approximation algorithms for MAXECC on hypergraphs via LP-rounding with ratio (2/e)^r · (r+1)^{-1}, and on the earlier graph-specific line of work by Angel et al. (2016) through Ageev & Kononov (2020) and Crane et al.'s specialized 0.38 graph algorithm. The key departure is threefold: a refined analysis showing the prior exponential-decay bound was substantially loose (the same algorithm actually achieves 1/(r+1)), a novel dependent-rounding approach (Gandhi et al., 2006) introduced to the ECC literature for the first time, and a scaling modification with probabilistic analysis that goes beyond standard Jensen's inequality to achieve (1−e^{−r})/r. The paper positions itself as a theoretical improvement over a very recent ICML 2025 result, extending the graph-specific improvements to the general hypergraph setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The exponential-to-polynomial improvement in the approximation ratio (from (2/e)^r·(r+1)^{−1} to 1/(r+1) and further to (1−e^{−r})/r) is significant for the theory of edge-colored clustering, particularly for high-rank hypergraphs where the prior guarantee was essentially useless. For the practically important special case of graphs (r=2), the 0.432 ratio surpasses the previously best-known 0.38. However, the paper is purely theoretical with no empirical evaluation, and several open questions remain: whether the exponential scaling function is optimal, whether dependent rounding and scaling can be combined, and what hardness bounds exist for MAXECC as a function of r and k. The lack of empirical validation and the deferral of key analytical proofs to the full paper limit immediate practical impact.
