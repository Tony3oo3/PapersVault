## Problem

Applying Wasserstein-based distribution balancing (as in Counterfactual Regression) directly to high-dimensional LLM embeddings (~4096 dimensions) is statistically vacuous: the empirical Wasserstein distance converges as slowly as n^{-1/D}, so standard PEHE generalization guarantees become non-informative at realistic sample sizes. Existing remedies like Sliced Wasserstein average over random projections and can dilute sparse confounding signals, while full-space balancing risks over-balancing and discarding prognostic information.

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw unstructured text posts (clinical notes, social media discussions) that are first parsed via LLM prompting into tuples (T, Y, X̃) where X̃ is pre-treatment text explicitly excluding outcome-revealing content to prevent leakage. Posts are encoded with a frozen Llama-3-8B model using the 4th-to-last hidden state (L-4) to produce 4096-dimensional embeddings, which are then passed through a trainable adapter. The output is a per-unit CATE estimate τ̂(X) = μ̂₁(X) − μ̂₀(X) from two treatment-specific prediction heads, along with an averaged ATE estimate evaluated against ground-truth effects from RCTs or simulations.

## Method

SPIKED-CFR introduces Wasserstein Projection Pursuit (WPP), a min-max objective under a 'spiked structure' assumption that treatment selection bias concentrates in a low-dimensional subspace (k ≪ D). An adversary U constrained to the Stiefel manifold V_k (row-orthonormal k×D matrices) searches for the k-dimensional projection that maximizes the empirical Wasserstein distance between treated and control representations, while the representation learner simultaneously minimizes this worst-case projected discrepancy plus a factual prediction loss. Training alternates Riemannian gradient ascent on U (with polar or QR retraction) and standard Adam descent on the adapter and outcome heads, computing the Wasserstein term via a differentiable Sinkhorn solver. Under the spiked structure, the projected discrepancy is estimable at rate O(n^{-1/k}) rather than O(n^{-1/D}), yielding a tighter PEHE bound that depends on the intrinsic spike dimension k rather than the ambient embedding dimension D.

## Results

On the four real-world clinical benchmarks, SPIKED-CFR achieves the lowest RMSE(ATE) across all drug-pair comparisons: 0.65 (Sema vs. Tirz, GT ATE 10.11), 0.56 (Sema vs. Lira, GT −14.70), 1.68 (Eren vs. Topi, GT 28.30), and 1.38 (Onab vs. Topi, GT 41.00). On the semi-synthetic benchmarks with counterfactual supervision, SPIKED-CFR attains the best ε_PEHE among text-based methods: 2.47±0.20 on IHDP (GT ATE 4.19) and 2.18±0.03 on ACIC (GT ATE 3.47), while also achieving the lowest RMSE(ATE) on Hillstrom (0.33) and Retail Hero (0.72). The structured-tabular oracle CFR-Wass (Structured) remains stronger (e.g., ε_PEHE 1.03 on IHDP), confirming that the residual gap reflects text-extraction noise rather than a methodological limitation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPIKED-CFR outperforms all text-based baselines on every clinical benchmark in RMSE(ATE), with the largest margins over BERT-PSM (e.g., 0.65 vs. 31.15 on Sema vs. Tirz) and CausalBERT (1.68 vs. 17.60 on Eren vs. Topi). Among LLM-based CFR variants, SPIKED-CFR consistently beats Llama-CFR-Wass (0.65 vs. 1.52 on Sema vs. Tirz; 1.68 vs. 4.16 on Eren vs. Topi) and Llama-CFR-MMD, confirming that full-space Wasserstein balancing is statistically inefficient at D≈4096. Against Llama-TARNet and Llama-Dragonnet (which lack explicit balancing), SPIKED-CFR wins on RMSE across all four clinical pairs (e.g., 0.56 vs. 4.91 and 3.47 on Sema vs. Lira). NATURAL, the strongest end-to-end LLM baseline, is competitive on Sema vs. Lira (RMSE 2.24) but is outperformed by SPIKED-CFR on the other three clinical tasks (e.g., 1.68 vs. 3.99 on Eren vs. Topi) and is not applicable to IHDP/ACIC due to its binary-outcome assumption. Llama-CForest is the closest non-CFR competitor on ATE accuracy but still trails SPIKED-CFR in RMSE on all four clinical benchmarks. The structured-tabular oracle CFR-Wass (Structured) remains the strongest overall (e.g., ε_PEHE 1.03 on IHDP vs. 2.47 for SPIKED-CFR), but it requires clean tabular covariates unavailable in the text-only setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPIKED-CFR extends the Counterfactual Regression (CFR) framework of Shalit et al. (2017) by replacing its full-space Wasserstein balancing term with a Wasserstein Projection Pursuit (WPP) objective, drawing on the spiked transport model of Niles-Weed & Rigollet (2022) and the subspace-robust Wasserstein distances of Paty & Cuturi (2019) and Lin et al. (2020). It deliberately departs from the 'LLM-as-Estimator' paradigm exemplified by NATURAL (Dhawan et al., 2024), which relies on calibrated LLM probability outputs, in favor of an 'LLM-as-Extractor' approach that treats frozen Llama-3-8B embeddings as covariates and performs causal adjustment via a principled min-max objective on the Stiefel manifold. The key conceptual departure from prior CFR variants is identifying a low-dimensional 'spike' subspace where treated-control imbalance concentrates, rather than attempting (statistically vacuous) full-dimensional distribution matching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete statistical barrier—empirical Wasserstein estimation at rate n^{-1/D} is effectively zero for D≈4096 LLM embeddings—by showing that under a spiked structure the projected discrepancy recovers a meaningful n^{-1/k} rate, yielding a tighter PEHE bound. The constructed IHDP-post and ACIC-post benchmarks fill a notable gap by providing counterfactual supervision for text-based causal evaluation, which prior text-causal benchmarks lacked. Key limitations include the unverifiability of the spiked structure from observational data, dependence on upstream LLM extraction quality (missed confounders, residual leakage), and restriction to binary treatments; extending projected balancing to multi-valued, continuous, or time-varying treatments remains open.
