## Problem

Speech Emotion Recognition systems face a dual gap: consensus-based supervision collapses annotator disagreement into single labels (the 'Consensus Paradox'), discarding minority emotions that reflect genuine affective complexity, while existing models either achieve strong recognition without trustworthy explanations (SSL encoders) or produce plausible narratives without verifiable acoustic grounding (MLLMs). No prior framework treats SER as a sequential, evidence-driven reasoning process that actively probes targeted acoustic and semantic evidence to resolve ambiguity.

## Input / Output

The system consumes raw speech audio and its aligned transcript text, operating over a closed 8-way emotion taxonomy (Anger, Sadness, Happiness, Surprise, Fear, Disgust, Contempt, Neutral). Output is a structured multi-turn agent trajectory that terminates in primary emotion label(s) (preserving ties), minor emotion label(s) derived from minority annotator votes, and an explicit audit trail of tool-derived observations (verbatim transcript spans and discretized acoustic bins). Acoustic measurements are returned as interpretable descriptors (e.g., 'High Pitch', 'Volatile Energy') via Dual-Reference Bucketing rather than raw scalars, and the agent additionally emits a tie-prediction flag for high-uncertainty cases.

## Method

ADEPT transforms Qwen-3-Omni into a multi-turn agent executing a three-phase pipeline: (1) High-Recall Hypothesis Initialization producing a ranked candidate set that preserves ties, (2) Adaptive Evidence Accumulation where the agent dynamically invokes four tool families—Structural Prior (co-occurrence-based pair scheduling), Semantic Probing (7-factor CPM appraisal schema with literal-first span extraction), Acoustic Probing (alignment-anchored, coarse-to-fine signal measurement with Dual-Reference Bucketing), and Refinement (bidirectional cross-modal replay for conflict resolution)—under a soft budget constraint, and (3) Evidence-Closed Adjudication that prohibits further tool calls and synthesizes only Phase-2 observations into final predictions. Training uses GRPO with a composite reward (format validity, phase integrity, outcome accuracy, evidence grounding, strategic probing) modulated by an Evidence Trust Gate that scales evidence-related rewards by the degree to which correct trajectories exhibit stronger evidence-seeking than incorrect ones within each rollout group, preventing reward hacking on non-informative tool use. The label construction strategy retains tied primary labels and minority-vote minor emotions as informative supervision, enabling the model to learn co-occurring emotion structure rather than winner-takes-all classification.

## Results

On MSP-Podcast Test1, ADEPT+GRPO achieves a Primary Macro-F1 of 0.4224, outperforming the strongest SSL baseline (WavLM with CE Loss, 0.3640) by +5.8 points and the best generative SLLM (BLSP-Emo, 0.2915) by +13.1 points. On set-level ambiguity decoding, ADEPT+GRPO reaches 0.6192 Set Recall and 0.4751 Jaccard, surpassing all generative baselines (BLSP-Emo: 0.5320 Set R, 0.4280 Jaccard). In zero-shot transfer, ADEPT+GRPO attains 54.80% Set Recall on IEMOCAP, exceeding the supervised SOTA (BLSP-Emo, 52.30%), and 0.6832 Primary Macro-F1 on CREMA-D, outperforming all supervised SSL baselines except WavLM Large (0.7117).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ADEPT+GRPO outperforms all four SSL classifiers on Primary Macro-F1, with the largest margin over wav2vec2 (+18.4 pts) and a +5.8 pt gain over the strongest SSL baseline, WavLM with CE Loss (0.4224 vs. 0.3640). Against generative SLLM/MLLM baselines, ADEPT+GRPO dominates across every metric: it beats BLSP-Emo by +13.1 pts P-MacroF1, +11.3 pts Soft Recall, and +8.7 pts Set Recall, and surpasses Qwen-2-Audio and SALMONN by even wider margins (e.g., +28.4 pts P-MacroF1 over SALMONN). On IEMOCAP (zero-shot), ADEPT+GRPO slightly trails Emotion-LLaMA in single-label Accuracy (54.83 vs. 55.47) but substantially outperforms it in Set Recall (54.80 vs. 38.25), and exceeds the supervised BLSP-Emo on Set Recall (54.80 vs. 52.30). On CREMA-D (zero-shot), ADEPT+GRPO (0.6832 P-MacroF1) loses to the fully fine-tuned WavLM Large (0.7117) but beats XLS-R-1B (0.6764), HuBERT Large (0.6746), and W2V2 Large (0.6687) without any target-domain training.

## Relation to Prior Work

ADEPT sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) SSL speech encoders (WavLM, HuBERT) that achieve strong acoustic discrimination but remain opaque; (2) generative Speech-LLMs (SALMONN, BLSP-Emo, Emotion-LLaMA) that can articulate emotional narratives but lack verifiable acoustic grounding; and (3) agentic reasoning frameworks (Agent-MER, Affective-CoT) and RL-aligned audio models (EMO-RL, AffectGPT-R1) that introduce structured workflows but do not formulate emotion understanding as interactive evidence acquisition. The paper departs from prior agentic SER work by making evidence retrieval a hard structural constraint (Explicit Information Retrieval) rather than a soft planning step, and by grounding the semantic probing tool in Scherer's Component Process Model via the 7-factor appraisal schema of Tak et al. (2025). The optimization leverages GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) with a novel Evidence Trust Gate that modulates tool-use rewards based on whether evidence-seeking behavior is systematically more informative for correct trajectories.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central conceptual contribution—reframing SER from consensus-based single-label classification to ambiguity-driven multi-label reasoning where minority annotations are treated as informative supervision rather than noise—addresses a long-standing data-waste problem in the field (≈18% tie rate in MSP-Podcast). The zero-shot generalization results are notable: ADEPT outperforms supervised BLSP-Emo on IEMOCAP Set Recall (54.80 vs. 52.30) and approaches fully fine-tuned WavLM on CREMA-D (0.6832 vs. 0.7117) without any target-domain training, suggesting the evidence-probing policy captures transferable perceptual structure. Limitations include the 8-way closed-set taxonomy (the expanded 16-emotion secondary labels are unused), reliance on a single base model (Qwen-3-Omni), and the substantial inference cost of multi-turn tool calls (up to ~7 calls for low-consensus samples). Open questions include whether the evidence trail genuinely improves downstream decision-making in high-stakes clinical or customer-service deployments, and whether the agentic protocol scales to open-vocabulary or continuous affect dimensions.
