## Problem

Existing unsupervised infrared-visible image fusion methods, particularly translation-based frameworks, are susceptible to degenerate numerical shortcuts where encoders embed source-specific high-frequency details as imperceptible noise in intermediate representations to satisfy reconstruction objectives. Without explicit manifold regularization, the learned latent space lacks topological integrity, rendering fusion fragile under environmental shifts and attribute variations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264259/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes paired single-channel infrared and visible images (H×W×1) as input. During training, source images are cropped into non-overlapping 128×128 patches via sliding window, but the model supports arbitrary spatial dimensions at inference. The output is a single-channel fused image combining thermal saliency from the infrared modality with textural details from the visible modality, trained on 1,083 MSRS pairs and evaluated zero-shot on MSRS, TNO, Kaist, and M3FD.

## Method

LaRA-Fusion decomposes inputs into a shared modality-agnostic content anchor (C) and distinct attribute vectors (A) via a hybrid CNN-Transformer multi-scale encoder, then constrains the latent space through a dual-loop mechanism: an inner loop enforces geometric reversibility via cycle-consistency (I_V → Ĩ_I → Ĩ_V) using StyleGAN-style Modulated Synthesis Blocks with weight modulation/demodulation, while an outer loop deploys PatchGAN discriminators (LSGAN) to adversarially align intermediate translations with target data distributions. A Manifold Rectification Block performs residual feature aggregation with learnable frequency separation, and a Hierarchical Attribute Arbitration module injects a stochastic interpolation factor α as a logit-domain bias to dynamically balance cross-modal attribute fusion. A hybrid manifold exploration strategy (40% natural anchoring, 40% uniform sampling, 20% Beta-distribution boundary bias) simulates diverse imaging conditions during training, compelling the content encoder to decouple immutable geometric structure from transient attribute variations.

## Results

LaRA-Fusion achieves the best score on all six metrics (EN, SD, AG, MI, VIF, Q_abf) across all four evaluation datasets (Kaist, M3FD, MSRS, TNO). On the cross-dataset Kaist benchmark, it reaches MI=3.53, VIF=0.97, and Q_abf=0.70, surpassing the nearest competitor LUTFuse (MI=3.38, VIF=0.83, Q_abf=0.63). On M3FD, the VIF advantage is particularly pronounced at 0.86 versus 0.65 for LUTFuse. On the in-domain MSRS test set, LaRA-Fusion attains VIF=1.03 and Q_abf=0.66, maintaining consistent leads over all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LaRA-Fusion outperforms all nine baselines on every metric across all four datasets, with no losses or ties. The closest competitor is LUTFuse (ICCV'25), which also leads on MI and EN in most settings; LaRA-Fusion extends its MI lead from 0.15 on Kaist (3.53 vs 3.38) to 0.21 on M3FD (3.28 vs 3.07), while the VIF gap widens dramatically on cross-dataset benchmarks (0.86 vs 0.65 on M3FD; 0.82 vs 0.63 on TNO). MMDRFuse (ACM MM'24) and T2EA (TCSVT'25) are the next-strongest baselines on Q_abf, but both trail LaRA-Fusion by 0.07–0.13 on Kaist. On the in-domain MSRS test set, margins narrow (e.g., VIF 1.03 vs 0.93 for LUTFuse), yet LaRA-Fusion still leads on all six metrics. The largest absolute gains appear on VIF and Q_abf for cross-dataset evaluation, suggesting the dual-loop manifold constraints confer superior generalization beyond the training distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

LaRA-Fusion extends the translation-based IVIF paradigm (itself rooted in CycleGAN-style cycle-consistency and content-attribute disentanglement from Huang et al., 2018) by introducing an explicit dual-loop manifold constraint: an inner loop enforcing geometric reversibility and an outer loop applying adversarial distribution alignment to intermediate translation states. Architecturally, it repurposes StyleGAN's weight modulation/demodulation (Karras et al., 2020) within a Modulated Synthesis Block and adds a Manifold Rectification Block for residual feature aggregation, departing from prior IVIF methods (DenseFuse, RFN-Nest, FusionGAN, RFNet) that rely on pixel-wise reconstruction or single-loop cycle-consistency without explicit topological regularization. The key conceptual departure is the argument that unregularized translation objectives drive encoders toward degenerate numerical shortcuts—embedding source-specific high-frequency noise rather than domain-invariant semantics—and that stochastic manifold traversal (random α-interpolation) is necessary to prevent this collapse.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine vulnerability in unsupervised fusion: the absence of ground truth means models can satisfy reconstruction objectives through pathological optimization paths that compromise semantic robustness. The extensive interpretability analysis (Trustworthiness/Continuity metrics, LMI, PSD analysis, t-SNE topology) provides unusually thorough evidence that the learned latent space is physically grounded rather than noise-driven, which is valuable for safety-critical applications like autonomous driving. However, the computational overhead (4.08M params, 53.5G FLOPs) is moderate compared to lightweight alternatives like LUTFuse or MMDRFuse, and whether the dual-loop manifold regularization principle generalizes beyond IVIF to other multi-modal fusion or translation tasks remains unexplored. The stochastic manifold traversal strategy, while effective, adds training complexity and the optimal scheduling of the three exploration modes (Natural Anchoring, Uniform, Boundary Bias) is empirically tuned rather than theoretically justified.
