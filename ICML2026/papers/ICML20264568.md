## Problem

Foundation flow-matching (FM) models, despite being powerful generative models, perform substantially worse than domain-specific FM priors and even untrained priors (e.g., Deep Image Prior) when used as priors for inverse problems, because their generality makes them too weak to impose informative structural constraints on the solution. Prior attempts to strengthen foundation FM priors—such as D-Flow's mixed initialization and negative log-likelihood Gaussian regularization—yield negligible performance gains, leaving a significant and unaddressed gap between foundation and domain-specific priors for IP solving.

## Input / Output

The method takes a degraded measurement y (e.g., a 128×128 downsampled image for 4× super-resolution, a 70%-masked image for inpainting, or a blurred image for deblurring) along with the known forward operator A, and optionally K few-shot reference images close to the target for scientific IPs. For latent FM models (Stable Diffusion V3), y is first passed through the pretrained encoder; when dim(y)≠dim(x), y is upsampled or replaced by A†y. The output is a reconstructed image at the target resolution (512×512 for restoration tasks; 128×128, 320×320, or 64×64 for scientific IPs), produced by running the frozen FM generator G_θ from the learned warm-start time t* to t=1.

## Method

FMPlug is a plug-in framework that optimizes over a noise variable z and a learnable time parameter t∈[0,1] in the formulation min_{z,t} ℓ(y, A∘G_θ(α_t·y + β_t·z, t)), subject to a sharp shell constraint ‖z‖₂∈[(1−ε)√d, (1+ε)√d] with ε=0.025. The time-dependent warm-start exploits the closeness of x and y (or few-shot similarity) to skip the early, noise-dominated portion of the FM ODE, starting generation at an adaptive intermediate time rather than t=0; this is grounded in the observation that the approximation error α_t·ε can be made small by choosing t appropriately. The sharp Gaussianity regularization replaces D-Flow's weak negative log-likelihood penalty with a hard shell constraint motivated by the concentration-of-measure theorem for Gaussian vectors, enforced via a closed-form Euclidean projection at each gradient step. For the few-shot scientific IP setting, y is replaced by a learnable convex combination ∑w_k x_k of K reference images, with weights parameterized as softmax(v) to fix scale ambiguity and encourage sparsity over relevant instances.

## Results

On simple-distortion IPs (4× super-resolution, 70% inpainting, Gaussian deblurring, motion deblurring) across DIV2K, RealSR, and AFHQ at 512×512, FMPlug achieves the best PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS across all 12 task-dataset combinations. For example, on Gaussian deblurring (DIV2K), FMPlug reaches 26.26 PSNR / 0.73 SSIM / 0.41 LPIPS, surpassing D-Flow (23.64/0.64/0.52) by +2.6 dB and DIP (25.23/0.70/0.43) by +1.0 dB. On scientific IPs from InverseBench, few-shot FMPlug (5–10 guidance images) achieves 22.91 PSNR on black-hole imaging and 34.65 PSNR on linear inverse scattering, approaching the domain-specific Red-Diff baseline (23.77 and 36.55 PSNR respectively) while using only a foundation FM prior.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FMPlug consistently outperforms the untrained DIP baseline on PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS across all four simple-distortion tasks and three datasets (e.g., +1.0 dB PSNR on Gaussian deblurring DIV2K), though DIP retains an edge on the no-reference CLIPIQA metric. Against the plug-in competitor D-Flow, FMPlug gains substantial margins—+2.6 dB PSNR on Gaussian deblurring (DIV2K) and +2.4 dB on motion deblurring (AFHQ)—attributed to the time-dependent warm-start and sharp shell constraint replacing D-Flow's fixed initialization and weak χ² regularization. The interleaving methods FlowDPS and FlowChef (both with and without text prompts) underperform even DIP by large margins (e.g., 20.23 vs. 25.23 PSNR on Gaussian deblurring DIV2K), confirming the difficulty of simultaneously satisfying measurement and manifold feasibility in interleaved schemes. On scientific IPs, few-shot FMPlug beats D-Flow by >14 dB PSNR on LIS (34.65 vs. 19.67) and >5 dB on BKH (22.91 vs. 17.19), and matches or exceeds DIP on LIS and BKH. However, FMPlug (foundation prior) still trails the domain-specific Red-Diff baseline by 1.9 dB on LIS, 5.1 dB on MRI, and 0.9 dB on BKH, with the MRI gap attributed to spatial variability in the evaluation set that violates the few-shot similarity assumption.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on D-Flow (Ben-Hamu et al., 2024), the plug-in formulation for solving inverse problems with pretrained flow-matching priors, but identifies two fundamental deficiencies in its initialization and regularization strategies. The key departure is a theoretical argument grounded in the concentration-of-measure property of Gaussian vectors: D-Flow's warm-start places the optimization variable outside the ultra-thin shell where the FM model was effectively trained, and its chi-squared negative log-likelihood regularization changes too slowly to enforce Gaussianity. The paper replaces these with a learnable time-dependent warm-start (plugging the measurement into an intermediate flow time) and a hard shell constraint, and further extends the framework to a few-shot instance-guided setting for scientific inverse problems where domain-specific FM models are infeasible to train.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper documents a counterintuitive and well-evidenced performance gap: foundation FM models (Stable Diffusion V3) perform worse than untrained priors (DIP) as generative priors for inverse problems, challenging the assumption that more powerful generative models automatically yield better constrained solutions. FMPlug narrows this gap substantially and, in the few-shot scientific IP setting, approaches domain-specific prior performance with only 5–10 reference images, offering a practical path for scientific domains where training domain-specific models is prohibitively expensive. Open questions include whether the few-shot assumption (structurally similar guidance instances) holds broadly across scientific domains, whether the approach extends to non-image modalities (video, 3D), and whether the speed disadvantage relative to interleaving methods can be further reduced.
