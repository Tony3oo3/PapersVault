## Problem

Standard diffusion model training relies on the KL-based ELBO, which creates a persistent inverse correlation between likelihood (NLL) and perceptual quality (FID): models optimized for one metric degrade on the other. This trade-off has been attributed to architectural or weighting choices, but the paper argues it is fundamentally caused by the choice of divergence objective, motivating a more flexible family of training objectives that can interpolate between likelihood-favorable and quality-favorable regimes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264070/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes random Gaussian noise as input and generates natural RGB images at resolutions from 32×32 (CIFAR-10, ImageNet-32) up to 512×512 (ImageNet-512). For density estimation, it additionally produces per-sample negative log-likelihood estimates via a Monte Carlo evaluation of the Rényi bound. A key architectural detail is the use of a continuous diffusion process in a latent representation space coupled with a separate discrete decoder for pixel-space reconstruction, avoiding the train-test mismatch introduced by dequantization.

## Method

The paper establishes that Rényi divergence of order α naturally induces a generalized score-matching objective for diffusion processes (Theorem 3.1), where the derivative of D_α with respect to the noise variance yields a weighted score-matching loss. This is made tractable through a variational Rényi bound (Theorem 3.3) that tightens the standard ELBO for α ∈ (0,1], yielding a training loss of the form (1+α)/4 · w(λ)/ρ(λ) · ‖ε − ε̂‖² with importance-sampling-based time weighting. The order parameter α provides explicit control: α=1 recovers the standard KL/ELBO objective, while smaller α (e.g., α=1/2, related to Hellinger distance) produces tighter likelihood bounds and better NLL without sacrificing FID. The approach requires no architectural changes—only a modified loss weight and the existing ε-prediction parameterization—and is combined with a discrete decoder for the reconstruction term.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, RDM achieves a state-of-the-art NLL of 2.09 bits/dim while simultaneously attaining a strong FID of 2.08, outperforming the best prior diffusion-based likelihood model W-PCDM (2.35 BPD, 6.23 FID) and flow-based i-DODE (2.42 BPD, 3.76 FID). On ImageNet-32, RDM reaches 2.94 BPD and 3.75 FID, and on ImageNet-128 it achieves 2.57 BPD with 1.89 FID and 178.1 IS. On ImageNet-256, RDM obtains a competitive FID of 2.24 with 250 NFE and IS of 268.1, comparable to VDM++ (2.12 FID) and DiT-XL/2 (2.27 FID).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RDM achieves the lowest NLL across all evaluated datasets while maintaining competitive FID, effectively breaking the traditional NLL–FID trade-off. On CIFAR-10, RDM (2.09 BPD, 2.08 FID) substantially outperforms W-PCDM (2.35 BPD, 6.23 FID) and i-DODE (2.42 BPD, 3.76 FID) in both metrics simultaneously, and also surpasses likelihood-focused methods like MULAN (2.55 BPD but 17.62 FID) and DiffEnc (2.62 BPD, 11.20 FID) that sacrifice sample quality for likelihood. On ImageNet-32, RDM (2.94 BPD, 3.75 FID) improves over W-PCDM (3.32 BPD) and Flow Matching (3.53 BPD, 5.02 FID) while keeping FID competitive. On ImageNet-64 and -128, RDM achieves the best NLL (2.77 and 2.57 BPD respectively) among diffusion-based methods, outperforming W-PCDM (2.95 and 2.64 BPD). On ImageNet-256, RDM's FID of 2.24 is competitive with VDM++ (2.12) and DiT-XL/2 (2.27), though not state-of-the-art, as the architecture is not explicitly optimized for perceptual quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the variational Rényi bound (Li & Turner, 2016) from standard variational inference to the diffusion model setting, where prior likelihood training was restricted to the KL-based ELBO (Kingma et al., 2021; Song et al., 2021b). It establishes a formal connection between Rényi divergence and a generalized score-matching objective (Theorem 3.1), showing that the standard score matching loss is recovered at α=1. Conceptually, it departs from prior attempts to address the NLL–FID trade-off through reweighting schedules, noise schedule modifications, or architectural changes (Choi et al., 2022; Kingma & Gao, 2023; Li et al., 2024a), arguing instead that the trade-off is fundamentally a property of the divergence choice rather than the model architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, single-objective framework where the Rényi order α continuously interpolates between support-sensitive and likelihood-aware training, offering practitioners a tunable knob for the density-estimation vs. sample-quality trade-off without architectural modifications. The key open question is whether the empirical weighting function w(λ) can be theoretically characterized, and whether the likelihood gains consistently translate to perceptual improvements at higher resolutions or in conditional generation settings. The authors acknowledge that no theoretical guarantee links likelihood improvement to sample quality, and the method does not achieve state-of-the-art FID, leaving the full potential of the Rényi objective for perceptual optimization unexplored.
