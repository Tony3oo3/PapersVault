## Problem

Existing travel-planning benchmarks (TravelPlanner, ChinaTravel, TP-RAG) feature loosely coupled constraints solvable via local greedy decisions and assume constraint parameters are pre-extracted into structured text. They fail to capture two critical aspects of real deployment: (1) tightly coupled temporal dependencies where a single scheduling error cascades to invalidate the entire itinerary, and (2) the need to recover executable constraints from rendered web interfaces with visual cues like sold-out slots and tiered pricing. WorldTravel asks whether current agents can handle both challenges simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a natural-language user query specifying travelers, attractions, dining, and preferences, then interacts with 8 APIs (attraction booking, restaurant reservation, hotel search, route planning) that return rendered webpage screenshots rather than structured JSON, forcing OCR, UI-semantics parsing, and cross-page information aggregation. The output is a strict JSON itinerary containing an ordered activity list with start times, durations, discrete decisions (ticket types, hotel), and per-item costs. In the text ablation setting, the same APIs return structured textual descriptions instead of screenshots, isolating planning ability from visual extraction.

## Method

WorldTravel comprises 150 manually verified tasks across 5 European cities, each containing 15+ interdependent constraints (ranging from 10 to 23) with 0–5 fixed timed-entry anchors that propagate constraints across the full schedule. The companion environment, WorldTravel-Webscape, contains 2,000+ static HTML-rendered webpages (36 attractions, 25 restaurants, 26 hotels) with realistic UI layouts, grayed-out unavailable slots, and information deliberately fragmented across booking portals, travel guides, menus, and transportation matrices. Evaluation is fully automated via per-constraint boolean verification functions that partition into hard constraints (timed-entry slots, operational windows, minimum dwell times, inter-activity travel buffers) determining feasibility, and soft constraints (combinatorial cost matching, hotel selection) scored only among feasible solutions. The benchmark is constructed through a three-stage pipeline: multi-source data collection (official + user-generated), LLM-assisted webpage and task synthesis with city-specific reasoning challenges, and three-round quality control that adjusts ticket availability and blocks time slots to enforce tight coupling and near-unique solutions.

## Results

On WorldTravel (150 tasks, 5 European cities, avg. 15+ tightly coupled constraints), the best text-only model GPT-5.2 achieves only 28.00% Feasibility Rate, while the best multi-modal model Claude-Opus-4.5 reaches 13.33%. In the multi-modal setting, GPT-5.2 drops sharply to 3.42% feasibility (a 24.58 pp decline from text), revealing a substantial visual-to-plan gap. Human performance on a 30-task sample reaches 77.86% feasibility, indicating a large gap between current agents and human planners. Even under the Gold-θ ablation (all hard constraint parameters provided in structured form), GPT-5.2 achieves only 26.67% feasibility on high-coupling tasks, confirming that planning reasoning itself is the primary bottleneck.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a benchmark paper, so the 'baselines' are the 10 frontier LLMs and 7 VLMs evaluated under two settings. In the text setting, GPT-5.2 leads with 28.00% feasibility, followed by Deepseek v3.2 (17.33%) and GPT-5.1 (16.67%); proprietary models (GPT-5.2, GPT-5.1, Claude-Opus-4.5) all exceed 15% while open-source models (GLM-4.6, Qwen-3-A235B) remain below 10%. In the multi-modal setting, the ranking shifts dramatically: Claude-Opus-4.5 becomes the top performer at 13.33%, while GPT-5.2 collapses to 3.42% (a 24.58 pp drop), suggesting that strong text reasoning does not transfer to visual constraint extraction. All models fall far short of the 77.86% human feasibility rate. The Gold-θ ablation further shows that even with perfect parameter access, the best model (GPT-5.2) reaches only 26.67% on high-coupling tasks, isolating planning reasoning as an independent bottleneck from information extraction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the existing travel-planning benchmark lineage (TravelPlanner, ChinaTravel, TP-RAG) along two axes: constraint coupling and information modality. Prior benchmarks assume structured textual inputs and loosely coupled constraints where local failures degrade quality without invalidating the plan; WorldTravel instead requires agents to recover executable constraints from rendered webpage screenshots and satisfy tightly coupled temporal dependencies where a single scheduling error cascades to infeasibility. Rather than extending a specific planning algorithm, the paper redefines the evaluation setting to stress-test the intersection of multimodal information extraction and long-horizon constraint satisfaction, a combination absent from prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark provides a clean diagnostic decomposition: the Gold-θ ablation isolates pure planning difficulty (26.67% feasibility even with perfect parameters), while the text-to-multimodal gap (28% → 3.4%) isolates visual constraint extraction as an independent bottleneck. The identified planning-horizon threshold at ~10 constraints offers a concrete capacity metric for tracking progress across model generations. Limitations include the narrow geographic scope (5 European cities, 150 tasks), the static (non-live) webpage environment, and the somewhat artificial construction of tight coupling via blocked time slots, which may overstate real-world brittleness. Open questions include whether findings generalize to other constrained-planning domains and whether live web retrieval with dynamic availability changes the difficulty profile.
