## Problem

Modern LLMs rely on subword tokenization and autoregressive ordering as structural priors, but the computational cost of removing both simultaneously—via byte-level modeling and masked diffusion—remains uncharacterized. Prior byte-level work studied only autoregressive objectives, and prior diffusion work used subword tokens, leaving the interaction between granular byte representations and order-agnostic generation unexplored. This paper asks whether the scaling overhead of byte modeling is uniform across objectives and what mechanistic factors explain any disparity.

## Input / Output

Models consume text sequences in one of two representations: raw UTF-8 bytes (vocabulary size 256, context window 8192) or Llama-2 BPE tokens (vocabulary size 32,000, context window 1792), with sequence lengths normalized so both process equivalent raw data per step. AR models output next-token predictions under causal masking; MDM models output unmasked token sequences via iterative denoising with bidirectional attention. Performance is reported as Bits-per-Byte (BPB), which normalizes total NLL by raw byte count to decouple predictive quality from discretization strategy, enabling direct comparison across the 256 vs. 32,000 vocabulary gap.

## Method

The paper conducts a compute-matched scaling study using a standard Transformer backbone (pre-norm, SwiGLU, RoPE, full global attention) swept from 48M to 1.23B non-embedding parameters, training AR (causal) and MDM (bidirectional, linear masking schedule) models on both byte and BPE inputs across FLOPs budgets from 6×10¹⁸ to 2×10²¹. The key mechanistic investigation has two parts: (1) analyzing predictive entropy of AR byte models, revealing emergent segmentation where high-entropy regions align with BPE token boundaries (ROC AUC 0.829), indicating the model implicitly rediscover subword structure through its stable causal history; and (2) controlled permutation experiments (global random, inter-block-k, intra-block-k) that isolate local vs. global context destruction, using DEFLATE compressibility as a model-free baseline. The central finding is that the byte-modeling compute penalty is objective-dependent: isoFLOPs extrapolation places AR byte–BPE parity at ~1.3×10²² FLOPs but MDM byte–BPE parity at ~4.2×10²⁶ FLOPs, attributed to context fragility where stochastic masking destroys the local contiguity needed to resolve semantics from raw bytes.

## Results

In the compute-matched scaling study (48M–1.23B params, SlimPajama-627B), AR byte models converge toward BPE parity at ~F≈1.3×10²² FLOPs, while MDM byte models require an extrapolated ~F≈4.2×10²⁶ FLOPs to close the gap. At BPB=1.0, the AR compute gap is ~7.9× (2.3×10²⁰ vs 2.9×10¹⁹ FLOPs), shrinking to ~2.3× at BPB=0.8; the MDM gap remains ~20.5× at BPB=1.0. On downstream zero-shot reasoning (7 benchmarks), AR BPE 717M achieves 46.23 avg accuracy, AR byte 717M reaches 44.23 (capacity match), while MDM byte 717M stagnates at 36.76 despite the same capacity increase that benefited the AR model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is byte-level vs. BPE tokenization under two objectives (AR and MDM), evaluated at matched compute or matched capacity. AR byte models narrow the gap to BPE as capacity increases: from 41.71 avg (180M, compute-matched) to 44.23 avg (717M, capacity-matched), closing ~60% of the 4.5-point deficit to the 46.23 BPE baseline. In stark contrast, MDM byte models stagnate or degrade: 37.73 avg at 180M drops to 36.76 at 717M, failing to benefit from the same 4× capacity increase that helped AR. Across all settings, AR consistently outperforms MDM by 4–9 points in average accuracy, and BPE consistently outperforms byte by 2–4 points (AR) or 4–5 points (MDM). The isoFLOPs analysis quantifies this: the AR byte–BPE compute gap shrinks from ~7.9× to ~2.3× across the studied range, while the MDM gap remains ~20–34×, indicating that the order-agnostic diffusion objective is fundamentally more sensitive to the absence of pre-composed subword units.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads that have been studied largely in isolation: byte-level language modeling (ByT5, MEGABYTE, MambaByte, Byte Latent Transformer) and masked diffusion language models (D3PM, MDLM, MD4). Prior byte-level work has been conducted exclusively within the autoregressive paradigm, while prior diffusion LM work has predominantly relied on subword tokenization. The paper departs from both lines by systematically studying the *interaction* between data representation (byte vs. BPE) and modeling objective (AR vs. MDM) under a compute-matched protocol following the Chinchilla scaling laws framework, revealing that the byte-modeling penalty is objective-dependent—a dependency that neither prior thread could observe in isolation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that byte-level MDMs may require ~4×10²⁶ FLOPs to reach BPE parity (versus ~10²² for AR) provides a critical data point for anyone considering tokenizer-free diffusion architectures, suggesting that the combination of granular representations and order-agnostic generation is fundamentally more expensive than either axis alone. The mechanistic explanation—emergent subword segmentation in AR models that is destroyed by stochastic masking in MDM—offers a concrete hypothesis for why the gap exists and points toward architectural fixes (hierarchical masking, hybrid backbones). Open questions include whether the extreme FLOPs extrapolation holds beyond the studied 2×10²¹ range, whether non-Transformer backbones (SSMs, Mamba) alter the picture, and whether the span-masking failure indicates a deeper incompatibility between joint multi-byte prediction and diffusion objectives.
