## Problem

In multi-agent online learning, the long-run behavior of no-regret dynamics such as FTRL is set-valued and often chaotic, making it difficult to predict which regions of strategy space are stable outcomes. A natural question is whether the ordinal preference structure of a game—its preference graph encoding profitable unilateral deviations—suffices to determine these stable outcomes, or whether cardinal payoff information is also required. This paper delineates precisely when preferences are sufficient (subgames) and when they are not (general spans), and provides a payoff-based condition that restores stability in the latter case.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes as input a finite normal-form game (specified by a payoff table over pure profiles) and a choice of decomposable FTRL regularizer (e.g., entropic/softmax or Euclidean/projection). The outputs are theoretical characterizations rather than algorithmic predictions: necessary conditions on the skeleton of any stable set (s-clubness), an exact equivalence for subgames (clubness iff asymptotic stability of the span), a concrete 2×2×2 counterexample where a club set's span is dynamically unstable, and a verifiable payoff-based sufficient condition (radness) guaranteeing asymptotic stability of arbitrary spans. Radness can be checked in O(|N||H||A\H|) payoff queries, in contrast to the PPAD-hardness of computing mixed Nash equilibria.

## Method

The paper develops a bridge between combinatorial properties of the preference graph and dynamical properties of FTRL strategy flows through four key results. First, it proves that any FTRL-stable set must have an s-club skeleton, using a dual score-space argument that exploits bounded speed in score space to show strict better replies cause escape from any neighborhood. Second, for subgames, it constructs a Fenchel gap energy function F_B(y) = h*(y) − h*_B(y) that converts dissipativity in score space into asymptotic stability in strategy space, yielding the equivalence: a subgame is club iff its span is asymptotically stable under FTRL (for both steep and non-steep regularizers). Third, it constructs a 2×2×2 game where the unique proper club set has an unstable span, demonstrating that preferential and dynamical stability diverge outside the subgame case. Fourth, it introduces radness (resilience to aggregate deviations), a cardinal condition requiring that the aggregate payoff flux Φ(β,α) ≤ 0 for all β in the set and α outside, and proves via a Fenchel-coupling energy (for general FTRL) and a total-outside-mass Lyapunov function (for the replicator dynamics) that s-rad sets have asymptotically stable spans.

## Results

The paper establishes a hierarchy of implications between ordinal (preference-graph) and dynamical stability notions for FTRL. The core negative result (Proposition 2) constructs a 2×2×2 game where a unique proper club set has a dynamically unstable span, proving that preference information alone cannot determine long-run outcomes. The positive results show that for subgames, clubness is necessary and sufficient for asymptotic stability (Theorem 3 + Corollaries 1–2), and that the newly introduced radness condition (resilience to aggregate deviations) guarantees asymptotic stability of arbitrary spans (Theorem 4) and, for the replicator dynamics, of rad club sets (Theorem 5). In weakly acyclic games, minimal attractors coincide exactly with strict Nash equilibria (Corollary 3), and radness is checkable in O(|N||A|²) time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental baselines or quantitative comparisons. The results are positioned against prior theoretical work: Theorem 3 extends Ritzberger & Weibull's (1995) subgame stability result from the replicator dynamics to all FTRL regularizers (both steep and non-steep), using a Fenchel gap energy that overcomes a gap identified in Boone & Mertikopoulos (2023) and Cauvin et al. (2025). The counterexample in Proposition 2 partially disproves the Biggar & Papadimitriou (2025) conjecture that unstable sink equilibria must contain a pure local source, showing instability can arise from full-support initializations under every FTRL instance. The radness condition generalizes Biggar & Papadimitriou's two-player payoff-based stability criterion to arbitrary games and arbitrary sets of pure strategies, and intersects non-trivially with their concurrent result on club cycles (neither implies the other). The paper also complements Biggar & Shames (2023) by extending their chain-transitivity-based attractor containment results from the replicator dynamics to the full FTRL strategy flow.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work connecting preference graphs to attractors of game dynamics, initiated by Papadimitriou & Piliouras's conjecture and partially resolved by Biggar & Shames for the replicator dynamics. It generalizes the analysis from the replicator (a single FTRL instance) to the full class of FTRL dynamics with arbitrary decomposable regularizers, and sharpens the subgame stability result of Ritzberger & Weibull and Boone & Mertikopoulos by using a Fenchel-gap energy that works for both steep and non-steep regularizers. The paper departs from Biggar & Papadimitriou's two-player payoff-based stability condition by introducing radness, a setwise cardinal condition applicable to arbitrary games and arbitrary sets of pure strategies, and by constructing a concrete counterexample showing that ordinal closure (clubness) does not guarantee dynamic stability beyond subgames.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean 'preference-based toolbox' that delineates exactly when ordinal incentive information suffices to predict stable learning outcomes (subgames, weakly acyclic games) and when it does not (general spans), with radness as the first general payoff-based sufficient condition for asymptotic stability of arbitrary spans. The counterexample—a 2×2×2 game where a club set's span is dynamically unstable under every FTRL instance—partially disproves a conjecture of Biggar & Papadimitriou and shows that cardinal payoff values can be essential for predicting long-run behavior, with direct implications for reward design in multi-agent reinforcement learning. Open questions include whether radness is also necessary, whether a complete characterization of attractors exists in terms of pure-strategy payoff conditions, and whether all asymptotically stable limit sets must lie on the boundary of the strategy space.
