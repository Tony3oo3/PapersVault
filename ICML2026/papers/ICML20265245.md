## Problem

Policy Mirror Descent (PMD) for LLM post-training requires estimating the log-partition function to compute ideal Boltzmann-reweighted policy updates, but this estimation is unreliable from the limited rollouts available in practice. The Kimi K1.5/K2 approach (PMD-MEAN) approximates the log-partition with the mean reward under the sampling policy, yet no one had characterized what this approximation actually optimizes or why it empirically outperforms the partition-fitting variant (PMD-PART) in stale-rollout settings. The paper asks: what is the exact population solution of PMD-MEAN, and what implicit regularization does the mean-reward approximation induce?

## Input / Output

The method operates at the sequence level (contextual bandit): each input is a text prompt paired with K sampled responses from a potentially stale policy, scored by a binary correctness reward. The regression target is the log-policy ratio log(π(y|x)/π_t(y|x)), fitted via squared loss with a leave-one-out advantage estimator (r_i minus the mean of the other K−1 rewards) divided by temperature τ. In practice, the loss is length-normalized by 1/|y_i|, which introduces an action-dependent quadratic ratio penalty that regularizes longer sequences more strongly.

## Method

The paper derives the closed-form population minimizer of the PMD-MEAN regression loss, showing it takes a Lambert-W function form: π_{t+1}(y) = π_t(y) exp(Δ_y/τ − W(λ/τ² · exp(Δ_y/τ))), where W is the principal Lambert-W branch and λ is a normalization constant. Crucially, this solution is proven equivalent to solving a mirror descent subproblem with an adaptive mixed KL−χ² regularizer, where the χ² coefficient λ/τ scales with the pass rate p_t and remains O(1) even as τ→0, providing substantial regularization even when the nominal KL coefficient is minimal. The χ² term directly penalizes large probability-ratio spikes, making updates more conservative when mean reward is low (early training). A convergence analysis under finite rollouts shows PMD-MEAN's target estimation error is O(p_t(1−p_t)²/τ²) versus O(e^{2/τ}·p_t/n) for PMD-PART in the small-n, small-p_t regime, explaining PMD-MEAN's empirical stability under stale rollouts and enabling 4.6× throughput gains via larger rollout batches.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-7B with staleness-16 rollouts, PMD-MEAN (τ=0.005) achieves 19.69% AIME24 and 19.48% AIME25 (Avg@32), surpassing GRPO (17.08/10.52) by +2.6 and +9.0 points respectively, and matching the on-policy RLOO baseline (18.65/18.33) while using 16× more stale rollouts. On Qwen3-30B-A3B-Base, PMD-MEAN (τ=0.1) reaches 50.83% AIME24 and 37.19% AIME25, improving over GRPO (36.56/27.92) by +14.3 and +9.3 points. The larger global batch size (512 prompts vs. 32) yields a 4.6× wall-clock speedup (0.0126 vs. 0.0569 ms/token) with comparable final performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), PMD-MEAN wins decisively on both model families: +5.8 points average on Qwen2.5-7B (19.58 vs. 13.80) and +11.8 points on Qwen3-30B-A3B-Base (44.01 vs. 32.24), with the gap widening at higher staleness (Table 5 shows GRPO degrading to 36.9 average at staleness-64 while PMD-MEAN holds at 42.0). Against the on-policy RLOO baseline (Ahmadian et al., 2024), PMD-MEAN at staleness-16 matches or slightly exceeds on-policy performance (19.58 vs. 18.49 average on 7B) while achieving a 4.6× throughput improvement by amortizing inference over a larger rollout batch. Against GSPO (Zheng et al., 2025), PMD-MEAN wins on Qwen2.5-7B (19.58 vs. 13.75 average) and essentially ties on Qwen3-30B-A3B-Base (44.01 vs. 43.96), with individual benchmarks favoring different methods. In the additional Qwen3-8B-Base experiments (Table 5), PMD-MEAN outperforms both GRPO and GSPO by ~5–7 points at staleness-16 and maintains its lead at staleness-64 where GRPO and GSPO degrade substantially.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper provides the first exact theoretical characterization of PMD-MEAN, the practical log-partition approximation algorithm deployed in Kimi K1.5/K2, by showing it is not merely an approximation of the ideal KL-regularized PMD update but rather the exact solution to a different mirror-descent subproblem with an adaptive mixed KL-χ² regularizer. It builds directly on the PMD framework (Geist et al. 2019; Tomar et al. 2022) and the Kimi-style regression formulation (Team et al. 2025b), while drawing a conceptual parallel to χ²-PO (Huang et al. 2024) for preference optimization. The paper deliberately positions itself against the dominant paradigm of mitigating off-policy staleness through importance weighting and clipping (PPO, GRPO, GSPO, CISPO), arguing that a minimalist regression approach has built-in stability properties that these correction mechanisms must engineer externally.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The analysis provides a principled explanation for why a seemingly ad-hoc approximation (replacing log-partition with mean reward) produces stable training in production LLM RL systems, and identifies the adaptive χ² term as the mechanism that prevents aggressive probability-ratio spikes when pass rates are low. Open questions include whether the implicit regularization can be made explicit and tunable (e.g., pass-rate-dependent τ scheduling), whether the Lambert-W structure extends cleanly to full MDPs with token-level credit assignment, and whether other f-divergence choices in the regression target yield analogous implicit regularizers. The practical impact is significant: the 4.6× throughput gain from larger stale-rollout batches with competitive or superior accuracy suggests this approach could become a standard component of efficient LLM RL pipelines.
