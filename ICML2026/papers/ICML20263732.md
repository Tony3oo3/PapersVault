## Problem

GRPO post-training of LLMs suffers from a plasticity-stability dilemma: aggressive reasoning updates cause catastrophic forgetting while strict KL constraints block new skill acquisition. The paper identifies the root cause as high-dimensional geometric conflict between the plasticity and stability gradients, which causes destructive interference in the standard additive update rule. Existing conflict-resolution methods like PCGrad treat gradients as deterministic vectors, ignoring the inherent stochasticity of GRPO's group-based Monte Carlo estimates and risking the discard of valid learning signals.

## Input / Output

The method consumes per-layer plasticity gradients (from the clipped surrogate gain), stability gradients (from the KL divergence), and scalar variance estimates computed as the trace of intra-group gradient covariance. It produces a calibrated gradient vector per MLP layer via a closed-form soft projection, while attention and LayerNorm layers receive the standard GRPO additive update. The final output is the updated policy parameter set θ, with the projection coefficient α automatically determined per layer and per step from the instantaneous precision ratio.

## Method

PCR models the plasticity and stability gradients as Gaussian random variables (justified by the CLT over group samples) and performs an orthogonal decomposition of the plasticity gradient into a safe component (perpendicular to the stability gradient) and a conflicting component (parallel). A Bayesian MAP estimation over a scalar latent variable along the conflict axis yields a precision-weighted retention coefficient k = λ_pla/(λ_pla + λ_sta), producing a soft projection where the projection strength α = 1 − k is automatically calibrated by the signal-to-noise ratio rather than fixed. The paper proves this coefficient is the MMSE-optimal linear estimator, guaranteeing lower posterior risk than both hard projection (PCGrad) and naive summation. For computational feasibility, PCR is applied exclusively to MLP layers (identified as factual knowledge stores via interpretability literature), while attention layers use standard GRPO updates, yielding a hybrid strategy with negligible overhead.

## Results

PCR achieves the best average Pass@1 across all three base models on math reasoning benchmarks: 57.7% on DeepScaleR-1.5B-Preview (+3.2 over base), 48.5% on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B (+3.6), and 67.6% on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B (+3.5). On the hardest benchmark (AIME 2024), PCR reaches 48.1%, 32.3%, and 59.7% respectively, outperforming the strongest baseline GCPO by 1.4, 1.3, and 1.4 points. On code generation (HumanEval, Qwen2-7B-Instruct), PCR gains approximately 1.2% over the best baseline in both 0-shot and 5-shot settings. PCR also demonstrates superior training stability, maintaining a smooth gradient norm throughout training where GRPO exhibits pronounced oscillations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PCR consistently outperforms all four RL post-training baselines (GRPO, GVPO, MRT, GCPO) across every base model and every individual benchmark in Table 1, with average gains of +0.5 to +3.6% over GRPO, +1.6 to +1.8% over GVPO, +1.4 to +1.8% over MRT, and +0.9 to +1.1% over GCPO. The margin over the strongest baseline GCPO is most pronounced on the smaller 1.5B models (1.1% on Qwen-1.5B) and on the hardest benchmarks (AIME 2024: +1.4 to +1.4 points; MinervaMATH: +0.9 to +1.5 points). Notably, GRPO actually degrades on AMC 2023 (−1.5% on DeepScaleR) while PCR achieves positive gains on every dataset-model combination, indicating more stable optimization. On the MTL comparison (Appendix Table 4), PCR also beats deterministic PCGrad on MultiMNIST (90.4% vs. 89.6%) and CelebA (90.9% vs. 88.8%), confirming the advantage of probabilistic over hard projection. PCR is further shown to be plug-and-play: when stacked on top of GVPO, MRT, or GCPO (Table 2), it adds an additional +0.5 to +1.1% average improvement, demonstrating orthogonality to other GRPO variants.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the gradient-conflict resolution lineage from multi-task learning (PCGrad, Yu et al. 2020; conflict-averse descent, Liu et al. 2021) into the specific setting of GRPO-based LLM post-training. It departs from prior GRPO stabilization work (GVPO, MRT, GCPO, BNPO, GSPO) that operates on scalar reward/loss shaping by instead targeting the high-dimensional geometric antagonism between plasticity and stability gradients. The key conceptual departure from PCGrad is replacing deterministic hard projection with a Bayesian soft projection that accounts for the Monte Carlo stochasticity inherent in group-based gradient estimation, and restricting the computationally expensive projection to MLP layers based on interpretability findings (Geva et al., 2021).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PCR offers a plug-and-play module that consistently improves upon multiple existing GRPO variants (GVPO, MRT, GCPO) without modifying their reward or clipping mechanisms, making it immediately adoptable for practitioners already using these frameworks. The MMSE optimality guarantee provides a principled justification for the soft projection coefficient, moving beyond heuristic tuning of the KL penalty β. However, empirical validation is limited to models up to 7B parameters, leaving open whether the marginal benefit of uncertainty-aware arbitration diminishes at 70B+ scale where gradient estimates from large groups may already be sufficiently low-variance. The finding that gradient conflict concentrates in MLP layers rather than Attention layers, while consistent with prior interpretability work, has not been previously exploited for targeted optimization.
