## Problem

Standard preference optimization methods like DPO model preferences over entire sequences, yet language models generate text through a sequence of local, token-by-token decisions. Existing token-level extensions (TDPO, TIS-DPO) still assume a sequence-level Bradley-Terry model and merely redistribute the global preference signal across timesteps, leaving per-prefix (state-wise) optimality implicit rather than explicitly enforced. This paper asks how to recover a policy that is preference-optimal at every prefix state using only standard sequence-level pairwise comparison data.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the same pairwise preference triples (prompt x, chosen response y^w, rejected response y^l) as DPO, with no additional annotation or reward model required. Internally, each sequence-level label is lifted to a sequence of prefix-conditioned token comparisons: at every timestep t, the model compares the next token from the winning trajectory against the next token from the losing trajectory, conditioned on the respective prefixes. The output is a fine-tuned language model whose policy is provably token-level preference-optimal, producing generated text that is both better aligned and more diverse than sequence-level baselines.

## Method

TBPO posits a token-level Bradley-Terry preference model where the preference probability at step t is a sigmoid of the difference in a token-level score S(s,a) between the winning and losing next-token actions, conditioned on their respective prefixes. Two score choices yield two variants: TBPO-Q uses the reference-policy state-action value Q, and TBPO-A uses the reference-policy advantage A. The key technical step is casting the resulting policy-ratio relationship as a Bregman-divergence density-ratio matching problem, which yields a tractable loss (Eq. 15) parameterized by a convex generator h; the paper uses Scaled Basu's power divergence for tunable gradient reweighting. A state-dependent correction weight w_t (a partition-function ratio for TBPO-Q, estimated by a lightweight learned baseline head; a KL-baseline difference for TBPO-A, estimated via the K3 unbiased estimator) accounts for the fact that token comparisons are made across two different prefix states. Theorem 4.3 guarantees that under sufficient capacity, minimizing the TBPO objective recovers the exact token-level optimal policy for the KL-regularized objective, and Theorem 4.4 provides the equivalent tractable training loss.

## Results

On the Open LLM Leaderboard suite, TBPO-Q achieves the best average score on Mistral 7B v0.1 (60.95) and TBPO-A on Llama 3 8B (70.50), outperforming the strongest non-TBPO baselines (TIS-DPO: 59.66 / 66.82) by 1.3 and 3.7 points respectively. Gains are most pronounced on reasoning-centric benchmarks: TBPO-Q reaches 39.34 on GSM8K (vs. 34.87 for DPO, 18.49 for SFT) on Mistral, and TBPO-A attains 79.22 on GSM8K and 64.08 on ARC on Llama 3 8B. On MT-Bench and Anthropic HH-RLHF, TBPO variants achieve the highest length-controlled win rates while producing shorter responses than all baselines, and they also yield the best three-way diversity trade-off (Distinct-1, predictive entropy, Self-BLEU).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TBPO consistently outperforms all four baselines (DPO, TDPO, TIS-DPO, BPO) on the average Open LLM Leaderboard score for both backbones. Against DPO, TBPO-Q gains +1.65 (Mistral) and +4.14 (Llama 3) on average, with the largest individual gains on GSM8K (+4.47 / +4.72) and ARC (+0.34 / +8.02). Against TDPO (the strongest token-level baseline), TBPO-A leads by +2.32 (Mistral) and +2.59 (Llama 3), with TBPO-A achieving 79.22 on GSM8K vs. TDPO's 75.10 on Llama 3. Against TIS-DPO, the margin is +1.07 (Mistral) and +3.68 (Llama 3), and against BPO it is +2.44 (Mistral) and +4.78 (Llama 3). On MT-Bench and Anthropic HH-RLHF, TBPO variants achieve the highest length-controlled win rates with shorter outputs than all baselines, and on the diversity metrics (Distinct-1, predictive entropy, Self-BLEU) they occupy the best Pareto frontier, whereas TIS-DPO shows the strongest diversity collapse (lowest entropy, highest Self-BLEU).

## Relation to Prior Work

TBPO extends the DPO lineage by shifting the preference model from sequence-level to token-level Bradley-Terry, departing from TDPO and TIS-DPO which merely redistribute a sequence-level BT signal across timesteps. It also repurposes the Bregman-divergence density-ratio matching framework from BPO (Kim et al., 2025), but applies it at the token level with a novel state-dependent correction weight that accounts for cross-prefix comparisons. The resulting objective generalizes the logistic/DPO loss while preserving DPO-style simplicity (no explicit reward model, no on-policy RL), and the two instantiations (TBPO-Q via learned partition-function baseline, TBPO-A via K3 KL estimator) offer a plug-and-play family of losses parameterized by the Bregman generator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical guarantee that minimizing the TBPO objective recovers a token-level optimal policy provides a principled foundation that prior token-level methods (TDPO, TIS-DPO) lack, since their per-state optimality remains implicit. Practically, TBPO improves alignment quality, training stability, and—importantly—preserves generation diversity better than DPO-style baselines, addressing a known failure mode of preference optimization. Open questions include how the learned baseline head (TBPO-Q) scales to very long sequences, whether the token-level BT assumption holds under highly structured or multi-turn settings, and whether the moderate empirical gains (1–3 points over TDPO) persist at larger model scales or with more diverse preference data.
