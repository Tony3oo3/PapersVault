## Problem

Solving large-scale semidefinite programs (SDPs) is computationally expensive, motivating neural surrogates, yet no existing GNN architecture can faithfully represent even linear SDP solutions. The fundamental challenge is that SDP variables are matrix entries constrained to the PSD cone, introducing permutation-equivariance under simultaneous row and column permutations that standard variable-constraint bipartite representations fail to capture. This paper formally characterizes the exact level of GNN expressivity required to recover optimal SDP solutions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a linear SDP instance specified by a symmetric objective matrix C ∈ S^n, m symmetric constraint matrices A_k ∈ S^n, and constraint values b ∈ R^m, and produces the unique minimum-Frobenius-norm optimal solution X* ∈ S_+^n. The output must satisfy three structural constraints: symmetry (X = X^T), permutation equivariance under simultaneous row/column permutations of the input matrices, and invariance to constraint reordering. The n² variable entries are not independent but coupled through the PSD cone, which is the key structural difference from LPs where variables are scalar.

## Method

The paper proves that standard VC-WL (1-WL on the variable-constraint bipartite graph) and the higher-order VC-2-WL both fail to distinguish SDP variables that differ in the optimal solution, while VC-2-FWL is sufficient because its stable coloring subsumes the update operations of the PDHG first-order solver. The resulting neural architecture, VC-2-FMPNN, differs from weaker baselines in its variable update: instead of aggregating row and column neighborhoods independently (as in VC-2-WL), it aggregates joint pairs {v_uj, v_iu} as unordered 2-multisets over all u, capturing the coupled row-column structure inherent to PSD matrices. This design automatically enforces symmetry (v_ij = v_ji) without manual symmetrization. The model is trained with supervised MSE against ground-truth solution matrices, and its predictions can warm-start the SCS solver, yielding up to 80.7% runtime reduction on SDPLIB instances.

## Results

On the Max-Cut benchmark, VC-2-FMPNN achieves a test loss of 5.5×10⁻⁵ and a raw objective gap of 0.126%, outperforming the weakest baseline VC-MPNN (loss 0.216, gap 1.297%) by over three orders of magnitude in loss. After PSD projection, VC-2-FMPNN maintains a 0.111% gap while VC-MPNN's gap explodes to 26.0%, confirming that weaker architectures produce solutions far from the feasible cone. On SDPLIB max-cut instances, VC-2-FMPNN achieves training loss ~10⁻⁴ (e.g., 1.97×10⁻⁴ on MCP100) while VC-MPNN and VC-2-MPNN fail to overfit (loss ~0.20). Using VC-2-FMPNN predictions to warm-start the SCS solver yields up to 80.7% runtime reduction (e.g., MCP124-1: 3.24s → 0.62s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VC-2-FMPNN decisively outperforms all theoretically weaker baselines (VC-MPNN, VC-2-MPNN, δ-VC-2-MPNN, VC-2-IGN) on Max-Cut, where it achieves 5.5×10⁻⁵ test loss versus 0.014–0.216 for the others, and a projected objective gap of 0.111% versus 1.9–26%. The gap is most dramatic after PSD projection: VC-MPNN and VC-2-IGN collapse to ~25% projected gap on Max-Cut, while VC-2-FMPNN stays at 0.111%, indicating its predictions are geometrically close to the PSD cone. VC-ET (Edge Transformer with 2-FWL expressivity) is the closest competitor, achieving comparable losses (0.0001 vs 5.5×10⁻⁵ on Max-Cut) but slightly worse projected gaps (0.159% vs 0.111%) and substantially higher inference time (0.050s vs 0.013s at 150×150). On SDPLIB, VC-MPNN and VC-2-MPNN fail to overfit (training loss ~0.12–0.27), while VC-2-FMPNN reaches ~10⁻⁴, confirming the expressivity gap in practice. For solver acceleration, VC-2-FMPNN inference (0.013s at 150×150) is ~250× faster than SCS (3.357s), and warm-starting SCS with its prediction reduces solver time by 11–81% across SDPLIB instances.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the learning-to-optimize (L2O) paradigm from LPs/MILPs—where GNNs operate on variable-constraint bipartite graphs—to linear SDPs, where the matrix-valued PSD-cone constraint introduces simultaneous row/column permutation symmetries that standard bipartite representations cannot capture. It builds directly on the WL hierarchy of GNN expressivity (Morris et al., 2019; Maron et al., 2019a) and the PDHG first-order solver (Chambolle & Pock, 2016; Wang et al., 2024), proving that the solver's iterative updates are subsumed by the VC-2-FWL coloring process. The key conceptual departure from prior L2O work is the identification of a precise expressivity threshold: 1-WL (VC-WL) and even 2-WL (VC-2-WL) are provably insufficient, while 2-FWL-level joint aggregation is both necessary and sufficient.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first rigorous theoretical framework connecting GNN expressivity to SDP solution recovery, providing practitioners with a principled blueprint for neural SDP solver design rather than relying on architectural heuristics. The warm-starting results (up to 80.7% speedup on SDPLIB) demonstrate immediate practical value for large-scale SDP solving. Key limitations include non-uniform expressivity guarantees (existence of parameters per instance size, no size-generalization guarantee), no convergence guarantee for gradient-based training, and evaluation on moderate instance sizes (≤500×500). Open directions include self-supervised primal-dual training, efficient architectures for SDP subclasses where lower-order expressivity suffices, and extension to broader conic programs.
