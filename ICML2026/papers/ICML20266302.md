## Problem

Standard coupled encoder-pooling architectures for brain graph classification suffer from a fundamental theoretical limitation termed Spectral Unreachability: both GNNs (local low-pass filtering) and Graph Transformers (global homogenization) inevitably suppress the high-frequency topological signals required to delineate sharp functional module boundaries. This spectral mismatch between the smoothing imposed by the backbone and the sharp step-like transitions needed for valid parcellation means existing models can only produce partitions that are either spatially fragmented or spectrally oversmoothed, failing to recover structurally coherent functional brain organization.

## Input / Output

The method takes a brain functional connectivity graph with N ROIs (200 for ABIDE, 190 for ADHD-200, 90 for ADNI) where the adjacency matrix encodes pairwise correlation magnitudes and node attributes are the connectivity profiles themselves (D=N). It produces a disease classification label (binary or up to 6-class), a hierarchical functional parcellation as a soft cluster assignment matrix S ∈ R^{N×K} at each coarsening level, and interpretable biomarkers identified as disease-specific shifts in cluster assignments. The parcellation is generated at K=64 clusters for visualization, with the hierarchical structure progressively reducing node count across L layers.

## Method

HISP-NET adopts a project-then-align paradigm that structurally decouples partition learning from representation smoothing. In the project phase, a topology-agnostic node-wise MLP (with a dual-stream ensemble combining channel-wise affine subspaces and Bernoulli-masked topological views, fused via context-aware gating) directly maps node representations into a partition assignment space, preserving all-frequency spectral components that GNNs and GTs would attenuate. In the align phase, a Topology-Aware Alignment (TAA) regularizer enforces spatial coherence through a joint MinCut loss (intra-module cohesion) and Frobenius-normalized orthogonality constraint (inter-module repulsion), preventing the fragmentation typical of unconstrained topology-agnostic methods. Differentiable coarsening then aggregates representations using the aligned partitions, and stacking L such layers establishes a hierarchy capturing multi-scale functional organizations. Theoretically, the node-wise MLP backbone preserves gradient norms independent of depth, circumventing the spectral unreachability lower bound established in Theorem 1.

## Results

HISP-NET achieves the best average rank (1.17) across three benchmark datasets (ABIDE, ADHD-200, ADNI), with headline results of 78.05% AUC / 72.90% ACC on ABIDE, 83.72% AUC / 76.51% ACC on ADHD-200, and 78.54% AUC / 77.99% ACC on ADNI. On the small-sample ADNI dataset, HISP-NET outperforms the runner-up (BQN) by a substantial 9.37% in ACC (77.99% vs. 68.62%). In multi-class settings (ADNI 6-class, PPMI) and cross-site OOD evaluation, HISP-NET also consistently outperforms specialized baselines including BrainOOD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HISP-NET achieves the best average rank (1.17) across all six metric-dataset combinations, winning on 5 out of 6 metrics. Against the strongest NN baseline BQN, HISP-NET wins on ADHD-200 (AUC 83.72 vs. 83.34; ACC 76.51 vs. 75.68) and ADNI (AUC 78.54 vs. 74.18; ACC 77.99 vs. 68.62, a 9.37% ACC gap), but trails slightly on ABIDE AUC (78.05 vs. 79.85). Against the best Graph Transformer ALTER, HISP-NET outperforms on all six metrics, with the largest gap on ADNI ACC (77.99% vs. 66.92%). Against the best brain-specific GNN A-GCL, HISP-NET leads by 4.19% AUC on ABIDE, 8.94% AUC on ADHD-200, and 10.39% AUC on ADNI. Against the coupled encoder-pooling baseline DiffPool, HISP-NET improves ABIDE AUC by 15.71% and ADNI AUC by 12.33%, while also producing spatially coherent parcellations (ARI 0.89±0.05) versus DiffPool's mode-collapsed clusters (ARI 0.83±0.05). In cross-site OOD evaluation, HISP-NET outperforms the specialized OOD baseline BrainOOD by 3.73% AUC on ABIDE and 3.56% AUC on ADNI (6-class).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant coupled encoder-pooling paradigm in brain graph learning (exemplified by DiffPool, BrainGNN, BrainNETTF) by formally identifying a spectral bottleneck—termed Spectral Unreachability—that prevents GNNs (local low-pass filtering) and Graph Transformers (global homogenization) from preserving the high-frequency boundary signals needed for sharp functional parcellation. Architecturally, it borrows the differentiable coarsening mechanism from DiffPool and the stochastic edge masking from DropEdge, but restructures them around a topology-agnostic MLP projection that bypasses spectral smoothing entirely, followed by a Topology-Aware Alignment regularizer that re-injects spatial coherence. The paper also positions itself against BQN (a pure-MLP brain classifier), arguing that while BQN implicitly circumvents spectral unreachability, it sacrifices the structured parcellation output needed for clinical biomarker extraction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Spectral Unreachability theorem provides a clean theoretical justification for why existing brain graph models produce either fragmented or oversmoothed parcellations, giving practitioners a principled reason to prefer decoupled architectures over deeper GNN/GT stacks. The project-then-align paradigm is a general architectural principle that could transfer to other graph partitioning tasks beyond brain networks. However, the competitive performance of BQN (a structure-free MLP) on ABIDE and ADHD-200 raises an open question about how much the explicit graph topology actually contributes versus raw feature learning, and the paper's own ablation shows diminishing returns beyond two pooling layers. Future work on dynamic connectivity and multi-modal integration is noted but remains unexplored.
