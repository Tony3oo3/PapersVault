## Problem

For quadratically regularized optimal transport (QOT), the rate at which the optimizer's support concentrates around the Monge graph as the regularization parameter ε→0 was unknown in general dimensions: existing upper bounds under standard regularity assumptions fell short of the conjectured ε^{1/(d+2)} scale, and no general lower bound ruling out faster concentration was available. This paper closes both gaps by proving a matching lower bound and, in the affine Brenier regime, a matching pointwise upper bound.

## Input / Output

The theoretical results take compactly supported probability measures μ, ν on R^d with densities bounded above and below (Assumptions 1–2), a Lipschitz Monge map T (Assumption 3), and a regularization parameter ε>0, and produce quantitative bounds on the directed Hausdorff distance dist(spt π_ε; gr T), the mean-squared bias ∫‖y−T(x)‖² dπ_ε, and the pointwise tube thickness sup_{(x,y)∈spt π_ε}‖y−T(x)‖. In the affine case (Assumption 4, T(x)=Ax+a with A≻0), the bound additionally depends on λ_max(A) and det A. The empirical validation discretizes μ, ν into N=M=2000-point empirical measures, solves the discrete QOT via nonlinear Gauss-Seidel and regularized semismooth Newton solvers, and reports a log-log fitted scaling exponent β̂ compared against the theoretical 1/(d+2) for d∈{100,200,500,1000}.

## Method

The lower bound (Theorem 3.3) proceeds by a fiberwise Cauchy-Schwarz argument: for each target point y, the marginal constraint ∫h(x,y)μ(dx)=1 forces the L² norm of the density h=dπ_ε/dP over the fiber to be at least 1/μ(X_y), where X_y is the set of source points with positive density at y; bounding μ(X_y) via the Lipschitz graph geometry and the density upper bound on ν yields ‖h‖²_{L²(P)} ≥ C·r^{−d}, which combined with the sharp value-gap upper bound Δ_ε ≤ C_val·ε^{2/(d+2)} from Eckstein & Nutz (2024) gives r ≥ c·ε^{1/(d+2)}. The mean-squared bias bound (Lemma 3.5) converts the cost gap into a squared-deviation bound via the Fenchel-Young slack identity and the L-Lipschitz smoothness inequality D_φ(x,y) ≥ ‖y−∇φ(x)‖²/(2L). The affine-case upper bound (Theorem 3.7) exploits the fact that when T is affine, the QOT problem over Π(μ,ν) reduces exactly—via the invertible map (x,v)↦(x,T(v))—to a self-transport QOT problem over Π(μ,μ) with a weighted quadratic cost, after which a linear change of variables u=A^{1/2}x, w=A^{1/2}v recovers the standard self-transport form to which the sharp tube-thickness theorem of Wiesel & Xu (2025) applies directly, yielding the matching ε^{1/(d+2)} pointwise bound.

## Results

The paper establishes three main theoretical bounds for QOT with quadratic cost. (1) A general lower bound: dist(spt π_ε; gr T) ≥ c_sm · ε^{1/(d+2)} for ε ∈ (0,1], matching the conjectured optimal exponent. (2) A mean-squared bias bound: E_{π_ε}[‖y−T(x)‖²] ≤ 2L·C_val · ε^{2/(d+2)}, with a corresponding high-probability tail bound. (3) In the affine Brenier regime (e.g., Gaussian-to-Gaussian), a sharp pointwise tube bound: sup_{(x,y)∈spt π_ε} ‖y−T(x)‖ ≤ 8√(λ_max(A)) · (ε/(λ̲_{μ_A}·κ_A·ω_d))^{1/(d+2)}. In the synthetic experiment (d ∈ {100, 200, 500, 1000}, N=M=2000, R=10 seeds), the fitted log-log scaling exponent β̂ is below the theoretical 1/(d+2) for d=100 and d=200, and closest to the prediction at d=500 and d=1000, with the relative error (d+2)β̂−1 crossing zero between d=500 and d=1000.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is primarily a theoretical paper, so there are no competing methods to benchmark against. The key comparisons are against existing theoretical bounds. The general upper bound from Wiesel & Xu (2025) for non-self-transport gives sup ‖y−T(x)‖ ≲ ε^{1/(4(d+1)²)}, which is far from the conjectured ε^{1/(d+2)} scale; the present paper's lower bound (Theorem 3.3) rules out any faster-than-ε^{1/(d+2)} concentration, establishing a gap that remains open in the fully non-affine case. In the self-transport case (μ=ν), Wiesel & Xu (2025) already achieve matching upper and lower bounds at ε^{1/(d+2)}; the present paper extends this to the affine (non-self-transport) regime via an exact reduction to self-transport (Theorem 3.7), closing the gap in that special case. The value-gap rates from Eckstein & Nutz (2024) (Δ_ε = Θ(ε^{2/(d+2)})) serve as the quantitative input that converts the L²-density lower bound into the support lower bound. Empirically, the two deterministic solvers (nonlinear Gauss-Seidel and semismooth Newton from Lorenz et al., 2021) agree within reported variability, and the fitted exponents validate the theoretical ε^{1/(d+2)} scaling most closely in the highest dimensions tested (d=500, 1000), with deviations at lower d attributed to finite-sample and non-asymptotic effects.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three threads in regularized optimal transport: the QOT dual/hinge-density structure from Lorenz et al. (2021), the sharp value-gap convergence rates from Eckstein & Nutz (2024), and the self-transport sparsity bounds from Wiesel & Xu (2025). Prior work established sharp ε^(1/3) rates in one dimension (Gonzalez-Sanz & Nutz, 2024) and general-dimensional upper bounds (Wiesel & Xu, 2025), but the matching lower bound in general dimension was absent, and the affine (Gaussian-to-Gaussian) regime had no pointwise tube bound. The paper's key methodological move is reducing the affine QOT problem exactly to a self-transport problem via a linear change of variables, thereby importing the sharp Wiesel–Xu self-transport estimates into a previously unaddressed setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The general lower bound closes a conjecture from Wiesel & Xu (2025) that the ε^(1/(d+2)) exponent is optimal, ruling out any faster concentration of the QOT support around the Monge graph under standard regularity. This has direct implications for statistical analyses of sparse regularized OT (Gonzalez-Sanz et al., 2025a,b), where geometric control of the support is a key input for sample complexity. The main limitation is that the matching upper bound is restricted to the affine Brenier regime; closing the gap for general non-affine Monge maps remains open, as does extending the analysis to non-compact marginals (e.g., untruncated Gaussians) where the compact-support assumptions no longer hold.
