## Problem

In predict-then-optimize pipelines with integer decision variables, existing surrogate models (e.g., ReLU MLPs, LogSumExp) either lack the structural properties needed for tractable downstream integer optimization or impose rigid parametric assumptions that limit approximation power. No prior function approximator simultaneously achieves universality over convex-extendible discrete functions and guarantees efficient optimization over the integer domain.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260381/cropped_page10_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The DFN takes an integer vector x ∈ Z^d and produces a real-valued scalar equal to the optimal minimum-cost flow value on a fixed directed graph, where node imbalances are determined by the affine map Ax + b. The output is inherently piecewise-linear and convex-extendible in x. For downstream optimization, the trained DFN is minimized over a budget-constrained integer set X = {x ∈ Z^d : x_min ≤ x ≤ x_max, 1^T x = B}, yielding an optimal integer decision vector.

## Method

A DFN is a minimum-cost flow network on a layered directed graph where p special nodes have net outflows given by Ax + b (A ∈ Z^{p×d}, b ∈ Z^p), and the function value is the optimal flow cost. When A = I the induced function is M-convex and minimizable in polynomial time; for general A, the paper proves a generalized exchange property (G-EXC) and bounds optimization complexity via Δ(A) = 2·(2k)^{2k}·det*(A), where k is the row corank of A. Training differentiates through the min-cost flow layer using optimal dual variables as subgradients, and learns integer parameters (A, b, u) via straight-through estimation with a near-identity initialization of A to keep Δ(A) small. The layered topology with a balancing node and high-cost routability arcs guarantees feasibility for all inputs while letting learning shape the effective flow structure.

## Results

DFN achieves the lowest normalized test MSE across all three datasets: 3.4×10⁻³ on the convex quadratic dataset (vs. 12.5×10⁻³ for MLP and 80.4×10⁻³ for LSET), 5.3×10⁻⁴ on resource allocation (vs. 8.0×10⁻⁴ and 10.4×10⁻⁴), and 4.7×10⁻⁶ on MDVSP (vs. 22.1×10⁻⁶ and 9.1×10⁻⁶). For downstream integer optimization, DFN solves the budget-constrained problem in 0.1–1.8 s, while LSET hits the 3600 s time limit on both the quadratic and resource-allocation datasets. The optimality ratio (learned-model optimum / ground-truth optimum) for DFN is 1.0 on MDVSP and 1.1–1.6 on the other two datasets, indicating near-optimal recovered solutions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares DFN against two baselines: a two-hidden-layer ReLU MLP (Fischetti & Jo, 2018 MILP encoding) and the LogSumExp transform (LSET) of Calafiore et al. (2019). DFN outperforms both on normalized test MSE across all three datasets, with the largest margin on the convex quadratic dataset (3.4 vs. 12.5 for MLP and 80.4 for LSET, a ~24× improvement over LSET). On the MDVSP dataset, DFN also leads MLP by roughly 4.7× in MSE. For downstream integer optimization, DFN's structured IP formulation (network matrix + A) solves in under 2 s, whereas LSET's mixed-integer convex program times out at 3600 s on both the quadratic and resource-allocation datasets, and MLP's big-M MILP is similarly intractable at scale. The optimality gap for DFN is small (ratio ≤ 1.6 on RA, 1.0 on MDVSP), confirming that the speed advantage does not come at the cost of solution quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

DFNs generalize minimum-cost flow value functions (a core object in Murota's discrete convex analysis) by replacing the identity input mapping with a general integer matrix A, thereby moving beyond the M-convex regime while retaining a structured optimization guarantee via Δ(A). The paper positions itself against two prior strands: (i) continuous convex approximators like LogSumExp (Calafiore et al., 2019) that are tractable over ℝ^d but not over ℤ^d, and (ii) generic neural surrogates (MLPs, Deep Sets, Deep Submodular Functions) that lack any downstream integer-optimization structure. The theoretical machinery—Smith normal form, Steinitz's lemma, and KZ-reduced bases—draws from lattice theory and discrete convex analysis to bound the optimization complexity of the learned function, a connection not previously exploited in the learning literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DFNs offer the first principled surrogate family that simultaneously achieves strong approximation accuracy for convex-extendible discrete functions and guarantees polynomial-time downstream integer optimization (with complexity explicitly controlled by Δ(A)). This is directly relevant to operations-research pipelines (unit commitment, vehicle scheduling, inventory) where the predict-then-optimize bottleneck is the integer program over the learned model. Key open questions include: whether the convex-extendibility assumption holds for real-world objectives (the paper suggests differences of two DFNs could handle general functions), how training scales to larger instances (current experiments use d≤32, K≤6000), and whether explicit Δ(A) regularization during training can further close the gap between the easy A=I regime and the learned A. The evaluation is limited to synthetic datasets and a single hardware platform, so real-world validation remains to be done.
