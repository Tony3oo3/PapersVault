## Problem

Existing greenlist watermark detectors rely on a single global z-score that aggregates all token-level evidence into one statistic, making them vulnerable to paraphrasing attacks that non-uniformly dilute the signal and to short texts where the aggregate lacks sufficient evidence. The paper addresses two open gaps: robustness under multi-step paraphrasing (up to 8 rounds) and stable detection on passages as short as 300 tokens, where prior methods and deep-learning detectors collapse to near-random AUC.

## Input / Output

At test time the detector receives a single text passage at an unknown paraphrase depth, the watermark key and partition parameters (γ, δ), and generates subsequent paraphrases up to depth K to compute stability. It reconstructs a binary green/red indicator sequence via the keyed hash, slides a window (w=50, stride=10) to extract local statistics, and produces a 20-dimensional static feature vector augmented with per-window PSS values. The final output is a binary watermarked/human decision with a posterior probability from an XGBoost classifier, evaluated via AUC, precision, recall, and F1 at fixed FPR thresholds (1% and 5%).

## Method

The Pattern Stability Score (PSS) framework fuses two complementary signals: (i) local rolling-window statistics that preserve spatial structure of watermark evidence, and (ii) a stability functional measuring cross-depth persistence of that evidence. For each window, the method computes six summary statistics of the local z-score, lag-1 and lag-2 autocorrelations, six moments of the longest consecutive green-run length, and six moments of its frequency, yielding a compact 20-dimensional static feature set. PSS is then computed as the per-window standard deviation of aligned local z-scores across all subsequent paraphrase depths (Dj through DK), capturing whether local watermark evidence persists or fluctuates randomly. The intuition is that watermarked text exhibits low PSS (stable local bias across rewrites) while human text exhibits high PSS (no underlying signal to maintain consistency). These hybrid features are fed to a lightweight XGBoost classifier trained with a 70/30 stratified split, requiring no changes to the generation pipeline and operating solely on token identities rather than model logits.

## Results

On PG-19 at 1,500 tokens, PSS+Static achieves 96.1% AUC at D1 and maintains 91.2% AUC at D8 (eight rounds of paraphrasing), outperforming the global z-score baseline (66.8% at D8) by 24.4 percentage points and WinMax (72.3% at D8) by 18.9 percentage points. Deep-learning detectors (DeepTextMark, Binoculars, RADAR) collapse to 41–44% AUC at D8, a 30–50 point gap below PSS+Static. A single universal classifier trained on one configuration (Llama-3 + Mistral + PG-19 + D1–D3) generalizes to unseen LLMs, paraphrasers, and domains, retaining 87.8% AUC even when all components differ from training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PSS+Static decisively outperforms all five baselines across every paraphrase depth. Against the global z-score threshold, it gains 22–24 percentage points in AUC (e.g., 91.2% vs. 66.8% at D8), confirming that spatial aggregation destroys detectable watermark structure. WinMax, which captures locality via a single maximum local z-score, improves over the global baseline but still trails PSS+Static by 14–19 points because it discards the full distribution of window statistics and cross-depth stability. The three deep-learning detectors (DeepTextMark, Binoculars, RADAR) start competitive at D0 (91–96% AUC) but degrade catastrophically under paraphrasing, falling to 41–44% at D8—a 30–50 point gap—because their learned features overfit to surface token statistics that paraphrasing erases. In cross-domain transfer (e.g., trained on PG-19, tested on CNN/DailyMail), PSS+Static retains 84–89% AUC while all three deep-learning methods drop to 29–46%, near or below random chance. Under the DPO-optimized adaptive attack of Diaa et al. (2025), the global z-score collapses to ~52% AUC while PSS+Static holds 83.2%, a 30+ point advantage, because the attack targets the aggregate statistic rather than the local distributional patterns and cross-depth stability that PSS exploits.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the greenlist watermarking framework of Kirchenbauer et al. (2023), which defines the keyed hash partition and global z-score test that the entire detection pipeline reconstructs. It departs from the dominant research direction of modifying the generator (adaptive schemes, semantic watermarks, multi-bit codes) to instead improve the detector side, arguing that local spatial structure and cross-paraphrase-depth stability are signals that paraphrasing preserves only imperfectly. Conceptually, it extends WinMax (Kirchenbauer et al., 2024), which uses a single maximum local z-score, by summarizing the full distribution of local statistics across windows and adding a temporal stability dimension (PSS) that no prior detector exploits. The method is positioned as complementary to, rather than a replacement for, generator-side robustness improvements.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: the detector requires no changes to deployed generation pipelines, works retroactively on existing watermarked corpora, and runs in under 3 seconds with 200 MB memory, making it deployable at scale alongside systems like SynthID-Text. The finding that a 20-dimensional statistical feature set with XGBoost outperforms deep learning detectors by 30–50 percentage points under paraphrasing challenges the implicit assumption that more complex architectures are needed for robust detection. Key open questions include whether a fully PSS-adaptive attack (requiring white-box access to the feature design) can close the gap, how the method behaves under cross-lingual attacks and in low-entropy domains like code, and whether the supervised classifier's calibration remains stable under distribution shift beyond the three English prose domains tested.
