## Problem

Sampling from Gibbs distributions with non-smooth, non-weakly-convex difference-of-convex (DC) regularizers (e.g., ℓ₁−ℓ₂, Capped-ℓ₁, PiL, DICNNs) lacks Langevin-type convergence guarantees. Prior proximal MCMC results (Renaud et al., 2025a) require the regularizer to be weakly convex, and directly applying a Moreau envelope to the entire DC term is ill-posed (multi-valued, discontinuous). This paper closes the gap by providing the first q-Wasserstein convergence analysis for a Langevin sampler targeting non-weakly-convex DC potentials under distant dissipativity.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a smooth data-fidelity gradient ∇f, two convex DC components r₁ and r₂ (whose proximal operators must be evaluable, either in closed form or via fixed-point iteration), a step size γ, and a smoothing parameter λ. In the CT experiment, inputs are 512×512 parallel-beam projections (350 angles, 0°–120°) and pretrained DICNN parameters; outputs are 100 posterior samples from which a pixel-wise posterior mean and variance image are computed. In the synthetic 2D experiment, outputs are sample histograms compared against the analytically computed target density via binned KL divergence.

## Method

DC-LA is a forward-backward Langevin scheme whose splitting follows DC programming: the forward (gradient) step applies to f − r₂^λ while the backward (proximal) step applies to r₁^λ, where r₁^λ and r₂^λ are Moreau envelopes of the two convex DC components. This per-component smoothing avoids the ill-posedness of smoothing the full DC function r = r₁ − r₂. The unrolled iteration decomposes into elementary operators—∇f, Prox_{λr₂}, and Prox_{(γ+λ)r₁}—making it implementable whenever these proximal maps are available. Under distant dissipativity of V and Lipschitz continuity of r₁, the q-Wasserstein distance to the target π is bounded by O(ρ^{kγ}) + O(γ^{1/(2q)}) + O(λ^{1/q}) for all q ∈ ℕ*, with the smoothing error O(λ^{1/q}) vanishing as λ→0. A simplified variant DC-LA-S drops the r₂ smoothing when r₂ is already smooth (e.g., MCP, SCAD, Geman penalty), reducing per-iteration cost.

## Results

In the CT reconstruction experiment (512×512 Mayo Clinic scans), DC-LA's posterior mean achieves SSIM 0.8493 and PSNR 26.2303, outperforming the PSM MAP estimate at the same 2,000-iteration budget (SSIM 0.7586, PSNR 24.2803). In the synthetic 2D ℓ₁−ℓ₂ sampling experiment, DC-LA achieves the lowest binned KL divergence across all bin resolutions compared to ULA, Moreau ULA, and PSGLA. Theoretically, DC-LA converges to the target in q-Wasserstein distance at rate O(ρ^{kγ}) + O(γ^{1/(2q)}) + O(λ^{1/q}) for all q ∈ ℕ*, under distant dissipativity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the synthetic ℓ₁−ℓ₂ experiment, DC-LA achieves the lowest binned KL divergence across all bin resolutions, while ULA and Moreau ULA produce blurry samples and PSGLA (which lacks convergence guarantees for non-weakly-convex regularizers) overemphasizes coordinate axes. In the CT experiment, DC-LA at 2,000 iterations substantially outperforms PSM at the same budget (SSIM 0.8493 vs. 0.7586; PSNR 26.23 vs. 24.28), roughly matches PSM at 10,000 iterations (SSIM 0.8493 vs. 0.8488), and is slightly surpassed by PSM at 20,000 iterations (SSIM 0.8493 vs. 0.8501). The key practical advantage is that DC-LA provides full posterior samples (enabling uncertainty quantification via pixel-wise variance) at a fraction of the compute cost of running PSM to convergence, whereas PSM only yields a single MAP point estimate.

## Relation to Prior Work

DC-LA extends the forward-backward Langevin sampling framework of Renaud et al. (2025a), which established Wasserstein convergence for weakly convex regularizers under distant dissipativity, to the broader class of general DC regularizers that need not be weakly convex (e.g., ℓ1−ℓ2, DICNNs). The forward-backward splitting principle is inherited from DC programming (Pham Dinh & Le Thi, 1997), and the difference-of-Moreau-envelopes smoothing technique is adapted from the optimization literature (Sun & Sun, 2023). Notably, the paper demonstrates in Appendix E that a key convexity-at-infinity assumption in Renaud et al. (2025a) is actually infeasible, motivating the more general DC framework adopted here.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide q-Wasserstein convergence guarantees for Langevin-type sampling with non-weakly-convex DC regularizers, a class that includes widely used priors in compressed sensing and image reconstruction (ℓ1−ℓ2, Capped-ℓ1, PiL, DICNNs) for which no prior theoretical guarantees existed. The CT experiment demonstrates that DC-LA produces posterior variance maps offering uncertainty quantification that complements MAP estimation, though the evaluation is limited to a single dataset with 100 chains. Open questions include extending the analysis to inexact proximal operators (relevant for neural-network-based regularizers), relaxing the Lipschitz requirement on r1, and exploring downstream uses of the variance images in clinical or decision-making pipelines.
