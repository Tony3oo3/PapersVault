## Problem

Existing graph coarsening methods preserve spectral or spatial properties but fail to maintain topological features (connectivity, rings, voids) that are critical for GNN performance. The only topology-preserving method, GEC, relies on clique enumeration with exponential worst-case complexity O(3^{n/3}), rendering it unscalable for large graphs. This paper asks how to rigorously preserve graph topology during coarsening while achieving near-linear time complexity.

## Input / Output

The method takes an attributed graph G=(V,E) with d-dimensional node features X∈R^{n×d} and labels Y∈N^n, and produces a coarsened graph G^c with |V^c| << |V| where supernode features are computed as the average of constituent node features and labels as the most frequent label. The coarsening ratio c (|V^c|/|V|) is a user-specified target, and the output graph directly replaces the original in any GNN training pipeline as a plug-and-play preprocessing step without model modification. A DropEdge strategy (10% random edge removal, prioritizing heterophilic edges) is applied post-coarsening to mitigate density increase.

## Method

STPGC extends algebraic topology's strong collapse and edge collapse to graphs via three algorithms: GStrongCollapse removes dominated nodes (where N[u]⊆N[v]), GEdgeCollapse removes dominated edges (where N[x,y]⊆N[v]), and NeighborhoodConing inserts dominated edges between a node's neighbors to create new dominated nodes for further reduction. All three operations preserve homotopy equivalence (Lemma 2.5), guaranteeing identical Betti numbers and topological features in the coarsened graph. The key efficiency insight is replacing GEC's exponential clique enumeration with direct neighborhood-inclusion checks bounded by a degree threshold θ₁, yielding O(mθ₁) and O(mθ₁²) complexities. For GNN training, the paper proves that GStrongCollapse and NeighborhoodConing never increase shortest path distances (preserving receptive fields exactly), while GEdgeCollapse increases them by at most 1. When exact coarsening cannot reach the target ratio, ApproximateCoarsening relaxes the dominance condition to r-relaxed strong collapse (allowing up to r missing neighbors), incrementally increasing r to balance topological fidelity with reduction capability.

## Results

On node classification with GCN, STPGC achieves 82.5% on Cora and 78.8% on DBLP at c=0.1, outperforming the SOTA topology-preserving method GEC (78.0% and 75.0% respectively) by an average of 1.73% across five datasets. In terms of efficiency, STPGC is 15× faster than GEC on DBLP at c=0.1 (6.4s vs 100.4s), 8.7× faster on ogbn-arXiv (286s vs 2475s), and delivers up to 37× speedup on cit-Patent. STPGC strictly preserves Betti numbers during the ExactCoarsening phase, while all non-topology-preserving baselines lose topological features from the start of coarsening.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GEC (Meng et al., 2024), the only other topology-preserving method, STPGC wins on Cora (+4.5% at c=0.1), Citeseer (+4.4% at c=0.1), and DBLP (+3.8% at c=0.1) with GCN, but loses on ogbn-arXiv (63.2% vs 65.1% at c=0.1), which the authors attribute to excessive node reduction disrupting topology. STPGC is dramatically faster: 15× on DBLP and 8.7× on ogbn-arXiv at c=0.1, and up to 37× on cit-Patent, because it avoids GEC's exponential clique enumeration and graph partitioning. Against spectral/spatial baselines (Variation Neighborhoods, Variation Edges, Algebraic JC, Affinity GS, Kron), STPGC consistently leads, with the gap widening at aggressive coarsening (e.g., on Cora c=0.1, STPGC 82.5% vs. best spectral method Aff. GS 75.2%). Against FGC (Kumar et al., 2023) and UGC (Kataria et al., 2024), STPGC achieves higher accuracy on all reported datasets at c=0.1. MPG (Joly & Keriven, 2024) is the closest non-topology competitor on DBLP at c=0.5 (84.5% vs 83.0%), but degrades sharply at c=0.1 (74.3% vs 78.8%), confirming that topology preservation becomes increasingly critical under aggressive reduction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the strong collapse and edge collapse operators from algebraic topology (Boissonnat & Pritam, 2019; 2020; Barmak & Minian, 2012) from simplicial complexes to the graph domain, introducing graph strong collapse and graph edge collapse as scalable alternatives to clique-based elementary collapse. It directly addresses the scalability bottleneck of the only prior topology-preserving coarsening method, GEC (Meng et al., 2024a), which relies on exponential-time clique enumeration and graph partitioning that disrupts global topology. The paper also positions itself against the broader family of spectral and spatial coarsening methods (Loukas, 2019; Huang et al., 2021; Kumar et al., 2023) that preserve eigenstructure or local connectivity but ignore topological invariants like Betti numbers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving near-linear scalability while rigorously preserving homotopy equivalence, STPGC makes topology-preserving coarsening practical for large-scale GNN training (up to 37× faster than GEC), opening the door to topology-aware graph reduction in domains like drug discovery and biological network analysis. The receptive-field preservation guarantee provides a clean theoretical justification for why topology-preserving coarsening maintains GNN performance, complementing the empirical evidence. Limitations include heuristic feature averaging and majority-vote label aggregation for supernodes, and the inability of the exact phase to reach arbitrary coarsening ratios; the approximate phase trades topological fidelity for flexibility, leaving open the question of how much topology can be sacrificed before GNN performance degrades. Future work on learnable, task-specific aggregation and broader ML applications is identified by the authors.
