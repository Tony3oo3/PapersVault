## Problem

Under the big-world hypothesis, general agents cannot maintain uniform performance across an arbitrarily large goal space, making standard worst-case guarantees uninformative for identifying where an agent's internal world model is actually reliable. The paper asks whether one can structurally certify a general agent for specific tasks by localizing which transitions in its world model are provably accurate, rather than requiring global near-optimality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263041/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The certification algorithm consumes a deterministic goal-conditioned policy π, a target transition (s,a,s'), a horizon n, a failure rate δ∈[0,0.5), an anchor transition probability P_{ss'}(a) (used only for the supervised filtering step), and an alternative action b≠a. It outputs a binary certificate flag Cert(s,a,s') and, when certified, an unsupervised transition probability estimate P̂_{ss'}(a) derived purely from the agent's behavioral responses to constructed LTL probe goals of depth 2n+1. The anchor P_{ss'}(a) is an external reference that validates the filter but is not used in the model recovery step itself.

## Method

The core mechanism constructs a family of LTL probe goals ψ_{a,b}(r,n) that isolate a single transition (s,a,s') by requiring exactly r occurrences of s→a→s' out of n repeated trials, then queries the agent's preferred first action on competing disjunctive goals to detect a switching threshold. This threshold localizes P_{ss'}(a) between adjacent grid points on [0,1] partitioned into n+1 levels, yielding an error bound of O(1/n)+O(δ) that is strictly tighter than the O(1/√n) universal bound of Richens et al. (2025). The paper first proves an impossibility result (Proposition 3.1) establishing that no non-optimal agent can satisfy a uniform failure rate over arbitrarily deep composite goals, then provides constructive O(n)-time filtering algorithms (Algorithms 2 and 3) for both non-trivial P_{ss'}(a)∈(0,1) and trivial P_{ss'}(a)∈{0,1} regimes, and finally proves a matching lower bound (Theorem 3.3) showing that without such certification, any behavior-rationalizing model must incur a non-trivial transition-level mismatch of order (γ−δ)/n.

## Results

The paper's headline metric is the tightness gap (theoretical upper bound minus empirical recovery error), where lower is better. At n=100 with δ=0.01, the proposed structural certification achieves a gap of 0.35% versus 5.31% for Richens et al. (2025), a roughly 15× improvement. The certified bound scales as O(1/n) + O(δ), decaying linearly with goal depth, while the baseline's O(1/√n) bound remains significantly looser especially at small n. In the maze case study (|S|=625), certified transitions enable near-optimal key-door navigation while uncertified transitions produce erratic, looping behavior.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is Richens et al. (2025), which provides a global O(1/√n) upper bound on world-model recovery error under uniform goal-conditioned performance. The proposed structural certification strictly dominates this baseline on the tightness gap metric across every (δ, n) pair tested: at n=100, δ=0.01 the gap is 0.35% vs. 5.31% (~15× tighter), and even at the largest tested depth n=400 the advantage persists (0.16% vs. 2.68%). The advantage is most pronounced at small n and small δ, where the baseline's O(1/√n) rate has not yet converged. The paper also demonstrates qualitatively that uncertified transitions (those failing the filter) retain high, n-independent error, confirming that the certification mechanism successfully separates reliable from unreliable dynamics rather than merely tightening a global bound.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work by Richens & Everitt (2024) and Richens et al. (2025), who proved that globally near-optimal goal-conditioned agents must possess an internal world model recoverable from behavior. The key departure is a shift from universal (worst-case over all goals) guarantees to transition-local structural certification: the authors first prove an impossibility result showing no universal agent can maintain uniform performance over arbitrarily deep composite goals, then construct filtering algorithms using LTL probe goals that isolate individual transitions and certify entry-wise accuracy at O(1/n) rate rather than the O(1/√n) rate of prior global analysis. The framework also draws on controlled Markov process theory and temporal-logic goal specification, positioning itself at the intersection of model-based RL, formal verification, and the 'big-world hypothesis' from Javed & Sutton (2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled mechanism for scoped deployment of general agents: rather than requiring unverifiable global guarantees, one can certify specific bottleneck transitions where the agent's predictive model is provably accurate, enabling safe compositional planning. Key limitations include the requirement for supervised access to P_{ss'}(a) during filtering, restriction to deterministic policies, and experiments limited to small grid/maze environments (|S|≤625). Open questions the authors identify include extending to stochastic policies, developing purely behavioral (unsupervised) certification criteria, and multi-agent information exchange protocols for complementary partial knowledge. The framework could inform AI safety pipelines by providing a conservative, auditable envelope for agent deployment.
