## Problem

Generating synthetic survival data requires faithfully reproducing both covariate distributions and the joint time-to-event/censoring structure, yet no end-to-end diffusion model exists for this task. Existing survival-specific methods (SurvivalGAN, Ashhad) decompose generation into separate covariate and event-time stages, making them prone to mode collapse and error propagation, while generic tabular diffusion models ignore the censoring mechanism entirely.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260341/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a survival dataset of n samples, each containing continuous covariates (standardized), discrete covariates (one-hot encoded with an additional mask state per feature), a binary event indicator, and a non-negative observed time (event or censoring time). It produces synthetic samples of identical structure by jointly denoising all components in a single reverse diffusion pass, with administrative censoring optionally applied post hoc to match study-specific follow-up horizons.

## Method

SURVDIFF combines a variance-exploding Gaussian diffusion for continuous variables (covariates + event time) with a masked-diffusion process for discrete variables (one-hot covariates + event indicator), following the TabDiff architecture. The key novelty is a survival-tailored loss: a Cox partial negative log-likelihood computed over a survival head (MLP) that maps denoised covariates to risk scores, augmented with sparsity-aware exponential downweighting that reduces the contribution of late events with small risk sets. The total objective jointly optimizes the diffusion reconstruction loss and the survival loss end-to-end, with the survival weight λ_surv adaptively calibrated during a short warm-up to maintain a target fraction of the total gradient signal. This design ensures the model simultaneously preserves covariate fidelity, event-time distributions, and the censoring mechanism without multi-stage decomposition.

## Results

On the AIDS dataset, SURVDIFF achieves a C-Index of 0.7017 (exceeding the real-data baseline of 0.6844) and a Brier Score of 0.0522, outperforming all baselines. On GBSG2, SURVDIFF reaches a C-Index of 0.6613 (vs. real-data 0.6592) and Brier Score of 0.2036, again leading all methods. On METABRIC, SURVDIFF is competitive (C-Index 0.5992) but slightly trails TabDiff (0.6078). For covariate fidelity, SURVDIFF achieves the lowest marginal JS distance across all three datasets (0.0059 on AIDS, 0.0074 on GBSG2, 0.0062 on METABRIC).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SURVDIFF consistently outperforms the survival-specific baselines SurvivalGAN and Ashhad across all three datasets and both metrics; for example, on GBSG2 it improves C-Index from 0.6357 (SurvivalGAN) and 0.5062 (Ashhad) to 0.6613. Against the strongest generic tabular baseline TabDiff, SURVDIFF wins on AIDS (C-Index 0.7017 vs. 0.6572) and GBSG2 (0.6613 vs. 0.6286), but trails slightly on METABRIC (0.5992 vs. 0.6078), where TabDiff's generic tabular strength is more pronounced. For covariate fidelity (JS distance), SURVDIFF achieves the best values on all datasets (e.g., 0.0059 on AIDS vs. 0.0085 for TabDiff and 0.0135 for SurvivalGAN), while on joint Wasserstein distance the comparison is more mixed, with TVAE and TabDiff occasionally edging ahead on METABRIC. The older tabular baselines (NFlow, TVAE, CTGAN) are uniformly outperformed by SURVDIFF on both fidelity and downstream survival metrics. The paper notes that SURVDIFF's advantages are most pronounced on datasets with stronger right-censoring (AIDS at 91.7% censored, GBSG2 at 56.4%), where preserving the censoring mechanism is most critical.

## Relation to Prior Work

SURVDIFF builds directly on the TabDiff architecture (Shi et al., 2025a) for mixed-type diffusion—variance-exploding SDEs for continuous variables and masked diffusion for discrete ones—and on the Cox partial likelihood (Cox, 1972; Katzman et al., 2018) as the survival-specific training signal. It departs from the two prior survival-data generators, SurvivalGAN (Norcliffe et al., 2023) and Ashhad (Ashhad & Henao, 2025), which decompose generation into separate multi-stage pipelines for covariates and event-time/censoring, by unifying all components into a single end-to-end diffusion model with a sparsity-aware survival loss. The key conceptual departure is treating the event indicator and event time as first-class variables within the diffusion process rather than as outputs of a separate conditional model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SURVDIFF addresses a practically critical gap: synthetic survival data that faithfully preserves both covariate distributions and the censoring mechanism, which is essential for privacy-preserving clinical research and for augmenting small medical cohorts. The paper is scoped to single-endpoint right-censoring, and extending to competing risks, left-truncation, or interval censoring remains open. The differential-privacy extension (DP-SURVDIFF) is notable as the first DP method for synthetic survival data, but the privacy budget (ε=8) is relatively loose, and the DP results are reported on only two datasets. The more mixed results on METABRIC (where TabDiff remains competitive on downstream metrics) suggest that the survival-tailored loss provides the largest gains under strong censoring, which is the regime most common in clinical trials.
