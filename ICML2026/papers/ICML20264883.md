## Problem

Existing methods for sequential counterfactual target achievement (SCTA) rely on offline planning that pre-computes fixed intervention sequences via costly optimization, making them brittle to environmental stochasticity and unsuitable for real-time adaptive deployment. Learning a goal-conditioned online policy from observational clinical data is further complicated by three intertwined challenges: confounding in treatment assignment, distributional shift between behavior and learned policies, and sparse success signals in the data. No prior work formulates SCTA as an offline goal-conditioned MDP with a reactive policy learned directly from confounded observational trajectories.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a variable-length patient history sequence (time-varying covariates, past interventions, past outcomes, and static covariates repeated along time) together with a user-specified target outcome vector, encoded via an LSTM history encoder and a small MLP goal encoder fused into a composite state vector. At each decision step, the policy outputs a continuous intervention vector in (0,1)^d (e.g., dosage levels), rolled out autoregressively in closed-loop until the target region is reached or the horizon expires. The reward is sparse: 0 upon first entry into the target region, −1 at every other step, making the signal extremely sparse in observational data where successful trajectories are rare.

## Method

GIFT (Goal-conditioned Intervention via Factual-Targeted Training) learns a goal-conditioned SAC policy from fixed offline observational data through two core mechanisms. First, Hindsight Experience Replay relabels achieved future states as virtual goals, converting failed trajectories into successful learning samples to alleviate reward sparsity. Second, a clipped importance-weight reward rescaling multiplies each immediate reward by the ratio π_θ(a|ψ)/π_b(a|ψ) clipped to [ε₁, ε₂], skewing Bellman update targets toward the actor's action distribution while bounding variance from extreme density ratios. The paper proves the resulting modified soft Bellman operator is a γ-contraction with a unique fixed point, and derives an explicit upper bound on the gap between the learned Q-value and the true policy Q-value in terms of the operator difference. Causal identifiability of the Bellman operator from observational data is established via do-calculus under standard sequential ignorability, consistency, and positivity assumptions.

## Results

On the MIMIC-III semi-synthetic dataset (identical intervention strategies), GIFT achieves the lowest terminal-outcome RMSE across all horizons, reaching 0.39±0.18 at τ=6 compared to the next-best baselines SCRL (0.65±0.30) and VCIP (0.67±0.22). On the Tumor dataset under unseen intervention strategies (κ=4), GIFT achieves 0.46±0.08 RMSE at τ=6, substantially outperforming VCIP (1.10±0.20) and RMSN (1.15±0.21). GIFT also demonstrates the lowest variance across all settings, with standard deviations roughly 2–3× smaller than baselines, confirming the variance-reducing effect of clipped reward rescaling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GIFT outperforms all six baselines (RMSN, CRN, CT, ACTIN, VCIP, SCRL) on both the MIMIC-III and Tumor datasets across every horizon τ∈{1,…,6}. The gap is most pronounced at longer horizons: on MIMIC-III at τ=6, GIFT (0.39) beats the closest competitor SCRL (0.65) by 40% and CT (2.20) by 82%. On the Tumor dataset with unseen test-time strategies (κ=4), GIFT (0.46 at τ=6) nearly halves the error of the best baseline VCIP (1.10), demonstrating strong generalization beyond the training distribution. Among baselines, VCIP and SCRL are the strongest competitors, while CT and ACTIN degrade most severely with increasing horizon due to accumulated prediction errors in their learn-then-plan paradigm. GIFT also exhibits consistently lower standard deviations (e.g., 0.18 vs. 0.30 for SCRL at τ=6 on MIMIC), confirming that clipped reward rescaling reduces training variance without sacrificing accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

GIFT departs from the dominant 'learn-then-plan' paradigm for Sequential Counterfactual Target Achievement (SCTA), where a counterfactual world model (RMSN, CRN, Causal Transformer, ACTIN, VCIP) is trained and then a costly open-loop optimization searches for a fixed intervention sequence at inference time. Instead, it reformulates SCTA as an offline goal-conditioned MDP and learns a closed-loop reactive policy via SAC, borrowing Hindsight Experience Replay (HER) from robotics GCRL to address sparse rewards and introducing a clipped importance-sampling reward rescaling mechanism to handle distribution shift under confounded observational data. The theoretical contribution—a contraction proof for the modified soft Bellman operator and an explicit bias bound from clipping—extends standard offline RL analysis to the confounded, goal-conditioned setting, which prior GCRL work (SCRL, C-Learning, HIQL) does not address because it assumes unconfounded or simulator-generated data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The shift from open-loop planning to a learned reactive policy eliminates the expensive iterative optimization at inference time, making real-time clinical decision support computationally feasible, and the theoretical guarantees (unique fixed point, bounded approximation error) provide a principled foundation for offline policy learning under confounding. However, all experiments are on synthetic or semi-synthetic simulators with low-dimensional targets, the clipping interval remains a manual tuning hyperparameter without adaptive selection, and the standard causal assumptions (measured confounding, positivity, goal-independent dynamics) may be violated in real EHR data. Open questions include extending to unobserved confounding via proxy or instrumental-variable methods, adaptive clipping with certified offline policy evaluation, and prospective clinical validation before any deployment.
