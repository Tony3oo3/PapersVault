## Problem

For a fixed self-attention budget (total key dimension D_K = h·d_k), how many distinct token-token relations can a single layer reliably encode and recover? While mechanistic interpretability identifies individual attention circuits, it has not quantified how many independent circuits can coexist or the interference that limits their coexistence. The paper also asks whether multi-head attention provides a capacity advantage even in the simplest case where each query attends to only a single target, beyond the conventional 'parallel attention to multiple targets' rationale.

## Input / Output

The model receives a context of ℓ distinct vertex embeddings (frozen, L2-normalized Gaussian vectors in R^{d_model}) and must recover, for every source in the context, exactly which in-context targets it should attend to, as defined by a fixed directed graph G=(V,E) over the full vocabulary of m items. The output is a binary edge decision for every ordered pair within the context (via a learned global threshold τ on aggregated QK scores), or in the value-retrieval variant, a continuous neighbor message vector. Crucially, the same parameters must work correctly for all possible contexts of any length, not just the training distribution.

## Method

The paper introduces Relational Graph Recognition (RGR), casting self-attention as recovering edges of a relational graph, and analyzes two QK variants: a tractable max-over-heads aggregator and standard scaled-softmax. The core construction for the compressive regime (d_model ≪ m) first approximately de-embeds each source via X^T, then applies random Rademacher signatures in the one-hot space, partitioning sources across h ≈ m/d_model heads to keep per-head block size small and control superposition-induced interference noise. This yields matching information-theoretic lower bounds (D_K = Ω(m'/d_model · log(m²/m'))) and constructive upper bounds (D_K = O(m'/d_model · log m) for sparse graphs), with the key insight that multiple heads reduce noise by localizing de-embedding leakage rather than increasing per-head expressivity. Empirical validation across permutation graphs, r-regular graphs, softmax aggregation, value retrieval, and a full pre-LN Transformer block with frozen GPT-2 embeddings confirms sharp capacity phase transitions at the predicted D_K threshold and a persistent multi-head advantage even when no query ever needs to attend to multiple positions.

## Results

The paper validates its theoretical capacity law D_K* = Θ(m log m / d_model) empirically: plotting the minimum total key dimension D_K* against m log m / d_model yields a tight linear fit with slope 1.19 and R² = 0.944 (improving to R² = 0.992 when excluding points violating the d_model ≳ c₀ log m precondition). The optimal head count scales linearly with compression ratio, h* ≈ 1.65·(m/d_model) − 6.64 (R² = 0.824), with per-head widths at the threshold modest (d_k* ∈ [5, 24], median 11). For r-regular graphs, all densities collapse onto a single line D_K* ≈ 0.46·(rm/d_model)·log₂m, confirming that edge count—not vertex count—governs capacity. In a full single-layer Transformer block with frozen GPT-2 embeddings (m=6144, d_model=768), multi-head models (h∈{4,8}) approach 0.98 accuracy by D_K=512, while single-head models either collapse to the NULL baseline (accuracy ≈ 0) or saturate near 0.81.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is single-head versus multi-head attention at fixed total key dimension D_K, rather than comparison against external methods. In the compressive regime (m ≫ d_model), single-head models frequently fail to reach 0.99 F1 within the scanned D_K range (e.g., at (m, d_model) = (512, 64) and (256, 32)), while multi-head configurations pass at substantially smaller D_K—demonstrating a capacity advantage from noise reduction via smaller per-head block sizes. Replacing the max-over-heads aggregator with standard scaled-softmax (sum-over-heads) preserves the sharp F1 transition and multi-head advantage, though the threshold shifts right by the extra logarithmic factors predicted by the softmax construction (Thm. C.1). Adding a value channel and switching from binary edge classification to message retrieval (MSE) does not change the qualitative picture: single-head still fails in the compressed regime while multi-head succeeds. Finally, in the most realistic setting—a full pre-LN Transformer block with frozen GPT-2-small embeddings and an induction-style retrieval task that never requires attending to multiple positions—the multi-head advantage persists at every fixed D_K, with the best partitions using h ∈ {4, 8}, while single-head exhibits a bimodal failure mode (collapse to NULL or saturation at ~0.81) for D_K ≥ 256.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by introducing Relational Graph Recognition (RGR) as a task that isolates the key-query channel of self-attention, explicitly distinguishing it from memorization capacity (which maps a context to a single output) and from expressivity/universality results (which ask what is computable with unbounded resources). It draws on ideas from coding theory (random Rademacher signatures, approximate de-embedding via the embedding matrix transpose) and information-theoretic counting to derive matching lower and upper bounds on the total key dimension D_K = h·d_k needed to recover a graph's edges. The multi-head advantage it identifies—noise reduction from embedding superposition rather than parallel attention to multiple targets—complements and partially reinterprets findings from head-pruning/specialization studies (Voita et al., 2019; Michel et al., 2019) and the associative-memory perspective of Zhong et al. (2025), which also hypothesized interference but did not quantify it or test single-target scenarios.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a quantitative, falsifiable capacity law (D_K ∝ m′/d_model · log m) and a principled, capacity-based rationale for why LLMs use many small heads, explaining architectural choices (GPT-3's 96 heads, MQA/GQA tradeoffs, head pruning) through a single interference-reduction mechanism. Key limitations include the single-layer focus, controlled (Gaussian or frozen GPT-2) embeddings, and the primary use of a max-over-heads surrogate for softmax; open questions include whether the capacity law holds in multi-layer iterative routing, with learned embeddings, and in the very dense or highly degree-imbalanced graph regimes where the constructive bounds have gaps. The work opens a path toward resource-aware attention design where the key-query budget is allocated explicitly based on the number of relationships a layer must support.
