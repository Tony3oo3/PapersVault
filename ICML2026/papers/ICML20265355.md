## Problem

Existing embedding translation methods are trained and evaluated only in simplified pairwise, in-domain settings and operate as black boxes at inference time, causing catastrophic recall drops under out-of-distribution inputs, multi-model mixing, and multi-hop chaining. No prior work provides an interpretable explanation for why translation errors amplify under these practical deployment scenarios or a pre-translation reliability signal to flag risky inputs.

## Input / Output

The method takes pre-computed source embedding vectors (e.g., 3072-dim gemini or 4096-dim qwen vectors) and a training reference pool of paired source-target embeddings, and produces translated vectors in the target model's space along with a scalar translation confidence score TC(x) ∈ (0,1] computed purely from source-space geometry before any translation occurs. The confidence score requires only a nearest-neighbor index over the training reference set (e.g., FAISS) and a cached scale parameter, enabling real-time abstention decisions without access to target-side labels. The hierarchical router additionally consumes a binary tree of cluster centroids built offline over the source training embeddings.

## Method

The paper derives a pointwise Lipschitz-based error bound e(x) ≤ (L+L*)δ(x) + e(x_nn) that decomposes translation error into a local training-fit term and a coverage term growing with distance-to-reference, explaining systematic OOD failure. Guided by this bound, H-MoE partitions the source embedding space via hierarchical clustering into a binary tree (K leaves, 2K−1 nodes) and trains one expert per node as a shared 4-layer MLP backbone with SELU activations plus a node-specific LoRA adapter (rank 8), so additional capacity comes from localized low-rank updates rather than full per-expert networks. At inference, a top-down geometric router descends the tree while the query is unambiguously closer to one child centroid (ratio ρ ≤ τ) and backs off to a coarser expert at ambiguous boundaries, ensuring the serving expert's training support actually covers the query. The training objective combines pointwise regression, a local structure-preserving loss over 100 nearest neighbors, and a directional residual regularizer that concentrates mixing residuals along PC1 and chaining residuals along orthogonal PC2 to prevent constructive error accumulation across composed translations.

## Results

Across 10 embedding models, 6 BEIR datasets, and 90 directed translation directions, H-MoE achieves the highest Recall@100 in every OOD pairwise translation setting, outperforming the strongest baseline (EmbConv) by an average of 14.9% (e.g., 92.4 vs. 79.6 on ArguANA for gemini→openai). Under multi-model mixing, H-MoE degrades by only 0.5–2.6% Recall@100 versus up to 36.6% for baselines; under two-hop chaining, degradation is limited to ~3% versus up to 36.8% for baselines. Inference throughput is 1.33–1.39 M vectors/s on a single A100 GPU, translating 1M vectors in under 1 second with only ~2× the parameters of the monolithic EmbConv baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

H-MoE outperforms all five baselines (GW, PA, CCA, Vec2Vec, EmbConv) across all 90 translation directions and all three OOD training datasets. Against the strongest baseline EmbConv, H-MoE gains +12.0% to +14.9% Recall@100 on average, with the largest gaps on harder directions (e.g., +23.4 on e5→sfr: 89.7 vs. 66.3). The linear baselines PA and GW fail catastrophically under OOD shift, scoring near 0–22% Recall@100, confirming that rigid global mappings cannot accommodate the nonlinear anisotropy of LLM embedding spaces. CCA achieves moderate performance (45–67%) but is highly inconsistent across directions. Vec2Vec, despite its adversarial training, exhibits high variance (std > 10 in several settings) and remains 30–50 points below H-MoE. In multi-model settings, the gap widens further: EmbConv suffers a 21.1% chaining drop while H-MoE stays within 3%, and mixing degradation is 0.5–2.6% for H-MoE versus up to 36.6% for baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the embedding translation line from cross-lingual alignment (PA, CCA, GW) and recent neural translators (EmbConv, Vec2Vec) by deliberately moving beyond the simplified pairwise, in-domain regime. It builds directly on EmbConv's MLP backbone and local similarity loss but departs by (i) deriving a Lipschitz-based pointwise error bound that explains OOD failure, (ii) replacing the single global translator with a hierarchical mixture of LoRA-adapted local experts routed by source-space geometry, and (iii) introducing directional residual regularization (along PCA axes) to make multi-model mixing and chaining compositionally stable. The translation confidence metric is a novel translator-independent diagnostic that has no direct analogue in prior embedding-translation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Embedding model upgrades and multi-tenant vector databases make cross-model translation a practical necessity, and this paper shows that the standard in-domain evaluation severely overstates real-world reliability: baselines can lose up to 92% recall under chaining, while H-MoE limits degradation to under 3%. The geometric error bound and confidence score give practitioners a principled way to decide when to translate versus re-embed, which is operationally important for staged database migrations. Open questions include whether the approach extends to longer-than-two-hop chains, multimodal embeddings, and end-to-end RAG pipelines where generator and reranker effects compound translation residuals.
