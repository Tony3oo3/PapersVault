## Problem

Training GFlowNets from offline data typically requires a proxy reward model to provide feedback on online-sampled trajectories, but constructing reliable proxies is costly and error-prone, especially for out-of-distribution samples. Existing proxy-free alternatives (e.g., RO-GFlowNets, COFlowNet) impose coarse distributional constraints that restrict exploration and limit generalization. The paper asks how to extract rich, transition-level structural guidance from offline trajectories to guide GFlowNet training without proxy models or overly restrictive policy constraints.

## Input / Output

The method takes an offline dataset of M trajectories (each a sequence of DAG states from root to a terminal node) paired with ground-truth terminal rewards, along with the full environment DAG structure G=(V,E). It produces a trained GFlowNet forward policy P_F that samples compositional objects (molecules, peptide sequences, grid states) in proportion to their rewards. A key design constraint is that gradient updates use exclusively the recorded terminal rewards from the dataset; the IRL-learned edge rewards influence training only through DAG pruning and backward sampling, never entering the loss function directly.

## Method

TD-GFN operates in three phases. First, it applies GAIL-style adversarial inverse reinforcement learning on a reward-rebalanced dataset (trajectories sampled with probability proportional to terminal reward) to extract an edge-level reward function R_E(s,s') that quantifies the importance of each DAG transition; theoretically, R_E recovers the log conditional incoming-flow score (i.e., the canonical backward policy) in the population limit. Second, it prunes the environment DAG by removing edges whose R_E falls below a statistical threshold (mean − K·std), yielding a compact subgraph that retains high-utility paths while eliminating low-value transitions. Third, it trains the GFlowNet policy via prioritized backward sampling on the pruned graph: terminal states are drawn proportional to their ground-truth rewards, and backward paths are constructed using a softmax backward policy over R_E, so that sampling effort is allocated toward high-reward regions. This indirect use of edge rewards—guiding exploration through pruning and sampling while keeping gradients dependent solely on ground-truth terminal values—prevents the error propagation that plagues proxy-based methods and enables efficient discovery of high-reward modes unobserved in the training data.

## Results

On the Molecule Design benchmark, TD-GFN achieves the best top-k rewards among all offline methods: 7.733 (top-10), 7.450 (top-100), and 6.810 (top-1000), matching the Oracle-GFN (7.718/7.408/6.801) that has access to the ground-truth reward oracle. TD-GFN converges in 2.749×10⁴ trajectories, requiring 20× fewer trajectories than Oracle-GFN (44.141×10⁴) and 1.6× fewer than the best proxy-free baseline QM-COFlowNet (4.423×10⁴). On the 8⁴ Hypergrid task, TD-GFN identifies all 16 reward modes with fewer than 5,000 state visits—a six-fold improvement over baselines—and converges to the lowest Empirical L1 Error more than twice as fast as COFlowNet.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TD-GFN decisively outperforms COFlowNet (both FM and QM variants) on all three reward metrics: e.g., top-10 reward of 7.733 vs. 7.611 (QM) and 7.582 (FM), while also converging 1.6× faster (2.749×10⁴ vs. 4.423×10⁴ trajectories). Against offline RL baselines CQL and IQL, TD-GFN achieves substantially higher rewards (7.733 vs. 7.069 and 6.902 on top-10), reflecting the fundamental misalignment between standard RL objectives and GFlowNet's diversity-preserving sampling goal. BraVE converges fastest in trajectory count (0.645×10⁴) but produces significantly lower rewards (7.271 top-10) and fewer diverse high-reward modes, confirming that offline RL alone cannot match GFlowNet-based generation quality. Compared to GAIL (trained on the rebalanced dataset), TD-GFN improves top-10 reward from 7.528 to 7.733 and top-1000 from 6.406 to 6.810, demonstrating that the IRL-derived edge rewards provide meaningful structural guidance beyond simple imitation. On the Hypergrid task, TD-GFN identifies all 16 modes with <5,000 state visits versus >30,000 for COFlowNet, and maintains this advantage under noisy (Mixed) and scarce (150 trajectories) data conditions where baselines degrade significantly.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the GFlowNet-RL equivalence (Tiapkin et al., 2024) to repurpose maximum causal entropy IRL (Ziebart et al., 2008; Ho & Ermon, 2016) for extracting transition-level edge rewards from offline trajectories, then applies them indirectly via DAG pruning and prioritized backward sampling. It departs from prior proxy-free offline GFlowNet methods (RO-GFlowNets, COFlowNet) that impose coarse distribution-matching constraints, and from proxy-based methods that require a separate reward model, by using IRL-derived edge rewards as structural guidance that never enters the gradient. The insight that different DAG edges contribute unequally to policy learning is shared with Silva et al. (2025), but here it is operationalized through IRL rather than reweighted balance conditions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TD-GFN removes the practical bottleneck of constructing reliable proxy reward models for offline GFlowNet training, which is critical in domains like drug discovery and language modeling where reward queries are prohibitively expensive. The theoretical guarantees (unique recovery of the backward policy, robust pruning preservation) provide principled confidence that the indirect guidance mechanism is sound rather than heuristic. Open questions include how the IRL phase scales to very large DAGs, the sensitivity of the pruning threshold K in high-dimensional settings, and whether the framework extends naturally to stochastic or non-acyclic environments. The orthogonality to the specific GFlowNet objective (FM, TB, DB) suggests broad applicability, but validation on truly large-scale real-world tasks beyond the 1,500-trajectory molecule benchmark remains to be seen.
