## Problem

Existing algorithms for estimating forest matrix entries rely on fast Laplacian solvers or the unsigned forest matrix theorem, both of which break down for signed graphs where negative edges invalidate row-stochasticity and the spanning-forest interpretation. This leaves no theoretically guaranteed method to efficiently approximate the forest matrix of signed graphs, despite its centrality in the signed Friedkin-Johnsen opinion dynamics model and node centrality measures.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a directed signed graph G=(V,E,w) with edge weights in {+1,−1}, a sample budget l, and (for opinion estimation) an internal opinion vector s∈[−1,1]^n and a target node index i. It outputs the n-dimensional diagonal vector q=(q_11,…,q_nn)^⊤ of Q=(I+L)^{−1}, where each q_ii is strictly positive, and optionally a scalar expressed-opinion estimate ẑ_i. The graph need not be balanced or strongly connected, and the algorithm scales to graphs exceeding 23 million nodes within roughly one hour.

## Method

The paper introduces the Signed Forest Matrix Theorem, which expresses each entry q_ij as the ratio of total weight of generalized spanning converging forests (spanning subgraphs with out-degree ≤1 per node and only negative cycles) rooted at j for node i, to the total weight of all such forests, with each forest weighted by 2^{n^−(φ)} where n^−(φ) counts non-trivial negative cycles. To sample these forests, the GSCF algorithm extends Wilson's loop-erased random walk by preserving negative cycles upon encounter rather than erasing them, achieving expected O(n) time per forest. Two estimators are then built: FMDE directly counts root occurrences across l sampled forests, while FMDE+ additionally exploits the identity q_ii=(1/(1+d_i))(1+Σ_k q_ik w_ki) to incorporate neighbor information, provably reducing variance in balanced signed graphs. Both run in O(ln) total time, and a downstream FJOE algorithm reuses the pre-sampled forest list to answer expressed-opinion queries in O(l) time per node without resampling.

## Results

FMDE+ achieves an average relative error below 0.01 across all six small signed graphs, approximately 10× better than FMDE, with errors dropping below 0.001 on Wikielec and Wikipedia*. In terms of efficiency, FMDE+ reduces running time from 991.5 s (EXACT, direct matrix inversion) to 1.033 s on SlashdotZoo (79K nodes), a ~1000× speedup, and scales to FullUSA (23.9M nodes) in under 1 hour. The FJOE opinion estimator achieves absolute error under 2×10⁻² at ε=0.1 on all six small graphs, with query time under 3×10⁻⁴ s even on the largest graph.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against other sampling-based forest matrix estimators for signed graphs, as none exist — this is precisely the gap the paper addresses. The primary efficiency baseline is EXACT (direct O(n³) matrix inversion of I+L), which is infeasible beyond ~80K nodes due to memory constraints; FMDE and FMDE+ handle graphs up to 23.9M nodes within one hour. Among the two proposed estimators, FMDE+ consistently outperforms FMDE in accuracy (average relative error <0.01 vs. ~0.1 for FMDE, roughly a 10× improvement) at a modest runtime cost (~1.3–1.5× slower), attributed to its use of the variance-reduced estimator q̃_ii that leverages off-diagonal entries via the identity Q(I+L)=I. The FJOE opinion estimator, built on the same forest sampling, achieves absolute error <2×10⁻² at ε=0.1 with per-query time under 3×10⁻⁴ s, demonstrating that the pre-sampled forest list enables near-instantaneous opinion queries regardless of graph size.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the forest matrix estimation framework from unsigned to signed graphs, where the classical forest matrix theorem (Chebotarev & Shamis) breaks down and existing algorithms (fast Laplacian solvers, Wilson-based sampling) become inapplicable. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of 'generalized spanning converging forests' that permit negative cycles, paired with a modified loop-erased random walk (GSCF) that generalizes Wilson's algorithm. The variance-reduction estimator FMDE+ directly extends the technique from Sun & Zhang (2024), with the unsigned case recovered as a special case of balanced signed graphs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The forest matrix is the fundamental matrix of the signed Friedkin-Johnsen opinion dynamics model, where diagonal entries determine how much weight each agent retains on their initial opinion at equilibrium. This work provides the first theoretically guaranteed sampling-based estimator for signed graphs, filling a gap where all prior methods simply fail rather than degrade. The demonstrated scalability to 23M+ nodes makes it practically relevant for large-scale social network analysis. Open questions include extending the framework to optimization problems over the forest matrix and signed graph embedding, which the authors identify as future directions.
