## Problem

Widely adopted binary segmentation metrics (IoU, Dice, MAE, E-measure) treat pixels as independent statistical elements and cannot distinguish a structurally intact prediction from a severely fragmented one. The most structure-aware metric, S-measure, relies on statistical region comparisons rather than true topological connectivity, making it insensitive to critical structural fractures, background false positives, and small-object miss-detection. No existing metric explicitly quantifies topological consistency as a first-class evaluation dimension.

## Input / Output

The metric consumes a continuous predicted probability map P ∈ [0,1]^(H×W) and a binary ground truth mask G ∈ {0,1}^(H×W), producing a single scalar score T_m ∈ [0,1] along with three decomposable sub-scores (S_pix, I_topo, A_bdy) that enable error attribution. Binarization (threshold τ=0.5) is required only for the topological integrity term; S_pix and A_bdy operate directly on the raw probability map, allowing evaluation of un-thresholded model outputs. The boundary tolerance radius is adaptive, scaling with image diagonal as r = min(max(1, ⌊0.005·√(H²+W²)⌋), 5) to match typical annotation error margins.

## Method

T_m is formulated as S_pix × [α·I_topo + (1−α)·A_bdy] with α=0.8, where the pixel term gates a topological penalty coefficient. S_pix is a Fuzzy Jaccard Index using algebraic product (t-norm) and probabilistic sum (s-norm) to measure soft overlap between the probability map and binary GT. I_topo decomposes the GT into N connected components, then for each component computes a Fragmentation Consistency Ratio (area of largest predicted sub-component divided by total intersection area) weighted by the component's fractional foreground area, directly penalizing structural fractures. A_bdy extracts morphological gradients from both maps, dilates them into adaptive tolerance bands, and computes a bidirectional gradient-energy Precision/Recall combined via harmonic mean to capture local boundary alignment. The decoupled design allows users to inspect I_topo and A_bdy independently for error diagnosis, distinguishing topological failures from boundary noise.

## Results

On the topological fracture experiment (200 COD10K samples), T_m drops 84.3% (0.800 → 0.126) when the ground truth is shattered into fragments, while S_m drops only 11.6% (0.898 → 0.794) and pixel-based metrics (IoU, Dice, Fm, Em) drop 3–23%. On the small-object omission test (153 ISIC2017 samples with empty predictions), T_m correctly scores 0.00 whereas S_m misleadingly assigns 0.482. In the LPIPS perceptual consistency benchmark across four datasets, T_m achieves Spearman's ρ of 0.758–0.779, matching pixel-based MAE (0.770–0.791) while outperforming S_m (0.610–0.751) and 95HD (0.510–0.659). In the human preference study on 50 conflicting pairs, participants favored T_m's judgment 70.7% of the time over S_m's.

## Baselines & Comparisons

T_m dramatically outperforms all eight baselines on its core topological sensitivity test: it penalizes structural fragmentation 7–27× more severely than any existing metric (84.3% drop vs. 3.1% for Fm, 11.6% for S_m, 23.3% for IoU). Notably, 95HD and ASD actually assign *higher* scores to the fractured prediction than to the intact one (Table 1: 95HD drops from 27.00 to 12.65, i.e., a −53.1% 'improvement'), revealing a fundamental design flaw in distance-based metrics. On the LPIPS perceptual consistency benchmark, T_m matches the best pixel-based metrics (MAE, Fβw) within 0.01–0.02 Spearman's ρ across all four datasets, while surpassing S_m by 0.14–0.17 and 95HD by 0.15–0.17. On the small-object omission test, T_m (0.00) is the only metric that correctly assigns a zero score to a completely empty prediction, whereas S_m (0.482) and all other metrics remain above 0.3. In the human preference experiment, T_m wins 70.7% of head-to-head comparisons against S_m on conflicting pairs, confirming superior alignment with human visual judgment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the lineage of binary segmentation evaluation metrics from pixel-wise measures (IoU, Dice, MAE) through enhanced pixel-wise variants (F_β^w, E_m) to structure-aware metrics (S_m, 95HD, ASD), arguing that none of these capture true topological connectivity. The pixel-level component (S_pix) is grounded in the fuzzy intersection-over-union formulation of Csurka et al. (2013), while the topological integrity term introduces connected-component analysis as a scoring mechanism—a concept borrowed from image processing but not previously integrated into a unified segmentation metric. The paper explicitly departs from S_m by demonstrating that its 'structural' perception is fundamentally statistical (region-level luminance comparison) rather than topological, and from boundary-distance metrics (95HD, ASD) by showing they can paradoxically reward structural fractures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that S_m—the dominant structure-aware metric in salient and camouflaged object detection—assigns a 0.794 score to a mask shattered by 40 random cuts (vs. 0.126 for T_m) challenges a widely held assumption that S_m adequately evaluates structural quality, potentially prompting re-evaluation of published results across these communities. The decoupled design (I_topo vs. A_bdy) offers a practical diagnostic tool for model developers to distinguish boundary refinement issues from fundamental semantic failures. Open questions include whether the fixed binarization threshold (τ=0.5) for I_topo generalizes across domains with varying confidence calibration, whether the single-exemplar calibration of α=0.8 holds across diverse object morphologies, and whether topology-aware evaluation extends naturally to multi-class or 3D segmentation settings.
