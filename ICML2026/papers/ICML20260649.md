## Problem

Standard LoRA optimization independently updates its low-rank factors A and B, causing their composite weight update to systematically violate the orthogonality constraint needed to protect previously learned knowledge, leading to catastrophic forgetting. Prior methods enforce orthogonality at a high level without addressing this internal factor-coupling flaw, and naively projecting gradients onto a safe subspace further compromises plasticity by leaving no free representational space for new concepts.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (e.g., 224×224 for ImageNet-R, 32×32 for CIFAR-100) and outputs a class label prediction. It operates in an exemplar-free class-incremental setting: a frozen ViT-B/16 backbone (pre-trained on ImageNet-21K) is adapted via LoRA adapters (rank 10) inserted into Key and Value projection layers of all attention blocks, with no access to past-task data during training. The total loss combines standard cross-entropy with a Decoupled Margin Loss term, and the final model has identical inference FLOPs (33.726 GFLOPs) to the base backbone.

## Method

Janus-LoRA harmonizes parameter-level stability with feature-level plasticity through two coupled components. For stability, an Online Estimation (OE) module maintains an orthonormal basis V on the Stiefel manifold (optimized via projected gradient descent with QR re-orthogonalization) to track the historical activation subspace without storing past data; Gradient Rectification (GR) then projects the raw gradient into a safe direction ΔW_safe = ΔW_orig(I − VVᵀ) and 'reverse-engineers' it into decoupled factor updates via a hierarchical least-squares decomposition—first solving for ΔA_rect given fixed B, then solving for ΔB_rect on the residual—ensuring the composite update faithfully follows the orthogonal geodesic. For plasticity, a Decoupled Margin Loss (DML) combines a contrastive intra-task term that clusters new features around their class prototypes with an inter-task hinge penalty that repels new features from historical prototypes beyond a cosine margin, carving out a dedicated low-interference region for new learning. The total loss L_total = L_task + λ·L_DML is backpropagated, and the resulting gradient passes through the OE→GR pipeline before being applied to the LoRA factors.

## Results

On ImageNet-R (T=10), Janus-LoRA achieves 75.78% ACC, 81.64% MAA, and -6.05 BWT, outperforming the next-best method InfLoRA (73.90% ACC, 80.01% MAA) by ~1.9% in accuracy and ~1.6% in MAA. On the more challenging T=20 setting, the gap widens: Janus-LoRA reaches 77.11% MAA versus InfLoRA's 76.24%, while BiLoRA drops to 68.60% MAA. Across CIFAR-100, ImageNet-100, and DomainNet (Table 3), Janus-LoRA consistently leads in ACC and MAA, e.g., 88.68% ACC on CIFAR-100 vs. 86.69% for InfLoRA, and 73.82% ACC on DomainNet vs. 72.94% for InfLoRA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Janus-LoRA outperforms all seven baselines in ACC and MAA across every dataset and task-sequence length tested. On ImageNet-R (T=10), it leads InfLoRA by +1.88% ACC and +1.63% MAA, BiLoRA by +1.85% ACC, and LoRA-DRS by +3.20% ACC. The advantage grows with sequence length: at T=20, Janus-LoRA's 77.11% MAA exceeds InfLoRA (76.24%) by 0.87% and BiLoRA (68.60%) by a substantial 8.51%, indicating superior resistance to cumulative forgetting. On CIFAR-100, Janus-LoRA surpasses InfLoRA by +1.99% ACC (88.68 vs. 86.69) and +0.97% MAA. On DomainNet, it leads InfLoRA by +0.88% ACC and +0.69% MAA. Notably, Janus-LoRA's BWT (-6.05 at T=10) is slightly worse than BiLoRA (-2.07) and LoRA-DRS (-3.72), but this is offset by its substantially higher accuracy, reflecting a deliberate stability-plasticity trade-off that prioritizes overall performance over minimal forgetting in isolation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of LoRA-based continual learning methods (InfLoRA, BiLoRA, LoRA-DRS) that enforce orthogonality between task subspaces to prevent interference. Its key departure is a diagnosis that standard LoRA optimization—performing independent Euclidean updates on factors A and B—systematically violates the very orthogonality these prior methods assume is preserved, because the composite update deviates from the intended safe direction due to factor coupling. Janus-LoRA addresses this with Gradient Rectification (a closed-form decoupling of factor updates) and adds a Decoupled Margin Loss for feature-level separation, while replacing the offline subspace estimation of GPM with an online Stiefel-manifold-based basis tracker.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of LoRA's internal optimization coupling as a source of catastrophic forgetting is a useful diagnostic for the PEFT+CL community, and the GR module's demonstrated plug-and-play applicability (boosting LoRA-GPM by +6.93 BWT) suggests it could retrofit existing methods. The fully online design eliminates the offline processing bottleneck of GPM, making the approach more scalable to large-scale single-pass training. However, BWT remains negative across all settings (forgetting is reduced but not eliminated), evaluations are limited to ViT-B/16 and class-incremental exemplar-free scenarios, and it remains open whether the Stiefel-manifold basis tracking and factor-level rectification generalize to other PEFT paradigms (adapters, prompt tuning) or to task/domain-incremental settings.
