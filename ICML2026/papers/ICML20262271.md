## Problem

Existing ILT methods are limited to either differentiable optimization (unable to handle non-differentiable metrics like EPE and shot count) or learning-based approaches whose training data inherits quality limits from optimization-based generation. No prior framework unifies a generative model with reinforcement learning to jointly optimize both differentiable and non-differentiable lithographic metrics under a single reward signal.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262271/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a binary target layout (circuit pattern) at 512×512 resolution representing a 2048×2048 nm² region and produces a single-channel grayscale ILT mask pattern of the same spatial size. During generation, the target layout is concatenated with the noisy input along the channel dimension to condition the U-Net, and the mask is produced via a single Euler step (Δt=1) from Gaussian noise, yielding inference in ~0.1 s per sample.

## Method

LithoGRPO models mask generation as a rectified flow (linear interpolation between noise and data) trained in three stages: pretraining with the standard flow-matching MSE loss, supervised fine-tuning that adds differentiable L2 and PVB metrics computed on the projected endpoint, and GRPO-based RL fine-tuning that reformulates the ODE into an SDE with low-frequency colored noise to enable stochastic exploration of G=6 candidate masks per target. The GRPO reward is the negative sum of four normalized metrics (L2, PVB, EPE, Shot), with group-level advantage normalization canceling any constant offset in the fast shot-count proxy. A key efficiency contribution is an ultra-fast shot-count algorithm that replaces NP-hard non-overlapping rectangle partitioning with an ILP-based minimum set cover over maximal rectangles, using scan-line constraint aggregation to reduce the problem to sub-second solve times (130–490× speedup). The backbone is an 87M-parameter U-Net with GroupNorm and SiLU activations, and the colored noise (Fourier-domain low-pass filtered) is specifically designed to preserve mask topology and avoid the fragmentation that white noise would introduce.

## Results

LithoGRPO(RLFT) achieves the best overall average rank of 4.3 across 4 datasets × 4 metrics + runtime (17 columns), ahead of the best baseline (MultiLevel at 5.6). On MetalSet, it attains the lowest L2 (28,933), PVB (39,838), and Shot (444) among all methods, with EPE of 3.1 (second-best after MultiLevel's 2.5). On the out-of-distribution StdContact benchmark, it achieves the best L2 (19,102), PVB (41,359), EPE (0.2), and Shot (889). Inference takes only 0.104 s per mask with one-step rectified flow sampling, comparable to other learning-based methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LithoGRPO(RLFT) outperforms all 9 baselines in overall average rank (4.3 vs. best baseline 5.6 for MultiLevel). Against optimization-based methods, it dominates on L2 and PVB across all datasets while achieving dramatically lower Shot counts (e.g., 444 vs. 1250 for MultiLevel on MetalSet), though MultiLevel retains a slight EPE edge (2.5 vs. 3.1 on MetalSet). Among learning-based baselines, LithoGRPO(RLFT) achieves the best L2 on MetalSet (28,933 vs. 32,579 for DAMO) and ViaSet (4,276 vs. 4,382 for DOINN), and the best Shot on all four datasets. The closest competitor is ILILT, which edges out LithoGRPO on StdMetal L2 (14,596 vs. 14,840) but is outperformed on PVB, Shot, and all Via-layer metrics, and is 4× slower at inference (0.441 s vs. 0.104 s). On the out-of-distribution StdContact benchmark, LithoGRPO(RLFT) achieves the best scores on all four metrics, with particularly large margins on L2 (19,102 vs. 25,422 for Neural-ILT) and EPE (0.2 vs. 3.2 for Neural-ILT).

## Relation to Prior Work

LithoGRPO sits at the intersection of two recent lines of work: rectified-flow generative modeling (Liu et al., 2023; Lipman et al., 2023) and GRPO-based reinforcement learning for flow/diffusion models (Liu et al., 2025b; Shao et al., 2024). In the ILT literature, prior methods are either optimization-based (gradient descent on differentiable metrics like L2/PVB) or learning-based (image-to-image networks trained on optimization outputs), and both are fundamentally limited to differentiable objectives. The key departure here is using the explicit, physics-based ILT metrics as RL rewards, which naturally handles the non-differentiable EPE and Shot metrics that no prior ILT method could jointly optimize. The fast shot-count algorithm (ILP-based set cover replacing NP-hard rectangle partitioning) is a domain-specific enabler that makes GRPO sampling tractable, and the colored-noise SDE design is tailored to preserve mask topology—both are novel contributions beyond the generic Flow-GRPO recipe.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to unify flow matching and RL for mask optimization, providing a principled way to jointly optimize conflicting differentiable and non-differentiable lithographic metrics under a single reward signal. The 130×–490× shot-count speedup and one-step inference (0.1 s) make the approach practically deployable where prior ILT methods required minutes of iterative optimization. However, evaluation is confined to the academic LithoBench benchmark at 512×512 resolution; industrial-scale validation, extreme layout densities, and the added compute cost of three-stage training remain open. For the broader generative-modeling community, the paper is a clean demonstration that GRPO-style RL with domain-specific colored noise and fast proxy rewards transfers effectively to a structured, physics-constrained generation task.
