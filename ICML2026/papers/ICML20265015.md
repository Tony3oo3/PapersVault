## Problem

In expert-parallel distributed MoE inference, skewed token routing causes devices hosting lighter experts to idle while waiting for the heaviest, severely underutilizing GPUs. Existing remedies—expert replication and migration—require modifying the expert-device mapping, incurring memory or communication overhead and losing flexibility when routing patterns shift across tasks. No prior method achieves load balancing that is simultaneously mapping-agnostic, overhead-free, and scalable to large EP sizes.

## Input / Output

The scheduler consumes per-micro-batch per-device workload distributions (integer token counts per expert rank) at each MoE step, along with the fixed expert-device mapping and a minimum combination size m. It outputs a per-step RunSet—a subset of micro-batches (possibly at different layers) to execute jointly—and the resulting processed hidden states. Scheduling operates solely on lightweight routing metadata (a few integers per device), adding sub-millisecond overhead per step relative to multi-second end-to-end latency.

## Method

EasyBalance exploits two cross-layer observations: (1) experts of other layers already reside in GPU memory and act as naturally redundant experts for the current layer, eliminating the need for explicit replication; (2) jointly executing MoE workloads from different layers is worst-case performance-safe (max(Σw_j) ≤ Σmax(w_j)) and mostly beneficial because workloads rarely peak on the same device. At each MoE step, a greedy scheduler selects a subset of micro-batches from the active pool to run together, deferring the rest for future balancing opportunities, while each micro-batch independently advances through its own layer sequence. Three scheduling heuristics are evaluated—MaxUtil (exhaustive, O(2^n)), CumUtil (sequential, O(n)), and DiffPeak (peak-device diversity, O(n))—all of which consistently outperform the no-combination baseline. The approach requires no expert replication, no migration, and no modification to the expert-device mapping, making it orthogonal to and composable with existing methods like EPLB.

## Results

EasyBalance consistently reduces GPU under-utilization by over 40% across all tested models and tasks. For example, on Qwen3-30B with EP=8, GPU under-utilization drops from 0.37 to 0.21 on 2wikimqa and from 0.38 to 0.23 on repobench-p. The scheduling overhead is negligible (0.13–0.28 ms per step vs. ~8.2 s end-to-end latency). Across three models (Qwen3-30B, Moonlight-16B, Qwen3-235B) and 13 LongBench tasks, EasyBalance achieves consistent end-to-end latency reductions without any expert-mapping modification.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is vanilla expert parallelism without any load-balancing strategy (w/o EB). EasyBalance outperforms this baseline across all models, tasks, and EP sizes, reducing GPU under-utilization by 40–50% (e.g., 0.37→0.21 on Qwen3-30B/2wikimqa at EP=8; 0.46→0.29 on Moonlight-16B/repobench-p at EP=8). The paper also compares against EPLB (DeepSeek AI, 2025), an expert-replication-based load balancer. EasyBalance is orthogonal to EPLB: combining them yields additive gains (e.g., EPLB(128) alone gives 0.23 under-utilization on 2wikimqa, while EPLB(128)+EasyBalance drops it to 0.14). EPLB with redundant experts (136 physical) provides only marginal improvement over 128 (0.22 vs. 0.23) while incurring extra memory, whereas EasyBalance adds no memory overhead. EPLB's gains are highly task-dependent, whereas EasyBalance delivers consistent improvements across all 13 LongBench tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the micro-batching technique (Kwon et al., 2023; SGLang, 2024) as its operational substrate, but departs fundamentally from the dominant line of MoE load-balancing work (EPLB, Lina, Harmony) that operates within a single layer's expert-device mapping via replication or migration. The key conceptual departure is the cross-layer perspective: treating experts of other layers as naturally redundant for the current layer, and exploiting the fact that micro-batches can reside at different layers simultaneously to combine workloads. This reframes load balancing from a per-layer mapping problem into a cross-layer scheduling problem, requiring no modification to the expert-device mapping at all.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For practitioners deploying MoE models at scale, the zero-overhead, task-agnostic nature of EasyBalance makes it immediately deployable without the memory cost of expert replication or the communication cost of migration, and it scales favorably with larger EP sizes where imbalance worsens. The demonstrated orthogonality to EPLB means it can be stacked on top of existing load-balancing infrastructure. Open questions include whether the technique extends to single-sequence inference (without micro-batching), its effectiveness on heterogeneous hardware, and its interaction with emerging attention-FFN disaggregation architectures. The paper is limited to single-node A800 experiments, leaving multi-node and other GPU platforms untested.
