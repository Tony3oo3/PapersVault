## Problem

Existing incremental object detection methods follow a classification-style paradigm that filters old-model predictions to only high-confidence, low-overlap detections, treating overlap-heavy regions as noise. This separation-oriented approach overlooks object symbiosis—co-occurrence and occlusion between old and new classes—that creates spatial and semantic dependencies requiring shared representations. By discarding these symbiotic signals, prior methods distort the unified feature space, increase old-new confusion, and accelerate catastrophic forgetting.

## Input / Output

The method operates on RGB images at 1064×800 resolution and outputs bounding boxes with class labels across all learned categories. A key constraint is that during incremental training at task t, images contain both previously learned and newly introduced objects, but only the new categories carry ground-truth annotations; the frozen old model must be run on the same images to generate pseudo-labels and guide distillation. The CFE module is training-only and adds no test-time overhead, so inference remains a single forward pass of the updated Deformable DETR.

## Method

SIKD builds on Deformable DETR and introduces a symbiosis-aware query partitioning scheme that splits the frozen old model's queries into stable anchors (high confidence, low IoU to new GT), symbiotic regions (high IoU overlap from co-occurrence/occlusion), and residual queries. Spatial Symbiosis Distillation (SpSD) refines symbiotic queries via a Consistent Feature Enhancement module (multi-head self-attention + MLP) guided by anchor-based class prototypes, then performs slot-aligned, confidence-weighted, layer-wise logit and box distillation between the old and new decoders. Semantic Symbiosis Distillation (SeSD) aggregates last-layer decoder features into confidence-weighted L2-normalized prototypes per old class and aligns their soft rank orderings in the old-class logit subspace, preserving inter-class semantic topology independent of absolute confidence. The CFE module is trained with a separate cosine loss while the main detector is updated with the standard detection loss plus SpSD and SeSD terms, with gradients isolated between the two branches.

## Results

On COCO 2017 (70+10), SIKD achieves 44.3 AP, 62.9 AP50, and 47.8 AP75 with Deformable DETR, outperforming the strongest non-exemplar baselines DyQ-DETR (39.5 AP) and DCA (41.3 AP) by 4.8 and 3.0 AP points respectively. On the 40+40 split, SIKD reaches 43.3 AP and 61.7 AP50, surpassing DyQ-DETR (41.4 AP) and DCA (42.8 AP). On the DIOR remote-sensing dataset, SIKD improves the All AP50 by 6.2–9.0 points over the exemplar-based CL-DETR* across three two-task settings, and under multi-task DIOR (10+5+5) it reaches 66.5 AP50 versus 47.1 for CL-DETR*.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SIKD outperforms all nine baselines on both COCO 2017 two-task splits. Against the closest non-exemplar Deformable DETR competitors, SIKD gains +4.8 AP over DyQ-DETR (44.3 vs 39.5) and +3.0 AP over DCA (44.3 vs 41.3) on 70+10, and +1.9 AP over DyQ-DETR (43.3 vs 41.4) and +0.5 AP over DCA (43.3 vs 42.8) on 40+40. It also surpasses the exemplar-based CL-DETR* by +3.9 AP (70+10) and +1.3 AP (40+40), and the GLIP-based TLR by +1.5 and +2.9 AP respectively. The GFLv1-based methods (LwF, RILOD, SID, ERD) and the ACF method trail by 8–37 AP points, reflecting the architectural gap between anchor-free CNN detectors and the Deformable DETR backbone. On DIOR, SIKD leads CL-DETR* by 6.2–9.0 AP50 points across 10+10, 15+5, and 19+1 settings, with the largest margin (9.0) in the extreme 19+1 split where only one new class is added.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the CL-DETR / Deformable DETR incremental detection paradigm, where a frozen old model generates pseudo-labels on new-task data to preserve old-class knowledge. The key conceptual departure is from the prevailing 'separation' strategy (used by CL-DETR, BPF, DCA, DyQ-DETR) that discards high-IoU, high-confidence old-class predictions as contaminated by new objects. SIKD reframes these overlap-heavy predictions as 'symbiotic regions' encoding genuine spatial and semantic dependencies (co-occurrence, occlusion) and retains them as supervisory signals, maintaining a unified feature space rather than sharpening old/new boundaries. Methodologically, it introduces two new distillation pathways — slot-aligned spatial distillation with a Consistent Feature Enhancement module, and class-level soft-rank prototype alignment — that are orthogonal to the pseudo-label filtering step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges a widely adopted design assumption in IOD (filter out overlap-heavy predictions) and shows that structured exploitation of those signals yields consistent gains, especially in occlusion-heavy domains like remote sensing (DIOR gains of 6–9 AP50 over exemplar-based CL-DETR*). The unified-feature-space framing offers a principled alternative to the classification-inspired separation paradigm, potentially guiding future IOD methods beyond DETR. Limitations include added training cost (~40% more FLOPs from CFE and extra decoder forwards), reliance on a frozen old model's query space for partitioning, and evaluation limited to two DETR variants; whether the symbiosis argument transfers to anchor-based or language-grounded detectors remains open.
