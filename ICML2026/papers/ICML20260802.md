## Problem

Existing model merging techniques assume architecture-compatible models, preventing direct knowledge transfer from large high-resource LLMs to smaller heterogeneous targets. Cross-architecture approaches that do exist typically require costly distillation-based training to first project knowledge into a compatible parameter space. This paper asks whether direct weight-space fusion across heterogeneous architectures is possible without any distillation step.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260802/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a high-resource source LLM (e.g., LLaMA-3 8B), a low-resource target LLM with a different architecture (e.g., 1B models with different layer counts and hidden dimensions), and a small calibration set of ~2000 text samples. From these, it extracts intermediate activations at every transformer sublayer to compute two levels of optimal transport plans: feature-level matrices Q (n_ℓ × n'_m per layer pair) and a global layer-correspondence matrix P (L × M). The output is a fused model architecturally identical to the target, where only a sparse subset of top-k neurons (default k=128) per projection module has been modified with transported source parameters, plus an optional residual-frozen fine-tuning stage.

## Method

The core mechanism is a two-level optimal transport formulation that infers cross-architecture neuron correspondences purely from activation statistics. At the feature level, a correlation-based cost matrix between activation channels of each target-source layer pair is solved via entropically regularized OT (Sinkhorn iterations) to yield transport plans Q_in and Q_out for pre- and post-projection features. At the layer level, scalar transport costs aggregated from Q are used in a second OT problem to produce a global layer-mixing matrix P_eff. These transport matrices are then converted into weight-space fusion operators: source weights W_B are conjugated by Q_out and Q_in^T to map into target neuron coordinates, and the result is injected into the target via masked top-k neuron replacement with a tunable coefficient α. A residual-frozen adaptation stage follows, where the transported residual ΔW is held fixed while only the original base weights are fine-tuned, then the residual is folded back for inference. Theoretically, the paper proves (Theorem 4.1) that this weight transport is exactly equivalent to mapping target features into source coordinates, applying the source linear map, and projecting back, providing a principled representation-space interpretation of the fusion.

## Results

On MalayMMLU, Fused w/ Adaptation improves over the Base Model by +6.51 (Humanities), +3.31 (Language), +4.19 (Others), +3.97 (STEM), and +5.99 (Social Science) points. On Thai benchmarks, fusion alone boosts MGSM from 0.50 to 0.64, and adaptation further raises it to 0.72. Across Cantonese (CMMLU average 25.26→27.44), Indonesian (average 38.43→39.30), Finance (average 0.32→0.35), and Medical (average 46.30→47.87), the method consistently outperforms the unfused target model using only 2000 calibration samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three strategies: the unfused Base Model, SFT (supervised fine-tuning), and knowledge distillation (Hinton et al., 2015), evaluated under z-score and min-max normalization across six domains (Cantonese, Malay, Thai, Indonesian, Finance, Medical). The proposed Fused w/ Adaptation method achieves the highest relative performance in every domain, while SFT and distillation show domain-dependent variability—some domains benefit from SFT while others favor distillation, but neither consistently matches the fusion approach. Against the Base Model, fusion alone (without any post-training) already yields positive gains on most tasks (e.g., Thai MGSM 0.50→0.64, Malay Humanities 42.30→46.19), and the residual-frozen adaptation stage adds further improvements of 1–3 points on top. The method also preserves general capabilities (ARC-Easy, CommonsenseQA, PIQA, Social IQA, WinoGrande) at levels comparable to the Base Model, indicating that the selective top-k neuron injection does not degrade broad reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the model merging literature (Wortsman et al., Ilharco et al., Ainsworth et al.) and the OT-based merging paradigm (Singh & Jaggi 2020; Imfeld et al. 2023a) to the cross-architecture setting, where prior methods assume identical architectures or rely on distillation as an intermediate step (Wan et al. 2024, 2025). The key conceptual departure is using entropic OT on activation statistics to infer neuron-level correspondences between heterogeneous models, then lifting these activation-space transport plans directly into weight-space fusion operators—bypassing the distillation bridge entirely. The layer-level cost aggregation draws on the hierarchical OT framework of Shah & Khosla (2025), and the residual-frozen adaptation stage introduces a parameterization that separates transferred knowledge from base weights to prevent overwriting during fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a practical, training-light alternative to distillation for transferring knowledge from large high-resource LLMs to smaller heterogeneous targets, which is directly relevant to low-resource language and domain-specific deployment. However, the tested target models are all ~1B parameters and the source-target architecture gaps are moderate (e.g., LLaMA-3 8B → 1B), leaving open whether the approach scales to much larger capacity gaps or fundamentally different architectures (e.g., MoE vs. dense). The reliance on a task-relevant calibration set and the heuristic top-k neuron selection (k=128) are practical constraints that may limit applicability when no domain data is available. Open questions include whether the OT correspondences capture genuinely transferable structure or merely statistical correlation, and how to incorporate safety-aware objectives into the transport formulation to prevent bias propagation.
