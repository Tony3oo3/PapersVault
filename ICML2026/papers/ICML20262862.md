## Problem

Optimization over non-convex constraint sets that are homeomorphic to a ball (e.g., star-shaped, geodesic-convex sets) remains challenging: existing first-order methods require expensive projection or optimization oracles, achieve slow O(ε⁻³) or O(ε⁻⁴) convergence rates, or rely on restrictive structural assumptions like bounded local concavity or hidden convexity. The paper asks whether one can design a method that achieves the fast O(ε⁻²) rate typical of convex-constrained optimization while avoiding costly oracles, by exploiting the ball-homeomorphic topology of the constraint set.

## Input / Output

The method takes a parametric problem min f_θ(x) s.t. x ∈ K_θ where K_θ is a compact BH set defined by continuous inequality constraints, along with a pre-trained conditional INN Φ_θ (trained offline in an unsupervised manner) and an initial point z_0 in the transformed ball space. It outputs an approximate KKT stationary point x_K = Φ_θ(z_K) in the original space after O(L_H ε⁻²) iterations, with per-iteration cost Õ(W) where W is the INN parameter count (typically O(n²) for a 3-layer coupling INN of width O(n)). The only oracle required beyond gradient evaluations is a membership oracle for K_θ, which is substantially cheaper than projection or optimization oracles.

## Method

Hom-PGD+ leverages a conditional invertible neural network (coupling-layer GLOW architecture) to learn a homeomorphism Φ_θ mapping a unit ball to the non-convex BH constraint set K_θ, trained unsupervised by maximizing a loss combining log-volume maximization, constraint-violation penalty, and log-Lipschitz regularization. The original problem is reformulated as a ball-constrained optimization in the transformed space, where PGD is applied with a bisection-based projection operator that corrects infeasible iterates using only membership oracle queries (linear convergence per bisection). The key theoretical contribution is proving that this approach achieves an O(ε⁻²) convergence rate to an (ε + O(√ε_inn))-approximate KKT stationary point—matching the convex-constraint rate for non-convex problems—while the per-iteration complexity is Õ(W) rather than requiring expensive inner optimization subproblems. The INN is trained once offline and amortized across many parametric instances, making the online cost independent of the training expense.

## Results

On chance-constrained power flow (PGLIB 200-bus, 1000 scenarios), Hom-PGD+ achieves an objective of 0.768 with zero constraint violations in 246 s, outperforming EPM (0.933, 1 violation, 801 s), ALM (0.927, 1, 1452 s), and PPP (0.927, 1, 799 s) on all three metrics. On non-uniform adversarial attacks (CIFAR-10, p=0.5), Hom-PGD+ increases attack success rate from 2.0% (PGD) to 14.6%, a 7× improvement, while per-iteration time remains nearly identical (~0.042 s). In scaling tests, Hom-PGD+ maintains near-constant runtime as the number of quadratic constraints grows from 10 to 1000, whereas IPOPT's per-instance time jumps from 3 s to 70 s at n=50. Theoretically, Hom-PGD+ achieves an O(ε⁻²) iteration complexity to an (ε + O(√ε_inn))-approximate KKT stationary point, improving upon the O(ε⁻³) or O(ε⁻⁴) rates of existing non-convex-constraint methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against EPM (Cartis et al., 2011), ALM (Sahin et al., 2019), and PPP (Lin et al., 2022) on chance-constrained power flow, Hom-PGD+ dominates on all three reported metrics: it achieves the lowest objective (0.768 vs. 0.927–0.933 on 200-bus/1000 scen.), the only zero constraint violations (vs. 1 for all baselines), and 3–6× lower runtime (246 s vs. 799–1452 s). On the 500-bus system the same pattern holds with a 2.8–5.2× speedup. Against the PGD baseline of Erdemir et al. (2021) on non-uniform adversarial attacks, Hom-PGD+ wins decisively in the non-convex regime (p=0.5: 14.6% vs. 2.0% success; p=1: 16.4% vs. 2.8%) while performing comparably in the convex regime (p=2: 20.0% vs. 20.6%; p=4: 20.8% vs. 21.4%), with per-iteration time essentially unchanged (~0.042 s for both). In the QCQP scaling study, Hom-PGD+ also outperforms the second-order solver IPOPT in wall-clock time as the number of constraints grows, while maintaining a ~2.9% average objective gap with zero violations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the reparameterization-based optimization framework from convex to non-convex settings by replacing hand-crafted analytical mappings (Hadamard transformation for simplices, gauge mapping for compact convex sets) with a learned invertible neural network (INN) that approximates the homeomorphism between a ball and a general ball-homeomorphic (BH) constraint set. It builds directly on Liang et al. (ICML 2023) for the bisection projection operator and validity condition, and on Liu et al. (NeurIPS 2026) for the projection-free PGD formulation over convex sets. The key departure from prior non-convex constrained optimization work (PPP, ALM, EPM) is eliminating expensive inner optimization oracles in favor of a cheap membership oracle, and the key departure from Liang et al. (2024), which used INNs only for single-step feasibility correction, is applying the learned homeomorphism to drive an iterative PGD loop with full convergence analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central contribution is closing the convergence-rate gap between convex and non-convex constrained optimization: by exploiting the BH topological structure, Hom-PGD+ achieves O(ε⁻²) iterations (matching the convex-constraint rate) versus O(ε⁻³)–O(ε⁻⁴) for existing non-convex methods, with per-iteration cost O(n²) rather than an expensive optimization oracle. This is practically significant for parametric and real-time settings (power-grid dispatch, adversarial attacks) where the same constraint family is solved repeatedly with varying parameters. Key limitations include the BH assumption (not all non-convex sets qualify), the O(√ε_inn) approximation error term that degrades with INN quality, and the worst-case INN size bound of O(ε⁻ⁿ) that is loose in practice. Open questions include tighter INN approximation bounds, extension to non-BH compact sets with quantifiable optimality gaps, and applicability to manifold-constrained problems.
