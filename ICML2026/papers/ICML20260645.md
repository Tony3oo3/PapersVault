## Problem

Existing reasoning-augmented text-to-image generation methods rely on explicit chain-of-thought, forcing intermediate reasoning to be decoded into discrete text at fixed steps before being re-encoded back into the generator. This introduces three fundamental deficiencies: information loss from compressing rich internal states into a narrow symbolic channel, inefficiency from repeated decode-encode cycles, and cognitive mismatch with how humans actually create (relying on continuous implicit thoughts rather than verbalizing every judgment). The paper asks how to seamlessly interleave latent reasoning directly into the autoregressive generation stream, enabling the model to dynamically monitor its evolving visual state and intervene to refine generation without explicit textual CoT.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single text prompt as input and produces a 384×384 RGB image as output, generated autoregressively as a sequence of discrete image tokens by the Janus-Pro unified multimodal model. A key distinction from explicit-reasoning baselines is that no intermediate text is ever produced or consumed: the reasoning branch operates entirely on continuous hidden states, and the generation branch receives guidance via control tokens injected into its KV cache rather than through prompt rewriting. The system operates as a single forward pass (no iterative search or multi-cycle refinement), with the adaptive invoker checking every 64 tokens to decide whether to trigger a latent reasoning intervention.

## Method

LatentMorph introduces four lightweight components that close the loop between a UMM's understanding and generation branches entirely in latent space. A short-term condenser compresses recent hidden states into a 4-token local memory via cross-attention, feeding an RL-trained invoker (a 2-layer MLP) that adaptively decides when to reason based on semantic consistency, prediction uncertainty, temporal dynamics, and stability signals. Upon invocation, a long-term condenser summarizes the full generation history into an 8-token global visual memory, which is passed to the understanding branch to produce latent thoughts; a translator (MLP with tanh gating) fuses these thoughts with the visual memory and prompt embedding into a control signal, and a shaper expands this into 4 control tokens injected directly into the generator's KV cache to steer subsequent token predictions without altering the autoregressive structure. Training is two-stage: supervised fine-tuning on 20k text-image pairs with random intervention steps (using standard cross-entropy loss, no process-level supervision), followed by GRPO reinforcement learning (group size 8, CLIP + HPS-v2.1 rewards) to optimize the invoker's adaptive policy with a penalty for excessive reasoning frequency.

## Results

On GenEval, LatentMorph achieves 0.96 overall, improving the base Janus-Pro model (0.80) by 16% and surpassing the strongest explicit-reasoning baseline TwiG-ZS (0.86) by 10 points. On T2I-CompBench, LatentMorph reaches 64.53 overall, a 25.3% gain over the vanilla model (39.21) and an 8.29-point margin over the best reason-while-generating baseline TwiG-RL (56.24). On the more abstract WISE and IPV-Txt benchmarks, LatentMorph outperforms explicit paradigms by 15.6% and 11.3% respectively, while reducing inference time by 44.3% and token consumption by 51.0% compared to baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LatentMorph outperforms all seven compared baselines on both GenEval and T2I-CompBench. Against the strongest reason-while-generating baseline TwiG-RL (56.24 on T2I-CompBench), LatentMorph leads by 8.29 points overall and by 7.28 points on the challenging Non-Spatial sub-metric (39.56 vs. 31.99). Against the test-time latent search method MILR (53.25), LatentMorph gains 11.28 points on T2I-CompBench while matching it on GenEval (0.96 vs. 0.95), and does so in a single forward pass rather than iterative search. Reason-before/after paradigms (T2I-R1 at 52.81, TIR at 45.21, T2I-Copilot at 45.16, Self-CoT at 42.68) all trail LatentMorph by 11.7–21.9 points on T2I-CompBench. The GRPO-only baseline (41.61) shows that RL alone without reasoning yields only marginal gains, confirming that the latent reasoning mechanism—not just reward optimization—drives the improvement. On abstract reasoning (WISE, IPV-Txt), LatentMorph's advantage over explicit TwiG widens to 15.6% and 11.3%, suggesting that continuous latent states preserve non-verbalizable visual cues lost when thoughts are forced through discrete text.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) reasoning-augmented T2I generation, where prior work (TwiG, MILR, T2I-Copilot, Self-CoT) interleaves explicit chain-of-thought between understanding and generation branches, and (2) latent reasoning in LLMs (Coconut, SoftCoT), which compresses intermediate deliberation into continuous hidden states. LatentMorph departs from both by performing reasoning entirely in the latent space across the understanding–generation interface of a unified multimodal model, using KV-cache injection of control tokens rather than prompt replacement or iterative latent search. The key conceptual departure is addressing the 'bi-directional interface mismatch'—a perception gap and an execution gap—that prevents direct transfer of LLM latent reasoning to autoregressive image generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the decode-encode bottleneck of explicit CoT, LatentMorph offers a practical path toward more efficient and cognitively aligned T2I generation, with 44% inference-time reduction and 51% token savings while outperforming explicit paradigms. The KV-cache injection mechanism is a lightweight, model-agnostic technique that could be adopted by other autoregressive generators without architectural changes. Open questions include whether the approach extends to diffusion-based generators, how it scales to higher-resolution or video generation, and whether the RL-trained invoker generalizes across diverse prompt distributions beyond the evaluated benchmarks. The reliance on a single base model (Janus-Pro) and the absence of a diffusion-based validation leave the generality claim partially untested.
