## Problem

Transformer-based offline RL methods like Decision Transformer suffer from causal confusion: they latch onto spurious correlations in logged data that predict reward but are not causally related to the environment's true mechanisms, leading to brittle policies when those features shift at deployment. Existing counterfactual augmentation approaches either require online environment interaction or assume factored state spaces with independent entities, making them inapplicable to standard continuous-control benchmarks in the strictly offline setting. The paper addresses this gap by combining reward-preserving counterfactual state editing with causal architectural guidance, both designed to work without a learned dynamics model or environment access.

## Input / Output

The method consumes offline trajectories tokenized as repeating (return-to-go, state, action) triplets with context windows of 20 tokens (MuJoCo) or 50 tokens (AntMaze), and outputs the next action via autoregressive prediction. A key design constraint is that counterfactual augmentation replaces only the observation token in a trajectory copy while keeping the action, reward label, and the next factual observation unchanged—synthetic successor transitions are never created. The Causal Reward Model operates on (state, action, reward) tuples with a 16- or 32-dimensional latent space, and the Counterfactual State Generator produces edits in a normalized state space bounded by a move band of [0.2, 0.5] standard deviations.

## Method

CSET combines two complementary mechanisms. On the data side, a Causal Reward Model (CRM) trained as a conditional VAE performs abduction by inferring a posterior over latent reward disturbances from offline transitions; a Counterfactual State Generator (CSG) then proposes minimally edited states that preserve the factual reward under the fixed action and abduced disturbance, subject to a normalized move-band constraint and a dual acceptance gate (reward consistency within ε_r and move magnitude within ρ_high). On the model side, a hybrid architecture uses modality-specific 1D convolutional encoders (depthwise + pointwise, kernel 3) to extract local temporal structure from RTG, state, and action streams separately, then fuses them into a shared representation processed by a single final multi-head attention block. This attention block is softly supervised via a cross-entropy mask loss that encourages action-token queries to concentrate attention mass on their direct causal parents (current state and return-to-go at the same timestep), with a tunable weight λ balancing this guidance against the standard action prediction loss. A Proposition 1 guarantee shows that accepted edits are reward-consistent with the true reward SCM up to error δ + ε_r under a local CRM accuracy assumption.

## Results

On D4RL locomotion and AntMaze benchmarks, CSET achieves the highest expert-normalized return within the DT family on 10 of 11 datasets (e.g., Hopper-medium: 93.4, Walker2d-medium-expert: 110.7, AntMaze-umaze-diverse: 84.5), with LSDT marginally ahead only on HalfCheetah-medium-expert (93.2 vs. 93.1). On sequential recommendation, CSET attains the best cumulative and average reward on all three datasets (KuaiRand: 7.62/0.66, KuaiRec: 31.87/1.06, VirtualTB: 80.42/5.70). Under injected spurious distractors, CSET degrades by only 2–6% in expert-normalized return, compared to 16–37% for DT, 5–23% for DC, 23–52% for ReBRAC, and 12–21% for EDAC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Within the DT family, CSET outperforms standard DT on every dataset (e.g., Hopper-medium: 93.4 vs. 67.6; AntMaze-umaze: 83.1 vs. 69.8), and beats DC and LSDT on 10 of 11 D4RL tasks, with LSDT edging ahead by 0.1 on HalfCheetah-medium-expert. Against value-based baselines, ReBRAC and EDAC achieve higher absolute returns on several locomotion tasks (e.g., ReBRAC 98.4 vs. CSET 93.4 on Hopper-medium), but EDAC completely fails on AntMaze (0.0 on both umaze and umaze-diverse), where CSET scores 83.1 and 84.5. The most striking comparison is under spurious distractor intervention: CSET degrades by only 2–6% in expert-normalized return, while ReBRAC drops 23–52%, DT drops 16–37%, DC drops 5–23%, and EDAC drops 12–21%. On sequential recommendation, CSET surpasses all three DT-based recommenders (DT4Rec, CDT4Rec, EDT4Rec) on both cumulative and average reward across KuaiRand, KuaiRec, and VirtualTB, with margins of 0.04–0.80 in cumulative reward. The ablation confirms both components contribute: removing the causal hybrid architecture (w/o Arch) causes the largest drops (e.g., Hopper-medium: 84.2 vs. 93.4), while removing counterfactual editing (w/o Edit) hurts most on sparse-reward AntMaze (81.5/82.5 vs. 83.1/84.5).

## Relation to Prior Work

CSET extends the Decision Transformer (DT) paradigm for offline RL by injecting causal structure at both the data and architecture levels. On the data side, it departs from prior counterfactual augmentation methods (MOCODA, CAIAC, ACAMDA, CIP) that either assume factored state spaces or require online environment interaction; instead, CSET uses a CVAE-based Causal Reward Model and a Counterfactual State Generator to produce reward-preserving state edits under Pearl's abduction-action-prediction procedure, entirely from offline data. On the architecture side, it builds on the convolution-plus-attention hybrid designs of Decision ConvFormer (DC) and LSDT but adds explicit causal-parent attention supervision, constraining action-query attention toward the state and return-to-go tokens. The theoretical grounding draws on Structural Causal Models for MDPs, formalizing the reward mechanism as r = f_r(s, a, u_r) and providing a local-accuracy guarantee for accepted edits.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and underappreciated vulnerability in offline RL: policies trained on logged data can latch onto spurious reward-correlated features that vanish at deployment, and the robustness experiment (2–6% degradation for CSET vs. 12–52% for strong value-based baselines under distractor intervention) makes this gap concrete. Limitations include dependence on the learned reward model's local accuracy (the trust-region assumption is checkable only within the band), a small number of per-task hyperparameters (edit band radii, attention supervision weight λ), and the fact that counterfactual edits do not validate full transition dynamics. Open questions include uncertainty-aware acceptance (e.g., Bayesian reward model posterior variance replacing fixed ε_r), automatic hyperparameter schedules, and extension to higher-dimensional or partially observable settings.
