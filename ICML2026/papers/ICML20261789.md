## Problem

Top-k sparse gating in SMoE architectures introduces inherent discontinuities where arbitrarily close inputs can activate entirely different expert sets, yet no systematic theoretical analysis of their geometric structure or stochastic behavior had been undertaken. Prior mitigation approaches (SMEAR, Soft MoE, ReMoE) either compromise the causal structure needed for autoregressive generation or require costly retraining from scratch, leaving a gap between understanding why these discontinuities matter and fixing them in a way that preserves the sparsity and efficiency of standard Top-k routing.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard SMoE inputs (text sequences, RGB images, or image+text prompts) and produces the same outputs as vanilla Top-k SMoE (token probabilities, class labels, classification scores). The key distinction is that smoothing is applied in gating-logit space rather than input space: only non-top-k logits within an ℓ∞ distance ε of the k-th largest logit are modified, while all top-k logits remain unchanged. A boundary loss adaptively tunes ε under a fixed expert budget (e.g., k* = k + 0.5), so the number of additionally activated experts is controlled without manual hyperparameter tuning.

## Method

The paper first provides a rigorous geometric classification of SMoE discontinuities by order (number of tied experts at the top-k threshold), proving via measure-theoretic slicing arguments that ε-thickened order-1 discontinuity sets dominate in volume while higher-order sets vanish as (ε/R)^{n-m} in relative measure. Stochastically, modeling input perturbations as an Itô diffusion, the authors prove trajectories almost surely exit their top-k cell through an order-1 boundary in finite time, with explicit hitting-time lower bounds and occupation-time upper bounds that decrease with discontinuity order. Motivated by these results, the proposed ℓ∞,ε local smoothing applies a log-smoothstep function h to non-top-k gating logits within the ε-strip below z_[k](x), softly boosting near-tied experts while discarding far-below ones, leaving top-k logits untouched. Because higher-order discontinuities occupy vanishingly small volume, at most n additional experts are activated near an order-n discontinuity, keeping computational overhead small. A boundary loss L_boundary = αε(K − k*) adaptively adjusts ε during training, and an annealing schedule can progressively remove smoothing to recover exact Top-k inference while retaining the training benefits of a smoother loss landscape.

## Results

On WikiText-103, SmoothSMoE reduces validation/test PPL from 33.79/35.52 (SMoE) to 32.72/34.35, and on Attacked WikiText-103 from 42.21/44.18 to 40.99/42.85. On GLUE (k=2), SmoothSMoE achieves an average score of 81.65 vs. 81.17 for SMoE and 81.18 for ReMoE, with the largest gain on MRPC (+0.98%). On DomainBed, SmoothGMoE reaches 67.4% average accuracy vs. 66.84% for GMoE, with a notable 2.1% improvement on TerraInc. On a 5.6B-parameter VLM, SmoothSMoE achieves 55.07 average across 10 benchmarks vs. 54.53 for baseline SMoE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SmoothSMoE consistently outperforms the standard Top-k SMoE baseline (Shazeer et al., 2017) across all evaluated tasks: it reduces WikiText-103 test PPL by 1.17 (35.52→34.35) and Attacked WikiText-103 test PPL by 1.33 (44.18→42.85), and improves GLUE average by 0.48 points (81.17→81.65 at k=2). Against ReMoE (Wang et al., 2025), which replaces Top-k with ReLU-based gating, SmoothSMoE wins on every WikiText-103 metric (e.g., 34.35 vs. 35.35 test PPL) and on GLUE average (81.65 vs. 81.18), while requiring no retraining of the gating from scratch. On DomainBed, SmoothGMoE (67.4% avg) beats GMoE (66.84%), Soft MoE (65.66%), and Expert Choice routing (66.74%), with the largest margin on TerraInc (47.5% vs. 45.4% for GMoE). The annealed variant, which removes smoothing at inference, still improves over vanilla SMoE (34.62 vs. 35.52 test PPL), confirming that boundary smoothing benefits training dynamics even when the final model uses hard Top-k routing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SMoE lineage from Shazeer et al. (2017) and Switch Transformers (Fedus et al., 2022) by providing the first rigorous geometric and stochastic analysis of the discontinuities inherent to Top-k routing. It departs from prior continuous-routing approaches—SMEAR (expert merging), Soft MoE (token mixing), and ReMoE (ReLU gating)—by showing that a simple log-smoothstep perturbation applied only within an ℓ∞,ε-thickening of the discontinuity set can enforce continuity without retraining the gate or compromising autoregressive causality. The geometric classification of discontinuities by order and the diffusion-based hitting-time analysis are entirely new; prior work acknowledged the existence of discontinuities but offered no systematic structural characterization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical framework (lower-order discontinuities dominate in measure; diffusion almost surely hits order-1 boundaries first) provides a principled justification for why localized smoothing near pairwise ties is sufficient, guiding practitioners on where to intervene. The annealing result—that smoothing during training improves final hard Top-k performance even after smoothing is removed at inference—suggests a broader role for boundary regularization in discrete routing optimization. A key limitation is that the stochastic analysis assumes isotropic diffusion, so adversarial or structured perturbations that exploit higher-order discontinuities remain unaddressed; extending the theory to anisotropic or directed noise is an open direction.
