## Problem

State-of-the-art text-to-image diffusion models like FLUX.1-Schnell (17B parameters) deliver high visual quality but require expensive GPU infrastructure or cloud inference, creating a significant barrier for users with limited computational resources. No prior work has comprehensively studied architectural compression of large-scale MMDiT-based models to enable on-device deployment while preserving generation quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260461/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a free-form text prompt, which is encoded by a 330M T5-Large text encoder (replacing the original 5B T5-XXL) and a 120M CLIP-Large encoder. It produces 512×512 or 1024×1024 RGB images via a VAE decoder, generating a 512×512 image in approximately 2.5 seconds end-to-end on a Samsung S25U (Snapdragon 8 Elite). The 1024×1024 variant is obtained by fine-tuning the 512×512 checkpoint with Progressive Token Downsampling while keeping the downsampler/upsampler positions fixed.

## Method

NanoFLUX employs a three-stage progressive distillation pipeline from the 17B FLUX.1-Schnell teacher. First, the 12B MMDiT is compressed to 1.8B through sequential pruning: reducing attention heads (24→16), reducing head dimension (128→96), merging 15 redundant Single-Stream blocks into one based on input-output cosine similarity, and replacing dynamic AdaLN with precomputed Static-LN coefficients (validated by low variance-to-norm ratios). Second, Progressive Token Downsampling inserts ResNet-based down/upsampling modules around intermediate SS blocks so they operate on 1/4-resolution tokens during early timesteps (t > t_thresh=0.5) with hybrid RoPE, while early DS blocks and late SS blocks retain full resolution; a progressive training schedule stabilizes integration. Third, T5-XXL is distilled to T5-Large using a two-stage loss: initial MSE on prompt embeddings, followed by block-wise MSE on prompt hidden states from the first three MMDiT layers (exploiting visual cues from joint attention), with gradient cutoff at a random timestep to avoid unstable high-noise supervision.

## Results

NanoFLUX (2.4B total: 2B DiT + 330M T5-Large) achieves One-IG 42.1, DPG 75.5, GenEval 49.7, and HPSv3 10.41 at 512×512 resolution with 10 inference steps, compared to its 17B FLUX.1-Schnell teacher (One-IG 43.1, DPG 84.3, GenEval 66.0, HPSv3 11.45 at 4 steps). The model generates 512×512 images in approximately 2.5 seconds end-to-end on a Samsung S25U (Snapdragon 8 Elite), with 2.4 s denoising latency for the 1.8B DiT with Progressive Token Downsampling. At 1024×1024 resolution, NanoFLUX attains a lower (better) average benchmark rank of 1.25 versus 1.75 for the similarly sized SANA-Sprint.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against its 17B FLUX.1-Schnell teacher, NanoFLUX (2.4B) trades moderate quality for a 7× size reduction: it scores 1.0 lower on One-IG (42.1 vs 43.1), 8.8 lower on DPG (75.5 vs 84.3), 16.3 lower on GenEval (49.7 vs 66.0), and 1.04 lower on HPSv3 (10.41 vs 11.45). Versus SANA-1.5 (4.2B total), NanoFLUX is roughly half the size but trails on every metric, most notably DPG (75.5 vs 84.3) and GenEval (49.7 vs 65.8), while One-IG and HPSv3 are nearly matched (42.1 vs 42.6; 10.41 vs 10.59). Against SANA-Sprint (4.2B, 2 steps), NanoFLUX wins on DPG (75.5 vs 63.9) and HPSv3 (10.41 vs 10.26) but loses on One-IG (42.1 vs 43.2) and GenEval (49.7 vs 73.0), reflecting SANA-Sprint's strength in compositional reasoning at the cost of prompt fidelity. For text encoder distillation specifically, the paper's block-wise prompt-embedding approach (HPSv3 10.45) outperforms the per-timestep output distillation of Wang et al. 2025 (HPSv3 10.37 at 2.5B and 10.21 at 1.8B), confirming that leveraging visual cues from early MMDiT layers is more effective than supervising only final encoder outputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MMDiT architecture (Esser et al., 2024) and the FLUX.1-Schnell model (BFL, 2025) by systematically identifying and exploiting architectural redundancies—attention head redundancy, head-dimension redundancy, block-level redundancy, and AdaLN coefficient redundancy—through a progressive distillation pipeline. It departs from prior on-device T2I work (MobileDiffusion, SnapFusion, SANA-Sprint) which either operated at much smaller scales or focused solely on step distillation without architectural compression. The Progressive Token Downsampling strategy generalizes U-DiTs (Tian et al., 2024) from small-scale class-conditional DiTs to large-scale MMDiTs with joint text-image attention, and the text encoder distillation method introduces a novel use of visual signals from early MMDiT layers rather than relying on output-level or per-step supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first comprehensive study of compressing a 17B-parameter state-of-the-art T2I model to run on mobile hardware, providing a practical blueprint (progressive head pruning → dimension reduction → block merging → static normalization → token downsampling → text encoder distillation) that could generalize to future large generative models. The finding that 3.2B AdaLN parameters can be replaced by precomputed constants from as few as 2 calibration samples challenges the assumption that adaptive conditioning is essential, though this is demonstrated only on the FLUX architecture. A notable limitation is the significant GenEval drop (66.0 → 49.7), suggesting compositional and multi-object generation suffers disproportionately under compression. Open questions include whether the pipeline generalizes to non-FLUX MMDiT variants, whether the 2.4B model can be further compressed via quantization without additional quality loss, and whether the PTD strategy transfers to video generation.
