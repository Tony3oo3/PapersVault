## Problem

Existing adversarial attacks on safety-aligned LLMs face an inescapable tradeoff: gradient-based methods like GCG achieve high attack success rates but produce high-perplexity, incoherent suffixes easily flagged by filters, while prompt-rewriting methods produce fluent text but alter the semantic intent of the query (jailbreak tax). No prior method simultaneously achieves high ASR, low perplexity, and faithful preservation of the adversary's original malicious intent.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264512/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a malicious intent query and operates in a gray-box setting with access to the target LLM's token logprobs, using a frozen Dream-v0-Instruct 7B discrete diffusion model as a proposal distribution. The adversarial prompt is initialized as 80 <|mask|> tokens (unlike GCG's fixed string) and optimized for up to 800 steps. The output is a coherent 80-token adversarial prompt that forces the victim model to begin its response with a specified target prefix (e.g., 'Sure, here is how to...'), evaluated under no-defense, perplexity-filter, and Llama Guard defense conditions.

## Method

GCD replaces GCG's gradient-based token selection with a discrete diffusion model queried at masked positions to propose top-K semantically coherent candidate tokens, leveraging the diffusion model's full-context conditioning (left and right) to maintain long-range coherence even when the prompt is mostly masked. A one-shot diffusion (OSD) step fills all remaining mask tokens to produce a complete draft, bridging the token-space mismatch between the diffusion model's mask-augmented vocabulary and the autoregressive victim model that cannot process masks. Optimization proceeds via stochastic coordinate descent (subsampling 25% of positions and 10% of candidates per step) with greedy selection minimizing a composite loss of victim NLL, guard-model penalty, and self-perplexity. A two-phase pipeline first optimizes until the victim's greedy continuation matches the target prefix, then scores 512-token continuations with an LLM judge and applies a diversity loss to escape stagnation on evasive continuations, before a final selection step picks the best attack from a pool of at least 8 candidates.

## Results

GCD achieves the highest Attack Success Rate (ASR) across all three victim models: 85.42% on Llama-3-8B-Instruct, 97.92% on Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct, and 100.00% on Mistral-7B-Instruct (HarmBench binary classification). Under a perplexity-based defense (85th-percentile threshold), GCD retains its full ASR on all models, while gradient-based baselines (GCG, GBDA) collapse to 0.00%. Under Llama Guard 3-1B filtering, GCD maintains 87.50–97.92% HB Guard DEF, compared to at most 27.08% for the strongest baseline (Inpainting). On the StrongReject continuous harmfulness score, GCD scores 0.7159 (Llama-3-8B), 0.7789 (Qwen-2.5-7B), and 0.8234 (Mistral-7B), competitive with or exceeding all baselines except AutoDAN on Qwen-2.5-7B (0.8054).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GCD dominates GCG (Zou et al., 2023) across all settings: on Llama-3-8B, GCG achieves 0.00% HB ASR (failing to converge within the 1-hour budget) versus GCD's 85.42%, and GCG drops to 0.00% under both perplexity and guard defenses while GCD retains full performance. GBDA (Guo et al., 2021) similarly fails, scoring 0.00% under perplexity defense on all models and at most 54.17% HB ASR on Mistral-7B without defenses, far below GCD. PAIR (Chao et al., 2023) achieves only 4.17–41.67% HB ASR depending on the victim model, with GCD outperforming it by 44–81 percentage points; PAIR's human-readable prompts survive perplexity filtering but lack the token-level control to reliably elicit target prefixes. AutoDAN (Liu et al., 2023) is the closest competitor, reaching 91.67% and 95.83% HB ASR on Qwen-2.5-7B and Mistral-7B respectively, but GCD still leads by 6.25 and 4.17 points; notably, AutoDAN edges out GCD on the StrongReject score for Qwen-2.5-7B (0.8054 vs. 0.7789), suggesting slightly higher response quality in that specific setting, though GCD wins on the primary ASR metric and on all defense-robustness measures.

## Relation to Prior Work

GCD is a direct architectural modification of GCG (Zou et al., 2023), preserving the iterative coordinate-descent loop, batch candidate evaluation, and greedy selection structure while replacing gradient-based token proposals with a frozen discrete diffusion model (Dream-v0-Instruct) as the proposal distribution. This departs from the gradient-projection paradigm that produces high-perplexity suffixes, and from prompt-rewriting approaches (PAIR, TAP, AutoDAN) that sacrifice fine-grained token-level control for fluency. The one-shot diffusion projection (OSD) step is a novel technical bridge that resolves the token-space mismatch between the diffusion model's mask-augmented vocabulary and the autoregressive victim model, a problem that prior diffusion-based attacks (Lüdke et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2025) left unresolved.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that high attack success rate, low perplexity, and semantic fidelity to the original malicious intent need not be in tension, challenging the prevailing assumption that effective jailbreaks are necessarily high-perplexity statistical anomalies detectable by surface-level filters. This has direct implications for defense design: perplexity-based and guard-model filters, which are widely deployed as first-line defenses, may have limited coverage against semantically coherent adversarial prompts. Key open questions include transferability to proprietary or larger models, robustness under combined multi-layer defenses (rate limiting, human review), and whether the gray-box logprob assumption holds in practice for API-served models.
