## Problem

Robust average-reward RL lacks model-free algorithms with finite-sample guarantees: existing model-based methods require prior knowledge of the robust optimal span H, while model-free approaches either provide only asymptotic convergence or depend on an unknown contraction coefficient that can be arbitrarily close to 1. The core difficulty is that the robust Bellman operator is merely non-expansive (not contractive) under the span semi-norm, contains two coupled unknowns (Q and g*), and involves a nonlinear worst-case support function that makes naive plug-in estimation biased.

## Input / Output

The algorithm operates in the generative model setting, consuming i.i.d. next-state samples drawn from the nominal kernel P at any queried state-action pair, along with the uncertainty set specification (KL or χ² divergence radius ρ). It outputs a stationary ε-optimal robust policy π(s) ∈ Δ(A) for all states s, with the guarantee g*_P(s) − g^π_P(s) ≤ ε holding with probability at least 1−δ. The total sample budget scales as Õ(SAH²ε^(-(2+o(1)))) for KL/χ² sets, where H is the robust optimal bias span, and no prior knowledge of H is required in practice via a doubling trick (PF-RHI).

## Method

RHI solves the robust Bellman equation Q = T_P(Q) − g* by embedding it in the quotient space R^{SA}/~ (identifying vectors differing by a constant), which eliminates the unknown g* and reduces the problem to finding a fixed point of the non-expansive operator T_P. The iteration follows the Halpern scheme [Q_{k+1}] = [(1−β_k)Q_0 + β_k T_P(Q_k)] with β_k = k/(k+2), which converges for non-expansive maps without requiring a contraction coefficient. To estimate T_P from nominal samples, the paper introduces a K-order multi-level Monte Carlo estimator that forms a weighted linear combination of single-level plug-in estimators at geometrically increasing sample sizes N_ℓ = 2^ℓ N_0, with weights chosen to cancel all bias terms up to order 1/N^j (j = 1,…,K), yielding a residual bias of Õ(f(K)/N_0^{K+1}) instead of the O(1/N) of standard MLMC. This bias reduction is the key to achieving the near-optimal ε^(-(2+o(1))) rate: by setting K ≈ √(log(1/ε)/log 2) and N_0 ≈ ε^{-1/(K+1)}, the per-oracle-call cost C_rs becomes exp(Õ(√log(1/ε))) while the oracle bias stays below ε/16, and the overall RHI sample complexity matches the state-of-the-art model-based bound asymptotically.

## Results

The paper establishes that RHI achieves an ε-optimal robust policy with sample complexity Õ(SAH²ε^(-(2+o(1)))) for KL and χ² divergence uncertainty sets, and Õ(SAH²ε^(-2)) for ℓ_p-norm and contamination sets, where S and A are the number of states and actions, and H is the robust optimal span. This asymptotically matches the tightest known model-based bound (Roch et al., 2025) while being fully model-free and requiring no prior knowledge of H. Empirically, on the Garnet G(20,15) problem (20 states, 15 actions), RHI converges to the optimal robust average reward across contamination, ℓ_∞-norm, and ℓ_2-norm uncertainty models, as validated against the RRVI baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RHI outperforms all prior model-free baselines: Xu et al. (2025a,b) achieve Õ(SAt_m²/((1−γ)²ε²)), which depends on the unknown contraction coefficient γ that can be arbitrarily close to 1, making their bound vacuous in practice; RHI eliminates this dependence entirely by using a contraction-free Halpern iteration in the quotient space. Against model-based methods, RHI asymptotically matches Roch et al. (2025) at Õ(SAH²/ε²) but without requiring prior knowledge of H (the robust optimal span), which Roch et al.'s reduction factor γ=1−ε/H explicitly needs. Compared to Grand-Clément & Petrik (2023), whose reduction factor yields exponential sample complexity, RHI provides a polynomial guarantee. Against Chen et al. (2025), RHI avoids the stronger uniformly ergodic assumption and the small-radius requirement on the uncertainty set. The key technical advantage over prior MLMC-based methods (Wang et al., 2023d; 2024g) is the K-order MLMC estimator, which reduces the oracle bias from O(1/N) to Õ(f(K)/N_0^(K+1)), converting what would be an ε^(-3) rate into the near-optimal ε^(-(2+o(1))) rate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Halpern iteration framework (Halpern, 1967; Lee et al., 2025) from non-robust average-reward RL to the distributionally robust setting, where the Bellman operator is non-expansive and nonlinear. It builds on the MLMC estimation paradigm introduced for robust RL (Liu et al., 2022b; Wang et al., 2023d) but departs from prior work by introducing a K-order bias-cancellation scheme that reduces the estimator bias from O(1/N) to O(f(K)/N^{K+1}), which is the key technical ingredient enabling near-optimal sample complexity. The quotient-space formulation for handling the dual unknowns (Q, g*) generalizes the anchoring trick from non-robust AMDPs (Zhang et al., 2021; Lee et al., 2025) to the robust case, while the overall algorithm avoids the reduction-to-discounted-reward approach used by model-based methods (Roch et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2025) that require prior knowledge of the span H.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work closes a notable gap by providing the first finite-sample, model-free algorithm for robust average-reward RL with near-optimal complexity, matching the best model-based bounds asymptotically without requiring knowledge of H. The K-order MLMC estimator is a general technique applicable to any f-divergence uncertainty set with smooth dual objectives, potentially benefiting a broader class of robust RL problems. However, the analysis is restricted to the generative model setting (no exploration), the o(1) sub-polynomial gap in the exponent remains unresolved for model-free settings, and empirical validation is limited to a single small Garnet instance, leaving open questions about practical scalability and performance in online or offline regimes.
