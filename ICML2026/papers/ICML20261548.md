## Problem

Overactuated tendon-driven hands (musculoskeletal or robotic) have more actuators than mechanical degrees of freedom, making naive policy search intractable due to action redundancy. Existing synergy learning methods are either task-specific (demonstration-based or reward-optimized) or lack a formal objective that maximizes control over the body's mechanical degrees of freedom. This paper asks: can a task-agnostic, information-theoretic criterion discover a low-dimensional action manifold that gives an agent maximum control over its joints, independent of any particular task?

## Input / Output

The method consumes a state vector comprising joint angles, joint velocities, and actuator states (muscle activations for MyoHand; motor position/velocity for Adroit), augmented with object states in manipulation tasks. It produces a state-dependent precoder matrix of shape dim(A)×dim(Z) (e.g., 39×15 for MyoHand) that defines a low-dimensional action manifold; the final policy outputs a high-dimensional action vector (muscle activations or tendon forces) by applying this precoder to a latent action sampled from a task-specific base distribution. The controlled variable targeted by the empowerment objective is the next joint angular velocity, not the full state, deliberately abstracting away actuator-level dynamics.

## Method

JoSE defines the objective as the channel capacity (max mutual information over source distributions) between low-dimensional latent actions and next joint velocities, with the communication channel composed of a learnable state-dependent precoder, the system transition kernel, and a joint-velocity decoder. Under a control-affine Gaussian dynamics model p(y_{t+1}|s_t,a_t)=N(f(s_t)+G(s_t)a_t, Q(s_t)), the optimal precoder is derived in closed form as the right singular vectors of the noise-whitened input matrix G̃=Q^{-1/2}G, with power allocated via water-filling—directly analogous to MIMO precoding. A neural network ψ maps the state to dynamics parameters (f, G, Q), with MC dropout inducing an epistemic posterior over the precoder that drives Thompson-sampling-like exploration early in training. The resulting task-agnostic manifold is composed with a task-specific SAC base distribution over latent actions, so that policy improvement only updates the low-dimensional base distribution while the manifold is updated via MLE on the replay buffer, decoupling coordination learning from task learning.

## Results

JoSEPi is evaluated on contact-rich in-hand manipulation tasks (BaodingBalls, DieReorient, KeyTurn, PenTwirl) using the solved-fraction metric (ratio of solved timesteps to episode length). JoSEPi consistently outperforms all baselines across every task on both the MyoHand (23 DOF, 39 muscle-tendon units) and the Adroit hand (25 DOF, 46 tendons), achieving faster convergence and higher asymptotic performance. On KeyTurn, where two baselines approach JoSEPi's final solved fraction, JoSEPi still converges approximately 2–3× faster. Under sparse-reward conditions (binary reward of 1 only in solved states), JoSEPi substantially outperforms all model-free baselines in both learning speed and final performance, and achieves the best zero-shot generalization to 1000 unseen objects in both in-domain and out-of-domain geometric configurations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

JoSEPi outperforms all three baseline families across every experimental setting. Against **SAC** (Haarnoja et al., 2018), JoSEPi achieves faster learning and higher asymptotic solved fraction on all four MyoHand tasks and all four Adroit hand tasks, with the gap most pronounced on the high-dimensional coordination tasks (BaodingBalls, PenTwirl). Against **Lattice** (Chiappa et al., 2023, both SAC and recurrent-PPO variants), JoSEPi dominates on all MyoHand tasks; the advantage is especially large under sparse rewards, where Lattice's model-free full-covariance policy struggles to learn meaningful actuator correlations from infrequent reward signals. Against **SAR** (Berg et al., 2023) and its state-dependent variant SD-SAR, JoSEPi wins on the decoupled (play-then-task) protocol: SAR's ICA/PCA subspace and SD-SAR's reconstruction-optimized subspace are both task-specific to the play phase, whereas JoSE's task-agnostic manifold transfers more effectively to the harder downstream Reorient100 task and to zero-shot generalization on unseen objects. The comparison with SD-SAR is particularly informative because it shares JoSEPi's state-dependent parameterization but replaces the JoSE objective with a reconstruction loss, confirming that the performance gain is driven by the empowerment objective rather than representational capacity alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate threads: (1) muscle synergy learning in motor control, (2) empowerment as an information-theoretic measure of agent-environment control, and (3) optimal precoding from MIMO communication theory. It departs from prior synergy methods (demonstration-based, task-optimized, and task-agnostic) by deriving a task-agnostic, state-dependent action manifold directly from learned dynamics via a closed-form optimal precoder, rather than from demonstrations, reward signals, or analytical controllability conditions. The key conceptual departure from standard empowerment is targeting joint angular velocities (the mechanical degrees of freedom) rather than the full state, exploiting the functional asymmetry between actuators and joints in overactuated tendon-driven systems. The compositional policy architecture—separating a task-agnostic manifold (learned via JoSE maximization) from a task-specific base distribution (learned via SAC)—is novel relative to prior action-abstraction approaches that use bisimulation or MDP homomorphisms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, sample-efficient (linear in dim(Y)·dim(A) vs. exponential for variational MI) solution to the action-redundancy problem that plagues RL in overactuated systems, with the closed-form precoder eliminating the need for expensive mutual information estimation. Its robustness under sparse rewards—where dynamics-based manifold discovery provides a dense learning signal while reward-based baselines fail—offers a practical path to specifying manipulation goals without hand-crafted reward functions. Open questions include whether the one-step empowerment objective can be extended to multi-step spatiotemporal manifolds, whether co-contraction and stiffness control emerge naturally under JoSE optimization, and how the method performs on physical hardware with nonlinear tendon friction and elasticity. The control-affine and additive-noise assumptions, while well-motivated by the three-stage actuation mechanics, limit applicability to systems with strong action-dependent nonlinearities.
