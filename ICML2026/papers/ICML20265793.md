## Problem

While diffusion models can generate photorealistic images, it remains unclear which specific characteristics of synthetic data (e.g., scene composition, instance fidelity) actually drive downstream segmentation performance. Additionally, even when conditioning on ground-truth masks via ControlNet, generated images frequently exhibit local semantic inconsistencies, making rigid pixel-wise supervision unreliable and necessitating a more robust labeling strategy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265793/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes RGB images at task-specific resolutions (1024×1024 for Cityscapes, 640×640 for COCO and ADE20K), combining a small set of real images with ground-truth masks and a larger pool of unlabeled synthetic images generated by Flux/Flux-WLF diffusion models. It produces pixel-level semantic segmentation masks, operating within both dense pixel-based (DPT) and sparse query-based (Mask2Former) architectures, with the OT-based pseudo-labeling applied per mini-batch during semi-supervised training.

## Method

SENSE formulates synthetic-data label assignment as an entropy-regularized Optimal Transport problem, where the transport cost is the negative log-probability predicted by the segmentation model and the solution is obtained via the Sinkhorn-Knopp algorithm. Rather than using empirical marginals (which amplify synthetic-real distribution shift), SENSE employs uniform marginal priors that implicitly re-weight classes to alleviate long-tail imbalance. For pixel-based models, the OT plan directly provides soft pseudo-labels weighted by a confidence threshold; for query-based transformers, SENSE projects set-based query predictions into dense pixel space to compute transport costs, then maps the rectified targets back via Hungarian bipartite matching. This design makes SENSE model-agnostic, seamlessly integrating with DPT and Mask2Former while mitigating confirmation bias from generative instability.

## Results

SENSE delivers consistent mIoU gains across Cityscapes, COCO, and ADE20K when augmenting real training data with synthetic images from Flux/Flux-WLF. On Cityscapes, DPT (DINOv2-S/14) improves from 78.11 to 80.65 mIoU (+2.54) in single-scale, while Mask2Former (DINOv3-L/16) gains +1.54 s.s. and +1.59 m.s. On ADE20K, Mask2Former reaches 59.09 mIoU (s.s.) and 60.81 (m.s.), establishing a new state-of-the-art among scalable synthetic-data segmentation methods. Gains persist across backbone scales (DINOv2-S, DINOv2-B, DINOv3-L) and both pixel-based (DPT) and query-based (Mask2Former) architectures, with no additional inference cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ADE20K, SENSE (59.09 mIoU s.s., 60.81 m.s.) outperforms FreeMask (56.4 s.s.) by 2.69 points and SegGen (57.4 s.s., 58.7 m.s.) by 1.69 and 2.11 points respectively, while using substantially less synthetic data (2× real data volume vs. 20× for FreeMask and 50× for SegGen). This efficiency gap highlights that SENSE's OT-based label assignment extracts more signal per synthetic image rather than relying on brute-force data volume. The paper also reports internal ablations showing that removing the OT module costs 1.15 mIoU on Cityscapes (79.50 → 80.65), 0.56 on COCO (62.74 → 63.30), and 0.61 on ADE20K (49.62 → 50.23), confirming that the global transport optimization is the key differentiator over naive pseudo-labeling. Class-wise IoU analysis on Cityscapes further shows that the gains are concentrated on long-tail categories (fence, pole) where real data is scarce, rather than on already-saturated classes like road or sky.

## Relation to Prior Work

SENSE extends the optimal transport (OT) framework for label assignment—originally developed for semi-supervised classification (SLA, Tai et al. 2021) and pixel-wise segmentation (OTAMatch, Zhang et al. 2024)—to the setting of synthetic data augmentation and, critically, to query-based architectures like Mask2Former, which prior OT methods could not handle due to their reliance on dense spatial correspondence. On the synthetic data side, the paper departs from prior generative segmentation approaches (FreeMask, SegGen, DatasetDM) that either operated on limited category subsets or required 20×–50× synthetic data, by showing that only 2× synthetic images suffice when the data is characterized by dense scene composition and fine instance fidelity. The empirical analysis in Section 3, which systematically isolates composition and fidelity as the two governing factors, is a novel contribution not present in prior synthetic-data-for-segmentation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The systematic identification of dense scene composition and fine instance fidelity as the two critical factors for synthetic data utility provides actionable guidance for practitioners generating training data for segmentation, moving beyond the 'more synthetic data is better' heuristic. The OT-based label assignment offers a principled, convex-optimization alternative to ad-hoc pseudo-labeling that is robust to generative instability, and its extension to query-based transformers opens a path for applying global structural constraints in modern set-prediction architectures. Open questions include whether an end-to-end feedback loop between the generative model and the segmentation network could further close the synthetic–real gap, and whether the uniform marginal prior in the OT formulation is optimal or whether learned marginals could better handle long-tail class distributions. The paper also acknowledges that generative priors may inherit biases from their training data, a concern that remains unresolved.
