## Problem

Existing audio-visual benchmarks for Omni-MLLMs are fragmented across modality-specific or task-specific evaluations, lacking a unified framework that mirrors the hierarchical structure of human cognition (perception → understanding → reasoning). Moreover, no prior benchmark probes whether models generalize beyond their semantically rich training distributions to handle unfamiliar, low-semantic stimuli, leaving a critical gap in assessing true human-like audio-visual intelligence.

## Input / Output

Each of the 5,864 samples pairs an audio clip, video clip, or image with a text prompt, and the expected output varies by task: bounding boxes for localization (AVL, AVLG), multi-label class lists for multi-instance classification (AMIC, VMIC), retrieval indices for cross-modal matching (AVR, VAR), free-text captions for AVC, binary yes/no judgments for hallucination detection (AVH, VAH) and matching (AVM), and open-ended or MCQ answers for the remaining tasks. Evaluation produces per-task scores normalized to percentages, stage-level averages, and a four-level taxonomy score (L1–L4) that progressively penalizes modality imbalance, ungrounded reasoning, and unfamiliar-domain fragility.

## Method

AVI-Bench organizes 14 tasks into four cognitively grounded stages—Perception, Understanding, Reasoning, and Primitive Sensation (AVI-Bench-PriSe)—with deliberate audio/visual balance at each stage and 62% manually constructed samples. The key analytical contribution is a four-level taxonomy: L1 (Task-Adaptive) averages raw task scores; L2 (Modality-Adaptive) applies a multiplicative penalty proportional to the relative audio-visual performance gap; L3 (Stage-Adaptive) first headroom-normalizes each task score by its chance baseline, then penalizes reasoning that exceeds its weakest perceptual prerequisite via a bottleneck discrepancy term; L4 (Domain-Adaptive) takes the harmonic mean of the familiar-domain (L3) and unfamiliar-domain (PriSe) scores, so that near-zero performance on either domain collapses the final score. For the Primitive Sensation stage, a double-confirmation mechanism requires the model to answer both the original question and a verification query correctly, reducing false positives from hallucination on low-semantic stimuli.

## Results

Across 28 Omni-MLLMs evaluated on AVI-Bench (5,864 samples, 14 tasks, 9 metrics), Gemini-2.5-Pro achieves the highest overall average of 57.21%, followed by Gemini-2.5-Flash (46.02%) and Gemini-2.0-Flash (44.97%). Grounding tasks remain the most challenging: even Gemini-2.5-Pro scores only 39.13% on AVL and 35.08% on AVLG, and no open-source model exceeds 25% on either. Human participants average 92.6% across all tasks, revealing a 35-point gap to the best model, with the largest disparity on the AVSQA primitive-sensation task (16.50% vs. 90.55%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

As a benchmark paper, AVI-Bench's primary comparison is across 28 Omni-MLLMs rather than a single proposed method. Gemini-2.5-Pro leads all models by a wide margin (57.21% avg), outperforming GPT-4o (40.68%) by 16.53 points and the best open-source model Qwen2.5-Omni (41.33%) by 15.88 points. Under the proposed four-level taxonomy (Table 5), rankings shift dramatically: GPT-4o's near-zero ASQA score (0.40%) causes its Level-4 domain-adaptive score to collapse to 0.055, dropping it from rank 6 (L1) to rank 23 (L4), while Gemini-1.5-Pro (L4 = 23.28) overtakes it. Among open-source models, Qwen2.5-Omni (L4 = 25.89) and Baichuan-Omni (L4 = 24.10) lead, but no open-source model exceeds 25% on grounding tasks (AVL/AVLG). The primitive-sensation stage exposes the largest model–human gap: the relative performance drop from reasoning to primitive sensation ranges from 47.6% (Gemini-2.5-Pro) to 82.7% (Video-Salmonn), indicating that current models rely heavily on semantic pattern-matching rather than genuine low-level sensory processing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior audio-visual benchmarks (AVQA, AV-Odyssey, OmniBench, DailyOmni, AVHBench) that evaluate task diversity without a unifying cognitive structure. It repurposes well-studied tasks (localization, retrieval, captioning, hallucination detection) but reorganizes them into a cognitively grounded hierarchy of Perception → Understanding → Reasoning, and introduces a genuinely new Primitive Sensation stage (AVI-Bench-PriSe) that probes low-semantic, unfamiliar-domain stimuli. The four-level taxonomy (Task-, Modality-, Stage-, Domain-Adaptive) has no direct precedent in prior benchmarking literature, representing a new diagnostic framework rather than a new task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that even the strongest model (Gemini-2.5-Pro) achieves only 57.2% versus 92.6% for humans, with grounding tasks (AVL, AVLG) remaining below 40% for all models and audio-dominant tasks consistently lagging visual ones. The four-level taxonomy dramatically reshapes model rankings (e.g., GPT-4o drops from rank 6 to 19 at Level 4), exposing weaknesses invisible in aggregate scores. Open questions include whether the primitive sensation gap (47–83% below reasoning) reflects a fundamental architectural limitation or a training-data coverage issue, and whether improved audio encoders can close the modality imbalance without sacrificing visual performance.
