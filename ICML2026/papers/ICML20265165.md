## Problem

Building real-time omni-modal assistants that handle image/video, audio/speech, and text within multi-turn dialogues typically requires costly end-to-end multimodal alignment or backbone expansion with modality-specific encoders, both of which incur substantial data and compute costs and make component upgrades (e.g., swapping an ASR front-end or upgrading a VLM) trigger retraining. The paper asks whether a responsive, controllable, and modularly upgradable omni-modal assistant can be assembled from off-the-shelf experts with no gradient-based training for the integration layer itself.

## Input / Output

Stage 1 normalizes heterogeneous inputs (image/video clips, microphone audio streams, text queries) into a consolidated text query q_t and a set of evidence keys K_t; the controller and generator never access raw multimodal payloads, only these keys and textualized evidence. Video is sampled at 20 FPS with a cap of 20 frames per batch, and audio is segmented by VAD before ASR transcription. The system produces a text response o_t^text that is segmented into speakable chunks (7–15 words each) for parallel TTS synthesis and strictly ordered playback, with full-duplex barge-in support that cancels in-flight jobs on user interruption.

## Method

The framework comprises three tightly coupled components operating under a fixed protocol with no gradient-based training for integration. (C1) A Qwen2.5-14B controller emits a closed-vocabulary sequence of control tokens (e.g., [S.need_vision], [S.need_audio], [S.stop]) that a deterministic router validates against a static token-to-expert mapping E specifying target endpoints, I/O schemas, and runtime policies (timeouts, cancellability); invalid tokens are rejected with a safe text-only fallback. (C2) An evidence-keyed, text-centric cross-modal memory stores expert outputs as compact structured records indexed by keys of the form ⟨modality, segment_id, SHA-1 checksum⟩; a deterministic cache-or-call rule reuses a record if the key already exists in memory, otherwise invokes the expert, avoiding ambiguous semantic-similarity matching. (C3) A unified interaction layer executes routing and memory decisions as ordered streaming I/O, where [S.stop] triggers cancellation of pending TTS and cancellable expert jobs, clears playback buffers, and separates ephemeral state from committed memory to guarantee interruption consistency. The protocol decouples orchestration from expert implementations, so swapping or upgrading experts (e.g., from Qwen2.5-VL-32B/72B to Qwen3-VL-32B/235B) improves accuracy without any retraining of the controller or routing logic.

## Results

The proposed training-free orchestration system (fixed 14B Qwen2.5-14B controller + routed visual experts averaging 53.8B active parameters) achieves 69.37% on MMStar, 70.04% on MMMU, 65.58% on Video-MME, 88.54% on MMBench-EN, and 44.07% on WorldSense, with an end-to-end inference time of 3.2 s. Routing and orchestration overhead stays below 12% of total compute latency (average 10.36%), and evidence-keyed memory reduces redundant expert invocations by approximately 49% in multi-turn dialogues. The system is competitive with natively trained omni-models (e.g., Qwen2.5-Omni, VITA 1.5) on most benchmarks while requiring no joint end-to-end training for integration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against open-weight VLMs, the orchestration system outperforms Qwen2.5-VL-72B on MMBench-EN (88.54 vs. 86.61), MMStar (69.37 vs. 68.22), and LVBench (50.27 vs. 47.30), while trailing slightly on MME (1922 vs. 1980) and Video-MME (65.58 vs. 65.74). Versus jointly trained omni-models, it surpasses Qwen2.5-Omni on MMBench-EN (88.54 vs. 81.80), MMStar (69.37 vs. 64.00), MMMU (70.04 vs. 59.20), and Video-MME (65.58 vs. 64.30) but falls short on MME (1922 vs. 2340) and WorldSense (44.1 vs. 45.4); it clearly beats VITA 1.5 on MMBench-EN (88.54 vs. 71.80), MMStar (69.37 vs. 46.40), Video-MME (65.58 vs. 59.20), and WorldSense (44.1 vs. 36.9). Against commercial APIs, the system exceeds GPT-4o on MMBench-EN (88.54 vs. 83.10), MMStar (69.37 vs. 64.70), MMMU (70.04 vs. 59.20), LVBench (50.27 vs. 34.70), and WorldSense (44.1 vs. 42.6), but lags on Video-MME (65.58 vs. 71.90) and MME (1922 vs. 2310); it also trails Gemini-1.5-Pro on Video-MME (65.58 vs. 75.00) and WorldSense (44.1 vs. 48.0) while leading on LVBench (50.27 vs. 33.10). The expert-swapping ablation (Table 2) confirms that upgrading the visual expert pair from Qwen2.5-VL-32B/72B to Qwen3-VL-32B/235B-A22B lifts Video-MME accuracy from 65.58% to 72.31% without any controller retraining, validating the modular extensibility claim.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of jointly training omni-modal models (VITA, Qwen2.5-Omni, M2-omni) or augmenting LLM backbones with modality-specific encoders (LLaVA, Flamingo, MiniGPT-4), instead repurposing the LLM-as-controller idea from training-free tool-use agents (HuggingGPT, ViperGPT, ReAct) but constraining it to a closed-vocabulary control-token protocol with deterministic router-side validation. It also draws on cross-modal memory and caching ideas from agent literature but applies them to multimodal evidence reuse via exact evidence-key matching (image hash, video chunk ID) rather than semantic similarity. Unlike multi-agent frameworks (AutoGen, CrewAI) that rely on open-ended negotiation, the paper uses a single controller with a static token-to-expert mapping, trading flexibility for auditable, replayable routing traces and bounded latency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that component upgrades (swapping ASR, vision, or TTS modules) no longer trigger retraining, which directly addresses a major deployment bottleneck for omni-modal systems. The <12% routing overhead and 49% reduction in redundant expert calls make the approach viable for real-time interaction, and the expert-swapping ablation (65.58% → 72.31% on Video-MME with zero retraining) validates the modularity claim. Key limitations remain: sparse frame sampling degrades fine-grained temporal counting (23% on action counting in WorldSense), routing ambiguity in edge cases (~0.8–0.9% failure rate), and the system still depends on large off-the-shelf experts (up to 72B) for raw capability. Open questions include whether higher-resolution evidence retention, specialized temporal experts, or lightweight routing refinement can close the gap to dense omni models without sacrificing the training-free property.
