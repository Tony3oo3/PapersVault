## Problem

Standard phase-coupling metrics like PLV are restricted to pairwise comparisons and cannot distinguish direct from indirect interactions, while the Torus Graph (TG) model—though it captures multivariate conditional independence over phases—scales as O(d⁶) in its score-matching inference, capping practical use at ~100 variables. Modern multielectrode recordings produce thousands of phase variables (channels × frequency bins), and brain oscillations are both dynamic (state-dependent) and directional, none of which static, undirected TGs can address.

## Input / Output

The method consumes instantaneous phase angles (in [0, 2π)) extracted from multichannel LFP or EEG recordings via continuous wavelet transform at multiple center frequencies; in the primary application this yields 62 channels × 30 frequencies = 1,860 phase variables per timepoint. Outputs include sparse TG parameter matrices encoding conditional independence structure, TG-HMM hidden-state trajectories with per-state TG parameters, and multivariate transfer entropy estimates between channel pairs conditioned on all remaining channels, from which directed interaction graphs and edge-asymmetry scores are derived.

## Method

The key technical contribution is a stochastic score matching procedure that exploits the fact that each TG sufficient statistic depends on at most two phase variables, so the Jacobian is sparse (Θ(d²) nonzeros) and the score-matching gradient can be computed in O(d²) per iteration via a vector-Jacobian product (reverse-mode autodiff on φᵀS(x)), replacing the O(d⁶) exact linear solve and O(d⁴) memory requirement. On this scalable foundation, a TG-HMM is fitted with an approximate EM algorithm whose M-step uses a discriminative logistic-regression surrogate (with free log-normalizer parameters) to avoid intractable TG normalizing constants while approximately matching the exact M-step stationary conditions. An autoregressive TG (AR-TG) parameterizes conditional TG natural parameters linearly in cos/sin embeddings of lagged observations, enabling bivariate transfer entropy as a difference of cross-entropies and multivariate transfer entropy via a single full autoregressive model combined with a Gaussian imputation model for marginalizing out conditioning variables.

## Results

Stochastic score matching reduces per-iteration cost from O(d⁶) to O(d²), enabling TG inference on d=1,860 phase variables (62 channels × 30 frequencies) from mouse LFPs, where exact methods fail at d≈100 due to memory limits. On synthetic data, stochastic SM matches exact SM parameter recovery at low dimensions and extends inference to thousands of variables (310 s at d=1024 vs. out-of-memory for exact). For transfer entropy estimation, AR-TGs more reliably identify causal direction than Kraskov, KDE, and kNN estimators, and maintain accurate multivariate causal edge recovery across a wider range of dimensions and sparsities than multivariate Granger causality (which times out at 30 hours past d=64).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against exact score matching (Klein et al., 2020), stochastic SM matches parameter recovery accuracy at low dimensions (R² comparable at d=4–64) but enables inference at d=1,860 where exact methods exhaust 24 GB VRAM around d≈120; at d=1024, stochastic SM runs in 310 s while exact SM is infeasible. Against multivariate Granger causality (statsmodels VAR), AR-TGs maintain accurate causal edge recovery across a much wider range of channel counts and sparsity levels, whereas Granger causality degrades rapidly with dimension and hits a 30-hour timeout past d=64. Against alternative transfer entropy estimators (Kraskov/IDTxl, KDE/infomeasure, Kozachenko-Leonenko kNN/infomeasure), AR-TGs more reliably identify the true direction of interaction in bivariate causal discovery (Figure 4B), with the additional finding that score-matching-fitted AR-TGs outperform MLE-fitted AR-TGs in this setting. Against pairwise PLV (Lachaux et al., 1999), the TG-HMM reveals that spindle-associated coupling changes are sparse and spatially specific in the conditional independence structure, whereas PLV shows diffuse, widespread synchrony increases that largely reflect indirect interactions explained away by the full multivariate model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Torus Graph (TG) framework of Klein et al. (2020), which introduced a principled exponential-family distribution over phases with conditional independence structure but was limited to ~100 variables due to O(d⁶) exact score matching. The key methodological departure is replacing the closed-form linear solve with stochastic score matching via vector-Jacobian products, reducing per-iteration cost to O(d²) and memory to O(d²), drawing on the general score matching framework of Hyvärinen (2005). Building on this scalable foundation, the paper adds two extensions previously inaccessible to TGs: a hidden Markov model variant for state-dependent coupling and an autoregressive variant for transfer entropy estimation. The work also positions itself against classical pairwise circular statistics (PLV, coherence) and Kuramoto-type synchronization models, arguing that multivariate conditional independence modeling is necessary to distinguish direct from indirect phase interactions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By scaling TG inference from ~100 to 1,860 phase variables, this work makes large-scale multivariate phase analysis practically feasible for modern multielectrode recordings, revealing that much of the diffuse synchrony captured by PLV during sleep spindles is indirect interaction explained away by the full conditional structure. The directed motifs identified by AR-TGs (e.g., prelimbic → striatum, VTA → SNr) are described as not straightforward replications of existing connectivity findings, suggesting testable hypotheses about state-dependent information routing. Key limitations include the univariate target restriction for transfer entropy, the approximate (discriminative) M-step in the TG-HMM whose theoretical properties remain unproven, and the interpretability challenge of TG parameters spanning multiple frequencies. Open questions include extending the discriminative M-step to other exponential families, developing scalable TG marginalization, and clarifying the neuroscientific interpretation of cross-frequency coupling parameters.
