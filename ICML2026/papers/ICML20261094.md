## Problem

Time series foundation models pretrained on heterogeneous multi-domain datasets face substantial spectral discrepancies—different domains exhibit markedly different power spectral density (PSD) distributions that slow convergence and hinder the formation of unified, transferable representations. Existing approaches address domain diversity through architectural modularization or capacity-based specialization, but none explicitly reconcile the underlying second-order temporal structure differences across datasets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261094/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

Olivia takes univariate time series segments of length T=512 (also 1024 or 1536) as input, which are patchified into L tokens of embedding dimension d. It produces future forecasts at multiple horizons (τ ∈ {96, 192, 336, 720}) under zero-shot, few-shot, and full-shot settings. A key architectural detail is that the raw input passes through the Harmonizer's Aligner (an orthogonal temporal reparameterization) before patchification and encoding, and the decoder output passes through the Restorer (the inverse orthogonal map) before producing the final forecast in the original temporal domain.

## Method

The core mechanism is the Harmonizer, which applies a learnable orthogonal matrix Q (parameterized as a product of K Householder reflections to guarantee orthogonality by construction) to reorganize second-order temporal correlations into a shared subspace, implicitly harmonizing PSDs across datasets without direct divergence minimization. Proposition 1 proves this yields a block-diagonal moment matrix separating shared dynamics from dataset-specific variations. Building on this, HarmonicAttention replaces dense L×L self-attention with a three-stage resonator bottleneck: tokens aggregate into M compact resonators (M≪L), interact via M×M cross-resonator attention, and project back—reducing complexity from O(L²P) to O(LMP + M²P). Proposition 2 justifies this by showing the token Gram matrix decomposes into a low-rank principal term (rank ≤ dr) plus a bounded residual. The full Olivia model is an encoder-decoder HarmonicFormer pretrained via reconstruction loss and tuned with a joint forecasting-plus-reconstruction objective, achieving state-of-the-art zero-shot and full-shot forecasting with only 5.1M parameters.

## Results

On the TSLib benchmark (zero-shot, T=512), Olivia achieves the best MSE and MAE on 10 out of 12 dataset-metric pairs, including Electricity (MSE 0.188, MAE 0.285) and Traffic (MSE 0.458, MAE 0.330). On GluonTS datasets, Olivia reduces NRMSE by 32.0% over SEMPO on elecdemand (0.066 vs. 0.097) and by over 86% over Time-MoE on m4_hourly (0.179 vs. 5.819). Olivia also maintains the most compact parameter footprint (5.1M) among all compared foundation models while achieving the lowest MSE on ETTh1 (0.399), with inference time of 43s—orders of magnitude faster than Time-MoE (4050s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Olivia consistently outperforms the lightweight encoder-decoder baseline SEMPO (NeurIPS'25) across nearly all TSLib datasets, with the largest gap on Electricity (MSE 0.188 vs. 0.196) and Traffic (0.458 vs. 0.466), though SEMPO edges out Olivia on ETTm2 (0.289 vs. 0.291 MSE). Against the large-scale Time-MoE family (ICLR'25), Olivia achieves an average MSE reduction of 26.3% and MAE reduction of 14.6%, with particularly dramatic gains on GluonTS datasets (e.g., m4_hourly NRMSE 0.179 vs. 5.819). Olivia also surpasses Timer (ICML'24) by 12.4% MSE on ETTh1 (0.399 vs. 0.451) and Moirai (ICML'24) by 7.8% MSE on ETTh1 (0.399 vs. 0.433 for Moirai_B). Compared to Chronos (TMLR'24) and TimesFM (ICML'24), Olivia shows the most substantial margins, reducing ETTh1 MSE from 0.524 (Chronos_B) and 0.489 (TimesFM) to 0.399. In terms of efficiency, Olivia (5.1M params, 43s inference) is 22× smaller and 94× faster than Time-MoE_B (113M, 4050s) while delivering better accuracy, though it is slower than SEMPO (8s) due to the Householder-based Harmonizer overhead.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the time series foundation model line (MOMENT, MOIRAI, Chronos, Time-MoE, SEMPO, Sundial) by introducing a principled spectral-domain harmonization mechanism. While prior domain-generalization approaches (MOIRAI's frequency-aware patching, ROSE's spectral masking, Time-MoE's sparse routing) address cross-domain diversity through architectural modularization or capacity-based specialization, Olivia departs by explicitly reorganizing second-order temporal correlations via a shared orthogonal transformation (parameterized with Householder reflections) to align PSDs across datasets. The HarmonicAttention mechanism, which mediates token interactions through a compact set of resonators in a low-dimensional subspace, is motivated by a theoretical decomposition (Proposition 2) showing that PSD-aligned representations admit a dominant low-rank Gram structure, distinguishing it from standard full, linear, or Nyström attention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The PSD-harmonization framework offers a theoretically grounded, domain-agnostic mechanism for reducing cross-dataset spectral discrepancies during large-scale pretraining, which is a core bottleneck for time series foundation models. The 5.1M-parameter model consistently outperforming much larger competitors (Time-MoE at 113M+, Sundial at 128M) on zero-shot benchmarks suggests that structural alignment of temporal representations can substitute for raw model capacity. Key limitations include the inference overhead from sequential Householder reflections and the assumption that a single shared orthogonal transformation suffices for all domains; developing more efficient orthogonal parameterizations and validating the approach on multivariate and irregularly-sampled settings remain open.
