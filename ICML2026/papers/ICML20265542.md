## Problem

Current MLLMs lack the ability to understand and reason about the evolution of 3D space over time (4D spatiotemporal intelligence) from purely visual inputs. Existing spatial reasoning methods are constrained to static scenes with immobile objects, and the few 4D benchmarks available rely on manual annotation at benchmark scale (~2k samples), making them insufficient for training. This paper addresses both the data scarcity and the absence of specialized 4D-aware training strategies.

## Input / Output

The model takes up to 32 RGB video frames and a text question as input, with no auxiliary depth maps, point clouds, or spatial encoders required at inference. The output is a structured ST-CoT reasoning trajectory that explicitly reports 3D Object Center and Camera Center coordinates (as bracketed arrays) at the start and end frames, followed by a final multiple-choice answer. Training data is derived from stereoscopic video (Stereo4D) to obtain metric 3D ground truth, but the model itself operates on monocular RGB only.

## Method

MLLM-4D uses a two-stage post-training pipeline on a standard MLLM backbone (Qwen3-VL-8B or Qwen2.5-VL-7B) with LoRA, requiring no architectural modifications. Stage 1 performs SFT on 2M QA pairs (MLLM4D-2M) generated by an automated pipeline that extracts per-frame camera poses, object-level 3D point clouds, and semantic descriptions from stereo videos, then computes ground-truth spatiotemporal relations via physics-based formulations. Stage 2 first cold-start aligns the model to a five-step Spatiotemporal Chain-of-Thought (ST-CoT) format, then applies GRPO reinforcement learning on 30k samples (MLLM4D-R1-30k) with a composite reward combining accuracy, format compliance, and a novel ST-reward that penalizes hallucinated motion by computing exponential-decay MEE between predicted and ground-truth 3D camera/object coordinates at temporal anchors.

## Results

On MLLM4D-Bench, MLLM-4D (Qwen3-VL-8B) achieves a state-of-the-art average of 72.7%, surpassing the strongest proprietary model Gemini 2.5 Pro (46.6%) by 26.1 points and the best 3D spatial reasoning model VG-LLM (56.3%) by 16.4 points. On the out-of-distribution VLM4D benchmark, MLLM-4D (Qwen3-VL-8B) reaches 61.0% overall accuracy, outperforming VG-LLM (46.5%) and VLM-3R (33.9%) by 14.5 and 27.1 points respectively, though slightly trailing Gemini 2.5 Pro (62.0%). Across heterogeneous benchmarks (MMSI-Bench, OSI-Bench, ERQA, Video-MME), MLLM-4D consistently leads all baselines, e.g., 62.4% on Video-MME vs. 59.5% for VG-LLM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MLLM-4D decisively outperforms all proprietary MLLMs on MLLM4D-Bench: GPT-4o scores 44.9% and Gemini 2.5 Pro 46.6%, both trailing MLLM-4D (Qwen3-VL-8B) by over 26 points in average accuracy. Among 3D spatial reasoning models, VG-LLM (56.3%) is the strongest baseline but still falls 16.4 points short of MLLM-4D, while VLM-3R (30.6%) lags by nearly 40 points, confirming that static-scene 3D priors do not transfer to dynamic 4D reasoning. On the VLM4D benchmark, MLLM-4D (Qwen3-VL-8B) achieves 61.0% overall, narrowly behind Gemini 2.5 Pro (62.0%) but ahead of GPT-4o (57.5%), VG-LLM (46.5%), and VLM-3R (33.9%). On heterogeneous benchmarks (Table 4), MLLM-4D leads all baselines including Spatial-MLLM, VG-LLM, and VLM-3R across MMSI-Bench, OSI-Bench, ERQA, and Video-MME, with the largest margin on OSI-Bench (30.2% vs. 27.7% for Qwen3-VL). The consistent gains across both in-distribution and out-of-distribution evaluations indicate that the 4D spatiotemporal representations learned by MLLM-4D are transferable rather than overfit to the training pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of MLLM spatial reasoning work (VG-LLM, VLM-3R, Spatial-MLLM) that relies on auxiliary 3D encoders or reconstruction priors, by demonstrating that a standard MLLM architecture—without any architectural modification—can achieve superior 4D spatiotemporal reasoning when trained on high-quality, physics-grounded data. It builds on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) for the reinforcement fine-tuning stage and repurposes the Stereo4D dataset to construct scalable 4D instructional data, departing from prior 4D benchmarks (VLM4D, STI-Bench) that are limited to manual annotation at benchmark scale (~2k samples). The ST-CoT prompting extends conventional chain-of-thought by forcing explicit 3D coordinate anchoring at temporal boundaries, and the ST-reward introduces a physics-based regularizer absent from prior RL-for-MLLM work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a fundamental gap in MLLMs: the inability to reason about evolving 3D spatial relationships over time from purely 2D video, a capability critical for VR/AR, embodied agents, and autonomous systems. The 2M-scale MLLM4D-2M dataset and MLLM4D-Bench provide the first large-scale foundation for 4D spatiotemporal training and evaluation, moving the field beyond benchmark-scale manual annotations. A key open question is generalization to long-duration video sequences, as the current architecture relies on frame sampling (32 frames max), and whether the physics-grounded ST-reward concept transfers to other structured reasoning domains beyond spatiotemporal tasks.
