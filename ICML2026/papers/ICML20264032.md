## Problem

Existing unmasking strategies for Diffusion Language Models rely on local heuristics such as token confidence or entropy, which fail to capture sequence-level dependencies and the rich semantic information encoded in the model's hidden states. This myopic selection can commit the model to low-quality tokens early in generation, causing error accumulation and incoherent outputs. The paper asks whether the model's internal latent representations can provide complementary guidance signals that go beyond surface-level logit statistics to improve unmasking decisions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt of length L_p and produces a generated text sequence of length L_g (256 tokens for reasoning tasks, 512 for code). Generation follows a semi-autoregressive block-wise schedule: the output is partitioned into contiguous blocks of length L_b=32, and within each block the model performs S=16 denoising steps, unmasking B=2 positions per step. At each step, the frozen DLM produces token logits and last-layer hidden states for all positions, which are consumed jointly by the guidance module and heuristic scorers to determine which masked positions to reveal next.

## Method

LUGS introduces a lightweight Transformer-based guidance module f_θ (1 layer, hidden dim 4096) that ingests the DLM's last-layer hidden states, a binary mask indicator, and a sinusoidal timestep embedding to produce a per-position latent score r_i. This score is combined with a local heuristic (weighted confidence + inverse entropy) via a multiplicative sigmoid gate s_i = h_i · (β + (1−β)σ(r_i)), forming a candidate pool for beam search expansion. Training uses offline trajectory distillation: a wide-beam heuristic decoder (W=10, R=10) generates gold unmasking trajectories on task-specific data, and the guidance module is trained with a margin-based ranking loss that penalizes it when gold positions fall outside the top-K scored candidates. The underlying DLM remains frozen throughout, so only the small guidance head is updated, preserving architectural decoupling while distilling complex search knowledge into a compact scoring mechanism.

## Results

On LLaDA-8B-Instruct, LUGS achieves 55.93% average accuracy on Science & Reasoning tasks and 42.34% average Pass@1 on Code tasks, outperforming unguided Beam Search (54.79% and 37.29%) by 1.14% and 5.05% respectively. The largest single-task gain is +7.31% on HumanEval (42.68% vs. 35.37%). On LLaDA-1.5, LUGS reaches 58.38% (Science & Reasoning) and 42.04% (Code), improving over Beam Search by 1.18% and 2.02% respectively, with consistent gains across all seven benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LUGS is compared against four greedy heuristics (Random, Probability, Entropy, Margin) and an unguided Beam Search baseline. Against the greedy methods, LUGS wins on nearly every task: for example, on LLaDA-8B-Instruct it beats the best greedy method (Margin) by 2.45% on Countdown (26.07 vs. 20.90) and 9.18% on HumanEval (42.68 vs. 32.32). The most informative comparison is against unguided Beam Search (same W=3, R=3, K=5 structure but no latent guidance): LUGS improves the Science & Reasoning average by 1.14% (8B) / 1.18% (1.5) and the Code average by 5.05% (8B) / 2.02% (1.5). The single largest margin is +7.31% on HumanEval with LLaDA-8B-Instruct (42.68 vs. 35.37). The only tie is GSM8K on LLaDA-8B-Instruct, where both LUGS and Beam Search score 79.38%; on LLaDA-1.5, LUGS wins every individual benchmark against Beam Search, including GSM8K (81.27 vs. 80.14) and Math500 (37.60 vs. 35.40).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the LLaDA diffusion language model architecture and its semi-autoregressive block-wise decoding schedule (Nie et al., 2025), extending the unmasking decision problem beyond the local heuristics (confidence, entropy) and logit-based beam search methods established by Sahoo et al. (2024), Huang et al. (2025), and Lee et al. (2025). The key departure is introducing a learned latent-aware scoring module that consumes last-layer hidden states rather than relying solely on surface-level token probabilities, trained via offline trajectory distillation from a wide-beam teacher. This positions LUGS at the intersection of the DLM decoding literature and the broader guidance/steering paradigm used in AR decoding (e.g., value functions, reward models), but applied specifically to the unmasking-order problem unique to masked diffusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LUGS addresses a practical bottleneck in DLM inference: the unmasking order can cause error accumulation that degrades generation quality, and the paper demonstrates that hidden states carry complementary semantic signals that logits alone miss. The cross-task generalization results (Appendix D) suggest the learned guidance policy captures transferable structural knowledge rather than task-specific patterns, which is encouraging for broader adoption. However, the approach is validated only on LLaDA-family models, adds ~7% inference overhead, and requires expensive wide-beam trajectory generation for training. Open questions include whether the method scales to larger DLMs, whether RL-based training could replace distillation, and whether the latent-aware guidance generalizes to non-masked DLM variants or other discrete diffusion formulations.
