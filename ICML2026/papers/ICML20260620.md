## Problem

Existing MoE expert pruning methods rely on separable per-expert rankings (routing frequency, activation norms) that ignore combinatorial synergies between experts, leading to suboptimal selections especially under aggressive sparsity. In fine-grained MoEs with 64–128+ experts per layer, the combinatorial space is too large for exhaustive enumeration, and greedy search is prone to local traps, leaving a gap between theoretical optimality and computational tractability.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained MoE model and 512 unlabeled calibration sequences (2048 tokens each, sampled from C4) from which a single forward pass extracts per-expert contribution matrices C_i ∈ R^{N×d}, from which Gram matrices G and inner-product vectors u are computed. The output is a per-layer retained expert subset S* (a topology mask) plus optional linear-refitting coefficients β*; in practice the authors deploy Mask-Only inference, where pruned experts are set to −∞ in the router and the original dynamic gating weights are preserved, yielding a compressed model with ~42% of experts removed and no retraining.

## Method

SCHUR-A* formulates layer-wise pruning as minimizing a reconstruction objective L*(S) = min_β ‖Y − Σ β_i C_i‖²_F over a cardinality-constrained subset, solved analytically via OLS (β* = G_S^{-1} u_S). It then performs an A* search over the combinatorial space, guided by an admissible heuristic h(S,t) = Σ_{j=1}^{t} Top_j(ũ_i²) / (λ_min(Σ_R)+ε), where ũ_R and Σ_R are the residualized inner-product vector and Schur complement of the remaining experts—this bound is provably tight (median consistency ratio ρ ≈ 0.986) and guarantees convergence to the layer-wise global optimum. A Vectorized Gain Computation scheme caches Cholesky factors and uses batched triangular solves (TRSM) to evaluate all candidate marginal gains simultaneously, reducing per-node expansion from O(m|S|³) to O(|S|²·m). Finally, the Kneedle algorithm automatically selects the per-layer expert budget r_l by detecting the knee in the greedy reward trajectory, eliminating manual pruning-ratio specification and adapting to heterogeneous redundancy across layers.

## Results

On Qwen3-30B-A3B (E=128 experts/layer), SCHUR-A* at an adaptive ~42% pruning ratio achieves 66.1% average zero-shot accuracy across 8 benchmarks, outperforming the strongest 50%-pruned baseline (REAP, 51.8%) by +14.3% while remaining within 0.8% of the 25%-pruned REAP baseline (66.9%). On MMLU specifically, SCHUR-A* reaches 62.3% vs. 50.7% for 50%-REAP and 67.4% for 25%-REAP. Perplexity on WikiText-2 is 8.25 (vs. 7.74 unpruned), and the method generalizes to ERNIE-4.5-21B-A3B (66.1% avg at 32.93% pruning) and DeepSeek-V2-Lite (44.8% avg at ~39% pruning).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against REAP (Lasby et al., 2025), the strongest ranking-based baseline, SCHUR-A* at 42% pruning (0.661 avg) outperforms REAP at 50% (0.518) by a margin of +14.3% and trails REAP at 25% (0.669) by only 0.8%, despite removing 1.7× more parameters. Against Frequency (Xie et al., 2024), SCHUR-A* leads by +17.8% over the 50% setting (0.483) and +6.1% over the 25% setting (0.600). Against EAN (Lasby et al., 2025), the gap is +16.8% over 50% (0.493) and +5.8% over 25% (0.603). In a controlled fixed-budget comparison at the identical 42% ratio, SCHUR-A* still leads 42%-REAP (0.645) by +1.6%, and under uniformly applied Kneedle budgets it leads kne-REAP (0.642) by +1.9%, confirming the advantage stems from the global combinatorial search rather than a favorable retention ratio. On the Mixtral-8x7B brute-force verification, SCHUR-A* achieves 92.4–95.2% overlap with the enumerated optimal subset, versus 79.2–82.4% for Frequency and 86.1–88.5% for EAN.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the reconstruction-based expert pruning framework of Lu et al. (2024), which formulated MoE pruning as minimizing layer-output distortion, by replacing the intractable combinatorial search with a globally optimal A* algorithm guided by a Schur-complement-derived admissible heuristic. It departs from separable ranking methods (MoE-Pruner, REAP) that treat experts as independent entities, instead exploiting inter-expert correlations through the Gram matrix structure. The Kneedle algorithm (Satopaa et al., 2011) is repurposed from system behavior analysis to automatically determine per-layer expert budgets, eliminating manual pruning ratio selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method provides the first scalable, provably optimal expert selection for fine-grained MoEs (64–128+ experts), where greedy approaches fall into local traps and exhaustive enumeration is infeasible. The practical finding that reverting to original gating weights (Mask-Only) outperforms mathematically optimal refitted coefficients (Mask-and-Scale) challenges the assumption that calibration-optimal parameters generalize, and has direct deployment implications. Open questions include whether layer-wise optimality can be extended to end-to-end task performance, how the method behaves under extreme ill-conditioning in half-precision, and whether the approach generalizes to non-orthogonal expert architectures with more complex routing.
