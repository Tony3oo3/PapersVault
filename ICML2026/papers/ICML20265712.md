## Problem

Standard stochastic sampling for LLMs produces surface-level lexical variation without altering underlying reasoning strategies, causing test-time scaling to yield diminishing returns as sampled candidates remain semantically redundant. Existing remedies—structured search algorithms and heuristic logit constraints—either incur substantial computational overhead or fail to elicit genuinely novel solution patterns, leaving a gap in efficiently encouraging semantic exploration during decoding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265712/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text prompt and, during autoregressive decoding, reads the LLM's first-layer hidden state (h_t^1) and final-layer hidden state (h_t^L) at each step, along with the frozen language-modeling head weights W_head. It produces a reweighted logit vector that replaces the standard sampling distribution, yielding diverse generated tokens (reasoning solutions, code, or creative continuations). Unusually, the novelty signal is computed in continuous representation space rather than token space, and the distiller is trained online from the hidden states of the current generation batch, requiring no pre-training data.

## Method

ESamp introduces a lightweight 2-layer Gated SwiGLU MLP (the Latent Distiller) that is trained online at test time via MSE to predict the LLM's deep-layer hidden representation from its shallow-layer representation. Motivated by Random Network Distillation, the distiller's prediction error serves as a novelty signal: low error indicates a semantically familiar context, while high error flags an under-explored region. Within a KL-regularized policy optimization framework, this yields a closed-form reweighting where the sampling logits become logit_new = (1+β)·logit_ref − β·logit_dist, effectively boosting tokens whose LM-head direction aligns with the latent error vector. In parallel batch generation, the shared distiller acts as an implicit coordination channel: once one sequence explores a semantic pattern, the distiller learns that mapping and suppresses it for all other sequences. An asynchronous CUDA-stream pipeline overlaps the distiller's forward and backward passes with the LLM's middle-layer computation, keeping end-to-end overhead below 5% (1.2% in the optimized release).

## Results

On AIME25 with Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, ESamp (β=0.25) achieves Pass@64 of 46.7%, outperforming Vanilla sampling (40.0%) and the best subtraction-formulation baseline (40.0%). On Qwen3-8B, ESamp reaches Pass@64 of 80.0% on AIME24 and 67.8% on AIME25, versus 70.0% and 58.9% for Vanilla respectively. In creative writing (BookCorpus), ESamp achieves the best Vendi Score (1.67), lowest pairwise similarity (0.57), and lowest perplexity (3.55) simultaneously, breaking the diversity–coherence trade-off. The asynchronous pipeline incurs only 0.3%–4.25% throughput overhead across serving scenarios on an RTX 4090.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ESamp consistently outperforms Vanilla temperature sampling (Holtzman et al.) on Pass@k at larger budgets: on Qwen3-8B/AIME24, ESamp reaches 80.0% Pass@64 versus 70.0% for Vanilla, and on AIME25 it achieves 67.8% versus 58.9%. Against Min-p (Minh et al.), ESamp wins on both diversity (Vendi 1.67 vs 1.56) and Pass@k, though Min-p occasionally edges ahead on Pass@1 (e.g., 8.3% vs 6.0% on Qwen2.5-7B/AIME25). FIRE (Chen et al.) shows benchmark-specific strengths (e.g., higher Pass@1 on Qwen3-8B/AIME25 at 42.4% vs 39.1%) but hampers code generation on LiveCodeBench, whereas ESamp generalizes robustly across all domains. OverRIDE (Shi & Pan), the closest method in spirit, is outperformed by ESamp on Pass@k (80.0% vs 76.7% best-tuned on AIME24/Qwen3-8B) and on diversity metrics, because ESamp penalizes recurring semantics in representation space rather than token-level repetition. Tree of Thoughts (Yao et al.) and Contrastive Decoding (Li et al.) are surpassed by ESamp in the Pass@k scaling curves (Figure 3), with ESamp matching their high-k performance at a fraction of the sampling budget (e.g., Pass@8 of ESamp ≈ Pass@64 of baselines on GPT-OSS-20B).

## Relation to Prior Work

ESamp directly adapts the Random Network Distillation (RND) novelty signal—where prediction error of a lightweight network serves as a proxy for input novelty—from reinforcement learning exploration to LLM decoding. The theoretical backbone is the KL-regularized policy optimization framework (Peters et al., 2010), which yields the closed-form reweighting of base-model logits by an exponential novelty reward, a formulation also used in Controlled Decoding (Mudgal et al., 2024). The key conceptual departure from the closest prior work, OverRIDE (Shi & Pan, 2026), is operating in continuous hidden-representation space rather than vocabulary space: ESamp penalizes recurring *semantics* (detected via distiller prediction error on deep-layer hidden states) rather than recurring *tokens*, and the shared online distiller across a parallel batch provides implicit coordination without explicit cross-sequence communication.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is strong: an online-trained component incurs <5% throughput overhead (1.2% in the optimized open-source release), making representation-space exploration viable in production serving stacks built on vLLM. The method breaks the diversity–coherence trade-off in creative writing and substantially improves Pass@k scaling efficiency for reasoning models, particularly at moderate sampling budgets (e.g., matching Pass@64 baselines with only Pass@8 on GPT-OSS-20B). Open questions include whether the shared-distiller coordination generalizes to heterogeneous task batches, how ESamp interacts with reward-model reranking or verifier-based selection at scale, and whether the first-layer-to-final-layer distillation choice (fixed by the async pipeline) is truly optimal or merely a convenient engineering constraint. The vocabulary-space distiller's dramatic failure (Table 11) and the noise ablation (Table 10) together confirm that the continuous representation geometry is essential, but the paper does not fully characterize *which* aspects of the error direction carry the most information.
