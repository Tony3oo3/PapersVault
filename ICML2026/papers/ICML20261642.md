## Problem

Existing Test-Time Adaptation methods for Vision-Language Navigation treat each domain shift as an isolated, transient update, causing catastrophic forgetting of prior adaptations and negative transfer when dissimilar contexts are conflated. No prior work models online adaptation as the accumulation of structured, reusable knowledge that can be selectively retrieved and recomposed across recurring or related environments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261642/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a frozen pre-trained VLN policy (e.g., DUET, HAMT), a natural-language instruction, and a 360° panoramic visual observation at each navigation step, alongside a growing library of up to K_max=32 triplet-structured assets (each comprising a 4-token soft prompt, prompt-independent feature statistics from the final fusion layer, and a prediction-entropy uncertainty score). It outputs the next navigation action at each step, and as a side product either a training-free composite bridge prompt (a convex combination of historical prompts) or a newly optimized asset appended to the library, with the entire asset library requiring only ~0.58 MB of additional memory.

## Method

IDEA reformulates TTA as accumulation of composable assets rather than isolated parameter updates. It injects L=4 learnable soft prompts into the visual token stream and optimizes them via a multi-layer moment-matching loss that aligns prompt-augmented fusion-layer statistics to precomputed source-domain means and variances. A Fisher-guided weighting scheme uses the trace of the Fisher Information Matrix (derived as a first-order proxy for the Hessian via the expected score-function outer product) to adaptively up-weight layers whose representations are policy-sensitive, suppressing domain-specific noise. Each optimized prompt is packaged with its environment's feature statistics and an uncertainty score into a triplet asset. For new domains, IDEA constructs a training-free adaptation bridge by projecting the target statistics onto the convex hull of all historical assets under 2-Wasserstein distance, solved in closed form via KKT conditions with an uncertainty-aware L2 regularizer, yielding a composite prompt that bypasses iterative optimization. The two components form a complementary loop: a richer library enables more precise bridge construction, while the bridge provides superior initialization that accelerates subsequent asset optimization.

## Results

On REVERIE (the primary benchmark), IDEA with DUET achieves 56.92% SR and 38.03% SPL on Val Unseen, and 55.12% SR and 39.84% SPL on Test Unseen, outperforming the DUET baseline (46.98% SR, 33.73% SPL) by +9.9% SR and +4.3% SPL on Val Unseen. With HAMT, IDEA reaches 34.92% SR on Val Unseen and 32.81% SR on Test Unseen, yielding average gains of +2.5% SR and +2.8% SPL over the best competing TTA method on Test Unseen. On R2R, IDEA boosts DUET to 76% SR (Val Unseen) and BEVBert to 76% SR, while on R2R-CE it improves BEVBert to 62% SR and ETPNav to 59% SR on Val Unseen. IDEA also runs in ~246–344 ms per episode, roughly 2–3× faster than FSTTA and ~20× faster than feedback-driven methods like FeedTTA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IDEA consistently outperforms all five self-supervised TTA baselines (Tent, SAR, ViDA, FSTTA, ReCAP) across every split and both base models on REVERIE, R2R, and R2R-CE. The most notable margin is on REVERIE Test Unseen with DUET, where IDEA achieves 55.12% SR versus 53.40% (FSTTA) and 53.07% (ReCAP), a gap of +1.7–2.1% SR. On R2R Val Unseen, IDEA with BEVBert reaches 76% SR and 68% SPL, surpassing the next-best ReCAP (75% SR, 64% SPL) by +1% SR and +4% SPL. Against feedback-driven methods, IDEA is competitive or superior on unseen splits (e.g., 55.12% SR vs. ATENA's 54.28% and FeedTTA's 53.58% on REVERIE Test Unseen with DUET) while being approximately 15–20× faster in inference time (~344 ms vs. 5,420–7,640 ms). ViDA and Tent show the weakest adaptation, with Tent even degrading performance on REVERIE Test Unseen (23.73% SR vs. 30.40% baseline for HAMT), highlighting the negative-transfer problem that IDEA's asset-bridge design specifically addresses.

## Relation to Prior Work

IDEA departs from the dominant TTA-for-VLN paradigm (FSTTA, ReCAP, ViDA) that treats each domain shift as an isolated, transient update by reframing adaptation as the progressive accumulation of reusable 'assets'—soft prompts enriched with domain coordinates and uncertainty scores. Conceptually, it repurposes prompt tuning (from NLP/CLIP) as the knowledge-encoding mechanism, borrows the Fisher Information Matrix from sensitivity analysis to weight layer-wise alignment, and draws on optimal transport (Wasserstein distance over Gaussian statistics) to construct a training-free 'bridge' via convex-hull projection. The paper does not build on a single prior method but synthesizes these orthogonal ideas into a unified asset-library-and-bridge framework, positioning itself as the first to make TTA knowledge portable and composable across domains and agents.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The asset-library formulation offers a practical path toward lifelong, collaborative adaptation for embodied agents: a new agent can inherit a pre-built library and skip the cold-start phase, and multi-source aggregation yields measurable gains (+1.2% SR). The training-free bridge (closed-form KKT solution) is attractive for latency-constrained deployment, reducing per-step overhead compared to iterative TTA. However, two limitations temper broader impact: assets are coupled to a specific feature space (not architecture-agnostic), and all evaluations are single-agent, leaving multi-agent sharing and heterogeneous-model transfer unexplored. Open questions include whether the convex-hull bridge generalizes beyond Gaussian moment matching and whether asset protocols can be standardized across model families.
