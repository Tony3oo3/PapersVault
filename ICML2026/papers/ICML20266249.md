## Problem

Robotic manipulation of flat objects (books, fabrics, boards) is difficult because these objects often lack accessible grasp affordances, and no single manipulation strategy works across all shapes and materials. Existing methods are tied to one heuristic pre-manipulation approach and evaluated on closed object sets, limiting generalization. Additionally, no unified simulation benchmark exists that systematically covers both rigid and deformable flat objects with standardized evaluation protocols.

## Input / Output

The strategy generator consumes object-centric point clouds (2048 points, centered and normalized to a unit sphere, converted to color-independent tensors) and outputs a discrete strategy label from {A: push-to-edge, B: dual-arm lift, C: edge-squeeze}. The execution module takes the full scene point cloud (8192 points, zero-mean normalized) plus the predicted strategy label and outputs 6-D end-effector poses (3-D position + unit quaternion) for each action primitive, which are sequenced into a complete manipulation trajectory. The decoupled design means the two modules are trained independently and can be swapped or extended independently.

## Method

The framework decouples manipulation into a Strategy Generator and an Action Execution Module. The generator uses a shared-convolution point cloud encoder with global max pooling followed by a two-layer MLP head, trained jointly with cross-entropy and a temperature-scaled NT-Xent contrastive loss that pulls embeddings of different objects sharing the same strategy together while pushing apart same-object embeddings with different strategies; simulated data transformation (material swap and scale resampling) augments the training set to enforce object-invariant, strategy-centric representations. The execution module uses a PointNet++ encoder on the scene point cloud feeding two parallel MLPs that independently regress 3-D position and quaternion rotation, supervised by L2 position loss and geodesic rotation loss; long-horizon manipulation is decomposed into reusable action primitives (touch, slide, squeeze), each defined by start and end gripper poses, which are dynamically composed according to the predicted strategy. This primitive-based decomposition prevents overfitting to specific object geometries and enables stable generalization to unseen objects and categories.

## Results

On the FlatLab benchmark, the proposed unified framework achieves grasping success rates of 81.1% (Train), 74.2% (Test α, unseen objects), and 69.0% (Test β, unseen categories), with strategy discrimination accuracies of 99.2%, 91.3%, and 78.6% respectively. In real-world experiments on a Baxter robot, the method achieves 83.6%, 80.0%, and 80.0% grasping success on the three splits. On external datasets, the method reaches 80.7% (GraspLargeFlat), 82.5% (PreAfford), and 84.0% (AdaptPNP) average grasping success.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all seven baselines on every evaluation split. The strongest baseline is 3D Diffusion Policy (Ze et al., 2024), which reaches 66.2%/54.2%/51.2% on Train/Test α/Test β; FlatLab exceeds it by 14.9, 20.0, and 17.8 percentage points respectively. The single-strategy baselines (Slide at 32.3% and Lift at 41.2% on Train) confirm that no fixed manipulation strategy suffices across diverse flat objects. The VLA models (OpenVLA, π0, π0.5) show the most dramatic generalization collapse: while π0.5 achieves 68.6% on Train, it drops to 28.6% on Test α and 25.0% on Test β, a 43.6-point drop, whereas FlatLab's drop is only 12.1 points (81.1%→69.0%). Diffusion Policy (Chi et al., 2023) performs moderately on Train (48.8%) but degrades to 36.8% on Test α, indicating overfitting to training objects. The paper attributes FlatLab's advantage to the decoupled strategy-then-execution design, which learns strategy-centric object-invariant representations rather than memorizing object-specific action patterns.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes three previously separate research threads: (1) pre-manipulation strategies for flat objects (edge-pushing, wall-assisted tilting, dual-arm lifting), (2) contrastive representation learning for object-invariant features, and (3) simulation-based manipulation benchmarks (FluidLab, GarmentLab, LabUtopia). Rather than extending any single prior method, it carves new ground by decoupling strategy selection from action execution and framing flat object manipulation as a strategy-routing problem over a fixed set of action primitives. The contrastive learning component repurposes NT-Xent in a novel way—enforcing strategy-centric consistency across objects while suppressing object-specific geometry—combined with simulated data transformation (material and scale) as a form of physics-aware augmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FlatLab fills a clear gap as the first unified simulation benchmark covering both rigid and deformable flat objects with automated multi-modal data collection and standardized evaluation, which should accelerate research in this underexplored area. The substantial performance gap over VLA-based end-to-end models (69–74% vs. 17–28% on unseen categories) suggests that explicit strategy decomposition and primitive-based execution remain important for fine-grained contact manipulation tasks where action jitter is costly. Key open questions include bridging the sim-to-real gap more systematically, scaling to cluttered multi-object scenes, and whether the strategy set can be expanded or learned rather than hand-defined. The counterintuitive finding that real-world success rates exceed simulation rates (83.6% vs. 81.1% on Train) highlights that current physics engines may not faithfully capture friction and deformable contact behavior.
