## Problem

Generative models such as diffusion models and normalizing flows fundamentally require the target distribution to have full ambient-space support, but many scientific datasets are constrained to lower-dimensional manifolds (e.g., fixed bond lengths, total image flux), making the data distribution singular with respect to Lebesgue measure. This degeneracy causes score explosion in diffusion sampling and zero Jacobian determinants in NF training, while existing fixes—either isotropic noising or manifold-intrinsic SDEs—introduce distributional distortion or impose heavy per-step computational overhead.

## Input / Output

The method takes finite samples from a known equality-constrained manifold M = {x : h(x) = 0} along with the constraint function h (or its Jacobian J_h at each sample) and a single noise-scale hyperparameter σ. It produces samples guaranteed to lie on M by construction: a standard unconstrained generative model is trained on the perturbed distribution p_σ, and generated samples are snapped back to M via nearest-point projection (closed-form for linear/quadratic constraints, iterative point-triangle search for meshes, or a simple flux correction for images). The constraint manifold need only be C¹ differentiable in practice, with full-rank Jacobians at all points.

## Method

The core contribution is an anisotropic, manifold-aware perturbation: for each data sample z, Gaussian noise is added strictly along the normal space N_z M (computed via QR decomposition of J_h(z)ᵀ), producing a lifted distribution p_σ with full d-dimensional support while preserving the intrinsic tangential geometry of M. A standard unconstrained generative model (DDPM, SBDM, RealNVP, or Glow) is then trained on p_σ as if it were an ordinary full-dimensional distribution, and generated samples are projected back to M. The key theoretical results show that for linear constraints the projected distribution exactly recovers p_0, while for general C² manifolds the total variation distance between the projected distribution and p_0 is bounded by C₁ exp(−C₂ r²/σ²) where r < reach(M), with constants depending only on the codimension. This normal-only noising is the critical distinction from prior isotropic approaches: it eliminates tangential distortion (zero for linear manifolds, minimal for curved ones), yielding bounded scores, non-zero Jacobian determinants, and guaranteed constraint adherence without modifying the training or sampling algorithm.

## Results

On the sphere task (σ=0.05), the proposed p_σ approach with RealNVP achieves JSD 0.0769 and TVD 0.2638, dramatically outperforming raw RealNVP (JSD 0.3389, TVD 0.6672) and projected RealNVP (JSD 0.2817, TVD 0.5969). On the MNIST flux-constrained image task, p_σ achieves the best FID (1.512×10⁻⁵) and Class JSD (0.0730) among all methods. On the protein backbone task, p_σ achieves MMD 3.37×10⁻⁴ (competitive with DDPM's 3.35×10⁻⁴) while guaranteeing constraint adherence, with pairwise RMSD of 1.6262 indicating no mode collapse.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PDM (Christopher et al., 2024), p_σ is competitive on JSD and TVD for diffusion models (e.g., sphere: JSD 0.0777 vs. 0.0771) while achieving 4× faster sampling (0.1975s vs. 0.3763s) and guaranteeing constraint adherence by construction. Against PIDM (Bastek et al., 2025), p_σ matches or exceeds COV (0.7976 vs. 0.9105 on sphere diffusion, but 0.8675 vs. N/A for NFs) and is competitive on JSD/TVD, while PIDM requires expensive constraint-residual computation during training. Against raw DDPM (Ho et al., 2020), p_σ is competitive on distributional metrics but uniquely guarantees constraint satisfaction without post-hoc projection; raw DDPM on the sphere achieves slightly lower JSD (0.0662 for projected DDPM) but produces off-manifold samples. Against raw Glow (Kingma & Dhariwal, 2018) and RealNVP (Dinh et al., 2017), p_σ dramatically outperforms: on the sphere, RealNVP(p_σ) achieves JSD 0.0769 vs. 0.3389 for raw RealNVP, and Glow(p_σ) achieves JSD 0.0911 vs. 0.3475 for raw Glow, confirming that the anisotropic normal-bundle perturbation resolves the Jacobian determinant explosion that cripples NFs on degenerate distributions. Against isotropic noising baselines (DDPM proj. iso., RealNVP iso., Glow iso.), p_σ consistently matches or improves JSD and TVD, validating the paper's central claim that restricting perturbation to the normal bundle avoids the tangential distortion inherent in isotropic approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two main lines of prior work on equality-constrained generative modeling: isotropic noising approaches (Song & Ermon 2019, SoftFlow) that add ambient Gaussian noise to resolve dimension mismatch but distort the distribution upon projection, and manifold-intrinsic methods (Riemannian SDEs, PDM, PIDM) that enforce constraints during training or sampling at significant computational cost. The key conceptual departure is restricting perturbation strictly to the normal bundle of the constraint manifold, which the authors show yields perfect recovery for linear constraints and exponentially decaying TV error for nonlinear ones. The work also generalizes the inflation-deflation idea of Horvat & Pfister (2023) from exact density estimation with NFs to a broader, off-the-shelf procedure applicable to any generative paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a simple, single-hyperparameter modification that practitioners can apply to any existing diffusion or NF pipeline to handle equality constraints, with theoretical guarantees (perfect linear recovery, bounded TV for nonlinear) that are rare in this area. Limitations include requiring an analytically known C² manifold, a single global σ that may be suboptimal for manifolds with highly variable curvature, and potential computational cost of normal-basis computation and projection in high-dimensional settings. Open questions include spatially-dependent σ schedules, alternative noising distributions beyond Gaussian, and extending the SDE forward process to incorporate anisotropic noise continuously rather than as a one-time pre-processing step.
