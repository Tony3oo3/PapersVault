## Problem

Standard Transformers are not globally Lipschitz continuous because self-attention involves unbounded dot products, and while prior work has proposed architectural modifications to enforce Lipschitz bounds, no approximation-theoretic guarantees exist for Transformer architectures that explicitly preserve Lipschitz continuity. This paper asks whether a class of Lipschitz-constrained in-context Transformers can universally approximate arbitrary Lipschitz continuous in-context maps, and answers affirmatively.

## Input / Output

The model takes a probability measure μ over a compact set Ω ⊂ R^d (the context) and a query point x ∈ Ω, and produces a scalar output. The context is modeled as a general probability measure rather than a fixed-length token sequence, so all approximation guarantees are independent of token count. The Lipschitz constraint is anisotropic: 1-Lipschitz in the query variable under the Euclidean metric and C-Lipschitz in the context measure under the Wasserstein-1 metric, for any prescribed constant C > 0 independent of the target accuracy.

## Method

The architecture composes gradient-descent-type MLP layers (explicit Euler steps of negative gradient flows with ReLU) and a novel gradient-descent-type attention layer whose weights arise from a cumulant-generating function, with the value matrix constrained to equal the key matrix (V=A) to preserve the gradient-flow interpretation. The universal approximation theorem is proven via a variant of the Restricted Stone-Weierstrass theorem adapted to functions of two variables (a measure and a point), requiring verification of lattice closure and a Lipschitz separation property. Lattice closure for attention layers is achieved by representing parallel attention as a composition of two sequential attention layers with block-diagonal interaction matrices, while the separation property relies on Kantorovich-Rubinstein duality to construct Wasserstein-distance lower bounds. The proof is inherently constructive through minimax compositions of elementary Lipschitz primitives, though the optimizer in the Kantorovich-Rubinstein dual is obtained non-explicitly via Arzelà-Ascoli compactness.

## Results

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments or quantitative benchmarks. The main result (Theorem 8) is a universal approximation theorem: for any ε ∈ (0,1) and any target map Λ* in the class of (1,C)-Lipschitz in-context functions on a compact domain, there exists a gradient-descent-type in-context Transformer Λ in the proposed architecture class such that sup_{(μ,x)} |Λ(μ,x) − Λ*(μ,x)| ≤ ε. The approximation guarantee is independent of the number of tokens (context length), as the context is modeled as a probability measure. The proof is constructive in the sense that it relies on minimax compositions of elementary Lipschitz primitives via a Restricted Stone-Weierstrass argument, but it is non-quantitative (no explicit bounds on depth, width, or lifting dimension in terms of ε are provided).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not include empirical comparisons against any baselines, as it is a theoretical contribution establishing the first approximation-theoretic guarantee for Transformer architectures that explicitly preserve Lipschitz continuity. The closest prior theoretical results it builds upon are: (1) Murari et al. (2025) on 1-Lipschitz ResNets, which the present work generalizes from context-free to in-context mappings; (2) Furuya et al. (2025) on universal in-context learning, which provides token-independent approximation rates but does not control Lipschitz constants; and (3) Delattre et al. (2026), which constructs a 1-Lipschitz self-attention layer from convex gradient flows but does not study approximation properties. The paper's novelty lies in combining Lipschitz-preserving architecture with a measure-theoretic in-context formulation and proving density in the appropriate function space, rather than in outperforming competing methods on a task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate theoretical threads: the 1-Lipschitz ResNet approximation theory of Murari et al. (2025) and the measure-theoretic in-context Transformer framework of Furuya et al. (2025). The key architectural departure is replacing standard dot-product attention (which is not globally Lipschitz) with a gradient-descent-type attention layer whose 1-Lipschitz property follows from a covariance bound, analogous to how the MLP layer inherits Lipschitz continuity from the Sherry et al. (2024) Euler-step construction. The proof technique extends the Restricted Stone-Weierstrass theorem of Anil et al. (2019) from single-variable Lipschitz functions to two-variable (measure, point) settings, with the Kantorovich-Rubinstein duality providing the separation primitives specific to the Wasserstein-1 context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first universal approximation guarantee for Transformer architectures that provably preserve Lipschitz continuity, closing a gap where prior work either analyzed Lipschitz properties of standard Transformers or proved approximation for unconstrained in-context maps. The token-independent guarantees via the measure-theoretic formulation are practically relevant for variable-length sequences. However, the result is purely existential and non-quantitative: no bounds on depth, width, or lifting dimension are given, the step-size parameter depends on hard-to-estimate layerwise domain suprema, and the construction relies on a non-explicit optimizer from Arzelà-Ascoli. Extending from scalar to vector-valued outputs under the ℓ² norm is identified as a significant open problem due to the absence of a natural lattice structure.
