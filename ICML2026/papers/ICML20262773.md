## Problem

Existing periodic adaptations of Bayesian Flow Networks (e.g., CrysBFN) model periodic coordinates with circular distributions such as von Mises, whose posterior concentration updates are not additive in the observation accuracy. This breaks the strictly additive-accuracy property that underpins BFN's closed-form forward marginal and jumpable continuous-time training, making generation sensitive to hand-tuned discrete schedules. Additionally, periodic data carry nuisance symmetries (global translations in crystals, π-periodic sign equivalence in phase synthesis) that, if unaddressed during training, inflate gradient variance through objective mismatch.

## Input / Output

For crystal structure prediction, the input is an atom composition vector A ∈ {1,…,K}^N (one-hot encoded and embedded into per-atom features) and the output is a lattice matrix L ∈ R^{3×3} plus fractional coordinates F ∈ [0,1)^{N×3}, where each coordinate is a periodic scalar on a 3-torus. For CLF phase synthesis, the input is a target binocular intensity image b ∈ [0,1]^{2wh} and a viewer pose, and the output is three LCD layer phase patterns x ∈ [0, π/2]^{3wh}; the network operates in screen space at 360×640 for evaluation and 1080×1920 for deployment, with the fully convolutional U-Net being resolution-agnostic. In both tasks, periodic scalars are internally represented as 2D unit-circle embeddings, and the CLF forward model couples layers through a sparse ray-tracing operator P(pose) and a sin² nonlinearity.

## Method

PeriodicBFN embeds each periodic scalar f ∈ [0,1) onto the unit circle as u(f) = [cos 2πf, sin 2πf] ∈ R² and performs standard isotropic Gaussian Bayesian updates in this Cartesian space, restoring the strictly additive accuracy law ρ ← ρ + α and the exact closed-form forward marginal needed for continuous-time BFN training. The network reads the 2D belief state (μ_t, t) and predicts a denoised unit vector, with the radial magnitude of μ_t naturally encoding accumulated uncertainty. To handle nuisance symmetries, the paper derives Rao-Blackwellized training targets: for CSP, the latent global periodic translation δ is marginalized analytically, yielding a von Mises posterior whose first circular moment gives a closed-form shift-invariant target; for CLF, the binary π-shift sign latent is marginalized against a Bernoulli posterior, producing a tanh-parameterized target that respects sin² sign equivalence. The CSP model uses an E(3)-equivariant GNN backbone (inherited from CrysBFN) with a periodic embedding head for fractional coordinates and a standard Euclidean BFN head for lattice parameters, while the CLF model reuses the EyeReal U-Net with residual cosine/sine modulators that enlarge the predictive support from S¹ to R² to match the geometry of the RB target.

## Results

On crystal structure prediction, PeriodicBFN achieves 70.05% match rate and 0.0370 RMSE on MP-20, and 27.03% match rate and 0.1105 RMSE on MPTS-52, outperforming the strongest baseline CrysBFN by +5.7 and +6.5 match-rate points respectively. On compressive light-field phase synthesis, EyeReal+PeriodicBFN reaches 31.40 dB PSNR at 28.49 FPS, improving over EyeReal (28.86 dB) while remaining real-time, though slower than the one-shot EyeReal regressor (52.1 FPS) and lower in PSNR than the non-real-time conjugate gradient solver (35.51 dB at 1.4 FPS). Multi-seed evaluation confirms the MPTS-52 RMSE gap to CrysBFN (0.1105 vs. 0.1112 mean) is within noise, with PeriodicBFN showing substantially lower variance (±0.0024 vs. ±0.0077).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PeriodicBFN consistently outperforms all five CSP baselines on match rate across both MP-20 and MPTS-52, with the largest gains over diffusion-based methods: +18.6 points over DiffCSP and +12.5 over EquiCSP on MP-20. Against the closest BFN-based competitor CrysBFN, PeriodicBFN improves match rate by +5.7 (MP-20) and +6.5 (MPTS-52) points while also reducing RMSE on MP-20 (0.0370 vs. 0.0433); the only metric where CrysBFN edges ahead is MPTS-52 RMSE (0.1038 vs. 0.1105), which multi-seed analysis shows is not statistically significant. FlowMM, despite being a flow-matching method, trails PeriodicBFN by 8.7 match-rate points on MP-20 and 9.5 on MPTS-52. For CLF phase synthesis, EyeReal+PeriodicBFN improves PSNR by 2.54 dB over the one-shot EyeReal regressor while roughly doubling inference time (35.1 ms vs. 19.9 ms), still operating at 28.49 FPS—well within real-time display constraints—whereas the conjugate gradient solver achieves 3.9 dB higher PSNR but is 20× slower (692 ms, 1.4 FPS). The NFE sweep further shows PeriodicBFN saturates at 200–500 network forwards (70.05–70.06% match rate) while CrysBFN degrades from 64.35% to 62.04% when its schedule is shortened, validating the robustness of the additive-accuracy formulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Bayesian Flow Networks (Graves et al., 2023) to periodic data by identifying and fixing a fundamental gap in prior circular adaptations like CrysBFN (Wu et al., 2025): von Mises posterior updates lack the additive-accuracy property that underpins BFN's closed-form forward marginal and jumpable training. The fix—embedding each periodic scalar as a 2D unit-circle vector and performing standard Gaussian Bayes updates in Cartesian space—restores the full BFN schedule machinery while letting the network learn geometric coupling on the embedded circle. The Rao-Blackwellized invariant objective draws on equivariant denoising ideas from CSP diffusion (Lin et al., 2024) but achieves the averaging analytically via von Mises moments (CSP) or Bernoulli marginalization (CLF), yielding closed-form targets with guaranteed gradient-variance reduction. The CLF phase-synthesis application builds on the EyeReal regression pipeline (Ma et al., 2025) but reframes it as conditional generative modeling, which the authors note is the first such extension to glasses-free 3D display systems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Restoring additive accuracy for periodic BFNs removes a practical instability (schedule sensitivity, need for hand-tuned heuristics) that has limited the adoption of BFNs in materials science, and the NFE-robustness experiments confirm the theoretical benefit empirically. The cross-domain validation—crystal structure prediction and CLF phase synthesis—demonstrates that the 2D embedding + Rao-Blackwellization recipe is a general tool for any periodic generative task, with natural extensions to molecular torsion angles and periodic time-series. A practical limitation is that the iterative CLF inference (28.5 FPS) is roughly 2× slower than the one-shot EyeReal regressor (52.1 FPS), though still real-time; distillation or lighter periodic-aware backbones are identified as natural acceleration paths. The work also opens a new research direction at the intersection of generative modeling and computational optics, where multimodal phase solutions and physical constraints benefit from a probabilistic rather than point-estimation formulation.
