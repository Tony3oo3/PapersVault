## Problem

Sparse Mixture of Experts (SMoE) models suffer from expert collapse, where different experts converge to produce redundant outputs, degrading model capacity. Prior mitigation strategies focus on improving the learned router but require training from scratch or fine-tuning, and the paper demonstrates that collapse persists even in well-pretrained, state-of-the-art SMoE models. The central question is whether routing can be performed without a learned router at all.

## Input / Output

The method operates entirely on pretrained SMoE weights without any gradient updates: it consumes the FFN weight matrices (FFN₁ ∈ R^{d×h}, FFN₂ ∈ R^{h×d}) of each expert along with the existing router embeddings to pre-compute spectral descriptors, and for vision tasks additionally takes RGB images. Outputs include the standard routed expert combination (top-k weighted sum), but SSMoE also produces dense semantic embeddings suitable for MTEB-style retrieval/classification tasks, zero-shot image-text retrieval rankings, and image classification scores — all without fine-tuning or prompt engineering.

## Method

SSMoE replaces the conventional learned router with eigenvector-based routing derived from each expert's Gram matrices (A = FFN₁·FFN₁ᵀ and B = FFN₂ᵀ·FFN₂). For each expert, the top-c eigenvectors most similar to the router embedding are selected from both Gram matrices and averaged into a single spectral embedding; concatenating all N expert embeddings yields a fixed routing projection W_EV ∈ R^{d×N}. The final gating scores are a convex combination V(x) = softmax(x·W_EV)·α + S(x)·(1−α), where α ∈ [0,1] interpolates between the eigenvector signal and the original learned router (optimal α ≈ 0.5–0.9). Theoretically, Lemma 3.1 proves this convex combination strictly reduces pairwise logit covariance by a factor of (1−α)², directly mitigating collapse, while Lemma B.1 establishes asymptotic orthogonality of expert spectral embeddings under random-matrix assumptions. The framework is entirely training-free and additionally enables SSMoE-guided expert dropping (removing ~25% of experts) that reduces memory by ~23% while maintaining or improving accuracy.

## Results

On GPT-OSS-20B (10-shot), SSMoE achieves an average of 54.1 across eight reasoning benchmarks, surpassing the original model (48.8) by +10.9% while using 22.9% less memory (30.05 GB vs. 38.96 GB). On GPT-OSS-120B, SSMoE reaches 54.9 average vs. 52.6 for the original (+4.4%) with 24.5% memory reduction. On MTEB embedding tasks, SSMoE achieves 45.8 average on Qwen-MoE-7B and 41.1 on DeepSeekMoE-16B (clean setting), outperforming all baselines including MoEE and Router Embedding. On zero-shot image-text retrieval (COCO I2T), SSMoE improves Recall@1 from 65.0 to 65.7 over CLIP-MoE, with larger gains (+2.8%) under corrupted inputs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MoEE (Li & Zhou, 2025), SSMoE consistently outperforms on MTEB tasks: on OLMoE-7B, SSMoE achieves 39.7 average vs. MoEE's 35.2 (clean) and 24.8 vs. 21.2 (corrupt); on Qwen-MoE-7B, SSMoE reaches 45.8 vs. MoEE's 42.6 (clean). Against PromptEOL (Jiang et al., 2024), SSMoE achieves higher average scores and lower variance across all three LLMs in in-context learning evaluation (Figure 5). On vision tasks, SSMoE outperforms both CLIP (Radford et al., 2021) and CLIP-MoE (Zhang et al., 2025): on COCO I2T Recall@1, SSMoE scores 65.7 vs. CLIP-MoE's 65.0 and CLIP's 56.1; under corruption, the gap widens to +2.8% over CLIP-MoE. SSMoE also beats the Router Embedding baseline on 15/18 clean and 16/18 corrupt MTEB tasks, and surpasses the original GPT-OSS models by +10.9% (20B) and +4.4% (120B) average while using only 75% of experts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of work on improving SMoE routers (HyperRouter, StableMoE, VQMoE) by arguing that the router itself is the source of collapse and can be partially bypassed. It builds on two threads: (1) the expert-collapse literature (Chi et al., 2022; Do et al., 2025a) that diagnoses the problem, and (2) SVD-based analyses of neural network weights (Thamm et al., 2022; Sun et al., 2025) that show spectral structure encodes task-relevant information. The key conceptual move is repurposing eigenvectors of expert FFN Gram matrices as a training-free routing signal, complementing rather than replacing the learned router via a convex combination controlled by α.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free nature of SSMoE makes it immediately applicable to any pretrained MoE model without retraining, which is practically valuable given the cost of fine-tuning 100B+ parameter models. However, the theoretical guarantees (Lemma 3.1) rely on strong decorrelation assumptions that only approximately hold in the selective variant, and the vision-task gains (0.2–1.1 points) are modest despite statistical significance. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-FFN expert architectures (e.g., attention-based experts), whether the α hyperparameter can be made task-agnostic, and whether the GSM8K degradation under expert pruning reveals a fundamental tension between memory efficiency and mathematical reasoning capacity.
