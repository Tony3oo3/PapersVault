## Problem

In collaborative inference, existing model inversion attack (MIA) defences perturb shallow-layer intermediate representations at the cost of utility, and the prevailing intuition assumes that deeper partition points are inherently safer. This paper challenges that depth-based safety assumption, showing that depth alone does not guarantee MIA resistance—residual connections and persistent patch tokens can keep representations informative even at great depths. The key question addressed is: where should an edge-cloud model be partitioned to obtain intrinsic, architecture-level resistance to MIA without additional perturbation?

## Input / Output

The method operates on 64×64 RGB or grayscale images processed by four target vision models (IR-152, ResNet-50, VGG19, ViT-6). For GPZ localization, it consumes layer-wise intermediate representations Z (with dimensions ranging from 64×32×32 down to 512×4×4 depending on architecture) and ground-truth class labels to compute the intra-class mean-squared radius R_c² at each layer. The inversion model takes the transmitted Z (optionally augmented via FFT, normalization, or NN-based enhancement) and produces a reconstructed 64×64 image, evaluated by MSE, PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS. The primary output is the identified Golden Partition Zone (a layer range) plus quantitative inversion-resistance metrics at candidate split points.

## Method

The paper derives a lower bound on the conditional entropy H(X|Z) and shows that at the representational transition from feature-level to decision-level, the governing variance term shifts from global total variance σ_feat² to the intra-class mean-squared radius R_c², causing an abrupt rise in the bound. This yields an R_c²-based criterion (Algorithm 1) that actively locates the Golden Partition Zone by tracking the dimension-normalized percentage drop in R_c² across layers, validated against empirical MIA outcomes on IR-152 and VGG19. The paper further introduces the Neural Vortex: a training-dynamics analysis showing that label smoothing reshapes the backpropagated gradient coefficients (p_ic − 1 + α) to sustain contraction of R_c² at decision-level layers, thereby raising the H(X|Z) lower bound and strengthening intrinsic resistance. Experiments across four architectures and multiple datasets confirm that partitioning at the GPZ yields over 4× higher reconstruction MSE than shallow splits, and that decision-level representations maintain a 66% resistance advantage even under entropy-enhanced Z and stronger attention-based inversion models.

## Results

Partitioning at the Golden Partition Zone (GPZ) yields over 4× higher reconstruction MSE compared to shallow splits across four vision models (IR-152, ResNet-50, VGG19, ViT). On IR-152, the decision-level Block-50 baseline achieves test MSE of 0.0570 (PSNR 17.22) versus 0.0182 (PSNR 22.17) for the feature-level Block-40 baseline, a ~3.1× gap that widens to ~4.1× under combined entropy and inversion-model enhancements. Decision-level representations maintain a 66% average resistance advantage over feature-level ones even when both intermediate information entropy (FFT, normalization, NN augmentation) and inversion model capacity (attention mechanisms, reverse-structured residuals) are enhanced. VGG19 reaches its GPZ with only 2.5% edge-side parameters, while IR-152 requires over 78%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against external defense methods but instead compares partition strategies and representation types under a unified MIA protocol. Shallow splits (e.g., IR-152 Block 40, VGG19 Block 26) consistently yield MSE below the 0.02 high-fidelity threshold, confirming that depth alone does not resist inversion; even IR-152 Block 48 (with residual skip connections) remains vulnerable. The GPZ partition (IR-152 Block 49+, VGG19 Block 39+) produces a sharp MSE jump (e.g., VGG19: 0.066→0.137), and this gap persists under stress tests: adding FFT/normalization/NN augmentation to the transmitted Z and upgrading the inversion model with multi-head attention, SE, LSK, and MSCA modules narrows but does not close the gap (decision-level MSE stays ~4× higher). Label smoothing (α=0.3) further tightens intra-class radius R_c² around the GPZ, yielding slightly higher MSE at decision-level splits without degrading test accuracy (e.g., IR-152 CIFAR-10: 90.59% one-hot vs 90.76% LS+). ViT, which retains 256 patch tokens at every depth, shows no clear GPZ and remains vulnerable at all split points, confirming that architectural design (not just depth) governs the representational transition.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of MIA-in-CI defenses (noise perturbation, pruning, information bottlenecks, homomorphic encryption) by reframing the problem as a partition-point selection question rather than a perturbation-strength question. Its theoretical core builds on the data-processing inequality and maximum-entropy principles to derive a conditional-entropy lower bound whose decisive term shifts from global variance to the intra-class mean-squared radius R_c² at the representational transition. The Neural Vortex concept draws on label-smoothing dynamics and neural-collapse geometry but reframes them as a training-dynamics mechanism that actively contracts R_c², rather than a post-hoc description of end-stage representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway—that VGG-like backbones reach a GPZ with only 2.5% edge-side parameters while ResNet-style models require over 78%—gives CI deployers a concrete architecture-and-split selection criterion that prior perturbation-based defenses could not provide. Key limitations include the restriction to 64×64 vision models, the absence of a GPZ in ViT (where persistent patch tokens prevent the transition), and the unexplored text/sequence domain. Open questions include whether GPZ can be engineered into architectures that naturally resist the transition, how worst-case low-variance distributions shift the boundary, and whether the R_c²-based criterion generalizes to non-classification tasks.
