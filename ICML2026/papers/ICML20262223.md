## Problem

Recent stochastic image enhancement methods—diffusion models, Ornstein-Uhlenbeck processes, and diffusion bridges—are presented in disparate mathematical frameworks with tightly coupled schedulers, samplers, and discretizations, making it impossible to isolate the effect of the underlying process from implementation choices. The paper asks whether these methods are fundamentally different or merely reparameterizations of a common structure, and whether conditioning the stochastic trajectory on the degraded input genuinely improves restoration quality when all other factors are held fixed.

## Input / Output

Each method takes a degraded RGB image y (e.g., 64×64 downsampled for ×8 super-resolution, a dark frame for low-light enhancement, a grayscale image for colorization, or a rain-occluded frame for deraining) and produces a corresponding high-quality reconstruction. Some tasks (colorization, general super-resolution) operate in the latent space of a pretrained Stable Diffusion 2.1 VAE, while others work directly in pixel space. The conditioning image y is injected either as an additional network input (unconditional processes) or directly into the forward SDE drift and terminal distribution (OU and bridge processes).

## Method

The paper formulates a unified continuous-time SDE d x_t = f(x_t, t, y) dt + g(t) dw_t and shows that all considered methods are special cases differing only in drift, diffusion coefficient, terminal distribution, and boundary conditions. Key theoretical results include: (i) Flow Matching admits an SDE formulation whose reverse ODE recovers the original rectified flow (Prop. 2.1); (ii) ResShift's discrete Markov chain is the marginalization of a continuous OU process with a specific diffusion coefficient (Prop. 2.2); (iii) InDI is an OU process generalizing Flow Matching to arbitrary Gaussian priors (Prop. 2.3); and (iv) BBDM, DDBM-VE, and I²SB are provably equivalent via Doob's h-transform, differing only in scheduler choice (Remarks 2.4–2.5). Leveraging this unification, the authors conduct a controlled empirical study across four enhancement tasks using identical UNet/DiT backbones, MSE loss, and training protocols, finding no consistently dominant method. They identify two concrete failure mechanisms: low-temperature OU settings induce collinearity between intermediate states and the input (limiting detail recovery), and deterministic samplers on bridges cause oversmoothing by eliminating the sole source of stochasticity. The ItoVision PyTorch library implements the full framework for reproducible comparison.

## Results

Across five image enhancement tasks (facial super-resolution, low-light enhancement, colorization, deraining, and general super-resolution) evaluated with NFE=35, no single stochastic process family consistently dominates. Standard diffusion (DM-VP) achieves the best PSNR on facial SR (27.86) and low-light enhancement (23.01), while BBDM leads on deraining (PSNR 30.10, LPIPS 0.049) and DDBM-VE on colorization (PSNR 22.36, LPIPS 0.181). ResShift is the most consistently competitive method across tasks, and InDI is the clear outlier, with LPIPS degrading to 0.323 on low-light enhancement versus ~0.18–0.20 for all other methods, which the authors attribute to its low default temperature (τ=0.06).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central empirical finding is that, under identical architectures and training protocols, all 11 methods achieve broadly comparable performance, with no consistently dominant process family. DM-VP (standard diffusion) [Ho et al., 2020] matches or beats all conditioned methods on facial SR (PSNR 27.86 vs. 27.66 for ResShift) and low-light enhancement (PSNR 23.01, LPIPS 0.186), challenging the assumption that conditioning the trajectory on the degraded input inherently improves restoration. ResShift [Yue et al., 2023b] is the most consistently competitive OU-based method, ranking first or second on four of five tasks, while InDI [Delbracio & Milanfar, 2023] is the clear underperformer (LPIPS 0.323 on low-light vs. 0.186 for DM-VP), which the authors trace to its low default temperature (τ=0.06) inducing collinearity between intermediate states and the input. Among bridge methods, BBDM [Li et al., 2023] and DDBM-VE [Zhou et al., 2023] are the strongest, winning deraining (PSNR 30.10) and colorization (PSNR 22.36) respectively, while I²SB [Liu et al., 2023]—shown to be mathematically equivalent to DDBM-VE—performs slightly worse due to its original setup not conditioning the backbone on y. UniDB [Zhu et al., 2025] outperforms GOUB [Yue et al., 2023] on nearly all tasks, which the authors attribute to UniDB's endpoint regularization (finite γ) reducing overshooting rather than to any fundamental process difference. Flow Matching [Liu et al., 2022] and IR-SDE [Luo et al., 2023] fall in the middle of the pack, with IR-SDE showing a slight relative improvement when paired with a DiT backbone in supplementary experiments.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the reverse-time SDE framework of Anderson (1982) and Doob's h-transform (1984) to show that a wide range of stochastic image enhancement methods—unconditional diffusion (DM-VE/VP), flow matching, Ornstein-Uhlenbeck processes (IR-SDE, ResShift, InDI), and diffusion bridges (BBDM, DDBM, I²SB, GOUB, UniDB)—are all special cases of a single forward SDE differing only in drift, diffusion coefficient, terminal distribution, and boundary conditions. Conceptually, it follows the unification philosophy of EDM (Karras et al., 2022) and Albergo et al. (2025) for flows and diffusions, but extends it to the conditional image-enhancement setting where the degraded input y enters the process definition. A key departure from prior work is the explicit decoupling of process definitions from schedulers, samplers, and discretization strategies, which the authors argue were artificially coupled in the original method papers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the prevailing assumption that conditioning the stochastic trajectory on the degraded input inherently improves restoration quality, showing that under identical training protocols, plain diffusion models match or outperform specialized OU- and bridge-based approaches across multiple tasks. The identification of concrete failure mechanisms—low-temperature-induced collinearity in OU processes and oversmoothing from deterministic samplers in bridges—provides actionable diagnostics that go beyond surface-level benchmarking. The released ItoVision library and the equivalence results (e.g., BBDM = DDBM-VE = I²SB up to scheduler choice) establish a new baseline for fair comparison. Open questions include whether the unification and findings extend to unpaired data, video enhancement, or settings where the degradation operator is known, and whether the identified failure modes can be systematically mitigated without reverting to unconditional processes.
