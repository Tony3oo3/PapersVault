## Problem

Standard RL for legged locomotion assumes isotropic (direction-agnostic) costs and optimizes only expected return, overlooking two critical aspects: (1) directional anisotropy in effort (uphill vs. downhill, lateral vs. forward motion incur very different energy and risk), and (2) tail-risk sensitivity to rare but catastrophic events like slipping or hardware strain. The integration of a principled direction-dependent cost structure with explicit tail-risk optimization had not been addressed in prior work.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the robot's state vector (position, velocity) and local terrain geometry (uphill direction n(x), friction coefficient μ, slope angle θ(x)) to compute the per-step Finsler cost F(x,v); terrain information is used only in cost computation and is not provided to the policy as a privileged observation. The velocity v is read from the simulator state or approximated by finite differences. The output is a locomotion policy (joint torques) and a distributional critic that outputs K quantiles of the future cumulative cost distribution, from which CVaR_α is computed by averaging the worst m = ⌈αK⌉ quantiles.

## Method

FiRL replaces the standard scalar reward with a Finsler metric F(x,v) = w_e·√(vᵀM(x)v) + w_d·β(x)max(0, n(x)ᵀv) + w_f·λ‖v_⊥‖ that decomposes per-step cost into kinetic energy, uphill drift (asymmetric via the max operator), and lateral friction terms, making the cost positively 1-homogeneous and convex in v. The learning objective is a dynamic (nested) CVaR_α Bellman equation (TV)(x) = min_u {F(x,v(x,u)) + γ·CVaR_α[V(X′)]}, for which the paper proves a γ-contraction in the sup norm, guaranteeing a unique fixed point. The FiRL-AC algorithm uses a distributional critic outputting K quantiles to compute CVaR targets directly from the tail of the predicted cost distribution, combined with a PPO-style actor update using CVaR-based advantages and Bregman (KL) regularization for off-policy stability. The induced path cost d_F(x,y) = inf_τ ∫F(τ,τ̇)dt satisfies a triangle inequality while remaining asymmetric, providing a quasi-metric geometric interpretation of the learned anisotropic behavior.

## Results

On the aggregate MuJoCo locomotion benchmarks (SlopedHopper-12°, Walker2d-5°, HalfCheetah-5°), FiRL achieves 97.4% success rate, 0.87× normalized energy, and 0.80× normalized CVaR₀.₁ tail cost, all relative to the PPO baseline (=1.00). On the 12° sloped Hopper specifically, FiRL reduces worst-case CVaR₀.₁ impact forces by over 35% (from 1.60 to 0.72) and total energy by 16% (from 1.00 to 0.84) while improving success from 82.1% to 98.0%. In Isaac Sim quadruped trials, FiRL reduces fall rates by over 60% on the Staircase task (26%→10%) and cuts CVaR cost by ~44% on Ramp Climb (1.40→0.78) without the energy penalty seen in risk-averse baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FiRL dominates all five baselines on every reported metric (success, energy, CVaR). Against PPO (Schulman et al., 2017), FiRL improves success by 8.9 points (97.4% vs. 88.5%), reduces energy by 13%, and cuts tail cost by 47% (0.80 vs. 1.50). Against CVaR-PPO (Tamar et al., 2015), FiRL matches or exceeds the risk reduction (0.80 vs. 1.20 CVaR) while avoiding the 15% energy penalty that pure risk-aversion incurs through conservative 'freezing' behavior. Against the distributional QR-AC baseline (Lim & Malik, 2022), FiRL achieves 7.3 points higher success and 41% lower tail cost with 17% less energy. Against Riemannian PPO (Wang et al., 2020), FiRL gains 6.4 points in success and reduces CVaR by 43% (0.80 vs. 1.40), showing that symmetric Riemannian geometry cannot capture the directional asymmetry that Finsler cost encodes. Against Quasi-metric RL (Wang et al., 2023), FiRL improves success by 7.7 points and reduces both energy (0.87 vs. 1.10) and tail cost (0.80 vs. 1.32), indicating that enforcing triangle inequality on the value function alone is insufficient without direction-dependent local cost structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

FiRL sits at the intersection of three established threads: PPO-style actor-critic training (Schulman et al., 2017), CVaR-based risk-sensitive RL (Tamar et al., 2015; Chow et al., 2015), and distributional/quantile critics (Lim & Malik, 2022). It departs from prior geometry-aware RL (Riemannian PPO, quasi-metric RL) by replacing symmetric or goal-level geometric structure with a local, direction-dependent Finsler cost that encodes physical anisotropy (uphill vs. downhill, lateral slip) at the per-step level. The paper explicitly positions itself as the first to jointly optimize a Finsler anisotropic cost with a dynamic CVaR tail-risk objective, noting that prior work treated anisotropy (via reward shaping) and risk sensitivity (via CVaR or distributional RL) separately.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FiRL provides a principled geometric framework for direction-dependent locomotion costs with convergence guarantees (γ-contraction of the CVaR-Finsler Bellman operator) and a quasi-metric interpretation of the induced path cost, offering a cleaner inductive bias than ad-hoc reward shaping. The simultaneous 35% CVaR reduction and 15% energy reduction across MuJoCo and Isaac Sim benchmarks, supported by a 2×2 ablation isolating each component, makes the contribution practically relevant for safe legged-robot deployment. Key limitations include reliance on accurate terrain estimates (slope, friction), manually tuned anisotropy weights, and a fixed risk level α; open questions include learning F(x,v) from data, adaptive risk tuning, and full end-to-end hardware deployment with onboard perception and latency constraints.
