## Problem

Existing statistical detectors for AI-generated text suffer from two structural weaknesses: (i) boilerplate dominance, where high-probability tokens shared across human and LLM writing dilute discriminative signals when aggregating over all positions, and (ii) brittle point estimates, where collapsing each conditional distribution to a single probability score makes decisions highly sensitive to surface-level perturbations such as paraphrasing or decoding changes. The paper asks whether focusing on low-probability tokens and capturing distributional shape can simultaneously address both issues.

## Input / Output

The method takes a candidate text and a proxy language model (GPT-J-6B in the black-box setting) and produces a single scalar detection score that can be thresholded for binary human-vs-AI classification. No labeled training data is required (zero-shot). The score is computed by first identifying the bottom-ρ percentile of token positions by observed log-probability, then aggregating statistics only over those tail positions, making the output invariant to the length and domain of the input text through the percentile-based selection.

## Method

The core mechanism is a multiscale uncertainty estimator that selectively aggregates only low-probability tokens (bottom ρ percentile) to avoid boilerplate dominance. Locally, it computes the mean log-probability over these tail positions; globally, it computes Rényi entropy of the conditional distribution averaged over the same positions, capturing distributional shape beyond point estimates. These two signals are fused via a weighted combination into a unified score (Uncertainty). The enhanced variant (Uncertainty++) replaces the raw local statistic with a normalized discrepancy obtained via conditional independent sampling at each position (extending Fast-DetectGPT to the low-probability setting), yielding a more stable estimate. Theoretical analysis establishes concavity of the percentile operator (yielding a Jensen amplification effect for AI text) and a bounded perturbation guarantee for Rényi entropy at low-probability positions, showing it remains stable while log-probability diverges under multiplicative perturbations.

## Results

In the black-box setting (GPT-J as proxy), Uncertainty achieves 88.74% average AUROC across 12 source models, outperforming the strongest probability-based baseline Lastde (86.49%) by 2.25 points. Uncertainty++ reaches 93.24% average AUROC, surpassing the strongest sampling-based baseline Lastde++ (91.51%) by 1.73 points. In the white-box setting, Uncertainty++ achieves 99.07% average AUROC. Cross-domain generalization on FiQA, Wiki-csai, and arXiv yields 85.60% (Uncertainty) and 86.45% (Uncertainty++) average AUROC, improving over the strongest baselines by 9.85 and 3.62 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the probability-based baselines, Uncertainty (88.74% avg AUROC) substantially outperforms Likelihood (71.33%) and LogRank (74.83%) from GLTR by 17.4 and 13.9 points respectively, and beats DetectLRR (80.28%) by 8.5 points, indicating that low-probability token selection and Rényi entropy capture signals that uniform aggregation over all tokens misses. Against the closest probability-based competitor Lastde (86.49%), Uncertainty gains 2.25 points on average, with the largest per-model improvements on Llama2-13 (+3.54) and Gemma-7 (+3.82). In the sampling-based group, Uncertainty++ (93.24%) dominates DetectGPT (70.94%) by 22.3 points and Fast-DetectGPT (85.64%) by 7.6 points, while edging out Lastde++ (91.51%) by 1.73 points. The advantage over Lastde++ is most pronounced on Llama-13 (+4.06) and Llama2-13 (+3.39), though Uncertainty++ underperforms on GPT-4-Turbo (−3.35) and Llama3-8 (−1.79). Under paraphrasing attacks, Uncertainty maintains 82.39% average AUROC versus Lastde's 81.46%, and Uncertainty++ reaches 81.65% versus Lastde++'s 78.98%, confirming that the multiscale uncertainty signal is more robust to surface-level perturbations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the statistical detection family for AI-generated text by introducing two key departures from prior methods: selective aggregation over low-probability tokens (rather than uniform aggregation over all positions) and a multiscale uncertainty signal combining local log-probability with global Rényi entropy. The conditional independent sampling normalization in Uncertainty++ directly extends Fast-DetectGPT's framework to the percentile-aggregation setting, while the low-probability focus draws conceptual inspiration from membership inference methods like Min-K% Prob. The theoretical contribution (Proposition 3.4) provides a formal perturbation-robustness bound for Rényi entropy that has no direct analogue in prior detection literature, which typically relies on empirical robustness claims.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The empirical validation that low-probability tokens carry a 9.69× probability ratio (vs. 1.38× for high-probability tokens) provides a clear, actionable principle that improves not just the proposed method but also existing detectors like Likelihood (+24.53 AUROC when restricted to tail tokens). However, the practical impact is tempered by modest average gains over the strongest baseline (Lastde/Lastde++ by ~2 points), significant degradation under high-quality paraphrasing (>10 AUROC drop with GPT-5 paraphraser), and persistent difficulty on domain-specific content like FiQA. The ablation revealing that the Rényi entropy component contributes only ~0.9 AUROC (vs. 31.8 for log-probability) raises the question of whether the 'multiscale' framing overstates the role of the global signal, and whether simpler tail-aggregation variants might suffice in practice.
