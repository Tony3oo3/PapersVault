## Problem

Pre-trained graph models with fixed architectures cannot adjust their receptive field to match the diverse multi-hop requirements of different datasets, unlike foundation models in NLP or vision. Existing recurrent graph models (e.g., N²) assume the same iteration budget for training and testing, preventing flexible test-time computing. This paper asks whether a single pre-trained graph backbone can adapt to varying receptive field requirements at inference time without any architectural or parameter changes.

## Input / Output

The model takes a graph with node features X ∈ R^{n×d_in}, target label embeddings C ∈ R^{c×d}, and a user-specified iteration budget S as input. The step information is normalized to [0,1] via sinusoidal position encoding (fixing total time T=1), so the same model handles budgets from 8 to 300 without retraining. Output is a representation matrix Z ∈ R^{n_o×d} whose inner-product similarity with C yields predictions (softmax for classification, identity for regression), where n_o varies by task (n for node-level, m+m_neg for edge-level, 1 for graph-level).

## Method

AdaR reuses a single message-passing layer recursively, where the velocity function v_Θ explicitly depends on three signals: (1) current node representations via global+local message passing, (2) representation-target relations R computed through pseudo-node proxies that aggregate variable-sized target sets into fixed-size abstractions, and (3) normalized step information encoded via sinusoidal position encoding with fixed total time T=1. The theoretical contribution establishes step dependence as a necessary and sufficient condition for uniform convergence of the recurrent trajectory to a target ODE solution (Lemma 3.2, Theorem 3.3). Training uses gradient-based supervision at every recurrent step: L_step aligns per-step displacement with the task-loss gradient at intermediate representations, while L_full enforces global consistency between accumulated displacement and the initial gradient. This allows training with a small budget (S=8) and inference with much larger budgets (S=300) without retraining, with an early-exit criterion based on the average node-target inner-product similarity.

## Results

In the zero-shot inductive transfer setting, AdaR (0.4M params) achieves 36.15% on arxiv-year, 37.81% on WikiCS, 31.56% on SportsFit, 41.28% on Cora, 59.39% on CiteSeer, and 59.83% on DBLP, outperforming all baselines across all six downstream datasets. The paper reports an average absolute improvement of 15.89% over traditional GNN backbones, 13.52% over the recurrent model N², and 21.99% over zero-shot graph foundation models. In the supervised transductive setting, AdaR achieves 79.27% AUC on Questions, 94.08% on Minesweeper (heterophilic) and 97.89% on CoauthorPhysics (homophilic), yielding a 1.33% average improvement over N² and graph transformers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaR consistently outperforms all 24 baselines across both evaluation settings. Against traditional GNNs (GCN, GAT, GraphSAGE, GCNII), AdaR achieves an average absolute improvement of 15.89%, with the largest gap on CiteSeer (59.39% vs. 36.52% for GAT) and DBLP (59.83% vs. 55.93% for GAT). Against the closest recurrent baseline N², AdaR improves by 13.52% on average, demonstrating the critical role of explicit step information and target-aware relations. Among graph foundation models, AdaR (0.4M params) surpasses UniGTE (13.7B params) on WikiCS (37.81% vs. 34.91%) and SportsFit (31.56% vs. 29.89%), and outperforms OFA on arxiv-year (36.15% vs. 4.67%) and DBLP (59.83% vs. 51.01%), achieving a 21.99% average improvement over foundation models. In the transductive setting, AdaR edges out N² by 1.33% on average and beats graph transformers (Graphormer, GraphGPS, Exphormer, NAGphormer) on all four homophilic datasets, e.g., 97.89% vs. 97.56% on CoauthorPhysics and 95.93% vs. 95.77% on CoauthorCS. Notably, AdaR achieves these gains with only 0.4M trainable parameters and a training budget of 8 iterations, while being evaluated with up to 300 iterations at test time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the recurrent graph model paradigm introduced by N² (Sun et al., 2024), which enabled global message passing via pseudo nodes but assumed a fixed iteration budget for both training and testing. The key departure is twofold: theoretically, the authors prove that explicit step dependence is a necessary and sufficient condition for a recurrent process to converge uniformly across different step budgets (Lemma 3.2, Theorem 3.3); architecturally, AdaR encodes normalized step information via sinusoidal position encoding (fixing total time T=1) and representation-target relations through proxy pseudo nodes, neither of which N² or earlier gated recurrent GNNs (Li et al., 2017; Ruiz et al., 2020) incorporate. This positions AdaR as the first recurrent graph model that can be trained with a small iteration budget and evaluated with substantially larger budgets without retraining or architectural modification, distinguishing it from both fixed-depth GNNs and LLM-based graph foundation models that rely on external knowledge for adaptation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental obstacle to 'one-for-all' pre-training on graphs: the mismatch between diverse receptive field requirements across datasets and the rigid layer-stacking design of standard GNNs. By enabling flexible test-time computing, AdaR offers a parameter-free adaptation mechanism that avoids the resource costs of fine-tuning, prompting, or LLM integration. However, the model still operates under a pre-defined maximum iteration budget (S=300 in practice), and determining an input-adaptive stopping criterion that automatically infers the optimal budget remains open. The large gaps over graph foundation models (21.99% average) are somewhat inflated by the weak performance of those baselines (several near random), so the most meaningful comparison is the 13.52% improvement over N², the direct architectural predecessor.
