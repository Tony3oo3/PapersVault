## Problem

Diffusion model attention enables beneficial global context integration but simultaneously causes spurious mixing of incompatible features (e.g., blending materials between distinct objects), and distinguishing these two effects remains non-trivial because they share the same underlying mechanism. Prior analyses treat attention at a token-wise retrieval level and overlook the dynamical consequences of the asymmetric structure in the pre-softmax attention matrix, leaving no principled way to identify when retrieval is trapped in metastable mixtures or to control the resulting fidelity-diversity tradeoff.

## Input / Output

The method is a training-free, inference-time intervention applied to the self-attention layers of a pretrained diffusion model (SDXL UNet or SD3 MMDiT). It consumes the text prompt and the noisy latent state at each denoising step, modifying the pre-softmax attention logits within the UNet/MMDiT blocks before the softmax and value-mixing. The output is the final generated RGB image (1024×1024 for SDXL), evaluated on COCO2014 captions with 30 sampling steps and classifier-free guidance ω=5.0. No additional inputs or architectural changes are required beyond two scalar hyperparameters (α, β) controlling circulation strength and blending magnitude.

## Method

The paper characterizes the pre-softmax attention matrix QK^T = XWX^T as a dynamic associative memory encoding pairwise feature associations, then decomposes it into a symmetric component (defining a Hopfield-style quadratic energy landscape) and a skew-symmetric component (driving circulation with zero quadratic energy). From the symmetric component, three stability measures are derived: global Hopfield energy, a spatially resolved instability fraction (fraction of positions where the local field conflicts with the state), and a cosine alignment score, all of which correlate with the fidelity-diversity tradeoff in generated samples. The core intervention scales the skew-symmetric component by a factor α to inject circulation-driven drift that perturbs metastable mixtures, then blends the resulting retrieval with the baseline retrieval using a second coefficient β, preserving the energy landscape while selectively destabilizing incoherent configurations. An adaptive variant computes a per-sample functional symmetry index η_M from the realized attention and modulates both α and β so that already-stable retrievals receive less perturbation, preventing degradation of high-quality samples.

## Results

On 1K COCO2014 prompts with SDXL (ω=5.0, 30 steps), the proposed skew-symmetric perturbation improves Aesthetic Score from 5.644 (baseline) to 5.735 at (α,β)=(1.10,6), while ImageReward degrades from 0.546 to 0.442 at the same setting, illustrating the fidelity-diversity trade-off. On the worst-20% subset by ImageReward, the method recovers up to +0.526 IR (α=1.05, β=6) and +0.353 Aesthetic (α=1.15, β=4), while the top-20% subset suffers up to −0.190 IR degradation. The adaptive variant (Table 6) prevents the collapse seen with excessive static perturbation: at (α,β)=(1.20,5), static perturbation drops IR to −1.486, whereas adaptive control recovers to 0.568 (above baseline 0.487).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external competing methods; instead it evaluates the proposed training-free intervention against the unmodified SDXL baseline and against a simpler attention-temperature scaling baseline. Against the baseline, the method shows a clear regime-dependent effect: on the worst-20% samples it yields large gains (up to +0.526 ImageReward, +0.353 Aesthetic), while on the top-20% samples it causes moderate degradation (up to −0.190 ImageReward, −0.104 Aesthetic), confirming that circulation injection selectively repairs metastable mixtures without uniformly altering all samples. Against the attention-temperature baseline (QK^T/τ), the proposed method qualitatively outperforms by preserving strongly supported structure while suppressing weakly supported mixture artifacts, whereas temperature scaling introduces unintended structures such as duplicated limbs (Figure 9). The adaptive variant (using the functional symmetry index η_M to modulate α and β) dominates the static variant at excessive perturbation strengths: at (α,β)=(1.20,5), static perturbation collapses ImageReward to −1.486, while adaptive control recovers to 0.568, exceeding the baseline of 0.487. On the broader SD3 MMDiT architecture (Table 7), the method generalizes, improving IR by +0.439 to +0.505 on the low-quality subset while keeping Aesthetic and CLIP broadly stable on the full 1K set.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line connecting transformer self-attention to Hopfield networks (Ramsauer et al., 2021) by shifting the analysis from token-level retrieval to the structure of the pre-softmax attention matrix QK^T itself. It draws on classical results from asymmetric Hopfield networks (Singh et al., 1995; Chengxiang et al., 2000) showing that skew-symmetric couplings reduce the number of stable attractors, and repurposes this as a practical, training-free 'circulation knob' for diffusion model generation. Unlike prior attention-as-memory work that treats QK^T as a static retrieval operator or focuses on parameter-level memories (Bietti et al., 2023), this paper decomposes the realized interaction matrix into symmetric (energy) and skew-symmetric (circulation) parts and shows each plays a distinct, controllable role in the fidelity-diversity tradeoff.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Hopfield-style stability measures (energy, instability fraction, alignment) offer a diagnostic lens for identifying when attention has settled into spurious feature mixtures, which is a long-standing but poorly understood failure mode in diffusion models. The training-free skew-symmetric perturbation provides a lightweight test-time intervention, and the adaptive variant using the functional symmetry index η_M addresses the state-dependence problem (helping unstable samples while avoiding degradation of stable ones). However, aggregate gains are modest and mixed—stronger perturbation improves Aesthetic Score but degrades ImageReward and CLIPScore—and the method's effectiveness is inherently regime-dependent, limiting its plug-and-play utility. Open questions include whether the stability measures can inform training-time objectives, whether the approach transfers to autoregressive or video generation, and whether the circulation control can be made fully automatic without manual (α, β) tuning.
