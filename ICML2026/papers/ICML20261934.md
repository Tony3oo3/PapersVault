## Problem

Most document parsing systems—both traditional pipelines and recent MLLM-based methods—still rely on explicit or implicit Crop-OCR-Merge cascades, where early detection or layout errors propagate and corrupt the final output. The paper asks whether a compact end-to-end model can directly map full-page images to structured Markdown without any slicing or layout-detection dependency, while simultaneously handling heterogeneous elements (dense text, complex tables, formulas) whose optimization objectives conflict.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261934/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single full-page document image at high resolution (up to 16384-token context with dynamic resolution) and directly generates a structured Markdown document. Tables are emitted as HTML, formulas as LaTeX, and embedded images as HTML img tags with bounding-box coordinates scaled to [0, 1000). No intermediate crops, layout annotations, or multi-stage coordination are involved; the 1B-parameter model (SigLIP2-400m encoder + Qwen3-0.8B LLM) produces the entire serialization in one autoregressive pass.

## Method

OvisOCR combines two synergistic pillars. For data construction, a cross-model distillation pipeline uses PaddleOCR-VL as a specialized perceiver to extract dense bounding-box/content candidates, then Gemini-2.5-Pro as a general-reasoning auditor to filter samples with hallucinations, incomplete content, broken table/formula syntax, or incorrect reading order. For optimization, the paper extends GDPO to document parsing by normalizing category-specific rewards (text NED, table TEDS, formula CDM) independently within each group before summing advantages, preventing the reward collapse that causes standard GRPO to sacrifice fine-grained perception for structural formatting. Training proceeds in two stages: full-parameter SFT on the audited dataset (1 epoch, batch 512, lr 5e-6), followed by GDPO preference alignment with 8 rollouts per image (2 epochs, batch 256, lr 5e-7 cosine decay, KL coefficient 0.2) to refine structural precision while preserving SFT fluency.

## Results

On OmniDocBench v1.5, OvisOCR (1B) achieves the best Overall score of 93.19, with Text Edit Distance of 0.032, Formula CDM of 91.99, Table TEDS of 90.79, and Reading Order Edit of 0.033. It also attains the best inference efficiency (744.57 s total, 1.820 pages/s) among all compared methods under a unified vLLM backend. On generalization benchmarks, OvisOCR scores 701 on OCRBench, 23.7 on OCRBench v2-EN, and 43.2 on OCRBench v2-CN, outperforming all specialized parsers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OvisOCR (1B) outperforms the closest specialized MLLM, PaddleOCR-VL (0.9B), by 0.33 points in Overall score (93.19 vs. 92.86), with clear gains in Text Edit (0.032 vs. 0.035), Formula CDM (91.99 vs. 91.22), and Reading Order (0.033 vs. 0.043), though it trails slightly on Table TEDS (90.79 vs. 90.89) and Table TEDS-S (93.75 vs. 94.76). Against larger specialized models, OvisOCR surpasses DeepSeek-OCR-2 (3B, 91.09) by 2.1 points and MinerU2.5 (1.2B, 90.67) by 2.5 points despite having fewer or comparable parameters. Compared to massive general MLLMs, OvisOCR leads Qwen3-VL-235B (235B, 89.15) by 4.0 points and Gemini-2.5 Pro (88.03) by 5.2 points, demonstrating that parameter scale alone does not guarantee fine-grained OCR fidelity. Against pipeline tools, the gap is even wider: OvisOCR exceeds PP-StructureV3 (86.73) by 6.5 points and Marker (71.30) by 21.9 points, with Reading Order Edit of 0.033 versus Marker's 0.250 (nearly 6× worse). On generalization benchmarks (OCRBench, v2-EN, v2-CN), OvisOCR leads all specialized parsers by 46–70 points on OCRBench and 1.9–2.1 points on the v2 variants.

## Relation to Prior Work

OvisOCR builds on the Ovis architecture (Lu et al., 2024; 2025) as its base vision-language model and extends the GDPO framework (Liu et al., 2026) from general multi-reward RL to document parsing via category-specific rewards (NED for text, TEDS for tables, CDM for formulas). Conceptually, it departs from the dominant 'Crop-OCR-Merge' pipeline paradigm (PaddleOCR, MinerU, Marker) and from recent specialized MLLMs that still rely on implicit image slicing, by committing to a strictly end-to-end full-page-to-Markdown generation. The data construction pipeline is novel in combining a specialized OCR engine (PaddleOCR-VL) for dense local evidence with a general MLLM (Gemini-2.5-Pro) as a semantic auditor, rather than using either alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a 1B end-to-end model can match or exceed 235B general MLLMs on document parsing, suggesting that specialized alignment and training recipe matter more than raw scale for perception-heavy tasks. The GDPO extension with decoupled, category-wise advantage normalization offers a reusable recipe for any multi-element generation task where heterogeneous components have conflicting optimization dynamics. A notable limitation is that table reconstruction (TEDS) still lags behind PaddleOCR-VL, particularly on complex layouts (newspaper: 64.5 vs 78.7), indicating that structural fidelity for highly irregular tables remains an open challenge. The cross-model distillation pipeline, while effective, depends on access to a strong proprietary auditor (Gemini-2.5-Pro), raising questions about reproducibility and cost at scale.
