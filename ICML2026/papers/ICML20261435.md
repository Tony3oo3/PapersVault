## Problem

Vision-language models like CLIP are highly vulnerable to adversarial perturbations, and existing test-time adaptation methods that rely on random multi-view augmentation tend to destroy the discriminative regions critical for fine-grained recognition. Semantics-preserving augmentation methods operate in feature space, where adversarial attacks have already pushed representations beyond decision boundaries, making them unreliable as semantic anchors. The paper asks whether discriminative semantic regions can be identified and preserved at test time without any training in feature space, even under adversarial attacks.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single test image (224×224 RGB, potentially adversarially perturbed via PGD with ε=4/255) and a set of text class prompts (e.g., 'a photo of a {class}') as input, and outputs a predicted class label. Internally, it generates N augmented views (default 64) per sample and computes per-view attention maps that serve as intermediate semantic anchors. The final prediction is a weighted ensemble over selected low-entropy views, where weights are derived from the anisotropic Total Variation of each view's attention map. The method is entirely test-time and per-sample, requiring no labeled data or additional models beyond the pretrained CLIP backbone (ViT-B/16, ViT-B/32, or ResNet50).

## Method

A-TPT consists of three components operating entirely at test time. First, Gradient Attention Refinement replaces the standard attention-based gradient signal in Gradient Attention Rollout with a token-based weighting vector computed as the ℓ₁-normalized rectified inner product of token embeddings and their gradients with respect to the target logit, decoupling the weighting from the second-order-sensitive attention gradient and yielding robust CLS-to-patch attention maps under adversarial perturbations. Second, Attention-Guided Multi-View Augmentation uses the refined attention map as a spatial mask to apply position-dependent mixing strengths: high-attention regions (top-r fraction) receive low mixing strength (m_high=0.8) to preserve discriminative parts, while low-attention regions receive high mixing strength (m_low=0.2) to introduce diversity, generating semantics-preserving views from AugMix perturbations. Third, TV-Based Ensemble computes the anisotropic Total Variation of each view's attention map to assign reliability weights—views with smoother, less fragmented attention distributions receive higher weights—then aggregates predictions via a weighted sum over selected low-entropy views. Prompt tuning minimizes entropy across these attention-guided views using one step of Adam optimization per sample.

## Results

On eight fine-grained classification tasks (ViT-B/16), A-TPT achieves 45.7% average adversarial accuracy under PGD (ε=4/255, 100 steps), substantially outperforming the SOTA test-time method R-TPT (39.9%) and MTA (27.4%). On clean data, A-TPT reaches 63.0% average accuracy, surpassing CLIP's zero-shot 61.4% and R-TPT's 61.1%. On ImageNet-OOD benchmarks (ResNet50), A-TPT attains 35.8% average adversarial accuracy versus CLIP's 0.3%, while maintaining competitive clean accuracy (48.0% vs. 47.1% for R-TPT).

## Baselines & Comparisons

A-TPT consistently outperforms all test-time baselines on adversarial accuracy across all eight fine-grained datasets. Against the strongest test-time competitor R-TPT (CVPR 2025), A-TPT gains +5.8% average adversarial accuracy (45.7% vs. 39.9%, ViT-B/16) and +5.0% (ViT-B/32), with the largest margins on fine-grained tasks like Flower102 (+8.0%) and UCF101 (+8.5%). MTA (CVPR 2024) trails by 18.3% on ViT-B/16, and TPT-Ensemble (NeurIPS 2022) by 13.8%, confirming that attention-guided semantic preservation is critical for fine-grained robustness. On clean data, A-TPT (63.0%) also edges out MTA (62.4%) and R-TPT (61.1%), showing that the method does not sacrifice standard performance. Compared to training-time defenses (FARE, APT+TeCoA, TeCoA), A-TPT matches or exceeds their adversarial accuracy (40.3% vs. 40.3% for FARE on ViT-B/32) while achieving substantially higher clean accuracy (58.6% vs. 42.2% for FARE), without requiring any labeled adversarial training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the test-time prompt tuning (TPT) framework of Shu et al. (2022) and the Gradient Attention Rollout (GAR) interpretability method of Chefer et al. (2021) into a unified adversarial defense pipeline. It departs from prior test-time adaptation methods (R-TPT, MTA, TPT-Ensemble) that apply uniform or random multi-view augmentation, by introducing attention-guided spatially varying augmentation that explicitly protects discriminative regions. It also departs from semantics-preserving augmentation literature (NAS, FN-NET, implicit semantic augmentation) that operates in feature space and requires training, by identifying semantic anchors directly from robustified attention maps at test time without any learned components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap where fine-grained VLMs are especially vulnerable to adversarial attacks because their discriminative regions are small and easily corrupted by random augmentation. The training-free, model-free nature of A-TPT makes it deployable without retraining or auxiliary networks, and its competitive performance against training-time defenses (FARE, APT+TeCoA) while preserving clean accuracy is practically significant. However, the method still relies on multi-view augmentation and is not entirely immune to adversarial attacks, and the authors note that exploring discriminative semantics in latent space remains an open direction. The theoretical analysis showing second-order sensitivity of standard GAR versus first-order stability of token-based weighting provides a clean justification but does not fundamentally change how the field approaches adversarial robustness.
