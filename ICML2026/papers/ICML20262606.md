## Problem

Speculative decoding with EAGLE-3 has become the de facto standard for LLM inference acceleration, yet its adoption is limited by three gaps: scarce high-quality draft models for mainstream LLMs, insufficient quality of existing open-source drafts trained on narrow datasets, and the absence of scalable training infrastructure. Existing implementations couple the frozen target model and lightweight draft model under a single parallelization strategy (typically FSDP/ZeRO), which is fundamentally suboptimal given the massive scale asymmetry between the two and prevents the target model from leveraging inference-optimized execution.

## Input / Output

SpecForge takes a frozen target model (8B to 235B+ parameters, including MoE architectures), a conversation corpus (e.g., OpenPerfectBlend with 1.4M balanced samples across chat, math, coding, and instruction domains), and a draft architecture config (typically a single Transformer layer at 3–5% of target size). The target model is consumed exclusively in inference mode to produce hidden states for the training prefix, while the draft model is trained from scratch. The output is a production-ready EAGLE-3 draft checkpoint directly deployable in SGLang, vLLM, or TensorRT-LLM without modification, released as SpecBundle covering Llama3/4, Qwen-3, GPT-OSS, Kimi K2, and Ling-Flash.

## Method

SpecForge's core contribution is target-draft decoupling with hybrid parallelism: the target model executes through SGLang's inference runner (supporting TP, EP, and DP with FlashAttention and CUDA Graphs), while the draft model trains independently via ZeRO-2/FSDP, eliminating the communication overhead of ZeRO-3 all-gathers during target inference. Each target instance produces M×N hidden-state samples per step, and co-located draft models consume disjoint 1/N shards without cross-device communication. For TTT optimization, SpecForge introduces sparse tree attention using FlexAttention with a BlockMask that encodes the candidate-tree structure (reducing peak attention memory by 93.5%) and a custom Triton kernel for in-place log-softmax backward that overwrites logits with gradients (reducing loss memory by 23.3%). Together these techniques achieve up to 9.9× end-to-end training speedup over the monolithic baseline on Qwen3-235B-A22B.

## Results

SpecForge achieves up to 9.99× end-to-end training speedup over the SafeAILab EAGLE-3 baseline on Qwen3-235B-A22B (20,227.2 vs. 2,025.7 tokens/s on 8×H200), with consistent gains across all tested scales: 2.01× on LLaMA3.1-8B, 1.39× on LLaMA3.3-70B, and 4.31× on Qwen3-30B-A3B. The TTT sparse-tree-attention kernel reduces attention execution time by 62.1% and peak memory by 93.5% at sequence length 4096, while the in-place log-softmax backward kernel cuts peak memory from 7.32 GB to 5.62 GB (23.3% reduction). Released SpecBundle draft models deliver up to 4.48× inference speedup over non-speculative baselines and 1.61×–4.48× over existing open-source EAGLE-3 checkpoints on math and coding benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the SafeAILab EAGLE-3 official implementation (the primary training baseline), SpecForge wins on every model tested, with the gap widening dramatically at scale: 2.01× on LLaMA3.1-8B (where both use no target-model parallelism, isolating the benefit of SGLang's optimized prefill), 4.31× on Qwen3-30B-A3B, and 9.99× on Qwen3-235B-A22B where the baseline's ZeRO-3 all-gather overhead makes it communication-bound. The baseline also OOMs under ZeRO-2 on both LLaMA3.3-70B and Qwen3-235B-A22B, whereas SpecForge handles these models comfortably. Against existing open-source EAGLE-3 draft checkpoints (from the original EAGLE-3 authors and LMSYS), SpecBundle achieves 1.61×–4.48× higher inference throughput on math and coding benchmarks (e.g., 4.48× on LiveCodeBench for Llama-4-Scout, 3.76× on Math500), attributed to training on the more diverse OpenPerfectBlend corpus with regenerated responses rather than the ShareGPT/UltraChat data used for prior checkpoints. For models lacking any existing draft (Qwen-30B-A3B, Ling-Flash-V2, Kimi-K2), SpecBundle is the only available option, delivering 1.40×–2.29× speedup over non-speculative inference.

## Relation to Prior Work

SpecForge builds directly on EAGLE-3's Training-Time Test (TTT) mechanism but departs fundamentally from prior EAGLE training implementations (SafeAILab, NVIDIA Model Optimizer) that couple the frozen target model and trainable draft model under a single parallelization strategy (typically FSDP/ZeRO). The key architectural departure is target-draft decoupling: the target model is executed through a production inference engine (SGLang) with inference-optimized parallelism (TP/EP), while the draft model is trained with a lightweight training framework (DeepSpeed ZeRO-2). This is positioned as the first integration of a production-grade inference engine into the speculative decoding training loop, complementing algorithmic improvements like FastEAGLE's curriculum redesign and orthogonal to new paradigms like DFlash's diffusion-based drafting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SpecForge addresses a critical practical bottleneck: the lack of scalable infrastructure and high-quality draft models that has limited speculative decoding adoption in production. The release of SpecBundle (draft models for Llama, Qwen, Kimi, GPT-OSS, Ling-Flash) fills a concrete ecosystem gap, and the systematic training recipes (TTT length selection, data regeneration, dense-vs-MoE architecture) provide actionable guidance. Open questions include whether disaggregated target-draft placement can further improve scalability, whether adaptive sample-dependent TTT can reduce training cost, and whether the dense-over-MoE finding for drafts generalizes to larger training corpora. The framework's extensibility to new speculative decoding paradigms (e.g., diffusion-based) positions it as a potential standard training substrate.
