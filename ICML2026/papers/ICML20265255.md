## Problem

Current latent visual reasoning methods reshape low-level image features under visual supervision, producing latents that capture local appearance but carry limited high-level abstract semantics such as color, texture, object category, state, and relation. Richer reasoning requires a semantic carrier that represents region attributes and remains stable across different questions grounded in the same visual region, a requirement that existing approaches do not address.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265255/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image and a text question as input, where the image is encoded by a frozen vision encoder into patch features and a region of interest is specified by an index set of patch tokens. Internally, the model produces two latent representations: a region-level visual latent (a sequence of hidden states aligned to vision encoder features) and a single semantic latent (a 4096-dimensional vector aligned to attribute-level semantic embeddings). The final output is a text answer generated autoregressively after the latent tokens, with no explicit visual cropping or tool invocation at inference time.

## Method

SLVR is a two-stage framework built on Qwen2.5-VL-7B. In Stage 1, a dedicated semantic attribute extraction head (a special <sem> token inserted after the region latent segment) is trained to produce a 4096-dim semantic latent aligned via MSE loss to attribute-level embeddings derived from structured region profiles (entities, color, shape, material, action, spatial relations, text), while the region-level visual latent is simultaneously aligned to vision encoder features following the LVR paradigm. In Stage 2, Multi-query Group Relative Policy Optimization (M-GRPO) groups two semantically distinct questions grounded in the same region and jointly optimizes the latent-generation policy using a composite reward: binary answer correctness (judged by Qwen3-Max), pairwise latent consistency across queries (penalizing L2 distance between semantic and visual latents of different questions), and a margin-based stability regularization that anchors optimized latents to frozen Stage 1 references within tolerance thresholds τ_sem and τ_vis. The key insight is that intrinsic region attributes should remain invariant across different questions, so M-GRPO constrains the degree of attribute variation while preserving per-query answer correctness.

## Results

On standard VQA benchmarks, SLVR-7B achieves 61.8% on OKVQA, 57.8% on VizWiz, 77.2% on ChartQA, and 79.3% on TextVQA, outperforming the primary latent-reasoning baseline LVR by +11.2, +24.7, +12.8, and +4.2 points respectively. On the proposed SV-QA benchmark (591 paired region-centric samples), SLVR achieves 69.1% joint correctness (Both Correct) on V*, 61.1% on HRBench-4K, and 50.6% on HRBench-8K, improving over LVR by +3.7, +3.2, and +4.0 points. On VisualPuzzles, SLVR reaches 34.2% overall accuracy versus 29.4% for LVR (+4.8), with the largest gains in Deductive (+5.0) and Spatial (+7.4) reasoning. On broader SoTA benchmarks (Table 6), SLVR scores 866 on OCRBench, 63.4 on MMStar, 59.2 on MMMU, and 82.9 on MMB_1.1, surpassing LVR by +6.0, +2.3, +11.6, and +35.1 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary latent-reasoning baseline LVR, SLVR achieves large gains on knowledge-intensive and fine-grained benchmarks (OKVQA +11.2, VizWiz +24.7, ChartQA +12.8) but slightly underperforms on GQA (−1.8) and AI2D (−1.3), suggesting the semantic enrichment is most beneficial when external knowledge or attribute-level reasoning is required. Compared to think-with-image methods (GRIT, DeepEyes, Visual-SR1), SLVR dominates across nearly all VQA benchmarks without incurring the computational overhead of explicit visual cropping at inference time. On the SV-QA benchmark, SLVR achieves the best joint-correctness (Both Correct) scores on HRBench-4K (61.1%) and HRBench-8K (50.6%), while DeepEyes achieves higher single-question Q1 accuracy on V* (83.3 vs. 82.2) at the cost of explicit cropping. In the broader SoTA comparison (Table 6), SLVR-7B outperforms all open-source think-with-image baselines (GRIT, ViCrop, DeepEyes, Visual-SR1, Chain-of-Focus, Pixel Reasoner) on OCRBench and MMMU, and approaches or matches closed-source GPT-4o on OCRBench (866 vs. 736) and MMB_1.1 (82.9 vs. 82.2), though it trails GPT-4o on MMMU (59.2 vs. 69.2) and Gemini-1.5-Pro on MMMU (59.2 vs. 62.2). The most striking single-baseline gap is the +35.1-point improvement over LVR on MMB_1.1 (82.9 vs. 47.8), indicating that semantic-enriched latents substantially close the gap to text-only reasoning on general multimodal understanding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the latent visual reasoning paradigm introduced by LVR (Li et al., 2025), which performs autoregressive reasoning in visual embedding space under visual supervision, by adding a dedicated semantic attribute extraction head that encodes region-level properties (color, texture, object category, state, relation) into a compact semantic latent. For the optimization stage, it adapts the GRPO objective from DeepSeek-R1 (Guo et al., 2025) into M-GRPO, which groups multiple semantically distinct queries grounded in the same visual region and enforces cross-query latent consistency alongside answer correctness. The work also positions itself against explicit 'thinking with images' methods (GRIT, DeepEyes, Chain-of-Focus) that rely on iterative visual cropping at inference time, arguing that a semantically enriched latent representation can achieve comparable reasoning without such computational overhead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete gap in latent visual reasoning: existing methods preserve low-level appearance but lack structured high-level semantics, limiting their ability to reason consistently when the same region is queried from different angles. The SV-QA benchmark (591 paired samples) provides a useful evaluation protocol for measuring this semantic-variation robustness, and the M-GRPO cross-query consistency constraint is a principled training signal that could transfer to other multi-view or multi-query settings. However, the improvements over LVR are modest (typically 2–4 points on SV-QA, with some regressions on GQA and AI2D), the annotation pipeline carries a 7.29% error rate, and the authors acknowledge that latent representations are not sampled, limiting reasoning-path diversity. Scaling beyond 7B and exploring latent-space sampling remain open questions.
