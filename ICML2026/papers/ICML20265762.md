## Problem

Time-reversal sampling methods for generative inbetweening maintain two denoising trajectories conditioned on the start and end frames, but these trajectories are driven by distinct motion priors that are simply stitched together without explicit reconciliation. This leads to temporal inconsistencies, ghosting, and reverse-motion artifacts, particularly when the two keyframes imply incompatible motions for the same scene.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265762/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of RGB keyframes (start and end) as input and synthesizes a temporally coherent intermediate video clip (25 frames at 1024×576) as output. It operates purely at inference time as a plug-and-play module on top of a pre-trained I2V diffusion backbone (SVD-XT, UNet-based, EDM parameterization, 25-step Euler solver), requiring no additional training or architectural modification. It can be seamlessly integrated into both parallel (TRF) and sequential (ViBiD) time-reversal samplers.

## Method

MR-CATR constructs a consensus motion axis at each reverse diffusion step by computing frame-to-frame temporal residuals from both the start-conditioned and end-conditioned denoised estimates, then combining them via a weighted blend (κ=0.2) gated by their cosine similarity. The backward update direction is projected onto this motion axis via orthogonal projection, removing components that conflict with the shared motion direction while preserving the end-conditioned magnitude along the axis. A conflict-aware fusion weight, modulated by both directional agreement (cosine similarity between forward and aligned backward updates) and a monotonic noise-level schedule (ρ_t = 1 − (σ_t/σ_T)^2), determines how much the aligned backward correction contributes to the final update. A shared-region mask restricts strong alignment to spatial locations where both keyframes depict the same objects, preserving flexibility in regions with new or rearranged content. The module is activated only during the first 0.2T highest-noise steps, where global motion trajectory is being established.

## Results

On DAVIS, MR-CATR+ViBiD achieves the best LPIPS (0.2103) and FVD (519.47), while MR-CATR+TRF achieves the best FID (35.482). On Pexels, MR-CATR+ViBiD leads in LPIPS (0.1598) and FVD (462.77), and MR-CATR+TRF leads in FID (35.261). Across both datasets, MR-CATR consistently outperforms the strongest prior method (MPD) by 4–22 FVD points and 0.01–0.03 LPIPS, with the largest gains in FVD indicating substantially improved temporal coherence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MR-CATR outperforms all five baselines on the majority of metrics across both DAVIS and Pexels. Against the strongest prior method MPD, MR-CATR+ViBiD reduces FVD from 527.44 to 519.47 on DAVIS (−8.0) and from 488.45 to 462.77 on Pexels (−25.7), while also improving LPIPS by 0.021 and 0.016 respectively. Against the parallel TRF backbone, MR-CATR cuts FVD by 120 points on DAVIS (643.92→523.56) and 141 points on Pexels (608.54→467.23). Against the sequential ViBiD backbone, FVD drops from 548.25 to 519.47 on DAVIS and from 517.84 to 462.77 on Pexels. The non-generative FILM baseline is clearly outperformed on all metrics, with FVD gaps exceeding 200 points. On VBench, MR-CATR+ViBiD achieves the highest subject consistency (0.9520 on DAVIS, 0.9274 on Pexels) and background consistency (0.9238 on Pexels), confirming gains in perceptual quality beyond distribution-level metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the time-reversal sampling paradigm for generative inbetweening, building directly on TRF (parallel linear blending) and ViBiD (sequential re-noising) as backbone samplers. It departs from the most recent prior work, MPD, which resolves bidirectional motion conflict by collapsing both paths into a single start-conditioned prior; MR-CATR instead preserves end-frame motion cues by projecting the backward update onto a residual-guided consensus axis and applying conflict-aware gated fusion. The method is entirely inference-time and training-free, requiring no architectural changes to the underlying I2V diffusion model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MR-CATR offers a practical, plug-and-play module that can be inserted into existing time-reversal samplers with only 5–7% runtime overhead, making it immediately deployable for practitioners working with SVD or similar I2V backbones. The residual-guided projection is grounded in orthogonal decomposition rather than heuristic blending, and the motion-magnitude analysis (Table 10) shows that resolving early conflict actually *increases* generated motion strength toward ground truth, a non-obvious side effect. Open questions include how the shared-region mask should be computed in practice (treated as given), whether the early-window-only activation strategy transfers to longer sequences or different noise schedules, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the two tested backbones (SVD-XT, Wan-I2V) to more diverse diffusion parameterizations.
