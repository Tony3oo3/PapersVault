## Problem

Existing multi-agent communication topology design methods either rely on fixed hand-crafted structures or generate topologies in a single step, limiting fine-grained structural exploration and failing to account for communication redundancy. No prior approach jointly reasons about structure formation and redundancy control during topology construction, leading to excessive token overhead on simple tasks and insufficient capability on complex ones.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266005/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a task query (encoded to a 384-dimensional embedding via all-MiniLM-L6-v2), a pool of candidate LLM agents (5 gpt-4o-mini instances with assigned roles), and available tools as input, and produces a directed communication topology as a binary adjacency matrix over the agent set. The generated topology is then executed via topological-sort-ordered message passing over K rounds, with an aggregation function (majority voting, concatenation, or last-agent output) yielding the final task answer. Edge generation is performed jointly per node (not autoregressively per edge), reducing generation steps to O(N).

## Method

RADAR formulates topology design as a conditional discrete graph diffusion problem with a redundancy-aware ordering network (3-layer RGCN) that selects which node to mask at each forward step based on the node's effective size—a graph-theoretic measure of non-redundant information sources in its ego network—and a denoising network (3-layer GAT) that reconstructs the masked node's role and all its edges to previously denoised nodes simultaneously via a mixture of multinomial distributions. The effective size, combining incoming and outgoing components with a trade-off parameter β, is injected as a bias into both networks to explicitly penalize redundant connections during generation. Training jointly optimizes the ordering network via REINFORCE (since node orderings are discrete), the denoising network via a weighted variational lower bound, and a policy-gradient term that directly maximizes downstream task utility from black-box evaluations, enabling the model to balance structural fidelity with task performance.

## Results

RADAR achieves an average accuracy of 90.32% across six benchmarks (MMLU, GSM8K, MultiArith, SVAMP, AQuA, HumanEval), outperforming all 19 baselines on every individual dataset. The strongest autonomous baseline, ARG-Designer, reaches 88.57% average, while RADAR delivers a 1.75% average improvement over it. On the most challenging code generation task (HumanEval), RADAR achieves 91.28% pass@1, a 4.20% gain over the single-agent GPT-4o-mini baseline (87.08%). RADAR also consumes only 6.5×10⁶ total tokens on GSM8K, roughly half the cost of G-Designer, while attaining the highest accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RADAR outperforms all three categories of baselines. Against single-agent methods (CoT, ComplexCoT, SC), RADAR gains 3.04–4.77% on average, with the largest margin on SVAMP (+6.59%) and MMLU (+5.12%). Among fixed multi-agent systems (LLM-Debate, DyLAN, MacNet, etc.), the strongest is DyLAN at 87.61% average; RADAR exceeds it by 2.71%, and notably several multi-agent baselines (MacNet, ADAS) actually underperform the vanilla single-agent baseline, confirming that poorly designed topologies can be counterproductive. Among autonomous topology-design methods, RADAR beats the closest competitor ARG-Designer (88.57%) by 1.75% on average, with the largest per-dataset gains on SVAMP (+1.05%) and MMLU (+4.56%). On token efficiency, RADAR uses 6.5×10⁶ tokens on GSM8K versus 1.1×10⁷ for AgentPrune and 1.4×10⁷ for AFlow, while achieving higher accuracy than both. In robustness tests under prompt injection attacks, RADAR maintains nearly identical performance (83.66→83.14 on MMLU), whereas the complete graph topology drops by up to 4.47%.

## Relation to Prior Work

RADAR builds directly on the autoregressive discrete graph diffusion framework of Kong et al. (ICML 2023) and the multi-agent topology generation approach of GTD (Jiang et al., 2025), which first applied conditional graph diffusion to LLM agent communication design. It departs from prior one-step generation methods (G-Designer, MaAS, ARG-Designer) by introducing an iterative, node-by-node denoising process, and from post-hoc pruning methods (AgentPrune, AgentDropout) by embedding redundancy control directly into the generative objective via the effective-size metric from social network theory (Burt, 1992). The key conceptual departure is treating structural redundancy as a first-class signal during topology construction rather than as a property to be corrected after the fact.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RADAR addresses a practical bottleneck in multi-agent LLM systems—excessive token consumption from over-connected topologies—by generating sparser, task-adaptive structures that achieve higher accuracy with roughly half the token cost of comparable baselines. The near-invariance to adversarial prompt attacks (unlike most baselines that degrade 1–4.5%) suggests that redundancy-aware topologies are inherently more robust, though this finding is demonstrated on limited attack configurations. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond 5 agents, whether the 50-query training set generalizes to substantially different task distributions, and whether dynamic role composition (noted as future work) can further improve adaptability.
