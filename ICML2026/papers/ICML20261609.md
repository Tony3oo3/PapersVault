## Problem

Standard policy gradient RL fails in proactive recommendation systems (PRSs) due to two structural deficiencies: (1) a length shortcut, where path-level rewards decompose into step-level rewards with positive mean, causing gradients to favor path extension over meaningful exploration; and (2) high gradient variance from weighting each step's gradient by the entire path-level reward rather than exploiting the reward decomposition structure. The paper provides a theoretical characterization of the length collapse (O(1/s) rate) and proposes targeted rectifications.

## Input / Output

The method takes a user's interaction history (a sequence of previously interacted items) and a platform-specified target item the user has not yet engaged with, and produces a recommendation path of up to L_max=10 intermediate items that gradually shifts user preference toward the target. Items are represented as 4-token semantic IDs generated via RQ-VAE over GPT-4-derived item profiles, and the policy autoregressively generates these tokens. A pretrained SASRec user simulator provides acceptance probability estimates for computing the multi-objective reward (IoI, IoR, CTR) without online feedback.

## Method

ProRL is a two-stage framework: a T5-based sequence-to-sequence model (~2M params) is first pretrained on semantically coherent expert paths mined from historical logs, then fine-tuned via rectified policy gradient RL. The first rectification, Stepwise Reward Centering, subtracts the global expected per-step reward from each step's reward so that path extension yields zero expected gain, eliminating the length shortcut. The second, Position-Specific Advantage Estimation, computes a position-specific baseline as the average reward-to-go at each step across group rollouts (inspired by GRPO but adapted to the per-step reward decomposition), yielding lower-variance advantage estimates without a learned critic. The multi-objective reward combines normalized IoI, IoR, and CTR components, and a KL penalty to the pretrained policy prevents degeneration. A formal theorem proves that under positive-mean step rewards, standard gradient flow drives stopping probability to zero at O(1/s) rate, establishing length collapse as a structural consequence rather than a tuning artifact.

## Results

ProRL achieves state-of-the-art performance across all three datasets on all four metrics (CTR, Coherence, IoI, IoR). On MovieLens-1M, ProRL reaches CTR 0.8543, Coherence 0.8422, IoI 2.8504, and IoR 728.18; on Steam, CTR 0.5625, Coherence 0.8707, IoI 1.1188, IoR 340.18; and on Amazon-Book, CTR 0.8568, Coherence 0.6775, IoI 2.9812, IoR 1383.41. All improvements over the strongest baseline are statistically significant (p < 0.05). Notably, Coherence is not part of the reward function, yet ProRL still substantially outperforms all baselines on this unrewarded metric.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProRL dominates all nine external baselines across every metric and dataset. Among sequential recommenders, FEARec is the strongest (e.g., IoI 1.8770 on ML-1M), yet ProRL still leads by 51% in IoI and 420% in IoR. Among proactive methods, LLM-IPP is the closest competitor on ML-1M (IoI 2.4680 vs. 2.8504; IoR 662.52 vs. 728.18), while T-PRA leads on Steam IoI (0.3339) but ProRL more than triples it (1.1188). The supervised method IRN achieves the highest CTR among non-RL baselines on ML-1M (0.8398) but trails ProRL on all guidance metrics. In the gradient-estimator ablation (Table 5), ProRL outperforms REINFORCE, GRPO, A2C, and RTG on IoI and IoR while maintaining stable path lengths (~3–4 steps) and the lowest advantage variance (~5% of REINFORCE), confirming that the rectification mechanisms—not just the reward design—drive the gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the supervised proactive recommendation framework of IRN (Zhu et al., 2023), which uses a T5-based seq2seq model to imitate historical interaction paths, but replaces imitation with RL-based exploration to discover paths beyond the training distribution. The rectified gradient estimator draws on classical variance-reduction techniques (reward-to-go, baseline subtraction from Williams 1992 and Greensmith et al. 2001) and adapts the critic-free group Monte Carlo baseline idea from GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) to the specific reward decomposition structure of PRS. The key departure is identifying that PRS rewards decompose into step-level increments with positive mean, creating a 'length shortcut' that no prior RL-for-recommendation work had addressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of the length shortcut as a structural failure mode of standard policy gradients in PRS provides a clear diagnostic for why naive RL approaches degenerate, and the two rectification mechanisms (Stepwise Reward Centering and Position-Specific Advantage Estimation) offer a principled fix that generalizes to any sequential task with positive-mean step rewards. The Rollout@K analysis revealing that RL functions as a 'probabilistic rectifier' redistributing mass toward high-quality paths already latent in the pretrained distribution raises open questions about whether more sophisticated exploration strategies could further exploit this latent capacity. Limitations include reliance on a fixed user simulator (SASRec) for reward computation, and the positive-mean step reward assumption, which may not hold in all reward formulations.
