## Problem

Existing phase-based motion models such as DeepPhase are locked to fixed skeleton topologies, decode motion as discrete frame sequences, and cannot be extended to probabilistic or generative frameworks. This limits their applicability across diverse characters, prevents arbitrary-resolution sampling, and blocks integration with modern generative pipelines like latent diffusion. The paper asks whether a phase-manifold representation can be made both skeleton-agnostic and function-space-native to unify motion prediction, reconstruction, and generation in a single interpretable framework.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 60-frame motion clips represented as 6D continuous joint rotations (J×6 per frame) plus 3D root positions, along with optional class labels, sparse keyframes, or partial-body observations. Because the decoder operates in function space, it outputs a continuous spatio-temporal motion function that can be evaluated at any temporal resolution or joint subset, rather than a fixed-length frame sequence. The latent representation is extremely compact—each latent channel is parameterized by four periodic scalars (amplitude, frequency, phase shift, offset), yielding at most 1024 parameters per clip for the 256-channel variant.

## Method

FunPhase is a two-stage framework. The first stage is a Perceiver-based autoencoder with separate joint and root encoders that project inputs into a shared latent space via a 1D convolutional bottleneck; each latent channel is then decomposed via a differentiable FFT into sinusoidal parameters (amplitude, frequency, phase shift, offset), and the decoder reconstructs motion by evaluating these sinusoids at arbitrary spatio-temporal query positions through cross-attention. The second stage trains a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) with v-parameterization and min-SNR weighting directly on the periodic parameters, after applying a phase transformation (phase→Cartesian cos/sin, frequency→probit) to make the bounded, periodic domains compatible with Gaussian diffusion. The key architectural insight is that the periodic decomposition acts as a structural regularizer: it enforces local periodicity in the latent space, which improves physical plausibility (reducing foot sliding and penetration) and denoising stability during generation, while the function-space formulation provides skeleton-agnostic encoding and arbitrary-resolution decoding that frame-based autoencoders cannot offer.

## Results

On the 100STYLE latent diffusion benchmark, FunPhase achieves the lowest FID (0.51±0.16) and lowest foot-sliding error (0.52) among all methods, while maintaining competitive conditioning accuracy (76.17%) and the best acceleration smoothness (ACL 1.33). On the DOG reconstruction task, FunPhase reduces position error by ~57% (144→61.4 cm) and foot sliding by ~88% (1.76→0.21) relative to DeepPhase. On the multi-skeleton ZOO dataset, FunPhase attains the highest coverage (96±7%) and lowest intra-class diversity discrepancy (0.06±0.03), outperforming AnyTop and SMDM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CAMDM (the strongest baseline on 100STYLE), FunPhase achieves a substantially lower FID (0.51 vs. 0.91) and foot-sliding error (0.52 vs. 0.69), and a dramatically lower ACL (1.33 vs. 6.02), indicating far better physical plausibility; however, CAMDM retains a clear advantage in class-conditioning accuracy (88.22% vs. 76.17%). Compared to MLD and ACMDM, FunPhase dominates on every metric, with FID improvements of ~4× and ~10× respectively. Against Function Diffusion (the same functional-space framework without phase decomposition), FunPhase improves FID from 1.19 to 0.51 and accuracy from 34.27% to 76.17%, confirming that the periodic latent decomposition acts as a strong regularizer for denoising stability. On the multi-skeleton ZOO dataset, FunPhase achieves the highest coverage (96% vs. 80% for AnyTop, 89% for SMDM) and the lowest intra-class diversity discrepancy (0.06 vs. 0.14 for both baselines), though AnyTop reports higher raw local and inter diversity at the cost of coverage and stability. On the DOG reconstruction task, FunPhase outperforms DeepPhase across all six metrics (position, orientation, NPSS, sliding, penetration, ACL), with the largest gains in sliding (88% reduction) and penetration (61% reduction).

## Relation to Prior Work

FunPhase sits at the intersection of two research threads: the phase-manifold literature (PFNN → DeepPhase) that encodes temporal periodicity in motion, and the function-space generative modeling line (FunDiff) that replaces discrete frame sequences with continuous spatio-temporal functions. The paper departs from DeepPhase by replacing its 1D-convolutional, fixed-skeleton architecture with Perceiver-based encoders and a functional decoder, and from FunDiff by injecting a periodic (FFT-based) decomposition of the latent space as an inductive bias. It further extends the generative side by training a DiT-based latent diffusion directly on the transformed periodic parameters, a setting not explored in prior phase-space diffusion work (PhaseDiff, Deep Compositional Phase Diffusion), which inherit the original DeepPhase autoencoder unchanged.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The function-space formulation enables capabilities absent from frame-based motion models—arbitrary-resolution decoding, temporal super-resolution, and partial-body completion—while the phase decomposition acts as a regularizer that improves physical plausibility in both reconstruction and diffusion-based generation. The skeleton-agnostic design (graph-Laplacian or heat-diffusion positional encodings) broadens applicability to heterogeneous characters, a practical need in animation and robotics. Key limitations remain: the phase inductive bias degrades on weakly periodic or transition-heavy motions, evaluation is confined to locomotion-dominant datasets and class-conditional generation, and extension to text-to-motion benchmarks (HumanML3D, KIT-ML) is left as future work. Whether the function-space paradigm generalizes beyond periodic locomotion to diverse, semantically rich motion will determine its broader impact.
