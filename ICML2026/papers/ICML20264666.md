## Problem

Existing LALM jailbreaks all treat audio as a carrier for malicious payloads, requiring instance-specific optimization, online audio generation, or coarse-grained discrete style parameters. This paper challenges that necessity by revealing that LALM safety alignment can be compromised solely by specific paralinguistic acoustic features (Acoustic Latent Semantics) in semantically benign audio, opening a new universal, optimization-free attack paradigm that decouples the attack payload from the audio modality entirely.

## Input / Output

The attack takes a standard malicious text query (e.g., PAIR-generated jailbreak artifacts) and a pre-computed, ranked set of universal interference audio files as input. Each audio file is synthesized offline via Bark TTS with a specific ALS history prompt but fixed to one of three neutral instructions (e.g., 'Sure, here is'), ensuring the audio content is semantically harmless. The output is the LALM's response, evaluated via two ASR metrics (rule-based and model-based GPT-4o scoring). No online AGM generation or per-instance optimization is required during the attack loop, making it a pure black-box query-based method.

## Method

The core contribution is the Acoustic Interference Attack (AIA), which exploits Acoustic Latent Semantics (ALS) — high-dimensional paralinguistic features mined from the native generative manifold of Bark via unconditional sampling with null history prompts. The ALS arsenal is constructed by generating 5,000 audio candidates, embedding them via CLAP and WavLM, performing hierarchical k-means clustering with density-based representative selection, and indexing each sample with a 12-dimensional interpretable labeling system (fusing GPT-4o perceptual scores with deterministic signal-processing metrics). At attack time, AIA operates in two stages: it first queries the LALM with the jailbreak text alone for m attempts, and if refused, iterates through a pre-ranked interference audio set (ranked offline via difficulty-aware weighting that prioritizes effectiveness on weak/medium jailbreak texts), pairing each audio with the failed text to form multimodal queries. Interpretability analysis via logit margin shifts, latent-space projection onto a refusal vector, and causal activation patching confirms that ALS induces a directional inference path drift away from the safety alignment subspace, particularly in late transformer layers, rather than adding isotropic noise.

## Results

AIA achieves state-of-the-art universal jailbreak ASR-M across 10 LALMs, reaching 100% on Qwen2.5-Omni and Gemini-2.5-Pro, 96.30% on Qwen2-Audio, 93.10% on Gemini-3-Pro, and 75.61% on GPT-4o-Audio (JBB dataset). Compared to text-only jailbreak, AIA yields absolute ASR-M gains of 14–63 percentage points (e.g., +49.02 on Qwen2.5-Omni, +50.00 on Gemini-2.5-Pro, +58.19 on Kimi-Audio). The average query time is 11.7, ranking second among universal methods and fifth among all ten compared methods, while requiring no online audio generation during the attack loop.

## Baselines & Comparisons

As a universal (optimization-free) method, AIA outperforms both existing universal baselines by large margins: it surpasses AMSE by 54.4 pp on Qwen2-Audio (96.30 vs 41.90), 70.0 pp on Kimi-Audio (72.73 vs 2.73 implied), and 69.9 pp on Gemini-2.5-Pro (100.00 vs 30.10 implied), and dominates AudioJailbreak on every model where both report results. Against instance-specific methods, AIA wins on most models: it beats SACRED-Bench on Qwen2.5-Omni (100.00 vs 92.83), Kimi-Audio (72.73 vs 70.05), and GPT-4o-Audio (75.61 vs 70.05); it beats BoN on GPT-4o-Audio (75.61 vs 59.00) and Gemini-2.5-Pro (100.00 vs 97.60). However, AIA loses to AdvWave (black-box) on LLaMA-Omni (32.26 vs 100.00), GPT-4o-Audio (75.61 vs 91.10), and Qwen2-Audio (96.30 vs 96.70, near-tie), and to SACRED-Bench on Qwen2-Audio (96.30 vs 98.16). The paper notes that AIA's advantage is amplified in real-world settings because it requires no online AGM generation, unlike all instance-specific baselines, and its universal audio arsenal is pre-computed once and reusable across all future targets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from all three established paradigms of LALM jailbreaking—semantic optimization before audio generation, explicit acoustic parameter control, and additive adversarial perturbation—by arguing that the audio modality need not carry any malicious content at all. Instead, it introduces Acoustic Latent Semantics (ALS), high-dimensional paralinguistic features mined from the native manifold of the Bark generative model, and shows that semantically benign audio infused with specific ALS patterns can universally trigger safety-alignment drift. The method repurposes existing tools (Bark for latent sampling, CLAP/WavLM for embedding, k-means for clustering) in a novel pipeline that decouples the attack payload (jailbreak text) from the trigger (interference audio), eliminating the need for instance-specific optimization or online AGM calls during the attack loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a fundamental vulnerability in cross-modal safety alignment: LALMs' refusal mechanisms are unstable under paralinguistic interference even when audio content is entirely benign, and the effect is directional (drifting inference away from the safety subspace in late transformer layers) rather than isotropic noise. This has immediate practical implications for LALM developers, as the attack is universal, optimization-free, and black-box, with a pre-computed audio arsenal that works across unseen targets. Open questions include how to design cross-modal defenses that are robust to paralinguistic interference, whether more advanced optimization of the interference audio (beyond the current ranking) could further amplify the effect, and whether the identified ALS patterns (urgency, benevolence, youth bias) reflect broader biases in how LALMs process audio that extend beyond the jailbreak setting.
