## Problem

Semi-supervised domain translation—learning conditional distributions π*(y|x) from a mix of limited paired data and abundant unpaired marginals—lacks a principled loss that naturally integrates both data types. Existing methods heuristically combine paired and unpaired objectives (e.g., GAN losses plus cycle consistency), and the paper shows these composite losses fail to recover the true conditional even in simple 2D settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263642/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes three data streams: P paired samples (x,y)~π*, Q unpaired source samples x~π*_x, and R unpaired target samples y~π*_y, with P typically much smaller than Q and R. The output is a parametric conditional distribution π^θ(y|x) expressed as a Gaussian mixture (Eq. 19) whose means, covariances, and weights are functions of x, enabling fast sampling of target-domain samples y given any new x. In the image-translation experiments, x and y live in the 512-dim ALAE latent space of 1024×1024 FFHQ images.

## Method

Starting from KL-divergence minimization, the authors derive a likelihood-based loss (Eq. 15) with three terms: a joint cost over paired data, a target-marginal term over unpaired y, and a source-marginal log-partition term over unpaired x. They prove this loss is equivalent (up to scaling) to the inverse entropic optimal transport problem, where one recovers a cost function c* such that the given joint π* is the entropic OT plan. To make optimization tractable, they parameterize the cost as a log-sum-exp of neural-network features (Eq. 17) and the dual potential as a Gaussian mixture (Eq. 18), which yields a closed-form normalization constant Z^θ(x) and Gaussian-mixture conditionals. Training minimizes the empirical three-term loss via gradient descent; inference is a single Gaussian-mixture evaluation. A universal approximation theorem guarantees the parameterization can approximate any sufficiently regular conditional distribution to arbitrary accuracy.

## Results

On the weather prediction task (90 paired, 500 unpaired samples), EBiEOT-GMM achieves a test log-likelihood of 20.5±0.09, substantially outperforming the best semi-supervised baseline CGMM (SS) at 0.32±0.03 and CondNF (SS) at 0.52±0.02. On the ALAE-based image translation tasks (FFHQ, 2K paired samples), the method achieves FID 9.3±0.1 (Woman-to-Man) and 9.4±0.2 (Old-to-Young), beating FSBM's 10.2±0.6 and 11.5±0.6 respectively, while training in 3 minutes versus FSBM's 5 hours on an A100 GPU. On the Gaussian→Swiss Roll synthetic task (P=128, Q=R=1024), EBiEOT-GMM is the only method that recovers the bimodal conditional structure, while all baselines either collapse to deterministic mappings or fail to capture the target distribution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EBiEOT-GMM decisively outperforms all likelihood-based baselines on the weather prediction task: it achieves LL 20.5 versus 0.32 for CGMM (SS) and 0.52 for CondNF (SS), a >40× improvement, while matching CGMM (SS) on CFD (7.21 vs. 7.17). On image translation (FFHQ/ALAE), EBiEOT-GMM beats FSBM on all three metrics for both Woman-to-Man (FID 9.3 vs. 10.2; SSIM 0.5315 vs. 0.5237; LPIPS 0.5531 vs. 0.5625) and Old-to-Young (FID 9.4 vs. 11.5; SSIM 0.5361 vs. 0.5285; LPIPS 0.5560 vs. 0.5628), while training ~100× faster. On the Swiss Roll task, GNOT, DCPEME, and parOT all learn deterministic mappings and fail to capture the bimodal conditional; OTCS and FSBM also fail due to biased keypoint-based objectives. CGAN cannot learn the target marginal, UGAN+ℓ² misses the conditional structure, CondNF overfits with limited paired data, and CondNF (SS) provides no improvement over CondNF. CGMM (SS) collapses to a degenerate solution, confirming that heuristic semi-supervised objectives fail even in 2D.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the log-sum-exp cost parameterization from Korotin et al. (2024) and the Gaussian-mixture dual potential from Gushchin et al. (2024a), extending them within a likelihood-maximization framework derived from KL divergence minimization with a Gibbs-Boltzmann energy parameterization. The key conceptual departure is from prior semi-supervised domain translation methods (FSBM, OTCS, parOT), which heuristically combine paired and unpaired losses, and from prior inverse OT work (Dupuy et al. 2019, Ma et al. 2020), which focuses on recovering cost functions in discrete or fully paired settings. The paper's central insight is proving equivalence between its proposed likelihood-based loss and the inverse entropic OT problem, thereby reframing inverse EOT as a semi-supervised domain translation task and vice versa.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled (non-heuristic) loss for semi-supervised domain translation with a universal approximation guarantee, addressing a gap the authors identify in the literature where existing composite objectives fail even in 2D toy cases. The inverse EOT ↔ likelihood maximization equivalence opens a path to applying advanced EOT solvers (diffusion Schrödinger bridges, flow matching) to semi-supervised learning. However, the GMM parameterization limits scalability to high-dimensional image spaces, and the neural-network variant (EBiEOT-NN) exhibits instability and mode collapse, leaving the question of practical scaling to large-scale generative tasks open.
