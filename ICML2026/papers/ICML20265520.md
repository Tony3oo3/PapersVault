## Problem

GPU solvers for entropic optimal transport via Sinkhorn iterations remain inefficient at scale: tensorized implementations incur quadratic HBM traffic from repeatedly traversing dense n×m interaction matrices, while existing online backends (e.g., KeOps) avoid O(nm) storage but execute each Sinkhorn update as generic map-reduce reduction kernels with limited fusion and tiling. This systems bottleneck makes OT prohibitively expensive for repeated large point-cloud solves in downstream pipelines, especially in high dimensions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes source and target point clouds X ∈ R^{n×d}, Y ∈ R^{m×d} with probability weights a, b and a regularization parameter ε, and requires the cost to be expressible in the form C_ij = α_i + β_j − Q_i^T K_j (squared Euclidean is the primary instance; cosine distance and OTDD's label-augmented cost also fit). Outputs include dual potentials, OT cost/Sinkhorn divergence, the barycentric projection, the EOT gradient, and Hessian-vector products — all computed via streaming operators that never materialize the n×m transport matrix, maintaining only O((n+m)d) resident state.

## Method

The key insight is that for squared Euclidean cost, stabilized log-domain Sinkhorn updates are exactly row-wise LogSumExp reductions of a biased dot-product score matrix (QK^T + bias)/ε — the same per-row normalization underlying scaled dot-product attention. This algebraic equivalence enables FlashAttention-style IO-aware streaming: fused Triton kernels stage a row block of Q in on-chip SRAM, stream column tiles of K to form score tiles via tiled GEMM, maintain online max/sumexp accumulators, and write only the updated dual potentials back to HBM, reducing HBM traffic from Θ(nd + md + nm) to Θ(nd + md + nmd²/M) per half-step. The paper further derives streaming kernels for transport matrix application (PV, P^T U) as fused matmul-softmax-matmul passes, and a streaming Hessian-vector product oracle that decomposes the HVP into a small number of transport-vector, transport-matrix, and Hadamard-weighted transport applications, with the implicit Schur-complement system solved by damped conjugate gradient — reducing working memory from O(n²d² + nm) to O((n+m)d).

## Results

On A100 GPUs, FlashSinkhorn achieves 9–32× forward-pass speedup over KeOps (GeomLoss online) and up to 161× end-to-end (forward+backward) speedup at d=512, while maintaining O((n+m)d) memory versus O(nm) for tensorized baselines. NCU profiling at n=m=10,000, d=64 shows FlashSinkhorn moves only 0.08 GB through HBM (vs. 98 GB for tensorized and 0.14 GB for KeOps) with 3% memory stalls and 74% SM utilization, yielding 8.2 ms runtime versus 125.5 ms (KeOps) and 54.0 ms (tensorized). For Hessian-vector products, FlashSinkhorn achieves 3–6× speedup at d=64 and up to 25× at d=128, completing n=m=50,000, d=64 HVPs in 4.2 s where KeOps takes 14.5 s and OTT-JAX does not finish within the time budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlashSinkhorn consistently outperforms all three baselines across forward, backward, and HVP computations. Against KeOps (GeomLoss online), the speedup is 9–32× on the forward pass and up to 161× end-to-end at d=512, driven by fused streaming kernels that issue 6.6× fewer kernel launches and route 2.9× more compute through the tensor pipeline; KeOps's generic map-reduce reductions achieve only 49% SM utilization. Against the tensorized GeomLoss backend, FlashSinkhorn is 1.7–3.5× faster in the settings shown (up to 12× in a broader sweep), and critically avoids O(nm) memory, enabling n=50k runs where tensorized OOMs; however, at small n (<2000) or very high d where the dense cost matrix fits in cache, tensorized can be competitive. Against OTT-JAX, FlashSinkhorn achieves up to 5.1× forward speedup and 1.2–3.0× end-to-end speedup, with the gap widening at larger n and higher d where OTT-JAX's XLA-compiled kernels become memory-bound. On HVPs, all methods use the same matrix-free Schur-complement/CG oracle, so differences come solely from the efficiency of the underlying transport-application operators, where FlashSinkhorn's streamed kernels yield 3–25× speedups.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transfers FlashAttention's IO-aware tiling and online log-sum-exp normalization (Dao et al., 2022; Milakov & Gimelshein, 2018) to entropic optimal transport, showing that stabilized log-domain Sinkhorn half-steps for squared Euclidean cost are algebraically identical to row-wise LSE reductions of biased dot-product scores. It departs from existing GPU OT backends—GeomLoss/KeOps (generic tiled map-reduce) and OTT-JAX (online but unfused)—by fusing cost computation, bias addition, and LSE reduction into single Triton kernels that stream tiles through SRAM. The streaming Hessian-vector-product oracle builds on the Hessian structure of Li et al. (2025b) but makes it practical at scale by decomposing it into transport-vector and Hadamard-weighted transport applications that reuse the same streaming machinery. Conceptually, it sits alongside recent work connecting attention to EOT (Litman, 2025) but targets computation rather than interpretation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing HBM traffic from quadratic to near-linear per Sinkhorn iteration, FlashSinkhorn makes large-scale EOT (n=50k, d=512) and second-order OT optimization (NewtonCG via streaming HVPs) practical on a single A100, removing a persistent systems bottleneck that has limited OT's use in downstream pipelines. The streaming HVP oracle is a new capability: it enables Lanczos eigenvalue monitoring and saddle-escape detection in OT-based non-convex optimization at n=40k, a regime where prior methods materializing the Hessian are infeasible. The main limitation is scope: the fused-streaming form requires the cost to decompose as α_i + β_j − Q_i^T K_j, excluding raw Euclidean distance and learned neural costs, which the authors identify as future work. Whether the Triton-kernel approach generalizes cleanly to multi-GPU or to the broader family of regularized OT variants (e.g., unbalanced, partial) remains open.
