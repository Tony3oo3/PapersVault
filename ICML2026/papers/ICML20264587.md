## Problem

Under fixed token and frame budgets, long-video QA systems waste most visual capacity on background frames while overlooking the brief evidence moments that carry the answer. Public training sets rarely bind each question to ground-truth temporal spans, so models learn to produce answers without reliably learning where to look, and localization and answering are optimized in isolation—gains in span prediction do not consistently translate into better QA accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264587/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a long video (arbitrary duration) and a multiple-choice question, consuming them in two passes under a fixed total frame budget: a low-fps global skim (e.g., 64 frames uniformly sampled) followed by a high-fps zoom (e.g., 4–8 fps) restricted to the predicted evidence spans. Each frame is serialized with a human-readable absolute timestamp (e.g., `<image>@12.34s`), and the model emits a single interleaved response containing structured temporal spans in `<span>[t_s, t_e]</span>` tags followed by the final option in `<answer>` tags, yielding direct attribution of the answer to specific video intervals.

## Method

ViTL is a two-stage 'skim → zoom' pipeline built on Qwen2.5-VL (3B/7B) that reallocates a fixed visual token budget from background to question-relevant evidence. Stage 1 performs global temporal localization over a low-fps sweep, predicting one or more disjoint evidence spans; Stage 2 re-encodes only frames within those spans at higher effective frame rate and resolution to answer the original MCQA. Training follows a three-step schedule—supervised warm-up, teacher-forced two-stage coupling, then interleaved GRPO—where a composite reward couples multi-span temporal IoU (localization quality) with answer correctness (utility), enabling credit to flow from the answering step back to the localization step without increasing compute. The companion dataset VGrounding-QA (5,240 instances from 1,612 videos) is constructed via event knowledge graphs with semantic chunking, pairing each MCQA item with ground-truth time spans and same-video distractors to supply the span supervision missing from existing benchmarks.

## Results

On long-video QA, ViTL (Qwen2.5-VL 7B, 128 total frames) achieves 63.3% on LongVideoBench, 47.4% on LVBench, and 62.3% on MLVU (M-Avg), outperforming the Qwen2.5-VL-7B baseline (61.8%/43.7%/54.5%) while using only 128 frames versus 256. On zero-shot temporal grounding, ViTL attains 77.7% R@0.3 and 54.0% mIoU on Charades-STA, surpassing the previous best E.T.Chat (4B) by +12.0 and +11.7 points respectively. On ActivityNet-Captions, ViTL reaches 55.1% R@0.3 and 46.3% R@0.5, though its mIoU (24.1%) trails Momentor (29.3%). The headline claim is up to 8.6% accuracy gain with 50% less frame input under matched token budgets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On LVBench, ViTL (47.4%) decisively outperforms all open-source baselines—Qwen2.5-VL-7B (43.7%), InternVL3-8B (45.6%), VideoLLaMA3-17B (45.3%), and LongVA-7B (39.4%)—and even surpasses closed-source GPT-4o (27.0%) and Gemini-1.5-Pro (33.1%) by a wide margin, despite using only 128 frames. On LongVideoBench, ViTL (63.3%) edges out InternVL3 (62.8%) and Qwen2.5-VL retest (61.8%) but remains below GPT-4o (66.7%) and GLM-4V-Plus (70.8%). On MLVU, ViTL (62.3%) improves over Qwen2.5-VL (54.5%) and LongVA (52.0%) but trails GPT-4o (64.6%). For temporal grounding on Charades-STA, ViTL (7B) dominates all baselines: 77.7% R@0.3 vs. E.T.Chat's 65.7% and VideoChat-TPO's 58.3%, with mIoU of 54.0% vs. 42.3% and 38.1% respectively. On ActivityNet-Captions, ViTL leads on recall (55.1% R@0.3 vs. Momentor's 42.9%) but its mIoU (24.1%) is lower than Momentor (29.3%) and ChatVTG (27.2%), suggesting the model localizes broader spans that capture more of the relevant interval at the cost of tighter boundary precision.

## Relation to Prior Work

ViTL sits at the intersection of two active threads: (1) coarse-to-fine video zooming (VideoZoomer, LOVE-R1), which revisits selected temporal regions at higher fidelity, and (2) GRPO-based post-training (DeepSeek-R1, Video-R1), which optimizes multi-step reasoning via group-relative rewards. The key departure is that prior zoom-in methods use CLIP-style retrieval or decouple grounding from answering, so utility cannot flow back to refine selection; ViTL instead emits spans and answers in a single interleaved sequence and couples temporal-IoU with answer-correctness in one composite GRPO reward. For supervision, the paper repurposes event-knowledge-graph construction (Yan et al., 2025) to generate VGrounding-QA, a dataset that—unlike Charades-STA, QVHighlights, or Video-MME—binds every MCQA item to ground-truth temporal spans with same-video distractors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck: under fixed token budgets, uniform sampling wastes capacity on background, and prior training sets never teach models *where* to look. VGrounding-QA fills this supervision gap, and the interleaved GRPO objective makes span quality directly accountable for answer accuracy, yielding interpretable, auditable outputs. The oracle-span analysis (70.5% vs. 63.3% on LongVideoBench) reveals that localization fidelity remains the dominant bottleneck, suggesting future gains will come from better span prediction rather than larger answerers. Open questions include whether the event-graph construction pipeline (which relies on GPT-4o/o3 for chunk descriptions and question synthesis) scales to hours-long or multi-camera video, and whether the two-stage latency overhead (~17% increase) is acceptable in real-time settings.
