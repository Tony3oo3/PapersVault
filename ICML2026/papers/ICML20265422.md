## Problem

Existing LLM-based Verus proof repair systems rely on coarse verifier error messages (e.g., 'invariant not satisfied at end of loop body') that fail to reveal the root cause of verification failures, forcing them to depend on expensive handcrafted repair heuristics or large-scale synthetic data training. The natural remedy—counterexamples—has been unavailable in practice because Verus's lossy lowering into SMT introduces SSA artifacts and quantifier-heavy VCs that make backend counterexamples noisy, uninterpretable, or simply unsolvable (returning 'unknown').

## Input / Output

EXVERUS takes a Rust program with pre/post-condition specifications and an initial (possibly buggy) set of proof annotations, along with a chosen LLM backend, and iteratively produces a repaired Verus proof that passes verification. A distinctive aspect of the pipeline is that it synthesizes standalone Z3Py scripts at the source level (bypassing Verus's own SMT compilation), and the resulting counterexamples are returned as structured JSON with source-level variable names and types, enabling replay and validation. For invariant errors, a dedicated validation module injects counterexample values into an extracted loop function and re-checks with Verus to confirm the counterexample is genuine before it guides repair.

## Method

EXVERUS's key insight is to have the LLM synthesize concise, source-level Z3Py queries that isolate the failing obligation and search for concrete variable assignments violating it, rather than attempting to decompile counterexamples from Verus's massive, quantifier-laden SMT VCs. The pipeline operates in two iterative phases: (1) Counterexample Generation with Validation, where the LLM translates proof annotations into Z3Py (concretizing variables to avoid quantifiers, encoding types in naming conventions), solves for multiple counterexamples, and then validates invariant-related counterexamples by injecting them into an extracted loop body and checking expected assertion-failure symptoms with Verus; (2) Mutation-based Counterexample-guided Repair, where an LLM-based error triage module classifies the failure (e.g., invariant too weak vs. factually wrong), selects a corresponding mutator (strengthen or replace), generates candidate repairs conditioned on the counterexamples and triage rationale, and ranks candidates by the number of validated counterexamples they block—inspired by PDR's blocking principle. This transforms open-ended proof search into an incremental, data-driven refinement loop where each iteration is grounded in concrete, verifiable program states.

## Results

EXVERUS solves 38% more tasks on average than the state-of-the-art (AUTOVERUS) across all benchmarks, with the advantage widening to ~2× on harder benchmarks (LCBench, HumanEval). On VerusBench, EXVERUS outperforms AUTOVERUS by 60.92% on average across five LLM backbones. EXVERUS also achieves substantially lower cost ($0.04 vs. $0.17 per task, 4.25× cheaper) and runs over 4× faster (720s vs. 2989s per task). On the robustness benchmark ObfsBench, EXVERUS maintains success rates above 73% under code obfuscation, while AUTOVERUS drops below 50%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EXVERUS is compared against two baselines: AUTOVERUS (Yang et al., 2025a), the state-of-the-art LLM-based Verus proof generator using handcrafted error-specific repair prompts, and Iterative Refinement (inspired by Shefer et al., 2025), which prompts the LLM with error messages and a generic repair prompt. Against AUTOVERUS, EXVERUS wins on nearly all benchmark–model combinations: on VerusBench it improves success rates by 4–19 percentage points (e.g., 71.9% vs. 24.7% with DeepSeek-V3.1), and on the harder LCBench and HumanEval benchmarks it solves roughly 2× and 1.5× as many tasks, respectively. On the robustness benchmark ObfsBench, EXVERUS maintains >73% success under all obfuscation strategies while AUTOVERUS stays below 50%, indicating that AUTOVERUS's heuristics-heavy prompting is brittle to out-of-distribution inputs. Against Iterative Refinement, EXVERUS also generally wins, though the gap narrows on easier benchmarks with stronger models (e.g., Sonnet-4.5 on DafnyBench where both reach 95.5%). In terms of cost, EXVERUS uses 4.25× fewer dollars per task ($0.04 vs. $0.17) and 4× less wall-clock time, with the token gap widening on complex tasks (≥5 invariants: 111k vs. 431k input tokens).

## Relation to Prior Work

EXVERUS extends the iterative LLM-based proof repair paradigm established by AutoVerus and SAFE, but departs fundamentally from their reliance on coarse verifier error messages or handcrafted per-error-type repair heuristics. Its core idea repurposes the classical CEGAR/PDR counterexample-blocking loop to the LLM setting: rather than decompiling counterexamples from Verus's lossy SMT backend (which strips source-level semantics), it instructs the LLM to synthesize concise, source-level Z3Py queries that directly produce interpretable counterexamples. This positions the work at the intersection of LLM-based verification and traditional model-checking techniques, with the LLM serving as both the query synthesizer and the repair mutator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework makes counterexample-guided proof repair practical for Verus by sidestepping the intractable decompilation problem, and the 4× cost reduction and 4× speedup make it economically viable for iterative development workflows. The robustness results on ObfsBench (73%+ vs. <40% under obfuscation) suggest that grounding repair in concrete program states is fundamentally more generalizable than heuristic prompting, which has implications for how LLM-based verification tools should be designed. Key limitations include validation being restricted to invariant errors (not assertions or postconditions), reuse of AutoVerus's initial proof generation, and the fact that absolute success rates on hard benchmarks (LCBench, HumanEval) remain low (10–40%), leaving substantial room for improvement in invariant synthesis and system-level temporal reasoning.
