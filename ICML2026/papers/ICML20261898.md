## Problem

Physics-informed diffusion models enforce PDE constraints only on final outputs, leaving intermediate representations unconstrained and prone to shortcut learning where the network memorizes distribution-specific patterns rather than internalizing governing laws. This output-only supervision allows the deep backbone to bypass physical reasoning, resulting in fragility under out-of-distribution boundary conditions and physical inconsistencies masked by superficial pattern matching. No prior framework enforces physical decodability directly within the latent space of a diffusion model.

## Input / Output

The diffusion backbone (U-Net or DiT) consumes a noisy physical field on a 64×64 or 128×48 grid concatenated with the diffusion timestep, plus task-specific conditioning channels (charge density ρ for the Poisson problem, boundary-condition indicator and load vector for topology optimization, or a binary observation mask for sparse reconstruction). The model outputs the clean physical field—pressure and permeability for Darcy flow, material density for topology optimization, electric potential for the charge problem, or streamwise velocity fluctuation for turbulent channel flow. Lightweight 1×1 projection heads attached to intermediate layers are discarded at inference, so the inference pipeline and cost are identical to the unmodified baseline.

## Method

REPA-P attaches lightweight 1×1 convolutional projection heads to selected intermediate layers (bottleneck, encoder, or decoder blocks for U-Net; early, middle, or late blocks for DiT) that decode hidden activations into physical quantity space via a channel projection followed by bilinear upsampling to the output resolution. The same discretized PDE and boundary-condition residual operator R(·) is then applied to these decoded intermediate states, and the resulting squared residuals—scaled by the time-dependent diffusion posterior variance σ²(t)—are backpropagated as an additional physics loss. This creates short-path gradient signals that bypass the deep Jacobian chain of the remaining backbone, formally shown to avoid the exponential attenuation of output-only physics gradients (Proposition D.1). The total training objective combines the standard denoising data loss, an output-level physics loss, and the mid-layer alignment loss, with hyperparameters c_out and c_mid balancing the three terms. A zero-mean centering constraint is applied to shift-invariant variables (e.g., pressure) to eliminate the null-space ambiguity in the optimization landscape.

## Results

REPA-P consistently outperforms all four baselines (PG-Diffusion, DiffusionPDE, CoCoGen, PIDM) across all four PDE benchmarks on both U-Net and DiT backbones. Headline gains include up to 66.4% reduction in physics residuals (electrostatic charge potential vs. PIDM), up to 54.9% reduction in compliance error for topology optimization (in-distribution), and up to 49.3% improvement in out-of-distribution robustness (topology OOD CE% vs. DiffusionPDE). On Darcy flow generation, REPA-P reduces data loss by 33.9% and physics loss by 45.0% relative to PIDM, while on turbulent channel flow it improves PSNR from 37.64 dB to 39.95 dB with zero additional inference cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

REPA-P is compared against four physics-informed diffusion baselines: PG-Diffusion, DiffusionPDE, CoCoGen, and PIDM (the strongest, from Bastek et al. 2025). Against PIDM, REPA-P wins on every metric across all four tasks: it reduces Darcy flow generation data loss by 33.9% and physics loss by 45.0%, cuts topology optimization compliance error from 9.24% to 4.17% (in-distribution) and from 7.93% to 5.05% (out-of-distribution), lowers charge potential physics loss by 66.4%, and improves turbulent channel flow PSNR by 2.31 dB. Against the weaker baselines, gains are even larger — e.g., REPA-P reduces topology OOD compliance error by 49.3% relative to DiffusionPDE (19.58% → 9.93%) and by 57.4% relative to CoCoGen (17.34% → 10.82%). The DiT architecture comparison (Table 2) confirms these gains transfer: REPA-P reduces Darcy flow data loss by 57.64% and physics loss by 25.73% over the DiT-PIDM baseline, with consistent improvements on all other tasks. No comparison shows REPA-P losing to any baseline on any reported metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the physics-informed diffusion model (PIDM) framework of Bastek et al. (2025), which enforces PDE and boundary-condition residuals only on the final denoised output, by adding intermediate-layer supervision through lightweight 1×1 projection heads that decode hidden features into physical states. The key conceptual departure is from output-only physics losses to in-situ PDE residual supervision at selected backbone layers, motivated by a shortcut-learning argument formalized via a gradient-flow analysis (Appendix D) showing that deep Jacobian chains attenuate physics gradients. The approach also draws on the broader representation-alignment literature (e.g., REPA for image generation) but repurposes it with first-principles PDE residuals as the alignment target rather than an external visual teacher, making it teacher-free and architecture-agnostic across U-Net and DiT backbones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

REPA-P offers a practical, zero-inference-overhead mechanism to improve physical consistency and OOD robustness in scientific diffusion models, with consistent gains across four diverse PDE benchmarks and two backbone architectures. The gradient-flow formalization provides a clear theoretical rationale for why intermediate physics supervision outperforms output-only constraints, which should guide future physics-informed generative model design. Key limitations include the requirement for known, differentiable governing equations, additional training cost from residual evaluations, and validation restricted to 2D benchmarks; open questions include extension to 3D problems, unstructured meshes, coupled multi-physics systems, and handling partially unknown or noisy physical constraints.
