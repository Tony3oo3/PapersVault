## Problem

Mid-training of LLMs for software engineering lacks a reliable proxy metric: standard Perplexity (PPL) is corrupted by the 'Long-Context Tax' (a transient PPL spike induced by RoPE/YaRN positional rescaling) and correlates poorly with downstream SWE-BENCH performance because it measures replication precision rather than latent reasoning. The paper addresses this by asking whether a token-level entropy-based metric can isolate SFT-invariant signals that predict agentic SWE capability before the expensive SFT stage.

## Input / Output

The method consumes per-token Top-10 probability distributions from a base model evaluated on 500 curated SWE-BENCH-VERIFIED trajectories (~12.5M tokens), restricted to Action components after a multi-stage filtering pipeline (regex XML stripping, AST-based comment removal, whitespace/symbol cleanup mapped to token granularity via character-offset alignment). It outputs a single HE-SNR scalar per checkpoint, computed only over the High-Entropy Decision Set where Top-10 entropy exceeds ε = (ln3+ln4)/2 and the ground-truth token falls within the Top-10 candidate set, thereby excluding tokens with severe distributional divergence.

## Method

The paper proposes the Entropy Compression Hypothesis, which redefines intelligence as the capacity to structure uncertainty into Entropy-Compressed States of low order (uniform probability over k candidates, yielding entropy ≈ ln k), rather than scalar Top-1 compression. Empirically, superior models exhibit a 'Shift to ln3': Non-Top-2 token entropy migrates from a broad ln4-centered distribution toward a sharp ln3 peak, indicating compression of the valid candidate space from ≥4 to ~3 options. HE-SNR is then formulated as the mean ratio p(x_t)/H_top10(x_t) over the High-Entropy Decision Set, measuring how prominently the target signal stands above the local uncertainty floor. This ratio-based design normalizes global entropy shifts caused by the Long-Context Tax, yielding a metric that maintains strict linear correlation with SWE-BENCH Pass@1 across models up to 560B parameters and a 10× scaling span, while standard PPL and HE-PPL degrade.

## Results

HE-SNR (computed on filtered Action tokens) achieves a Kendall τ of 0.9794 and Pearson r of 0.9649 in correlation with downstream SWE-BENCH Pass@1 scores, substantially outperforming standard PPL which exhibits poor correlation and is severely degraded by the Long-Context Tax. The metric is validated across models ranging from 68B (3B active) to 560B (26B active) parameters with 32K and 128K context windows, demonstrating a strict linear relationship with SWE-BENCH performance across a 10× scaling span. Additionally, the paper reveals that SFT degrades HE-SNR on high-entropy tokens (the subset most predictive of SWE success), identifying a concrete source of the Alignment Tax in agentic tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is standard Perplexity (PPL), the de facto metric for evaluating language model progress during training. HE-SNR decisively outperforms PPL on two critical dimensions: (1) correlation with downstream SWE-BENCH performance — PPL computed over all tokens (Thought + Action) shows poor and non-linear correlation with SWE scores, while HE-SNR (Filtered) maintains strict rank consistency and linearity across the vast majority of checkpoints; and (2) robustness to the Long-Context Tax — when extending context from 32K to 128K via RoPE/YaRN scaling, PPL spikes dramatically (especially for the 560B MoE-A26B model at Step 200, where PPL degrades to levels comparable to the 32K baseline despite improved SWE capability), whereas HE-SNR maintains a consistent upward trend aligned with actual capability growth. An intermediate metric, HE-PPL (PPL restricted to high-entropy tokens), also outperforms raw PPL but remains sensitive to the Long-Context Tax, confirming that the signal-to-noise ratio formulation (dividing by entropy) is essential for robustness. The paper does not compare against LongPPL or other recent long-context metrics directly, noting their focus on retrieval tasks rather than agentic reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper refines the Compression-Intelligence Hypothesis (Huang et al., 2024) by shifting from scalar PPL-based compression to a distributional, entropy-based framework where intelligence is measured by the structure of uncertainty rather than its magnitude. It departs from LongPPL (Fang et al., 2025), which addresses position bias for retrieval tasks, by targeting SFT-invariant reasoning signals in agentic SWE tasks. The Entropy Compression Hypothesis draws on prior work linking high-entropy tokens to reasoning forking points (Wang et al., 2025b; Kadavath et al., 2022), but formalizes these observations into a principled metric (HE-SNR) with explicit theoretical bounds (Lemma 4.1) and a concrete threshold grounded in the ln k entropy boundary structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HE-SNR offers a practical, low-cost proxy (500 trajectories, ~12.5M tokens) for predicting post-SFT SWE-BENCH performance during mid-training, potentially saving substantial compute by identifying suboptimal checkpoints before expensive SFT. The finding that SFT degrades performance on high-entropy decision tokens while improving global PPL provides a mechanistic explanation for the Alignment Tax in agentic tasks, suggesting that current SFT strategies may actively impair complex reasoning. Open questions include whether the static threshold generalizes across domains, whether code canonicalization can decouple stylistic from logical uncertainty, and whether higher-order Entropy-Compressed States (k≥4) emerge in tasks with greater intrinsic complexity. The evaluation is limited to two proprietary MoE models and one benchmark, leaving generalizability to other architectures and task families partially unverified.
