## Problem

Existing text-to-image benchmarks evaluate isolated capabilities (attribute alignment, spatial reasoning) but none quantify the iterative diagnose-reflect-rectify loop essential for reflective visual generation. Moreover, evaluation on the proposed R³-Bench reveals a critical capability misalignment: state-of-the-art MLLMs accurately identify image-text inconsistencies (strong reasoning) yet fail to produce actionable rectification instructions (weak rectification), breaking the closed-loop refinement pipeline.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264743/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The policy π_θ takes a text prompt P and a current generated image I^(t) (minimum 512², maximum ~1088² pixels) and produces a structured JSON response containing three fields: a binary verification verdict (True/False), a reflective explanation localizing semantic discrepancies, and a rectification instruction specifying targeted edits. The rectification instruction is consumed by an external image editor (e.g., Qwen-Image-Edit-2511, Bagel, or GPT-Image) to produce a refined image I^(t+1), which is then re-evaluated in the next iteration of the loop until the verdict confirms consistency or a maximum iteration count is reached.

## Method

R³-Refiner is a dual-stage reinforcement learning framework built on GRPO that optimizes the full Reason-Reflect-Rectify loop via a Hierarchical Reward Mechanism (HRM). Stage I (Reasoning Alignment Reward) supervises the verification verdict against ground-truth labels using a format-compliance term plus an accuracy term, indirectly encouraging logical consistency in the reflective explanation. Stage II (Rectification Alignment Reward) closes the loop by executing the generated rectification instruction through a frozen external editor, then using the policy's own confidence in the rectified image-text pair as a self-reward signal, which penalizes shortcut behaviors (e.g., rewriting the prompt instead of fixing the image) and encourages genuine visual correction. The two stages are weighted (α₁=0.25, α₂=0.75) so that the enhanced verifier from Stage I provides more reliable execution-grounded supervision in Stage II. Training uses Qwen2.5-VL-7B or Qwen3-VL-8B as base policies with 8 rollout samples per prompt, a learning rate of 1×10⁻⁶, and approximately 3 days of wall-clock time on 24,925 curated paired samples.

## Results

On R³-Bench, R³-Refiner (built on Qwen3-VL-8B) achieves an average Reflective Verdict Score (S_ref) of 0.87 and Rectification Score (S_rect) of 0.62, matching Gemini-3-Pro on S_ref (0.87) and approaching GPT-5.2 on S_rect (0.65 vs. 0.62). Compared to its Qwen3-VL-8B base, R³-Refiner improves S_ref by +7.5% (0.80→0.87) and S_rect by +14.8% (0.54→0.62). As a plug-and-play module on GenEval++, R³-Refiner consistently improves diverse generators: Bagel (0.421→0.532), Qwen-Image (0.654→0.714), GPT Image (0.793→0.829), and Banana (0.736→0.800).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On R³-Bench, R³-Refiner (Qwen3-VL-8B) ties Gemini-3-Pro on S_ref (0.87) and trails GPT-5.2 on S_rect by only 0.03 (0.62 vs. 0.65), while substantially outperforming all open-source RVG methods: ThinkGen (S_rect 0.37), OmniVerifier (0.17), ReflectionFlow (0.26), and SLD (0.28). Among UMMs, R³-Refiner improves S_rect from −0.19 (OmniGen2) and 0.21 (Bagel) to 0.62, demonstrating that the RL-trained policy bridges the critical gap between diagnostic reasoning and actionable rectification. On GenEval++, R³-Refiner as a plug-and-play module improves every tested generator, with the largest gains on weaker models (Bagel +11.1, OmniGen2 +5.0) and consistent improvements on strong closed-source models (GPT Image +3.6, Banana +6.4). On T2I-CompBench, R³-Refiner lifts Bagel from 0.553 to 0.588 and OmniGen2 from 0.553 to 0.564. The ablation (Table 4) confirms that the Rectification Alignment Reward is essential: using only the Reasoning Reward causes S_rect to *degrade* (0.38→0.29) due to illusory rectification, while the full HRM achieves the best S_rect of 0.47 (Qwen2.5-VL-7B base). R³-Refiner also outperforms Best-of-N sampling on GenEval++ with a single iteration (0.703 vs. peak 0.686 at N=5), showing that serial iterative refinement is more sample-efficient than parallel selection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the GRPO reinforcement learning framework (Shao et al., 2024) and the self-reflection paradigm established in LLMs (Reflexion, Self-Refine) to formalize a structured Reason-Reflect-Rectify (R³) loop for visual generation. It departs from prior RVG methods (SLD, ReflectionFlow, Reflect-DiT, ThinkGen, OmniVerifier) that treat verification and rectification as loosely coupled steps, by explicitly identifying and addressing the capability misalignment between them through a Hierarchical Reward Mechanism. The benchmark construction draws on existing T2I evaluation suites (GenEval++, T2I-CompBench) and editing datasets (GEdit, PICO-Banana) but reorganizes them around the iterative R³ loop rather than single-pass generation quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of 'illusory visual rectification'—where optimizing only for correct verification causes the model to rewrite prompts instead of fixing images—challenges the assumption that stronger diagnostic ability automatically transfers to constructive correction, and motivates the need for execution-grounded reward signals. The plug-and-play nature of R³-Refiner (applicable to Bagel, OmniGen2, Qwen-Image, GPT-Image, Banana) makes it practically relevant for improving any T2I pipeline. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond two refinement iterations, how it handles multi-object or structural edits that exceed a single editor pass, and whether the 670-sample benchmark is sufficient to capture the full diversity of real-world generation failures.
