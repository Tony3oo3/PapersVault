## Problem

Existing 3D denoising pre-training methods for molecular property prediction (e.g., Coord, Frad) apply uniform noise distributions and magnitudes across all molecules, ignoring structural heterogeneity between rigid and flexible systems. This introduces systematic bias—excessive noise disrupts chemically valid rigid conformations while insufficient noise fails to explore the conformational space of flexible molecules—and their energy functions are too coarse-grained to capture bond- and atom-type-specific interactions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261608/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3D atomic coordinates of equilibrium conformations along with molecular graph connectivity, and internally decomposes each molecule into chemically meaningful motifs via the BRICS algorithm to compute interatomic influence matrices. During pre-training, noise is decomposed into stretching, bending, and torsional vibrational components (each gated by a binary switch) plus isotropic Gaussian coordinate noise, all sampled within physically reasonable ranges. The model outputs atomic-level force and energy predictions on MD22/ISO17, and 12 quantum chemical property regressions (dipole moment, polarizability, HOMO/LUMO, gap, ZPVE, internal energy, enthalpy, free energy, heat capacity) on QM9.

## Method

MOES-Pred introduces an energy sentinel that iteratively generates diverse molecule-specific noise perturbation schemes, scores each by combining geometric reconstruction error with a weighted potential energy penalty (where atomic importance weights are derived from BRICS-based intra/inter-motif influence matrices), and retains only the top-scoring schemes for the next training iteration. The influence matrix is constructed by measuring L2-norm changes in GNN node representations when individual atom embeddings are ablated, then aggregated at the motif level to capture long-range cross-motif interactions. These long-range motif features are propagated back to atomic representations via residual connections before the equivariant Transformer update layers. The entire framework is built on the TorchMD-Net equivariant Transformer backbone, pre-trained on PCQM4Mv2 with a denoising objective proven equivalent to force field learning, and fine-tuned with an auxiliary denoising loss that uses decoupled inputs for stable convergence on force prediction tasks.

## Results

On the MD22 force prediction benchmark, MOES-Pred achieves the best RMSE on 7 out of 7 molecular systems (e.g., 0.402 kcal/mol/Å on AT-AT, 1.257 on Buckyball, 1.156 on Double-walled), outperforming the strongest baseline F-VRN on all systems. On ISO17, MOES-Pred achieves 1.42 kcal/mol energy RMSE and 1.26 kcal/mol/Å force RMSE, surpassing F-VRN (1.55 / 1.38) and Coord (3.32 / 1.96). On QM9, the method attains the best or tied-best result on 7 of 12 quantum chemical property tasks, including HOMO (15.2 meV), U₀ (5.38 meV), U (5.31 meV), and G (6.01 meV).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two denoising pre-training baselines sharing the same TorchMD-Net backbone, MOES-Pred consistently outperforms Coord (Zaidi et al., 2023) on all 9 MD22/ISO17 tasks (e.g., 1.156 vs. 3.247 RMSE on Double-walled) and on 10 of 12 QM9 property tasks. The strongest competitor is F-VRN (Ni et al., 2024a), which MOES-Pred surpasses on 7 of 9 force-prediction tasks (e.g., 1.42 vs. 1.55 energy RMSE on ISO17) but is narrowly outperformed on two MD22 sub-tasks (Stack: 0.382 vs. 0.366; AT-AT: 0.402 vs. 0.389). F-RN, the weaker Frad variant, is beaten on all 9 force tasks. On QM9, MOES-Pred leads F-VRN on U₀ (5.38 vs. 5.39 meV), U (5.31 vs. 6.48), and G (6.01 vs. 6.45), while F-VRN retains an edge on R² (0.357 vs. 0.344) and ZPVE (1.42 vs. 1.43). Among non-pre-training baselines, MABNet (Rao et al., 2025) and PaiNN (Schütt et al., 2021) are competitive on individual QM9 tasks (e.g., PaiNN achieves 0.012 D on dipole moment) but are uniformly outperformed by MOES-Pred on the majority of properties. Transformer-M (Luo et al., 2023) and SphereNet (Liu et al., 2022) each win one QM9 task (Gap and α respectively) but trail MOES-Pred on the remaining 11. SE(3)-DDM (Liu et al., 2023) and 3D-EMGP (Jiao et al., 2023) are consistently outperformed across all QM9 tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

MOES-Pred extends the denoising pre-training lineage established by Coordinate Denoising (Zaidi et al., 2023) and Fractional Denoising (Ni et al., 2024a), which proved that 3D conformational denoising is mathematically equivalent to learning molecular force fields. The key departure is replacing the uniform, molecule-agnostic noise distributions used by Coord and Frad with an adaptive 'energy sentinel' mechanism that iteratively selects and refines molecule-specific perturbation schemes (stretching, bending, torsional, and Gaussian components) based on reconstruction quality and weighted physical plausibility. Additionally, the paper introduces BRICS-based motif decomposition with interatomic influence matrices to inject long-range structural information into the equivariant transformer backbone, going beyond the purely local geometric features exploited by prior denoising methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The adaptive noise strategy addresses a genuine limitation in molecular pre-training: rigid molecules (e.g., aromatic rings) and flexible molecules (e.g., long aliphatic chains) have fundamentally different conformational energy landscapes, yet prior methods apply identical noise magnitudes to all. The energy sentinel's co-evolution with the backbone GNN—shifting emphasis from physical rationality to reconstruction accuracy as training progresses—is a practical design choice that avoids manual hyperparameter tuning. However, the method is restricted to 3D structural inputs and cannot handle SMILES or 2D graph modalities, limiting its applicability in multi-modal drug discovery pipelines. Open questions include whether the energy sentinel approach scales to very large biomolecular systems (beyond MD22's 370-atom limit), whether the BRICS decomposition generalizes to non-organic or macromolecular systems, and whether the gains over Frad persist with larger pre-training corpora or different backbone architectures.
