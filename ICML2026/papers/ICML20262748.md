## Problem

Latent actions offer a unified supervision signal for training VLA models on heterogeneous robotic data, but existing integration strategies are fragmented and lack systematic comparison. It remains unclear how the choice between image-based and action-based latent actions, and between different architectural integration mechanisms (implicit alignment, explicit decoding, conditional generation, token mapping), affects downstream policy performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262748/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The VLA model consumes multi-view RGB images (primary + wrist cameras, resized to 224×224), a natural language instruction, and robot proprioceptive state, producing a continuous action chunk of H steps (H=8 for LIBERO, H=25 for RoboTwin, H=20 for JAKA) in a single forward pass. Image-based latent actions are pre-computed as 4 discrete tokens per temporal step (codebook size 16, embedding dim 128) from a frozen fine-tuned UniVLA, while action-based latent actions discretize each action chunk into H tokens (codebook size 256, embedding dim 128) via a frozen custom VQ-VAE. All four strategies introduce no additional inference stages beyond the baseline's single forward pass.

## Method

The paper builds a unified VLA baseline on Qwen3-VL-2B with LoRA (r=64) and a shared action head, then instantiates four latent action supervision strategies organized by two perspectives: (1) Regularizing the Trajectory via image-based latent actions (LA-Align: cosine-similarity alignment of mid-layer VLM features to latent embeddings; LA-Direct: VLM predicts discrete latent tokens at dedicated placeholders; LA-Cond: VLM jointly predicts latent tokens and action representations with causal conditioning), and (2) Unifying the Target Space via action-based latent actions (LA-Tok: VLM predicts discrete action tokens from a custom VQ-VAE that combines FFT frequency features, 1D temporal convolutions, and a Transformer encoder with a latent consistency masking loss). The key empirical findings are a formulation-task correspondence (image-based latent actions benefit long-horizon reasoning and OOD scene generalization, while action-based latent actions excel at motorically complex dual-arm tasks), discrete token supervision consistently outperforms continuous regression (+2.2–2.7%), and latent action supervision eliminates negative transfer under multi-task joint training on 10 RoboTwin tasks.

## Results

On LIBERO, the best strategy LA-Direct achieves 97.1% average success rate, matching OpenVLA-OFT (97.1%) and outperforming UniVLA (95.2%), π0 (94.2%), and GR00T (93.9%). On RoboTwin 2.0, LA-Tok (action-based) achieves the highest average of 78.0%, surpassing DP3 (71.0%) and π0 (64.0%) by 7.0 and 14.0 points respectively. On real-world JAKA pick-and-place tasks, image-based strategies show the strongest OOD generalization, with LA-Cond reaching 70% OOD average versus only 17% for the baseline. Across all benchmarks, latent action supervision yields consistent gains of +2.4% to +17.5% over the unified baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On LIBERO, LA-Direct (97.1% avg) ties OpenVLA-OFT (97.1%) and outperforms UniVLA (95.2%), π0 (94.2%), GR00T (93.9%), π0-FAST (85.5%), ThinkAct (84.4%), and LAPA (65.7%), with the largest gain on LIBERO-Long (+10.8% over baseline). On RoboTwin 2.0, LA-Tok (78.0% avg) clearly surpasses DP3 (71.0%), π0 (64.0%), RDT (47.0%), and ACT (40.3%), while LA-Cond (73.8%) also exceeds DP3. In multi-task joint training on 10 RoboTwin tasks, LA-Cond achieves 74.5% average with zero negative transfer, outperforming Qwen3OFT (57.9%), RDT (49.6%), π0 (58.1%), ACT (46.5%), and DP (39.2%), and matching DP3 (73.9%). On real-world OOD pick-and-place, image-based strategies (LA-Cond 70%, LA-Direct 67%) dramatically outperform the baseline (17%), while LA-Tok (50%) also improves substantially. The key finding is a formulation-task correspondence: image-based latent actions (LA-Direct) lead on long-horizon LIBERO and OOD generalization, while action-based latent actions (LA-Tok) lead on motorically complex RoboTwin dual-arm tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on UniVLA's image-based latent action model and extends the broader line of work on latent actions for VLA training (LAPA, Moto-GPT, villa-x, IGOR, GR00T) by isolating the effect of latent action supervision from architectural design. Prior methods entangle the choice of latent action formulation with specific VLA architectures, making it unclear whether gains come from the latent action itself or from co-designed components. Here, all four strategies are instantiated within a single unified Qwen3-VL-2B baseline with identical backbone, action head, and placeholder design, enabling a controlled comparison. The paper also introduces a custom action-based latent action model (VQ-VAE with FFT + 1D conv + Transformer encoder and a latent consistency masking loss) that is designed for controlled comparison rather than standalone SOTA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides practitioners with a practical decision framework: image-based latent actions for long-horizon reasoning and scene-level generalization, action-based latent actions for motorically complex (e.g., dual-arm) tasks, and discrete token supervision over continuous regression as the preferred signal. The finding that LA-Cond eliminates negative transfer in multi-task joint training (no task degrades) is practically important for scaling VLA policies to heterogeneous task distributions. Key open questions include whether stronger latent action models would amplify or narrow the observed strategy gaps, whether findings generalize beyond single-arm platforms, and whether the formulation-task correspondence holds at larger scale or with more diverse embodiments.
