## Problem

Existing vision-language alignment models like CLIP excel at global image-text matching but fail to capture fine-grained details such as object attributes, spatial relations, and subtle linguistic distinctions. Prior fine-grained approaches (FineCLIP, FG-CLIP) are predominantly English-only, while Chinese models (Chinese-CLIP, R2D2) focus on coarse-grained alignment, leaving bilingual fine-grained vision-language understanding largely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265170/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes RGB images at data-adaptive resolutions (selected from {128, 256, 576, 784, 1024} per mini-batch) and text descriptions in English or Chinese up to 196 tokens (extended from SigLIP 2's 64). It produces global image-text similarity scores via masked attention pooling, region-level embeddings via a self-attention module over RoIAlign-extracted patch features, and when plugged into downstream frameworks yields class labels, bounding boxes with calibrated category scores, or pixel-level segmentation masks.

## Method

FG-CLIP 2 builds on the SigLIP 2 dual-encoder with a two-stage training paradigm: Stage I performs bilingual global alignment using both short captions and LMM-generated long captions with a sigmoid loss; Stage II adds four fine-grained objectives—region-text contrastive loss (L_FGV), hard-negative textual discrimination (L_FGT), a novel Textual Intra-modal Contrastive (TIC) loss that sharpens the text encoder's representation space by contrasting top-10 filtered hard negatives among high-similarity text pairs, and a Cross-modal Rank loss with global threshold synchronization across GPUs. The architecture extends text input to 196 tokens, replaces stochastic resolution sampling with data-adaptive selection, and adds a self-attention module for region embeddings alongside masked attention pooling for global features. Training uses 1.6B English (LAION-2B-enhanced) and 850M Chinese image-text pairs in Stage I, then 12M English (FineHARD) and 12M Chinese (newly released FineRegion-CN) region-text pairs in Stage II, with fixed loss weights λ₁=0.1, λ₂=0.5, λ₃=0.1, λ₄=0.4.

## Results

On the fine-grained understanding benchmark FG-OVD, FG-CLIP 2 (ViT-B/16) achieves 52.3% Top-1 accuracy on the Hard subset, surpassing FG-CLIP (46.1%) and SigLIP 2 (24.9%) by 6.2 and 27.4 points respectively. On zero-shot bounding box classification (COCO80), FG-CLIP 2 (ViT-B/16) reaches 74.9% Top-1, a 21.5-point gain over SigLIP 2 (53.4%). For long-caption retrieval on DCI, FG-CLIP 2 (ViT-L/16) attains 70.0/71.6 R@1 (I→T/T→I), outperforming FG-CLIP (66.7/66.1) and SigLIP 2 (48.0/49.3). On the new Chinese long-caption retrieval benchmark LIT-CN, FG-CLIP 2 (ViT-So/16) achieves 87.6/86.3 R@1, exceeding Meta CLIP 2 (77.2/67.6) by over 10 points despite using a smaller backbone (1.0B vs. 1.8B parameters).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FG-CLIP 2 consistently outperforms all 21 compared baselines across the 29 evaluation datasets. Against its direct predecessor FG-CLIP, gains are substantial on fine-grained tasks (e.g., +6.2 pts on FG-OVD Hard, +22.6 pts on COCO80 Top-1) and moderate on retrieval (e.g., +3.3 pts on DCI I→T). Compared to SigLIP 2, FG-CLIP 2 achieves dramatic improvements on fine-grained and region-level tasks (e.g., +27.4 pts on FG-OVD Hard at ViT-B/16) while remaining competitive on zero-shot ImageNet-1K classification (79.5% vs. 81.2% at ViT-B/16). Against the larger Meta CLIP 2 (ViT-H/14, 1.8B params), FG-CLIP 2 (ViT-So/16, 1.0B params) wins on all Chinese benchmarks (e.g., +10.4 pts on LIT-CN I→T) and on fine-grained understanding (54.0% vs. 16.5% on FG-OVD Hard). Among open-vocabulary detection baselines, plugging FG-CLIP 2 into LLMDet yields the best AP among open-source methods (53.1 on LVIS minival vs. 51.1 for LLMDet alone and 52.2 for LLMDet+Meta CLIP 2). On Chinese retrieval, FG-CLIP 2 surpasses R2D2 and Chinese-CLIP by 30–50+ points on long-caption tasks, where those models were not designed to operate. FineCLIP and Long-CLIP, while competitive on some retrieval tasks, are significantly outperformed on fine-grained discrimination (e.g., FineCLIP 26.8% vs. FG-CLIP 2 52.3% on FG-OVD Hard at ViT-B/16).

## Relation to Prior Work

FG-CLIP 2 is a direct sequel to FG-CLIP (Xie et al., 2025), extending its fine-grained region-text alignment framework with a two-stage training paradigm, the novel Textual Intra-modal Contrastive (TIC) loss for sharpening text-encoder discrimination, and bilingual (English–Chinese) training data. Architecturally it builds on SigLIP 2's dual-encoder design, adopting its sigmoid contrastive loss and adding a data-adaptive resolution strategy, masked attention pooling, and an auxiliary self-attention module for region embeddings. The CMR loss with global threshold synchronization is borrowed from Zhang et al. (2024b). Conceptually, the paper departs from prior fine-grained work (FineCLIP, LongCLIP, FG-CLIP) by unifying fine-grained and multilingual understanding in a single framework, whereas those approaches were English-only, and from Chinese VL models (Chinese-CLIP, R2D2) by introducing region-level and long-caption supervision that those models lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a notable gap in Chinese fine-grained vision-language research by releasing FineRegion-CN (12M region-text pairs) and four new Chinese evaluation benchmarks (LIT-CN, DCI-CN, DOCCI-CN, BoxClass-CN), resources that were previously absent. The ablation showing mutual promotion between English and Chinese training data (bilingual training improves English-only metrics) is a useful empirical signal for multilingual VL pre-training. Limitations acknowledged by the authors include restricted coverage to English and Chinese only, inability to handle very long textual inputs, and lack of explicit relational modeling among objects—areas that remain open for future work. The released model, code, and benchmarks make this a practical reference point for anyone building bilingual fine-grained VL systems.
