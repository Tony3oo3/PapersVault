## Problem

Directly applying existing visual GRPO methods (e.g., DanceGRPO) to state-of-the-art image-to-video models such as Wan 2.2 and HunyuanVideo 1.5 fails to yield consistent reward improvements. The root cause is that these methods treat each generated sample independently, overlooking the rich relational structure among videos produced from the same conditioning image, which provides critical directional guidance for optimization in the I2V setting.

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference image (serving as the first frame) and an associated text prompt as conditioning, and produces a temporally coherent video. Training is conducted at 320p resolution with 53 frames, 16 denoising steps, and classifier-free guidance of 3.5; groups of G=8 rollouts are generated from identical initial noise to control inference variation. Rewards are computed via HPSv3 (averaging per-frame scores at 2 fps) or Q-Save (combining Visual Quality, Dynamic Quality, and Image Alignment), and the model generalizes to 720p inference despite 320p-only training.

## Method

TAGRPO introduces a trajectory alignment loss (J_align) applied to intermediate latents during the denoising process. Within each group of G rollouts sharing the same initial noise, the highest- and lowest-reward videos serve as positive and negative trajectory anchors; every sample's latent is optimized to increase its likelihood of following the positive anchor's transitions while decreasing its likelihood of following the negative anchor's, via clipped importance ratios weighted by the anchors' normalized advantages. This is combined with standard GRPO as J_TAGRPO = J_GRPO + γ·J_align. A FIFO memory bank stores historical video latents and their reward signals across training steps, reducing per-step rollout cost while providing diverse contrastive context and preventing policy drift. The approach is inspired by contrastive learning principles, treating inter-sample trajectory relationships as the primary optimization signal rather than per-sample reward modulation alone.

## Results

On Wan 2.2, TAGRPO achieves HPSv3 scores of 4.29 (320p) and 5.03 (720p), substantially outperforming both the base model (3.63 / 4.34) and DanceGRPO (3.70 / 4.40). On HunyuanVideo 1.5, TAGRPO reaches HPSv3 of 2.41 (320p) and 4.58 (720p), while DanceGRPO actually degrades performance below the base model (1.84 / 4.33 vs. 2.00 / 4.42). Q-Save improvements are more modest but consistent: 8.81 vs. 8.75 vs. 8.73 (Wan 2.2, 320p) and 10.26 vs. 10.17 vs. 10.13 (Wan 2.2, 720p). Notably, models trained at 320p generalize to 720p without additional fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DanceGRPO (Xue et al., 2025b), TAGRPO wins decisively on HPSv3 across both backbones: on Wan 2.2 it gains +0.59 (320p) and +0.63 (720p) over DanceGRPO, while on HunyuanVideo 1.5 DanceGRPO actually degrades below the base model (1.84 vs. 2.00 at 320p; 4.33 vs. 4.42 at 720p) whereas TAGRPO improves it to 2.41 and 4.58 respectively. On Q-Save, TAGRPO's advantage over DanceGRPO is smaller but consistent (+0.06 to +0.09). Against BranchGRPO (Li et al., 2025b), reproduced for the I2V setting in Appendix B, TAGRPO achieves superior reward convergence on Wan 2.2, attributed to its trajectory alignment loss providing more discriminative signals by leveraging shared structural context among group members. The paper also notes that standard GRPO (as in DanceGRPO) is sensitive to model-specific tuning and prone to stagnation on high-performance models, whereas TAGRPO remains robust across hyperparameter settings and diverse base architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

TAGRPO extends the GRPO lineage from LLMs (DeepSeekMath) through Flow-GRPO's SDE reformulation for flow-matching models to the image-to-video setting, departing from DanceGRPO's per-sample advantage formulation by introducing a contrastive-style trajectory alignment loss on intermediate latents. The memory bank mechanism draws directly from MoCo-style contrastive learning, repurposing the queue of negative samples as a repository of diverse historical rollouts. Conceptually, the paper reframes I2V RL post-training as a relational optimization problem—exploiting shared structural context among videos generated from the same conditioning image—rather than the independent per-sample policy update that prior visual GRPO methods assume.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that standard GRPO stagnates on strong I2V backbones (Wan 2.2, HunyuanVideo 1.5) while working on weaker ones (SkyReels-I2V) highlights a gap in the RL-for-video literature and motivates task-specific optimization strategies. The trajectory alignment loss is simple and generalizes to T2V settings, suggesting the inter-sample relational signal may be broadly useful beyond I2V. Limitations include reliance on image-level reward models (HPSv3, Q-Save) rather than dedicated video reward models, training at 320p resolution, and an internal 10K-pair dataset, leaving open whether video-specific rewards and larger-scale training would yield further gains or alter the relative importance of each component.
