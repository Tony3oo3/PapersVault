## Problem

Existing diffusion-based camouflaged object detection (COD) methods inherit the conventional multi-step sampling paradigm (10–1000 NFEs), treating mask prediction as a complex stochastic generation process. However, the conditional distribution of camouflaged object masks given an input image is inherently low-complexity and near-deterministic, making iterative refinement unnecessary. This paper asks whether COD can be accurately solved in a single reverse diffusion step and provides both theoretical justification and a practical architecture to achieve this.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single 448×448 RGB image as input and produces a binary segmentation mask of the same spatial resolution identifying camouflaged objects. A frozen DINOv2 ViT-B14 backbone extracts multi-level condition features (from layers 3, 6, 9, 12) and a global <cls> token that guide the diffusion decoder. Inference requires exactly one network forward pass (NFE = 1), compared to 10 or 1000 steps for prior diffusion-based COD methods, yielding a sampling time of 0.07 s/image versus 0.18 s/image for CamoDiffusion.

## Method

CODiff reformulates the reverse diffusion process via the Probability Flow ODE, showing that the clean mask can be expressed as x̂₀ = x_t − F_θ(x_t, t, I), where F_θ is a single neural network call that replaces the entire iterative denoising trajectory. The architecture is a UNet-based decoder with three key mechanisms: (1) Global Semantic Guidance (GSG), which uses the DINOv2 <cls> token and timestep embedding to produce per-block modulation parameters (α, β, γ) that scale and shift features in self-attention and cross-attention layers; (2) Hierarchical Condition Integration (HCI) blocks, which perform cross-attention between UNet encoder features and multi-scale DINOv2 features to capture fine-grained structural semantics for complex boundaries; and (3) a Representation Alignment Loss (L_RA) that enforces cosine similarity between the UNet's deepest encoder output and the DINOv2 features computed from clean masks, bridging the semantic gap between the frozen backbone and the trainable diffusion model. The total training objective combines weighted BCE, weighted IoU, SSIM, and MAE segmentation losses with the alignment term (λ = 0.75).

## Results

CODiff achieves state-of-the-art performance on all three COD benchmarks with only 1 NFE. On COD10K, it reaches Sα=0.896, Eφ=0.953, Fβω=0.852, and M=0.014; on NC4K, Sα=0.911, Eφ=0.955, Fβω=0.893, M=0.020; and on CAMO, Sα=0.894, Eφ=0.947, Fβω=0.881, M=0.033. Compared to the second-best method, CODiff reduces MAE by 21.3% on average and improves Fβ by 2.8% on average. Inference time is 0.07 s/img (254.41 GFLOPs), roughly 2.6× faster than CamoDiffusion (0.18 s/img, 506.65 GFLOPs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CODiff outperforms all 18 baselines across every metric on all three datasets. Against the strongest Transformer-based method ZoomNeXt-P (Sα=0.898, Fβω=0.827, M=0.018 on COD10K), CODiff achieves higher Sα (0.896 vs 0.898 is a loss on Sα only) but substantially better Fβω (0.852 vs 0.827) and M (0.014 vs 0.018). Against the best prior diffusion-based method UGDNet (10 NFEs; Sα=0.885, Fβω=0.822, M=0.019 on COD10K), CODiff improves Fβω by 3.0 points and reduces M by 26% while using 10× fewer sampling steps. Compared to CamoDiffusion (10 NFEs), CODiff gains +2.8 Fβω and reduces M from 0.021 to 0.014 on COD10K, and cuts inference time from 0.18 s to 0.07 s per image. Against diffCOD (1000 NFEs), CODiff improves Fβω by 16.8 points on COD10K (0.852 vs 0.684) while being 1000× faster in sampling. The paper also shows that replacing the frozen DINOv2 backbone with a trainable PVTv2 in CamoDiffusion yields Sα=0.880, confirming that CODiff's gains stem from its architecture rather than the backbone choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the diffusion model framework (DDPM, Score-based SDE/PF ODE) and the line of diffusion-based COD methods (diffCOD, CamoDiffusion, UGDNet), but departs fundamentally from their multi-step sampling paradigm. The key conceptual departure is the argument that COD's conditional mask distribution is inherently low-complexity and near-deterministic given the input image, making iterative denoising unnecessary. Architecturally, it repurposes DINOv2 as a frozen feature backbone and introduces GSG (global semantic + temporal modulation) and HCI (multi-scale cross-attention conditioning) to replace the simplistic concatenation used by prior diffusion COD methods, along with a Representation Alignment Loss to bridge the semantic gap between the frozen encoder and the diffusion decoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The one-step formulation offers a 10× reduction in sampling steps (1 vs. 10 NFEs) while simultaneously improving accuracy, which is a clean efficiency-accuracy win for a task where inference speed matters in deployment. The theoretical framing—that structured prediction tasks with low-entropy outputs can bypass iterative diffusion—provides a transferable principle that could apply to other dense prediction problems (e.g., medical segmentation, instance segmentation) where the output distribution is similarly constrained. Open questions include whether the one-step approach degrades on truly ambiguous or multi-modal mask distributions, and whether the reliance on a large frozen DINOv2 backbone limits applicability in resource-constrained settings.
