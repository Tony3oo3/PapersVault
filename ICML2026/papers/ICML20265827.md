## Problem

GRPO-style RL for LLM reasoning is highly sensitive to training-sample difficulty: medium-difficulty samples produce the strongest gradient signal, yet they are scarce in static datasets and rapidly deplete as the model improves (Dynamic Difficulty Shifts). Existing self-play methods that generate new training data suffer from anchor-free question generation (leading to entropy collapse), independent modeling of Questioner and Solver (ignoring co-evolution), and difficulty mismatch between generated questions and the Solver's current capability.

## Input / Output

The Questioner receives an anchor problem, its chain-of-thought solution, and a subject label, and must output a new multi-step reasoning problem wrapped in <question> tags with a verified final answer in \boxed{}. The Solver receives a reasoning problem and produces a step-by-step trajectory enclosed in <think> tags followed by a final answer in \boxed{}. Difficulty is quantified as (1 − correct/N)×100 over N=32 rollouts, with mid-difficulty defined as pass rate in [0.4, 0.8]; generated questions are pseudo-labeled by majority vote over 10 Solver rollouts and double-checked by GPT-5.2 for answer consistency.

## Method

D²Evo is an iterative self-evolution loop in which a single model plays both Questioner and Solver roles (shared weights), with dual difficulty awareness governing both components. Each iteration: (1) the frozen Solver estimates difficulty of real data via 32 rollouts and selects mid-difficulty anchors; (2) the Questioner is trained with GRPO under a difficulty-aware reward that gives full credit only when the Solver's accuracy on the generated question falls in a target band [0.4, 0.6], with power-law decay (exponent a=2) outside; (3) the trained Questioner generates new questions, which are filtered to the same mid-difficulty band and pseudo-labeled; (4) the Solver is updated via GRPO on a hybrid buffer of real anchors and filtered synthetic questions. Because the Questioner and Solver share parameters, learning to generate well-formed questions at a specific difficulty sharpens problem-structure understanding, while improved solving guides more calibrated question generation. As the Solver improves, the anchor set naturally shifts toward harder samples, continuously refreshing the mid-difficulty training signal without requiring new labeled data.

## Results

On mathematical reasoning benchmarks (7-benchmark average), D²Evo achieves 51.35% with Qwen3-4B-Base and 55.32% with Qwen3-8B-Base after 3 iterations, using only 1.4K and 1.7K real training samples respectively. This outperforms the Full Data baseline (19K samples) by 2.07% and 2.62%, and SPICE (20K samples) by 0.76% and 0.98%, on the 4B and 8B models respectively. On general reasoning benchmarks (SuperGPQA, MMLU-Pro, BBEH), D²Evo achieves 32.16% and 36.13% average on the 4B and 8B models, surpassing the Base Model by 4.2% and 2.59% despite being trained exclusively on mathematical data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

D²Evo consistently outperforms all three self-play baselines on mathematical reasoning. Against R-Zero (anchor-free, independent Questioner/Solver), D²Evo gains 4.44% (4B) and 4.91% (8B) on the 7-benchmark math average, with R-Zero also showing unstable performance across iterations (e.g., dropping from 51.87 to 50.41 on 8B). Against AZR (anchor-free, code-generation-oriented), D²Evo leads by 4.99% (4B) and 2.67% (8B). Against SPICE (20K document anchors, no difficulty control), D²Evo achieves higher math scores (51.35 vs. 50.59 on 4B; 55.32 vs. 54.34 on 8B) while using only ~8% of the real data (1.4K/1.7K vs. 20K). On general reasoning, D²Evo (4B: 32.16, 8B: 36.13) trails SPICE (33.53, 38.53) but surpasses Full Data (31.89, 35.61) and all zero-data baselines, demonstrating cross-domain transfer from math-only training. Notably, D²Evo also beats the Full Data baseline (19K samples) on math by 2.07% (4B) and 2.62% (8B), confirming that difficulty-aware self-evolution with far fewer samples is more effective than brute-force training on the full dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-evolving self-play paradigm for LLM reasoning (R-Zero, Absolute-Zero, SPICE) by introducing dual difficulty awareness into both the Questioner and Solver, grounded in the observation that GRPO's gradient signal collapses at difficulty extremes. It departs from prior self-play methods in three specific ways: conditioning question generation on mid-difficulty anchors mined from real data (vs. anchor-free generation), sharing weights between Questioner and Solver to enable co-evolution (vs. independent models), and applying difficulty-aware reward shaping to both roles (vs. difficulty-agnostic training). The difficulty-aware filtering builds on curriculum learning for GRPO, but replaces static easy-to-hard scheduling with an iterative, model-adaptive anchor mining loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~14× reduction in real training samples (1.4K vs. 19–20K) while outperforming full-data and SPICE baselines makes this practically relevant for settings where labeled reasoning data is expensive or scarce. The co-evolution design (shared weights) is a simple but effective mechanism that avoids the entropy collapse seen in anchor-free self-play, and the ablations confirm each component contributes meaningfully. Open questions include scalability to non-mathematical domains, the cost of 32-rollout difficulty estimation per iteration, and whether the framework extends to tool-use or long-horizon reasoning where difficulty is harder to define. The continued reliance on GPT-5.2 for pseudo-label verification, while ablated as non-critical, remains a practical dependency.
