## Problem

Current TAG-FGL methods face three coupled challenges: LLM-based text processing incurs prohibitive token and compute overhead; one-shot graph condensation with fixed LLM surrogates becomes mismatched with the evolving global model, yielding suboptimal accuracy; and LLM-generated summaries lack faithful attribution to specific source spans, creating a black-box bottleneck that hinders local inspection and auditing in regulated settings.

## Input / Output

Each client receives a local TAG subgraph (nodes with raw text, adjacency matrix) and the current global model parameters. The method produces a condensed core graph with budgeted evidence embeddings and a reconstructed sparse adjacency for local GNN training, uploads only standard model updates to the server, and retains locally-stored evidence packs (top gated neighbors per hop plus selected source text chunks with their attention weights) that map predictions back to human-readable spans. Hard budgets constrain each core node to at most B_ℓ neighbors per hop and B_tok text chunks, and the frozen SBERT text encoder is never updated or transmitted.

## Method

DANCE performs round-wise (every 10 rounds) model-in-the-loop condensation with three sequential modules: (1) label-aware node condensation assigns model-conditioned pseudo-labels with a confidence threshold, clusters per-class embeddings via minibatch k-means to obtain prototypes, and selects a class-proportional condensed core by top prototype-match score; (2) hierarchical text condensation scores candidate neighbors via cross-modal attention between graph and text embeddings, applies entmax followed by hard top-B truncation per hop to enforce sparse neighbor gating, then performs a second entmax+truncation pass at the chunk level under a token budget to distill a compact evidence embedding per core node; (3) self-expressive topology reconstruction fuses graph and condensed-text embeddings via a gated cross-modal mechanism, then solves a sparse self-expression problem regularized by an evidence-induced prior to synthesize a lightweight O(Kk)-edge propagation graph. The key differentiator is that all three modules are re-executed with the latest global model each refresh round, preventing surrogate-model mismatch, while locally cached evidence packs and on-device LLM summaries provide auditable, human-readable traces without transmitting raw texts.

## Results

On 8 TAG datasets, DANCE achieves the best or second-best node classification accuracy at 8% condensation ratio, improving over the strongest TAG-FGL baseline (LLaTA) by 2.33% on average while using 33.42% fewer tokens per condensed node. Headline numbers include 88.87% on Cora (vs. 87.26% LLaTA), 80.19% on Arxiv2023 (vs. 78.21% LLaTA), and 78.26% on Instagram (vs. 73.24% LLaTA). On the large-scale Children dataset (1% ratio), DANCE (47.17%) is slightly below FedC4/FedGVD (48.24%) but still competitive. End-to-end runtime on Cora is 0.299 h with 956 LLM calls and 0.26M total tokens, versus 2.492 h / 4,062 calls / 3.66M tokens for LLaTA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DANCE outperforms all 11 baselines on 4 of 5 primary datasets (Cora, Arxiv2023, Instagram, Ratings), with the largest margin over LLaTA (+5.02 pp on Instagram) and over FedC4 (+2.60 pp on Ratings). On the large-scale Children dataset, DANCE (47.17%) trails FedC4 and FedGVD (both 48.24%) by ~1 pp, the only clear loss in the main comparison. Among LLM-based TAG-FGL baselines, LLaTA is the strongest competitor (beating DANCE on no dataset), while LLM4FGL and LLM4RGNN are consistently weaker and frequently time out (OOT) on larger graphs. Federated condensation baselines (FedC4, FedGVD, FedGM) are competitive on Children but fall 2–5 pp behind DANCE on the other datasets. In terms of efficiency, DANCE uses 33.42% fewer tokens per condensed node and completes Cora training in 0.299 h versus 2.492 h (LLaTA) and 10.79 h (LLM4RGNN), confirming that the performance gains come without the token overhead of LLM-heavy baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

DANCE sits at the intersection of federated graph learning (subgraph-FL protocols like FedSage+ and FedGTA), graph condensation (GCond, SFGC, FedC4, FedGVD, FedGM), and LLM-augmented TAG-FGL (LLM4FGL, LLaTA, LLM4RGNN). It departs from prior condensation methods by making the condensed core and textual evidence dynamic—refreshed every 10 rounds using the latest global model—rather than one-shot, and from LLM-based TAG-FGL methods by using LLMs only for local summary generation of pre-selected evidence chunks rather than for structure reasoning or data augmentation. The entmax-based sparse attention for neighbor and chunk selection, combined with self-expressive topology reconstruction, is a novel architectural combination not present in any single prior line of work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DANCE addresses a practical bottleneck in TAG-FGL: the high token and compute cost of repeatedly invoking LLMs on long node texts across federated rounds, while simultaneously providing locally inspectable evidence packs that support regulatory auditing without transmitting raw data. The model-in-the-loop refresh study (showing +6.1% on Cora over static condensation) validates that one-shot surrogates become genuinely mismatched as the global model evolves, a finding that likely applies beyond this specific framework. Open questions include formal differential privacy guarantees (the current noise test is explicitly not a DP proof), standardized interpretability/auditability benchmarks, and extension to heterogeneous, temporal, or multimodal graph settings.
