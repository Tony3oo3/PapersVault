## Problem

In sparse-reward multi-stage manipulation with limited demonstrations, jointly optimizing a learned reward function and policy introduces a moving-target non-stationarity that amplifies both aleatoric and epistemic uncertainty, causing the world model to produce unreliable predictions in out-of-distribution states and destabilizing policy learning. Existing methods either over-explore into task-irrelevant regions or over-trust erroneous model predictions, and lack a principled mechanism to adaptively balance exploration against conservative planning as training progresses.

## Input / Output

The agent receives multi-modal observations comprising RGB images (single viewpoint) and proprioceptive states, which are encoded into a 512-dim latent representation. Planning is performed via MPPI over a 5-member ensemble of latent dynamics models, producing a 7-dimensional continuous action vector (mean and std for a Gaussian policy prior) executed in a receding-horizon fashion. Only 10 expert demonstrations are provided per task, and the system must learn a dense reward function online from sparse stage-based environmental rewards.

## Method

QUEST builds on TD-MPC2 and introduces three coupled mechanisms: (1) RND intrinsic rewards are injected exclusively into the Q-function TD target, leaving the world-model and reward-predictor losses untouched so the dynamics remain aligned with the true environment; (2) a bootstrap-aggregated ensemble of 5 latent dynamics models quantifies uncertainty via pairwise disagreement, which is fed into the MPPI planning objective through an adaptively scheduled coefficient α that transitions from a positive exploration bonus (when discriminator loss is high) to a negative conservative penalty (when the reward function stabilizes), with theoretical guarantees showing the penalty converts two-sided model error into a one-sided lower bound on true performance; (3) a hybrid sampling strategy combines a prioritized replay buffer (weighted by stage) with an expert demonstration buffer at a fixed 50% ratio to prevent rare successful stage transitions from being diluted. Stage-specific binary discriminators convert sparse rewards into dense signals, and the entire system is trained jointly with the encoder, ensemble dynamics, Q-functions, policy prior, and discriminators.

## Results

Across 16 sparse-reward multi-stage manipulation tasks (Meta-World, Robosuite, ManiSkill3) with only 10 expert demonstrations, QUEST achieves a 17% average success-rate improvement over the best baseline, with gains reaching 60% on the most challenging tasks. On ManiSkill3 (the hardest domain), QUEST reaches 97.2% average success rate, outperforming the second-best method by 38%. In zero-shot sim-to-real transfer on a Franka Research 3 robot across five tasks, QUEST achieves 62% average success rate versus 32% for Demo³ and 6% for behavior cloning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QUEST outperforms all four baselines across every benchmark. Against Demo³ (the closest competitor, which also jointly learns reward and policy), QUEST gains roughly 11 percentage points on Robosuite (92.5% vs. 81.4%) and nearly doubles the real-world success rate (62% vs. 32%). MoDem2, which uses demonstration oversampling but lacks online reward learning, trails QUEST by about 6 points (86.7% vs. 92.5%). LaNE, the strongest model-free baseline, achieves 90.1% but at 3.3× the wall-clock cost of QUEST (100.2 h vs. 30.2 h per 500k steps). TD-MPC2 without any demonstration-augmented reward mechanism collapses to 8.8% success, confirming that the learned dense reward is essential for sparse-reward manipulation. The gap widens substantially on high-uncertainty tasks (e.g., ManiSkill PegInsert and StackCube), where QUEST is the only method to reliably solve both within the interaction budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

QUEST builds directly on TD-MPC2's latent-space world model and MPPI planning architecture, and adapts Demo³'s stage-specific discriminator framework for dense reward learning from sparse signals. Its key departure is the adaptive uncertainty-guided planning mechanism: rather than using ensemble disagreement solely for exploration (as in curiosity-driven methods) or as a fixed pessimistic penalty (as in MOPO-style offline RL), QUEST schedules a single coefficient α from positive (exploration bonus) to negative (conservative penalty) based on discriminator loss, with formal theorems showing when the conservative bound is strictly tighter than the vanilla model-error bound. The restriction of RND intrinsic rewards to Q-function updates only—keeping the world model aligned with the true environment—differs from prior curiosity-driven approaches that add intrinsic signals to the total reward and distort dynamics learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: multi-stage manipulation with only 10 expert demonstrations, where the joint reward-policy optimization creates a moving-target problem that destabilizes learning. The zero-shot sim-to-real transfer on five Franka Research 3 tasks (62% average success vs. 32% for Demo³) validates that the uncertainty-guided approach generalizes beyond simulation. However, the method still requires sparse environment rewards and the sim-to-real evaluation is limited to accurately simulatable scenarios; the authors note that leveraging vision-language models for reward signals could enable fully reward-free learning, and that real-world teleoperated demonstrations mapped to simulation remain an open direction.
