## Problem

While interpolation and aggregation are both ubiquitous in practice, their theoretical interplay in regression was largely unexplored. This paper asks: when a hypothesis class is rich enough to interpolate training data, can aggregation provably reduce error compared to a single predictor, which complexity measure governs learnability for natural aggregation rules, and how do these procedures compare to proper learning and general improper learning?

## Input / Output

The framework operates on a hypothesis class H ⊆ [0,1]^X and a realizable distribution D over X × [0,1]. The median-of-three algorithm takes three independent i.i.d. samples S₁, S₂, S₃ of size n from D and an arbitrary interpolator A (any algorithm returning a hypothesis in H matching all training labels), then outputs the pointwise median of the three resulting hypotheses. Performance is measured by the γ-cutoff loss, which quantifies the probability of deviating from the true label by more than γ, and bounds on this loss imply L_p loss bounds via integration.

## Method

The paper establishes that the γ-graph dimension d_γ governs the worst-case sample complexity of a broad class of aggregation procedures, with lower bounds Ω(d_γ/ε) for both proper aggregation of interpolators and finite aggregation with interpolating rules. The key upper bound shows that the pointwise median of just three interpolators trained on independent samples achieves O(d_γ/n) expected cutoff loss, matching the lower bounds and establishing optimality. A critical technical ingredient is an improved interpolator generalization bound with ln²(n/d_γ) dependence (replacing the prior ln²(n)), which is essential for the optimality proof. The paper further shows that proper learning requires an extra ln(1/ε) factor, making aggregation strictly more powerful, and constructs classes with infinite γ-graph dimension but constant γ-OIG dimension where finite interpolating aggregation fails entirely.

## Results

The paper establishes that the γ-graph dimension d_γ governs the worst-case sample complexity of a broad class of aggregation procedures in realizable regression. The headline result (Theorem 3.11) shows that the median-of-three interpolator algorithm achieves expected cutoff loss O(d_γ/n), yielding sample complexity O(d_γ/ε), which is optimal up to constants among all interpolator-based aggregation with proper rules (Theorem 3.5) and finite aggregation with interpolating rules (Theorem 3.8). A key separation result (Theorem 3.12) shows that any proper learner requires Ω((d_γ/ε)·ln(1/ε)) samples, strictly worse than the O(d_γ/ε) achieved by median-of-three. Additionally, Theorem 3.10 demonstrates that some learnable classes (γ-OIG dimension 3) cannot be learned at all by any finite interpolating aggregation, regardless of sample size.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's main comparison is between algorithm classes rather than empirical baselines. Median-of-three interpolators (Theorem 3.11) achieve O(d_γ/ε) sample complexity, matching the lower bounds of Theorem 3.5 (proper aggregation of interpolators) and Theorem 3.8 (finite interpolating aggregation), establishing optimality among these methods. This strictly improves upon proper learning, which requires an additional ln(1/ε) factor (Theorem 3.12 vs. Theorem 3.11). Compared to the prior work of Attias et al. (2023), the paper improves the single-interpolator upper bound from O(d_γ ln²(n)/n) to O(d_γ ln²(n/d_γ)/n) (Lemma B.2), a refinement that is crucial for proving the median-of-three optimality. The paper also extends the binary classification result of Aden-Ali et al. (2024) (majority-of-three for 0-1 loss) to the regression setting with cutoff loss. Finally, Theorem 3.10 shows that finite interpolating aggregation is fundamentally weaker than the OIG-based algorithm of Attias et al. (2023), which achieves Õ(1/ε) on classes where finite interpolating aggregation fails entirely.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the realizable regression framework of Attias et al. (2023), which introduced the γ-graph dimension and γ-OIG dimension as complexity measures, and extends the median-of-three optimality result from Aden-Ali et al. (2024) in binary classification to the regression setting with cutoff loss. The lower-bound constructions are inspired by the Cantor class constructions of Daniely et al. (2011) for multiclass proper learning, adapted here to regression with a key distinction: hypotheses share the same input space and use unique values rather than distinct labels. A key technical contribution is an improved disambiguation size bound (strengthening Alon et al., 2021) that yields a tighter interpolator error bound with ln²(n/d_γ) instead of ln²(n), which is essential for proving optimality of the median-of-three algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a clean hierarchy: general improper learning (governed by γ-OIG dimension) > finite interpolating aggregation (governed by γ-graph dimension) > proper learning (γ-graph dimension times an extra log factor), with the striking result that median-of-three interpolators achieves the optimal rate among all aggregation procedures. The negative result (Theorem 3.10) that some learnable classes are fundamentally unlearnable by any finite interpolating aggregation—regardless of sample size—is a sharp limitation that clarifies when practitioners must resort to non-interpolating rules or infinite ensembles. Open questions include whether the OIG-based optimal learner can be made efficiently implementable, and whether non-interpolating aggregation rules (e.g., predicting outside the input range) can close the gap to general learning for the hard classes identified.
