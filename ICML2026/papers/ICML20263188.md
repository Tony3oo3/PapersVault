## Problem

Equity markets exhibit persistent cross-sectional risk geometry—volatility clustering, correlation structure, and effective factor dimensionality—that evolves more slowly than noisy directional returns, yet no self-supervised method has learned a compact continuous market-state embedding directly from daily equity cross-sections. Existing approaches either fit explicit regime models to hand-designed second-moment statistics or optimize for return prediction, leaving open whether a latent-space predictive objective can naturally emphasize persistent risk structure while discarding unpredictable directional shocks.

## Input / Output

The method consumes daily cross-sections of up to 512 U.S. equities, each described by 28 per-asset features (multi-horizon log returns, realized/EWMA volatility, OHLC candle geometry, volume/liquidity, and market-relative quantities), treated as an unordered set with a validity mask for missing assets. It produces a 128-dimensional market-state embedding per trading day, derived from 24 learned factor tokens (K=24, d=128) that compress the cross-section, over a 21-day temporal context window with a stride curriculum extending the effective horizon to 420 trading days.

## Method

The pipeline has two jointly trained stages: a permutation-invariant Set Transformer tokenizer (row-wise MLP → 2 ISAB blocks with 32 inducing points → PMA pooling to K=24 tokens → final SAB) that maps each day's unordered equity set to fixed factor tokens, and a 6-layer temporal JEPA encoder that operates on L×K time-token grids. The JEPA objective uses structured spatiotemporal masking (contiguous time spans, random factor slots) with ℓ1 regression against an EMA target encoder, forcing the model to predict masked tokens from temporally distant context where persistent cross-sectional patterns provide signal but short-horizon returns do not. Positional encodings use Fourier features of trading-day lag rather than sequence index, enabling a multi-stride curriculum (s∈{1,3,7,21}) that progressively introduces longer temporal horizons. The key design insight is that structured masking acts as an inductive bias: it suppresses directional leakage (ρ=0.14 vs. 0.19 for random masking) while preserving risk-geometry encoding, and the latent prediction objective outperforms reconstruction (MAE) specifically on correlation-geometry descriptors (PC1 share +0.17, effective rank +0.09 in kNN R²).

## Results

The JEPA market-state embedding achieves strong kNN retrieval alignment with cross-sectional risk geometry (Spearman ρ=0.78 for RV21, ρ=0.68 for PC1 variance share) while showing negligible association with market direction (ρ=0.11). On geometry fidelity, latent distances correlate with correlation-matrix distances at Mantel ρ=0.35 (gap≥63d), with kNN neighbors 6.7% closer in geometry than random. The embedding's clearest predictive gain is on diversification recovery (effective-rank increase): ΔAUC=+0.24 and ΔPR-AUC=+0.37 over geometry scalars alone, while stress-onset prediction gains are minimal (ΔAUC=+0.11). Embedding displacement δ_t enriches crash/VIX-spike days at OR=32.3 (p<10⁻⁴) but shows no effect on scheduled macro announcements (OR=1.3, p=0.47).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the MAE reconstruction baseline (He et al., 2021), JEPA wins on all six structure descriptors, with the largest margins on correlation geometry: PC1 variance share (R²=0.29 vs 0.12, Δ=+0.17) and effective rank (0.24 vs 0.15, Δ=+0.09). On the transition-prediction task, JEPA's recovery gain (ΔAUC=+0.110 over the 18-feature handcrafted baseline) is roughly 3× MAE's (+0.033), while MAE's marginal contributions are not statistically supported across the four tasks. Against the HAR volatility model (Corsi, 2009), JEPA's only clear additive gain is on diversification recovery (recovery_eff_rank_UP: ROC-AUC 0.690→0.800, AP 0.139→0.324); on stress-onset and other transition tasks the gain is small or negative. On geometry alignment (Mantel ρ, gap≥63d), a volatility-only baseline actually exceeds JEPA (0.44 vs 0.35), reflecting the well-known dominance of volatility in correlation dynamics; JEPA's contribution is the additional structure beyond volatility, evidenced by its incremental recovery prediction gains and cross-modal news alignment that the vol-only baseline does not achieve. A time-only baseline collapses (ρ=−0.10), confirming the embedding is not merely a temporal index.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the JEPA training paradigm (Assran et al., 2023; V-JEPA 2) to daily equity cross-sections by introducing a permutation-invariant Set Transformer tokenizer (Lee et al., 2019) that compresses each day's unordered asset features into K learned factor tokens, then applies masked spatiotemporal prediction in embedding space over a temporal window of these tokens. It departs from prior financial representation learning (factor models, autoencoder asset pricing) by not optimizing for return prediction or pricing error, and from prior JEPA applications to time series by operating on a time × token grid with set-structured inputs rather than fixed-length sequences. The stride curriculum and EMA teacher schedule follow V-JEPA 2, while the set-tokenization design draws on Perceiver-style latent bottlenecks and Deep Sets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a self-supervised JEPA objective on equity cross-sections alone can produce embeddings that encode persistent risk geometry (volatility, correlation structure, effective factor dimensionality) while largely discarding market direction, and that these embeddings show statistically significant cross-modal alignment with news topic composition without any text supervision. Key limitations include a single-market (U.S.) scope, a small 6-layer model, top-ADV liquidity selection bias, and the finding that the embedding's predictive gains are concentrated on slow recovery dynamics rather than stress onsets. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other markets or eras, whether scaling (deeper encoders, longer contexts, more assets) strengthens the signal, and whether action-conditioned or counterfactual extensions (analogous to V-JEPA 2's robot planning) can enable 'what-if' market analysis.
