## Problem

Existing adjoint-based diffusion samplers (e.g., Adjoint Sampling) are restricted to finite-dimensional state spaces, which precludes their application to sampling Gibbs distributions defined on infinite-dimensional trajectory spaces such as those arising in transition path sampling (TPS) for molecular dynamics. No scalable, matching-based training objective existed for function-space diffusion samplers targeting sample-to-energy settings.

## Input / Output

The method takes an unnormalized energy functional U defined on a Hilbert space (e.g., molecular potential energy computed via OpenMM), two boundary states A and B specifying Dirichlet conditions, and a time horizon T. It produces sampled transition paths as functions X: [0,L] → R^d expanded in a Dirichlet sine basis, so that boundary values X[0]=A and X[L]=B are enforced by construction. A notable property is discretization invariance: the learned sampler can generate paths at arbitrary time resolutions (up to ×100 finer than training) without retraining.

## Method

FAS recasts non-equilibrium sampling in infinite-dimensional Hilbert spaces as a stochastic optimal control (SOC) problem over path space, then applies the Stochastic Maximum Principle (SMP) to derive a local, point-wise matching objective that avoids expensive path-wise backpropagation. The training loss matches a learned control α^θ against a simulation-free target σ_t Q^{1/2} e^{-(T-t)A†} D_x g(X_T), where the adjoint process is replaced by its conditional expectation to yield a closed-form expression. Numerically, the infinite-dimensional SDE is simulated via Galerkin truncation in a Dirichlet sine basis, reducing the problem to K independent 1D coefficient SDEs that can be evolved in parallel. The Dirichlet basis naturally enforces boundary conditions without projection or penalty terms, and the formulation strictly generalizes finite-dimensional Adjoint Matching (recovering it when H = R^d with Q = I).

## Results

On the Muller-Brown synthetic potential, FAS achieves 100% transition hit probability (THP), ETS of −36.70±3.09 kJ/mol, and log-likelihood of 11.48±0.05, outperforming the prior best method DL (Du et al., 2024) which scores 82% THP and −14.93±12.61 ETS. On Alanine Dipeptide, FAS attains perfect THP (100%), zero RMSD, and ETS of −29.46±3.87 kJ/mol, compared to the best baseline TR-LV (61.25% THP, 0.29 Å RMSD, 49.11 kJ/mol ETS). On the more challenging Chignolin protein (138 atoms), FAS again achieves 100% THP and zero RMSD with ETS of −360.85±53.71 kJ/mol, while the best baseline TR-LV reaches only 43.95% THP and −303.98 kJ/mol ETS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FAS dominates all five baselines across every metric on both molecular systems. Against the closest competitor TR-LV (Blessing et al., 2026), FAS improves THP from 61.25% to 100% on Alanine Dipeptide and from 43.95% to 100% on Chignolin, while reducing ETS by roughly 78 kJ/mol and 57 kJ/mol respectively. PIPS (Holdijk et al., 2022) completely fails on Chignolin (0% THP), and TPS-DPS (Seong et al., 2025) achieves only 25% THP there, whereas FAS maintains perfect endpoint satisfaction due to its Dirichlet boundary construction. On the synthetic Muller-Brown benchmark, FAS also outperforms DL (Du et al., 2024) with 100% vs. 82% THP and a substantially lower ETS (−36.70 vs. −14.93 kJ/mol), indicating paths that stay in lower-energy valleys. The zero RMSD achieved by FAS on both molecular systems is a direct consequence of enforcing boundary conditions through the sine-basis expansion, eliminating endpoint drift that plagues gradient-driven or steered baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the finite-dimensional Adjoint Sampling framework (Havens et al., 2025) and its Adjoint Matching training objective (Domingo-Enrich et al., 2025) to infinite-dimensional Hilbert spaces, using the Stochastic Maximum Principle (Bensoussan, 1983; Du and Meng, 2013) to derive a well-posed backward SDE for the adjoint process. The non-equilibrium sampling formulation generalizes the finite-dimensional SOC-based samplers of Tzen and Raginsky (2019) and Zhang and Chen (2022) by replacing Lebesgue measure with a trace-class Gaussian reference measure and deriving an explicit Radon-Nikodym derivative via the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck semigroup. For transition path sampling specifically, prior learning-based methods (Holdijk et al., 2022; Seong et al., 2025; Blessing et al., 2026) operate in finite-dimensional state spaces with local gradient-driven reference dynamics; FAS departs by formulating TPS as sampling from a path-space Gibbs measure in Hilbert space, using a Feynman-Kac-based transition path distribution that avoids reliance on local potential gradients.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The infinite-dimensional formulation provides two practical advantages not available in finite-dimensional TPS: (1) Dirichlet boundary conditions are enforced by construction via the sine basis, yielding perfect endpoint matching without penalty terms or projection, and (2) the learned control is discretization-invariant, transferring across path resolutions without retraining. Limitations include restriction to one-dimensional spatial domains and relatively small molecular systems (≤138 atoms); the authors note extension to PDE generation and graph-based domains as future work. A key open question is whether the Galerkin truncation error and the choice of operator pair (A, Q) remain well-behaved for larger, more complex molecular systems or multi-dimensional function spaces.
