## Problem

State-of-the-art multimodal representation learning methods (e.g., PMRL, GRAM) require all modalities to be present for each instance to achieve unbiased alignment, yet prevalent datasets contain only two modalities (e.g., vision-text or audio-text). This paper identifies and formally characterizes the resulting 'anchor shift'—a provable deviation of the alignment centroid from its optimal position—and shows that existing work either bypasses this issue or relies on a fixed anchor modality that bottlenecks mutual improvement.

## Input / Output

The method consumes up to four modalities per instance: 8 sampled video frames (224×224), audio clips (63 mel-frequency bins, 1024 frames), text captions (≤40 tokens), and subtitles (≤70 tokens), encoded into 512-dimensional representations. Crucially, any subset of modalities may be absent for a given instance; the method imputes missing modality representations in the latent space before alignment. Outputs are unified multimodal embeddings used for bidirectional retrieval (text↔video, text↔audio) and fine-tuned classification across four benchmarks.

## Method

CalMRL introduces a lightweight generative imputation module built on a specialized probabilistic PCA where each modality's representation is modeled as z^m = W^m β + μ^m + ε^m, sharing a common latent β across modalities. A bi-step (EM-like) optimization alternates between (1) computing the closed-form Gaussian posterior p(β | z^Ω) using only observed modalities, and (2) updating generative parameters {W^m, μ^m, σ^m} via closed-form solutions, guaranteeing monotonic likelihood increase. Missing modality representations are then imputed as W^{m'}·m + μ^{m'}, and the concatenated observed-plus-imputed matrix is aligned by maximizing the largest singular value of its GRAM matrix (the PMRL objective). The paper proves via Davis-Kahan bounds that calibration reduces anchor shift whenever the imputation error ε is below a threshold determined by the spectral gap σ₁−σ₂, and provides convergence guarantees for the overall alternating optimization.

## Results

On multimodal retrieval (Recall@1), CalMRL achieves an average of 54.2% across 12 benchmarks (6 vision-text, 6 audio-text), outperforming PMRL (53.8%), VAST (53.3%), GRAM (52.9%), and TRIANGLE (51.6%) under the same missing-modality continual training protocol. The largest gains appear on audio-text retrieval: +16.4 on AudioCaps T→A and +18.8 on AudioCaps A→T over the VAST backbone. On multimodal classification (Accuracy), CalMRL reaches 45.19% average across VGGSound, UCF101, AudioSet, and ESC50, surpassing PMRL (44.04%) and VAST (43.89%), with a notable +1.78% improvement on UCF101.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CalMRL outperforms all four alignment baselines (VAST, GRAM, TRIANGLE, PMRL) under the missing-modality continual training setting, achieving the highest average Recall@1 (54.2%) and the best or second-best result on 10 of 12 retrieval benchmarks. The margin over the strongest baseline PMRL is +0.4% on average, with the largest per-benchmark wins on MSR-VTT V→T (+1.9), DiDeMo T→V (+0.3), and VATEX T→V (+0.9). On classification, CalMRL leads PMRL by +1.15% average accuracy, with the clearest gap on UCF101 (+1.78%). Against the older anchor-based methods (ImageBind, LanguageBind), CalMRL gains 9–10 points on average, confirming that the simultaneous-alignment paradigm with calibration substantially surpasses fixed-anchor strategies. In the sole-dataset ablation (Table 3), CalMRL uniquely resists cross-modal degradation: when trained only on audio-text data, it maintains vision-text performance within 1–2 points of the full-mix setting, whereas GRAM drops 17.8 points on VATEX T→V and VAST drops 3.6 on DiDeMo T→V.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on PMRL (Liu et al., 2026), which introduced an anchor-free multimodal alignment objective by maximizing the largest singular value of the GRAM matrix, and on Probabilistic PCA (Tipping & Bishop, 1999), whose linear-Gaussian generative model is specialized here for cross-modal imputation. It departs from prior missing-modality approaches (SMIL, Knowledge Bridger, MVAE) that operate on downstream tasks or use complex nonlinear generative models, instead applying a lightweight closed-form imputation at the representation level within the simultaneous multi-modality alignment paradigm. The theoretical contribution—bounding the 'anchor shift' via the Davis-Kahan theorem—connects classical matrix perturbation theory to the emerging GRAM/SVD-based alignment literature, a link not previously drawn.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck: state-of-the-art simultaneous multi-modality alignment methods (GRAM, PMRL, TRIANGLE) require all modalities to be present, yet most real-world datasets contain only two modalities per instance. By showing that a simple pPCA-style imputation can calibrate the alignment without additional neural networks, CalMRL offers a drop-in module that makes these methods usable on prevalent incomplete datasets. Limitations include reliance on the linear-Gaussian assumption at the representation level, evaluation primarily on the VAST backbone, and modest absolute gains (~0.5–1% over PMRL). Open questions include whether calibration can be achieved without explicit imputation, how the method behaves under more complex missing patterns (e.g., varying subsets per instance), and whether the approach transfers to non-linear alignment objectives.
