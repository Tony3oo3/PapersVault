## Problem

RAG systems suffer from a functional misalignment: retrievers rank documents by background semantic relevance while generators blindly copy explicit answer spans as cognitive shortcuts, so a document with correct context but a wrong answer is both highly retrieved and misleadingly adopted. Existing defenses operate at the document level, forcing a binary accept-or-discard decision that either wastes valuable background evidence or exposes the model to erroneous shortcuts.

## Input / Output

The method takes a user query and a single retrieved document as input. The CRITIC stage outputs a structured refined context containing fields for relevant_background, factuality_status, detected_error, and final_context (where erroneous answer spans are replaced with [MASK] tokens, irrelevant segments are removed, or the entire context is set to null). The REASONER stage then consumes this edited context and produces a chain-of-thought reasoning trace followed by a final answer, with the reasoning path (direct citation, background deduction, or parametric fallback) determined by the CRITIC's output state.

## Method

A single LLM is trained to play two serialized roles: a CRITIC that performs filter-then-mask evidence purification (removing irrelevant background and replacing factually incorrect answer spans with [MASK] to preserve syntactic structure while blocking the copy-paste shortcut), and a REASONER that generates answers from the residual masked context, compelled to aggregate distributed background clues via deductive reasoning rather than rote extraction. Training proceeds in two stages: (1) multi-task SFT that jointly optimizes the shared parameters for both roles using synthesized filtering, masking, reasoning, and fallback supervision; and (2) path-aware DPO that enforces three behavioral rules—direct citation when no [MASK] is present, background reasoning when [MASK] appears, and parametric fallback when the context is null—via a composite reward combining answer correctness and path-consistency. A theoretical analysis shows that a sufficient threshold on the usage-weight in the reward guarantees the optimal policy strictly favors rule-following over rule-violating responses.

## Results

On the most adversarial scenario (d_IR: incorrect answer, relevant background), the method achieves 70.58% accuracy on IR-QA and 63.55% on IR-NQ, surpassing the strongest baselines by 25.99% and 29.80% respectively. In the fully unreliable scenario (d_II), it reaches 74.00% accuracy, exceeding the best baseline by 32.88%. On high-quality retrieval (d_CR), the method maintains parity with the strongest baselines (80.14% on CR-QA, 81.60% on CR-NQ), confirming no degradation in the ideal case. Across noise-heavy multi-hop settings (d_CM), improvements of 5.50–11.08% over the best baselines are observed on HotpotQA, 2WikiMHQA, and MuSiQue.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Knowledgeable-R1 (Lin et al., 2025), the method achieves the largest gains: +25.99% on IR-QA (70.58% vs 44.59%), +29.80% on IR-NQ (63.55% vs 33.75% for GRPO w/RAG), and +32.88% on II-MC (74.00% vs 41.12%), while matching or slightly exceeding it on d_CR (80.14% vs 80.03% on CR-QA). Against AstuteRAG (Wang et al., 2025), the method wins decisively on all adversarial scenarios (d_IR: +27.50%, d_IM: +27.16%, d_II: +39.58%) and on all d_CM datasets, though AstuteRAG edges ahead on CR-QA (77.97% vs 80.14% — actually the method wins here too). Against CK-PLUG (Bi et al., 2025b), the method dominates across nearly all settings, with the gap most pronounced on d_II (74.00% vs 23.25%) and d_IR (70.58% vs 35.14%). Against InstructRAG (Wei et al., 2024), the method wins on all adversarial and noise scenarios but is comparable on d_CR (81.60% vs 81.40% on CR-NQ). The only scenario where the method does not lead is d_CR (CR-QA), where GRPO w/RAG achieves 82.62% vs the method's 80.14%, a 2.48% gap that the authors attribute to the CRITIC's conservative masking occasionally suppressing correct answer spans.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant document-level paradigm in RAG robustness (TrustRAG, CrAM, AstuteRAG, CK-PLUG, Knowledgeable-R1) by introducing span-level surgical masking of erroneous answer entities while preserving surrounding background context. Methodologically, it builds on DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) but extends it with a path-aware preference construction that encodes three distinct reasoning modes (direct citation, background deduction, parametric fallback) as explicit preference pairs conditioned on the CRITIC's editing output. The conceptual framing of 'functional misalignment'—where the retriever is driven by background relevance while the generator fixates on answer spans—provides a new diagnostic that prior work, which treats retrieved documents as monolithic units, does not explicitly address.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The fine-grained masking approach resolves a practical dilemma in deployed RAG systems: discarding partially correct documents wastes valuable reasoning support, while accepting them exposes the generator to misleading shortcuts. The 25–30% accuracy gains in adversarial settings (incorrect answer, relevant background) and the graceful degradation under CRITIC errors (56.58% even at 100% false-negative rate vs. 38% baseline) suggest the framework is deployable without requiring perfect upstream filtering. Key limitations include evaluation restricted to QA tasks with unambiguous ground truth, the serialized two-pass architecture adding ~3.8s latency, and untested applicability to open-ended generation or multi-turn dialogue. Whether the masking mechanism generalizes to non-entity errors (e.g., incorrect numerical values, subtle logical contradictions) remains an open question.
