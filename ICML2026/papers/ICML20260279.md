## Problem

Existing RL-based reasoning training for LLMs suffers from two coupled limitations: rewards are sparse and domain-restricted (relying on verifiable single-domain tasks like math or code), and purely online exploration is confined to a narrow policy space that leads to early entropy collapse. This paper asks whether structured rubrics can simultaneously provide dense, multi-domain reward signals and serve as targeted offline guidance to break the exploration bottleneck of on-policy RL.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260279/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text question q from a multi-domain scientific corpus (math, physics, chemistry, business, finance, etc.) and produces a reasoning trajectory o_i ending in a final answer. For each question, a question-specific rubric C is pre-generated by OpenAI O3, comprising Factual criteria (verifying intermediate and final results) and Process criteria (checking logical soundness), each with an adaptive integer weight from 1 to 5. A judge model (Qwen3-32B) assigns a binary 0/1 score per criterion, which are then aggregated into a normalized scalar reward in [0,1]. During the off-policy refinement step, the model additionally receives the best on-policy rollout and the set of failed rubric items as conditioning context to produce an improved trajectory.

## Method

RGR-GRPO extends GRPO with two rubric-driven mechanisms. First, it replaces sparse outcome rewards with dense rubric-based rewards: if all Factual criteria are satisfied the reward is set to 1 (allowing alternative valid reasoning paths), otherwise a weighted sum of binary criterion scores provides fine-grained feedback that reduces intra-group variance while mitigating reward hacking. Second, it introduces a three-step off-policy exploration loop: (1) Exploration Assessment checks whether the best on-policy rollout already satisfies all rubric criteria, and if so falls back to standard on-policy GRPO; (2) if not, the best rollout is refined by conditioning the policy on its failed rubric items to generate an off-policy response; (3) a Mix-Policy GRPO objective jointly optimizes on-policy rollouts with the off-policy refined rollout, where the off-policy term is modulated by a shaping function f_shape that upweights low-probability tokens to encourage learning from out-of-distribution successes while suppressing failed refinements. The Exploration Assessment acts as an adaptive variance controller, engaging the high-variance off-policy gradient only when on-policy exploration is insufficient, thereby preventing entropy explosion while sustaining exploration beyond the on-policy boundary.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-7B, RGR-GRPO achieves an average score of 55.8% across 12 benchmarks spanning mathematics, physics, chemistry, and general reasoning, outperforming the base model (44.6%) by 11.2 points and the official instruct model (52.3%) by 3.5 points. Compared to the strongest on-policy baseline Rubric-GRPO (53.7%), RGR-GRPO gains +2.1 points on average, with domain-specific improvements of +7.0% (math), +5.4% (physics), +8.4% (chemistry), and +6.6% (general) over the Outcome-GRPO verifiable-reward baseline. On Qwen2.5-3B, RGR-GRPO reaches 45.5% average, again the best among all methods. RGR-GRPO also achieves superior pass@k performance on SciBench and the best out-of-distribution results on MedMCQA and CS-Bench.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RGR-GRPO outperforms both off-policy baselines across all 12 benchmarks. Against Critique-GRPO (Zhang et al., 2025b), RGR-GRPO gains +5.2 points on average (55.8 vs. 50.6), with particularly large margins on GPQA-Diamond (+11.1), MMLU-Pro (+7.6), and Sci-Chemistry (+5.0). Against LUFFY (Yan et al., 2025), the average improvement is +5.3 points (55.8 vs. 50.5), with the largest gaps on GPQA-Diamond (+9.1) and Sci-Chemistry (+5.0). Both off-policy baselines also underperform the best on-policy method (Rubric-GRPO, 53.7%), which the authors attribute to training instability and entropy collapse caused by uncontrolled distributional shift. RGR-GRPO's Exploration Assessment mechanism prevents this by conditionally applying off-policy refinement only when on-policy exploration is insufficient, yielding stable training dynamics comparable to purely on-policy methods while still achieving the highest scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) as its policy optimization backbone and extends the RLVR paradigm—previously limited to single-domain tasks with sparse verifiable rewards (math, code)—into a multi-domain setting via rubric-based dense rewards. It also draws on recent off-policy guidance methods (LUFFY, Critique-GRPO) but departs from them by using rubrics in a dual role: as fine-grained reward signals during on-policy updates AND as targeted guidance for conditional off-policy self-refinement. The key conceptual departure is the Exploration Assessment mechanism, which adaptively gates off-policy refinement based on whether the current on-policy group already contains a perfect solution, addressing the instability (entropy explosion) that plagues unconditional off-policy mixing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: most RLVR methods are confined to domains with rule-based verifiers, leaving open-ended scientific reasoning (physics, chemistry, general knowledge) underexplored. The pass@k analysis provides a useful diagnostic—showing that standard on-policy RL sharpens existing capabilities without expanding the reasoning frontier (base model catches up at high k), while rubric-guided off-policy exploration genuinely widens it. Limitations include reliance on a strong external model (O3) for rubric generation and a 32B judge for reward computation, raising cost and scalability questions; the approach is validated only on Qwen2.5 at 3B/7B scale. Open questions include whether the method transfers to larger models, whether rubric quality degrades in less-structured domains, and whether the conditional off-policy mechanism generalizes beyond the GRPO objective.
