## Problem

Existing test-time policy adaptation for multi-turn LLM interactions treats the problem as single-axis—either refining prompts or updating weights—yet interaction failures stem from a coupled mixture of context ambiguity and model incapacity. This bifurcated approach causes prompt-only methods to stall at capability ceilings (Deficit Trap) and param-only methods to overfit to noisy interaction histories (Overfitting Trap), resulting in premature stagnation and unnecessarily prolonged dialogues.

## Input / Output

The system consumes a user query, potentially vague or noisy user feedback (treated as raw observational evidence rather than a direct optimization target), and frozen base model parameters, operating within a bounded 10-turn interaction loop. It produces a refined text response, a rank-1 LoRA adapter weight update (θ_t), and a semantically canonicalized instruction for the next turn. Notably, even when explicit user feedback is entirely absent, the semantic stream autonomously synthesizes a corrective instruction from the error signal, and the two update streams (textual and parametric) execute in parallel after each rejection.

## Method

ROSA2 reformulates T²PAM as a joint optimization over the coupled variables φ_t = {x_{t+1}, θ_t}, approximating the full gradient of the KL-divergence interaction loss via importance sampling with reward-weighted log-likelihood. The Semantic Stream (TextGrad) computes textual gradients to refine noisy user feedback into a clarified instruction x_{t+1}*, while the Parametric Stream (ROSA) applies a closed-form linearized LoRA update (rank r=1) to align the policy with a reward-weighted target distribution. The key theoretical contribution is proving that semantic pre-conditioning strictly reduces the norm of the required parameter shift (Theorem 4.1) and deriving a unified convergence bound (Theorem 4.2) that quantifies how joint adaptation amplifies the improvement term while controlling approximation error. This cross-turn coupling ensures parametric updates are computed on semantically clarified trajectories rather than raw noisy prompts, enabling ROSA2 to achieve up to 37.8% accuracy gains while reducing average interaction turns by 40%.

## Results

On Qwen3-8B, ROSA2 achieves 80.8% accuracy on MATH and 80.6% on MATH-500 (a +37.8% gain over the 42.8% baseline), substantially outperforming TextGrad (63.4%/62.4%) and ROSA (62.2%/60.8%). Across all five model sizes (0.5B–8B) and seven benchmark domains, ROSA2 consistently leads, with the largest margins on mathematical and general reasoning (e.g., +27.4% on MMLU-R, +28.2% on SuperGPQA). In terms of interaction efficiency, ROSA2 reduces average turns by 40% (4.4 vs. 6.3 for ROSA on MATH) while achieving the highest Correction Uplift (81.4%). Peak memory overhead is negligible at +3.1 GB on MATH.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ROSA2 dominates both single-axis baselines across all model scales and task domains. Against TextGrad (Words-only), ROSA2 gains +17.4 to +30.8 points on MATH and +13.8 to +37.8 points on MATH-500, confirming that prompt refinement alone hits a capability ceiling the parametric stream can break. Against ROSA (Weights-only), ROSA2 gains +18.6 to +30.8 points on MATH and +13.6 to +37.8 points on MATH-500, validating that parameter updates on unrefined, noisy prompts overfit and stagnate. On general reasoning (MMLU-R, SuperGPQA), ROSA2 outperforms both baselines by 8–28 points, with the largest gap on SuperGPQA (+28.2 over ROSA on Qwen3-8B). In sparse-reward UI agent settings (OSWorld, AndroidWorld), ROSA2 achieves +10.4 and +7.7 points over the SFT baseline, while TextGrad gains only +0.5 and +0.7, and ROSA gains +4.6 and +3.3. ROSA2 also surpasses stronger paradigms: Best-of-16 sampling (67.8%), offline SFT (64.4%), Self-Critique/Reflexion (70.8%), and train+test SDPO (69.0%) on Qwen3-8B/MATH, reaching 80.8% without any prior task-specific training.

## Relation to Prior Work

ROSA2 unifies two previously separate test-time adaptation axes—prompt refinement (TextGrad, Li et al. 2025) and low-rank parameter updates (ROSA, Wei et al. 2025b)—into a single joint optimization over the coupled (context, weights) space. The theoretical target policy construction follows reward-weighted regression (Rafailov et al., 2023), and the parametric stream reuses ROSA's closed-form linearized LoRA update. The key departure from prior work is the cross-turn semantic pre-conditioning mechanism: the Words stream is not merely a parallel prompt optimizer but a feedback normalizer that cleanses noisy user feedback before the Weights stream computes its update, a coupling absent in both single-axis methods and in static joint-optimization frameworks like MetaTuner (Bo et al., 2026).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 37.8% accuracy gain and 40% turn reduction, achieved with rank-1 LoRA and negligible memory overhead, make test-time co-adaptation practically deployable in real-time multi-turn settings without interrupting token streaming. The theoretical guarantee (Theorem 4.1) that semantic refinement strictly bounds the required parameter shift provides a principled explanation for why the two streams are synergistic rather than merely additive. A clear limitation is that ROSA2 cannot synthesize fundamentally absent domain knowledge (e.g., multilingual mathematical concepts, niche API behaviors), so it complements rather than replaces retrieval-augmented approaches. Open questions include how the framework behaves in truly open-ended settings with no verifiable reward signal and whether the rank-1 adapter suffices for substantially larger models or longer interaction horizons.
