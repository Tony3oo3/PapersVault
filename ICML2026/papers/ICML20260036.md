## Problem

Neural networks trained with ERM degrade sharply under distribution shift, and existing robust training methods either require environment annotations (IRM, REx), are overly conservative (DRO), or incur prohibitive computational cost (KL-RS requires multiple full trainings via threshold search). Robust Satisficing (RS) offers a principled alternative by minimizing fragility—the rate of performance degradation under distributional departure—while maintaining a target in-distribution performance level, but lacked an efficient gradient-based algorithm for neural network training.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard supervised inputs: RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10-LT, 224×224 for Waterbirds and TerraIncognita) or tabular feature vectors, and produces class labels or scalar regression predictions. A key operational detail is that the fragility objective is evaluated per mini-batch, where the adversary reweights samples (instance-wise) or groups (group-wise/class-wise) within that batch, and the satisficing threshold τ is fixed a priori (0.1 for cross-entropy classification, 0.5 for standardized MSE regression) rather than tuned on validation data.

## Method

IRS optimizes the RS fragility objective via a single gradient-based training run. The central technical insight is that the inner maximization over the probability simplex (finding the worst-case reweighting p*) is reduced to a one-dimensional scalar search along an exponential-tilting trajectory c(h) = p̂·exp(h·ℓ_θ) / Σ p̂_j·exp(h·ℓ_θ_j), parameterized by h > 0, when the divergence is KL. Danskin's theorem then yields the fragility gradient as p*ᵀ∇_θℓ_θ / D_KL(p*‖p̂), requiring no backpropagation through the argmax map. A threshold-scheduling strategy (τ_t = max{τ, (1+ε)·L_emp(θ_t)}) stabilizes early training, and gradient steps are skipped entirely whenever the current mini-batch already satisfies the threshold under p*, yielding wall-clock speedups. The per-step cost is one forward pass, one backward pass, and a low-cost 1D scalar search—matching standard SGD—while the method can be instantiated in instance-wise, group-wise, or class-wise modes to exploit known shift structure.

## Results

On CIFAR-10-LT at IF=100, IRS achieves 57.5% average accuracy, 28.5% tail-class accuracy, and 17.4% worst-class accuracy, substantially outperforming ERM (55.0%/20.8%/8.8%) and all robust baselines. On Waterbirds, IRS reaches 88.01% worst-group accuracy versus 63.08% for ERM and 85.51% for GroupDRO, while maintaining 91.77% overall accuracy. On tabular regression, IRS achieves the lowest test MSE on both Bike Sharing (0.815 vs. 1.009 for ERM) and Concrete Strength (1.116 vs. 1.316 for ERM). IRS also trains faster than ERM on Waterbirds (12:30 vs. 15:45 wall-clock) due to skipped updates when the satisficing threshold is already met.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IRS consistently outperforms all baselines on tail-class and worst-group metrics. Against SAM and KL-RS, the gap is dramatic: at IF=100, SAM and KL-RS collapse to near-zero worst-class accuracy (0.000) while IRS maintains 17.4%, and KL-RS's reliance on multiple full retrainings makes it impractical at scale. Against V-REx and MM-REx, IRS leads by 9.7 and 9.6 percentage points on tail accuracy at IF=100 (28.5% vs. 18.8%/18.9%). Against IRM, IRS dominates across all imbalance factors (e.g., 17.4% vs. 1.2% worst-class at IF=100). Against GroupDRO, IRS achieves higher worst-group accuracy on Waterbirds (88.01% vs. 85.51%) with comparable overall accuracy (91.77% vs. 92.13%), and superior tail/worst-class accuracy on CIFAR-10-LT at all imbalance levels. Against χ²-DRO and CVaR-DRO, IRS leads on worst-class accuracy at IF=100 (17.4% vs. 12.5%/8.0%) while matching or exceeding their average accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper operationalizes the Robust Satisficing (RS) objective from operations research (Long et al., 2023) and the KL-divergence-based neural network formulation of Yan et al. (2024, KL-RS) into a single-run gradient-based training procedure. The key technical departure from KL-RS is reducing the inner adversarial maximization over the probability simplex to a one-dimensional exponential-tilting trajectory via Lagrangian stationarity and Danskin's theorem, eliminating the need for multiple full retraining passes. Conceptually, IRS sits between worst-case DRO (which is pessimistic and requires committing to an ambiguity radius) and ERM (which ignores shift), offering a satisficing criterion that certifies performance against arbitrary deployment distributions without a fixed radius. The method is positioned as complementary to, rather than a replacement for, invariance-based (IRM, REx) and sharpness-based (SAM) approaches, and it requires no group or environment annotations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that IRS achieves robustness gains at essentially the same per-step cost as SGD (one forward + one backward pass plus a cheap scalar search), and can even be faster in wall-clock time due to skipped updates when the satisficing threshold is already met. This makes fragility minimization a drop-in alternative to ERM for practitioners who need OOD robustness without the overhead of DRO or the annotation requirements of IRM/REx. Key open questions include principled, data-dependent selection of the satisficing threshold τ (currently fixed a priori), extending the adversarial support beyond the training set, and validating at larger scale (ImageNet, ViT-scale models). The restriction to reweightings of the observed support means IRS cannot handle truly novel feature regions, limiting its applicability to shifts that are mixtures of training modes.
