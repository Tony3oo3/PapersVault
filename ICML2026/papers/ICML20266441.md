## Problem

Neural solvers for constraint satisfaction problems achieve high in-distribution accuracy but exhibit persistent constraint violations under distribution shift that no amount of test-time compute eliminates. This paper argues that when hard constraints are specifiable, verification is polynomial-time, and violation costs are high, purely statistical learning is fundamentally insufficient for certified correctness, and symbolic integration is architecturally necessary rather than merely beneficial.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266441/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The PVS framework consumes a problem instance x (e.g., a partially filled Sudoku grid, digit images, or a natural-language problem description) together with an explicit constraint set C and a refinement budget T. The neural proposer handles unstructured modalities (images, text) via learned encoders, while the symbolic verifier operates on the discrete candidate assignment and returns structured, spatially localized diagnostics (e.g., 'cell (3,4) conflicts with row 3') rather than a simple boolean. The system outputs either a certified solution y⊨C, a verified proof of unsatisfiability, or—after exhausting the refinement budget—a solution from the complete symbolic fallback solver, with the certification status (fast-path verified vs. solver fallback) recorded per instance.

## Method

The Proposer-Verifier-Solver (PVS) framework decomposes constraint reasoning into three modules: a Neural Proposer P_θ (System 1) that generates candidate assignments, a Symbolic Verifier V that checks constraint satisfaction in O(n²) time and returns structured diagnostics D_t, and a Symbolic Solver S that serves as a complete fallback. The core mechanism is an agentic refinement loop (Algorithm 1) in which the verifier's localized diagnostics are folded back into the proposer's conditioning input via a CONSTRUCTFEEDBACK operator—implemented as in-context prepending for LLMs, edge masks for GNNs, or additive symbolic-energy terms for oscillator models—without updating any parameters. The critical architectural property is Inversion of Control: the verifier is a mandatory system-level gatekeeper that the proposer is structurally precluded from bypassing, distinguishing PVS from both soft-regularizer approaches (Paradigm A) and ad-hoc tool-use agents (Paradigm B). Proposition 5.1 guarantees that every returned output satisfies the constraints by construction, independent of the neural proposer's reliability, because the only two exit paths are verifier-certified acceptance or complete-solver fallback.

## Results

The PVS framework achieves 100% certified correctness on Sudoku OOD (vs. 10.5% residual violations for AKOrN at maximum compute), 91% pass@1 on HumanEval (vs. 20% GPT-4 zero-shot failure), 99.97% feasibility on TSPTW-100 (vs. 38.22% POMO infeasible routes), and 95.08% on miniF2F theorem proving (vs. ~53% Kimina-Prover pass@1). The core claim is that the propose-verify-refine pattern with a mandatory symbolic gatekeeper eliminates the certification gap that persists in all neural-only baselines regardless of test-time compute. Sample efficiency gains are also dramatic: NeurASP requires only 25 training examples versus 216,000 for RRN to reach comparable in-distribution performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SATNet (Wang et al., ICML 2019), the PVS position shows a 95.1-point OOD collapse (98.3% ID → 3.2% OOD) that no amount of architectural softening resolves, whereas PVS-style certification maintains 100% by construction. Against AKOrN (Miyato et al., ICLR 2025), the strongest neural-only solver with 128 Kuramoto steps and 4,096 energy-based samples, PVS still wins: AKOrN plateaus at 10.5% OOD violations while PVS achieves 0% via the symbolic fallback. NeurASP (Yang et al., IJCAI 2020) is treated as a control baseline rather than a competitor—it achieves 100% constraint satisfaction with 25 examples but only handles perception, whereas PVS extends the pattern to active neural proposal. RRN (Palm et al., NeurIPS 2018) requires 216,000 examples (~8,640× NeurASP) and still collapses under the same OOD shift, confirming the certification gap is architectural. In cross-domain comparisons, PVS-style pipelines dominate: Reflexion (Shinn et al., 2023) lifts HumanEval from 80% to 91%, CaR (Bi et al., ICLR 2026) drives TSPTW-100 infeasibility from 38.22% to 0.03%, and BFS-Prover-V2 (Xin et al., 2025) reaches 95.08% on miniF2F versus Kimina-Prover's (Wang et al., 2025) ~53%—all instances of the same mandatory-verifier pattern the paper advocates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This position paper synthesizes four existing paradigms for constraint satisfaction (deterministic solvers, metaheuristics, end-to-end neural learning, and LLM-based reasoning) into a unified taxonomy and proposes the Proposer-Verifier-Solver (PVS) framework as an architectural principle. It shares the core insight with Kambhampati et al. (2024) that neural systems require external symbolic verification, but departs by advocating bidirectional integration (neural perception/heuristics feeding symbolic solvers, and symbolic verification gating neural outputs) rather than the unidirectional generator-critic pattern of LLM-Modulo. The 'Inversion of Control' principle—making the symbolic verifier a mandatory system-level gatekeeper rather than an optional tool call—repurposes existing agentic loop ideas (Reflexion, BFS-Prover-V2, CaR) under a single structural guarantee (Proposition 5.1) that no output path bypasses verification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a falsifiable criterion (a purely neural system achieving <1% OOD violation without symbolic verification or architectural constraint enforcement) that gives the community a concrete benchmark for measuring progress toward closing the certification gap. The PVS pattern, validated across four domains (Sudoku, code generation, vehicle routing, theorem proving), offers a practical architectural template for safety-critical deployment where constraint violations are costly. Open questions include whether the framework degrades gracefully when verification itself is expensive (beyond NP), how to handle soft constraints with learned violation costs, and whether sufficiently large reasoning models might eventually internalize constraint satisfaction well enough to make the symbolic gatekeeper redundant—though the paper argues this would be an economic inefficiency even if achievable.
