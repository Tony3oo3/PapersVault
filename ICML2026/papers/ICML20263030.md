## Problem

The diagonal loading-based non-convex relaxation for Densest k-Subgraph (DkS) proposed by Lu et al. (2025) achieves strong empirical performance with λ=1, but three critical theoretical gaps remain: tightness is only guaranteed for subgraph sizes k ≤ ω(G), the optimization landscape lacks a rigorous geometric characterization, and there is no explanation for why Frank-Wolfe naturally converges to integral solutions without rounding. This paper resolves all three open questions.

## Input / Output

The method takes an unweighted undirected graph's adjacency matrix A ∈ {0,1}^{n×n}, a target subgraph size k, and a non-integral penalty parameter λ > 1 (e.g., λ=1.5), with a feasible initialization x^(0) = (k/n)·1. It outputs an integral binary vector x ∈ {0,1}^n identifying the densest k-subgraph, with per-iteration cost O(m+n) dominated by sparse matrix-vector multiplication and O(n) Quickselect operations. The key property is that the algorithm guarantees finite-step exact convergence to an integral local maximizer with no post-processing rounding step required.

## Method

The paper establishes three theoretical pillars and builds an algorithm on them. First, it proves the relaxation is tight for all k when λ ≥ 1 (extending Barman's λ=2 result via a Motzkin-Straus-style argument). Second, it proves a strict dichotomy of stationary points: all integral stationary points are local maximizers, while all non-integral stationary points are strict saddles with explicit positive curvature 2(λ − a_ij) > 0 along feasible directions. Third, it proposes Saddle-Escaping Frank-Wolfe (SE-FW), which augments standard FW updates with a deterministic escape step triggered when the Frank-Wolfe gap falls below a threshold and the iterate is not in a local maximizer's basin. The escape step identifies the k-th and (k+1)-th largest entries of the current iterate and moves mass between them along e_j − e_l, exploiting the positive curvature to guarantee Ω(1/n²) objective ascent per escape. Combined with an adaptive step-size rule that enables exact convergence in the basin of a local maximizer, the algorithm achieves finite-step exact convergence to an integral local maximizer with at most O(k²n²) saddle escapes.

## Results

On real-world SNAP datasets (Facebook, Web-Stanford), SE-FW achieves identical subgraph density to Standard Frank-Wolfe (e.g., 1.0 on Facebook k=20, 0.9895 on Web-Stanford k=20) with at most 1 additional iteration, confirming that the saddle-escape mechanism is rarely triggered on sparse real graphs. On synthetic planted clique graphs (n=10,000, p=0.05, clique size k=30), SE-FW recovers the planted clique in 9 out of 20 instances, compared to only 2/20 for greedy peeling, and succeeds in every case where greedy peeling succeeds. The finite-step exact convergence is empirically verified: the Frank-Wolfe gap drops to zero and integrality reaches 1 in a single 'final jump' step, in contrast to the sublinear asymptotic convergence of the Lacoste-Julien step-size rule.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Standard Frank-Wolfe (as configured in Lu et al., 2025 with λ=1), SE-FW produces essentially identical results on both Facebook and Web-Stanford: the same density values (1.0, 0.9895, 0.7298) and iteration counts within ±1. This confirms the paper's theoretical prediction that the benign landscape of the relaxation means saddle points are rarely encountered on real-world sparse graphs, so the escape mechanism adds negligible overhead. Against greedy peeling (Charikar, 2000) on synthetic planted clique graphs, SE-FW substantially outperforms: it recovers the planted 30-clique in 9/20 Erdős-Rényi instances versus only 2/20 for greedy peeling, and succeeds in all instances where greedy peeling succeeds. The paper also contrasts its adaptive step-size rule (Bertsekas) with the Lacoste-Julien (2016) rule, showing that the latter's constant denominator prevents the 'final jump' to exact integral convergence, yielding only sublinear asymptotic convergence instead of finite-step exact convergence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper provides the missing theoretical foundation for the diagonal loading relaxation introduced by Lu et al. (AAAI 2025), which empirically showed that a graph-independent penalty λ=1 yields a benign landscape solvable by Frank-Wolfe. It extends Barman's (2018) tightness result from λ=2 to all λ≥1, and generalizes the tightness guarantee from k≤ω(G) to arbitrary subgraph sizes. Algorithmically, it departs from the random-perturbation saddle-escape paradigm (Ge et al., 2015; Jin et al., 2017; Lu et al., 2020) by exploiting the explicit positive curvature at non-integral stationary points to construct deterministic ascent directions, yielding finite-step exact convergence rather than the asymptotic guarantees of prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The strict dichotomy of stationary points (integral → local maximizer, non-integral → strict saddle) and the finite-step exact convergence guarantee resolve the central open question of why Frank-Wolfe naturally produces integral solutions without rounding for this relaxation. The monotonicity result (Theorem 3.2) provides a principled explanation for the empirical observation that smaller λ>1 yields better solutions, guiding practitioners toward λ≈1.5. However, the practical impact is tempered: on real-world graphs the saddle-escape mechanism is almost never triggered (Table 1 shows SE-FW ≈ standard FW), and the convergence bounds depend on problem-dependent constants (τ, θ) that are infeasible to compute exactly. Open questions include whether the framework extends to weighted graphs, directed graphs, or other cardinality-constrained quadratic problems, and whether the non-integrality requirement on λ for the strict dichotomy can be relaxed.
