## Problem

LLM-based Automatic Heuristic Design (AHD) methods rely on fixed evolutionary operators applied indiscriminately across all candidates, ignoring operator heterogeneity and state-dependent utility. Additionally, existing experience mechanisms use outcome-based summarization that treats algorithms as isolated items, failing to capture parent-to-offspring trajectories and the situational conditions under which a lesson is applicable, leading to misleading guidance in incompatible search states.

## Input / Output

The system takes a combinatorial optimization instance set (e.g., 64 Euclidean TSP instances of varying scale, Weibull-distributed BPP streams, or knapsack instances), an LLM backbone (DeepSeek-V3 by default, also tested with GPT-4o and GPT-4o-mini), and an initial library of five operator prompt templates. It outputs a heuristic as a tuple (d, c) — a natural-language description paired with executable code — that can be a constructive scoring function, an ACO heuristic information matrix, or a GLS perturbation rule. The evolutionary loop runs for 30 generations with population size 10, and each candidate is evaluated on the full instance set to compute a scalar fitness.

## Method

RefineEvo is a multi-agent evolutionary framework with three coordinated components. A Planner perceives population-level statistics (normalized fitness diversity, improvement rate) and per-operator utility (validity rate, parent-beating success rate) to dynamically switch between exploration and exploitation modes, select a subset of operators from a five-operator library, and trigger event-driven operator refinement that rewrites prompt descriptions from generic instructions into context-specific guidelines when an operator stagnates. A Reflector analyzes each parent-to-offspring trajectory and distills structured experiences — each containing a modification summary, a do/avoid recommendation, and an explicit applicable condition — into a Bidirectional Experience Pool (BEP) that stores both positive insights and negative pitfalls separately. Before generating offspring, an Evolver formulates a semantic query from the operator goal and parent context, retrieves top-k experiences via cosine similarity on Qwen text-embedding-v4 vectors, and injects them as 'Insights to Follow' and 'Pitfalls to Avoid' into the generation prompt. A lightweight credit-assignment mechanism updates each experience's utility score based on downstream offspring performance, pruning entries that fall below zero to maintain pool quality.

## Results

On synthetic TSP (n=500), RefineEvo achieves an objective of 18.476, outperforming the closest LLM-based baseline OpenEvolve (18.792) and the NCO method POMO (22.115). On the real-world TSPLIB benchmark, RefineEvo attains the lowest mean optimality gap of 11.54%, beating OpenEvolve (12.92%) by a clear margin. On Online BPP at the largest scale (100k_C500), RefineEvo achieves a 0.00% excess-bin ratio, while MCTS-AHD and HSEvo degrade to 0.42%. On CVRP (n=500), RefineEvo reduces total cost to 84.601 versus 88.086 for OpenEvolve and 88.096 for PartEvo.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RefineEvo outperforms all 12 baselines across the majority of evaluated settings. The closest competitor is OpenEvolve, which RefineEvo beats on TSP (e.g., 18.476 vs. 18.792 at n=500), CVRP (84.601 vs. 88.086 at n=500), and TSPLIB (11.54% vs. 12.92% gap). The NCO method POMO retains an advantage only on small-scale TSP (n≤100) where its fixed training distribution fits well, but is decisively outperformed at n≥200. Among LLM-based AHD methods, EoH and ReEvo are consistently outperformed on both TSP and KP, while MCTS-AHD and HSEvo degrade notably on large-capacity BPP (0.42–0.50% excess-bin ratio, comparable to the Best Fit handcrafted baseline) whereas RefineEvo sustains 0.00–0.05%. On GLS for TSP, RefineEvo achieves the best or tied-best optimality gap (0.00% at n=100) while also reducing runtime to 843.7 ms versus MCTS-AHD's 849.2 ms. Handcrafted baselines (Greedy, First Fit, Best Fit) are uniformly outperformed by all LLM-based methods, confirming the value of automated heuristic design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM-based Automatic Heuristic Design (AHD) paradigm established by FunSearch (Romera-Paredes et al., 2024) and EoH (Liu et al., 2024), which couple population-based evolutionary search with LLM-generated code. It departs from prior methods (EoH, ReEvo, HSEvo, MCTS-AHD) in two key ways: replacing fixed, uniformly-applied prompt operators with a Planner that performs state-aware scheduling and event-triggered operator refinement, and replacing outcome-centric experience summarization with a Bidirectional Experience Pool that stores trajectory-aware, situation-conditioned positive and negative insights. The multi-agent coordination (Planner–Evolver–Reflector) draws on retrieval-augmented generation and reflective memory ideas from the broader LLM agent literature, but applies them specifically to the evolutionary search loop for heuristic design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RefineEvo demonstrates that planning-guided, experience-driven evolution can consistently outperform static-operator baselines across diverse combinatorial optimization problems (TSP, CVRP, VRPTW, BPP, KP, MKP) and algorithmic paradigms (constructive heuristics, ACO, GLS), while also improving token efficiency by roughly 2×. The cross-task transferability of experiences (e.g., TSP-derived insights aiding BPP) suggests that structured experience pools may capture transferable algorithmic patterns beyond single-problem settings. However, the evaluation remains confined to classic CO benchmarks with synthetic or standard-library instances, and the framework's reliance on LLM code-generation quality limits generalizability to industrial problems with complex, multi-constraint structures. Open questions include theoretical convergence guarantees for experience-guided evolutionary search and extension to multi-objective or dynamically changing problem settings.
