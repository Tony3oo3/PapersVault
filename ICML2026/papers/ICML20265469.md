## Problem

Long-tailed multi-label text classification is typically addressed via heuristic re-sampling or static re-weighting, which the paper argues is structurally insufficient: frequent labels form dominant coalitions that monopolize shared representations and gradient allocation during optimization, forcing rare labels onto superficial shortcuts. The missing ingredient is a principled mechanism that treats training as a structured resource negotiation among label groups, dynamically balancing rapid learning of frequent concepts with preservation of rare signals.

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw text documents (truncated to 128–512 tokens depending on dataset) encoded by a RoBERTa-base backbone, and produces an L-dimensional probability vector via per-label sigmoid outputs. An intermediate learned artifact is a sparse L×L directed adjacency matrix that partitions labels into K causally coherent coalitions (typically K=5), each of which acts as a bargaining player during optimization. The final prediction is a multi-hot label assignment, but the training signal is structured per-coalition rather than per-label.

## Method

NBCG operates in two phases. Phase 1 (Coalition Induction) trains a Neural Structural Equation Model to learn a directed label-dependency matrix A under L1 sparsity and NOTEARS DAG constraints, then thresholds A and extracts maximal weakly connected components as causally coherent coalitions. Phase 2 (Nash-Bargained Optimization) replaces the standard weighted-sum BCE loss with the Nash Bargaining Solution: each coalition's utility is exp(−aggregated BCE risk), an adaptive disagreement point tracks the EMA of each coalition's utility history, and the model maximizes the log-Nash product of utility gains relative to these disagreement points. The resulting gradient decomposes into per-coalition terms weighted by 1/(U_k − d_k), which automatically amplifies gradients for under-served (rare) coalitions while suppressing dominant ones, yielding a Pareto-efficient, self-adapting credit allocation without manual weight tuning.

## Results

NBCG achieves state-of-the-art Rare-Label F1 across all four benchmarks, outperforming the strongest baseline CCG by 2.0–3.5 percentage points (e.g., 65.8 vs. 62.9 on DBpedia Rare-F1@30%, 78.5 vs. 76.1 on 20 Newsgroups Rare-F1@30%). NBCG also exhibits the lowest variance (±0.2–0.3) among all methods, indicating superior training stability. On high-cardinality samples (≥6 labels) in Reuters, NBCG outperforms CCG by 5.4% mAP (78.5 vs. 73.1), demonstrating the largest gains where label correlations are most complex.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NBCG wins against all seven baselines on every dataset and every sparsity threshold. The closest competitor is CCG (a recent causal cooperative framework), which NBCG surpasses by 2.0–3.5 points on Rare-F1 (e.g., 65.8 vs. 62.9 on DBpedia@30%, 65.5 vs. 62.9 on Reuters@40%). Among graph-based methods, TextING is the strongest (74.6 on 20NG@30%), still trailing NBCG by 3.9 points. The plain RoBERTa baseline, while competitive on head labels, lags significantly on rare labels (e.g., 55.4 vs. 65.8 on DBpedia@30%), confirming that standard fine-tuning fails to break dominant coalition capture. NBCG also shows the tightest standard deviations (±0.2–0.3) across all settings, indicating more stable optimization than baselines (±0.3–0.7). The ablation confirms the Nash bargaining mechanism is the primary driver: removing it causes a 4.0-point drop, while removing the Neural SEM structure costs 2.3 points and fixing the disagreement point costs 1.6 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

NBCG sits at the intersection of two threads: causal structure learning via Neural SEMs (Yu et al., 2019) and game-theoretic multi-objective optimization. Its closest predecessor is CCG (Fan et al., 2025), which also uses Neural SEM to discover causal label structure but relies on a static cooperative objective; NBCG departs by replacing the utilitarian weighted-sum with a Nash Bargaining Solution over causally-induced coalitions, introducing an adaptive disagreement point that dynamically re-allocates gradient credit. This positions the paper as a principled upgrade over both re-weighting heuristics (Cui et al., 2019; Lin et al., 2017) and correlational label-graph GNNs (HGAT, TextGCN, HyperGAT), which model dependencies but leave the optimization objective unchanged.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reframing of long-tailed multi-label learning as a resource-negotiation problem among causal coalitions offers a theoretically grounded alternative to ad-hoc re-weighting, with the Nash gradient providing automatic, Pareto-efficient credit allocation without manual tuning. However, the evaluation is limited to small label spaces (14–90 labels) and four standard benchmarks; scalability to thousands of labels (e.g., medical coding with 10k+ ICD codes) remains untested, and the one-shot causal structure learning may not adapt to distribution shifts. Open questions include whether the learned DAG truly captures mechanism-relevant dependencies versus statistical co-occurrence, and whether the N=5 coalition granularity generalizes across domains or requires per-dataset tuning.
