## Problem

Standard Riemannian radial distributions (e.g., Riemannian Gaussian, von Mises-Fisher) are inherently isotropic, making them unable to capture anisotropic dispersion and latent heterogeneity in manifold-valued data with heterogeneous subpopulations. Moreover, no convergence guarantees existed for mixtures of Riemannian radial distributions, leaving a theoretical gap in manifold mixture modeling. This paper addresses both gaps by introducing a geometry-aware mixture model with latent factor structure and establishing the first nonasymptotic convergence theory for mixture models on general Riemannian homogeneous spaces.

## Input / Output

The method takes n data points on a Riemannian homogeneous space (sphere, complex projective shape space, or hyperbolic space), a number of mixture components K, and a latent dimension q. It outputs cluster labels and per-component parameters: a location point on the manifold, a loading matrix whose columns lie in the tangent space at that location, a positive scale parameter, and a mixture weight. Because the marginal density after integrating out the latent factor has no closed form, the algorithm approximates the likelihood via Monte Carlo sampling of the latent vector during label assignment, and the scale parameter is updated by maximizing a geometric objective involving the normalizing constant Z(σ).

## Method

MGFA models each mixture component as a geodesic factor model x = Exp(Exp(α, Vz), ε), where α is a location on the manifold, V is a loading matrix in the tangent space, z ~ N(0, I_q) is a latent factor, and ε is Riemannian Gaussian noise; conditional on z, x follows a Riemannian Gaussian centered at Exp_α(Vz). Estimation alternates between a parameter update (Fréchet mean for location, PCA on log-mapped residuals for loadings, a geometric MLE for scale, and empirical proportions for weights) and a label update (MAP assignment using Monte Carlo-approximated component densities). The theoretical analysis derives bracketing entropy bounds of order Km·max{q,1}·log(1/ε) by combining the volume comparison theorem for manifold covering numbers with a Jacobi-field-based Lipschitz bound on the geodesic factor map, yielding a root-n convergence rate for the MLE in Hellinger distance that matches the classical Euclidean benchmark.

## Results

On the shape space CP³, MGFA achieves a mean Rand Index of 0.89 (std 0.06) and Adjusted Rand Index of 0.79 (std 0.12), substantially outperforming Ward clustering (RI 0.70, ARI 0.40) and Mixtures of Riemannian Gaussians (RI 0.59, ARI 0.18). On the sphere S², MGFA reaches RI 0.93 versus at most 0.77 for all competing methods. On hyperbolic space H², MGFA achieves RI 0.99 compared to ≤ 0.74 for Ward and MoRN. On the real ADNI left hippocampus dataset (n=320 LMCI subjects), MGFA with q=1 identifies four morphologically distinct subgroups, with G2 showing the largest shape deviation and the lowest MMSE and Hipp/ICV ratio.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MGFA consistently outperforms all four baselines across every manifold tested. On CP³ shape space, MGFA (RI 0.89) beats Ward clustering (RI 0.70) by 19 percentage points and MoRN (RI 0.59) by 30 points, with the ARI gap even wider (0.79 vs. 0.40 and 0.18). On S², MGFA (RI 0.93) exceeds the best alternative (movMF or skmeans, RI ≤ 0.77) by at least 16 points. On H², the advantage is most dramatic: MGFA (RI 0.99) surpasses both Ward and MoRN (RI ≤ 0.74) by over 25 points, recovering the latent factor structure that the isotropic baselines cannot capture. The key differentiator is that Ward and MoRN lack any mechanism for anisotropic, low-dimensional variation within each cluster, while movMF and skmeans are restricted to spherical data and also assume isotropic dispersion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends geodesic regression (Fletcher, 2013) by treating the regression coefficients as latent factors, creating a manifold analogue of the classical factor model, and then wraps this in a finite mixture to form MGFA. It generalizes the Euclidean mixtures-of-factor-analyzers framework (Ghahramani et al., 1996) to Riemannian homogeneous spaces, departing from prior manifold mixture models that are restricted to isotropic radial distributions (Riemannian Gaussian, von Mises-Fisher). The parameter update step draws on principal geodesic analysis (Fletcher et al., 2004), and the theoretical analysis departs from existing manifold convergence results (Petersen & Müller, 2019; Dubey & Müller, 2019, 2020) that rely on entropy conditions difficult to verify on general manifolds and do not address mixture models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first non-asymptotic convergence rate for mixture models on general Riemannian manifolds, filling a notable theoretical gap that also covers mixtures of Riemannian radial distributions as a special case. Practically, MGFA offers a geometry-aware alternative to isotropic radial mixtures for data with anisotropic subpopulation structure, demonstrated on spheres, shape spaces, and hyperbolic spaces with applications to Alzheimer's disease hippocampal shape analysis. Open questions include whether parameter-level (not just density-level) convergence rates can be established, how to perform principled model selection for K and q on manifolds, and whether the approach scales to high-dimensional manifold-valued data.
