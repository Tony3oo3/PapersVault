## Problem

Existing neural compression methods (HiFiC, CDC, DLF, DDCM, etc.) operate at fixed points on the rate-distortion-perception (RDP) surface, requiring separate trained models or adapters for each desired tradeoff. No prior approach enables full, flexible traversal of the ternary RDP tradeoff—simultaneously controlling rate, distortion, and perceptual quality—using a single pre-trained diffusion model without any additional training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264591/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The encoder takes a source image and a target diffusion time index t, progressively transmitting a Gaussian-perturbed observation Z_t via the Poisson Functional Representation (PFR) algorithm to produce a compressed bitstream. The decoder receives the bitstream, recovers Z_t, and simulates the score-scaled PF-ODE with a user-chosen parameter ρ ∈ [0,1] to produce the reconstructed image. For latent diffusion models (Stable Diffusion, Flux), all operations occur in the autoencoder's latent space, and the supported ρ range may differ from the theoretical [0,1] interval.

## Method

The framework combines a reverse channel coding (RCC) module using the PFR algorithm with a novel score-scaled probability flow ODE (PF-ODE) decoder, both operating on a pre-trained diffusion model without any training. The key innovation is the score-scaled PF-ODE, which modifies the standard PF-ODE by replacing the score coefficient 1/2 with (2−ρ)/2, creating a continuous interpolation between the MMSE estimator (ρ=0, minimum distortion) and the original PF-ODE (ρ=1, perfect perception). The rate parameter t selects the noise level at which the observation is transmitted, while ρ controls the distortion-perception balance at that rate, giving two independent knobs for navigating the full RDP surface. The paper proves that the score-scaled PF-ODE is optimal for the DP tradeoff under AWGN observations in the multivariate Gaussian case, and that the full RCC + score-scaled PF-ODE framework achieves the information-theoretic RDP function for scalar Gaussian sources.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, the proposed training-free framework achieves lower MSE distortion and superior perceptual quality (lower LPIPS and FID) compared to JPEG, BPG, and PSC at comparable bitrates, while providing full control over the rate-distortion-perception tradeoff via two parameters (t and ρ). On Kodak and DIV2K, the method traverses convex RDP curves that shift toward lower distortion and better perception as the rate increases, using a single pre-trained diffusion model (SD-2.1 or Flux) without any retraining. In terms of efficiency, the method requires 950M parameters (same as DDCM) with total latency of 2.31–9.47 s per image on Kodak, compared to 75.67 s for DDCM, though it is slower than one-step distillation methods like OneDC (0.74 s) and StableCodec (0.74 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PSC on CIFAR-10, the proposed method achieves lower distortion and better perceptual quality at comparable bitrates, while additionally supporting DP-axis control that PSC lacks (PSC only offers adaptive rate control). On Kodak/DIV2K, HiFiC and CDC occasionally outperform on a single metric (e.g., MSE or LPIPS) due to their targeted training losses, but they cannot dynamically navigate the RDP tradeoff and would require 50 separate trained models to cover 10 bitrates × 5 DP points. DDCM achieves lower LPIPS but at a much higher bitrate and with 8× the total latency (75.67 s vs. 2.31–9.47 s). OneDC and StableCodec are significantly faster (0.74 s total) but require retraining additional adapters for each RDP operating point, making their effective storage cost N×492M or N×109M parameters for N RDP points. The original DiffC is a strict special case of the proposed framework at ρ=1, meaning the new method generalizes it by adding the DP-axis control via the score-scaling parameter ρ without any additional training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DiffC framework (Theis et al., 2022; Vonderfecht & Liu, 2025), which transmits Gaussian-perturbed data via reverse channel coding (RCC/PFR) and reconstructs using a pre-trained diffusion model, by introducing a score-scaled probability flow ODE decoder. While DiffC and DDCM support progressive rate control, and methods like HiFiC, CDC, and StableCodec target specific RDP operating points through dedicated training, none enable full RDP traversal with a single model. The key conceptual departure is the (2−ρ) scaling on the score term in the PF-ODE, which the authors prove achieves the optimal distortion-perception tradeoff (building on Freirich et al., 2021) for Gaussian sources, thereby providing a second control axis (ρ) complementary to the rate axis (t) from the RCC module.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free nature is practically significant: a single pre-trained diffusion model (e.g., SD-2.1 or Flux) can cover arbitrary rate-distortion-perception operating points without retraining, eliminating the need to train and store dozens of specialized models for adaptive streaming. However, theoretical optimality is established only for scalar/multivariate Gaussian sources, and extending to general sources requires dimension-specific water-filling that is computationally prohibitive in practice. The latent-space operation (inherent to SD/Flux) lacks rigorous theoretical grounding, and encoding/decoding latency remains higher than lightweight codecs like HiFiC or one-step distillation methods, limiting real-time deployment.
