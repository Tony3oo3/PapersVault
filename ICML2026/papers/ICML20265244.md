## Problem

Existing parallel thinking methods for Large Reasoning Models generate multiple reasoning trajectories independently, producing highly redundant paths that fail to meaningfully explore the reasoning space, and apply trajectory selection only post-hoc after all sequences complete. This paper addresses both limitations by proposing a structured exploration mechanism and a principled, length-aware selection criterion that operates during decoding rather than after.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265244/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

DTS takes a text prompt and a pre-trained LRM as input and operates entirely at decoding time with no model modification or additional training. It produces a single reasoning trajectory (chain-of-thought) and a final answer, selected via early termination: either the first trajectory to emit an EOS token (DTS-Greedy) or a majority vote among B completed trajectories (DTS-Stable). The method is constrained by a maximum of 48 simultaneously active branches to prevent exponential tree growth, and uses fixed thresholds (τ_v=1.5, τ_h=2.5, K=3) for branching decisions.

## Method

DTS constructs a compact decoding tree by selectively branching at 'decision tokens' identified via high varentropy and low entropy in the next-token distribution, where the model commits strongly to a small set of semantically distinct continuations. At these tokens, the top-K candidate tokens spawn parallel branches; at all other steps, standard single-token autoregressive decoding is used, and all active branches expand simultaneously in a breadth-first manner. For trajectory selection, DTS exploits a proven length-accuracy anti-correlation—supported both empirically and theoretically through GRPO's 1/|ξ| length normalization, which creates an asymmetric bias favoring short correct trajectories—by terminating decoding as soon as trajectories complete and selecting the shortest (or majority-voted among the first B) completed path. This design ensures that repetitive or overly verbose trajectories are naturally overridden by concise, reliable completions, reducing repetition by 8% on average while improving accuracy by 14%.

## Results

DTS achieves an average accuracy improvement of 14% over standard inference across four LRMs and four reasoning datasets (AIME24, AIME25, GPQA-Diamond, LiveBench). DTS-Stable reaches 40.45% average accuracy on DS-Distill-Qwen-1.5B (+18.11 over standard), 58.45% on DS-Distill-Qwen-7B (+17.16), and 58.09% on Phi4-mini-reasoning-4B (+13.61). DTS-Greedy (single-trajectory variant) already surpasses multi-trajectory baselines like Self-Consistency and DeepConf in most settings, e.g., 54.67% vs. 40.67% on AIME24 with DS-Distill-Qwen-1.5B. DTS also reduces the repetition rate by 8% on average (e.g., from 17.45% to 2.67% on DS-Distill-Qwen-1.5B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DTS consistently outperforms all three baseline families across every model and dataset. Against Self-Consistency, DTS-Stable gains 10.56 points on average (e.g., 64.67% vs. 40.67% on AIME24 with DS-Distill-Qwen-1.5B; 78.67% vs. 69.33% with DS-Distill-Qwen-7B), and even the single-trajectory DTS-Greedy already beats Self-Consistency in most settings, showing that structured branching is more effective than independent sampling. Against DeepConf (both low and high thresholds), DTS-Stable leads by 8–15 points on average; notably, DeepConf-high sometimes degrades below standard inference (e.g., 43.94% vs. 44.48% on Phi4-mini-reasoning-4B), while DTS never underperforms the baseline. Against top-k and top-p stochastic sampling (Appendix E), DTS-Greedy achieves 54.67% vs. 43.33% (top-k) and 34.67% (top-p) on AIME24 with DS-Distill-Qwen-1.5B, confirming that entropy/varentropy-guided branching plus early termination is superior to unstructured stochastic decoding.

## Relation to Prior Work

DTS departs from the dominant parallel-thinking paradigm (Self-Consistency, Best-of-N, DeepConf) that generates independent, often redundant trajectories and applies selection only post hoc. Instead, it introduces a structured decoding tree that branches selectively at 'decision tokens' identified by a varentropy-and-entropy criterion, and couples this with length-aware early termination grounded in a theoretically motivated length-accuracy anti-correlation derived from GRPO's length-normalized objective. The varentropy-based branching signal and the explicit tree-sketching formulation are the primary conceptual departures from prior multi-trajectory decoding work, while the adaptive-thinking line (AutoL2S, DAST, L1) is positioned as a training-time alternative that DTS avoids entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As a training-free, plug-and-play decoding module with a vLLM implementation, DTS offers a low-friction path to improve LRM reasoning without retraining, and the consistent gains across four model families and four datasets (math, STEM, logic) suggest broad applicability. The demonstrated ability of a 1.5B model with DTS to match or exceed a 70B flagship under matched compute budgets is practically significant for deployment cost. Open questions include how the 48-branch cap and threshold parameters scale to longer or more open-ended reasoning tasks, whether the length-accuracy anti-correlation persists in domains where verbose reasoning is genuinely necessary, and whether the structured tree can be further pruned or guided by external verifiers.
