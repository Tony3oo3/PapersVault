## Problem

Existing 3D hierarchical semantic segmentation (3DHS) methods that learn multiple hierarchy labels with a shared model suffer from two unresolved issues: (1) multi-hierarchy optimization conflicts where shared parameters cause overfitting at some hierarchies and underfitting at others, and (2) severe class imbalance that compounds across hierarchies, causing the model to be dominated by majority classes (e.g., wall, floor) while degrading on minority classes (e.g., column, board).

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud X ∈ R^{N×3} as input and produces a set of H hierarchical semantic label predictions {Y^(h)}_{h=1}^H, where each Y^(h) ∈ R^{N×K^(h)} gives per-point class probabilities for the h-th hierarchy with K^(h) classes. The hierarchies follow a parent-child structure (e.g., 'Furniture' → 'table, chair, sofa'), and the mapping between adjacent hierarchies is encoded in a fixed binary matrix A^(h,h-1). Experiments use 2-3 hierarchy configurations on datasets like Campus3D (3 levels), S3DIS-H (2 levels), and SensatUrban-H (2 levels).

## Method

ML3DHS introduces a late-decoupled architecture where a shared point cloud encoder feeds into H individual decoders (one per hierarchy), theoretically proven (Theorem 1) to achieve per-hierarchy risk minima that dominate the shared-decoder solution. A coarse-to-fine guidance module concatenates the semantic prediction of hierarchy h-1 (projected via MLP) with the intermediate features of hierarchy h before classification, and a cross-hierarchy consistency loss enforces parent-child label coherence via the mapping matrix. In parallel, an auxiliary contrastive discrimination branch extracts class-wise features, applies supervised contrastive loss to learn discriminative representations, and computes semantic cluster prototypes (EMA-updated class means) that mutually supervise both branches through a bi-directional Smooth L1 loss, selectively activated per hierarchy via a Gini coefficient threshold to target only severely imbalanced levels.

## Results

On the three main benchmarks, ML3DHS achieves the best Avg. mIoU across all backbone–dataset combinations. With PointNet++, it reaches 63.28% on Campus3D, 66.43% on S3DIS-H, and 49.73% on SensatUrban-H; with Point Transformer v2, it reaches 66.87%, 76.71%, and 53.56% respectively. The largest single-dataset gain is +3.38% Avg. mIoU over DHL on S3DIS-H (PointNet++), and the method also improves per-hierarchy mIoU at the finest level (e.g., L2 on Campus3D: 44.61% vs. 41.12% for DHL). Add-on experiments confirm that integrating ML3DHS into MTHS or DHL yields consistent +0.5% to +2.9% Avg. mIoU gains across all three datasets and backbones.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two 3DHS baselines: MTHS (Li et al., 2020), which stacks parallel classifiers on a shared encoder-decoder with a multi-task loss, and DHL (Li et al., 2025), which adds top-bottom and bottom-top fusion with a cross-hierarchy consistency loss. ML3DHS outperforms both baselines on every dataset–backbone combination tested. Against DHL, the gains range from +0.72% (Campus3D, PointNet++) to +3.38% (S3DIS-H, PointNet++) in Avg. mIoU, with the largest per-hierarchy improvement at the finest level (e.g., +3.49% on Campus3D L2 with PointNet++). Against MTHS, the advantages are even larger, reaching +4.68% on S3DIS-H (PointNet++) and +4.29% on SensatUrban-H (Point TF v2). Add-on experiments (Tables 2–4) further show that grafting ML3DHS's late-decoupled architecture and contrastive self-supervision onto MTHS or DHL yields consistent +0.5% to +2.9% Avg. mIoU improvements, confirming the method's plug-and-play utility.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions 3D hierarchical semantic segmentation (3DHS) as a multi-label learning problem, departing from prior 3DHS methods (MTHS, DHL) that use shared decoders across all hierarchies. It draws on multi-task learning theory (Sener & Koltun, 2018; Chen et al., 2018) to argue that shared parameters cause overfitting/underfitting conflicts, and on supervised contrastive learning (Khosla et al., 2020) to construct a class-wise discriminative auxiliary branch. The late-decoupled decoder architecture with coarse-to-fine guidance is a novel architectural contribution, while the cross-hierarchy consistency loss builds on the parent-child label mapping idea from DHL (Li et al., 2025).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The late-decoupled framework and contrastive cluster self-supervision offer a plug-and-play improvement for existing 3DHS pipelines, as demonstrated by consistent gains when integrated into MTHS and DHL across three backbones and four datasets. The Gini-coefficient-based activation strategy provides a practical mechanism to selectively address severe class imbalance without incurring unnecessary overhead. However, the non-shared decoders and auxiliary branch increase model size, and the paper acknowledges that lightweight and open-vocabulary extensions remain open. The modest but consistent improvements (+0.5% to +3.4% Avg.mIoU) suggest the multi-hierarchy conflict is a real but bounded bottleneck in current 3DHS methods.
