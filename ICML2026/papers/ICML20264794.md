## Problem

Adaptive reasoning in LLMs converts the token-budget problem into a threshold-setting problem, but thresholds are uninterpretable, signal-dependent, and lack finite-sample guarantees. Moreover, existing upper-threshold-only stopping rules waste tokens on unsolvable instances that never reach the confidence cutoff, running to the full budget. This paper reframes budget setting as distribution-free risk control and introduces a novel parametric lower threshold that preemptively halts hopeless reasoning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a question prompt, a growing partial reasoning trajectory (chunked at \n\n boundaries), and a scalar uncertainty signal computed per chunk (confidence via length-normalized log-likelihood rollout, EAT entropy, or a trained MLP probe on hidden states). It outputs a binary stopping decision at each chunk, a final boxed answer upon stopping, and—during calibration—two threshold parameters: a static upper threshold λ+ and a parametric lower threshold c (encoding slope, shift, and bounds of a sigmoid schedule over token usage). The calibration interface requires only a user-specified risk tolerance ε and a held-out validation set.

## Method

Conformal Thinking defines a dual-threshold early-exit policy: reasoning stops when the uncertainty signal crosses an upper threshold λ+ (model is confident, risking a false positive) or falls below a dynamic lower threshold λ−(t; c, s, l, u) that is a sigmoid function of cumulative token usage, enforcing a schedule by which confidence must increase to justify continued reasoning (risking a false negative). Four loss functions capture correctness (false-positive and false-negative error) and efficiency (normalized regret past the oracle stopping time, and fraction of budget spent on incorrect intermediate answers). Thresholds are selected via a two-step UCB-based conformal risk control procedure: first calibrate λ+ to control false-positive risk at level ε+, then conditionally calibrate the lower-threshold parameters to control false-negative risk at level ε−, with finite-sample corrections preventing validation overfitting. Among all signal-threshold pairs satisfying the risk budget, the method selects the one minimizing the efficiency loss, enabling automatic ensembling across heterogeneous uncertainty signals.

## Results

The paper's primary quantitative finding is that UCB-based risk control keeps realized test risk below the user-specified target ε with high probability, whereas naive cross-validation frequently exceeds the target (Fig. 4). On efficiency, the dual-threshold (LOWER+UPPER) mechanism yields a clear leftward shift in the accuracy-vs-tokens curve compared to UPPER-ONLY when unsolvable instances are prevalent (1:1 and 1:3 solvable:unsolvable ratios), meaning similar accuracy is achieved with substantially fewer tokens (Fig. 6). Signal ensembling via the risk-control framework consistently selects the most efficient signal (e.g., a trained probe on Qwen3-8B/AIME) across all risk levels, outperforming any single fixed signal (Fig. 5). Under distribution shift (AIME→GPQA) and length shift, UCB continues to bound test risk while Naive shows severe excess risk.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external named baselines but instead ablates its own design choices. (1) Naive cross-validation vs. UCB: Naive frequently violates the target risk ε (standard deviation bands cross the y=x line in Fig. 4), while UCB consistently keeps test risk below ε across 40 random splits; this gap widens as validation set size decreases (8 vs. 40 samples, Fig. 7). (2) UPPER-ONLY vs. LOWER+UPPER: When solvable instances dominate (3:1 ratio), UPPER-ONLY captures most savings and the lower threshold adds little; however, at 1:1 and 1:3 ratios, UPPER-ONLY clusters near the high-token regime because many runs never reach the confidence cutoff, while LOWER+UPPER achieves comparable accuracy with fewer tokens (Fig. 6). (3) Single signal vs. ensemble: The risk-control framework's signal selection (Algorithm 1, line 11) picks the most efficient signal-threshold pair per ε, and this validation-set efficiency transfers to the test set, outperforming any single fixed signal across all four models evaluated (Fig. 5). (4) A uniform fixed-token-budget baseline is included in Fig. 6 and is dominated by all adaptive methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adaptive early-stopping literature for reasoning LLMs (Wang et al. 2025a; Yang et al. 2025), which relies on a single upper confidence threshold with hand-tuned values, by introducing a parametric lower threshold that enforces a confidence-increase schedule and halts hopeless instances. The calibration mechanism is borrowed from distribution-free risk control for early-exit neural networks (Jazbec et al. 2024; Schuster et al. 2022) and conformal risk control (Bates et al. 2021; Angelopoulos et al. 2024), adapted here to the LLM reasoning setting. The closest prior work, Thought Calibration (Wu et al. 2025), also applies risk control to early stopping but uses a consistency-based probe signal and a single threshold, whereas Conformal Thinking targets generic uncertainty signals and explicitly separates solvable from unsolvable instances via the dual-threshold design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework shifts the practitioner's burden from tuning opaque, signal-dependent thresholds to specifying an interpretable risk tolerance, with finite-sample guarantees that protect against validation overfitting—a practical concern given that calibration sets for reasoning tasks are often small. The lower threshold addresses a real and previously under-addressed inefficiency: wasting the full token budget on instances the model cannot solve, with gains that scale with the fraction of unsolvable problems in the deployment distribution. Key limitations include the monotonicity assumption on risk, the two-step decoupled calibration that sacrifices some theoretical rigor, and evaluation restricted to math/science reasoning where uncertainty signals are well-defined; extending to open-ended or agentic reasoning remains open.
