## Problem

Representation alignment (REPA) in diffusion Transformers for waveform generation implicitly entangles latent representations, causing a capacity mismatch that degrades generative quality—particularly high-frequency synthesis—in extremely low-bitrate settings. The paper identifies that regularizing intermediate DiT features toward fixed semantic targets biases the model toward overly smoothed reconstructions and limits its ability to allocate capacity to both semantics and acoustic detail simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262311/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes highly compressed audio latents (FSQ at 25 Hz / 400 bps for the codec, or a 32-dim VAE latent at 12.5 Hz) along with masked multi-prompts spanning three representation spaces (SSL features from MMS, Mel-spectrograms, and the waveform itself), and optionally text tokens for TTS. It produces a 24 kHz waveform via a single-stage Wave-DiT decoder operating at 50 Hz, with the LDM variant (ReGenVoice) performing latent diffusion at 6.25 Hz before decoding. The masked-infilling strategy means prompts are partially dropped during training (0.3 prompt drop, 0.2 condition drop), enabling both reconstruction and zero-shot cross-sentence style transfer from short (≤1.2 s) prompts to generate over 30 s of speech.

## Method

ReGen replaces REPA's fixed-target regularization with joint vector-field estimation across three representation spaces (SSL, Mel, waveform) within a single hierarchical DiT, where higher-level representations progressively constrain lower-level generation via U-Net-like skip connections. A masked multi-prompt mechanism conditions each level's flow-matching objective on partially masked targets from all three spaces, so the model learns to generate representations and data simultaneously rather than treating them as fixed conditioning. Generalized Flow Matching (GFM) augments the standard CFM loss with an asymmetric repulsive term in velocity space—using a stop-gradient reference trajectory—to prevent distinct stochastic paths from collapsing into a single conditional mean, preserving high-frequency energy. The architecture uses a linear-reshape transformation to operate diffusion at 50 Hz, AdaLN-SOLA for parameter-efficient time conditioning, and is trained with CFM pre-training followed by 4-step adversarial post-training with multi-period, multi-scale STFT, and sub-band CQT discriminators.

## Results

On the Seed-en prompted reconstruction benchmark, ReGenVAE (single-stage, 12.5 Hz, 32-dim latent) achieves WER 2.03 and SPK-SIM 0.72, outperforming the best two-stage baseline TaDiCodec (WER 2.57, SPK-SIM 0.69) and all three-stage systems (e.g., CosyVoice 2: WER 4.10, SPK-SIM 0.68). For zero-shot TTS on Seed-en, ReGenVoice (0.5B params) trained on only 0.5k hours achieves WER 1.46 and SIM 0.64, surpassing models trained on 100k+ hours (e.g., F5-TTS: WER 2.00, SIM 0.67; CosyVoice2: WER 2.57, SIM 0.65). ReGenVoice also achieves an RTF of 0.08 with 25 NFE, compared to 1.05 for CosyVoice2, while matching GT-level MOS (4.029 vs. 3.978).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReGenVAE (single-stage, 12.5 Hz) outperforms all baselines on SPK-SIM in prompted reconstruction: 0.72–0.73 vs. TaDiCodec's 0.69 (two-stage), CosyVoice 2's 0.68 (three-stage), and X-codec2's 0.62 (single-stage). On WER, ReGenVAE achieves 2.03–2.09, beating TaDiCodec (2.57–2.73) and all GAN-based codecs (EnCodec 5.36, DAC 20.08, WavTokenizer 6.65). In zero-shot TTS (Table 6), ReGenVoice with only 0.5k hours of training data achieves WER 1.46, lower than F5-TTS (2.00, 100k hrs), CosyVoice2 (2.57, 166k hrs), and SparkTTS (1.98, 100k hrs), while matching GT-level SIM (0.64 vs. GT 0.73). ReGenTokenizer at 25 Hz/400 bps achieves SPK-SIM 0.61–0.71, surpassing all single-stage GAN codecs (EnCodec 0.48, DAC 0.39–0.45, WavTokenizer 0.48) and matching or exceeding two-stage TaDiCodec (0.69). The key structural advantage is that ReGen operates as a single-stage Wave-CFM decoder, whereas competing prompted-generation systems (CosyVoice, FireRedTTS, TaDiCodec) require separate speaker encoders, Mel-spectrogram decoders, and neural vocoders across 2–3 stages.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the REPA (representation alignment) paradigm introduced in StreamFlow, which regularizes intermediate DiT representations toward SSL features to accelerate training. The authors argue that REPA implicitly entangles latents and causes a capacity mismatch that limits generative ability, particularly at low bitrates. Instead, ReGen jointly estimates vector fields for both representations (SSL, Mel) and data (waveform) within a single hierarchical DiT, using a masked-infilling strategy borrowed from Voicebox. The generalized flow matching (GFM) objective reformulates the repulsive regularization of Gritsenko et al. (2020) from sample space into velocity space, aligning it with the CFM optimization domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that REPA can degrade generative capacity challenges a widely adopted training recipe for diffusion Transformers in audio, and the ReGen framework offers a principled alternative that disentangles semantic and acoustic generation. Practically, the single-stage architecture (no separate vocoder or Mel decoder) combined with 6.25 Hz latent diffusion yields an RTF of 0.08 and 1-day training on 4 GPUs, making high-quality waveform generation substantially cheaper to deploy. Open questions include whether adversarial post-training can be eliminated entirely, whether the hierarchical multi-prompting can enable one-step generation, and whether the approach generalizes beyond speech to general audio.
