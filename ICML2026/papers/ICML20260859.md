## Problem

Current multimodal LLMs process audio as a mono stream, rendering them 'audio-blind' to spatial cues, while existing spatial audio models are locked to fixed microphone geometries and cannot transfer across devices. No prior work has demonstrated a microphone-geometry-agnostic spatial audio encoder that produces embeddings suitable for complex LLM reasoning tasks such as spatial question answering and targeted transcription.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260859/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

PhaseCoder consumes 250 ms segments of raw 16 kHz multichannel audio (3–8 omnidirectional MEMS channels) together with the Cartesian coordinates of each microphone. It produces a single 256-dimensional spatial audio token (independent of channel count) via a [CLS] token, plus three classification heads for azimuth (38 bins, ~10°), elevation (18 bins), and distance (13 bins, 0.5 m). When integrated with Gemma 3n, the token is projected to 2048 dimensions and prepended as a sequence of ~6.25 spatial tokens per second alongside the model's existing 188 mono audio tokens, enabling the LLM to output spatial reasoning answers and directionally-targeted transcriptions.

## Method

PhaseCoder is a compact (~6M parameter) transformer-only encoder that flattens per-microphone STFT patches (258-dim magnitude+phase features projected to 256-dim) into a single sequence and injects three fixed positional embeddings: a sequential sinusoidal code, a shared frame index code, and a microphone-specific code derived from spherical coordinates via phase modulation (adapted from GI-DOAEnet). The model is trained end-to-end with a weighted cross-entropy loss over discretized azimuth, elevation, and distance bins on 4 million synthetic 250 ms snippets generated by convolving LibriSpeech with 1.5 million simulated room impulse responses across random 3–8 microphone geometries, using a two-stage curriculum (clean speech, then noisy). For LLM integration, a two-layer MLP projects the 256-dim spatial token into Gemma 3n's 2048-dim embedding space, and the model is fine-tuned with LoRA (r=8) under a 5-stage curriculum that progressively introduces four tasks of increasing complexity—from source localization to spatially-cued targeted transcription—while retaining simpler tasks to prevent catastrophic forgetting.

## Results

On the LOCATA benchmark (12-mic pseudo-spherical array, reverberant far-field), PhaseCoder-6M achieves 7.44° azimuth MAE and 86.96% Acc@10, outperforming the SOTA GI-DOAEnet (7.82° MAE, 82.48% Acc@10). On RSL2019, GI-DOAEnet retains a slight edge on the test set (9.17° vs. 11.63° MAE), which the authors attribute to PhaseCoder's 10° classification resolution versus GI-DOAEnet's 1° azimuth-only resolution. For the LLM spatial understanding tasks, fine-tuned Gemma 3n with PhaseCoder spatial tokens reaches 76.76% accuracy on spatial reasoning (Task 2) on synthetic data and 73.83% on RSL2019, compared to ~48–54% for the unmodified Gemma and Gemini baselines. PhaseCoder also demonstrates superior computational efficiency, using 405 MB peak memory and 2.24 ms GPU inference for 2-channel input versus 702 MB and 12.54 ms for GI-DOAEnet.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GI-DOAEnet (Baek et al., 2025), the SOTA microphone-invariant localization model, PhaseCoder-6M wins on the challenging LOCATA dataset, achieving 86.96% Acc@10 vs. 82.48% and 7.44° vs. 7.82° MAE, which the authors attribute to more diverse noise augmentation during training. However, GI-DOAEnet outperforms PhaseCoder on RSL2019, particularly the test set (9.17° vs. 11.63° MAE), due to its finer 1° azimuth resolution versus PhaseCoder's 10° classification bins. In the compute comparison (Table 2), PhaseCoder uses roughly half the peak memory of GI-DOAEnet (405 MB vs. 702 MB for 2 mics) and runs 5.6× faster on GPU (2.24 ms vs. 12.54 ms), despite a higher theoretical FLOP count, thanks to its purely parallelizable transformer architecture. Compared to BAT's Spatial-AST encoder (Zheng et al., 2024), PhaseCoder uses 405 MB vs. 808 MB memory and 2.24 ms vs. 2.96 ms inference for 2-channel input, while also being geometry-agnostic rather than restricted to binaural audio. For the LLM spatial reasoning tasks, the fine-tuned Gemma with PhaseCoder tokens substantially outperforms both the zero-shot Gemma (57.61% vs. 76.76% on Task 2 synthetic) and Gemini 3 Flash (47.85%), demonstrating that the spatial tokens enable capabilities no existing baseline provides.

## Relation to Prior Work

PhaseCoder sits at the intersection of two previously disconnected research threads: microphone-geometry-agnostic sound localization (Neural-SRP, GI-DOAEnet) and LLM-based spatial audio understanding (BAT/Spatial-AST, Tang et al.). It adopts the phase-modulation positional embeddings from GI-DOAEnet but replaces the hybrid CNN/MLP backbone with a pure transformer encoder, and for the first time feeds the resulting spatial tokens into a multimodal LLM (Gemma 3n) alongside its native mono audio tokens. Prior LLM spatial-audio work was locked to fixed geometries (binaural in BAT, ego-centric beamformers in M-BEST-RQ), while prior geometry-agnostic localizers produced only scalar DOA outputs with no downstream language-model integration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that most consumer devices already carry 3–8 MEMS microphones yet cannot exploit them for spatial reasoning; PhaseCoder provides a single, geometry-agnostic encoder that plugs into an existing LLM without hardware-specific retraining. Key limitations remain: the model handles only a single dominant speech source (no multi-event SELD), ignores device-specific acoustic baffling, and distance estimation is fundamentally underdetermined without visual or room-acoustic priors. Open questions include whether the approach scales to overlapping multi-source scenes, whether visual depth fusion can resolve distance ambiguity, and whether the 6M-parameter encoder is sufficient for production-quality robustness across diverse real-world rooms.
