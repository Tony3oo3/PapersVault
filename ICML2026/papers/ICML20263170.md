## Problem

Deploying flow-based generative models in safety-critical physical systems (robot planning, locomotion control) requires strict satisfaction of geometric and dynamic constraints, yet standard flow matching produces unsafe trajectories due to fitting error and numerical integration error. Existing constrained generation methods rely on post-hoc corrections (projection, CBF-based QP solving, reflection) that are computationally expensive, can distort the learned distribution, and still fail to guarantee zero constraint violation under reduced sampling budgets.

## Input / Output

The method takes an initial state sampled uniformly from the Chebyshev ball of the safety polytope (ensuring supp(p₀) ⊆ X_c), a set of linear inequality constraints Ax ≤ b (which may be time-varying, e.g., state-dependent friction cones for quadruped locomotion), and optionally a goal position for planning tasks. It outputs a complete trajectory of T discrete steps (typically T=10) where every intermediate state strictly satisfies all polyhedral constraints by construction. For control tasks, the output is a state-action sequence over a receding horizon, with constraints enforced on predicted states or directly on actions depending on the task.

## Method

PolyFlow reformulates flow matching from continuous-time ODEs to a discrete-time difference equation (x_{t+1} = x_t + u_t(x_t)), which structurally eliminates numerical integration error as a source of constraint violation. The core architectural contribution is a projection-free parameterization inspired by Frank-Wolfe: the update is u_θ(x_t, t) = γ_θ(x_t,t) · (RS(x_t, d_θ(x_t,t)) − x_t), where d_θ is a neural network predicting an unconstrained direction, RS is a differentiable ray-shooting operator that computes the intersection of that ray with the polytope boundary ∂X_c, and γ_θ ∈ [0,1] is a learned sigmoid gating factor controlling step magnitude. This guarantees x_{t+1} ∈ X_c by convexity without any iterative solver. The constraint set is encoded by a permutation-invariant Transformer (or MLP) block without positional encoding, fused into a DiT backbone via cross-attention. Training uses the standard CFM regression objective with linear interpolation paths, and Theorem 4.5 proves that interior safety of conditional flows (guaranteed by convexity of X_c) implies interior safety of the marginal flow, providing a rigorous end-to-end safety guarantee.

## Results

PolyFlow achieves 100% safety rate (zero constraint violation) across all evaluated tasks—2D Maze, Hopper, Walker2d, HalfCheetah, and Unitree Go2 quadruped locomotion—while maintaining competitive or superior distributional fidelity. In the 2D Maze task with equal N=10 sampling steps, PolyFlow-mlp attains MMD 2.62×10⁻⁶, W₂ 0.033, and KL 0.050, outperforming even the unconstrained Flow baseline (MMD 3.50×10⁻⁵, W₂ 0.054, KL 0.266) while completing inference in 0.58 s (vs. 7.56 s for SafeFlow). On Gym locomotion tasks, PolyFlow achieves the highest rollout returns (e.g., 3187±753 on Hopper-Simple vs. 2450±878 for Flow) with the lowest inference latency (0.075–0.089 s per trajectory vs. 0.35–0.70 s for Flow and up to 15.1 s for SafeFlow).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the unconstrained Flow baseline, PolyFlow achieves perfect safety (1.0 vs. 0.845 in Maze, 0.005–0.755 in Gym) and superior distributional fidelity (lower MMD, W₂, KL in Maze), while incurring only modest additional inference cost in Maze (0.58 s vs. 0.32 s) but being dramatically faster in Gym tasks (0.075–0.089 s vs. 0.35–0.70 s). Against SafeFlow (CBF-based), PolyFlow dominates on all axes: safety (1.0 vs. 0.530 in Maze at N=10), distributional quality (MMD 2.62×10⁻⁶ vs. 6.55×10⁻³, KL 0.050 vs. 3.140), and inference speed (0.58 s vs. 7.56 s in Maze; 0.075 s vs. 0.83–15.1 s in Gym). Against SafeDiffuser variants (RoSD/ReSD/TVSD), PolyFlow matches or exceeds safety (1.0 vs. 0.990/0.145/0.0) while achieving substantially better distributional fidelity (e.g., KL 0.050 vs. 50.2 for RoSD) and lower inference time. Against FlowTrunc (projection-based), PolyFlow achieves 100% safety versus 0% for FlowTrunc, with orders-of-magnitude better distributional metrics. Against GaugeFlow, PolyFlow is faster (0.075 s vs. 0.867 s on Hopper-Simple) and achieves comparable or better Dismatching Scores in 4 of 5 Gym tasks, while also handling dynamic time-varying constraints that GaugeFlow cannot address.

## Relation to Prior Work

PolyFlow builds directly on Conditional Flow Matching (Lipman et al., 2023) and departs from the two dominant paradigms for constrained generation: post-hoc correction (projection-based methods, CBF-based methods like SafeFlow and SafeDiffuser) and intrinsic embedding (reflection, Riemannian flow, gauge/mirror maps). Its key conceptual departure is a projection-free architecture inspired by the Frank-Wolfe algorithm, where the update direction is learned freely and then mapped onto the polytope boundary via a differentiable ray-shooting operator, combined with a learned gating factor. The discrete-time reformulation is a theoretical contribution that structurally eliminates the numerical integration error source of constraint violation, which continuous-time ODE-based methods must handle through external safety filters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For safety-critical robotics (locomotion, manipulation, autonomous driving), this work demonstrates that strict constraint satisfaction and high distributional fidelity need not be in tension, and that the computational cost of safety enforcement can be reduced by orders of magnitude compared to CBF-based or projection-based approaches. The finding that constrained generation can actually improve task return over unconstrained baselines (by steering toward safer data modes) is a useful conceptual insight for practitioners. Key limitations remain: the framework is restricted to convex polytopes, requires the initial distribution to lie within the feasible region, and the non-convex case (e.g., maze navigation) requires a local convex decomposition heuristic that may not generalize cleanly to highly complex geometries.
