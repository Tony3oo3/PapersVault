## Problem

Existing time series reasoning benchmarks either underrepresent health signals, rely on synthetic data that misses real clinical noise, or are confined to a single domain and modality (e.g., ECG only). This leaves it unclear whether LLMs perform genuine multi-step reasoning over physiological time series or merely exploit surface patterns and domain priors. HEARTS addresses this by providing the first unified, health-focused benchmark spanning diverse real-world datasets, temporal scales, and hierarchical reasoning levels.

## Input / Output

Each task provides a health time series signal (ranging from 60 to over 1M steps at frequencies from daily aggregates to 48 kHz) stored as a data file, along with a natural-language task instruction. The LLM accesses the signal via the CodeAct framework—writing Python code to read and analyze the file—rather than ingesting the raw sequence in its prompt. Outputs are task-specific: exact numerical values (statistics, bandpower, AHI), classification labels (sleep stage, disease type), full generated signal arrays (imputation, forecasting, cross-modal translation), or temporal ordering judgments, scored with Accuracy, IoU, or normalized sMAPE.

## Method

HEARTS is a benchmark and evaluation protocol rather than a new model. It curates 16 real-world datasets across 12 health domains and 20 signal modalities, defining 110 tasks organized into four hierarchical reasoning categories—Perception (statistical calculation, feature extraction), Inference (event localization, physiological classification, subject profiling), Generation (future forecasting, signal imputation, cross-modal translation), and Deduction (temporal ordering, trajectory analysis). Task construction follows three design principles (evidence-grounded significance, context-grounded label validity, data-grounded multi-step reasoning) and formalizes the reasoning process as an MDP trajectory to enforce multi-step solutions. Evaluation standardizes on the CodeAct framework with a restricted Python environment (e.g., neurokit2) to isolate intrinsic reasoning from tool-calling ability, and 16 SOTA LLMs spanning 11 families are assessed on 20,226 test cases. The benchmark is designed as a living ecosystem with standardized APIs for community submission of new datasets, tasks, and agent architectures.

## Results

Across 16 SOTA LLMs evaluated on 20,226 test samples spanning 110 tasks, the best overall score is 0.69 (Gemini 3.1 Pro), with GLM 5 at 0.68 and Grok 4.1 Fast / Kimi K2 Thinking at 0.65. The naive baseline achieves 0.61, meaning even the top LLMs gain only ~8 points over trivial heuristics. On the 32-task Inference/Deduction subset where SOTA ML baselines are available, specialized models reach 0.74–0.86, while the best LLM (best-case across 16 models) remains substantially lower, confirming a large gap between general-purpose LLM agents and domain-specific time-series models. Performance is only weakly correlated with general intelligence index scores, and degrades significantly with longer input sequences and higher sampling frequencies.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the 32-task Inference/Deduction subset where published SOTA ML baselines are available, specialized time-series models achieve 0.74–0.86, while even the best LLM (best-case across all 16 models) remains substantially lower, indicating a clear and non-overlapping performance gap. OpenTSLM, a family of time-series language models designed specifically for temporal reasoning, underperforms relative to CodeAct-enabled general-purpose LLMs on three representative HEARTS tasks (Appendix C.6). The domain-specialist agent Biomni, tested with both GLM 4.7 and Llama 4 Maverick as backbones, fails to yield statistically significant improvements (Paired T-test) over the standard CodeAct agent, because its retrieved action space lacks modality-specific computational primitives for health time-series analysis. Overall, no LLM configuration tested in this paper surpasses task-specific SOTA ML methods, and the performance gap is most pronounced on Inference tasks requiring fine-grained physiological discrimination (e.g., raw EMG interpretation, sleep staging) and Deduction tasks requiring long-horizon temporal causality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of LLM-based time series reasoning and health signal analysis. Prior time series reasoning benchmarks (TimeSeriesExam, BEDTime, TSR-SUITE, Time-MQA) either use synthetic data, target general domains, or cover only a single health modality (e.g., ECG-QA, TSAIA). HEARTS departs from this by integrating 16 real-world datasets across 12 health domains and 20 signal modalities under a unified four-level hierarchical taxonomy (Perception, Inference, Generation, Deduction), and by evaluating LLMs through the CodeAct code-generation framework rather than pure in-context prompting. It also positions itself against specialized time-series ML models and domain-specific agents (OpenTSLM, Biomni) to quantify the gap between general-purpose LLM reasoning and task-specific methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HEARTS provides the first comprehensive, extensible testbed for measuring whether LLMs can perform genuine multi-step reasoning over diverse physiological signals, and its findings—that SOTA LLMs substantially lag specialized models, that general reasoning scores poorly predict health time series performance, and that scaling within model families yields limited gains—challenge the assumption that broader LLM capability will naturally transfer to clinical signal analysis. Key open questions include how to incorporate verified domain priors (clinical rules, physiological constraints) into agent reasoning, how to evaluate intermediate reasoning steps rather than only final answers, and whether new architectures or training paradigms can close the gap on long-horizon, multi-channel temporal reasoning. The living-ecosystem design and standardized APIs lower the barrier for community expansion, making this a likely reference benchmark for health AI evaluation.
