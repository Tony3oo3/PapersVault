## Problem

Graph Contrastive Learning (GCL) for heterophilic graphs suffers from two critical issues: (1) unconstrained data augmentation induces large distribution shifts in high-frequency spectral signals, causing semantic collapse, and (2) high-pass filters used to extract local dissimilarity features are inherently fragile to noise and perturbations. Existing frequency-aware GCL methods either ignore spectral consistency during augmentation or directly use raw high-pass features without a mechanism to distinguish informative signals from noise.

## Input / Output

The method takes an undirected graph G=(V,E) with node feature matrix X ∈ R^{n×d} and adjacency matrix A, along with an augmentation ratio r_da ∈ (0,1) controlling the fraction of edges to modify. It produces a fused node representation Z ∈ R^{n×2d_h} (concatenation of low-pass and confidence-weighted high-pass features) for node-level tasks, and a graph-level embedding via a READOUT aggregation for graph classification. The encoder parameters (W_lpf, W_hpf) are shared between the original and augmented graph views to enforce semantic consistency in a common feature space.

## Method

SFCLTA comprises two coupled modules. The Topology-Adaptive Graph Augmentation (TAGA) module adaptively rewires edges by selecting high-degree nodes and computing pairwise edge weights via an MLP with sigmoid activation, guided by a loss that maximizes heterophily difference while penalizing spectral distortion through a Dirichlet energy term tr((A−A′)ᵀLA−A′)), which provably bounds the KL-divergence of high-frequency energy distributions (Theorem 1). The Spectral Fusion Contrastive Learning (SFCL) module extracts low-pass features (via adjacency-based aggregation) and high-pass features (via Laplacian-based filtering) from both views, applies an entropy-based confidence weighting (Hadamard product) to suppress noisy high-frequency components, concatenates the weighted high-pass with low-pass features, and optimizes an InfoNCE contrastive loss with k-hop local negative sampling. A multi-layer GCN feature reconstruction pretext task explicitly aligns the learned representations with original node features in both spatial and spectral domains (Theorem 3), and the confidence weighting provably suppresses gradient contributions from noisy nodes (Theorem 2).

## Results

On the primary node classification task across 8 datasets spanning homophily values from 0.0007 (PPI) to 0.8272 (Amazon-Photo), SFCLTA achieves the best or near-best accuracy on all datasets, with headline results of 84.1% on Cora, 86.1% on Amazon-Photo, 54.8% on PPI, and 85.3% on Roman-Empire. On the secondary graph classification task (4 datasets), SFCLTA attains 78.5% on PROTEINS, 80.1% on NCI1, 74.9% on IMDB-B, and 52.4% on IMDB-M, outperforming all baselines. The largest gains over the strongest frequency-aware GCL baselines (PolyGCL, HLCL) appear on highly heterophilic graphs such as Amazon-Ratings (+1.6% over HLCL) and Roman-Empire (+0.8% over HLCL).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SFCLTA outperforms all 10 baselines on 7 of 8 node classification datasets, with the most notable margins on heterophilic graphs: +1.6% over HLCL on Amazon-Ratings (54.3 vs 52.7), +0.8% over HLCL on Roman-Empire (85.3 vs 84.5), and +2.6% over H2GCN on Roman-Empire (85.3 vs 60.7). The sole exception is Squirrel, where PolyGCL achieves 47.5% versus SFCLTA's 46.7%, though SFCLTA still surpasses HLCL (44.9%) and H2GCN (46.5%). On homophilic datasets (Cora, CiteSeer, Amazon-Computers, Amazon-Photo), SFCLTA leads by 0.8–2.6% over the strongest GCL baselines (PolyGCL/HLCL), confirming that the spectral fusion mechanism does not sacrifice performance where homophily already favors standard GNNs. On the graph classification task, SFCLTA wins on all 4 datasets, beating the closest competitor HLCL by 0.5–0.9% (e.g., 78.5 vs 77.9 on PROTEINS, 80.1 vs 79.2 on NCI1). Traditional GNNs (GCN, VGAE) and heterophily-specific models (H2GCN) are consistently outperformed by 5–25% depending on the dataset's heterophily level, with the gap widening as homophily decreases.

## Relation to Prior Work

SFCLTA extends the graph contrastive learning paradigm established by DGI and the spectral graph signal processing framework of Sandryhaila & Moura, which underpins the LPF/HPF encoder design. It departs from recent multi-frequency GCL methods (PolyGCL, HLCL) that perform separate contrastive losses on distinct frequency views, instead fusing low- and high-frequency signals before contrastive learning, drawing an analogy to spectral fusion in hyperspectral imaging. The topology-adaptive augmentation module builds on the Dirichlet energy principle (Zhou et al., 2021) to constrain spectral distortion, and the theoretical analysis leverages the Davis-Kahan theorem to formalize why high-frequency eigenvectors are disproportionately sensitive to topological perturbations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides formal upper bounds (Theorems 1–3) on high-frequency distribution shift and noise-induced gradient deviation, offering a principled justification for why naive augmentation corrupts heterophilic graph representations. However, the practical gains over strong baselines like PolyGCL and HLCL are modest (typically 1–2 percentage points), and the method's sensitivity to the augmentation ratio (performance degrades sharply above r_da = 0.2) limits its ease of use. Open questions include whether the spectral fusion strategy generalizes to dynamic or multi-view graphs (acknowledged as future work), and whether the confidence-weighting mechanism can be made more computationally efficient for very large-scale graphs.
