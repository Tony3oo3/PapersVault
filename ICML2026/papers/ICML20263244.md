## Problem

Activation steering is a standard tool for modifying LLM behavior, but whether models can detect and recover from such perturbations mid-generation was unexplored. This paper asks whether the observed recovery reflects a genuine internal coherence-monitoring mechanism or is fully explained by autoregressive conditioning on the model's own off-topic tokens, with direct implications for both adversarial robustness and the reliability of steering-based safety interventions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263244/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes 38 object-level text prompts and a single SAE latent direction, applied as an additive perturbation (A_ℓ ← A_ℓ + b·W_D[k,:]) to residual-stream activations at a fixed layer on every generated token, with per-latent boost calibrated via probabilistic bisection to yield a 30/100 first-attempt judge score. A judge model (Claude 4.5 Haiku) segments each response into discrete attempts using explicit restart phrases as boundary markers and scores each 0–100 for prompt relevance; the headline ESR rate is the product of multi-attempt rate and conditional improvement rate, capturing only verbalized (not implicit) recovery.

## Method

The paper steers five Llama-3/Gemma-2 models using SAE latents filtered for irrelevance and concreteness, then dissociates ESR into two components via targeted controls: a prefilling experiment shows the restart event is largely text-conditioned (unsteered model given off-topic prefix self-corrects 7–13.5% vs. 2–3% under active steering), while a matched-prefix experiment on 447 episodes shows natural post-correction text improves steered continuation scores 2.1× more than a length-matched on-topic prefix (p<0.001), leaving roughly half of sustained resistance unexplained by autoregressive conditioning. Mechanistically, contrastive search via the Ember API identifies 26 self-correction-associated SAE latents whose zero-ablation reduces multi-attempt rate by 25% (with random-latent and held-out-prompt controls confirming specificity). ESR is further enhanced through meta-prompting (4.3× multi-attempt increase in Llama-3.3-70B) and LoRA fine-tuning on synthetic self-correction examples, which raises attempt rate but leaves correction success unchanged, dissociating behavioral imitation from effective monitoring.

## Results

Llama-3.3-70B exhibits an ESR rate of 3.8% (multi-attempt rate 7.4%, conditional improvement rate ~51%), substantially exceeding all other tested models (Llama-3.1-8B, Gemma-2-27B/9B/2B), which all fall below 1% ESR. A no-steering control across 7,892 trials confirms 0% spontaneous multi-attempt behavior, establishing that ESR is specifically induced by steering. Ablating 26 self-correction-associated SAE latents reduces the multi-attempt rate by 25% (7.4%→5.5%) and ESR by 27% (3.8%→2.8%), while a matched random-latent ablation shows no such reduction. Meta-prompting increases Llama-3.3-70B's multi-attempt rate 4.3× (7.4%→31.7%), and a matched-prefix experiment shows natural post-correction text improves steered continuation scores 2.1× more than a length-matched on-topic prefix (+45.8 vs. +21.8, p<0.001).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external steering-resistance methods; instead it benchmarks five models against each other and against several internal controls. Llama-3.3-70B is the only model showing substantial explicit ESR (3.8%), with a 6×+ gap over the next-best model; the authors note they cannot disentangle whether this reflects scale, architecture, or training. The no-steering baseline (0% multi-attempt across 7,892 trials) rules out spontaneous self-correction as an explanation. The prefilling control dissociates detection from resistance: an unsteered model given an off-topic prefix self-corrects 7–13.5% of the time with ~95% success, versus 2–3% / ~50% under active steering, showing the restart event is largely text-conditioned while sustained on-topic generation under ongoing perturbation is not fully explained by autoregressive conditioning. The random-latent ablation control (ESR 4.2%, within CI of baseline 3.8%) confirms the 27% ESR reduction from targeted ablation is specific to the 26 identified latents rather than a general effect of ablating active features. ESR also generalizes beyond SAE-derived directions: Wikipedia-derived contrastive vectors yield a comparable 3.6% ESR rate (p≈0.9 vs. SAE baseline).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes activation steering (Turner et al., 2023; Zou et al., 2023) and SAE-derived feature directions (Cunningham et al., 2023; Templeton et al., 2024) not as behavioral interventions but as a diagnostic probe for internal coherence monitoring. It positions itself against the Hydra Effect (McGrath et al., 2023), which involves silent downstream compensation after layer ablation, and against R1-style 'aha moments' (Guo et al., 2025; Yang et al., 2025), which occur in clean generation without an ongoing perturbation. The biological parallel to endogenous attention control (Graziano, 2017) and the connection to emergent introspection (Lindsey, 2025) frame ESR as a measurable, steering-induced instance of a broader self-monitoring capacity, while the prefilling and matched-prefix controls deliberately deflate the 'internal monitor' interpretation in favor of a two-component (detection + sustained resistance) decomposition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ESR has dual safety implications: it could harden models against adversarial activation-space manipulation, but the same mechanism may interfere with beneficial steering-based interventions (e.g., Inference-Time Intervention, Representation Engineering) since the model cannot distinguish the two. Key open questions include whether fine-tuning under active steering can install genuine sustained resistance (as opposed to the surface-level imitation shown here), whether the 26 self-correction-associated latents form a coherent circuit (cross-layer analysis is needed), and how ESR behaves under safety-relevant steering directions. Limitations include testing only five models across two families (preventing scale/architecture/training disentanglement), a metric that captures only explicit verbalized recovery, and modest effect sizes (3.8% ESR rate, 25% ablation reduction) that leave the practical magnitude of the phenomenon uncertain.
