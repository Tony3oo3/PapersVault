## Problem

Existing backdoor attacks on reinforcement learning assume unrealistic whitebox access to the victim's rewards, observations, policies, or transition functions—access levels that would obviate the need for an attack in the first place. This paper identifies that the realistic threat vector is the RL supply chain, where externally sourced pre-trained agents (e.g., from HuggingFace) interact with a victim through only legitimate actions, and demonstrates that even this minimal access is sufficient to embed a high-impact backdoor.

## Input / Output

The attacker consumes only its own environment observations (10 stacked frames) and produces legitimate actions, with no read or write access to the victim's observations, rewards, or policy. The victim receives standard stacked-frame inputs and outputs actions as usual; the backdoor manifests as a conditional response where a 4-step no-op trigger sequence elicits a 4-step backdoor action sequence (e.g., four consecutive move-downs in Pong). At test time, a separate trigger network outputs a scalar gate that determines when an opponent should inject the trigger, balancing reward gain against a penalty on trigger frequency.

## Method

SCAB operates as a four-state finite-state machine (Winning → Triggering → Observing → Rewarding) executed by a pre-trained attacker policy that is indistinguishable from a benign agent. The key mechanism is implicit rewarding: after the detector d confirms the victim executed the correct backdoor action sequence, the attacker switches to a rewarding policy π_rwd^att that deliberately and rapidly loses, thereby associating the backdoor actions with high cumulative reward in the victim's Q-function. The rewarding policy is trained via a max-min objective to maximize the opponent's expected return from arbitrary states, ensuring it works against any victim policy. A Trigger Injection Probability (TIP) hyperparameter controls the fraction of episodes where triggers are injected, balancing attack effectiveness against stealthiness—3% TIP achieves >90% trigger success while keeping training metrics statistically indistinguishable from clean training.

## Results

SCAB achieves a peak trigger success rate of 91.3% (Boxing, LSTM, PPO, 3% TIP) and reduces the victim's average episodic return by over 80% (from 82.6 to 13.1 at 30% trigger proportion). Across all three main environments (Pong, Surround, Boxing) and both algorithms (PPO, DQN), SCAB consistently achieves trigger success rates of 31–91%, with LSTM architectures outperforming CNN by 10–50 percentage points. The attack requires only 3% trigger injection during training and no access to the victim's observations, rewards, or policy parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCAB is compared against two prior backdoor attacks under a common protocol (Boxing, LSTM, PPO, 3% corruption). Against TrojDRL (Kiourti et al., 2020), which requires write access to the victim's observations and rewards during training and observation access at test time, SCAB achieves a slightly lower trigger success rate (91.3% vs. 99.1%) but operates with zero access to any victim internals. Against BACKDOORL (Wang et al., 2021a; Chen et al., 2022a), which requires the attacker to directly construct the victim's policy via imitation learning (write access to π^vic), SCAB achieves a comparable trigger success rate (91.3% vs. 93.6%) while requiring no policy access whatsoever. In terms of victim performance degradation at 30% trigger proportion, SCAB reduces the return to 13.1 (84.1% drop), slightly outperforming BACKDOORL (14.3, 82.4% drop) and approaching TrojDRL (6.7, 92.1% drop). The key distinction is that SCAB's attacker only uses legitimate actions and read-only state observations—matching the access profile of any benign agent—making it the most realistic threat model among the three methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the established line of RL backdoor attacks (TrojDRL, BAFFLE, Temporal-Pattern Backdoor, MARNet, Stop-and-Go, BACKDOORL) by rejecting their shared assumption of privileged read/write access to the victim's observations, rewards, or policy. Instead, SCAB operates under a supply-chain threat model where the attacker is merely a pre-trained agent with the same access as any legitimate opponent—read-only observations and write-only own actions. The mechanism repurposes the concept of implicit reward shaping (the attacker deliberately loses to inflate the victim's Q-values for backdoor actions) within a finite-state-machine controller, a design not present in prior RL backdoor work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that >90% trigger success and 80% performance degradation are achievable with only 3% data corruption and no privileged access fundamentally reframes the security posture of the RL model-sharing ecosystem (2.6B+ HuggingFace downloads). The stealthiness result—training dynamics statistically indistinguishable from clean runs—means existing monitoring practices offer no protection, and the limited effectiveness of fine-tuning defense (TSR only drops from 83.2% to 69.5% after 128K steps) leaves a significant gap. Open questions include detection mechanisms for supply-chain backdoors, whether the attacker's need to track cumulative reward and switch behaviors can be hidden within a pure neural network, and whether dynamic or more intricate backdoor patterns would further increase attack potency.
