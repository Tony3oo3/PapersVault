## Problem

Machine learning research has bifurcated into benchmark-driven engineering (optimizing a single metric on large systems) and idealized theory (rigorous proofs in simplified settings), with the central scientific object—the mechanistic idea—neglected in both modes. This dual fixation creates a 'complexity premium' that favors well-resourced labs, produces non-cumulative findings, and excludes low-resource researchers from contributing rigorous scientific work.

## Input / Output

As a position paper, the framework takes a scope-bearing mechanistic hypothesis (an 'idea') as input and produces a concrete observable 'signature' (a measurable behavioral pattern in modern models) plus a tailored experimental design to detect or falsify that signature. The illustrative examples span text (LLM topic-inertia probes using SBERT embeddings on 10–200 token prompts) and RGB images (CIFAR-10/100, MNIST for NTK linearization and max-margin tracking), with models ranging from small MLPs to 20B-parameter LLMs (LLaMA 2, GPT-NeoX-20B, MPT-7B).

## Method

The paper proposes the 'Ideas First' framework, formalized as a three-link chain: idea → signature → tailored experiment. An idea is a scope-bearing claim about how a learning system works, established in a simplified setting; a signature translates that claim into a concrete, measurable behavioral commitment (e.g., a specific geometric trend, error pattern, or causal response) expected in modern models; and a tailored experiment is designed to detect the signature using targeted readouts, perturbations, and negative controls rather than leaderboard optimization. The paper validates the framework through three canonical examples (NTK linearization, implicit max-margin bias, Mixup linearity) and a hypothetical LLM case study on 'Topic Inertia,' then provides a detailed field guide with explicit 'aim to' and 'avoid' criteria for both authors and reviewers to operationalize idea-centric evaluation in peer review.

## Results

This is a position paper without quantitative experimental results. The paper proposes the 'Ideas First' framework (Idea → Signature → Tailored Experiment) and illustrates it through three existing works (NTK linearization, implicit max-margin bias, and Mixup) and one hypothetical case study on 'Topic Inertia' in LLMs. The hypothetical case study (Section 6) predicts and observes an increasing cosine similarity between prompt and generated text as prompt length grows from 10 to 200 tokens across LLaMA 2, GPT-NeoX-20B, and MPT-7B, with a flat trend in an RNN negative control, but these results are explicitly illustrative rather than empirical contributions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

As a position paper, this work does not benchmark a proposed method against baselines. Instead, it contrasts two dominant research modes—benchmark-driven engineering (Mode A, exemplified by AlexNet, ResNet, GPT-3, Chinchilla) and idealized theory (Mode B, exemplified by NTK, margin bounds, benign overfitting results)—and argues both are insufficient without a central idea. The three illustrative examples (NTK linearization via Lee et al. 2019, max-margin implicit bias via Lyu & Li 2020, and Mixup via Zhang et al. 2018) are presented not as competing methods but as demonstrations that the Idea→Signature→Experiment chain already underlies influential work. The hypothetical Topic Inertia case study uses an RNN as a negative control to rule out generic artifacts, but no head-to-head performance comparison is made against other LLMs or attention mechanisms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This position paper does not build on a specific technical method but rather synthesizes and repurposes existing threads: critiques of benchmark-driven evaluation (Lipton & Steinhardt, 2018; Dehghani et al., 2021), the transfer gap in idealized theory (Baraniuk et al., 2020; Zhang et al., 2017), and equity concerns in AI research (Ahmed & Wahed, 2020; Strubell et al., 2019). It draws on philosophy of science (Merton, Kuhn, Cartwright, Hacking, Giere) to frame ML research as hypothesis-driven inquiry, and uses existing results on NTK, implicit bias, and Mixup as illustrative cases where the proposed 'idea → signature → tailored experiment' chain is already implicitly present. The novelty is in packaging these scattered critiques into a single operational framework with concrete definitions and a field guide for authors and reviewers, rather than in any new empirical or theoretical result.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central claim—that the field's dual fixation on leaderboard gains and idealized theorems systematically excludes low-resource researchers and fragments cumulative progress—is well-argued and resonates with long-standing community frustrations. However, as a position paper it offers no empirical validation that the Ideas First framework would actually change publication outcomes or improve scientific quality; the hypothetical LLM case study is illustrative but untested. Key open questions include how peer review norms would concretely shift, whether the framework can coexist with the legitimate need for benchmarks and rigorous theory, and whether 'signatures' can be standardized enough to be evaluated consistently across venues. The equity argument is the strongest and most actionable component, but its implementation depends on institutional changes the paper does not specify.
