## Problem

4D radar scene flow estimation lacks pointwise ground-truth labels, and existing solutions either rely on self-supervised losses that underperform due to radar's inherent sparsity and noise, or on cross-modal supervision from dense 3D LiDAR point clouds that requires expensive sensors, complex multi-task architectures, and seven or more loss terms. This paper asks whether a lightweight, task-specific framework can achieve competitive radar scene flow estimation using only inexpensive RGB images and vehicle odometry as auxiliary supervision during training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261174/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes two consecutive single-scan 4D radar frames (256 randomly sampled points each, restricted to a [-3m, 3m] height range, with 3D coordinates, RCS, and RRV per point) and outputs a 3D scene flow vector per source-frame point. During training, synchronized RGB images and ego-vehicle odometry are additionally consumed to construct supervision signals, but are not required at test time. The 2D instance labels are generated offline by YOLO11 tracking and SAM segmentation, then back-projected via camera intrinsics and radar-to-camera extrinsics to assign per-point instance IDs in 3D.

## Method

IterFlow is a lightweight (113K parameter) iterative network that replaces KNN-based cross-frame correlation with ball query grouping (radius R=1m, L=8 neighbors) to avoid mismatches in sparse radar clouds, and uses a GRU-based recurrent scheme to progressively refine flow over K=12 iterations, each producing a residual flow update. Training uses only three complementary losses: a rigid static loss that constrains static-point flow to match odometry-derived rigid motion, an instance-aware Chamfer loss that computes Chamfer distance exclusively between same-instance point pairs across frames (guided by 2D tracking/segmentation masks back-projected into 3D), and an instance-level flow smoothness loss that enforces flow coherence within each foreground object rather than across KNN neighborhoods. This design eliminates the need for LiDAR-derived pseudo flow labels, 2D optical flow, or multi-task architectures, yet surpasses both cross-modal supervised and fully supervised baselines on the VoD dataset.

## Results

On the View-of-Delft (VoD) validation set, IterFlow achieves an EPE of 0.1045 m, AccS of 33.40%, AccR of 63.75%, and RNE of 0.0420 m, establishing a new state of the art for 4D radar scene flow estimation. The method uses only 113K parameters and 0.40 GFLOPs, roughly 40× fewer parameters and 30× lower computational cost than the previous best radar-based method CMFlow (4.2M params, 12.83 GFLOPs). On the MAN TruckScenes dataset, IterFlow achieves EPE of 0.1103 m and AccS of 64.30%, again outperforming all radar-based baselines and approaching fully supervised LiDAR methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest radar-based competitor CMFlow (Ding et al., 2023), IterFlow reduces EPE from 0.1600 m to 0.1045 m (34.7% improvement), increases AccS from 19.83% to 33.40% (+13.6 pp), and cuts SRNE from 0.0625 m to 0.0380 m (39.2% reduction), while using only 3 loss terms versus CMFlow's 7. Compared to the best fully supervised LiDAR method PV-RAFT (Wei et al., 2021), IterFlow achieves a lower EPE (0.1045 vs. 0.1140 m, 8.3% improvement) and higher AccS (33.40% vs. 23.24%) despite requiring no 3D flow labels at all. Against the self-supervised radar baseline RaFlow (Ding et al., 2022), IterFlow improves EPE by 62.1% (0.2753 → 0.1045 m) and AccR by 46.3 pp (17.48% → 63.75%). Versus LiDAR-based self-supervised methods (PointPWC self-supervised: EPE 0.4314 m; FlowStep3D: EPE 0.2607 m), IterFlow is substantially more accurate, confirming that radar-specific loss design is critical. On the MAN TruckScenes dataset, IterFlow (EPE 0.1103 m) outperforms CMFlow (0.1342 m) and RaFlow (0.4537 m), and approaches the fully supervised PointPWC (0.1151 m) while using far fewer parameters (113K vs. 7.7M).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the radar scene flow estimation line initiated by RaFlow (self-supervised, RRV-guided) and CMFlow (cross-modal supervised with LiDAR, optical flow, and odometry), but deliberately departs from the prevailing trend of increasing architectural and loss complexity. Rather than stacking seven or more loss terms in a multi-task network, IterFlow uses a lightweight GRU-based iterative refinement architecture with ball query cross-frame correlation and only three targeted losses. The key conceptual departure is replacing expensive 3D LiDAR-derived pseudo labels with 2D instance semantics (from off-the-shelf YOLO11 tracking and SAM segmentation) back-projected into 3D, combined with odometry-based rigid static supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: a 113K-parameter model trained only with camera and odometry outperforms a 4.2M-parameter model requiring dense LiDAR and 3D tracking, making radar scene flow feasible on consumer vehicles without costly LiDAR sensors. However, the approach remains limited by radar's inherent sparsity (failure cases on distant pedestrians and large displacements) and by 2D model accuracy in adverse weather, as evidenced by the widened performance gap on MAN TruckScenes. Open questions include whether the 2D semantic guidance can be further simplified or made more robust, and whether the iterative refinement scheme generalizes to even sparser or higher-velocity scenarios.
