## Problem

For fixed-confidence top-k identification from noisy pairwise comparisons under latent utility models, no asymptotically optimal algorithm had been established, despite such guarantees existing for other pure-exploration bandit problems. The key obstacle is that the information-theoretic lower bound is governed by a max-min oracle allocation over all pairs, and it was unclear how to characterize this oracle structure or track it efficiently online when the true utilities are unknown.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261295/cropped_page8_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm adaptively selects pairs from n items to compare, observing outcomes from a one-parameter exponential family (binary Bradley-Terry or cardinal Gaussian differences) parameterized by unknown utilities θ. It takes a risk level δ and target set size k as inputs, and outputs the top-k set upon stopping. The stopping time is the total number of comparisons made; the algorithm guarantees P(correct top-k) ≥ 1−δ and almost-sure termination. Comparison outcomes are consumed sequentially, and the algorithm must decide which pair to query next based on all previously observed outcomes.

## Method

The paper reduces the alternative-parameter region to boundary pairs (one item in the true top-k, one outside), showing the oracle allocation problem is a two-player saddle-point game: a designer allocates comparison proportions over all C(n,2) pairs while an adversary places mass on the most vulnerable boundary pair. An entropic FTRL primal-dual procedure learns the saddle point online, using stochastic gradients obtained by sampling a single boundary pair per round (O(n²) per step rather than O(k(n−k)·n²)). C-tracking with a decaying uniform mixture ensures empirical pair counts track the learned optimal allocation, and a likelihood-ratio stopping rule with a self-normalized concentration threshold triggers termination. The main theorem proves the expected stopping time satisfies E[τ_δ]/log(1/δ) → 1/Γ*(θ) as δ→0, matching the information-theoretic lower bound exactly.

## Results

The paper evaluates Algorithm 1 on three synthetic instances (random utilities, equally spaced utilities, and SST preference matrices) with δ=0.01, varying n (k=5 fixed) and k (n=100 fixed), reporting mean stopping time over 100 simulations on a log-log scale. On random utilities, Algorithm 1 consistently outperforms both SEEKS/SEEKS-v2 and Active Ranking as n and k increase. On equally spaced utilities, Algorithm 1 closely tracks an oracle benchmark that uses the true optimal allocation w*(θ), confirming near-optimal performance even at δ=0.01, though its stopping time grows with n due to the ||θ̂_t||² term in the threshold. Under SST misspecification, Algorithm 1 remains competitive across all n values.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baseline families: SEEKS/SEEKS-v2 (Ren et al., 2020) and Active Ranking (Heckel et al., 2019). On the random utilities instance, Algorithm 1 outperforms both baselines across all values of n and k, with the gap widening as instances become harder (smaller boundary gaps). On the equally spaced instance, Algorithm 1 tracks the oracle allocation closely, but its stopping time increases with n (driven by the growing ||θ̂_t||² in the stopping threshold), whereas SEEKS and SEEKS-v2 do not exhibit this increase, making them competitive at moderate δ=0.01 for large n. The authors note that Theorem 6.5 guarantees Algorithm 1 will eventually outperform all baselines as δ→0, and Figure 2 confirms this for smaller δ values. Under SST misspecification, Algorithm 1 remains competitive with baselines across n, though the same threshold-looseness issue appears. Overall, Algorithm 1 wins on random utilities and matches or slightly trails baselines on equally spaced instances at δ=0.01 for large n, but is guaranteed to dominate asymptotically.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Track-and-Stop framework of Garivier & Kaufmann (2016) and the game-theoretic oracle-tracking approach of Degenne et al. (2019) from best-arm identification to top-k identification from pairwise comparisons under latent utility models. The key structural contribution is showing that the alternative-parameter region reduces to boundary-pair inversions, yielding a clean two-player saddle-point game between a designer allocating comparisons and an adversary targeting boundary pairs. Unlike Wang et al. (2021), whose Frank-Wolfe oracle-tracking requires a combinatorial best-response oracle, the boundary-pair structure here admits an unbiased stochastic gradient from a single sampled boundary pair, making the primal-dual FTRL updates computationally tractable. Compared to SEEKS (Ren et al., 2020), which is optimal only up to a log n factor under restrictive conditions (Thurstone model, bounded utilities, δ < 0.01), this paper matches the information-theoretic lower bound exactly in the asymptotic regime δ→0 under more general one-parameter exponential family models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first asymptotically optimal algorithm for fixed-confidence top-k identification from pairwise comparisons under latent utility models, closing a gap that has persisted in the pure-exploration literature. The saddle-point formulation with boundary pairs provides a reusable template for other structured comparison problems where alternatives have a combinatorial but low-dimensional characterization. The main limitation acknowledged by the authors is that the stopping threshold (which scales with ‖θ̂‖²) is loose at moderate δ, sometimes causing the algorithm to be outperformed by simpler baselines like SEEKS; constructing a tighter threshold that preserves asymptotic optimality remains an open problem. The practical relevance is significant for settings like LLM evaluation via Arena or crowdsourced pairwise annotation where comparison budgets are limited and adaptivity saves cost.
