## Problem

Prior LLM forecasting work has focused on broad mixed-topic benchmarks with shallow context such as news headlines, leaving it unclear whether language-model-based forecasting can add value in specialized, high-stakes domains that require long-context reasoning over hundreds of pages of technical documents. This paper tackles that gap in the domain of merger arbitrage, where accurate probabilistic forecasts of M&A deal outcomes are directly monetized through position sizing and risk management.

## Input / Output

The system takes an announced M&A deal as input and assembles 6.8k–10k tokens of structured context via 12 specialized research agents that query a large vector store of regulatory filings, company transcripts, shareholder registries, historical M&A records, and expert commentary. It produces a probability distribution over three mutually exclusive outcomes (deal closes as announced, terminates with a higher bid, or terminates without one), a point estimate of days to completion, and a detailed deal report with cited red and green flags. The three-way outcome distinction—separating positive from negative termination—is essential for commercializing predictions and for comparison against market-implied probabilities derived from a two-state mixture model of the target's share price.

## Method

The architecture is a two-stage pipeline: 12 tool-augmented ReAct agents (each handling a specific dimension such as regulatory risk, financing risk, stakeholder ownership, or precedent mergers) gather and analyze deal context, and an ensembled finetuned GPT-4o forecasting module synthesizes the outputs into probabilistic predictions via N=5 samples at temperature 0.2 with median aggregation. The key training innovation is hindsight-guided supervision: for each historical deal, a post-mortem agent with access to full future information identifies which ex-ante signals proved predictive, and a teacher model (GPT-5.1) writes gold deal reports that reason strictly from pre-forecast-date evidence while using the post-mortem only to calibrate which facts to emphasize. Gold outcome targets blend one-hot labels with market-implied probability through a time-varying smoothing parameter that decays from 0.3 at announcement to zero near resolution, preventing overconfident forecasts early in the deal lifecycle. Retrieval is restricted to timestamped regulatory filings and commercial data sources (no open-web content) to enforce strict temporal integrity, and the embedding and reranking models are domain-finetuned on financial text.

## Results

On a held-out test set of 404 large M&A deals spanning 42 countries, the finetuned GPT-4o system achieves a class-balanced Brier score (Brier_B) of 0.151 on the +/- outcome prediction task, outperforming all baselines. This is 24% below Platt-scaled market-implied probabilities (0.199), 19% below XGBoost (0.186), and 25–42% below frontier LLMs given identical context (0.201–0.259). Finetuning reduces calibration error from 0.089 (GPT-4o base) to 0.039, and the system achieves a MAPE of 0.385 on days-to-close prediction versus 0.463 for the base model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The finetuned system (GPT-4o-FT) outperforms every baseline across all weighted Brier score variants. Against the strongest structured baseline, XGBoost (Brier_B = 0.186), the finetuned system improves by 19% (0.151), with the gap widening under surprise-weighted (0.187 vs. 0.280) and P&L-weighted (0.178 vs. 0.246) scoring, exposing XGBoost's over-reliance on the market-implied probability feature. Against calibrated market-implied probabilities (Platt-scaled, Brier_B = 0.199), the improvement is 24%. All frontier LLMs given identical research-agent context underperform XGBoost on Brier_B (range 0.201–0.259), and even post-hoc isotonic calibration of GPT-4o (0.186) falls well short of the finetuned model (0.151), confirming that the gain comes from learned reasoning rather than simple recalibration. Notably, the finetuned system has a much lower correlation with market-implied probability (ρ = 0.358) than XGBoost (ρ = 0.753), indicating it captures deal-specific information beyond what is already priced in.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM-based judgmental forecasting line (Halawi et al. 2024; Schoenegger et al. 2024; Alur et al. 2025) from broad mixed-topic benchmarks with shallow news-snippet context to a single specialized domain requiring long-context reasoning over hundreds of pages of technical financial documents. Architecturally, it builds on ReAct tool-augmented agents (Yao et al. 2022) for multi-dimensional information retrieval and on process supervision (Uesato et al. 2022; Lightman et al. 2024) for constructing hindsight-guided training targets. The key methodological departure is the use of ex-post deal outcomes to shape ex-ante gold reasoning traces via a teacher model, combined with market-implied probability smoothing to avoid overconfident one-hot supervision—neither of which has been systematically applied in prior LLM forecasting work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that LLM-based forecasting can outperform both market-implied probabilities (by 24% in class-balanced Brier score) and structured-feature baselines in a high-stakes, economically important domain, provided that expert-designed context engineering and hindsight-guided finetuning are used. A notable practical limitation is that 60% of the worst errors stem from missing or stale context rather than faulty reasoning, capping the system's ceiling until information-access gaps are closed. Open questions include whether the hindsight-guided supervision recipe generalizes to other specialized long-context forecasting domains, whether enabling web search (disabled here for leakage prevention) would close the remaining error gap, and whether the approach scales to even longer document contexts or more complex multi-outcome settings.
