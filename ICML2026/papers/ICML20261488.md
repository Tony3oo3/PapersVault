## Problem

Existing post-training linearization methods that distill quadratic-attention LLMs into sub-quadratic architectures consistently fail to match teacher performance on harder generative tasks such as mathematical reasoning and code synthesis, despite recovering language-understanding benchmarks. The paper formalizes a rigorous criterion for 'lossless distillation' via a tolerance-corrected Win-and-Tie rate C_α and demonstrates that prior linearization recipes (LoLCATs, RADLADS, Mamba-in-Llama) fall well short of this standard, particularly on free-form generation evaluations.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a pretrained causal Transformer LLM (Llama 3.1 8B, Qwen 2.5 7B, or Olmo 3 7B) as the frozen teacher and produces a fully linearized student of comparable parameter count that replaces every multi-head attention block with a hybrid mLSTM + sliding-window attention unit. At inference, the student consumes text token sequences and emits next-token logits with a constant-size recurrent state (mLSTM matrix state plus a fixed-length SWA KV cache of 512 tokens plus 4 sink tokens), enabling O(1) memory per step during autoregressive decoding regardless of context length. The distillation supervision uses precomputed top-256 teacher logit distributions, so the teacher need not be loaded during the end-to-end fine-tuning stage.

## Method

The core architecture replaces each softmax attention layer with an intra-layer hybrid: a global mLSTM branch (with per-head scalar output gates, head-wise softmax feature maps, and the original normalizer state) runs in parallel with a local sliding-window attention branch (window size 512, 4 sink tokens), and their outputs are fused by a learned data-dependent sigmoid gate. Training proceeds in three stages: (I) layer-wise MSE alignment of student hidden states to teacher attention outputs with frozen embeddings/MLPs, (II) end-to-end sparse knowledge distillation using a mixed objective of 0.9·CE + 0.1·KL over top-256 teacher logits, and (III) optional expert merging where K domain-specialized students (math, code, STEM, chat) are independently distilled and consolidated via linear weight averaging with validation-tuned coefficients. The key empirical finding is that this decentralized branch-train-merge workflow closes the generative-task gap that single generalist distillation leaves open, and that all three architectural components (mLSTM gating, SWA, sink tokens) are individually necessary for strong performance.

## Results

The paper's primary metric is the tolerance-corrected Win-and-Tie rate C_α and its critical tolerance α* (minimum α such that C_α ≥ 0.5). On generation benchmarks, xLSTM-LLAMA3.1-8B achieves α* = 0.0 and xLSTM-OLMO3-7B achieves α* = 0.01, while instruction-tuned students xLSTM-LLAMA3.1-8B-IT and xLSTM-QWEN2.5-7B-IT achieve α* = 0.02 and α* = 0.05 respectively. All baselines (LoLCATs, QRWKV6-7B, QRWKV7-7B-IT) yield α* = 1.0 on generation tasks, indicating they fail to match the teacher on any benchmark without full tolerance. On inference, the hybrid student achieves ~2× higher prefill throughput at B=1/C=65K, ~4× higher generation throughput with prefill at B=8, and roughly halves both latency and GPU memory at G=131K tokens compared to the Transformer teacher.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three prior linearization methods: LoLCATs (Zhang et al., 2025), RADLADS/QRWKV (Goldstein et al., 2025), and Mamba-in-Llama (Wang et al., 2024). On language understanding benchmarks (MMLU, etc.), the xLSTM students achieve full or near-full teacher parity (recovery rate ≈ 1.0), while LoLCATs and QRWKV6-7B exhibit a significant performance gap. The most dramatic separation appears on generation tasks (math, code, STEM, chat): all baselines yield α* = 1.0, meaning they fail to match the teacher on any benchmark even with 100% tolerance, whereas the xLSTM students achieve α* between 0.0 and 0.05. For instruction-tuned models, Mamba-in-Llama (which retains 50% of original softmax attention layers) and QRWKV7-7B-IT both underperform the fully linearized xLSTM student on code generation and math reasoning. The paper notes these comparisons are not fully controlled for data mixture, token budget, or architectural constraints, so they represent end-to-end recipe comparisons rather than isolated ablations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the post-training linearization line (LoLCATs, RADLADS, Mamba-in-Llama, Llamba) by proposing an intra-layer hybrid that couples mLSTM with sliding-window attention and sink tokens, gated by a learned data-dependent output gate. It departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) a formalized evaluation criterion (tolerance-corrected Win-and-Tie rate C_α) that distinguishes truly lossless distillation from partial recovery, and (2) a decentralized expert-merging stage where domain-specialized linearized students are consolidated via simple weight-space averaging (Wortsman et al., 2022), a technique previously applied to transformer fine-tuning but not to fully linearized recurrent architectures. The architecture design draws on attention-sink preservation (Xiao et al., 2024) and the mLSTM cell (Beck et al., 2024), while the distillation recipe uses sparse top-k KL to enable precomputed teacher targets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, reproducible recipe for converting existing 7–8B transformer LLMs into sub-quadratic models that recover most teacher performance while delivering 2–4× inference speedups, making them viable drop-in replacements for cost-sensitive deployment. The Win-and-Tie rate formalization gives the field a principled, tolerance-aware metric for assessing distillation quality beyond per-benchmark recovery rates. Key open questions include: whether long-context retrieval can be restored without reintroducing full-attention layers, how to mitigate STEM-reasoning interference during expert merging, and whether the pipeline scales to larger teachers or sparse MoE architectures. The finding that simple linear averaging suffices for merging fully linearized models (outperforming TIES-style baselines) suggests weight-space consolidation may be more robust to architectural changes than previously assumed.
