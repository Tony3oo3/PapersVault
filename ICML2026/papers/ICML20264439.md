## Problem

Standard Neural Rough Differential Equations (NRDEs) rely on the shuffle algebra of geometric signatures, which encodes Stratonovich calculus and cannot expose the quadratic-variation terms required by Itô integration or the ordered covariant derivatives governing Itô flows on connection-equipped manifolds. This algebraic mismatch prevents signature-based neural dynamics from modelling non-anticipative stochastic processes or strictly preserving manifold constraints under Itô semantics.

## Input / Output

The method consumes irregularly sampled time series (optionally augmented with quadratic-variation/bracket coordinates for Itô settings) and manifold-valued observations such as SO(3) rotation matrices or SPD covariance matrices. Each path segment is summarised by a log-signature in the chosen Hopf algebra's primitive coordinates, and the hidden state is advanced over coarse windows via a log-ODE step. Outputs are continuous-time trajectories that remain on the state manifold at every solver substep (enforced through group action on homogeneous spaces), generated sample paths for distribution matching, or predicted rotations/SPD matrices for forecasting.

## Method

B-NRDEs recast the NRDE log-ODE step as geometric numerical integration by matching the driving Hopf algebra to the governing calculus: the shuffle algebra for Stratonovich dynamics, the Grossman-Larson algebra of non-planar rooted trees for Euclidean Itô dynamics (exposing quadratic variation as explicit tree coordinates), and the Munthe-Kaas-Wright algebra of planar rooted trees for manifold Itô dynamics (preserving ordered covariant derivatives). Pseudo bialgebra maps convert each primitive basis element into a differential operator on the state manifold, and the resulting log-ODE vector field is integrated using Heun's method in Euclidean space or a commutator-free scheme (CF-EES(2,5)) on homogeneous spaces. A branched signature-kernel training objective makes quadratic-variation terms visible during distribution-matching, enabling Itô-consistent law learning without lead-lag path augmentation. The framework is instantiated in a JAX package (Roughrax) supporting autodifferentiable RDE solution over manifolds.

## Results

On the rough Bergomi volatility task (Euclidean Itô), B-NRDE(GK) achieves the lowest KS distance at the 128 and 256 horizons (6.89 and 6.91 ×10⁻²), outperforming NRDE (8.74, 8.41) and log-NCDE (7.75, 7.38). On SO(3) sim-to-real rotation forecasting, B-NRDE attains RGE of 3.23–3.70° with only 2 solver steps, approaching SG-NCDE's 2.80–3.31° which requires 20 steps. On the SPD manifold Itô diffusion, B-NRDE achieves 1-Wasserstein distances of 5.73–6.28 ×10⁻² at the 128–384 horizons, a 56.2% average improvement over Euclidean log-NCDE (12.10–16.17 ×10⁻²).

## Baselines & Comparisons

B-NRDE outperforms all signature-based baselines (NRDE, log-NCDE) on the rBergomi Itô task at the 128 and 256 horizons, with B-NRDE(GK) achieving 6.89 vs. NRDE's 8.74 and log-NCDE's 7.75 KS at 128. On SO(3) forecasting, B-NRDE (3.23–3.70° RGE) is competitive with SG-NCDE (2.80–3.31°) while using only 2 solver steps versus SG-NCDE's 20, and dramatically outperforms NRDE (7.32–7.45°), xLSTM (16.67–17.36°), SO(3)-GRU (20.25–21.13°), and M-NODE (118–132°). On the SPD manifold Itô diffusion, B-NRDE achieves 5.73–6.28 ×10⁻² W1 at the 128–384 horizons, beating Stacked xLSTM (5.50–11.47), log-NCDE (12.10–16.17), NRDE (11.80–17.36), and xLSTM (8.36–13.16), with a 56.2% average improvement over log-NCDE. The branched kernel (BK) variant is competitive with the geometric kernel (GK) on SPD but slightly weaker on rBergomi at longer horizons. GRU and NCDE baselines are consistently outperformed across all three tasks, confirming that the Hopf-algebraic structure provides a meaningful inductive advantage over generic sequence models and standard CDE formulations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the neural rough differential equation (NRDE) line of work (Morrill et al., 2021; Walker et al., 2024) by replacing the shuffle algebra underlying geometric (Stratonovich) signatures with Grossman-Larson or Munthe-Kaas-Wright Hopf algebras, thereby accommodating Itô quadratic-variation terms and ordered covariant derivatives on manifolds. The algebraic machinery for mapping Hopf-algebra elements to differential operators on a manifold is drawn from pseudo bialgebra maps (Kern & Lyons, 2023), and the log-ODE integration scheme is inherited from NRDEs and log-NCDEs. The key departure is that prior signature-based neural dynamics were confined to the Stratonovich/geometric setting; B-NRDE is the first to use planar rooted trees (MKW) for neural dynamics and to introduce a branched signature-kernel training objective that makes quadratic variation visible during optimisation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework unifies three previously disjoint regimes—Stratonovich, Euclidean Itô, and manifold Itô—under a single log-ODE construction, which is practically important for finance (where non-anticipative Itô modelling is mandatory) and for Riemannian generative models. Manifold constraints are preserved exactly at every solver substep via group-action integration, avoiding the projection/retraction errors of extrinsic methods. Key limitations include the quadratic growth of quadratic-variation features with dimension, the absence of a PDE-based solver for branched kernels (unlike the geometric case), and the observation that branched versus geometric kernels show no meaningful difference on the SPD experiment, suggesting the kernel's advantage may be regime-dependent. Future directions include planar regularity structures for SPDEs and multi-index rough paths for scalar equations.
