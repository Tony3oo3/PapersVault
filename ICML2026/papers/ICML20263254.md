## Problem

Prior-data fitted networks (PFNs) have shown strong empirical performance for causal inference, but it is unclear whether their Bayesian uncertainty quantification is consistent with classical frequentist estimators. This paper shows that existing PFNs suffer from prior-induced confounding bias: their implicit prior (learned from synthetic training data) systematically shrinks observed confounding toward zero, preventing the posterior from being asymptotically overwritten by data and thus blocking frequentist consistency.

## Input / Output

The method takes an observational i.i.d. dataset D = {(x_i, a_i, y_i)} with binary treatment A and real-valued outcome Y, plus a pretrained PFN (e.g., TabPFN) that provides only pointwise posterior predictive densities P(y|D, x, a) and P(A|D, x) rather than explicit functional posteriors. It outputs a calibrated ATE posterior distribution P(ψ^OSPC(η̃)|D) obtained by Bayesian-bootstrapping the uncentered efficient influence function over B=100 posterior draws of the nuisance functions (μ_0, μ_1, π), yielding uncertainty intervals that asymptotically match the A-IPTW estimator's normal distribution.

## Method

The paper introduces MP-OSPC, a calibration procedure with three components. First, it diagnoses prior-induced confounding bias in PFNs by showing their implicit prior concentrates the degree of observed confounding Δ near zero. Second, it applies a one-step posterior correction (OSPC) that augments the plug-in ATE posterior with a Bayesian-bootstrap average of the efficient influence function, restoring a semi-parametric Bernstein-von Mises theorem under L2-concentration, uniform bounding, and Donsker conditions. Third, it implements the OSPC via a PFN+copula martingale posterior: TabPFN provides the initial PPD at Step 1, and subsequent steps use a bivariate Gaussian copula (for outcomes) or beta-Bernoulli mixture copula (for propensity) with a single correlation hyperparameter ρ to sequentially update the predictive, recovering smooth functional posteriors over μ_a and π without retraining the PFN.

## Results

On 77 ACIC-2016 datasets, the best MP-OSPC variant (CausalPFN T=0.1 + TabPFN+copula ρ=0.25) achieves 49.37% of runs with the best finite-sample calibration (d_KS), outperforming the corresponding naive plug-in (31.65%). For TabPFN-based estimators, MP-OSPC (ρ=0.5) improves asymptotic alignment from 14.29% to 37.66% of best d_TV runs over the MP-plug-in baseline. On synthetic data with large confounding (Δ≈−0.5), MP-OSPC variants consistently reduce the total variation distance to the A-IPTW asymptotic distribution compared to uncalibrated plug-in estimators across varying n and d_x. The L₂-concentration check confirms that the second-order remainder R₂ decreases with data size for most MP variants, though it plateaus or increases for n_train > 5000 due to TabPFN's difficulty recovering extreme propensity scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CausalPFN (Balazadeh et al., 2025) as a naive plug-in baseline, MP-OSPC wins on finite-sample calibration (d_KS): the best MP-OSPC variant (CausalPFN T=0.1 + ρ=0.25) achieves 49.37% best d_KS runs versus 31.65% for the naive plug-in, a gain of ~18 percentage points. However, MP-OSPC loses on asymptotic alignment (d_TV) for CausalPFN: the naive plug-in (T=0.5) achieves 54.16% best d_TV runs, well above any MP-OSPC variant (max 34.94%), because CausalPFN's training on counterfactual data already yields approximately debiased posteriors for the ACIC-2016 confounding levels. Against TabPFN (Hollmann et al., 2023) as a plug-in estimator, MP-OSPC consistently wins on both metrics: for ρ=0.5, d_TV improves from 14.29% (MP-plug-in) to 37.66% (MP-OSPC), and d_KS improves from 20.45% to 34.09% for ρ=0.25. The paper attributes the CausalPFN d_TV gap to the ACIC-2016 confounding distribution aligning well with CausalPFN's implicit prior, meaning the OSPC correction term is near-zero and provides no additional benefit in that regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) prior-data fitted networks (PFNs) for causal inference (CausalPFN, CausalFM, Do-PFN, TabPFN), which frame causal estimation as in-context learning; (2) martingale posteriors (Fong et al., 2023; Nagler & Rügamer, 2025), which recover functional posteriors from pointwise PPDs; and (3) one-step posterior corrections (Yiu et al., 2025), which debias Bayesian estimators via efficient influence functions. The paper departs from prior PFN-for-causal-inference work by being the first to formally analyze frequentist consistency (Bernstein-von Mises) of PFN-based ATE estimators, identifying that the implicit prior learned during synthetic pretraining induces a confounding bias that is not asymptotically washed out. Methodologically, it extends the marginal pointwise MPs of Nagler & Rügamer (2025) to conditional MPs for joint functional posteriors and combines them with the OSPC in a new calibration pipeline (MP-OSPC).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of prior-induced confounding bias in PFNs is practically important: it means that the 'out-of-the-box' uncertainty quantification of existing causal PFNs can be systematically miscalibrated, undermining their use for decision-making in medicine, policy, and marketing. The MP-OSPC provides a lightweight, retraining-free calibration procedure that restores asymptotic agreement with efficient frequentist estimators while preserving finite-sample Bayesian flexibility. Key limitations include the approximate (not exact) BvM property due to TabPFN's difficulty recovering propensity posteriors at large n, and the restriction to finite-dimensional estimands like ATE; extension to heterogeneous treatment effects (infinite-dimensional targets) remains open. The paper also highlights that PFNs are currently best suited for medium-sized datasets, limiting their applicability as data scales grow.
