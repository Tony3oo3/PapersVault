## Problem

Existing Bayesian nonparametric sequential models cannot simultaneously discover an unknown number of latent states, model non-geometric state durations, and allow those durations to depend on observed data. HDP-HMM variants retain implicit geometric durations, HDP-HSMM introduces explicit durations but keeps them observation-independent, and recurrent switching models condition on observations but assume finite state spaces—leaving a principled framework for observation-dependent durations in an infinite-state setting unexplored.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of M-dimensional multivariate observations y_1:T (e.g., 4D bee dance coordinates, 5D PCA-reduced neural calcium traces) and produces a full latent trajectory of state labels z_t and remaining-duration counters d_t, along with state-specific VAR(1) emission parameters (A_j, b_j, Σ_j) and state-specific duration regression coefficients (β_j^D, β_j^D_bias). Inference is fully self-supervised via Gibbs sampling with a weak-limit truncation (L=50 states, D_max=200), and the AR(1) emission model means each observation's likelihood depends on the previous observation, not just the current state.

## Method

RED-HDP-HMM extends the HDP-HMM by replacing implicit geometric durations with explicit Negative Binomial durations whose success probability is a logistic function of the preceding observation: p_{t-1} = σ(y_{t-1}^T β_{z_t}^D + β_{z_t}^{D_bias}), so the expected sojourn time in a state is modulated by what the system just observed. State transitions follow the standard HDP stick-breaking construction with nonloopy re-normalization (self-transitions removed to prevent duration-transition aliasing), and emissions use a state-specific VAR(1) model with MNIW conjugate priors. Inference employs a weak-limit Gibbs sampler that jointly samples (z_t, d_t) via a forward-backward pass exploiting the deterministic countdown structure of durations, achieving O(TL(L+D_max)) complexity, with Pólya-Gamma augmentation to maintain conjugacy for the NB regression coefficients. The paper proves exponential truncation error bounds for both state and duration caps, and establishes strict expressiveness over HDP-HSMM by showing that setting β_j^D = 0 recovers the HDP-HSMM while nonzero coefficients produce conditional duration laws no observation-independent model can match.

## Results

RED-HDP-HMM (Nonloopy) achieves the best segmentation accuracy on 6 of 7 benchmarks, with the largest gains on C. elegans neural recordings (3–8 percentage points over all baselines; e.g., 0.485 vs. 0.422 for S-HDP-HMM on Neural Data 4, a 15% relative improvement). On Bee Dance, it reaches 0.899 accuracy and 0.884 weighted F1, outperforming S-HDP-HMM (0.873) and RS-HDP-HMM (0.853). On the synthetic NASCAR benchmark, DS-HDP-HMM retains the edge (0.982 accuracy vs. 0.918 for RED-HDP-HMM Nonloopy), where regular state transitions make explicit durations less beneficial. HDP-HSMM variants (Poisson and NB) perform poorly across all benchmarks (accuracy ≈ 0.33–0.39), confirming that observation-independent durations are insufficient for these tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against S-HDP-HMM (Fox et al., 2011), RED-HDP-HMM (Nonloopy) wins on 6 of 7 benchmarks, with the largest margin on Neural Data 4 (0.485 vs. 0.422, +6.3 pp) and Neural Data 1 (0.423 vs. 0.351, +7.2 pp); it loses only on NASCAR (0.918 vs. 0.942). Against DS-HDP-HMM (Zhou et al., 2021), the same pattern holds: RED wins on 6/7 with gains of 2.6–10.4 pp on real-world data, while DS-HDP-HMM retains a narrow edge on NASCAR (0.982 vs. 0.918). Against RS-HDP-HMM (Słupiński & Lipiński, 2025), which adds observation-dependent self-persistence but retains implicit geometric durations, RED-HDP-HMM wins on all 7 benchmarks, confirming that explicit duration modeling provides benefits beyond recurrent transition dynamics alone. Against HDP-HSMM (Johnson & Willsky, 2013), RED-HDP-HMM dominates by a wide margin on every benchmark (e.g., 0.899 vs. 0.335 on Bee Dance; 0.485 vs. 0.348 on Neural Data 4), demonstrating that observation-independent duration distributions are fundamentally insufficient for these tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate threads: the HDP-HMM family (Teh et al., 2006; Fox, 2009; Zhou et al., 2021) providing infinite state spaces with geometric durations; the HDP-HSMM (Johnson & Willsky, 2013) adding explicit but observation-independent duration distributions; and recurrent switching models (Linderman et al., 2017; Ansari et al., 2021; Slupiński & Lipiński, 2024a) making switching dynamics observation-dependent but restricted to finite state spaces. The closest prior work, RS-HDP-HMM (Slupiński & Lipiński, 2025), introduces observation-dependence into the BNP HMM setting but only through the self-persistence probability, retaining implicit geometric durations. RED-HDP-HMM departs from all of these by parameterizing durations through Gamma-mixed Negative Binomial regression (adapted from Zhou et al., 2012) conditioned on the preceding observation, while preserving the HDP transition machinery and Pólya-Gamma conjugacy for tractable Gibbs sampling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a concrete gap in the BNP sequential modeling landscape: no prior model combined infinite states, explicit non-geometric durations, and observation-dependent duration dynamics. The strict expressiveness theorem (Theorem 5.4) and truncation error bounds provide theoretical grounding that is largely absent from the HDP-HMM literature. However, identifiability of observation-dependent duration models remains unresolved, the computational cost scales linearly with D_max limiting applicability to very long segments, and the gains, while consistent (3–8 pp on neural data), are moderate in absolute terms. A natural next step is integrating this duration mechanism with switching linear dynamical systems to jointly infer discrete and continuous latent structure at scale.
