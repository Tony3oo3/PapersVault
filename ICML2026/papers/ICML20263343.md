## Problem

Selecting the best pretrained LLM to fine-tune for a task-specific dataset is expensive and typically done via exhaustive search or single-objective heuristics (e.g., pick the largest model). In practice, practitioners face multi-objective trade-offs between performance, model size, inference cost, and deployment constraints, yet no principled, preference-free method exists to efficiently identify the Pareto-optimal subset of candidate models without evaluating every one of them to convergence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263343/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a target dataset (with train/validation splits), a pool of candidate pretrained LLMs (70 models from a few million to 8B parameters), and a landmark schedule K (number of exponentially growing data subsets, default K=8). It produces a *set* of non-dominated fine-tuned models (the approximate Pareto front) rather than a single model, making the output preference-free and allowing practitioners to revisit trade-offs later. A pre-constructed meta-dataset of fully recorded fine-tuning trajectories across multiple datasets is required to train the policy, but is not needed at deployment time.

## Method

LAMPS combines two mechanisms: (1) landmark fine-tuning, which splits the training set into K exponentially larger subsets and fine-tunes each candidate incrementally to obtain early performance signals before full convergence, and (2) a multi-task RL policy (PPO) meta-trained on historical fine-tuning trajectories that sequentially allocates one fine-tuning epoch at a time to the most promising candidate, using invalid action masking to skip completed models. The policy observes each candidate's current objective values and completed epochs, and the sparse terminal reward (number of non-Pareto-optimal models evaluated divided by wall-clock time) incentivizes fast identification of the full Pareto front while minimizing wasted compute on dominated candidates. A formal lemma proves this reward is policy-invariant under the training-time termination condition, preserving the optimal solutions of the original multi-objective objective. At deployment, the trained policy runs on a new dataset until a search budget is exhausted, after which dominated solutions are filtered to report only the non-dominated set.

## Results

On 38 held-out machine translation directions (2D objectives: model size and validation loss), LAMPS matches or exceeds the Oracle upper bound in 32/38 cases and reduces search time by an average of 73.6% compared to exhaustive search while recovering 99% of the optimal hypervolume. On the 12 text classification datasets, LAMPS achieves the best search time in 9/12 (75%) versus MO-ASHA's 3/12. In the 3-objective setting (adding BLEU), LAMPS still wins 27/38 (71%) while MO-ASHA's win rate rises only to 10/38 (26%), and in the single-objective ablation LAMPS wins 35/38 (92.1%). A concrete cost example: on the Amazon dataset, exhaustive search costs $5,141.67 on a single A100, while LAMPS reduces this to $449.58 with only 1% hypervolume degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MO-ASHA (Schmucker et al., 2021) is the strongest multi-objective baseline, winning 3/12 text classification datasets and 10/38 translation directions in the 3D setting, but LAMPS outperforms it on 9/12 and 27/38 respectively; MO-ASHA also exhibits markedly higher variance, occasionally degrading to Blind-level performance on several language pairs. In the single-objective ablation (Table 3), LAMPS dominates all multi-fidelity baselines: SHA frequently fails to reach the 99% hypervolume threshold within budget, Hyperband requires 2–20× more GPU-hours (e.g., 93.2 vs. 14.8 on EN-FR), and BOHB similarly lags (e.g., 76.1 vs. 14.8 on EN-FR). LAMPS matches the Oracle upper bound in 32/38 two-objective cases and 35/38 single-objective cases, indicating near-optimal resource allocation. The consistent failure mode for LAMPS is on Russian translation directions (ES-RU, DE-RU, EN-RU, IT-RU, FR-RU) and DE-EN, where the policy's meta-learned distribution does not cover these atypical tasks, causing search times to inflate 3–8× over Oracle.

## Relation to Prior Work

LAMPS synthesizes three previously separate research threads: subsampling landmarks for early performance estimation (Soares et al., 2001), multi-task reinforcement learning for sequential decision-making (Teh et al., 2017; Sodhani et al., 2021), and multi-objective Pareto-based search (Schmucker et al., 2021). The key departure from prior model-selection work (You et al., 2021; Arango et al., 2024) is the explicit multi-objective Pareto formulation, which prior approaches treat as single-objective. Unlike MO-ASHA, which operates independently per task, LAMPS meta-learns a dataset-agnostic scheduling policy via PPO over a historical fine-tuning trajectory database, enabling transfer across unseen tasks without per-task reoptimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 73% average search-time reduction with >99% hypervolume coverage is practically significant: the 5,268 GPU-hour upfront meta-training cost amortizes after roughly 14 comparable tasks, making the framework cost-effective for teams running ongoing model-selection pipelines. The objective-agnostic design and open-source release lower the barrier to adoption. Open questions include whether landmark trajectories and allocation policies remain reliable under parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA), how to balance meta-dataset size against the cost of integrating new models, and how to handle out-of-distribution target tasks (e.g., Russian translations) where the policy degrades noticeably.
