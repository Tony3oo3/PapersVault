## Problem

Steering pretrained unconditional diffusion models toward conditions unseen during training (new classes, fine-grained species, visual attributes) typically requires either training noise-conditioned classifiers or per-step gradient backpropagation through off-the-shelf classifiers during inference, both of which impose substantial computational overhead. The paper asks whether one can achieve strong target control with a fully gradient-free online sampler whose guidance signals are constructed entirely offline from a small set of labeled examples.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264589/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen pretrained unconditional diffusion model (U-Net or transformer), a set of labeled target examples (e.g., 1,000 CIFAR-10 images per class, 1,300 ImageNet images per target class), and the full training set for PCA statistics. Offline, it produces per-target PCA denoiser parameters and a unit-norm activation-steering vector for a chosen network block. Online, it consumes only Gaussian noise and produces steered RGB images at the model's native resolution (32×32 for CIFAR-10, 256×256 for ImageNet/CelebA/Birds), using deterministic DDIM sampling with 100 steps and no classifier evaluations or backpropagation at any step.

## Method

NA-RFM combines two offline-computed, noise-level-specific guidance signals. At high noise (σ_t ≥ σ_end), a pixel-space correction is added to the denoised estimate, computed as the difference between a class-conditional PCA denoiser and an unconditional PCA denoiser, mimicking classifier-free guidance with a Gaussian approximation. At intermediate/low noise (σ_t in [σ_R^lo, σ_R^hi]), a steering direction learned via Recursive Feature Machines from forward-process activations at a reference noise level is added to a selected U-Net block's activation tensor, with a CFG-like amplification step that extrapolates from the unsteered to the steered denoised estimate. The key empirical observation enabling this design is that forward-process activation directions remain temporally stable across the intermediate/late sampling window (validated by cosine-similarity probes and a shared-covariance Gaussian analysis in Appendix A), so a single direction learned at one noise level can be reused over many timesteps. All high-dimensional eigendecompositions (PCA and RFM AGOP) are computed in sample space to handle the regime where activation dimension far exceeds the number of labeled examples.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, NA-RFM achieves 96.6% target accuracy with FID 41.4, compared to 77.1%/73.9 for TFG-4 and 86.0%/41.9 for noise-conditioned classifier guidance, while running 16× faster than TFG. On ImageNet 256×256, NA-RFM reaches 75.8% accuracy (FID 98) versus 59.8% (FID 165) for TFG-4. On fine-grained out-of-distribution bird species, NA-RFM achieves 14.1% accuracy (FID 72) versus 2.2% (FID 259) for TFG-4. On CelebA-HQ multi-attribute guidance, NA-RFM attains 96.0% average accuracy for gender+age and 83.3% for gender+hair, outperforming TFG on 7 of 8 attribute combinations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NA-RFM outperforms all seven baselines on every benchmark task. Against the primary baseline TFG-4 (Ye et al., 2024), NA-RFM gains +19.5 pp accuracy on CIFAR-10 (96.6% vs 77.1%), +16.0 pp on ImageNet (75.8% vs 59.8%), and +11.9 pp on fine-grained birds (14.1% vs 2.2%), while also improving FID in all three cases (41.4 vs 73.9; 98 vs 165; 72 vs 259). Against noise-conditioned classifier guidance (Nichol & Dhariwal, 2021), NA-RFM achieves higher accuracy (96.6% vs 86.0%) and marginally better FID (41.4 vs 41.9) on CIFAR-10, while being 16× faster in wall-clock time. The remaining training-free gradient baselines—DPS (Chung et al., 2023), LGD (Song et al., 2023), FreeDoM (Yu et al., 2023), MPGD (He et al., 2024), and UGD (Bansal et al., 2023)—all fall well below NA-RFM on accuracy across all four tasks, with the largest gaps on fine-grained species guidance where they achieve 0.0–1.1% versus NA-RFM's 14.1%. On CelebA-HQ, NA-RFM exceeds TFG on 7 of 8 attribute combinations, with the largest gain on Old+Male (+30.9 pp); TFG is only higher on Female+Non-Blond (92.2% vs 86.4%). In terms of speed, NA-RFM samples at 7.90 s/image on ImageNet versus 79.70 s/image for TFG-4, a roughly 10× per-image speedup after amortizing a one-time 25-minute offline setup.

## Relation to Prior Work

NA-RFM sits at the intersection of three prior threads: (1) PCA/Gaussian denoiser approximations of diffusion models at high noise (Li et al., 2024; 2026), which the paper repurposes as a class-conditional vs. unconditional pixel-space correction; (2) Recursive Feature Machines (Radhakrishnan et al., 2024), borrowed from the language-model steering literature (Beaglehole et al., 2026; Turner et al., 2023) and adapted to high-dimensional U-Net/transformer activations via sample-space eigendecomposition; and (3) the classifier-free guidance framework (Ho & Salimans, 2022), whose interpolation structure is mimicked both by the PCA noise-alignment signal and by the RFM amplification step. The key departure from all prior post-hoc guidance methods (DPS, LGD, FreeDoM, MPGD, UGD, TFG) is that no per-step gradient is computed: both steering signals are fixed offline and applied as lightweight matrix-vector products and activation additions during sampling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 16× inference speedup over TFG combined with substantially higher target accuracy (96.6% vs. 77.1% on CIFAR-10, 75.8% vs. 59.8% on ImageNet) makes gradient-free post-hoc steering practically viable for settings where per-step backpropagation is prohibitive. The method's generality across U-Net, transformer (SiT-XL/2), and latent-diffusion (Stable Diffusion 1.5) backbones suggests the offline-direction / online-activation-edit paradigm is architecture-agnostic. Limitations include the requirement for labeled target examples (ruling out purely text-specified novel concepts), modest absolute accuracy on fine-grained out-of-distribution targets (14.1% on Birds-525), and the fact that the CelebA multi-attribute setting still lags TFG on one of eight combinations. Open questions include whether the approach extends to more complex compositional conditions, whether the RFM direction can be learned from fewer or unlabeled examples, and whether the temporal-transfer property (Figure 3) holds for deeper or differently parameterized samplers.
