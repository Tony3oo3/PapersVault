## Problem

Slot Attention (SA), the core aggregation mechanism in Object-Centric Learning, suffers from two unaddressed issues: (i) cold-start queries on images or video's first frame lack sample-specific cues, hindering precise object aggregation; and (ii) in video OCL, the homogeneous aggregation transform (identical 3 SA iterations) is applied to both non-informative first-frame queries and already-informative non-first-frame queries, failing to adapt to their significant information gap.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266075/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images at 224×224 resolution (encoded by DINO2 ViT-S/14) or sequences of video frames, and produces n object slots (n×c vectors) along with corresponding segmentation masks (n×h×w). For video, non-first-frame queries are recurrently transitioned from the previous frame's slots via a learned transitioner, while the first frame uses the same cold-start initialization as the image case. The model is instantiated on DIAS for images and RandSF.Q for videos, with the preheater adding negligible overhead (a single Transformer decoder block).

## Method

SmoothSA introduces two techniques to smooth SA iterations and recurrences. First, a tiny preheater (a single Transformer decoder block with self- and cross-attention switched) injects input-feature information into cold-start queries before SA iterations begin; it is trained via self-distillation with an MSE loss between preheated queries and stopped-gradient slots, and gradient flow from the SA module to preheated queries is severed to disentangle training. Second, the homogeneous aggregation transform is differentiated by applying 3 full SA iterations on the first frame (where queries are non-informative) but only 1 SA iteration on each non-first frame (where queries are already sample-specific), while keeping SA parameters shared across all frames to enforce general aggregation capability. The theoretical motivation draws on the Banach fixed-point theorem: preheating brings queries closer to the contraction's fixed point, so fewer iterations suffice for convergence, and the gradient analysis shows that non-first frames need fewer iterations because their reconstruction gradients are already small.

## Results

On image OCL (Table 1), SmoothSA^i achieves ARI 29.3 / mBO 33.4 / mIoU 31.8 on COCO and ARI 35.0 / mBO 45.2 / mIoU 43.9 on VOC, outperforming the prior SotA DIAS by ~3.7 ARI and ~1.7 mIoU on both real-world datasets. On video OCL (Table 2), SmoothSA^v achieves ARI 42.4 / mBO 38.9 / mIoU 38.3 on YTVIS-HQ and ARI 42.0 / mBO 36.0 / mIoU 34.9 on YTVIS-2022, surpassing the prior SotA RandSF.Q by ~2.3 ARI and ~1.1 mIoU on YTVIS-HQ. On downstream tasks, SmoothSA^i + Aloe improves GQA VQA accuracy from 52.3% to 56.7%, and SmoothSA^v + Aloe improves CLEVRER per-question accuracy from 95.6% to 96.9%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On image OCL, SmoothSA^i decisively outperforms all earlier baselines: SLATE (ARI 17.5 on COCO), DINOSAUR (18.2), SlotDiffusion (17.7), and SPOT (23.7) are all surpassed by 6+ ARI points on COCO. Against the closest competitor DIAS (the base model SmoothSA is built upon), SmoothSA^i wins on COCO (29.3 vs 25.6 ARI) and VOC (35.0 vs 30.9 ARI) but trails slightly on the synthetic ClevrTex (76.8 vs 80.9 ARI). On video OCL, SmoothSA^v outperforms STEVE, VideoSAUR, and SlotContrast by large margins on YTVIS (e.g., 42.4 vs 37.2 ARI over SlotContrast on YTVIS-HQ). Against the strongest video baseline RandSF.Q, SmoothSA^v wins on YTVIS-HQ (42.4 vs 40.1 ARI) and YTVIS-2022 (42.0 vs 37.9 ARI) but shows mixed results on synthetic MOVi datasets where RandSF.Q leads on ARI while SmoothSA^v leads on the stricter mIoU metric (e.g., 30.2 vs 25.6 mIoU on MOVi-D). On downstream VQA, SmoothSA^i + Aloe beats SPOT + Aloe by 4.4 points on GQA, and SmoothSA^v + Aloe beats SlotContrast + Aloe by 1.3 points on CLEVRER per-question accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Slot Attention (SA) framework that underpins mainstream Object-Centric Learning, building directly on DIAS for image OCL and RandSF.Q for video OCL as its base architectures. It departs from the entire SA lineage (Locatello et al. 2020, STEVE, SAVi, SlotContrast, etc.) by explicitly identifying and addressing two structural issues that prior work treated as implicit: the cold-start problem where initial queries lack sample-specific information, and the transform homogeneity problem where identical SA iteration counts are applied to both non-informative first-frame queries and informative non-first-frame queries. The preheating mechanism draws on self-distillation (as in DIAS) but applies it to a lightweight Transformer decoder block that predicts slot-approximating vectors from cold-start queries, while the transform differentiation is a minimal architectural change (3 vs. 1 iterations) grounded in a Banach fixed-point contraction analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clear diagnostic for why SA-based OCL methods underperform on first frames and across frame transitions, and offers a low-overhead fix that achieves new SOTA on both image and video benchmarks with negligible additional computation. The Banach fixed-point framing gives practitioners a principled lens for understanding iterative refinement convergence, and the gradient visualization showing balanced signals across frames validates the transform differentiation beyond just accuracy numbers. Open questions remain: the 3+1 iteration split is empirical rather than adaptive, and the preheater's reliance on slot quality as its self-distillation target creates a potential failure mode if early training produces poor slots (though the authors' stress test with 99.9% corruption suggests robustness). Whether the differentiated-iteration principle generalizes to other iterative attention mechanisms beyond SA is unexplored.
