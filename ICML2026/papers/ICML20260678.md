## Problem

Self-Play Preference Optimization (SPPO) treats estimated win rates as precise regression targets, but practical reward models are overconfident, assigning extreme win probabilities even to semantically indistinguishable response pairs. This miscalibration injects high-magnitude gradient noise into the self-play loop, destabilizing optimization and causing policy degeneration rather than convergence to a meaningful Nash Equilibrium.

## Input / Output

The method consumes text prompts (approximately 60k from UltraFeedback, split into three portions for iterative training) and generates K=5 candidate responses per prompt from the current policy. A lightweight 0.4B PairRM model annotates pairwise win probabilities, while a separate semantic encoder (all-MiniLM-L6-v2, 22.7M parameters) computes cosine similarity between response pairs to gate the supervision signal. The output is an aligned LLM policy that produces text responses, evaluated via win rates against GPT-4 Turbo on AlpacaEval 2.0, MT-Bench scores, and Arena-Hard win rates.

## Method

S-SPPO reframes preference optimization as a dual-space min-max game with two calibration mechanisms. Supervision Calibration computes semantic similarity S between winner-loser pairs via an external encoder and anneals the raw win-rate target toward 0.5 proportionally to S (P_c = 0.5 + (1−S)(P̂ − 0.5)), effectively masking gradients for semantically ambiguous pairs. Representation Calibration adds a repulsive term λ·h_θ(y_w)ᵀh_θ(y_l) on the policy's own normalized final hidden states, enforcing geometric diversity in latent space to prevent manifold collapse caused by the supervision annealing. Theoretically, the paper proves that semantic calibration preserves the antisymmetry (constant-sum) property of the pairwise game, guaranteeing O(1/√T) duality-gap convergence to a Nash Equilibrium, and that the latent repulsion yields an ε-Nash Equilibrium of the unregularized game with ε ≤ 2λM.

## Results

On AlpacaEval 2.0 (GPT-4 Turbo Annotator), Llama-3-8B-S-SPPO Iter3 achieves a 52.19% win rate and 47.46% length-controlled (LC) win rate, surpassing the SPPO baseline (46.84% / 44.79%) and the base Llama-3-8B-Instruct (28.52% / 28.31%). On MT-Bench, S-SPPO reaches the highest average score of 8.22 (vs. 7.93 for SPPO Iter3 on Llama-3), and on Arena-Hard-Auto it achieves 31.5 (vs. 29.8 for SPPO Iter3), demonstrating stable improvement across iterations where SPPO degrades. On GSM8K, S-SPPO preserves reasoning capability (78.9% strict-match on Llama-3-8B vs. 76.4% base).

## Baselines & Comparisons

S-SPPO outperforms SPPO (Wu et al., 2025) on every benchmark: on Llama-3-8B AlpacaEval 2.0 (GPT-4 Turbo), S-SPPO Iter3 reaches 47.46% LC win rate vs. SPPO's 44.79%, and on MT-Bench S-SPPO maintains a stable 8.22 average while SPPO degrades from 8.01 to 7.93 across iterations. Against Iterative DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) and Iterative IPO (Azar et al., 2024), S-SPPO achieves substantially higher LC win rates (47.46% vs. ~24% for DPO Iter3 and ~20% for IPO Iter3 on Mistral-7B) and avoids the multi-turn degradation seen in those methods on MT-Bench. Compared to Self-Rewarding LM (Yuan et al., 2024), which uses the LLM itself as judge, S-SPPO achieves 40.26% win rate on Mistral-7B vs. 20.44% for Self-Rewarding 70B Iter3. Over the base models (Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2 and Llama-3-8B-Instruct), S-SPPO more than doubles the LC win rate on Mistral-7B (18.84% → 36.13%) and nearly doubles it on Llama-3-8B (28.31% → 47.46%). On Arena-Hard-Auto, S-SPPO also avoids the performance collapse observed in SPPO on Llama-3 (31.0 → 29.8), instead improving steadily to 31.5.

## Relation to Prior Work

S-SPPO extends SPPO (Wu et al., 2025), which itself reframes DPO's Bradley-Terry-based preference optimization as a two-player constant-sum game to handle intransitive human preferences. The key departure is identifying that SPPO's regression objective is destabilized when the preference oracle (e.g., PairRM) assigns overconfident win rates to semantically indistinguishable response pairs, and proposing a dual-space fix: semantic gating in the label space (analogous in spirit to Tilted ERM's outlier suppression but guided by semantic similarity rather than loss magnitude) and latent repulsion in the representation space. The theoretical contribution shows this calibration preserves the constant-sum game structure, so Nash Equilibrium convergence guarantees carry over from SPPO.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical failure mode in iterative self-play alignment that was previously unacknowledged: performance degradation across iterations due to reward-model miscalibration on ambiguous pairs. The dual-space calibration is lightweight (≈5 s overhead, ~6 GB extra memory) and requires no additional human-annotated data, making it a drop-in stabilization for existing SPPO pipelines. Open questions include whether the fixed λ and off-the-shelf semantic encoder generalize to specialized domains (math, code) where token-level semantics matter, and whether dynamic λ scheduling or end-to-end encoder learning could further improve the equilibrium. The framework's reliance on an external encoder as a semantic anchor also raises questions about circularity if the encoder's notion of similarity diverges from the policy's internal representation.
