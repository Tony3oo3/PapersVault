## Problem

Evaluating agentic AI on open-ended professional tasks faces a fundamental stability-adaptivity dilemma: static rubrics provide reproducible criteria but cannot accommodate diverse valid response strategies, while LLM-as-a-judge methods adapt to individual responses yet suffer from stochastic variance, systemic biases, and a lack of domain-calibrated evaluation principles. No existing framework simultaneously achieves expert-grounded rigor and report-specific adaptivity, and no benchmark captures the temporal dynamics and open-endedness of real professional workflows like strategic sourcing.

## Input / Output

JADE takes a user query q (annotated with a hierarchical multi-label taxonomy of primary intent, information needs, and operational constraints), an agent-generated response report r, and a pre-defined set of expert-authored evaluation skills S as input. It produces a final score S(q,r) ∈ [0,1] decomposed into a reasoning score (aggregated from signed weighted checklist items scored 0/0.5/1) and an evidence score (mean verification reliability from real-time web checks), along with per-item structured feedback including source credibility grades and dependency-gating decisions that zero out conclusions built on refuted claims.

## Method

JADE is a two-layer evaluation framework that decouples stable expert-grounded criteria from adaptive report-specific verification. Layer 1 performs deterministic skill activation via taxonomy-based multi-label mapping to compose a query-level rubric, from which an LLM generates a query-specific checklist of atomic Yes/No questions with signed weights (positive for quality requirements, negative for critical flaws). Layer 2 generates a report-specific checklist typed into evidence items (verifiable factual claims) and reasoning items (judgment quality), where evidence items are validated by a Verification Agent performing real-time web search and content analysis, and reasoning items are scored by an LLM Judge conditioned on verification results. A dependency-gating mechanism explicitly links reasoning items to the evidence they rely on: if any dependent evidence item falls below a confidence threshold τ, the reasoning score is set to zero, preventing conclusions built on invalid facts from contributing to the final score. The overall score is the product of the clipped reasoning score and the evidence score, ensuring that strong reasoning unsupported by reliable evidence—or reliable evidence paired with poor reasoning—is consistently penalized.

## Results

On BizBench (150 strategic sourcing queries), the average final score across 18 agent configurations is 42.6%, with Gemini Deep Research leading at 57.1% and Shopping Research at 56.2%—both well below the 80%+ threshold expected for professional deliverables. JADE's structured evaluation reveals a systematic Evidence-Reasoning Gap: average evidence score is 84.0% while reasoning averages only 50.5% (a 33.5 pp gap), and source credibility lags at 48.2%. For evaluation quality, JADE achieves Pearson r=0.858 with human experts (vs. r=0.667 for vanilla LLM-as-Judge), a 28.6% improvement, with low run-to-run variance (σ=1.12%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is vanilla LLM-as-a-Judge (pointwise scoring, following Zheng et al., 2023), which achieves Pearson r=0.667 and Spearman ρ=0.736 with human experts. JADE outperforms this baseline by a wide margin: r=0.858 (+28.6%) and ρ=0.862, while also reducing run-to-run variance from 1.45% to 1.12%. Ablation confirms both layers contribute: skill activation alone (Layer 1) reaches r=0.844, report-specific checklist alone (Layer 2) reaches r=0.786, and their combination yields the full r=0.858 with an additional +1.4% synergy. JADE also produces stricter, better-calibrated absolute scores compared to the vanilla judge, which systematically inflates scores relative to human experts. On cross-domain transfer (DR.BENCH, 214 tasks, 10 domains), JADE with auto-summarized skills achieves r=0.736 / ρ=0.789 overall, with significant correlations in 9 of 10 domains; the weakest domain (Sports & Competitions, ρ=0.300, p=0.297) involves closed-form named-entity recall rather than open-ended professional judgment.

## Relation to Prior Work

JADE synthesizes and departs from two existing evaluation paradigms: expert-authored rubrics (HealthBench, DR.BENCH) that provide rigor but incur per-query human cost, and LLM-as-a-judge methods (Zheng et al., 2023) that adapt to individual responses but suffer from stochastic variance and lack domain calibration. Rather than building directly on either, JADE introduces a compositional two-layer design—deterministic skill activation for stable criteria (Layer 1) and report-specific claim-level verification with evidence-dependency gating (Layer 2)—that explicitly decouples domain-general principles from case-specific evidence. The dependency gating mechanism, which zeros out reasoning scores when their factual premises fail verification, is a novel structural constraint not present in prior checklist-based or holistic evaluation approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

JADE's structured evaluation reveals two systematic agent failure modes—the evidence-reasoning gap (84% evidence vs. 50.5% reasoning) and citation laundering (48.2% source credibility despite 84% factual accuracy)—that holistic LLM evaluators miss, providing actionable diagnostic signals for agent developers. The cross-domain transfer to DR.BENCH and HealthBench demonstrates generalizability, though the weaker HealthBench results (ρ=0.228 full, 0.530 with 20% trimming) and the Sports domain failure on DR.BENCH delineate a clear boundary: JADE excels at open-ended professional judgment but struggles with exhaustive factual recall. Open questions include whether auto-summarized skills can eventually match expert-authored ones, how the framework handles multi-turn interactive reports, and whether the per-query inference cost (~$0.126, 19.6 LLM calls) is acceptable for large-scale deployment.
