## Problem

Existing mixed-precision quantization methods for MoE-LLMs perform bit allocation locally at the layer level, ignoring heterogeneous importance across layers, and overlook the substantial router distribution shifts induced by expert quantization (over 40% of tokens are rerouted after 1.5-bit quantization). These two limitations result in suboptimal bit allocation and degraded routing in quantized models, particularly under extreme low-bit settings.

## Input / Output

The method consumes full-precision MoE-LLM weights, a calibration set of 128 sequences (2048 tokens each, typically from C4 or a C4+MATH mix), and a target bits-per-expert budget (e.g., 2.5 bpe). It produces per-expert quantized weights at 1, 2, or 3 bits (determined by a global LP) with attention fixed at 4 bits, plus fine-tuned router weights retained at 16-bit precision. The LP uses a candidate bit set B={1,2,3} and enforces a per-layer constraint requiring at least one highest-bit expert, while the router fine-tuning uses the same calibration set with AdamW at lr=1e-4 for one epoch.

## Method

GEMQ formulates expert bit-width allocation as a global binary linear program where each expert's importance is estimated via an output-space error proxy (Eq. 6) combining local quantization perturbations with layer-output gradients under a diagonal FIM approximation, enabling direct cross-layer comparison without hand-tuned heuristic coefficients. After GPTQ quantization, a parameter-efficient router fine-tuning step updates only the router weights (<0.04% of total parameters) to minimize cross-entropy loss, adapting routing to the altered expert landscape. A progressive quantization framework then iterates from high to low bit budgets, using the previously fine-tuned quantized model (rather than the FP model) for importance estimation at each stage, which mitigates Taylor approximation inaccuracy caused by abrupt loss changes in the MoE loss landscape under large quantization errors.

## Results

On Mixtral-8×7B quantized to 2.5 bits per expert, GEMQ achieves 5.03 WikiText2 perplexity and 65.13% average zero-shot accuracy, outperforming PMQ (5.10 / 64.34%) and reducing model size from 87 GB to 16 GB (82% reduction) with only a 7% drop on 5-shot MMLU. At the extreme 1.5-bit setting, GEMQ achieves 7.93 WikiText2 perplexity and 52.00% zero-shot accuracy, while SpQR completely collapses (infinite perplexity). The 2.5-bit quantized Mixtral-8×7B deploys on a single NVIDIA H100 GPU at 82.5 tokens/sec decoding speed.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GEMQ consistently outperforms the uniform quantization baseline across all models and bit widths, with the gap widening at lower precision (e.g., 1.5-bit Mixtral: 7.93 vs. 10.67 WikiText2 perplexity). Against the closest competitor PMQ (layer-wise local LP allocation), GEMQ wins on most metrics, with the largest gains at 1.5 bits where global allocation and router fine-tuning matter most (e.g., 7.93 vs. 8.47 WikiText2, 16.20 vs. 20.77 C4 on Mixtral). Against SpQR (sub-tensor-level mixed precision for dense LLMs), GEMQ slightly wins at 2.5 bits (5.03 vs. 5.40 WikiText2) and dramatically outperforms at 1.5 bits where SpQR collapses to infinite perplexity. Against the uniform MoE-LLM methods MoEQuant and EAQuant (both targeting ≥3 bpe), GEMQ achieves lower perplexity and higher zero-shot accuracy at comparable or more aggressive bit budgets (e.g., GEMQ 3-bit: 4.37 WikiText2 / 59.49% 0-shot vs. MoEQuant 3-bit: 4.90 / 57.24%; GEMQ 3-bit: 4.37 / 76.10% vs. EAQuant 3-bit: 5.27 / 71.23%).

## Relation to Prior Work

GEMQ extends the layer-wise local linear programming formulation of PMQ (Huang et al., 2024a) into a global LP that jointly optimizes expert bit-widths across all layers, using a task-loss-based importance proxy derived from the output-space error transformation of BRECQ (Li et al., 2021). It departs from prior MoE quantization work in two key ways: (1) replacing heuristic, layer-local importance coefficients with a hyperparameter-free global formulation grounded in quantization error analysis, and (2) introducing a post-quantization router fine-tuning step that prior methods (including PMQ, EAQuant, and MoEQuant) largely overlook. The progressive quantization framework further integrates these two components, using a previously quantized-and-fine-tuned model as the basis for importance estimation at lower bit budgets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GEMQ makes extreme low-bit MoE deployment practical: reducing Mixtral-8×7B from 87 GB to 16 GB (82% reduction) while preserving ~93% of 5-shot MMLU accuracy, and enabling single-H100 inference at 82.5 tok/s. The router fine-tuning component is particularly attractive for practitioners because it costs <0.04% of parameters and <5% of quantization time yet yields the largest single ablation gain, especially at 1.5 bits. Open questions remain around calibration data sensitivity (domain-mismatched calibration degrades math reasoning) and the peak GPU memory overhead of gradient computation, which may limit applicability to very large MoE models on single-GPU setups. A more principled approach to calibration data construction and memory-efficient gradient estimation are natural next steps.
