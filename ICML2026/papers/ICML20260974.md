## Problem

Current multimodal RAG systems suffer from two coupled failures: flat chunking severs document-native structure and cross-modal alignment (e.g., separating a figure from its caption and referring text), producing semantic fragments that are hard to interpret in isolation; and iterative retrieval over long contexts loops on partial evidence or drifts into irrelevant sections because each step is guided only by the current snippet with no persistent global search state. G²-Reader addresses both by jointly restructuring how evidence is represented and how retrieval-reasoning is orchestrated.

## Input / Output

The system takes a natural-language query and a set of multimodal documents (each paired with five candidate documents containing relevant evidence and distractors on VisDoMBench) as input. Documents are parsed via MinerU into atomic units—paragraphs and multimodal elements (tables/figures with captions)—preserving layout and reading order. The output is a single grounded answer generated by a VLM reasoner conditioned on the original query and the assembled evidence subgraph. Retrieval operates under a fixed node budget of k=5 per query, and the entire pipeline is driven by Qwen3-VL-32B-Instruct with role-specific prompts (decomposer, worker, evidence checker, reasoner).

## Method

G²-Reader couples two evolving graphs. The Content Graph (G_C) is a heterogeneous multimodal graph whose nodes are atomic document elements carrying VLM-generated summaries, keywords, and latent embeddings, and whose edges encode document-native relations (reference, cross-modal grounding, causality, etc.). Over T=3 iterations, a VLM performs joint message passing that simultaneously refines each node's attributes (enriching summaries, resolving implicit references) and its neighborhood links (adding semantically meaningful edges beyond surface adjacency), executed asynchronously for efficiency. The Planning Graph (G_P) is an agentic DAG of atomic sub-questions with dependency edges, constructed by a decomposer and executed in topological order via Kahn's algorithm. For each sub-question, a structured subgraph readout greedily selects top-ranked nodes from G_C and expands them with their immediate neighbors to form a context-aware evidence subgraph; a worker VLM then produces an intermediate answer conditioned on that subgraph plus child-node answers. An evidence checker evaluates global sufficiency, and if gaps are identified, the decomposer performs targeted CRUD operations on the DAG (adding, updating, or removing nodes) for up to τ_max=3 refinement rounds before a final reasoner synthesizes the answer from the verified evidence set.

## Results

On VisDoMBench (2,271 samples across five multimodal domains), G²-Reader with Qwen3-VL-32B-Instruct achieves 66.21% average accuracy, the best overall result among all baselines. This surpasses the standalone GPT-5 (53.08%) by 13.13 absolute points and the strongest RAG baseline VisDoMRAG (65.01%) by 1.2 points. The largest per-domain gains are on SlideVQA (72.31%, best) and PaperTab (57.10%, best), while G²-Reader trails on individual subsets such as SciGraphQA (61.56% vs. LightRAG's 75.00%) and SPIQA (73.19% vs. VisDoMRAG's 75.44%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

G²-Reader (66.21% avg) outperforms all eight RAG baselines in overall accuracy, with the largest margins over MA-RAG (+33.1 pts), RAGAnything (+14.0 pts), and GraphRAG (+15.4 pts). Among the strongest competitors, it edges out VisDoMRAG (65.01%) by 1.2 pts, LightRAG (63.74%) by 2.5 pts, and MMGraphRAG (63.40%) by 2.8 pts. However, G²-Reader does not win on every subset: VisDoMRAG leads on SPIQA (75.44 vs. 73.19), MMGraphRAG leads on FetaTab (72.40 vs. 66.89), and LightRAG leads on SciGraphQA (75.00 vs. 61.56), which the authors attribute to resolution constraints in initial graph nodes for fine-grained figure details. Against the single-VLM baselines, G²-Reader surpasses GPT-5 (53.08%) by 13.13 pts and its own backbone Qwen3-VL-32B in zero-shot mode (29.90%) by 36.3 pts, demonstrating that the dual-graph architecture compensates for the long-context limitations of medium-sized open-source models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established RAG research threads: structured document memory (GraphRAG, LightRAG, MMGraphRAG, VisDoMRAG) and agentic/iterative retrieval (Self-RAG, FLARE, IRCoT, MA-RAG). It departs from prior graph-based RAG by treating the document graph not as a static, single-pass index but as an iteratively VLM-evolved structure where both node attributes and topology co-refine over multiple rounds. It departs from prior agentic retrieval by replacing the typical linear chain-of-thought or sequential sub-question pipeline with an explicit directed acyclic graph (Planning Graph) that supports parallel sub-questions, dependency-aware execution, and iterative structural revision. The dual-graph coupling—where a fixed, evolved Content Graph provides the evidence space over which a dynamic Planning Graph navigates—is the paper's core architectural novelty, and no prior system combines both in this manner.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a 32B open-source VLM wrapped in a dual-graph architecture outperforms a standalone GPT-5 by over 13 points on multimodal document QA suggests that structured evidence organization and explicit reasoning planning can substitute for raw model scale, which is practically significant for cost-sensitive deployments. The efficiency finding that k=5 retrieved nodes with G²-Reader matches k=20 with vanilla RAG further underscores the value of structural priors over brute-force retrieval. Open questions remain around the reliability of VLM-induced edge relations (which can introduce noise), the brittleness of the evidence-sufficiency check that gates the refinement loop, and whether the approach scales to documents with extremely fine-grained visual details where initial node resolution is insufficient. The over-smoothing and over-refinement phenomena observed at higher iteration counts also suggest that the current fixed-iteration schedule is suboptimal and that adaptive stopping criteria are needed.
