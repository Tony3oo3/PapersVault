## Problem

LLMs frequently fail on complex tables with multi-level nested headers, merged cells, and irregular layouts because existing representations—flat linearization, schema-aligned programmatic modeling, and geometry-driven topology reconstruction—either collapse hierarchical dependencies into one-dimensional sequences, assume canonical N×M grid structures, or ignore the synergy between spatial positioning and linguistic semantics. This leads to misalignment between structural semantics and textual representations, causing incorrect reasoning on non-standard tables.

## Input / Output

The framework takes as input a complex table where each cell has been pre-identified with a semantic role (column header, row header, or data) along with a natural-language question. It produces a structure-aware textual representation that reconstructs the full multi-level semantic lineage of each data cell, which is then fed to an LLM to generate the final answer. The entire pipeline operates in a zero-shot setting with no task-specific training; LLMs are invoked as semantic arbitrators at two distinct stages (edge validation during tree induction and final representation selection).

## Method

OHD factorizes a complex table into two independent hierarchical structures—a column tree and a row tree—via Orthogonal Tree Induction (OTI), which enforces a semantic-agency constraint (only column headers branch in the column tree; only row headers branch in the row tree) and validates each parent-child edge through a joint spatial-containment and LLM-judged semantic-subsumption predicate. A dual-pathway association protocol then reconstructs each data cell's semantic lineage by performing a DFS on one tree for premise context and a bottom-up traversal on the orthogonal tree for attribute context, yielding two complementary textual representations (column-first and row-first). Finally, an LLM-based semantic arbitration stage selects and refines the representation best suited to the specific question, optimizing for logical cohesion, information completeness, and syntactic readability before the answer is generated.

## Results

Using Qwen2-72B as the backbone, OHD achieves 69.34 EM and 89.12 LLM Eval Avg. on AITQA, surpassing the strongest baseline ST-RAPTOR by +8.76 EM and +18.09 LLM Eval. On HiTab and its curated Subset, OHD reaches 60.16 and 64.74 EM respectively, leading all baselines by 6.31 and 9.01 points over ST-RAPTOR. With the TableLLaMA-7B backbone, OHD attains a peak EM of 73.78 on AITQA and 68.37 on HiTab Subset, though it slightly trails the vanilla backbone on full HiTab (63.62 vs. 64.71) due to token overhead from explicit structural decomposition.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Chain-of-Table (flat serialization), OHD gains +19.02 EM on AITQA and +15.90 EM on HiTab Subset with Qwen2-72B, confirming that one-dimensional Markdown linearization loses critical hierarchical dependencies. Compared to E5 (embedding-optimized serialization), OHD leads by +13.03 EM on AITQA and +20.25 EM on HiTab, with E5 showing the weakest LLM Eval scores overall (47.93 on HiTab), indicating severe structural collapse. Versus ST-RAPTOR (geometry-driven topology reconstruction), OHD improves by +8.76 EM and +18.09 LLM Eval on AITQA, demonstrating that spatial-semantic co-constraints outperform purely geometric heuristics for resolving misaligned headers. Against TableLLaMA-7B (schema-alignment paradigm), OHD achieves +5.43 EM on AITQA and +1.62 EM on HiTab Subset, but slightly underperforms on full HiTab (−1.09 EM) due to context truncation from the token cost of explicit dual-tree decomposition, suggesting a trade-off between representation fidelity and sequence-length constraints for smaller models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from three established paradigms for complex table understanding—flat serialization (E5, Chain-of-Table), schema-alignment modeling (TableLLaMA), and geometry-driven topology reconstruction (ST-RAPTOR, GraphOtter)—by proposing an orthogonal decomposition into independent row and column hierarchical trees. The closest conceptual ancestor is TUTA's tree-based table representation, but OHD extends this by (1) decoupling the single tree into two orthogonal trees to isolate structural noise, (2) introducing an LLM-based semantic predicate (C_semantic) to adjudicate parent-child edges beyond pure spatial containment, and (3) designing a dual-pathway association protocol that symmetrically reconstructs cross-dimensional semantic lineage for each data cell. The framework is explicitly positioned as a structure-preserving input representation layer that is agnostic to the downstream LLM backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The orthogonal decomposition offers a clean, modular principle for handling the two dominant failure modes of complex tables (hierarchical collapse and spatial-logical discontinuity) without requiring end-to-end retraining, making it a practical plug-and-play preprocessing step. However, the paper acknowledges a token-overhead limitation: on the full HiTab set with TableLLaMA-7B, OHD's EM actually drops slightly below the vanilla backbone due to context truncation, suggesting the approach may struggle with very large tables where the explicit structural expansion exceeds the model's context window. Open questions include whether the framework scales to ultra-large financial reports, whether the LLM-as-arbitrator step can be replaced by lighter-weight heuristics to reduce inference cost, and whether multimodal layout signals could further improve the semantic agency identification that currently relies on pre-labeled cell roles.
