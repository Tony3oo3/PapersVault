## Problem

Spectral and walk-based positional encodings for graph neural networks are theoretically equivalent in their complete forms, but practitioners must use truncated variants (first k eigenvectors or k adjacency powers) to avoid O(n³) computational costs. The expressive power of these truncated encodings was entirely uncharacterized, leaving practitioners without guidance on which truncated PE to use or whether any single family suffices.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261141/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes undirected graphs with node features and optional edge features, and computes relative positional encodings (RPEs) that assign a value to every pair of nodes. These RPEs are injected into a Graphormer-GD transformer (via MLP or DeepSets modules before the attention layer) or used as edge features in a GINE MPNN. On BREC, the output is a learned node embedding for contrastive graph isomorphism classification; on ZINC-12k, the output is a scalar molecular solubility prediction. PE dimensionality is fixed at 8 for fair comparison across families.

## Method

The paper proves that truncated spectral PEs (k-EP-WL), walk PEs (k-Walk-WL), and k-harmonic distances have fundamentally different expressive powers: constant-sized walk PEs can distinguish graphs that Ω(n)-sized spectral PEs cannot, and Ω(n)-sized spectral PEs can be weaker than the 1-WL test. It further shows that effective resistance (1-harmonic distance) is strictly weaker than eigenspace projections on weighted graphs, and introduces the k-harmonic distance family as a natural bridge between spectral and polynomial encodings, proving that [2n]-harmonic distances recover the full EP-WL expressivity. Empirically, the paper demonstrates on BREC and ZINC-12k that concatenating PEs from different truncated families (e.g., walks + k-harmonics) consistently outperforms any single family, validating the theoretical finding that no single truncated PE dominates.

## Results

On ZINC-12k (graph regression, lower MAE is better), the mixture of Walks + k-harmonics (4+4 dimensions) achieves the best test MAE of 0.064±0.002, outperforming k-harmonics alone (0.076±0.006), Walks alone (0.082±0.003), and Projections alone (0.117±0.005). On the BREC expressivity benchmark, the biharmonic (2-harmonic) distance achieves 55% total accuracy across graph families, surpassing all k-EP-WL variants (best: 48% at k=7) and the resistance distance (54.25%), while approaching the 3-WL upper bound on Basic, Regular, and Extension graph families.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four PE families: eigenspace projections (Lim et al., 2023), walk encodings (Ma et al., 2023), k-harmonic distances (Black et al., 2024a), and their mixtures. On ZINC-12k, the Walks + k-harmonics mixture (0.064 MAE) beats k-harmonics alone (0.076) by 16%, Walks alone (0.082) by 22%, and Projections alone (0.117) by 45%. On BREC, the biharmonic distance (55% total) outperforms all truncated eigenspace projections (k-EP-WL, best 48% at k=7) and the resistance distance (54.25%), confirming that different k-harmonic orders capture complementary structure. The 3-WL test (Wang & Zhang, 2024) serves as the theoretical upper bound, achieving 100% on Basic/Extension and 60% on CFI graphs, which no truncated PE matches on CFI. Notably, Projections + k-harmonics (0.078) does not improve over k-harmonics alone (0.076), suggesting the inductive biases of eigenspace projections are redundant when k-harmonics are present.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the expressivity analysis of positional encodings (PEs) for graph transformers from complete to truncated forms. Prior work (Lim et al., 2023; Ma et al., 2023; Black et al., 2024b; Gai et al., 2025) established that complete spectral (eigenspace projection) and walk-based (adjacency matrix powers) PEs are equivalent in expressive power, both lying between 1-WL and 3-WL. This paper departs from that equivalence by showing truncated versions of these families are fundamentally different: constant-sized walk PEs can distinguish graphs that Ω(n)-sized spectral PEs cannot, and vice versa. The k-harmonic distances from Black et al. (2024a) are repurposed here as a new PE family bridging spectral and walk encodings, with a Sparse-ψ-WL test introduced to analyze their power for MPNNs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that practitioners using truncated PEs (the standard approach due to O(n³) cost of complete PEs) have no single 'best' family; the paper's recommendation to mix PEs from different families is supported by both theory and experiments on BREC and ZINC-12k. A key open question is whether the resistance-WL test is weaker than 1-WL on unweighted graphs (Conjecture B.1), which would disprove a conjecture by Zhang et al. (2023) that resistance encodes the full spectrum. The empirical evaluation is limited to two benchmarks and fixed dimensionality (8), leaving open how these findings scale to larger, more diverse graph datasets and whether the theoretical worst-case separations manifest in typical downstream tasks.
