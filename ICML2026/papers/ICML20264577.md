## Problem

Discrete flow models have demonstrated superior performance over discrete diffusion models for learning distributions over discrete state spaces, yet their convergence properties and error analysis remain largely unexplored. Existing theoretical work on discrete diffusion decomposes errors into initialization, score estimation, and discretization components, but discrete flow models have a fundamentally different error structure where transition rate estimation error (tied to the early stopping parameter) has received little attention. This paper provides the first comprehensive non-asymptotic error analysis for discrete flow models.

## Input / Output

The method takes i.i.d. samples from the target data distribution over a D-dimensional discrete state space S^D (vocabulary size |S|) and a source distribution (uniform or masked) as input. The learned transition rate is a sparse matrix where only Hamming-distance-1 transitions are nonzero, parameterized by a ReLU neural network with a clipping parameter m_n. Sampling produces discrete state vectors from the estimated distribution at time 1−τ via the uniformization algorithm, which guarantees zero discretization error but requires a time partition with interval-specific rate upper bounds λ_k.

## Method

The paper establishes a unified theoretical framework grounded in stochastic calculus for continuous-time Markov chains, using a Girsanov-type theorem for path measures of two CTMCs to bound the KL divergence between marginal distributions in terms of Bregman divergences of the transition rates. Training uses a generator matching objective with Bregman divergence (F(x) = x log x) minimized via empirical risk minimization over a ReLU network class, while sampling uses uniformization to eliminate discretization error. The estimation error is decomposed into three components: stochastic error (from ERM, bounded via empirical process theory and covering numbers), approximation error (of the ReLU function class under Hölder smoothness), and early stopping error (from truncating the time horizon at 1−τ). Without a boundedness condition on the oracle rate, the total variation error scales as (nτ²)^{−β/(2(2β+D+1))} + τD; under a lower-boundedness assumption on the oracle rate, the rate improves to (nτ²)^{−β/(2β+D+1)} + τD, which is nearly optimal in sample size.

## Results

The paper establishes the first non-asymptotic total variation error bounds for discrete flow models. Without a boundedness condition on the oracle rate (Theorem 5), the TV error scales as O(|S|^{β+1} D^{⌊β⌋+1} τ^{-1/2} (nτ²)^{-β/(2(2β+D+1))} + CτD). Under the boundedness condition (Theorem 7), a faster rate is achieved: O(|S|^{β+1} D^{⌊β⌋+3/2} τ^{-1} (nτ²)^{-β/(2β+D+1)} + CτD), which is nearly optimal in sample size, matching the nonparametric regression rate of Stone (1982) up to logarithmic factors. Simulations confirm that the estimation error is nearly linear in dimension D, decreases with sample size n, and the total variation is minimized around τ ≈ 0.03–0.05.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper with no direct method-vs-method comparison table; instead, it positions its error bounds relative to prior theoretical results. Compared to Su et al. (2025), which established a TV bound of O(|S|^{7d₀} n^{-1/(9|S|d₀)} (log n)^{1/(9|S|d₀)}) for Transformer-based discrete flow matching, the present work avoids the exponential dependence on vocabulary size |S| and explicitly accounts for early stopping error, which Su et al. do not consider. The early stopping error rate (CτD) matches the discrete diffusion result of Zhang et al. (2025) under a linear schedule, while the stochastic error bound aligns with the continuous flow matching analysis of Gao et al. (2024b). Unlike discrete diffusion analyses (Chen & Ying, 2024; Ren et al., 2025a), the discrete flow framework incurs zero initialization error and zero discretization error (via uniformization), simplifying the error decomposition to three terms: stochastic, approximation, and early stopping. The fast rate under boundedness (Theorem 7) parallels the fast rate in continuous flow matching (Zhou & Liu, 2025), providing a discrete analogue.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the error analysis framework developed for continuous flow matching (Gao et al., 2024b; Zhou & Liu, 2025) and discrete diffusion models (Zhang et al., 2025; Ren et al., 2025a,b) to the discrete flow model family (Campbell et al., 2024; Gat et al., 2024). It adopts the generator matching training objective from Holderrieth et al. (2025) and the uniformization sampling algorithm from Chen & Ying (2024), combining them with a Girsanov-type theorem for CTMCs to obtain a unified KL-divergence-based error decomposition. A key structural departure from discrete diffusion is the absence of initialization error: because discrete flow interpolates directly from source to target without a separate noising-then-reversal process, truncating the time horizon introduces only early stopping error, not the additional initialization error that plagues discrete diffusion analyses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first non-asymptotic error analysis for discrete flow models, filling a notable theoretical gap given the model family's growing adoption in language, graph, and video generation. The nearly optimal sample-size rate under the boundedness condition (Theorem 7) and the explicit decomposition into stochastic, approximation, and early stopping errors provide concrete guidance for hyperparameter selection (τ, m_n, network architecture). However, the analysis is limited to ERM without optimization error, relies on computationally expensive uniformization for zero discretization error, and the practical relevance of the bounds for large-scale systems (large D, large |S|) remains unclear. Open questions include extending the analysis to efficient samplers like τ-leaping, incorporating self-attention networks into the approximation error, and jointly analyzing optimization and statistical errors.
