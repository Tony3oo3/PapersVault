## Problem

Despite widespread empirical evidence that DP-SGD training benefits substantially from enhanced features (e.g., pre-trained representations), no theoretical framework has explained how feature learning dynamics unfold under differential privacy. Existing DP analyses focus on utility bounds for convex or non-convex optimization but overlook the critical distinction between label-dependent feature signals and label-independent data noise, leaving the role of signal-to-noise ratio in private training uncharacterized.

## Input / Output

The theoretical model consumes binary-labeled multi-patch inputs x = [y·v, ξ] ∈ (ℝ^d)², where the first patch y·v encodes the label-dependent feature signal and the second patch ξ ~ N(0, σ_ξ²H) is label-independent Gaussian noise orthogonal to v. The privacy budget (ε, δ) governs the scale of Gaussian noise injected at each NoisyGD step. The output is the trained two-layer CNN weight matrix W, whose filters are decomposed into a signal-learning component (coefficient Γ) and a noise-memorization component (coefficients Φ), enabling separate tracking of what the model has learned from signal versus noise.

## Method

The paper develops the first theoretical framework analyzing NoisyGD (a simplified DP-SGD) through a feature-learning lens, using a two-layer CNN with polynomial ReLU activation σ(z) = max{0,z}^q (q > 2) on multi-patch data. The key technical contribution is a decomposition of each convolution filter w_{j,r}^{(t)} into initial weights, a signal-learning coefficient Γ_{j,r}^{(t)} along the v direction, noise-memorization coefficients Φ_{j,r,i}^{(t)} along each ξ_i direction, and accumulated private noise, with iterative update equations for each component. By bounding the cumulative effect of private noise via the signal-to-noise ratio SNR = ||v||₂/||ξ||₂, the paper proves two main results: (1) effective private signal learning requires min{SNR·nε, SNR^q·n} ≥ Õ(1), meaning a higher SNR is needed compared to non-private training (which only requires SNR^q·n ≥ Õ(1)); and (2) data noise memorization that occurs in non-private learning persists in private learning whenever ε ≥ SNR^{1-q}·n^{-1}, causing poor generalization despite small training loss. Experiments on synthetic data and CIFAR-10 (with ResNet-20 and controlled noise corruption) validate these theoretical predictions across varying privacy budgets and SNR values.

## Results

The paper's main contributions are theoretical. Under the signal learning condition min{SNR·nε, SNR^q·n} ≥ Ω̃(1), the private CNN achieves a test loss of O(κ + exp((nε)^{-1-1/q})), where κ is the training loss threshold. Under the data noise memorization condition min{SNR^{-1}·ε, SNR^{-q}·n^{-1}} ≥ Ω̃(1), the model achieves arbitrarily small training loss O(κ) but incurs a constant test loss ≥ 0.1 regardless of sample size n or privacy budget ε. On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-20, higher SNR consistently improves accuracy across all privacy budgets (ε ∈ {2, 4, 8}), with the most pronounced degradation at low SNR under strong privacy (ε = 2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare against other DP methods or feature-enhancement baselines in the traditional sense; instead, it contrasts private (NoisyGD) training against standard non-private training on the same model and data. The key finding is that private learning requires a strictly higher SNR than non-private training: even when the non-private feature learning condition (SNR^q·n ≥ Ω̃(1)) is satisfied, private learning can still fail to capture the signal if SNR·nε < Ω̃(1). Conversely, when data noise memorization occurs in non-private learning (SNR^{-q}·n^{-1} ≥ Ω̃(1)), it also occurs in private learning as long as ε ≥ SNR^{1-q}·n^{-1}, a condition that is easily met in practice. Synthetic experiments (Figure 2) confirm that at SNR = 0.6, non-private training successfully learns the signal while private training at ε = 0.2 fails and instead memorizes noise; increasing SNR to 3 restores private signal learning. The CIFAR-10 experiments (Figure 3) further validate that higher SNR improves accuracy across all privacy budgets, with the gap between high-SNR and low-SNR settings widening as ε decreases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the feature learning framework of Allen-Zhu & Li (2020) and Cao et al. (2022)—which decompose learned weights into signal and noise components for non-private CNN training—into the differentially private setting via NoisyGD. It departs from the two prior theoretical studies of features in private learning (Sun et al., 2023, which restricted to linear classifiers; Wang et al., 2024, which assumed neural collapse/ETF structure) by analyzing a two-layer CNN with polynomial ReLU and explicitly separating label-dependent signal from label-independent data noise, a distinction absent from prior DP-SGD analyses that focused on utility bounds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical explanation for why feature enhancement (e.g., pre-trained features, data augmentation) improves DP-SGD performance: it raises the SNR, which is a necessary condition for private signal learning that is stricter than its non-private counterpart. A key open question is whether these SNR-based thresholds and the noise-memorization amplification effect generalize beyond the simplified two-layer CNN with polynomial ReLU to deeper architectures, standard ReLU, and practical DP-SGD with per-example clipping. The analysis also assumes an over-parameterized regime and binary classification, leaving multi-class and under-parameterized settings unaddressed.
