## Problem

Alimohammadi et al. (2023) showed that standard auctions like SPA fail to be Auto-bidding Incentive Compatible (AIC) because their definition requires the worst-case truthful equilibrium to dominate the best-case deviation equilibrium — an overly stringent criterion that conflates equilibrium selection with strategic incentives. This paper asks whether, under more natural and consistent equilibrium-selection rules (always comparing worst-to-worst or best-to-best), standard auctions can still be shown to incentivize truthful reporting of tCPA constraints.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes as input a combinatorial auction instance: a set of advertisers each with a tCPA target T_i, a set of n queries with per-advertiser values v_{i,j}, and a mechanism (SPA or FPA). It produces (i) a proof that the mechanism satisfies RAIC and/or OAIC, (ii) a closed-form characterization of when an equilibrium with a specific allocation N_k exists, expressed purely as inequalities over reported targets and query values (no bid multipliers), and (iii) a total ordering N_0 ≺ N_1 ≺ … ≺ N_n on equilibria that corresponds to advertiser ordinal preferences. The analysis is split into two regimes — non-uniform bidding (arbitrary per-query bids) and uniform bidding (b_{i,j} = μ_i T_i v_{i,j}) — with the uniform case restricted to two advertisers.

## Method

For non-uniform bidding, the proof constructs explicit bid profiles that form equilibria after an advertiser lowers their reported target: the misreporting advertiser wins only a subset of their original queries, all tCPA constraints hold, and no bidder can profitably deviate. For uniform bidding with two advertisers, the key insight is that sorting queries by the value ratio v_{1,j}/v_{2,j} forces every equilibrium to be a prefix allocation N_k (bidder 1 wins queries 1…k, bidder 2 wins k+1…n), yielding a total order on equilibria. Theorem 4 eliminates the bid multipliers μ_1, μ_2 from the equilibrium existence conditions, reducing them to four inequalities involving only reported targets and cumulative value sums V_i(L_k), V_i(R_k). The main RAIC argument (Theorem 5) then iterates k' downward from k, using the mediant inequality to show that if N_{k'} fails one stability inequality, the same inequality is automatically satisfied at N_{k'-1}, guaranteeing an equilibrium exists at some k' ≤ k. OAIC (Theorem 6) mirrors this by iterating k upward from k'.

## Results

The paper's main results are theoretical: SPA is proven to satisfy both RAIC and OAIC under non-uniform bidding for any number of advertisers (Theorems 1–2), and under uniform bidding for two advertisers (Theorems 5–6). FPA is shown to be OAIC under non-uniform bidding (Theorem 3) and AIC (hence both RAIC and OAIC) under uniform bidding. The key technical contribution is Theorem 4, which characterizes equilibrium existence for allocation N_k via a set of inequalities depending only on reported targets and query values, eliminating the need to solve for bid multipliers explicitly.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or datasets. The relevant comparison is against the original AIC framework of Alimohammadi et al. (2023), which showed that both FPA and SPA fail AIC when advertisers have both tCPA and budget constraints. The paper's RAIC and OAIC definitions relax AIC by using consistent equilibrium selection (worst-worst for RAIC, best-best for OAIC) rather than the cross-comparison of worst-truthful vs. best-deviation that AIC requires. Under these relaxed definitions, SPA—which was previously shown to fail AIC—actually satisfies both RAIC and OAIC, providing a more optimistic view of standard auction incentive properties. The paper also notes that FPA with uniform bidding is already AIC (and thus trivially RAIC and OAIC), while FPA with non-uniform bidding is only shown to be OAIC, not RAIC. The uniform-bidding case for more than two advertisers remains open, representing a gap relative to the non-uniform setting where arbitrary numbers of advertisers are handled.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the AIC framework introduced by Alimohammadi et al. (2023), who modeled auto-bidding as a two-stage game and showed that both FPA and SPA fail the original AIC definition (which compares worst-case truthful outcomes to best-case deviation outcomes). The key departure is the introduction of RAIC and OAIC, which use consistent equilibrium selection (worst-worst and best-best, respectively) to enable well-defined ordinal preferences over reported targets. The technical analysis builds on the uniform bidding characterization from Aggarwal et al. (2019) and the mediant property to derive closed-form equilibrium existence conditions (Theorem 4), a tool not previously available in the auto-bidding incentive compatibility literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The main practical implication is that ad platforms may not need to radically redesign auction architectures to handle strategic auto-bidding: under reasonable equilibrium selection assumptions, SPA already provides reasonable incentive alignment for tCPA-constrained advertisers. However, the results are limited to tCPA constraints (not budget constraints), and the uniform bidding analysis only covers two advertisers, leaving the multi-advertiser uniform bidding case open. The mediant-based equilibrium characterization (Theorem 4) is a reusable technical tool that could facilitate future work on more complex constraint combinations or larger bidder sets.
