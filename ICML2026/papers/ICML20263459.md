## Problem

Standard GNN aggregation functions (mean, max, sum) are not bijective, which prevents GNNs from being used in tasks that require invertible transformations such as exact density estimation, generative modeling, and likelihood-based outlier detection. This paper asks whether one can design a GNN layer that is both fully invertible and expressive enough to match the 1-WL algorithm, without sacrificing empirical performance on standard graph classification benchmarks.

## Input / Output

The model consumes an undirected graph's adjacency matrix A ∈ ℝ^{n×n} and a node feature matrix H⁽⁰⁾ ∈ ℝ^{n×d}. Because the graph operator exp(A) is dense for connected graphs, the layer operates on all pairs of nodes within a connected component simultaneously (O(n²) time and memory per layer), unlike standard GNNs that only aggregate over edges (O(m)). Beyond producing node representations for classification, the invertibility enables three additional outputs: exact log-likelihood scores via the change-of-variables formula, generated node features by sampling from a base Gaussian and applying the inverse mapping, and counterfactual feature perturbations that would flip a misclassified node's prediction.

## Method

The INVGNN layer updates node representations via h_i^(k) = Σ_j exp(A)_{i,j} · f(h_j^(k-1) W^(k) + b^(k)), where exp(A) is the matrix exponential of the adjacency matrix (computed via eigenvalue decomposition for undirected graphs), W^(k) is an invertible weight matrix parameterized by its LU decomposition (initialized as a random rotation), and f is an invertible activation (e.g., Tanh or LeakyReLU). The inverse mapping is H^(k) = (f⁻¹(exp(−A) H^(k+1)) − b^(k+1)) (W^(k+1))⁻¹, making the entire stack of K layers a bijection on the space of node representation matrices. A key theoretical result (Theorem 3.2) shows that with an analytic non-polynomial activation and hidden dimension d′ ≥ 2n+1, the model matches 1-WL expressiveness for Lebesgue-almost-all parameter values, relying on the Lindemann-Weierstrass theorem to prove that equal multisets of exp(A) row entries imply identical local graph structure. For scalability to large sparse graphs, a Chebyshev polynomial approximation of exp(A)M is provided that requires only O(Kmd) time via repeated sparse matrix-vector products without ever forming the dense n×n matrix.

## Results

On the six TUDataset benchmarks, INVGNN achieves the best accuracy on three datasets (PROTEINS 75.1%, IMDB-BINARY 71.5%, IMDB-MULTI 48.5%), matching or exceeding GIN (the closest 1-WL-equivalent baseline) on most. On the larger ogbg-molhiv dataset (41,127 graphs), INVGNN achieves 77.61% ROC-AUC, outperforming GIN by 2.03% absolute but trailing the best baseline π-GNN (79.12%) by 1.5%. For node-level outlier detection on five real-world datasets, INVGNN achieves the best ROC-AUC on Disney (65.7) and competitive results on Books (59.2) and Reddit (57.2), while Radar dominates on Weibo (99.0 vs. 88.9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

INVGNN is most directly comparable to GIN, since both match 1-WL expressiveness. On TUDataset, INVGNN beats GIN on PROTEINS (+1.8%), IMDB-BINARY (+0.3%), and ties on IMDB-MULTI (48.5%), but loses on NCI1 (76.4% vs. 80.0%, a 3.6% gap) and MUTAG (84.9% vs. 84.7%, within noise). On ogbg-molhiv, INVGNN (77.61% ROC-AUC) outperforms GIN (75.58%) by 2.03% but trails π-GNN (79.12%) and ESAN (78.00%), which are more expressive architectures. Among the 18 baselines, INVGNN is best on 3 of 6 TUDataset columns and 1 of 5 outlier-detection datasets (Disney, 65.7 vs. AdONE 59.2), while Radar (99.0) and DOMINANT (85.0) dominate on Weibo and Enron respectively. The paper explicitly notes that beating more expressive models (e.g., 2-WL, 3-WL) is not the goal; the aim is to show invertibility does not degrade empirical performance relative to same-expressiveness baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: invertible neural networks (notably the LU-decomposed invertible 1×1 convolution from Glow, Kingma & Dhariwal 2018) and graph neural networks with neighborhood aggregation. It departs from standard GNNs (GCN, GIN, GraphSAGE) whose aggregation operators (mean, max, sum) are provably non-invertible, and from prior graph normalizing flows (Liu et al., 2019) which did not target 1-WL expressiveness. The theoretical guarantee of 1-WL equivalence relies on a recent injectivity result for neural functions over multisets (Amir et al., 2023), which the authors adapt to the graph setting via the matrix exponential operator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that invertibility and 1-WL expressiveness are not in tension for GNNs, opening the door to exact likelihood computation, generative modeling, and interpretability on graph-structured data without sacrificing discriminative power. The principal limitation is the O(n²) time and memory cost of the dense exp(A) matrix, which precludes application to graphs with hundreds of thousands of nodes; the Chebyshev approximation in Appendix B mitigates but does not eliminate this. An open question is whether sparser invertible graph operators (e.g., truncated or structured exponentials) can preserve both invertibility and 1-WL expressiveness at near-linear cost, and whether the analytic-activation requirement (Theorem 3.2) can be relaxed to piecewise-linear functions with a different architectural modification.
