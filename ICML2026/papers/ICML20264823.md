## Problem

Existing unbalanced flow matching methods for single-cell dynamics, including WFR-FM, require iterative ODE trajectory simulation at inference time to obtain terminal cell states, creating a computational bottleneck that scales poorly with the number of cells and perturbation conditions. This paper addresses the gap by formulating a mean-flow framework for unbalanced dynamics that enables direct one-step state-to-state generation without any trajectory simulation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264823/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes initial cell states (x₀, m₀) where m₀ is the per-cell mass/weight, and evaluates the learned mean velocity v(x,t,T) and mean growth rate h(x,t,T) over a chosen time interval [t,T] (typically [0,1] for one-step inference). The output is the terminal state (x₁, m₁) computed as x₁ = x₀ + v(x₀,0,1) and m₁ = m₀·exp(h(x₀,0,1)) in a single network evaluation. In the perturbation setting, a condition embedding c is additionally fed to the networks, enabling a single model to handle thousands of distinct perturbation conditions. Multi-step inference (K>1) refines accuracy by applying the update over K sub-intervals using additive consistency.

## Method

WFR-MFM replaces instantaneous velocity and growth-rate fields with time-averaged mean-flow variables v(x,t,T) and h(x,t,T) defined over arbitrary intervals, which satisfy additive consistency and derivative identities linking them back to the instantaneous fields. Training uses a stop-gradient self-referential objective where regression targets are computed via the derivative identity v = u_t + (T−t)[∂_t v_θ + (∇_x v_θ)u_t] rather than direct time averaging—a construction that is essential for proving equivalence between conditional and unconditional objectives (Theorem 4.1). Under the WFR geometry, conditional paths are constructed from traveling Dirac geodesics with OET couplings, yielding closed-form instantaneous fields u_t(x|z) and g_t(x|z) for supervised regression. Inference is a single forward pass (or K passes for multi-step refinement), achieving 2–3 orders of magnitude speedup over ODE-based baselines while maintaining competitive W₁ and RME accuracy across synthetic and real scRNA-seq datasets.

## Results

WFR-MFM achieves 2–3 orders of magnitude inference speedup over ODE-based baselines (38×–2965× vs. adaptive RK5(4), 94×–124× vs. 100-step Euler) across seven datasets. On synthetic benchmarks, it attains W₁ of 0.021 (Gene 2D), 0.176 (Dyngen 5D), and 2.233 (Gaussian 1000D) with RME of 0.003, 0.023, and 0.001 respectively, ranking first or second among all methods. On a large-scale perturbation benchmark with 5,000 unseen conditions and 10,000 cells, WFR-MFM completes inference in 6.55 s (W₁=0.115, RME=0.069) versus an estimated ~2,000 s for WFR-FM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WFR-MFM is most directly compared to WFR-FM (Peng et al., 2026a), the closest prior method: WFR-MFM is slightly worse on Gene (W₁ 0.021 vs. 0.019) and Dyngen (W₁ 0.176 vs. 0.135) but ties on Gaussian W₁ (2.233) and substantially outperforms on Gaussian RME (0.001 vs. 0.044), all while being 38×–2965× faster at inference. Against unbalanced OT baselines, WFR-MFM dominates: it beats TIGON (Sha et al., 2024) on all three datasets (e.g., Gene W₁ 0.021 vs. 0.045), DeepRUOT (Zhang et al., 2025b) (Gene 0.021 vs. 0.043, Gaussian 2.233 vs. 3.785), Var-RUOT (Sun et al., 2025) (Gene 0.021 vs. 0.079), UOT-FM (Eyring et al., 2024) (Gene 0.021 vs. 0.093, Dyngen 0.176 vs. 1.204), and VGFM (Wang et al., 2025) (Gene 0.021 vs. 0.046, Dyngen 0.176 vs. 0.598). Against mass-preserving flow-matching baselines, WFR-MFM also wins across the board: MIOFlow (Huguet et al., 2022) (Gene 0.021 vs. 0.148), SF2M (Tong et al., 2024b) (Gene 0.021 vs. 0.224), MMFM (Rohbeck et al., 2025) (Gene 0.021 vs. 0.298), and MetricFM (Kapusniak et al., 2024) (Gene 0.021 vs. 0.311). The only method that edges out WFR-MFM on any metric is WFR-FM itself on Gene and Dyngen W₁, but at a cost of 2–3 orders of magnitude slower inference, making WFR-MFM the clear winner on the speed–accuracy Pareto frontier.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MeanFlow framework (Geng et al., 2025a), which enables one-step inference by parameterizing time-averaged velocity fields, to the unbalanced setting by introducing a parallel mean mass-growth field h alongside the mean velocity field v. It then specializes this generalized mean-flow framework to the Wasserstein-Fisher-Rao geometry, building directly on the WFR-FM construction (Peng et al., 2026a) for the traveling-Dirac conditional paths and optimal entropy transport couplings, while replacing WFR-FM's trajectory-based ODE inference with direct state-to-state updates. The key theoretical departure from MeanFlow is the derivation of derivative identities for both v and h and the proof (Theorem 4.1) that the conditional and unconditional training objectives remain equivalent in the unbalanced setting—a non-trivial extension since the mass-growth field introduces additional coupling between the two learned quantities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2–3 order-of-magnitude inference speedup (up to ~3000× over adaptive RK solvers) with maintained predictive accuracy makes dynamic unbalanced OT practically viable for large-scale perturbation response prediction, where the combinatorial explosion of conditions renders ODE-based inference infeasible. The controllable speed-accuracy trade-off via multi-step consistency (Algorithm 1) gives practitioners a simple knob without retraining. However, the paper does not incorporate recent MeanFlow refinements (SplitMeanFlow, AlphaFlow, improved MeanFlow) that address training stability, and validation on real experimental perturbation datasets remains future work; the large-scale perturbation benchmark is synthetic. Extending beyond WFR geometry to other unbalanced transport formulations is also left open.
