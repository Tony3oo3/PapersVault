## Problem

Information-theoretic acquisition functions like Entropy Search (ES) provide principled exploration-exploitation for Bayesian optimization, but their practical computation relies on expensive Monte Carlo sampling and handcrafted approximations that introduce numerical errors and require specialized implementations. No existing method can evaluate ES-based acquisitions (PES, MES, JES) in a single forward pass, making them impractical for high-throughput optimization settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263811/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The α-PFN takes the observation dataset D (context points with input-output pairs) and a single query point x as input, and outputs a full distribution over the information gain (parameterized as a Riemann/Full-Bar distribution with 5000 bins); the acquisition value is the mean of this distribution. The auxiliary base PFN additionally accepts optional conditioning tokens for x*, f*, or both, encoded via a separate encoder to distinguish them from ordinary data points. Models are trained on 1–6D data with context sizes up to 50 but generalize to 16D and 100 BO iterations at test time.

## Method

The method uses a two-stage amortization strategy: first, a base PFN (TabPFNv2 transformer, ~4.7M parameters) is trained to compute posterior predictive distributions conditioned on optimum information I ∈ {x*, f*, (x*,f*)}, with the conditioning randomly sampled at 50% probability per training step so one model handles all four cases. Second, the α-PFN is trained to predict the differential entropy difference H(q(y|D,x)) − H(q(y|D,x,I)) using the base PFN's outputs as targets, with the true precomputed optima from RFF-approximated GP samples providing the conditioning during training only. A formal proof shows the α-PFN's training loss equals the KL divergence between the true information-gain distribution and the model's output, so the output mean recovers the PES/MES/JES acquisition. Training uses 100M datasets from a fully Bayesian GP prior (squared-exponential kernel with ARD lengthscales, log-normal noise), and a synthetic BO trace generation procedure (Algorithm 1) mimics the clustering of query points around optima to combat domain shift.

## Results

α-PFN achieves speedups ranging from 1.6× to 72.4× over GP-MCMC baselines across all benchmarks, with HPO-B speedups commonly exceeding 30× (e.g., 72.4× for PES on HPO-B-7609, 65.0× for MES on HPO-B-7609). On optimization quality (inference regret), α-PFN variants closely match their GP counterparts on synthetic functions (Branin, Hartmann, Ackley), with JES-α-PFN performing consistently best across LCBench tasks. On HPO-B, MES-α-PFN is often outperformed by the GP baseline, while JES-α-PFN and PES-α-PFN remain competitive.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GP-MCMC JES (Hvarfner et al., 2022), α-PFN JES matches or slightly underperforms on inference regret for synthetic functions but delivers 4.4×–37.6× speedups; on LCBench, JES-α-PFN consistently outperforms the GP baseline across all five tasks. Against GP-MCMC MES-GIBBON (Moss et al., 2021), α-PFN MES is competitive on synthetic functions and LCBench (best on Higgs) but is often outperformed on HPO-B, while still achieving 3.4×–65.0× speedups. Against GP-MCMC PES (Hernandez-Lobato et al., 2014), α-PFN PES is generally competitive or superior on average regret for synthetic functions, with speedups of 4.1×–72.4×. Against EI (Močkus, 1975) as a reference, EI yields strong performance especially on HPO-B, but the paper's goal is to compare ES variants; α-PFN EI is shown to be competitive with GP EI in Appendix G. Overall, α-PFN trades a small, task-dependent quality gap for order-of-magnitude runtime reductions, with the largest gains on high-dimensional discrete HPO-B search spaces.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PFN paradigm (Müller et al., 2022; Müller et al., 2023) from approximating GP surrogates to amortizing information-theoretic acquisition functions themselves. It builds directly on the TabPFNv2 architecture (Hollmann et al., 2025) and the conditional-inference idea of Chang et al. (2025), who amortized conditioning on f* for MES but still required MC sampling at test time. The key departure is a two-stage strategy: a base PFN learns the conditioned PPD p(y|D,x,I), and a second α-PFN learns the full information-gain distribution in one forward pass, eliminating all test-time MC sampling over optima. This generalizes Chang et al.'s single-variant approach to PES, MES, and JES simultaneously, and makes fully Bayesian ES tractable by integrating out hyperparameter uncertainty within the learned model rather than averaging over MCMC samples.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 50×+ speedups (reaching 72× on HPO-B) make information-theoretic acquisition functions practical for high-throughput and large-scale BO settings where GP-MCMC runtimes are prohibitive, while matching optimization quality under a shared prior. The fully Bayesian treatment is a notable conceptual gain: a single learned acquisition replaces the expensive per-hyperparameter-sample averaging, and the model can query points to reduce hyperparameter uncertainty. Open questions include scaling beyond 6D training dimensions (tested to 16D), OOD robustness under distribution shift, and the need to retrain for each surrogate prior—addressing which would broaden the framework's applicability beyond GP-based BO.
