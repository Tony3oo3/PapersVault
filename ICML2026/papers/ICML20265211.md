## Problem

Existing offline federated DRL methods aggregate client models using either simple averaging or Q-values alone, failing to jointly account for both expected returns (Q-values) and policy inconsistency (the distributional gap between the learned policy and the offline data policy). Additionally, local training forces clients to fully adopt the global model, so a weak global model degrades stronger local models. These two limitations cause clients with advantages in one aspect but disadvantages in the other to disproportionately harm the global model.

## Input / Output

Each client holds a local static dataset of offline transition tuples (s, a, r, s') of size |D_i| (default 5000), sampled without replacement from D4RL expert or medium datasets. Clients and the server are unaware of dataset quality and have no environment access. Per FL round, a random subset of N_a clients (default 10 out of 20) participates, each performing T=20 local epochs (~380 gradient steps) on mini-batches of 256 samples. The output is a weighted-aggregated global actor (policy) and global critic (Q-function), evaluated on the D4RL expert dataset for cumulative reward and normalized D4RL score.

## Method

The paper proposes a plug-in FL framework with two mechanisms. For global aggregation, each client computes an importance score I_i = E[κ_i·Q(s,a) − Dis(θ_i^μ(s), a_i)], where Dis is the Jensen-Shannon Divergence between a multivariate Gaussian fitted to the action prediction errors (π_θ(s) − a) and a standard Gaussian N(0, 0.15·I), and κ_i = 1/(|D_0i|·Σ|Q|) rescales Q-values to avoid numerical dominance over the bounded discrepancy term. Client weights are derived via softmax over the importance vector, and the global model is a weighted sum of local actors and critics. For local training, a decay factor β_i is applied to the local loss whenever the client's own importance exceeds the global model's importance; β_i decays multiplicatively by ζ=0.99 per consecutive update where the global model underperforms, progressively reducing the influence of a weaker global model on stronger local models. The framework is model-agnostic and integrates seamlessly into any existing offline FDRL baseline by replacing its aggregation and local-update steps.

## Results

On the D4RL benchmark (20 clients, 10 participating per round, 20 local epochs), the proposed framework improves the final D4RL score of 7 SOTA offline FDRL baselines across four MuJoCo tasks (HalfCheetah, Hopper, Walker2d, Ant) in the majority of cases. The largest gains are Ours+CSC on Hopper (42.15 → 97.07), Ours+MOON on Ant (15.59 → 80.90), and Ours+FEDORA on Walker2d (67.99 → 109.29). The method incurs negligible computational overhead, with FLOPs and VRAM usage matching the baseline (e.g., ~4.11e+06 FLOPs and ~89 MB VRAM for HalfCheetah).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed framework is integrated into seven SOTA offline FDRL methods and evaluated on final D4RL score. It outperforms all seven baselines on at least three of four tasks, with the most dramatic improvements seen when the baseline is weakest: Ours+MOON on Ant jumps from 15.59 to 80.90 (+65.3), Ours+CSC on Hopper from 42.15 to 97.07 (+54.9), and Ours+FEDORA on Walker2d from 67.99 to 109.29 (+41.3). Three exceptions exist where the integrated method underperforms its baseline: Ours+FOVA on HalfCheetah (42.35 vs. 46.99), Ours+FDRLDR on Hopper (43.11 vs. 44.44), and notably Ours+CSC on Walker2d (65.12 vs. 108.56), where CSC's already near-optimal baseline score leaves little room for improvement and the added aggregation overhead appears to hurt. Among the averaging-based baselines (FDQL, FDRLDR, FORL, CSC, FOVA, MOON), the gains are consistent and moderate (typically +3 to +12 points), while the Q-value-weighted baseline FEDORA shows the largest relative improvement, suggesting that incorporating policy inconsistency into weight computation is especially beneficial when the existing weighting is one-dimensional.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a generic, plug-and-play framework that augments existing offline FDRL methods rather than proposing a new offline DRL algorithm. It departs from two lines of prior work: (1) aggregation strategies that use either uniform averaging (Yue et al., Zhou et al., Park & Woo, Woo et al., Wen et al.) or Q-value-only weighting (Rengarajan et al., 2024), and (2) local training protocols that force clients to fully adopt the global model. The key conceptual departure is jointly measuring client importance via a scaled Q-value and a Jensen-Shannon Divergence-based policy inconsistency metric, and introducing a multiplicative decay factor that attenuates the global model's influence on local updates when the local model outperforms it. The theoretical analysis draws on Lipschitz continuity assumptions and occupancy measure bounds standard in DRL convergence literature (Xiong et al., 2022; Bertsekas, 2003).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a low-overhead, architecture-agnostic improvement that practitioners can drop into any existing offline FDRL pipeline without modifying the underlying actor-critic algorithm, which is practically valuable given the fragmented landscape of offline FDRL methods. However, the experimental scope is limited to four MuJoCo D4RL tasks with 20 clients and fixed dataset sizes, and the method shows inconsistent gains (e.g., Ours+CSC regresses on Walker2d from 108.56 to 65.12, Ours+FOVA regresses on HalfCheetah), suggesting sensitivity to the interaction between baseline method and environment. Open questions include robustness to adversarial client behavior (data poisoning, model poisoning), scalability to hundreds of clients, applicability to continuous-action domains beyond MuJoCo (e.g., robotics, autonomous driving), and whether the fixed decay rate ζ=0.99 generalizes or requires per-task tuning.
