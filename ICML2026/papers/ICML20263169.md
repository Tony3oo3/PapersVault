## Problem

Existing Transformer-based visual trackers that employ token pruning are trained and evaluated under a fixed computational budget, making them unable to adapt to the diverse and dynamic computational demands of real-world deployment (e.g., drones, robots, autonomous platforms). No prior framework enables a single tracker to perform robust tracking across a continuous range of token budgets without retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263169/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a template image and a search region image (standard RGB patches) and outputs a target bounding box, identical to conventional visual trackers. The distinguishing aspect is that after the k-th Transformer layer, a policy network selects a variable number of template tokens (B_z) and search region tokens (B_x) to retain, and this budget pair can be dynamically adjusted at inference time without retraining. The policy network adds negligible overhead (~0.78M params, ~0.3 GFLOPs for ViT-base models).

## Method

ETBTrack is a post-training framework with two alternating optimization processes. First, a lightweight policy network inserted after the k-th Transformer layer is trained via a GRPO-inspired reinforcement learning objective: it scores each token's importance, tokens are sampled in groups via multinomial distribution, and the reward is the IoU between predicted and ground-truth bounding boxes, with group-relative advantages driving the policy toward higher-precision token selections. Second, a budget-collaborative optimization strategy jointly fine-tunes the tracker across multiple token budgets simultaneously (rather than alternating between them), identifying a common optimization direction that raises the performance upper bound under all budgets. The two processes are alternated twice, and the framework is architecture-agnostic, validated on SeqTrack (autoregressive causal Transformer) and ODTrack (temporal token propagation with convolutional box head) at multiple scales.

## Results

On LaSOT and TrackingNet, ETBTrack(ODTrack-L384) at token budget [288,184] achieves 74.0 AUC on LaSOT and 85.9 AUC on TrackingNet at 44.3 FPS, matching the full-budget ODTrack-L384 (74.0/86.1) while delivering 27% higher FPS. SeqTrack-L256 trained with ETBTrack retains 99.5% average performance using only 33.3% of tokens, 37.7% peak memory, and 2.0× inference speed. In the token-pruning comparison (Table 1), ETBTrack[256,128] achieves 99.3% average AUC ratio versus 86.9% for OSTrack* and 72.4% for LMTrack* at comparable FPS (~71 GPU).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ETBTrack(ODTrack-L384)[288,184] matches the full-budget ODTrack-L384 on LaSOT (74.0 vs 74.0) and is within 0.2 AUC on TrackingNet (85.9 vs 86.1) while running 27% faster (44.3 vs 34.8 FPS). Against other full-budget trackers, it outperforms ARTrackV2-L384 on LaSOT (74.0 vs 73.6) and FPS (44.3 vs 43.2), and surpasses LoRAT-g-224 on TrackingNet (85.9 vs 85.2) and FPS (44.3 vs 41.6), though LoRAT leads on LaSOT (74.9 vs 74.0). Among token-pruning baselines, ETBTrack dominates: it achieves 99.3% average AUC ratio versus 86.9% for OSTrack (attention-score pruning) and 72.4% for LMTrack (aggregated attention+classification pruning) at the same [256,128] budget. In the broader pruning comparison (Table 15), ETBTrack's 67.03 average AUC far exceeds VisionZip (56.60), ToMe (55.77), FastV (33.23), and DivPrune (10.63), confirming that result-driven policy-based selection substantially outperforms training-free or manually-designed pruning criteria under elastic budgets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Transformer-based visual tracking paradigm (SeqTrack, ODTrack, MixFormer) and the line of token-pruning acceleration methods (OSTrack, LMTrack, RFGM-Track), which all rely on manual importance criteria (attention scores, classification scores) under a fixed token budget. The key conceptual departure is the adoption of a GRPO-style reinforcement learning objective (from DeepSeekMath) to train a lightweight policy network that learns token importance directly from tracking precision, replacing hand-crafted heuristics. The budget-collaborative optimization strategy further departs from prior work by jointly optimizing across multiple budgets rather than training at a single fixed budget, addressing the performance gap between training and inference token configurations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ETBTrack addresses a practical deployment bottleneck: a single tracker must adapt to heterogeneous computational environments (drones, robots, edge devices) without retraining. The finding that attention-based pruning degrades catastrophically under small budgets (AUC 63→46 or 27) challenges the field's implicit assumption that attention scores are a reliable proxy for token importance in tracking. Limitations include the two-stage alternating training pipeline (not end-to-end), validation on only two tracker architectures, and the open question of whether combining elastic token pruning with network pruning (layer/width) yields compounding efficiency gains. The GRPO-inspired formulation for discrete token selection also suggests broader applicability to other token-based architectures beyond tracking.
