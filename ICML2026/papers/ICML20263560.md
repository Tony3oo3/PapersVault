## Problem

LLM-based multi-agent systems (MAS) are vulnerable to jailbreak and adversarial collaboration attacks, yet existing defenses are inadequate: self-verification by individual agents lacks capacity to detect cross-agent unsafe chains, while external guard agents introduce a single point of failure and prohibitive scaling costs. Static safety training on fixed datasets further leads to overfitting and poor generalization against adaptive adversaries. The paper asks whether safety can be internalized directly into task agents through collaborative, adaptive training that accounts for multi-agent interaction dynamics.

## Input / Output

The system takes a user instruction (e.g., a math problem from MATH-500), inter-agent communication messages, and agent role profiles as input across three topology configurations (chain, tree, complete graph) with three Qwen2.5 agents (3B or 7B). Each agent must produce a structured output with reasoning enclosed in <think> tags and a final answer in <response> tags; the multi-agent system's final output is evaluated for both safety compliance and task correctness. During adversarial evaluation, the input is additionally corrupted via NetSafe (profile injection), AutoInject (message injection), or UserHijack (user instruction manipulation) to test robustness.

## Method

AdvEvo-MARL jointly evolves attacker and defender agents through a two-stage pipeline: (1) an attacker warm-up phase where SFT on ~4,000 curated jailbreak prompts with chain-of-thought reasoning traces injects prior knowledge of attack strategies, and (2) an adversarial co-evolutionary RL stage using REINFORCE++ where attackers generate increasingly sophisticated jailbreak prompts while defenders learn to resist attacks and maintain task performance. The key architectural innovation is a public baseline for advantage estimation: agents within the same functional group (all attackers or all defenders) share the group's mean return as their baseline, reducing variance in policy updates and fostering intra-group coordination. Reward design is asymmetric—attackers receive a negative safety signal plus format compliance, while defenders receive a weighted combination of safety, task utility, and format rewards, with a safety-first curriculum that shifts emphasis to task performance in the second half of training. Unlike prior MARL-for-LLM work that trains a single shared-parameter backbone, AdvEvo-MARL trains multiple distinct backbone models collaboratively, enabling genuine agent-level diversity and co-evolution.

## Results

Across three attack scenarios (NetSafe, AutoInject, UserHijack) and three topologies (chain, tree, complete graph), AdvEvo-MARL-7B maintains ASR consistently below 10% in chain and tree settings, with a maximum of 17.68% in the hardest complete-graph topology, while open-source baselines reach up to 38.33% (Challenger-7B) and 65.53% (Vanilla-3B). In rank-based aggregation over all topology–scenario settings, AdvEvo-MARL-7B achieves the best overall average rank (1.68) among open-source methods, followed by AdvEvo-MARL-3B (2.46). On task benchmarks (AIME, GPQA-diamond, LiveCodeBench), AdvEvo-MARL-7B preserves or even improves task accuracy by up to +3.67% over its vanilla counterpart, demonstrating that safety training need not sacrifice utility.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Challenger (self-verification) baseline, AdvEvo-MARL-7B achieves substantially lower ASR across all topologies; most strikingly, under UserHijack in the complete-graph setting, Challenger-7B reaches 38.33% ASR (a 10-point increase over its own vanilla variant), while AdvEvo-MARL-7B stays well below 10%. Against the Inspector (external guard agent) baseline, AdvEvo-MARL-7B outperforms Inspector-7B in overall rank (1.68 vs. 2.98), with the gap widening in the complete-graph topology where Inspector-7B still suffers elevated CR (~17–48%) due to the single-point-of-failure vulnerability. Both baselines are competitive in simpler chain/tree topologies, but AdvEvo-MARL's advantage grows with system interconnectivity, confirming that internalized co-evolutionary safety scales better than external monitoring. On task utility, AdvEvo-MARL-7B matches or exceeds both baselines on AIME, GPQA, and LiveCodeBench, whereas Challenger and Inspector show no task-performance benefit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research lines: safety defenses for LLM-based multi-agent systems (self-verification à la Challenger, external guard agents à la Inspector, peer inspection) and multi-agent reinforcement learning for LLM post-training (MAPORL, REMA, RAG-MARL). It departs from prior safety work by rejecting both external guards and isolated self-verification in favor of internalizing safety through adversarial co-evolution, and departs from prior LLM-MARL work by training multiple distinct backbone models rather than a single shared-parameter model. The public-baseline advantage estimation mechanism is a novel stabilization technique not present in standard MAPPO/GRPO/REINFORCE++ formulations, drawing loosely on group-relative ideas but applied at the functional-group level across heterogeneous agents.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that safety and task utility can be jointly improved in multi-agent systems without adding guard agents or system overhead, which is practically significant for deployment where extra inference passes are costly. The co-evolutionary framing addresses a real gap: static safety training overfits to fixed attack distributions, and the dynamic attacker shows measurable robustness gains (12% ASR reduction) over static baselines. Key open questions include scaling beyond six agents to larger heterogeneous ecosystems, generalization to structurally different adversarial strategies (the paper acknowledges degradation under OOD attacks), and whether the training cost of multi-model co-evolution is justified relative to simpler single-model RLHF approaches for safety.
