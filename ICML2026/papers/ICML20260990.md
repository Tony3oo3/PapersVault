## Problem

Despite strong benchmark performance, biological foundation models (Bio-FMs) have not been systematically evaluated for robustness to the small-but-real perturbations that inevitably arise in practice—both from biological data curation (experimental noise, annotation errors, geometric distortions) and from ML pipeline choices (graph construction, tokenization, preprocessing). This paper asks whether Bio-FMs are robust enough for real-world deployment and identifies which specific perturbation sources compromise their reliability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260990/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The evaluation suite consumes protein structures in PDB format, amino-acid sequences, and cryo-EM particle images in MRC format, applying 11 biologically plausible perturbations (e.g., Gaussian coordinate noise at σ = 0.10–1.20 Å, local residue deletion up to 12% of chain length, B-factor/occupancy scrambling, CONECT record loss) and ML transformations (varying graph construction hyperparameters such as kNN radius from 5–15 Å, neighbor count, and noise level). Outputs are structured as robustness boundary curves (dissimilarity vs. task performance), per-perturbation performance tables (AUPRC, F1, accuracy, recovery rate, Spearman AUC, FSC-derived resolution in Å), and graph dissimilarity scores (Jaccard, Frobenius, spectral distance) quantifying how much the input representation actually changed.

## Method

The paper introduces a dual-axis perturbation taxonomy that separates biologically plausible perturbations (geometric/coordinate-level and annotation/format-level corruptions grounded in experimental literature) from ML-induced transformations (inference-time changes to internal graph construction, tokenization, and preprocessing hyperparameters). For each of 11 state-of-the-art Bio-FMs across 7 bio-tasks and 4 task categories, 2,128 experiments probe the robustness boundary by sweeping perturbation severity and measuring the resulting graph dissimilarity (default: Jaccard edge similarity) against task performance, revealing that even 1% graph dissimilarity can cause catastrophic drops (e.g., GearNet from 0.7 to 0.1 AUPRC). The study further demonstrates that Bio-FMs are significantly less robust than non-FM tools (BLAST shows near-zero degradation while GearNet drops up to 15.2%), that spatial-proximity-based graph edges are more brittle than density-based kNN edges, and that cryo-EM reconstruction models (CryoDRGN, CryoNeRF) exhibit surprising robustness even under worst-case PGD adversarial attacks, attributed to information aggregation, training objectives, and input continuity.

## Results

Across 2,128 experiments on 11 Bio-FMs, most models exhibit severe vulnerability: GearNet drops from 0.7 to 0.1 AUPRC@MICRO with only 1% graph dissimilarity, and loses up to 15.2% accuracy under local residue deletion (vs. 0.0% for BLAST). Cryo-EM models (CryoDRGN, CryoNeRF) are a notable exception, maintaining ~3.50 Å FSC resolution under Gaussian blur, rotation, and even PGD adversarial attacks, though translation at severity 5 degrades resolution to 64.66–66.75 Å. In agentic pipelines, AlphaFold3's pTM/iPTM scores remain consistent across 5 trials while downstream Rosetta energies show large variance, and RFDiffusion2-based design success rates drop substantially under severity-3 coordinate noise or local deletions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The central comparison is between Bio-FMs and the non-FM tool BLAST: under all four biological perturbations, BLAST shows 0.0–0.5% accuracy drop while GearNet degrades by 5.7–15.2%, establishing that high-capacity Bio-FMs are substantially more fragile than classical tools. Among Bio-FMs, GearNet is the most vulnerable (0.7→0.1 AUPRC at 1% graph dissimilarity), while ESM-GearNet is more robust to k-NN changes but still sensitive to radius, and ProteinMPNN shows the greatest resilience to graph-construction perturbations. For biological perturbations, ESM-3 is notably weaker than ProNet under rename perturbation (sequence-reliant vs. structure-focused design), and both collapse at severity >4. CryoDRGN and CryoNeRF stand out as robust: CryoDRGN maintains 3.50 Å FSC resolution even under PGD adversarial attacks at all severities, whereas structure/sequence models fail catastrophically. In the agentic pipeline, AlphaFold3's stable pTM/iPTM scores mask large downstream Rosetta energy variance, and RFDiffusion2+LigandMPNN+Chai-1 design success rates drop sharply under severity-3 coordinate noise or local deletions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by introducing a dual-axis robustness taxonomy—biologically plausible perturbations and ML-induced transformations—for evaluating Bio-FMs, departing from prior work that was either narrowly task-specific (Jha et al. 2021, Akdel et al. 2022 focused on protein folding reliability) or borrowed adversarial-attack frameworks from general ML (Yuan et al. 2023, SafeGenes). Unlike those studies, it systematically separates curation-side corruptions from pipeline-side representation choices and connects both to concrete deployment risk in agentic pipelines. The paper does not build on a specific prior method but repurposes standard robustness-evaluation machinery (PGD, similarity metrics) within a new biological context, positioning itself as the first comprehensive cross-modal audit of 11 Bio-FMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that imperceptible biological perturbations (e.g., 1% graph dissimilarity) can collapse Bio-FM performance, and that stable upstream confidence (pTM/iPTM) does not guarantee stable downstream objectives (Rosetta energy), directly threatens the reliability of agentic bio-design pipelines now entering therapeutic workflows. The surprising robustness of Cryo-EM models (CryoDRGN) even under PGD attacks suggests that information-aggregation architectures and continuous input spaces may be inherently more robust than discrete graph-based encoders, offering a design principle for next-generation Bio-FMs. Key limitations include the absence of wet-lab validation, the protein-centric scope (no nucleic acids or small molecules), and the fact that ML-transform perturbations, while justified as proxies for structural shifts, still differ from true biological noise in their distributional character.
