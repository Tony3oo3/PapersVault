## Problem

Gaussian process predictive distributions in Bayesian optimization are sensitive to kernel and hyperparameter misspecification, which can cause local overconfidence in sparsely sampled regions and underestimate lower-tail probabilities. Since EI-based acquisition functions depend critically on the probability mass below the current best value and the shape of the predictive CDF in that region, such miscalibration misdirects exploration. Existing calibration methods target global or conformal coverage over the entire design space, leaving the lower-tail region that actually drives EI unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The method takes a noiseless dataset {(X_i, f(X_i))_{i=1}^n} over a compact design space X ⊂ R^d, GP hyperparameters selected by maximum likelihood at each iteration, and a quantile level δ (e.g., 0.05) that determines the adaptive calibration threshold t_n as the empirical δ-quantile of observed responses. It produces a family of calibrated predictive CDFs of the form GN(β, f_n(x), λσ_n(x)) for each x ∈ X, from which a closed-form EI acquisition function is computed to select the next evaluation point. The GN parameters (β, λ) are re-estimated at every BO iteration via a weighted leave-one-out criterion with density-ratio reweighting to account for the non-uniform adaptive design.

## Method

TCGP replaces the standard Gaussian predictive CDF with a generalized normal (GN) distribution GN(β, f_n(x), λσ_n(x)), where β controls tail heaviness (β=2 recovers the Gaussian) and λ controls dispersion, while fixing the location at the GP mean f_n(x). The parameters (β, λ) are selected by minimizing a joint criterion J(β,λ) that simultaneously enforces two forms of spatial calibration below a threshold t: occurrence calibration (matching the μ-average predicted probability of {f(X)≤t} to its true frequency) and thresholded μ-calibration (ensuring the probability integral transform of the truncated CDF is uniform on the sublevel set {f(X)≤t}). This criterion is approximated via weighted leave-one-out with density-ratio weights estimated by KDE to correct for the non-uniform BO design. A closed-form expression for EI under the GN model is derived using the upper incomplete gamma function (Proposition 5.1), and a convergence result (Proposition 5.3) proves that EI with TCGP and fixed GP hyperparameters generates a sequence dense in X, extending the classical Vazquez–Bect exploration guarantee to the GN family.

## Results

On four standard benchmarks (Goldstein-Price, Dixon-Price d=4, Rosenbrock d=6, Ackley d=4) with 100 runs each, TCGP (δ=0.05) achieves the lowest median excursion probability p_{m_n} = P(f(X) ≤ m_n) among all methods on Ackley, where it provides the largest gains over the standard GP. On smoother objectives (Goldstein-Price, Dixon-Price, Rosenbrock), REGP (δ=0.25) performs best, with TCGP close behind. In calibration diagnostics at the operational threshold (δ=0.05, n=30d), TCGP reduces tKS-PIT from 0.91 to 0.65 on Goldstein-Price and from 0.39 to 0.18 on Hartmann-6, while halving the occurrence discrepancy r_{t,n} on Goldstein-Price (0.16 → 0.089). TCGP requires comparable per-iteration computation to the standard GP, whereas REGP is approximately 10× slower.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against REGP (Petit et al., 2025), TCGP wins decisively on Ackley (d=4), where it provides the largest BO gains among all methods, while REGP performs best on the smoother Goldstein-Price, Dixon-Price, and Rosenbrock benchmarks; REGP also incurs roughly 10× the per-iteration computational cost of TCGP. Against onGP (Deshpande et al., 2024), the online conformal recalibration method tracks the standard GP closely on all four main test functions except Ackley, where TCGP clearly outperforms both. Against BCRGP (Pion & Vazquez, 2025), the globally calibrated GP variant, TCGP consistently achieves lower excursion probability p_{m_n} and better lower-tail calibration metrics (tKS-PIT, r_{t,n}), confirming that global calibration over X is insufficient for EI-driven BO. The ablation (Figure 2) further shows that the joint TCGP criterion outperforms both the thresholded-only variant (TCGP-thres) and the occurrence-only variant (TCGP-occ), with occurrence calibration having the stronger individual effect on BO performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

TCGP directly extends the BCRGP framework of Pion & Vazquez (2025), which models GP standardized residuals with a generalized normal family and selects parameters for global μ-probabilistic calibration, by restricting the calibration target to the lower tail below a data-driven threshold t. The two complementary calibration notions—occurrence calibration and thresholded μ-calibration—adapt the severity-occurrence decomposition from tail calibration (Allen et al., 2025) to a spatial design-marginal setting. The convergence result for EI with TCGP and fixed hyperparameters generalizes the exploration guarantee of Vazquez & Bect (2010) from Gaussian to the broader GN predictive family. The method is positioned as a post-hoc alternative to REGP (Petit et al., 2025), which modifies the GP interpolation itself, and to online conformal recalibration (Deshpande et al., 2024), which targets quantile calibration within the BO loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that global calibration of GP predictive distributions (BCRGP) does not consistently improve EI-driven BO, while goal-oriented lower-tail calibration does, challenging the assumption that 'better overall calibration = better optimization.' The ablation revealing that occurrence calibration (matching the probability mass below t) is more impactful for EI than thresholded shape calibration alone provides practical guidance for practitioners. However, the study is restricted to noiseless evaluations and low-to-moderate dimensions (d ≤ 20), and extending to noisy BO or higher-dimensional problems where weighted LOO estimates become unstable remains open. The convergence proof requires fixed GP hyperparameters, a condition rarely met in practice, leaving the sequential reestimation case unaddressed.
