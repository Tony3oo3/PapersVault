## Problem

Existing white-box uncertainty quantification methods for retrieval-augmented generation collapse the model's sequential layer-wise computation into a single snapshot (e.g., one attention layer or max-pooled statistics), losing the progressive propagation of context token contributions through the network. This prevents them from capturing how each retrieved token is transformed and accumulated into the final prediction, making it difficult to assess whether a response is genuinely grounded in query-relevant evidence.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a question–context pair fed to an open-source LLM (LLaMA-3.2-3B, LLaMA-3-8B, or Gemma-3-4B), extracts layer-wise T×T lower-triangular contribution matrices from the model's internal attention and residual computations, and additionally takes a scalar relevance score from an external ranker (Qwen-3-Reranker-8B). It produces a single calibrated confidence score via an XGBoost calibrator, alongside interpretable intermediates: an emergence-order vector ranking context tokens by their entry into the principal flow, a contribution-layout vector aggregating all admissible multi-layer paths, and per-granularity (token, word, phrase) simulatability and concentration scores. The method is strictly white-box, requiring access to per-layer hidden states and attention weights, and operates on short-form answers constrained to ≤5 words.

## Method

The core mechanism builds a complete layer-wise information-flow trace using Manhattan-distance-based contribution matrices (from Ferrando et al., 2022) at every transformer layer, then extracts two complementary views. First, the Auto-Emergence algorithm performs a backward greedy search from the final-layer last-token representation, iteratively selecting the strongest flow from a candidate pool to produce an Emergence Order that ranks context tokens by their direct decision-criticality. Second, the Contribution Layout is computed as the matrix product C^(L)·C^(L−1)·…·C^(1), whose last row aggregates all admissible multi-hop paths from each input token to the final representation, capturing both direct and indirect influence. These are converted into uncertainty signals via two measures: Simulatability, which computes Rank-Biased Overlap (p=0.7) between model-derived token rankings and a Shapley-decomposed relevance layout from the ranker at three granularities; and Concentration, which measures KL divergence of the normalized layout from a uniform distribution to detect whether the model focuses on a narrow evidence subset. A 12-dimensional feature vector (9 simulatability + 2 concentration + 1 relevance score) is fed to an XGBoost calibrator trained with Optuna hyperparameter search to yield the final confidence score.

## Results

The proposed information-flow method achieves an average AUROC of 0.709 across three QA models (LLaMA-3.2-3B, LLaMA-3-8B, Gemma-3-4B) and three datasets (SQuAD2.0, HotpotQA, MS MARCO), exceeding the runner-up Focus method (0.676). It attains the best Mean Rank (1.17–1.33) for all three base models, with peak AUROC of 0.778 on SQuAD2.0 with LLaMA-3-8B and peak AUPRC of 0.934 on HotpotQA with LLaMA-3.2-3B. The method also achieves the lowest expected calibration error (ECE 0.006–0.062) and highest calibration accuracy (0.644–0.879) among methods producing calibrated confidence scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all eight baselines on the overall Mean Rank across all three QA models. The closest competitor is Focus (Zhang et al., 2023), which leverages max-pooled attention matrices; the proposed method beats it by 3.2–12.3 AUROC points on SQuAD2.0 and by 3.3–13.2 points on MS MARCO, though Focus retains a slight edge on HotpotQA AUPRC (0.944 vs. 0.934 for LLaMA-3.2-3B). Likelihood-based PPL (Margatina et al.) and verbalized P(True) (Kadavath et al.) are consistently outperformed by 10–25 AUROC points across all settings. Consistency-based methods (Regular Entropy, Semantic Entropy) are also surpassed, with Semantic Entropy showing the weakest performance on LLaMA-3-8B (AUROC as low as 0.493 on HotpotQA). Attention-based Attention Score (Sriramanan et al.) performs near chance level (AUROC 0.459–0.529), while embedding-based KnowingMore and Utility Ranker are outperformed by 5–15 AUROC points. Ablation in Table 6 confirms that the gains come from the information-flow features themselves rather than the ranker score alone, as ranker-score-only achieves only 0.504–0.622 AUROC versus 0.617–0.752 for the full information-flow features.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the information-flow framework of Ferrando & Voita (2024) and the Manhattan-distance contribution matrices of Ferrando et al. (2022) from interpretability into uncertainty quantification for retrieval-augmented generation. It departs from prior white-box UQ methods (Sriramanan et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2023) that collapse layer-wise computation into single-layer statistics or max-pooled attention, by instead tracing the full progressive propagation of context tokens through all layers. The Auto-Emergence algorithm replaces the binary threshold-based principal flow extraction with a continuous ranking, and the novel combination of Shapley-decomposed relevance layouts with rank-biased overlap and KL-divergence concentration creates a multi-signal UQ framework not present in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a mechanistically grounded, interpretable UQ method for RAG that outperforms both black-box (perplexity, semantic entropy) and white-box (attention-based) baselines, with the faithfulness ablation offering causal evidence that identified tokens are functionally necessary. A key limitation is the white-box requirement, which excludes closed-source LLMs and limits immediate industrial applicability. Open questions include whether the O(T²d) memory cost can be made practical for very long contexts, whether gray-box approximations of the information-flow signals are feasible, and whether the approach transfers to generation tasks beyond short-form QA.
