## Problem

Large diffusion vision-language models (LDVLMs) suffer from two underexplored failure modes during long-form generation: repetitive token output driven by mask prior drift (all generation tokens, initialized as a shared mask token, progressively converge toward a common representational direction), and degraded visual grounding caused by positional attention collapse (RoPE's inherent locality bias, combined with iterative parallel unmasking, suppresses attention to distant informative visual tokens). These issues worsen as the number of generation steps decreases, undermining the reliability of LDVLMs for detailed multimodal description.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a text instruction prompt as input and produces text descriptions, visual grounding captions, or long-form image captions depending on the task. It operates as a plug-and-play inference-time modification on existing LDVLM checkpoints (LLaDA-V, LaViDa), intervening at the final transformer layer's hidden states and RoPE rotation angles during iterative unmasking decoding. Generation length and unmasking steps vary by benchmark, ranging from 8 tokens over 4 steps (RefCOCOg) to 256 tokens over 128 steps (DetailCaps).

## Method

The paper proposes two training-free, inference-time techniques that require no parameter updates. Mask Prior Suppression (MPS) constructs a low-dimensional prior subspace via PCA on layer-wise vocabulary-mean representations, then adaptively suppresses the component of each masked token's final hidden state that aligns with this prior direction, modifying only k=3 of d=4096 dimensions at the final layer. Monotonic RoPE Scaling (MRS) applies a sigmoid-gated, frequency-index-dependent scaling factor to RoPE rotation angles, progressively amplifying low-frequency components (which govern long-range positional interactions) while preserving high-frequency components, thereby maintaining stable attention to distant visual tokens during iterative unmasking. Both techniques are applied to all masked positions at each generation step and are validated across two LDVLM backbones and nine benchmarks spanning general reasoning, visual grounding, and long-form generation, yielding consistent improvements without retraining.

## Results

On LLaDA-V, the proposed training-free method improves Ferret from 60.4 to 62.9, LLaVA-Bench from 61.3 to 64.1, and DetailCaps from 59.8 to 63.6, while maintaining competitive general reasoning (MME 2003, MMBench 83.3, MMMU 49.3). On LaViDa, gains are substantially larger: RefCOCOg improves from 36.9 to 44.0, Ferret from 25.9 to 35.7, LLaVA-Bench from 39.5 to 46.5, and DetailCaps from 8.3 to 56.1. Hallucination metrics also improve, with CHAIR_s dropping from 29.4 to 27.0 and CHAIR_i from 9.5 to 8.3 on COCO, while AMBER-G coverage is preserved at 61.6.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against autoregressive baselines, LLaDA-V+Ours still trails Qwen2.5-VL and InternVL3 on general reasoning (MME 2003 vs. 2448/2415; MMMU 49.3 vs. 58.6/53.6) and long-form generation (LLaVA-Bench 64.1 vs. 96.6/82.3), but surpasses them on RefCOCOg (65.0 vs. 16.6/67.1) and Ferret (62.9 vs. 53.8/59.0). Compared to the weaker AR baseline LLaVA-1.6, the method wins on MME, MMBench, MMMU, RefCOCOg, and DetailCaps but loses on GQA (61.6 vs. 75.8) and MIA (67.0 vs. 76.1). Among RoPE scaling alternatives (Table 3), Monotonic RoPE Scaling outperforms NTK-aware scaling (65.0 vs. 61.9 RefCOCOg; 62.9 vs. 59.6 Ferret) and YaRN (65.0 vs. 59.3; 62.9 vs. 56.0) on both backbones. Against the DPad decoding-level baseline (Table 10), the method achieves higher scores on all three benchmarks for LLaDA-V (65.0/62.9/63.6 vs. 64.7/60.3/60.2) and LaViDa (44.0/35.7/56.1 vs. 39.6/30.8/7.9). On unified architectures (Table 11), the method improves MMaDA's MME-P from 1070.6 to 1117.2 and MMMU from 31.1 to 32.7, and Lumina-DiMOO's MMMU from 59.3 to 62.0, confirming cross-architecture generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the emerging LDVLM architecture (LLaDA-V, LaViDa) and the RoPE positional encoding framework, but departs from prior RoPE scaling work (NTK-aware, YaRN) that targets autoregressive long-context extrapolation; the authors show these methods actually degrade visual grounding under bidirectional iterative unmasking. It also draws on representation-level interventions in autoregressive LLMs (e.g., inference-time steering vectors) but applies them to the specific mask-token prior geometry unique to masked diffusion decoding. The approach is positioned as orthogonal to structural decoding modifications like DPad suffix dropout or blockwise unmasking, operating instead at the hidden-state and positional-encoding levels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As LDVLMs gain traction as an alternative to autoregressive VLMs, understanding and fixing their intrinsic failure modes (repetition, degraded grounding) is critical for the paradigm to be reliable in practice. The training-free, plug-and-play nature means the fixes can be applied to any existing LDVLM checkpoint without retraining, lowering the barrier to adoption. However, the method's effectiveness depends on the quality of pretrained representations, and its generalization to alternative decoding paradigms (semi-autoregressive, blockwise diffusion) remains untested. The finding that increasing generation steps can *worsen* LaViDa's caption quality due to |eot| token repetition highlights a non-obvious speed-quality relationship that challenges standard assumptions about diffusion decoding.
