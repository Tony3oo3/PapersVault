## Problem

Diffusion-based egocentric motion recovery methods rely on global distribution matching, which fails to enforce explicit geometric constraints, leading to joint misalignment, foot skating, ground penetration, and motion jitter. While RL post-training (e.g., GRPO) could inject fine-grained guidance, directly applying it to motion recovery suffers from vanishing gradients because the strong head-trajectory conditioning collapses intra-group sample diversity, making group-relative advantage normalization numerically unstable.

## Input / Output

The method takes millimeter-accurate central pupil frame (CPF) poses in SE(3) from HMDs like Project Aria (rotation R and translation τ over T=128 frames) as the primary condition, processed through an invariant conditioning function g(·) inherited from EgoAllo. Optional egocentric images can be incorporated via a hand estimation model for additional structural priors. The output is full-body 3D motion in SMPL-H parameterization: 51 local joint rotations (3×3 matrices) and 16 temporal-invariant body shape parameters, converted to global joint positions and velocities via forward kinematics.

## Method

MOTIONGRPO formulates the reverse diffusion sampling process as a multi-step MDP and optimizes the denoiser policy via GRPO within an SDE-based framework, using a hybrid reward that combines a trajectory-conditioned perceptual model (trained with online contrastive learning and on-the-fly hard negative synthesis via InfoNCE) for global visual plausibility with four explicit joint-level sub-rewards (rotation, aligned/unaligned position, velocity) for local precision. The key technical contribution is identifying that the deterministic nature of motion recovery—where head signals tightly constrain outputs—causes near-zero reward variance within GRPO groups, leading to vanishing gradients. To fix this, the method injects temporally smoothed Perlin noise (scaled by λ=0.1) into the translation component of head conditions during training, simulating pseudo out-of-distribution inputs that restore output diversity and stabilize advantage estimation. At inference, the noise injection is deactivated, so the method incurs zero additional computational overhead over the base diffusion model.

## Results

On AMASS, MOTIONGRPO reduces MPJPE from 124.985 mm (EgoAllo) to 114.207 mm and PA-MPJPE from 103.958 mm to 95.512 mm, while also lowering Ground Penetration from 1.143 m to 0.901 m and MPJRE from 8.777° to 8.413°. On RICH, the method achieves MPJPE of 187.223 mm and PA-MPJPE of 169.146 mm, with Ground Penetration dropping from 4.145 m to 3.161 m. With test-time processing (MOTIONGRPO^N), the best results reach 111.776 mm MPJPE and 93.702 mm PA-MPJPE on AMASS, and 184.992 mm MPJPE on RICH. Inference speed and VRAM remain comparable to the baseline (1.139 s, 0.64 GB per sequence).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MOTIONGRPO consistently outperforms EgoEgo (Li et al., 2023) across all metrics on both datasets, with MPJPE improving from 177.231 mm to 114.207 mm on AMASS and from 221.45 mm to 187.223 mm on RICH. Against the strongest baseline EgoAllo (Yi et al., 2025), MOTIONGRPO wins on MPJPE (−10.8 mm on AMASS, −5.5 mm on RICH), PA-MPJPE (−8.4 mm, −3.6 mm), MPJRE (−0.36°, −0.79°), and Ground Penetration (−0.24 m, −0.98 m), but trails on MPJVE (531.2 vs 553.2 mm/s on AMASS is actually worse than EgoAllo's 553.2, yet better than EgoAllo^N's 483.5) and Jitter (2.000 vs 2.394 on AMASS is better, but 3.685 vs 4.135 on RICH is also better). In the post-training paradigm comparison (Table 6), MOTIONGRPO surpasses both direct fine-tuning (MPJPE 124.980) and DPO (MPJPE 118.043), achieving the best MPJPE of 114.207 mm and the lowest Ground Penetration of 0.901 m on AMASS, confirming GRPO with hybrid rewards as a more effective alignment strategy than distribution-matching fine-tuning or pairwise preference optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the EgoAllo diffusion-based egocentric motion recovery framework by adding an RL post-training stage, adapting the GRPO optimization paradigm (originally developed for LLM reasoning) to the diffusion sampling process via the MDP formulation from Black et al. (2024) and SDE-based stochastic sampling from DanceGRPO. The key conceptual departure is identifying that GRPO's group-relative advantage normalization breaks down in strongly-conditioned reconstruction tasks due to low intra-group sample diversity, and proposing temporally smoothed Perlin noise injection on input conditions as a targeted fix. The hybrid reward design—combining a contrastively-trained trajectory-conditioned perceptual model with explicit joint-level constraints—repurposes ideas from DPO-based human mesh recovery (Shen et al., 2025a) and physics-guided motion generation into a unified RL objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in egocentric motion recovery where diffusion models produce visually plausible but geometrically inaccurate motions (foot skating, ground penetration, joint misalignment), and demonstrates that RL post-training with a well-designed reward can close this gap without altering inference cost. The low-intra-group-diversity diagnosis is a useful caution for anyone applying GRPO to constrained reconstruction rather than open-ended generation. However, the framework assumes flat ground, has not been validated beyond a single transformer-diffusion backbone, and the GRPO training cost (~72 GPU hours) remains non-trivial; extending to scene-aware recovery and more sample-efficient optimization are clear open directions.
