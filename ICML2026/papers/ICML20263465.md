## Problem

Adaptive density control in 3D Gaussian Splatting relies on handcrafted heuristics—fixed thresholds on positional gradients, opacity, and scale—that fail to generalize across scenes with varying geometric and textural complexity. These rigid rules cannot adaptively allocate Gaussians where they are most needed, leading to under-densification in detail-rich regions and redundant Gaussians elsewhere.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263465/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a calibrated RGB image set and an SfM point cloud (standard 3DGS initialization) and produces a 3D Gaussian scene representation rendered via differentiable rasterization. At each density-control step, the policy network receives per-Gaussian features (position, covariance, color, opacity) concatenated with a sensitivity score computed over K randomly sampled training views, and outputs a 4-dimensional action probability vector over {maintain, clone, split, prune}. The sensitivity score is computed by analytically simulating the removal of each Gaussian from the α-blending accumulation, requiring no additional rendering passes.

## Method

LeGS reformulates density control as a discrete-action reinforcement learning problem where a 3-layer MLP policy network (SwiGLU activations, hidden dim 64) selects per-Gaussian actions. The reward is a sensitivity-based score that quantifies each Gaussian's marginal contribution to reconstruction quality by measuring the rendering error change induced by its removal, aggregated over its 2D footprint. A key technical contribution is a closed-form derivation of the ablated rendering color C_{-i}(p) = Σ_{i-1} + (C(p) − Σ_i)/(1−α_i) from the α-blending formulation, reducing sensitivity computation from O(N²) re-rendering to O(N) with two linear passes. Training uses PPO with a novel maintain-action-based value (KAV) estimator that replaces the critic network with the average reward of the 'do nothing' action, stabilizing advantage estimation without a separate value function. The policy is optimized jointly with standard 3DGS Gaussian parameter optimization (L1 + SSIM loss) over the full training schedule.

## Results

LeGS achieves state-of-the-art novel view synthesis quality across all three benchmarks: on Mip-NeRF 360 it reaches 28.30 dB PSNR, 0.837 SSIM, and 0.184 LPIPS with 2.17M Gaussians; on Tanks & Temples it attains 24.74 dB PSNR, 0.871 SSIM, and 0.142 LPIPS with 1.63M Gaussians; and on Deep Blending it scores 30.35 dB PSNR, 0.914 SSIM, and 0.227 LPIPS with only 1.05M Gaussians. These gains correspond to +0.81 dB PSNR over vanilla 3DGS on Mip-NeRF 360 and +0.99 dB on Tanks & Temples, while maintaining compact representations (second-fewest Gaussians on two of three datasets). Training time is comparable to FastGS (745 s vs. 599 s on Mip-NeRF 360), with the additional ~150 s attributed to reward computation and RL updates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LeGS outperforms all six baselines on every quality metric (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) across all three datasets. Against vanilla 3DGS, the gains are most pronounced on complex scenes: +0.81 dB PSNR on Mip-NeRF 360 and +0.99 dB on Tanks & Temples, while also reducing the Gaussian count from 2.74M to 2.17M on Mip-NeRF 360. Compared to FastGS (the rendering backend LeGS builds upon), LeGS achieves substantially higher quality (e.g., 28.30 vs. 27.87 dB PSNR on Mip-NeRF 360) at the cost of roughly 2× more Gaussians; the matched-count variant FastGS* (2.49M Gaussians) still trails LeGS by 0.57 dB PSNR, confirming the advantage is not merely from extra capacity. Against 3DGS-MCMC, LeGS wins on all three metrics on every dataset while using fewer Gaussians on Tanks & Temples (1.63M vs. 1.85M). Perceptual-GS and Pixel-GS are dominated on all metrics, with Pixel-GS requiring 2.6× more Gaussians on Mip-NeRF 360 (5.59M vs. 2.17M) yet delivering lower PSNR. Taming-3DGS is the closest competitor on Deep Blending (30.35 vs. 29.98 dB PSNR) but still loses on SSIM and LPIPS.

## Relation to Prior Work

LeGS departs from the entire lineage of heuristic density control in 3DGS (Pixel-GS, Taming-3DGS, Perceptual-GS, 3DGS-MCMC, AbsGS) by replacing handcrafted threshold rules with a learned policy network optimized via PPO. It builds directly on the FastGS rendering backend for efficiency, and borrows the conceptual framing of sensitivity analysis from CNN interpretability (Zeiler & Fergus, 2013) to design its reward function. The key architectural departure is treating each Gaussian as a state and each densification/pruning operation as a discrete action in an MDP, with a novel maintain-action-based value estimator replacing the standard PPO critic to stabilize training over the large state space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to frame 3DGS density control as a reinforcement learning problem, establishing a template for replacing other handcrafted heuristics in the 3DGS pipeline with learned policies. The O(N) closed-form sensitivity computation is a reusable technical contribution that could benefit any method needing per-Gaussian credit assignment. Limitations include the dependence on ground-truth images for reward computation (training-time only), modest additional GPU memory overhead (~2–4 GB over FastGS*), and the policy network's small capacity (3-layer MLP, hidden dim 64) raising questions about generalization to unseen scene types. Open questions include whether the learned policy transfers across scenes without retraining, whether the RL formulation extends to dynamic or large-scale 3DGS settings, and whether the sensitivity reward can be replaced with a learned critic at scale.
