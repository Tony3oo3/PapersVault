## Problem

Native GUI agents built on end-to-end vision-language models have made rapid progress on general-purpose interaction tasks but remain poorly equipped for modern CAPTCHA challenges, which demand robust OCR under heavy distortion, fine-grained spatial localization, continuous drag control, and multi-step visual-semantic reasoning. Meanwhile, specialized CAPTCHA-solving pipelines (e.g., Halligan, Oedipus) are effective on narrow benchmarks but cannot generalize to broader GUI interaction. There is no systematic study of how to endow a single native GUI agent with CAPTCHA-solving capability while preserving its general GUI skills.

## Input / Output

The agent receives raw CAPTCHA screenshots rendered by a dynamic Flask-based engine that injects stochastic CSS variables (padding, margins, color palettes, font sizes) and composes assets from a large visual bank, producing effectively unbounded visual variation. Upon each action, the environment returns structured JSON feedback (solved: true/false). The model outputs an interleaved sequence of chain-of-thought reasoning text and one or more Qwen3-VL-style computer_use tool calls (click, type, drag, scroll, etc.) expressed in a 1000×1000 relative coordinate grid; unlike typical single-action-per-step GUI agents, a single response may contain multiple ordered actions to meet CAPTCHA time limits.

## Method

ReCAP introduces a dynamic CAPTCHA system spanning seven challenge types (Text, Compact Text, Icon Match, Icon Selection, Paged, Slider, Image Grid) that isolate four interaction primitives—OCR, continuous control, spatial localization, and visual-semantic comprehension—with stochastic rendering and an unbounded visual-asset bank to prevent layout overfitting. A scalable data pipeline generates ~160K CAPTCHA trajectories: solution traces are produced by a strong expert model (Qwen2.5-VL-72B) in an answer-conditioned rationale-bootstrapping setup (analogous to STaR), while self-correction traces are built by having the student model attempt each challenge, collecting failures, and prompting the expert to retrospectively diagnose and correct the error. Training uses full-parameter supervised fine-tuning on Qwen3-VL-8B/32B with a unified negative-log-likelihood loss that applies separate group-level weights (λ_think, λ_act) to reasoning and action tokens, preventing long CoT sequences from dominating short action sequences. The final training mixture (~230K samples) also includes ~73K general GUI trajectories from Aguvis and AgentNet to preserve broad interaction capability.

## Results

On the held-out dynamic CAPTCHA benchmark (1,000 instances across 7 challenge types), ReCAP-32B (Instruct) achieves 84.20% overall solve rate, up from 39.30% for its Qwen3-VL-32B-Instruct base, while requiring only 1.38 average model-call steps on successful cases. ReCAP-32B (Thinking) reaches 81.00% and ReCAP-8B (Instruct) reaches 78.60%, all substantially outperforming frontier proprietary agents GPT-5.4 (56.80%) and Gemini-3-Flash-Preview (59.90%). On the real-world Halligan benchmark (26 CAPTCHA variants, zero-shot), ReCAP-8B (Instruct) and ReCAP-32B (Instruct) improve over their Qwen3-VL baselines by 14.50 pp and 11.42 pp respectively, and both slightly outperform the Halligan framework on average. On general GUI benchmarks (Android Control, ScreenSpot-V2, Multimodal-Mind2Web, UI-Vision), the 32B ReCAP variants largely preserve or even improve baseline performance, while 8B variants show some degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Qwen3-VL base models (the direct finetuning targets), ReCAP-32B (Instruct) improves overall CAPTCHA solve rate from 39.30% to 84.20% (+44.9 pp), and ReCAP-8B (Instruct) from 24.70% to 78.60% (+53.9 pp), with the largest per-type gains on Slider (0%→100% for 32B-Instruct) and Image Grid (0%→70.71% for 8B-Instruct). Against UI-TARS-1.5-7B (33.60% overall), all ReCAP variants win by 38–51 pp, and against the specialized Halligan pipeline (25.14% overall), ReCAP wins by 46–59 pp while also using far fewer interaction steps (1.33–1.54 vs. ≥3). Against frontier proprietary agents, ReCAP-32B (Instruct) outperforms GPT-5.4 (56.80%) by 27.4 pp and Gemini-3-Flash-Preview (59.90%) by 24.3 pp on overall solve rate, though Gemini remains competitive on individual categories like Compact Text (71.83% vs. 66.20%) and Slider (79.72% vs. 100.00% for ReCAP). On the real-world Halligan benchmark (zero-shot), ReCAP Instruct variants beat their Qwen3-VL baselines by 14.50 pp and 11.42 pp on average and slightly outperform Halligan overall, but Halligan retains clear advantages on rotation/pattern tasks (e.g., baidu/rotate: 79% vs. 21–22%) and geetest/iconcrush (70% vs. 6–8%). On general GUI benchmarks, ReCAP-32B (Thinking) and ReCAP-32B (Instruct) match or slightly exceed their Qwen3-VL baselines (e.g., UI-Vision Layout IoU improves from 0.4843 to 0.5566), while ReCAP-8B (Instruct) drops on ScreenSpot-V2 (91.12%→89.54%) and UI-Vision Element (29.72%→22.05%), indicating capacity-dependent interference from CAPTCHA-specific training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the native GUI agent line (Qwen3-VL, UI-TARS, OpenCUA) by integrating CAPTCHA solving as an internalized capability rather than relying on the standalone specialized pipelines (Halligan, Oedipus, GeeSolver) that dominate prior CAPTCHA-solving work. The data generation pipeline explicitly borrows the answer-conditioned rationale bootstrapping idea from STaR, but applies it to multi-step GUI interaction traces with a novel self-correction component built from rejected student attempts. The weighted reasoning-action loss is a practical adaptation to the length imbalance between CoT tokens and structured action tokens, and the dynamic CAPTCHA system extends prior evaluation-only benchmarks (Open CaptchaWorld, MCA-Bench) by adding executable feedback and ground-truth APIs needed for closed-loop policy training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that CAPTCHA solving can be absorbed into a general-purpose GUI agent via targeted fine-tuning, reducing the need for separate specialized solvers and their associated latency from repeated API calls. The self-correction data generation from failed trajectories is a reusable technique for any multi-step interactive task where intermediate errors are common. Key limitations include the synthetic-to-real gap (real-world transfer is mixed, with frontier proprietary models still competitive on several categories), the absence of behavioral biometric simulation, and the fact that reasoning traces are answer-conditioned rather than independently verified. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to truly novel CAPTCHA paradigms and whether the capacity-dependent degradation of smaller models on general GUI tasks can be mitigated.
