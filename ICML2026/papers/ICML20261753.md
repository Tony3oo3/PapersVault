## Problem

In RLHF, the reward model remains fixed while the policy distribution shifts continuously, causing increasing reward misalignment and overoptimization. Existing mitigations (uncertainty penalties, RM retraining) rely on surface semantic information and are either computationally prohibitive or fail to capture the deeper behavioral patterns encoded in the policy's internal states. The paper asks whether a lightweight framework can achieve real-time alignment of the reward model to the policy's evolving distribution without full RM retraining.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a query-response pair (x, y) together with the policy's last-layer hidden state sequence h ∈ R^{(S-1)×D_p}, which is extracted during trajectory sampling and refreshed in the final epoch of each policy optimization step. It outputs a scalar reward score r_φ(x, y, h) and, at each training round, updated parameters for only the cross-attention module and scoring head (the LLM backbone of the RM stays frozen). The hidden states are the key unusual input: they are not static features but are recomputed in real time as the policy evolves, making the reward signal dynamically coupled to the current policy distribution.

## Method

R2M augments a standard reward model with a Sequence-to-Token Cross Attention module that uses the policy's last-layer hidden states as keys/values and the RM's Reward Token Embedding as query, producing an information-rich aggregated representation that bridges the semantic gap between heterogeneous policy and reward models. A cosine-scheduled Time-Step-Based Weighted Combination gradually shifts weight from the original embedding toward the policy-feedback-informed representation over training rounds. For optimization, R2M introduces the GREBT loss, which combines a Bradley-Terry preference-ranking term with a Group Reward Entropy term that sharpens within-group reward distributions to counteract group degeneration (where the RM assigns near-identical scores to homogeneous responses). Crucially, only the cross-attention weights and scoring head are updated at each step (the RM's LLM backbone is frozen), and the policy hidden states are refreshed during the final policy-optimization epoch, enabling real-time synchronization at negligible computational cost. Theoretical analysis (Theorem 3.1) shows that incorporating policy hidden states strictly tightens the upper bound on reward misalignment by a factor of (1−γ)^{1/2} relative to the vanilla RM.

## Results

On dialogue tasks (AlpacaEval 2), RLOO+R2M achieves 34.5% LC win rate and 38.2% raw win rate with LLaMA3-8B-Instruct, outperforming vanilla RLOO (28.4% LC, 30.2% WR) by +6.1% and +8.0% respectively. GRPO+R2M reaches 35.6% LC and 39.4% WR on LLaMA3-8B, surpassing GRPO (29.5% LC, 32.6% WR) by +6.1% and +6.8%. On the TL;DR summarization task, RLOO+R2M achieves 81.6% win rate versus 75.3% for vanilla RLOO (+6.3%). The reward model accuracy on UltraFeedback test set improves from 72.3% (vanilla RM) to 78.6% (R2M after training) for LLaMA3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

R2M consistently outperforms all four baseline RL algorithms across every metric and both model scales. Compared to RLOO (Ahmadian et al., 2024), RLOO+R2M gains +2.9% to +6.1% on AlpacaEval LC, +5.2% to +8.0% on raw WR, and +6.3% on TL;DR WR, with the sole difference being the iteratively updated reward model. Against GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), GRPO+R2M improves LC by +2.9% to +6.1% and WR by +2.7% to +6.8%. ReMax (Li et al., 2023) and REINFORCE++ (Hu, 2025) are both substantially outperformed: on LLaMA3-8B, RLOO+R2M exceeds ReMax by +5.8% LC and +7.5% WR, and exceeds REINFORCE++ by +5.2% LC and +6.4% WR. Notably, the ablation variant R2M w/o Train (which uses policy feedback without iterative RM updates) actually degrades performance below the vanilla baselines, confirming that the iterative lightweight optimization is essential rather than the feedback injection alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

R2M builds directly on REINFORCE-style RLHF algorithms (RLOO, GRPO) and departs from prior reward-overoptimization mitigations that rely on surface semantic signals (uncertainty penalties, kernel-based estimates, mutual-information retraining). Its core conceptual departure is importing the implicit-reward insight from DPO and PRIME—namely that policy hidden states encode preference-relevant information—into an explicit reward model via a lightweight cross-attention module, rather than treating the policy and reward model as independent components. The GREBT loss further extends standard Bradley-Terry preference training by adding a group-entropy regularizer to counter the 'group degeneration' phenomenon observed in late-stage RL, a problem previously noted in RLVR but not formally addressed in explicit RM optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

R2M offers a practical, drop-in mechanism for keeping reward models synchronized with evolving policies at negligible computational cost, which is critical for long-horizon RLHF training where distribution drift accumulates. The theoretical guarantee (Theorem 3.1) that policy-feedback fusion strictly tightens the reward-misalignment bound provides a principled justification beyond empirical gains. Open questions include whether the approach scales to much larger policy/RM pairs (tested only up to 8B), whether the cross-attention aggregation generalizes to multi-turn or tool-use settings where hidden-state semantics shift more dramatically, and how the method interacts with generative or process-level reward models. The finding that simply injecting unadapted policy feedback (R2M w/o Train) actively degrades performance underscores that the iterative alignment loop, not the feedback signal alone, is the essential ingredient.
