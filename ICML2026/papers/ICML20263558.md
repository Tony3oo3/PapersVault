## Problem

Existing safe and constrained RL methods cannot jointly enforce a probabilistic reach-avoid constraint (reach target without entering unsafe states with probability ≥ p) and minimize cumulative cost in stochastic environments. Prior approaches either scalarize reach-avoid into the reward (blurring value semantics) or use CMDP-style surrogates that lose the original problem structure, while the only minimum-cost reach-avoid RL method (RC-PPO) is restricted to deterministic dynamics.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a continuous state vector from a stochastic MDP together with a reach-avoid specification (disjoint target set T, unsafe set F, and probability threshold p ∈ (0,1)). It outputs a continuous action, a RAPC value V_{g,h}^π(x) that serves as a certified lower bound on the reach-avoid probability, and a learned compensation factor φ_γ^π(x) that normalizes the Bellman value to counteract discount-induced attenuation. The state space is dynamically partitioned at each iteration: inside the critic-induced surrogate-feasible set the policy optimizes cost, while outside it prioritizes improving reach-avoid probability.

## Method

The paper introduces Reach-Avoid Probability Certificates (RAPCs) grounded in a max-min clamped Bellman operator B^π[V](x) = max{h(x), min{g(x), γ E[V(x')]}} that is a γ-contraction with a unique fixed point, providing denser learning signals than the prior indicator-based formulation. A compensation factor φ_γ^π(x) = E[γ^T | RA_x] is learned from successful rollouts to normalize the Bellman value, yielding a less conservative surrogate p̂_π(x) = −V_{g,h}^π(x)/(M·φ_γ^π(x)) for policy optimization. RAPCPO is an actor-critic algorithm built on PPO that alternates between TD-based critic updates (for both the RAPC value and the cost value), compensation-factor regression on successful trajectories, and a partitioned policy update where in-set gradients for cost and reach-avoid are reconciled via symmetric projection when negatively aligned. The algorithm is shown to converge almost surely to locally optimal policies of the surrogate objective under standard multi-timescale stochastic approximation conditions.

## Results

RAPCPO achieves the lowest average cumulative cost across all four main environments (PointGoal: 61.53, FixedWing: 36.82, Safety Hopper: 43.17, Safety HalfCheetah: 42.65) while simultaneously attaining the highest reach-avoid satisfaction rates. On deterministic benchmarks under the same iteration budget, RAPCPO reaches 78.49% on PointGoal and 88.67% on FixedWing, compared to 62.29% and 73.98% for RC-PPO. The enhanced Bellman formulation with the compensation factor improves reach rates by 30–62 percentage points over the fixed-γ formulation (e.g., Safety Hopper: 94.34% vs. 32.18%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAPCPO outperforms all four baselines on both cost and reach rate across every environment. Against RC-PPO (the closest reach-avoid-specific competitor), RAPCPO achieves substantially higher reach rates under the same iteration budget (78.49% vs. 62.29% on PointGoal; 88.67% vs. 73.98% on FixedWing) and lower cumulative cost in all four environments (e.g., 61.53 vs. 108.34 on PointGoal). Against Saute and CPPO, which use CVaR-based constraints, RAPCPO achieves 15–20% lower cost while maintaining higher reach rates; Saute and CPPO are overly conservative when costs are incurred at every state, leading to degraded task completion. Against RESPO (iterative reachability estimation), RAPCPO reduces cost by 20–50% (e.g., 43.17 vs. 73.41 on Safety Hopper) while achieving higher reach rates. The weighted-sum baseline PPO_β performs worst on cost (236.19 on PointGoal, 121.87 on Safety HalfCheetah), confirming the structural misalignment between scalarized objectives and the minimum-cost reach-avoid problem.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends RC-PPO (So et al., 2024), which solved minimum-cost reach-avoid via Hamilton-Jacobi reachability in deterministic settings, to stochastic environments by replacing HJ reachability with a contraction-based Bellman operator whose fixed point admits a probabilistic certificate interpretation. The indicator-based RAPC characterization (Proposition 4.3) is inherited from Xue (2026) on barrier-like conditions for stochastic reach-avoid verification, but the enhanced Bellman formulation (Eq. 9) is a new contribution that overcomes the sparse learning signal of the indicator-based version. The policy optimization layer combines PPO's clipped surrogate with symmetric gradient projection (Xu et al., 2021; Gu et al., 2024) to resolve conflicts between cost and reach-avoid objectives, and introduces a compensation factor to counteract discount-induced attenuation of the certificate in long-horizon tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to jointly enforce a probabilistic reach-avoid constraint (not merely a safety constraint) and minimize cumulative cost in stochastic environments, filling a gap between safe RL (which handles safety but not liveness) and reachability-based methods (which handle reach-avoid but not cost). The RAPC provides a rigorous, verifiable lower bound on reach-avoid probability, giving practitioners a certificate-based feasibility check rather than a heuristic. Key limitations include: Theorem 4.5 is only a sufficient condition (not necessary and sufficient), the convergence guarantee applies to the surrogate objective rather than the original problem, and the compensation factor is estimated heuristically from successful rollouts. Open questions include finding necessary-and-sufficient certificate conditions that remain computationally tractable, and whether the approach extends to more complex temporal logic specifications beyond simple reach-avoid.
