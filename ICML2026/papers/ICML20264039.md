## Problem

Analytical diffusion models compute the denoising score as an empirical Bayes posterior mean over the entire training set, requiring a full-dataset scan at every timestep with O(ND) complexity. This makes them impractical at scale, and the prevailing assumption that all N training points are equally relevant is both computationally wasteful and can introduce bias from irrelevant or harmful samples. The paper asks whether a dynamically selected, time-varying subset of the training data can preserve estimation accuracy while decoupling inference cost from dataset size.

## Input / Output

The method takes a noisy image x_t at diffusion timestep t (resolutions range from 28×28 for MNIST to 64×64 for CelebA-HQ, AFHQ, and ImageNet-1K), the full training dataset D of N samples, and an optional class label for conditional generation. It outputs the analytical denoising score E[x₀|x_t], which is a weighted average over a dynamically selected Golden Subset rather than the full dataset. The method is entirely training-free and operates as a plug-and-play module atop existing analytical denoisers (e.g., PCA), with no learned parameters.

## Method

GOLDDIFF exploits the phenomenon of Posterior Progressive Concentration: as the signal-to-noise ratio increases during reverse diffusion, the effective posterior support shrinks from the global data manifold to a tight local neighborhood. The method employs a counter-monotonic two-stage retrieval: (1) a coarse screening stage computes ℓ₂ distances in a downsampled proxy space (d = D/16) to select a candidate pool C_t of size m_t that grows as noise decreases, and (2) a fine selection stage computes exact full-resolution distances within C_t to extract the top-k_t Golden Subset, where k_t shrinks as noise decreases. On this purified support, an unbiased streaming softmax replaces the biased weighted softmax used by prior PCA methods, eliminating over-smoothing bias. A rigorous truncation error bound of 2R(N−k)exp(−Δ_k) guarantees convergence to the exact score, and the overall complexity drops from O(ND) to O(Nd + m_t·p_t·D), yielding a 71× speedup on AFHQ and the first successful scaling of analytical diffusion to ImageNet-1K.

## Results

GOLDDIFF achieves a 71× speedup on AFHQ (0.351s vs 24.896s per step) while improving MSE from 0.008 to 0.007 and r² from 0.703 to 0.731 over the SOTA PCA baseline. On CIFAR-10, it reaches MSE 0.007 and r² 0.683 with a 28.1× speedup; on CelebA-HQ, MSE 0.008 and r² 0.836 with a 17.4× speedup. On ImageNet-1K (first analytical diffusion scaling), GOLDDIFF achieves MSE 0.039 / r² 0.458 (unconditional, 10 steps) with ~42× acceleration over PCA, and MSE 0.031 / r² 0.490 (conditional, 10 steps) at 0.100s per step.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the SOTA PCA denoiser (Lukoianov et al., 2025), GOLDDIFF wins on every metric across all three main datasets: it reduces MSE by ~12.5% and improves r² by 1.9–4.2% while accelerating inference by 17–71×. Compared to the Optimal denoiser (De Bortoli, 2022), GOLDDIFF is dramatically superior in both quality (r² 0.683 vs −0.401 on CIFAR-10; 0.731 vs −0.713 on AFHQ) and speed, since the Optimal denoiser suffers from memorization and produces negative r². Against Kamb et al. (2024), GOLDDIFF achieves lower MSE (0.007 vs 0.011 on CIFAR-10) and is 69× faster (0.087s vs 5.980s), while also using less memory. The Wiener filter is the only method faster than GOLDDIFF on small datasets (0.011s vs 0.087s on CIFAR-10), but it lags substantially in quality (r² 0.553 vs 0.683) and requires significantly more memory (0.290 GB vs 0.088 GB on CIFAR-10). On ImageNet-1K, GOLDDIFF outperforms both vanilla PCA and its unbiased variant in both unconditional and conditional settings, with the unbiased PCA variant actually degrading with more steps (r² drops from 0.473 to 0.411 at T=100 for conditional), while GOLDDIFF improves (r² 0.490→0.578).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the analytical diffusion line (Kamb & Ganguli, 2024; De Bortoli, 2022; Lukoianov et al., 2025), which formulates the denoising score as an empirical Bayes posterior mean over the training set, by challenging the assumption that full-dataset scanning is necessary at every timestep. It departs from static k-NN retrieval strategies (Sheynin et al., 2023; Blattmann et al., 2022; Niedoba et al., 2024) by introducing a time-varying, coarse-to-fine selection mechanism grounded in the newly identified Posterior Progressive Concentration phenomenon. The method also replaces the biased weighted streaming softmax used by the SOTA PCA denoiser (Lukoianov et al., 2025) with a simple unbiased streaming softmax applied only to a dynamically filtered 'Golden Subset,' decoupling numerical stability from estimation bias.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The first successful scaling of analytical diffusion to ImageNet-1K (albeit at 64×64) and the 71× speedup on AFHQ while matching or exceeding full-scan quality make a previously impractical, mathematically transparent framework viable at scale. The clean truncation error bound (Theorem 1) provides theoretical justification for the dynamic schedule, and the plug-and-play, training-free design means the technique can be dropped into existing analytical baselines without retraining. Open questions include whether the low-dimensional proxy metric and hand-crafted monotonic schedules generalize to higher resolutions, non-image modalities, or learned adaptive schedules, and whether the quality gap between analytical and neural denoisers narrows further with this acceleration.
