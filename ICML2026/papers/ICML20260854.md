## Problem

Existing exponents for classifying computational hardness in high-dimensional feature learning (information exponent, generative exponent) are either bypassable by data reuse or restricted to brittle, fine-tuned targets. Within the generic symmetry-induced regime (GE=2), it was unclear whether and how noise could create large statistical-to-computational gaps, and no simple activation-dependent quantity was known to govern the magnitude of such gaps across both single-index and multi-index settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260854/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The analysis takes as input a scalar activation function σ:ℝ→ℝ (polynomially bounded), a signal-to-noise ratio λ (single-index) or noise strength Δ and width p (multi-index), and a sample ratio α=n/d in the proportional limit d,n→∞. It produces sharp asymptotic scaling laws: the computational weak-recovery threshold α_Algo as a power of λ^{-β*} or p^{β*}, the information-theoretic threshold as λ^{-1} or Θ(p), and the specialization threshold for committee machines. The NSE β* itself is a simple integer computed as the smallest β with E[σ^β(z)(z²−1)]≠0, making the classification directly readable from the activation's moment structure.

## Method

The core technique is a small-noise (λ→0) or large-width (p→∞) Taylor expansion of the proven AMP weak-recovery threshold formula (α_WR^Alg)^{-1}=E[(E[z²−1|y])²], showing that the first non-vanishing term appears at order λ^{β*} (or p^{-β*}), where β* is the NSE. For the information-theoretic side, a Taylor expansion of the replica-symmetric free entropy functional f_RS(m) around λ=0 yields a threshold scaling as λ^{-1} independent of β*, establishing the gap. For multi-index committee machines, the central limit theorem reduces the p-component sum to an effective single-index problem with SNR λ=1/p, so the same expansion machinery applies with p playing the role of 1/λ. For non-even activations, a large-p expansion of the replica-symmetric potential under the committee-symmetry ansatz characterizes when the unspecialized subspace becomes locally unstable, yielding the specialization threshold.

## Results

For single-index models with even activation and NSE β*, the computational weak-recovery threshold scales as α_WR^Alg = Θ(λ^{-β*}) (Theorem 3.2), while the information-theoretic threshold scales as α_WR^IT = Θ(λ^{-1}) (Theorem 3.3), yielding a widening statistical-to-computational gap of Θ(λ^{-β*}) whenever β* > 1. For even separable multi-index (committee) machines, the specialization threshold satisfies α_spec^Alg = α_WR^Alg = Θ_p(p^{β*}) (Theorem 4.2), contrasting with the information-theoretic scaling α_spec^IT = Θ_p(p). For general (non-even) activations, Result 4.3 shows that AMP achieves specialization at α = Θ(p) if and only if β* = 1, while for β* > 1 the AMP iterates remain unspecialized for all α = o(p^{3/2}).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against competing algorithms on datasets; instead, it positions the NSE as a refinement of two prior classification frameworks. The Information Exponent (IE) of Ben Arous et al. (2021) is shown to be too coarse: it can be bypassed by data reuse or non-square losses, and does not capture the noise-dependent hardness identified here. The Generative Exponent (GE) of Damian et al. (2024) provides a more robust symmetry-based dichotomy (GE=1 vs. GE=2), but treats all GE=2 models uniformly; the NSE splits this class into β*=1 (computationally accessible at all noise levels) and β*>1 (genuine statistical-to-computational gap at high noise). The computational thresholds derived here (via Taylor expansion of the AMP formula from Mondelli & Montanari, 2019) are consistent with prior numerical observations but now come with a closed-form scaling law in λ governed by β*. For the multi-index setting, the results confirm and sharpen predictions from Aubin et al. (2019) and Citton et al. (2025) that relied on annealed approximations, providing rigorous large-p scalings for even activations and analytical (non-rigorous) predictions for general activations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the statistical-physics and AMP-based analysis of single-index and committee-machine models (Mondelli & Montanari 2019; Barbier et al. 2019; Aubin et al. 2019; Troiani et al. 2025) by performing large-noise and large-p Taylor expansions of the known threshold formulas. It positions itself as a refinement of the generative exponent (GE) framework of Damian et al. (2024): while GE classifies models into broad learnability regimes, the newly introduced Noise Sensitivity Exponent (NSE) resolves the internal structure of the GE=2 (symmetry-induced) class, revealing that noise robustness and feature specialization are governed by a single activation-dependent quantity. The paper departs from the IE/GE literature by showing that even in the 'generic' GE=2 regime—previously considered computationally tractable with O(d) samples—arbitrarily large statistical-to-computational gaps can emerge.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The NSE provides a simple, computable diagnostic (a single expectation over the activation) that unifies two previously separate phenomena: noise-induced computational hardness in single-index models and the specialization transition in wide committee machines. This suggests a common mechanism—higher-order Hermite cancellations in the activation—underlying both barriers, offering a cleaner organizing principle than the brittle GE>2 constructions. Key open questions include whether the conjectured α_spec^Alg = Θ(p^{β*}) scaling for generic (non-even) activations can be rigorously proven (Open Question 4.5), whether Result 4.3 can be made fully rigorous beyond the statistical-physics symmetry ansatz, and whether the Gaussian-covariate assumption can be relaxed. The framework also naturally extends to hierarchical multi-index models with power-law signal strengths, where the NSE directly controls scaling laws for mean-squared error.
