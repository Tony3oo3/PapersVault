## Problem

Goal-Oriented Navigation requires robots to localize an unobserved goal within a partially explored BEV map, but existing map-based methods either rely on complete maps or suffer from inconsistent semantic association in unobserved regions. Prior diffusion-based map completion approaches focus on semantic correlations while neglecting the structural consistency of obstacle layouts, leading to room boundary drift and semantic hallucinations in generated regions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264091/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB-D observations and robot pose to construct egocentric semantic maps (40 class channels) and obstacle maps (occupancy + free-space), which are rendered as visualized label maps with fixed color palettes at 256×256 resolution for diffusion and 480×480 for navigation. The diffusion model takes masked versions of both maps (unobserved regions zeroed by a binary mask) and produces a predicted label map L_t^P = [S_t^P, C_t^P] that fills only the unobserved regions while observed regions remain anchored by live sensor data. Goal candidates are extracted from the predicted map via HDBSCAN clustering with a composite score weighting density (50%), cluster size (40%), and distance to start (10%).

## Method

PLMD employs two decoupled diffusion networks within an SDE-based DDPM framework: an obstacle map network G_φ is pre-trained first to learn robust geometric priors, then frozen while a semantic map network G̃_φ is trained conditioned on the obstacle prediction c_{τ-1} at each denoising step. The conditioning is implemented via SPADE (spatially-adaptive denormalization) residual blocks that modulate semantic feature maps with spatially-varying scale and shift parameters derived from the obstacle map, providing stable structural constraints during early denoising steps when sparse semantic signals are weakest. Training data (238,800 observation pairs) is collected by running Frontier-Based Exploration in HM3D_v0.1, storing incomplete label maps every 25 steps against the fully explored ground-truth map. At inference, PLMD operates as a plug-and-play module that activates every 50 navigation steps after step 100, generating predicted label maps that any downstream navigation strategy (RL, LLM-based, or frontier-based) can use for long-term goal selection without retraining.

## Results

On ObjectNav (HM3D_v0.1), PLMD (SSL) achieves 61.8% SR and 30.4% SPL, outperforming the best prior method SGM (60.2% SR) by 5.4% relative. On Instance-ImageNav (HM3D_v0.2), PLMD combined with RL reaches 77.6% SR and 28.3% SPL, surpassing IEVE (70.2% SR) by 7%. On Multi-Robot ObjectNav (HM3D_v0.2), PLMD achieves 76.2% SR and 40.6% SPL, exceeding MCoCoNav (71.6% SR, 38.7% SPL). In the diffusion-method comparison (Table 2), PLMD attains 34.28 PSNR versus 31.49 (StrDiffusion) and 29.90 (IR-SDE), confirming superior map completion quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PLMD (SSL) outperforms all non-RL baselines across every task and dataset: on ON (HM3D_v0.1) it beats SGM by +1.6% SR (0.618 vs 0.602) and SG-Nav by +7.8% SR; on ON (HM3D_v0.2) it surpasses MCoCoNav by +3.1% SR (0.665 vs 0.634); on IIN (HM3D_v0.2) it exceeds UniGoal by +4.0% SR (0.642 vs 0.602); and on MRON (HM3D_v0.2 / MP3D) it leads MCoCoNav by +4.6% / +2.3% SR respectively. When combined with RL (SSL+RL), PLMD further dominates: on IIN it surpasses IEVE by +7.4% SR (0.776 vs 0.702) and on ON (HM3D_v0.1) it reaches 0.656 SR, well above SGM's 0.602. In the dedicated diffusion-method comparison (Table 2, MRON task), PLMD outperforms StrDiffusion by +3.3% SR and +2.80 PSNR, and IR-SDE by +6.4% SR and +4.39 PSNR, confirming that obstacle-guided label-map diffusion yields both better navigation outcomes and higher-fidelity map completion than pixel-level or structure-only inpainting approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DDPM/SDE diffusion framework (Ho et al., 2020; Song et al., 2020) and repurposes it for BEV label map completion in embodied navigation, a setting where prior diffusion work (Ji et al., 2024) focused solely on semantic pixel generation without structural constraints. The key architectural departure is the introduction of a two-stage denoising pipeline where a pre-trained obstacle map network provides spatially-adaptive conditioning (via SPADE, Park et al., 2019) to the semantic denoising network, explicitly encoding the structural-semantic coupling that prior map-prediction methods (Georgakis et al., 2021; Zhang et al., 2024) handled implicitly or ignored. Unlike end-to-end RL navigators or LLM-based planners that require complete maps, PLMD is positioned as a plug-and-play auxiliary module that augments any existing map-based strategy without retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design and cross-task generalization (ON, IIN, MRON) without retraining make this a practically useful component for the embodied navigation community, particularly for teams already invested in map-based strategies. The obstacle-as-structural-prior insight addresses a real failure mode (room boundary drift, semantic hallucination in sparse BEV maps) that purely semantic inpainting approaches cannot resolve. However, the work remains confined to indoor simulator benchmarks (HM3D/MP3D), the 10–20% inference time overhead limits real-time applicability, and the paper acknowledges that prediction errors could lead to unsafe robot behavior in deployment. Open questions include extension to outdoor/dynamic scenes, reducing diffusion steps further without quality loss, and whether the label-map formulation generalizes beyond the 40-class HM3D taxonomy.
