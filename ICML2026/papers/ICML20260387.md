## Problem

Entropy coding (e.g., rANS) is the primary latency bottleneck in learned image compression because its sequential control flow is difficult to parallelize on GPUs, often taking over 100 ms per image. Prior attempts to eliminate entropy coding either degrade compression to JPEG-level quality (COIN) or require prohibitive diffusion-model inference cost (OSCAR). The paper asks how to remove both statistical and correlation redundancy without entropy coding while preserving competitive compression performance.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image of arbitrary resolution (evaluated at 512×768 up to 4K) and produces a fixed-length bitstream of concatenated RVQ indices from five quantizers (one hyperprior Q_z and four latent quantizers Q_1–Q_4), preceded by a 32-bit header encoding image dimensions and a rate-selection key. Multi-rate operation is achieved by selecting how many RVQ codebooks (m ∈ {1,…,5}) to use per quantizer at inference time, with no retraining needed. The decoder reconstructs the image entirely on GPU from these indices, requiring no CPU-side entropy-model synchronization.

## Method

EF-LIC removes redundancy through two GPU-parallelizable mechanisms: (1) unconstrained vector quantization, which the paper proves drives the index distribution toward the maximum-entropy bound (Proposition 3.1), thereby eliminating statistical redundancy without variable-length codes; and (2) a context-conditioned autoregressive transform that reparameterizes each latent group via an affine projection σ⁻¹⊙(y−μ) conditioned on previously decoded groups and a hyperprior feature, directly reducing inter-latent correlation in the representation domain rather than predicting conditional probabilities for an entropy coder. Theoretical analysis (Theorem 3.5) shows this representation-domain decorrelation achieves the same rate-distortion performance as typical LIC with context modeling and entropy coding, up to a vanishing entropy-gap overhead. The backbone uses DC Blocks (depthwise-separable convolutions) from DCVC-RT, and training combines L1, LPIPS, adaptive PatchGAN adversarial, and codebook losses on ImageNet. A lightweight variant EF-LIC-s drops the hyperprior and reduces codebook sizes to match the decoding latency of a plain VQ baseline while retaining most of the compression gain.

## Results

EF-LIC achieves a BD-rate of −67.86% (LPIPS) on Kodak, −55.46% on Tecnick, −62.33% on DIV2K, and −63.22% on CLIC 2020 relative to MS-ILLM, the strongest GAN-based generative codec. The lightweight variant EF-LIC-s still delivers −55.38% BD-rate on Kodak while using only 11.51M parameters and 179 GFLOPs. In terms of latency, EF-LIC encodes in 17.62 ms and decodes in 13.72 ms per image on an A100 GPU, which is over 9× faster encoding and 10× faster decoding than MS-ILLM (165.38 ms / 147.79 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EF-LIC outperforms all 13 baselines on BD-rate (LPIPS) across every dataset. Against the anchor MS-ILLM, EF-LIC reduces bitrate by 67.86% on Kodak while also being 9× faster to encode and 10× faster to decode. Among diffusion-based methods, EF-LIC beats RDEIC (−52.08% vs −67.86% on Kodak) by 15.78 percentage points and OSCAR (−37.31%) by 30.55 points, while using roughly 25–40× fewer FLOPs. Against the strongest pixel-level LIC (DCVC-RT, 393.72% BD-rate) and traditional H.266/VVC (313.84%), the gap is over 380 percentage points, reflecting the fundamental advantage of perceptual optimization. In the supplementary Table 6, EF-LIC also beats the most recent generative codecs GLC (21.50%), DLF (20.63%), StableCodec (8.03%), and OneDC (0.00%) on Kodak LPIPS, while using only 279 GFLOPs and 35.74M parameters versus 2466–7143 GFLOPs and 164–1406M parameters for those methods. The lightweight EF-LIC-s (11.51M params) still surpasses RDEIC on Kodak (−55.38% vs −52.08%) and all other baselines, confirming that the gains come from the decorrelation architecture rather than model scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

EF-LIC builds directly on the DCVC-RT backbone (Jia et al., 2025) for its analysis/synthesis transforms and context model, on RVQGAN (Kumar et al., 2023) for multi-rate residual vector quantization, and on the hyperprior architecture of Ballé et al. (2018). Its key departure from the standard LIC pipeline (Ballé et al., Minnen et al., Cheng et al., He et al.) is the complete removal of entropy coding: instead of predicting conditional probabilities and encoding them with rANS, EF-LIC uses unconstrained VQ to push index distributions toward uniformity (eliminating statistical redundancy) and a context-conditional autoregressive transform in the representation domain to decorrelate latents (eliminating correlation redundancy). This contrasts with prior entropy-coding-free attempts like COIN (Dupont et al., 2021) and OSCAR (Guo et al., 2025), which either matched only JPEG-level quality or incurred prohibitive diffusion-based inference costs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the sequential, CPU-bound entropy coding step, EF-LIC enables end-to-end GPU execution, ONNX/TorchScript export, and reliable cross-device deployment—capabilities that hybrid GPU-CPU LIC systems fundamentally lack. The theoretical guarantees (Proposition 3.1, 3.3, Theorem 3.5) provide a rigorous information-theoretic foundation showing the performance gap to entropy-coded LIC can be made arbitrarily small, which is a strong conceptual contribution beyond empirical validation. Open questions remain: codebook sizes are hand-designed, RVQ runtime is still non-negligible at high resolutions, and performance under pixel-wise metrics (PSNR) is less competitive, suggesting the approach is best suited for perceptual-quality compression rather than all distortion regimes.
