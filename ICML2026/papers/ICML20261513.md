## Problem

Approximation theory for standard Transformers (with Softmax, ReLU, and residual connections) has been limited to Universal Approximation Theorems, with no quantitative error rates. Existing approximation bounds for Hölder or Lipschitz classes rely on Transformer variants that omit residual connections in feed-forward layers, and no approximation lower bounds for Transformers have been established. This paper closes both gaps by deriving matching upper and lower bounds for the standard architecture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261513/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a d₀-dimensional Hölder function f ∈ H^α_{d₀}([0,1]^{d₀}, K) with smoothness α ∈ (0,1] and norm constraint K, reshaped into a d×L sequence matrix (d₀ = dL) via a reshape layer, and a Transformer encoder with D blocks. It produces theoretical bounds: an upper bound of O(ε^{-d₀/α}) blocks for ε-approximation and a lower bound of Ω(ε^{-d₀/(4α)}), plus an excess risk convergence rate of Õ(N^{-α/(2d₀+α)}) for finite-sample regression. The analysis is purely constructive and theoretical—no model is trained; the bounds characterize the minimum architectural complexity required.

## Method

The upper bound is established via a three-module Transformer construction that preserves all residual connections and uses standard Softmax: (i) a quantization module of O(ε^{-1/α}) ReLU feed-forward layers with positional encoding maps continuous inputs to discrete grid points with separation margin δ*; (ii) a single Softmax self-attention layer acts as a contextual mapping, generating unique contextual IDs for each grid point by exploiting token-wise separatedness; (iii) a value mapping of O(ε^{-d₀/α}) feed-forward layers assigns target outputs anchored on those contextual IDs. The lower bound leverages the VC-dimension upper bound of Anthony & Bartlett (2009, Theorem 8.14), which accounts for exponential operations in Softmax, to show that D⁴ ≳ VCdim(T_R^D) ≳ ε^{-d₀/α}, yielding D ≳ ε^{-d₀/(4α)}. A key design choice is avoiding Hardmax-to-Softmax transformations (which cause weight norm explosion) and instead using the single-layer contextual mapping of Kajitsuka & Sato (2024) directly, while a novel network construction strategy retains all residual connections through deep block compositions.

## Results

The paper establishes that a standard Transformer (with Softmax, ReLU, and residual connections) composed of at most O(ε^{-d₀/α}) blocks can approximate any bounded Hölder function with d₀-dimensional input and smoothness α ∈ (0,1] to accuracy ε. The first approximation lower bound for Transformers is proven: at least Ω(ε^{-d₀/(4α)}) blocks are required, yielding a gap of a factor of 4 in the exponent. For the regression application, the excess risk converges at rate N^{-α/(2d₀+α)} log(N), which is sub-optimal compared to the minimax rate N^{-2α/(d₀+2α)} for deep ReLU networks, attributed to the loose O(D⁴) VC-dimension bound.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper, so comparisons are against prior approximation-theoretic results rather than empirical baselines. The key distinction is that all prior works providing quantitative upper bounds for Hölder or Lipschitz classes (Jiao et al. 2025/2026, Hu et al. 2025a/b) rely on Transformer variants that omit residual connections in feed-forward layers, whereas this paper's O(ε^{-d₀/α}) upper bound holds for the standard architecture with all residual connections preserved. No prior work had established any approximation lower bound for Transformers; this paper's Ω(ε^{-d₀/(4α)}) lower bound via VC-dimension analysis is the first of its kind. The upper bound rate matches that of Jiao et al. (2025/2026) for the Hölder class, but the architectural setting is strictly more general (standard vs. variant). The lower bound leaves a factor-of-4 gap in the exponent relative to the upper bound, which the authors attribute to the loose O(D⁴) VC-dimension upper bound (vs. the near-tight O(D²) known for ReLU FNNs) and the inability to exploit higher smoothness in the value mapping construction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Transformer approximation theory lineage initiated by Yun et al. (2020) and refined by Kajitsuka & Sato (2024), who established the single-layer Softmax contextual mapping framework. The key departure is that all prior quantitative bounds (Jiao et al. 2025/2026, Hu et al. 2025a/b, Takakura & Suzuki 2023) relied on Transformer variants omitting residual connections in feed-forward layers, whereas this work preserves the full standard architecture (Softmax + ReLU + residual connections). Additionally, by adapting the VC-dimension upper bound technique from Anthony & Bartlett (2009) and Park et al. (2021), the authors introduce the first approximation lower bound for Transformers, a direction entirely absent from prior Transformer approximation literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first paper to provide both upper and lower approximation bounds for standard Transformers on the Hölder class, establishing a quantitative baseline for the architecture's expressive power that was previously only characterized by universal approximation theorems. The acknowledged gap between the upper bound O(ε^{-d₀/α}) and lower bound Ω(ε^{-d₀/(4α)}) blocks, along with the sub-optimal regression convergence rate (attributed to the loose O(D⁴) VC-dimension bound versus the near-tight O(D²) for ReLU FNNs), clearly delineates open problems: tightening the VC-dimension analysis, exploiting higher smoothness in value mapping, and understanding multi-attention-layer compositions. For practitioners, the depth-width trade-off corollary and the optimal model complexity D ∝ N^{d₀/(4d₀+2α)} provide concrete architectural sizing guidance for regression tasks.
