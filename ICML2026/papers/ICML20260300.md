## Problem

In low-data imitation learning (as few as 20 transitions), behavioral cloning fails to generalize and existing off-policy adversarial IL methods (DAC, SAM) require gradient penalization for training stability, adding computational overhead and a sensitive hyperparameter. The paper asks whether a reward learning objective can be derived that is theoretically grounded in optimal transport, practically stable without gradient penalties, and lightweight enough to scale to high-dimensional tasks like Humanoid under extreme data scarcity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260300/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes expert demonstration trajectories (state-action pairs, state-state pairs, or states only) subsampled at a configurable rate, alongside online environment interactions stored in a replay buffer. The input space X is S×A, S×S, or S depending on whether expert actions are available, and the reward model processes the triplet (s_t, a_t, s_{t+1}) to accommodate all three settings. The output is a learned policy π_θ (SAC actor-critic) and a reward function r_ξ(x) = exp(-h_ξ(x)) bounded in [0,1], where h_ξ is a 1-Lipschitz potential measuring predictor-prior discrepancy in an m-dimensional embedding space.

## Method

NGT derives its adversarial reward from the problem of learning from random priors: a frozen randomly-initialized network f_ξ† provides deterministic m-dimensional targets, and a predictor f_ξ is trained to match them on expert data while being pushed away on agent data via L(ξ) = E_expert[h_ξ] − E_agent[h_ξ], where h_ξ(x) = ℓ(f_ξ(x), f_ξ†(x)). The paper proves this objective is equivalent to maximizing the earth mover's distance between expert and agent distributions under the Kantorovich-Rubinstein duality, with the 1-Lipschitz constraint enforced via spectral normalization and orthogonal initialization—eliminating the gradient penalty required by all other off-policy adversarial baselines. The pairing loss ℓ is typically Huber loss, or a histogram loss (Gaussian type) for high-dimensional tasks like Humanoid, which introduces an architectural asymmetry where the predictor outputs N×m bin distributions compared to the prior's m scalar targets. The agent is trained with SAC using the learned reward r_ξ = exp(-h_ξ), with policy, critic, and reward networks updated jointly in an off-policy fashion with a shared replay buffer.

## Results

NGT achieves expert-level normalized returns (≈1.0) across all five Gymnasium environments (Ant-v4, HalfCheetah-v4, Pusher-v4, Walker2d-v4, Humanoid-v4) in the ultra-low-data regime of 1, 4, and 11 demonstrations (as few as 20 transitions), outperforming all baselines in 720 total experiments. On the high-dimensional Humanoid-v4 task (376-dim state, 17-dim action), NGT is the only method besides DiffAIL to reach expert performance, and it does so without gradient penalization. In the state-only (state-state) setting where expert actions are unavailable, NGT remains the only method to make consistent progress on Humanoid-v4. Runtime is competitive at 712 steps/s on Humanoid-v4 and 967 steps/s on Walker2d-v4, comparable to the fastest baselines (W-DAC/SAM at 763/978 sps).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NGT outperforms all six baseline families across the board. Against BC (behavioral cloning), NGT clearly wins in the low-data regime (1–4 demos) where BC suffers compounding errors, though BC(1) with the full 11K pairs matches NGT on Humanoid-v4 when data is abundant. Against PWIL (primal EMD), NGT achieves higher returns and is ~13% faster (712 vs. 631 sps on Humanoid). Against DAC/SAM (JS-GAN discriminator), NGT wins on all environments and critically does not require gradient penalization, which DAC/SAM needs for stable off-policy training. Against W-DAC/SAM and MMD-DAC/SAM (dual OT / MMD variants), NGT outperforms despite both also targeting EMD, suggesting that the random-prior-based potential function yields smoother learning dynamics than a raw WGAN critic. Against RED* (offline random-prior detection without the adversarial agent term), NGT's binary adversarial formulation provides a stronger reward signal. Against DiffAIL (diffusion-based AIL), NGT wins on all non-Humanoid tasks and matches or exceeds DiffAIL on Humanoid-v4 while being faster (712 vs. 659 sps) and not requiring a diffusion model in the reward network.

## Relation to Prior Work

NGT sits at the intersection of three research threads: off-policy adversarial imitation learning (GAIL → DAC → SAM), optimal transport via the Kantorovich-Rubinstein dual (as in WGAN), and prediction from random priors (RND, RED). Its key departure is replacing the binary JS-divergence discriminator or WGAN critic with a predictor-prior matching loss, showing that this seemingly unrelated objective is in fact an EMD maximization over 1-Lipschitz potentials. The method further repurposes distributional (histogram) losses originally designed for value learning in RL as the pairing loss, enabling scaling to high-dimensional Humanoid tasks where standard regression losses fail.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NGT demonstrates that humanoid locomotion can be learned from as few as 20 expert transitions without gradient penalization—a requirement that prior off-policy adversarial IL methods (DAC, SAM, W-DAC) could not dispense with. The OT equivalence provides a clean theoretical lens, and the concentration bound quantifies sample efficiency. Open questions include whether the random-prior objective generalizes to broader generative modeling tasks, whether the method extends to partially observable or multi-agent settings, and how it scales beyond MuJoCo/DMC to real-world robotics where demonstrations are genuinely scarce.
