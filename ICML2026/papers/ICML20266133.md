## Problem

Existing hallucination detection methods—whether entropy-based (black-box) or static representation-based (white-box)—fail on 'Stubborn Hallucinations,' where LLMs produce factually incorrect answers with high confidence and low output entropy, mimicking the statistical signature of robust knowledge. No prior method can distinguish a genuinely grounded fact from an entrenched memorized error when both appear equally confident at the output level.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text query and the LLM's dense input embedding matrix (E ∈ R^{L×d}) as input, operating in a reference-free setting where the model's own greedy-decoded response serves as a pseudo-label. An external NER pipeline extracts the key entity from the response to construct a binary token mask, ensuring gradients are computed only over factual content rather than syntactic filler. The output is a single scalar EPGS score S, where higher values indicate greater geometric instability and thus higher hallucination likelihood; the score is threshold-independent and evaluated via AUROC.

## Method

EPGS (Embedding-Perturbed Gradient Sensitivity) is grounded in the Curvature Hypothesis: generalized facts reside in flat minima (small Hessian spectral norm) while stubborn hallucinations occupy sharp minima (large spectral norm) due to brittle memorization. Rather than computing the prohibitively expensive Hessian, the method injects isotropic Gaussian noise into the input embedding space and measures the resulting gradient spike in the final transformer block, leveraging a proven Input-Parameter Isomorphism (Lemma 3.3) that makes input perturbation locally equivalent to parameter perturbation. The EPGS score couples two signals: the clean gradient magnitude (capturing local steepness) and the directional divergence (1 − CosSim) between clean and perturbed gradients (capturing stochastic instability), yielding a unified metric sensitive to both sharp memorized errors and unstable transient hallucinations. Gradient computation is restricted to the last transformer block with entity-token masking, making the backward-pass cost independent of model depth and avoiding the O(P²) complexity of full Hessian estimation.

## Results

EPGS achieves the highest AUROC across all 12 model-dataset configurations in the general hallucination detection benchmark (Table 1), with particularly strong gains on reasoning tasks: 0.9732 on SVAMP (Llama-3-8B) and 0.8947 (Llama-2-7B). On knowledge-intensive tasks, EPGS outperforms the strongest white-box baseline (Eigenscore) by up to 9% (TriviaQA, Llama-3-8B: 0.8127 vs. 0.7226) and the strongest black-box baseline (Semantic Entropy) by up to 21% (SQuAD, Llama-2-7B: 0.6924 vs. 0.4839). On the Stubborn Hallucination Subset (Table 2), where entropy-based methods collapse near random (SE: 0.5842 on Llama-2-7B SQuAD), EPGS maintains 0.7373 AUROC, a 15-point margin over the best baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EPGS outperforms all five baselines across every model-dataset configuration in the main benchmark. Against black-box methods, the gap is most dramatic on knowledge-intensive tasks: Semantic Entropy (Farquhar et al., 2024) drops to 0.4839 AUROC on SQuAD (Llama-2-7B), essentially random, while EPGS reaches 0.6924; P(False) (Kadavath et al., 2022) similarly fails on SQuAD (0.5279 on Llama-3-8B) but remains competitive on SVAMP. Against white-box methods, EPGS surpasses Eigenscore (Chen et al., 2024) by 9.0% on TriviaQA (Llama-3-8B: 0.8127 vs. 0.7226) and by 9.7% on SQuAD (Llama-3-8B: 0.7742 vs. 0.6775), and outperforms Effective Rank (Wang et al., 2025) by up to 10.7% on SVAMP (Llama-2-7B: 0.8947 vs. 0.7860). On the Stubborn Hallucination Subset, where all entropy-based baselines degrade to near-random (SE: 0.5842, PF: 0.4978 on SQuAD), EPGS maintains 0.7373–0.7816 AUROC, confirming that gradient curvature captures a signal orthogonal to predictive confidence. The only configuration where EPGS does not lead is SQuAD-F1 labelling (Table 5), where Effective Rank (0.7578) and Semantic Entropy (0.7511) marginally exceed EPGS (0.7109) on Llama-2-7B, which the authors attribute to lexical rigidity of the F1 metric rather than a detection failure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant uncertainty-based framing of hallucination detection (entropy, semantic consistency, static representation analysis) by importing the flat-vs-sharp minima distinction from generalization theory (Hochreiter & Schmidhuber, Keskar et al.) into the LLM hallucination setting. Rather than building on a single prior detection method, it repurposes the memorization-vs-generalization literature (Feldman, Arpit et al.) to argue that stubborn hallucinations occupy topologically distinct regions of the loss landscape, and introduces a practical proxy (embedding-perturbed gradient sensitivity) for the otherwise intractable Hessian spectrum. The Input-Parameter Isomorphism lemma connects this to adversarial robustness ideas (input perturbations revealing model sensitivity) but applies them to a fundamentally different diagnostic purpose.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and empirically validates a concrete failure mode of all current detection methods: when a model has memorized an incorrect fact, entropy and representation metrics collapse to near-random performance, creating a blind spot in high-stakes deployments. The geometric framing offers a principled explanation for *why* these methods fail and provides a theoretically grounded alternative. Key open questions include extending the entity-centric, white-box framework to closed-source models, free-form generation without discrete entities, and producing fine-grained diagnostic labels (transient vs. stubborn) rather than a single binary score. The computational overhead of dual forward-backward passes also limits real-time applicability.
