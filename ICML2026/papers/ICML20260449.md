## Problem

ADMM is widely used for non-convex optimization, but existing convergence analyses either require linear constraints, provide only weaker guarantees (e.g., averaged-iterate convergence), or lack explicit convergence rates. For multi-affine quadratic equality constrained problems—which arise naturally in robotic locomotion, matrix factorization, and neural network training—no prior work established last-iterate convergence with an explicit rate. This paper asks: does ADMM converge to a stationary point on such problems, and under what conditions on the degree of non-convexity does it achieve linear convergence?

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a multi-affine quadratic equality constrained problem with block-structured variables x = (x_1,...,x_n), a coupling variable z, a full-row-rank matrix Q, and a penalty parameter ρ chosen above a threshold depending on L_φ, μ_φ, and the eigenvalues of Q^T Q. At each iteration, the subproblems for each block x_i and for z are strongly convex (due to the multi-affine structure fixing all other blocks), making them efficiently solvable. The output is a last-iterate convergence guarantee: sublinear rate O(k^{-α/(2-α)}) always, and linear rate O(c^{-k}) when the non-convex coefficient norm ‖C‖ is sufficiently small relative to the linear constraint structure, with the limit point characterized as a local minimum.

## Method

The paper proves convergence by showing the augmented Lagrangian is monotonically decreasing (using strong convexity of f per block and of φ in z), establishing boundedness of all iterates via a lower bound on L involving the strong convexity parameters, and then invoking the α-PL property (guaranteed by subanalyticity of the Lagrangian) to derive the sublinear rate. For linear convergence, the key insight is that the Hessian of the Lagrangian at the stationary point is invertible when ‖C‖ is small enough relative to Q, which is quantified by an explicit condition involving (QQ^T)^{-1}Q and the problem's smoothness/strong-convexity constants. The multi-affine structure is essential: it ensures each block subproblem is convex (affine in the updated block), so ADMM's sequential updates are well-defined and tractable. The result is validated on 2D locomotion and dynamic humanoid/quadruped trajectory planning, where the cross-product term c×f in angular momentum dynamics produces the multi-affine constraint, and linear convergence is observed for sufficiently small time discretization Δt.

## Results

The paper establishes that ADMM applied to multi-affine quadratic equality-constrained problems achieves sublinear convergence of the augmented Lagrangian, L(x^k, z^k, w^k) − L(x*, z*, w*) ∈ o(1/k), under mild assumptions (strong convexity, smoothness, full row rank Q). When the non-convexity degree ‖C‖ is sufficiently small relative to the linear constraint coefficients (condition in Eq. 4), the convergence rate improves to linear: O(c₁⁻ᵏ), with computational complexity T_iter·log(1/ε). In the 2D locomotion experiment, the theoretical bound Δt ≤ 0.005 s for linear convergence is conservative—empirically, ADMM converges linearly for significantly larger time steps and regardless of random initialization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its ADMM against three methods designed for linearly constrained problems: PADMM (Yashtini, 2021), IPDS-ADMM (Yuan, 2025), and IADMM (Tang & Toh, 2024). In the nonlinear (multi-affine) constraint scenario, the proposed method achieves superior convergence performance, while the three baselines—which lack theoretical guarantees for nonlinear constraints—degrade in performance. In the convex-objective-with-linear-constraints setting, all methods perform comparably, confirming that the proposed approach does not sacrifice efficiency in the linear regime. In the non-convex-objective-with-linear-constraints setting, the proposed method remains competitive. The key differentiator is that the proposed method is the only one providing last-iterate convergence guarantees with explicit rates for multi-affine (nonlinear) equality constraints, whereas the baselines either require linear constraints or only guarantee weaker forms of convergence (average or best iterate).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ADMM convergence analysis lineage from linearly constrained problems (Lin et al., 2015a) to multi-affine quadratic equality constraints, a class that naturally arises in robotic locomotion and manipulation. It builds directly on Gao et al. (2020), who established convergence of ADMM for multi-affine constraints but without any rate guarantee and under stronger assumptions (f must be independent of certain variables). The paper departs from prior non-convex ADMM analyses (Boț & Nguyen, 2020; Wang et al., 2019; Yuan, 2025) by providing last-iterate convergence with explicit rates rather than weaker forms (average or best iterate), and by handling nonlinear constraints without requiring a proximal solver or full column rank of the constraint Jacobian.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that ADMM achieves linear convergence when the degree of non-convexity (measured by ‖C‖) is small relative to the linear constraint components provides a practical certificate for real-time trajectory planning in robotics, where solutions must be computed within tight time budgets. The sublinear convergence guarantee that always holds (under mild assumptions) closes a gap left by Gao et al. (2020). Limitations include the requirement of strong convexity for both f and φ, full row rank of Q, and subanalyticity; the authors note future work on higher-order nonlinearities and real-world validation. For the broader optimization community, the framework of characterizing when non-convexity is 'small enough' to preserve linear convergence could inform algorithm design for other structured non-convex problems.
