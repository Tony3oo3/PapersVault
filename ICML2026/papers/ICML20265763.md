## Problem

Skeletal motion representation is fragmented across datasets and species due to varying joint counts, hierarchies, and naming conventions, preventing the construction of a unified latent manifold for generative modeling. Existing topology-flexible methods either rely on local graph connectivity without explicit semantic grounding (SAME) or on rigid zero-padding that introduces computational redundancy and constrains joint counts (AnyTop, MoMask), leaving functional correspondences across disparate biological structures ambiguous. A compact, semantics-aware encoder that intrinsically adapts to arbitrary topologies without paired cross-species supervision remains missing.

## Input / Output

The encoder consumes a raw BVH motion sequence as a dynamic graph (sequence of per-frame undirected graphs with variable joint counts), where each node carries 6D rotations, relative positions, velocities, root motion, and contact features, plus static skeleton features (global offset, rest-pose parent-relative coordinates) and semantic joint tags generated by Gemini 2.5 Pro and embedded via a frozen T5 encoder. The decoder takes the topology-agnostic latent code (128-D continuous VAE or 256-D RVQ-VAE with 6 residual quantizers) broadcast to all nodes of an arbitrary target skeleton, conditioned only on that skeleton's rest-pose geometry and semantic tags, and predicts per-joint 6D rotations, root motion, and contact features for forward-kinematics recovery. No canonical joint set, source-target motion pairs, or hand-crafted correspondences are required.

## Method

SATA is a symmetric graph autoencoder whose core novelty is a Semantic-Aware Feature Modulation (SeAM) module that injects MLLM-generated functional joint descriptions and sinusoidal spatial coordinates into motion features via a FiLM-style mechanism: a non-linear network maps the concatenated spatial-semantic condition to scaling (γ) and shifting (β) parameters, applied after layer normalization with a learned gating residual to control information flow. The encoder-decoder backbone uses Spatio-Temporal Interleaved Graph Blocks that alternate between a dual-path spatial module (GINEConv for local structural priors + Spatial Transformer for long-range limb coordination) and a TimeSformer-style temporal branch that reshapes the batched graph into joint-wise temporal streams for long-range dependency modeling, preventing joint jittering and structural drift. A spatial max-pooling layer aggregates node features into a frame-level latent, regularized by KL divergence (VAE) or commitment loss (RVQ-VAE), forming a topology-independent manifold. Training is end-to-end with reconstruction MSE on rotations, positions, and velocities, physical regularization for foot contact and ground penetration, and the chosen latent regularization, using 64-frame sliding windows with 16-frame overlap at inference.

## Results

On AT-HumanML3D (single-dataset training), SATA achieves JR=0.0568 rad, RT=0.96 cm, JP=1.36 cm, outperforming SAME (JR=0.0831, RT=2.16, JP=2.81) by ~30% on all geometric metrics. In the critical zero-shot Human→Animal transfer, SATA maintains JP≈35 cm while SAME catastrophically fails at JP≈398 cm. Under multi-dataset joint training, SATA reduces reconstruction errors by ~30% on AT-HumanML3D and ~12% on AT-AniMo4D relative to SAME. For retargeting, SATA (VAE) achieves 0.21 internal / 0.19 cross error, beating SAME (1.48 / 0.96) by over 7×, and for text-to-motion, SATA (RVQ) reaches FID=0.158 and Top-3=0.639 versus SAME's FID=1.628 and Top-3=0.554.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SATA outperforms SAME (Lee et al., 2023) across nearly all settings: ~30% lower JR/RT/JP on in-domain human reconstruction, a dramatic 11× improvement in zero-shot Human→Animal transfer (JP 35 vs. 398 cm), and ~7× lower retargeting error (0.21 vs. 1.48 internal). On the animal in-domain benchmark, SAME edges out SATA slightly on trajectory metrics (RT 1.44 vs. 1.65 cm) but SATA achieves better pose fidelity (JR 0.39 vs. 0.52 rad) and superior physical plausibility (FS 0.0087 vs. 0.0088, GP 0.0055 vs. 0.0025). MoMask (Guo et al., 2024), adapted via zero-padding, is inapplicable to cross-species transfer and shows 14× worse reconstruction on human data (JR 0.81 vs. 0.06), confirming that rigid padding cannot handle heterogeneous topologies. AniMo (Wang et al., 2025) is restricted to the animal domain and cannot generalize to human skeletons. SAN (Aberman et al., 2020) is limited to human-to-human retargeting and is outperformed by SATA by 75× on internal retargeting error (15.96 vs. 0.21). In text-to-motion generation, SATA (RVQ) surpasses SAME by 10× on FID (0.158 vs. 1.628) and by 15% on Top-3 retrieval (0.639 vs. 0.554), demonstrating that the topology-agnostic latent space is more generative-friendly.

## Relation to Prior Work

SATA extends the graph-based motion autoencoder lineage initiated by SAME (Lee et al., 2023) by introducing explicit semantic grounding via MLLM-generated joint descriptions, addressing SAME's reliance on local graph connectivity alone for cross-topology alignment. The modulation mechanism directly adapts FiLM (Perez et al., 2018) to the skeletal domain, while the spatio-temporal interleaved architecture borrows the alternating spatial/temporal attention pattern from TimeSformer (Bertasius et al., 2021) and combines it with GINEConv message passing. Unlike padding-based any-topology generators (AnyTop, Lee et al. 2025) that sacrifice compact latent representations, and unlike character-specific autoencoders (WalkTheDog) that limit scalability, SATA constructs a single unified, padding-free encoder that decodes to arbitrary target skeletons conditioned only on rest-pose geometry and semantic tags.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a practical pathway toward scalable, cross-species motion representation by demonstrating that MLLM-derived semantic tags can bridge functional correspondences between biologically disparate skeletons without paired supervision. The introduced AT-HumanML3D and AT-AniMo4D benchmarks (80K+ and 30K sequences respectively, with phylogenetically-aware splits) fill a gap in topology-agnostic evaluation. Key limitations remain: zero-shot retargeting can produce biomechanically implausible poses when source dynamics exceed target anatomical ranges, no formal quantitative cross-species benchmark exists, and the approach is scoped to biological articulated systems rather than arbitrary mechanical structures. Future work on physics-aware priors and explicit anatomical constraints would be needed to make retargeted motions fully executable.
