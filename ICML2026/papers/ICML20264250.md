## Problem

Standard RAG systems deploy a single retriever with fixed hyperparameters uniformly across all queries, yet query distributions are highly heterogeneous, ranging from simple factoid lookups to multi-hop reasoning over diverse evidence. While adaptive retrieval methods exist (e.g., Adaptive-RAG, Vendi-RAG), they either operate over a tiny hand-designed strategy menu or perform expensive per-query iterative search. The paper addresses the missing principled formulation: how to select a small, diverse portfolio of retrievers from a large candidate pool that is provably near-optimal for covering the full query distribution.

## Input / Output

The system takes a text query and a chunked document corpus (512-token chunks with 50-token overlap, indexed via FAISS per embedding backbone) and produces a final short answer. At the retrieval stage, each portfolio member returns n=4 document chunks from a prefiltered candidate set of M=1000; the router selects the top-ℓ portfolio members (ℓ=2 or 3) whose retrievals are executed in parallel. The answer LLM generates a structured response per retriever, and a selector LLM aggregates the ℓ candidate answers into a single final answer. Portfolio construction itself consumes a score matrix of Recall@k values over training queries × 360 candidate retrievers.

## Method

The paper formalizes an expected best-of-k objective F(S) = E_q[max_{r∈S} s(q,r)] over the query distribution and proves it is monotone submodular, yielding a (1−1/e) approximation guarantee for the greedy selection algorithm (Algorithm 1) with O(k|R|·(k log|R| + log(1/δ))/ε²) score evaluations. The candidate pool comprises 360 heterogeneous retrievers spanning three families—DiscountedSimilarity (141 per backbone), Vendi-score-based diversity (21 per backbone), and graph-dense entity expansion (36 total)—each instantiated over MPNet and E5 embedding backbones. At inference time, a contrastive router (frozen Flan-T5-Large encoder fused with MPNet and E5 query embeddings via a small MLP) ranks portfolio members by cosine similarity to learned per-retriever prototypes, trained with a multi-positive contrastive argmax loss where positives are the retrievers achieving maximum Recall@k per training query. The top-ℓ routed members execute in parallel, and a selector LLM picks the most trustworthy answer from their structured outputs, amortizing adaptation cost offline while enabling predictable, parallelizable inference.

## Results

On end-to-end QA (Exact Match), the all-pool routed portfolio (k=4, ℓ=3) achieves average EM of 0.464 with Gemma-3-27B-It and 0.474 with Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct across HotpotQA, MusiQue, TriviaQA, and 2WikiMultiHopQA, outperforming the best single-family retrievers (DS: 0.423, Vendi: 0.424, GraphDense: 0.397 for Gemma) and Vendi-RAG adaptive tuning (0.421 for Llama at T=20). In retrieval-only evaluation, the size-5 portfolio reaches 0.594 Support Recall and 0.500 Support F1, versus 0.492/0.432 for the top-k-by-average baseline. In the controlled Vendi-only efficiency comparison, fixed portfolios match or exceed Vendi-RAG accuracy while using substantially fewer generated tokens and lower wall-clock latency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is Vendi-RAG (Rezaei & Dieng, 2025), which adaptively tunes the Vendi diversity parameter s at inference time via iterative retrieval–generation–judging loops. The all-pool portfolio (k=4, ℓ=3) substantially outperforms Vendi-RAG (T=20) on average EM: 0.464 vs. ~0.335 for Gemma-3-27B-It and 0.474 vs. 0.421 for Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct, with the gap most pronounced on multi-hop datasets (MusiQue: 0.195 vs. 0.131 for Gemma; 2WikiMultiHopQA: 0.414 vs. 0.256 for Gemma). In the controlled Vendi-only efficiency comparison (same s search space, MPNet backbone), fixed portfolios achieve comparable or higher EM than Vendi-RAG while using significantly fewer tokens and lower wall-clock time, because portfolio members execute in parallel rather than sequentially. The portfolio also dominates single-retriever baselines (best DS, Vendi, GraphDense by average performance) by 4–7 EM points on average, confirming that cross-family complementarity—not better tuning within one family—drives the gains. On TriviaQA, where queries are largely factoid, the advantage narrows (all methods cluster around 0.68–0.71 EM), consistent with the portfolio's design targeting heterogeneous query distributions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper instantiates the data-driven solution portfolio framework (Drygala et al., 2025; Kleinberg et al., 2004) for RAG, treating retrievers as candidate algorithms and queries as problem instances, and proves a (1−1/e) approximation guarantee via submodular maximization. For the routing component, it adapts the multi-positive contrastive objective from RouterDC (Chen et al., 2024) to route queries to retrievers rather than generator LLMs. It departs from inference-time adaptive methods like Vendi-RAG (Rezaei & Dieng, 2025) and score-level fusion approaches like MoR (Kalra et al., 2025) by shifting the optimization burden entirely offline: a fixed portfolio is selected once, and a lightweight router handles per-query selection at inference time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first provably near-optimal method for selecting complementary retrievers in RAG, with the practical benefit that portfolio members can be executed in parallel, yielding predictable and lower inference cost compared to sequential per-query search. A key open question is scalability: the greedy algorithm's O(k|R|N) cost and the sample complexity O(k log|R|/ε²) may become prohibitive for very large retriever pools or fine-grained hyperparameter grids. Whether the (1−1/e) bound is tight in practice for real retrieval score matrices (as opposed to worst-case coverage functions) remains unexplored, and generalization beyond QA to other RAG tasks (summarization, code generation) is untested.
