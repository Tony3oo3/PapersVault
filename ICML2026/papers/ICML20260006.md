## Problem

Standard representation steering methods rely on a single global difference-in-means vector, implicitly assuming that a target concept is homogeneously represented across the embedding space. In practice, LLM hidden activations exhibit clustered, context-dependent structure, and prior OT-based steering formulations (Singh et al., 2024; Rodriguez et al., 2025) only handle unimodal Gaussian distributions, leaving the multimodal, non-Gaussian geometry of real representations unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The method consumes last-token hidden activations collected from two contrastive prompt sets (e.g., 416 ADVBENCH jailbreak prompts as source and 512 ALPACA prompts as target), clusters each set into K components via k-means, and computes an entropy-regularized OT coupling between the resulting centroids. At inference time, for each token's activation vector x, the method produces a single input-dependent steering vector v̂(x) that is added to (or projected out of) the residual stream at a chosen layer, yielding steered activations that drive the model to produce controlled text or styled images. The steering strength α is a scalar hyperparameter, and the kernel bandwidth σ is set to the median squared distance from x to the cluster centroids.

## Method

CHaRS models source and target activation distributions as Gaussian mixture models and formulates steering as a discrete optimal transport problem between semantic clusters, solved via entropy-regularized OT (Sinkhorn iterations) to obtain a soft coupling matrix P*. From this coupling, the paper derives an explicit input-dependent steering map via barycentric projection: for a given activation x, the steering vector is a kernel-weighted average of per-cluster translation vectors v_ij = b_j − a_i, where the weights combine the OT coupling mass P*_ij with a Gaussian RBF kernel k(x, a_i) centered at source cluster centroids. Under the equal-covariance assumption per matched cluster pair, each component transport map reduces to a pure translation, making the final map a smooth, position-dependent vector field over the representation manifold. A spectral variant, CHaRS-PCT, exploits the fact that the weighted steering covariance has rank at most 2K−2 ≪ d, performing PCA on the cluster-wise vectors and retaining only the top L principal components to obtain a disentangled, low-dimensional steering field that acts as an implicit regularizer in sequential (layer-wise) steering.

## Results

On the jailbreaking task (ADVBENCH), CHaRS with ActAdd achieves up to 98.08% Attack Success Rate (ASR) on Gemma2-9B-Instruct and Llama3.1-8B-Instruct, outperforming the ActAdd baseline (91.35% and 95.19% respectively) by up to 6.7 percentage points. On toxicity mitigation (RealToxicityPrompts), CHaRS-PCT reduces classifier-based toxicity to 1.17% on Llama3-8B and 1.07% on Qwen2.5-7B, compared to 1.93% and 1.80% for Linear-AcT, while preserving perplexity and MMLU scores. For image style control on FLUX.1, CHaRS achieves a 78.94% human preference rate for sketch style induction versus 21.06% for Linear-AcT, and 54.32% for cyberpunk style.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ActAdd (Turner et al., 2024), CHaRS consistently improves ASR across all seven evaluated models, with the largest gains of +6.7 pp on Gemma2-9B (98.08% vs. 91.35%) and +6.7 pp on Qwen2.5-32B (89.42% vs. 82.69%), while tinyBenchmark scores remain within ~1 point of the baseline. Against DirAbl (Arditi et al., 2024), CHaRS-PCT achieves the best ASR on Gemma2-9B (85.58% vs. 79.81%) and Qwen2.5-7B (91.35% vs. 89.42%), and also preserves general utility better than ActAdd-based methods. Against Linear-AcT (Rodriguez et al., 2025) on toxicity mitigation, CHaRS-PCT achieves the lowest toxicity scores on all three models (e.g., 1.17% vs. 1.93% CLS toxicity on Llama3-8B; 4.47% vs. 7.73% zero-shot on Llama3-8B) without degrading perplexity or MMLU. On image style control, CHaRS dominates Linear-AcT in human evaluation, winning 78.94% of sketch-style comparisons and 54.32% of cyberpunk comparisons, and achieves lower LPIPS (0.67 vs. 0.70 for cyberpunk; 0.69 vs. 0.74 for sketch), indicating better perceptual alignment with the target style.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of activation steering from difference-in-means (DiM) vectors to input-adaptive, context-dependent steering maps. It builds directly on the OT interpretation of DiM steering established by Singh et al. (2024) and Rodriguez et al. (2025), which showed that DiM corresponds to the OT map between two identical Gaussians with different means. CHaRS generalizes this by modeling source and target representations as Gaussian mixture models and formulating steering as a discrete OT problem between semantic clusters, drawing on the Mixture Wasserstein distance of Delon & Desolneux (2020) and barycentric projection (Deb et al., 2021) to derive an explicit, smooth steering field. The most direct prior work, Linear-AcT (Rodriguez et al., 2025), performs OT between unimodal Gaussians; CHaRS departs by capturing multimodal, clustered structure in concept representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine limitation of global steering: LLM representations of a single concept are often heterogeneous and context-dependent, making a single translation vector brittle. The GMM-OT formulation provides a principled, theoretically grounded generalization that consistently improves behavioral control across 3B–32B models and across both text and image generation tasks. However, the empirical gains are modest (a few percentage points in ASR), and the method still relies on the equal-covariance assumption and k-means clustering, which the authors acknowledge may not be optimal. Open questions include whether anisotropic mixtures or learned cluster structures yield further gains, and whether the low-rank PCT factorization can be exploited for more efficient or interpretable steering in production settings.
