## Problem

Dense cross-variable interactions in multivariate time series forecasting amplify spurious correlations and cause representation oversmoothing, because real-world dependencies are state-dependent, time-varying, and noisy. The paper argues the core failure mode is not insufficient interaction but excessive interaction: unconstrained communication among all variables propagates redundant and misleading couplings, degrading generalization.

## Input / Output

The model takes a multivariate time series window X ∈ R^{C×L} (C variables, L=96 timesteps) and produces an H-step forecast Ŷ ∈ R^{C×H}, where H ranges over {96, 192, 336, 720} for long-term and {12, 24, 48} for short-term tasks. The input is segmented into overlapping patches (patch length 16 or 24, stride 8) before processing, and the output is generated by a single shared linear projection from the flattened patch-feature representation, followed by reversible instance normalization to undo input standardization.

## Method

MS-FLOW replaces dense cross-variable mixing with a capacity-limited sparse routing bottleneck in three stages. First, Temporal Patch Encoding maps overlapping patches to hidden-dimension tokens via a 1D convolution. Second, Patch-wise Temporal Interaction (PTI) applies a two-layer MLP along the patch axis per feature channel to capture within-variable temporal patterns at a noise-robust segment scale. Third, the Sparse Dependency Bottleneck (SDB) compresses each variable's patch tokens to a single window-level state via average pooling, computes a C×C similarity matrix through learned QK projections, and enforces a Top-K mask so that each variable retains only K outgoing dependency edges; the resulting sparse adjacency is shared across all patch positions and used to perform GELU-activated cross-variable aggregation. This hard-sparsification-after-logits strategy is differentiable through the masked softmax, and the sample-adaptive graph ensures dependencies reflect the current input window rather than a fixed structure. The design forces the model to 'forget' redundant connections and route information only along a few critical paths, suppressing spurious-correlation propagation from a mechanistic standpoint.

## Results

On long-term forecasting (L=96, averaged over H∈{96,192,336,720}), MS-FLOW achieves the best MSE and MAE on 7 out of 8 datasets, with headline values of 0.415/0.415 on ETTh1, 0.306/0.355 on ETTh2, 0.157/0.246 on Electricity, and 0.440/0.258 on Traffic. On short-term forecasting (PEMS series, L=96, H∈{12,24,48}), MS-FLOW attains the best MSE on PEMS03 (0.079), PEMS07 (0.069), and PEMS08 (0.088), and the best MAE on all four PEMS datasets. Overall, MS-FLOW ranks top-1 on 22 out of 24 averaged MSE/MAE metrics across 12 benchmarks, outperforming the strongest baseline TimeFilter by 2.72% (MSE) and 5.69% (MAE) on average.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MS-FLOW consistently outperforms the strongest baseline TimeFilter (ICML'25) across all 8 long-term datasets, with average gains of 2.72% (MSE) and 5.69% (MAE), and also wins on 3 of 4 PEMS short-term datasets. Against the recent patch-based methods TimeMosaic (AAAI'26) and xPatch (AAAI'25), MS-FLOW improves by 7.18%/6.53% and 10.61%/8.32% respectively, indicating that patch partitioning alone is insufficient without structured cross-variable routing. Compared to the dense-attention iTransformer (ICLR'24), MS-FLOW achieves 8.98%/9.44% average improvements, supporting the paper's core claim that constraining cross-variable information flow is more effective than dense interaction. Channel-independent baselines (PatchTST, DLinear) are substantially outperformed, especially on high-dimensional datasets like Electricity and Traffic where cross-variable dependencies matter most. On the Traffic dataset (862 variables), MS-FLOW's MSE of 0.440 is notably lower than TimeFilter's 0.407 in MAE terms (0.258 vs 0.268), though TimeFilter achieves a lower MSE (0.407 vs 0.440), suggesting the sparse bottleneck's advantage is most pronounced in MAE and on smaller-scale datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

MS-FLOW carves a middle path between two established paradigms in multivariate time series forecasting: channel-independent methods (PatchTST, DLinear, TimeMosaic) that discard cross-variable information entirely, and channel-dependent methods (iTransformer, Crossformer, TimesNet) that allow unconstrained dense mixing across all variables. The paper's conceptual departure is reframing the bottleneck as 'too much interaction' rather than 'too little,' drawing on the information-bottleneck principle and sparse-routing ideas from NLP to impose a Top-K capacity constraint on cross-variable information flow. Technically, it repurposes patch-based tokenization (from PatchTST/iTransformer) and attention-style dependency scoring (from iTransformer) but replaces the dense attention map with a hard Top-K mask, yielding a structurally sparse dependency graph that is sample-adaptive yet shared across patch positions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a mechanistic explanation for why dense cross-variable mixing can hurt forecasting (spurious-correlation propagation, representation oversmoothing) and validates this with a noise-injection robustness experiment showing MS-FLOW's sparse graph actively excludes noisy channels. The learned sparse dependency graph also offers interpretability that dense attention maps lack, which is valuable in safety-critical domains like power grid or traffic management. Open questions include whether the fixed Top-K budget can be made fully adaptive per sample or per time step, how the approach extends to irregularly sampled or streaming time series, and whether the sparsity principle generalizes beyond forecasting to other multivariate sequence tasks such as anomaly detection or imputation.
