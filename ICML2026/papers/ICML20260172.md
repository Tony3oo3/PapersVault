## Problem

Bayesian optimization with many observations (BOMO) is impeded by Gaussian-process surrogates: hyperparameter fitting scales as O(N³) (or ~O(N²) with modern solvers) and is repeated at every BO iteration, making it the dominant cost when N reaches thousands or tens of thousands. The paper asks whether a state-of-the-art BO algorithm can be made significantly faster on BOMO problems while preserving solution quality.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a black-box objective f: [0,1]^D → R and a growing observation set {(x_i, y_i, s_i)} where s_i is an optional per-observation aleatoric noise scale. At each round it outputs a single proposed design x_arm (or a batch via parallel arms), along with the surrogate's mean μ(x) and total uncertainty σ(x) = √(σ_a² + σ_e²) at any query point. For noise-free problems the method is fitting-free: it sets s_0=0, c_e=1 and selects arms via non-dominated sorting over (μ, σ) without any hyperparameter optimization, reducing per-round cost to a single KNN lookup plus a Pareto sort over RAASP candidates.

## Method

Epistemic Nearest Neighbors (ENN) replaces the GP surrogate by treating each of the K nearest observations as an independent estimate of f(x), combining them via a precision-weighted average where the per-observation variance decomposes into an aleatoric term (s_0² + s_m²) and an epistemic term (c_e · d²(x, x_m)). The epistemic variance of the combined mean is the inverse of the total precision, yielding O(N) querying via a max-heap KNN search (implemented with Faiss) and O(PN log K) hyperparameter fitting via a fixed-size P subsample of the LOOCV pseudolikelihood. TuRBO-ENN embeds ENN inside TuRBO's trust-region loop: for noisy objectives it fits (s_0, c_e) each round and selects arms by UCB = μ + σ over RAASP candidates; for noise-free objectives it skips fitting entirely and selects arms by random sampling from the first Pareto front of a non-dominated sort over (μ, σ). The paper proves ENN satisfies the Pseudo-Bayesian Optimization axioms (local consistency, sequential no-empty-ball, and an improvement property), inheriting convergence guarantees, and derives a bias-variance bound for the noisy weighted-kNN estimator showing L² consistency under standard random-design conditions.

## Results

Across five simulation benchmarks (LunarLander-v3, Hopper-v5, BipedalWalker-v3, Push-v5, LASSO-DNA) with up to 50,000 observations, TuRBO-ENN reduces total proposal time by 9×–151× compared to TuRBO (turbo-one) while matching its optimization quality. For example, on Hopper-v5 with frozen noise (N=50,000), TuRBO-ENN completes in 41.2 s versus 5,532.4 s for TuRBO (134× speedup), and on Push-v5 frozen noise (N=15,000) in 5.8 s versus 873.8 s (151× speedup). The method achieves O(N) proposal-time scaling versus O(N²) for GP-based methods, and the noise-free variant (ENN + non-dominated sorting, no hyperparameter fitting) yields the largest speedups.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TuRBO-ENN matches TuRBO (turbo-one) in optimization quality (best observed objective) across all five simulators while reducing proposal time by 9×–151×, with the largest gains at higher N and in the noise-free (frozen) setting where hyperparameter fitting is omitted. Against GP-UCB (Botorch), SMAC, and DNGO, TuRBO-ENN is dramatically faster: many of these baselines exceed the 5-hour timeout at N=10,000–50,000, whereas TuRBO-ENN finishes in under 10 minutes. Optuna (Parzen estimator) is competitive in scaling (also O(N)) but is 10–30× slower in absolute proposal time than TuRBO-ENN on most tasks. CMA-ES, which is O(1) in N and uses no surrogate, is the fastest method in absolute time but does not leverage observations to guide proposals; TuRBO-ENN matches or exceeds its optimization quality on most benchmarks. In the pure fitting benchmark (Figure 8, Ackley D=10), ENN fits in ~0.3 s at N=10⁶ on a single CPU, while the exact GP exceeds the 5-hour limit, and ENN's held-out log-likelihood ranks near the median across all surrogates tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on TuRBO (Eriksson et al., 2019b), replacing its Gaussian-process surrogate and Thompson-sampling acquisition with a K-nearest-neighbor surrogate (ENN) and UCB or non-dominated sorting. It is conceptually adjacent to Vecchia GP methods (Jimenez & Katzfuss, 2023) and other nearest-neighbor GP approximations, but departs from them by abandoning the GP framework entirely in favor of a simpler precision-weighted KNN estimator that requires no pairwise covariance computation. The trust-region and RAASP candidate-generation machinery is retained from TuRBO, and the convergence guarantee is inherited from the Pseudo-Bayesian Optimization framework (Chen & Lam, 2025), which the authors verify applies to ENN.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The O(N) scaling (versus O(N²) for GP-based methods) makes BO practical for simulation-optimization workloads with tens of thousands of evaluations, where proposal time previously dominated wall-clock time. The noise-free variant that eliminates hyperparameter fitting entirely via non-dominated sorting is a clean simplification that further reduces overhead. Open questions include whether approximate nearest-neighbor data structures (e.g., HNSW) can push scaling to O(log N), whether ENN can support joint sampling for Thompson sampling, and whether dimension-weighted distance metrics (analogous to ARD in GPs) would improve performance in high dimensions. The paper provides convergence guarantees via PBO but no regret bounds, leaving a gap in the theoretical characterization.
