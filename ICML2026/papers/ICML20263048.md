## Problem

Existing guided skill discovery (GSD) methods either rely on pre-defined rules or expert demonstrations that are hard to obtain in complex environments, or require training auxiliary guidance models that overfit when human feedback is sparse and collected online. This paper asks whether one can construct a reliable, training-free guidance signal from minimal sparse human labels without any additional model training beyond the skill learner itself.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263048/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes state vectors or 64×64 RGB pixel observations from robotic locomotion environments, plus sparse 3-class human labels (good/bad/neutral) assigned to fixed-length state segments (H=20) collected online via an active query strategy—only 40 labels for state-based tasks and 100 for pixel-based tasks. It outputs a set of diverse skill-conditioned policies π(a|s,z) and a dense, training-free guidance signal w(s) that modulates the DSD intrinsic reward, effectively reweighting exploration toward human-desirable regions of the state space.

## Method

COLLIE builds on the METRA/DSD framework but enforces a semantically coherent latent space by constraining embeddings of temporally adjacent states to remain close (‖φ(s′)−φ(s)‖₂ ≤ δ₀), which ensures nearby embeddings share similar human desirability. The guidance signal w(s) is then constructed training-free as a softmax over negative L2-distances from s to each labeled-state set in the latent space, propagating sparse labels into a dense signal without any auxiliary network. An active query strategy selects under-explored segments for human labeling using particle-based state entropy estimation, and a smoothing schedule gradually transitions from pure unsupervised exploration to guided exploration. A variable substitution φ′(s)=φ(s)/w(s) decouples the guidance signal from latent space learning, yielding a stable multiplicative intrinsic reward r(s,z,s′)=w(s)(φ(s′)−φ(s))ᵀz. A theoretical result (Proposition 3.1) bounds the asymptotic classification error of the induced guidance signal by 2P*(s)−(3/2)[P*(s)]², where P*(s) is the Bayes error rate.

## Results

On safe state coverage (the primary metric), COLLIE achieves near-oracle or even exceeds oracle performance on 5 out of 12 tasks, e.g., HalfCheetah Right (102.20 vs. oracle 97.80), Quadruped North (128.40 vs. 112.60), Humanoid Hole (80.60 vs. 75.20), HalfCheetah Not-Flip (215.60 vs. 209.60), and Safety-Gym Hazard (−16.00 vs. −20.80). On zero-shot downstream Ant tasks, COLLIE attains the best average performance across all three task variants (Hole: 650.67, North: 1054.25, Range: −913.48) and the best single-skill performance on Hole (1251.92) and Range (673.55). On the HalfCheetah hierarchical downstream task, COLLIE reaches 45.26±15.78, approaching the oracle's 47.46±33.27 while substantially outperforming all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COLLIE decisively outperforms all three unsupervised baselines (DIAYN, LSD, METRA) on every task, as these methods explore indiscriminately and frequently enter hazardous regions, yielding negative or near-zero safe state coverage (e.g., METRA scores −1425.80 on Ant North vs. COLLIE's 1333.20). Among the two guided baselines, DoDont* (Kim et al., 2024) is the strongest competitor, achieving competitive results on Ant North (1307.20) and Ant Hole (1132.20), but it fails catastrophically on Ant Range (−427.60) and Safety-Gym Hazard (−37.60), likely due to instability of its trained instruction network under sparse online feedback. DDG* (Klemsdal et al., 2021) underperforms DoDont* on most tasks, with particularly poor results on Ant Range (−539.20) and Quadruped North (−59.60). COLLIE surpasses DoDont* on 10 out of 12 tasks and exceeds the oracle upper bound on 5 tasks, demonstrating that its training-free guidance signal from sparse labels is not only reliable but can outperform manually designed guidance. On downstream tasks, COLLIE achieves the best zero-shot average on all Ant variants and the best hierarchical control score on HalfCheetah (45.26 vs. DoDont*'s 30.44 and METRA's 21.58), confirming that the learned skills are both safe and task-relevant.

## Relation to Prior Work

COLLIE builds directly on the METRA/DSD framework (Park et al., 2024), which formalized distance-maximizing skill discovery via the Wasserstein dependency measure, and on DoDont (Kim et al., 2024), from which it inherits the variable-substitution trick that decouples the guidance signal from latent space learning. The key departure is conceptual: rather than training an auxiliary classifier or reward model to encode human intent (as DoDont, DDG, and preference-based GSD methods do), COLLIE exploits a semantic coherence property of the latent space—constraining temporally adjacent states to remain close—to propagate sparse human labels via nearest-neighbor distance, yielding a training-free guidance signal with a theoretical error bound. An active query strategy based on state-space entropy further distinguishes COLLIE by selecting under-explored regions for labeling rather than relying on pre-collected expert trajectories.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for expert demonstrations, pre-defined constraint formulas, or auxiliary model training, COLLIE substantially lowers the practical barrier to guided skill discovery in complex, safety-critical environments where expert knowledge is unavailable or expensive to obtain. The theoretical guarantee (error bounded by 2× Bayes error) and the demonstrated robustness to noisy labels and as few as 10 feedback segments make the approach credible for real-world deployment. However, the inherited dead-zone limitation from METRA—where skills fail to explore regions occluded by obstacles—remains an open problem, and the semantic coherence assumption may not hold in all state spaces. Future work combining DSD with pure exploration mechanisms (prediction errors, state entropy bonuses) could address this gap, and extending the framework to continuous or multi-modal human feedback beyond discrete good/bad/neutral labels is a natural next step.
