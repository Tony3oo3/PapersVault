## Problem

Standard semi-supervised learning methods for molecules rely on label-preserving data augmentations or confidence-based pseudo-labeling, both of which are ill-suited to the molecular domain where minor structural changes can drastically alter predicted properties. This paper addresses the gap by proposing an SSL approach that requires no explicit augmentations and avoids the confirmation-bias risk of pseudo-labeling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264460/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes molecular graphs (2D) or 3D atomic coordinates as input, split into a small labeled set (10% of training data) and a large unlabeled set (the remaining 90%). All M ensemble members are trained on identical labeled mini-batches, while unlabeled mini-batches are used exclusively for the consensus loss. Outputs are continuous molecular property values (e.g., HOMO/LUMO energies, internal energy) for regression tasks or class probabilities for classification tasks (e.g., ogbg-molhiv, ogbg-molpcba).

## Method

The method trains a deep ensemble of M models where each member minimizes a composite loss: a standard supervised loss (MSE or CE) on labeled data plus a coupling-weighted consistency loss that penalizes the discrepancy between the member's prediction and the arithmetic mean of all ensemble members' predictions on unlabeled data. The gradient through the ensemble mean is detached, preventing the ensemble from being pulled toward less-accurate individual predictions. The theoretical justification comes from the ensemble loss ambiguity decomposition (Ensemble Loss = Average Individual Loss − Ambiguity), which guarantees the ensemble mean is at least as accurate as the average individual model, making it a valid supervisory signal without warm-up. For non-linear models, the analysis reveals an additional regularization effect: consensus training biases members toward flatter minima in the loss landscape (verified via Hessian trace measurements), which explains why a single consensus-trained member can outperform an entire traditionally-trained ensemble at inference time.

## Results

On QM9 (12 regression targets, PaiNN, M=4), ensemble consensus SSL reduces MAE across all targets for both individual models and the full ensemble. The headline finding is that a single individual model from the coupled ensemble outperforms the full supervised ensemble on every target (e.g., U0: 19.96 vs 20.91 meV; ε_HOMO: 73.98 vs 76.47 meV). On the GNN+ benchmark suite (ZINC, Peptides, ogbg-molhiv, ogbg-molpcba) with GCN/GIN/GatedGCN, the SSL method consistently improves over supervised-only baselines, with a single SSL-trained model often matching or exceeding the full supervised ensemble (e.g., ZINC GCN: individual SSL 0.2406 vs ensemble supervised 0.2934 MAE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against mean-teacher (Tarvainen & Valpola, 2017), the proposed ensemble consensus method wins decisively on all QM9 targets (e.g., U₀: 19.96 vs 24.73 meV individual; ε_HOMO: 73.98 vs 80.61 meV) and on all GNN+ benchmarks (e.g., ZINC GCN: 0.2406 vs 0.2884 MAE; ogbg-molpcba GatedGCN: 0.1701 vs 0.1669 AP). Against PSEUDσ (Huang et al., 2022), the method outperforms on 11 of 12 QM9 targets (e.g., U₀: 19.96 vs 24.00 meV; Δε: 117.04 vs 122.5 meV), with the sole exception being ⟨R²⟩ where PSEUDσ achieves 0.9099 vs 0.6100 MAE. Against Frad (Feng et al., 2023) on PCQM4Mv2, consensus training provides a modest additional gain on top of 3D denoising pre-training (ε_HOMO: 15.49 vs 16.90 MAE with pre-train+denoise), confirming that ensemble consensus is complementary to modality-specific objectives. The key structural advantage over all baselines is that a single SSL-trained model matches or exceeds the full supervised ensemble, eliminating the M× inference cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the consistency-training family of SSL methods (Pi-models, mean-teacher) by replacing data augmentations or a single EMA teacher with a full deep ensemble's consensus prediction as the consistency target. The theoretical justification comes from the ensemble loss ambiguity decomposition (Wood et al., 2023), which guarantees the ensemble consensus is a better-than-average supervisory signal. The key departure from prior ensemble-based SSL (cross pseudo supervision, agreement-based learning) is that the method requires no warm-up, no hard-labeling of pseudo-predictions, and yields individual models with ensemble-level performance through a knowledge-distillation-like effect.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is significant for molecular screening: a single model from the SSL ensemble outperforms a full supervised ensemble at a fraction of the inference cost, making it attractive for high-throughput property prediction where inference dominates. However, the O(2M) training overhead limits applicability to smaller models, and the coupling weight γ is highly dataset-dependent, requiring per-task tuning. Open questions include whether the method benefits from truly external unlabeled chemical libraries (vs. the artificial 10/90 split used here), whether more sophisticated coupling schedules can reduce training cost, and whether the regularisation effect (flatter minima) can be harnessed independently of the ensemble.
