## Problem

Popular deep learning optimizers Lion and Muon lack a unified theoretical framework connecting them to classical constrained optimization, and Stochastic Frank-Wolfe (SFW) methods have not been analyzed under the empirically observed heavy-tailed noise regime (bounded p-th moment for p < 2). This paper addresses both gaps by showing Lion and Muon are exact instances of SFW under specific norm constraints, and by developing the first SFW variants with high-probability convergence guarantees under heavy-tailed noise for non-convex optimization.

## Input / Output

The algorithms consume an unbiased stochastic gradient oracle satisfying a bounded p-th moment noise condition (p ∈ (1,2]), a convex compact constraint set C of diameter D, and momentum/step-size hyperparameters. The primary output is a convergence bound on the Frank-Wolfe gap G(x), which for norm-ball constraints (ℓ∞ for Lion, spectral norm for Muon) is exactly equivalent to convergence to a KKT point of the constrained problem. Practically, the framework yields LION+ and MUON+ variants that replace the raw stochastic gradient with a clipped version, validated on nanoGPT training over the Shakespeare dataset.

## Method

The key unification shows that Lion's sign-based update is the linear minimization oracle over an ℓ∞-norm ball, and Muon's orthogonalization step is the oracle over a spectral-norm ball, both embedded in a single Stochastic FW template (Algorithm 3) with momentum. For the heavy-tailed regime, Algorithm 5 integrates gradient clipping into this template, achieving a high-probability FW-gap rate of Õ(T^((1-p)/(3p-2))) with batch size 1, while Algorithm 6 further incorporates STORM variance reduction to improve the rate to Õ(T^((1-p)/(2p-1))) under an additional Lipschitz-gradient assumption. In the bounded-variance (p=2) regime, Algorithm 4 combines STORM with the SFW template to reach O(1/ε³) SFO complexity using only a single large batch at initialization and unit batch size thereafter, matching the best-known rates while avoiding the large average batch sizes required by prior SFW variance-reduction methods.

## Results

On nanoGPT training on the Shakespeare dataset (averaged over 5 seeds), the proposed clipped variants consistently achieve lower validation loss than their unclipped counterparts. Specifically, LION+ reaches a validation loss below 1.47 in 2950 steps, a 19.18% reduction compared to Lion (3650 steps). Similarly, MUON+ reaches the same target in 4000 steps, a 5.88% reduction compared to Muon (4250 steps). The theoretical contributions establish that the base Stochastic FW (Algorithm 3) achieves an expected Frank-Wolfe gap of O(T^{-1/2}) under bounded variance, and the heavy-tailed variants (Algorithms 5 and 6) achieve high-probability rates of Õ(T^{(1-p)/(3p-2)}) and Õ(T^{(1-p)/(2p-1)}) respectively for p ∈ (1,2].

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares two proposed clipped variants against their unclipped baselines on nanoGPT training on Shakespeare. LION+ (Lion with gradient clipping) outperforms Lion by reaching the target validation loss in 2950 steps versus 3650 steps, a 19.18% reduction in training steps. MUON+ (Muon with gradient clipping) outperforms Muon by reaching the target in 4000 steps versus 4250 steps, a 5.88% reduction. In both cases, the clipped variants consistently achieve lower validation loss throughout training, validating the theoretical motivation that gradient clipping improves robustness under heavy-tailed gradient noise. The improvement is more pronounced for Lion (19.18%) than for Muon (5.88%), suggesting that the sign-based update in Lion is more sensitive to heavy-tailed noise than the spectral-norm-orthogonalized update in Muon.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two recently popular deep learning optimizers—Lion (Chen et al., 2024b) and Muon (Jordan et al., 2024)—as exact instances of a single Stochastic Frank-Wolfe algorithm under different norm constraints (ℓ∞ and spectral norm, respectively), building on prior observations by Chen et al. (2024a), Bernstein & Newhouse (2024), and Pethick et al. (2025) that linked individual optimizers to norm-constrained linear minimization oracles. It then extends the Stochastic FW framework to the heavy-tailed noise regime (bounded p-th moment, p ∈ (1,2]) by integrating gradient clipping and STORM-style variance reduction (Cutkosky & Orabona, 2019), filling a gap where prior FW analyses assumed bounded variance. The O(1/ε³) SFO complexity with amortized batch size 1 matches the best-known rates of Yurtsever et al. (2019) and Hassani et al. (2020) while avoiding their large-batch requirements.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first rigorous non-convex convergence guarantees for Lion and Muon in terms of the Frank-Wolfe gap (equivalently, KKT-point convergence under norm constraints), and the first Stochastic FW results under heavy-tailed noise—both of which were previously missing from the literature. The derived clipped variants (LION+, MUON+) show modest but consistent empirical gains (~19% and ~6% fewer steps to target validation loss on nanoGPT/Shakespeare), though the experimental scope is limited to a single small-scale task. Open questions include whether the heavy-tailed bounds can adapt to the noise level σ, whether the analysis extends to Banach-space norms, and whether varying the constraint set can yield new optimizer variants with improved dynamics.
