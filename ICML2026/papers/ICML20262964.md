## Problem

Existing spectral graph anomaly detection methods characterize anomalies exclusively through high-variance 'right-shift' patterns in spectral energy, overlooking camouflaged anomalies that mimic normal nodes and instead exhibit low-variance 'left-shift' behavior. This blind spot persists across multiple public benchmarks (e.g., 26 of 32 features on YelpChi show lower energy for anomalies), and the reliance on global graph-level spectral computation further limits scalability to large graphs.

## Input / Output

The method consumes d-dimensional node feature vectors and the graph adjacency matrix, with partial node labels for semi-supervised training. For temporal graphs, it operates on a sliding window of w consecutive graph snapshots sharing a fixed node set, computing localized spectral energy at each time step. The output is a per-node anomaly probability in (0,1) produced by a linear classifier on GraphSAGE embeddings, trained with a weighted cross-entropy loss where the anomaly ratio α rebalances the class imbalance.

## Method

EGNN computes a node-level local spectral energy via the Rayleigh quotient over each node's 1-hop neighborhood (Eq. 3), avoiding costly global eigendecomposition. It constructs two complementary energy representations: the standard right-shift energy E^(R) capturing high-frequency anomalies, and a left-shift energy E^(L) = 2 − E^(R) obtained by flipping the normalized Laplacian spectrum (2I − L) to capture low-frequency camouflaged anomalies. A lightweight MLP gating network maps raw node features to a feature-wise binary weight matrix that adaptively fuses the two energy branches, followed by batch normalization and a GraphSAGE backbone for spatial message passing. For time-series graphs, the same architecture is applied across all time steps within a sliding window without introducing recurrent or attention-based temporal modules, making the model robust to window length and substantially more parameter-efficient than Transformer-based baselines.

## Results

On the large-scale T-Social dataset (5.7M nodes, 73M edges), EGNN achieves 95.40 F1-macro, 99.69 AUC, and 95.89 AUPRC, outperforming the next-best baseline BWGNN by over 13 points in F1-macro and 35 points in AUPRC. On YelpChi, EGNN reaches 76.89 F1-macro and 66.26 AUPRC, surpassing UniGAD (71.23 F1-m) and BWGNN (71.78 F1-m). On time-series benchmarks, EGNN attains the highest average F1-macro of 72.65 across MSL, SWaT, and WADI with only 53,953 parameters, compared to 261K (TranAD) and 1.28M (USAD).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EGNN outperforms all 14 baselines across all 7 datasets. On the most challenging T-Social dataset, EGNN's 95.40 F1-macro exceeds BWGNN (81.78) by 13.6 points and UniGAD (78.67) by 16.7 points, while its 95.89 AUPRC dwarfs BWGNN's 60.81 and UniGAD's 58.33. On YelpChi, EGNN leads BWGNN by 5.1 F1-macro points (76.89 vs. 71.78) and UniGAD by 5.7 points, with a particularly large AUPRC gap (66.26 vs. 54.67 and 53.97). On Amazon, gains are more marginal (91.52 vs. BWGNN's 91.40 F1-macro), which the authors attribute to the dataset's simplicity. On time-series benchmarks, EGNN achieves 86.03 F1-macro on SWaT (vs. TranAD 76.45, GDN 75.45) and 61.41 on WADI (vs. TranAD 48.50), while using 5–24× fewer parameters than TranAD and USAD. Classical GNN baselines (GCN, GAT, GraphSAGE, SGC) are consistently outperformed by 10–30+ F1-macro points on most datasets, confirming that spectral-energy-aware feature transformation is critical rather than generic message passing alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the spectral-energy formulation for graph anomaly detection introduced by Tang et al. (BWGNN, ICML 2022), which characterized anomalies via a 'right-shift' in spectral energy (increased high-frequency variance). The key departure is the identification of 'camouflaged anomalies' that produce a 'left-shift' (decreased variance, energy concentration at low frequencies), a pattern the authors show is prevalent in real-world datasets but invisible to existing right-shift-only formulations. Methodologically, the paper moves from costly graph-level eigendecomposition to a node-local Rayleigh quotient surrogate compatible with message passing, and extends the unified architecture to temporal graphs without introducing RNN or Transformer modules, contrasting with the dominant decouple-spatial-then-stack-temporal paradigm (e.g., EvolveGCN, TranAD).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The empirical demonstration that a majority of features on YelpChi (26/32) and Amazon (12/25) exhibit lower spectral energy for anomalous nodes challenges the field's implicit assumption that anomalies are always high-variance, and provides a principled spectral explanation for why camouflaged fraudsters evade detection. The unified static-to-temporal architecture with orders-of-magnitude fewer parameters than Transformer-based baselines offers a practical, data-efficient alternative for label-scarce deployment. Open questions include extending the formulation to directed and weighted graphs (where Laplacian eigenvalues are not guaranteed real), handling edge- and subgraph-level anomalies, and whether the semi-supervised requirement can be relaxed to fully unsupervised settings without performance degradation.
