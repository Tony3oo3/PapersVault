## Problem

Existing structure-based binder design methods (RFdiffusion, BindCraft, BoltzGen) optimize binding to static conformations and cannot represent or control non-reversible, directional effects such as agonist versus antagonist behavior. Because static structures do not encode transition asymmetry, these approaches lack a mechanism to systematically distinguish or design binders that bias protein state transitions in a specified direction, leaving directional allosteric control outside their representational scope.

## Input / Output

The method takes a target protein sequence and a desired direction label d* ∈ {+1, −1} (agonist or antagonist) as input, and produces peptide binder sequences (length 16–128 residues) whose predicted functional effect matches the requested direction. Notably, TD3B operates entirely on sequence representations rather than 3D structures: no docking, structural filtering, or energy minimization is applied to generated outputs. The target protein and direction condition only the reward function during fine-tuning, keeping the generative backbone architecturally target-agnostic at inference time.

## Method

TD3B formulates directional allosteric control as a sequence-conditioned transition operator over protein macrostates, using only the binary sign of transition asymmetry Δ(y) as supervision rather than regressing continuous kinetic rates. Three co-designed components enable direction-controlled generation: (i) a target-aware Direction Oracle (gated fusion of ESM2 protein embeddings and SPE-tokenized peptide embeddings through an MLP) that classifies agonist vs. antagonist effect; (ii) a gated reward R(y; d*, x) = g_ψ(y,x) · σ(d*·f_φ(y,x)/τ) where a pre-trained affinity predictor acts as a soft binding gate and the oracle supplies the directional signal, decoupling direction from raw affinity; and (iii) amortized fine-tuning of a pre-trained MDLM (PepTune) via importance-weighted denoising cross-entropy (WDCE) with trajectory-level importance weights from tree search, a margin-based contrastive loss enforcing agonist/antagonist separation in latent space, and a KL regularization term preserving the pre-trained prior. At inference, weighted resampling (Algorithm 2) further biases the output distribution toward the desired direction.

## Results

TD3B achieves the highest gated reward (5.33 ± 0.03) among all methods, substantially outperforming the next-best baseline TR2-D2 (3.36 ± 0.24) and SMC (3.18 ± 0.31). It reaches 100% direction accuracy for antagonist generation (DA(d*=-1) = 1.000) and 79.5% for agonist generation (DA(d*=+1) = 0.795), while also achieving the highest predicted binding affinity in both directions (6.00 and 6.32). The pre-trained model exhibits a strong agonist bias (DA(+1)=0.822 vs. DA(-1)=0.174), which TD3B effectively breaks through directional fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TD3B outperforms all six baselines on the gated reward metric (5.33 vs. 3.36 for TR2-D2, 3.18 for SMC, 2.61 for PepTune, 2.42 for TDS, 2.38 for CG, and 2.06 for the pre-trained model). Against inference-only guidance methods (CG, SMC, TDS), TD3B achieves substantially higher direction accuracy for antagonists (1.000 vs. 0.470/0.235/0.176) and higher affinity, confirming that amortized fine-tuning is necessary rather than inference-time steering alone. Compared to the closest fine-tuning baseline TR2-D2, TD3B improves gated reward by 59% (5.33 vs. 3.36) and achieves 100% antagonist direction accuracy versus 87.5%, while also recovering agonist accuracy (79.5% vs. 17.8%) that TR2-D2 essentially loses. PepTune achieves moderate affinity gains but with high variance and no directional control (DA values near chance). RFDiffusion, used as a structure-based external baseline, produces lower predicted binding affinity than TD3B (Figure 3A), and its static-structure paradigm cannot represent directional transition effects at all. The ablation in Table 6 further shows that TD3B's training-time innovations alone (gated reward + contrastive loss + KL) yield a 26.5% gated reward improvement over TR2-D2, with weighted resampling providing an additional 25.4% gain on top.

## Relation to Prior Work

TD3B builds directly on the masked discrete diffusion language model (MDLM) backbone from PepTune and the tree-search amortized fine-tuning framework of TR2-D2, repurposing their importance-weighted denoising machinery for a new objective. Its key conceptual departure is from structure-centric binder design methods (RFDiffusion, BindCraft, BoltzGen, RareFoldGPCR), which optimize binding to static conformations and cannot represent non-reversible, directional effects. Rather than introducing a new generative architecture, TD3B layers a direction-specific guidance objective—comprising a target-aware Direction Oracle, a soft affinity gate, and a contrastive separation loss—on top of the existing diffusion backbone, reframing allosteric binder design as a non-equilibrium transport problem over macrostate transitions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap in computational drug design: the inability of current structure-based methods to systematically generate agonists versus antagonists for the same target, a distinction that is clinically critical for GPCRs. The decoupling of binding affinity (as a gate) from directional effect (as the optimization target) is a clean and practical design principle that could generalize to other non-equilibrium biomolecular control tasks. However, the empirical evidence remains limited: the training set is small (130 bidirectional pairs), case-study structural analyses rely on static AlphaFold3 predictions rather than dynamics, and no wet-lab functional validation (cAMP, β-arrestin assays) has been performed. Open questions include whether the binary sign supervision can be enriched with continuous kinetic rates from Markov state models, how the framework scales to multi-state biased agonism, and whether the observed directional asymmetry (100% antagonist vs. ~80% agonist accuracy) reflects a fundamental limitation or simply data imbalance.
