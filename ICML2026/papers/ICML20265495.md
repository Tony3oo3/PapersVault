## Problem

Developing multimodal deep research agents that can iteratively invoke diverse search tools during reasoning faces three fundamental obstacles: publicly available search-intensive multimodal QA data with multi-turn, multi-tool requirements is extremely scarce; existing trajectory synthesis methods are designed for single-turn search and fail in multi-round agentic settings; and training with online search APIs (e.g., SerpAPI, Jina) incurs thousands of dollars per run, making systematic RL exploration infeasible. Prior workflow-based approaches (RAG, prompt-based pipelines) further decouple retrieval from reasoning, preventing the model from adaptively adjusting search strategies as its reasoning state evolves.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a query image paired with a natural-language question and produces a structured multi-turn trajectory consisting of alternating thinking steps, tool invocations (image_search_by_lens, image_search_by_text_query, text_search), and tool responses, terminated by a final answer enclosed in <answer> tags. Up to 5 tool invocations are permitted per trajectory, and the training context is extended to 70,000 tokens to accommodate long multimodal tool responses. Tool responses are masked from the SFT loss and treated as observations conditioning subsequent predictions, while the final answer is supervised only at the last step.

## Method

MM-DeepResearch combines three complementary components. First, Hyper-Search constructs a depth-D hypergraph over web images and webpage content, where hyperedges group all nodes expanded from a common source via reverse image search, visual similarity search, URL extraction, and image-link extraction; QA pairs are then generated at intra-hyperedge and inter-hyperedge levels to guarantee multi-turn, multi-tool search necessity. Second, DR-TTS (Decompose-Recompose Tool Tree Search) first trains specialized per-tool experts via RL on tool-categorized subsets, then recomposes all experts to jointly explore a search tree where each active node expands to at most M children (one per expert), terminating when an LLM-verified correct final answer is found; successful paths become SFT trajectories. Third, an offline search engine pre-collects a multimodal corpus (GPT-generated queries fetched via SerpAPI/Jina plus Wikipedia, indexed with e5-base-v2 for text and jina-embeddings-v4 for images via FlashRAG) to replace costly online APIs during GRPO-based multi-turn RL. Training proceeds in two stages: multi-turn SFT on 10K DR-TTS trajectories as a cold start, followed by GRPO with format and LLM-judged accuracy rewards on 6K search-intensive QA samples.

## Results

MM-DeepResearch-8B achieves an average score of 63.0 across six multimodal deep research benchmarks (SimpleVQA, MMSearch, LiveVQA, FVQA-test, InfoSeek, BrowseComp-VL), outperforming the strongest prior agentic baseline SenseNova-MARS-8B (59.6) by 3.4 points. Scaling to 32B, MM-DeepResearch-32B reaches 65.3 average, surpassing the Qwen3-VL-32B baseline (50.4) by 14.9%. The 7B variant (trained from Qwen2.5-VL-7B without native tool-use) achieves 56.1 average, exceeding MMSearch-R1-7B (49.0) by 7.1 points and Visual-ARFT-7B (33.1) by 23 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MM-DeepResearch-8B (63.0 avg) outperforms the closest agentic competitor SenseNova-MARS-8B (59.6 avg) by 3.4 points, with gains of 4.2 on SimpleVQA, 0.4 on MMSearch, 8.8 on LiveVQA, 2.1 on FVQA-test, 2.2 on InfoSeek, and 2.8 on BrowseComp-VL. Against the Qwen3-VL-8B agentic baseline (46.2 avg), MM-DeepResearch-8B improves by 17% on average. At the 7B scale (trained from Qwen2.5-VL-7B without native tool-use), MM-DeepResearch-7B (56.1 avg) surpasses MMSearch-R1-7B (49.0) by 7.1 points and Visual-ARFT-7B (33.1) by 23 points, and beats WebWatcher-7B by 7.7% on SimpleVQA and 12.3% on MMSearch. At 32B, MM-DeepResearch-32B (65.3 avg) exceeds Qwen3-VL-32B agentic (50.4) by 14.9% and WebWatcher-32B on all reported benchmarks. Notably, MM-DeepResearch-8B also outperforms GPT-5 under the RAG workflow paradigm (58.8 avg) by 4.2 points, demonstrating that agentic multi-turn search with a smaller open model can surpass larger proprietary models using static retrieval pipelines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the agentic search paradigm for MLLMs (established by MMSearch-R1, WebWatcher, and SenseNova-MARS) by addressing three practical bottlenecks: search-intensive data scarcity, trajectory quality, and RL training cost. It builds on GRPO (from DeepSeek-R1) as the RL backbone and on Qwen3-VL/Qwen2.5-VL as base models, but departs from prior agentic search agents by introducing a hypergraph-based data synthesis method (Hyper-Search) and a decompose-recompose tree search strategy (DR-TTS) for trajectory generation. The offline search engine is a practical departure from prior work that relies on costly online APIs (SerpAPI, Jina) during RL, replacing them with a pre-constructed multimodal corpus and dense retrieval.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a complete, reproducible pipeline for training multimodal deep research agents from scratch, which is valuable given that most prior agentic search agents are closed-source in their data and trajectories. The offline search engine's 60× latency reduction and elimination of per-call API costs ($640 → $0 per hundred steps) makes large-scale GRPO training with multiple rollouts practically feasible for academic labs. Open questions include whether the offline corpus quality gap (e.g., 62.7 vs 67.8 on MMSearch) becomes more pronounced on harder, more open-ended tasks, and whether the decompose-recompose strategy generalizes to larger tool inventories or more complex multi-hop reasoning chains.
