## Problem

Prior abductive-Bayesian frameworks for LLM-based probability estimation (e.g., BIRD) face a dual failure mode: forward abduction produces sparse factor spaces that yield 'unknown' abstentions, while naively expanding factors to improve coverage injects statistical noise and spurious correlations that violate the Naive Bayes conditional-independence assumption, degrading calibration. No existing method simultaneously resolves sparsity and dependency violation in a single coherent architecture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263773/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a context C decomposed into a high-level scenario S_cen (defining a neutral reasoning space) and a downstream condition u, along with two competing outcomes O1 and O2. It outputs calibrated conditional probabilities P(Oi|C) for each outcome via a convex combination of Naive Bayes and Causal Bayesian Network posteriors, plus a binary decision label. An 'unknown' prediction is emitted only when the factor-mapping operator returns an empty set or the maximum posterior falls below a preset abstention threshold τ; in the preference-based evaluation τ=0 so abstention occurs solely on zero-factor matches.

## Method

ANCHOR is a three-stage pipeline. First, iterative bottom-up abduction generates a dense factor pool by repeatedly prompting an LLM for diverse supporting/refuting sentences, harvesting and validating new factors until a target size is reached, then organizing them into a two-level hierarchy via MiniLM embeddings, UMAP dimensionality reduction, HDBSCAN clustering, and LLM-assigned theme labels. Second, context-aware mapping performs coarse-to-fine KNN retrieval over cluster prototypes (weighted blend of theme embedding and member-factor mean) followed by self-consistency voting and a reflective LLM filter to select a compact, high-confidence factor subset for the condition. Third, probabilistic inference constructs both a Naive Bayes model and a Causal Bayesian Network whose structure is dynamically discovered by prompting the LLM to identify latent variables that mediate factor dependencies; both models are parameterized by LLM-elicited conditional probabilities (using a symmetric proxy-likelihood mapping) and their posteriors are aggregated via a Linear Opinion Pool to produce the final calibrated estimate.

## Results

On the Common2Sense preference-based pairwise evaluation, ANCHOR with Qwen2.5-72B achieves a micro-averaged F1 of 60.6% with 99.95% coverage, outperforming the strongest baselines (Compare with GPT-4 at 55.6%, BIRD‡ at 52.6%). On decision-making tasks (Table 2), ANCHOR (Qwen2.5-72B) reaches 81.7% accuracy on Today, 76.4% on Plasma, 62.1% on CNN, and 61.1% on ExpertQA, surpassing both BIRD and the 671B DeepSeek-V3 model on the harder temporal and long-context tasks. The smaller Qwen2.5-32B variant still outperforms all baselines built on the larger 72B model, achieving 56.3% F1 on Common2Sense.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against BIRD (Feng et al., 2025), the primary abductive-Bayesian baseline, ANCHOR wins decisively: BIRD† achieves only 42.1% F1 with 87.77% coverage (frequent 'unknown' abstentions), while BIRD‡ with relaxed mapping reaches 52.6% F1 at 99.99% coverage; ANCHOR (72B) surpasses both by 8+ points in F1 while maintaining near-complete coverage without manual intervention. Against CoT prompting (Wei et al., 2022), ANCHOR leads by a wide margin—CoT with DeepSeek-V3-671B scores only 33.8% F1 on Common2Sense, and on decision-making tasks CoT (671B) reaches 78.3% on Today versus ANCHOR's 81.7%. Against Vanilla verbalized probability (Wang et al., 2023), ANCHOR (72B) improves F1 from 47.0% to 60.6% on Common2Sense and from 58.3% to 81.7% on Today. Notably, even the unfair baselines (Compare with GPT-4 at 55.6%, Factor-based at 52.5%) are outperformed by ANCHOR, and the 32B ANCHOR model beats all baselines built on the larger 72B model.

## Relation to Prior Work

ANCHOR directly extends BIRD (Feng et al., 2025), which pioneered the abductive-Bayesian paradigm of generating a factor space from a scenario and performing Naive Bayes inference over factor combinations. The key departures are: (1) inverting BIRD's forward abduction (factor → attributes) into a bottom-up strategy that iteratively generates diverse sentences, harvests factors, and organizes them into a two-level hierarchy via UMAP+HDBSCAN clustering and LLM-guided theming; (2) augmenting the Naive Bayes model with a Causal Bayesian Network whose latent variables capture inter-factor dependencies, relaxing the conditional-independence assumption; and (3) replacing BIRD's CPT training with LLM-elicited priors, drawing on recent work showing LLMs can provide informative Bayesian priors. The hierarchical retrieval pipeline (coarse-to-fine KNN over cluster prototypes) also connects to structured RAG literature, but ANCHOR constructs its knowledge source from scratch rather than indexing existing corpora.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in LLM-based decision support: sparse factor spaces produce 'unknown' abstentions, while naive expansion injects noise that violates Naive Bayes independence. ANCHOR's near-complete coverage (99.95%) with improved calibration over BIRD and direct LLM baselines makes it a more deployable option for high-stakes binary decisions. However, the framework's reliability is tightly coupled to LLM quality at multiple stages (factor generation, latent discovery, parameter elicitation), and the authors acknowledge that reducing abstentions can trade off as confident-but-incorrect mappings under distribution shift. Open questions include whether the LLM-elicited proxy-likelihood approximation (φ_f ≈ θ_f) remains valid under non-uniform priors, how the CBN structure generalizes beyond binary latent variables, and whether the approach scales to multi-class or continuous-outcome settings.
