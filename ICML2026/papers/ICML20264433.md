## Problem

Reinforcement learning post-training has proven effective for NLP and short regulatory DNA elements (<1 kb), but no prior work has applied structural reward shaping to whole-plasmid generation, where a model must coordinate multiple functional components (ORIs, promoters, CDS, terminators, markers) across several kilobases simultaneously. This paper asks whether a biologically motivated reward function can guide a DNA language model to produce valid plasmid architectures without degrading its general sequence-prediction ability.

## Input / Output

The model consumes short nucleotide prompts (4–300 bp DNA seeds, either random or structured cassette seeds encoding marker genes) and autoregressively generates whole circular plasmid sequences capped at 256 BPE tokens (~5–15 kb). Generated sequences are scored by a composite reward in [0,1] combining functional annotation counts, a length prior favoring 3–6 kb, and a repeat penalty, then validated by a bioinformatics QC pipeline requiring exactly one ORI (≥99% BLAST identity), one or two AMR genes (100% identity via AMRFinderPlus), and no internal repeats >50 bp.

## Method

The paper applies Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) to the PlasmidGPT foundation model using a three-component reward: (i) functional annotation scoring via Pyrodigal and PlasmidKit with a location-aware cassette bonus rewarding correct promoter→CDS→terminator ordering within 300 bp, (ii) a length prior with full bonus in the 3–6 kb range tapering to zero at 2 kb and 30 kb bounds, and (iii) a −0.1 penalty per repeat ≥50 bp. A five-model ablation identifies the cassette bonus as the critical component (removal drops pass rate from 66.9% to 19.8%), while rejection-sampling baselines confirm the gain reflects genuine distributional shift rather than selective filtering. Notably, 3-mer composition and minimum free energy density converge to real-plasmid values under both SFT and RL despite neither being directly optimized, and held-out continuation log-likelihood improves over the pretrained baseline on all 29 non-Addgene plasmids (mean Δ = +0.83 nats).

## Results

On the 8-prompt, 4,000-sequence evaluation, the GRPO-trained RL model achieves a 71.6% bioinformatics QC pass rate, compared to 4.3% for the pretrained PlasmidGPT base model and 11.0% for the SFT baseline. RL mean sequence length (6,517 bp) is closest to the Addgene reference (7,470 bp), and RL 3-mer JSD (0.022) and MFE density (−0.149) both converge to the real-plasmid regime (0.012 and −0.151 respectively). On 29 held-out non-Addgene plasmids, RL improves continuation log-likelihood over Base on all 29 sequences (mean Δ = +0.83 nats, Cohen's d = +1.41) and CDS-junction surprisal (mean Δ = +2.40 nats, 29/29).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares GRPO post-training against two parallel baselines: the pretrained PlasmidGPT base model and a supervised fine-tuned (SFT) model from prior lab work. RL dominates on the primary QC pass-rate metric, achieving 71.6% versus 4.3% (Base, ~17× improvement) and 11.0% (SFT, ~6.5× improvement). On held-out continuation log-likelihood (29 non-Addgene plasmids), RL beats Base on all 29 sequences (mean Δ = +0.83 nats, p = 2.75×10⁻⁸) but underperforms SFT (−8.16 vs. −7.35 mean log-prob), so RL does not claim superiority over SFT on predictive performance. Rejection-sampling baselines confirm the gain is a genuine distributional shift: Base at K=64 reaches only 54.5% success, while RL saturates at 99.0% by K=16. RL diversity (0.596) is lower than both baselines, reflecting concentration on the valid plasmid subspace rather than model collapse (Addgene reference: 0.783).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PlasmidGPT foundation model (Shao, 2024a) and the SFT/QC pipeline from the authors' prior lab work (Cunningham et al., 2025) by applying Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO, from DeepSeekMath) as a post-training objective for whole-plasmid generation. It departs from prior RL-for-DNA efforts (TACO, GENERator) that targeted short regulatory elements under 1 kb, instead operating on multi-kilobase constructs where the model must coordinate ORIs, promoters, CDS, terminators, and markers simultaneously. The reward function is grounded in domain-specific plasmid engineering constraints (Brophy & Voigt, 2014; Meng & Ellis, 2020) rather than learned preferences, and the QC pipeline is reused directly from the SFT baseline to enable controlled comparison.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 71.6% QC pass rate (vs. 4.3% base) demonstrates that structural reward shaping can navigate the combinatorial plasmid design space far more effectively than supervised fine-tuning or rejection sampling at practical budgets, with the ablation isolating the cassette-arrangement bonus as the critical component. The three-tier taxonomy (directly optimized, reward-correlated, indirectly shaped) and the independent convergence of MFE density under both SFT and RL suggest that structural constraints in DNA are tightly coupled to biophysical outcomes, offering a conceptual framework for reward design in other sequence domains. Key limitations include the absence of wet-lab validation, the narrow scope to E. coli expression vectors with ColE1-dominant origins, and the reduction in architectural diversity; bridging in silico structural validity to organismal-level function remains the central open problem.
