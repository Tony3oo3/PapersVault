## Problem

Tree ensembles dominate tabular prediction but lack the Bayesian generative structure required by Thompson Sampling, forcing existing tree-based bandits to rely on heuristic uncertainty quantification that severs the link between the posterior and regret guarantees. Meanwhile, linear TS is too biased for the nonlinear, sparse reward landscapes typical of mobile health, and kernel or neural TS are computationally expensive and brittle to hyperparameters in online settings where cross-validation is infeasible.

## Input / Output

At each round the agent receives a context vector X_t ∈ [0,1]^p (tabular features) and must select one of K discrete actions; it then observes a scalar reward. The method maintains K independent BART ensembles (one per arm) and produces posterior draws via batched MCMC (Metropolis-within-Gibbs with 4 parallel chains, 500 burn-in + 500 post-burn-in iterations), which are consumed sequentially from a pooled sample set between refresh points defined by a logarithmic schedule r(t) = ⌈8 log t⌉. The first τK rounds use forced round-robin exploration before switching to posterior-sampling-based action selection.

## Method

BFTS replaces heuristic tree-ensemble uncertainty with the exact BART posterior: each arm a is modeled by an independent sum-of-m-trees BART with a depth-exponential splitting prior (α_qd = 0.45), Gaussian leaf priors with shrinkage κ = 2, and a Dirichlet-sparse split-variable prior. Thompson Sampling draws a parameter θ̂ from the MCMC posterior pool and acts greedily on the sampled reward function. The key theoretical contribution is an information-theoretic Bayesian regret bound Õ(K√T), derived by combining the Russo–Van Roy information ratio with a novel mutual-information decomposition (Lemma 2) that exploits the arm-wise posterior factorization to separate tree-structure entropy from leaf-parameter capacity. A complementary frequentist minimax bound Õ(KT^((α+d)/(2α+d))) is established for a 'feel-good' variant under α-Hölder smoothness, certifying that the same BART prior supports minimax-rate learning with a vanishing optimism perturbation.

## Results

On nine OpenML contextual bandit benchmarks (T=10,000), BFTS achieves the best average rank (1.89) and the lowest mean final cumulative regret on 7 of 9 datasets, e.g., 1538.7 on Adult, 53.9 on Mushroom, and 106.9 on Shuttle. On eight synthetic scenarios, BFTS attains the best average rank (1.63) with final regret as low as 44.5 on the model-matched SynBART task and 377.4 on Friedman2. On the Drink Less mHealth trial (T=10,470), BFTS achieves the highest estimated policy value, improving engagement probability by +38.2% over the original random policy at the final horizon. The paper also derives a Bayesian regret bound of Õ(K√T) for ideal posterior sampling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BFTS is compared against four families of baselines: linear (LinTS, LinUCB), neural (NeuralTS), and tree-ensemble (RFTS, XGBoostTS). Against the closest competitor XGBoostTS, BFTS wins on 5 of 9 OpenML datasets (5-2-2 win-tie-lose), with the largest gaps on Mushroom (53.9 vs. 77.4) and Shuttle (106.9 vs. 170.4), while XGBoostTS edges ahead on Covertype (3541 vs. 3734) and MNIST (4081 vs. 4385). Against linear baselines, BFTS dominates: it beats LinTS on 7/9 datasets (7-0-2) and LinUCB on 8/9 (8-1-0), with particularly large margins on Mushroom (53.9 vs. 500.6 for LinTS) and GasDrift (818 vs. 481 for LinTS, where the linear model actually wins due to the dataset's simpler structure). Against NeuralTS, BFTS wins on 8/9 datasets (8-0-1); the sole loss is on MNIST (4385 vs. 1701), which is expected since NeuralTS leverages inductive biases suited to high-dimensional image data. On synthetic benchmarks, BFTS achieves the best average rank (1.63 vs. 3.00 for NeuralTS, 3.13 for XGBoostTS), with the largest gains in nonlinear Friedman scenarios where linear baselines suffer regret exceeding 8,000–26,000 versus BFTS's 660–814.

## Relation to Prior Work

BFTS sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines: Bayesian Additive Regression Trees (Chipman et al., 2010; Hill et al., 2020) as a flexible nonparametric regression model, and Thompson Sampling (Thompson, 1933; Russo & Van Roy, 2016) as a principled exploration strategy. Prior tree-based bandit methods (Nilsson et al., 2024; Elmachtoub et al., 2017) rely on heuristic uncertainty quantification that severs the link between the posterior and regret guarantees, while non-linear alternatives like NeuralTS (Zhang et al., 2021) and KernelTS (Chowdhury & Gopalan, 2017) are computationally expensive and hyperparameter-sensitive in online settings. BFTS departs from both by using BART's exact MCMC posterior for Thompson Sampling, leveraging the depth-exponential splitting prior of Ročkova & van der Pas (2020) and the Dirichlet-sparse split prior of Linero (2018) to obtain a clean information-theoretic regret bound via the Russo–Van Roy information ratio framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BFTS provides the first rigorous Bayesian regret guarantee for a tree-ensemble-based Thompson Sampling method, closing a gap that has kept tree ensembles (despite their dominance on tabular data) largely outside the theoretically grounded bandit literature. The practical relevance is strong for mHealth and other tabular decision-making settings where linear models are biased and neural models are brittle, and the sensitivity analysis showing robustness to hyperparameters makes it a low-tuning-burden alternative. Key open questions remain: the theoretical bridge between Ideal BFTS (exact posterior) and Practical BFTS (batched MCMC with elevated R-hat) is unresolved, the arm-wise design limits applicability to moderate-K settings, and the method's extrapolation behavior in unexplored covariate regions is a known limitation of standard BART that could matter in long-horizon online deployment.
