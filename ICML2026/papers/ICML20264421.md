## Problem

In retrieval-augmented generation with precomputed document-level KV caches, selectively recomputing a small subset of tokens under a global causal mask is essential to restore cross-chunk interactions, but existing methods (CacheBlend, EPIC) rely on shallow-layer representation discrepancies or fixed positional heuristics that fail to capture whether a token is both semantically relevant to the query and structurally positioned to propagate information during decoding. No prior criterion explicitly aligns token selection with the information pathways required for answer generation under inference-time attention geometry.

## Input / Output

The method takes independently prefetched KV caches for N context chunks (each computed under chunk-local RoPE positions), a query prompt, and reconstructs global positional assignments for the concatenated sequence. It outputs a small set of recomputed KV states (typically 15% of context tokens) that replace their chunk-local counterparts, after which standard autoregressive decoding produces the final answer. The method is training-free and operates entirely at inference time; chunks may be text documents, passages, or visual token grids, and the recomputation budget is a fixed ratio rather than an absolute token count.

## Method

The core mechanism is a prompt-conditioned attention-norm criterion: after concatenating prefetched chunks with the prompt under reconstructed GLOBAL RoPE positions, the method computes the aggregated attention mass from all prompt tokens to each context token at intermediate-to-late layers (22–25) and selects the top-k tokens for full-context KV recomputation. A critical finding is that this signal is only reliable under inference-consistent RoPE geometry—chunk-local or mismatched positional configurations produce unstable and misleading rankings—motivating the global positional reconstruction step. An optional chunk reordering strategy scores each chunk by the density of selected tokens and places higher-scoring chunks closer to the prompt, followed by a second selection pass under the new GLOBAL layout to refine targets. The approach is plug-and-play, requires no model modification or additional training, and reduces inter-GPU communication under sequence-parallel execution by avoiding full KV all-gathering.

## Results

On LongBench passage-split QA (15% recomputation ratio, chunk size 2048), InfoFlow KV achieves F1 of 0.5019/0.3386/0.5954/0.2288 on 2WikiMQA/MuSiQue/HotpotQA/NarrativeQA with Qwen3-14B, recovering 97–103% of full-context baseline performance (0.5161/0.3718/0.5922/0.1654) while No Recompute drops to 0.1162/0.1012/0.3059/0.2078. On VLM benchmarks (Qwen3-VL-8B, k=4 visual chunks), the method scores 802 on OCRBench and 65.68 on ChartQA versus 781/62.00 for No Recompute. Under 4×H100 sequence-parallel execution at 32K tokens, InfoFlow KV reduces TTFT to 914 ms (3.49× speedup over single-GPU prefill) while simultaneously improving F1 over ring attention by +0.013 to +0.026 across three QA benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two primary KV-recomputation baselines, InfoFlow KV wins consistently: on Qwen passage-split it beats CacheBlend by +0.069 F1 on 2WikiMQA (0.5019 vs 0.4330) and +0.062 on MuSiQue (0.3386 vs 0.2765), and beats EPIC by +0.132 on 2WikiMQA (0.5019 vs 0.3697) and +0.091 on MuSiQue (0.3386 vs 0.2480); the same pattern holds on LLaMA and GLM-4. On VLM benchmarks (Qwen3-VL-8B, k=4), the method outperforms CacheBlend on OCRBench (802 vs 786) and ChartQA (65.68 vs 62.68), and outperforms EPIC on all five VLM tasks. Under sequence-parallel execution, InfoFlow KV simultaneously achieves lower TTFT than ring attention (914 ms vs 2350 ms at 32K) and higher F1 (e.g., 0.5783 vs 0.5653 on HotpotQA). Against training-based KVLink5, the method matches average F1 (0.3482 vs 0.3480 on LLaMA-3-3B) while being fully training-free. Against training-free chunked-inference baselines TurboRAG and PCW, the method leads by large margins (e.g., 0.5019 vs 0.2880 on 2WikiMQA with Qwen). Against KV compression/eviction methods (PyramidKV, SnapKV, StreamingLLM), the method outperforms by 0.15–0.19 F1 on 2WikiMQA and 0.15–0.19 on MuSiQue with LLaMA, confirming that selective recomputation preserves far more cross-chunk information than irreversible token eviction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits within the selective KV recomputation line initiated by CacheBlend (representation-discrepancy scoring in shallow layers) and EPIC (fixed positional heuristics at chunk boundaries). It departs from both by reframing token selection as an information-flow problem and showing that a simple prompt-conditioned attention-norm signal, computed under inference-consistent RoPE geometry, jointly captures semantic relevance and positional influence. The RoPE-geometry requirement—that scoring positions must match decoding positions—is the key conceptual departure, and the chunk-reordering step extends the idea from token-level to chunk-level layout. The method is positioned as complementary to KV eviction/compression (H2O, SnapKV, PyramidKV) and to parallel-context approaches (CEPE, PCW), operating at prefilling rather than decoding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is in RAG and multi-query serving pipelines where document-level KV caches are amortized across queries; the method recovers most of full-context accuracy at ~15% recomputation cost, with a 3.5× TTFT speedup over single-GPU prefill at 32K tokens under sequence parallelism. A key limitation is the reliance on FlashInfer-style irregular-attention kernels, which incur up to 2× overhead relative to ideal FLOPs, making custom sparse causal-attention kernels an important open problem. The RoPE-geometry finding also raises the broader question of how positional-encoding consistency should be enforced in any pipeline that decouples prefilling from decoding, a concern that will grow as long-context and multimodal models become standard.
