key_contribution: Proposes Energy-Based Data Reassembly (EBDR), a new memory replay paradigm for continual learning that partitions training images into patches and reassembles them into composite replay images via an energy-based optimization (unary discriminative alignment + pairwise coherence) solved with mean-field variational inference, achieving up to 6% higher accuracy and up to 10% better backward transfer than state-of-the-art methods under the same memory budget.
limitations: The method is evaluated exclusively on image classification benchmarks (CIFAR-10/100, Tiny-ImageNet, ImageNet-R, MedMNIST) and does not address non-image modalities or very long task sequences. The reassembly is performed only once per task and requires access to the full current-task training set, and the ablation reveals that the pairwise coherence term contributes less than the paper's emphasis suggests, leaving open whether simpler patch-selection heuristics could capture most of the benefit.

surprise_factor: The ablation showing that removing the pairwise coherence term still yields a large gain over raw replay (43.71 vs 36.37 Task-IL on CIFAR-100) is mildly surprising, suggesting the dominant benefit is information density from packing multiple patches rather than the energy-based coherence enforcement the paper emphasizes. The ~2× improvement in backward transfer over DER++ on CIFAR-100 Task-IL is a solid but not extraordinary margin, and the consistent gains across five datasets and two architectures make the result credible rather than anomalous.

## Problem

Memory-based replay methods for continual learning store raw images in a fixed-capacity buffer, where each image occupies an entire memory slot regardless of its information content. Under tight memory budgets, this 'one image = one slot' design yields sparse, poorly representative replay sets that severely limit the model's ability to approximate historical distributions and resist catastrophic forgetting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264793/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes the current task's labeled training images (e.g., 32×32 CIFAR, 64×64 Tiny-ImageNet, 224×224 MedMNIST), crops K candidate patches per image, and uses the model's encoder to compute normalized features and per-patch cross-entropy losses. It produces Q composite images (each assembled from N=4 patches) paired with soft labels obtained by averaging the model's patch-level predictions, which are then stored in the memory buffer for replay during subsequent tasks.

## Method

EBDR formulates patch-to-slot assignment as an energy minimization problem with two terms: a unary energy measuring each patch's individual discriminative alignment with the target class, and a pairwise interaction energy (cosine-based) penalizing incoherent patch combinations within the same composite image. Because exact minimization is combinatorially intractable, the paper introduces a Boltzmann distribution over assignments and derives an efficient mean-field variational algorithm with coordinate updates that iteratively refine per-slot patch distributions. Patches are selected without replacement to ensure complementary information across slots, and the reassembly is performed only once per task with minimal overhead. Theoretical analysis proves that minimizing the interaction energy bounds within-composite gradient variance, leading to a tighter forgetting bound and superior representation fidelity compared to raw replay under the same memory budget.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 with ResNet-18 (memory size 500), EBDR achieves 46.24% Class-IL accuracy and 85.02% Task-IL accuracy, outperforming the strongest baselines (CSReL-LODE-DER++ at 41.96% Class-IL, LODE-DER++ at 38.95% Class-IL / 78.92% Task-IL). On Tiny-ImageNet, EBDR reaches 29.76% Class-IL and 65.36% Task-IL, surpassing LODE-DER++ (22.01% / 57.26%) by over 7 points. Backward transfer improves substantially: EBDR achieves −7.18 BWT on CIFAR-100 Task-IL versus −15.24 for LODE-DER++, and −7.65 on Tiny-ImageNet Task-IL versus −19.13. With a ViT backbone, EBDR attains 86.71% Class-IL on CIFAR-100 and 76.06% Class-IL on ImageNet-R, exceeding the best prompt-based method CODA-P (86.25% and 73.51% respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EBDR consistently outperforms all 23 baselines across every dataset and setting where comparisons are available. Against the strongest memory-replay baselines, EBDR gains +4.29 points over CSReL-LODE-DER++ (41.96%) and +7.29 over LODE-DER++ (38.95%) on CIFAR-100 Class-IL, and +7.75 over LODE-DER++ (57.26%) on Tiny-ImageNet Task-IL. On CIFAR-10, EBDR (77.16% Class-IL, 96.57% Task-IL) surpasses CILA (76.03%, 96.40%) and LODE-DER++ (75.45%, 94.41%). Backward transfer improvements are particularly pronounced: EBDR achieves −7.18 BWT on CIFAR-100 Task-IL versus −15.24 for LODE-DER++ and −14.57 for ER-ACE, and −7.65 on Tiny-ImageNet Task-IL versus −19.13 for LODE-DER++. With ViT, EBDR (86.71% CIFAR-100 Class-IL, 76.06% ImageNet-R Class-IL) edges out CODA-P (86.25%, 73.51%) and substantially outperforms DER++ (63.64%, 58.29%). Regularization-based methods (SI, LwF, oEWC, CPR) and architecture-based methods (HAT) remain far behind, confirming that memory replay with reassembly is the dominant paradigm under these settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard memory-replay paradigm in continual learning (ER, DER++, iCaRL) where each buffer slot stores one raw image, and from coreset-selection methods (GSS, CSReL) that choose subsets of raw images, by redefining the memory unit itself as a composite image assembled from patches of multiple samples. The energy-based formulation with unary and pairwise interaction terms draws on Boltzmann-distribution modeling and mean-field variational inference from statistical physics and probabilistic graphical models, repurposing these tools to solve a combinatorial patch-assignment problem. It is explicitly distinguished from exemplar-compression approaches (MRDC, CIM, SFEC++), which reduce bits per stored image, whereas EBDR reorganizes information across images into a single structured composite.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a fundamental information-density bottleneck in memory-based CL and provides a principled, theoretically grounded solution (gradient-variance bounds, bounded forgetting, representation-fidelity guarantees) that is computationally cheap since reassembly runs once per task. The ablation revealing that even unary-only reassembly (without coherence enforcement) substantially outperforms raw replay suggests the primary gain comes from information packing rather than structural coherence, which opens questions about whether simpler selection heuristics could capture most of the benefit. Open questions include scalability to very long task sequences, applicability to non-image modalities (text, audio), and whether the composite-image assumption holds when patches from semantically distant classes are combined.
