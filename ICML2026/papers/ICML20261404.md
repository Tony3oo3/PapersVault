## Problem

Existing AIGI detectors learn a single, entangled forgery representation that conflates content-dependent semantic flaws (e.g., distorted faces) with content-agnostic universal artifacts (e.g., frequency patterns), causing poor cross-domain generalization. Compounding this, prevailing benchmarks rely on outdated generators (GANs, early Stable Diffusion), so detectors that score well on academic leaderboards collapse on modern, in-the-wild threats.

## Input / Output

The model accepts a single RGB image, resized to 512×512 and then cropped/padded to 336×336 to match the CLIP-ViT L/14@336px backbone. It outputs a real/fake binary prediction along with per-expert routing weights (top-k gating probabilities over semantic experts), which remain interpretable and can be visualized to show which semantic domain and artifact evidence drove the decision. The Universal Artifact Expert is always active regardless of routing, so the output always incorporates content-agnostic forensic evidence.

## Method

OmniAID decomposes each CLIP-ViT attention weight via SVD into a frozen principal subspace W_M and a low-rank residual subspace W_R, within which a hybrid expert pool is instantiated: N_S Routable Specialized Semantic Experts (one per content domain) and a single Fixed Universal Artifact Expert trained on semantically aligned real/reconstruction pairs. A two-stage training strategy first specializes each expert independently on domain-specific hard-sampled data with an orthogonality loss that penalizes overlap with the principal subspace and all previously trained experts, then freezes all experts and trains a lightweight global MLP router (operating on a separate frozen CLIP encoder) with supervised gating and load-balancing losses. At inference, the final layer weight is composed as W_M + W_{R,U} + Σ g_i · W_{R,i} over the top-k_S routed semantic experts, explicitly separating 'what is generated' from 'how it is generated.' The paper also introduces Mirage, a large-scale dataset with a modern training set (~2.6M images from 2025-era generators) and a challenging held-out test set from photorealism-optimized SOTA models.

## Results

On the GenImage benchmark, OmniAID (trained on GenImage-SDv1.4) achieves 95.9% mean accuracy, surpassing the prior SOTA Effort (91.1%) by 4.8 points, with particularly strong gains on unseen GANs (BigGAN: 98.7% vs. 77.6%) and diffusion models (ADM: 91.4% vs. 78.7%). OmniAID-Mirage (trained on the new Mirage-Train dataset) further improves to 97.2% on GenImage and sets a new SOTA of 91.4% on the in-the-wild Chameleon benchmark, where GenImage-trained baselines collapse (e.g., Effort drops to 43.03% on Mirage-Test). On the authors' new Mirage-Test, OmniAID-Mirage achieves 88.39% mean accuracy and 96.81% mean AP, compared to 43.03% for the best prior method (Effort).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OmniAID decisively outperforms all 18 baselines across every benchmark. On GenImage, it beats the strongest VFM-based competitor Effort (Yan et al., 2025b) by 4.8 points (95.9% vs. 91.1%) and the artifact-focused DRCT (Chen et al., 2024) by 6.4 points, with the largest margins on unseen generators (ADM: +12.7 over Effort; BigGAN: +21.1 over Effort). On the in-the-wild Chameleon benchmark, GenImage-trained baselines exhibit severe Real/Fake bias (e.g., NPR and Fusing achieve ~100% Real but ~0% Fake accuracy), while OmniAID maintains balanced 77.4% mean accuracy and OmniAID-Mirage reaches 91.4%. On the new Mirage-Test, all GenImage-trained baselines fail dramatically (Effort: 43.03%, AIDE: 31.25%, DIRE: 40.43%), whereas OmniAID-Mirage achieves 88.39% accuracy and 96.81% AP. On AIGCDetectBenchmark (legacy GANs), OmniAID-Mirage achieves 92.88% mean accuracy, surpassing AIDE (93.03% AP but 92.88% ACC) and Effort (77.31% ACC), confirming backward compatibility. On DRCT-2M, OmniAID-Mirage leads with 91.91% mean accuracy and 90.26% F1, particularly excelling on reconstruction-based DR variants (98.15% on SDXL-DR) where most baselines drop below 55%.

## Relation to Prior Work

OmniAID extends the SVD-based orthogonal subspace decomposition introduced by Effort (Yan et al., 2025b) from a single low-rank adapter to a multi-expert pool, where each expert occupies a distinct orthogonal residual subspace. The routing mechanism and load-balancing loss are adapted from Switch Transformers (Fedus et al., 2022), while the purified artifact training set follows the reconstruction-pair strategy of AlignedForensics (Rajan et al., 2024). Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant monolithic VFM-fine-tuning paradigm (UnivFD, Effort, AIDE) by explicitly separating content-dependent semantic flaws from content-agnostic generator artifacts into distinct expert pathways, rather than learning a single entangled forgery representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Mirage dataset (particularly Mirage-Test with held-out, photorealism-optimized generators) addresses a widely acknowledged but under-addressed gap: existing benchmarks are dominated by legacy GANs and early diffusion models, making leaderboard performance misleading for real-world deployment. The stark performance collapse of SOTA methods on Mirage-Test (e.g., Effort dropping to 43%) versus OmniAID-Mirage's 88% underscores how training-data currency, not just architecture, drives practical robustness. Open questions include whether the decoupling paradigm scales to video or 3D content, whether the fixed taxonomy of semantic experts can be made truly open-set, and whether the two-stage training strategy remains efficient as the number of semantic domains grows. The negative transfer observed (Effort-Mirage underperforming Effort on GenImage) also raises questions about the interaction between modern and legacy artifact signatures.
