## Problem

Deep time series forecasters overfit noisy samples because uniform regularization (e.g., fixed-rate dropout) implicitly assumes identical learning difficulty across all inputs. Existing robustness strategies either binary-prune data (causing irreversible information loss) or impose rigid probabilistic priors (incurring high computational overhead), leaving a gap for a method that dynamically calibrates model capacity per-sample without architectural changes or auxiliary estimators.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266148/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

DropoutTS consumes a multivariate look-back window of shape L×C (L=96 for most datasets, L=24 for ILI) and produces a standard forecast of shape H×C (H∈{96,192,336,720} or {24,36,48,60} for ILI). The plugin performs a single forward-pass spectral analysis on the raw input to compute a per-sample noise score, then modulates dropout rates across the backbone's hidden layers during training; at inference the adaptive mechanism is fully deactivated, incurring zero extra latency. No architectural modifications or pre-processing steps are required beyond the backbone's standard input pipeline.

## Method

DropoutTS comprises two modules: (1) a Spectral Noise Scorer that applies global OLS detrending, FFT, log-scale min-max spectral normalization, and an SFM-anchored learnable soft mask to reconstruct the sparse signal component, using the reconstruction residual (MAE) as a proxy-free instance-level noise score; and (2) a Sample-Adaptive Dropout that maps the batch-normalized score through a learnable tanh sensitivity curve to a bounded rate p∈[0.05, 0.5], then generates a differentiable Bernoulli mask via the Straight-Through Estimator so that task-loss gradients backpropagate to the spectral filter parameters. The key insight is that valid temporal signals concentrate energy in sparse dominant frequencies while noise manifests as diffuse broadband background, so the spectral reconstruction residual directly quantifies per-sample noise without auxiliary models. This creates an implicit feedback loop: clean samples receive low dropout (preserving fine-grained detail) while noisy samples receive high dropout (forcing the network to retain only dominant patterns), dynamically matching model capacity to each sample's effective spectral dimension.

## Results

On the controlled Synth-12 noise benchmark (σ ∈ [0.1, 0.9]), DropoutTS reduces average MSE by 46.0% for Informer (peak 48.2% at σ=0.3), 11.4% for Crossformer, 3.6% for TimesNet, 2.8% for PatchTST, 1.0% for iTransformer, and 0.7% for TimeMixer. On seven real-world benchmarks, average MSE improvements are 34.2% (Informer), 7.3% (Crossformer), 4.5% (TimesNet), 2.4% (TimeMixer), 2.1% (PatchTST), and 1.1% (iTransformer), with peak gains of 68.0% (Informer on Electricity) and 51.3% (Informer on ETTm2). The plugin adds only 4 parameters (0.000034% overhead) and incurs zero inference latency, while accelerating training convergence by up to 47% fewer epochs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DropoutTS is a plug-and-play module that improves every backbone it is attached to, so all comparisons are wins. The largest gains appear on the most noise-sensitive architecture, Informer (2021), where average MSE drops 46.0% on Synth-12 and 34.2% on real-world data (68.0% on Electricity, 47.6% on ETTh2), effectively closing the gap with 2023–2024 models. Crossformer (2023) benefits by 11.4% (Synth-12) and 7.3% (real-world), while TimesNet (2023) gains 3.6%/4.5% and PatchTST (2023) gains 2.8%/2.1%. The most recent backbones—iTransformer (2024) and TimeMixer (2024)—show smaller but consistent improvements (≈1–2.4%), confirming that even state-of-the-art architectures still suffer from uniform regularization. Against the data-centric Selective Learning baseline (Fu et al., NeurIPS 2025), DropoutTS is complementary rather than competitive: combining SL + DropoutTS on ILI yields 21.2% MSE improvement versus 19.6% for SL alone, while also cutting total training time from 44.1 s to 35.5 s.

## Relation to Prior Work

DropoutTS extends the classical fixed-rate dropout mechanism (Srivastava et al., 2014) into a sample-adaptive, per-instance capacity modulator, departing from both Data-Centric Selection (RobustTSF, Selective Learning) and Prior-Centric Modeling (BayesTSF, RSTIB) paradigms that dominate robust time series forecasting. Its core novelty is introducing a third paradigm—Capacity-Centric Modulation—that acts on the functional space rather than screening data or constraining latent distributions, making it orthogonal and composable with existing strategies. The spectral noise scorer draws on the compressed-sensing intuition (Donoho, 2006) that valid temporal signals concentrate in sparse dominant frequencies, and the Straight-Through Estimator enables end-to-end gradient flow through the discrete dropout sampling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of the 'Fixed Dropout Paradox'—where static regularization simultaneously under-regularizes noisy samples and over-regularizes clean ones, producing non-monotonic error curves—provides a concrete diagnostic for a previously unarticulated failure mode in deep forecasters. The method's extreme lightness (+4 parameters, zero inference overhead, model-agnostic) makes it a practical drop-in plugin, and its demonstrated synergy with data-centric methods (Selective Learning) suggests a compositional robustness stack. However, the assumption of spectral sparsity limits applicability to processes with broadly distributed energy (random walks, chaotic dynamics), and the largest gains (46% MSE) occur on weaker baselines like Informer, while SOTA models see only 0.7–2.8% improvements, leaving open whether capacity modulation can yield transformative gains on already-robust architectures.
