## Problem

Classical semi-supervised learning theory assumes clean covariates, but modern ML pipelines routinely train downstream predictors on noisy proxy representations (e.g., pretrained embeddings) while labels remain scarce. It is unclear whether and under what conditions unlabeled proxy covariates can improve generalization despite measurement error, and how this interacts with the spectral structure of the kernel integral operator. This paper provides the first finite-sample generalization bounds for semi-supervised regression with noisy proxy covariates, identifying precisely when unlabeled proxies recover oracle feature rates.

## Input / Output

The method consumes N noisy proxy covariates (labeled and unlabeled) and n response labels, where the latent covariate X is never directly observed—only its proxy X̂ through an unknown measurement channel. In the distribution regression special case, each covariate is a probability distribution P observed through a finite bag of m i.i.d. samples, making the proxy perturbation explicitly controlled by bag size. The output is a regression function estimate ĝ constructed as a linear combination of s learned kernel eigenfeatures, yielding scalar predictions at new proxy covariates.

## Method

The estimator is a two-stage spectral procedure: Stage 1 computes the leading s eigenpairs of the empirical kernel integral operator built from all N proxy covariates, defining a data-dependent feature map Φ̂_s; Stage 2 fits an s-dimensional ridge regression on the labeled pairs using these eigenfeatures, so that unlabeled data influence only the feature geometry while labels determine the coefficients. The core theoretical contribution is a finite-sample excess risk bound decomposing into spectral truncation (s^{-q}), operator and proxy perturbation (r_s^{-2}(N^{-1} + C_{β',θ})), coefficient estimation (s/n), and ridge shrinkage, where r_s is a local eigengap lower bound. Under polynomial eigenvalue decay λ_s ∝ s^{-q}, the oracle rate n^{-q/(q+1)} is recovered whenever the proxy perturbation vanishes and N grows faster than n^{(q+2α)/(q+1)} for eigengap scaling r_s ≳ s^{-α}. Distribution regression is shown to be a direct specialization where the generic proxy perturbation constant is replaced by a bag-size-dependent envelope a_m = ρ(m)^{β'}, and the same oracle rate holds when m grows sufficiently fast.

## Results

On the Galaxy Clusters dataset (N=1632), SSDR achieves the lowest normalized RMSE across all label fractions, ranging from 0.75 at 5% labels to 0.37 at 40% labels, consistently outperforming the best supervised baseline (SDM: 0.88→0.43) and the best semi-supervised baseline (LapRLS: 1.01→0.48). On the Korea Stock Exchange default risk task, SSDR is best or tied for best at both 5% and 40% label budgets under both FnGuide and Bloomberg labels (e.g., 0.06 vs. 0.07 for LapRLS at 5% FnGuide). In the synthetic noisy Euclidean experiment, SSDR improves from 0.091 to 0.057 normalized RMSE as N grows from 100 to 1000 under mild noise (τ=0.10), approaching the oracle (0.053), while under strong noise (τ=0.40) gains saturate at 0.142, consistent with the theoretical proxy perturbation floor.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSDR outperforms all nine baselines (SDM, LASSO1, SVR, RF, KRR, LASSO2, CNN, LapRLS, E-CNN) on the Galaxy Clusters task at every label fraction, with the largest margins in the low-label regime: at 5% labels, SSDR (0.75) beats the best supervised method SDM (0.88) by 15% and the best semi-supervised method LapRLS (1.01) by 26%. At 40% labels the gap narrows but SSDR (0.37) still leads SDM (0.43) by 14% and LapRLS (0.48) by 23%. On the stock default risk task, SSDR is tied for best at 5% labels (0.06, matching E-CNN) and strictly best at 40% labels (0.05 vs. 0.04 for LapRLS under FnGuide; 0.06 vs. 0.07 for LapRLS under Bloomberg). The supervised baselines SL2 and SL3 (LASSO, SVR, RF, KRR, CNN) are consistently weaker, with SL3 methods (LASSO2, CNN, RF) performing worst on Galaxy Clusters, suggesting that raw feature-based approaches struggle with distributional covariates without the spectral structure exploited by SSDR.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the kernel projection semi-supervised learning framework of Guo & Zhou (2012) and Ji et al. (2012), which learns spectral features from unlabeled data and fits ridge regression on labeled data, by explicitly modeling the covariates as noisy proxies of latent variables. It departs from classical SSL theory (manifold/cluster assumptions à la Belkin & Niyogi) by addressing a complementary obstacle—measurement error in covariates—and unifies this with distribution regression (Póczos et al., 2013) by showing the latter is a direct special case where the proxy is a finite-sample empirical distribution. The technical apparatus draws on Davis-Kahan eigenspace perturbation, RKHS spectral approximation, and measurement error literature, but the combined SSL + noisy-proxy + distribution-regression framework is new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first finite-sample generalization bounds for semi-supervised regression with noisy proxy covariates, identifying precise conditions (eigengap, proxy quality, unlabeled sample size) under which unlabeled proxies recover oracle feature rates. A key limitation is that a fixed proxy error floor cannot be removed by more unlabeled data, which constrains practical applicability when proxy quality is bounded. Open questions include adaptive diagnostics for spectral and proxy quality, weaker eigengap analyses, and sharper bounds for modern learned representations (e.g., foundation-model embeddings) where the proxy mechanism is complex and data-dependent.
