## Problem

Dense retrievers for RAG operate exclusively in Euclidean space, yet natural language exhibits hierarchical structure (broad topics to specific entities) that Euclidean geometry distorts through polynomial volume growth. This causes semantically distant documents to appear spuriously similar, degrading retrieval precision and increasing hallucination risk. No prior work has built end-to-end hyperbolic dense retrievers for RAG with a theoretically grounded pooling operator that preserves hierarchical structure during sequence aggregation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263184/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a natural-language query and a document corpus, producing top-k retrieved documents that condition a downstream LLM (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct) to generate a grounded answer. All embeddings live on the Lorentz hyperboloid in R^{d+1} (768-dim spatial + 1 time coordinate), and retrieval reduces to standard MIPS via a sign-flip of the time coordinate, making HyTE a drop-in replacement for Euclidean encoders in existing FAISS/HNSW stacks. Both model variants share a 149M-parameter, 12-layer, 768-dim architecture; HyTE-H additionally accepts pre-trained Euclidean encoder outputs as input to its projection head.

## Method

The paper introduces two hyperbolic encoder architectures in the Lorentz model: HyTE-FH, a fully hyperbolic transformer using Lorentz Linear Layers, Hyperbolic LayerNorm, Lorentz Residual Connections, and geodesic-distance-based self-attention; and HyTE-H, a hybrid that projects pre-trained Euclidean embeddings into hyperbolic space. The key technical contribution is the Outward Einstein Midpoint (OEM), a pooling operator that weights tokens by x_{i,0}^{p+1} (p=1) to amplify peripheral tokens, with proofs (Theorems 4.6–4.7) showing it provably dominates both Euclidean averaging and the standard Einstein midpoint in preserving radial hierarchy. Training follows a three-stage pipeline: hyperbolic masked language modeling, large-scale contrastive pre-training (235M pairs, batch size 16384) with InfoNCE loss using negative geodesic distance as similarity, and contrastive fine-tuning on BEIR with mined hard negatives. Empirically, HyTE-FH achieves +20.2% radial increase from general to specific concepts, and the hybrid variant yields up to 29% gains in context/answer relevance over Euclidean baselines on RAG-Bench while using models 2–3× smaller than state-of-the-art retrievers.

## Results

On RAGBench, HyTE-H^Qwen achieves the best average Faithfulness (0.803), Context Relevance (0.934), and Answer Relevance (0.845), outperforming the strongest Euclidean baseline Qwen-Embedding (0.784 / 0.907 / 0.814) and smaller models like GTE (0.659 / 0.701 / 0.650) by up to 29% in context relevance. On MTEB, the fully hyperbolic HyTE-FH scores 56.41 Mean(Task), surpassing its Euclidean twin EucBERT (54.11) by 2.3 points. On the zero-shot MeSH hierarchy-sensitive benchmark, HyTE-FH retrieves the correct-depth document first 96.3% of the time versus 81.0% for EucBERT, with Specificity Hit Rate improving from 0.883 to 0.970.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HyTE-H^bert (built on modernbert-base) decisively outperforms all four smaller Euclidean baselines: it beats ModernBert by +0.146 F / +0.156 CR / +0.200 AR, GTE by +0.104 F / +0.203 CR / +0.182 AR, Gemma by +0.160 F / +0.169 CR / +0.148 AR, and KaLM-mini-v1 by +0.139 F / +0.185 CR / +0.241 AR on average RAGBench metrics. Against the strongest baseline, Qwen-Embedding, the hybrid variant HyTE-H^Qwen (which projects Qwen's own Euclidean embeddings into hyperbolic space) still wins on all three metrics (0.803 vs 0.784 F, 0.934 vs 0.907 CR, 0.845 vs 0.814 AR), confirming that the hyperbolic projection adds value even on top of a strong encoder. The 29% context-relevance gain over GTE (0.904 vs 0.701) is the paper's headline number, and the MeSH zero-shot results (96.3% R@1 vs 81.0%) further demonstrate that the advantage is specifically in distinguishing hierarchical specificity rather than general topic matching.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the hyperbolic transformer components from HypFormer (Yang et al., KDD 2024) and the three-stage contrastive training pipeline from Nomic Embed (Nussbaum et al., TMLR 2025), repurposing them for dense retrieval rather than general-purpose embedding. It departs from HyperbolicRAG (Cao et al., 2025), which projects Euclidean embeddings into the Poincaré ball over a static knowledge graph, by operating end-to-end in the Lorentz model and addressing the previously unaddressed problem of sequence aggregation in hyperbolic space. The Outward Einstein Midpoint extends the canonical Einstein midpoint (Gulcehre et al., ICLR 2019) with a radius-dependent weighting, motivated by a proof that the standard Lorentz-factor weighting is asymptotically insufficient to counteract hyperbolic volume growth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is amplified by the sign-flip MIPS reduction, which makes hyperbolic retrieval a literal drop-in replacement for existing FAISS/HNSW infrastructure with no custom distance functions, and the surprising 3× speedup in ANN search attributable to OEM's more tree-like embedding geometry. Open questions include whether the hyperbolic advantage persists at multi-billion-parameter scale, whether the 48% encoding overhead for fully hyperbolic models closes with optimized CUDA kernels, and whether the approach extends to multi-modal or cross-lingual retrieval. The MeSH hierarchy-sensitive benchmark fills a gap in evaluation: no prior public benchmark tests whether a retriever can distinguish documents at different taxonomic depths, which is precisely the failure mode hyperbolic geometry targets.
