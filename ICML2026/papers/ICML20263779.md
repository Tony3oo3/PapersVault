## Problem

Existing agentic long-video QA systems use a single coupled model for both evidence retrieval and final answer generation, leading to evidence misalignment: agents produce correct answers unsupported by their own interaction traces. The paper identifies two structural drivers—prompt pressure (inference-time context saturation pushing premature commitment) and reward pressure (outcome-only RL encouraging parametric shortcuts over grounded verification)—and shows that both stem from conflating long-horizon planning with answer authority in one shared context.

## Input / Output

The system takes a long video (partitioned into 16-second clips, pre-indexed with MLLM-generated captions and text-embedding-3-large vectors) and a text question (multiple-choice or open-ended). At each inspection call, frames are sampled at 1 fps, capped at 64 frames, and resized to 1024 px for the MLLM inspector. The output is a final answer (letter or short text) emitted only when the inspector's sufficiency verdict z=1, accompanied by a full interaction trace of retrieval and inspection steps; if evidence is insufficient, the inspector returns a SEARCH_MORE signal with a missing-evidence description that guides the planner's next retrieval.

## Method

VideoSEAL decouples the agent into two entities: a planner (Qwen3-8B LLM) that performs long-horizon evidence seeking via two tools—VisualRetrieve (cosine-similarity search over a pre-computed clip index, filtered by DeepSeek-V3.2) and VisualInspect (submits candidate spans to the inspector)—and a frozen MLLM inspector (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-7B) that exclusively controls termination and answer authority. The inspector receives only the question and submitted video frames (not the planner's reasoning history), returns a binary sufficiency verdict and feedback, and the planner terminates only upon approval. The planner is trained with GRPO while the inspector remains frozen, using either an outcome-only reward or an evidence-gated reward that multiplies answer correctness by a soft temporal-IoU overlap score. This architectural separation gives the planner a sparse, structured search memory (mitigating prompt pressure) and forces answers to be grounded in pixel-level verification rather than parametric priors (mitigating reward pressure), enabling plug-and-play inspector upgrades without retraining the planner.

## Results

On LVBench, the decoupled VideoSEAL framework achieves 55.1% accuracy, outperforming its coupled counterpart (48.2%) by 6.9 points and the strongest coupled agentic baseline DrVideo (51.4%) by 3.7 points. On LongVideoBench, it reaches 62.0% (vs. 52.2% coupled, +9.8 points), and on MLVU it scores 68.2% (vs. 64.6% coupled, +3.6 points). In grounding evaluation on LVBench, VideoSEAL achieves the best temporal groundedness (Recall@0.05 = 0.528) and semantic groundedness (G_s = 0.808), while reducing semantic hallucination to 0.113 (vs. 0.414–0.708 for baselines).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest coupled agentic baseline DrVideo (GPT-4o planner + Qwen2.5-VL inspector), VideoSEAL improves LVBench accuracy from 51.4% to 55.1% (+3.7) and LongVideoBench from 55.9% to 62.0% (+6.1), while using a smaller open-source planner (Qwen3-8B vs. GPT-4o). Compared to Video-MTR (42.0% LVBench, 57.3% LongVideoBench) and VideoMind (42.4%, 55.7%), the gains are larger: +13.1 and +12.7 on LVBench respectively. The controlled coupled-vs-decoupled comparison (same Qwen3-8B planner, same Qwen2.5-VL-7B inspector, same GRPO training) isolates the architectural contribution: decoupling alone yields +6.9 on LVBench, +9.8 on LongVideoBench, and +3.6 on MLVU. On LongVideoBench, VideoSEAL (62.0%) still trails GPT-4o at 1 fps (66.7%) and Gemini-1.5-Pro (64.0%), but those proprietary systems consume far more frames (1 fps vs. 64 frames total), and VideoSEAL matches or exceeds them on LVBench (55.1% vs. 34.7% and 33.1%). In grounding metrics (Table 2), VideoSEAL dominates all baselines on both temporal (Recall@0.05: 0.528 vs. 0.448 for DrVideo) and semantic groundedness (0.808 vs. 0.472 for DrVideo), confirming that the accuracy gains are accompanied by genuinely evidence-aligned trajectories rather than luck.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant coupled-agent paradigm in long video understanding (VideoAgent, DrVideo, Video-MTR, LongVT, VideoMind, etc.), where a single monolithic model handles retrieval, inspection, and final answering within one shared context. Rather than extending any specific prior method, it identifies a structural flaw common to all coupled designs—conflation of planning with answer authority—and proposes an architectural remedy: a decoupled planner-inspector framework where a lightweight LLM planner navigates the timeline while a frozen MLLM inspector holds exclusive answer authority via a sufficiency gate. The training procedure leverages GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) for planner optimization, and the retrieval stack repurposes standard dense-embedding indexing with an LLM filter, but the core contribution is the separation of concerns rather than any new learning algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupled planner-inspector pattern offers a clean, generalizable architectural principle for agentic video systems: separate the searcher from the verifier, and gate answering on pixel-level confirmation. This enables plug-and-play inspector upgrades (7B→72B→frontier models) without retraining, and the paper shows the system is perception-bound rather than reasoning-bound, a practically important finding for resource allocation. Open questions include whether the decoupled paradigm extends to open-ended (non-multiple-choice) video QA, whether the inspector gate can itself be learned end-to-end, and whether the LLM-judge-based semantic groundedness metric is reliable enough for high-stakes deployment. The reliance on CG-Bench for training and the single-seed evaluation of the planner-scaling asymmetry (GPT-4o regression) leave some claims under-validated.
