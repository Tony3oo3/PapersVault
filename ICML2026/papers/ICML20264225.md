## Problem

Existing watermarking schemes for language models state their guarantees in piecemeal, inconsistent ways without reference to a ground-truth attribution target, leading to ambiguity, misinterpretation, and fragmentation across papers. This paper introduces ideal attribution mechanisms—a formal abstraction grounded in an append-only ledger of prompt-response interactions—as a principled ground truth, and reframes watermarking design as faithful representation of these ideal mechanisms.

## Input / Output

The watermarking scheme takes a language model Q, a user prompt x, and a fixed watermark key (pk, sk) generated at initialization, and produces watermarked text via autoregressive token sampling where PRC codeword bits are embedded into the model's next-token probabilities. The verifier Ver_pk takes a candidate string ζ and outputs a binary attribution decision without access to the conversation history, operating at block-aligned time boundaries (every n tokens). The ideal attribution mechanism it approximates is defined over the full ledger Π_t, so the key insight is that the succinct fixed key pk serves as a commitment to the entire evolving ledger.

## Method

The paper defines ideal attribution mechanisms as sequences of deterministic Boolean functions R_t over strings, parameterized by a transcript-level attribution map R satisfying three axioms (empty initialization, monotonicity, suffix-jump) and an explicit selection rule Z that decides on-the-spot whether each response suffix is attributable. Robustness is captured via predicate-based expansion (e.g., Hamming distance), yielding a unified framework where distortion-freeness reduces to undetectability, robustness to control over false negatives, and soundness to control over false positives. Faithfulness is defined as: no PPT adversary with black-box access to the watermarked sampler and verifier can produce a string ζ for which Ver(ζ) ≠ R(ζ). The paper shows that zero-bit ideal pseudorandom codes (PRCs) precisely instantiate undetectable and faithful watermarking for the uniform language model, and that PRC-based schemes for general LMs achieve undetectability and robustness but provably cannot achieve full faithfulness due to embedding noise, establishing a fundamental gap for autoregressive samplers with irrevocable commitment.

## Results

The paper's main contributions are theoretical rather than empirical. Theorem D.10 shows that PRC-based watermarking (Algorithm 1) is undetectable and robust with respect to the β-potential selection rule for any general language model, provided the underlying ideal PRC is robust under the composite predicate Φ∘Ham_{γn}. Corollary D.6 establishes that a zero-bit ideal PRC yields an undetectable and faithful watermarking scheme for the uniform language model under the block selection rule. Lemma D.14 proves a fundamental faithfulness gap: no autoregressive watermarking sampler with irrevocable commitment that is faithful under the uniform model can remain faithful under all general language models, showing that the PRC-based scheme's failure of soundness (Example D.12) is inherent rather than a construction artifact.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or quantitative comparisons against prior watermarking schemes. The paper positions its framework relative to prior work conceptually: it reinterprets the security guarantees of ideal PRCs (Alrabiah et al., 2025) as faithfulness guarantees with respect to a specific ideal attribution mechanism, showing that the ideal PRC construction is the first scheme to achieve both undetectability and faithfulness (for the uniform model). For general language models, the PRC-based scheme of Christ & Gunn (2024) achieves undetectability and robustness but provably fails soundness against black-box adversaries (Example D.12), a gap the paper shows is fundamental for any autoregressive irrevocable sampler (Lemma D.14). The framework also generalizes the AEB robustness framework of Cohen et al. (2024) by relaxing the syntactic requirement that blocks partition the response, allowing overlapping attribution sets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the ideal/real paradigm of cryptographic security definitions, drawing directly from ideal pseudorandom codes (Alrabiah et al., 2025) and PRC-based watermarking for general language models (Christ & Gunn, 2024). It departs from prior watermarking work by introducing 'ideal attribution mechanisms'—a formal, ledger-based ground truth—and reframing watermarking guarantees as faithfulness to these ideals, rather than stating properties in isolation. The framework also generalizes the AEB robustness framework of Cohen et al. (2024) by relaxing its syntactic partitioning constraint, and it subsumes prior notions of distortion-freeness, robustness, and soundness under a single unified definition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a principled roadmap for future watermarking schemes by specifying ideal functionalities first, enabling precise reasoning about desiderata (e.g., unforgeability against white-box adversaries) that no current construction achieves. A key negative result (Lemma D.14) shows that no autoregressive, irrevocable watermarking scheme faithful under the uniform model can remain faithful under all language models, establishing a fundamental limitation of the current design paradigm. The paper is purely theoretical with no new constructions or experiments, so its impact is conceptual: it clarifies ambiguities in the literature (adaptive vs. non-adaptive robustness, single- vs. multi-query distortion-freeness) and identifies open problems, but practitioners seeking implementable schemes will need to wait for follow-up work.
