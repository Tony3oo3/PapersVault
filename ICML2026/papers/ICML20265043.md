## Problem

Despite rapid progress in video generation, it remains unclear which training clips influence the motion (temporal dynamics) in generated videos. Existing data attribution methods for diffusion models focus on static image appearance, and naively extending them to video conflates motion with appearance, missing the temporal structure that distinguishes video from images. This gap is especially acute in the fine-tuning regime, where carefully selected clips can have outsized impact on temporal coherence and physical plausibility.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained video diffusion or flow-matching model (e.g., Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B), a fine-tuning corpus of video clips with text conditioning (standardized to 81 frames at 16 fps, 480×832 resolution), and a small set of query videos representing target motion primitives. It produces per-clip motion-aware influence scores computed in VAE latent space (s=8 spatial downsampling, C=16 channels), which are then aggregated across queries via majority voting to yield a ranked list and a selected top-K fine-tuning subset (typically top 10% of the dataset).

## Method

MOTIVE computes motion-specific influence by reweighting per-location diffusion loss gradients with spatial masks derived from AllTracker optical flow magnitudes, so that the attribution signal emphasizes dynamic regions while suppressing static backgrounds. The motion-weighted loss is defined in latent space at a fixed timestep t_fix with a shared noise draw ε_fix, making the estimator a single-sample gradient inner product that avoids costly multi-timestep averaging. Gradients are normalized by frame count to correct length bias, then projected from D≈1.4B to D'=512 dimensions via a Fastfood Johnson-Lindenstrauss transform for tractable storage and O(D') dot-product scoring. Influence between a training clip and a query is the cosine similarity of their projected, motion-weighted gradients, and multi-query selection uses majority-vote aggregation (ICONS-style percentile thresholding) to identify clips consistently influential across diverse motion targets.

## Results

On VBench, MOTIVE achieves the highest dynamic degree scores across both models: 47.6% on Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B and 48.3% on Wan2.2-TI2V-5B, significantly outperforming random selection (41.3%/41.6%) and surpassing full-dataset fine-tuning (42.0%/45.3%) using only 10% of training data. MOTIVE also leads in aesthetic quality (46.0%/45.6%) while maintaining competitive motion smoothness (96.3%/97.6%). In human evaluation, MOTIVE achieves a 74.1% win rate over the base model and 53.1% over full fine-tuning across 850 pairwise judgments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MOTIVE outperforms all baselines on the primary motion metric (dynamic degree) by a wide margin: 47.6% vs. V-JEPA's 41.6% (+6.0 pts) and random's 41.3% (+6.3 pts) on Wan2.1, and 48.3% vs. V-JEPA's 45.6% (+2.7 pts) and random's 41.6% (+6.7 pts) on Wan2.2. Notably, MOTIVE surpasses full-dataset fine-tuning on dynamic degree (47.6% vs. 42.0% on Wan2.1; 48.3% vs. 45.3% on Wan2.2) while using only 10% of the data, demonstrating that motion-specific attribution identifies a more effective subset than indiscriminate scaling. The ablation without motion masking (Ours w/o MM) achieves 43.8% dynamic degree on both models, confirming that the motion-weighted loss contributes a consistent +3.8 pt gain over whole-video attribution. V-JEPA, which selects clips by high-level motion semantics, performs competitively on Wan2.2 (45.6%) but lags MOTIVE by 2.7 pts, suggesting that gradient-based influence captures finer-grained motion transfer than embedding similarity. In human evaluation, MOTIVE wins 74.1% of pairwise comparisons against the base model and 53.1% against full fine-tuning, confirming perceptual gains beyond automated metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

MOTIVE builds directly on the gradient-similarity attribution lineage (TracIn, TRAK) and the diffusion-specific corrections from Diffusion-ReTrac (timestep normalization, shared randomness for variance reduction), as well as ICONS's majority-vote aggregation for multi-query selection. The key departure is the introduction of motion-weighted loss masks derived from optical flow (via AllTracker) to reweight per-location gradients, isolating temporal dynamics from static appearance—a distinction that prior image and whole-video attribution methods do not make. The frame-length normalization is a video-specific correction absent from image attribution, addressing the confound that longer clips produce larger raw gradient magnitudes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to attribute motion (rather than visual appearance) in video generative models and to use that attribution for targeted fine-tuning data curation, achieving a 74.1% human preference win rate with only 10% of training data and surpassing full fine-tuning on dynamic degree. Open questions include segment-level attribution (current method treats each clip as a whole, potentially diluting informative intervals), disentanglement of camera motion from object motion, and accounting for classifier-free guidance which alters inference-time dynamics relative to training-time attribution. The computational cost (~150 GPU-hours for 10k samples) remains a barrier for very large corpora, though it is amortized across queries and parallelizable.
