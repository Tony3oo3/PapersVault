## Problem

Temporal filtering of privatized gradients reduces DP noise variance before it reaches AdamW's second-moment accumulator, but existing bias corrections (e.g., subtracting σ_w²) are derived for unfiltered noise and become miscalibrated under filtering. No prior work models or corrects how the filter-induced attenuation of DP noise alters the statistics of AdamW's internal preconditioner state, leaving a gap between gradient-estimation improvements and adaptive-optimizer calibration.

## Input / Output

At each iteration, FIBER consumes a minibatch of size B, current parameters θ_t, and the previous step direction d_{t-1} to form a two-point per-example gradient observation (evaluating ∇f at both θ_t and θ_t + γd_{t-1}), followed by per-example ℓ₂ clipping at norm C and additive Gaussian noise with per-coordinate variance σ_w² = (σ_DP·C/B)². The output is the updated parameter vector θ_{t+1} computed via a decoupled-weight-decay AdamW step whose preconditioner uses a filter-aware corrected second moment v̄_t = max(v̂_t − A(ω)σ_w², ε_v), rather than the raw bias-corrected accumulator.

## Method

FIBER replaces DiSK's first-order gradient-state EMA with a second-order innovation-space filter: it computes the residual ν_t = g_t − g̃_{t-1}, smooths it via r_t = (1−ω)r_{t-1} + ων_t, and integrates to form g̃_t = g̃_{t-1} + r_t, yielding a constant-gain α-β recursion that tracks persistent gradient drift. The two-point observation geometry (κ, γ) is decoupled from the denoising gain ω, enabling independent tuning of extrapolation and temporal smoothing. The key calibration step subtracts the attenuated DP noise variance A(ω)σ_w² from AdamW's bias-corrected second moment, where A(ω) = (2−ω)/(4−3ω) is derived in closed form by solving the discrete Lyapunov equation for the 2D state-space realization of the innovation filter. This correction is exact in steady state under the uncorrelatedness assumption E[s_t n_t] = 0, and a small floor ε_v prevents over-correction during early iterations.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 fine-tuning (ViT-small), FIBER achieves 88.6% top-1 accuracy at ε=0.5, 89.4% at ε=1.0, and 90.5% at ε=8.0, outperforming DiSK by +5.1 to +2.0 points and DP-AdamW by +25.4 to +3.7 points. On CIFAR-10 (CNN5, training from scratch), FIBER reaches 76.99% at ε=8, an average improvement of +18.38 points over DP-AdamW and +13.87 over DiSK. On GLUE, FIBER leads on QNLI (90.5 at ε=1 vs. 88.7 for DiSK) and SST-2 (93.1 at ε=1 vs. 91.5 for DiSK), while on E2E text generation it achieves BLEU 67.90% at ε=8, competitive with DiSK (68.73%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FIBER consistently outperforms DP-AdamW across all tasks and privacy budgets, with the largest gaps at tight budgets (e.g., +25.4 points on CIFAR-100 at ε=0.5, +22.15 on CIFAR-10 at ε=0.5). Against DiSK (the closest temporal-filtering baseline), FIBER gains +5.1 to +2.0 points on CIFAR-100 fine-tuning and +6.63 to +1.92 points on CIFAR-10 training from scratch, with the advantage shrinking at higher ε where DP noise is less dominant. On GLUE, FIBER surpasses DiSK on QNLI (+1.8 at ε=1) and SST-2 (+1.6 at ε=1) but is slightly behind on MNLI (−0.8 at ε=1), while it clearly beats DOPPLER on all four GLUE tasks (e.g., +2.87 on QNLI at ε=1). On E2E text generation, FIBER is within 0.8 BLEU of DiSK at both ε=3 and ε=8, with slightly better METEOR and CIDEr at ε=3. The ablation (Table 5) further shows that the two-point construction contributes +15.74 points on average over a one-point variant, confirming it is a major driver of FIBER's gains over DP-AdamW.

## Relation to Prior Work

FIBER extends the DiSK line of work (Zhang et al., 2024a), which introduced two-point gradient observations with EMA gradient-state filtering for DP training, by replacing the first-order EMA with a second-order innovation-space (α-β) filter and decoupling the two-point geometry parameters (κ, γ) from the denoising gain ω. It also builds on Tang et al. (2024), who showed that DP-Adam's second-moment accumulator accumulates a positive bias from DP noise and proposed subtracting σ_w²; FIBER identifies that temporal filtering attenuates this noise before it reaches the accumulator, making the unfiltered correction miscalibrated, and derives the correct attenuated factor A(ω)σ_w². The broader context includes correlated-noise mechanisms (Kairouz et al., 2021; Koloskova et al., 2023) and low-pass filtering (DOPPLER, Zhang et al., 2024b), which improve gradient estimation but do not model how filtering alters AdamW's internal noise statistics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The filter-aware second-moment correction identifies and fixes a previously unmodeled failure mode: any temporal filter applied to privatized gradients changes the DP noise variance seen by adaptive optimizers, invalidating existing bias corrections. This is practically important for the growing body of DP training that combines adaptive optimizers with temporal denoising. Limitations include the approximate uncorrelatedness assumption E[s_t n_t]=0 (empirically ~0.14 correlation), the tied-gain simplification (α=β=ω) that does not match optimal Kalman gains, and diminishing benefits at higher ε where DP noise is less dominant. Open questions include whether the constant-velocity latent model is universally appropriate, whether the approach extends to non-Gaussian or structured noise, and whether the two backward passes per update remain acceptable at scale.
