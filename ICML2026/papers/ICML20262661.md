## Problem

Applying text-only LLM editors to multimodal LLMs in the online (sequential) setting suffers from two distinct failure modes: cross-modal conflict, where high-energy visual activations dominate shared second-order statistics and bias updates toward the visual subspace, and inter-edit interference, where successive writes collapse into a shared 'edit core' and progressively overwrite earlier edits. Existing MLLM editors either incur edit-growing retrieval/composition costs or accumulate drift under sequential weight updates, and no principled framework simultaneously mitigates both failure modes while maintaining constant per-edit overhead.

## Input / Output

At each edit step the method consumes a single multimodal sample (one image plus one text query) together with the desired corrected response, using a per-edit batch size of 1. It produces additive low-rank updates ΔW = BA to the FFN output matrices of the last seven transformer layers and the visual projector, where A is a frozen orthonormal basis of rank r (512 for LLaVA-v1.5, 128 for BLIP2-OPT) and B ∈ R^{d_out × r} is the online-updated write matrix. The only persistent per-layer state is a compact r×r preconditioner P updated via Sherman-Morrison recursion, yielding O(1) memory and time with respect to the edit stream length t.

## Method

M-ORE is derived from a unified proximal-projection objective that balances fitting the current edit against a history-dependent quadratic locality penalty, yielding the closed-form update ΔB_t = −η G_t P_{t−1}. To resolve cross-modal conflict, it maintains a separate preconditioner P^{(l)} for each edited module (text layers vs. visual projector), so that high-energy visual features are confined to projector-side statistics and cannot contaminate text-side update geometry. To mitigate inter-edit interference, it restricts all writes to a fixed orthogonal low-rank subspace (A A^T = I_r, A frozen) and accumulates a steady-space locality matrix S_t via rank-one outer products of pooled features; the Sherman-Morrison recursion updates P_t = (I + S_t)^{−1} in O(r²) per edit, adaptively suppressing writes into coordinates that have been heavily occupied by prior edits. This design achieves constant O(|L| d_out r²) per-edit time and O(|L| d_out r) memory without dense second-order solvers or edit-growing storage.

## Results

On the long-horizon (t=100) online editing setting, M-ORE achieves the best average score across both MLLM backbones and both tasks: 92.56 (E-VQA) and 82.49 (E-IC) on BLIP2-OPT, and 93.07 (E-VQA) and 90.73 (E-IC) on LLaVA-v1.5. The advantage is most pronounced on LLaVA-v1.5 E-IC, where M-ORE reaches 90.73 average versus 83.66 for the strongest baseline LiveEdit (+7.07 points), with reliability jumping from 78.49 to 94.64. Parameter-modifying baselines collapse dramatically at t=100 (e.g., MEND drops to ~1% reliability on both backbones), while M-ORE maintains >91% reliability throughout. At t=1, M-ORE also leads with 98.95/98.75 average on BLIP2-OPT and 97.02/98.82 on LLaVA-v1.5, confirming strong single-edit reliability alongside long-horizon stability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

M-ORE outperforms all six baselines at t=100 on both backbones and both tasks. Against the strongest parameter-preserving baseline LiveEdit (Chen et al., 2025a), M-ORE gains +0.54 avg on BLIP2-OPT E-VQA, +1.89 on BLIP2-OPT E-IC, +1.79 on LLaVA-v1.5 E-VQA, and a substantial +7.07 on LLaVA-v1.5 E-IC (90.73 vs. 83.66), with reliability improving from 78.49 to 94.64. Parameter-modifying baselines degrade catastrophically: MEND (Mitchell et al., 2022a) collapses to near-zero reliability (1.00 on BLIP2-OPT, 1.09 on LLaVA-v1.5) after 100 edits, and AlphaEdit (Fang et al., 2025) drops to 36.83/71.33 reliability on E-VQA, confirming that null-space constraints do not prevent long-horizon drift in multimodal settings. Fine-tuning baselines FT-L/FT-M (Cheng et al., 2023) show moderate degradation (e.g., FT-L drops from 90.95 to 38.88 avg on BLIP2-OPT E-VQA). Among parameter-preserving methods, SERAC (Mitchell et al., 2022b) and IKE (Zheng et al., 2023) suffer from growing retrieval/composition costs and multimodal locality failures (IKE M-Loc drops to 6.37 on BLIP2-OPT), while M-ORE maintains >88% multimodal locality throughout. The paper also verifies that M-ORE's gains generalize to newer architectures (Qwen2-VL, Qwen3-VL) with unchanged hyperparameters, achieving 89.50 and 89.97 average at t=100 on E-IC.

## Relation to Prior Work

M-ORE builds on the FFN key-value memory decomposition (Geva et al., 2021) and the locate-then-edit quadratic target-matching formulation (ROME, Meng et al., 2022), which it recasts as a proximal-projection step with a history-dependent locality geometry. It departs from text-only editors (MEND, AlphaEdit) by identifying two MLLM-specific failure modes—cross-modal conflict from modality scale mismatch and inter-edit interference from shared edit-core collapse—and addresses them via module-wise decoupled statistics and a fixed orthogonal low-rank write subspace updated by a Sherman-Morrison recursion. Unlike parameter-preserving lifelong editors (SERAC, IKE, LiveEdit) that incur edit-growing storage or retrieval costs, M-ORE achieves constant per-edit overhead while modifying parameters directly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Online MLLM editing is a practical necessity as deployed vision-language models encounter evolving real-world facts, yet parameter-modifying online editing for MLLMs was largely unexplored before this work. The constant O(1) per-edit time and memory guarantee makes M-ORE deployable in streaming settings where retrieval-based or expert-composition methods become infeasible. Limitations include evaluation on only two primary backbones (BLIP2-OPT, LLaVA-v1.5) with modest gains over LiveEdit on the smaller model, and the rank-one sketch approximation, while shown to be robust, has not been stress-tested under highly heterogeneous edit distributions. Open questions include extension to video inputs, whether the fixed orthogonal basis can be adapted without sacrificing the drift-free guarantee, and how the method behaves under adversarial or contradictory edit streams.
