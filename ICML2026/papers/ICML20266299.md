## Problem

Existing Graph Transformers employ diverse topology injection paradigms that construct different propagation operators, yet no principled criterion exists for what makes a propagation operator desirable. Without explicit structural constraints, score-level GTs suffer from dense all-pair mixing that introduces noisy cross-class propagation, while representation-level GTs inherit topological noise from the raw graph. This paper identifies a block-diagonal structure as the ideal structural prior for graph signal denoising and proposes an efficient mechanism to enforce it.

## Input / Output

BDFormer consumes a standard attributed graph (adjacency matrix A ∈ R^{n×n} and node feature matrix X ∈ R^{n×f}) with a small set of labeled nodes for semi-supervised training, and outputs per-node class probabilities via a linear projection followed by softmax. A distinguishing input detail is the use of class-wise centroids from labeled nodes to initialize k latent anchors (k ≪ n), though an unsupervised K-means alternative is also supported. The topology refinement step produces a pruned adjacency A′ ⊂ A by retaining only a fraction ρ of edges with high anchor-induced affinity scores, which then feeds the local GNN branch.

## Method

BDFormer factorizes global node-to-node interactions through a compact set of k latent anchors via bidirectional cross-attention: nodes aggregate into anchors (N2A) and anchors broadcast back to nodes (A2N), with Straight-Through Estimator discretization enforcing sparse, hard node-anchor assignments at linear O(nk) cost. A spectral block regularization term minimizes the g smallest eigenvalues of the anchor affinity Laplacian, provably encouraging the anchor graph to partition into g disconnected components that correspond to semantic blocks. The learned anchor-induced affinity scores are then used to prune structurally inconsistent inter-class edges from the input topology, so the local GNN channel propagates only along block-coherent neighborhoods. Global and local representations are fused via a weighted sum, and the overall loss combines cross-entropy classification with the spectral regularization. The key insight is that this anchor-mediated bottleneck with spectral constraints simultaneously suppresses noisy cross-class propagation in both the global attention channel and the local topology, achieving the block-diagonal denoising prior at linear complexity.

## Results

BDFormer achieves the best or near-best accuracy on 6 out of 8 standard node classification benchmarks (average rank 1.25), with headline results of 89.01% on Cora, 96.27% on Amazon-Photo, and 97.35% ROC-AUC on Minesweeper. Its advantage is most pronounced on heterophilic graphs, where it reaches 47.90% on Chameleon and 44.20% on Squirrel, substantially outperforming the strongest GT baseline SGFormer (43.77% and 41.09%). On large-scale OGB datasets, BDFormer attains SOTA ROC-AUC of 80.31% on ogbn-proteins and 83.98% accuracy on ogbn-products, while on long-range benchmarks it leads with 59.01% on Paris and 71.69% on Shanghai.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BDFormer outperforms all 15 baselines on 6 of 8 standard benchmarks, with the largest margins on heterophilic graphs: it beats SGFormer by +4.13 pp on Chameleon (47.90% vs. 43.77%) and +3.11 pp on Squirrel (44.20% vs. 41.09%), and surpasses NodeFormer by +4.17 pp and +3.00 pp respectively. On homophilic datasets the gaps are smaller but consistent (e.g., +0.62 pp over SGFormer on Cora, +0.17 pp on Photo), while SGFormer narrowly edges BDFormer on Minesweeper (97.83% vs. 97.35%). On large-scale OGB graphs, BDFormer achieves the best ROC-AUC on ogbn-proteins (80.31% vs. 79.53% for SGFormer) and the best accuracy on ogbn-products (83.98% vs. 83.82% for Polynormer), though UGCFormer slightly leads on ogbn-arxiv (74.02% vs. 73.66%). On the long-range Paris and Shanghai benchmarks, BDFormer leads all baselines by a clear margin (59.01% vs. 56.38% for Polynormer on Paris; 71.69% vs. 71.42% on Shanghai), demonstrating that the block-diagonal constraint enables more selective long-range propagation than unconstrained global attention used by SGFormer or NodeFormer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Graph Transformer (GT) literature by reframing the design question from 'how to inject topology' (score-level vs. representation-level paradigms) to 'what structural form should the propagation operator take.' It draws on the block-diagonal representation (BDR) framework from Lu et al. (2018) and adapts it to graph signal denoising, while the anchor-based attention mechanism is conceptually related to GOAT's k-means clustering and SGFormer's linear global attention. The key departure is providing a principled structural prior (block-diagonal) grounded in a graph signal denoising interpretation, rather than the mechanism-oriented unifications offered by UGC-Former or the hierarchical masking perspective of Xing et al. (2025).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The GSD-based reframing offers a unifying lens for understanding why existing GTs fail on heterophilic graphs and long-range tasks: unconstrained propagation (whether topology-driven or attention-driven) introduces cross-class noise. The block-diagonal prior, instantiated via spectral anchor regularization and STE-based discretization, provides a principled and linearly scalable remedy. A notable limitation is the reliance on class-guided anchor initialization, though the authors show K-means initialization degrades performance by less than 1.5%. Open questions include extending the fixed anchor set to adaptive allocation and generalizing the block-diagonal prior to heterogeneous graphs with diverse relation types.
