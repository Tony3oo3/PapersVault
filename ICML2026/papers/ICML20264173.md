## Problem

Diffusion language models (DLMs) offer flexible non-sequential generation but are bottlenecked by their Transformer backbones: each denoising step requires full bidirectional attention with quadratic cost in sequence length, and KV-cache memory grows linearly with context, making long-sequence inference prohibitively slow. Whether structured state-space recurrences (e.g., Mamba) can serve as effective denoisers for masked discrete diffusion while achieving linear-time scaling remains unexplored.

## Input / Output

At each denoising step the model receives a partially masked token sequence (tokens replaced by a [MASK] token according to a log-linear noise schedule) plus a scalar diffusion timestep t, and optionally a conditioning prompt for conditional generation. It outputs a categorical distribution (token logits) over the vocabulary at every position, from which unmasked tokens are sampled. Training uses a fixed context of 1024 tokens, but inference is evaluated up to 65K tokens with the number of denoising steps scaled as K = L/p (p ∈ {8, 16}).

## Method

DiffuMamba replaces all multi-head attention layers in a standard masked diffusion denoiser with bidirectional Mamba-2 blocks: each block runs a forward scan and a reverse scan over the sequence, fusing the two directional hidden states additively to provide symmetric context without quadratic attention. A hybrid variant, DiffuMamba-H, interleaves one attention layer every five Mamba layers (~20% attention) to capture complementary global token interactions while retaining most of the linear-time efficiency. Training uses the standard absorbing-state masked diffusion objective (reweighted cross-entropy over masked positions) with timestep conditioning via adaptive layer normalization (AdaLN), and the MLP expansion ratio is halved relative to the Transformer baseline to keep parameter budgets comparable. The key architectural insight is that Mamba's fixed-size recurrent state is position-agnostic, enabling clean length extrapolation beyond the training horizon without positional encoding extension, and that eliminating the O(Ld) attention matmul term makes per-token FLOPs constant in sequence length, yielding up to 8.2× throughput gains at 65K tokens.

## Results

At the 1.3B scale, DiffuMamba-H achieves the best validation PPL of 20.17 (Quokka budget) vs. DiffuTran's 22.72, and the best zero-shot average PPL of 51.18 across 7 benchmarks vs. DiffuTran's 55.43. On generation quality, DiffuMamba-H leads with Gen PPL 37.34 and MAUVE 0.744 at L=1024, and extrapolates cleanly to 2× training length (L=2048) with only −1.2% Avg. PPL change, while DiffuTran degrades by +75.7%. In inference throughput at 65K tokens, DiffuMamba achieves 8.2× and DiffuMamba-H 4.3× higher tokens/sec than DiffuTran under full-sequence denoising, and 2.3×/1.9× under block-autoregressive decoding.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three architectures under identical training data, tokenization, noise schedules, and decoding steps: DiffuTran (all-attention DLM), DiffuMamba (all-Mamba DLM), and DiffuMamba-H (hybrid with 1 attention layer per 5 Mamba layers). At 1.3B, DiffuMamba-H wins on every metric: Val. PPL 20.17 vs. 22.72 (−2.55), zero-shot Avg. PPL 51.18 vs. 55.43 (−4.25), Gen PPL 37.34 vs. 43.06 (−5.72), MAUVE 0.744 vs. 0.712, and downstream accuracy 38.24 vs. 33.81 (+4.43). DiffuMamba (pure Mamba) also beats DiffuTran at 1.3B on Val. PPL (21.41 vs. 22.72) and zero-shot Avg. PPL (55.13 vs. 55.43), but at 240M DiffuTran actually outperforms both Mamba variants on 4 of 7 zero-shot PPL benchmarks, indicating the hybrid advantage emerges at larger scales. On throughput, DiffuMamba achieves 8.2× and DiffuMamba-H 4.3× higher tokens/sec than DiffuTran at 65K sequence length (K=L/16), and under block-autoregressive decoding at 260K tokens the gap narrows to 2.3× and 1.9× respectively. For length generalization at 2× training context (L=2048), DiffuTran's Avg. PPL collapses by +75.7% while DiffuMamba-H changes by only −1.2%, and on Needle-in-a-Haystack at 4× training length DiffuMamba-H retains 28% retrieval accuracy vs. DiffuTran's 10%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: masked discrete diffusion language models (Sahoo et al., 2024; Nie et al., 2026) and selective state-space sequence mixers (Mamba-2, Dao & Gu, 2024). While SSMs have been shown to match or exceed Transformers in autoregressive LMs and have been applied to diffusion in vision (DiM, ZigMa) and biological sequences (Caduceus), no prior work had replaced the Transformer denoiser in a text diffusion LM with a linear-time backbone. The bidirectional scanning design draws directly from Vision Mamba (Zhu et al., 2024), and the hybrid interleaving strategy follows the Jamba/Nemotron-H paradigm but is the first to apply it under a masked diffusion objective rather than autoregressive training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that iterative denoising in diffusion LMs does not require dense attention, opening a path to linear-time diffusion-based generation that could make DLMs competitive with AR models on throughput at long contexts. The length-generalization finding (DiffuTran's PPL degrades 76% at 2× training length while Mamba variants remain stable) suggests that position-agnostic recurrence is a structural advantage for diffusion models that must handle variable-length sequences. Key open questions include whether these gains persist at 7B+ scale, whether training with block-diffusion inductive biases can close the remaining quality gap at small block sizes, and whether alternative linear mixers (gated delta networks, Griffin) offer further improvements.
