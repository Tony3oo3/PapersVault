## Problem

Existing generative symbolic regression methods suffer from training-evaluation misalignment (token-level cross-entropy training vs. fitting-error evaluation) and uncontrollable discrete generation, forcing reliance on heuristic post-processing like GP or CMA-ES. Meanwhile, direct optimization methods (GP, RL) scale exponentially with dimensionality and face non-differentiability. No prior framework unifies learned distributional priors with inference-time objective optimization in a single model.

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of numerical data points {(x_i, y_i)} with x_i ∈ R^d and y_i ∈ R, and produces a mathematical expression serialized as a token sequence over a vocabulary of operators, functions, variables, and constants. The expression is generated in a token-level continuous embedding space (L × d_e) rather than a pooled global vector, and discrete tokens are recovered via argmax on weight-tied output logits. A separate DFEXHead maps the embedding to a fixed-depth tree (depth 8, sequence length 2^{d+2}−1) whose relaxed evaluation provides the differentiable signal for gradient guidance.

## Method

MOD-SR trains a continuous diffusion model (DiffusionLM-style) in token embedding space, conditioned via cross-attention on a frozen SNIP numerical encoder, with an additional REPA-inspired representation alignment loss against a frozen symbolic encoder from the same contrastive framework. At inference, gradient guidance steers the DDIM sampling trajectory by computing a look-ahead loss on the Tweedie-estimated clean sample x̂₀, backpropagating through the DFEX differentiable tree relaxation to obtain ∂L_fit/∂x̂₀. The DFEX tree uses alternating binary/unary layers with identity placeholders, and gradients are restricted to a randomly sampled subtree with top-k logit relaxation to keep optimization tractable. A perturbation-based post-processing step (reverse-SDE noise injection followed by short guided DDIM reconstruction) enables thorough expression search without external heuristics.

## Results

Without post-processing, MOD-SR achieves an average R² of 0.968 across 10 standard benchmarks (Nguyen, Keijzer, Koza, Constant, Livermore, Vladislavleva, R, Jin, Feynman, Strogatz) with an average complexity of 17.0 nodes. With its native perturbation-based post-processing, MOD-SR improves to R² = 0.999 on Nguyen and R² = 1.000 on Koza, R, and Jin, outperforming SNIP+LSO and DiffuSR+GP on most benchmarks. Gradient guidance with MSE objective lifts average R² from 0.968 to 0.976, while length guidance reduces average complexity from 17.0 to 16.0 with minimal accuracy loss (R² = 0.974).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SNIP (Meidani et al., 2024), MOD-SR wins on 8 of 10 benchmarks (e.g., Nguyen 0.984 vs 0.938, Keijzer 0.970 vs 0.802, Vladislavleva 0.907 vs 0.769, Feynman 0.788 vs 0.616) and ties on Koza (both 1.000), with the only loss being R (0.985 vs 0.991 for E2E, not SNIP). Against E2E (Kamienny et al., 2022), MOD-SR dominates on 7 of 10 benchmarks but E2E edges ahead on R (0.991 vs 0.985) and Koza complexity (19.0 vs 10.0 nodes in favor of MOD-SR). Against SymbolicGPT (Valipour, 2021), MOD-SR wins on all 10 benchmarks by large margins (e.g., Vladislavleva 0.907 vs 0.541, Strogatz 0.970 vs 0.523). Against DiffuSR (Han et al., 2025), MOD-SR is marginally behind on Nguyen (0.984 vs 0.986) but ahead on Constant (0.983 vs 0.964), Livermore (0.989 vs 0.943), and Jin (0.995 vs 0.797); however, DiffuSR reports only 4 benchmarks and achieves lower complexity (8.6–14.5 vs 15.5–18.5 nodes). SymQ (Tian et al., 2024) is compared only on inference runtime: MOD-SR with gradient guidance takes 18.44 s per instance versus 192.60 s for SymQ with REINFORCE, a ~10× speedup.

## Relation to Prior Work

MOD-SR unifies two previously separate paradigms in symbolic regression: multimodal distribution learning (following DiffusionLM, SNIP's contrastive pre-training, and REPA-style representation alignment) and direct inference-time optimization (following the look-ahead gradient guidance framework of Guo et al., 2024). Prior generative methods (E2E, SNIP, DiffuSR, GenSR) learned expression distributions but relied on heuristic post-processing (GP, CMA-ES) to align with the fitting objective, while optimization-centric methods (GP, RL, FEX) directly minimized fitting error but lacked learned priors. The key architectural departure is using a continuous diffusion model in token-level embedding space as the bridge, with DFEX serving as an auxiliary differentiable interface for gradient routing rather than a standalone optimization framework, and a frozen numerical encoder providing stable cross-modal conditioning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By making the fitting objective directly differentiable through the generation trajectory, MOD-SR eliminates the training-evaluation misalignment that has forced all prior generative SR methods to depend on expensive heuristic post-processing, and enables controllable inference-time trade-offs between accuracy and complexity without retraining. The DFEX relaxation provides a reusable differentiable interface for symbolic expression optimization that could benefit other differentiable SR approaches. Limitations include substantially higher inference cost than AR models (18.44s vs ~1.1s with gradient guidance), evaluation restricted to a subset of SRBench due to data availability, and the framework not yet extended to ODE/PDE discovery. Open questions include whether faster diffusion samplers (consistency models) can close the runtime gap and whether the gradient guidance approach scales to higher-dimensional input spaces.
