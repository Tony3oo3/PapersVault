## Problem

Standard offline RL algorithms assume no unobserved confounding between actions and outcomes, but this assumption is violated in pixel-based tasks where the demonstrator acts on structured state observations while the learner only sees images, introducing implicit confounding bias that causes up to 80% performance degradation. Existing causal RL methods for handling confounding are restricted to discrete action spaces and cannot accommodate the expressive, multimodal continuous-action policies (e.g., flow-matching) that modern offline RL relies on.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 64×64×3 RGB image observations (with frame stacking and random crop augmentations) encoded via an IMPALA image encoder, paired with offline trajectory tuples (s, x, y, s′) collected by an expert operating in a richer state space. It produces a continuous action through a one-step flow policy π_θ that maps a single noise sample z to an action x, while a separate multi-step BC flow policy μ_ω (trained via flow-matching) models the full behavioral distribution for regularization and discriminator training.

## Method

CFQL derives a closed-form lower bound on the state value function (Theorem 3.1) under a Confounded MDP, where the Bellman update follows the standard path when the behavioral and target policies agree on an action but switches to a worst-case value (min over next states) when they disagree. In practice, the worst-case term is approximated by taking the minimum over an ensemble of Q-critics, and the agreement indicator 1_{x=x′} is approximated by a learned discriminator D_ψ trained as a binary classifier to distinguish BC-flow samples from target-policy samples. The resulting confounding-robust Q-value Q_*(s,x) = D_ψ(s,x)·Q̄_φ(s,x) + (1−D_ψ(s,x))·min_i Q_φ_i(s,x) replaces the standard Q-target in the policy gradient, and the target policy is further regularized toward the BC flow via a distillation loss. The full training loop jointly updates four components—Q-critic ensemble, BC flow-matching policy, discriminator, and one-step target policy—using standard gradient-based optimization.

## Results

On 25 pixel-based OGBench manipulation tasks, Causal-FQL achieves a normalized mean success rate of 1.21× relative to vanilla FQL (1.0×), representing a 120% average improvement. Causal-FQL attains the best or near-best performance in 19 out of 25 individual tasks, with particularly large gains on visual-cube-double (11% vs. FQL's 6%) and visual-puzzle-3x3 (26% vs. 20%). In offline-to-online fine-tuning, Causal-FQL brings an additional 8% improvement over FQL's fine-tuning performance and is the first method to fully solve visual-puzzle-4x4 task 1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Causal-FQL outperforms all six baselines (IQL, ReBRAC, FBRAC, IQN, IFQL, FQL) on the aggregated normalized mean across five task groups. Against the closest baseline FQL (the direct predecessor), Causal-FQL achieves a 1.2× success rate improvement on average, with the largest relative gain on visual-cube-double (11% vs. 6%, nearly doubling) and visual-puzzle-3x3 (26% vs. 20%). Against the strongest Gaussian-policy baseline ReBRAC, Causal-FQL matches performance on visual-cube-single (81% vs. 82%) while substantially exceeding it on visual-cube-double (11% vs. 1%), visual-scene (43% vs. 28%), and visual-puzzle-4x4 (13% vs. 5%). The flow-policy baselines FBRAC and IFQL are consistently outperformed by Causal-FQL across all task groups, with IFQL particularly weak on visual-puzzle-4x4 (4% vs. 13%). IQN, a distributional Q-value method, is competitive on visual-scene (41% vs. 43%) and visual-puzzle-4x4 (13% vs. 13%) but trails Causal-FQL on the remaining groups.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Flow Q-Learning (FQL, Park et al. 2025) by replacing its standard Bellman-based Q-target with a confounding-robust worst-case objective derived from the Causal Bellman equation of Zhang & Bareinboim (2025). The key departure is that prior causal RL work (Zhang & Bareinboim 2025; Li et al. 2025b) was restricted to discrete action spaces, whereas this paper handles continuous, multimodal actions via flow-matching policies (Lipman et al. 2022) and introduces a discriminator-based approximation of the worst-case value function. The method also draws on the behavioral-cloning flow architecture of FQL and the IMPALA image encoder, positioning itself as a drop-in causal augmentation to existing flow-based offline RL pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reframes a commonly dismissed observation-space mismatch (structured state vs. pixels) as a causal confounding problem, showing it can cause up to 80% performance degradation and that a principled worst-case objective recovers much of the lost performance. Open questions include whether the discriminator-based approximation of the worst-case bound remains tight at scale, how to more finely control the objective switch during offline-to-online fine-tuning, and whether simpler confounding-robust methods can avoid the additional hyperparameter burden. The approach is also demonstrated to be portable beyond FQL (e.g., to Value Flows), suggesting broader applicability to other expressive policy classes.
