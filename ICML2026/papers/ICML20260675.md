## Problem

Existing T2I fairness evaluations focus only on default output distributions or explicit occupational biases, leaving deep semantic-demographic entanglements (e.g., 'a professional person' collapsing to one gender) undetected. Meanwhile, mainstream debiasing strategies either require prohibitively expensive full fine-tuning with catastrophic forgetting risk or introduce unacceptable inference-time latency, leaving a gap between rigorous evaluation and practical, quality-preserving mitigation.

## Input / Output

For evaluation, the HoloFair pipeline feeds 750 prompts (300 neutral + 450 semantically-triggered) into a T2I model, generating 20 images per prompt (224×224 RGB face crops), which are classified by the SpaFreq dual-stream DINOv2 classifier into gender/age/race labels; the MGBI score is then computed as the geometric mean of intrinsic diversity (ID) and the 10th-percentile conditional diversity (CA_q). For debiasing, Fair-GRPO consumes 10,000 training prompts, generates N=20 images per prompt, computes a multi-attribute log-ratio reward from classifier counts, and outputs a LoRA adapter (rank 32 on q/k/v and output projections) that shifts the diffusion model's demographic distribution toward uniformity.

## Method

The SpaFreq classifier augments a DINOv2-Base backbone with a frequency stream: the input image is decomposed via db4 discrete wavelet transform into cA/cH/cV sub-bands, min-max normalized, and concatenated into a 3-channel frequency view that is batch-concatenated with the spatial view before a single backbone pass; a learnable scalar gate α (sigmoid of a trained parameter) controls weighted channel concatenation of the two CLS embeddings before an MLP head. The MGBI metric uses normalized entropy as its base diversity measure (penalizing mode collapse via logarithmic divergence), aggregates across attributes with a geometric mean for ID, takes the 10th-percentile across semantic triggers for CA_q, and combines both via an unweighted geometric mean so that weakness in either dimension cannot be compensated. Fair-GRPO formulates debiasing as KL-regularized PPO-Clip policy optimization: for each prompt group of N=20 images, an adaptive log-ratio reward r_base(k,a)=log((N−N_k^a+ε)/(N_k^a+ε)) is zero-centered per attribute, clipped to [−5,5], and weighted-summed across gender/age/race; advantages are normalized per prompt group using running mean/variance, and the total loss adds a pixel-space KL penalty (β=0.05) against a frozen reference policy to prevent reward hacking and quality degradation.

## Results

On SD3.5-Medium, Fair-GRPO improves MGBI from 0.5211 to 0.6772 (+29.9%), with ID rising from 0.7316 to 0.8221 and CA₀.₁ from 0.3711 to 0.5579, while maintaining CLIP-Score (0.2317 vs. 0.2288) and achieving the best FID (135.09) among all methods. On SD1.5, MGBI improves from 0.6554 to 0.7881 (+20.2%), with ID reaching 0.8591 and CA₀.₁ reaching 0.7230. In the HoloFair benchmark evaluation across eight T2I models, MGBI scores range from 0.6780 (Flux1-dev) to 0.2735 (Blip3-o), revealing that high intrinsic diversity (e.g., SDXL's ID of 0.8186) does not guarantee conditional robustness (SDXL's CA₀.₁ is only 0.2865).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On SD1.5, Fair-GRPO (MGBI 0.7881) substantially outperforms all four debiasing baselines: EFA (0.7084), Balancing_Act (0.6636), InterpretDiffusion (0.6617), and UCE (0.6508), with the largest gap over EFA (+0.0797 MGBI). On SD3.5M, Fair-GRPO (0.6772) beats Balancing_Act (0.5785), UCE (0.5769), and the fine-tuning baseline SD3.5M+F.T. (0.6190), though EFA and InterpretDiffusion are not directly applicable to the MMDiT architecture. Critically, Fair-GRPO does not sacrifice quality: on SD3.5M it achieves the best FID (135.09 vs. 143.26 baseline) and highest CLIP-Score (0.2317), while on SD1.5 its FID (134.51) is the lowest of all methods. The only minor regression is a 0.0019 drop in Asr on SD3.5M, which the authors attribute to negligible reward hacking. In the HoloFair benchmark (Table 2), SDXL's high ID (0.8186) masks its poor CA₀.₁ (0.2865), demonstrating that prior single-dimension evaluations would incorrectly rank it as the fairest model, whereas MGBI correctly penalizes this context-dependent bias.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the T2I fairness evaluation line (Stable Bias, DALL-Eval, classifier-based audits) by moving from single-attribute or default-output metrics to a multi-attribute, context-conditional formulation (MGBI) grounded in the Stereotype Content Model. For debiasing, it departs from the dominant fine-tuning-on-balanced-data and inference-time intervention paradigms by adopting GRPO-style reinforcement learning with a multi-attribute per-prompt reward, building on the Flow-GRPO objective for diffusion/flow-matching models. The SpaFreq classifier extends DINOv2-based attribute classifiers by adding a wavelet frequency-domain stream with learned fusion, addressing the domain gap between real and AI-generated faces that prior classifiers (FairFace, EFA) do not explicitly handle.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The HoloFair benchmark provides a standardized, extensible evaluation pipeline that exposes a critical blind spot: models with high default-output diversity (e.g., SDXL) can exhibit severe diversity collapse under semantically loaded prompts, invalidating prior single-dimension audits. Fair-GRPO offers a practical, architecture-agnostic debiasing route that avoids the compute cost of full fine-tuning and the latency of inference-time methods, while the KL-regularized objective mitigates reward hacking. Key limitations remain: the framework is restricted to face-centric single-subject images, uses discrete demographic categories that simplify identity, and optimizes marginal rather than joint (intersectional) distributions; extending to multi-person scenes and continuous phenotypic descriptors is left open.
