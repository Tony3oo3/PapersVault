## Problem

Existing query-free (black-box) watermark evasion attacks either achieve limited success rates or severely distort the semantic meaning of the original text. The paper addresses this gap by providing a first-principles theoretical analysis of rewriting-based evasion, showing that even a small consistent reduction in the average conditional probability of sampling green tokens causes the detection probability to decay exponentially, and then translating this insight into a practical attack that outperforms prior methods on both evasion and fidelity.

## Input / Output

The method takes a watermarked text sequence, a public language model (independent of the watermarked model), and a paraphrasing prompt as input, and produces a rewritten text that evades watermark detection. The rewriting model must expose logits (or support token-level logit biasing via API) to apply the negative bias; for models like GPT-4o-mini that do not expose logits, an auxiliary model estimates high-surprisal tokens which are then re-tokenized. The proxy suppression set is constructed from the input text's top-q percentile tokens by self-information, and the output is a full document-level paraphrase rather than token-level substitutions.

## Method

BIRA (Bias-Inversion Rewriting Attack) operates in two phases. First, it computes token self-information under the rewriting model and selects the top-q percentile tokens as a proxy suppression set Ĝ, targeting high-surprisal tokens that are most likely to carry watermark traces. Second, during autoregressive rewriting, it applies a negative logit bias β<0 to all tokens in Ĝ at every decoding step, inverting the positive bias that watermarking schemes apply to green tokens. The theoretical foundation (Theorem 4.2) shows that if the average conditional green-token probability is kept at least δ below the detector threshold, the detection probability is bounded by exp(−Nδ²/2), meaning even a small per-step suppression suffices. An adaptive mechanism monitors the distinct 1-gram ratio over a sliding window to detect text degeneration and incrementally reduces |β|, allowing the attack to start with a strong bias for effective watermark removal while gracefully preventing repetitive output. No training is required, and the method operates entirely in a black-box setting without knowledge of the watermarking scheme or the watermarked model.

## Results

BIRA achieves state-of-the-art Attack Success Rate (ASR) of 98.0–100.0% across all seven evaluated watermarking schemes (KGW, Unigram, UPV, EWD, DIP, SIR, EXP) using three independent rewriting models (Llama-3.1-8B, Llama-3.1-70B, GPT-4o-mini). The most challenging watermark, SIR, sees BIRA reach 98.8–99.6% ASR versus 57.6–72.8% for the strongest baseline SIRA. In terms of text quality, BIRA attains an LLM judgment score of ~4.2 (out of 5) while producing lower Self-BLEU scores (stronger paraphrasing) than all baselines, and its z-scores drop to near-zero (e.g., −0.06 ± 0.09 on SIR, −0.34 ± 1.61 on Unigram), rendering attacked text statistically indistinguishable from human writing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SIRA (Cheng et al., 2025), the prior state-of-the-art query-free attack, BIRA wins on every watermark scheme and every rewriting model. The gap is most dramatic on SIR: SIRA achieves only 72.8% ASR with Llama-3.1-8B and drops to 57.6% with GPT-4o-mini, while BIRA maintains 99.4–99.6%. SIRA also suffers severe semantic degradation on longer texts (LLM judge score of 1.33 on 600-token SIR texts vs. BIRA's 4.14). Against DIPPER (Krishna et al., 2023), a trained paraphrasing expert, BIRA outperforms both DIPPER-1 and DIPPER-2 across all schemes; DIPPER-2's best result is 97.2% on KGW versus BIRA's 99.8%, and DIPPER lags by 20–40 points on SIR. Against the adaptive attack ADA (Diaa et al., 2024), which assumes knowledge of the watermarking scheme and fine-tunes a paraphraser specifically for it, BIRA still achieves higher ASR (e.g., 99.8% vs. 98.0% on KGW, 99.6% vs. 71.0% on SIR) despite operating under a more restrictive black-box threat model, though ADA attains a slightly higher LLM judgment score, indicating a small trade-off in semantic fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the query-free watermark evasion line initiated by Krishna et al. (DIPPER) and Cheng et al. (SIRA), which rely on LLM-based rewriting in a black-box setting. It departs from SIRA's masking-and-regeneration strategy by instead applying a global negative logit bias to a proxy suppression set identified via token self-information, guided by a new theoretical analysis (Theorem 4.2) showing that a small per-step reduction in green-token sampling probability causes detection probability to decay exponentially. The surprisal-based token selection draws on SIRA's observation that watermarking concentrates on high-entropy positions, but repurposes it as a global suppression signal rather than a local masking criterion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that all seven evaluated token-level watermarking schemes (KGW, Unigram, UPV, EWD, DIP, SIR, EXP) and sentence-level schemes (SimMark, PMARK) are vulnerable to a simple, training-free, query-free attack achieving >99% evasion while preserving semantics, revealing that current robustness claims based on insertion/substitution/deletion stress tests are insufficient. The theoretical framework (exponential decay in δ²) provides a clear design target for future watermarking: any scheme must ensure that no small per-step suppression of green-token probability can accumulate to break detection. Open questions include whether context-adaptive or position-wise biasing can close the gap, how mixed-domain (text + code) settings affect the attack, and whether the approach generalizes to non-text modalities.
