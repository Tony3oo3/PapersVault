## Problem

LLMs allocate uniform computation to every token, wasting resources on trivially predictable sequences while under-serving semantically dense positions that demand deep reasoning. Existing compression approaches—vocabulary expansion, fixed-length merging, and byte-level chunking—either yield limited compression gains, lack adaptive boundary identification, or confound architectural benefits with parameter scaling in their evaluations. ConceptMoE addresses this by introducing learnable, similarity-driven token-to-concept compression within a MoE framework that enables rigorous FLOPs- and parameter-matched comparison.

## Input / Output

The model accepts a sequence of N text tokens (or interleaved image+text tokens in the VLM setting) and produces standard next-token logits. Before the compute-intensive concept model, the chunk module compresses N tokens into M ≤ N concept representations at a target ratio R, and the dechunk module maps them back so every token in the decoder retains an associated concept for joint decoding. In the VLM configuration, compression is applied to both visual and textual tokens, with the model learning to compress images more aggressively than text due to higher visual redundancy.

## Method

ConceptMoE inserts a learnable chunk module between a shallow MoE encoder and a deep MoE concept model: the chunk module computes cosine similarity between adjacent token embeddings via learned Q/K projections, identifies boundaries where semantic shift exceeds a threshold, and merges consecutive similar tokens into single concept vectors. An auxiliary loss (analogous to MoE load-balancing) constrains the achieved compression ratio to a target R, while Bernoulli boundary-flipping during training prevents over-compression under distribution shift. After the concept model, a dechunk module applies EMA over concept boundaries and scatters concepts back to token positions, and the decoder performs joint decoding where Q/K/V in each attention layer are augmented with the associated concept embedding. The MoE architecture is critical for fair evaluation: saved FLOPs from compression are reallocated by activating more experts, looping intermediate layers, or scaling hidden size, keeping total parameters and per-token FLOPs identical to the baseline so that gains are attributable purely to concept-level processing.

## Results

On small-scale language pretraining (24B params, 559B tokens), ConceptMoE achieves 50.9 average OpenBench score vs. 50.0 for the FLOPs-matched MoE baseline (+0.9), with gains in reasoning (56.8 vs. 54.4) and math (50.0 vs. 47.2). On the 60B vision-language model, ConceptMoE gains +2.3 on long-context tasks (51.7 vs. 49.4) and +0.6 on multimodal benchmarks (54.2 vs. 53.6). The largest gains appear in continual training at 90B parameters: ConceptMoE-top11-loop8 reaches 46.4 overall (+5.5 over MoE's 40.9), with reasoning at 38.6 (+8.3) and math at 50.3 (+12.2). At R=2, inference speedups reach 175% for prefill and 117% for decoding on 300B-parameter models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All comparisons are against standard MoE baselines with identical total parameters and per-token FLOPs, isolating the architectural benefit of concept-level processing. ConceptMoE consistently outperforms the MoE baseline across every setting: +0.8 to +0.9 on small-scale pretraining (12B/24B), +0.9 on text and +2.3 on long-context for the 60B VLM, and +5.5 (layer-looping) or +6.4 (from-scratch) on the 90B CT benchmark. The only losses are minor: ConceptMoE-top15 slightly underperforms on reasoning (28.4 vs. 30.3) and knowledge (26.2 vs. 28.2) in the CT setting, and the 60B VLM shows small drops on fine-grained visual tasks (location 81.9 vs. 82.2, chart Q&A 33.6 vs. 34.7), likely due to sequential processing disrupting spatial relationships. The layer-looping strategy (Strategy 2) substantially outperforms simply activating more experts (Strategy 1), gaining +5.1 additional points over top15, confirming that reallocating compute to depth is more effective than width for concept-level processing.

## Relation to Prior Work

ConceptMoE extends the dynamic chunking paradigm from H-Net (byte-level, 9× compression) to the token level, replacing rule-based or entropy-based merging with a learnable cosine-similarity router that adaptively identifies semantic boundaries. It departs from prior token-merging work (Dai et al., Shao et al.) by making chunking fully learnable and from concurrent DLCM by using the MoE architecture as a controlled evaluation scaffold: saved FLOPs from compression are reallocated to match baseline compute, isolating genuine architectural gains from brute-force parameter scaling. The joint decoding mechanism (augmenting QKV with concept embeddings) and EMA-based dechunking are novel components not present in prior chunking approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the lossless CT conversion path: existing MoE checkpoints can be converted to ConceptMoE with minimal structural changes (adding chunk/dechunk modules and zero-initialized QKV projectors), gaining +5.5 points and up to 53% decoding speedup without retraining from scratch. This makes the technique immediately deployable in production systems. Open questions include: the optimal compression ratio is dataset-dependent (R=4 degrades performance), fine-grained spatial tasks (visual localization, chart Q&A) suffer from sequential token merging, and whether the approach generalizes beyond MoE to dense architectures where FLOPs-matched comparison is harder to construct.
