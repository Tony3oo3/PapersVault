## Problem

Existing TTA methods extensively investigate what to adapt (normalization layers, prompts, loss functions) but treat the optimization process as a black box, typically employing constant or off-the-shelf learning rate schedules. This work identifies that the stability-plasticity trade-off in TTA—adapting to novel distributions while retaining pretrained knowledge—necessitates a time-varying, confidence-and-gradient-aware LR strategy, since standard schedulers fail under non-stationary data streams and noisy pseudo-labels.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled RGB image batches (batch size 64) in an online streaming fashion, where each batch is encountered exactly once during inference, along with the current pretrained model parameters and their EMA-tracked norms. It produces standard class predictions and, at each optimization step, a scalar adaptive learning rate constrained within a time-varying interval [η_min,t, η_max,t]. CONGA operates as a loss-agnostic plug-in that modifies only the LR schedule without altering the model architecture, loss function, or optimizer type, maintaining 1× backpropagation overhead and ≤0.15 MB additional memory.

## Method

CONGA constrains the per-step learning rate within an adaptive interval defined by two gates, then selects the specific LR via a cosine decay with periodic restarts. The Safety Gate (η_max) applies logarithmic damping to the LARS trust-region ratio (‖θ‖/‖g‖) to suppress high-frequency gradient noise from unreliable pseudo-labels, while the Learning Gate (η_min) is set proportional to the squared fraction of high-confidence samples (C̄_t²), encoding the theoretical result that optimal step size scales as 1/σ². The Cycle Regulator uses a cosine decay Φ(t)=1+cos(πt/T_i) within each cycle of length T_i (default 100 batches), with periodic restarts to escape suboptimal local minima caused by structural drift. The final LR is η_t = η_min,t + ½(η_max,t − η_min,t)Φ(t), and a Safe Guard mechanism resets η_t to η_min,t when the model is overconfident and the upper bound exceeds 3×η_base. The theoretical foundation derives from a tracking-error recurrence (Theorem 3.1) showing the competition between error contraction and noise variance, and an optimal-LR analysis (Theorem 3.2) proving η* ∝ 1/σ².

## Results

On ImageNet-C (level 5, standard TTA), CONGA integrated with Tent achieves 45.9% average accuracy on ResNet-50-BN (+2.7% over Tent alone) and 64.3% on ViT-Base (+3.2% over Tent), surpassing the optimization-based SAR baseline (44.1% / 62.7%). When combined with the stronger DeYO baseline on ViT, CONGA reaches 68.5% average accuracy (+2.3%). In the more challenging CTTA setting (10 recurring cycles), CONGA+Reservoir achieves 66.6% on ViT-Base (+0.6% over Reservoir), and on ImageNet-A/Sketch/R, CONGA yields consistent gains of +0.3% to +2.2% across baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CONGA consistently outperforms all baselines it is applied to, with the largest gains observed when combined with Tent: +3.2% on ViT-Base (64.3% vs. 61.1%) and +2.7% on ResNet-50-BN (45.9% vs. 43.2%), enabling Tent+CONGA to surpass the strong optimization-based SAR baseline (62.7% / 44.1%). Against DeYO on ViT, CONGA adds +2.3% (68.5% vs. 66.2%), and against FOA it adds +0.6% (64.0% vs. 63.4%). In the CTTA setting, CONGA+Reservoir beats Reservoir by +0.6% (66.6% vs. 66.0%) and PALM by +2.2% (66.6% vs. 64.4%) on ViT, while CONGA+EATA improves over EATA by +0.4% on ResNet. On the severe-shift benchmarks (ImageNet-A, Sketch, R), CONGA yields +0.7%/+1.6%/+1.3% with Tent and +0.3%/+0.8%/+2.2% with DeYO, confirming robustness across distribution shift severity. Notably, CONGA maintains 1× backpropagation overhead and ≤0.15 MB memory, whereas PALM requires 2× BP and 4566 MB memory, and MGG incurs 22.51 MB for meta-gradient computation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant TTA paradigm that focuses on *what* to adapt (loss functions, which parameters to update) by investigating *how* to adapt—specifically, the learning rate schedule during optimization. It builds directly on LARS (You et al., 2017) for its Safety Gate concept but replaces the linear trust-region ratio with a logarithmic damping to handle the noisy, unreliable gradients inherent in TTA. The Cycle Regulator draws on classical SGDR-style cosine decay with warm restarts, reinterpreted through a tracking-error analysis (Theorem 3.1) that shows why constant or standard decay schedules fail under non-stationary, pseudo-label-driven optimization. The method is explicitly orthogonal to existing TTA objectives (Tent, DeYO, EATA, FOA, Reservoir), positioning itself as a plug-in scheduler rather than a competing adaptation strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, theoretically grounded argument that learning rate scheduling is not a minor hyperparameter but a critical determinant of TTA stability, which had been largely overlooked in the field. Its plug-in design with 1× backpropagation overhead and ≤0.15 MB memory makes it immediately deployable alongside existing TTA pipelines. However, two limitations constrain broader impact: CONGA requires gradient computation during inference (incompatible with forward-pass-only TTA like FOA's CMA-ES variant), and its behavior under extremely limited test samples (fewer than one full cycle) remains uncharacterized. An open question is whether the confidence-as-noise-proxy assumption (1/σ² ∝ C̄²) holds robustly across all TTA objectives or is specific to entropy-based losses.
