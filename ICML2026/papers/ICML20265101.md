## Problem

Standard LoRA enforces a fixed rank at initialization, creating a rigid capacity constraint that either underfits complex tasks or overfits with noise. Dynamic alternatives like MoE-LoRA (GOAT, MoLoRA) resolve capacity but incur 43%+ memory overhead from duplicated experts, while SVD-based methods (LoRA-One, PiSSA) fix the optimal subspace at step zero and cannot track its drift during training. No existing method dynamically adapts rank capacity within a single adapter without heuristic instability or prohibitive initialization costs.

## Input / Output

SBVI takes frozen pre-trained LLM weights (LLaMA-2-7B, Mistral-7B, LLaMA-3-8B, stored in NF4 4-bit quantization) and a task-specific supervised dataset (GSM8K, HumanEval, or Commonsense170k). It produces a single pair of low-rank factor matrices (A ∈ R^{d_out×r}, B ∈ R^{d_in×r}) initialized at a deliberately redundant rank r=64, which the spectral friction mechanism automatically compresses to an effective rank of ~6.8. Unlike MoE baselines that maintain multiple expert adapters, the output is a single adapter that can be algebraically merged into the base weights, yielding zero additional inference latency.

## Method

SBVI reformulates LoRA as a continuous Wasserstein gradient flow on the Bures-Wasserstein manifold of Gaussian measures, where the weight update is treated as a diffusion process constrained by a spectral bridge prior p_spec(W) ∝ exp(−Σ λ_k σ_k). The key mechanism is a Spectral Bridge SDE governing each singular value: dσ_k = (S_k − βλ_k)dt + √(2ν_k)dB_t, where task signal S_k competes against adaptive friction βλ_k, inducing a bifurcation that amplifies informative modes and exponentially suppresses noise modes. To avoid cubic SVD cost, the flow is realized via a factorized retraction M=BA^T where the balanced regularization (λ_k/2)(‖a_k‖²+‖b_k‖²) implicitly recovers the BW Riemannian gradient structure (preconditioning by the singular value matrix). The friction coefficients λ_k are updated online via a Moment Matching condition (λ_k* = (d_in+d_out)/E_k) with EMA smoothing, creating a self-reinforcing feedback loop that automatically discovers a layer-wise 'spindle' rank distribution without any manual rank search.

## Results

On LLaMA-2-7B, SBVI achieves 60.85% on GSM8K, 28.54% on HumanEval, and 47.51% on MMLU (average 45.63%), outperforming the SOTA MoE method GOAT (44.20% avg) and the SVD-based LoRA-One (45.45% avg) while automatically compressing to an effective rank of only 6.8. On commonsense reasoning (8 benchmarks), SBVI reaches 83.22% average accuracy, surpassing GOAT (82.73%) and standard LoRA (77.61%). In terms of efficiency, SBVI uses only 4.2M trainable parameters and 19.8 GB peak memory (43% less than GOAT's 34.9 GB) while maintaining the highest inference throughput at 45.4 tokens/s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SBVI outperforms all static single-adapter baselines (LoRA, DoRA, PiSSA, MiLoRA) by a wide margin on GSM8K (60.85% vs. 52.84–55.42%) and HumanEval (28.54% vs. 19.51–21.34%), confirming that dynamic spectral adaptation substantially exceeds fixed-rank methods. Against the closest competitor LoRA-One (ICML 2025), SBVI wins on GSM8K (60.85 vs. 60.44) and MMLU (47.51 vs. 47.24) with a slightly lower HumanEval score (28.54 vs. 28.66), while achieving a lower effective rank (6.8 vs. 8.0) and avoiding the O(d³) SVD initialization cost. Against the SOTA MoE method GOAT (ICML 2025), SBVI surpasses it on all three hard-task metrics (GSM8K: 60.85 vs. 60.20; HumanEval: 28.54 vs. 25.61; MMLU: 47.51 vs. 46.80) while using a single adapter instead of 8 experts, reducing peak memory from 34.9 GB to 19.8 GB. On commonsense reasoning, SBVI (83.22% avg) beats GOAT (82.73%), HydraLoRA (81.12%), MoLoRA (80.43%), and AdaLoRA (78.48%), while PiSSA underperforms even standard LoRA (73.78% vs. 77.61%), highlighting the fragility of static spectral initialization. On modern architectures (Mistral-7B, LLaMA-3-8B), SBVI consistently outperforms RP-LoRA (Riemannian preconditioning) by 1.4–1.5 points on GSM8K and 1.8–2.6 points on HumanEval, with statistically significant improvements (p < 0.05) over LoRA-One.

## Relation to Prior Work

SBVI unifies two previously separate threads in PEFT: the SVD-based spectral initialization line (PiSSA, LoRA-One) and the dynamic-capacity line (GOAT, MoLoRA, AdaLoRA). Conceptually, it departs from both by reframing rank selection as a continuous Bures-Wasserstein gradient flow rather than a one-time SVD truncation or a multi-expert routing decision, drawing its geometric machinery from Lambert et al. (2022) and Diao et al. (2023) and its bridge-diffusion structure from Xu et al. (2025). The practical algorithm reduces to standard LoRA factorization (M=BA^T) with an adaptive per-rank L2 penalty whose coefficients are updated via a moment-matching rule, making the heavy Riemannian theory a justification for what is computationally a lightweight regularization schedule.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The main practical contribution is eliminating manual rank tuning: initializing with a redundant r=64 and letting the adaptive friction auto-compress to r_eff≈7 across diverse tasks is a genuinely useful property for practitioners who currently grid-search over {8,16,32,64}. The 43% peak-memory reduction over GOAT (19.8 GB vs 34.9 GB) while matching or slightly exceeding its accuracy is a concrete efficiency win. However, the empirical margins over LoRA-One are modest (e.g., 60.85 vs 60.44 on GSM8K), and the method still requires tuning λ_max and γ, which the authors acknowledge as a limitation. Open questions include whether the greedy bridge approximation (local moment matching) can be tightened, whether the spindle structure generalizes to non-Transformer architectures, and whether the BW geometric framing yields practical benefits beyond what a well-tuned adaptive weight decay would achieve.
