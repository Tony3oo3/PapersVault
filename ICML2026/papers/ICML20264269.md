## Problem

Existing automatic evaluation metrics for LLMs—designed for summarization, translation, or short-form QA—fail to capture the nuanced qualities of human-scale multi-turn dialogue such as stylistic consistency, content coherence, and conversational engagement. Prior conversational benchmarks rely on synthetic data, reference-free LLM-as-a-judge scoring, or crowdsourced annotations of unknown quality, leaving a gap in human-grounded, per-turn evaluation of realistic task-oriented conversations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264269/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each turn of a multi-turn dialogue, the evaluation protocol presents annotators with the conversation history, a reference human-written continuation (Option A), and a model-generated candidate (Option B), with provenance hidden from the annotator. Each turn receives three independent scores from five annotators: content equivalence on a 5-point Likert scale, style equivalence on a 3-point scale (1/3/5), and reasonableness as a binary judgment (1 or 5). The ensemble metric then consumes the full vector of 12 individual automatic metric scores (ROUGE variants, BERTScore, embedding cosine similarity, LLM-judge outputs) as features and outputs a single predicted human-aligned quality score.

## Method

The paper introduces UPHELD, a benchmark of 800 professional script-writer dialogues (9,203 turns) with 153,578 per-turn human labels, and a Mixture-of-Judges ensemble framework for conversational evaluation. The ensemble trains standard supervised models—linear regression, SVM, and random forest—using the scores from 12 individual automatic metrics spanning token-based, semantic, and LLM-as-a-judge families as input features to predict the human-annotated quality scores. The key finding is that no single metric achieves strong correlation with expert human judgment on UPHELD (best individual: ~0.58 for content), but the learned ensemble improves Pearson correlation by approximately 30–40% over the best single metric. The SVM ensemble demonstrated the strongest out-of-domain transferability, maintaining high correlation on LLM Arena and Topical-Chat without retraining, while the random forest overfit to UPHELD. A fine-tuning validation experiment further confirms the dataset's utility: a Llama-3.1-70b model fine-tuned on UPHELD conversations achieves ~40% improvement in content and style scores, rivaling SOTA models like GPT-5 and DeepSeek-v3.

## Results

On UPHELD (content accuracy), the proposed Random Forest ensemble achieves a Pearson correlation of 0.63 with human judgments, outperforming the best individual metric (SentenceBERT cosine similarity at 0.58) and the best LLM-as-a-judge metric (binary yes/no at 0.40). For style accuracy, the Random Forest ensemble reaches 0.61 versus 0.41 for the best individual metric. The ensemble metrics trained solely on UPHELD also transfer to verification datasets, with the SVM ensemble achieving 0.54 on Topical-Chat and 0.49 on LLM Arena for content, while individual metrics drop to 0.32–0.45 in the same settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The ensemble metrics clearly outperform all individual baselines on UPHELD for both content and style accuracy. Compared to ROUGE (token-based), the best ROUGE variant (rouge1) achieves only 0.34 correlation on UPHELD content, roughly half the ensemble's 0.63. BERTScore (semantic) performs moderately at 0.40 for content F1, still well below the ensemble. SentenceBERT cosine similarity is the strongest single metric at 0.58 (content) and 0.41 (style), but the Random Forest ensemble still improves upon it by 5–49% depending on the task. LLM-as-a-judge metrics are the weakest individual evaluators on UPHELD, with the best variant (binary yes/no) reaching only 0.40 for content and 0.35 for style, confirming the paper's thesis that LLM judges are unreliable for human-scale dialogue. On the out-of-domain verification datasets, the SVM and Linear Regression ensembles transfer well (0.49–0.58), while Random Forest degrades sharply (0.23 on LLM Arena), suggesting that simpler ensemble models generalize better beyond UPHELD.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of dialogue dataset construction and evaluation metric design. It departs from the dominant LLM-as-a-judge paradigm (MT-bench, Chatbot Arena) by demonstrating that reference-free LLM judges are unreliable for human-scale multi-turn conversations, and from existing multi-turn dialogue datasets (MuTal, Topical-Chat, DailyDialogue) by using professional script writers and per-turn human annotations rather than crowdsourced or synthetic data. The Mixture-of-Judges ensemble repurposes standard supervised learning (linear regression, SVM, random forest) to combine traditional NLP metrics (ROUGE, BERTScore, embedding cosine similarity) with LLM-judge scores, treating evaluation metric design as a regression problem rather than relying on a single judge.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UPHELD provides a human-grounded, reference-full benchmark that fills a genuine gap: no prior dataset combines professional human-authored multi-turn dialogues with dense per-turn annotations across content, style, and reasonableness dimensions at this scale (153,578 labels). The finding that LLM-as-a-judge underperforms simple token-based metrics on conversational quality challenges the current evaluation trend and suggests the field may be over-relying on a single paradigm. Limitations include English-only coverage, task-oriented (not fully open-ended) dialogues, a lopsided reasonableness distribution that renders it nearly uninformative, and moderate (not strong) correlations even for the best ensemble metrics (~0.63). Open questions include whether the ensemble approach generalizes to truly open-ended or multilingual settings, and whether the reasonableness signal can be repurposed as an out-of-distribution detector.
