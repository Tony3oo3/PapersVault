## Problem

Extending online reinforcement learning to discrete flow matching-based graph generation models (GFMs) faces two fundamental obstacles: (1) existing GFMs estimate transition probabilities via Monte Carlo sampling, which breaks the differentiability required by policy gradient methods, and (2) de novo generation over the full graph space yields sparse reward signals, making it difficult to locate task-specific high-quality regions. The paper asks whether GFMs can be trained end-to-end with modern RL frameworks while efficiently exploring narrow, high-reward subspaces of chemical space.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a noisy graph sampled from an adaptive prior distribution (or a high-reward candidate graph during refinement) along with a task-specific reward function (e.g., docking score, QED, SA, novelty, or PMO oracle scores). It produces a fully denoised molecular graph after 50 discrete-time CTMC steps, where each step transitions every node and edge independently through a categorical distribution. During refinement, the output graph is re-noised to an intermediate time t_ε (typically 0.8) and regenerated, effectively increasing the total denoising budget without changing the model architecture. Graphs are represented as N + N² discrete variables with categorical labels for node types and edge types.

## Method

Graph-GRPO's central technical contribution is an analytical closed-form expression for the CTMC rate matrix (Proposition 3.1) that replaces DeFoG's non-differentiable Monte Carlo sampling with a fully differentiable function of the denoiser's softmax output and the prior distribution. The rate decomposes into a target-driven term V₁ (proportional to the model's predicted probability of the destination state) and a prior-correction term V₂ (governed by the ReLU of prior probability differences), both precomputable per time step. This enables standard GRPO training: K trajectories are rolled out from the same noisy graph, rewards are normalized to group-relative advantages, and the policy is updated via clipped importance-sampling ratios with a KL penalty against the base model. A refinement strategy then iteratively re-noises top-M high-reward graphs to t_ε and regenerates them, concentrating exploration in promising regions. A dynamic prior mechanism adaptively updates node-type, edge-type, and graph-size distributions via exponential moving averages over a buffer of high-reward samples, further sharpening the search toward task-relevant structural patterns.

## Results

On synthetic graph generation, Graph-GRPO achieves 95.0% and 97.5% Valid-Unique-Novelty (V.U.N.) on Planar and Tree datasets respectively, using only 50 denoising steps—outperforming graph diffusion models (DiGress, GBD, DisCo) that require 1,000 steps. On protein docking, Graph-GRPO obtains the best docking score on all five targets (e.g., −12.515 on parp1) and a 60.8% hit ratio, roughly 6× higher than the best baseline GDPO (9.8%). On the 23-task PMO benchmark, Graph-GRPO achieves a state-of-the-art AUC-top10 of 19.270 with prescreening and 18.987 in cold-start, surpassing all fragment-based and graph-based baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Graph-GRPO decisively outperforms all graph generation baselines on both synthetic datasets: it matches DeFoG's 95.0% V.U.N. on Planar while halving the ratio (1.5 vs. 3.2), and boosts Tree V.U.N. from 73.5% (DeFoG) to 97.5%, surpassing DiGress (90.0%) and GraphGen (95.0%) which use 1,000× more steps. On protein docking, Graph-GRPO dominates all RL-optimized baselines (GDPO, DDPO, FREED, REINVENT, GCPN) and basic generators (DiGress, MOOD) on both docking score and hit ratio across all five targets; for example, on parp1 it achieves −12.515 DS vs. GDPO's −10.938 and a 60.8% hit ratio vs. GDPO's 9.8%. On the PMO benchmark, Graph-GRPO (19.270 AUC-top10 with prescreening; 18.987 cold-start) outperforms InVirtuoGen (18.993/16.676), GenMol (18.362/16.213), f-RAG (16.928), Genetic GFN (16.213), MolGA (14.708), and REINVENT (14.184), with the largest margins on hard tasks like Valsartan SMARTS (0.841 vs. 0.210 for InVirtuoGen cold-start) and Thiothixene Rediscovery (0.842 vs. 0.625). The only baselines that narrowly match or exceed Graph-GRPO on individual PMO tasks are InVirtuoGen on GSK3b (0.988 vs. 0.948) and Deco Hop (0.968 vs. 0.942) with prescreening, but Graph-GRPO wins decisively in cold-start where InVirtuoGen drops to 0.952 and 0.748 respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of discrete flow matching for graph generation (DeFoG, Qin et al. 2025) and online RL alignment of generative models (GRPO, Shao et al. 2024). The key technical departure is replacing DeFoG's non-differentiable Monte Carlo rate matrix estimation with a closed-form analytical transition probability (Proposition 3.1), derived from the conditional rate matrix of Campbell et al. (2024), thereby making GFMs compatible with policy-gradient RL for the first time. The refinement strategy—iteratively re-noising and regenerating high-reward candidates—extends the de novo generation paradigm of GFMs toward localized, goal-directed exploration, drawing conceptual parallels to iterative refinement in diffusion models but adapted to the discrete CTMC setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By making GFMs differentiable end-to-end, this work opens a previously blocked pathway for aligning flow-based graph generators with arbitrary verifiable rewards, which is critical for drug discovery and materials design where task objectives are complex and multi-faceted. The refinement strategy addresses a fundamental limitation of de novo generation in sparse-reward regimes (e.g., Valsartan SMARTS), where most samples are invalid and gradient signal vanishes. Open questions include whether the analytical rate matrix generalizes to other discrete flow formulations (e.g., non-uniform priors with bounded support), how the method scales to 3D molecular or protein structures, and whether the two-stage curriculum for sparse-reward tasks is necessary or an artifact of the geometric-mean reward design.
