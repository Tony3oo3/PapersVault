## Problem

Knowledge distillation for LLMs is governed by tightly coupled design axes—divergence direction (forward vs. reverse KL), optimization strategy (loss vs. reward), and data regime (on-policy vs. off-policy)—that existing methods address in isolation. Forward KL promotes mode coverage but over-smooths predictions, while reverse KL is mode-seeking yet unstable under large student-teacher gaps, and no prior method simultaneously balances both directions while retaining the computational efficiency of one-hot supervision.

## Input / Output

HPD consumes an offline expert dataset of (state, expert-token) pairs, the teacher model's log-probabilities at each token position, and the student model's parameters. At each position it operates on only two tokens—the ground-truth expert token and a single student-sampled token drawn during prefilling—rather than the full vocabulary or full sequence rollouts, reducing the distribution-matching buffer from the TiB scale to ~278 GiB. The output is a distilled student model that can optionally serve as initialization for subsequent on-policy distillation or DPO stages.

## Method

HPD reformulates distillation as a reweighted log-likelihood objective where the weight at each token is a negative K1 estimator measuring the student-teacher probability gap. For the expert token, a positive k1 (student underestimates) triggers forward-KL reinforcement with weight p(a*|s)+k1, while a negative k1 (student overestimates) applies only k1 to suppress over-assignment, effectively masking the forward-KL component to avoid conflicting gradients. For a student-sampled non-expert token, positive k1' is zeroed out to prevent reinforcing non-expert actions, while negative k1' suppresses overestimated student tokens and—when the expert is simultaneously underestimated—doubles the expert weight to 2p(a*|s)+k1, explicitly redirecting suppressed probability mass toward the expert action. This asymmetric, token-level hybridization captures both forward-KL mode coverage and reverse-KL mode-seeking within a single objective, requiring only one teacher forward pass and one student sample per position, with no additional hyperparameters.

## Results

On off-policy math reasoning (Table 2), HPD achieves the highest average score across all four model pairs: 30.24 (Qwen2.5 7B→1.5B), 39.83 (Qwen2.5 7B→3B), 18.01 (LLaMA3 8B→1B), and 34.56 (LLaMA3 8B→3B), outperforming the best baseline (JSD) by 3.81, 3.22, 2.39, and 4.61 points respectively. HPD improves the 3B student models by 41.0% (Qwen2.5: 28.25→39.83) and 77.9% (LLaMA3: 19.43→34.56) over the untrained student. On coding (Table 4), HPD reaches 77.35 avg pass@1 for Qwen-Coder (7B→1.5B) and 66.35 for DS-Coder (6.7B→1.3B), the best among all methods. In the on-policy setting (Table 5), HPD+OPD further lifts Qwen2.5 1.5B to 33.41 avg and LLaMA3 1B to 20.40 avg, surpassing SFT+OPD by 3.85 and 3.34 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HPD consistently outperforms all seven baselines across every model family and task. Against SFT (Ouyang et al., 2022), HPD gains 7.44 points on Qwen2.5 1.5B (30.24 vs 22.80) and 15.11 on LLaMA3 3B (34.56 vs 29.45). Against SeqKD (Kim & Rush, 2016), HPD leads by 3.97 (Qwen 1.5B) to 7.73 (LLaMA 3B) points. Against RKLD/MiniLLM (Gu et al., 2023), HPD wins by 4.09 (Qwen 1.5B) to 4.15 (LLaMA 3B) points, with the gap widening on harder benchmarks like AIME. Against JSD/GKD (Agarwal et al., 2024), HPD leads by 3.81 (Qwen 1.5B) to 4.61 (LLaMA 3B) points, and is the only method that avoids entropy collapse during training. In the coding setting, HPD surpasses KD (Hinton et al., 2015) by 1.75 avg pass@1 on DS-Coder and 4.90 on Qwen-Coder. In the efficiency comparison (Table 6), HPD achieves 79.30/75.40 on HumanEval/MBPP while requiring only ~2.0–2.5× SFT wall-clock time, compared to 3.5–5.0× for AKL (Wu et al., 2025) and 2.8–3.3× for ABKD (Wang et al., 2025), with substantially lower memory overhead (278 GiB vs >1.5 TiB for AKL).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies SFT, forward KL distillation (SeqKD), and reverse KL distillation (MiniLLM) under a single reweighted log-likelihood objective, showing that each method corresponds to a specific choice of token-level weight. Building on Schulman's K1 estimator and the on-policy distillation (OPD) framework of Lu & Lab, HPD repurposes the K1 signal as a token-level reward that selectively applies forward-KL reinforcement or reverse-KL suppression depending on whether the student under- or over-estimates the expert token. The key departure from prior work is the hybrid masking mechanism that combines both KL directions at the token level without fixed coefficients, coupled with lightweight single-token student sampling during prefilling rather than full-sequence rollouts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified reweighted log-likelihood view provides a principled design space for future distillation methods, making it easier to reason about the interplay between divergence direction, optimization strategy, and data regime. Practically, HPD reduces the additional memory overhead from the TiB scale (full-vocabulary or top-k matching) to ~278 GiB while matching or exceeding SFT+OPD pipelines, making white-box KD more accessible for long-generation tasks. Limitations include the requirement that teacher and student share a tokenizer, and the method has not been tested at pre-training or mid-training scale. Open questions include whether the token-level hybridization generalizes to cross-tokenizer settings and whether the approach can be integrated into iterative self-improvement loops beyond the two-iteration study presented.
