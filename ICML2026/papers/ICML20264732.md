## Problem

Existing QA benchmarks either provide evidence documents upfront or operate over small curated corpora, failing to capture the real-world challenge where useful evidence is scattered across millions of heterogeneous documents in a data lake. No prior benchmark simultaneously scales both search intensity (the number of documents an agent must search through) and reasoning intensity (the number of dependent reasoning hops), leaving a gap in evaluating LLM agents on realistic exploratory question answering over large-scale, mixed-format data collections.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a single natural-language question and a fixed tool suite (search, list_files, download, inspect_file, execute_code, submit_answer) for interacting with a ~9.5 TB data lake of ~40 million documents spanning structured (CSV, JSON) and unstructured (TXT, PDF, HTML) formats sourced from Wikipedia and Data.gov. The agent iteratively calls tools to discover, download, and analyze documents, ultimately producing a short natural-language answer—typically a normalized numeric or temporal value covering 70.4% of tasks—evaluated by exact match against a gold answer and supplemented by LLM-as-a-judge for rare surface-form mismatches.

## Method

LAKEQA is constructed via a human-annotated subquestion composition process inspired by COMPLEXWEBQUESTION's COMPOSITION operation: annotators iteratively select documents, derive facts, and chain subquestions where each answer serves as a necessary prerequisite for instantiating the next subquestion, yielding multi-hop reasoning chains averaging 13.12 hops. The underlying data lake combines the latest English Wikipedia dump (~24 GB compressed) with Harvard LIL's data.gov archive (~17.9 TB), deduplicated to ~40 million unique files. Each task undergoes multi-stage quality control by nine annotators (five PhD students and four screened undergraduates), with at least four independent reviewers per task including at least one PhD adjudicator verifying both the reasoning chain's validity and the final question's naturalness and absence of document-identifying leakage. For evaluation, seven frontier LLMs are tested end-to-end with the tool suite, supplemented by RAG retrieval baselines (BM25 and hybrid BM25+HNSW with reciprocal rank fusion) over 10K–50K document subsets, and trajectory-level failure analysis that decomposes errors into mutually exclusive categories: search dataset missing, retrieved but not selected, retrieved but not analyzed, and wrong after analysis.

## Results

On LAKEQA-full (1,007 tasks), the best end-to-end Exact Match is achieved by Claude-sonnet-4.5 at 32.87%, followed by GPT-5.2 (18.37%) and DeepSeek-R1 (15.99%); all seven frontier LLMs remain below 35% EM. On the smaller LAKEQA-mini (135 tasks), Claude-opus-4.5 reaches 45.93% EM, while GPT-5.2 drops to 17.04% and GPT-5-mini to just 2.22%. Document-level retrieval recall (D_ret) is consistently low across models (≤54%), and accessed-set recall (D_acc) is even lower, confirming that document discovery—not reasoning over found evidence—is the primary bottleneck. Performance degrades sharply as the number of required documents per task increases from ≤8 to >18 hops.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates seven frontier LLMs (GPT-5.2, GPT-5-mini, Claude Opus/Sonnet/Haiku 4.5, DeepSeek-R1, Llama-3.3-70B) as agents equipped with a fixed tool suite (search, list, download, inspect, execute_code) over a ~40M-document, ~9.5 TB data lake. Claude-sonnet-4.5 leads on LAKEQA-full with 32.87% EM, outperforming GPT-5.2 (18.37%) by ~14.5 points and DeepSeek-R1 (15.99%) by ~17 points; on LAKEQA-mini, Claude-opus-4.5 extends the lead to 45.93% EM. Trajectory-level failure analysis (Table 6) shows that the dominant failure mode is 'Search Dataset Missing' (31/135 for GPT-5.2, 112/135 for GPT-5-mini) and 'Retrieved but Not Selected' (56/135 for GPT-5.2), indicating that agents frequently fail to surface or even open the gold documents rather than failing at the reasoning step. RAG-style retrieval baselines (BM25 and hybrid BM25+HNSW over 10K–50K document subsets) yield modest and unstable gains (e.g., Claude-sonnet-4.5: 37.04% with BM25 on 10K docs vs. 33.33% baseline), confirming that single-shot retrieval does not solve the iterative query-planning problem inherent to EQA. Performance drops steeply with reasoning intensity: average EM falls from the highest values at ≤8 documents per task to near-zero at >18 documents, demonstrating that long-horizon multi-document integration amplifies compounding retrieval and reasoning errors.

## Relation to Prior Work

LAKEQA carves new ground by formalizing Exploratory Question Answering (EQA), a task that jointly requires high search intensity (~40M documents, ~9.5 TB) and high reasoning intensity (avg. 13.12 dependent hops). Prior multi-hop QA benchmarks (HotpotQA, MuSiQue, StrategyQA) provide gold evidence or operate over small curated corpora, while open-domain retrieval benchmarks (OTT-QA, BrowseComp) scale search but keep reasoning shallow (≤2 hops). The data-management literature on data-lake document discovery (Fan et al., Chapman et al.) addresses retrieval but not multi-hop QA as a downstream task. LAKEQA is the first benchmark to scale both axes simultaneously, bridging the QA and data-management communities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LAKEQA provides a realistic, contamination-resistant testbed (built on government open data with expert annotation) that is likely to become a standard benchmark for evaluating LLM agents on practical data-analysis workflows. The finding that document discovery—not reasoning over found evidence—is the dominant failure mode reframes where agent-system effort should be directed, suggesting that better search interfaces, document-level pointers, or exploration strategies may yield larger gains than improving reasoning alone. Open questions include whether iterative query-planning can be made more reliable at 40M-document scale, how to prevent error accumulation over long reasoning chains, and whether the benchmark's reliance on a fixed tool suite limits generalizability to other data-lake architectures.
