## Problem

Distilling multiple heterogeneous Vision Foundation Models (e.g., CLIP, DINOv2, SAM) into a single student causes severe gradient conflict in dense architectures, where contradictory supervision signals lead to destructive interference and a compromised average that excels at neither semantics nor geometry. Prior approaches like SAK mitigate this with static teacher-specific adapters, but this rigid partitioning assumes hard boundaries between knowledge domains, creating parameter redundancy when representations partially overlap (e.g., both CLIP and DINOv2 encode a cat at different frequency bands). The paper asks whether a dynamic architecture can automatically share compatible knowledge and branch only where functional conflicts arise.

## Input / Output

The student (ViT-B/16 or ViT-L backbone) consumes standard RGB images and, in Stage 1, is conditioned on a Teacher ID (DINOv2, CLIP, or SAM) to distill features extracted from teacher layers 5, 11, 17, and 23. In Stage 2, the same model is conditioned on a Task ID and produces dense prediction outputs across up to five tasks simultaneously: semantic segmentation masks, human parsing masks, saliency maps, surface normal maps, and boundary maps (plus depth maps on NYUD-v2). The dual-stream MoE blocks are inserted at layers 2, 5, 8, and 11, with 15 sparse experts per block using Top-3 routing plus one internal shared expert.

## Method

PRISM replaces standard ViT FFNs with a Dual-Stream Conditioned MoE block that fuses a dense Universal Anchor (stability stream for shared consensus) and a sparse Specialized Delta (plasticity stream for conflict resolution) via a learnable gate λ. A FiLM-based router modulates input features with a context embedding (Teacher ID in Stage 1, Task ID in Stage 2) before dispatching tokens to Top-3 experts, enabling token-level, context-dependent routing that dynamically shares or separates knowledge. A Locality-Aware Decorrelation Loss is applied to shallow layers to penalize high cosine similarity between spatially distant tokens, preventing 'semantic short-circuiting' where strong high-level supervision causes shallow features to collapse into homogeneous global representations and the router loses discriminative signal. Training proceeds in two stages: Stage 1 performs emergent knowledge decomposition by randomly sampling one teacher per iteration and using its ID as routing context, while Stage 2 recombines the learned expert primitives for downstream multi-task fine-tuning. The learnable gate λ naturally converges to higher values in shallow layers (~0.7, favoring shared stability) and lower values in deeper layers (~0.5, favoring specialized experts), reflecting the hierarchical nature of knowledge conflicts.

## Results

On PASCAL-Context (ViT-B/16), PRISM achieves a new state-of-the-art average improvement (Δm) of 2.29% over single-task baselines, surpassing the previous best SAK (0.83%) on all five tasks: Semantic Segmentation 82.20 mIoU, Human Parsing 75.34 mIoU, Saliency 84.81 maxF, Normal 13.47 mErr, and Boundary 75.92 odsF. On NYUD-v2, PRISM reaches 60.22 mIoU (SemSeg) and 0.4883 RMSE (Depth), slightly trailing SAK on Normal (17.81 vs. 17.60 mErr) and Boundary (76.59 vs. 78.60 odsF). Scaling to ViT-L on PASCAL-Context, PRISM extends its lead to Δm = 3.16% versus SAK's 2.30%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PRISM outperforms all 14 baselines on PASCAL-Context. Against the closest competitor SAK (static teacher-specific adapters), PRISM gains +0.32 mIoU on SemSeg, +1.04 on Parsing, +0.02 on Saliency, −0.55 mErr on Normal, and +1.83 odsF on Boundary. Among unified foundation model distillation methods, PRISM surpasses RADIOv2.5 by +0.45 mIoU on SemSeg and +3.85 on Parsing, though RADIOv2.5 reports incomplete results (no Boundary). PRISM also clearly beats RADIO (+4.14 SemSeg mIoU), UNIC (+6.30), and Theia (+5.69). On NYUD-v2, PRISM wins on SemSeg (60.22 vs. SAK's 59.93) and Depth (0.4883 vs. 0.4942 RMSE) but trails SAK on Normal (17.81 vs. 17.60 mErr) and Boundary (76.59 vs. 78.60 odsF), suggesting SAK's dedicated adapters retain an edge for indoor geometric cues. Among MTL baselines on NYUD-v2, PRISM dominates all methods including BFCI (+9.08 SemSeg mIoU), SEM (+8.88), and ECS (+9.76).

## Relation to Prior Work

PRISM sits at the intersection of multi-teacher VFM distillation (RADIO, SAK, UNIC, Theia) and MoE-based multi-task architectures (Mod-Squad, TaskExpert), but departs from both in a specific way. Against SAK's static 'Teacher-Agnostic Stem + Teacher-Specific Adapters' design, PRISM replaces rigid architectural branching with a context-modulated FiLM router that dispatches tokens to sparse experts dynamically, arguing that visual knowledge has soft boundaries rather than the hard partitions SAK assumes. Against generic MoE multi-task methods, PRISM introduces a dual-stream design (Universal Anchor + Specialized Delta) and a locality-aware decorrelation loss specifically motivated by the gradient-conflict structure of heterogeneous VFM teachers, rather than task-level interference in standard MTL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in deploying VFMs: consolidating complementary models (CLIP, DINOv2, SAM) into one student without the negative transfer that plagues dense distillation. The emergent expert specialization—where the router spontaneously partitions experts by teacher without explicit assignment—is a clean validation that sparse, context-conditioned routing can discover soft knowledge boundaries that static architectures miss. However, the gains over SAK are modest and dataset-dependent (PRISM wins on PASCAL-Context but trails on NYUD-v2 boundary/normal tasks), and the sparse dispatch roughly doubles inference latency (47.5 ms vs. 20.3 ms for SAK-lite), limiting immediate deployment appeal. Open questions include whether the approach scales to more than three teachers, to non-dense-prediction tasks, or to settings where the teacher set is not fixed a priori.
