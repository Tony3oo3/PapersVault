## Problem

Long-horizon forecasting of irregular multivariate time series faces a fundamental tradeoff: discrete re-gridding distorts temporal structure under severe irregularity, while continuous-time models (Neural ODEs/CDEs, continuous Transformers) require sequential numerical integration that accumulates error and scales poorly with horizon length. Existing diffusion-based forecasters denoise directly in observation space without explicit dynamical structure or stability guarantees, making long-horizon generation fragile when sampling gaps are large and observations sparse.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an observed history of irregularly timestamped multivariate measurements (with per-channel missingness masks) and a set of arbitrary query timestamps—either future points for forecasting or historical points with missing entries for imputation. The history is compressed into a fixed-length conditioning token sequence via a pretrained summarizer, and the model outputs a full probabilistic trajectory (multiple samples) over all query timestamps simultaneously, evaluated in a single parallelizable pass rather than sequential rollout. Because generation conditions only on context up to the last observation time, the same model supports causal (filtering-style) imputation without bidirectional smoothing.

## Method

LLapDiff performs diffusion in a low-dimensional VAE latent space rather than observation space, and parameterizes the denoiser's mean prediction via a stable modal decomposition in the Laplace domain: K complex-conjugate pole pairs (ρ_k, ω_k) with learned residue vectors (c_k, b_k) that synthesize the trajectory as a closed-form sum of damped oscillatory basis functions evaluated at arbitrary query times. The pole parameterization is grounded in stochastic port-Hamiltonian dynamics, where the energy balance (derived via Itô's lemma) justifies enforcing strictly positive damping ρ_k > 0 for exponential stability without requiring explicit Hamiltonian learning. A renewal-averaging analysis maps continuous-time poles to effective event-domain log-poles under random sampling gaps, motivating a gap-aware history summarizer that encodes port signals, finite-difference dynamics, and timestamp/Δt irregularity into conditioning tokens. Training uses standard DDPM forward corruption on the latent trajectory with x₀-parameterization and classifier-free guidance; inference uses a deterministic DDIM sampler where all query timestamps are evaluated in one parallel pass after modal parameters are predicted, avoiding any physical-time integration.

## Results

LLapDiff achieves the best average CRPS rank (2.1 ± 1.7) across seven datasets and multiple horizons, with the largest gains on irregular datasets: CRPS of 0.318 on PhysioNet (h=12), 0.557 on NOAA UK (h=168), 0.401 on US Equity (h=60), and 0.350 on Crypto (h=100). On BMS Air (h=168), it reaches CRPS 0.516 vs. TimeGrad's 0.537 and mr-Diff's 0.555. Inference latency is nearly flat across horizons (~450 ms on NOAA), making it 1.24×–150.6× faster than NeuralCDE, mr-Diff, TimeGrad, and ContiFormer respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LLapDiff achieves the best average CRPS rank (2.1) across all seven datasets, clearly outperforming mr-Diff (rank 4.1), TimeGrad (3.8), and ContiFormer (6.6). On PhysioNet, it dominates all probabilistic baselines by a wide margin (e.g., CRPS 0.318 vs. mr-Diff 0.434 at h=12), and on NOAA UK it beats TimeGrad by ~0.08 CRPS at every horizon (0.557 vs. 0.639 at h=168). However, mTAN remains stronger on NOAA US (CRPS 0.239–0.255 vs. LLapDiff 0.432–0.440) and UCI Air at short horizons (0.834–0.910 vs. 0.935–0.936), while TimeGrad edges out LLapDiff on Crypto at shorter horizons (0.334–0.356 vs. 0.350–0.367). The paper notes that LLapDiff's advantages are most pronounced at longer horizons and on more irregular datasets, where sequential-integration baselines (NeuralCDE, ContiFormer) accumulate error and deterministic re-gridding methods (PatchTST, DLinear) distort temporal structure. Inference speed is a clear differentiator: LLapDiff runs in ~450 ms regardless of horizon, versus 17,890 ms (TimeGrad) and 67,750 ms (ContiFormer) at h=168.

## Relation to Prior Work

LLapDiff unifies three previously separate threads: (1) latent diffusion for time series (building on DDPM and TimeGrad/CSDI-style conditional denoising), (2) continuous-time dynamical modeling (Neural ODE/CDE, ContiFormer, mTAN), and (3) physics-informed stability priors (port-Hamiltonian systems, SSMs). The key departure is replacing sequential numerical integration over physical time with a closed-form Laplace-domain modal parameterization (stable complex-conjugate poles), so that horizon-wide generation is a single parallelizable evaluation rather than a step-by-step rollout. The renewal-averaging analysis, which maps continuous-time poles to effective event-domain multipliers under random sampling gaps, is a novel theoretical bridge that motivates the gap-aware history summarizer and has no direct analogue in prior diffusion or continuous-time TS literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The solver-free design yields nearly constant inference latency (~450 ms) regardless of forecast horizon, a 150× speedup over ContiFormer and 40× over TimeGrad, making long-horizon probabilistic forecasting over irregular series practically feasible at scale. The dual capability of forecasting and causal imputation from the same model (without retraining) broadens its utility in healthcare and sensor-network settings where missingness is the norm. Limitations include reliance on a pretrained VAE for the latent space, a locally linear modal approximation that may underfit strongly nonlinear transients, and the need for multiple DDIM reverse steps (64 steps for best results). Open questions include whether richer nonlinear modal bases or learned non-renewal gap models can further close the gap on the most irregular datasets, and whether the port-Hamiltonian inductive bias generalizes beyond the damped-oscillatory regime it was designed for.
