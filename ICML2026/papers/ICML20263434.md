## Problem

While Nesterov-style acceleration improves convergence rates from O(1/t) to O(1/t²) in Euclidean multi-objective optimization, no analogous acceleration existed for multi-objective optimization over probability spaces. The MWGraD algorithm, which jointly optimizes multiple objectives in Wasserstein space, lacked a continuous-time flow formulation and any accelerated variant, leaving a gap between the theoretical convergence guarantees and the practical convergence speed achievable.

## Input / Output

The method takes K ≥ 2 objective functionals defined on P₂(X) (probability distributions with finite second moment), typically KL divergences to unnormalized target distributions π_k ∝ exp(−f_k), along with an initial empirical measure of m particles sampled from a simple distribution (e.g., standard Gaussian). It produces a Pareto stationary distribution represented as m particle samples, where the Wasserstein gradients of each objective are approximated via kernel-based estimators (Gaussian-kernel SVGD or Blob methods) rather than exact computation, and the multi-objective weights w_n are solved at each iteration by minimizing the squared norm of the combined descent direction over the simplex.

## Method

The paper constructs the A-MWGraD flow by embedding the multi-objective projection term (projection of 0 onto the convex hull of first variations under the Wasserstein-2 metric) into a damped Hamiltonian system, yielding a two-equation ODE: a continuity equation for the distribution ρ_t and a momentum equation with time-dependent damping α_t. The key theoretical result is that the merit function M(ρ_t) = sup_q min_k {F_k(ρ_t) − F_k(q)} converges at O(1/t²) for geodesically convex objectives (α_t = 3/t) and at O(e^{−√β t}) for β-strongly geodesically convex objectives (α_t = 2√β), improving upon the O(1/t) rate of the unaccelerated MWGraD flow. The discrete-time particle implementation updates positions and velocities with a momentum coefficient α_n = (n−1)/(n+2), where the Wasserstein gradient of each KL objective is approximated by the SVGD or Blob kernel estimator, and the multi-objective weights are re-solved at each step. The paper further provides explicit Gaussian-family characterizations reducing the infinite-dimensional flow to finite-dimensional matrix ODEs for the covariance and momentum matrices.

## Results

On Multi-Fashion+MNIST, A-MWGraD-SVGD achieves the best ensemble accuracy of 96.4% (Task 1) and A-MWGraD-Blob achieves 90.7% (Task 2), outperforming all baselines. On Multi-MNIST, A-MWGraD-Blob leads on both tasks (95.6% and 94.2%), while on Multi-Fashion, A-MWGraD-Blob and A-MWGraD-SVGD achieve the best results on Task 1 (86.3%) and Task 2 (87.4%) respectively. In the toy multi-target sampling experiment, A-MWGraD-Blob covers the shared high-density region in ~50 iterations versus ~500 for MWGraD-Blob, and GradNorm (a proxy for Pareto stationarity) drops significantly faster across all step sizes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares A-MWGraD (SVGD and Blob variants) against two families of baselines: MOO-SVGD (Liu et al., 2021) and MWGraD (Nguyen et al., 2025a). Against MWGraD, A-MWGraD consistently wins on final accuracy across most dataset-task combinations: e.g., on Multi-Fashion+MNIST Task 1, A-MWGraD-SVGD reaches 96.4% vs. 94.7% (MWGraD-SVGD) and 94.1% (MWGraD-Blob), a 1.7–2.3 pp gain. On Multi-MNIST, A-MWGraD-Blob leads both tasks by 0.3–0.6 pp over MWGraD-Blob. Against MOO-SVGD, the gap is even larger (up to 10.8 pp on Multi-Fashion+MNIST Task 2), confirming that the Wasserstein-space formulation itself provides a substantial advantage over Euclidean multi-objective SVGD. In terms of convergence speed (Figure 2), A-MWGraD variants reach their final accuracy roughly 2–3× faster than MWGraD, and in the toy experiment A-MWGraD-Blob converges in ~50 iterations where MWGraD-Blob needs ~500. The one case where A-MWGraD does not clearly win is Multi-Fashion Task 1, where A-MWGraD-SVGD (85.1%) trails MWGraD-SVGD (85.9%), though A-MWGraD-Blob still leads at 86.3%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MWGraD algorithm (Nguyen et al., 2025a) for multi-objective distributional optimization by introducing Nesterov-style acceleration via damped Hamiltonian dynamics, a framework previously applied to single-objective problems in probability spaces by Taghvaei & Mehta (2019) and Wang & Li (2022). The multi-objective projection structure (convex hull of first variations) is carried over from MWGraD, while the momentum term and Lyapunov-based convergence analysis follow the accelerated gradient flow literature in Euclidean spaces (Su et al., 2016; Sonntag & Peitz, 2024). The paper is explicitly positioned as the first acceleration result for multi-objective optimization in probability spaces, bridging the gap between single-objective Wasserstein acceleration and multi-objective Pareto convergence theory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The O(1/t²) and exponential convergence rates for geodesically convex and strongly geodesically convex objectives, respectively, provide the first theoretical justification for momentum in multi-objective distributional optimization, with practical implications for multi-target sampling and Bayesian multi-task learning. However, two significant limitations remain: discrete-time convergence rates are unestablished, and the analysis assumes exact computation of Wasserstein gradients, which is infeasible in practice and is handled only heuristically via kernel approximations (SVGD, Blob). Open questions include whether the acceleration gap persists under stochastic gradient estimates, whether the results extend beyond KL-divergence objectives, and whether the framework generalizes to non-geodesically-convex landscapes common in deep learning.
