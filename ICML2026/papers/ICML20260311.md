## Problem

All prior work on online learning in Bayesian Stackelberg Games assumes the leader knows the payoffs of every possible follower type, an assumption that is unrealistic in applications like security games. This paper is the first to study the setting where the leader has no knowledge of follower payoffs or the type distribution, and proves that no-regret learning is impossible under action feedback (where only the follower's best response is observed), necessitating the use of type feedback.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260311/cropped_page11_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes as input the number of rounds T, a confidence parameter δ, the game structure (m leader actions, n follower actions, K follower types, the leader's utility function, and the bit-complexity L of payoffs), but crucially does NOT receive the follower's payoff functions. Under type feedback, at each round the leader observes both the follower's best-response action and the follower's type identifier, but never the underlying payoffs. The output is a sequence of leader commitments (mixed strategies over the m actions) achieving Õ(√T) regret when m is fixed, with polynomial dependence on n, K, and L.

## Method

The algorithm splits the T rounds into epochs indexed by h with a precision parameter ε_h = 2^{-h}/K that halves each epoch. Within each epoch, three sub-procedures execute sequentially: (1) Find-Types uses O(1/ε_h²) rounds to estimate the type distribution μ and identify types with probability ≥ ε_h; (2) Find-Partition adapts the polytope-learning procedure of Bacchiocchi et al. (2025) to learn the best-response regions P_θ(a) ∩ X_h for newly identified types, requiring O(1/ε_h) rounds per query since each type appears with probability at least ε_h; (3) Prune computes a lower bound on OPT and intersects each learned region with a half-space to produce a refined decision space X_{h+1} where every commitment is O(Kε_h)-suboptimal. The key insight is that progressively restricting the decision space to near-optimal commitments while halving the precision parameter yields a geometric series in the regret bound, giving Õ(√T) overall. A complementary negative result shows that under action feedback, regret must grow exponentially in the bit-complexity L of follower payoffs, via a Yao's minimax argument over exponentially many indistinguishable instances.

## Results

The paper establishes two main theoretical results. First, a strong negative result (Theorem 3.1): under action feedback, there exist BSG instances where any algorithm suffers regret Ω(2^{2L}), where L is the bit-complexity of follower payoffs — meaning no-regret is unattainable. Second, a positive result (Theorem 4.7): under type feedback, the proposed epoch-based algorithm achieves regret Õ(K² log(1/δ) √T + β log²(T)), where β = poly(n, K, L, log(1/δ)) when the number of leader actions m is fixed, yielding a Õ(√T) regret bound. The algorithm requires no prior knowledge of follower payoffs or the type distribution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental baselines. In terms of theoretical positioning, the paper's Õ(√T) regret upper bound matches the Ω(√T) lower bound established by Personnat et al. (2025), who showed this lower bound holds even when the leader knows all follower payoffs — making the paper's result tight in T. The exponential dependence on m in the regret is shown to be unavoidable by Peng et al. (2019), who proved the same exponential scaling in the simpler non-Bayesian single-type setting. The key distinction from all prior work (Balcan et al. 2015, 2025a; Personnat et al. 2025; Bacchiocchi et al. 2025) is that this paper is the first to achieve no-regret guarantees without assuming the leader knows the follower's payoff functions, which is the most realistic and challenging setting. The negative result for action feedback (Theorem 3.1) has no direct counterpart in prior literature, as previous works either assumed known payoffs or did not analyze the action-feedback model for the unknown-payoff case.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on online learning in Bayesian Stackelberg games (Balcan et al., 2015; 2025a; Personnat et al., 2025), all of which assume the leader knows the payoffs of every follower type. The core algorithmic machinery adapts the best-response region learning procedure of Bacchiocchi et al. (2025) from the single-typed non-Bayesian setting to the multi-type Bayesian setting, wrapping it in an epoch-based framework that progressively refines the leader's decision space. The key conceptual departure is removing the known-payoffs assumption entirely, which the authors argue is 'rather unreasonable in practice' (e.g., a security defender cannot be assumed to know every attacker's target preferences).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The strong negative result for action feedback—showing regret grows exponentially in the bit-complexity of payoffs, reaching 2^64 even under standard 32-bit representations—is a fundamental impossibility that redirects the field toward type-feedback models. The positive Ō(√T) regret algorithm is the first no-regret guarantee for BSGs requiring zero prior knowledge, opening the door to applications where the leader genuinely cannot enumerate follower preferences. Limitations include the requirement of type feedback (the follower's identity must be observable), the exponential dependence on the number of leader actions m (though shown unavoidable by Peng et al., 2019), and the purely theoretical nature with no empirical validation. Open questions include whether the type-feedback requirement can be relaxed with additional structural assumptions, and whether the epoch-based approach can be made more sample-efficient.
