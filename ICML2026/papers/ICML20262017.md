## Problem

Asynchronous pipeline parallelism eliminates pipeline bubbles but suffers from gradient staleness that scales linearly with pipeline depth, causing a 5.81× convergence slowdown at 32 stages. The paper identifies the root cause as basis misalignment between the Hessian eigenbasis and the standard coordinate basis, which causes Adam's coordinate-wise adaptivity to produce oscillatory trajectories that render delayed gradients adversarial to the current iterate.

## Input / Output

The method operates on individual weight matrices W ∈ R^{m×n} (MLP and attention layers only, excluding embeddings, LM head, biases, and layer norms), consuming the batch gradient G_t and maintaining first/second moment buffers. It produces the updated weight matrix and two rotation matrices U ∈ R^{m×m} and V ∈ R^{n×n} (or V = I for unilateral rotation), which are recomputed infrequently (every 10 iterations by default) via a single power-iteration step followed by QR decomposition. The Kronecker factorization of the full mn×mn rotation into m×m and n×n factors keeps the overhead tractable for large models.

## Method

Basis rotation transforms the optimization space so that Adam's coordinate-wise adaptivity operates in a frame aligned with the Hessian eigenbasis. Concretely, it estimates eigenvectors of the Kronecker-factored empirical Fisher (E[GG^⊤] and E[G^⊤G]) via power iteration, rotates the gradient and momentum into that eigenbasis (G̃ = U^⊤GV), applies standard Adam's per-coordinate scaling in the rotated space, and projects the update back (W_t = W_{t-1} − η·U(M̃/√(Ṽ+ε))V^⊤). The key insight is that in the aligned frame the update trajectory becomes smooth and non-oscillatory, so a gradient computed τ steps ago still points in a direction close to the current true update, keeping delayed information useful. A theoretical convergence bound shows the delay penalty enters multiplicatively with the Hessian (1,1)-norm (a misalignment proxy), and bilateral rotation provably minimizes this norm over all rotations. The framework unifies SOAP (2nd-moment, bilateral) and full-rank GaLore (1st-moment, unilateral) as special cases within a two-axis design space (approximation source × rotation geometry).

## Results

On a 95M-parameter Transformer with 32 pipeline stages, basis rotation (2nd/bilateral) achieves a slowdown ratio of only 1.27× (iterations at P=32 relative to P=1), compared to 4.24× for the best baseline (PipeDream-LR and Nesterov). This translates to reaching the same training loss with 71.6% fewer iterations than the best baseline at P=32. The advantage scales with model size: 76.8% fewer iterations at 1B parameters (P=24) and 81.7% fewer at 3B scale. In wall-clock terms, basis rotation reaches the same loss with 54.3% fewer GPU hours than the most competitive baseline, and a stage-aware rotation strategy yields an additional 29.2% convergence speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Basis rotation decisively outperforms all three primary asynchronous pipeline parallelism baselines. PipeDream (Narayanan et al., 2019), the vanilla baseline without delay mitigation, shows the most severe degradation as pipeline depth increases, with convergence loss worsening dramatically at P=32. PipeDream-LR (Yang et al., 2021), which schedules stage-wise learning rates based on delay level, and Nesterov (Ajanthan et al., 2025), which incorporates Nesterov momentum for delayed gradients, both achieve a 4.24× slowdown at P=32, while basis rotation (2nd/bilateral) achieves only 1.27×. Even the weakest basis rotation variant (1st/unilateral, slowdown 2.55×) substantially outperforms both baselines. Against preconditioned optimizers (SOAP, Muon, Scion) compared in Appendix I, basis rotation also maintains its advantage, confirming that the benefit stems from the basis-alignment mechanism rather than from preconditioning alone. The performance gap widens with model scale: at 1B parameters the iteration reduction grows from 62.4% (95M) to 76.8%, and reaches 81.7% at 3B scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of asynchronous pipeline parallelism (PipeDream, PipeMare) and the emerging understanding of why Adam's coordinate-wise adaptivity depends on Hessian eigenbasis alignment (Zhang et al. 2024, 2025a; Xie et al. 2025). Prior staleness-mitigation strategies—stage-wise learning rate scheduling (Yang et al. 2021), Nesterov momentum (Ajanthan et al. 2025), and weight prediction (Chen et al. 2018)—all operate on the gradient or the schedule, leaving the optimizer's geometric sensitivity untouched. The paper departs from this line by identifying basis misalignment as the root mechanism and proposing basis rotation, which unifies the Kronecker-factored empirical Fisher rotations of SOAP (Vyas et al. 2025) and GaLore (Zhao et al. 2024) into a single framework explicitly motivated by delay robustness rather than zero-delay convergence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that gradient delay scales linearly with pipeline depth—and that this delay penalty is multiplicatively amplified by Hessian misalignment—reframes asynchronous pipeline parallelism's scalability bottleneck from an unavoidable systems cost to an optimizer-geometry problem with a principled fix. The restoration of scaling-law behavior (baselines show *worsening* loss with model size, basis rotation reverses this) is a strong signal that the identified mechanism is the dominant one. Open questions include whether the Kronecker-factorization and block-diagonality assumptions hold for all layer types at extreme scale, how the method interacts with other parallelism strategies (tensor, context), and whether the eigenbasis estimation frequency can be further reduced without degrading the alignment benefit. The practical impact is significant for any deployment where pipeline depth exceeds a handful of stages, which Table 1 shows is the norm for models above ~13B on commodity hardware.
