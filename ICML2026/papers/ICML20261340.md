## Problem

MoE models require all expert parameters to remain in memory despite sparse activation, creating a severe deployment bottleneck. Existing low-rank PTQ methods for MoE either decompose each expert independently (high memory overhead) or share factors along only one dimension (1D-tiling), which still incurs non-negligible storage costs and >50% inference latency on sparse models. The paper targets the joint challenge of achieving high compression ratios and minimal inference latency simultaneously without fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-trained MoE expert weight matrices (W_k ∈ R^{o×i} for K experts) and 128 calibration sequences from C4 (2048 tokens each) to compute activation-aware scaling vectors and SVD embeddings. At inference, it takes token embeddings X ∈ R^{B×i}, routing weights, and selected expert IDs, then produces quantized residual weights plus a compact set of shared low-rank factors (U ∈ R^{(Mi)×r}, V ∈ R^{r×(No)}, Σ ∈ R^{r×r}) stored in mixed fp8/fp16 precision, yielding the final MoE output. The 2D tiling grid (M×N, with M·N ≥ K) assigns each expert a unique tile position via a greedy spatial placement that resolves clustering collisions.

## Method

TILEQ arranges K MoE experts into a 2D tiled layout by biclustering their normalized left/right SVD embeddings (via KMeans on row and column dimensions independently), then constructs a large block matrix W_big ∈ R^{Mi×No} and applies a single global low-rank decomposition to share U factors across row tiles and V factors across column tiles. A Gaussian sketch approximation replaces costly full SVD, and residuals are quantized using GPTQ/GPTVQ with LOPRO rotation. At inference, the fused LoTileMoE algorithm projects the entire batch through the shared UΣ factor in one dense GEMM, scatters routing-weighted activations into a compact 2D buffer indexed by tile column, and multiplies by the reshaped V factor—replacing B·K irregular per-expert GEMMs with two large, hardware-friendly matrix multiplications. This 2D sharing reduces low-rank storage by √K versus 1D methods and 2√K versus per-expert methods, while the structured computation pattern cuts inference latency to ~5%.

## Results

On Mixtral-8×7B at 2-bit, TILEQ_v achieves a WikiText-2 PPL of 4.78 (vs. 15.3 for GPTQ and 5.28 for GPTVQ) and 61.4 MMLU accuracy, while at 3-bit TILEQ_s reaches PPL 4.10 and 69.4 MMLU—nearly matching FP16 (PPL 3.87, MMLU 71.2). On the sparser Qwen3-30B-A3B, TILEQ_v attains PPL 10.1 and 71.3 MMLU at 2-bit, outperforming GPTQ (PPL 14.6, MMLU 53.1) by a wide margin. Across all four MoE models and both bit-widths, TILEQ consistently achieves the lowest PPL and highest downstream accuracy among all no-fine-tuning baselines, while using only 0.03–0.04 extra bits for the low-rank component (vs. 0.31–0.46 for LOPRO).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TILEQ decisively outperforms GPTQ and GPTVQ at 2-bit across all models: on Mixtral-8×7B, TILEQ_v achieves PPL 4.78 versus GPTQ's 15.3 and GPTVQ's 5.28, and on Qwen3-30B-A3B it reaches PPL 10.1 versus GPTQ's 14.6. MoEQuant (MOEQ) performs catastrophically at 2-bit (PPL 1e3 on DeepSeek-MoE-16B, 3e4 on Qwen3-30B-A3B), making it non-competitive in the extreme low-bit regime. LOPRO is the closest competitor, with TILEQ matching or marginally exceeding it at 3-bit (e.g., PPL 4.10 vs. 4.13 on Mixtral; 8.58 vs. 8.57 on Qwen3-30B) while using substantially fewer extra bits (0.03–0.04 vs. 0.08–0.46). Against the MoE-specific baselines MiLO and MXMoE (compared in Appendix D.2), TILEQ achieves comparable or better accuracy while reducing inference latency to ~5% versus MiLO's >50% on Qwen3-30B-A3B, and cutting low-rank memory overhead by up to 10× compared to per-expert methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

TileQ extends the 1D shared low-rank quantization paradigm for MoE (as in MoE-SVD and MiLO, which share a single U or V factor across all experts) to a 2D-tiling layout that simultaneously shares both left and right low-rank factors across a grid of experts, guided by biclustering of singular-vector embeddings. It composes this structural innovation with established PTQ components—GPTQ/GPTVQ Hessian optimization for the residual, LoPRO's block-wise rotation, and FLRQ's Gaussian sketch to replace costly SVD—into a unified fine-tuning-free pipeline. The key departure from prior work is the fusion inference algorithm that replaces per-expert sparse GEMMs with two batched dense matrix multiplications, a hardware-aware restructuring absent from earlier MoE low-rank methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As MoE models (Qwen3-Next-80B, DeepSeek) become the dominant scaling paradigm, their memory footprint is the primary barrier to on-premise and cost-effective cloud deployment; TileQ's 10× reduction in low-rank storage and ~5% inference latency directly address this bottleneck without requiring retraining. A notable limitation is that quantization time remains dominated by GPTQ on sparse experts (e.g., 5.5 hours for Qwen3-30B), and the method's accuracy guarantee depends on experts exhibiting clustered singular subspaces—a condition that may weaken in future architectures with more heterogeneous expert specialization. Open questions include whether the 2D-tiling structure generalizes to trillion-parameter MoE models with thousands of experts, and whether the greedy spatial placement can be replaced by a globally optimal assignment for larger grids.
