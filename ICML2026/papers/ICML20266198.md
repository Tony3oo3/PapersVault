## Problem

Most seriation methods operate on a precomputed pairwise similarity matrix, which discards the local geometric information (tangent directions, signed displacements) available in the ambient coordinates of a point cloud. This paper asks whether one can recover the latent 1D ordering of noisy samples from an unknown curve in R^d by working directly on the point coordinates, and provides the first finite-sample Kendall's tau recovery bound for such a point-cloud seriation model.

## Input / Output

The method takes n noisy points in R^d (each a true curve point plus i.i.d. sub-Gaussian noise) and a neighborhood radius r (or k-NN parameter k=50 in high dimensions) that defines the local graph. It outputs a centered 1D scalar embedding y ∈ R^n (with ⟨1,y⟩=0) and the corresponding permutation obtained by sorting y; the ordering is determined only up to global reversal since the curve's direction is not identifiable from the data. The embedding is unique up to this sign, and the method requires the neighborhood graph to be connected.

## Method

STAGE proceeds in four stages: (1) local PCA on each point's r-neighborhood yields an unoriented tangent direction v̂_i; (2) a graph-wide Z₂ spectral synchronization (principal eigenvector of the affinity matrix Q̂ with entries ⟨v̂_i, v̂_j⟩ on edges) resolves the sign ambiguity to produce globally oriented tangents ŝ_i v̂_i; (3) signed local increments Ŵ_ij = ½(ŝ_i v̂_i + ŝ_j v̂_j)ᵀ(x_j − x_i) are constructed for each edge, and a 1D embedding is obtained by solving the inhomogeneous least-squares problem min_y Σ(y_j − y_i − Ŵ_ij)² subject to centering, equivalently the Laplacian linear system Ly = η̂ with η̂_i = Σ_j A_ij Ŵ_ij; (4) the final order is argsort of y. The key distinction from similarity-matrix seriation is that the regression targets Ŵ_ij are signed projections of ambient displacements onto oriented tangents, preserving directional geometric cues that squared-distance or kernel-based objectives lose; the distinction from tangent-space manifold methods (LTSA, VDM, LPD) is the explicit sign synchronization step and the use of these signed increments as inhomogeneous regression targets rather than as entries in a homogeneous spectral problem.

## Results

On high-dimensional random Fourier curves (n=2000), STAGE achieves the highest Kendall's τ across nearly all dimension–noise combinations, reaching 95.47 at d=200, σ=0.5 and 81.18 at d=200, σ=2, compared to 77.47 for 1D t-SNE and 78.88 for Recanati at the same setting. On real single-cell RNA-seq data, STAGE attains Kendall's τ of 0.926 on Packer Seam Cells and 0.822 on Packer Hypodermis, outperforming t-SNE (0.889, 0.801) and Recanati (0.792, 0.712), though it trails t-SNE slightly on the Saelens embryo dataset (0.786 vs. 0.808). The theoretical bound shows that in the noiseless linear case, the error scales as (d/n)^{1/3} + n^{-1/2}, confirming consistency as n→∞.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STAGE consistently outperforms the Fiedler vector (spectral seriation) baseline by large margins, especially at higher noise and dimension (e.g., 81.18 vs. 66.52 at d=200, σ=2), and also beats 1D UMAP across all settings. Against the two strongest competitors—1D t-SNE and Recanati's spectral method—STAGE wins in most high-dimensional settings (d=100, 200) by 1–4 τ points, with the gap widening at higher noise (e.g., 81.18 vs. 77.47 for t-SNE at d=200, σ=2). At d=50, σ=2, Recanati narrowly edges STAGE (64.39 vs. 64.04), the only cell where STAGE does not lead. On real scRNA-seq data, STAGE clearly wins on both Packer datasets (0.926 and 0.822 vs. t-SNE's 0.889 and 0.801) but trails t-SNE and Recanati slightly on the Saelens embryo dataset (0.786 vs. 0.808 and 0.800). In the appendix, STAGE also outperforms LTSA and SABRE on high-dimensional settings, and is roughly 2× faster than t-SNE and 3–5× faster than Recanati and Fiedler at n=2000.

## Relation to Prior Work

STAGE carves out a distinct niche within the seriation literature by operating directly on noisy point-cloud coordinates rather than a precomputed similarity matrix, thereby preserving local geometric information that matrix-based methods (Atkins et al., Recanati et al., SABRE) discard. Its pipeline repurposes standard manifold-learning tools—neighborhood PCA for tangent estimation, spectral Z2 synchronization for global orientation, and graph-Laplacian least-squares for embedding—but assembles them into a seriation-specific objective with signed displacement targets that prior tangent-based methods (LTSA, VDM, LPD) do not construct. The theoretical analysis draws on Davis-Kahan perturbation, Perron-Frobenius for sign consistency, and a novel MSE-to-Kendall's-tau conversion (Proposition B.5) to deliver finite-sample guarantees for a point-cloud model that existing minimax seriation results (Flammarion et al., Issartel et al.) do not cover.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes the first finite-sample Kendall's tau recovery bound for seriation from noisy point clouds on a 1D manifold, making explicit the roles of curvature, noise, neighborhood scale, and graph connectivity, and identifies a fundamental information limit: isotropic noise prevents consistent order recovery regardless of method. Practically, STAGE is simple, fast (~2× faster than t-SNE), and produces meaningful pseudotime orderings on single-cell RNA-seq data, offering a lightweight alternative to heavier embedding methods. Open questions include minimax optimality for this point-cloud model, extension to k>1 dimensional manifolds (which requires O(k) frame synchronization rather than Z2 sign alignment), and handling branching topologies where a single total order is ill-defined.
