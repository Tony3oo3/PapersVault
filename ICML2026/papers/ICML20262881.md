## Problem

Existing feed-forward 3D reconstruction methods are fragmented: they either accept only RGB images while ignoring geometric priors (camera poses, intrinsics, depth) that are often available in practice, or they are specialized for a single output task. No unified architecture simultaneously supports flexible prior conditioning and comprehensive multi-task 3D prediction (point maps, depth, normals, cameras, 3D Gaussians) in a single feed-forward pass.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262881/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes N multi-view RGB images at dynamic resolution (100K–250K total pixels, aspect ratio 0.5–2.0) along with any subset of optional priors: SE(3) camera poses, 3×3 intrinsic matrices, and per-pixel depth maps. During training each prior is independently dropped with probability 0.5, enabling arbitrary prior combinations at inference. A single forward pass produces dense point clouds, multi-view depth maps, camera parameters, unit-length surface normals, and per-pixel 3D Gaussian attributes (position, color, opacity, scale, rotation) that support novel view rendering. Geometry tasks scale to 1024 views while 3DGS is limited to 360 views on a single H20 GPU at 518×378 resolution.

## Method

WorldMirror introduces two core components. First, Multi-modal Tokenization encodes all input modalities as a unified token sequence: poses (quaternion + normalized translation) and intrinsics (normalized focal/principal point) are each compressed into a single token via a two-layer MLP and concatenated to the image token sequence, while depth maps are patch-embedded and added element-wise to image tokens to preserve spatial alignment without doubling the token count. Second, Unified Spatial Prediction uses a VGGT-inspired transformer backbone with global-local attention and DPT decoders for dense geometry outputs, plus a separate DPT decoder for 3DGS attributes. A key design choice is decoupled sequential (curriculum) training: geometry heads are first trained from VGGT pretrained weights, normals are added next, and finally all parameters are frozen while only the 3DGS head is trained, preventing the entanglement between geometry accuracy and appearance rendering that degrades joint training. The 3DGS head predicts Gaussian positions independently (not reusing depth-head outputs), renders with ground-truth cameras to avoid error accumulation, and uses dual rendering supervision on both input and novel views plus a gradient consistency loss to suppress floating artifacts.

## Results

On point map reconstruction, WorldMirror without priors achieves 0.043 mean accuracy on 7-Scenes and 1.017 on DTU, surpassing VGGT/π³ (0.048 and 1.198) by 10.4% and 17.8% respectively; with all priors (intrinsics + depth + camera pose), accuracy drops to 0.018 on 7-Scenes and 0.735 on DTU, a 58.1% and 53.1% gain over its own no-prior baseline. For surface normal estimation, WorldMirror achieves 13.8° mean angular error on ScanNet, outperforming StableNormal (16.0°) and GeoWizard (16.7°). In novel view synthesis on DL3DV (8 views, no prior), WorldMirror reaches 17.50 PSNR vs. AnySplat's 15.62, and with intrinsics+poses priors reaches 19.08 PSNR vs. DepthSplat's 18.79.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against VGGT and π³ (which report identical numbers), WorldMirror wins on 7-Scenes (0.043 vs. 0.048 Acc) and DTU (1.017 vs. 1.198 Acc) without priors, but loses on NRGBD (0.041 vs. 0.026 Acc), suggesting VGGT/π³ have an advantage on that particular scene-level benchmark. Against FLARE, WorldMirror dominates across all three datasets without priors (e.g., 0.043 vs. 0.046 on 7-Scenes, 1.017 vs. 1.338 on DTU). For camera pose estimation, WorldMirror achieves the best AUC@30 on RealEstate10K (86.28 vs. π³'s 85.90) and the best ATE on TUM-dynamics (0.010 vs. π³'s 0.014), but π³ retains an edge on Sintel ATE (0.074 vs. 0.096), likely due to limited outdoor dynamic training data. In surface normal estimation, WorldMirror outperforms all baselines including StableNormal (13.8° vs. 16.0° mean on ScanNet) and GeoWizard (13.8° vs. 16.7°), with the largest margin on NYUv2 (15.1° vs. 18.5° for StableNormal). For novel view synthesis, WorldMirror surpasses AnySplat by ~1.9 dB PSNR in the pose-free 8-view setting (17.50 vs. 15.62) and beats DepthSplat when intrinsics+poses are provided (19.08 vs. 18.79 PSNR at 8 views), with the gap widening at 64 views (20.30 vs. 16.80). Against Pow3R and MapAnything in prior-guided comparisons, WorldMirror consistently outperforms both across most prior conditions, attributed to its multi-view-friendly token embedding and VGGT-based initialization.

## Relation to Prior Work

WorldMirror builds directly on VGGT's transformer backbone and DPT decoder architecture, initializing from its pretrained weights, while extending the DUSt3R line of feed-forward 3D reconstruction. Its key conceptual departure is unifying two previously separate research threads: flexible prior conditioning (explored by Pow3R for binocular reconstruction and MapAnything for metric reconstruction) and comprehensive multi-task geometric prediction (explored by VGGT and π³). The multi-modal tokenization design—treating poses, intrinsics, and depth as tokens alongside image patches—is a novel architectural choice that avoids modality-specific fusion modules, and the decoupled sequential training (geometry first, then 3DGS with frozen features) departs from the joint multi-task optimization common in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WorldMirror offers a practical 'one-for-all' 3D geometry model that eliminates the need for separate depth, normal, camera, and NVS pipelines, with the added flexibility of accepting whatever priors are available in a given deployment scenario. The finding that any single prior modality universally improves all output tasks (not just its corresponding one) suggests shared geometric representations create cross-task synergy that isolated models cannot exploit. Limitations include the 3DGS head's memory ceiling (360 views on a single H20 GPU), a modest gap on KITTI due to under-represented driving scenes in training, and the ~70-hour training cost on 32 GPUs. Open questions include whether the token-based prior injection generalizes to additional modalities (optical flow, semantics) and whether the decoupled training strategy scales to even more heterogeneous tasks.
