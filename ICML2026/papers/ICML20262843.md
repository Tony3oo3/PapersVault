## Problem

E(3)-equivariant neural networks produce excellent tensor-valued point predictions but are inherently deterministic, offering no confidence measure for downstream scientific decisions. The core difficulty is that the predictive covariance must itself transform equivariantly under rotations (Σ(R·X) = ρc(R)Σ(X)ρc(R)ᵀ), creating a parameterization conflict: Cholesky-type heads guarantee SPD but break equivariance in Kelvin-Mandel coordinates, while direct equivariant regression preserves the transformation law but does not guarantee positive-definiteness.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3D point clouds (ModelNet40) or atomic structures with coordinates and species (Materials Project), processed through an equivariant message-passing backbone with ℓ_max=4. The output is a symmetric rank-2 tensor mean plus a full 6×6 SPD covariance matrix over the six Kelvin-Mandel coordinates—where the √2 scaling on shear components preserves the Frobenius-norm isometry—rather than a naive 3×3 tensor-space covariance. Both mean and covariance satisfy exact O(3) equivariance (including reflections), verified to machine precision (~10⁻⁷ relative Frobenius error).

## Method

An e3nn-based equivariant encoder produces spherical tensor features up to ℓ=4, from which a covariance head assembles Clebsch-Gordan combinations over the irreducible decomposition Sym²(ρc) ≅ 2×(ℓ=0)⊕2×(ℓ=2)⊕1×(ℓ=4) into a symmetric operator A(X) ∈ sym(6). The key mechanism maps from the flat Lie algebra to the curved SPD manifold via Σ = exp(A), which simultaneously guarantees positive-definiteness and exact equivariance because exp(ρc(R)Aρc(R)ᵀ) = ρc(R)exp(A)ρc(R)ᵀ for orthogonal ρc(R). Training uses a Log-Euclidean Equivariant Scoring Objective (LE-ESO) derived from the Multivariate Laplace NLL with a log-tail robustification threshold, providing robustness to heavy-tailed residuals while maintaining a strictly proper scoring-rule interpretation at α=1. Joint end-to-end optimization of mean and covariance heads is enabled without gradient detachment by the numerical stability of the Lie-algebra parameterization combined with eigenvalue clamping and the log-tail loss, allowing the shared backbone to receive informative gradients from both prediction accuracy and uncertainty calibration.

## Results

On the Materials Project dielectric tensor dataset, the full-covariance model achieves MAE 1.55 (log-Kelvin-Mandel space), outperforming the MACE deep ensemble (MAE 1.96) and diagonal UQ baseline (MAE 2.25) while remaining close to the best deterministic predictor GoeCTP (MAE 1.41). After temperature scaling (T≈0.05), the calibrated model reduces the Multivariate Energy Score from 0.87 to 0.66 and achieves a MACE of 0.0489. On ModelNet40 inertia tensors, the method achieves MAE 0.078 with >99.9% SPD validity and equivariance errors at the 10⁻⁷ level, reducing MAE by 15% relative to the diagonal baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SecConv (Heilman et al., 2024), the proposed method achieves a substantially lower MAE of 1.55 versus 4.702, a 3.15-point improvement, though SecConv does not provide any uncertainty quantification. Compared to DTNet (Mao et al., 2024), the full-covariance model improves MAE from 1.91 to 1.55 (−0.36) while additionally delivering a calibrated 6×6 SPD covariance; DTNet is a deterministic point predictor with no confidence measure. Against GoeCTP (Hua et al., 2026), the method is slightly less accurate in point prediction (MAE 1.55 vs. 1.41, a 0.14 gap) but uniquely provides equivariant full-covariance uncertainty with strict SPD validity and near-machine-precision equivariance (10⁻⁷), which GoeCTP does not offer. Among UQ-specific baselines, the full-covariance model outperforms both the MACE 5-model ensemble (MAE 1.96, ES 0.78) and the diagonal UQ model (MAE 2.25, ES 0.87) on both accuracy and calibration, with the calibrated variant further reducing ES to 0.66.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three established lines: E(3)-equivariant neural networks for tensor prediction (MACE, e3nn, GoeCTP), SPD-constrained covariance parameterization (Cholesky, Riemannian optimization), and Log-Euclidean metrics from diffusion tensor imaging (Arsigny et al., 2006). The key departure is that prior SPD methods guarantee positive-definiteness in a fixed coordinate frame but are not equivariant under the Kelvin-Mandel covariance representation, while prior equivariant probabilistic approaches (ensembles, equivariant GPs) are restricted to scalars, vectors, or diagonal/isotropic covariance approximations. The matrix-exponential head resolves this by exploiting the fact that orthogonal conjugation commutes with the matrix exponential, yielding a parameterization that is simultaneously equivariant and SPD without ad-hoc regularization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides the first principled, backbone-agnostic mechanism for full-covariance equivariant UQ on symmetric rank-2 tensors, which is directly relevant to materials screening where anisotropic uncertainty (e.g., directional dielectric response) matters for risk-informed decision making. The modular design—where the UQ head can be attached to any equivariant backbone—lowers the barrier for practitioners to add calibrated uncertainty to existing equivariant models. However, validation is limited to a single message-passing backbone, rank-2 tensors are the primary setting (rank-4 elasticity is preliminary), and the 4.4× computational overhead relative to deterministic prediction may limit adoption in large-scale screening. Extending to higher-order tensors, other symmetry groups (e.g., point groups), and more diverse equivariant architectures remains open.
