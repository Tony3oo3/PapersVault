## Problem

Audio LLMs achieve strong performance on general speech understanding but systematically fail at paralinguistic perception when lexical content contradicts acoustic evidence, defaulting to transcript-implied answers rather than non-verbal cues. Existing benchmarks do not explicitly decouple lexical from acoustic signals, making it impossible to isolate whether models truly listen or merely read. The paper further identifies two complementary bottlenecks: paralinguistic information degrades in deeper encoder layers and at the encoder-LLM projection boundary, and even when cues remain retrievable in internal representations, the LLM frequently fails to deploy them at decision time.

## Input / Output

The system takes a speech audio clip (averaging ~7.8 seconds) paired with a text question and multiple-choice options, and outputs a single paralinguistic attribute label selected from the candidate choices. In VoxParadox, each example is constructed so that the spoken transcript explicitly asserts an incorrect paralinguistic attribute (y_adv) while the vocal delivery reliably conveys the true attribute (y_true), forcing the model to resolve the contradiction using acoustics alone. Evaluation uses two complementary metrics: GT accuracy (fraction matching y_true) and adversarial-label agreement (ALA, fraction matching y_adv), where high ALA with low GT accuracy exposes transcript-following shortcutting.

## Method

The paper introduces PCLM (Prompt-Conditioned Layer Mixer), a lightweight fusion module that taps four intermediate AF-Whisper encoder layers (5, 15, 25, 30) plus the final layer, projects each into the LLM hidden space via learnable projectors, and computes a prompt-conditioned weighted mixture using BERT-small → MLP → softmax to produce the audio conditioning tokens fed to the LLM. This is trained in two stages: (i) projector and PCLM alignment with the LLM frozen, and (ii) end-to-end LLM fine-tuning with PCLM enabled, both using a mixture of paralinguistic and general audio QA data (SpeechCraft, AudioSkills-XL, VoxCeleb2-derived MCQs) without VoxParadox. On top of this, DPO is applied with preference triples constructed from paralinguistic MCQ sources, where the preferred response is the acoustically grounded answer and the dispreferred response is a language-implied incorrect option, with β=0.1 and only LLM parameters updated. The combination improves AF3 from 17.40% to 65.20% on VoxParadox and reduces ALA from 68.50% to 22.60%, while generalizing to MMSU paralinguistic (37.74% → 54.78%) without degrading broader speech understanding.

## Results

On VoxParadox, AF3 + PCLM + DPO achieves 65.20% GT accuracy (up from 17.40% for base AF3, +47.80 pp), while Qwen2-Audio + PCLM + DPO reaches 72.30% (up from 14.85%, +57.45 pp). On the MMSU paralinguistic subset, AF3 improves from 37.74% to 54.78% and Qwen2-Audio from 34.37% to 63.26%, confirming gains transfer beyond the adversarial benchmark. Adversarial-label agreement (ALA) drops sharply—AF3 from 68.50% to 22.60% and Qwen2-Audio from 70.25% to 15.95%—indicating the models no longer default to transcript-implied answers. General MMSU (All) performance is preserved or slightly improved (AF3: 51.43%→50.62%; Qwen2-Audio: 50.82%→55.43%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

All 12 evaluated baselines (AF2, AF3, Qwen2-Audio, SALMONN, Kimi-Audio, VITA-Audio, MiMo-Audio, Step-Audio-R1, Qwen2.5-Omni, Qwen3-Omni, GPT-4o Audio, Gemini 2.5 Flash) score below 31% GT accuracy on VoxParadox, with AF2 leading at 30.85% and GPT-4o Audio at just 8.60%. The proposed AF3 + PCLM + DPO (65.20%) more than doubles the best baseline (AF2, 30.85%) and outperforms the next-best baseline (Gemini 2.5 Flash, 24.70%) by over 40 pp. Qwen2-Audio + PCLM + DPO (72.30%) similarly dominates all baselines by 41+ pp. On the MMSU paralinguistic subset, the method also wins: AF3 + PCLM + DPO (54.78%) surpasses the previous best (Step-Audio-R1, 54.51%), and Qwen2-Audio + PCLM + DPO (63.26%) exceeds all baselines including Gemini 2.5 Flash (51.05%). Notably, PCLM alone (without DPO) already yields large gains (AF3: 60.00%, Qwen2-Audio: 68.65%), and DPO adds a further 3.65–5.20 pp while drastically reducing adversarial-label agreement, confirming that both the representation-quality and decision-policy bottlenecks contribute to the original failure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the audio LLM architecture line (AF3, Qwen2-Audio) by inserting a prompt-conditioned multi-layer fusion module (PCLM) at the encoder-LLM interface, drawing on multi-layer feature fusion ideas from vision-language models (Bolya et al., 2025; Lin et al., 2025; Cao et al., 2024) and adapting them to speech with BERT-based prompt conditioning. The DPO stage repurposes the standard preference-optimization framework (Rafailov et al., 2023) to a novel audio setting where preference pairs contrast acoustically grounded answers against transcript-implied shortcuts. The VoxParadox benchmark itself is inspired by FOIL-style contradiction-by-design evaluation from vision-language (Shekhar et al., 2017) and complements existing paralinguistic benchmarks (CP-Bench, LISTEN, MULTIVOX) by explicitly decoupling lexical from acoustic evidence through controlled TTS synthesis and deterministic signal processing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The near-universal transcript-following behavior across 12 diverse Audio LLMs (including GPT-4o and Gemini 2.5) reveals a fundamental modality imbalance that standard paralinguistic benchmarks fail to expose, and the ALA metric provides a practical diagnostic for this failure mode. The two-bottleneck diagnosis (intermediate-layer representation degradation + LLM utilization gap) is actionable and suggests that future Audio LLM pretraining should preserve multi-layer acoustic access rather than projecting only the final encoder layer. Key limitations include the post-hoc nature of PCLM (ceiling effects from encoder-level degradation), the TTS-synthesized and adversarial-by-design evaluation (may not fully capture naturalistic paralinguistic reasoning), and the fact that gains on higher-level tasks (intonation, speaker identity) remain modest compared to signal-level comparisons. Open questions include whether multi-layer access and acoustic-grounding incentives can be integrated into pretraining, and whether the findings generalize to multi-speaker, noisy, or conversational settings beyond controlled MCQ.
