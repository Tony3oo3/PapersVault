## Problem

Diffusion Transformers (DiT) incur substantial inference costs due to compute-intensive linear layers across many iterative diffusion steps. While N:M semi-structured sparsity (e.g., 2:4) can nearly halve FLOPs with native hardware support, prior work has focused almost exclusively on weight sparsification, which removes critical model capacity and severely degrades generation quality. The paper identifies that DiT activations are intrinsically sparse due to token-level superposition and are far more robust to N:M sparsification than weights, but this activation-sparsity paradigm has not been systematically exploited for DiT inference acceleration.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and produces a 1024×1024 RGB image, operating within standard DiT inference pipelines (Qwen-Image, FLUX.1-dev, Z-Image). During inference, the sparsification is applied online to activation tensors in selected linear layers (QKV projections and MLP Up/Down mappings), where each group of 4 elements along the channel dimension is reduced to 2 nonzero values via Top-K selection. A lightweight LoRA residual (rank 64) is computed in parallel on dense tensor cores and accumulated on-chip, so the final output is a single image indistinguishable from the dense model's output.

## Method

RT-Lynx shifts the sparsification target from static weights to dynamic per-token activations, exploiting the observation that only ~5–10% of activation channels are substantially active per token. Three error-compensation mechanisms are combined: (1) norm-compensated sparsification rescales the sparse activation vector so its L2 norm matches the original, eliminating magnitude attenuation; (2) a rank-64 LoRA branch trained via a teacher-student distillation objective (converging in ~2k steps) recovers high-frequency visual details lost by pruning near-zero activations; (3) selective layer skipping in single-stream DiT paths avoids layers where cross-modal shared representations are most sensitive to sparse perturbation. At the system level, a fused CUDA kernel performs pattern determination, Top-K selection, and 2:4 structured compression in a single pass at the register level, then executes sparse GEMM on Sparse Tensor Cores using a streamK-style pipelined tiling strategy, while the dense LoRA branch is interleaved on-chip to avoid intermediate materialization—achieving up to 1.88× sparse GEMM speedup and 1.55× linear-layer speedup with <10% sparsification overhead.

## Results

On Qwen-Image, RT-Lynx achieves FID 21.25 and IR 1.304 on MJHQ-30K, and FID 25.78 and IR 1.226 on sDCI, surpassing the dense FP16 baseline (FID 21.98 / 31.15) on both benchmarks. The method delivers up to 1.88× sparse GEMM speedup and 1.55× average linear-layer speedup on H20 GPUs, translating to ~1.2× end-to-end inference acceleration. When combined with other optimizations (distillation, quantization, caching, sparse attention), RT-Lynx composes additively, e.g., achieving 11.86× end-to-end speedup with 8-step distillation on Z-Image.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RT-Lynx decisively outperforms all four SOTA weight-sparsification baselines. On MJHQ FID, it achieves 21.25 versus 40.81 (Wanda), 43.02 (RIA), 39.68 (BaWA), and 22.25 (Slim); on sDCI FID, 25.78 versus 55.61, 58.90, 54.54, and 29.26 respectively. Notably, RT-Lynx even surpasses the unpruned dense model on FID (21.25 vs. 21.98 on MJHQ; 25.78 vs. 31.15 on sDCI) and Image Reward (1.304 vs. 1.219; 1.226 vs. 1.172), indicating near-lossless sparsification. Slim is the closest competitor (using a much larger LoRA rank R=0.1·d), yet RT-Lynx with only R=64 still leads on FID and IR across both benchmarks. At the kernel level, RT-Lynx's fused CUDA kernel achieves 1.47×–1.88× speedup over PyTorch-GEMM, outperforming CUTLASS (0.92×–1.59×), cuSparseLt (0.71×–1.50×), and PyTorch-SpMM (0.62×–1.45×) across all tested matrix shapes, while keeping online sparsification overhead below 10% (vs. 23–59% for existing backends).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant N:M semi-structured sparsity literature (SparseGPT, Wanda, RIA, BaWA, Slim), which has focused almost exclusively on weight sparsification for LLMs, by arguing that for Diffusion Transformers the sparsity should be applied to activations rather than weights. It builds on the interpretability finding (Cunningham et al., 2023) that Transformer tokens exhibit superposition, activating only a small subset of neurons, and extends this observation to DiT image-generation models where prior activation-sparsity work (Mirzadeh et al., An et al., Haziza et al.) was limited to small token counts or LLM pretraining. The system-level contribution draws on NVIDIA Sparse Tensor Core hardware and CUTLASS-style kernel engineering, but the key conceptual move—treating online activation sparsification as the primary sparsity target and compensating the residual with a lightweight LoRA branch—is novel for the diffusion-model setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RT-Lynx provides the first demonstrated path to near-lossless 2:4 sparsification for DiT models with practical speedups (1.55× linear-layer, ~1.2× end-to-end), and its composability with quantization, distillation, caching, and sparse attention makes it a plug-and-play module for production DiT inference stacks. The conceptual insight that DiT activations are intrinsically sparse while weights are not reframes where sparsity should be applied, potentially guiding future work on video DiTs and other transformer-based generative models. Open questions include whether higher sparsity ratios (beyond 2:4) remain viable, whether the LoRA fine-tuning step (2k steps) can be eliminated or made training-free, and whether the layer-skipping heuristic for single-stream architectures generalizes to new model families.
