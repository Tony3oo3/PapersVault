## Problem

Safety alignment of LLMs is fragile: even benign fine-tuning elastically reverts behaviors toward pretraining, with degradation inversely proportional to alignment set size. While empirical augmentation strategies (refusal cues, Markov-chain formulations) restore safety, their theoretical grounding under the elasticity/compression framework is absent, and no principled method exists for selecting the minimal augmentation set that provably hardens alignment against elastic reversion.

## Input / Output

The method takes an alignment dataset D, a candidate pool of |A|=50 augmented sequences per harm category (generated via cyclic group actions on output token sequences), and a target robustness threshold. It produces a minimal subset S (k=12 per category) of augmentations selected by greedy submodular optimization, which is then used for supervised fine-tuning. Augmentation features are extracted via sentence-transformers (all-MiniLM-L6-v2) and combined through an RBF kernel with bandwidth γ=1/d; the log-det surrogate uses a fixed noise parameter σ=0.1. The final output is a safety-aligned model maintaining baseline inference latency (1.0×).

## Method

The paper formulates minimal augmentation as a submodular cover problem: selecting the smallest set S of group actions on output sequences such that robust performance R_ε(D^S) meets a target, with the robustness gain f(S) assumed monotone submodular (diminishing returns). A greedy algorithm (Algorithm 1) achieves a logarithmic-factor approximation to the optimal set size (Theorem 3.1), while a log-determinant spectral surrogate g(S)=log det(I+σ^{-2}K_{S,S}) provides a tractable online proxy with (1−1/e) guarantee under a cardinality budget (Proposition 3.2). The log-det objective is shown to equal mutual information under a Gaussian linearization (Proposition 3.3), grounding the selection in information-theoretic terms. Critically, Theorem 6.1 proves that cyclic augmentation of order n reduces the model's compression-theoretic elasticity sensitivity by a factor of 1/n, multiplying the Robustness Factor by n, thereby providing a theoretical guarantee that augmentation hardens alignment against elastic reversion. Lazy and stochastic greedy variants (Algorithms 2–3) further reduce oracle complexity to near-linear while preserving approximation guarantees.

## Results

The Submodular method achieves 0% Attack Success Rate (ASR) on Mistral-7B and Qwen2.5-7B, 4.4% on Llama-3.1-8B, and 2.2% on Phi-3-mini, outperforming all baselines across every model. Critically, it does so at 1.0× inference latency (no overhead), whereas reasoning-heavy baselines like STAIR and RATIONAL incur 2.0×–3.1× latency penalties. Under adaptive white-box attacks (AutoDAN, GCG) on Qwen2.5-7B, the Submodular-aligned model maintains 0% ASR versus 53.3% and 66.7% for the vanilla baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The Submodular method achieves the lowest ASR on all four models, beating the strongest baseline STAIR by 2.2 pp on Mistral-7B (0.0% vs 2.2%), 22.3 pp on Llama-3.1-8B (4.4% vs 26.7%), 10.7 pp on Qwen2.5-7B (0.0% vs 10.7%), and 26.7 pp on Phi-3-mini (2.2% vs 28.9%). It also outperforms the diversity-driven Greedy Farthest baseline (same 1.0× latency) by 8.9 pp on Mistral-7B, 8.9 pp on Llama-3.1-8B, 2.2 pp on Qwen2.5-7B, and 2.2 pp on Phi-3-mini, confirming that the submodular selection criterion—not merely data augmentation—drives the gains. Decoding-time CARE and training-time TARS/SRG/RATIONAL all incur 1.3×–3.1× inference overhead while still trailing the Submodular method on at least three of four models. Under adaptive attacks (AutoDAN, GCG) on Qwen2.5-7B, the Submodular model holds at 0% ASR while the vanilla baseline suffers 53.3% and 66.7% respectively, validating the theoretical 1/n sensitivity reduction from cyclic augmentation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges the elasticity framework (Ji et al., 2025), which formalizes how LLMs elastically revert toward pre-training behavior under perturbations, with classical submodular optimization (Chvatal, 1979; Krause & Guestrin, 2005; Das & Kempe, 2011) to provide the first theoretical guarantees for minimal safety augmentation. Prior empirical work (Qi et al., 2025; Kao et al., 2025) demonstrated that inserting refusal cues at strategic positions restores safety, but lacked formal justification; here, cyclic augmentation is shown to reduce compression-theoretic sensitivity by a factor of 1/n (Theorem 6.1), and the selection problem is cast as a submodular cover with logarithmic approximation guarantees. The log-det spectral surrogate connects to mutual information under Gaussian linearization (Proposition 3.3), unifying the kernel-based selection with an information-theoretic interpretation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers an architecture-agnostic, zero-inference-overhead recipe for robust safety alignment, which is practically significant for high-throughput deployment where reasoning-heavy methods (STAIR, TARS) add 2–3× latency. The 1/n sensitivity reduction via cyclic augmentation provides a clean, scalable knob: increasing the cyclic order proportionally hardens alignment against elastic reversion. Open questions include whether the submodularity assumption (Assumption 2.5) holds beyond the empirical validation on 7–8B models, whether cyclic augmentation is optimal among all group actions, and how the framework extends to multi-turn or agentic settings where the token-tree structure changes. The 0% ASR results, while striking, are demonstrated on four mid-scale models and specific benchmarks, leaving open whether the guarantees transfer to frontier-scale models with larger pre-training corpora where elasticity effects intensify.
