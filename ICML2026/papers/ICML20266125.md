## Problem

Continual learning methods that mitigate catastrophic forgetting (e.g., ER, MAS, GPM) constrain parameter updates, which inadvertently suppresses most gradient directions and impairs the model's ability to learn new tasks. The paper identifies this as 'singular value collapse'—a small set of dominant singular values followed by a long tail of near-zero values—and asks how to activate the suppressed directions while preserving the dominant forgetting-mitigation directions.

## Input / Output

PAPO operates on the per-layer gradient matrix G (4D convolutional tensors are reshaped to 2D matrices of shape (C_i·K²)×C_o before processing). At each application step, it produces a modified gradient G + λ·polar(G) that is fed into the standard parameter update. The polar factor is approximated via 2–5 iterations of Polar Express (pure matrix multiplications and additions, no SVD), and PAPO is applied periodically every s epochs rather than at every step to limit overhead.

## Method

PAPO modifies the gradient as G ← G + λ·polar(G), where polar(G) = UV^T is the polar factor from the SVD of G. This uniformly shifts every singular value σ_i to σ_i + λ while leaving all singular vectors unchanged, so the dominant forgetting-mitigation directions retain their relative dominance (stability) while previously near-zero singular values are lifted to activate suppressed update directions (plasticity). To avoid the cost of explicit SVD at every step, the polar factor is approximated using the Polar Express scheme—an iteration-dependent Newton-Schulz variant with pre-computed minimax coefficients that converges faster than fixed-coefficient alternatives and avoids the non-uniform scaling artifact of the Jordan/MUON iteration. The plug-in is applied every s epochs (typically 1–5) and requires no changes to model architecture or loss function, making it broadly composable with replay, regularization, and orthogonal-projection CL methods.

## Results

On MiniImageNet (20 tasks), PAPO yields the largest gains: ER improves from 63.40% to 72.41% ACC (+9.01%), GPM from 60.20% to 69.10% (+8.90%), TRGP from 61.78% to 70.97% (+9.19%), and MAS from 57.31% to 62.07% (+4.76%), while also improving BWT by 0.01–0.04. Averaged across all four vision benchmarks (PMNIST, CIFAR-100, 5-Datasets, MiniImageNet), PAPO adds 3.52%, 3.18%, 2.80%, and 2.91% ACC to ER, MAS, GPM, and TRGP respectively. On the SuperNI language benchmark with Llama-2-7B, PAPO improves InfLoRA by 3.37% ACC and reduces forgetting by 3.22%, and improves GainLoRA by 1.24% ACC with 1.39% less forgetting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PAPO is a plug-in applied on top of existing CL methods rather than a standalone competitor, so the primary comparison is each base method with vs. without PAPO. Across all four vision benchmarks, PAPO consistently outperforms its base method on both ACC and BWT, with the largest gains on MiniImageNet (up to +9.19% for TRGP). Against alternative gradient-modification strategies on MiniImageNet (Table 11), PAPO substantially outperforms isotropic Gaussian noise (+8.73% over ER+Gaussian), gradient rescaling (+8.82%), spectral regularization (+7.79%), SWA (+7.97%), and SAM (+7.31%) when applied to ER. For GPM, PAPO also beats SAM (69.10% vs. 67.74%) and all other alternatives. The one exception is MAS on MiniImageNet, where SAM (62.94%) slightly edges out PAPO (62.07%), though PAPO still improves BWT more (−0.02 vs. −0.08). On the SuperNI language benchmark, PAPO improves both InfLoRA and GainLoRA on ACC and reduces forgetting, confirming generality beyond vision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Polar Express iterative scheme (Amsel et al., 2025) for efficient polar-factor computation and extends the gradient-rank analysis of Lewandowski et al. (2025) from plasticity-enhancing methods to forgetting-mitigating CL algorithms. It departs from prior stability-plasticity work (auxiliary networks, null-space methods, model merging) by identifying a shared spectral pathology—singular value collapse—across replay, regularization, and projection-based methods, and by proposing a single architecture-free gradient modification (uniform singular-value shift via the polar operator) rather than task-specific architectural or loss-function changes. The theoretical analysis generalizes the spectral cliff observed in GPM to all orthogonal-projection-based methods through a rank-reduction argument.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PAPO offers a drop-in, GPU-efficient plug-in that consistently improves accuracy and reduces forgetting across vision (ER, MAS, GPM, TRGP) and language (InfLoRA, GainLoRA) CL baselines, with particularly large gains (~9%) on MiniImageNet. The identification of singular value collapse as a unifying failure mode across method families gives practitioners a diagnostic lens for understanding why their CL method plateaus. Key open questions include adaptive selection of the balance coefficient λ (currently hand-tuned per method and dataset), extension to multimodal LLMs, and whether the uniform-shift principle generalizes to non-orthogonal or non-linear gradient modifications.
