## Problem

Existing vision-tabular methods are designed for either image-only inference (MMCL, TGV) or joint image-tabular inference (TIP), leaving a gap for the full spectrum of tabular data availability from 0% to 100%. In realistic clinical settings, tabular attributes are often partially or entirely missing, and prior methods can actually degrade performance below image-only baselines when incomplete tabular data is included, because they lack mechanisms to suppress noisy or uninformative attributes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261484/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes cardiac MRI volumes (11 slices × 10 frames, 128×128, cropped around the heart center) or 2D natural images, paired with a variable-length set of tabular attributes (129 for UK Biobank, 16 for M&Ms, 10 for DVM) where any subset from 0% to 100% may be present at inference. The tabular encoder (TARTE) natively handles variable-length attribute sequences, so no padding or imputation is required for missing entries. Outputs are class labels for multilabel/multiclass classification (CAD, cardiac disease, car model) or continuous values for regression (BMI).

## Method

RoVTL operates in two stages. First, CLIP-style contrastive pretraining uses tabular missingness as a data augmentation: at each step, a random subset of attributes is dropped (rather than corrupted), preserving the factual integrity of the remaining entries while teaching the tabular encoder robust representations. Second, downstream task tuning introduces the TabMoFe loss, which samples two nested attribute subsets t⁻ ⊂ t⁺ and penalizes the model if performance with more attributes is worse than with fewer, enforcing monotonic improvement with data availability. Multimodal fusion is performed via a gated cross-attention module where image tokens query tabular tokens, and a sigmoid-gated scalar (derived from the tabular [CLS] token through an MLP) adaptively scales the cross-attention contribution, allowing the model to suppress uninformative tabular signals. Disentangled Gradient Learning (DGL) decouples unimodal encoder optimization from multimodal fusion optimization via two separate backward passes to prevent gradient interference.

## Results

On UK Biobank CAD classification (AUC↑), RoVTL achieves 84.68 (frozen) and 84.91 (trainable), surpassing the strongest image-tabular baseline TIP by 4.6% and 4.42% respectively. For BMI regression (MAE↓), RoVTL scores 1.51 (frozen) and 1.69 (trainable), and on DVM car model classification (accuracy↑) it reaches 94.10 (frozen) and 96.11 (trainable). These results are reported as the mean across all tabular data completeness levels (0%–100%), demonstrating consistent performance regardless of how many tabular attributes are available at inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RoVTL outperforms TIP (Du et al., 2024), the closest image-tabular pretraining baseline, by 4.6% AUC (frozen) and 4.42% AUC (trainable) on CAD classification, with the largest gap appearing when little to no tabular data is available. Against image-only pretraining methods MMCL and TGV, RoVTL matches or exceeds their image-only performance while additionally benefiting from tabular data when present. Among fusion baselines, RoVTL surpasses Max Fuse* by 7.07 AUC (frozen CAD) and 2.0 AUC (frozen BMI), though Max Fuse* narrowly edges out RoVTL on trainable BMI (1.59 vs. 1.69 MAE). Concat Fuse* is outperformed across all settings. The end-to-end supervised methods DAFT and Interact Fuse are substantially outperformed (e.g., 84.91 vs. 68.59 AUC on trainable CAD), confirming the advantage of the contrastive pretraining strategy. The CF* and MF* variants (trained under RoVTL's tuning pipeline) outperform their standard counterparts, validating the transferability of the proposed training procedure to other architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

RoVTL sits at the intersection of two existing lines of vision-tabular pretraining: the image-only inference approach (MMCL, TGV) that discards tabular data at inference, and the joint image-tabular inference approach (TIP) that assumes some tabular entries are always available. The paper departs from both by treating tabular missingness as a first-class training signal rather than an inference-time nuisance, using random attribute dropout as a pretraining augmentation (in contrast to MMCL's value corruption) and introducing the TabMoFe ranking loss during fine-tuning to enforce monotonic performance with respect to attribute count. The gated cross-attention fusion module and Disentangled Gradient Learning (DGL) are borrowed from the broader multimodal learning literature and adapted to the tabular setting where 'missingness' is graded rather than binary.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: biobank-scale training data (129+ tabular attributes) is rarely available in clinical deployment, yet prior methods either ignore tabular data entirely or degrade when it is partially absent. RoVTL's ability to span 0–100% tabular availability with consistent performance makes it a candidate for a vision-tabular foundation model that transfers across attribute-constrained settings (demonstrated on M&M's with only 16 attributes). Key open questions remain: the authors acknowledge that MNAR missingness patterns (common in real clinics) are untested, the UK Biobank cohort is predominantly white raising demographic bias concerns, and the method's behavior under adversarial or systematically biased missingness is unexplored.
