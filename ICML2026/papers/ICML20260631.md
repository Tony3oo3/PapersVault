## Problem

Existing OOD detection methods for complex-valued data (e.g., SAR imagery) rely on real-valued magnitude features, discarding the semantic richness of phase information, and lack a formal theoretical framework for uncertainty modeling—typically resorting to heuristic rejection thresholds rather than explicitly representing the OOD state within a principled probabilistic structure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260631/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 64×64, 3-channel images (SAR or multi-spectral satellite) as input, which are converted into the complex domain via a learnable Gabor convolutional layer. It produces a per-class reliability weight (derived from Weibull-distributed distances to class prototypes), a complex-valued feature vector augmented with an OOD dimension, and a final decision (ID class label or OOD rejection) obtained by fusing N ensemble GQBPAs through the GQECR. The OOD state is formally encoded as the quantum empty set ∅ in a Hilbert-space frame of discernment, not merely a score threshold.

## Method

TrustworthyQENN combines a supervised complex-valued contrastive learning (SCVCL) module with a generalized quantum evidence theory (GQET) inference pipeline. SCVCL enforces intra-class compactness via a joint amplitude-phase loss (JAP-Loss): the amplitude term uses cosine similarity on feature magnitudes, while the phase term operates on a 2D cos/sin projection vector that resolves the cyclic ambiguity of angular data, trained in a two-stage paradigm (contrastive pre-training of encoder+projector, then frozen-encoder classifier fine-tuning). For uncertainty modeling, a Weibull-based reliability weight modulates per-class activations and routes residual confidence into an explicit OOD dimension; the resulting augmented complex vector is mapped to a valid GQBPA via an exponential amplification factor ξ(z)=e^{|z|}/|z| that sharpens magnitudes while preserving phase. Finally, N independently initialized ensemble models produce GQBPAs that are fused through the GQECR, whose quantum interference term resolves conflicting evidence and yields a GQBPD from which the final ID/OOD decision is extracted.

## Results

TrustworthyQENN achieves a peak AUROC of 95.94% on MSTAR (OSCR 95.93%, FPR95 53.14%), 89.53% on EuroSAT (OSCR 89.50%, FPR95 57.14%), and 82.67% on FUSAR-Ship (OSCR 82.19%, FPR95 66.23%), consistently outperforming all baselines across all three metrics. On closed-set accuracy (Table 2), it also achieves the highest values: 99.48% (MSTAR), 98.37% (EuroSAT), and 96.79% (FUSAR-Ship), demonstrating that the OOD rejection capability does not come at the cost of ID classification performance. The margin over the strongest baseline (CW) is +0.93% AUROC on MSTAR, +3.58% on EuroSAT, and +2.17% on FUSAR-Ship.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TrustworthyQENN outperforms all seven baselines across every dataset and metric. Against the strongest competitor CW (credal wrapper of model averaging, ICLR 2025), it gains +0.93% AUROC on MSTAR, +3.58% on EuroSAT, and +2.17% on FUSAR-Ship, with the largest gap appearing on EuroSAT where the multi-spectral nature of the data benefits most from complex-valued phase modeling. The neural-collapse-based DEF method (AAAI 2025) is the second-strongest baseline, trailing by 5.79% AUROC on MSTAR and 3.96% on FUSAR-Ship. Among the earlier open-set recognition methods, ConOSR (AAAI 2023) and ASC-RPL (IEEE TAES 2025) are surpassed by 6.5–9.5% AUROC across datasets, while the EVT-based OpenMax (CVPR 2016) and ODL/ODL+ (IEEE TPAMI 2022) lag by over 10% AUROC on MSTAR. Notably, TrustworthyQENN also achieves the highest closed-set accuracy (99.48% on MSTAR, 98.37% on EuroSAT, 96.79% on FUSAR-Ship), confirming that its OOD rejection does not degrade ID classification, unlike some baselines that sacrifice accuracy for rejection sensitivity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: complex-valued neural networks (CVNNs) for signal processing, contrastive learning for open-set recognition (ConOSR, ODL), and generalized quantum evidence theory (GQET) for uncertainty modeling. It departs from prior OOD detection work in two key ways: (1) it replaces real-valued magnitude-only features with joint amplitude-phase representations via a novel supervised complex-valued contrastive learning (SCVCL) objective, and (2) it grounds the OOD state formally as the quantum empty set in a Hilbert space using GQBPA, rather than relying on heuristic rejection thresholds. The Weibull-based reliability weighting is adapted from OpenMax (Bendale & Boult, 2016), while the multi-view fusion mechanism replaces classical Dempster-Shafer combination with the GQECR's quantum interference-based aggregation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap in OOD detection for complex-valued data (SAR, multi-spectral) where phase information carries physical semantics that real-valued pipelines discard. The formal grounding of OOD as a quantum empty set provides a theoretically principled alternative to ad-hoc thresholding, and the GQECR fusion offers a structured way to resolve conflicting evidence across ensemble views. However, the approach is inherently tied to complex-valued inputs and ensemble-based inference, limiting immediate applicability to standard real-valued vision tasks. Open questions include whether the quantum evidential framing generalizes beyond the specific GQET formalism, whether the computational overhead of complex arithmetic plus N-model ensembles is acceptable in deployment, and whether self-adaptive thresholding can eliminate the reliance on manually tuned δ and α.
