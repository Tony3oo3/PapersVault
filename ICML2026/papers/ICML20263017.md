## Problem

Existing black-box LLM watermarking methods (backdoor-based and model-editing-based) verify ownership by modifying the final answer, creating an unavoidable zero-sum tradeoff between watermarking strength and model utility. This paper asks whether a paradigm exists that embeds ownership signals while strictly preserving reasoning fidelity, and identifies the Chain-of-Thought reasoning trace as a functionally decoupled verification space that resolves this conflict.

## Input / Output

During embedding, the provider has full white-box access to the source model and injects the watermark via parameter updates. During verification, the provider submits ordinary task prompts to the suspect API and observes only Top-k (k=40) next-token log-probabilities for a small set of pre-identified sentinel tokens—no access to weights, hidden states, gradients, training data, or full-vocabulary logits is required. The verifier outputs a calibrated Z-score and a binary ownership decision via a hypothesis test against a fitted Gaussian null distribution.

## Method

BiCoT formulates watermarking as a bi-level optimization that aligns saliency-defined structural anchors (e.g., digit tokens in math reasoning) onto a private signature subspace while enforcing orthogonality for control tokens, using a geometric loss that minimizes ‖h̄−v‖² for anchors and ⟨h̄,v⟩² for controls. The inner loop computes a virtual hidden-state perturbation δ* maximizing projection onto the signature, while the outer loop jointly updates model parameters to minimize task cross-entropy, the geometric alignment loss, and a KL consistency regularizer against the frozen base model. For verification under representation drift, Robust Subspace Registration (RSR) estimates a systematic drift vector via the median deviation of sentinel tokens, subtracts it from observed logits, and projects the calibrated signal onto the signature direction for a Z-score hypothesis test. Theoretical guarantees include drift invariance of the RSR score under affine perturbations and a uniform Type-I error bound that holds for arbitrary drift magnitude, ensuring no false accusations regardless of attack strength.

## Results

BiCoT achieves perfect detection (AUC = 1.0, pAUC = 1.0, WSR@0.1% FPR = 100%) across all four evaluated models (Qwen2.5-7B, Mistral-7B, DeepSeek-7B, Qwen2.5-14B), with Mahalanobis Distance values of 5.45–11.68 indicating strong signal separation. Fidelity loss is minimal: MMLU drops by only 0.83% and most sentence-level tasks show neutral or positive deltas (e.g., MRPC +6.13%, QQP +3.90%). Under robustness attacks (SFT, LoRA, 8-bit quantization, Gaussian noise, and targeted ANCHOR masking), BiCoT retains AUC ≥ 0.9896 and WSR@1% FPR ≥ 96.52%, with the ANCHOR attack (masking all digit tokens) causing only a 1.52% WSR drop at 0.1% FPR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BiCoT matches SEAL (Dai et al., 2025) on AUC (1.0) and WSR (100%) across all four models, but substantially outperforms it on Mahalanobis Distance (e.g., 11.68 vs. 1.89 on Qwen2.5-7B), indicating a much wider detection margin and greater robustness headroom. Against iSeal (Xiong et al., 2026), the strongest non-SEAL baseline, BiCoT wins decisively: iSeal achieves only 9.09% WSR@0.1% FPR on Mistral-7B and 57.58% on Qwen2.5-7B, while BiCoT maintains 100% in both cases. The instructional fingerprinting baselines (IF-sft and IF-emb from Xu et al., 2024) are effectively at chance level (AUC = 0.5, WSR = 0%) across all models, confirming that static knowledge-injection watermarks are undetectable under the black-box logprob protocol. LLMmap (Pasquini et al., 2025) performs poorly (AUC ≤ 0.33) and met (Gao et al., 2025) is near-random (AUC 0.47–0.87), further highlighting that BiCoT's geometry-based approach in the CoT manifold is fundamentally more detectable than output-distribution or parameter-similarity methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

BiCoT departs from both dominant black-box LLM watermarking paradigms—backdoor-based trigger injection and model-editing-based knowledge injection—by embedding ownership signals into the internal geometry of chain-of-thought reasoning traces rather than modifying final answers or relying on fixed trigger patterns. Conceptually, it repurposes bi-level optimization (common in adversarial robustness) and manifold alignment to enforce a geometric constraint: saliency-defined anchor tokens collapse onto a private signature subspace while control tokens remain orthogonal. The verification stage introduces Robust Subspace Registration (RSR), a sentinel-token-based drift calibration mechanism with formal Type-I error guarantees, which has no direct analogue in prior watermarking verifiers that typically rely on fixed correlation thresholds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a fundamental tension in LLM watermarking—the zero-sum trade-off between detection strength and reasoning fidelity—by showing that CoT representations offer a high-capacity, distributed carrier that can absorb watermark signals without degrading downstream task performance. The practical limitation is the logprob-enabled API assumption: strictly text-only or fully hidden-CoT endpoints fall outside the threat model, and the current k=40 Top-k budget exceeds what many commercial APIs expose (typically k≤20), motivating future work on low-k verification. Open questions include generalization of the anchor concept beyond mathematical reasoning (where digits dominate saliency) to domains like legal or code reasoning, and whether the geometric entanglement survives more sophisticated non-affine attacks beyond the tested quantization and merging scenarios.
