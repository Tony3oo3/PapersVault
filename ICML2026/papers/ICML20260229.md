## Problem

While diffusion models achieve state-of-the-art generative performance, the theoretical understanding of why they learn so efficiently remains limited compared to supervised learning. In particular, whether diffusion models exhibit a distributional simplicity bias—learning lower-order input statistics before higher-order correlations—was an open question, and no framework existed to characterize the sample complexity required to recover different statistical features of the data distribution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260229/cropped_page1_idx4.webp)

## Input / Output

For experiments, the method takes grayscale images (CIFAR-10 at 32×32, CelebA at 80×80) corrupted by Gaussian noise at various diffusion times as input and produces a score function estimate (equivalently, a denoised image) as output; generated samples are produced by iteratively denoising from pure Gaussian noise. For the theoretical analysis, inputs are drawn from the Mixed Cumulant Model—a distribution that is isotropically Gaussian except along two planted spike directions (a covariance spike and a cumulant spike)—and the denoiser outputs a rank-1 correction to the identity map. Clone datasets (Gaussian distributions matching the mean or mean+covariance of the real data) serve as diagnostic probes to identify which statistical order the model has learned.

## Method

The paper combines empirical demonstrations with a rigorous analysis of projected SGD dynamics for a rank-1 non-linear autoencoder denoiser (S_t^w(x) = -x - σ(w·x)w) trained via denoising score matching on the Mixed Cumulant Model. The key theoretical contribution is the diffusion information exponent k*, defined as the smallest integer k for which both the k-th Hermite coefficient of the likelihood ratio and the (k−1)-th Hermite coefficient of the effective nonlinearity F_σ are simultaneously non-zero; this scalar invariant determines the sample complexity threshold (roughly d^{k*−1} samples) for recovering a given statistical feature. The paper proves that pairwise statistics (covariance spike) are learned at quasi-linear sample complexity while higher-order statistics (fourth cumulant spike) require cubic sample complexity, establishing a rigorous separation of timescales that constitutes the distributional simplicity bias. Additionally, the paper shows that correlated latent variables between the covariance and cumulant spikes reduce the higher-order learning complexity to quasi-linear, and that the spherical projection constraint on SGD is essential—without it, an additional radial contraction term can trap the dynamics at the trivial solution w=0 for many common nonlinearities.

## Results

Empirically, a U-Net trained with denoising score matching on grayscale CIFAR-10 achieves identical test loss on real images and on Gaussian clones (matching mean + covariance) for the first ≈10³ SGD steps, only separating afterward as it begins exploiting higher-order pixel correlations. Theoretically, on the Mixed Cumulant Model (MCM), the diffusion information exponent is k* = 4 for the cumulant spike, implying that weak recovery of the fourth-order statistic requires ω(d³ log²d) samples, while the covariance spike is recovered at quasi-linear complexity Θ(d polylog(d)). When the latent variables λ and ν are positively correlated, the cumulant spike is also recovered at Θ(d polylog(d)) samples, collapsing the gap. Additionally, unconstrained SGD provably contracts to the trivial solution w = 0 for many common nonlinearities (e.g., σ* matched to the score), whereas projected (spherical) SGD enables successful learning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper without a standard method-vs-baseline comparison table; the key contrasts are internal to the analysis. Projected SGD (spherical constraint) is shown to be essential: Proposition 4.7 proves that for the matched denoiser σ* and several common activations (tanh, sigmoid, ReLU²), unconstrained SGD exhibits contraction dynamics with both the overlap α_τ and the norm ‖w_τ‖ decaying geometrically to zero, while projected SGD reaches weak recovery. The sample complexity for the cumulant spike drops from cubic (d³) to quasi-linear (d polylog(d)) when the latent variables λ and ν are positively correlated, mirroring the 'sliding down the stairs' effect of Bardone & Goldt (2024) in supervised classification. Overparametrization (wide two-layer autoencoders trained with Adam) is shown empirically to rescue learning for nonlinearities where Λ < 0 (e.g., ReLU, ReLU²), whereas a narrow (m=1) network remains stuck at zero. Deeper ReLU² networks lose the ability to recover the spike due to exploding activations, while deeper tanh/sigmoid networks retain it, suggesting depth can effectively reparameterize the nonlinearity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the distributional simplicity bias framework—established for supervised classification (Ingrosso & Goldt, 2022; Refinetti et al., 2023) and transformers (Rende et al., 2024)—to denoising diffusion models, and introduces the 'diffusion information exponent' as a new scalar invariant governing sample complexity, analogous to the information exponent in single-index models (Ben Arous et al., 2021) and the generative exponent (Damian et al., 2024). It departs from prior diffusion dynamics analyses (Li et al., 2024; Cui et al., 2025; Wang & Pehlevan, 2025) by handling non-Gaussian, non-isotropic data with a nonlinear denoiser, going beyond the linear sample complexity regime. The mixed cumulant model and correlated-latent-variable analysis build directly on Bardone & Goldt (2024), while the Hermite-decomposition technique for the diffusion loss follows Shah et al. (2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first rigorous theoretical account of why diffusion models learn statistics in order of increasing complexity, providing a computable quantity (the diffusion information exponent) that predicts sample complexity for recovering specific data features. The finding that the teacher-student (matched-architecture) setting is suboptimal for the MSE diffusion loss is a novel negative result with implications for architecture design, and the demonstration that the spherical constraint is not a minor technicality but essential for escaping the trivial solution has practical relevance for training protocols. Key limitations include the single-neuron denoiser, the idealized two-spike data model, and the gap between the predicted cubic sample complexity and the rapid separation from Gaussian clones observed empirically; open questions include how overparametrization and depth modify the information exponent, and whether smoothing techniques can close the gap to the information-theoretic lower bound.
