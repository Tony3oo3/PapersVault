## Problem

Classical linear bandit theory (OFUL, RLS-based confidence sets) relies fundamentally on an inner product structure in a Hilbert space, yet the name 'linear bandits' suggests linearity alone should suffice. The paper identifies a concrete gap: the Kantorovich optimal transport problem is a linear functional on probability measures where the bilinear form is a duality pairing between functions and measures, not an inner product, so neither Riesz representation nor Cauchy-Schwarz applies and the standard confidence-set construction breaks down. The paper asks whether sub-linear regret is still achievable in this non-Hilbertian setting.

## Input / Output

The agent knows the marginal measures (μ, ν) and hence the action space Π(μ,ν) of admissible transport plans a priori, but does not know the true cost function c*. At each round it selects a plan π_t ∈ Π(μ,ν) and receives a single scalar noisy observation C_t = ∫c* dπ_t + ξ_t. The unusual constraint is that actions are probability measures (not vectors in a common space with the parameter), so the algorithm must ensure every played plan has an L² density with respect to a reference measure ϱ to keep the frequency-domain representation valid; the entropic regularisation enforces this by construction, restricting the algorithm to a dense subspace Π_H(μ,ν) of couplings.

## Method

The algorithm (EntUCB) has three key ingredients. First, it applies the Fourier transform to both the cost function and the transport plan, converting the duality pairing ⟨c|π⟩ into a genuine L²(ℝ^d;ϱ) inner product ⟨Fc*(−·)|Fπ⟩, which restricts the action space to plans with square-integrable densities. Second, in this frequency domain it constructs a regularised least-squares estimator of Fc* and confidence sets following the Abbasi-Yadkori (2012) framework, valid uniformly over time with high probability. Third, to prevent the algorithm from selecting plans that violate the L²-density requirement (which would invalidate future confidence sets), the optimism step is regularised with an entropic penalty ε_t·H(π|ϱ), whose solutions provably have bounded densities; the resulting approximation error is controlled via the Γ-convergence rate of entropic OT to Kantorovich OT (Carlier et al., 2023), yielding a lower-order regret term. For worst-case bounds, a basis-truncation variant (Algorithm 2) with Kolmogorov-type regularity on the Fourier coefficients of c* gives regret interpolating between Õ(√T) (parametric) and Õ(T^{(q+4)/(2q+4)}) (non-parametric with Fourier decay t^{−q}).

## Results

The paper establishes that the EntUCB algorithm achieves a trajectorial regret bound (Theorem 4.1) of the same form as classical OFUL: R_T ≤ 2C̄β_T(δ)√(T log det(Id + M_T(DΛ)^{-1}M_T^*/(2λC̄))) + (κη/(1−η))(T^{1−η}log T + ...) + σ√(2T log(2/δ)), with probability ≥ 1−δ. For worst-case bounds (Theorem 5.3), the regret interpolates between Õ(√T) (as q→∞) and Õ(T) (as q↓0), yielding the non-parametric rate Õ(T^{(q+4)/(2q+4)}) where q governs the Fourier coefficient decay. In the parametric case with N basis terms (Proposition 5.5), the tight rate Õ(N√T) is recovered, matching finite-dimensional linear bandits.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental baselines or datasets. The key comparison is against the classical OFUL algorithm of Abbasi-Yadkori et al. (2011): the trajectorial regret bound of Theorem 4.1 exactly matches the Hilbert-space OFUL bound, demonstrating that the inner product structure is not necessary for the same type of guarantees. In the finite-dimensional (parametric) setting, the paper recovers the standard Õ(d√T) rate, confirming no loss from the non-Hilbertian formulation. The novel contribution is the non-parametric worst-case rate Õ(T^{(q+4)/(2q+4)}), which has no direct analogue in the existing linear bandits literature since prior work required a Hilbert space structure. The entropic regularisation approximation error (controlled via Carlier et al., 2023) is shown to be a lower-order term (O(T^{1−η} log T)) relative to the main estimation term when η ∈ (1/2, 1), so the regularisation does not degrade the leading-order rate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the OFUL/linear bandit framework of Abbasi-Yadkori (2012) and Abbasi-Yadkori et al. (2011), which fundamentally relies on a Hilbert-space inner product structure for confidence set construction via Cauchy-Schwarz, to the setting where the objective is bilinear but not an inner product. The key technical departure is a Fourier-domain embedding that recovers a Hilbertian structure on a restricted action subspace, combined with entropic regularization (drawing on Carlier et al., 2023, for Γ-convergence rates) to keep actions within that subspace. The non-parametric regret analysis further builds on Kolmogorov-width-style approximation theory and functional regression techniques, connecting bandit learning to classical approximation theory in a way that parallels but generalizes kernelized bandit results (Chowdhury & Gopalan, 2017; Valko et al., 2013).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a genuine conceptual gap: the name 'linear bandits' suggested linearity was the operative property, but the entire literature implicitly required an inner product. Showing that OFUL-type trajectorial bounds survive without one (via Fourier embedding + entropic barrier) opens the door to a broader theory of bilinear bandits. However, significant open questions remain: whether linearity alone is sufficient (or whether the Γ-convergence condition on a barrier regularisation is necessary), whether the approach extends beyond the Kantorovich problem to other measure-valued bilinear functionals, and the computational tractability of the optimism step (Sinkhorn does not output a valid plan in finite time). The absence of any empirical validation limits immediate practical impact.
