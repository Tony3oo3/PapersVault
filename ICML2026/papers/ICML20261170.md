## Problem

Existing time series foundation models (TSFMs) achieve zero-shot performance only on forecasting, requiring task-specific fine-tuning for imputation, anomaly detection, and classification. This limitation stems from two fundamental dilemmas: (1) patch-wise tokenization sacrifices point-level fidelity needed for reconstruction tasks, while point-wise tokenization is computationally prohibitive on long sequences; and (2) a single monolithic training objective (BERT-style or GPT-style) cannot simultaneously capture the divergent inductive biases required by different tasks (extrapolation, interpolation, global abstraction).

## Input / Output

ZEUS accepts univariate time series of variable length (up to 4096 steps), with multivariate inputs handled via a channel-independent strategy. Each time step is mapped to a token through a gated embedding layer after instance normalization, and [MASK]/[PAD] tokens are inserted to uniformly formulate all downstream tasks as masked reconstruction. The model outputs a quantile reconstruction tensor with 9 quantile levels per time step, from which point forecasts (quantile mean), probabilistic forecasts (full quantile set), imputed values, anomaly scores (reconstruction error), and class labels (1-NN on globally pooled representations) are derived without any task-specific head training.

## Method

ZEUS is a ~100M-parameter encoder-only Transformer with a U-shaped multi-scale hierarchy (scales [1,8,32,8,1], 12 layers) that performs fine-to-coarse-to-fine information flow: lightweight blocks at fine scales preserve local point-level details, while deeper/wider blocks at coarse scales efficiently capture long-range dependencies, yielding a 3.8× reduction in self-attention FLOPs over a vanilla Transformer. The key training innovation is Multi-Objective Temporal Masking (MOTM), which jointly optimizes extrapolation (predictive suffix masking), interpolation (random point and multi-block masking with uniform block lengths), and global abstraction (single long-block masking) within a single quantile-loss reconstruction objective, with the overall masking ratio sampled from U(0, 0.5). The model is pretrained on ~300B observations from Chronos, GiftEvalPretrain, and the newly introduced Aegis-Syn synthetic dataset (extending KernelSynth with non-smooth and discontinuous patterns), using FlashAttention v2, rotary positional embeddings, and gated feed-forward networks.

## Results

On the long-term point forecasting benchmark (averaged over prediction lengths {96, 192, 336, 720}), ZEUS achieves the best MSE on 5 out of 6 datasets (e.g., 0.157 on ECL, 0.249 on ETTm2) and wins 19/24 MSE comparisons overall. On GIFT-Eval probabilistic forecasting (97 tasks), ZEUS attains the best MASE (0.693) and CRPS (0.480) among all pretrained models. For imputation, ZEUS reduces averaged MSE by 24.4% (random masking) and 18.8% (block masking) over the strongest task-specific baselines, while on the 42-dataset UCR anomaly benchmark it achieves a 21.0% adjusted-F1 improvement over the second-best TSFM (UniTS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ZEUS decisively outperforms all prior TSFMs in zero-shot point forecasting: compared to Timer (the strongest prior TSFM), it reduces MSE by 40.3% and MAE by 25.3% on average, and it surpasses MOMENT by an even wider margin (e.g., 0.377 vs. 0.715 MSE on ETTh1). Against task-specific supervised models (ModernTCN, TimesNet, PatchTST), ZEUS matches or exceeds them in zero-shot on 5 of 6 datasets, closing the traditional gap between foundation models and task-specific training. On GIFT-Eval probabilistic forecasting, ZEUS ranks first on both MASE (0.693 vs. 0.698 for Chronos-2) and CRPS (0.480 vs. 0.485), edging out all pretrained forecasting models. For imputation, patch-based TSFMs (MOMENT, Timer, UniTS) degrade severely under point-level missingness (e.g., MOMENT drops >20% from patch to point masking), while ZEUS achieves the best MSE across all six datasets under both random and block masking. On anomaly detection (42 UCR datasets), ZEUS achieves the highest average adjusted F1, surpassing even full-shot task-specific models and improving 21.0% over UniTS. On UEA classification (26 datasets), ZEUS with linear probing attains the highest averaged accuracy among all methods, and with 1-NN it outperforms MOMENT by 7.0 percentage points while matching TimesNet and MOMENT-LP.

## Relation to Prior Work

ZEUS departs from the dominant patch-wise tokenization paradigm in prior TSFMs (MOMENT, Timer, UniTS, TimesBERT) by adopting point-wise tokenization combined with a U-shaped multi-scale hierarchy, a design borrowed from task-specific multi-scale Transformers (Crossformer, DsFormer, HutFormer) but applied for the first time in a foundation-model setting. On the training side, it replaces the single-objective BERT-style or GPT-style pretraining used by prior TSFMs with MOTM, a multi-objective temporal masking strategy that jointly targets extrapolation, interpolation, and global abstraction. The quantile-based probabilistic head and channel-independent formulation connect to the Chronos/TimesFM and PatchTST lineages, respectively, while the 300B-observation pretraining corpus extends the Chronos and GiftEvalPretrain datasets with the newly constructed Aegis-Syn synthetic data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ZEUS is the first TSFM to report competitive tuning-free performance across five heterogeneous tasks (point/probabilistic forecasting, imputation, anomaly detection, classification), addressing a gap where prior TSFMs required task-specific fine-tuning for anything beyond forecasting. The 3.8× self-attention FLOP reduction and 2.1× speedup over Time-MoE make point-wise tokenization practically viable for long sequences, removing a key scalability objection. Limitations include the univariate (channel-independent) formulation, which precludes native multivariate modeling, and the relatively modest 100M parameter scale; whether the multi-scale design and MOTM strategy scale gracefully to billion-parameter models remains an open question. Future work on multivariate integration and broader task coverage (e.g., change-point detection, causal inference) would further validate the general-purpose framing.
