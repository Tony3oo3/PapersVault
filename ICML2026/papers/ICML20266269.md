## Problem

Diffusion Bridge and Flow Matching are both widely used for distribution-to-distribution transformation, yet no unified theoretical framework exists to rigorously compare their relative merits. The substantial discrepancies in their modeling assumptions (SDE vs. ODE, drift term presence) and practical implementations have left the fundamental question—'which is better?'—open, with no principled account of when each approach excels or fails.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266269/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Both models operate on 256×256 RGB images encoded into a latent space (h/8 × w/8 × 4) via a pre-trained Stable Diffusion VAE with 8× downsampling. The DB network receives the noisy latent state z_t, the target latent condition z_T, and the time step t as input, while the standard FM network only receives z_t and t; the paper also tests a modified FM variant v_θ(z_t, z_T, t) to isolate the effect of input conditioning from the underlying modeling difference. Outputs are decoded back to pixel space as restored or translated images at the original resolution.

## Method

The paper unifies both models under a Stochastic Optimal Control (SOC) framework, proving that FM is a special case of DB (when drift θ_t→0 and diffusion g_t=1) and that DB's cost functional is strictly lower due to the drift term θ_t(x_1 − x_t^u), which guides trajectories toward more stable paths. From an Optimal Transport perspective, it shows that FM's fixed linear interpolation coefficients t and 1-t violate the Brenier absolute-continuity assumption for finite empirical measures, causing performance to degrade sharply under limited training data. To enable fair empirical comparison, the authors propose a novel Latent Transformer architecture for DB built on the DiT backbone (485M parameters, 24 layers, 16 heads), matching the FM model's structure exactly. Experiments across inpainting, deblurring, super-resolution, and image translation tasks—systematically varying mask size (50×50 to 128×128) and training data size (500 to 27000)—confirm that DB achieves superior perceptual quality (LPIPS, FID) especially under large distributional discrepancies or scarce data, while FM converges faster and achieves better pixel-level metrics (PSNR, SSIM) due to its noise-free ODE sampling.

## Results

Across six image-to-image tasks (Inpainting, Deblurring, 4× Super-Resolution, Edges→Handbags, Edges→Shoes, Nights→Days), Diffusion Bridge (DB) consistently achieves substantially better perceptual quality than Flow Matching (FM) under the same Latent Transformer architecture. For example, on Inpainting (128×128 box mask), DB reaches FID 7.71 vs. FM's 17.84 and LPIPS 0.078 vs. 0.106; on Deblurring, DB achieves FID 19.03 vs. FM's 38.18. FM retains an edge on pixel-level metrics (PSNR, SSIM) in restoration tasks due to its noise-free ODE sampling, but the gap narrows or reverses on translation tasks where DB's FID advantage is dramatic (e.g., Nights→Days: FID 38.28 vs. 121.53).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is DB vs. FM under an identical 485M-parameter Latent Transformer (DiT) backbone. DB wins decisively on FID across all six tasks (e.g., Inpainting FID 7.71 vs. 17.84; Deblurring FID 19.03 vs. 38.18; Nights→Days FID 38.28 vs. 121.53), while FM retains modest advantages on pixel-level metrics (PSNR/SSIM) in restoration tasks due to its deterministic ODE sampling. Against the U-Net-based diffusion bridge baselines GOUB and UniDB (Table 11, 4× Super-Resolution), the proposed Latent Transformer DB achieves FID 8.50 vs. GOUB's 19.02 and UniDB's 17.59, confirming that the architectural upgrade (U-Net → DiT) yields a large additional gain beyond the modeling difference. The paper also shows that even when FM is given the same network input (z_t, z_T, t) as DB, its FID remains inferior (14.66 vs. 7.71 on Box128 Inpainting), indicating the gap stems from the underlying SDE drift term rather than input conditioning. Under limited training data (500–1000 samples), FM's FID degrades far more steeply than DB's, consistent with the theoretical prediction that FM's fixed t/(1−t) interpolation loses validity when empirical measures violate absolute continuity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies Diffusion Bridge (DB) and Flow Matching (FM) under a common Stochastic Optimal Control (SOC) framework, building directly on UniDB's SOC formulation of diffusion bridges and Lipman et al.'s conditional flow matching objective. It departs from prior work by proving FM is a strict special case of DB (when the drift coefficient vanishes and diffusion coefficient is unit), and by recasting FM's linear interpolation as an oversimplification of McCann's OT interpolant that loses validity under finite-sample regimes. Architecturally, it extends the DiT latent Transformer backbone—previously used only for FM—to DB for the first time, enabling a controlled same-architecture comparison that prior U-Net-based DB work (DDBMs, GOUB, UniDB) could not support.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides practitioners with a clear theoretical and empirical decision framework: DB's drift term yields lower SOC cost and more stable trajectories, making it preferable for large distributional discrepancies and limited-data regimes, while FM's simpler ODE structure offers faster convergence and better pixel-level metrics (PSNR/SSIM) when data is abundant and the task is easy. Key limitations include evaluation restricted to 256×256 image-to-image tasks (no text-to-image, no high-resolution generation), and the theoretical cost inequality relies on the g_t=1 assumption for direct comparability. Open questions include whether the DB advantage persists at higher resolutions, in autoregressive or video settings, and whether the slower convergence of DB can be mitigated without sacrificing its trajectory stability.
