## Problem

Despite widespread adoption of inference-time strategies like best-of-k sampling and reward-based re-ranking in LLMs, the principles governing how to optimally configure the number of samples k, the selection temperature, and the reward model remain poorly understood. No simple, analytically solvable model exists that yields closed-form prescriptions for generalization error as a function of these hyperparameters or for allocating a fixed compute budget between training and inference.

## Input / Output

The model takes a d-dimensional input vector x drawn from a Gaussian distribution, generates k candidate outputs from a Bayesian posterior predictive (a Gaussian whose mean and variance are determined by n training samples and a ridge-regularized prior), scores each candidate with a quadratic reward r(y,x) = -(y - w_R·x/√d)², and selects one via softmax at temperature T. The analytical outputs are closed-form expressions for the generalization error δ in the proportional limit (d,n→∞ with α=d/n fixed), including optimal values of k, T, and the reward weight w_R as functions of the teacher weight w_T, noise level σ, and prior scale γ. The reward weight w_R is deliberately allowed to differ from the teacher w_T to model imperfect LLM-as-a-judge scenarios.

## Method

The paper constructs a Bayesian linear regression model where the ground truth is generated by a teacher weight w_T and the reward is a quadratic function parameterized by a potentially misaligned reward weight w_R, with k inference-time samples selected via softmax at temperature T. Using the method of deterministic equivalents from random matrix theory, the authors derive closed-form expressions for the posterior predictive mean m(x) and variance s²(x) in the high-dimensional proportional limit, reducing the generalization error to a finite-dimensional expectation over k i.i.d. Gaussian draws. In the high-temperature regime (T ≫ s²), a cumulant expansion of the partition function yields a perturbative series in 1/t that explicitly reveals how reward-teacher misalignment (Δ_R) controls non-monotonicity in k and the existence of an optimal temperature. In the low-temperature (T→0) best-of-k limit, extreme value theory (Weibull distribution for the minimum of chi-squared variables) proves that δ scales as Θ(k⁻²) when w_R = w_T, with the leading constant determined in closed form. The framework further quantifies the regime where inference-time compute scaling (∂log δ/∂log k = -2) dominates over training-data scaling, and shows this advantage degrades as task difficulty (noise σ) increases.

## Results

In the best-of-k limit (T→0) with an ideal reward model (w_R = w_T), the generalization error decays as δ = πσ²/k² · 1/√(1 − (2/σ²d)·uᵀΣu), yielding a Θ(1/k²) power-law scaling (Result 3, Remark 5). When the reward is misspecified, the paper proves the existence of a finite optimal k ≈ 2t*/(t*−t) (Remark 3) and an optimal temperature t(x) = 2(1−2/k)·C₂/C₁ (Remark 4), both confirmed qualitatively on GSM8K with Llama-3-8B-Instruct and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B. In the regime where R ≪ σ² and the prior is broad, |∂log δ/∂log k| = 2 greatly exceeds |∂log δ/∂log n|, showing that inference-time compute is more effective than additional training data (Remark 6). Empirically, using Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-7B as a near-ideal judge, the error vs. k follows a power-law consistent with the theoretical prediction (Figure 9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper that does not benchmark against competing inference-time scaling methods in the standard sense. Instead, it validates its analytical predictions against empirical observations from prior LLM work: the non-monotonicity of error in k (matching Snell et al., 2025 and Chen et al., 2024a), the existence of an optimal sampling temperature (a new observation not previously reported for the reward-temperature, distinct from the model's generation temperature studied by Du et al., 2025), and the power-law decay in best-of-k (complementing the pass@k scaling laws of Brown et al., 2024 and Schaeffer et al., 2025a, which measure correctness rather than generalization error under an imperfect judge). The paper's key differentiator is providing closed-form, analytically tractable expressions for the generalization error as a function of k, T, n, d, and reward quality, which no prior empirical scaling-law paper achieves. The empirical validation on GSM8K is explicitly framed as qualitative confirmation rather than a head-to-head comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the method of deterministic equivalence from random matrix theory (Voiculescu et al., 1992) to obtain closed-form expressions for the posterior predictive in high-dimensional Bayesian linear regression, and on extreme value theory (de Haan & Ferreira, 2007) to analyze the best-of-k limit. It positions itself against the growing empirical literature on inference-time scaling (Snell et al., 2025; Brown et al., 2024; Schaeffer et al., 2025) and the few existing theoretical treatments (Chen et al., 2024a; Huang et al., 2025c; Di et al., 2025), which either focus on pass@k correctness or majority voting. The key departure is providing a single analytically tractable model—Bayesian regression with a quadratic reward and softmax-weighted selection—that simultaneously captures the interplay of sample count k, reward temperature T, and reward quality, yielding explicit formulas for generalization error and its optima.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first closed-form characterization of when inference-time compute is provably preferable to additional training data, and identifies a counterintuitive result that the optimal reward model need not coincide with the ground-truth teacher. It also establishes the existence of an optimal temperature for the reward sampling process (distinct from the model's generation temperature), a phenomenon not previously identified in the LLM literature. However, the validation on LLMs (Llama-3-8B-Instruct, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B on GSM8K) is explicitly qualitative, and the linear-Gaussian model is a significant simplification of real LLM behavior. Open questions include extending the framework to non-i.i.d. decoding (beam search), multimodal or heavy-tailed predictive distributions, and quantifying the training-vs-inference compute trade-off in realistic LLM settings.
