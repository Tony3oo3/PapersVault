## Problem

RAG systems deployed in sensitive domains (legal, financial, healthcare) rely on opaque similarity-based re-ranking with arbitrary top-k cutoffs that offer no explanation for evidence selection and provide no mechanism to detect adversarial corpus poisoning. Existing approaches treat interpretability and robustness as separate concerns, leaving a fundamental gap: without understanding why evidence was selected, organizations cannot audit decisions or detect when the selection process has been compromised.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263963/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a text query and a set of retrieved evidence chunks (512-token chunks in main experiments), METEORA produces an adaptively sized evidence subset whose cardinality is determined by statistical elbow detection rather than a fixed k, along with explicit retrieval rationales (XML-tagged semantic search strategies) generated by a DPO-tuned LLM conditioned only on the query. The Verifier LLM outputs structured JSON decisions per chunk including a binary flag, a chunk summary, and violation type (instruction, contradiction, or factual), and the final pipeline yields a generated answer grounded only in verified evidence.

## Method

METEORA replaces re-ranking with a three-component rationale-driven selection pipeline. First, a DPO-tuned LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct generates query-aligned rationales using preference pairs automatically constructed from QA annotations (rationales leading to correct evidence selection are preferred over those selecting incorrect evidence), eliminating manual annotation. Second, the Evidence Chunk Selection Engine (ECSE) pairs each rationale with its most semantically similar evidence chunk via cosine similarity, then computes a pooled rationale embedding and applies z-score-normalized first-order difference elbow detection to determine an adaptive cutoff k*, with a second-order curvature fallback when no significant drop is detected. Third, a Verifier LLM (same LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct) uses the same rationales as flagging instructions to conservatively filter poisoned evidence (>90% confidence threshold), flagging instruction violations, contradictions, and factual errors. The key insight is that the rationales serving as interpretability artifacts simultaneously enable adversarial detection, making transparency and security synergistic rather than competing objectives.

## Results

On the Context Prioritization (CP) task across six datasets, METEORA achieves an average recall of 0.93 and precision of 0.19, outperforming the best traditional re-ranker (Cross-Encoder BGE-Reranker-Large: R=0.82, P=0.18) by 13.4% in recall. Without context expansion, METEORA reaches 0.23 average precision, a 21% improvement over all baselines. METEORA also reduces evidence volume by ~80% (baselines require 4.98–6.41× more chunks for comparable recall) and achieves 4.4× higher adversarial F1 (0.43 avg vs. 0.10 for perplexity-based defense). Human evaluation confirms interpretability with 3.64/5 annotator confidence and 86% ground-truth agreement on poisoned-evidence classification.

## Baselines & Comparisons

METEORA outperforms all seven baselines on average recall (0.93 vs. 0.68–0.82), with the largest gaps on complex legal datasets: MAUD recall improves from 0.51 (Cross-Encoder BGE) to 0.72, and CUAD from 0.78 to 0.93. Against SBERT variants and Contriever, METEORA gains 11–13% recall while matching or exceeding precision (0.19 vs. 0.17–0.19). LLM-based re-rankers RankRAG (8b) and RankLLaMA (7b) are substantially outperformed, with METEORA achieving 0.93 vs. 0.68/0.69 recall and 0.19 vs. 0.12/0.13 precision, indicating that LLM re-ranking degrades on long documents due to context-length constraints. On adversarial robustness (Table 5), METEORA's verifier achieves F1 scores of 0.33–0.51 across datasets, compared to 0.06–0.15 for the perplexity-based defense (Zhou et al., 2024b) and 0.00 for no defense, a 4.4× improvement. In terms of efficiency, Table 4 shows baselines require 4.98× (SBERT), 5.06× (Contriever), and 6.41× (RankRAG) more evidence chunks to reach comparable recall, confirming METEORA's ~80% evidence reduction. The one exception is FinQA, where short passage-based documents (~700 tokens) favor traditional similarity re-rankers on recall (0.98 vs. 0.95), though METEORA still reduces evidence volume and improves generation accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

METEORA builds on DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) but repurposes it for a novel use: training rationale generators rather than aligning generation quality, with preference pairs constructed automatically from QA annotations rather than human judgments. It departs from the re-ranking paradigm (SBERT, Cross-Encoder, Contriever, RankRAG, RankLLaMA) by eliminating ranking entirely in favor of rationale-driven selection with statistical elbow detection for adaptive cutoffs. Prior rationale-based retrieval work (RAG², RADIO, Set-R) still relies on downstream re-ranking and lacks adversarial verification; METEORA unifies selection and verification under a single rationale framework, using the same rationales both to guide evidence selection and to flag poisoned content.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that interpretability, adversarial robustness, and computational efficiency in RAG are synergistic rather than competing, which challenges the prevailing assumption that explainability imposes a performance tax. The 80% reduction in evidence volume with improved recall and the 4.4× robustness gain over perplexity-based defenses are practically significant for deploying RAG in regulated domains (legal, financial, healthcare). However, absolute adversarial F1 scores remain modest (0.33–0.51), the framework is validated only on an 8B model, and the verifier's conservative thresholding may discard valid evidence. Open questions include whether the rationale-verification synergy generalizes to larger models, whether new attack vectors can bypass instruction-based flagging, and how to extend interpretability beyond evidence selection to the full generation pipeline.
