## Problem

EEG-based multimodal learning must integrate heterogeneous signals (EEG, audio, video, EMG, EOG, NIRS) that each exhibit hierarchical cognitive structure, yet Euclidean embeddings used in all prior multimodal EEG frameworks cannot faithfully represent such tree-like geometry. No prior work has explored hyperbolic geometry for multimodal EEG, and existing hyperbolic EEG methods (e.g., HEEGNet) handle only single-modality signals with fixed curvature, leaving unaddressed how to adaptively model the varying degrees of hierarchical structure across modalities.

## Input / Output

The method consumes preprocessed multimodal recordings: EEG (30-channel, 100 Hz, 0.5–45 Hz bandpass), audio (16 kHz mel-spectrograms), video (OpenFace features at 5 fps), EMG/EOG (100 Hz, bandpass filtered), and NIRS (0.2 Hz lowpass, baseline-corrected), depending on the dataset. It outputs a discrete class label—emotion (5 classes), sleep stage (5 stages), or cognitive difficulty level (3 levels)—via hyperbolic multinomial logistic regression with geodesic hyperplane decision boundaries. All evaluation is cross-subject (leave-one-subject-out or 10-fold grouped CV), making the method's domain-adaptation via hyperbolic batch normalization with domain-specific momentum a critical design element.

## Method

EEG-MoCE assigns each modality to a dedicated expert operating in its own Lorentz hyperboloid with a learnable negative curvature K^(m), so the model adaptively discovers the embedding geometry best suited to each modality's hierarchical complexity. A curvature-oriented cross-modal fusion mechanism then uses the learned curvatures as importance signals: a curvature-scaled temperature (τ^(m) = τ₀/√|K^(m)|) makes hierarchically richer modalities more selective in attention, and a learnable curvature prior bias (λ·log(|K^(j)|+ε)) explicitly up-weights contributions from high-curvature modalities. Cross-modal attention computes similarity via negative squared geodesic distance on a shared fusion manifold (curvature = mean of modality curvatures), with aggregation performed via weighted Fréchet mean to preserve hyperbolic geometry. The pipeline composes Euclidean encoders (EEGNet for EEG, CNNs for other signals) with hyperbolic experts (exponential-map lifting, hyperbolic batch normalization with domain-specific momentum, Lorentz activations), stacked curvature-guided cross-attention layers, and a hyperbolic MLR classifier, all trained end-to-end with Riemannian Adam for hyperbolic parameters.

## Results

On the EAV emotion recognition dataset (n=42, 5 classes), EEG-MoCE achieves 75.88% balanced accuracy and 75.47% macro F1 under cross-subject 10-fold CV, outperforming the strongest baseline HEEGNet (61.74% Acc) by 14.14 points. On ISRUC-Sleep (n=10, 5 stages, leave-one-subject-out), it reaches 78.53% Acc / 75.38% F1, a 3.34-point gain over XSleepFusion with notably lower variance (±2.95 vs ±5.20). On the Cognitive N-back task (n=26, 3 levels), it attains 62.39% Acc / 59.67% F1, improving over EF-Net by 7.98 points in accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EEG-MoCE outperforms all 19 baselines across all three datasets. On EAV, the gap over the best Euclidean multimodal baseline (GA2MIF, 61.10%) is 14.78 points, and over the only hyperbolic EEG baseline HEEGNet (61.74%) it is 14.14 points, with all differences statistically significant (p < 0.01). On ISRUC-Sleep, the improvement over the strongest baseline XSleepFusion (75.19%) is 3.34 points in accuracy, accompanied by a substantial variance reduction (±2.95 vs ±5.20), indicating more stable cross-subject generalization. On the Cognitive N-back task, EEG-MoCE exceeds EF-Net (54.41%) by 7.98 points and the next-best TSMMF (53.84%) by 8.55 points. General-purpose multimodal fusion methods (LMF, CTMWA, MMML) consistently underperform task-specific baselines on all three datasets, suggesting that domain-specific architectural choices matter. The hyperbolic baselines (HEEGNet, Hyper-MML) are the closest competitors but remain 14+ points behind on EAV, confirming that the curvature-oriented fusion mechanism provides gains beyond simply using hyperbolic geometry for a single modality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the mixture-of-curvature paradigm from single-modality settings (HELM for LLMs, Gu et al. for product spaces) to multimodal EEG-based neurotechnology, where each modality (EEG, audio, video, EMG, EOG, NIRS) is assigned its own learnable-curvature Lorentz manifold. It builds directly on HEEGNet's hyperbolic EEG encoder with domain-specific batch normalization for cross-subject generalization, and on Hypformer's hyperbolic attention using negative squared geodesic distance as similarity. The key conceptual departure is using learned curvature magnitude as a data-driven proxy for modality importance in cross-modal fusion, rather than treating all modalities symmetrically or relying on learned scalar weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic hyperbolic framework for EEG-based multimodal learning, and the empirical validation that all physiological and behavioral modalities exhibit measurable hierarchical structure (δ_rel < 0.3) provides a principled geometric justification for the approach. The curvature-as-importance mechanism offers an interpretable, data-driven alternative to ad-hoc modality weighting in fusion. Limitations include the computational overhead of hyperbolic operations (~2.4× slower training), the assumption that all relevant modalities share hierarchical structure (excluding, e.g., flat or cyclic signal types), and the reliance on a single unified fusion manifold rather than richer transport mechanisms. Open questions include whether the curvature-importance correlation holds for modalities with fundamentally different geometric priors and whether learned representations can be mapped back to interpretable cognitive processes.
