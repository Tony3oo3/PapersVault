## Problem

Existing EEG foundation models suffer from two fundamental limitations: (1) they inadequately model the non-Euclidean spatiotemporal topology of brain signals, relying on unidirectional hierarchical encoding or implicit representations that irreversibly lose critical micro-macro evolutionary information; and (2) they fail to bridge the modality gap between low-level physiological signals and high-level textual semantics, as current alignment strategies lack medical knowledge grounding, task-context interaction, and instance-level adaptability.

## Input / Output

The model takes raw multi-channel EEG recordings (C channels × T time points) resampled to 200 Hz with 0.1–75 Hz band-pass filtering, partitioned into non-overlapping patches of 200 samples (1 s). It produces discrete EEG tokens via a VQ-VAE codebook (8192×128), KASP-generated expert-level medical text profiles, and STAR-refined semantic summary tokens, which are jointly fed into a Qwen2.5-based autoregressive backbone. Downstream, the model outputs class labels for six tasks (workload, emotion, abnormality, sleep staging, event type, slowing) via instruction-tuned generation.

## Method

KAST-BAR establishes a 'Topological Encoding → Knowledge Anchoring → Guided Refinement' pipeline. The DSHA encoder performs bidirectional Cross-Scale Attention over a 5-level Brain Topology Hierarchy (whole-brain → individual electrodes), with a Global Refinement Stream zooming in and a Local Context Stream zooming out, then fuses both streams with intermediate features and discretizes via VQ-VAE. The KASP module extracts deterministic physical features (statistical, spectral, spatial topological), verbalizes them, and prompts a frozen Qwen2.5-7B LLM with label-free structured prompts to synthesize instance-specific expert medical profiles. The STAR refiner uses learnable latent expert queries that are first calibrated by KASP text via cross-attention, then dynamically aggregate discrete EEG tokens, with an orthogonality loss preventing expert collapse. Training proceeds in two pre-training stages (self-supervised VQ reconstruction, then joint autoregressive next-token prediction over text+EEG tokens with LoRA on Qwen2.5-0.5B/1.5B) followed by decoupled multi-task instruction fine-tuning.

## Results

KAST-BAR-Large (2.2B) achieves the best Balanced Accuracy across all six downstream EEG tasks, reaching 71.2% on Workload, 77.4% on TUSL, 83.2% on TUAB, 70.8% on TUEV, 74.3% on SEED, and 73.9% on HMC. The smaller KAST-BAR-Base (0.8B) also outperforms all multi-task baselines on five of six tasks. On the TUAB and TUEV datasets, KAST-BAR-Large further leads in AUC-PR (87.1% and 85.5% respectively) and AUROC (90.7%), confirming strong discriminative capability beyond balanced accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest multi-task foundation model THD-BAR-Huge (1.6B), KAST-BAR-Large wins on all six datasets with margins of 4.1–8.2% (e.g., +8.2% on TUSL: 77.4 vs 69.2; +5.5% on TUEV: 70.8 vs 65.3), despite a comparable parameter scale. Against NeuroLM-XL (1.7B), gains are even larger: +7.7% on Workload and +14.0% on SEED, with KAST-BAR-Base (0.8B) already surpassing NeuroLM-XL on all tasks. Among single-task specialists, KAST-BAR-Large outperforms EEGNet, SPaRCNet, ST-Transformer, BIOT, LaBraM-Base, and CBraMod on all six datasets. The sole exception is CSBrain (4.9M), which achieves 85.7% on TUSL versus KAST-BAR-Large's 77.4%, attributed to CSBrain's specialized focus on fine-grained slow-wave spectral features. EEGPT (25M) is consistently outperformed by both KAST-BAR variants across all tasks, confirming that the KASP-STAR semantic orchestration provides advantages beyond mere model scaling.

## Relation to Prior Work

KAST-BAR extends the Brain Topology Hierarchy (BTH) framework from THD-BAR by replacing its unidirectional coarse-to-fine propagation with a bidirectional Dual-Stream Hierarchical Attention (DSHA) encoder that simultaneously refines global features with local details and anchors local signals in global context. For cross-modal alignment, it departs from prior EEG-language approaches (NeuroLM's general-corpus alignment, ELM's clinical-report pairing, LEAF's static instructions) by introducing a Knowledge-Anchored Semantic Profiler (KASP) that uses a frozen LLM to synthesize instance-level medical profiles from deterministic physical features, which then calibrate learnable latent experts in a Q-Former-inspired STAR refiner. The discretization pipeline follows the VQ-VAE paradigm, and the autoregressive backbone adopts the Qwen2.5 LLM family with LoRA, positioning the work within the broader BAR (Brain Autoregressive) modeling lineage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that injecting expert-level medical knowledge via LLM-generated semantic profiles (rather than relying on scarce paired clinical reports or static task instructions) can meaningfully close the EEG-text modality gap, yielding consistent gains across six heterogeneous downstream tasks. However, the model still trails specialized single-task architectures on fine-grained spectral tasks (TUSL slowing detection), suggesting that multi-task semantic alignment may sacrifice task-specific frequency sensitivity. Open questions include whether the KASP mechanism scales beyond the frozen 7B LLM, whether the bidirectional DSHA provides genuine gains over simpler unidirectional variants at larger scale, and how the approach generalizes to truly unseen task distributions beyond the six evaluated benchmarks.
