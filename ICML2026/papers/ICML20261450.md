## Problem

Image-to-video diffusion models produce motion that violates physical laws (physical hallucination), yet the paper reveals a counter-intuitive finding: a 2-step generation often exhibits better physical consistency than a 50-step output from the same model. Through spectral analysis, the authors trace this to phase erosion during denoising—the phase spectrum degrades by ~18% from step 2 to step 50 while magnitude remains stable—indicating that the model retrieves valid motion priors early but overwrites them during visual refinement.

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference image (fixing the initial scene) and a text prompt as conditioning, and produces a generated video clip via a pre-trained video diffusion backbone (CogVideoX-5B: 49 frames at 8 fps; Wan 2.1: 81 frames at 16 fps; LTX-Video: 121 frames at 30 fps). It is entirely training-free and plug-and-play: the same frozen backbone is run twice—once for a 2-step motion prior extraction and once for the standard 50-step high-fidelity generation—sharing the same initial noise. The first frame of the output is kept as the image-condition anchor and is never modified by the guidance.

## Method

PhaseLock is a two-stage, training-free framework. In Stage 1 (Motion Prior Extraction), a few-step inference (K_few=2) produces a coarse latent, from which inter-frame latent deltas are computed via a Latent Delta Operator to form a motion template M^prior. In Stage 2 (Latent Delta Guidance), during the standard 50-step denoising, the current inter-frame deltas are compared against M^prior at each step, and the residual is injected into subsequent frames with a linearly decaying strength λ(k) active only over the first half of denoising. The key theoretical insight is that the latent delta magnitude is approximately proportional to the inter-frame phase difference (|F(Δ)| ≈ A·|φ_f − φ_{f−1}|), so constraining deltas in the spatial domain implicitly preserves phase evolution without explicit FFT operations. The authors show that direct frequency-domain manipulation (phase injection, substitution, blending) fails catastrophically due to spectral leakage, latent-space manifold mismatch, and the fact that physical constraints span all frequencies, whereas the spatial-domain L2 constraint on deltas aggregates phase alignment across all frequencies via Parseval's theorem while respecting the diffusion model's learned trajectory.

## Results

On the Physics-IQ benchmark, PhaseLock improves physical consistency by an average of 6.2 points across three open-source video diffusion backbones: CogVideoX-5B (30.8→36.0, +5.2), LTX-Video (26.4→32.0, +5.6), and Wan 2.1 (20.9→28.7, +7.8). On PhyGenBench, the method yields average gains of +23.9% (CogVideoX) and +21.4% (Wan 2.1) in physical plausibility scores. The overhead is minimal at 1.06× inference time and 1.02× memory, while VBench visual quality metrics remain broadly comparable to baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PhaseLock, applied as a training-free plug-in, surpasses all proprietary video generation models on Physics-IQ: CogVideoX+PhaseLock (36.0) outperforms Runway Gen-3 Alpha (22.8), VideoPoet (20.3), Lumiere (19.0), and Sora (10.0), and also exceeds the 24B-parameter MAGI-1 (30.2). Against the closest training-free competitor WMReward (Yuan et al., 2026), PhaseLock achieves a near-identical Physics-IQ score (36.0 vs. 36.3) but at only 1.06× inference time versus WMReward's ~5× overhead, eliminating the need for an external world-model reward (VJEPA-2) and Best-of-N search. On PhyGenBench, PhaseLock consistently improves all four physical sub-domains (mechanics, optics, thermodynamics, materials) by 8–42% relative to the unmodified baselines. The method also generalizes to step-distilled models, yielding a +1.7-point gain on the 4-step Wan 2.1 variant, though the smaller improvement is consistent with less phase erosion to correct in already few-step regimes. Human evaluation confirms the quantitative gains: PhaseLock wins 78–88% of pairwise comparisons on Physics Plausibility and Visual Quality against both CogVideoX and Wan 2.1 baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of injecting external physical knowledge (physics engines, curated datasets, world-model reward signals like WMReward) by identifying that the model already encodes valid motion priors in its earliest denoising steps but progressively erases them during visual refinement. It builds on the coarse-to-fine understanding of diffusion dynamics (Song et al., 2020; Qi et al., 2023) and frequency-domain analyses (FreeU, FreeInit, FreqPrior), but isolates a specific mechanism—phase erosion—that prior frequency-based methods did not target. The Latent Delta Guidance is positioned as a spatial-domain proxy that achieves aggregate phase preservation via Parseval's theorem, explicitly avoiding the spectral artifacts that plague direct frequency manipulation (demonstrated in Table 7).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that 2-step inference outperforms 50-step inference on physical consistency reframes the denoising process as a double-edged sword: it refines appearance while destroying structural dynamics. This mechanistic understanding (phase erosion under L2-style losses) opens a path toward phase-aware training objectives that could address the root cause rather than patching it at inference time. Key limitations remain: PhaseLock cannot correct an incorrect few-step prior, is inapplicable to autoregressive generators, and the per-scenario analysis shows it degrades performance in ~26–33% of scenarios. The broader implication—that smarter preservation may matter more than more computation for physical fidelity—could influence how future video models are trained and evaluated.
