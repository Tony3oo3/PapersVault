## Problem

Standard knowledge distillation assumes globally normalized outcome probabilities, but survival and case-control analyses (Cox partial likelihood, nested case-control, conditional logistic regression) are built on locally normalized objectives where only within-set relative risks are identifiable and nuisance terms are conditioned away. Existing distillation approaches therefore cannot transfer external predictive information in these settings without requiring globally calibrated probabilities or shared parameterizations that are not available under design-induced partial specification.

## Input / Output

The student model takes tabular patient covariates (e.g., donor and recipient features in the OPTN transplant setting) and produces a time-to-event risk score. The teacher provides only per-individual real-valued risk scores evaluated on the student cohort's comparability sets—no calibrated probabilities, no access to external individual-level data, and no requirement that the teacher share the student's parameterization. The distillation signal is consumed entirely within design-defined comparability sets (risk sets, matched strata, or sampled NCC sets), so the student output remains a relative risk score identified under within-set normalization rather than a globally calibrated event probability.

## Method

The key insight is that although composite likelihoods do not define a global outcome distribution, each likelihood component induces a normalized probability model on its restricted outcome space (e.g., all allocations of m_k events within a comparability set). The method constructs both a teacher-induced and a student-induced component distribution on this same restricted space by exponentiating and locally normalizing scores, then minimizes a component-wise KL divergence aggregated across all components. Proposition 4.1 shows this KL penalty is equivalent to replacing empirical event indicators with soft labels that blend the observed indicator and the teacher-implied marginal event probability, with the distillation weight η interpolating between empirical training (η=0) and full within-set imitation (η→∞). The framework unifies conditional logistic regression, Cox partial likelihood, and nested case-control designs under a single component-level construction, and a local excess-risk expansion (Theorem A.3) formalizes the bias-variance tradeoff: teacher-aligned component information reduces estimation variance while teacher-target mismatch introduces quadratic bias controlled by η.

## Results

In the OPTN donor-matched recipient-only setting (Table 2), component-wise KL achieves the best stratified C-index (0.5875) and lowest stratified predictive deviance (0.2127) among all flexible MLP-based students under the NCC objective, outperforming internal-only (0.5389 / 0.2199), fine-tuning (0.5458 / 0.2170), and MSE distillation (0.5818 / 0.2132). Under the Cox-type objective, component-wise KL also leads with stratified C-index 0.5663 and deviance 0.2133. In the KI→KP transfer (Figure 4), the distilled KP student matches or exceeds the KI-trained teacher on discrimination while achieving the best predictive deviance and IBS on the KP test set. In simulations, distillation from a good-quality teacher lifts C-index from 0.550 (internal-only) to 0.681, and performance remains stable under affine score scaling and shifting (Table 1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary external baseline is CoxKL (Wang et al., 2026), which regularizes a Cox model via a KL divergence defined on Cox partial-likelihood objects. CoxKL is only applicable to the linear Cox student (stratified C-index 0.5467, deviance 0.2128) and cannot be applied to the flexible MLP-based DeepNCC student because it is tied to Cox partial-likelihood objects; component-wise KL, by contrast, extends to both objectives and achieves substantially higher stratified C-index (0.5663 for Cox-type, 0.5875 for NCC-type) with lower deviance. Against the other transfer baselines in Table 2, component-wise KL wins on both metrics for both student objectives: it improves stratified C-index by +0.013 over MSE distillation (0.5875 vs. 0.5818) and by +0.049 over internal-only (0.5875 vs. 0.5389) under the NCC objective, while also achieving the lowest stratified predictive deviance (0.2127 vs. 0.2132 for MSE and 0.2199 for internal-only). Under the Cox-type objective, the gains are more modest but consistent (C-index 0.5663 vs. 0.5533 for MSE, 0.5253 for internal-only). The paper also notes that the distilled student in the KI→KP transfer (Figure 4) matches or exceeds the out-of-domain KI teacher on KP discrimination while achieving the best IBS and predictive deviance, indicating that within-set distillation allows the student to recalibrate to the target cohort rather than inheriting the teacher's risk scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies composite likelihood inference (Lindsay, 1988; Varin et al., 2011) with knowledge distillation (Hinton et al., 2015) by showing that each locally normalized likelihood component—whether from Cox partial likelihood, conditional logistic regression, or nested case-control sampling—induces a well-defined probability distribution on its restricted outcome space, making a component-wise KL divergence a valid distillation target. It departs from standard response-based distillation, which assumes globally calibrated per-instance probabilities, and from CoxKL (Wang et al., 2026), which performs KL alignment but is tied to Cox-specific objects rather than a general composite-likelihood framework. The paper also connects to the broader transfer-learning-in-survival literature (Chen et al., 2021; Sheng et al., 2021; Cheng et al., 2023) by providing a design-aware, model-agnostic distillation principle that extends beyond any single survival regression formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical bottleneck in biomedical survival modeling: transferring predictive knowledge from large external cohorts to small, heterogeneous target cohorts without sharing individual-level data, which is critical under privacy constraints and when covariates are expensive to collect. The theoretical bias-variance expansion (Appendix A.2) clarifies when distillation helps (teacher-aligned component information reduces variance) and when it hurts (teacher-target mismatch introduces bias), providing principled guidance for tuning the distillation weight. Key limitations include the requirement that the teacher be evaluable on all units in each comparability set, computational cost growing with component complexity under ties, and the local nature of the improvement guarantee. Open questions include adaptive or component-dependent weighting based on shift diagnostics and extensions to more complex censoring and sampling mechanisms.
