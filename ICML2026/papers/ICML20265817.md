## Problem

In federated LoRA, the standard practice of averaging LoRA factors (A and B) separately across clients introduces aggregation error relative to the mathematically correct average of local weight updates. This paper identifies rotational misalignment—arising from the rotational invariance of low-rank factorizations, where semantically equivalent updates occupy different latent subspaces across clients—as a major underexplored source of this error, causing destructive interference and unstable training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265817/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client receives the previous round's global LoRA adapters (Ā^{t-1} ∈ R^{r×d}, B̄^{t-1} ∈ R^{d×r}) and its own private data, then produces locally trained factors A_i^t and B_i^t. Before transmission, each client applies a client-specific orthogonal rotation to its factors, yielding aligned factors Ã_i^t and B̃_i^t of the same dimensions. The server aggregates these aligned factors via simple averaging to form the new global adapters, so the communication payload remains identical to standard federated LoRA (two r×d and d×r matrices per client).

## Method

FedRot-LoRA aligns client LoRA factors to a common reference (the previous global adapters) via an orthogonal Procrustes problem solved in closed form through SVD of an r×r correlation matrix. To balance alignment across both factors, the method alternates: A is aligned in odd rounds and B in even rounds. A soft rotation mechanism interpolates between the identity and the optimal rotation (R' = (1−λ)I + λR*) and re-projects onto the orthogonal group, preventing over-correction when the reference is noisy in early training. The alignment preserves semantic equivalence (B̃Ã = BA = ΔW_i) while reducing cross-client subspace mismatch, and the per-round cost is O(dr² + r³) with no additional communication. A convergence analysis shows that rotational alignment yields a strictly tighter upper bound on the factor-wise aggregation error compared to naive averaging, with a feasible range of λ guaranteed by Corollary 4.9.

## Results

On GLUE (RoBERTa-Large, r=4, Dirichlet h=0.5), FedRot-LoRA achieves the highest average accuracy across all client scales: 0.8932 (N=3), 0.8818 (N=10), and 0.8734 (N=50), outperforming the strongest baseline RoLoRA by 0.7%, 0.3%, and 4.9% respectively. On generative tasks with Llama 3-8B, FedRot-LoRA reaches 40.88% pass@1 on HumanEval and 44.37% exact-match on GSM8K, surpassing the next-best FFA-LoRA (38.51% and 43.61%). FedRot-LoRA also achieves an order-of-magnitude reduction in aggregation error (e.g., 4.72e-4 vs. 7.49e-3 on SST-2) compared to naive factor-wise averaging (FedIT).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedRot-LoRA consistently outperforms all four baselines across every client scale and task. Against FedIT (naive factor-wise averaging), the advantage grows with scale: +2.2% avg at N=3, +4.6% at N=10, and +10.5% at N=50, reflecting that rotational misalignment worsens with more heterogeneous clients. Against RoLoRA (alternating factor freezing), FedRot-LoRA leads by 0.7% at N=3 and 4.9% at N=50, with notably lower variance (e.g., 0.0034 vs. 0.0058 avg std at N=10). Against FFA-LoRA (single-factor freezing), FedRot-LoRA wins by 4.8% at N=3 and 10.2% at N=50, confirming that restricting the parameter space hurts adaptation capacity. Against FlexLoRA (SVD-based full-space aggregation), FedRot-LoRA leads by 2.3% at N=3 and 16.7% at N=50, while avoiding the expensive O(d³) server-side SVD. On generative tasks, FedRot-LoRA beats FFA-LoRA (the strongest generative baseline) by 2.4% on HumanEval and 0.8% on GSM8K, while RoLoRA and FlexLoRA show substantially higher variance, indicating training instability in these settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on LoRA's low-rank factorization (ΔW = BA) and the federated LoRA aggregation problem identified by FedIT (Zhang et al., 2024a). It departs from existing mitigation strategies—parameter freezing (FFA-LoRA, RoLoRA), SVD-based reconstruction (FlexLoRA, FedSRD), and high-rank residual transmission (FedEx-LoRA, FLoRA)—by isolating rotational invariance of the LoRA decomposition as a distinct and underexplored source of aggregation error. Rather than restricting the parameter space or increasing communication, FedRot-LoRA applies a lightweight orthogonal Procrustes alignment on the client side before aggregation, preserving both low-rank structure and full expressivity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of rotational misalignment as a first-class contributor to federated LoRA aggregation error reframes a problem previously attributed mainly to algebraic non-commutativity, and the proposed fix is nearly free in communication and modest in computation (O(dr² + r³) per round). The theoretical analysis provides concrete, checkable conditions (learning rate, alignment strength λ) under which alignment strictly tightens the convergence bound, giving practitioners a principled tuning guideline. Open questions include whether the alternating A/B alignment schedule is optimal, how the method interacts with client dropout or stragglers, and whether the same rotational-alignment idea transfers to other low-rank or structured parameterizations beyond LoRA.
