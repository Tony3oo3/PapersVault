## Problem

While diverse VLA models (diffusion-based, flow-based) have demonstrated strong robotic manipulation capabilities, no principled mechanism exists to ensemble them into a stronger unified policy. Conventional ensemble techniques designed for discriminative tasks cannot be directly applied to generative action policies with high-dimensional, multimodal distributions, leaving the question of how to effectively combine complementary VLA models largely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264207/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each base policy receives multi-camera RGB observations (L515 depth cameras in simulation; wrist-mounted and front-facing cameras on Cobot Mobile ALOHA in real-world) plus a natural language instruction as conditioning; DP3 additionally consumes point cloud observations. The ensemble produces a single 14-dimensional action vector (dual-arm ALOHA-AgileX configuration) by iteratively integrating a composed velocity field from a Gaussian noise initialization over N deterministic ODE steps, followed by a gated bounded residual correction (|δ|∞ ≤ 0.001).

## Method

EnsembleVLA establishes a unified energy-based framework showing that both diffusion score functions and flow-matching velocity fields correspond to the negative gradient of an implicit energy function (via Langevin dynamics for diffusion and Helmholtz decomposition for flow matching). Policies are composed at the distribution level by summing weighted energy functions, which yields a geometric mixture of distributions and a linear combination of score/velocity fields. Learnable composition weights (softmax-parameterized on the probability simplex) are optimized end-to-end to adaptively balance policy contributions. A confidence-aware gating module refines the composed action: a Delta-Net (two-layer MLP with spectral normalization) predicts bounded residual corrections via tanh, while a Gate-Net (three-layer MLP with sigmoid) estimates whether correction is beneficial, initialized conservatively at g≈0.12. Training uses a composite loss combining MSE reconstruction, binary cross-entropy gate supervision (based on whether correction reduces error), and direct delta supervision with clipped targets.

## Results

On the RoboTwin 2.0 benchmark (8 bimanual manipulation tasks, 100 demos, 100 eval episodes), EnsembleVLA consistently outperforms all individual base policies across three composition settings. The strongest gain is in the diffusion-diffusion composition (DP+DP3), where EnsembleVLA achieves 82.8% average success rate, improving 14.4 points over the best base policy DP3 (68.4%). In the flow-flow composition (π0+π0.5), EnsembleVLA reaches 74.6%, a 9.5-point improvement over π0.5 (65.1%). In the cross-paradigm composition (DP+π0.5), the gain is more modest at 70.1% (+5.0 over π0.5), attributed to velocity-field transformation complexity. On real-world Cobot Mobile ALOHA tasks, EnsembleVLA achieves 65.8% average success versus 57.5% for π0.5 and 40.0% for DP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EnsembleVLA is evaluated against four individual VLA policies (DP, DP3, π0, π0.5) and one additional policy (OpenVLA-OFT) in the multi-policy scaling experiment. In the DP+DP3 composition, EnsembleVLA (82.8%) surpasses both DP (52.0%) and DP3 (68.4%) by 30.8 and 14.4 points respectively, with the largest per-task gain on Handover Block (70.0% vs. 49.0% for DP3). In the π0+π0.5 composition, EnsembleVLA (74.6%) outperforms π0 (52.5%) by 22.1 points and π0.5 (65.1%) by 9.5 points. The cross-paradigm DP+π0.5 composition yields a smaller improvement (70.1% vs. 65.1% for π0.5), which the authors attribute to precision loss in converting diffusion noise predictions to velocity-space displacements. In the three-policy scaling experiment (DP+DP3+OpenVLA-OFT), adding OpenVLA-OFT to the DP+DP3 ensemble further improves success rates from 96/89/93% to 99/95/98% on three tasks, confirming that gains scale with the number of composed policies. On real-world tasks, EnsembleVLA (65.8% avg) beats π0.5 (57.5%) by 8.3 points and DP (40.0%) by 25.8 points across all six manipulation tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the compositional generation principle of energy-based models (Du et al., 2020) from visual generation to robotic action policies, establishing a theoretical bridge between diffusion-based (Song et al., 2020b) and flow-matching (Lipman et al., 2022) VLA paradigms by showing both correspond to negative energy gradients. It departs from classical ensemble learning (bagging, boosting, stacking), which operates on discriminative outputs, by formulating policy composition at the distribution level through additive energy summation. The work also builds on the authors' prior EnergyAction paper (Song et al., 2026) on unimanual-to-bimanual composition, generalizing the EBM framework to multi-policy ensembles across heterogeneous generative architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical unification of diffusion and flow matching under a common EBM formalism provides a principled mechanism for combining pre-trained VLA policies without retraining, which is practically valuable as the VLA ecosystem fragments across architectures. However, the cross-paradigm composition (diffusion + flow) yields only ~5% improvement versus ~14% for same-paradigm pairs, revealing that the Helmholtz-decomposition approximation (r ≈ 0) introduces non-trivial precision loss in practice. Open questions include extending the framework to non-gradient-based policies (direct regression, autoregressive), developing principled model selection among many candidate policies, and whether the energy-based composition generalizes to longer-horizon or multi-task settings beyond the 8-task benchmark.
