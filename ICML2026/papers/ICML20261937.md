## Problem

Triangle cut sparsification, which preserves weighted triangle counts across every cut, is a natural higher-order analogue of classical cut sparsification but has remained unexplored from a quantum perspective. Classical constructions (Kapralov et al., 2022) are bottlenecked by explicit triangle enumeration and repeated Õ(m)-time post-sampling, and no quantum lower bounds or speedups were known for the underlying triangle listing primitive.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes query access (via QRAM) to an undirected weighted graph G with n vertices and m edges, supporting degree, neighbor, and vertex-pair queries, along with a sparsification parameter ε. It outputs a reweighted subgraph G' = (V, E', w') with Õ(n/ε²) edges such that for every cut (S, V\S), the weighted sum of crossing triangles is preserved within a (1±ε) factor. The triangle listing subroutine separately outputs all t triangles in the graph, with runtime depending on n, m, and t.

## Method

The core contribution is a quantum triangle listing algorithm combining three paradigms: (i) a heavy-light vertex partition (threshold Δ=√m) where light-vertex triangles are found via repeated Grover search over neighbor pairs and heavy-vertex triangles via Grover search over triples, yielding Õ(m + m^{3/4}t^{1/2}); (ii) a two-level quantum walk on Johnson graphs J(V,h) extending Le Gall's triangle detection to listing, with a pre-listing phase that samples a vertex set S to reduce the candidate search space by a factor of s, achieving Õ(n^{5/4}t^{7/12} + n^{7/6}t^{7/9}); and (iii) direct Grover search giving Õ(n^{3/2}t^{1/2}). The sparsification algorithm then accelerates the post-sampling stage by encoding sampling decisions implicitly via random strings (following Apers & De Wolf), deferring explicit edge output to a final Grover search, reducing the per-iteration cost from Õ(m) to Õ(√(mn)/ε). The overall construction runs in T_q-list + Õ(√(mn)/ε) and achieves a matching Ω(n/ε²) lower bound on sparsifier size.

## Results

The quantum triangle listing algorithm (Theorem 1.3) achieves a running time of $\tilde{O}(\min(n^{5/4}t^{7/12}+n^{7/6}t^{7/9},\; m+m^{3/4}t^{1/2},\; n^{3/2}t^{1/2}))$, improving upon the best classical bound $\tilde{O}(m^{4/3}+mt^{1/3})$ (assuming $\omega=2$) for all graphs with $t\leq m^{3/2}$. The quantum triangle cut sparsifier (Theorem 1.2) is constructed in $\tilde{O}(T_{\text{q-list}}+\sqrt{mn}/\varepsilon)$ time with $\tilde{O}(n/\varepsilon^2)$ edges, reducing the post-sampling cost from the classical $\tilde{O}(m)$ to $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{mn}/\varepsilon)$. A matching lower bound of $\Omega(n/\varepsilon^2)$ edges (Theorem 5.2) establishes near-tight sparsity. In the extreme case $t=1$, the listing time reduces to $\tilde{O}(n^{5/4})$, matching the best known quantum triangle detection bound of Le Gall (2014).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against the classical triangle cut sparsification algorithm of Kapralov et al. (2022), which runs in $\tilde{O}(\min\{T_{\text{c-list}},n^{\omega}\}+m)$ time. The quantum algorithm wins on both components: the triangle listing phase is accelerated for a broad class of graphs (sparse graphs and those with moderate triangle counts), and the post-sampling phase is reduced from $\tilde{O}(m)$ to $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{mn}/\varepsilon)$, a polynomial improvement. For triangle listing specifically, the quantum heavy-light bound $m+m^{3/4}t^{1/2}$ dominates the classical $\tilde{O}(m^{4/3}+mt^{1/3})$ (assuming $\omega=2$, the conjectured optimal) whenever $t\leq m^{3/2}$, which holds for all graphs by Fact C.6. The quantum walk approach extends Le Gall's (2014) $\tilde{O}(n^{5/4})$ triangle detection to listing, with the $t=1$ case exactly matching that bound. The $\Omega(n/\varepsilon^2)$ sparsifier-size lower bound (Theorem 5.2) is the first matching lower bound specialized to triangle motifs, complementing the upper bound and establishing near-optimality of the sparsity guarantee inherited from Kapralov et al.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of quantum graph algorithms and motif sparsification, extending three distinct lines of work. It generalizes Le Gall's (2014) quantum triangle detection algorithm—based on quantum walks over Johnson graphs—to the harder triangle listing problem, requiring careful duplicate-avoidance via edge removal and a two-level walk structure. The sparsification framework builds directly on Kapralov et al.'s (2022) classical strength-based motif cut sparsification, with the quantum speedup applied to both the triangle-listing bottleneck and the post-sampling stage (adapting Apers & De Wolf's implicit random-string sampling). A new heavy-light vertex partition approach, combined with repeated Grover search, provides a complementary algorithm for sparse graphs that has no direct classical quantum analogue.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first quantum algorithm for triangle cut sparsification and the first provable quantum speedup for triangle listing, filling a natural gap in the quantum sparsification literature that has covered edge and hypergraph sparsifiers but not higher-order motif sparsifiers. The near-tight lower bound (Ω(n/ε²) edges) establishes that the sparsifier size is essentially optimal, closing a question left open by Kapralov et al. Key open questions include extending the quantum speedup to general motifs (e.g., 4-cycles), establishing quantum query lower bounds for triangle listing (none currently exist), and determining practical crossover points once fault-tolerant quantum hardware matures. The triangle-listing primitive also yields immediate speedups for triangle-based spectral and PageRank clustering pipelines.
