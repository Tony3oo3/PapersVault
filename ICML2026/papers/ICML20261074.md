## Problem

Sequence models implicitly overfit to the temporal or spatial scales observed during training and degrade sharply when evaluated at substantially longer horizons or higher resolutions without retraining. Existing hierarchical architectures (U-Net-style, Swin) use disjoint, scale-specific parameters and lack mechanisms to enforce consistent dynamics across scales, preventing zero-shot extrapolation beyond the training regime.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261074/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For time-series forecasting, the model consumes multivariate sequences with lookback windows of 96–512 tokens and predicts future values at horizons up to 720 steps. For spatial transcriptomics, it takes a spatial gene expression matrix (top 3,000 highly variable genes per spot) and produces unsupervised cluster labels for anatomical domains. For computational pathology, it ingests 512×512 histopathology images but is trained exclusively on 128×128 or 256×256 random crops (token lengths 64 or 256 with 16×16 patches) and evaluated zero-shot on the full 512×512 image (token length 1024), outputting a 4-class breast cancer diagnosis.

## Method

DF-Mamba applies a single shared selective SSM operator Φ_θ recursively across K depths, halving the sequence length at each level so the total FLOPs form a convergent geometric series (≈2× a single Mamba pass). The key mechanism is exponential time-step scaling Δ_k = Δ_0·2^k, derived from RG-flow time-dilation consistency, which expands the effective receptive field exponentially while keeping parameters fixed. A learned pair-concat coarse-graining MLP (shared across all depths) aggregates adjacent tokens into the next scale, and an RG gate fuses the local SSM output with the upsampled global context. Training is stabilized by two physics-inspired auxiliary losses: a reconstruction loss enforcing information conservation through a symmetric decoder, and a cross-scale consistency loss that penalizes divergence between local and upsampled-global features (a fixed-point regularizer). The consistency loss also acts as a local spectral regularizer on the recursive Jacobian, keeping singular values near unity and preventing gradient vanishing/exploration across recursion depth.

## Results

On time-series forecasting, DF-Mamba-TS achieves the best MSE of 0.158 (MAE 0.261) on ETTm2 at 96-step horizon, outperforming Affirm (0.167), DTMamba (0.177), and iTransformer (0.180), with advantages widening at longer horizons (e.g., 0.316 vs. 0.351 for Affirm at 720-step). On spatial transcriptomics, DF-Mamba-ST attains the highest average ARI of 0.51 and NMI of 0.63 on DLPFC, and a substantial leap to ARI 0.65 / NMI 0.69 on Breast Cancer, surpassing the second-best DiffusionST by 14% in ARI. Under zero-shot resolution extrapolation in computational pathology (trained on 128×128 crops, tested on 512×512), DF-Mamba reaches 71.11% accuracy with only 0.89M parameters, outperforming ResNet (62.22%), ViT (57.78%), and ViM (64.44%), and further improves to 77.78% when trained on 256×256 crops while all baselines stagnate or improve marginally.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On time-series forecasting, DF-Mamba-TS consistently outperforms all eight baselines on ETTm2 across all four horizons, with the largest margins over Crossformer (which degrades severely at long horizons, e.g., MSE 1.730 at 720-step vs. 0.316 for DF-Mamba) and moderate but consistent gains over the strongest Mamba baselines Affirm and DTMamba. On Weather and Electricity, DF-Mamba-TS is competitive with TSLANet and DLinear at short horizons but pulls ahead at 336 and 720-step predictions. In spatial transcriptomics, DF-Mamba-ST achieves the best average ARI (0.51) on DLPFC, narrowly edging out BASS (0.50) and GraphST (0.46), and dominates on Breast Cancer with ARI 0.65 vs. DiffusionST's 0.57 and GraphST's 0.64. In computational pathology under zero-shot scale extrapolation, DF-Mamba (0.89M params) beats ResNet (11.18M), ViT (2.30M), ViM (1.92M), Swin Transformer (27.5M), ResNeSt (25.4M), and PVTv2 (3.4M) on both accuracy and FLOPs; critically, DF-Mamba shows positive scaling (71.11%→77.78% as training crop size increases from 128 to 256), while ViT and ViM show zero improvement and ResNet gains only 6.7 points, confirming that the recursive shared-dynamics design—not merely more parameters or hierarchical structure—drives cross-scale generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

DF-Mamba builds directly on the Selective State Space Model (Mamba) as its per-scale backbone, but departs from the flat, single-resolution processing paradigm by introducing a recursive, parameter-shared architecture inspired by Renormalization Group (RG) flow in statistical physics. Unlike hierarchical backbones such as U-Net or Swin Transformer that learn disjoint, scale-specific parameters at each resolution, DF-Mamba enforces a single shared SSM operator across all recursion depths with an exponentially scaled discretization step (Δ_k = Δ_0 · 2^k), a constraint derived from discretization consistency under time dilation. The learned coarse-graining module (a shared MLP over token pairs) and the auxiliary reconstruction/consistency losses are novel additions that have no direct analogue in prior SSM or hierarchical vision architectures, positioning the work at the intersection of efficient sequence modeling and physics-inspired inductive biases.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-shot scale extrapolation capability—training on short sequences or low-resolution crops and evaluating on substantially larger inputs without retraining—addresses a practical bottleneck in scientific and medical deployment where inference routinely occurs beyond the training scale. The linear-time complexity with exponentially expanding receptive field is attractive for ultra-long sequences where Transformers become infeasible. However, the RG connection is a variational approximation rather than an exact equivalence, the time-series gains over strong Mamba baselines (Affirm, DTMamba) are modest, and the authors themselves note that benefits are most pronounced when data exhibits inherent multi-scale structure. Open questions include whether the recursive depth can be increased beyond the tested K≈log₂(L) levels, how the approach extends to genuinely 2D/3D data beyond 1D sequence scanning, and whether the exponential time-scaling constraint is truly necessary or whether softer alternatives suffice.
