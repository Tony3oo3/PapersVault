## Problem

Causal language models factorize sequence probabilities using only preceding context, leaving the future tokens available in the training data entirely unexploited during optimization. This creates an inherent asymmetry: the model is trained to predict each token from partial observations even though the full sequence is present, wasting statistical dependencies that extend beyond unidirectional causality (e.g., coreference, syntactic agreement, long-range semantic coherence). The paper asks whether future context can be incorporated as privileged information during training without compromising the causal structure required for autoregressive generation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes packed text sequences of fixed length 2,048 tokens (containing 3–8 documents per sequence) and produces the standard next-token probability distribution over the vocabulary at each position. During training, each sequence is processed twice through the same model weights: once under a causal attention mask (student view) and once under a future-aware mask (teacher view), with the teacher pass executed in inference mode with all gradients detached. The final output remains a single causal distribution; the teacher distribution is used only as a soft target for the auxiliary regret loss and is discarded at inference time.

## Method

Regret Pre-training introduces a dual-view architecture in which a single transformer (OLMoE-1B-7B, a sparse MoE with 1B active / 7B total parameters) generates both a causal Student distribution P_S and a future-conditioned Teacher distribution P_T, differing only in their attention masks. The training objective augments standard negative log-likelihood with a forward KL divergence regret loss (teacher-to-student) weighted by α, instantiating Vapnik's Learning Using Privileged Information paradigm where the future context serves as privileged information. Two teacher configurations are studied: LOCALREGRET extends causal attention by exactly one future token, while GLOBALREGRET grants bidirectional attention over the full sequence with the target position masked. For packed sequences, composite masks enforce both target-position masking and document-boundary isolation to prevent cross-document leakage. The approach adds no parameters and requires only one extra inference-mode forward pass per training step, with MoE load-balancing statistics computed exclusively from the student pass to avoid optimizing the router for a distribution unavailable at inference.

## Results

After 4 billion tokens of pre-training on OLMoE-1B-7B, GLOBALREGRET achieves the highest average zero-shot accuracy of 33.9%, followed by LOCALREGRET at 32.2%, compared to the standard causal baseline at 30.2%. The largest single-task gain is on BoolQ, where GLOBALREGRET reaches 61.0% versus 42.9% for the baseline (+18.1 pp). LOCALREGRET leads on commonsense reasoning tasks (PIQA 58.9%, HellaSwag 27.5%, CSQA 26.5%), while GLOBALREGRET leads on reading comprehension and factual knowledge (BoolQ 61.0%, ARC-Challenge 25.8%, WinoGrande 50.8%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares both regret configurations against a standard causal language modeling (CLM) baseline trained with identical architecture, data (DCLM-Baseline), and compute budget (4B tokens, 8×A100). GLOBALREGRET outperforms the baseline on all seven tasks, with the most dramatic gain on BoolQ (+18.1 pp, 61.0% vs 42.9%) and ARC-Challenge (+4.1 pp, 25.8% vs 21.7%), reflecting the benefit of bidirectional context for long-range reasoning. LOCALREGRET also beats the baseline on every task, with its largest advantages on PIQA (+2.5 pp), HellaSwag (+3.2 pp), and CommonsenseQA (+4.4 pp), indicating that a single future token provides a strong local transition signal for commonsense plausibility. Between the two regret variants, GLOBALREGRET wins on BoolQ, ARC-Challenge, and WinoGrande, while LOCALREGRET wins on PIQA, HellaSwag, CSQA, and MMLU, yielding complementary performance profiles. Neither configuration is uniformly dominant; the choice of teacher scope (one future token vs. full bidirectional context) determines which inductive bias is transferred to the causal student.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the bidirectional-to-unidirectional distillation paradigm introduced by Chen et al. (2020) for NMT, but eliminates the separate teacher model by using the same transformer weights under two different attention masks (causal student vs. future-aware teacher). The theoretical grounding is drawn from Vapnik's Learning Using Privileged Information (LUPI) framework, where future context serves as privileged information available only during training. Unlike prior approaches that modify the architecture (ProphetNet, GLM, XLNet) or require non-standard inference procedures (Fill-in-the-Middle), Regret Pre-training keeps the standard causal transformer unchanged and confines all modifications to attention mask construction, making it a pure objective-level intervention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practically attractive recipe—no extra parameters, no architectural changes, only one additional inference-mode forward pass—that could be dropped into existing pretraining pipelines with minimal engineering. The observed dichotomy (GLOBALREGRET excels on long-range reading comprehension, LOCALREGRET on local commonsense) suggests the teacher's context scope acts as a tunable inductive bias, opening a design axis for task-targeted pretraining. However, all evidence comes from a single 1B-active-parameter MoE model trained on only 4B tokens, leaving open whether the gains persist at larger scales, whether intermediate context scopes outperform the two endpoints, and whether the approach interacts favorably with longer training budgets where the baseline may already capture much of the available signal.
