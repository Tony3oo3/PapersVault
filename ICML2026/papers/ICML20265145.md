## Problem

Existing contrastive decoding methods for Video-LLMs construct perturbed views via random perturbations (Gaussian noise, frame dropping) that are not aligned with the specific visual cues driving hallucination or the model's actual weaknesses. This misalignment leads to unstable or even degraded performance, as the contrastive signal fails to target the query-relevant evidence the model is most likely to hallucinate about.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265145/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video clip (sequence of frames) and a text query as input, and produces text response tokens via contrastive decoding. Object detection (YOLOv11) is applied per frame to generate soft spatial masks, which are combined with a temporal frame mask to form the counterfactual view. The approach is training-free and adds only one extra forward pass per decoding step, making it compatible with greedy search, beam search, or nucleus sampling without modifying model parameters.

## Method

MACD operates in three stages: (1) object detection with YOLOv11 yields per-object soft masks and a frame-level mask, initialized with a uniform strength r₀=0.75; (2) model-aware optimization performs gradient ascent on the VLM's query-token reconstruction loss to maximize the loss, pushing mask strengths toward 1 for query-critical objects and toward 0 for irrelevant ones, then discretizing to {0, r₀, 1}; (3) contrastive decoding scores each candidate token as (1+α)·log p(t|V,q,y<n) − α·log p(t|V_d(r*),q,y<n) with a plausibility filter β that retains only tokens whose base-view probability exceeds β·max. The key insight is that the model's own loss signal identifies which visual regions are evidence-critical for the current query, producing a targeted counterfactual that exposes hallucination-prone tokens rather than applying indiscriminate corruption.

## Results

Across six Video-LLM backbones (Qwen3-VL-2B, Qwen2.5-VL-3B/7B, Qwen2-VL-2B/7B, InternVL3-8B) and four benchmarks, MACD consistently achieves the best scores on all metrics. On EventHallusion, MACD reaches F1=0.91 and Accuracy=0.82 with Qwen3-VL-2B (vs. 0.82/0.72 for VCD and 0.78/0.72 for SID), and F1=0.87/Accuracy=0.79 with Qwen2-VL-7B. Gains extend beyond hallucination-specific evaluation: on MVBench, MACD achieves 0.77 accuracy with Qwen3-VL-2B (vs. 0.56 VCD, 0.48 SID), and on Video-MME it reaches 0.64 (vs. 0.56 SID, 0.44 VCD). In the Video-POPE object-hallucination test, MACD reduces the false-'yes' rate on absent-object questions from 40.0% to 17.0% (McNemar's p=0.0061).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MACD outperforms both VCD (Leng et al., 2024) and SID (Huo et al., 2024) on every metric across all six backbones and four benchmarks, with no single cell where a baseline wins. Against VCD, which applies heuristic global degradations (Gaussian noise, patch masking), MACD's model-aware object-level and frame-level masks yield substantially larger gains: e.g., on EventHallusion with Qwen3-VL-2B, MACD improves F1 from 0.82 to 0.91 and Recall from 0.91 to 0.98, and on MVBench accuracy jumps from 0.56 to 0.77. Against SID, which suppresses low-importance visual tokens internally via attention, MACD avoids the collateral damage of removing useful cues; this is most visible on Qwen2.5-VL-7B where SID's Recall collapses to 0.18 while MACD sustains 0.57, and on Perception Test where SID drops to 0.35 accuracy (vs. MACD's 0.70). The advantage is most pronounced on smaller models (Qwen2-VL-2B, Qwen2.5-VL-3B) where spurious language priors are stronger, and on EventHallusion where co-occurrence bias and tight temporal dependencies make targeted counterfactual masking most impactful.

## Relation to Prior Work

MACD extends the contrastive decoding paradigm (Su & Xu, 2022; VCD, Leng et al. 2024) by replacing its heuristic perturbation step—random noise, patch masking, or internal token suppression (SID, Huo et al. 2024)—with a model-aware counterfactual construction that uses the VLM's own gradient signal to optimize object-level and frame-level soft masks. The key conceptual departure is that the 'weaker view' is no longer a fixed or random degradation but a query-conditioned, backbone-specific perturbation that targets the exact visual evidence the model relies on, turning the perturbation from a generic corruption into a targeted evidence-removal operation. The method remains in the training-free, inference-time intervention family alongside self-consistency and self-refinement approaches, and is explicitly complementary to them (as shown by the MACD+SC combination in the appendix).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MACD addresses a concrete and acknowledged weakness of prior contrastive decoding methods—the misalignment between random perturbations and the visual cues that actually drive hallucination—by making the counterfactual view model- and query-aware. The consistent gains across six backbones (Qwen2/2.5/3-VL at 2B–7B, InternVL3-8B) and four benchmarks, with especially strong improvements on small/occluded/co-occurring objects, suggest the approach generalizes beyond a single architecture. However, the reliance on an external object detector (YOLOv11) limits applicability to non-object-centric phenomena (smoke, lighting, abstract concepts), and the ~35% latency and ~62% memory overhead, while moderate, are non-trivial for real-time deployment. Open questions include whether learned or open-vocabulary proposal mechanisms can eliminate the detector dependency, and whether the gradient-ascent mask optimization scales efficiently to very long videos or high frame counts.
