## Problem

Estimating the score difference ∇log q − ∇log p is fundamental to transfer learning and post-training of diffusion models, yet existing estimators either lack statistical consistency (classify-then-differentiate pipelines overfit high-frequency noise, producing catastrophic gradient oscillations) or are computationally infeasible at scale (kernel-based methods require O(n) evaluations per inference step). No prior work provides both a statistically consistent convergence guarantee and a scalable neural-network-based estimator for this problem.

## Input / Output

The method takes n i.i.d. samples from a source distribution p and n i.i.d. samples from a target distribution q (a fixed source-target design), augmented with independent uniform time draws t_i ∈ [t_0, T] for the diffusion-model extension. For unbounded domains, inputs are projected onto a ball of radius 2R and rescaled to the unit ball before network evaluation. The output is the score difference vector field ∇_x log(q_t/p_t) at each (t, x), obtained by first fitting the scalar log-density ratio f* = log(q/p) with a clipped sparse ReLU³ network and then computing its spatial gradient via backpropagation.

## Method

The core contribution is a Sobolev-regularized empirical risk minimization objective: the standard binary cross-entropy loss for the log-density ratio is augmented with a gradient penalty λ‖∇f‖²_{L²(μ)}, which constrains the smoothness of the estimated ratio and prevents the high-frequency overfitting that destroys gradient estimates after differentiation. The function class is a clipped sparse ReLU³ neural network with bounded L∞ norm and bounded spatial gradient, ensuring the hypothesis space is closed, convex, and amenable to localized Rademacher complexity analysis. Theoretically, the paper establishes an H¹(μ) convergence rate of Õ(n^{-(s-1)/(d+2s-2)}) via strong convexity of the penalized risk on the bounded logit set, a B-spline quasi-interpolation approximation bound, and a shifted oracle inequality, along with a matching minimax lower bound up to a saturation gap. For diffusion models, the framework extends to the augmented time-space domain [t_0,T]×ℝ^d through a truncation-rescaling-projection pipeline that balances a core estimation error against a sub-Gaussian tail error, yielding a rate of Õ(n^{-(s-1)/(d+1+2s-2)}) in the time-averaged H¹ norm.

## Results

On the ECG transfer learning benchmark (PTB-XL → ICBEB2018), the proposed TGDP-SoB method achieves AUC 0.915, macro F_β=2 0.693, and macro G_β=2 0.453, outperforming the unregularized TGDP baseline (AUC 0.905, F_β=2 0.662, G_β=2 0.436) and the Finetune Generator (AUC 0.862). On the Office-Caltech-10 domain adaptation benchmark, the Sobolev-regularized classifier (Cls w sob) achieves the best average accuracy across 12 transfer tasks, with notable gains over the unregularized classifier (e.g., 0.7046 vs. 0.6002 on Webcam→Amazon) and competitive performance against the kernel-based estimator (Liu et al., 2023). In synthetic gradient estimation, the Sobolev penalty yields over 50% relative error reduction at N=20 samples across all three distribution pairs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the kernel-based score difference estimator of Liu et al. (2023), the Sobolev-regularized classifier (Cls w sob) wins on 7 of 12 Office-Caltech-10 transfer tasks (e.g., Webcam→Amazon: 0.7046 vs. 0.5877; Caltech→DSLR: 0.8623 vs. 0.8344) but loses on 5 (e.g., Amazon→Webcam: 0.8203 vs. 0.8678; DSLR→Amazon: 0.8006 vs. 0.8716), with the kernel method's advantage concentrated in high-accuracy source-to-target pairs. Crucially, the classification-based approach is ~5000× faster per gradient evaluation (0.0004s vs. 2.06s), making it scalable to diffusion-model settings where the kernel method is infeasible. Against the TGDP baseline of Ouyang et al. (2024) on the ECG benchmark, TGDP-SoB consistently improves all three metrics: +1.0 AUC points, +3.1 F_β points, and +1.7 G_β points, confirming that Sobolev regularization stabilizes the score difference estimate under limited target data. The unregularized classifier (Cls w/o sob) is consistently outperformed by its Sobolev-regularized counterpart on both synthetic and real-world tasks, with the gap largest in the small-sample regime (>50% error reduction at N=20).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 'classify-then-differentiate' pipeline for score difference estimation (Ouyang et al., 2024; Kim et al., 2022) by adding a Sobolev gradient penalty to the classification loss, drawing on the Sobolev-regularized estimation framework of Ding et al. (2025) and the ReLU³ neural network approximation theory of Lu et al. (2021). It departs from kernel-based consistent estimators (Liu et al., 2023; Srikanth et al.) that suffer from prohibitive computational cost, and from unregularized classification approaches that lack gradient convergence guarantees. The key conceptual contribution is showing that the Sobolev penalty simultaneously controls the hypothesis space to prevent high-frequency overfitting and enables a localized Rademacher complexity analysis yielding explicit convergence rates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide a statistically consistent, computationally scalable estimator for score differences with explicit convergence rates and a minimax lower bound, filling a gap between theoretically guaranteed but infeasible kernel methods and fast but unguaranteed classification-based approaches. The acknowledged gap between the upper rate Õ(n^{-(s-1)/(d+2s-2)}) and the minimax lower bound Õ(n^{-2(s-1)/(d+2s)})—attributed to saturation in single-step Tikhonov regularization—raises the open question of whether iterative regularization schemes can close this gap without prohibitive sequential training costs. For practitioners, the >50% error reduction in small-sample regimes and the ECG transfer learning results suggest the method is directly applicable to diffusion model post-training where target data is scarce.
