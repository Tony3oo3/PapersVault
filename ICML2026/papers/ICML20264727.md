## Problem

State-of-the-art AIGI detectors built on the 'static artifact hypothesis'—that invariant features learned from historical data generalize universally—suffer catastrophic performance collapse (down to near-random or 0.12% accuracy) when confronted with rapidly evolving generators like SD3, Nano Banana Pro, and Doubao Seedream 4.0. The field lacks both a benchmark that reflects the current explosion of heterogeneous and closed-source commercial engines and a practical mechanism for rapid adaptation to novel generative threats without forgetting previously learned distributions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and produces a binary AI/Non-AI classification. Internally, the image is consumed by two parallel branches: a high-frequency branch that applies FFT, zero-outs low-frequency components via a high-pass mask, and feeds the residual into an Xception encoder to produce a d-dimensional routing signal; and a semantic branch that passes the image through a frozen DINOv3 backbone with LoRA adapters to yield a D=1024-dimensional feature vector. The final output is a weighted concatenation of C=128-dimensional subspace features (gated by softmax routing weights) projected through a fusion network, then classified against learned AI and Non-AI prototype vectors.

## Method

Fleet introduces a mutually exclusive subspace routing framework where C learnable subspaces are decoupled into AI-oriented and Non-AI-oriented groups during pre-training via an orthogonality constraint (L_orth) and a coverage constraint (L_cov) alongside InfoNCE contrastive loss. The key mechanism is frequency-guided routing: a high-pass-filtered FFT signal is encoded by a CNN to produce a routing vector whose dot-product with each subspace's learned address, passed through softmax, determines which subspaces activate. During few-shot adaptation, an avoidance routing loss (L_avoid) enforces orthogonality between novel AI samples' routing distributions and the Non-AI anchor centroid (and vice versa), implementing a 'Shunt-and-Isolate' strategy that redirects forgery features into the AI subspace while suppressing their activation in Non-AI routes. A replay-based distillation loss (L_distill) preserves the geometric configuration of previously seen samples to prevent catastrophic forgetting, and the first 5 adaptation epochs prioritize routing correction (λ_cls=0) before enabling classification, reducing optimization conflicts.

## Results

On the Treasure benchmark (64 generators), Fleet achieves 92.63% mAcc and 95.66% mAP with 10-shot adaptation, surpassing the best zero-shot method PLM (88.18% mAcc, 90.44% mAP) and the few-shot baseline FSD at 10-shot (75.87% mAcc, 80.54% mAP). Notably, Fleet's 1-shot result (86.99% mAcc) already exceeds FSD's 10-shot performance by 11.12 points. On AIGIBench-13, Fleet reaches 94.89% mAcc at 10-shot versus PLM's 88.57%. Critically, Fleet maintains >99.9% accuracy on the pre-training validation set (anti-forgetting), while FSD collapses to 74.81%, demonstrating that the avoidance routing mechanism prevents catastrophic forgetting during adaptation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Fleet decisively outperforms all six baselines on both benchmarks. Against the strongest zero-shot method PLM (AAAI 2026), Fleet at 10-shot gains +4.45 mAcc on Treasure (92.63% vs. 88.18%) and +6.32 mAcc on AIGIBench-13 (94.89% vs. 88.57%), while also achieving substantially higher mAP (95.66% vs. 90.44% on Treasure). Against the dedicated few-shot baseline FSD (ICML 2025), Fleet's advantage is even more pronounced: at 10-shot, Fleet leads by +16.76 mAcc on Treasure (92.63% vs. 75.87%) and +19.58 mAcc on AIGIBench-13 (94.89% vs. 75.31%). A striking finding is that Fleet's 1-shot result (86.99% mAcc on Treasure) already surpasses FSD's 10-shot (75.87%) by over 11 points, indicating the routing pre-training provides a strong inductive structure. On the anti-forgetting metric (Pretrain Val accuracy), Fleet maintains ≥99.69% across all shot settings, whereas FSD degrades severely to 74.81% at 10-shot, exposing catastrophic forgetting in the baseline. The remaining zero-shot baselines (FreqNet, ClipDetection, AIDE, SAFE) all trail Fleet by 4–19 mAcc points on Treasure, with SAFE and AIDE showing particularly poor AI-class accuracy (73.53% and 70.94% respectively) despite high Non-AI accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant 'static artifact hypothesis' in AIGI detection, which assumes invariant features learned from historical data generalize zero-shot to unseen generators. It builds on frequency-domain analysis (FreqNet, Frank et al.), subspace decomposition (Nguyen et al., Yan et al.), and LoRA-based parameter-efficient fine-tuning, but reframes adaptation as constrained subspace routing rather than unconstrained feature updates. The avoidance routing mechanism draws on continual learning replay (Rebuffi et al.) and MoE-style gating, but applies them specifically to redirect novel forgery features away from Non-AI-dominated subspaces. The Treasure benchmark extends the lineage from GenImage and WildFake by explicitly integrating 20 closed-source commercial engines that prior benchmarks lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The systematic documentation of catastrophic SOTA failure (near-random accuracy on modern closed-source generators like Nano Banana Pro) exposes a critical blind spot in the field's evaluation practices, and Treasure provides a much-needed stress-test environment. The 20-stage sequential adaptation results suggest practical viability for deployment, but the reliance on a replay buffer and the focus on full-image generation (not localized editing or face-swapping) limit immediate applicability. Open questions include whether replay-free adaptation mechanisms can achieve similar retention, whether the subspace routing framework extends to video or multimodal generation, and how the method performs under adversarial post-processing specifically designed to evade frequency-domain cues.
