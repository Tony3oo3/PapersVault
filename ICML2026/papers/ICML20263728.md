## Problem

In knowledge distillation for small language models (SLMs), practitioners assume that reasoning traces with higher reward-model scores provide more useful supervision. This paper identifies the Quality-Utility Paradox: data refined or synthesized by a stronger Oracle consistently receives higher perceived quality scores yet yields inferior downstream mathematical reasoning performance compared to traces the SLM generates itself via rejection sampling, across Qwen2.5, LLaMA-3, and DeepSeek families.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes ~34,000 math problems (filtered from 100,000 NuminaMath CoT problems via rejection sampling with N=8, T=1.0 on Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B) and constructs four parallel training datasets on the identical problem set: SLM-RFT (native SLM solutions with a conservative artifact-stripping filter), Oracle-Refined (GPT-5.2 surgical repair of SLM traces), Oracle-Synthesized (GPT-5.2 from-scratch solutions), and NuminaMath Subset (original ground-truth CoT). The output is fine-tuned SLMs (1.5B–7B) evaluated via Avg@16 zero-shot CoT accuracy (T=1.0, max 4096 tokens) on MATH500, AIME 2024, AMC 2023, Minerva Math, and OlympiadBench, with perplexity and token-distribution analyses as intermediate diagnostics.

## Method

The paper's core mechanism analysis shows that Oracle refinement couples logical repair with 'Syntactic Compaction'—replacing the SLM's spaced natural scaffolding tokens (e.g., ' \ ', ' =', ' the') with dense non-spaced operators (raw '\'), increasing the target model's perplexity from 1.52 (SLM-RFT) to 1.85 (Oracle-Refined) and raising adaptation cost throughout all reasoning segments. To test this causal hypothesis, the authors introduce Style-Aligned Refinement, a prompt-based intervention that instructs the Oracle to correct logical errors while strictly emulating the SLM's native linguistic style, preserving trial-and-error steps and structural scaffolding. This intervention reduces perplexity below the native baseline (1.46 for Qwen-aligned) and achieves the highest downstream accuracy (39.12 Avg@16), surpassing both SLM-RFT (37.06) and Oracle-Refined (34.06), while receiving the lowest reward-model score (1.37). The mechanism is validated across two training paradigms (SFT and Dynamic Fine-Tuning), multiple model scales, and hyperparameter settings, with cross-model reward evaluation confirming that the perceived-quality ranking is not evaluator-specific.

## Results

Under DFT training on Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B, SLM-RFT data achieves the highest Avg@16 accuracy of 37.06% across MATH500, AIME24, AMC23, Minerva, and OlympiadBench, despite receiving the lowest reward-model score (1.47) among all four data sources. Oracle-Synthesized data receives the highest perceived quality (1.88) but yields only 30.02% Avg@16, a 7-point gap. The Style-Aligned (Qwen) intervention further improves to 39.12% Avg@16 while maintaining the lowest perplexity (1.46) and lowest reward score (1.37), confirming that distributional compatibility with the learner outweighs perceived data quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four data construction strategies on an identical ~34K problem set: Oracle-Synthesized (GPT-5.2 generates solutions from scratch), NuminaMath Subset (ground-truth CoT retrieved from source), Oracle-Refined (GPT-5.2 surgically repairs SLM-generated traces), and SLM-RFT (SLM's own correct traces via rejection sampling). Under DFT, SLM-RFT (37.06%) outperforms Oracle-Refined (34.06%) by 3 points and Oracle-Synthesized (30.02%) by 7 points, despite having the lowest reward score. The Style-Aligned (Qwen) intervention, which preserves the SLM's native style while retaining Oracle logical repair, achieves the best result at 39.12%, surpassing even the native SLM-RFT baseline by 2 points. The Style-Aligned (GPT-5.2) variant also improves over standard Oracle-Refined (38.21% vs. 34.06%), supporting the mechanism that reducing stylistic drift restores utility. Cross-model validation with three independent reward models (Qwen2.5-Math-RM, Skywork-RM-27B, Nemotron-70B-RM) confirms that SLM-RFT and Style-Aligned (Qwen) are consistently ranked in the lower tiers for perceived quality while achieving the highest downstream utility.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of knowledge distillation, on-policy distillation, and rejection sampling fine-tuning, but carves new ground by identifying a data-construction-level phenomenon rather than proposing a new training algorithm. It connects to the on-policy distillation literature's concern about teacher-student distribution mismatch (Agarwal et al., 2024; Xu et al., 2025) and to recent work questioning whether stronger teachers always produce better students (Li et al., 2025; Bansal et al., 2025), but isolates the mechanism through a controlled design where the only variable is whether an Oracle rewrites the SLM's own trace. The Style-Aligned Refinement intervention repurposes the idea of distributional compatibility from algorithmic OPD into a prompt-based data curation strategy, bridging the gap between logical quality and learner compatibility without changing the training objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that reward-model scores systematically misrank training data for SLMs challenges a widely-adopted practice in the distillation pipeline and suggests that current data selection criteria conflate two orthogonal properties (logical correctness and distributional compatibility). The Style-Aligned Refinement provides a simple, prompt-based fix that practitioners can adopt immediately, and the perplexity-based diagnostic offers a practical alternative metric. However, the evidence is confined to the SLM regime (≤7B) and mathematical reasoning; whether the same tradeoff holds for larger models, non-mathematical domains, or mixed-task instruction tuning remains open. Future work on learner-aware reward models or automated style transfer could make the principle more broadly applicable.
