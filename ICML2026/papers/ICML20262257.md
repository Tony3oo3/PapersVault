## Problem

Deep neural networks store knowledge in globally entangled parameters, making it intractable to identify the specific causal substrate responsible for a given memory. While neuroscientific engram criteria (specificity, reactivation, sufficiency, necessity) define what a memory trace should satisfy, no prior method translates these biological constraints into a scalable, closed-form estimator that isolates individual memory traces from the entangled weight space without iterative optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262257/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a trained model's weight matrices W, a target input set X+ (e.g., cat images for the 'cat' concept), and a reference input set X− (all other classes), requiring only a single forward pass to accumulate uncentered covariance matrices Σ+ = X+X+ᵀ and Σ− = X−X−ᵀ per layer. It outputs a layer-wise engram W+ = W Σ+(Σ+ + Σ−)†, which is a block-diagonal spectral projection of the full weight vector; the engram can then be subtracted (erasure), scaled (continuous control), or linearly combined across concepts (compositional arithmetic) to produce modified model weights without any backpropagation.

## Method

The paper formalizes four neuroscientific engram axioms as algebraic constraints on the parameter space, yielding a constrained optimization problem whose Lagrangian/KKT solution gives the closed-form estimator W+ = W Σ+(Σ+ + Σ−)†, where the Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse ensures minimum-norm recovery under rank deficiency. A key theoretical result shows this estimator is equivalent to the minimum-norm projection under the Fisher information metric (via K-FAC approximation with isotropic output curvature), meaning engram ablation corresponds to a unit-step natural gradient descent on a forgetting objective. The block-diagonal structure of the spectral projector decouples layers, enabling parallel computation in a single forward pass with O(d²) memory per layer rather than O(Nd). Because each concept's engram is computed independently and the projectors are linear, any subset of n concepts can be composed or erased via simple vector arithmetic, reducing combinatorial unlearning from O(2ⁿ · T_unlearn) to O(n · T_stat) pre-computation.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (ResNet-18), Engram with the best α achieves a Tug-of-War score of 0.984 (vs. 0.999 for retraining from scratch), outperforming all iterative baselines including Fine-tune (0.952), NegGrad+ (0.936), and SalUn (0.878). On CIFAR-100 (ResNet-50), Engram(α=1) reaches ToW 0.988, exceeding the retrain baseline of 0.985. On the LLM benchmark (Llama-3.2-1B, TOFU forget10), Engram with adaptive W-Norm scaling achieves Overall 0.818 and Exact Memorization 0.028, dominating closed-form baselines UCE (Overall 0.659) and Task Arithmetic (Overall 0.584) while requiring ~30× fewer FLOPs than gradient-based methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On CIFAR-10/100, Engram decisively outperforms all six iterative unlearning baselines on the ToW metric: it beats Fine-tune (Warnecke, 0.952), NegGrad (Thudi, 0.711), NegGrad+ (Kurmanji, 0.936), l1-Sparse (Jia, 0.956), Random Label (Golatkar, 0.916), and SalUn (Fan, 0.878) by margins of 0.03–0.27. On the LLM TOFU benchmark, Engram(α_W-Norm) wins on Exact Memorization (0.028) against every gradient-based baseline—GradDiff (0.718), IdkNLL (0.621), Undial (0.740), RMU (0.295), AltPO (0.095), NPO (0.095), SimNPO (0.104), and IdkDPO (0.116)—but trails the top iterative methods AltPO (Overall 0.955), SimNPO (0.945), and NPO (0.944) on the composite Overall score, reflecting its single-pass, gradient-free design. Among closed-form editing methods, Engram dominates both UCE (Gandikota) and Task Arithmetic (Ilharco) on Overall (0.818 vs. 0.659 and 0.584), Memorization (0.963 vs. 0.901 and 0.900), and Privacy (0.645 vs. 0.418 and 0.392), while UCE retains a slight edge on Utility (0.951 vs. 0.926). The paper positions Engram as occupying a distinct compute-accuracy frontier point: ~30× fewer FLOPs and ~2.3× less peak memory than gradient-based unlearning, with wall-clock times of ~2 minutes versus tens of minutes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the linearity assumption from Task Arithmetic (Ilharco et al., 2023) and the closed-form editing paradigm of UCE (Gandikota et al., 2024), but derives the projection operator from a fundamentally different starting point: four neuroscientific engram criteria (specificity, reactivation, sufficiency, necessity) formalized as algebraic constraints. Unlike Task Arithmetic's delta-weight construction or UCE's uniform λI damping, the resulting spectral projector P⁺ = Σ⁺(Σ⁺+Σ⁻)† emerges from a Lagrangian optimization with a null-space constraint, and the paper's key theoretical contribution is showing this operator coincides with a minimum-norm projection under the Fisher information metric (K-FAC approximation). The work also departs from prior parameter-decomposition methods (APD, SPD) by yielding a unique, one-shot solution rather than non-unique or iteratively-optimized subcomponents.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Fisher–natural-gradient equivalence provides a principled geometric justification for why linear spectral arithmetic can isolate memories, moving the field from heuristic attribution toward a constrained inverse problem with a closed-form solution. The empirical discovery that LLM memory traces concentrate in Q/K and MLP-Gate projections (validated by layer-type ablation) offers a new structural interpretability lens for Transformer architectures. However, the method still trails iterative unlearning (AltPO, SimNPO) on Overall and Privacy metrics for LLMs, and the tabula-rasa assumption (ΔW ≈ W) and isotropic curvature approximation limit the generality of the Fisher equivalence. Open questions include whether the linear theory extends to continual/sequential learning settings and whether the W-Norm localization pattern holds across model families and training regimes.
