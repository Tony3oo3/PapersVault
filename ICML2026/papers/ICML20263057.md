## Problem

CVaR policy gradient (CVaR-PG) in risk-averse RL suffers from severe sample inefficiency: only the α-fraction of worst-performing trajectories contributes to the gradient, the remaining 1−α portion is discarded, and good-performing trajectories provide no learning signal (termed 'blindness to success'). Additionally, when the return distribution has a flat left tail, the gradient can vanish entirely. The paper asks whether augmenting CVaR-PG with a quantile (VaR) gradient component—motivated by the identity CVaR = E[VaR_β | β ~ U[0,α]]—can recover the discarded data and accelerate convergence.

## Input / Output

The method consumes state observations from a standard MDP environment (discrete or continuous action spaces) and produces two outputs: a Markovian policy π̄(s) mapping states to action distributions, and a quantile value function v(s, α) parameterized as a deep network that outputs I quantile values at uniformly discretized levels {½((i−1)/I + i/I)}_{i=1}^I. To enforce monotonicity in the quantile level, the network's first output serves as a base value and the remaining I−1 outputs are interpreted as non-negative increments via a softplus activation, preventing the quantile-crossing issue common in distributional RL. The policy is updated on-policy after collecting N=20 trajectories per iteration, with the initial quantile level α₀ drawn from U[0, α] and subsequent levels determined by tracking cumulative rewards.

## Method

The core contribution is a new Bellman operator for the state-only quantile value function (Eq. 9) that, unlike the Q-learning-style update in Hau et al. (2025), is directly amenable to actor-critic policy gradients with stochastic policies. The paper then proves (Proposition 3.3) that the optimal Markovian VaR policy can be recovered from its own quantile value function by tracking cumulative rewards and selecting actions via argmax_b q^{π̄*}(s, α, b), yielding a Markovian VaR-PG with advantage A̅(s_t, α_t, a_t) = E[∂l_{ε,α_t}^κ(δ_v)] estimated via multi-step GAE. The final algorithm (CVaR-VaR) computes the policy gradient as a weighted sum: (1−ω)·CVaR-PG gradient (using only the α worst trajectories) + ω·VaR-PG gradient (using all trajectories with quantile-regression-based advantages), where ω can be decayed to zero so the method reduces to standard CVaR-PG. The quantile value function is updated via quantile regression (identical to QR-DQN), and the two gradient components are combined per-iteration, allowing the VaR component to provide a dense, all-data learning signal that overcomes CVaR-PG's blindness to success.

## Results

The paper evaluates on three domains (Maze, LunarLander, InvertedPendulum) using learning curves rather than final numerical tables. In Maze, CVaR-VaR rapidly converges to a stable risk-averse (long-path) policy, while CVaR-PG fails entirely due to vanishing gradients from a flat left tail and PCVaR-PG ultimately fails. In LunarLander, CVaR-VaR achieves the highest 0.2-CVaR of return and approaches a left-landing rate close to 1, outperforming RET-CAP which does not consistently converge to the risk-averse policy across seeds. In InvertedPendulum, CVaR-VaR achieves both high return and strong risk aversion (high X < 0.01 rate), while PCVaR-PG and RET-CAP achieve high returns but fail to consistently avoid the noisy reward region.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CVaR-PG (Tamar et al., 2015) is the primary baseline and is outperformed by CVaR-VaR in all three domains: it fails completely in Maze due to vanishing gradients, learns slowly in InvertedPendulum, and is surpassed in return and risk-averse rate in LunarLander. PCVaR-PG (Kim & Min, 2024) fails to learn a reasonable policy in both Maze and LunarLander, and in InvertedPendulum achieves high returns but fails to consistently avoid the noisy reward region. RET-CAP (Mead et al., 2025) achieves high returns in all domains but does not consistently converge to the risk-averse policy in Maze or LunarLander, and in InvertedPendulum also fails to consistently avoid the noisy region. MIX (Luo et al., 2024) provides only marginal improvement over CVaR-PG in LunarLander and accelerates learning in InvertedPendulum but remains slower than CVaR-VaR and RET-CAP. REINFORCE with baseline (Sutton et al., 1998) serves as a risk-neutral reference and is not the focus of the comparison. Overall, CVaR-VaR is the only method that consistently achieves both high return and strong risk-averse behavior across all three domains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the nested VaR Bellman equation and Q-learning framework of Hau et al. (2025), deriving a new state-only Bellman operator that enables actor-critic updates with stochastic policies. It augments the standard trajectory-based CVaR-PG of Tamar et al. (2015) with a VaR-PG component, departing from concurrent sample-efficiency approaches (PCVaR-PG, RET-CAP, MIX) that reformulate the CVaR objective into per-step rewards. The key conceptual move is exploiting the identity CVaR = E[VaR over the tail] to add a separate quantile-gradient signal that leverages dynamic programming and uses all sampled data, rather than discarding the 1−α fraction of trajectories.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a well-recognized practical bottleneck in risk-averse RL: CVaR-PG's sample inefficiency and blindness to success, which can cause complete training failure (vanishing gradients) in domains with flat left-tail return distributions. The open question of whether the Markovian VaR algorithm converges from arbitrary initialization limits theoretical guarantees, and the empirical validation, while consistent across three domains, is confined to relatively simple environments. The method is modular and could potentially be combined with other sample-efficiency techniques (cross-entropy sampling, return capping), suggesting a productive direction for future work in risk-averse policy optimization.
