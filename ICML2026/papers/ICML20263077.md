## Problem

Finetuning pretrained diffusion policies via reinforcement learning suffers from poor sample efficiency because online experience collection uses naive single-action sampling that neither balances action quality with uncertainty reduction nor avoids redundant exploration across parallel agents. The paper asks how to design a principled exploration strategy that leverages the diffusion policy's multimodal structure and critic ensembles to select high-value, high-uncertainty actions, and how to extend this to collaborative fleet-level exploration.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263077/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes robot proprioception and RGB camera views (from all available camera views in DexMimicGen tasks) as conditioning for the diffusion policy, which generates M candidate action sequences (horizon 4–16 depending on task). At each timestep, the output is a single selected action from the candidate set, chosen by maximizing fleet-normalized exploration interest. The critic ensemble (size 10) and fleet statistics (N=4 agents) are auxiliary inputs consumed only during online inference for action selection; no additional components are needed at test-time evaluation.

## Method

DF-ExpEnse performs three steps at each online inference timestep: (1) it samples M candidate actions from the current diffusion policy, exploiting its multimodal distribution to filter the continuous action space into a tractable, diverse candidate set; (2) it scores each candidate's exploration interest as a min-value UCB term — the minimum Q-value across a K-sized critic ensemble plus α times the ensemble's standard deviation — to balance quality against uncertainty; (3) in fleet settings, it z-normalizes both the value and disagreement terms across all N×M candidates from all agents before selection, enabling group-aware collaborative exploration. A Behavior Cloning Sampling Regularization (BC-SR) component injects p samples from the frozen initial pretrained policy into the candidate set to prevent multimodal collapse during finetuning. The method is plug-and-play: it only modifies the action selection step during online data collection while leaving the underlying RL optimizer (DSRL's SAC noise selector or ResFiT's residual policy) unchanged.

## Results

The paper reports results as learning curves (success rate or reward score vs. environment timesteps) rather than single-point numerical metrics, averaged over 100 evaluation rollouts and three random seeds. DF-ExpEnse consistently achieves higher success rates on RoboMimic (Lift, Can, Square, Tool Hang) and DexMimicGen (Can Sort, Box Cleanup, Coffee) tasks, and higher reward scores on OpenAI Gym (HalfCheetah-v2, Hopper-v2, Walker2D-v2) tasks at any given number of collected timesteps compared to all baselines. The most pronounced gains appear on harder tasks (Tool Hang, Can, Square) where the pretrained policy's initial performance is lower and exploration is more productive. No single-point numerical comparisons are provided in the text; all quantitative evidence is in Figures 2 and 3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DF-ExpEnse outperforms all five baselines across every task and environment tested. Against vanilla DSRL (the underlying RL algorithm without exploration enhancement), DF-ExpEnse achieves consistently higher success/reward curves, with the largest gaps on harder RoboMimic tasks (Can, Square, Tool Hang) and OpenAI Gym locomotion tasks. Against Max-Q (greedy value-based selection, equivalent to DF-ExpEnse with α=0), DF-ExpEnse wins on all tasks; notably, Max-Q actually underperforms even vanilla DSRL on all RoboMimic manipulation tasks, suggesting pure exploitation causes mode collapse. Against E3B-State and E3B-DINO (which require an additional learned dynamics model), DF-ExpEnse wins on all tasks and does not depend on state representation quality; E3B-State performs the same as or worse than E3B-DINO, highlighting sensitivity to encoding choice. Against Plan2Explore (which uses an ensemble of one-step dynamics models), DF-ExpEnse again wins across all tasks without needing extra model components. Against vanilla ResFiT on DexMimicGen bimanual tasks, DF-ExpEnse+ResFiT achieves higher success rates, confirming the method's generality across different RL finetuning strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

DF-ExpEnse sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) RL finetuning of pretrained diffusion policies (DSRL, ResFiT, DPPO), (2) UCB-style exploration via critic ensembles (Chen et al., 2017), and (3) fleet-based parallelized RL (Rudin et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2024). The key conceptual departure is that prior UCB ensemble methods assume discrete action spaces, while prior fleet learning focuses on data merging or policy aggregation rather than real-time collaborative exploration. DF-ExpEnse bridges these gaps by using the diffusion policy's multimodal sampling to create a tractable candidate set for UCB evaluation in continuous action spaces, and by introducing cross-fleet normalization of exploration interest to enable group-aware action selection. The BC-SR component draws an analogy to BC regularization in offline-to-online RL but applies it at inference time over the candidate set rather than as a training loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Sample efficiency in finetuning pretrained generative policies is a practical bottleneck for real-world robotic deployment, and DF-ExpEnse offers a drop-in exploration improvement that requires no additional model components beyond the critic ensemble already needed for policy gradient optimization. The fleet normalization mechanism opens a direction for collaborative exploration that is underexplored as parallelized simulators and real-world robotic fleets scale. Open questions include whether exploration at every timestep is optimal (the authors note it may be wasteful in mastered sub-behaviors), whether fleet communication is practical under real-world latency constraints, and whether the approach extends to settings where the base policy is not a diffusion model. The method's generality across DSRL and ResFiT suggests broad applicability, but all experiments remain in simulation.
