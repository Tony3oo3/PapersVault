## Problem

Group-based RL methods like GRPO rely on within-group reward variance to compute advantages, but absolute scoring creates a structural bottleneck: binary verifiers produce zero-variance groups (all correct or all incorrect) that yield no gradient signal, while scalar reward models generate unbounded scores that drift during training and destabilize group-normalized advantage estimation. The paper asks whether replacing absolute reward magnitudes with relative intra-group rankings can resolve both signal sparsity and score instability simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text prompt and a group of G responses sampled from the current policy, where G is a multiple of the Ranking RM's sortable subset size n (default n=4, G=8). The Ranking RM processes n responses simultaneously as a collective input and outputs a relative permutation (ranking) rather than independent scalar scores; responses are partitioned into G/n subgroups for this listwise evaluation. The output is an updated LLM policy obtained by maximizing the GRPO clipped surrogate objective, with advantages derived from bounded relative rewards rather than raw scalar scores.

## Method

RLRR replaces absolute reward values with intra-group relative rankings through two shaping strategies: Hybrid Relative Reward (HRR) adds a bounded tanh-based rank correction to binary rule-based scores for verifiable tasks, while Pure Relative Reward (PRR) linearly maps ranks to [0,1] for open-ended tasks lacking ground truth. A novel Ranking Reward Model, trained with cross-entropy on ranking permutations, accepts n responses as a collective input and directly outputs their relative ordering, avoiding the positional and magnitude instabilities of pointwise scalar scoring. A hierarchical re-ranking step lexicographically sorts responses by (correctness, length bin, raw rank) to enforce that correct solutions always outrank incorrect ones and to counteract verbosity bias. Correctness-aware advantage clipping prevents the policy from being penalized for generating valid but suboptimal responses. Theoretically, Popoviciu's inequality guarantees that the bounded reward support caps variance at (b−a)²/4, providing a formal stability guarantee absent from unbounded SRM scores.

## Results

On the DeepSeek-LLaMA-8B model, RLRR(H) achieves the best average score of 65.9 on mathematical reasoning (vs. 64.2 for DAPO, 63.0 for GPG/Dr.GRPO, and 62.9 for GRPO) and 64.8 on logic reasoning (vs. 63.3 for DAPO, 54.3 for GRPO). On the smaller DeepSeek-Qwen-1.5B model, RLRR(H) reaches 55.4 math average and 24.8 logic average, outperforming all baselines by 1.2–2.2 points on math and 7.2–12.2 points on logic. RLRR also achieves the lowest average token length (5,025 tokens for 8B, 6,484 for 1.5B), indicating more efficient reasoning. On Writing-Bench, the Ranking RM achieves an overall score of 81.33, substantially exceeding Skywork-8B (72.88) and URM-8B (73.12).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RLRR(H) outperforms all four GRPO-family baselines on the 8B model: it beats DAPO (the strongest baseline) by 1.7 points on math average (65.9 vs. 64.2) and 1.5 on logic (64.8 vs. 63.3), while also reducing token length by 14% (5,025 vs. 5,871). Against GRPO, Dr.GRPO, and GPG, the math average gains are 3.0, 2.9, and 2.9 points respectively, with logic average gains of 10.5, 15.8, and 13.1 points. On the 1.5B model, RLRR(H) leads DAPO by 1.4 math points and 7.2 logic points. For open-ended writing, applying PRR on top of Skywork-8B improves the overall Writing-Bench score from 72.88 to 73.64 (+0.76), and on URM-8B from 73.12 to 74.71 (+1.59); the dedicated Ranking RM achieves 81.33, a 7.2–8.2 point gain over both SRMs. RLRR also generalizes to CISPO and RLOO, yielding +1.0 average improvement on both algorithms across five math benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GRPO lineage (itself a memory-efficient PPO variant from DeepSeekMath) by identifying a structural mismatch: GRPO's advantage estimation is inherently a within-group relative comparison, yet the reward signals fed into it are absolute (binary rule-based scores or unbounded SRM scalars). It departs from DAPO and GRESO, which address the resulting zero-variance problem by discarding uninformative prompts, by instead extracting fine-grained signal from those same prompts via intra-group ranking. The Ranking Reward Model introduces a listwise preference formulation that contrasts with the pointwise Bradley-Terry scoring of mainstream SRMs (Skywork-Reward-V2, URM), and the theoretical analysis in Appendix B formalizes why the BT objective's gradient structure drives score divergence, grounding the design choice in Popoviciu's variance bound.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a principled, drop-in replacement for absolute reward shaping in any group-based RL algorithm (validated on GRPO, CISPO, and RLOO), with the practical benefit of 100% effective data utilization versus ~48% for rule-based verifiers and lower GPU-hour cost than SRM-based pipelines. A key open limitation is that the Ranking RM's input context grows linearly with group size, constraining scalability to very large groups or long responses; the authors note future work on more efficient listwise ranking mechanisms. The finding that merely converting existing SRM scores into relative rankings (without training a new model) already yields substantial gains suggests the relative-reward paradigm may be broadly applicable beyond the specific Ranking RM architecture proposed here.
