## Problem

On-premise small expert models typically stop at supervised fine-tuning and never reach the RL alignment stage, because sustainable human preference labeling is costly and training/maintaining a reliable reward model is impractical under privacy and budget constraints. The paper asks whether the preference-optimization capability of RL can be distilled from a black-box large model into a locally trainable small model without any human preference labels or reward model, reducing teacher dependence from O(NK) candidate evaluations to O(N) one-anchor-per-prompt queries.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an unlabeled prompt pool (text prompts or image+question pairs), a black-box teacher accessible only via generation (queried exactly once per prompt to produce a single anchor response), and an SFT-initialized student model that locally samples K candidate responses per prompt. The output is a preference-optimized student policy; the intermediate training signal is a soft preference label distribution D_x over the K-candidate group, constructed entirely from local anchor-conditioned self-evaluation scores without any external judge or reward model call.

## Method

The pipeline has two stages: Stage I performs standard SFT on teacher-generated responses to warm-start the student; Stage II (RLCD) queries the teacher once per prompt for an anchor a, samples K student candidates, and uses the SFT-initialized student as its own anchor-conditioned evaluator to score each candidate y_k via g_θ(x, a, y_k), calibrated against the anchor self-score s* = g_θ(x, a, a) to yield margins r_k. A PU-aware soft label distribution D_x(k) ∝ σ(γr_k)·exp(r_k/τ) is then constructed, treating the single anchor as a positive seed and all candidates as unlabeled data that may contain latent positives. The student is updated via LDL-GRPO, which minimizes KL(D_x ‖ q_θ) (where q_θ is the group-normalized policy likelihood) plus a KL penalty to the frozen SFT reference, or alternatively via AnchorRank-DPO on induced pairwise preferences. Two theorems guarantee that D_x is strictly order-preserving and concentrates on near-optimal candidates with a gap bounded by τ log K, justifying stable group-level preference optimization without explicit rewards.

## Results

LDL-GRPO achieves the best or second-best Raw and LC win rate against GPT-4o across all six tasks and both backbone groups. On the strongest backbone (LLaMA3-8B), it reaches 0.834 Raw / 0.623 LC on PBFG creative writing, 0.724 / 0.709 on Geometry math, and 0.432 / 0.417 on A-OKVQA-RG multimodal rationale generation. Compared to the SFT-only baseline, LDL-GRPO gains 5–35 percentage points in Raw win rate depending on task and backbone, with the largest gains on creative writing (e.g., +0.352 on PBFG/LLaMA3-8B) and multimodal tasks (e.g., +0.181 on A-OKVQA-RG/LLaVA-7B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LDL-GRPO outperforms all three main baselines on every task and backbone. Against SinglePair-DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023), LDL-GRPO gains 9.6–24.1 points in Raw win rate (e.g., 0.834 vs. 0.683 on PBFG/LLaMA3-8B; 0.724 vs. 0.457 on Geometry/LLaMA3-8B), confirming that anchor-induced multi-candidate preferences provide substantially richer supervision than a single teacher-vs-student pair. Against Anchor-GRPO (Guo et al., 2025), LDL-GRPO wins by 1.0–10.5 points (e.g., 0.724 vs. 0.628 on Geometry; 0.236 vs. 0.146 on A-OKVQA-MC/Qwen2.5-VL-7B), with the largest gap on multimodal tasks where Anchor-GRPO's scalar-reward formulation is most sensitive to evaluation noise. Against Self-PPO (Schulman et al., 2017), LDL-GRPO leads by 4.0–15.0 points (e.g., 0.683 vs. 0.628 on PBFG; 0.432 vs. 0.382 on A-OKVQA-RG/LLaVA-7B), showing that the teacher anchor provides a critical quality reference that purely self-rewarded PPO lacks. Notably, SFT→SFT underperforms plain SFT on most tasks, validating that additional imitation without preference signals is counterproductive.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of knowledge distillation, preference-based alignment (DPO/GRPO), and positive-unlabeled learning. It departs from standard KD, which transfers static response quality via SFT imitation, by explicitly targeting the distillation of the teacher's preference-optimization capability (formalized as RLCD). It also departs from teacher-as-judge RL, which requires O(NK) teacher calls to score all student candidates, by reducing teacher dependence to O(N) single-anchor queries and replacing external scoring with anchor-conditioned local self-evaluation. The LDL-GRPO objective builds directly on GRPO's group-relative structure and label-distribution learning, while the PU calibration mechanism draws on the non-negative risk estimator literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for on-premise and edge deployment: a single teacher anchor per prompt (O(N) calls) suffices to drive a full SFT-to-RL alignment loop without human preference labels, reward-model training, or repeated teacher scoring, making RL alignment feasible under strict cost and privacy constraints. Key limitations include dependence on the student's self-evaluation quality (which degrades for weak students or fine-grained judgment tasks) and the additional computation from sampling K candidates per prompt. Open questions include whether more robust self-evaluation mechanisms can further reduce noise, whether the required group size K can be shrunk without sacrificing stability, and whether the approach extends to multi-turn or agentic settings.
