## Problem

Existing multimodal grounding benchmarks evaluate only simplified observation-based scenarios or a single modality, leaving a gap in assessing whether MLLMs can attribute individual factual claims to precise temporal segments across heterogeneous sources (video, audio, figures) when multi-step reasoning is required. The paper identifies that even state-of-the-art MLLMs frequently hallucinate citations while producing correct answers, and that no scalable evaluation protocol exists to measure this fact-level attribution quality in complex multimodal reasoning settings.

## Input / Output

The model receives a text question alongside multimodal inputs (video clips, audio streams, and figures/graphs) and must produce a long-form response where each verifiable sentence carries explicit citations specifying both the modality and a timestamped segment (e.g., 'audio, 0:42–0:46'). The evaluation pipeline then decomposes the response into verifiable sentences, further into atomic facts with propagated citation sets, and scores each fact-citation pair for entailment, yielding a single MURGAT-SCORE defined as Coverage × Attribution F1. Unlike prior video grounding tasks where the target segment is given in the prompt, the model must self-select which segments support each claim.

## Method

MURGAT-SCORE is a three-stage automatic evaluation pipeline: (1) verifiable claim identification uses an LLM verifier to separate observable claims from reasoning statements at the sentence level; (2) atomic fact decomposition applies explicit decontextualization followed by sentence-level fact splitting with citation propagation to each atomic fact; (3) attribution quality performs two-sided citation verification—recall checks whether the union of cited segments fully entails the fact, and precision checks whether each individual citation is strictly necessary. The pipeline assigns different Gemini models to each subtask (Gemini-3-Pro for verifiability, Gemini-3-Flash for decomposition, Gemini-2.5-Flash for multimodal entailment), achieving a Pearson r of 0.86 with human judgments. The paper further extends program-aided generation to the multimodal setting, exploring logic-centric vs. narrative-centric reasoning paradigms and planner-defined vs. executor-discovered grounding mechanisms, finding that imperative (retrieval-tool-based) grounding improves attribution by ~9.6 points on average but costs ~7.4 points in answer accuracy.

## Results

On WorldSense, Gemini-3-Flash with Post-hoc Attribution achieves the highest MURGAT-SCORE of 69.2 (Coverage 95.1, Attribution 71.4), while on Video-MMMU, Gemini-3-Flash with +Citation leads at 56.9 (Coverage 88.2, Attribution 64.5). Even the best models fail to ground roughly 30–35% of their claims, and a clear accuracy–grounding gap emerges: Gemini-3-Pro matches Gemini-3-Flash's 86.0% accuracy on Video-MMMU but scores only 41.8 MURGAT-S versus 56.9. Program-aided generation (Logic Imperative) improves MURGAT-S by +9.6 points over BASE+CITATION on WorldSense (76.4 vs. 64.4) but reduces accuracy by 7.4 points on average, confirming a fundamental trade-off between verifiable attribution and reasoning flexibility.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the five omni-modal models, Gemini-3-Flash dominates on attribution quality: its +Citation MURGAT-S of 56.9 on Video-MMMU exceeds Gemini-3-Pro (41.8) by 15.1 points and Qwen3-Omni-Thinking (4.8) by 52.1 points, despite all three achieving comparable or higher QA accuracy. Gemini-3-Pro achieves the highest accuracy on both datasets (71.4% WorldSense, 86.0% Video-MMMU) but consistently trails Gemini-3-Flash on MURGAT-S, illustrating that stronger reasoning does not translate to better grounding. Qwen3-Omni-Thinking exhibits a unique failure mode: citations boost its accuracy by +9.0% on Video-MMMU (51.0→60.0 with Qwen3-VL-Thinking) yet it produces invalid timestamp formats, collapsing its +Citation MURGAT-S to 4.8; Post-hoc Attribution recovers this to 12.8. Vision-language-only models (Qwen3-VL, Molmo2) score competitively on WorldSense attribution (Qwen3-VL-Instruct Post-hoc: 69.4) but hallucinate audio citations comprising up to 31.6% of their references despite lacking an audio encoder. Post-hoc Attribution universally raises Coverage (often >95%) but its effect on Attribution F1 is domain-dependent: it improves MURGAT-S on recognition-heavy WorldSense (e.g., Gemini-3-Pro 51.7→65.2) yet degrades it on reasoning-heavy Video-MMMU (e.g., Gemini-2.5-Flash 41.5→38.0), where forced alignment of abstract reasoning steps to random segments creates false positives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the text attribution evaluation pipeline (FActScore-style atomic fact decomposition, Gao et al.'s citation precision/recall protocol, Liu et al.'s verifiability filtering) to the multimodal setting with fine-grained temporal and per-modality citations. It departs from prior multimodal grounding work (MCiteBench, MIRAGE, video moment retrieval) in two key ways: requiring reasoning beyond direct observation rather than simple evidence retrieval, and explicitly separating verifiable claims from reasoning steps so that attribution is only scored on observable content. The programmatic grounding experiments build on ViperGPT and Generation Programs, extending the plan-then-execute paradigm to handle heterogeneous video/audio/figure inputs with a retrieval tool for temporal localization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that strong MLLMs achieve high QA accuracy while systematically hallucinating citations—exposes a critical gap between internal reasoning and verifiable grounding that existing accuracy metrics miss. The task-dependent nature of citation generation (a 'reasoning tax' on recognition tasks but a scaffold on complex reasoning) and the trade-off where forcing explicit grounding degrades reasoning accuracy are actionable insights for practitioners building grounded multimodal systems. Open questions include whether scaling test-time compute can close the reasoning-attribution gap (the paper shows diverging trends by model size), and whether the evaluation protocol generalizes beyond the two datasets used. A limitation is the small human validation set (20 inputs, 80 responses), though the automated pipeline's high correlation (r=0.84) and bootstrap stability analyses partially mitigate this concern.
