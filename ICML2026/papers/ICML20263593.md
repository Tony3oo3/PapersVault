## Problem

Linear representation methods for probing and steering AI models implicitly assume a flat (Euclidean) geometry in representation space, yet softmax-based models induce a non-Euclidean Bregman geometry where the natural notion of 'closeness' between representations is KL divergence between output distributions. This geometric mismatch causes standard Euclidean steering to leak probability mass to off-target tokens, making concept manipulation brittle and unreliable. The paper asks: what is the correct intrinsic geometry for softmax representation spaces, and how should it be exploited to make steering robust?

## Input / Output

The method consumes a context embedding vector λ from the final transformer layer (or CLIP text encoder), a linear probe direction β_W computed as either the primal or dual mean difference between base/target concept embedding sets, and the full unembedding matrix G ∈ R^{k×d} (softmax weight matrix). It produces a discrete steering path {λ_t}_{t=0}^{T} in the primal space, where each step is computed by solving a regularized linear system involving the top-K token covariance. Evaluation tracks the target concept probability P(W=1|λ_t) alongside three off-target preservation metrics (counterfactual mass, KL divergence of off-target distribution, and weighted rank differences) as functions of steering progress.

## Method

The key insight is that the KL divergence between softmax distributions is exactly the Bregman divergence induced by the log-normalizer A(λ), endowing the representation space with a dually flat geometry whose dual map is φ(λ)=∇A(λ)=E[γ|λ]. Dual steering adds the probe vector in the dual space—φ(λ_t)=φ(λ_0)+tβ_W—rather than the primal space, and Theorem 3.1 proves this is the forward-KL projection onto the concept hyperplane that minimally perturbs the off-target distribution under a concept-factorizability assumption. Practically, because the dual space Φ is a bounded convex hull (the dual coordinate must remain feasible), the method traces the corresponding nonlinear primal path via regularized Newton updates: solving (Cov[γ|λ]+αI)Δλ=εβ_W at each step, with α>0 ensuring invertibility when the softmax is concentrated and the Hessian is rank-deficient. The linear system is solved efficiently via conjugate gradient on a top-K token approximation, reducing complexity from O(kd²+d³) to O(nKd) per step.

## Results

Both Euclidean and dual steering successfully increase the target concept probability P(W=1|λ_t) to approximately 1 across all tested concepts (verb→third-person, verb→ing, verb→past, English→French on Gemma-3-4B; blue→red, dog→cat, yellow→green, carrot→broccoli on MetaCLIP-2). However, dual steering consistently outperforms Euclidean steering on all three off-target robustness metrics: it maintains higher total probability mass on counterfactual pairs, achieves lower KL divergence of off-target distributions, and produces smaller rank differences in off-target components. The advantage of dual steering grows as the target concept probability approaches 1, where Euclidean steering degrades most severely by leaking probability mass to unrelated neutral tokens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is standard Euclidean steering (λ_t = λ_0 + tβ_W), the approach established by Li et al. (2023), Turner et al. (2023), and Zou et al. (2023), which adds the linear probe vector directly in the primal space. Dual steering wins on all three robustness metrics across every concept and model tested: it preserves a higher total probability mass on counterfactual pairs (avoiding leakage to neutral tokens), achieves lower KL divergence of the off-target distribution P^Z, and produces smaller weighted rank differences among off-target components. Both methods reach the target concept probability of ~1, so the distinction is purely in off-target preservation. The advantage holds regardless of whether the Primal MD or Dual MD is used as the linear probe direction. Euclidean steering occasionally matches dual steering when the counterfactual sum remains constant (e.g., with Primal MD on certain concepts), but even in those cases the relative ranking of off-target components can shift significantly, making dual steering the more robust choice overall.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges information geometry (Amari; Banerjee et al.) with the linear representation hypothesis and model steering literature (Li et al. 2023; Turner et al. 2023; Zou et al. 2023; Rimsky et al. 2024). Prior steering methods implicitly treat the representation space as Euclidean, adding a probe vector directly to the context embedding. The key conceptual departure here is identifying the natural geometry as Bregman (dually flat) via the KL divergence between softmax distributions, revealing a 'type mismatch' in standard steering: the probe vector lives in the dual space, so adding it to a primal-space embedding is only valid under Euclidean geometry. This connects to Arvanitidis et al. (2022) on pulling back information-geometric structure to latent spaces, but focuses specifically on the softmax parameter space and its duality structure for probing and steering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled geometric explanation for why standard (Euclidean) steering leaks probability mass to off-target tokens, and offers a drop-in replacement (dual steering via regularized Newton updates) that provably minimizes off-target distribution change under a concept-factorizability assumption. Limitations include: the theory applies directly only to softmax-based output layers (not intermediate layers), the probing assumption (concept probability constant on the probe hyperplane) is not strictly satisfied in practice, and the concept-factorizability condition may fail for entangled concepts. An important open direction is understanding how the softmax-layer geometry propagates to and influences intermediate representation layers where steering is typically applied in practice.
