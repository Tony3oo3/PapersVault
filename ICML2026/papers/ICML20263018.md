## Problem

Current MLLMs exhibit geometric fragility: their visual representations remain entangled with the view-dependent pixel grid and degrade rapidly under viewpoint or viewing-distance changes. Existing approaches—whether input-level feature fusion or latent reconstruction—lack explicit mechanisms to decouple intrinsic 3D structure from extrinsic camera pose, causing spatial understanding to collapse under affine perturbations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263018/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of S multi-view video frames (resized to the first frame's spatial size, bounded between 3,136 pixels and 196 visual tokens per image) along with a text question, and produces autoregressive text outputs covering QA answers, dense scene captions, and 3D object detection boxes. A dual-path encoder processes the frames in parallel: a 2D visual encoder (Qwen2.5-VL's vision tower) yields visual tokens, while a frozen VGGT 3D encoder produces geometric features and per-frame pose features. One special <INV> token is assigned per frame, whose hidden states serve as the carrier for affine-invariant representations.

## Method

AFFIN-SPACE introduces a two-stage explicit decoupling of geometry from pose. First, an Affine Matrix Estimator predicts decomposed affine quantities (two rotations via cos/sin vectors, tilt, scale, and translation) from VGGT pose features, following the ASIFT parameterization with bounded ranges; these are composed into affine parameters (A_i, b_i) that drive a differentiable spatial resampling of 3D features onto a canonical grid before multimodal fusion. Second, within the MLLM, an orthogonal projection mechanism constructs a rank-r orthonormal basis from the affine parameters via a lightweight MLP and Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization, then projects each <INV> hidden state onto the orthogonal complement of that subspace, mathematically zeroing out pose-dependent components. A conditional reconstruction head maps the invariant representations back to the mean-pooled resampled 3D features via an L2 loss, preventing representation collapse while preserving recoverable geometric semantics. Training minimizes a joint cross-entropy (autoregressive decoding) plus reconstruction objective, with the reconstruction coefficient tuned to λ=1.0.

## Results

AFFIN-SPACE-8B achieves state-of-the-art performance across all five benchmarks: 57.7 average on VSI-Bench (vs. 57.3 for 3DThinker-SFT-8B), CIDEr 99.4 on ScanQA (vs. 91.8 for Spatial-MLLM-4B), EM 59.2 on SQA3D (vs. 58.1 for SPAR-7B), CIDEr 87.4 on Scan2Cap (vs. 84.1 for LLaVA-3D), and F1 44.5 on EmbodiedScan 6-frame (vs. 40.8 for VG-LLM-8B). The smaller 4B variant also remains competitive, reaching 53.8 on VSI-Bench and CIDEr 93.8 on ScanQA. Notably, AFFIN-SPACE-8B surpasses proprietary APIs (GPT-4o: 34.0, Gemini-1.5 Pro: 45.4) by 12–24 points on VSI-Bench average.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AFFIN-SPACE-8B outperforms all video-input baselines on VSI-Bench, beating 3DThinker-SFT-8B (57.3) by 0.4 and VG-LLM-8B (50.7) by 7.0 in average score. On ScanQA, it sets a new record among video-based methods with CIDEr 99.4, surpassing Spatial-MLLM-4B (91.8) by 7.6 and SPAR-7B (90.7) by 8.7, though it remains slightly below the 3D-input model Video-3D LLM (102.1). On SQA3D, AFFIN-SPACE-8B (EM 59.2) edges out both SPAR-7B (58.1) and Video-3D LLM (58.6) despite requiring no depth maps or point clouds. On Scan2Cap, it surpasses LLaVA-3D (CIDEr 84.1) by 3.3 and Video-3D LLM (83.8) by 3.6. On EmbodiedScan, AFFIN-SPACE-8B beats VG-LLM-8B by +3.1 F1 (4-frame) and +3.7 F1 (6-frame), while the 4B variant gains +11.4 F1 over the Qwen2.5-VL-3B backbone, confirming that explicit geometric alignment is critical for 3D localization.

## Relation to Prior Work

AFFIN-SPACE builds on the ASIFT framework (Morel & Yu, 2009) for decomposing affine transforms into physically interpretable quantities (scale, two rotations, tilt) and repurposes VGGT (Wang et al., 2025) not as a reconstruction pipeline but as a frozen 3D feature encoder whose pose outputs drive explicit geometric resampling. It departs from the two dominant paradigms in video-based spatial MLLMs—input-level feature fusion and latent reconstruction with implicit alignment—by arguing that both remain entangled with the view-dependent pixel grid. The key conceptual shift is from implicit geometric learning to explicit affine modeling: a two-stage mechanism (spatial resampling at the input, orthogonal-complement projection in the latent space) that mathematically decouples intrinsic 3D structure from extrinsic camera pose rather than hoping the network learns invariance as a side effect.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, mathematically grounded alternative to the prevailing 'fuse and hope' approach in 3D spatial MLLMs, and the ablations convincingly show that both the resampling and the projection constraints are individually necessary (removing either causes 5–11 point CIDEr drops). This matters because geometric fragility is a fundamental bottleneck for deploying spatial MLLMs in embodied and safety-critical settings where viewpoint changes are unavoidable. Open questions include whether the 2D affine model is sufficient for large-baseline-shift or perspective-dominated scenes (vs. full projective transforms), how the approach scales to longer video sequences where VGGT's per-frame pose estimation may accumulate error, and whether the orthogonal projection mechanism generalizes beyond the specific affine parameterization used here.
