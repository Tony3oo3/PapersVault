## Problem

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models generalize well to generic instructions like 'pick up the cup' but fail on personalized commands like 'bring my cup,' where the robot must identify and manipulate one specific instance among visually similar same-category distractors. Because natural language abstracts rich visual details into broad semantic categories, frozen VLAs collapse to category-level recognition and mis-ground the intended instance, and per-object fine-tuning is impractical at deployment.

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view RGB images, proprioceptive state, a language instruction containing a possessive reference (e.g., 'my cup'), and a small set of K≈5 reference photographs of the user's specific object. It produces prompted inputs for the frozen VLA: each camera view is overlaid with a semi-transparent mask-aligned tint on the grounded target region, and the instruction is rewritten to replace the personal span with a color-matched generic phrase (e.g., 'pick up my cup' → 'pick up the red cup'). The VLA then outputs standard robot actions on these modified inputs, with no parameter updates to the policy.

## Method

VAP is a training-free inference adapter that couples two components operating strictly in the input space. First, a grounding pipeline uses the reference images as a non-parametric visual memory: Grounding DINO proposes category-level bounding boxes, DINOv2 embeddings with a vote-aggregation scheme over reference views select the target instance, SAM2 refines the selection into a pixel-wise mask, and a real-time tracker propagates the mask across frames to maintain identity consistency during closed-loop execution. Second, a prompting function overlays a semi-transparent tint on the masked region and rewrites the instruction to reference the highlight color, so the mask supplies a pixel-level spatial anchor while the rewrite supplies the language token that tells the policy which anchor to attend to. The two signals act as a single binding mechanism—ablations show neither alone reliably closes the gap—and the spatio-temporal tracking is essential because static per-frame re-grounding induces identity switches that cascade into manipulation failures.

## Results

On Personalized-SIMPLER (Google Robot, 1,685 episodes), VAP boosts Correct Movement Ratio (CMR) and Success Rate (SR) from 10.5%/8.5% to 89.2%/60.3% on the pen-holder task and from 50.4%/48.6% to 83.2%/75.0% on the bottle task (Track 1: visual matching). On the WidowX platform, VAP achieves 71.3–95.0% SR across four manipulation tasks, compared to 0–31.7% for the generic π0 backbone. On Personalized-VLABench (multi-view selection), VAP reaches 89.2% on the leather-bag task versus 33.6% for π0.5, and on the real-world SO-101 benchmark it lifts selection SR from 30.0% to 80.0% and pick-and-place SR from 18.8% to 58.8%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VAP decisively outperforms the generic VLA baseline (π0/π0.5) across all four benchmarks: on the hardest task (pen-holder pick, Fractal) it improves SR from 8.5% to 60.3%, and on the WidowX pick-shaver task it goes from 0% to 71.3%. Against the Soft Prompt (token-learning) baseline adapted from Yo'LLaVA, VAP wins on every task; the token baseline achieves at most 24.0% SR on the pen-holder task versus VAP's 60.3%, and the paper's attention analysis (Appendix B.3) shows the learned token's spatial focus drifts across frames during closed-loop execution, preventing stable instance-level control. The Hard Prompt (textual description) baseline is competitive on the simpler bottle task (57.5–58.8% SR vs. VAP's 75.0%) but collapses on the pen-holder task (8.9–9.1% SR vs. 60.3%), confirming that language alone cannot resolve same-category instance ambiguity. On the real-world benchmark, VAP's 80.0% selection SR exceeds the best text-based baseline (40–45%) by roughly 35–40 points, and its 58.8% pick-and-place SR more than doubles the 18.8% of the generic policy while closing the CMR-to-SR gap that plagues all baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three threads: (1) VLA policies (π0, π0.5) that generalize to category-level commands but fail on instance-level personalization; (2) visual prompting for VLMs and robotics (MOKA, PIVOT, TraceVLA) that injects spatial/affordance cues but not identity cues; and (3) personalization in foundation models (DreamBooth, Yo'LLaVA, MyVLM) that learns concept tokens for static generation or QA but has not been applied to embodied control. VAP departs from all three by repurposing reference-image matching (from personalization) and mask-aligned highlighting (from visual prompting) as a training-free input adapter for frozen VLAs, coupled with SAM2-based temporal tracking to maintain identity across closed-loop execution. The key conceptual departure is from token-learning personalization: the paper explicitly shows that learned concept tokens, even when achieving >95% VQA recognition accuracy, fail to produce stable instance-level control in a VLA's separate action head.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that instance-level personalization of frozen VLAs is achievable through a lightweight, training-free input-side adapter, removing the need for per-object fine-tuning that would be impractical at deployment. The negative result on token learning is particularly valuable for the VLA community: it reveals that recognition-level success (correct attention heatmaps) does not translate to manipulation success when the action head is a separate flow-matching expert, suggesting a fundamental architectural bottleneck for instance-specific conditioning. Limitations include dependence on segmentation quality, multi-view inconsistency (per-view factorization can lock onto different instances), and restriction to single-object, tabletop-scale settings. Open questions include cross-view identity fusion, long-horizon multi-object personalization, and whether the mask-highlight + instruction-rewrite pairing is truly necessary or whether richer visual prompts could replace the linguistic bridge.
