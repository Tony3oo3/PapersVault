## Problem

Current MLLM-based AI-generated video detectors face a fundamental tension: pure RL on detection data (e.g., BusterX++) produces superficial reasoning focused on coarse features like lighting and environment, while task-oriented SFT with grounded CoT (e.g., Skyra) yields mechanical, template-like analysis that fails on fact-based videos such as AI parodies. The paper asks whether fine-grained perception can be sharpened through simple, label-free pretext tasks rather than through expensive domain-specific annotation, and whether this perceptual grounding can be integrated with general reasoning to achieve balanced detection across diverse video types.

## Input / Output

The model (Qwen3-VL-8B) consumes video clips sampled at 3 FPS for up to 5 seconds at native resolution, along with a text prompt asking whether the video is real or fake. It produces a free-form reasoning explanation followed by a final verdict enclosed in <answer></answer> tags. During the RL stage, the same model also processes perception pretext inputs: spatiotemporal grounding queries (requiring JSON output of time spans and per-second bounding boxes) and object counting prompts (requiring comma-separated counts of circles, squares, and triangles), with rewards computed from IoU or relative prediction error.

## Method

VIDEOVERITAS employs a two-stage training pipeline on Qwen3-VL-8B with LoRA (rank=64). Stage 1 is Joint Preference Alignment (J-DPO), which constructs preference pairs at both the response level (using an 'anti-label' strategy to trigger the base model's intrinsic hallucinations as non-preferred responses for perception-heavy videos, while inverting the preference for fact-based videos where the base model's own reasoning is preferred) and the video level (encouraging fine-grained analysis for perception videos and factual reasoning for reasoning videos). Stage 2 is Perception Pretext RL (PPRL), which interleaves simple perception tasks—spatiotemporal grounding/tracking from OneThinker and self-supervised object counting with randomized shape sizes and durations from OpenVid-1M—with AIGC detection, all optimized via Group Sequence Policy Optimization (GSPO). The key insight is that sharpening granular spatiotemporal perception through these cheap, label-free pretext tasks (5K samples) improves detection accuracy by ~11% on challenging subsets without any domain-specific artifact annotations, and the paper empirically shows this reshapes the model's reasoning toward finer component granularity, better spatiotemporal tracking, and deeper physics-based explanations.

## Results

VIDEOVERITAS achieves 88.8% average accuracy across in-domain (ID), out-of-domain (OOD), and the newly introduced MintVid benchmarks, outperforming the previous best MLLM-based detector BusterX++ (74.7%) by 14.1% and the strongest proprietary MLLM Gemini-3-Pro-Preview (77.8%) by 11.0%. On in-domain data it reaches 92.3% average, while on the challenging MintVid OOD set it achieves 79.2%. The method also transfers to image-level detection, scoring 86.4% accuracy on GenBuster++ despite being trained exclusively on video data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MLLM-based detectors, VIDEOVERITAS outperforms BusterX++ by 14.1% in average accuracy, with particularly large gains on GenBuster++ (+11.8%), LOKI (+7.2%), and perception-heavy subsets like Jimeng3.0-Pro (+9.1%); it also achieves a more balanced result on the fact-based subset where BusterX++ degrades. Skyra-RL is largely ineffective (52.5% avg), likely due to its strict 256p preprocessing biasing the model toward superficial inconsistencies. Against binary/statistical detectors (DeMamba, D3, NSG-VD, ReStraV), VIDEOVERITAS leads by 33–36% on average; these methods collapse to near-chance levels (49–55%) on modern generators despite strong scores on older datasets like Emu3. Compared to generic MLLMs, VIDEOVERITAS improves over its Qwen3-VL-8B base by 25.2% and surpasses all open-source MLLMs (Qwen3-VL-235B, GLM-4.5V, Keye-VL-1.5) by 15–25%. The only method that remains competitive on individual subsets is Gemini-3-Pro-Preview (77.8% avg), which still trails VIDEOVERITAS by 11.0% overall and shows notably weaker recall on facial and perception-heavy subsets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MLLM-based video forgery detection line initiated by Veritas (Tan et al., 2025) and positioned against two competing paradigms: SFT-based grounded CoT detectors (Skyra, DAVID-XR1) and pure-RL detectors (BusterX++). The key conceptual departure is the introduction of Perception Pretext RL (PPRL), which repurposes generic spatiotemporal grounding and self-supervised object counting as RL-stage tasks to sharpen fine-grained perception before detection, rather than directly optimizing for the AIGC classification objective. The Joint DPO cold-start stage also departs from standard preference optimization by incorporating both response-level and video-level alignments, using the base model's own hallucinations (via an 'anti-label' strategy) as non-preferred signals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that a few thousand samples of generic perception data (counting shapes, tracking objects) can yield meaningful detection gains without any domain-specific AIGC annotations, lowering the barrier for training explainable detectors. MintVid addresses a real evaluation gap by including videos from 2025-era proprietary generators (Sora2, Kling 2.5, Jimeng 3.0) and a fact-based subset that exposes the mechanical-reasoning failure mode of fine-tuned models. However, the 8B model still trails Gemini-3-Pro-Preview by ~9 points on average, and the authors acknowledge that higher-quality artifact-specific grounding annotations (which they found to degrade performance when coarse) remain an open direction for closing the gap to proprietary systems.
