## Problem

Traditional 3D visual grounding assumes that a natural language referring expression uniquely identifies a target object, but real-world human instructions are frequently ambiguous or underspecified, causing existing models to match multiple objects and produce incorrect predictions. This paper introduces a new task formulation—3D visual grounding with referential ambiguity—that explicitly allows queries to correspond to multiple candidate objects and requires interactive disambiguation to resolve the ambiguity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266257/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a 3D point cloud scene (N×D with spatial coordinates and per-point features), an intentionally ambiguous referring expression, and human-provided answers to model-generated clarifying questions across up to R=6 rounds. At each round, the model renders M=3 multi-view images per candidate object for visual encoding. The output is the uniquely identified target object's 3D bounding box, along with the sequence of discriminative clarifying questions (parameterized as structured tuples of type, slot, value over object attributes and spatial relationships) that were posed during the interaction.

## Method

The framework operates in two stages: (1) candidate generation using a fine-tuned multi-object 3D grounding backbone (M3DRef-CLIP or D-LISA) to retrieve all objects whose semantic relevance exceeds a threshold τ, and (2) interactive disambiguation that iteratively performs constraint selection and candidate filtering. Constraint selection encodes each candidate via ViT-B/32 on multi-view renders, then a selector combines a global state representation, the query embedding, per-candidate visual features, a target-centered representation, and variability cues (average and max discriminative margins) to pick the most informative constraint from a discrete space. Candidate filtering uses a cross-attention mask head where candidate features attend to the joint query-constraint embedding, producing per-object compatibility scores that prune the set via a sigmoid threshold. Training jointly optimizes four losses: grounding (detection + reference + contrastive), constraint selection (cross-entropy over the constraint inventory), candidate discrimination (cross-entropy over the candidate set), and stopping prediction (binary cross-entropy), with the key design insight being that the selector is guided by explicit target-centered variability signals to maximize information gain per interaction round.

## Results

On the Mixture ambiguity setting (real-world scenes), D-LISA+Ours achieves 41.57% final accuracy with 3.39 average interaction rounds, substantially outperforming D-LISA alone (9.81% Acc) and M3DRef-CLIP alone (36.15% Acc). The upper-bound Ours† setting (with ground-truth candidate sets) reaches 49.51% Acc, indicating that candidate generation quality is a key bottleneck. Against MLLM-based baselines, the proposed method achieves 41.57% Acc vs. 26.80% (Inst3D-LMM) and 22.35% (Video-3D LLM), while also requiring fewer rounds (3.39 vs. 4.85 and 5.24).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four baselines across two categories. Among traditional 3D grounding backbones, D-LISA (Zhang et al., 2024) suffers the most from ambiguity, dropping to 9.81% Acc on Mixture (vs. 36.15% for M3DRef-CLIP), but benefits most from the interactive framework, jumping to 41.57% Acc (+31.8 points). M3DRef-CLIP (Zhang et al., 2023) is more robust to ambiguity in its one-shot form (36.15% Acc) but still gains +3.9 points from the interactive stage (40.03% Acc). Against MLLM-based interactive baselines, the proposed method clearly wins: Inst3D-LMM (Yu et al., 2025) achieves 26.80% Acc with 4.85 average rounds, and Video-3D LLM (Zheng et al., 2025) achieves 22.35% Acc with 5.24 rounds, while Ours reaches 41.57% Acc in only 3.39 rounds—outperforming both by 14.8 and 19.2 points in accuracy while requiring ~30% fewer interaction rounds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D visual grounding lineage (ScanRefer, ReferIt3D, Multi3DRefer) by relaxing the long-standing assumption that referring expressions uniquely identify a target object, and by importing the interactive disambiguation paradigm from 2D conversational search (Visual Dialog, GuessWhat) into the 3D setting. Methodologically, it builds on M3DRef-CLIP and D-LISA as candidate-generation backbones and introduces a lightweight two-stage pipeline (ViT-B/32 multi-view encoding + cross-attention mask head) for constraint-conditioned filtering. The dataset construction leverages LLM-based semantic ablation and scene-graph-grounded clarification generation, drawing on SceneVerse-style structured representations and Qwen3-VL for scene-conditioned perception.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap: existing 3D grounding benchmarks assume unambiguous queries, yet real-world embodied and robotic instructions are frequently underspecified, making the proposed task formulation and the AmbiRefer3D benchmark (47K samples, 7.3K scenes) a useful resource for the community. However, absolute performance remains modest (best Acc ≈ 49.5% on real-world, ≈ 32.5% on synthetic), and the 40× inference latency overhead from multi-view rendering limits near-term deployment. Open questions include whether the interactive paradigm scales to outdoor or large-scale scenes, how to handle cases where the human cannot provide the needed clarification, and whether the candidate-generation bottleneck (the Ours vs. Ours† gap) can be closed without oracle access.
