## Problem

Practitioners increasingly rely on pretrained tabular foundation models like TabPFN for state-of-the-art predictions, yet these models do not return posterior uncertainty for scientific estimands θ defined through a loss function. The martingale posterior (MGP) framework could recover such posteriors from a predictive rule, but existing MGP constructions depend on handcrafted predictive rules satisfying exact martingale properties, making them incompatible with high-capacity pretrained transformers. The paper asks whether a foundation transformer can serve directly as the predictive rule in an MGP.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an observed tabular dataset of feature-response pairs z_{1:n}, a user-specified loss function ℓ(z,θ), a forward-sampling depth N (typically n+500), and a number of posterior samples L (100 in experiments). It produces L posterior samples of θ(F_∞), from which joint credible sets (empirical-cutoff ellipsoids with diagonal covariance) or marginal credible intervals are constructed. The only tuning parameter is the rollout length N; no prior, likelihood, or bandwidth hyperparameters are required, and the rollouts are embarrassingly parallel.

## Method

TabMGP instantiates the martingale posterior by using TabPFN's in-context learning as the predictive rule. Forward sampling proceeds by drawing each new covariate x_{i+1} from the empirical distribution of observed features (Bayesian bootstrap) and each new response y_{i+1} from TabPFN's conditional predictive distribution given x_{i+1} and the growing context z_{1:i}. After N total observations, the empirical risk minimiser θ(F_N) is computed as one posterior sample; repeating L times yields the approximate posterior. TabPFN is well-suited because it is row-permutation invariant (no manual averaging over orderings), trained to approximate Bayesian posterior predictive distributions, and requires no retraining or finetuning on new data. Crucially, the paper demonstrates that TabPFN's predictive rule need not satisfy the martingale or a.c.i.d. sufficient conditions for the resulting posteriors to remain empirically stable, with near-nominal coverage and tight credible sets across 30 synthetic and real-data setups.

## Results

TabMGP achieves near-nominal 95% coverage (0.99–1.00) in 6 of 7 synthetic linear regression setups with credible-set sizes of 0.33–0.51 (median trace of posterior covariance), and maintains 0.95–1.00 coverage on most real-data setups with sizes as small as 0.03 (quake) and 0.04 (energy). In logistic regression on real data, TabMGP is the most consistent method, often achieving the smallest credible sets among all baselines (e.g., 0.69 on sepsis vs. 2.42 for BB and 10.00 for Copula). The Bayesian bootstrap (BB) severely undercovers in synthetic setups (0.46–0.66) and low n/p real-data settings, while Bayes and Asymptotic produce excessively wide credible sets (size 0.65–1.31 in synthetic) due to reliance on asymptotic approximations that break down at small n.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Bayesian bootstrap (BB, Rubin 1981), TabMGP wins decisively on coverage: BB severely undercovers in all synthetic linear regression setups (0.46–0.66 vs. TabMGP's 0.99–1.00) and in low n/p real-data settings (e.g., abalone: 0.66 vs. 0.97), because bootstrapping from small n produces limited variability in forward samples. Against the bivariate copula update (Fong et al. 2023), TabMGP is competitive in synthetic linear regression (both achieve ~0.97–1.00 coverage) but outperforms Copula on real data where the copula's Gaussian assumptions break down (kin8nm: 0.63 vs. 0.95; Copula also produces overly large sets in logistic regression, e.g., telescope: size 50.69 vs. TabMGP's 5.96). Against the NUTS-based Bayes posterior (Hoffman & Gelman 2014), TabMGP produces substantially smaller credible sets in all synthetic setups (size 0.33–0.51 vs. 0.65) while matching or exceeding coverage, and avoids the model-misspecification sensitivity that causes Bayes to undercover in some real-data setups (e.g., concrete: 0.87 vs. 0.91). Against the TabPFN-initialised copula (Nagler & Rügamer 2025), results are mixed with no substantial advantage over the standard copula, and in some cases (energy: 0.17 coverage; concrete: 0.67) it performs worse than both TabMGP and the standard copula.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesises the martingale posterior (MGP) framework (Fong et al., 2023), which itself unifies Bayesian predictive inference and generalised Bayes, with the TabPFN prior-data-fitted transformer (Hollmann et al., 2022; 2025) as a predictive rule. Prior MGP constructions relied on handcrafted predictive rules (copula-based updates, Bayesian bootstrap, Newton's algorithm) that satisfy exact martingale or a.c.i.d. properties; TabMGP departs by retaining a pretrained foundation transformer throughout the forward-sampling process, arguing these formal conditions are sufficient but not necessary. It also contrasts with Nagler & Rügamer (2025), who found TabPFN violates the martingale property and abandoned it after the first step in favour of a copula-based update, whereas TabMGP keeps TabPFN as the sole predictive engine.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TabMGP opens the MGP framework to the era of foundation models, offering a practical route to posterior uncertainty for scientific estimands without prior-likelihood specification or hyperparameter tuning—addressing a growing need as practitioners increasingly default to pretrained tabular transformers. The empirical finding that near-nominal coverage and stable posteriors persist despite demonstrable violations of both the martingale and a.c.i.d. conditions suggests the current theory is overly restrictive, motivating development of weaker, more realistic sufficient conditions. Limitations include undercoverage in small n/p classification settings, slow convergence in some setups, and computational cost (~70–200 s per sample), and the method is currently restricted to linear models on tabular data.
