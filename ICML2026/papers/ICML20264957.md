## Problem

Hierarchical GraphRAG systems (e.g., RAPTOR, Microsoft GraphRAG) rely on recursive LLM-based summarization to build community abstractions, incurring prohibitive token costs and diluting semantic nuance into fixed-length synopses. Meanwhile, knowledge-graph-based approaches require expensive LLM triplet extraction during indexing. The paper asks whether a principled, information-theoretic objective can replace both heuristic clustering and LLM summarization to produce a topologically-faithful hierarchy at near-zero indexing cost.

## Input / Output

The method takes a corpus of text chunks (embedded with all-MiniLM-L6-v2) and a user query. During indexing, no LLM calls are made: the system produces a hierarchical encoding tree whose internal nodes are represented by weighted embedding vectors rather than text summaries. At query time, a single LLM call generates a pseudo-document to augment the query, after which a single-pass hybrid scoring function retrieves k evidence chunks (default k=3) that are passed to GPT-4o-mini for answer generation. The retrieval is non-iterative, avoiding multi-step graph traversal.

## Method

SeRAG constructs a multi-perspective weighted graph by fusing three edge layers—semantic cosine similarity, named-entity overlap (logical), and Gaussian distance decay (narrative continuity)—then minimizes K-dimensional structural entropy over all possible encoding trees to obtain a topologically-faithful hierarchy T*. Instead of LLM summarization, each community node's representation is computed as a normalized weighted average of its constituent chunk embeddings, where weights are derived from localized structural entropy accumulated along the leaf-to-community path, yielding a token-free multi-granularity semantic space. At retrieval, a self-query augmentation step expands the user query into a pseudo-document, and a single-pass hybrid score combines community-level cosine similarity (structural prior) with fine-grained semantic similarity plus a gated entity-occurrence bonus, enabling coarse-to-fine navigation without iterative LLM reasoning.

## Results

SeRAG achieves state-of-the-art multi-hop QA accuracy across all three benchmarks: 63.4% Str-Acc / 63.9% LLM-Acc on HotpotQA, 64.4% / 61.5% on 2WikiMultiHopQA, and 29.7% / 32.1% on MuSiQue. The headline improvement is a +14.4 percentage-point absolute gain over the strongest baseline (HippoRAG2, 50.0% Str-Acc) on 2WikiMultiHopQA. In terms of efficiency, SeRAG indexes in 572 s (≈1.6× faster than HippoRAG) with only 0.38M prompt tokens (a >90× reduction vs. LightRAG's 35.52M), while maintaining a competitive 1.09 s average retrieval latency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SeRAG outperforms all nine baselines on every dataset and metric. Against the closest competitor HippoRAG2 (Gutiérrez et al., ICML 2025), SeRAG gains +6.7 pp Str-Acc on HotpotQA (63.4 vs 56.7), +14.4 pp on 2WikiMultiHopQA (64.4 vs 50.0), and +2.7 pp on MuSiQue (29.7 vs 27.0). Over the hierarchical baselines RAPTOR (Sarthi et al., ICLR 2024) and GraphRAG (Edge et al., 2024), SeRAG leads by 15.3 pp and 23.8 pp Str-Acc respectively on 2WikiMultiHopQA, while also using 53% fewer prompt tokens than RAPTOR and no LLM summarization calls. Flat graph methods G-Retriever (He et al., NeurIPS 2024), KGP (Wang et al., AAAI 2024), and LightRAG (Guo et al., EMNLP 2025) trail SeRAG by 34.9, 17.0, and 21.3 pp Str-Acc on 2WikiMultiHopQA respectively. Zero-shot LLMs (Llama3, GPT-3.5-turbo, GPT-4o-mini) and vanilla dense retrieval (Top-1/3/5) are far behind, confirming that structured hierarchical indexing is essential for multi-hop reasoning. Notably, SeRAG also surpasses HippoRAG (Jimenez Gutierrez et al., NeurIPS 2024) by 16.7 pp Str-Acc on 2WikiMultiHopQA while indexing 1.6× faster and consuming 8× fewer prompt tokens.

## Relation to Prior Work

SeRAG builds directly on the structural information theory framework of Li & Pan (2016) for community detection, repurposing structural entropy minimization as the objective for hierarchical index construction in RAG. It departs from the dominant paradigm in hierarchical GraphRAG (RAPTOR, Microsoft GraphRAG, LightRAG) where community nodes are represented by LLM-generated text summaries, replacing them with weighted vector aggregation in embedding space. The multi-perspective graph construction (semantic + logical entity overlap + sequential distance) extends flat graph RAG methods like HippoRAG and G-Retriever by adding a principled hierarchical structure, while the self-query augmentation draws on the pseudo-document expansion idea from prior retrieval-augmentation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core practical contribution is eliminating the LLM summarization bottleneck in hierarchical RAG indexing, achieving 90× token reduction versus LightRAG and 1.6× faster indexing while simultaneously improving accuracy by 14.4% over the best baseline on 2WikiMultiHopQA. This challenges the working assumption that natural-language community summaries are necessary for coarse-grained retrieval, suggesting that information-theoretic structure plus weighted vector aggregation is both cheaper and more faithful. Open questions include whether the structural entropy approach scales to truly massive corpora (experiments use 1,000-question subsets), whether the single-pass retrieval strategy generalizes to domains requiring iterative multi-step reasoning, and whether the results hold with stronger embedding models or different LLM backbones.
