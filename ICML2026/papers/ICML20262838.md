## Problem

Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models entangle high-level reasoning with low-level action generation, forcing the action module to parse semantically rich inputs (visual features, abstract embeddings) while remaining lightweight for real-time control. This semantic burden hinders cross-task generalization and necessitates costly task-specific fine-tuning. The paper asks whether a task-agnostic action expert can be trained once and reused across tasks by relying purely on sparse geometric guidance rather than semantic information.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262838/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The VLM receives an RGB image and a natural language instruction, and outputs a sparse set of 5 3D waypoints plus a final end-effector pose, all expressed in the camera frame (not the robot base frame) to align with the VLM's image-centric pre-training. The GAE receives three conditioning signals: robot proprioceptive state (joint positions, gripper state), guidance poses sampled every 8 steps from a B-spline-interpolated trajectory, and a cropped uncolored point cloud of 512 points. The output is a dense 7-dimensional SE(3) end-effector action chunk of length 8 (3D Cartesian position + 4D quaternion), generated via 10-step diffusion denoising at inference.

## Method

The core contribution is a sparse 3D pose trajectory interface that cleanly separates semantic reasoning (VLM) from motor control (GAE). The VLM (Qwen2.5VL) is lightly fine-tuned for only 1k SFT steps on keyframes identified by gripper state changes, predicting sparse waypoints in the camera frame; these are transformed to the robot base frame and B-spline interpolated into a smooth trajectory. The GAE is a conditional diffusion policy (inspired by 3D Diffusion Policy) that uses a PointNet encoder for the uncolored point cloud and MLPs for proprioception and guidance poses, trained with a two-stage Action Pre-training, Pointcloud Fine-tuning (APPF) scheme: first learning trajectory-following on 150k trajectories at batch size 32,768, then fine-tuning with point cloud conditioning at batch size 256. After training, the GAE is frozen and reused across all downstream tasks without task-specific adaptation; only the VLM requires lightweight fine-tuning to emit the sparse geometric interface. A closed-loop receding-horizon merging strategy enables online correction of execution deviations without stopping motion.

## Results

On the RoboTwin benchmark, GAE achieves 0.82 / 0.77 / 0.60 average success rates on short-, middle-, and long-horizon tasks, surpassing all generalist baselines (π0: 0.62/0.47/0.14, π0-FAST: 0.74/0.48/0.18) and matching or exceeding the DP3 expert (0.82/0.49/0.02) without any task-specific fine-tuning. On SIMPLER, GAE reaches 74.1% overall success, outperforming SoFAR (69.8%) and π0-FAST (60.5%). In real-world evaluation, GAE maintains the smallest ID-to-OOD performance drop (e.g., 0.90→0.90 on pick toy) compared to all baselines, and achieves 0.65→0.55 on the long-horizon fold-towel task where the next-best SoFAR reaches only 0.50→0.40.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GAE outperforms all generalist VLA baselines across every horizon: on RoboTwin long-horizon tasks it achieves 0.60 vs. π0 (0.14), π0-FAST (0.18), π0.5 (0.50), and RDT (0.02), a gap of 10–58 points. Against the DP3 expert (the strongest single-task baseline), GAE matches on short-horizon (0.82 vs. 0.82) but substantially exceeds it on middle (0.77 vs. 0.49) and long-horizon (0.60 vs. 0.02) tasks, and does so without any per-task fine-tuning (0 extra steps vs. 32×30k for DP3). On SIMPLER, GAE (74.1%) beats SoFAR (69.8%), π0-FAST (60.5%), and π0 (56.8%), while on real-world long-horizon tasks (fold towel, insert flower) it leads SoFAR by 15–20 points. In the trajectory-planning quality comparison (Table 18), GAE's VLM outperforms Magma, HAMSTER, and LLARVA on all 11 metrics (e.g., DTW 0.4564 vs. 0.8353/0.7945/0.4894; endpoint error 0.0739 vs. 0.1784/0.1181/0.0771). The only comparison where GAE does not clearly win is short-horizon RoboTwin, where it ties DP3 at 0.82, and the real-world 'pick toy' task where π0.5 achieves a slightly higher ID score (1.00 vs. 0.90), though GAE shows a smaller OOD drop (0.90 vs. 0.95).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the 3D Diffusion Policy architecture (Ze et al., 2024) for its action expert, but departs from the broader VLA lineage (π0, OpenVLA, HAMSTER, ThinkAct) by replacing semantically rich interfaces—visual features, abstract embeddings, or dense trajectories—with a minimal sparse 3D waypoint representation. Where prior dual-system frameworks still require the low-level policy to parse complex structured inputs from the VLM, GAE reduces the interface to a few geometric poses in the camera frame, making the action expert task-agnostic and frozen after a single training run. The APPF two-stage training paradigm and the 150k pointcloud-trajectory dataset are novel contributions that address the data scarcity and coupled-training challenges that have limited prior geometry-conditioned policies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practically significant recipe for decoupling VLM reasoning from motor control: a frozen, reusable action expert eliminates the need for per-task fine-tuning and mitigates catastrophic forgetting, while the sparse waypoint interface keeps VLM adaptation lightweight. The waypoint-density ablation (5 vs. 30 points) is a useful empirical signal that dense action supervision may be actively counterproductive for VLM generalization, though the evidence is confined to a single benchmark and model family. Open questions include whether the manually designed sparse interface can be learned end-to-end, whether the reliance on external depth sensing limits deployment, and whether the frozen-expert paradigm scales to substantially more diverse embodiments or contact-rich tasks beyond tabletop manipulation.
