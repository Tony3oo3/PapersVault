## Problem

Linear contextual bandits with latent-state dynamics have been studied under restrictive assumptions: prior work either modeled rewards as functions of posterior beliefs (rather than directly on hidden states), restricted rewards to finite spaces, or required infrequent state changes. This paper tackles the more natural and complex setting where continuously-valued rewards depend jointly on the unobserved hidden state, the observed context, and the action, with the hidden state following a homogeneous HMM that may switch arbitrarily often.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262559/cropped_page8_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each round the learner observes a context vector x_t drawn from an HMM emission distribution, feeds the full context history x_{1:t} into a belief-estimation subroutine (spectral method + Bayes update) to obtain an estimated belief b̂_t over H hidden states, and uses past rewards to periodically update reward-parameter estimates θ̂. The output is an action a_t selected optimistically via upper confidence bounds, along with high-probability pseudo-regret guarantees. A key design constraint is that beliefs are estimated solely from contexts (never from rewards), and reward-model parameters are updated only at stage boundaries of length ℓ, not every round.

## Method

The paper introduces a Staged LinUCB on Estimated Beliefs algorithm that proceeds in stages of length ℓ, updating the LinUCB parameter estimates θ̂ only at stage boundaries to create a filtration U_t under which actions are measurable while posterior probabilities remain close to context-only beliefs (guaranteed by an exponential HMM forgetting condition). For the simplified belief-dependent reward model of Nelson et al. (2022), the problem reduces directly to standard linear contextual bandits with b_t ⊗ φ(a,x_t) as the context, yielding a clean T^{3/4} high-probability regret bound via a LinUCB-style analysis that accounts for belief estimation error. For the general state-dependent model, no such reduction exists; the key technical difficulty is controlling the term S^Δ that captures the gap between the true state-dependent reward and its belief-weighted approximation, which is handled via an L²-Markov inequality exploiting the exponential decay of E[z_τ z_τ'] under the forgetting condition. The final T^{7/8} regret bound is achieved by optimizing the regularization λ = T^{3/4} and stage length ℓ = ⌈T^{3/4}⌉, with the T^{1/8} overhead over the simplified model attributed to the staged-update structure and the L²-Markov argument.

## Results

The paper's main contributions are theoretical regret bounds: a $\tilde{O}(T^{3/4})$ high-probability pseudo-regret bound for the simplified belief-dependent reward model of Nelson et al. (2022) (Theorem A.1), and a $\tilde{O}(T^{7/8})$ high-probability pseudo-regret bound for the more complex state-dependent reward model (Theorem 4.2). The $T^{1/4}$ factor in the first bound is attributed solely to belief estimation (recovering $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{T})$ if beliefs were known), while the additional $T^{1/8}$ factor in the second bound comes from the staged-update procedure. Numerical simulations on a UCI Credit Card dataset (100 runs) confirm that both staged ($\ell=37$) and non-staged ($\ell=1$) variants achieve sublinear pseudo-regret, in contrast to the linear pseudo-regret of a plain LinUCB baseline that ignores the latent-state dynamics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its Staged LinUCB strategy against Plain LinUCB (Abbasi-Yadkori et al., 2011) in numerical simulations on a UCI Credit Card dataset with two latent states (inflation/recession) and three actions. Plain LinUCB, which ignores the HMM structure and belief estimates entirely, suffers linear pseudo-regret, while both the staged ($\ell=37$) and non-staged ($\ell=1$) variants of the proposed method achieve sublinear pseudo-regret over 100 runs. Theoretically, the paper also improves upon Nelson et al. (2022) in four ways: (1) the $T^{3/4}$ bound explicitly accounts for belief estimation error, whereas Nelson et al.'s $\sqrt{T}$ bound assumes known HMM parameters; (2) the bound holds with high probability rather than only in expectation; (3) it is model-free (does not depend on reward gaps or require a non-degeneracy condition on the population design matrix); and (4) it does not require an HMM forgetting condition under sub-Gaussian noise, unlike Nelson et al.'s analysis which needs one to handle weaker second-moment noise assumptions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the belief-dependent linear reward model of Nelson et al. (2022) in two directions: it provides a more direct LinUCB-style analysis of their simplified model that accounts for HMM parameter estimation (yielding high-probability, model-free bounds), and it tackles the more natural but substantially harder model where rewards depend directly on hidden states rather than on posterior beliefs. The core algorithmic tool is a staged LinUCB strategy that periodically updates reward-model parameters to manage the complex statistical dependencies between actions, rewards, and hidden states. For belief estimation, the paper leverages spectral methods for HMM parameter recovery (Hsu et al., 2012; Anandkumar et al., 2012) combined with a Bayes' update rule, and relies on classic HMM forgetting properties (Cappé et al., 2005) to control the difficult non-martingale terms in the regret analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide sublinear high-probability regret bounds for linear contextual bandits where rewards depend jointly on hidden states, observed contexts, and actions with continuously-valued rewards and arbitrarily frequent state switches—a setting that prior work either avoided or could only handle under restrictive assumptions (finite reward spaces, infrequent state changes, or belief-dependent rewards). The key conceptual insight is that the seemingly innocuous substitution of hidden states by beliefs in the reward model fundamentally changes the problem's structure, enabling a reduction to standard linear contextual bandits that is unavailable in the state-dependent case. Open questions include whether the T^{7/8} rate can be improved to T^{3/4} (matching the simplified model), whether the staged approach is truly necessary, and whether T^{3/4} is optimal for the belief-dependent model given belief estimation overhead.
