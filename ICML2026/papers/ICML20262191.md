## Problem

Hypergradient estimation in bilevel optimization requires computing or approximating the Hessian inverse of the inner objective, which is the dominant computational bottleneck. Existing methods follow an optimize-then-approximate paradigm where the Hessian inverse is estimated post hoc via Neumann series, conjugate gradient, or other numerical solvers, introducing sequential overhead that cannot be parallelized with inner optimization. The paper asks whether the statistical structure of KL-divergence inner problems can be exploited to approximate the Hessian inverse at negligible additional cost during inner SGD.

## Input / Output

The method requires the inner objective to be a KL-divergence minimization (i.e., the model distribution p(·;v,θ) matches the data distribution q(·) at the inner optimum), which is the standard setting for maximum-likelihood training. At each inner iteration, the algorithm consumes a single data sample ξ~q(·) and reuses the stochastic gradient ∇_θ l(v,θ,ξ) already computed for the SGD update to perform a rank-one EFIM inverse update via the Sherman-Morrison formula. The output is an ε-stationary outer variable v with total sample complexity O~(ε⁻²), achieved with a constant batch size of O(1) per inner step.

## Method

NHGD replaces the Hessian inverse H(θ*(v))⁻¹ in the hypergradient formula with the inverse of the Empirical Fisher Information Matrix (EFIM), justified by the fact that the FIM equals the Hessian at the inner optimum under the model-matching assumption. The EFIM inverse A_t is maintained via efficient rank-one Sherman-Morrison updates that reuse stochastic gradients from the inner SGD loop, and the cross-partial derivative ∇²_{θ,v}ℓ is approximated by a running average along the inner trajectory. This design enables a parallel optimize-and-approximate paradigm: the EFIM inverse and cross-derivative updates run synchronously on a separate device with only one-way gradient communication from the inner SGD device, eliminating the sequential post-hoc Hessian inversion overhead. The paper establishes high-probability (not just mean-square) convergence guarantees, showing the EFIM inverse converges to the true Hessian inverse at rate O(1/√T · √log(1/δ)) and the outer objective reaches an ε-stationary point in O~(ε⁻²) total samples, matching state-of-the-art optimize-then-approximate methods while reducing wall-clock time.

## Results

NHGD achieves a high-probability sample complexity of Õ(ε⁻²) for reaching an ε-stationary point, matching the best existing optimize-then-approximate methods (stocBiO, ALSET, AmIGO) while providing stronger high-probability guarantees rather than mean-square bounds. On the PDE-constrained optimization task, NHGD converges 3–4× faster in wall-clock time than the Broyden-based state-of-the-art method while achieving a comparable final objective. On hyper-data cleaning (MNIST) and data distillation (Fashion-MNIST), NHGD consistently converges faster and attains higher test accuracy than all baselines, even when Neumann or CG approximations use up to 40 truncation terms.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the double-loop baselines (BSA, stocBiO, ALSET, AmIGO), NHGD matches the best sample complexity of Õ(ε⁻²) while uniquely providing high-probability (rather than MSE) guarantees and using O(1) batch size with parallel Hessian inversion. In the hyper-data cleaning and data distillation experiments, NHGD converges faster and reaches higher final test accuracy than stocBiO, AmIGO, TTSA, and SOBA across all settings tested. Compared to the Broyden-based method (Hao et al., 2023) on the PDE-constrained optimization task, NHGD achieves a comparable final objective while converging 3–4× faster in wall-clock time. The key practical advantage over all baselines is that NHGD's EFIM-based Hessian inverse approximation runs in parallel with inner-loop SGD at negligible additional cost, eliminating the sequential post-hoc Hessian inversion overhead inherent in Neumann-series, CG, and Broyden approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

NHGD bridges two previously separate lines of work: hypergradient descent for bilevel optimization (Ghadimi & Wang, 2018; Ji et al., 2021; Arbel & Mairal, 2022), which relies on numerical Hessian-inverse approximations (Neumann series, conjugate gradient, Broyden), and natural gradient descent (Amari, 1998; Martens, 2020), which uses the Fisher information matrix as a Hessian surrogate in single-level KL-divergence minimization. The key departure is recognizing that at the inner optimum, the FIM coincides with the true Hessian regardless of outer convergence, making the EFIM a more principled surrogate in bilevel than in single-level settings. This enables a parallel 'optimize-and-approximate' paradigm that replaces the sequential 'optimize-then-approximate' structure of all prior methods, with the EFIM inverse updated via Sherman-Morrison rank-one updates at negligible cost alongside inner-loop SGD.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NHGD offers a practical computational efficiency gain—eliminating the serial Hessian-inverse bottleneck—while matching the sample complexity of state-of-the-art methods, making it attractive for large-scale bilevel problems where post-hoc Hessian inversion is the dominant cost. However, the approach is restricted to KL-divergence inner objectives (Assumption 4.6) and the double-loop structure, leaving open extensions to misspecified models, single-loop algorithms, and variance-reduction techniques. The bounded-gradient-noise assumption (Assumption 4.4) also limits applicability to heavy-tailed loss landscapes. For practitioners, the K-FAC integration path suggests the method could scale to deep network hyperparameter optimization, but this remains untested in the paper.
