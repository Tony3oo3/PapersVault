## Problem

Previous analyses of the Accelerated Noisy Power Method (ANPM) require noise magnitudes of order ε^μ, where μ is a very large power that grows with the inverse eigengap, rendering the guarantees impractical for ill-conditioned problems and blocking application to decentralized PCA. This paper provides an improved analysis that preserves the accelerated convergence rate under noise conditions as mild as those of the non-accelerated Noisy Power Method, and proves both the rate and the noise conditions are worst-case optimal.

## Input / Output

The method consumes only inexact matrix-vector products x → Ax + Ξ_t (no explicit access to A is needed), a target rank k, and a momentum parameter β satisfying λ_k > 2√β ≥ λ_{k+1}. In the decentralized variant ADePM, each agent i holds a local matrix A_i and communicates solely with neighbors through a doubly stochastic gossip matrix W, with L gossip rounds per ANPM iteration. The output is a sequence of orthonormal matrices X_t ∈ St(d,k), with convergence certified when sin θ_k(U_k, X_t) ≤ ε, where θ_k is the largest principal angle between the estimated and true top-k eigenspaces.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a novel analysis of ANPM that tracks the matrix H_t = (U_{-k}^T X_t)(U_k^T X_t)^{-1} (whose spectral norm equals tan θ_k) rather than the iterates X_t directly. By introducing an auxiliary sequence {C_t} built from the QR factors, the authors derive a three-term recurrence for H_t that admits a closed-form solution in terms of scaled Chebyshev polynomials, enabling tight per-component control of the noise without the coarse bounds on ‖R_t‖₂ that forced the restrictive ε^μ conditions in prior work. This yields noise conditions of order O((λ_k − 2√β)ε) and O((λ_k − 2√β)cos θ_k(U_k, X_t)), matching the non-accelerated NPM, while preserving the accelerated rate Õ(√(λ_k/(λ_k − λ_{k+1}))). The authors further prove worst-case lower bounds showing neither the rate nor the noise constants can be improved. For decentralized PCA, they combine ANPM with accelerated gossip (Liu & Morse, 2011) to produce ADePM, the first decentralized PCA algorithm with provably accelerated convergence and per-iteration communication costs comparable to non-accelerated baselines.

## Results

The paper's main theoretical contribution (Theorem 2.2) shows that ANPM achieves the accelerated convergence rate O(√(λ_k/(λ_k−λ_{k+1})) log(1/ε)) under noise conditions of only O((λ_k−λ_{k+1})ε) — the same mild conditions as the non-accelerated Noisy Power Method of Hardt & Price (2014). This is a significant improvement over Xu (2023), which requires noise of order Õ((λ_k−λ_{k+1})ε^{μ}) where μ = Ω(log(λ_1/λ_{k+1})·√(λ_k/(λ_k−λ_{k+1}))), an exponentially more restrictive bound for ill-conditioned problems. The analysis is proven worst-case optimal (Theorem 2.3) and the noise conditions are tight up to constants (Theorems 2.4–2.5). For decentralized PCA (Theorem 3.3), ADePM achieves the same accelerated rate with communication costs O(1/√γ_W log(M/λ_k · λ_k/(λ_k−λ_{k+1}) · 1/ε)) gossip rounds per iteration, matching non-accelerated methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to Hardt & Price (2014), this paper achieves the same mild noise conditions (linear in ε and the eigengap) but improves the convergence rate from O(1/Δ_k) to O(1/√Δ_k) in the eigengap, a square-root speedup for ill-conditioned matrices. Compared to Xu (2023), the paper matches the accelerated rate exactly but relaxes the noise conditions from Õ(ε^μ) to O(ε), where μ grows as Ω(√(1/Δ_k) log(λ_1/λ_{k+1})); for small eigengaps this is an exponential relaxation, making the guarantees practically applicable. For decentralized PCA, ADePM (Theorem 3.3) outperforms both DePM (Wai et al., 2017) and DeEPCA (Ye & Zhang, 2021) in convergence speed (accelerated vs. non-accelerated rate) while requiring comparable gossip rounds per iteration — O(1/√γ_W log(·)) vs. O(1/√γ_W log(·)) — whereas applying Xu (2023)'s analysis to the same algorithm would inflate communication costs by a factor of O(log(λ_1/λ_{k+1})·√(λ_k/(λ_k−λ_{k+1}))). Experiments on Fed-Heart-Disease, Digits, and Ego-Facebook confirm that ADePM with adaptive β tuning significantly outperforms DePM and DeEPCA in convergence speed while reaching similar final accuracy at fixed communication budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of the Noisy Power Method line (Hardt & Price 2014; Mai & Johansson 2019) and the accelerated Krylov-subspace analysis of Xu (2023). The key departure is technical: rather than analyzing the iterate X_t directly (as Xu 2023 did, requiring coarse bounds on ||R_t||_2 that forced exponentially restrictive noise conditions), the authors study the ratio matrix H_t = (U_{-k}^T X_t)(U_k^T X_t)^{-1} together with an auxiliary sequence {C_t}, yielding a three-term recurrence in scaled Chebyshev polynomials. This recovers the same accelerated rate O(√(λ_k/(λ_k−λ_{k+1}))) under noise conditions identical to the non-accelerated NPM. For the decentralized application, the paper combines this analysis with the accelerated gossip of Liu & Morse (2011) and the decentralized PCA framework of Wai et al. (2017) / Ye & Zhang (2021).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that acceleration previously came at the cost of noise bounds decaying exponentially in the eigengap, which made accelerated methods inapplicable to decentralized PCA under realistic communication budgets. By showing the same mild noise conditions suffice, the paper unlocks the first provably accelerated decentralized PCA algorithm (ADePM) with communication costs comparable to non-accelerated baselines. The analysis is shown to be worst-case tight (Theorems 2.3–2.5), so the gap cannot be closed further. Open directions include extending to wider-gap settings (p > k columns), stochastic noise, and accelerating DeEPCA's subspace-tracking technique.
