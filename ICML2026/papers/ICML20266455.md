## Problem

Existing self-play systems for LLMs (e.g., Absolute Zero, R-Zero) plateau or collapse after a few iterations because they optimise reward signals without ensuring the self-synthesised data pipeline actually increases learnable information for the bounded observer model. The paper argues that reward optimisation is necessary but insufficient: a system can improve its reward via hacking or memorisation while the underlying learnable structure remains unchanged or even degrades.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266455/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a single pre-trained LLM (Qwen2.5 0.5B–14B or Qwen3 4B) whose weights are shared across all three roles (PROPOSER, SOLVER, VERIFIER), and optionally external context that conditions task generation. It produces self-synthetic training instances (code-based tasks in abduction, deduction, and induction formats, with 2048-token sequences) and updated model weights via LoRA fine-tuning (rank=16). A key diagnostic output is the epiplexity estimate, computed as the prequential code length at the MDL-optimal checkpoint, which quantifies learnable information in bits per token for a given observer capacity.

## Method

The paper formalises learnable information via epiplexity — a bounded MDL objective optimised over an observer family constrained by explicit parameter budget C and inference-time budget T — and identifies three system-level design principles that jointly ensure monotonic learnable information gain: (1) Asymmetric Co-evolution, which exploits the computational gap between proposing/verifying and solving to create a weak-to-strong-to-weak supervision ladder; (2) Capacity Growth, which expands both parameter and inference-time budgets across iterations so the observer can absorb newly exposed structure; and (3) Proactive Information Seeking, where the internal environment actively acquires external context and learns new synthetic directions to prevent saturation. Diagnostic experiments on Absolute Zero-style self-play coding tasks use prequential coding to estimate epiplexity, revealing that without explicit mechanisms to close the co-evolution loop, learnable information fluctuates erratically while rewards appear to improve steadily, confirming that reward signals alone cannot detect collapse.

## Results

In Experiment 1, epiplexity (learnable information) increases with PROPOSER capacity (Qwen2.5 7B → 14B → Qwen3 4B), but as SOLVER size grows, learnable information first rises then falls, consistent with an emergence threshold beyond which the model memorises rather than learns structure. Induction tasks yield substantially higher epiplexity than abduction or deduction. In Experiment 2, across nine self-play iterations, epiplexity fluctuates dramatically (e.g., Induction ranges from 75.7 to 474.3 ×10³ bits) while both proposer and solver rewards increase monotonically, demonstrating that reward improvement does not guarantee sustained learnable information gain.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This position paper does not benchmark a single method against named baselines; instead, its diagnostic experiments compare configurations along three axes. Across PROPOSER capacities (Qwen2.5 7B, 14B, Qwen3 4B), stronger proposers consistently produce synthetic data with higher epiplexity, confirming that the internal environment's quality gates learnable information. Across SOLVER capacities, a non-monotonic pattern emerges: learnable information peaks at an intermediate model size before declining, mirroring the emergence phenomenon reported by Finzi et al. (2026) where larger models under a fixed budget shift from structural learning to memorisation. Across task types, induction (program synthesis from I/O pairs) yields the highest epiplexity, followed by deduction, then abduction, aligning with the computational asymmetry the paper predicts. The most critical comparison is between reward and epiplexity over self-play iterations: while both proposer and solver rewards improve steadily (e.g., Abduction solver reward rises from 0.147 to 0.941), epiplexity oscillates without monotonic growth, demonstrating that reward optimisation alone is insufficient to detect or prevent the collapse the paper diagnoses.

## Relation to Prior Work

This position paper reframes the well-studied problem of LLM self-improvement through self-generated data by importing epiplexity (a bounded-observer MDL measure from Finzi et al., 2026) as a diagnostic for 'learnable information gain,' and grounds its experiments in the Absolute Zero self-play coding setup (Zhao et al., 2025a). It departs from prior self-play and solver-verifier co-evolution work (SPIN, Self-Rewarding, URPO, GenEnv) by arguing that the triadic PROPOSER–SOLVER–VERIFIER loop should be understood as a self-synthetic data pipeline whose success criterion is monotonic growth of bounded learnable information, rather than reward optimisation or difficulty matching. The Rissanen MDL decomposition provides the theoretical backbone, while the three proposed design principles (asymmetric co-evolution, capacity growth, proactive information seeking) synthesise scattered observations from the self-play literature into a unified system-level framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a concrete diagnostic (epiplexity via prequential coding) that can distinguish genuine structural learning from reward gaming in self-evolving loops, which is practically valuable given that existing systems report early plateaus and collapse. However, the empirical evidence is preliminary—limited to small-scale LoRA fine-tuning on Qwen2.5/3 models in a single coding-task domain—and the epiplexity metric itself comes from a very recent, not-yet-widely-validated source. Key open questions include how to close the asymmetry gap in hard-to-verify domains, whether learnable information correlates with downstream task accuracy, and how to implement proactive information seeking without the model needing to recognise its own ignorance. The framework is most impactful as a design checklist and evaluation lens for the growing self-evolving-agent literature rather than as a ready-to-deploy method.
