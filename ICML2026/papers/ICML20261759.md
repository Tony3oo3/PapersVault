## Problem

Existing model merging methods for transferring reasoning capabilities from Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) to Vision-Language Models (VLMs) operate at the coarse-grained layer level, which inherently entangles reasoning and visual representations within each layer. This entanglement creates an unavoidable trade-off: tuning a single layer-level coefficient to boost reasoning simultaneously degrades visual perception, and vice versa. The paper asks whether a finer-grained merging paradigm can selectively inject reasoning while preserving visual capabilities.

## Input / Output

The method consumes three sets of model weights (VLM, LRM, and their shared base model) and a small unlabeled visual perception calibration set (VizWiz VQA), requiring no VL reasoning annotations. It produces a merged VLM whose LLM backbone has been modified while the visual tower and projection layers remain frozen. The merged model outputs text responses with reasoning chains, and the entire training loop operates on unlabeled perception data only, making it label-free with respect to reasoning supervision.

## Method

FRISM decomposes each LRM task vector via SVD into orthogonal rank-1 subspaces, then introduces a lightweight learnable gate vector g per layer (initialized to zero) that modulates each subspace's contribution through a Sigmoid-gated scaling of the singular values. The merged layer is computed as θ_vlm + λ_lrm · U(Sigmoid(g) ⊙ S)Vᵀ, where U, S, V are frozen from the SVD. Training uses a dual-objective loss: a KL-divergence distillation term against the frozen base VLM (preserving visual perception) and a negative Frobenius-norm term on the effective singular values (maximizing reasoning injection intensity), balanced by a hyperparameter α. Only the gating parameters are trainable (~0.53M for 7B), making the method extremely parameter-efficient. A theoretical analysis via Hessian-based Taylor expansion shows the optimization acts as a spectral filter: subspaces aligned with high-curvature visual directions are suppressed while those in flat directions or with large reasoning magnitude are amplified.

## Results

On the 7B scale (Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct merged with DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B), FRISM achieves an average VL reasoning score of 49.4, surpassing the base VLM (47.4) by 2.0 points and the best baseline DARE (47.8) by 1.6 points, while maintaining VL perception at 83.0 (vs. base 82.9). At the 3B scale, FRISM gains +1.8 points in reasoning (35.0 vs. 33.2 base) with zero perception loss; at the 32B scale, it gains +2.4 points (59.7 vs. 57.3). In terms of efficiency, FRISM requires only 0.53M trainable parameters and ~0.49 hours of training on a single GPU, compared to ~7000M parameters and ~2 hours for the post-training method DRIFT, while achieving higher average reasoning (44.0 vs. 43.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FRISM consistently outperforms all five merging baselines (Task Arithmetic, Ties-Merging, DARE, IP-Merging) across all three model scales (3B, 7B, 32B) on average VL reasoning, with the largest margin over Task Arithmetic at λ=0.15/0.2 where TA suffers catastrophic degradation (e.g., 26.9 vs. FRISM's 49.4 at 7B). Against the closest merging baseline DARE (47.8 avg reasoning at 7B), FRISM gains +1.6 points while also preserving perception better (83.0 vs. 82.5). Compared to the post-training method DRIFT (~7000M params, ~2h training), FRISM (0.53M params, ~0.49h) achieves higher average reasoning (44.0 vs. 43.2) with ~13,000× fewer trainable parameters. Against the stronger post-trained models OpenVLThinker-7B and VLAA-Thinker-7B (Table A2), FRISM achieves the highest normalized reasoning score (104.2% vs. 101.7% and 103.8%) while retaining 99.6% of base perception, compared to 94.5% and 98.6% respectively. IP-Merging, the most directly comparable prior method for reasoning injection, is outperformed by FRISM on every benchmark at 7B (e.g., MathVista 70.3 vs. 67.2, R1-OneVision 37.5 vs. 36.0) and avoids the performance collapse that IP-Merging exhibits under certain threshold settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

FRISM extends the model-merging paradigm for reasoning injection from LRMs to VLMs, building directly on Task Arithmetic's task-vector formulation, BR2V's demonstration that such cross-modal merging is feasible, and IP-Merging's layer-level projection strategy. The key departure is shifting the merging granularity from the layer level to the SVD subspace level within each layer, introducing per-subspace learnable gates trained via a label-free self-distillation objective (KL divergence for visual preservation plus spectral-norm maximization for reasoning injection). This contrasts with prior methods that apply a single scalar coefficient per layer or per model, which the paper argues inherently entangles reasoning and visual capabilities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FRISM offers a practically compelling recipe: 0.53M trainable parameters, ~30 minutes on a single GPU, and no labeled reasoning data, yet it matches or slightly exceeds post-training methods (DRIFT, OpenVLThinker) on reasoning benchmarks while preserving visual perception more faithfully. The main open question is whether the core assumption—that reasoning and visual representations occupy approximately orthogonal subspaces—holds for more heterogeneous model pairs or for transferring capabilities beyond reasoning (e.g., safety, code). The modest absolute gains (1–2.4 points on reasoning averages) suggest that subspace-level merging captures a meaningful but bounded fraction of the reasoning capability gap, leaving room for complementary approaches.
