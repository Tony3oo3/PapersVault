## Problem

Existing time-series foundation models either couple training tightly to forecasting losses (yielding brittle, task-specific representations) or rely on reconstruction/contrastive objectives that entangle semantic structure with sensor-level noise. Additionally, virtually all models treat input channels as unlabeled, interchangeable streams, discarding the rich sensor-identity information that domain experts use to interpret multivariate signals. This paper asks whether a channel-aware, latent-space prediction objective can produce general-purpose time-series embeddings that transfer across classification, forecasting, and anomaly detection without task-specific pretraining.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a tuple (T, D, pos) where T ∈ R^{T×C} is a full-resolution multivariate time series (no patching or downsampling), D is an ordered list of C free-text channel descriptions encoded by a frozen text embedder, and pos provides positional indices for RoPE. It produces per-time-step, per-channel H-dimensional embeddings of shape T×C×H, ℓ₂-normalized along the embedding dimension. The architecture is equivariant to channel permutation (verified to <10⁻⁴ output difference under random reordering), and the full O(C²T²) attention matrix is computed over all channel-time pairs, which constrains practical context length.

## Method

CHARM combines three architectural innovations trained under a JEPA objective. First, a contextual TCN replaces static patch featurization: convolution kernels are generated linearly from text-description embeddings (contextual kernels), and per-layer sigmoid gates derived from the same descriptions modulate the effective receptive field, allowing the featurizer to adapt its inductive bias per sensor without manual patch-size tuning. Second, each transformer layer augments standard self-attention with two description-conditioned terms: a gating matrix G_d = ReLU(Z − S) that suppresses cross-channel attention between semantically unrelated sensor pairs, and a symmetric inter-channel time-offset tensor Δ that encodes learned dependency strengths between channel pairs at each temporal lag, constructed from pairwise description embeddings to enforce Δ[i,j,t] = Δ[j,i,−t]. Third, training uses an I-JEPA-style predictor–target–encoder setup with two SSL tasks (causal prediction and smoothing), a multi-resolution ℓ₁ loss aligning embeddings at per-channel, per-timepoint, and global aggregation levels, and regularization terms that push the gating threshold toward 1 (sparsity) and penalize variance in the temporal-offset tensor (stability). The ablations show the JEPA objective is the single largest performance driver (4.4 pp classification gain over a reconstruction baseline with identical architecture), followed by the text-conditioned attention components.

## Results

On the LSF forecasting benchmark (per-dataset mean MSE across horizons 96/192/336/720), CHARM with a frozen linear probe achieves 0.209 MSE on ETTm2 and 0.324 on ETTh2, outperforming billion-parameter models like Moirai-Large (0.285, 0.354) and Toto (0.266, 0.349). On UEA multivariate classification, CHARM finetuned reaches 80.9% average accuracy (7,799 total correct), surpassing the best baseline MiniROCKET (77.6%, 7,569). For anomaly detection, CHARM attains F1=0.86 on SKAB (vs. 0.82 for MOMENT-LP) and F1=0.754 on UCR (vs. 0.684 for MOMENT), with the lowest Missed Alarm Rate (12.69%) among all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CHARM's frozen linear probe (CHARM-LP) decisively outperforms all forecasting baselines on ETTm2 (MSE 0.209 vs. 0.259–0.285 for MOMENT-LP, PatchTST-LP, Moirai-Large, and Toto) and ETTh2 (0.324 vs. 0.333–0.365), but underperforms on ETTh1 (0.554 vs. 0.392–0.435) and ETTm1 (0.413 vs. 0.344–0.396), where reconstruction-based pretraining (MOMENT-LP, PatchTST-LP) retains an advantage. In classification, CHARM frozen+SVM (79.6% avg accuracy) beats all representation-learning baselines—TS2Vec (78.1%), T-Rep (78.5%), T-Loss (72.8%), TS-TCC (74.4%), and MOMENT (72.5%)—and is competitive with the task-specific MiniROCKET (77.6%); finetuning further lifts CHARM to 80.9%. On SKAB anomaly detection, CHARM achieves the highest F1 (0.86) and lowest MAR (12.69%), surpassing MOMENT-LP (F1 0.82, MAR 20.73), T-Rep (F1 0.78), and TS2Vec (F1 0.79). On UCR univariate anomaly detection, CHARM (F1 0.754) outperforms MOMENT (0.684), TimesNet (0.627), and all task-specific methods. The paper notes that MAE leaders skew toward MAE-optimized models (Toto, VisionTS, Moirai), explaining CHARM's relative MAE gap under its MSE-optimized head.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the I-JEPA latent-prediction framework (Assran et al., 2023) from vision to multivariate time series, replacing the reconstruction or contrastive objectives used by MOMENT, UniTS, and TS2Vec with a predictor that operates entirely in embedding space. Architecturally, it extends dilated temporal convolutional networks (Bai et al., 2018) into a context-aware featurizer whose kernels and gates are generated from frozen text embeddings of channel descriptions, and augments standard self-attention with description-conditioned inter-channel gating and symmetric time-offset tensors. Unlike prior multimodal text+time-series work (JOLT, TEnt, Time-LLM) that aligns instance-level text labels with series embeddings via contrastive objectives, CHARM uses sensor-level descriptions as architectural conditioning signals, enabling channel-order equivariance and cross-dataset generalization without instance-level annotations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CHARM is the first foundation embedding model to incorporate granular textual channel metadata directly into the encoder, opening a path toward sensor-aware, interpretable time-series representations whose gating matrices reveal learned causal structure across channels. The ablation identifying the JEPA objective (not the text content) as the dominant performance driver reframes the role of descriptions as a channel-addressing mechanism rather than a knowledge source, which has implications for how multimodal conditioning should be designed in other domains. Key open questions include scalability beyond the O(C²T²) full-attention bottleneck to longer horizons and higher channel counts, the practical overhead of curating quality sensor descriptions for large unlabeled corpora, and whether systematic multi-task post-training can close the remaining gap to task-specific SOTA.
