## Problem

Standard RL post-training for LLM-as-a-Judge relies on binary accuracy rewards that ignore the ordinal structure of numeric scoring (e.g., a prediction of 4 is meaningfully closer to a ground truth of 5 than a prediction of 1). Existing regression-aware methods like RAFT and TRACT address this but are confined to supervised fine-tuning, lacking a mechanism to actively explore and refine self-generated reasoning trajectories. The core technical obstacle is that a regression objective computed over the model's own predictive distribution is explicitly policy-dependent, which invalidates the standard policy gradient assumption that the reward is independent of the policy parameters.

## Input / Output

The model receives an instruction (optionally with embedded input), a response to evaluate, a fine-grained score rubric, and optionally a reference answer that is known to score 5. It produces a chain-of-thought reasoning trace followed by a single-digit numeric score. At inference, the RAIL predictor computes the expected value over the single-digit token set {0,…,9} weighted by the model's output probabilities, yielding a continuous-valued prediction (e.g., 4.99) rather than a discrete token. Ground-truth labels can be continuous (averages of multiple human or GPT-4 ratings), and score ranges beyond [0,9] are handled via normalization during training and de-normalization at inference.

## Method

REAL defines a policy-dependent regression reward r_REAL(θ,x,c) = −(ŷ_θ(x,c) − y*)² + λ log π_θ(y*|x,c), where ŷ_θ is the RAIL expected-value predictor over digit tokens. Because this reward depends on θ, the standard REINFORCE gradient is invalid; instead, the generalized policy gradient (Schulman et al., 2015) is applied, which decomposes the update into two terms: (1) a CoT exploration term that performs policy-gradient updates on the reasoning trajectory weighted by the regression reward, and (2) a prediction refinement term that backpropagates the squared-error loss directly through the digit-token probabilities to correct the final score. For stabilization, the CoT term uses RLOO leave-one-out advantages with clipping, and a prediction weight β scales the refinement term. The paper proves that minimizing the squared-error regression loss is equivalent to maximizing Pearson correlation (Lemma 3.1), providing a theoretical justification for the regression objective as a proxy for the correlation metrics used in evaluation. Experiments across Mistral2-7B, Qwen3-8B, and Qwen3-32B show consistent gains over both SFT and standard RL baselines, with particularly strong out-of-domain generalization.

## Results

On Qwen3-32B, REAL achieves an average Pearson correlation of 71.0 and Spearman of 70.0 across four LLM-as-a-Judge benchmarks, representing gains of +8.40 Pearson and +7.20 Spearman over the TRACT SFT baseline and +18.30/+11.20 over the base model. On Mistral2-7B, REAL reaches 67.9 Pearson / 66.2 Spearman / 53.0 Kendall (average), outperforming the best SFT baseline (TRACT: 63.2/62.2/49.8) and standard RL (64.1/62.7/50.3). Gains are most pronounced on out-of-domain benchmarks (FLASK, Vicuna Bench, MT Bench), where REAL improves Pearson by 5–10 points over TRACT, while in-domain Feedback Bench shows smaller margins (~1 point).

## Baselines & Comparisons

REAL consistently outperforms all baselines across every model scale and benchmark. Against the strongest SFT baseline TRACT, REAL gains +4.7/+4.0/+3.2 (Pearson/Spearman/Kendall) on Mistral2-7B, +3.9/+2.2/+4.9 on Qwen3-8B, and +8.4/+7.2/+10.8 on Qwen3-32B, with the largest improvements on out-of-domain benchmarks (FLASK, Vicuna, MT Bench). Against Standard RL (binary accuracy reward), REAL improves by +3.8/+3.5/+2.7 on Mistral2-7B and +3.8/+2.0/+4.7 on Qwen3-8B, confirming that the policy-dependent regression reward (not merely the regression loss) drives the gains. Against JEPO (NeurIPS 2025), which optimizes marginal log-likelihood via Jensen's bound, REAL achieves +1.6/+1.4/+1.2 average correlation on Mistral2-7B. REAL also surpasses the Prometheus-2-7B reward model (60.7/59.8/47.8) by +7.2/+6.4/+5.2 on Mistral2-7B, and substantially outperforms zero-shot GPT-3.5-Turbo (38.3/34.8/28.5). A policy-independent regression reward (Standard RL with r_reg = −(y−y*)²) fails to improve over TRACT, confirming that the generalized policy gradient with policy-dependent reward is the critical ingredient.

## Relation to Prior Work

REAL extends the regression-aware fine-tuning lineage (RAIL → RAFT → TRACT) from the SFT regime into reinforcement learning, replacing static ground-truth CoT supervision with active policy-gradient exploration over self-generated reasoning trajectories. The key technical departure is the use of the generalized policy gradient (Schulman et al., 2015) to handle the fact that the regression reward is explicitly policy-dependent, which invalidates the standard REINFORCE/GRPO formulation. This yields a clean decomposition into a CoT exploration term (weighted by the regression reward) and a prediction refinement term (direct backpropagation through the expected-value predictor), positioning REAL as the first framework to unify ordinal-aware scoring with RL-based reasoning search.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LLM-as-a-Judge is becoming a critical infrastructure component for automated model evaluation, and the demonstrated correlation collapse under standard binary-reward RL (Fig. 2) highlights a practical failure mode that practitioners may encounter when applying off-the-shelf RL pipelines to scoring tasks. The theoretical result that squared-error minimization is also optimal for Pearson correlation provides principled justification for the regression objective beyond empirical tuning. Open questions include whether the approach extends to more complex multi-dimensional evaluation criteria, whether alternative stabilizers (PPO, GRPO) can replace RLOO without sacrificing the policy-dependent reward structure, and how sensitive the method is to noisy or biased training labels.
