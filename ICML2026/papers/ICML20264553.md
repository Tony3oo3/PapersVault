## Problem

Adversarial robustness literature treats all perturbations within a norm ball uniformly, conflating attacks that genuinely deceive the model (consistent: ground-truth label preserved) with those that merely alter the true class (inconsistent). No prior work provides a geometric characterization of when consistent attacks exist, separate error metrics isolating their impact, or an exact asymptotic analysis of how overparameterization modulates vulnerability to label-preserving perturbations in high-dimensional linear classification.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264553/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a high-dimensional covariate vector x ∈ ℝ^d (Gaussian), a trained linear classifier with weights ŵ, and an adversarial perturbation budget ε under a q-norm constraint. The key structural constraint is target-score invariance ⟨w⋆, δ⟩ = 0, which restricts admissible perturbations to a hyperplane orthogonal to the ground-truth weights. Outputs include a closed-form probability of consistent attack existence (Eq. 12), two novel error metrics (consistent robust error and consistent boundary error) expressed as two-dimensional Gaussian integrals in the asymptotic limit, and self-consistent equations characterizing the robust ERM estimator in both well-specified and latent-variable (overparameterized) settings.

## Method

The paper derives a geometric existence condition (Proposition 3.2) showing a consistent attack exists iff ε·d_{q⋆}⋆(ŵ⊥) ≥ |⟨ŵ, x⟩|, where ŵ⊥ is the predictor component orthogonal to the target and d_{q⋆}⋆ is the dual-norm distance to the target span. It then introduces two refined metrics—consistent robust error (excluding inconsistent perturbations) and consistent boundary error (counting only labels flipped by the attack, not already misclassified)—and derives their exact high-dimensional limits via the Gordon Min-Max theorem under proportional scaling (n, d, p → ∞ at fixed ratios). In the latent variable model capturing overparameterization, the robust ERM estimator is characterized by a system of self-consistent equations (Theorem 4.8), from which the consistent metrics reduce to integrals over a bivariate Gaussian with an attack-surface constant C depending on the predictor's latent-space projection. A key finding is that overparameterization has a regime-dependent effect: in the extreme overparameterized limit, the consistent robust error increases while the consistent boundary error decreases (Proposition 4.11), a dual behavior verified empirically on MNIST/Fashion-MNIST and a two-layer ReLU network.

## Results

The paper derives exact high-dimensional asymptotic formulas for three adversarial error metrics (E_rob, E_rob^cns, E_bnd^cns) in both well-specified and latent variable linear classification models. A key quantitative finding is that for ℓ₂ attacks, flipping 50% of labels requires a rescaled perturbation strength ε̃ ≈ 1 (i.e., ε ≈ d^{-1/2}), and consistent attacks are strictly less effective than inconsistent ones since the attack surface depends on √(τ−m²) rather than √τ. In the extreme overparameterization limit (ψ = p/n → ∞), Proposition 4.11 shows that E_rob^cns increases with overparameterization (∂_ψ E_rob^cns > 0) while E_bnd^cns decreases (∂_ψ E_bnd^cns < 0), revealing a regime-dependent dual effect. Empirical validation on MNIST (digits 0 vs 1) and Fashion-MNIST (Sandals vs Ankle Boots) with d=784 features confirms the qualitative predictions of the Gaussian theory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper without a traditional method-vs-method comparison table; instead, it compares internal variants of the adversarial error metrics and model settings. Consistent attacks (E_rob^cns) are always weaker than unrestricted attacks (E_rob) for the same perturbation budget, with the gap governed by the predictor's alignment m with the target weights. Among attack geometries, ℓ₂ attacks (q=2) produce higher robust errors than ℓ_∞ attacks (q=∞) under the same rescaled budget, showing that norm geometry matters beyond the raw perturbation size. In the latent variable model, the three metrics diverge with overparameterization: E_rob and E_bnd^cns increase mildly with ψ, while E_rob^cns decreases, and this dual behavior is confirmed to persist when replacing the linear estimator with a two-layer ReLU MLP (Figure 11). The paper also validates its Gaussian-theory predictions against the hidden manifold model (random Gaussian feature matrix) and real image data, finding consistent qualitative trends across all settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the high-dimensional asymptotic analysis of robust ERM (Vilucchio et al., 2026; Tanner et al., 2025; Javanmard et al., 2020) by introducing a consistency constraint that isolates label-preserving perturbations from arbitrary adversarial perturbations. It builds on the latent variable model of Hastie et al. (2022) and the Gaussian universality results for random features (Mei & Montanari, 2022; Goldt et al., 2022a), extending them from regression to binary classification with adversarial attacks. The geometric existence conditions (Propositions 3.2, 4.1) and the self-consistent equations for the robust estimator under misspecification (Theorem 4.8) are new technical contributions that go beyond prior robust ERM analyses, which characterized only the standard robust error E_rob without distinguishing consistent from inconsistent attacks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first exact asymptotic characterization of consistent adversarial attacks, revealing that overparameterization has a regime-dependent, non-monotonic effect on consistent robustness that contradicts the conventional narrative that more parameters always increase vulnerability. The introduction of the consistent boundary error E_bnd^cns as a separate diagnostic from E_rob^cns enables finer-grained analysis of where model failures originate. Key limitations include the Gaussian design assumption, the proportional high-dimensional regime, and the restriction to linear/random-features models (no feature learning); extending to approximate score invariance, non-Gaussian designs, and deeper architectures are identified as important next steps. The findings offer theoretical guidance for regularization and attack-budget selection in robust training, but practical transfer to deep networks remains to be established.
