## Problem

Most model merging methods operate on task vectors (differences between fine-tuned and pretrained weights) while treating the pretrained model as a static reference, leaving the role of pretrained weights in supporting broad generalization across merged tasks largely unexplored. This paper asks whether pretrained parameters are already near-optimal for downstream tasks and, if so, how their generalization should be preserved during merging.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264292/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained model's weight matrices and a merged task vector τ* (produced by any existing merging strategy such as Task Arithmetic, TSV, ISO, or LOT Merging) as input. For each layer, it computes the SVD of the pretrained weight to identify the top-r singular directions, then projects τ* onto the orthogonal complement via P_U^⊥ τ* P_V^⊥. The output is a merged model W_mtl = W_pre + λ·Φ^csp(τ*), requiring no additional data, training, or fine-tuning—only a lightweight per-layer SVD (5 s for ViT-B/32, 10 s for ViT-L/14 on a single GPU).

## Method

The paper introduces Core Subspace Preservation (CSP), a training-free plug-and-play module grounded in a constrained-optimization analysis showing that pretrained weights are near first-order stationary with predominantly non-negative curvature within their core subspace (the subspace spanned by dominant left and right singular vectors). Empirically, gradients of the multi-task objective at W_pre are predominantly orthogonal to this subspace, and ~89% of randomly sampled directions within it yield non-negative Hessian curvature, confirming near-optimality. CSP exploits this by projecting the merged task vector onto the orthogonal complement of the core subspace, discarding components that would disrupt pretrained generalization while retaining task-specific adaptation in the remaining directions. The ablation reveals an importance ordering U_r > V_r > Σ_r, and the method consistently improves all tested baselines across vision, vision-language, and language model merging with negligible computational overhead.

## Results

On eight vision classification tasks with ViT-B/32, CSP (Core Subspace Preservation) consistently improves all tested merging methods: Task Arithmetic rises from 69.1% to 72.7% average accuracy (+3.6), TSV from 84.1% to 85.1% (+1.0), and ISO-C from 84.1% to 84.9% (+0.8). With the larger ViT-L/14 backbone, TSV w/ CSP reaches 92.5% and ISO-CTS w/ CSP reaches 93.4%, both setting new bests among training-free methods. On vision-language tasks (BLIP), CSP substantially recovers VQA accuracy (e.g., OKVQA from 17.71% to 24.67% with Task Arithmetic; TextVQA from 0.49% to 10.28%), while on LLM merging (Qwen3-8B) TSV w/ CSP improves NDCG@10 on ArguAna from 0.72 to 0.73 and Banking77 accuracy from 84.72% to 84.83%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CSP is a plug-and-play projection module applied on top of existing merging methods, so it is evaluated as an augmentation rather than a standalone competitor. Against Task Arithmetic (Ilharco et al., 2023), CSP yields the largest absolute gains (+3.6% on ViT-B/32, +1.7% on ViT-L/14), indicating that naive linear combination of task vectors introduces the most harmful drift in the core subspace. Against the strongest training-free baselines—TSV (Gargiulo et al., 2025) and ISO-CTS (Marczak et al., 2025)—CSP still provides consistent improvements of +0.4 to +1.0% on vision tasks, with TSV + CSP achieving the best overall average accuracy (85.1% on ViT-B/32, 92.5% on ViT-L/14). Against LOT Merging (Sun et al., 2025c), gains are smaller (+0.5 to +0.9%) since LOT already partially addresses feature drift. On vision-language tasks, CSP dramatically improves VQA (OKVQA +5.4% with TSV, TextVQA +0.9%) but slightly degrades captioning CIDEr scores (−0.02 to −0.03), which the authors attribute to BLIP's weak zero-shot captioning violating the near-optimality assumption. On LLM merging (Qwen3-0.6B/8B), CSP improves NDCG@10 and Pearson correlation on ranking/embedding tasks by small but consistent margins (e.g., +0.02 NDCG on ArguAna for Qwen3-8B TSV). For LoRA-based merging, CSP improves KnOTS from 54.51% to 56.38% and RobustMerge from 55.05% to 56.97% average accuracy across seven vision tasks. CSP does not directly compete with test-time training methods (AdaMerging, Surgery, DOGE) in the main tables; those are reported only in the appendix (Tables 8–9) where CSP-augmented training-free methods (TSV + CSP at 85.1%) still outperform most test-time baselines (AdaMerging at 80.1%, Surgery at 80.9%) on ViT-B/32.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the task vector framework introduced by Task Arithmetic (Ilharco et al., 2023), where merging operates on differences between fine-tuned and pretrained weights. While prior methods (TSV, ISO Merging, LOT Merging) focus on resolving conflicts among task vectors or exploiting their spectral structure, this paper shifts the lens to the pretrained weights themselves, arguing they are near-optimal within their dominant singular subspace (the 'core subspace'). The proposed Core Subspace Preservation (CSP) module is conceptually related to Tang et al. (2026), which uses pretrained parameters as a shared MoE trunk, but provides a principled first- and second-order optimization explanation for why preserving those directions is effective. CSP is positioned as a plug-and-play post-processing step orthogonal to existing merging strategies rather than a replacement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CSP offers a nearly free (5–10 s SVD overhead) performance boost across vision, vision-language, and language model merging, making it practically attractive for practitioners who already use task-vector-based merging. The near-optimality characterization of pretrained weights in their core subspace provides a useful theoretical lens for understanding why certain merging operations are harmful, and the ablation revealing the importance ordering U_r > V_r > Σ_r opens a path toward finer-grained, layer-adaptive merging. Key open questions include automated rank selection (the paper shows Top-p spectral-energy thresholds help but are not fully explored), scalability of SVD to very large models, and whether the near-optimality assumption holds when pretraining and downstream distributions are substantially misaligned (as evidenced by the BLIP captioning failure case).
