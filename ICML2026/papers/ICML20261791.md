## Problem

While weight and activation quantization is well-studied, partial sums (psums) accumulated during tiled GEMM on systolic arrays retain high precision (e.g., 24-bit for a 128×128 array with 8-bit operands), making psum storage and data movement the dominant energy bottleneck. This paper identifies that within-tile psum outliers are input-dependent and proposes a post-training, input-aware quantizer that compresses psums to 7–8 bits with minimal accuracy loss.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261791/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on 8-bit quantized activations and weights (W8A8) streamed into a tiled systolic array. It produces 8-bit (or 7-bit) quantized partial sums in place of the conventional 24-bit psums, with a look-ahead scale predictor computing the step size S_PQ = |a·v| + b from the incoming activation vector before psums are latched into pipeline registers. At the global buffer, psums are accumulated with a wider bit-width B_acc = B_PQ + ⌈log₂(K/R)⌉ using shift-add alignment across distinct per-tile scales.

## Method

PsumQuant assigns a shared learnable projection vector v ∈ ℝ^R per output tile and predicts the quantization scale on-the-fly as S_PQ = |a·v| + b, where a is the incoming R-dimensional activation vector. The key empirical insight is that extreme psums within a tile are strongly driven by extreme input activations (correlation r=0.85 at 32×32 granularity in Llama-3.1), making input statistics a reliable predictor of the optimal scale. Calibration is performed post-training via layer-wise MSE minimization using LSQ-style gradients, with an adaptive weighting term κ_nz/E[κ_nz] that amplifies learning on outlier-heavy tiles. The predictor is implemented as zero-latency combinational logic (a look-ahead quantizer) placed before pipeline registers, avoiding the O(log²C) max-finder tree and deep FIFO buffers of dynamic quantization; the projection vector is stored locally in PEs following the weight-stationary dataflow, adding only 1/C computational overhead (less than 1% for C=128).

## Results

On Llama-3.1 8B with a 128×128 systolic array, PsumQuant achieves 6.14 perplexity on WikiText-2 (vs. 6.10 for the INT32-psum baseline and 6.46 for static quantization), a mere +0.04 PPL degradation. On ImageNet at B_PQ=6, PsumQuant recovers 70.04% top-1 accuracy on ResNet-18 (vs. 67.66% static, 71.12% INT32) and 81.02% on Swin-T (vs. 78.78% static, 81.24% INT32). Energy analysis on the 128×128 array shows total energy drops from 595.75 mJ (32-bit psum) to 197.06 mJ (8-bit psum), a 66.9% reduction, with the paper reporting a 45% reduction after accounting for the predictor overhead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PsumQuant is compared against two baselines: (1) INT32 psum (no psum quantization, the accuracy upper bound) and (2) tile-wise static psum quantization (a learnable fixed scale per tile, similar in spirit to APSQ but without QAT). Against the INT32 baseline, PsumQuant is near-lossless: +0.04 PPL on Llama-3.1 8B and −1.08% top-1 on ResNet-18 at 6-bit. Against static quantization, PsumQuant delivers substantial gains: 0.32 PPL lower on Llama-3.1 (6.14 vs. 6.46), +2.38% top-1 on ResNet-18 (70.04% vs. 67.66%), and +2.24% on Swin-T (81.02% vs. 78.78%). The paper also references a dynamic max-based quantizer in Fig. 10: PsumQuant matches it on Llama-3.1 at 7–8-bit and surpasses it on ResNet-18 at low bit-widths, while incurring far less hardware overhead (look-ahead logic <2% area/power vs. 4–15% for the max-finder tree). The advantage over static is most pronounced at aggressive 6-bit psums and on smaller array sizes (32×32, 64×64), where outlier severity is highest.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the partial-sum quantization line initiated by A2Q (Colbert et al., 2023) and APSQ (Tan et al., 2025), both of which require quantization-aware training, by formulating psum quantization as a post-training, input-aware scale prediction problem. It builds on the LSQ gradient formulation (Esser et al., 2020) for scale calibration but departs from prior CIM-focused psum work (Bai et al., 2023; Kim et al., 2022) by targeting the digital-domain buffer and accumulator bottleneck outside the compute array rather than ADC precision. The key conceptual departure is the use of a learnable projection vector over input activations as a zero-latency look-ahead scale predictor, combined with a nonzero crest-factor–aware gradient modulation that has no direct analogue in prior weight/activation quantization literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 45% total energy reduction on a 128×128 systolic array with only ~1% accuracy loss (ResNet-18) or +0.04 PPL (Llama-3.1) makes 8-bit psum accumulation practically viable for edge NPU deployment, addressing a bottleneck that prior W8A8 quantization work has left unaddressed. The post-training calibration (16 sequences, <1 hour on one GPU) is a significant practical advantage over QAT-based alternatives, especially for large LLMs where retraining is prohibitive. Open questions include whether the approach extends to microscaling formats (MXFP4/8) where block-shared exponents change the psum distribution, and whether the input-predictability of psum outliers holds for architectures with very different activation statistics (e.g., mixture-of-experts). The method is also limited to the specific tiled systolic-array dataflow and may require re-derivation for other accelerator topologies.
