## Problem

Online RL with purely numerical (scalar) feedback for LLM reasoning suffers from three fundamental limitations: performance plateaus despite data/compute scaling, ineffective spontaneous self-reflection, and persistent failures on specific training problems. While natural language critiques can correct these failures in a one-shot in-context setting, integrating expressive critiques into the online RL loop for active exploration and real-time policy adaptation remains unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265987/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a text question, the method samples n initial responses from the current policy, then a reward system (rule-based string matching or a model-based CoT critic) produces both a scalar reward and a natural language critique for each response. When all n responses are incorrect, the model performs in-context self-refinement conditioned on the (question, response, critique) triplet plus a refinement instruction, yielding k refined responses. The final training group combines initial and refined responses, and the output is an updated policy that has internalized both standard exploration trajectories and critique-guided correction trajectories.

## Method

Critique-GRPO extends GRPO with a dual objective: a standard clipped-surrogate loss on initial responses plus a shaping-based loss on a selectively sampled subset of critique-guided refinements. The shaping function replaces the standard importance-sampling ratio for refinements with ρ_t = π_θ(y_t|y_<t) / (π_θ(y_t|y_<t) + γ), which amplifies gradients on tokens that are currently low-probability under the policy, enabling efficient absorption of unfamiliar but correct reasoning patterns. Refinements are triggered only when all initial responses fail, and a quality-based selection (prioritizing correct refinements) mitigates entropy explosion from distribution shift. Theoretically, the paper shows via the Transfer Eluder Dimension framework that CoT critiques reduce the effective search complexity from O(|S|^L) (binary reward) to O(|S|·L) or O(L) (when the critique localizes the first error step), explaining why critique-guided exploration reaches high-quality trajectories unreachable by scalar-reward trial-and-error within the same computational budget.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-7B-Base, Critique-GRPO (CoT Critique) achieves 47.08% average Pass@1 across eight reasoning tasks, improving over R1-GRPO (41.18%) by +5.9 points and R1-Dr.GRPO (42.66%) by +4.4 points. On the stronger Qwen3-8B reasoning model, Critique-GRPO (CoT Critique) reaches 68.26% average Pass@1, surpassing R1-GRPO (63.75%) by +4.5 points and R1-Dr.GRPO (64.46%) by +3.8 points. On the math-centric Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Base, Critique-GRPO achieves 51.06% average using only 4k training prompts, outperforming PRIME-Zero (which uses 46k prompts with dense intermediate rewards) and SimpleRL-Zero. Self-critiquing with Critique-GRPO yields a +16.7% Pass@1 gain over GRPO on AIME 2024 (60.0% vs. 50.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Critique-GRPO (CoT Critique) outperforms all eight baselines on both Qwen2.5-7B-Base and Qwen3-8B. Against the strongest RL baseline R1-Dr.GRPO (Liu et al., 2025a), Critique-GRPO gains +4.4 points (47.08% vs. 42.66%) on Qwen2.5-7B-Base and +3.8 points (68.26% vs. 64.46%) on Qwen3-8B in average Pass@1. Against the best SFT-based method CITL-FT (Xi et al., 2024), Critique-GRPO leads by +11.4 points (47.08% vs. 35.66%) on Qwen2.5-7B-Base and +12.4 points (68.26% vs. 55.84%) on Qwen3-8B, demonstrating that online joint optimization substantially surpasses offline imitation of critiques and refinements. On the math-centric Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Base, Critique-GRPO (51.06% avg with 4k prompts) outperforms PRIME-Zero (Cui et al., 2025a, which uses 46k prompts with dense process rewards) and SimpleRL-Zero (Zeng et al., 2025), confirming that verbal credit assignment from critiques is more sample-efficient than dense numerical rewards. RAFT (Dong et al., 2023) and Refinement FT (Chen et al., 2024) trail Critique-GRPO by 12.8 and 11.9 points respectively on Qwen2.5-7B-Base, while Critique FT (Wang et al., 2025) lags by 12.3 points, indicating that learning to generate critiques alone is insufficient without the RL exploration loop.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GRPO online RL framework (Shao et al., 2024a) by integrating natural language critiques as an additional feedback channel alongside scalar rewards, building on the shaping function from Yan et al. (2025) for off-policy refinement learning and the bias-removal modifications from Liu et al. (2025a). It departs from prior work on learning from natural language feedback (Wang et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2024; Xi et al., 2024), which relies on offline SFT to imitate static critiques, by embedding critique-guided refinement directly into the online RL loop. The theoretical analysis draws on the Transfer Eluder Dimension framework (Xu et al., 2025) to formalize why informative language feedback reduces search-space complexity from exponential to linear, providing a principled justification for the empirical gains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a simple architectural addition to GRPO—sampling critique-guided refinements and jointly optimizing on both initial and refined responses—yields consistent improvements across five model architectures and eight reasoning benchmarks, with a striking 10× data efficiency advantage (4k vs. 46k prompts) over dense-reward methods like PRIME-Zero. The self-critiquing capability (+16.7% on AIME 2024) suggests a path toward autonomous improvement without external oracle models. Open questions include the dependency on high-quality critique models (GPT-4o, Llama-3.1-405B), whether the approach scales beyond 32B parameters, and whether the gains persist when the critique model is significantly weaker than the policy model (the weak-to-strong experiment uses only indicative critiques with ground truth, not full CoT critiques).
