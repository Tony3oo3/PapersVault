## Problem

Standard decoding in Masked Diffusion Models suffers from context rigidity: early tokens are locked in based on transient confidence under sparse context, and existing revision strategies (e.g., ReMDM) rely on static confidence scores that become stale as the surrounding context evolves. This causes the model to miss context-brittle tokens—those that appear confident but are structurally incompatible with the updated context—leading to cascading inconsistencies, particularly in structure-sensitive domains like code generation.

## Input / Output

CORE operates as a plug-in revision module on top of a base masked diffusion decoder (LLaDA-8B-Base or Dream-7B), taking a text prompt and the partially unmasked sequence state at each diffusion step. It intervenes every E=8 steps within a normalized decoding window [0.25, 0.75), evaluating m=32 candidate positions per invocation with k_rm=1 revision, adding exactly one auxiliary forward pass per revision step (~6% overhead over 128 base steps). The output is the final generated text sequence of length L=512, produced under greedy decoding to isolate structural consistency from sampling variance.

## Method

CORE reframes revision as a distributionally robust optimization problem: rather than trusting static token probabilities, it identifies context-brittle tokens by probing their sensitivity to adversarial context perturbations. Concretely, it (1) selects a candidate subset S_t of low top-2 probability margin positions (justified by Theorem 4.1, which proves high-margin tokens resist probability inversion under Lipschitz-bounded context shifts), (2) simultaneously masks all positions in S_t to form a perturbed context, and (3) computes an instability score for each candidate as the negative log-likelihood of its current token under the perturbed context in a single forward pass. The top-k_rm most unstable tokens are then revised by assigning the argmax token under the perturbed context. This zero-temperature limit of a continuous masking distribution provides a certified lower bound on the worst-case instability objective, enabling tractable inference-time revision without any training or architectural modification.

## Results

On LLaDA-8B-Base with greedy decoding (N=128, L=512), CORE achieves 24.80% pass@1 on MBPP (3-shot), a +9.2% absolute gain over the Low-Confidence base (15.60%), and 29.60% under the Top-k Margin base (21.20%). HumanEval (0-shot) improves from 12.20% to 17.07% (+4.87 pp) under Low-Confidence and from 17.07% to 22.56% (+5.49 pp) under Top-k Margin. On reasoning benchmarks, CORE gains +1.37 pp on BBH (45.81→47.18) and +0.34 pp on MATH (16.72→17.06) over the Low-Confidence base, with statistical significance (p<0.05) on BBH, HumanEval, and MBPP. Cross-architecture validation on Dream-7B yields comparable ~9% absolute gains on both HumanEval (+9.16 pp) and MBPP (+9.00 pp).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ReMDM-conf (Wang et al., 2025), the primary training-free revision baseline, CORE wins decisively on code generation: under Low-Confidence unmasking, CORE reaches 24.80% on MBPP while ReMDM-conf drops to 15.20% (a 9.6 pp gap), and under Top-k Margin, CORE hits 29.60% versus ReMDM-conf's 14.80% (14.8 pp gap). ReMDM-conf also degrades HumanEval (10.98% vs. 17.07% under Low-Confidence), confirming that stale confidence signals are counterproductive for structure-sensitive tasks. Against the LLaDA Low-Confidence base (Nie et al., 2025), CORE improves every benchmark, with the largest deltas on code (MBPP +9.2 pp, HumanEval +4.87 pp) and a significant +1.37 pp on BBH. Against the Top-k Margin base (Kim et al., 2025b), CORE again dominates on code (MBPP +8.4 pp, HumanEval +5.49 pp) and BBH (+0.68 pp), while matching or slightly exceeding the base on GSM8K and MATH. Cross-architecture, CORE on Dream-7B (Ye et al., 2025) yields +9.16 pp on HumanEval (20.10→29.26) and +9.00 pp on MBPP (25.60→34.60), confirming the instability metric transfers without retuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the masked diffusion language model (MDM) decoding pipeline (LLaDA, Dream-7B) and the emerging line of inference-time revision strategies (ReMDM, P2-Self, PRISM, RemeDi, GIDD) that attempt to revisit previously committed tokens. Its key conceptual departure is reframing revision target selection from static confidence or top-k margin heuristics—computed under a single decoding state—to a distributionally robust optimization problem that probes token stability under adversarial context perturbations. The theoretical grounding via Lipschitz-continuity bounds on probability margins (Theorem 4.1) and the lower-bound certification of computed instability against worst-case risk (Appendix C) provide a formal justification absent from prior heuristic remasking work. Unlike learned-policy approaches (PRISM, RemeDi) or architecture-modifying methods (VMD), CORE is training-free and plug-and-play, requiring only one additional forward pass per revision interval.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CORE offers a practical, zero-training mechanism to improve structural consistency in diffusion LMs with minimal overhead (~6% more forward passes), and the ~9% absolute gains on code generation (MBPP, HumanEval) across two architectures (LLaDA-8B, Dream-7B) make it immediately applicable to latency-sensitive deployment. The finding that confidence-based remasking (ReMDM-conf) can actively degrade code performance by 6.4% highlights a previously underappreciated failure mode: stale confidence signals reinforce rather than resolve structural errors. The main limitation is that CORE improves internal structural consistency but does not guarantee external factual correctness, and the method's benefit is most pronounced on syntax-constrained tasks (code) rather than open-ended reasoning. Open questions include whether the robustness framing extends to multi-token or span-level revisions, and whether the instability signal can be combined with reward or verifier guidance for broader quality improvements.
