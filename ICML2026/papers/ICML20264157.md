## Problem

Existing POI representation learning either encodes static textual metadata (capturing identity but not function) or learns trajectory-context-dependent embeddings optimized for next-location prediction (capturing movement regularities but not how places are actually used). The paper argues that POI function — the temporal usage patterns that distinguish, e.g., a quick-turnover chain coffee shop from a local café where people linger — is a missing but essential signal for general, context-independent POI representations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264157/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes static POI text embeddings (768-dim, from models like OpenAI or Gemini, constructed via GeoLLM-style prompts including coordinates, category, address, and 10 nearest POIs) alongside anonymized GPS-derived visit sequences (arrival/departure timestamps and POI coordinates, windowed at length 32). It produces a single 512-dim mobility-enriched POI embedding per location that is context-independent and usable across five downstream map-enrichment tasks (opening hours, closure detection, visit intent, busyness, price level) via lightweight frozen-embedding probing heads.

## Method

ME-POIs encodes each visit by concatenating a Space2Vec multi-scale location code with Time2Vec arrival and departure codes, then contextualizes the sequence with a 4-layer, 8-head Transformer. A learnable POI prototype matrix is aligned to these contextualized visit embeddings via an InfoNCE contrastive objective, so each prototype aggregates usage patterns across all users and timestamps into a single functional centroid. To handle long-tail sparsity, a multi-scale Gaussian-kernel mechanism (bandwidths 0.3/1.0/3.0 km) transfers empirical weekly visit-distribution priors from top-k anchor POIs to sparse neighbors, enforced through a KL-divergence loss on a softmax head over the prototype. A second KL loss directly supervises anchor POI prototypes against their own empirical distributions, and a cosine-alignment loss projects the static text embedding into the mobility space to preserve semantic grounding. The total pretraining loss combines all four terms with equal weights, and downstream tasks are solved by concatenating independently projected mobility and text embeddings into a small task-specific MLP head.

## Results

On the Los Angeles dataset, ME-POIs achieves 0.554 F1 / 0.722 AUROC on weekly opening hours, 0.766 F1 on permanent closure detection, 0.291 F1 on visit intent classification, 0.154 MAE on busyness estimation, and 0.609 accuracy on price level classification, outperforming all 11 mobility-based baselines across every task. When augmenting text embeddings, ME-POIs yields up to 16.2% F1 gain on opening hours (OPENAI-LARGE), 81.9% F1 gain on visit intent (GEMINI), 75.1% F1 gain on price level (GTR-T5, Houston), and a 24.7% MAE reduction on busyness (GEMINI). Notably, ME-POIs trained without any text alignment (mobility-only) already surpasses all mobility baselines and even outperforms GEMINI text embeddings on price level classification (0.600 vs. 0.559 accuracy in LA).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ME-POIs outperforms all 11 mobility-based baselines (SKIP-GRAM, POI2VEC, GEO-TEASER, TALE, HIER, CTLE, DEEPMOVE, STAN, GRAPH-FLASHBACK, GETNEXT, TRAJGPT) on every task in both Los Angeles and Houston. The strongest mobility baseline is HIER, which reaches 0.473 F1 on opening hours and 0.660 F1 on permanent closure in LA, yet ME-POIs still leads by 8.1 and 10.6 F1 points respectively; on visit intent, ME-POIs (0.291 F1) exceeds the best mobility baseline (HIER, 0.183 F1) by 59%. Among text baselines, ME-POIs augmentation consistently improves every model: OPENAI-LARGE gains 16.2% F1 on opening hours, GEMINI gains 81.9% F1 on visit intent and 20.2% accuracy on price level, and E5 gains 79.4% F1 on visit intent in LA. The mobility-only variant (ME-POIs w/o L_text-align) already beats all mobility baselines and even surpasses GEMINI text embeddings on price level (0.600 vs. 0.559 accuracy), confirming that the architectural design—not textual metadata—drives the gains. On Houston, the same pattern holds: ME-POIs leads all baselines on all four evaluated tasks, with the largest margins on visit intent (0.306 F1 vs. 0.214 for SKIP-GRAM) and price level (0.590 accuracy vs. 0.555 for POI2VEC).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of two established lines of work: static POI representation learning (GeoLLM, GeoLM, SPA-BERT) and mobility-informed POI embeddings (POI2Vec, DeepMove, TrajGPT). It departs from prior mobility models by replacing trajectory-centric, next-location-prediction objectives with a POI-centric contrastive alignment that aggregates visit-level signals into a context-independent global prototype, and from prior text-based methods by explicitly encoding 'function' (how a place is used over time) rather than only 'identity' (what a place is). The multi-scale Gaussian-kernel distribution transfer for sparse POIs is a novel mechanism not present in either lineage, borrowing the multi-scale spatial encoding idea from Space2Vec but repurposing it for temporal pattern propagation rather than coordinate encoding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that POI function—captured through longitudinal visitation patterns—is a signal that neither static text metadata nor trajectory-prediction embeddings encode, and that integrating it yields consistent gains across diverse map-enrichment tasks. The introduction of five new benchmark tasks (opening hours, closure detection, visit intent, busyness, price level) provides a reusable evaluation suite for POI representation quality beyond next-location prediction. Limitations include evaluation on only two US cities, reliance on SafeGraph/Google Maps labels that may not generalize globally, and the 'function' concept remaining somewhat loosely operationalized. Open questions include whether the framework extends to other geospatial objects (road segments, administrative regions) as the authors note, and whether the contrastive objective scales to much larger POI corpora without the in-batch negative approximation becoming a bottleneck.
