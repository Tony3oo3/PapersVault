## Problem

Audio foundation models excel at processing individual sounds but fail on complex multi-source acoustic scenes—what the authors term 'audio stories'—that combine multiple speakers, foreground/background effects, and ambient noise with semantic, temporal, and physical interdependencies. No existing model can simultaneously understand, generate, and perform open-ended fine-grained editing of such scenes, and the field lacks both training data with per-source annotations and evaluation metrics that measure task performance rather than distributional similarity.

## Input / Output

The model accepts stereo 48kHz audio (encoded to 40Hz continuous 256-dim latents via a frozen DAC-VAE-style tokenizer) and free-form text instructions as input, supporting multi-turn interactions where prior audio outputs serve as context for subsequent edits. It produces 48kHz stereo waveforms, a structured chain-of-thought reasoning chain that decomposes abstract instructions into individual sound sources with explicit parameters (caption, loudness in dB, panning, start time, duration), and timestamped audio captions for understanding tasks. Unlike prior unified audio LLMs limited to 16kHz mono, AudioChat preserves spatial characteristics (panning, per-source loudness) and generates intelligible speech alongside non-speech effects.

## Method

AudioChat uses a Self-Cascaded Transformer (SCT) architecture that splits a 39-layer Gemma 2 2B backbone into 26 understanding layers (optimized by a causal LM head) and 13 generation layers (optimized by a diffusion head), with a single continuous audio tokenizer shared across both tasks. Training employs Audio Transfusion Forcing, which combines a causal language modeling loss for structured CoT reasoning with a multi-turn diffusion forcing loss that independently samples a noise timestep for each audio span across editing turns—preventing the model from trivially copying input latents and enabling parallel training over arbitrary numbers of editing turns. Training data is synthesized by AudioCopilot, a tool-calling LLM agent (Gemma 3 27B) that simulates 6M multi-turn user–sound-designer dialogues, invoking TTS and T2A tools to render and mix individual sound sources into coherent scenes. A custom attention mask applies causal attention to text, bidirectional attention within each audio span, and causal attention between spans, enabling multi-turn editing while preserving generation quality.

## Results

On the audio editing task (StoryGen-Eval), AudioChat achieves the best scores across all six metrics: KAD 0.22, FAD 0.02, human consistency 3.92/5, ΔmultiFLAM 11.7, human instruction following 3.12/5, and editFLAM 18.6. For audio storytelling, AudioChat achieves KAD 2.52 and multiFLAM 44.1 with 32s latency, substantially outperforming SAO (KAD 7.98) and WavJourney (KAD 10.82, 628s latency). On fine-grained audio understanding, AudioChat achieves tcpWER 9.7 and multiFLAM 86.3, far surpassing WhisperX (tcpWER 55.9) and approaching the dedicated Whisper-Story system (tcpWER 5.5, multiFLAM 88.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AudioChat decisively outperforms the DiT baseline [Peebles & Xie, 2023] on every editing metric, with KAD 0.22 vs. 1.74 and editFLAM 18.6 vs. 2.7, indicating that the Transfusion Forcing objective and MLLM architecture are critical for successful editing. Against Stable Audio Open [Evans et al., 2025] on storytelling, AudioChat achieves lower KAD (2.52 vs. 7.98) and higher multiFLAM (44.1 vs. 35.6), though SAO is 3.5× faster (9.1s vs. 32s). Compared to WavJourney [Liu et al., 2025b], AudioChat is 20× faster (32s vs. 628s) with much better KAD (2.52 vs. 10.82), while WavJourney edges ahead on multiFLAM (47.1 vs. 44.1). On audio understanding, AudioChat (tcpWER 9.7, multiFLAM 86.3) substantially outperforms Qwen2-Audio [Chu et al., 2024] (tcpWER 74.5, multiFLAM 63.6) and Audio Flamingo 3 [Ghosh et al., 2025] (tcpWER 100+, multiFLAM 76.6), and far exceeds WhisperX [Bain et al., 2023] (tcpWER 55.9), though the fine-tuned Whisper-Story variant remains slightly stronger (tcpWER 5.5, multiFLAM 88.1). In the T2A ablation, AudioChat's SCT architecture (FAD 0.12, CLAP 65.5) matches or exceeds TangoFlux [Hung et al., 2024] (FAD 0.23, CLAP 56.1) and SAO (FAD 0.13, CLAP 63.4) on generation quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

AudioChat extends Transfusion (Zhou et al., 2025) from single-turn vision to multi-turn audio by introducing the Transfusion Forcing objective, and adapts Diffusion Forcing (Chen et al., 2024a) to independently noise audio latents across editing turns to prevent trivial copying. Architecturally, it departs from BAGEL and MoT-based approaches by proposing the simpler Self-Cascaded Transformer (SCT), which sequentially splits layers between understanding and generation rather than duplicating the full LLM. The paper also positions itself against unified audio MLLMs (UALM, Bagpiper, MingUniAudio) by using a single continuous tokenizer instead of separate discrete ones, and against cascaded agent systems (WavJourney) by unifying generation, editing, and understanding end-to-end with structured CoT reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AudioChat is the first model to unify open-ended multi-source audio storytelling, editing, and fine-grained understanding in a single end-to-end system, and the three proposed metrics (multiFLAM, ΔmultiFLAM, editFLAM) address a genuine gap in evaluating multi-source audio scenes where distribution-based metrics like FAD/KAD and CLAP-based scores fail. The AudioCopilot synthetic data pipeline offers a scalable recipe for generating fine-grained multi-source training data without manual annotation. Key limitations include reliance on synthetic training data (with only modest transfer to real audio), 8-second clip length, and the model not being publicly released; the authors note future work on adding visual capabilities for omni-modal processing.
