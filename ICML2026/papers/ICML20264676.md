## Problem

Autoregressive single-cell foundation models impose an artificial sequential order on inherently unordered gene expression profiles, introducing ordering bias and error accumulation, while Gaussian diffusion models fail to capture the discrete, dropout-like nature of gene non-detection. Existing tokenization strategies also struggle to jointly model discrete gene identity and continuous expression magnitude within a fixed token budget, often sacrificing numerical fidelity for binary presence/absence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264676/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a length-1,200 serialized gene sequence per cell, where each position carries a discrete gene identity (from a 41,818-gene vocabulary) and a continuous log-normalized expression value, plus a prepended [LAT] anchor token that is never masked. For perturbation prediction, a dedicated [PERT] token encodes the intervention identity. The primary output is a cell embedding extracted from the [LAT] hidden state, which serves as the representation for all downstream tasks; the denoising heads additionally produce per-position gene identity logits and scalar value predictions for reconstruction.

## Method

ScDiVa formulates generation as a masked discrete diffusion process where the forward step replaces each gene token with an absorbing [MASK] state with probability t (t ~ Unif(0,1)), directly mirroring dropout-like signal loss in scRNA-seq. A 12-layer bidirectional Transformer (94.5M parameters, RoPE attention, SwiGLU, Pre-RMSNorm) performs the reverse denoising, predicting both gene identity (cross-entropy) and expression value (MSE) on masked positions via a dual denoising loss. Entropy-normalized serialization allocates the fixed 1,200-token budget by ranking genes with v_k / (H(g_k) + ε), prioritizing discriminative over ubiquitous housekeeping genes. A latent anchor token [LAT] aggregates global cell context through self-attention and anchors generation to the cell-state manifold even when 90% of tokens are masked, preventing posterior collapse. The model is pre-trained self-supervised on 59M cells over 4 epochs, then adapted via task-specific fine-tuning (full encoder for integration/perturbation, last-4-layer fine-tuning for annotation, frozen backbone + MLP for zero-shot).

## Results

On rank-value reconstruction, scDiVa achieves the best Spearman correlation on Immune (0.970, +14.9% over GeneMamba) and PBMC12k (0.812, +14.2%), with the lowest L-Dist on PBMC12k (5) and Immune (4). On multi-batch integration across five datasets, scDiVa attains the best Avg-batch on 4/5 datasets and best Avg-bio on 4/5, with particularly strong gains on BMMC (0.8712 vs. 0.8014 for CellFM) and COVID-19 (0.6689 vs. 0.6476 for scGPT). On perturbation prediction, scDiVa leads all learned methods on Adamson across C-DEP (0.337), DE-AUPRC (0.421), LFCSpear (0.543), and HR@20 (0.529), and achieves the best C-DEP (0.271) and LFCSpear (0.433) among learned models on Norman.

## Baselines & Comparisons

scDiVa outperforms Geneformer, scGPT, scFoundation, UCE, scELMO, GeneCompass, GEARS, and scBERT across nearly all evaluated tasks and metrics. On multi-batch integration, scDiVa leads in Avg-batch on 4/5 datasets and Avg-bio on 4/5, with the largest margin on BMMC (0.8712 vs. 0.8014 for CellFM, +8.7%) and COVID-19 (0.6689 vs. 0.6476 for scGPT, +3.3%). The closest competitor is CellFM, which edges out scDiVa on PBMC12k Avg-bio (0.9741 vs. 0.9566) and Immune Avg-bio (0.7934 vs. 0.7785), while GeneMamba slightly leads on Perirhinal Cortex Avg-batch (0.9673 vs. 0.9542). On perturbation prediction, scDiVa dominates all learned baselines on Adamson (e.g., C-DEP 0.337 vs. 0.289 for CellFM; HR@20 0.529 vs. 0.458 for CellFM), though the simple Additive baseline remains competitive on Norman (C-DEP 0.269 vs. scDiVa's 0.271). On rank-value reconstruction, scDiVa achieves the highest Spearman on three of four datasets, with the most notable gain being +14.9% over GeneMamba on Immune (0.970 vs. 0.844).

## Relation to Prior Work

ScDiVa adapts the absorbing-state masked discrete diffusion framework from Austin et al. (2021) and the simplified continuous-time formulation of Shi et al. (2024) to the single-cell genomics domain, replacing the autoregressive factorization used by scBERT, Geneformer, scGPT, and scFoundation. The key conceptual departure is aligning the [MASK] absorbing state with biological dropout in scRNA-seq, providing a biologically grounded noise model where Gaussian perturbation (scDiffusion, scVAEDer) or sequential token prediction (AR models) are structurally mismatched. Additional design choices—entropy-normalized serialization to allocate a fixed token budget toward discriminative genes, and a latent anchor token for high-mask stability—are novel to this application but build on standard information-theoretic and conditional-generation ideas.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a principled, biologically motivated alternative to autoregression for single-cell foundation models, and the dropout-diffusion isomorphism (Theorem A.1) gives a clean formal justification for why masked corruption is the right noise model for sparse count data. However, the proprietary 59M-cell pre-training corpus and withheld training code limit independent verification, and the results are competitive rather than uniformly dominant—GeneMamba and CellFM outperform scDiVa on several integration and annotation metrics, and simple Additive baselines remain strong in combinatorial perturbation prediction. Open questions include whether the masked-diffusion advantage persists at even larger scale, whether entropy-normalized serialization is truly necessary versus simpler ranking, and whether the framework extends naturally to multi-omics or spatial transcriptomics settings.
