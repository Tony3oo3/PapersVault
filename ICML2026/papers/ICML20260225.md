## Problem

Existing layout-to-image (L2I) diffusion methods suffer from representation fragmentation under few-shot atypical settings (aerial, underwater, low-light), producing fragmented textures and geometric distortions. This failure stems from a granularity mismatch: high-level semantic identity demands global cross-instance consistency, while low-level visual details are inherently local and highly variable, yet existing methods entangle both in a single representation. Few-shot L2I generation in atypical domains remains largely unexplored, with no method explicitly separating these heterogeneous granularities.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a structured layout (bounding boxes with category labels), a global text caption, and exactly K=5 exemplar images per novel category, all resized to 512×512. The exemplar pool is constructed by cropping foreground objects from the few-shot images using ground-truth boxes, serving as the source for both semantic anchors and visual primitives. The base model (Stable Diffusion v1.5) is fully frozen during novel adaptation; only the parameters of the three proposed disentanglement modules are updated over 100 full-batch fine-tuning steps, producing a 512×512 RGB image conditioned on the novel layout.

## Method

The framework explicitly disentangles representations into two granularities injected at different U-Net stages. Semantic Anchoring encodes exemplar crops with frozen DINOv2, condenses them via a Resampler cross-attention, then computes intra-exemplar self-attention averages to form per-exemplar semantic anchors; these are fused into foreground embeddings via a gated cross-attention (gating factor η initialized to 0) and injected into middle/early upsampling blocks. Primitive Imbuing learns s=128 recomposable visual prototypes per category via alternating minimization with Tikhonov-regularized ridge regression on flattened DINOv2 features, then injects them into the final two upsampling stages through a spatially gated cross-attention that confines influence to the most salient foreground regions. Conceptual Steering introduces a saliency-aware loss that computes Grad-CAM activation divergence between ground-truth and predicted images, producing a spatial penalty mask Ω that amplifies gradients in under-activated foreground regions, preventing the model from shortcutting to background fitting.

## Results

Under the 5-shot setting, the proposed method achieves the best performance across all three atypical domains. On DIOR (aerial), it reduces Bootstrap FID to 74.34 (vs. 82.51 for CC-Diff) and reaches 26.06% mAP, 57.22% AP50, and 20.46% AP75. On RUOD (underwater), it attains 45.44 FID and 19.45% mAP, and on ExDark (low-light), it achieves 91.36 FID and 35.93% mAP, consistently outperforming all baselines on both fidelity and layout alignment metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three state-of-the-art L2I methods: MIGC (Zhou et al., CVPR 2024), CC-Diff (Zhang et al., 2024), and CC-Diff++ (Zhang et al., IEEE TGRS 2025). The proposed method wins on every metric across all three datasets. The largest margin is on DIOR, where Bootstrap FID improves by 8.17 over CC-Diff (74.34 vs. 82.51) and 14.86 over MIGC (89.20), while mAP gains 1.15 points over CC-Diff and 3.31 over MIGC. On RUOD, the FID advantage is smaller (45.44 vs. 46.46 for CC-Diff++) but mAP still leads by 1.08 points. On ExDark, the FID gap narrows to ~1.7–2.8 points, yet the method still achieves the best mAP (35.93%) and AP75 (33.13%). A secondary evaluation with YOLOv8 on DIOR confirms detector-agnostic gains of 1.30% mAP and 2.14% AP50 over the strongest baseline, reinforcing that the improvements are not an artifact of a specific detection backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the layout-to-image (L2I) generation line (MIGC, CC-Diff, CC-Diff++) by introducing a representation-disentanglement framework specifically for few-shot atypical domains. It draws on latent diffusion (Stable Diffusion v1.5) as the backbone, Perceiver-style Resampler modules for feature condensation, and ridge-regression-based prototype learning (inspired by Wertheimer et al., 2021) for local visual primitives. The key conceptual departure from prior L2I methods is the explicit separation of globally invariant semantic identity (Semantic Anchoring via DINOv2 + cross-attention) from locally variable visual details (Primitive Imbuing via K-Means-initialized prototypes), coupled with a Grad-CAM-based saliency-aware loss (Conceptual Steering) to counteract the foreground-background distributional asymmetry in few-shot transfer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: generating high-fidelity images in data-scarce atypical domains (aerial, underwater, low-light) where collecting large paired datasets is infeasible, and demonstrates that only 5 exemplars and 100 fine-tuning steps suffice for meaningful improvement. However, the framework's reliance on external frozen encoders (DINOv2, CLIP, Grad-CAM) limits its self-sufficiency, and validation is confined to UNet-based Stable Diffusion v1.5 rather than modern Diffusion Transformers, leaving open whether the disentanglement principle transfers to sequence-based attention architectures. The ablation showing that swapping the roles of Semantic Anchoring and Primitive Imbuing causes severe performance collapse (mAP 26→12) underscores that the granularity hierarchy is not arbitrary, suggesting the semantic-primitive separation is a genuine structural requirement rather than a design choice.
