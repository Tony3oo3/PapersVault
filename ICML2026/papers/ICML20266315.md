## Problem

Outcome-based RL for LLM agents in active reasoning can induce a systematic failure mode called information self-locking (SeL), where agents fail both to elicit informative feedback and to internalize obtained evidence. The paper identifies the root cause as a bidirectional credit-assignment bottleneck: weak belief tracking masks the reward credit of informative actions, while weak action selection deprives belief tracking of useful evidence, causing both capabilities to stagnate under sparse outcome rewards.

## Input / Output

AREW consumes on-policy trajectories from the agent, the standard outcome reward, and stepwise binary directional critiques z_t ∈ {-1, 0, +1} assigned to each action-selection and belief-tracking step. AS critiques are derived from whether the environment reveals novel evidence in response to a query; BT critiques are derived from the sign of change in a prompted confidence readout over the ground-truth candidate. The output is a minimally modified policy-gradient update where per-step advantages are additively reweighted (Â_t = A_t + λu_t), leaving the reward function, critic target, and RL algorithm unchanged.

## Method

AREW (Advantage Reweighting) breaks SeL by converting uncalibrated directional critiques into intra-trajectory credit reallocation at the policy-gradient level. For each trajectory, it partitions steps into positively and negatively critiqued sets, then constructs a margin-aware auxiliary objective that increases log-probability mass on positive steps relative to negative ones. The gradient of this objective has the same form as a standard policy gradient with per-step coefficients u_t = 1/|P_τ| (positive), −1/|N_τ| (negative), or 0 (abstained), which are injected as an additive advantage shift while preserving the original outcome reward. A theoretical proposition shows AREW improves AS informativeness whenever the weighted accuracy of critiques exceeds 1/2, meaning it only requires directional signals to be better than random rather than calibrated step rewards. The approach is calibration-free, requires no separate reward model, and is compatible with PPO, GRPO, and GSPO.

## Results

Across 7 agentic active-reasoning tasks and two model families (Qwen-2.5-7B, LLaMA-3.1-8B), AREW-AS+BT consistently outperforms vanilla PPO, with gains ranging from +3.7 (FloDial-Easy, Qwen) to +66.7 (PE-G_{S=3}, LLaMA) in average outcome reward. The largest single-task improvement is 62.0 points on PE-G_{S=3} (Qwen: 18.33 → 80.33) and 66.7 points on the same task for LLaMA (11.00 → 77.67). On the practical τ²-bench dual-control setting, AREW raises average reward from 0.20 to 0.50 (2.5× relative increase) over vanilla PPO. AREW also improves AS and BT capability proxies in 27 of 28 evaluated settings, confirming that the gains reflect genuine information-acquisition and belief-tracking improvements rather than shortcut exploitation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AREW is compared against vanilla PPO, GRPO, and GSPO as RL baselines, as well as direct-inference baselines (o4-mini, Qwen-2.5-7B, LLaMA-3.1-8B). Against vanilla PPO, AREW-AS+BT wins on all 7 tasks for both model families, with the most dramatic gains on PE-G_{S=3} (+62.0 Qwen, +66.7 LLaMA) and PE-F_{D=6} (+12.4 Qwen, +48.7 LLaMA); the smallest gain is +3.7 on FloDial-Easy (Qwen). Against GRPO and GSPO (Fig. 6b–c), AREW similarly improves final task performance while strengthening AS and BT proxies, indicating that group-based sampling alone does not resolve the self-locking coupling. On τ²-bench dual-control, AREW achieves 0.50 average reward versus 0.20 for vanilla PPO (2.5×), while also reducing over-reliance on user-side repair shortcuts. AREW-trained agents substantially outperform their direct-inference counterparts (e.g., Qwen PPO+AREW reaches 80.33 on PE-G_{S=3} vs. 10.00 direct inference), and even surpass o4-mini on several tasks (e.g., 80.33 vs. 21.15 on PE-G_{S=3}). The AS-ONLY variant already yields measurable BT improvements, confirming the intrinsic coupling between information acquisition and belief updates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on standard policy-gradient RL (PPO, GRPO, GSPO) for training LLM agents but departs from the dominant credit-assignment literature that relies on calibrated intermediate rewards, learned reward models, or rule-based progress functions (e.g., CURIO, Sotopia-RL, SPA-RL). Instead, it identifies a structural failure mode—information self-locking (SeL)—arising from bidirectional coupling between action selection and belief tracking, and addresses it with a minimal, calibration-free advantage reweighting (AREW) derived from a margin-aware auxiliary objective. Conceptually, it extends the active-reasoning line of work (Proactive CoT, UoT, MediQ) by shifting focus from single-turn information-seeking strategies to the training dynamics that determine whether multi-turn agents actually learn to acquire and use information.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SeL diagnosis provides a concrete, theoretically grounded explanation for why outcome-based RL can stall in agentic settings even when surface rewards improve, which is likely a common but under-diagnosed problem for practitioners training interactive agents. AREW's calibration-free design (binary directional critiques injected as advantage offsets) makes it easy to adopt without reward-model training or dense supervision, and the robustness to 50% critique noise broadens its applicability. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to settings where step-level directional signals are harder to obtain (e.g., open-ended research agents), whether the theoretical assumptions (Lipschitz reward-belief coupling, action-invariant harmful drift) hold in more complex POMDPs, and whether SeL is the only structural failure mode in long-horizon agentic RL.
