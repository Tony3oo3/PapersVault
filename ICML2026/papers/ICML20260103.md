## Problem

Learned mesh-based cochain Laplacians can achieve low PDE prediction loss while silently violating the cochain-complex identity D₁D₀=0, introducing spurious near-kernel directions, conditioning blow-up under refinement, and corrupted low-frequency spectral multiplicities that standard regression losses cannot detect. No prior work provides a computable, deployment-neutral certificate that quantifies how far a learned operator pair is from chain-compatibility and translates that distance into explicit solver-stability and spectral-reliability guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a learned triple (D₀,φ, D₁,φ, W₁,φ) of incidence-like maps and a discrete Hodge star defined on a finite cell complex K_h, and produces a scalar certificate ratio ρ(φ) = δ(φ)/(λ*/2) that certifies whether the realized operator remains in a perturbatively stable regime. The projected chain-compatible reference  D̃₀,φ = P_ker(D₁,φ) D₀,φ is computed for analysis only (it is global and may destroy sparsity) and is never deployed; all downstream solves and Betti estimates use the original sparse learned operators. The certificate is evaluated on the realized sparse matrices at the evaluation mesh, making it deployment-neutral and independent of the training architecture.

## Method

The paper defines the cochain-compatibility defect D_comp(φ) = ‖D₁,φ D₀,φ‖_F and proves that the Euclidean-orthogonal projection of D₀,φ onto ker(D₁,φ) is the unique Frobenius-nearest chain-compatible reference, so the defect directly bounds the distance-to-compatibility (Theorem 4.2). Expanding the induced Laplacian around this reference yields a linear-quadratic operator-norm drift bound ‖L₀,φ − L̃₀,φ‖_op ≤ A·D_comp + B·D_comp² (Theorem 4.4), which propagates via Weyl and Davis–Kahan to SPD solve sensitivity and fixed-resolution spectral Betti-count stability under a spectral-gap assumption. In a FEEC setting, a Strang-type decomposition (Theorem 5.6) isolates the defect as an explicit additive term in the PDE error, separate from standard discretization error and reference-family deviation. The operational criterion ρ(φ) < 1 combines the drift budget with a once-estimated spectral margin into a single scalar that serves as both a training monitor (soft defect regularization or early stopping) and a post-hoc deployment check, and experiments on Poisson benchmarks at multiple resolutions and under mesh/topology shift confirm that defect-aware training prevents condition-number blow-up and maintains correct spectral Betti estimates.

## Results

On a constant-coefficient Poisson benchmark (PDE1), unconstrained training suffers severe conditioning deterioration with resolution: Cond(L₀) grows from ~1.9×10² at N=16 to ~1.7×10⁴ at N=32 and ~3.6×10⁶ at N=64 (solver failure), while both defect-controlled regimes maintain Cond(L₀) ≈ 2.4×10²–9.6×10² across all resolutions. Test MSE for unconstrained training degrades from (8.73×10⁻⁴) at N=16 to (1.94×10⁴) at N=64, whereas chain-compatible training improves from (8.74×10⁻⁷) to (7.21×10⁻¹⁰). Under topology shift (β₀ ∈ {1,2,3}), the spectral Betti estimator accuracy is 0.71 (unconstrained), 0.93 (soft defect), and 0.98 (chain-compatible), and the certificate ratio ρ(φ) predicts solver/topology failures with AUC ≈ 0.85 versus near-chance for validation PDE loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three training regimes: unconstrained PDE-loss minimization, soft defect regularisation (adding λ_comp‖D₁D₀‖²_F + λ_E R_E(φ)² to the loss), and chain-compatible parameterisation (hard constraint D₀ ∈ ker(D₁^ref)). At coarse resolution (N=16) all three achieve comparable test accuracy, but at N=32 and N=64 the unconstrained baseline collapses: its condition number grows by 2–4 orders of magnitude and its test MSE increases by 6–8 orders of magnitude, while both defect-controlled methods remain in the certified regime (ρ<1) with stable conditioning around 10². On the topology-shift evaluation (Table 2), the chain-compatible hard constraint achieves the best Betti accuracy (0.98) and zero defect, soft defect regularisation is close behind (0.93 accuracy, D_comp = 6.0×10⁻³), and unconstrained training lags significantly (0.71 accuracy, D_comp = 2.1×10⁻²). In terms of PDE accuracy, chain-compatible and soft defect are nearly matched (nRMSE 4.3×10⁻² vs 4.1×10⁻²), both substantially outperforming unconstrained (4.6×10⁻²) on max error (6.1×10⁻² / 6.9×10⁻² vs 1.18×10⁻¹). The paper's key finding is that the certificate ratio ρ(φ) predicts deployment failures (solver breakdown or incorrect Betti count) with AUC ≈ 0.85, while validation PDE loss is near chance, demonstrating that loss values alone are insufficient to detect the structural instabilities that defect control prevents.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of FEEC/DEC algebraic discretization theory and learned operator surrogates. In classical FEEC and DEC, the cochain-complex identity D₁D₀=0 is enforced by construction through fixed incidence operators, and the paper's theoretical machinery (Weyl, Davis-Kahan, Strang's lemma) is standard perturbation theory. The novel contribution is the bridge: when incidence-like maps are themselves learned (as in neural operator or mesh-based surrogate pipelines), this identity can be silently violated, and the paper converts the resulting Frobenius defect into explicit operator-norm perturbation budgets for the induced Hodge Laplacian. This is orthogonal to prior work on learned discretizations (Kovachki, Li, Bar-Sinai) that enforce PSD or equivariance but not higher-order chain identities, and to topological signal processing (Isufi, Zia) that assumes fixed incidence operators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The certificate ratio ρ(φ) < 1 provides a cheap, deployment-neutral diagnostic that detects algebraic failure modes invisible to standard PDE loss, which is practically important for anyone training mesh-based operator surrogates intended for iterative solvers or topological diagnostics. Key limitations include: the projected reference is global and destroys sparsity (analysis-only, not deployable), the certificate is sufficient but not necessary, the nondegeneracy of D₁ must be actively monitored when learned, and the raw defect is basis- and resolution-dependent. Open questions include whether the linear-quadratic perturbation regime remains tight under aggressive refinement, whether the framework extends cleanly to time-dependent or nonlinear PDE settings, and whether the spectral gap assumption (Assumption 4.6) holds robustly under the topology shifts encountered in real deployment.
