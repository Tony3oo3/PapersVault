## Problem

Graph neural networks trained on noisy labels suffer because existing methods either rely on small-loss criteria that ignore graph topology, or use regularization signals that cannot cleanly separate reliable from unreliable nodes. No prior approach explicitly leverages the relational structure of the graph to dynamically re-evaluate per-node label reliability during optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266070/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an attributed graph G=(V,E,X) with X∈R^{N×d_in} and a set of noisy labels Y over a labeled subset S⊂V, using a fixed 2-layer GCN backbone (hidden dim 64). It produces per-node class probabilities via a linear classifier and, critically, a per-node semantic homogeneity score H(n_t; A(n_t); τ) that acts as a dynamic reliability weight. These weights are recomputed every epoch from the current latent representations, so the effective loss landscape shifts throughout training rather than being fixed at initialization.

## Method

DREAM selects two complementary anchor sets for each target node: proximity-aware anchors (top-k_P same-label nodes by cosine similarity of learned embeddings) and topology-aware anchors (top-k_T nodes within geodesic distance d_max, also ranked by cosine similarity). The semantic homogeneity score is the mean anchor similarity raised to 1/τ, which amplifies the gap between clean and noisy nodes. This score reweights the per-node cross-entropy loss, and because anchor selection depends on the current encoder output, the reliability estimates are refreshed each epoch, creating a positive feedback loop between representation quality and supervision quality. An auxiliary GraphCL contrastive loss regularizes the encoder. A convergence theorem under the Polyak-Łojasiewicz condition shows that if the homogeneity score is a (β,ε)-approximation to the true distribution-drift ratio, DREAM achieves an ε-error bound on the optimal parameters after O((L/μ)log(1/ε)) iterations.

## Results

DREAM achieves the best classification accuracy across all six datasets (Cora, CiteSeer, PubMed, DBLP, A-Photo, Flickr) under all three noise types (pair, uniform, asymmetric) at 30% noise rate. Under the default 30% uniform noise, DREAM reaches 80.40% on Cora, 71.06% on CiteSeer, 71.75% on PubMed, 79.06% on DBLP, 89.19% on A-Photo, and 53.84% on Flickr. The most striking gain is a 14.9% relative improvement over the second-best method (NRGNN, 61.87%) on CiteSeer under uniform noise. DREAM also maintains a substantial margin on the heterophilic Flickr dataset (homophily 0.24), where most baselines degrade severely.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DREAM outperforms all 17 baselines on every dataset and noise type. Among graph-specific methods (GLN), NRGNN is the closest competitor on Cora (74.86% vs. 80.40%) and DBLP (76.21% vs. 79.06%), while PIGNN is competitive on PubMed (69.02% vs. 71.75%) and A-Photo (84.90% vs. 89.19%). Traditional LLN methods designed for Euclidean data (JoCoR, APL, SCE, Forward, Backward) generally perform at or below vanilla GCN on graphs, confirming that they fail to exploit topological structure. On the heterophilic Flickr dataset (homophily 0.24), most GLN baselines collapse (e.g., CGNN 11.71%, UnionNET 21.19%, RNCGLN 23.99%), whereas DREAM achieves 53.84%, a margin of over 4 percentage points over the best baseline (GCN at 49.77%). The paper also reports that DREAM is among the fastest methods, with training times of 3.66s (Cora) and 12.45s (Flickr) over 10 runs, significantly faster than RTGNN (16.13s, 73.87s) and UnionNET (13.54s, 66.11s) on those datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

DREAM sits at the intersection of two established lines of work on graph learning with label noise: regularization-based methods (NRGNN, PIGNN, CR-GNN) that inject auxiliary supervision from graph structure or self-consistency, and reweighting-based methods (small-loss criterion, Coteaching, JoCoR) that down-weight unreliable samples. The paper departs from both by explicitly leveraging relational information in the graph topology—through a dual-standard (proximity-aware and topology-aware) anchor selection mechanism—to dynamically re-evaluate node reliability during optimization, rather than relying on loss magnitudes or fixed regularization terms. The theoretical framing via the Radon-Nikodym change-of-measure connects the semantic homogeneity score to the unknown distribution-drift ratio, providing a principled justification for the reweighting that prior reweighting methods on graphs lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practical, efficient solution to a persistent real-world problem: GNNs trained on noisily labeled graphs degrade significantly, and existing robust methods often fail to distinguish reliable from unreliable nodes in complex topologies. The dual-standard anchor selection is a clean, interpretable mechanism that could be adapted to other reweighting or curriculum-learning settings on graphs. Limitations include reliance on a 2-layer GCN backbone, the strong (β,ε)-approximation assumption on the semantic homogeneity score, and evaluation limited to node classification on relatively small-to-medium graphs; scalability to very large graphs and compatibility with heterogeneous GNN architectures remain open.
