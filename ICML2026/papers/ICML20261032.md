## Problem

Standard SAT-style benchmarks for LLM reasoners conflate surface difficulty (instance length, wording, clause order) with the structural phenomena that actually determine satisfiability, making it impossible to distinguish transferable algorithmic competence from format-specific pattern reuse. This paper asks how to isolate and stress-test specific structural reasoning competencies—long-range implication tracking, contradiction-core localization, backbone propagation, and invariance to presentation—while holding the verification objective fixed.

## Input / Output

Each instance is a 2-CNF formula rendered into natural language via one of six deterministic template verbalizers (logic, letter, team, social, room, door) or an LLM-generated multi-sentence narrative paragraph per clause. The model must output a SAT/UNSAT decision and, when predicting SAT, a full variable assignment in JSON using scheme-specific entity values; truncated or unparsable outputs are scored as incorrect. Evaluation reports two metrics per setting: decision accuracy (Sat.) and witness validity (Wit., SAT instances only), with the 6 verbalizations per formula treated as repeated measurements for significance testing.

## Method

The benchmark is built from five parameterized generators for 2-CNF formulas, each targeting a distinct structural competency: (i) ImplicationCycle constructs UNSAT cores as directed cycles in the implication graph with a controllable split position k/L that governs long-range tracking difficulty; (ii) EquivalenceCore creates SAT instances with a prescribed free-to-bound variable ratio to control solution multiplicity and propagation depth; (iii) Backbone plants a forced assignment via auxiliary-variable pairs and appends a monotone remainder; (iv) MonoBridge couples two monotone regions with a single cross-polarity clause whose insertion position probes sensitivity to late coupling; and (v) a Symmetry/Redundancy probe duplicates the base formula under a disjoint renaming to test invariance. The key design choice is 2-SAT, whose satisfiability is characterized by SCCs of the implication graph, enabling solver-certified labels and interpretable structural manipulation without confounding reasoning failures with search hardness. Semantics-preserving perturbations—clause reordering, filler clauses over fresh variables, and variable renaming—are applied to quantify robustness, and the evaluation suite separates decision correctness from constructive witness validity to expose brittleness invisible to aggregate SAT/UNSAT accuracy.

## Results

Across seven reasoning models (15B–120B) evaluated on four parameterized 2-SAT generators, Qwen3-Next-80B achieves the highest overall decision accuracy (96.3%) and the most balanced error profile, while GPT-OSS-120B follows at 90.0%. On the hardest generator (ImplicationCycle, UNSAT), performance drops sharply with clause count: at |C|=100, scores range from 0% (Phi-4-reasoning, Phi-4-reasoning-plus) to 76.7% (Qwen3-Next-80B). A critical finding is the large gap between decision accuracy and witness validity: on SAT generators at |C|=50–200, witness validity collapses to 0–23% for most models even when decision accuracy remains above 80%, revealing that correct SAT/UNSAT predictions often mask an inability to construct a valid satisfying assignment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a diagnostic benchmark paper, so the 'baselines' are the seven evaluated reasoning models compared against each other on a common protocol. Qwen3-Next-80B is the most robust model overall, maintaining 76.7% decision accuracy on ImplicationCycle at |C|=100 where Phi-4-reasoning and Phi-4-reasoning-plus drop to 0%, and achieving the highest overall confusion-matrix accuracy (96.3%). GPT-OSS-120B is the second-most robust, but its witness validity on MonoBridge degrades to 23.3% at |C|=200, the lowest among top models. Phi-4-reasoning-plus suffers from a 30.5% output truncation rate, which mechanically inflates its error rate. Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct exhibits a severe SAT prediction bias (88.3% of UNSAT instances incorrectly predicted as SAT), yielding the lowest overall accuracy (70.9%). Proprietary models (o3-mini, o4-mini, GPT-5.4-mini) show similar degradation patterns, with combined micro-averages of 67–72%, confirming that the brittleness is not specific to open-weight architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior SAT-based LLM evaluations (Hazra et al. 2025; Richardson & Sabharwal 2022; SATBench, PARAT, SATQuest) that primarily vary surface difficulty (instance size, wording, clause order) and conflate it with the structural phenomena that actually determine satisfiability. It also differs from logical reasoning benchmarks (FOLIO, RuleTaker, LogiQA) that entangle formal inference with world knowledge and linguistic priors. The key methodological innovation is leveraging 2-SAT's implication-graph and SCC characterization to build parameterized families that isolate specific structural competencies (long-range implication tracking, contradiction-core localization, backbone propagation) and then probing invariance under semantics-preserving perturbations (clause reordering, filler clauses, variable renaming) — a diagnostic regime not present in prior SAT or logic benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals brittleness regimes invisible to aggregate SAT/UNSAT accuracy: sharp performance transitions under targeted structural interventions at fixed instance size, a significant gap between decision correctness and witness construction, and sensitivity to clause order, fillers, and repeated structure. The tool-calling experiment (Section P) adds a practically important nuance: even with access to PySAT, the NL→CNF parsing step remains a residual bottleneck for UNSAT instances, suggesting that solver access does not fully resolve reasoning failures. A key limitation is the restriction to 2-SAT (polynomial-time solvable), which avoids confounding structural reasoning with search hardness but leaves open whether these brittleness patterns generalize to harder constraint classes like 3-SAT or CSPs.
