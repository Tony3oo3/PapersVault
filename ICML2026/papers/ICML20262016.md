## Problem

Latent-vector-centric disentanglement methods impose statistical or geometric constraints on the latent space via objective functions, but prior analyses show that improvements in intrinsic disentanglement metrics do not consistently translate into downstream generalization gains. The paper addresses this gap by shifting the disentanglement inductive bias from the latent-space objective to the network architecture itself, asking whether a structural, layer-level mechanism can promote factor separation and improve downstream performance without requiring task-specific regularizers.

## Input / Output

The OSP layer operates on intermediate activations of any standard linear or convolutional layer, projecting them into K mutually orthogonal subspaces of dimension d (where m = K·d is the output channel dimension). For disentanglement experiments it processes 64×64 RGB images through VAE encoders; for downstream tasks it handles standard-resolution images (CIFAR-100, ImageNet, 640×640 for detection) and tokenized text sequences (BERT/RoBERTa). The layer's output has the same dimensionality as its input (m), so it is a drop-in replacement that does not alter the network's input/output interface; the only added parameters are a d×d skew-symmetric matrix per layer.

## Method

The OSP layer projects intermediate features into K mutually orthogonal subspaces by right-multiplying the layer weight matrix with a structured orthogonal matrix Q(I_k ⊗ C), where Q is a fixed (non-trainable) orthogonal basis obtained from a one-time QR decomposition and C is a shared d×d rotation learned via the Cayley transform of a learnable skew-symmetric matrix. To avoid the k-fold memory expansion of storing all projected vectors, each projection is represented by its intrinsic d-dimensional coordinates z_i = Q_i^T v, which are concatenated to form the output. This confines the additional cubic cost to inverting a d×d system (O(d³) with d ≪ m) rather than O(m³) full QR retractions. The layer is model-agnostic: it can be inserted atop any convolutional or linear layer in CNNs, Transformers, or VAEs, and for fine-tuning it initializes Q = I and C = I so the network starts exactly as the pretrained baseline, updating only the lightweight OSP parameters.

## Results

On ImageNet classification, OSP consistently improves top-1 accuracy across architectures: OSP-ResNet-18 reaches 65.71% (vs. 64.44%), OSP-ResNet-50 reaches 70.79% (vs. 69.86%), and OSP-ViT-B/16 reaches 80.33% (vs. 78.22%), with parameter overhead of ≤0.2%. On CIFAR-100, OSP-ResNet-50 achieves 74.64% (vs. 73.28%). For disentanglement on 3D Shapes, OSP-β-TCVAE improves FVM from 88.80 to 90.80 and MIG from 34.27 to 41.32. In parameter-efficient fine-tuning on ImageNet, OSP-fine-tuning with only 0.5M trainable parameters achieves 67.78% top-1, surpassing full fine-tuning (66.69%, 11.69M params) and S-OPT (67.74%, 3.39M params).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OSP outperforms all disentanglement baselines: OSP-β-VAE improves FVM from 85.80 to 90.60 and MIG from 35.41 to 43.33 on 3D Shapes; OSP-β-TCVAE raises FVM from 88.80 to 90.80 and MIG from 34.27 to 41.32; OSP-CLG-VAE lifts FVM from 79.60 to 85.80. On CV classification, OSP-ResNet-18/50 and OSP-ViT-B/16 beat their respective baselines by 0.8–2.1 points on ImageNet with negligible parameter increase. For detection, OSP-YOLOv8-large-seg improves CarParts box mAP50 from 67.77 to 69.07 and mask mAP50 from 69.13 to 70.63. In NLP, OSP-RoBERTa-large achieves the best GLUE average, notably jumping RTE from 72.20 to 94.62 (OSP-BERT-Large) and improving word analogy average ppl from 33.60 to 35.07. For fine-tuning, OSP-fine-tuning (0.5M params) beats full fine-tuning (11.69M) by +1.09 points on ImageNet and matches S-OPT (3.39M) while OPT runs out of memory. OSP also extends to GAN/diffusion baselines, improving DisDiff FVM from 90.20 to 92.00 and IB-GAN FVM from 75.10 to 78.49.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant latent-vector-centric paradigm in disentanglement learning (β-VAE, β-TCVAE, CLG-VAE), which imposes statistical or geometric regularizers on a bottleneck latent space, by instead injecting disentanglement inductive bias directly into intermediate network layers via orthogonal subspace projections. It draws on orthogonality-based designs (CapProNet, LSOP) and weight-reparameterization methods (OPT, OFT) but distinguishes itself as a model-agnostic, plug-and-play architectural module that operates on arbitrary intermediate activations rather than being tied to specific heads, losses, or frozen-weight adaptation schemes. The motivation is grounded in the well-documented finding that latent-space disentanglement objectives do not reliably translate into downstream generalization gains (Locatello et al., 2019b; 2020a), motivating a shift from objective-driven to structure-driven disentanglement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The OSP layer offers a practically attractive recipe: a negligible parameter overhead (~0.2%) that can be dropped into any CNN or Transformer backbone to yield consistent but modest downstream improvements across CV, NLP, and fine-tuning settings. The most striking result is in parameter-efficient fine-tuning, where 0.5M trainable OSP parameters on ResNet-18 outperform full fine-tuning (11.69M parameters) on ImageNet, suggesting that structured low-rank orthogonal updates can be more effective than unconstrained full updates. However, the OOD generalization gains are small and dataset-dependent, and the paper does not fully isolate whether the downstream improvements stem from genuine factor separation or from the orthogonal constraint acting as an implicit regularizer. Open questions include whether the benefits persist at larger scales (e.g., ViT-Large, LLMs) and whether the number of subspaces K should be matched to the true number of generative factors.
