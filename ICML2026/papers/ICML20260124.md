## Problem

Existing theoretical frameworks for post-training robustness—wide minima, implicit optimization bias, benign overfitting, and NTK theory—each operate through different analytical lenses and characterize which solution an optimizer finds, rather than the local robustness of a given trained model to the specific perturbations it faces after training (SGD noise, label noise, quantization, pruning). No single quantity directly governs how a fixed model's test predictions vary under these heterogeneous, realistic perturbations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260124/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a trained model's parameters, its training data, and a specification of the perturbation source (e.g., label-noise variance, SGD learning rate/batch size, quantization step size, or pruning mask). It produces a scalar TPV estimate computed as Tr(H_eff C), which can be evaluated on the training set alone (via TPV stability) to serve as a label-free proxy for test-time robustness. Downstream, this scalar yields per-parameter-group importance scores for JBR pruning or a ranking over candidate models/recipes for selection without test labels.

## Method

TPV is defined as the local variance of a trained model's predictions under zero-mean parameter perturbations δw around w*, and under first-order Taylor approximation reduces to the trace form Tr(H_eff C), where H_eff = E_x[J(x)ᵀJ(x)] is the second moment of the output-parameter Jacobian and C is the perturbation covariance. This decomposition separates label-free model geometry (H_eff) from the noise mechanism (C), unifying SGD noise, label noise, quantization, and pruning under one expression. The key theoretical result (Theorem 3.1) proves that training-set TPV converges to test-set TPV in the overparameterized limit via NTK stability and the law of large numbers, irrespective of generalization performance. For label noise, Theorem 4.2 yields a Jacobian-spectral characterization (σ²Σ B_ii/s_i²) that recovers benign overfitting in linear models and extends it to nonlinear networks; for SGD and quantization noise, TPV recovers the wide-minima hypothesis. Empirically, TPV is estimated via a perturb-and-retrain procedure (perturb predicted logits, fine-tune a copy for a few steps, measure prediction variance across runs), and the resulting JBR pruning criterion matches or exceeds seven baselines on CIFAR-10/100 and ImageNet.

## Results

Across 324 synthetic configurations spanning more than five orders of magnitude in TPV, training-set and test-set TPV concentrate tightly along the diagonal (TPV_train ≈ TPV_test), even at width=1 and across varying generalization gaps. On CIFAR-10/100 with MobileNetV2 width multipliers, TPV stability holds for n_train=10,000 but breaks at n_train=1. For the JBR pruning criterion, the paper reports that it matches or exceeds all seven baselines (Jacobian, L1, BN Scale, FPGM, WHC, Taylor, Random) across CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet under the OBC global channel-pruning protocol with no fine-tuning between iterations. Empirically, TPV correlates with test loss in the low-training-loss regime and inversely in the high-training-loss regime, producing a U-shaped relationship across regularizers and architectures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary empirical comparison is the JBR pruning criterion against seven established baselines: Jacobian Criterion (Chen et al., 2025), L1 norm (Li et al., 2017), BN Scale (Liu et al., 2017), FPGM (He et al., 2019), WHC (Chen et al., 2023), Taylor (Molchanov et al., 2016), and Random pruning. JBR is reported to match or exceed all seven baselines across all four evaluation settings (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, ImageNet, and a fourth setting detailed in Appendix H, Figures 21–24), using the OBC global channel-pruning protocol without fine-tuning between iterations. The paper notes that JBR is closely related to the Jacobian Criterion (JC) but differs in its derivation from TPV geometry; the specific connection and differences are discussed in Appendix H.1. No specific accuracy numbers are reported in the main text—detailed per-setting comparisons appear only in the appendix figures. For model selection, the paper demonstrates that training-set label-noise TPV correctly identifies the most robust training recipe, whereas sharpness (Tr(H_eff), i.e., SGD-noise TPV) fails to correlate with label-noise sensitivity, providing a direct comparison showing TPV's advantage over the standard flatness proxy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies several previously separate analytical lenses—wide/flat minima (Hochreiter & Schmidhuber, 1997; Keskar et al., 2017), implicit SGD bias (Mandt et al., 2017; Soudry et al., 2018), and benign overfitting (Bartlett et al., 2020)—under a single local quantity: the trace of the Jacobian second-moment times the perturbation covariance, Tr(H_eff C). It departs from global bias-variance analyses (Geman et al., 1992; Neal et al., 2018) and NTK-based generalization theory (Jacot et al., 2018; Allen-Zhu et al., 2019) by being a post-training, fixed-solution object that is agnostic to how the optimum was reached. The TPV stability theorem borrows NTK stability and the Law of Large Numbers to bridge training-set and test-set estimates, while the label-noise result extends the linear benign-overfitting formula to nonlinear networks via Jacobian spectral geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TPV provides a label-free, training-set-only diagnostic for post-training robustness that practitioners can use for model selection and pruning without held-out test labels, reducing deployment cost. The empirical finding that TPV stability holds even at width=1—far beyond the overparameterized regime required by the NTK-based theorem—suggests the theory is significantly conservative and motivates tighter finite-width analyses. Open questions include extending stability guarantees beyond isotropic perturbations and NTK assumptions, formalizing TPV under input distribution shift, and making the exact linearized label-noise TPV computationally tractable for modern architectures (the paper's CG-based computation fails due to extreme ill-conditioning).
