## Problem

Post-training of large pretrained models is typically done via sequential iterative optimization (gradient descent, PPO, GRPO, ES), but it is unclear whether the local weight-space neighborhood around pretrained weights already contains abundant task-specific solutions that could be found by simpler means. This paper asks how the multi-task loss landscape around pretrained weights changes with scale, and whether the resulting density and diversity of nearby task experts can be exploited by a fully parallel, gradient-free post-training procedure.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained model's full weight vector θ and a small training set (200 samples) for scoring perturbations; at inference it receives a text question (or image + text question for VLMs, where the visual encoder is frozen) and produces a text answer or code. Each of the N perturbed models is generated by adding a Gaussian noise vector ε ~ N(0, I) scaled by a noise level σ drawn from a small set (e.g., {1,2,3}×10⁻³), so the 'input' to the search is purely the seed and σ, with no gradient information. The final output is the majority vote over K selected models' predictions, meaning inference cost is K forward passes per query.

## Method

RandOpt is a two-phase, fully parallel, gradient-free post-training algorithm. In the training phase, N random Gaussian perturbations of the pretrained weights are generated (each with a noise scale sampled from a set Σ), and every perturbed model is evaluated on a small validation set; the top-K models by score are retained. In the inference phase, all K models generate predictions independently and the final answer is the majority vote over their outputs. The key empirical finding enabling this approach is that solution density (the fraction of random perturbations that improve task accuracy) and solution diversity (measured by Spectral Discordance, showing perturbations are task-specialists rather than generalists) both increase monotonically with model scale, so that for sufficiently large pretrained models the neighborhood is a 'thicket' of complementary experts. Because no sequential optimization steps are needed, RandOpt runs in O(1) wall-clock time given sufficient parallel compute, and a distillation step (SFT on hard examples generated by the top-K ensemble) can collapse the K-model ensemble into a single model at roughly 2% of the original training cost.

## Results

On GSM8K, RandOpt (K=50) achieves 87.1% accuracy on Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct (base: 79.8%), 80.4% on Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct (base: 58.8%), and 61.2% on Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct (base: 39.9%), matching or exceeding ES+TT-MV, GRPO+TT-MV, and PPO+TT-MV at equal training FLOPs. On the VLM benchmark GQA, RandOpt improves Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct from 56.6% to 69.0% (+12.4%). On Countdown with OLMo3-7B-Instruct, RandOpt (N=2000, K=50) reaches 70% accuracy in 3.2 minutes on a 200-GPU GH200 cluster, demonstrating O(1) wall-clock training versus O(T) for sequential baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RandOpt (K=50) outperforms 1-pass PPO and GRPO by a wide margin (e.g., 87.1% vs. ~78% and ~83.5% on Qwen2.5-3B GSM8K), and also beats their TT-MV-ensembled versions at the 1.5B scale (80.4% vs. 71.7% for GRPO+TT-MV). Against ES+TT-MV, RandOpt ties at the 0.5B and 3B scales (61.2% and 87.1% respectively) but clearly wins at 1.5B (80.4% vs. 76.4%). RandOpt consistently outperforms TT-MV applied to the unmodified base model (e.g., 87.1% vs. 83.5% on Qwen2.5-3B GSM8K), confirming that the gains come from weight-space selection rather than mere test-time sampling diversity. Best-of-N is also outperformed (87.1% vs. 85.0% on Qwen2.5-3B GSM8K). The one notable caveat is that at the 3B scale, ES+TT-MV matches RandOpt exactly, and the paper notes that some gains (particularly on GSM8K) are partially attributable to answer-format corrections rather than pure reasoning improvements.

## Relation to Prior Work

RandOpt is conceptually closest to PEP (Mehrtash et al., 2020), which ensembles predictions from Gaussian weight perturbations, but adds a critical top-K selection step based on task performance. The paper positions itself against the gradient-based post-training paradigm (PPO, GRPO) and gradient-free sequential methods (ES), arguing that the parallel, single-step nature of random guessing is sufficient once a model enters the 'thicket regime.' It also connects to the Lottery Ticket Hypothesis by showing a qualitatively different regime after pretraining—where the neighborhood around pretrained weights is dense with good solutions rather than sparse—and to MAML by suggesting pretraining implicitly discovers initializations from which task-specific solutions are a short random step away.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the near-universal assumption that post-training requires structured, sequential optimization, showing that for sufficiently large pretrained models, random sampling plus ensembling is competitive with PPO, GRPO, and ES at equal FLOPs. This reframes pretrained weights as a distribution over task experts rather than a single point, with practical implications for parallel and federated adaptation. Key open questions include: exactly what in the pretraining objective creates the thicket regime, how far beyond the base model's capabilities random guessing can push performance (scaling appears to saturate), and whether the approach extends to structured outputs (code, images, molecules) where majority-vote ensembling is ill-defined. The authors also note that a non-trivial fraction of gains (≈19% on GSM8K) come from formatting fixes rather than reasoning improvements, tempering the interpretive claims.
