## Problem

VLA models suffer from high inference latency that hinders real-time robotic control, and existing model pruning approaches are inadequate: static pruning cannot adapt to the dynamically evolving sparsity patterns as the physical environment changes during execution, while fixed-interval dynamic layer pruning introduces auxiliary routers with retraining overhead and operates at too coarse a granularity. A training-free, fine-grained, environment-aware adaptive pruning method that can be deployed without adding latency to the control loop is needed.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard VLA inputs—RGB images (multi-view), a text instruction, and proprioceptive state—processed as single-sample streaming (batch size = 1) for real-time control. Beyond the primary output of a continuous robotic action sequence, EcoVLA produces per-layer structured sparsity masks (binary channel-retention indices) that are updated adaptively at variable intervals rather than every step. The sparsity pattern computation is triggered only when a temporal-context-conditioned similarity threshold is crossed, meaning most frames reuse the previous pattern without recomputation.

## Method

EcoVLA comprises two components. Environment-aware Adaptive Pruning (EAP) detects sparsity-pattern shifts via a lightweight visual-feature cosine similarity between consecutive frames, gated by a sliding-window quantile trigger that self-regulates sensitivity to distribution drift. Upon triggering, it computes per-channel importance scores by fusing instantaneous activation norms with an exponential-moving-average historical prior (temporal inertia parameter α), then selects channels to prune using a weight-magnitude × fused-feature criterion (following PPsp). Interleaved Inference Orchestration (I²O) exploits the complementary resource profiles of the VLM backbone (compute-saturated, memory-bandwidth-undersaturated) and the lightweight Action Expert (Tensor-Core-undersaturated) to schedule the pruning computation in a parallel stream during FLOPs bubbles, reducing the added latency to ~4.5 ms. Hardware-level optimizations include custom Triton sparse linear kernels, column-major storage for coalesced access, fused gate-up-SiLU-multiply kernels, and pre-computed weight L2-norm vectors to eliminate redundant memory traffic.

## Results

On OpenVLA-OFT in LIBERO, EcoVLA achieves 1.26× speedup at 25% pruning (96.8% avg success rate vs. 96.7% vanilla) and 1.41× at 40% (94.0% avg). When combined with FastV (50% token pruning), the speedup reaches 2.18× at 25% with only a 0.5% success-rate drop, and 2.35× at 40%. On π₀.₅, EcoVLA delivers 1.31× and 1.46× speedups at 25% and 37.5% sparsity respectively, and on CogACT in SIMPLER it reaches up to 1.60× at 40% pruning with a marginal 0.4% accuracy reduction. Real-robot evaluation on a Kinova Gen3 confirms a 1.26× wall-clock speedup with minimal performance loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the static pruning baseline Wanda, EcoVLA wins on both success rate and speedup at every pruning ratio: at 25% sparsity EcoVLA achieves 96.8% vs. Wanda's 93.8% (a 3.0-point gap) with 1.26× vs. 1.15× speedup, and at 40% the gap widens to 5.2 points (94.0% vs. 88.8%) with 1.41× vs. 1.35×. The advantage is most pronounced on the pruning-sensitive LIBERO-Goal suite, where EcoVLA leads Wanda by 7.4% and 12.4% at 25% and 40% respectively. Compared to the token-pruning method FastV (1.21× standalone), EcoVLA is orthogonal and complementary: stacking them yields 2.18× at 25% and 2.35× at 40%, while recovering most of FastV's accuracy loss (96.2% vs. FastV's 94.4%). Against VLA-Cache (1.10× standalone), the combination reaches 1.34× at 25% and 1.50× at 40% with only a 1.2% success-rate drop, confirming that EcoVLA's model-level sparsification is broadly stackable with input-level and caching-based accelerators.

## Relation to Prior Work

EcoVLA extends the layer-wise structured pruning framework from An et al. (2024) and the importance-score formulation from Probe Pruning (Le et al., 2025) to the VLA setting, but departs from both by making the sparsity pattern environment-adaptive rather than input-adaptive, and by operating at channel granularity rather than layer granularity. It explicitly positions itself against static pruning (RLRC, GLUESTICK) that cannot track environmental dynamics, and against fixed-interval dynamic layer-skipping (MoLe-VLA, DeeR-VLA) that relies on trained routers and coarse granularity. The Interleaved Inference Orchestration component is a novel systems contribution that exploits the compute-bound VLM / under-utilized Action Expert asymmetry in VLA pipelines, a resource profile not previously leveraged for hiding auxiliary computation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By being training-free and plug-and-play, EcoVLA removes the retraining bottleneck that has limited prior VLA pruning methods, and its orthogonal compatibility with token pruning (FastV) and KV caching (VLA-Cache) makes it a practical building block for production VLA deployment stacks. The real-robot validation on Kinova Gen3 and Franka with XHand, plus edge-device results on Jetson Orin, strengthen the case for practical adoption. Open questions include whether the FLOPs-bubble assumption holds on heterogeneous or multi-GPU hardware, how the method scales to substantially larger VLM backbones (e.g., 70B+), and whether the temporal-consistency EMA strategy generalizes to highly non-stationary environments (e.g., multi-robot interaction or rapidly changing scenes).
