## Problem

Existing few-shot adaptation methods combine pretrained model knowledge and few-shot support-set evidence through ad-hoc architectural choices (residual connections, weighted sums) without a principled theoretical justification. The paper asks: what is the formal relationship between these two information sources, and how should they be integrated to produce an optimal posterior?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266216/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image, a few-shot support set of K labeled examples per novel class, and class-name text prompts (e.g., 'a photo of a [CLASS]'). The frozen CLIP visual encoder produces a d-dimensional embedding z_i, while the text encoder generates a classifier matrix W of class-specific embeddings. The output is a single class label obtained by argmax over the fused logit vector, with the framework evaluated at 1, 2, 4, 8, and 16 shots per class across 11 benchmarks.

## Method

BOIF derives a Bayes-optimal additive logit rule: under conditional independence of the pretraining data and support set, the log-posterior decomposes into the sum of prior logits (from the pretrained model) and likelihood logits (from the support-set module), justifying equal-weight logit fusion. The prior pathway f_θ refines CLIP's zero-shot logits by projecting multi-level features (extracted via a Multi-level Adapter Fusion module) into the text-embedding space through a Feature Converter, while the likelihood pathway g_φ combines a learnable cache-based similarity score over support embeddings with an MLP on the enriched features. The two pathways are trained independently with auxiliary cross-entropy losses and L1 consistency penalties anchoring each to the original zero-shot logits, then combined via parameter-free 0.5·log f_θ + 0.5·log g_φ. This decoupled design, grounded in the theoretical derivation, outperforms heuristic fusion baselines across 11 datasets and OOD targets.

## Results

BOIF achieves state-of-the-art average accuracy across 11 few-shot benchmarks: 66.35% (1-shot), 72.50% (4-shot), and 80.61% (16-shot), consistently outperforming the strongest competitor LDC (65.71%, 71.17%, 79.78%) by 0.6–0.8 percentage points. Under out-of-distribution evaluation (16-shot ImageNet → ImageNet-V2/Sketch), BOIF with ViT-B/16 backbone attains 66.25% on ImageNet-V2 and 49.41% on ImageNet-Sketch, surpassing RPO (65.13%, 49.27%) and LDC (66.10%, 48.85%). The method also generalizes to vision-only backbones, achieving 82.40% average accuracy with DINOv2 at 16-shot.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BOIF outperforms all 10 in-distribution baselines across every shot setting on the 11-dataset average. The closest competitor is LDC (CVPR'25), which BOIF surpasses by 0.64% (1-shot), 0.85% (4-shot), and 0.83% (16-shot); Tip-Adapter-F (ECCV'22) trails by 1.80% at 1-shot and 4.92% at 16-shot. Prompt-based methods (CoOp, CoCoOp) and training-free approaches (SuS-X, LP-CLIP) lag substantially, with LP-CLIP underperforming by over 29 points at 1-shot. In the OOD setting (Table 2), BOIF with ViT-B/16 beats RPO by 1.12% on ImageNet-V2 and 0.14% on Sketch, and outperforms LDC by 0.15% and 0.56% respectively; with ResNet-50, it leads LDC by 0.42% on ImageNet-V2. The method also generalizes to DINOv2 (vision-only), where it surpasses Tip-Adapter-F by 0.54% at 16-shot, confirming the model-agnostic nature of the Bayesian logit-fusion principle.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the CLIP-based few-shot adaptation lineage (Tip-Adapter, CoOp, CLIP-Adapter, LDC) but departs from the prevailing heuristic fusion paradigm—where pretrained logits and support-set evidence are combined via residual connections or empirically tuned weighted sums—by deriving an additive logit combination rule from a Bayesian conditional-independence argument. The cache-based likelihood module is a simplified variant of Tip-Adapter's non-parametric adapter, and the multi-level feature adapter borrows from MAF (Li et al., 2025), but the key conceptual departure is the explicit decoupling of the prior pathway f_θ and likelihood pathway g_φ with a theoretically grounded, parameter-free equal-weight logit summation. The authors also retroactively interpret Prototypical Networks, LDC, AMU-Tuning, and Tip-Adapter as implicit instantiations of their two-path framework, positioning their work as a unifying theoretical lens rather than a purely architectural novelty.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean, model-agnostic design principle (additive logit fusion with equal weights) that replaces trial-and-error architectural choices in few-shot adaptation, and validates it across 11 benchmarks plus OOD targets and a vision-only backbone (DINOv2). However, the conditional-independence assumption is acknowledged as approximate, the equal-weight fusion is fixed rather than task-adaptive, and the MAF module adds non-trivial parameter overhead (28.44M tunable params). Open questions include whether uncertainty-aware or data-dependent weighting could further improve performance, whether the independence assumption holds under more severe distribution shifts, and how to reduce the computational cost of multi-level feature extraction for deployment in resource-constrained settings.
