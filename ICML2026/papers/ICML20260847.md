## Problem

Parameter-based regularization methods for continual learning (EWC, SI, oEWC) suffer from two fundamental limitations: stored curvature estimates become progressively misaligned as parameters move through new-task regions, and they cannot filter the sample-dependent interference term that causes class-incremental failure because corrections are applied additively after gradients are already computed. This paper asks whether learning and preservation can be coupled *during* the formation of learning signals rather than decoupled as post-hoc penalties.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard classification images (28×28 grayscale MNIST, 32×32 RGB CIFAR-10, 64×64 RGB Tiny-ImageNet) along with two pieces of stored information from prior tasks: the diagonal Fisher information matrix and the optimal parameters θ*_A. Unlike standard backpropagation, each training step requires running the network's coupled forward-feedback dynamics to equilibrium (multiple iterations) before a single Hebbian-like weight update is applied, making the per-sample computation substantially more expensive. The output is a class prediction over the full (growing) class space in class-incremental settings, or a task-specific prediction in task-incremental settings.

## Method

Equilibrium Fisher Control (EFC) reformulates continual learning as a multiplicative least-control problem. A neuron-specific preservation signal γ_k = β φ_k^T F_{A,k}^D (θ_k − θ_{A,k}^*) is constructed by projecting the EWC regularizer gradient onto current presynaptic activity, making it active only when the input engages previously relevant synapses. Both the learning signal ψ and preservation signal γ modulate the feedforward mapping multiplicatively in the network dynamics τφ̇ = −φ + e^(ψ+γ) ⊙ f(φ,θ), so that learning can only overcome preservation by expending opposing control effort. The learning objective minimizes ||ψ||² subject to reaching a loss-minimizing equilibrium, solved via a PI feedback controller that drives activities to steady state. At equilibrium, the resulting weight update ΔW_i = (r_{i,★} − σ(W_i r_{i−1,★})) r_{i−1,★}^T implicitly preconditions by the full prior-task Fisher F_A^{-1} (the 'continual-natural gradient'), recovering off-diagonal curvature through the network's feedforward-feedback interactions without ever storing or computing the full matrix explicitly.

## Results

On Split-MNIST, EFC achieves 98.3% Task-IL accuracy (comparable to oEWC's 99.2%) and 51.4% Class-IL accuracy, dramatically outperforming all parameter-based regularization methods (19.2–20.2%) and substantially narrowing the gap to the replay-based DER++ baseline (71.1%). On Split-CIFAR10, EFC reaches 96.2% Task-IL and 50.2% Class-IL, again far exceeding regularization baselines (~20% Class-IL) while approaching DER++ (62.3%). On Split-Tiny-ImageNet, EFC achieves 37.2% Task-IL and 8.8% Class-IL, outperforming all regularization methods (34.2–35.5% Task-IL; 4.6–4.8% Class-IL) but remaining below DER++ (39.0% / 10.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

In task-incremental settings, EFC matches or slightly exceeds all parameter-based regularization baselines: on Split-MNIST it reaches 98.3% (vs. oEWC 99.2%, EWC 97.2%, SI 97.5%, CSQN 98.2%), on Split-CIFAR10 it leads at 96.2% (vs. oEWC 95.9%, EWC 95.5%, CSQN 95.4%, SI 92.6%), and on Split-Tiny-ImageNet it achieves 37.2% (vs. oEWC/SI/CSQN 35.5%, EWC 34.2%). The decisive advantage appears in class-incremental learning, where EFC achieves 51.4% on Split-MNIST, 50.2% on Split-CIFAR10, and 8.8% on Split-Tiny-ImageNet, roughly doubling or tripling the ~20% (or ~4.7%) accuracy of EWC, oEWC, SI, and CSQN. This gap is attributed to EFC's ability to filter sample-dependent interference within the dynamics before weight updates, a capability the paper proves is fundamentally unavailable to additive parameter-space regularizers. Against the replay-based DER++ baseline (200-sample buffer), EFC narrows the previously described 'unbridgeable gap' substantially: from a 51-point deficit to a 20-point deficit on Split-MNIST Class-IL, from 41 to 12 points on Split-CIFAR10, and from 5.9 to 1.7 points on Split-Tiny-ImageNet, all without any replay buffer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the least-control principle (Meulemans et al., 2022b) from an additive to a multiplicative formulation, and repurposes the EWC Fisher-based regularizer (Kirkpatrick et al., 2017) by projecting it into activity-space as a neuron-specific preservation signal that modulates network dynamics rather than penalizing parameter updates. The key conceptual departure from all prior parameter-based regularization methods (EWC, SI, oEWC, CSQN) is that preservation is embedded within the learning dynamics as a competing signal, rather than applied as a post-hoc additive correction to gradients. The recovery of full Fisher curvature from only diagonal storage relies on the dynamical inversion property of Podlaski & Machens (2020) and the feedback controller structure of Meulemans et al. (2021).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical impossibility result: no parameter-based regularization method, regardless of curvature quality, can cancel the sample-dependent interference term in class-incremental learning because corrections are applied after gradient formation. This reframes a long-standing empirical observation (the 'unbridgeable gap' between regularization and replay) as a structural limitation rather than a tuning problem. However, practical impact is currently limited: the method requires running dynamics to equilibrium at each step (substantially more expensive than backpropagation), experiments are confined to moderate-sized MLPs and frozen-encoder + linear-head setups, and the stored diagonal Fisher still suffers from drift. The primary open challenge is reducing the cost of equilibrium computation to make control-based continual learning tractable at scale, and a fully dynamical curvature estimation that eliminates even the diagonal storage would be a natural next step.
