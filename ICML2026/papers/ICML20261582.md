## Problem

Under partial observability in multimodal systems, two training runs can match on average performance (Full and Train-Avg scores) over the same observable support yet diverge substantially on adverse-tail metrics. The paper identifies this as a coverage-exposure mismatch: the training mixture covers all environments, but gated routing sends different environments through different parameter groups, so high-leverage groups may receive few effective updates despite complete coverage, and tail aggregation amplifies their errors.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multimodal inputs (image+text for vision-language routes; audio+vision+text for sentiment routes) together with an observable environment signal ω = (b, q), where b ∈ {0,1}^M encodes modality availability and q ∈ [0,1]^M encodes measurable per-modality quality. This signal drives a deterministic gate that scales only the trainable increment branches during both training and inference. Outputs are standard task predictions (text answers for VLM benchmarks, sentiment scores for MSA datasets), evaluated under a locked same-support contract that fixes the environment support Ω_head, a worst-case subset Ω_worst, and a shift reweighting π_sh before any method comparison.

## Method

H-CES (Heterogeneity-aware Closed-loop Exposure Stabilizer) is a lightweight training-time controller built around a deterministic increment-branch gate w_g(ω) derived from modality availability and quality. The theoretical core (Lemma 2, Proposition 5) shows that under gated increments, group gradients scale with w_g and curvature with w_g², so step-aligned effective information for a group scales with E[w_g(ω)²]; this motivates TailPressure, an exposure-normalized statistic combining contract-fixed tail leverage ℓ_tail(g), a strength proxy Ĉ_g (increment-to-base Frobenius norm ratio), and an EMA exposure e_eff(g). At each controller tick, if TailPressure leaves a calibrated band, H-CES adjusts only the per-group decoupled weight decay in AdamW, leaving the task loss, forward graph, and inference interface unchanged. The controller is replayable from gate logs and checkpoint norms, requires no auxiliary branches or extra trainable parameters, and generalizes across LoRA, adapters, and prefix-tuning interfaces.

## Results

Under the locked same-support contract, H-CES improves Worst-Head by 2.1–3.2 points and Shift-Reweight by 1.4–2.5 points over the closest static baseline (Static-TP) across six vision-language routes (LLaVA-NeXT/Vicuna-7B and Qwen3-VL-8B on MMBench-EN, MMMU, MathVista), while Full scores remain within 0.2 points. On the three sentiment routes (MOSI, MOSEI, CH-SIMS with MulT/HME), H-CES gains 2.5–2.6 points on Worst-Head and 2.2–2.7 points on Shift-Reweight. For example, on MMBench-EN with LLaVA-NeXT/Vicuna-7B, H-CES achieves Worst-Head 53.1 and Shift-Reweight 56.5 versus 50.4/54.2 for Static-TP and 45.9/50.8 for LoRA with global weight decay, while Full stays at 67.1. Five-seed fixed-configuration reruns confirm the gains are not seed-driven (e.g., MMBench-EN Worst-Head: 52.6±0.6 for H-CES vs. 50.1±0.7 for Static-TP).

## Baselines & Comparisons

H-CES outperforms all seven baselines on both tail metrics (Worst-Head and Shift-Reweight) across every route while keeping Full within 0.2 points. Against LoRA with global weight decay, H-CES gains 7.2 points on Worst-Head (53.1 vs. 45.9) and 5.7 on Shift-Reweight (56.5 vs. 50.8) on MMBench-EN/LLaVA-NeXT, with similar margins on other routes. LoRA+GroupDRO, which directly optimizes environment groups, still trails H-CES by 4.4 points on Worst-Head (48.7 vs. 53.1) on the same route, indicating that group-level DRO does not address the exposure mismatch. DiGraP, which modifies gradient directions, achieves 49.4 Worst-Head, still 3.7 points below H-CES. The closest baseline, Static-TP (same contract leverage and exposure information but no closed loop), is beaten by 2.7 points on Worst-Head and 2.3 on Shift-Reweight on MMBench-EN, confirming the closed-loop regulation adds value beyond static exposure-aware decay. On sentiment routes, H-CES beats LNLN by 5.3 points on MOSI Worst-Head (74.5 vs. 69.2) and P-RMF by 3.9 points (74.5 vs. 70.6). For non-LoRA interfaces, Adapters+H-CES improves Worst-Head from 45.3 to 50.6 and Prefix+H-CES from 44.6 to 49.8, showing the controller is interface-agnostic.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA, adapters, prefix-tuning), conditional computation (gated routing, MoE), and distributionally robust optimization (GroupDRO, CVaR), but departs from all three by identifying a training-time mechanism—coverage-exposure mismatch in gated architectures—that none of these lines address. Rather than modifying the loss (as GroupDRO does) or augmenting data (as ModDrop does), H-CES repurposes per-group decoupled weight decay as a closed-loop actuator driven by a novel auditable diagnostic (TailPressure) computed from gate logs and checkpoint norms. The locked same-support contract is a new evaluation protocol that differs from standard robustness benchmarks by fixing the environment support before method comparison and separating stress-only regimes from headline claims, drawing on missing-data theory (MNAR) only as a boundary statement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that matched-average multimodal runs can diverge by 4+ points on contract-defined tail metrics while a same-support Random-Worst control collapses to 1 point is a concrete warning for evaluation methodology: headline averages are insufficient for reliability claims under partial observability. H-CES is practically attractive (2.4% throughput overhead, no inference change, works across LoRA/adapters/prefix interfaces and multiple backbones), but its diagnostic is explicitly scoped to an 'audited regime' defined by applicability thresholds, and the surrogate-risk analysis does not formally bound reported task scores. Open questions include whether the mechanism generalizes to learned (non-deterministic) routers, whether the contract can be extended to truly MNAR settings where the support itself is unidentifiable, and whether the per-group weight-decay actuator remains effective at much larger scales or with more heterogeneous group structures.
