## Problem

LLM-based agents in interactive environments suffer from objective drift and token inefficiency over long horizons: small execution errors compound, goals gradually deviate, and agents rely on ever-growing context to maintain intent. Existing hierarchical approaches decompose tasks into sub-goals but leave the executor untrained to correct compounding errors during interaction, so execution still breaks down over extended rollouts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263585/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

System 1 (planner) receives the natural-language task description plus a compact state summary (grouped action history concatenated with the current observation) and outputs a single sub-goal string. System 2 (executor) receives the active sub-goal and the current observation, and outputs an atomic action string paired with a boolean completion flag; it iterates until the flag is true, at which point control returns to System 1 for the next sub-goal. Both agents share a frozen backbone (Qwen-2.5 3B, Mistral 7B, or Llama-3.1 8B) with separate LoRA adapters (rank 16), and generation is capped at 32 tokens (16 during online RL) to keep actions concise.

## Method

Multi² decouples planning and execution into two role-specialized agents trained with different objectives: System 1 is fine-tuned via supervised behavior cloning on observation–sub-goal pairs, while System 2 is optimized with a novel offline-to-online RL pipeline. The offline stage uses an actor-critic structure where the value function is learned via expectile regression (IQL-style), the Q-function via TD-error minimization, and the actor via a loss that combines advantage-weighted imitation with a policy-anchored advantage term that regularizes toward critic-preferred actions sampled from the current policy. The online stage continues self-improvement by mixing the offline dataset with newly collected trajectories in a replay buffer, updating the actor with advantage-weighted log-likelihood and a KL-regularization term that constrains the online policy to remain close to the offline-trained policy, preventing mode collapse and destabilizing shifts. At inference, the two LoRA adapters are activated sequentially—System 1 proposes a sub-goal, System 2 executes atomic actions until completion—enabling selective invocation and token-efficient interaction without in-context demonstrations.

## Results

Multi² achieves the strongest overall performance across three interactive benchmarks (ScienceWorld, ALFWorld, TextCraft) and three backbone LLMs (Qwen-2.5 3B, Mistral 7B, Llama-3.1 8B). On ScienceWorld ID, Multi² scores 60.68 / 69.97 / 67.61 (Qwen-2.5 3B / Mistral 7B / Llama-3.1 8B), outperforming the closest hierarchical baseline Glider (54.69 / 58.33 / 60.48) by 6–11 points. On ALFWorld OOD, Multi² reaches 49.29 / 50.71 / 56.43, exceeding Glider (41.43 / 45.71 / 37.86) by 8–19 points. On TextCraft, Multi² achieves 28.50 / 44.50 / 35.60 success rate, substantially ahead of all baselines (best competitor: ADaPT at 30.20 for Mistral 7B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Multi² consistently outperforms all five baselines on the majority of benchmark–backbone combinations. Against the closest competitor Glider (hierarchical offline RL with shared LoRA), Multi² gains 6–11 points on ScienceWorld ID and 8–19 points on ALFWorld OOD, with the sole exception being ScienceWorld OOD on Llama-3.1 8B where Glider edges ahead (34.36 vs. 30.68). On TextCraft, Multi²'s advantage over Glider is especially large (e.g., 44.50 vs. 28.50 for Mistral 7B; 35.60 vs. 9.50 for Llama-3.1 8B), reflecting the benefit of role-specialized RL training for compositional crafting. Prompt-based baselines (ReAct, Reflexion, ADaPT) trail by 30–55 points on ScienceWorld and ALFWorld, confirming that parameter updates and hierarchical decomposition are essential for long-horizon interaction. The single-agent RL baseline GRPO performs comparably to or worse than prompt-based methods, indicating that a unified policy without explicit role separation is insufficient for multi-turn tasks. Reflexion is the strongest prompt-based method on ALFWorld (up to 37.14 ID for Qwen-2.5 3B) thanks to its multi-trial self-reflection, but still falls 24–25 points short of Multi² on the same split.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the hierarchical LLM-agent line (Glider, ADaPT, ArCHer) by decoupling the planner and executor not just architecturally but at the training level: System 1 is trained with SFT while System 2 is trained with a purpose-built offline-to-online RL objective (policy-anchored IQL offline, KL-regularized AWAC online). It departs from Glider's single-shared-LoRA design by using separate role-specific adapters, and from prior offline-to-online RL work by tailoring the objective to the structured execution role rather than generic policy improvement. The dataset construction pipeline builds on Glider's framework but adds deterministic, rule-based sub-goal extraction to improve reproducibility.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two practical bottlenecks—objective drift and token inefficiency—that limit real-world deployment of LLM agents in interactive settings, and it releases hierarchical datasets that fill a gap in training/evaluation infrastructure for this area. However, the performance gains over the strongest baseline (Glider) are moderate (roughly 5–10 points on ScienceWorld ID), and the offline-to-online pipeline adds significant training complexity; whether the specific loss design (policy-anchored advantage, expectile regression, KL coefficient) generalizes beyond the three text-based benchmarks tested remains an open question. The finding that ID performance is largely scale-invariant while OOD improves monotonically with model size (within Qwen-2.5 1.5B–7B) hints that the bottleneck for generalization may be data coverage rather than capacity, but this is tested on a single model family.
