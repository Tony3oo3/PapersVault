## Problem

While the functional equivalence of vanilla multi-head attention is fully characterized by head permutations and GL(d_h) actions, it was unknown how the two most widely used positional encodings—sinusoidal (APE) and rotary (RoPE)—alter this symmetry structure. This gap matters because positional encodings are universal in practice, and their effect on parameter-space symmetries directly governs whether linear mode connectivity (LMC) can be established between independently trained Transformer models.

## Input / Output

The method consumes two trained MHA parameter sets, each consisting of h heads with W^Q, W^K, W^V, W^O ∈ R^{d×d_h} (plus optional biases via augmented matrices), along with the PE type (APE or RoPE). It produces an optimal head permutation σ* ∈ S_h and per-head continuous transformations: U_i ∈ GL(d_h) (APE) or U_i ∈ H(d_h) (RoPE) for the Q-K circuit, and V_i ∈ GL(d_h) for the V-O circuit. The alignment is entirely data-independent, operating on weight matrices alone, and the aligned parameters enable straight-line interpolation to measure LMC loss barriers at 25 evenly spaced points.

## Method

The core theoretical result characterizes the full symmetry group of MHA with RoPE as G_RoPE = S_h × (H(d_h) × GL(d_h))^h, where H(d_h) is an abelian subgroup of GL(d_h) isomorphic to (C^×)^(d_h/2) and strictly smaller than the GL(d_h) × GL(d_h) in the vanilla case. The mechanism is that RoPE inserts the relative rotation R_{m-n} between W^Q and W^K, which blocks the full GL(d_h) cancellation that preserves vanilla attention functionality; only the abelian subgroup H(d_h) (which contains all R_n) commutes with these rotations. The practical alignment algorithm is two-stage: Stage 1 solves a Linear Assignment Problem via the Hungarian algorithm (O(h³)) to find the optimal head permutation using a cost matrix built from centered QK^T and VO^T Frobenius distances; Stage 2 minimizes per-head Frobenius-norm losses over the appropriate symmetry group—gradient descent over GL(d_h) for APE, or decoupled 2D scalar minimization via Brent's method over H(d_h) for RoPE—initialized from an SVD-based orthogonal solution. Empirically, the method is applied to measure LMC across ViT, GPT-2, BERT, and Llama on vision and language benchmarks under four reinitialization strategies, revealing that LMC reliably emerges in encoder-only architectures but fails for large-scale decoder-only language modeling.

## Results

The paper's main theoretical result (Theorem 4.2) shows that RoPE reduces the attention symmetry group from GL(d_h) to the abelian subgroup H(d_h) ≅ (C×)^(d_h/2), making the RoPE function class strictly larger than vanilla or sinusoidal-PE attention. Empirically, LMC reliably emerges under first attention layer, first Transformer layer, and all-attention-layer re-initialization across vision (MNIST, CIFAR-10/100, ImageNet-1K) and text classification (AG News, IMDB, DBPedia) tasks, but fails under full-model re-initialization on large-scale benchmarks (ImageNet, WikiText-103, Enwik8, One Billion Word). The proposed two-stage matching algorithm reduces loss barrier ratios from ~78% (naive, no Stage 2) to 7–12% (full method) on average across datasets, with head permutation ranks typically 2–5 out of 24 possible permutations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not benchmark against other published alignment methods (e.g., Git Re-basin, Sinkhorn re-basin, or Theus et al.'s Transformer matching) in a head-to-head comparison; instead, it compares its own method against naive (unmatched) linear interpolation and against ablated variants of itself. The full two-stage method (head permutation + gradient-descent refinement) consistently outperforms Variant 1 (permutation only, no internal alignment) by a factor of ~8–10× in barrier reduction (e.g., 8.7% vs. 78.3% on CIFAR-10 APE), and outperforms Variant 2 (orthogonal initialization only, no gradient descent) by a further ~20–30% relative improvement. The head permutation stage (Stage 1) selects near-optimal permutations, ranking 2–5 out of 24 possible permutations on average, confirming that the LAP-based cost matrix effectively identifies the correct head correspondence. APE and RoPE settings show comparable barrier ratios (within ~1–2 percentage points), indicating that the matching algorithm generalizes across positional encoding types. The key empirical finding is that LMC is robust for encoder-only architectures (ViT, BERT) across all tested datasets but degrades or disappears for decoder-only models (GPT-2, Llama) on large-scale language modeling tasks under full-model re-initialization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the functional equivalence analysis of vanilla multi-head attention (Tran et al., 2025; Knyazev et al., 2025) to the setting with positional encodings, which prior work had overlooked. It builds on the Weight Matching framework of Ainsworth et al. (2023) by adapting the two-stage permutation-then-alignment procedure to attention-specific symmetry groups, and departs from Theus et al. (2025) by explicitly characterizing the internal Q-K and V-O component symmetries that their Transformer matching method ignores. The core theoretical contribution is showing that RoPE replaces the full GL(d_h) symmetry with the much smaller abelian group H(d_h) ≅ (C^×)^(d_h/2), while sinusoidal APE leaves the vanilla symmetry structure intact.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that RoPE's reduced symmetry group implies a strictly larger function class provides a principled theoretical rationale for RoPE's empirical dominance in modern LLMs, connecting a practical design choice to a formal expressivity argument. Empirically, the finding that LMC reliably emerges in encoder-only architectures but fails for large-scale decoder-only models (GPT-2, Llama) under full-model re-initialization raises important open questions about whether the convexity conjecture holds at scale. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is the difficulty of certifying LMC non-existence: without a complete symmetry characterization across all layers of deep models, one cannot rule out that a better alignment might still reveal connectivity.
