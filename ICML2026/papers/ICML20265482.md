## Problem

Existing OT-based pseudo-labeling methods for short text clustering only minimize the global transport cost between samples and candidate clusters, ignoring whether semantically similar samples receive consistent assignments. When transport costs to different candidate pseudo-labels are ambiguous (common near category boundaries in pretrained encoder spaces), these methods assign different pseudo-labels to nearby semantically similar samples, and the resulting noisy labels cascade through EM iterations to degrade clustering quality.

## Input / Output

The method takes short text samples (max 32 tokens) as input, encodes them via SBERT into 768-dimensional representations, and produces a cluster assignment per sample via argmax over the k-dimensional probability output of a clustering MLP. During training, each sample is contextually augmented into two views to support contrastive learning and attention-based similarity modeling. The framework is encoder-agnostic and generalizes to long-text documents and RGB images (STL-10, CIFAR-10/100) with the same CAOT formulation.

## Method

The core contribution is CAOT, a modified OT formulation that adds a semantic consistency term ⟨S, QQ^T⟩ to the standard entropic OT objective, where S = S^cos + S^att is a combined similarity matrix (cosine similarity of predicted probabilities plus attention-based similarity from a learned instance-level attention network G_h). This term drives the transport vectors of semantically similar samples toward alignment, ensuring they receive the same pseudo-label. Additionally, the target marginal b is no longer hard-constrained to uniform but is learned via a soft penalty Ψ(b) = −log(b) − log(1−b), allowing the method to adapt to varying degrees of category imbalance. The optimization is solved iteratively using first-order Taylor expansion of the consistency term combined with Lagrange multipliers. Training follows an EM schedule: the E-step solves CAOT for pseudo-labels, while the M-step jointly updates the encoder, projection network, attention network, and clustering network using a composite loss (L_A + L_P + λL_I) that includes contrastive learning for representation discriminability, attention optimization for similarity accuracy, and supervised cross-entropy against CAOT pseudo-labels.

## Results

On eight short-text clustering benchmarks, the proposed CAOT framework achieves the best ACC or NMI in 11 out of 16 evaluation slots. Headline gains include +5.01% ACC over the strongest OT-based baseline (RSTC) on StackOverflow (85.96 vs. 80.07), +2.32% NMI on Tweet (89.49 vs. 87.17), and +1.06% NMI on AgNews (66.55 vs. 65.49). On imbalanced datasets (GoogleNews-TS/T/S, Tweet), CAOT consistently outperforms conventional OT by 14–35% ACC, confirming that the semantic consistency term is critical under class imbalance. Cross-domain experiments show the framework generalizes to long-text (e.g., 83.24% ACC on SyskillWebert vs. 74.13% for TCLLM) and image clustering (85.96% on CIFAR-10, competitive with discDC's 85.28%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest OT-based competitor RSTC (Zheng et al., ACL 2023), CAOT wins on 7 of 8 datasets in ACC, with the largest margin on StackOverflow (+5.89%) and Tweet (+4.61%), but loses on GoogleNews-TS (83.53 vs. 86.97 for SCPCL; RSTC scores 83.30). Against the recent SOTA FNSCC (Zheng et al., EMNLP 2025), CAOT dominates on StackOverflow (85.96 vs. 70.87 ACC, +15.09%) and Biomedical (47.37 vs. 35.54), but FNSCC leads on SearchSnippets ACC (85.16 vs. 80.12). Against SCPCL (Liu et al., EAAI 2025), CAOT wins on 6 of 8 datasets, with notable gains on StackOverflow (+5.01% ACC) and Tweet (+2.02% ACC), while SCPCL retains the edge on GoogleNews-TS ACC (86.97 vs. 83.53). Conventional and contrastive-only baselines (TF-IDF, SimCSE, ESimCSE, SCL) trail by 5–30% ACC across all datasets, confirming that joint representation-learning-and-clustering with OT-based pseudo-labeling is essential. In cross-domain settings, CAOT outperforms TCLLM on all four long-text datasets (e.g., +9.11% on SyskillWebert) and matches or exceeds discDC on CIFAR-10 (85.96 vs. 85.28) while trailing slightly on CIFAR-100 (47.86 vs. 47.93).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of OT-based pseudo-labeling for short text clustering initiated by SELA, PPOT, and RSTC, which model sample-to-cluster global structure through transport cost minimization. The key departure is the identification and correction of a specific gap: existing OT formulations ignore sample-to-sample semantic consistency, causing semantically similar samples near decision boundaries to receive conflicting pseudo-labels. The proposed CAOT addresses this by adding a ⟨S, QQ^T⟩ consistency term to the OT objective and introducing a learnable instance-level attention network to supply the semantic similarity matrix S, while also replacing the hard uniformity constraint with a soft penalty Ψ(b) to handle class imbalance. The overall EM training paradigm and the use of contrastive learning for representation enhancement follow established practice in the short text clustering literature (SCCL, RSTC, SCPCL).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled fix for a well-motivated failure mode in OT-based clustering (inconsistent pseudo-labels for semantically similar samples), and the adaptive uniformity penalty offers a practical mechanism for handling category imbalance without dataset-specific tuning. The demonstrated cross-domain generalization to long-text and image clustering, combined with batch-level (rather than full-dataset) OT solving, suggests the framework could serve as a general-purpose pseudo-labeling module beyond short text. Open questions include whether the instance-level attention network is strictly necessary compared to simpler k-NN or graph-based local consistency constraints, and whether the approach maintains its advantage when the number of clusters grows very large (e.g., thousands), where the n×k transport matrix becomes expensive.
