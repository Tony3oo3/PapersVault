## Problem

Existing multimodal chain-of-thought (MCoT) methods rely on single-rationale supervision, causing models to learn surface-level mappings to a standard answer rather than capturing the diversity and self-corrective nature of human reasoning. This leaves MLLMs vulnerable to misleading or adversarial explanations, with rigid reasoning patterns, weak logical robustness, and no mechanism for identifying or correcting flawed reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265648/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes an RGB image (encoded by a frozen BLIP2-flan-t5-xxl), a question, multiple-choice options, and a frozen Qwen2.5-VL-72B-generated image caption, all within a 512-token sequence limit. In Phase II (P2CL-II), an additional rationale (either positive or negative) is injected as a semantic cue, and the model must output a concatenated [answer, positive rationale] sequence regardless of whether the input rationale was correct or inverted. The RAD paradigm expands each original sample's single rationale into pools of diverse positive and semantically inverted negative rationales (up to 1000× expansion).

## Method

MIND is built on a T5-based encoder-decoder (223M/738M, FLAN-Alpaca initialized) and introduces three synergistic components. The RAD paradigm uses structured positive prompts (10–50% content rephrasing) and negative prompts (minor edits that reverse logical meaning) to batch-generate diverse rationale pools from large models. The P2CL strategy trains in two phases: Phase I performs multi-rationale positive learning via maximum-likelihood generation over randomly sampled positive rationales, while Phase II injects positive or negative rationales as input and supervises the model to always produce the correct answer with a positive rationale, thereby learning to identify and correct logical errors. The MCA optimization strategy projects rationale embeddings into a contrastive space and applies a margin-based loss that pulls Bottom-k hard positives closer and pushes Top-k hard negatives farther from the predicted embedding, sharpening discriminative boundaries in the multi-rationale semantic space.

## Results

On ScienceQA, MIND_base (223M parameters) achieves 92.29% average accuracy, setting a new SOTA and improving 6.98% over Multimodal-CoT_base (85.31%). On A-OKVQA, MIND_base reaches 70.6% accuracy, a 20.0% absolute gain over Multimodal-CoT_base (50.6%). On M³CoT, MIND_base achieves 57.38% and MIND_large (738M) achieves 61.56%, outperforming Multimodal-CoT by 12.53% and 12.83% respectively, and surpassing GPT-4V (56.95%) with a fraction of the parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MIND_base (223M) outperforms all same-scale CoT-based baselines on ScienceQA: +1.65% over MC-CoT_base (90.64%), +1.32% over DPMM-CoT_base (90.97%), and +0.54% over Multimodal-T-SciQ_base (91.75%), while achieving a dramatic +6.98% gain over its direct predecessor Multimodal-CoT_base (85.31%). On A-OKVQA, MIND_base (70.6%) surpasses the strongest fine-tuning baseline GPV-2 (60.3%) by 10.3% and Multimodal-CoT_base (50.6%) by 20.0%. On M³CoT, MIND_large (738M, 61.56%) exceeds GPT-4V zero-shot (56.95%) by 4.61% with far fewer parameters, and beats MC-CoT_large (57.69%) by 3.87%. The paper also demonstrates that MIND's gains are not attributable to stronger external modules: under matched visual encoder and caption generator settings, MIND_base still improves 3.82%–4.50% over Multimodal-CoT_base. Robustness experiments (Table 9) show MIND_base maintains 88–93% accuracy under six perturbation types where Multimodal-CoT_base drops to 4–47%, confirming the discriminative correction mechanism's effectiveness.

## Relation to Prior Work

MIND builds directly on Multimodal-CoT (Zhang et al., 2024b), adopting its two-stage encoder-decoder training framework as the base architecture. It departs from the single-rationale supervision paradigm shared by MC-CoT, DPMM-CoT, and T-SciQ by introducing a multi-rationale data foundation (RAD) that co-generates diverse positive and semantically inverted negative rationales. The P2CL strategy operationalizes a human cognitive loop ('Understand → Rethink → Correct') that prior MCoT methods lack, while the MCA strategy introduces a margin-based contrastive loss (rather than InfoNCE) to sharpen discriminative boundaries in the rationale embedding space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical vulnerability of current MLLMs: susceptibility to misleading or adversarial reasoning cues, which is critical for real-world deployment. The robustness results (maintaining ~90% accuracy under six perturbation types while Multimodal-CoT drops to 4–47%) suggest a path toward more reliable multimodal reasoning. However, validation is limited to VQA-style benchmarks at small model scale (223M/738M), and the offline RAD data construction adds a one-time preparation cost. Open questions include whether the multi-rationale discriminative paradigm scales to billion-parameter models and extends beyond multiple-choice VQA to open-ended or multi-step reasoning tasks.
