## Problem

Existing forget gate mechanisms in softmax attention, such as FoX, are limited to scalar gates that apply a uniform decay across all feature dimensions. While fine-grained element-wise gates have proven more effective in linear attention variants, directly extending them to softmax attention is algebraically infeasible because a vector-valued bias cannot be added to the scalar attention logit q_i·k_j^T. This paper asks how to bring fine-grained forgetting into softmax attention without sacrificing its foundational strengths.

## Input / Output

FiX takes a standard text token sequence as input and produces a text token sequence as output in a causal language modeling setting. The key internal detail is that at each time step, a per-dimension forget gate vector f_i ∈ (0,1)^{d_v} is generated from the hidden state and applied element-wise to value vectors, making the mechanism a positional encoding on the V-O axis rather than the Q-K axis. The attention output is RMS-normalized with an unusually small ε=10^{-30} to preserve the mathematical equivalence that underpins the formulation.

## Method

The core insight is that under a subsequent RMSNorm layer (with ε→0), the softmax normalization denominator becomes redundant, making the standard QK-FoX formulation mathematically equivalent to applying cumulative forget gates as element-wise multiplication on value vectors (VO-FoX). This reformulation enables fine-grained vector-valued forget gates on v and o, which FiX implements via a low-rank projection with a Mamba-style activation for wider dynamic range. For efficient training, the authors derive a rescaling trick that decomposes the attention computation into a numerically stable matrix-matrix multiplication (for off-diagonal blocks) and a per-element loop (for the diagonal block), yielding Flash Fine-grained Attention. They further fuse the attention, RMSNorm, and subsequent linear layer into a single operator to maintain float32 precision throughout, and design a paged VF cache that absorbs forget gates into value pages to minimize inference memory overhead.

## Results

On zero-shot common sense benchmarks (760M-parameter models trained on 48B tokens), FiX achieves the best average accuracy (55.24%) and geometric mean (53.79%) across 10 tasks, outperforming RoPE (54.82% avg), FoX (54.42% avg), and FoX-RoPE (54.67% avg). FiX also attains the lowest LAMBADA perplexity among standalone methods (12.69 vs. 13.39 for RoPE and 13.35 for FoX) and the highest LAMBADA accuracy (47.78%). On long-context tasks, FiX maintains stable per-token loss up to 16,384 tokens (trained at 4,096) and achieves the highest average accuracy on BABILong, particularly in the 1k–4k context range.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FiX is compared against RoPE (standard rotary positional encoding) and FoX (scalar forget gate in softmax attention), as well as their combinations (FiX-RoPE, FoX-RoPE). On the primary common-sense benchmark suite, standalone FiX achieves the highest average accuracy (55.24%) and geometric mean (53.79%), beating RoPE by +0.42 avg and FoX by +0.82 avg; it also yields the best LAMBADA perplexity (12.69) and accuracy (47.78%) among non-combined methods. FiX-RoPE achieves the lowest LAMBADA perplexity (12.32) and highest LAMBADA accuracy (48.55) overall, and has the lowest training loss, but its average accuracy (54.85%) is slightly below standalone FiX, suggesting that the pretraining loss does not perfectly correlate with downstream task performance. On length extrapolation (trained at 4,096, tested up to 16,384), both FiX and FoX maintain stable loss while RoPE collapses sharply beyond the training length; FiX consistently achieves slightly lower per-token loss than FoX across CodeParrot, PG-19, and NarrativeQA. On BABILong long-context reasoning, FiX significantly outperforms all baselines, with the largest margins in the 1k–4k context range.

## Relation to Prior Work

FiX extends the Forgetting Transformer (FoX, ICLR 2025) line of work, which introduced scalar forget gates into softmax attention, by upgrading to element-wise (fine-grained) gates. The key algebraic insight—that the softmax denominator becomes redundant under a subsequent RMSNorm layer, making a VO-decay formulation equivalent to the standard QK-decay formulation—enables this upgrade, which was previously infeasible because a vector-valued bias cannot be added to a scalar attention logit. The fine-grained gate design borrows the Mamba-style activation for wider dynamic range, and the implementation builds on FlashAttention's tiling strategy and PagedAttention's memory management. FiX is positioned as the first positional encoding applied to value-output (VO) pairs, making it complementary to existing query-key (QK) encodings like RoPE rather than a replacement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The VO positional encoding opens a new axis of design for attention mechanisms that is orthogonal to and combinable with QK encodings, potentially allowing future architectures to independently tune both. The practical implementation work (fused Flash Fine-grained Attention kernel, paged VF cache with ε=10⁻³⁰ RMSNorm) addresses real deployment challenges but introduces an unusual numerical requirement that may complicate integration into existing training stacks. Limitations include evaluation only at 760M parameter scale, modest absolute gains over FoX, and the observation that FiX-RoPE achieves lower training loss yet slightly worse downstream accuracy than standalone FiX, leaving open whether the fine-grained advantage persists or shifts at larger scales. Whether the RMSNorm equivalence holds robustly across all architectural variants (e.g., without O-Norm) remains an open question.
