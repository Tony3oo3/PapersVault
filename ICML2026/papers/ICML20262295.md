## Problem

Existing annotation-free T2V post-training methods derive reward signals from pre-trained VLMs whose text and video embeddings are misaligned due to non-distributional training objectives (contrastive learning, pointwise matching), resulting in suboptimal supervision that cannot match annotation-based approaches. The paper asks whether an annotation-free method can match or outperform annotation-based T2V post-training by properly aligning the text-video embedding space before computing rewards.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262295/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

PISCES takes a text prompt as input and produces a generated video as output, operating as a post-training stage on existing T2V diffusion/flow-matching models (VideoCrafter2 at 2s/8FPS/512×320 for short video, HunyuanVideo at 5s/25FPS/848×480 for long video). During training, the prompt is encoded via frozen InternVideo2 into text embeddings, and generated videos are encoded into video patch tokens; the OT-aligned rewards are computed on these embeddings to supervise the denoiser. At inference, no additional reward models are needed, and GRPO-tuned models reduce denoising steps from 50 to 16 via consistency distillation for ~3× faster generation.

## Method

PISCES introduces a Dual OT-aligned Rewards module that aligns text and video embeddings at two levels before computing supervision. First, a Distributional OT-aligned Quality Reward learns a Neural OT transport map T (via NOT with squared Euclidean cost) that pushes the text embedding distribution onto the real-video embedding manifold; the reward is then the cosine similarity between the OT-projected text [CLS] token and the generated video [CLS] token, acting as a proxy for real-video quality. Second, a Discrete Token-level OT-aligned Semantic Reward constructs a per-head cost matrix over text tokens and video patch tokens combining semantic similarity, temporal (expected frame index), and spatial (expected 2D position) penalties, then solves a partial OT problem (mass fraction m=0.9) via entropic unbalanced Sinkhorn; the resulting transport plan is fused with vanilla cross-attention in log-space and passed through InternVideo2's VTM classifier to yield the semantic reward. The dual rewards are integrated into post-training via either direct backpropagation through a consistency distillation loss or GRPO reinforcement learning, with LoRA applied to the denoiser.

## Results

On VBench, PISCES achieves the highest scores across all metrics for both short-video (VideoCrafter2: Total 82.75, Quality 84.05, Semantic 77.54) and long-video (HunyuanVideo: Total 85.45, Quality 86.73, Semantic 80.33) generation, outperforming all annotation-based and annotation-free baselines. The Semantic Score improvement is particularly notable, with PISCES reaching 80.33 on HunyuanVideo versus 77.52 for the best baseline (T2V-Turbo-v2), a +2.81 point gain. In broader comparisons (Table 2), PISCES-post-trained HunyuanVideo surpasses closed-source models including Gen-3 (Total 82.32) and Kling (Total 81.85) on all VBench dimensions. Human preference studies (n=85, 400 prompts) confirm consistent preference for PISCES over T2V-Turbo-v2 and VideoReward-DPO in visual quality, motion quality, and semantic alignment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PISCES outperforms all 11 baselines on every VBench metric for both short- and long-video generation. Among annotation-free methods, T2V-Turbo-v2 (Li et al., 2025a) is the strongest baseline (HV: Total 84.25, Semantic 77.52), but PISCES exceeds it by +1.20 Total and +2.81 Semantic on HunyuanVideo. Among annotation-based methods, VideoDPO (Liu et al., 2025d) leads (HV: Total 84.13, Semantic 77.83), yet PISCES surpasses it by +1.32 Total and +2.50 Semantic. VCM (Wang et al., 2023b) performs worst among post-training methods (VC2 Semantic drops to 55.66), while UnifiedReward (Wang et al., 2025b) and VideoReward-DPO (Liu et al., 2025b) show modest gains over vanilla but fall well short of PISCES. In the broader model comparison (Table 2), PISCES-post-trained HunyuanVideo (Total 85.45) exceeds all closed-source systems including Gen-3 (82.32), Kling (81.85), and Pika-1.0 (80.69), as well as open-source models like Show-1 (78.93) and ModelScope (75.75). The paper also confirms via human evaluation that PISCES is consistently preferred over T2V-Turbo-v2 and VideoReward-DPO across visual quality, motion, and text alignment dimensions.

## Relation to Prior Work

PISCES builds on Neural Optimal Transport (NOT) for learning distributional transport maps, Consistency Distillation for efficient single-step denoising, and GRPO for RL-based fine-tuning, but departs fundamentally from prior annotation-free T2V post-training (T2V-Turbo, T2V-Turbo-v2) which computes rewards directly on misaligned VLM embeddings via cosine similarity. The key conceptual departure is identifying distributional misalignment between text and video embeddings in pre-trained VLMs as the core bottleneck, and addressing it with a Dual OT-aligned Rewards module that operates at both the distributional level (Neural OT map) and the discrete token level (Partial OT with spatio-temporal constraints via Sinkhorn). This positions PISCES as the first work to apply OT as a reward-space alignment mechanism in generative post-training, rather than in domain adaptation or retrieval settings where OT has been previously explored.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PISCES demonstrates that annotation-free reward-based post-training can match or exceed annotation-based methods (VideoReward-DPO, VideoDPO, UnifiedReward) on VBench and human evaluation, removing the scalability bottleneck of large-scale human preference collection. The OT alignment framework is modular and compatible with multiple optimization paradigms (direct backpropagation, GRPO) and base VLMs (InternVideo2, ViCLIP), suggesting broad applicability. However, performance still depends on the representational precision of the underlying VLM for fine-grained grounding (e.g., partial OT fails on small objects like 'glasses'), and the approach has only been validated on two T2V architectures. Open questions include whether OT-aligned rewards transfer to other generative modalities (image, 3D, audio) and whether the distributional alignment step can be made adaptive per-prompt rather than learned globally.
