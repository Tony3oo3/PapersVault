## Problem

Existing methods for identifying long-context attention heads (e.g., retrieval heads, induction heads) require running the model on actual long inputs or hand-crafted probes, making them expensive, task-dependent, and unable to reveal the intrinsic parameter properties that give heads their long-context capability. This paper asks whether the long-context potential of an attention head can be determined purely from its static weight matrices, without any inference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261443/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes only the static W_Q and W_K projection matrices (each of size d_h × h) for every attention head, decomposed into d = d_h/2 frequency blocks of size 2×h, together with the RoPE rotation frequencies ω_f = base^{-f/d}. All kernel computations are evaluated at a single fixed relative distance n = −32768, since the energy properties are nearly invariant to the rotation angle. Outputs include a per-head LPS scalar, a per-head RPS scalar (LPS minus a symmetry penalty to exclude induction heads), and optionally modified query frequency blocks q_f scaled by per-frequency amplification factors c_f, which change the kernel energy by a factor of c_f².

## Method

The paper decomposes each attention head's kernel K(n) into a sum of d frequency kernels K_f(n) = q_f^T R_f(n) k_f, one per RoPE frequency pair, and identifies two static properties that characterize long-context heads: (1) energy concentration in the low-frequency band, captured by a weighted distribution score s_dist = Σ f·p_f/d, and (2) a low effective rank of the head kernel, captured by s_eRank = 1 − eRank(K(n))/d, where eRank is the exponential of the Shannon entropy of the frequency-energy distribution. The Long-context Potential Score combines these as LPS = s_dist^α · s_eRank^(1−α) (α = 0.4). To separate retrieval heads from induction heads, a symmetry score s_sym measuring the self-correlation of the kernel is subtracted to yield the Retrieval Potential Score RPS = LPS − β·s_sym (β = 0.5). For enhancement, the method scales the query frequency blocks q_f of top-RPS heads by per-frequency factors c_f that amplify low-frequency components and suppress high-frequency ones, directly increasing the low-frequency kernel energy and improving long-context retrieval performance without any retraining.

## Results

Masking the top-10 LPS heads in Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct causes RULER 128K to collapse from 65.78 to 11.13, while masking 10 random heads barely changes it (66.49). The same pattern holds across models: Llama-3.1-8B drops from 75.81 to 7.93 and Qwen3-4B from 66.30 to 7.61 at 128K, whereas general benchmarks (MMLU, HellaSwag, ARC-C, OBQA) remain within 1–2 points. Amplifying the low-frequency kernels of top-RPS heads improves RULER 128K by +2.11 points on Qwen3-4B (66.30→68.41) and +1.08 on Llama-3.1-8B (75.81→76.20), with the largest NIAH gains at 128K context. The entire identification and amplification pipeline is fully static, requiring no forward pass on long inputs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is random head removal/amplification, which serves as a control to confirm that the LPS-identified heads are specifically responsible for long-context performance rather than any arbitrary subset. Across all three models, masking top-LPS heads degrades RULER 128K by 54–68 points, while masking the same number of random heads changes performance by less than 5 points, confirming the specificity of the identification. For head amplification, top-RPS heads outperform random-head amplification on Qwen3-4B at 128K (68.41 vs. 61.11, +7.3 points) and on Llama-3.1-8B at 64K (85.56 vs. 84.25). The paper also compares its static retrieval potential score against Wu et al.'s (2025) online retrieval score as a gold standard: at Top-10% overlap, 58.2% of Wu's retrieval heads are recovered by the static method on Llama-3.2-3B and 49.6% on Qwen3-4B, substantially above the ~10% expected by chance. This demonstrates that the purely static frequency-kernel analysis captures the majority of functionally important long-context heads without any inference on long inputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RoPE kernel analysis initiated by Chiang & Yogatama (2025), who showed that high-frequency components cause dimension inefficiency in long-distance retrieval, by analyzing the *whole-head* kernel properties (energy distribution across frequencies and effective rank) rather than individual frequency dimensions. It departs from the dominant paradigm of identifying long-context heads (retrieval heads, induction heads) through expensive online inference on long inputs (Wu et al. 2025; Zhang et al. 2025) by proposing a purely static, parameter-only scoring method. Notably, it takes the opposite direction from DoPE (Xiong et al. 2025), which suppresses low-frequency RoPE components to denoise in-context learning, by instead amplifying those same low-frequency kernels to enhance long-context retrieval.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to identify functionally critical attention heads from static parameters alone—without any forward pass on long sequences—opens a fast, low-resource path to model interpretability and targeted enhancement that could be integrated into model development pipelines. The dramatic masking results (e.g., RULER 128K dropping from 65.8 to 11.1 on Llama-3.2-3B) across three architectures provide strong evidence that a small subset of heads carries most of the long-context retrieval load. However, the practical amplification gains are modest (1–3 points on RULER, a few points on NIAH at 64K/128K), and the method is specific to RoPE-based decoder-only transformers; whether the frequency-kernel perspective generalizes to other positional encodings (ALiBi, NoPE) or to training-time interventions remains open.
