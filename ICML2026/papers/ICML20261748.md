## Problem

Existing diffusion distillation methods preserve perceptual fidelity but severely degrade the student's sensitivity to initial noise, because standard pointwise alignment objectives (MSE, KL divergence) encourage the student to approximate a smoothed conditional expectation, flattening the local input-output geometry. This loss of differential response to noise perturbations undermines generative diversity and breaks a broad class of noise-based control techniques (layout steering, noise retrieval, test-time optimization) that rely on the model's precise responsiveness to input changes.

## Input / Output

The method takes an initial Gaussian noise vector z ~ N(0,I) and a text prompt as input, producing a generated image in 1–4 steps. Unusually, GAD consumes *paired* inputs during training: the original noise z and a perturbed version z' = z + hv (where v ~ N(0,I) and h is a small timestep-adaptive scale). Both are fed through teacher and student, and the loss operates on the *difference* between paired outputs (response vectors) rather than on individual pointwise predictions, making the objective relational rather than absolute.

## Method

GAD is a model-agnostic plug-and-play regularizer that aligns the local functional geometry of teacher and student by matching their Jacobian-vector products (JVPs) with respect to the input noise. Since computing full Jacobians is infeasible at image scale (d ≈ 10^5), GAD uses a finite-difference approximation: it perturbs the input by hv, computes the output difference, and enforces that the student's response vector matches the teacher's (with stop-gradient on the teacher). Theoretically, matching responses to random perturbations is equivalent in expectation to minimizing the Frobenius norm of the Jacobian difference (Hutchinson estimator), so the objective implicitly aligns the full local geometry. The total training loss is L_base + λ·L_GAD, and GAD is instantiated across three distillation paradigms—output matching (LADD), distribution matching (TDM), and score identity distillation (SiD)—on UNet, DiT, and Flow-DiT backbones, requiring only one extra forward pass per training step with zero inference overhead.

## Results

On the seed identifiability diagnostic (the paper's core metric), GAD restores noise sensitivity to 92.40% self-identifiability and 87.40% teacher alignment, closely matching the multi-step teacher (93.70%) and substantially outperforming the best baseline LADD (87.60% / 83.70%). On the layout control task, GAD achieves AP 5.8 (recovering 87% of the teacher's 6.6), compared to LADD at 5.0 and SD-Turbo at 3.0. On general generation quality, GAD slightly improves fidelity across all three architectures (e.g., SiD+GAD raises CLIP from 32.75 to 34.40 on SANA), and reduces FID-vs-Teacher from 11.29 to 7.85 on SD2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GAD outperforms all 11 compared baselines across every evaluated metric. On seed identifiability, the closest competitor LADD (87.60%) trails GAD (92.40%) by 4.8 points, while SD-Turbo (77.80%) and SwiftBrush (52.90%) show severe sensitivity collapse. On layout control (AP), GAD (5.8) beats LADD (5.0) by 0.8, TCD (4.8) by 1.0, and SD-Turbo (3.0) by 2.8, recovering 87% of the teacher's 6.6. On zero-shot NoiseQuery transfer, GAD consistently improves HPSv2 over its base method (e.g., SiD+GAD 0.289 vs. SiD 0.249 on SANA), confirming functional alignment with the teacher. On diversity (Vendi Score), GAD sits in the upper-right region of the diversity-fidelity trade-off, outperforming DMD, LCM, YOSO, and FLASH which all exhibit the typical diversity-fidelity trade-off. On FID-vs-Teacher, GAD reduces the gap by 30% on SD2 (7.85 vs. 11.29) and 11% on SANA (7.36 vs. 8.48), while maintaining or slightly improving COCO-30k FID.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion distillation literature (LADD, TDM, SiD, DMD, LCM) by identifying a systematic failure mode of standard pointwise alignment objectives: they flatten the input-output landscape and suppress the teacher's local geometric structure, degrading sensitivity to initial noise. Conceptually, it draws on relational knowledge distillation (Park et al., 2019; Tung & Mori, 2019) and Jacobian matching in knowledge transfer (Srinivas & Fleuret, 2018), but repurposes these ideas for generative models by aligning Jacobian-vector products via a finite-difference approximation. The resulting GAD loss is positioned as a model-agnostic, plug-and-play regularizer orthogonal to the base distillation objective, rather than a new distillation paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for anyone deploying few-step T2I models that need to support noise-based control (layout steering, NoiseQuery retrieval, test-time optimization), since standard distillation silently breaks these capabilities. The finding that geometric alignment also slightly improves fidelity metrics (CLIP, PickScore) without additional inference cost makes it an attractive drop-in enhancement. Limitations include 36–66% training overhead from the extra forward pass, and the finite-difference approximation introduces a hyperparameter (h) that requires tuning per architecture. Open questions include extending GAD to video diffusion where temporal noise correlations add complexity, and whether more sample-efficient Jacobian estimators can reduce the training cost.
