## Problem

Under limited supervision, prompt tuning on CLIP drives adapted representations away from the pretrained feature manifold toward directions that capture dataset-specific shortcuts (background patterns, texture artifacts) rather than transferable semantics, degrading generalization to unseen classes and cross-domain distributions. Existing prompt-based methods mitigate overfitting through heuristic regularization on logits or parameters but do not explicitly constrain how prompt updates alter feature geometry relative to the frozen CLIP representations.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (processed by a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 backbone) and a set of class labels, producing a predicted class label via cosine similarity matching between fused visual and text features. Unusually, the text side is anchored not only by the prompted class template but also by LLM-generated (GPT-3) class descriptions that form a semantic prototype bank, and the visual side requires both a frozen-branch and a prompt-branch forward pass per image, with the two branches fused before classification.

## Method

ManiPT introduces two complementary mechanisms to perform prompt tuning on the pretrained manifold. First, cosine consistency losses are imposed on both modalities: the visual-side loss penalizes deviation of prompt-tuned image features from frozen CLIP features, while the text-side loss anchors prompted text features to LLM-derived semantic prototypes, confining adaptation within the pretrained geometric neighborhood. Second, a structural bias is implemented via normalized additive fusion (f = normalize(z_frozen + h_prompt)), which geometrically contracts the final representation toward the frozen reference and enforces incremental corrections rather than arbitrary replacement. Theoretically, the paper proves that this fusion induces a contraction factor of at most 1/2 in squared distance, that the consistency loss bounds the logit perturbation magnitude, and that the population risk upper bound scales with the square root of the consistency loss, providing a formal guarantee that ManiPT alleviates overfitting under limited data.

## Results

On the base-to-novel generalization benchmark across 11 datasets, ManiPT achieves an average harmonic mean of 82.09% (Base: 85.82%, Novel: 78.67%), outperforming the strongest baselines TAC (81.24%) and LLaMP (81.27%). In cross-dataset transfer from ImageNet, ManiPT attains 68.04% average accuracy, exceeding CoPrompt (66.99%) and TAC (66.53%). For domain generalization on four ImageNet variants, ManiPT achieves 61.88% average, and in few-shot classification it leads across all shot settings (e.g., 74.75% at 1-shot vs. 72.32% for PromptSRC).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ManiPT outperforms all nine baselines on the average harmonic mean across 11 datasets (82.09% vs. 81.27% for LLaMP and 81.24% for TAC, the two closest competitors). The gap is most pronounced on fine-grained and texture datasets: on FGVCAircraft, ManiPT achieves 43.68% HM versus 41.90% (LLaMP) and 40.86% (TAC), and on DTD it reaches 75.65% versus 75.24% (TAP) and 72.79% (CoPrompt). In cross-dataset transfer, ManiPT leads by a clear margin (68.04% vs. 66.99% for CoPrompt, a ~1-point gap), with the largest advantage on FGVCAircraft (27.00% vs. 24.74% for MaPLe). For domain generalization, ManiPT edges out TAC by 0.25 points (61.88% vs. 61.63%) and CoPrompt by 1.46 points. In the few-shot regime, ManiPT consistently leads at every shot count, with the largest relative gains at 1-shot (74.75% vs. 72.32% for PromptSRC, a 2.4-point improvement), confirming that the manifold-preserving mechanism is most beneficial under extreme data scarcity.

## Relation to Prior Work

ManiPT extends the CLIP prompt tuning paradigm established by CoOp and deep prompting (Jia et al., 2022) by reframing the adaptation problem geometrically: rather than making prompts more expressive or adding heuristic regularization (as in CoCoOp, MaPLe, PromptSRC, CoPrompt, TAC, TAP), it treats frozen CLIP features as a proxy for the pretrained manifold and constrains prompt-induced feature movement via cosine consistency losses and a normalized additive fusion (structural bias). The conceptual lineage draws on manifold learning (Belkin et al., 2006; Rifai et al., 2011) and manifold mixup (Verma et al., 2019), but applies these ideas to the specific problem of preventing prompt-tuned representations from drifting off the pretrained feature geometry. The LLM-based knowledge enrichment component is positioned alongside TAC, TAP, and LLaMP, but is used here specifically to construct stable semantic anchors for the text-side consistency constraint rather than to enrich prompt content.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clear geometric diagnosis (manifold drift quantified via PCA) for why prompt tuning overfits under limited supervision, and the contraction lemma gives a clean theoretical justification for the additive fusion design. However, the performance gains over strong baselines like CoPrompt and TAC are modest (roughly 1–2 points in harmonic mean), and the method is validated only on CLIP ViT-B/16, leaving open whether the manifold-preservation principle transfers to larger or differently-architected VLMs. The PCA-based drift metric is an approximation rather than a true manifold measure, and the non-near-opposition condition required for the fusion to be well-defined is assumed rather than guaranteed. Future work could explore whether the structural bias generalizes to other parameter-efficient adaptation methods (adapters, LoRA) and whether the geometric constraints interact differently with larger backbones.
