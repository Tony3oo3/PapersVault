## Problem

Existing 3D generative models produce static, non-interactive geometry that cannot be grasped, pushed, or manipulated by agents, creating a critical bottleneck for embodied AI simulators and interactive virtual worlds. The paper argues that interactive asset generation must be driven by functional logic and hierarchical physics (materials, kinematics, affordances) rather than holistic shape synthesis alone, and that no prior method jointly generates part-aware geometry with precise articulation parameters and physical property annotations from a single image.

## Input / Output

The pipeline accepts a single RGB image, an optional 2D part mask for granularity control, and 3D voxels produced by TRELLIS's first stage as spatial conditioning. The output is a simulation-ready 3D asset comprising part-aware textured geometry, per-part bounding boxes, a four-tier physical annotation (holistic scale/scene, static material/mass, functional state machine, and interactive joint type with 8-dimensional kinematic parameters: 3D origin, 3D axis, 2D motion limits), and parent-child part hierarchy. The kinematic parameters are continuous values predicted by the diffusion head, while the qualitative joint type (revolute, prismatic, etc.) is a discrete token from the VLM planner.

## Method

PhysForge is a decoupled two-stage framework. Stage 1 fine-tunes Qwen2.5-VL as a 'physical architect' that autoregressively generates a Hierarchical Physical Blueprint: part bounding boxes (encoded via 66 special tokens, 6 per box), parent-child relationships, joint types, materials, and functions, conditioned on the input image, optional 2D mask, and 3D voxels processed by a Part-Field encoder plus position-aware 3D convolution. A key finding is that co-predicting physical properties alongside geometry resolves part-granularity ambiguity, enabling reasonable decomposition even without mask input. Stage 2 extends OmniPart's diffusion architecture with a novel KineVoxel Injection (KVI) mechanism: 8D articulation vectors are encoded by lightweight 2-layer MLPs into a 'KineVoxel' latent, injected after downsampling and concatenated with geometry voxel latents before the middle transformer, with a joint-type embedding from Stage 1 providing a functional prior. The model is trained with Conditional Flow Matching using a composite loss that separately penalizes geometry and kinematic velocity prediction, with a weighting factor λ_kine=10 to prioritize articulation accuracy.

## Results

On articulated object generation (Table 4), PhysForge achieves a Chamfer Distance of 10.21 and CLIP-Similarity of 0.93, substantially outperforming Articulate Anything (CD 23.31), Singapo (CD 21.10), and URDFormer (CD 25.42). Joint parameter accuracy is the headline result: PhysForge reports Joint-Axis-Err-5 of 0.101 and Joint-Pivot-Err-5 of 0.071, roughly 2.4× lower than Singapo (0.241/0.153) and 6× lower than Articulate Anything (0.608/0.257). On physics property generation (Table 1, PhysXNet), PhysForge reduces material prediction MAE to 0.81 (vs. 1.59 for PhysXGen) and affordance MAE to 1.22 (vs. 3.69), while also achieving the best geometry (CD 9.21, F1-0.05 75.43). For part structure planning (Table 3), PhysForge attains BBox IoU of 42.95 and Voxel Recall of 77.16, surpassing OmniPart (41.66/73.79) and PartField (37.33/69.65).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PhysForge outperforms all seven baselines across every evaluated metric. Against TRELLIS (Xiang et al., 2024) and PhysXGen (Cao et al., 2025) on physics property generation, PhysForge achieves lower CD (9.21 vs. 10.10/9.81 on PhysXNet; 22.89 vs. 24.32/25.30 on PhysDB), better F1-0.05 (75.43 vs. 72.47/73.60), and substantially lower material MAE (0.81 vs. 1.59) and affordance MAE (1.22 vs. 3.69). For part structure planning, PhysForge beats OmniPart (Yang et al., 2025) by +1.29 BBox IoU and +3.37 Voxel Recall, and PartField (Liu et al., 2025) by +5.62 BBox IoU and +7.51 Voxel Recall. On articulated object generation, PhysForge reduces Joint-Axis-Err-5 to 0.101, a 2.4× improvement over Singapo (Liu et al., 2024) at 0.241, a 6× improvement over Articulate Anything (Le et al., 2024) at 0.608, and a 7.7× improvement over URDFormer (Chen et al., 2024) at 0.781, while also achieving the best geometry fidelity (CD 10.21 vs. 21–25) and image consistency (CLIP-Sim 0.93 vs. 0.84–0.87). Notably, even without a 2D mask input, PhysForge's physics-guided planning surpasses OmniPart's SAM-mask-guided planning (BBox IoU 36.32 vs. 34.33), demonstrating that physical property co-prediction resolves part-granularity ambiguity.

## Relation to Prior Work

PhysForge sits at the intersection of three research threads: part-aware 3D generation (OmniPart, PartField), physics-annotated 3D asset synthesis (PhysX3D/PhysXGen), and articulated object generation (Articulate Anything, Singapo, URDFormer). It departs from prior part-aware methods by arguing that parts must be defined by functional and physical logic rather than purely geometric boundaries, and from prior physics-grounded methods by decoupling high-level physical planning (handled by a finetuned VLM) from precise kinematic parameter synthesis (handled by a diffusion model with the novel KineVoxel Injection mechanism). The architectural choice to use a VLM as a 'physical architect' that outputs hierarchical blueprints, followed by a diffusion head that realizes geometry and continuous kinematic values jointly, is the paper's core methodological contribution over the monolithic approaches in PhysX3D or template-based articulated generation pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 150k-asset PhysDB dataset with its four-tier annotation hierarchy (holistic, static, functional, interactive) fills a significant data gap for training physics-aware 3D generators, and the demonstrated deployment in RoboTwin simulation and game engines shows immediate practical utility for embodied AI and interactive content creation. A key limitation is that PhysDB does not annotate precise numerical joint axes at scale, relying instead on PartNet-Mobility and Infinite-Mobility to supply ground-truth kinematics for the diffusion stage, which constrains the diversity of articulated categories the model can learn from. Open questions include whether the VLM planning stage generalizes to truly novel object categories outside the seven covered by PhysDB, and whether the KineVoxel mechanism extends naturally to more complex multi-joint or deformable articulations.
