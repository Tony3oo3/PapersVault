## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models consistently fail to generate the precise number of objects specified in a prompt, even for simple cases like 'three birds.' The paper identifies the root cause as a Noise-Induced Layout Prior: the initial latent noise establishes a coarse spatial structure early in denoising that dominates object placement and count, effectively overriding the numerical constraints in the text prompt. Prior methods addressed counting errors through layout refinement or conditional guidance during denoising but overlooked this fundamental noise-driven constraint on layout formation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language prompt specifying one or more object categories with target counts (e.g., 'a photo of three apples and four peaches') and the initial latent noise z_T as input, and produces a 1024×1024 RGB image containing the specified number of objects per category. It is fully training-free and plugs into existing diffusion backbones (SDXL UNet, SD v3 DiT) without modifying model weights. For multi-category prompts, the method dynamically assigns spatial regions to object categories via a Hungarian-algorithm-based bipartite matching at each denoising step, and object detection for evaluation uses Grounding DINO.

## Method

The framework has two complementary components. First, a Count-Aware Noise Adjustment Strategy partitions the latent space into K mutually exclusive Voronoi regions (K = target count) by K-means clustering on a timestep-averaged cross-attention map, then reorganizes latent features within each region so that high-attention vectors are relocated toward the region center (Latent Representation Reorganization) and applies saliency-aware modulation to amplify object-relevant activations while suppressing background (Object Saliency Enhancement). Second, an Attention-Guided Layout Consistency Strategy performs test-time gradient optimization on the latent during the first few denoising steps, minimizing a composite loss of Attention Concentration Loss (penalizing attention spread away from region centroids and drift toward competing regions), Dispersion Regularization Loss (penalizing spatially over-extended attention), and Semantic Suppression Loss (for multi-category settings, penalizing cross-category attention leakage within each region). The approach requires no auxiliary models, no layout supervision, and no retraining.

## Results

On the COCOCount benchmark (single-category, counts 2–10), Ours (SD v3) achieves STA 0.8920, Acc 0.6225, MAE 0.6392, and F1 0.9491, outperforming the strongest baseline SD v3 (STA 0.8353, Acc 0.5058) and all count-control methods. On the more challenging MultiCount benchmark (multi-category, total counts 3–10), Ours (SD v3) reaches STA 0.7903 and Acc 0.3500, surpassing FLUX.1 (STA 0.7441, Acc 0.2292) which was the best baseline in this setting. Image quality (ImageReward) is preserved: Ours (SD v3) scores 1.2681 on COCOCount and 1.4945 on MultiCount, comparable to or exceeding most baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method dominates all 12 baselines on every counting metric (STA, Acc, MAE, Precision, Recall, F1) across all three benchmarks. Against the strongest base model SD v3, Ours (SD v3) improves STA by +0.057 (0.8353→0.8920) and Acc by +0.117 (0.5058→0.6225) on COCOCount, while reducing MAE from 0.9492 to 0.6392. Against the best dedicated count-control method CountGen, the gap is even larger: STA 0.8920 vs. 0.7678 and Acc 0.6225 vs. 0.4633 on COCOCount. On the multi-category MultiCount benchmark, Ours (SD v3) surpasses FLUX.1 (the best baseline there) by +0.046 STA and +0.121 Acc. The only metric where the method does not achieve the absolute best score is ImageReward: Playground v2.5 edges out Ours (SD v3) on COCOCount (1.3255 vs. 1.2681), and FLUX.1 leads on MultiCount (1.4826 vs. 1.4945 is actually a win for Ours), indicating that counting gains come at negligible or no image-quality cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in object-count control for T2I diffusion, which either decomposes generation into text-to-layout and layout-to-image stages (Attention Refocus, CountGen, CountLoop) or applies gradient-based guidance during denoising (Counting Guidance, IoCo). Its key conceptual departure is identifying the initial latent noise as the primary determinant of spatial layout (the 'Noise-Induced Layout Prior'), a factor that prior noise-sensitivity work (Crystal Ball, GoodSeed, InitNo) explored only for visual quality or text-image alignment, not for numerical object control. Methodologically, it repurposes classical geometric tools (K-means clustering, Voronoi diagrams) and attention manipulation into a unified training-free pipeline that intervenes at the earliest denoising stages, rather than relying on auxiliary layout models or learned counting networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By pinpointing initial noise as the root cause of counting failures, the paper reframes a problem previously addressed through mid-to-late denoising corrections, suggesting that effective count control must happen before layouts solidify. The training-free, plug-and-play nature (validated on both UNet-based SDXL and DiT-based SD v3) makes it broadly applicable, and the new MultiCount benchmark fills a gap in multi-category counting evaluation. However, the method's reliance on rigid Voronoi partitioning limits scalability to high-density scenes (N > 30) and complex spatial relationships (nested/overlapping objects), and the attention constraints can produce stylized or grid-like outputs, leaving open the question of how to enforce count fidelity without sacrificing the model's intrinsic generative priors.
