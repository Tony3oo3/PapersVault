## Problem

Online non-monotone DR-submodular maximization over down-closed convex sets has been stuck at O(T^{2/3}) static regret for single-query projection-free algorithms, because achieving the best-known 1/e approximation ratio was strictly bottlenecked by Frank-Wolfe mechanics that couple non-convexity to the update rule. No adaptive or dynamic regret guarantees existed for this setting, and existing methods either required O(T^{3/2}) queries per round to reach O(T^{1/2}) regret or settled for suboptimal rates with O(1) queries.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives a down-closed convex constraint set K ⊆ [0,1]^d (with bounded diameter D and access to a separation oracle), a sequence of adversarial M₁-Lipschitz non-monotone DR-submodular functions, and a stochastic gradient oracle returning unbiased estimates bounded by B₁. Each round, the base learner produces an internal point x_t ∈ K, the algorithm plays the reparameterized action x̂_t = 1 − e^{−x_t}, and queries the oracle at a single non-trivial point u = 1 − e^{−zx} (where z is sampled from a specific exponential distribution) to obtain one gradient estimate. The output is the played action x̂_t and a regret guarantee against the 1/e-approximate static, adaptive, or dynamic optimum.

## Method

The core contribution is proving that non-monotone DR-submodular functions over down-closed convex sets are 1/e-linearizable, via an exponential reparametrization h(x) = 1 − e^{−x} and a surrogate potential F(x) = ∫₀¹ [e^{z−1}/((1−e^{−1})z)] (f(1−e^{−zx}) − f(0)) dz. The key insight is that F acts as an integrating factor whose exponential weighting of early trajectory stages produces an exact cancellation of the non-monotone penalty during integration-by-parts, yielding the clean inequality (1/e)f(y) − f(h(x)) ≤ (1−e^{−1})⟨∇F(x), y−x⟩. To compute ∇F(x) with a single oracle query, the BQND estimator samples z from density p(z) = e^{z−1}/(1−e^{−1}), queries the gradient at u = 1−e^{−zx}, and returns the Jacobian-corrected vector g = v ⊙ e^{−zx}, which is unbiased and norm-bounded by B₁. This decouples the non-convex oracle interaction from constraint handling, so any efficient OLO base learner (SO-OGA for static/adaptive regret, Improved Ader for dynamic regret) can be plugged in, yielding O(T^{1/2}) static regret with O(1) queries and the first adaptive/dynamic regret guarantees for this problem class.

## Results

The paper achieves O(T^{1/2}) 1/e-approximation static regret with only O(1) gradient queries per round, improving upon the previous best of O(T^{2/3}) (Pedramfar et al., 2024a) and O(T^{4/5}) (Zhang et al., 2023) in the full-information gradient setting. It provides the first adaptive regret guarantee (O(T^{1/2})) and first dynamic regret guarantee (Õ(T^{1/2})) for non-monotone DR-submodular maximization over down-closed convex sets. Under limited feedback, the method achieves O(T^{2/3}) for semi-bandit, O(T^{3/4}) for zeroth-order full-information, and O(T^{4/5}) for bandit feedback, all strictly improving prior bounds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Pedramfar et al. (2024a, ICLR 2024), which achieves O(T^{2/3}) static regret with 1 gradient query, this paper improves to O(T^{1/2}) in the full-information setting and also provides the first adaptive and dynamic regret guarantees for this problem class. Against Zhang et al. (2023, AISTATS 2023), which requires T^{3/2} queries per round to achieve O(T^{1/2}) regret (or only O(T^{4/5}) with 1 query), this paper matches the O(T^{1/2}) rate with just 1 query per round. In the semi-bandit setting, the paper improves from O(T^{3/4}) (Pedramfar et al.) and O(T^{4/5}) (Zhang et al.) to O(T^{2/3}); in the bandit setting, it improves from O(T^{5/6}) (Pedramfar et al.) and O(T^{8/9}) (Zhang et al.) to O(T^{4/5}). Thang & Srivastav (2021, AAAI) achieves O(T^{3/4}) regret only with T^{3/4} queries per round, making it strictly dominated by this paper's O(T^{1/2}) with 1 query. Across all feedback models, the paper strictly improves the state of the art and is the first to provide adaptive and dynamic regret guarantees simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the linearizable optimization framework of Pedramfar & Aggarwal (2024b), which previously covered monotone DR-submodular and non-monotone DR-submodular over general convex sets (achieving only 1/4 approximation), to the harder non-monotone down-closed convex set regime where the best known ratio is 1/e. The structural proof builds on the DR-submodular inequalities of Buchbinder & Feldman (2024), specializing their Lemma 4.1 to the constant-path case to obtain the key lower bound needed for the online adversarial setting. The critical departure from prior work is that achieving 1/e previously required Frank-Wolfe or continuous greedy mechanics, which coupled non-convexity to the update rule and trapped single-query algorithms at O(T^{2/3}) regret; this paper proves that 1/e is achievable through a clean reduction to Online Linear Optimization with a single gradient query per round, via a novel exponential reparametrization and a Jacobian-corrected gradient estimator (BQND).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work breaks a long-standing Frank-Wolfe bottleneck in online non-monotone DR-submodular optimization, yielding the first O(T^{1/2}) static regret with O(1) queries and the first adaptive and dynamic regret guarantees for this problem class. The modular linearizable reduction means that any future improvement in OLO base learners (e.g., new projection-free methods) automatically transfers to this non-convex setting without re-derivation. A fundamental open question remains: the best offline approximation for down-closed sets is 0.401 (Buchbinder & Feldman, 2024), while the best online guarantee is 1/e ≈ 0.367, and it is unclear whether the adversarial revelation of the non-monotone penalty fundamentally prevents closing this gap. Tight lower bounds for the limited-feedback (semi-bandit, bandit) regimes explored here also remain open.
