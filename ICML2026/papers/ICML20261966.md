## Problem

The diffusion sampling literature has converged on the belief that increasing the discretization order of the reverse-time ODE solver is the primary lever for reducing neural function evaluations (NFEs). This paper challenges that view by identifying an under-explored, largely independent design axis: where along the reverse-time trajectory the pre-trained DPM is evaluated (forward-value vs. backward-value) can substantially affect sampling accuracy in the low-NFE regime, even at fixed first-order convergence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261966/cropped_page7_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method is entirely training-free: it consumes a fixed pre-trained noise prediction model ε_θ(x,t), a Gaussian noise initialization x_T, and a time grid of M steps, and outputs a single generated image x_0. Each iteration requires exactly one NFE (matching DDIM's cost), with the DPM evaluated at a lookahead estimate of the next state rather than the current state. Experiments cover pixel-space EDM/EDM2 models on CIFAR-10 (32×32), ImageNet (64×64, 512×512), and latent-space Stable Diffusion on FFHQ, LSUN Bedroom, and text-to-image generation.

## Method

The core insight is that a forward-value discretization of the diffusion ODE—evaluating the data prediction model μ_θ at the next time step (x_{t_i}, t_i) instead of the current one (x_{t_{i-1}}, t_{i-1}) as in DDIM—produces a leading O(1/M) discretization error with the opposite sign to DDIM's, so the two errors cancel to second order (Theorem 3). The practical sampler F-DPMSolver approximates this idealized forward-value update by first generating a cheap one-step lookahead estimate x̂_{t_i} (e.g., a single DDIM step from x_{t_{i-1}}), then evaluating μ_θ at (x̂_{t_i}, t_i), and combining that evaluation with the current iterate via a closed-form update to produce x_{t_i}. Theoretically, the sampler retains first-order convergence (Theorem 4) provided the lookahead error is o(1/M), which both F-DDIM and F-DPMSolver2 satisfy naturally. Empirically, this single change in evaluation placement yields FID improvements comparable to or exceeding those of second- and third-order solvers (DPMSolver-2/3, UniPC-3) at the same NFE budget, with the largest gains at 4–6 NFEs.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (EDM-uncond), F-DDIM achieves FID 25.01 at NFE=4, substantially outperforming DDIM (66.72), DPMSolver-2 (43.55), DPMSolver-3 (33.77), and UniPC-3 (106.07). At NFE=10, F-DDIM reaches FID 3.46, competitive with DPMSolver-3 (3.88) and UniPC-3 (2.73). On ImageNet-512 with EDM2-XXL, F-DDIM is the top performer across all tested NFEs (4–20), achieving FID 3.06 at NFE=10 and 2.11 at NFE=20, matching or beating all higher-order baselines. The advantage is most pronounced in the low-NFE regime (4–6 steps), where F-DDIM leads by 10–40 FID points over the best higher-order method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

F-DDIM (a first-order method) consistently and dramatically outperforms DDIM (also first-order) across all four datasets and all NFE budgets, with gaps of 20–60 FID points at low NFEs. Against the second-order DPMSolver-2, F-DDIM achieves lower FID at NFE=4–8 on every dataset (e.g., 25.01 vs. 43.55 on CIFAR-10 at NFE=4; 5.04 vs. 12.06 on ImageNet-512/XXL at NFE=8), and is competitive at NFE=10. Against the third-order DPMSolver-3 and UniPC-3, F-DDIM wins at NFE=4–6 on all datasets (e.g., 9.47 vs. 10.68/50.09 on CIFAR-10 at NFE=6) and is roughly tied or slightly behind at NFE=8–10 (e.g., 3.46 vs. 3.88/2.73 on CIFAR-10 at NFE=10). On ImageNet-512 with EDM2-XXL, F-DDIM is the single best method at every NFE from 4 to 20, including matching UniPC-3 at NFE=20 (2.11 vs. 2.11). The hybrid variant F-DPMSolver2 (using a second-order lookahead predictor) further narrows the gap at NFE=10, reaching FID 2.79 on CIFAR-10 uncond and 2.89 on ImageNet-512/XXL, approaching the best third-order results.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of accelerating diffusion sampling by increasing ODE solver order (DPMSolver-2/3, UniPC-3). Instead, it identifies an orthogonal design axis: the placement of DPM evaluations along the reverse-time trajectory. The method is built directly on DDIM's backward-value update (Song et al., 2020a) and the DPMSolver framework (Lu et al., 2022a,b), but flips the evaluation point from the current state to a cheap one-step lookahead estimate of the next state. Unlike UniPC's predictor-corrector design, which explicitly targets higher-order accuracy, F-DPMSolver remains first-order and exploits the fact that forward-value and backward-value discretizations produce leading errors of opposite sign (Theorem 3), making the forward-value choice systematically more accurate in the low-NFE regime.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result challenges the widespread assumption that solver order is the primary (or only) lever for diffusion sampling speed, revealing that evaluation placement is a complementary and largely independent design dimension. Practically, the method is training-free, requires only one DPM evaluation per step, and is a drop-in replacement that works with any pre-trained noise prediction model, making it immediately deployable. Open questions include extending the framework to SDE-based sampling, understanding behavior under classifier-free guidance (where guidance strength may amplify or alter the error structure), and applying the forward-value principle to diffusion-prior inverse problems. The paper also notes that a more rigorous characterization of *why* the forward-value scheme yields practical gains beyond what the first-order convergence rate predicts remains an open theoretical question.
