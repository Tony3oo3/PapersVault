## Problem

Aligning flow-matching text-to-image models with human preferences via reinforcement learning typically requires expensive parameter fine-tuning on curated reward data, producing rigid policies tightly coupled to specific reward models and configurations. This creates a fundamental bottleneck: the computational cost of fine-tuning becomes prohibitive for large-scale models, and the resulting policies cannot adapt to personalized or diverse downstream tasks without retraining. The paper asks whether RL-based alignment can be achieved entirely at inference time, navigating the latent manifold to locate high-reward regions without any parameter updates.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt as input and operates on a frozen flow-matching backbone (SD 3.5-M or Flux.1 Dev) at inference time, producing a single generated image. At each of the 40 denoising steps, it samples G=8 candidate latents via SDE rollouts, projects each to an approximate clean image using a single-step Tweedie-like formula, and evaluates a composite reward (0.7×HPS v2 + 0.3×CLIP Score) after VAE decoding. The entire optimization modifies only the intermediate latent vectors—no network weights, LoRA adapters, or additional training data are involved—and the process completes in roughly 2–5 minutes per image on a single GPU.

## Method

Flow-TTRL reinterprets each intermediate latent x_t as an implicit policy: perturbing x_t via a learnable offset δ_t modulates the effective vector field output (first-order Taylor expansion), making latent optimization mathematically equivalent to action rectification. To explore the action space, it replaces the deterministic ODE with an SDE formulation at each step, generating a group of G candidate trajectories whose reward variance provides dense supervision. A two-stage coarse-to-fine strategy governs the optimization: in the high-noise early steps, Proximal Reward Difference Prediction (PRDP) regresses on pairwise reward differences with a clipped PPO-style objective to ensure structural stability; once the global structure stabilizes, Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) maximizes group-normalized advantages to refine fine-grained aesthetic details. A parallel reference trajectory (vanilla ODE from the same initial noise) serves as a KL-regularization anchor, and a dynamic noise reweighting schedule (σ_t√Δt) modulates update magnitude across denoising stages to balance exploration and stability.

## Results

On GenEval, Flow-TTRL elevates SD 3.5-Medium from 63% to 87% overall accuracy and Flux.1 Dev from 66% to 83%, surpassing all test-time optimization baselines (DAS: 83%/80%, TITAN-Guide: 74%/76%). On T2I-CompBench, Flow-TTRL achieves a 15–20% average gain over native backbones, with SD 3.5-M+Flow-TTRL reaching 0.9042 on Color attribute binding and 0.7361 on Shape—both best among all compared methods. On human preference metrics (Pick-a-Pic), Flux.1 Dev+Flow-TTRL scores 22.67 PickScore, 0.322 HPS v2, and 1.340 ImageReward, all exceeding the base model and the 5-minute Best-of-N baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Flow-TTRL (training-free) outperforms both test-time optimization baselines—DAS (0.83/0.80) and TITAN-Guide (0.74/0.76)—on GenEval overall for SD 3.5-M (0.87) and Flux.1 Dev (0.83), respectively. On T2I-CompBench, SD 3.5-M+Flow-TTRL achieves the best Color (0.9042), Shape (0.7361), and Texture (0.8261) scores, beating DAS (0.8561/0.6482/0.7717) and TITAN-Guide (0.8468/0.6235/0.7689), though it trails Flow-GRPO on 2D-Spatial (0.4414 vs. 0.5447). Against the RL fine-tuning baselines, Flow-TTRL falls short on GenEval overall (0.87 vs. 0.95 for Flow-GRPO, 0.97 for TempFlow-GRPO) but matches or exceeds them on T2I-CompBench attribute-binding metrics without any parameter updates. On human preference alignment (Pick-a-Pic), Flux.1 Dev+Flow-TTRL (22.67 PickScore, 0.322 HPS v2, 1.340 ImageReward) surpasses both the base model and the 5-minute Best-of-N baseline (22.51/0.314/1.081), confirming that targeted latent optimization is more sample-efficient than brute-force re-sampling. The paper positions Flow-TTRL as a plug-and-play alternative that achieves ~80–90% of the GenEval gains of full RL fine-tuning at a fraction of the compute cost (~2.2 min inference vs. >1k GPU-hours training).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of flow-matching generative models and RL-based alignment, departing from the dominant paradigm of fine-tuning model parameters (as in Flow-GRPO, DPOK, DPO-based methods) by instead treating intermediate latent representations as an implicit policy that can be optimized at inference time. It draws on PRDP (Deng et al., 2024) for its warm-up stage and GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) for refinement, but repurposes both as test-time latent optimization objectives rather than training losses. Compared to prior test-time optimization methods (DAS, TITAN-Guide, InitNO), Flow-TTRL's key distinction is formulating the denoising process as an MDP with SDE-based rollouts, enabling compatibility with non-differentiable reward models and providing KL-regularized alignment that resists reward hacking.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: Flow-TTRL enables plug-and-play preference alignment on any frozen flow-matching backbone without training data, LoRA adapters, or multi-GPU training runs, making alignment accessible on consumer hardware (24GB VRAM with quantization). However, the method incurs a meaningful inference overhead (~2–5 minutes per image vs. seconds for standard sampling), and it still requires a reward model, so it is not a fully autonomous solution. Open questions include whether the latent-as-policy formulation extends to video generation or multi-objective alignment, how it behaves under adversarially designed reward models, and whether the two-stage PRDP→GRPO schedule is optimal or whether adaptive switching could further improve results.
