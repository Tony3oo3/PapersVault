## Problem

Vision-language models universally rely on fixed 2D patch grids from vision encoders, yet analysis shows that VLM fine-tuning discards spatial coherence in favor of abstract, globally summarizing representations. The paper asks whether the summarization capability—rather than raw 2D spatial alignment—is the critical factor for VLM performance, and whether images can be compressed into compact, variable-length 1D sequences without sacrificing multimodal accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image of size H×W, patchified with patch size P into a 2D grid of H/P × W/P tokens, and produces a variable-length 1D token sequence of length ℓ. During training, ℓ is sampled stochastically from a triangular distribution (concentrating probability on shorter sequences); at inference, the user specifies ℓ directly, enabling a continuous accuracy-latency tradeoff from 1 to 256 tokens. A training-only decoder reconstructs a 2D-compatible patch grid from the 1D tokens via cross-attention, serving solely for teacher alignment losses.

## Method

RADIO1D employs a pure 1D Vision Transformer encoder that processes flattened image patches and outputs an elastic 1D token sequence, with Patch Merging blocks integrated mid-backbone to reduce sequence length by 4× while expanding channel dimension. Training uses multi-teacher knowledge distillation from SigLIP2-g, DINOv3-7B, and SAM3, where lightweight per-teacher MLP adaptors project student features into each teacher's space for similarity losses. A lightweight ViT decoder (active only during training) receives duplicated learnable query tokens plus the 1D encoder output as register tokens, using cross-attention to rearrange unstructured 1D information into a 2D patch grid for teacher alignment. Elastic sequence generation via stochastic slicing (nested dropout) samples a length ℓ per iteration and retains only the first ℓ tokens, inducing hierarchical ordering where early tokens encode global semantics and later tokens capture finer details. The triangular sampling distribution (PDF p(x)=2−2x) concentrates training on shorter sequences, yielding superior low-token performance while maintaining quality at higher counts.

## Results

On 10 multimodal VLM benchmarks (paired with a 9B Nemotron LLM), RADIO1D at 256 tokens achieves 73.29 average accuracy, outperforming C-RADIOv4-H (73.09), SigLIP2-g (72.81), and SigLIP2-SO400m (72.67), while also reducing Time-To-First-Token from 440–518 ms to 453 ms. The key advantage is the elastic token spectrum: at 128 tokens RADIO1D retains 71.64 average accuracy with 373 ms TTFT, and at 64 tokens still reaches 70.07 with 346 ms TTFT—offering a continuous accuracy-latency tradeoff that fixed-output baselines cannot match. On composition-based retrieval (Comp@1), RADIO1D-H scores 0.5158 on MS-COCO and 0.4294 on nuImages, surpassing all baselines (best prior: C-RADIOv4-H at 0.5050/0.4046).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three fixed-256-token VLM baselines, RADIO1D at 256 tokens wins on average accuracy (73.29 vs. 73.09 for C-RADIOv4-H, 72.81 for SigLIP2-g, 72.67 for SigLIP2-SO400m) while matching or beating them on most individual benchmarks (e.g., DocVQA 93.2 vs. 93.3, OCRBench 84.4 vs. 84.3). More critically, RADIO1D at 128 tokens (71.64 avg, 373 ms TTFT) still outperforms C-RADIOv4+ToMe at 192 tokens (71.49 avg) and C-RADIOv4+CDPruner at 192 tokens (72.23 avg) on average, and decisively beats both post-hoc pruning methods at 128 tokens (71.64 vs. 70.31 for ToMe, 69.05 for CDPruner), with the gap widening sharply on OCR-heavy tasks (DocVQA: 92.5 vs. 90.8/89.2). On composition-based retrieval, RADIO1D-H leads all baselines by 1.1–3.5 points in Comp@1 on both MS-COCO and nuImages, indicating its single-token embedding captures scene layout more faithfully than any 2D-grid encoder. The main weakness is at extreme compression: below 128 tokens, OCR-centric benchmarks (DocVQA, InfoVQA, OCRBench) degrade sharply, whereas holistic-reasoning tasks (MMMU, LongVideoBench) remain robust even at 1 token.

## Relation to Prior Work

RADIO1D extends the agglomerative multi-teacher distillation framework of C-RADIOv4/AM-RADIO by replacing the fixed 2D patch grid with a variable-length 1D elastic sequence, drawing on FlexTok's nested dropout mechanism for hierarchical token ordering. Unlike TiTok and FlexTok, which optimize for image reconstruction or generative fidelity via quantization, RADIO1D operates in continuous embedding space and is trained purely for semantic understanding via distillation from diverse foundation models (SigLIP2, DINOv3, SAM3). The paper's conceptual departure from prior VLM work is the argument that summarization capability, not spatial coherence or direct image-text alignment, is the critical property VLMs extract from vision encoders—a claim supported by CKA analysis showing fine-tuning destroys spatial structure while preserving global summarization tokens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work challenges a widely held assumption in the VLM community that 2D spatially-organized patch features are necessary for effective vision-language integration, with practical implications for model deployment (flexible token budgets, lower TTFT, permissive license from NVIDIA). The composition-based retrieval benchmark is a useful new evaluation axis, though its scope is currently limited to two datasets. Key open questions include whether content-aware dynamic token selection can further improve the accuracy-efficiency frontier, whether the 1D paradigm extends naturally to video, and why OCR/dense-reading tasks still require the full 256-token budget—suggesting the summarization story has limits for fine-grained spatial tasks.
