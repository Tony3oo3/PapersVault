## Problem

Standard video world models for robot manipulation predict future RGB pixels, forcing the model to allocate capacity to nuisance factors like texture, illumination, and dynamic backgrounds that are weakly related to action selection. This photometric objective misaligns with control: appearance-driven errors compound over long horizons, causing predictive drift and brittle policies under modest visual distribution shifts. The paper asks whether predicting decision-relevant semantic structure (object identity, spatial layout, contact geometry) instead of pixels yields more robust and generalizable manipulation policies.

## Input / Output

At training time, the model consumes multi-view RGB frames (third-person + wrist, 256×256) paired with offline semantic masks (generated by RoboEngine) and a language instruction; at deployment it requires only raw multi-view RGB with no external segmenter. The VAE compresses each frame into an 8×8 latent grid (D=128), and a 4-frame memory window conditions prediction of 5 future latent frames. The policy outputs a 36-step action chunk (15-dim: 7-DoF end-effector pose, 1-DoF gripper, 7-DoF proprioceptive state) executed via receding-horizon control with 10-step Euler diffusion sampling.

## Method

MWM replaces RGB pixel prediction with future semantic mask prediction using a 28-layer DiT backbone (2048 hidden, 32 heads) trained via conditional flow matching, where discrete masks are rendered into RGB-compatible images via a fixed color palette and encoded by the same pretrained video VAE as the RGB context, creating a geometric information bottleneck. The backbone uses 3D RoPE with compression-aware scaling, AdaIN-style timestep modulation, and cross-view attention every third block to handle multi-view inputs. Training is two-stage: Stage 1 pretrains the backbone on mask dynamics (flow matching loss with caption dropout and noise injection), and Stage 2 attaches a 28-layer diffusion policy head (512 hidden, 16 heads) that attends to per-layer backbone features via cross-attention and is trained end-to-end with only the action denoising loss, so gradients reshape the backbone's predictive features to be maximally control-aligned. At inference, the model runs purely on raw RGB, sampling a 36-step action chunk in 10 diffusion steps and executing the first action before replanning.

## Results

On LIBERO, MWM achieves a 98.3% average success rate across four suites (Spatial 98.8%, Object 100%, Goal 98.2%, LIBERO-10 96.0%), outperforming the strongest RGB-centric baseline GE-ACT (96.5%) and π0 (94.2%). On RLBench, MWM reaches 68.3% average success rate, substantially exceeding FiS-VLA (50.0%), CogACT (42.5%), and GE-ACT (30.8%). On real-world Franka robot tasks, MWM achieves 67.5% average success versus 38.8% for π0 and 23.8% for GE-ACT, with the largest gains on contact-sensitive tasks like drawer manipulation and pouring.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MWM outperforms all four compared baselines on both simulation benchmarks. Against OpenVLA (Kim et al., 2024), MWM leads by 21.8 points on LIBERO (98.3% vs. 76.5%) and 45.0 points on RLBench (68.3% vs. 23.3%), with the largest gap on long-horizon LIBERO-10 (96.0% vs. 53.7%). Against CogACT (Li et al., 2024), MWM gains 4.7 points on LIBERO and 25.8 points on RLBench. Against π0 (Black et al., 2026), MWM improves by 4.1 points on LIBERO and 35.0 points on RLBench, with particularly large margins on RLBench tasks requiring precise object interaction (e.g., Wine at Rack: 90% vs. 10%). Against Cosmos (NVIDIA et al., 2025) world-model baselines, MWM-C1 (mask + IDM) surpasses Cosmos w/ IDM by 13.5 points on LIBERO average, and MWM-C2 (mask latent features + diffusion policy) surpasses Cosmos w/ Latent IDM by 0.1 points on average but with a 6.8-point gain on LIBERO-10, indicating reduced rollout drift over longer horizons.

## Relation to Prior Work

MWM sits at the intersection of video world models for robotics (Cosmos, GE-ACT, LADi-WM) and diffusion-based action policies (Diffusion Policy, π0), but departs from both by shifting the world model's prediction target from RGB pixels/latents to future semantic masks. The flow-matching objective and DiT backbone follow the standard video-diffusion recipe, while the action head directly adopts the Diffusion Policy paradigm; the key architectural departure is the geometric information bottleneck imposed by mask prediction, which echoes earlier masked world models (Seo et al., 2023) but is realized here within a modern diffusion-transformer stack with 3D RoPE and AdaIN timestep conditioning. Unlike VLA methods that inject semantics as input cues on the current observation, MWM learns predictive mask dynamics offline and consumes only the resulting latent features at inference, requiring no external segmenter at deployment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a clean, well-motivated argument that photometric prediction is misaligned with control utility, and the consistent gains across LIBERO, RLBench, and real-robot visual-shift evaluations provide credible evidence that a semantic bottleneck improves robustness to nuisance appearance variation. Open questions include whether the approach extends to tasks where fine-grained texture or material properties are genuinely decision-relevant, how it scales to larger and more diverse manipulation suites beyond the 130 LIBERO / 100 RLBench tasks, and whether the 50-demonstration real-world setting is sufficient to validate the method's data-efficiency claims at scale. The modest token-pruning robustness gap (0.648 vs 0.629) also suggests the bottleneck's benefits may be more pronounced under appearance shifts than under partial observability.
