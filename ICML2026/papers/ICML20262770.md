## Problem

Standard GANs trained from scratch with transformer architectures diverge because the adversarial objective alone does not constrain the transport map, leaving the generator free to drift among infinitely many valid mappings. Meanwhile, consistency-based one-step/few-step generators must train across all timesteps to propagate consistency, wasting model capacity and accumulating discretization error. The paper asks whether adversarial training can be stabilized by imposing a deterministic transport structure while retaining the distribution-matching quality of GANs.

## Input / Output

The generator takes a Gaussian noise vector z ∈ R^n of the same dimensionality as the data (a 32×32×4 VAE latent) plus a class label c, and produces a single 256×256 image in one forward pass (1 NFE) or in a small number of steps. The same-dimensionality constraint on z is inherited from flow-based models and is required for the Brenier optimal transport formulation. For multi-step generation, the generator additionally receives source and target timesteps (s, t) and operates on interpolated states x_s = (1−s)x + sz.

## Method

The core contribution is adding a quadratic optimal-transport regularization loss L_ot = E_z[‖G(z)−z‖²/n] to the standard relativistic GAN objective, which biases the generator toward the unique Brenier optimal transport map and prevents the drift that destabilizes vanilla GAN training. The discriminator uses a relativistic objective with finite-difference R1/R2 gradient penalties and logit-centering, while a gradient-normalization operator φ rescales the adversarial gradient to unit scale so that the OT weight λ_ot transfers across model sizes. The generator and discriminator both use a standard DiT architecture with only minimal modifications (a [CLS] token for D, timestep projections for multi-step). For conditional generation, a flow-based classifier guidance loss interpolates generated samples to random timesteps before scoring, recovering the accumulated guidance gradient of CFG even in the single-step setting. The framework extends to extra-deep models (56- and 112-layer) via transformer block repetition, trained end-to-end with a single-step objective and no intermediate supervision.

## Results

On ImageNet-256px with 1NFE and classifier guidance, AFM-XL/2 achieves a new best FID of 2.38, outperforming the best consistency-based method AlphaFlow-XL/2 (FID 2.81) by 0.43. The B/2 model (FID 3.05) surpasses several XL/2 consistency-based models, demonstrating that preserving model capacity (not wasting it on intermediate timesteps) is critical. In the extra-deep setting, 56-layer and 112-layer 1NFE models achieve FIDs of 2.08 and 1.94 respectively, surpassing their 28-layer 2NFE (2.11) and 4NFE (2.02) counterparts with equal parameters and compute.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against consistency-based methods, AFM-XL/2 (FID 2.38) beats AlphaFlow-XL/2 (2.81), TiM-XL/2 (3.26), MeanFlow-XL/2 (3.43), and Shortcut-XL/2 (10.60) by 0.43–8.22 FID points at 1NFE. Notably, AFM-B/2 (3.05) outperforms MeanFlow-XL/2 (3.43) and TiM-XL/2 (3.26), showing that the smaller adversarial flow model preserves more effective capacity than larger consistency models. Among GANs, AFM-XL/2 (2.38) beats GigaGAN (3.45) and GAT-XL/2+REPA (2.96), while StyleGAN-XL (2.30) remains slightly ahead but operates in pixel space with a different architecture. In the few-step setting, AFM-XL/2 2NFE (2.11) edges out AlphaFlow-XL/2 2NFE (2.16) and MeanFlow-XL/2 2NFE (2.20). Without any guidance, AFM-XL/2 1NFE (3.98) surpasses SiT-XL/2+REPA at 250NFE (5.90) and DiT-XL/2 at 250NFE (9.62), demonstrating that the adversarial objective produces perceptually sharper samples than flow matching's L2 criterion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate generative model families—adversarial training (GANs) and flow matching—by training a generator with an adversarial objective while constraining it to learn a deterministic optimal transport map, grounded in Brenier's theorem. It departs from standard GANs, which learn arbitrary transport maps and suffer from generator drift, and from consistency-based methods, which must train across all timesteps to propagate consistency and thus waste model capacity. Unlike recent transformer GANs (R3GAN, GAT) that rely on non-standard architectures or frozen feature networks, this work demonstrates that a standard DiT architecture suffices when the transport map is properly regularized, and it introduces a gradient-normalization technique to stabilize the adversarial-OT loss balance across model scales.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that single-step generation quality is bounded more by generator depth than by the number of inference steps, as a 112-layer 1NFE model (FID 1.94) outperforms its 28-layer 4NFE counterpart (FID 2.02) with identical parameters and compute. This opens a promising depth-scaling direction for few-step generation that is orthogonal to the multi-step paradigm. Key limitations include the 1.88× compute overhead from the discriminator's gradient penalties and the continued reliance on adversarial training tricks (EMA reload, D reload, DA); extending the framework to continuous-time flow modeling and post-training of existing flow-matching models are identified as important future directions.
