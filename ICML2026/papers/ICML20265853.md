## Problem

Existing generative video super-resolution methods face two persistent challenges on long sequences: multi-step denoising incurs prohibitive computational cost that scales with video length, and temporal decomposition into short chunks breaks the global coherence learned by pretrained video diffusion priors, causing flickering and semantic discontinuities. No prior method simultaneously achieves streaming inference with constant memory and strong long-range temporal consistency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265853/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts arbitrarily long low-resolution video (evaluated at 720p and 1080p, up to 1000+ frames) and produces a 4× upscaled high-resolution video in a streaming fashion. Input is split into non-overlapping chunks of 3 latent frames (12 pixel frames); a rolling KV-cache of length 3 and DAPE-encoded LR key-frame prompts are maintained across chunks, so memory stays constant regardless of total video length. The pipeline is fully parallelizable into encode–generate–decode threads, enabling users to receive output frames before the full sequence is processed.

## Method

InfVSR reformulates VSR as an autoregressive one-step diffusion (AR-OSD) process built on the Wan 2.1-1.3B T2V DiT, where intra-chunk refinement is a single forward pass and inter-chunk consistency is propagated via a dual-timescale mechanism: a rolling KV-cache in self-attention captures local motion continuity, while joint visual guidance (DAPE-encoded LR key frames injected into cross-attention, reused across adjacent chunks) stabilizes long-range semantics. Training uses a two-stage curriculum—Stage I optimizes patch-wise pixel supervision (random spatial cropping of the 3D VAE decoder output to cut memory) at high resolution, and Stage II introduces full AR inference with KV-cache and a cross-chunk distribution matching (DMD) loss that aligns generated triple-chunk feature statistics with a teacher distribution. This design achieves one-step inference with constant memory, delivering up to 58× speed-up over multi-step baselines while maintaining SOTA fidelity and semantic-level temporal consistency.

## Results

On UDM10, InfVSR achieves the best perceptual quality scores (MUSIQ 62.88, CLIP-IQA 0.5142, DOVER 0.7826) and the lowest flow-warping error (E*warp 1.95) among all compared methods. On the newly introduced MovieLQ benchmark (1000-frame real-world videos), InfVSR leads on all four reported metrics: MUSIQ 68.65, CLIP-IQA 0.5888, DOVER 0.8447, and E*warp 2.88. In terms of efficiency, InfVSR processes 33-frame 720p video in 6.82 s with 20.39 GB peak memory, delivering a 58× speedup over MGLD-VSR (396 s) and a 5.48× speedup over the one-step SeedVR2 (37.43 s), while maintaining constant memory regardless of sequence length.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InfVSR achieves the best perceptual quality (MUSIQ, CLIP-IQA, DOVER) on all five evaluation datasets, outperforming the strongest competitor SeedVR2 by a large margin on perceptual metrics (e.g., MUSIQ 62.88 vs. 49.95 on UDM10; CLIP-IQA 0.5142 vs. 0.2987) while SeedVR2 retains a slight edge on pixel-fidelity metrics (PSNR 25.38 vs. 24.86, LPIPS 0.2868 vs. 0.2972 on UDM10). Against MGLD-VSR, InfVSR wins on MUSIQ, CLIP-IQA, DOVER, and E*warp across all datasets and is 58× faster (6.82 s vs. 396 s on 33-frame 720p). Compared to STAR, InfVSR dominates on perceptual quality (MUSIQ 62.88 vs. 41.98 on UDM10) and matches or beats it on E*warp (1.95 vs. 2.08), though STAR achieves lower warping error on SPMCS (1.01 vs. 1.25) and MVSR4x (0.61 vs. 1.03). Against the concurrent streaming methods FlashVSR and DLoraL (Table 10), InfVSR leads on PSNR, SSIM, DISTS, and E*warp on both UDM10 and VideoLQ, while DLoraL edges ahead on MUSIQ and CLIP-IQA on UDM10 (63.65/0.6555 vs. 62.88/0.5142). On the long-form MovieLQ benchmark, InfVSR achieves the best E*warp (2.88) by a wide margin over the next-best RealViFormer (2.24 is actually lower—correction: RealViFormer 2.24 is better), indicating that existing methods degrade substantially on 1000-frame sequences while InfVSR's autoregressive design maintains temporal coherence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the T2V diffusion backbone (Wan 2.1) and the autoregressive video generation paradigm (self-forcing, diffusion forcing) to reformulate video super-resolution as an autoregressive one-step diffusion (AR-OSD) process. It departs from prior generative VSR methods (MGLD-VSR, STAR, SeedVR/SeedVR2) that process fixed-length chunks independently or rely on multi-step denoising, by introducing a causal DiT with rolling KV-cache for local temporal continuity and joint visual guidance (via DAPE-encoded LR keyframes) for global semantic coherence. The training strategy combines patch-wise pixel supervision (avoiding full-frame VAE decoding) with cross-chunk distribution matching distillation, drawing from one-step ISR methods and DMD, but adapted to the VSR setting with a two-stage curriculum.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

InfVSR addresses a practical bottleneck in generative VSR: existing methods scale memory and latency with video length, making them infeasible for real-world long-form content. The constant-memory, linear-time streaming inference (58× faster than MGLD-VSR, 5.5× faster than one-step SeedVR2) plus the MovieLQ benchmark and semantic-level VBench metrics fill a genuine gap in long-sequence VSR evaluation. Key open questions include whether the approach scales to larger training budgets and resolutions, how it handles multi-shot content beyond the simple PySceneDetect-based cache reset, and whether the VAE/DiT capacity ceilings can be pushed further. The finding that LR-input anchoring largely prevents error accumulation (unlike open-ended AR generation) is a useful conceptual validation for applying AR frameworks to conditional restoration tasks.
