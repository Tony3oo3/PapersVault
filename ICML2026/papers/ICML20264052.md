## Problem

In-Context Learning (ICL) video editing concatenates context and source tokens, effectively doubling sequence length and quadrupling the already quadratic attention cost. Existing sparse attention mechanisms are designed for general video generation and do not exploit the structural asymmetry between context and source tokens, leaving a critical gap in efficient ICL-specific attention.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264052/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a source video, a context (reference) video showing the desired edit, and a text editing instruction as input, and produces an edited video as output. Source and context tokens are concatenated into a single sequence (typically ~64K tokens at 544×960 resolution on Wan-2.2) and processed jointly via attention. A decoupled RoPE strategy resets positional indices independently for source and context tokens to handle their variable length discrepancy.

## Method

ISA (In-context Sparse Attention) exploits two key findings: context tokens contribute negligibly to attention scores compared to source tokens, and query sharpness (block-wise variance of the coarse attention distribution) upper-bounds the 0-th order Taylor approximation error (Theorem 3.1). ISA first prunes redundant context tokens via a pooling-attention-based Top-K pre-selection, then partitions queries by sharpness: high-sharpness queries are routed to standard FlashAttention for exact computation, while low-sharpness queries use a block-wise 0-th order Taylor sparse kernel that replaces per-token K/V with block means, reducing complexity from O(N²D) to O(N²D/b). LIVEditor-14B is built by post-training the high-noise branch of Wan 2.2 with ISA in a two-stage paradigm (1.7M mixed-quality samples, then 0.089M curated high-quality samples), achieving ~60% attention-module latency reduction and a 1.47× end-to-end speedup over FlashAttention-3 while surpassing full-attention quality on EditVerseBench, VIE-Bench, IVE-Bench, and FiVE-Bench.

## Results

On EditVerseBench, LIVEditor-14B (ISA) achieves the best scores across all VLM evaluation metrics: Quality 7.89, Text Alignment 20.09, Temporal Consistency 27.19, and Editing Quality 24.55, surpassing the previous best (EditVerse) by margins of 0.24, 0.02, 0.46, and 0.62 respectively. It also attains the highest Pick Scores of 99.32 (Frame) and 99.22 (Video), exceeding the prior best by 0.76 and 0.78. In the training-free sparse attention comparison, ISA achieves a 1.47× speedup over FlashAttention-3 while simultaneously outperforming full attention on all VLM metrics. On IVE-Bench, LIVEditor-14B (ISA) reaches a Total score of 0.67 with the best Instruction Compliance (0.47) and Video Quality (0.79), and on VIE-Bench it leads across all five editing tasks (Add 8.84, Swap 8.14, Remove 6.81, Style 8.16, Hybrid 8.12).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LIVEditor-14B (ISA) decisively outperforms all instruction-guided video editing baselines on EditVerseBench: EditVerse (7.65/20.07/26.73/23.93) is the closest competitor but trails by 0.24 in Quality and 0.62 in Editing Quality; Lucy Edit (6.70/23.28) and InsV2V (5.95/22.77) lag further behind. Training-free methods (TokenFlow, STDF) and first-frame propagation (Señorita-2M) are substantially weaker, with STDF scoring only 5.70 in Quality. In the sparse attention comparison (Table 2), ISA (1.47× speedup, Quality 7.78) dominates all alternatives: STA achieves 2.09× speedup but collapses to 4.45 Quality, VSA reaches only 3.60, and even the strongest sparse baselines (Sparge 7.44, Radial 7.09) fall short of ISA. On IVE-Bench, LIVEditor-14B (ISA) ties Ditto and InsV2V on Total (0.67) but leads in Instruction Compliance (0.47 vs. 0.49/0.39) and Video Quality (0.79 vs. 0.78/0.80); it clearly surpasses VACE (0.63), ICVE (0.60), Omni-Video (0.59), AnyV2V (0.58), and StableV2V (0.51). On VIE-Bench, ISA leads in Add (8.84 vs. Omni-Video 6.24), Style (8.16 vs. InsV2V 7.45), and Hybrid (8.12 vs. Omni-Video 5.43), while in Swap it edges out Pika (8.14 vs. 7.41) and in Remove it surpasses MiniMax (6.81 vs. 6.84 on Avg, but leads in Preservation 8.57 vs. 7.52).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Wan 2.2 video generation foundation model (post-training on its high-noise branch) and adapts the pooling-attention mechanism from VSA (Zhang et al., 2025a) for context token pre-selection. It departs from existing sparse attention methods (STA, SWA, Radial, Sparge, VSA) that were designed for general video generation and treat all tokens uniformly, by exploiting the structural asymmetry between source and context tokens inherent to In-Context Learning (ICL) video editing. The key conceptual departure is the theoretical link between query sharpness and 0-th order Taylor approximation error (Theorem 3.1), which provides a principled, hardware-efficient criterion for dynamically routing queries to full vs. sparse attention—something prior sparse attention work lacks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and growing bottleneck: ICL video editing doubles sequence length (context + source tokens), making quadratic attention the dominant cost. ISA's ~60% attention latency reduction with near-lossless (or even slightly improved) quality is practically significant for deploying long-sequence video editing at scale. The counterintuitive finding that sparse attention outperforms full attention after training—attributed to noise-token pruning acting as implicit regularization—challenges the assumption that approximation must degrade fidelity, though the margins are modest and demonstrated on a single model family. Open questions include whether ISA generalizes to other ICL tasks (e.g., image editing, multi-turn video generation), whether the sharpness-based routing transfers across architectures, and whether the 1.7M synthetic dataset's quality ceiling limits downstream performance.
