## Problem

Video generation models have advanced rapidly in visual quality, but their reasoning capabilities remain largely unexplored due to the absence of large-scale, diverse training data and verifiable evaluation frameworks. Existing video reasoning benchmarks are tiny (collectively ~12.8K samples), mostly lack training splits, and rely on stochastic VLM-as-a-judge scoring, making systematic scaling studies and reproducible diagnosis of reasoning capabilities infeasible.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264817/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each task instance is a 4-tuple: a first-frame image (first_frame.png), a text prompt describing the reasoning task (prompt.txt), a final-frame image (final_frame.png), and a ground-truth video (ground_truth.mp4). The model receives the first frame and prompt as input and must generate a video whose trajectory satisfies the task's logical, spatial, and temporal constraints. Evaluation is fully rule-based: each of the 100 test tasks has a dedicated scorer that checks deterministic criteria (spatial accuracy, trajectory correctness, temporal consistency, logical validity) against the ground truth, producing weighted sub-scores rather than a single VLM judgment.

## Method

VBVR introduces a 150-task dataset organized by a five-dimension cognitive taxonomy (perception, transformation, spatiality, abstraction, knowledge) grounded in developmental psychology, with all instances procedurally generated by parameterized generators that emit deterministic, verifiable 4-tuples at scale (1M training + 7.5K test samples). Evaluation uses a dual-split design: 50 in-domain tasks (seen task types, unseen parameter configurations) and 50 out-of-domain tasks (entirely novel task structures), each scored by task-specific rule-based evaluators validated against human preferences (Spearman ρ > 0.9). For the scaling study, the authors apply LoRA fine-tuning (rank 32 on q, k, v, o, ffn.0, ffn.2 modules of the DiT backbone, learning rate 1e-4, one epoch) to Wan2.2-I2V-A14B, progressively increasing training data from 0K to 500K samples. This yields VBVR-Wan2.2 (overall 0.685, +84.6% over base), revealing saturating in-domain gains, a persistent ~15% ID-OOD generalization gap, and emergent controllability and multi-step planning behaviors on held-out task families.

## Results

On VBVR-Bench (100 tasks, 500 test cases), VBVR-Wan2.2 achieves an overall score of 0.685, representing an 84.6% relative improvement over its Wan2.2-I2V-A14B base (0.371) and surpassing the strongest proprietary model, Sora 2 (0.546), by 0.139. In-domain performance reaches 0.760 while out-of-domain reaches 0.610, revealing a persistent ~15% generalization gap. Human performance is 0.974, indicating a substantial remaining gap. On VBench-I2V, VBVR-Wan2.2 maintains the base model's generative quality (total score 0.8835 vs. 0.8816) while improving camera motion consistency from 0.5444 to 0.6592.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VBVR-Wan2.2 (0.685) outperforms all eight baselines on VBVR-Bench overall. Among open-source models, it surpasses Wan2.2-I2V-A14B (0.371) by 0.314, LTX-2 (0.313) by 0.372, and CogVideoX1.5/HunyuanVideo (both 0.273) by 0.412. Among proprietary models, it beats Sora 2 (0.546) by 0.139, Veo 3.1 (0.480) by 0.205, Runway Gen-4 Turbo (0.403) by 0.282, and Kling 2.6 (0.369) by 0.316. The secondary fine-tune, VBVR-LTX2.3 (0.516), also surpasses Runway Gen-4 Turbo, Kling 2.6, and Veo 3.1, confirming that VBVR data benefits multiple backbone architectures. Notably, even the best model (VBVR-Wan2.2) remains 0.289 below human performance (0.974), and the OOD score (0.610) lags ID (0.760) by 15 percentage points, indicating that data scaling alone does not close the generalization gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 'generation-as-reasoning' line initiated by Wiedemer et al. (2025) and subsequent benchmarks (V-ReasonBench, VideoThinkBench, MME-CoF, Gen-ViRe, Ruler-Bench), which demonstrated that video models can perform zero-shot perceptual and manipulation tasks but remained evaluation-heavy with no training splits. VBVR departs from this ecosystem by (1) grounding task design in a cognitive-psychology taxonomy (Spelke, Carey, Anderson) rather than ad-hoc task collections, (2) replacing VLM-as-a-judge evaluation with deterministic rule-based scorers for reproducibility, and (3) introducing the first large-scale training dataset (2M images, 1M videos) enabling systematic scaling studies. The procedural-generation approach borrows design logic from ARC and CLEVR to isolate reasoning from perceptual confounds, while the LoRA-on-DiT fine-tuning protocol follows standard transfer-learning practice for diffusion video models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VBVR provides the first infrastructure for studying video reasoning as a scalable, trainable capability rather than a zero-shot curiosity, and its rule-based evaluation toolkit addresses a real reproducibility gap in the field. The key open question it surfaces is architectural: performance saturates around 200K–400K samples with a persistent ~15% ID-OOD gap and a large residual gap to human performance (0.685 vs. 0.974), suggesting that current diffusion-based video generation architectures have fundamental representational bottlenecks for long-horizon temporal reasoning and symbolic manipulation that data scaling alone cannot resolve. Future work will need explicit state-tracking, structured reasoning modules, or self-correction mechanisms, and the dataset's extensible pipeline positions VBVR as a likely standard testbed for such architectural advances.
