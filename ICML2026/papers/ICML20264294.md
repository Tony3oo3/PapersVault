## Problem

Existing VLM privacy benchmarks evaluate only privacy perception—detecting isolated sensitive attributes like phone numbers or faces—while ignoring the more critical risk of privacy reasoning, where models link distributed multimodal fragments to reconstruct identifiable individual profiles. This gap leaves the dominant threat from reasoning-capable VLMs (e.g., associating a face with a name, location, and habits across multiple images) entirely unmeasured.

## Input / Output

The benchmark uses a VQA-style format where each sample pairs one or more images (from a pool of 1,119) with a text question in English or Chinese. Outputs vary by task: recognition tasks yield attribute labels, extraction tasks require exact-match text strings, localization tasks produce bounding-box coordinates, and reasoning tasks (single-step, chained, re-identification, cross-modal) produce free-form text answers that are scored for correctness or step-completion. The 40 synthetic individual profiles each contain 10 linked multimodal instances, enabling cross-image and cross-modal linkage evaluation.

## Method

MultiPriv introduces the Privacy Perception and Reasoning (PPR) framework, formalizing privacy risk as a two-stage hierarchical mapping: a perception function Φ that decodes raw multimodal input into sensitive attribute sets, and a reasoning function Ψ that consolidates those attributes into a target individual identity. The benchmark comprises 36 fine-grained privacy attributes across 7 categories (biometric, identity document, medical health, financial account, location trajectory, property identity, social attributes) organized into 9 tasks spanning both stages. Data construction uses a controlled synthesis pipeline combining ~80% publicly licensed attribute-level images with fully synthetic individual profiles generated via Doubao and Photoshop, ensuring stable identifier-to-attribute links without exposing real personal data. Evaluation covers 50+ open-source and commercial VLMs using F1/IEA/mIoU for perception and accuracy/completion-percentage for reasoning, with safety-alignment refusals treated as task failures to measure the model's true capability boundary.

## Results

On the MultiPriv benchmark, 60% of the 50+ evaluated VLMs achieve up to 80% accuracy on individual-level privacy reasoning tasks. For privacy reasoning (the paper's core contribution), Qwen3-VL-32B-Thinking attains the highest overall risk score of 0.874, with 0.878 on chained reasoning and 0.963 on cross-modal association; Gemini-2.5-Flash follows at 0.825. For privacy perception, Gemini-2.5-Pro leads at 0.579 overall, while GPT-5 reaches 0.505. Critically, reasoning scores far exceed perception scores for top models (e.g., Qwen3-VL-32B-Thinking: 0.874 reasoning vs. 0.380 perception for its non-thinking counterpart), confirming that reasoning—not perception—is the primary driver of individual-level privacy risk.

## Baselines & Comparisons

As a benchmark paper, MultiPriv does not propose a new method but evaluates 50+ VLMs, enabling direct cross-model comparisons. Open-source reasoning-capable models (Qwen3-VL-Thinking series, InternVL3.5) dominate privacy reasoning, with Qwen3-VL-32B-Thinking (0.874) outperforming all commercial models by 0.05–0.42 points. Notably, the 4B Thinking variant (0.871) nearly matches its 32B counterpart (0.874) and far exceeds QvQ-72B-Preview (0.632), showing that model scale does not linearly predict privacy risk. Commercial models exhibit a stark capability-alignment trade-off: GPT-5 has an answered-only accuracy of 0.91 but an overall score of only 0.459 due to a 45% refusal rate, while Qwen3-VL achieves 0.87 overall with near-zero refusal, directly exposing its linkage capability. Cross-lingual gaps are significant—Qwen3-VL-32B-Thinking scores 0.878 on English chained reasoning but only 0.731 on Chinese—indicating that single-language evaluations systematically underestimate risk. Reasoning prompts (CoT, step-by-step) further amplify leakage, raising GPT-5's answered accuracy from 0.87 to 0.95 and Qwen3-VL from 0.86 to 0.93.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from existing VLM privacy benchmarks (Priv-IQ, MultiPA, Tsaprazlis et al.) that evaluate only privacy perception (detecting sensitive attributes) or query refusal, by introducing the Privacy Perception and Reasoning (PPR) framework that adds a second, higher-order dimension: the model's ability to link distributed multimodal cues into a coherent individual profile. It draws conceptual inspiration from text-only inference-privacy work (Staab et al., 2024; Green et al., 2025) and the DOXING benchmark (Luo et al., 2026) for geolocation, but is the first to formalize and benchmark this linkage threat in the multimodal VLM setting. The dataset construction repurposes public perception datasets (WIDER FACE, Open-i, PII External) for the attribute-level component while introducing 40 fully synthetic individual profiles to enable controlled identity-linkage evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that 60% of widely deployed VLMs achieve up to 80% accuracy on individual-level privacy reasoning tasks, and that reasoning (not perception) is the dominant risk driver, reframes how the field should think about VLM safety: current alignment mechanisms are inconsistent across languages, attribute categories, and reasoning effort levels, leaving systematic gaps. The striking GPT-5 result—where higher reasoning effort paradoxically increases refusal rates from 35% to 69%, masking a 0.91 answered-only accuracy behind a 0.46 overall score—reveals that capability and alignment are entangled in non-obvious ways. Open questions include whether these findings generalize beyond synthetic profiles to noisier real-world data, how agentic or long-horizon interaction settings change the threat landscape, and whether multi-layered dynamic alignment can close the cross-lingual and task-level gaps identified here.
