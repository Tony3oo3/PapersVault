## Problem

High-fidelity co-folding docking models (e.g., AlphaFold3) achieve strong pose accuracy but rely on iterative generative refinement and heavy computation, making them too slow for screening-scale virtual screening or repeatedly invoked agent-centered discovery loops. Additionally, existing benchmarks conflate model quality with input-information strength by comparing methods that use different levels of structural prior (blind vs. interface-informed), obscuring true capability differences.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265889/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model is MSA-free and takes a protein receptor structure, a ligand structure, and protocol-dependent structural priors (pocket center coordinates and residue-level interface indicators) as input; in blind docking these priors are absent or set to defaults, while in the interface-informed protocol they are provided explicitly. Output is a set of 3D protein-ligand complex conformations: 25 candidates are sampled for accuracy evaluation (top-5 ranked by model confidence, success defined as ligand RMSD < 2.0 Å after pocket alignment), while the latency protocol generates 5 conformations for a representative 256-token complex in 0.17 s on a single NVIDIA H20 GPU.

## Method

The framework combines a streamlined MSA-free Pairformer–Dual-Scale DiT backbone (single recycling pass, R=1) with two key innovations. First, Progressive Consistency Regularization (PCR) compresses diffusion sampling into reliable few-step inference by enforcing cross-noise-level consistency between adjacent points on the same Gaussian interpolation path, anchored by reconstruction losses to prevent geometric erosion; a progressive curriculum over the root-NSR grid gradually tightens the consistency constraint. Second, Residual-Safe Quantization keeps geometry-sensitive residual streams and operations (attention softmax, triangle multiplication, normalization) in BF16 while quantizing selected compute-heavy linear transformations inside Pairformer and DiT blocks to INT8 (W8A16 or W8A8), confining quantization noise to the local transformation branch. A central empirical finding is that the Linear (rectified-flow) parameterization, with its time-independent pairwise velocity target, degrades more gracefully than EDM-style denoising under ultra-low NFE, because its Euler-step update accumulates less discretization error than EDM's time-dependent denoising projection.

## Results

On the PDBBind2020 test set (428 complexes), the Linear-parameterized model achieves 83.18% docking success rate (top-5, RMSD < 2.0 Å) at 20 NFE under the interface-informed protocol, with the EDM variant reaching 82.48%. Under the practical 4–10 NFE operating window enabled by Progressive Consistency Regularization, the Linear-PCR model retains strong accuracy while reducing sampling cost. For deployment, the full pipeline (R=1 recycling, NFE=5, W8A8 Residual-Safe Quantization, JIT compilation) generates 5 conformations for a 256-token complex in 0.17 s on a single NVIDIA H20 GPU, a >300× speedup over AlphaFold3 under the same input-shape setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The Linear-parameterized model (83.18% SR) outperforms the closest generative baseline SurfDock (81.07%) by ~2.1 points and Interformer (77.00%) by ~6.2 points under the interface-informed protocol. Against classical and regression baselines, the gap is larger: Vina (52.30%), TANKBind (14.95%), and EquiBind (2.58%) are all substantially below. Among generative methods, DiffDock (47.66%) and DiffDock-L (50.00%) trail by over 30 points, while Uni-Mol Docking V2 (61.21%) is ~22 points lower. In the latency benchmark (Figure 3), the proposed model at 0.17 s for 5 conformations achieves a >300× speedup over AlphaFold3 and is also faster than Chai-1 under the same 256-token, 5-conformation protocol on a single H20 GPU. A critical caveat is that the headline accuracy is reported under an interface-informed strong-prior protocol (pocket center + residue-level interface indicators), so direct comparison with blind-docking methods is not apples-to-apples; the authors explicitly separate blind and interface-informed evaluations to avoid conflating model quality with input prior strength.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: co-folding models (AlphaFold3, Boltz, Chai-1) that achieve high-fidelity protein-ligand structure prediction but suffer from iterative generative refinement and heavy computation; few-step generative sampling via consistency models (Song et al., 2023) and rectified/linear flow (Liu et al., 2022; Esser et al., 2024) that compress diffusion trajectories; and post-training quantization for efficient Transformer deployment. The key conceptual departure is treating docking latency as a first-class design constraint rather than an afterthought, formalizing an information-level-matched evaluation protocol, and arguing that trajectory geometry (Linear vs. EDM parameterization) matters as much as solver count for few-step molecular generation. The PCR component extends consistency-model ideas with a reconstruction anchor and progressive curriculum, while Residual-Safe Quantization introduces a structured mixed-precision strategy specific to the geometry-sensitive residual pathways in co-folding architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 0.17 s latency for 5 conformations on a single H20 GPU (>300× over AlphaFold3) is a substantial practical step toward making high-fidelity docking callable inside iterative agent loops or screening-scale virtual screening, where current co-folding models are too slow to serve as real-time feedback. The information-level-matched evaluation protocol is a useful methodological correction that prevents conflating model quality with input-prior strength in docking benchmarks. Open questions include whether the speedup and accuracy hold on larger or more diverse complexes beyond the 256-token PDBBind2020 test set, how blind-docking performance compares to dedicated blind methods under truly matched conditions, and whether the agent-centered workflow framing translates into demonstrated end-to-end discovery gains rather than remaining a deployment motivation.
