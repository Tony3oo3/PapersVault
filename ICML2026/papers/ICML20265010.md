## Problem

Existing post-hoc GNN explainers extract a single sparse key subgraph at one granularity, which fails to capture multi-level semantics and makes the optimization highly non-convex and sensitive to initialization and noise. This single-scale local view leads to unstable explanations, entrapment in local optima, and susceptibility to spurious correlations, particularly when the combinatorial search space lacks global topological priors.

## Input / Output

The method takes an undirected graph G=(V,E) with node feature matrix X∈R^{|V|×d} and a frozen trained GNN f(·) as input. It produces a hierarchical set of M key subgraphs {G_{k_m}} and complementary subgraphs {G_{k_m}^c}, each controlled by an edge-retention ratio k_m∈(0,1) (nine scales from 10%–90% in experiments), all generated from a single set of shared explainer parameters. During training, a Gumbel-Sigmoid relaxation provides differentiable soft masks; at inference, hard Top-K truncation on edge-importance scores S(e_ij)∈(−1,1) yields the final discrete subgraphs.

## Method

MSExplainer employs a parameter-sharing architecture where all M scales share one GCN encoder and one edge-scoring MLP (S(e_ij)=tanh(φ_ω(h_i⊙h_j))), avoiding linear parameter growth. Two synergistic mechanisms drive training: (1) multi-scale consistency guidance, which uses an L1 edge-importance loss to propagate prediction-consistency signals from higher-scale (more global) subgraphs down to lower-scale subgraphs, effectively performing fine-grained pruning guided by global topology; and (2) single-scale adaptive learning, which applies a contrastive loss at each scale to maximize the cosine-similarity gap between the key subgraph's prediction and the original graph's prediction versus the complementary subgraph's prediction. The joint loss L_total=λ₁L_edge+λ₂L_contrastive is optimized with AdamW, and the paper proves an O(1/ln M) upper bound on representation-consistency deviation plus same-order computational complexity as single-scale methods under the shared-parameter design.

## Results

On the six graph classification benchmarks, MSExplainer achieves the highest prediction accuracy (ACC@p) at the standard 70% edge ratio on 5 out of 6 datasets: 85.7% on Mutagenicity, 78.8% on NCI1, 73.4% on BBBP, 98.0% on BA-2Motif, and 67.4% on MNIST-7K. On BA-3Motif it ties with MixupExplainer at 62.5%. In edge-level explanation accuracy (Edge AUC), MSExplainer scores 93.0±4.9 on Mutagenicity and 91.5±3.2 on BA-2Motif, outperforming the second-best method (GSCExplainer) by 1.7 and 2.6 percentage points respectively. Fidelity@p curves confirm that removing MSExplainer's identified key subgraphs causes the largest drop in prediction confidence across most datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MSExplainer outperforms the strongest prior baseline GSCExplainer on Mutagenicity (85.7 vs. 81.1, +4.6 pts), BA-2Motif (98.0 vs. 91.0, +7.0 pts), and MNIST-7K (67.4 vs. 47.9, +19.5 pts) at the 70% edge ratio. Against MixupExplainer, it leads on Mutagenicity (+21.9 pts), BBBP (+2.2 pts), and BA-2Motif (+6.0 pts), while tying on BA-3Motif (62.5 vs. 62.5) and narrowly beating it on NCI1 (78.8 vs. 78.1). OrphicX, the strongest causal-based baseline, is surpassed by MSExplainer on all six datasets, with the largest gaps on MNIST-7K (+23.1 pts) and BA-2Motif (+8.0 pts). The older mask-based methods (GNNExplainer, PGExplainer) and the multi-granularity ReFine are consistently outperformed by 10–30 points across datasets, confirming that the multi-scale consistency guidance and contrastive adaptive learning jointly yield substantially more faithful subgraph explanations than single-scale or single-mechanism approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

MSExplainer extends the post-hoc instance-level GNN explanation paradigm (GNNExplainer, PGExplainer, OrphicX, MixupExplainer, GSCExplainer) by replacing the single-scale sparse subgraph search with a hierarchical multi-scale decomposition under a parameter-sharing design. Conceptually, it repurposes the multi-scale analysis idea common in vision (coarse-to-fine) for graph explanation, coupling a top-down consistency guidance mechanism (higher-scale global structure informs lower-scale local pruning) with a bottom-up single-scale contrastive loss that separates key from complementary subgraphs. The paper departs from prior work primarily in the optimization formulation: rather than searching a single combinatorial subgraph space, it jointly optimizes M coupled subgraphs with cross-scale constraints, and provides a theoretical upper-bound argument (O(1/ln M) representation deviation) that no prior single-scale explainer offers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The parameter-sharing design showing that multi-scale training is asymptotically equivalent in complexity to single-scale training is practically important: it removes the main objection to multi-scale approaches (computational overhead) and makes the technique deployable at scale. However, the method is limited to undirected graphs and graph classification, uses a fixed number of scales (M=9), and the theoretical bound relies on Lipschitz continuity and small-perturbation assumptions that may not hold for all GNN architectures. Open questions include whether dynamic scale adaptation (choosing M per-instance) can further improve efficiency on large-scale graphs, and whether the approach extends to directed, heterogeneous, or temporal graphs where the notion of 'scale' becomes less straightforward.
