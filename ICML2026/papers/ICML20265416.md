## Problem

While adversarial robustness research has extensively studied feature disentanglement, the nature of robust and non-robust features under natural image corruptions (fog, blur, noise) remains poorly understood. Prior methods focus on implicit representation learning through augmentation or regularization, but no prior work explicitly characterizes how internal computational pathways contribute to corruption robustness or manipulates them directly.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265416/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

S&D operates on standard RGB images (224×224 for classification, variable resolution for detection and segmentation) and produces class labels, bounding boxes, or pixel-wise segmentation masks depending on the downstream task. The method modifies only the weight matrices of the stem stage (first convolutional layer) during training via symmetry-preserving transformations, requiring no architectural changes, no additional parameters, and zero test-time overhead. It is architecture-agnostic, validated across CNNs (ResNet, VGG, MobileNetV2, ConvNeXt), Vision Transformers (ViT, MobileViT, EfficientFormer), and state-space models (MambaOut).

## Method

S&D (Suppress and Diversify) is a non-intrusive training-time refinement built on two components. First, the Memory-aware Dynamic Selection Mechanism (MDSM) maintains a memory bank of pathway groups, iteratively updated during epoch gaps using a fitness score that balances feature invariance (CKA similarity between clean and corrupted feature maps) and discriminability (a sigmoid-scheduled term), identifying a stable group G′ of robust pathways. Second, the Structure-consistent Path Tweaking Strategy (SPTS) filters the top-K most robust pathways from G′ and generates augmented counterparts by applying symmetry-preserving transformations (channel shuffling, horizontal/vertical flipping, matrix transpose) to their weight matrices, forming a diversified optimal group G* that is integrated into the model for standard training. The key empirical insight driving this design is that robust features (characterized via local Lipschitz constants) exhibit progressive decay across network depth, and model-wide corruption robustness is functionally dependent on the prevalence of these invariant features in early layers.

## Results

On ImageNet-C (severity 5), S&D achieves 42.7% top-1 accuracy, surpassing the second-best baseline DAMP (41.4%) by 1.3% and the baseline (39.2%) by 3.5%. With strong augmentation (S&D*), the method reaches 46.3%, a 7.1% absolute gain over the baseline and 3.0% above AdaSAP_P*. Across diverse backbones (MobileNetV2, ResNet-50, VGG19, WideResNet-50.2, ConvNeXt-base), S&D consistently reduces average mCE by 0.5–5.5 points while incurring zero test-time overhead. For dense prediction, S&D improves mIoU by 1.4–6.3% on segmentation benchmarks and mAP by 0.5–2.2% on COCO-C detection.

## Baselines & Comparisons

S&D (42.7%) outperforms all four categories of robustness baselines on ImageNet-C: sub-network selection (Stochastic Depth 38.9%, AdaSAP_P 43.3%), dynamic weight evolution (EWS 40.6%, DST 38.7%, DAMP 41.4%), consistency learning (DAT 41.1%, TVM 39.8%), and bio-inspired designs (VOneNet 40.3%, GaborNet 37.5%). The only method exceeding S&D without augmentation is AdaSAP_P (43.3%), but S&D with strong augmentation (S&D*, 46.3%) surpasses it by 3.0%. The largest margins are over GaborNet (+5.2%), DST (+4.0%), and Stochastic Depth (+3.8%). In test-time adaptation (Table 7), S&D provides consistent orthogonal gains across all eight TTA methods, with the largest improvement on AdaContrast (+3.2%) and RMT (+2.0%), confirming compatibility with existing adaptation pipelines. For object detection, S&D improves YOLOv5s by 2.2 mAP (19.9→22.1) and Faster R-CNN by 0.7 mAP (17.5→18.2) on COCO-C, while for segmentation it boosts Mask2Former by 6.3 mIoU on Cityscapes-C and GCNet by 3.8 mIoU on ACDC.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the robust/non-robust feature disentanglement framework originally developed for adversarial examples (Ilyas et al., 2019) to the distinct setting of natural image corruptions, where distortions exhibit global structural patterns rather than local pixel-level noise. It departs from EWS (Guo et al., 2022), which refines non-robust sub-networks via knowledge distillation with a model-specific controller, by proposing a parameter-free, architecture-agnostic pathway refinement that requires no additional model structures. The Lipschitz-constant-based formalization draws on Parseval network literature (Cissé et al., 2017; Fazlyab et al., 2019), but applies it to characterize internal feature stability under corruptions rather than adversarial perturbations. S&D is positioned as orthogonal to and complementary with data augmentation and regularization methods (AugMix, Mixup, etc.), which it can be stacked on top of for additional gains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The progressive decay of robust features across network depth provides a concrete, actionable diagnostic for where corruption vulnerability originates, shifting the field's focus from input-level augmentation to internal pathway-level intervention. S&D's zero test-time overhead and plug-and-play nature make it immediately deployable in safety-critical pipelines (autonomous driving, medical imaging) where inference latency is constrained. However, the consistent clean-corruption trade-off (0.5–5.5% clean accuracy loss) remains an unresolved tension, and the evaluation is limited to moderate-scale models; scalability to large pretrained models and full ImageNet-scale training is unverified. Open questions include developing a theoretical framework for why robust features decay, mitigating the clean-accuracy cost, and extending the pathway-refinement paradigm to audio, video, and multimodal settings.
