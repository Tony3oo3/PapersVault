## Problem

Current reasoning LLMs can generate arbitrarily long chain-of-thought traces but lack the ability to predict the marginal benefit of additional thinking tokens for a given query, making it impossible to decide when to stop reasoning, switch models, or allocate compute across multiple samples. This gap prevents principled, user-adaptive control over the accuracy–cost trade-off at inference time, since no canonical cost-sensitivity parameter exists and the optimal compute budget is query-dependent.

## Input / Output

Given a text query x, a partial chain-of-thought trace z (a prefix of thinking tokens), a frozen reasoning model π, and a user-specified cost-sensitivity scalar λ, the forecaster outputs Beta(α_t, β_t) parameters for each token budget t on a uniform grid {0, 512, 1024, …, 8192}, yielding a continuous expected-reward curve via linear interpolation. At inference the Beta mean ψ̂(t|x,z,π) is plugged into a Gittins-index threshold to produce a binary stop/continue decision, a ranked model-selection ordering, or a step-level trace-advancement choice across multiple partial trajectories.

## Method

Re-FORC trains a lightweight adapter (self-attention pooling over penultimate-layer hidden states followed by a linear head projecting to 2|T| dimensions) on top of frozen Qwen3 reasoning models (1.7B/4B/8B), predicting Beta-distribution parameters for expected reward at each future token horizon. Training data is generated by sampling full unconstrained trajectories from DeepScaleR-Preview, truncating at grid intervals, and reusing trajectory segments across horizons to obtain Monte Carlo reward estimates in O(N) samples rather than O(|T|·N·L). The decision layer applies a Gittins-index policy derived from Pandora's-box theory: for binary rewards the index has closed form g = 1 − λt/ψ(t), and the agent continues reasoning (or advances a particular trace/model) only when g exceeds the current best reward R_best. This single mechanism unifies early stopping, joint model-and-length selection, and step-level test-time scaling, with the user controlling the compute–accuracy frontier through λ alone.

## Results

Re-FORC reduces inference compute by up to 26% (vs. S1 fixed-budget cutoffs) and 21% (vs. DeepConf) for the Qwen3 4B model while maintaining accuracy in early stopping. In joint model-and-length selection, Re-FORC Pandora reaches 1.7 percentage points higher peak accuracy than the largest model (8B) alone while requiring 12% less compute to match its accuracy. In test-time scaling at peak compute, Re-FORC improves accuracy over the strongest DeepConf variant by +13.9 pp (1.7B), +8.4 pp (4B), and +7.5 pp (8B), averaging +9.9 pp over confidence-based baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against S1 (Muennighoff et al., 2025), which uses fixed-length token cutoffs, Re-FORC dominates across all three model sizes in the early-stopping setting, saving 26% compute on average for the 4B model while preserving accuracy and achieving up to +4.2 pp higher accuracy at equal token budgets. Against DeepConf (Fu et al., 2026), a confidence-based adaptive method, Re-FORC reduces compute by 21% in early stopping and outperforms the strongest DeepConf variant by up to +13.9 pp (1.7B) in test-time scaling, with the gap narrowing to +7.5 pp for the 8B model. Against majority-vote self-consistency (Wang et al., 2023b), both Re-FORC-scaling and Re-FORC Pandora outperform across most compute budgets in the test-time scaling experiments, with the advantage most pronounced in the medium-to-high compute regime (≥50k tokens). In model selection, Re-FORC Pandora surpasses running any single model in isolation (All-1.7B, All-4B, All-8B) in peak accuracy, demonstrating that joint model-and-length optimization yields gains beyond what the largest model alone can achieve.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper operationalizes the Pandora's box / Gittins index framework from Achille & Soatto (2026) and Weitzman (1979) for LLM reasoning, where prior work had only sketched the theoretical connection. It departs from existing forecasting literature (which predicts restart benefits or initial correctness in non-CoT settings) by learning a continuous reward-vs-tokens curve via a lightweight adapter, and from confidence-based stopping methods (S1, DeepConf) by grounding decisions in an explicit cost-utility objective rather than heuristic thresholds. The multi-model, multi-trajectory Pandora's box greedy search generalizes single-model early stopping and static model routing into a unified sequential decision problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Re-FORC provides a practical, training-free (for the base model) mechanism to let users dial in their accuracy-cost trade-off via a single λ parameter, which is a meaningful UX improvement over fixed token budgets. The 26% compute reduction at matched accuracy and the 1.7pp gain over the largest model alone in joint model-length selection are practically significant for deployment. Key open questions include: whether the forecaster's overconfidence (noted by the authors) degrades on harder or out-of-distribution tasks, whether the approach transfers beyond math benchmarks and the Qwen3 family, and how training-data cost scales with model size and domain diversity.
