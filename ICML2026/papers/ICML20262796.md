## Problem

Post-training LVLMs via SFT injects external knowledge but causes catastrophic forgetting and sub-optimal generalization, while RLVR enhances performance through internal reinforcement but is capped by the model's existing knowledge boundaries. The sequential SFT→RLVR pipeline attempts to combine both but introduces prohibitive computational overhead and remains vulnerable to forgetting. The paper asks how to unify both paradigms into a single, theoretically grounded training stage.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262796/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image paired with a text instruction as input and produces task-specific outputs: segmentation masks (via SAM2), bounding boxes, 2D point coordinates for GUI grounding, or free-form text answers for math and VQA. During training, the key structural detail is that ground-truth labels are interleaved with G on-policy rollouts within each mini-batch, and the labels are reformatted to match the model's preferred tokenization (e.g., whitespace in JSON structures) to maintain the π_θ ≈ π_θ_old assumption.

## Method

ViSurf unifies SFT and RLVR by showing their gradients share an identical form (weighted log-likelihood), differing only in guidance signal and coefficient. The core mechanism injects the ground-truth label y as an additional high-reward sample into the GRPO rollout group, computing group-relative advantages over the augmented set {y} ∪ {o_1,...,o_G}, yielding a single objective whose gradient decomposes into an RLVR term and an SFT term. Three reward control strategies ensure stability: (i) reformatting ground-truth to match the policy's stylistic preferences, (ii) zeroing the reasoning-format reward for ground-truth (which lacks annotated reasoning traces), and (iii) smoothing the ground-truth reward to the mean of rollouts whenever rollouts already exceed it, which drives A_y→0 and automatically shifts optimization from external supervision to pure RLVR. This creates a self-adaptive balance: when the policy fails to produce correct rollouts, the SFT term dominates; when it succeeds, the RLVR term takes over, all within one training phase at the same computational cost as standalone RLVR.

## Results

ViSurf achieves an average score of 69.6 across six diverse domains (non-object segmentation, reasoning segmentation, GUI grounding, anomaly detection, medical imaging, and mathematical reasoning), representing a 38.6% relative improvement over the Qwen2.5-VL-7B+SAM2 baseline (50.2). It consistently outperforms standalone SFT (56.2), RLVR (56.1), and the sequential SFT→RLVR pipeline (65.4). The gains are most pronounced in out-of-distribution settings: on gRefCOCO non-object detection, ViSurf reaches 57.1% N-Acc versus 0.0% for RLVR and 3.3% for SFT, and on RealIAD anomaly detection it achieves 69.3 ROC_AUC versus 50.0 for RLVR. On VQA benchmarks (ChartQA, DocVQA), ViSurf preserves baseline performance (87.4, 95.0) while SFT and SFT→RLVR suffer catastrophic forgetting (80.8, 89.6 and 85.0, 92.9 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LISA-7B (CVPR 2024), ViSurf improves gRefCOCO gIoU from 61.6 to 72.9 and N-Acc from 54.7 to 74.1, while also surpassing it on ReasonSeg (66.5 vs. 53.6 val gIoU). GSVA-7B (CVPR 2024) achieves 66.5 gIoU on gRefCOCO but does not report ReasonSeg results; ViSurf exceeds it by 6.4 gIoU points and adds strong non-object detection (74.1% N-Acc vs. 62.4%). SAM4MLLM-7B (ECCV 2024) reaches 69.0 gIoU on gRefCOCO and 46.7 on ReasonSeg val; ViSurf outperforms it on both (72.9 and 66.5). SegZero-7B (arXiv 2025) reports 62.6/57.5 gIoU on ReasonSeg val/test but no gRefCOCO numbers; ViSurf matches or exceeds it on ReasonSeg (66.5/65.0) while adding substantial gRefCOCO performance. VisionReasoner-7B (arXiv 2025), the architecture ViSurf builds upon, achieves only 41.5 gIoU and 0.0% N-Acc on gRefCOCO (pure RLVR) and 66.3/63.6 on ReasonSeg; ViSurf dramatically improves gRefCOCO (72.9/74.1) while maintaining comparable ReasonSeg performance (66.5/65.0), confirming that the unified SFT+RLVR objective is the source of the gain rather than architectural changes.

## Relation to Prior Work

ViSurf sits at the intersection of two well-established LVLM post-training paradigms: Supervised Fine-Tuning (LLaVA, QwenVL, InternVL) and Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (GRPO, DAPO, SegZero, VisualRFT, VisionReasoner). While prior work (Zhang et al., 2025; Ma et al., 2025) has attempted to combine SFT and RLVR by simply summing their losses, ViSurf provides a theoretically grounded unification by showing that both objectives share a common gradient structure, enabling ground-truth labels to be injected directly into the RLVR rollout set as a high-reward sample. The three reward control strategies (preference alignment, thinking-reward elimination, reward smoothing) are novel engineering contributions that address stability issues specific to this unified formulation rather than to either paradigm in isolation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ViSurf offers a practically attractive single-stage alternative to the sequential SFT→RLVR pipeline, matching RLVR's memory footprint while eliminating the additional SFT phase and its associated catastrophic forgetting (demonstrated on ChartQA/DocVQA). The self-adaptive balance—where the SFT term dominates when rollouts fail and vanishes when the policy already succeeds—provides an elegant mechanism that practitioners can adopt without tuning a weighting hyperparameter. Key limitations include reliance on the VeRL framework (currently Qwen-VL only) and the restriction of ground-truth labels to final answers without reasoning traces, though the authors note compatibility with distillation-based reasoning injection as future work. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to architectures beyond Qwen-VL and whether the reward smoothing heuristic holds under different reward function designs.
