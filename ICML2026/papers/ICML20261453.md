## Problem

Existing concept erasing methods for text-to-image diffusion models tend to excessively remove unsafe concepts while simultaneously suppressing benign semantic content embedded in harmful prompts, degrading model utility for human-oriented and safe content generation. Additionally, these methods designed for text-to-image settings fail to generalize to image-to-image (I2I) scenarios, leaving a significant safety gap when users provide initial images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261453/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a text prompt (processed through CLIP text encoder into 77-token embeddings) and optionally an initial image for I2I tasks, producing a 512×512 RGB image with the target concept erased. During training, a small prompt pool of general unsafe concepts (e.g., 'nudity', 'sexual', 'naked', 'erotic') drives generation, while at inference the model handles arbitrary prompts including adversarial ones. For I2I, the initial image is encoded into the latent space and combined with the text prompt at a fixed denoising strength of 0.5.

## Method

ForceForget reformulates concept erasing as a reinforcement learning reward optimization problem, using DDPO-style policy gradient updates with PPO clipping to fine-tune LoRA weights on the UNet. The Concept Erasing Reward (CER) combines a safety reward (weighted NSFW classifier scores) with an alignment reward (CLIP similarity between the generated image and a BLIP-derived caption filtered of NSFW keywords, plus a target condition), preventing over-erasure of safe content. A key architectural contribution is the Safe Adapter: a lightweight linear layer applied only to the last 4 tokens of the CLIP text embedding in cross-attention layers, which learns to dominantly represent unsafe concepts in the K/V projections while the remaining tokens (processed by LoRA) focus on safe semantics. This partial-token modification decouples targeted unsafe concepts without disrupting the full text feature distribution, enabling effective erasing while preserving prompt-following capability and human-oriented content generation.

## Results

On the I2P benchmark (4703 prompts), ForceForget detects only 38 total nude body parts, the lowest among all 10 baselines (next best: DuMo at 45, Co-Erasing at 53, vs. SD v1.4's 810). Against adversarial attacks, ForceForget achieves 100% Nudity Removal Rate (NRR) on both Ring-A-Bell and MMA, and 99.63% on P4D, outperforming all competitors. On benign content retention (COCO-30K), ForceForget scores 30.53 CLIP and 26.73 FID, remaining competitive with the best baselines while achieving superior erasure. In I2I transfer, ForceForget reaches 96.4% NRR with nude image input, whereas all other methods fall below 30%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ForceForget outperforms all 10 baselines on total nudity detection (38 vs. DuMo's 45 and Co-Erasing's 53) and achieves perfect 100% NRR on both Ring-A-Bell and MMA attacks, surpassing the next-best DuMo (99.65% / 96.40%) and MACE (73.10% / 99.90%). On P4D, ForceForget (99.63%) edges out MACE and DuMo (both 97.79%). On benign content quality, ForceForget's CLIP score (30.53) is slightly below CA (31.03) and SAFREE (30.66), and its FID (26.73) is higher than RECE (22.12) and SafeGen (22.61), indicating a modest utility trade-off. However, ForceForget dramatically outperforms all baselines in I2I transfer: with a nude input image, it achieves 96.4% NRR while the best competitor (RECE) reaches only 28.6%, and with a clean input it achieves 100% NRR versus SafeGen's 59.2% and RECE's 60.6%. On safe semantic alignment (VQAScore-SC), ForceForget maintains high scores comparable to CA and SAFREE, while Co-Erasing drops to 44.1%, confirming that ForceForget avoids the over-erasure problem that plagues several baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DDPO framework (Black et al., 2023) for RL-based fine-tuning of diffusion models, repurposing it from aesthetic/quality optimization to concept erasure. It departs from the dominant paradigm of supervised fine-tuning (ESD, CA, SafeGen) and closed-form solutions (RECE, MACE) by reformulating erasure as reward maximization with a composite Concept Erasing Reward (CER) combining safety and alignment signals. The Safe Adapter, which modifies only the last 4 tokens of text embeddings in cross-attention, is a lightweight architectural addition distinct from prior methods that either neutralize full text embeddings (ESD) or require additional reference images (SafeGen, MACE).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a practical gap in existing erasure methods: over-erasure of safe semantic content within harmful prompts and degradation of human-oriented generation. The finding that T2I erasure methods fail catastrophically in I2I settings (NRR < 30% for most baselines with nude input) while ForceForget maintains 96.4% highlights an underexplored safety dimension. Limitations include fixed CER weights that apply uniform erasure strength regardless of prompt severity, primary evaluation on SD v1.4, and the Safe Adapter's incompatibility with FLUX's multimodal attention architecture. Open questions include whether the RL approach scales to more complex multi-concept erasure and whether the I2I transferability advantage persists under stronger adversarial I2I attacks.
