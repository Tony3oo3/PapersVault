## Problem

Standard multi-armed bandit algorithms assume feedback comes from a single homogeneous data source, yet real-world systems (clinical trials across hospitals, recommender systems with diverse user populations, multi-pipeline logging) receive observations from multiple sources with unknown and distinct noise variances. No existing framework jointly adapts to arm rewards and source selection in an online setting: uniform source selection is dominated by the noisiest sources, while explore-then-commit strategies waste regret distinguishing near-identical variances or require an intractable tolerance parameter.

## Input / Output

At each round t, the learner selects an arm i_t ∈ [K] and a source j_t ∈ [M], then observes a scalar reward X_t = μ_{i_t} + ε_t where the noise ε_t is drawn from a mean-zero distribution whose variance σ_{j_t}² depends only on the chosen source (not the arm). The output is the sequence of (arm, source) pairs and the resulting cumulative regret relative to the optimal arm. A key structural constraint is that source selection affects only the noise scale, not the reward mean, so the learner must infer which sources are reliable purely from the spread of observed rewards.

## Method

SOAR operates in two stages. Stage 1 (PREPROCESS) queries each of the M sources on a fixed arm for τ_p rounds, constructs high-probability UCBs and LCBs on the empirical variance estimates via Bernstein concentration, and eliminates any source whose LCB exceeds the minimum UCB across all sources, pruning the set to S_G of sources within c*² of the minimum variance. Stage 2 (adaptive LCB-UCB) first performs a short initial-exploration phase to ensure confidence bounds hold, then at each subsequent round selects the arm with the largest UCB on mean reward (Equation 10, using pooled empirical variance) and the source with the smallest LCB on variance (Equation 8, using a Freedman-based martingale concentration bound with a fourth-moment proxy Q_j(t)). The key insight is that this joint argmax-UCB / argmin-LCB mechanism simultaneously drives optimistic arm exploration and cautious, variance-aware source selection, yielding an instance-dependent regret of Õ(σ*² Σ 1/Δ_i) that matches the oracle single-source MAB lower bound up to logarithmic factors, despite the learner never knowing σ* in advance.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 5.1) establishes that SOAR achieves an instance-dependent regret of $\tilde{O}\!\left(\sigma^{*2}\sum_{i=2}^{K} 1/\Delta_i\right)$, matching the oracle lower bound for a single-source MAB with known optimal variance, up to logarithmic factors. The preprocessing and initial-exploration overheads contribute only $O(\log T)$ regret, so the bound is asymptotically dominated by the instance-difficulty term. On the MovieLens 32M panel (15 reviewers × 500 movies, 7,500 ratings), SOAR rapidly concentrates on the lowest-variance reviewer and achieves substantially lower cumulative regret than both baselines (Figures 2–3), with regret growing linearly only during the fixed preprocessing/exploration phases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SOAR is compared against two UCB-based baselines: UUCB (Uniform-UCB, which samples sources uniformly) and ETC-UCB (Explore-then-Commit, which first identifies the best source via BAI then runs standard UCB). Under worst-case instance WC-1 (all non-optimal sources share high variance $\sigma_{\max}^2$), UUCB's regret degrades to $\tilde{O}(\sigma_{\max}^2 \sum_i 1/\Delta_i)$, while SOAR's dominant term remains $\tilde{O}(\sigma^{*2}\sum_i 1/\Delta_i)$, a factor of $(\sigma_{\max}/\sigma^*)^2$ improvement. Under WC-2 (all source variances nearly identical, $\Delta_j^{\sigma^2}\approx 0$), ETC-UCB incurs unbounded Phase-1 regret $\tilde{O}(\sum_j \bar{\mu}/(\Delta_j^{\sigma^2})^2)$ because it wastes budget distinguishing indistinguishable sources, whereas SOAR's LCB-UCB mechanism avoids this by jointly exploring arms and sources. On the MovieLens 32M panel, SOAR outperforms both baselines in cumulative regret, confirming the theoretical predictions empirically.

## Relation to Prior Work

SOAR extends the variance-adaptive bandit line (UCB-V, Cowan et al., Howard et al.) by replacing the single-source assumption with M heterogeneous sources of unknown variance, and departs from multi-fidelity bandits (Kandasamy et al., Song et al., Poloczek et al.) by removing explicit cost-accuracy tradeoffs in favor of purely noise-driven source selection. The key conceptual departure is the joint LCB-UCB mechanism: upper confidence bounds on arm rewards paired with lower confidence bounds on source variances, a combination absent from both prior families. The authors explicitly note no prior work addresses their exact setting and construct two natural baselines (uniform-source UCB and explore-then-commit) to demonstrate the gap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The oracle-optimality result—matching the instance-dependent regret of a single-source MAB with known optimal variance despite not knowing which source is best—provides a clean theoretical target for multi-source online learning and suggests that adaptive source selection can be done 'for free' up to logarithmic factors. Open questions include extending to contextual bandits or RL, handling non-stationary source quality, incorporating explicit per-source query costs, and moving beyond bounded-support noise. The empirical validation is limited to a small MovieLens panel (15 reviewers, 500 movies) and self-constructed baselines, leaving practical scalability and robustness to distributional assumptions largely untested.
