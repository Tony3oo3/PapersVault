## Problem

Transformer-based WiFi pose estimation methods suffer from quadratic computational complexity and lack explicit inductive biases for the physical structure of Channel State Information (CSI), making them parameter-heavy and unsuitable for resource-constrained edge deployment. Moving from single to multi-person estimation compounds the challenge because CSI is a coherent superposition of all propagation paths, producing entangled signals rather than separable per-person signatures. No prior work has explored State Space Models for efficient sequential modeling in WiFi-based human pose estimation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a complex-valued CSI tensor of shape N_tx × N_rx × N_ant × N_sub × T (e.g., 3×3 MIMO, 30 OFDM subcarriers, 100 Hz sampling), which is first decomposed into amplitude and phase streams via magnitude and atan2 extraction, then denoised with learnable DWT soft-thresholding (amplitude) and PhaseFi linear-regression correction (phase). The model outputs 3D joint positions for up to N_p persons (J keypoints × 3 coordinates each), per-person confidence scores, and a person count prediction, with Hungarian matching used for bipartite assignment between K learned pose queries and ground-truth persons during training.

## Method

WiFi-Mamba replaces the Transformer backbone with a Dual-Stream Selective State Space Model (DS³M) that processes amplitude and phase through parallel S6 (Mamba) pathways, coupled at the hidden-state level via a learned gating mechanism (g_t) that adaptively modulates cross-stream information flow while preserving independent parameters for each modality's distinct statistical properties. The decoder introduces Selective State Attention (SSA), which contextualizes pose queries through a lightweight SSM before cross-attention, so that each query encodes inter-query dependencies rather than attending independently. Persistent SSM Memory prepends learnable memory tokens to the query sequence and carries the SSM hidden state across frames (with gradient detachment at frame boundaries), providing temporal consistency without recurrent memory explosion. The full architecture achieves O(TDN) time complexity versus O(T²D) for Transformers, using only 2.14M parameters (4.4% of the 48.2M Person-in-WiFi 3D baseline) while improving MPJPE by 16–27% across 1–3 person scenarios.

## Results

On the Person-in-WiFi 3D dataset, WiFi-Mamba achieves MPJPE of 76.75 mm (1 person), 89.08 mm (2 persons), and 104.81 mm (3 persons), representing 16.4%, 24.1%, and 27.3% reductions over the Person-in-WiFi 3D Transformer baseline (91.77, 121.04, 144.24 mm) while using only 2.14M parameters vs. 48.2M (4.4%). Depth (MPJDLE(d)) improves by 30–31% across all person counts, and PCK@10 improves from 14.51% to 32.82% in the 3-person scenario. On the WiPose single-person benchmark, WiFi-Mamba reaches 35.82 mm MPJPE, second only to DT-Pose (34.14 mm) while using less than half its parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WiFi-Mamba outperforms the primary Transformer baseline Person-in-WiFi 3D (Yan et al., 2024) on every metric across all person counts, with the gap widening from 16.4% MPJPE reduction (1P) to 27.3% (3P), while using only 4.4% of its parameters. Against MetaFi++ (Zhou et al., 2023a), WiFi-Mamba achieves 76.75 mm vs. 116.40 mm MPJPE on Person-in-WiFi 3D (34% improvement) and 35.82 mm vs. 58.17 mm on WiPose (38.4% improvement). Compared to DT-Pose (Chen et al., 2025), WiFi-Mamba wins decisively on Person-in-WiFi 3D (76.75 vs. 92.70 mm MPJPE) but trails slightly on WiPose (35.82 vs. 34.14 mm), though with fewer parameters (2.14M vs. 4.8M). Against HPE-Li (Gian et al., 2025), WiFi-Mamba dominates on both datasets (76.75 vs. 117.70 mm on Person-in-WiFi 3D; 35.82 vs. 39.83 mm on WiPose), confirming that the SSM-based sequential modeling and cross-stream coupling provide substantial gains over lightweight convolutional approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research lines: Transformer-based WiFi pose estimation (Person-in-WiFi 3D, MetaFi++, DT-Pose) and State Space Models (Mamba, Vision Mamba, Video Mamba). It departs from the DETR-style quadratic-complexity decoder by replacing self-attention with selective SSMs, and introduces a dual-stream architecture that processes CSI amplitude and phase through parallel SSM pathways with cross-stream state coupling—a mechanism absent from prior dual-stream WiFi sensing work that typically fuses only final outputs. The Selective State Attention and Persistent SSM Memory components repurpose SSM hidden-state propagation for pose query decoding and temporal consistency, respectively, rather than the standard recurrent gating used in prior temporal pose models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 96% parameter reduction (48.2M → 2.14M) with simultaneous 16–27% MPJPE improvement opens a realistic path to edge deployment of continuous WiFi-based pose monitoring in privacy-sensitive settings (elderly care, bathrooms), where camera systems are impractical. However, evaluation is confined to a single primary dataset (Person-in-WiFi 3D, max 3 persons) and the NLOS sensitivity analysis reveals a sharp performance cliff beyond 45% signal attenuation, indicating that heavily occluded or reflected environments remain unsolved. Open questions include whether the SSM-based approach generalizes to larger crowds, more diverse hardware, and whether the linear-complexity advantage translates to meaningful real-time latency gains on actual WiFi CSI acquisition pipelines.
