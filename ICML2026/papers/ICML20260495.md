## Problem

Generating physically consistent, collision-free 3D tabletop scenes for robotic simulation remains unsolved: agent-based solvers hit a symbolic bottleneck where LLMs produce geometrically infeasible graphs, image-mediated pipelines accumulate errors across stages, and end-to-end regression models blindly replicate the physical violations (collisions, levitation) present in noisy human-annotated training data. No existing framework can simultaneously enforce dense hierarchical spatial topology (e.g., pen inside holder on a book) and produce layouts that are physically more plausible than their supervision.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural language instruction (≤30 words), the tabletop's item_placement_zone boundaries in centimeters, and a list of objects with instance names as input. It outputs a hierarchical scene layout where each object is represented by a 6D AABB (x_min, x_max, y_min, y_max, z_min, z_max) plus yaw rotation, organized as a parent-child sequence with explicit relation types (in/on/near) and anchor-relative coordinates rather than absolute positions. The generation order is autoregressive: a base anchor is placed first, then horizontal neighbors expand outward, and container contents are recursively planned bottom-up.

## Method

PhyScene3D reformulates scene generation as a Human-Mimetic Constructive Process with two integrated modules. The Cognitive Topological Reasoning Chain (CTRC) imposes a structural inductive bias by linearizing a Hierarchical Scene Graph into an anchor-based sequential plan, using 3D AABB relative coordinates that decouple the z-axis to encode physical affordance (z_rel measured from parent's top for 'on', from parent's bottom for 'in'). The Physics-Aware Denoising Alignment (PADA) module addresses noisy supervision via a hybrid RL strategy: a GPU-resident differentiable SDF engine computes collision energy and provides gradients for Test-Time Optimization (TTO), which projects SFT model outputs onto a physics-feasible manifold while preserving semantic structure through hierarchy-constrained relative-coordinate penalties. These TTO-refined layouts serve as 'physically-projected semantic anchors' in a GRPO objective, combining a collision-based RL reward with a cross-entropy anchor term that prevents the semantic drift typical of pure reinforcement learning. Training proceeds in two stages: full-parameter SFT on CTRC-format sequences, followed by LoRA-adapted GRPO with SDF reward computation, enabling the VLM to internalize physical constraints and generate layouts that surpass the collision rate of its own training data.

## Results

On the MesaTask-CTRC benchmark, PhyScene3D achieves a Quality Pass Rate (QPR) of 46.5% at τ=7, nearly 3× the human-annotated Reference data (17.1%) and 2.2× the best LLM baseline GPT-4o (27.6%). The method reduces the Scene-wise Collision Rate to 41.6% (vs. 81.5% for Reference and 78.3% for MesaTask) and the Asset-wise Collision Rate to 3.86% (vs. 8.19% for both Reference and MesaTask), while maintaining the highest average GPT Score of 8.93. On the OOD benchmark, PhyScene3D (+CTRC&PADA) reaches 29.1% QPR(τ=7) and 2.92% ACR, substantially outperforming MesaTask (1.01% QPR, 19.01% ACR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PhyScene3D outperforms all baselines on the composite QPR metric and GPT Score, while also achieving the lowest Asset-wise Collision Rate (3.86%). Against the end-to-end regression baseline MesaTask, PhyScene3D more than doubles QPR(τ=7) (46.5% vs. 21.1%) and halves the ACR (3.86% vs. 8.19%), confirming that hierarchical planning plus physical denoising transcends the noisy supervision ceiling. Against zero-shot LLMs (GPT-4o, GPT-4o-mini), PhyScene3D leads on every metric, with the largest gap in PPI (9.91 vs. 9.54) and ACR (3.86% vs. 7.87%), indicating that even frontier LLMs lack fine-grained 3D spatial grounding. The agent-based solvers (Holodeck, I-Design) produce physically valid but semantically impoverished layouts: Holodeck achieves the lowest SCR (2.7%) and ACR (0.47%) but collapses on QPR (2.7%) and GPT Score (4.60), while I-Design is competitive on SCR (39.1% vs. 41.6%) yet trails PhyScene3D by 27.5 points on QPR and 2.4 points on GPT Score. Notably, PhyScene3D surpasses the human-annotated Reference data on QPR (46.5% vs. 17.1%), GPT Score (8.93 vs. 8.87), and both collision metrics, demonstrating that the model generates physically more plausible scenes than its own training supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from three existing paradigms for 3D scene generation: agent-based solvers (Holodeck, I-Design) that suffer from a symbolic bottleneck, image-mediated pipelines with error accumulation, and end-to-end regression models (MesaTask) that blindly replicate noisy supervision. Its core conceptual departure is reformulating generation as a sequential, anchor-conditioned constructive process (CTRC) rather than a single-shot distribution-matching task, and injecting a differentiable SDF physics engine directly into the VLM's reinforcement learning loop (PADA) to enable the model to transcend the physical quality of its training data. The work builds on Qwen-3 VL 8B as a backbone, GRPO for policy optimization, and the MesaTask-10k dataset, but introduces the hierarchical scene graph with AABB-relative parameterization and TTO-refined semantic anchoring as new architectural and training components not present in prior tabletop generation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability of a generative model to produce scenes that are physically more plausible than its own training data (41.6% vs. 81.5% scene-wise collision) is a meaningful step toward scalable, high-fidelity simulation environments for robotic learning, where manual scene construction is infeasible. The TTO-refined anchor strategy for preventing semantic drift during physics-aware RL is a reusable technique applicable beyond tabletop scenes. However, the 41.6% residual scene-wise collision rate, the reliance on AABB approximations for irregular shapes, and evaluation on a single 8B backbone limit the generality of the claims; open questions include whether the differentiable-physics-in-RL paradigm scales to larger models, more complex multi-room scenes, and whether the sim-to-real gap for robotic manipulation is truly closed by these physically consistent layouts.
