## Problem

Inferring protein abundance from single-cell RNA data is a stochastic inverse problem, but existing deterministic regression and standard generative methods fail because they enforce rigid point-wise correspondences on heteroscedastic, over-dispersed counts. This collapses intrinsic biological uncertainty into geometric irregularities on the latent manifold, producing stiff vector fields that destabilize continuous-dynamics learning and yield over-smoothed or mode-collapsed predictions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265814/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single-cell RNA expression profile (top 1,000 highly variable genes, log-normalized, ~93–96% sparse) and a batch identifier as input, and produces a predicted protein abundance vector decoded from a 32-dimensional latent space via the ZINB VAE decoder. Inference requires multi-step ODE integration (dopri5, 50 steps) with classifier-free guidance (w=3.0), making it slower than regression baselines but producing full distributional samples rather than point estimates. All metrics are evaluated in log-normalized, per-protein z-scored space.

## Method

scChord is a two-stage framework. Stage 1 (Probabilistic Manifold Rectification) trains a VAE with a ZINB decoder that parameterizes protein counts via mean, gene-wise dispersion θ, and dropout probability π; the resulting 'Variance-Adaptive Gradient Rescaling' suppresses encoder updates in high-uncertainty regions (small θ or π→1), absorbing technical noise into distributional parameters and yielding a smooth latent manifold. Stage 2 (Consistency-Regularized OT-CFM) learns RNA-conditioned transport from a Gaussian prior to the rectified protein posterior using Optimal Transport Conditional Flow Matching with straight-line interpolation paths, augmented by a self-supervised consistency loss that masks a random fraction of expressed RNA genes and penalizes embedding divergence, plus Classifier-Free Guidance for controllable generation. The stages are trained sequentially rather than end-to-end to prevent the CFM loss from undoing noise absorption and to avoid moving-target drift in the flow regression.

## Results

On the standard GSE100866 benchmark (8,617 cells, 13 proteins), scChord achieves the best score on all five metrics: PCC-P 0.8655, PCC-C 0.9392, CMD-P 0.0029, CMD-C 0.0316, and RMSE 0.5384. On the large-scale GSE164378-3P atlas (161,764 cells, 228 proteins), scChord leads in PCC-P (0.5274), CMD-C (0.0033), and RMSE (0.7779), while trailing totalVI slightly on PCC-C (0.8934 vs. 0.9243) and sciPENN on CMD-P (0.1690 vs. 0.1678). The order-of-magnitude improvement in CMD-P over scVAEIT (0.0029 vs. 0.2543) indicates faithful recovery of protein co-expression structure.

## Baselines & Comparisons

scChord outperforms all eight baselines on every metric in the GSE100866 benchmark. The closest competitor is sciPENN (Lakkis et al., 2022), which trails by 0.035 in PCC-P (0.8305 vs. 0.8655) and 0.015 in RMSE (0.5538 vs. 0.5384), but is far behind on CMD-C (0.1960 vs. 0.0316), indicating scChord preserves cell-level correlation structure much better. scVAEIT (Du et al., 2022) achieves competitive PCC-P (0.8525) but suffers a 88× worse CMD-P (0.2543 vs. 0.0029), revealing poor protein co-expression recovery. totalVI (Gayoso et al., 2021) and Seurat (Hao et al., 2024) are substantially weaker across all metrics (RMSE 0.9779 and 0.9895 respectively). On the 161k-cell GSE164378-3P atlas, scChord still leads in RMSE (0.7779 vs. sciPENN 0.7840) and CMD-C (0.0033 vs. totalVI 0.0529), though totalVI edges ahead on PCC-C (0.9243 vs. 0.8934). In the leave-one-group-out generalization study on ECCITE-seq, scChord surpasses the foundation model scTranslator (Liu et al., 2025) on cell-type hold-out, confirming that manifold rectification captures transferable gene–protein dependencies beyond memorized cell identities.

## Relation to Prior Work

scChord sits at the intersection of three research threads: probabilistic count modeling for single-cell multi-omics (totalVI's ZINB likelihood, scVAEIT), optimal-transport conditional flow matching (Tong et al., 2023), and cross-modal RNA-to-protein translation (cTP-net, sciPENN, scTranslator). Its key departure is a geometric diagnosis: deterministic reconstruction losses force encoders to embed heteroscedastic measurement noise into the latent space, producing rough manifolds whose induced vector fields are too stiff for stable ODE-based flow learning. Rather than treating the ZINB decoder as a mere reconstruction component (as in totalVI), scChord repurposes it as a *geometric preconditioner*—absorbing over-dispersion and dropout into distributional parameters so that the downstream OT-CFM operates on a smoothed target manifold. The consistency-regularized RNA encoder and classifier-free guidance are borrowed from standard practice but are specifically motivated by the sparsity of scRNA-seq inputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled explanation for why naively applying flow matching or diffusion to single-cell proteomics underperforms regression baselines, and offers a concrete two-stage remedy that is empirically validated through NFE, encoder-sensitivity, and manifold-interpolation analyses. However, the performance gains over strong baselines (sciPENN, scVAEIT) are modest—particularly on the 161k-cell GSE164378-3P dataset where PCC-P margins are under 0.01—and the authors acknowledge that CMD-P degrades as protein dimensionality grows, pointing to an open need for pathway-informed or hierarchical structural priors. Inference latency from multi-step ODE integration remains a practical bottleneck relative to single-pass regression methods. The framework is most impactful as a conceptual template: decoupling noise absorption from transport learning as a general strategy for applying continuous dynamics to discrete, over-dispersed biological data.
