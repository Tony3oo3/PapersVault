## Problem

Parallel thinking improves LLM reasoning by exploring multiple trajectories simultaneously, but existing efficiency methods rely on local per-trajectory signals or sequential control loops that ignore global cross-branch dynamics such as consensus and divergence. This leads to suboptimal resource allocation, redundant long-tail trajectories, and increased latency that cancels out the hardware parallelism advantage. The paper asks whether lightweight global signals can enable principled, hardware-friendly control of online parallel thinking.

## Input / Output

The method takes a reasoning problem as a text prompt and produces a final answer determined by majority voting over parallel branches. During generation, a 2D probing interface periodically appends a termination-triggering sequence (e.g., ` The final answer is`) to each branch's current prefix at fixed token intervals, eliciting intermediate answers that form an N×T matrix indexed by branch (width) and probing step (depth). The final output is the stable consensus answer when early stopping triggers, or the majority vote among surviving branches at maximum budget.

## Method

Parallel-Probe is a training-free online controller built on 2D probing, which periodically forces all N parallel branches to emit intermediate answers, constructing a structured matrix that exposes width-depth dynamics. Two complementary mechanisms regulate the ensemble: (1) consensus-based early stopping monitors the column-wise majority vote and halts all branches once the consensus remains invariant for u consecutive probing steps, reclaiming compute wasted on long-tail trajectories; (2) deviation-based branch pruning deactivates any branch whose output consistently diverges from the global consensus within a lookback window of k steps, dynamically reducing effective width. A warmup stage of W steps suppresses both mechanisms to preserve early reasoning diversity. The approach is model-agnostic and requires no fine-tuning, operating purely on black-box intermediate answer signals.

## Results

Across four Qwen3 model sizes (0.6B–8B) and three benchmarks (AIME24, AIME25, HMMT25), Parallel-Probe reduces sequential tokens by 31.3–35.8% and total tokens by 22.2–25.8% relative to SC@64 while maintaining or slightly improving accuracy (e.g., 66.8% on Qwen3-4B avg, matching SC@64's 66.8%; 23.3% on Qwen3-0.6B vs. 22.8% for SC@64). On Qwen3-8B, Parallel-Probe achieves 68.5% accuracy with 21.6k sequential tokens and 824.9k total tokens, versus SC@64's 68.6% / 31.9k / 1100.7k. Online wall-clock latency on AIME25 confirms practical gains: Parallel-Probe runs in 113.2 s/problem (0.6B), 155.8 s (4B), and 196.9 s (8B) versus 170.0 s, 260.2 s, and 273.1 s for SC@64 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SC@64 (standard self-consistency with 64 branches), Parallel-Probe reduces sequential tokens by 31–36% and total tokens by 22–26% across all four model sizes while matching or slightly exceeding accuracy (e.g., +0.5 pp on 0.6B, +0.1 pp on 4B). Against ASC (Adaptive Self-Consistency), which sequentially samples until a 0.95 consensus threshold, Parallel-Probe wins decisively on sequential tokens (e.g., 20.3k vs. 680.0k on 0.6B, a 33× reduction) while also using fewer total tokens (735.3k vs. 680.0k is close, but on 1.7B: 794.8k vs. 556.6k — here ASC uses fewer total tokens but at 26× the sequential cost). Against ESC (Early Stopping Consistency, chunk-based hybrid), Parallel-Probe achieves lower sequential tokens (20.9k vs. 136.6k on 4B) and comparable or lower total tokens while preserving accuracy. Against SC@64+SAC (per-trajectory answer-convergence early stopping), Parallel-Probe maintains accuracy (68.5% vs. 63.2% on 8B; 66.8% vs. 65.1% on 4B) while achieving larger token reductions (−25.1% vs. −19.2% total tokens on 8B), demonstrating that global cross-branch signals outperform local per-trajectory stopping in parallel settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of efficient test-time scaling work (adaptive self-consistency, early-stopping consistency, per-trajectory answer convergence) by arguing that local, per-branch signals are fundamentally insufficient for controlling parallel thinking. It repurposes the simple idea of injecting a termination token to elicit intermediate answers—previously used as a diagnostic or confidence signal—into a structured 2D probing matrix that exposes cross-branch width-depth dynamics. The resulting Parallel-Probe controller (consensus-based early stopping + deviation-based pruning) is positioned as the first training-free, fully parallel online controller that jointly regulates both dimensions using global signals, rather than the sequential control loops used by ASC/ESC or the per-trajectory stopping used by SAC.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a training-free, model-agnostic method that cuts sequential tokens by ~30–36% and total tokens by ~20–26% while matching or slightly exceeding SC@64 accuracy across four model scales and multiple benchmarks, with confirmed wall-clock latency gains on H200 GPUs. The SCOUT testbed also provides a reusable offline evaluation harness that decouples generation from strategy exploration, lowering the barrier to studying width-depth trade-offs. Open questions include whether the approach extends to larger frontier models (≥70B), non-mathematical reasoning tasks, and whether learned controllers could outperform the hand-designed consensus/pruning rules; the paper also notes that probing adds a small per-step overhead that was not fully characterized at scale.
