## Problem

Temporal-difference value learning with function approximation is prone to divergence and oscillation, and existing geometric remedies (symmetry encoding, data augmentation, order restrictions) lack a unified framework with rigorous convergence guarantees. No prior work systematically exploits the order-theoretic structure of value functions—specifically, the poset of state-action pairs under a minimal partial order sufficient to determine optimal actions—while simultaneously handling symmetry-induced antisymmetry violations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard off-policy RL inputs: state observations in grid, RGB-pixel, or tabular form, paired with actions and TD targets from a replay buffer. Beyond the usual Q-value estimates and optimal action, it outputs two structural artifacts: a bank of learned near-orthogonal feature transforms with Sinkhorn-projected action permutation matrices (approximating MDP automorphisms), and a batchwise directed acyclic graph whose reachability relation defines the current refinement of the value poset. The quotient set X/~ (state-action pairs modulo learned symmetry orbits) serves as the ground set on which the partial order is maintained.

## Method

GCR-RL recasts value learning as constructing a sequence of super-poset refinements on symmetry-quotiented elements, where each refinement adds TD-evidenced order relations while preserving reflexivity, transitivity, and antisymmetry. Antisymmetry is enforced by learning a small bank of near-automorphisms (orthogonal feature transforms + action relabelings via Sinkhorn) and quotienting orbits before imposing order constraints; the logic-order branch scores TD nudges with confidence weights, greedily DAGifies candidate comparisons to remove cycles, and applies a differentiable isotonic projection onto the resulting monotone cone. Two enforcement routes are provided: a soft route that adds equivariance-consistency and isotonic-alignment regularizers to the standard TD loss, and a hard (0-loss) route that treats the batchwise constraint set as a manifold-with-corners and performs a single tangent-normal decomposition step per iteration (projected TD descent along the tangent space plus minimum-norm normal contraction to eliminate violations). Theoretical guarantees include Bellman residual decrease under order alignment, variance reduction from symmetry sharing (effective sample amplification by 1+(K−1)ρ), identifiability of the learned equivariance up to isomorphism, and a joint convergence rate of O(√(ℜ(N)/N)) for both equivariance residual and order violation.

## Results

GCR-RL achieves the best final return across all nine evaluated environments. On symmetry-rich tasks, gains are largest: E1 RotMirror-Grid reaches −136.7 ± 7.1 (vs. −164.6 for the best baseline) and E2 ButtonPermutation-Minigrid reaches 3.70 ± 0.00 (vs. 1.60 ± 2.70). On Atari, GCR-RL improves final return by 4× on Pong (−1.00 vs. −4.00), 1.5× on Asterix (368.2 vs. 249.2), and 1.7× on Space Invaders (101.5 vs. 59.5) relative to the strongest non-ours baseline. On the cyclic-order task E4, GCR-RL achieves 21.60 ± 0.20 with the lowest variance across seeds, confirming that DAGification suppresses non-transitive oscillations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares GCR-RL against two strong off-policy baselines: Rainbow (Hessel et al., 2018) and DrQ-v2 (Yarats et al., 2021). GCR-RL outperforms Rainbow on final return in every environment, with the largest margins on symmetry-rich tasks (E1: −136.7 vs. −190.3; E2: 3.70 vs. 1.60) and on visually heterogeneous Atari titles (Asterix: 368.2 vs. 161.6; Space Invaders: 101.5 vs. 59.5). Against DrQ-v2, GCR-RL also wins across the board, most dramatically on E2 ButtonPermutation (3.70 vs. −5.40) and Space Invaders (101.5 vs. 27.8), where DrQ-v2's augmentation-based invariance fails to capture the required action-relabeling equivariance. On the cyclic-order task E4, both baselines achieve similar final returns (~20.7), but GCR-RL reaches threshold 2× faster (36 vs. 672 steps for DrQ-v2) and exhibits the tightest across-seed variance (±0.20 vs. ±13.8 for DrQ-v2), confirming that the logic-order branch specifically suppresses non-transitive oscillations rather than merely improving asymptotic reward.

## Relation to Prior Work

GCR-RL sits at the intersection of three prior threads: group-equivariant architectures (Cohen & Welling, Kondor & Trivedi) that hard-code known symmetry groups, data-augmentation RL (RAD, DrQ, CURL) that transforms states without coupled action relabeling, and isotonic/monotone regression (Barlow) embedded via differentiable optimization layers (Amos & Kolter, Agrawal et al.). The paper departs from all three by learning near-automorphisms with action permutations end-to-end from TD signals and by constructing a batchwise DAG from TD preferences, then unifying both under a single order-theoretic (poset) framework where value learning is recast as a sequence of super-poset refinements on a symmetry-quotiented ground set. Unlike bisimulation-metric or quasimetric approaches that ask whether two states are close, GCR-RL learns a directed order relation and enforces antisymmetry via quotienting, which the authors argue is a fundamentally different geometric constraint.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The poset reframing provides a principled, unified lens connecting symmetry discovery and order enforcement in RL, with convergence-rate guarantees (O(√(ℜ(N)/N))) and oracle speedup bounds that clarify when structural regularization helps. Practically, the method improves sample efficiency and stability across tabular, grid, RGB, and pixel-based benchmarks, but its gains are most pronounced when exploitable symmetries or reliable directed order evidence exist; when both are weak, the structural losses risk adding bias. Open questions include scaling online DAG construction to large state spaces, extending to continuous-control actor-critic settings, and determining whether the learned poset structure can be leveraged for exploration or planning beyond value regularization.
