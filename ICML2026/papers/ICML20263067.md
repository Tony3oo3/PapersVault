## Problem

Standard Rectified Flow for precipitation nowcasting parameterizes the network to regress a flow-space velocity field, but small velocity estimation errors compound during recursive ODE integration, driving the sampling trajectory off the valid radar data manifold and causing progressive morphological distortion at extended lead times. Existing generative nowcasters address over-smoothing but leave this numerical instability of the transport ODE unaddressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263067/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 5 historical radar reflectivity frames (resized to 128×128, linearly normalized to [0,1]) and generates a 20-frame future sequence at the same resolution (CIKM uses a 5→10 setting for a 60-minute horizon). At inference, the future sequence is produced via a fixed 5-step stabilized Euler ODE sampler starting from Gaussian noise, with the final output being the network's clean-data prediction at the terminal time step rather than the raw ODE state.

## Method

PDRF re-parameterizes the conditional Rectified Flow ODE in data space: the U-KAN backbone predicts the clean future sequence X̂₁ rather than the velocity, and the velocity is analytically induced as (X̂₁ − Z_t)/t̄, which introduces an implicit restoring force (gain 1/t̄) that damps off-manifold perturbations during integration. A Semi-Lagrangian advection prior—constructed via Farneback optical flow backtracing from the latest observation—is mapped into the same induced-velocity space and used as a soft regularization target (L_phy), constraining large-scale transport coherence while leaving local growth/decay/deformation to be learned from data. The architecture integrates Condition-Guided Spatial Transform Fusion (learned displacement warping for feature alignment), Wavelet-Guided Skip Connections (DWT-based frequency-aware gating), a Feature Communication Module (bidirectional cross-scale attention), and KAN-VRWKV blocks for linear-time long-range temporal mixing at deep stages.

## Results

PDRF achieves the best CSI-M across all four benchmarks: 0.3622 on SEVIR, 0.4386 on MeteoNet, 0.4409 on Shanghai, and 0.3329 on CIKM, outperforming the strongest generative baseline PercpCast by 1.3–5.5% relative. It also leads on HSS (e.g., 0.5747 on MeteoNet vs. 0.5436 for PercpCast) and SSIM (e.g., 0.8385 on MeteoNet vs. 0.8266 for PercpCast), while using only 27.15M parameters and 28.63 GFLOPs—roughly 2× fewer parameters and 10× fewer FLOPs than the next-lightest generative model (PercpCast: 55.87M / 324.93 GFLOPs). The only consistent trade-off is slightly higher MSE than deterministic methods on SEVIR and Shanghai, reflecting the sharper, higher-variance outputs of the generative formulation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PDRF outperforms all 12 baselines on CSI-M, HSS, and SSIM across every dataset. Against the closest generative competitor PercpCast (ICML 2025), PDRF gains +1.3% CSI-M on SEVIR (0.3622 vs 0.3492), +8.6% on MeteoNet (0.4386 vs 0.4038), +10.4% on Shanghai (0.4409 vs 0.3995), and +5.3% on CIKM (0.3329 vs 0.3162), while also improving SSIM by 0.2–1.9 percentage points. Versus the strongest deterministic baseline AlphaPre (CVPR 2025), PDRF leads by +11.2% CSI-M on SEVIR and +5.3% on MeteoNet, but AlphaPre achieves lower MSE on Shanghai (28.02 vs 30.02) and CIKM (35.18 vs 37.69), reflecting the expected trade-off of sharper generative outputs. Against classical optical-flow extrapolation (pySTEPS), PDRF improves CSI-M by 28–17% across datasets, confirming it captures nonlinear intensity evolution beyond simple advection. Among diffusion-based methods, PDRF surpasses DiffCast (CVPR 2024) by +18.8% CSI-M on SEVIR and +24.7% on MeteoNet, while using ~1070× fewer GFLOPs (28.6 vs 30616.4). The only metric where PDRF is not best is MSE on SEVIR, where deterministic methods like AlphaPre (345.18) and MAU (354.01) achieve lower pixel-wise error due to their regression-to-the-mean behaviour.

## Relation to Prior Work

PDRF extends Rectified Flow (Liu et al., 2022) to conditional precipitation nowcasting by re-parameterizing the generative ODE from velocity-space to data-space, drawing on the x-pred insight from Li & He (2025) for image generation and reframing it as a geometric stabilizer against off-manifold drift in dynamical systems. It departs from prior generative nowcasters (DiffCast, PercpCast) that inherit trajectory-level drift from iterative ODE integration, and from physics-informed deterministic models (PhyDNet, AlphaPre) that impose rigid constraints, by introducing a soft Semi-Lagrangian advection regularizer that constrains large-scale transport while leaving local nonlinear evolution to data. The architecture (U-KAN backbone with VRWKV, CGSTF, WGSC, FCM) is a purpose-built instantiation rather than a direct adaptation of any single prior design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The data-space parameterization with its analytically induced restoring force offers a general recipe for stabilizing ODE-based generative models in any high-dimensional physical forecasting setting, not just radar nowcasting, and the 5-step inference at 28.6 GFLOPs is orders of magnitude cheaper than diffusion-based alternatives. However, the model remains radar-only (no wind, humidity, or thermodynamic variables), the Semi-Lagrangian prior is a lightweight transport scaffold rather than a full dynamical simulator, and the performance gains over the strongest baseline (PercpCast) are consistent but moderate. Open questions include whether the parameterization advantage persists under multi-modal conditioning, whether the restoring-force analysis holds for non-linear (non-rectified) flow paths, and how the framework extends to 3D volumetric or multi-sensor inputs.
