## Problem

Model-free RL algorithms like TD3 and SAC collapse on high-dimensional continuous control tasks (e.g., 38-D Dog Run) because value functions trained from sparse point samples must extrapolate over continuous action manifolds, causing multiplicative error amplification through the learning pipeline. Existing stabilization techniques (target networks, clipped double Q, entropy regularization) are heuristic and lack a unified geometric explanation for why they work in some environments but not others.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a continuous environment state vector (e.g., 223-D for Dog) and outputs a continuous action vector (e.g., 38-D). The key modification is in how the Q-function is queried: instead of a single point evaluation Q(s', π(s')), the method evaluates Q at K=8 orthogonally-sampled points in an ε-neighborhood (ε=0.2×a_scale) around the target policy output, then averages the results. The orthogonal directions are generated via QR decomposition of a random Gaussian matrix, ensuring unit-norm, mutually perpendicular perturbation vectors that maximize local manifold coverage.

## Method

Action Manifold Smoothing (AMS) replaces pointwise TD targets with neighborhood-averaged targets (Neighborhood TD, NTD), which the paper proves is equivalent to implicit Laplacian smoothing of the Q-landscape: the expected NTD target contains a correction term (ε²/2d_a)·Δ_a Q that suppresses spurious peaks and fills valleys. Orthogonal sampling via QR decomposition ensures the K perturbation directions span a maximal-volume subspace with zero cross-covariance, improving estimator efficiency over i.i.d. Gaussian noise. A second component, LayerNorm in the Q-network (LN-Q), provides an implicit Lipschitz bound on Q-network curvature. Together, NTD regularizes the TD target stage (L4) while LN-Q constrains Q-network smoothness (L3), jointly controlling the two downstream stages of the proposed Lipschitz pathway (L1→L2→L3→L4) where errors compound multiplicatively. The framework is algorithm-agnostic, requiring only a change to the TD target computation, and is validated on both TD3 and SAC across 12 locomotion tasks.

## Results

On the 38-dimensional Dog-Run task, AMS-SAC achieves 427±25 mean episodic return within 1M steps, compared to 92±121 for TD3 and 45±95 for SAC, representing a 9.5× improvement over TD3. On Quadruped-Run (12D), AMS-SAC reaches 781±61 versus 576±212 (TD3) and 301±8 (SAC), a 2.6× gain. On H1-Sit (HumanoidBench), AMS-TD3 achieves 855±22 versus 527±476 for vanilla TD3, a 62% mean improvement with >20× variance reduction. On lower-dimensional tasks like Cheetah-Run (6D), AMS variants match or slightly exceed baselines (757±31 for AMS-SAC vs. 753±27 for TD3), confirming no performance regression where the discrete-continuous mismatch is less severe.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AMS decisively outperforms both TD3 and SAC on all high-dimensional tasks (≥12D actions). On Dog-Run (38D), AMS-SAC (427±25) surpasses TD3 (92±121) by 4.6× and SAC (45±95) by 9.5×; on Dog-Walk, the gap is 7.4× over TD3. On Quadruped-Run, AMS-SAC (781±61) beats TD3 (576±212) by 36% and SAC (301±8) by 2.6×, while also reducing inter-seed variance by 6.6×. On HumanoidBench, AMS-TD3 dominates H1-Sit (855±22 vs. 527±476) and H1-Slide (577±141 vs. 315±92 for TD3), though TD3 retains a slight edge on H1-Walk (787 vs. 775) and H1-Run (658 vs. 614/625), consistent with the paper's claim that L3-4 smoothing helps most when downstream geometry is the limiting factor. PPO is uniformly weakest across all tasks, particularly collapsing on Humanoid-Run (1±0) and H1-Run (9±2). On low-dimensional Cheetah-Run, AMS-SAC (757±31) roughly matches TD3 (753±27), confirming no regression where the discrete-continuous mismatch is mild.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies a scattered collection of stabilization techniques (target networks, clipped double Q, spectral normalization, policy smoothing, ensembles) under a single Lipschitz pathway framework (L1–L4) that decomposes error amplification along the actor-critic learning pipeline. It builds directly on TD3 and SAC as base algorithms and draws on geometric perspectives in RL (Bellemare et al., 2019) and Lipschitz-aware training (Gogianu et al., 2021), but departs from prior work by arguing these techniques are heuristic and lack a shared geometric grounding. The key conceptual departure is showing that SAC's entropy regularization constrains only L1 (policy diversity) while leaving L3 (Q-landscape curvature) uncontrolled, and that a simple neighborhood-averaging mechanism (NTD) jointly regularizes L3 and L4 via an implicit Laplacian effect—a connection not previously made explicit in the actor-critic literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Lipschitz pathway provides a diagnostic lens for practitioners to identify which stage (action geometry, dynamics sensitivity, Q-curvature, or TD-target stability) is the bottleneck in a given task, potentially reducing the trial-and-error that currently dominates deep RL tuning. The practical impact is significant: AMS enables both TD3 and SAC to solve the 38-dimensional Dog Run task where vanilla algorithms fail entirely, with a modest ~25–30% computational overhead. Open questions include whether the fixed isotropic neighborhood kernel can be replaced by anisotropic, curvature-adaptive smoothing, and how L1–L4 constraints interact across different action dimensionalities—a unified theoretical treatment remains absent. The duality with model-based methods (Dreamer smooths L2, AMS smooths L3–4) suggests a broader principle that the discrete-continuous mismatch can be attacked from either end of the pipeline, but a formal comparison of the two strategies under matched compute budgets is left for future work.
