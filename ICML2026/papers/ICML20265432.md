## Problem

Existing 3DGS style transfer methods face a fundamental trade-off: VGG- and CLIP-based approaches cannot model multi-view consistency within the model, while diffusion-based methods that do capture such consistency rely on predicted denoising directions, leading to unstable training and over-smoothing artifacts. No prior method achieves both stable optimization and explicit cross-view correspondence modeling for 3D stylization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265432/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained 3DGS scene (with frozen geometry), a single style image, and per-view camera intrinsics/extrinsics with depth maps. During test-time optimization, N=4 rendered views per batch are fed through Stable Diffusion 1.5's VAE encoder and UNet at a fixed timestep t=1; only the spherical harmonic color coefficients are updated (learning rates 0.02 for basis, 0.000167 for higher-order terms) over 600 iterations. The output is a stylized 3DGS scene renderable from arbitrary viewpoints.

## Method

DiffStyle3D introduces an Attention-Aware Loss that performs style transfer via direct latent-space optimization rather than following denoising directions. The style loss injects keys and values from the style image into the rendered image's queries within self-attention, while the content loss aligns attention outputs between the content and rendered images; both are computed on centered and normalized features to emphasize directional consistency over absolute magnitudes. For multi-view consistency, the method computes explicit geometric correspondences via back-projection/re-projection using known camera parameters and fixed depth maps, then warps K and V from all other views in the batch into each view's self-attention (Geometry-Guided Attention) with visibility masks to handle occlusions. A geometry-aware mask further restricts the loss to non-overlapping pixel regions across views, preventing redundant optimization that could disrupt cross-view coherence.

## Results

On scene-level stylization (8 scenes × 14 styles), DiffStyle3D achieves the best content preservation (CLIP-C 0.71), the highest multi-view consistency (CLIP-CONS 0.128, short-range LPIPS 0.075, long-range LPIPS 0.141), and the lowest FID (204.8) among all compared methods. On object-level stylization (10 objects × 14 styles), it attains the best CLIP-C (0.84) and the fastest training time (~4 min per instance). The method matches or leads on most metrics while CLIPGaussian scores higher on CLIP-S (0.79 vs. 0.65 scene-level), which the authors attribute to content leakage rather than genuine style alignment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against StyleGaussian (VGG-based), DiffStyle3D improves CLIP-C from 0.61 to 0.71, CLIP-CONS from 0.032 to 0.128, and FID from 334.9 to 204.8, while also reducing training time from ~21 to ~16 min. Against SGSST (VGG-based, multi-scale), DiffStyle3D wins on CLIP-C (0.71 vs. 0.64), CLIP-CONS (0.128 vs. 0.066), FID (204.8 vs. 289.1), and both short- and long-range consistency metrics, with a 2.5× speedup (~16 vs. ~40 min); SGSST edges out slightly on S_vgg (20.60 vs. 20.63). Against FantasyStyle (diffusion-based, IP-Adapter), DiffStyle3D leads on CLIP-C (0.71 vs. 0.67), CLIP-CONS (0.128 vs. 0.084), FID (204.8 vs. 228.6), and all consistency metrics, while training 2× faster (~16 vs. ~34 min). Against CLIPGaussian (CLIP-based), DiffStyle3D achieves substantially better content preservation (CLIP-C 0.71 vs. 0.63), consistency (CLIP-CONS 0.128 vs. 0.058), and FID (204.8 vs. 280.7), though CLIPGaussian scores higher on CLIP-S (0.79 vs. 0.65)—a gap the authors attribute to CLIPGaussian's tendency to leak semantic content (e.g., cat faces, human eyes) from the style image rather than genuinely transferring style. On object-level, DiffStyle3D is the fastest method (~4 min vs. 5–13 min for baselines) and achieves the best CLIP-C (0.84) and FID (255.8), while CLIPGaussian retains a lead on CLIP-CONS (0.504 vs. 0.412).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) and latent diffusion models (Rombach et al., 2022), departing from both VGG/CLIP-based 3DGS stylization methods (which struggle with multi-view consistency) and prior diffusion-based 3D stylization (FantasyStyle) that relies on unstable denoising-direction gradients. The key conceptual departure is replacing denoising guidance with direct self-attention feature alignment in the diffusion latent space, and augmenting self-attention with explicit geometric warping (Geometry-Guided Attention) to enforce cross-view correspondences — a mechanism borrowed from multi-view geometry but applied inside the attention computation of a frozen diffusion model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The geometry-guided attention mechanism offers a clean, generalizable recipe for injecting explicit 3D geometric constraints into diffusion-model attention, which could transfer to other multi-view 3D generation or editing tasks. However, the method is limited to color-only optimization (frozen geometry), cannot handle styles requiring geometric deformation, and is tied to the SD1.5 architecture, making extension to modern diffusion backbones (SD3, FLUX) an open question. The per-scene test-time optimization paradigm also limits scalability to large-scale 3D asset pipelines.
