## Problem

Existing implicit in-context learning methods inject task-specific shift vectors into residual streams, which are inherently tied to particular tasks and fail to generalize to out-of-domain settings. The paper asks whether a universal, cross-task ICL pattern exists within LLM attention that can be extracted once and reused for zero-shot inference on arbitrary new tasks without retrieval or retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262474/cropped_page2_idx4.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, ICR takes a standard zero-shot text prompt (no demonstrations) and a mean-pooled MiniLM sentence embedding of the query. The embedding feeds a small two-branch router that produces per-layer routing vectors (α ∈ R^{L×r}) and per-head gates (γ ∈ R^{L×H}), which are applied only to the last one-third of LLM layers. The output is a predicted class label, but the mechanism operates by adding a low-rank bias to attention logits before the softmax, so the model's internal attention distribution is reshaped rather than its residual activations. Pre-extracted PIDs (2rdL parameters) are cached and frozen; only the router (~a few thousand parameters) is trained.

## Method

ICR operates in three stages. First, it performs explicit multi-domain ICL across five datasets, collects last-token Q/K projections at every layer, and applies PCA to extract Principal ICL Directions (PIDs) — low-rank subspaces U_q^l, U_k^l ∈ R^{d×r} that capture shared attention structure across tasks. A spiked covariance model analysis shows that pooling across domains amplifies the shared ICL signal while domain-specific components average toward isotropy, so PCA reliably recovers the general pattern. Second, a query-conditioned router (two 2-layer MLPs) maps the MiniLM embedding to a routing vector α(x) and head gates γ(x), enabling input-adaptive modulation. Third, during zero-shot inference, ICR adds a low-rank bias ΔA^l = (Q_zs^l U_q^l) diag(α^l(x)) (K_zs^l U_k^l)^T to the attention logits of each head, scaled by γ^{l,h}(x), effectively reparameterizing the attention kernel along PID directions. Training updates only the router with a combined objective of cross-entropy, confidence alignment (preventing entropy inflation), and layer-weighted L1 sparsity on α and γ, while the LLM backbone remains fully frozen.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-7B, ICR achieves an overall average accuracy of 80.2% across 12 datasets (5 in-domain, 3 near-OOD, 4 far-OOD), surpassing few-shot prompting (77.5%) by +2.7% and the best implicit ICL baseline M2IV (76.2%) by +4.0%, with zero collapses below zero-shot. On Llama2-7B, ICR reaches 59.9% average, outperforming M2IV (56.9%), LIVE (56.6%), and I2CL (55.0%), again with 0 collapses versus 2 for the other baselines. ICR also matches or exceeds few-shot prompting on in-domain tasks (e.g., 86.6% on AGNews vs. 81.0% for 5-shot on Llama2-7B) while requiring no explicit demonstrations at inference time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ICR consistently outperforms all three training-based implicit ICL baselines (I2CL, LIVE, M2IV) across every dataset on both Llama2-7B and Qwen2.5-7B, with the largest gaps on OOD tasks (e.g., +18.2% on MR and +9.8% on SST-5 over M2IV on Qwen2.5-7B). Critically, ICR achieves 0 collapses (never below zero-shot) while I2CL, LIVE, and M2IV suffer 2–4 collapses each, indicating that vector-based residual injection degrades performance on certain OOD tasks. Against the task-specific vector baselines evaluated only in-domain (TV, FV, ICV, ELICIT, IV), ICR leads by 4.5–7.5% average accuracy on Qwen2.5-7B (81.2% vs. 73.7–76.7%), confirming that attention-logit routing captures more generalizable ICL structure than additive hidden-state shifts. Compared to LoRA (rank 32 on the classification head), ICR uses 2.1× fewer trainable parameters and achieves comparable in-domain accuracy (80.2% vs. 80.1% on Qwen2.5-7B) while clearly winning on far-OOD tasks (e.g., +4.6% on CREAK, +1.7% on AI2SciE). On Llama3.1-8B, ICR again surpasses all baselines with 0 collapses versus 5–8 for I2CL/LIVE/M2IV, and on the larger Qwen3-32B and Llama3.1-70B models it maintains its lead over FV and I2CL, validating scale robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant vector-based implicit ICL paradigm (Task Vectors, Function Vectors, In-Context Vectors, I2CL, LIVE, M2IV), which injects additive shift vectors into residual streams post-hoc, by proposing 'attention routing'—modulating attention logits directly via low-rank directions extracted from multi-domain ICL. It builds on the established understanding that multi-head attention is the core mechanism of ICL (Olsson et al., 2022; Todd et al., 2023) and repurposes the spiked covariance model from random matrix theory to provide a theoretical justification for why PCA over pooled multi-domain Q/K projections recovers a domain-stable ICL subspace. The query-conditioned router (a small MLP over a frozen MiniLM embedding) is a lightweight adaptation mechanism distinct from PEFT approaches like LoRA, which the paper benchmarks against.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The train-once-reuse-everywhere property—requiring no per-task retrieval, calibration, or retraining—addresses a key practical bottleneck of existing implicit ICL methods, and the 0-collapse OOD result across 7 diverse datasets and multiple model scales (7B–70B) is a meaningful robustness claim. However, the method is currently validated only on classification-style NLP tasks with relatively short inputs, and the PIDs extraction step still requires upfront multi-domain ICL data collection, limiting its applicability to open-ended generation or truly novel task families. Open questions include whether the attention-routing paradigm extends to multimodal settings, longer-context tasks, or autoregressive generation, and whether the PCA-based extraction can be replaced by learned or adaptive directions to reduce the dependency on a fixed multi-domain corpus.
