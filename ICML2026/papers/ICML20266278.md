## Problem

In offline multi-objective optimization, surrogate models trained on a fixed dataset suffer from out-of-distribution (OOD) errors that cause the optimizer to select solutions biased toward Pareto front extremes, severely degrading both diversity and convergence. Existing OOD mitigation techniques from single-objective offline optimization cannot be directly transferred to MOO because surrogate overestimation in one objective can artificially dominate others under Pareto dominance, collapsing solution diversity in ways that per-objective conservatism does not address.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a fixed offline dataset of solution-objective pairs (no active evaluations permitted) and a set of preference vectors sampled from the M-dimensional simplex. It produces a final set of 256 Pareto-optimal solutions selected via a two-stage diversity-driven strategy, along with a trained Pareto set model h_φ* that maps any valid preference to a corresponding Pareto solution. A novel IGD_offline indicator replaces the unavailable true Pareto front with a min-max-normalized, shifted offline reference to enable diversity evaluation without active queries.

## Method

DOMOO proposes Nested Pareto Set Learning (NPSL) with accumulative risk control: an energy model trained via contrastive divergence with Langevin dynamics computes a risk suppression factor R(x) that is embedded directly into the preference gradient update (inner loop), while the Pareto set model is trained via standard gradient descent under these risk-guided preferences (outer loop). Training proceeds in three phases—pretraining on offline Pareto front preferences, exploration via Dirichlet sampling, and risk-modulated preference gradient descent—allowing the model to adapt to diverse Pareto front geometries. For final solution selection, DOMOO introduces IGD_offline (a shifted, normalized offline reference distance) for greedy diversity screening of 128 candidates, followed by hypervolume-based filling of the remaining 128, explicitly countering HV's known bias toward extreme solutions in offline settings.

## Results

On Off-MOO-Bench, DOMOO achieves the best average HV rank of 4.63±0.38 across all five task categories (Synthetic, MO-NAS, MORL, Sci-Design, RE), outperforming the next-best methods Multiple Models+IOM (6.41±0.68) and End-to-End (6.81±0.54). On the diversity metric IGD_offline, DOMOO also achieves the best average rank of 6.27±0.23, ahead of Multiple Models+IOM (6.63±0.52) and End-to-End (7.12±1.02). The offline dataset baseline D(best) ranks 12.73 on HV and 9.71 on IGD_offline, confirming that learned surrogates substantially outperform simple dataset selection.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DOMOO outperforms all DNN-based baselines (End-to-End, Multi Head, Multiple Models and their OOD-mitigated variants) on average HV rank, with the largest margins over Multiple Models+COMs (8.30 vs. 4.63) and Multiple Models+Tri-Mentoring (8.93 vs. 4.63). Among GP-based methods, MOBO (qNEHVI) is the strongest baseline at 7.62 average rank but still trails DOMOO by ~3 ranks, while MOBO-JES is the worst overall (13.91) and fails on most tasks. The flow-based ParetoFlow (10.19) is also clearly outperformed. On the diversity metric IGD_offline, DOMOO (6.27) edges out Multiple Models+IOM (6.63) and End-to-End (7.12), while GP-based methods and ParetoFlow (8.20) show notably weaker diversity. DOMOO's advantage is most pronounced on Synthetic (HV rank 3.89) and RE (3.26) tasks, though it is slightly behind Multiple Models+IOM on MORL (3.60 vs. 3.50) and MO-NAS (6.65 vs. 5.96), which the authors attribute to the discrete one-hot encoding challenge in NAS subtasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

DOMOO extends the Pareto Set Learning (PSL) framework of Lin et al. (2022) to the offline setting by embedding ARCOO's (Lu et al., 2023) accumulative risk control into the preference gradient update, creating a nested optimization where the inner loop adapts preferences under risk modulation and the outer loop refines the preference-to-solution mapping. It departs from prior offline MOO methods (Xue et al., 2024) that apply OOD-mitigating surrogates (COMs, IOM, Tri-Mentoring) at the per-objective level, instead integrating risk suppression directly into the multi-objective preference dynamics. The proposed IGD_offline metric also extends the standard IGD by replacing the inaccessible true Pareto front with a shifted offline reference, addressing a gap in evaluation methodology for offline MOO.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: OOD-induced Pareto front distortion in offline MOO, where surrogate overestimation in one objective can collapse solution diversity under Pareto dominance. The IGD_offline metric is a useful contribution for the community, revealing that hypervolume can be misleading in offline settings (e.g., RE tasks where methods beat offline data in HV but score worse in IGD_offline). Open questions include extending the approach to highly discrete search spaces with high-cardinality one-hot encodings, providing formal calibration guarantees for the energy-based risk model, and scaling to higher-dimensional objective spaces where preference sampling becomes intractable.
