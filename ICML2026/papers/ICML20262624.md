## Problem

Infrared-visible video fusion suffers from a fundamental tension: frame-by-frame diffusion models produce temporal flickering, while optical-flow-based alignment introduces geometric rigidity and ghosting artifacts. Moreover, when diffusion models are extended to autoregressive video generation, they lack intrinsic temporal constraints and accumulate errors over time, causing severe drift where minor artifacts amplify across frames.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262624/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes paired infrared and visible video sequences and produces a single fused video sequence in a unified spatiotemporal latent space. During inference, a sliding history window of T=8 frames is maintained, and the 3D-DiT generates each frame conditioned on this stabilized history plus the current visible latent and infrared structural guidance. The fused output is decoded from the refined latent after 50 DDIM sampling steps, with latent refinement applied every 5 steps to sharpen thermal target edges.

## Method

DRFusion reformulates video fusion as a history-conditioned probabilistic motion generation process built on a frozen pre-trained 3D-DiT. A two-stage decoupled training strategy first fine-tunes a VQ-VAE with an occlusion-aware optical-flow regularization to build a temporally smooth latent space, then trains a lightweight Condition Adapter (with zero-initialized convolutions) to inject infrared structural priors into the frozen DiT without disturbing its motion generation capacity. At inference, Stabilized History Guidance constructs three parallel attention contexts—pure noise (unconditional), minimally perturbed history (low-pass filtered), and single-frame context (short-term)—and composes their velocity estimates via a linear combination that subtracts local flicker from spectrally stabilized motion, effectively treating temporal consistency as a spectral filtering problem. A gradient-guided Latent Refinement step periodically projects the predicted latent back toward the input modalities to recover fine thermal detail lost during denoising.

## Results

On the four video fusion benchmarks (HDO, M3SVD, NOT-156, VTMOT), DRFusion achieves the best SSIM across all datasets (0.595, 0.635, 0.612, 0.450) and the highest CC on three of four (0.682 on HDO, 0.458 on NOT-156, 0.639 on VTMOT). For temporal consistency, it attains the lowest BiSWE and MS2R on HDO (6.225 / 0.211) and VTMOT (7.418 / 0.560), confirming superior frame-to-frame stability. In downstream object tracking (NOT-156), DRFusion yields the best AUC (0.252), SR@0.5 (0.263), and SR@0.75 (0.121) with ByteTrack+YOLOv11n, outperforming the next-best DDFM by 1.2 AUC points and 4.2 SR@0.5 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DRFusion outperforms all 12 baselines on SSIM across every dataset, with the largest margins over Mask-Dif (e.g., 0.612 vs 0.474 on NOT-156) and MetaFus (0.612 vs 0.511). On CC, it beats the strongest competitor DDFM on HDO (0.682 vs 0.679), NOT-156 (0.458 vs 0.441), and VTMOT (0.639 vs 0.623), though DDFM retains a slight edge on M3SVD (0.604 vs 0.594). For temporal consistency (BiSWE/MS2R), DRFusion achieves the best scores on HDO and VTMOT, while TemCOCO and UniVF are competitive on M3SVD and NOT-156. In the object tracking evaluation, DRFusion leads all baselines on AUC (0.252 vs DDFM's 0.240) and SR@0.75 (0.121 vs LRRNet's 0.108), indicating that its temporally stable fusion translates into more reliable downstream perception. The closest overall competitor is DDFM, which matches or leads on EN in several settings but trails on structural fidelity and temporal stability.

## Relation to Prior Work

DRFusion builds directly on the History-guided video diffusion framework (Song et al., 2025), repurposing a pre-trained 3D-DiT as a frozen motion prior and attaching a lightweight Condition Adapter to inject infrared structural constraints. It departs from two dominant prior paradigms: optical-flow-based video fusion (which the authors argue introduces 'geometric rigidity' and ghosting) and frame-by-frame diffusion fusion (which lacks intrinsic temporal modeling). The key conceptual departure is reframing temporal consistency as a spectral filtering problem—using controlled noise injection on historical latents to isolate low-frequency motion trends from high-frequency autoregressive error—rather than enforcing explicit geometric alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent practical bottleneck in multi-modal video perception: maintaining temporal coherence without sacrificing structural fidelity, which is critical for downstream tasks like tracking in autonomous driving and surveillance. The decoupled two-stage design (temporally-constrained VAE + frozen DiT with adapter) is practically appealing because it avoids full fine-tuning of the video foundation model, reducing training cost. Open questions include whether the spectral-filtering interpretation generalizes beyond the 8-frame sliding window, how the method scales to longer sequences or higher resolutions, and whether the 50-step DDIM sampling can be further compressed for near-real-time deployment. The reliance on a specific pre-trained 3D-DiT also raises questions about portability to other video generative backbones.
