## Problem

Standard Actor-Critic methods treat all state dimensions equally during policy-gradient updates, offering no dimension-level interpretability of which features drive the return. Existing explainable RL approaches either provide post-hoc explanations that do not influence training, or constrain the policy class (decision trees, symbolic rules) without yielding the additive, dimension-level attributions that the PG update structure naturally requires. The paper asks how to compute state attributions online via RKHS-SHAP and inject them directly into the learning dynamics while preserving non-asymptotic convergence guarantees under state perturbations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a d-dimensional continuous state vector s ∈ R^d from an MDP and produces a Gaussian action a ~ N(h(s), Σ) where the mean h(s) is a vector-valued RKHS function over a sparse dictionary of at most q_A centers. In addition to the action, RSA2C outputs per-dimension SHAP attributions φ_i ∈ R_{≥0} computed from the Value Critic, which are then converted into a diagonal Mahalanobis weight matrix W = diag(φ̃_i) that rescales the kernel similarity between states. The Value Critic uses an unweighted scalar kernel to avoid circular dependence between value estimates and their own attribution scores, while the Actor and Advantage Critic share a single dictionary maintained by approximate linear dependence (ALD) sparsification.

## Method

RSA2C is a two-timescale, on-policy AC algorithm in which the Actor's Gaussian policy mean is represented in a vector-valued RKHS with an adaptive Mahalanobis-weighted operator-valued kernel κ_φ(s,s_j) = exp(−½(s−s_j)ᵀ W (s−s_j)), where W is populated by RKHS-SHAP importances from the Value Critic. SHAP values are computed analytically via kernel mean embeddings (KME, on-manifold) or conditional mean embeddings (CME, off-manifold), converting the exponential coalition-sum into tractable inner products in the RKHS. These attributions are gated by a floor ε_0 to ensure positive definiteness and then modulate both the Actor's Fréchet-gradient update and the Advantage Critic's target, making the kernel geometry itself attribution-aware rather than merely post-hoc. The Value Critic is trained on a faster timescale via regularized TD loss in a separate scalar RKHS, while the Actor updates on a slower timescale, ensuring attributions remain stable before the policy shifts. The paper derives a global non-asymptotic convergence bound under adversarial state perturbations by decomposing the learning gap into a perturbation-error term (attribution-induced plus policy-learning) and a convergence-error term (tracking plus two-timescale approximation), yielding O(log²T / T^{1/4}) sample complexity at the canonical stepsize schedule.

## Results

On Pendulum-v1 (noise-free), RSA2C-KME achieves a mean return of −139.83±26.00 and RSA2C-CME achieves −141.86±26.77, substantially outperforming the kernel-based baselines RKHS-AC (−899.85) and Advanced AC without SHAP (−719.00). On BipedalWalker-v3, RSA2C-KME reaches 255.09±37.19 and RSA2C-CME reaches 249.72±41.47, competitive with SAC (277.59±93.18) while exhibiting far lower variance. On the high-dimensional Ant-v5 task, both RSA2C variants plateau around 930–960 return, well below SAC (4477.33), confirming the representational limitation of linear RKHS in high-dimensional state spaces. Under state perturbations (noise variance 0.01), RSA2C-KME degrades only from −139.83 to −153.41 on Pendulum-v1, whereas PPO drops to −271.61±198.36 and RKHS-AC to −952.13.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the kernel-based baseline RKHS-AC (Lever & Stafford, 2015), RSA2C achieves a dramatic improvement: on Pendulum-v1 the return improves from −899.85 to −139.83 (KME), a gap of over 760 points, demonstrating that SHAP-guided Mahalanobis weighting is essential rather than merely the kernelized architecture. Compared to SAC, RSA2C is competitive on Pendulum-v1 (−139.83 vs. −160.50, RSA2C slightly better) and BipedalWalker-v3 (255.09 vs. 277.59, SAC marginally ahead), but SAC dominates on Ant-v5 (4477 vs. 960) due to its deep network's representational capacity in 105-dimensional state spaces; however, RSA2C runs on CPU with ~1.4 GFLOPs versus SAC's 29.3 GFLOPs on GPU, and RSA2C is far more stable under noise (std ~26–40 vs. SAC's std up to 2276 on Ant-v5). Against PPO, RSA2C wins consistently across all three environments and all noise levels, with PPO showing severe degradation at higher perturbations (e.g., −271.61±198.36 on Pendulum-v1 at noise 0.01). The ablation against Advanced AC (same RKHS architecture without learned SHAP) confirms that the performance gain is attributable to the RKHS-SHAP attribution signal: removing it drops Pendulum-v1 return from −139.83 to −719.00, a 5× degradation, while Uniform SHAP (fixed equal weights) performs similarly to no SHAP, ruling out the possibility that any reweighting alone explains the improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

RSA2C sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) RKHS-based policy representation (Lever & Stafford, 2015), which the paper extends by replacing the uniform kernel with a Mahalanobis-weighted operator-valued kernel whose diagonal is driven by learned SHAP importances; (2) RKHS-SHAP (Chau et al., 2022), which provides the analytic Shapley computation via kernel mean and conditional mean embeddings, repurposed here not as a post-hoc explanation tool but as an active training signal that modulates Actor gradients and Advantage Critic targets; and (3) two-timescale actor-critic theory (Konda & Tsitsiklis, 1999; Xu et al., 2020), for which the paper fills a gap by providing the first non-asymptotic convergence guarantee in an RKHS-enhanced setting under state perturbations. The ALD-based sparse dictionary management follows Yang et al. (2022), adapted to both vector-valued and scalar-valued kernels. The key conceptual departure from prior XRL work is that attribution is injected into the kernel geometry itself rather than applied as a post-hoc saliency overlay.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled mechanism where dimension-level Shapley attributions actively shape the learning dynamics of an actor-critic algorithm, with a matching non-asymptotic convergence bound that decomposes the learning gap into a perturbation-stability term and a convergence-efficiency term. This is valuable for safety-critical and interpretability-driven RL applications where understanding which state features drive policy decisions is as important as performance. However, the scalability limitation is significant: on Ant-v5 (105-dim state), all RKHS variants plateau near 1000 return while SAC exceeds 4000, confirming that linear kernel representations cannot capture the nonlinear structure needed in high-dimensional continuous control. Open questions include whether combining kernel-based attributions with deep RL backbones, or using scalable approximations like RFFs, can close this gap while preserving the interpretability guarantee.
