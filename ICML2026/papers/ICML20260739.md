## Problem

Existing post-training quantization (PTQ) methods for LLMs cannot efficiently compress weights below 1 bit per parameter: binary PTQ approaches are structurally bounded at 1 bit due to in-place binarization with full-precision scales, and their grouping metadata pushes effective bitrates to 2–3 bits. Binary quantization-aware training (QAT) methods can reach sub-1-bit levels via low-rank factorization but require hundreds of millions to billions of tokens and multi-GPU, multi-day training, making them impractical for 70B+ models. The paper asks whether a data- and compute-efficient PTQ method can achieve genuine sub-1-bit compression without these prohibitive resource demands.

## Input / Output

The method takes full-precision LLM weights (up to 70B parameters) and a minimal calibration set of 128 text samples (0.26M tokens, sequence length 2048) from WikiText-2. It produces a quantized model where each linear layer is represented by two low-rank binary matrices (U_{±1}, V_{±1} with entries in {−1,+1}) packed into 8-bit integer blocks, plus two full-precision per-channel scaling vectors (s1, s2). The entire compression of Llama-2-70B (137.95 GB → 5.75 GB, 24× reduction) completes in ~13 hours on a single H100 GPU, and the resulting model runs on a consumer 8 GB GPU at up to 20 tokens/s.

## Method

NANOQUANT formulates sub-1-bit compression as a low-rank binary factorization W ≈ s1 ⊙ (U_{±1} V_{±1}^T) ⊙ s2^T, and solves it through a three-phase pipeline. Phase 1 computes Hessian-aware diagonal preconditioners (KFAC with Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage) from calibration statistics. Phase 2 performs block-wise reconstruction: (a) error propagation mitigation tunes full-precision weights to absorb prior quantization error, (b) Latent Binary ADMM (LB-ADMM) initializes the low-rank factors by alternating continuous SPD solves (stabilized Cholesky, O(r³/3)) with Sign-Value Independent Decomposition (SVID) proxy updates, followed by magnitude balancing to equalize factor Frobenius norms and extract channel-wise scales, and (c) factorized component refinement jointly optimizes continuous latent proxies and scales via Straight-Through Estimators against block-level MSE. Phase 3 freezes all binary weights and optimizes only the global scaling vectors via KL-divergence minimization against the full-precision teacher's logits. Custom binary GEMV and GEMM CUDA kernels (using packed bitfields and fused FMA, no Tensor Cores required) enable up to 3.6× throughput and 5.4× memory reduction on consumer hardware.

## Results

NANOQUANT achieves 1-bit WikiText-2 perplexity of 10.34 on Llama-2-7B and 6.52 on Llama-2-70B, and extends to sub-1-bit (0.8-bit) with PPL of 12.20 and 7.61 respectively. On zero-shot commonsense reasoning, NANOQUANT reaches 45.95% average accuracy on Llama-3-8B and 48.94% on Qwen3-8B at 1-bit. The method compresses Llama-2-70B from 137.95 GB to 5.75 GB (24×) in 13 hours on a single H100, enabling 70B inference on an 8 GB consumer GPU at up to 20.11 tokens/s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

At 1-bit (1.00 total bits), NANOQUANT substantially outperforms BiLLM (2.88 bits) and ARB-LLM (2.51 bits) on perplexity across most models (e.g., Llama-2-7B: 10.34 vs. 19.87 and 11.80; Llama-3-8B: 14.97 vs. 31.20 and 19.06), while using 2–4× fewer effective bits. Against STBLLM (4.13 bits), NANOQUANT is competitive or better on Llama-3 and Qwen3 models but slightly worse on Llama-2-7B (10.34 vs. 10.12). HBLLM (3.25 bits) remains the strongest PTQ baseline on raw perplexity (e.g., 7.60 vs. 10.34 on Llama-2-7B), but NANOQUANT uses 3.25× fewer bits. On zero-shot reasoning (Table 3), NANOQUANT at 1-bit (45.95% avg on Llama-3-8B) beats BiLLM (38.16%), STBLLM (39.83%), and GPTQ-2bit (36.99%), but trails HBLLM (50.45%) and ARB-LLM (44.23% on Llama-3-8B; 51.86% on Qwen3-8B). Compared to binary QAT methods (DBF, LittleBit, OneBit), NANOQUANT achieves comparable perplexity (e.g., 9.01 vs. 9.08/9.25 on Llama-2-7B) while using 0.26M calibration tokens and 1.7 GPU-hours versus 155M–1.38B tokens and 37–700 GPU-hours. Against vector quantization (AQLM+PV, QTIP), NANOQUANT is competitive at 2-bit (6.52 vs. 5.84 PPL on Llama-2-7B) with 1.05M tokens vs. 8M, and at 1-bit (8.99 vs. 8.28) with substantially less compute.

## Relation to Prior Work

NANOQUANT bridges a gap between two existing lines of work: binary PTQ methods (BiLLM, STBLLM, ARB-LLM, HBLLM) that use in-place binarization with full-precision scales but are structurally bounded at 1 bit per parameter due to metadata overhead, and binary QAT methods (LittleBit, DBF) that achieve sub-1-bit compression via low-rank binary factorization but require hundreds of millions to billions of tokens and multi-GPU training. The paper adopts the low-rank binary factorization formulation from DBF and LittleBit, and the SVID operator from Pouransari et al., but makes the approach viable in a PTQ setting through a Hessian-aware ADMM initialization (LB-ADMM) and a block-wise reconstruction pipeline with STE refinement, requiring only 128 calibration samples on a single GPU.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: compressing Llama-2-70B from 138 GB to 5.75 GB (24×) enables 70B inference on an 8 GB consumer GPU at ~20 tokens/s, and the entire compression pipeline runs in ~13 hours on a single H100. The data efficiency result—matching or exceeding QAT methods that use 100M–1.38B tokens with only 0.26M tokens—is well-supported across five model families and multiple scales, suggesting that precise ADMM-based initialization can substitute for expensive end-to-end retraining. Open questions include whether the method can close the remaining gap to 2-bit PTQ quality at sub-1-bit rates, whether adaptive rank allocation across layers can further improve the accuracy-per-bit frontier, and how the approach scales to even larger (100B+) or MoE architectures.
