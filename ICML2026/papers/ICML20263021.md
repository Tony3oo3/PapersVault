## Problem

Long-horizon prediction of spatiotemporal PDE dynamics from real-world data is hindered by the computational cost of high-fidelity solvers and the irregular, partial, and noisy nature of observations. Existing neural operators assume structured grids, PINNs require known governing equations, and finite-dimensional Koopman methods lack a principled treatment of the infinite-dimensional observable space where exact linear closure is generally unavailable. No unified framework simultaneously handles random partial observations on irregular grids, provides an interpretable low-dimensional surrogate, and maintains stable long-horizon rollouts.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a short sequence of l conditioning frames, each consisting of field values sampled at a different number of randomly located spatial points (time-varying irregular grids with no fixed sensor layout). At inference, the decoder accepts arbitrary query locations and produces the predicted field at any resolution or grid, enabling data completion and super-resolution without retraining. The latent trajectory is a D-dimensional sequence of Koopman observables (as few as 8 for Navier-Stokes), which can be further projected to d dimensions via a Stiefel-constrained head for reduced-order modeling.

## Method

MERLIN is a two-phase Koopman learning framework grounded in a functional Koopman–Mori-Zwanzig theory for PDEs. Phase I trains a discretization-invariant encoder (Galerkin Transformer with self-attention as learnable integral operators and cross-attention pooling to a fixed-size latent) and a resolution-free FourierNet decoder under a joint reconstruction + latent-linearity + one-step-prediction loss, while the linear Koopman propagator is fitted in closed form via ridge regression with EMA sufficient statistics. Phase II freezes the encoder and linear backbone, then trains a memory correction module (leaky-memory with exponential decay or finite-memory LSTM) to capture the non-Markovian feedback from unresolved observables, as prescribed by the derived generalized Langevin equation. The key theoretical result proves that for any finite family of observables of a PDE, the exact closed evolution decomposes into a Markovian linear term, a time-nonlocal memory kernel, and a fluctuation term, justifying the linear-plus-memory architecture. This yields an interpretable surrogate where the linear backbone approximates Koopman eigenfunctionals and the memory term corrects for the loss of finite-dimensional invariance, enabling stable rollouts even in ultra-low-dimensional latent spaces.

## Results

On the real-world SST dataset, MERLIN(FMM) achieves the best MSE across all baselines: 2.893e-2 (training horizon) and 7.510e-2 (test horizon), outperforming FNO (6.579e-2 / 2.693e-1) by roughly 2.3× on both windows. On synthetic PDEs (Wave, Navier-Stokes), FNO attains the lowest in-horizon MSE, but MERLIN with memory augmentation is consistently second-best and exhibits substantially lower error accumulation beyond the training horizon (Wave: ~2.7× train-to-test ratio for MERLIN-FMM vs. ~5.4× for FNO; Navier-Stokes: ~4.4× vs. ~14.2×). The linear Koopman backbone alone already surpasses the finite-dimensional DeepKAE baseline, and the memory correction provides further gains, confirming that both components contribute.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MERLIN(FMM) decisively outperforms all nine baselines on the real-world SST dataset, achieving 7.510e-2 test-horizon MSE versus FNO's 2.693e-1 (a 3.6× improvement) and DINo's 1.169e-1. On the synthetic Wave and Navier-Stokes benchmarks, FNO retains the best in-horizon MSE (1.012e-5 and 2.797e-5 respectively), but MERLIN(FMM) is the clear second-best and shows far superior long-horizon extrapolation: its test-to-train MSE ratio is roughly 2.7× (Wave) and 4.4× (NS) compared to FNO's 5.4× and 14.2×, indicating much slower error accumulation. Against the closest structured baselines, MERLIN(FMM) beats DINo (1.361e-4 / 4.455e-4 on Wave) and KNO (6.256e-4 / 4.623e-3 on Wave) on both horizons, and surpasses the finite-dimensional Koopman baseline DeepKAE (3.569e-2 / 5.291e-2 on Wave) by over 50× in test-horizon MSE. The linear backbone alone already outperforms DeepKAE on all three datasets, confirming that the functional (infinite-dimensional) Koopman formulation is more expressive than the finite-dimensional autoencoder. The two memory variants (LMM and FMM) yield nearly identical performance, suggesting robustness to the specific memory parameterization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate threads: (i) the extension of Koopman operator theory from finite-dimensional ODEs to infinite-dimensional PDEs (Nakao & Mezić, 2020; Mauroy, 2021), (ii) the Mori-Zwanzig projection-operator formalism for deriving memory-corrected reduced dynamics (Mori, 1965; Zwanzig, 1973; Lin et al., 2021), and (iii) discretization-invariant function encoders and resolution-free implicit decoders (Galerkin Transformer, FourierNet, DINo). Prior Koopman learning (DeepKAE, KNO) assumes a finite-dimensional invariant subspace and operates on regular grids; MERLIN departs by working in function space with arbitrary observables, explicitly compensating for the failure of finite-dimensional closure via learned memory terms, and natively accommodating irregular and partial observations through integral-transform parameterizations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a theoretically grounded, interpretable surrogate for PDE dynamics that handles the practically common but technically difficult setting of random, partial, irregular observations—something FNO and most neural operators cannot do natively. The finding that as few as 8 observables capture most Navier-Stokes variance, with a sharp critical transition in ROM dimensionality, suggests a parsimonious finite-dimensional closure for effectively infinite-dimensional dynamics. A key limitation is the neglect of the Mori-Zwanzig fluctuation term, which is justified only when unresolved dynamics are dissipative; extending to non-dissipative or chaotic regimes with richer fluctuation closures remains open. The in-horizon MSE gap versus FNO on Fourier-friendly synthetic PDEs (partly addressed by injecting spectral bias) limits immediate adoption for standard benchmarks, though the long-horizon stability advantage and real-data (SST, ERA5) superiority are compelling.
