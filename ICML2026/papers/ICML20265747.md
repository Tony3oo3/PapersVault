## Problem

Multilingual LLM-as-a-Judge systems exhibit a previously uncharacterized 'translationese bias,' systematically favoring machine-translated text over human-authored references, with severity inversely correlated to language resource availability. The paper attributes this bias to two spurious factors—latent manifold alignment with English and cross-lingual predictability—that standard supervised fine-tuning inadvertently exploits, and shows that existing multilingual reward models remain vulnerable to these shortcuts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265747/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The judge receives a query paired with two candidate responses (a human-authored reference and a back-translated machine variant) and outputs a preference judgment, evaluated under both forward and reverse orderings to control for position bias. During training, the input is consumed by two parallel encoders that produce a robust representation (used to condition the LoRA-adapted LLM judge) and a bias representation (used only for auxiliary proxy losses), while at inference only the robust pathway is active. The final output is a token-level generation from the LLM judge producing the preference decision, scored as accuracy on pairwise benchmarks.

## Method

DIBJUDGE decomposes the input into a robust representation Z_r and a bias representation Z_b via separate encoders, then optimizes a four-term disentangled information bottleneck objective: (1) a variational IB compression loss (KL divergence of Z_r's Gaussian posterior to a standard normal prior) that enforces minimal sufficiency, (2) a task loss where a LoRA-adapted Qwen3 LLM judge predicts the preference conditioned on Z_r, (3) a bias-capture loss that trains Z_b to encode spurious factors through two proxy tasks—Cross-Lingual Alignment (InfoNCE contrastive loss aligning Z_b with the English-translated manifold) and Log-Probability Bin Classification (predicting discretized cross-lingual surprisal bins), and (4) a cross-covariance penalty (squared Frobenius norm of the empirical cross-covariance matrix) that suppresses statistical dependence between Z_r and Z_b under a Gaussian approximation. The entire framework is trained end-to-end in a single stage with LoRA, requiring no gold parallel corpora for the proxy tasks, and the theoretical contribution includes proving the variational upper bound on I(X;Z_r), the variational lower bound on I(U;V), and a second-order expansion linking mutual information to cross-covariance for jointly Gaussian variables.

## Results

On m-RewardBench (23 languages), DIBJudge-Qwen3-8B achieves 91.37% accuracy, establishing a new SOTA among open-weight models and significantly outperforming its backbone-matched counterpart mR3-Qwen3-8B (88.58%) and the much larger Nemotron-Multi-49B (88.83%). On the English-centric RewardBench, DIBJudge-Qwen3-8B reaches 91.01%, surpassing all baselines including Gemini-2.5-Flash (88.83%) and GPT-4o (85.96%). On MM-Eval (18 languages), it scores 87.53%, outperforming mR3-Qwen3-8B (85.29%) and GPT-4o (71.85%). The 4B variant also improves over its backbone (Qwen3-4B: 85.06% → 89.84% on m-RewardBench).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIBJudge-Qwen3-8B wins on all three benchmarks against every baseline. Against its direct backbone-matched competitor mR3-Qwen3-8B (same Qwen3-8B base, same training corpus), DIBJudge gains +2.79 on m-RewardBench, +0.91 on RewardBench, and +2.24 on MM-Eval, with statistical significance (p<0.01) on m-RewardBench and MM-Eval. Against the substantially larger Nemotron-Multi-49B (49B parameters vs. 8B), DIBJudge still leads by +2.54 on m-RewardBench and +11.22 on MM-Eval, while narrowly surpassing it on RewardBench (91.01 vs. 89.71). Against proprietary models, DIBJudge-Qwen3-8B outperforms GPT-4o by +5.62 on m-RewardBench and +15.68 on MM-Eval, and beats Gemini-2.5-Flash by +3.31 on m-RewardBench and +10.06 on MM-Eval. The 4B variant also surpasses all multilingual open reward models (M-Prometheus, mR3-4B, Think-as-Locals) on m-RewardBench, confirming that the gains come from the training recipe rather than base model scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Variational Information Bottleneck (Alemi et al., 2017) and the original IB principle (Tishby et al., 2000) by coupling them with disentangled representation learning (Wang et al., 2024d) to create a two-channel architecture: a compressed robust channel for task prediction and a dedicated bias channel that absorbs spurious factors. It departs from prior multilingual reward models (mR3, M-Prometheus, Think-as-Locals), which rely on standard SFT and are thus susceptible to exploiting spurious correlations, by introducing an explicit cross-covariance penalty (inspired by Barlow Twins / ICA) to enforce statistical independence between the two channels. The proxy tasks (CLA via InfoNCE and LPBC) operationalize the identified spurious factors without requiring gold parallel corpora, making the framework practical for LoRA-based fine-tuning of existing LLM backbones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Translationese bias is a concrete, previously unquantified failure mode that disproportionately harms low-resource languages in multilingual evaluation, and this paper provides both a diagnostic framework (CAD/SSR metrics) and a principled mitigation. The zero-shot generalization to unseen biases (length, self-preference) suggests the disentangled IB learns a genuinely robust representation rather than a narrow translationese filter. Open questions include whether the Gaussian assumption underlying the cross-covariance penalty holds for very high-dimensional LLM representations, whether the two proxy tasks capture all relevant spurious factors, and how the approach scales to languages with fundamentally different morphological structures. The method's reliance on back-translation for constructing bias signals also raises questions about whether it addresses the root cause (English-dominated pre-training) or merely compensates downstream.
