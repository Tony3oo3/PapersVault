## Problem

Muon's Newton-Schulz orthogonalization effectively reduces the condition number of parameter updates, but the resulting per-neuron update norms exhibit high variance, causing certain neurons to dominate optimization. The paper asks whether orthogonalization (matrix-level conditioning) and adaptive learning rates (neuron-level scaling) are inherently conflicting or can be combined for complementary benefits, a question that had not been systematically explored.

## Input / Output

NorMuon operates on 2D weight matrices in hidden layers (attention QKV/output projections and MLP up/down projections), taking the per-step loss gradient and current weight matrix as input and producing an updated weight matrix. The per-neuron second-moment state stores only m scalars for an m×n matrix (negligible overhead over Muon's mn first-moment state), and a learning-rate scaling factor 0.2η√(mn)/‖Ô‖_F is computed per matrix to match Adam's RMS norm. Embeddings, layer norms, biases, and the LM head are handled separately with standard AdamW.

## Method

NorMuon augments Muon's update rule by adding a neuron-wise adaptive normalization step after Newton-Schulz orthogonalization: it computes the row-wise mean squared value of the orthogonalized update O_t, accumulates it into a per-neuron second-moment vector v_t via exponential moving average (β₂=0.95), and divides each row of O_t by √(v_t)+ε to equalize neuron update magnitudes. A post-normalization learning-rate rescaling (0.2η√(mn)/‖Ô‖_F) compensates for the norm inflation caused by normalization, and bias correction is omitted since the scaling already regulates RMS. For distributed training under FSDP2, the implementation sorts parameters by size and assigns orthogonalization to specific GPUs via round-robin, gathering full momentum only on the assigned device and scattering the result back, while the row-wise normalization exploits FSDP2's row-wise sharding to proceed entirely on local shards with zero additional communication.

## Results

On 1.1B pretraining (SlimPajama, 50B tokens), NorMuon achieves a 21.74% reduction in training steps to reach AdamW's final validation loss, compared to 10.43% for Muon and Dion. On 5.4B pretraining (100B tokens), the efficiency gain is 13.17% over AdamW versus 5.60% for Muon. Downstream, NorMuon attains the best average benchmark score of 55.34 (1.1B) and 61.74 (5.4B) across seven tasks, while incurring only a 2.9% increase in per-step wall-clock time and using ~50% less optimizer-state memory than AdamW.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AdamW, NorMuon consistently wins: it requires 21.74% fewer training steps on 1.1B and 13.17% fewer on 5.4B to match AdamW's final loss, while also achieving higher average downstream scores (55.34 vs 53.80 on 1.1B; 61.74 vs 60.59 on 5.4B). Against Muon, NorMuon's advantage is 11.31 percentage points in efficiency gain on 1.1B (21.74% vs 10.43%) and 7.57 points on 5.4B (13.17% vs 5.60%); on Modded-NanoGPT, Muon needs 6% more iterations (124M) and 15% more (350M) to reach NorMuon's validation loss. Against Dion (a concurrent distributed orthogonalization method), NorMuon achieves 21.74% efficiency gain versus Dion's 10.43% on 1.1B, though Dion was not evaluated at 5.4B scale. Against the normalization-based optimizers SWAN and SCALE on 124M Modded-NanoGPT, both underperform Muon and NorMuon, suggesting that pure normalization/whitening without matrix-level orthogonalization is insufficient under the 1024-token sequence length used here.

## Relation to Prior Work

NorMuon sits at the intersection of two optimizer design axes: Muon's matrix-level orthogonalization via Newton-Schulz iterations (Jordan et al., 2024b), which improves singular-value conditioning, and Adam-mini's per-neuron (block-wise) adaptive learning rates (Zhang et al., 2025), which reduce variance in per-row update magnitudes. The paper's core conceptual move is to argue these two mechanisms address orthogonal failure modes—condition number vs. neuron-norm imbalance—and are therefore complementary rather than redundant, a position not previously explored in the Muon or Adam-mini literature. On the systems side, the distributed FSDP2 implementation departs from the prior ZeRO-1/Megatron-LM distributed Muon (Liu et al., 2025a) by distributing orthogonalization computation across devices rather than replicating it, and leverages FSDP2's row-wise sharding to make neuron-wise normalization communication-free.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 21.74% step reduction over AdamW and the doubling of Muon's efficiency gain (11.31pp) are practically significant for LLM pretraining budgets, and the near-zero memory overhead (m scalars vs. mn for Adam) plus 2.9% wall-clock overhead make NorMuon a drop-in candidate for existing FSDP2 training stacks. The broader design principle—that matrix-level geometric operations and coordinate/block-level adaptive scaling target different aspects of update geometry and can be stacked—opens a combinatorial space for future optimizer design. Open questions include whether the post-NS5 placement is optimal across all architectures, whether the empirically tuned 0.2 scaling constant generalizes, and whether the benefit persists in regimes where orthogonalization is near-exact (e.g., very wide matrices).
