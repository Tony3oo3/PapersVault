## Problem

Conditional time series generation methods are evaluated on incompatible datasets with heterogeneous condition modalities, making systematic cross-method comparison infeasible. Prior benchmarks also overlook critical practical capabilities—semantic abstraction level of conditions, fine-grained local controllability, compositional generalization to novel attribute combinations, and downstream task utility—leaving the performance landscape of conditional generators incomplete.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262277/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark accepts conditions in three aligned modalities per time series: a discrete class label (one-hot over unique attribute combinations), a structured attribute vector with heterogeneous categorical fields, and a natural-language text description. For selected datasets (PTB-XL, Weather), paired morphological and conceptual conditions are provided for the same series to enable controlled cross-abstraction comparison. The output is a set of K generated time series per condition, evaluated on both generation fidelity (marginal realism) and condition adherence (joint alignment) using embedding-based and statistical metrics.

## Method

ConTSG-Bench is a unified evaluation framework rather than a new generator. Its dataset construction uses a three-stage LLM pipeline: (1) Gemini-2.5-flash generates morphological captions from raw time series, (2) an iterative attribute-schema discovery algorithm (mini-batch sampling, stability hashing, K-consecutive convergence) extracts a compact discrete attribute set, and (3) class labels are assigned by indexing unique attribute combinations. Evaluation employs a per-dataset Contrastive Text-Time Series Pretraining (CTTP) model to produce aligned time-series and text embeddings for FID, Precision/Recall, CTTP Score, and Joint-FTSD metrics, alongside reference-based DTW/CRPS for semantic-abstraction comparison, segment-level classifier/retrieval protocols for fine-grained control, Hamming-distance-stratified retrieval for compositional generalization, and a drop-rate metric for downstream classification utility. Eleven representative models spanning GAN, VQ-VAE, diffusion, and flow-matching families are trained under identical splits and hyperparameter search, then benchmarked across eight datasets covering healthcare, meteorology, energy, traffic, and network telemetry.

## Results

ConTSG-Bench evaluates 11 conditional time series generation models across 9 datasets using five research questions. Text-conditioned models (VerbalTS, BRIDGE, T2S, DiffuSETS, Text2Motion) achieve the highest performance ceiling but exhibit the largest variance, while attribute-conditioned methods cluster in the upper-middle tier and label-conditioned baselines (TimeVQVAE, TTS-CGAN, Diffusion-TS) fall into the lower tier. On fine-grained segment-level control, only VerbalTS and DiffuSETS consistently outperform a naive retrieval baseline on Synth-U, and on real-world TelecomTS-Segment most generators rapidly approach the random baseline as candidate pool size grows. For compositional generalization, most models show performance degradation from in-distribution (head) to out-of-distribution (tail) attribute combinations, and for downstream utility, most models achieve mean Drop Rates below 1.0 but several exceed 1.0 on complex datasets, indicating synthetic data can sometimes harm downstream training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the 11 evaluated models, VerbalTS (Gu et al., 2025) emerges as the strongest overall performer, achieving consistent top-tier rankings on both generation fidelity and condition adherence (RQ1), outperforming the naive retrieval baseline on fine-grained segment control (RQ3), and maintaining the highest absolute tail accuracy under compositional generalization despite showing larger head-to-tail drops (RQ4). DiffuSETS (Lai et al., 2025) shows significant rank improvements only under conditional evaluation, confirming that good fidelity does not guarantee condition adherence, and also outperforms naive retrieval on Synth-U. Attribute-conditioned models (TimeWeaver, TEdit, WaveStitch) generally cluster in the upper-middle tier, with TEdit uniquely supporting compositional generalization evaluation. Label-conditioned baselines (TimeVQVAE, TTS-CGAN, Diffusion-TS) consistently rank in the lower tier; notably, TimeVQVAE shows minimal compositional degradation but this stems from weak condition responsiveness rather than true compositional understanding. On practical utility (RQ5), no single model consistently dominates across all dataset configurations, and the utility of generated data cannot be reliably predicted from fidelity metrics alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by unifying the fragmented landscape of conditional time series generation, where prior methods (TimeVQVAE, TTS-CGAN, TimeWeaver, TEdit, WaveStitch, BRIDGE, VerbalTS, T2S, DiffuSETS, Text2Motion) were each evaluated on incompatible datasets with single-modality conditions and non-overlapping metrics. It extends the unconditional generation benchmarking paradigm established by TSGBench to the conditional setting, while introducing two orthogonal evaluation axes—conditioning modality (label/attribute/text) and semantic abstraction level (morphological/conceptual)—that no prior benchmark jointly covers. The evaluation infrastructure repurposes the CTTP contrastive pretraining model from VerbalTS as a shared embedding backbone, and the dataset construction pipeline (LLM-based captioning, iterative attribute schema discovery) is a novel methodological contribution that enables aligned multimodal conditions at scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that fine-grained segment-level control and compositional generalization to novel attribute combinations remain largely unsolved, with most generators failing to surpass simple retrieval baselines on real-world data—a gap that aggregate global metrics in prior evaluations have obscured. The finding that downstream utility of generated data is highly dataset-dependent and not predictable from fidelity metrics alone has direct practical implications for data-scarcity applications in healthcare, climate, and network monitoring. Open questions include whether segment-aware objectives, compositional inductive biases, or domain-agnostic training strategies can close the controllability gap, and whether the LLM-based annotation pipeline (69.45% 4-way matching accuracy) is sufficient for high-stakes domains. As an open-sourced, multi-dataset, multi-model benchmark, ConTSG-Bench is positioned to become the standard evaluation protocol for the conditional time series generation community.
