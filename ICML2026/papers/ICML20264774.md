## Problem

Despite the empirical success of numerous knowledge distillation (KD) methods for LLMs, the underlying mechanism explaining why distillation consistently improves student model performance remains unclear. Existing work focuses on improving KD performance but lacks a unified, interpretable framework that explains the common mechanism shared across different distillation approaches and accounts for the performance variance between them.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264774/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The interaction analysis extracts n=13 meaningful words (non-stopword, non-punctuation tokens) from the 'Instruction' segment of each input as input variables, while the system prompt is treated as fixed background context. The student model takes a text instruction and produces a generated text response, evaluated via ROUGE-L and GPT-5 feedback on instruction-following benchmarks. The CIP loss operates on the same 13-word input variables during training, computing interaction effects over all 2^13−1 subsets via a partition-based sampling approximation.

## Method

The paper decomposes the LLM's scalar output into a sum of Harsanyi (AND) interaction effects over all subsets of input variables, then uses this decomposition to analyze how KD transforms the student's interaction landscape. The key finding is that distillation causes sparsification of interactions—suppressing most to near-zero effects—primarily driven by the suppression of complex interactions (involving ≥⌈n/3⌉ variables) while retaining salient simple interactions learned from the teacher. Motivated by the observed positive correlation between complex-interaction sparsity and model performance, the authors propose Complex Interaction Penalty (CIP), a plug-and-play loss that penalizes the mean absolute effect of complex interactions. To make CIP computationally tractable (since enumerating all complex interactions is NP-hard), they partition the n input variables into m≤3 disjoint subsets and approximate the loss by summing over all 2^m−1 non-empty unions of these subsets, reducing complexity from 2^n to 2^m. The total training objective is L = L_KD + λ·L_CIP', and CIP is shown to improve all six KD baselines (KD, SeqKD, ImitKD, MiniLLM, GKD, DistiLLM) across GPT-2, OPT, and LLaMA families on both in-domain and out-of-distribution benchmarks.

## Results

Adding the CIP loss (λ=0.001) to existing KD methods consistently improves ROUGE-L and GPT-5 scores across GPT-2, OPT, and LLaMA families on both in-domain (Dolly) and out-of-distribution (Self-Instruct, SNI, Vicuna) benchmarks. The largest gains appear for smaller models: GPT-2-0.3B with KD improves from 24.78 to 26.73 ROUGE-L (+1.95) and from 37.19 to 42.91 GPT-5 (+5.72) on Dolly. For larger models the gains are smaller but still positive (e.g., OPT-1.3B MiniLLM: 26.63→27.64 ROUGE-L; LLaMA-7B DistiLLM: 28.02→28.12). In the regularization comparison (Table 2), CIP outperforms both L1 penalty and Tf-KD, which consistently degrade performance (e.g., GPT-0.3B GKD: Base 26.35, +L1 25.18, +Tf-KD 25.88, +CIP 26.52).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CIP is a plug-and-play loss that improves every KD baseline it is added to: KD (Hinton et al.), SeqKD (Kim & Rush), ImitKD (Lin et al.), MiniLLM (Gu et al.), GKD (Agarwal et al.), and DistiLLM (Ko et al.) all show positive ROUGE-L and GPT-5 deltas when CIP is appended, with the most substantial gains on smaller GPT-2 models (up to +1.95 ROUGE-L and +6.29 GPT-5 for SeqKD on GPT-2-0.3B). Against the regularization baselines, CIP clearly wins: both L1 penalty and Tf-KD (Yuan et al.) consistently degrade distillation performance by 0.04–1.17 ROUGE-L points, whereas CIP improves performance in all tested configurations, confirming that the gains stem from interaction-specific sparsification rather than generic regularization. The paper also notes that CIP's computational overhead is minimal (3–11% additional training time per batch), making it a low-cost addition to any existing KD pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the game-theoretic interaction framework (Li & Zhang, 2023; Ren et al., 2023a) as an analytical lens for knowledge distillation, a setting where it had not previously been applied. While prior KD research has focused on designing better divergence objectives (GKD, MiniLLM, DistiLLM) or providing theoretical guarantees of convergence (Phuong & Lampert, 2019; Allen-Zhu & Li, 2023), this work asks a mechanistic question: what do all successful KD methods have in common? The CIP loss operationalizes the theoretical insight by adapting the partition-based sampling technique from Zhang et al. (2020a) and the simple/complex interaction partition from Li et al. (2025) into a trainable penalty term.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified explanation that KD works by sparsifying complex interactions while retaining salient simple ones offers a principled diagnostic for why some KD methods outperform others, potentially guiding future method design beyond trial-and-error divergence selection. However, the practical gains from CIP are modest (typically 0.1–2 ROUGE-L points), the analysis is restricted to instruction-following with n=13 input variables on a single training dataset, and the interaction framework's exponential cost (mitigated by sampling) limits scalability to longer sequences. Open questions include whether the sparsification mechanism generalizes to pre-training distillation, classification tasks, or substantially longer contexts, and whether the β = ⌈n/3⌉ threshold for simple/complex partition is truly optimal.
