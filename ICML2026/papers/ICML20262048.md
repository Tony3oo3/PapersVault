## Problem

Looped models (weight tying across depth) and depth-grown models (shallow-to-deep training via middle-layer duplication) have both been linked to improved reasoning, but whether their gains arise from the same underlying mechanism remained unclear. This paper asks whether the two techniques share a common form of iterative computation and whether they can be composed for stronger, more scalable reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262048/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The models are standard text transformers (SmolLM 360M and 1.7B) that consume text prompts with optional in-context examples and produce next-token predictions. The key architectural distinction is that looped models reuse a fixed block of layers k times during the forward pass (e.g., Loop(4×6) has 4 unique layers but effective depth 24), while depth-grown models keep all layers untied but were trained by progressively duplicating middle blocks. For reasoning evaluation, inputs are structured prompts such as sequences of variable assignments ending in a query, and outputs are the single predicted token completing the blank.

## Method

The paper provides a mechanistic unification by showing that looped and depth-grown models exhibit convergent depth-wise signatures: both shift indispensable computation to later layers (via depth score, Tuned Lens early-exit accuracy, and top-5 vocabulary overlap) and induce periodic, block-aligned patterns in residual stream norms and attention sublayer contributions matching the looped/grown block size. Layer-swapping interventions reveal that fully looped models are more order-sensitive, while looping only the middle block with unique encoder-decoder layers (Loop(4-4×4-4)) recovers the robustness of depth-grown models. The key practical contribution is composability: depth-grown models can be looped at inference time by repeating a middle 4-layer block, yielding up to 2× accuracy gains on reasoning primitives despite never being trained with weight tying. Retrofitting this recurrence during a math-heavy cooldown phase (20% Nemotron-CC-Math-4+) combined with the LIDAS growing procedure produces the strongest reasoning performance under matched data and inference FLOPs, establishing a 'grow first, loop later' recipe.

## Results

At 1.7B parameters, the depth-grown LIDAS model achieves 48.02% on Reasoning Primitives and 18.18% on Math Word Problems, substantially outperforming the standard baseline (34.62% and 13.61%) while using only ~80% of the pre-training FLOPs. The best looped variant, Loop(4-4×4-4), reaches 39.88% on Reasoning Primitives with 12 unique layers (vs. 24 for the baseline). When combined with a high-quality math cooldown (Nemotron-CC-Math-4+) and retrofitted recurrence on a middle 4-layer block, LIDAS further improves to 54.84% on Reasoning Primitives and 17.36% on GSM8K, the strongest reasoning performance among all configurations under matched data and inference FLOPs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the standard SmolLM baseline (24/24 layers at 1.7B), LIDAS wins decisively on reasoning: 48.02% vs. 34.62% on Reasoning Primitives (+13.4 pts) and 18.18% vs. 13.61% on Math Word Problems (+4.6 pts), while matching the baseline on NLL (1.96 vs. 1.96) and slightly exceeding it on Open-book Q&A (29.84 vs. 29.57). MIDAS also beats the baseline on reasoning (40.88% vs. 34.62% Reasoning Primitives) but trails LIDAS by ~7 points on that metric. Under iso-param comparison (same unique layer count), looped models consistently outperform their standard counterparts: Loop(16×2) beats the 16-layer standard model by 9.64 points on Reasoning Primitives (31.26% vs. 21.62%) at 360M, and Loop(12×2) beats the 12-layer standard by 5.80 points (41.22% vs. 35.42%) at 1.7B. Under iso-inference (same effective depth), looped models remain competitive on reasoning but lag on knowledge benchmarks, reflecting the known trade-off between unique parameters and knowledge capacity. The Loop(4-4×4-4) design (unique encoder-decoder + middle loop) is the best overall looped variant, achieving 39.88% Reasoning Primitives and 6.91% Math Word Problems at 1.7B with only 12 unique layers, and it also recovers the intervention robustness that fully looped models lack.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of work: looped/Universal Transformers (Dehghani et al., 2019; Saunshi et al., 2025; Zhu et al., 2025b) that tie weights across depth for iterative computation, and depth-growing methods (MIDAS from Saunshi et al., 2024; LIDAS from Kapl et al., 2025) that train shallow-to-deep by duplicating middle layers. While prior work independently observed reasoning gains from each technique, this paper provides the first mechanistic evidence—via depth-utilization diagnostics, residual-stream interventions, and layer-swap robustness tests—that both approaches induce a shared form of iterative, block-periodic computation. It further departs from prior work by demonstrating that the two techniques are composable: depth-grown models can be looped at inference time without any retraining, and that a 'grow first, loop later' recipe (retrofitted recurrence during cooldown) yields the strongest reasoning performance under matched budgets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The composability finding—depth-grown models benefiting from inference-time looping of a middle block without ever being trained with weight tying—offers a practical, low-cost recipe for scaling reasoning that practitioners can adopt without architectural retraining. The mechanistic unification also clarifies *why* both methods work, suggesting that the middle of the network acts as a 'relaxed loop' where iterative refinement naturally emerges. Limitations include evaluation at only 360M and 1.7B parameters on the SmolLM architecture, and the largest gains (up to 2×) are on narrow reasoning primitives rather than broad benchmarks like GSM8K, where improvements are more modest. Open questions include whether the composability and mechanistic signatures persist at frontier scale, across different architectures, and whether the optimal loop position and block size generalize beyond the 4-layer middle block studied here.
