## Problem

Diffusion models are widely used as priors for inverse problems, but standard approaches assume a high-fidelity, domain-matched model. In practice, one often must use a weak prior—either a heavily truncated few-step sampler or a model trained on a mismatched dataset—yet these weak priors empirically perform nearly as well as strong baselines. This paper asks when and why this robustness holds, and when it breaks down, providing a systematic theoretical and empirical characterization that was previously missing.

## Input / Output

The method takes a noisy measurement y = A(x) + ε (e.g., a 70%-masked image with Gaussian noise, a blurred image, or a 4× downsampled image), the corresponding forward operator A, and a pretrained diffusion generator G (typically a 3-step DDIM sampler trained on CelebA-HQ, LSUN Bedroom, or LSUN Church). It outputs a reconstructed RGB image at the original resolution. The generator G is treated as a frozen black box; only the initial noise vector z ∈ ℝ^d is optimized, with the constraint that z remains on the typical Gaussian sphere ‖z‖ ≈ √d throughout optimization.

## Method

The core algorithm is initial-noise optimization: given a weak diffusion generator G, it solves argmin_z ‖A(G(z)) − y‖² using a novel ADAMSPHERE optimizer that projects gradients onto the tangent space of the sphere and retracts iterates back to the sphere, preventing the noise from drifting off the typical Gaussian shell. An improved HOLDOUTTOPK early-stopping rule splits observed pixels into fit and holdout subsets, tracks holdout MSE each iteration, and returns the latest iterate among the top-K best holdout losses to avoid overfitting measurement noise. The theoretical contribution combines a Bayesian-consistency argument (Theorem 3.2) showing that in a Gaussian-mixture surrogate, the posterior concentrates exponentially fast on the best measurement-consistent mode when the per-dimension score gap δ₀ is bounded away from zero and the measurement dimension m is large, with a local spatial-autocorrelation diagnostic demonstrating that 3-step and 20-step, in-domain and out-of-domain natural-image priors share nearly identical short-range correlation decay (Pearson r ≈ 0.9987), explaining why weak priors still propagate local structural constraints effectively.

## Results

On 70% random inpainting, the 3-step DDIM prior with initial-noise optimization achieves 33.78 dB PSNR on CelebA-HQ (vs. 31.98 for DPS with a 1000-step in-domain prior), a gain of 1.80 dB. On 4× super-resolution, the in-domain 3-step prior reaches 31.27 dB on CelebA-HQ versus 26.82 for DPS, a 4.45 dB improvement. Even with a fully mismatched prior (e.g., Bedroom-trained model for CelebA inpainting), PSNR of 32.76 still exceeds DPS's 31.98. Across all three datasets and both tasks, the weak-prior method matches or surpasses the strong-prior DPS baseline in PSNR and SSIM, while LPIPS remains comparable.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DPS (1000-step in-domain DDPM), the 3-step DDIM prior with initial-noise optimization wins on PSNR and SSIM across all six task-dataset combinations in Table 3, with gains of 0.88–1.80 dB on inpainting and 2.40–4.45 dB on super-resolution; LPIPS is slightly higher (worse) for the weak prior on inpainting but lower (better) on super-resolution. Against DMPlug (the closest optimization-based competitor using the same 3-step in-domain prior), the paper's ADAMSPHERE + HOLDOUTTOPK strategy improves PSNR by 0.15–1.0 dB and reduces LPIPS by up to 30% on inpainting and nonlinear deblurring (Table 4). Against DAPS and DiffPIR (Table 8), the method achieves higher PSNR on CelebA-HQ inpainting (33.78 vs. 32.35 and 31.96) and remains competitive across all tasks, though DAPS achieves lower LPIPS on some settings. Against Deep Random Projector (a randomly initialized frozen network with no learned prior), the 3-step diffusion prior is 2.1–4.7 dB higher in PSNR, confirming that even weak diffusion priors carry useful pretrained structure absent from random networks. In failure-mode experiments (box inpainting, 16× SR), DPS with its strong prior clearly outperforms the weak-prior method, with the gap widening as the masked region grows or the SR factor increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the initial-noise optimization framework of DMPlug (Wang et al., 2024), which demonstrated empirically that a 3-step DDIM prior can outperform full 1000-step diffusion posterior sampling (DPS) for inverse problems. The key departure is providing a *theoretical* explanation for this empirical success: the authors combine classical Bayesian-consistency arguments (posterior concentration under informative data) with a novel local spatial-autocorrelation diagnostic showing that weak and strong natural-image priors share similar short-range pixel correlations. Algorithmically, they refine DMPlug's optimizer (ADAMSPHERE, a sphere-constrained Adam) and early-stopping rule (HOLDOUTTOPK), but the conceptual contribution is the principled characterization of *when* and *why* weak priors suffice, rather than the optimization procedure itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is significant: practitioners in data-scarce domains (medical imaging, scientific inverse problems) can use weak or mismatched diffusion priors as reasonable defaults when measurements are informative, without needing expensive domain-specific models. The paper also clearly delineates failure modes (large box inpainting, high-factor super-resolution) where the prior dominates and weak priors produce semantically inconsistent reconstructions, providing a useful decision framework. Open questions include deriving sharp thresholds for measurement informativeness, extending the Gaussian-mixture surrogate to more realistic prior families, and designing task-specific optimization objectives for scientific inverse problems where generic pixel-space losses underperform.
