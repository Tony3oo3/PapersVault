## Problem

Existing MARL methods rely on shaped reward functions that are implicitly universally quantified (∀∀), making them unable to express relational objectives where one agent's behavior must respond to another's (∀∃). Formal specification languages offer mathematical rigor, expressiveness for inter-agent constraints, and the ability to encode tactics, but these benefits remain largely unexplored in multi-agent settings, particularly under partial observability where agents cannot observe each other's full state.

## Input / Output

The method takes a discrete-state POMDP environment and a closed HyperLTL formula (with explicit ∀/∃ quantifier structure over trace variables) as its specification input, replacing traditional reward shaping. It produces a tuple of decentralized policies—one per agent—where universally quantified agents act on their own local observation history, while existentially quantified agents act on a history induced by a learned Skolem witness function that depends on the traces of preceding universal agents. The Skolem functions are approximated at execution time by an encoder-decoder model that predicts other agents' state sequences from local observations.

## Method

HYPOLE proceeds in three steps: (1) Skolemization of the HyperLTL formula to eliminate quantifier alternation, replacing each ∃-quantified trace variable with a Skolem function of preceding ∀-quantified traces; (2) defining continuous robustness functions over zipped multi-agent traces under quantitative LTL semantics, converting the satisfaction problem into maximizing the probability that robustness converges to ρ_max; (3) lifting the shared POMDP to a Dec-POMDP and training value-based CTDE algorithms (VDN, QMIX, QTRAN) using robustness values as reward signals. For existential agents, a feedforward encoder-decoder (3-layer MLP with ReLU/Tanh) is trained on a replay buffer to predict the global state sequences of preceding universal agents, whose outputs feed the Skolem function to construct the agent's policy input. The IGM property of CTDE (satisfied by QTRAN and QMIX via monotonicity) provides the theoretical guarantee that decentralized greedy execution recovers the joint optimal policy.

## Results

On the SMAC corridor map (one of the hardest benchmarks), HYPOLE+QMIX achieves a median test win rate of ~60%, substantially outperforming vanilla QMIX which achieves less than 5%. On MessySMAC 8m (K=12, φ=0.15), HYPOLE+QTRAN and HYPOLE+QMIX reach up to 25% win rate while all vanilla baselines fail to achieve any wins. On MessySMAC MMM, HYPOLE+VDN achieves ~35% win rate versus <10% for vanilla VDN. In the WildFire 8P_3F2V scenario, the ∀∃ variant of HYPOLE+VDN converges after 500K training steps compared to 800K for the ∀∀ variant.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HYPOLE consistently outperforms all four vanilla CTDE baselines (QMIX, VDN, QTRAN, IQL) across SMAC, MessySMAC, and WildFire. The most dramatic improvement is on the SMAC corridor map, where HYPOLE+QMIX reaches ~60% win rate versus <5% for vanilla QMIX, and on MessySMAC 8m where HYPOLE+QTRAN/QMIX achieve ~25% while vanilla baselines score 0%. On SMAC 8m, HYPOLE+IQL surpasses not only vanilla IQL but also vanilla QTRAN, a more complex CTDE method. On MessySMAC MMM, HYPOLE+VDN achieves ~35% versus <10% for vanilla VDN. The ∀∃ quantifier variant further improves over ∀∀: on WildFire 8P_3F2V, HYPOLE+VDN(∀∃) converges 300K steps faster than HYPOLE+VDN(∀∀), and on MessySMAC MMM, HYPOLE+QMIX(∀∃) outperforms both HYPOLE+QMIX(∀∀) and vanilla QMIX. The one scenario where HYPOLE does not clearly dominate is SMAC 5m_vs_6m with the kiting formula, where HYPOLE+MARL merely competes with (rather than decisively beats) vanilla baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

HYPOLE extends HYPRL (Hsu et al., 2025), which studied hyperproperty-guided MARL in a fully observable, centralized setting, to the more realistic partially observable, decentralized execution regime. It integrates the Skolemization technique from logic (used in HYPRL to handle quantifier alternation) with off-the-shelf CTDE algorithms (QMIX, VDN, QTRAN), using HyperLTL robustness values as reward signals in place of hand-crafted shaped rewards. The paper also positions itself against a broader line of specification-guided RL work (Jothimurugan et al., 2021; Li et al., 2017; Hammond et al., 2021) that is either limited to single-agent settings, relies on centralized constructions, or supports only co-safety properties, arguing that HyperLTL's quantifier alternation (∀∃) is essential for expressing inter-agent dependencies that ∀∀ specifications cannot capture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HYPOLE demonstrates that formal hyperproperty specifications can serve as a drop-in replacement for shaped rewards in CTDE-based MARL, yielding substantially better policies on hard scenarios (e.g., ~60% vs <5% win rate on SMAC corridor) and enabling the expression of distinct combat tactics (kiting, focus-fire, defense) as precise logical formulas. The ∀∃ vs ∀∀ comparison reveals that relaxing universal quantification for one agent to existential can expand the policy search space and improve learning, a finding with implications for how multi-agent objectives should be formally specified. Key limitations include the need for hand-written HyperLTL specifications (motivating future AI-assisted spec generation), restriction to discrete action spaces due to reliance on value-based CTDE, and the observation that the framework offers no advantage when standard reward design suffices (independent objectives, non-temporal requirements).
