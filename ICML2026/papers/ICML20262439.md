## Problem

Standard RL training for LLM reasoning generates independent sequential rollouts per query, wasting computation on duplicated shared prefixes and lacking early termination for clearly flawed paths. Additionally, sparse outcome rewards make it difficult to attribute credit to specific reasoning steps, especially when parallel-generated responses appear homogeneous at the sequence level. These two issues—computational inefficiency and imprecise credit assignment—limit the scalability and stability of RL-based post-training.

## Input / Output

The method consumes text reasoning queries (math problems) and produces a set of w=16 reasoning trajectories per query, organized as a tree where shared prefixes are computed once and branched at segment boundaries of fixed token length (e.g., 512 tokens per segment). Unlike standard sampling that generates complete sequences independently, TreePO generates segments iteratively, forking active paths b times before each segment inference and pruning branches that exhibit repetitive substrings or contain a boxed answer. The trained output is a policy model (e.g., Qwen2.5-7B) that can be evaluated via majority voting over 1000 samples on benchmarks like AIME and MATH500.

## Method

TreePO reformulates on-policy rollouts as a segment-level tree search: each trajectory is decomposed into fixed-length segments, and at each segment boundary the active paths are forked according to a dynamic branching budget (forming a binary or wider tree), with early stopping for flawed paths and a depth-first fallback mechanism to ensure the required number of trajectories. A tree-based advantage estimator then computes relative rewards within sub-groups of trajectories that share the same predecessor node at each depth, averages these sub-group advantages, and applies global variance normalization—yielding a more granular credit signal than flat group-level baselines. Heuristic sampling strategies, including probability-driven dynamic divergence (allocating more branches to uncertain segments) and probability-based fallback (selecting stopped paths for re-expansion), steer exploration without additional inference cost. The full pipeline builds on GRPO/DAPO's clipped policy objective with token-level loss and dynamic rejection sampling, and is validated from a base model without prior SFT.

## Results

On the aggregated benchmark (Maj@16 accuracy across AIME 2024, AMC 2023, MATH500, MINERVA, and Olympiad Bench), TreePO w/ More Init Divergence achieves 58.21% overall, compared to 46.63% for the GRPO baseline — an absolute gain of 11.58 points. Adding only the tree-based sampling to GRPO (without the tree advantage estimator) already lifts overall accuracy to 54.61%. In terms of efficiency, tree-based sampling reduces GPU hours by 12%–43% depending on the branching factor (b=2 yields the largest 43% reduction), while maintaining or slightly improving accuracy over the sequential baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's sole explicit baseline is GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), the standard group-relative policy optimization used in DeepSeekMath. TreePO w/ More Init Divergence outperforms GRPO by 11.58 points overall (58.21% vs. 46.63%), with the largest per-benchmark gains on AIME (+10.70 pts) and Olympiad Bench (+14.06 pts). Even the intermediate variant, GRPO w/ TreePO Sampling (which adds only the tree rollout without the tree advantage estimator), improves over GRPO by 7.98 points overall. The full TreePO w/ Fixed Init Divergence setting adds another 2.27 points over the sampling-only variant, confirming that the hierarchical advantage estimator contributes meaningfully. On the efficiency axis (Table 2), tree-based sampling with b=2 cuts GPU hours by 43% (3.65 vs. 6.40) while retaining 94% of the sequential baseline's accuracy, and b=8 reduces GPU hours by 22% while nearly matching sequential accuracy (58.06% vs. 58.21%).

## Relation to Prior Work

TreePO builds directly on the GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) and DAPO (Yu et al., 2025) optimization objectives, incorporating REINFORCE++ (Hu et al., 2025) variance normalization, while replacing their i.i.d. sequential rollout with a segment-level tree search that exploits KV-cache prefix sharing. It departs from MCTS-inspired methods like TreeRL (Hou et al., 2025) and SPO (Guo et al., 2025b) in two key ways: its advantage estimator aggregates over entire sub-tree descendant groups rather than parent-child value differences, and it is validated from a base model (RL-zero) rather than requiring prior SFT. The heuristic branching and fallback mechanisms position it between full MCTS (too sequential for parallel inference engines) and flat batched sampling (no structural reuse), targeting the practical constraint of vLLM-style continuous batching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 22–43% GPU-hour reduction in sampling compute, combined with a ~8-point accuracy gain over vanilla GRPO, offers a practically significant efficiency-accuracy trade-off for teams scaling RL post-training. However, the gains are demonstrated only on 7B-parameter models and math reasoning benchmarks, leaving open whether the tree structure and segment-level advantage estimation transfer to larger models, non-math domains, or multi-turn agentic settings. The negative result that probability-based branching heuristics (both low-prob and high-prob encouragement) underperform uniform branching is a useful caution against over-engineering exploration, and the finding that the root-group advantage term is dispensable suggests the credit-assignment mechanism may be simpler than initially designed. Open questions include whether tree search in latent/continuous representation spaces (where shared prefixes are shorter) could amplify the efficiency gains further.
