## Problem

Backdoor attacks on text dataset distillation have been limited to naive 'Poison-then-Distill' strategies that treat the distillation pipeline as a black box, failing to exploit the distilled attention labels as manipulatable security vectors. Under extreme compression (1 sample per class), this leads to an unstable trade-off where either the attack fails or clean utility collapses, particularly for high-frequency triggers. The paper identifies this structural oversight in attention-guided distillation as a critical, unexplored vulnerability.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an original training corpus (e.g., SST-2 or AG News), a prefix-position trigger word selected via document-frequency stratification, and a target class label, then produces a compact poisoned synthetic dataset at a specified Samples-Per-Class budget (default SPC=1) with fixed one-hot attention labels locked to the trigger index. The synthetic data consists of learnable token embeddings, soft labels, and a learnable learning rate, all optimized jointly; the attention labels are frozen as invariant constraints rather than optimized variables. The resulting dataset, when used to fine-tune any encoder-only Transformer victim model, causes it to predict the target class upon trigger presence while preserving clean test accuracy.

## Method

Attention Hijacking (AH) transforms the attention guidance in bi-level dataset distillation from a soft KL-divergence optimization target into an immutable structural constraint. A saturation-based initialization constructs a quasi-one-hot attention distribution (Softmax of logits with value α at the trigger position and −α elsewhere, α=20), which is then frozen as an invariant constraint, removing it from the outer-loop optimization space. This structural freezing reshapes gradient flow so that ∂L_outer/∂ã = 0, forcing all gradients through the input embedding at the trigger index and deterministically evolving that position into a potent adversarial feature. The Semantic Anchoring Hypothesis explains the adaptive mechanism: for domain-specific semantic triggers (e.g., 'film' in sentiment analysis), AH acts as a Synergistic Enhancer coupling the backdoor with task-relevant features to boost both ASR (>99%) and clean accuracy; for high-frequency noise triggers (e.g., 'the'), it enforces attention segregation to prevent utility collapse where baseline dynamic-injection methods fail.

## Results

On SST-2 with the semantic anchor trigger 'film', AH achieves 89.29% Clean Test Accuracy (CTA) and 99.39% Attack Success Rate (ASR), exceeding the clean attention-guided distillation reference (Clean-Attn: 85.02% CTA) while maintaining near-perfect attack effectiveness. On AG News with the high-interference stopword trigger 'the', AH achieves 91.53% CTA and 62.53% ASR, substantially outperforming the DI-Attn baseline (43.05% ASR) while preserving utility. Across all settings, AH maintains ASR above 84% and CTA above 86%, demonstrating a stable ASR-CTA trade-off that degrades gracefully as trigger interference increases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the attention-guided distillation baseline (Clean-Attn, Maekawa et al., ACL 2023), AH on SST-2 with 'film' actually exceeds the clean reference CTA (89.29% vs. 85.02%), demonstrating that the synergistic coupling of the semantic trigger with task features acts as a regularizer under extreme compression (SPC=1). On AG News, AH's CTA (91.08–91.63%) closely matches Clean-Attn (92.03%) across all trigger levels, confirming no utility degradation. Against the standard soft-label distillation baseline (Clean-Std, Sucholutsky & Schonlau, IJCNN 2021), AH substantially outperforms on both datasets (e.g., 89.29% vs. 51.51% on SST-2), highlighting that the attention-guided framework is essential for viable distillation at SPC=1. Among attack baselines, AH dominates DI-Attn on the critical high-interference 'the' trigger for AG News (62.53% vs. 43.05% ASR) while maintaining comparable CTA, and matches or exceeds DI-Attn on SST-2 across all trigger levels. The naive Static Injection (SI) baseline is consistently inferior, achieving ASR below 50% on SST-2 and below 3% on AG News, confirming that decoupling the attack from the distillation optimization is fundamentally insufficient.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the attention-guided text dataset distillation framework of Maekawa et al. (ACL 2023), which introduced Distilled Attention Labels as structural supervision for knowledge transfer, and the bi-level optimization paradigm of Wang et al. (2018) that underpins optimization-oriented distillation. It departs from prior backdoor-on-DD work (Chen et al. 2024; Sun et al. 2024; Zhang et al. 2024b), which treats distillation as a black box and applies classical 'Poison-then-Distill' strategies, by instead exploiting the attention labels themselves as a manipulatable security vector—converting them from soft optimization targets into frozen structural constraints. The Semantic Anchoring Hypothesis further distinguishes this work by providing a mechanistic account of why trigger semantics (domain-relevant vs. functional noise) fundamentally alter the attack's interaction with the distillation optimization landscape.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a structural vulnerability in attention-guided distillation that is particularly concerning under the emerging 'Dataset-as-a-Service' paradigm, where resource-constrained users trust third-party distilled datasets without access to the optimization process. The finding that a backdoor can simultaneously improve clean accuracy (via the Synergistic Enhancer) while achieving near-perfect ASR challenges the assumed ASR-utility trade-off and suggests that attention-based distillation is a double-edged sword. Key limitations include restriction to encoder-only BERT architectures, dependence on architectural matching between proxy and victim models, and the fact that high-frequency triggers like 'the' still degrade ASR substantially on complex tasks. Open questions include whether structural defenses (e.g., attention smoothing at inference) can mitigate such attacks and how to extend the analysis to causal LLMs where explicit attention matrices are not distilled.
