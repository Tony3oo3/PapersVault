## Problem

In self-supervised goal-conditioned RL, goals are typically specified by single observations, which are either too concrete (requiring exact pixel-level matches) or too abstract (inherently ambiguous), with no unified representation bridging both levels. The paper addresses this by asking how to construct a shared latent goal space where a learned subset relation naturally induces a hierarchy from concrete to abstract goals, enabling agents to both disambiguate specific states and generalize to shared concepts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265092/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes observation sequences (trajectories of variable length, sampled uniformly from 3 to 50 steps) from LiDAR sensors (GridWorld) or 64×64×3 RGB images (MemoryMaze, FetchPush), and produces discrete latent goal embeddings (16×16 multi-categorical one-hot vectors) organized by a learned partial order. Unlike standard goal representations that map single observations to goals, here entire sequences are encoded, and the asymmetric energy function E_θ(x,y) outputs a scalar indicating whether goal x is a subset of goal y. During evaluation, novel reward functions are translated into goals by optimizing a parametrized goal vector via binary cross-entropy against reward-goal similarity scores collected from environment rollouts.

## Method

The core contribution is learning a partial order over a latent goal space via an asymmetric energy function trained with contrastive learning. A GRU encoder maps observation sequences to context vectors, which are then discretized through a categorical VAE (16 categories × 16 values) to form goal embeddings; the energy function is a 3-layer MLP trained with binary cross-entropy on positive pairs (subsequence relations) and negative pairs (non-subsequence, inverted, or disjoint pairs). The key insight is that the subset relation is a partial order, so the induced lattice structure supports join (more abstract) and meet (more concrete) operations, enabling gradient-ascent traversal of the hierarchy to generate goals at arbitrary abstraction levels. For policy training, the method uses contrastive RL with abstract hindsight relabeling: sequences of observations are relabeled as single abstract goals, and in 10% of cases the relabeled goal is further abstracted via energy-driven or reward-driven optimization (Eq. 1 or Eq. 3), giving the policy richer supervision across abstraction levels. This reconstruction-free, contrastive objective replaces the VAE reconstruction loss used in prior discrete-VAE goal encoders, training the representation purely from the subset structure of trajectories.

## Results

On the GridWorld spatial reward group, the proposed method (Abstract Eq.3) achieves 0.63 goal attainment and 0.62 total reward, substantially outperforming observational goals (0.37 / 0.23) and DGR (0.58 / 0.57). On FetchPush object-based rewards, the gap widens further: the proposed method reaches 0.71 goal attainment versus 0.28 for observational goals and 0.56 for DGR. DGR and TRA consistently achieve higher reward-alignment scores (e.g., 0.98–0.99 vs. 0.88 on GridWorld spatial), but this does not translate into better policy performance, as they lack a mechanism to traverse the goal hierarchy during training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method clearly outperforms observational goals on all spatial and compositional tasks: on GridWorld spatial rewards, goal attainment rises from 0.37 (Observation) to 0.63 (Ours), and on FetchPush object rewards from 0.28 to 0.71. Against DGR, the proposed method wins on goal attainment across spatial tasks (0.63 vs. 0.58 on GridWorld; 0.71 vs. 0.56 on FetchPush object), while DGR achieves higher reward-alignment scores (0.98 vs. 0.88 on GridWorld spatial), indicating DGR captures the reward structure but cannot effectively train the policy on abstract goals due to the absence of a hierarchy-traversal mechanism. TRA mirrors DGR's behavior closely (shared bilinear structure), with marginally higher alignment (0.99) but similar attainment. VIP and β-VAE are consistently weaker: VIP achieves only 0.51 goal attainment on GridWorld spatial and 0.71 on FetchPush (matching Ours on attainment but with much lower alignment at 0.32), while β-VAE degrades sharply in FetchPush (0.44 goal attainment on object tasks). The key differentiator is that the proposed method's energy-based partial order enables gradient-based traversal of the goal hierarchy, which bilinear baselines (DGR, TRA) cannot replicate, explaining their alignment-attainment gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends temporal contrastive learning (Schneider et al., 2021; Laskin et al., 2020) from pairwise image relations to sequence-level subset relations, and introduces a partial-order structure on the latent goal space that is absent in prior contrastive RL methods. It builds on hindsight relabeling in contrastive RL (Eysenbach et al., 2022) and discrete VAE encodings (Hafner et al., 2022, 2025), but departs from reconstruction-based objectives in favor of a reconstruction-free energy function that learns an asymmetric containment relation. The closest baselines—Dual Goal Representations (Park et al., 2026) and TRA (Myers et al., 2026)—also learn asymmetric compatibility measures via bilinear forms, but encode observations and goals in separate latent spaces, making hierarchy traversal non-trivial; this paper's key departure is a shared latent space where the energy function directly enables optimization-based traversal between abstraction levels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete gap in goal-conditioned RL: observation-space goals are locked to a single abstraction level, and while bilinear representations (DGR, TRA) can encode abstract reward structures, they lack a mechanism to actually traverse the hierarchy to train policies on those abstract goals. The finding that DGR achieves high reward alignment but low goal attainment on spatial and manipulation tasks is a useful diagnostic for the field, separating 'can the representation encode the structure' from 'can the agent use it.' Key limitations include a distribution shift during pre-training (the policy mostly encounters locally abstract goals), exploration of only spatial abstraction, and computational overhead from encoding observation sets. Open questions include multi-level and compositional abstraction, curriculum learning to bridge the pre-training gap, and integration with hierarchical RL frameworks where goal vectors could mediate communication between levels.
