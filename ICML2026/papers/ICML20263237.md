## Problem

In the interpolation regime, overparameterized models achieve near-zero training error on both meaningful data and impossible data such as random labels, rendering existing PAC-Bayesian bounds for the Gibbs posterior vacuous because they scale as β/n or √(β/n) and are independent of the data distribution. The paper asks whether nontrivial, data-dependent generalization bounds can be recovered from training data alone in this low-temperature regime where standard bounds fail.

## Input / Output

The method consumes labeled training data (2000–8000 examples from MNIST, CIFAR-10, SVHN), a neural network with an isotropic Gaussian prior (σ=5 for bounded losses), a loss function, and a discrete sequence of inverse temperatures 0=β₀<β₁<⋯<β_K=β. It produces a data-dependent upper bound on the 0-1 test error via the κ⁻¹ inversion of the Bernoulli relative entropy, along with the mean training error curve across temperatures. A calibration factor r is computed from a parallel run on random-label data (where true error is known to be 1/2) to correct for LMC approximation errors, yielding tight empirical risk certificates.

## Method

The core insight is an integral representation (Lemma 3.1) showing that the log-density of the Gibbs posterior at inverse temperature β equals an integral from 0 to β of mean training errors at intermediate temperatures, minus the training error of the drawn hypothesis. Substituting this into the PAC-Bayesian theorem yields bounds that depend on the entire temperature trajectory rather than just the final training error, making them data-dependent and informative even when training error is zero at low temperature. For practical LMC algorithms (SGLD, ULA), the bounds are shown to be stable under relative entropy approximation, with the continuous temperature integral replaced by a Riemann sum Γ(ν₀^{K−1}, x, β₀^K) over discrete temperature levels, and approximation errors controlled via Pinsker's inequality or Wasserstein bounds under LSI. A calibration step using random-label data determines a correction factor r that accounts for the gap between LMC invariant distributions and the true Gibbs posterior, producing tight upper bounds on test error for true labels while maintaining correctness for random labels.

## Results

On the full MNIST 10-class classification task, the proposed method achieves the tightest true 0-1 risk bound among all compared methods: 0.2470 for FCN (vs. 0.3155–0.5516 for baselines) and 0.2025 for CNN (vs. 0.2165–0.3645), while also attaining the lowest training 0-1 error (0.0118 FCN, 0.0032 CNN). On CIFAR-10 with 8000 training examples, the calibration factor is 0.89, and the bound for random labels remains above 0.5 while the bound for true labels is tight. The method correctly certifies that overparameterized networks trained with SGLD at β=64000 achieve near-zero training error on random labels (≈0.1) yet the generalization bound stays above 0.5, confirming the interpolation-regime prediction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its risk certificates against the self-certified network methods of Pérez-Ortiz et al. (2021) (f_quad, f_lambda, f_classic, f_bbb) on the full MNIST 10-class task using identical FCN and CNN architectures with a data-independent prior. The proposed method achieves the tightest true 0-1 risk bound in every configuration: 0.2470 vs. 0.3155–0.5516 for FCN and 0.2025 vs. 0.2165–0.3645 for CNN, while also reaching the lowest training error. Notably, the f_bbb baseline achieves a lower test error (0.0293 FCN, 0.0154 CNN) but at the cost of a much looser bound (0.5516, 0.3645), whereas the proposed method's bound is 2.2× tighter for FCN and 1.8× tighter for CNN. The key advantage is that the proposed approach requires no specialized self-certified training algorithm—standard SGLD training suffices—while still producing the tightest certificates. The calibration procedure (factor 0.77 for MNIST, 0.89 for CIFAR-10) depends only on training data and correctly maintains the bound above 0.5 for random labels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the PAC-Bayesian framework of Rivasplata et al. (2020) and the Markov-process generalization bounds of Harel et al. (2026), but introduces a key missing ingredient: the integral representation of the Gibbs log-partition function as an integral of mean training errors over temperature (Lemma 3.1), a standard result from statistical mechanics that the authors note 'seems to have been overlooked in the literature on generalization.' By substituting this integral into the PAC-Bayesian theorem, the paper obtains data-dependent bounds that remain informative in the low-temperature (interpolation) regime where prior bounds of order β/n or √(β/n) become vacuous. The stability analysis under LMC approximation draws on convergence guarantees from Vempala & Wibisono (2019) for the Unadjusted Langevin Algorithm under log-Sobolev inequalities, and the empirical validation uses SGLD (Welling & Teh, 2011) as the primary sampling algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The interpolation regime—where overparameterized models achieve near-zero training error on both meaningful and random-label data—remains one of the central open problems in deep learning theory, and this paper provides the first PAC-Bayesian bounds that distinguish the two cases. The conceptual insight that high-temperature training errors 'announce' low-temperature generalization is clean and potentially transferable to other sampling-based algorithms. However, the practical utility is limited: the theoretical step-size requirements (order 10⁻⁷ for networks with ~400K parameters) are infeasible, and the calibration step that makes bounds tight is explicitly acknowledged as a 'purely experimental finding' requiring further theoretical justification. Open questions include whether the calibration ratio holds beyond the tested settings, whether guarantees transfer to SGD (the zero-temperature limit of SGLD), and why the calibrated bounds are so tight.
