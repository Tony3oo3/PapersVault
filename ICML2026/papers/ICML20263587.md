## Problem

Koopman-based operator-learning methods model temporal propagation as unconstrained black-box maps, causing compounding errors and loss of physical fidelity in long-horizon rollouts. While geometric mechanics provides an elegant Lie-group formulation of nonlinear dynamics, no prior work has systematically embedded this structure into data-driven operator learning, and the resulting sequential Lie-group computations are prohibitively expensive for long-horizon scientific simulation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263587/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes atomistic molecular configurations (all-atom coordinates with explicit solvent, e.g., 582 atoms for Villin, 284 for TRP-CAGE) as either an initial state (IVP, z = x₀) or a terminal state (TVP, z = x_T), with the same neural parameterization handling both directions via a conditioning variable. It produces a full predicted trajectory of atomic coordinates over the rollout horizon and, implicitly, the discrete parallel transport operator on the Lie group that maps states forward or backward. The model is trained on MD trajectories generated with all-atom force fields and evaluated at rollout lengths up to 10⁵ steps.

## Method

EPND recasts the Koopman evolution operator as parallel transport along geodesics on a Lie group G, where the geodesic is governed by the Euler-Arnold equation and parameterized by neural geodesic fields—neural networks that output Lie-algebra coefficients α_a(t,z;θ) whose time evolution follows a closed-form ODE derived from the structure constants and Gram matrix of the chosen Lie algebra. The state evolution is then x_t = PT_{0→t}^ρ(x₀;θ), where PT is a time-ordered exponential of the representation-pushed adjoint-dual dynamics, enforcing symmetry, curvature, and reversibility by construction. To overcome sequential computation bottlenecks, the paper constructs a parallel transport group H_PT = G ⋉ (g ⊕ g∧g) via a semidirect product whose multiplication is associative, enabling an Euler-Poincaré Prefix Scan that computes cumulative geometric states (group element, velocity, curvature) in O(log M) parallel steps rather than O(M) sequential updates. Training is supervised, minimizing MSE between the parallel-transport-predicted trajectory and ground-truth MD data, with the same trained parameters supporting both forward IVP and backward TVP inference without retraining.

## Results

On the Villin headpiece (35-residue) benchmark at 1000-step rollout, EPND achieves a BestMSE of 5.56×10⁻³ and Cα-RMSD of 0.509 Å, outperforming the next-best Koopa (MSE 2.32×10⁻², Cα-RMSD 1.51 Å) by a factor of ~4 in MSE. On TRP-CAGE (20-residue), EPND reaches BestMSE 7.60×10⁻³ and Cα-RMSD 2.91 Å, and on Mg²⁺ hydration it achieves BestMSE 3.03×10⁻² and Mg–O distance deviation of 0.093 Å. Inference time is 1.5–5× faster than Koopman-based baselines (e.g., 0.026 s vs 0.037 s on Villin; 0.016 s vs 0.064 s on Mg²⁺), with training 1.5–6× faster, attributed to the O(log M) prefix-scan update.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EPND achieves the lowest BestMSE across all three benchmarks: 5.56×10⁻³ on Villin (vs. Koopa 2.32×10⁻², a 4.2× improvement), 7.60×10⁻³ on TRP-CAGE (vs. GeoTDM 3.70×10⁻², a 4.9× improvement), and 3.03×10⁻² on Mg²⁺ hydration (vs. KoVAE 3.61×10⁻²). On Cα-RMSD, EPND dominates Villin (0.509 Å vs. KoVAE's 1.03 Å) but is marginally behind KooNPro on TRP-CAGE (2.91 vs. 2.81 Å). Among Koopman-based baselines, Koopa and KoVAE are the strongest competitors on MSE, while KoNODE and MamKO show severe degradation (MSE > 0.1) at 1000 steps. Geometric baselines SympNets, SymODEN, and EqMotion exhibit large structural drift (Cα-RMSD > 3 Å or MSE > 1), confirming that symmetry-preserving parameterization alone is insufficient without the Lie-group geodesic structure. GeoTDM achieves competitive MSE (5.23×10⁻² on Villin) but at a prohibitive inference cost of 266 s (vs. 0.026 s for EPND), reflecting its O(M²) transformer complexity versus EPND's O(log M) prefix scan.

## Relation to Prior Work

EPND sits at the intersection of Koopman operator learning (Koopa, KooNPro, KoNODE, MamKO, KoVAE) and geometric mechanics, explicitly filling a gap the authors identify: prior Koopman-based methods treat evolution as unconstrained linear operators in extrinsic or learned coordinate spaces, while symplectic approaches (SympNets, SymODEN) and equivariant models (EqMotion, GeoTDM) impose only partial geometric structure. The paper's conceptual departure is to recast the Koopman evolution operator as a parallel-transport operator along Lie-group geodesics governed by the Euler-Arnold equation, turning operator learning into a structured grey-box model with explicit algebraic symmetries. The Euler-Poincaré prefix scan then exploits the associativity of the semidirect-product group to achieve O(log M) parallel composition, a computational strategy not previously applied to geometric dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a principled path to long-horizon scientific simulation where structural drift and error compounding plague black-box surrogates, and the unified IVP/TVP capability (backward prediction without retraining) opens a route to inverse problems in pharmacokinetics, atmospheric science, and cosmology. However, the approach is validated only on small molecular systems (≤582 atoms) and requires the dynamics to be faithfully representable as Lie-group flows, which may not hold for all physical systems. Open questions include whether the geometric constraints become overly restrictive for larger or more heterogeneous systems, how the method scales beyond the tested benchmarks, and whether the theoretical error bounds (dependent on curvature and diameter) remain tight in practice for high-dimensional groups.
