## Problem

Pasteris et al. (2024) established optimal switching regret for general convex functions in online convex optimization, but it remained open whether analogous logarithmic bounds (free of the O(log T) factor) are attainable for exp-concave or strongly convex functions. Existing strongly adaptive algorithms imply switching regret bounds with an O(log T) gap from the minimax lower bound of Ω(Σ log|I|), leaving a fundamental question about whether this gap is inherent or an artifact of the meta-algorithm design.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265433/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each round t, the learner receives a convex loss function f_t over a bounded convex set X ⊆ R^d (diameter D, gradient norm ≤ G) and observes gradient feedback after committing to a decision. The algorithm internally maintains O(log T) expert instances over Dense Geometric Covering intervals and sequentially aggregates their outputs via a meta-algorithm, producing a single decision x_t ∈ X per round. The performance guarantee is expressed as switching regret over an arbitrary segmentation S of [T], with the bound depending on segment lengths |I| rather than the total horizon T.

## Method

IRESET creates multiple experts over DGC intervals (OGD for strongly convex, ONS for exp-concave, or Maler for a universal variant) and sequentially aggregates their decisions using Adapt-ML-Prod with linearized losses, which provides second-order meta-regret bounds that automatically exploit function structure. The key analytical novelty is performing recursive equations over a segment tree: fundamental vertices (maximal stationary nodes) serve as base cases bounding expert regret, while non-fundamental relevant vertices propagate bounds upward with a geometrically decaying meta-regret term. A new structural proposition (Proposition 2) shows each segment contains at most 2⌈log₂|I|⌉ fundamental vertices, enabling conversion from tree-based regret to a segment-length formulation. This yields nearly-optimal O(Σ (d/α) log²|I|) and O(Σ (1/λ) log²|I|) switching regret for exp-concave and strongly convex functions respectively, improving the O(log T) factor from prior strongly adaptive guarantees to O(log|I|).

## Results

Experiments are conducted on the ijcnn1 dataset (d=22) with labels flipped every 200 rounds to simulate non-stationarity, using logistic loss (exp-concave), regularized hinge loss (strongly convex), and absolute loss (general convex). The IRESET-based methods (IRESET-OGD, IRESET-ONS, IRESET-Maler) consistently achieve smaller cumulative losses than all baselines across all three function types (Figure 3). After each label flip, IRESET methods recover quickly and maintain relatively low instantaneous losses (Figure 4), while IRESET-Maler demonstrates competitive universality across all function classes without prior knowledge of the function type.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares IRESET variants against three baselines: OGD (Zinkevich, 2003), SCB (Jun et al., 2017), and RESET (Pasteris et al., 2024). IRESET-based methods consistently outperform all three baselines in cumulative loss across exp-concave, strongly convex, and general convex settings. Against OGD, which does not adapt to changing environments, IRESET shows substantially lower cumulative loss after label flips. Against SCB, which uses sleeping coin betting for strong adaptivity, IRESET achieves lower cumulative loss by avoiding the O(log T) factor inherent in sleeping-expert meta-algorithms. Against RESET, the direct predecessor for general convex functions, IRESET-OGD matches or slightly improves performance on general convex losses while IRESET-ONS and IRESET-OGD (for strongly convex) achieve significantly lower losses on their respective function types. IRESET-Maler, the universal variant, matches the performance of the specialized IRESET variants across all three function classes without requiring knowledge of the function type or its parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the RESET framework of Pasteris et al. (2024), which achieved optimal switching regret for general convex functions using Hedge as the meta-algorithm over DGC intervals (Zhang et al., 2020). The key departure is replacing Hedge with Adapt-ML-Prod (Gaillard et al., 2014), a second-order meta-algorithm that yields O(1) meta-regret for strongly convex and exp-concave functions rather than the O(√τ) that is suboptimal for these classes. The analysis also requires a new structural proposition (Proposition 2) to convert tree-based regret into segment-length terms, since the O(log τ) scaling of expert regret for these function classes is incompatible with Pasteris et al.'s inductive technique designed for O(√τ) terms. The universal variant IRESET-Maler builds on Maler (Wang et al., 2019) as the expert-algorithm to handle all three function classes simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work closes a specific open question left by Pasteris et al. (2024): whether optimal switching regret is attainable for exp-concave and strongly convex functions. The nearly-optimal O(log²|I|) bound (vs. the Ω(log|I|) lower bound) represents a meaningful improvement over the O(log|I|·log T) implied by strongly adaptive regret, eliminating the horizon-dependent factor. However, the remaining O(log|I|) gap from the lower bound is acknowledged as an open problem, and the authors note that extending the framework to dynamic regret for these function classes faces fundamental obstacles since OGD/ONS do not admit path-length-dependent dynamic regret bounds. The universal IRESET-Maler algorithm is practically useful for settings where the function class is unknown a priori.
