## Problem

Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD) suffers from training instability in 'Forbidden Zones'—regions where the real teacher's score estimates become unreliable due to distribution shift while the fake teacher's repulsive force vanishes, creating an optimization stalemate that perpetuates generative distortions. Prior DMD variants (DMD2, D-DMD, MagicDistillation, DMDR) implicitly mitigate these zones through external forces, noise scheduling, or teacher adaptation, but none explicitly detect when the student enters a Forbidden Zone or provide a direct mechanism to escape it.

## Input / Output

The method accepts text prompts (for T2I/T2V), class labels (for ImageNet), or reference images (for I2V) and produces few-step (4 NFE) generated images at 572×572 (SDXL) or 256×256 (SiT), and 81-frame videos at 16 FPS (Wan2.1). A distinctive aspect is that for each prompt, the student generates a group of K samples, and a frozen reward model (HPSv2, DINOv2, or VideoAlign) scores them to compute group-relative advantages that drive the adaptive distillation dynamics.

## Method

AMD introduces two synergistic mechanisms. First, Dynamic Score Adaptation decomposes the standard DMD gradient into a Distribution Matching (DM) term and a Conditional Alignment (CA) term, then adaptively modulates their coefficients (α, β) via a linear rule driven by the reward-derived advantage: low-advantage samples receive amplified repulsive force to escape the Forbidden Zone, while high-advantage samples receive amplified attractive force to refine fidelity. Second, Repulsive Landscape Sharpening reweights the fake teacher's denoising loss with an advantage-aware factor (e.g., exp(-ã)), forcing it to learn a steep energy landscape specifically in failure regions so it provides a strong, targeted repulsive gradient rather than a uniform dispersive one. The key design choice over naive scalar rescaling is the structural decomposition into DM and CA components, which prevents destructive interference between conflicting attractive and repulsive signals within the Forbidden Zone.

## Results

On the 10k COCO-2014-val benchmark (SDXL, 4 NFEs), AMD achieves an ImageReward of 88.37 and HPSv2 of 31.25, substantially outperforming DMD2 (71.01 / 30.64) and D-DMD (78.61 / 30.34). On GenEval (SDXL), AMD secures the top overall score of 0.57 among all 4-step distilled models, surpassing DMDR (0.56) and DMD2 (0.51). For streaming video generation (Wan2.1-1.3B), AMD improves the VBench Total Score from 173.59 (LongLive) to 197.45, with a ~67% boost in Motion Quality (35.51 → 59.26). On ImageNet 50K (SiT-XL/2), AMD achieves FID 3.4690 and sFID 5.7464, outperforming standard DMD while avoiding the reward-hacking mode collapse seen in DMDR (FID 9.63).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AMD decisively outperforms all 7 image-generation baselines on COCO-10k, with the largest margin over DMD2 (+17.36 ImageReward, +0.61 HPSv2) and D-DMD (+9.76 ImageReward, +0.91 HPSv2). On GenEval, AMD (0.57) edges out DMDR (0.56) and SDXL-Turbo (0.56) while clearly surpassing DMD2 (0.51) and LCM (0.50). For video, AMD beats the LongLive baseline on Total Score (82.21 vs 81.42) and Quality (85.29 vs 84.20) but shows a slight trade-off in Semantic alignment (69.91 vs 70.38), attributed to the VideoAlign reward prioritizing motion over rigid text constraints. On ImageNet, AMD improves FID over standard DMD (3.47 vs 3.56) while DMDR suffers severe reward hacking (FID 9.63, IS 391.79), confirming AMD's balanced optimization. On HPDv2, AMD consistently surpasses DMD2 across all four style categories (Anime, Photo, Concept-art, Painting) in PickScore and HPSv2, with the largest ImageReward gain in the Photo category (84.01 vs 71.20).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD) family, providing a unified optimization reformulation (Proposition 3.1) that recasts DMD's parameter update as sample-space gradient descent on a contrastive potential. It draws the gradient decomposition (DM Term vs. CA Term) from D-DMD and the group-relative advantage computation from GRPO, but departs from all prior DMD variants by making Forbidden Zone detection explicit via a reward proxy and by repurposing the fake teacher from a passive diversity promoter into an active failure detector through Repulsive Landscape Sharpening. Where prior methods (DMD2's adversarial loss, D-DMD's noise scheduling, MagicDistillation's LoRA teacher adaptation) implicitly constrain the student away from corrupted regions, AMD explicitly diagnoses and corrects within them.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Forbidden Zone framework offers a useful diagnostic lens for understanding why DMD training collapses, and the unified taxonomy (Table 1) clarifies how seemingly disparate methods address the same underlying pathology. Practically, AMD delivers consistent gains across image (SDXL, SiT) and video (Wan2.1) backbones with a simple reward-aware modulation, suggesting the approach is broadly applicable to any DMD-style pipeline. Key limitations include dependence on reward model calibration for Z_f detection and the relatively simple linear adaptive rule for α/β; the authors note that more sophisticated adaptation (momentum, second-order information) and robust unsupervised Z_f detection remain open.
