## Problem

Continual reinforcement learning agents face catastrophic forgetting, negative transfer, and plasticity loss when sequentially learning new tasks. Existing structure-based methods either reactivate all previously learned parameters (wasting capacity) or lack principled mechanisms for selectively reusing prior knowledge and optimizing task-specific connectivity. The paper asks how to design a fixed-capacity policy that rapidly adapts to new tasks by reusing semantically related neurons while provably preserving previously acquired skills.

## Input / Output

The agent receives environment state vectors (e.g., 39-dim Metaworld observations or Brax proprioceptive states) and produces continuous robot actions via a fixed-capacity monolithic policy (4 hidden layers, 1024 neurons for actor, 256 for critic). Task descriptions (short natural-language strings) are encoded by Sentence-BERT solely for mask construction via cosine similarity matching against a task-mask archive; they are never fed into the policy network. The output also includes task-specific binary masks applied element-wise to each hidden layer's output vector, defining which neurons are active for that task.

## Method

Nevo-CRL maintains a single fixed-capacity monolithic policy and assigns each task a sparse binary mask over hidden-layer outputs, decoupling connectivity optimization (evolutionary) from parameter optimization (SAC). For each new task, a semantic-similarity-based construction reuses masks from the most similar prior tasks (frozen to prevent forgetting) and adaptively allocates additional neurons inversely proportional to similarity. A population of mask-policy pairs is maintained; every T_Interval steps, the top-2 masks by fitness undergo importance-guided crossover that restricts recombination to the top-k% most important exclusive units (ranked by average absolute activation through forward propagation), compressing the search space. Low-activity connections below a threshold are pruned to recycle neurons, and non-elite individuals inherit offspring masks with parameters copied from the corresponding parent, avoiding training from scratch. All policies share a single replay buffer, improving sample efficiency across the population.

## Results

On Continual World (CW10), Nevo-CRL achieves 0.96±0.01 average performance with zero forgetting (F=0.00) and 0.41±0.01 generalization, outperforming the strongest baseline CoTASP (0.89±0.05, F=0.00, G=0.47). On the longer CW20 sequence, Nevo-CRL reaches 0.93±0.00 performance with negative forgetting (F=−0.02), the only method to exceed 90% on CW20. On 12 Brax task sequences spanning Ant, Humanoid, and HalfCheetah, Nevo-CRL consistently outperforms CoTASP across all four continual learning dimensions (forgetting, transfer, robustness, compositionality).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Nevo-CRL outperforms all 11 baselines on average performance (P) across both CW10 and CW20. Against the strongest structure-based baseline CoTASP, Nevo-CRL gains +7% on CW10 (0.96 vs 0.89) and +7% on CW20 (0.93 vs 0.86), while also achieving better generalization (G=0.41 vs 0.47 on CW10; G=0.38 vs 0.43 on CW20). PackNet, the other structure-based method, trails by 13% on CW10 (0.83) and 13% on CW20 (0.80). Among rehearsal-based methods, ClonEx-SAC is the closest competitor (0.86/0.87) but still lags by 10%/6%; Reservoir and A-GEM perform poorly (≤0.17). All four regularization-based methods (L2, EWC, MAS, VCL) achieve at most 0.64 on CW10, well below Nevo-CRL. The multi-task upper bounds MTL and MTL+PopArt (0.52 and 0.70 on CW10) are also surpassed. On Brax, Nevo-CRL beats CoTASP on all 12 task sequences, with the largest margins in the Ant domain (e.g., 4003 vs 2746 on Forgetting, 4728 vs 2446 on Robustness).

## Relation to Prior Work

Nevo-CRL extends the neuron-level structure-based continual RL lineage (PathNet, HAT, CTR, SpaceNet, CoTASP) by introducing evolutionary algorithms as the mechanism for optimizing task-specific sparse masks, positioning itself as the first ERL framework explicitly designed for continual problems. It builds directly on CoTASP's codebase, architecture, and evaluation protocol, but departs from prior structure-based methods in three key ways: (1) semantic-similarity-based mask construction using Sentence-BERT for selective neuron reuse rather than reactivating all prior parameters, (2) population-based mask evolution with importance-guided crossover instead of gradient-based or dictionary-learning mask optimization, and (3) integrated pruning to recycle low-activity neurons within a fixed-capacity network. The neuroscience framing (sparse connectivity, synaptic rewiring, neural stability, fixed capacity) provides a unifying conceptual motivation that prior structure-based methods addressed only partially.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Nevo-CRL demonstrates that a fixed-capacity monolithic network with evolutionary mask optimization can achieve near-perfect retention (F=0.00) and SOTA average performance (0.96 on CW10, 0.93 on CW20) while using fewer active parameters than competing structure-based methods, suggesting that connectivity optimization via evolution is a viable alternative to gradient-based mask learning. However, the 54% training time overhead from population-based training, the heuristic nature of the framework lacking theoretical guarantees, and the absence of a mechanism for handling network capacity exhaustion limit its practical deployment. Open questions include whether the evolutionary mask search generalizes beyond the tested robotic manipulation domains, whether the semantic-similarity-based reuse strategy transfers to tasks without natural language descriptions, and how to scale the approach when the fixed-capacity network is fully utilized.
