## Problem

Existing methods for integrating supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) in LLM reasoning either decouple the two stages (SFT→RL pipeline) or naively combine their objectives with fixed or scheduled weights. The paper shows that supervised updates are not uniformly beneficial for reward optimization—naive SFT+RL mixing can actually decrease RL reward—yet no prior method characterizes *when* a supervised update is helpful. The central question is how to dynamically extract only the useful components of the supervised signal that actively facilitate RL reward optimization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes text prompts (math, logic, code, or science questions) paired with expert reasoning traces (distilled from DeepSeek-R1) for the SFT objective and verifiable answer correctness for the RL reward. At inference, the LoRA teacher is merged into the backbone so the model produces a single reasoning trace and final answer with zero additional cost; maximum response lengths are 4K tokens (Qwen2.5-3B) or 6K tokens (Llama-3.2-3B, Qwen3-8B-Base). Evaluation uses greedy decoding with pass@1 accuracy, and Pass@K for diversity assessment.

## Method

BRIDGE casts SFT-RL integration as a bilevel optimization problem: SFT acts as an upper-level teacher (a lightweight LoRA adapter w) and RL as a lower-level student (the LLM parameters θ), with the teacher explicitly conditioned on the student's RL solution. To avoid prohibitive second-order derivatives at LLM scale, the paper applies a penalty-based relaxation that converts the bilevel problem into a single-level objective, yielding a first-order algorithm where the student updates with a convex combination of SFT and RL gradients (weighted by λ) and the teacher updates to maximize a cooperative-gain signal—the reward improvement of joint SFT-RL training over an RL-only baseline computed via Danskin's theorem with a single-step approximation. The LoRA teacher reshapes the loss landscape the student optimizes over, selectively gating which supervised gradients reach θ rather than merely rescaling a scalar weight, and at inference it merges into the backbone at zero cost.

## Results

BRIDGE achieves the highest average pass@1 accuracy across all three model scales: 36.0% on Qwen2.5-3B, 24.7% on Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct, and 49.9% on Qwen3-8B-Base, evaluated on five math benchmarks (MATH500, Minerva Math, OlympiadBench, AIME 2024, AMC 2023). These correspond to absolute gains of +3.5, +2.8, and +4.0 points over the strongest baseline in each setting. BRIDGE also achieves the best Pass@K diversity (e.g., 30.0 vs. 20.0 at Pass@32 on AIME24) and the largest OOD transfer gains on LiveCodeBench (34.55) and GPQA Diamond (42.93) without additional training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BRIDGE outperforms all five baselines across all three model families. Against the two-stage SFT→RL pipeline (DeepSeek-R1 recipe), BRIDGE gains +3.8 points on Qwen2.5-3B (36.0 vs. 32.2), +5.8 on Llama-3.2-3B (24.7 vs. 18.9), and +4.4 on Qwen3-8B-Base (49.9 vs. 45.5), while also reducing wall-clock training time by 44% (3B) and 14% (8B). Against the strongest single-stage hybrid CHORD (dynamic token-wise reweighting), BRIDGE leads by +3.5 (Qwen2.5-3B), +3.9 (Llama-3.2-3B), and +4.0 (Qwen3-8B-Base) points. LUFFY (data-augmented RL) is the closest competitor on Llama-3.2-3B (21.9 vs. 24.7) but trails by 3.6–5.9 points on the Qwen models. SRFT (entropy-aware weighting) is consistently the weakest hybrid, lagging BRIDGE by 4.9–10.1 points. Notably, naive SFT+RL mixing underperforms even pure RL on Qwen2.5-3B (29.4 vs. 32.2), confirming the paper's central claim that uncoordinated supervised updates can harm reward optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

BRIDGE builds on the two-stage SFT→RL pipeline (DeepSeek-R1, SimpleRL) and single-stage hybrid methods (CHORD, SRFT, LUFFY) that combine SFT and RL objectives via fixed weights, scheduling, or data augmentation. It departs from this lineage by reframing the integration as a bilevel optimization problem: SFT becomes an upper-level 'teacher' (a LoRA adapter) that meta-learns to supervise an RL 'student,' rather than the two objectives being coupled by heuristic reweighting on shared parameters. The penalty-based relaxation (Shen & Chen, 2023) makes the bilevel formulation tractable at LLM scale, avoiding second-order derivatives, and the 'cooperative gain' signal—reward of joint SFT-RL training over RL-only—provides a principled criterion for which supervised updates to retain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that naive SFT+RL mixing can be counterproductive (worse than RL alone), challenging the common assumption that more supervision always helps reward optimization, and provides a principled mechanism to selectively extract beneficial supervised signals. The method generalizes beyond mathematics to logic, code, and science without additional training, and its advantage over RL grows under noisy verifiers, suggesting broad applicability. Open questions include scalability to 70B+ models (where the auxiliary iterate θ̂ dominates memory), applicability to open-ended generation with subjective rewards, and whether the bilevel framing extends to multi-objective settings beyond SFT-RL.
