## Problem

Existing neural optimal transport methods are designed for Euclidean geometry, and extending them to Riemannian manifolds remains an open challenge. The paper proves that any discretization-based approach to manifold OT (including RCPMs) necessarily suffers from the curse of dimensionality, requiring exponentially many parameters in the manifold dimension to achieve fixed accuracy. This motivates a continuous, non-discretized neural OT framework with provable sub-exponential complexity.

## Input / Output

The method consumes samples from a source distribution μ and a target distribution ν on a compact Riemannian manifold (M, g), along with access to the intrinsic geometry (geodesic distances, exponential/logarithm maps, and Riemannian gradients). Points on M are represented via a distance-to-landmarks feature map φ(x) = (d(x, ℓ_j))_{j=1}^M ∈ R^M, where landmarks are selected by farthest-point sampling or random sampling. The output is a continuous manifold-valued transport map T: M → M defined by T(x) = exp_x(−∇φ(x)), enabling amortized generation by pushing forward samples from the reference distribution; unlike discrete baselines, the pushforward T_#μ is not restricted to finitely many target locations.

## Method

RNOT parameterizes a prepotential ψ_θ = f_θ ∘ φ with a feedforward neural network f_θ acting on landmark-distance features, then recovers the c-concave OT potential via the c-transform φ = ψ_θ^c, enforcing the structural OT constraint by construction rather than through an external penalty. The associated transport map is T(x) = exp_x(−∇φ(x)), and training optimizes the Kantorovich semi-dual objective L(θ) = −E_{x~μ}[ψ_θ^c(x)] − E_{y~ν}[ψ_θ(y)] using mini-batch Monte Carlo estimates, with the inner c-transform minimization solved by Riemannian gradient descent. This avoids the expensive Jacobian-determinant evaluations required by likelihood-based manifold flow training. The key theoretical contribution is proving that, under mild smoothness assumptions on the prepotential, RNOT networks with non-affine piecewise-linear activations approximate the optimal transport map with parameter count and depth scaling polynomially in ε^{-1} (specifically O(ε^{−4p/(3(kp+1))} parameters and O(ε^{−2p/(3(kp+1))} depth)), thereby breaking the exponential curse-of-dimensionality barrier inherent to discrete-output parameterizations.

## Results

On S², RCPM achieves the best KL divergence (0.0037) and ESS (0.996), with RNOT (FPS) performing comparably at KL 0.03 and ESS 0.97. On T², RNOT (FPS) substantially outperforms RCPM, attaining KL 0.13 versus 0.93 and ESS 0.93 versus 0.55. In the dimension sweep (p = 2 to 10), RCPM degrades sharply as dimension increases—especially at small regularization γ—while RNOT remains essentially flat, confirming the theoretical prediction that discretization-based methods suffer from the curse of dimensionality. RNOT's training time (~1000 s) is roughly 15–25× longer than RCPM (~37–62 s) due to the iterative inner c-transform minimization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RCPM (Cohen et al., 2021), the primary discretization-based OT baseline, RNOT (FPS) wins decisively on T² (KL 0.13 vs. 0.93, ESS 0.93 vs. 0.55) but loses on S² (KL 0.03 vs. 0.0037, ESS 0.97 vs. 0.996); however, the dimension sweep shows RCPM degrading sharply with increasing manifold dimension while RNOT stays flat, confirming the theoretical CoD barrier. Against RCNF (Mathieu & Nickel, 2020), RNOT dominates on both S² (KL 0.03 vs. 2.38) and T² (KL 0.13 vs. 6.31) by a wide margin, with RCNF also suffering from low ESS (0.48 and 0.36 respectively). Against Moser Flow (Rozen et al., 2021), RNOT again wins on both manifolds (S²: KL 0.03 vs. 1.16; T²: KL 0.13 vs. 6.84), though Moser Flow achieves moderate ESS (0.82 on S², 0.56 on T²). Overall, RNOT is competitive or superior in low dimensions and clearly superior in high dimensions, at the cost of ~15–25× longer wall-clock training time compared to RCPM.

## Relation to Prior Work

RNOT sits at the intersection of three threads: (i) McCann's (2001) structural theorem that Riemannian OT maps take the form T(x)=exp_x(−∇φ(x)) for c-concave potentials; (ii) the RCPM framework of Cohen et al. (2021), which is the only prior method that directly constructs Riemannian OT maps but does so via discrete finite-site potentials; and (iii) the manifold approximation theory of Kratsios & Bilokopytov (2020) and Kratsios & Papon (2022), which shows that neural networks composed with injective feature maps (e.g., Gromov's distance-to-landmarks embedding) can approximate smooth functions on manifolds without exponential dimension dependence. The key departure from RCPMs is replacing the discrete min-over-sites parameterization with a continuous neural network prepotential, enforcing c-concavity by construction via the c-transform rather than through a finite template set.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first negative result showing that all discrete-output Riemannian OT methods (including RCPMs) suffer an unavoidable curse of dimensionality, and the first positive result showing that a continuous neural parameterization (RNOT) escapes this barrier with polynomial complexity in ε⁻¹. This gives practitioners a principled reason to prefer continuous over discrete manifold OT parameterizations in high-dimensional settings. Key limitations include the restriction to compact boundaryless manifolds, the requirement that intrinsic geometry (geodesic distances, exp/log maps) is available, and the computational cost of the iterative inner c-transform minimization. Open directions include extending to manifolds with boundary, handling approximate geometry from point clouds, and accelerating the inner solve.
