## Problem

Existing projection-based state-space models (HiPPO, S4, and their variants) construct their latent states from globally supported orthogonal polynomial bases, meaning every state coefficient mixes information from the entire input history. This global inductive bias is poorly matched to signals with localized transients, sharp transitions, or non-stationary structure, and fundamentally prevents the model from selectively attending to or independently retrieving disjoint temporal windows. The paper asks whether replacing polynomial bases with temporally localized wavelet frames can yield SSMs with addressable, multiscale state representations.

## Input / Output

WaveSSM consumes raw time-domain signals directly—10-second ECG waveforms (PTB-XL), 1-second audio clips at 16 kHz (Speech Commands), univariate/multivariate time series (Informer benchmarks), and long sequences up to 16,384 tokens (LRA)—without any spectral or hand-crafted feature extraction. The latent state is organized into N temporally localized coordinates (N=64 or 128), where each coordinate has a bounded temporal receptive field determined by its wavelet scale and shift, enabling independent readout of disjoint input segments. Outputs are standard classification labels, forecasted time-series values, or reconstructed signal segments depending on the task.

## Method

WaveSSM derives continuous-time SSM dynamics by projecting the input signal onto a wavelet frame (Morlet, Gaussian-derivative, Mexican hat, DPSS, or Daubechies) using the SaFARi framework, which generalizes HiPPO to arbitrary frames. The state transition matrix A is obtained by solving a least-squares projection of the time derivatives of frame atoms back onto the frame span, yielding A = ḞF*·S⁻¹ where S is the frame operator; a frame-tightening step (F ← S^{-1/2}F) whitens the frame to ensure numerical stability. The resulting dense dynamics are then embedded into S4's diagonal-plus-low-rank (DPLR) parameterization, reducing per-step cost from O(N²) to O(N) while preserving the wavelet-induced locality. The key architectural insight is that each state dimension corresponds to a localized time-frequency atom, so disjoint temporal windows in the input activate largely non-overlapping subsets of state coordinates, producing an attention-like mechanism for selective retrieval that LTI polynomial-based SSMs cannot replicate.

## Results

On PTB-XL ECG classification, WaveSSM_db6T achieves the best overall AUROC of 0.942, outperforming S4 (0.934), Mamba (0.915), and the prior SOTA MS-SSM (0.937). On Informer time-series forecasting, WaveSSM variants reduce MSE by up to 38% over S4 (e.g., ECL: 0.268 vs. 0.432; ETTm1: 0.231 vs. 0.292). On the Long Range Arena, WaveSSM surpasses S4 on 4 of 6 tasks (ListOps: 61.79 vs. 59.60; Text: 89.23 vs. 86.82; Retrieval: 91.88 vs. 90.90; Image: 89.86 vs. 88.65) but struggles on very long-context Pathfinder and PathX. On SC35 raw audio, WaveSSM_dpssS reaches 96.55% accuracy, marginally above S4_LegS (96.08%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against S4 (the direct polynomial-basis SSM counterpart), WaveSSM wins on PTB-XL overall AUROC (0.942 vs. 0.934), Informer forecasting MSE across all four datasets (e.g., ECL 0.268 vs. 0.432), SC35 raw audio accuracy (96.55% vs. 96.08%), and 4 of 6 LRA tasks. Against Mamba (selective SSM), WaveSSM achieves substantially higher PTB-XL AUROC (0.942 vs. 0.915) and lower Informer MSE, though Mamba's input-dependent selectivity narrows the gap on the Window Copying task. Against the prior SOTA MS-SSM on PTB-XL, WaveSSM_db6T edges out the overall score (0.942 vs. 0.937) but MS-SSM retains a marginal advantage on the Rhythm sub-task (0.980 vs. 0.973). Against Transformer and Spacetime on PTB-XL, WaveSSM improves overall AUROC by 9.3 and 1.1 points respectively. On LRA, WaveSSM matches or exceeds S4 on ListOps, Text, Retrieval, and Image, but S4 retains a clear advantage on Pathfinder (94.20 vs. 73.31) and both methods fail on PathX, indicating a limitation of the localized inductive bias for very long-range global dependencies. On SC35 under zero-shot 8 kHz resampling, S4_LegS (95.27%) remains more robust than most WaveSSM variants, though WaveSSM_MorletS (96.42%) and WaveSSM_GaussT (96.17%) are exceptions.

## Relation to Prior Work

WaveSSM builds directly on the SaFARi framework (Babaei et al., 2025), which generalizes HiPPO's polynomial-projection construction to arbitrary frames, and embeds the resulting dynamics into the S4 DPLR architecture. The key conceptual departure from the HiPPO/S4/Mamba lineage is replacing globally-supported orthogonal polynomial bases (Legendre, Laguerre, Chebyshev) with locally-supported wavelet frames (Morlet, Gaussian-derivative, Mexican hat, Daubechies, DPSS), shifting the inductive bias from global smoothness to multiscale time-frequency localization. The paper also draws on classical wavelet approximation theory (Daubechies, Mallat) to provide a rate-separation theorem justifying why wavelet frames should outperform polynomial modes on signals with transient irregularities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a principled, approximation-theoretic justification for why SSMs with localized bases should behave more like attention on non-stationary signals, and the Continuous Window Copying diagnostic cleanly isolates the 'addressability' property that LTI polynomial kernels fundamentally lack. However, the practical gains on real benchmarks (PTB-XL, SC35, Informer) are modest—typically 0.1–0.5% AUROC or accuracy over S4—and WaveSSM fails entirely on the longest LRA contexts (Pathfinder, PathX), revealing a trade-off between locality and very long-range dependency. Open questions include whether hybrid architectures combining localized and global bases can recover long-context performance, and whether the frame-tightening procedure generalizes to adaptive or learned frame constructions.
