## Problem

Existing decoding-time safety interventions that use rollback rely on heuristic or engineering signals to select which prefix step to revisit, lacking a principled explanation for why a particular step is most influential for the terminal violation. Moreover, rollback alone does not specify how the post-rollback decoding dynamics should be constrained to prevent the same unsafe trajectory from recurring. The paper asks: (1) which prefix step should be rolled back to, and (2) how should corrective constraints be imposed on the next-token distribution to ensure sustained safety recovery.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text prompt, the autoregressively generated prefix, per-rule safety margins computed by a lightweight external scorer (Qwen2-0.5B) via log-likelihood differences of SAFE/UNSAFE label tokens evaluated every 2 tokens, and the base model's next-token distribution restricted to a truncated decoding support (top-k or nucleus). It outputs a single intervention step t*, a sequence of corrected next-token distributions over a short window of N steps (default N=20), and the regenerated suffix text. The correction operates on the probability simplex over the truncated vocabulary, with the CBF constraints expressed as affine half-space inequalities in the distribution space.

## Method

RBCBF couples two components grounded in discrete-time Control Barrier Function theory. For rollback selection, it defines a per-step drift mass u_t from the negative part of CBF forward-invariance increments, weighted by margin depth below an activation threshold, then identifies the temporally compact window within a critical interval (where the minimum margin enters the activation band) that covers an α-fraction of the total risk mass, yielding a principled intervention step. For distribution-level correction, it expresses the discrete-time CBF condition as affine constraints on the token distribution (half-spaces on the probability simplex), constructs a directionally-tilted reference distribution via exponential reweighting informed by terminal violation deficits, and solves a slack-augmented KL projection with a forcing term that maps terminal violation magnitudes into constraint targets. The slack variables guarantee feasibility under truncated decoding support, and the KKT conditions yield an explicit exponential reweighting form parameterized by low-dimensional dual multipliers bounded by the slack penalty.

## Results

On the WildJailbreak adversarial prompt set, RBCBF achieves a terminal safety deficit D_term of 0.539, a quality win rate W of 0.495, and a per-token control cost C of 0.266. Compared to LOGIT-STEER, RBCBF reduces D_term by 15.5% (0.539 vs. 0.638) and improves W by 13.5% (0.495 vs. 0.436) at a comparable cost (0.266 vs. 0.256). Compared to ROLLBACK-INTROSPECT, RBCBF trades a higher D_term (0.539 vs. 0.126) for a 61% lower per-token cost (0.266 vs. 0.686) and a higher quality win rate (0.495 vs. 0.436).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RBCBF outperforms LOGIT-STEER (Liu et al., 2024) on both safety and quality: it reduces the terminal deficit D_term from 0.638 to 0.539 (−15.5%) and raises the quality win rate W from 0.436 to 0.495 (+13.5%), while incurring only a marginal cost increase (0.266 vs. 0.256, +3.8%). Against ROLLBACK-INTROSPECT (Hu et al., 2025), RBCBF achieves a substantially lower per-token cost (0.266 vs. 0.686, −61%) and a higher quality win rate (0.495 vs. 0.436), but at the expense of a much higher terminal deficit (0.539 vs. 0.126, +328%). The paper positions RBCBF as the preferred operating point when both safety and generation quality matter under a bounded correction budget, whereas INTROSPECT is more aggressive on safety at roughly 2.6× the computational overhead.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper imports Control Barrier Function (CBF) theory from safety-critical control (Ames et al., 2019) into the LLM decoding-time safety setting, using the discrete-time forward invariance condition to define both a risk-drift signal for rollback localization and affine constraints on the next-token distribution. It extends the rollback/backtracking mechanism for generation safety (Zhang et al., 2024; Hu et al., 2025) by replacing heuristic or fixed-rule rollback targeting with a trajectory-level risk-concentration formulation grounded in CBF drift analysis. The distribution-level correction via slack-augmented KL projection departs from logit-level steering (Liu et al., 2024) and template-based regeneration (Hu et al., 2025) by operating directly on the probability simplex with guaranteed convex feasibility, while the forcing mechanism couples terminal violation deficits to constraint targets in a way not present in prior CBF-for-LLM work (Miyaoka & Inoue, 2025).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled (non-heuristic) formulation of *when* to rollback by connecting terminal safety violations to accumulated negative drift along the prefix, and gives a theoretical guarantee of forward invariance in expectation under sustained CBF-constrained decoding. However, the empirical gains are moderate: RBCBF substantially outperforms LOGIT-STEER but is far outperformed on the primary safety metric by the simpler ROLLBACK-INTROSPECT baseline (D_term 0.539 vs. 0.126), suggesting the distribution-level correction, while theoretically cleaner, is less aggressive than template-based regeneration. Key open questions include whether the risk-concentration perspective generalizes beyond jailbreak-style adversarial prompts, how the method scales to longer generations or more complex multi-turn safety specifications, and whether the dependence on an external scorer's calibration limits practical deployment.
