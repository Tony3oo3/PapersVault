## Problem

Existing adversarial attacks on LLMs face a fundamental trade-off: discrete prompt-based methods (e.g., SECA) preserve semantic equivalence and coherence but search only a sparse set of rephrasings, while continuous latent-space attacks (e.g., LARGO) explore a richer optimization space but frequently decode into prompts that violate semantic equivalence or coherence. The paper asks how to leverage continuous optimization for broader adversarial exploration while maintaining the semantic realism required for genuine hallucination elicitation.

## Input / Output

The method takes an original MMLU multiple-choice question as input, along with a target LLM and an input-dependent edit dictionary of ~100 concept directions constructed per-prompt via LLM-guided rephrasing of WordNet adjective concepts. It produces an adversarial prompt that is a semantically equivalent, coherent rephrasing of the original question, parameterized by a sparse editing strength vector δ over the concept dictionary. The encoder and decoder are both instantiated by the same LLM, with the decoder reconstructing the prompt from the perturbed latent via an instructed reconstruction prompt.

## Method

REALISTA constructs an input-dependent edit dictionary where each direction z^(i) = φ(x_SE^(i)) − φ(x₀) encodes a semantically preserving transformation in latent space, then formulates the attack as optimizing a continuous editing strength vector δ over a scaled simplex constraint (δ ≽ 0, ‖δ‖₁ ≤ ε). The optimization uses Projected Langevin Dynamics with Gumbel-Softmax reparameterization to handle the non-differentiable LLM decoder, combined with a single-concept initialization that explores each direction individually before refining the top-N candidates. A semantic equivalence safeguard discards gradient signals whenever the decoded prompt violates equivalence to the original, preventing drift into unrealistic regions. The key insight is that constraining perturbations to lie in the span of interpretable, semantically-preserving editing directions—rather than arbitrary latent directions—makes proximity in the simplex a reliable proxy for semantic equivalence, enabling realistic attacks that prior continuous methods cannot achieve.

## Results

On open-source LLMs (MMLU subset, 16 subjects), REALISTA achieves ASR@30 of 97.11% on Llama-3-3B, 93.60% on Llama-3-8B, 41.61% on Qwen-2.5-7B, and 27.24% on Qwen-2.5-14B, while maintaining low semantic errors (SCE ≤ 3.32%, SEE ≤ 3.48%). On commercial reasoning models with free-form outputs, REALISTA reaches ASR@30 of 23.61% on GPT-5-Nano and 20.72% on GPT-5-Mini, a ~20% absolute improvement over raw prompting, in a setting where SECA and LARGO are not applicable. The average number of activated concepts per attack is sparse (0.42–1.78), confirming that attacks rely on minimal, interpretable latent edits.

## Baselines & Comparisons

REALISTA outperforms SECA (the strongest realistic baseline) by 10–20 percentage points in ASR@30 on Llama-3 models (97.11% vs. 79.61% on 3B; 93.60% vs. 82.97% on 8B) and by ~9 points on Qwen-2.5-7B (41.61% vs. 32.47%), while maintaining comparable semantic errors (SCE and SEE within 1–3%). Against LARGO, REALISTA achieves higher ASR on all four open-source models while keeping SCE below 4% versus LARGO's 38–47%, and SEE below 4% versus LARGO's 96–97%, indicating LARGO's high attack success comes at the cost of severe semantic equivalence violations. ICD achieves competitive ASR on Llama-3 models (90.77%, 87.32%) but has 100% SEE across all models, meaning its prompts completely alter the original question's meaning. On commercial reasoning models (GPT-5-Nano, GPT-5-Mini), SECA and LARGO are inapplicable due to black-box access and free-form output requirements; REALISTA achieves 23.61% and 20.72% ASR@30 respectively, substantially outperforming ICD (6.32%, 2.57%) and raw prompting (4.02%, 2.01%) while maintaining SEE below 2%.

## Relation to Prior Work

REALISTA bridges two previously separate lines of adversarial attack research on LLMs: discrete prompt-space methods like SECA, which enforce semantic equivalence and coherence but search only over a finite set of LLM-generated rephrasings, and continuous latent-space methods like LARGO, which optimize flexibly but often decode into prompts that violate semantic equivalence. The key conceptual move is to parameterize the continuous perturbation as a non-negative combination of input-dependent 'edit directions' (each derived from a semantically equivalent rephrasing), drawing on the linear representation structure established by Representation Engineering (Zou et al., 2025) and concept engineering (PaCE, Luo et al., 2024). A scaled simplex constraint on the edit strengths then enforces sparsity and bounded deviation, making the continuous search effectively constrained to the subspace of semantically preserving transformations. The encoder-decoder architecture (same LLM serving as both φ and ψ) follows LARGO and SelfIE.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most practically significant contribution is extending realistic hallucination elicitation to commercial black-box reasoning models (GPT-5-Nano, GPT-5-Mini) with free-form outputs, a setting where SECA (requires token-level logits and fixed answer-choice format) and LARGO (requires latent access) are architecturally inapplicable. This opens a path for systematic red-teaming of frontier reasoning systems that prior realistic-attack methods simply cannot reach. The finding that successful attacks typically activate only 1–2 concepts suggests that LLM hallucination susceptibility is concentrated along a small number of interpretable semantic axes (polarity-flipping, logical restructuring, instructional framing), which could inform targeted defenses. Open questions include whether the linear-combination assumption on edit directions is sufficient for more complex semantic transformations, and whether the approach generalizes beyond MMLU-style MCQA to open-ended generation tasks.
