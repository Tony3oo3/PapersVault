## Problem

Existing multimodal learning approaches either assume all modalities are available or treat missing modalities as a reconstruction problem, where improved generative quality does not necessarily translate to better discriminative performance. No prior method jointly optimizes a discriminative objective while supporting partially observed modalities during both training and inference, and none provides instance-level quantification of how a missing modality would change the prediction for a given example.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263181/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an always-available observed modality x_0 (e.g., static patient demographics, audio waveform, or image) and an optional additional modality x_m (e.g., clinical time-series, image, or audio) that may be absent for any subset of examples. Beyond the standard class prediction, PRIMO outputs a predictive impact score V (expected total variation distance between per-sample predictions and their mean) and a set of plausible label clusters obtained by applying DPGMM to the output logits across K Monte Carlo latent samples, revealing how different completions of the missing modality shift the prediction.

## Method

PRIMO introduces a continuous latent variable z to capture the label-relevant information in the missing modality, trained end-to-end by jointly maximizing two ELBOs—one for complete-modality examples and one for missing-modality examples—both of which are purely discriminative with no reconstruction term for the missing input. A regularizer anchors the unimodal prior to N(0,I) and ties the multimodal prior to the unimodal prior for the same x_0, breaking a shift symmetry that would otherwise make the latent space unidentifiable. At inference, K samples are drawn from the appropriate conditional prior p(z|x_0) or p(z|x_0,x_m), and predictions are averaged to obtain the marginal predictive distribution. The predictive impact of the missing modality is quantified per instance by the expected TVD between individual latent-conditioned predictions and their mean, and DPGMM clustering of the resulting logits reveals the set of plausible labels induced by different latent completions.

## Results

On MIMIC-III, PRIMO achieves 76.17% accuracy on mortality prediction with time-series missing (vs. 76.36% unimodal baseline) and 77.08% with complete inputs (vs. 77.89% multimodal baseline). For ICD-9 neoplasm prediction (140–239), PRIMO reaches 91.41% in both missing and complete settings, matching the unimodal baseline (91.42%) and multimodal baseline (91.37%). For ICD-9 respiratory prediction (460–519), PRIMO obtains 54.95% with time-series missing and 65.78% with complete inputs, compared to 56.22% and 68.22% for the respective baselines. On AV-MNIST, PRIMO achieves 63.06% (audio missing) and 68.17% (complete) for audio, and 37.58% (vision missing) and 68.27% (complete) for vision, closely tracking the unimodal and multimodal oracles.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the synthetic XOR dataset, PRIMO and LVAE (Ramchandran et al., 2024) are the only methods that match the multimodal oracle when both modalities are available, while generative baselines MVAE (Wu & Goodman, 2018) and MMVAE (Shi et al., 2019) fall short because their ELBOs optimize reconstruction rather than classification. CMMD (Mancisidor et al., 2024) is not directly applicable to the complete-modality scenario because it always uses the unimodal conditional prior at inference, even when xₘ is observed. On AV-MNIST, PRIMO performs comparably to the unimodal baseline (x₀) when a modality is missing (e.g., 63.06% vs. 64.23% for audio-missing) and approaches the multimodal I2M2 baseline (Madaan et al., 2024) when both modalities are present (68.17% vs. 71.14% for audio). On MIMIC-III, PRIMO closely tracks the unimodal baseline across all three tasks when time-series is missing (within ~1.3% on mortality, ~1.3% on respiratory ICD-9) and narrows the gap to the multimodal baseline when time-series is available (77.08% vs. 77.89% on mortality; 65.78% vs. 68.22% on respiratory ICD-9). Overall, no single baseline consistently matches both the unimodal and multimodal oracles across all datasets and modality-availability settings, whereas PRIMO does so within a small margin.

## Relation to Prior Work

PRIMO extends the conditional-prior discriminative latent-variable framework of CMMD (Mancisidor et al., 2024) by removing its assumption of fully observed training data, enabling joint optimization over complete and missing-modality examples. It also builds on the latent-variable imputation idea of Ramchandran et al. (2024), but departs from it by modeling a dedicated latent per missing modality and introducing an instance-level predictive-impact metric (TVD-based variance) rather than a single aggregate imputation. The key conceptual departure from the broader VAE-based multimodal literature (MVAE, MMVAE, MEME, VS-VAE) is the removal of any reconstruction objective: the latent is trained purely to serve the discriminative prediction, aligning it with the decision boundary rather than input variation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PRIMO provides a principled, instance-level diagnostic for when an additional modality is likely to change a prediction, which is directly actionable in settings like healthcare where acquiring extra measurements is costly or risky. The finding that static patient demographics largely suffice for mortality and neoplasm prediction on MIMIC-III, while time-series is essential only for respiratory diagnosis, challenges the common assumption that clinical time-series is broadly critical and could inform targeted data-acquisition policies. Open questions remain around validating instance-level modality importance without ground-truth labels, scaling the framework to three or more modalities with heterogeneous missingness patterns, and whether the TVD-based impact metric transfers to high-dimensional or continuous-output settings.
