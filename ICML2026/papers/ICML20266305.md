## Problem

Local self-supervised learning (local-SSL) algorithms, which use only layer-wise Hebbian-like updates without backpropagation, have consistently underperformed global BP-SSL in deep networks, and no theory existed to explain when or why they should match. The paper asks whether a theoretical foundation can be established linking local-SSL to BP-SSL, and whether theory-guided modifications can close the performance gap.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266305/cropped_page1_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (32×32 CIFAR-10, 96×96 STL-10, 64×64 Tiny-ImageNet, 224×224 ImageNet) and produces class labels via a frozen linear probe on concatenated multi-layer representations. Training is fully self-supervised: positive and negative contrastive pairs are generated from SimCLR-style augmentations (random crop, flip, color jitter, grayscale, Gaussian blur) of the same or different images, requiring no labels during the pre-training phase.

## Method

The paper proves that in deep linear networks with orthonormal weight matrices, layer-wise local-SSL (CLAPP, Forward-forward) computes gradients exactly identical to global BP-SSL (Theorem 3.1). Building on this, two theory-guided modifications are introduced: (1) direct feedback (DFB), where the reference signal c^l for every layer is taken from the last layer's activity rather than the same layer, which provably improves gradient alignment when layer widths shrink; and (2) 2D spatial dependence, where the feedback matrix B^l is allowed to vary across spatial locations in convolutional feature maps (via local patch pooling) instead of being shared globally, enabling exact BP gradient recovery in linear convnets (Proposition 3.5). The resulting CLAPP++ variants (with and without DFB) are trained on VGG-style convnets and achieve state-of-the-art local-SSL accuracy, matching BP-SSL on CIFAR-10, STL-10, and Tiny-ImageNet while saving 38–60% peak VRAM by avoiding storage of all layer activations.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, the best local-SSL variant (CLAPP++both) achieves 81.18±0.18% linear-probe accuracy, matching the global BP-SSL baseline (BP-CLAPP++: 80.49±0.20%) and BP-InfoNCE (80.69±0.84%). On STL-10, CLAPP++both reaches 79.62±0.28%, and on Tiny-ImageNet it reaches 37.78±0.67%, both within or above the corresponding BP-SSL baselines. On ImageNet, CLAPP++DFB achieves 44.16% accuracy, still trailing BP-CLAPP++ (48.52%) and BP-InfoNCE (55.19%). All CLAPP++ variants set a new state-of-the-art for local-SSL across all four benchmark datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLAPP++both (81.18% CIFAR-10) outperforms all prior local-SSL baselines: SCFF (80.60%), SoftHebb (80.31%), Stochastic FF (76.96%), original CLAPP (73.6% STL-10), and LPL (59.4% CIFAR-10). On STL-10, CLAPP++both (79.62%) surpasses SCFF (77.14%) and SoftHebb (76.23%) by 2.5–3.5 points. On Tiny-ImageNet, CLAPP++both (37.78%) exceeds SCFF (35.67%) by over 2 points. Critically, CLAPP++ variants match or exceed the global BP-SSL baseline (BP-CLAPP++) on CIFAR-10 (81.18 vs. 80.49) and Tiny-ImageNet (37.78 vs. 37.55), and are within 0.7 points on STL-10 (79.62 vs. 80.36); the gap persists only on ImageNet (44.16 vs. 48.52). Both BP-approximation baselines—Naive CLAPP DFA (52.30% STL-10) and Naive CLAPP Predictive Coding (46.52% CIFAR-10)—are substantially outperformed by all CLAPP++ variants, confirming that layer-wise local optimization with spatial dependence is far more effective than top-down feedback alignment or predictive coding for self-supervised objectives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CLAPP local self-supervised learning framework (Illing et al., 2021) and the Forward-forward algorithm (Hinton, 2022) by establishing, for the first time, a rigorous theoretical equivalence between layer-wise local-SSL updates and global BP-SSL gradients in deep linear networks with orthonormal weights. It then uses this theory to motivate two specific algorithmic modifications—2D spatial dependence in the lateral projection matrix B^l and direct top-down feedback from the final layer—that were not present in prior local-SSL rules. The work also positions itself against BP-approximation methods (DFA, predictive coding) by showing that a purely local, Hebbian-style rule can outperform these partially-local approaches on SSL benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a fully local, backpropagation-free learning rule matches end-to-end BP-SSL performance on CIFAR-10, STL-10, and Tiny ImageNet, closing a gap that has persisted since the early days of local learning research. The 38–60% VRAM savings from not storing intermediate activations make local-SSL practically attractive for memory-constrained or neuromorphic hardware. Key limitations include the restriction to VGG architectures (no residual connections), a remaining ImageNet gap (~4.4 points), and the fact that the theoretical equivalence requires orthonormal weights and linearity—conditions far from practical training. Open questions include whether the spatial-dependence and direct-feedback principles transfer to ResNets, transformers, or larger-scale SSL settings.
