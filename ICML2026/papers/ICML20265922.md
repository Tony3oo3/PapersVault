## Problem

Score-based diffusion models for Bayesian inverse problems require evaluating the time-dependent posterior score, which involves an intractable likelihood term ∇log p(y|x_t) that existing methods approximate with heuristics requiring expensive backpropagation through a posterior mean estimator and lacking convergence guarantees. The paper asks whether an alternative score function can replace the time-dependent posterior score while still guaranteeing correct posterior sampling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265922/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, PGM takes a degraded measurement y = A(x) + ξ (e.g., a 4× downsampled, 70% masked, or blurred image) and the known forward operator A, and produces posterior samples from π(x) ∝ exp{-βf_y(x) - g(x)}. The proximal network is trained once in an unsupervised manner on prior samples only (no paired data), and at sampling time requires only a single forward pass through the network per step—no backpropagation—making it a true zero-shot posterior sampler. The method is evaluated on 256×256 RGB faces (FFHQ, CelebA-HQ) and 256×256 bedroom scenes (LSUN-Bedroom).

## Method

PGM establishes a theoretical equivalence between Gaussian convolution in the diffusion forward process and Moreau-Yosida regularization, then replaces the traditional score ∇log π_t(x_t) in the reverse SDE with the Moreau score ∇log π_t^λ(x_t) = (Prox_U^λ(x_t) − x_t)/λ(t), which admits a closed form via proximal operators. A proximal splitting decomposes Prox_U into a gradient step on the known data fidelity f_y and a proximal step on the unknown prior g; the latter is learned via 'Moreau score matching,' an unsupervised objective that trains a U-Net to approximate Prox_g^λ by minimizing a Bayes risk with sharply concentrated Gaussian loss on prior samples alone. The resulting reverse SDE is discretized with exponential interpolation, yielding a sampling iteration that requires only one forward pass of the proximal network per step. This design eliminates the early-stopping bias inherent in score-based methods (the Moreau score is well-defined at t=0), avoids backpropagation through a posterior mean estimator, and achieves a 9× speedup over DPS while delivering state-of-the-art PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS across super-resolution, inpainting, and deblurring tasks.

## Results

On FFHQ, PGM achieves state-of-the-art performance across all three inverse problem tasks: PSNR 29.82 / SSIM 0.956 / LPIPS 0.158 for 4× super-resolution, PSNR 32.48 / SSIM 0.962 / LPIPS 0.092 for 70% random inpainting, and PSNR 30.67 / SSIM 0.959 / LPIPS 0.148 for Gaussian deblurring. On CelebA-HQ, PGM further improves to PSNR 33.41 / SSIM 0.960 / LPIPS 0.076 on inpainting. In terms of efficiency, PGM uses 4682 MB total memory and completes sampling in 4 s, compared to 5369 MB / 36 s for DPS and 9485 MB / 65 s for PSLD, yielding a 9× speedup over DPS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PGM outperforms all 15 baselines on every metric across all datasets and tasks. The closest competitor is ReSample (Song et al., 2023a), which PGM surpasses by +0.92 dB PSNR and +0.152 SSIM on FFHQ super-resolution, and by +0.96 dB PSNR and +0.157 SSIM on CelebA-HQ inpainting. DPS (Chung et al., 2022a) and DMPS (Meng & Kabashima, 2022) are the strongest diffusion-based baselines but lag PGM by 1.3–1.9 dB PSNR and 0.16–0.26 SSIM, largely because they require backpropagation through a posterior mean estimator, inflating memory (5369–7168 MB vs. PGM's 4682 MB) and sampling time (36–43 s vs. 4 s). Optimization-based baselines (ADMM-PnP, MCG) and latent-diffusion methods (PSLD-LDM) show substantially larger gaps, with PSLD-LDM dropping to PSNR 20.08 on FFHQ deblurring versus PGM's 30.67. On the MNIST inpainting toy task, PGM (PSNR 28.69, SSIM 0.991) also beats DeepONet (PSNR 28.11, SSIM 0.967) and DDPM (PSNR 26.03, SSIM 0.945), confirming the advantage extends beyond face images.

## Relation to Prior Work

PGM bridges two previously separate lines of work: score-based diffusion models for inverse problems (DPS, DDRM, DMPS, ReSample) and proximal Langevin sampling for nonsmooth potentials (P-ULA, proximal MCMC). The key conceptual departure is the Moreau-Yosida-Gaussian equivalence (Lemma 3.1), which shows that for quadratic potentials the forward diffusion kernel is exactly a Moreau envelope, allowing the intractable time-dependent posterior score to be replaced by a closed-form Moreau score computed via a learned proximal operator. Unlike DPS-family methods that require backpropagation through a posterior mean estimator at every sampling step, PGM learns the proximal operator once in an unsupervised manner from prior samples and then applies it as a plug-and-play module, drawing on proximal splitting (Condat et al., 2023) to handle the composite data-fidelity-plus-prior structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The elimination of the early-stopping bias (the O(√ε) error that plagues all existing diffusion-based inverse solvers when the true score diverges near t=0) is a genuine theoretical advance, yielding non-asymptotic Wasserstein-1 convergence guarantees for inverse problems. Practically, the 9× sampling speedup (4 s vs. 36 s for DPS) and reduced memory footprint (no backprop through the posterior mean estimator) make PGM attractive for real-time or resource-constrained imaging applications. However, the convergence theory is restricted to convex priors and strongly convex data fidelity, leaving the most common nonconvex learned-prior setting (e.g., deep generative priors) without guarantees. Extending the framework to nonconvex proximal operators and deriving dimension-dependent convergence rates are the main open questions.
