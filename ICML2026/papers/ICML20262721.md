## Problem

While SNNs have shown empirical advantages over ANNs for mmWave sensing, it remains unclear *when and why* spiking dynamics are beneficial and how their neuronal parameters should be configured to exploit signal characteristics. Existing work emphasizes energy savings or reports task-specific tuning gains without a mechanistic link between spiking dynamics and the spectral structure of mmWave measurements.

## Input / Output

Each sample is a 4-D tensor X_i ∈ R^{L×C×H×W} consisting of L consecutive temporal frames, each with C channels and an H×W spatial sensing map, produced by standard mmWave radar preprocessing. The temporal axis (length L) is the axis over which LIF membrane dynamics integrate, making the sequence length the effective filter order. The model outputs a single class label y_i ∈ {1,…,C} for the activity or gesture category.

## Method

The paper establishes that the subthreshold LIF update is a first-order IIR low-pass filter whose DC-normalized power template H̃(ω_k; β) is monotonically controlled by the membrane decay factor β, and derives a principled criterion for setting β by matching the LIF effective bandwidth to the data's discriminative spectral content. Concretely, they compute a Fisher-style discriminative index DI_norm(ω_k) on DFT amplitude features of a scalar temporal proxy (frame-wise mean over non-temporal axes), then define a Frequency-Matching Score FMS_avg(β) = Σ DI_norm(ω_k)·H̃(ω_k;β) that quantifies how much discriminative spectral mass the LIF filter retains. A deterministic reference boundary β† is identified via a maximum-deviation rule on the FMS curve, partitioning β into under-filter, stability-window, and over-low-pass regimes. The practical model is a LeNet-style SNN (SpikingLeNet, ~4.19M params) trained with surrogate gradients under a unified protocol, with β selected within the stability window to balance high-frequency noise suppression against retention of mid-frequency discriminative cues.

## Results

SpikingLeNet achieves an average test-accuracy improvement of 6.22% over the best ANN baseline across four mmWave datasets (AOPHand, mmFiT, Pantomime, MMActivity), while using the same ~4.19M parameter budget as LeNet. On AOPHand it reaches 83.70% accuracy (vs. 74.39% for VGG9), and on MMActivity it reaches 75.00% (vs. 64.17% for ResNet101). In terms of energy, SpikingLeNet consumes only 1.45–2.53 μJ per sample, a ~3.64× reduction compared to the most energy-efficient ANN (RNN at 7.35 μJ) and ~119× lower than LeNet (251 μJ).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpikingLeNet outperforms every ANN baseline on all four datasets. Against the VGG family (Simonyan et al., ICLR 2015), SpikingLeNet leads by 9.31 points on AOPHand (83.70% vs. VGG9's 74.39%) and by 12.41 points on MMActivity (75.00% vs. VGG9's 70.00%), while using roughly 7–17× fewer parameters. Against the broader Sensefi benchmark suite (Yang et al., Patterns 2023), which includes MLP, LeNet, ResNet, RNN/GRU/LSTM/BiLSTM, CNN-GRU, and ViT, SpikingLeNet achieves the highest accuracy on every dataset: 83.70% on AOPHand (vs. best ANN VGG9 at 74.39%), 73.67% on mmFiT (vs. ResNet101 at 72.64%), 78.31% on Pantomime (vs. ResNet50 at 73.90%), and 75.00% on MMActivity (vs. ResNet101 at 64.17%). Recurrent baselines (RNN, GRU, LSTM, BiLSTM) show severe dataset-specific failures (e.g., GRU drops to 14.11% on mmFiT), whereas SpikingLeNet maintains stable performance across all four. In energy, SpikingLeNet (1.45–2.53 μJ) is ~3.64× more efficient than the best ANN (RNN at 7.35 μJ) and ~119× more efficient than LeNet (251 μJ), confirming that the accuracy gains are not purchased at the cost of efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the LIF-to-first-order-IIR equivalence established by Fang et al. (2025), repurposing it from a general SNN analysis tool into a mechanism-data alignment framework specifically for mmWave sensing. It departs from prior SNN-for-mmWave work (Arsalan et al., 2022b; Safa et al., 2021; Zheng et al., 2024), which demonstrated empirical energy or accuracy gains without explaining *why* spiking dynamics help, by introducing a frequency-domain diagnostic (Fisher-style discriminative index) and a principled β-selection criterion. The Fisher discriminant formulation and spectral-bias literature (Fridovich-Keil et al., 2022) inform the data-side characterization, while the knee-point detection methodology (Satopaa et al., 2011) is adapted to define the over-low-pass boundary β†.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The frequency-matching criterion gives practitioners a deterministic, accuracy-agnostic rule for setting the membrane decay factor, reducing the need for costly per-dataset β sweeps and providing a mechanistic explanation for when SNNs should outperform ANNs on noisy temporal signals. However, the scalar DI (averaging over spatial/channel dimensions) is a coarse diagnostic that may miss spatially-structured discriminative cues, and the 4× GPU latency overhead relative to LeNet limits near-term deployment on conventional accelerators. The full efficiency story depends on neuromorphic hardware (e.g., Darwin3) that is not yet widely available, and the framework's validity is bounded by the subthreshold regime—extreme β values that cause spike saturation or collapse invalidate the linear spectral interpretation.
