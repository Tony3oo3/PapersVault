## Problem

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) in large vision-language models (LVLMs) is assumed to be universally beneficial for knowledge-based VQA, yet the paper identifies a previously overlooked failure mode—Attention Distraction (AD)—where even highly relevant retrieved context systematically suppresses visual attention and shifts focus away from question-relevant image regions, causing the model to fail on questions it could answer correctly in closed-book. Prior work (e.g., ALFAR) attributed RAG failures to under-attention on retrieved text, but this paper shows the opposite: the bottleneck is over-attention on context that crowds out essential visual evidence, a cross-modal and intra-image distraction that no existing method addresses.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (encoded into V visual tokens), a text question, and retrieved text context (C tokens) as input, and produces a short text answer (single word or phrase) for knowledge-based VQA. At inference time, the question is duplicated into two token groups—Q_I placed immediately after image tokens and Q_C placed after the retrieved context—forming the sequence [I, Q_I, C, Q_C]. The intervention operates purely on attention weights at every transformer layer during autoregressive decoding, requiring no retraining, no retriever modification, and adding only ~9–11% inference latency over vanilla RAG.

## Method

MAD-RAG is a training-free, plug-and-play inference-time intervention with two complementary components. First, a dual-question formulation decouples visual grounding from context integration: Q_I (after image tokens) establishes image-prioritized grounding, while Q_C (after retrieved context) handles knowledge-aware reasoning, resolving the information bottleneck in the standard [I, Q, C] layout where the causal mask prevents question tokens from attending to context. Second, an attention mixing mechanism counteracts the model's inherent recency bias toward long retrieved contexts by injecting image-grounded attention from Q_I into Q_C at each layer via a convex combination: Â_QC,All = α·[A_QI,I, 0, 0] + (1−α)·A_QC,All, with α=0.5 as default. This preserves question-relevant visual focus during generation without altering model weights or the retrieval pipeline, and the paper demonstrates it recovers up to 74.68% of RAG-induced failure cases across OK-VQA, E-VQA, and InfoSeek with negligible computational overhead.

## Results

MAD-RAG achieves absolute gains of up to 4.76% on OK-VQA, 9.20% on E-VQA, and 6.18% on InfoSeek over vanilla RAG across four LVLM families (LLaVA-1.5-7B/13B, Qwen2.5-VL-3B/7B). For example, with LLaVA-1.5-7B, MAD-RAG reaches 70.22% on OK-VQA, 63.09% on E-VQA, and 50.19% on InfoSeek, compared to 65.46%, 53.89%, and 44.01% for vanilla RAG. The method also recovers 74.68% of attention-distraction failure cases (where closed-book is correct but RAG fails) on OK-VQA, with only ~9–11% inference overhead versus 1.3×–4.5× for competing methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAD-RAG consistently outperforms all eight baselines across every dataset and model family. Among RAG-oriented methods, the largest margins are over ALFAR (e.g., +4.90% on OK-VQA and +11.39% on E-VQA with LLaVA-1.5-7B) and CAD (e.g., +1.78% on OK-VQA but +11.13% on InfoSeek), indicating that simply reweighting context logits or reallocating attention toward text is insufficient to prevent visual grounding collapse. Among VLM hallucination-mitigation methods, MAD-RAG leads by 3.75–8.67% over DoLa, VCD, OPERA, VHR, and SPIN on OK-VQA with LLaVA-1.5-7B; notably, VHR and SPIN suffer severe degradation on Qwen2.5-VL (e.g., VHR drops to 34.67% on OK-VQA vs. 66.44% for MAD-RAG), and OPERA/SPIN are entirely incompatible with Qwen2.5-VL's dynamic visual tokenization. The only near-tie is ALFAR on Qwen2.5-VL-7B E-VQA (83.31 vs. 82.40), where MAD-RAG still leads by 0.91%. Overall, MAD-RAG achieves the best score in 12 out of 12 evaluated model–dataset cells, with the most dramatic gains on E-VQA where the attention-distraction effect is strongest.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant diagnostic framing in retrieval-augmented LVLM research. Prior work such as ALFAR attributes RAG failures to excessive visual attention relative to context tokens and proposes reallocating attention toward retrieved text, while LLM-side calibration methods (CAD, AdaCAD) balance parametric and retrieved knowledge in the textual domain. MAD-RAG instead identifies the opposite pathology: retrieved context globally suppresses visual attention and shifts intra-image focus away from question-relevant regions, a failure mode that persists even with oracle-quality retrieval. Methodologically, it repurposes attention-manipulation ideas from VLM hallucination mitigation (DoLa, VCD, OPERA) but applies them to a newly characterized cross-modal interference problem via a dual-question input construction and convex attention mixing, rather than logit-level decoding interventions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of Attention Distraction as a distinct, retrieval-quality-independent failure mode reframes how the community diagnoses RAG degradation in LVLMs, with direct implications for any system that injects long textual context alongside visual inputs. The training-free, plug-and-play nature of MAD-RAG (≈10% overhead, no retraining) makes it immediately deployable, and the 74.68% recovery rate on AD cases demonstrates substantial practical value. Open questions include: whether the mixing weight α can be made adaptive per-sample or per-layer, a deeper theoretical account of why retrieved text systematically induces cross-modal suppression, and extension to closed-source models where attention access is unavailable. The paper also honestly identifies a boundary condition—when the model's own visual grounding is weak, restoring closed-book attention can be counterproductive—highlighting that AD mitigation presupposes adequate visual perception.
