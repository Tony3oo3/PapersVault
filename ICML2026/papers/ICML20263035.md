## Problem

Standard preference datasets for text-to-image models store only final winner/loser images, discarding the prior noise that defines each generation trajectory. For rectified flow models, whose sampling follows near-straight paths indexed by a specific noise sample, this omission forces DPO-style methods to reconstruct trajectories via independent forward noising, introducing a mismatch between the training surrogate and the model's actual reverse dynamics. This mismatch inflates variance, destabilizes training, and wastes compute.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a text prompt and produces a single RGB image via 50-step Euler sampling with guidance scale 1 on FLUX.1-dev or SD3-M. During training, the input is a sextuple (prompt, winner prior noise, winner image, loser prior noise, loser image, continuous reward gap δr) rather than the conventional (prompt, winner, loser) triplet, with the prior noises being the exact Gaussian samples used to generate each image. The reward gap is computed by a pre-trained HPSv2.1 reward model and serves as both a pseudo-label and a difficulty signal for dynamic regularization.

## Method

PNAPO exploits the straight-line property of rectified flow to estimate intermediate latent states via simple noise-image interpolation x_t = (1−t)x_0 + t·x_T, where x_T is the stored prior noise and x_0 is the generated image, replacing Diffusion-DPO's independent forward noising. This endpoint-conditioned interpolation yields a tighter DPO-style surrogate: the KL divergence between the interpolation approximation and the true reverse process is provably bounded above by the KL of Diffusion-DPO's forward-noising estimator (Eq. 12). The resulting loss compares policy and reference velocity predictions at interpolated states, weighted by a dynamic regularization coefficient β(δr, n) = β·f(δr)·g(n), where f(δr) = 2σ(δr)−1 scales updates by reward-gap difficulty and g(n) is a cosine-annealed schedule that halves the regularization after a user-defined step threshold. This combination reduces training compute by ~12× over Diffusion-DPO while improving preference metrics across FLUX and SD3-M.

## Results

On HPDv2, PNAPO-FLUX achieves HPSv2.1 of 31.71 and CLIP of 34.71, while PNAPO-SD3-M reaches HPSv2.1 of 31.62 and CLIP of 33.65, both outperforming their base models and all baselines across every metric. On OPDv1, PNAPO-FLUX attains HPSv2.1 of 32.10 (+1.36 over base FLUX) and PNAPO-SD3-M reaches 33.09 (+1.13 over base SD3-M). On GenEval, PNAPO improves overall scores from 0.68→0.73 (SD3-M) and 0.65→0.69 (FLUX). Training cost is reduced by ~12×: 35.2 GPU-hours for PNAPO-FLUX vs. 422.4 for Diffusion-DPO-FLUX.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Diffusion-DPO (Wallace et al., 2024), PNAPO wins on every metric for both backbones: on FLUX/HPDv2, HPSv2.1 improves from 30.84 to 31.71 (+0.87) with an 84.6% win rate, and on FLUX/OPDv1 from 30.79 to 32.10 (+1.31). Against IPO (Azar et al., 2024), PNAPO-FLUX achieves 31.71 vs. 30.87 HPSv2.1 on HPDv2 (81.1% win rate) and 32.10 vs. 30.91 on OPDv1. Against CaPO (Lee et al., 2025b), PNAPO-SD3-M leads on GenEval overall (0.73 vs. 0.71), with the largest gap in the Count sub-metric (0.71 vs. 0.63). PNAPO also requires roughly 12× fewer GPU-hours than Diffusion-DPO (35.2 vs. 422.4 for FLUX; 20.8 vs. 249.6 for SD3-M), making it the only method that simultaneously improves quality and reduces training cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

PNAPO extends the DPO-style preference optimization lineage (Rafailov et al. 2023; Wallace et al. 2024) by exploiting a structural property specific to rectified flow: the near-straight-line trajectory between prior noise and data. Where Diffusion-DPO and its variants estimate intermediate latents via an independent forward noising process (which mismatches the true reverse dynamics in diffusion), PNAPO stores the actual prior noise used at generation time and recovers intermediate states through simple linear interpolation. This shifts the trajectory-estimation problem from a stochastic approximation to a deterministic one, yielding a tighter KL-based surrogate. The dynamic regularization schedule (β(δr, n)) is a further departure from fixed-β DPO, adapting update magnitude to both reward-gap difficulty and training progress.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 12× reduction in GPU-hours versus Diffusion-DPO (35.2 vs. 422.4 H800-hours on FLUX) makes offline preference alignment on large RF backbones substantially more accessible, lowering the barrier for iterative quality tuning. The conceptual reframing of prior noise as trajectory identity (rather than incidental bookkeeping) provides a principled justification for why RF models benefit from noise-tracked data in ways that standard diffusion models do not. Key limitations include the restriction to self-generated pairs (cross-model transfer is not possible due to noise-distribution mismatch), reliance on a single reward model for pseudo-labeling, and the offline-only setting that precludes continuous policy improvement. Open questions include whether the interpolation-based estimator degrades for highly curved trajectories (e.g., early in RF training before straightening), and whether online or semi-online variants can retain the compute advantage while adapting to distribution shift.
