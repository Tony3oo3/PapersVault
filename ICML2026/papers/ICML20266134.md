## Problem

Specializing LLMs for specific inference scenarios without scenario-specific training data remains difficult because existing post-training compression methods rely on global or average importance criteria that are brittle across diverse, long-tailed real-world scenarios, and MoE-style routing is vulnerable to router mis-specification. The paper asks whether one can practically and robustly extract scenario-specific pruned subnetworks from a frozen LLM base at inference time while preserving reasoning capability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266134/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen LLM backbone, input-only text query pools organized by semantic domain (used only for offline axis construction, with no ground-truth labels to avoid data leaks), a new scenario's first input (or small prefix), and a hardware-dependent budget B mapped to a head budget K_B. It produces a binary head pruning mask m_s ∈ {0,1}^{|H|} that disables specific attention heads across all layers, yielding an executable pruned subnetwork f_θ^(m_s) compiled once at scenario start and reused for all subsequent turns with zero further optimization overhead.

## Method

The core insight is subspace-pathway coupling: quasi-orthogonal domain subspaces in embedding space correspond to sparse, partially separable sets of attention-head pathways in parameter space. Domain-Basis Synthesis (DBS) constructs a stable coordinate system by stopword-filtering inputs, projecting embeddings via shared PCA to d'=M−1 dimensions, computing per-domain centroid axes, and greedily selecting a compact axis subset that balances orthogonality and coverage. Probe-based Scenario Pruning (PSP) trains layer-wise linear probes on post-attention residual slices to estimate axis alignment, computes cached head-wise importance as expected axis-aligned energy of residual write-backs, and identifies a domain-invariant whitelist via statistical significance testing. At inference, probe outputs are aggregated into a scenario-level domain mixture whose normalized entropy (knowledge breadth) modulates the keep-ratio; a non-linear transformation amplifies high-similarity domains, and heads are scored as a weighted sum of effective domain similarity times cached importance, with the top-scoring heads selected under the budget to form the final mask.

## Results

On LLaMA-2-13B-chat, SubspacePath Pruner achieves 43.0/32.5/20.2 token-level Recall on XDomainBench (Selected/OOD/Cross) at moderate pruning, exceeding the dense model (29.6/26.1/18.4) with retention values of 1.48/1.56/1.78. On cross-dataset tests, it reaches 33.7 Recall on Natural Questions (vs. 30.3 dense, retention 1.20) while maintaining competitive scores on CommonsenseQA (19.4) and ARC (22.9). Under aggressive pruning, cross-domain Recall remains above dense (22.9 vs. 18.4) with 1.92× retention, while most baselines drop below 0.5 retention in OOD settings. Pruning compilation takes only 0.027–0.068 s per scenario, yielding up to 3.22× inference speedup on LLaMA-13B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SubspacePath Pruner decisively outperforms all five pruning baselines on XDomainBench at both moderate and aggressive pruning ratios. At moderate pruning, it achieves 43.0 Recall on Selected-domain (vs. 33.82 for DaSS, the strongest baseline) and 32.5 on OOD (vs. 28.62 DaSS), with retention values above 1.4 across all splits—indicating that the pruned model is both more accurate and faster than the dense model. Under aggressive pruning, the gap widens dramatically: SubspacePath maintains 22.9 cross-domain Recall while Wanda collapses to 8.77 and RIA to 8.52, and retention stays above 1.2 versus below 0.5 for most baselines in OOD settings. On cross-dataset tests, SubspacePath leads on Natural Questions (33.66 vs. 27.64 for Probe Pruning, the closest competitor) but trails slightly on CommonsenseQA (19.43 vs. 21.75 Probe Pruning), suggesting the method's gains are most pronounced in knowledge-intensive, domain-specific reasoning. Against routed sparse models (Table 5), Qwen2.5-14B + SubspacePath achieves 44.1/31.3 OOD/Cross Recall with only 14.0B loaded parameters and 0.53 s inference time, outperforming Mixtral top-2 (40.8/24.0 Recall, 46.7B loaded, 4.24 s) and Qwen2-57B-A14B (41.9/25.1, 57.0B loaded, 4.08 s) at comparable active-parameter budgets. Pruning compilation is also the fastest: 0.060 s for LLaMA-13B versus 0.275–0.599 s for all baselines, and inference speedup reaches 3.22× (light) / 1.51× (heavy) on LLaMA-13B, far exceeding the 0.39–0.96× range of competing methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: representation probing (Alain & Bengio, 2016) and transformer circuit analysis (Elhage et al., 2021), using the former as a diagnostic signal to guide the latter's head-level decomposition. It departs from global-importance structured pruning (Wanda, LLM-Pruner) and token-level MoE routing by introducing a scenario-conditioned, embedding-space-anchored criterion for head selection. The closest prior work, probe-driven pruning (Le et al., 2025), uses probing signals but lacks the formal subspace-alignment grounding that this paper provides through its Domain-Basis Synthesis coordinate system.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that scenario-aligned head pruning can *improve* accuracy over the dense model (retention > 1.0) reframes pruning as a structural regularizer that suppresses cross-domain pathway interference, not merely a compression tool. However, the practical impact is tempered by the requirement for offline domain pools, modest speedups (1.27× at 30% pruning), and gains that are most pronounced on the authors' own XDomainBench rather than standard benchmarks. Open questions include whether the subspace-pathway coupling generalizes to non-Transformer architectures, whether the domain-pool requirement can be relaxed, and whether the approach extends to FFN-level or layer-level pruning.
