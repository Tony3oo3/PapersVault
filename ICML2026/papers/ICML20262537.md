## Problem

MLLMs struggle with fine-grained perception in ultra-high-resolution images where tiny objects occupy only 0.2–1% of the frame. Existing visual search strategies face a fundamental dilemma: prior-free methods (greedy grid search, MCTS) are inefficient and require many MLLM interactions, while prior-driven methods (attention maps, CLIP similarity) rely on static heuristics that lose logical reasoning context and cannot correct initial biases through posterior updates. No prior approach formulates the search as a principled global optimization over a continuous spatial-scale space with both prior guidance and iterative correction.

## Input / Output

The method takes an ultra-high-resolution RGB image (average resolutions up to ~7400×5400 on HR-Bench8K) and a free-form text query as input. During the search loop, the MLLM is queried as a scoring oracle on individual crops, returning a scalar relevance score in [0,1] per candidate (scale, position) pair. The final output is a consolidated, information-dense visual representation — a squeezed composite image that collapses uninformative background between distant relevant regions — which is fed back to the MLLM to produce the text answer. The entire pipeline is training-free and uses a smaller proxy model (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-3B/7B) to precompute attention priors offline.

## Method

BVS formulates visual search as Bayesian Optimization over a continuous 3D manifold of (scale, x, y) coordinates, unifying a reasoning-aware prior with iterative posterior correction. The prior is constructed via an Early-stop Attention Rollout that propagates relevance from the final query token back to visual tokens but terminates at the middle layer to avoid a newly identified 'Attention Divergence' phenomenon where shallow-layer attention over-smooths. The core search uses a Scale-Aware Non-stationary Kernel (SANK) whose spatial length-scale ℓ(s) increases with observation scale, explicitly modeling the fact that small displacements matter more at fine zoom levels; the kernel is proven PSD via Paciorek–Schervish's non-stationary covariance framework. A GP-UCB acquisition function balances exploration of unobserved scales against exploitation of high-relevance regions, and the paper establishes a sub-linear cumulative regret bound under an RKHS norm assumption on the residual function. After convergence, multi-scale posterior aggregation and adaptive-threshold semantic masking produce a compact squeezed image for the final MLLM inference.

## Results

BVS achieves state-of-the-art fine-grained perception across all evaluated benchmarks. With Qwen3-VL-8B as the base model, BVS reaches 93.2% overall on V*Bench (+3.1 over the best baseline ZoomEye at 90.1%), 84.8% on HR-Bench4K (+6.0 over ZoomEye at 78.8%), and 79.5% on HR-Bench8K (+4.0 over ZoomEye at 75.5%). These gains are consistent across all four base models tested (InternVL3.5-4B/8B, Qwen3-VL-4B/8B), with the largest absolute improvements observed on the 8B-scale models. BVS also outperforms all training-based visual search methods, surpassing the strongest learned baseline (TreeVGR-7B) by +2.1 on V*Bench and +7.7 on HR-Bench4K.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BVS outperforms all ten baselines across every benchmark. Among training-free methods, BVS beats ZoomEye by +3.1 on V*Bench, +6.0 on HR-Bench4K, and +4.0 on HR-Bench8K (Qwen3-VL-8B); it surpasses RAP by +4.7, +8.9, and +7.0 respectively; and exceeds ViCrop by +8.4, +6.3, and +5.5. Against training-based methods, BVS (Qwen3-VL-8B) leads DeepEyes-7B by +3.1 on V*Bench and +9.7 on HR-Bench4K, TreeVGR-7B by +2.1 and +7.7, Thyme-7B by +11.0 and +7.8, Mini-o3-7B by +5.0, Simple O3-7B by +2.8, and Pixel Reasoner-7B by +8.9 on V*Bench. BVS also surpasses the native tool-based search of Qwen3-VL-8B (90.1 / 82.3 / 78.0) by +3.1 / +2.5 / +1.5 on V*Bench / HR-Bench4K / HR-Bench8K, confirming that the Bayesian optimization loop adds value beyond the model's built-in cropping capability.

## Relation to Prior Work

BVS synthesizes three distinct research threads: Gaussian Process Upper Confidence Bound (GP-UCB) acquisition from Bayesian optimization (Srinivas et al., 2009), attention rollout for interpretability (Abnar & Zuidema, 2020), and non-stationary covariance construction (Paciorek & Schervish, 2006). It departs from prior-free visual search methods (ZoomEye, MCTS-based approaches, learning-based agents like DeepEyes) by introducing a reasoning-aware prior that accelerates convergence, and from prior-driven methods (RAP, ViCrop, FOCUS) by adding iterative posterior correction that rectifies initial biases. The key conceptual departure is formulating visual search as global optimization over a continuous spatial-scale manifold rather than discrete grid traversal, combined with a scale-aware kernel that explicitly models how spatial correlation length varies with zoom level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BVS offers a principled, training-free alternative to both heuristic and learned visual search strategies, with the practical advantage of plug-and-play compatibility across MLLM backbones (demonstrated on InternVL3.5 and Qwen3-VL at 4B and 8B scales). The sub-linear regret guarantee and Mercer's theorem proof provide rare theoretical grounding in an area dominated by empirical heuristics. Open questions include: how the method scales to video or 3D scenes, whether the proxy-model attention cache strategy generalizes when the proxy and target models diverge significantly in architecture, and whether the fixed 5-step budget is sufficient for extremely cluttered scenes with multiple tiny targets. The reliance on a hand-crafted scoring prompt for the MLLM oracle also raises questions about robustness to adversarial or ambiguous queries.
