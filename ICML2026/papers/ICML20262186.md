## Problem

Neural networks in continual reinforcement learning suffer from plasticity loss — a progressive inability to learn new tasks — driven by NTK rank collapse, unbounded weight growth, and representational churn. Existing mitigations are algorithmic (resets, shrink-perturb, regularization) and require task-boundary information, sensitive hyperparameters, or external optimization-level intervention. The paper asks whether a lightweight, task-agnostic architectural modification can intrinsically bound representational drift and preserve gradient diversity without any of these external constraints.

## Input / Output

The agent receives RGB image observations from ProcGen environments (Coinrun, Jumper, Fruitbot, Heist) or low-dimensional state vectors from Gym Control, processed by a 4-convolutional-layer + 1-linear-layer CNN encoder followed by PPO policy and value heads. The agent has no access to task boundaries or identities; distribution shifts (pixel permutation, level re-seeding, or level-set expansion) are applied every 5,000 epochs over a 50,000-epoch horizon, and the shared policy must adapt online. Output is the PPO action distribution; performance is measured as mean reward over the final 50 episodes of each task, normalized to the initial task.

## Method

InterpLayers replace standard network layers with a dual-pathway design: a fixed, parameter-free reference pathway (identity, Johnson-Lindenstrauss projection, or zero-padding depending on dimensionality) and a standard learnable projection pathway, combined via input-dependent sigmoid interpolation weights z(x) ∈ (0,1)^h. The reference pathway anchors the representation geometrically, while the projection pathway adapts through backpropagation; the interpolation weights learn when to trust each pathway. Theoretically, this structure upper-bounds per-update output variability (Theorem 3.1) and guarantees a non-zero NTK rank contribution from the interpolation pathway (Theorem 3.2), preventing rank collapse even if the projection degenerates. Dropout is applied exclusively to the projection pathway to inflate the representational gap D(x) = ‖h_proj − h_ref‖, keeping interpolation gradients active throughout training. The architecture is orthogonal to algorithmic interventions (SSP+LN, ReDo, L2-init) and can be combined with them, requiring no reset schedules, perturbation magnitudes, or other sensitive hyperparameters.

## Results

On four ProcGen environments (Coinrun, Jumper, Fruitbot, Heist) under three distribution shifts (permute, window, expand), InterpLayers achieve performance retention comparable to the strongest algorithmic baseline SSP+LN, winning on 7 of 12 environment-shift combinations (e.g., Coinrun-permute: 0.655 vs 0.546; Jumper-permute: 0.276 vs −0.133; Heist-permute: 0.465 vs −1.787). InterpLayers outperform all architectural baselines (ResNet-like, Highway, HyperNet-like) and algorithmic baselines (ReDo, L2-init) across nearly all conditions. On continual Gym Control, InterpLayers achieve an aggregate score of −97.101, second only to SSP+LN (−94.490), while outperforming Standard (124.831), L2-init (119.018), and HyperNet-like (112.742).

## Baselines & Comparisons

InterpLayers achieve performance on par with SSP+LN (Juliani & Ash, 2024), the strongest algorithmic baseline, winning 7 of 12 ProcGen environment-shift combinations and losing 5, with no single method dominating across all conditions. Against architectural baselines, InterpLayers clearly outperform ResNet-like (He et al., 2016), Highway Networks (Srivastava et al., 2015), and HyperNet-like (Ha et al., 2016) across all three distribution shifts, with ResNet-like dropping below zero relative performance in permute and Highway showing consistent plasticity loss in window. InterpLayers also surpass Standard+Dropout (Srivastava et al., 2014), which retains some performance in Coinrun and Heist but fails to match InterpLayers in Jumper and Fruitbot. Among algorithmic baselines, InterpLayers outperform ReDo (Sokar et al., 2023) and L2-init (Juliani & Ash, 2024) in most environments, with L2-init showing particularly weak performance in the expand shift. On Gym Control, InterpLayers are strongest in C-CartPole (56.360) and C-MountainCar (−165.166), while SSP+LN leads in C-LunarLander (−153.874 vs −174.383), confirming that InterpLayers are competitive with the best algorithmic methods without requiring optimization-level intervention.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant algorithmic paradigm for mitigating plasticity loss in continual reinforcement learning (reset schedules, shrink-perturb, ReDo, regenerative regularization) by proposing a purely architectural intervention: InterpLayers, which combine a fixed parameter-free reference pathway with a learnable projection pathway via input-dependent interpolation weights. Conceptually, it repurposes gating mechanisms from Highway Networks and GRUs, but with a critical distinction—the reference pathway is a frozen identity/Johnson-Lindenstrauss projection rather than a recurrent hidden state or learnable skip, making the mechanism self-regulating without external optimization-level intervention. The theoretical analysis draws on NTK rank theory (Lyle et al., 2024) and churn analysis (Tang et al., 2025) to formalize why the dual-pathway structure bounds representational drift and preserves gradient diversity, positioning the contribution as orthogonal to and combinable with existing algorithmic solutions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

InterpLayers offer a hyperparameter-free, drop-in architectural complement to algorithmic plasticity methods, which is practically valuable given that methods like SSP+LN and ReDo require careful tuning of reset frequencies, perturbation magnitudes, or regularization strengths. The finding that interpolation weights self-organize toward a reference-preferring regime (~0.2) rather than degenerating into a ResNet-like skip (0.5) suggests the learned gating captures a meaningful stability-plasticity tradeoff that fixed architectures cannot. However, the performance gains over the strongest algorithmic baseline (SSP+LN) are modest and inconsistent across environments and shift types, and the evaluation is limited to relatively small ProcGen and Gym Control environments. Open questions include whether the mechanism scales to larger networks, whether the encoder-side NTK rank diagnostics generalize to full-network analysis, and whether the architectural benefit persists under different sparsity regimes or longer training horizons.
