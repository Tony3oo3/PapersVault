## Problem

Existing graph and hypergraph generative models face a fundamental scalability–expressiveness tradeoff: flat architectures (diffusion, flow matching) that jointly model topology and features suffer quadratic complexity and fail on large structures, while hierarchical coarsen-expand methods scale well but generate only unfeatured topology. No prior method can jointly generate both topology and node/hyperedge features at scale for either graphs or hypergraphs, leaving a gap for applications like 3D mesh and point cloud generation where geometric features are essential.

## Input / Output

At inference, FAHNES requires only a target node count N and the feature dimension as input; it starts from a single super-node carrying the full scale encoding N and iteratively expands and refines until the target size is reached. The output is a complete featured bipartite graph (for hypergraphs) or graph, from which the hypergraph is reconstructed by collapsing right-side nodes into hyperedges. For 3D mesh and point cloud tasks, node features are 3D coordinates (d=3), while hyperedge/edge features are absent; the model jointly predicts connectivity decisions, scale-encoding splits, and feature refinements at every refinement step.

## Method

FAHNES builds multi-scale representations via spectrum-preserving coarsening on the clique expansion of the hypergraph, then learns to invert this process through a coarsen-expand pipeline on the bipartite representation. Two key innovations drive the approach: (1) a hierarchical scale encoding that assigns each cluster an integer count of remaining expansions, recursively split among children, providing local control over growth and preventing the uneven-region inconsistency that plagues prior hierarchical methods; and (2) a multi-scale graph OT coupling that aligns permuted children within each cluster expansion (only 2 or 6 permutations for 2–3 children) to reduce training variance without breaking structural or feature consistency. The generative model is an endpoint-parameterized flow-matching network with a local PPGN backbone and SignNet positional encodings, trained with a masked MSE loss that excludes stationary (scale-encoding-1) nodes via graph inpainting to concentrate gradient signal on active refinement regions. Scale-encoding splits are projected onto the simplex, and features are conditioned on parent features via FiLM layers, extending the scale-wise autoregressive idea so predictions at one scale guide the next.

## Results

On featureless hypergraphs, FAHNES achieves 87.8% validity and 0.006 spectral MMD on SBM, 99.5% validity on Ego, and reduces node-count error to 0.135 (vs. 69.73 for HYGENE) on ModelNet40 Bookshelf. On featured 3D meshes (ManifoldNet), FAHNES achieves the lowest Chamfer distance of 0.048 (airplane) and 0.064 (bench), outperforming the best baseline (Sequential) by 41% and 62% respectively. On large point-cloud graphs (~1434 nodes), FAHNES is the only method that completes evaluation (Spectral 0.005, Ratio 3.2), while all flat baselines (DiGress, DeFoG, Flow Matching) run out of memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FAHNES decisively outperforms all flat hypergraph baselines (HyperPA, VAE, GAN, 2D Diffusion), which achieve 0% validity on Ego and Tree and high spectral errors (0.089–0.273) on SBM, while FAHNES reaches 87.8–99.5% validity and spectral MMD ≤ 0.006. Against the closest hierarchical baseline HYGENE, FAHNES improves validity by 12–22 percentage points and reduces node-count error by 50–99× on ModelNet40 meshes. On unfeatured graphs, FAHNES matches or beats HSpectre (the prior hierarchical method) on Protein (Ratio 3.8 vs. 5.9) and Point Cloud (Ratio 3.2 vs. 7.0), and surpasses BwR on all five graph datasets. On large point-cloud graphs (~1434 nodes), FAHNES is the only method that avoids OOM, achieving Spectral 0.005 and Ratio 3.2, while DiGress, DeFoG, and Flow Matching all fail. On featured 3D meshes, FAHNES beats the sequential disjoint baseline (ChamDist 0.048 vs. 0.082 airplane; 0.064 vs. 0.169 bench) and all naive-joint baselines (WGAN, Flow Matching, VAE), confirming that joint topology–feature generation is essential for hypergraph meshes.

## Relation to Prior Work

FAHNES extends the hierarchical coarsen-expand paradigm introduced by HSpectre (Bergmeister et al., 2024) for graphs and HYGENE (Gailhard et al., 2025) for hypergraphs, which previously generated only unfeatured topology. The key departure is the joint modeling of topology and features at every scale, enabled by a novel hierarchical scale encoding (replacing the simpler size-concatenation of prior work) and a multi-scale graph OT coupling that generalizes minibatch OT (Tong et al., 2024; Pooladian et al., 2023) to the hierarchical setting. The generative backbone uses flow matching (Lipman et al., 2023) over a local PPGN architecture, and the next-scale prediction framing follows Tian et al. (2024), while spectrum-preserving coarsening (Loukas, 2019) provides the multi-scale decomposition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FAHNES is the first method to achieve quasi-linear O((n+m+k) log n) complexity for jointly generating topology and features in both graphs and hypergraphs, enabling generation of structures (e.g., ~1400-node point clouds, 3D meshes) where flat methods like DiGress and DeFoG run out of memory. This opens the door to scalable generative modeling of real-world structured data with semantic attributes (molecular features, circuit netlists, 3D geometry). Key limitations remain: the method still struggles with very large hypergraphs (ModelNet Bookshelf/Piano), assumes continuous feature distributions, and training is computationally expensive. Extending to categorical, multi-modal, or heterogeneous features and further reducing training cost are natural next steps.
