## Problem

Existing RL methods for LLM agents become unstable in long-horizon interactions because Monte Carlo estimators propagate a single outcome reward uniformly across all actions in a trajectory, failing to distinguish effective from ineffective steps. This point-wise comparison in the exponentially large discrete language action space ignores underlying semantic structure, yielding unbounded gradient variance that grows with horizon length. The paper asks whether aggregating semantically similar state-action pairs into a lower-dimensional intent space can produce bounded-variance learning signals that stabilize critic-free policy optimization.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a task prompt (e.g., a multi-hop QA question or a TextCraft game objective) and iteratively produces natural language actions—search queries, reasoning traces, or game commands—over up to 4 (SearchQA) or 30 (TextCraft) turns, with environment feedback (retrieved documents or game state) appended between turns. Each state-action pair is encoded by a frozen Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B encoder into a d-dimensional semantic vector, and the method operates on these embeddings rather than raw token sequences. The final output is a natural language answer or a sequence of game actions, with rewards being binary (correct/incorrect) at the episode level.

## Method

HPO reformulates the policy gradient as a comparison between the current policy distribution and a hindsight distribution (constructed from only successful trajectories) in a semantic intent space, rather than in the discrete token space. Both distributions are represented as empirical measures over encoder embeddings of state-action pairs, and their discrepancy is measured by the 1-Wasserstein distance rather than KL divergence. The Kantorovich potential from the Wasserstein dual provides step-level advantages that are 1-Lipschitz in the intent space, guaranteeing variance bounded by D²/4 (the metric diameter squared over four), in contrast to the χ²-divergence-bounded variance of KL-based signals which can be arbitrarily large. The final advantage is a weighted interpolation between the standard GRPO episode-level advantage and the Wasserstein-derived step-level advantage, controlled by a hyperparameter ω that trades off bias against variance. The entire procedure is built on top of GRPO's clipped policy update, adding only ~0.8% computational overhead for the embedding and Wasserstein computation.

## Results

On SearchQA with Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct, offline HPO achieves 39.2% average accuracy across seven QA benchmarks, outperforming GRPO (34.7%), PPO (34.6%), Search-R1 (32.5%), and ZeroSearch (31.7%). With Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, online HPO reaches 44.1% average, a 4.6-point gain over GRPO (39.5%) and 3.6 points over PPO (40.5%). On the TextCraft long-horizon agent task, HPO achieves 86.0% (3B) and 93.0% (7B) success rate, surpassing GRPO (81.0%/87.0%) and PPO (76.0%/89.0%). The gains are most pronounced on multi-hop benchmarks (e.g., Bamboogle: 36.0% vs. 28.4% for GRPO on 3B), and HPO exhibits substantially lower variance across seeds (±0.8 vs. ±1.5 for GRPO).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HPO consistently outperforms all four baselines across both model scales. Against GRPO (the primary critic-free baseline), HPOoff gains +4.5 points (3B) and HPOon gains +4.6 points (7B) in average SearchQA accuracy, with the largest margins on multi-hop tasks (e.g., Bamboogle: +7.6 pts on 3B, +6.0 pts on 7B). Against PPO (the actor-critic baseline), HPO gains +4.6 pts (3B) and +3.6 pts (7B), while also avoiding the slow critic warm-up that delays PPO convergence. Against the recent RL-trained search agents Search-R1 and ZeroSearch, HPO leads by +6.7 to +10.5 points on average, indicating that the hindsight-based step-level credit assignment provides a meaningful advantage over purely outcome-reward-driven training. On TextCraft, HPO achieves the highest success rate in all settings (86.0%/93.0% vs. GRPO's 81.0%/87.0%), confirming generalizability beyond QA. Notably, HPOoff exhibits the lowest variance across seeds (±0.8 on 3B vs. ±1.5 for GRPO), reflecting the bounded-variance property of the Wasserstein-based learning signal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) as its base policy optimization algorithm, extending it with a hindsight-distribution-based step-level advantage computed via Wasserstein distance in a semantic intent space. Conceptually, it reframes existing Monte Carlo policy gradient methods (GRPO, REINFORCE++, PPO) through a unified hindsight-distribution lens (Lemma 4.2), then departs from the standard KL-divergence formulation by replacing point-wise comparisons in the discrete action space with optimal-transport-based comparisons in a lower-dimensional embedding space. The approach draws on Kantorovich potential theory from optimal transport (Villani et al., 2008) and the practical observation that LLM action spaces, while exponentially large, span a comparatively small set of semantic intents.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HPO addresses a well-documented practical failure mode—GRPO's instability and reward collapse in long-horizon agent training—without the computational overhead of a critic model, making it a potentially drop-in replacement for critic-free RL pipelines. The finding that the Wasserstein-derived step-level advantage (A_S) alone can drive effective policy updates without outcome rewards opens a path toward training on tasks where reward specification is difficult or costly. Open questions include how the method scales to more complex multimodal or open-ended environments, whether the intent-space construction can be made more fine-grained or learned end-to-end, and whether the bias introduced by the Wasserstein approximation remains acceptable as task complexity grows.
