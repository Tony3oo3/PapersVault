## Problem

Standard video retrieval encodes clips independently and operates under a context-agnostic inference paradigm, prioritizing local semantic alignment while ignoring the evolving visual state and identity cues from preceding clips. This produces two recurrent failure modes in procedural narratives: state inconsistency (violations of procedural causality, e.g., retrieving a later step showing already-sliced tomatoes) and identity inconsistency (abrupt shifts in actor or environment). The paper formalizes Consistent Video Retrieval (CVR) to make retrieval explicitly conditioned on visual history, and introduces a diagnostic benchmark with hard negatives that isolate these two failure modes.

## Input / Output

At inference, CAST takes a text instruction, a sequence of up to L=5 preceding context clips (encoded by a frozen backbone), and a pre-indexed video gallery, and outputs a ranked list over a fixed 1-vs-9 candidate pool (1 ground-truth + up to 3 state negatives + up to 3 identity negatives + easy negatives). The method operates exclusively on the query side: the gallery is indexed once with the frozen backbone, and CAST computes a predicted next-state embedding on-the-fly from the visual history and instruction, scoring candidates in the same frozen embedding space without re-indexing. The evaluation protocol is a multiple-choice ranking task where semantic matching alone is insufficient by construction, requiring the model to resolve both state and identity consistency.

## Method

CAST is a lightweight plug-and-play adapter atop a frozen vision-language backbone that models procedural progression as a residual state transition: it predicts a transition vector Δ = Δ_cond + Δ_ctx and computes the target embedding as v̂_t = Norm(v_{t-1} + Δ), so the anchor state carries persistent identity cues through the residual connection while Δ captures procedural change. The dual-path design decomposes Δ into (1) an instruction-conditioned MLP over the concatenation of text and anchor-state embeddings, and (2) a multi-head cross-attention branch where the text embedding queries the visual history sequence to capture longer-range narrative context. Training uses a type-aware contrastive objective with separate state and identity contrastive losses (weighted λ_s=5.0, λ_i=1.0) alongside in-batch InfoNCE, explicitly preventing overlapping identity negatives from dominating gradients. At inference, a Full Ensemble score combines semantic text-video matching, visual continuity with the anchor, and CAST-predicted next-state compatibility, with coefficients selected on a held-out validation split.

## Results

On the CVR benchmark with a frozen CLIP ViT-B/32 backbone, CAST achieves 44.77% accuracy on YouCook2, 40.47% on COIN, and 47.39% on CrossTask (1-vs-9 protocol), with a mean rank of ~2.1 across all three datasets. CAST attains the highest State diagnostic accuracy (53.81%) among all methods, indicating superior procedural-state discrimination. Across diverse frozen backbones (InternVideo2, VideoPrism, GME-Qwen2-VL-2B, Qwen3-VL-Embedding), CAST consistently improves over the corresponding zero-shot baseline by 15–35 percentage points in accuracy, and raises identity preservation from ~30% to 69–78%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAST outperforms the zero-shot CLIP baseline by a wide margin on all three datasets (e.g., +19.7 pts on YouCook2, +23.6 pts on CrossTask), confirming that context-aware state-transition modeling is essential beyond semantic matching. Against the strongest aggregation baseline, Late Fusion (Learned), CAST wins decisively on YouCook2 (44.77% vs. 36.60%) and CrossTask (47.39% vs. 25.52%), but loses on COIN (40.47% vs. 44.66%), where consecutive steps share strong visual similarity and scalar re-weighting can exploit visual inertia. Early Fusion is competitive on CrossTask (35.29%) but degrades sharply on COIN (15.12%), while CAST maintains a balanced profile across all three. On the diagnostic State metric, CAST leads all baselines (53.81% vs. 45.52% for CLIP and 40.06% for Late Fusion Learned), whereas on the Ident. metric, Early Fusion (83.59%) and Late Fusion Learned (76.06%) slightly edge out CAST (74.67%), though all substantially exceed the context-free CLIP baseline (28.90%). In the cross-backbone universality study (Table 3), CAST improves over the zero-shot baseline on every backbone tested, with gains of +24.6 pts (GME-Qwen2-VL-2B, YouCook2) to +34.9 pts (InternVideo2, YouCook2), demonstrating that the adapter transfers effectively across frozen representation spaces.

## Relation to Prior Work

CAST carves new ground by repurposing the residual-connection principle (He et al., 2016) and content-motion decomposition intuition (Villegas et al., 2017) into a retrieval setting: rather than matching a query to a static clip embedding, it predicts a residual state-transition vector Δ that extrapolates the current visual state toward a causally plausible next step. The dual-path design (instruction-conditioned MLP + cross-attention over visual history) draws on standard transformer components but applies them to a novel sequential retrieval objective. Conceptually, the work sits at the intersection of procedural video understanding (YouCook2/COIN/CrossTask literature), parameter-efficient adapters (LoRA, Houlsby et al.), and latent-space world models (V-JEPA 2), but departs from all three by framing retrieval as context-conditioned state evolution rather than static alignment or discrete state classification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CVR benchmark with its state/identity hard-negative protocol provides a diagnostic tool that standard retrieval metrics (MSR-VTT-style) cannot offer, and the plug-and-play adapter design makes the approach immediately applicable to any frozen video-text embedding space without re-indexing. The preliminary generation-reranking result (CAST preferred 55.1% vs. 38.6% over text matching on Veo candidates) is timely given the rapid adoption of video generation models, though the evidence is thin (158 non-overlap prompts, 3 annotators). Key open questions include whether the fixed L=5 context window suffices for longer narratives, whether the residual formulation generalizes beyond procedural activities to open-ended video, and whether explicit geometric constraints on Δ could further separate state from identity in the latent space.
