## Problem

Feed-forward 3D reconstruction models like MASt3R produce dense pointmaps with heuristic confidence scores that lack probabilistic interpretation and are poorly aligned with actual geometric errors under occlusions, textureless regions, and distribution shifts. Existing uncertainty quantification methods (MC dropout, deep ensembles) require multiple stochastic forward passes or multiple trained models, making them prohibitively expensive for dense pointmap prediction with hundreds of thousands of correlated 3D outputs per image pair. The paper addresses the need for a single-pass, probabilistically grounded per-point uncertainty estimate that is both efficient and well-calibrated to empirical reconstruction error.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of RGB images at 224 resolution (with random crop augmentation during training) and produces, per view, a dense H×W×3 pointmap in a shared coordinate frame along with per-pixel NIW evidential parameters (κ, Cholesky factor L of Ψ, ν). These parameters define a closed-form multivariate Student-t predictive distribution for each 3D point, from which aleatoric uncertainty (E[Σ] = Ψ/(ν−4)), epistemic uncertainty (Var[μ] = Ψ/(κ(ν−4))), and total uncertainty are extracted in closed form. The backbone is a frozen MASt3R ViT; only lightweight add-on heads (evidential UQ head and gated residual head) are trained, keeping inference to a single forward pass with ~11% latency overhead over the base model.

## Method

Trust3R augments a frozen MASt3R backbone with two lightweight heads: (1) an evidential UQ head that predicts per-pixel NIW parameters (m, κ, Ψ via Cholesky factor L, ν) from concatenated encoder/decoder ViT tokens, inducing a multivariate Student-t predictive distribution over each 3D point; and (2) a gated residual head that refines the frozen pointmap mean via a learned residual Δm modulated by a sigmoid gate σ(G), initialized near zero so training starts from the pretrained prediction. Training minimizes the negative log-likelihood of the Student-t predictive distribution plus an evidence regularizer L_evi = ‖x̂ − m‖²(κ + ν) that penalizes unjustified confidence when prediction errors are large, combined with the base regression loss via convex mixing. A depthwise-separable post-upsampling smoothing operator (identity-initialized) suppresses patch-grid artifacts from token-to-pixel upsampling. The key design insight is that the NIW prior captures cross-coordinate covariance within each 3D point (unlike factorized NIG), while the gated residual preserves pretrained geometric accuracy and stabilizes evidential learning under challenging conditions.

## Results

On the primary uncertainty-ranking evaluation (Table 1), Trust3R achieves the best single-pass AURC (0.3861 avg), AUSE (0.1684 avg), and Spearman ρ (0.4898 avg) across ScanNet++, TUM RGB-D, and KITTI, outperforming MASt3R's heuristic confidence (AURC 0.4155, ρ 0.4057) and heteroscedastic regression (AURC 0.3973, ρ 0.4183). On ScanNet++, Trust3R reduces AURC by 25% and AUSE by 41% relative to MASt3R. In reconstruction accuracy (Table 2), Trust3R improves MAE from 0.2164 to 0.1959 on ScanNet++ and from 0.0938 to 0.0873 on TUM RGB-D, while incurring only ~31 ms additional inference latency (80.92 vs. 49.35 ms/pair) over the frozen MASt3R backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MASt3R's built-in heuristic confidence, Trust3R wins on all three UQ metrics across every dataset (e.g., ScanNet++ AURC 0.1233 vs. 0.1649, ρ 0.4930 vs. 0.2837) and also improves reconstruction MAE/RMSE on indoor benchmarks. Versus the heteroscedastic Gaussian baseline (Hetero), Trust3R consistently achieves lower AURC/AUSE and higher ρ, with the gap most pronounced on ScanNet++ (AURC 0.1233 vs. 0.1616). Against MC Dropout (T=16), Trust3R substantially outperforms on all UQ metrics (avg AURC 0.3861 vs. 0.4902) while being ~15× faster at inference (81 ms vs. 1226 ms/pair). Deep Ensembles (K=5) remain the strongest on AURC/AUSE (0.2992/0.0916 avg) but require 100 h of training and 317 ms inference; Trust3R narrows this gap to within ~29% on AURC while matching or exceeding DeepEns on Spearman ρ (0.4898 vs. 0.4556) at a fraction of the cost. In the cross-architecture VGGT experiment, attaching the Trust3R head improves ρ from 0.3162 to 0.6419 and AUSE from 0.0452 to 0.0209 over VGGT's native confidence. In the downstream MASt3R-SLAM evaluation, replacing the original weighting with Trust3R uncertainty reduces ATE from 0.0287 to 0.0268 and RPE from 0.0321 to 0.0278 on TUM RGB-D.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the feed-forward pointmap reconstruction paradigm established by DUSt3R and MASt3R, which predict dense 3D pointmaps from image pairs but only provide heuristic confidence scores lacking probabilistic interpretation. It adapts the multivariate Normal-Inverse-Wishart evidential regression framework of Meinert & Lavin (2021)—originally designed for low-dimensional, independent regression outputs—to the dense, spatially-correlated 3D pointmap setting, introducing a gated residual refinement head to preserve pretrained geometric accuracy while stabilizing evidential training. The approach departs from sampling-based UQ methods (MC Dropout, Deep Ensembles) by yielding closed-form Student-t predictive distributions in a single forward pass, and from conformal prediction by providing fine-grained per-point uncertainty rather than worst-case guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Trust3R provides a practical, single-pass reliability signal for feed-forward 3D reconstruction that is directly consumable by downstream geometry pipelines (SLAM, alignment, fusion) without the T× or K× inference overhead of sampling-based methods, making it attractive for real-time robotic and mapping applications. The cross-architecture validation on VGGT (more than doubling Spearman correlation) suggests the evidential head is a transferable component rather than a MASt3R-specific trick. Key limitations include the unimodal per-point distribution (cannot represent multiple plausible 3D hypotheses under strong ambiguity) and the mean-field factorization that omits cross-pixel covariance, both of which are acknowledged as open directions for future work.
