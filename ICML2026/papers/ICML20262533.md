## Problem

Existing LLM-based formal theorem provers excel on Olympiad-level mathematics but fail on undergraduate optimization, where domain-specific formalisms (convexity, KKT conditions, algorithmic convergence) create a severe distribution shift. Naive continual training on optimization corpora degrades performance due to two coupled failures: catastrophic forgetting of general proving skills, and the generation of syntactically valid but strategically stagnant tactics that act as search sinks in tree-based inference.

## Input / Output

The model takes a Lean 4 theorem statement (with type class assumptions and local hypotheses) as input and produces a sequence of Lean 4 tactics that, when executed by the Lean 4.10.0 kernel via LeanDojo, close all proof goals. Inference uses best-first search with temperature 1.3, expansion width 4, and length normalization α=2.0, where the model iteratively proposes single tactics conditioned on the current proof state and receives immediate verifier feedback. Evaluation is reported as Pass@1 and Pass@32 under a fixed cumulative search budget for step-level proving, and Pass@32/256 for whole-proof baselines.

## Method

OptProver is a 7B model fine-tuned from BFS-Prover-V2 through a three-stage continual training pipeline. Stage 1 performs large-scale self-distillation via expert iteration over Mathlib, formalized textbooks (Convex Analysis, Real Analysis), and ~7k DeepTheorem problems, translating static library knowledge into the model's own tactic language. Stage 2 introduces Utility-Aware Preference Optimization (UAPO), which assigns a three-level utility score (proven=2, stagnant=1, invalid=0) to state-tactic pairs from search trees and constructs DPO preference pairs that explicitly penalize valid-but-unhelpful tactics that exhaust the search budget without resolution. Stage 3 applies Perplexity-Weighted DPO (PW-DPO), which modulates the DPO loss with token-level inverse-perplexity weights under the reference model to suppress gradient contributions from out-of-distribution tokens and stabilize training. The combined PW-UAPO objective achieves 55.25% Pass@32 on the new OptBench (400 optimization problems) while improving ProofNet and maintaining MiniF2F performance, demonstrating effective domain transfer without catastrophic forgetting.

## Results

On the newly introduced OptBench (400 undergraduate optimization problems in Lean 4), OptProver with the full pipeline (EI + PW-UAPO) achieves 37.75% Pass@1 and 55.25% Pass@32, a 2.5× improvement over the BFS-Prover-V2-7B base model (13.25% Pass@1, 32.00% Pass@32). The best whole-proof baseline (Goedel-Prover-V2-8B) reaches only 12.50% Pass@32 / 19.75% Pass@256, highlighting the advantage of step-level search on this domain. On out-of-domain benchmarks, OptProver maintains MiniF2F performance parity and improves ProofNet from 21→31 solved (Pass@1) and 42→49 (Pass@32), confirming no catastrophic forgetting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OptProver (EI+PW-UAPO) substantially outperforms all three whole-proof baselines on OptBench: 55.25% Pass@32 vs. DeepSeek-Prover-V2-7B (14.25%), Goedel-Prover-V2-8B (12.50%), and Kimina-Prover-7B (5.75%), a gap of 35–50 percentage points that the authors attribute to the brittleness of end-to-end generation on optimization-specific definitions and high-dimensional calculus. Against the step-level base model BFS-Prover-V2-7B, OptProver more than doubles Pass@1 (37.75% vs. 13.25%) and raises Pass@32 by 23 points (55.25% vs. 32.00%), with the largest relative gains on the 'algorithmic' subcategory (28.47% vs. 6.25% Pass@1). On the out-of-domain ProofNet benchmark, OptProver improves over the base model (31 vs. 21 at Pass@1; 49 vs. 42 at Pass@32), while on MiniF2F it maintains Pass@32 parity and slightly improves Pass@1, demonstrating that the optimization-focused continual training does not cause catastrophic forgetting of general Olympiad-level proving skills.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the step-level formal proving paradigm established by BFS-Prover-V2 (Xin et al., 2025b) and the best-first search framework of Xin et al. (2025a) into a new domain: undergraduate optimization. Its methodological core builds on Direct Preference Optimization (Rafailov et al., 2023), but departs from standard DPO in two ways: introducing a three-level utility signal (proven/stagnant/invalid) to penalize valid-but-unhelpful tactics, and reweighting token-level contributions by reference-model perplexity to stabilize training. The paper also draws on the expert iteration loop and perplexity-based data selection literature, but argues that these tools are insufficient for interactive proving where syntactic validity and strategic utility are decoupled.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of 'interactive distribution shift'—where perplexity minimization on target-domain data actively degrades proving performance due to stylistic mismatch in tactic idioms—challenges a common assumption in LLM fine-tuning and provides a concrete mechanism (valid-but-stagnant tactics as search distractors) for why naive domain adaptation fails in formal proving. OptBench fills a genuine gap as the first dedicated benchmark for formal optimization proofs, and the PW-UAPO recipe offers a practical template for domain-specific adaptation of step-level provers. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other specialized domains (e.g., topology, algebraic geometry), whether the utility-aware preference signal scales to larger search budgets where 'stagnant' and 'proven' become harder to distinguish, and whether the 7B-scale results transfer to larger models where the base policy may already handle optimization syntax more gracefully.
