## Problem

Large multimodal models (LMMs) are trained exclusively with language modeling objectives and lack explicit taxonomic knowledge, causing them to fail at maintaining hierarchical visual recognition (HVR) consistency. No prior work has integrated hierarchical representation learning as a regularization mechanism into LMMs, leaving a gap between the rich tree-structured semantics of real-world concepts and the flat, next-token-prediction paradigm used during fine-tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261528/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image and a text prompt specifying the category query, processed through a standard LMM (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-3B). Crucially, it extracts hidden states from the last LLM layer corresponding to image tokens and uses textual embeddings at each taxonomy level as cross-attention queries to produce hierarchy-specific visual features. The output is a full hierarchical class path from root to leaf (e.g., Plant → Flower → Rose), evaluated under both base-to-base and base-to-novel splits at 1-shot training.

## Method

HiR² is a parameter-free plug-and-play regularizer that augments standard LMM fine-tuning with two geometry-aware losses. First, a non-parametric cross-attention module uses text embeddings at each hierarchy level as queries over visual token hidden states to construct a semantic-aware visual tree with coarse-to-fine semantics; these Euclidean features are then mapped into the Lorentz model via curvature-aware batch scaling and the exponential map at the origin. Second, a taxonomic entailment loss enforces parent-child containment using hyperbolic entailment cones (penalizing children outside the parent's cone), while a discriminative dispersive loss promotes angular separation among sibling embeddings on the unit hypersphere. Theoretical analysis (Theorems 4.1–4.3) proves that the spherical angular formulation induces zero gradient on hyperbolic radial components, preserving the hierarchical depth encoded by the entailment loss while improving fine-grained sibling discrimination.

## Results

On iNaturalist-2021 (Qwen2.5-VL-3B), HiR² improves HCA from 14.06 to 18.07 (+4.01) on iNat-Animal base classes under SFT, and from 8.15 to 11.66 (+3.51) under DFT on iNat-Plant. On CUB-200-2011, the gains are dramatically larger: HCA jumps from 9.36 to 35.38 (+26.02) on base classes and from 8.76 to 32.83 (+24.07) on novel classes. Improvements are consistent across all four metrics (HCA, POR, S-POR, TOR) and generalize to unseen novel classes, with S-POR gains reaching +47.57 on CUB-200 base.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares HiR² against two LMM fine-tuning baselines: standard supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and dynamic fine-tuning (DFT, Wu et al. 2025). HiR² consistently outperforms both baselines on every metric (HCA, POR, S-POR, TOR) across all datasets and both base and novel class splits. Under SFT on iNat-Animal, HiR² improves HCA by +4.01 (14.06→18.07) and S-POR by +3.87 (60.40→64.27); under DFT on iNat-Plant, the HCA gain is even larger at +3.51 (8.15→11.66) with S-POR improving by +9.36 (40.47→49.83). On CUB-200, the advantage over SFT is most dramatic, with HCA improving by +26.02 on base and +24.07 on novel classes. The method also generalizes to other LMMs (Intern3.5-VL-1B, LLaVA-OV-1.5-4B, Qwen2-VL-2B), where it again beats SFT on all metrics, confirming that the gains are not model-specific.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the hyperbolic embedding literature (Nickel & Kiela 2018; Ganea et al. 2018) and hyperbolic vision-language work (Desai et al. 2023; Pal et al. 2024) from discriminative CLIP-style models to the LMM fine-tuning setting, where prior hierarchical representation learning has been largely absent. It departs from prior dispersive/contrastive objectives that operate in hyperbolic or tangent spaces by proving (Theorem 4.1) that such formulations entangle angular sibling separation with radial hierarchical depth, and instead proposes a spherical angular loss that provably preserves the radial structure (Theorem 4.2). The semantic-aware visual tree construction via non-parametric cross-attention over LLM hidden states is a new mechanism for extracting hierarchy-aligned features from generative LMMs, distinct from the encoder-based feature extraction used in prior discriminative HVR work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play, parameter-free design makes HiR² broadly applicable to any LMM and fine-tuning strategy, lowering the barrier for injecting taxonomic structure into generative multimodal models that are increasingly deployed for visual recognition. The clean radial-angular decomposition (Theorems 4.1–4.3) provides a principled design guideline for future hierarchical regularizers beyond this specific task. Open questions include whether the approach scales to much deeper or wider taxonomies (e.g., full iNaturalist with 100k+ classes), whether it can be integrated during pre-training rather than fine-tuning, and whether the gains transfer to open-world or zero-shot hierarchical recognition settings where the current closed-set four-choice VQA evaluation does not apply.
