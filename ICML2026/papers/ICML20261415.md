## Problem

Standard dense retrieval encodes documents into high-dimensional embeddings that are memory-intensive, computationally expensive at inference, and offer no human-readable structure for inspecting why certain items are retrieved. Hierarchical retrieval methods (e.g., RAPTOR) provide interpretable tree organizations but rely on LLM calls for construction and navigation, making them 100–200× slower and impractical even for medium-sized corpora. The paper asks whether a tree-based retriever can be learned end-to-end for retrieval performance without any LLM involvement, matching flat-retrieval efficiency while retaining inspectable semantic structure.

## Input / Output

RETREEVER takes a frozen encoder (DistilBERT for 512-token text, DINOv2 for images, AST for audio) that produces 1024-dim embeddings, and routes them through a learned depth-10 binary tree. The output is a probability vector over tree nodes at any chosen level: leaf-level (1024-dim) gives the finest representation, while intermediate levels (e.g., 16-dim at level 4) yield progressively coarser ones, enabling a single trained model to serve multiple compute budgets. The tree itself is an inspectable artifact whose nodes correspond to semantically coherent document groupings.

## Method

RETREEVER couples a frozen encoder with a learnable binary tree whose internal nodes each host a cross-attention split function: learnable level embeddings attend to the encoder's token-level representations (with globally shared W_q, W_k, W_v and node-specific linear readouts), producing a routing score per node. Routing probabilities are propagated via product propagation (multiplying ancestor probabilities along the path), ensuring valid tree-constrained distributions. Training uses a bidirectional InfoNCE contrastive loss with negative Total Variation Distance as the similarity metric between query and context leaf-assignment vectors, optionally with stochastic depth training that randomly samples a tree level per iteration to strengthen intermediate representations. At inference, documents are indexed by their node-assignment vectors at a chosen level (single-index) or partitioned into per-leaf clusters for multi-index search where the query is routed to top-k leaves before local retrieval, achieving up to 2× latency reduction with minimal quality loss.

## Results

On fine-level retrieval (Table 2), RETREEVER achieves 77.50% RECALL@10 and 54.58% NDCG@10 on Natural Questions, 94.41% / 79.68% on ImageNet-1K, and 99.43% / 93.48% on VoxCeleb2, consistently outperforming the frozen encoder baseline (57.53% / 42.15%, 92.89% / 73.60%, 85.65% / 44.94% respectively). Among tree-based hierarchical methods (Table 1), RETREEVER attains the best NDCG@10 across all four text datasets (e.g., 54.48% on NQ, 85.04% on HotpotQA) at ~10 ms per query, roughly 160× faster than RAPTOR (1758 ms on NQ) while improving NDCG@10 by ~30%. At coarse representation sizes, RETREEVER-STOCHASTIC retains 95% of peak NDCG@10 at only 256 dimensions (1/4 of full size), enabling a 75% reduction in inference cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RAPTOR (Sarthi et al., 2024), RETREEVER is approximately 160× faster (10.65 ms vs. 1758 ms on NQ) while achieving substantially higher NDCG@10 (54.48% vs. 40.28% on NQ; 85.04% vs. 59.37% on HotpotQA), despite not using any LLM calls for tree construction or navigation. Compared to Matryoshka Representation Learning (Kusupati et al., 2022), RETREEVER-STOCHASTIC delivers stronger coarse representations at every dimension size, while RETREEVER at the finest level outperforms ENCODER+MRL by 10.4 points in RECALL@10 on NQ (77.50% vs. 67.10%) and 48.5 points in NDCG@10 on VoxCeleb2 (93.48% vs. 70.27%). Against ScaNN (Guo et al., 2020) approximate search, RETREEVER's multi-index retrieval consistently outperforms at all data-access budgets below 30% on most datasets, with the gap widening at low budgets. When combined with HNSW (Malkov et al., 2018) as a within-leaf backend, RETREEVER's tree-based partitioning still outperforms HNSW applied directly to ENCODER or ENCODER+MRL representations across all four text datasets, confirming the learned hierarchy is complementary to strong ANN backends.

## Relation to Prior Work

RETREEVER extends the differentiable tree learning literature (Irsoy et al., 2012; Zantedeschi et al., 2021) by applying probabilistic tree relaxations to the retrieval objective via contrastive learning, rather than classification or clustering. It departs from LLM-based hierarchical retrievers (RAPTOR, MemWalker, GraphRAG) by eliminating all LLM calls during both tree construction and navigation, operating entirely on frozen encoder embeddings. The coarse-to-fine representation design is conceptually related to Matryoshka Representation Learning (Kusupati et al., 2022), but RETREEVER derives its nested granularity from the tree's depth levels rather than from explicit truncation training, and additionally provides an inspectable semantic organization of the corpus.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RETREEVER addresses a practical gap: hierarchical retrieval methods offer interpretability but are too slow for real-world use, while flat dense retrieval is fast but opaque. By learning the tree end-to-end on embeddings, the method achieves ~160× speedup over RAPTOR with better accuracy, and scales to 124.5M documents where standard ANN indexes (ScaNN) run out of memory. Open questions include whether the frozen-encoder assumption limits ceiling performance, whether adaptive or incremental tree updates can handle evolving corpora, and whether variable-depth trees could allocate capacity more efficiently. The finding that semantic coherence emerges without explicit clustering supervision is encouraging but remains to be validated in adversarial or out-of-distribution settings.
