## Problem

Streaming RL discards each transition after a single update, making agents severely sample-inefficient because value-based losses alone cannot extract meaningful representations from transient, highly correlated data. Existing streaming algorithms (Stream Q(λ), QRC(λ)) address stability but leave this inefficiency unaddressed, and no prior work combines representation learning with deep streaming RL.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263460/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent consumes a stream of single transitions (s_t, a_t, r_t, s_{t+1}) where observations are 4-frame-stacked grayscale images (84×84 for Atari, 32×64 boolean for Octax, 10×10×n for MinAtar), processed one at a time with no replay buffer. The SPR module additionally retains a fixed window of K=5 recent states for the prediction horizon, a negligible memory cost compared to a replay buffer. Output is a Q-value vector over the action space from which an ε-greedy action is selected; the shared encoder simultaneously produces latent representations used by the dynamics model for self-predictive supervision.

## Method

The paper augments streaming Q-learning agents with a Self-Predictive Representations (SPR) auxiliary loss that trains a shared encoder to predict K=5 future latent states via a lightweight dynamics model, using negative cosine similarity against EMA target encoders. Two orthogonalization mechanisms stabilize this in the streaming regime: (1) each SPR gradient is projected onto the subspace orthogonal to its own exponential moving-average history, decorrelating updates from the highly temporally correlated data stream; and (2) when the RL objective uses the adaptive ObGD optimizer (Stream Q(λ)), SPR gradients are additionally projected orthogonal to the RL update direction, preventing the fixed-rate SPR step from disrupting ObGD's stability-calibrated step size. The final parameter update combines the trace-based RL update with the orthogonalized SPR gradient additively, leaving the RL update untouched while constraining the auxiliary signal to non-conflicting directions.

## Results

On Atari (40M frames, human-normalized), QRC(λ)+SPR+orth achieves a mean return of 1.25±1.55 versus 0.04±0.28 for QRC(λ) alone, and streaming DQN+SPR reaches 0.35±0.53 versus 0.29±0.52 for streaming DQN. On MinAtar (5M frames), QRC(λ)+SPR+orth scores 52.70±36.27 mean versus 42.50±30.47 for QRC(λ), and streaming DQN+SPR scores 38.06±24.98 versus 15.99±15.46. Naively adding SPR to Stream Q(λ) degrades performance (0.28 vs. 0.48 mean on Atari 40M), but the orth² projection recovers and surpasses the baseline (0.61 vs. 0.48). Effective rank of encoder latents increases from 42.7 to 1256.9 for QRC(λ) with SPR, confirming richer representations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against QRC(λ) (Elelimy et al., 2025), adding SPR with orthogonalization yields dramatic gains: mean return jumps from 0.04 to 1.25 on Atari 40M (31×), from 42.5 to 52.7 on MinAtar, and from 38.8 to 52.8 on Octax, with consistent improvements in median and IQM as well. Against Stream Q(λ) (Elsayed et al., 2024), naively adding SPR causes severe degradation (0.28 vs. 0.48 on Atari 40M) due to gradient conflicts between ObGD and SGD; the proposed orth² projection (projecting SPR gradients orthogonal to the ObGD update direction) recovers performance to 0.61, surpassing the Stream Q(λ) baseline. Against PCGrad (Yu et al., 2020), the asymmetric orth² update outperforms PCGrad on Breakout (1.46 vs. 0.77) and Pong (0.71 vs. 0.35) on Atari 40M, while PCGrad falls below the no-SPR Stream Q(λ) baseline on Breakout and Seaquest. Against CURL (Laskin et al., 2020), the contrastive representation learning approach completely fails in the streaming setting (−0.03 mean on Atari 40M), confirming that self-predictive objectives are far more suitable for the highly correlated, single-sample-per-update regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two previously separate lines of work: streaming deep RL (Stream Q(λ) with ObGD, QRC(λ)) and auxiliary representation learning for RL (SPR, CURL, successor features). Prior streaming RL methods focused almost exclusively on stabilizing value-based updates under non-stationarity and correlated gradients, leaving sample efficiency largely unaddressed. Conversely, prior representation-learning-for-RL methods (Schwarzer et al., Laskin et al.) relied on large replay buffers to decorrelate data, making them incompatible with the streaming constraint. The paper's core methodological contribution is adapting SPR to the streaming regime and introducing two orthogonalization mechanisms—one against the SPR gradient's own momentum history (following Han et al.'s streaming video work) and one against the ObGD RL update—to resolve the gradient conflicts that arise when an adaptive optimizer and a fixed-rate auxiliary loss share an encoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The results demonstrate that dense auxiliary supervision (predicting future latents) can substantially close the performance gap between streaming and replay-buffer-based RL, which is practically important for on-device and privacy-sensitive deployments where storing transitions is infeasible. The effective-rank analysis (30× increase for QRC) and linear-probing results provide mechanistic evidence that the gains come from genuinely richer representations rather than incidental optimization effects. Key limitations include the persistent performance gap with ObGD even after orth², the unused latent dynamics model (a missed opportunity for model-based planning in streaming), and the added wall-clock cost per step. Open questions include whether streaming model-based RL can exploit the learned dynamics, whether the approach extends to continuous control, and whether learning transition dynamics impairs the rapid adaptability that is a core motivation for streaming RL.
