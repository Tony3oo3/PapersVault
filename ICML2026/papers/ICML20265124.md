## Problem

In federated learning, a client's influence becomes deeply entangled through iterative aggregation, making post-hoc unlearning expensive, unstable, and dependent on heavy server-side history or strong assumptions. No existing training-time mechanism makes per-client influence explicitly separable and cheaply removable, and there is no predictive link between the degree of entanglement and the resulting retrain fidelity gap.

## Input / Output

During training, IDFT consumes the same inputs as standard FedAvg (client local SGD updates Δ_{t,i} ∈ R^d) and additionally produces a compact per-client influence bank: each client i stores an s-dimensional coefficient vector z_{T,i} ∈ R^s (default s=128) in a deterministic orthonormal basis V_i generated by a seeded PRNG, so no full model copies are retained. At deletion time, the method takes the trained global model w_T, the stored influence φ_{T,u}=V_u z_{T,u} for the target client, and the retained clients' local datasets, and outputs a single unlearned model w^{-u} after R≪T proximal repair rounds.

## Method

IDFT instruments vanilla FedAvg with a three-stage influence-logging pipeline executed at every round: (1) a thin SVD of the round's weighted update matrix extracts a shared subspace U_t (rank r) that absorbs cross-client covarying directions, leaving an orthogonal residual r_{t,i}; (2) each residual is projected onto a client-specific orthonormal influence subspace span(V_i) to obtain a compact, separable component; (3) an entanglement score combining cross-client covariance, layer-energy entropy, and projection leakage drives a shrinkage factor s_{t,i}=1/(1+λE_{t,i}) that suppresses non-removable components before accumulating the influence coefficient. Deletion then reduces to a single subtraction w^{(-u,0)}=w_T−φ_{T,u} followed by R short proximal repair rounds on retained clients with a trust-region anchor. The theory provides a stability-style bound showing the retrain gap is geometrically controlled by the accumulated unremoved residual, plus a matrix-Bernstein/Davis-Kahan result quantifying when the shared subspace is reliably recovered from the weighted update matrix.

## Results

On the primary client-wise retrain-fidelity benchmark (Table 1A, IID, 10 clients), IDFT achieves the lowest Avg. Gap across all five dataset/architecture pairs: 0.26 (CIFAR-10 CNN), 0.72 (CIFAR-100 CNN), 0.78 (CIFAR-10 ResNet-18), 2.33 (CIFAR-100 ResNet-18), and 0.68 (Caltech-101 ViT-B/16), reducing the gap by 4–14% over the strongest prior baseline per column. On the reversible FU benchmark (Table 1B), IDFT matches or exceeds retrain-level forgetting (FA = 0.00 on FashionMNIST and CIFAR-100, 0.04 on CIFAR-10) while delivering the best ReA (0.40 on CIFAR-10, 0.12 on CIFAR-100) and lowest computation (25.80 on FashionMNIST, 225.10 on CIFAR-10), with communication roughly 40× smaller than FedEraser on CIFAR-10/100.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IDFT outperforms all nine baselines on the primary Avg. Gap metric across every dataset/architecture pair. Against the closest competitor NoT (weight negation), IDFT reduces the gap from 0.29→0.26 (CIFAR-10 CNN), 0.75→0.72 (CIFAR-100 CNN), 1.78→0.78 (CIFAR-10 ResNet-18, a 56% reduction), 4.73→2.33 (CIFAR-100 ResNet-18, a 51% reduction), and 0.79→0.68 (Caltech-101 ViT). Against FCU (feature-level unlearning), IDFT wins on all five pairs with margins of 0.55–2.13 in Avg. Gap. On the reversible FU benchmark (Table 1B), IDFT ties FUSED on communication (1.05M vs 0.98M on CIFAR-10) but achieves substantially better ReA (0.40 vs 0.42) and MIA (0.45 vs 0.65) on CIFAR-10, and dominates on CIFAR-100 with ReA 0.12 vs 0.14 and MIA 0.10 vs 0.48. History-heavy methods (FedEraser, Exact-Fun) incur 40× more communication (42.73M vs 1.05M on CIFAR-10) with worse or comparable fidelity, while EraseClient is fast but sacrifices utility (RA 0.64 vs IDFT's 0.68 on CIFAR-10). Overall, IDFT consistently wins on the fidelity-cost frontier, matching retrain-level forgetting at a fraction of the communication and compute cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant post-hoc federated unlearning paradigm (FedEraser, PGD, FUKD, rapid retraining) that attempts to remove client influence after training via history reconstruction or extra optimization, and also from recent training-time interventions (NoT's weight negation, FUSED's sparse adapters, FUCRT's representation transformation) that modify parameters or representations to enable deletion. IDFT instead keeps the FedAvg optimization path exactly unchanged and adds a parallel influence-logging mechanism: each round's updates are decomposed via SVD into a shared covarying subspace and a client-separable residual, which is then shrunk by an entanglement-aware score and stored in a compact per-client coefficient vector. The theoretical analysis draws on stability/sensitivity bounds from machine unlearning (Bourtoule et al., 2021) and matrix concentration inequalities (Tropp, 2012) to connect the logged residual to retrain fidelity, a predictive link the paper argues prior methods lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

IDFT offers a practical pathway to unlearning-friendly federated systems by designing for removability during training rather than relying on expensive post-hoc corrections, which is increasingly relevant as privacy regulations (GDPR, CCPA) extend to decentralized learning. The method's main limitation is that it remains approximate: the influence bank captures only a low-entanglement residual, and the short repair is a trust-region correction rather than an exact retraining, so formal compliance guarantees are not provided. Open questions include scalability of the per-client influence bank to thousands of clients, verifiability of the unlearning operation to third-party auditors, and extension to personalized or continual federated settings where the shared subspace structure may drift over time.
