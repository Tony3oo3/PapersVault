## Problem

Standard MBR decoding computes expected utility in a single reference-to-hypothesis direction, yet commonly used evaluation metrics (BLEU, COMET, chrF) are inherently asymmetric and their scores depend on comparison direction. This mismatch means the hypothesis-to-reference interaction and the separate prior effects over hypotheses and references are never explicitly modeled, leaving a fundamental design gap in how MBR scoring aligns with the metrics it optimizes.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of 256 candidate hypotheses and an identically sized set of pseudo-references, both sampled via ε-sampling (ε=0.02) from a frozen generation model (e.g., M2M100 for translation, BART for summarization, BLIP-2 for captioning), along with a chosen utility function (BLEU, chrF, COMET, or BERTScore). The utility function is evaluated in both directions—f_θ(h_i, r_j) and f_θ(r_j, h_i)—and the model's per-candidate generation probabilities P(h_i|x;φ) and P(r_j|x;φ) serve as the hypothesis and reference priors respectively. The output is a single selected hypothesis from the candidate set, determined by the weighted four-channel score.

## Method

The paper derives a noisy-channel decomposition of the standard MBR scoring formula by first rewriting the Monte Carlo MBR objective as argmax_{h_i} Σ_{r_j} P(h_i|r_j)P(r_j), then applying the identity P(h_i|r_j) = P(r_j|h_i)P(h_i)/P(r_j) to factor the score into four probabilistic terms: hypothesis-to-reference likelihood P(r_j|h_i), hypothesis prior P(h_i), reference-to-hypothesis likelihood P(h_i|r_j), and reference prior P(r_j). The hypothesis-to-reference likelihood is computed by swapping the metric's arguments (f_θ(r_j, h_i) normalized over references), explicitly capturing the inverse direction that standard MBR ignores. Inspired by the source-channel model, each term is raised to a tunable exponent (α, β, γ, δ), yielding a reweighted scoring rule that subsumes MAP, original MBR, conditional (BOC), swap, and inverse MBR as special cases. Empirically, grid-searching these four weights across 12 translation scenarios, 3 summarization datasets, and 2 captioning benchmarks reveals that channel importance is primarily metric-driven and largely task-agnostic, with the hypothesis-to-reference likelihood and reference prior being the most influential channels for BERTScore.

## Results

The paper's primary contribution is analytical rather than a single headline number. The key quantitative demonstration is that applying the best channel weights found on WMT22 En→De to WMT23 En→De improves ChrF from 50.05 (standard MBR, i.e., (α,β,γ,δ)=(0,0,1,1)) to 50.18, showing that reweighting transfers across settings. Across 12 machine translation scenarios (WMT22/23, En↔De, Ja↔En, Zh↔En) and summarization/captioning tasks, the analysis reveals that BERTScore consistently benefits from high weights on the hypothesis-to-reference likelihood (α) and reference prior (δ), while BLEU and chrF prefer low α and moderate-to-high δ. Channel-wise trends are metric-specific but task-agnostic: the same qualitative patterns hold across translation, summarization, and captioning when the utility function is fixed.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark a new decoding method against competing decoders in the traditional sense; instead, it provides a unified probabilistic lens under which existing MBR variants are special cases. Standard MBR (Eikema & Aziz, 2020) corresponds to (α,β,γ,δ)=(0,0,1,1), while model-based MBR (Jinnai et al., 2024) is shown to implicitly emphasize the reference prior P(r_j), explaining why it yields gains primarily on BLEU and BERTScore but not on chrF or COMET. The proposed free-weight formulation improves over standard MBR: on WMT23 En→De, ChrF rises from 50.05 to 50.18 when optimal weights from WMT22 are transferred. For BERTScore specifically, assigning moderately high α (≈1.25–1.5) together with high δ produces consistent gains across all evaluated tasks (translation, summarization, captioning), whereas the original MBR setting (γ=1, δ=1, α=0, β=0) is suboptimal. The hypothesis prior P(h_i) (β) has limited impact for semantic metrics (COMET, BERTScore) but provides modest gains for surface-level metrics (BLEU, chrF) when β>1.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reformulates the standard Monte Carlo MBR decoding formulation (Eikema & Aziz, 2020) through a noisy-channel decomposition, borrowing the source-channel reweighting technique from statistical machine translation (Och & Ney, 2002) to express MBR scoring as a product of four weighted probabilistic terms. It provides a unified parameterization under which MAP decoding, original MBR, conditional (BOC-style) scoring, and model-based MBR (Jinnai et al., 2024) all emerge as special cases, making explicit the bidirectional hypothesis-reference interactions that prior formulations treat implicitly. The paper departs from the broader MBR literature—which has focused on efficiency improvements, utility function design, and task-specific applications—by shifting the lens to interpretability: isolating which probabilistic channel drives performance for a given metric.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decomposition offers a principled, low-overhead mechanism for tuning MBR decoding per metric, and the finding that channel importance is metric-dependent yet task-agnostic gives practitioners a transferable recipe (e.g., boost α and δ for BERTScore, keep γ low for BLEU). It also retrospectively explains why model-based MBR (which explicitly models the reference prior) excels with BERTScore but not with chrF or COMET, bridging previously conflicting empirical reports. Open questions include whether the four-channel framework can guide the design of new utility functions from a source-channel perspective, whether optimal weights discovered on one dataset reliably transfer to unseen tasks, and whether the decomposition extends naturally to LLM-based generation settings where the candidate set is much larger and the model prior is far more complex.
