## Problem

Unified social behavior models must learn from inherently heterogeneous data spanning diverse modalities (acoustic prosody, facial expressions, text) and prediction targets (emotion classification, social inference, clinical detection), which produces uneven training signals across samples. Existing RL methods like GRPO aggregate policy updates across these heterogeneous samples without explicitly regulating disparities in their learning dynamics, causing optimization to be disproportionately dominated by specific tasks or samples and hindering multitask performance and cross-context generalization.

## Input / Output

The model (built on Qwen 2.5 Omni-7B) accepts multimodal inputs—text, audio clips, and video clips—drawn from 13 datasets totaling ~100k samples across 10 behavioral tasks. For classification tasks (emotion, sentiment, humor, sarcasm, anxiety, depression, PTSD), it outputs a discrete class label; for free-text QA tasks (social reasoning, intent recognition, non-verbal communication), it outputs a natural-language answer graded by an LLM judge. All outputs are structured as a reasoning trace enclosed in think tags followed by a final prediction in a boxed format, with prompt and response lengths capped at 4096 and 2048 tokens respectively.

## Method

HARPO (Heterogeneity-Aware Relative Policy Optimization) is a critic-free, on-policy RL method that extends GRPO by dynamically rebalancing advantage magnitudes across heterogeneous behavioral tasks. It constructs contribution signals at both the sample-level (mean absolute group-normalized advantage per rollout group) and task-level (mean absolute advantage across all rollouts of a task), then computes modulation factors as the reciprocal of each signal's ratio to a geometric-mean reference, ensuring the factors have a geometric mean of 1 and cannot simultaneously enlarge or shrink all advantages. These modulation factors are updated via inertial smoothing—exponential moving averages for contribution signals and multiplicative updates for the ratio-based factors—to track persistent trends rather than stochastic fluctuations. The resulting HARPO-modulated advantages replace GRPO's group-normalized advantages in the PPO clipped surrogate objective, with the same trust-region structure and KL penalty retained. The reward combines task accuracy (binary for classification, cosine-similarity for QA), a format-adherence bonus, and an overlong-length penalty.

## Results

OmniSapiens-7B 2.0 (trained with HARPO) achieves the best average task rank of 1.90 across 10 behavioral tasks, outperforming all prior models and RL baselines. On zero-shot generalization, it attains the best weighted F1 on all five held-out benchmarks (AUT 39.91, SER 72.11, IDR 72.43, SMSA 58.47, SIR 69.27), with gains of up to +9.37% over the next-best model. Among RL training algorithms, HARPO outperforms GRPO by up to +42.29% (on HUM: 69.85 vs. 27.56) and achieves the most consistent per-task performance (avg. rank 2.10 among algorithms). Reasoning traces are also more interpretable, with 87.7% consistency (vs. 66.5% next best) and 19.86 average tokens (vs. 57.69 next best).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RL baselines on the same backbone (Qwen 2.5-Omni-7B) and reward design, HARPO wins on 8 of 10 tasks versus GRPO (e.g., HUM 69.85 vs. 27.56, SAR 70.64 vs. 53.58), with GRPO's collapse on HUM being the most dramatic gap (+42.29%). GPG is the closest competitor, edging out HARPO on EMO (77.70 vs. 76.55) and INT (54.21 vs. 50.52), but HARPO substantially outperforms GPG on SAR (70.64 vs. 45.96) and NVC (14.54 vs. 12.79). RLOO wins narrowly on INT (51.73 vs. 50.52) and SOC (29.54 vs. 25.40), while EMAGRPO leads on SAR (77.75 vs. 70.64) and SOC (30.58 vs. 25.40), but HARPO dominates on EMO, HUM, ANX, DEP, and SEN. Among prior behavioral foundation models, OmniSapiens-7B 2.0 surpasses OmniSapiens-7B RL (trained with GRPO) on 9 of 10 tasks (e.g., SEN 77.61 vs. 39.60, EMO 76.55 vs. 57.28) and HumanOmniV2-7B on 8 of 10 tasks, while also outperforming general-purpose LLMs (Qwen 3-VL-8B, Gemma 4) across nearly all behavioral dimensions. On zero-shot held-out tasks, OmniSapiens-7B 2.0 achieves the best score on all five benchmarks, beating OmniSapiens-7B RL by up to +12.64% (SER: 72.11 vs. 55.77) and HumanOmniV2-7B by up to +50.46% (IDR: 72.43 vs. 21.97).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) by introducing HARPO, which modulates group-normalized advantages via geometric-mean-referenced scaling factors and inertial smoothing to rebalance learning across heterogeneous multitask data. It positions itself against a family of critic-free RL methods (RLOO, REINFORCE++, GPG, EMAGRPO) that address variance reduction or multitask balancing but do not explicitly regulate per-sample or per-task advantage magnitudes. The empirical setting builds on the Human Behavior Atlas benchmark (Ong et al., 2026) and the prior OmniSapiens-7B RL model, which used standard GRPO on the same data, making HARPO a direct methodological upgrade rather than a new problem formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a relatively simple advantage-modulation mechanism can simultaneously improve multitask performance, zero-shot generalization, and reasoning-trace interpretability, suggesting that imbalanced learning signals are a meaningful bottleneck in heterogeneous multitask RL. However, the causal link between uniform learning and generalization is acknowledged as correlational, and all results are tied to a single 7B base model and one benchmark, leaving open whether the mechanism transfers to larger models or different task compositions. The technique is lightweight (two EMA hyperparameters) and could be adopted by practitioners training multitask RL policies beyond behavioral AI.
