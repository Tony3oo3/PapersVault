## Problem

Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for autonomous driving rely on autoregressive or diffusion-based decoding, requiring multiple sequential inference steps that are unsuitable for real-time high-frequency control. Additionally, current VLAs lack robust generalization to novel vehicle configurations and unseen driving scenarios, and their fine-tuning strategies fail to fully exploit the reasoning potential of large vision-language backbones.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265972/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes three camera views (front, front-left, front-right) along with the ego vehicle's trajectory history over the preceding 3 seconds (x, y coordinates sampled at 0.5-second intervals) and corresponding velocity/acceleration vectors. It produces a continuous 10-step future trajectory (x, y in meters at 0.5-second intervals, covering 5 seconds) in a single parallel forward pass, accompanied by a chain-of-thought reasoning explanation. Unlike autoregressive VLAs that emit discretized action tokens sequentially, Reasoning-VLA regresses all trajectory coordinates simultaneously, eliminating the need for action tokenization.

## Method

Reasoning-VLA builds on Qwen2.5-VL as a reasoning-enhanced VLM backbone and introduces a set of learnable action queries (T×N×D) that are initialized via implicit spatial guidance—Gaussian sampling from ground-truth trajectory statistics—to encode spatial awareness from the start. These queries interact with the VLM's hidden states through self-attention followed by cross-attention under a bidirectional mask, extracting reasoning-rich features and generating all future action coordinates in one parallel pass rather than sequentially. An Action Refinement Module (MLP + attention) then refines the query outputs into smooth continuous trajectories. Training proceeds in two stages: supervised fine-tuning on a unified Chain-of-Thought dataset (merging eight public driving datasets with VLM-generated reasoning annotations) for 4 epochs, followed by GRPO-based reinforcement learning with rule-based rewards that jointly penalize trajectory deviation, excessive steering angles (>40°), and non-physical acceleration (>0.6g), yielding a 61× inference speedup over autoregressive baselines.

## Results

On the nuScenes open-loop benchmark, Reasoning-VLA-7B achieves an average L2 error of 0.23 m and an average collision rate of 0.08%, surpassing the best prior VLA method (Impromptu-VLA, 0.30 m L2) by 23.3% in L2 and outperforming AutoDrive-R² (0.31 m, 0.15% collision) by 25.8% in L2 and 46.7% in collision rate. On the closed-loop NeuroNCAP simulator, Reasoning-VLA-7B attains an average score of 2.25 with a 59.4% collision rate, exceeding the best prior method (Impromptu-VLA, 2.15 score, 65.5% collision). On NAVSIM, Reasoning-VLA-7B achieves a PDMS of 91.7, improving over Para-Drive (84.0) by 7.7 points. Inference speed is approximately 61× faster than autoregressive Qwen2.5-VL-7B (0.089 s vs. 5.472 s for 10 trajectories).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Reasoning-VLA-7B outperforms all 14 baselines across every reported metric. Against end-to-end AD methods, it reduces average L2 from 0.63 m (SparseDrive, the best) to 0.23 m (−63.5%) and collision rate from 0.10% to 0.08%. Among VLA competitors, it beats the closest rivals Impromptu-VLA (0.30 m L2) and AutoDrive-R² (0.31 m L2, 0.15% collision) by 23–26% in L2 and 47–88% in collision rate. On the closed-loop NeuroNCAP benchmark, Reasoning-VLA-7B (score 2.25, collision 59.4%) surpasses Impromptu-VLA (2.15, 65.5%) and AutoDrive-R² (1.89, 63.3%), while the 3B variant (2.04, 62.7%) still exceeds BridgeAD-B (1.60, 72.6%). On NAVSIM, Reasoning-VLA-7B achieves PDMS 91.7 versus 84.0 for both TransFuser and Para-Drive, with gains of +5.1 in TTC and +1.4 in EP over Para-Drive. Notably, even the smaller Reasoning-VLA-3B model matches or exceeds several 7B-scale baselines, and the additional RL fine-tuning (7B+) yields a further 4.3% L2 reduction over 7B, confirming the training pipeline's effectiveness.

## Relation to Prior Work

Reasoning-VLA departs from the dominant autoregressive and diffusion-based VLA paradigms (OpenVLA, π0, DriveMOE) by replacing sequential token-by-token action generation with a set of learnable action queries that interact with a reasoning-enhanced VLM (Qwen2.5-VL) via cross-attention, enabling single-step parallel trajectory prediction. The RL fine-tuning stage adopts GRPO from the LLM reasoning literature (DeepSeekMath), repurposing it for continuous action generation with physics-informed reward functions rather than discrete token scoring. The unified dataset construction, merging eight heterogeneous AD datasets into a CoT-annotated format, extends the data-centric approach seen in VLM pretraining to the VLA setting, addressing the fragmentation that limits generalization in prior AD VLA work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 61× inference speedup over autoregressive VLAs (0.089s vs 5.47s for 10 trajectories) is practically significant for real-time high-frequency vehicle control, where latency budgets are tight. The unified 8-dataset CoT resource and the demonstration that a single model generalizes across diverse vehicle platforms and driving environments (zero-shot L2 variance of only 0.012–0.014 across sub-datasets) provide a useful foundation for the community. However, closed-loop collision rates remain high (~59–64%), and the finding that domain-specific RL fine-tuning (7B+) degrades closed-loop generalization suggests the SFT+RL recipe has not yet fully resolved the exploration-exploitation tension in AD. Open questions include whether the parallel action-query design extends to richer action spaces (e.g., multi-agent coordination, discrete maneuver selection) and whether the implicit spatial guidance generalizes to fundamentally different vehicle kinematics.
