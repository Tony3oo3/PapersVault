## Problem

Deep-learning-based CT reconstructions produce high-fidelity point estimates but offer no uncertainty certificates, making it impossible to distinguish reliable structural details from generative hallucinations or to adapt acquisition protocols in real time. Existing uncertainty quantification methods for tomography (Bayesian MCMC, bootstrap, conformal prediction) either lack rigorous finite-sample frequentist coverage guarantees or do not account for the non-linear Beer-Lambert forward model with Poisson detector noise. This paper fills that gap by constructing confidence regions with provable anytime-valid coverage that are tight enough to be practically useful.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequential stream of Poisson photon-count vectors (one r-dimensional detector reading per view angle, r=128) acquired at deterministic angles over a 190-step horizon, together with a trained reconstruction model (U-Net, 10-member U-Net ensemble, or conditional diffusion model) that serves as the mixing distribution. It produces a confidence sequence (C_t)_{t=1}^{190}, where each C_t is a level set in the 128×128 image space [0,1]^{128×128} guaranteed to contain the ground-truth attenuation map with probability ≥ 1−δ at all times simultaneously. Downstream, the confidence set is projected into pixel-wise Student-t intervals via diffusion boundary sampling or worst-case optimization, and used as a membership test to flag hallucinated reconstructions.

## Method

The framework builds on sequential likelihood mixing (Kirschner et al., 2025), which exploits the martingale structure of the sequential likelihood ratio and Ville's inequality to yield finite-sample, anytime-valid confidence sets. The confidence set C_t is defined as the level set {x : L_t(x) ≤ β_t + log(1/δ)}, where L_t is the cumulative negative log-likelihood under the Beer-Lambert/Poisson model and β_t is a data-driven threshold computed as the sequential negative log-likelihood of the observed data under a sequence of mixing distributions μ_{s-1}. For point predictors (FBP, MLE, single U-Net), μ_{s-1} is a Dirac mass; for ensembles or diffusion models, it is a uniform mixture of K Diracs over generated samples, yielding a log-sum-exp confidence coefficient that is robust to individual member errors. The key design insight is that the tightness of C_t is governed entirely by the predictive accuracy of the mixing sequence: more accurate deep predictors shrink β_t and thus the confidence region, while the coverage guarantee remains valid for any admissible mixing distribution. The paper further develops two projection strategies—worst-case projected gradient descent and diversity-guided diffusion boundary sampling—to convert the high-dimensional set into interpretable pixel-wise uncertainty maps, and demonstrates that the resulting intervals achieve nominal 95% coverage with tighter widths than bootstrap, MCMC, and ensemble baselines.

## Results

On the Lamino dataset at total intensity 10⁹, the diffusion-based confidence set achieves a sequential NLL gap (β_t − L_t(x*)) of ≈4.4×10⁴, an order of magnitude tighter than MLE (≈2.3×10⁵) and ~36× tighter than FBP (≈1.6×10⁶). All methods satisfy the theoretical 5% coverage guarantee (crossover rate ≤ δ), with the diffusion model approaching the nominal 5% error rate at low intensities (≈3% on Composite), indicating non-conservative bounds. For pixel-wise uncertainty quantification, Diffusion C_t-Boundary Sampling is the only method that simultaneously achieves nominal 95% coverage and the narrowest confidence intervals across the full intensity range (10⁴–10⁹), outperforming SK-ROCK, Worst-Case optimization, and FBP Bootstrap on width while Equivariant Bootstrap, U-Net Bootstrap, and U-Net Ensemble produce tighter intervals but systematically undershoot the coverage target.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FBP (Bracewell & Riddle, 1967), the diffusion-based confidence set is ~36× tighter in NLL gap on Lamino at 10⁹ photons, and FBP is unable to exclude even 4°-rotated ground truth images while the diffusion set rejects 1° rotations with high probability. Against MLE (Shepp & Vardi, 1982), the diffusion mixing achieves roughly a 5× tighter gap (4.4×10⁴ vs. 2.3×10⁵), with the U-Net Ensemble falling in between. For pixel-wise uncertainty quantification, SK-ROCK (Pereyra et al., 2020) achieves nominal 95% coverage with competitive interval widths, but Diffusion C_t-Boundary Sampling produces consistently narrower intervals at low and intermediate intensities, converging to comparable widths only in the high-dose regime. Equivariant Bootstrap (Pereyra & Tachella, 2024) produces visibly tighter intervals than the likelihood-based methods but systematically undershoots the 95% coverage target across all datasets and intensities, even after per-cell hyperparameter calibration, indicating that rotation-augmented bootstrapping underestimates posterior spread for these learned estimators.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies the Sequential Likelihood Mixing framework of Kirschner et al. (2025) to computed tomography for the first time, instantiating the abstract confidence-sequence construction with the non-linear Beer-Lambert/Poisson forward model and Radon transform geometry specific to X-ray imaging. It departs from the dominant Bayesian UQ tradition in tomography (MCMC, ensembles, Laplace approximation) by working in a frequentist, anytime-valid setting that requires no prior specification and provides finite-sample coverage guarantees at arbitrary stopping times. The paper also positions itself against conformal prediction approaches for inverse problems, noting that standard marginal coverage holds only on average over the population rather than for specific objects, and against regret-based confidence set constructions that yield looser thresholds and are unavailable for deep learning predictors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first principled, finite-sample, anytime-valid confidence regions for CT reconstruction that remain valid regardless of when acquisition is terminated, directly enabling practical applications like early stopping, data-driven experimental design, and hallucination detection in safety-critical medical and industrial imaging. Key limitations include the assumption of a well-specified monochromatic Beer-Lambert/Poisson model (beam hardening, scatter, and calibration errors are not captured), the focus on 2D slices rather than full 3D volumes, and the observation that at high intensities the empirical coverage becomes overly conservative (approaching 100% rather than the nominal 95%), suggesting the bounds are not yet optimally calibrated. Open questions include extending to 3D volumetric reconstruction, handling model misspecification more formally, and whether the conservative high-dose behavior can be corrected via empirical calibration without sacrificing the theoretical guarantee.
