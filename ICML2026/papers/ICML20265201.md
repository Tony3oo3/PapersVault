## Problem

Test-Time Adaptation (TTA) methods that update models online via entropy minimization become unstable over long horizons, with accuracy collapsing to near zero. Prevailing explanations attribute this to statistical error accumulation from noisy pseudo-labels and mitigate it through sample filtering or parameter reset. The paper argues this view is insufficient: the root cause is geometric — spurious gradients from confident mispredictions carry a persistent directional bias into a low-rank protected subspace hosting core knowledge, and this coherent leakage accumulates over time to erode the model's representations regardless of how many samples are filtered.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a stream of unlabeled RGB test images (50K validation images per benchmark at standard ImageNet resolution) and the parameters of a pre-trained classifier (ResNet-50 or ViT-B/16 from torchvision). It outputs updated normalization-layer affine parameters and per-sample class predictions. Operationally, MGP maintains a FIFO buffer of the most recent n=32 raw entropy gradients and refreshes its protected-subspace estimate every K=100 steps, incurring O(d·r_t) overhead per update step. Only the affine parameters of normalization layers are adapted, following the TENT protocol.

## Method

MGP identifies a geometric failure mode called manifold erosion: reliable gradients concentrate in a low-rank protected subspace S_know, while gradients from confident mispredictions (Entropy-Spurious samples) exhibit a persistent, structured projection into this subspace that accumulates coherently over time. To block this leakage, MGP projects each entropy gradient onto the orthogonal complement S_t^⊥ of an online-tracked subspace estimate, forcing all adaptation to occur in the high-dimensional residual space where updates remain incoherent. The subspace is tracked without labels via Marchenko-Pastur bulk-edge spectral separation on a FIFO gradient buffer: the noise variance is estimated from the spectral median, the bulk edge is computed analytically, and only eigenvalues above the edge define the spike subspace. Smooth tracking is achieved through inertial fusion on the Grassmann manifold — candidate directions are residualized against the current basis, only novel energy exceeding a threshold is admitted, and the combined basis is re-orthogonalized and truncated by Rayleigh quotient. Theoretically, MGP guarantees exact zero drift in the tracked subspace per step, bounds long-horizon drift in any fixed reference subspace by the tracking misalignment δ_t, and achieves sublinear O(√T) drift under a martingale-difference condition, in contrast to the linear O(T) drift of unconstrained updates with biased leakage.

## Results

On long-horizon cyclic revisits (40 rounds, 600 domain visits), MGP maintains stable or improving accuracy across all benchmarks: 42.96% on ImageNet-C (Δ=+1.06), 46.72% on ImageNet-C-Bar (Δ=+1.45), and 61.51% on ImageNet-R (Δ=+4.50), while most baselines collapse to near-zero accuracy. On the inter-dataset protocol (IN-C→3DCC→C-Bar), MGP achieves 43.08% (Δ=+1.45) versus SAR at 0.37% (Δ=−37.75) and PTTA at 0.09% (Δ=−42.57). In the blind-spot regime where the source model has zero accuracy, MGP recovers to 39.69% on IN-C, 27.01% on IN-K, and 32.85% on IN-R, while confidence-based methods (PTTA, DeYO, SPA) collapse to <1%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MGP outperforms all 11 baselines on every benchmark and protocol tested. On intra-dataset synthetic revisits, MGP is the only method with positive Δ across all three corruption suites (IN-C: +1.06, IN-3DCC: +0.55, IN-C-Bar: +1.45), while TENT, RoTTA, DeYO, COME, and SPA all collapse to <1% accuracy by Round 40. SAR and ETA are the closest competitors on intra-dataset synthetic shifts (SAR: Δ=−0.84 to +1.90; ETA: Δ=−1.22 to −2.62), but both fail catastrophically on inter-dataset revisits (SAR: Δ=−37.75; ETA: Δ=−4.73) and natural shifts (SAR: Δ=−5.76 to −6.49; ETA: Δ=−20.20 to −41.21). On natural shifts (Table 3), MGP achieves the best R40 accuracy on both IN-K (45.94%, Δ=+2.53) and IN-R (61.51%, Δ=+4.50), whereas DeYO (Δ=−48.32 on IN-R) and PTTA (Δ=−46.50 on IN-R) suffer the most severe forgetting. In the blind-spot regime (Table 4), where the source model has zero accuracy and confidence is uninformative, MGP is the only method that recovers meaningful performance (39.69% on IN-C, 32.85% on IN-R), while PTTA, DeYO, SPA, and COME all drop to <1%. CoTTA is the only other method that avoids full collapse on intra-dataset synthetic shifts (Δ=−9.22 on IN-C) but degrades substantially on inter-dataset (Δ=−20.06) and provides no benefit on natural shifts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on TENT's entropy-minimization framework and ETA's gating mechanism, but departs fundamentally from the prevailing 'statistical' diagnosis of TTA collapse (noisy pseudo-labels accumulating error). Prior stabilizers—confidence filtering (ETA, DeYO, PTTA), sharpness-aware updates (SAR), mean-teacher consistency (CoTTA), and parameter reset/recovery—control *when* or *how much* to update, whereas MGP controls *where* updates occur in parameter space via orthogonal projection onto a tracked low-rank subspace's complement. The geometric framing also distinguishes MGP from representation-alignment methods (SSP, SSA, ViDA) that operate on feature geometry rather than update directionality, and from subspace-alignment work for regression TTA (Adachi et al., 2025) which does not address online gradient leakage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reframes long-horizon TTA instability from a sample-quality problem to a geometric one (manifold erosion), offering a principled mechanism—Marchenko-Pastur spectral distillation plus Grassmann-manifold tracking—that is both theoretically grounded (drift bounds tied to tracking error) and practically lightweight (O(dr) per step). The blind-spot experiment, where MGP recovers 39.7% accuracy on ImageNet-C from a stream of *entirely misclassified* samples while all confidence-based baselines collapse to near zero, suggests a broader principle: useful adaptation signal persists in high-dimensional residual directions even when predictions are wrong, challenging the assumption that reliable pseudo-labels are necessary for learning. Open questions include whether the low-rank protected-subspace structure holds when adapting full model parameters (not just normalization layers), whether the Marchenko-Pastur bulk-edge separation remains valid under highly non-stationary or adversarial streams, and whether the approach extends to generative or multimodal models where the gradient geometry may differ substantially.
