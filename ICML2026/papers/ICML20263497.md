## Problem

Standard OT-DRO pipelines design the ambiguity set geometry (e.g., an isotropic Wasserstein ball) independently of the downstream optimization problem, forcing uniform robustness in all directions even when only a few directions materially affect the loss. This separation between uncertainty quantification and decision-making leads to excessive conservatism. The paper asks whether the ambiguity set geometry can be learned end-to-end so that the loss function itself shapes which adversarial distributions are excluded.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263497/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes J empirical samples {ξ̂_j}, a loss function ℓ(w, ξ), a reliability level β, and a parametrized transportation cost κ(·,·;θ) (e.g., a Mahalanobis matrix L ∈ L_{++}^d). The radius ε is fixed once via bootstrap quantile calibration and is not learned; only the geometry parameter θ is optimized. The output is the learned geometry θ* (defining the anisotropic shape of the ambiguity set) together with the corresponding optimal decision ŵ_θ*, which is guaranteed to satisfy the out-of-sample coverage constraint Pr(P ∈ B_ε(ĤP; θ*)) ≥ 1−β.

## Method

The core contribution is a bilevel optimization formulation where the upper level selects the ambiguity set geometry θ (via a parametrized transport cost) and the lower level solves the resulting OT-DRO problem, which is reformulated as a finite-dimensional conic program. The hypergradient of the upper-level objective c(θ)ᵀx*(θ) is computed by differentiating through the conic program's KKT conditions using the nonsmooth conservative implicit function theorem, yielding a conservative Jacobian that handles the non-differentiability of the solution map. A bootstrap-based coverage constraint—replacing standard measure-concentration bounds—is enforced through a smooth sigmoid penalty on the fraction of resampled datasets whose distance to the nominal distribution exceeds ε. Under definability and metric-regularity assumptions, the resulting hypergradient descent is proven to converge to a critical point of the bilevel problem. The approach is general: any transport cost parametrization (Mahalanobis, functional compositions of norms, conic combinations) that yields a conic reformulation can be plugged in, and the learned OT-DRO layer can be embedded in larger differentiable architectures.

## Results

The paper evaluates its method on two synthetic tasks: portfolio optimization (k=3, 50 independent Gaussian distributions × 10 datasets each) and distributionally robust linear regression (10 independent distributions × 10 datasets each). In both settings, the learned Mahalanobis geometry L* reduces the worst-case objective and the out-of-sample loss relative to the standard isotropic initialization L₀=I, with the relative improvement being larger for smaller sample sizes J. The bootstrap coverage penalty (Eq. 13) preserves the empirical coverage of the true distribution: removing it (λ_p=0) leads to pronounced undercoverage, as shown in Figure 2. No single headline number is reported in tabular form; results are presented as convergence plots and average relative-improvement curves across experiments (Figures 3 and 5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against named external baselines in a tabular sense; the implicit baseline is the standard OT-DRO pipeline with an isotropic (identity) transportation cost L₀=I, which allocates robustness budget uniformly in all directions. The proposed end-to-end bilevel procedure consistently outperforms this baseline on both the worst-case objective (f₀→f*) and the out-of-sample performance (ℓ₀→ℓ*) across all 500 portfolio instances and 100 regression instances, confirming a reduction in conservatism. The improvement is most pronounced when J is small (e.g., J=10–30), where the isotropic set is most wasteful. The authors also note that omitting the bootstrap coverage penalty causes the learned set to shrink in coverage-critical directions, degrading the out-of-sample guarantee — a failure mode not present in the full method. No direct comparison is made with the Mahalanobis calibration of Blanchet et al. (2019b) or the cost-aware sets of Schuurmans & Patrinos (2023), though the paper positions itself as a generalization of these approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the standard two-step OT-DRO pipeline (design ambiguity set, then solve DRO) by making the ambiguity set geometry a trainable parameter informed by the downstream loss function, formulated as a bilevel optimization problem. The algorithmic backbone draws on the nonsmooth conservative implicit function theorem (Bolte et al., 2021) to compute hypergradients through the conic-program reformulation of the lower-level DRO problem, building directly on the differentiable conic programming framework of Bolte et al. (2021) and the bilevel convergence machinery of Zuliani et al. (2025). For coverage enforcement, it departs from classical measure-concentration bounds by adopting a bootstrap-based constraint (Summers & Kamgarpour, 2021), and positions itself against prior decision-focused uncertainty-set learning (Wang et al., 2025; Chenreddy et al., 2022) by operating in the space of probability distributions rather than finite-dimensional parameter spaces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a principled way to reduce the 'price of robustness' in OT-DRO without sacrificing out-of-sample coverage, which is practically important for portfolio optimization, regression, and other data-driven decision-making tasks where isotropic Wasserstein balls are overly conservative. A key open question is the lack of theoretical guarantees for the bootstrap coverage constraint (Eq. 11b), which is enforced heuristically; establishing rigorous lower bounds on coverage probability under resampling remains an important direction. The framework is general enough to embed as a differentiable layer in any ML framework and extends to any conic-representable ambiguity set, suggesting broad applicability beyond the two testbeds examined.
