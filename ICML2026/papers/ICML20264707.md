## Problem

Existing Federated Incremental Learning methods rely on sample-based replay—either caching raw historical data or regenerating it via generative models—which introduces serious privacy risks and imposes prohibitive storage and communication overhead on resource-constrained edge devices. The paper asks how to achieve effective replay for preventing catastrophic forgetting without ever storing or regenerating raw data, while simultaneously reducing resource costs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264707/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Clients receive new-task RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet) and extract 512-dimensional ResNet-18 features, which are curated by temperature-scaled confidence, enriched via mixup, privatized with Laplace noise, and uploaded to the server. The server's CVAE then synthesizes feature-label pairs from all past tasks, which are distributed back to clients as replay signals. The final output is an updated global model (encoder + classifier) capable of classifying all previously seen and new classes, evaluated by Top-1 (or Top-10 for Tiny-ImageNet) accuracy.

## Method

Li-FIL replaces sample-level replay with feature-level replay via a server-side conditional VAE (CVAE) trained on privacy-protected, mixup-enriched features uploaded incrementally by clients. The key architectural insight is a decoupled client update: the classifier is stabilized by direct cross-entropy replay on synthetic features, while the encoder is regularized by a structure-aware loss combining global MMD distribution alignment and local contrastive nearest-neighbor alignment between new-task features and synthetic features. This separation is motivated by the observation that encoders and classifiers exhibit distinct vulnerabilities to forgetting—uniform replay either over-rigidifies the encoder or under-protects the classifier. The CVAE operates on 512-dim features rather than raw images, yielding a 22× smaller generator and 6–24× lower per-round communication cost, while the Laplace mechanism on clipped mixup features provides ε-LDP guarantees for each uploaded feature.

## Results

Li-FIL achieves the highest final accuracy A(f) and average accuracy Ā across all four datasets: 44.39% / 67.91% on Fashion-MNIST, 40.29% / 62.56% on CIFAR-10, 35.61% / 45.73% on CIFAR-100, and 40.31% / 55.31% on Tiny-ImageNet (all with α=1.0, ResNet-18 backbone). The gains are most pronounced on the harder datasets with more classes, where Li-FIL surpasses the strongest generative baseline (CAN) by 3.82% A(f) on CIFAR-100 and 6.19% A(f) on Tiny-ImageNet. In terms of efficiency, Li-FIL requires the fewest communication rounds (e.g., 567 on CIFAR-100 vs. 579–631 for other methods) and the lowest per-round time (50.1 s on CIFAR-100 vs. 53.9–67.5 s for competitors).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Li-FIL outperforms all eight baselines on every dataset for both A(f) and Ā. Against the strongest memory-buffer method ReFed+ (38.24% A(f) on CIFAR-10, 32.15% on CIFAR-100, 37.39% on Tiny-ImageNet), Li-FIL gains 2.05%, 3.46%, and 2.92% respectively. Against the strongest generative replay method CAN (38.46%, 31.79%, 34.12% A(f) on the same three datasets), the margins widen to 1.83%, 3.82%, and 6.19%, showing that the advantage grows with task difficulty. Standard FL baselines (FedAvg, FedProx) lag far behind, with CIFAR-100 A(f) below 27%, confirming that naive federated aggregation cannot handle sequential class-incremental settings. Li-FIL also leads in efficiency: it converges in fewer rounds (e.g., 273 vs. 283–312 on CIFAR-10) and has the lowest per-round wall-clock time (99.6 s on CIFAR-10 vs. 101–143 s for buffer/generative methods), attributable to its lightweight 0.17M-parameter feature-level CVAE versus ~3.8M for image-level generators.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of federated learning and incremental learning by shifting the replay mechanism from raw samples to privacy-protected dense features. It departs from sample-based FIL methods (GLFC, ReFed, ReFed+) that cache historical data on clients, and from generative FIL methods (FedCIL, Target, CAN) that synthesize raw images, by instead training a server-side CVAE on mixup-augmented, differentially-private features uploaded by clients. The decoupled update strategy—applying direct feature replay to the classifier and structure-aware MMD/contrastive alignment to the encoder—draws on the 'anatomy of forgetting' literature (Ramasesh et al., 2020) but is the first to operationalize this insight within a federated, privacy-constrained setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Li-FIL addresses a practical bottleneck for deploying FIL on resource-constrained edge devices: the 6–24× reduction in per-round communication (512-dim features vs. 3,072-dim CIFAR images) and the elimination of local sample storage make the approach viable in regulated domains like healthcare where raw data cannot be cached or regenerated. The ablation confirming that the contrastive local-structure loss is the single most critical component highlights that preserving feature-space topology, not just global distribution matching, is what prevents representation drift across tasks. Open questions include whether the CVAE-based feature generator scales gracefully to very long task sequences (beyond 10 tasks), how the method performs under non-IID label distributions rather than Dirichlet-partitioned data, and whether the privacy-utility trade-off (visible in the ε sensitivity analysis) can be tightened without further accuracy loss.
