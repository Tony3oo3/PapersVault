## Problem

Existing translation-equivariant neural processes face a dual limitation: convolutional designs (ConvCNPs) require embedding irregular inputs onto dense uniform grids whose cost scales exponentially with dimensionality and whose local kernels cannot capture long-range dependencies, while attention-based alternatives (TE-TNPs) operate on unstructured points but incur quadratic complexity. Moreover, both families stack opaque non-linear modules, obscuring the induced function class and precluding principled architectural analysis.

## Input / Output

The model consumes an unordered context set of irregularly sampled (x, y) pairs and a set of query locations, with no requirement that inputs lie on a uniform grid. The functional embedding ρ_e maps the context set to a continuous function via a Gaussian kernel, and the decoder produces a mean-field Gaussian predictive (independent per query point) parameterized by a learned mean and variance. The key structural constraint is that the frequency grid Ξ (typically |Ξ| ≪ |G| for any spatial grid G) replaces spatial discretization, and Hermitian symmetry W_{-ξ} = W̄_ξ is enforced to guarantee real-valued outputs.

## Method

The paper introduces set Fourier convolutions (SFConvs), which parameterize the convolution kernel directly in the frequency domain as complex weight matrices W_ξ at a finite set of frequencies Ξ. The critical insight is that the convolutional deep set functional embedding admits a closed-form Fourier transform (Eq. 9), so spectral coefficients are computed exactly from the raw context points without any spatial gridding; the inverse transform is then approximated by a finite sum over Ξ, yielding linear cost in |D_c| + |D_q| with approximately global receptive fields. For the Volterra variant (SFVConvCNPs), the translation-equivariant operator ρ_d is approximated by a truncated Volterra series of higher-order convolutions, whose cascade decomposition (via Fubini's theorem and a low-rank Hilbert-Schmidt factorization of second-order kernels) reduces evaluation to 2R+1 first-order SFConv modules per recursion step. Two model classes are proposed: SFConvCNPs (stacked SFConv blocks with pointwise nonlinearities) and SFVConvCNPs (Volterra construction with SFConv parameterization), both trained by minimizing expected negative log-likelihood under the Gaussian predictive.

## Results

On 1D synthetic benchmarks, SFConvCNP and SFVConvCNP match or outperform all seven baselines across all five tasks (RBF, Matérn-5/2, Periodic, Sawtooth, Square). On the Sawtooth task, SFConvCNP achieves a log-likelihood of 1.19 while every attention-based model (AttnCNP, TNP, TE-TNP, TE-PT-TNP) collapses to −0.87. On 2D image completion, SFConvCNP attains the best log-likelihood on both CIFAR-10 (1.74) and SVHN (2.93). On 3D Kolmogorov Flow, SFConvCNP reaches 1.89 log-likelihood (CRPS 0.03), and on ERA5 climate regression it generalizes to out-of-distribution regions (log-lik 1.71–1.89) where non-equivariant baselines collapse below −10.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the plain CNP, both SFConvCNP and SFVConvCNP win decisively on every task, with the largest gaps on Periodic (−0.04 vs −1.17 log-lik) and Square (−0.10 vs −1.39). All four attention-based baselines (AttnCNP, TNP, TE-TNP, TE-PT-TNP) collapse to the trivial marginal predictor on Sawtooth (log-lik −0.87), while SFConvCNP reaches 1.19 and SFVConvCNP 1.18; on Periodic, SFConvCNP (−0.04) outperforms the best attention model TNP (−0.47) by 0.43 log-lik. ConvCNP is competitive on GP tasks but trails SFConvCNP on Sawtooth (0.60 vs 1.19) and Square (−0.81 vs −0.36). The strongest baseline, SConvCNP, is closely matched by SFConvCNP on RBF and Matérn-5/2 (both 0.28–0.29 and 0.07) but is outperformed on Periodic (−0.04 vs −0.12), Sawtooth (1.19 vs 1.00), and Square (−0.10 vs −0.30 for SFVConvCNP). On the 3D ERA5 task, the equivariant SFConvCNP generalizes to out-of-distribution regions (log-lik 1.71–1.89) while non-equivariant baselines (CNP, AttnCNP, TNP) collapse below −10, and even TE-PT-TNP (0.34) trails by over 1.5 log-lik.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the convolutional deep set framework of Gordon et al. (2019) by replacing the unspecified translation-equivariant operator ρ_d with two concrete constructions: a Volterra series expansion (building on Roheda et al., 2024) that makes the induced function class analytically transparent, and a frequency-domain parameterization (set Fourier convolutions) inspired by Fourier Neural Operators (Li et al., 2020) that operates directly on irregular points without spatial gridding. It departs from prior translation-equivariant NPs (ConvCNPs, TE-TNPs) by resolving their complementary scalability limitations—grid cost for convolutions and quadratic cost for attention—through a single linear-complexity, grid-free operator. The Volterra construction also departs from the standard practice of stacking opaque non-linear layers, offering an explicit sum-of-convolutions characterization of the learned operator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck for translation-equivariant NPs in high-dimensional spatiotemporal settings (3D Kolmogorov flow, ERA5 climate), where grid-based convolutions become infeasible and attention-based equivariant variants remain quadratic. The frequency-domain formulation with |Ξ| ≪ |G| offers a concrete path to scaling equivariant NPs to larger domains, and the Volterra construction provides the first analytically transparent operator characterization within the NP family. Open questions include whether the Volterra interpretability translates into principled architecture design beyond empirical tuning, whether the parameter-budget constraint limiting SFVConvCNP on 3D tasks can be resolved without sacrificing the Volterra structure, and whether the approach extends to other symmetry groups (rotation, scaling) beyond translation.
