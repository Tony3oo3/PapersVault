## Problem

GRPO and similar RLVR algorithms exhibit diversity collapse: the policy concentrates probability mass onto a single correct reasoning mode (winner-take-all), narrowing the range of solution families the model uses. This paper shows the collapse is not a tuning artifact but a geometric inevitability of the group-normalized update dynamics, and that standard diagnostics like token entropy and pass@k can miss it entirely.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263796/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For each prompt, the method samples G rollouts (G=16 in experiments), verifies them with a binary exact-match reward, and clusters the correct rollouts in embedding space (all-MiniLM-L6-v2 + DBSCAN) to estimate mode masses ŷ. The per-sample bonus B_i = λ(1/(Kŷ_j) − 1) is added to the standard group-normalized advantage and broadcast over response tokens before the usual clipped GRPO/PPO update. A neutralizer term B⁻ is applied to incorrect rollouts to preserve the bad-mass decay rate; the entire modification is a one-line advantage-level change with no architectural or optimizer changes.

## Method

The paper formalizes GRPO as a mean-field ODE on the probability simplex, decomposing the policy into total bad mass p, within-good composition y, and within-bad composition z. It proves that the within-good dynamics follow a 'collision field' V(y) = y⊙y − ‖y‖²y that monotonically drives y toward a simplex vertex (Lemma 4.1), explaining winner-take-all diversity collapse. G²RPO edits this vector field by adding an inverse-probability granularity bonus B_j = λ(1/(Ky_j) − 1) to correct-mode advantages, which is shown to be the unique (up to scaling) permutation-equivariant bonus whose induced drift is collinear with V(y), acting as a single scalar gain that cancels (λ̃=1) or flips (λ̃>1) the collision drift. A closed-form neutralizer B⁻ = λ((1/K − ‖y‖²)/(p(‖y‖²+‖z‖²))) applied to incorrect modes preserves the baseline bad-mass learning channel, ensuring no accuracy-entropy trade-off. Empirically, this one-line advantage modification increases active correct-mode coverage by 172–205% over the base model and improves pass@1 by +1.4 to +7.9 points over GRPO on AIME 2024/2025.

## Results

On AIME 2025, G²RPO achieves 48.9% pass@1 on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B (+2.2 over GRPO's 46.7%) and 40.3% on Qwen3-14B-Base (+2.8 over GRPO's 37.5%); on AIME 2024 the gains are +1.4 (7B) and +7.9 (14B). In terms of diversity, G²RPO increases average correct-mode coverage (Avg K) by +225% (7B: 6.40 vs. GRPO's 1.97) and +607% (14B: 10.25 vs. 1.45) relative to GRPO, while reducing within-good concentration L_y from 0.84 to 0.46 (7B) and from 0.81 to 0.15 (14B). Token-level entropy is also sustained (1.01 vs. 0.49 for 7B; 1.90 vs. 0.18 for 14B), confirming that G²RPO avoids the late-stage entropy crash characteristic of GRPO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

G²RPO consistently outperforms GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) on both accuracy and diversity across both model backbones: pass@1 improves by +1.4 to +7.9 points while mode coverage (Avg K) increases by 225–607% and within-good concentration L_y drops by 45–82%. Against GTPo (Simoni et al., 2025), which stabilizes GRPO via gradient and entropy control, G²RPO achieves 46% accuracy and 4.1 Avg K at epoch 2 versus GTPo's 42% and 2.3, showing that GTPo's entropy regularization does not translate into meaningful mode-level diversity gains. Similarly, QAE (Wu et al., 2025), a quantile-based advantage estimator for entropy-safe reasoning, reaches only 43% accuracy and 2.4 Avg K at epoch 2, well below G²RPO's 46% and 4.1. A PPO-style entropy bonus (included as an additional control) keeps token entropy higher but fails to increase discovered correct-mode count (1.8 Avg K at epoch 2), confirming the paper's distinction between token-level and mode-level diversity. On zero-shot transfer (LiveCodeBench, GPQA, MMLU-Pro), G²RPO beats GRPO on 5 of 6 model–task pairs, with the largest margin on GPQA (+5.5 points for both backbones).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the GRPO/DAPO family of group-normalized policy-gradient algorithms for RLVR post-training (Shao et al., 2024; Yu et al., 2025) and the mean-field RLVR analysis of Rad et al. (2026), which already identified a replicator-dynamics structure on the probability simplex. Its key departure is reframing the well-observed entropy collapse not as a tuning artifact but as a geometric inevitability: GRPO induces a collision field V(y) = y⊙y − ‖y‖²y that generically drives winner-take-all among correct modes. The proposed fix—an inverse-probability granularity bonus—is shown to be essentially unique (Theorem C.8) among permutation-equivariant scalar bonuses that preserve collinearity with the native collision field, distinguishing it from ad-hoc entropy bonuses or KL regularization used in prior work (Cui et al., 2025; Ouyang et al., 2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The geometric diagnosis gives practitioners a principled, single-knob (λ) intervention that breaks the assumed accuracy-entropy trade-off: G²RPO improves pass@1 by +1.4 to +7.9 points over GRPO while expanding correct-mode coverage by 172–205%, suggesting diversity is a controllable geometric property rather than a cost. Open questions include whether the mean-field collision picture survives non-binary verifiers, multi-turn reasoning, or non-math domains; whether embedding+DBSCAN mode discovery can be replaced by verifier-aware or trajectory-level features; and whether the finite-rollout noise (G=16) limits the method's effectiveness on long-tail modes. The one-line implementation cost makes it easy to adopt, but the narrow evaluation scope (two models, AIME only) leaves broader generalizability untested.
