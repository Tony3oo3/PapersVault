## Problem

Existing audio-visual instance segmentation (AVIS) benchmarks are dominated by short (5–10 s), visually simple clips with static cameras and single-instance scenes, biasing evaluation toward frame-level audio-visual co-occurrence rather than robust long-range spatiotemporal modeling. This gap is especially acute for human-centric scenarios involving frequent occlusions, multi-turn speech, camera motion, and relative position changes, where current models fail to maintain consistent speaker identity over time.

## Input / Output

The system consumes video frames sampled at 1 FPS together with a synchronized audio track; no pre-segmented audio or pre-detected objects are required. Output is a set of per-frame binary instance masks (one per sounding person) linked into cross-frame identity trajectories that span the full ~54 s average clip duration. AVTrack itself is released exclusively as a test set (871 clips, 3,120 tracklets, 100% test split), so all reported numbers are zero-training evaluations on held-out data.

## Method

AVTracker is a training-free, modular three-stage pipeline that orchestrates off-the-shelf pretrained components. Stage 1 (Speaker Chunks Aggregation) transcribes audio with Whisper, extracts ECAPA-TDNN speaker embeddings per ASR chunk, and merges temporally adjacent chunks whose cosine similarity exceeds τ=0.35 to form longer speaker chunks, optionally applying MossFormer2 speech separation for overlapping speech. Stage 2 (Local Window Process) feeds each chunk's transcript and corresponding video frames to a Qwen3-VL-8B Local Reasoner that predicts a bounding box for the active speaker per frame; this box is matched by IoU to SAM3-Video person detections to retrieve pixel-level masks, forming a local tracklet with a key frame selected by maximum mask area. Stage 3 (Global Window Process) passes all key frames to a Qwen3-VL Global Reasoner that groups them by person identity, after which local tracklets are unioned into global speaker trajectories with empty masks inserted for unobserved frames. The key design insight is using textual speech content as a semantic bridge between audio and visual streams within a dynamic, content-driven windowing scheme, rather than fixed-size temporal windows.

## Results

On the AVTrack benchmark, AVTracker achieves HOTA 29.08, DetA 31.18, AssA 28.47, IDF1 34.55, and MOTA 16.20, substantially outperforming all prior VIS and AVIS methods. The strongest end-to-end AVIS baseline (AVTrackFormer) reaches only HOTA 21.47, while the best VIS-only method (CAVIS) scores just 11.46, revealing a ~17.6-point gap for visual-only approaches. AVTracker's MOTA of 16.20 is nearly 4× higher than the best baseline (4.23), highlighting the critical importance of long-range identity association in complex human-centric scenes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AVTracker dominates all five baselines across every metric on AVTrack. Against the three VIS-only methods (VITA, LBVQ, CAVIS), AVTracker gains 17.6–19.4 HOTA points, confirming that visual cues alone are fundamentally insufficient for human-centric tracking in complex audio-visual scenes. Against the two trained AVIS baselines (AVISM at HOTA 20.84 and ACVIS at 20.60), AVTracker improves by 8.2–8.5 HOTA points, with the largest relative gains on AssA (+8.2 over AVISM) and MOTA (+12.3 over ACVIS), indicating that the modular local-to-global tracklet grouping is the key differentiator for long-range identity consistency. The paper's own end-to-end variant AVTrackFormer (HOTA 21.47) also trails AVTracker by 7.6 HOTA, suggesting that the pipeline's use of a VLM reasoner for semantic audio-visual alignment provides a structural advantage over purely learned cross-attention fusion. Notably, even the weakest AVIS baseline (ACVIS) already roughly doubles the HOTA of the best VIS method, underscoring that audio information is essential but current audio-visual modeling remains far from solving the tracking problem in complex scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a benchmark extension of the AVIS task introduced by AVISM (Guo et al., 2025), deliberately moving beyond the simple, static, short-clip settings of prior audio-visual segmentation benchmarks (AVS-Bench, AVISeg) toward complex human-centric scenes with eight explicitly defined challenges (occlusion, camera motion, multi-turn speech, etc.). Methodologically, AVTracker departs from the end-to-end learned paradigm (Mask2Former + VITA-style token association) by composing off-the-shelf pretrained components—Whisper for ASR, Qwen3-VL for visual reasoning, SAM3 for mask generation, and ECAPA-TDNN for speaker embeddings—into a training-free, modular local-to-global pipeline. This contrasts with the field's dominant trend of task-specific end-to-end training and instead leverages VLM reasoning as a semantic bridge between audio and visual streams.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AVTrack exposes a substantial performance gap: all prior AVIS methods score below 21 HOTA while the training-free VLM pipeline reaches 29, suggesting that current end-to-end audio-visual models lack the long-range identity reasoning needed for realistic human-centric scenes. The test-only, annotation-intensive design positions AVTrack as a stable long-term reference, but the absolute scores remain low (best HOTA ~29), leaving open whether scaling training data or architectural changes (agentic memory, reflection-based correction, structured scene graphs) can close the gap. The finding that a ~60× more expensive VLM pipeline outperforms purpose-trained models also raises practical questions about the cost-accuracy tradeoff and whether foundation-model orchestration will become the dominant paradigm for fine-grained multimodal tracking.
