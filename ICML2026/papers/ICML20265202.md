## Problem

Existing multimodal fusion designs distort the gradient feedback loop to feature extractors: coupled fusion entangles modality representations, making extractors fragile when inputs are missing, while decoupled fusion relies on auxiliary separation losses that conflict with the primary task gradient. No prior fusion architecture simultaneously preserves clean task-gradient flow to each encoder and maintains modality-specific feature organization without introducing competing optimization objectives.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265202/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

SCFAE consumes modality-specific features of potentially different dimensions (e.g., 4096-d video vs. 128-d image on ActivityNet, or 512-d audio and 512-d visual on FakeAVCeleb) produced by pre-trained or domain-specific encoders, and outputs a fused representation of dimension l^(m)+l* per modality that feeds a task head for classification, retrieval ranking, or sentiment scoring. A key design constraint is that no forced dimensional alignment is required: the expansion mechanism natively handles unequal input lengths, and the reconstruction loss guarantees that the reconfigured representation remains recoverable to the original encoder output via cosine similarity.

## Method

SCFAE applies the Self-Consistent Field principle (analogous to Poisson-Nernst-Planck drift-diffusion) as a dual-force optimization: the task loss acts as drift, selecting predictive features, while a reconstruction loss acts as diffusion, preserving information in modality-specific subspaces. Concretely, each modality's features are expanded to n·l^(m) dimensions via SwiGLU+linear mappings, split at a boundary b into shared and specific components, and the shared components are cyclically substituted across modalities (modality m receives modality k's shared evidence) to form a self-consistent reference field. The reconfigured features are then projected to target sizes and optimized under a unified objective Φ = L_task + λ·L_recon, where L_recon enforces cosine-similarity recoverability of the original encoder output. Because both forces derive from a single scalar objective operating on complementary subspaces, the task and reconstruction gradients remain nearly orthogonal (cosine similarity ≈ 0), eliminating the optimization conflicts that plague auxiliary-loss-based decoupled fusion.

## Results

On CMU-MOSEI (sentiment analysis with missing modalities), SCFAE achieves 80.3% average accuracy and 81.1% average F1 across all seven input combinations, outperforming the strongest baseline AdaMMS (80.1%/80.8%) and DLF (79.9%/80.5%). On FakeAVCeleb (audio-visual deepfake detection), SCFAE reaches 95.74% accuracy and 99.51% AUC on the audio-only condition, surpassing AdaMMS (93.45%/96.30%) by over 2% in both metrics. On ActivityNet image-to-video retrieval with unequal-length inputs (4096d video vs. 128d image), SCFAE achieves mAP@100 of 0.326, edging out AdaMMS (0.319) and DLF (0.314).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCFAE consistently outperforms all 12 baselines on CMU-MOSEI across every missing-modality condition, with the largest gains on the audio-only ({a}) and audio-visual ({a,v}) settings where the dominant text modality is absent (e.g., 69.3% vs. 67.2% for AdaMMS on {a}; 72.0% vs. 71.2% on {a,v}). On FakeAVCeleb, SCFAE achieves 95.74% accuracy and 99.51% AUC on the audio-only condition, beating the next-best AdaMMS (93.45%/96.30%) by 2.3% ACC and 3.2% AUC, while matching the 97.68% ceiling on full AV inputs. On ActivityNet with unequal-length inputs (4096d video, 128d image), SCFAE leads AdaMMS by 0.007 in mAP@100 (0.326 vs. 0.319) and DLF by 0.012, confirming that avoiding forced dimensional alignment preserves retrieval quality. Against older baselines like MISA, DrFuse, and MCTN, the margins are substantially larger (e.g., 11.4% ACC on {a} over MCTN), indicating that the self-consistent reconstruction design addresses a fundamental gradient-flow limitation rather than merely tuning existing fusion heuristics.

## Relation to Prior Work

SCFAE carves new ground by repurposing the autoencoder reconstruction objective as a feature-organization force rather than a denoising or generative one, and by framing multimodal fusion through the Self-Consistent Field (SCF) principle borrowed from Poisson-Nernst-Planck physics. Conceptually, it sits between coupled fusion (cross-attention, aggressive mixing) and decoupled fusion (contrastive or mutual-information auxiliary losses), but departs from both by using a single unified loss where task prediction acts as 'drift' and reconstruction acts as 'diffusion' through complementary subspaces, avoiding the optimization conflicts that plague auxiliary-objective designs. The shared-specific decomposition echoes MISA and DrFuse, but the cyclic substitution of shared components across modalities and the explicit gradient-flow analysis are novel architectural and diagnostic contributions not present in prior fusion work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The gradient-degradation diagnostic (Table 6) provides a useful and somewhat underexplored lens for evaluating fusion methods: it shows that aggressive cross-attention can silently destroy 10+ points of unimodal AUC, a cost invisible in end-to-end accuracy. The finding that a simple cosine-similarity reconstruction loss outperforms contrastive objectives for feature separation challenges the field's default reliance on contrastive learning for disentanglement. However, the physical SCF/PNP analogy remains primarily motivational rather than a formal derivation, and the quadratic parameter growth from the expansion mechanism limits scalability to very high-dimensional features. Open questions include whether stacking multiple SCFAE layers or extending beyond three modalities preserves the gradient-coherence property, and whether the approach transfers to end-to-end foundation-model settings where modality-specific encoders are not pre-fixed.
