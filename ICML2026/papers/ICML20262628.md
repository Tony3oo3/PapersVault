## Problem

Existing long-context benchmarks evaluate primarily at the document level in high-resource languages, offering limited control over evidence positioning and rarely examining fine-grained comprehension across different discourse units. This leaves open the question of how LLMs perform across varying levels of linguistic granularity (word, sentence, paragraph, document) in long contexts, particularly in lower-resource languages, and whether positional sensitivity and discourse consistency differ across these dimensions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262628/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each instance pairs a full UN report (8K–128K tokens) in one of six official UN languages with a task-specific instruction (QA question, cloze blank, paragraph-filling prompt, or summarization/translation prompt). A key structural constraint is that all six language versions of the same report are position-aligned: paragraphs and sentences at identical indices convey equivalent semantics, enabling controlled cross-lingual comparison. Evidence position is stratified into begin/middle/end at both the document and paragraph levels, and the decision anchor (instruction + options) is appended after the document, making anchor-evidence distance a controlled variable.

## Method

MGAL is constructed from ~4,000 structurally suitable UN reports sampled from a pool of ~70,000, with GPT-4 generating candidate instances (QA pairs, cloze distractors, paragraph targets) that are then verified by two trained human annotators for grounding, positional correctness, and cross-lingual consistency. The benchmark decomposes evaluation along two orthogonal axes—linguistic granularity (word, sentence, paragraph, document) and evidence position (begin, middle, end at both document and paragraph levels)—yielding seven tasks with 420 query-response pairs per language. Evaluation combines task-specific reference metrics (Accuracy for QA/cloze, ROUGE-L for filling/summarization, sacreBLEU for translation) with an LLM-as-a-judge protocol (GPT-5, Gemini-2.5-Flash, Grok-4 scoring five dimensions: Topic Fidelity, Local Coherence, Entity Consistency, Instruction Following, Format Compliance) for open-ended generation tasks. Twelve long-context LLMs are evaluated zero-shot with external tools disabled, and ablation studies probe instruction placement, option-order bias, and context memorization to isolate specific failure modes.

## Results

Across 12 long-context LLMs evaluated zero-shot on MGAL, word-level tasks (Single-QA, Multi-QA) yield the highest average scores (73.09% and 72.59% accuracy), while performance drops sharply at coarser granularities: sentence cloze averages 21.07%, paragraph filling 15.08% (ROUGE-L), summarization 19.00% (ROUGE-L), and translation 16.85% (BLEU). GPT-5 achieves the top word-level scores (79.79% Single-QA, 74.99% Multi-QA) and strong cloze accuracy (30.62%), while Gemini-2.5-Flash leads on translation (36.17 BLEU) and Grok-4 on paragraph filling (19.90 ROUGE-L). A consistent pattern emerges: closed-source models retain a clear advantage over open-source models in lower-resource languages (Arabic, Russian), with the gap widening for smaller open-source models such as Gemma-3-27B and Mistral-Small-3.2-24B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

No single model dominates all six tasks: GPT-5 leads on Single-QA (79.79%) and is competitive on Multi-QA (74.99%), while Gemini-2.5-Flash edges out GPT-5 on Multi-QA (75.49% vs. 74.99%) and Translation (36.17 vs. 35.30 BLEU). Grok-4 achieves the best paragraph-filling ROUGE-L (19.90), outperforming the runner-up GLM-4.5 (16.15) by nearly 4 points, and GLM-4.5 leads summarization (26.88 ROUGE-L) ahead of DeepSeek-V3.1 (26.15). Notably, the smaller open-source Mistral-Small-3.2-24B achieves the highest cloze accuracy (32.35%), surpassing GPT-5 (30.62%) by ~2 points, suggesting that cloze performance is not purely a function of model scale. In higher-resource languages (English, French, Spanish), large open-source models such as Qwen3-235B-A22B and DeepSeek-V3.1 perform on par with closed-source systems, but in lower-resource languages (Arabic, Russian), closed-source models maintain a clear advantage; for instance, GPT-5 scores 78.54 on Arabic Single-QA versus 53.33 for GLM-4.5, and Gemini-2.5-Flash leads translation in all six languages. Smaller open-source models (Gemma-3-27B, Mistral-Small-3.2-24B) show the largest cross-language gaps, with translation BLEU scores dropping to 1.63–1.95, far below the 16.85 average.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in long-context benchmarking by combining three dimensions that prior benchmarks have not jointly addressed: multilingual coverage (six UN official languages), fine-grained linguistic granularity (word, sentence, paragraph, document), and controlled evidence position (begin, middle, end). Existing benchmarks such as LongBench, RULER, NeedleBench, M4LE, and ONERULER evaluate document-level comprehension in one or two languages with limited positional control, leaving fine-grained discourse understanding and cross-lingual position sensitivity unexamined. MGAL repurposes the structural alignment inherent in UN multilingual reports (same paragraph counts, sentence counts, and semantic equivalence across languages) to enable consistent cross-lingual comparison without synthetic data, a design choice that departs from the synthetic-needle approach of RULER and NeedleBench.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MGAL provides the first systematic diagnostic tool for decomposing long-context model failures along granularity and position, revealing that performance degrades sharply from word-level to document-level tasks and that closed-source models retain a clear advantage in lower-resource languages. The identification of local semantic crowding (models following surface cues over discourse roles) and the fluency-consistency gap (fluent but factually drifting outputs) offers actionable targets for training objectives and evaluation design. Open questions include whether these granularity-dependent degradations can be addressed through targeted training signals, whether the position sensitivity observed here persists in retrieval-augmented settings, and whether the benchmark's 420-pair-per-task scale is sufficient for stable cross-language comparisons given the reliance on coarse reference metrics like ROUGE-L and BLEU.
