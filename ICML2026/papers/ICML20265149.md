## Problem

While data contamination in LLM benchmarks is widely observed, the internal mechanisms by which leaked training data alters reasoning behavior remain unexplained. Prior work operates at the dataset or output level (detection, filtering, decoding intervention), leaving it impossible to distinguish genuine generalization from memorization or to design principled mitigations. This paper asks: what specific representational and computational shifts does contamination induce inside the model, and can those shifts be characterized and exploited?

## Input / Output

The analysis consumes a contaminated LLM (obtained by SFT on a leaked GSM8K subset) and a base model, feeding math prompts and extracting input activations A ∈ R^{N×d_i} and output gradients G ∈ R^{N×d_o} at every linear projection layer (primarily mlp.up_proj) across all generated CoT tokens. Outputs include per-layer Gaussian mutual information I(A;G), the Eigenspectrum Concentration Index (ECI), the Relative Truncation Error (RTE) for varying k, and the whitened cross-covariance singular values. For the restoration application, the method produces modified activations a_new by subtracting a dynamically scaled projection onto the top-k principal subspace, yielding a model whose reasoning trajectory reverts toward the base model's behavior on leaked samples.

## Method

The paper identifies mutual information decay between activations and gradients as a distinctive signal of contaminated reasoning, then explains it through a mechanistic chain: contamination induces eigenspectrum concentration in representations (low ECI), which causally drives a low-dimensional computation regime where module outputs are determined by a small set of principal eigenvectors (verified via spectrum-reshaping interventions). Under this regime, mutual information is confined to the active computation subspace (MISA), and on leaked inputs, optimization-induced gradient shrinkage weakens the linear coupling within that subspace, manifesting as a structured singular-value partial-order decay in the whitened cross-covariance space. A formal proposition shows this SVPO condition strictly implies lower Gaussian mutual information on leaked data. The practical application, Top-Subspace Suppression (TSS), subtracts activation components in the U_k subspace with a dynamically adjusted strength γ^α, recovering ~50% of leaked samples while losing ≤6% consistency on clean data—outperforming TED and LNE-Blocking baselines by up to 11.03% in average consistency.

## Results

On the reasoning restoration task, the proposed Top-Subspace Suppression (TSS) method recovers approximately half of the leaked samples while preserving clean-data consistency. On Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, TSS achieves 51.65% restoration and 94.37% retain (average consistency 73.01%), and on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct it achieves 45.45% restoration and 97.19% retain (average consistency 71.32%). This represents up to an 11.03% absolute improvement in average consistency over the strongest baseline (LNE-Blocking) on the 7B model. Mechanistically, the paper confirms that the contaminated model exhibits a lower Eigenspectrum Concentration Index (more concentrated spectrum), a lower Relative Truncation Error (more low-dimensional computation, with <10% error at k=64, i.e., 1.79% of total directions), and a strictly positive minimum spectral gap validating the Singular Value Partial Order condition.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares TSS against two contamination-mitigation baselines: TED (Dong et al., 2024, ACL Findings) which filters responses based on generalization-vs-memorization signals, and LNE-Blocking (Hou et al., 2025, EMNLP Findings) which intervenes at decoding time. TSS outperforms both baselines on every metric for both model sizes. On Qwen2.5-1.5B, TSS achieves 73.01% average consistency, surpassing LNE-Blocking (64.76%) by 8.25 points and TED (57.15%) by 15.86 points; on Qwen2.5-7B, TSS reaches 71.32%, exceeding LNE-Blocking (60.29%) by 11.03 points and TED (56.03%) by 15.29 points. TSS also maintains substantially higher retain scores (94.37% and 97.19%) compared to LNE-Blocking (89.96% and 88.76%) and TED (86.83% and 89.33%), indicating that the mechanism-driven intervention causes less collateral damage to clean-data reasoning. The gap over LNE-Blocking is particularly notable on the 7B model, where TSS recovers 45.45% of leaked samples versus only 31.82% for LNE-Blocking, while simultaneously preserving 97.19% of clean-data consistency versus 88.76%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant contamination literature, which operates at the dataset or output level (detection via perplexity/min-k%, leakage-free benchmarks, response filtering, decoding-time intervention), by providing the first mechanistic, representation-level account of how data contamination reshapes internal computation. It repurposes tools from mechanistic interpretability—eigenspectrum analysis, mutual information, canonical correlations—and connects them to the loss-landscape memorization literature (Garg et al. 2024; Ravikumar et al. 2024; Merullo et al. 2025) to build a causal chain from spectral concentration to degraded representation-gradient coupling. The TSS intervention draws conceptually on subspace projection techniques from interpretability but applies them as a contamination-mitigation strategy, positioning itself between the TED (Dong et al. 2024) and LNE-Blocking (Hou et al. 2025) baselines it benchmarks against.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The mutual information decay signal offers a layer-wise, model-internal contamination detector that does not require access to training data or output-level heuristics, potentially enabling more principled evaluation frameworks. The narrowing-geometry explanation (eigenspectrum concentration → low-dimensional computation → structured singular-value decay) gives a testable mechanistic account that could guide future contamination-mitigation strategies beyond the math-reasoning setting studied here. Key limitations include the restriction to SFT-injected contamination on arithmetic tasks, analysis of only linear projections, and the somewhat artificial 'restoration' goal of matching base-model errors; whether the geometry shifts generalize to RL-trained reasoning models, pre-training contamination, or non-mathematical reasoning remains open.
