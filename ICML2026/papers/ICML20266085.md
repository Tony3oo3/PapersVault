## Problem

Multi-step geometry theorem prediction suffers from a scalability bottleneck the authors term Structural Drift: as reasoning chains lengthen, vanilla in-context learning degrades sharply (approaching zero accuracy at depth L5–L6) because LLMs cannot recover the latent topological dependencies among theorems, leading to unstructured exploration over an exponentially growing action space. Existing neural-symbolic solvers address this with supervised parametric models, but their reliance on task-specific training over fixed theorem sets hampers generalization to unseen or expanded libraries without costly re-training.

## Input / Output

The system receives a geometry problem as a tuple of natural-language text, a diagram image, a pre-parsed formal state (a set of symbolic predicates), and a goal statement; it does not perform upstream parsing or formalization. At each reasoning step, the LLM is presented with a dynamically pruned and ranked candidate theorem list (typically ~30 out of 300), the current symbolic state, the last five execution steps, and the pruned TPG subgraph, and must output exactly one theorem name as a JSON call. The symbolic executor then verifies preconditions, applies the theorem, and updates the state; the loop repeats until the goal is satisfied or a 600-second per-problem timeout is reached.

## Method

Pri-TPG introduces a non-parametric structural prior called the Theorem Precedence Graph (TPG), a directed graph whose edges encode which theorem conclusions serve as prerequisites for other theorems, extracted from historical solution traces. A query-adaptive prior is constructed on the fly via multimodal retrieval (jina-embeddings-v4 over text + diagram + formal state) that retrieves top-K similar problems, unions their TPGs into a query-specific graph G_q with frequency-weighted edges, and adds a virtual START node. At each step, a state-aware prior further prunes G_q to the descendants of the last applied theorem and filters candidates by symbolic precondition checking, then ranks survivors with a composite score combining goal-keyword alignment, graph-successor promotion (first- and second-order children weighted by edge frequency), and a history penalty to prevent loops. The LLM acts as a planner selecting one theorem from this verified, prioritized subset, while a symbolic solver executes the step and feeds back the updated state, creating a closed-loop iterative process that requires no gradient-based optimization.

## Results

On FormalGeo7K (1,400 test problems), Pri-TPG with GPT-5.2 achieves 89.29% overall accuracy, outperforming the best LLM-only baseline (Claude 4.5 Sonnet, 75.79%) by 13.5 points and the strongest training-based solver (FGeo-HyperGNet, 88.36%) by 0.93 points, all in a training-free setting. On Geometry3K, Pri-TPG reaches 95.16% accuracy, surpassing FGeo-HyperGNet (91.99%), while on GeoQA it achieves 85.02%, slightly below FGeo-HyperGNet (85.64%). The method's advantage is most pronounced at mid-to-high difficulty: at L5 (9–10 theorems), Pri-TPG (GPT-5.2) attains 66.13% versus 56.45% for FGeo-HyperGNet, whereas Vanilla ICL collapses to 0%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Pri-TPG (GPT-5.2) at 89.29% on FormalGeo7K outperforms all six LLM-only direct-solving baselines by 13.5–31.5 points, with the largest gap over DeepSeek v3.2 (57.79%). Against training-based neural-symbolic solvers, it surpasses FGeo-HyperGNet (88.36%) by 0.93 points and FGeo-TP/FGeo-DRL (~80.9%) by roughly 8.4 points, while remaining entirely training-free. Among training-free baselines, the improvement is dramatic: Pri-TPG (GPT-5 mini) at 84.42% exceeds ForwardSearch (39.71%) by 44.7 points and Vanilla ICL (26.29%) by 58.1 points, demonstrating that the structural prior is the dominant factor. On Geometry3K, Pri-TPG (95.16%) beats FGeo-HyperGNet (91.99%) by 3.17 points; on GeoQA, FGeo-HyperGNet (85.64%) edges out Pri-TPG (85.02%) by 0.62 points, the only comparison where the proposed method trails. At the hardest difficulty tier (L6, ≥11 theorems), FGeo-HyperGNet (56.67%) still leads Pri-TPG (GPT-5.2, 30.00%), indicating that the non-parametric prior has not yet fully closed the gap on extremely long-horizon proofs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the FormalGeo formal system (Zhang et al., 2024) and its symbolic executor, but departs sharply from the dominant paradigm of supervised parametric theorem-selection policies (FGeo-HyperGNet, FGeo-DRL, Inter-GPS) by proposing a fully training-free alternative. Conceptually, it bridges three threads: in-context learning (identifying its failure mode as 'structural drift'), retrieval-augmented generation (repurposing RAG not for content injection but for constructing a directed precedence graph), and LLM-as-planner frameworks (replacing RL-based alignment with explicit topological constraints extracted from historical solution traces). The Theorem Precedence Graph is the key novelty: it converts flat theorem classification into guided traversal over a partial order, a structural inductive bias that prior neural-symbolic GPS pipelines encode implicitly in learned weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a training-free framework matches or exceeds the best supervised solver (89.29% vs. 88.36%) is practically significant: it removes the costly re-training cycle whenever theorem libraries expand, and the TPG module is explicitly designed as a plug-and-play component for existing trained solvers. The identification of structural drift—vanilla ICL collapsing to 0% at L5-L6—provides a clear diagnostic for why LLMs fail at long-horizon symbolic reasoning and motivates explicit structural priors as a general remedy. Open questions include whether the approach scales to truly novel theorem sets (the retrieval database is drawn from the training split), how to encode global reasoning depth rather than local precedence, and whether the repeated LLM calls (up to 20 steps × recovery attempts) remain tractable as proof length grows.
