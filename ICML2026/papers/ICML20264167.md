## Problem

Unsupervised disentanglement has long been considered fundamentally impossible without strong inductive biases, as infinitely many latent representations can explain the same observed distribution. Prior identifiability results either required statistical independence of latent factors or restricted the generative function to specific classes (e.g., conformal maps). This paper challenges the impossibility claim by arguing that the correct structural property defining a 'concept' is not statistical independence but orthogonal functional influence, and proves that this single geometric condition—combined with a mild full-support assumption on the latent domain—yields identifiability even for dependent latent factors and general nonlinear mixing functions.

## Input / Output

The method takes observed data x ∈ R^n (synthetic observations or RGB images from dSprites/3DShapes) that lie on a d-dimensional manifold with d << n, and recovers the underlying latent factors z ∈ R^d up to permutation and coordinate-wise reparameterization. Unlike standard ICA settings, the latent factors are explicitly allowed to be statistically dependent, modeled through a triangular (autoregressive) transformation of independent noise, while the generative mapping f: R^d → R^n is real-analytic, invertible, and has a pointwise column-orthogonal Jacobian. The output is a set of disentangled latent coordinates where each dimension corresponds to a distinct, manipulable degree of freedom.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-part identifiability framework: (1) a local constraint (Asm.1) requiring the Jacobian of the generative mapping to have orthogonal columns at every point, formalizing that each latent factor influences observations through a distinct local direction; and (2) a global constraint (Asm.2) requiring the latent support to be a hypercube (up to coordinate-wise reparameterization), ensuring all combinations of factor values are realizable. The paper proves that these two conditions together yield identifiability for general nonlinear mixing functions with dependent latent factors (Prop.2), extending prior results that required independence or restricted function classes. Empirically, the authors validate the theory by training Residual Flows with an orthogonality-regularized objective (C_IMA loss) on synthetic data with both independent and dependent latent factors, showing near-perfect recovery of ground-truth factors. They further demonstrate that VAEs implicitly enforce the local orthogonality condition through their factorized Gaussian posterior, and that removing this orthogonality bias (by replacing the posterior with a flow) systematically degrades disentanglement performance, confirming that orthogonality—not just independence—is the critical mechanism.

## Results

On synthetic data (d ∈ {3,6,9}), orthogonally constrained Normalizing Flows achieve near-perfect identifiability (Amari distance approaching 0, MCC approaching 1) in both independent and dependent latent settings, while unconstrained NFs fail to recover factors despite matching likelihoods. On dSprites, standard VAEs with orthogonal Jacobians (β-VAE, FactorVAE, β-TCVAE) achieve DCI disentanglement scores of 0.19–0.30, whereas the β-FlowVAE (which removes the orthogonality bias) drops to 0.10. On 3DShapes, the gap widens: standard VAEs reach DCI dis. of 0.53–0.68 while β-FlowVAE falls to 0.18, confirming that Jacobian orthogonality—not merely the factorized prior—is the critical mechanism driving disentanglement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central empirical comparison is between standard VAEs (β-VAE, FactorVAE, β-TCVAE), which implicitly enforce Jacobian orthogonality via their diagonal Gaussian posterior, and a β-FlowVAE variant that replaces the posterior with a Normalizing Flow, thereby removing the orthogonality bias while preserving the same factorized-prior independence pressure. Across all five disentanglement metrics on both dSprites and 3DShapes, the β-FlowVAE is systematically and substantially outperformed: on 3DShapes, DCI dis. drops from 0.53–0.68 (standard VAEs) to 0.18, and MIG drops from 0.23–0.30 to 0.06. Among the standard VAEs, β-TCVAE and FactorVAE generally lead on dSprites (DCI 0.30 and 0.25 respectively), while FactorVAE leads on 3DShapes DCI (0.68). The key takeaway is not which VAE variant wins, but that all three with orthogonal Jacobians dramatically outperform the flow-posterior variant, isolating orthogonality as the decisive factor. In the synthetic identifiability experiments (Fig. 3), orthogonally constrained Residual Flows achieve near-zero Amari distance and near-perfect MCC, while unconstrained flows of identical architecture fail to recover any latent structure, further confirming that the geometric constraint—not the likelihood objective or the independence prior alone—drives disentanglement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Independent Mechanism Analysis (IMA) by Gresele et al. (2021), which first formalized Jacobian orthogonality as a sufficient condition for identifiability under statistical independence and restricted functional families (conformal maps, local isometries). The key departures are: (1) generalizing from conformal/restricted mappings to arbitrary nonlinear mappings with orthogonal Jacobians, and (2) removing the statistical independence requirement by introducing a full-support copula assumption (Asm.2) that allows dependent latent factors. The paper also repositions the entire disentanglement literature by arguing that the classical impossibility results (Locatello et al., 2019a; Hyvärinen & Pajunen, 1999) arise from conflating statistical independence with functional independence, and that the latter is the correct defining property of a 'concept.'

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled resolution to the long-standing question of why VAEs empirically disentangle despite theoretical impossibility results: the dual mechanism of global independence (factorized prior) plus local orthogonality (diagonal posterior) is necessary, and removing either component degrades performance. This reframing opens a path to transplanting orthogonality constraints into other generative architectures (NFs, GANs, diffusion models) without VAE-specific limitations like posterior collapse. Key open questions include whether the orthogonal Jacobian assumption can be verified or approximated in high-dimensional real-world settings, whether Asm.2 (full-support copula / bounded domain) holds for natural data, and how to scale the C_IMA regularization to large models. The assumptions remain strong, and all empirical validation is on synthetic or low-dimensional structured data.
