## Problem

Incomplete multi-view clustering is hindered by the entanglement of shared cross-view semantics with view-specific factors in latent representations, and by the fact that existing imputation-based methods either rely on strong pairing assumptions or perform generation in a two-stage pipeline that does not embed clustering structure during training. This leads to error propagation, biased shared representations under high missing rates, and unreliable missing-view generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260500/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts V heterogeneous views (e.g., RGB, infrared, thermal images or text features) where each instance may have an arbitrary subset of views observed, indicated by binary vectors g^v ∈ {0,1}^n. It operates in a learned latent space rather than raw observation space, and produces both K-way cluster assignments (via a GMM prior over the aggregated semantic latent) and reconstructed missing views decoded from the latent diffusion output. The number of views V and their individual dimensions d_v can differ across views, and the framework handles any missing-view pattern without requiring a fixed observed-view configuration.

## Method

Each view is encoded into a latent h^v that is variationally factorized into a view-wise semantic latent z^v and a view-specific factor w^v. The semantic latents are aggregated via a Mixture of Wasserstein Barycenters (MWB) into a unified global representation z, which is regularized by a Gaussian mixture prior p(z|c) that explicitly couples representation learning with clustering. Information-theoretic objectives enforce disentanglement (minimizing I(z;{w^v})), semantic sufficiency (maximizing I({h^v}; z,{w^v})), and cross-view alignment (InfoNCE over {z^v}). A conditional latent diffusion model (U-Net with AdaGN and cross-attention) is then conditioned on both z and w^v to denoise and generate missing views, with the entire pipeline trained end-to-end under a unified ELBO that jointly optimizes diffusion reconstruction, hierarchical KL regularization, and contrastive alignment.

## Results

IDCD achieves the best ACC, NMI, and ARI across all five benchmark datasets (CUB, HandWritten, LandUse_21, CCV, Reuters) at every missing rate from 0.1 to 0.7. On CUB at 10% missing, IDCD reaches 82.00% ACC / 79.31% NMI / 69.21% ARI, outperforming the strongest baseline DCG (74.57/72.11/59.77) by ~7.4% ACC and ~9.4% ARI. On HandWritten at 10% missing, IDCD attains 97.17% ACC / 93.56% NMI / 93.83% ARI, surpassing the next-best RPCIC (90.86/83.15/81.15) by over 6% ACC. On the reconstruction task (CARL, 10% missing), IDCD achieves the lowest RMSE (0.14–0.19) and highest PSNR (14.30–17.09) across all three views, substantially outperforming DCG (RMSE 0.35–0.61, PSNR 4.34–9.16).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IDCD consistently outperforms all nine baselines (DIMVC, DSIMVC, APADC, CPSPAN, DVIMC, RPCIC, DCG, BURG, DIMVC-HIA) on every dataset and at every missing rate. The largest margins appear on CUB and HandWritten: at 10% missing, IDCD beats DCG by 7.4% ACC on CUB (82.00 vs 74.57) and RPCIC by 6.3% ACC on HandWritten (97.17 vs 90.86). Among imputation-based methods, DCG and DIMVC-HIA are the strongest competitors, but IDCD still leads by 5–7% ACC on CUB and 2–6% ACC on HandWritten. On the harder datasets (LandUse_21, CCV, Reuters), absolute scores are lower for all methods, yet IDCD maintains a clear 1–3% ACC advantage over the best baseline at each missing rate. For reconstruction quality on CARL (10% missing), IDCD reduces RMSE by 5–10% over BURG and by 60–70% over DCG, while improving PSNR by 1–2 dB over BURG and 5–8 dB over DCG, confirming that the disentangled conditional diffusion produces more faithful missing-view recovery.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: latent diffusion models (Rombach et al., 2022; Ho et al., 2020), variational multi-view representation learning with Wasserstein-based aggregation (Qiu et al., 2025), and incomplete multi-view clustering (IMVC). It departs from prior diffusion-based IMVC methods (Wen et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2025) by embedding a hierarchical clustering prior (GMM over a shared latent) directly into the generative process rather than treating clustering as a post-hoc step, and from VAE-based IMVC approaches by replacing naive mean or product-of-experts fusion with a Mixture of Wasserstein Barycenters that preserves geometric structure under arbitrary missing-view patterns. The information-theoretic disentanglement objective (Theorems 3.1–3.2) provides a principled bridge between the variational ELBO and explicit mutual-information constraints, unifying reconstruction, disentanglement, and cross-view alignment under a single lower bound on the data log-likelihood.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By coupling disentanglement, Wasserstein-based semantic aggregation, and conditional diffusion within a unified ELBO, the paper offers a theoretically grounded template for incomplete multi-view generative modeling that avoids the error propagation and two-stage decoupling common in prior imputation-based IMVC methods. The consistent gains across five datasets and four missing rates (up to ~6% ACC on CUB/HandWritten) suggest the framework is robust to view incompleteness, though improvements on harder datasets (CCV, Reuters) are more modest. Key open questions include whether equal modality weighting can be replaced by adaptive importance estimation, how to reduce the iterative denoising cost at inference, and whether the framework generalizes to open-set or streaming multi-view settings where the view set itself changes over time.
