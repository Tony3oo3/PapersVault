## Problem

Reconstructing full spatiotemporal boiling fields (velocity and temperature) from sparse image-derived observations is fundamentally ill-posed: partial observability induces a non-Markovian posterior that cannot be resolved from a single timestep, per the Mori-Zwanzig formalism. Existing generative inverse models produce snapshot reconstructions that violate temporal consistency, while autoregressive predictors face a cold-start problem when ground-truth initial states are unavailable. This paper addresses the gap by using observation history to bootstrap the initial reconstruction and then propagate it autoregressively within a unified flow matching framework.

## Input / Output

The method takes two 2D spatial fields per timestep as input: the signed distance function (SDF) encoding the liquid-vapor interface geometry (from segmentation) and the interface normal velocity (from optical flow), both extracted from high-speed imaging. It produces the complete thermofluid state — a scalar temperature field and a 2D velocity field defined over both liquid and vapor phases — at every timestep. Fields are downsampled by a factor of 4 (pool boiling) or 2 (flow boiling) for training, and the model operates on a fixed history window of 64 past observation frames for bootstrapping.

## Method

HB-ARFM uses a two-stage architecture within a single conditional flow matching formulation. A factored space-time transformer history encoder (spatial self-attention within frames, causal temporal self-attention across frames with FlashAttention and SwiGLU FFN) aggregates w past observations into an initial bulk state estimate, which conditions a Residual U-Net velocity field to reconstruct the first hidden state via OT-CFM. For subsequent timesteps, the same velocity field operates autoregressively, conditioning on the concatenation of the current observation and the previously predicted state, solving the ODE with a 4th-order Runge-Kutta (Heun) solver. Training jointly optimizes a bootstrap reconstruction loss and an autoregressive rollout loss over K steps, with the context updated from the model's own predictions rather than ground truth, enabling end-to-end learning of both initialization and temporal propagation.

## Results

On Task 2 (joint velocity and temperature reconstruction from interface geometry and motion) in subcooled pool boiling, HB-ARFM achieves the lowest wall heat flux relative error (2.25%) among all models, outperforming the next-best stochastic model (Flow Matching at 2.57%) and deterministic baselines (FFNO at 3.18%, UNet at 3.46%). HB-ARFM also attains the best temperature high-frequency energy ratio (0.920) among stochastic models, indicating superior preservation of fine-scale thermal structure, and maintains stable reconstruction over 300 autoregressive rollout steps with velocity relative L2 error of 0.863 and temperature relative L2 of 0.035. VE-SDE and PDEDiff fail catastrophically (velocity relative L2 of 1.94 and 10.58 respectively), while deterministic surrogates (UNet, FFNO) achieve lower pixelwise errors but systematically oversmooth physically important transport features.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HB-ARFM wins decisively on wall heat flux error (2.25%) against all baselines, with the closest competitors being Flow Matching (2.57%) and FFNO (3.18%). Against VE-SDE and PDEDiff, the comparison is stark: both models collapse catastrophically (velocity relative L2 of 1.94 and 10.58, wall heat flux errors of 87.8% and 111.4%), rendering them non-functional for this task. HistoryFM, the closest architectural competitor (same flow matching backbone but without autoregressive feedback), achieves slightly lower velocity BRMSE (0.510 vs. 0.537) and amplitude ratio (1.002 vs. 0.851) on single snapshots, but fails on temporal consistency during rollout, producing frame-to-frame drift and stochastic wake appearance/disappearance. Deterministic surrogates (UNet, FFNO) achieve the lowest pixelwise velocity errors (0.695, 0.827 relative L2) but do so through oversmoothing that suppresses fine-scale thermal gradients and velocity wakes, as evidenced by their lower HF energy ratios (0.862, 0.692) and inability to maintain coherent dynamics during autoregressive rollout. DDPM and DiffusionPDE produce fragmented or spatially oversmoothed fields with substantially higher heat flux errors (6.30% and 19.70%). Bubbleformer, a transformer-based forward predictor, fails entirely under cold-start initialization from partial observations, collapsing to temporally averaged fields with suppressed dynamics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate threads: conditional flow matching for inverse PDE reconstruction (Lipman et al., 2023; Huang et al., 2024) and autoregressive flow matching for temporal generative modeling (El-Gazzar & van Gerven, 2025; Jin et al., 2025). Prior generative inverse methods (DiffusionPDE, FunDPS) target snapshot reconstruction and ignore temporal structure, while prior AR flow matching methods assume access to full initial states for forward generation. HB-ARFM departs from both by introducing a history-conditioned bootstrap stage that resolves the cold-start problem unique to inverse settings, grounded in the Mori-Zwanzig formalism's prediction that partial observability induces non-Markovian posteriors requiring temporal context to resolve.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration of full spatiotemporal boiling field reconstruction (velocity + temperature) from imaging-derived observations alone, establishing boiling as a rigorous benchmark for inverse SciML where sharp interfaces, multi-physics coupling, and partial observability coexist. Key open questions include: whether explicit PDE/conservation constraints can be incorporated without breaking the generative framework, whether the lossy history encoder can be made more expressive to better capture the MZ memory kernel, and whether the approach transfers to real experimental data given the sim-to-real gap in multiphase flows. The method's generalization from pool to flow boiling suggests broader applicability to other coupled PDE systems with moving interfaces.
