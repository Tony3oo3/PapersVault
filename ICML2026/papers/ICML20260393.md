## Problem

Imbalanced Domain Generalization (IDG) requires handling both domain shift and heterogeneous long-tailed label shift simultaneously, yet remains underexplored due to the entanglement of these two shifts and the absence of formal generalization guarantees. Existing domain generalization methods primarily align marginal input distributions P(X) while neglecting label shift, and imbalanced-data methods ignore cross-domain posterior inconsistency, leaving a theoretical and methodological gap for jointly addressing both effects.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260393/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images from N source domains, each with a potentially different long-tailed class distribution over K semantic classes, and outputs a K-dimensional prediction vector (class probabilities) via a ResNet-50 backbone. The contrastive and alignment objectives operate directly in the prediction space rather than the feature space, and the hard-negative augmentation dynamically constructs mixed samples within each mini-batch based on current model confidence scores, making the training procedure adaptive to the evolving decision boundary.

## Method

NDCL reformulates the InfoNCE objective by replacing cosine similarity with cosine dissimilarity and positioning negative pairs in the numerator, which shifts the dominant learning signal toward negatives and naturally amplifies gradient repulsion for minority-class samples surrounded by majority-class negatives. A hard negative augmentation strategy (mixup between low-confidence in-class samples and high-confidence out-of-class samples) focuses contrastive learning on semantically ambiguous boundary regions. Posterior consistency across domains is enforced through a prediction-central alignment loss that aligns per-class, per-domain prediction prototypes using a SupCon-inspired objective, decoupling alignment from sample proportions. Intra-class compactness is promoted via a cross-entropy loss with adaptive per-sample re-weighting that emphasizes hard examples. The entire framework is motivated by a novel H-divergence-based generalization bound for IDG that explicitly decomposes target risk into source error, a margin term, prior distribution discrepancy, and posterior distribution discrepancy, justifying the focus on decision-boundary steering rather than explicit P(X) alignment.

## Results

Under the GINIDG setting, NDCL achieves 78.0% average accuracy on VLCS and 85.7% on PACS, outperforming all 22 baselines. Under the more challenging TotalHeavyTail setting, NDCL reaches 75.6% (VLCS), 76.4% (PACS), and 49.0% (OfficeHome) average accuracy, with particularly strong few-shot performance (61.0%, 79.2%, 30.5% respectively). Under the Duality setting with heterogeneous label shifts, NDCL attains 61.6% (VLCS), 71.1% (PACS), and 55.7% (OfficeHome), again leading all methods. The few-shot class improvements are most pronounced, e.g., 79.4% on PACS-Few (GINIDG) vs. 76.1% for the next-best DANN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NDCL outperforms all 22 baselines in average accuracy across all three imbalance settings (GINIDG, TotalHeavyTail, Duality) on all three benchmarks. Against IDG-specific methods (GINIDG, BoDA, SAMALTDG), NDCL improves average accuracy by over 1.5% consistently, with the largest gains on few-shot classes (e.g., 79.4% vs. 71.6% for SAMALTDG on PACS-Few under GINIDG). Conventional domain generalization methods like PGrad (84.3% PACS Avg) and DANN (76.1% PACS Few) are competitive on majority classes but degrade sharply on minority classes, whereas NDCL maintains balanced performance across Many/Medium/Few sub-groups. Among imbalanced-data methods, BSoftmax achieves the highest PACS-Few score (81.8%) under GINIDG, slightly exceeding NDCL's 79.4%, but NDCL dominates in overall average and on VLCS. Under the TotalHeavyTail setting, BSoftmax becomes more competitive on minority classes (62.1% VLCS-Few vs. NDCL's 61.0%), yet NDCL still leads in average accuracy across all three benchmarks. Under Duality, conventional DG methods (DANN at 60.9% VLCS Avg, PGrad at 55.7%) close the gap due to heterogeneous label shifts reducing inter-class competition, but NDCL still leads by 0.7–3.7% in average accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical H-divergence domain adaptation bound (Ben-David et al., 2010) and the multi-source convex-hull formulation (Albuquerque et al., 2019) by reformulating the discrepancy over joint distributions P(X,Y) rather than marginal P(X), explicitly surfacing posterior discrepancy and decision margin as first-class terms. Methodologically, it inverts the InfoNCE contrastive objective (Khosla et al., 2020) so that negatives become the primary learning signal rather than positives, a departure from both standard InfoNCE and SupCon. It also departs from prior IDG methods (GINIDG, SAMALTDG, BoDA) that rely on divide-and-conquer strategies or explicit re-weighting, instead leveraging the natural abundance of negatives for minority classes to shape decision boundaries without modifying the data distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical generalization bound for Imbalanced Domain Generalization, filling a notable gap where prior DG theory only modeled prior distribution alignment and ignored the compounding effects of label shift on posteriors and margins. The negative-dominant contrastive learning framework is practical (no generative models, no resampling) and offers a principled alternative to ad-hoc re-weighting. However, the paper acknowledges that explicit modeling of domain priors remains ill-posed under severe imbalance, and the performance gains, while consistent across three benchmarks and three imbalance settings, are moderate (1–3% over the best baselines). Open questions include whether the approach extends to more extreme imbalance ratios, non-i.i.d. domain mixtures, or settings where the label space itself varies across domains.
