## Problem

Existing multi-step retrosynthesis planners commit to a single static single-step retrosynthesis (SSR) model for the entire search, despite pronounced skill disparity: no SSR is universally optimal across molecular states, and different models dominate in different chemical spaces. This static transition-model choice prevents the planner from exploiting complementary strengths of diverse SSRs, and the problem is compounded by sparse, delayed rewards that make it difficult to attribute credit to individual model-selection decisions during search.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260447/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives a target molecule SMILES, a fixed portfolio of seven SSR models (template-based, template-free, and editing-based), and a 23.1M-molecule commercial inventory, and produces a complete retrosynthesis route in which every leaf node is purchasable. At each step the LLM emits a structured action (EXPAND <smiles> using <model_name> (predictions=<n>) or TERMINATE) within an AND-OR search tree; the observation fed back includes the set of expandable molecules, solved leaves, tree statistics, remaining budget, and cumulative reward, which together form the textual context the LLM processes as its implicit belief state.

## Method

RetrOrchestrator reframes multi-step retrosynthesis as a POMDP in which the LLM's prefilled KV cache serves as the implicit belief state, and the action space jointly selects which molecule to expand, which SSR to invoke, and how many predictions to request. Training uses Scaffold-aware Group-Relative Policy Optimization (SA-GRPO), which augments the standard GRPO episodic advantage with a step-level advantage computed by clustering intermediate observations across rollouts via Bemis-Murcko scaffold similarity (Tanimoto coefficient, threshold δ=0.95), reducing singleton clusters from ~20% to ~6% and yielding denser, lower-variance credit signals for individual SSR choices. A two-stage curriculum first trains on easy molecules (SCScore < 4) to enforce chemical-rule adherence, then on hard molecules (SCScore > 4) to develop strategic planning, with the full pipeline built on Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct and the verl-agent RL framework.

## Results

On the Retro*-190 benchmark, RetrOrchestrator with Scaffold-aware GRPO achieves a state-of-the-art 94.21% success rate (95% bootstrap CI [90.53%, 97.26%]), surpassing the strongest LLM baseline AOT* (Song et al., 90.53%, backed by 671B DeepSeek-R1) by 3.7 points and the best static MDP planner Retro*+EditRetro (80.53%) by 13.7 points. The method also attains the highest average SCScore (3.97) among solved molecules and a 76.40% utilization ratio, indicating it tackles chemically complex targets efficiently. On the out-of-distribution PDB-600 set, RetrOrchestrator reaches 68.83%, exceeding the best static baseline (Retro*+EditRetro, 59.67%) by 9.2 points while remaining Pareto-optimal in both wall-clock time and SSR-call count.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RetrOrchestrator (SA-GRPO, 94.21%) outperforms all 12 baselines on Retro*-190. Against the strongest LLM planner AOT* (Song et al., 90.53%, using 671B DeepSeek-R1), it gains +3.7 points with a 1.5B model; against Wang et al. (2025a, GPT-4o, 64.74%), the margin is +29.5 points. Among static MDP planners, it beats Retro*+EditRetro (80.53%) by +13.7 points and Retro*+Ensemble (77.89%) by +16.3 points, while also requiring fewer expansions (15.35 vs. 20.67 and 4.48 respectively). In the budget-matched comparison with PDVN (Liu et al., ICML 2023), RetrOrchestrator achieves 94.21% vs. 47.89% at 10 expansions (~2× gap) and 96.84% vs. 93.68% at 50 expansions. The non-LLM state-dependent SSR router (82.63%) is outperformed by +11.6 points (McNemar p=3×10⁻⁴), confirming the gain comes from the joint policy over molecule, SSR, and prediction count rather than routing alone. On OOD PDB-600, RetrOrchestrator (68.83%) leads Retro*+EditRetro (59.67%) by +9.2 points and Retro*+Ensemble (47.33%) by +21.5 points, with the gap preserved at 5× the original benchmark scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MDP formulation of multi-step retrosynthesis planning (Retro*, MCTS) by reframing it as a POMDP where the single-step retrosynthesis (SSR) model is a selectable tool rather than a fixed transition operator, drawing on the LLM-as-POMDP-planner literature (Kim et al., 2025; Piotrowski et al., 2025) that treats the KV cache as an implicit belief state. For training, it builds on GRPO's critic-free group-based advantage estimation (Shao et al., 2024) but departs from both trajectory-level tool-calling RL (ToolRL, ReTool) and exact-state-matching step-level credit (GiGPO) by introducing scaffold-aware clustering that groups observations by Bemis-Murcko structural similarity, yielding denser and more stable step-level advantages in the sparse chemical state space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The POMDP framing elevates SSR model selection from a static design decision to a learned, state-conditioned policy, and the 94.21% success rate with a 1.5B model (vs. 90.53% from a 671B Deepseek-R1-based planner) suggests that orchestration intelligence can substitute for raw model scale in sequential planning. The scaffold-aware clustering technique for step-level credit assignment is potentially transferable to other sparse-reward, tool-use settings where exact state recurrence is rare. Key limitations include evaluation on a single 190-molecule benchmark, a fixed set of seven SSRs, and a notable OOD drop (68.83% on PDB-600), leaving open whether the POMDP advantage persists with larger tool portfolios, more diverse chemical spaces, or at scale beyond 1.5B parameters.
