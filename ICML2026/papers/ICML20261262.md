## Problem

Integrating high-dimensional bulk RNA-seq and DNA methylation into multimodal cancer models remains challenging: purely transformer-based architectures suffer quadratic memory scaling over ~17k gene sequences, existing multi-omics integration methods (late fusion, VAEs) underperform joint representation learning, and real clinical settings frequently present missing modalities that degrade model performance. The paper addresses all three gaps simultaneously with a single pre-trained bimodal model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261262/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes paired bulk RNA-seq (TPM per gene, log10-transformed) and DNA methylation (beta values per CpG site from Illumina 450k arrays, aggregated to gene-level via simple averaging), both tokenized into 64 linear bins over 17,116 genes. It produces a 512-dimensional mean-pooled embedding from the last attention layer, which feeds downstream MLP heads for 33-class cancer-type classification, Cox survival prediction, or zero-shot k-NN/Leiden clustering. Because methylation is aggregated at the gene level, the architecture is inherently array-agnostic, enabling zero-shot transfer to legacy 27k platforms without retraining.

## Method

MOJO combines a U-Net-style convolutional tower (8 successive 2× downsampling operations reducing 17,116 tokens to 67) with an 8-layer, 16-head transformer operating on the compressed sequence, followed by a deconvolutional tower with residual connections to restore the original length for per-gene prediction heads. Pre-training uses standard bimodal masked language modeling (15% corruption: 80% MASK, 10% random, 10% unchanged) over 192 billion tokens on 9,252 TCGA pairs, yielding a ~100× speedup over purely transformer baselines at equivalent or better accuracy. A gene embedding vector serves as positional encoding since the omics data is permutation-invariant. For missing-modality robustness, an auxiliary mutual information loss is added during fine-tuning to encourage modality-invariant predictions, and an extended pre-training variant incorporates samples with one modality entirely masked.

## Results

On the pan-cancer TCGA dataset (33 cohorts, 9,252 samples), MOJO achieves a test weighted-F1 of 0.952±0.006 for cancer-type classification and a C-index of 0.771±0.006 (weighted C-index 0.670±0.009) for survival analysis, both state-of-the-art among all compared methods. In zero-shot settings, MOJO embeddings yield 0.928 accuracy and 0.862 NMI on pan-cancer clustering versus 0.870/0.771 for late integration. On the external ICGC cohort (405 donors, 4 cancer types), MOJO generalizes with a C-index of 0.823±0.019, outperforming both unimodal baselines. MOJO also achieves a ~100× speedup in update time (0.059 s vs. 4.5–18 s) and supports batch sizes up to 1,024 versus 4–16 for transformer-based alternatives.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MOJO outperforms all 11 baselines on both primary tasks. Against the strongest unimodal encoder, BulkRNABert (0.943 weighted-F1; 0.749 C-index), MOJO gains +0.9 F1 points and +0.022 C-index, confirming the bimodal advantage. The closest bimodal competitor, late integration with cross-attention (0.945 F1; 0.756 C-index), is beaten by MOJO by +0.7 F1 and +0.015 C-index, demonstrating that joint pre-training surpasses post-hoc fusion. CustOmics (end-to-end, 0.946 F1) is the nearest deep-learning multi-omics baseline on classification but trails MOJO by 0.6 F1 points and a larger 0.085 C-index gap on survival (0.686 vs. 0.771). Classical methods (MOFA 0.852, MFA 0.848, NMF 0.827) and GNN-based approaches (GAT 0.915, IntegrAO 0.911) are consistently 3–10 F1 points behind. Notably, MOJO's probing-only variant (0.945 F1) already matches late integration, and the full fine-tuned model extends this to a clear margin, while MOJO without pre-training (0.891) confirms the self-supervised objective is essential. On the external ICGC cohort, MOJO (0.823 C-index) beats BulkRNABert (0.805) and MethFormer (0.810), and on TARGET zero-shot clustering it achieves NMI 0.949 vs. 0.794 for late integration.

## Relation to Prior Work

MOJO extends the masked language modeling paradigm for omics representation learning (established by BulkRNABert for RNA-seq and scGPT for single-cell data) to the bimodal setting of joint RNA-seq and DNA methylation, departing from the dominant late-integration strategy (concatenation, cross-attention, MultiSurv max-pooling) in favor of joint self-supervised pre-training. Architecturally, it draws on long-range genomic models (Avsec et al., Linder et al., Joshi et al.) that combine convolutional downsampling with transformer attention to handle sequences of ~17k tokens, and adapts the mutual information auxiliary loss from Ramazanova et al. (2025)—originally a test-time adaptation technique for audio/vision—to a fine-tuning-time constraint for missing-modality robustness. The gene-level methylation aggregation strategy positions MOJO as inherently array-agnostic, contrasting with site-level models like MethylBERT that are locked to specific microarray platforms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical clinical relevance is significant: MOJO's ~100× training speedup over purely transformer-based models makes large-scale multi-omics fine-tuning feasible, and its zero-shot cross-platform transferability (450k→27k arrays) and missing-modality recovery (weighted-F1 from 0.538 to 0.916 via mutual information loss) address two major deployment barriers in real-world oncology. Key limitations remain: gene-level averaging discards site-level biological variation, the TCGA pre-training set (~9k samples) is small and demographically biased, and the mutual information approach still does not fully match unimodal performance when a modality is absent. Open questions include extending to more than two modalities, relaxing the strict gene-level alignment requirement, and prospective validation on diverse populations before clinical deployment.
