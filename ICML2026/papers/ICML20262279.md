## Problem

Autoregressive neural PDE surrogates for conservation laws suffer from conservation drift and physical bound violations (e.g., negative concentrations) that compound during long rollouts. Existing flux-rate-based conservative surrogates preserve conservation by construction but inherit CFL stability constraints from explicit temporal integration, coupling temporal stride to spatial resolution, and do not address bound preservation. No prior method simultaneously guarantees exact conservation, structural bound enforcement, and large-timestep prediction at full spatial resolution.

## Input / Output

The network takes a PDE state field on a regular grid (conserved quantities such as concentration, water depth, or momentum) concatenated with optional external fields (e.g., velocity) and, for non-periodic domains, a binary identity channel distinguishing interior from ghost cells. Rather than predicting the next state directly, it outputs cumulative transport amounts between each cell and a configurable neighborhood of radius R, which are applied via a divergence-form update to yield the next state. The neighborhood size is set per temporal stride, decoupling the surrogate's time step from spatial resolution and enabling large strides without grid coarsening.

## Method

FluxNet learns cumulative transport amounts—the total conserved quantity redistributed between neighboring cells over the entire surrogate interval—rather than instantaneous flux rates, eliminating temporal integration and the associated CFL constraint. A shared convolutional backbone (ResNet or FNO) maps inputs to feature maps, and modular transport heads apply 1×1 convolutions to produce raw parameters transformed via sigmoid, softmax, or softplus into physically constrained quantities. The L-head parameterizes outflow as a learned fraction of available surplus above a lower bound; the U-head dually limits inflow by remaining capacity below an upper bound; and the D-head combines both branches with a dual consistency loss for dual-bounded fields. Conservation holds by construction because every outflow from one cell is an inflow to another, so all contributions cancel upon domain summation regardless of how transport amounts are computed. Training uses pushforward unrolling with stop-gradient to mitigate error accumulation during autoregressive rollout.

## Results

On the 2D shallow water benchmark (T=2, 2× extrapolation), FluxNet-LAP with a ResNet backbone achieves depth MAE of 1.75×10⁻³ (81% lower than the best projection baseline at 9.41×10⁻³) with conservation error at machine precision (~10⁻⁸) and zero depth violations. On 1D traffic flow with periodic boundaries, FluxNet-D achieves MAE 2.79×10⁻³ with conservation error 6.7×10⁻⁸, compared to 8.09×10⁻³ for ResNet-AR. At the large temporal stride Δt_model=50Δt, FluxNet-D maintains MAE 50.1×10⁻⁴ while the flux-rate baseline FluxGNN diverges to 1150×10⁻⁴. On 2D spinodal decomposition, the 1000Δt model achieves a 17.3× wall-clock speedup over a GPU phase-field solver while preserving two-point correlation statistics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FNO-AR (Li et al., ICLR 2021), FluxNet achieves substantially lower error across all benchmarks: on shallow water, FluxNet-LAP (FNO) reduces depth MAE from 5.93×10⁻³ to 1.37×10⁻³ (77% reduction) while FNO-AR exhibits 0.80% depth violations versus zero for FluxNet; on traffic flow, FluxNet-D (FNO) achieves MAE 3.17×10⁻³ versus 7.30×10⁻³ for FNO-AR. Against FluxGNN (Horie & Mitsume, ICML 2024), the comparison is nuanced: at the standard stride Δt_model=10Δt on Dirichlet traffic flow, FluxGNN achieves lower MAE (3.54×10⁻⁴ vs. 11.7×10⁻⁴) owing to its more expressive GNN backbone, but equipping FluxGNN with the paper's D-head (FluxGNN-D) improves it to 2.73×10⁻⁴, confirming head modularity. The decisive advantage emerges at the large stride Δt_model=50Δt: FluxNet-D maintains MAE 50.1×10⁻⁴ while FluxGNN diverges to 1150×10⁻⁴, validating the CFL-free cumulative-transport paradigm over flux-rate integration. Overall, FluxNet wins on conservation precision (10⁻⁸ vs. 10⁻²–10⁻³), bound preservation (zero violations vs. 0.8–3.15%), and large-timestep stability, while FluxGNN retains a narrow accuracy edge at small timesteps due to backbone capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

FluxNet departs from the FVM-inspired flux-rate surrogate lineage (FINN, FluxGNN, entropy-stable flux-form networks) by replacing instantaneous inter-cell flux rates with cumulative transport amounts over the full surrogate interval, thereby eliminating the explicit temporal integration step and its associated CFL constraint. The capacity-constrained transport heads (L, U, D) draw conceptually on positivity-preserving limiter design from classical numerical schemes (Van Leer, Zhang & Shu) but recast it as a neural parameterization with structural guarantees. The modular backbone-head factorization and pushforward training strategy are inherited from the broader neural-operator and autoregressive PDE surrogate literature (FNO, message-passing GNNs), while the LBM-inspired stencil channelization is purely structural and imposes no collision or equilibrium operators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CFL-free large-timestep capability translates directly into practical acceleration (17.3× at 1000Δt for spinodal decomposition), a regime inaccessible to flux-rate surrogates that inherit explicit-integration stability limits. The modular transport heads are demonstrated to plug into heterogeneous backbones (ResNet, FNO, FluxGNN's GNN), suggesting broad applicability. Key open questions include: the D-head provides only empirical (not strict) dual-bound guarantees; the fixed stencil sacrifices the resolution invariance of spectral architectures; non-periodic validation is limited to 1D Dirichlet conditions; and extension to source terms, unstructured meshes, and adaptive neighborhoods remains future work.
