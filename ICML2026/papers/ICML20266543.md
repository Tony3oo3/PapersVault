## Problem

AI4Science workflows in indirect-observation domains treat released datasets as fixed, objective data, but these datasets are actually derivative representations produced by multi-stage measurement-to-dataset pipelines whose configuration choices are rarely specified, constrained, or audited. This 'frozen lens' collapses observation-model uncertainty before inference begins, producing three structural failure modes: hidden hypothesis spaces, uncertified transportability across measurement regimes, and ungoverned multiplicity of defensible pipelines. The paper argues these pipelines should be treated as first-class inference components rather than frozen dataset definitions.

## Input / Output

The empirical audit consumes raw resting-state EEG time series from two independent datasets (CISIR: 34 MDD + 30 HC; MODMA: 24 MDD + 29 HC), each processed through 864 literature-derived preprocessing configurations (spanning filtering, referencing, denoising, downsampling, and windowing) and 284 Statistical Pairwise Interaction (SPI) connectivity operators, yielding 245,376 pipeline-operator configurations. The output is a per-configuration MDD-HC hemispheric functional-connectivity asymmetry effect estimate scored on two axes: within-dataset significance (Mann-Whitney U with FDR control) and cross-dataset stability (bootstrap-supported directional agreement and relative ordering). The headline result is a survival rate of 1/245,376 ≈ 0.0004% under the joint criteria, and the broader deliverable is a three-layer Computable Observation Framework specification (typed pipeline DAG, stability-aligned optimization, and shared governance platform).

## Method

The paper combines a formal theoretical framework with a large-scale empirical stress test. Theoretically, it formalizes the pipeline as an observation model P_φ mapping raw measurements x to a released representation z_φ, showing that fixing φ collapses the effective hypothesis space H_Φ to a single H_φ₀ (C1), commits measurements to an irreversible quotient space before learning (C2), and leaves a nontrivial admissible set Φ whose dispersion is resolved by untracked selection rather than uncertainty propagation (C3). Empirically, the audit sweeps 864 × 284 = 245,376 pipeline-operator configurations across two EEG datasets, scoring each on within-dataset MDD-HC significance and cross-dataset bootstrap stability, demonstrating that only one configuration survives both criteria. The constructive proposal is a domain-specific Computable Observation Framework with three layers: (1) a versioned, executable pipeline specification as a typed workflow DAG with admissible parameter domains, (2) stability-aligned optimization that searches over Φ for configurations maximizing cross-setting claim stability rather than proxy fit, and (3) a governance platform that accumulates auditable evidence over the observation-model space for cross-study comparability.

## Results

The empirical EEG audit evaluates 864 preprocessing pipelines × 284 Statistical Pairwise Interaction (SPI) operators = 245,376 pipeline-operator configurations across two independent resting-state EEG datasets (MODMA and CISIR). Under a joint criterion requiring both within-dataset MDD-HC significance (Mann-Whitney U with FDR control) and cross-dataset stability (bootstrap-supported directional and rank agreement), only 1 of 245,376 configurations survives, yielding a survival rate of ≈0.0004%. The sole surviving configuration is Squared Cross-Corr, which maintains consistent hemispheric asymmetry effect direction and relative ordering across both datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a position paper with an empirical stress-test rather than a method-vs-baseline comparison, so there are no external baselines to beat or match. The internal comparison is across the 245,376 admissible pipeline-operator configurations: many attain within-dataset significance in at least one dataset, but the vast majority fail cross-dataset directional or rank stability, exposing the (C1)–(C3) failure modes. The paper contrasts its stability-first criterion against common practice (proxy-metric selection, single-default pipelines, post-hoc robustness checks), arguing that proxy fit (accuracy, AUC, MSE) weakly identifies the pipeline and does not determine the mechanistic conclusion. The ≈0.0004% survival rate is presented as a warning signal about frozen-lens fragility in AI4Science rather than a performance benchmark, and the authors explicitly scope it to the audited space and protocol rather than claiming it generalizes as a universal rate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes and reframes several existing threads—multiverse analysis (Steegen et al., 2016), many-analysts studies (Silberzahn et al., 2018; Botvinik-Nezer et al., 2020), specification-curve robustness checks (Simonsohn et al., 2020), pipeline benchmarking (Maier-Hein et al., 2018), and uncertain-evidence formalisms in probabilistic inference (Jeffrey, 1990; Pearl, 2014; Munk et al., 2023)—into a unified argument that the measurement-to-dataset pipeline is itself an inference component, not a fixed dataset interface. It departs from prior work by elevating pipeline variation from a study-level robustness nuisance to a governed, quantifiable object of scientific inference, and by proposing domain-specific Computable Observation Frameworks as the infrastructure to make this operational. The EEG audit operationalizes the multiverse/many-analysts paradigm at the pipeline level (864 preprocessing × 284 connectivity operators) rather than at the analysis-choice level, providing a concrete stress test of the 'frozen lens' critique.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The near-zero cross-dataset survival rate (1/245,376 ≈ 0.0004%) is a stark quantitative warning that pipeline-dependent mechanistic claims in AI4Science may be far more fragile than the community assumes, even under 'best practice' conventions. The proposed three-layer Computable Observation Framework (specification, stability-first optimization, governance platform) is a concrete but ambitious infrastructure proposal whose practical adoption depends on domain communities defining admissible pipeline spaces and on tooling that does not yet exist at scale. Key open questions include: how to define scientifically admissible Φ without circularity, whether stability-first objectives can be computed efficiently for high-dimensional pipeline spaces, and whether the framework generalizes beyond EEG to domains like genomics, cryo-EM, and radio astronomy where the paper argues the same structural failure modes apply. The audit is limited to two EEG datasets and one scientific target, so the 0.0004% figure should be read as a domain-specific stress test rather than a universal constant.
