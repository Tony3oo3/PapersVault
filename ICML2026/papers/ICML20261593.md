## Problem

Researchers fitting nonnegative tensor factorizations face a critical gap: beyond a handful of mature implementations for CP and Tucker, tailored decompositions require either scratch implementation or gradient-based automatic differentiation, which struggles under nonnegativity constraints and suffers from slow convergence and hyperparameter sensitivity. This makes it difficult to explore domain-specific tensor models that capture the structure of real multi-way data.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a nonnegative M-mode tensor (with optional missing entries handled via a binary mask), a model specification given entirely as an einsum string (e.g., 'wr, dr, hr, irk, jrk → wdhij'), and loss parameters (α, β) selecting from the (α,β)-divergence family or other decomposable losses. It outputs nonnegative factor matrices/tensors Θ^(ℓ) and the fitted tensor approximation Ŷ. The model string is the sole interface for specifying arbitrary tensor network structures, and switching loss functions requires changing only one or two scalar parameters.

## Method

NNEinFact performs block-coordinate multiplicative updates where each iteration requires exactly three einsum calls: one to compute the fitted tensor Ŷ via the model string, and two to compute the numerator A and denominator B of the update ratio by swapping the model output indices with the factor being updated. The theoretical foundation is a majorization-minimization framework: the loss is decomposed into convex and concave parts, a surrogate Q is constructed via Jensen's inequality (convex part) and first-order Taylor expansion (concave part), and minimizing Q in closed form yields the multiplicative update. A decomposability property (Theorem 3.1) characterizes the broad family of loss functions admitting this update form, unifying (α,β)-divergences, squared Euclidean distance, KL divergence, Itakura-Saito, and others under a single framework with convergence to a stationary point guaranteed.

## Results

On the WITS trade dataset, the custom NNEinFact model achieves a heldout (α,β)-divergence of 0.0356 (α=1.0, β=−0.5), substantially outperforming CP (0.0570), Tucker (0.0782), and tensor-train (0.0780) by 37–55%. On ICEWS, the custom model reaches 0.0273 (α=1.0) versus 0.0285 for CP and 0.0342 for Tucker. In the optimization comparison, NNEinFact's multiplicative updates attain less than half the heldout loss of the best Adam baseline across most (α,β) configurations (e.g., 0.00759 vs. 0.00973 on Uber at α=0.7, β=0) while converging 2–11× faster in wall-clock time (e.g., 9.98 s vs. 19.79 s on Uber; 17.69 s vs. 193.25 s on WITS at α=1.3, β=1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against gradient-based Adam (the only practical alternative for fitting custom einsum models), NNEinFact's multiplicative updates win on heldout loss in 11 of 18 (dataset, α, β) configurations, with the largest gaps on WITS (e.g., 2.17 vs. 13.56 at α=1.3, β=1) and ICEWS (e.g., 0.0185 vs. 0.0279 at α=0.7, β=0); the sole tie is WITS at α=0.7, β=0 (both 0.0086). Runtime to convergence favours NNEinFact in most settings, with speedups ranging from ~1.5× (Uber, α=0.7, β=1) to ~11× (WITS, α=1.3, β=1). Against standard decompositions (CP, Tucker, tensor-train, and their low-rank variants), the dataset-tailored custom models achieve the lowest heldout loss in 8 of 9 (dataset, α) settings; the single exception is ICEWS at α=0.8 where CP edges the custom model by 1% (0.0201 vs. 0.0203). The multiplicative-update versions of CP and Tucker consistently outperform their HALS counterparts (HALS-CP, HALS-Tucker), confirming that the MM-based updates are superior even for standard models. On the WITS dataset the advantage is most pronounced: the custom model beats CP by 37% (α=1.0) and 25% (α=1.2), and beats tensor-train by 55% and 54% respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

NNEinFact extends the line of work on multiplicative updates for nonnegative tensor factorization initiated by Yılmaz & Cemgil (2010) and Yilmaz et al. (2011), who derived heuristic updates for specific model-loss pairs (CP/Tucker under Euclidean or β-divergence) without convergence guarantees. The paper's key conceptual departure is the decomposability property (Theorem 3.1), which provides a clean algebraic condition under which a majorization-minimization surrogate yields a closed-form multiplicative update, thereby unifying and extending prior results from Cichocki et al. (2011) on (α,β)-divergence NMF to arbitrary einsum factorization structures. By centering the einsum operation as the computational backbone, the framework subsumes CP, Tucker, tensor-train, tensor-ring, tensor-wheel, and manybody-approximation decompositions under a single string-specified interface, a level of generality not present in prior specialized algorithms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: researchers wanting tailored nonnegative tensor factorizations beyond CP and Tucker have had to either implement custom algorithms from scratch or rely on gradient-based methods that converge slowly and are sensitive to hyperparameters. NNEinFact's string-based model specification and guaranteed monotone convergence lower the barrier to exploring domain-specific tensor structures, and the >37% heldout-loss improvement from custom models over standard ones demonstrates tangible scientific value. Open questions include scaling to tensors that exceed memory (the authors suggest drawing from the generalized CP decomposition and stochastic gradient methods), and whether the decomposability property can be further expanded to cover additional loss families. The framework's reliance on multiple random initializations and its restriction to nonnegative parameters are acknowledged limitations.
