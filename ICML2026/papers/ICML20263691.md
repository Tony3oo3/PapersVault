## Problem

Gradient-based planning through learned world models suffers from two fundamental failures at long horizons: (1) serial backpropagation through T stacked dynamics evaluations produces exponentially ill-conditioned gradients, and (2) state-input Jacobians in high-dimensional visual world models are brittle and adversarial, allowing the optimizer to exploit spurious directions rather than discover physically meaningful transitions. Existing planners either avoid gradients entirely (CEM, MPPI) or backpropagate through full rollouts, both of which degrade sharply as horizon length and action dimensionality increase.

## Input / Output

The planner receives an initial visual observation and a goal visual observation, each encoded into a fixed-dimensional latent vector by a frozen DINOv2 backbone (224×224 RGB images). The pre-trained world model F_θ operates in this latent space, consuming 5 stacked actions (a 10-dim vector for 2-D action spaces) and predicting 5 steps ahead, so a planning horizon of H environment steps requires H/5 world model evaluations. The output is a continuous action sequence of length H, which is then executed in the simulated environment to measure task success.

## Method

GRASP replaces the serial rollout with a lifted, time-parallel formulation where intermediate states are independent optimization variables coupled only by soft one-step dynamics penalties (a collocation-style objective). To prevent the optimizer from exploiting adversarial state-input gradients, it applies a stop-gradient to the state argument of F_θ while retaining action gradients, and adds a dense one-step goal loss on each prediction to supply task-aligned signal without state backpropagation. Langevin-style Gaussian noise is injected into the state iterates at every step to promote exploration and escape local minima, and every K_sync iterations a brief full-gradient rollout step (standard backprop through the T-step chain) refines the plan toward a true dynamics-consistent trajectory. The paper provides a theoretical analysis showing that the lifted formulation has O(1) smoothness independent of horizon (versus exponential growth for shooting), that the stop-gradient objective converges linearly to a unique fixed point in the linear case, and that the noisy state updates form a Gaussian tube around one-step predictions whose center is driven toward the goal by the dense shaping term.

## Results

On long-horizon Push-T planning (H=40–80), GRASP achieves 59.0% success at H=40 (8.5s) and 10.4% at H=80 (58.9s), outperforming CEM (2.8% at H=80, 132.2s), GD (6.4% at H=80, 161.3s), and LatCo (0% at H≥70) in both success rate and wall-clock time. On short-horizon tasks (H=10–30), GRASP matches CEM and GD in success rate while maintaining consistently low completion times (e.g., 1.5s at H=10 Push-T vs. 25.3s for GD at H=30). On additional world models (le-wm, jepa-wm), GRASP achieves 70% on Reacher (H=75) vs. 42% for GD and 38% for CEM, and 19% on Metaworld (H=30) vs. 12% for CEM and 6% for GD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GRASP decisively outperforms all three baselines on long-horizon Push-T planning: at H=80 it achieves 10.4% success in 58.9s versus CEM's 2.8% in 132.2s, GD's 6.4% in 161.3s, and LatCo's 0% (complete failure). The advantage grows with horizon length, as GRASP's stochastic state exploration finds non-greedy trajectories that greedy rollout-based methods miss. On short-horizon tasks (H=10–30), GRASP matches CEM and GD in success rate (e.g., 100% at H=10 Push-T) while being among the fastest methods (1.5s vs. 25.3s for GD at H=30). On the le-wm and jepa-wm world models, GRASP substantially outperforms both CEM and GD (e.g., 70% vs. 42%/38% on Reacher H=75; 19% vs. 12%/6% on Metaworld H=30), demonstrating generality across different world model architectures. LatCo, the closest methodological baseline (lifted-state optimization without stochasticity or gradient detaching), degrades most severely at longer horizons, failing entirely at H≥70 on Push-T.

## Relation to Prior Work

GRASP adapts the lifted-states / multiple-shooting / collocation framework from classical optimal control (Tamimi & Li, 2009; Von Stryk, 1993) and the latent-space collocation idea from LatCo (Rybkin et al., 2021) to the setting of learned visual world models, where prior collocation methods were not designed to operate. The key departure is a stop-gradient on state inputs to the world model, motivated by the adversarial sensitivity of high-dimensional learned state spaces (analogous to adversarial examples in image classifiers), combined with Langevin-style noise on states rather than actions for exploration. This positions GRASP between zero-order shooting methods (CEM, MPPI) and vanilla backprop-through-time gradient descent, inheriting the parallelism of collocation while avoiding the ill-conditioning of deep sequential rollouts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Long-horizon planning with visual world models is a central bottleneck for embodied AI and robot manipulation, and GRASP demonstrates that a practical, parallelizable gradient-based planner can outperform both CEM and vanilla GD in success rate and wall-clock time at horizons where greedy rollouts fail. The ablation showing that removing the periodic full-rollout sync step collapses accuracy from 59% to 1.6% underscores that the stochastic lifted optimization alone is insufficient without occasional strict-descent refinement. Open questions include whether smoother or adversarially-regularized state representations could eliminate the need for stop-gradient entirely, whether the method extends to hybrid planners combining CEM and gradient steps, and how it scales to higher-dimensional action spaces and more complex multi-task settings.
