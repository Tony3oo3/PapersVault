## Problem

Many practical RL algorithms employ evolving reward functions through reward shaping, adaptive entropy/KL regularization, or curriculum learning, yet no finite-time convergence guarantees existed for actor-critic methods under such non-stationary rewards. The fundamental question addressed is: how fast can the reward parameters change while still ensuring the algorithm converges at the canonical O(1/√T) rate?

## Input / Output

The analysis considers a single-sample, single-timescale actor-critic algorithm with linear function approximation for the critic under Markovian sampling, where the reward parameter φ (encompassing base reward and regularization factor) is updated by an arbitrary oracle at each step. The output is a pair of non-asymptotic bounds on the time-averaged actor error (squared policy gradient norm) and critic error (distance to the time-varying optimal critic), expressed as functions of T and the cumulative reward variation F_T = (1/(T/2))Σ‖φ_{t+1}−φ_t‖². A key corollary shows that gradient-based reward updates with step size O(1/√t) and bounded gradient automatically satisfy F_T = O(1/T), preserving the O(1/√T) rate.

## Method

The proof proceeds in three coupled steps: (1) bounding the actor error via a Taylor expansion of the time-varying objective J_{φ_t}(θ_t), decomposing the gradient estimator bias into approximation error, critic error, and Markovian distribution mismatch; (2) bounding the critic error by tracking the moving target ω_t* = ω*(φ_t, θ_t), which requires establishing Lipschitz continuity of ω* with respect to both θ and φ; (3) solving the resulting system of two coupled inequalities via an algebraic decoupling argument. Two key technical innovations enable the analysis: exploiting the Lipschitz continuity of J_φ(θ) and ω*(φ,θ) in φ to absorb reward-variation terms, and a novel distribution mismatch bound under Markovian sampling that directly uses contraction properties of the induced operator rather than mixing-time arguments, improving the static-reward rate by a factor of log²T over prior work.

## Results

The main theorem (Theorem 4.11) establishes that both the actor error G_T and critic error W_T are bounded by O(1/√T) + O(F_T√T) + O(√(F_T/T)) + O(ε), where F_T is the average squared reward parameter change and ε is the linear approximation error. In the static-reward case (F_T = 0), this recovers the canonical O(1/√T) rate, matching the best-known single-timescale actor-critic bounds under i.i.d. sampling. Corollary 4.12 further shows that when the reward follows a gradient-based update with bounded gradient and step size η_t^φ = c_φ/√t, the condition F_T = O(1/T) is automatically satisfied, yielding the clean O(1/√T) + O(ε) rate. As a secondary contribution, the novel Markovian distribution mismatch analysis (Proposition 4.13) eliminates a log²T factor present in prior static-reward analyses (Tian et al., 2023; Chen & Zhao, 2023, 2025).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or experimental comparisons. The comparisons are against prior theoretical convergence guarantees for single-timescale actor-critic. Under i.i.d. sampling, the O(1/√T) rate matches Chen et al. (2021) and Olshevsky & Gharesifard (2023). Under Markovian sampling, the paper improves upon Tian et al. (2023), Chen & Zhao (2023), and Chen & Zhao (2025), which all carry an O(log²T/√T) rate, by achieving a tighter O(1/√T) bound through a novel contraction-based analysis of the distribution mismatch that avoids relying on mixing time. Compared to Kumar et al. (2026), which achieves Õ(ε^{-3}) sample complexity for global convergence, the present work is limited to local convergence and linear critics but handles the more general evolving-reward setting and continuous action spaces. The paper does not claim superiority over adversarial RL or performative RL frameworks, noting those address fundamentally different problem formulations (online regret minimization vs. offline training convergence).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the finite-time convergence analysis of single-timescale actor-critic methods (Chen et al. 2021; Olshevsky & Gharesifard 2023; Chen & Zhao 2023, 2025; Tian et al. 2023) from the static-reward setting to one where the reward parameter evolves at every iteration. The core analytical machinery—Lipschitz continuity of the policy objective and optimal critic with respect to the reward parameter, combined with a novel distribution-mismatch bound under Markovian sampling—builds directly on the linear-critic, single-sample framework of these prior works. The Markovian sampling analysis independently improves upon Chen & Zhao (2025) by eliminating a log²T factor, and the evolving-reward treatment is orthogonal to the adversarial/non-stationary RL and performative RL literatures, which target regret minimization or deployment-retraining loops rather than training-time reward evolution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first finite-time guarantee for a setting that underpins many widely used RL techniques (curiosity-driven reward shaping, random network distillation, automated entropy adjustment in SAC, curriculum learning), which previously lacked any convergence theory. The practical takeaway—that gradient-based reward updates at the same timescale as the actor and critic preserve the canonical O(1/√T) rate—gives practitioners a concrete, checkable condition for designing stable evolving-reward schemes. Key limitations include the restriction to linear function approximation for the critic, a single-timescale update, and the requirement that reward changes be bounded in a specific averaged sense; extending to neural critics, multi-timescale methods, or adversarial reward perturbations remains open.
