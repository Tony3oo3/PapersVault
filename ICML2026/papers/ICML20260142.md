## Problem

Standard model stitching evaluates functional similarity by probing only forward compatibility—whether one model's representations can be aligned to another's to maintain task performance. This one-directional probe is inherently blind to the invariance properties of the stitched models, causing models that rely on fundamentally different information cues to appear misleadingly similar, and allowing the stitching layer to exploit previously unseen predictive shortcuts. The paper identifies this as a systematic failure of forward compatibility as a proxy for functional similarity.

## Input / Output

The method takes two independently trained image classification networks (front and end models, e.g., ResNet-18, VGG-16, ViT-Tiny/Base) along with labeled training and test image sets. A key preprocessing step generates identically represented inputs (IRIs) by optimizing each training image to be nearly indistinguishable from the original at the front model's stitch level, producing a modified training set used for alignment. The output is a stitching plot reporting per-depth agreement probability (a sample-wise cosine-like measure between full class probability distributions, rather than top-1 accuracy) between the stitched composition and the standalone end model, alongside the learned affine stitching transformation.

## Method

The paper introduces invariance-aware model stitching, which operationalizes a forward-backward compatibility requirement: for inputs the front model maps identically at the stitch level, the standalone end model and the end model within the stitched composition must process the representation similarly. This is achieved by training the stitching alignment on IRIs (inputs optimized to be indistinguishable at the stitch level), so that any deviation from the standard forward-compatible optimum signals a backward-compatibility violation. The paper also proposes Functional Latent Alignment (FuLA), a new stitching objective inspired by feature distillation that supervises the stitched composition to mimic the end model's internal activations at multiple depths between the stitch level and the penultimate layer, bridging the gap between output-level (SLM/HLM) and stitch-level (DM) matching. Among invariance-enabling formulations (those incorporating supervision from the standalone end model), FuLA and SLM achieve comparable similarity while DM consistently reports lower values, revealing that misalignment often emerges already at the latent level immediately following the stitch.

## Results

The paper's primary metric is agreement probability (sample-wise cosine similarity of class probability distributions) plotted against stitching depth. Under standard model stitching, all formulations (HLM, SLM, FuLA, DM) report high functional similarity (~90%+ agreement) even in the cross-bias configuration where the front model relies on a pixel shortcut, confirming the failure mode identified by Smith et al. (2025). Under invariance-aware stitching, the cross-bias agreement probability rapidly drops to random chance (~10% for 10 classes), while identity and cross-seed configurations retain high similarity, yielding a progressively sharper decay as semantic divergence increases. For robust vs. non-robust models, invariance-aware stitching reveals that robust-to-robust configurations achieve consistently higher agreement than any other pairing, a distinction invisible under standard stitching. Among invariance-enabling formulations, FuLA and SLM achieve comparable functional similarity, while DM consistently yields the lowest, indicating misalignment already at the latent level.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against HLM (Bansal et al., 2021), invariance-aware stitching demonstrates that the high cross-bias similarity reported under standard HLM is an artifact of forward-only compatibility; under the invariance-aware setting, HLM behaves similarly to standard stitching (since it is not invariance-enabling), confirming the IRIs construction preserves semantic structure. Against SLM and DM (Csiszárik et al., 2021), the paper shows the same shortcut-exploitation failure generalizes beyond HLM to all forward compatibility formulations, but under invariance-aware stitching, FuLA (the new latent-level objective) and SLM achieve comparable agreement while DM is consistently lower, suggesting DM's misalignment originates at the representation level immediately after the stitch. Against Balogh & Jelasity (2023), who reported robust and non-robust networks as functionally similar under HLM stitching, invariance-aware stitching reveals clear functional incompatibilities between robust and non-robust models, with robust-to-robust pairs showing the highest agreement — contradicting the prior conclusion. Against CKA (Kornblith et al., 2019), the invariance-aware variant of CKA can deviate significantly from invariance-aware model stitching, indicating that functional similarity via stitching captures information not accessible to representational geometry metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the model stitching paradigm for functional similarity evaluation (Bansal et al., 2021; Csiszárik et al., 2021) by introducing a forward-backward compatibility requirement that standard stitching lacks. The backward compatibility mechanism is operationalized through identically represented inputs (IRIs) from Nanda et al. (2022), while the proposed Functional Latent Alignment (FuLA) objective draws on the Hints mechanism from feature distillation (Romero et al., 2015) to provide intermediate-depth supervision between the extremes of direct matching and output-level label matching. The work is directly motivated by Smith et al. (2025), who showed that standard stitching reports misleadingly high similarity between models relying on different information cues, and generalizes this critique from HLM to all forward compatibility formulations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that robust and non-robust networks are not as functionally similar as previously reported (Balogh & Jelasity, 2023) is a meaningful correction to the literature, with implications for understanding what adversarial training actually changes in learned representations. The invariance-aware stitching framework provides a more principled tool for model similarity evaluation, but the absence of an oracle for functional similarity means definitive validation remains out of reach. Open questions include which invariance-enabling formulation is optimal (the paper identifies a trade-off between alignment quality and shortcut exploitation that depends on architecture), and whether the forward-backward compatibility framework extends beyond image classification to other modalities and tasks.
