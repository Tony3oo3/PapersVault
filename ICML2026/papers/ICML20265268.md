## Problem

Synthesizing phonocardiograms (PCG) from electrocardiograms (ECG) requires simultaneously capturing precise electromechanical timing and rich acoustic texture, but existing methods face a persistent trade-off: regression-based models (e.g., Transformers) preserve temporal alignment yet produce oversmoothed waveforms, while standalone diffusion models generate realistic audio but struggle with strict physiological conditioning and precise event timing. No prior work has systematically resolved this tension for the ECG-to-PCG cross-modal translation task.

## Input / Output

The model takes a 12-second single-lead ECG segment (resampled to 1 kHz, band-pass filtered 0.5–45 Hz with notch filtering) and produces a synchronized 12-second PCG waveform (20–400 Hz band-pass). The ECG is encoded into a spatially-resolved condition feature map (not a global vector) to preserve beat-level temporal structure, while the PCG target is represented as a structured latent map z₀ ∈ ℝ^{128×750}. Normalization statistics are frozen from the source training set and applied unchanged during zero-shot evaluation, and shorter recordings are right-padded to the fixed 12,000-sample window.

## Method

The architecture decouples representation learning from generative synthesis: a VAE encoder maps PCG to a structured latent map z₀ (with a separate 32-D posterior used only for mild KL regularization at β=10⁻⁶), and a conditional 1D U-Net denoises in this latent space guided by the ECG condition map. Two physiology-motivated modules drive temporal coherence: Enhanced Condition Fusion injects multi-scale ECG cues (3/5/7-kernel convolutions) via cross-attention at each encoder level, and Temporal Attention Blocks apply 8-head self-attention to capture long-range electromechanical delays (e.g., R-peak to S1/S2). Training follows a three-phase curriculum—VAE pre-training, frozen-VAE latent diffusion training, then joint fine-tuning that co-adapts the VAE manifold, diffusion prior, and a weight-shared decoder with dual input projections (FC for the 32-D vector during VAE training; 1×1 Conv for z₀ during generation). Conditioning is injected exclusively in the encoder path to preserve generative freedom in the decoder, and the composite objective L_VAE + λL_diff (λ=1.0) is optimized stage-wise rather than as a single closed-form ELBO.

## Results

On the EPHNOGRAM in-distribution test set, the Temporally-Aware VAE-Diffusion model achieves a Pearson correlation of 0.810 ± 0.008, SNR of 4.2 ± 0.2 dB, RMSE of 0.616 ± 0.013, S1 detection accuracy of 95.95 ± 0.5%, and S1 timing error of 12.0 ± 0.5 ms, outperforming all 11 baselines across every metric. On the zero-shot PhysioNet/CinC 2016 MITHSDB target subset (no target-domain training), the model retains a correlation of 0.750 ± 0.01 and S1 detection of 91.3 ± 0.4%, again leading all baselines. Distributional metrics (FD 0.167, MMD 2.18×10⁻³) confirm improvements beyond pointwise waveform fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed model outperforms all 11 baselines on every reported metric in both in-distribution and zero-shot settings. The closest competitor is the DiT-style Transformer-diffusion control (Corr 0.792, S1 Err 12.9 ms), which the proposed method surpasses by 0.018 in correlation and 0.9 ms in S1 timing error, with a 9.3% relative reduction in FD (0.167 vs. 0.183). Among regression baselines, TFT (Corr 0.735, S1 Err 15.8 ms) and Transformer (Corr 0.712, S1 Err 16.5 ms) achieve reasonable temporal alignment but produce oversmoothed waveforms, reflected in substantially higher FD (0.331, 0.358) and MMD (4.88, 5.12) than the proposed model. Waveform diffusion baselines (DiffWave, RDDM) generate richer acoustic texture but sacrifice physiological coupling, yielding correlations of only 0.615–0.653 and S1 errors of 18.3–19.5 ms. The AudioLDM-style generic latent-diffusion adaptation (Corr 0.740, S1 Err 15.1 ms) confirms that a task-agnostic two-stage recipe is insufficient without the proposed Enhanced Condition Fusion and joint fine-tuning. In zero-shot transfer, the gap widens: the proposed model leads DiT-style by 0.029 in correlation and 1.6 ms in S1 error, while foundational models (cGAN, cVAE) degrade severely (Corr ≤ 0.251, S1 Det ≤ 61.3%). Recent conditional time-series generators (ImagenTime, SDformer, KoVAE) improve over older baselines but still trail the proposed method by 0.03–0.06 in correlation and 1.2–3.8 ms in S1 error under matched budgets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a task-specific adaptation of latent diffusion (in the AudioLDM lineage) to paired ECG-to-PCG synthesis, rather than a new generic diffusion family. It departs from two prior paradigms: deterministic regressors (Transformer, TFT) that preserve temporal alignment but oversmooth acoustic texture, and waveform-space diffusion (DiffWave, RDDM) that generates realistic audio but lacks precise physiological conditioning. The architectural novelty lies in combining Enhanced Condition Fusion (multi-scale cross-attention for ECG injection) with Temporal Attention Blocks inside a U-Net denoiser, plus a three-phase curriculum that jointly fine-tunes the VAE manifold and diffusion prior through a shared decoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: ECG is ubiquitous while PCG requires specialized stethoscope hardware, so reliable ECG-to-PCG synthesis could enable dual-modality cardiac assessment in low-resource settings and support data augmentation for multimodal cardiac algorithms. However, the paper is explicitly scoped as a synthesis and timing-preservation result, not a diagnostic validation; downstream tasks like murmur classification on generated PCGs, pathology-specific evaluation, and demographic/device-shift analysis remain open. The zero-shot transfer to PhysioNet/CinC 2016 is a useful robustness signal but involves only one target dataset and a coarse normal/pathological split, leaving broader external validation as a clear next step.
