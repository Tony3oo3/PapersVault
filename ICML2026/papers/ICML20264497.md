## Problem

Existing active 3D reconstruction methods suffer from two coupled limitations: they require re-optimizing the full 3D representation (e.g., 3DGS or NeRF) at every selection step, causing redundant computation, and they initialize geometry via global SfM over the entire candidate image pool, leaking geometric information from views that should remain unseen. This violates the active selection protocol and biases evaluation of selection strategies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264497/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method receives a pool of candidate camera poses (known a priori) and a small seed set of 3 initial image-pose pairs; images for unselected poses are only revealed after their pose is chosen, so selection must rely solely on previously acquired images and pose geometry. It consumes 2D feature correspondences (from LightGlue or MASt3R) between all pairs of selected views to build a 3D scaffold via DLT triangulation. The outputs are an ordered sequence of selected views, an incremental 3D point cloud scaffold with a per-point confidence field, and a final 3DGS (or 2DGS) reconstruction optimized in only 5,000 iterations thanks to the reliable scaffold initialization.

## Method

Creat3r decouples view selection entirely from 3DGS optimization by maintaining a lightweight geometric proxy. First, it incrementally triangulates 3D points from pairwise 2D correspondences (via DLT) between all selected views, building a scaffold that expands each round without any global SfM. Second, it computes a 3D confidence field by reaggregating per-point photometric consistency (exponential of mean color deviation across visible cameras) weighted by the fraction of cameras observing the point. Third, it projects the scaffold and confidence field to each candidate pose using a simplified isotropic Gaussian splatting renderer (fixed opacity 0.8, radius tuned to ~1 pixel footprint), producing a 2D confidence map and an inverted exploration map per candidate. The next-best-view is selected by maximizing an exploration measure: the fraction of pixels with non-zero exploration signal minus τ times the mean confidence over observed pixels (τ=1), balancing discovery of unobserved regions against refinement of uncertain but already-seen areas.

## Results

On Mip-NeRF 360 novel view synthesis with 20 selected views, Creat3r-MASt3R achieves 20.68 PSNR, 0.601 SSIM, and 0.397 LPIPS, outperforming the best baseline (Lyu et al.) by 4.4 dB in PSNR and 0.098 in SSIM. With only 10 views, Creat3r-MASt3R still leads with 17.81 PSNR vs. 14.28 for the next-best method. On the more challenging surface reconstruction task (Tanks&Temples), Creat3r achieves an F1-score of 18.05%, nearly doubling the best baseline (FisherRF at 8.61%) and substantially outperforming Kopanas & Drettakis (0.84%) and Lyu et al. (5.61%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Creat3r outperforms all four baselines (FisherRF, Kopanas & Drettakis, Lyu et al., and FPS) on every metric in both novel view synthesis and surface reconstruction. In the 20-view Mip-NeRF 360 setting, Creat3r-MASt3R leads the best baseline (Lyu et al.†) by 4.41 dB PSNR (20.68 vs. 16.26) and 0.098 SSIM, while the gap over FisherRF† is 4.65 dB and over Kopanas & Drettakis† is 4.95 dB. The advantage is even more pronounced in the 10-view regime, where Creat3r gains 3.53 dB over Lyu et al. and 4.13 dB over Kopanas & Drettakis. On surface reconstruction (Tanks&Temples), Creat3r's F1 of 18.05% is more than double FisherRF (8.61%), over 3× Lyu et al. (5.61%), and over 20× Kopanas & Drettakis (0.84%), whose extremely low recall (0.58%) severely limits its F1. Notably, Creat3r with only 20 selected views matches or exceeds original 3DGS trained on 200–400 views on the Truck scene (F1 0.19 vs. 0.19) and surpasses it on Caterpillar (0.10 vs. 0.08) and Ignatius (0.25 vs. 0.04), demonstrating substantial data efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

Creat3r builds on 3D Gaussian Splatting as the downstream reconstruction backbone but deliberately departs from the dominant paradigm in active view selection, where methods like FisherRF, Lyu et al., and Kopanas & Drettakis tightly couple each selection step to iterative re-optimization of the 3D representation. Instead, it repurposes learned 2D correspondence networks (LightGlue, MASt3R) and classical DLT triangulation to maintain a lightweight geometric scaffold, from which a 3D confidence field and per-view exploration maps are projected to score candidates. It also identifies and corrects a protocol flaw in prior benchmarks—global SfM initialization over the full candidate pool introduces information leakage that biases evaluation—by restricting geometry estimation to only the currently selected views.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By decoupling view selection from 3DGS optimization, Creat3r cuts per-selection cost roughly in half (≈10 s vs. 24–39 s) while achieving substantially better reconstruction quality, making active 3D reconstruction more practical for real-world deployment. The framework's model-agnosticism (demonstrated with 2DGS and Depth Anything 3) suggests it could serve as a general active-perception front-end for diverse reconstruction pipelines. Key open questions include handling outward-facing or sparsely overlapping trajectories where the scaffold provides little geometric constraint, and extending the fixed-pose-pool protocol to continuous online pose generation for embodied agents.
