## Problem

Multimodal LLMs struggle to systematically model the temporal evolution of visual scenes, failing to capture multi-level dynamic constituents (high-level actions, low-level visual transformations) and how they drive scene progression. Prior interleaved vision-text pre-training either relies on weakly-aligned image-text sequences or covers only a single type of visual dynamics (e.g., robot actions, game interactions), leaving models with unreliable and uncontrollable visual sequence generation.

## Input / Output

The model consumes interleaved state-transition sequences where states are video keyframes (extracted at ~1-second intervals with Laplacian-variance sharpness filtering and CLIP-similarity thresholding at 0.925, yielding 0.8–167.1 s gaps) and transitions are hierarchical JSON-schema texts describing activities, seven transformation types, and three contribution analyses. For downstream tasks, it takes a narrative image plus text description (VinaBench) or an egocentric image plus action prompt (LEGO), and produces simulated state images via a FLUX VAE decoder along with structured transition predictions. Training sequences are split into sliding windows of 6 states (stride 3) to fit GPU memory.

## Method

DynaVieW adopts a Mixture-of-Transformer-Experts (MoT) architecture initialized from BAGEL-7B-MoT, with a Qwen2.5-7B understanding expert (fed by SigLIP2 ViT encodings) for transition prediction and a Qwen2.5-7B generation expert (fed by FLUX VAE encodings with rectified-flow noise) for state simulation, both sharing a novel multimodal selective attention mechanism. This attention masks each transition's access to prior transitions (retaining only preceding states' ViT encodings) and each state's access to earlier transitions (retaining only the immediately preceding transition), with an additional 0.3-probability dropout over earlier state contexts to prevent overfitting. A schema-token re-weighted cross-entropy loss assigns weight 0.1 to static JSON-structure tokens and 1.0 to variable slot-filling tokens, preventing overfitting to the schema format while under-learning dynamic content. The model is trained with equal-weighted CE (transition) and MSE (diffusion state simulation) losses on ~48K state-transition sub-sequences from Ego4D, AgiBotWorld-Alpha, and ShareGPT4Video, with transitions annotated by InternVL-78B-Instruct under constrained decoding to enforce valid JSON output.

## Results

On VinaBench (visual narrative generation), DynaVieW achieves an Average score of 0.630 in zero-shot generalization, outperforming BAGEL (0.567), Story2Board (0.566), and Emu2 (0.425), with particularly strong cross-scene location consistency (0.835 vs. 0.675 for BAGEL). Under supervised fine-tuning, DynaVieW reaches 0.610 Average, surpassing BAGEL (0.547), MM-Interleaved (0.528), ARLDM (0.506), and StoryGen (0.389). On the LEGO world simulation benchmark, DynaVieW (SFT) achieves FID 20.96 and CLIP 80.99 on Ego4D, and FID 10.31 and CLIP 84.19 on Epic-Kitchens, consistently outperforming BAGEL (SFT) across all six metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DynaVieW outperforms all six baselines across both evaluation settings. In zero-shot generalization on VinaBench, DynaVieW (0.630) beats BAGEL (0.567) by 0.063, Story2Board (0.566) by 0.064, and Emu2 (0.425) by 0.205, with the largest gains in cross-scene character consistency (0.550 vs. 0.370 for BAGEL) and location consistency (0.835 vs. 0.675). Under SFT, DynaVieW (0.610) surpasses BAGEL (0.547) by 0.063, MM-Interleaved (0.528) by 0.082, ARLDM (0.506) by 0.104, and StoryGen (0.389) by 0.221, showing balanced improvements in both per-scene fidelity and long-range consistency. On the LEGO world simulation benchmark, DynaVieW (SFT) consistently beats BAGEL (SFT) on all six metrics for both Ego4D (e.g., FID 20.96 vs. 22.02, CLIP 80.99 vs. 79.37) and Epic-Kitchens (e.g., FID 10.31 vs. 11.66, CLIP 84.19 vs. 82.71). In the controllability experiment (Table 3), DynaVieW with JSON-schema prompts achieves 0.604 Average versus only 0.283 for BAGEL with the same schema, demonstrating that the hierarchical dynamic modeling itself—not merely the input format—drives the performance gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

DynaVieW builds directly on BAGEL's Mixture-of-Transformer-Experts (MoT) architecture, initializing from its 7B checkpoint and continuing pre-training on a new state-transition dataset. It departs from prior interleaved vision-text LLMs (Flamingo, MM-Interleaved, Emu2, Anole) that either used weakly-aligned web image-text pairs or focused on a single type of visual dynamics (game interactions, robot actions, movie plots), by introducing a hierarchical JSON schema that systematically captures multi-level visual dynamics from high-level activities down to seven types of low-level transformations. Two novel training components — multimodal selective attention to prevent naive copying of historical transitions, and a schema-token re-weighted cross-entropy loss to balance structural format learning against dynamic slot-filling — are introduced on top of the inherited MoT framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and well-motivated gap: multimodal LLMs produce unreliable and uncontrollable visual sequences because they lack structured understanding of how scenes evolve over time. The hierarchical schema provides a reusable conceptual framework for verbalizing visual dynamics that could inform future world-model training data construction. Key limitations include reliance on a 78B oracle VLM (InternVL) for annotation (introducing potential bias and cost), evaluation primarily on egocentric and narrative domains, and unclear scaling behavior beyond 7B parameters. Open questions include whether the schema granularity is optimal, whether the annotation pipeline generalizes to domains with very different dynamics (e.g., abstract art, scientific visualization), and whether the selective attention mechanism remains beneficial at much longer sequence lengths.
