## Problem

The AI hardware industry is increasingly adopting low-precision floating-point formats (e.g., MXFP8, NVFP4 in NVIDIA Blackwell) to handle activation outliers in LLMs, yet no unified, systematic comparison of FP versus integer (INT) quantization exists across varying block granularities. This leaves algorithm-hardware co-design without clear guidance on whether the FP-centric trajectory is actually optimal, especially at the fine-grained (block-wise) levels that are now standard practice.

## Input / Output

The method takes pretrained BFloat16 LLMs (weights, activations, gradients) and quantizes all six GEMM operations in a linear layer's forward and backward passes to low-bit block-wise formats with matched block sizes (32 for MX, 16 for NV) and identical scale-factor structures (UE8M0 or E4M3). For training experiments, it trains Llama-3-style 1B/3B models from scratch on OLMo2-Mix with 100B/200B tokens. Outputs include per-tensor QSNR, KL divergence on WikiText2 (top-25 logits), perplexity, six-task accuracy averages, and gate-level hardware energy/area costs normalized to a reference format.

## Method

The paper introduces integer variants (MXINT8, MXINT6, MXINT4, NVINT4) that mirror their FP counterparts with identical block sizes and scale structures, enabling direct comparison. The core theoretical contribution derives closed-form QSNR expressions for both INT (Theorem 1) and FP (Theorem 2) quantization as functions of crest factor κ, bit width, block size, and scale format, revealing a critical crossover point: INT outperforms FP when κ falls below a format-specific threshold (e.g., κ < 7.55 for 8-bit, κ < 2.04 for 4-bit). A symmetric clipping method (Q_min = −(2^{b−1}−1)) resolves a persistent negative gradient bias in fine-grained INT training, enabling nearly lossless MXINT8 training. Random Hadamard rotation is applied to reduce crest factor, which shifts the balance in favor of INT at 4-bit (NVINT4 surpasses NVFP4 post-rotation). A gate-level hardware model further shows MXINT8 and NVINT4 reduce energy by 37% and 38% respectively versus their FP counterparts at matched throughput.

## Results

In direct-cast inference across 12 LLMs (0.6B–235B), MXINT8 outperforms MXFP8 on all 12 models (KL divergence, e.g., 191 vs. 579 ×10⁻⁶ on Qwen3-0.6B), while MXINT6 and MXINT4 lose to their FP counterparts. With random Hadamard rotation, NVINT4 reverses its deficit and beats NVFP4 on all 12 models (e.g., 7771 vs. 12031 ×10⁻⁶ on Qwen3-0.6B). For low-bit training, MXINT8 achieves 57.02% average accuracy on six reasoning tasks (1B model, 100B tokens), matching BF16 (56.89%) and slightly exceeding MXFP8 (56.86%). At matched throughput, MXINT8 reduces energy by 37% and area by 21% relative to MXFP8, and the mixed MXINT8+NVINT4 configuration cuts area by 34% versus MXFP8+NVFP4.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the MXFP8 baseline (Rouhani et al., 2023), the proposed MXINT8 format wins on all 12 evaluated models in direct-cast inference, with KL divergence roughly 2–3× lower (e.g., 96 vs. 300 ×10⁻⁶ on Qwen3-14B), and also achieves a lower training loss (~0.001 gap) and marginally higher task accuracy in low-bit training. Against the NVFP4 baseline (NVIDIA, 2024), the proposed NVINT4 format loses on all 12 models without rotation (e.g., 10560 vs. 8104 ×10⁻⁶ on Qwen3-0.6B) but wins on all 12 when combined with random Hadamard rotation (7771 vs. 12031 ×10⁻⁶), a reversal driven by the reduced crest factor (Q3 drops from 2.39 to 2.11, crossing below the theoretical crossover point of 2.39). For 6-bit and 4-bit MX formats, the FP variants (MXFP6, MXFP4) retain a clear advantage even after rotation, consistent with the theoretical QSNR crossover points (κ < 1.96 for 6-bit, κ < 2.04 for 4-bit) being below the observed block-32 crest factor of 2.96. On hardware efficiency, MXINT8 and NVINT4 reduce energy by 37% and 66% respectively relative to their FP counterparts at the same throughput, with the mixed INT configuration further saving 34% area over the mixed FP configuration due to simpler circuit reuse.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Microscaling (MX) format introduced by Rouhani et al. (2023) and NVIDIA's NVFP4 format by introducing direct integer counterparts (MXINT8, MXINT6, MXINT4, NVINT4) that share the same block structure and scale-factor hierarchy, enabling a controlled comparison. Prior work on INT vs. FP comparison (SageAttention, Kuzmin et al., Van Baalen et al.) was limited to coarse-grained (per-channel) settings or specific attention sub-operations, and most quantization studies focus on a single format family. This paper departs from that fragmented landscape by providing a unified theoretical QSNR framework (Theorems 1–2) parameterized by crest factor, bit width, and block size, and by systematically evaluating all format pairs across 12 LLMs at fine-grained block sizes of 16 and 32.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that MXINT8 consistently outperforms MXFP8 across all 12 evaluated models directly challenges the industry trajectory toward FP-centric hardware (e.g., NVIDIA Blackwell), suggesting that a one-size-fits-all FP approach is suboptimal for fine-grained quantization. The symmetric clipping method that resolves gradient bias in INT8 training is a practical contribution enabling nearly lossless low-bit training with integer formats. Open questions include whether the i.i.d. Gaussian assumption holds for heavy-tailed LLM activations at extreme low bit-widths, whether adaptive clipping methods (e.g., Four-Over-Six) alter the crossover points, and whether hardware vendors will actually pivot to INT pipelines given the sunk cost of FP silicon.
