## Problem

The widely-used Unlearning Accuracy (UA) metric suffers from a 'fake forgetting' blind spot: even when a model misclassifies forget data, over 50% of those misclassified instances still have their ground truth labels recoverable within the conformal prediction set, meaning the model retains significant information about supposedly forgotten data. This paper identifies this limitation and asks whether a more reliable evaluation metric and a corresponding unlearning framework can be built on conformal prediction to ensure the true label is genuinely excluded from the model's predictive uncertainty.

## Input / Output

The framework operates on standard RGB images (CIFAR-10 at 32×32, Tiny ImageNet at 64×64, Oxford-Pets) and produces two outputs: a class label prediction and a conformal prediction set (a subset of class labels with a theoretically guaranteed 1−α coverage probability). The CR metric is computed as the ratio of Coverage (fraction of forget/test points whose true label falls in the prediction set) to Set Size (average number of labels in the prediction set), evaluated separately on forget data D_f and test data D_test. A held-out calibration set of 2000 (CIFAR-10) or 10000 (Tiny ImageNet) unseen samples is required to compute the conformal threshold q̂.

## Method

The paper contributes two tightly coupled components. First, the Conformal Ratio (CR) metric, defined as Coverage divided by Set Size over the conformal prediction set, captures both whether the true label is retained (fake forgetting) and how much spurious information the prediction set carries. Second, the CPU unlearning framework adapts the Carlini–Wagner adversarial loss by replacing the probability of the true class and the max competing probability with the conformal threshold q̄ and the non-conformity score S(x, y_t) of the true label, yielding L_cpu = max{q̄ − S(x, y_t), −Δ}. This loss directly pushes the true label's non-conformity score beyond the conformal threshold so the label is excluded from the prediction set, rather than merely reducing its softmax probability. The final training objective combines the original unlearning loss with a weighted CPU term (L_total = L_original + λ·L_cpu), where λ controls forgetting strength and q̄ is updated each epoch from a separate calibration subset D_c′, introducing negligible computational overhead.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18 (10% random forgetting), the RT baseline achieves UA=8.6%, RA=99.7%, TA=91.8%, and CR_Df=0.864. The CPU framework applied to FT (CPU-FT, λ=0.5) reduces the UA gap to RT from 4.8% to 0.7% on CIFAR-10 and from 7.8% to 0.9% on Tiny ImageNet, while CR_Df drops by an average of 0.08 across methods with only a 0.03 average drop in CR_Dtest. Training overhead is negligible: FT goes from 6.3 to 6.8 minutes on CIFAR-10. The paper further shows that over 50% of UA-misclassified forget data points still have their true label within the conformal prediction set, exposing the 'fake forgetting' problem.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates 12 unlearning methods (RT, FT, RL, GA, Teacher, SSD, NegGrad+, SCRUB, LoTUS, MUNBa, Salun, SFRon) under both the standard UA metric and the proposed CR metric. A key finding is that rankings shift substantially: under UA, NegGrad+ and RL rank near the top on CIFAR-10, but under CR, Salun and NegGrad+ lead while RL drops, revealing that RL suffers from fake forgetting (45.5% of its misclassified points remain in the conformal set). The CPU framework most dramatically improves FT: CPU-FT (λ=0.5) closes the UA gap to RT from 4.8% to 0.7% on CIFAR-10 and from 7.8% to 0.9% on Tiny ImageNet, while CR_Df drops from 0.986 to 0.853 (CIFAR-10) and from 0.466 to 0.413 (Tiny ImageNet). CPU-RL shows limited additional benefit because RL already over-forgets (UA=26.9% on Tiny ImageNet), confirming that CPU is most valuable for methods prone to under-forgetting. On Oxford-Pets with Swin-T, CPU-FT reduces the UA gap from 3.5% to 0.8% and CR_Df from 0.950 to 0.902, demonstrating generalizability across architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of conformal prediction (Angelopoulos & Bates, 2021) and adversarial attack losses (Carlini & Wagner, 2017), repurposing both for machine unlearning. It departs from prior uncertainty-in-unlearning work (Becker & Liebig, 2022) by shifting from parameter-level sensitivity (Fisher Information Matrix) to output-level prediction set coverage, and from concurrent work (Spartalis et al., 2025) by using conformal prediction as a principled evaluation metric rather than a training heuristic. The CPU loss is a direct adaptation of the C&W margin loss where the conformal threshold replaces the max-probability term, creating a bridge between adversarial robustness and unlearning objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that over 50% of UA-misclassified forget data remains recoverable via conformal prediction sets challenges the field's reliance on unlearning accuracy as a primary metric, suggesting that many published unlearning results may overstate forgetting quality. The CR metric and CPU framework are simple, model-agnostic additions that practitioners can adopt with minimal overhead (~3 minutes extra training). Open questions include whether the fake forgetting phenomenon extends to generative models, NLP, and multi-modal settings, and whether the conformal prediction calibration requirement (held-out data) limits applicability in privacy-sensitive deployment scenarios where no additional data is available.
