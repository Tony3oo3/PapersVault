## Problem

Existing VQA methods rely on classification-based pretraining (e.g., ImageNet, Kinetics-400) that captures only semantic knowledge while video quality depends on multiple dimensions (distortion, motion, aesthetics), and this pretraining incurs enormous computational cost (10–200× more than fine-tuning). Although CVLMs offer richer cross-modal representations, all prior CVLM-based VQA methods use them merely as auxiliary feature extractors alongside separate backbone networks, leaving open whether a fully CVLM-based VQA framework can be both effective and computationally lightweight.

## Input / Output

The model takes 8 sampled video frames (selected via strategies including frame-difference-based MSE ranking) and five learnable quality-level text prompts of the form 'a video of <level> quality' (excellent/good/fair/poor/bad), each prefixed with three learnable tokens. The backbone is a video-tuned CLIP variant (Perception Encoder) that processes frames independently with mean pooling for temporal aggregation. The output is a single continuous quality score computed as a learnable weighted sum of cosine similarities between the aggregated video embedding and each of the five prompt embeddings.

## Method

Q-CLIP introduces a Shared Cross-Modal Adapter (SCMA), a residual bottleneck module (project to r=128, apply a shared nonlinear core, project back with LayerNorm and learnable residual scaling) that is shared across both the visual and textual branches and across all encoder layers, yielding only 0.14M trainable parameters while the entire CVLM backbone remains frozen. Five learnable quality-level prompts provide fine-grained textual guidance, replacing coarse antonym pairs; the final score is a weighted sum of cosine similarities between the video embedding and each prompt embedding. The paper also proposes frame-difference-based sampling (using pixel-wise MSE between adjacent frames) and a mixed-sampling ensemble strategy that outperforms random or uniform sampling, particularly in cross-dataset generalization. Training uses a combined monotonicity-induced and linearity-induced loss, and the entire pipeline requires training only the SCMA parameters, reducing fine-tuned parameters by up to 58,550× compared to MLLM-based methods.

## Results

After pre-training on LSVQ and fine-tuning on five small VQA datasets, Q-CLIP achieves state-of-the-art SRCC/PLCC on all benchmarks: 0.881/0.901 (LIVE-VQC), 0.915/0.920 (KoNViD-1k), 0.911/0.911 (YouTube-UGC), 0.897/0.907 (CVD2014), and 0.846/0.884 (LIVE-Qualcomm). On the LSVQ pre-training evaluation, Q-CLIP-Mixed reaches 0.899/0.900 SRCC/PLCC on LSVQ_test and 0.896/0.901 on KoNViD-1k cross-dataset testing. The method requires only 0.14M trainable parameters (the SCMA adapter), achieving a 200× reduction in fine-tuned parameters compared to FAST-VQA and up to 58,550× compared to Q-Align.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Q-CLIP outperforms all 21 compared baselines across all five fine-tuning datasets. Against the strongest data-driven method MBVQA (CVPR'24), Q-CLIP gains +2.1% SRCC on LIVE-VQC, +1.4% on KoNViD-1k, +3.5% on YouTube-UGC, +1.4% on CVD2014, and +1.4% on LIVE-Qualcomm. Against the best CVLM-utilizing method CLIPVQA (TBC'25), Q-CLIP improves average SRCC by 1.74% and PLCC by 1.72%, with the largest gap on YouTube-UGC (+3.0% SRCC). Compared to the MLLM-based Q-Align (ICML'24), Q-CLIP achieves comparable or better accuracy while using 58,550× fewer fine-tuned parameters (0.14M vs. ~8.2B). On the LSVQ pre-training evaluation (Table 1), Q-CLIP-Mixed surpasses MBVQA by +0.4% SRCC on LSVQ_test and +1.8% on KoNViD-1k cross-dataset, and outperforms CLiF-VQA by up to +3.7% SRCC. The only marginal exception is LIVE-VQC in the pre-training setting, where CLiF-VQA and CLIPVQA slightly edge out Q-CLIP by ~0.5–1.3% SRCC, but Q-CLIP recovers the lead after fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

Q-CLIP builds on CLIP-style contrastive vision-language models (specifically the video-tuned Perception Encoder) and departs fundamentally from prior CVLMs-utilizing VQA methods (CLiF-VQA, PTM-VQA, MaxVQA, CLIPVQA) that treat CVLMs as auxiliary feature extractors bolted onto dedicated video backbones. Instead, it is the first framework to use CVLMs as the sole architecture for VQA, adapting them via a Shared Cross-Modal Adapter (SCMA) that shares bottleneck projections across both modalities and across encoder layers. The learnable five-level prompt mechanism extends the antonym-prompt line (MaxVQA, CLIPVQA) toward finer-grained quality discrimination, while the frame-difference-based sampling strategy introduces a motion-aware dimension largely unexplored in prior VQA sampling literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for a separate video backbone and requiring only 0.14M trainable parameters, Q-CLIP offers a practical, low-cost path to strong VQA that could be readily deployed in content-moderation pipelines where compute budgets are tight. The finding that zero-parameter mean pooling outperforms Transformer and Mamba temporal heads when paired with a video-tuned backbone challenges the assumption that explicit temporal modeling modules are necessary, suggesting the pretrained representation already encodes sufficient temporal structure. Open questions include whether the five-level prompt scheme and SCMA generalize to other quality dimensions (e.g., audio-visual quality, multi-dimensional UGC quality) and whether the approach extends to higher-resolution or longer-duration video settings where frame sampling becomes more critical.
