## Problem

Maximum likelihood inference from quantized observations is intractable under general distributions because the likelihood involves coupled multidimensional integrals over both the quantization region and a latent mixing variable. Existing likelihood-based methods are restricted to Gaussian assumptions or one-bit quantization, leaving a gap for robust inference under heavy-tailed or skewed data with arbitrary multi-bit quantizers.

## Input / Output

The method consumes discrete quantized observations y ∈ K^d (where K is a finite alphabet of e levels defined by thresholds τ) along with the quantization function Q, and produces the full NVMM parameter set (location μ, skewness ξ, scatter matrix Σ) via closed-form iterative updates. In structured extensions, the same framework outputs regression coefficients β, a low-rank factorization M = AB^⊤, a sparse signal θ, or a correlation matrix, depending on the downstream task. A notable constraint is that in the one-bit Gaussian special case, only the ratio μ_i/σ_i² is identifiable, requiring one parameter block to be fixed.

## Method

The paper proposes an ECM algorithm that augments the problem with two latent variables: the continuous signal x (to handle quantization) and the mixing variable z (to handle the NVMM structure). The E-step applies Jensen's inequality sequentially over x|y and z|x to construct a tractable surrogate objective, while the CM-step exploits the conditional Gaussian structure of the NVMM family to derive closed-form updates for μ, ξ, and Σ at each iteration. Global linear convergence is established by proving component-wise strict concavity of the log-likelihood via the Brascamp-Lieb inequality, yielding explicit contraction constants for each parameter. The framework extends naturally to structured tasks (quantized regression, matrix completion, compressive sensing, covariance estimation) by substituting the location parameter with the task-specific signal and solving the resulting surrogate with standard solvers (e.g., FISTA for the ℓ₁-regularized compressive sensing case).

## Results

On the MovieLens 1M matrix completion task, the proposed multi-bit GH ECM method achieves the best accuracy of 0.4503 and lowest RMSE of 0.8908, outperforming the multi-bit Gaussian baseline (0.4453 accuracy, 0.9151 RMSE) and all classical methods. In 1-bit compressive sensing with skewed heavy-tailed noise, the 1-bit GH ECM method attains the highest cosine similarity of 0.8906 at SNR=0 dB and 0.5386 at SNR=−10 dB, substantially exceeding the 1-bit Gaussian MLE (0.8220 and 0.2896) and the AOP baseline (0.7052 and 0.2209). The proposed methods also demonstrate superior robustness under t-distributed and log-normal noise in quantized linear regression, where OLS and Gaussian EM degrade significantly.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On MovieLens 1M matrix completion, the proposed multi-bit GH method (0.4503 accuracy, 0.8908 RMSE) outperforms the multi-bit Gaussian baseline of Bhaskar (0.4453, 0.9151) by ~0.5% accuracy and ~0.7% RMSE, and substantially beats the 1-bit Gaussian method of Davenport et al. (0.4217, 0.9659) and the nuclear norm approach of Cai et al. (0.3802, 1.1662). The SVD and L2-regularized baselines of Sarwar et al. and Paterek are also clearly surpassed in both accuracy and RMSE. In 1-bit compressive sensing, the 1-bit GH ECM method dominates all baselines across all SNR levels: at SNR=0 dB it achieves 0.8906 cosine similarity versus 0.8220 for the 1-bit Gaussian MLE of Zymnis et al., 0.7872 for RSS (Laska et al.), and 0.7052 for AOP (Yan et al.); the gap widens at lower SNR (0.5386 vs. 0.2896 at −10 dB). In quantized linear regression, the GHST and GH ECM methods significantly outperform OLS and the Gaussian EM algorithm of Finesso et al. under heavy-tailed (t, ν=3) and asymmetric (log-normal) noise, where the Gaussian-based baselines degrade sharply. For correlation estimation under a t(ν=3) distribution, the proposed t ECM method achieves the lowest Frobenius norm error compared to the arcsine law (Van Vleck & Middleton), one-bit autocorrelation (Liu & Lin), one-bit Hermite law (Liu & Chou), one-bit MLE (Xiao et al.), dithering-based methods (Dirksen et al., Eamaz et al.), and multi-bit estimators (Chen et al., Chen & Ng), with the advantage most pronounced at smaller sample sizes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the quantized inference literature, which has been largely confined to Gaussian assumptions and one-bit quantization (Ribeiro & Giannakis, 2006; Davenport et al., 2014; Bhaskar, 2016), by formulating maximum likelihood estimation under the general normal variance-mean mixture (NVMM) family with arbitrary multi-bit quantizers. Methodologically, it builds on the ECM framework (McLachlan & Krishnan, 2008) and the NVMM data-augmentation structure (Polson & Scott, 2013), introducing latent-variable augmentations for both the quantization indicator and the mixing variable z to obtain closed-form parameter updates. The theoretical analysis departs from prior EM convergence results by employing the Brascamp-Lieb inequality to establish component-wise strict concavity of the log-likelihood, yielding global linear convergence guarantees that were previously unavailable in the quantized NVMM setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a unified, tractable approach to quantized inference under heavy-tailed and skewed distributions, which is practically important for recommendation systems, compressed sensing, and wireless sensing where outliers and asymmetry are common. The global linear convergence guarantee with explicit rate constants gives practitioners confidence in the algorithm's behavior, and the closed-form updates make the method computationally efficient relative to gradient-based alternatives. Open questions include the statistical estimation properties (minimax rates, consistency) of the resulting estimators, scalability to very high-dimensional settings where the d×d scatter matrix becomes intractable, and whether the identifiability issue in the one-bit elliptical case can be resolved without fixing one parameter block.
