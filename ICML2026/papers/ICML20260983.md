## Problem

Instruction tuning for speech language models (SLMs) is substantially harder than for text LLMs because speech sequences are ~20× longer than text (making data scaling intractable) and SLMs must handle entirely new speech-specific instructions (prosody understanding, expressive generation) beyond what text LLMs support. Existing SLM training pipelines replicate text LLM recipes—massive speech pre-training followed by instruction tuning—but this causes severe catastrophic forgetting of the base text LLM's knowledge and requires orders of magnitude more data. The paper asks whether both objectives (preserving text LLM capabilities and acquiring speech-specific skills) can be achieved without any instruction tuning at all.

## Input / Output

Input consists of a speech utterance (converted to discrete prosody tokens via a frozen ProsodyLM encoder, encoding per-word pitch, duration, and energy at 512 quantization levels) paired with its text transcription from an external Whisper-large-v3 ASR system, plus a text instruction. Output is a response in the same paired [text][speech] format, where the speech portion is decoded back to audio by a frozen TTS decoder. The model never sees raw audio; it operates entirely on discrete prosody tokens and text, with format-forcing logits processors ensuring correct structural alternation between text and speech sections during generation.

## Method

SPEECHCOMBINE constructs an instruction-following SLM through a single round of continuous pre-training (30k hours, LoRA rank 64) on speech data from a text LLM base model (Qwen3-8B-base), followed by a weight-space combination: θ_SC = θ_base + λ·(θ_speech − θ_base) + (θ_inst − θ_base), with λ=0.85. The key insight is that the instruction-following direction Δθ_inst (computed as the difference between the instruct and base text LLM) can be transplanted onto the speech-adapted model, transferring instruction-following capabilities to the speech modality without any speech instruction tuning. Pre-training uses a structured data format of [caption][text][speech] triplets with randomized caption placement, where captions (generated by GPT-OSS 120B) describe prosodic attributes like pitch, speaking rate, emotion, and emphasis. Critically, pre-training must start from the base model rather than the instruct model to preserve the compositional parameter structure that enables successful weight combination. Long-thinking capability is inherited 'for free' by simply inserting a <think> token in the answer template, with no additional training.

## Results

On speech understanding, SPEECHCOMBINE achieves a state-of-the-art F1 of 60.84 on emphasis detection, nearly doubling the next-best baseline (Fun-Audio-Chat at 28.76) and substantially outperforming GPT-4o-Audio (42.99). On speech generation, it scores 45.42 on GenEmotion (second-best after GPT-4o's 33.46 is actually lower, so SPEECHCOMBINE is best among comparable-size models) and 31.42 F1 on emphasis generation (best among all baselines except GPT-4o). On text-oriented tasks, SPEECHCOMBINE matches or exceeds the ASR+TextLLM upper bound on OpenbookQA (86.59 vs. 83.29) and MMSU (73.38 vs. 73.22), while remaining competitive on GSM8k (90.03) and MLC (93.97). All results are achieved with only 30k hours of speech pre-training and no instruction tuning, compared to millions of hours for baselines like Fun-Audio-Chat.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPEECHCOMBINE achieves the best emphasis detection F1 (60.84) among all comparable-size models, more than doubling Fun-Audio-Chat (28.76) and Kimi-Audio (19.88), and substantially exceeding GPT-4o-Audio (42.99). On emphasis generation F1, it leads all baselines except GPT-4o-Audio (31.42 vs. 65.02), outperforming Fun-Audio-Chat (22.91) and GLM-4-Voice (26.55). On GenEmotion score, SPEECHCOMBINE (45.42) surpasses Fun-Audio-Chat (39.30) and GLM-4-Voice (48.13 is actually higher, so it's second), while GPT-4o-Audio scores only 33.46. On text-oriented tasks (Table 1), SPEECHCOMBINE matches or beats the ASR+TextLLM upper bound on OpenbookQA (86.59 vs. 83.29) and MMSU (73.38 vs. 73.22), and is competitive with Fun-Audio-Chat (93.97 vs. 93.97 on MLC) despite using <1% of its training data. The Group A ablation baselines (Cont. Pre-Train, Cont. Pre-Train+SFT) confirm that conventional SLM training paradigms fail catastrophically on speech-specific tasks (F1 < 3 on emphasis detection), whereas SPEECHCOMBINE's weight-combination approach enables these capabilities without any explicit instruction tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies the weight-difference transfer technique from Chat Vector (Huang et al., 2024) to the speech domain, combining it with a single round of continuous pre-training on speech data. It departs from the dominant SLM training paradigm (large-scale speech pre-training followed by multi-round instruction tuning on synthesized speech instruction data) by eliminating instruction tuning entirely and instead transplanting the instruction-following direction (Δθ_inst) from a text LLM onto a speech-adapted model. The speech tokenization builds on ProsodyLM (Qian et al., 2025), and the overall framework is positioned as a data-efficient alternative to native multimodal SLMs (e.g., Fun-Audio-Chat, which also uses model merging but from a different base) and modular-aligned approaches (e.g., Freeze-Omni, which only supports text-oriented instructions).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical impact is a ~100× reduction in training data (30k hours vs. millions of hours for competitors) while achieving competitive or SOTA performance across text-oriented, speech understanding, and speech generation tasks, offering a scalable path for SLMs to rapidly inherit emerging text LLM capabilities without retraining. The emergent compositional effects—speech understanding and generation capabilities arising without explicit instruction tuning on those tasks—suggest that instruction-following is a transferable, modality-agnostic property in weight space. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to timbre and speaker identity, whether the external ASR dependency can be internalized, and whether the format instability (requiring logits-level forcing) can be resolved through better pre-training recipes or larger λ ranges.
