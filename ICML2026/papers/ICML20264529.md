## Problem

Last-iterate convergence rates for SGD without bounded variance were recently established for unconstrained problems, but the extension to regularized (composite) problems is nontrivial because the proximal operator is nonlinear and breaks the closed-form iterate expansion those analyses rely on. This paper fills that gap by proving optimal (up-to-log) Õ(1/√T) last-iterate rates for proximal SGD and the randomized incremental proximal method under only componentwise convexity and smoothness, with variance bounded solely at the solution.

## Input / Output

The algorithms consume an unbiased stochastic gradient oracle ∇f_i(x) drawn i.i.d. from a distribution D, access to either the full proximal operator prox_g (for SPGD) or individual component proximal operators prox_{g_j} (for RIPM), an initial point x₀, and a step size τ = O(1/√T). The output is the last iterate x_{T+1} with the guarantee E[h(x_{T+1}) − h*] = Õ(1/√T). A key constraint is that the smooth components f_i must be convex and L-smooth, and the second moment of stochastic gradients need only be finite at the solution (σ*² < ∞) rather than uniformly bounded; for RIPM, each regularizer component g_j must additionally be L_g-Lipschitz.

## Method

The core technical contribution is adapting the last-iterate analysis of Garrigos et al. (2025) and Zamani & Glineur (2025) to the proximal setting, where two obstacles arise: the proximal operator's nonlinearity invalidates the standard squared-distance expansion (replaced by the three-point identity plus the proximal inequality), and the cancellation E[⟨∇f_i(x*), x_t − x*⟩] = 0 fails because ∇f(x*) ≠ 0 when g ≠ 0 (overcome via a variance transfer lemma using co-coercivity and the optimality condition 0 ∈ ∇f(x*) + ∂g(x*)). The per-iteration bound involves both h(x_{t+1}) and h(x_t) on the left-hand side, requiring an extension of the Zamani–Glineur telescoping argument with a carefully chosen auxiliary sequence z_t = (1−p_t)x_t + p_t z_{t−1} and weights α_t. For RIPM, the coupling between the randomly selected proximal operator and the iterate is decoupled using the L_g-Lipschitzness of each g_j, introducing an additional 8τm²L_g² error term. The step size τ = 1/(CL√T) with C > 2 (or C > 4 for RIPM) yields the final Õ(1/√T) rate, and the analysis extends directly to projected SGD, stochastic proximal point, and the BlockProx algorithm for graph-structured regularizers.

## Results

The paper proves that the last iterate of proximal SGD (SPGD) achieves an expected suboptimality of $\widetilde{O}(1/\sqrt{T})$ under componentwise convexity and $L$-smoothness, with the stochastic gradient second moment bounded only at the solution (Theorem 3.1). The same $\widetilde{O}(1/\sqrt{T})$ rate is established for the randomized incremental proximal method (RIPM) when the regularizer decomposes as $g = \sum_j g_j$ and only individual proximal operators $\mathrm{prox}_{g_j}$ are available (Theorem 4.1). The bound is independent of the number of smooth component functions $N$, and the only additional cost over the optimal $O(1/\sqrt{T})$ rate (achievable under bounded variance) is a $\ln(T+1)$ factor. Numerical experiments on synthetic Lasso, MNIST logistic regression, and graph-structured network Lasso problems confirm that the last iterate outperforms the averaged iterate in practice.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's main contribution is filling a gap in the literature: prior last-iterate results for proximal SGD (Liu & Zhou, 2024) require bounded variance, while the recent unbounded-variance last-iterate results for unconstrained SGD (Attia et al., 2025; Garrigos et al., 2025) do not handle the proximal operator. This paper is the first to combine both: last-iterate $\widetilde{O}(1/\sqrt{T})$ rates for proximal SGD without bounded variance. For RIPM, Bertsekas (2011) proved only asymptotic convergence under bounded gradients; this paper provides the first nonasymptotic last-iterate rate under the weaker componentwise smoothness assumption. For BlockProx (Lin et al., 2025), prior analysis gave last-iterate rates only under bounded gradients or ergodic rates under smoothness; this paper bridges the gap by proving last-iterate $\widetilde{O}(1/\sqrt{T})$ rates under smoothness alone, matching the setting actually used in the experiments of Lin et al. (2025). Compared to Cai & Diakonikolas (2025) and Liu & Zhou (2025), which handle finite-$N$ incremental methods with bounds depending polynomially on $N$, this paper's bounds are $N$-independent, showing progress in a single pass over the data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the last-iterate convergence analysis of unconstrained SGD under componentwise smoothness (Attia et al., 2025; Garrigos et al., 2025) to the proximal/regularized setting, where the nonlinear proximal operator breaks the closed-form iterate expansion used in those works. The key technical adaptation replaces the linear SGD expansion with the three-point identity and prox-inequality, and handles the fact that ∇f(x*)≠0 at the solution when g≠0. For the incremental proximal method (RIPM), the paper provides the first nonasymptotic last-iterate rate, going beyond Bertsekas (2011) who proved only asymptotic convergence under bounded gradients. The BlockProx extension builds on Lin et al. (2025) by replacing their bounded-gradient or averaged-iterate guarantees with last-iterate rates under mere smoothness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This fills a notable gap: proximal SGD for Lasso in the large-scale regime (where N is unbounded) previously lacked last-iterate guarantees without the bounded variance assumption, which fails even for linear least squares. The result justifies the standard practitioner practice of outputting the last iterate rather than the average, with a rate matching the unconstrained case up to a log factor. Open questions include extending to random reshuffling/sampling without replacement, removing the Lipschitzness requirement on individual g_j components (which is needed to decouple the stochastic proximal selection from the iterate), and obtaining high-probability or Bregman-distance variants.
