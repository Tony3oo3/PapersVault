## Problem

Aligning LLMs to specific personality personas typically requires expensive, monolithic fine-tuning (SFT, RLHF, DPO), where each trait combination demands a separate model. While inference-time activation steering offers a parameter-efficient alternative, naive multi-vector steering fails due to destructive interference: preceding interventions shift the activation manifold, causing subsequent probes trained on unsteered distributions to misalign and degrade generation into incoherence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261596/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a standard text prompt plus a set of per-trait steering coefficients (α) that the user adjusts to specify desired personality intensity along each Big Five dimension. The steering vectors are unit-normalized and injected into the residual stream at a single selected layer for every generated token (not just prompt tokens), so the behavioral shift persists throughout the full response. Evaluation outputs are Big Five trait scores (1–5 Likert scale) derived by aggregating GPT-4-as-a-judge ratings over 44 BFI questionnaire items, alongside perplexity and F1 coherence metrics.

## Method

The core contribution is Sequential Adaptive Steering (SAS), which constructs personality probes as linear logistic regression classifiers on residual-stream activations, with the critical twist that each subsequent probe is trained on a composite dataset mixing unsteered activations with activations shifted by randomly sampled intensities (α_train) of all predecessor probes. This exposure to the distributional shifts induced by prior interventions forces each new probe to learn a direction invariant to those perturbations, effectively orthogonalizing it relative to the subspaces spanned by earlier traits. Layer selection is automated via the Fisher Ratio, which measures class separability across middle layers to identify where each trait is most disentangled. At deployment, composing a multi-trait persona requires only adding α_i·v_i to the residual stream at each trait's selected layer — a single O(d) vector addition per layer with negligible latency overhead (<5 ms for 50 tokens on Llama-3-8B). The paper validates that this approach achieves Pareto dominance over naive steering, Gram-Schmidt post-hoc orthogonalization, and LoRA Soups weight merging on both goal adherence and coherence.

## Results

On the multi-trait target (High Extraversion, High Neuroticism, Low Agreeableness), SAS shifts Extraversion from 3.50 to 4.20 (+0.70), Neuroticism from 2.88 to 4.10 (+1.22), and Agreeableness from 4.00 to 2.62 (−1.38), while preserving general capability (TruthfulQA 0.32, HellaSwag 0.64, matching baseline). Perplexity increases modestly to 14.54 (vs. 9.95 baseline), whereas naive multi-vector steering degrades to 27.16 PPL with only marginal trait movement (EXT +0.12, NEU +0.22, AGR −0.39). The Pareto frontier analysis confirms SAS dominates naive steering across the entire personality-score-vs-perplexity trade-off curve.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the DPO fine-tuned model (Rafailov et al., 2023), SAS achieves substantially larger multi-dimensional trait shifts: the DPO model remains virtually indistinguishable from the unsteered baseline on the radar plot (Figure 4), failing to manifest the required simultaneous shifts in Extraversion, Neuroticism, and Agreeableness, while SAS hits all three targets. Naive multi-vector steering (independently trained probes applied sequentially) collapses to 27.16 PPL with only ~10–20% of the trait movement achieved by SAS, confirming the destructive interference hypothesis. Gram-Schmidt post-hoc orthogonalization provides no practical benefit over naive steering at α=1.0 (PPL 17.15 vs. 17.10) and suffers coherence collapse at α=1.5 (PPL >83), whereas SAS remains coherent (PPL 18.65). LoRA Soups weight merging barely moves targeted traits (EXT −0.12, AGR −0.22) while non-selectively degrading Conscientiousness (−1.11), and system-prompt-based control leaves the personality profile indistinguishable from the base model. SAS is the only method that simultaneously achieves the intended multi-trait configuration while preserving TruthfulQA and HellaSwag scores at baseline levels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the activation-steering paradigm established by Turner et al. (2023) and Zou et al. (2023), which demonstrated that adding fixed vectors to residual streams can elicit single behaviors, to the multi-trait regime. It builds directly on recent personality-specific work—Chen et al. (2025) on persona vectors and Frising & Balcells (2025) on Big Five linear probing—but departs from their single-vector focus by addressing the interference problem that arises when multiple steering vectors are composed. The key methodological departure is Sequential Adaptive Steering, which trains each probe on activations already shifted by predecessor probes, effectively replacing post-hoc geometric fixes (e.g., Gram-Schmidt) with a distribution-aware training procedure. The paper also positions itself against weight-space composition methods (Task Arithmetic, LoRA Soups) by demonstrating that activation-space intervention avoids the cancellation effects inherent in merging conflicting fine-tuned adapters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, zero-parameter-update path to modular personality control, sidestepping the combinatorial explosion of training separate models for each trait combination—a significant efficiency gain for applications requiring diverse personas (therapy, roleplay, customer support). However, several open questions limit near-term impact: the method requires white-box access (ruling out API models), introduces an order dependency that necessitates retraining downstream probes when adding new traits, and is validated only on 7–8B architectures with LLM-as-a-judge evaluation rather than downstream behavioral tasks. Whether the geometric orthogonality findings scale to 70B+ models, whether nonlinear controllers can further improve composition, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the Big Five to arbitrary steerable attributes (safety, writing style) remain important directions.
