## Problem

Deep classification networks trained past interpolation exhibit low-dimensional bulk-outlier spectra in their Hessians, gradients, and weights, with K² outliers (K = number of classes) that prior theory could only partially explain. Existing analytic approaches captured subsets of the observed behavior or relied on assumptions that cannot hold in practice, and the causal link to deep neural collapse (DNC)—the geometric phenomenon where features collapse to class means forming orthogonal frames—had never been established rigorously. This paper provides the first unified analytic explanation showing that DNC is the source of all these spectral phenomena simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes an overparameterized deep network (L layers, width d ≥ K) trained with MSE or cross-entropy loss on K-class data with n samples per class, and produces closed-form eigen-decompositions for layer-wise Hessians, gradient eigenspace projections, and weight matrices. All eigenvectors are expressed as Kronecker products of normalized class feature means (μ̂_c^(l+1) ⊗ μ̂_c'^(l)), and all nonzero eigenvalues collapse to a single value determined by a scalar C solving an algebraic equation in the regularization parameter λ and depth L. The full-network Hessian inherits the same K²-rank outlier structure in the limit of large depth with linearly decaying regularization, while the gradient is confined to only a K-dimensional subspace of the K²-dimensional outlier eigenspace.

## Method

The paper uses the deep unconstrained feature model (UFM), where the expressive feature map is replaced by freely optimized feature vectors, making the problem analytically tractable while preserving the geometric structure of overparameterized networks. The core mechanism is a Kronecker product decomposition of the layer-wise Hessian (Hess_l = A_{l+1}^T A_{l+1} ⊗ Av{h_ic^(l) h_ic^(l)T}), which under DNC reduces to a rank-K² sum of outer products of Kronecker products of feature means, yielding K² equal nonzero eigenvalues. The paper further proves that the gradient occupies only the c = c' diagonal subspace (K dimensions) of the Hessian's outlier eigenspace, and that weight matrices have rank K with singular vectors given by the feature means. Results extend from linear to ReLU activations by showing that optimal DNC solutions in the ReLU case coincide with nonnegative linear-model solutions, and the full Hessian inherits the layer-wise spectral structure in the depth limit under minimal assumptions on the feature-map blocks.

## Results

The paper proves that at DNC global optima of the deep linear UFM, the layer-wise Hessian has rank K² with all nonzero eigenvalues equal to (1/K)·n^(−1/(L+1))·C^(2L/(L+1)), and eigenvectors given by μ̂_c^(l+1)⊗μ̂_c'^(l). The gradient occupies only a K-dimensional subspace of the Hessian's K²-dimensional outlier eigenspace, with each c=c' coefficient equal to 1/K and all c≠c' coefficients vanishing. Empirically, on MNIST (K=10) with a ResNet-20 backbone, the predicted eigenvector alignment (squared cosine similarity) approaches 1, the c=c' gradient coefficients concentrate near 1/K=0.1, and K²=100 outlier eigenvalues emerge from the bulk during training. In the UFM setting (K=4, L=4), alignment values match theory to eight significant figures at convergence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper that does not benchmark against competing methods; instead, it validates its analytic predictions against prior empirical observations and real-network experiments. The theory recovers and extends the Hessian decomposition of Papyan (2020) by providing closed-form eigenvectors (not just eigenvalue counts) and showing the mini-bulk and outlier spikes arise from orthogonal eigenspaces of G_class and G_cross. It explains the gradient-alignment phenomenon of Gur-Ari et al. (2018) by showing the gradient is confined to the span of only K of the K² outlier eigenvectors. On real MNIST networks (both linear and ReLU FC heads), the predicted spectral structure and eigenvector directions are confirmed with only residual noise from finite-width and finite-training effects, demonstrating that the UFM-based theory generalizes beyond its idealized modeling assumptions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the deep unconstrained feature model (UFM) lineage initiated by Mixon et al. (2020) for neural collapse and extended by Súkenik et al. (2023) to deep neural collapse (DNC), but repurposes it as a tool for analyzing deep-learning matrices rather than feature geometry. It analytically recovers and extends Papyan's (2020) empirical Hessian decomposition (G_class + G_cross + G_within) and the bulk-outlier spectra documented by Sagun et al. (2016, 2017), while departing from prior Hessian analyses (random matrix theory, spin-glass, NTK) that captured only subsets of the observed behavior or relied on unrealistic assumptions. The key conceptual departure is establishing DNC as the causal source of low-dimensional spectra across the Hessian, gradients, and weights simultaneously, rather than treating these as separate phenomena.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By showing that feature means (the characterizing objects of DNC) fully determine Hessian eigenvalues, eigenvectors, gradient alignment, and weight singular values, the paper provides a geometrically interpretable lens on curvature that was previously accessible only through the notoriously difficult Hessian. This opens a practical avenue: shaping DNC through architecture or regularization choices could serve as a proxy for controlling flatness, which matters for generalization and robustness. Limitations include reliance on the UFM assumption, focus on MSE loss in the main text (CE is deferred to an appendix), and the overparameterized/over-training limit; architectural effects and finite-width corrections remain open.
