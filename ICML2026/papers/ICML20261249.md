## Problem

The standard practice of early-stopping diffusion models at the test-loss minimum assumes that generalization and memorization are mutually exclusive, with bias toward training data only appearing after overfitting begins. This paper challenges that dichotomy by identifying a 'biased generalization' phase in which the test loss is still decreasing yet the model's outputs already exhibit anomalous proximity to specific training samples, revealing a blind spot in current evaluation protocols for privacy-critical deployments.

## Input / Output

The diagnostic pipeline consumes trained DDPM checkpoints (U-Net on 32×32 grayscale CelebA images; 8-layer transformer on one-hot-encoded discrete sequences of length 16) along with their training and held-out test sets, and evaluates them at fixed diffusion times t where denoising is most input-dependent. Outputs are multi-scale bias metrics: sample-level cosine distances between identically-seeded generations from two disjoint-split models, nearest-neighbor KL divergence comparing generated-to-training distance distributions against i.i.d. ground truth, denoiser-level per-site KL divergences against exact Belief Propagation oracles (in the controlled setting), and U-turn overlap ratios that isolate training-set conditioning effects along reverse trajectories. In the controlled hierarchical model, the availability of exact BP posterior means at every noise level enables a loss decomposition that separates a data-independent 'distillation' term from a data-dependent 'excess' term, making the bias mechanism quantitatively transparent.

## Method

The paper introduces a two-pronged diagnostic framework for detecting biased generalization. On real images, a sample-split protocol trains 15 U-Net DDPMs on disjoint CelebA subsets and tracks the cosine distance between identically-seeded generated samples and between denoiser predictions at fixed diffusion times, revealing a U-shaped divergence curve whose minimum precedes the test-loss minimum. In a controlled tree-based hierarchical data model (ℓ=4 layers, q=6 symbols), a transformer denoiser is trained and compared against exact Belief Propagation oracles at multiple hierarchical filtering levels (BP_k), enabling precise localization of which structural scales the model has resolved and when data-dependent bias sets in. The mechanistic explanation attributes the phenomenon to sequential feature learning: coarse, short-range structure is acquired in a data-independent 'mean-field' regime, while finer, longer-range features are approximated in a data-dependent manner once training statistics become insufficient, causing simultaneous test-loss improvement and bias growth. A loss decomposition formalizes this orthogonality by showing the excess data-dependent term vanishes in expectation under the true distribution (hence invisible to test loss) but is finite and optimizable under the empirical training distribution. A training-free single-parameter model with sharpness ε further confirms that biased generalization is not an artifact of neural network inductive bias or SGD dynamics.

## Results

On CelebA (32×32 grayscale, n=1024, 15 models / 105 pairs), the sample-split cosine distance between generated samples reaches its minimum significantly earlier than the DSM test loss minimum, identifying a biased generalization phase while the test loss is still decreasing; the standard memorization indicator (Eq. 3) remains below 10⁻³ throughout. On the controlled hierarchical model (ℓ=4, q=6, N=16), the nearest-neighbor divergence minimum and the sample-split denoiser divergence onset both precede the model–oracle KL divergence minimum, with the best-matching filtered oracle shifting from BP₂ (n=5k) to BP₁ (n=12k) to BP₀ (n=70k), showing that bias narrows with more data. The U-turn experiment at the test-loss minimum reveals a clear separation: corrupted training samples are significantly more likely to be recovered than unseen test samples, while both curves simultaneously move closer to oracle (unity) performance. A training-free sharpness-parameterized model exhibits markedly more severe bias than the trained transformer, confirming the phenomenon is not an artifact of SGD dynamics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper is diagnostic rather than a method-vs-baseline comparison, so no external baselines are benchmarked. Internally, the authors compare three complementary bias diagnostics (sample-split cosine distance, nearest-neighbor KL divergence, and denoiser-level KL divergence against filtered BP oracles) and show they all identify the same biased-generalization window. Across training set sizes on the hierarchical model, increasing n from 5k to 70k shifts the best-matching oracle from BP₂ to BP₀, progressively shrinking the biased region until bias becomes negligible. The trained transformer is contrasted with a training-free ε-sharpness model on the same data: the training-free model shows substantially larger bias at the test-loss minimum because it lacks the sequential feature-learning inductive bias that partially protects the neural network. On CelebA, the Haar wavelet coarse-to-fine filtering experiment provides an image-domain analogue of the BPₖ oracle comparison, confirming that coarse features (L0) saturate early without bias while finer scales (L1, L2) peak later, consistent with the hierarchical mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the established framework of DDPM training (Ho et al., 2020) and the recent line of work characterizing the generalization-to-memorization transition in diffusion models (Bonnaire et al., 2025; Favero et al., 2025b; Li et al., 2023), which established that diffusion models generalize on the way to memorization and motivated early stopping at the test-loss minimum. It departs from the prevailing view that generalization and memorization are mutually exclusive (Yoon et al., 2023) by identifying a previously unrecognized 'biased generalization' phase in which the test loss still decreases while the model's outputs become increasingly dependent on specific training samples. The controlled analysis leverages the hierarchical data model and Belief Propagation oracles from Garnier-Brun et al. (2025) and Cagnetta et al. (2024), while the sample-split diagnostic methodology follows Kadkhodaie et al. (2024). The training-free example connects to Biroli & Mézard's (2026) exactly solvable KDE analysis, showing the phenomenon is not an artifact of neural network inductive biases.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that early stopping at the test-loss minimum is insufficient to prevent training-data bias has direct practical implications for deploying generative models in privacy-sensitive or copyright-critical domains, where even subtle systematic reuse of training-specific features may be unacceptable. The paper provides concrete diagnostic tools (nearest-neighbor divergence, sample-split denoiser comparison, U-turn experiments) that practitioners can apply to detect this bias, but it explicitly does not propose mitigation strategies. Key open questions include how the biased regime scales with data dimension and training set size, whether other generative model classes (discrete diffusion, autoregressive models) exhibit analogous behavior, and how conditioning and guidance techniques (e.g., classifier-free guidance) might amplify or interact with the identified bias.
