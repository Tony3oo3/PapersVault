## Problem

Visually grounded Chain-of-Thought (CoT) that mandates explicit bounding boxes or crop tool calls during inference often degrades MLLM performance compared to standard textual CoT, despite being trained for grounded reasoning. The paper identifies that the visual localization capability can be internalized into textual reasoning, but the mandatory generation of explicit coordinates introduces task interference that detracts from answer prediction. The open question is how to explicitly transfer grounding capability into the textual reasoning process so the model localizes accurately without emitting coordinates at test time.

## Input / Output

The model takes a standard RGB image paired with a text question (VQA format) and produces a structured response containing a reasoning trace between  tags followed by a final answer in <answer> tags. During training, the same query is duplicated into two streams: a grounded stream that must emit bounding box coordinates (e.g., <box>[x1,y1,x2,y2]</box>) when referring to objects, and a textual stream that performs pure natural-language reasoning. At inference, the model defaults to textual CoT but retains the ability to generate bounding boxes on demand, enabling an optional tool-assisted workflow where predicted boxes drive a crop tool to produce local and union-crop views as additional visual inputs.

## Method

iVGR employs a dual-stream GRPO-based reinforcement learning strategy where, for each training query, the policy MLLM samples N rollouts in both a grounded stream (with explicit box predictions rewarded via bidirectional IoU matching) and a textual stream (standard reasoning). The key mechanism is a consistency reward for the textual stream: a high-quality grounded rollout (selected from the grounded stream via a persistent rollout archive that retains the best IoU-scoring reference per query) serves as a visual guide, and an external LLM judge (Qwen2.5-72B) scores the semantic alignment between the textual CoT and this reference on a four-level rubric (1.0 identical, 0.7 single deviation, 0.3 compound deviation, 0.0 contradiction). This reward explicitly transfers localization knowledge into the textual stream without requiring coordinate emission at inference. A cold-start SFT phase on a mixture of grounded and textual CoT data initializes both capabilities before RL training, and the dual-stream design maintains computational parity with single-stream baselines by duplicating queries rather than increasing total rollouts.

## Results

On fine-grained VQA benchmarks, iVGR-Qwen2.5-VL-7B achieves 86.4% on V*, 78.3% on HR4K, and 75.5% on HR8K, surpassing the previous SOTA TreeVGR-7B (83.8/77.1/73.1) by 2.6/1.2/2.4 points respectively. The method scales effectively: iVGR-Qwen3-VL-8B reaches 80.6% average (vs. 76.2% baseline, +4.4) and iVGR-Qwen3-VL-32B reaches 82.9% average (vs. 79.6% baseline, +3.3), approaching proprietary models like GPT-5.4 (83.1%) and Gemini-3.1-Pro (83.9%). On chart understanding and multidisciplinary reasoning, iVGR yields consistent gains of +2.5 to +3.3 points over baselines across ChartQA, AI2D, WeMath, MMStar, MMMU, and MMK12.

## Baselines & Comparisons

iVGR-Qwen2.5-VL-7B outperforms all open-source visually grounded reasoning baselines without requiring any external tool at inference: it beats TreeVGR-7B (74.7→76.6 avg, +1.9), DeepEyes-7B (74.1→76.6, +2.5), and Thyme-7B (74.6→76.6, +2.0), with the largest margins on fine-grained tasks (V*: +2.6 over TreeVGR; HR8K: +2.4 over TreeVGR). Against the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B model, iVGR yields a +6.5 average improvement, with particularly large gains on MME-RW-Lite (+11.1) and HR8K (+10.4). Scaling to Qwen3-VL-8B, iVGR achieves 80.6% average, surpassing the 32B base model (79.6%) and approaching GPT-5.4 (83.1%) with only 8B parameters. On the 32B scale, iVGR-Qwen3-VL-32B reaches 82.9%, within 1.0 point of Gemini-3.1-Pro (83.9%) and 0.2 points of GPT-5.4 (83.1), while remaining tool-free. Notably, iVGR does not degrade on general VQA: POPE and RealWorldQA scores are maintained or slightly improved, confirming that internalized grounding does not sacrifice general reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the visually grounded reasoning line initiated by TreeVGR (explicit bounding boxes in CoT) and the GRPO reinforcement learning framework, while departing from the broader family of tool-based (DeepEyes, PixelReasoner, Mini-o3) and box-based (GRIT, TreeVGR) approaches that mandate explicit visual grounding at inference. The key conceptual departure is the empirical finding that this mandated grounding actually degrades performance, motivating a dual-stream RL strategy with a consistency reward that transfers localization capability into pure textual CoT. The method also retains compatibility with crop-tool workflows at test time, bridging the internalized and tool-assisted paradigms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that explicit grounding interferes with answer prediction challenges a core assumption in the visually grounded reasoning literature and suggests that future work should focus on implicit rather than explicit localization during inference. Practical limitations include the dependency on bounding-box annotations for training and an external LLM judge for the consistency reward, both of which add cost and potential noise. An open direction identified by the authors is replacing the grounded stream during training with a crop-tool-augmented mechanism to produce higher-quality reference CoTs, which could further strengthen the textual stream's supervision.
