## Problem

Existing ECG-report alignment methods rely on CLIP-style contrastive objectives that enforce point-wise instance similarity, implicitly assuming distributional compatibility between ECG and report embeddings. However, ECG signals are continuous, high-resolution physiological signals while clinical reports are discrete, high-level semantic summaries, creating pronounced distributional mismatches that make instance-level alignment unstable and biased. The paper identifies that no prior method explicitly measures and reduces the distributional discrepancy between these heterogeneous modalities.

## Input / Output

The method takes 12-lead ECG signals sampled at 500 Hz over 10 seconds paired with free-text clinical reports as input. The ECG encoder (1D ResNet18) and text encoder (Med-CPT) produce embeddings that are further parameterized as Gaussian latent variables (mean and standard deviation) via reparameterization, enabling both deterministic and stochastic sampling. The output is a shared latent-space ECG embedding used for retrieval-based zero-shot classification against category prompt embeddings, linear probing with a frozen backbone, and cross-dataset transfer evaluation.

## Method

SGERA replaces CLIP-style InfoNCE with a dual-level Stein-kernel alignment: (1) an instance-level RBF-Stein kernel that uses negative squared Euclidean distance (without the exponential mapping) to produce smoother, linear-in-distance gradients for pairwise alignment, combined with KL regularization on the Gaussian latent variables; and (2) a distribution-level score-based Stein kernel that leverages Stein's identity to compute higher-order distributional discrepancies between ECG and report embeddings via learned score networks, aligning three kernel matrices (ECG-ECG, report-report, ECG-report) through squared cosine distance. An adaptive median-distance heuristic updates the kernel bandwidth during training. An auxiliary ECG-Report Matching task with similarity-proportional hard negative mining (intra-modal and cross-modal) refines discriminative boundaries. The total loss jointly optimizes all four components with unit weights, and the framework is pre-trained on 760K MIMIC-IV-ECG pairs before transfer to downstream classification.

## Results

On zero-shot classification across six ECG benchmarks, SGERA achieves the best average AUC of 79.5%, outperforming MELP (79.0%), DERI (78.7%), D-BETA (77.1%), and MERL (75.3%). In linear probing with 1% labeled data, SGERA attains the highest or near-highest AUC across all six sub-tasks (e.g., 86.44% on PTBXL-Super, 80.50% on PTBXL-Sub). Under distribution shift evaluation, SGERA achieves the best average AUC of 83.34%, surpassing DERI (81.78%) and MELP (79.58%), with particularly strong gains on CPSC2018→CSN transfer (90.65% vs. 79.50% for DERI).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SGERA achieves the best average zero-shot AUC (79.5%) over all four multi-modal baselines, with the largest margins on PTBXL-Form (+2.2 over MELP) and CPSC2018 (+1.2 over DERI). However, SGERA trails D-BETA on PTBXL-Rhythm (83.3 vs. 88.6) and MELP on PTBXL-Sub (79.5 vs. 81.2), reflecting a design trade-off favoring generalizability over dataset-specific optimization. In linear probing at 1% data, SGERA leads on PTBXL-Super (86.44 vs. 85.82 MELP) and PTBXL-Form (66.21 vs. 63.41 MELP), while DERI remains competitive at 100% on PTBXL-Rhythm (92.34 vs. 93.68 SGERA). Under distribution shift, SGERA's advantage is most pronounced on CPSC2018→CSN (90.65 vs. 81.02 DERI, +9.63) and PTBXL-Super→CSN (86.26 vs. 81.02 DERI), while DERI slightly edges SGERA on PTBXL-Super→CPSC2018 (88.78 vs. 83.08). Among single-modal SSL baselines (SimCLR, BYOL, MoCo-v3, etc.), SGERA's multi-modal pretraining yields substantially higher AUC across all settings, with gaps exceeding 10–20 points in most cases.

## Relation to Prior Work

SGERA builds directly on the CLIP-style ECG-report alignment paradigm established by MERL (Liu et al., 2024), adopting its dual-encoder pipeline (1D ResNet18 + Med-CPT), CKEPE prompts, and experimental protocol, while replacing the InfoNCE contrastive objective with a Stein-kernel-based dual-level alignment. The theoretical foundation draws on Stein Variational Gradient Descent (Liu & Wang, 2016) and kernelized Stein discrepancies, repurposing score-based distributional matching from Bayesian inference to cross-modal representation learning. The key conceptual departure from prior ECG-report alignment methods (MERL, DERI, MELP, D-BETA) is shifting the alignment objective from point-wise instance similarity to joint instance-level and distribution-level kernel matching, motivated by the inherent heterogeneity between continuous ECG signals and discrete clinical text.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine limitation in multi-modal ECG alignment—CLIP-style objectives implicitly assume distributional compatibility between modalities that is violated in practice—and provides a theoretically grounded alternative with consistent (if modest) gains across zero-shot, linear probing, and domain shift settings. The extensibility analysis showing that the Stein loss improves existing baselines (MERL, DERI) when plugged in suggests the alignment objective itself is transferable beyond SGERA's specific architecture. However, the practical gains are small (0.5–1.5% AUC over the best baselines), the method introduces additional complexity (score networks, adaptive bandwidth, reparameterization), and the authors acknowledge that fine-grained intra-modal feature modeling remains unaddressed. Open questions include whether the distribution-level Stein alignment generalizes to other heterogeneous medical modality pairs and whether the gains persist at larger model scales.
