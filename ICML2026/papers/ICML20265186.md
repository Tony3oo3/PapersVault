## Problem

While MLLMs have achieved strong performance on high-level visual reasoning tasks, their fundamental ability to perceive geometric shapes and spatial relationships—termed geometric perception—remains unexplored and unevaluated. Existing geometry benchmarks (GeoQA, Geometry3K, UniGeo, GeoMM) target mathematical reasoning such as numeric calculations and proof generation, implicitly assuming perceptual skills that are never directly tested. This leaves a critical gap: no systematic benchmark exists to measure whether MLLMs can even recognize shapes, count them, locate them, or discern their spatial relationships.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265186/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each evaluation instance pairs a 640×640 geometric figure image (rendered via Matplotlib, optionally with Gaussian background noise, salt-and-pepper outline disturbance, or Perlin noise at 50% probability) with a multiple-choice question text following a fixed prompt template. The model must output a single option letter (A/B/C/D), parsed by extracting the last standalone letter in the response. Questions span six aspects—existence, counting, location, size, reference, and relationship—and are split into easy (≤4 shapes, no noise) and hard (>4 shapes, with noise) levels, yielding 80K figures and 285K questions total.

## Method

GePBench is built on a data synthesis engine that first generates structured textual descriptions by sampling from a pool of 15 geometric shape types and 11 spatial relationships, enforcing plausibility constraints (e.g., minimum area, axis-ratio thresholds, intersection limits) via rejection sampling. These descriptions are rendered into images with Matplotlib, and template-based pipelines then construct multiple-choice QA pairs with carefully designed plausible distractors (e.g., ±1 for counting, ±0.15 for size, adjacent quadrants for location). The paper evaluates 32 MLLMs zero-shot and additionally proposes LLaVA-GeP, a model trained on 300K GePBench-generated samples using a two-stage procedure (MLP alignment on image-caption pairs, then joint instruction tuning on QA pairs), demonstrating that geometric perception training transfers to downstream tasks including medical VQA, chart understanding, and document comprehension with 2.1–2.2% average gains.

## Results

On GePBench, the best-performing model Qwen3.5-397B-A17B achieves 79.2% average accuracy, followed by Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B (78.8%) and Gemini-2.5-Pro (78.6%), while humans reach 99.3%. Size estimation is the most challenging aspect: GPT-5.2 scores only 29.0% on easy-size and GPT-4o just 16.1%, far below the 99.2% human baseline. Location is the second-hardest aspect, with even the strongest open-source model (Qwen3.5-397B-A17B) reaching only 65.3% on easy-location versus 98.4% for humans. Specialized geometric/reasoning models (G-LLaVA, Math-LLaVA, Math-PUMA, QVQ-preview) all underperform their general-purpose base models on GePBench.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among closed-source models, Gemini-2.5-Pro (78.6% avg) outperforms GPT-5.2 (71.0%) and GPT-4o (63.2%), with the gap most pronounced on size estimation (73.1% vs. 29.0% vs. 16.1% on easy-size). Among open-source models, Qwen3.5-397B-A17B (79.2%) and Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B (78.8%) lead, surpassing all closed-source models except matching Gemini-2.5-Pro, while smaller models (≤9B) generally fall below 70% average. Specialized geometric/reasoning models consistently underperform their general-purpose counterparts: G-LLaVA-7B (26.5%) is far below LLaVA-1.5-7B (37.1%), Math-PUMA-7B (44.3%) trails Qwen2-VL-7B (58.6%), and QVQ-preview-72B (57.0%) lags behind Qwen2-VL-72B (65.3%). The LLaVA-GeP model trained with GePBench data shows downstream improvements of +2.1% on medical image analysis and +2.2% on chart/document understanding over the LLaVA-1.5-7B baseline. Across all 32 evaluated models, size and location remain the weakest aspects, with most models scoring near the 25% random-guessing baseline on these tasks, highlighting a fundamental spatial-perception gap that no current architecture has resolved.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the LLaVA architecture (Liu et al., CVPR 2024) as its model foundation and positions itself in deliberate contrast to prior geometric reasoning benchmarks (GeoQA, Geometry3K, UniGeo, geomVerse-V0, GeoMM, MAVIS-Instruct), which target higher-order mathematical tasks like numeric calculation and proof generation. The key conceptual departure is isolating *geometric perception*—shape recognition, spatial localization, size estimation, and relationship identification—as a distinct, foundational capability that prior benchmarks only implicitly assume. The data synthesis pipeline (structured description → Matplotlib rendering → template-based QA) draws on synthetic data generation techniques but applies them to perceptual rather than reasoning tasks, and the downstream transfer experiments connect to the broader instruction-tuning literature for MLLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that even GPT-5.2 and Qwen3-VL-235B achieve near-random accuracy (29% and 54.9%) on basic size estimation reveals a fundamental perceptual gap that undermines the assumption that scaling and general visual training automatically yield spatial understanding. The demonstration that geometric perception training transfers to medical imaging, chart understanding, and document comprehension (2.1–2.2% gains) suggests this is a bottleneck for real-world MLLM deployment, not just an academic curiosity. Open questions include how to reconcile the invariance properties that make modern visual encoders robust for general recognition with the spatial sensitivity needed for geometric perception, and whether the near-random size/location performance reflects a fundamental architectural limitation of transformer-based positional encodings or a data/training gap that can be closed with targeted pretraining.
