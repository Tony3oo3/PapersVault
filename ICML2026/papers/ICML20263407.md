## Problem

LLM post-training methods like RLHF and RLVR depend heavily on human-labeled preferences or external verifiers, which are expensive to collect and inapplicable to non-verifiable domains. The paper asks whether models can self-improve using only intrinsic signals derived from their own outputs, without any external supervision, larger models, or ground-truth data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263407/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a prompt x (and optionally a user-specific context c for personalization) and self-generates contrastive preference pairs: a chosen response sampled from the reference policy conditioned on the correct prompt/context, and a rejected response sampled from the reference policy conditioned on a random or missing prompt/context. No human annotations, verifiers, or external labels are required at any stage. The output is a DPO-trained policy that produces text responses, evaluated via LLM-judge win rates on personalization tasks and exact-match accuracy on math/MCQ benchmarks.

## Method

MIPO constructs contrastive data augmentation pairs by generating a positive response from π_ref(y|x) and a negative from π_ref(y|x') with x'≠x (or, for personalization, from π_ref(y|x) or π_ref(y|x,c') versus π_ref(y|x,c)), then trains with standard DPO. The key theoretical insight is that DPO's implicit reward is equivalent to the InfoNCE objective, whose optimal critic is the pointwise mutual information log π_ref(y|x)/π_ref(y) under the reference model, so DPO on these self-generated pairs maximizes mutual information between prompts and responses. For personalization, the objective shifts to maximizing conditional mutual information I(Y;C|X), encouraging the policy to attend to user-specific context rather than generic prompt information. The reward is static (defined under π_ref rather than the learned policy π_θ), which the authors argue prevents reward hacking and is empirically validated by ablations showing PPO-based on-policy MI underperforms. The entire procedure reduces to a simple data-preprocessing step before standard DPO training, requiring no new loss function or architecture.

## Results

On personalization tasks (Table 1), MIPO achieves 3–51% win-rate gains over personalized prompting baselines across five 1–7B Llama and Qwen models, with the largest improvement being +35.3 points on Multi-Bench for Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct (74.60 vs. 39.29). On verifiable reasoning benchmarks (Table 2), MIPO yields 1–4% average accuracy gains over instruction-tuned baselines without any ground-truth or verifier signal, reaching +7.5 average points for Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct (45.25 vs. 37.72) and occasionally matching or exceeding RLVR with access to privileged answers (e.g., Qwen2.5-3B: MIPO 87.86 vs. RLVR 81.45). MIPO also improves output diversity, reducing Self-BLEU-4 by 0.01–0.05 across all models compared to SFT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MIPO outperforms SAMI (Fränken et al., 2024) on most model-dataset combinations in personalization, e.g., 93.67 vs. 91.17 on CA for Llama-1B and 74.60 vs. 64.69 on MB for Qwen-1.5B, though SAMI is competitive on Llama-3B (94.84 vs. 94.84 on MB). MIPO also beats the extended InfoNCE baseline (van den Oord et al., 2019) across nearly all settings, with the gap widening for smaller models (e.g., 74.60 vs. 59.52 on MB for Qwen-1.5B). Against RLAIF (Lee et al., 2024), MIPO wins decisively at the 1B scale (93.67 vs. 79.83 on CA for Llama-1B) where the LLM-as-judge critic is unreliable, but RLAIF is competitive or slightly ahead for larger 7B models. On verifiable reasoning tasks (Table 2), MIPO without any ground-truth signal matches or exceeds RLVR (Guo et al., 2025) in several cases: Qwen2.5-3B achieves 87.86 average vs. RLVR's 81.45, and Llama-3.2-3B reaches 76.15 vs. RLVR's 74.35, while RLVR retains an edge for Llama-1B (47.55 vs. 45.25). MIPO consistently and substantially outperforms plain SFT on reasoning benchmarks, particularly for smaller models (e.g., +7.5 average for Llama-1B vs. SFT's −2.2), confirming that the contrastive signal provides meaningful learning beyond mere supervised fine-tuning on self-generated data.

## Relation to Prior Work

MIPO connects two previously separate threads: DPO's implicit reward structure and InfoNCE's optimal critic, showing that DPO trained on contrastive pairs (correct-prompt responses vs. random-prompt responses) maximizes pointwise mutual information under the reference policy. This positions the method as a data-augmentation analogue to computer vision techniques (rotation, translation) applied to LLM post-training, requiring no external supervision. It departs from SAMI (Fränken et al., 2024), which also uses InfoNCE but is restricted to decoupled prompt-constitution settings, and from prior DPO data-construction work (Yao et al., 2024; Geng et al., 2025) that still relies on larger models or ground-truth labels to guarantee chosen-over-rejected quality. The key conceptual departure is using the *reference* policy's mutual information as a fixed reward rather than the learned policy's, which the authors argue reduces reward hacking.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that LLMs can self-improve through an intrinsic contrastive signal without human labels, verifiers, or larger teacher models, addressing the 'fossil fuel' bottleneck in post-training. The practical simplicity (a data-preprocessing step before standard DPO) makes it easy to adopt, and the gains for small models (up to 20% on math, 51% on personalization) are particularly relevant for resource-constrained deployment. Key limitations include reliance on LLM judges for personalization evaluation, use of GPT-4o to extract user contexts from real datasets, and the observation that learning curves plateau around 6–7k steps, suggesting self-improvement saturates. Open questions include whether the fixed reference-policy MI remains optimal as the learned policy diverges substantially, and whether gains persist on harder, less-saturated benchmarks.
