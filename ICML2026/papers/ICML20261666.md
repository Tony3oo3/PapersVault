## Problem

Existing interactive segmentation methods that employ focus views for local refinement either use a two-stage crop-and-refine pipeline (computationally expensive) or a fixed-size focus window (e.g., FCFI's 0.3 ratio), which fails to adapt to object geometry and causes detail leakage for thin, irregular, or clustered objects. FCFI additionally suffers from no local refinement on the first click, incomplete feature-only affinity for feedback correction, and unstable convolution-based feedback integration.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a sequence of positive/negative clicks (encoded as a 2-channel disk map concatenated with the image) and produces a binary segmentation mask at full resolution. Uniquely, it also outputs a dynamic focus view as a tight bounding box derived from the max-connected component of a predicted erroneous mask, which adapts per-click to the spatial extent of misclassified pixels. In the one-click scenario, the focus view expands to cover the entire object, effectively converting the predictor into an instance segmentation task.

## Method

EFPNet is a one-stage framework built on FCFI with three core modules. (1) A Focus View Predictor (FVP) uses a lightweight decoder (three Xception blocks + classifier conv) over backbone features fused with a patch-embedded encoding of the previous feedback and new click to predict an erroneous mask; the focus view is the bounding box of its max-connected component, supervised by focal loss. (2) Triple-Affinity Correction (TAC) replaces FCFI's single feature affinity with a weighted combination of pixel affinity (image-level color/texture distance), feature affinity (cosine similarity in backbone space), and mask affinity (distance in prediction space with a 0.5-center penalty), all computed within the predicted focus view to refine the previous feedback. (3) Clicked-with-Focus Integration (CFI) comprises Inter-Focus Contrastive Learning, which enforces cosine-similarity constraints across historical click focus features to preserve temporal consistency, and Intra-Focus Attention, a SAM-style decoder where a learnable mask token and click embeddings attend over decoder features with a log-masked attention bias that restricts each click's perception to its focus window while the token retains global access.

## Results

On the four standard benchmarks (GrabCut, Berkeley, DAVIS, SBD), EFPNet with ViT-B backbone trained on COCO+LVIS achieves NoC%85 of 1.34/1.30/3.39/3.14 and NoC%90 of 1.42/1.87/4.83/5.27 respectively, outperforming its direct predecessor FCFI (HRNet-18s, C+L) by 0.06–0.30 NoC across all datasets. On SBD, EFPNet (ViT-B) reduces NoC%90 from 6.24 (FCFI) to 5.27, a 15.5% relative improvement. The method also achieves the best FIoU (focus view IoU) of 0.673 on Berkeley and 0.539 on SBD, far exceeding FCFI's 0.114 and 0.106, confirming superior dynamic focus prediction. Efficiency is maintained with only 0.041 s/click (ViT-B) vs. 0.037 s/click for MFP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EFPNet consistently outperforms its direct predecessor FCFI across all four datasets and both backbone configurations: with HRNet-18s on C+L, it reduces NoC%90 by 0.12–0.30 (e.g., SBD 5.94 vs. 6.24, DAVIS 5.02 vs. 5.16). Against the strongest no-refinement baselines, EFPNet (ViT-B, C+L) beats MFP on Berkeley (1.87 vs. 1.90 NoC%90) and SBD (5.27 vs. 5.34), and beats SegNext by a large margin on SBD (5.27 vs. 6.37 NoC%90) and DAVIS (4.83 vs. 5.46). Compared to two-stage methods, EFPNet surpasses FocalClick (MiT-B0, C+L) on all four datasets (e.g., SBD 5.27 vs. 6.86 NoC%90) while being significantly faster (0.041 vs. 0.38 s/click). The only close contest is with MFP on DAVIS, where MFP edges ahead by 0.02 NoC%85 (3.37 vs. 3.39) and 0.02 NoC%90 (4.81 vs. 4.83), but EFPNet recovers the lead on all other datasets. On the SAM-style HQSeg44K benchmark, EFPNet achieves 91.87 5-mIoU and 5.32 NoC%90 at 1024², matching or exceeding HRSAM++ and Inter2Former while using fewer clicks at 2048² resolution (4.29 NoC%90 vs. 4.57 and 4.33).

## Relation to Prior Work

EFPNet is a direct extension of FCFI (Wei et al., 2023), the current SOTA one-stage local refinement method for click-based interactive segmentation. It departs from FCFI's fixed 0.3×0.3 focus scope by introducing a learnable Focus View Predictor that dynamically determines the refinement region from an end-to-end erroneous-mask prediction, and replaces FCFI's single feature-affinity correction with a triple-affinity scheme (pixel, feature, mask). The Clicked-with-Focus Integration module further departs from FCFI's convolution-based fusion by incorporating inter-focus contrastive learning and focus-oriented attention, drawing design inspiration from SAM's decoder architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dynamic focus view addresses a practical limitation of fixed-scope refinement (detail leakage on thin/irregular objects) without incurring the multi-pass inference cost of two-stage methods like FocusCut, making it a useful middle ground for real-time interactive segmentation. However, the gains over FCFI are modest (0.2–0.4 NoC), and the method remains restricted to click prompts; whether the focus-view-prediction paradigm transfers to scribble, box, or text prompts is unexplored. An open question is whether the learned focus view can be further exploited for video segmentation or multi-object scenarios where temporal consistency of the focus region matters.
