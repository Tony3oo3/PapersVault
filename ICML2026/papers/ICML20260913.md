## Problem

Reinforcement learning for vision-language models provides only outcome-level rewards, so effective self-correction behavior (wrong→correct transitions within a single response) emerges extremely rarely—under 0.3% of rollouts even with explicit prompting. This sparsity makes it impossible to learn self-correction as a controllable, triggerable capability, and existing reward-shaping approaches (e.g., SRPO) induce reward hacking and mode collapse when jointly optimizing self-correction with direct reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260913/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives an RGB image paired with a text question and produces a single-pass reasoning trajectory in the structured format o₁ ⊕ <self-correction> ⊕ o₂, where o₁ is the initial chain-of-thought with a boxed answer, and o₂ is a complete, self-contained re-derivation after the correction token. The <sc> token is a controllable trigger: at inference time, additional <sc> tokens can be appended to force further correction rounds (test-time scaling), progressively improving accuracy without re-prompting. Inference is capped at 16,384 tokens for reasoning models, and the final answer is extracted from \boxed{}.

## Method

Octopus is a rollout augmentation framework that synthesizes dense self-correction training signals by recombining components of standard RL rollouts. After a cold-start SFT stage (using mixed sampling where o₁ comes from the policy model and o₂ from a stronger 30B model to avoid entropy collapse), the method generates n=8 rollouts in the o₁⊕<sc>⊕o₂ format per input, then cross-pairs o₁ from one rollout with o₂ from another to produce n²=64 augmented samples, from which 16 balanced positive/negative examples are selected for each policy update. Training uses a two-stage response-masking strategy built on GSPO: Stage I masks all tokens in o₁ (treating it as fixed context with KL regularization) so the policy learns only the corrective response o₂; Stage II selectively unmasks o₁ only for samples whose correctness is unchanged before and after <sc>, avoiding gradient conflicts between reinforcing a wrong initial answer and rewarding the correction. This design simultaneously improves sample efficiency (0.72× training time per step vs. GSPO with 16 rollouts) and stabilizes optimization through balanced advantage distributions.

## Results

Octopus-8B achieves an average accuracy of 71.7 across 7 benchmarks (MathVista, MathVerse, WeMath, HallusionBench, MMStar, MMMU, CharXiv), outperforming the base Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct by 9.5 points and the official reasoning-enhanced Qwen3-VL-8B-Thinking by 1.2 points. It surpasses the strongest RLVR baseline (GSPO with n=16 rollouts) by 1.0 average accuracy while requiring only 0.72× the per-step training time. On individual benchmarks, Octopus-8B reaches 82.1 on MathVista, 84.0 on WeMath, and 75.2 on MMStar, setting new state-of-the-art among open-source VLMs of comparable size.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Octopus-8B outperforms all open-source baselines: it beats Qwen3-VL-8B-Thinking (70.5) by 1.2 points, MiMo-VL-7B-RL (68.0) by 3.7 points, and InternVL3.5-8B-RL (63.0) by 8.7 points on average. Among RLVR baselines initialized from the same Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct backbone, Octopus surpasses GSPO (n=16) by 1.0 average accuracy and DAPO (n=16, 70.5) by 1.2 points, while also exceeding GRPO and the self-correction-specific SRPO baseline across all evaluated tasks. Against closed-source models, Octopus-8B (71.7) substantially outperforms GPT-4o (60.5) and Claude-3.7-Sonnet (63.9), though OpenAI-o1 achieves higher scores on math benchmarks (e.g., 98.7 on WeMath) with partial evaluation. In terms of efficiency, Octopus achieves its gains with only 8 generated rollouts augmented to 16, requiring 0.72× the per-step training time of GSPO with n=16, and its inference latency (166 s) and token count (1063) on MathVista are comparable to all baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RLVR paradigm (GRPO, DAPO, GSPO) by introducing a rollout augmentation mechanism specifically designed to synthesize dense self-correction training signals from existing rollouts, departing from the outcome-level reward structure that leaves self-correction to emerge implicitly. Its closest predecessor, SRPO (Wan et al., 2025), also targets single-pass self-correction in VLMs via RL but relies on prompting and shaped rewards, leaving effective correction examples sparse (<1%) and susceptible to reward hacking. Octopus addresses both issues: it recombines pre- and post-correction segments across rollouts to create n² structured examples at zero additional generation cost, and it introduces a response-masking two-stage schedule that decouples self-correction learning from direct reasoning to avoid the gradient conflicts that cause SRPO's mode collapse.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The rollout-augmentation trick is a practical, low-cost technique that any RLVR pipeline could adopt to enrich structured supervision signals without extra inference, and the 0.72× per-step training time reduction makes it attractive for practitioners training VLMs at scale. The response-masking strategy identifies and resolves a concrete optimization conflict (reward hacking under shaped rewards) that likely affects other multi-behavior RL settings beyond self-correction. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond 8B models, whether the single-pass <sc> formulation generalizes to multi-step or tool-augmented correction, and whether the text-only transfer observed on AIME/MMLU-Pro extends to genuinely novel reasoning domains.
