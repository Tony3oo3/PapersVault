## Problem

Existing methods for enforcing hard constraints on neural network outputs either provide no satisfaction guarantee (soft/penalty-based), require iterative post-processing (asymptotic only), or assume the constraint matrix A(x) has full row rank, limiting the number of constraints to the output dimension. No prior closed-form framework handles an arbitrary number of input-dependent affine inequality constraints with linearly dependent or redundant rows while preserving the universal approximation property of the underlying network.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an input vector x ∈ R^{n_in} together with input-dependent affine constraint functions A(x) ∈ R^{m×n_out} and b(x) ∈ R^m, where m is unrestricted relative to n_out and A(x) need not be full rank. It produces a single output vector y = P*(x) ∈ R^{n_out} that is provably feasible (A(x)y ≤ b(x)) for every x. Equality constraints are encoded as paired inequalities, and the constraint functions can be arbitrary continuous mappings of x, making the feasible region vary with the input.

## Method

CAffNet inserts a trainable CAffine layer after an unconstrained backbone network (FFNN or transformer) that outputs f_θ(x). The layer first decomposes the full constraint set into sub-constraints of at most n_out rows by enumerating combinations corresponding to minimal faces of the feasible polyhedron. For each sub-constraint (A_γ, b_γ), it computes a closed-form projection via the Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse: P_γ(x) = f_θ(x) − A_γ†(A_γ f_θ(x) − b_γ) + (I − A_γ†A_γ)w_φ(x), where w_φ(x) is a learned null-space vector that selects an optimal point when the projection is non-unique. The final output is the feasible candidate closest to f_θ(x) in p-norm, or f_θ(x) itself if already feasible. This construction requires no full-rank assumption, handles linearly dependent constraints, and provably preserves universal approximation while guaranteeing zero constraint violations for all inputs.

## Results

On the piecewise-constrained function learning task, CAffNet-TF achieves the lowest MSE of 0.0012, reducing it by 73.33% compared to the soft-constrained NN (MSE 0.0045), while both CAffNet variants achieve zero constraint violations (max and mean = 0.0000). In the optimization solver experiment, CAffNet-FF attains an objective value of −0.3433, nearly matching the IPOPT benchmark (−0.3494), with zero inequality and equality violations, whereas HardNet violates 99.98% of equality constraints. In the safety-critical unicycle control task, CAffNet-FF achieves zero constraint violations across the entire trajectory, while both NN and HardNet collide with obstacles and incur 2.60% inequality violations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares CAffNet against two baselines: a standard feedforward NN with soft (penalty-based) constraints and HardNet (Min & Azizan, 2025), the strongest prior hard-constraint method. CAffNet decisively outperforms both on constraint satisfaction: it achieves zero violations across all three experiments, while the soft-constrained NN and HardNet both exhibit nonzero violations (e.g., HardNet violates 99.98% of equality constraints in the optimization solver task and 2.60% of inequality constraints in the control task). On approximation quality, CAffNet-TF reduces MSE by 73.33% over the soft-constrained NN on the piecewise function task, and CAffNet-FF achieves an objective value within 1.7% of the IPOPT benchmark on the optimization solver task. The trade-off is computational: CAffNet variants require roughly 2–3× more training time and 2–10× more inference time than the baselines due to the constraint decomposition and pseudoinverse computations, though the CAffNet-Lite variant recovers ~45% of that overhead with no loss in constraint satisfaction or accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

CAffNet directly extends HardNet (Min & Azizan, 2025), which projects NN outputs into safe regions for input-dependent affine constraints but requires A(x) to have full row rank, limiting constraint cardinality and failing on linearly dependent constraints. The key departure is a constraint decomposition strategy grounded in polyhedral minimal-face theory (Schrijver, 1998) combined with a trainable null-space component via Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse, enabling closed-form projection for an arbitrary number of constraints without rank assumptions. The paper also positions itself against iterative methods (DC3, FSNet) that offer only asymptotic feasibility, and against equality-only methods (POLICE, KKT-hPINN, ACnet), establishing a strictly broader guarantee class.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing provable hard constraint satisfaction for arbitrary-cardinality input-dependent affine constraints while preserving universal approximation, CAffNet removes a practical barrier to deploying learned controllers in safety-critical settings where constraint violations are unacceptable. The unicycle control experiment demonstrates that joint training with the CAffine layer is essential—post-hoc projection causes the robot to get stuck—highlighting that the architecture must be co-optimized with the network. Open questions include scalability to large constraint sets (the full combination set is exponential in m), extension beyond affine constraints, and validation on physical robotic platforms, all acknowledged by the authors as future work.
