## Problem

Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) expose their intermediate chain-of-thought traces, creating a new attack surface that static adversarial suffixes and prompt-injection methods cannot effectively exploit. Existing CoT-based jailbreak approaches (e.g., H-CoT) rely on fixed reasoning templates, which suffer from limited diversity, poor adaptability across models, and constrained effectiveness because they cannot dynamically explore the combinatorial space of reasoning structures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262543/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single harmful goal string (e.g., 'How to sabotage oil and gas pipelines') as input and produces a fully assembled adversarial prompt P = M + S + C, where M is a teacher-style rewritten prompt, S is a fixed structural suffix enforcing a Scheme/Implementation/Functional Example/Rationale format, and C is an evolved CoT suffix rendered from a 9-dimensional parameter vector. The evolved CoT suffix is parameterized over a structured search space Θ with up to 50 discrete options per dimension (scheme type, implementation steps, context scenario, thinking style, etc.), and the final prompt is issued to the target LLM under deterministic decoding (temperature=0, max 4096 tokens) to elicit a policy-violating response scored on a 0–5 harmfulness scale by a judge LLM.

## Method

AE-CoT reformulates jailbreaking as a black-box evolutionary optimization over a structured 9-dimensional CoT search space Θ (scheme type, scheme details, implementation steps, context scenario, example actions, rationale benefits, rationale necessities, thinking style, key nouns), where each dimension holds up to 50 domain-informed options derived from decomposing H-CoT traces. A population of N=10 candidate CoT suffixes is evolved over up to 3 generations using tournament selection (τ=3), fragment-level crossover (probability 0.5), and a key innovation: an adaptive mutation-rate schedule that decreases μ by 0.1 (floor 0.1) when generational best fitness improves and increases μ by 0.1 (ceiling 0.3) when it stagnates, dynamically balancing exploration and exploitation. Fitness is evaluated by a judge LLM scoring the target model's response on a discrete [0,5] harmfulness scale, with deterministic re-verification of score-5 candidates to suppress false positives. Top-performing evolved CoTs are then integrated into a harmful CoT amplification template to produce the final adversarial prompt, and the entire pipeline is preceded by a teacher-style rewriting step that recasts the raw harmful goal into a neutral pedagogical framing to bypass initial refusal filters.

## Results

On the AdvBench 50-behavior subset, AE-CoT achieves the highest Attack Success Rate (ASR) and Harmfulness Score (HS) across five of six reasoning-capable models: 92%/70.4 on o1-mini, 88%/72.0 on o3-mini, 54%/71.6 on GPT-5, 96%/78.8 on DeepSeek-R1, 96%/80.0 on Qwen3, and 96%/82.8 on Gemini-2.5-Flash-Thinking. On the Malicious-Educator benchmark, AE-CoT reaches 100% ASR on o1-mini, DeepSeek-R1, and Gemini-2.5, with HS up to 93.6. In transfer experiments to non-reasoning models, the Grok-3-evolved prompt achieves 98–100% ASR on GPT-4o, Gemini-2.5, GPT-3.5-turbo, and DeepSeek-v3.1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AE-CoT outperforms all six baselines on the majority of model–metric pairs. Against the closest CoT-based competitor H-CoT, AE-CoT gains the largest margins on o1-mini (92% vs. 54% ASR) and GPT-5 (54% vs. 36% ASR), while the gap narrows on o3-mini (88% vs. 86%) and DeepSeek-R1 (96% vs. 92%). Against CodeAttack, AE-CoT wins decisively on o1-mini (92% vs. 54%), o3-mini (88% vs. 56%), and GPT-5 (54% vs. 2%), ties on DeepSeek-R1 (96% vs. 96%), and leads on Qwen3 (96% vs. 94%) and Gemini-2.5 (96% vs. 24%). Against ICRT, AE-CoT dominates on o1-mini (92% vs. 74%), o3-mini (88% vs. 10%), and GPT-5 (54% vs. 16%), with smaller margins on DeepSeek-R1 (96% vs. 86%) and Qwen3 (96% vs. 78%). CL-GSO, PAP, and ArtPrompt are consistently outperformed by wide margins across all models, with ArtPrompt and PAP achieving near-zero ASR on GPT-5 and Gemini-2.5. In the transfer setting (Table 3), AE-CoT's Grok-3 seed achieves 98–100% ASR on non-reasoning models, surpassing H-CoT's transferred prompts (84–98%) on GPT-4o, Gemini-2.5, Qwen3-235B, and DeepSeek-v3.1.

## Relation to Prior Work

AE-CoT extends H-CoT (Kuo et al., 2025), which jailbreaks reasoning models via static CoT templates, by replacing fixed templates with a structured 9-dimensional search space explored through genetic-algorithm operators (fragment-level crossover and adaptive mutation). The teacher-style rewriting step adapts persona modulation from Shah et al. (2023) to reduce immediate refusals before the evolutionary search begins. More broadly, the paper sits at the intersection of evolutionary prompt optimization (AutoDAN, GrIPS, APE) and the emerging line of LRM-specific attacks (Jinx, BoT, ExtendAttack, Response Attack), but is the first to combine structured CoT decomposition with adaptive evolutionary search specifically targeting the intermediate reasoning space of LRMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the CoT reasoning trace itself—not just the final output—is a meaningful attack surface for large reasoning models, and that surface-level defenses (length limiting, low temperature, safety reminders, output filtering) reduce ASR only from 100% to 60% even when combined. This points toward a need for process-level defenses that monitor or verify intermediate reasoning steps rather than relying solely on final-output filtering, a direction the authors explicitly call out as open. Limitations include the search space being bootstrapped from H-CoT traces (raising a mild circularity concern), a maximum of only 3 evolutionary generations, and reliance on LLM judges (partially mitigated by human evaluation). The method's practical relevance is high for red-teaming teams evaluating reasoning-capable models before deployment.
