## Problem

Current LLM watermarking research assumes adversaries access only a single watermarked model, but in practice users can freely query 10+ frontier LLMs with independent watermark keys. This paper reveals that this multi-provider reality is the Achilles' heel of watermarking: because watermark perturbations are independent across providers, simply averaging output probability distributions cancels the watermark signal and recovers the unwatermarked distribution, rendering detection statistically impossible.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265018/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and parallel access to N (typically 3–5) independently watermarked LLMs that may use different tokenizers and vocabularies. It produces autoregressively generated text whose token-level statistics are indistinguishable from unwatermarked output (z-scores below the detection threshold of 4). A key operational detail is that the ensemble distribution is computed over the vocabulary union of all models, and a decode-encode re-synchronisation cycle is applied after each routed span so that all models maintain semantically equivalent contexts despite tokenizer differences.

## Method

WASH (Watermark Attenuation via Statistical Hybridisation) combines a theoretical guarantee with a practical generation algorithm. Theoretically, the paper proves that under bounded, independent, zero-mean perturbations, the uniform average of N watermarked distributions converges to the consensus distribution at rate O(1/√N) with a second-order bias term controlled by the perturbation variance. Practically, WASH handles the vocabulary mismatch problem through three mechanisms: (1) a union-based ensemble that assigns zero probability to tokens absent from a model's vocabulary, (2) fluency-aware routing that, when a sampled token falls outside the shared vocabulary intersection, restricts subsequent generation to 'specialist' models whose vocabularies admit that token, using random (not max-likelihood) selection to avoid watermark-correlated biases, and (3) context re-synchronisation via decode-encode cycles that re-tokenise the generated string in each model's native tokenizer. The entire pipeline runs in constant time per token via parallel inference with synchronised KV caches, achieving roughly 6× speedup over prior removal baselines while improving generation quality by 27.5%.

## Results

WASH suppresses watermark detection z-scores from 5–300 (strongly detectable) to below 2 (below the detection threshold of 4) using only 3 independent models, and reduces TPR@5%FPR on native sequence detectors to below 50%. On generation quality, WASH(N=5) improves GSM8K accuracy by up to 27.5% over the watermarked baseline (e.g., 0.698 vs. 0.511 on Llama3.1-8B) while running at only 1.0×–2.4× the baseline inference cost, roughly 6× faster than the best competing removal method. A control experiment with coordinated watermarks confirms that the removal mechanism specifically exploits independence across providers, as coordinated signals persist at z > 4 even under ensembling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against De-mark (Chen et al., 2025), WASH achieves comparable or better z-scores across all six watermark schemes, while De-mark only functions on red-green-list methods (KGW, Water-Bag) and fails catastrophically on distribution-modifying schemes (z = 99.3 on DIPMark for Llama). WASH also runs 15–30× faster than De-mark (2.3× vs. 38.8× baseline cost on GSM8K). Against ToBlend (Huang et al., 2024), WASH matches detection efficacy (both achieve z < 4 with N=3) but substantially outperforms on quality: on GSM8K (Llama), WASH(N=5) reaches 0.698 vs. ToBlend's 0.568, and on SQuAD 0.764 vs. 0.633, while running 6× faster (2.28× vs. 14.6× baseline). Against RandomWalk (Liu et al., 2025) on final-text detection, WASH(N=5) outperforms on 4 of 5 watermark schemes in TPR@5%FPR (e.g., 16.2 vs. 53.6 on ITS-Edit) and improves GSM8K accuracy from 0.467 to 0.698 while halving the runtime overhead (2.28× vs. 4.39×).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the LLM ensemble paradigm (Yu et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2025b) as a watermark removal mechanism, departing from prior attacks that either probe a single model iteratively (De-Mark, Chen et al., 2025a), concatenate tokens across models with context re-encoding (ToBlend, Huang et al., 2024), or rewrite final text (RandomWalk, Liu et al., 2025). Theoretically, it builds on the unbiased watermarking framework (Hu et al., 2024; Kuditipudi et al., 2024) but inverts its purpose: rather than proving watermarks are detectable, it proves they are asymptotically erasable under linear averaging. The fluency-aware routing component addresses the vocabulary-mismatch problem identified in heterogeneous ensemble literature, but applies it specifically to preserve watermark-neutral generation rather than to improve ensemble accuracy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that three independently watermarked models suffice to suppress detection z-scores below threshold challenges the foundational assumption underpinning the entire LLM watermarking literature: that a single provider's watermark is robust to user-side manipulation. The control experiment showing that coordinated watermarks resist the attack highlights that the vulnerability is structural (market independence) rather than algorithmic, pointing toward industry-wide key standardisation as the only viable defence—a requirement with significant antitrust and competitive implications. Open questions include whether the result extends to non-autoregressive or multimodal generation, whether the zero-mean perturbation assumption holds for all practical watermark schemes at scale, and whether the 27.5% quality improvement from ensembling generalises beyond the 8B parameter regime tested here.
