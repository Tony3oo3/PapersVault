## Problem

Primal-dual safe RL methods suffer from a dual-lag effect: Lagrange multipliers update slowly, causing unsafe behavior when they are small and excessive conservatism when they grow large, producing oscillatory dynamics near the safety boundary. Moreover, existing methods apply uniform constraint corrections regardless of local constraint steepness, leading to overshooting in high-sensitivity regions and slow recovery in flat regions. CSPO addresses both issues by incorporating local first-order constraint sensitivity directly into the policy update.

## Input / Output

The method consumes state observations from continuous-control environments (Safety Gymnasium locomotion and navigation tasks) and outputs actions sampled from a parameterized stochastic policy π_θ(a|s) implemented as a two-layer tanh network. A distinguishing feature is the sensitivity weight w_k = 1/(‖∇_θ ĝ(θ_k)‖² + ε), computed once per episodic iteration via automatic differentiation of the PPO-style surrogate constraint, then detached and held constant during all inner-loop policy updates. The algorithm also maintains a scalar Lagrange multiplier λ updated via projected gradient ascent on the constraint violation.

## Method

CSPO augments the standard PPO-Lag primal-dual objective with a constraint-sensitive correction term q_k(θ) = (α/2)·w_k·[g(θ)]_+², where w_k = 1/(‖∇_θ g(θ_k)‖² + ε) is the inverse squared constraint-gradient norm. This term is derived from the shortest signed distance to the linearized safety boundary in parameter space: in steep regions (large ‖∇g‖) the correction is small to prevent overshooting, while in flat regions (small ‖∇g‖) it is stronger to accelerate feasibility recovery. The correction activates only when g(θ) > 0, so the effective multiplier becomes λ_eff = λ + α·w_k·[g(θ)]_+, strengthening constraint enforcement during violations while letting λ adapt smoothly for long-term satisfaction. The paper proves that CSPO preserves the KKT solutions of the original constrained problem, establishes an O(L³G²λ_max²/ε⁶) convergence rate for the nonconvex-concave min-max formulation, and shows that one primal update guarantees constraint decrease whenever the violation exceeds a threshold proportional to 1/α.

## Results

On 9 Safety Gymnasium continuous-control tasks (5 locomotion, 4 navigation, all with cost threshold 25), CSPO achieves the best constrained IQM return among methods satisfying the cost limit on 8 of 9 environments. Gains are most pronounced in navigation: CSPO reaches 10.15 on PointButton (vs. 9.05 PPO-Lag, 9.01 APPO) and 23.79 on PointGoal (vs. 21.78 PPO-Lag, 23.35 IPO which violates the constraint). On locomotion, CSPO leads on Ant (3231.6 vs. 3190.0 CPPO-PID), Humanoid (6496.85 vs. 6417.05 CPPO-PID), Hopper (1691.92 vs. 1661.60 CUP), and Swimmer (91.40 vs. 68.20 C-TRPO), while FOCOPS (2870.70) and CPPO-PID (2797.52) outperform CSPO (2007.90) on HalfCheetah. CSPO also achieves the lowest Time-To-Safety (TTS) on 7 of 9 tasks (e.g., 3.1 epochs on PointGoal vs. 7.1 for PPO-Lag) with near-perfect Reward Preservation (RP ≈ 1.00).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CSPO outperforms all 11 baselines on constrained return in 8 of 9 environments (losing only to FOCOPS/CPPO-PID on HalfCheetah). Against primal-dual methods, CSPO beats PPO-Lag by large margins on navigation (e.g., +1.10 on PointButton, +2.01 on PointGoal) and locomotion (e.g., +97.4 on Ant, +82.3 on Humanoid), while also achieving lower TTS (3.1 vs. 7.1 epochs on PointGoal). Against augmented Lagrangian APPO, CSPO gains +270 on Ant, +404 on Humanoid, and +1.14 on PointButton. Against penalty methods, CSPO dominates P3O (e.g., +717 on Ant, +5.99 on PointButton) and IPO (which frequently violates constraints, e.g., cost 62.0 on Humanoid, 34.7 on CarButton). Against second-order trust-region methods, CSPO outperforms CPO on 7/9 tasks (CPO violates constraints on Hopper and PointGoal), PCPO on all 9 tasks (PCPO is weakest overall, e.g., 1728.7 on Ant vs. CSPO's 3231.6), and C-TRPO on 7/9 tasks. Against projection-based methods FOCOPS and CUP, CSPO wins on 8/9 and 9/9 tasks respectively, with the sole HalfCheetah exception where FOCOPS achieves 2870.7 vs. CSPO's 2007.9. Against CPPO-PID, CSPO matches or exceeds on 8/9 tasks while achieving substantially lower TTS (e.g., 3.1 vs. 6.5 on PointGoal, 2.3 vs. 9.5 on Hopper).

## Relation to Prior Work

CSPO extends the PPO-Lag primal-dual framework (Ray et al., 2019) by adding a constraint-sensitive correction term that scales the feasibility enforcement by the inverse squared norm of the constraint gradient, drawing on the geometric insight that the shortest signed distance to the safety boundary depends on local constraint steepness. It departs from uniform-correction primal-dual methods (PPO-Lag, CPPO-PID, FOCOPS, CUP) and fixed-penalty approaches (P3O, IPO, EPO) by adapting correction strength to local constraint geometry, while avoiding the second-order Fisher inversions required by trust-region methods (CPO, PCPO, C-TRPO). The method is positioned as a first-order alternative that preserves KKT equivalence with the original CMDP, unlike projection-based methods that can alter the feasible set.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CSPO addresses the well-known dual-lag oscillation problem in primal-dual safe RL with a minimal, interpretable modification (one extra gradient-norm evaluation per epoch), making it a practical drop-in improvement for existing PPO-based safe RL pipelines. The introduction of TTS, RP, and VF metrics provides a more granular safety-recovery evaluation beyond average cost satisfaction. Open questions include robustness to noisy or sparse cost signals, the task-dependent choice of α (though an adaptive schedule is proposed), and whether the Euclidean-norm sensitivity scaling generalizes to curvature-aware metrics like the Fisher information. The method's reliance on accurate cost-gradient estimates remains a limitation in discontinuous or high-variance environments.
