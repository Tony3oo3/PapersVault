## Problem

Revocable parallel diffusion decoding accelerates dLLM inference by drafting multiple tokens per step and rechecking a subset of previously unmasked positions, but existing verification schemes (WINO, Saber) frequently trigger flip-flop oscillations where tokens are remasked and later restored unchanged. This wastes computation in two coupled ways: remasking replaces content-bearing embeddings with [MASK], weakening the conditioning context for parallel drafting, and each ineffective remask consumes the unmask budget merely to restore the same token, reducing net denoising progress under any fixed step budget.

## Input / Output

COVER operates on a text prompt X and a partially masked token sequence Y^(t) of fixed length L (256 or 512 tokens), processed in semi-autoregressive blocks of 32–64 tokens generated left-to-right. At each denoising step it consumes the current partial state plus a small seed set of previously unmasked positions, and outputs an updated partial state where newly drafted tokens are committed and verified seeds are either kept, replaced, or (rarely) remasked. Decoding terminates when no [MASK] tokens remain or an end-of-sequence token is produced, yielding a fully generated text sequence in substantially fewer steps than one-token-per-step greedy decoding.

## Method

COVER performs leave-one-out verification and stable drafting within a single forward pass via KV cache override: selected seed positions are masked in the input, but their cached key-value states from the previous step are injected into the memory columns so that all non-seed queries continue to condition on an unchanged context. A closed-form diagonal correction then restores the diagonal column at each seed to the masked-input KV and applies a row-wise softmax rescaling (derived from a single-score-change lemma) to prevent self-leakage, yielding a faithful verification distribution without an extra attention pass. Seed selection uses a stability-aware score combining token uncertainty, downstream attention influence on still-masked positions, and a draft-sensitivity penalty (d_out) that discourages reusing cached states likely to drift after the current draft. The verification budget adapts per step via the empirical CDF of scores, and a three-way KEEP/REPLACE/REMASK rule suppresses ineffective flip-flop cycles, reducing total revisions by over 95% compared to Saber while preserving or improving accuracy across four dLLMs and four benchmarks.

## Results

Across four dLLMs (LLaDA-Base-8B, LLaDA-Ins-8B, LLaDA-1.5-8B, Dream-Ins-7B) and four benchmarks, COVER generally preserves or improves accuracy while reducing decoding steps by 2–12×. The most dramatic speedup is 11.64× on Dream-Ins-7B/MBPP (length 512), where COVER achieves 57.60% pass@1 (vs. 57.20% baseline) in only 35.84 steps. On GSM8K with Dream-Ins-7B at length 512, COVER reaches 79.30% accuracy (vs. 76.88% baseline) with an 8.48× speedup. Effective revision ratios jump from <1% (Saber) and ~3–11% (WINO) to 72–79% under COVER, confirming that most prior remask operations were ineffective flip-flops.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Saber, COVER wins on both accuracy and speed in nearly all settings. Saber frequently degrades quality (e.g., Dream-Ins-7B GSM8K drops from 76.88% to 72.10% at length 512; LLaDA-Base-8B HumanEval drops from 33.54% to 32.93%), while COVER consistently matches or exceeds the greedy baseline. Saber's speed advantage is also smaller than COVER's in most configurations (e.g., 1.24× vs. 5.91× on Dream-Ins-7B/HumanEval-512). Against WINO, COVER achieves comparable or better accuracy while delivering substantially higher speedups, particularly on code tasks (11.64× vs. 7.82× on Dream-Ins-7B/MBPP-512). WINO's auxiliary shadow block inflates sequence length and memory, capping its speedup; COVER's in-place KV override avoids this overhead. On flip-flop statistics, COVER's effective revision ratio (72–79%) dwarfs Saber's (<1.5%) and WINO's (2–12%), confirming that COVER's verification actions are far more productive per revision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two active threads in dLLM acceleration: revocable parallel decoding (WINO's shadow-block verification, Saber's confidence-drop remasking) and KV-cache reuse across diffusion steps (Fast-dLLM). It departs from both by identifying that the explicit remasking at the core of WINO and Saber causes flip-flop oscillations—tokens are remasked and then restored unchanged—wasting the revision budget and degrading the drafting context. COVER repurposes the KV-cache concept from Fast-dLLM not merely for step-level amortisation but as a verification mechanism: cached key-value states are injected into the attention memory so that non-seed queries see an unchanged context while seed positions receive a faithful leave-one-out re-prediction via a closed-form diagonal correction, all within a single forward pass.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The empirical finding that roughly 99% of Saber's and ~90% of WINO's remask operations are ineffective flip-flops reframes the revocable-decoding literature: the verification step that was supposed to improve quality is, in practice, almost pure overhead. COVER's context-preserving in-place verification eliminates this waste while delivering up to 11.64× end-to-end speedup (Dream-Ins-7B, MBPP-512) with equal or better accuracy, and the method is training-free and model-agnostic. Open questions include whether the approach extends to continuous or non-absorbing diffusion formulations, how it behaves at much longer generation lengths where KV drift may accumulate, and whether the stability-aware seed selection generalises to architectures without standard multi-head attention.
