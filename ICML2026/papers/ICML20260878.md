## Problem

Matrix-based preconditioned optimizers like MUON capture rich curvature information for neural network training, but their Newton-Schulz orthogonalization step incurs O(mn·min(m,n)) per-iteration cost that becomes a dominant bottleneck as model dimensions grow. The paper asks whether this preconditioning overhead can be reduced to O(mn) without sacrificing the matrix-level adaptivity that distinguishes these methods from diagonal optimizers like AdamW.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260878/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each iteration, RMNP consumes the current parameter matrix W ∈ R^{m×n}, a stochastic gradient estimate G_t, and a momentum state V_t (updated via V_t = βV_{t-1} + (1−β)G_t). It produces a preconditioned update direction D_t whose rows are individually ℓ2-normalized (each row has unit norm), which is then subtracted from W scaled by the learning rate. In practice, RMNP is applied only to matrix parameters in a mixed-update strategy, with AdamW handling embeddings, biases, and other non-matrix parameters.

## Method

RMNP replaces MUON's Newton-Schulz iteration with a single row-wise ℓ2 normalization, motivated by the empirically observed row-wise block-diagonal dominance of Transformer layerwise Hessians. Formally, the MUON preconditioner (V_t V_t^T)^{1/2} ⊗ I_n is approximated by retaining only its diagonal blocks, yielding diag(V_t V_t^T)^{1/2} ⊗ I_n, which algebraically reduces to dividing each row of V_t by its own ℓ2 norm. This drops the per-iteration complexity from O(mn·min(m,n)) to O(mn) while preserving matrix-level adaptivity. The paper empirically validates the diagonal-dominance hypothesis across GPT-2 and LLaMA scales (r_min consistently above 1, r_avg exceeding 5), and establishes non-convex convergence guarantees matching MUON's best-known results: O(m²L_F σ²Δε⁻⁴) under Frobenius smoothness and an improved O(mL_{∞,2} σ²Δε⁻⁴) under ‖·‖_{∞,2}-smoothness, both achieving minimax optimality.

## Results

RMNP achieves a 13–44× speedup over MUON in preconditioning wall-clock time across GPT-2 scales from 60M to 1.5B parameters (e.g., 1.48 s → 0.115 s at 60M; 36.65 s → 0.855 s at 1.5B per 100 steps). In terms of optimization quality, RMNP attains the lowest final validation perplexity in every experimental cell: on GPT-2/OpenWebText it improves over MUON by 0.04–0.24 and over AdamW by 0.84–1.49; on LLaMA/C4 it improves over MUON by 0.02–0.63 and over AdamW by 1.10–4.33. The per-iteration preconditioning complexity drops from O(mn·min(m,n)) to O(mn).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MUON (the primary baseline), RMNP consistently achieves lower final validation perplexity across all GPT-2 and LLaMA scales (improvements of 0.02–0.63 in perplexity) while reducing preconditioning wall-clock time by 13–44×, meaning RMNP wins on both quality and speed simultaneously. Against AdamW, RMNP delivers substantially larger perplexity gains (0.84–4.33 lower perplexity on GPT-2; 1.10–4.33 on LLaMA), confirming that the matrix-level row normalization captures curvature information that diagonal AdamW cannot. The speed advantage over MUON grows with model size (12.9× at 60M to 44.3× at 770M), indicating that Newton-Schulz orthogonalization becomes an increasingly dominant bottleneck at scale, whereas RMNP's O(mn) row normalization remains lightweight. RMNP also offers a practical distributed-training advantage: its row normalization requires only a complete row per device, enabling column-wise sharding under PyTorch FSDP2 without cross-device synchronization, unlike MUON's all-gather requirement.

## Relation to Prior Work

RMNP directly extends MUON (Jordan et al., 2024) by replacing its Newton-Schulz orthogonalization step with a row-wise ℓ₂ normalization, motivated by the empirically observed block-diagonal dominance of Transformer layerwise Hessians (Zhang et al., 2024; Dong et al., 2025). Conceptually, it sits at the intersection of the structured-preconditioner lineage (K-FAC, SHAMPOO, SOAP) and the recent wave of row/column-normalization optimizers (SRON, SCALE, SWAN, NORMUON), but departs from the steepest-descent/LMO analysis framework used by most of those works by grounding its design in the concrete curvature geometry of neural networks rather than worst-case norm-constrained descent. The theoretical analysis parallels and matches recent MUON convergence results (Chang et al., 2025; Kim & Oh, 2026), establishing RMNP as a lower-complexity variant within the same geometric framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 13–44× reduction in preconditioning wall-clock time (scaling with model size) makes matrix-based adaptive optimization substantially more practical for large-scale LLM training, where Newton-Schulz iteration becomes a dominant bottleneck. The empirical validation of diagonal dominance across GPT-2 and LLaMA at multiple scales provides strong evidence that the simplification is not architecture-specific. Open questions include whether the block-diagonal dominance assumption holds for non-Transformer architectures (e.g., Mamba, state-space models) and whether the row-normalization approximation degrades at extreme scale or with different initialization schemes. The compatibility with column-wise FSDP sharding (no all-gather needed) is a practical advantage for distributed training that MUON lacks.
