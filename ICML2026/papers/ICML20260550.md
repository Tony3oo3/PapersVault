## Problem

Adapting large pretrained text-to-video diffusion transformers to real-world video super-resolution faces two fundamental mismatches: the generative training objective conflicts with the restoration objective, and the VAE encoders in these models are optimized for perceptual synthesis rather than precise reconstruction, systematically suppressing high-frequency information. Additionally, complex real-world degradations (compression, motion blur, noise, aliasing) break the assumptions embedded in generative priors, causing artifacts and compromised fidelity.

## Input / Output

The method takes low-resolution video clips (8 consecutive frames at 256×256 to 768×768 during training) suffering from complex real-world degradations and produces temporally stable high-resolution video. The pipeline operates in a latent space with 4× temporal and 8× spatial compression and 16 latent channels (matching WAN 2.1's VAE configuration). LR training data are synthesized via the Real-ESRGAN degradation pipeline, while evaluation covers both synthetic degradations and genuinely captured real-world low-quality video.

## Method

WEVSR adapts the WAN 2.1 flow-matching video DiT to VSR through three components. First, a multi-level discrete wavelet transform (DWT) front-end is prepended to the VAE encoder: at each downsampling level, the input is decomposed into LL/LH/HL/HH subbands, the high-frequency subbands are channel-concatenated with the downsampled features and projected via a 1×1 convolution, while LL recurses into the next DWT level—injecting explicit frequency priors without altering the frozen decoder. Second, a fidelity-aware condition controller reshapes the flow-matching dynamics by (a) injecting degradation-aware noise perturbations into the latent space and (b) biasing the HR timestep sampling toward the central region (normal distribution) while sampling the LR timestep uniformly from an interval determined by the PSNR/CLIP-IQA trade-off intersection, controlling the fidelity–generation balance. Third, a two-stage training strategy first aligns the wavelet-enhanced encoder to the pretrained latent distribution (50k iterations, DiT and decoder frozen), then fine-tunes the DiT for restoration-specific flow dynamics (300k iterations, VAE frozen), with a combined loss of rectified-flow supervision and frozen-decoder reconstruction.

## Results

On synthetic benchmarks, WEVSR achieves the best SSIM, LPIPS, and DISTS across all three datasets (e.g., SSIM 0.845, LPIPS 0.130, DISTS 0.070 on UDM10), and the best PSNR on UDM10 (28.10) and YouHQ40 (23.67). On real-world benchmarks, it attains the best DOVER on all three datasets (8.234 on VideoLQ, 14.07 on RealVSR, 8.731 on AIGC30) and the best MUSIQ and CLIP-IQA on RealVSR (75.690, 0.568) and AIGC30 (60.092, 0.414). The wavelet-enhanced VAE also achieves the highest PSNR (33.65) and SSIM (0.9203) in video reconstruction, surpassing the base WAN VAE (33.40, 0.9189) with the same 127M parameter budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest transformer-based baseline RealViFormer, WEVSR wins on SSIM, LPIPS, DISTS across all synthetic datasets and on DOVER across all real-world datasets, but concedes PSNR on SPMCS (23.82 vs. 24.185) and MUSIQ/CLIP-IQA on VideoLQ (54.13 vs. 54.65; 0.356 vs. 0.411). Among diffusion-based VSR methods, WEVSR dominates: it outperforms SeedVR2-7B by 1.84 dB PSNR on UDM10 (28.10 vs. 26.26) and by 2.15 DOVER points on RealVSR (14.07 vs. 12.34), while STAR and Upscale-A-Video trail by large margins on nearly every metric (e.g., STAR scores only 40.50 MUSIQ on VideoLQ vs. 54.13 for WEVSR). MGLD-VSR is the closest competitor on NIQE for RealVSR (3.627 vs. 3.792) but is surpassed on all other real-world metrics. In the VAE reconstruction comparison (Table 2), the wavelet-enhanced encoder improves PSNR from 33.40 (WAN) to 33.65 and SSIM from 0.9189 to 0.9203 with identical 127M parameters, while achieving a lower rFVD (2.96) than WAN (3.38) and approaching SeedVR's 1.76 despite using fewer than half the parameters (127M vs. 251M).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the pretrained WAN 2.1 video diffusion transformer (a flow-matching DiT) to real-world video super-resolution, departing from prior diffusion-based VSR methods (SeedVR, STAR, Upscale-A-Video) that typically train restoration-specific diffusion models from scratch or rely on heavy retraining. The wavelet-enhanced VAE encoder is motivated by FreqWarm's finding that VAE encoders systematically suppress high-frequency latent signals, and the fidelity-aware condition controller (timestep sampling + noise augmentation) reshapes the flow-matching trajectory to favor restoration over generation. The two-stage training strategy—first aligning the wavelet encoder to the pretrained decoder's latent distribution, then fine-tuning the DiT—positions this as a lightweight adaptation of a generative prior rather than a new architecture from the ground up.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in demonstrating that a 1.5B-parameter model using only 24 GB VRAM can match or exceed 8.2B-parameter, 72 GB baselines (SeedVR/SeedVR2) on both fidelity and perceptual metrics, making diffusion-based VSR substantially more accessible for real-world deployment. The wavelet-enhanced VAE encoder is a drop-in, decoder-compatible improvement that could benefit other latent video generation tasks beyond VSR. Open questions remain around generalization to extreme or unseen degradations (the paper acknowledges reliance on synthetic Real-ESRGAN-style training data), real-time streaming inference, and whether the fidelity-aware timestep strategy transfers to other restoration tasks like denoising or deblurring.
