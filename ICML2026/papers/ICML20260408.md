## Problem

Progress in neural combinatorial optimization for Dynamic Flexible Job Shop Scheduling (DFJSP) is hindered by a methodological tension: static benchmarks (Taillard, DMU) encourage overfitting to fixed instance structures, while uncalibrated procedural generators introduce uncontrolled stochastic noise that makes it impossible to disentangle genuine algorithmic gains from favorable random event sequences. This evaluation gap prevents reliable benchmarking of emerging LLM-based scheduling agents against established solvers, and no principled mechanism exists to control instance difficulty and distribution for dynamic scheduling.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a high-level input configuration tuple (plant structure with machines/groups/templates, stochastic distribution parameters, a target metric vector specifying utilization, SCV, tightness, disturbance, and bottleneck targets, and dynamic scenario probabilities) and produces calibrated discrete event streams whose realized metrics match the targets within tolerance. For LLM agent evaluation, the simulator exposes immutable state snapshots at each decision epoch, encoded at three observability levels (L1: local machine status; L2: adds statistical counters; L3: adds full structural priors), and the agent outputs a dispatch action selecting an operation-machine pair. The SSI maps realized metrics to a scalar 0–100 difficulty score, and the evaluation engine reports normalized makespan relative to the best observed schedule per instance.

## Method

The core technical contribution is the Sequential Event-Space Calibrator (SESC), which operates directly on the realized discrete-event stream rather than optimizing generative hyperparameters. SESC applies a greedy search over a catalog of four structural transformation operators (arrival-structure adjustment, adaptive slack scaling, isomorphic processing-time resampling, and bottleneck engineering), selecting at each step the operator that most reduces a normalized multi-metric error vector while penalizing degradation of already-converged metrics via an asymmetric weighting function. Difficulty is quantified by the Schedule Stress Index (SSI), a four-component vector (congestion via Kingman-style heavy-traffic approximation, time-pressure as inverse slack, structural complexity, and disruption intensity) that is log-compressed and averaged into a 0–100 scalar score. The framework wraps a discrete-event simulation kernel in a Gymnasium-compatible interface with snapshot-based state isolation, supporting both memoryless and lookahead agent policies. LLM agents are evaluated under matched protocols across seven model families using direct, Chain-of-Thought, tool-augmented, reflection, and best-of-n strategies, revealing an 'Observability Paradox' where full structural priors (L3) degrade performance relative to concise statistical summaries (L2).

## Results

On the DynaSched-Subset (70 calibrated instances), LLM-based scheduling agents cluster in a narrow band of 1.01%–1.93% mean normalized makespan (C_max) relative to the best observed schedule, with Qwen3-8B achieving the best result at 1.01% (SE 0.22%). The best fixed heuristic (LIFO+LIT) achieves 1.11% (SE 0.15%), and paired tests confirm Qwen3-8B is statistically indistinguishable from it (Δ = −0.099 pp, 95% CI [−0.531, 0.389], FDR p = 0.397). The SESC calibrator achieves a median L₂ calibration error of 0.0539 in 0.2 s, versus 0.0826 in 57.9 s for MOO and 0.0595 in 185.8 s for Hybrid, with 3–6× lower seed-to-seed variance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All ten LLM agents (Qwen3-4B/8B/14B/32B, Kimi-K2, Claude-4.5 Haiku, DeepSeek-V3.2, Gemini-2.5 FlashLite, Grok-4 Fast, GPT-5 Mini) fall within a 1.01%–1.93% mean C_max band, matching but never exceeding the best fixed heuristic (LIFO+LIT at 1.11%). Qwen3-8B (1.01%) is the strongest LLM and is statistically indistinguishable from LIFO+LIT (paired Δ = −0.099 pp, p = 0.397), while all LLMs significantly outperform the weakest PDR (SPT+SPT at ~50.5% gap, p < 0.001). The paper further reveals an 'Observability Paradox': providing full structural priors (L3 CoT, 1.66% mean) degrades performance relative to concise statistical summaries (L2 CoT, 0.65%), and tool-augmented exploration (L1+Tool, 2.03%) incurs a 3× token cost over L1 CoT (1.95%) while performing worse. For the calibrator, SESC dominates both MOO and Hybrid baselines on accuracy (median L₂ 0.0539 vs. 0.0826 and 0.0595), runtime (0.2 s vs. 57.9 s and 185.8 s), and seed-level stability (std L₂ 0.0236 vs. 0.1532 and 0.0562), confirming that direct event-space manipulation outperforms parameter-space multi-objective optimization for calibrated instance generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of three established lines: (1) static scheduling benchmarks (Taillard, DMU) that the authors argue are fundamentally misaligned with dynamic stochastic environments; (2) DRL-based dispatching policies (L2D, ScheduleNet, DAN, PPO variants) that suffer from overfitting to fixed instance sets; and (3) LLM reasoning frameworks (CoT, ReAct, Reflexion, tool-augmented prompting) recently applied to combinatorial optimization. The key departure is methodological: rather than sampling instance parameters and accepting uncontrolled difficulty, the paper introduces direct event-space manipulation (SESC) and a theoretically grounded difficulty metric (SSI, motivated by Kingman's heavy-traffic approximation) to calibrate instances to prescribed stress profiles. The LLM evaluation then repurposes standard prompting and tool-use paradigms within a Gymnasium-style discrete-event simulation loop, but the central empirical contribution—the Observability Paradox—is new to the scheduling literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The calibrated benchmark addresses a genuine evaluation gap: without controlled difficulty, it is impossible to disentangle algorithmic progress from stochastic luck in dynamic scheduling. The Observability Paradox (L3 structural priors underperform L2 statistical summaries) is a practical design warning for anyone building LLM-based decision agents in high-dimensional state spaces, suggesting that information curation matters more than information volume. However, the finding is demonstrated on 70 instances with a fixed set of 2025-era LLMs, and the paper itself notes that the inability of LLMs to exceed strong PDRs may reflect the reactive single-step dispatching paradigm rather than a fundamental LLM limitation—multi-step trajectory planning remains an open direction. The SESC's ~100× speedup over NSGA-II-based calibration is practically useful but the broader question of whether calibrated benchmarks change which scheduling algorithms are deemed 'best' remains to be tested by the community.
