## Problem

Standard covariance estimation theory assumes i.i.d. samples, but in practice MCMC produces dependent iterates for which naive concentration arguments fail (the estimator may never concentrate without additional structure). The paper asks: under what verifiable conditions on the target distribution and the Markov kernel can one obtain non-asymptotic sample and query complexity guarantees for mean and covariance estimation from a single Markov chain, and what concrete improvements does this yield over i.i.d.-based estimators?

## Input / Output

The method takes a reversible Markov kernel P with stationary distribution π satisfying a Poincaré inequality (PI) and spectral gap λ ≥ 0.99, along with an initial warm-start distribution π₀ (or a warm point for the convex-body application). For the convex-body application, input is a membership oracle for K; for the unconstrained application, oracles for V, ∇V, and a proximal oracle. The output is a covariance estimate Σ̂ and mean estimate μ̂ from N retained chain iterates, or in the rounding application, an affine map A(x) = Σ̂^{-1/2}(Tx − μ̂) that isotropizes the body. Complexity is tracked in both sample count N and total query count N_tot (including burn-in and per-step oracle calls).

## Method

The core result (Theorem 3.4) shows that the estimator Σ̄ = (1/N)Σ(X_i − X̄)⊗2 satisfies |Σ̄ − Σ| ≼ εΣ + δI_d with high probability when N = Õ((trΣ + C_PI)/(εδ)), matching i.i.d. sample complexity up to logs. The proof decomposes the error into three terms: a bounded-matrix concentration term handled by the Markov-dependent matrix Bernstein inequality of Neeman et al. (2024), and two tail terms controlled via the sub-exponential one-sample concentration implied by PI combined with Markov's inequality on the empirical average. The key practical consequence is a factor-d reduction in query complexity: the warm-start cost is paid once, and subsequent dependent samples from the same chain are far cheaper than restarting i.i.d. sampling. This is instantiated for the In-and-Out sampler on convex bodies (yielding Õ(d³) queries for isotropic rounding vs. Õ(d⁴) for i.i.d.) and for the Proximal sampler on unconstrained log-concave targets (yielding Õ(κd^{3/2}/ε²) vs. Õ(κd^{5/2}/ε²)).

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 3.4) shows that under a Poincaré inequality and spectral gap, the MCMC covariance estimator achieves |Σ̄ − Σ| ≼ εΣ + δI_d with N = Õ((trΣ + C_PI)/(εδ)) samples. In the convex body rounding application (Lemma 4.6), a well-rounded body is isotropized using Õ(C⁴d³) expected membership queries, improving over the ~d⁴ queries required by i.i.d.-based approaches. For unconstrained sampling (Theorem 4.14), the multiplicative covariance estimate ‖Σ̂ − Σ‖ ≤ ε‖Σ‖ is achieved with Õ(κd^{3/2}φ/ε²) queries, where κ = β·C_PI is the condition number and φ = (trΣ + C_PI)/(trΣ).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is between MCMC-based covariance estimation and the standard i.i.d.-sample-based approach. In the convex body rounding setting, the MCMC approach achieves Õ(d³) expected membership queries versus ~d⁴ for i.i.d. samples, a factor-of-d improvement that comes from paying the warm-start cost once and then drawing dependent samples from a single chain. In unconstrained sampling, the MCMC query complexity is Õ(κd^{3/2}φ/ε²) versus Õ(κd^{5/2}φ/ε²) for i.i.d. samples, again a factor-of-d gain. The paper also compares its analysis framework to Jia et al. (2021), showing that the same Õ(d³) rounding bound follows from a general Markovian covariance estimation theorem with a transparent spectral-gap + Poincaré-constant dependence, avoiding the ad-hoc μ-independence (α-mixing) and boosting arguments used in the prior work. The sample complexity (number of retained samples N) is the same in both MCMC and i.i.d. settings; the improvement is purely in query complexity (oracle calls per sample).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate threads: the matrix Bernstein inequality for Markov-dependent random matrices (Neeman et al., 2024) and the Poincaré-inequality-based concentration framework for log-concave targets. Prior covariance estimation theory (Kannan et al., 1997; Adamczak et al., 2010) assumed i.i.d. samples, while MCMC convergence theory focused on mixing rather than statistical estimation from dependent iterates. The paper departs from Jia et al. (2021) by replacing their ad-hoc α-mixing and boosting arguments for isotropic rounding with a clean spectral-gap + PI analysis, and from the unconstrained sampling literature by showing that a single warm-started chain yields a d-factor query-complexity improvement over restarting for each i.i.d. sample.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This provides the first non-asymptotic justification for the standard MCMC practice of running one long chain after burn-in and thinning for posterior covariance estimation, under verifiable functional-inequality assumptions. The d-factor query-complexity improvement is practically significant for high-dimensional convex body rounding (a key subroutine in volume estimation and constrained sampling) and for Bayesian inference with expensive oracle evaluations. Open questions include closing the gap between the polynomial tail decay obtained here (via Markov's inequality on dependent samples) and the subexponential decay available in the i.i.d. setting, establishing matching lower bounds, and extending to discrete-state or generalized Dirichlet-form settings.
