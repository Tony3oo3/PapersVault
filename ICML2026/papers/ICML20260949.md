## Problem

Recent methods that use frozen Vision Foundation Model (VFM) features directly as latents for diffusion transformers suffer from high dimensionality and redundancy: a standard ViT-B/16 encoder produces 768-channel features whose total element count equals the original image, contradicting the core LDM assumption of operating on a compact representation. This redundancy slows DiT convergence significantly, as the model must learn a distribution dominated by low-variance, semantically unimportant dimensions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a class label (ImageNet-1K, 1000 classes) and Gaussian noise as input, producing a 256×256 RGB image. The input image is first encoded by a frozen DINOv3-B/16 backbone (not a trainable VAE), and the final output is generated through a two-stage decode: a RePack decoder produces a semantically coherent base image from the 32-channel latent, followed by a single-step U-Net Refiner that restores high-frequency textures conditioned on both the base image and the upsampled latent (35 input channels total).

## Method

The framework operates in three stages. First, a bias-free linear projection (0.04M parameters) compresses 768-dimensional frozen DINOv3 features into a 32-channel compact manifold, justified by PCA showing ~77% of variance concentrated in the first 32 principal components; this RePack module is trained end-to-end with a composite loss (L1, LPIPS, adversarial, Watson perceptual, and Focal Frequency). Second, a LightningDiT-XL/1 backbone is trained via rectified flow (velocity prediction) on the packed 32-channel latents, achieving FID 1.82 in 64 epochs. Third, a Latent-Guided Refiner (U-Net with skip connections and attention at 32×32) is trained offline to restore high-frequency details by taking the channel-concatenation of the decoded base image and bilinearly upsampled latent as input, improving FID to 1.65. The key design choice is decoupling semantic structure generation (in the compact latent space) from texture synthesis (in pixel space), with the bias-free projection ensuring pure subspace rotation without distribution shift.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K 256×256, RePack-DiT-XL/1 achieves a gFID of 1.82 after only 64 training epochs, compared to 1,400 epochs (FID 2.27) for the original DiT-XL. With the Latent-Guided Refiner, the gFID further improves to 1.65 (250-step sampling) or 1.77 (as reported in Table 1), with sFID of 4.35–4.42 and IS of 268.9–273.8. The RePack encoder adds only 0.04M trainable parameters (a single bias-free linear projection), while the full VAE pipeline totals 127.13M parameters (182.71M with the Refiner).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RePack's primary advantage is convergence speed: at 64 epochs it achieves gFID 1.82, outperforming VA-VAE (2.11 at 64 ep), FAE (2.01 at 64 ep), and the original DiT-XL (2.27 at 1,400 ep) by 0.29–0.45 FID with 22× fewer epochs than DiT. Against raw-feature methods, RePack (1.82 at 64 ep) beats SVG-XL (2.10 at 500 ep) and RAE-DiT-XL (1.41 at 800 ep, but with 415M VAE params vs. RePack's 127M). With the Refiner, RePack+Refine reaches FID 1.65–1.77 at 64 epochs, surpassing DeCo (1.69 at 600 ep) and approaching PixelDiT (1.61 at 320 ep) with 5–9× fewer training epochs. In the unguided setting, RePack achieves gFID 3.81 at 64 ep, outperforming VA-VAE (5.14) and DiT (9.62 at 1,400 ep). The method does not match the absolute best FID of RAE-DiT-DH-XL (1.13 at 800 ep) or REPA (1.42 at 800 ep), but those require 12.5× more training and substantially larger VAE parameter counts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two recent trends in latent diffusion: (1) replacing trainable VAE encoders with frozen Vision Foundation Models (as in RAE, SVG) and (2) using VFM features to accelerate DiT convergence (as in VA-VAE, REPA). It departs from both by arguing that raw 768-dimensional VFM features are fundamentally uncompressed and redundant, and that a trivial bias-free linear projection to 32 channels—motivated by a PCA elbow—yields a cleaner generative target than either raw features or complex alignment losses. The generative backbone (LightningDiT) and decoder are adopted directly from VA-VAE, while the Latent-Guided Refiner borrows the frequency-decoupling philosophy of Pixel Diffusion Models (DeCo, PixelDiT) but implements it as a single-step U-Net rather than an iterative pixel-space diffusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that a 0.04M-parameter linear projection can replace the need for 800–1400-epoch DiT training with 64-epoch training while matching or exceeding FID, which is a substantial compute saving for practitioners iterating on class-conditional generation. However, the evaluation is confined to ImageNet-1K at 256×256 with a maximum of 80 epochs, and the Refiner's benefit does not transfer to other VAEs (VA-VAE, SD-VAE), suggesting the gains are tightly coupled to the specific compact-manifold design. Open questions include whether the compression strategy scales to text-to-image generation, higher resolutions, and whether the 32-channel bottleneck is truly optimal or merely a convenient comparison point (d=16 actually yields better gFID in ablations).
