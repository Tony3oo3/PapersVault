## Problem

Sharp approximation rates for standard softmax-activated Transformers, particularly in the L^∞ norm, remained open: prior results either established only universality without explicit rates or avoided softmax by substituting hardmax or ReLU attention. Additionally, statistical theory for Transformer-based nonparametric regression under temporal dependence (stationary β-mixing observations) was largely unexplored, despite being the natural setting for sequence models.

## Input / Output

The approximation results take a matrix-valued target F: [0,1]^{d_x × n} → R^{d_y × n} whose entries are γ-Hölder or first-order Sobolev smooth, and produce a Transformer network N whose every entry simultaneously approximates the corresponding entry of F in L^p norm for all p ∈ [1,∞]. For regression, the input is a sliding window of n consecutive observations (x_{t-n+1},…,x_t) drawn from a stationary β-mixing process supported on [0,1]^{d_x × n}, and the output is a scalar prediction ŷ_t; the estimator is trained via empirical risk minimization over m such windows.

## Method

The proof strategy is inspired by the Kolmogorov-Arnold representation theorem: the unit cube is uniformly partitioned into K^{d_x n} cells, a piecewise-constant approximation is constructed, and a two-layer Transformer realizes this via a self-attention layer that acts as a contextual mapping (memorizing grid indices) followed by a feed-forward layer that retrieves the corresponding function values. A key observation is that with W^(K)=W^(Q)=O, the softmax self-attention reduces to a column-wise averaging operator, decoupling the approximation argument from softmax-specific properties and extending the results to general attention mechanisms. The horizontal shift technique (iterative mid-operation over coordinates) extends the approximation from the 'good' region to the full domain, yielding the L^∞ endpoint. For regression, the excess risk is decomposed into approximation error (governed by the O(ε^{-d_x n/γ}) parameter count) and statistical error (governed by the VC-dimension, which grows quadratically in N due to exponential operations in softmax), producing explicit rates under geometric, algebraic, and i.i.d. dependence.

## Results

For γ-Hölder sequence-to-sequence targets, the paper establishes that a standard softmax-activated Transformer with O(ε^{-d_x n / γ}) parameters achieves L^p approximation error ≤ 4(d_x n)^2 K_H ε for all p ∈ [1, ∞], including the previously elusive endpoint p = ∞. For Sobolev targets, O(ε^{-d_x n}) parameters suffice. In nonparametric regression under geometric β-mixing dependence, the excess risk rate is Õ(m^{-γ/(γ+d_x n)}), matching the i.i.d. benchmark rate, while under algebraic β-mixing the rate is Õ(m^{-rγ/((r+2)γ+(r+1)d_x n)}), which approaches the i.i.d. rate as the mixing parameter r grows.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central advantage over prior rate results (Jiao et al. 2024a; Havrilla & Liao 2024) is that it achieves the same O(ε^{-d_x n/γ}) width scaling using the standard softmax attention σ_S, whereas those works required replacing softmax with X⊙σ_H(X) or ReLU attention. Additionally, the paper covers the full range p ∈ [1,∞] including p = ∞, while prior rate results were limited to p < ∞. The depth requirement is O(1) (fixed at 2 layers) versus O(log 1/ε) for the competing rate results. Against the universality results of Yun et al. (2020) and Kajitsuka & Sato (2024), the paper provides explicit quantitative rates rather than qualitative convergence guarantees. For the regression component (Table 2), the Transformer rates under geometric β-mixing and i.i.d. settings match the i.i.d. minimax rate m^{-γ/(γ+d_x n)}, while the algebraic β-mixing rate m^{-rγ/((r+2)γ+(r+1)d_x n)} improves monotonically with the mixing parameter r and converges to the i.i.d. rate as r → ∞. The rates are noted to be suboptimal due to a quadratic VC-dimension bound for softmax-based networks (vs. linear for ReLU FNNs), which the authors identify as an open gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Transformer approximation theory line initiated by Yun et al. (2020) and Kajitsuka & Sato (2024), which established L^p universality for p < ∞ but provided no explicit rates, by delivering sharp parameter-count bounds for Hölder and Sobolev targets including the previously inaccessible L^∞ endpoint. The proof strategy draws on the horizontal shift technique from Lu et al. (2021) and the memorization construction from Kajitsuka & Sato (2024), but introduces a Kolmogorov-Arnold-inspired decomposition where self-attention layers reduce to column-wise averaging operators, decoupling the approximation argument from softmax-specific properties. On the regression side, the paper extends the dependent-data nonparametric regression framework of Jiao et al. (2024b) for RNNs and Feng et al. (2023) for FNNs to the standard softmax Transformer architecture, using a sliding-window empirical risk minimization scheme.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The L^∞ approximation guarantee for standard softmax Transformers closes a notable gap in the theory, and the observation that attention effectively acts as a column-wise averaging operator provides a new interpretable lens that extends naturally to beyond-softmax mechanisms (hardmax, linear attention, etc.). However, the regression rates are suboptimal relative to the minimax benchmark because the VC-dimension of softmax-based Transformers grows quadratically in the number of parameters (vs. linearly for ReLU FNNs), and the authors explicitly flag this as an open problem. Further open directions include adaptive rates for higher-order smoothness (the current piecewise-constant discretization hits the classical saturation barrier) and whether Transformers can circumvent the curse of dimensionality under low-intrinsic-dimension or hierarchical structure assumptions, as FNNs can.
