## Problem

Real-world 6D pose estimation and tracking pipelines remain brittle because they depend on curated CAD models, manual masking, or per-object fine-tuning, and they lack any principled mechanism to detect when tracking has silently drifted under occlusion or rapid motion. Existing reference-based trackers use shallow metric learning (e.g., cosine similarity) that fails under non-linear feature distortions, and operate 'blindly' without a built-in tracking-validity signal, making autonomous recovery from failure impossible.

## Input / Output

STORM takes a single reference image (or up to 16 rendered/segmented reference views) and a query image at 512×512 resolution, with an optional cached text descriptor generated once per object by a VLM and encoded by CLIP. It produces three outputs: a dense object segmentation mask from the SOM module, a 6D pose (rotation + translation) via FoundationPose registration on the SAM3D-reconstructed mesh, and a continuous energy-like tracking validity score from TOM that is EMA-smoothed over time and thresholded with a 3-frame persistence rule to trigger re-localization.

## Method

STORM couples two trainable modules on top of frozen foundation components (DINOv3, CLIP, SAM3D, FoundationPose). The Segmenting Object Module (SOM) uses Hierarchical Spatial Fusion Attention (HSFA), which iteratively fuses variable-count reference views via self-attention into a canonical representation, then aligns query features to it through cross-attention (raw features in early layers for global semantics, refined features in deeper layers for local geometry), with optional zero-initialized AdaLN/FiLM conditioning from VLM-derived text embeddings that modulate both query tokens and reference key/value channels. Alignment is learned implicitly through segmentation mask supervision rather than explicit correspondence loss. The Tracking Object Module (TOM) is a lightweight attention-based binary compatibility verifier trained with BCE on synthetically generated positive (same-object) and negative (identity-confusion, drift) crop pairs; at inference its logit is negated into an energy-like score, EMA-smoothed (α=0.9), and tracking loss is declared only when the score exceeds a validation-calibrated threshold for L=3 consecutive frames, triggering SOM re-localization and memory-pool reset.

## Results

On the full-pipeline pose estimation task (Table 1), STORM achieves 74.0 ADD-AUC and 89.0 ADD-S-AUC on LineMOD-Occluded, closing the gap to the ground-truth-mask oracle (78.0/93.0) to within 4%, while outperforming the CNOS-based annotation-free baseline by 17 points in ADD-AUC. On YCB-Video, STORM reaches 77.0/98.0 ADD-AUC/ADD-S-AUC, matching the ground-truth upper bound (78.0/99.0) and exceeding CNOS by 4 points. For the segmentation module alone (Table 2), STORM (SOM) attains a mean AP of 67.7% across five BOP datasets with a single reference view, surpassing the strongest supervised baseline ZebraPoseSAT-EffNetB4 (67.5%) while running 1.7× faster (46 ms vs. 80 ms per frame on an H100).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CNOS (Nguyen et al., 2023), STORM's SOM segmentation module improves mean AP from 47.2% (CNOS+FastSAM) to 67.7%, and the full pipeline lifts ADD-AUC from 57.0 to 74.0 on LM-O (+17 pts) and from 73.0 to 77.0 on YCB-V (+4 pts). Against the supervised ZebraPoseSAT-EffNetB4 (Su et al., 2022), STORM (SOM) edges out the mean AP (67.7% vs. 67.5%) while being 1.7× faster (46 ms vs. 80 ms), and substantially outperforms CosyPose (Labbé et al., 2020; 48.0% AP) and Mask R-CNN (He et al., 2017; 46.2% AP) by roughly 20 points. Against SAM-6D (Lin et al., 2024), STORM (SOM) leads by 14.1 points in mean AP (67.7% vs. 53.6%) and runs 61× faster (46 ms vs. 2795 ms). FoundationPose (Wen et al., 2024) serves as the shared downstream pose estimator across all comparisons; STORM's contribution is the annotation-free mask source and the TOM failure-detection loop, which together narrow the gap to the FP+Ground-Truth oracle from 21 points (CNOS) to 4 points on LM-O and to 1 point on YCB-V.

## Relation to Prior Work

STORM sits at the intersection of reference-conditioned segmentation (CNOS, PerSAM, SAM-6D) and 6D pose tracking (FoundationPose, PVD3D), but departs from both in key ways. Rather than the shallow cosine-similarity matching used by CNOS/PerSAM, it introduces Hierarchical Spatial Fusion Attention (HSFA) as a learned, multi-scale reference-query alignment mechanism with optional VLM/CLIP semantic conditioning. Its most distinctive departure is the Tracking Object Module (TOM), which repurposes the energy-based OOD-detection idea (LeCun et al., Liu et al.) as a learned binary compatibility verifier for tracking drift, filling a gap the authors identify in 'blind' trackers like FoundationPose that lack any built-in failure signal. The pipeline also leverages SAM3D for single-image mesh reconstruction as a CAD-free geometric anchor, and uses frozen DINOv3/CLIP/FoundationPose components with only HSFA and TOM as trainable modules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

STORM addresses a concrete deployment bottleneck: most 6D tracking pipelines require curated CAD models, manual masks, or per-object fine-tuning, making real-world rollout labor-intensive. By closing the gap to ground-truth-mask performance (within ~4% on LM-O) in a fully annotation-free setting and adding explicit drift detection with automatic re-localization, it offers a practical path toward self-sufficient robotic perception. Key open questions remain: the system still depends on SAM3D reconstruction fidelity and a frozen FoundationPose backend, the TOM threshold is calibrated offline (online adaptation is future work), and the 'zero-shot' claim is annotation/adaptation-free rather than category-disjoint. Transparent, reflective, and highly symmetric objects remain under-evaluated, and multi-object tracking and manipulation integration are left for future work.
