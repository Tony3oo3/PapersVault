## Problem

Existing causal abstraction learning methods assume fixed, well-specified exogenous distributions, leaving learned abstractions brittle under environmental shifts and model misspecification. Exact abstractions require consistency under a single environment, while uniform abstractions demand consistency across all environments (computationally intractable). This paper introduces the first class of distributionally robust causal abstractions that enforce interventional consistency across a constrained, relevant subset of environments, bridging the gap between these two extremes.

## Input / Output

The method takes endogenous samples from paired low-level (ℓ-dimensional) and high-level (h-dimensional) SCMs, along with known or estimated structural functions, intervention sets with a surjective order-preserving mapping ω, and causal DAGs. Unobserved exogenous noise is recovered via abduction (residual computation), and the empirical joint environment is formed as a product measure. The output is a linear abstraction matrix T ∈ R^{h×ℓ} mapping low-level to high-level variables, together with the worst-case joint environment identified within the ambiguity set. For the cMNIST setting, robustness is enforced only in the low-level pixel space (ε_h = 0), reflecting the sensory nature of the shifts.

## Method

DiRoCA casts robust causal abstraction learning as a min-max optimization: min_T sup_{ρ ∈ B_{ε,2}(ρ̂)} E_{ι~q}[e_τ^{ρ,ι}], where the supremum is over a 2-Wasserstein product ambiguity set centered at the empirical joint environment. For Additive Noise Models, this reduces to a tractable Frobenius-norm problem: the adversary perturbs noise samples within Frobenius budgets r_d = ε_d√N, and the objective minimizes the worst-case expected squared misalignment ||Z_T^ι(Θ)||_F², solved via alternating projected gradient descent-ascent. Two specialized variants exploit structure: an empirical variant using explicit linear mixing matrices for faster gradients, and a Gaussian variant optimizing over moments via the Gelbrich distance. Theoretical contributions include Wasserstein concentration bounds that provide principled radius selection (ε = O(N^{-1/d})) and a closed-form robustness certificate bounding the worst-case abstraction error for any learned T.

## Results

Under full distributional corruption (α=1), DIROCA with tuned radius (ε*) achieves the lowest abstraction error on both real-world benchmarks: 268.4±141.8 on EBM and 48.2±0.6 on cMNIST, compared to 320.2±148.2 (GRAD) and 357.4±144.9 (BARY) on EBM, and 22200.7±1492.7 (GRAD) and 15348.5±629.4 (BARY) on cMNIST. On the cMNIST geometric rotation task, DIROCA_ε* reduces relative L2 error to 28.0±0.4% (30°) and 30.7±0.7% (90°), an order-of-magnitude improvement over the best baseline AbsLin_n (276.7±6.5% and 287.9±9.3%). On the nonlinear nLUCAS misspecification setting, DIROCA_ε* achieves 9.1±0.3 abstraction error versus 18.7±0.9 for GRAD and 21.4±1.0 for BARY.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIROCA consistently outperforms all baselines under distributional shift. On cMNIST (full corruption), DIROCA_ε* (48.2) reduces abstraction error by ~460× over GRAD(τ,ω) (22200.7) and ~318× over BARY(τ,ω) (15348.5), and by ~10× over the best observational baseline AbsLin_n (470.7). On EBM, DIROCA_ε* (268.4) beats GRAD (320.2) by ~16% and BARY (357.4) by ~25%, while AbsLin variants fail catastrophically (errors >3000). Under structural and intervention-mapping misspecification on nLUCAS, DIROCA_ε* (9.1) halves the error of GRAD (18.7) and BARY (21.4). On the high-dimensional cMNIST rotation task, DIROCA_ε* (28.0–30.7%) is ~10× better than AbsLin_n (276.7–287.9%) and ~60× better than BARY (2291–4135%), indicating that the min-max robustness objective acts as an implicit regularizer that recovers a meaningful linear approximation even under severe non-linear geometric perturbations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Causal Abstraction (CA) learning literature by introducing distributionally robust optimization over Wasserstein ambiguity sets, bridging the gap between exact transformations (Rubenstein et al., 2017; fixed environment) and uniform transformations (Beckers & Halpern, 2019; all environments). Prior CAL methods—BARY_OT (Felekis et al., 2024), joint differentiable programming (Zennaro et al., 2023), and AbsLin (Massidda et al., 2024)—all implicitly assume a fixed nominal environment (ε=0 in the paper's hierarchy). The paper's (ρ,ι)-abstraction framework and DIROCA algorithm are the first to explicitly model environmental uncertainty via a Wasserstein ball, drawing on the concentration and finite-dimensional reduction results of Kuhn et al. (2019) for Wasserstein DRO.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a principled, tunable robustness knob (the Wasserstein radius ε) that interpolates between exact and uniform abstractions, with concentration bounds guiding radius selection and a closed-form worst-case error certificate. A notable empirical finding is that robustness to environmental shifts also confers resilience to structural equation misspecification and intervention mapping errors, suggesting the min-max objective acts as an implicit regularizer beyond its primary purpose. Key limitations include restriction to linear abstractions, Additive Noise SCMs, and assumed knowledge of the causal DAG and intervention mapping ω; extending to non-linear abstractions, unknown structures, and high-dimensional settings remains open.
