## Problem

Conformal prediction and conformal risk control provide distribution-free uncertainty guarantees for supervised learning, but they are not directly applicable to generative models such as LLMs, image generators, and AI agents. Existing attempts to apply conformal ideas to generative tasks are highly task-specific, and CRC's theoretical assumptions (strict monotonicity, set-valued outputs, callable utility) are overly restrictive for generative settings. This paper introduces Conf-Gen, a general framework that extends CRC to generative tasks while relaxing these assumptions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a conditioning variable X (e.g., a question, image prompt, or agent task), a sequence of generations Y produced by a generative model, and a calibration dataset of (X, Y, Y_GT) triples with pre-computed admissibility values. It outputs a selected subsequence or subset of Y (not necessarily a set) together with a formal guarantee that the expected admissibility of the output is at least a user-specified target γ. Notably, the admissibility function need not be callable (it can be defined by human evaluation), and the framework supports partial generation, allowing the model to stop producing generations once sufficient information has been gathered.

## Method

Conf-Gen comprises three components: a parameterized family of selection functions C_λ: X × Y* → S that map an input and a sequence of generations to a structured output (sequence, set, or single element), an admissibility function A: X × S × Y_GT → [0,∞) generalizing CRC's utility, and a calibration dataset. Calibration finds λ̂ as the smallest λ such that the average admissibility on the calibration set exceeds (n+1)/n · γ, yielding the guarantee E[A^(n+1)(λ̂)] ≥ γ. The key theoretical relaxation weakens CRC's almost-sure monotonicity requirement to monotonicity in conditional expectation, and the output space S is no longer restricted to sets. A practical enabler is the finite-image property of score-based selection functions: when C_λ selects a subsequence based on per-element scores, the number of distinct outputs is finite, allowing calibration even when A is not callable by evaluating A only at those finitely many outputs. The framework also supports partial generation, where a stopping time τ allows early termination of generation at test time.

## Results

On TriviaQA, Conf-Gen satisfies the conformal guarantee (average admissibility ≥ γ) while producing shorter answer sequences than the Quach et al. (2024) baseline across most target coverage levels γ ∈ [0.62, 0.84], requiring fewer LLM calls at inference due to partial generation. On WebVoyager, the agent achieves ~60% single-attempt success, and Conf-Gen formally guarantees above 65% success with two attempts, rising to nearly 80% when more trajectories are permitted. On the random forest task (Click_prediction_small, T=100 trees, k=30), the average number of selected trees remains below 2k−1=59 across a wide range of γ values, ensuring majority-vote correctness. All novel tasks (non-memorized images, conversational chatbot, agentic AI) empirically verify the conformal guarantee with no existing baseline for comparison.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The only direct head-to-head comparison is against Quach et al. (2024) (Conformal Language Modeling, ICLR 2024) on the TriviaQA dataset using LLaMA-13B. Both methods satisfy the conformal coverage guarantee (average admissibility stays above the γ diagonal), but Conf-Gen produces meaningfully shorter answer sequences across most γ values, making its outputs more practically useful. This advantage stems from Conf-Gen's unified single-parameter calibration versus Quach et al.'s sequential multi-filter approach, which compounds conservatism by calibrating each filter independently on disjoint data subsets. Conf-Gen also benefits from partial generation, requiring fewer LLM calls at inference for a given γ ≤ 0.84. However, at γ ≥ 0.85, Conf-Gen's calibrated λ̂ reaches +∞ (the maximally conservative output), a degenerate case that Quach et al. avoid through their Pareto-testing cross-validation of hyperparameters—though that cross-validation incurs additional calibration cost and still yields larger sets at lower γ values.

## Relation to Prior Work

Conf-Gen directly extends Conformal Risk Control (CRC; Angelopoulos et al., 2024) from supervised prediction sets to generative tasks, relaxing CRC's almost-sure monotonicity requirement to a weaker conditional-expectation condition and allowing outputs beyond sets (e.g., sequences, single elements). It unifies several prior task-specific conformal methods for LLMs—conformal language modeling (Quach et al., 2024), conformal factuality (Mohri & Hashimoto, 2024), conformal summarization (Kuwahara et al., 2025), and conformal agent error attribution (Feng et al., 2026)—as special cases of a single framework. The key conceptual departure from CRC is that the admissibility function need not be callable (e.g., it can be human evaluation) and the selection function operates on sequences of generations rather than a single feature vector, enabling partial generation at inference time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first general, distribution-free conformal guarantee framework for generative models, filling a gap that has hindered deployment of LLMs, image generators, and AI agents in safety-critical settings. The relaxed γ-sensibility assumptions make the framework applicable to tasks where strict monotonicity fails (e.g., image memorization detection), and the accompanying Python package lowers the barrier to adoption. Open questions include whether the conditional-expectation monotonicity assumption can be further weakened or verified automatically, how the framework scales to very long generation sequences, and whether the score functions and selection strategies proposed here are near-optimal for each task.
