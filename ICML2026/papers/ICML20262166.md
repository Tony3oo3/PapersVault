## Problem

Most equivariant neural networks target compact groups (SO(3), E(n)) or semisimple Lie algebras, but many scientific and geometric problems involve the non-compact general linear group GL(n), whose Lie algebra gl(n) is reductive but not semisimple. The Killing form — the canonical Ad-invariant bilinear form underpinning prior Lie-algebraic architectures like Lie Neurons — is degenerate on the center of gl(n), rendering such networks blind to trace and scale signals that carry physical meaning in matrix-valued data (covariance magnitude, flow divergence, global expansion). No general-purpose, exactly GL(n)-adjoint-equivariant architecture existed that could natively process heterogeneous matrix-valued features while preserving the central component.

## Input / Output

ReLNs consume heterogeneous geometric inputs — 3D vectors (velocities, positions), SPD covariance matrices, and general similarity-transforming operators (dynamics matrices, stress tensors) — by lifting each into a shared gl(n) feature space: vectors via the hat map into so(3)⊂gl(3), covariances via the matrix logarithm into Sym(n)⊂gl(n), and operators as direct gl(n) features. All channels are then processed by a single adjoint-equivariant backbone under the unified conjugation rule Ad_g(X)=gXg⁻¹. Outputs are task-specific (class labels, invariant scalars, recovered dynamics matrices, 3D trajectory estimates, reconstructed Gaussian-splat features, or rollout state predictions) and are produced through B̃-contraction readouts that are Ad-invariant by construction.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a non-degenerate Ad-invariant bilinear form B̃(X,Y)=2n·Tr(XY)−Tr(X)Tr(Y) on gl(n) that orthogonally decomposes into the Killing form on the semisimple ideal sl(n) plus a non-degenerate inner product on the center RI, resolving the degeneracy that makes Killing-form-only architectures center-blind. Built on B̃, the ReLN layer toolbox provides equivariant primitives: a channel-mixing linear layer, a B̃-gated ReLU that uses an invariant scalar to gate an equivariant direction, a Lie-bracket (commutator) nonlinearity for nonlinear channel interactions, a B̃-scored max-pooling for unordered sets, and a B̃ self-contraction invariant readout. A unified lifting embeds vectors, congruence-transforming matrices, and similarity-transforming operators into the same gl(n) space so that a single adjoint-equivariant backbone processes all feature types without per-symmetry redesign. The architecture is exactly GL(n)-adjoint equivariant by construction, reduces to Lie Neurons on semisimple subalgebras, and empirically matches or outperforms specialized equivariant baselines (Lie Neurons, Vector Neurons, TFN, SE(3)-Transformer, EMLP) across algebraic benchmarks, drone state estimation, 3D Gaussian Splatting, and the double-pendulum task while using substantially fewer FLOPs per step.

## Results

On the drone state-estimation task, ReLN with (v, log C) input achieves the best ATE of 13.92 m and RTE of 11.04 m, outperforming Lie Neurons (15.65/12.04), TFN (17.56/14.40), and SE(3)-Transformer (21.67/16.77). On 3D Gaussian Splatting classification (ModelNet10), ReLN attains 94.82% standard and 95.15% rotated accuracy, surpassing the non-equivariant baseline (93.39%/18.28%) and Lie Neurons (92.20%/92.20%). On the native GL(3) system-identification task, ReLN achieves Trace MSE 0.0133 and Canonical MSE 0.0039, while Lie Neurons' Trace MSE is two orders of magnitude worse (1.6850) due to Killing-form center-blindness. On the EMLP double-pendulum benchmark, ReLN matches or slightly improves rollout error (e.g., 0.010 vs. 0.015 on SO(2)) while reducing FLOPs by 11.18× and inference latency by 27.81×.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Lie Neurons (Lin et al., 2024a), ReLN wins decisively on the native GL(3) system-identification task (Trace MSE 0.0133 vs. 1.6850, a 127× gap) because the Killing form is blind to the center, and also wins on drone estimation with log-covariance (ATE 13.92 vs. 15.65) and 3DGS classification (94.82%/95.15% vs. 92.20%/92.20%), while matching on semisimple benchmarks (Platonic: 99.78% vs. 99.62%; sp(4): 5.14×10⁻⁴ vs. 5.83×10⁻⁴ MSE). Against Vector Neurons (Deng et al., 2021), ReLN is competitive on velocity-only input (ATE 16.85 vs. 17.36) but substantially outperforms VN when covariance is added (13.92 vs. 191.78 ATE), as VN's eigendecomposition-based interface destabilizes the coupled signal. Against TFN (Thomas et al., 2018) and SE(3)-Transformer (Fuchs et al., 2020), ReLN achieves the best ATE/RTE on all covariance-integrated variants (13.92/11.04 vs. 17.56/14.40 and 21.67/16.77 respectively). Against EMLP (Finzi et al., 2021) on the double-pendulum benchmark, ReLN matches or slightly improves rollout error across O(2), SO(2), and D₆ while using 11.18× fewer FLOPs per step (142,190 vs. 1,589,909) and 27.81× lower inference latency (2.216 ms vs. 61.635 ms). Against the 3DGS self-supervised baseline (Ma et al., 2025), ReLN improves standard accuracy by 1.43 points (94.82% vs. 93.39%) and rotated accuracy by 76.87 points (95.15% vs. 18.28%), demonstrating that enforcing coupled equivariance of (μ, Σ) is critical for rotation robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReLNs directly extend Lie Neurons (Lin et al., 2024a), which established Ad-equivariant layers on semisimple Lie algebras using the Killing form, by resolving the Killing form's degeneracy on the center of reductive algebras like gl(n) through a non-degenerate Ad-invariant bilinear form. The unified lifting of heterogeneous inputs (left-acting vectors and congruence-transforming matrices) into a shared gl(n) feature space generalizes the separate treatments in Vector Neurons (SO(3) vectors) and steerable irrep-based architectures (TFN, SE(3)-Transformer), while the closed-form matrix operations contrast with EMLP's symmetry-dependent basis construction. The paper positions itself as a reusable backbone that recovers Lie Neurons on semisimple subalgebras and Vector Neurons on so(3) as special cases, unifying them under a single reductive construction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a practical, reusable equivariant backbone for the broad class of problems where features are matrix-valued and the symmetry is the adjoint action (covariances, inertias, stress tensors, linear dynamics), eliminating per-symmetry architectural redesign. The 11× FLOPs and 28× latency reduction over EMLP while matching accuracy makes exact equivariance computationally accessible for iterative or real-time settings. Key limitations include the congruence-vs-adjoint distinction for non-orthogonal coordinate changes (the unified interface is most natural under rigid frame changes), the restriction to reductive (not non-reductive) algebras, and the absence of formal guarantees on sample complexity or universal approximation within the GL(n)-adjoint-equivariant function class.
