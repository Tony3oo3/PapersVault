## Problem

Existing protein binder design methods operate under a single-target, single-state assumption, optimizing one binder against one rigid target structure. This paradigm cannot address function-oriented design goals where a single sequence must simultaneously satisfy binding constraints across multiple conformational states of the same protein (multi-state) or across structurally distinct targets (multi-target), as no current architecture provides decoupled sequence-structure noise schedules, inference-time optimization for conflicting structural constraints, or evaluation across a contextual ensemble.

## Input / Output

The method takes multiple target protein structures (one per binding context, each specified as Cα translations, SO(3) rotation matrices, and amino acid types) along with a hotspot residue mask indicating critical interface residues, and produces a single shared binder amino acid sequence that adopts distinct 3D conformations for each target context. The key structural constraint is that the sequence is identical across all contexts while the backbone geometries diverge to match each target's interface. Training data is drawn from PDB chain pairs filtered by resolution (≤5 Å), chain length (16–512 residues combined), inter-chain contact (≥1 residue pair <8 Å), and AlphaFold2-Multimer quality thresholds (ipAE ≤10, pLDDT ≥80, ipTM ≥0.5).

## Method

Chamaileon combines two key innovations. First, In-Context Complex Co-Design (I3CD) formulates binder generation as in-context denoising: clean target signals are concatenated with noisy binder signals across translation, rotation, and amino acid modalities, with independent noise schedules (t for structure, t̃ for sequence) that decouple the corruption of the two modalities, enabling the model to learn their interplay and support both forward and inverse folding. Second, Mixture-of-Paths Sampling (MoPS) is an inference-time strategy that discretizes the generative trajectory into intervals and alternates roles among K conformations: in each interval, one conformation undergoes joint sequence-structure co-design (updating the shared sequence and its own structure) while the remaining conformations are updated via sequence-conditioned forward folding, ensuring the single sequence is iteratively optimized against all structural constraints. To enable beam search over structural trajectories, the deterministic ODE for translation is converted to an SDE via Fokker-Planck correction, introducing controlled stochasticity; candidates at each beam-search checkpoint are scored by a weighted composite of ipAE, binder pLDDT, and scRMSD, and only the top-scoring path is retained. The 21.8M-parameter model is trained via supervised flow matching (categorical cross-entropy for sequence, L2 for translations and rotations) on 60,692 PDB chain pairs.

## Results

On the CROSS benchmark (100 curated multi-state/multi-target entries), Chamaileon achieves a mean ipAE of 4.32/4.23 Å, binder pLDDT of 90.6/88.7, and binder scRMSD of 1.96/2.07 Å across the two conformations, with 8 and 10 unique successes respectively and 7 cases where both conformations are simultaneously successful. Neither constructed baseline (RFDiffusion+ProteinMPNN or BindCraft alternating optimization) produced a single binder satisfying both contexts (0 both-success). Ablation studies confirm that MoPS is essential for balancing conformational constraints (removing it drops both-success from 7 to 2) and that beam search further improves both-success from 5 to 7.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Chamaileon decisively outperforms both constructed baselines, which are the only available comparisons since no prior method directly addresses cross-context binder design. Baseline 1 (RFDiffusion + ProteinMPNN with equal-weight sequence fusion) fails catastrophically on scRMSD (15.5/13.5 Å, far exceeding the 5 Å success threshold) because the two-stage backbone-then-sequence paradigm cannot identify a shared scaffold across targets, and the sequence fusion step disrupts individual binding interfaces. Baseline 2 (BindCraft with alternating gradient updates between two target contexts) achieves reasonable scRMSD (1.37/3.20 Å) but fails on ipAE (19.1/18.2 Å) and pLDDT (67.7/68.1), as frequent target switching destabilizes gradient backpropagation and prevents convergence on either interface. Chamaileon achieves 7 both-success cases versus 0 for both baselines, with ipAE roughly 4× lower than Baseline 2 and pLDDT ~23 points higher, demonstrating that the unified MoPS sampling strategy is essential for navigating the multi-objective trade-offs that defeat sequential or alternating approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

Chamaileon builds directly on the multimodal discrete flow matching framework of Campbell et al. (2024) for joint sequence-structure protein co-design, and on the ODE-to-SDE conversion of Song et al. (2021) to inject stochasticity into structure sampling. Conceptually, it departs from prior multi-state design methods (ProteinGenerator, DynamicMPNN, ProDiT) that either average parallel trajectories or train on scarce conformational ensembles, by instead treating multi-state and multi-target binder design as a single 'cross-context' problem solvable at inference time via Mixture-of-Paths Sampling. The in-context training paradigm (I3CD) borrows the clean-condition/noisy-target concatenation strategy from vision in-context generation, repurposing it to inject target structure into the binder denoising process without requiring multi-state training pairs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first unified framework that treats multi-state and multi-target binder design as the same computational problem, and the first to address the fundamental data-scarcity barrier for multi-conformational design through inference-time optimization rather than training on ensembles. The CROSS benchmark (100 curated entries) provides a much-needed evaluation standard for cross-context binder design. However, practical impact is currently limited: the model is small (21.8M parameters, underperforming APM on single-state design), there is no wet-lab validation, only 7 of 100 entries achieve simultaneous success on both contexts, and multi-target cases are rare (15% of the benchmark). Open questions include whether the approach scales to continuous conformational landscapes, whether larger models close the gap with state-of-the-art single-state methods, and whether the active-negative design mechanism generalizes beyond the four tested cases.
