## Problem

Existing camera-conditioned video generation methods require dense per-frame camera poses, which are difficult to obtain for in-the-wild content. When only sparse keyframe poses are available, naive interpolation (e.g., SLERP) produces severe pose drift and motion discontinuities because the model lacks robust 3D geometric priors to hallucinate consistent intermediate viewpoints. The paper asks whether a diffusion model can be trained directly on sparse camera conditions to generate geometrically coherent videos without any inference-time geometric module.

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference image, a text prompt, and a sparse set of camera poses (extrinsic 3×4 and intrinsic 3×3 matrices) defined on a subset of frames S where |S| ≪ F, with sparsity ratios of 1/2, 1/3, or 1/4. Camera poses are encoded via Plücker embeddings and injected into a pre-trained latent diffusion I2V model (SVD with UNet or CogVideoX with DiT). The output is a full video sequence of F frames that adheres to the sparse keyframe poses while maintaining 3D geometric consistency and temporal smoothness across all unconstrained intermediate frames.

## Method

CamGeo employs a training-only distillation strategy where a frozen VGGT teacher provides two supervisory signals: (1) Keyframe Trajectory Distillation, which enforces cycle-consistency between VGGT-estimated camera parameters and ground-truth sparse poses at keyframes using quaternion-based L1 rotation comparison; and (2) Cross-frame Consistency Distillation, which applies geometry-aware depth warping and trajectory smoothness constraints across unsupervised frames with a dynamic weight that prioritizes long-range anchoring and reduces penalties in high-gradient regions. A three-phase coarse-to-fine curriculum (warm-up → strong pose constraint → depth refinement) with sigmoidal scheduling progressively introduces geometric complexity to stabilize optimization. Crucially, VGGT and all auxiliary losses are discarded at inference, yielding zero additional computational overhead while the distilled 3D knowledge remains embedded in the diffusion weights.

## Results

On RealEstate10K at 1/2 sparsity, SVD-CamGeo achieves RotError 1.34, TransError 4.89, and CamMC 5.49, outperforming the dense-supervision SVD-Full (1.46/6.26/6.83) and the sparse-trained SVD-Base (1.55/5.13/5.85). For the DiT backbone, CogVideoX-CamGeo reaches RotError 1.27 and FVD-StyleGAN 83.4, surpassing CogVideoX-Full (1.39/94.6). At the most challenging 1/4 sparsity, SVD-CamGeo reduces RotError by 0.17 over SVD-Full and 0.25 over SVD-Base, while CogVideoX-CamGeo achieves RotError 1.33 vs. 1.45 for CogVideoX-Full. Out-of-distribution on MannequinChallenge confirms consistent gains (e.g., SVD-CamGeo RotError 3.02 vs. SVD-Full 4.62 at 1/2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CamI2V-interp. and CameraCtrl-interp. are the two external baselines adapted to sparse conditioning via linear pose interpolation; both are substantially outperformed by CamGeo across all metrics (e.g., at 1/2 sparsity, SVD-CamGeo reduces RotError from 1.58/1.48 to 1.34 and CamMC from 7.12/6.95 to 5.49). More critically, CamGeo also beats the dense-supervision baselines (SVD-Full, CogVideoX-Full) that have access to per-frame camera poses, demonstrating that the VGGT distillation compensates for the missing supervision. The paper further shows that forcing interpolated poses on a dense model (SVD-Full-interp., CogVideoX-Full-interp.) actually degrades performance relative to sparse inference without interpolation, confirming that rigid geometric interpolation conflicts with learned motion priors. Against the sparse-trained baseline without geometry (SVD-Base, CogVideoX-Base), CamGeo consistently improves RotError by 0.17–0.25 and TransError by 0.3–0.5 at 1/4 sparsity, with the gap widening as sparsity increases. A user study with 73 participants confirms these quantitative gains, with CamGeo preferred in 71.2% of comparisons over both Full and Base configurations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of camera-conditioned video generation (CameraCtrl, CamI2V, VD3D) and sparse conditioning (SparseCtrl, YODA, MagicMotion), but departs from both lines in a key way: prior camera-control methods require dense per-frame poses, while prior sparse methods lack explicit camera pose control. The novelty is in using VGGT as a frozen, training-only teacher to distill 3D geometric priors (camera trajectories and depth) into a standard diffusion backbone, combined with a coarse-to-fine curriculum that progressively introduces geometric constraints. The camera conditioning mechanism itself (Plücker embeddings) is inherited from CameraCtrl, and the diffusion backbones (SVD, CogVideoX) are pre-trained models that are fine-tuned rather than redesigned.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: dense per-frame camera poses from COLMAP are fragile under fast motion and complex dynamics, making sparse keyframe conditioning a more realistic deployment scenario. The training-only distillation design is elegant because it eliminates all inference overhead while still injecting rich 3D structure, and the architecture-agnostic validation (UNet + DiT) broadens applicability. Open questions remain around scalability to long video sequences (temporal drift beyond the modeled window), disocclusion in large scene transitions, and view-dependent effects like dynamic shadows, all acknowledged as limitations. Whether the VGGT teacher can be replaced by lighter or learned alternatives to reduce training cost is an unexplored direction.
