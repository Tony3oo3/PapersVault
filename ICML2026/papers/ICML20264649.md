## Problem

When Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE) is applied to vision-language models, text and image position indices are embedded in a shared index space, causing cross-modal relative positions to be determined by concatenation order rather than semantic alignment. This introduces spurious positional bias that distorts spatial grounding and visual reasoning. Existing fixes either collapse intra-image spatial structure (shared-index) or retain cross-modal coupling (1D flattening or 2D grid concatenation), leaving no method that simultaneously achieves cross-modal disentanglement and fine-grained spatial preservation.

## Input / Output

The method operates at the positional-encoding level of a standard VLM transformer: it takes tokenized RGB images (512×512 in experiments, yielding 2D width-height grid coordinates per patch) and a text question as input, and produces a text response. Only the index transformation before RoPE rotation is modified; the vision encoder, projection layers, and LLM weights remain architecturally unchanged. The method is applied per-layer during SFT on a 1M-sample subset of MAmmoTH-VL, with the vision encoder frozen.

## Method

Circle-RoPE introduces two components. First, Circular Image Token Index Projection (CIP) remaps 2D image-token grid coordinates onto a 3D annulus whose plane is orthogonal to the text-index axis via three steps: coordinate centralization, mixed-angle annular mapping (a weighted blend of spatial-origin angle and raster-scan grid-index angle, controlled by α), and target-plane rotation that aligns the annulus normal with the text direction. This cone-like geometry guarantees each text token is equidistant to all image tokens, achieving PTD = 0 (a formally proven sufficient condition for eliminating RoPE-induced geometric attention bias). Second, Alternating Geometry Encoding (AGE) alternates Circle-RoPE layers (for unbiased cross-modal grounding) with M-RoPE layers (for grid-based intra-image locality) across the transformer depth, acting as a geometric regularizer that preserves fine-grained spatial structure while removing cross-modal positional bias.

## Results

Circle-RoPE (3B) achieves an average score of 58.46 across five multimodal benchmarks (MMMU-val, MMMU-Pro, MathVista-mini, AI2D, RealWorldQA), improving over the Qwen2.5-VL (SFT) 3B baseline (57.46) by +1.00 on average. The largest individual gain is +2.58 on AI2D (81.80 vs. 79.22), followed by +1.00 on MathVista-mini (63.40 vs. 62.40). Circle-RoPE also outperforms the larger InternVL2.5-4B (57.03 avg) and the original Qwen2.5-VL 3B (56.89 avg) without any architecture-specific tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the direct baseline Qwen2.5-VL (SFT) 3B, Circle-RoPE wins on all five benchmarks, with the most substantial margin on AI2D (+2.58) and MathVista-mini (+1.00), confirming that the positional-encoding change alone drives the gains. Compared to the original (non-SFT) Qwen2.5-VL 3B, Circle-RoPE improves by +1.89 to +3.66 points per benchmark, showing that the method also benefits from the SFT adaptation. Circle-RoPE (3B) surpasses the larger InternVL2.5-4B (57.03 avg) by +1.43 on average, winning on every individual benchmark including AI2D (81.80 vs. 81.38) and MMMU-Pro (28.44 vs. 26.65). Against SAIL-VL 2B, MiniCPM-V variants, and Phi-3.5-Vision 4.2B, Circle-RoPE leads by 6–16 points on average, though these comparisons are less controlled due to differing training recipes. On the TAM diagnostic benchmark, Circle-RoPE improves decoupling capability (Func-IoU) by +3.45 over the baseline while maintaining spatial grounding (Obj-IoU +0.08), validating the PTD=0 design goal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RoPE → M-RoPE lineage for vision-language models by identifying a geometric root cause of cross-modal positional bias: the coupling of text and image indices in a shared coordinate space. Where prior VLMs (LLaVA, DeepSeek-VL2) flatten image tokens into 1D sequences or M-RoPE (Qwen2-VL) preserves 2D grid indices but still concatenates them with text, Circle-RoPE remaps image token indices onto an annulus orthogonal to the text axis, achieving a formal PTD = 0 disentanglement criterion. The Alternating Geometry Encoding (AGE) component further departs from prior single-geometry designs by interleaving the decoupled cone-like and grid-based priors across layers, treating them as complementary inductive biases rather than competing alternatives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The PTD metric and its formal connection to RoPE-induced geometric attention bias (Theorem in Appendix A) provide a principled diagnostic for evaluating any multimodal positional encoding, which is a useful contribution beyond the specific Circle-RoPE construction. However, the empirical gains are modest (+1.0 average over the Qwen2.5-VL SFT baseline), the method is validated only on 2D images with SFT-based adaptation (not from-scratch training or video), and the adaptation cost—where Circle-RoPE initially lags the baseline before surpassing it—suggests practical deployment requires non-trivial retraining. Open questions include extension to spatio-temporal (n > 2) inputs, whether the cone-like geometry generalizes to architectures without RoPE, and whether the AGE schedule can be learned rather than hand-specified.
