## Problem

Current GNNs represent molecular geometry only through first-order vectors (directions, displacements), missing second-order relationships between directions (bond angles, ring planarity, local anisotropy) that are naturally captured by SPD matrices. Meanwhile, sheaf neural networks—which model edge-heterogeneous relationships via edge-specific restriction maps—are confined to Euclidean vector spaces and cannot propagate matrix-valued features. This paper addresses both gaps simultaneously by constructing the first sheaf framework that operates natively on the SPD manifold.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263629/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3D molecular graphs where atomic coordinates are lifted to effectively rank-1 SPD matrices via centered displacement outer products (û_v û_v^T + εI ∈ SPD_3), while atom types feed a parallel semantic stream. The output is a scalar molecular property score per molecule (binary classification), produced by fusing a power-Euclidean-pooled SPD matrix with mean-pooled semantic features through factorized bilinear pooling. The architecture is invariant by design, suitable for intrinsic molecular properties rather than equivariant tensor outputs.

## Method

The core contribution is extending cellular sheaf theory to SPD-valued stalks by exploiting the Lie group structure of SPD_n (via the global log/exp diffeomorphism to Sym_n). Restriction maps are orthogonal congruences P ↦ MPM^T (proven isometries under both AIRM and Log-Euclidean metrics), the coboundary operator replaces vector subtraction with Lie group subtraction in the log domain, and the adjoint is defined through an SPD pairing compatible with the group structure. The resulting SPD sheaf Laplacian drives a dual-stream architecture: a geometric stream applies SPD sheaf convolution with Cayley-transform-parameterized restriction maps, while a semantic stream uses GraphSAGE on atom features, with asymmetric cross-modal interaction allowing geometry to modulate semantics. Theoretically, the paper proves SPD sheaves strictly generalize Euclidean sheaves (Theorem 4.15), admitting more global sections via an explicit index jump. Empirically, sheaf convolution transforms rank-1 directional initializations into full-rank matrices (effective rank 1.00 → 1.72–2.28), encoding local geometric structure, and the architecture retains 97% performance at 32 layers.

## Results

SPD-Sheaf achieves state-of-the-art ROC-AUC on 6 of 7 MoleculeNet benchmarks: BACE (89.0%), BBBP (77.4%), ClinTox (99.4%), SIDER (84.3%), Tox21 (80.1%), and MUV (82.3%), ranking second only on HIV (80.9% vs. SMPT's 81.2%). The largest gains appear on geometry-sensitive tasks: +4.0% over the best prior method on BBBP and +6.7% on SIDER. The model is trained from scratch (no pre-training), yet matches or exceeds methods that rely on millions of pre-trained molecules. At 32 layers, SPD-Sheaf retains 97% of peak performance (86.7% vs. 89.0% on BACE), while GCN drops to 51.1%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPD-Sheaf outperforms all 17 baselines on 6 of 7 datasets, losing only on HIV (80.9% vs. SMPT's 81.2%), where SMPT benefits from pre-training on millions of molecules while SPD-Sheaf trains from scratch. Against the strongest geometry-aware baselines, gains are substantial: +9.6% over EGNN on BACE, +5.0% on BBBP, and +13.7% on MUV, confirming that second-order SPD representations capture geometric structure that vector-based equivariant methods miss. Compared to the best transformer-based method SMPT, SPD-Sheaf leads by +1.7% on BACE, +4.0% on BBBP, +6.7% on SIDER, and +0.1% on MUV, while trailing by only 0.3% on HIV. Against the strongest 3D molecular representation Uni-Mol, improvements range from +0.1% (HIV) to +18.4% (SIDER). The ablation in Table 2 further shows that SPD geometry alone (SPD4GNN, HyperbolicGCN) and sheaf structure alone (Euclidean SheafNN) each contribute, but neither matches the combined SPD-Sheaf, with the full model gaining +3.3% over Euclidean SheafNN on BBBP and +7.0% over SPD4GNN on MUV.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads: sheaf neural networks (Hansen & Gebhart 2020; Bodnar et al. 2022; Barbero et al. 2022), which provide edge-specific transformations but are confined to Euclidean vector stalks, and SPD matrix learning for graphs (Huang & Gool 2017; Zhao et al. 2023; Wang & Chang 2026), which captures second-order geometry but relies on standard message passing. The key conceptual departure is exploiting the Lie group structure of SPD_n (via the global log/exp diffeomorphism) to define coboundary operators, adjoints, and Laplacians natively on the manifold, rather than projecting to a tangent space. The dual-stream architecture also draws on GraphSAGE for the semantic stream and the TgReEig nonlinearity from Zhao et al. (2023) for the geometric stream, but the sheaf convolution on SPD stalks is entirely new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The strict generalization theorem (Theorem 4.15) provides a clean theoretical justification for why SPD-valued sheaves should outperform Euclidean sheaves: the kernel of the SPD Laplacian strictly contains that of the Euclidean Laplacian, meaning richer global sections survive at depth. Empirically, the 97% performance retention at 32 layers versus GCN's 59% confirms practical depth robustness, and the second-order emergence (effective rank 1→2.3) gives an interpretable mechanism for how directional inputs become full geometric representations. Key open questions include extending the framework from invariance to equivariance (needed for force-field or stress-tensor prediction) and resolving whether the sign ambiguity of the vv^⊤ embedding is a fundamental limitation or a useful inductive bias for broader task families beyond molecular property prediction.
