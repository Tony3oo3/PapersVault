## Problem

Zeroth-order normal-based decision attacks implicitly assume dimensionally homogeneous input spaces, treating all pixel coordinates as equally informative during refinement. This paper shows that heterogeneous input sensitivity creates an intrinsic expectation-level bias: under one-bit hard-label feedback and a finite query budget, only O(N) coordinates receive non-negligible expected updates, while low-sensitivity dimensions are dominated by initialization and sampling noise, persistently inflating the ℓ2 distortion. No prior work had formally characterized this structural limitation or leveraged the resulting sensitivity signal for refinement.

## Input / Output

SAR operates as a plug-in post-processor on the output of a base decision-based attacker (HSJA, CGBA, or TtBA). It consumes the current best boundary perturbation d_best (a D-dimensional vector where D=C×W×H for the flattened image) and a small additional query budget Q_SAR=300 (3% of the total 10,000-query budget) of hard-label queries. The importance map is constructed by channel-aggregating |d_best| into an H×W spatial map, then smoothed via 2D average pooling (factor r=16 for ImageNet, 4 for CIFAR-10, 7 for MNIST) and upsampling. The output is a refined perturbation with reduced ℓ2 norm that remains adversarial, verified by boundary projection and hard-label feasibility checks.

## Method

The paper first proves (Theorem 4.3) that at directional stationarity, the coordinate-wise magnitudes of the perturbation produced by any ZO normal-based attack are proportional to the local pixel sensitivities |∇_i φ|, providing a query-free sensitivity ranking. Building on this, SAR constructs a denoised importance map from the current best perturbation and applies a coarse-to-fine percentile schedule following a dyadic traversal (0.5, 0.25, 0.75, 0.125, 0.375, …) over (0,1). At each step, the p least-important spatial locations are suppressed by a factor (1−s) with s=0.1, the rescaled perturbation is renormalized to the reference ℓ2 norm, projected back onto the decision boundary via binary search, and accepted only if the resulting boundary distance decreases. This conservative, progressive suppression directly targets the stagnation on low-sensitivity dimensions identified by Proposition 4.2, redistributing perturbation mass toward high-sensitivity regions without requiring gradient access or additional model architecture assumptions.

## Results

SAR achieves consistent ~10% reductions in average ℓ₂ distortion across all nine target models when appended to any base attacker (HSJA, CGBA, or TtBA) under a fixed 10,000-query budget. TtBA+SAR attains the best average ℓ₂ on all models, e.g., 1.3613 on ImageNet-ViT, 0.9829 on ImageNet-VGG, 0.1682 on CIFAR-10-CNN, and 1.9725 on MNIST-LMT. Attack success rate also improves: TtBA+SAR reaches 90.6% ASR on ImageNet-ViT (vs. 86.0% for TtBA alone) and 90.6% on MNIST-LMT (vs. 87.4%). SSIM gains confirm improved visual imperceptibility, with TtBA+SAR achieving 0.9955 on ViT and 0.8115 on MNIST-LMT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAR is evaluated as a plug-in on top of three state-of-the-art ZO normal-based attackers (HSJA, CGBA, TtBA) and against PAR (Patchwise Adversarial Removal) as a region-sensitive baseline. Against each base attacker, SAR consistently reduces average ℓ₂ by roughly 10% (e.g., TtBA 1.5019 → TtBA+SAR 1.3613 on ImageNet-ViT; CGBA 6.6303 → CGBA+SAR 3.9326 on ResNet) while also improving ASR and SSIM, using only 300 of the 10,000 total queries. Against PAR, SAR achieves lower ℓ₂ and higher ASR on both ImageNet-ViT and ImageNet-VGG (e.g., TtBA+SAR: 1.3613 ℓ₂ / 90.6% ASR vs. TtBA+PAR: 1.4793 ℓ₂ / 88.2% ASR on ViT), attributed to SAR's continuous fine-grained rescaling versus PAR's coarse binary all-or-nothing region removal. TtBA+SAR achieves the best ℓ₂ on all nine models and the highest ASR on 8 of 9; CGBA+SAR edges out TtBA+SAR only on ImageNet-VGG for ASR (95.8% vs. 94.4%) and SSIM (0.9973 vs. 0.9972). Under a tighter 5,000-query budget, SAR still delivers substantial gains (e.g., TtBA 2.193 → TtBA+SAR 2.070 on ViT), confirming robustness when the base attack is less converged.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the zeroth-order Monte Carlo normal estimation paradigm that underpins state-of-the-art decision-based attacks (HSJA, CGBA, TtBA), but departs from prior theoretical analyses that assumed isotropic, uniformly informative sampling. Where previous work (Liu et al. 2019; Rahmati et al. 2020; Yang et al. 2024) characterized how local boundary regularity enables efficient normal estimation, this paper identifies a previously unacknowledged structural bias: heterogeneous pixel-wise sensitivity causes the MC estimator to allocate vanishing expected updates to low-sensitivity coordinates, limiting reliable refinement to only O(N) dimensions per iteration. The sensitivity-encoding property (Theorem 4.3)—that the stationary perturbation's coordinate magnitudes mirror the local sensitivity ranking—is a new theoretical result that no prior analysis of ZO normal-based attacks had established.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The O(N) effective-dimensionality bound and the sensitivity-encoding theorem reframe a long-standing practical observation (that decision-based attacks struggle in high dimensions) as an intrinsic information bottleneck of one-bit feedback rather than a fixable variance issue, shifting the design principle from 'sample more directions' to 'concentrate queries on high-sensitivity coordinates.' SAR demonstrates that even a 3% query-budget post-processing step can consistently reduce ℓ2 norms by ~10% and improve ASR across nine model-dataset combinations, validating the theory with minimal overhead. Open questions include establishing minimax lower bounds for general adaptive decision-based attacks, developing estimator-agnostic analyses beyond the MC normal estimator, and designing sampling distributions that dynamically adapt to the inferred sensitivity landscape.
