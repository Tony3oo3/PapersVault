## Problem

MLLMs struggle with fine-grained perception where decisive evidence is small and easily overwhelmed by global context. Recent 'Thinking-with-Images' methods address this by iteratively zooming into regions during inference, but incur high latency from repeated tool calls and visual re-encoding. The paper asks whether the accuracy benefits of inference-time zooming can be internalized into a single forward pass.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264936/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes a single high-resolution RGB image (typically >800×800 px) and a text question about fine-grained details (tiny text, subtle color/material attributes, small-object counts, structural properties, or object identification) and produces a text answer in either multiple-choice or open-ended format. During training, the input image is augmented with a bounding-box overlay and the question is appended with a spatial constraint, but at test time no box or tool is provided. The proposed ZoomBench additionally supplies a dual-view setup (full image plus the corresponding micro-crop) to measure the global-regional zooming gap.

## Method

Region-to-Image Distillation (R2I) decouples zooming from inference and repurposes it as a training-time primitive. First, a zoom-in synthesis step uses Qwen3-VL-235B and SAM3 to propose object-centric bounding boxes on raw images, filters to regions with area ratio < 0.1, crops and 2×-resizes them, then prompts teacher models (Qwen3-VL-235B and GLM-4.5V) to generate perception-centric questions and answers on the crops, retaining only QA pairs with >6/8 majority-vote consensus. Second, a zoom-out distillation step maps each QA pair back to the full image by overlaying the bounding box directly on the image (bbox-in-image) and appending a spatial constraint to the question, resolving referential ambiguity without costly per-sample verification. The resulting 37k synthetic samples are used to train smaller student models (Qwen3-VL-4B/7B/8B, Qwen2.5-VL-7B) purely via RL (DAPO) without SFT. The bounding box serves as privileged information during training that forces the visual encoder and cross-modal projector to learn a direct mapping between global context and the target micro-region; at inference, with the box removed, the model has internalized this 'zoom-in' capability and achieves fine-grained perception in a single forward pass.

## Results

On the overall average across 12 benchmarks, ZwZ-8B achieves 68.12%, improving over the Qwen3-VL-8B baseline (62.86%) by +5.26 points, with the largest gain on ZoomBench (37.87→58.11, +20.24). On the general perception average (GP-Avg), ZwZ-8B reaches 77.64%, surpassing all open-source baselines including Kimi-K2.5 (75.50%), Qwen3-VL-235B (74.61%), and GLM-4.5V (71.90%), while remaining competitive with the closed-source Gemini-3-Flash (79.37%). In the agentic comparison (Table 4), ZwZ-8B attains the best average (81.9%) among all single-pass and tool-use models, exceeding Skywork-R1V4-30B (80.5%) and SenseNova-MARS-8B (80.4%) while using only one forward pass (~10× faster inference).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ZwZ-8B (68.12 avg) outperforms Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B (67.55) and GLM-4.5V-108B (65.04) on overall average despite using a 4B–8B backbone, and leads all open-source models on the general perception average (77.64 vs. Kimi-K2.5's 75.50 and Qwen3-VL-235B's 74.61). On the proposed ZoomBench, ZwZ-8B (58.11) surpasses Kimi-K2.5 (56.33) and Qwen3-VL-235B (49.11) but trails Gemini-3-Flash (59.29) by ~1 point. Against agentic 'Thinking-with-Images' models (Table 4), ZwZ-8B achieves the best average (81.9%) over Skywork-R1V4-30B (80.5%), SenseNova-MARS-8B (80.4%), and Qwen3-VL-8B+tool (79.1%), while ZwZ-4B (80.6%) also exceeds all 7B agentic baselines (DeepEyes 74.4, Thyme 74.0, Mini-o3 76.1). On VStar, ZwZ-4B reaches 92.67%, the highest among all listed models, surpassing even SenseNova-MARS-8B (92.2%) and Qwen3-VL-8B+tool (90.1%). In the data-efficiency comparison (Table 3), 37k R2I samples outperform Oasis-500K (63.24), Thyme-RL-55K (66.13), and DeepEyes-47K (65.84), confirming that region-grounded quality beats raw volume. The only consistent loss is on CountQA and BabyVision, where the much larger Kimi-K2.5 (52.81 / 33.25) and Gemini-3-Flash (66.88 / 34.51) retain a clear advantage, reflecting the limits of a small backbone on tasks requiring extensive world knowledge or complex multi-step visual reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the 'Thinking-with-Images' paradigm (DeepEyes, Thyme, Pixel-Reasoner), which performs iterative zoom-in/zoom-out tool calls at inference time, by converting the zooming operation into a training-time data synthesis primitive. It builds on the privileged information learning framework (Pechyony & Vapnik, 2010) to justify using bounding-box overlays only during training, and on DAPO reinforcement learning for student optimization. The key departure from prior multimodal synthetic data methods (Oasis, MM-Evol, Genixer) is the shift from 'global-to-global' teacher-student distillation to a 'region-to-image' strategy: questions and answers are generated on micro-crops where evidence is unambiguous, then distilled back to the full image with explicit spatial grounding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that fine-grained perception, long considered to require multi-step agentic inference, can be achieved in a single forward pass with a small model trained on only 37K synthetic samples—outperforming models 30–100× larger. The dual-view evaluation protocol and the information-gain vs. information-neutral action taxonomy (Table 7) provide a useful conceptual framework for deciding when tool-based image manipulation is truly necessary versus when its benefits can be internalized. Open questions include whether the approach extends to genuinely information-gain actions (e.g., web retrieval), how it handles global relational reasoning across distant objects, and whether the teacher-consensus filtering can fully eliminate inherited hallucinations at scale.
