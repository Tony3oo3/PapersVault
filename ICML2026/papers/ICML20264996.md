## Problem

World-model augmented VLAs must jointly predict future observations and actions, but these modalities have fundamentally different statistical properties (low-dimensional smooth trajectories vs. high-dimensional spatially complex images). Existing approaches either force both into a single latent space (unified joint diffusion) or restrict information flow to one direction (causal designs), creating an unresolved trade-off between cross-modal integration and modality-specific fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264996/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes multi-view RGB images, a natural-language task instruction, and the robot's proprioceptive state (joint positions + gripper). A frozen Eagle-2 VLM backbone extracts semantic features from the image-instruction pair, which condition the diffusion head. The diffusion head outputs a chunked action sequence (16 tokens of joint-space commands) and a future observation embedding (64 SigLIP-2 tokens, downsampled from 256 via 2×2 average pooling), both generated via flow matching. The world-modeling target is deliberately in embedding space rather than pixel space to avoid modeling control-irrelevant high-frequency details.

## Method

DUST builds a multimodal diffusion transformer (MMDiT) with separate token streams for actions and vision that interact only through shared cross-modal attention layers, then pass through modality-specific DiT blocks for specialized denoising. Training uses a decoupled noise schedule where each modality receives an independently sampled timestep (τ_A, τ_o), so the model simultaneously learns inverse dynamics (clean vision → noisy action) and forward dynamics (clean action → noisy vision) under varied noise configurations. The joint loss decomposes into a sum of per-modality flow matching objectives (L_A + λ_WM · L_WM), justified by the independent-noise decomposition result of Rojas et al. (2025). At inference, an asynchronous Euler sampler updates vision tokens at every fine-grained step while action tokens update only every q steps, enabling test-time scaling of visual refinement without degrading the already-converged action trajectory.

## Results

On RoboCasa (24 kitchen tasks), DUST achieves 50.1% average success rate with 100 demos, 58.5% with 300 demos, and 66.3% with 1,000 demos, outperforming the strongest baseline FLARE by 5.5%, 3.2%, and 1.7% respectively. On the more challenging GR-1 humanoid benchmark (24 tabletop tasks), DUST reaches 36.0% (300 demos) and 42.0% (1,000 demos), surpassing FLARE by 2.3% and 5.7%. On real-world Franka Research 3 tasks (7 diverse manipulation tasks), DUST achieves 59.9% average success rate, a 10.4% improvement over FLARE and 13.4% over GR00T-N1.5. On LIBERO, DUST matches FLARE at 96.2% average, and on CALVIN ABC-D it completes 3.91 tasks in a row versus 3.75 for FLARE and 0.98 for GR00T-N1.5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DUST consistently outperforms all 14 baselines across every evaluated benchmark. On RoboCasa with 100 demos, DUST (50.1%) leads FLARE (44.6%) by 5.5%, GR00T-N1.5 (41.7%) by 8.4%, π₀ (43.0%) by 7.1%, VPP (37.1%) by 13.0%, PAD (26.7%) by 23.4%, and π₀-FAST (13.1%) by 37.0%. On GR-1 with 1,000 demos, DUST (42.0%) surpasses FLARE (36.3%) by 5.7%, GR00T-N1.5 (30.8%) by 11.2%, and π₀ (24.2%) by 17.8%. On the real-world Franka setup, DUST (59.9%) beats FLARE (49.5%) by 10.4% and GR00T-N1.5 (46.5%) by 13.4%, with the largest per-task gain on PnP-1 (83.3% vs. 62.5% for FLARE). On LIBERO, DUST (96.2%) ties FLARE (96.2%) and both exceed GR00T-N1.5 (92.8%) and π₀ (92.9%). On CALVIN ABC-D, DUST completes 3.91 tasks in a row, edging out FLARE (3.75) and Seer (3.64) while dramatically outperforming GR00T-N1.5 (0.98) and UP-VLA (2.74). The advantage of DUST over FLARE is most pronounced in low-data regimes (5.5% at 100 demos on RoboCasa) and on the more complex GR-1 humanoid tasks, suggesting the dual-stream architecture and decoupled noising provide stronger cross-modal causal learning when data is scarce or the action space is high-dimensional.

## Relation to Prior Work

DUST positions itself between two existing paradigms for world-model-augmented VLAs: unified joint diffusion (PAD, EnerVerse) that forces actions and observations into a shared latent space, and causal/unidirectional designs (Video Policy, VPP) that condition one modality on the other. It builds on the MMDiT architecture (Esser et al., 2024) and flow matching (Lipman et al., 2023), adapting the independent-noise principle from diffusion forcing (Chen et al., 2025a) from per-token to per-modality granularity, and leverages the loss-decomposition result of Rojas et al. (2025) to justify separate modality-specific flow matching objectives. The key departure is maintaining structurally separate token streams that interact only through shared cross-modal attention, combined with decoupled noise schedules that expose the model to bidirectional causal configurations (clean action + noisy vision, and vice versa) during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, well-ablated recipe for integrating world-modeling into diffusion-based VLAs without the modality-mismatch penalty of unified architectures, and the asynchronous sampling strategy offers a simple test-time scaling knob (2–5% gains) that is immediately deployable. The transfer-learning results—particularly that action-free video pretraining yields an 8-point gain and heterogeneous robot-human joint training adds another 6 points—suggest a viable path toward scaling VLA training with inexpensive data. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond single-arm and humanoid settings, how it behaves under severe visual occlusion (acknowledged as a failure mode), and whether the non-monotonic performance at high denoising steps can be resolved with stochastic solvers.
