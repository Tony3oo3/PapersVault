## Problem

Existing concept-based models (CBMs) learn all concepts as a flat set at a single layer immediately before classification, ignoring the hierarchical structure of human semantic understanding where general concepts subsume specific ones. While deep networks do learn hierarchical visual features, this structure is rarely aligned with explicit semantic hierarchies, limiting interpretability to a single granularity level and preventing multi-level interventions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264543/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (ImageNet100, AwA2, CIFAR100) along with a class-attribute incidence matrix (binary relation specifying which attributes each class possesses) as offline preprocessing input. Beyond the final class label, the model produces hierarchical attribute predictions and class group predictions at multiple intermediate network depths, where each depth corresponds to a different level of semantic generality — early layers predict broad attribute sets and large class groups, while deeper layers predict fine-grained attributes and narrow class groups converging to singletons.

## Method

The paper constructs a formal concept lattice from the class-attribute incidence relation using Formal Concept Analysis, which organizes class-attribute subsets into a hierarchy where higher levels are more general (more classes, fewer attributes) and lower levels more specific. A class-cluster density mechanism aligns lattice levels with network blocks by matching the average number of distinct classes per k-means cluster at each depth to the average extent size at each lattice level. At each aligned block, a concept encoder projects features into the attribute space of that lattice level, and a classifier is supervised with the corresponding class group (union of extents containing the true class). Training uses iterative refinement where each layer's prediction is gated by the previous layer's output (element-wise multiplication with sigmoid), progressively eliminating implausible classes, with a combined loss of attribute BCE, group-level BCE, and final cross-entropy. Theoretical analysis via the Lovász Local Lemma shows this lattice-ordered supervision preserves semantic ordering with high probability under bounded generalization error, and an information-theoretic bound guarantees monotonic information gain between consecutively supervised layers.

## Results

FoCA CBM achieves 91.88% test accuracy on ImageNet100, 92.13% on AwA2, and 79.47% on CIFAR-100, matching or exceeding all baselines on accuracy. More notably, FoCA CBM consistently achieves the best Cluster Impurity (CI) and Davies-Bouldin Index (DBI) scores across all three datasets (e.g., CI 0.573 and DBI 1.862 on ImageNet100 vs. 0.662/2.197 for Vanilla CBM), indicating substantially more semantically structured embeddings throughout network depth. On ViT backbones (CIFAR-100), FoCA ViT also outperforms ViT CBM on all metrics (86.65% vs. 84.49% accuracy, 0.755 vs. 0.774 CI, 1.983 vs. 2.237 DBI).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FoCA CBM achieves the highest accuracy on ImageNet100 (91.88%) and CIFAR-100 (79.47%), outperforming the strongest baselines Vanilla CBM (88.27%) and SCBM (79.13%) respectively. On AwA2, CEM edges out FoCA CBM on accuracy (93.11% vs. 92.13%), but FoCA CBM still leads on both embedding quality metrics. The most consistent advantage is in embedding structure: FoCA CBM achieves the best CI and DBI scores across all three datasets, with particularly large margins on ImageNet100 (CI 0.573 vs. 0.662 for Vanilla CBM; DBI 1.862 vs. 2.197) and CIFAR-100 (CI 0.622 vs. 0.712; DBI 1.855 vs. 2.238). Against the most recent hierarchical CBMs (CF-CBM, HybridCBM), FoCA CBM wins on every metric across all datasets, with CF-CBM and HybridCBM showing particularly weak performance on CIFAR-100 (60.02% and 59.52% accuracy respectively). The naive variant FoCA CBM-N (which stacks all lattice-level attribute sets after the backbone without intermediate supervision) already matches or beats most baselines on accuracy, confirming that the lattice-derived attribute sets themselves carry value, while the full iterative-refinement training further improves both accuracy and embedding structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Concept Bottleneck Model (CBM) paradigm, which has been well-established since Koh et al. (2020) and refined by numerous follow-ups addressing leakage, uncertainty, and robustness, by introducing a hierarchical semantic scaffold derived from Formal Concept Analysis (FCA). While a few recent works (Panousis et al. 2024, Sun et al. 2024) explored two-level hierarchical concept sets, they remain limited to the pre-classification step; this paper generalizes to arbitrary-depth lattices (up to 26 levels) aligned across network depth via a novel class-cluster density mechanism. The approach also draws on FCA's mathematical foundations (Ganter & Wille, 2024) in a way that is, to the authors' knowledge, the first application of FCA to concept-based learning in vision, distinguishing it from prior FCA-in-ML efforts focused on closure operators or bipartite graph embeddings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a principled, dataset-driven way to organize concept learning across network depth, enabling multi-level interpretability and interventions that target corrections at the appropriate semantic granularity—a capability absent in standard single-layer CBMs. The theoretical guarantees on order consistency and information gain, combined with the practical finding that lattice construction takes seconds and incurs negligible FLOP overhead, make this approach broadly applicable. Open questions include whether the method extends to weakly supervised or open-vocabulary settings, whether it generalizes beyond classification to detection or segmentation, and how sensitive performance is to the quality of LLM-generated attribute annotations (the GPT4-hierarchy comparison hints at fragility in concept leakage).
