## Problem

Distillation is widely promoted as a path to cheaper, more efficient LLMs, yet prior work typically reports only student-side savings (FLOPs, runtime, training energy) while omitting substantial teacher-side costs such as synthetic data generation, logit caching, and evaluation. This makes sustainability and cost claims for distillation pipelines hard to substantiate, and leaves practitioners without a clear picture of when distillation actually reduces end-to-end energy and emissions relative to a strong supervised fine-tuning baseline.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes NVML GPU power telemetry sampled every 0.5 s, explicit stage start/end timestamps, and per-stage token counts from a single distillation pipeline run on fixed hardware (one H100 SXM 80 GB). It produces a stage-wise energy decomposition in kWh (prerun, teacher-side, student training, evaluation), a normalized Joules-per-token efficiency metric, an energy-quality Pareto frontier across pipelines and student scales, a derived CO₂e estimate under stated PUE and grid-intensity assumptions, and a closed-form break-even reuse threshold N* indicating how many student runs must share one teacher artifact before distillation becomes energy-competitive.

## Method

The paper formalizes a distillation-specific energy accounting protocol that partitions each pipeline into disjoint stages with explicit boundaries, then measures GPU energy via numerical integration of NVML power traces (ground truth) supplemented by CodeCarbon-style CPU energy and CO₂e estimators. Under fixed hardware, software, tokenizer, and training budgets, it benchmarks three pipelines—baseline SFT, logit-based KD (top-100 logit caching with temperature-scaled KL loss), and synthetic SFT (teacher-generated hard labels)—across 1B/7B/13B OLMo-2 students distilled from a 32B teacher, on three supervised workloads. The key analytical contribution is constructing energy-quality Pareto frontiers that expose teacher-side work as a near-fixed overhead shifting KD and synthetic SFT right of baseline SFT, and deriving a closed-form break-even reuse threshold (N* = E_teacher / (E_student^baseline − E_student^distill)) that quantifies how many student runs must amortize a single teacher artifact before distillation inverts the energy ranking. Sensitivity sweeps over KD temperature, soft-label weight α, and synthetic generation budget (max_new_tokens, prompt count) further map how hyperparameters move models along the frontier.

## Results

Across 1B/7B/13B OLMo-2 students distilled from a 32B teacher, baseline SFT achieves the best end-to-end energy-quality tradeoff: at 7B it reaches Q=0.90 at 19.50 kWh, while KD (Q=0.78, 28.40 kWh) and Synthetic SFT (Q=0.79, 28.25 kWh) consume ~45% more energy for lower quality. At 1B, distillation methods achieve marginally higher Q (0.70–0.71 vs. 0.69) but at roughly 2.4× the energy cost (16.65–16.90 kWh vs. 7.00 kWh). Stage-wise breakdown reveals that teacher-side work (logit caching ≈11.00 kWh; synthetic generation ≈10.60 kWh) is a near-fixed overhead that dominates total energy for smaller students, and break-even teacher-reuse thresholds are ≈10 runs (1B), ≈5–6 runs (7B), and ≈2–4 runs (13B) depending on pipeline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three training pipelines—baseline SFT, logit-based KD, and synthetic SFT—under identical hardware (single H100), tokenizer, and training budgets. In one-off full-pipeline accounting, baseline SFT strictly dominates both distillation methods on the energy-quality frontier at 7B and 13B (e.g., 19.50 kWh / Q=0.90 vs. 28.40 kWh / Q=0.78 for KD at 7B). At 1B, KD and Synthetic SFT achieve a marginal Q advantage (+0.01–0.02) but at 2.4× the energy cost, making them Pareto-dominated in practice. The key differentiator is the teacher-side overhead: logit caching (11.00 kWh) and synthetic generation (10.60 kWh) are near-fixed costs that shift distillation curves rightward, an effect most severe for smaller students. With teacher-artifact reuse across N student runs, the amortized teacher cost drops as 1/N, and distillation becomes energy-competitive at N≈10 (1B), N≈5–6 (7B), and N≈2–4 (13B), inverting the one-off ranking. Student-side training energy is consistently lower under KD and Synthetic SFT than baseline SFT at the same scale (a convergence effect from the extra supervision signal), but this saving is insufficient to offset the teacher overhead without reuse.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Green AI energy-accounting tradition (Schwartz et al., 2020; Henderson et al., 2020) and prior distillation-energy studies (Rafat et al., 2023 for CNNs; Yuan et al., 2024 for NLP) by formalizing a stage-wise, end-to-end protocol that explicitly includes teacher-side workloads—logit caching and synthetic data generation—as first-class energy costs rather than sunk overheads. It repurposes existing measurement tooling (NVML GPU telemetry, CodeCarbon CPU/carbon estimators) into a distillation-specific harness with defined stage boundaries, rather than proposing a new measurement technique. The key departure from prior work is the accounting scope: where earlier studies reported student-only training energy or inference-time savings, this paper measures the full pipeline (preprocessing → teacher artifact creation → student training → evaluation) under a fixed hardware and budget protocol.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the widely held assumption that distillation is inherently a 'greener' or cheaper path by showing that, under one-off full-pipeline accounting, KD and synthetic SFT consume roughly 2.4× more energy than baseline SFT at 1B scale with only marginal quality gains, and are strictly dominated at 7B/13B. The practical contribution—break-even reuse thresholds (Eq. 4), stage-wise attribution, and a released open-source harness—gives practitioners concrete criteria for when distillation is energy-competitive versus when plain SFT is preferable. Key limitations constrain generalizability: single H100 node, single OLMo-2 model family, three supervised tasks, and no multi-GPU or power-capped settings, so the absolute kWh values and reuse thresholds are configuration-specific. Open questions include whether the teacher-cost dominance persists with smaller teachers, different parallelism strategies, or quantized/distilled-then-quantized pipelines, and how embodied hardware emissions shift the frontier.
