## Problem

Hyperspectral image single-image super-resolution (HSI-SISR) faces a fundamental conflict between global non-local modeling and computational efficiency: standard Transformer self-attention incurs quadratic O(N²) complexity that is prohibitive for high-dimensional HSIs, while window-based approximations sever global context and spectral-wise attention neglects spatial non-local self-similarity. The paper addresses how to capture full spatial-spectral correlations with linear complexity while preserving spectral fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265463/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution HSI of size C×h×w (generated via the Wald protocol: 8×8 Gaussian blur followed by downsampling to 1/2, 1/4, or 1/8) and produces a super-resolution HSI of size C×(s·h)×(s·w). Reconstruction proceeds through a pyramid of ×1, ×2, ×4 stages connected by bicubic upsampling; for non-power-of-2 scale factors, the last upsampling is dynamically adjusted to s/s_near. Each intermediate level produces a partial reconstruction that is concatenated with the upsampled input for the next stage.

## Method

The core module, SSDCB, replaces point-to-point attention with content-driven K-Means clustering in both spectral and spatial dimensions: spectral bands are clustered via cosine-similarity K-Means to yield K_c cluster centers and a spectral factor matrix U_spe, then spatial pixels (already reduced to K_c dimensions) are clustered to produce a compact core tensor G and spatial factor U_spa, achieving global modeling at O(KN) complexity. A Factor Matrix Nonlinear Correction Unit (FMNCU) applies learned nonlinear mappings (Linear–LReLU–Linear–Sigmoid–Softmax) to the linearly-derived factor matrices, enabling them to reconstruct high-resolution content. At higher pyramid levels, the Feature Reuse Reconstruction Block (FRRB) reuses G and U_SR_Spe from the ×1 stage and recomputes only the spatial factor, minimizing redundant spectral decomposition. Training uses a joint loss combining L1 reconstruction, spectral fidelity (SAM-based), Sobel gradient, and a multi-scale hierarchical reconstruction loss that explicitly supervises each pyramid level to curb error accumulation.

## Results

SSDCN achieves state-of-the-art performance across all three benchmark datasets (Pavia Center, Botswana, Washington DC Mall) at ×2, ×4, and ×8 scales, winning on all five metrics (PSNR, SSIM, SAM, ERGAS, CC). On Washington DC Mall at ×4, it reaches 46.48 dB PSNR and 0.9198 SSIM, surpassing the closest competitor SCPSN (46.41 dB, 0.9194 SSIM) and CST (46.26 dB, 0.9179 SSIM). On Botswana at ×4, it achieves 37.89 dB PSNR and 2.28° SAM, outperforming SCPSN (37.80 dB, 2.32° SAM) and LDERT (37.65 dB, 2.33° SAM). The model requires only 1.31M parameters and 3.07G FLOPs, compared to 92.78M/15.62G for LDERT and 32.99M/24.07G for MSDFormer, while also achieving the fastest inference (0.51 s) and lowest peak memory (0.029 GB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSDCN outperforms all 10 baselines on every metric across all three datasets and all three scales (×2, ×4, ×8). The closest competitors are SCPSN (spectral clustering pyramid) and LDERT (latent diffusion rectangle transformer): on Washington DC Mall ×4, SSDCN leads SCPSN by 0.07 dB PSNR and 0.05° SAM, and leads LDERT by 0.75 dB PSNR. On Botswana ×4, the margin over SCPSN narrows to 0.09 dB PSNR, while over CST it is 0.23 dB. The advantage is most pronounced at larger scales: on Washington DC Mall ×2, SSDCN achieves 52.73 dB PSNR versus 51.51 dB for CST (a 1.2 dB gap). In terms of efficiency, SSDCN (1.31M params, 3.07G FLOPs, 0.51 s runtime) is dramatically lighter than LDERT (92.78M, 15.62G, 0.68 s) and MSDFormer (32.99M, 24.07G), while only slightly heavier than SSRMamba (8.31M, 1.75G) yet delivering 0.5 dB higher PSNR.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant Transformer-based HSI-SISR paradigm (CST, ESSAFormer, LDERT, MSDFormer) by replacing quadratic-complexity self-attention with content-driven K-Means clustering in both spatial and spectral dimensions, achieving linear O(KN) global modeling. Conceptually, it repurposes classical tensor decomposition (core tensor + factor matrices) as a learnable, data-adaptive mechanism rather than a fixed linear projection, and borrows the progressive pyramid upsampling strategy from multi-scale SR literature while adding a feature-reuse mechanism (FRRB) that exploits cross-scale invariance of spectral structure. The spectral clustering dimensionality reduction connects to prior band-selection work (SCPSN, DHCA) but extends it by jointly clustering spatial pixels after spectral reduction, creating a coupled spatial-spectral factorization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the extreme efficiency gap: 1.31M parameters and 3.07G FLOPs versus 92.78M/15.62G for LDERT while matching or exceeding performance, making HSI-SISR feasible on resource-constrained platforms. The pyramid feature-reuse strategy (reusing core tensor and spectral factors, updating only spatial factors) offers a general principle for reducing redundancy in multi-scale reconstruction that could transfer to other progressive architectures. Open questions include whether the non-differentiable K-Means step limits the model's ability to adapt cluster structure during training, whether the approach generalizes to higher-dimensional or non-hyperspectral data, and whether the modest performance margins over SCPSN/LDERT (~0.1–0.2 dB) would persist on larger or more diverse datasets.
