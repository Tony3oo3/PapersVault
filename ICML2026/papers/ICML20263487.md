## Problem

Formal expressivity results for recurrent neural networks in the literature are contradictory: some prove Turing-completeness while others show equivalence to finite automata. The paper identifies the root cause as differing, often implicit, assumptions about the underlying arithmetic model (real arithmetic, floating-point, integer quantization), and notes that no unified framework exists in which the arithmetic semantics is an explicit, first-class parameter of the object being analyzed rather than a numerical afterthought.

## Input / Output

The framework takes three inputs: a formal language over a finite alphabet Σ, a recurrent architecture specified as a typed cascade of algebraic cores (each a Mealy machine with transition and readout maps) connected by wiring maps, and an arithmetic model fixing the value domain, binary operations, and rounding/overflow semantics. The output is a divisibility chain M(L) ≺ M_R^{e(Σ)} ≤ W_R^{e(Σ)} ≤ W_R relating the syntactic monoid of the target language to the realized wreath product of the architecture, from which recognizability follows as a finite-monoid divisibility question decidable via Krohn-Rhodes decomposition. The arithmetic model is not a perturbation parameter but structurally determines which pseudovariety the realized monoids inhabit.

## Method

The paper strips recurrent architectures down to algebraic cores (finite Mealy machines) composed via wiring maps into algebraic semi-RNNs, then proves a factorization lemma showing that the global state-update monoid is contained in an iterated wreath product of per-layer transition monoids, refined to a 'realized' wreath product that respects the actual wiring constraints. Language recognition is then characterized by the syntactic monoid dividing this realized wreath product, reducing expressivity to a finite-monoid divisibility question. The arithmetic model enters as the parameter that fixes the concrete sets and transition functions each core realizes: floating-point arithmetic yields a non-associative magma (breaking monoid structure), while unsigned-integer wraparound yields a commutative ring (preserving associativity and enabling cyclic group structure). A case study on diagonal state-space models (Mamba-like architectures) demonstrates the consequence: the same idealized recurrence cannot implement even-modulus counting under nonnegative floating-point multipliers but realizes every even-modulus counter under unsigned-integer quantization, recovering and refining prior impossibility results as instances of the divisibility chain.

## Results

The paper establishes a divisibility chain (Eq. 3.1) showing that the syntactic monoid of any language recognized by an algebraic RNN divides the realized wreath product of its layer transition monoids: M(L(A)) ≺ M_{R+}^{e(Σ)} ≤ W_{R+}^{e(Σ)} ≤ W_{R+}. The key case study on diagonal SSMs demonstrates that the same idealized architecture cannot implement an even-modulus counter under nonnegative floating-point recurrences (recovering Sarrof et al., 2024), yet realizes every even-modulus counter under unsigned-integer quantization with wrap-around. The framework reduces expressivity questions to finite monoid divisibility, decidable via Krohn-Rhodes decomposition, and shows that the arithmetic semantics (domain, rounding, evaluation order) is the single parameter determining which pseudovariety the realized monoid inhabits.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare empirical methods but unifies and refines conflicting theoretical results in the RNN expressivity literature. Siegelmann & Sontag (1992) claim Turing-completeness under exact real arithmetic, while Merrill (2019) shows equivalence to finite automata under finite precision; this paper shows both are correct but rely on incompatible arithmetic models, with the discrepancy fully explained by the algebraic structure of the underlying domain. Sarrof et al. (2024) prove that nonnegative diagonal SSMs (e.g., Mamba) cannot recognize languages requiring modulo counters; this result is recovered as a direct consequence of the divisibility chain when the arithmetic is floating-point with nonnegative multipliers. Conversely, the paper shows that unsigned-integer quantization with wrap-around restores the cyclic group structure ℤ/2^pℤ, enabling arbitrary even-modulus counters in the same architecture. The framework also corrects Merrill et al. (2024, Theorem 4.2), showing that their impossibility result for linear RNNs relies on a non-recurrence-consistent evaluation order, and that under consistent evaluation, nominally linear recurrences can simulate arbitrary finite machines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies a fragmented body of expressivity results for recurrent architectures that have produced seemingly contradictory conclusions: Siegelmann and Sontag (1992) showed Turing-completeness over reals, while Merrill (2019) and Weiss et al. (2018) showed equivalence to finite automata under finite precision, and Sarrof et al. (2024) proved impossibility of modular counting for nonnegative diagonal SSMs. Rather than building on a single prior framework, the paper repurposes classical algebraic automata theory (transformation monoids, wreath products, Krohn-Rhodes decomposition) to subsume all these results under one algebraic account where the arithmetic model is an explicit parameter of the object being analyzed. The key departure from prior work is treating discretization not as a perturbation of real-valued dynamics but as a structural change that determines which pseudovariety the realized transition monoid inhabits.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework resolves the long-standing confusion in the expressivity literature by showing that contradictory results are consequences of different arithmetic assumptions rather than genuine disagreements, and provides a systematic procedure (divisibility of syntactic monoids by realized wreath products) for settling expressivity questions for any recurrent architecture under any fixed numerical semantics. The diagonal-SSM case study has direct practical relevance: it explains why Mamba-style architectures cannot implement even-modulus counters in floating point yet can under unsigned-integer quantization, informing both architecture design and deployment choices. Key limitations include the restriction to finite-precision (hence only regular languages), the focus on expressivity rather than learnability, and the absence of a treatment of transformer/attention architectures, which the authors flag as future work.
