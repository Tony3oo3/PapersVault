## Problem

Existing agent distillation methods train small student models to imitate full teacher trajectories via behavior cloning, but reasoning and knowledge gaps between teacher and student cause per-step errors to compound as O(H²) over the horizon. This makes small models unable to reproduce the teacher's logical decomposition or execute complex actions, leaving a large agentic performance gap between 7B and 72B backbones on multi-step tool-use tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263125/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The student agent receives a task question and interacts with two tools (a Python code interpreter for math and a Google Search API for factual QA) in a ReAct loop, producing a trajectory of Thought-Code-Observation triplets that ends with a final answer. During training, the MPS phase generates student-authored trajectories where the teacher intervenes only at the first erroneous step, yielding a verified prefix plus a single corrected step; the RL phase then consumes these verified prefixes as rollout starting points with a maximum of 8 reasoning-action cycles per episode.

## Method

SCoRe (Student-Centered One-step Reinforcement) replaces the 'teacher-acts, student-clones' paradigm with a three-phase pipeline: (1) BC initialization on a small set of teacher trajectories to give the student basic reasoning-acting skills; (2) Mentored Problem-Solving (MPS), where the student independently generates full trajectories and the teacher corrects only the earliest error, after which the student resumes from the corrected prefix—producing capability-matched, deficiency-localized training data that reduces compounding error from O(H²) to O(H); (3) short-horizon RL using GRPO, where rollouts begin from the verified prefix preceding the original error (shortening the horizon and reducing gradient variance) and a key-step reward scheme assigns a larger bonus for reproducing the teacher's correction and a smaller format reward for merely avoiding the original mistake, providing dense credit assignment at the student's weakest step. A subset of Hard-to-Teach cases (unsolved after 5 corrections) is reserved for RL, allowing the student to explore strategies beyond the teacher's behavior.

## Results

On 8 reasoning benchmarks (AIME24, AIME25, MATH500, OlymMath, HotpotQA, 2Wiki, MuSiQ, Bamboogle), SCoRe-RL with Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct achieves an average score of 52.3, surpassing the 72B teacher (Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct, 51.1) and outperforming BC+ARPO (49.2) and BC+GRPO (48.2) by 3.1 and 4.1 points respectively. On 4 deep-search benchmarks (GAIA, HLE, xBench, WebWalker), SCoRe-RL with Qwen3-8B scores 30.5 average, exceeding the 72B teacher (27.3) and beating BC+ARPO (25.8) by 4.7 points. The SFT-only variant (SCoRe-SFT) already improves over BC by 3.1 points on reasoning (46.1 vs. 43.0), confirming that capability-matched correction data alone reduces compounding errors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCoRe-RL (Qwen2.5-7B) outperforms the 72B teacher Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct under TIR prompting (52.3 vs. 51.1 avg on reasoning; 30.5 vs. 27.3 on deep search), demonstrating that a 7B student can match or exceed a 10× larger teacher. Against the primary distillation baseline, Agent Distillation / Behavior Cloning (Kang et al., 2025), SCoRe-RL gains +9.3 points on reasoning (52.3 vs. 43.0) and +7.7 on deep search (30.5 vs. 22.8). Among RL baselines, SCoRe-RL beats BC+GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) by +4.1 on reasoning and +8.3 on deep search, and beats BC+ARPO (Dong et al., 2026) by +3.1 and +4.7 respectively. On the more challenging deep-search benchmarks, SCoRe-RL also surpasses stronger teacher-guided baselines: On-Policy Distillation (Lu & Lab, 2025) at 24.2, PRM-style step-reward GRPO (Zhang et al., 2025) at 25.5, and ReLIFT (Ma et al., 2026) at 26.7, with SCoRe-RL reaching 30.5. The only baseline that outperforms SCoRe-RL is DeepSeek-V3 (671B) under TIR prompting (54.0 on reasoning, 32.4 on deep search), which uses a model roughly 96× larger.

## Relation to Prior Work

SCoRe departs from the dominant 'teacher-acts, student-clones' paradigm in agent distillation (Kang et al., 2025; Toolformer) by inverting the roles: the student generates trajectories and the teacher intervenes only at the earliest error. This is conceptually related to DAgger (Ross et al., 2011) and LEMMA (Pan et al., 2025), but SCoRe is explicitly student-centered—the student continues after correction, and the verified prefix is reused as a short-horizon RL starting point—whereas DAgger is teacher-led at every step and LEMMA does not leverage the prefix for RL. The RL phase builds on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) but introduces two targeted modifications (short-horizon rollouts from verified prefixes and key-step rewards) that address the sparse-reward and high-variance problems specific to long-horizon agentic tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 7B student matches or exceeds a 72B teacher on 12 agentic benchmarks, offering a concrete recipe for deploying capable agents at a fraction of the inference cost. The O(H) vs. O(H²) compounding-error bound provides a clean theoretical justification for why first-error correction is superior to full-trajectory cloning, and the finding that RL can push the student beyond the teacher on deep-search tasks (e.g., GAIA 40.8 vs. 32.0) suggests the framework is not capped by teacher quality. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multimodal agents, how sensitive it is to teacher quality at scale, and whether the O(H) bound holds under the more complex distribution shifts of real-world multi-tool environments.
