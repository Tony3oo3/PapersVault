## Problem

Existing online risk control methods (ACI, BCI, CDT) rely on linearity of expectation and fundamentally break down for nonlinear tail risk functionals like CVaR. While offline conformal tail risk control provides statistical guarantees for CVaR, these guarantees do not extend to online, non-stationary, or adversarial data-generating processes. This paper asks whether CVaR can be controlled in an online, distribution-free, and adversarial setting with provable safety guarantees.

## Input / Output

At each round t, the algorithm observes an adversarially chosen loss function R_t(λ) mapping a bounded interval [λ_min, λ_max] to [R_min, R_max], along with a CVaR level β and target risk α. The algorithm outputs a calibration parameter λ_t (intentionally unprojected, so it may temporarily leave [0,1]) and an auxiliary threshold c_t ∈ [R_min, R_max]; the realized loss is R_t(λ_t) with the loss extended to constant boundary values outside the domain. The guarantee is on the realized empirical CVaR of the entire loss sequence, not on per-round losses, and holds regardless of how adversarially the loss functions are chosen.

## Method

The core insight is that the Rockafellar-Uryasev variational representation of CVaR reduces the nonlinear tail risk control problem to a two-level online optimization: an outer CDT-style update on λ_t that enforces the target risk level, and an inner AdaGrad-FTRL update on the threshold c_t that adaptively tracks the optimal quantile. The inner level solves a one-dimensional convex problem whose subgradient takes only two values {1, −β/(1−β)}, enabling a tight O(√T) regret bound without manual learning-rate tuning. Proposition 2.1 shows that sublinear regret in the inner level plus the outer CDT control jointly imply CVaR_β ≤ α + o(1). The overall guarantee is CVaR_β ≤ α + C·T^{−1/2}, where the O(1/√T) rate (versus O(1/T) for linear objectives) reflects the inherent statistical cost of controlling tail risk from rare events.

## Results

The main theoretical guarantee (Theorem 2.2) establishes that the realized empirical CVaR_β is bounded by α + C·T^(−1/2), where C depends only on β, α, γ, and the loss bounds. Empirically, on LLM toxicity control (β=0.85, α=0.1, γ=0.05), the RUCC controller stabilizes the realized CVaR_β near the target across uniform, adversarial, and adversarial-jump sampling regimes, while the static baseline (Chen et al., 2025) selects a fixed λ that keeps CVaR_β well below target, making the LLM severely overly conservative. On portfolio management (β=0.85, α=0.01, γ=0.05), the 180-day rolling CVaR_β remains close to the target over the full 1991–2025 horizon spanning the dot-com crash, 2008 financial crisis, and post-COVID turbulence, whereas the static baseline drifts substantially below or above target depending on the market regime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares RUCC against a single static baseline: a fixed threshold λ estimated via distortion risk control with L-statistics (Chen et al., 2025), calibrated on the first 1000 samples. In the LLM toxicity experiment, the static baseline selects a very small fixed λ that keeps CVaR_β well below the target α=0.1, rendering the LLM severely overly conservative (it filters out most responses), while RUCC adaptively adjusts λ_t over time to maintain tail risk at the target without excessive conservatism. In the portfolio management experiment, the static baseline is overly conservative during the stable 1991–2001 period (CVaR_β falls below target) and fails to adapt during the volatile 2009–2018 post-crisis period, whereas RUCC dynamically increases risky-asset exposure as market conditions improve and reduces it during stress episodes, keeping the rolling CVaR_β tightly around α=0.01. RUCC wins on both experiments in terms of tightness (avoiding over-conservatism) while maintaining the same validity guarantee, demonstrating the advantage of online adaptation over a single offline-calibrated threshold under non-stationary and adversarial data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Rockafellar-Uryasev variational representation of CVaR (Rockafellar & Uryasev, 2000) to reduce nonlinear tail risk control to a two-level online optimization problem, combining Conformal Decision Theory (Lekeufack et al., 2024) for the outer calibration parameter with AdaGrad-FTRL (McMahan, 2011) for the inner threshold. It departs from existing online risk control methods (ACI, BCI, CDT) that rely on linearity of expectation and provably break down for CVaR, and from offline conformal tail risk control (Chen et al., 2025; Snell et al., 2022) whose statistical guarantees do not extend to adversarial or non-stationary regimes. The key conceptual move is showing that the RU representation converts the non-additive CVaR objective into a standard online regret minimization problem over an auxiliary threshold, making no-regret algorithms directly applicable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework providing provable, distribution-free online CVaR control under adversarial data, filling a gap where prior methods either assumed stationarity or handled only linear risk functionals. The O(1/√T) tightness gap (versus O(1/T) for linear risk) quantifies the fundamental statistical cost of controlling tail risk from rare events, and the matching lower bound shows this rate is optimal. The framework naturally extends to any risk measure with a variational representation (OCE measures, variance), suggesting a general template for online nonlinear risk control. Open questions include whether the conservatism gap can be tightened in practice, how the method behaves with unbounded or heavy-tailed losses, and whether the two-level structure can be replaced by a single end-to-end optimization.
