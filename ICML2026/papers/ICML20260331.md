## Problem

Current non-generative vision models require web-scale pretraining to achieve compositional generalization (perceiving unseen concept combinations), while humans accomplish this from minimal data. The paper asks whether this data-efficiency gap is a fundamental limitation of the non-generative paradigm or merely a shortcoming of current approaches, by formally characterizing the inductive biases each paradigm requires for compositional generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260331/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Input consists of 224×224×3 RGB images from the PUG synthetic dataset, where each scene contains one or two animals (32 categories, 5 textures) on a background (10 categories), partitioned into ID and OOD splits with unseen concept combinations. The model outputs K=3 latent slots of dimension 64, each representing a visual concept; a shared linear readout trained on ID slots predicts animal and background labels, with slot-to-label assignments resolved via the Hungarian algorithm. Compositional generalization is quantified as the readout's accuracy on OOD images where all three labels must be correct simultaneously.

## Method

The paper proves a fundamental asymmetry: enforcing compositional generalization on a decoder requires a simple, data-independent block-diagonal structure on its derivative matrix (enforceable via architecture or a Hessian regularizer), whereas the corresponding encoder constraint depends on the unknown tangent space of the data manifold, making it infeasible to impose explicitly out-of-domain. Empirically, they train a VAE comprising a ViT base encoder, a slot encoder (cross-attention Transformer or Slot Attention), and a cross-attention Transformer decoder regularized to match the F_int function class. For OOD decoder inversion, they employ gradient-based search (optimizing z* to minimize reconstruction error, initialized by the encoder's 'System 1' guess) and generative replay (training the encoder on OOD images synthesized by recombining ID slots through the decoder). The key empirical finding is that generative methods with these inversion strategies yield substantial OOD accuracy gains without additional data, while non-generative methods require progressively larger pretrained encoders (DINOv1 → DINOv2 → SigLIP2) to compensate for the absence of explicit inductive biases.

## Results

On PUG-Background, non-generative methods trained from scratch achieve near-perfect ID accuracy (99.8–100%) but collapse to near-zero OOD accuracy (0.002–7.9%), while on PUG-Texture they reach 53–65% OOD. On PUG-Object, where concepts do not interact (n=0), all non-generative methods achieve 95–100% OOD accuracy. Generative methods (VAE + replay + search) yield substantial OOD gains on PUG-Background and PUG-Texture without requiring additional training data, whereas non-generative methods only recover OOD performance when using large-scale pretrained base encoders (e.g., DINOv2, SigLIP2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper contrasts non-generative encoders (Slot Attention and cross-attention Transformer, trained with supervised or unsupervised objectives) against generative VAEs augmented with gradient-based search and generative replay. On PUG-Background, non-generative methods trained from scratch achieve only 0.002–7.9% OOD accuracy, while generative methods with replay and search show a significant OOD increase across all base encoders. On PUG-Texture, non-generative methods reach 53–65% OOD, and generative methods with search yield further clear improvements. The critical distinction is data efficiency: non-generative methods require large-scale pretraining (DINOv2, SigLIP2, CLIP) to recover OOD performance, whereas generative methods achieve comparable or superior OOD gains without any additional data. On PUG-Object (n=0, no concept interactions), both paradigms perform well, consistent with the theory that simpler inverse-generator structure makes encoder-based generalization feasible.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the compositional latent variable model framework from Brady et al. (2025, ICLR), which defines the F_int function class of generators with bounded interaction degree, and on the generative replay objective from Wiedemer et al. (2024, ICLR) for object-centric compositional generalization. It departs from the recent consensus (Balestriero & LeCun, 2024) that non-generative encoders are sufficient for human-level perception by formally showing that the inductive biases required for compositional generalization are fundamentally asymmetric: decoder constraints are simple, data-independent, and enforceable via architecture or regularization, while encoder constraints depend on the unknown OOD data manifold geometry and are infeasible to impose explicitly. The work also connects to the causal/anti-causal learning literature (Kilbertus et al., 2018), providing a formal justification for the heuristic that generalization is easier in the causal (generative) direction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a rigorous theoretical argument for why data-efficient perception may require a generative paradigm, challenging the assumption that scaling non-generative methods with web-scale data is the only path forward. The key open question is whether these findings extend beyond the relatively simple PUG synthetic datasets to real-world visual complexity, and how to construct rigorous benchmarks for compositional generalization at scale. The result that non-generative methods can compensate for missing inductive biases only through large-scale pretraining (at the expense of data efficiency) has direct implications for resource-constrained settings and for understanding the limits of current foundation models.
