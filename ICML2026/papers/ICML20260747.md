## Problem

VLM-based reward models for robotic manipulation typically produce only episodic, whole-episode scores that lack step-wise credit assignment, are prone to hallucination and viewpoint sensitivity, and do not help agents diagnose what went wrong, when, and why. This paper asks whether structured natural-language self-reflection, grounded in time via keyframe localization, can serve as a reliable error-reasoning signal that turns failures into actionable, temporally grounded guidance for reinforcement learning in embodied manipulation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB image observations from a robotic camera, a natural-language task instruction, and a goal image. After each episode, uniformly sampled trajectory frames are fed to a frozen Qwen-2.5-VL-3B VLM alongside the instruction and a compact error taxonomy, which returns a schema-constrained JSON reflection (error code, key-frame indices, suggested fix, confidence). This reflection is encoded by a lightweight GPT-2 into a 768-dimensional feedback vector. The output is a trained SAC manipulation policy plus a dense, tanh-bounded shaped reward signal in [-1,1] that is added to the sparse binary environment reward under an adaptive, failure-aware coefficient.

## Method

LAGEA operates as a three-stage pipeline. First, a deterministic keyframe selector identifies causal moments (approach, contact, reversal) from the goal-similarity trajectory using cosine similarity, its temporal derivative, and acceleration, then diffuses their influence via triangular per-step weights. Second, a hybrid alignment objective (BCE calibration on success/failure labels + InfoNCE discrimination + symmetric weighted contrastive refinement) trains small MLP projectors to map image states, goal images, instruction text, and VLM feedback into a shared unit-norm embedding space where feedback geometry is control-relevant. Third, two tanh-bounded potentials are computed from this space—a goal potential averaging instruction-text and image-goal affinities, and a feedback potential measuring state-feedback cosine—and their temporal differences (potential-based shaping) yield per-step delta rewards. These are fused via a confidence-aware mixture weighted by instruction-feedback agreement, then gated by an adaptive coefficient ρ that is large during early exploration (low success EMA) and anneals as competence grows, applied only on failure steps. The final reward feeds a standard SAC agent, preserving the sparse task reward while letting VLM-derived shaping accelerate early credit assignment and gracefully recede.

## Results

On the Meta-World MT10 fixed-goal benchmark, LAGEA achieves 80% average success across ten manipulation tasks, outperforming FuRL (76%), Relay (54%), SAC (37%), LIV (28%), and LIV-Proj (24%). On the harder random-goal setting, LAGEA reaches 70.4% average success, a 9% absolute improvement over FuRL (64.6%). On the Robotic Fetch suite, LAGEA attains 51.67% average success, a 17% absolute gain over the strongest baseline FuRL (44.17%), while also converging faster in wall-clock time (92.36 vs. 94.90 minutes on average).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LAGEA outperforms all four baselines across every benchmark. Against SAC (sparse-reward only), LAGEA gains 43 points on fixed-goal MT10 (80% vs. 37%), 20.6 points on random-goal MT10 (70.4% vs. 49.8%), and 17.5 points on Fetch (51.67% vs. 34.17%). Against LIV (pre-trained language-image reward model), LAGEA improves by 52 points on fixed-goal MT10 and 17.5 points on Fetch, showing that learned VLM feedback substantially exceeds a frozen pre-trained reward signal. Against Relay (relay RL + LIV), LAGEA leads by 26 points on fixed-goal MT10, 13 points on random-goal MT10, and 14.2 points on Fetch. The closest competitor is FuRL (contrastive VLM reward alignment + relay RL), which LAGEA surpasses by 4 points on fixed-goal MT10, 5.8 points on random-goal MT10, and 7.5 points on Fetch; the gap widens on harder tasks such as drawer-open-v2 (100% vs. 80%) and push-v2 (100% vs. 80%) in the fixed-goal setting, and on PickAndPlace-v2 (43.33% vs. 33.33%) in the random-goal setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

LAGEA extends the line of work using VLMs as reward signals for RL (Rocamonde et al., 2023; Fu et al./FuRL, 2024) by shifting from episode-level scoring to temporally localized, step-wise credit assignment. It draws on self-reflection paradigms from text-only settings (Shinn et al./Reflexion, 2023; Madaan et al., 2023) but grounds them in visual manipulation trajectories via keyframe selection and contrastive feedback-vision alignment. The potential-based shaping formulation (Wiewiora, 2003) and adaptive failure-aware scheduling distinguish it from prior contrastive reward alignment (FuRL) that lacks temporal focus and structured error reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates a practical recipe—schema-constrained VLM reflections, keyframe-gated alignment, and adaptive reward scheduling—that converts language into actionable, temporally grounded learning signals for sparse-reward manipulation, reducing reliance on handcrafted rewards. Limitations include persistent VLM hallucinations (mitigated but not eliminated), evaluation confined to simulation, and untested sim-to-real transfer. Open questions include whether the approach scales to long-horizon, multi-step tasks with severe occlusion, and whether the structured feedback protocol generalizes beyond the five-code error taxonomy used here.
