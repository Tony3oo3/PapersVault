## Problem

In heterogeneous federated learning, clients with different architectures produce incompatible feature representations, making direct parameter or prototype averaging cause semantic confusion and distorted decision boundaries. Existing knowledge-sharing carriers—proxy models, nested sub-models, and shared prototypes—either incur prohibitive communication/computation costs or blur multi-scale cues into a single vector, failing under strong architectural heterogeneity. The paper asks whether a lightweight, interpretable, and architecture-invariant carrier can mediate collaboration without forcing incompatible features into a shared vector space.

## Input / Output

Each client processes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet) through its own backbone (e.g., AlexNet, ResNet10, ViT-B/16) and uploads per-class feature prototypes (d-dimensional vectors) plus a one-hot architecture label to the server. The server's rule network consumes these slot representations [prototype; architecture code] and outputs interpretable class decision rules—Boolean AND/OR combinations of threshold predicates over calibrated feature coordinates—along with a compact rule-network parameter set. Clients receive only the pruned top-π positive rule indices per class and the frozen rule-network weights, which together form the entire downlink payload.

## Method

RFL replaces feature-space averaging with collaboration in a shared rule space. The server trains a rule network that first applies architecture-conditioned normalization and affine calibration (SoftBin) to align prototype scales across backbones, then discretizes each calibrated coordinate against shared learnable thresholds to produce Boolean atoms, and finally composes higher-order AND/OR rule units through logic layers with binarized connection weights, ending in a shared linear classifier. The server broadcasts only a sparse, high-coverage subset of positive rules per class (selected by classifier weight magnitude), and each client selectively activates only the rules relevant to its locally observed classes via a margin-based soft-hinge loss on rule activations. A complementary temperature-scaled KL distillation loss aligns local logits with the rule network's class scores, jointly enforcing discriminative feature activation and decision-boundary transfer. Theoretical analysis establishes an architecture-invariant rule space, a margin lower bound controlled by the rule loss, and a generalization bound showing that driving the rule loss below a threshold shrinks the effective hypothesis complexity.

## Results

On the pathological (most challenging) partition, RFL achieves 86.14% on CIFAR-10, 63.02% on CIFAR-100, and 43.87% on Tiny-ImageNet, outperforming all prototype-based baselines by up to 3.65% (CIFAR-100) and matching or exceeding proxy-model baselines (FedSCE: 85.22/63.62/43.52%). Communication cost per round is 36.7/41.98/46.24 MB for RFL versus 260–4030 MB for proxy-model methods, a ~79% average reduction. Under Dirichlet (α=0.1) splits, RFL similarly leads: 83.80% (CIFAR-10), 58.25% (CIFAR-100), and 36.92% (Tiny-ImageNet).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against prototype-based baselines (FedProto, FedTGP, FedSA), RFL consistently wins on all three datasets under both pathological and Dirichlet splits, with the largest gains on CIFAR-100 (+3.65% over FedTGP) and Tiny-ImageNet (+3.13% over FedSA) where architectural heterogeneity is strongest. Against data-free proxy methods (FedGen, FedZGE), RFL achieves higher accuracy on every dataset while reducing communication by 92–99% (e.g., 46.24 MB vs. 4030 MB for FedZGE on Tiny-ImageNet). Against real-sample proxy methods (FedMRL, FedSCE), RFL is competitive: it slightly trails FedSCE on CIFAR-100 pathological (63.02% vs. 63.62%) but surpasses it on CIFAR-10 (86.14% vs. 85.22%) and Tiny-ImageNet (43.87% vs. 43.52%), all while using roughly 79% less communication (36.7–46.24 MB vs. 260–271 MB). Overall, RFL occupies the 'low-cost, high-accuracy' region of the accuracy–communication trade-off, outperforming prototype methods on accuracy and proxy methods on efficiency simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Wang et al. (NeurIPS 2021) for the rule network architecture (SoftBin discretization into Boolean atoms plus AND/OR logic layers), repurposing it as a server-side collaborative knowledge carrier in federated learning. It departs from the three dominant HtFL paradigms—proxy-model distillation (FedGen, FedMRL, FedZGE, FedSCE), nested sub-models, and prototype averaging (FedProto, FedTGP, FedSA)—by replacing feature-space alignment with rule-space collaboration, motivated by the observation that averaging incompatible representations causes semantic confusion. The selective rule absorption mechanism (margin-based hinge on class-relevant rules plus logit distillation) is a new loss design not present in prior HtFL work, and the theoretical analysis via architecture-invariant rule-space margins is a fresh contribution to the FL generalization literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RFL offers a practically attractive trade-off: 79% lower communication than proxy-model methods with comparable or higher accuracy, plus interpretable decision rules that practitioners can inspect and audit—valuable in privacy-sensitive or regulated deployments. However, the approach is limited to classification with class-level prototypes (no instance-level or spatial information), the rule network is intentionally shallow (1–2 logic layers), and gains over strong prototype baselines are modest (1–4%). Open questions include whether rule-based carriers scale to dense prediction tasks (detection, segmentation), whether richer intra-class statistics beyond mean prototypes improve rule quality, and whether the architecture-conditioned calibration generalizes to truly open-ended architecture pools rather than a fixed small set.
