## Problem

Existing MARL fine-tuning methods for LLM collaboration rely on predefined execution protocols that require centralized execution, and use Monte Carlo return estimates that suffer from high variance, poor sample efficiency, and exponential rollout-tree growth in multi-turn settings. How to effectively optimize decentralized LLM agents—where each agent runs inference independently in parallel—remains an open question, particularly for long-horizon or sparse-reward tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262791/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each agent receives a role-specific natural-language prompt (e.g., summarizer, coder, builder) plus its own dialog history maintained as a Transformer KV-cache of the most recent C token pairs. A shared global information vector m_t (current turn index, last reward) is appended to the critic's input during training but is unavailable at execution. Each agent outputs a full text response of up to M tokens; the joint response is evaluated by a verifiable reward model (e.g., pass-rate, IoU, Jaccard similarity), and the interaction repeats for H turns with environment feedback before the episode terminates.

## Method

The paper proposes two Multi-Agent Actor-Critic (MAAC) methods under the CTDE paradigm: CoLLM-CC uses a single centralized LLM critic conditioned on the joint history h_t and global information m_t to compute low-variance TD advantages, while CoLLM-DC gives each agent an independent critic on its local history. A key design choice is using teacher-forced forward passes to compute the log-probability of an entire response sequence in one parallel pass, treating the full text output as a macro-action rather than individual tokens. The paper provides theoretical analysis showing that K-sampling MA-REINFORCE reduces variance as 1/K^(H−t) but requires nK(K^H−1)/(K−1) inference calls per episode (Proposition 4.3), motivating the critic-based approach. Training uses a replay buffer with multi-epoch updates: the critic minimizes a TD loss while each actor is updated via importance-sampled policy gradients with clipped advantages. Experiments across writing, coding, and Minecraft tasks show CoLLM-CC consistently outperforms both Monte Carlo (MAGRPO) and CoLLM-DC, especially on long-horizon sparse-reward tasks where the latter two fail to converge or require substantially more samples.

## Results

On the five evaluation tasks, CoLLM-CC achieves the best or near-best performance across all domains: 95.2% on TLDR summarization, 95.0% on arXiv expansion, 75.2% pass rate on CoopHE code generation, 68.5% IoU on StrBuild, and 52.7% IoU on HouseBuild. On the long-horizon Minecraft tasks (H=4), CoLLM-CC's advantage is most pronounced, outperforming MAGRPO by 17.9 points on StrBuild IoU (68.5% vs 50.6%) and 1.8 points on HouseBuild IoU (52.7% vs 50.9%), while CoLLM-DC lags further behind at 44.6% and 46.8% respectively. On short-horizon writing tasks (H=1), all three MARL methods converge to comparable performance (93–95%), with CoLLM-DC even slightly edging CoLLM-CC on TLDR (95.4% vs 95.2%). CoLLM-CC also achieves the lowest inference cost among MARL methods (181–239 tokens/agent/turn) and fastest response times (1.8–9.5 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MAGRPO (Liu et al., AAAI 2026), the primary MARL baseline using K-sampling MA-REINFORCE, CoLLM-CC wins decisively on long-horizon and sparse-reward tasks: 75.2% vs 74.3% pass rate on CoopHE (H=2), 68.5% vs 50.6% IoU on StrBuild (H=4), and 52.7% vs 50.9% IoU on HouseBuild (H=4). CoLLM-CC also converges roughly 2.5× faster on CoopHE (~2000 vs ~5000 timesteps) and uses fewer total samples (8438 vs 9640 in the CoopHE training overhead comparison). On short-horizon writing tasks (H=1), the gap narrows to ~1.5–2 points in CoLLM-CC's favor, and CoLLM-DC actually matches or slightly exceeds CoLLM-CC (95.4% vs 95.2% on TLDR). Against the Discussion baseline (Du et al., 2023), which implements prompt-based multi-agent debate, CoLLM-CC dominates by large margins on every task: 95.2% vs 22.3% on TLDR, 75.2% vs 25.0% on CoopHE, and 68.5% vs 6.5% IoU on StrBuild, confirming that unoptimized prompt-based interaction protocols are far inferior to MARL-optimized decentralized collaboration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the centralized-training-decentralized-execution (CTDE) paradigm from classical cooperative MARL—specifically the centralized critic of MADDPG (Lowe et al., 2017) and the decentralized critic of COMA (Foerster et al., 2018)—into the LLM collaboration setting, where prior MARL-for-LLM work (e.g., MAGRPO) has relied on Monte Carlo policy gradients with K-sampling. The key departure is formalizing decentralized LLM collaboration as a Dec-POMDP and showing that actor-critic methods with a shared value function over joint history (CoLLM-CC) or per-agent local critics (CoLLM-DC) can replace the high-variance, exponentially costly rollout trees of MA-REINFORCE. The theoretical analysis (Propositions 4.1–4.3) makes explicit the variance–compute tradeoff of K-sampling, providing a principled justification for why critic-based methods are needed as horizons grow.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Decentralized LLM collaboration is practically important for parallel inference, flexible deployment across smaller models, and privacy, yet the paper shows that the standard Monte Carlo fine-tuning approaches become prohibitively sample-inefficient (K^(H−t) rollouts) and that decentralized critics fail to converge on long-horizon or sparse-reward tasks. The consistent superiority of CoLLM-CC across writing, coding, and Minecraft domains, including scaling to n=3 agents, provides a concrete recipe for training cooperative LLM agents without centralized execution at inference time. Open questions remain: the methods have only been tested up to 4 turns, online learning is unexplored, and whether relaxed communication or gradient-conflict mitigation for parameter-shared critics can close the remaining gap to centralized critics are left for future work.
