## Problem

Advertising platforms continually improve prediction models (pCTR, pCVR) with the expectation that better predictions yield better platform metrics, but the feedback loop between model quality, auction allocation, and autobidder strategy adaptation makes this relationship non-trivial. No prior work formally characterized when a refinement of the prediction information structure guarantees improvements in revenue, welfare, or liquid welfare across different auction formats and bidder types.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a user partition (defining the prediction model's granularity), true per-advertiser-user conversion probabilities, an auction format (FPA or VCG/SPA), and bidder specifications (tCPA target or MAX-CPA value, with or without budget) as input. It produces a monotonicity verdict (monotone or non-monotone) for each (bidder type, auction, budget) combination, along with explicit numerical counterexamples demonstrating the magnitude of metric decreases when non-monotonicity occurs. The refinement relation is a partial order on partitions, so the analysis applies to any pair of nested information structures rather than a single model swap.

## Method

The paper defines model improvement as a cluster-partition refinement (each fine cluster is contained in a coarse cluster), inspired by filtrations in probability theory, and proves that refinement improves all proper scoring losses via Jensen's inequality. For the positive FPA-tCPA result, revenue equals the weighted sum of max_i t_i·p_{i,C} over clusters, and since this max-of-linears function is convex, Jensen's inequality directly yields revenue (and welfare, under v_i = t_i) monotonicity. The VCG-MAX-CPA welfare result follows by the identical convexity argument with v_i replacing t_i. For negative results, the paper constructs explicit numerical instances (e.g., two tCPA bidders with four auctions) where refinement reallocates high-value impressions to lower-value bidders, reducing both revenue and welfare by 6.2%. The LP benchmark monotonicity is proven via a lifting argument: any feasible coarse allocation can be copied to all sub-clusters while preserving supply, budget, and objective constraints.

## Results

The paper's central finding is that ECM monotonicity is rare: only 3 of 8 studied settings guarantee that model refinement improves platform metrics. The positive results are (1) FPA with tCPA bidders without budgets (revenue and welfare monotone via Jensen's inequality), (2) VCG with MAX-CPA bidders without budgets (welfare monotone only), and (3) LP-based fractional allocation with budgets (welfare monotone; surrogate revenue monotone for tCPA). Non-monotonicity counterexamples demonstrate concrete losses: VCG/SPA with tCPA bidders loses 6.2% in both revenue and welfare; FPA with budget constraints loses 16.8% in revenue; VCG with MAX-CPA bidders loses 41.7% in revenue (while welfare still increases); and FPA with MAX-CPA bidders under designated multiplier profiles loses 66.2% in revenue and 0.09% in welfare.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external baselines but instead provides a systematic comparison across auction formats (FPA, VCG/SPA, LP benchmark) and bidder types (tCPA, MAX-CPA) with and without budget constraints. FPA is the only strategic auction format that guarantees revenue monotonicity, but only for tCPA bidders without budgets; it fails for MAX-CPA bidders (66.2% revenue loss) and for any bidder type with budgets (16.8% revenue loss). VCG/SPA provides welfare monotonicity only for MAX-CPA bidders without budgets, but fails on both revenue and welfare for tCPA bidders (6.2% loss) and on revenue for MAX-CPA bidders (41.7% loss). The LP benchmark is the only setting that handles budget constraints while preserving monotonicity, but it is not incentive compatible since it requires the platform to know private bidder parameters. The key trade-off identified is between incentive compatibility (FPA) and monotonicity under budgets (LP): no known mechanism achieves both simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the autobidding equilibrium framework of Aggarwal et al. (2019, 2024) and the liquid welfare benchmark of Dobzinski & Paes Leme (2014), but departs from prior work by studying how changes in prediction model granularity (formalized as partition refinement, inspired by filtrations in probability theory) propagate through auction equilibria to affect platform metrics. Prior autobidding literature analyzes bidder behavior given fixed prediction models, while prediction-quality literature (e.g., Vasile et al., 2017) notes misalignment between prediction accuracy and business objectives without formalizing the mechanism. This paper introduces the first formal ECM monotonicity framework that systematically characterizes when model refinement guarantees metric improvements across auction formats, bidder types, and budget constraints, using Jensen's inequality for positive results and explicit numerical constructions for negative results.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that model monotonicity holds in only 3 of 8 studied settings—challenges the default assumption in advertising platforms that deploying a more accurate pCTR/pCVR model will improve revenue or welfare, with direct implications for model deployment pipelines and A/B testing protocols. The identified trade-off between incentive compatibility and monotonicity under budget constraints (FPA is IC but non-monotone with budgets; LP is monotone but not IC) reveals a fundamental design tension with no known resolution. Open questions include whether the results extend beyond uniform bidding to non-uniform or learning-based autobidding, whether randomization (à la Mehta, 2022) can restore monotonicity in non-monotone settings, and whether the LP benchmark's monotonicity can be approximated by any incentive-compatible mechanism.
