## Problem

Existing street-level socioeconomic prediction methods fail to exploit three key learning signals in urban scenes: the compositional structure of object co-occurrence and spatial arrangement in visual encoding, task-relevant textual semantics, and the potential of unlabeled streets to actively participate in representation learning. Under extreme label scarcity (~5% annotation rate), these limitations compound, as weak multimodal representations reduce the discriminative power of embeddings learned from the small labeled set while the majority of streets remain excluded from the core training objective.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multiple street view images per street (varying count, 57K–101K total across datasets), a k=5 nearest-neighbor spatial proximity graph built from OpenStreetMap street segments, and a task-specific prompt string. Images are first processed by a frozen ADE20K segmentation model (48 urban categories retained from 150) and a VLM captioner (Sphinx); the output is a probability distribution over C socioeconomic classes (10 for street function, 4 for economic activity or house price) produced by a softmax head on GAT-refined street embeddings.

## Method

MetaStreet combines three components in a unified semi-supervised pipeline. The semantic-spatial visual encoder jointly models object co-occurrence (via Skip-gram–learned category embeddings from image-level category sets) and spatial adjacency (via a 4-connected pixel-level adjacency matrix), producing per-image representations S = Ã·diag(p)·C that are aggregated along each street with an LSTM. The task-aware textual encoder concatenates image captions and segmentation statistics into a street-level document, prepends a task-specific prompt, and extracts features from a frozen Qwen3-8B LLM's final hidden state; visual and textual embeddings are fused via cross-attention. A 2-layer GAT propagates information over the k-NN street graph, and a geography-aware InfoNCE contrastive loss extends supervision to unlabeled streets by letting them inherit negative samples from labeled neighbors (grounded in spatial autocorrelation), with the joint objective L = λ·L_sup + (1−λ)·L_con balancing supervised cross-entropy and contrastive objectives.

## Results

On the Wuhan dataset, MetaStreet achieves 51.42% ACC@1 and 48.34% Weighted-F1 on the 10-class street function prediction task, and 45.52% ACC and 43.46% Macro-F1 on the 4-class economic activity prediction task. On the Xi'an dataset, it reaches 53.26% ACC and 52.64% Macro-F1 for house price prediction, and 45.77% ACC for economic activity prediction. Across all four task-city combinations, MetaStreet improves ACC by 4.19–9.38 percentage points over the strongest baseline, yielding an average 6.16% relative ACC gain (p<0.01, paired t-test).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MetaStreet outperforms all 12 baselines on every metric across both Wuhan and Xi'an. The closest competitor is USPM (Chen et al., KDD 2024), the prior state-of-the-art multimodal+graph method; MetaStreet beats it by 4.41 pp in ACC@1 on street function (51.42 vs. 47.01) and by 1.83 pp in ACC on Wuhan economic activity (45.52 vs. 43.69). HybridSGN (Zhang et al., ISPRS 2024), the strongest visual+graph baseline, trails MetaStreet by 4.94 pp in ACC@1 on street function and by 1.84 pp in ACC on economic activity. Among unimodal methods, SemSeg (Zhou et al., IJCV 2019) and ResNet (He et al., CVPR 2016) perform worst, confirming that generic visual features or independent category proportions are insufficient for compositional urban scenes. VLM baselines (Qwen3-VL-235B zero-shot and Qwen3-VL-8B fine-tuned) underperform structured multimodal+graph methods despite strong visual reasoning, indicating that explicit spatial graph modeling and task-aware prompting are critical. Textual+graph KT-BERT (Zhang et al., ISPRS 2023) and multimodal SemiGTX (Cao et al., ISPRS 2025) both lag MetaStreet by 4–8 pp in ACC, highlighting the benefit of jointly modeling semantic-spatial structure, task-specific prompts, and geography-aware contrastive supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

MetaStreet synthesizes and extends three distinct research threads in street-level urban analytics: (1) visual encoding via pretrained encoders or segmentation-based proportion vectors, (2) multimodal captioning with LLMs (as in UrbanCLIP, KT-BERT), and (3) semi-supervised graph propagation (as in USPM, SemiGTX). Rather than building on a single prior architecture, it repurposes Skip-gram for category-level semantic embeddings, introduces a spatial adjacency matrix to capture compositional structure that proportion-based methods discard, and reframes the semi-supervised graph setting by converting unlabeled nodes from passive recipients into active contrastive anchors via a geography-aware negative sampling strategy grounded in spatial autocorrelation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical recipe for label-scarce urban analytics: the semantic-spatial encoding paradigm and geography-aware contrastive learning are transferable to other geospatial prediction tasks where relational structure implies semantic similarity. However, the method inherits vulnerabilities from its upstream perception modules (segmentation and captioning errors propagate downstream), and it models only static snapshots, leaving temporal evolution of socioeconomic conditions unaddressed. Cross-city generalization remains an open question, and the reliance on a frozen 8B LLM for textual encoding raises questions about whether the task-aware prompting gains would persist with smaller or more efficient language models.
