## Problem

Existing semi-supervised graph anomaly detection methods learn normality exclusively from a small set of labeled normal nodes, causing them to overfit annotated patterns and produce highly overlapping anomaly scores for normal and abnormal classes. This leads to elevated false positive and false negative rates, as normal nodes dissimilar to the labeled subset are misclassified as anomalies. The paper asks how to calibrate and refine the normality learned by a pre-trained teacher model by jointly exploiting both labeled and unlabeled data.

## Input / Output

The method takes an attributed graph (node feature matrix X ∈ R^{N×M} and adjacency matrix A ∈ R^{N×N}) along with a small set of labeled normal nodes (15% in experiments) and a frozen pre-trained semi-supervised GAD teacher model. The student model is a 2-layer GNN followed by an MLP head that outputs a scalar anomaly score per node. During training, an augmented graph is created by randomly masking a proportion ω (default 0.30) of attributes on labeled normal nodes to generate a second view for the consistency loss.

## Method

GraphNC is a teacher-student calibration framework with two jointly optimized components. ScoreDA minimizes the MSE between the student's per-node anomaly scores and the teacher's score distribution over all nodes, pulling normal and abnormal scores toward opposite ends of the score range. NormReg applies a perturbation-guided consistency loss (MSE between original and attribute-masked representations) exclusively on labeled normal nodes, enforcing compact normal representations to counteract inaccurate teacher scores. The total loss is L_ScoreDA + α·L_NormReg (α=0.01). A theoretical result (Theorem 4.1) proves that minimizing NormReg while applying ScoreDA shrinks the intra-class score variance of the normal class below the teacher's variance, thereby reducing both FPR and FNR under a sub-Gaussian score assumption.

## Results

GraphNC achieves the best average AUROC of 0.7533 and AUPRC of 0.3610 across six semi-supervised GAD benchmarks (15% labeled normal nodes), outperforming the second-best method RHO by 4.58% AUROC and 7.35% AUPRC on average. On the Photo dataset, GraphNC reaches 0.7693 AUROC and 0.3561 AUPRC, a substantial margin over RHO (0.7129/0.2337) and GGAD (0.6476/0.1442). The method also consistently surpasses its own teacher model GGAD (0.7003 AUROC / 0.2745 AUPRC average) by 5.30% AUROC and 8.65% AUPRC, demonstrating that the calibration framework adds value beyond the teacher's raw scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GraphNC (using GGAD as teacher) wins on 5 of 6 datasets for AUROC and 5 of 6 for AUPRC, with the sole AUROC loss to RHO on T-Finance (0.8340 vs. 0.8623) and AUPRC loss to RHO on T-Finance (0.3667 vs. 0.4893). Against its own teacher GGAD, GraphNC improves AUROC on all six datasets (e.g., +1.70 on Amazon, +11.65 on Photo) and AUPRC on all six (e.g., +4.81 on Amazon, +21.19 on Photo). The largest absolute gains over the runner-up RHO appear on Photo (+5.64 AUROC, +12.24 AUPRC) and Tolokers (+2.50 AUROC, −1.74 AUPRC where RHO edges ahead). Among the adapted unsupervised baselines, reconstruction-based methods (DOMINANT, AnomalyDAE) and one-class methods (OCGNN) are the strongest competitors on Amazon and T-Finance, yet GraphNC still leads by 4–13% AUROC on those datasets. The anomaly-generation-based GGAD is the closest single baseline overall, confirming that GraphNC's calibration adds meaningful value on top of the strongest existing semi-supervised approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

GraphNC positions itself as a post-hoc calibration layer on top of existing semi-supervised GAD methods, building most directly on GGAD (Qiao et al., 2024) as the default teacher but remaining agnostic to the teacher's architecture. Conceptually, it repurposes two well-studied semi-supervised learning ideas—score distillation (analogous to knowledge distillation) and perturbation-based consistency regularization (à la temporal ensembling)—into the graph anomaly detection setting, where prior work (GGAD, RHO, and adapted unsupervised methods) learns normality exclusively from a small labeled subset and tends to overfit. The paper explicitly distinguishes its approach from pseudo-labeling-based semi-supervised methods by avoiding the quantity-quality trade-off: it never assigns hard labels to unlabeled nodes, instead aligning continuous score distributions and enforcing representation consistency only on the labeled normal set.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design means any existing semi-supervised GAD model can be improved without retraining, which is practically valuable given the diversity of GAD architectures in use. The theoretical guarantee (Theorem 4.1) that the student's intra-class score variance strictly shrinks relative to the teacher provides a principled explanation for the FPR/FNR reductions, going beyond empirical observation. A key open question is the framework's dependence on the teacher providing at least coarse supervisory signal; the authors acknowledge that when the teacher is near-random, ScoreDA's benefit diminishes, and future work should explore reducing this dependency through additional supervision sources or expert knowledge.
