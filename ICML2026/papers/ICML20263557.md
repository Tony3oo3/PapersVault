## Problem

State-of-the-art text-to-video models, even billion-parameter DiT-based systems, produce temporally incoherent and physically implausible motion because they directly apply image-oriented Flow Matching objectives that treat each frame independently. This frame-wise, temporally decoupled flow fails to explicitly encode motion priors or inter-frame dependencies, forcing models to rely on implicit attention mechanisms for temporal structure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263557/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and generates an 81-frame video at 832×480 resolution and 15 fps. TFM is a training-time modification only: it changes the flow formulation and loss during fine-tuning but introduces zero additional inference cost or architectural changes, making it a drop-in replacement for any FM-based video generator. Fine-tuning uses LoRA (rank 32) on ~40k ShareGPT4Video samples, roughly 0.01% of the original pre-training data.

## Method

TFM reformulates the standard FM governing equation into a coupled system of ODEs across frames by adding inter-frame distance terms (Eq 6), so that the flow for each frame is explicitly constrained by its neighbors. Under the practical choices of Euclidean distance and fixed velocity direction, this reduces to a tridiagonal linear system Mū = C solved efficiently via the Thomas algorithm. The training loss is a residual-type objective (minimizing ‖Mū_θ − C‖²) rather than the solution-type loss of standard FM, which the paper proves is a 'consistent objective' that better ranks intermediate predictions. Theoretically, TFM is shown to yield strictly positive KL divergence when frame order is perturbed (unlike FM, which is invariant to such swaps), demonstrating enhanced temporal perception. The approach is model-agnostic, adds only one hyperparameter ρ=4, and requires no architectural modifications.

## Results

On VideoJam-Bench (motion-focused), TFM achieves a Motion score of 0.961, substantially outperforming CogVideoX1.5 (0.894), HunyuanVideo (0.898), and its base model Wan2.1 (0.888). On the general-purpose MovieGen Bench, TFM reaches 0.858 Motion versus 0.801–0.806 for all baselines, while maintaining competitive Appearance scores (0.783 and 0.795 respectively). In user studies, TFM receives a 74.3% preference rate over Wan2.1 on VideoJam-Bench motion quality and 64.6% on MovieGen motion quality, confirming that the motion gains are perceptually meaningful without sacrificing visual quality or semantic coherence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TFM is evaluated against three state-of-the-art open-source video generators: CogVideoX1.5-5B, HunyuanVideo-13B, and Wan2.1-T2V-14B (its own base model). On the motion metric, TFM decisively wins across both benchmarks: 0.961 vs. 0.894/0.898/0.888 on VideoJam-Bench and 0.858 vs. 0.801/0.806/0.805 on MovieGen. On appearance-related metrics, TFM is competitive with or slightly ahead of its base model Wan2.1 (0.783 vs. 0.780 on VideoJam; 0.795 vs. 0.791 on MovieGen) and marginally above CogVideoX1.5, indicating that the motion improvement does not come at the cost of visual quality. In the 2AFC user study, TFM is preferred over Wan2.1 in 74.3% of VideoJam motion comparisons and 64.6% of MovieGen motion comparisons, while also winning on overall quality (85.7% vs. Wan2.1 on VideoJam) and semantic coherence (82.8% vs. Wan2.1 on VideoJam). HunyuanVideo is the weakest baseline on user-study quality (56.2% on VideoJam, 53.8% on MovieGen), suggesting its motion and overall generation quality lag behind the other two competitors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Flow Matching framework (Lipman et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2023a) and its generalization to arbitrary geometries (Chen & Lipman, 2024) by reformulating the per-frame FM governing equation into a coupled system that explicitly encodes inter-frame distance constraints. Unlike prior video generation models (Wan2.1, HunyuanVideo, CogVideoX) that apply FM independently to each frame—effectively treating video as a bag of images—TFM introduces a tridiagonal ODE system across frames whose solution yields temporally correlated velocity fields. The paper also departs from externally-guided motion approaches (optical flow conditioning, physics-informed PDEs) by arguing that motion priors are learnable from data alone when the training objective properly encodes temporal structure, and it introduces a residual-type loss that is provably more consistent with the TFM equations than the standard solution-type FM loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TFM offers a model-agnostic, zero-inference-overhead training paradigm that can be plugged into any FM-based video generator, and the theoretical result that FM is provably blind to temporal ordering (zero KL divergence to frame permutations) while TFM achieves a positive lower bound provides a clean formal justification for why existing models fail at motion. The practical impact is significant: only ~0.01% of pre-training data is needed for fine-tuning, and the method requires no architectural changes. Open questions include whether the adjacent-frame constraint is optimal versus longer-range temporal dependencies, whether the approach generalizes to longer video sequences or other temporally structured modalities (audio, 3D), and whether the gains persist when applied from scratch rather than as a fine-tuning step on a single DiT architecture.
