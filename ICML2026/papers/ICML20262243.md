## Problem

Frontier image generative models (SD3.5, Flux, Infinity) can produce undesirable content such as copyrighted artistic styles or inappropriate objects, but existing removal methods are either computationally prohibitive (training-based), degrade under sequential edits (closed-form weight projections), or remain external and bypassable under white-box access (SAE-based or inference-time modules). No prior approach simultaneously achieves persistent, architecture-agnostic, and multi-concept removal across both diffusion and autoregressive frontier models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262243/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard text prompt as input and produces a generated image in which one or more target concepts (artistic styles or object categories) are suppressed while unrelated content is preserved. The transcoder operates on the text encoder's output embeddings (a single pooled vector for DMs or per-token projections for IARs), so no full-model inference is needed during training. The intervention modifies only the decoder weight matrix of the transcoder, adding a small per-image inference overhead (0.17–0.60 s) compared to the base model.

## Method

BLOCK replaces the architecture-mandated bottleneck layer (a linear projection or MLP between the text encoder and the generative backbone) with a transcoder that uses a TopK activation function instead of ReLU, enforcing a direct ℓ₀ sparsity constraint on the latent representation. The transcoder is trained in isolation—without backpropagation through the full model—using a composite loss of fidelity, multi-TopK (4k budget for progressive code utilization), and an auxiliary dead-unit penalty. For concept removal, the method identifies which latents activate for the target concept's tokens, then modifies the corresponding decoder columns to redirect their contribution to reproduce the empty token's output, effectively erasing the concept while preserving a coherent representation. Because the transcoder is permanently integrated into the model backbone rather than attached externally, the intervention persists under white-box access and supports sequential or simultaneous removal of multiple concepts without compounding degradation.

## Results

BLOCK achieves state-of-the-art concept removal across four frontier models (SD3.5, Flux, Infinity-2B, Infinity-8B) on both style and object removal. On Flux (12B parameters), BLOCK reaches 88.6% Unlearning Accuracy (UA) and 96.4% Cross-domain Retain Accuracy (CRA) for style removal, surpassing the best baseline (UCE) by 21.2 and 19.8 percentage points respectively. On Infinity-8B, BLOCK attains 95.5% CRA for style and 95.7% In-domain Retain Accuracy (IRA) for object removal. BLOCK also maintains competitive image quality (FID 51.67 on Flux vs. 55.56–58.90 for baselines) and achieves the lowest adversarial attack success rates (e.g., 25.21% Ring-A-Bell on SD3.5 vs. 58.08% original).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BLOCK outperforms both primary cross-architecture baselines (LOCOEDIT and UCE) on nearly every metric across all four models. On Flux, BLOCK leads UCE by 21.2 pp in style UA (88.6 vs. 67.4) and by 14.2 pp in object UA (93.2 vs. 87.5); LOCOEDIT lags further behind at 66.5% style UA. On SD3.5, UCE achieves a strong 84.5% object UA, but BLOCK still wins on object IRA (93.0 vs. 82.2) and CRA (67.5 vs. 60.9), while dominating style removal (69.6 vs. 61.8 UA). Among the additional SD3.5 baselines (EraseAnything, SAeUron, AGE, ConceptPrune, GLoCE, AdaVD), BLOCK achieves the highest score on every metric in Table 14, with the closest competitor (SAeUron) trailing by 3.1 pp in style UA. On the autoregressive Infinity models, BLOCK surpasses SafetyGap on all metrics, with the largest gap on Infinity-2B where BLOCK improves style IRA by 14.7 pp and object UA by 8 pp. In sequential multi-concept removal, baselines degrade sharply (LOCOEDIT drops >30 pp by N=10 on SD3.5), while BLOCK remains stable, confirming its advantage for iterative deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

BLOCK repurposes transcoders—originally developed for interpretable feature circuit analysis in LLMs (Dunefsky et al., 2024)—as a mechanism for concept removal in image generative models, a domain where they had not been applied. It borrows the multi-TopK and auxiliary dead-unit loss components from SAE training (Gao et al., 2024) to enforce sharper sparsity than the original ReLU-based transcoder. Conceptually, the paper departs from both training-based erasure (which backpropagates through the full model) and closed-form cross-attention editing (UCE, LOCOEDIT) by intervening at a single architecture-mandated text-to-backbone bottleneck layer, and from external SAE-based methods (SAeUron) by integrating the transcoder permanently into the backbone rather than attaching a removable module.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BLOCK addresses a practical deployment gap: prior methods either cannot scale to frontier transformer-based DMs and IARs, degrade under sequential multi-concept removal, or are trivially bypassed under white-box access. The finding that a single bottleneck-layer substitution suffices for effective concept suppression—without editing cross-attention throughout the network—suggests that text conditioning in modern architectures is more concentrated than previously assumed. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video generation, how it handles concepts that are not cleanly separable at the bottleneck (e.g., compositional or emergent concepts), and whether the ~100-minute transcoder training cost remains acceptable as model architectures evolve. The method's residual vulnerability to adversarial prompting (25% attack success on SD3.5) also signals that bottleneck-level intervention alone may not be sufficient for full safety guarantees.
