## Problem

U-statistics are fundamental for estimating parameters defined as expectations of symmetric kernels (e.g., Gini index, AUC, Kendall's τ), but acquiring the labels they require is often prohibitively expensive. Existing active inference methods for mean-type functionals do not extend to U-statistics because the combinatorial tuple structure and cross-sample dependence change the optimal sampling design. This paper identifies that the variance-optimal sampling rule for U-statistics is driven by the first-order Hoeffding projection residual rather than the raw prediction residual, and develops a full active inference framework with valid confidence intervals under a fixed labeling budget.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pool of unlabeled covariates X₁,…,Xₙ, a pre-trained predictive model μ(·) (e.g., XGBoost or an LLM), a labeling budget n_b ≪ n, a symmetric kernel h of degree r, and a small pilot labeled set used to estimate the first-order Hoeffding projections and fit the uncertainty score V(x). It outputs a point estimate of the target U-statistic θ*, a (1−α) confidence interval with asymptotically correct coverage, the data-adaptive sampling probabilities π̂_τ(X_i), and—when extended to U-estimation—a parameter vector θ̂ minimizing the active U-risk. The sampling is stochastic (Bernoulli draws per sample), so results are typically averaged over many replicates to report coverage and effective sample size.

## Method

The core estimator is an augmented inverse probability weighted (AIPW) U-statistic that combines a plug-in term over all predicted labels with an IPW correction over the actively queried true labels, corrected for selection bias. The key theoretical contribution is a variance-optimal sampling rule π*(X) ∝ √(E[|h₁(Y)−h₁^μ(Ŷ)|²|X]), where h₁ and h₁^μ are the first-order Hoeffding projections of the true and predicted kernels; this differs from the mean-estimation rule π ∝ √(E[|Y−Ŷ|²|X]) and reduces to it only when r=1 and h(y)=y. To control finite-sample variance from extreme inverse-probability weights, the authors introduce a Hajek-type random normalization and an adaptive trimming step that blends the active rule with uniform sampling. Asymptotic normality under the data-adaptive (plug-in) sampling policy is established via a common-random-number coupling argument that shows the difference between the plug-in and oracle estimators is o_p(n^{-1/2}). The framework is further extended to U-statistic-based empirical risk minimization (e.g., pairwise ranking), where the optimal sampling rule is derived via A-optimality of the covariance matrix and implemented through a two-stage pilot-then-active procedure.

## Results

Across all four real-world datasets, the proposed active U-statistic achieves substantially narrower confidence intervals than both baselines while maintaining near-nominal 90% coverage. On the ACS Income dataset (Gini index), active sampling yields roughly a 60% labeling-budget reduction relative to the classical IPW baseline. On the VitalDB perioperative dataset (Wilcoxon signed-rank) and the Political Bias dataset (Kendall's τ), active sampling requires approximately 20% fewer labels than the classical baseline and 10% fewer than the uniform AIPW baseline to achieve the same inferential precision. On the UCI Bike Sharing dataset (third central moment), the active method achieves 46–50% effective sample size gains over the classical baseline across all budgets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two main baselines: (1) the classical IPW U-statistic with uniform sampling, and (2) the uniform AIPW U-statistic (equivalent to the semi-supervised U-statistic of Kim et al., 2025). The proposed active method wins on every dataset and every budget level, achieving 20–60% effective sample size gains over the classical baseline and 10–20% gains over the uniform AIPW baseline. In Appendix C.2, the paper additionally compares against the sampling rule from Zrnic & Candès (2024a) (π ∝ √E[|Y−Ŷ|²|X]), which is optimal for mean estimation but suboptimal for U-statistics; the proposed rule (driven by the first-order Hoeffding projection residual |h₁(Y)−h₁^μ(Ŷ)|) uniformly outperforms it in both coverage and effective sample size. Under intentional predictive-model misspecification (linear model on VitalDB, Table 3), the active method still achieves 11–19% savings over the classical baseline, while the uniform AIPW baseline actually underperforms the classical estimator (negative savings of −6% to −11%), demonstrating that the efficiency gain comes from the data-driven sampling policy rather than prediction accuracy alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the active statistical inference framework of Zrnic & Candès (2024a), which was developed for convex M-estimation problems, to the fundamentally different setting of U-statistics with their combinatorial pairwise/higher-order structure. It builds on the AIPW methodology (Cassel et al., 1976) and Hajek normalization (Trotter & Tukey, 1956) from survey sampling, and subsumes the semi-supervised U-statistics of Kim et al. (2025) as a special case (uniform sampling). The key conceptual departure is that the variance-optimal sampling rule for U-statistics is driven by the residual in the first-order Hoeffding projection rather than the prediction residual that is natural for mean estimation, requiring a new theoretical analysis via Hoeffding decomposition and a CRN coupling argument to handle data-adaptive sampling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

U-statistics underpin widely used measures (Gini index, AUC, Kendall's tau, pairwise ranking), yet label acquisition for them is often prohibitively expensive; this paper provides the first principled active inference framework that achieves valid confidence intervals while reducing labeling budgets by 20–60% on real datasets. The extension to U-estimation (empirical risk minimization over U-risks) opens the door to label-efficient ranking and metric learning. Open questions include handling degenerate U-statistics where the first-order projection vanishes, obtaining finite-sample guarantees, and reducing the O(n^r) computational cost of the plug-in term for large-scale applications.
