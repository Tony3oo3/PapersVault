## Problem

Existing VLMs are trained and evaluated exclusively on high-bitrate, clean images, yet real-world deployment requires processing heavily compressed inputs. No benchmark existed to quantify how 11 diverse image codecs (traditional, learned, and generative) degrade VLM performance, and no method addressed the generalization gap that prevents VLMs from adapting to compression distortions independent of a specific codec.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265946/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The adapter takes a compressed RGB image (randomly cropped to 336×336 patches) together with a 12-dimensional one-hot vector encoding the codec identity (3 types) and bitrate level (4 levels), which is injected as a conditional signal into the vision encoder's positional embeddings. The output is standard VLM predictions—VQA answers, image captions, OCR text, and multiple-choice selections—evaluated across seven benchmarks (POPE, GQA, COCO-Caption, OCRBench, MMBench, MME, SEEDBench) spanning over one million compressed images. Notably, the adapter generalizes to unseen codecs (HM, MLICpp, DiffEIC) by mapping them to the nearest trained codec family, and transfers across different VLM backbones (Qwen-VL2.5, InternVL3).

## Method

The proposed Conditional Vision Encoder (CVE) is a lightweight adapter that fine-tunes only the ViT-based vision encoder of a VLM via knowledge distillation, leaving the language backbone frozen. A one-hot vector for codec type and bitrate level is mapped through an embedding layer to produce a codec condition embedding, which is added to the RoPE positional embeddings at every spatial token position, inspired by conditional diffusion model fusion strategies. Training minimizes the MSE between features extracted from compressed images through the CVE and features from the corresponding uncompressed images through the frozen original encoder, effectively distilling clean-image representations into the compressed domain. A single adapter trained on JPEG, ELIC, and ILLM at four bitrates (11w COCO images, 100k iterations on 4×A100) achieves 10–30% BD-Metric improvements across all tested codecs and generalizes to previously unseen codecs and VLM architectures without retraining.

## Results

The proposed VLM adapter achieves BD-Metric improvements of 10%–30% across diverse codecs and bitrate levels. On Qwen-VL2.5-3B, the adapter yields the largest gains on JPEG (BD-POPE +12.62, BD-OCRBench +52.51, BD-MME +285.4) and consistent improvements on learning-based (ELIC: +3.42 POPE, +10.51 OCR) and generative (ILLM: +3.52 POPE, +14.34 OCR) codecs. The method also generalizes to unseen codecs (HM, MLICpp, DiffEIC) and a different VLM family (InternVL3-1B), with MLICpp achieving +3.32 BD-POPE despite never being seen during training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its adapter against two ICM baselines on the POPE benchmark (Table 6). The base codec TIC (Lu et al., DCC 2022) achieves a BD-POPE of 0.00, while TransTIC (Chen et al., ICCV 2023), which fine-tunes the codec for machine perception, reaches only 0.18. The proposed adapter applied to TIC (TIC-Adapter) jumps to 2.43, and combining both approaches (TransTIC-Adapter) yields the best result of 3.02, confirming that addressing both the information gap (via ICM) and the generalization gap (via the adapter) is most effective. The adapter also generalizes to previously unseen codecs: on HM, MLICpp, and DiffEIC (never used in training), it still produces positive BD-Metric gains across all six benchmarks for both Qwen-VL2.5-3B and InternVL3-1B, with MLICpp achieving +3.32 BD-POPE and +5.73 BD-OCRBench. The authors note that TransTIC's d3 variant (which jointly optimizes codec and VLM encoder) outperforms the adapter alone, but requires training a separate model per bitrate, whereas the proposed method is a single unified model generalizing across all codecs and bitrates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of image compression and VLM evaluation, where no prior benchmark existed. It departs from Image Coding for Machines (ICM/FCM) work, which is tied to specific codecs and fixed backbones (e.g., Faster R-CNN, Mask R-CNN), by proposing a codec-agnostic adapter that conditions the VLM vision encoder on compression metadata. The adapter design borrows the conditional-embedding + RoPE fusion strategy from diffusion models (Rombach et al., Preechakul et al.), repurposing it for distortion-aware feature distillation rather than generative denoising. The information-gap / generalization-gap decomposition (Eq. 1) is a new analytical framing not present in prior ICM or VLM literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As VLMs are increasingly deployed in bandwidth-constrained pipelines (mobile, IoT, streaming), understanding and mitigating compression-induced degradation is practically urgent. The 1M+ image benchmark across 11 codecs and 9 VLMs provides the first systematic reference point, and the lightweight adapter offers a drop-in solution that generalizes to unseen codecs without retraining. Key limitations include the exclusion of proprietary VLMs (API cost), the irreducibility of the information gap (which can only be addressed by better codecs), and the fact that the adapter was validated primarily on Qwen-VL2.5 and InternVL3. Open questions include whether the decomposition holds for video compression, whether the adapter transfers to next-generation VLM architectures, and whether the information gap can be further narrowed by jointly optimizing codecs and VLMs.
