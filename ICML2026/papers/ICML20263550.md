## Problem

Compiler auto-tuning faces a dichotomy: traditional search methods (GA, Bayesian optimization, RL) treat the compiler as a black box without modeling the semantics of program transformations, while recent LLM-based approaches rely on superficial pattern matching between source code and optimization flags, failing to capture the causal mechanism by which passes alter code structure to yield performance gains. ECCO addresses this by bridging interpretable causal reasoning with combinatorial search.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263550/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 56-dimensional static Autophase feature vector extracted from LLVM IR (capturing basic block topology, instruction mix, memory operations, and control flow statistics) as its sole input at inference time. It produces a Chain-of-Thought rationale that explicitly predicts per-pass feature deltas and performance outcomes, followed by a JSON array of LLVM pass flags drawn from a fixed vocabulary of 100+ valid passes. The rationale is constrained to appear derived solely from static features, even though it is trained on ground-truth dynamic evidence.

## Method

ECCO operates in three phases. First, a reverse-engineering pipeline prunes CFSAT-generated sequences via iterative greedy removal (reducing average length from 18.5 to 4.5 passes), then performs forensic reconstruction extracting structural IR diffs, Autophase feature deltas, and marginal cycle improvements per pass, and finally uses a teacher model (Claude-4.5-Sonnet) to distill 'simulated predictive reasoning' rationales that encode causal logic while appearing to rely only on static features. Second, the Qwen2.5-Instruct policy is trained via SFT on this evidence-driven dataset followed by GRPO reinforcement learning with a composite reward combining format validity and relative speedup over LLVM -O3. Third, at inference, the LLM acts as a Strategist producing an intent distribution over optimization categories, which soft-biases the mutation operator of a Genetic Algorithm (the Tactician) via a probabilistic mixing formula that preserves ergodicity, allowing the GA to recover from LLM hallucinations while being guided toward high-utility subspaces.

## Results

ECCO (Qwen2.5-7B, Best-of-32) achieves an average 24.44% reduction in cycles over the LLVM -O3 baseline across seven benchmark suites, outperforming the strongest traditional search baseline CFSAT (23.70%) and GRACE (23.14%). The largest per-benchmark gains are on cbench (35.19%), chstone (35.50%), and mibench (27.12%). On real end-to-end execution time (PolyBench), ECCO achieves 44.54% runtime reduction at a 5-minute tuning budget versus 42.87% for CFSAT, and 49.65% at 20 minutes versus 49.48%. All direct LLM prompting baselines (GPT-5, Kimi-K2, Qwen3-Coder, DeepSeek-V3.2) cluster around 16.0–16.6%, well below even the weakest traditional heuristic (TPE at 19.88%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ECCO outperforms all 12 baselines on the average OverO3 metric. Against traditional search heuristics, ECCO leads CFSAT (23.70%) by 0.74 pp, GRACE (23.14%) by 1.30 pp, CompTuner (22.88%) by 1.56 pp, PDCAT (22.79%) by 1.65 pp, GA (22.14%) and OpenTuner (22.14%) by 2.30 pp, RIO (21.98%) by 2.46 pp, and TPE (19.88%) by 4.56 pp. Against direct LLM prompting with Best-of-32 sampling, ECCO surpasses Kimi-K2 (16.61%) by 7.83 pp, GPT-5-chat (16.56%) by 7.88 pp, Qwen3-Coder (16.25%) by 8.19 pp, and DeepSeek-V3.2 (16.06%) by 8.38 pp. Notably, ECCO wins on every individual benchmark except blas (12.99% vs. CFSAT's 16.44%) and npb (32.97% vs. CFSAT's 34.65%), where CFSAT retains a narrow edge. The paper also reports that Ant Colony Optimization achieves 24.70% average, marginally above ECCO, though this is mentioned in the text rather than the main table.

## Relation to Prior Work

ECCO builds directly on CFSAT (Pan et al., 2025) for generating initial high-quality pass sequences, which are then pruned via iterative greedy removal to isolate causal drivers. The RL stage adopts GRPO (Shao et al.) for exploration beyond imitation. Conceptually, the paper departs from both black-box search heuristics (GA, Bayesian optimization, RL) and prior LLM-based approaches by introducing a forensic reverse-engineering pipeline that converts raw optimization trajectories into evidence-annotated causal rationales, and by decoupling semantic planning (LLM) from combinatorial execution (GA) in a Strategist-Tactician paradigm rather than relying on either alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that even frontier LLMs (GPT-5, DeepSeek-V3.2) with Best-of-32 sampling underperform traditional search heuristics on compiler phase-ordering, challenging the assumption that general-purpose reasoning capability transfers to combinatorial systems tasks. The quantified ~5% 'tactical gap' between standalone LLM inference (~19.4%) and the collaborative system (24.44%) provides concrete evidence that fine-grained pass ordering requires structured search rather than generative modeling. Open questions include whether the forensic evidence approach generalizes beyond LLVM 10 to newer compiler architectures, whether the 56-feature Autophase representation is sufficient for more complex vectorization workloads (where interpretability scores dip), and whether the Strategist-Tactician decomposition extends to other combinatorial optimization domains.
