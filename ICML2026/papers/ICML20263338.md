## Problem

Next-token prediction models trained on stochastic sequences perform well in-distribution but can incur constant regret when the state sequence is adversarially chosen. The paper asks whether any next-token predictor can be minimally modified (in total variation distance) to guarantee sublinear adversarial regret, and whether this robustification is possible under the bounded context windows inherent to transformer architectures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263338/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an existing next-token prediction model M₀ (implementing distribution D₀), a known utility function U: A×Θ→[-1,1], and a sequence of previously observed tokens as input, and outputs a next-token distribution at each step. For bounded-context robustification, the robustified model requires a slightly larger context window L' > L, using L tokens for the original distribution and Δ = L'−L additional tokens for the regret-monitoring mechanism. The output model is a hybrid that returns M₀'s prediction in-distribution and switches to a Polya urn prediction when a regret threshold is exceeded.

## Method

The core mechanism is Algorithm 1, which monitors the cumulative regret of quantal best responses to M₀'s predictions against the regret of a Polya urn baseline; when the gap exceeds a threshold of (1/√T)log|A| + √(8(1+α)log T/s), the model permanently switches to Polya urn predictions, guaranteeing O(log(|A|·T)/√T) worst-case regret with TV distance |A|·T^{−α} from the original distribution. For bounded context, the paper proves an impossibility result (Theorem 4.1) showing some L-bounded models are Θ(1)-far from any low-regret L-bounded model, then shows that expanding the context to L' restores robustification with O(1/√(L'−L)) regret via an AverageRestartHedge-style averaging over Δ shifted copies of Algorithm 1. The transformer construction (Theorem 5.1) adds exactly 4 layers to a base transformer to implement the Polya urn computation, QBR evaluation, regret comparison, and switching logic using self-attention for rolling averages and positional-encoding-based retrieval. Empirically, small NanoDO transformers are trained on robustified distributions using a masking procedure that discards the loss on high-regret prefixes of Polya urn sequences, achieving vanishing regret while preserving in-distribution performance.

## Results

Theoretically, the paper proves that for unbounded-context models, any next-token prediction distribution can be robustified to a low-regret distribution with TV distance at most |A|·T^(−α) (Theorem 3.1), while for bounded-context models, robustification is impossible at the same context length (Theorem 4.1) but achievable with a modest context increase L′ > L yielding O(1/√(L′−L)) per-round regret (Theorem 4.2). Empirically, using the NanoDO transformer (context 1024, 256-dim embeddings, 3 layers), ROBUST_BERNOULLI achieves vanishing regret on all eight ground-truth distributions while maintaining a Next-Token TV distance of only 0.0199±0.0001 from the original BERNOULLI model, compared to 0.1529±0.0003 for the POLYAURN baseline. The full-sequence TV distance between ROBUST_BERNOULLI and BERNOULLI is 0.7602±0.0267, which the authors note is an artifact of compounding tiny per-token differences over 1024 steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external baselines; instead, it validates the robustification procedure by comparing three variants of the same NanoDO transformer architecture. ROBUST_BERNOULLI (the robustified model) matches the in-distribution performance of the original BERNOULLI model (both achieve negative regret ≈ −0.16 on the training distribution) while also achieving vanishing regret on all out-of-distribution and adversarially drifting sequences, matching the POLYAURN model's regret guarantees. In terms of distributional fidelity, ROBUST_BERNOULLI is substantially closer to the original BERNOULLI distribution than POLYAURN is: the Next-Token TV distance is 0.0199 vs. 0.1529 (roughly 8× smaller), confirming that the robustification procedure preserves the original model's predictive behavior while adding adversarial robustness. The POLYAURN model, which has no distributional information, achieves the same regret guarantees but at the cost of completely abandoning the original distribution (full-sequence TV distance of 1.0000). These results demonstrate that the theoretical robustification guarantee (Theorem 3.1) is practically achievable with a small transformer trained via a simple masking procedure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two well-studied areas—adversarial online learning (Hedge, regret minimization) and autoregressive next-token prediction (transformer architectures)—by formalizing a 'robustification' problem: minimally modifying a next-token model so that quantal best responses to its predictions achieve adversarial regret guarantees. The key conceptual link is that quantal best responses to the Polya urn process closely simulate Hedge (Lemma 2.3), and the bounded-context impossibility result draws on de Bruijn sequence constructions to show information-theoretic limits. For the bounded-context positive result, the paper adapts the AverageRestartHedge framework of Schneider & Vodrahalli (2024), and the transformer expressiveness construction relies on the formal model and attention-pattern techniques of Sanford et al. (2024). Unlike Park et al. (2025), which empirically studies existing LLMs as game agents, this paper provides clean theoretical characterizations of when robustification is possible and what architectural modifications suffice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The impossibility result (Theorem 4.1) establishes a fundamental limitation of standard bounded-context transformer architectures: no amount of training can make them simultaneously distributionally faithful and adversarially robust, which has direct implications for deploying LLMs in strategic decision-making. The positive results—that a constant number of additional layers suffices for unbounded context, and that a modest context increase (L' > L) restores near-optimal regret for bounded context—provide concrete architectural guidance. However, the empirical validation is limited to binary-state prediction with a minimal NanoDO transformer on simple distributions, leaving open whether these robustification procedures scale to real LLMs, multi-dimensional state spaces, or practical training regimes. The exponentially small TV distance guarantee also raises a practical tension: the robustified distribution is nearly indistinguishable from the original, making it unclear whether standard training objectives can efficiently learn the perturbation.
