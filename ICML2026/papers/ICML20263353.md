## Problem

Diffusion and flow-matching models learn complex data distributions but provide only individual samples, not the pathways connecting them. Standard latent-space interpolations traverse low-density regions and ignore the geometry of the learned distribution, while the global modal structure, barriers, and connectivity remain unexplored. The paper further identifies a fundamental tension: paths that maximize likelihood (minimum energy paths) produce perceptually unrealistic 'cartoon' intermediates in high dimensions, because high density does not imply high probability mass without accounting for entropy.

## Input / Output

The method takes two data samples (e.g., 256×256 ImageNet images encoded to a 4×32×32 VAE latent space, or protein conformations in SE(3) or R³) and a pretrained diffusion/flow model providing only the learned velocity field b_t and score s_t—no retraining or explicit energy function is required. It outputs a discrete string of N+1 equally arc-length-spaced configurations (typically N=50–70) connecting the two endpoints, evolved across the full generative time interval t∈[0,1]. For principal curves, each string image is additionally accompanied by a stochastic walker whose Voronoi-cell samples are averaged via EMA to produce the final path.

## Method

The core contribution is adapting the string method from computational chemistry to time-dependent score functions from pretrained generative models. A curve of N+1 points evolves under a velocity field v_t with an arc-length constraint enforced by iterative reparametrization (cubic spline redistribution), while endpoints follow the probability flow ODE. Three regimes are controlled by the score weight γ_t and temperature T: pure transport (γ=0) yields geometrically unmotivated morphs; gradient-dominated dynamics (γ≫1, T=0) recover minimum energy paths that maximize likelihood but traverse unrealistic high-density modes; finite-temperature dynamics (γ≫1, T>0) compute principal curves where stochastic walkers sample Voronoi cells and drag string images toward their conditional expectations, balancing energy and entropy to stay within the typical set. A key practical detail is quenching γ_t→0 as t→1 to avoid amplifying score estimation errors near the data distribution, where Stein's identity degrades.

## Results

The paper's central quantitative finding is the likelihood-realism paradox: MEP intermediates (γ→∞, T=0) reach log-likelihoods substantially exceeding the ImageNet validation set distribution (Figure 6), yet appear perceptually unrealistic ('cartoonish'), while principal curves at T=0.9 produce intermediates whose likelihoods align with the typical set and appear realistic. Varying the score weight γ from 15 to 10⁻² at T=0 shows a continuous transition from abstract single-color images (high likelihood) to visually plausible morphs (typical likelihood). For protein folding (BBA, Chignolin), converged MEPs achieve significantly lower free energy than initial pure-transport strings when projected onto TIC coordinates, with intermediates preserving secondary structure and avoiding steric clashes. The method operates with N=50–70 string images and requires no retraining of the underlying model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against external baselines in a quantitative sense; instead, it compares its three internal regimes (pure transport, MEP, principal curve) against each other and validates findings against prior qualitative observations. The MEP regime reproduces and explains the 'cartoon' phenomenon reported by Guth et al. (2025) and Karczewski et al. (2025): as γ increases, intermediates become increasingly stylized and their log-likelihoods rise well above the ImageNet validation distribution, confirming that likelihood maxima lie outside the typical set. The principal curve regime (T>0) resolves this paradox by incorporating entropic effects, yielding realistic morphs whose likelihoods match the typical set—something pure transport achieves only by accident (no geometric grounding) and MEPs cannot. For protein pathways, the method is compared implicitly against pure transport (γ=0) initialization: the converged MEP consistently achieves lower free energy and more physically plausible intermediates, demonstrating that the string relaxation step is essential. No direct comparison is made with other pathway-finding methods (e.g., nudged elastic band, Onsager-Machlup minimization) or with existing image morphing approaches (e.g., DiffMorpher), which is a limitation for assessing relative performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the classical string method from computational chemistry (E et al. 2002; Ren et al. 2007) and its finite-temperature extension (E et al. 2005) to the setting of learned score functions from diffusion and flow-matching models. The key departure is that prior string methods require explicit, time-independent energy functions, whereas here the energy landscape V_t = -log ρ_t is time-dependent and only accessible through its gradient (the learned score). The finite-temperature regime directly computes principal curves (Hastie & Stuetzle, 1989), and the framework is grounded in the stochastic interpolant formalism (Albergo & Vanden-Eijnden, 2023). The paper also connects to recent observations of 'cartoon' images in diffusion models (Guth et al. 2025; Karczewski et al. 2025), providing a geometric explanation via concentration of measure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, retraining-free tool for probing the modal structure of pretrained diffusion models—identifying modes, characterizing barriers, and mapping connectivity—which is currently absent from the generative modeling toolkit. The explanation of the likelihood-realism paradox as a consequence of concentration of measure (rather than a model defect) reframes a known observation and suggests that entropy-aware methods are essential for realistic high-dimensional generation. Limitations include dependence on the underlying model's quality and score estimation errors near t=1; open questions include scaling to text-to-image models, theoretical convergence guarantees, and combining string dynamics with conditional generation for targeted pathway computation.
