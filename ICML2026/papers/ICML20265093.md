## Problem

Codebook-based generators built on masked language model transformers have become highly effective in text and vision, yet remain underused for tabular data because codebooks typically act as information bottlenecks, whereas synthetic tabular generation requires a code space larger than the training sample with additional codes that decode to sensible new rows. Naive quantization in this low-dimensional, data-scarce regime risks collapsing to memorization of observed rows rather than generalization.

## Input / Output

The method takes a mixed-type tabular table (numerical and categorical columns) and produces synthetic rows of the same schema. Numerical features are first mapped to [0,1] via empirical CDF, then to unconstrained Euclidean space via the inverse normal CDF before tokenization; categorical features are one-hot encoded. Each row is serialized into a fixed-length token sequence of length 2·d_num + d_cat (one primary and one residual LFQ token per numerical feature, plus one-hot categorical tokens), and synthetic rows are generated by autoregressively sampling tokens in a coarse-to-fine order (categorical → primary numerical → residual numerical) and decoding back through the frozen LFQ decoder and inverted marginal transforms.

## Method

Q-Tab introduces a residual lookup-free quantizer (LFQ) that jointly tokenizes all numerical features into a shared combinatorial code space: a primary sign-quantization level fixes a coarse orthant, and a residual sign-quantization level refines within that orthant, with bit-flip corruption applied only to residual bits. This residual-only corruption induces a kernel whose support components are exactly the orthants of the encoder-latent space, which the paper formalizes via a moving-target regression analysis (Propositions 4.1–4.5) showing that cross-orthant variance and drift channels vanish, leaving only manageable within-orthant dynamics. A BERT-style MLM transformer is then trained on the resulting token sequences with selective label smoothing on residual numerical tokens to activate the latent generalization structure in the code space, and the LFQ decoder is adversarially trained to keep barycentric reconstructions on the data manifold. The combination of orthant-local corruption, residual label smoothing, and adversarial decoding yields state-of-the-art predictive utility (23% average MLE improvement) while matching the distributional fidelity of diffusion-based generators.

## Results

Q-Tab achieves a 4.6% average MLE gap to real-data-trained models across six UCI benchmarks, improving over the best prior method TabDiff (6.0%) by 23.3% relative. On direct label prediction, Q-Tab exceeds TabDiff by 20.07% in average AUC improvement over the XGBoost baseline (4.02% vs. 3.33%). For distributional fidelity, Q-Tab matches TabDiff closely (DF gap 2.2% vs. 2.0%) while attaining the best average α-Precision (0.9863) and β-Recall (0.5150), and achieves the best MLE (3.8%) among the top three methods on the DCR privacy splits.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Q-Tab outperforms all six baselines on average MLE gap. Against the closest competitor TabDiff (6.0% gap), Q-Tab achieves a 23.3% relative reduction to 4.6%, with the largest per-dataset gains on Beijing (RMSE .536 vs. .568) and Default (AUC .762 vs. .760). TabSyn (6.1%) is also clearly beaten, while TabDDPM (9.5%) and TabNat (11.7%) trail further behind. The older GAN/VAE baselines CTGAN (28.3%) and TVAE (28.8%) are substantially outperformed. On direct label prediction (Table 2), Q-Tab leads TabDiff by 20.07% in average AUC improvement (4.02% vs. 3.33%) and TabSyn by 98% (4.02% vs. 2.03%), while on the privacy–utility trade-off (Table 3) Q-Tab attains the best MLE (3.8%) among the top three methods with competitive DCR (.529) and DF (2.2%), yielding the most favorable overall balance without temperature tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

Q-Tab extends the established three-stage pipeline for tabular generation (Sklar's theorem decomposition into marginals + dependence + inversion) by replacing the Stage-2 dependence model with a codebook-based discrete token representation, drawing on lookup-free quantization (LFQ) from visual generation (Yu et al., 2024) and residual VQ from audio coding (Zeghidour et al., 2021). The key conceptual departure is inverting the codebook's role: in images/video, codebooks act as information bottlenecks compressing high-dimensional signals, whereas in tabular data the code space must be *larger* than the training set to support novel rows. To make this work, the paper introduces residual LFQ with bit-flip corruption restricted to secondary bits, and provides a learning-theoretic analysis (Nadaraya-Watson kernel regression, moving-target stochastic approximation) that explains why the residual structure's orthant-locality is critical for stable training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Q-Tab establishes codebook-based latent modeling as a viable and competitive paradigm for tabular generation, achieving SOTA predictive utility (23% MLE improvement) and label prediction (20%) while matching diffusion-based distributional fidelity. The theoretical framework—showing that corruption induces kernel regression and that support-locality eliminates cross-compartment variance/drift channels—provides a principled lens for designing future discrete generative models beyond tabular data. Key limitations include linear sequence-length scaling with feature count (limiting high-dimensional tables), direct one-hot encoding of categoricals (suboptimal under high cardinality), and fixed hyperparameters; open directions include compact tokenization via latent downsampling, learned categorical embeddings, and adaptive corruption that controls support-component occupancy.
