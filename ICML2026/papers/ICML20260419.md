## Problem

LLMs are widely deployed for zero-shot annotation and LLM-as-a-judge tasks under the implicit assumption that user-provided definitions govern model behavior, yet models develop internalized concepts through pre-training and alignment that may conflict with user specifications. No prior work quantified whether definition alignment (as opposed to text memorization) predicts annotation performance, characterized the limits of prompting as an error-correction mechanism, or examined how models respond to misaligned task definitions while maintaining high confidence.

## Input / Output

The annotation pipeline takes a text instance paired with a task definition prompt (zero-shot, aligned, few-shot, or misaligned variants) and produces a binary class label plus a verbalized confidence score on a 0–100 scale, enabling evaluation of closed-source models that do not expose token probabilities. For the DSF diagnostic, the input is a model's free-text explanation of the target concept (elicited via a single prompt) and the dataset's reference definition; the output is a consensus cosine similarity across six sentence encoders. For memorization baselines, a 40% word-count prefix is fed to the model for continuation, and the generated suffix is scored against the ground-truth remainder using ROUGE-L, BERTScore, and embedding similarity.

## Method

The paper introduces Definition-Specific Familiarity (DSF), which prompts each model to explain the target concept in its own words and computes the mean cosine similarity between that explanation and the dataset's operational definition across six diverse sentence encoders (MiniLM, MPNet, BGE-large, E5-large, Instructor-large, OpenAI text-embedding-3-small), yielding an encoder-agnostic alignment score computable without labeled data. Steerability is quantified via Rescue Rate (probability of correcting a zero-shot error under prompted conditions) and Corruption Rate (probability of breaking a correct prediction), with decision stickiness characterized through the relationship between zero-shot confidence and correctability. Misalignment is probed via concept substitution—applying definitions from semantically related but distinct datasets across six scope variants—and analyzed through mixed-effects logistic regression with message-level random intercepts across nine models, five toxicity datasets, and ten prompting conditions including two DSPy automated optimization baselines.

## Results

Across nine LLMs and five toxicity datasets, Definition-Specific Familiarity (DSF) is positively associated with zero-shot annotation accuracy after controlling for dataset identity (partial r = +0.41, 95% CI [+0.15, +0.62], N = 54 model-dataset pairs), while three text-memorization metrics (ROUGE-L, BERTScore, embedding similarity) show no positive association (partial r = −0.19, −0.15, −0.16). Only 34.8% of zero-shot errors are rescued by aligned definitions, few-shot examples, or DSPy optimization, with high-confidence errors (>0.9) dropping to just 20.8% rescue. Under misaligned definitions, models shift prediction bias by −7% to +13% depending on definition scope, yet verbalized confidence remains flat at 84–91%, making confidence unreliable for detecting definition-policy mismatch. Definition choice induces 17% accuracy variation across conditions, compared to only 5% variation from model choice.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is between Definition-Specific Familiarity (DSF) and three text-memorization proxies (ROUGE-L, BERTScore, embedding similarity) as predictors of annotation accuracy. After controlling for dataset identity, DSF yields a positive partial correlation (+0.41) while all three memorization metrics are non-positive (−0.19, −0.15, −0.16), establishing conceptual alignment as the operative signal rather than text overlap. Among prompting strategies, aligned definitions improve mean accuracy by only 2.2 points over zero-shot (82.0% vs. 80.3%), few-shot by 1.2 points, and DSPy optimization actually degrades two Llama models (Llama-3.1-8B drops from 76.7% to 72.3%; Llama-3.3-70B from 80.5% to 77.5%), consistent with prior findings that prompt optimizers can underperform unoptimized baselines. On the steerability front, Mistral-Small-24B achieves the highest rescue rate (44.2%) despite being substantially smaller than Llama-3.1-70B (31.3%), showing that steerability is not monotonic in model size. Under misaligned definitions, the narrowest scope (Fox News hate speech) produces the highest confidence (90.6%) and even outperforms the aligned condition by 0.6% accuracy, while the broadest (Gaming toxicity) drops accuracy to 76.4%—yet confidence remains at 88.5%, confirming that confidence cannot flag definition errors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM-as-annotator literature (Gilardi et al., 2023; Zheng et al., 2023; Baumann et al., 2025) by shifting the explanatory focus from prompt configuration to model-internalized conceptual priors. It draws on steerability frameworks from text generation (Chang et al., 2026; Miehling et al., 2025) but applies them to discrete label prediction, where the output space is a single token rather than a free-form sequence. The introduction of Definition-Specific Familiarity (DSF) repurposes sentence-embedding similarity—previously used for contamination detection (Golchin & Surdeanu, 2024; Shi et al., 2024)—as a model-task alignment diagnostic, explicitly contrasting conceptual alignment with text memorization. The confidence analysis builds on calibration work (Geng et al., 2024; Han et al., 2025) but tests a specific failure mode: whether confidence can detect definition-policy mismatch, a question prior work has not addressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that only 34.8% of zero-shot errors are rescued by any prompting strategy (definitions, few-shot examples, DSPy optimization, or multi-turn correction) directly challenges the common practitioner assumption that better prompting can fix annotation quality, and the 17%-vs-5% gap between definition-choice and model-choice effects inverts the usual model-selection-first workflow. The calibration failure under misalignment—models remain 85–91% confident while applying incorrect definitions—poses a concrete deployment risk for pipelines that use confidence thresholds as quality gates. Open questions include whether DSF generalizes to multi-class, span-level, or open-ended annotation tasks; whether causal interventions (not just correlational evidence) can confirm that definition alignment drives performance; and whether matched base/post-trained model pairs could isolate which training stage produces the concept anchoring that resists prompting.
