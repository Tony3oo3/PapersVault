## Problem

Existing MLLMs for long-video understanding rely on uniform frame sampling and single-turn inference, diluting sparse critical evidence within extensive redundancy. While early clue-seeking prototypes exist, they depend on hand-crafted heuristics, decouple localization from reasoning into isolated single-turn modules, and lack end-to-end optimization for multi-hop evidence aggregation. The paper addresses how to enable native multi-turn tool invocation for long-video reasoning while solving two core challenges: attention dispersion from interleaving heterogeneous clue-seeking and answer-reasoning tasks, and uncontrolled context growth across multiple tool invocations.

## Input / Output

The model receives a long video sampled at 2 fps (capped at 768 frames) with an initial 16k visual-token overview budget, plus a text query. During inference, the model autonomously generates structured tool directives specifying a temporal window [t_s, t_e] and a discrete visual token quota (coarse: 2048, medium: 4096, fine: 6144 tokens), which an external VideoCrop tool uses to extract and resample the target segment. The process iterates up to 8 rounds; the final output is a direct answer synthesized from all accumulated high-resolution observations, with the model deciding when evidence is sufficient to terminate.

## Method

Video-o3 performs multi-hop reasoning within a single shared attention context, where the model alternates between clue-seeking (invoking VideoCrop to inspect specific segments) and answer-reasoning (synthesizing the final response) without resetting context between turns. Two key mechanisms enable stable training: (1) Task-Decoupled Attention Masking (TDAM) applied to 10% of SFT data, which masks global video tokens during the answer phase (preventing 'fake thinking' where the model ignores retrieved evidence) and masks local tool outputs during tool-call planning (forcing reliance on the global view for localization); (2) a Verifiable Trajectory-Guided Reward (VTGR) that multiplies the answer reward by a trajectory-dependent bonus combining a Hybrid Clue Score (IoU/IoP/IoG alignment with ground-truth segments) and a Turn Decay Factor (penalizing redundant tool invocations beyond a reference limit). Training proceeds in two SFT stages (418k cold-start trajectories, then 150k tool-use examples) followed by RL with DAPO (a GRPO variant) using over-turn masking to exclude trajectories exceeding the turn limit from gradient updates. The approach is supported by Seeker-173K, a 173K-sample dataset of synthesized tool-interaction trajectories generated through a four-stage pipeline (clue localization, validity verification, trajectory generation, logical consistency check).

## Results

Video-o3 (SFT+RL, 7B) achieves 72.1% on MLVU, 47.6% on LVBench, 60.5% on LongVideoBench, 66.5% on VideoMME, 51.7% on VideoMMU, 67.2% on MMVU, 46.5% on Video-Holmes, and 44.7% on MMR-V, setting new state-of-the-art across all eight long-video benchmarks. On temporal grounding (Charades-STA), Video-o3 reaches mIoU 60.7, substantially exceeding Qwen2.5-VL (43.6) and LongVT (27.2). In efficiency, Video-o3 achieves the highest MLVU accuracy (72.1%) with only 10.2 s inference time and 18,020 input visual tokens, outperforming decoupled iterative methods by 32.9–46.0% in wall-clock time while using fewer tokens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Video-o3 (SFT+RL) outperforms the strongest single-turn baseline Qwen2.5-VL by +11.8 on Video-Holmes (46.5 vs 34.7), +12.3 on MMR-V (44.7 vs 32.4), and +4.5 on LongVideoBench (60.5 vs 56.0). Among native multi-turn competitors, Video-o3 surpasses VideoZoomer (the closest rival) on every reported benchmark, with the largest gaps on MLVU (+2.2), LVBench (+3.6), and Video-Holmes (+2.7), and also beats Conan by +8.7 on MLVU and +3.8 on LVBench. Against decoupled iterative methods, Video-o3 leads LOVE-R1 by +7.0 on Video-Holmes (46.5 vs 39.5) and +2.5 on LVBench, while also using 43.8% fewer input visual tokens (18,020 vs 32,108) and 32.9% less inference time (10.2 s vs 15.2 s). On temporal grounding, Video-o3 achieves mIoU 60.7, exceeding Qwen2.5-VL (43.6) by 17.1 points and LongVT (27.2) by 33.5 points, confirming superior clue-localization precision. The RL-only variant already matches or exceeds all baselines on most benchmarks, demonstrating that the trajectory-guided reward is the primary driver of gains rather than SFT alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

Video-o3 extends the native multi-turn tool invocation paradigm for video understanding pioneered by Conan, VideoZoomer, and LongVT, but departs from them in two key respects: it explicitly decouples clue-seeking attention from answer-reasoning attention via Task-Decoupled Attention Masking, and it introduces a verifiable trajectory-level reward (VTGR) that prior native multi-turn methods lacked. The framework builds directly on Mini-o3's over-turn masking and DAPO/GRPO optimization protocols, and on the 'think with images' lineage from OpenAI o3 and DeepEyes, but adapts these to the video domain where temporal localization and multi-hop evidence aggregation add substantial complexity. Unlike decoupled iterative methods (VideoChat-R1.5, LOVE-R1) that reset context between turns, Video-o3 maintains a unified shared context across all interleaved turns, leveraging KV-cache to avoid redundant re-encoding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that native interleaved tool invocation can outperform both single-turn and decoupled iterative approaches on long-video benchmarks while using fewer visual tokens and less inference time, validating the shared-context paradigm as a practical path toward agentic video understanding. However, the 8-turn limit and static VideoCrop tool definition constrain applicability to extreme scenarios (full-length movies, deep multi-hop chains), and the framework's performance remains dependent on the base model's temporal grounding quality. Open questions include whether dynamic context management (pruning failed exploration paths) and a code-as-action paradigm can extend the approach beyond the current fixed-tool setting, and whether the TDAM and VTGR components transfer to other modalities or longer-horizon agentic tasks.
