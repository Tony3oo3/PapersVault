## Problem

Standard pretraining optimization selects hyperparameters to minimize base-model pretraining loss, implicitly assuming that a stronger base model yields a stronger model after post-training. However, this overlooks the geometry of the loss landscape: models that are sharp (high curvature) at their pretrained checkpoint suffer disproportionate forgetting when parameters are perturbed by fine-tuning or quantization, regardless of how strong they appear as base models. The paper asks whether pretraining optimization choices can produce checkpoints that are more robust to downstream parameter shifts even when they do not improve (or even slightly worsen) base-model pretraining loss.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard pretraining text tokens (DCLM-Baseline for 20M–150M controlled experiments; Dolmino mixture for the 1B mid-training) through OLMo-style transformer architectures, with the only change being the optimizer or learning-rate schedule during pretraining. The output is a pretrained checkpoint whose quality is measured not by its own pretraining loss but by the learning-forgetting Pareto frontier (fine-tuning loss vs. retained pretraining loss across a sweep of fine-tuning learning rates) and the compression-forgetting tradeoff (pretraining loss at 4-bit vs. bf16). At 1B scale, the checkpoint is further evaluated on a 10-benchmark OLMo suite before and after SFT on MetaMath, StackMathQA, Tulu-3, or MusicPile, and after 4-bit bitsandbytes NF4 quantization.

## Method

The paper identifies three pretraining interventions that bias optimization toward flatter minima, grounded in a second-order Taylor expansion showing that post-fine-tuning loss increase is governed by the directional curvature κ(Δ_FT; H) along the fine-tuning perturbation. (1) SAM explicitly solves a min-max robust optimization problem, taking an ascent step in the gradient direction (radius ρ=0.05) before the descent step, thereby reducing Hessian trace. (2) Higher peak learning rates implicitly cap the maximum Hessian eigenvalue via the Edge-of-Stability mechanism (λ_max ≈ 2/η). (3) Shorter WSD annealing periods concentrate the final convergence in a regime where sharpness is most sensitive to the schedule. The key practical recipe applies SAM only during the WSD decay phase (~10% of total steps), recovering most of full-run SAM's benefit at roughly 10% additional compute rather than ~100%. Hessian analysis in Appendix F confirms that SAM and higher peak LRs reduce directional sharpness along the actual fine-tuning direction, and that the quadratic approximation upper-bounds observed forgetting, validating the curvature-based explanation.

## Results

Across 20M–150M parameter models pretrained on 4B–192B tokens, SAM consistently improves the learning-forgetting Pareto frontier over AdamW on all five fine-tuning datasets, with the headline result being 80% less StarCoder forgetting at matched fine-tuning loss (pretraining loss degradation of +0.1 vs. +0.5). At 1B scale, a 50B-token SAM mid-training phase on OLMo-2-1B reduces post-training forgetting by 31% on MetaMath, 22% on StackMathQA, and 35% on Tulu-3, while also cutting 4-bit quantization benchmark degradation by 40% (avg. 40.6 vs. 38.9). In high-token regimes (≥96B), SAM yields roughly 2–3× less quantization-induced pretraining-loss increase than AdamW, and the SAM–AdamW gap widens monotonically with both token budget and model size.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the AdamW baseline (the standard OLMo/LLaMA-3 recipe), SAM dominates on the learning-forgetting frontier across all model sizes and datasets: at 60M/192B tokens it cuts StarCoder forgetting by 80% at matched fine-tuning loss, and the advantage grows with token budget and model scale. At 1B scale, SAM mid-training produces a marginally weaker base checkpoint (42.9 vs. 43.2 avg.) but substantially outperforms the OLMo-2-1B baseline after post-training—31% less forgetting on MetaMath, 35% on Tulu-3, and 40% less 4-bit quantization degradation—demonstrating that the stronger base model is not necessarily the stronger post-trained model. Against EWC (Kirkpatrick et al., 2017) as a fine-tuning-time continual-learning method, SAM's pretraining-time intervention is orthogonal and complementary: SAM+EWC achieves a strictly better learning-forgetting Pareto frontier than AdamW+EWC, confirming that the two approaches address different aspects of forgetting. The paper also shows that simply increasing peak learning rate or shortening WSD annealing (implicit sharpness control) reproduces the same qualitative gains as SAM, though SAM provides the largest and most consistent improvements.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Sharpness-Aware Minimization (Foret et al., 2021) from its original goal of improving test-set generalization to a new objective: reducing sensitivity to downstream parameter modifications (fine-tuning, quantization). It connects this to the Edge-of-Stability phenomenon (Cohen et al., 2021) to show that learning-rate dynamics implicitly control the same curvature, and builds on Springer et al. (2025)'s demonstration that overtrained models become harder to fine-tune. Unlike continual learning methods (EWC, replay, gradient projection) that intervene at fine-tuning time, this work intervenes at pretraining time, making it orthogonal and composable with those approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical recipe—applying SAM only during the WSD annealing phase—adds roughly 10% compute versus ~100% for full-run SAM while capturing most of the benefit, making it immediately deployable in existing pretraining pipelines. The finding that a slightly weaker base model (SAM) yields a substantially more robust post-trained model (31–40% less forgetting at 1B scale) challenges the standard assumption that pretraining should be optimized solely for base-model loss. Open questions include why MusicPile did not benefit at 1B scale, how these results extend to RL, preference optimization, and adapter-based post-training, and whether a direct validation criterion for post-training robustness can replace the sharpness proxy.
