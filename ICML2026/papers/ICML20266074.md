## Problem

Existing Tree-RAG methods like RAPTOR, which use k-means-type clustering to build hierarchical indexes, face three critical limitations when scaling from single-document to cross-document multi-hop retrieval: (1) the 'uniform effect' of k-means distorts skewed data distributions, (2) tree leaf nodes lack explicit cross-document connections needed for multi-hop reasoning, and (3) coarse upper-level abstracts obscure fine-grained factual details. To the authors' knowledge, no prior Tree-RAG framework handles cross-document multi-hop scenarios at a level comparable to Graph-RAG methods.

## Input / Output

The system takes a user query (ranging from a short factual question to a document-level summarization prompt) and a document corpus that is chunked into ~100-token segments. The R&A agent performs iterative retrieval, potentially issuing multiple reorganized sub-queries across up to i_max retrieval rounds (e.g., 2 for HotpotQA/2Wiki, 4 for MuSiQue/MultiHop-RAG), before producing a final text answer or summary. For summarization tasks, iterative retrieval and the sparse index are disabled, and a single top-k retrieval suffices.

## Method

Ψ-RAG comprises two components. First, an abstract tree index is built via an AHC-inspired 'merging and collapse' process: all pairwise chunk similarities are ranked, then the most similar pairs are iteratively linked under new or existing abstract nodes (merging, leaf-node collapse, or abstract-node collapse), producing a non-equi-depth tree that is subsequently rebalanced. This avoids k-means' uniform effect, as formalized via Dasgupta's cost analysis showing Ψ-RAG preserves skewed distributions. Second, a multi-granular agentic retriever employs an R&A agent that reasons over retrieved chunks, decides whether to answer or issue a reorganized sub-query (enriched with descriptive context), and combines top-down tree retrieval with BM25 sparse retrieval fused by a reranker or reciprocal rank fusion. The entire pipeline is zero-shot, using open-source LLMs (Qwen3-Embedding-8B for encoding, Llama-3.3-70B for abstraction and reasoning) with no training or fine-tuning.

## Results

On cross-document multi-hop QA, Ψ-RAG (summative abstract) achieves an average F1 of 62.77% across six datasets (NQ, PopQA, HotpotQA, 2Wiki, MuSiQue, MultiHop-RAG), outperforming RAPTOR+Q3R (36.69%) by 25.9% and HippoRAG 2 (55.41%) by 7.4%. On retrieval, Ψ-RAG achieves 78.98% average Recall@5, surpassing HippoRAG 2 (77.19%) and RAPTOR+Q3R (50.27%). For narrative QA and summarization, Ψ-RAG leads all baselines, surpassing RAPTOR by 9.7% average F1 on NarrativeQA/∞-LongBook and GoR by 3.2% average ROUGE-L on QMSum/WCEP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RAPTOR+Q3R (the only open-source Tree-RAG baseline), Ψ-RAG achieves a dramatic 25.9% average F1 gain, with the largest margin on 2Wiki (76.73% vs. 22.16%, a 54.78% absolute gain) and MuSiQue (48.90% vs. 21.10%), confirming that k-means-based tree indexing degrades severely at corpus scale. Against HippoRAG 2 (the strongest Graph-RAG baseline), Ψ-RAG wins by 7.4% average F1, with notable advantages on 2Wiki (+5.38%) and MultiHop-RAG (+36.87%), though HippoRAG 2 retains a small edge on HotpotQA (75.40% vs. 74.59%). Traditional RAG baselines (BM25, DPR, BM25+DPR+Q3R, DPR+IRCoT+Q3R) are competitive on single-hop datasets like NQ and PopQA but fall 10–25 points behind on multi-hop benchmarks, underscoring the necessity of structured indexing for cross-document reasoning. GraphRAG (Microsoft) underperforms all other structured methods, achieving only 47.61% average F1. On summarization, Ψ-RAG surpasses GoR+Q3R by 3.2% average ROUGE-L (20.28% vs. 13.98% on WCEP; 16.87% vs. 15.98% on QMSum), while on narrative QA it leads RAPTOR+Q3R by 6.5% F1 on NarrativeQA and 14.5% on ∞-LongBook.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Tree-RAG paradigm established by RAPTOR (Sarthi et al., 2024), which organizes documents into hierarchical abstract trees with top-down dense retrieval, by replacing its k-means-based clustering with an agglomerative hierarchical clustering (AHC)-inspired 'merging and collapse' process. It further departs from the single-document, single-hop focus of prior Tree-RAG by introducing an agentic retrieval-and-answering loop and a hybrid sparse-dense retriever to handle cross-document multi-hop queries, a setting previously dominated by Graph-RAG methods like HippoRAG 2. The theoretical analysis draws on Dasgupta's cost (2016) as the implicit objective of AHC, providing a formal contrast with the 'uniform effect' of k-means-type clustering identified by Liang et al. (2012) and Assran et al. (2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Ψ-RAG demonstrates that tree-structured indexes, long considered inferior to graph structures for multi-hop reasoning, can match or exceed state-of-the-art Graph-RAG frameworks while being an order of magnitude faster to index, all with open-source components. The theoretical results showing that AHC-based trees inherently preserve skewed data distributions (unlike k-means) provide a principled explanation for the empirical gains and suggest that the choice of clustering algorithm, not just the index topology, is critical for retrieval quality. Key limitations include the O(n²) similarity matrix computation that restricts applicability to domain-specific corpora (the authors sketch HNSW-based extensions but do not fully evaluate them), and the agentic retrieval loop adds latency proportional to query complexity. Open questions include whether the framework scales to industrial corpora of 100M+ tokens and whether the theoretical guarantees hold under non-uniform distance distributions.
