## Problem

Hamiltonian neural networks (HNNs) enforce energy conservation by construction but are limited to low-dimensional systems (2–5 DoF), while high-dimensional physical systems (robots, fluids, continua) require model order reduction. Existing structure-preserving MOR methods assume known full-order dynamics, and data-driven HNNs cannot scale to intrinsically high-dimensional phase spaces. The paper addresses the gap of jointly learning a structure-preserving reduced-order model and its unknown Hamiltonian dynamics from data, with symplectic and Riemannian constraints enforced as hard architectural constraints rather than soft penalties.

## Input / Output

The method consumes sequences of high-dimensional position-momentum pairs {q_i, p_i} sampled over time intervals, plus measured external constraint forces τ_ext in the dissipative case (e.g., cloth). During training, the loss integrates H forward symplectic steps in the latent space before decoding back to the original high-dimensional coordinates, so the network is trained on multi-step trajectory prediction rather than single-step regression. The output is a predicted high-dimensional trajectory (q̃_p, p̃_p) over the prediction horizon, decoded via the learned cotangent-lifted embedding φ.

## Method

RO-HNN combines three geometrically-constrained components trained jointly. First, a cotangent-lifted symplectic autoencoder learns a low-dimensional symplectic submanifold by constructing the embedding φ and reduction ρ from a constrained AE on the configuration manifold, where biorthogonal weight pairs (Φ_l, Ψ_l) are optimized on the biorthogonal manifold via Riemannian Adam, guaranteeing symplecticity (dφᵀJ₂ₙdφ = J₂d) and projection properties by construction. Second, a geometric HNN models the reduced Hamiltonian as ½p̃ᵀM̃⁻¹(q̃)p̃ + Ṽ(q̃), parametrizing the inverse mass-inertia and damping matrices via SPD networks (Euclidean MLP + exponential map layer) that respect the affine-invariant Riemannian geometry of the SPD manifold, rather than Cholesky decompositions. Third, trajectories are predicted by integrating the learned non-separable Hamiltonian flow with a second-order Strang-symplectic integrator (Tao, 2016) that handles non-separability via an augmented Hamiltonian in extended phase space. The joint training loss combines AE reconstruction, latent HNN prediction, and decoded HNN prediction terms over H integration steps.

## Results

On the 15-DoF coupled pendulum, RO-HNN achieves a relative position prediction error of (1.66±1.38)×10⁻¹ at 0.25 s and (7.08±7.56)×10⁻¹ at 5 s, substantially outperforming the 15-DoF HNN (which diverges at 5 s) and the HNKO (1.32×10⁰ at 5 s). On the 600-DoF cloth, the d=10 RO-HNN with SPD damping achieves a position error of (2.31±0.70)×10⁻², outperforming the RO-LNN baseline (1.05×10⁻¹) by over 4×. On the 90-DoF particle vortex, the geometric RO-HNN (d=6) reaches a position error of (4.00±2.01)×10⁻¹, beating the black-box variant (6.73×10⁻¹) and the HNKO (1.60×10⁰) by 40% and 75% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Hamiltonian Neural Koopman Operator (HNKO, Zhang et al. 2024), RO-HNN wins decisively on all three systems: on the pendulum at 5 s, RO-HNN's position error (0.71) is nearly half that of HNKO (1.32); on the particle vortex, RO-HNN (0.40) beats HNKO (1.60) by 75%; on the cloth, RO-HNN (0.023) outperforms HNKO by an order of magnitude. Against the linear and quadratic symplectic manifold Galerkin (SMG) projections (Peng & Mohseni 2016; Sharma et al. 2023), the geometrically-constrained symplectic AE achieves the lowest reconstruction error (0.088 vs. 0.221 for linear SMG) and the only stable long-term predictions, while both SMG methods have zero symplecticity error by construction but lack expressivity. Against the weakly-symplectic AE (Buchfink et al. 2023), RO-HNN achieves lower reconstruction (0.088 vs. 0.143) and prediction errors, and is the only method besides SMG that satisfies the symplecticity condition exactly (error = 0.0 vs. 1.67×10⁻² for the weakly-symplectic AE). Against the RO-LNN (Friedl et al. 2025) on the 600-DoF cloth, RO-HNN achieves a position error of 0.023 versus 0.105 for RO-LNN, despite RO-HNN learning the dissipation forces rather than receiving them as ground truth. The 15-DoF HNN baseline diverges at 5 s, confirming that direct high-dimensional HNN training is infeasible without reduction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads: Hamiltonian Neural Networks (HNNs), which embed symplectic structure as hard constraints but are limited to 2–5 DoF systems, and structure-preserving model order reduction (MOR), which reduces known high-dimensional Hamiltonian systems but requires the full-order model. The authors extend their own prior work on reduced-order Lagrangian neural networks (RO-LNN, Friedl et al. 2025) to the Hamiltonian setting, replacing the biorthogonal autoencoder with a cotangent-lifted symplectic autoencoder that guarantees symplecticity by construction (unlike soft-constrained AEs of Buchfink et al. 2023) while retaining nonlinear expressivity (unlike linear/quadratic SMG projections of Peng & Mohseni 2016, Sharma et al. 2023). The geometric HNN component further departs from standard HNNs by parametrizing mass-inertia and damping matrices via SPD networks on the Riemannian manifold S_{++}^d rather than Cholesky decompositions, and by integrating trajectories with a Strang-symplectic scheme suited to non-separable Hamiltonians.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RO-HNN closes a significant scalability gap: HNNs have been unable to handle systems beyond ~5 DoF, while MOR methods require known dynamics. By jointly learning a symplectic submanifold and its dynamics from data, the approach makes structure-preserving reduction non-intrusive and applicable to 15–600 DoF systems, with demonstrated long-horizon stability and generalization beyond training data. Key open questions include extending to non-canonical symplectic forms (via local Darboux charts), generalizing to Port-Hamiltonian and contact Hamiltonian systems, and developing model-based control in the learned latent space. A practical limitation is the trade-off between latent dimension and HNN trainability: larger latent spaces capture more dynamics but become harder to optimize, ceding some expressivity to the reduction.
