## Problem

Existing SAE training for instruct models inherits the Block Training paradigm from LLM pre-training, which concatenates unrelated documents into fixed-length blocks and introduces attention contamination—leakage from predecessor contexts that acts as destructive gradient noise in instruct models' low-entropy, precision-critical regime. This mismatch degrades both reconstruction fidelity and feature interpretability, yet has gone unaddressed in large-scale SAE efforts like Gemma Scope and Llama Scope.

## Input / Output

The method consumes residual stream activations from instruct models (Llama-3.1/3.2, Qwen2.5) at selected layers, where each instruction-response pair is processed independently with the model's native chat template (introducing special tokens) rather than being concatenated into fixed 2048-token blocks; a 262,144-entry mixing activation buffer manages storage by shuffling and partially replacing activations each cycle. The SAE produces sparse latent features and reconstructed activations, evaluated via overall and special-token MSE, Delta Loss, KL divergence, and GPT-4o-scored monosemanticity (1–5 scale). Feature steering experiments additionally produce steered model outputs by scaling selected latent dimensions in the residual stream.

## Method

FAST (Finetuning-aligned Sequential Training) eliminates cross-instance attention leakage by processing each instruction-response pair as an independent sequence, enforcing a clean causal attention mask so gradient updates are driven solely by valid within-instance signals. The theoretical contribution formalizes this via GSNR analysis: under an orthogonality assumption, BT's gradient variance decomposes as σ_signal² + σ_poll², provably degrading the signal-to-noise ratio, with a derived efficiency penalty ρ ≈ 1 + σ_poll²/σ_signal² on SGD iteration complexity. Two SAE architectures are trained—Standard ReLU (L1 sparsity) and JumpReLU (learnable threshold approximating L0)—using a producer-consumer mixing buffer for storage efficiency. Ablations confirm the gains stem from respecting instance boundaries rather than longer context windows, and a controlled-prefix experiment directly demonstrates that reintroducing unrelated context degrades GSNR, validating the attention-contamination mechanism.

## Results

FAST achieves a log-scaled MSE_st of 0.6468 on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (Layer 18, 8X, Standard SAE), dramatically outperforming BT(P) (5.1985) and BT(F) (1.5093). Delta Loss remains near zero (−0.51% to 0.37%) for FAST, while baselines deviate up to 51.43% (BT(P)) and 41.19% (BT(F)). On Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct, FAST yields 21.1% high-quality interpretable features (scores 4–5), compared to 7.0% for BT(P) and 10.2% for BT(F). GSNR during early training is nearly doubled by FAST (0.50 vs. 0.26 on Llama-3.1-8B), confirming reduced gradient noise.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares FAST against two Block Training variants: BT(P) (concatenated pretraining data from pile-uncopyrighted) and BT(F) (concatenated finetuning data). FAST wins on every metric across all seven models tested (Llama-3.1-8B, Llama-3.2-3B/1B, Qwen2.5-7B/3B/1.5B/0.5B-Instruct). The most dramatic gap is in special-token reconstruction (MSE_st): on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct Layer 18, FAST achieves 0.6468 vs. 5.1985 for BT(P) and 1.5093 for BT(F) under Standard SAE, and −9.7604 vs. −4.0005 and −8.0743 under JumpReLU. BT(F) consistently outperforms BT(P), confirming that using the correct data distribution matters, but both still suffer from attention contamination due to arbitrary concatenation. For feature interpretability, FAST produces 21.1% high-quality features (scores 4–5) on Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct, roughly tripling BT(P) (7.0%) and doubling BT(F) (10.2%). The controlled-prefix experiment (RandomConcat) further validates that the gains come specifically from eliminating cross-instance attention leakage rather than from longer context windows, as FAST-2048 (truncated to match BT's 2048-token limit) still drastically outperforms BT(F) at the same context length.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the SAE architectures of Bricken et al. (2023) and Rajamanoharan et al. (2024b), but its core contribution is a training-paradigm shift rather than an architectural one. It departs from the Block Training (BT) protocol inherited from LLM pre-training (as used in Gemma Scope and Llama Scope) by identifying, via GSNR analysis (Liu et al., 2020), that BT's document concatenation introduces destructive attention contamination specifically for instruct models. The authors explicitly position FAST as orthogonal to concurrent architectural improvements like RouteSAE and SAIF, targeting the data-alignment and optimization dimension instead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is immediate: anyone training SAEs on instruct models should switch from BT to instance-level sequential processing, and the ablation (FAST-2048 vs. BT at the same context length) confirms the gain is structural, not a context-window artifact. The GSNR framework provides a reusable diagnostic for gradient-noise sources in other sequential training settings. Open questions include whether the orthogonality assumption (Assumption 3.2) holds in practice for very long multi-turn dialogues, whether the effect persists at 70B+ scale, and whether the special-token steering results generalize beyond the small α-range tested.
