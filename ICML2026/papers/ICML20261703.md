## Problem

Low-rank decomposition of LLMs causes approximation errors to propagate and amplify non-linearly through deep transformer layers, progressively distorting internal representations and degrading downstream performance. Existing methods minimize per-layer reconstruction error but fail to address this cumulative drift, leaving a significant gap between theoretical parameter reduction and practical quality preservation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261703/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained LLM (or vision-language model) already decomposed via SVD and a small general-domain calibration set (512–2048 sequences of 256 tokens from WikiText2 or C4). It produces a compensated decomposed model where learned bias vectors are fused into existing linear layer biases, adding zero inference overhead. For the vision-language extension (Qwen2.5-VL), the input is an image plus text prompt and the output is a generated caption evaluated on COCO.

## Method

Decomper introduces a 'compress-then-align' paradigm: after SVD decomposition, it sequentially processes each transformer block and learns a low-dimensional compensation vector c per linear layer by minimizing the L2 divergence between the decomposed block's output and the original block's output on a small calibration set. Crucially, the optimization uses the drifted input from the previous decomposed block, so c corrects both local approximation error and accumulated upstream drift. The theoretical analysis (Proposition 3.1) shows the optimal c equals Rμ (residual times input mean), but the empirical block-level optimization handles non-linear distributional shifts that a static mean correction cannot. Only O(d) parameters are optimized per layer while all pre-trained weights remain frozen, and the final c is absorbed into the existing bias term (b′ = b + c), preserving the computational graph and adding no inference cost.

## Results

At 20% compression on LLaMA-2-7B, Decomper achieves a WikiText2 perplexity of 6.76 and average common-sense accuracy of 56.05%, outperforming the strongest baselines (Basis Sharing: 7.70 PPL / 51.72% acc; FLAP: 6.76 PPL / 53.18% acc). On LLaMA-3-8B at 20%, Decomper reaches 11.17 PPL and 54.59% average accuracy, surpassing Basis Sharing (13.80 PPL / 52.55%) and SVD-LLM (17.23 PPL / 50.63%). Under aggressive 40% compression on OPT-13B, Decomper reduces perplexity from 68.0 (SVD-LLM) to 15.88 and lifts average accuracy from 42.6% to 52.7%, a 77% PPL reduction and 10-point accuracy gain. On MMLU, Decomper boosts Qwen3-8B accuracy from 50.2% (SVD-LLM) to 61.3% at 20% compression.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Decomper consistently outperforms all seven baselines across every model and compression ratio tested. Against the strongest low-rank decomposition baseline, Basis Sharing (Wang et al., ICLR 2025), Decomper achieves 4.33 points higher average accuracy on LLaMA-2-7B (56.05% vs. 51.72%) and 2.04 points on LLaMA-3-8B (54.59% vs. 52.55%), while also delivering lower perplexity (6.76 vs. 7.70 and 11.17 vs. 13.80 respectively). Against SVD-LLM (Wang et al., ICLR 2025), the gap widens under aggressive compression: on OPT-13B at 40%, Decomper achieves 15.88 PPL vs. SVD-LLM's 68.00 and 52.67% vs. 42.60% accuracy. Among structured pruning methods, FLAP (An et al., AAAI 2024) is the closest competitor on LLaMA-2-7B (6.76 PPL, 53.18% acc), but Decomper still leads by 2.87 accuracy points; SliceGPT (Ashkboos et al., ICLR 2024) and LLM-Pruner (Ma et al., NeurIPS 2023) trail substantially, with SliceGPT dropping to 37.05% average accuracy on LLaMA-3-8B. Dobi-SVD (Wang et al., ICLR 2025) is competitive on LLaMA-2-13B (61.84% vs. 61.90% accuracy) but Decomper still achieves lower perplexity (5.47 vs. 5.83). ASVD (Yuan et al., 2023) performs weakest among decomposition methods, often producing NaN perplexity at 40% compression on LLaMA-2-7B, while Decomper maintains 10.43 PPL under the same setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the SVD-LLM data-whitening decomposition pipeline as its base compression method, but departs from the prevailing paradigm of minimizing per-layer weight reconstruction error. Prior low-rank decomposition work (SVD-LLM, Dobi-SVD, Basis Sharing, ASVD) and structured pruning (SliceGPT, FLAP) all target individual matrix or activation fidelity, yet the authors argue this is insufficient because small per-layer residuals are non-linearly amplified through deep transformer stacks. The proposed 'compress-then-align' paradigm introduces a block-level output alignment step (a learned bias vector per linear layer) that is orthogonal to the choice of decomposition technique, positioning Decomper as a universal post-hoc enhancement rather than a competing decomposition algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of representation drift as the dominant failure mode—rather than per-layer reconstruction error—provides a useful diagnostic lens for the broader compression community, and the near-zero inference cost (bias fusion) makes the technique immediately deployable alongside existing quantization and serving stacks. The demonstrated synergy with 4-bit quantization (beating 3-bit at lower memory) is practically significant for resource-constrained deployment. Open questions include whether the 'compress-then-align' principle generalizes to pruning or other compression modalities at the distributional level, and whether the acknowledged degradation on high-precision reasoning tasks (e.g., math) can be addressed without resorting to full fine-tuning.
