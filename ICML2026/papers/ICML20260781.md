## Problem

In two-stage learning to defer, a small subset of experts is often disproportionately favored across most inputs (expert imbalance), causing existing deferral algorithms like TDEF to collapse to the majority expert and ignore specialized, less frequently applicable experts. Traditional imbalance remedies (oversampling, class-weighted losses) are inapplicable because the imbalance is over experts rather than class labels, and the deferral problem already incorporates instance-specific expert costs. The paper asks whether principled, theoretically grounded deferral algorithms can correct expert imbalance while preserving strong generalization guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method takes an input instance (a 32×32 or 64×64 RGB image, or a text query) and a fixed set of pre-trained experts (e.g., ResNet-18 classifiers for images, Qwen 2.5 7B/1.5B/0.5B LLMs for text), along with an offline-estimated cost matrix c_k(x,y) encoding each expert's error and inference cost. The output is a single routing decision: which expert index k ∈ [p] handles the input. The router itself is a lightweight model (ResNet-18 for images, DeBERTa-v3xsmall for text) trained on a small fixed dataset (e.g., 4,000 queries for MMLU), after which deployment requires only one forward pass through the router with zero additional expert evaluation overhead.

## Method

MILD (Margin-based Imbalanced Learning to Defer) reformulates deferral loss minimization as a cost-sensitive multi-class classification problem over the input-expert domain by introducing a new distribution P over (x,k) pairs whose conditional p(k|x) is inversely proportional to expert cost. It then derives imbalanced cost-sensitive margin losses with per-expert margin parameters ρ_k, establishes generalization bounds via class-sensitive Rademacher complexity, and proves the first F-consistency bound for a cost-sensitive surrogate loss. The resulting algorithm minimizes a regularized objective where each expert's margin is scaled by ρ_k ∝ (m_k X_k²)^(1/3), dynamically raising the confidence threshold required to defer to dominant or expensive experts while lowering it for underutilized specialists. This prevents the router from lazily collapsing to the majority expert and yields a √2ε excess-error guarantee on the target deferral loss.

## Results

MILD consistently reduces deferral loss across all benchmarks. On the MMLU LLM routing task (Error+Cost setting), MILD achieves 0.813±0.040 deferral loss versus TDEF's 0.928±0.030, a 12.4% relative improvement, while also outperforming LDAM (0.915), CWCE (0.942), and Standard CE (0.985). On image classification with real experts (CIFAR-100, Setup I, error-only), MILD reduces loss from 0.4368 (TDEF) to 0.4265. Across all four image datasets and three imbalance setups, MILD achieves lower deferral loss than TDEF in every configuration, with the largest gains on SVHN (e.g., 0.0752→0.0579 in Setup I) and Tiny ImageNet (0.5857→0.5656 in Setup III, error-only).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MILD outperforms TDEF (Mao et al., 2023a) on every dataset and setting tested: on MMLU Error+Cost, MILD achieves 0.813 vs. TDEF's 0.928 (12.4% lower loss); on CIFAR-100 Setup I (error-only, real experts), MILD reaches 0.4265 vs. TDEF's 0.4368; and on SVHN Setup I, MILD drops from 0.0752 to 0.0579. The key qualitative difference is that TDEF collapses to extreme routing (99.6% to one expert in MMLU setting a; 100% to the cheapest in setting b), while MILD recovers a distribution close to the optimal oracle. Against LDAM (Cao et al., 2019), MILD achieves 0.813 vs. 0.915 on MMLU Error+Cost, a 11.2% improvement; CWCE (0.942) and Standard CE (0.985) are also clearly outperformed. The paper attributes these gaps to TDEF and standard cost-sensitive methods ignoring the instance-dependent, expert-specific cost structure inherent to deferral, whereas MILD's margin-based formulation with per-expert ρ_k parameters directly addresses expert imbalance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the two-stage learning-to-defer framework of Mao et al. (2023a) (TDEF) by addressing expert imbalance, a practical challenge where a small subset of experts dominates routing decisions. The key conceptual departure is reformulating deferral loss minimization as a cost-sensitive classification problem over the input-expert domain rather than the input-label domain, which is essential because expert imbalance is orthogonal to class-label imbalance. Theoretically, the paper builds on the imbalanced margin framework of Cortes et al. (2025a) and the comp-sum loss family of Mao et al. (2023e), extending both to the instance-dependent cost-sensitive setting with per-expert margin parameters ρ_k. This yields the first F-consistency guarantee for a cost-sensitive surrogate loss, a result that does not follow from prior Bayes-consistency or standard F-consistency analyses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical relevance is significant for LLM routing and multi-model inference systems, where expert imbalance is the norm (e.g., a 7B model outperforming 0.5B/1.5B models on most queries). The paper demonstrates that standard deferral algorithms catastrophically collapse under imbalance (TDEF routes 99.6% to one expert or 100% to the cheapest), while MILD recovers near-optimal routing distributions with a lightweight router. Open questions include scalability to very large expert pools, non-stationary expert performance, and whether the offline cost-matrix estimation step remains tractable in dynamic deployment settings. The model-agnostic formulation and one-time training cost make the approach broadly applicable, but the current evaluation is limited to at most 5 experts and 3 LLM sizes.
