## Problem

Classical MCE-IRL guarantees monotonic dual improvement but requires solving a full RL problem at every iteration, making it intractable for complex continuous-control tasks. Adversarial methods like GAIL avoid this cost by using local discriminator rewards, but this introduces training instability and forfeits monotonic improvement guarantees. The paper asks whether one can retain the monotonic dual-ascent structure of MCE-IRL while replacing the expensive full RL solve with a cheaper local policy update.

## Input / Output

The method consumes expert demonstration trajectories (30 per Mujoco task; ~35 walking and ~9 running trajectories for Unitree G1) or, in observation-only settings, motion-capture observations without access to expert states/actions. A binary classifier is trained on expert vs. agent samples to estimate the log density ratio, which drives the function-space reward update. The output is a global, re-optimizable reward function (stored as a parametric model fitted from a circular buffer of k discriminators) and an imitation policy; the reward can be frozen and re-optimized from scratch with PPO, or transferred to environments with altered dynamics (e.g., flipped mazes, disabled legs, changed gravity).

## Method

The key theoretical result (Theorem 3.2) shows that a trust-region optimal policy for a function-space reward update with step size ε is in fact the MCE-optimal policy for a smaller update with step size ε_tr = ε/(1+η), where η is the Lagrangian multiplier of the reverse-KL trust-region constraint. Algorithmically, TRIRL alternates between: (i) a function-space reward update r̃ = (1−ε)r + εβ·log(ρ_E/ρ_π) using discriminator logits, (ii) a trust-region policy update (via PPO with differentiable trust-region projection layers, TRPL) that only performs a local search around the previous policy, and (iii) a reward correction that rescales the step to ε_tr so the local policy becomes globally MCE-optimal for the corrected reward. A circular buffer of the k most recent discriminators handles the recursive dependency of function-space updates without storing the full history, and the max-η variant takes the maximum state-dependent Lagrangian multiplier to enforce a conservative, strictly satisfied trust region. This yields monotonic improvement of the dual objective at each iteration while requiring only a local policy search rather than a full RL solve.

## Results

TRIRL (max η variant) achieves an aggregate normalized inter-quartile mean (IQM) of 0.7881 across all imitation learning tasks, outperforming the best baseline GAIL (0.3297) by a factor of 2.4×. In the transfer setting (re-optimizing the learnt reward under changed dynamics), TRIRL achieves 0.96 on Point Maze Flipped and 0.89 on Ant Disabled, compared to 0.06 and 0.42 for AIRL and 0.29 and 0.33 for NEAR. Against SFM, TRIRL wins on 4 of 5 Mujoco tasks (e.g., Ant: 1.00 vs 0.36; Humanoid: 0.92 vs 0.70), with SFM narrowly leading on Half Cheetah (1.07 vs 1.05).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TRIRL decisively outperforms all six baselines on the aggregate IQM metric. Against adversarial methods, TRIRL (0.7881) beats GAIL (0.3297) by 2.4×, AIRL (0.1694) by 4.7×, and AMP (0.1167) by 6.8×, confirming that the non-adversarial dual-ascent formulation avoids the instability of discriminator-based local rewards. Against non-adversarial baselines, TRIRL also dominates LSIQ (0.1795) and NEAR (0.0986), with the gap widening in transfer settings where baselines' local rewards fail to generalize (e.g., Point Maze Flipped: TRIRL 0.96 vs AIRL 0.06, NEAR 0.29). Against SFM (Jain et al., 2025), TRIRL wins on Ant (1.00 vs 0.36), Walker (0.73 vs 0.51), Hopper (0.81 vs 0.56), and Humanoid (0.92 vs 0.70), while SFM narrowly leads on Half Cheetah (1.07 vs 1.05). The TR loss variant of TRIRL (0.6293 IQM) also outperforms all baselines, offering a lighter-weight alternative at modest performance cost. Notably, TRIRL's global reward function enables reliable retraining from scratch (e.g., Ant retraining: 0.63 vs AIRL 0.10, NEAR 0.46), whereas baselines show high variance and frequent convergence failures due to their entangled, local reward formulations.

## Relation to Prior Work

TRIRL sits at the intersection of three prior threads: the Maximum Causal Entropy IRL framework (Ziebart et al., 2008; 2010), which provides the dual-ascent formulation but requires solving full RL at each iteration; the function-space reward update operator from Arenz et al. (2016), which makes dual ascent tractable but was previously restricted to linear/Gaussian settings; and differentiable trust-region projection layers (Otto et al., 2021), which enable exact per-state trust-region enforcement in deep RL. The paper's key departure is from adversarial IL methods (GAIL, AIRL, AMP), which replace the global reward with a local discriminator signal and sacrifice monotonic dual improvement for computational efficiency. TRIRL recovers the global reward structure of classical IRL while using only local (trust-region) policy updates, bridging the gap between the two paradigms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that TRIRL offers monotonic improvement guarantees and stable training without the prohibitive cost of full RL solves at each IRL iteration, making principled IRL viable in high-dimensional continuous control. The ability to recover a global, transferable reward function (validated on dynamics-shifted environments) distinguishes it from adversarial methods whose rewards are entangled with the learned policy. Open questions include extending the framework to general f-divergences, understanding the properties of function-space reward updates under function approximation, and addressing the inherent ill-posedness of IRL due to temporal credit assignment. The method's reliance on a discriminator buffer and TRPL adds computational overhead (though modest), and the trust-region bound remains a sensitive hyperparameter in practice.
