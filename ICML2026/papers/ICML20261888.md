## Problem

Diffusion Policy's multi-step denoising process is too slow for real-time robotic control (e.g., 50 steps at 1 ms each yields only 20 Hz, far below the 50–1000 Hz required by arms like the Franka). Existing caching-based acceleration methods rely on static, pre-defined schedules that cannot adapt to the evolving sparsity patterns across rollout iterations, as demonstrated by the paper's leave-one-out analysis showing that the optimal schedule changes significantly with rollout progress.

## Input / Output

The pruner takes the current RGB observation o_t (from the robot's front camera) along with sinusoidally-encoded timestep and block indices, and produces a single binary pruning mask M ∈ {0,1}^{K×3L} in one forward pass before the diffusion process begins. This mask then drives the frozen Diffusion Policy (DP-T) to generate the continuous action chunk, where pruned blocks are substituted with cached activations rather than recomputed. The pruner is a lightweight network (visual encoder + Transformer encoder + 3-layer MLP) that adds negligible overhead to the overall inference pipeline.

## Method

SAG introduces a rollout-adaptive prune-then-reuse pipeline with two key components. First, a real-time diffusion pruner G_ψ is conditioned on the current observation to predict the sparsity pattern a priori, using sinusoidal positional embeddings for (timestep, block) coordinates, a Transformer encoder for hierarchical temporal-structural dependencies, and a 3-layer MLP with STE to output the binary mask in a single forward pass. Second, a one-for-all reusing strategy maintains a shared cache buffer τ per block type, enabling cross-block and cross-timestep activation reuse in a zig-zag manner rather than the conventional block-wise caching. Training freezes the policy and optimizes the pruner with a fidelity loss (matching ground-truth actions on ~5% reference data) plus an end-to-end global sparsity loss that enforces a target pruning rate ρ (default 91%) while allowing non-uniform compute allocation across timesteps and blocks.

## Results

On the RoboMimic PH benchmark (5 tasks), SAG achieves an average success rate of 84.4% with a 3.63× speedup, pruning over 90% of computations. On the MH benchmark (4 tasks), SAG reaches 80.8% average success at 3.77× speedup. On the multi-stage Franka Kitchen task, SAG maintains 100% success rate on Kitp1–Kitp3 and 99% on Kitp4 while delivering a 4.03× speedup. In real-world Franka R3 experiments, SAG sustains 44–46 Hz inference frequency (vs. ~7 Hz for full computation) and improves Assembly Line success from 26% to 58%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAG outperforms all caching-based and trajectory-reuse baselines at comparable or higher speedups. Against EfficientVLA (uniform caching), SAG gains +23% on Can, +25% on Transport, and +12% on Tool while matching or exceeding its speedup. Against BAC (block-wise adaptive caching), SAG improves Transport by +7% and Can by +4% at a higher speedup (3.63× vs. 3.30×). Against L2C (learned layer caching), SAG is dramatically better on Square (+66%) and Tool (+48%) while being nearly 3× faster (3.63× vs. 1.28×). Against Falcon and SDP (trajectory-reuse methods), SAG leads by +29% on Square and +35% on Transport (vs. Falcon) and +33% on Tool (vs. SDP). Consistency Policy (CP) achieves a much higher 10–15× speedup but at severe performance cost (e.g., 0% on Tool, 22% on Square), whereas SAG preserves near-full-computation accuracy at 3.6× speedup. SAG also matches or slightly exceeds the Full Computation baseline on most tasks (e.g., +1% on Square, +7% on Transport) while running 3.6× faster.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAG builds directly on Diffusion Policy's DiT-based denoiser architecture and departs from the dominant line of caching-based acceleration methods (EfficientVLA, BAC, L2C, DeepCache) that rely on static, offline-defined schedules. The key conceptual departure is treating the pruning schedule as a learned, observation-conditioned function rather than a fixed hyperparameter, motivated by the paper's leave-one-out analysis showing that no single schedule generalizes across rollout iterations. The one-for-all zig-zag reusing strategy also challenges the widely adopted block-wise caching paradigm by exploiting cross-block activation similarity, and the global sparsity loss replaces per-block budget constraints with an end-to-end allocation objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Achieving 4× speedup with >90% computation pruned while maintaining comparable task success rates is practically significant for closing the gap between diffusion policy inference latency (~50 ms) and the 50–1000 Hz control frequencies required by real robotic arms. The observation-conditioned pruner offers a general recipe for making any caching schedule adaptive to closed-loop dynamics, potentially transferable to other iterative inference settings. However, the current scope is limited to DiT-based Diffusion Policy on Franka arms and short-horizon manipulation tasks; extending to larger VLA models, longer-horizon tasks, and adaptive compute budgets remains open. The paper also notes that the fixed target pruning rate and reliance on representative reference data for pruner training may not generalize to open-world deployment.
