## Problem

Existing flow-based planners for robotic manipulation either operate in 2D image space (lacking 3D geometry) or rely on modular pipelines that stack video generation, depth estimation, and point-tracking experts, incurring minute-level latencies incompatible with real-time deployment. This paper addresses the need for a lightweight, end-to-end model that directly predicts 3D motion flows from 2D observations and language, enabling low-latency, goal-oriented planning for closed-loop manipulation.

## Input / Output

RoboFlow4D takes a sequence of historical RGB images, an optional set of 2D query points on the gripper, and a textual task instruction as input. It outputs a multi-frame 3D flow tensor of shape (n_kp × K × 3) representing gripper keypoint trajectories in metric, robot-centric coordinates, where K is an adaptive number of keyframes determined by the atomic task horizon rather than a fixed window. This flow plan then conditions a lightweight action policy (Diffusion Policy or DiT Policy) that produces action chunks of H=20 proprioceptive steps, with the planner and executor operating at different frequencies in a slow-fast closed loop.

## Method

RoboFlow4D is an end-to-end diffusion-based world model built on a unified FlowDiT architecture that eliminates modular expert stacking. A 3D Perceiver distills geometric knowledge from a frozen VGGT foundation model via an alignment loss, injecting 3D spatial awareness into features extracted by DINOv2 (local patches) and SigLIP (global tokens) encoders. The FlowDiT core consists of stacked DiT blocks with spatiotemporal cross-attention, trained with v-prediction parameterization, classifier-free guidance, a VGGT alignment loss, and a Charbonnier smoothness loss on second-order temporal differences. Training data is generated by tracking gripper-centric 3D flows via Grounded-SAM2 + SpatialTrackerV2, segmenting episodes into atomic tasks by gripper open/close transitions, and resampling keyframes with a monotonic warping rule that densifies samples near the task goal. At inference, the model operates in a slow-fast dual-system: RoboFlow4D produces a single goal-oriented flow plan in ~0.68 s, while the action policy executes multiple 20-step chunks conditioned on that plan, enabling corrective replanning when execution errors occur.

## Results

On LIBERO, DiT w/ RoboFlow4D achieves 87.7% average success rate (+4.0% over base DiT), competitive with the strongest VLA baseline 4D-VLA (88.6%), while DP w/ RoboFlow4D reaches 85.1% (+6.2% over base DP). On the more challenging ManiSkill3 single-view setting, RoboFlow4D boosts DiT from 12.7% to 23.7% average success (+11.0%) and DP from 12.3% to 22.0% (+9.7%). In real-world experiments, DiT w/ RoboFlow4D attains 43.8% average success rate and 38.3 s completion time, surpassing the state-of-the-art π0 (41.3%, 40.7 s) while using a far lighter model (0.76B params) with <1 s planning latency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On LIBERO, DiT w/ RoboFlow4D (87.7% avg) outperforms all VLA baselines—Octo (75.1%), CogACT (77.3%), OpenVLA (76.5%), TraceVLA (74.8%), and SpatialVLA (78.1%)—by 9.6–13.6 points, trailing only 4D-VLA (88.6%) by 0.9 points. DP w/ RoboFlow4D (85.1%) similarly surpasses every VLA except 4D-VLA. On ManiSkill3, where all baselines struggle under the difficult single-third-view setting, RoboFlow4D lifts DiT from 12.7% to 23.7% and DP from 12.3% to 22.0%, both far exceeding BC (9.0%), ACT (11.0%), and OpenVLA (4.0%). In real-world experiments, DiT w/ RoboFlow4D (43.8% SR, 38.3 s) beats both π0 (41.3%, 40.7 s) and π0-Fast (32.5%, 60.5 s) on average success rate and completion time, while DP w/ RoboFlow4D (40.0%, 36.8 s) also surpasses π0-Fast and matches or exceeds π0 on most individual tasks. The consistent gains across both lightweight policies (DP, DiT) and across simulation and real-world settings demonstrate that the 3D flow prior is a plug-and-play improvement rather than policy-specific.

## Relation to Prior Work

RoboFlow4D extends the flow-guided manipulation paradigm from 2D pixel-space trajectories (Track2Act, Im2Flow2Act) and modular 3D pipelines (Dream2Flow, NovaFlow, 3DFlowAction) to a unified end-to-end architecture. It builds directly on the DiT diffusion transformer as its denoising backbone and distills 3D geometric knowledge from VGGT via a lightweight 3D Perceiver, avoiding the multi-stage video-generation-plus-lifting pipeline that dominates prior 3D flow methods. Unlike PointWorld, which requires RGB-D input and predicts fixed-horizon flows, RoboFlow4D operates on RGB images plus text and introduces goal-oriented adaptive temporal horizons tied to atomic task boundaries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 120× inference speedup (from minutes to <1 s) makes flow-guided planning practically deployable on real robots, a regime where prior modular pipelines were infeasible. The plug-and-play design—where RoboFlow4D conditions any lightweight policy (DP, DiT) without retraining the flow model—lowers the barrier for adopting explicit 3D planning in manipulation stacks. Open questions include generalization to multi-object and highly dynamic scenes, whether the 0.76B scale is sufficient for long-horizon tasks beyond the four real-world tasks tested, and how the goal-oriented horizon adapts when atomic task boundaries are ambiguous or the gripper signal is noisy.
