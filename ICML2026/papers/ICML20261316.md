## Problem

The DAA literature is fragmented, with many methods claiming superiority based on marginal differences, yet the key performance drivers remain underexplored. One-stage odds-ratio methods (ORPO, ASFT) operate under a different training protocol (no explicit SFT phase, no β parameter) than two-stage reference-ratio methods, making fair comparison impossible. The ranking objective—pairwise versus pointwise—has been largely overlooked as a structural axis of comparison despite being a fundamental design choice.

## Input / Output

Each DAA method consumes a text prompt x paired with a binary preference pair (y_w, y_l) of chosen and rejected responses, and produces an updated policy π_θ that generates text responses. Training uses static offline preference data (UltraFeedback, Math_CoT) with no online sampling or listwise supervision. Evaluation spans instruction-following benchmarks (AlpacaEval 2 LC/WR, ArenaHard) judged by GPT-4o or length-controlled win-rates, and math-reasoning benchmarks (GSM8K, MATH500, AMC23, MinervaMath, AIME24/25) with verifiable answer extraction, across model scales from 3B to 14B.

## Method

The paper establishes a unified two-stage training protocol for all DAAs by (i) removing the explicit SFT loss term from one-stage odds-ratio methods (ORPO, ASFT) and replacing it with a separate SFT phase, and (ii) introducing a β tempering parameter that scales the odds-ratio score, placing all methods in a common hyperparameter space. Under this unified setup, the authors show that the ranking objective (pairwise: DPO, IPO, SimPO, ORPO vs. pointwise: APO-Zero, NCA, Cal-DPO, ASFT) is the primary determinant of alignment quality, while the scalar score family (policy-reference ratio r_θ^ref vs. odds ratio r_θ^odds) plays only a secondary role. A theoretical analysis in Appendix G links this effect to how each objective interacts with prompt-specific bias b_θ(x) = E_y[r_θ(x,y)]: pointwise objectives induce a non-zero total score gradient that incentivizes uniform score shifts (bias unlearning), consuming capacity that pairwise objectives (which satisfy G_θ(x)=0) can instead allocate to harder examples. This explains why pairwise methods outperform pointwise ones specifically at intermediate task difficulty, while the gap vanishes at the easy and hard extremes.

## Results

On the primary Llama 3.1 8B UltraFeedback setup, pairwise DAAs (DPO, IPO, SimPO, ORPO) achieve AlpacaEval 2 LC Win Rates of 26.8–28.3% and ArenaHard WR of 19.0–21.5%, while pointwise DAAs (APO-Zero, NCA, Cal-DPO, ASFT) lag at 20.8–23.2% LC and 13.5–17.3% AH. ORPO attains the best overall score (28.25% LC, 28.59% WR, 20.9% AH), with DPO close behind (26.82% LC, 23.69% WR, 19.0% AH). At the smaller Llama 3.2 3B scale, all methods converge to ~10–12% LC with overlapping confidence intervals, and on the easy Reddit TL;DR task every method exceeds 90% GPT-4 Win Rate. On Qwen 2.5 14B math reasoning, the pairwise-over-pointwise gap re-emerges (e.g., ORPO 0.328 vs. ASFT 0.308 mean avg@k), while the scalar score axis (r_ref vs. r_odds) yields no consistent separation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

After unifying all methods under a two-stage SFT+alignment protocol with a shared β parameter, the paper finds that pairwise DAAs (DPO, IPO, SimPO, ORPO) consistently outperform pointwise DAAs (APO-Zero, NCA, Cal-DPO, ASFT) at intermediate task difficulty (Llama 3.1 8B UF, Mistral 7B UF, Qwen 2.5 14B math), with ORPO achieving the best overall LC/WR/AH scores at 8B (28.25/28.59/20.9). DPO and IPO remain competitive with ORPO within ~1–2% on most metrics, while SimPO leads on ArenaHard for Mistral 7B (22.3%). Among pointwise methods, APO-Zero and Cal-DPO are closest to the pairwise cluster but still trail by 3–5% LC at 8B; ASFT is the weakest pointwise method (20.82% LC). The scalar score axis (r_ref for DPO/IPO/SimPO vs. r_odds for ORPO/ASFT) shows no consistent advantage: ORPO (r_odds, pairwise) beats DPO (r_ref, pairwise) at 8B, while ASFT (r_odds, pointwise) loses to APO-Zero (r_ref, pointwise). On the easy TL;DR task and the small 3B model, all methods converge to statistically indistinguishable performance, and on Qwen 2.5 7B math the pairwise/pointwise gap largely vanishes, confirming the effect is scale- and difficulty-dependent. Additional scalar score families (AlphaPO r_α, f-DPO Forward-KL) replicate the same pattern: pairwise variants outperform their pointwise counterparts regardless of the underlying score.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DAA lineage starting from DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) and its variants (IPO, SimPO, NCA, Cal-DPO, APO-Zero), as well as the one-stage odds-ratio methods ORPO and ASFT. Its key departure is unifying these previously incomparable families under a single two-stage protocol by (i) extracting the SFT term from one-stage losses into a separate phase and (ii) introducing a β tempering parameter to odds-ratio objectives, thereby placing all methods in a common hyperparameter space. The paper also extends the comparison to two additional scalar score families (AlphaPO's r_α and f-DPO's Forward-KL score) to test robustness of its main conclusion. Conceptually, it reframes the DAA design space along two orthogonal axes—scalar score type and ranking objective—that prior work had conflated or treated as secondary.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that ranking type (pairwise vs. pointwise), rather than scalar score choice, is the primary determinant of alignment quality challenges the narrative in much of the DAA literature where each new loss function claims superiority over DPO. The practical implication that 5–10% of SFT data captures ≥95% of alignment potential is actionable for practitioners with limited compute. The bias-capacity trade-off explanation (pointwise objectives waste capacity 'unlearning' prompt-specific bias at intermediate difficulty) is a novel mechanistic account, though it remains a hypothesis supported by toy experiments and ICC analysis rather than a formal theorem. Key limitations include the restriction to offline static binary preferences (no online/iterative settings), reliance on GPT-based evaluation for instruction-following, and the open question of whether bias removal is ever beneficial.
