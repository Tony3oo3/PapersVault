## Problem

Certified training methods for IBP-based robustness are conventionally evaluated by reporting a single hyperparameter configuration tuned to one point on the natural-vs-certified accuracy trade-off, making comparisons highly dependent on the chosen operating point and potentially biased. This single-configuration paradigm obscures the true state of the art, hides performance complementarities between methods, and has led to substantial under-tuning in the literature. The paper asks whether a standardised, multi-objective evaluation over the full trade-off space can yield a fairer and more informative assessment.

## Input / Output

Each certified training method (IBP, CROWN-IBP, SABR, MTL-IBP) is applied to standard RGB classification benchmarks — CIFAR-10 (32×32, 10 classes), Tiny ImageNet (64×64, 200 classes), and MNIST (28×28, 10 classes) — under an ℓ∞ perturbation radius ε (e.g., 2/255, 8/255, 1/255, 0.3). Rather than producing a single trained network, the procedure outputs a Pareto front of hyperparameter configurations for the CNN7 architecture, where each point on the front corresponds to a distinct clean-accuracy / certified-accuracy trade-off. The final certified accuracy at each front point is assessed via complete verification (αβ-CROWN with a 1000 s per-instance timeout), while the optimisation loop itself uses cheaper incomplete verifiers as a proxy objective.

## Method

The paper replaces single-objective tuning with multi-objective Bayesian hyperparameter optimisation using a Gaussian-process surrogate and a differentiable EHVI acquisition function (Daulton et al., 2020), run with three pseudo-random seeds and 100 trials each (300 total per benchmark) via BoTorch/Optuna. To keep the loop tractable, certified accuracy is approximated during optimisation by incomplete verification (IBP → CROWN-IBP → CROWN, escalating only when cheaper methods fail), and the search is constrained to a region of interest via minimum clean/certified accuracy thresholds. A broad, method-agnostic search space covers learning rate, optimiser, warm-up and ramp-up epochs, trade-off parameters (κ, α, τ, β), PGD attack settings, ε-scaling, and regularisation weights. After optimisation, single-linkage clustering (d_min = 0.05) prunes near-duplicate configurations before expensive complete verification is applied to one representative per cluster, yielding the final Pareto front. The resulting fronts are then aggregated across methods per benchmark to reassess the state of the art and to compare against previously reported single-configuration results.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (ε=2/255), the multi-objective evaluation yields SABR at 81.96% clean / 64.11% certified accuracy (vs. 79.24/62.84 in the original paper) and CROWN-IBP at 76.78% clean / 59.86% certified (vs. 71.52/53.97), representing gains of >1% and ~6% in clean accuracy respectively. On Tiny ImageNet (ε=1/255), MTL-IBP reaches 39.80% clean / 30.45% certified (vs. 37.56/26.09), and SABR reaches 40.61% clean / 28.86% certified (vs. 28.85/20.46), establishing new state-of-the-art results. On CIFAR-10 (ε=8/255), all four methods (IBP, CROWN-IBP, SABR, MTL-IBP) contribute to the combined Pareto front, with performance differences being relatively modest across the trade-off space. Validation-set tuning experiments confirm that test-set tuning inflates results, with validation-tuned fronts strictly dominated by test-tuned fronts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's multi-objective evaluation Pareto-dominates nearly all previously reported single-configuration results across all six baselines. Against SABR (Müller et al., 2023), the paper achieves >1% gains in both clean and certified accuracy on CIFAR-10 (ε=2/255) and a dramatic ~12% clean accuracy improvement on Tiny ImageNet (40.61% vs. 28.85%). Against MTL-IBP (De Palma et al., 2024b), the paper shows ~2% improvements in both metrics on Tiny ImageNet and comparable certified accuracy at higher clean accuracy on CIFAR-10. Against IBP (Shi et al., 2021) and CROWN-IBP (Zhang et al., 2020), the paper reveals substantial under-tuning in prior work, with CROWN-IBP gaining ~6% clean accuracy on CIFAR-10 (ε=2/255) and IBP gaining ~4.5% clean accuracy. Against CTBench (Mao et al., 2025), the paper's fronts generally dominate or match, with the most notable finding that CTBench's single-objective tuning favoured certified accuracy at the expense of clean accuracy. On MNIST (ε=0.3), all methods converge to very similar trade-offs (~93–94% certified), suggesting a performance barrier has been reached on that benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes multi-objective Bayesian optimisation (specifically the EHVI acquisition function from Daulton et al., 2020) to the certified training domain, where prior evaluation has relied on single-configuration comparisons tuned to one specific trade-off point. It builds directly on the certified training methods it evaluates (IBP, CROWN-IBP, SABR, MTL-IBP) and on the CTBench benchmark (Mao et al., 2025), which was the first to apply systematic grid search to certified training but still optimised for a single objective. The key conceptual departure is treating the natural-certified accuracy trade-off as a multi-objective problem whose full Pareto front should be discovered and compared, rather than selecting one operating point per method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a new evaluation standard for the certified training community and reveals that prior comparisons substantially underestimated older methods (IBP, CROWN-IBP) while overestimating the gap to newer methods (SABR, MTL-IBP), complicating the narrative of steady progress. It also exposes that the common practice of tuning on the test set inflates reported performance, and that no single method dominates across the trade-off space—methods are complementary. Limitations include restriction to ℓ∞ perturbations, the CNN7 architecture as the primary testbed, and the acknowledged computational cost of the protocol, which may limit adoption to well-resourced groups. Open questions include whether the Pareto-front evaluation paradigm generalises to other threat models, architectures, and more complex datasets.
