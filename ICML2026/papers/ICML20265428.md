## Problem

Existing higher-order Langevin Monte Carlo methods that achieve strong order 1.5 convergence (e.g., SRK-LD) require three gradient evaluations per iteration and their non-asymptotic error guarantees are only established under strong log-concavity. This paper asks whether a Runge-Kutta type LMC can be designed with only two gradient evaluations per step while still attaining order 1.5 accuracy, and whether rigorous W2-distance convergence bounds can be proven in the more general non-log-concave (log-Sobolev inequality) setting.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes only the gradient ∇U of the potential (no Hessian or higher-order derivatives), an initial point ξ, and a uniform step size h, producing a sequence of samples approximating the target π ∝ e^{-U(x)}dx. The key practical constraint is that each iteration requires exactly two evaluations of ∇U (one at Y_n and one at the intermediate stage Φ^n), compared to three for the prior SRK-LD scheme. The theoretical output is a uniform-in-time W2-distance error bound of order O(d^{3/2} h^{3/2}) and a mixing time of Õ(d ε^{-2/3}) to reach accuracy ε.

## Method

The paper introduces a family of three-stage explicit stochastic Runge-Kutta methods (RKLMC) for the overdamped Langevin SDE, parameterized by coefficients (α, β, a_{11}, a_{21}, a_{22}, b_1, b_2). Four order conditions are derived by Taylor-expanding the RKLMC scheme and matching terms against the order-1.5 Taylor-expanded LMC (TELMC) reference; setting a_{11} = b_1 = 0 (so the first stage collapses to Y_n) yields a unique two-gradient scheme (RKLMC-2G) with coefficients 1/3, 2/3, 3/4, and 3√2/2. The non-asymptotic error analysis proceeds in two steps: a finite-time strong mean-square error bound of O(h^3) via the fundamental convergence theorem for SDEs, combined with exponential ergodicity of the continuous-time Langevin semigroup under the log-Sobolev inequality, to obtain the uniform-in-time W2 rate O(d^{3/2} h^{3/2}) without requiring log-concavity or contractivity of the error propagation.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 2.6) establishes a uniform-in-time W2-distance error bound of O(d^{3/2} h^{3/2}) for the proposed RKLMC-2G algorithm in a non-log-concave (log-Sobolev inequality) setting, matching the rate previously proved only under strong log-concavity (Li et al., 2019). The corresponding mixing time to reach accuracy ε is O~(d ε^{-2/3}) (Proposition 2.7). Numerically, RKLMC-2G achieves an empirical convergence rate of ~1.5 in mean-square error (vs ~1 for standard LMC) and a dimension dependence of ~d^{1.5}, both consistent with theory, while requiring only 2 gradient evaluations per step versus 3 for the prior SRK-LD method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SRK-LD (Li et al., 2019), RKLMC-2G achieves the identical mixing time O~(d ε^{-2/3}) and the same W2 error rate O(d^{3/2} h^{3/2}), but reduces gradient evaluations per step from 3 to 2 (a one-third saving). Empirically, the mean-square errors of RKLMC-2G and SRK-LD are nearly indistinguishable at the same stepsize, while both substantially outperform standard LMC (order 1 vs order 1.5 convergence). Against the TELMC scheme of Sabanis & Zhang (2019), RKLMC-2G improves the mixing time from O~(d^{4/3} ε^{-2/3}) to O~(d ε^{-2/3}) and eliminates the need for Hessian and ∇(ΔU) evaluations. Against the non-convex TELMC analysis of Neufeld & Zhang (2024), RKLMC-2G replaces the exponential dimension dependence e^{O(d)} ε^{-4/3} with the polynomial d ε^{-2/3}, a dramatic improvement. Overall, RKLMC-2G is the first Hessian-free higher-order LMC method with a convergence guarantee beyond log-concavity, matching the best known rate while being the cheapest in gradient evaluations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the stochastic Runge-Kutta Langevin Monte Carlo (SRK-LD) method of Li et al. (2019), which achieved strong order 1.5 convergence under log-concavity with three gradient evaluations per step, by deriving a unique two-gradient variant (RKLMC-2G) through a constrained solution of the order conditions. It also builds on the Taylor-expanded LMC (TELMC) of Sabanis & Zhang (2019) and Neufeld & Zhang (2024) as the reference scheme for matching Ito Taylor terms, while departing from the log-concavity assumption by establishing convergence under the weaker log-Sobolev inequality, following the non-convex analysis framework of Yang & Wang (2025). The key conceptual departure is showing that the third gradient evaluation in SRK-LD is not necessary to maintain the O(d^{3/2} h^{3/2}) convergence rate, and that this rate persists beyond log-concavity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first non-asymptotic convergence guarantee for a higher-order, Hessian-free LMC algorithm in a non-log-concave (LSI) setting, closing a gap between the strong-order-1.5 theory (previously restricted to log-concave potentials) and the more realistic non-convex targets encountered in Bayesian inference and molecular dynamics. The practical saving of one gradient evaluation per step (2 vs. 3) is modest but meaningful in high-dimensional settings where gradient computation dominates cost. Open questions include extending the framework to underdamped Langevin dynamics and to potentials with superlinear growth, both of which the authors flag as future work. The mixing time bound O~(d ε^{-2/3}) matches the best known for log-concave settings, suggesting the LSI assumption may be near-optimal for this class of methods.
