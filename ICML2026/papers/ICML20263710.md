## Problem

Existing SVD-based LLM compression methods face a fundamental trade-off: activation-aware approaches that minimize reconstruction error (e.g., SVD-LLM, Dobi-SVD) rely on Cholesky decomposition or repeated SVDs, introducing numerical instability and prohibitive computational cost at scale, while simpler methods (e.g., ASVD, FWSVD) are computationally cheap but suboptimal in reconstruction loss. Additionally, dynamic rank allocation strategies lack an efficient way to estimate per-layer compression loss, forcing reliance on heuristics that can underperform even uniform allocation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes LLM weight matrices (W ∈ R^{m×n} for each linear layer) and a small calibration set of N=256 text samples (2048-token sequences for text corpora, variable-length formatted prompts for conversational/reasoning data). It outputs, for each layer, a pair of low-rank factors (A_k ∈ R^{m×k}, B_k ∈ R^{k×n}) replacing the original weight, a per-layer rank k_i determined by the dynamic allocation strategy, and reduced KV cache latents (XA_k ∈ R^{l×k} instead of XW ∈ R^{l×n}). Because the full spectral solution is computed in one pass, any target compression ratio can be realized by simply truncating the cached singular values without re-running the compression pipeline.

## Method

Swift-SVD establishes a closed-form spectral solution (Theorem 3.1) showing that the optimal activation-aware rank-k approximation is W_k* = W V_k V_k^T, where V_k are the top-k right singular vectors of Y = XW, with minimal loss ε_k* = (Σ_{j>k} σ_j²)^{1/2}. This is computed by incrementally aggregating the output covariance C = Y^T Y over calibration samples (Algorithm 1) and performing a single eigenvalue decomposition of the n×n matrix C, bypassing Cholesky factorization and repeated SVDs entirely. For dynamic rank allocation, the method combines local compressibility (measured via effective rank of the singular spectrum) with end-to-end layer importance scores through a weighted scoring function s_i = β_i^α · (log(e + ε_{k̄,i}*))^{1−α}, with a preserved ratio δ=0.5 guaranteeing a minimum rank per layer. A lightweight grid search over 11 values of α (each candidate compressed via the closed-form solution and evaluated on a validation set) selects the optimal allocation, requiring no retraining and enabling parallel evaluation.

## Results

On LLaMA-7B, Swift-SVD* (dynamic compression) achieves the best PPL and average zero-shot accuracy across all three compression ratios: at ratio 0.8, PPL 7.84 (WikiText-2) / 11.15 (C4) with 0.51 avg accuracy; at 0.6, PPL 13.29 / 21.92 with 0.44; and at 0.4, PPL 62.32 / 137.77 with 0.34. End-to-end compression time is reduced by 3.2×–76.9× compared to Dobi-SVD and 1.3×–19.8× compared to SVD-LLM(W), scaling favorably with calibration sample size. On numerical stability, Swift-SVD achieves exactly the theoretical minimum reconstruction loss (0.0000 absolute error) across all tested matrix sizes up to 4096×4096, while SVD-LLM and Dobi-SVD exhibit errors growing from +0.46 to +133.88.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Swift-SVD* outperforms all five baselines on both PPL and average zero-shot accuracy at every compression ratio on LLaMA-7B. Against the closest competitor SVD-LLM(W), Swift-SVD* reduces C4 PPL from 15.84→11.15 (ratio 0.8) and 75.42→21.92 (ratio 0.6), while improving average accuracy from 0.49→0.51 and 0.38→0.44 respectively. Dobi-SVD(w) achieves competitive WikiText-2 PPL at ratio 0.8 (8.54 vs. 7.84) but degrades sharply at lower ratios (C4 PPL 190.62 at 0.4 vs. 137.77 for Swift-SVD*), and its training-based dynamic allocation is 76.9× slower in compression time. FWSVD and ASVD are substantially worse, with FWSVD producing near-random PPL (>1500) at all ratios. On cross-model evaluation (Table 2), Swift-SVD* also dominates SVD-LLM(W) and ASVD on OPT-6.7B, LLaMA2-7B, and Mistral-7B, and on Qwen3-4B (Table B.3) it outperforms Dobi-SVD(w) by 0.16 average accuracy at ratio 0.8. In the numerical stability test (Table 5), Swift-SVD is the only method that matches the theoretical minimum loss exactly, while SVD-LLM and Dobi-SVD accumulate increasing absolute error with matrix dimension.

## Relation to Prior Work

Swift-SVD sits at the intersection of two threads in LLM compression: activation-aware low-rank approximation (DRONE, ASVD, SVD-LLM, Dobi-SVD) and dynamic per-layer rank allocation (SVD-LLM v2, Dobi-SVD). It departs from SVD-LLM's Cholesky-based pipeline and Dobi-SVD's incremental-PCA-plus-gradient approach by reformulating the optimal projection as a single eigenvalue decomposition of the output-activation covariance Y^T Y, which can be aggregated incrementally in O(n²) memory. The dynamic allocation strategy is novel in jointly weighting local spectral compressibility (effective rank) against end-to-end layer importance, a combination not explored in prior SVD-based compression work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: a training-free method that is 3–70× faster than prior theoretically-optimal baselines, scales to 32B models where competitors OOM, and achieves the best compression accuracy across six architectures. The discovered negative correlation between layer importance and local compressibility challenges the intuitive assumption that critical layers are inherently harder to compress, and motivates a principled (rather than heuristic) rank-allocation design. Open questions include whether the closed-form initialization can be further exploited via LoRA fine-tuning (explicitly deferred), how well the method generalizes across calibration domains (Table 3 shows significant cross-domain PPL degradation), and whether the dynamic allocation gains persist at even more aggressive compression ratios.
