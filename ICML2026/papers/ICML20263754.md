## Problem

Existing copyright protection for diffusion models is either passive (watermarking/fingerprinting for post-hoc attribution) or only degrades output quality under unauthorized use (e.g., PCDiff), leaving a gap where thieves can still obtain usable images. The paper asks whether a proactive, model-level mechanism can completely prevent unauthorized generation at the diffusion process level rather than merely degrading or tracing outputs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263754/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on 256×256 RGB images for I2I tasks (super-resolution from 64×64 inputs, inpainting with 20–30% freeform masks, deblurring from Gaussian-blurred inputs). The authorized input is a blended image γ·x₁ + (1−γ)·k(x₁) with γ=0.9, where k(x₁) is a sample-specific signature produced by a separate Learnable Signature Network (UNet++ with pretrained ResNeXt encoder) maintained by the model owner. Authorized queries yield high-quality target images; unauthorized queries (clean inputs without valid signatures) yield a predefined warning image with text, completely different from any target.

## Method

GoodDiffusion repurposes backdoor mechanisms for copyright protection by training a diffusion bridge model (DDBM, I2SB, or DBIM) to integrate two distinct diffusion bridges in a single network: an authorized bridge mapping signed inputs to high-quality targets and an unauthorized bridge mapping clean inputs to a fixed warning image, trained via a mixture denoising score-matching objective with π_k=0.5. The paper proves (Theorem 4.1) that a naive static signature is fundamentally vulnerable: a white-box adversary can recover a surrogate via gradient-based optimization that perfectly matches the score function. To address this, a Learnable Signature Network (LSN) generates input-dependent signatures, breaking the universality of any single perturbation so that no transferable surrogate exists across inputs. The LSN is jointly optimized with the bridge model, and the sample-specific design ensures that even if an adversary recovers a signature for one input, it cannot be applied to others, achieving white-box security (G2) while preserving generation quality (G1).

## Results

On CelebA, GoodDiffusion reduces the Abuse Rate from 100% (unprotected) to 0% (or at most 0.06%) across all four bridge models and both super-resolution and deblurring tasks, while maintaining competitive generation quality. For example, with DDBM-VP on super-resolution, FID increases modestly from 12.68 to 16.22, PSNR drops only from 32.43 to 32.13 dB, and SSIM actually improves from 89.43 to 90.31. The Error Rate (fraction of authorized requests incorrectly blocked) stays at or below 0.25% in all settings. On ImageNet, GoodDiffusion similarly achieves 0% AR across all tasks and models while preserving PSNR and SSIM within a few points of the unprotected baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares GoodDiffusion against the unprotected versions of three diffusion bridge model families: DDBM (VP and VE variants, Zhou et al. ICLR 2024), I2SB (Liu et al. ICML 2023), and DBIM (Zheng et al. ICLR 2025). GoodDiffusion achieves a decisive win on protection effectiveness: the Abuse Rate drops from 100% to 0% (or ≤0.06%) in every setting, meaning unauthorized inputs are completely blocked from producing high-quality outputs. On generation quality, GoodDiffusion incurs a modest cost: FID increases by 2–15 points (e.g., 12.68→16.22 for DDBM-VP SR; 7.88→13.91 for DBIM SR), PSNR decreases by 0.3–6.4 dB, and SSIM drops by 0–5.7 points, with the largest degradation observed on DDBM-VE and I2SB. Notably, for DDBM-VP super-resolution, SSIM actually improves (89.43→90.31) and PSNR is nearly unchanged (32.43→32.13), indicating near-lossless protection for the strongest baseline. The Error Rate for authorized users remains at or below 0.25% across all configurations, confirming that the protection mechanism rarely misfires against legitimate requests.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the backdoor attack paradigm (Chou et al., 2023; Zhai et al., 2023) from a malicious tool into a protective mechanism, inverting the conventional objective so that triggered inputs produce high-quality outputs while clean inputs yield a warning image. It departs from prior diffusion-model copyright protection work—watermarking/fingerprinting (Zhao et al., 2023; Xie et al., 2025) and PCDiff's degradation-only defense (Gai et al., 2025)—by aiming for complete generation blocking rather than post-hoc attribution or quality reduction. The theoretical vulnerability analysis (Theorem 4.1) draws on trigger inversion techniques (Tao et al., 2022) to show why static signatures fail, motivating the Learnable Signature Network that produces input-dependent perturbations, a design choice informed by the geometric observation that sample-specific signatures deform the image manifold non-linearly rather than translating it.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GoodDiffusion shifts diffusion-model copyright protection from passive, after-the-fact watermarking to proactive, model-level prevention, which is practically important as diffusion models become valuable commercial assets. However, the fine-tuning attack (Table 5) breaks protection within 50 steps, and the threat model relies on the signature service being separately maintained—a separation that may not hold in all deployment scenarios. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger text-to-image models (only preliminary SD V1.4/V1.5 results are provided), how robust the sample-specific signatures are to adaptive attacks with more compute, and whether the protection survives distribution shift at inference time.
