## Problem

Deep learning models are increasingly derived from pretrained bases through fine-tuning, distillation, or structural modification, yet no principled method exists to quantitatively measure similarity between models or detect cloning relationships. Existing code clone detection techniques are inapplicable because models are directed, weighted computation graphs rather than source code, and the open-source model ecosystem (e.g., HuggingFace with 2.3M+ models, <30% with explicit licenses) lacks tools for provenance tracking and intellectual property protection.

## Input / Output

MCDetector takes two deep learning models as input, converted to ONNX format to ensure framework-agnostic interoperability across PyTorch, TensorFlow, Keras, and PaddlePaddle. It produces a two-dimensional similarity vector (S_struct, S_weight) where S_struct ∈ [0,1] captures topology similarity via SimHash Hamming distance and S_weight ∈ [0,1] captures parameter-distribution similarity via SDOCS. These scores are jointly thresholded (with τ_s = 0.7 for structural) to classify the pair as Type-1 (identical structure + weights), Type-2 (identical structure, different weights), or Type-3 (similar structure above threshold, consistent weight distributions).

## Method

MCDetector is a training-free, framework-agnostic pipeline with three stages. First, each model is abstracted as a heterogeneous DAG over ONNX, where nodes are operators annotated with type and tensor shape (symbolic dimensions normalized to a <VAR> token). Second, structural similarity is computed by serializing the DAG into a topological sequence of [operator, shape] tokens, extracting n-gram fragments, and applying SimHash to produce a compact fingerprint; similarity is 1 minus normalized Hamming distance between fingerprints. Third, weight similarity is computed by aligning layers via a sliding window of size 2 over the serialized sequences (matching on both operator type and shape), then applying bidirectional maximum cosine matching (SDOCS) on column-vectorized weight matrices of each aligned pair, aggregated by parameter count. The bidirectional max-cosine mechanism avoids explicit neuron alignment and is robust to channel permutations and sparsification, while the structural and weight components are complementary: structure alone cannot distinguish Type-1 from Type-2, and weight alone cannot identify Type-3 structural modifications.

## Results

On the synthetic ModelCloneBench (208 model clones), MCDetector achieves perfect precision, recall, and F1 (1.0000) for both Type-1 and Type-2 clones, and F1 of 0.9554 (P=1.0000, R=0.9146) for Type-3 clones with a structural similarity threshold τ_s=0.7. On real-world HuggingFace model families (LLaMA3.2, Qwen2.5, Phi-4-mini, Gemma3-1B), the method retrieves a documented related model within the top-3 results for all queries (Hit@3=1.00), with Hit@1=0.58 and Hit@2=0.92. The ablation confirms that structural and weight similarity are complementary: structure alone detects Type-3 but cannot separate Type-1 from Type-2, while weight alone is insufficient for any clone type.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against external clone-detection baselines; instead, it validates the method through component ablations and metric comparisons. In the ablation (Table 2), using only structural similarity achieves F1=0.96 on Type-3 but is entirely unable to classify Type-1 or Type-2 (marked N/A), while using only weight similarity is also N/A for all types, confirming that neither component alone suffices. In Appendix B, the proposed bidirectional maximum cosine matching (SDOCS) is compared against direct cosine similarity and Frobenius-norm-based similarity: under local permutation perturbations, the direct and Frobenius variants show reduced recall on Type-1 clones, whereas MCDetector maintains perfect Type-1 and Type-2 performance while matching the same Type-3 F1 of 0.96. On the real-world retrieval task, the lower Hit@1 (0.58) is attributed to cases involving substantial fine-tuning or model merging where weight similarity to the base model decreases, but Hit@3 reaches 1.00, indicating the method reliably surfaces documented derivation relationships within a small candidate set.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the code clone detection paradigm from software engineering—where clone taxonomies, structural fingerprints, and similarity metrics are well established—into the domain of deep learning models. It combines SimHash-based structural fingerprinting (Manku et al., 2007) applied to ONNX computation graphs with the bidirectional maximum cosine matching metric from DOCS (Min & Wang, 2025) for weight-level comparison, unifying them under a single detection pipeline. The key departure from prior model-similarity work (model merging, SVCCA/CKA representation comparison, gradient fingerprinting) is the explicit clone-type taxonomy and the joint structural-plus-weight scoring that mirrors the Type-1/2/3/4 hierarchy from code clone research.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

With over 2.3 million open-weight models on HuggingFace and fewer than 30% carrying explicit license information, a principled, framework-agnostic method for quantifying model similarity is practically important for IP protection, provenance tracking, and security auditing. The main limitations are that MCDetector is strictly white-box (requiring full parameter access), depends on ONNX exportability, and does not address Type-4 behavioral clones or scale to the full HuggingFace catalog. Open questions include whether the SimHash structural fingerprint remains discriminative for very large or highly regular architectures, how the method behaves on multimodal or diffusion models, and whether the synthetic benchmark's controlled transformations adequately capture the messiness of real-world model derivation.
