## Problem

Despite empirical success, test-time adaptation (TTA) lacks a principled theoretical framework that simultaneously captures its core objective—maintaining instantaneous reliability after distribution shifts—and the intrinsic information constraints imposed by unlabeled data, misaligned proxy supervision, and complex non-stationarity. Existing online learning analyses focus on cumulative regret rather than per-step recovery, and prior TTA theory either assumes label access or restricts to specific algorithm classes, leaving the fundamental question of when TTA is learnable over non-stationary streams unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The framework operates on a pre-trained model receiving a stream of unlabeled batches D_t drawn from time-varying distributions P_t, with access only to a stochastic proxy-gradient oracle (no ground-truth labels or task-loss values). It produces updated model parameters via single-step proxy-loss gradient descent at each time step, along with class predictions on each batch. The theoretical outputs are quantitative: a recovery complexity τ(ε,δ) bounding the post-shift time to sustain excess risk below ε with probability ≥ 1−δ, and a learnability fraction ρ bounding the long-term proportion of time steps where the target accuracy is violated.

## Method

The paper introduces a unified non-stationary stream model combining a Wasserstein-1 quantized piecewise-constant surrogate for distribution shifts (with bounded path variation V_T and resolution Δ_W) and a φ-mixing process with geometric decay for temporal correlation, yielding an effective batch size reduction B_eff = B/C_φ. On this model, it defines (ε,δ)-Recovery Complexity and derives a minimax lower bound via Le Cam's two-point testing method, showing τ ≥ Ω(C_φ/(B·α(√(ζ+2αε)+√ζ)²)), which reveals an intrinsic adaptivity-information trade-off governed by proxy-task alignment α, misalignment bias ζ, batch size B, and temporal correlation C_φ. A matching upper bound for a simple SGD-on-proxy-loss baseline is established under Polyak-Łojasiewicz and local alignment assumptions, closing the gap up to a logarithmic factor in Δ_W/ε. The local recovery guarantee is then lifted to a global (ε,ρ)-TTA Learnability via a bridging constant Λ = L_x + GL_{∇ψ}/μ that accounts for quantization error, and finally connected to dynamic regret, showing that persistent shifts force linear regret while vanishing shifts recover sublinear bounds.

## Results

The paper's primary results are theoretical: a minimax lower bound τ ≥ Ω(C_φ / (B · α(√(ζ+2αε)+√ζ)²)) and a matching upper bound τ ≤ O(C_φ / (Bα²ε) · log((Δ_W+ε)/ε)) for the (ε,δ)-recovery complexity, revealing an intrinsic adaptivity–information trade-off governed by proxy alignment α, batch size B, and temporal correlation C_φ. Empirically, on a controlled 1-D synthetic problem, the measured recovery time τ follows the predicted O(1/α²) and O(1/B) scaling and stays above the minimax lower bound, confirming near-tightness. On real-world benchmarks (CIFAR-10-C, CIFAR-100-C, ImageNet-C), Tent improves accuracy on all 15 corruptions of each dataset, and the empirical proxy–task alignment α̃ is positive on every benchmark, validating Assumption 2.1. Under strong temporal correlation, however, the empirical alignment turns negative and Tent collapses, providing evidence that alignment is the core factor governing TTA success.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is primarily a theoretical paper and does not perform a standard head-to-head comparison against multiple TTA baselines. Instead, it uses Tent (Wang et al., 2021) as a representative TTA algorithm to validate the theoretical framework: Tent's empirical proxy–task alignment is confirmed positive across all 15 corruptions of CIFAR-10-C, CIFAR-100-C, and ImageNet-C, and Tent improves accuracy on every corruption, consistent with the alignment assumption (Assumption 2.1) underpinning the recovery bounds. The TTA baseline in Definition 3.8 (single stochastic gradient step on the proxy loss) is analyzed to show that its recovery complexity matches the minimax lower bound up to a logarithmic factor, establishing near-minimax optimality. The paper also contrasts its (ε,ρ)-TTA learnability framework with dynamic regret from online learning, showing that persistent distribution shifts (K̃_S(T) = O(T)) force linear regret Reg(T) = O(T), a fundamental distinction from sublinear dynamic-regret guarantees in standard online learning. No direct numerical comparison against CoTTA, NOTE, ODS, or other TTA methods is provided in the main text; detailed experimental results are deferred to Appendix G.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant regret-based paradigm in non-stationary online learning by introducing TTA-specific metrics—(ε,δ)-Recovery Complexity and (ε,ρ)-TTA Learnability—that capture instantaneous post-shift reliability rather than cumulative average performance. It builds on Le Cam's two-point testing method for information-theoretic lower bounds and Bradley's φ-mixing conditions for temporal dependence, combining them with a Wasserstein-quantized surrogate to unify gradual and abrupt distribution shifts in a single tractable model. Unlike prior TTA theory (AdaNPC, ATTA), which required memory banks or active label acquisition, this framework operates under the standard unlabeled proxy-gradient oracle, making it directly applicable to practical TTA algorithms like Tent.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first principled framework establishing when TTA is provably learnable under unlabeled non-stationary streams, revealing that proxy-task misalignment (ζ) induces a non-vanishing error floor that no amount of recovery time can eliminate—a fundamental constraint practitioners have observed empirically but lacked a formal account for. The matching minimax bounds show the TTA baseline is near-optimal, leaving little room for algorithmic improvement without stronger feedback or structural assumptions. Limitations include reliance on local smoothness/PL conditions, a single proxy-gradient step per batch, and empirical validation limited to three corruption benchmarks; extending the framework to multi-step adaptation, representation-level shifts, or LLM-based TTA remains open.
