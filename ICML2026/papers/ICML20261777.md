## Problem

CoT-reasoning VLA models have been adopted to improve generalization and interpretability in robotic manipulation, but their security remains largely unexplored. Existing adversarial attacks on VLAs are predominantly untargeted and operate on vanilla architectures, leaving open whether the CoT reasoning pathway itself can be exploited for targeted behavior hijacking—e.g., causing a robot to deliver a knife instead of an apple—without modifying the user's instruction.

## Input / Output

The attack takes as input a clean offline rollout dataset of (observation, CoT, action) tuples collected from the victim VLA, along with a target CoT/action pair from a different task instance. The optimized output is a physical adversarial patch (printed at 20×20 cm or 57×43 cm as a tablecloth) that, when placed in the scene, causes the VLA to produce hijacked CoT tokens (bounding boxes, spatial traces, or subtask text depending on the architecture) and corresponding robot actions directed at the attacker's target object. The patch must remain effective across multiple inference steps and generalize to unseen object layouts.

## Method

TRAP optimizes an adversarial patch δ via PGD with a composite objective that jointly minimizes a CoT hijacking loss (cross-entropy aligning generated CoT tokens to a target sequence), an action loss (CE for discrete-token VLAs, MSE on trajectory waypoints for continuous-regression VLAs), a CNN-based content loss for visual camouflage, and a total-variation loss for smoothness. The key insight, validated through instruction-masking and cross-sample-shuffling experiments, is that CoT competitively governs action generation—sometimes dominating the instruction—so corrupting the reasoning pathway is more effective than directly perturbing action outputs. For physical deployment, the patch is parameterized through a Deep Image Prior CNN (DIP) to suppress high-frequency artifacts, and robustness is ensured via homography transformation, an MLP-based color calibration module trained on a printed color board, and Expectation over Transformation (EoT) augmentation. The attack is evaluated on three reasoning VLAs spanning integrated discrete (MolmoAct), integrated continuous (GraspVLA), and hierarchical (InstructVLA) paradigms, with real-world validation on a Franka Panda arm.

## Results

TRAP achieves an average Attack Success Rate (ASR) of 52.54% and average trajectory hijack Score of 0.3294 across three reasoning VLAs (MolmoAct, InstructVLA, GraspVLA), substantially outperforming the Action Attack baseline (5.48% ASR, −0.1467 Score) and Random Noise (1.56% ASR, −0.3371 Score). On GraspVLA, TRAP reaches 75.84% ASR with a hijack score of 0.4253, while on MolmoAct it achieves 48.06% ASR. In real-world physical deployment on a Franka Panda arm, the printed adversarial patch achieves 86.7% CoT hijacking rate and 33.3% full behavior hijacking in the occlusion-free setting, and 38.0% ASR under object-occluded tablecloth deployment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is Action Attack (TMA, Wang et al. 2025a), a standard end-to-end adversarial patch attack that optimizes only an action-guided loss without targeting the CoT reasoning pathway. TRAP outperforms Action Attack by a large margin on all three models: on MolmoAct, TRAP achieves 48.06% ASR versus 9.68% for Action Attack (≈5× improvement); on InstructVLA, 33.71% versus 6.77% (≈5×); and on GraspVLA, 75.84% versus 0.00% (Action Attack completely fails). The hijack Score metric further confirms this gap: TRAP produces positive scores (0.39, 0.17, 0.43) indicating trajectories aligned with the attacker's target, while Action Attack yields negative or near-zero scores (0.13, −0.27, −0.29), meaning its induced behaviors do not reliably match the intended target. The paper attributes this advantage to TRAP's CoT hijacking loss, which corrupts the intermediate reasoning that strongly governs action generation in reasoning VLAs, whereas Action Attack only perturbs the final action output without addressing the reasoning bottleneck.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of adversarial patch attacks on VLA models (Wang et al. 2025a, Lu et al. 2025b) by shifting the attack target from perception or action heads to the intermediate CoT reasoning pathway, enabling targeted behavior hijacking rather than generic performance degradation. It also draws on the CoT-attack literature for LLMs (H-CoT, Mousetrap, Preemptive Answer Attacks) but repurposes the concept for embodied settings where CoT takes structured forms (bounding boxes, traces, subtasks) rather than free-form text. The physical patch optimization borrows content-loss and DIP formulations from TPatch (Zhu et al. 2023) and EoT (Athalye et al. 2018), adapted with homography and color calibration for sim-to-real deployment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reveals that CoT reasoning—introduced to improve interpretability and safety in VLA models—actually expands the attack surface by making task intent explicit and manipulable, a finding with direct implications for the safety case of reasoning VLAs in physical environments. The real-world demonstration with a printed tablecloth patch redirecting a robot from 'pick up carrot' to 'pick up knife' (33–38% full-task success) makes the threat concrete, though the white-box/surrogate threat model and moderate success rates limit immediate practical risk. Open questions include whether the vulnerability persists in long-horizon tasks, whether cross-architecture transferability can be improved, and whether the proposed lightweight consistency-check defense is sufficient against adaptive adversaries.
