## Problem

Most Generalized Category Discovery methods focus on designing better clustering or contrastive objectives but inherit a critical representation-side bottleneck: standard vision transformer backbones produce high-rank, entangled patch tokens where foreground parts, textures, and background context occupy nearby directions. This unstructured geometry makes it difficult for discovery heads to separate novel categories, causing both over-splitting and over-merging of semantic groups.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260814/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

CPF-GCD consumes RGB images processed by a frozen DINO ViT-B/16 backbone, operating on the N×D patch-token sequence (N=196 patches for 224×224 input, D=768). The module outputs a refined N×D token matrix and an updated CLS token, which are then passed to an unchanged GCD head (prototype-based, clustering-based, or contrastive) that produces class labels for known categories and cluster assignments for novel categories via Hungarian-alignment evaluation.

## Method

CPF is a plug-and-play token organizer inserted between the backbone and GCD head that enforces a low-rank compositional bottleneck (M≪min{N,D}) through three stages. First, an image-conditioned primitive codebook is built by adding a learned offset (from mean/max pooled token statistics) to a shared base codebook, and initial token-to-primitive assignments are computed via column-wise softmax so primitives concentrate on informative regions. Second, primitive states are refined through token-primitive attention (primitives re-query tokens) and primitive-primitive attention (primitives exchange information to remove redundancy). Third, two assignment signals—a stable prior from codebook scores and an adaptive data-driven signal from refinement dynamics—are fused in log-potential space with learnable scalar weights, then decoded back to patch-token space via a residual MLP. The key insight is that novel categories need not occupy entirely new feature axes; they emerge as new compositional activation patterns and spatial arrangements over a shared compact primitive vocabulary.

## Results

On fine-grained benchmarks, CPF-GCD as a plug-and-play module yields average gains of +2.95% (All), +0.83% (Old), and +3.98% (New) across four host baselines (SimGCD, LegoGCD, CMS, SelEx). The largest improvements appear on Stanford Cars (+4.75% All, +6.00% New) and CUB-200 (+3.03% All, +4.05% New), with SelEx+CPF-GCD reaching 79.8% overall accuracy on CUB-200. On coarse-grained datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, ImageNet-100), average gains are +0.95% (All) and +2.00% (New), with a slight trade-off of −0.20% on known classes, indicating reallocation of representational capacity toward novel-cluster formation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CPF-GCD is evaluated as a plug-and-play module on four host baselines (SimGCD, LegoGCD, CMS, SelEx) and compared against eight standalone GCD methods (GCD, PromptCAL, AMEND, μGCD, ProtoGCD, InfoSieve, APL, ConGCD) on fine-grained benchmarks, plus PIM and DCCL on coarse-grained benchmarks. The strongest CPF-enhanced result, SelEx+CPF-GCD (79.8% All on CUB-200), surpasses the best standalone baseline ConGCD (68.1%) by 11.7 points, and also outperforms InfoSieve (69.4%) and μGCD (65.7%). On Stanford Cars, LegoGCD+CPF-GCD gains +8.1% on novel classes (44.9→53.0), the single largest improvement reported, while CMS+CPF-GCD gains +7.4% novel (40.6→48.0). On coarse-grained datasets, CPF-enhanced methods consistently improve novel-class accuracy (e.g., SelEx+CPF: 77.7→80.8 on ImageNet-100, +3.1%) but show small known-class drops (−0.3 to −1.4%), whereas standalone baselines like ConGCD (88.6% All on CIFAR-10) and ProtoGCD (87.7% All) remain competitive on known classes. The paper does not directly compare CPF-enhanced methods against PIM or DCCL on fine-grained tasks, but on coarse-grained benchmarks, SimGCD+CPF (87.5% All on CIFAR-10) and SelEx+CPF (88.1% All) match or exceed PIM (85.4%) and DCCL (84.0%) on overall accuracy while providing substantially better novel-class separation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the backbone-to-head interface in Generalized Category Discovery, a position that prior GCD work (Vaze et al., 2022; Wen et al., 2023; Choi et al., 2024; Rastegar et al., 2024b) has largely left untouched, as those methods focus on improving the discovery head (clustering, contrastive, or prototype objectives) while consuming backbone features as given. Conceptually, CPF repurposes the slot-attention / object-centric decomposition idea (Locatello et al., 2020) and the compositional-recognition intuition (Biederman, 1987; Lake et al., 2015) into a discriminative, plug-and-play token-rewriting module, rather than a generative or segmentation objective. The low-rank bottleneck framing also draws on neural-collapse and efficient-coding observations (Papyan et al., 2020; Yerxa et al., 2023), but operationalizes them as an image-conditioned primitive codebook with spatial assignment fields rather than a training-time regularizer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By shifting part of the GCD design burden from the output head to the representation interface, CPF offers a generic inductive bias that can be stacked on top of any existing GCD pipeline without modifying its loss or architecture, which is practically valuable for the rapidly growing GCD literature. The Von Neumann Entropy and effective-rank analyses provide a mechanistic explanation (spectral purification) for why the gains occur, and the cardinality-estimation improvement suggests the structured representation also helps the model 'know how many' novel classes exist. Open questions include whether the fixed-size primitive codebook scales to truly open-ended category spaces, whether the frozen DINO backbone assumption limits transfer to non-ImageNet domains, and whether dynamic or hierarchical primitive fields could further improve fine-grained discovery.
