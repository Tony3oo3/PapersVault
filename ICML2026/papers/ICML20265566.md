## Problem

Standard on-policy distillation for Vision-Language Models treats the learning signal as a single monolithic objective, but the paper reveals that the language prior and visual grounding components of this loss have nearly orthogonal gradients. This geometric pathology causes standard optimization to passively follow a suboptimal compromise trajectory that fails to prioritize visual grounding—the primary bottleneck for vision-language reasoning. The paper asks whether explicitly steering the gradient toward the visual subspace can break this symmetry and improve perceptual fidelity without degrading linguistic capability.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a text prompt as input, with the student generating a chain-of-thought reasoning trajectory (intermediate steps enclosed in <reason></reason> tags) followed by a final answer in \boxed{} format. During training, each student rollout is evaluated under both multimodal (image + text) and unimodal (text-only) contexts, requiring the teacher to provide full vocabulary log-probabilities at every token position for both settings. The visual target distribution is constructed by combining the student's own text-only prior with the teacher's visual information gain, making the method dependent on the teacher's multimodal/unimodal distributional contrast.

## Method

The paper decomposes the standard monolithic reverse-KL distillation loss via Bayes' Rule into a Language Prior term (matching the teacher's text-only distribution) and a Visual Grounding term (matching a constructed target q_T* that isolates the teacher's visual information gain as the log-ratio of posterior to prior). Gradient analysis across tokens binned by Visual Dependency Score reveals these components become nearly orthogonal (≈92°) at high visual dependency, confirming the standard loss acts as a static bisector. Visual Gradient Steering (VGS) adds a weighted visual grounding term (γ·L_Vis, γ=2.0) to the standard loss, with gradient norm normalization to preserve step magnitude while reorienting the update toward the visual subspace. A Language Preservation regularizer (λ=0.01) selectively penalizes deviation from the teacher's language prior on the top 30% of tokens by VDS, preventing destructive gradient interference in high-conflict regimes. The method also integrates with GRPO reinforcement learning as a visual grounding regularizer in a unified objective.

## Results

On the 2B student (Qwen3-VL-2B-Instruct distilled from Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct), VGS achieves an average Acc@1 of 46.10% across seven vision-language reasoning benchmarks, surpassing the standard monolithic on-policy distillation baseline (43.74%) by +2.37%. The largest gains appear on visually intensive tasks: VisualPuzzles (+3.68%) and LogicVista (+3.35%). For the 4B student, VGS reaches 58.12% average Acc@1 (+1.56% over the 56.64% baseline), narrowing the gap to the 8B teacher (61.37%). In the combined RL+distillation setting (GRPO+VGS), the 2B student further improves to 46.57% average Acc@1, outperforming both pure GRPO (45.68%) and GRPO+Standard distillation (45.22%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is Standard On-Policy Distillation (Agarwal et al., 2024), which applies the monolithic reverse-KL objective directly to the multimodal setting. VGS wins on 6 of 7 benchmarks for the 2B student (losing only marginally on VisualPuzzles for the 4B student, −0.44%) and on all 7 for the 4B student, with average Acc@1 gains of +2.37% (2B) and +1.56% (4B). The gains are most pronounced on high-visual-dependency tasks (VisualPuzzles +3.68%, LogicVista +3.35% for 2B), consistent with the paper's hypothesis that visual grounding is the bottleneck. The second baseline is pure GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) in the RL+distillation ablation (Table 2): GRPO+VGS achieves 46.57% average Acc@1 versus 45.68% for GRPO alone and 45.22% for GRPO+Standard distillation, confirming that the visual steering regularizer provides an orthogonal benefit to the RL reward signal. Notably, VGS also improves Acc@16 (stochastic sampling) over the standard baseline on 6 of 7 benchmarks for the 2B student, indicating the gains reflect genuine policy improvement rather than a single lucky greedy path.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the on-policy distillation framework of Agarwal et al. (2024) from text-only language models to the multimodal VLM setting, where it had been largely unexplored (e.g., Qwen3-VL restricts distillation to text-only data). The key conceptual departure is a Bayesian decomposition of the monolithic KL objective into a Language Prior and a Visual Grounding component, revealing that their gradients are nearly orthogonal at high visual dependency. Building on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) for teacher training, the paper introduces Visual Gradient Steering (VGS), a gradient reorientation rule that explicitly prioritizes the visual subspace, and validates it as a regularizer within the RL+distillation loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The gradient-orthogonality diagnosis provides a reusable analytical lens for understanding why naive multimodal distillation underperforms, and the VGS mechanism is a lightweight (1.375× overhead) drop-in that can be plugged into existing RL post-training pipelines. The Asymmetric Maturity Hypothesis—that visual grounding is the bottleneck while language prior is already robust—is validated by the inverse-steering experiment, but its generality across architectures and task types remains open. Limitations include dependence on teacher calibration (if the teacher is visually blind, VGS degenerates to standard distillation) and the fixed-γ formulation, though the adaptive token-level variant in Appendix E suggests a natural next step.
