## Problem

Existing federated learning methods assume restricted heterogeneity structures—such as a fixed number of clusters or a fixed global-plus-local personalization form—making them brittle under complex, multi-granular non-IID patterns where the appropriate sharing hierarchy is unknown a priori. The paper asks whether an FL system can adaptively discover and exploit multi-level client similarity (global → subgroup → client-specific) without committing to a pre-specified structure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260561/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client holds a private dataset of images (32×32 CIFAR-100, 64×64 Tiny ImageNet) or short text sequences (<64 tokens from FLAN-T5 tasks) and produces a class label or generated text, respectively. The key structural detail is that a client's final prediction is not a single model's output but an additive sum of outputs from one selected model at each accepted level, so the effective predictor depth varies per client (typically 1–5 models). At inference, a client stores up to L models but structure pruning usually reduces this to far fewer.

## Method

FeMAM organizes shared knowledge into a hierarchy of L levels, where each level l maintains K_l models capturing knowledge at a different granularity (coarse-to-fine). Each client's personalized predictor is constructed by additively composing one selected model per level in a residual style, so lower-level models act as corrections on top of higher-level shared structure. Training follows a boosting-like schedule: levels are learned sequentially, with only the current level's parameters updated while all previously learned levels are frozen, keeping per-round communication identical to single-model FL. Client-side structure pruning accepts a new level only if it reduces validation loss, yielding client-specific personalization depth without a fixed hierarchy. The server aggregates level-wise (FedAvg at Level 1, K-means-based clustered FL at intermediate levels, local fine-tuning at the finest level), and a convergence analysis proves the objective is monotone non-increasing across levels with a standard averaged-gradient bound within each level.

## Results

FeMAM achieves the best test accuracy across all six non-IID settings on both Tiny ImageNet and CIFAR-100. On the most challenging Multi-level non-IID partition, FeMAM reaches 65.30% accuracy on Tiny ImageNet and 61.86% on CIFAR-100, outperforming the strongest baselines (pFedMoE at 59.11%/56.70% and PM-MoE at 55.66%/59.44%) by 5–6 points. On the FLAN-T5 foundation-model setting, FeMAM attains the lowest loss (1.040) and highest ROUGE-1 (59.25%), surpassing the best baseline FeSEM (58.65% ROUGE-1) and Ditto (1.087 loss).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FeMAM consistently outperforms all seven baselines across every non-IID setting and both image datasets. Against the strongest clustered FL baselines, FeMAM leads FedCAM (Ma et al., 2023) by 0.43–10.52 points in accuracy (e.g., 61.86% vs 51.53% on CIFAR-100 Multi-level) and FeSEM (Xie et al., 2021) by 10.38–38.12 points (e.g., 60.46% vs 22.34% on Tiny ImageNet Client-wise), confirming that multi-level additive composition captures structure beyond a single cluster level. Against personalized FL baselines, FeMAM surpasses Ditto (Li et al., 2021) by 3.13–11.63 points (e.g., 61.86% vs 51.73% on CIFAR-100 Multi-level) and pFedMoE (Yi et al., 2024) by 5.16–10.58 points (e.g., 65.30% vs 59.11% on Tiny ImageNet Multi-level), showing that explicit multi-granular sharing beats a fixed global-plus-local design. Against multi-model baselines, FeMAM outperforms FedEM (Marfoq et al., 2021) by 14.97–32.12 points and PM-MoE (Feng et al., 2025) by 2.42–18.09 points, with the largest gaps appearing under complex multi-level heterogeneity where fixed-structure methods struggle most. On the FLAN-T5 foundation-model task, FeMAM achieves 59.25% ROUGE-1 and 1.040 loss, edging out FeSEM (58.65% ROUGE-1) and Ditto (1.087 loss) while also beating FedAvg (55.25%), FedCAM (58.00%), and pFedMoE (54.73%), demonstrating that multi-level knowledge sharing remains beneficial even in sequence-to-sequence generation with LoRA-based PEFT.

## Relation to Prior Work

FeMAM generalizes the two-level additive residual composition introduced in Federated Residual Learning (Agarwal et al., 2020) and FedCAM (Ma et al., 2023) to an arbitrary number of hierarchical levels, where each level captures knowledge at a different granularity. It departs from prior clustered FL methods (IFCA, FeSEM, FedCAM) that commit to a fixed number of clusters, and from personalized FL methods (Ditto, pFedMoE) that model only a global-plus-local two-level structure, by introducing a boosting-like grow-and-prune mechanism that adaptively determines hierarchy depth per client. The convergence analysis extends standard federated averaging bounds to the sequential, level-wise training protocol with frozen lower levels, a setting not previously analyzed in the FL literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FeMAM addresses a practical gap: real-world federated deployments often have unknown, multi-granular heterogeneity that cannot be captured by a single fixed clustering or a simple global-local split. The grow-and-prune design makes the framework cost-adaptive (one model communicated per round, client-specific depth), which is important for heterogeneous device fleets. However, the paper acknowledges limited exploration of alternative level-wise FL strategies (e.g., IFCA at intermediate levels) and a relatively small-scale foundation-model evaluation (71 short-sequence tasks), leaving open whether the multi-level advantage persists at larger scale with more diverse tasks and whether level-wise method selection can be automated.
