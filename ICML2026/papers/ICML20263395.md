## Problem

Existing fine-tuning methods for time-series foundation models (TSFMs) lack a principled criterion for identifying which datasets genuinely benefit from adaptation, and generic PEFT approaches like LoRA fail to capture the intrinsic temporal and cross-channel complexity of challenging datasets (e.g., chaotic systems, fetal ECG). This paper introduces data-driven complexity metrics and a complexity-aware PEFT framework that scales its gains with dataset difficulty.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263395/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a multivariate time-series look-back window (96 steps for most backbones, 512 for TTM due to pre-training constraints) and a frozen pre-trained TSFM backbone (MOMENT, UniTS, Chronos, or TTM). It produces multivariate forecasts at horizons 96, 192, and 336. The backbone embedding is processed along two axes simultaneously: a patch-level frequency domain (for temporal filtering) and a channel-level shared/specific projection (for multichannel modeling), before being passed to a forecast head.

## Method

Time-PEFT augments LoRA on a frozen TSFM backbone with two specialized adapters grounded in information theory. The frequency adapter applies FFT along the patch dimension, retains only the top-k dominant frequency components (k=3), applies IFFT, and projects the result to a filtered embedding, reducing spectral entropy and clarifying temporal patterns (motivated by the Kolmogorov-Szegő formula linking peaky spectra to lower irreducible prediction error). The channel adapter concatenates the backbone and filtered embeddings per channel, applies a shared down-projection (encouraging channel-common representations) followed by channel-specific up-projections (preserving individual channel knowledge), motivated by Fano's inequality showing that cross-channel information reduces the conditional entropy bound. Both the filtered and original backbone embeddings are retained to avoid discarding salient patterns through aggressive filtering. The framework is guided by two dataset-level complexity metrics—normalized spectral entropy (temporal) and time-delayed transfer entropy (multichannel)—that predict when and how much fine-tuning will help.

## Results

On seven complex datasets (five chaotic systems and two fetal ECG recordings) with MOMENT_base as the backbone, Time-PEFT achieves the best average MSE across all datasets, reaching 0.385 on Lorenz, 0.428 on Hopfield, and 0.494 on LCoupled. The headline improvement is up to 38% lower MSE than LoRA (e.g., 0.494 vs. 0.797 on LCoupled) and up to 47% lower than ChannelMixing (0.385 vs. 0.734 on Lorenz). Time-PEFT also outperforms full fine-tuning on most datasets while using only 3.97% of the trainable parameters (4.43M vs. 111.55M). Across all five backbone TSFMs (MOMENT_small, MOMENT_base, UniTS, Chronos_small, TTM_r2), Time-PEFT ranks first or second in most dataset-metric pairs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LoRA (the primary PEFT baseline), Time-PEFT achieves 35–38% lower MSE on MOMENT_base across the high-complexity chaotic datasets (Lorenz: 0.385 vs. 0.594; Hopfield: 0.428 vs. 0.654; LCoupled: 0.494 vs. 0.797), with the gap widening as temporal and multichannel complexity increase. Against FourierFT, Time-PEFT similarly dominates on MOMENT_base (e.g., 0.385 vs. 0.606 on Lorenz) and also outperforms it on UniTS and Chronos_small. Against ChannelMixing, Time-PEFT shows the largest relative gains (up to 47% MSE reduction on Lorenz with MOMENT_base), confirming that a dedicated channel-common component outperforms implicit channel mixing. Against full fine-tuning, Time-PEFT matches or exceeds FullFT on most datasets (e.g., 0.385 vs. 0.465 on Lorenz, 0.428 vs. 0.631 on Hopfield with MOMENT_base) while using only ~4% of the trainable parameters. Against end-to-end models PatchTST and iTransformer, Time-PEFT with MOMENT_base achieves lower MSE on all five chaotic systems and both ECG datasets, though the end-to-end models remain competitive on the lower-complexity ECGCA515 dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PEFT lineage for time-series foundation models by building on LoRA as the underlying adaptation mechanism while adding two purpose-built adapters (frequency and channel) that prior TSFM fine-tuning methods (PCD, ChannelMixing, AdaPTS, MSFT, FourierFT) did not jointly address. Conceptually, it departs from the standard 'apply generic PEFT to any dataset' paradigm by introducing data-driven complexity metrics—spectral entropy for temporal difficulty and transfer entropy for cross-channel information flow—as principled criteria for when and how to adapt. The theoretical grounding via the Kolmogorov-Szegő formula (linking spectral entropy to irreducible prediction error) and Fano's inequality (linking transfer entropy to error-bound reduction) provides an information-theoretic justification absent from prior TSFM fine-tuning work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The complexity metrics offer practitioners a practical, dataset-level diagnostic for predicting whether fine-tuning will yield substantial gains, which is valuable given the growing ecosystem of TSFMs with varying zero-shot capabilities. However, the evaluation is confined to seven datasets (five synthetic chaotic systems and two fetal ECG recordings), leaving open whether the complexity-based framing generalizes to other challenging real-world domains such as finance, climate, or industrial sensor data. The theoretical motivation relies on strong idealized assumptions (local stationarity, Gaussianity, quantized forecasting) that the authors acknowledge are not strictly met, and the top-k filtering hyperparameter, while shown to be robust in a small range, may require per-dataset tuning in practice. A key open question is whether the complexity metrics can be computed efficiently at scale for large multivariate datasets and whether the adapter design transfers to non-Transformer TSFM architectures beyond the five tested.
