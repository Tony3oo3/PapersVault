## Problem

Zero-shot Behavioral Foundation Models (BFMs) trained on reward-free offline data must select a task latent at test time, but the standard inference procedure (Eq. 8) is dominated by dataset support bias and can recover suboptimal policies that query out-of-distribution state regions, causing value overestimation. The paper asks whether optimal stationary occupancy correction ratios can be estimated for an entire family of BFM-induced policies without per-task online optimization, enabling fast test-time adaptation while avoiding off-policy pathologies.

## Input / Output

The method consumes frozen pretrained Forward-Backward embeddings (forward F and backward B networks), a static offline dataset D with reward labels for the target task, and a small set of reset states sampled from the environment. It produces a single optimized latent vector z (same dimensionality as the pretrained latent space) that parameterizes the adapted policy π_z, along with an implicit low-rank stationary density ratio evaluated only on in-support dataset samples. No network weights are updated during adaptation; the entire procedure operates in the d-dimensional latent space with a fixed number of gradient steps (typically 100–500).

## Method

The key theoretical contribution is showing that the stationary density ratio w_{π/β}(s,a) equals (1−γ) times the expected successor density under π averaged over initial states (Eq. 9), and that the FB bilinear factorization m^{π_z} ≈ F(s₀,a₀,z)ᵀB(s,a) turns this into a compact low-rank estimator w ≈ (1−γ) W_{π_z}ᵀB(s,a), where W_{π_z} is the Monte Carlo estimate of the expected forward embedding. At test time, ZOL initializes z from the standard FB reward regression (Eq. 11), then performs gradient ascent on a regularized offline return objective J_total = J_ret − λ₁χ² − λ₂L_trust, where J_ret is the density-ratio-weighted centered return over dataset samples, the χ² term penalizes ratio variance, and the trust-region term constrains latent drift. Stability is enforced via softplus activation for positive ratios, batch self-normalization to unit mean, and weight clipping. The entire adaptation is training-free and interaction-free: all pretrained networks remain frozen, and the ratio is evaluated exclusively on d^β support, guaranteeing no extrapolation beyond the dataset.

## Results

On the ExORL DMC-state benchmark (16 tasks across Walker, Cheetah, Quadruped, PointMass), ZOL achieves an average unnormalized return of 627.2 over 100 rollouts, outperforming LoLA (616.9), FB (592.5), ReLA (585.8), TD-JEPA (553.2), and HILP (538.5). On the long-horizon OGBench (8 tasks including AntMaze and manipulation), ZOL reaches an average success rate of 0.47, ahead of FB (0.45), LoLA (0.42), and ReLA (0.39), without any online environment interaction. On the SMPL Humanoid motion-tracking benchmark (45 tasks), ZOL consistently improves over the FB-CPR baseline, with notable gains on tasks like RAISEARMS-H-L (+467%) and ROTATE-X-5-0.8 (+463%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ZOL outperforms all five baselines on average across both ExORL and OGBench. Against FB (Touati & Ollivier, 2021), the closest zero-shot baseline, ZOL gains +34.7 return on ExORL (627.2 vs. 592.5, +10.03%) and +0.02 success rate on OGBench (0.47 vs. 0.45), with the largest per-task improvements on Cheetah Run (+51.8%) and PointMass Bottom Right (+36.0%). Against the online adaptation methods LoLA and ReLA (Sikchi et al., 2025), ZOL achieves higher average returns (627.2 vs. 616.9 and 585.8) while requiring zero environment interaction at test time; however, LoLA and ReLA occasionally surpass ZOL on individual Quadruped tasks (e.g., Jump: 558/566 vs. 510) where online rollouts provide additional signal. Against HILP (Park et al., 2024), ZOL leads by +88.7 average return (627.2 vs. 538.5), with particularly large gaps on Cheetah Walk (+23.0) and PointMass Bottom Right (+85.0). Against TD-JEPA (Bagatella et al., 2025), ZOL wins by +74.0 average return (627.2 vs. 553.2), though TD-JEPA is competitive on Cheetah Run Backward (334 vs. 263) and Quadruped Jump (570 vs. 510). On the SMPL Humanoid benchmark, ZOL improves over the pretrained FB-CPR (Tirinzoni et al.) on the majority of 45 motion-tracking tasks, with dramatic gains on previously near-failing tasks (e.g., RAISEARMS-H-L: 42.6→241.4) but occasional regressions on a small subset (e.g., RAISEARMS-L-M: 257.9→160.3).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper establishes an exact theoretical identity (Eq. 9) connecting Forward-Backward successor measure representations (Touati & Ollivier, 2021) to the stationary density ratios central to the DICE family of off-policy correction algorithms (Nachum et al., 2019; Lee et al., 2021). While DICE methods estimate these ratios via per-task saddle-point optimization and online adaptation methods like ReLA/LoLA (Sikchi et al., 2025) require test-time environment interaction, ZOL shows that the bilinear FB structure implicitly encodes a low-rank, data-only ratio estimator that can be evaluated purely on the offline dataset. This positions the work at the intersection of two previously separate threads—offline distribution correction and zero-shot BFM inference—by demonstrating that the latter's pretrained components already contain the correction weights needed by the former.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing a training-free, interaction-free occupancy correction for BFM latent selection, ZOL offers a principled remedy for the well-documented suboptimality of standard FB inference (Eq. 8) under support bias, without altering the pretraining pipeline. The practical impact is that practitioners using FB-based BFMs can plug in a lightweight test-time optimization step to mitigate OOD extrapolation and overestimation. However, the method inherits the representational limitations of the underlying FB factorization, remains conservative when the ratio estimate is inaccurate, and has not been extended to stochastic or partially observable settings—key open questions for broader applicability.
