## Problem

Sparse autoencoders (SAEs) are widely used to analyze embedding spaces, but their practical value is debated due to failures in steering and inferiority to linear probes on downstream tasks. The paper addresses two questions: how should SAEs be conceptually understood, and how can their strengths be best leveraged? It argues that SAEs are naturally topic models rather than monosemantic steerable mechanisms, providing both a theoretical derivation and a practical framework to exploit this perspective.

## Input / Output

The method takes precomputed document embeddings (GRANITE-R2 for text, ViT-B-16-SigLIP for images) as primary input, plus text captions (generated by InternVL3.5-14B for images) used only for post-hoc word-emission interpretation. The frozen SAE produces sparse non-negative activations per document, which are interpreted as topic weights. A learned word-emission matrix maps each SAE feature to a distribution over a 5,000-word vocabulary, and k-means clustering of feature embeddings merges the thousands of atomic features into any user-chosen number of coarse-grained topics without retraining. The final output is a set of topic word distributions plus per-document topic proportions.

## Method

The core theoretical contribution is a Continuous Topic Model (CTM) for embedding spaces, structurally analogous to LDA but with Gaussian-distributed topic directions and Gamma-distributed contribution strengths replacing discrete word sampling. Under three assumptions (high-activity/small-contribution limit, concentrated topic directions, and independence across topics), the paper derives the standard L1-penalty SAE objective as the exact MAP estimator of the CTM, establishing that SAE features are thematic components whose activations combine to explain an embedding. Practically, SAE-TM operates in three stages: (1) pretrain a BatchTopK SAE once on a large corpus (480M text sections or 360M images) to learn reusable topic atoms; (2) learn a word-emission matrix B by maximizing bag-of-words likelihood with a background unigram prior (π=0.3) and IDF-weighted document loss, giving each feature an interpretable word distribution; (3) merge features into K′ topics by computing top-p denoised word-embedding projections for each feature and running weighted k-means, then aggregating word distributions within clusters. The SAE remains frozen throughout, making the topic count freely adjustable and the approach transferable across datasets and modalities.

## Results

On five text datasets (News-20K, IMDB, Yelp, DailyMail, Twitter), SAE-TM achieves the highest topic coherence across all topic counts: intruder detection accuracy C_I = 54.31 and coherence rating C_R = 77.25 at 50 topics, maintaining C_I = 40.49 and C_R = 71.22 even at 500 topics. On three image datasets (CIFAR-100, Food-101, SUN397), SAE-TM again leads in coherence with C_R = 85.05 at 50 topics. Topic diversity (WMD) is second-best, trailing only TSCTM, but SAE-TM's coherence remains stable as topic count increases, whereas TSCTM's C_R collapses from 69.75 (50 topics) to 17.67 (500 topics).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAE-TM outperforms all eight baselines on both coherence metrics (C_I and C_R) across every topic count on text and image datasets. The closest coherence competitors are AVITM (C_R = 69.05 at 50 topics) and CombinedTM (C_R = 70.24), both trailing SAE-TM by 7+ points in C_R. TSCTM achieves the highest diversity (D = 3.87 at 50 topics) but its coherence degrades catastrophically as topics increase, dropping to C_R = 17.67 at 500 topics, while SAE-TM retains C_R = 71.22. On image datasets, SAE-TM leads in coherence (C_R = 85.05 at 50 topics vs. 80.40 for TSCTM) but its diversity (D = 3.70) is below TSCTM (3.91) and DecTM (3.94), which the authors attribute to image embeddings focusing on foreground objects. Among embedding-based baselines, FASTopic and CombinedTM are the strongest, with FASTopic showing the most stable coherence across topic counts (C_R = 67.27 at 500 topics vs. 51.25 for FASTopic on text), confirming the viability of embedding-space topic modeling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: sparse autoencoders (developed for mechanistic interpretability of LLM activations, Bricken et al. 2023) and probabilistic topic modeling (rooted in LDA, Blei et al. 2003). The key conceptual departure is from the SAE interpretability literature, which frames features as monosemantic, steerable directions; here, the authors derive the L1-penalty SAE objective as a MAP estimator under a continuous topic model (CTM) for embedding spaces, reinterpreting SAE features as thematic atoms whose activations combine to explain a document. On the topic-modeling side, the work extends the line of embedding-space topic models (ETM, Dieng et al. 2020; FASTopic, Wu et al. 2024) by replacing the VAE-based generative process with a frozen SAE decoder plus post-hoc word-emission learning and k-means merging, and it distinguishes itself from recent SAE-for-dataset-inspection work (Jiang et al. 2025; Choi et al. 2025) by providing an explicit probabilistic generative model rather than a heuristic pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical derivation gives SAEs a principled role as topic-discovery tools, which is practically important because it enables cross-modal topic modeling (text and images) with a single pretrained SAE and flexible topic granularity without retraining. The consistent coherence advantage over eight strong neural topic-model baselines across text and image datasets, combined with the stable performance at high topic counts, makes SAE-TM a credible default for large-scale thematic analysis. Open questions include whether the derivation extends to hierarchical SAEs (Matryoshka, Matching-Pursuit), whether activation strengths can be better aligned with semantic importance, and how to handle non-thematic information (sentiment, style) that embeddings inevitably encode alongside topics.
