## Problem

Existing collaborative ML data valuation methods either ensure collaborative fairness (via semivalues like the Shapley value) but do not incentivize or verify data truthfulness, or they incentivize truthful submission but fail to ensure that rewards depend on others' contributions. This paper asks whether a single mechanism can provably guarantee both collaborative fairness and truthfulness at equilibrium for Bayesian models, and what assumptions and relaxations are needed when the mediator lacks a validation set or has a limited budget.

## Input / Output

The mechanism takes (i) datasets D_i submitted by each of n data sources, (ii) a held-out validation set T* that is unknown to all sources (modeled as a random variable), and (iii) a jointly agreed Bayesian model specification including the prior p(θ), the validation-set likelihood p(T|θ), and each source's data likelihood p(D_i|θ). It outputs a fair reward r_i for each source computed as a semivalue (e.g., Shapley value) of the cooperative game whose characteristic function is the DVF v(D_C) = log p(T=T*|D_C) − log p(T=T*). When no external validation set is available, each source's data is randomly split (e.g., 75/25) into training and validation portions, and source i's reward excludes its own validation-set-based semivalue to preserve truthfulness.

## Method

The core mechanism combines a truthful data valuation function with semivalue-based reward allocation. The DVF v(D_C) is defined as the pointwise mutual information between coalition C's data and the held-out validation set, which is proven to be i-s-truthful (Prop. 3.2) because the expected value difference between submitting the true dataset D̄_i and any alternative D_i equals a non-negative KL divergence. Rewards are then assigned as semivalues φ_i[ν^v] of the cooperative game with characteristic function ν^v(C) = v(D_C), which satisfy four fairness conditions (uselessness, symmetry, monotonicity, strict monotonicity). The key theoretical result (Prop. 4.1) proves that these semivalues remain i-s-truthful under the additional assumption A3 that each source believes others submit data drawn from p(D_j|θ), establishing a truthful Nash equilibrium. The paper further shows that when the mediator lacks a validation set, using cross-source validation sets with the reward ř_i = Σ_{j≠i} φ_i[ν^{T_j}] preserves truthfulness while relaxing symmetry to a modified condition, and that a fixed scaling factor is required to maintain truthfulness under a limited budget constraint.

## Results

Across all four datasets (GP-FR, LO-HE, NN-CY, LO-BL), the truthful strategy (T) consistently yields the highest data value, Shapley value, and reward for source 0 compared to five untruthful strategies (subset, noise, duplication, injection, feature noise), regardless of validation set size. In the GP-FR 3-source example, the paper's Shapley values correctly assign source 1 (unique data) a higher value (0.049) than source 2 (redundant subset of source 0) (0.042), whereas Zheng et al. (2024) unfairly gives source 2 a higher raw value (0.1346 vs. 0.1306). Under minor model misspecifications (e.g., small noise in validation outputs), truthfulness still holds, but under large noise the DVF can fail to be empirically i-s-truthful.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's closest theoretical baseline is Zheng et al. (2024), which uses the same Bayesian DVF (pointwise mutual information with a held-out validation set) but rewards each source with its individual data value v(D_i) rather than a fair semivalue. The paper demonstrates that Zheng et al.'s approach can unfairly value a redundant source (whose data is a subset of another source's) higher than a more unique source: in the GP-FR 3-source experiment, source 2 (redundant) receives v(D₂)=0.1346 vs. source 1 (unique) v(D₁)=0.1306, while the paper's Shapley values correctly reverse this to φ₂=0.042 < φ₁=0.049. The paper also contrasts with Chen et al. (2020), whose peer-prediction mechanism provably assigns equal rewards to two sources regardless of data quality (shown algebraically for 2 sources), whereas the paper's Shapley-based approach gives source 0 a value of 0.098 vs. source 1's 0.066 in the GP-FR 2-source setting. The paper does not claim to outperform accuracy- or similarity-based DVFs (e.g., Data Shapley) on raw model performance, as those lack theoretical truthfulness guarantees; the comparison is specifically on the joint satisfaction of fairness and truthfulness properties.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of work: truthful data valuation via pointwise mutual information (Zheng et al., 2024) and fair reward allocation via semivalues/Shapley values in cooperative games (Dubey et al., 1981; Sim et al., 2020). Prior collaborative ML works (Sim et al., 2020; Xu et al., 2021b) ensured fairness through semivalues but used validation-set-free DVFs (information gain, volume) that provably incentivize untruthful submissions like data duplication. Conversely, truthfulness-focused works (Chen et al., 2020; Zheng et al., 2024) achieved truthful incentives but failed collaborative fairness conditions because rewards did not depend on others' contributions. The key departure is proving that the semivalue of a source—despite incorporating terms that depend on other sources' data—still preserves the truthfulness incentive under the assumption that other sources submit truthfully (A3), thereby bridging the gap between the two literatures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first mechanism with a provable guarantee that both collaborative fairness and truthfulness hold simultaneously at a Nash equilibrium, resolving a fundamental tension in data valuation where fairness (depending on others) and truthfulness (not being gamed) were previously achieved in isolation. The practical implication is that mediators in healthcare or finance collaborations can now design reward schemes that are both fair and resistant to strategic manipulation. Key limitations include the restriction to Bayesian models (non-Bayesian models with loss-minimization training steps are left open), the need for a held-out validation set (with a relaxation in Sec. 5.2 that weakens symmetry), and the reliance on assumption A3 (other sources are truthful) for the full semivalue truthfulness result. Open questions include extending to federated learning settings, non-Bayesian models, and whether the truthful equilibrium is robust to correlated deviations.
