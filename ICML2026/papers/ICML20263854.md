## Problem

Existing transfer learning theory for nonparametric regression is largely restricted to kernel-based estimators, fixed numbers of groups, or parametric formulations, leaving open whether deep neural networks can provably benefit from transfer learning while overcoming the curse of dimensionality. This paper asks: under what conditions does a two-stage pretraining-style procedure with deep ReLU networks yield faster convergence rates than single-group estimation, and can this be established for a general class of nonparametric estimators with a growing number of groups?

## Input / Output

The method consumes n i.i.d. samples (x_i, y_i, z_i) where x_i ∈ [0,1]^p are covariates, y_i ∈ R is a scalar response, and z_i ∈ {1,…,L} is a discrete group label with L allowed to grow with n. It outputs, for each group ℓ, an estimate of the group-specific conditional mean g_{0,ℓ}(x) = E(Y|X=x, Z=ℓ) constructed as the additive sum of a pooled overall-mean estimator and a group-specific offset estimator. In the UTKFace experiment, inputs are 512-dim FaceNet embeddings of facial images (not raw pixels), and the output is a continuous age prediction per ethnic group.

## Method

The core procedure is a two-stage offset learning framework. In Stage 1, all n samples from all L groups are pooled and the overall conditional mean f̄(x) = E(Y|X=x) is estimated by empirical risk minimization over a deep ReLU network class F(M,ν) with depth M and width ν. In Stage 2, for each target group ℓ, the residual offset G_ℓ(x) = f_{0,ℓ}(x) − Σ_k f_{0,k}(x)P(Z=k|X=x) is estimated by ERM over a separate ReLU network class F(M_ℓ,ν_ℓ) using only group-ℓ data, regressing the Stage-1 residuals. The final estimator is the additive combination ĝ_ℓ(x) = f̂_{A_n}(x) + f̂_{ℓ,B_n}(x). The key theoretical contribution is an L₂ error bound decomposing into a first-stage rate φ_n (estimating f̄ from n samples) plus a second-stage rate φ_{ℓ,n} (estimating G_ℓ from nπ_ℓ samples), where under hierarchical composition models both rates depend on intrinsic dimension and smoothness rather than ambient dimension, thereby overcoming the curse of dimensionality. Positive transfer is guaranteed when f̄ or G_ℓ is simpler than the full group-specific mean g_{0,ℓ}, or when pooling provides data augmentation for the shared component.

## Results

In the low-dimensional Scenario 1 (SNR=5, n=50000), the proposed 2-Stage(NN) achieves MSE 0.27×10², substantially outperforming Pooled(NN) (1.93×10²), Separate(NN) (0.49×10²), Top-FT(NN) (0.92×10²), and Pool-w-L(NN) (0.48×10²). In the high-dimensional Scenario 3 (Var(ε)=1, n=50000), 2-Stage(NN) reaches MSE 1.77 versus 5.70 for Separate(NN) and 2.12 for Pooled(NN). On the UTKFace real-data experiment, 2-Stage attains the lowest overall MSE of 59.6, beating Pooled (61.8), Separate (63.1), Top-FT (61.4), and Pool-w-L (60.7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Random Forest (Breiman, 2001), the 2-Stage strategy applied to RF also wins: in Scenario 1 at n=50000, 2-Stage(RF) achieves MSE 2.36×10² versus 6.69×10² for Pooled(RF) and 5.00×10² for Separate(RF), confirming the framework's estimator-agnostic benefit. However, NN-based methods dominate RF across all scenarios by a wide margin (e.g., 0.27 vs 2.36 in Scenario 1). Against ptLasso (Craig et al., 2026), the proposed 2-Stage(NN) is dramatically superior in high-dimensional settings: in Scenario 3 at n=50000, 2-Stage(NN) achieves MSE 1.77 while ptLasso reaches 18.51, and in Scenario 4 the gap is 3.19 vs 19.20. The 2-Stage(NN) also consistently beats the other NN training strategies (Pooled, Separate, Top-FT, Pool-w-L) across all four simulation scenarios and the UTKFace experiment, with the largest gains observed when group sizes are unbalanced or the shared structure is strong.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the two-stage offset transfer learning framework originally developed for kernel ridge regression (Wang & Schneider, 2015; Wang et al., 2016; Du et al., 2017; Lin & Reimherr, 2024) to a general nonparametric setting, and instantiates it with deep ReLU networks under hierarchical composition models (Kohler & Langer, 2021; Padilla et al., 2024a). Unlike prior transfer learning results that assume a fixed number of groups and kernel-based estimators, the framework accommodates a growing number of groups L and arbitrary function classes, yielding the first convergence guarantees for trend filtering in a transfer setting. The additive offset formulation also distinguishes this work from the shared-representation transfer learning line (Tripuraneni et al., 2020; Jiao et al., 2024), which typically assumes a common low-dimensional encoder with task-specific heads.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical justification that the curse-of-dimensionality avoidance property of deep ReLU networks under hierarchical composition is preserved within a transfer learning framework, and identifies concrete conditions (simpler offset functions, data pooling across groups) under which transfer yields strictly faster rates. Open questions include formal detection of negative transfer when groups are substantially different, extending the additive decomposition to other coupling forms, developing analogous theory for last-layer fine-tuning, and generalizing to CNNs and Transformers. The practical relevance is moderate: the two-stage offset procedure is a natural formalization of the widely-used pretrain-then-adapt paradigm, and the theory confirms when and why it helps.
