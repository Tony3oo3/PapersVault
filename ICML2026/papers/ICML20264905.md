## Problem

Standard RoPE enforces a uniform frequency schedule and attention scaling factor across all attention heads, ignoring that heads with different functional roles (e.g., local retrieval vs. global aggregation) require distinct frequency bands and scaling behaviors. This uniformity leads to underutilization of embedding dimensions and degraded long-context performance, as uniform scaling distorts the precise rotational alignment needed by short-range heads while failing to adequately extend long-range heads.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard query and key vectors at token positions i and j, along with the current context length L. It produces head-specific rotated query/key vectors via per-head rotation matrices and scaled attention logits where the scaling is applied only to the query vector (q_i^(h) ← λ^(h)(L)·q_i^(h)), leaving the key/value cache layout and attention kernel unchanged. For grouped-query attention, AdaFreq is learned at the KV-group level while AdaScale remains per-head, and all parameters are stored in fp32 for numerical stability.

## Method

AdaRoPE introduces two learnable, head-specific components as a drop-in replacement for RoPE. AdaFreq replaces the fixed geometric schedule θ_f = b^(−2f/d) with per-head, per-dimension frequencies parameterized as θ_f^(h) = exp(ξ_f^(h)), allowing each head to select its own spectral band. AdaScale multiplies each head's logits by a length-aware inverse temperature λ^(h)(L) = (1/τ^(h))·[ln(1 + max{L, L_ref}/L_ref)]^γ^(h), where τ^(h) sets the global scale and γ^(h) controls how aggressively attention sharpens as context grows. The theoretical motivation comes from Fourier analysis showing that retrieval tasks require frequency mass concentrated in a window-dependent range, and from effective-sample-size analysis showing that retrieval heads need constant effective length while global heads need linearly growing effective length. The method adds only ~10–50K parameters, is fully compatible with FlashAttention and KV caching, and can be applied to pre-trained RoPE models by freezing the backbone and fine-tuning only the frequency and scaling parameters.

## Results

Across four pretrained model scales (LLaMA-430M, LLaMA-1.3B, OLMoE-2.7B, Qwen3-430M) trained on 100B FineWeb-Edu tokens, AdaRoPE achieves the best average NLU accuracy on all backbones: 51.23 (LLaMA-430M), 57.94 (LLaMA-1.3B), 56.66 (OLMoE-2.7B), and 51.36 (Qwen3-430M), improving over standard RoPE by 0.91 points on average. Pretraining loss is also reduced by 0.016 on average versus RoPE. For context extension from 8k to 64k, AdaRoPE achieves 69.09 RULER-64k on LLaMA-8B (vs. 12.80 for YaRN) and 31.07 on SmolLM-1.7B (vs. 25.06 for YaRN), while preserving short-context NLU scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaRoPE consistently outperforms all four pretraining baselines (RoPE, PRoPE, ALiBi, NoPE) on average NLU accuracy across all four model scales, with the largest margin over NoPE (up to +4.08 points on OLMoE-2.7B) and a more modest but consistent +0.5 to +1.2 point gain over RoPE. Against PRoPE (Partial RoPE), AdaRoPE wins on every backbone, with the gap widening at larger scale (e.g., +1.17 on LLaMA-1.3B vs. +0.06 on LLaMA-430M). ALiBi is competitive on smaller models (within 0.12 on Qwen3-430M) but falls further behind at 1.3B and 2.7B scale. For context extension, AdaRoPE substantially outperforms YaRN: on LLaMA-8B extrapolation, AdaRoPE reaches 51.87 RULER-64k while YaRN collapses to 12.80; under long-context continued pretraining (AdaRoPE→Joint), AdaRoPE achieves 69.09 RULER-64k versus 61.60 for YaRN→LoRA, while also better preserving short-context NLU scores (63.55 vs. 63.99, a negligible gap).

## Relation to Prior Work

AdaRoPE builds directly on RoPE (Su et al., 2021) and its context-extension variants (YaRN, Position Interpolation, LongRoPE), but departs from their core assumption of a shared frequency schedule and uniform attention scaling across all heads. It is conceptually motivated by Partial RoPE (Barbero et al., 2025), which demonstrated sparse frequency utilization, and by the attention-dilution literature (Chiang & Cholak, 2022; Li et al., 2025b), which proposed uniform length-dependent logit scaling. The paper's key departure is making both the rotation frequencies and the scaling factor learnable per head, grounded in a Fourier-analytic argument (Theorem 1) linking window width to required frequency mass and an effective-sample-size argument (Theorems 2–3) showing retrieval and global heads impose contradictory scaling requirements.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AdaRoPE offers a practically attractive recipe: extending a pre-trained RoPE model's context window by fine-tuning only ~10–50K parameters (frequencies and scaling exponents) on as few as 100 samples, with negligible runtime overhead and full FlashAttention/KV-cache compatibility. The consistent but modest NLU gains (~0.9 points) and the large 64k RULER gap over YaRN on LLaMA-8B (51.87 vs. 12.80) suggest head-level RoPE optimization is a meaningful, underexploited degree of freedom. Open questions include whether the two-stage warm-start strategy generalizes to much larger models (>70B) or to non-RoPE positional schemes, and whether the learned frequency spectra remain stable under continued pretraining at scale.
