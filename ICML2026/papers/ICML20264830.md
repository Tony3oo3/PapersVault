## Problem

Current generative reward models (GRMs) trained with RLVR rely on binary outcome-only supervision (preference label correctness), which is fundamentally inadequate for pairwise rewarding tasks. Because the solution space is restricted to just two outcomes, GRMs frequently guess the correct label while generating flawed or nitpicky critiques (20–30% of correct predictions in SOTA models), injecting noise into the reward signal and impairing learning. The paper asks how to provide accurate process-level reward signals for GRM training without requiring human critique annotations on every training sample.

## Input / Output

At inference, the GRM receives a query prompt and two candidate responses, and produces a chain-of-thought critique analyzing both responses followed by a binary preference label (A or B). During training, a subset of data additionally provides human-written critiques, which are used to compute an F1-based similarity score against the GRM's generated critique (using gpt-5-mini as the similarity calculator, focusing on 'core arguments' rather than all minor points). The online MetaRM, a regression head on the same base model, takes (query, y_A, y_B, GRM-generated critique) and outputs a continuous scalar reward in [0, 1+λ], enabling process reward prediction for data lacking human critiques.

## Method

RM-NLHF replaces binary outcome-only reward with a composite reward that adds a process term: R = 1 + λ·R_process when the outcome is correct, where R_process is a binary indicator of whether the F1 similarity between core arguments in human and GRM-generated critiques exceeds 0.5. A key design choice is outcome regularization—process reward is withheld when the outcome is wrong, since incorrect outcomes almost invariably indicate flawed critiques. To address the scalability bottleneck of human critique annotation, the paper introduces an online MetaRM: a regression model trained on the limited subset with human critiques that continuously adapts alongside the evolving GRM policy during RL training (unlike an offline variant that suffers distribution shift). The full pipeline uses GRPO for policy optimization, with the MetaRM updated each step on fresh human-critique data before scoring the outcome-only data, achieving performance comparable to full human supervision at a fraction of the annotation cost.

## Results

RM-NLHF-Qwen-7B achieves an overall score of 0.6481 across 7 pairwise reward benchmarks, substantially outperforming RM-R1-Qwen-7B (0.5759) and RRM-Qwen-7B (0.5895) trained with outcome-only reward on the same base model. At 32B scale, RM-NLHF-Qwen-32B reaches 0.7296, surpassing RM-R1-Qwen-32B (0.7037) and RRM-Qwen-32B (0.7009), and even exceeding the 70B scalar reward model INF-ORM-Llama3.1-70B (0.7182). The paper also demonstrates that GRMs scale more effectively with model size (7B→32B gains of ~0.08) compared to scalar RMs which show only marginal gains at 70B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two main specialized GRM baselines trained with outcome-only reward, RM-NLHF consistently wins: at 7B, RM-NLHF-Qwen-7B (0.6481) outperforms RM-R1-Qwen-7B (0.5759) by 7.2 points and RRM-Qwen-7B (0.5895) by 5.9 points; at 32B, RM-NLHF-Qwen-32B (0.7296) beats RM-R1-Qwen-32B (0.7037) by 2.6 points and RRM-Qwen-32B (0.7009) by 2.9 points. Against scalar reward models, RM-NLHF-Qwen-7B (0.6481) trails the best 8B scalar RMs (Skywork at 0.6867, URM at 0.6768), but RM-NLHF-Qwen-32B (0.7296) surpasses even the 70B INF-ORM-Llama3.1-70B (0.7182), demonstrating superior scaling. Compared to the untrained base model DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B (0.5214), RM-NLHF-Qwen-7B improves by 12.7 points. However, frontier LLM-as-a-Judge systems (gpt-5 at 0.7456, gemini-2.5-pro at 0.7232) still outperform RM-NLHF-Qwen-32B, indicating room for further improvement at larger scales.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the generative reward model (GRM) training paradigm, which itself builds on the LLM-as-a-Judge and Bradley-Terry reward modeling literature. While prior GRM work (RM-R1, RRM, J4R) trains models via RLVR using binary preference label correctness as the sole reward signal, this paper departs by arguing that the constrained binary solution space of pairwise tasks makes outcome-only supervision fundamentally noisy, and instead uses F1-based similarity between human and model-generated critiques as a process reward. The MetaRM component draws on meta-reward modeling ideas (Wu et al., 2024) but introduces an online adaptation mechanism to track the evolving policy distribution during RL training, distinguishing it from static reward models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that 20–30% of correct preference predictions in state-of-the-art GRMs are accompanied by flawed critiques challenges the implicit assumption that binary outcome reward is sufficient for pairwise evaluation tasks, and the proposed process reward signal offers a principled fix. The online MetaRM framework addresses the critical scalability bottleneck of human critique annotation, making the approach practical for large-scale training. Key limitations include dependence on external API calls for similarity computation, sensitivity to the quality and cultural generality of human critiques, and the observation that the method may not transfer to fully open-ended tasks without ground truth. Open questions include whether self-verification (eliminating external API dependency) can match the current approach, and whether process supervision is truly necessary or merely beneficial.
