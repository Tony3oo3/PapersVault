## Problem

Existing gaze estimation methods either regress gaze directly from appearance features without geometric constraints or reconstruct eyeball geometry but require dedicated hardware and personal calibration, leaving no unified representation that is both physically grounded and generalizable. Moreover, current models remain sensitive to viewpoint and domain shifts because they lack rotation-equivariant and geometric-structural consistency as inductive biases.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265123/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of near-eye RGB image crops (320×240, normalized) and jointly outputs a 3D unit gaze vector, a parametric 3D eyeball model (eyeball/iris/pupil radii, center translation, and SO(3) orientation), and 2D semantic edge projections of the iris and pupil. Structural supervision is deliberately weak: only 26 sparse iris edge points and 128 pupil contour points are used, avoiding dense 3D annotations or multi-view calibration.

## Method

SG-Gaze learns a Structurally and Geometrically Consistent Representation (SGR) through a dual-branch adversarial architecture. The Analytical Gaze Estimation (AGE) branch projects backbone features onto a 3D spherical manifold via Isomap and Spherical Fitting, enforcing that gaze directions respect the unit-sphere geodesic topology; the Model-Guided Reconstruction (MGR) branch decodes parametric 3D eyeball parameters and renders iris/pupil point clouds under weak 2D edge supervision. A cross-task adversarial discriminator with gradient reversal aligns the two branches into a unified SGR. View-Consistent Regularization (VCR) then applies synthetic SO(3) rotations to features and enforces rotation-equivariant consistency between predicted gaze vectors and structural 2D projections, simulating multi-view conditions without any multi-view data collection.

## Results

Across 12 cross-domain transfer scenarios on TEyeD and LPW, ResNet-50 + SG-Gaze achieves the best 3D gaze error of 1.88° on D_T1→D_T2 and 1.94° on D_T2→D_T1, outperforming the strongest baseline (AGG) by an average of 7.7%. The method delivers up to 38.61% error reduction over the ResNet-18 baseline in the most challenging transfer (D_T1→D_L: 6.83° → 3.75°). Within-dataset evaluation on TEyeD-D_T1 shows SG-Gaze (ResNet-18, 11.68M params) reaches 0.94° 3D gaze error with 92.1% semantic IoU, while maintaining low computational cost (11.24G FLOPs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SG-Gaze (ResNet-50) outperforms all six SOTA cross-domain baselines across nearly all 12 transfer tasks. Against the strongest competitor AGG (Bao & Lu, 2024), SG-Gaze reduces error from 2.21° to 1.88° on D_T1→D_T2 and from 2.15° to 1.94° on D_T2→D_T1, a consistent ~7.7% average improvement. De²Gaze (Xiao et al., 2025), the authors' prior work, is surpassed by a large margin (e.g., 2.88° → 1.88° on D_T1→D_T2; 5.88° → 4.21° on D_T2→D_L), confirming that the dual-branch SGR and VCR add substantial value beyond the deformable representation alone. PureGaze (Cheng et al., 2022) and RAT (Bao et al., 2022) trail by 1.0–1.5° on most transfers, while FFGaze (Zhang et al., 2017) and LatentGaze (Lee et al., 2022) lag further behind (3.1–3.5° on D_T1→D_T2). On within-dataset TEyeD evaluation (Table 3), SG-Gaze (ResNet-18, 0.94°) beats Popovic et al. (2023) at 1.04° (ResNet-50), QueryDETR at 3.08–3.12°, NVGaze at 3.65°, and the TEyeD baseline at 1.88°, while using fewer parameters (11.68M vs. 26–30M).

## Relation to Prior Work

SG-Gaze unifies two previously isolated lines of gaze estimation—appearance-based regression (FFGaze, PureGaze) and model-based 3D reconstruction (De²Gaze, Popovic et al.)—into a single dual-branch architecture coupled by adversarial alignment. The Analytical Gaze Estimation branch directly adapts the Isomap + Spherical Fitting pipeline from AGG (Bao & Lu, 2024), but repurposes it as an intermediate geometric prior rather than a final regression layer. The Model-Guided Reconstruction branch inherits the parametric eyeball and point-cloud deformation machinery from De²Gaze (Xiao et al., 2025), while the View-Consistent Regularization module extends the rotation-consistency idea from RAT (Bao et al., 2022) by applying it to both gaze vectors and structural projections without requiring multi-view inputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'physics-as-representation' framing positions structural and geometric inductive biases as a general principle for interpretable, domain-robust perception, potentially transferable to pose estimation, robotics, or other physically constrained tasks. However, the simplified spherical eyeball model and pinhole camera assumption limit anatomical fidelity (e.g., kappa angle is ignored), and the paper explicitly trades within-domain accuracy for cross-domain robustness. Open questions include whether the VCR equivariance constraint scales to more complex 3D structures, whether the adversarial alignment between branches remains stable at larger model scales, and whether the paradigm generalizes beyond the relatively constrained geometry of the human eyeball.
