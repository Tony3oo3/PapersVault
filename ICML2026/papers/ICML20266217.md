## Problem

DPO's explicit link to the Bradley-Terry-Luce (BTL) model of human choice has become a straitjacket for preference optimization design, constraining analytical choices or forcing risky departures from human-choice grounding. The full normative bedrock of DPO—Savage's properness theory—is far broader than BTL, but the connection between the two has never been made explicit. The paper asks how to generalize BTL to the full extent of Savage's framework and what ML design freedom this unlocks.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard preference triplets (x, y, y') indicating that y is chosen over y' given prompt x, along with a reference policy π_ref, and produces a trained policy π_θ. Unusually, the framework also supports Machina-Davidson-Marschak (MDM) binary lotteries (y₁y₂)_α as an extended action space, enabling abstention (p(y≻y'|x)+p(y'≻y|x)<1) that BTL forbids. The final loss to be minimized depends only on the policy parameters through a triptych of independently chosen functions (ℓ, F, ℓ̃), with no requirement that the loss ψ be convex—only strictly monotonic.

## Method

The paper introduces KLST*, a generalization of BTL built on Doignon-Falmagne axioms with MDM lotteries to bypass the infeasible solvability axiom, yielding a utility representation p(y≻y'|x)=F(u(x,y)−u(x,y')) where F is strictly increasing with F(z)+F(−z)≤1. The main result (Theorem 4.3) shows that for any strictly increasing ψ and any strictly increasing F̃:ℝ→[0,1], there exists a strictly proper loss (ℓ̃₀,ℓ̃₁) such that ψ=ℓ̃₀∘F̃, meaning any valid loss can be paired with any KLST*-compliant human choice model. The full generalization is parameterized by a triptych (ℓ, F, ℓ̃): a proper loss ℓ defining the Bregman-divergence reward difference, a choice function F, and a strictly proper loss ℓ̃ defining the final loss—three completely independent design knobs. This framework subsumes DPO (canonical connection + symmetric log-loss), SimPO margins, ORPO, IPO, RRHF, and length normalization as special cases, and a toy experiment with gemma2_2b_it on Alpaca Eval v2 demonstrates that non-convex losses can outperform the convex exponential loss.

## Results

The paper is primarily theoretical, with its main result (Theorem 4.3) showing that any strictly increasing loss ψ can be paired with any KLST* human choice model, eliminating the need to tie a preference optimization algorithm to a specific choice model. The sole empirical validation is a toy experiment on gemma2_2b_it evaluated on Alpaca Eval v2 (rater: gemini-2.5-flash-lite), comparing a non-convex loss ψ_a against the convex exponential loss. The non-convex variants with a=6 (54.50% win rate) and a=3 (53.00% win rate) outperform the exponential loss, while a=10 (44.60%) does not, suggesting that moderate non-convexity can provide a practical advantage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper with no formal baseline comparisons in the traditional sense; the `compares_to` set is empty. The only empirical comparison is a small-scale toy experiment (Section 6) where the proposed non-convex loss ψ_a is pitted against the standard convex exponential loss (the a→−∞ limit of ψ_a) on gemma2_2b_it with Alpaca Eval v2. The non-convex variant with a=6 wins 54.50% of pairwise comparisons, and a=3 wins 53.00%, both exceeding the 50% threshold, while a=10 (closer to the exponential) loses at 44.60%. The paper's broader empirical contribution is the classification in Table 1, which shows that prominent DPO variants (DPO, ORPO, IPO, RRHF, AlphaPO) all fit within the proposed PPPO framework, demonstrating that the theoretical generalization encompasses the current landscape rather than proposing a new SOTA method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes DPO's normative foundation from the specific Bradley-Terry-Luce (BTL) choice model to the full scope of Savage's properness theory, using a new axiomatic framework (KLST*) built on Doignon-Falmagne measurement theory and Machina-Davidson-Marschak lotteries to bypass the intractable solvability axiom. It positions itself against the large body of DPO follow-ups that either remain tethered to BTL (constraining analytical design) or abandon human choice grounding entirely, showing that the choice model and the loss function are in fact fully decoupled. Theorem 5.1 connects to a nearly 70-year-old result on separable proper losses (McCarthy 1956, Savage 1971, Bernardo 1979, Dawid 2007), extending it to maximal generality to show that DPO's KL divergence is the unique separable proper choice for n>2.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central result—that any strictly increasing loss ψ can be paired with any KLST*-compliant human choice model, and that convexity of ψ is not required—fundamentally reframes the design space for preference optimization, removing what the authors call a 'straitjacket' that has constrained DPO variants. The framework also provides normative justification for existing ad-hoc extensions (SimPO margins, length normalization in Park et al. and Meng et al.), unifying them under a single theoretical umbrella. However, the empirical validation is extremely thin (one 2B model, one benchmark, three hyperparameter values), so the practical question of whether non-convex losses or non-BTL choice models yield meaningful improvements remains largely open. The paper also acknowledges that some DPO variants (e.g., chi-squared preference optimization) escape the framework entirely, signaling an open problem in characterizing the full boundary of what Savage's properness can and cannot support.
