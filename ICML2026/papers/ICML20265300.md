## Problem

Model-Based Reinforcement Learning suffers from Historical Tethering: imagination rollouts are initialized exclusively from observed states in a replay buffer, creating a learning asymmetry where the world model's manifold discovery far outpaces the policy's sparse-reward optimization. This confines the agent to previously traversed regions, forcing costly random walks in out-of-distribution areas even when the learned world model already possesses the structural knowledge to bridge them.

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw RGB pixel observations (as in DreamerV3 on DMC) encoded into a latent manifold via an RSSM, and additionally takes a sampled latent state from the replay buffer plus a Gaussian noise vector ε~N(0,I) as input to the adversarial generator G_θ(s,ε). It produces two outputs: policy actions executed in the real environment, and synthesized interventional anchor states s' in latent space that serve as non-continuous starting points for imagination rollouts. The generator operates exclusively during the imagination phase, imposing zero additional environment interaction cost.

## Method

Mind Dreamer introduces an adversarial state generator G_θ that performs Active Causal Intervention by sampling imagination initial states from a learned distribution p_gen rather than the historical buffer, enabling non-continuous latent jumps to epistemic blind spots. To resolve the credit assignment paradox across these spatial ruptures, the paper derives two recursive Relay Potential Fields: a Pragmatic Relay Value Function (RVF) that treats anchors as intermediary via-points rather than terminal destinations, and an Epistemic Relay Uncertainty Function (RUF) that aggregates information gain with a theoretically necessary quadratic discount γ² to prevent compounding model variance. The generator is trained via an InfoNCE contrastive objective against a dynamic baseline of historical experiences, constrained by manifold self-consistency losses (dynamics coherence and cycle-consistency) that define a pessimistic trust region. Theoretically, the framework is shown to approximate a variance-minimizing importance sampler that expands the latent manifold's spectral gap, reducing hitting time to critical bottleneck states from O(poly(Φ⁻¹)) to O(log|M|).

## Results

On 20 DeepMind Control Suite vision tasks, Mind Dreamer (MD) achieves a 1.67× average sample-efficiency speedup over DreamerV3, reaching 90% of peak performance in 334.7k steps versus DreamerV3's 557.6k steps. In bottleneck/sparse-reward tasks, the acceleration reaches 8.8× (e.g., Pendulum Swingup). MD also achieves a higher average return of 831.1 versus DreamerV3's 780.3, with the largest gains in sparse-reward settings: +59.8% on Hopper Hop and +30.3% on Quadruped Run.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MD is compared against three baselines on 20 DMC vision tasks. Against DreamerV3 (the primary trajectory-tethered baseline), MD wins on both sample efficiency (1.67× average speedup, up to 8.8× on bottleneck tasks like Pendulum Swingup) and asymptotic performance (avg. return 831.1 vs. 780.3), with the largest gaps in sparse-reward tasks (Hopper Hop +59.8%, Quadruped Run +30.3%). Against DreamerV2, MD isolates the gains attributable to the ACI mechanism versus backbone improvements, confirming that the speedup is not merely an artifact of a stronger world model. Against Plan2Explore (curiosity-driven exploration), MD outperforms by leveraging non-continuous latent jumps rather than trajectory-bound planning, effectively converting a global discovery problem into a local refinement task. The ablation in Fig. 4 further confirms that removing either the pragmatic relay (V_RVF) or epistemic relay (V_RUF) degrades performance, and removing the manifold constraint L_mf causes catastrophic failure, validating that the gains are specific to the full ACI framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DreamerV3 latent imagination framework by replacing replay-buffer initialization with an adversarial generator that samples interventional anchors from a learned distribution p_gen, drawing on Active Inference's Expected Free Energy as a global discovery functional rather than a local policy metric. It departs from trajectory-bound exploration methods (Plan2Explore, intrinsic motivation) and trajectory-exploitation techniques (HER, goal-conditioned RL) by performing non-continuous latent jumps, and introduces Relay Value/Uncertainty Functions as novel credit-assignment mechanisms across spatial ruptures. The theoretical grounding connects EFE minimization to variance-minimizing importance sampling on manifold residuals via the Bernstein-von Mises theorem, positioning the generator as an active curriculum for manifold repair rather than a passive simulator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a genuine structural limitation in MBRL—imagination confined to historically visited states—which is particularly acute in sparse-reward and bottleneck environments where random-walk exploration is intractable. The 8.8× speedup in sparse tasks and the derived γ² epistemic horizon (showing uncertainty propagation must decay quadratically, not linearly) offer concrete theoretical guidance for future exploration mechanisms. However, the computational overhead of adversarial generator training, the entanglement of epistemic uncertainty with non-stationary environmental drift, and the single-architecture (RSSM/DreamerV3) evaluation limit immediate generalizability. Open questions include whether ACI scales to high-dimensional embodied settings and whether the manifold constraints can be relaxed for truly open-ended discovery.
