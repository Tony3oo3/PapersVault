## Problem

Conformal prediction provides distribution-free coverage guarantees only under exchangeability, but graph-structured multivariate time series violate this assumption due to strong cross-node coupling that induces system-level dependence. Existing approaches either calibrate each series independently (yielding only marginal guarantees) or impose restrictive sparsity assumptions on inter-series relations, leaving joint non-exchangeability in the spectral domain unaddressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261375/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

SCALE is a post-hoc module that consumes a window of W=12 residual snapshots (ground truth minus point forecasts from a frozen STGNN backbone), the graph adjacency matrix, and the point forecasts themselves. It outputs two-sided prediction intervals by predicting lower and upper residual quantiles (at α/2 and 1−α/2) for K future steps, which are added to the point forecasts. The low-frequency branch uses a learnable encoder while the high-frequency branch is parameter-free, so the method adds minimal trainable parameters on top of the fixed forecaster.

## Method

The core insight is Spectral Graph Conditional Exchangeability (SGCE): high-frequency graph-spectral components are approximately exchangeable when conditioned on low-frequency ones, because cross-node coupling concentrates in low-frequency global trends. SCALE operationalizes this via Spectral Graph Wavelet Transform (SGWT) to decompose residual snapshots into low- and high-frequency components, then applies conformal calibration only to the high-frequency stream. A lightweight STID-style encoder maps the low-frequency history to a conditioning embedding, while a parameter-free extractor computes per-node STD and RMS statistics of the high-frequency residuals. An adaptive sigmoid gate modulates the high-frequency feature map by the low-frequency embedding to produce the final residual quantile estimates, trained end-to-end with the pinball (quantile regression) loss. Theoretical analysis establishes finite-sample coverage under exact SGCE and a bounded coverage gap (spectral leakage + residual dependence) under imperfect wavelet approximation.

## Results

SCALE achieves near-nominal coverage (within 0.02 of 1−α) across all four cross-domain datasets while consistently producing the narrowest prediction intervals. At α=0.05, SCALE attains the best Winkler score on every dataset: 17.92 (METR-LA), 84.52 (AirQuality), 1321.51 (Electricity), and 18.80 (USHCN-West). On Electricity at α=0.05, SCALE reduces PI-Width by ≈19.6% over the strongest competing baseline EnbPI (776.05 vs. 965.59). At α=0.10, SCALE again achieves the lowest PI-Width and Winkler across all datasets, e.g., 599.07 PI-Width on Electricity vs. 701.52 (EnbPI) and 748.15 (CoREL).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCALE outperforms all seven baselines on both efficiency metrics (PI-Width and Winkler) across all four datasets and all three α levels. Against the closest competitor CoREL (which models cross-series dependence via a relational quantile predictor), SCALE achieves lower Winkler scores by 0.18 (METR-LA, α=0.05), 4.81 (AirQuality), 238.17 (Electricity), and 0.15 (USHCN-West), while maintaining comparable coverage. Against EnbPI, the strongest online-adaptive baseline, SCALE reduces PI-Width by ≈19.6% on Electricity (776.05 vs. 965.59 at α=0.05) and by ≈14.5% at α=0.10 (599.07 vs. 701.52). SeqCP and NexCP, which rely on recency-weighted or windowed quantiles, show the largest coverage violations (e.g., SeqCP drops to 0.9097 on METR-LA at α=0.05, well below the 0.95 target), while SCALE stays within 0.02 of nominal. HopCPT and ConForME exhibit high variance or oscillatory behavior (HopCPT's Winkler on Electricity at α=0.10 reaches 1140.75 with ±419.67 std), whereas SCALE remains stable. The ablation (SCALE w/o SGWT) confirms that removing the spectral decomposition degrades both coverage and efficiency, validating that the spectral conditioning is the key driver of the gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends split conformal prediction (Vovk et al., 2005) to graph-structured multivariate time series by introducing a spectral-domain conditioning mechanism. Prior CP methods for time series (NexCP, EnbPI, HopCPT, SeqCP) address temporal non-exchangeability through sequential or adaptive reweighting, while CoREL (Cini et al., 2025) handles cross-series dependence via relational quantile prediction under sparsity assumptions. SCALE departs from both lines by arguing that the non-exchangeability in graph-structured MTS is fundamentally a spectral phenomenon: the Spectral Graph Wavelet Transform (Hammond et al., 2011) naturally separates strongly coupled low-frequency components from approximately exchangeable high-frequency ones, enabling conformal calibration in the spectral domain under the new SGCE condition rather than in the original signal domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SGCE framework provides a principled, theoretically grounded route to valid conformal prediction for graph-structured MTS, with finite-sample coverage guarantees that are robust to low-frequency distribution shifts. The spectral decomposition perspective offers a new paradigm for addressing non-exchangeability that generalizes across domains (traffic, environment, energy, climate), suggesting that frequency-domain conditioning may be a broadly applicable strategy for coupled systems. Key limitations include reliance on the graph structure being accurate, the spectral separation being effective (weak when coupling is diffuse across frequencies), and the coverage guarantee degrading gracefully but not perfectly under imperfect wavelet approximation. Open questions include extending to dynamic/evolving graphs, obtaining joint (simultaneous) coverage across all nodes, and applying the framework to downstream risk-sensitive decision-making in transportation and power systems.
