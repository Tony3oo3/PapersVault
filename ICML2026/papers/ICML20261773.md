## Problem

Existing ECG representation learning methods operate entirely in the observable 12-lead signal space, where leads are treated as independent channels despite being linear projections of the same underlying 3D cardiac electrical field. This introduces substantial redundancy, entangles cardiac morphology with acquisition geometry (electrode placement, device-specific gains), and leads to poor generalization under domain shift. Although the Frank vectorcardiogram (VCG) provides a compact, physically grounded 3D abstraction of this field, no general self-supervised representation learning framework had been designed to operate in this latent space.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a 10-second, 12-lead ECG segment (resampled to 100 Hz, bandpass-filtered at 0.67–40 Hz, per-lead z-scored) along with a randomly sampled subset of K visible lead indices. It produces a fixed-length ECG embedding vector formed by concatenating a structural embedding (mean-pooled beat tokens), a dynamic embedding (final GRU hidden state), and a rhythm embedding (MLP-encoded R-R interval statistics), which is used for downstream linear probing. For the reconstruction task, it additionally outputs the full 12-lead ECG signal with the masked leads synthesized from the latent VCG trajectory.

## Method

LVCG learns view-invariant representations by mapping multi-lead ECG into a latent VCG space via a non-learnable geometry layer: a Tikhonov-regularized least-squares lift recovers a 3×P VCG patch from K visible leads, and a project operation maps decoded VCG patches back to target ECG leads using the fixed 12×3 lead direction matrix. A shared ResNet1D beat encoder compresses each VCG patch into a single D-dimensional token (information bottleneck), and a GRU-based autoregressive latent state-space model captures inter-beat dynamics by predicting successive tokens. Training is self-supervised via multi-lead masked reconstruction, optimizing a unified loss that combines masked-lead ECG reconstruction, VCG patch reconstruction (enforcing consistency in the physically grounded space), and a Huber temporal loss on token differences with stop-gradient on encoder outputs. The key architectural distinction from prior ECG-space methods is that all encoding, bottlenecking, and temporal modeling occur on the 3-channel VCG trajectory rather than on the 12-channel ECG signal, decoupling cardiac morphology from lead-specific projection geometry.

## Results

On linear probing classification across six datasets, LVCG achieves average AUC of 67.30 / 74.54 / 80.47 at 1% / 10% / 100% labels, outperforming the strongest ECG-specific baseline HeartLang (63.80 / 73.39 / 84.56) in the low-label regime while HeartLang leads at 100% on in-distribution PTB-XL. The advantage is most pronounced on external datasets under domain shift: at 1% labels, LVCG reaches 71.09 AUC on CPSC 2018 (vs. 60.44 for HeartLang, a 10.6 pp gap) and 62.47 on CSN (vs. 57.94). For multi-lead reconstruction in the (3,12) setting, LVCG achieves MSE of 0.49 on CPSC 2018 and 0.47 on PTB, substantially outperforming Nef-Net (0.68 and 1.56 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LVCG outperforms all five adapted CV baselines (SimCLR, BYOL, BarlowTwins, MoCo-v3, SimSiam) on average AUC at every label fraction, with the largest gaps at 1% labels (e.g., 67.30 vs. 58.24 for SimCLR). Among ECG-specific methods, LVCG surpasses TS-TCC, CLOCS, ASTCL, CRT, and ST-MEM across nearly all settings; ST-MEM is the weakest ECG baseline overall (69.60 avg at 100%). The closest competitor is HeartLang, which leads LVCG on in-distribution PTB-XL at 100% labels (e.g., 87.52 vs. 80.13 on Super-class) but is outperformed by 10+ pp on external datasets at 1% labels, confirming LVCG's domain-shift robustness. For multi-lead reconstruction, LVCG beats all four baselines (KIM, VAE-CNN, E-LSTM, Nef-Net) on both MSE and MAE in the (3,12) setting, with the largest margin over Nef-Net on PTB (MSE 0.47 vs. 1.56). In the (5,12) setting, LVCG matches Nef-Net on CPSC MAE (0.35) while improving MSE (0.40 vs. 0.44), and clearly leads on PTB (MSE 0.78 vs. 1.00). On non-cardiac detection (Table 3), LVCG outperforms both ST-MEM and HeartLang on all three tasks at 1% and 10% labels, with the largest gain on Chronic Kidney Disease at 1% (69.04 vs. 58.92 for HeartLang).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of ECG representation learning (SimCLR, ST-MEM, HeartLang, etc.), which treats multi-lead ECG as independent channels in the observable signal space, by instead learning in the physically grounded Frank vectorcardiogram (VCG) space. It repurposes the VCG framework previously used only for lead synthesis (Nef-Net, NEF-NET+) as a latent bottleneck for self-supervised representation learning, introducing a non-learnable geometric lift/project transformation, a token-bottleneck beat encoder, and a GRU-based temporal module. The key conceptual departure is that the lead direction matrix is fixed (not learned) and serves as a deterministic inductive bias that enforces cross-view consistency, rather than being treated as another set of parameters to optimize.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a physically grounded latent space can substantially improve cross-dataset generalization (over 10% AUC gain on external datasets at 1% labels) and label efficiency, with the added practical benefit of near-flat performance as input leads drop from 12 to 3—directly relevant to wearable and patch-type monitoring. The main limitation is the single-dipole VCG approximation, which the authors acknowledge is a simplification of the true thoracic potential field; extending to richer field models or combining VCG-space learning with supervised/multimodal pretraining are natural next steps. The finding that a fixed, non-learnable geometric transformation outperforms learnable alternatives also raises broader questions about when physical priors should be hard-coded versus learned in biomedical signal processing.
