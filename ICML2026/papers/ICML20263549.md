## Problem

Existing parallel decoding strategies for diffusion language models (dLLMs) suffer from low-confidence degeneration: when token confidence drops below a threshold, decoding collapses to single-token generation, wasting the model's inherent parallel capacity. Autoregressive speculative decoding cannot be directly transferred because dLLMs generate through iterative denoising with arbitrary unmasking orders and bidirectional attention, requiring speculation over complete denoising trajectories rather than fixed-order token sequences.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263549/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text prompt and the diffusion LM's per-step logits (with per-position confidence scores) to construct draft denoising trajectories over 32-token blocks. It produces the same generated text sequence as the baseline but with 30–40% fewer denoising iterations. The key structural difference from autoregressive speculation is that each 'draft' specifies not just token identities but also their positions and unmasking order across multiple denoising steps, and verification uses blockwise attention masks rather than causal masks.

## Method

The framework operates in three stages: (1) confidence-stratified tree construction, where high-confidence tokens form the root block and low-confidence positions trigger branching with top-k expansion at the root and top-1 expansion in deeper layers (yielding O(W·D) nodes instead of exponential); (2) blockwise parallel verification, where all draft blocks are concatenated and evaluated in a single forward pass using an attention mask that preserves bidirectional attention to cached prefix/suffix tokens while isolating draft branches from each other; (3) inter-block speculation, which exploits bidirectional visibility to monitor the next block's confidence during the current block's decoding and trigger joint speculation across block boundaries. The paper proves exactness under top-1 decoding with cached KV states (Theorem 1) and characterizes trajectory drift as the fundamental cost of increased parallelism.

## Results

On LLaDA-7B-Instruct, the proposed trajectory-level speculative decoding achieves 7–14× end-to-end speedup over vanilla dLLMs and 1.2–1.4× additional speedup over Fast-dLLM (Dual Cache), reducing denoising steps by 30–43% (e.g., 179.2 → 106.7 steps on HumanEval) while increasing tokens-per-step from 2.6 to 3.8–4.3. Accuracy remains within 1% of the baseline across GSM8K, MATH, HumanEval, and MBPP. On the IFEval benchmark (a low-confidence-heavy task), the method achieves a 1.9× speedup over Fast-dLLM (36.8 → 69.1 TPS) with a 44% reduction in steps, demonstrating that gains scale with the prevalence of low-confidence tokens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Fast-dLLM (Dual Cache), the primary baseline, the proposed method consistently wins on throughput and step count across all four benchmarks while matching or slightly exceeding accuracy: e.g., on HumanEval it achieves 94.4 TPS vs. 70.6 TPS (+34%) and 106.7 vs. 179.2 denoising steps (−40%), with accuracy of 46.3% vs. 45.7%. On GSM8K the speedup is 13.6× vs. 11.9×, and on MATH it is 10.2× vs. 8.4×. Against Spiffy (concurrent intra-block speculative decoding with graph-based calibration), the paper reports a reference comparison on LLaDA-7B showing roughly 2× higher NFE-based speedup (e.g., 7.18× vs. 3.04× on GSM8K, 4.87× vs. 2.88× on HumanEval), though the authors note this is not a controlled reproduction since Spiffy is not publicly open-sourced. The key differentiator is that the proposed method additionally performs inter-block speculation and tree-based trajectory exploration, whereas Spiffy is limited to intra-block speculation with calibrated exact verification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends autoregressive speculative decoding (Leviathan et al., 2023; Medusa; EAGLE) to diffusion language models by reformulating the problem from token-space to trajectory-space speculation, where a 'draft' must specify not just token identities but also positions and unmasking orders across multiple denoising steps. It builds directly on Fast-dLLM's dual-cache infrastructure (Wu et al., 2025) for per-step efficiency, while addressing the orthogonal bottleneck of total denoising steps through confidence-stratified tree exploration and blockwise parallel verification. Concurrent with Spiffy (Agrawal et al., 2025), which performs intra-block speculation with graph-based calibration for lossless verification, this paper differentiates by introducing inter-block speculation that exploits bidirectional attention for cross-block lookahead—a capability single-block designs cannot express—and by using a simpler tree-based verification that achieves higher acceptance rates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of low-confidence degeneration (occurring in 80%+ of steps) as the fundamental throughput bottleneck in diffusion LM decoding, and the demonstration that correct tokens frequently appear in top-k candidates even under low confidence, provides a principled justification for trajectory-level speculation over simply raising parallelism budgets. The formal characterization of trajectory drift as the fundamental cost of increased parallelism (Theorem 1) gives practitioners a clear exactness boundary and explains why accuracy loss concentrates on multi-step reasoning tasks. Open questions include developing provably lossless verification for diffusion models, adaptive tree construction that adjusts to per-prompt confidence distributions, and whether the approach extends to continuous diffusion or larger-scale models. The method's orthogonality to system-level optimizations (KV caching, quantization, sparse attention) suggests cumulative gains are achievable as diffusion LMs mature.
