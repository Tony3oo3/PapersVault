## Problem

Existing hallucination detectors for LLMs either require training a separate classifier (sensitive to distribution shift) or rely on comparing multiple sampled outputs (computationally expensive and conceptually ill-suited to reasoning tasks where valid reasoning paths are diverse). The paper asks whether the well-studied out-of-distribution (OOD) detection literature from computer vision can be adapted to provide training-free, single-sample hallucination detectors that work effectively on reasoning tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262887/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each decoding step, the method consumes the penultimate-layer feature vector (d_model-dimensional) and the language head's weight matrix and bias vector, requiring no access to the training corpus or multiple generated samples. The per-step geometric uncertainty score is averaged over all T decoding steps to produce a single sequence-level scalar, which is compared against a threshold τ to yield a binary hallucination flag. The approach is fully training-free and operates on a single generated sequence, incurring only O(d_model) or O(d_model·k) additional computation per step.

## Method

The paper reframes next-token prediction as a high-dimensional linear classification over the vocabulary and adapts two geometric OOD detectors to this setting. NCI measures the cosine proximity of the centered penultimate-layer feature to the weight vector of the most confident token, where the centering point μ_G is replaced by an analytical Decision-Neutral Closest Point (the feature minimizing logit variance across the vocabulary, solvable in closed form via a Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse) rather than an infeasible empirical training-data mean. fDBD measures the distance from the feature to decision boundaries of alternative tokens using a derived lower-bound approximation, restricted to the top-k highest-logit alternative tokens to handle the massive vocabulary efficiently. Both scores are averaged across decoding steps to form a sequence-level hallucination score, and the paper proves that the fDBD lower bound is tight for the nearest boundary, establishing the geometric validity of the approximation.

## Results

On Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct, fDBD with a selected top-k alternative set achieves AUROC of 69.24 (CSQA), 76.36 (GSM8K), and 76.20 (AQuA), while NCI reaches 66.07, 76.32, and 74.41 respectively. On Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct, fDBD (selected k) attains 72.47, 77.19, and 78.22 AUROC, and NCI reaches 71.60, 75.83, and 78.19. Both methods are training-free, single-sample, and incur minimal latency overhead (~0.6–0.8 ms/token above standard decoding). On the open-ended TruthfulQA benchmark, NCI (68.17) and fDBD (67.83) also outperform all single-sample baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NCI and fDBD consistently outperform all single-sample baselines across both model families and all three reasoning datasets. Perplexity (Ren et al., 2023) is the strongest single-sample baseline on Llama-3.2-3B for GSM8K (69.63) and AQuA (72.85), but fDBD (selected k) surpasses it by 6.7 and 3.4 AUROC points respectively; on Qwen-2.5-7B, fDBD leads Perplexity by up to 5.7 points on GSM8K. The latent-space CoE methods (Wang et al., 2024) — Max P, CoE-R, CoE-C — are substantially weaker, with CoE-R and CoE-C often near or below chance (50 AUROC) on Llama-3.2-3B, while fDBD achieves 75–78 AUROC. The verbalized P(True) method (Kadavath et al., 2022) performs poorly on Llama-3.2-3B (39–51 AUROC) and moderately on Qwen-2.5-7B (68–73 AUROC), still trailing fDBD by 4–10 points. Among multi-sample baselines, SelfCheckGPT NLI (Manakul et al., 2023) is the closest competitor on GSM8K (74.29 on Llama-3.2-3B, 76.22 on Qwen-2.5-7B), but fDBD (selected k) exceeds it by 2.1 and 1.0 points while requiring only a single forward pass. Lexical Similarity (Lin et al., 2022b, 2023) and Semantic Entropy (Kuhn et al., 2023) are consistently outperformed by 3–13 AUROC points, confirming that cross-sample consistency measures struggle on reasoning tasks with diverse valid paths.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges the well-developed OOD detection literature in computer vision (specifically the geometric detectors NCI and fDBD) to hallucination detection in LLMs by reframing next-token prediction as a high-dimensional classification problem. It departs from prior hallucination detection work in two key ways: it is both training-free and single-sample, avoiding the training cost of classifier-based methods and the inference overhead of multi-sample consistency methods (SelfCheckGPT, Semantic Entropy). A notable methodological contribution is the Decision-Neutral Closest Point, an analytical proxy for the intractable training feature mean that NCI requires, derived from the geometry of the language head itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, scalable pathway for hallucination detection in reasoning tasks where existing multi-sample methods struggle due to the diversity of valid reasoning paths. The OOD-to-hallucination framing opens a systematic route for importing the rich OOD detection literature (energy-based, Mahalanobis, deep nearest-neighbor methods, etc.) into LLM safety, which the authors explicitly flag as future work. Limitations include a performance gap on short-answer datasets (e.g., TriviaQA), reliance on only two geometric OOD scores, and the fact that the analytical proxy for training statistics, while effective, is an approximation whose theoretical guarantees are not fully characterized.
