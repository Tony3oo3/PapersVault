## Problem

Existing unified multimodal models that combine understanding and generation suffer from objective interference and representation entanglement: sharing parameters between cross-entropy decoding and diffusion-style regression destabilizes joint optimization, and improving one capability inadvertently erodes the other. Additionally, prior alignment stages (scalar-reward RL, multimodal reflection) optimize outputs in isolation without modeling relative preference under identical conditioning, failing to learn explicit decision boundaries between faithful and subtly incorrect edits.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264332/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes text instructions paired with one or more input/reference images (discretized into visual token sequences via PyraTok, a text-guided multi-scale quantizer) and produces text responses, generated or edited images (decoded from discrete visual tokens), and a parallel reflection sequence that provides reasoning traces for the edit. Inference requires only 20 denoising steps, and the model supports zero-shot generalization to inpainting, in-context subject-driven generation from multiple reference images, and compositional generation without task-specific training. Sequence lengths extend up to 4096 tokens in Stage III to accommodate long-context reasoning over visual content.

## Method

UniDFlow repurposes a frozen Qwen3-VL backbone as a discrete flow-matching (DFM) generator over multimodal tokens, unifying understanding, generation, and editing under a single categorical NLL objective. To prevent parameter entanglement, it trains separate LoRA adapters for understanding (Δθ_u) and generation (Δθ_g), combined at inference by a lightweight Mixture-of-LoRA Router (MoRA) that dynamically weights them based on the hidden state at each diffusion step. Time conditioning is injected via TSG-RMSNorm, which modulates the pretrained RMSNorm scale parameters with zero-initialized multiplicative and additive terms, preserving the backbone's reasoning priors exactly at initialization. The three-stage training pipeline progresses from text alignment (with KL regularization against the original AR distribution) to vision alignment, and finally to mRef-DPO, a reference-anchored multimodal preference objective that optimizes pairwise log-likelihood margins between preferred and rejected text, visual, and reflection outputs relative to a frozen reference policy, stabilizing cross-modal credit assignment and improving editing faithfulness.

## Results

UniDFlow (4B) achieves state-of-the-art performance across all three task categories. On multimodal understanding (EVALVLM), it scores 91.2 on MMBench, 74.3 on MMMU, 85.9 on MathVista, and 80.2 on MMVP, outperforming the next-best unified model MammothModa2 (4B) by 5.3%, 4.3%, 5.0%, and 4.1% respectively. On text-to-image generation, UniDFlow reaches 0.95 on GenEval and 91.19 on DPG-Bench, surpassing EMMA (4B) by +2.2% and +6.5%, and even exceeding the much larger Qwen-Image (7B+20B) by +4.0% and +3.2%. On image editing, it achieves 4.24 on ImgEdit, 0.921 CLIP-I on EMU-Edit, and 7.12 on GEdit-Bench-EN, leading EMMA by +5.7%, +1.1%, and +9.2% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniDFlow (4B) consistently outperforms all unified understanding-generation baselines. Against the strongest same-scale competitor MammothModa2 (4B), UniDFlow gains +5.3% on MMBench, +4.3% on MMMU, +5.0% on MathVista, and +4.1% on MMVP, while also leading on MME-S by a large margin (2555 vs. 1998, +27.9%). Compared to the larger BAGEL (7B MoT), UniDFlow improves MME-P by +6.9%, MME-S by +7.0%, MMMU by +19.0%, and MathVista by +12.8%, despite using roughly half the parameters. In generation, UniDFlow (4B) surpasses EMMA (4B) by +2.2% on GenEval and +6.5% on DPG-Bench, and beats the substantially larger Qwen-Image (7B+20B) by +4.0% and +3.2% respectively. For editing, UniDFlow leads EMMA by +5.7% on ImgEdit overall (4.24 vs. 4.01) and by +9.2% on GEdit-Bench-EN (7.12 vs. 6.52), with the largest gains in Extract and Remove operations. Against understanding-only models, UniDFlow achieves 20.4% higher overall performance than Qwen2.5-VL (3B) and outperforms Qwen3-VL (4B) on MMBench (91.2 vs. 85.1) and MMMU (74.3 vs. 64.1). The paper also reports that UniDFlow-14B further extends these leads, reaching 89.24 on EVALVLM average and 0.98 on GenEval, while maintaining the highest inference throughput among compared models at that scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniDFlow sits at the intersection of discrete flow matching (Gat et al., 2024) as a unified generative objective and reference-anchored DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) as a preference alignment mechanism, applied on top of a frozen Qwen3-VL backbone. It departs from hybrid AR+diffusion unified models (EMMA, BAGEL, MammothModa2) that couple cross-entropy and diffusion regression in shared parameters, and from fully discrete diffusion models (UniDisc, MUDDIT) that underperform at scale, by instead using task-specific LoRA adapters with a learned router to decouple understanding from generation. The TSG-RMSNorm time-conditioning mechanism is a novel architectural contribution that preserves pretrained feature geometry, and mRef-DPO extends DPO to jointly align text, vision, and reflection streams under a frozen reference policy rather than optimizing scalar rewards or isolated outputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The parameter-efficient design (frozen 4B backbone + LoRA adapters) achieving SOTA across understanding, generation, and editing benchmarks while outperforming models with 3× more parameters offers a practical recipe for unified multimodal systems without full-model retraining. The zero-shot generalization to inpainting, in-context subject-driven generation, and reference-based editing without explicit task-specific training suggests the unified discrete flow-matching formulation captures transferable multimodal structure. Open questions include whether the three-stage pipeline and preference data curation (3.5M curated pairs via proprietary LLMs) scale to video or higher-resolution generation, whether the MoRA router generalizes to unseen task combinations, and whether the reliance on a specific VLM backbone (Qwen3-VL) limits portability. The paper also flags risks of misuse for deceptive media and bias propagation from the pretrained backbone.
