## Problem

Existing Variate Transformers (e.g., iTransformer) model inter-variate dependencies exclusively in shallow self-attention layers, while deeper FFN layers capture only intra-series temporal dynamics. This structural inconsistency causes spurious correlation modeling, poor generalization under distribution shifts, and optimization difficulties. Additionally, the field over-relies on timestamp encodings for periodicity capture, whose actual efficacy remains unverified.

## Input / Output

The method takes a multivariate time series X ∈ R^{T×N} (T input steps, N variates) and forecasts Y ∈ R^{F×N} for F future steps. Before entering the Transformer, the input is transformed via DCT into the frequency domain where variable-specific learnable masks are applied and a linear resampling layer expands (or contracts) the sequence length to a hyperparameter D, after which iDCT returns the signal to the time domain. This FMR module is position-agnostic and can replace any existing timestamp-embedding or up-sampling strategy.

## Method

CGTFra introduces three plug-and-play components into any Variate Transformer. First, FMR performs learnable frequency-domain masking and linear resampling via DCT/iDCT to enhance input features and preserve periodicity without timestamps. Second, DGL replaces the FFN with a dynamic graph learning module: it constructs a per-layer adjacency matrix from the self-attention output and learnable node embeddings via a gating mechanism, then uses a multi-hop GCN (with the same graph across hops) to aggregate sparse, indirect inter-variate dependencies at the deep feature level. Third, CAL imposes a KL-divergence alignment between the shallow attention score distribution (MCM) and the deep graph adjacency matrix (A), derived as a tractable upper bound on I(Z;X) under the Variational Information Bottleneck principle, so that minimizing the total loss L_MAE + λ·L_KL simultaneously maximizes predictive information and compresses noisy IVD. The framework is validated as a drop-in replacement for FFNs in iTransformer, DUET, VCformer, FilterNet, and CASA, achieving state-of-the-art results on 13 datasets.

## Results

On long-term forecasting (T=96, F∈{96,192,336,720}, averaged), CGTFra achieves the best MSE and MAE across all 9 reported datasets, with headline results including MSE 0.165 / MAE 0.253 on ECL (321 variables), MSE 0.427 / MAE 0.257 on Traffic (862 variables), and MSE 0.238 / MAE 0.260 on Weather. Compared to the strongest baseline DUET (KDD'25), CGTFra reduces MSE by 5.1% and MAE by 4.5% on Traffic, and by 1.7% on ECL. The gains are most pronounced on high-variate datasets (ECL, Traffic) where inter-variate dependency modeling is most challenging.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CGTFra outperforms all 14 baselines on every dataset where results are reported, with the largest margins on high-variate datasets. Against DUET (KDD'25), the closest competitor, CGTFra reduces MSE by 5.1% on Traffic (0.427 vs 0.451) and by 4.1% on ECL (0.165 vs 0.172). Against TimePro (ICML'25) and Soatten (AAAI'25), CGTFra leads by 1–12% MSE depending on the dataset, with the gap widening on ECL and Traffic. Against the original iTransformer (ICLR'24), CGTFra improves MSE by 4.9% on Weather and 7.4% on ECL, confirming the value of deep-layer IVD modeling. The framework-generality experiments (Table 2) further show that simply adding DGL+CAL to iTransformer or VCformer yields 4.9–10.2% MSE reductions, approaching or surpassing DUET and TimePro without architectural changes. On low-variate datasets (ETT, 7 variables), the margins are smaller (1–3% MSE) but still consistent, suggesting the gains are driven primarily by the number of inter-variate relationships to model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Variate Transformer paradigm introduced by iTransformer, which encodes each variable as an independent token to model inter-variate dependencies (IVD) via self-attention. It departs from prior IVD modeling approaches (DUET, VCformer, Crossformer, SageFormer, STGAGRTN, GL-STGTN) that integrate learned dependencies as attention masks or biases in shallow layers, by arguing that IVD must also be modeled in deep network layers. The paper grounds its consistency alignment loss in the Information Bottleneck principle (Tishby et al., 2000), deriving the KL-divergence-based constraint as a tractable upper bound on the compression term I(Z;X), and introduces a Dynamic Graph Learning module that replaces the standard FFN to perform deep-layer IVD aggregation via multi-hop GCN.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of the three components (FMR, DGL, CAL) makes them broadly applicable to any existing Variate Transformer without architectural redesign, which is practically valuable for the MTSF community. The finding that timestamp embeddings are largely unnecessary (and sometimes harmful) challenges a widely-adopted design assumption, though the evidence is primarily from iTransformer. Open questions include whether consistent IVD modeling remains necessary for non-variate Transformer frameworks, how the approach scales to extremely large variable counts (beyond 862), and whether the IB-based alignment generalizes to other dependency structures beyond pairwise variable correlations.
