## Problem

Despite over a decade of work on Stochastic Variance Reduced Gradient (SVRG) and its variants, these methods have never been connected to any Bayesian method at a fundamental level. This paper addresses that gap by showing SVRG is a special case of Posterior Correction (PoCo), a Bayesian knowledge-adaptation mechanism, and uses this connection to derive novel SVRG extensions that cannot be obtained through existing variance-reduction techniques.

## Input / Output

The method consumes per-example loss functions ℓ_i (i=1,…,N) and initial model parameters, operating in the natural-parameter space of an exponential-family posterior q(θ). It produces both a point estimate (the posterior mean m) and an uncertainty estimate (precision matrix S or diagonal variance s), with the latter enabling Newton-like preconditioning. For the scalable IVON-PoCo variant, the output is a diagonal Gaussian posterior with per-parameter variance, matching the memory footprint of AdamW while adding Hessian-based corrections.

## Method

The core mechanism is to express the Bayesian Learning Rule (natural-gradient descent over natural parameters) in a form analogous to Bayes' rule, then apply Posterior Correction: multiplying and dividing by an older posterior q̂_out built from stale gradients to create a correction term that stabilizes mini-batch updates. When the posterior is restricted to an isotropic Gaussian N(θ|m, I), this recovers SVRG exactly (Thm. 2). Using more flexible posteriors automatically yields new extensions: full Gaussians produce a Newton-like variant (VON-PoCo) where both gradient and Hessian are variance-reduced via a Stochastic Variance-Reduced Hessian (SVRH) estimate, and diagonal Gaussians produce an Adam-like variant (IVON-PoCo) that scales to large problems using mega-batches instead of full-batch computations. The natural-gradient formulation is essential—naively applying SVRG to a Bayesian algorithm does not produce the Hessian corrections that emerge here.

## Results

On GPT-2-125M pretraining (50B tokens, OpenWebText), IVON-PoCoMo achieves a final validation perplexity of 17.4, compared to 18.0 for IVON and 18.4 for AdamW. On logistic regression (MNIST, Covertype, CIFAR-10), IVON-PoCo reaches the full-batch L-BFGS minimizer accuracy while IVON does not, and VSGD-PoCo matches SVRG's convergence speed over VSGD/SGD. On ImageNet ResNet-50, IVON-PoCo yields clear accuracy gains when counting optimization steps, but these do not translate into speed-ups when counting data examples seen or gradient evaluations. On CIFAR-10 ResNet-20, IVON-PoCo outperforms both α-SVRG+SGD and IVON at the end of training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against IVON (Shen et al., 2024), IVON-PoCo/IVON-PoCoMo consistently wins on final quality: 17.4 vs 18.0 validation perplexity on GPT-2-125M, reaching the L-BFGS minimizer on logistic regression where IVON falls short, and higher final accuracy on ImageNet ResNet-50 and CIFAR-10 ResNet-20. However, when measured by gradient evaluations or wall-clock time, IVON-PoCoMo requires more compute to match IVON's trajectory, consistent with the known ineffectiveness of variance reduction for deep learning (Defazio & Bottou, 2019). Against SVRG (Johnson & Zhang, 2013), VSGD-PoCo matches SVRG's convergence speed on logistic regression, confirming the theoretical equivalence (Thm. 2) that SVRG is a special case of PoCo with isotropic Gaussians. Against α-SVRG (Yin et al., 2025), IVON-PoCoMo achieves better final validation perplexity on continual pretraining of GPT-125M, and on CIFAR-10 ResNet-20 both methods improve over their respective baselines with IVON-PoCo performing best at the end of training. Overall, the paper's methods win on final quality and convergence in optimization-step terms but lose or tie on compute-efficiency metrics for deep learning, while clearly winning on convex problems like logistic regression.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously disconnected research threads: stochastic variance-reduced gradient methods (SVRG and its decade of variants) and Bayesian knowledge-adaptation methods (specifically Posterior Correction, PoCo). The mathematical foundation is the Bayesian Learning Rule (Khan & Rue, 2023), which provides the natural-gradient framework over exponential-family posteriors; the paper shows that restricting PoCo to isotropic Gaussians recovers SVRG exactly, and that richer Gaussian families (full covariance, diagonal) automatically yield Newton-like and Adam-like SVRG extensions. The Adam-like variant (IVON-PoCo) is explicitly derived as a variance-reduced version of IVON (Shen et al., 2024), while the Newton-like variant (VON-PoCo) extends VON (Khan et al., 2018) with Hessian corrections that no prior Newton-SVRG work implements.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical connection reframes SVRG's gradient corrections as a knowledge-transfer mechanism between old and new posteriors, offering a principled route to derive new variance-reduced optimizers by simply choosing different posterior families. However, the practical impact is currently limited: on convex problems (logistic regression) the speed-ups are clear and strong, but on deep learning tasks (GPT-2 pretraining, ImageNet ResNet-50) the methods do not reduce the number of gradient evaluations needed to reach a target accuracy, consistent with the well-known ineffectiveness of variance reduction for deep learning (Defazio & Bottou, 2019). A key open question is whether the Bayesian perspective can eventually unlock effective variance reduction for large-scale deep learning, and whether the Hessian-correction mechanism in VON-PoCo can be made to outperform existing Newton-SVRG variants in practice.
