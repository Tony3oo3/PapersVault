## Problem

Despite the empirical success of Best-of-N (BoN) sampling and sequential revision for test-time compute, their fundamental limits remain unclear: it is unknown whether BoN is optimal under a fixed inference budget, and the theoretical understanding of sequential TTC is very limited. This paper formalizes TTC with reward models under a mixture-of-reference-policies pretraining assumption and proves that standard BoN is inherently suboptimal, then identifies what the best achievable sequential strategy can be.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265582/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives a text prompt x and a scalar reward function r(a,x) ∈ [-1,1] (in practice, a Process Reward Model score in [0,1]). It operates under a sequential sample-and-evaluate framework where the LLM is queried n times, each time with a potentially different history h_i constructed from the prompt and previously accepted answers. The output is the single highest-reward answer among all n generations, selected via argmax. In practice, the context window is bounded by a sliding-window (FIFO) truncation to respect the LLM's maximum token limit.

## Method

RF-SeqBoN (Reward-Filtered Sequential Best-of-n) is a simple sequential inference procedure that, at each of n iterations, samples an answer from the LLM conditioned on a running history h̄, and appends that answer to h̄ only if its reward score exceeds a threshold γ. The key insight is that under a mixture-of-reference-policies model, conditioning on high-reward actions progressively concentrates the LLM's conditional distribution toward the optimal reference policy π_ref^{τ*(x)}, since the posterior weight of the optimal policy grows exponentially with the number of accepted high-reward actions. Theoretically, the paper proves that RF-SeqBoN achieves sample complexity polylog(1/ε)·κ(x)·C*/ε, where κ(x) = max{p_ref(τ*(x)), e^{-Δ(x)}} < 1, strictly improving over the parallel BoN bound of C*/ε. The improvement is largest when the optimal reference policy has small prior mass but a large probability margin Δ over suboptimal policies, and the method requires no tree search, branching heuristics, or separate verifier-generation roles beyond modifying the history construction rule.

## Results

RF-SeqBoN consistently outperforms vanilla BoN and PureSeq across all five benchmarks (MATH500, GPQA-Diamond, AMC'23, AIME'24, AIME'25) and three backbone models (Qwen3-4B-Instruct, Qwen3-0.6B-Thinking, GPT5-Nano). On MATH500 and AIME'24, the accuracy advantage of RF-SeqBoN over BoN persists or grows as the generation budget N increases, while on GPQA-Diamond, AMC'23, and AIME'25 the baselines gradually close the gap at high budgets, suggesting convergence to the backbone model's intrinsic limit. A difficulty-level breakdown on MATH500 (N=128) shows RF-SeqBoN outperforming both baselines at all five difficulty levels, with the largest gains on the hardest problems (levels 4–5) where PureSeq also surpasses BoN. The method remains robust across model scales, with smaller models (Qwen3-0.6B-Thinking, GPT5-Nano) showing lower absolute accuracy but consistent relative improvements from reward-filtered history.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On MATH500 with Qwen3-4B-Instruct, RF-SeqBoN consistently outperforms Top-k (relative-ranking history) across the full budget range, particularly in low and middle budget regimes, because the absolute reward threshold γ prevents locally-best-but-incorrect candidates from contaminating the context. Against Beam Search (Snell et al., 2024), DVTS (Beeching et al., 2024), and PAG (Jiang et al., 2025), RF-SeqBoN matches or exceeds these stronger verifier-guided baselines at larger budgets despite using a substantially simpler single-sequence reward-filtering mechanism rather than explicit tree search or verify-then-revise procedures. Beam Search and DVTS allocate computation to branching over multiple search paths with beam-width M=4, while PAG alternates between policy generation and generative verification; RF-SeqBoN recovers much of their benefit by selectively reusing high-reward trajectories. All comparisons use the same Llama3.1-8B-PRM-Deepseek-Data reward model and matched token budgets, ensuring improvements are attributable to the history-construction strategy rather than a stronger verifier. The paper also shows that replacing the PRM with a weaker outcome reward model (AceMath-7B-RM) preserves RF-SeqBoN's advantage over baselines, though a minor performance drop appears at large budgets, likely due to reward over-optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sequential sample-and-evaluate framework and E_M-divergence/coverage machinery from Huang et al. (2025a) by introducing a mixture-of-reference-policies model for LLM pretraining, which enables a clean theoretical separation between parallel and sequential test-time compute. The proposed RF-SeqBoN algorithm is explicitly positioned as a hybrid of Best-of-N sampling and the rewind-and-repeat strategy from Kim et al. (2024) and Beirami et al. (2024), but departs from both by using an absolute reward threshold to gate which generations enter the context rather than relying on likelihood-based selection or unfiltered history. Unlike prior sequential TTC work (Tree-of-Thoughts, Beam Search, PAG), the method deliberately avoids complex search structures, instead showing that a single-sequence reward-filtering rule suffices to provably dominate parallel BoN under the mixture model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first provable separation showing that parallel TTC (BoN) is fundamentally suboptimal under a realistic mixture-of-policies pretraining assumption, and that a simple reward-filtering mechanism achieves strictly better sample complexity. This reframes the design space for inference-time compute: rather than investing in more complex search trees or branching heuristics, concentrating the proposal distribution through selective history construction may be the key lever. Open questions include how the approach behaves under reward over-optimization (observed empirically with weaker ORM signals at large budgets), whether the margin assumptions (Assumption 5.4) hold in practice for open-ended generation tasks, and whether the separation result extends beyond the mixture-of-reference-policies model to more general pretraining distributions.
