## Problem

Parallel decoding in diffusion LLMs (dLLMs) relies on conservative confidence thresholds (>0.9) to suppress the Joint Probability Dependence Error (JPDE) from simultaneously accepting multiple tokens, which discards 61% of correct predictions in the 0.65–0.9 confidence range. Sequential decoding eliminates JPDE by construction but is prohibitively slow and forces a left-to-right conditioning pattern that mismatches the bidirectional denoising distribution dLLMs are trained under, leaving bidirectional attention underutilized at inference.

## Input / Output

DC-Leap is a training-free decoding strategy applied to pre-trained dLLMs (LLaDA-1.5, LLaDA-8B-Instruct, Dream-v0-7B-Instruct) that takes a text prompt and produces a generated text sequence via iterative denoising. Generation follows a semi-autoregressive remasking schedule with block size 32; standard evaluation uses 256-token outputs (512 for HumanEval/IFEval, 1024 for long-sequence experiments). The method requires only two scalar hyperparameters—commitment threshold τ_commit and draft threshold τ_draft—set identically across all models and benchmarks, with no additional training or architectural modification.

## Method

DC-Leap introduces two complementary mechanisms. First, Dynamic Contiguous Verification (DCV) replaces the global conditional-independence assumption of parallel decoding with a local, window-level factorization: a dynamic decoding window of variable length K (bounded by a maximum L) advances left-to-right, and within each window all tokens are decoded in parallel but only the longest contiguous prefix whose confidences exceed τ_commit is committed, truncating at the first sub-threshold token. This neutralizes JPDE without requiring the >0.9 thresholds of prior methods, safely lowering τ_commit to 0.70. Second, Draft-guided Decoding maintains uncommitted high-confidence tokens (τ_draft=0.98) predicted beyond the current window as look-ahead placeholders that are updated in place each iteration and re-masked once they enter the window's eligible range. These drafts supply right-side context that sharpens the predictive confidence gap (P_top1 − P_top2) and aligns inference-time conditioning with the bidirectional denoising distribution, preserving the structural benefits of bidirectional attention that pure sequential commitment would forfeit.

## Results

On LLaDA-1.5, DC-Leap achieves an average 8.29× speedup over standard diffusion decoding, with peak throughput of 171.35 TPS (13.04×) on MBPP and 51.72 TPS (14.95×) on MATH, while maintaining or improving accuracy (e.g., MBPP Pass@1: 42.60% vs. 37.40% baseline). On LLaDA-8B-Instruct, DC-Leap reaches 6.44× average speedup (90.72 TPS on HumanEval), and on Dream-v0-7B-Instruct it achieves 4.71× average speedup (116.21 TPS on HumanEval). For long-sequence (1024-token) generation, DC-Leap reaches 53.19× on MBPP and 105.02× when combined with dLLM-Cache (809.71 TPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DC-Leap consistently outperforms all three parallel decoding baselines (Fast-dLLM, LocalLeap, L2P) in throughput across all five benchmarks and all three models, while maintaining comparable or slightly better accuracy. On LLaDA-1.5, DC-Leap's 8.29× average speedup surpasses L2P (6.26×), LocalLeap (6.01×), and Fast-dLLM (4.72×); the gap is most dramatic on MBPP where DC-Leap reaches 13.04× vs. 7.34× (L2P) and 6.85× (LocalLeap). On LLaDA-8B-Instruct, DC-Leap achieves 6.44× average speedup vs. L2P's 5.73× and LocalLeap's 5.45×. Against the step-distilled dParallel model, DC-Leap adds 1.35–2.40× additional throughput (e.g., 290.10 TPS vs. 120.53 TPS on MBPP for dParallel-LLaDA-8B) with minimal accuracy loss (≤1.5%). DC-Leap is also orthogonal to KV-cache: combining with dLLM-Cache yields a compound 105.02× speedup (809.71 TPS) on 1024-token MBPP generation, far exceeding either method alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the parallel decoding line for diffusion LLMs (Fast-dLLM, LocalLeap, L2P/dParallel), which all rely on confidence-thresholding to accept multiple tokens per denoising step. It departs from these methods by identifying the Joint Probability Dependence Error (JPDE) as the root cause of their conservative thresholds, and replaces the global conditional independence assumption with a local, window-level contiguous verification that structurally eliminates JPDE without requiring strict thresholds. The draft-guided mechanism repurposes the speculative-decoding draft-verify paradigm for dLLMs, but inverts its role: rather than drafting to reduce forward passes, drafts here serve as look-ahead context to preserve bidirectional attention benefits that sequential verification would otherwise destroy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DC-Leap provides a training-free, plug-and-play acceleration module for dLLMs that is orthogonal to KV-cache optimizations, enabling compound speedups (up to 105×) without retraining. The key open question is whether the contiguous-verification + draft framework generalizes beyond the three masked-diffusion architectures tested, and whether the accuracy trade-offs (1–3 points on some benchmarks) remain acceptable as dLLM quality improves. The negative result on contiguous drafts (Appendix E) also raises the broader question of whether non-contiguous, scattered high-confidence tokens are an inherent property of diffusion denoising that future acceleration methods must account for.
