## Problem

Existing Open-Vocabulary Mobile Manipulation (OVMM) methods treat navigation and manipulation as separate stages with independent policies, producing navigation endpoints that are suboptimal for subsequent grasping or placement (e.g., blocked by obstacles) and manipulation-feasible poses that are globally inefficient across the full task. The paper addresses this by asking how to jointly plan navigation and manipulation across all four OVMM stages so that the full action sequence is both manipulation-feasible and path-efficient.

## Input / Output

The system consumes streaming RGB-D observations and camera poses to incrementally build a 3D feature map (0.05 m voxel resolution) with ViT patch features, Grounding DINO + Mobile SAM semantic labels, and confidence scores based on angular offset from the optical axis. Given a natural-language instruction of the form 'Move <object> from <start_receptacle> to <goal_receptacle>', the high-level VLM planner outputs an optimal interaction chain—a sequence of 3D waypoints (x, y, z, θ) each paired with a discrete action token (move, grasp, or place). Low-level policies then translate the next waypoint into either discrete base actions (Forward/Left/Right) via Fast Marching Method or continuous 6-DoF end-effector commands via off-the-shelf grasp/place modules.

## Method

3D-IC unifies multi-stage planning through three components: (1) a shared 3D feature map that subsumes both the 2D semantic map and egocentric view, enabling a single representation for navigation and manipulation; (2) stage-specific waypoint candidate sets linked into interaction chains under monotonic stage-progression and holding-dependent admissibility constraints; and (3) a hierarchical policy where a fine-tuned Qwen2.5-VL-7B scores candidate chains by jointly weighing stage progress, VLM-estimated waypoint feasibility (from ray-cast aggregated 3D features fed as visual tokens), and Euclidean transition cost. The VLM is fine-tuned on a 120K-sample three-stage curriculum—token interpretation, token preference learning, and trajectory selection—using in-context distillation from teacher models with simulator-executed feedback for supervision. At runtime, the robot executes the next waypoint in the selected chain and replans as new observations update the 3D feature map.

## Results

On the OVMM benchmark, 3D-IC achieves an average success rate of 52.7%, surpassing the best prior method (UniTeam, 50.4%) by 2.3 points and the OVMM RL baseline (48.4%) by 4.3 points. The method also attains the lowest average step count (844.1) among all compared approaches, indicating superior trajectory efficiency. On the Place stage (which serves as the overall task success rate), 3D-IC reaches 21.9% SR and 4.9 SPL, compared to 20.6% SR for MoTo and 14.8% SR / 1.4 SPL for OVMM (RL). Real-world experiments on a Stretch 3 robot confirm consistent gains, with 3D-IC improving average SR from 25.0% to 40.0% on cross-room tasks and from 33.0% to 44.0% on intra-room tasks over the heuristic baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

3D-IC outperforms all four compared baselines on every individual stage and on average success rate. Against the strongest prior method UniTeam (50.4% avg. SR), 3D-IC gains +2.3 points overall, with the largest margin on the Place stage (+3.9 points: 21.9% vs. 18.0%) and a 18% reduction in average steps (844.1 vs. 1027.7). Compared to MoTo (49.6%), which is the closest competitor on the Place stage (20.6%), 3D-IC still leads by 1.3 points while using 29% fewer steps (844.1 vs. 1195.1). Against MoManipVLA (49.4%), the gap widens to +3.3 points in average SR and a 32% step reduction (844.1 vs. 1240.5). Notably, 3D-IC is built upon the OVMM Heuristic baseline (34.8% avg. SR), yet surpasses the OVMM RL variant (48.4%) by 4.3 points, demonstrating that the unified chain-based planning framework provides substantial gains over both heuristic and RL-based decoupled policies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the OVMM (HomeRobot) task formulation and heuristic baseline from Yenamandra et al. (2023), which established the four-stage open-vocabulary mobile manipulation pipeline. It departs from the dominant modular paradigm—where navigation (conditioned on a 2D semantic map) and manipulation (conditioned on egocentric RGB-D) are handled by independent policies—by introducing a shared 3D feature map and a VLM-scored interaction chain that jointly plans across all stages. The 3D feature map draws on recent work in 3D-language feature fields (G3D-LF, DYNAM3D), while the VLM-based feasibility scoring extends the trend of using large vision-language models as planners in embodied tasks, applied here to multi-stage chain selection rather than single-step action prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that cross-stage joint planning yields measurable gains over stage-wise decomposition, particularly in the Place stage (21.9% vs. 18.0–20.6% for prior methods) and in long-horizon cross-room real-world tasks. However, the dominant failure mode remains placement instability (38.3% of all failures), suggesting that the VLM's feasibility scoring captures scene-level suitability but not fine-grained contact dynamics. Open questions include whether the 4.3-second VLM inference bottleneck limits real-time deployment, whether the approach generalizes beyond the constrained 'pick-and-place' OVMM formulation to more complex multi-object or deformable manipulation, and whether the heuristic local policies (FMM, CuRobo, AnyGrasp) can be replaced by learned controllers without sacrificing the chain-level planning gains.
