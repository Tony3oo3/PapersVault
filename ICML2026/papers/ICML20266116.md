## Problem

Existing unsupervised coreset selection methods for few-shot in-context learning suffer from skewed selections: geometry-based approaches oversample peripheral examples and high-density regions, leading to local redundancy, while static diversity-regularized methods lack a unified mechanism to dynamically calibrate the trade-off between diversity and representativeness as the selection set evolves. No prior framework simultaneously achieves model- and label-agnostic operation, budget-adaptivity, and process-aware representativeness.

## Input / Output

The method takes a fully unlabeled pool of n text samples (ranging from ~1k to ~392k), their SentenceBERT embeddings (768-d), and an annotation budget m (typically 18 or 100, with m << n). It outputs a query-independent global coreset of exactly m samples, which is then annotated and used to construct ICL prompts. For m=18, all selected examples form the prompt directly; for m=100, Top-18 examples are retrieved per test instance via similarity ranking. The selection is entirely model-free and requires no ground-truth labels or LLM inference during the coreset construction phase.

## Method

PaCS-ICL maximizes a monotone adaptive submodular objective f(S) = Div(S) + α·Rep(S) via greedy selection with a (1−1/e) approximation guarantee. The diversity term Div(S) is a normalized DPP log-det (bounded in [0, |S|] via a largest-eigenvalue denominator), while the representativeness term Rep(S) = Σ ρᵢ·γᵢ couples a local density score ρᵢ (fraction of pool within a data-adaptive radius r) with a process-aware exponential decay γᵢ = exp(−N_ψ(x)) that penalizes candidates near already-selected neighbors. The radius r is set to the median k-th nearest-neighbor distance with k = ⌈√(κ·n/m)⌉, linking neighborhood scale to the budget. The balance factor α is estimated adaptively as φ(m)·E[ΔDiv]/E[ΔRep], where φ(m) = exp(clip(⌈n/C⌉−m, −1, 1)/C) shifts priority toward representativeness under tight budgets and toward diversity under larger budgets, and the magnitude ratio is calibrated from a T=2m-step probe greedy pass. This design ensures that early selections favor high-density prototypical regions while later selections are steered toward underrepresented areas, producing a coreset with balanced coverage and minimal local redundancy.

## Results

On LLaMA3-8B with budget m=18, PaCS-ICL achieves an average accuracy/ROUGE-L of 64.7 across 7 NLP datasets, outperforming the strongest baseline InfoMax (61.5) by 3.2 points and IDEAL (61.1) by 3.6 points. Under the relaxed budget m=100, PaCS-ICL reaches 63.8 average, still ahead of InfoMax (62.7) by 1.1 points. On LLaMA2-7B, the advantage is even more pronounced: PaCS-ICL scores 57.7 (m=18) and 57.0 (m=100) versus 53.7 and 54.3 for InfoMax respectively. The method is particularly effective under tight budgets, where each selected example must be highly informative, and shows the largest per-dataset gains on semantically complex tasks like SICK (+7.8 over IDEAL at m=18) and MNLI (+2.1 over IDEAL at m=18).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PaCS-ICL consistently outperforms all three model-free baselines (RDSS, IDEAL, InfoMax) across all 7 datasets, both budgets, and both LLM backbones. Against InfoMax (the strongest baseline, based on mutual information maximization), PaCS-ICL gains 3.2 points on average at m=18 and 1.1 points at m=100 on LLaMA3-8B; the gap widens to 4.0 and 2.7 points on LLaMA2-7B. Against IDEAL (influence-driven diffusion-based selection), PaCS-ICL leads by 3.6 points at m=18, with the largest single-dataset margin on SICK (+7.0 at m=18, where IDEAL drops to 58.3 vs. PaCS-ICL's 75.3). Against RDSS (kernel-based diversity + distributional consistency), PaCS-ICL gains 3.3 points at m=18, again with SICK showing the most dramatic difference (+7.8). In the appendix, PaCS-ICL also outperforms the query-based GradeRE method (which uses LLM gradients and 500 labeled queries) on CQA, ARC, MRPC, and SST-5, despite using a far smaller labeling budget, suggesting that for tasks with balanced classes a well-constructed global coreset can rival query-adaptive selection.

## Relation to Prior Work

PaCS-ICL builds on two theoretical pillars: the DPP log-determinant formulation for diversity (Kulesza & Taskar, 2012) and the adaptive submodular optimization framework (Golovin & Krause, 2011), combining them into a single monotone adaptive submodular objective. It departs from prior model-free coreset selection methods (RDSS, InfoMax, IDEAL) that either optimize representativeness alone—leading to local redundancy—or impose a static, hand-tuned trade-off between diversity and representativeness. The key conceptual departure is making the balance *process-aware*: the redundancy decay factor γ_i depends on the evolving selected set, and the budget-adaptive coefficient φ(m) shifts priority from representativeness (tight budget) to diversity (loose budget) automatically, rather than requiring a fixed hyperparameter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, model-free coreset selection method with a (1−1/e) approximation guarantee that requires no iterative training, no labels, and no model internals—making it suitable for cold-start ICL scenarios where annotation budgets are extremely tight. A notable limitation is that PaCS-ICL constructs a query-independent global coreset, which may be less precise for extremely long-tailed or heterogeneous query distributions compared to query-adaptive methods; the authors acknowledge this and suggest pre-filtering or larger budgets as mitigations. Open questions include extending the framework to streaming/incremental unlabeled pools, adapting to query-specific needs without full gradient access, and understanding how embedding geometry quality (e.g., SentenceBERT vs. task-specific encoders) interacts with the selection objective.
