## Problem

Deep RL agents achieve strong performance but remain opaque, limiting trust and debuggability in high-stakes domains. Existing causal explanation approaches for RL, such as the Action Influence Model (AIM), require predefined causal structures, bind causal edges directly to individual actions (making them impractical for multi-unit environments like RTS games where edges become deterministic), and rely on conditional independence tests with O(n^(k+2)) worst-case complexity. CausalXRL addresses these gaps by learning causal dependencies among human-interpretable strategic representations in a fully data-driven manner with O(n²) structural learning.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes raw RL experience tuples (state, action, reward, next state) from trained agents, which are mapped via expert-specified deterministic functions into interpretable representations—e.g., counts of ally/enemy unit types in MicroRTS—collected across 200 replay episodes into a representation pool. The global causal graph outputs unweighted directed edges over all representations, while local causal models output a context-specific subgraph with each edge parameterized by a CATE weight, enabling counterfactual what-if reasoning and weighted action prediction from parent-node contributions.

## Method

CausalXRL is a three-stage post-hoc framework: (1) Representation Extraction maps raw experiences to interpretable representations via expert-specified functions; (2) Causal Dependency Inference fits BART models under the Rubin Causal Model for every ordered pair of representations, treating one as treatment and the other as outcome with remaining representations as covariates, then estimates CATEs from BART posterior samples; (3) Explainable Causal Models construct a global causal graph (edge included if the 2.5th or 97.5th percentile of CATE samples is non-zero) and local causal models that reuse trained BART models on a single experience to produce weighted, context-specific directed edges. The key efficiency gain comes from replacing CIT-based structure learning (O(n^(k+2))) with pairwise BART CATE estimation (O(n²)), and the key interpretability gain comes from operating over strategic representations rather than binding edges to individual actions, which preserves probabilistic structure in multi-agent settings.

## Results

On behavior prediction, CausalXRL achieves 86.0% accuracy and 85.9% F1 on MicroRTS-Lite (14 representations), outperforming the linear model baseline (75.3%/74.5%) and decision tree baseline (80.3%/82.2%). On the simpler CartPole environment (4 representations), CausalXRL scores 91.4% accuracy, slightly below the decision tree (94.1%) but above the linear model (88.5%). For structural learning efficiency, CausalXRL constructs the global causal graph in 0.1–0.5 s across all four environments, compared to 0.8–23.2 s for PC and 0.8–8.7 s for FCI, with the gap widening as the number of representations grows.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares CausalXRL against two CIT-based structural learning baselines—PC (Spirtes et al., 2001) and FCI (Spirtes et al., 1995)—on global causal graph construction time, and against linear model (LM) and decision tree (DT) baselines on behavior prediction accuracy and F1. On structural learning efficiency, CausalXRL dominates both CIT baselines by a wide margin: 0.5 s vs. 20.9 s (PC) and 7.4 s (FCI) on MicroRTS-Lite, and 0.3 s vs. 12.6 s (PC) and 5.1 s (FCI) on LunarLander, reflecting the O(n²) vs. O(n^{k+2}) complexity difference. On behavior prediction, CausalXRL wins on both accuracy and F1 in the two most complex environments (PongDuel: 90.2% vs. 88.3% DT; MicroRTS-Lite: 86.0% vs. 80.3% DT), but trails the decision tree on the simpler CartPole (91.4% vs. 94.1%) and LunarLander (88.7% vs. 85.1%—actually wins here). The paper notes that AIM-style methods are excluded as baselines because their explanatory target (action-to-state influence) differs fundamentally from CausalXRL's (representation-to-representation dependencies).

## Relation to Prior Work

CausalXRL extends the causal-explanation line of work in XRL initiated by Madumal et al.'s Action Influence Model (AIM) and its CIT-based extension by Yu et al. (2023), but departs from both by operating over expert-specified interpretable representations rather than binding causal edges directly to actions, making it applicable to multi-unit environments like RTS games. For causal effect estimation, it adopts the Rubin Causal Model (Rubin, 1974) with Bayesian Additive Regression Trees (Hill, 2011) as the inference engine, replacing the conditional independence tests used in prior structural learning with CATE-based edge detection at O(n²) complexity. The representation extraction stage draws on concept-bottleneck-model ideas (Koh et al., 2020) to translate raw RL experience into human-readable strategic features before causal discovery.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CausalXRL offers a practical, data-driven pipeline for generating structured, multilevel causal explanations of RL agent behavior in complex multi-agent settings, with a clear efficiency advantage over CIT-based structure learning that could enable scaling to dozens of interpretable features. However, the framework's reliance on expert-specified representations limits its applicability when suitable abstractions are unavailable, and scaling causal discovery and visualization to hundreds of variables remains an open challenge for industrial deployments. The expert study (n=8) is explicitly exploratory, and the behavior-prediction gains over baselines, while consistent, are modest in absolute terms. Future work on interactive alignment—letting experts edit causal graphs to steer agent behavior—could transform post-hoc explanations into a control mechanism, but this remains speculative.
