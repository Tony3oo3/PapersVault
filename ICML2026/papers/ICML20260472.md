## Problem

Including Huber ε-contamination (linear-vacuous) in a DRO ambiguity set makes the worst-case risk infinite in unbounded spaces with unbounded losses, rendering the objective vacuous unless strong boundedness or support assumptions are imposed. Existing contamination-based DRO either restricts the sample space to be bounded or relies on function-class restrictions, leaving a gap for the common setting of continuous, unbounded domains with standard loss functions. This paper asks how to make forward Huber contamination well-posed and computationally tractable without such restrictive assumptions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes i.i.d. training samples split into a fitting set (for the score function and centre distribution P_c) and a held-out selection set (for DKW bulk calibration), along with a loss function f_x, a decision variable x, and hyperparameters ε (contamination budget), γ (tail mass budget), and δ (confidence level). It produces a robust decision x̂ minimizing the closed-form worst-case risk (1−ε)E_{P_{c,Ξ₀}}[f_x] + ε sup_{ξ∈Ξ₀} f_x, the calibrated bulk set Ξ₀ with a finite-sample mass certificate Pr{P*(Ξ₀) ≥ 1−γ} ≥ 1−δ, and a high-probability risk bound that separates in-bulk robustness from a tail term controlled by a p-moment condition. The bulk set is constructed in O(m log m) time via a single sort of selection scores, and the downstream optimisation reduces to an LP or SOCP for standard losses and ellipsoid/box geometries.

## Method

The paper introduces bulk-calibrated credal ambiguity sets: a support-restricted linear-vacuous (LV) set A_{ε,Ξ₀}^{LV}(P_{c,Ξ₀}) = {(1−ε)P_{c,Ξ₀} + εR : R ∈ P(Ξ₀)} that coincides with Huber contamination on a data-calibrated bounded bulk set Ξ₀. The key insight is that restricting the adversarial mass to Ξ₀ yields a finite closed-form worst-case risk of mean + sup, while the residual tail Ξ₀ᶜ is controlled separately via a Hölder moment bound, giving a high-probability risk certificate (Theorem 3.4) with an effective in-bulk tolerance ε_eff that combines centre mismatch and in-bulk contamination. The bulk set Ξ₀ is calibrated from data using a DKW-based score selection: a scalar score (e.g., Mahalanobis or box distance) is fit on a training subset, and the smallest threshold clearing a target mass 1−γ is selected on a held-out set, yielding a finite-sample coverage guarantee. The paper further shows the set equals a forward LV divergence ball, connects it to reverse LV (CVaR) and TV balls via a similarity-decomposition argument, and provides closed-form sup values for linear, ReLU, absolute-value, and piecewise-linear losses under ellipsoid and box bulk geometries, enabling tractable LP/SOCP reformulations. The centre P_c can be a Bayesian posterior predictive, a frequentist plug-in, or the empirical distribution, and the framework extends to blockwise bulk sets for heterogeneous dimensions.

## Results

On the California Housing East→West deployment shift, LV achieves MAE 10.7, RMSE 12.2, p98 21.9, and CVaR₂% 23.9 (×10⁴), outperforming the strongest baseline (Wasserstein: MAE 12.3, CVaR₂% 26.5) by 13% on MAE and ~10% on tail metrics. On CivilComments, LV-Group attains the highest worst-group accuracy (0.516) versus GroupDRO (0.456) and χ²-DRO (0.247), while maintaining competitive mean accuracy (0.828 vs. ERM's 0.891). In the synthetic newsvendor, LV is the fastest method (1.3 s vs. 23.5 s for OR-WDRO) and achieves the lowest mean-standard-deviation criterion under 10–20% contamination.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the synthetic newsvendor, LV outperforms KL-BDRO (Shapiro et al., 2023) and KL-BAS_PP (Dellaporta et al., 2025) in mean-variance trade-off under 10–20% contamination, while KL-BDRO saturates at finite-scenario limits; LV also beats KL-Empirical (Hu & Hong, 2013) and is ~18× faster than OR-WDRO (Nietert et al., 2023) at 1.3 s vs. 23.5 s per solve. On California Housing, LV dominates both the CVaR and Wasserstein DRO baselines (Chen & Paschalidis, 2018) on all four metrics, reducing MAE by 13% and tail losses by ~10% relative to Wasserstein. On CivilComments, LV-Group surpasses GroupDRO (Sagawa et al., 2020) in worst-group accuracy (0.516 vs. 0.456) and mean accuracy (0.828 vs. 0.770), and substantially outperforms χ²-DRO (Namkoong & Duchi, 2016) on worst-group accuracy (0.516 vs. 0.247); ERM achieves the highest mean accuracy (0.891) but collapses on worst-group (0.022), highlighting LV-Group's fairness advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two communities that have largely developed in parallel: classical robust statistics (Huber's 1964 ε-contamination) and modern distributionally robust optimization (built around f-divergences and Wasserstein balls). It answers a call made by Dichi & Namkoong (2021) to clarify the connection between divergence-ball DRO and Huber robustness by showing that the Huber contamination set is exactly a forward linear-vacuous (LV) ball in the IP sense, and that its worst-case risk takes a closed-form mean+sup structure. The bulk-calibration step (DKW-based score selection) is inspired by conformal prediction but targets a different guarantee (high-probability bulk mass rather than marginal coverage), and the paper unifies forward LV, reverse LV (CVaR), and TV contamination neighbourhoods under a single similarity-decomposition argument.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper makes a classical but previously ill-posed construction (forward Huber contamination in unbounded spaces) into a practical, fast DRO method with LP/SOCP formulations, offering a computationally cheaper alternative to KL- and Wasserstein-based DRO that remains competitive under contamination. The IP–DRO equivalence (upper expectation = worst-case risk) provides a principled translation protocol between the two literatures, potentially unlocking further credal-set constructions for DRO. Key limitations include the DKW calibration requiring roughly 10³ samples for tight certificates, the non-identifiability of the true contamination level ε* from training data alone, and the current restriction to linear/piecewise-linear losses and simple bulk geometries. Open directions include online bulk-set calibration, discounted reverse-LV models, and extending the framework to nonlinear losses and deeper architectures.
