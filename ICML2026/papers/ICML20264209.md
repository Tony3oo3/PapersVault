## Problem

Evaluating LM agents currently relies on goal-centric benchmarks with manually designed objectives, which are labor-intensive and blind to open-ended capability growth. Classical empowerment estimators, which quantify an agent's control over future states via mutual information, cannot scale to text-based environments where observations are high-dimensional, linguistically variable, and semantically redundant. This paper addresses both gaps by proposing empowerment as a goal-agnostic capability metric and developing a scalable estimator for text-based LM agents.

## Input / Output

EELMA consumes multi-turn text trajectories as (observation, action, future observation) tuples, where observations are natural-language renderings of environment states (e.g., HTML accessibility trees in WebArena, grid coordinates in Gridworld) and actions are free-text or structured commands. It produces an effective empowerment estimate in bits (the average conditional mutual information between actions and future states), along with finer-grained state-conditional and state-action-conditional empowerment values. Crucially, the estimator never references task goals or rewards; goal-directed prompts are used only to elicit diverse behaviors during trajectory collection, not as inputs to the metric.

## Method

EELMA maps textual observations and actions into compact 32-dimensional embeddings via a frozen pretrained sentence encoder (E5) augmented with a LoRA adapter and a shared MLP projection, then estimates conditional mutual information using two neural encoders (φ for current observations and observation-action pairs, ψ for future observations) trained jointly with dual InfoNCE contrastive objectives. The empowerment estimate is computed as the difference between the obs-action InfoNCE score and the obs-only InfoNCE score, which algebraically cancels the marginal future-observation term and yields an approximation of the pointwise log-density ratio log P(o*|o_t, a_t) / P(o*|o_t). This design handles the two key challenges of text-based settings: observation redundancy (semantically equivalent descriptions are projected to similar embeddings rather than treated as distinct states) and high-dimensionality (the compact embedding space enables tractable estimation without enumerating the full observation space). Negative samples for InfoNCE are drawn from other trajectories, and the entire pipeline is trained end-to-end for approximately 10,000–20,000 steps on a single A100 GPU.

## Results

EELMA validates against ground-truth empowerment in controlled games (Gridworld, Tower of Hanoi) with Spearman R_s = 0.80–0.94, and remains robust to natural-language paraphrasing (RMSE 0.048/0.127 bits vs. 0.302/0.438 for direct estimation). In WebArena, estimated empowerment correlates with mean discounted reward at R_s = 0.83–0.94 across GitLab, Reddit, and Shopping Admin domains. In pooled comparisons against task-agnostic diversity baselines, empowerment achieves the strongest positive association with performance: R_s = 0.39 in WebArena and R_s = 0.71 in τ-bench, versus baselines ranging from R_s = −0.71 to 0.60. In the authentication case study, valid credential-typing actions yield 0.268 bits empowerment versus −0.094 bits for invalid attempts (p < 0.001).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares EELMA against two categories of baselines. First, against direct counting-based estimation (DE) in controlled games: under natural-language observation variability, DE's state-conditional RMSE degrades to 0.302 (Gridworld) and 0.438 (ToH) bits, while EELMA maintains 0.048 and 0.127 bits respectively—a 5–3× improvement. Second, against five task-agnostic diversity metrics (visitation entropy, action entropy, state coverage, trajectory length, unique-state ratio) as capability proxies: EELMA achieves the strongest positive Spearman correlation with discounted reward in both WebArena (R_s = 0.39 vs. best baseline 0.16) and τ-bench (R_s = 0.71 vs. best baseline 0.60 from unique-state ratio). Notably, in τ-bench the diversity baselines invert (visitation entropy R_s = −0.71), predicting less capable models are better, while empowerment correctly tracks model scale. The paper also identifies boundary conditions where empowerment fails: in WebArena Shopping (reasoning bottleneck) and τ-bench retail (floor-performance regime), the correlation flattens or inverts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the information-theoretic empowerment framework (Klyubin et al., 2005; Salge et al., 2014) from symbolic, noise-free environments to natural-language agent settings, where prior counting-based estimators fail due to linguistic variability and high-dimensional observation spaces. The estimation algorithm (EELMA) adapts the dual-encoder variational mutual information architecture from Myers et al. (2024) and the InfoNCE contrastive objective from Le-Khac et al. (2020), adding a LoRA-adapted pretrained text encoder and MLP projection to handle semantic equivalence of paraphrased observations. Conceptually, the paper positions empowerment as a goal-agnostic complement to the dominant goal-centric benchmark paradigm (WebArena, τ-bench, SWE-bench), rather than a replacement, and provides the theoretical grounding (uniform-reward lower bound) from Myers et al. (2024) to justify the correlation with task performance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EELMA offers a practical, goal-agnostic evaluation signal for LM agents that can be computed from trajectories alone without hand-labeled objectives, which is valuable for monitoring capability growth in open-ended or safety-critical deployments where exhaustive task suites are infeasible. The paper's honest delineation of failure modes—empowerment becomes uninformative when the bottleneck is reasoning rather than state reachability (Shopping domain), when agents exhibit task-irrelevant diversity (Qwen jailbreaking), or when all models operate near floor performance (retail τ-bench)—clarifies the metric's boundary conditions and prevents over-interpretation. Open questions include scaling to multimodal and longer-horizon environments, whether empowerment can serve as an intrinsic reward for training (not just evaluation), and how to handle the non-stationarity of real-world web environments where the state space itself evolves.
