## Problem

Concept erasure methods developed for U-Net (Stable Diffusion) and dual-stream (Flux) architectures consistently cause generation collapse when applied to single-stream diffusion transformers (e.g., Z-Image), where text and image tokens share projection weights within a unified self-attention mechanism. No effective erasure method existed for this emerging single-stream paradigm, which is becoming the blueprint for next-generation foundational T2I models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261910/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained single-stream diffusion transformer (Z-Image Turbo 6B or HunyuanImage-3.0), a set of erasure concept prompts (e.g., 'nude', 'Claude Monet'), and a set of preservation concept prompts (unrelated concepts to protect). Training runs for 1,000 AdamW steps with a 9-step flow-matching Euler sampler, producing a set of LoRA parameters (Δθ) that are applied exclusively to the textual hidden-state pathway via a token-wise gating matrix. The final output is θ′ = θ₀ + S_T(Δθ_M), a modified model where only text-side projections are altered while the visual backbone remains frozen.

## Method

Z-Erase introduces two complementary components. First, the Stream Disentangled Concept Erasure Framework applies a binary token-wise selection operator S_T = diag(0_{n_I}, I_{n_T}) to gate LoRA updates exclusively onto textual hidden states, creating a safe optimization subspace that prevents erasure gradients from corrupting the shared visual synthesis pathway. Second, the Lagrangian-Guided Adaptive Erasure Modulation algorithm formulates the erasure-preservation trade-off as a constrained optimization problem: it maximizes descent on the erasure loss (combining flow-matching negative-guidance trajectory intervention and attention-column suppression on localized tokens) subject to a preservation tolerance ε, solving for a dynamic Lagrange multiplier λ via an implicit first-order Taylor approximation of the dual gradient (avoiding an extra backward pass). The algorithm provably converges to a Pareto stationary point, with preservation degradation bounded by O(t·ε·α).

## Results

On the I2P dataset (4,703 prompts), Z-Erase achieves 161 total detected nudity instances (second-lowest, behind only UCE at 157) and the best violence erasure at 324 detected instances, while maintaining near-original utility (FID 26.46, CLIP 31.25 vs. Z-Image Turbo's 26.33/31.47). On celebrity erasure (CelebA), Z-Erase attains the highest balance score H_a = 4.82 with competitive FID (27.83) and CLIP (30.06). Under adversarial attacks (Ring-A-Bell, ReFlux, UnlearnDiffAtk), Z-Erase maintains attack success rates of 24.91%–42.81%, substantially outperforming token-zeroing (82.81%–92.85%) and comparable to EraseAnything.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Z-Erase outperforms all nine baselines on violence erasure (324 vs. next-best MACE at 382) and achieves the best overall balance between erasure efficacy and utility preservation. On nudity erasure, Z-Erase (161) is second only to UCE (157), but UCE suffers catastrophic quality degradation (FID 38.38, CLIP 21.66) versus Z-Erase's near-original scores (FID 26.46, CLIP 31.25). Among methods that preserve utility well, Z-Erase substantially outperforms EraseAnything (294 nudity, no violence results), MACE (199/382), and EAP (268/447). DiT-Localization and Minimalist CE show the weakest erasure (356/376 nudity) with poor utility (FID 32.65/27.23). Under adversarial attacks, Z-Erase (24.91% ASR on Ring-A-Bell) is comparable to EraseAnything (26.67%) but far more robust than token-zeroing (82.81%), confirming that the fine-tuning-based approach generalizes to prompt perturbuations where attention-column zeroing fails.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the concept erasure lineage (AC, ESD, UCE, MACE, EraseAnything) from U-Net and dual-stream architectures to the emerging single-stream diffusion transformer paradigm. The erasure loss directly adapts ESD's negative guidance mechanism to flow matching, while the Lagrangian-Guided Adaptive Erasure Modulation algorithm is derived from the constrained gradient projection framework of gradient surgery (Yu et al., 2020). The key departure is the Stream Disentangled Concept Erasure Framework, a structural intervention that freezes visual pathways and restricts LoRA updates to textual hidden states—something unnecessary in prior architectures where cross-attention layers provided natural modality separation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As single-stream DiTs (Z-Image, HunyuanImage-3.0) become the dominant T2I paradigm, this work identifies that their architectural unification fundamentally breaks all existing safety/erasure mechanisms, creating an urgent gap in responsible deployment. The Lagrangian formulation with Pareto convergence guarantees offers a principled, tunable trade-off between erasure strength and utility preservation, and the bounded degradation result (O(tεα)) provides a theoretical safety certificate. Open questions include whether the LoRA-based approach suffices for deeply entangled concepts, whether the ~25% residual attack success rate under adversarial prompts is acceptable in practice, and whether the framework generalizes to other single-stream architectures beyond the two tested.
