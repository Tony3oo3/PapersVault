## Problem

Training-free conditional generation with flow-based models can be cast as a deterministic optimal control problem, but solving the resulting trajectory optimisation requires differentiating through the full sampling dynamics or adjoint solves, leading to prohibitive memory and compute costs that prevent scaling to large architectures. Existing heuristic guidance methods avoid this cost but lack theoretical guarantees on measurement consistency and can be numerically unstable.

## Input / Output

The method takes a degraded measurement y = A(x) + ε (where A is a possibly non-linear forward operator), a pre-trained flow-matching model v_θ, and an initial state x_0 sampled from the base distribution, and produces a reconstructed image x that satisfies the measurement constraint while remaining close to the learned data manifold. For style transfer and colourisation tasks with FLUX.2 (32B, 4-bit quantised), the input additionally includes a reference style image or a luminance image, and the terminal loss is defined in latent space via the VAE decoder. All experiments run training-free at inference time; the FLUX.2 experiments fit within 24 GB VRAM on a single consumer GPU via staged model loading.

## Method

MPC-Flow decomposes the global trajectory optimisation into a sequence of finite-horizon optimal control sub-problems solved in a receding-horizon fashion: at each time step t, a local control u is optimised over a planning horizon [t, t+H] subject to the controlled dynamics ẋ = v_θ(x,t) + u, only the first Δt of the planned control is applied, and the problem is re-solved from the updated state. Two regimes are analysed: (i) Receding-Horizon Control (RHC) with H = 1−t, which provably recovers the global optimal control via Bellman's principle of optimality (Theorem 3.1), and (ii) Δt-horizon control with H = Δt, which is optimal when the terminal cost equals the true value function (Theorem 3.2) and uses a single-Euler-step surrogate V(t,x) ≈ Φ(x + (1−t)v_θ(x,t)) in practice. A key practical variant, single-step RHC (K=1), linearises the remaining flow path so that the gradient of the control objective does not require backpropagation through v_θ, dramatically reducing memory and enabling training-free guidance of 32B-parameter models like FLUX.2 on consumer hardware.

## Results

On CelebA image restoration (100 test images), MPC-Δt achieves PSNR/SSIM of 31.55/0.877 (denoising), 33.65/0.928 (deblurring), 31.89/0.911 (super-resolution), and 25.44/0.718 (non-linear deblurring), consistently ranking second to PnP-Flow on linear tasks while matching or exceeding it on super-resolution and non-linear deblurring. The single-step variant MPC-RHC(K=1) runs in 2.05 s/image (vs. 315.66 s for FlowGrad and 234.76 s for OC-Flow) but underperforms on tasks with large null spaces. On FLUX.2 (32B, 4-bit quantised, 24 GB VRAM), MPC-RHC(K=1) achieves PSNR 23.8 / SSIM 0.891 for image colouration, outperforming FlowChef (21.3 / 0.844) and the unconditional baseline (18.0 / 0.739), with only ~9 s additional runtime over unconditional generation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MPC-Δt consistently outperforms the global optimal-control baselines FlowGrad and OC-Flow across all six CelebA tasks by large margins (e.g., +5.5 dB PSNR on denoising vs. FlowGrad, +12.2 dB vs. OC-Flow on random inpainting), while also running 3.5×–7.5× faster. Against the strongest plug-and-play baseline PnP-Flow, MPC-Δt trails by ~0.9 dB PSNR on denoising and deblurring but wins on super-resolution (31.89 vs. 31.49 PSNR) and achieves the best SSIM on non-linear deblurring (0.718 vs. 0.643), where PnP-Flow degrades substantially. On the non-linear deblurring task, D-Flow attains the highest PSNR (26.41) but MPC-Δt delivers the best SSIM (0.718). The single-step MPC-RHC(K=1) is the fastest method (2.05 s/image) but suffers on tasks with large null spaces (super-resolution, inpainting) due to its inability to fill unobserved directions, a limitation the authors attribute to the gradient being orthogonal to the null space of A. On FLUX.2 (32B, 4-bit), MPC-RHC(K=1) outperforms FlowChef by 2.5 dB PSNR and 0.047 SSIM on image colouration, and exhibits a superior style–content Pareto frontier for style transfer, whereas FlowChef introduces pronounced artefacts at higher conditioning strengths.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies the well-established Model Predictive Control (MPC) framework from robotics and process control to conditional generation with flow-matching models, building directly on the optimal control formulations of FlowGrad (Liu et al., 2023) and OC-Flow (Wang et al., 2025) that recast trajectory steering as a deterministic optimal control problem. The key departure is decomposing the intractable global trajectory optimisation into a sequence of short-horizon sub-problems solved in a receding-horizon fashion, which eliminates the need for backpropagation through the full ODE trajectory or costly adjoint solves. The paper also positions itself against heuristic training-free guidance methods (DPS, FlowChef, PnP-Flow, D-Flow, FlowDPS) by providing formal optimality guarantees via Bellman's principle that these approaches lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in enabling training-free optimal-control guidance of 32B-parameter flow models (FLUX.2) on consumer hardware (24 GB GPU) via the K=1 single-step variant that avoids backpropagation through the generative network entirely. However, notable limitations remain: the K=1 variant degrades substantially on linear inverse problems with large null spaces (super-resolution, inpainting), the method depends on the initialisation x_0, and it requires a differentiable terminal objective. Open questions include whether more expressive control parameterisations (non-linear controls, LoRA-style weight adaptation) or learned value functions could close the gap with PnP-Flow on linear tasks, and whether mean-flow models can eliminate the Euler discretisation error that currently limits theoretical guarantees to the continuous-time formulation.
