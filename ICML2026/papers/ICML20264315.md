## Problem

Diffusion Language Models (DLMs) rely on bidirectional attention and arbitrary-order decoding, rendering standard KV caching incompatible and forcing full hidden-state recomputation at every decoding step. Existing DLM caching methods suffer from two key limitations: (i) update identification requires expensive high-dimensional similarity computations that offset the gains from sparse updates, and (ii) a uniform update budget across all layers ignores the significant layer-wise heterogeneity in hidden state drift, leading to either redundant computation in stable layers or accuracy loss in volatile ones.

## Input / Output

At each decoding step, the method consumes the current input hidden states H ∈ R^{N×d}, the cached KV pairs and FFN outputs from the previous step, and cached low-dimensional proxy identifiers P^c ∈ R^{N×r}. It outputs the updated layer output states (with only k = N·ρ tokens recomputed), the refreshed KV cache, the updated FFN output cache, and the new proxy identifiers for the updated positions. The proxy rank r (default 128 for d=4096) is the key hyperparameter controlling the identification subspace, and the per-layer update ratio ρ(l) is dynamically determined by a piecewise Gaussian schedule rather than a fixed constant.

## Method

SPA-Cache jointly optimizes update identification and budget allocation through two mechanisms. First, a singular proxy projects the Value matrix W ∈ R^{d×d} via truncated SVD to retain only the top-r right singular vectors, reducing the per-token identification cost from O(d) to O(r) while Theorem 3.4 guarantees the cosine similarity divergence is bounded by 2(λ_{r+1}/λ_r)². Theorems 3.1 and 3.2 provide the theoretical foundation showing that Value-state stability is a sufficient condition for bounding both attention output and FFN output drift, justifying Value states as the principled identifier. Second, an adaptive budget allocation scheme parameterizes the layer-wise update ratio ρ(l) with a piecewise Gaussian function (Equation 5), concentrating updates on high-variance middle layers (e.g., ρ_p=25% at layer l_p=24 for LLaDA-8B) while aggressively caching stable early and late layers (ρ_1=3%, ρ_L=13%), reducing the average update ratio from 25% to 16% without accuracy loss.

## Results

SPA-Cache achieves up to 8× throughput improvement over vanilla DLM decoding (46.12 vs. 5.75 TPS on MBPP with LLaDA-8B) while maintaining accuracy within 0.2% of the baseline. Across seven benchmarks and two models (LLaDA-8B, Dream-7B), SPA-Cache delivers 2–4× speedups over the strongest existing caching baselines (dLLM-Cache, Fast-dLLM). When combined with parallel decoding, the total throughput gain reaches 28× on BBH (693.96 vs. 24.85 TPS) with negligible accuracy loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPA-Cache consistently outperforms all five baselines in throughput while maintaining accuracy within ~0.5% of vanilla decoding. Against dLLM-Cache (Liu et al., 2025b), SPA-Cache achieves 2.8–5.5× higher TPS (e.g., 190.73 vs. 68.62 on GSM8K/LLaDA-8B) by replacing high-dimensional Value-state similarity checks with a low-rank singular proxy and adding adaptive per-layer budget allocation. Against Fast-dLLM (Wu et al., 2025b), SPA-Cache delivers 1.4–3.7× higher TPS (e.g., 124.06 vs. 81.25 on MMLU-Pro/LLaDA-8B) while matching or exceeding accuracy, since Fast-dLLM's block-wise semi-autoregressive decoding sacrifices global dependency modeling. On the code generation benchmark MBPP, SPA-Cache (46.12 TPS, 8.0×) substantially surpasses d2Cache (25.08 TPS, 4.4×), dKV-Cache (8.38 TPS, 1.5×), and ElasticCache (12.49 TPS, 2.2×), with the gap attributed to those methods' reliance on explicit attention weight computation that is incompatible with FlashAttention kernels. On Dream-7B, SPA-Cache also leads on MMLU-Pro (62.13 vs. 50.58 TPS over Fast-dLLM) and MBPP (114.85 vs. 58.42 TPS), confirming cross-architecture generalizability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends dLLM-Cache (Liu et al., 2025b), which first proposed monitoring Value-state drift to identify update-critical tokens in diffusion language models, by providing the first theoretical justification for why Value states are a principled identifier (Theorems 3.1–3.2) and by replacing the full-dimensional Value projection with a low-rank singular proxy (SVD truncation) that reduces identification overhead from O(d) to O(r). It also departs from the uniform update budget used by all prior DLM caching methods (dLLM-Cache, Fast-dLLM, dKV-Cache) by introducing a piecewise-Gaussian adaptive allocation that exploits the empirically observed layer-wise heterogeneity in hidden-state drift. The approach is positioned as orthogonal to window-based locality heuristics and block-wise semi-autoregressive decoding, and is compatible with parallel decoding frameworks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SPA-Cache addresses the primary practical barrier to DLM adoption—prohibitive inference latency—by delivering up to 8× throughput (28× combined with parallel decoding) while preserving generation quality, making DLMs a more viable alternative to autoregressive models for high-throughput applications. The training-free, kernel-compatible design (FlashAttention-friendly) lowers the barrier to integration. Key open questions remain: performance under high-temperature sampling, behavior on ultra-long sequences (>32k tokens) not yet supported by open-source DLMs, prefill latency (TTFT) which remains unaddressed, and deployment in distributed/tensor-parallel settings. The method's reliance on temporal smoothness of hidden-state transitions also limits its applicability to highly stochastic decoding regimes.
