## Problem

Standard speculative decoding rejects draft tokens that are semantically equivalent but lexically different from the target model's output, limiting speedup. Recent judge verification methods relax this by learning a token-level acceptor, but they depend on human annotations or verifiable ground truths (e.g., math answers, code correctness), restricting applicability to a narrow set of tasks and failing on general NLP domains like open-ended QA and summarization.

## Input / Output

During training-data generation, the method takes a target model response y, identifies positions where the draft model's top-1 prediction mismatches the target token, and computes a bidirectional semantic preservation score using N=20 future tokens as suffix context. At inference, the verifier receives the target model's hidden state at each draft token position (a single forward pass, no future tokens) and outputs a scalar score compared against a threshold θ to produce a binary accept/reject decision. The final output is the accepted draft token sequence appended to the generation, yielding accelerated text with near-identical task performance.

## Method

SelfJudge generates verifier training labels entirely from the target model's own likelihoods: for each mismatched token position, it computes a semantic preservation score s(y, z_i) = log P(z_i|y_<i) − log P(y_i|y_<i) + log P(y_>i|y_<i, z_i) − log P(y_>i|y_≤i), decomposing the bidirectional comparison into a prefix term and a suffix likelihood term that are both computable via autoregressive forward passes. Tokens whose substitution score exceeds a threshold τ (calibrated so that all AutoJudge-rejected tokens are also rejected) are labeled acceptable; the resulting (hidden-state, label) pairs train a lightweight logistic regression classifier. At inference, a two-stage verification runs in parallel: standard rejection sampling handles distribution-aligned tokens, while the judge verifier recovers semantically coherent tokens that alignment would conservatively reject, accepting a token if either stage approves. This self-supervised, answer-agnostic labeling makes the approach applicable to any NLP task without ground-truth constraints.

## Results

On Llama-3.1-8B/Llama-3.2-1B, SelfJudge-F achieves an average accepted length improvement of +2.17 tokens over standard SD across GSM8K, MATH-500, MMLU, and CNN/DailyMail, with only −1.6% average task performance degradation. In wall-clock throughput (vLLM, A100), SelfJudge-F delivers 23.5% speedup over SD on GSM8K and 46.3% on MMLU while maintaining comparable accuracy. On the larger Llama-3.1-70B/8B configuration, SelfJudge-F achieves 22.4% and 22.7% throughput improvements on GSM8K and MMLU respectively, with accuracy maintained or slightly improved.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to standard SD (Leviathan et al., 2023), SelfJudge-F increases average accepted length by +2.17 tokens (e.g., 9.14→11.29 on GSM8K, 4.36→6.38 on MMLU) while degrading task performance by only −1.6% on average, versus −10.7% for the naive Top-k relaxed verification. Against AutoJudge (Garipov et al., 2025), SelfJudge-F achieves higher accepted length (+2.17 vs. +1.70) with less task degradation (−1.6% vs. −2.3%), and critically generalizes across all four evaluated domains, whereas AutoJudge shows almost no speedup on MMLU (+0.11) and CNN/DM (+0.39). On LiveCodeBench, the gap is stark: SelfJudge-F actually improves Pass@1 from 8.6% to 10.0% (+1.4), while AutoJudge-F collapses it to 2.1% (−6.5). The paper attributes this to SelfJudge's semantic-preservation criterion being task-agnostic, whereas AutoJudge's answer-preservation training is restricted to math/coding ground truths and fails on open-ended tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

SelfJudge extends the judge decoding paradigm introduced by Bachmann et al. (2025), which relaxed strict token-alignment verification in speculative decoding by training a lightweight classifier on hidden states. The key departure is from AutoJudge (Garipov et al., 2025), which automates verifier training but is restricted to tasks with verifiable ground truth (math, code). SelfJudge replaces both human annotation and answer-preservation labeling with a self-supervised semantic preservation score computed from the target model's own bidirectional likelihoods, enabling verifier training across arbitrary NLP tasks including open-ended generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for ground-truth answers or human labels, SelfJudge makes judge verification applicable to the full spectrum of NLP tasks, and its 14× faster data generation (prefill-only vs. full generation) makes it practical for 70B-scale models where AutoJudge becomes infeasible. The finding that local semantic coherence is competitive with global answer correctness even on math/coding tasks suggests a more general principle for token acceptability. Open questions include whether more complex verifier architectures (beyond logistic regression) yield further gains, and how verifier performance scales with training data volume—neither explored here.
