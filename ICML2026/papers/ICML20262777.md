## Problem

Existing video-based 3D scene understanding models rely on SFT with per-token cross-entropy loss, which optimizes in discrete token space while evaluation occurs in continuous 3D coordinate space (e.g., 3D IoU, F1-Score), creating a fundamental misalignment between training objectives and task performance. Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) has proven effective for LLM reasoning but its applicability to 3D perception and spatial reasoning tasks remains largely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262777/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB video frames (4, 6, or 32 frames depending on the task) combined with a text query, augmented with VGGT-1B visual geometry features fused via element-wise addition into the Qwen2.5-VL-3B backbone. Outputs follow a structured format: a reasoning chain in  tags followed by a final answer in <answer> tags. For 3D detection, the answer is a JSON list of 9-DoF bounding boxes (x, y, z, w, h, d, ψ, θ, φ) unified to the first frame's coordinate system; for grounding, a frame index plus a 9-DoF box in that frame's local camera coordinates; for spatial reasoning, a free-text answer.

## Method

3D-RFT employs a two-stage pipeline: (1) SFT warm-up to inject 3D awareness and enforce the structured output format, establishing a stable initial policy; (2) RL fine-tuning via GRPO where the model samples G completions per prompt and receives scalar rewards computed by a deterministic verifier that parses the text output into geometric structures and evaluates them against ground truth using the actual task metrics. Task-specific verifiable rewards include Average IoU + F1-Score for detection, a linear-decay temporal frame reward + global 3D IoU (after extrinsic and axis-alignment coordinate transforms) for grounding, and exact-match or Mean Relative Accuracy for spatial reasoning. This design bypasses the non-differentiable parsing and step-function evaluation pipeline by treating metrics as black-box rewards in a policy gradient framework, with a KL-divergence penalty against a frozen reference model. A loss-chunking technique partitions the GRPO group into micro-batches to reduce peak GPU memory from O(B×G) to O(M_micro), enabling large group sizes with long video contexts.

## Results

On VSI-Bench (3D spatial reasoning), 3D-RFT-4B achieves 62.8 average accuracy, surpassing the previous best SFT model VLM-3R-7B (60.9) and the RL-based VST-RL-3B (57.7) by 1.9 and 5.1 points respectively. On ScanNetDetection (3D video detection, 4-frame setting), 3D-RFT-4B reaches P25=54.2, R25=38.2, F1_25=43.7, outperforming the larger VG LLM-8B (P25=43.4, F1_25=41.2) by +10.8 in precision and +2.5 in F1. On ScanRefer (3D visual grounding), 3D-RFT-4B achieves Acc@0.25=42.9 and Acc@0.5=15.9, exceeding VG LLM-8B (41.6 / 14.9) with half the parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

3D-RFT-4B outperforms all 22 compared baselines on VSI-Bench, with the closest competitor being VLM-3R-7B (60.9 avg, a 7B SFT model) by +1.9 points, and the strongest RL baseline VST-RL-3B (57.7) by +5.1 points. On 3D video detection (ScanNetDetection), 3D-RFT-4B surpasses the 2×-larger VG LLM-8B on F1-Score (43.7 vs 41.2) and Precision (54.2 vs 43.4) in the 4-frame setting, while also beating Qwen2.5-VL-3B/7B by over 20 points in P25. On 3D visual grounding (ScanRefer), 3D-RFT-4B (Acc@0.25=42.9) exceeds VG LLM-8B (41.6) and all prior video-based methods (SPAR 31.9, VG LLM-4B 36.4), though it trails point-cloud-based methods like Video-3D LLM (58.1) and ChatScene (55.5) that use explicit 3D scene input rather than video. Among RL-based spatial reasoning baselines, 3D-RFT-4B leads by a wide margin over vsGRPO-7B (40.7), SpaceR-7B (43.5), Spatial-MLLM-4B (48.4), ViLaSR-7B (45.4), and SpatialLadder-3B (45.7), confirming that task-specific verifiable rewards (3D IoU, F1, accuracy) provide substantially stronger learning signals than generic RL objectives. The only notable weakness is on the 'Approach Order' sub-task (67.8) where Cambrian-S-3B (78.8) and VST-SFT-3B (70.2) perform better, and on MMSI-Bench (29.9) where VST-7B (34.8) leads, which the authors attribute to limited training data diversity (300K vs 6M samples).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RLVR paradigm (established for math and code reasoning via GRPO in DeepSeekMath and GPT-o1) to video-based 3D scene understanding, building directly on VG LLM's SFT-trained video-3D MLLM architecture (Qwen2.5-VL + VGGT). The key conceptual departure is from the dominant SFT paradigm in 3D perception, where token-level cross-entropy loss serves as an indirect proxy for geometric metrics, to direct policy-gradient optimization against verifiable rewards (3D IoU, F1-Score, Accuracy). While concurrent work VST explores RLVR for spatial tasks, this paper provides a more systematic treatment spanning both 3D perception and spatial reasoning with task-specific reward designs and training dynamics analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that optimization objective alignment can overcome a 2× parameter gap (4B > 8B), suggesting that the SFT paradigm imposes a fundamental ceiling on 3D perception tasks that better training signals can break. Open questions include how to unify multi-task RL training with balanced rewards, whether CoT data can be effectively incorporated for perception tasks, and whether process-level rewards (beyond outcome metrics) are needed to ensure reasoning soundness in 3D scenes. The loss-chunking technique for handling long video contexts in GRPO is also a practical contribution for the broader RL-for-MLLMs community.
