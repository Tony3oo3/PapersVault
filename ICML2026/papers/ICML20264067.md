## Problem

Despite LLMs' growing role in scientific discovery, no existing benchmark evaluates whether they can genuinely integrate knowledge across disciplines for interdisciplinary research (IDR). The absence of a dedicated IDR benchmark makes it impossible to measure progress, compare approaches, or establish baselines for this fundamental capability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264067/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark operates on structured paper triplets [P_A; (P_B, P_C)] sourced from ArXiv, where only titles and abstracts are provided (closed-book setting relative to P_A). IPI takes a single paper's title+abstract and outputs a yes/no IDR verdict with discipline labels; I3 takes two papers from different disciplines and outputs a feasibility verdict, confidence score, a generated working-paper abstract, and integration reasoning; I2R takes a seed paper plus a candidate list of K=30 papers and produces a re-ranked list via pairwise Swiss tournament comparisons (10 rounds). All classification tasks maintain a 1:10 positive-to-negative ratio reflecting the documented sparsity of genuine IDR in the literature.

## Method

IDRBench introduces a three-task progressive evaluation suite (IPI → I3 → I2R) grounded in 335 human-annotated paper triplets from 271,348 ArXiv papers (Nov 2024–Oct 2025), with disciplines merged to increase conceptual distance (e.g., CS+EE+Systems Science combined). Positive triplets require dual-annotator agreement, and negative sampling is validated with a 5.8% false-negative rate. Ten LLMs (5 non-reasoning, 5 reasoning) are evaluated under zero-shot and few-shot prompting, with I2R using a Swiss tournament ranking protocol. A key empirical finding is that reasoning-oriented models systematically degrade IDR integration performance, and post-training alignment targets (usability vs. safety) predictably shift models toward optimistic (high TPR, low TNR) or pessimistic (low TPR, high TNR) verdicts. A LoRA fine-tuning experiment on llama-3.1-8B-instruct using augmented IDRBench data yields a 12.72% average Macro-F1 improvement on I3, demonstrating the dataset's utility for model training.

## Results

On the IPI task (Macro-F1), zero-shot performance ranges from 0.486 (gpt-5-nano) to 0.630 (o3-mini), with an average of 0.556 across 10 models; few-shot does not consistently help and can degrade performance (e.g., llama-3.1 drops from 0.551 to 0.336). On I3-Set 1 (feasibility), non-reasoning models substantially outperform reasoning models: llama-3.3-70B achieves 0.814 (zero-shot) and Qwen-2.5 reaches 0.860 (few-shot), while reasoning models score only 0.25–0.56. On I3-Set 2 (awareness of same-discipline negatives), all models struggle, with the best being claude-4 at 0.592 (few-shot) versus 0.555 (zero-shot). On I2R (MRR), all models converge to approximately 0.64–0.66, with Qwen-2.5 achieving the highest at 0.661 and no statistically significant differences among the top performers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IDRBench is the first benchmark to explicitly target interdisciplinary research (IDR), a dimension absent from all three prior benchmarks it compares against (Table 1). Compared to SchNovel (Lin et al., 2024; ~15,000 papers, paper-similarity scores) and IdeaBench (Guo et al., 2024; ~2,374 biomedical papers + reference lists), IDRBench uniquely provides structured paper triplets [P_A; (P_B, P_C)] with human-annotated integration logic and covers six disciplines from ArXiv. Against ResearchBench (Liu et al., 2025b; 588/798 papers), which also offers progressive evaluation, IDRBench provides a substantially larger dataset (1,014 positive / 31,437 total papers) and is the only one grounded in verified IDR paper triplets rather than general research-idea generation. In terms of task design, IDRBench's three progressive tasks (IPI → I3 → I2R) increase in complexity from binary classification to open-ended idea generation to multi-round recommendation, whereas SchNovel and IdeaBench offer single-task evaluation. The empirical findings further differentiate IDRBench: it reveals that reasoning-oriented LLMs systematically underperform non-reasoning counterparts on creative integration (I3 Set 1: 0.25–0.56 vs. 0.67–0.86 Macro-F1), a phenomenon not captured by any of the prior benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the citation-cluster insight from Zhu et al. (2015) — that a paper's core innovation stems from leading ideas within its citation network — into a structured paper-triplet format [P_A; (P_B, P_C)] specifically designed to capture interdisciplinary integration. For the I2R ranking task, it adopts the Swiss tournament pairwise-comparison protocol from Si et al. (2025), but repurposes it for interdisciplinary paper recommendation rather than general research-idea novelty. Conceptually, it departs from prior LLM-for-science benchmarks (SchNovel, IdeaBench, ResearchBench) by being the first to target interdisciplinary research as the evaluation axis, and from IDR-assistance frameworks (Disciplink, PersonaFlow) by shifting from helping humans find IDR topics to measuring whether LLMs themselves can perform the integration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

IDRBench provides the first systematic, closed-book evaluation of LLMs on interdisciplinary research, establishing baselines that future IDR-oriented frameworks can be measured against. The finding that reasoning-oriented models systematically underperform non-reasoning models on creative integration tasks (I3) has broader implications for how alignment and post-training objectives are designed for any creative or open-ended scientific task. Key limitations include the restriction to six ArXiv disciplines, a relatively small positive set (335 triplets), and the inherent subjectivity of judging interdisciplinary novelty; open questions include whether the reasoning-creativity tradeoff is fundamental or an artifact of current alignment strategies, and whether the benchmark generalizes beyond the ArXiv ecosystem to fields like medicine, law, or social sciences.
