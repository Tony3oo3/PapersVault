## Problem

State-of-the-art text embedding models increasingly rely on repurposing massive decoder-based LLMs, creating prohibitive training and inference costs while simultaneously narrowing linguistic coverage toward a few high-resource languages. Existing efficiency techniques like Matryoshka Representation Learning only reduce storage, leaving training and inference burdens untouched, and many top-performing multilingual models are closed-source or lack training transparency, hindering reproducible research.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265848/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts text queries and documents (including code in 40+ programming languages) as input and produces a dense embedding vector via the EOS token representation of a causal decoder. Uniquely, the output is multi-resolution: thanks to MRL, any prefix of the embedding vector is a valid lower-dimensional representation; thanks to MLL, the model can be truncated to fewer Transformer layers at inference by simply modifying a config parameter; and thanks to MEL, the embedding layer can be deployed in factorized low-rank form (two smaller matrices) for aggressive memory compression on-device.

## Method

The core contribution is the 3-Dimensional Matryoshka Learning (3D-ML) framework, which applies nested-structure principles simultaneously across three axes: parameters, depth, and representation size. Matryoshka Embedding Learning (MEL) factorizes the embedding matrix into two low-rank matrices (E_A, E_B) initialized via truncated SVD, with dynamic sub-rank sampling during training to force information concentration in leading dimensions—reducing both trainable and total parameters unlike LoRA-style methods. Matryoshka Layer Learning (MLL) applies the contrastive loss at logarithmically spaced intermediate layers, enabling depth truncation at inference without retraining. Matryoshka Representation Learning (MRL) applies loss on nested prefixes of the embedding for variable-dimension storage. The unified objective sums a contrastive loss (cosine similarity with hard negatives) over all selected layers and all MRL dimensions. Models are fine-tuned from Qwen3 causal LLMs (140M–8B) using a two-stage strategy on 50M samples spanning 282 languages, with task-specific instructions applied in stage 2.

## Results

ML-Embed-8B sets new state-of-the-art scores on 9 of 17 MTEB benchmarks, with the largest gains in historically underserved languages: Polish (73.84, +22.89 over previous best), Vietnamese (61.62, +6.88), Indic (76.76, +6.61), German (66.43, +6.47), and Japanese (77.81, +4.63). On the highly competitive English benchmark, ML-Embed-8B scores 73.26 (vs. top-1 of 75.97 and top-5 of 74.61), and on Multilingual it scores 66.79 (vs. top-1 of 72.32). The average score across all 17 benchmarks is 70.24 for the 8B model, exceeding the previous top-1 average of 68.46, with a clear scaling trend from 50.76 (140M) to 70.24 (8B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B, ML-Embed-0.6B wins on 10 of 17 benchmarks (Code 77.27 vs 75.42, Japanese 71.30 vs 67.28, Korean 68.74 vs 65.29, Polish 68.13 vs 67.42, French 66.64 vs 63.01, Persian 65.32 vs 62.88, Dutch 56.59 vs 54.27, Vietnamese 57.85 vs 56.01, German 61.58 vs 59.45, Scandinavian 62.00 vs 60.99) but trails on Multilingual (61.30 vs 64.34), English (70.01 vs 70.47), Chinese (62.64 vs 66.71), and Medical (57.58 vs 60.16), yielding a narrow average advantage of 64.69 vs 64.02. Against EmbeddingGemma-0.3B, ML-Embed-330M wins on 10 benchmarks including large margins on Vietnamese (54.17 vs 43.45), Japanese (66.00 vs 60.82), and Chinese (59.38 vs 50.40), but loses on Multilingual (58.06 vs 61.15), English (67.99 vs 69.67), and Persian (60.16 vs 67.11), with an average of 61.18 vs 59.55. In the data ablation (Figure 6), models trained on the proposed 50M-sample multilingual corpus outperform those trained on KaLM-Embedding's data on 9 of 17 benchmarks (including Multilingual, European, Scandinavian, English, French, German, Japanese, Persian, and Code), while KaLM-Embedding data produces a stronger Chinese model—consistent with its heavy English/Chinese skew—confirming that linguistic diversity in training data yields broader global competence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two existing matryoshka techniques—Matryoshka Representation Learning (MRL) for storage and Matryoshka Layer Learning (MLL) for inference depth—into a single framework and adds a third, novel axis: Matryoshka Embedding Learning (MEL), which factorizes the embedding layer into nested low-rank matrices via SVD initialization. Unlike LoRA-style parameter-efficient fine-tuning, MEL reduces both trainable and total parameters, improving inference efficiency as well. The work also departs from the trend of closed-source or opaque multilingual embedding models (Qwen3-Embedding, Gemini-Embedding) by releasing a 50M-sample, 282-language training corpus and full training code, positioning itself as a reproducible alternative to the current landscape.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3D-ML framework offers a practical, ecosystem-compatible recipe (Hugging Face, sentence-transformers) for building embedding models that are simultaneously parameter-efficient, depth-flexible, and storage-adaptive, which is directly relevant to on-device and resource-constrained deployment. The dramatic gains in historically underserved languages (Polish +22.89, Vietnamese +6.88) suggest that data curation driven by real-world language availability rather than benchmark coverage can close significant performance gaps. Open questions include whether the efficiency gains of MEL and MLL hold at the 8B+ scale, whether the nested-rank training objective generalizes to encoder architectures beyond Qwen3, and whether the multilingual data strategy can be extended to cover the long tail of truly low-resource languages with even fewer available samples.
