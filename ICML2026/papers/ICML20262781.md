## Problem

Existing training-free object removal methods (e.g., AttentiveEraser) apply binary, indiscriminate suppression of self-attention in masked regions to eliminate target objects. While this removes the object, it simultaneously disrupts the background reconstruction process, because the pretrained diffusion model relies on unmodulated self-attention to generate plausible background content in the vacated area. The paper addresses this fundamental conflict by asking: how can attention suppression be dynamically regulated so that it is strong when the object is still present and relaxes as the object is progressively removed?

## Input / Output

The method takes a source RGB image and a binary object mask (defining the region to erase) as input, and produces a single edited image where the masked object is removed and the background is plausibly reconstructed. It operates entirely in the latent space of a pretrained text-to-image diffusion model (SDXL by default, also SD1.5, SD2.1, FLUX) with an empty text prompt. At each denoising step, a foreground-background blending step composites the denoised target latent with the source latent outside the mask to preserve untouched regions, and the final output is decoded via the VAE-Decoder.

## Method

AdaEraser runs two parallel branches through the denoising UNet: a source branch that applies t steps of forward diffusion plus 1 step of denoising to the encoded source image (yielding reference self-attention maps at each layer), and a target branch that performs the actual iterative denoising from a noisy initialization. For every token within the mask, a presence score is computed as the cosine similarity between its token-wise self-attention map in the target branch and the corresponding map in the source branch at the same timestep and layer. This score is converted into a suppression coefficient η(i) = 1 − p(i) (and η = 1 outside the mask), which multiplicatively reweights the unnormalized attention logits before the softmax, effectively reducing attention to key tokens that still strongly resemble the original object. As denoising progresses and the object is removed, the presence score naturally decays, causing suppression to relax and allowing the model's native generative prior to reconstruct background content. The approach is entirely training-free, requires no additional parameters, and is theoretically justified as a monotonic semantic proxy and a KL-regularized attention reweighting.

## Results

On the Mulan dataset, AdaEraser achieves the best FID of 51.11, LPIPS of 0.2026, PSNR of 23.59, ReMOVE of 0.9065, CFD of 0.2002, and AHR of 7.08±0.34, outperforming all seven baselines across every metric. On OABench, it similarly leads with FID 38.47, LPIPS 0.1562, PSNR 23.50, ReMOVE 0.8316, CFD 0.2450, and AHR 6.81±0.21. Notably, AdaEraser is training-free yet surpasses the strongest training-based competitor RORem (FID 53.47, AHR 6.22 on Mulan) and the closest training-free method AttentiveEraser (FID 54.04, AHR 5.46 on Mulan). Inference time is 15.41 s with 9014 MiB memory, only ~10% slower than AttentiveEraser (13.98 s, 7966 MiB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaEraser outperforms all seven baselines on every metric across both Mulan and OABench. Against the closest training-free competitor AttentiveEraser, AdaEraser improves FID from 54.04 to 51.11 and AHR from 5.46 to 7.08 on Mulan, with the gap widening on OABench (FID 40.37→38.47, AHR 5.43→6.81), confirming that adaptive suppression resolves the background-quality degradation caused by AttentiveEraser's binary attention blocking. Against the strongest training-based method RORem, AdaEraser achieves lower FID (51.11 vs. 53.47 on Mulan; 38.47 vs. 39.22 on OABench) and higher AHR (7.08 vs. 6.22; 6.81 vs. 6.23) without any task-specific training. The remaining baselines—SDXL-INP, PowerPaint, Clipaway, SmartEraser, and OmniPaint—are substantially outperformed, with FID gaps of 11–84 points on Mulan, indicating that their removal or inpainting strategies leave significant artifacts or incomplete erasure. Efficiency-wise, AdaEraser adds only ~1.4 s and ~1048 MiB over AttentiveEraser, while OmniPaint requires 33,478 MiB (3.7× more memory) and 20.37 s inference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of training-free object removal methods that modulate self-attention in pretrained diffusion models, most directly building on AttentiveEraser (Sun et al., 2025), which blocks attention from image tokens to object-region tokens. The key departure is replacing indiscriminate full suppression with a token-wise adaptive mechanism: by comparing self-attention maps between a source reference branch and the target denoising branch via cosine similarity, AdaEraser estimates per-token object presence and scales suppression accordingly. This addresses a specific failure mode identified in prior work—over-suppression degrading background reconstruction—by allowing attention to gradually recover as the object is removed. The theoretical appendix further frames the suppression as a KL-regularized attention reweighting, connecting it to variational optimization principles.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AdaEraser demonstrates that a training-free method can match or exceed training-based object removal approaches (RORem, OmniPaint) across multiple metrics and architectures, suggesting that the generative prior in pretrained diffusion models is underutilized by current training-based pipelines. The adaptive suppression principle—monitoring semantic presence and modulating intervention strength accordingly—is a transferable idea applicable to other attention-modulation editing tasks. However, the method remains sensitive to incomplete masks (missing shadows, reflections), does not transfer well to few-step distilled models like SDXL-Lightning, and struggles with structurally complex or semantically ambiguous reconstruction, leaving open questions about robustness and compatibility with accelerated inference.
