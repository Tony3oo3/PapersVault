## Problem

Reasoning-capable LLMs are increasingly used as automated judges, but it remains unclear when explicit reasoning actually improves judgment accuracy versus when it wastes compute or degrades performance, and existing LLM routing methods that selectively invoke reasoning modes are trained on static distributions and degrade under the distribution shift inevitable at deployment. No prior work provides a principled routing framework that simultaneously handles the accuracy-cost trade-off and robustness to non-stationary query distributions, nor theoretical guarantees on router convergence.

## Input / Output

The router consumes a fixed-dimensional text embedding (from Qwen3-embedding-4B) of the concatenated prompt and two candidate responses, along with a scalar budget constraint C expressed as a cost ratio (e.g., C=4 means the average token usage of the selected mode should not exceed 4× the non-reasoning baseline). It outputs a binary routing decision—reasoning (1) or non-reasoning (0)—which determines which inference mode of a hybrid model (e.g., Qwen3) produces the final binary preference label. The embedding-based input means the router is a lightweight 4-layer MLP rather than a full LLM, and the budget is enforced as a hard constraint on expected token consumption rather than a soft penalty.

## Method

RACER formulates router learning as a constrained distributionally robust optimization problem: maximize the worst-case reward (minimum expected correctness over a KL-divergence uncertainty set around the empirical training distribution) subject to a worst-case cost constraint (maximum expected token usage over the same set). A key theoretical result (Theorem 3.1) shows the worst-case distributions admit a closed-form exponential reweighting—downweighting high-reward samples for the reward term and upweighting high-cost samples for the cost term—making the otherwise intractable minimax over distributions solvable via a practical primal-dual algorithm with entropy regularization. The policy update has a closed-form softmax solution analogous to DPO, and the paper proves (Theorem 4.2) that the router policy iterates converge to the unique optimal policy at a linear rate in KL divergence, the first such guarantee in the LLM routing literature. In practice, the router is a 4-layer MLP trained with AdamW, where the dual variable λ is updated via projected gradient ascent to enforce the budget, and the temperature τ controls the aggressiveness of the robust reweighting.

## Results

Under a fixed budget of C=4, RACER achieves the highest accuracy across all three Qwen3 model scales: 72.2% (1.7B), 85.8% (4B), and 90.0% (8B), while remaining within the cost budget. On the OOD benchmarks (JudgeBench, RewardBench, RewardBench-2), RACER matches or surpasses the All-Reasoning judge's accuracy at roughly half its cost, demonstrating that selective reasoning activation concentrates compute where it delivers the largest marginal benefit. The Random baseline, which activates reasoning at a fixed rate, traces a linear interpolation between All-Instruct and All-Reasoning, whereas RACER traces a concave, higher-accuracy frontier, confirming that instance-level selection outperforms indiscriminate activation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RACER outperforms all three advanced routing baselines on accuracy at every model scale while staying within the C=4 budget. At 1.7B, RACER (72.2%) beats the strongest baseline M-IRT (71.6%) by 0.64 points; at 4B, it leads M-IRT (84.3%) by 1.10 points; and at 8B, it surpasses M-IRT (88.9%) by 1.06 points. RouterBench-KNN achieves the lowest cost (2.5–2.6) but sacrifices 0.9–3.2 accuracy points, while RouteLLM-MF uses the most compute (3.8–4.1) yet still trails RACER in accuracy at every scale. The paper also reports that RACER generalizes to the Llama-3.1-8B / DeepSeek-R1-Distill-8B model family, consistently outperforming random routing at matched budgets and often surpassing all-reasoning accuracy at lower cost, confirming that the gains are not specific to the Qwen3 architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) distributionally robust optimization with KL-divergence uncertainty sets (Namkoong & Duchi, 2016; Duchi et al., 2021), (2) constrained policy optimization with entropy-regularized primal-dual updates and linear convergence (Ding et al., 2023; Wei et al., 2021), and (3) LLM routing for cost-performance trade-offs (RouteLLM, RouterBench, M-IRT). The key departure from prior routing work is twofold: existing methods route between distinct models or are heuristic, whereas RACER formulates binary mode-switching within a single hybrid reasoning model as a distributionally robust constrained policy optimization problem. The closed-form worst-case distribution under KL constraints borrows from robust MDP literature (Gadot et al., 2024), and the entropy-regularized policy update follows the DPO-style derivation (Rafailov et al., 2023), but the combination with a budget constraint and distributional robustness for the judge-routing setting is new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical finding that reasoning improves judgment on math/coding but degrades it on safety/knowledge tasks provides actionable guidance for deploying LLM judges under compute budgets, and the DRO formulation offers a principled way to handle the distribution shift that inevitably occurs between training and deployment. The first linear convergence guarantee for an LLM routing policy strengthens the theoretical foundation of the routing literature, which has been largely empirical. However, the method is limited to binary routing with a KL uncertainty set, which the authors acknowledge can become overly conservative under large shifts; extending to multi-model routing, richer uncertainty sets (e.g., Wasserstein), and online adaptation remain open. The modest empirical gains over strong baselines (0.6–1.1 pp) suggest the framework's value is more in robustness guarantees than raw accuracy.
