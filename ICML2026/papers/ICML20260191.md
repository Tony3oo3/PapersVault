## Problem

Conditioning score-based diffusion models to sample from posterior distributions in infinite-dimensional function spaces remains an open problem. While finite-dimensional conditioning via Doob's h-transform is well-established, extending it to function spaces is obstructed by the absence of Lebesgue densities, the Feldman-Hajek singularity theorem, and the lack of differentiability guarantees for transition operators. No principled method existed for fine-tuning a trained infinite-dimensional diffusion model to accurately sample from a posterior.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained unconditional score model s_θ(t,z), noisy observations y = G(f)+η from a known forward operator G, and a trace-class covariance operator C defining the diffusion. It outputs posterior function samples by integrating the conditioned reverse SDE dZ_t^y = [½Z_t^y + s^y(T-t,Z_t^y)]dt + √C dW_t. The guidance network u_φ takes the noisy state x_t, the observation y, and time t as inputs, and the training loss requires synthetic pairs (X_0, Y) generated via Y = G(X_0)+η, making it simulation-free. All experiments operate in L²(D,ℝ^d) with covariance operators C_ν f = Σ k^{-ν}⟨f,e_k⟩e_k.

## Method

The paper proves that under two settings (prior in the Cameron-Martin space, or prior absolutely continuous w.r.t. a Gaussian), the conditional score decomposes as s^y(t,x) = s(t,x) + C∇log h^y(t,x) via an infinite-dimensional Doob's h-transform, where h^y(t,x) = ξ^{-1}E[exp(-Φ(X_0,y))|X_t=x]. To learn the intractable guidance term C∇log h^y, the authors introduce Supervised Guidance Training (SGT), a simulation-free denoising score matching loss: E[||(X_t - e^{-t/2}X_0)/(1-e^{-t}) + s(t,X_t) + u(t,X_t,Y)||²_K], whose minimiser is provably the true guidance term. The guidance network u_φ employs a likelihood-informed inductive bias, decomposing as u_φ¹(t,x_t,y) + u_φ²(t)·∇_fΦ(f,y)|_{f=x̂_t(x_t)} where x̂_t is the Tweedie estimate, and is preconditioned by C^k (k∈{0,½,1}) to encode regularity. Both the unconditional score and guidance networks use time-modulated Fourier Neural Operators, with SGT requiring only a forward pass through the frozen score model during training.

## Results

On the sparse observations task, SGT with k=1 preconditioning achieves RMSE 0.373±0.210 and energy score 3.009±1.645, outperforming FunDPS (RMSE 0.456, ES 3.709) and the same-size conditional diffusion model (RMSE 0.406, ES 3.230), while approaching the full-size conditional diffusion upper bound (RMSE 0.347, ES 2.766). On the heat equation, SGT (k=0 or k=1/2) achieves RMSE 0.0443 and ES 0.345, matching or slightly beating the full-size conditional diffusion (RMSE 0.0450, ES 0.351) and clearly outperforming FunDPS (RMSE 0.0519, ES 0.403). On shape inpainting with 8 EFD coefficients, SGT achieves RMSE 0.42±0.04 and ES 0.212±0.035, beating both FunDPS (RMSE 0.64, ES 0.332) and conditional diffusion (RMSE 0.50, ES 0.265).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SGT consistently outperforms FunDPS (the infinite-dimensional Tweedie/DPS approximation) across all three experiments: on sparse observations SGT k=1 reduces RMSE from 0.456 to 0.373 (−18%) and ES from 3.709 to 3.009 (−19%); on the heat equation RMSE drops from 0.0519 to 0.0443 (−15%); and on shape inpainting (8 coefficients) RMSE drops from 0.64 to 0.42 (−34%). Against the full-size conditional diffusion model (Baldassari et al., 2023), SGT is slightly behind on sparse observations (RMSE 0.373 vs. 0.347) but matches or edges ahead on the heat equation (RMSE 0.0443 vs. 0.0450) and clearly wins on shape inpainting (RMSE 0.42 vs. 0.50). Crucially, when the conditional diffusion model is constrained to the same parameter count and training budget as SGT, SGT wins on sparse observations (RMSE 0.373 vs. 0.406) and heat equation (RMSE 0.0443 vs. 0.0483), demonstrating that explicitly learning the guidance term is more parameter-efficient than training a task-specific conditional model from scratch. SGT also shows robustness to imperfect base models: with a randomly initialised unconditional score, SGT still achieves RMSE 1.238 while FunDPS fails catastrophically (RMSE 558.25).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the finite-dimensional DEFT framework (Denker et al., 2024) for learning the h-transform guidance term to infinite-dimensional function spaces, building on the infinite-dimensional Doob's h-transform theory of Baker et al. (2024) and the infinite-dimensional SDM formulation of Pidstrigach et al. (2024). It departs from Baldassari et al. (2023), which trains a conditional score model from scratch, by instead fine-tuning a pre-trained unconditional model with a lightweight guidance network trained via a simulation-free denoising score matching loss. The paper also generalizes the FunDPS Tweedie approximation (Yao et al., 2025) from the restrictive Cameron-Martin setting to the broader absolute-continuity-with-respect-to-Gaussian setting, providing a unified theoretical treatment under two assumptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first principled function-space method for fine-tuning a trained diffusion model to sample from a posterior, offering a resolution-invariant framework that avoids the discretisation-then-train pipeline common in finite-dimensional approaches. The theoretical guarantees (existence of the conditional SDE, score decomposition into unconditional score plus guidance) under two general settings provide a foundation for future work on conditioning infinite-dimensional generative models. A key open question the authors identify is how approximation errors in the learned unconditional score propagate through the guidance term to affect posterior accuracy, which remains unaddressed. The framework's practical impact is currently limited to 1D function-space experiments, and scaling to higher-dimensional or image-level function spaces remains to be demonstrated.
