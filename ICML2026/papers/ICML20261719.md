## Problem

Transductive test-time adaptation methods for VLMs suffer severe performance degradation or collapse under realistic class imbalance, where only a subset of classes appears in each batch. The paper identifies two root causes: the absence of anchoring mechanisms that prevent overfitting to locally biased statistics, and the use of static shrinkage strength that ignores varying reliability across instances and classes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261719/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a batch of unlabeled test images and a fixed set of text prompts (one per candidate class, e.g., 'a photo of a [classname]'). Images are encoded by a pre-trained VLM's visual encoder and L2-normalized onto the unit hypersphere S^{d-1}; text prompts are encoded by the textual encoder to produce class anchor vectors. The output is a soft assignment vector z_i ∈ Δ_K per sample, representing posterior class probabilities over all K candidate classes, which can be thresholded for hard predictions. The method is training-free and model-agnostic, requiring only black-box access to the VLM's forward pass.

## Method

MOON models class-conditional feature distributions using a mixture of von Mises-Fisher (vMF) distributions on the unit hypersphere, which is the natural geometry for L2-normalized VLM embeddings. The core theoretical contribution shows that penalized likelihood estimation (PLE) with a KL-divergence anchor term yields a closed-form adaptive shrinkage: class statistics update as a convex combination β_k·v̂_k + (1−β_k)·μ'_k between empirical estimates and zero-shot text priors. The key novelty is making the shrinkage strength dynamic at two levels: instance-level entropy weights γ_i = 1 − H(z_i)/log K suppress unreliable predictions, and class-level confidence weights α_k = 1/λ_k (where λ_k is the geometric mean of average and max zero-shot confidence) prevent harmful updates from absent outlier classes. Optimization proceeds via BSUM-style block-coordinate descent with a single-pass linear approximation for assignments and closed-form vMF parameter updates, requiring no gradient backpropagation or task-specific hyperparameter tuning.

## Results

Under the most challenging batch adaptation setting (Very Low, 1–4 effective classes, batch size 64), MOON achieves 74.6% average Top-1 accuracy across 11 datasets, improving over zero-shot CLIP (65.2%) by 9.4 points and over the strongest baseline StatA (70.4%) by 4.2 points. On ImageNet specifically, MOON reaches 82.8% (+16.2 over CLIP), while transductive baselines like ZLaP (14.5%) and TransCLIP (21.6%) collapse to near-random performance. In the online adaptation setting (ξ=0.001, Separate), MOON outperforms StatA by 10.7 points on ImageNet (82.4% vs. 71.7%). MOON processes a 1,000-sample batch in 0.03 s, only 3.3% of CLIP's 8.99 s forward-pass latency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MOON consistently outperforms all 12 baselines under realistic class imbalance. Against the closest competitor StatA (Gaussian Mixture with static KL anchor), MOON gains +4.2 points average in the Very Low batch setting and +10.7 points on ImageNet in the most correlated online stream (ξ=0.001). Transductive methods without anchoring—ZLaP, GDA-CLIP, and TransCLIP—suffer catastrophic collapse under imbalance, dropping 26–38 points below CLIP (e.g., ZLaP falls to 14.5% on ImageNet vs. CLIP's 66.6%), while MOON maintains or exceeds CLIP on every dataset. ADAPT, the strongest non-anchored transductive method, still trails MOON by 13.9 points on average in the Very Low scenario. Among online TTA baselines (TENT, TDA, DMN, OGA), MOON achieves the highest accuracy across all four correlation strengths (ξ = 0.1, 0.01, 0.001, Separate), with the largest margins under strong temporal correlation. MTA and EM-Dirichlet, which do not explicitly model class sparsity, show modest gains over CLIP but are outperformed by MOON by 6–8 points on average.

## Relation to Prior Work

MOON extends the KL-anchored penalized likelihood estimation (PLE) framework introduced by StatA (Zanella et al., 2025) for transductive VLM adaptation under class imbalance, and inherits the BSUM-style iterative optimization from TransCLIP (Zanella et al., 2024). The key conceptual departure is replacing StatA's static scalar anchor weight α with a fully data-driven, two-level dynamic shrinkage: instance-level entropy weighting (γ) and class-level confidence-based weighting (α_k), grounded in a theoretical proof that KL-anchored PLE over any exponential family yields closed-form convex-combination updates. Additionally, MOON swaps the Gaussian mixture model for a von Mises-Fisher mixture, which is topologically native to the unit hypersphere where normalized VLM embeddings reside, though the paper itself shows this swap alone is insufficient without the dynamic shrinkage mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a critical practical gap: most test-time adaptation benchmarks assume balanced class marginals, but real deployments (data streams, mini-batches with few effective classes) are inherently imbalanced, causing existing transductive methods to degrade or collapse. MOON's training-free, model-agnostic, hyperparameter-free design with 3.3% of CLIP's inference latency makes it immediately deployable, and its validation across 11 datasets and 7 VLM backbones (CLIP variants, OpenCLIP, SigLIP, EVA-CLIP) demonstrates broad applicability. Open questions include whether the isotropic vMF concentration parameter can be extended to capture the anisotropic geometry of VLM representations (the paper acknowledges this limitation), whether the approach transfers to non-classification VLM tasks (VQA, retrieval), and how the method behaves under extreme domain shift rather than mere class sparsity.
