## Problem

Existing single-image 3D scene generation methods either produce the entire scene in one feed-forward pass, inherently capping spatial resolution and detail fidelity, or decompose into independent single-object generations without accounting for the varying levels of refinement each component requires. There is no framework that progressively enhances geometric resolution of individual scene components in a coarse-to-fine manner while preserving the globally coherent layout established by the initial generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265695/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB scene image as input and produces a high-resolution 3D scene in 3D Gaussian Splatting format. Internally, the pipeline operates within TRELLIS's 64³ canonical voxel space, and the hierarchical refinement progressively upsamples individual components (e.g., a small object occupying 4³ voxels in the coarse scene is re-represented at 16³ then 64³). The voxel super-resolution model consumes a 518×518 cropped image (encoded by DINOv2) and a coarse voxel grid (encoded by TRELLIS's sparse structure VAE into a 16×16×16×8 latent reshaped to 16 tokens of dimension 2048) as dual conditions.

## Method

HIVE-3D constructs a hierarchical scene tree by performing 2D instance segmentation (Florence-2 + SAM 2) on the input image and lifting the resulting masks into 3D voxel space via cross-attention maps extracted from TRELLIS's structure latent flow transformer G_L. The core technical contribution is a voxel super-resolution model built as an IP-Adapter-style module on TRELLIS's sparse structure flow transformer G_S: the base model is frozen, and a trainable projection layer plus dedicated cross-attention layers are added to each DiT block to inject the coarse voxel condition alongside the original image condition, trained with a conditional flow matching objective. The pipeline then performs top-down recursive refinement, where each parent node's coarse voxels condition the generation of finer child components, followed by shape-based scale alignment (mean surface-to-centroid distance) and RANSAC-based point cloud registration to reassemble each refined component into the global scene.

## Results

On the 3D-FRONT dataset, HIVE-3D achieves the best Chamfer Distance (CD = 0.0035) and F-Score (84.34) among all compared methods, outperforming TRELLIS (CD = 0.0038, F-Score = 81.29) and significantly surpassing compositional baselines like Gen3DSR (CD = 0.429, F-Score = 5.096). However, TRELLIS retains a higher IoU (0.8603 vs. 0.7449), suggesting HIVE-3D's hierarchical refinement introduces some geometric overlap. On visual quality metrics (Table 6), HIVE-3D achieves the best PSNR (13.39) and CLIP score (0.97), with a runtime of 36.5 s per scene—substantially faster than Gen3DSR (9 min) but slower than TRELLIS (6.3 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HIVE-3D is compared against four quantitative baselines (TRELLIS, MIDI, SceneGen, Gen3DSR) and two qualitative-only baselines (PartPacker, SAM3D). Against TRELLIS (its own coarse-scene backbone), HIVE-3D improves CD from 0.0038 to 0.0035 and F-Score from 81.29 to 84.34, but trades off IoU (0.8603 → 0.7449), likely due to the registration of independently refined components. Against the compositional methods MIDI, SceneGen, and Gen3DSR, HIVE-3D dominates on all three geometry metrics by large margins (e.g., F-Score 84.34 vs. 19.83 for MIDI and 5.096 for Gen3DSR). On perceptual quality (Table 6), HIVE-3D achieves the highest PSNR (13.39) and CLIP score (0.97), though MIDI attains a lower LPIPS (0.25 vs. 0.33). Qualitatively, HIVE-3D outperforms PartPacker (which produces untextured meshes with structural deficiencies) and SAM3D (which suffers from low-resolution holistic generation and object fusion artifacts), producing scenes with both geometric precision and high-fidelity textures.

## Relation to Prior Work

HIVE-3D builds directly on TRELLIS as its 3D generation backbone, extending it with a hierarchical coarse-to-fine refinement pipeline that prior scene generation methods (Gen3DSR, MIDI, SceneGen) lack. The voxel super-resolution module adapts the IP-Adapter paradigm—originally designed for image-conditioned diffusion—to inject coarse voxel geometry as an additional conditioning signal into a frozen flow transformer, preserving structural consistency across resolution levels. The 2D-to-3D scene tree construction repurposes Fuse3D's observation that TRELLIS's cross-attention layers encode image-to-voxel correspondences, enabling instance-level 3D segmentation via off-the-shelf 2D models (Florence-2 + SAM 2) rather than requiring dedicated 3D segmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a core bottleneck in single-image 3D scene generation: the fixed resolution of feed-forward models limits geometric detail. The hierarchical approach is modular and practical, and the voxel super-resolution model could be adapted to other TRELLIS-based pipelines. However, the method remains bounded by TRELLIS's 64³ canonical space, struggles with large-scale scenes, and depends on 2D segmentation quality. Notably, the IoU metric actually drops (0.74 vs. 0.86 for TRELLIS) despite improved F-Score and CD, suggesting the refined geometry extends beyond coarse boundaries in ways that reduce volumetric overlap—a trade-off the paper under-discusses. Open questions include whether the approach scales to multi-view or video inputs and whether the recursive depth can be increased beyond 3 levels without compounding registration errors.
