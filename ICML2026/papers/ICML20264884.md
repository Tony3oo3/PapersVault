## Problem

Applying DP-SGD directly to LoRA's factor parameters (A, B) is fundamentally misaligned with LoRA's non-identifiable low-rank structure: the gauge symmetry (A,B)~(AR, BR^{-T}) means factor-space clipping and noising produce gauge-dependent, potentially unbounded intrinsic noise on the effective update Z=AB^T, along with spurious bilinear second-order noise terms. No prior DP-LoRA method enforces DP in a gauge-invariant manner on the intrinsic update space.

## Input / Output

PRISM takes a frozen pretrained backbone (e.g., Gemma-3-4B, Gemma-2-9B, Gemma-3-12B), a private training dataset, and LoRA factor pairs (A_ℓ, B_ℓ) of rank r≪min(m,n) for each of L LoRA modules. It produces updated LoRA factors after each DP-SGD step, where the intrinsic update Z_ℓ=A_ℓB_ℓ^T is perturbed by an isotropic tangent-space Gaussian whose energy is the closed-form scalar (σC/b)√(r(m_ℓ+n_ℓ−r)), independent of the factor gauge. A single global clipping threshold C is applied across all modules per example, and the final update is retracted back to the fixed-rank manifold via truncated SVD.

## Method

PRISM (Projected Riemannian Invariant Subspace Mechanism) performs DP-SGD on the tangent space T_Z M_r of the fixed-rank manifold rather than in factor coordinates. For each example, it computes a gauge-invariant tangent projection P_{A,B}(G) of the intrinsic gradient, lifts it to a canonical horizontal factor representation, applies global Frobenius-norm clipping across all LoRA modules, and injects isotropic tangent Gaussian noise sampled efficiently via an O((m+n)r²) low-dimensional factor sampler. A DP-aware adaptive update rule floors the rank-space preconditioners at a level proportional to the known DP noise variance, bounding noise amplification under inverse-square-root preconditioning. The noisy tangent direction is retracted back to rank r via truncated SVD, which is first-order exact and avoids the bilinear η²ξ_Aξ_B^T term inherent to independently noising both factors. The full procedure satisfies (ε,δ)-DP under Poisson subsampled Gaussian accounting with standard PRV composition.

## Results

Under DP (ε=6, δ=10⁻⁵), PRISM achieves the best average accuracy of 0.690 across 12 tasks (GLUE8 + Math-10K), outperforming LoRA+ (0.674), AdamW (0.641), LAMB (0.621), FFA (0.589), and RITE (0.490). At the tighter budget ε=3, PRISM again leads with 0.680 average, beating LoRA+ (0.665) and AdamW (0.634). In the non-DP setting, PRISM (0.737) trails RITE (0.782) and LoRA+ (0.769), consistent with its design focus on DP-specific issues rather than non-private optimization. PRISM wins 8/12 individual tasks at ε=6 and 7/12 at ε=3, with particularly strong gains on multi-step reasoning tasks (GSM8K, MAWPS, SVAMP).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Under DP (ε=6), PRISM (0.690) outperforms all five baselines: LoRA+ (0.674, +1.6 pts), AdamW (0.641, +4.9 pts), LAMB (0.621, +6.9 pts), FFA/one-sided (0.589, +10.1 pts), and RITE (0.490, +20.0 pts). The gap is most pronounced against RITE and FFA, which suffer from gauge-dependent noise amplification and factor-space clipping issues that PRISM's tangent-space mechanism eliminates. At ε=3, PRISM maintains its lead (0.680 vs. LoRA+ 0.665, AdamW 0.634), with the advantage over LoRA+ narrowing slightly. In the non-DP setting, PRISM (0.737) is competitive but trails RITE (0.782) and LoRA+ (0.769), which is expected since PRISM's geometric overhead (tangent projection, retraction) provides no benefit without DP noise. The paper notes PRISM is not best on every individual task (e.g., SST-2, RTE, AQuA), reflecting heterogeneous convergence rates across the shared-hyperparameter task suite.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the intersection of LoRA (Hu et al., 2022) and DP-SGD (Abadi et al., 2016), but departs fundamentally from prior DP-LoRA work (Yu et al., 2022; Liu et al., 2025; Xu et al., 2025; Sun et al., 2024) that applies the Gaussian mechanism directly to the factor parameters (A, B). The key conceptual departure is recognizing that LoRA's gauge symmetry (A,B) ~ (AR, BR^{-T}) makes factor-space DP ill-defined: clipping and noise injection depend on the arbitrary factorization, leading to unbounded intrinsic noise amplification. PRISM instead formulates DP on the intrinsic rank-r manifold via tangent-space projection and retraction, drawing on Riemannian optimization on fixed-rank matrices (Mishra et al., 2014) and DP-aware adaptive optimization (Li et al., 2022, 2023; Tang et al., 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification that naive factor-space DP-LoRA induces gauge-dependent, potentially unbounded intrinsic noise is a clean theoretical result that invalidates a natural design choice and provides a principled alternative. Practically, PRISM achieves the best average DP utility across multiple backbones (Gemma-2-9B, Gemma-3-4B, Gemma-3-12B) and LoRA ranks, though with roughly 2× runtime overhead. Open questions include extending the intrinsic-geometry approach to other PEFT methods (adapters, prefix tuning), reducing the computational overhead of tangent-space operations, and whether the DP-aware preconditioner floors generalize beyond the isotropic tangent noise assumption.
