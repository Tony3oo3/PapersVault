## Problem

Fine-tuning pre-trained models improves in-distribution accuracy but degrades out-of-distribution robustness due to representation collapse. Multi-checkpoint ensembling methods like Model Soups mitigate this by averaging weights, but require training and storing dozens of fine-tuned models, which is computationally prohibitive for large-scale deployment. The paper asks whether the benefits of model soups can be retained when only a single fine-tuned checkpoint is available.

## Input / Output

The method requires only two weight tensors: the pre-trained initialization W₀ and a single fine-tuned checkpoint W_FT. No training data, validation set, or hyperparameter tuning is needed at inference time. The output is a single edited checkpoint where each layer's weight update has been spectrally reweighted, preserving the original model architecture and parameter count. The entire procedure is a one-shot, layer-wise SVD-based edit that completes in seconds to minutes depending on model size (e.g., ~3 s for CLIP ViT-B/32, ~83 s for Qwen3-14B).

## Method

MonoSoup decomposes each layer's weight update ΔW = W_FT − W₀ via SVD into a high-energy principal subspace (task-specific adaptation) and a low-energy orthogonal complement (residual directions). The partition index k is set automatically by the entropy-based effective rank of the singular value spectrum, eliminating any manual threshold. Rather than discarding the low-energy component (which prior work shows is harmful in large-scale fine-tuning with natural distribution shifts), MonoSoup reweights both components using two per-layer signals: a spectral decay ratio ρ (measuring how concentrated the update is in leading singular values) and a fractional energy term cos α (measuring how much update mass resides in the low-energy subspace). The mixing coefficient follows the bilinear rule λ_Low = ρ + (1−ρ)cos α, which satisfies four natural boundary conditions (suppression under sharp/misaligned spectra, retention under flat/aligned spectra, and appropriate fallbacks when one signal vanishes). This yields a data-free, hyperparameter-free, architecture-agnostic post-hoc edit that restores OOD robustness while preserving ID accuracy, matching or exceeding multi-checkpoint baselines like Model Soups and Model Stock using only one fine-tuned model.

## Results

On CLIP ViT-B/32 fine-tuned on ImageNet, MonoSoup (R=0.8) improves average OOD accuracy from 50.67% to 51.60% on the best-OOD checkpoint while maintaining 78.29% ID accuracy, surpassing Greedy Soup (50.7% OOD) without aggregating 70 models. On the worst-OOD checkpoint, MonoSoup recovers collapsed representations, boosting OOD from 36.71% to 44.21% (+7.5 pp) and ID from 76.53% to 78.55%. On Qwen3-0.6B mathematical reasoning benchmarks, MonoSoup consistently outperforms LiNeS across all five tasks (e.g., GSM8K: 56.9 vs. 56.2; GSMPlus: 30.2 vs. 30.1) and matches or exceeds ModelStock while requiring only a single checkpoint. The method is data-free, hyperparameter-free, and completes in 2–83 seconds depending on model size (88M to 14B parameters).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Model Soups (Uniform and Greedy), MonoSoup matches or exceeds their OOD performance (51.60% vs. 51.40% and 50.70%) while requiring only 1 checkpoint instead of 70, reducing wall-clock cost by ~2,900×. Against ModelStock (which requires 2 aligned checkpoints), MonoSoup dominates on both ID and OOD in the (ID+, OOD+) pair (80.10%/51.37% vs. 79.39%/50.53%) and in the (OOD+, OOD−) pair (78.44%/49.26% vs. 76.76%/48.41%), while using only 1 checkpoint. Against Wise-FT, MonoSoup provides a strictly stronger interpolation endpoint: when combined, the resulting Pareto fronts consistently dominate those of Wise-FT alone across all five OOD shifts. Against LiNeS, MonoSoup outperforms on every Qwen3-0.6B benchmark (e.g., GSM8K 56.9 vs. 56.2, MMLU-Pro 37.5 vs. 36.7) and on CLIP (e.g., worst-OOD: 44.21% vs. 41.33% OOD), while being fully data-free and layer-wise anisotropic rather than using a single per-block scalar.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of weight-space ensembling (Model Soups, Model Stock) and single-checkpoint robustness methods (Wise-FT, LiNeS), but departs from both by applying SVD to each layer's fine-tuning update and reweighting high- and low-energy spectral components with automatically determined, layer-specific coefficients. It repurposes the SVD truncation idea from task-arithmetic merging (Gargiulo et al., 2025; Stoica et al., 2025) in the opposite direction: rather than discarding low-energy singular vectors as noise, MonoSoup retains and re-emphasizes them based on the finding that, under large-scale fine-tuning, these directions encode OOD-critical information. The geometric alignment analysis (cosine similarity of task vectors predicts merging success) provides a unifying lens connecting Model Stock's pairwise rule to the single-model spectral partition, and the entropy-based effective rank (Roy & Vetterli, 2007) is borrowed from information theory to eliminate the manual spectral threshold.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MonoSoup offers a practical, plug-and-play post-hoc edit that reduces the compute and storage cost of robust deployment by roughly 10^3–10^4× relative to multi-checkpoint soups, making OOD-robust fine-tuning accessible to practitioners who only have a single best checkpoint. The finding that low-energy singular directions are essential for OOD generalization at ImageNet scale (contradicting the low-rank-suffices assumption from task-arithmetic benchmarks) opens a question about how spectral structure of fine-tuning updates changes with task complexity and scale. Limitations include evaluation only on natural distribution shifts (not adversarial perturbations), a less rigid ID/OOD split in the language experiments, and modest absolute gains (~1%) on already-strong checkpoints, with the largest benefits reserved for representation-collapsed models.
