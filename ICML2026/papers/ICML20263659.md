## Problem

In Gaussian single-index models, prior work has studied learning the direction w* or the link function φ* in isolation, but the joint gradient flow dynamics when both are learned simultaneously remain poorly understood. A key open question is whether the weak initial alignment (order 1/√d in high dimensions) suffices for recovery, and whether joint learning can overcome the symmetry traps that cause the planted model (fixed function) to fail when the initial correlation is negative.

## Input / Output

The method consumes i.i.d. pairs (X, Y) with X ~ N(0, I_d) and Y = φ*(⟨w*, X⟩) + ε, where the link function φ* lives in the infinite-dimensional Hilbert space L²_γ(R) under the standard Gaussian measure. The output is a predictor f(x) = φ(⟨w, x⟩) with w on the unit sphere S^{d-1} and φ represented by its Hermite coefficients. In practice, the infinite-dimensional function space is replaced by a finite-dimensional RKHS truncation at level k, making the problem a coupled ODE on (k+1) scalar coefficients plus one alignment parameter m = ⟨w, w*⟩.

## Method

The paper derives the continuous-time gradient flow on the product space S^{d-1} × L²_γ(R) and shows, via the Hermite polynomial basis, that the dynamics reduce to a coupled system of ODEs: each Hermite coefficient a_{k,t} follows a linear relaxation toward a_k* m_t^k, while the correlation m_t evolves according to a polynomial vector field of degree 2s−1 where s is the information exponent (first nonzero Hermite mode of φ*). This creates a fast-slow separation: coefficients rapidly track quasi-static equilibria while m_t creeps at rate ∝ m^{2s−1}, yielding a concentration time τ_c ∼ d^{s−1} before exponential convergence. Crucially, the joint dynamics break the symmetry obstruction of the planted model—negative initial correlation leads to convergence at m = −1 with sign-flipped coefficients (a_{k,t} → (−1)^k a_k*), so the composite predictor f_t(⟨w_t, x⟩) still recovers φ*(⟨w*, x⟩). The practical algorithm implements this via a Hermite-based RKHS with a weighted kernel K(x,y) = Σ c_k h_k(x)h_k(y), truncated to k terms, and trained by full-batch gradient descent with spherical re-projection of w at each step.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 4.2) establishes that the joint gradient flow converges exponentially to the target after a concentration time τ_c ~ d^{s-1} for information exponent s ≥ 2, and immediately (no transient) for s = 1. Specifically, for s ≥ 2, |1 − m_t| ≤ Ce^{−c(t−τ_c)} and |a_{k,t} − a_k^*| ≤ Cb_k e^{−c(t−τ_c)} (or the sign-flipped version if m_0 < 0), where b ∈ ℓ_2 is normalized. Numerical experiments (Figures 1–2) with planted functions φ* = (3/2)h_2 + 2h_3 − h_4 (s=2, d=100, n=10^5) confirm the predicted three-phase fast-slow dynamics: rapid coefficient stabilization, a slow m-growth plateau of order d^{s−1}, and a sharp exponential convergence transition.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare against external baselines on standard benchmarks; the `compares_to` list is empty. The central comparison is internal and theoretical: the joint learning dynamics (Theorem 4.2) versus the planted model where the target function φ* is fixed a priori (Proposition 4.1). In the planted model, a negative initial correlation m_0 < 0 causes learning failure — the flow converges to m = 0 (for odd s ≥ 3) or to a spurious fixed point at m = −m̄ (for even s with m̄ < 1), never reaching the global minimum at m = 1. In contrast, the joint learning dynamics successfully recover the target in both sign regimes: for m_0 < 0, the system converges to m_t → −1 with sign-flipped Hermite coefficients a_{k,t} → (−1)^k a_k^*, yielding the correct predictor f_t(⟨w_t, x⟩) → φ*(⟨w*, x⟩). This demonstrates that the flexibility of simultaneously learning the function allows the system to escape the symmetry traps that immobilize the planted gradient flow.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Gaussian single-index model literature (Dudeja & Hsu, 2018; Ben Arous et al., 2022) by analyzing the full joint gradient flow where both the direction w and the link function φ evolve simultaneously, rather than fixing one component as in the planted model. The loss decomposition in Hermite coefficients (Lemma 2.1) and the information exponent framework are inherited directly from prior work, while the practical RKHS implementation draws on the Hermite-based kernel construction of Follain & Bach (2024). The key departure is showing that the coupled dynamics fundamentally alter the landscape: the planted model's negative-initialization trap (Proposition 4.1) is overcome in the joint setting because the adaptive function f_t corrects early misalignment of w.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that joint learning escapes the planted model's spurious basins of attraction is a clean theoretical demonstration that learning flexibility can be beneficial rather than merely a complication, echoing broader observations in overparameterized networks. However, the analysis is entirely in the population-risk, infinite-sample regime with continuous-time gradient flow; the paper explicitly identifies finite-sample complexity, discrete-time convergence, and the interplay between statistical noise and the fast-slow timescale separation as essential open problems. The practical RKHS implementation is validated only on small-scale synthetic experiments (d=100, n=10^5), leaving the method's viability at realistic scales untested.
