## Problem

Existing cultural alignment datasets for LLMs suffer from two fundamental deficiencies identified by emic-etic culture theory: they lack representativeness (failing to capture a culture's most salient and central characteristics, leading to redundancy) and lack distinctiveness (failing to separate a target culture's unique nuances from patterns shared with related cultures, e.g., China/Japan/Korea). No prior dataset explicitly formulates or optimizes for both properties simultaneously, limiting data efficiency and alignment effectiveness.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a set of initial cultural questions, a target culture specification, K non-target culture specifications, and an LLM (either the target model to be aligned or a stronger one like GPT-4o/5) for role-playing simulation. It outputs optimized question-response pairs (CARDSet, 1,000 per culture across 15 cultures) that are used for SFT or DPO fine-tuning, with as few as 100–200 samples per culture sufficient for strong alignment. The entire data generation process is in-context (no parameter updates), making it compatible with both open-source and black-box LLMs, and a diversity filter (cosine similarity threshold τ=0.85) removes semantically redundant samples.

## Method

CAReDiO is an iterative in-context data optimization framework that alternately refines questions and responses to maximize a combined score with three terms: representativeness, distinctiveness, and diversity. For representativeness, it applies Cultural Consensus Theory by prompting M diverse LLM personas (general individuals, cultural experts, cross-cultural researchers) to role-play culturally competent raters, then scores candidate responses via point-wise mutual information (an Information-Directed Sampling formulation) to identify those that maximally reduce the LLM's cultural uncertainty. For distinctiveness, it applies Cognitive Conflict Theory by formulating a generalized JS divergence between the target culture's response distribution and K non-target cultures, approximated point-wise via a classifier (LLM-generated responses + text-embedding-3-small similarity) that estimates the probability a response does NOT originate from any non-target culture; Proposition 3.2 proves this maximizes a lower bound of GJS with bounded approximation error. The iterative schema (Algorithm 1) alternates: given a question, generate n candidate responses, score them via Eq.(5), select the best; then given the selected response, refine the question to increase generation probability of representative and distinctive answers, repeating until convergence.

## Results

CAReDiO achieves the best average score across all four benchmarks (CulturalBench-Easy/Hard, Prism, GlobalOpinionQA, WVS) on every tested backbone: 62.51 on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, 70.04 on Gemma-3-27B-IT, and 73.37 on GPT-4.1, outperforming the strongest baselines by 2.1, 1.4, and 1.0 points respectively. Gains are statistically significant (p<0.05) on Prism and CulturalBench-Hard, with improvements broadly distributed across all 15 cultures (e.g., 14/14 cultures improved on Prism). The method achieves competitive performance with as few as 100–200 training samples, and the ablation confirms both the representativeness (+15.3%) and distinctiveness (+13.5%) objectives contribute independently, with their combination yielding the largest gain (+19.9%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAReDiO consistently outperforms all five compared cultural datasets (CultureLLM, CulturePark, CultureSPA, CultureBank, CultureInstruct) on the average score across all three open-source/proprietary backbones tested. On Qwen2.5-7B, CAReDiO (62.51 avg) beats the strongest baseline CultureData (60.43) by 2.1 points and CultureLLM (58.32) by 4.2 points; on Gemma-3-27B it leads CulturePark (68.67) by 1.4 points; on GPT-4.1 it surpasses Role-Play (72.35) by 1.0 points. The largest margins appear on Prism (open-ended cultural alignment), where CAReDiO achieves 3.871 vs. 3.354 (CultureData) on Qwen2.5-7B and 4.627 vs. 4.571 (Role-Play) on Gemma-3-27B, with statistically significant p<0.05. The one exception is GlobalOpinionQA and WVS, where CultureLLM occasionally edges out CAReDiO (e.g., 57.11 vs. 56.23 on Qwen2.5-7B), which the authors attribute to CultureLLM's inherent advantage from augmenting real WVS survey data (potential data leakage). CAReDiO also demonstrates that data synthesized by the aligned backbone itself (without a stronger teacher model) still yields clear gains, confirming the improvement stems from the information-theoretic objectives rather than mere knowledge distillation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Cultural Consensus Theory (Weller, 2007) and Cognitive Conflict Theory (Cosier & Rose, 1977) from cultural anthropology to formalize two information-theoretic objectives—mutual information for representativeness and generalized JS divergence for distinctiveness—that prior cultural alignment datasets (CultureLLM, CulturePark, CultureSPA, CultureBank, CultureInstruct) lack explicit optimization for. It departs from the dominant paradigm of prompt-engineering-based data synthesis or web-data filtering by introducing an iterative in-context optimization schema that alternately refines questions and responses against these objectives, drawing on the Barber-Agakov bound and Information-Directed Sampling for theoretical grounding. The emic-etic framework from cross-cultural psychology provides the conceptual motivation, positioning the work at the intersection of cultural theory and LLM data curation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in sample efficiency: CAReDiO achieves competitive cultural alignment with as few as 200 training samples per culture, making culturally aligned LLMs feasible for underrepresented communities with limited annotation budgets. The framework is model-agnostic and works for both open-source (Llama, Qwen, Gemma) and proprietary (GPT-4.1, GPT-5) backbones. Key limitations include coverage of only 15 cultures, reliance on LLMs to simulate culturally competent raters (an unresolved challenge in persona fidelity), and a small gap on survey-aligned benchmarks (WVS, GlobalOpinionQA) where CultureLLM's use of real survey data provides an inherent advantage. Open questions include whether the information-theoretic objectives generalize to very low-resource cultures, whether the simple embedding-based classifier scales to more culturally proximate pairs, and whether the approach can be extended to DPO or RLHF-style alignment beyond SFT.
