## Problem

In decentralized learning with heterogeneous node weights, two natural strategies exist for incorporating the weights: absorbing them into local losses (yielding a doubly stochastic matrix) or encoding them in a row-stochastic mixing matrix. Prior work confirmed both target the same weighted global loss, but it remained unclear whether standard Euclidean convergence guarantees are tight under heterogeneous weights and what structurally differentiates the two strategies' convergence behavior.

## Input / Output

The method takes a prescribed heterogeneous weight vector λ (summing to n), local data distributions per node, an undirected communication graph, and a step size α as input. It outputs converged model parameters θ* along with explicit non-asymptotic convergence rate bounds on the average gradient norm, where the constants depend on the spectral gaps of the mixing matrices and the weight heterogeneity ratio κ_λ = √(λ_max/λ_min). Notably, the weights are fixed problem parameters (e.g., proportional to local data sizes), not learned variables, and the topology itself can be co-designed with λ via a degree-matching procedure.

## Method

The paper introduces a weighted Hilbert space L²(λ; ℝ^d) in which the λ-induced row-stochastic matrix (constructed via a modified Metropolis-Hastings rule with laziness) is self-adjoint, while the doubly stochastic matrix is not. This self-adjointness asymmetry is the key structural insight: in the weighted geometry, the doubly stochastic matrix's non-normality introduces multiplicative penalty factors κ_λ² and λ_max² into the consensus error recursion, amplifying both the consensus and stochastic-gradient terms in the convergence rate. The paper derives tight convergence rates for decentralized stochastic gradient tracking under both strategies, showing Strategy II can converge strictly faster even when its spectral gap is smaller, and translates the spectral condition into a degree-weight matching constraint (d_i ∝ λ_i) that yields practical topology-design guidelines via a Havel-Hakimi-based graph construction algorithm.

## Results

The paper is primarily theoretical, with experiments serving as validation rather than producing a single headline accuracy number. On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18 (16 and 32 nodes), Strategy II (row-stochastic mixing) consistently achieves lower weighted interval loss than Strategy I (doubly stochastic mixing) across all tested weight vectors (λ_A, λ_B, λ_C) and topologies, with the gap widening at 32 nodes under λ_C. On synthetic least-squares tasks (16, 32, 64 nodes), Strategy II reaches a tighter steady-state gradient norm and converges faster even when its spectral gap ρ_Λ is smaller than ρ_J of the doubly stochastic matrix. On tailored topologies generated by Algorithm 2 (degree-weight matching), the row-stochastic matrix W achieves a substantially larger spectral gap than the corresponding W^ds, further amplifying the convergence advantage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external methods; instead it performs a head-to-head comparison between two weighting strategies within the same decentralized gradient-tracking (GT) framework. Strategy I (absorbing λ_i into local losses with a doubly stochastic W^ds) incurs multiplicative penalty factors κ_λ² and λ_max² in its convergence bound (Theorem 6.5, Eq. 7), while Strategy II (keeping original losses with a λ-induced row-stochastic W) avoids these penalties entirely (Eq. 8). Empirically, Strategy II wins on every tested configuration: lower gradient norms on least-squares (16/32/64 nodes) and lower interval loss on CIFAR-10 (16/32 nodes), regardless of whether W^ds has a larger spectral gap. The step-size advantage is also demonstrated: Strategy II admits a larger maximum step size (α_max^II ≥ α_max^I) when 1−ρ_Λ ≥ λ_max^(−1/2)(1−ρ_J), enabling faster convergence with improved noise robustness. The comparison is scoped to single-loop GT without extra communication rounds or acceleration, so it does not rule out more sophisticated doubly stochastic variants.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the standard decentralized gradient tracking (GT) framework of Xu et al. (2017) and the improved consensus-error analysis of Koloskova et al. (2021), extending both to handle heterogeneous node weights. It departs from the diffusion and exact-diffusion literature (Sayed 2014; Yuan et al. 2018b,c), which treats row-stochastic and doubly stochastic mixing as equivalent in terms of the target loss, by asking a sharper question: are the Euclidean-space convergence guarantees tight, and what geometrically distinguishes the two strategies? The key methodological departure is the introduction of a weighted Hilbert space L²(λ;ℝᵈ) in which the λ-induced row-stochastic matrix becomes self-adjoint while the doubly stochastic one does not, yielding strictly tighter rates and revealing penalty terms invisible in standard Euclidean analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled, rate-level comparison of the two natural ways to encode heterogeneous weights in decentralized GT, showing that the choice of mixing matrix geometry (self-adjointness in the weighted space) can dominate the spectral gap in determining convergence speed. The resulting degree-weight matching principle and Algorithm 2 offer concrete topology-design guidelines for practitioners deploying heterogeneous-weight decentralized systems. Limitations include the restriction to undirected graphs, single-loop GT without acceleration, and the absence of formal lower bounds or minimax optimality statements; extending the framework to directed graphs and establishing matching lower bounds are identified as important open directions.
