## Problem

Existing combinatorial sparse PCA algorithms (diagonal thresholding, covariance thresholding, greedy correlation) are only provably correct under the spiked identity covariance model, and the paper constructs explicit counterexamples showing they fail with constant probability under the general model where the covariance is arbitrary but has a sparse top eigenvector. The central question is whether a lightweight combinatorial method can solve sparse PCA under this general model with poly(s log d) samples and d²·poly(s log d) time, matching the efficiency of Model 1 methods while avoiding the Ω(d^{2ω}) runtime of SDP-based approaches.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes n i.i.d. sub-Gaussian samples in R^d along with a truncation parameter r (set to Ω(s²) for the main guarantee) and an iteration count T = Ω(log s). It first computes the sample covariance, then runs the restarted truncated power method from all d standard basis vector initializations in parallel, selecting the final output by maximizing the empirical quadratic form ⟨u, Σ̂u⟩. The returned vector is r-sparse and can be post-processed via a final top-s truncation to yield an exactly s-sparse estimate at constant overhead to the approximation error.

## Method

The Restarted Truncated Power Method (RTPM) modifies the truncated power method of Yuan and Zhang (2013) by restarting from every standard basis vector e_i (i ∈ [d]) rather than requiring a good local initialization. The key insight is that the basis vector corresponding to the largest coordinate of the true sparse eigenvector v already achieves poly(1/s) correlation, which is sufficient as a local starting point; by exhaustively trying all d initializations, the algorithm achieves global convergence. The analysis tracks a potential function ψ_t = ‖V_p^T u_t‖₂ (correlation with the top-p eigenspace) in two phases: early iterations with low correlation use oversampling (r ≫ s) to prevent truncation from destroying signal, while later iterations show geometric progress in the residual. A technical challenge is converting between the correlation-based potential used in the analysis and the empirical quadratic form used in the selection step, since the true correlation is not observable from the data.

## Results

The main theoretical guarantee (Theorem 2) shows that RTPM recovers a sparse principal component with ‖Vᵀu‖² ≥ 1−Δ under Model 3 using n = Ω((σ²/λₖ)² · (s³k⁶/Δ⁶γ⁶ + s²k⁶/Δ¹⁰γ¹⁰) · log(d/δ)) samples, r = Ω(s²k²/Δ²γ² + sk²/Δ⁴γ⁴) truncation, and T = Ω(k·log(s/Δγ)/(Δγ)) iterations, in O(nd²T) time. For the special case Δ=γ=1/10, this simplifies to n = Ω(s³ log(d/δ)), r = Ω(s²), T = Ω(log s), yielding ⟨v,u⟩² ≥ 9/10. Empirically, RTPM is the only method that consistently achieves high squared-cosine correlation across all sample sizes and covariance instances in both the spiked identity model (d=1000, s=8) and the general Model 2 counterexample, while all three combinatorial baselines fail on the counterexample.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares RTPM against four baselines: DiagThresh (Amini & Wainwright, 2008), CovThresh (Deshpande & Montanari, 2016), GreedyCorr (adapted from Błasiok et al., 2024), and the SDP-based FPS method (Vu et al., 2013). All three combinatorial baselines provably fail under Model 2: DiagThresh outputs a vector with sin²∠(u,v)=1 (zero correlation) with probability ≥1/2 (Lemma 10), CovThresh similarly achieves sin²∠(u,v)=1 (Lemma 12), and GreedyCorr recovers at most one true support element, achieving sin²∠(u,v)≥1−1/s (Lemma 13). RTPM overcomes all three counterexamples and consistently achieves high correlation in the empirical experiments (Figures 1–2). Against the SDP-based FPS method, RTPM matches or exceeds correlation quality while improving runtime by Ω(d^{2.5}) (O(nd²T) vs. Ω(d^{2ω}) for the SDP), though at the cost of a slightly higher sample complexity (s³ log d vs. the conjectured s² log d for SDP methods).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the truncated power method of Yuan and Zhang (2013), which was previously only analyzed under a suitable local initialization, by providing the first global convergence guarantee via a restarted version that tries all standard basis vectors as initializations. It departs from the dominant SDP-based approaches (d'Aspremont et al. 2008; Vu et al. 2013) that solve sparse PCA under general covariance but require Ω(d^{2ω}) time, and from lightweight combinatorial methods (diagonal/covariance thresholding) that are efficient but only provable under the spiked identity model. The paper also draws on the semi-random model philosophy from theoretical computer science (Blum and Spencer 1995; Blasiok et al. 2024) to frame the gap between Model 1 and Model 2 as a robustness question, and connects to the planted clique problem via the reduction of Berthet and Rigollet (2013).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first combinatorial algorithm for sparse PCA under general covariance achieving poly(s log d) samples and d²·poly(s log d) time, improving over SDP-based methods by Ω(d^{2.5}) in runtime. The explicit counterexamples showing that diagonal thresholding, covariance thresholding, and greedy correlation all fail under Model 2 (a model that can only amplify the signal) are a notable negative result with broader implications for algorithm robustness. The deflation barrier (Lemma 9) — showing that even with s=2 and arbitrarily good correlation, projecting out one component can destroy sparsity — is a significant obstacle for k-sparse PCA that remains open. Key limitations include the s³ log d sample complexity (vs. the conjectured optimal s² log d) and suboptimal dependence on the gap and error parameters, both flagged as open problems.
