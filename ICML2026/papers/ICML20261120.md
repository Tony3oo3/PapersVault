## Problem

RGB-to-RAW reconstruction is an ill-posed inverse problem where regression-based methods produce over-smoothed predictions that drift from the authentic RAW signal manifold, while generative methods (diffusion, multi-step flow) achieve higher fidelity at prohibitive computational cost. The paper identifies that single-step deterministic flow captures global transport trends robust to ISP-induced perturbations, and that homogeneous representation alignment within a shared DINO feature space can further amplify reconstruction fidelity in a single inference step.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard sRGB image (produced from RAW via the RawPy library) and outputs a packed Bayer-pattern RAW sensor image with dimensions H/2 × W/2 × 4 channels (R, G1, G2, B). A Reversible Dimensional Alignment module deterministically expands the packed RAW into an H × W × 3 spatial layout for feature extraction, with the inverse operation simply sampling anchor indices to guarantee lossless round-trip. The framework is evaluated at resolutions from 512×512 up to 4096×4096, with the encoder using 16×16 patches to match DINOv3's pre-training configuration.

## Method

SHADE encodes both the input RGB and target RAW images into a shared latent space via a trainable student DINOv3 encoder (initialized from a frozen teacher DINOv3), then trains a lightweight 14-layer DiT (hidden dim 128) to predict a velocity field along a linear probability path between the two latents. At inference, a single Euler step (z₁ = z₀ + v̂) transports the RGB latent to the RAW manifold, avoiding multi-step ODE solvers. The Homogeneous Representation Alignment mechanism extracts DiT hidden states from the 7th layer, projects them to 384 dimensions, and enforces patch-wise cosine similarity with the frozen teacher's representations of the clean RAW image, ensuring the DiT operates within a stable, pre-aligned feature space. Training jointly optimizes flow matching, the alignment loss, and two image-space reconstruction losses (autoencoder and DiT-guided), each combining L2 and log-L1 terms to preserve both absolute radiance and relative intensity in the RAW domain.

## Results

SHADE achieves state-of-the-art PSNR across all five RGB-to-RAW benchmarks: 39.07 dB on PASCALRAW, 30.89 dB on FiveK-Nikon, 33.69 dB on FiveK-Canon, 40.61 dB on NOD-Nikon, and 39.07 dB on NOD-Sony. The largest margin is on NOD-Nikon (+1.50 dB over the second-best ReRAW-S) and PASCALRAW (+1.45 dB over RAW-Flow). Inference requires only a single forward pass (1.51 s at 4096×4096), roughly 23× faster than DDIM and 6× faster than RAW-Flow at the same resolution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SHADE outperforms all 13 baselines on PSNR across every dataset where they overlap. Against the closest generative competitor RAW-Flow (Liu et al., 2026), SHADE gains +1.45 dB on PASCALRAW (39.07 vs 37.62), +0.10 dB on FiveK-Nikon (30.89 vs 30.79), and +1.14 dB on FiveK-Canon (33.69 vs 32.55), while also being ~6× faster at inference (1.51 s vs 9.36 s at 4096×4096). Against the strongest regression baseline ReRAW-S (Berdan et al., 2025), SHADE leads by +1.78 dB on PASCALRAW, +2.85 dB on FiveK-Nikon, +1.93 dB on FiveK-Canon, +1.50 dB on NOD-Nikon, and +0.81 dB on NOD-Sony. Generative baselines DDPM and RAW-Diff trail by 5–10 dB in PSNR, confirming that multi-step stochastic sampling is both slower and less accurate for this task. The only metric where SHADE does not lead is NOD-Sony SSIM (0.9687 vs ReRAW-R's 0.9813), though it still wins on PSNR for that dataset. In the downstream object-detection evaluation (Appendix E), SHADE-synthesized RAW data yields 50.54 mAP in daylight and 38.13 mAP at night, surpassing the best competing reconstruction (CycleR2R at 47.75 / 33.60 mAP) by +2.79 and +4.53 mAP respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on RAW-Flow (Liu et al., 2026), which introduced deterministic latent flow matching for RGB-to-RAW reconstruction but still relied on multi-step ODE solvers. SHADE adopts the Single-step Deterministic Flow (SSDF) formulation from that work and validates—via a controlled Circle-to-Star heuristic experiment—that it is inherently more robust to ISP-induced perturbations than point-to-point regression. The key methodological departure is the introduction of Homogeneous Representation Alignment (HRA), which repurposes the REPA-style representation alignment paradigm (Yu et al., 2025) but instantiates it with a homogeneously initialized student-teacher DINOv3 pair to enforce alignment within a shared feature space, a design not previously explored in single-step flow-based reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SHADE reconciles the long-standing quality-speed trade-off in RGB-to-RAW reconstruction by achieving state-of-the-art PSNR/SSIM across five benchmarks with a single inference step (~23× faster than DDIM, ~6× faster than RAW-Flow), making high-fidelity sensor data recovery practical for high-resolution and edge deployment. The HRA mechanism offers a reusable recipe for stabilizing single-step flow training via homogeneous distillation, potentially transferable to other inverse-problem settings. However, the method remains deterministic (cannot sample diverse RAW noise realizations) and is evaluated only under a camera-specific paired-data protocol, leaving cross-sensor generalization and stochastic noise modeling as important open questions.
