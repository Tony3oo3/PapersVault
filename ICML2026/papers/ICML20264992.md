## Problem

Computing do-Shapley values exactly requires evaluating 2^d interventional coalitions, making it intractable for moderate feature counts. While prior work identified that SCM graph structure renders some interventions redundant (irreducible sets), no exact algorithm exploited this structure to avoid the exponential blowup, and fixed-budget estimators wasted queries on redundant coalitions within the same equivalence class. Additionally, verifying non-parametric identifiability of the full do-Shapley value required r separate ID-algorithm calls, risking costly mid-computation failures.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a causal graph (DAG or ADMG with latent confounders), fitted structural equations (e.g., gradient boosting regressors for conditional distributions), a specific instance x ∈ R^d, and optionally a query budget m. The value function ν(S) = E[Y | do(S = x_S)] is evaluated through the SCM oracle. Output is the vector of d do-Shapley values φ ∈ R^d, computed as a weighted sum over r equivalence classes where each class weight w_i(c_j) is derived in closed form from the basis and closure sizes. When m ≥ r, the output achieves machine-precision exactness; when m < r, it provides a structure-aware approximation.

## Method

The core reformulation partitions the 2^d coalitions into r equivalence classes defined by basis-closure pairs, reducing the Shapley sum to a linear combination of r class values with analytically computable weights w_i(c_j) (Equation 5). An exact algorithm (Algorithm 2) enumerates all r irreducible sets by traversing the lattice of closed sets in decreasing size, using Lemma 3.1 to guarantee that every closed set is generated from a larger one, achieving O(r(d+e+T)) time. For fixed-budget settings, a boundary sampler (Algorithm 4) maintains a priority queue of candidate classes ordered by expected weight magnitude, exploring lattice neighbors (lower and upper) to guarantee discovery of min(m,r) distinct classes in O(m·d(d+e)) time, then feeds these into a simulated estimator (LeverageSHAP or RegressionMSR) for variance reduction. A structural sufficiency theorem (Theorem 5.1) proves that non-parametric identifiability of all 2^d coalition values is equivalent to identifiability of only the d singleton interventions, reducing the ID-algorithm check from O(r) to O(d) calls.

## Results

Across 156 TALENT benchmark datasets, the number of irreducible sets r is typically well below the worst-case 2^d, scaling between the linear lower bound d and the exponential upper bound. In the sparse-budget regime (m < r), doRegressionMSR consistently achieves the lowest relative MSE among all estimators, with the advantage growing as the budget ratio m/r increases. A phase transition occurs at m = r: both doRegressionMSR and doLeverageSHAP drop to machine-precision error, while the structure-agnostic baselines (RegressionMSR and LeverageSHAP with FRA caching) continue to exhibit slow convergence and fail to reach exactness even at m = 2r. The paper reports error reductions of 'several orders of magnitude' for the doEstimator variants over the best value-function-agnostic estimators as m approaches r.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its structure-aware estimators (doRegressionMSR and doLeverageSHAP) against two state-of-the-art model-agnostic baselines: RegressionMSR (Witter et al., 2025) and LeverageSHAP (Musco & Witter, 2025), both augmented with the FRA caching scheme of Parafita et al. (2025). The doEstimator variants win decisively on relative MSE in both the runtime-adjusted comparison (Figure 5a) and the budget-ratio comparison (Figure 5b). The key differentiator is sample efficiency: because the boundary sampler guarantees discovery of min(m, r) distinct equivalence classes, the doEstimators avoid the redundant queries that plague the structure-agnostic baselines, which blindly sample coalitions that may map to already-seen classes. The advantage is most dramatic near the phase transition at m = r, where the doEstimators achieve machine precision while the baselines still show substantial error even at double the budget (m = 2r). In the sparse-budget regime (m well below r), doRegressionMSR shows the strongest sample efficiency, consistently achieving the lowest error across the TALENT datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the irreducible-set caching framework of Parafita et al. (2025) from a heuristic speedup into a full structural reformulation: by introducing the closure of a coalition alongside its basis, the authors show that the powerset partitions into equivalence classes, compressing the Shapley sum from 2^d terms to r terms. The estimation pipeline repurposes the regression machinery of LeverageSHAP (Musco & Witter, 2025) and RegressionMSR (Witter et al., 2025) as base estimators, but feeds them structure-aware samples from a boundary sampler rather than blind coalition draws. The identifiability theorem (Theorem 5.1) is a new theoretical contribution that has no direct analogue in prior do-Shapley work, reducing the ID-algorithm burden from r calls to d.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is a shift from exponential worst-case to linear-in-r computation for do-Shapley values, with r typically far below 2^d on sparse real-world causal graphs (Figure 4). The identifiability reduction to d singleton checks is a clean, actionable result that prevents costly mid-computation failures. Two limitations temper the gains: the worst case r = 2^d still exists (tighter topology-dependent bounds remain open), and all results assume a correctly specified SCM, so graph misspecification silently corrupts the attributions. The framework generalizes naturally to Banzhaf, Beta Shapley, and Shapley Interaction Indices, suggesting a broader structural template for probabilistic values on SCMs.
