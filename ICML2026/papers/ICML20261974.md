## Problem

Standard on-policy distillation relies on reverse KL divergence, which is mode-seeking and causes the student to collapse generation diversity (retaining only 6.8% of high-entropy tokens versus 18.5% in the teacher) while producing unstable gradient signals at positions where the teacher distribution is uncertain. This is particularly harmful for reasoning tasks, where high-entropy tokens often encode multiple valid reasoning paths at critical decision points.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261974/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a math problem prompt as input and the student model generates a full reasoning chain ending in a boxed final answer. During training, the student produces on-policy rollouts (single rollout per prompt for OPD/EOPD, eight for GRPO) with max response length 4096 tokens; the teacher is queried once per token position to supply log-probabilities, token-level entropy, and a top-k token set. Evaluation samples 8 responses per question at temperature 1.0 with top-p 0.8 and max length 8192, reporting Avg@8 and Pass@8.

## Method

EOPD augments the standard clipped reverse KL on-policy distillation objective with a selective forward KL term that activates only at token positions where the teacher's entropy exceeds a threshold τ=0.8. The forward KL is computed over the teacher's top-k=16 tokens (renormalized to form a valid distribution) to avoid forcing the student to cover low-probability tails, and is weighted by a coefficient α=1.0. The key insight is that reverse KL and forward KL are complementary: reverse KL provides efficient, stable learning on confident teacher predictions, while forward KL's mode-covering property transfers the teacher's uncertainty and global distributional structure at ambiguous positions. The full objective is optimized via a PPO-style clipped surrogate with importance sampling, where the reverse KL term uses a single-sample Monte Carlo estimate from the rollout and the forward KL term is computed as an explicit sum over the top-k teacher tokens, adding only ~4.5% overhead per training step.

## Results

On six mathematical reasoning benchmarks, EOPD improves Pass@8 by +1.37 (Qwen3-0.6B-Base), +2.39 (Qwen3-1.7B-Base), and +5.05 (Qwen3-4B-Base) over standard on-policy distillation (OPD), with corresponding Avg@8 gains of +1.16, +0.99, and +1.80. For the Qwen3-1.7B-Base model, EOPD achieves 68.73% Avg@8 on MATH500, 10.42% on AIME24, and 5.83% on AIME25, outperforming OPD (67.76/8.33/6.25) and GRPO (68.83/9.17/4.58). The largest absolute gains appear on harder benchmarks: AIME25 Pass@8 improves from 30.00 (OPD) to 33.33 (EOPD) for the 4B model, and AIME24 Pass@8 from 26.67 to 36.67.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EOPD consistently outperforms OPD (the primary baseline it extends) across all three model scales, with the largest Pass@8 gains on harder benchmarks (AIME24: +3.33 for 1.7B, +10.00 for 4B). Against GRPO, EOPD wins on most benchmarks but GRPO is competitive on MATH500 Avg@8 (68.83 vs 68.73 for 1.7B) and MATH500 for the 4B model (80.47 vs 80.20); EOPD clearly dominates on AIME25 (5.83 vs 4.58 Avg@8 for 1.7B; 17.50 vs 12.66 for 4B). KD (off-policy) is the weakest baseline across all settings, trailing EOPD by 5–10 points on Avg@8. Among entropy-driven baselines, EOPD beats both OPD+Entropy Bonus and OPD+Advantage Shaping on MATH500 and AIME24 (Table 4), with the key differentiator being lower forward KL at high-entropy positions rather than higher policy entropy. Against GKD (λ=1.0, fully on-policy), EOPD wins on all four benchmarks tested (e.g., AIME24: 10.42 vs 9.17 Avg@8). Finally, EOPD outperforms the adaptive-KL baselines BD-KD, AKL, and ToDi on MATH500 and AIME24 (Table 11), confirming that teacher-entropy-based selective forward KL is more effective than entropy-gap, head-tail mismatch, or vocabulary-discrepancy-based weighting.

## Relation to Prior Work

EOPD directly extends the on-policy distillation (OPD) framework of Lu & Lab (2025), which uses clipped reverse KL with PPO-style updates, and the broader OPD/GKD lineage from Agarwal et al. (2024). The key departure is diagnosing reverse KL's mode-seeking behavior as a source of diversity collapse and training instability specifically at high-entropy teacher tokens, then selectively augmenting the objective with a top-k forward KL term gated by a teacher-entropy threshold. This positions EOPD as a targeted fix within the existing on-policy distillation paradigm rather than a new training framework, and it contrasts with entropy-promoting RL techniques (entropy bonus, advantage shaping) by using teacher-guided KL selection rather than intrinsic policy entropy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

On-policy distillation has become a standard, compute-efficient recipe for transferring reasoning capabilities (matching RL at ~10× lower cost), so a drop-in modification that preserves diversity and stabilizes training at uncertain tokens has immediate practical value for anyone distilling reasoning models. The paper's limitation is that gains, while consistent, are moderate (+1.4 to +5.05 Pass@8), and the method adds a hyperparameter (τ) and a small memory/compute overhead. Open questions include whether the entropy threshold generalizes to non-math domains, whether the top-k forward KL approximation is sufficient for very high-entropy positions, and whether the approach composes with other recent distillation advances (e.g., speculative KD, interleaved sampling).
