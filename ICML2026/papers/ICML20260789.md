## Problem

The practical Muon optimizer (MuonAdam) applies spectral-norm steepest descent to hidden weight matrices and Adam to all other parameters, but this side-by-side design lacks a unified interpretation as steepest descent on the full parameter space. The design space of norm choices—outer norm for aggregating layers, per-layer norms, and whether to normalize updates—has not been systematically explored, and Muon is highly sensitive to learning rate tuning, making it fragile for new tasks.

## Input / Output

The method consumes stochastic gradients from backpropagation, exponential moving average momentum buffers, and two learning rates (η_m for hidden weight matrices, η_b for all other parameters), along with a loss lower bound F_* for model truncation. It produces updated network parameters where each hidden weight matrix is updated via its polar factor scaled by a layer-aggregated nuclear norm, while non-matrix parameters receive Adam-style updates. A key practical detail is the stale nuclear norm approximation, which reuses the previous step's dual norms (one scalar per layer) to avoid storing all polar factors or recomputing them, adding only 5% wall-clock time over Muon.

## Method

The paper introduces a steepest descent framework on product spaces where a per-layer norm (e.g., spectral norm for matrices, adaptive ℓ∞ or ℓ2 norms for other parameters) is aggregated by an outer norm (ℓ∞, ℓ2, or a hybrid) to define a norm on the full parameter space, with a choice between constrained (normalized) and regularized (magnitude-scaled) updates. This framework formally recovers MuonAdam, Scion, and PolarGrad as specific norm choices, and yields a new optimizer MuonMax that uses regularized steepest descent with a hybrid outer norm combining max spectral norm and adaptive ℓ2 norm. The paper then extends Momo (model truncation with momentum) to arbitrary norms, producing adaptive step sizes that cap the update magnitude based on the current loss gap to F_*, which dramatically widens the range of competitive learning rates. The combination MuonMax-Momo with stale nuclear norm approximations achieves near-identical loss to MuonAdam while being robust to learning rates spanning five orders of magnitude.

## Results

On FineWeb-1B (GPT2-Small, 124M params), MuonAdam-Momo achieves the lowest final validation loss (3.551) among all 36 tested variations, with MuonMax-Momo close behind (3.576). On SlimPajama (GPT2-Large, 774M params, 1B tokens), MuonMax-Momo achieves the lowest loss and remains between 3.13 and 3.24 even as the Muon learning rate varies over five orders of magnitude (1e-3 to 10), whereas MuonAdam and Scion degrade sharply with a 10× shift. The proportion of learning rates yielding loss below 3.65 is 25% for MuonAdam/Scion, 50% for MuonMax-Momo, and 62.5% for MuonAdam-Momo on FineWeb-1B. The stale nuclear norm approximation increases loss by at most 0.004 while reducing the wall-clock overhead of MuonMax-Momo from 11% to 5% relative to MuonAdam.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MuonMax-Momo consistently matches or outperforms MuonAdam (Jordan et al., 2024b) across all evaluated settings: on SlimPajama-15B (GPT2-Large), MuonMax-Momo achieves similar or lower loss than MuonAdam for every learning rate tested, and exhibits substantially less variation as LRs are shifted from their optima. Against Scion (Pethick et al., 2025), MuonMax-Momo achieves comparable or better final loss while enjoying a much wider basin of competitive learning rates (50% vs. 25% of the LR sweep yielding loss < 3.65 on FineWeb-1B). All four non-Muon baselines—AdamW (Loshchilov & Hutter, 2019), Lion (Chen et al., 2023), Adan (Xie et al., 2024), and Sophia (Liu et al., 2024)—lag behind the four best Muon-family variations even after additional hyperparameter tuning, consistent with prior work. The Momo (Schaipp et al., 2024) model-truncation step size, when combined with MuonMax, increases tuning robustness for all norm variants and often improves the best achievable validation loss. The stale nuclear norm approximation (a practical efficiency trick) degrades loss by at most 0.004 across all tested methods, confirming negligible impact on final performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the practical Muon optimizer (Muon for hidden weight matrices, Adam for all other parameters) under a single steepest descent framework on the full parameter space, showing that the side-by-side implementation is exactly constrained steepest descent with respect to a specific product norm. It builds on the Muon line of work (Jordan et al., 2024b; Bernstein & Newhouse, 2024a) and the model truncation / Momo line (Asi & Duchi, 2019b; Schaipp et al., 2024), but departs from prior per-layer analyses by treating the entire network as a product space with a chosen outer norm. The framework also recovers Scion and PolarGrad as special cases, and takes an orthogonal approach to the modular norm literature by exploring general product norms rather than the specific Lipschitz-enforcing norm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is that MuonMax-Momo achieves comparable or better loss than MuonAdam while tolerating learning rates spanning five orders of magnitude, which could substantially reduce hyperparameter search costs for new LLM training runs. The stale nuclear norm approximation (5% overhead vs. 11%) makes the non-separable update nearly as cheap as standard Muon. A key open question the paper identifies is when and why one product norm yields faster convergence than another — the framework enables this analysis but does not resolve it, and the authors note that Muon does not converge in the nonsmooth Lipschitz setting, limiting the applicability of existing convergence theory.
