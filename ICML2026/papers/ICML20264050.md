## Problem

Existing programmatic skill induction methods for LLM-driven agents distill interaction experience into state-blind parameterized scripts (e.g., Open(Receptacle) → Pick(Object)) that encode *what* to do but not *when* or *why*. These scripts lack explicit control flow, state predicates, and dynamic variable binding, causing them to fail under environmental deviations and limiting generalization to unseen goals or topologies.

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw text observations from partially observable environments (ALFWorld, WebShop, TextCraft) along with a small set of expert interaction trajectories (2 per task type for ALFWorld, 1 for WebShop, 3 for TextCraft) and a task goal. The LLM first grounds each unstructured observation into a set of First-Order Logic predicate instances (e.g., is_open(fridge_1), contains(dresser_1, mug_1)), which then serve as the symbolic state for program execution. The output is a logic-grounded skill program — a directed workflow graph with typed nodes (DataOp, CheckOp, LoopOp, PrimitiveOp, TerminalOp) and a shared variable scope — that can be invoked by a planner with parameter bindings and returns either a success signal or a symbolic failure diagnosis.

## Method

NSI redefines skills as a triple (θ_ω, φ_ω, G_ω): invocation parameters, a neural grounding module (the LLM acting as a semantic perceptor that maps observations to FOL predicates), and a symbolic execution graph interpreted by a deterministic controller. Induction proceeds in two stages: intra-trajectory consolidation fits a local expert to each demonstration via iterative consistency checking and structural refinement, then inter-trajectory merging greedily unifies local experts into a global skill under a Feasibility Dominance criterion that strictly expands empirical coverage while minimizing program complexity (MDL). Four structural operators — Conditional Branching, Modular Crossover, Variable Lifting, and Loop Folding — enable the LLM synthesizer to invent discriminative predicates, graft subgraphs, abstract constants into parameters, and fold repetitions into loops. At deployment, a Reflective Planning loop converts runtime failures into permanent skill growth: the failure node emits a symbolic diagnosis, the planner generates a corrective trajectory, and successful recovery is grafted onto the failure node as a new conditional branch, incrementally expanding the skill's feasible region without altering correctly functioning subgraphs.

## Results

NSI achieves state-of-the-art performance across all three agentic benchmarks: 98.0% success rate on ALFWorld, 76.5% score / 44.5% success rate on WebShop, and 95.2% success rate on TextCraft. The largest margin is on WebShop, where NSI's 76.5% score surpasses the best baseline (AWM offline, 51.8%) by over 24 points. Ablation without online skill honing (NSI w.o. online honing) still outperforms all baselines on ALFWorld (93.5%) and WebShop score (58.8%), confirming the strength of the offline logic-grounded induction. NSI also demonstrates superior long-horizon robustness, sustaining valid execution up to 53+ steps where baselines collapse after ~22 steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NSI outperforms all six baselines on every benchmark. Against the strongest skill-induction baseline AWM (91.3% ALFWorld, 92.5% TextCraft), NSI gains +6.7 points on ALFWorld and +2.7 on TextCraft, while the gap widens dramatically on WebShop (76.5 vs. 49.2 score). Notably, ASI (parameterized programmatic skills) underperforms even the text-based AWM on WebShop (7.7 vs. 51.8 score), revealing that rigid linear scripts are less effective than unstructured text summaries when they cannot express conditional logic. Among prompting-based baselines, ReAct (85.8% ALFWorld) and StateAct (83.0% TextCraft) each lead on one domain but are consistently outperformed by NSI across the board. The ablation NSI w/o online honing (93.5% ALFWorld, 58.8% WebShop score) confirms that the offline trace-to-logic induction alone already surpasses all baselines, while the reflective planning and skill honing mechanism adds a further +4.5 points on ALFWorld and +17.7 on WebShop score.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the programmatic skill induction paradigm established by ASI (Wang et al., 2025b) and AWM (Wang et al., 2025c), which distill interaction trajectories into reusable but state-blind parameterized scripts. The key departure is a neuro-symbolic re-architecting of the skill representation: rather than linear action sequences with fixed parameters, NSI synthesizes explicit First-Order Logic predicates, conditional branching (CheckOp), dynamic variable binding (DataOp), and loop structures into a directed execution graph. The online skill honing mechanism also builds on SkillWeaver (Zheng et al., 2025a), but replaces ad-hoc trajectory replay with structured Logic Consolidation operators (Branching, Crossover, Lifting, LoopFold) that graft recovery subgraphs onto specific failure nodes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a concrete representational gap—state-blind scripts cannot encode the 'when' and 'why' of actions—making it a meaningful step toward robust, inspectable agentic skills that generalize beyond their training trajectories. The long-horizon collapse analysis (baselines failing past ~22 steps while NSI sustains to 50+) provides a clear mechanistic explanation for why logic-grounded compression helps. Open questions include the hand-crafted predicate bottleneck (the paper acknowledges this as a limitation), whether the approach transfers to multimodal or continuous-action settings, and whether the GPT-4o-dependent induction generalizes across model families. The reflective planning loop also raises questions about unbounded skill graph growth and when to prune versus extend.
