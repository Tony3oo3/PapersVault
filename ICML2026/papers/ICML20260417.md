## Problem

Discrete diffusion samplers, while recently adapted from continuous-space diffusion sampling, do not leverage the off-policy reinforcement learning techniques that have proven effective for continuous samplers, leading to poor mode coverage on highly multimodal targets. Additionally, the Schrödinger bridge problem in discrete spaces has only been addressed in the data-to-data setting, leaving the data-to-energy case (where one distribution is given only by an unnormalised energy) unexplored. The paper also identifies that discrete diffusion samplers have not been applied to posterior inference in the discrete latent spaces of pretrained generative models.

## Input / Output

The sampler takes a pointwise-queryable energy function E: S → ℝ over a discrete state space S = {1,…,C}^d (e.g., spin configurations on an L×L lattice, 8-bit Gray-coded discretisations of continuous densities, or VQ-VAE token sequences) and produces samples from p_target(x) ∝ exp(−E(x)) without knowledge of the normalising constant. For Schrödinger bridges, the input additionally includes samples from a source distribution p_0, and the output is a pair of forward/backward kernels defining a transport trajectory between p_0 and the energy-defined target. For latent-space posterior sampling, the input is a frozen VQ-VAE with an autoregressive prior p_latent(z) and a likelihood p(y|f(z)), and the output is decoded images f(z) drawn from the posterior p(z|y).

## Method

The core contribution is three off-policy training strategies for discrete diffusion samplers: (1) a replay buffer that stores terminal states X_N and resamples backward trajectories via the noising kernel, (2) an importance-weighted buffer that prioritises buffer entries by the trajectory importance weight w = [e^{−E(X_N)} ∏ p̄(X_n|X_{n+1})] / [p_0(X_0) ∏ p_θ(X_{n+1}|X_n)], and (3) MCMC exploration that refines buffer samples with a few steps of a target-specific MCMC kernel (e.g., Swendsen-Wang for Ising/Potts, Hamming-ball MH for synthetics) before training. Training uses second-moment objectives—Trajectory Balance (TB) or Log-Variance (LV)—that match the forward trajectory distribution p_0 ⊗ p_θ^{⊗N} to the backward distribution p_target ⊗ p̄^{⊗N}, with the off-policy choice of trajectory distribution P decoupled from the on-policy forward process. For data-to-energy Schrödinger bridges, the method extends iterative proportional fitting (IPF) to discrete spaces using a uniform diffusion reference process, alternately fitting the backward kernel by maximum likelihood (given source samples) and the forward kernel via a log-variance divergence (given only the target energy). The noising kernel is masked diffusion for sampling tasks and uniform diffusion for bridges, with multiple unmaskings per step allowed during training but single-step unmasking used at inference.

## Results

On the 16×16 Ising model at the hardest temperature (β=1.2), the proposed TB+Buffer+MCMC method achieves a Sinkhorn distance of 0.02±0.00 and correlation error of 0.00±0.00, while all on-policy methods (LV, TB) and the MDNS baseline suffer severe mode collapse (Sinkhorn ≈126–127, correlation error = 1.00). On the Potts model (q=3, β=1.2), TB+Buffer+MCMC achieves Sinkhorn 12.37±0.82 versus 156–162 for on-policy methods. On the discretised 40GMM (d=32), TB+Buffer+MCMC reduces Sinkhorn from ~2143 (on-policy TB) to 4.25±0.30, and is the only method that discovers all 40 modes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: a learning-free Metropolis-Hastings MCMC (1-Hamming-ball proposal) and MDNS (Zhu et al., 2025), the primary discrete diffusion sampler baseline using the WDCE loss. Against MDNS, the proposed off-policy methods (TB+Buffer+MCMC) consistently outperform on all metrics, with the gap widening dramatically at higher inverse temperatures: on Ising β=1.2, MDNS collapses to a single mode (Sinkhorn 126.27, correlation error 1.00) while TB+Buffer+MCMC achieves Sinkhorn 0.02 and correlation error 0.00. Against MH MCMC, the proposed methods match or slightly exceed the MCMC baseline on moderate-temperature settings (e.g., Ising β=0.44: Sinkhorn 46.22 vs. 48.47) and substantially outperform it on the hardest Potts setting (β=1.2: Sinkhorn 12.37 vs. 47.27). On the discretised 40GMM (d=32), TB+Buffer+MCMC achieves Sinkhorn 4.25 versus 349.31 for MDNS and 2142.65 for on-policy TB, and is the only learned method that recovers all 40 modes. The key finding is that off-policy training (importance-weighted buffer + MCMC exploration) is essential for mode coverage on multimodal targets, whereas on-policy training and MDNS both suffer mode collapse at low temperatures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of discrete diffusion samplers (MDNS, Holderrieth et al., Sanokowski et al.) and off-policy reinforcement learning for continuous-space diffusion samplers (Sendera et al., Choi et al.), transferring replay buffers, importance-weighted prioritisation, and MCMC exploration from the latter to the former. A key conceptual contribution is revealing that the single-unmasking discrete diffusion sampler is mathematically equivalent to the GFlowNet formulation of Zhang et al. (2022a), a connection the authors note 'has not been recognised until now.' For Schrödinger bridges, the paper extends the continuous data-to-energy IPF algorithm of Tamogashev & Malkin (2026) into discrete state spaces, and generalises the outsourced posterior sampling framework of Venkatraman et al. (2025) from continuous to discrete latent spaces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The off-policy techniques address a critical practical failure mode—mode collapse at low temperatures—where on-policy discrete samplers and even MDNS completely fail, while TB+Buffer+MCMC achieves near-perfect metrics. The first discrete data-to-energy Schrödinger bridge and the first application of discrete diffusion samplers to posterior inference in VQ-VAE latent spaces open new application avenues. However, the latent-space experiments are limited to MNIST with a small 16-token codebook, and the authors acknowledge that scaling to large image generative models (where continuous-space outsourced sampling has been successful) remains an open challenge. The need to fix a noising kernel and the computational cost of trajectory rollouts are further limitations that constrain practical adoption.
