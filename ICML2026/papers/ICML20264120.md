## Problem

Message-passing GNNs suffer from oversquashing, where long-range dependencies are compressed and lost through limited propagation channels. Recent spectral GNNs (e.g., S²GNN) provide theoretical lower bounds on Jacobian sensitivity that guarantee non-vanishing long-range influence, but these bounds rely on restrictive assumptions (identity feature transforms, zero spatial term, pure diagonal spectral filtering) that are not met by the actual implementations. The paper identifies this theory-practice gap empirically and asks whether effective OSQ mitigation can be achieved with a simpler, more practical architecture that still admits a distance-independent non-vanishing influence guarantee.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a graph's normalized adjacency matrix and per-node feature vectors, and produces updated node representations at each layer. The key implementation detail is that the global mixing operator P_θ is a block-constant matrix with entries α_{θ,r}/n_r within each connected component C_r and zero elsewhere, so it reduces to a per-component mean aggregation computable in O(Nd) without any eigendecomposition. Per-layer complexity is O(|E|d + Nd²), matching standard GCN, and the model is trained end-to-end with standard supervised losses (MAE, cross-entropy, F1) depending on the downstream task.

## Method

S³GNN replaces the expensive spectral filtering of S²GNN with a lightweight two-term update: H(ℓ+1) = H(ℓ) + ε(P_θ(ℓ)H(ℓ)Ŵ(ℓ) + ÂH(ℓ)Ŵ(ℓ)), where P_θ is a learnable scalar-reweighted projection onto the 0-eigenspace of the Laplacian (i.e., a fully-connected subgraph per connected component with uniform weights) and Â is the standard normalized adjacency. By restricting the spectral filter to g_θ(λ)=0 for λ>0, the global mixing term becomes a simple mean aggregation that requires no eigendecomposition, yet Proposition 1 proves a distance-independent lower bound on ∂h_i(ℓ)/∂x_s that scales as ∏(ε·α_{θ,r}^{(p)}/n_r · σ_min(W(p))). To prevent Jacobian energy blow-up in depth, the feature transformation Ŵ is constrained to be antisymmetric, and Proposition 2 shows this yields ‖J(ℓ)‖₂ = 1 + O(ε²), i.e., no exponential growth or decay per layer. The mixing coefficient α is learned end-to-end and empirically grows with depth, naturally strengthening long-range communication in deeper layers where it is most needed.

## Results

On the Long-Range Graph Benchmarks (LRGB), S³GNN achieves 73.20±0.14 AP on peptides-func and 0.2429±0.0014 MAE on peptides-struct, outperforming S²GNN (72.75, 0.2487) and Stable-ChebNet (70.32, 0.2542) without requiring eigendecomposition or positional encodings. On the Graph Property Prediction dataset, S³GNN yields an order-of-magnitude improvement on Eccentricity (−2.4801 vs. −1.4622 log₁₀(MSE) for S²GNN) and consistently leads on Diameter and SSSP. On large-scale OGB node classification, S³GNN reaches 76.38% on ogbn-arxiv and 81.90% on ogbn-proteins, surpassing Stable-ChebNet (75.42%, 79.89%) and graph transformer baselines such as SGFormer (79.53%) and Spexphormer (80.65%) on proteins.

## Baselines & Comparisons

S³GNN outperforms all 28 baselines across the evaluated tasks. On LRGB, it beats the closest spectral competitor S²GNN by 0.45 AP (peptides-func) and 0.0058 MAE (peptides-struct), and surpasses Stable-ChebNet by 2.88 AP and 0.0113 MAE, all without eigendecomposition or positional encodings that S²GNN requires. On Graph Property Prediction, S³GNN improves over Stable-ChebNet by 0.38 in log₁₀(MSE) on Eccentricity (−2.48 vs. −2.10) and over S²GNN by a full order of magnitude (−2.48 vs. −1.46). On OGB node classification, S³GNN leads Stable-ChebNet by 0.96% on arxiv and 2.01% on proteins, and outperforms graph transformers (SGFormer, Spexphormer, DIFFormer) on proteins by 1.37–4.21%. In the KGQA setting, replacing the reasoning GNN with S³GNN yields +10.8% F1 on WebQSP multi-hop and +11.1% on CWQ multi-hop over the original Reasoning GNN, and in some cases matches or exceeds the RAG-augmented variant. On cylinder flow, S³MGN reduces velocity RMSE by 43% (0.000333 vs. 0.000589) and test loss by 41% compared to the standard MeshGraphNet baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

S³GNN is directly motivated by S²GNN (Geisler et al., 2024), a spatio-spectral GNN that combines a learnable spectral filter with local message passing and provides a theoretical non-vanishing Jacobian lower bound for long-range influence. The paper's key departure is showing that this theoretical guarantee is not practically attainable by S²GNN's actual implementation (which uses Gaussian-smearing spectral filters and nonlinear gating), and that a much simpler dynamic—replacing the full spectral filter with a low-rank projection onto the 0-eigenspace (i.e., per-component mean aggregation) while retaining local message passing—recovers the essential global-mixing benefit at GCN-level complexity. For Jacobian stability, S³GNN adopts the antisymmetric weight constraint from Stable-ChebNet (Hariri et al., 2025), extending its applicability from purely spectral dynamics to the combined global-local setting. The paper also positions itself against spatial rewiring, graph transformers, virtual-node methods, and state-space models, arguing that an explicit low-rank global mixing term is a more lightweight and effective alternative to full attention or expensive eigendecomposition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of a theory-practice gap in S²GNN's OSQ guarantees is a useful corrective for the field: it cautions against treating theoretical lower bounds as practical performance predictors when the assumptions (identity feature transform, zero spatial term, pure diagonal filtering) are far from the actual architecture. Practically, S³GNN offers a drop-in replacement for the processor in existing GNN pipelines (demonstrated in KGQA, fluid dynamics, and topological interpolation) that requires no eigendecomposition and adds only O(Nd) per layer, making it attractive for large-scale graphs where spectral methods become infeasible. Open questions include whether the antisymmetric constraint is truly optimal versus orthogonal or free weights across different propagation schemes, how the method interacts with more sophisticated retrieval or attention mechanisms, and whether the learned α dynamics (positivity and growth with depth) can be formally guaranteed rather than merely observed empirically.
