## Problem

Ensemble inference faces a fundamental tradeoff: stacking all models maximizes accuracy but multiplies latency, while cascades reduce cost by exiting early but rely on single-model confidence without aggregating accumulated evidence. No prior method learns both the optimal model execution order and a calibrated aggregation mechanism at every intermediate step, leaving a gap between cascade-level efficiency and ensemble-level accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260770/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

LazyStack operates purely on probability vectors (p_j ∈ R^C) from pre-trained black-box models, requiring no architectural access or gradient flow through base models. At inference, it sequentially executes models according to a learned policy, feeding each model's probability output into a lightweight substacker that produces a calibrated probability distribution over classes; the final output is the argmax class label, with execution stopping when the substacker's max probability exceeds a calibrated threshold θ. The method handles heterogeneous ensembles spanning vision (32×32 to 224×224 images), text (up to 128 tokens), tabular features, and LLM multiple-choice queries (4-way answer token probabilities).

## Method

LazyStack unifies stacking and cascading through two jointly trained components. First, trajectory discovery formulates model sequencing as a Markov Decision Process over validation data, where states encode the executed-model set, discretized entropy, and predicted class; value iteration with a reward balancing correctness (β) against per-model cost (α·c_j) yields a policy that discovers 3–8 high-coverage trajectories (covering 95%+ of samples), revealing counterintuitive orderings such as starting with a moderately expensive model whose higher confidence enables earlier overall exit. Second, prefix meta-learning trains lightweight two-layer MLP substackers for every prefix of every discovered trajectory, reducing the aggregator count from 2^k to O(k) per trajectory; two variants exist—LazyStack-Sub (specialized MLP per prefix) and LazyStack-Mask (single shared MLP with a binary execution mask plus random-mask augmentation for calibration on unseen combinations). At test time, the system follows the MDP policy, aggregates all predictions seen so far via the appropriate substacker, and exits when calibrated confidence exceeds θ, achieving ensemble-quality predictions at cascade-level cost.

## Results

Across 8 datasets spanning vision, tabular, text, and LLM routing, LazyStack-Sub achieves up to 38× speedup while retaining 97%+ of full ensemble accuracy, averaging only 2–3 models executed per sample. On NSL-KDD (65× cost ratio), it reaches 76.8% accuracy at 38× speedup versus ABC's 75.7% at 5.1×. On LLM routing, it attains 77.2% on MMLU, 88.2% on ARC-Challenge, and 64.3% on HEADLINES, outperforming both ABC and FrugalGPT on all three. On vision, it retains 97% of full stacker accuracy (75.9% vs 78%) on CIFAR-100 at 2.5× speedup and achieves 82.4% at 2.7× on ImageNet-1K.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LazyStack-Sub outperforms ABC (Agreement-Based Cascading) on every dataset tested: on NSL-KDD it achieves 76.8% at 38× speedup versus ABC's 75.7% at 5.1× (7× faster and 1.1% more accurate), and on LLM routing it leads by 0.7–1.1% accuracy (e.g., 77.2% vs 76.5% on MMLU). Against FrugalGPT, LazyStack-Sub wins on all three LLM benchmarks (77.2% vs 75.8% on MMLU; 88.2% vs 86.8% on ARC-Challenge; 64.3% vs 62.1% on HEADLINES), with the gap largest on HEADLINES where semantic class patterns help substackers. RouteLLM is surpassed on LLM routing as LazyStack's progressive aggregation exceeds the single-model ceiling that pure routing is bounded by. On vision tasks, LazyStack-Sub matches or exceeds Gatekeeper (a white-box method with privileged model access) on both accuracy and speedup—on ImageNet-1K it achieves 82.4% at 2.7×, exceeding Gatekeeper's best 2-model configuration by 0.8% accuracy. Against Dekoninck et al.'s unified routing-and-cascading approach, LazyStack's progressive stacking surpasses the single-model ceiling that routing alone cannot exceed.

## Relation to Prior Work

LazyStack unifies two previously separate paradigms: stacked generalization (Wolpert, 1992), which trains a meta-learner over all model outputs, and cascade classifiers (Viola & Jones, 2001), which execute models sequentially and stop on single-model confidence. Prior cascade methods (Cost Cascade, FrugalGPT, ABC, Gatekeeper) share a critical limitation: they either use fixed cheapest-first orderings or rely on uncalibrated single-model confidence for exit decisions, ignoring the value of aggregating multiple predictions. LazyStack departs by formulating ordering as an MDP (learning non-obvious sequences from data) and by training prefix-specific substackers that aggregate all predictions seen so far at every step, enabled by the empirical finding that 95%+ of samples concentrate on only 3–8 trajectories despite 2^k possible paths.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: 2.5–38× speedup at 97%+ accuracy retention across vision, text, tabular, and LLM routing, with most samples requiring only 2–3 models regardless of ensemble size. The black-box requirement (only probability outputs needed) makes it broadly deployable without architectural access to base models. Open questions include whether trajectory concentration holds for highly heterogeneous or adversarially diverse model pools, whether class- or difficulty-dependent thresholds could close the remaining accuracy gap on hard classes, and how the method behaves under distribution shift between validation and test data. The MDP's discovery that a moderately expensive model should lead (contradicting cheapest-first) suggests broader implications for any sequential decision-making under cost constraints.
