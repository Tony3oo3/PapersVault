## Problem

Existing agentic medical reasoning methods (multi-agent debate, iterative retrieval, self-refinement) follow a blind scaling paradigm where increasing computational cost yields progressively smaller accuracy gains, as confirmed by a fitted logarithmic scaling curve (R²=0.996). The paper identifies that indiscriminate knowledge augmentation—adding knowledge graph paths or episodic memory to every query—can actually degrade performance below the zero-shot baseline due to knowledge noise and over-reasoning. The core gap is the absence of a mechanism to selectively activate different knowledge types based on what the LLM itself needs for each specific question, rather than applying a fixed pipeline to all inputs.

## Input / Output

The system takes a medical multiple-choice question with candidate options as primary input, and conditionally accesses two external resources: PrimeKG (4M+ biomedical relationships) for factual verification and an episodic case bank of 1,300–2,700 SCoT trajectories for experience replay. A key design detail is that KG retrieval is not performed on the raw question but on decomposed verification-hypothesis pairs (atomic query + LLM's hypothesis), which narrows the search space and reduces noise. The output is a structured pipeline: a 3-dimensional meta-cognitive state vector (complexity, familiarity, knowledge density, each 0–1), a selected strategy from a 5-element space, structural entity-relation paths, a step-by-step reasoning chain, and a final answer letter.

## Method

MedCoG consists of a Meta-Cognition Regulator (Monitoring → Planning → Evaluating) governing a Knowledge Executor with three knowledge types. In Monitoring, the LLM scores the question on complexity (multi-hop need), familiarity (typicality of the case), and knowledge density (dependency on specific facts). Planning implements a non-parametric threshold gate that maps the state vector to one of five strategies (Zero-Shot, SCoT, SCoT+Memory, SCoT+KG, SCoT+KG+Memory); for KG-activated samples, it additionally generates verification plans that decompose the question into atomic query-hypothesis pairs to constrain KG search. The Executor decouples SCoT into structural fact elicitation (entity-relation paths) followed by a reasoning chain, performs KG entity grounding via embedding similarity + shortest-path retrieval + question-level ranking, and retrieves similar cases from the episodic bank by embedding similarity. Evaluating checks whether retrieved KG paths are sufficient and allows one refinement cycle. The entire system is training-free, using GPT-4o as the backbone with thresholds calibrated on 50 held-out samples, and achieves 6.2× inference density by approximating the Oracle strategy selection (98.98% on MedQA-Full) at a fraction of the cost.

## Results

On five hard medical reasoning subsets (MedQA-H, MedMCQA-H, MMLU-H, MMLU-Pro-H, PubMedQA-H), MEDCOG-META achieves 37.5% average accuracy, outperforming the second-best baseline AFlow (34.5%) by 8.7%. It also attains the highest Inference Incremental Efficiency (IIE*) of 0.438, more than 3× that of AFlow (0.141), and a 6.2× Inference Density relative to the fitted logarithmic scaling curve of existing methods. On the full MedQA set, MEDCOG-META reaches 90.02% accuracy, while the Oracle upper bound reaches 98.98%, surpassing the then-SOTA o1 (96.52%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MEDCOG-META outperforms all eight baselines on average accuracy across the five hard sets. Against the strongest general-reasoning baseline AFlow (34.5% avg, IIE 0.141), MEDCOG-META gains +3.0 points in accuracy and 3.1× in IIE, with the largest per-dataset margins on MedQA-H (+4.0) and MMLU-Pro-H (+7.0). Self-Refine (31.2%, IIE 0.345) is the closest competitor on IIE but still trails MEDCOG-META by 6.3 points in accuracy. Notably, all three medical-specific agentic baselines—MedPrompt (23.8%), MedAgents (25.0%), and MDAgents (20.3%)—achieve negative IIE values, meaning their additional inference cost actually degrades accuracy below the CoT reference (28.0%), indicating that indiscriminate multi-agent or retrieval pipelines introduce harmful noise on hard cases. MEDCOG-META also consistently beats its own ablation MEDCOG-ALL (33.2%, IIE 0.181), confirming that meta-cognitive on-demand regulation is essential rather than simply stacking all knowledge types.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of agentic medical reasoning (MedAgents, MDAgents, MedPrompt), knowledge-augmented LLM reasoning (MedReason, MedGraphRAG), and emerging meta-cognitive regulation for LLMs (MetaRAG, KnowSelf, MCTR). It departs from the prevailing 'scale up the agent' paradigm by arguing that the bottleneck is not knowledge scope but per-sample strategy allocation, and introduces a training-free, threshold-gated meta-cognitive regulator that dynamically activates procedural (SCoT), factual (KG verification), and episodic (case bank) knowledge. Unlike prior meta-cognitive approaches that rely on iterative reactive correction (Self-Refine, Reflexion) or expensive SFT (KnowSelf, MCTR), MEDCOG performs pre-emptive, interpretable routing before any reasoning is executed, and it is the first to jointly regulate all three knowledge types under a unified monitoring framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that indiscriminate knowledge augmentation (KG, memory) can *degrade* performance below zero-shot on hard medical questions challenges a core assumption in agentic medical reasoning and motivates a shift toward on-demand, LLM-centric reasoning. The 6.2× inference density and the Oracle gap (98.98% upper bound vs. 52% achieved) highlight substantial headroom for meta-cognitive calibration, while the threshold-based gating remains a heuristic that may not generalize under distribution shift. Open questions include adaptive or learnable policy networks for regulation, knowledge evolution for lifelong medical agents, and whether the meta-cognitive monitoring quality (which varies substantially across backbones, e.g., Qwen3-8B collapsing on Knowledge Density) can be improved without per-backbone calibration.
