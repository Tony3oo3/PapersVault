## Problem

Standard RL post-training (PPO, GRPO) provides only O(1) bits of terminal feedback per episode, creating a severe credit assignment bottleneck for long-horizon tasks with dozens of intermediate decisions. Existing reflection mechanisms (Reflexion, Self-Refine) either operate solely at inference time or induce semantic drift when reflections are iteratively appended within a trajectory. The paper asks whether self-reflection can be internalized into the policy itself as a dense, token-level training signal without requiring external critics, separate reward models, or larger teacher models.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text prompt (task instruction) and, for agentic tasks, environment observations accumulated over multi-turn interactions; it produces a text completion (reasoning trajectory ending in an answer) or a sequence of environment actions. A key structural detail is the two-stage asymmetry: during training, the model first generates a compact 2–5 bullet-point reflection patch from a completed trajectory, which is prepended to the original prompt to form the teacher's conditioning context; at inference time, the learned policy operates on the bare prompt alone with no reflection overhead. The reflection patch is deliberately kept short (~90 tokens) to act as a stable prior rather than a verbose solution.

## Method

SRPO is a two-stage self-distillation framework where the same model π_θ serves as both student and teacher. In Stage 1 (Reflection-Guided State Augmentation), the model samples an initial rollout, receives a sparse terminal outcome, and generates a concise hindsight reflection patch p; this patch is prepended to the original prompt via a reset-with-memory mechanism (x̃ = [p; x]) to define a reflection-augmented teacher distribution π_T(·|x) := π_θ(·|[p;x]). In Stage 2 (On-Policy Self-Distillation), the student generates on-policy rollouts from the bare prompt x, and the teacher scores each student token via teacher-forcing under the privileged context [p;x], yielding a per-token reverse-KL reward r_t = log π_T(a_t|s_t) − log π_old(a_t|s_t). This converts O(1) bits of terminal supervision into O(T) bits of dense token-level signal, optimized with a PPO-style clipped objective and a trajectory-level baseline for advantage estimation. The reset-with-memory design (prepending rather than appending reflections) prevents the semantic drift that plagues iterative reflection methods, and the on-policy sampling eliminates exposure bias, while the mode-seeking reverse KL concentrates learning on the high-leverage forking points identified by reflection.

## Results

On AIME'24, SRPO achieves 73.3% (±1.4), outperforming GRPO (68.0%) by +5.3 points and the strongest external-teacher distillation baseline OPD-72B (72.5%) by +0.8 points, while using only 0.26× the training FLOPs of GRPO. On long-horizon agentic tasks, SRPO reaches 64.7% on WebShop, 76.8% on ALFWorld, and 31.2% on SWE-Bench-Lite, with the shortest average episode length (10.2 steps). Cross-domain transfer to GPQA Diamond (+4.7 over GRPO) and LogiQA 2.0 (+5.3) confirms gains generalize beyond mathematics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SRPO outperforms all eight baselines across every benchmark. Against the primary RL baseline GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), SRPO gains +5.3 on AIME'24 (73.3 vs. 68.0), +9.0 on MATH-500, and +13.5 on WebShop (64.7 vs. 51.2), with p < 0.005 (paired bootstrap). Against on-policy distillation from external teachers (Lu & Lab, 2025), SRPO beats the Qwen3-72B teacher by +0.8 on AIME'24 and +3.9 on the OOD DeepScaleR set (59.7 vs. 55.8), while requiring 9× fewer teacher FLOPs. Among reflection-trained baselines, SRPO surpasses R³L (Shi et al., 2026) by +1.8, SCoRe (Kumar et al., 2025) by +3.1, and RISE (Qu et al., 2024) by +3.5 on AIME'24. Inference-time methods Reflexion (Shinn et al., 2023) and Self-Refine (Madaan et al., 2023) trail SRPO by +6.6 and +8.3 respectively on AIME'24, and by +6.3 and +9.1 on WebShop. PPO (Schulman et al., 2017) is the weakest baseline at 55.3 on AIME'24, underscoring the credit-assignment bottleneck that SRPO's dense token-level supervision addresses.

## Relation to Prior Work

SRPO sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) RL-based post-training (PPO, GRPO), which provides the clipped policy-gradient optimization backbone; (2) self-reflection mechanisms (Reflexion, Self-Refine, SCoRe, RISE, R3L), which supply the idea of converting sparse outcomes into textual diagnostic guidance; and (3) on-policy distillation (Agarwal et al. 2024; Lu & Lab 2025), which provides the teacher-forced scoring framework. The key departure is the 'reset-with-memory' design—prepending a compact reflection patch to the original prompt rather than appending to a drifted context—and the reframing of the resulting reflection-conditioned distribution as a self-teacher whose per-token log-probabilities serve as dense O(T)-bit rewards in a PPO-style objective. Unlike SCoRe (two-turn RL), RISE (supervised fine-tuning on self-improvement trajectories), or R3L (local pivot-token repair), SRPO exposes the full reflection-augmented teacher distribution at every student-rollout token and internalizes it so that no reflection is needed at inference time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: SRPO achieves state-of-the-art results across 10 benchmarks in 5 domains while requiring ~3.8× fewer total FLOPs than GRPO and no external teacher model, making dense-supervision post-training accessible at 8B scale. The finding that a self-generated reflection teacher outperforms a 32B external teacher (attributed to capability alignment and lower teacher-student KL) challenges the assumption that distillation requires a strictly more capable model. Key limitations include dependence on verifiable outcome signals (ruling out open-ended generation), evaluation restricted to Qwen3 and Llama-3.1 families, and the small AIME'24 sample size (30 problems) that inflates per-seed variance. Open questions include whether the framework extends to multimodal reasoning, longer-horizon tool-use trajectories, and settings without binary correctness signals.
