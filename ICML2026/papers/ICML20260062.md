## Problem

Existing stochastic variational inequality (SVI) algorithms for min-max optimization rely on a bounded variance assumption that is violated by standard sampling schemes (e.g., row/column sampling) on unbounded domains, which are common in practice (e.g., linear programming reformulated as min-max). This paper provides the first complexity guarantees for constrained SVIs under the weaker assumption that variance may grow as the squared norm of the iterate, covering both monotone and structured nonmonotone problems satisfying the weak Minty VI condition.

## Input / Output

The algorithms consume unbiased stochastic oracle samples of the operator G whose variance satisfies E‖G̃(z)−G(z)‖² ≤ B²‖z−z₀‖²+σ², along with a proper convex regularizer r (or indicator of a constraint set C) and an initial point z₀. The output is an iterate z^out selected by uniform random index from the generated sequence, guaranteed to satisfy E[res(z^out)] ≤ ε, where res(z) = dist(0, (G+∂r)z) generalizes the gradient norm to the constrained case. For min-max problems, this residual reduces to the combined gradient norm √(‖∇ₓf‖²+‖∇ᵧf‖²).

## Method

The paper develops three complementary algorithms, all built on the forward-backward-forward (FBF) iteration of Tseng (2000), achieving Õ(ε⁻⁴) stochastic oracle complexity under the weak MVI assumption (wMVI) with nonmonotonicity parameter ρ. (1) A minibatched FBF with increasing batch sizes b_k = Θ(k log²k) handles ρ < 1/(12L) with deterministic per-iteration oracle counts. (2) An inexact Krasnoselskii-Mann iteration applied to the resolvent J_{η(G+∂r)}, with the proximal subproblem solved via a multilevel Monte Carlo (MLMC) estimator wrapping a stochastic FBF subsolver, achieves the best-known range ρ < 1/L. (3) A single-loop variance-reduced FBF combining the STORM estimator, Halpern anchoring (β_k = Θ(1/k)), and three stochastic oracle calls per iteration handles ρ < 1/(16L) without large mini-batches. The key analytical insight is that the FBF structure yields an inclusion (z_{k+1/2}, η⁻¹(z_k−z_{k+1}) + G(z_{k+1/2})−Ĝ(z_{k+1/2})) ∈ gra(G+∂r), which, combined with (wMVI), produces a recursion where the nonmonotonicity error term ρ‖·‖² can be absorbed by the negative progress terms when ρ is sufficiently small relative to L and the step size.

## Results

All three proposed algorithms achieve a stochastic first-order oracle (sfo) complexity of Õ(ε⁻⁴) for making the expected residual norm ≤ ε, matching the best-known complexity even under bounded variance. The key differentiator is the tolerated nonmonotonicity parameter ρ: the minibatching FBF (Thm. 2.2) handles ρ < 1/(12L), the MLMC-based inexact KM iteration (Thm. 3.1) achieves the best-known range ρ < 1/L, and the single-loop variance-reduced FBF with Halpern anchoring (Thm. 4.1) handles ρ < 1/(16L). All results hold under the relaxed variance assumption E‖G̃(z)−G(z)‖² ≤ B²‖z−z₀‖² + σ², which is satisfied by bilinear min-max problems with unbounded domains where bounded variance fails. Numerically, Alg. 1 converges on a counter-example where deterministic extragradient diverges, and Alg. 4 shows superior robustness to step-size tuning compared to Pethick et al. (2023).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Choudhury et al. (2023), the paper handles constrained problems (they are unconstrained only) and does not require knowledge of hard-to-compute solution quantities (E‖F̃(x⋆)‖², ‖z₀−z⋆‖²) for setting mini-batch sizes, while also extending the ρ range from 1/(2L) to 1/(12L) in the minibatching setting. Compared to Kotsalis et al. (2022), Boț et al. (2021), and Iusem et al. (2017), the paper extends all results from ρ = 0 (monotone) to ρ > 0 (nonmonotone), which is the primary theoretical advance. Against Alacaoglu et al. (2025), the paper uses the weaker Assumption 1 (standard L-Lipschitz) rather than Assumption 2 (expected Lipschitz with multi-point oracle access), though the single-loop result (Thm. 4.1) does require Assumption 2. Numerically, on the Gorbunov et al. (2023) counter-example with ρ = 1/(2L), Alg. 1 converges while deterministic extragradient diverges, demonstrating the practical benefit of the wider ρ range. Against Pethick et al. (2023) on unconstrained quadratic problems with Student's-t and Laplace noise, Alg. 4 remains stable and convergent over a broad range of step sizes γ, whereas Pethick et al.'s algorithm diverges for small γ, showing that Halpern anchoring provides meaningful robustness to noisy oracles.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the FBF (Tseng, 2000) and Halpern-anchored variance-reduction frameworks to the setting where the stochastic oracle variance grows with the iterate norm (Assumption 3) rather than being uniformly bounded. It builds directly on the inexact fixed-point/MLMC construction of Alacaoglu et al. (2024) for the weak Minty VI (wMVI) setting, the STORM-based single-loop method of Pethick et al. (2023), and the minibatched FBF analyses of Iusem et al. (2017) and Kotsalis et al. (2022), all of which previously required either bounded variance or bounded domains. The key departure is showing that the weaker variance condition (1.7) — which is trivially satisfied for linear operators but violated by standard row/column sampling on unbounded bilinear problems — is sufficient to recover the same Õ(ε⁻⁴) residual complexity, thereby unifying the monotone and nonmonotone (ρ > 0) cases under a single, realistic noise model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first set of complexity guarantees for constrained nonmonotone SVI without the bounded variance assumption, closing a gap that made standard bilinear min-max problems with unbounded domains (e.g., linear programming via Lagrangian duality) intractable under prior theory. The three complementary algorithms (minibatched FBF, MLMC-based inexact KM, and single-loop STORM+Halpern) offer practitioners a menu of trade-offs between deterministic per-iteration cost, single-loop simplicity, and the tolerable range of nonmonotonicity ρ. A key open question the authors highlight is whether a unified single-loop method can simultaneously achieve the best ρ range (ρ < 1/L), avoid large mini-batches, and maintain Õ(ε⁻⁴) complexity; additionally, whether the ε⁻⁴ rate is fundamental for wMVI problems even under bounded variance remains unresolved.
