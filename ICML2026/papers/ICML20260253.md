## Problem

As AI model hubs grow to host thousands of pre-trained models, selecting the right model for a given query before inference becomes a scaling and adaptation challenge: existing routing methods assume a fixed candidate pool or rely on post-inference comparison, and none address the non-stationary nature of hubs where new experts are continuously added. This paper formalises the setting as Continual Model Routing (CMR), a class-incremental classification problem where the label space expands over time, and introduces CMRBench, a four-experience benchmark spanning over 2,000 candidate models to evaluate routers under realistic hub expansion.

## Input / Output

The router takes a natural-language text query (a user instruction describing a task) and outputs a single model identifier in Hugging Face owner/repo_id format, selected purely by embedding similarity without executing any candidate model. At inference time, the query is embedded and scored against a fixed-size candidate set (default K=64) drawn from a persistent registry of 2,000+ model embeddings; the highest-scoring model-ID is returned. The system is evaluated at three granularities—exact model-ID match, model-family match (grouping variants like yolov8m/n/s), and domain match—alongside a forgetting metric tracking backward transfer across sequential experiences.

## Method

CARvE routes by comparing a query embedding (produced by a frozen LLaMA2-7B backbone with LoRA adapters, pooled and projected through a learned matrix W, then L2-normalised) against a continually updated registry of per-model-ID embeddings using temperature-scaled cosine similarity over fixed-size candidate sets, avoiding a full softmax over the entire hub. Continual adaptation is achieved through three mechanisms: (i) checkpoint-based asymmetric anchoring that penalises cosine drift in previously seen model embeddings and MSE drift in the projection matrix relative to a snapshot from the prior experience, while leaving newly introduced model embeddings unanchored; (ii) domain-stratified coreset replay using farthest-point sampling to maintain diverse, domain-balanced rehearsal under heavy-tailed model distributions; and (iii) periodic hard-negative mining that refreshes confusable candidates within domains as the hub expands. Model embeddings are initialised randomly rather than from model-card text, since card-based initialisation was found to encode linguistic similarity rather than routing discriminability, degrading accuracy by 3–5 percentage points.

## Results

On CMRBench (four sequential experiences, >2,000 candidate models), CARvE at 10% domain replay achieves 80.7% domain accuracy with only 5.9% domain forgetting, compared to 75.9% D-Acc and 13.1% D-Fgt for standard random replay at the same budget. At 20% replay, CARvE reaches 82.9% D-Acc and 46.4% model-ID accuracy with 3.0% D-Fgt, surpassing LoRA joint training (79.3% D-Acc) while operating under the continual constraint. CARvE also outperforms the non-continual from-scratch upper bound (85.7% D-Acc) by only 2.8pp in D-Acc while requiring ~47% fewer TFLOPs and ~61% fewer GPU GB-hours.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CARvE substantially outperforms all retrieval and controller baselines: BGE-M3 retrieval-only achieves only 13.6% M-Acc and 44.0% D-Acc, Gorilla RAG scores 6.7% M-Acc and 43.0% D-Acc, and HuggingGPT (Qwen3-32B) reaches 51.7% D-Acc—each far below CARvE's 80.7% D-Acc at 10% replay. Among continual learning baselines, CARvE (10% replay) improves D-Acc by 4.8pp over random replay (80.7% vs. 75.9%) while halving domain forgetting (5.9% vs. 13.1%); it also beats EWC (66.2% D-Acc, 31.4% D-Fgt) and LwF (56.4% D-Acc, 39.5% D-Fgt) by 14.5pp and 24.3pp in D-Acc respectively. Model merging via TIES performs worst among learned methods (28.6% D-Acc, 32.6% D-Fgt), confirming that parameter-space merging is ill-suited to the expanding label space. Against the non-continual upper bounds, CARvE at 20% replay (82.9% D-Acc) closes the gap to joint training CARvE (84.6%) to within 1.7pp and to from-scratch CARvE (85.7%) to within 2.8pp, while using ~47% fewer TFLOPs than from-scratch retraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes continual learning machinery—replay buffers, parameter anchoring, and contrastive objectives—for the model routing problem, which prior work (Gorilla, HuggingGPT, MLLMTool, Routoo) treats as a one-shot classification or retrieval task over a fixed candidate pool. The method CARvE draws on class-incremental learning (checkpoint-based anchoring, structured replay) and contrastive embedding retrieval (temperature-scaled cosine scoring over a candidate set), but combines them in a way specifically designed for a growing label space of model identifiers. The benchmark CMRBench extends APIBench and ToolMMBench by adding a temporally structured HuggingBench component, shifting the evaluation from static routing to sequential hub expansion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CMRBench provides the first large-scale, temporally structured benchmark for model routing (2,000+ models across four experiences), filling a gap where prior routing benchmarks assumed a fixed candidate pool. The key open limitation is that CARvE cannot route zero-shot to newly added models without a continual training step, and the frozen backbone constrains long-horizon adaptation; the authors note that periodic backbone updates and self-supervised objectives are needed for truly open-ended hub growth. A practical open question is whether the embedding-registry approach scales to the millions of models now on Hugging Face, and whether hybrid pre-/post-inference routing (predict domain, then re-rank a small candidate set) can close the remaining gap to from-scratch training.
