## Problem

Existing test-time reward optimization methods for diffusion-based protein design suffer from three critical limitations in multi-objective settings: they operate memorylessly (repeating design errors across iterations), rely exclusively on high-reward samples while ignoring failure signals that define functional boundaries, and use naive scalarization (e.g., weighted summation) that introduces dominant-objective bias and fails to capture complex biophysical trade-offs. This paper addresses how to enable flexible, training-free adaptation to shifting multi-functional protein design targets without the prohibitive cost of retraining.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained masked diffusion model (EvoDiff 38M), a target protein length L, and D black-box reward functions (e.g., hydrophobicity, pLDDT, globularity, surface exposure, ss-match, cRMSD) as input. At each denoising step, a single random position is edited from a candidate pool of C=20 variants, with the full sequence re-evaluated by all D reward functions after each edit. The output is a set of Pareto-optimal protein amino acid sequences that simultaneously satisfy multiple conflicting biophysical constraints, with the method operating entirely at test time without any parameter updates to the base model.

## Method

MoMST transforms iterative test-time refinement into a memory-aware evolutionary process through three coupled mechanisms. First, a dual-tier Memory Bank (50% elite retention for long-term high-reward patterns, 50% sliding-window exploration buffer for recent dynamics) archives historical sequences with their multi-objective reward vectors, preventing repetitive errors by maintaining both global optima and local search context. Second, Self-Contrastive Learning computes residue-level preference signals by taking the log-ratio of empirical amino acid distributions from high-reward (positive) versus low-reward (negative) sequences in the memory bank, then modulates the pre-trained diffusion distribution via p^M = p^pre · exp(λ·s^M), pulling generation toward historically successful residues and away from failure-linked ones. Third, an Inference-time Pareto Alignment strategy combines Pareto frontier filtering (eliminating dominated solutions), elite preservation (merging current non-dominated sets with historical elites), and Pareto sampling that maximizes the minimum normalized objective to ensure balanced realization of all functional goals. The paper provides a theoretical guarantee (Theorem 4.1) showing that as the memory bank grows sufficiently large with adequate coverage, the guided sampling distribution converges in total variation distance to the entropy-regularized reward-optimal distribution.

## Results

On single-objective tasks, MoMST achieves the best P50/P95 across all four reward metrics (e.g., hydrophobicity P50 = −0.2242 vs. RERD's −0.3812; cRMSD P50 = 0.9109 vs. RERD's 0.9577). On the most challenging triple-objective task (hydrophobicity + globularity + pLDDT), MoMST reaches pLDDT P50 = 0.7657 while RERD collapses to 0.4187, and MoMST's pTM of 0.5454 is nearly 4× RERD's 0.1446 on the hydrophobicity + surface exposure + pLDDT task. MoMST also maintains the highest log-likelihood (LL) scores, confirming that reward gains do not come at the cost of sequence naturalness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoMST outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric across single-, dual-, and triple-objective tasks. Against the single-shot methods SMC (Wu et al., 2023) and SVDD (Li et al., 2024), MoMST gains are consistent but moderate on single-objective tasks (e.g., hydrophobicity P50: −0.2242 vs. −0.6246/−0.5386) and become dramatic in multi-objective settings where single-shot sampling cannot locate the narrow intersection of multiple high-reward regions. Against the memoryless iterative baseline RERD (Uehara et al., 2025a), MoMST's advantage is most pronounced: on the triple-objective hydrophobicity + globularity + pLDDT task, RERD suffers objective collapse (pLDDT P50 drops to 0.4187, below the 0.5 foldability threshold), while MoMST maintains pLDDT P50 = 0.7657 and pTM = 0.6439. The GA baseline (Hie et al., 2022) is consistently the weakest, confirming that discrete mutation without reward-guided denoising is insufficient for complex biophysical constraints. MoMST also achieves the best AvgRank (1.00–1.71) across all eight tasks in Table 7, confirming near-universal superiority.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the iterative test-time refinement paradigm of RERD (Uehara et al., 2025a) by introducing a memory-aware mechanism that distills both successful and failed trajectories into residue-level preference signals via self-contrastive learning. It draws conceptual inspiration from memory-augmented agent architectures (Ouyang et al., 2025) and applies them to the protein design setting, where the non-differentiable, black-box nature of biophysical reward functions makes gradient-based guidance infeasible. The key departure from prior test-time scaling methods (SMC, SVDD, RERD) is the dual-tier memory bank combined with Pareto alignment, which explicitly addresses the multi-objective balancing problem that linear weighted summation in RERD fails to resolve.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, multi-objective formulation is practically significant for therapeutic protein design where multiple conflicting biophysical constraints (stability, solubility, immunogenicity) must be satisfied simultaneously without retraining for each new target combination. The pTM results—nearly 4× improvement over RERD in the triple-objective setting—suggest that memory-aware contrastive guidance can prevent the reward hacking that plagues naive scalarization approaches. However, the evaluation is limited to a single 38M-parameter base model (EvoDiff) and a fixed set of structural reward functions; open questions include whether the approach scales to larger generative backbones, more diverse biological objectives (e.g., binding affinity, catalytic activity), and whether the memory bank's fixed 50/50 elite/exploration split generalizes across problem regimes.
