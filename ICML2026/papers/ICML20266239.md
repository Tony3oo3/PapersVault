## Problem

In centralized multi-agent systems, the Orchestrator is responsible for roughly 68% of all task failures, yet existing benchmarks are process-opaque (providing only initial queries and final answers) and offer no formal mechanism to explain why orchestration collapses under long-horizon planning. The paper asks whether the orchestration process can be captured by a physically interpretable dynamical model that quantifies the competing forces of task resolution and cumulative context loading, and whether the resulting parameters predict orchestrator performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266239/cropped_page6_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The entropy dynamics model consumes per-step scheduling probability vectors (p_k) from a 7-executor MAS running up to 20 steps, fitting a 6-parameter continuous-time equation over a 6-step observation window chosen to precede the observed entropy-saturation regime. The IWG pipeline takes seed QA pairs and a target MAS configuration (tool sets, capability descriptions) as input and produces 679 process-verifiable task instances with dense intermediate checkpoints and deterministic string-match or API-based verification logic. Outputs include both fitted physical parameters (A_task, γ, ω, β, H_0) that characterize each orchestrator's behavioral regime and a two-tier metric suite (system-level TS and LCS-F1; orchestrator-level Step-SR, EH-F1, Faithfulness, Consistency).

## Method

The core contribution is a Mean-Field Entropy Dynamics model that decomposes the orchestrator's macroscopic scheduling entropy into two physically grounded operators: a Focusing Operator derived from Nesterov accelerated gradient dynamics (yielding a damped harmonic oscillator for task resolution) and a Dispersion Operator derived from Fokker-Planck diffusion of the probability wave packet (yielding a logarithmic context-accumulation term). Discrete scheduling vectors are reinterpreted as stroboscopic samples of a continuous probability field over the executor space, enabling a mean-field approximation that collapses the high-dimensional stochastic multi-agent interaction into a single scalar ODE of the form H(t) = A_task·e^(−γt)·sin(ωt+φ) + β·ln(t+1) + H_0. To supply the dense step-level data required for fitting, the paper introduces Inverse Workflow Generation (IWG), a multi-agent pipeline where a Scout agent performs capability-aware inverse planning from target answers, a Wrapper agent synthesizes executable environments with deterministic checkpoints, and a three-tier Validation Committee (open-source model, proprietary model, human expert) enforces solvability and factual correctness. Validation across 22 LLMs reveals two behavioral archetypes (Oscillatory-Aggressive vs. Smooth-Conservative) and a counterintuitive 'Reasoning Trap' in which heavy-thinking models underperform as orchestrators because self-generated Chain-of-Thought tokens saturate the attention budget and squeeze out external context signals.

## Results

Across 24 LLM orchestrators evaluated on 679 IWG-generated tasks, Gemini-2.5-Pro achieves the highest Task Success rate (44.14%) and LCS-F1 (81.28%), while Claude-4.5-Sonnet leads in Step-SR (60.83%) and Exception Handling F1 (97.19%). The entropy dynamics fitting (Table 4) reveals two distinct behavioral archetypes: Oscillatory-Aggressive models (DeepSeek-V3.1 with ω=3.42, Qwen-3-Max with A_task=7.65) show high initial exploration but suffer precipitous accuracy collapse at Step 2, while Smooth-Conservative models (Claude-4.5-Sonnet with β=0.87, Gemini-2.5-Pro with γ=0.35) maintain stable performance through context accumulation. Critically, the 'Reasoning Trap' shows that suppressing overthinking yields substantial gains: GPT-5-Low improves Step-SR from 0.251 to 0.302 (69% token reduction), and Qwen3-Max-NoThink boosts Step-SR to 0.413 versus the standard variant.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks 24 LLMs (13 proprietary, 11 open-source) as central orchestrators in a 7-executor MAS, comparing them along two behavioral archetypes derived from entropy dynamics fitting. Smooth-Conservative models (Gemini-2.5-Pro, Claude-4.5-Sonnet) dominate on task completion: Gemini-2.5-Pro achieves the best TS (44.14%) and LCS-F1 (81.28%), while Claude-4.5-Sonnet leads in fine-grained orchestration quality (Step-SR 60.83%, EH-F1 97.19%, Orchestrator Avg 77.18). Oscillatory-Aggressive models (DeepSeek-V3.1, Qwen-3-Max) achieve high structural similarity (LCS-F1 80.12% and 49.96% respectively) but suffer a precipitous accuracy collapse at Step 2 (DeepSeek drops from 92% to 22%), confirming that rapid hypothesis switching exceeds the orchestrator's epistemic threshold. The most striking comparison is the 'Reasoning Trap': reasoning-heavy variants (GPT-5-High, Qwen3-Max) underperform their light-thinking counterparts (GPT-5-Low: Step-SR 0.302 vs 0.251; Qwen3-Max-NoThink: Step-SR 0.413), with GPT-5-Low reducing token consumption by ~69% while improving accuracy. Among open-source models, DeepSeek-V3.1 (LCS-F1 80.12%) and Llama4-maverick (TS 36.14%) are the strongest, but all trail the top proprietary models by 10–15 points on Task Success. The entropy parameters (Table 4) provide a physically interpretable explanation: low β (context sensitivity, e.g., Claude 0.87 vs DeepSeek 1.37) and moderate ω (exploration frequency) correlate with sustained accuracy, while high A_task and ω predict early-step collapse.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes three distinct theoretical tools—mean-field games (Lasry & Lions, 2007), the damped-oscillator limit of Nesterov accelerated gradient (Su et al., 2016), and Fokker-Planck diffusion in probability-measure space (Wibisono, 2018)—to construct a unified entropy-dynamics model for LLM orchestrator behavior, a framing absent from prior MAS work. It positions itself within the centralized/hierarchical MAS lineage (MetaGPT, AutoGen, Magentic-One, WebPilot, AgentOrchestra) but shifts the analytical focus from agent capabilities to the orchestrator's macroscopic scheduling dynamics. The Inverse Workflow Generation pipeline departs from forward-execution data collection (GAIA, AssistantBench) by synthesizing environments backward from verified answers, enabling dense step-level entropy observation that prior process-opaque benchmarks cannot provide.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'Reasoning Trap' finding—that reasoning-heavy models (GPT-5-High, Qwen3-Max) underperform their lighter counterparts as orchestrators due to context squeezing—challenges the prevailing assumption that more internal reasoning improves orchestration, with direct implications for model selection in production MAS deployments. The entropy-dynamics parameters (A_task, γ, ω, β) offer physically interpretable diagnostics for system stability, potentially guiding architectural choices (e.g., context window sizing, agent granularity) before full system deployment. Open questions include whether the 5-parameter model generalizes beyond 7-agent topologies, whether the parameters can predict performance under distribution shift, and whether the Reasoning Trap persists as context windows and attention mechanisms improve. The IWG benchmark, while process-verifiable, remains limited to 679 instances and a fixed agent set, constraining its utility as a general-purpose evaluation standard.
