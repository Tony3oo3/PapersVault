## Problem

Existing LLM routers implicitly assume each LLM has a single fixed quality-cost profile per query, treating models as static points in the quality-cost space. This causes routers to exclude powerful LLMs whenever their estimated cost exceeds the budget, overlooking that the same LLM's quality varies with output length and that a constrained powerful model can outperform a weaker one at comparable cost. No prior routing dataset or method captures this intra-model, cost-dependent quality variation.

## Input / Output

Given a text query, a user-defined trade-off coefficient λ ∈ [0,1], and an optional budget limit B, R2-ROUTER outputs an optimal (LLM, token budget) pair (M*, b*). The token budget is not merely a cost estimate but an actionable constraint: the router appends a length-constrained instruction ("use at most b* tokens") to the prompt of the selected LLM, making the budget a controllable input rather than a predicted outcome. Quality predictions are produced across K=16 discrete anchor budgets per LLM, forming a per-LLM quality-cost curve from which the optimal operating point is selected.

## Method

R2-ROUTER replaces point-based model selection with curve-based search over (LLM, budget) pairs. A shared encoder (Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B) maps the query to a 1024-d embedding, which feeds K independent 3-layer MLP heads per LLM, each predicting quality at one anchor token budget via a Sigmoid output. The decision maker then exhaustively maximizes (1−λ)·Q̂(x,M,b) − λ·C(b) over all LLMs and budgets, and optionally applies PCHIP interpolation between anchors to approximate a continuous quality-cost curve with as few as 6–8 trained heads. Training is supervised MSE on R2-BENCH, which records multiple quality-scored responses per (query, LLM) under different length constraints, so the router learns each model's actual quality-cost curve rather than assuming a single operating point. A theoretical result (Theorem 4.3) guarantees that the reasoning search space strictly contains the reactive search space, formalizing why curve-based routing dominates point-based routing.

## Results

R2-ROUTER achieves state-of-the-art routing performance at 4–5× lower total inference cost compared to all reactive baselines. On R2-BENCH, it reaches an average quality of 0.8 at a cost of approximately $0.5×10^{-3}$, whereas reactive baselines require 4–5× that budget to match. On the RouterArena benchmark (exact-match scoring, no LLM judge), R2-ROUTER leads the strongest baseline (CARROT-L) across all 7 task categories with margins of +4.1 to +8.1 points. On out-of-distribution queries (MMLU-Pro STEM→non-STEM), R2-ROUTER attains AUDC 0.71 and QNC 0.26, versus 0.67/0.56 for CARROT-L and 0.63/0.72 for MIRT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MIRT-IRT and NIRT-IRT (Song et al., 2025), R2-ROUTER wins decisively on all metrics: on OOD queries it improves AUDC from 0.63 to 0.71 and cuts QNC from 0.72 to 0.26, reflecting that IRT-based routers treat cost as a fixed per-LLM constant and cannot exploit budget-constrained configurations. Against CARROT-KNN and CARROT-Linear (Somerstep et al., 2025), R2-ROUTER again dominates: AUDC 0.71 vs. 0.67 and QNC 0.26 vs. 0.56 on OOD, and on RouterArena it leads CARROT-L by +4.1 to +8.1 points across all 7 categories (e.g., Math: 76.0 vs. 67.9; Code: 69.7 vs. 62.0). Against UniRouter (Jitkrittum et al., 2025) in the dynamic-pool generalization setting, the integrated UNI-R2ROUTER achieves AUDC 0.623 vs. 0.590 for vanilla UniRouter while reducing QNC by 80%, confirming that the curve-based reasoning module is a drop-in improvement that preserves UniRouter's ability to handle unseen LLMs without retraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM routing literature (FrugalGPT, CARROT, MIRT-IRT, UniRouter) by introducing output length as a joint decision variable alongside model selection, building on the length-constrained instruction mechanism from Lee et al. (2025) as the controllability primitive. Prior routers treat each LLM as a fixed quality-cost point per query; R2-ROUTER reframes this as a quality-cost curve, expanding the search space from a set of points to the Cartesian product of LLMs and token budgets. The theoretical dominance result (Theorem 4.3) formalizes why this expanded space strictly subsumes reactive routing, and the plug-in integration with UniRouter demonstrates orthogonality to existing quality-prediction methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 4–5× cost reduction at comparable quality is practically significant for LLM deployment at scale, and the lightweight training (30 min on one GPU, <400 ms inference overhead) makes the approach broadly adoptable. R2-BENCH, the first routing dataset capturing quality-cost curves across 16 token budgets and 15 LLMs, provides a new evaluation standard that prior single-response benchmarks cannot support. Open questions include whether the paradigm extends to other controllable variables (system prompts, decoding strategies, reasoning depth), how it performs with proprietary models that do not expose token budgets, and whether the LLM-as-a-judge quality scoring introduces systematic biases that affect routing decisions.
