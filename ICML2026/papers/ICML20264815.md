## Problem

Current safety alignment for large reasoning models embeds explicit safety rules directly into chain-of-thought training data, creating a rigid association between rule memorization and refusal that causes over-refusal on benign queries. This approach conflates the act of retrieving relevant safety guidelines with the act of reasoning about whether a violation actually occurs, preventing the model from dynamically assessing risk and selectively consulting rules only when needed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264815/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a user prompt and has access to an external safety policy document (107 terms across 21 categories such as violence, privacy, biosafety) via a retrieval tool. It produces a multi-turn ReAct-style trajectory in which it may issue zero or more tool calls to fetch relevant policy clauses, followed by a final compliant or refusal response. The tool call format follows the Qwen-agent MCP specification, and the model must generate thinking tokens before and after each retrieval to maintain a valid agentic loop.

## Method

ASCL formulates safety alignment as a multi-turn tool-use process where the model autonomously decides whether to invoke a safety-policy retrieval tool before generating its answer, decoupling rule access from the reasoning chain. Training proceeds in two stages: behavior cloning on ~3.5k distilled samples (harmful, sensitive-with-retrieval, and sensitive-without-retrieval in a 2:3:2 ratio) to establish the retrieval behavior, followed by RL with a composite reward (safety, compliance, hallucination, retrieval, and format penalties). To counteract the RL-induced bias toward excessive rule consultation, IFPO reweights group-level advantages by the inverse frequency of each action type (tool-use vs. direct response) raised to a temperature τ, upweighting underrepresented actions and downweighting overrepresented ones so that policy updates are decoupled from sampling frequency. This prevents the policy from collapsing into a high-frequency tool-calling pattern while preserving safety performance.

## Results

ASCL with IFPO achieves the best safety-utility trade-off across all three model scales (Qwen3-4B/8B/14B). On safety benchmarks, IFPO reaches 98.87%, 98.98%, and 98.64% average across 4B, 8B, and 14B respectively, while simultaneously reducing over-refusal to 6.00%, 4.33%, and 5.84%—substantially lower than all baselines. On general reasoning benchmarks (MATH-500, GSM8K, GPQA-Diamond, MMLU-Pro, ARC-Challenge), IFPO maintains competitive performance with a harmonic average of 89.82% (4B), 91.09% (8B), and 91.43% (14B), confirming that the safety alignment does not degrade core reasoning capabilities.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SafeChain (Jiang et al., 2025), IFPO achieves a decisive advantage on safety: 98.87% vs. 65.83% average on Qwen3-4B (a 33-point gap), while over-refusal is comparable or slightly better (6.00% vs. 8.23%). Against STAR-1 (Wang et al., 2025c), IFPO wins on both axes—safety improves from 90.23% to 98.87% and over-refusal drops from 22.03% to 6.00% on 4B—indicating that STAR-1's rule-memorization approach trades utility for safety. Even the strengthened STAR-1-mix variant (which adds all benign data to reduce over-refusal) underperforms IFPO on both metrics at 4B and 8B scales (e.g., 83.10% safety / 18.18% over-refusal vs. 98.87% / 6.00%). The paper's own BC stage already surpasses STAR-1 by over 5% on safety, and IFPO further reduces over-refusal by roughly 10 percentage points relative to BC, confirming that the inverse-frequency reweighting is the critical component for closing the safety-utility gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant safety-alignment paradigm for reasoning models (Deliberate Alignment, SafeChain, STAR-1, TARS), which embeds explicit safety rules into chain-of-thought training data via context distillation and then fine-tunes the model to internalize them. Instead, ASCL reformulates safety alignment as a multi-turn tool-use process built on the ReAct loop, placing safety rules in an external retrievable document that the model consults only when it judges a query to be potentially risky. On the RL side, IFPO extends GRPO by introducing an inverse-frequency importance weight on group-level advantages, specifically targeting the distributional bias that causes tool-use policies to over-sample retrieval trajectories during optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central empirical finding—that zero-shot access to safety rules as a retrievable tool already achieves near-SOTA safety performance (92–97% across benchmarks) without any fine-tuning—challenges the assumption that expensive rule-embedded CoT distillation is necessary for safety alignment, suggesting the field may be over-investing in memorization-based approaches. However, the post-training gains from BC+IFPO remain modest and the method shows no clear transfer to general reasoning tasks (Table 2), leaving open whether the agentic safety framework can scale to more complex or multi-modal settings. The IFPO mechanism, while effective, is specific to the binary tool-use/non-tool-use action split and may not generalize to richer tool-use taxonomies.
