## Problem

Noise schedules in pixel diffusion models are typically handcrafted as global curves (cosine, linear) that apply the same noise progression to every image, resulting in many steps where the noise level is either excessive or insufficient relative to the signal's spectral content. This inefficiency is particularly costly in the low-step regime, where pixel diffusion already lags behind latent diffusion models. The paper asks whether per-instance schedules derived from each image's power spectrum can eliminate redundant steps and improve generative quality.

## Input / Output

During training, the method takes a clean RGB image (128×128, 256×256, or 512×512) and a class label or text prompt, computing the image's radially-averaged power spectral density (RAPSD) on the fly to determine its noise schedule. At inference, a lightweight GMM sampler predicts two spectral parameters (α, β) from the conditioning, which fully specify the per-instance schedule before any denoising begins. The output is a generated RGB image, with the model additionally conditioned on the minimum and maximum logSNR of the schedule via FiLM layers.

## Method

The core contribution is deriving per-instance noise schedules from the image's RAPSD, which for natural images follows a power law Ψ(k) ≈ βk^α. The authors derive theoretical bounds on the minimum and maximum effective noise levels by analyzing how noise at a given frequency interacts with the signal's power at that frequency, parameterized by κ_min and κ_max (kept constant across all resolutions). Between these bounds, they define three schedule variants—frequency-focused (uniform frequency coverage), power-focused (weighted by spectral energy), and a mixed schedule that averages the two—and show the mixed schedule best balances coarse structure and fine detail. At inference, a small GMM sampler (a linear layer for class-conditional, an MLP for text-to-image) predicts (α, β) from the conditioning, enabling the schedule to be computed before sampling. The backbone is the SiD2 U-ViT architecture with two modifications: additional FiLM conditioning on the schedule's min/max logSNR, and classifier-free guidance intervals defined in t-space rather than logSNR-space to accommodate per-instance schedule shapes.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 class-conditional generation, the proposed method (flop heavy) achieves FID 1.32 with 256 NFE, outperforming SiD2 (FID 1.37, 512 NFE) while using half the denoising steps. At 512×512, the small model reaches FID 1.79 with 256 NFE versus SiD2's 2.19 at 512 NFE. On zero-shot text-to-image (MS-COCO 512×512), the method achieves FID 7.08 with 96 NFE, beating the SiD2 baseline (FID 7.56 at 160 NFE). The advantage is most pronounced in the low-step regime, where the gap widens significantly as NFE decreases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against SiD2 (Hoogeboom et al., 2025), the same architecture and training protocol with only the noise schedule changed. The proposed method outperforms SiD2 on FID at all three resolutions while using roughly half the denoising steps: FID 1.42 vs 1.68 (small, 256×256) and 1.32 vs 1.37 (flop heavy, 256×256), with the gap widening at lower NFE. Against other single-stage pixel diffusion models, the method achieves substantially better FID: 1.42 vs 1.98 (PixelFlow-XL), 1.42 vs 2.15 (PixNerd-XL/16), and 1.42 vs 1.61 (PixelDiT-XL) at 256×256, all with comparable or fewer parameters. JIT-G (Li & He, 2025) reports a slightly better FID (1.78 vs 1.79 at 512×512) but uses 2B parameters and does not report NFE, making direct comparison difficult. On zero-shot text-to-image (MS-COCO 512×512), the method achieves FID 7.08 at 96 NFE, outperforming both SiD2 (7.56 at 160 NFE) and SiD (9.6 at 256 NFE). On unconditional ImageNet 256×256, the method improves FID from 17.2 (SiD2) to 16.8 with half the steps, and from 26.2 (ADM) to 16.8.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Simpler Diffusion (SiD2) for architecture and training protocol, and on the Variational Diffusion Models framework for the loss formulation. It departs from the long line of global noise schedule design (cosine, shifted-cosine, log-normal EDM) by deriving per-instance schedules from each image's radially-averaged power spectral density (RAPSD), grounded in theoretical bounds on minimum and maximum effective noise. The spectral perspective connects to recent work linking diffusion to spectral autoregression (Rissanen et al., Dieleman) and to dataset-level spectral schedule optimization (Benita et al.), but extends these from aggregate/dataset-level to per-instance adaptation, and introduces a lightweight GMM-based RAPSD sampler to make the schedules available at inference time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for pixel diffusion: the method achieves better FID with roughly half the denoising steps (160–256 vs. 512), and the improvement widens in the low-step regime where pixel diffusion is most vulnerable. The two hyperparameters (κ_min, κ_max) remain constant across resolutions and datasets, eliminating a common tuning burden. Open questions include whether the technique transfers to latent diffusion or distilled models, whether the observed FID degradation at very high NFE (a loss of diversity, not quality) can be fully resolved, and whether other schedule-adjacent hyperparameters (loss bias, guidance intervals) can also be tied to spectral properties.
