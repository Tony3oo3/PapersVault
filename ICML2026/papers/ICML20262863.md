## Problem

Existing multi-target drug design methods either model protein pockets in isolation or incorporate structural information only through post hoc docking-based rewards, failing to jointly reason about heterogeneous binding geometries during the generative process. This limits the ability to produce a single ligand that simultaneously satisfies coupled structural and pharmacological constraints across multiple targets with diverse pocket topologies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262863/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3D Cα coordinates of binding pocket residues (from AlphaFold, with spatial edges masked beyond 20 Å), amino acid sequences (≤1000 residues), nucleotide sequences (≤6400 bases), and an 8-dimensional pharmacological property vector (MW, HBD, HBA, RotB, LogP, NumR, MaxR, NumA) normalized to [0,1]. It autoregressively generates SMILES token sequences (length 34–74) for candidate molecules, which are then evaluated via AutoDock Vina docking against both target proteins to report binding affinities in kcal/mol.

## Method

The core contribution is a geometric-topological protein graph whose edges encode both pocket-scale spatial proximity (inverse squared Cα–Cα distance) and local backbone folding geometry (arccos of consecutive segment vectors), with ligand-irrelevant internal residues masked to focus on binding-relevant topology. This graph is encoded by a two-layer KAN-GAT that replaces all linear projections with B-spline-parameterized Kolmogorov Arnold Networks, enabling flexible piecewise-smooth nonlinearities under limited parameter budgets while preserving translation and rotation invariance. The structural embedding is fused with ProtTrans amino acid embeddings and multi-scale CNN nucleotide embeddings (kernel sizes 1, 3, 9) via cross-modal attention, then aggregated across targets through residual multi-head self-attention. A Transformer-based autoregressive generator produces SMILES tokens conditioned on the fused multi-target representation and a Bernoulli-masked pharmacological property vector, allowing the model to handle partially specified constraints while preventing overfitting to specific attribute combinations.

## Results

The proposed method achieves the strongest binding affinities across all three disease settings evaluated via AutoDock Vina docking. On COVID-19 targets (Mpro/Epro), it reaches −11.4 and −12.3 kcal/mol; on schizophrenia targets (TAAR1/5HT2C), −13.3 and −12.1 kcal/mol; and on tumour targets (MEK1/mTOR), −12.5 and −11.6 kcal/mol. The method outperforms all baselines on 5 out of 6 individual target comparisons, with the sole exception being mTOR where Yuan's diffusion-based method achieves −14.1 kcal/mol versus the proposed −11.6 kcal/mol.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Zhou et al. (E(3)-equivariant conditional generation) on COVID-19, the proposed method improves binding affinity by 1.1 kcal/mol on Mpro (−11.4 vs. −10.3) and 1.4 kcal/mol on Epro (−12.3 vs. −10.9). Against Lu et al. (expert-guided pocket-similarity design) on schizophrenia, the improvement is dramatic: 7.1 kcal/mol on TAAR1 (−13.3 vs. −6.2) and 5.3 kcal/mol on 5HT2C (−12.1 vs. −6.8), reflecting the advantage of learned multimodal encoding over hand-crafted pocket similarity priors. On tumour targets, the method surpasses Munson et al. (SMILES-based RL) by 5.1 kcal/mol on MEK1 and 3.2 kcal/mol on mTOR, and edges out Yuan et al. (3D diffusion + RL) on MEK1 (−12.5 vs. −11.9) but trails by 2.5 kcal/mol on mTOR (−11.6 vs. −14.1). Overall, the proposed framework wins 5 of 6 head-to-head target comparisons, with the mTOR gap being the only loss, suggesting that the pocket-centric geometric encoding is particularly effective for GPCR and protease pockets but may underfit the deep hydrophobic cleft of mTOR.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of protein structure modeling and conditional molecular generation for polypharmacology. Prior multi-target drug design methods (Munson's POLYGON, Yuan's MDRL, Zhou's E(3)-equivariant framework) either omit 3D protein structure during generation or incorporate it only through post-hoc docking scores and affinity embeddings. The key departure here is a pocket-centric geometric-topological protein representation that encodes backbone folding angles and spatial proximity directly as edge features in a KAN-based graph attention encoder, feeding structural information into the autoregressive generator as a conditioning signal rather than a selection criterion. The repurposing of Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks as nonlinear operators replacing linear projections in GAT layers is a creative architectural choice not previously applied to protein structure encoding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Multi-target drug design is a practically important problem for treating complex diseases where single-target therapies face resistance and compensatory pathway activation, and this paper demonstrates that explicitly encoding pocket geometry during generation (rather than filtering post hoc) can yield molecules with stronger and more balanced affinities across heterogeneous targets. However, several open questions remain: the framework is validated only on pairs of targets (K=2) with semi-empirical AutoDock Vina scoring and no wet-lab confirmation, the contribution of the KAN component versus a standard GAT is not isolated through ablation, and the added value of nucleotide sequences over protein sequences alone is unclear. Scalability to three or more simultaneous targets, and whether the pocket-centric masking strategy generalizes to targets without resolved structures, are important next steps.
