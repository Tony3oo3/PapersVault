## Problem

It remains unclear whether verbalized confidence calibration in LLMs generalizes across heterogeneous tasks without degrading accuracy. Prior work either omits out-of-distribution evaluation or reports non-negligible accuracy trade-offs, leaving open whether confidence alignment is truly transferable. This paper asks: can a single scalar confidence be consistently calibrated across diverse task families, and if not, what structure does the failure follow?

## Input / Output

The model receives a text query (optionally containing a long-context passage for retrieval tasks) and produces a structured response: a chain-of-thought reasoning trace in think tags, a final answer in answer tags, and one or two discrete confidence scores in confidence tags, each restricted to the set {0, 10, ..., 100}. In the disentangled variant, two separate confidence values are emitted (reasoning confidence and evidence confidence), each in its own dedicated tag. The confidence prompt is appended as a suffix after the answer so that confidence elicitation does not interfere with answer generation, and during training only the confidence token spans receive gradient signal while reasoning and answer tokens remain unconstrained.

## Method

The paper introduces Confidence Supervised Fine-Tuning (CSFT), a minimal SFT protocol that uses Monte Carlo self-consistency labels (K sampled generations from the frozen base model, discretized empirical accuracy) to supervise only the confidence tokens via masked cross-entropy, with LoRA adapters on the base LLM. The central empirical finding is that a single scalar confidence is incompatible across two task families: reasoning-centric tasks (GSM8K, MATH, MMLU) where confidence reflects internal reasoning stability, and retrieval/copy tasks (RULER) where it tracks evidence localization; supervision on one family systematically miscalibrates the other. To resolve this, the authors propose disentangled verbalized confidence, jointly supervising a reasoning-confidence component and an evidence-confidence component via a prompting-based output structure, restoring cross-family generalization with SFT alone (no RL). This decomposition also preserves axis-specific emergent behaviors: number-token smoothing and confidence-conditioned CoT length with self-verification appear under reasoning supervision, while likelihood-driven calibration patterns appear under retrieval supervision.

## Results

On LLaMA-3.2-3B-Instruct, the disentangled confidence method (Ours) achieves ECE of 0.1162 on MATH, 0.1466 on RULER-8K, and 0.1181 on ContractNLI, with AUROC of 0.8498, 0.8263, and 0.6089 respectively, while also improving accuracy over the base model on all three (0.3776 vs 0.3504 on MATH; 0.6700 vs 0.6677 on RULER-8K; 0.4907 vs 0.4308 on ContractNLI). Single-cluster CSFT methods show strong within-family calibration (e.g., CSFT on GSM8K: MATH ECE 0.1424) but severe cross-family collapse (RULER-8K ECE 0.6351, AUROC 0.4911 near chance). On Qwen-3-8B, the method improves ECE from 0.2833→0.1317 (Cluster 1), 0.1326→0.1100 (Cluster 2), and 0.3895→0.2857 (Cluster 3/hybrid) over the base model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the task-level CSFT baseline adapted from Lin et al. (2022), the proposed disentangled method achieves substantially lower ECE on MATH (0.1162 vs. 0.2042) and ContractNLI (0.1181 vs. 0.2423), while the task-level baseline retains an advantage on RULER-8K (ECE 0.0762 vs. 0.1466) because it is partially in-domain there. Against the Log Score RL baseline (Band et al., 2024, via GRPO), the proposed method improves Cluster-1 ECE from 0.2125 to 0.1086 (CSFT on GSM8K) and avoids the severe Cluster-2 degradation (ECE 0.3832 → 0.1466 for Ours on RULER-8K). Against the Brier RL baseline (Damani et al., 2025, via GRPO), the proposed method similarly outperforms on both clusters: Cluster-1 ECE 0.1086 vs. 0.2079 and Cluster-2 ECE 0.1466 vs. 0.2837, while also improving accuracy (0.6282 vs. 0.6368 on Cluster 1; 0.6700 vs. 0.6854 on Cluster 2). Critically, the RL baselines operate under a strictly more favorable training setting (they receive explicit correctness rewards in addition to confidence alignment), yet still fail to generalize across task families, underscoring that the cross-family breakdown is a semantic incompatibility rather than an optimization limitation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the growing line of work on verbalized confidence calibration in LLMs (Lin et al. 2022, Band et al. 2024, Stangel et al. 2025, Damani et al. 2025), but deliberately repurposes a simple SFT-based confidence supervision protocol (CSFT) as a diagnostic instrument rather than a task-specific optimization method. Where prior work has focused on increasingly sophisticated RL objectives (log-score, Brier-score) and typically evaluates on narrow task distributions, this paper isolates the effect of confidence supervision itself to study cross-task generalization. Its key conceptual departure is the finding that confidence is not a universal scalar but decomposes into at least two incompatible semantics (reasoning uncertainty vs. evidence-localization uncertainty), and that a minimal structural remedy—disentangled dual-confidence SFT—restores cross-family generalization without any RL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a single verbalized confidence score cannot be simultaneously calibrated across reasoning and retrieval tasks challenges a common implicit assumption in LLM deployment and calibration research, and the disentangled remedy is practical (SFT-only, no RL) and model-agnostic (validated on both LLaMA-3.2-3B and Qwen-3-8B). Open questions include whether the two-family taxonomy is exhaustive or whether additional orthogonal confidence axes exist for other task types (e.g., creative generation, multi-turn dialogue), how the approach scales to larger models where base-model self-consistency may already be more reliable, and whether the emergent confidence-dependent reasoning-length behavior is a desirable side effect or a potential failure mode under adversarial prompting. The paper's scope is limited to relatively small models (≤8B) and a fixed set of benchmarks, so the generality of the two-family structure to production-scale systems remains to be confirmed.
