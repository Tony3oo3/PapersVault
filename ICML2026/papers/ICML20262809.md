## Problem

Pre-trained vision-language models like CLIP are highly vulnerable to imperceptible adversarial perturbations that disrupt image-text alignment, yet existing defenses either require expensive adversarial fine-tuning with labeled data or rely on unstable test-time counterattacks that suffer from semantic drift, view sensitivity, and hard-negative dominance. The paper identifies these three specific fragilities in prior test-time methods (notably TTC) and asks whether a training-free, plug-and-play inference strategy can simultaneously address cross-modal misalignment and spatial instability.

## Input / Output

The method takes an adversarially perturbed RGB image (within an ℓ_p ball of radius ε_a, typically 1/255 or 4/255) and a set of K text prompts encoding class labels as input. At test time it produces a corrective perturbation δ (bounded by ε_d = 4/255) applied to the adversarial image, yielding a final class prediction via cosine similarity between the corrected image embedding and text embeddings. The pipeline internally generates L=2 semantics-preserving augmented views (horizontal flip plus low-variance Gaussian noise with σ=6/255) and a short counterattack warm-up to construct soft pseudo-labels, all without access to ground-truth labels or model retraining.

## Method

SCC is a test-time defense composed of two complementary consistency modules optimized jointly over a corrective perturbation δ via PGD ascent. The semantic consistency module constructs a soft prototype t_soft by averaging multi-view predictions from a short TTC warm-up, sharpening with temperature T=0.5, and forming a weighted combination of text embeddings; it then optimizes a cross-modal margin objective L_cm that maximizes cosine similarity to t_soft while repelling the hardest negative class embedding, monotonically increasing the semantic margin up to O(α²). The spatial consistency module aggregates L augmented views of the corrected image by averaging logits before softmax, which provably reduces prediction variance by 1/L and suppresses view-dependent spurious negatives. The final objective couples the cross-modal margin with a feature-deviation term (inherited from TTC) to escape pseudo-stability, and a step-weighted fusion across PGD iterations yields a smoother corrective perturbation. The entire procedure is plug-and-play, requiring no retraining, and adds only ~0.0005s per image over TTC while delivering +12.5% average robust accuracy gains across 22 benchmarks.

## Results

Under 10-step PGD (ε_a = 1/255) across 16 zero-shot benchmarks, SCC achieves an average robust accuracy of 51.68% versus 2.70% for vanilla CLIP and 39.17% for the strongest prior test-time defense TTC, while retaining 60.21% clean accuracy (only −1.30% vs. CLIP). Under a stronger budget (ε_a = 4/255), SCC still reaches 27.88% robust accuracy, outperforming TTC (20.63%) by +7.25 points. On the medical BioMedCLIP backbone, SCC lifts average robust accuracy from 0.08% (vanilla) to 34.00%, a +23.38-point gain over TTC (10.62%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCC outperforms all test-time defenses across all 16 datasets. Against the strongest prior test-time method TTC, SCC gains +12.51 points in average robust accuracy (51.68% vs. 39.17%) while matching its clean accuracy (60.21% vs. 59.75%); the gap widens to +7.25 points under the stronger ε_a = 4/255 budget. DOC, the second-best test-time defense, trails SCC by 5.64 points on average. Adversarial fine-tuning baselines (TeCoA, PMG-AFT, FARE) achieve comparable or slightly lower robust accuracy (26–29%) but sacrifice 10–21 points of clean accuracy due to overfitting, whereas SCC loses only 1.30 points. Simpler defenses—Anti-Adversarial (3.86%), HD (12.01%), and random-noise RN (13.81%)—are far less effective, confirming that counterattack-based methods are necessary but insufficient without SCC's semantic and spatial consistency. On the medical BioMedCLIP backbone, SCC more than triples TTC's robust accuracy (34.00% vs. 10.62%), demonstrating plug-and-play transferability to domain-specific VLMs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Test-Time Counterattack (TTC, Xing et al., CVPR 2025), which uses CLIP's visual encoder to apply corrective perturbations at inference time. The key departure is a diagnostic analysis identifying three specific failure modes of TTC—semantic drift toward hard negatives, view sensitivity, and pseudo-stability—and addressing them with a dual consistency framework: cross-modal semantic consistency (soft pseudo-labels from multi-view warm-up to anchor the target embedding) and spatial consistency (multi-view logit averaging to suppress view-dependent spurious negatives). The paper also positions SCC against training-based defenses (AFT, APT) that require labeled data and overfit, and against other test-time methods (R-TPT, DOC) that remain semantically misaligned, arguing that unifying both consistency axes in a single plug-and-play objective is the missing ingredient.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SCC offers a practical, retraining-free defense that lifts average adversarial accuracy from ~2.7% to ~51.7% across 16 diverse benchmarks with only a 1.3% clean-accuracy cost and negligible inference overhead (~0.0005s/image over TTC), making it immediately deployable for safety-critical VLM applications including medical imaging (BioMedCLIP: 0% → 34%). Open questions remain: performance degrades under very large perturbation budgets (ε ≥ 6/255) and on datasets lacking distinctive visual anchors (e.g., COVID-19 chest X-rays), and it is unclear whether the consistency framework extends to generative VLMs or multi-turn dialogue settings. The plug-and-play nature and cross-architecture generalization (OpenCLIP, BioMedCLIP) suggest a template for hardening other foundation models without retraining.
