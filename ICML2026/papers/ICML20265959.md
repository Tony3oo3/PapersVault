## Problem

Existing PSRO variants expand strategy populations by computing best responses to meta-strategies derived solely from restricted-game payoffs, which can produce locally strong but globally unhelpful expansions that fail to reduce full-game exploitability. No prior method explicitly evaluates whether a candidate expansion improves the population-level approximation of the full-game equilibrium, leaving a structural gap between the expansion rule and the ultimate objective of minimizing population exploitability.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a two-player zero-sum extensive-form game (implemented in OpenSpiel), an initial restricted strategy population, and a base meta-strategy solver (e.g., restricted-game NE). It produces a growing set of neural-network policies (the restricted population) whose induced game approximates the full game, along with estimated PE values used for candidate ranking. Policies are trained via PPO against meta-strategy mixtures, and PE estimation uses regret-minimization best-response (RM-BR) with warm-started conditional evaluators, all under a shared environment-interaction budget.

## Method

Global PSRO introduces a two-phase exploration-selection framework that directly optimizes post-expansion population exploitability. In the exploration phase, a pool of K meta-strategies (one from the base MSS plus K−1 uniform Dirichlet samples) is used to train a single conditional explorer policy π_θ(a|s,σ) via PPO, yielding K candidate best responses through slicing. In the selection phase, a conditional evaluator β_ψ(a|s,σ) is trained via RM-BR against each candidate's least-exploitable mixture to estimate post-expansion PE, and the candidate minimizing a regularized PE score (which penalizes candidates where the new strategy receives negligible mixture mass) is selected. Both the selected candidate and its evaluation BR are added to the population per round. Parameter sharing across candidates via conditional neural networks amortizes the multi-candidate training cost, making the approach practical for large-scale games.

## Results

On Goofspiel (13 cards), the largest benchmark, Global PSRO achieves a final PE of 0.046±0.016 at 153.6M environment steps, compared to 0.193±0.044 for PSRO with restricted-game NE and 0.132±0.041 for the best NeuPL variant (AlphaRank). The gap widens at earlier budgets: at 76.8M steps, Global PSRO reaches PE 0.056±0.037 while PSRO w/Nash is at 0.284±0.043. On Kuhn Poker (the smallest game), restricted-game NE is competitive and can slightly outperform Global PSRO, as the restricted game quickly approximates the full game. Across Liar's Dice, Leduc Poker, and both Goofspiel variants, Global PSRO consistently achieves the lowest PE under matched interaction budgets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard PSRO with various meta-strategy solvers (Nash, AlphaRank, PRD, Uniform, Anytime PSRO), Global PSRO achieves substantially lower PE on all games except Kuhn Poker, where the small game size limits the benefit of global information. On Goofspiel (13 cards) at 153.6M steps, Global PSRO (PE 0.046) outperforms the best PSRO variant (AlphaRank, PE 0.178) by roughly 4×. Against NeuPL (conditional-population PSRO), which shares the parameter-sharing architecture but uses standard PSRO expansion, Global PSRO achieves lower final PE across all tested MSS configurations, confirming that the gain comes from the post-expansion PE selection mechanism rather than parameter sharing alone. Against the diversity-driven PSD-PSRO, Global PSRO also wins on all large games, showing that encouraging diversity in candidate generation is insufficient without explicit population-level quality evaluation. On Kuhn Poker, restricted-game NE PSRO can slightly outperform Global PSRO, as the restricted game rapidly converges to the full game in this small setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PSRO framework (Lanctot et al., 2017) by replacing the standard single-meta-strategy expansion with a two-phase exploration-selection mechanism that explicitly minimizes post-expansion Population Exploitability. It borrows the conditional neural network architecture from NeuPL (Liu et al., 2022) for parameter-shared candidate generation and the RM-BR procedure from Anytime PSRO (McAleer et al., 2022) for PE estimation, but its core departure is the selection criterion: rather than adding a BR to a single meta-strategy computed from restricted-game or semi-restricted-game payoffs, it generates multiple candidates and ranks them by the PE of the resulting expanded population. The theoretical contribution formalizes why restricted-game-based MSSs are structurally limited (Theorem 3.2) and shows the proposed framework preserves convergence while circumventing worst-case behaviors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first clean worst-case lower bound showing that all restricted-game-based PSRO variants can require N-1 iterations, giving a principled justification for incorporating global information into population expansion. The practical algorithm is modular and composable (diversity objectives, different base MSSs can be plugged in), making it a useful template for future PSRO improvements. Key open questions include extending the framework to multiplayer general-sum games where PE loses its saddle-point structure, and whether the PE-based selection criterion remains effective when the full-game BR oracle is highly approximate in very large games.
