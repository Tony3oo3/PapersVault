## Problem

Hardware-implemented Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) suffer severe performance degradation from permanent synaptic faults (e.g., stuck-at faults), yet all existing fault-tolerance mechanisms require direct access to internal circuits to modify weights or monitor neuron states, making them incompatible with off-the-shelf neuromorphic devices. The paper identifies the underlying cause as a 'bottleneck problem' where faults reduce usable learning capacity, pushing membrane potentials outside the surrogate gradient corridor and causing gradient vanishing, and asks whether fault tolerance can be improved purely through input-side control without touching internal hardware.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262349/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a single input sample (2D images at 28×28, 32×32, or 64×64; 1D sensor/audio sequences reshaped into a 2D grid) and produces a class label. The key unusual aspect is that the input is not fed as a whole but is spatially partitioned into T temporal fragments (T learned from {2, 4, 8}) via learnable division lines, with one fragment injected per time step. At inference, per-time-step SNN outputs are aggregated using entropy-weighted decoding that emphasizes time steps producing more confident (lower-entropy) predictions.

## Method

The proposed mechanism operates exclusively at the input level and has three jointly trained components: (1) learnable division lines, where T−1 lines parameterized by bounded reparameterizations (h_k, v_k, r_k) define spatial masks that partition the input into T fragments, with binary masks in the forward pass and soft masks via a straight-through estimator in the backward pass; (2) a Gumbel-Softmax selector over a candidate set T={2,4,8} that learns the optimal fragment count by mixing fragments from different T values during training and committing to a single T at inference; and (3) a balance-loss regularizer that penalizes uneven per-fragment energy to prevent degenerate configurations. All fragmentation parameters are updated jointly with SNN weights via standard backpropagation (surrogate-gradient STBP), requiring no access to internal circuits. The core insight is that reducing per-step input energy keeps membrane potentials within the surrogate gradient corridor, directly mitigating the fault-induced gradient vanishing that causes the bottleneck.

## Results

Under 30% SAFs, the proposed mechanism achieves 95.89% accuracy on MLP/MNIST, 88.43% on MLP/FMNIST, and 69.36% on VGG-7/CIFAR-10 in software, outperforming all lightweight baselines (Input Suppression, Fault Hopping, Threshold Tuning) by 1.3–10.5 percentage points. On a real FPGA platform, the method reaches 92.96% (MLP/MNIST) and 64.71% (VGG-7/CIFAR-10) while matching or beating the fastest baseline in inference time (e.g., 204.16 s vs. 204.56 s for Threshold Tuning on MLP/MNIST). In the motivation study, learnable fragmentation under 50% SAFs reduces the gradient-vanishing probability from 0.99 to 0.29 and lifts MLP/MNIST accuracy from 10.82% to 51.05%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed mechanism outperforms all six internal-circuit-based benchmarks (ECOC, SoftSNN, Routing, Astrocyte, FalVolt, LIFA) across every model–dataset pair tested, with the largest gaps appearing at moderate-to-high fault ratios (e.g., on UCI-HAR at 30% SAF, the proposed method reaches 52.83% while the best benchmark, SoftSNN, manages only 21.90%; on Tiny-ImageNet/ResNet-34 at 30% SAF, the proposed method achieves 16.40% versus 10.15% for FalVolt, the strongest benchmark). Against the three lightweight input-side approaches (Input Suppression, Fault Hopping, Threshold Tuning), the proposed method wins on accuracy in every setting (up to +10.5 pp on VGG-7/CIFAR-10) while also matching or beating the fastest baseline in inference time on FPGA (204.16 s vs. 204.56 s for Threshold Tuning on MLP/MNIST). The astrocyte-based methods (Astrocyte, LIFA) show the weakest fault tolerance on deep ResNet models, dropping to chance level at 40–50% faults, whereas the proposed mechanism maintains meaningful accuracy up to 60–70% faults on ResNet-18/CIFAR-10. ECOC is the closest competitor under RWFs due to its error-correcting-code design, but still trails the proposed method by 2–5 pp on most datasets. The key practical advantage is that the proposed method requires no access to internal SNN circuits, making it deployable on non-reconfigurable neuromorphic hardware, whereas all six benchmarks assume direct weight or state manipulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in SNN fault tolerance, which has focused on internal modifications—weight reconfiguration, fault mapping, spike rerouting, or astrocyte-inspired regulatory circuits (SoftSNN, Routing, Astrocyte, FalVolt, LIFA). Instead, it draws an analogy to network flow control and proposes that the root cause of fault-induced degradation is a mismatch between input information load and reduced usable learning capacity, addressable purely through input-side data fragmentation. The entropy-based decoding component is borrowed from Qiu et al. (NAACL 2025), and the surrogate-gradient training framework follows standard SpikingJelly practice, but the learnable fragmentation strategy with Gumbel-Softmax selection of fragment count is novel within this fault-tolerance context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in enabling fault-tolerant SNN deployment on non-reconfigurable, off-the-shelf neuromorphic hardware where internal circuit access is unavailable—a constraint that has limited prior methods. The FPGA validation strengthens the claim, though the paper acknowledges limitations: the fragmentation and decoding do not yet account for intrinsic LIF temporal dynamics, and real-world neuromorphic operating conditions (drift, non-stationary inputs) remain untested. An open question is whether the input-side adaptation can be further refined to exploit spiking temporal structure, and whether the approach generalizes to on-chip learning scenarios where faults evolve during training.
