## Problem

The FCI-inspired learner I-FCI (Kocaoglu et al., 2019) for causal discovery from controlled soft interventions with latent confounding is sound but not complete: it can leave circle endpoints that are in fact compelled by the interventional Markov equivalence class. The structural reason is that intervention indicator (F) nodes impose constraints on inducing paths through their targets that the generic FCI orientation closure does not fully propagate, leaving identifiable edge marks undetermined.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a collection of interventional distributions (one per domain, including an observational regime), the known intervention target set for each domain, and the set of observed variables. It produces an augmented partial ancestral graph (I-PAG) over the observed variables plus F-nodes (one per unordered pair of intervention targets, connected to the symmetric difference of their targets), with each edge endpoint marked as arrowhead, arrowtail, or circle. The output is a maximally oriented representation of the interventional Markov equivalence class, where circle marks denote genuinely undetermined endpoints.

## Method

The paper proposes two complementary refinements to I-FCI, both exploiting the semantics that any inducing path from an F-node to a non-target variable must pass through one of the F-node's targets (a 'transit node'). First, an enumeration-based completion procedure (LIST) treats the I-FCI output as a PAG with local background knowledge at F-nodes, enumerates all consistent MAG completions via a polynomial-delay MAG listing algorithm, filters each candidate through a new I-MAG realizability oracle (which searches for valid transit-pair assignments and verifies the augmented MAG matches), and intersects endpoint marks across all surviving I-MAGs—yielding a sound and theoretically complete procedure. Second, three new local orientation rules (FAST) are added to the FCI closure: Rule 9 identifies a unique transit target when a non-target has only one possible target parent; Rule 10 orients edges away from a transit node to prevent spurious inducing paths to non-adjacent nodes; and Rule 11 orients edges from the unique source in the directed target-induced subgraph. These rules are sound, require only local checks, and do not increase the asymptotic complexity of the baseline FCI-style procedure.

## Results

On randomly generated ADMGs with n=5 observed variables, the proposed FAST method (local orientation rules) reduces PG-SHD from 11.21±4.70 (I-FCI) to 10.87±4.69 at ρ_bi=0.5, and from 14.23±4.90 to 13.90±5.08 at ρ_bi=0.8, while improving End-F1 from 0.771 to 0.777 and from 0.732 to 0.738 respectively. The enumeration-based LIST method also improves over I-FCI (PG-SHD 11.04, End-F1 0.774 at ρ_bi=0.5) but with smaller gains and less robustness under finite-sample errors. Both methods preserve the skeleton learned by the baseline, orienting only additional endpoint marks implied by F-node semantics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against I-FCI (Kocaoglu et al., 2019), the prior FCI-inspired learner for controlled soft interventions with latent variables. FAST (the proposed three local orientation rules) consistently outperforms I-FCI on both PG-SHD and End-F1 across both confounding densities: at ρ_bi=0.5 it reduces PG-SHD by 0.34 and increases End-F1 by 0.006; at ρ_bi=0.8 it reduces PG-SHD by 0.33 and increases End-F1 by 0.006. LIST (the MAG-listing-based completion) also beats I-FCI on both metrics in both settings, but its gains are smaller (PG-SHD reduction of 0.17 at ρ_bi=0.5) and it is less robust under finite-sample errors because it enforces global I-MAG realizability constraints. The paper notes that FAST provides the best empirical tradeoff, as it achieves comparable or better orientation recovery than LIST while avoiding the potentially super-exponential cost of MAG enumeration. All improvements are modest in absolute terms, reflecting that the additional F-node semantics primarily resolve a small number of circle endpoints that I-FCI's generic orientation closure misses.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the I-FCI framework of Kocaoglu et al. (2019) for causal discovery from controlled soft interventions with latent confounders, which encodes cross-environment invariances via an augmented graph with intervention indicator (F) nodes. The key departure is identifying that the FCI-inspired orientation closure in I-FCI is sound but incomplete: F-node adjacency semantics impose additional constraints on inducing paths through intervention targets that generic PAG orientation rules do not propagate. The paper draws on Wang et al. (2025) for a polynomial-delay MAG listing subroutine and on Wang et al. (2022) for the conceptual link between F-node semantics and local background knowledge, but goes beyond BK by exploiting the richer structure of known intervention targets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The incompleteness gap means that prior methods leave edge orientations undetermined even when the interventional equivalence class fully constrains them, reducing the practical identifiability of causal structure from multi-environment data. The three local orientation rules offer a drop-in efficiency improvement without changing asymptotic complexity, while the enumeration-based procedure provides a theoretical completeness guarantee at potentially super-exponential cost. A key open question is whether an efficient (polynomial-time) complete algorithm exists that avoids full MAG enumeration, and whether the approach extends to hard interventions or unknown-target settings where the F-node semantics are weaker.
