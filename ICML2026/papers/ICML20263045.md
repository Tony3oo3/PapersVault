## Problem

Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs) that share a single transformer backbone for both visual understanding and image generation suffer from mutual impairment: the understanding branch favors semantically invariant, low-frequency representations while the generation branch demands high-fidelity, fine-grained detail, and jointly optimizing these conflicting inductive biases degrades both capabilities. Prior work either decouples the architecture (compromising the promise of unification) or applies generic post-training without addressing the root cause of the interference. The paper asks whether the representation divergence itself can be restructured into a mechanism of mutual reinforcement rather than treated as a limitation to be suppressed.

## Input / Output

DIVA is a post-training framework applied to existing single-backbone UMMs (Nexus-Gen 7B, Show-o 1.5B, Liquid 7B) at 256×256 to 512×512 resolution. It constructs two complementary information flows from the same image-text pair: an understanding flow (image + captioning prompt → text description) and a generation flow (randomly masked image + text prompt → reconstructed image), ensuring a shared semantic anchor. The method extracts pooled image-token hidden states from middle layers (8–18) of the frozen backbone, factorizes them into shared and unique vectors via lightweight Gated-MLP encoders, and injects these as low-rank logit biases (rank-24) during training. At inference, no additional modules are needed—the post-trained backbone directly produces text responses, generated images, or edited images.

## Method

DIVA factorizes the visual representation into shared (task-agnostic semantic consensus) and unique (task-specific inductive bias) components, then enforces their separation via mutual information estimation. In Stage 1, the backbone is frozen and lightweight Gated-MLP shared/unique encoders are trained through cross-task logit injection: each flow's logits are augmented with the counter-flow's shared factors and its own unique factors, with an orthogonality constraint preventing the unique encoder from redundantly encoding shared semantics. In Stage 2, the encoders are frozen and the backbone is fine-tuned with an asymmetric mutual-information objective: an NCE-based lower bound maximizes alignment of shared representations across flows, while an NCE-CLUB upper bound minimizes information leakage between unique representations, with stop-gradient on the target view to stabilize optimization under heterogeneous task loss scales. The key design insight is that the middle layers spontaneously decouple task-specific information (revealed by gradient, effective-rank, and frequency analyses), making them the natural locus for this factorization, and that the shared anchor from identical image-text pairs provides the semantic grounding that transforms conflict into complementary transfer.

## Results

DIVA achieves consistent improvements across all three tested UMMs (Nexus-Gen, Show-o, Liquid) on both understanding and generation benchmarks. On understanding, the headline gains are +76.2 on MME (Nexus-Gen), +108.4 (Show-o), and +113.2 (Liquid), with POPE improvements of +3.8 to +7.1. On generation, GenEval improves by +0.06 to +0.11 and DPG-Bench by +2.84 to +6.57. The paper reports an average improvement of +7.82% on visual understanding and +8.46% on generation across all models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIVA is applied as a post-training framework on three single-backbone UMMs (Nexus-Gen, Show-o, Liquid) and consistently outperforms each base model across all understanding and generation metrics. Against other UMMs, Nexus-Gen+DIVA (MMMU 49.4, GenEval 0.83, DPG-Bench 87.87) surpasses Janus-Pro (40.6, 0.80, 84.22), OmniGen2 (52.6, 0.80, 83.59), and Emu3 (30.7, 0.64, 79.82), though it remains below the larger multi-backbone models BLIP3-o (MMMU 56.9) and Bagel (GenEval 0.84, DPG-Bench 85.04). On image editing (Table 5), Nexus-Gen+DIVA achieves an overall ImgEdit score of 3.35, outperforming Bagel (3.24), AnyEdit (2.67), UltraEdit (2.99), and approaching FLUX.1-Kontext (3.48), while also improving GEdit-Bench-EN from 4.61 to 4.92. Compared to the post-training method RecA (Xie et al., 2025) under the same SFT setting on Show-o, DIVA yields substantially larger gains: +108.4 on MME vs. RecA's +37.1, and +6.0 on POPE vs. +2.6. The paper notes that DIVA does not surpass dedicated generation-only models like Qwen-Image (GenEval 0.86) or SDXL on WISE (0.43 vs. 0.45 for Nexus-Gen+DIVA), acknowledging that its gains come from internal synergy rather than architectural scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

DIVA builds on the mutual information estimation framework of NCE-CLUB (Liang et al., 2023) and its underlying CLUB upper-bound estimator (Cheng et al., 2020), repurposing them to disentangle shared and unique information between understanding and generation flows within a single UMM backbone. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant prior strategy of resolving the understanding-generation conflict through architectural decoupling (separate encoders, MoT designs, or hybrid AR+diffusion pipelines) by instead treating the representation divergence as a structured signal to be explicitly factorized and leveraged. As a post-training paradigm, it also contrasts with pre-training-level interventions and with reconstruction-alignment methods like RecA, which condition generation on understanding embeddings without modeling the unique-information boundary.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the inductive-bias conflict in UMMs need not force a trade-off: by explicitly separating shared semantic anchors from task-specific residuals and aligning only the former via mutual information, both branches improve simultaneously without architectural changes. This is practically appealing because DIVA is a lightweight post-training procedure applicable to existing single-backbone UMMs. However, scalability beyond the 1.5B–8B range tested here remains unverified, and extension to video, interleaved generation, or larger-scale models is an open question. The moderate but consistent gains (+7.8% understanding, +8.5% generation) suggest the approach captures a real but bounded portion of the available synergy.
