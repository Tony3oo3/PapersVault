## Problem

Activation steering via the residual stream causes rapid coherency collapse when steering toward out-of-distribution persona directions, and this degradation is invisible to standard utility metrics like MMLU and Perplexity. Prior work has addressed *how* to steer (e.g., angular steering) and *when* to steer (e.g., dynamic scaling), but the *where*—the intervention site—has been largely unexplored, with all methods defaulting to the residual stream despite its aggregation of off-target noise.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a user text prompt paired with either a target or neutral system prompt, plus a pre-computed steering vector extracted via difference-in-means from 200 contrastive prompt samples (5 target/neutral pairs × 20 queries). Unlike standard residual-stream steering, the vector is applied via activation addition only to the outputs of three specific attention heads (heads 3, 5, 28 in layer 20 of Qwen2.5-7B; heads 24, 30, 32 in layer 14 of Llama-3.1-8B) before the output projection, rather than to the full hidden state. Evaluation produces trait scores and coherency scores (0–100) via GPT-4.1-mini as judge over 200 held-out samples, alongside MMLU, Perplexity, and IFEval for general capability tracking.

## Method

The paper identifies Style Modulation Heads through a two-stage geometric localization: (1) layer-wise cosine similarity analysis of persona vectors extracted at sub-layer inputs and outputs reveals a sharp directional transition at a specific attention layer, and (2) a Head Contribution Score (dot product between each head's projected contribution and the aggregate attention-output persona vector) isolates three heads with disproportionately high scores across all six personas. Steering is then applied exclusively to these heads via activation addition, bypassing the residual stream's aggregation of unrelated features. Causal validation via sequential zero ablation confirms these heads are functionally specialized for style/persona modulation while leaving coherency, MMLU, and IFEval intact. A Pareto frontier comparison across five intervention sites (MLP residual, attention residual, attention output, head-correlated, head-correlated+anti-correlated) demonstrates that head-level steering achieves the best trait-expression-to-coherency trade-off in 11 of 12 conditions for Qwen2.5-7B and 9 of 12 for Llama-3.1-8B.

## Results

Using the Right-normalized Constrained Envelope Area metric (τ=80.0) to quantify the trait-expression vs. coherency trade-off, steering on Style Modulation Heads (Head Cor) achieves the highest Pareto score in 11 of 12 conditions for Qwen2.5-7B and 9 of 12 for Llama-3.1-8B. In the Target−α (trait suppression) configuration on Qwen2.5-7B, Head Cor reaches near-perfect scores (100.0 on Evil and Humorous, 99.8 on Loser) while MLP Residual drops to 43.7 on Hallucination and 65.2 on Passionate. For Llama-3.1-8B Target−α, Head Cor scores 99.9–100.0 on Evil, Humorous, and Loser, whereas MLP Residual collapses to 18.7–48.6 across the same personas. General utility (MMLU, PPL, IFEval) remains largely stable under Head Cor steering, with MMLU degradation under 0.5% in most conditions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares five intervention sites rather than external methods: MLP Residual (residual stream after MLP), Attn Residual (residual stream after attention), Attn Output (attention sub-layer output), Head Cor (only the positively correlated Style Modulation Heads), and Head Cor+Anti (correlated plus anti-correlated heads). Head Cor dominates in 11/12 Qwen and 9/12 Llama conditions; the two Llama exceptions (Evil and Syco under Neutral+α) are won by Attn Residual, with Head Cor remaining second-best. MLP Residual consistently shows the steepest coherency collapse, scoring 43.7 (Hallu) and 65.2 (Passi) under Qwen Target−α versus Head Cor's 73.1 and 85.5. Head Cor+Anti underperforms Head Cor alone in most conditions (e.g., 36.5 vs. 69.0 on Qwen Hallu Neutral+α; 13.2 vs. 99.9 on Llama Evil Target−α), suggesting anti-correlated heads do not serve a protective role and may introduce additional noise. Attn Residual and Attn Output occupy an intermediate position, performing better than MLP Residual but substantially worse than Head Cor for trait-specific personas.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the difference-in-means activation steering framework (Belrose, 2023; Chen et al., 2025) by shifting the intervention site from the residual stream to a sparse subset of attention heads identified via geometric analysis. It positions itself as addressing the 'where to steer' question, explicitly orthogonal to prior work on 'how to steer' (Angular Steering, Vu & Nguyen 2025) and 'when to steer' (Dynamic Steering, Zhao et al. 2025; Ferrando et al. 2025). The head-localization methodology builds on the MHA decomposition (Equation 2) and the Persona Vectors extraction pipeline (Chen et al., 2025), but introduces head-wise contribution scores and layer-wise cosine similarity tracking as novel diagnostic tools for component-level localization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that coherency collapse is invisible to standard benchmarks (MMLU, PPL, IFEval) challenges the evaluation practices of the activation steering community and motivates coherency-aware metrics for safety-critical deployment. The identification of just three Style Modulation Heads governing all six personas suggests a high degree of functional modularity in Transformer attention, with implications for surgical fine-tuning and architecture design. Key open questions include whether the single-layer, three-head pattern generalizes to larger or architecturally different models (preliminary results on gemma-3-12b and Qwen3-30B-A3B show the pattern becomes less clean), how combining 'where' with 'how' and 'when' to steer further improves robustness, and whether finer-grained intervention (e.g., via SAEs) can push beyond the attention-head granularity limit.
