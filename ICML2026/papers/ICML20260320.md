## Problem

Gaussian variational inference requires minimizing a discrepancy functional (e.g., KL divergence) over the space of Gaussian measures, but existing first-order methods (BW gradient flow, SVGD, Fisher-Rao) are deterministic and can converge to local minima on non-log-concave targets. The natural stochastic consensus-based optimization (CBO) extension to the Bures-Wasserstein manifold is computationally expensive (barycenters require solving matrix equations) and ill-posed due to the geodesic incompleteness of the BW space, where random perturbations can drive covariance matrices to the singular boundary.

## Input / Output

The method takes a target measure μ^targ (typically a Gaussian mixture), a fixed reference Gaussian N(0, Σ⁰), and hyperparameters (λ, σ, α, N, Δt). Crucially, it is zeroth-order: only pointwise evaluations of the objective E at particle locations are required, with no gradient or Hessian computations. The output is a single consensus Gaussian N(m̄, exp_{Σ⁰}(T̄)) whose parameters are the weighted LBW barycenter of the particle ensemble. The covariance is never represented directly but through the tangent vector T ∈ Sym_d, and the objective is truncated to a large constant M when a particle's covariance becomes singular.

## Method

The core contribution is the Linearized Bures-Wasserstein (LBW) space, which parametrizes each Gaussian N(m, Σ) by the pair (m, T) with T = log_{Σ⁰}(Σ), mapping the constrained BW manifold to the unconstrained Hilbert space R^d × Sym_d equipped with the inner product ⟨m¹,m²⟩ + ⟨T¹,T²⟩_{Σ⁰}. In this space, the weighted barycenter reduces to a simple weighted average of the (m, T) pairs (no matrix equation), and Brownian motion is well-defined via an orthonormal basis of Sym_d. The CBO particle dynamics is then a system of N coupled SDEs where each particle drifts toward the weighted LBW barycenter (consensus point) with anisotropic noise scaled by its distance to consensus, making distant particles explore more aggressively. The extended exponential map exp_{Σ⁰}(T) = (I+T)Σ⁰(I+T) is defined for all T ∈ Sym_d, so particles can reach singular covariances without leaving the domain. Convergence to global minimizers is established via mean-field (McKean-Vlasov) analysis and the Laplace principle, showing the consensus point converges exponentially fast to a near-global minimizer as α → ∞.

## Results

The paper evaluates Gaussian CBO on Gaussian VI problems with KL divergence as the objective, comparing against three gradient-based baselines. In 2D (Figure 4, 100 runs), CBO achieves the lowest median KL divergence across all four test targets (2-component and 4-component Gaussian mixtures), outperforming BW gradient flow, SVGD, and Fisher-Rao flow in both non-log-concave and unimodal settings. In 10D (Figure 5, 20 random K=5 mixtures), CBO exhibits more stable convergence than BW and FR (smaller interquartile range) but is on average outperformed by SVGD in terms of final relative KL divergence. No single tabulated numerical summary is provided; results are reported as median and interquartile-range KL evolution curves.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the BW gradient flow (Lambert et al., 2022), Gaussian CBO consistently achieves lower KL divergence in both d=2 and d=10, with a notably tighter interquartile range in the 10D setting, indicating more robust convergence across random initializations. Against Gaussian SVGD (Liu et al., 2023), CBO wins in all four 2D test cases (including unimodal targets B and C) but loses on average in the 10D experiments, where SVGD reaches a lower final relative KL. Against the Fisher-Rao natural gradient flow (Barfoot, 2020), CBO outperforms FR in both dimensions, with FR requiring a reduced time step (Δt/2 in 2D, Δt/20 in 10D) for numerical stability. The paper notes that CBO's stochastic exploration via Brownian noise provides a global-optimization advantage over the deterministic gradient flows, particularly in non-convex (multi-modal) landscapes where gradient methods can get trapped in local minima.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Consensus-Based Optimization (CBO) framework from Pinnau et al. (2017) and its measure-valued extension by Borghi et al. (2025) into the geometry of Gaussian probability measures. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of the Linearized Bures-Wasserstein (LBW) space, which borrows the Linear Optimal Transport (LOT) parametrization of Kolouri et al. (2016) and applies it to the BW Riemannian manifold, yielding an unconstrained finite-dimensional Hilbert space where barycenters reduce to weighted averages and Brownian motion is naturally defined. This linearization is what makes the stochastic, gradient-free particle dynamics both well-posed and computationally tractable, in contrast to the deterministic BW consensus dynamics of Borghi et al. (2025) which required solving matrix equations for barycenters and could not accommodate noise without hitting the boundary of singular covariances.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The LBW parametrization is a reusable geometric tool that opens the door to a broader class of stochastic optimization dynamics on Gaussian measures beyond the specific CBO algorithm presented here. The paper demonstrates that a gradient-free particle method can outperform deterministic gradient flows (BW, Fisher-Rao, SVGD) in low-dimensional non-convex Gaussian VI, offering a practical alternative when convexity assumptions fail. However, the method's applicability is currently limited: it requires N parallel function evaluations, the noise parameter σ is dimension-sensitive, and in d=10 the method underperforms SVGD on average. Open questions include extending the dynamics to the full (non-linear) BW space, generalizing beyond single Gaussians to mixtures, and understanding the trade-off between the concentration parameter α and the accuracy of the mean-field approximation.
