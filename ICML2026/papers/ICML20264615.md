## Problem

Multi-agent coordination with unseen partners remains difficult because population-based methods lack efficient online adaptation, gradient-based fine-tuning is infeasible in few-shot settings, and prior context-based meta-RL approaches rely on compressed latent representations that fail when test-time partner behaviors deviate from training. The paper asks whether a fixed model can adapt to diverse partner styles purely through in-context observation of recent interactions, without any parameter updates.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264615/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each timestep, COOT receives a query state (current observation concatenated with a short history of prior states) and a fixed-length context buffer of 5 recent cross-episode trajectories of (state, action, reward) triplets from interactions with the current partner. The context is managed FIFO, pushing out the oldest episode after each new one is appended. The model outputs a categorical action distribution over the environment's discrete action space; during training, query states are sampled independently from the context to prevent trivial trajectory continuation.

## Method

COOT reframes partner adaptation as an in-context learning problem where each distinct partner behavior is treated as a separate 'task' to be solved. A GPT-2 transformer (4 layers, 2 heads, 128-dim embeddings) is trained via supervised cross-entropy to predict best-response actions conditioned on raw interaction histories, rather than compressed latent encodings. Training data is generated from a Hidden-Utility Markov Game: diverse behavior-preferring agents are induced by event-based hidden reward functions, and their corresponding best-response policies are trained with MAPPO; the model learns to map observed partner trajectories to the optimal complementary action. At deployment, COOT adapts gradient-free by simply appending new episodes to its context buffer, enabling few-shot alignment within ~5–6 episodes and rapid recalibration after sudden partner switches.

## Results

On Overcooked (5 layouts), COOT achieves an overall average return of 68.79±2.33 and BR-prox of 0.57±0.02, outperforming the strongest baseline HSP (56.16±2.38 return, 0.44 BR-prox) by ~22% in return. The advantage is most pronounced in coordination-heavy layouts: on Coord. Ring Multi-recipe, COOT reaches 45.96 return (BR-prox 0.50) versus HSP's 29.35 (0.33) and PACE's 4.43 (0.05). On GRF 3-vs-1 with Keeper, COOT achieves a goal rate of 2.50±0.59, surpassing BC (1.97), HSP (1.46), and MEP (1.11). In the human study, COOT earns the highest collaboration (4.0/5) and adaptivity (3.1/5) ratings and is selected as best partner by 50% of participants (18/36).

## Baselines & Comparisons

COOT outperforms all baselines across every Overcooked layout, with the largest margins in coordination-heavy settings. Against HSP (Yu et al., 2023), COOT gains +12.6 return overall (68.79 vs. 56.16) and +0.13 BR-prox (0.57 vs. 0.44); the gap widens to +16.6 return on Coord. Ring Multi-recipe. Against MEP (Zhao et al., 2023), COOT leads by +27.0 return overall and by a factor of ~15× on Counter Circ. (28.28 vs. 1.89), where MEP's population-based training fails to generalize. Against PACE (Ma et al., 2024), COOT dominates: 68.79 vs. 35.95 overall return, and on Coord. Ring Multi-recipe COOT scores 45.96 while PACE collapses to 4.43 (BR-prox 0.05). HSP-ft (gradient-based fine-tuning) matches vanilla HSP almost exactly, confirming that few-shot gradient updates provide no meaningful adaptation benefit. GAMMA (Liang et al., 2024) and PLASTIC (Barrett, 2015) are compared in Appendix F; the paper notes COOT's advantage over HSP/MEP is conservative since those baselines use handcrafted dense rewards while COOT relies solely on sparse environmental rewards.

## Relation to Prior Work

COOT builds architecturally on the Decision Pretrained Transformer (DPT; Lee et al., 2023), which reframes RL as sequence modeling over (s, a, r) triplets, but shifts the learning objective from task generalization to partner-specific best-response prediction. For dataset generation, it adopts the Hidden-Utility Self-Play (HSP; Yu et al., 2023) framework of event-based biased rewards to induce diverse partner behaviors. The key conceptual departure is from prior few-shot coordination methods (PACE, PEARL, LIAM) that compress interaction histories into fixed latent representations: COOT instead conditions directly on full raw trajectories through a transformer, treating each partner's behavioral style as a distinct 'task' to be solved in-context, thereby avoiding the information loss and instability introduced by auxiliary reconstruction or identity-prediction losses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

COOT demonstrates that in-context learning—a mechanism previously applied to task generalization—can be repurposed for partner generalization in multi-agent coordination, offering a gradient-free, few-shot adaptation mechanism that is practical for real-world human-AI collaboration where fine-tuning is infeasible. The human study and non-stationarity experiments (adaptation within ~4 episodes of a partner switch) provide evidence that this approach is not merely a benchmark artifact. Key limitations include the absence of explicit communication, restriction to small teams (2–3 agents) in simulation-based benchmarks, and a strict cold-start protocol; extending to larger teams, embodied settings with rich perception, and communication-based coordination are identified as important open directions.
