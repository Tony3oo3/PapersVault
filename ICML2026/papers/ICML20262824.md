## Problem

Existing KV cache eviction methods designed for LLMs suffer severe performance degradation when applied to Vision-Language Models because they ignore the modality gap: vision tokens are highly redundant and mutually similar, while text tokens are diverse, causing cumulative attention scores to disproportionately evict text KVs and collapse generation quality. This paper identifies three concrete manifestations of this gap (intra-modality attention dominance, low text-vision attention, and sharp attention shifts at the modality boundary) and shows that directly transferring LLM eviction heuristics to VLMs is fundamentally suboptimal.

## Input / Output

TGV-KV operates on the full KV cache produced after the prefill phase of a VLM, where the input sequence consists of vision tokens (from a ViT encoder, potentially thousands for high-resolution images or video) concatenated with text tokens (prompt + system instructions). Given a global retention budget (e.g., 5%–50%), the method outputs a layer-wise pruned KV cache in the same format as the original, which is then used during autoregressive decoding. The method is entirely training-free and plug-and-play: it requires only the attention matrices already computed during prefill, with no additional forward passes or calibration data.

## Method

TGV-KV comprises three synergistic submodules that ground vision KV eviction in textual guidance. (1) Text-Vision Budgeting (TVB) slices the text-vision cross-attention matrix at each layer, sums it, and normalizes across layers to assign more KV budget to layers with stronger cross-modal information exchange. (2) Text-Weighted Ranking (TWR) first identifies 'dominant text tokens' via positional-averaged column sums of the text-text attention (accounting for the causal mask), then re-weights each row of the text-vision attention by these normalized text importance scores to produce a text-grounded vision KV importance score; text KV importance is scored by standard self-attention column sums. (3) Text-Prioritised Retention (TPR) enforces a hard priority: all text KVs are retained whenever the layer budget exceeds the text token count, and text KVs are only evicted under extreme budget constraints (budget ≤ N_t), ensuring that the small, information-dense text context is never sacrificed to the large, redundant vision context. The key insight is that visual saliency is intrinsically text-dependent, so using text attention as a weighting signal for vision eviction resolves the modality gap that causes naive cumulative-attention eviction to fail.

## Results

TGV-KV preserves 99.2% of full-KV accuracy on VizWiz-VQA (63.2 vs. 63.7) and 97.4% on TextVQA (64.0 vs. 65.7) with LLaVA-NeXT at an extreme 5% KV retention budget. On Qwen3-VL-8B at 5% retention, it retains 92.5% DocVQA ANLS (88.0 vs. 95.1) and 92.1% VizWiz accuracy (64.4 vs. 70.0). In terms of efficiency, TGV-KV reduces KV cache memory from 3.91 GB to 0.20 GB at 5% retention with 8k context, and boosts end-to-end throughput by 52.6% (32.2 vs. 21.3 tokens/s) at 16k context length.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TGV-KV outperforms all six KV eviction baselines (StreamingLLM, SnapKV, H2O, ElasticCache, PrefixKV, AirCache) across every model, task, and budget setting tested. The gap is most dramatic at extreme budgets: at 5% retention on LLaVA-NeXT, TGV-KV achieves 63.2 on VizWiz while H2O drops to 24.0 and ElasticCache to 18.9; on Qwen3-VL-8B DocVQA, TGV-KV scores 88.0 versus SnapKV's 22.6 and H2O's 58.1. At 50% retention, TGV-KV is within 0.5–1.0 points of the vanilla model on most tasks, whereas H2O and ElasticCache already show 5–15 point drops. Against token pruning methods (VisionZip, CDPruner, DivPrune, VisPruner) in Table 6, TGV-KV maintains near-full accuracy (e.g., 60.4 vs. 60.7 GQA at 32 retained tokens) while pruning methods degrade by 3–8 points, confirming that layer-wise KV eviction preserves information that token pruning permanently discards. AirCache, the closest VLM-specific competitor, is consistently outperformed by TGV-KV (e.g., 82.8 vs. 76.5 ChartQA, 92.3 vs. 85.5 DocVQA on Qwen3-VL-8B at 5% vision KV retention), attributed to TGV-KV's text-weighted importance scoring and text-prioritised retention policy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of KV cache eviction for LLMs (H2O, SnapKV, StreamingLLM) and VLM-specific compression (ElasticCache, PrefixKV, AirCache), but departs from all of them by explicitly modeling the modality gap between text and vision tokens. Rather than treating all tokens uniformly or applying a fixed layer budget, TGV-KV repurposes the general eviction framework with three text-grounded policies: cross-modal attention for budget allocation, text-weighted cross-attention for vision importance scoring, and a text-prioritised retention rule. The closest prior work, AirCache, also uses text to guide vision eviction but lacks the mutual information flow analysis in budget allocation and the explicit text-priority retention criterion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: 95% memory reduction and 52.6% throughput gain at 5% retention across five VLM architectures makes this immediately relevant for deploying high-resolution VLMs on resource-constrained hardware. The systematic modality gap analysis (Observations 3.1–3.3) provides a reusable diagnostic framework for future multimodal compression work. Limitations include reliance on prefill attention maps (adding a one-time overhead), evaluation limited to LLaVA and Qwen3-VL families, and the TPR policy's assumption that text KV is always more critical, which the authors acknowledge may be suboptimal for long-text/short-vision regimes (Appendix D). Open questions include extension to audio or 3D modalities and whether the text-grounding principle transfers to other compression paradigms beyond KV eviction.
