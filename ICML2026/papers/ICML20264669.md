## Problem

GRPO-style training estimates thought-level advantages by group-normalizing rewards from a single sampled continuation per thought, leading to high-variance advantage estimates and unstable gradient updates. Although tree-style branching is widely adopted in practice to mitigate this, no principled theoretical explanation exists for why branching helps, and it remains unclear whether continuation-level branching is merely a helpful heuristic or a necessary mechanism for accurate advantage estimation under fixed-temperature GRPO-style estimators without value models.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt or an image-plus-text instruction and produces a structured output segmented by XML tags: a reasoning prefix (thought) ending at  or </analysis>, followed by one or more answer continuations. For Math, the continuation spans both <process> and <answer> segments; for other tasks it is the <answer> segment alone. The key structural constraint is that K independent thoughts are sampled per prompt, and M independent answer continuations are sampled per thought, with the thought-answer boundary serving as the branching point for advantage estimation.

## Method

The paper's core theoretical contribution is a multivariate delta-method analysis of shared-context advantage estimation under GRPO-style group normalization, revealing a fundamental sampling-dimension asymmetry: increasing continuations per thought (M) drives the leading-order advantage estimation variance to zero at rate 1/M, whereas increasing independently sampled thoughts (K) alone converges to a strictly positive variance floor of σ²_{R_i}/(M·σ²_π). This establishes continuation-level branching as a principled and potentially necessary mechanism rather than a heuristic. The practical instantiation, GRPO-MA, branches at explicit XML delimiters ( or </analysis>), samples M answers per thought, computes thought-level advantages by averaging continuation rewards into a value V(th_i) and then group-normalizing across K thoughts, and computes answer-level advantages by group-normalizing all individual rewards. The training objective combines clipped surrogate terms at both the thought and answer levels under the joint sampling distribution, with LoRA-based parameter-efficient fine-tuning on Qwen2.5-VL-3B/7B and Qwen3-4B across math, code, vision, and embodied manipulation tasks.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct, GRPO-MA (T4A4) achieves 70.58% Pass@1 on GSM8K and 3.40% Pass@1 on AIME 2024, outperforming the matched GRPO baseline (T4A1: 62.54% and 2.07%) by +8.04 and +1.33 points respectively. On code, GRPO-MA reaches 58.66% Pass@1 on HumanEval and 6.73% Pass@1 on LiveBench, surpassing GRPO-T4A1 (56.10%, 6.59%) and even the 4×-cost thought-scaling baseline T16A1 (57.80%, 6.30%) at only ~1.2× per-step cost. Across vision tasks (object detection, affordance, trajectory, OCR-VQA, demand prediction), GRPO-MA consistently achieves the best or near-best scores while recording the lowest Gradient Spike Score (GSS@10), e.g., 1 spike vs. 15–30 for GRPO baselines on object detection. On the sparse-reward manipulation task, GRPO-MA-T4A4 reaches 31.40% Seen / 16.00% Unseen success rate, nearly 3× the ManipLLM-7B baseline (22.80% / 7.63%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), GRPO-MA-T4A4 wins on every reported metric: +8.04 pts on GSM8K Pass@1, +1.33 on AIME Pass@1, +2.56 on HumanEval, and +0.14 on LiveBench, while also reducing gradient spikes (GSS@10) from 13–15 to 5 on math and from 24–25 to 10 on code at lower per-step cost than T16A1. On the trajectory-prediction comparison (Table 6), GRPO-MA outperforms DAPO (Yu et al., 2025) by 33.0 DFD / 29.5 HD / 24.6 RMSE, Dr.GRPO (Liu et al., 2025) by 29.5 DFD / 27.7 HD / 24.3 RMSE, and GRPO-CARE (Chen et al., 2025) by 37.1 DFD / 32.5 HD / 28.2 RMSE, confirming that loss-function refinements alone do not address the thought-level variance problem. TreeRPO (Yang et al., 2025) with 64-token segments is the closest competitor (157.03 DFD vs. 151.10 for GRPO-MA), but still trails by 5.9 DFD / 6.2 HD / 2.4 RMSE, which the authors attribute to higher-variance value estimation from token-length-based branching versus semantic XML-tag boundaries. On the manipulation task, GRPO-MA-T4A4 (31.40% Seen) surpasses ManipLLM-7B (Li et al., 2024) by +8.6 pts Seen and +8.4 pts Unseen, and also beats its own CoT-SFT variant (28.18%) and the no-thought GRPO-NoThink variant (10.60%), demonstrating that both CoT quality and answer-level branching are necessary. The efficiency comparison is particularly striking: GRPO-MA-T4A4 matches or exceeds T16A1 on final performance at roughly 55–60% of its per-step wall-clock cost, and on Qwen3-4B (Table 8) it matches T16A1's math Pass@1 (18.07 vs. 17.90) at ~55% of the step cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) by introducing multi-answer sampling per thought (GRPO-MA) and provides the first theoretical variance analysis explaining why tree-style branching reduces advantage estimation variance. It departs from prior tree-style methods (VinePPO, TreePO, TreeRL, TreeRPO, SPO) that demonstrated branching empirically but lacked a principled explanation, and from GRPO stabilization methods (DAPO, Dr.GRPO, GPG) that modify the loss or normalization without addressing the fundamental sampling-variance asymmetry. The key conceptual departure is framing branching as a variance-reduction mechanism via the multivariate delta method, revealing that continuation-level branching (M) and context-level branching (K) play fundamentally different roles.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides practitioners with a clear, actionable principle for allocating sampling budget in GRPO-style training: spend extra samples on continuations per thought rather than on more independent thoughts, since only the former drives estimation variance to zero. This is practically important because GRPO is now widely used for LLM reasoning, and the GRPO-MA instantiation requires minimal implementation changes (just sample multiple answers after a delimiter). Limitations include the first-order delta-method approximation under independence assumptions, fixed-temperature analysis, and validation primarily at the thought-answer breakpoint; open questions include adaptive breakpoints, variance-aware continuation allocation, and whether the asymmetry persists with value models or different normalizations.
