## Problem

Existing generalization analyses for supervised contrastive representation learning either assume i.i.d. tuples (violated in practice when tuples are built from a finite labeled pool) or, in the non-i.i.d. setting, require all class-wise risk estimators to concentrate uniformly, yielding sample complexity that scales with ρ_min^{-1} — the rarest class probability. This dependency is overly pessimistic in extreme multi-class regimes with many tail classes that contribute minimally to the overall population risk, and it reduces to a dependence on the number of classes R only under perfectly balanced distributions.

## Input / Output

The method takes N labeled data points drawn from a mixture of R class-conditional distributions, a number of negatives k, and a prefixed class of representation functions F, and constructs collision-free (k+2)-tuples where anchor-positive pairs share a class and all negatives are drawn from other classes. It outputs a U-Statistic estimator U_N for the population collision-free contrastive risk L_φ(f) together with a uniform excess risk bound over F. The estimator is structured as a debiased linear combination of two auxiliary risks (arbitrary-collision and at-least-one-collision) weighted by the plug-in collision probability τ̂, rather than a direct average over collision-free tuples.

## Method

The paper contributes two estimators with progressively tighter sample complexity. First, it refines the prior class-wise U-Statistic U_N^hl by imposing non-uniform concentration accuracy across classes, eliminating the ρ_min^{-1} factor and achieving O(R) sample complexity independent of the class distribution. Second, and more substantially, it introduces a new U-Statistic U_N that decomposes the collision-free risk via the identity L_φ = (1/(1−τ))L_Ω − (τ/(1−τ))L_Λ, where τ is the population class-collision probability, and estimates each component separately. To handle the within-tuple dependence that makes the natural estimators biased, the paper constructs auxiliary estimators by randomly permuting the dataset and partitioning it so that anchor-positive pairs and negative samples are drawn from disjoint subsets, then averaging over all permutations. The resulting sample complexity scales as k(1−τ)², recovering the classical O(k) rate in extreme multi-class settings where τ→0. Empirical experiments on MNIST, FashionMNIST, CIFAR-10, and CIFAR-100 (including long-tailed variants) confirm that U_N outperforms both U_N^hl and standard SupCon, particularly on rare classes.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 (100 balanced classes, the extreme multi-class regime), the proposed U_N estimator achieves a macro F1 of 0.3335 at k=3 negatives, outperforming both the prior class-wise estimator U_N^hl (0.2723) and standard SupCon (0.2919). On imbalanced MNIST (one dominant class at 50%), U_N reaches F1=0.9821 versus 0.7771 for U_N^hl, while SupCon is comparable at 0.9837. On imbalanced FashionMNIST, U_N achieves F1=0.4195 vs. 0.3222 for U_N^hl, though SupCon (0.8745) remains stronger due to the simpler task. Performance degrades for all methods as k increases, consistent with known long-tailed contrastive learning behavior.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the prior class-wise U-Statistic U_N^hl (Hieu & Ledent, ICML 2025), the proposed U_N estimator wins on nearly all metrics across all four datasets, with the largest gaps on rare-class F1 (e.g., +0.20 on imbalanced MNIST, +0.06 on CIFAR-100) and on the extreme multi-class CIFAR-100 setting where U_N surpasses U_N^hl by 6.1 F1 points at k=3. The only exceptions are a few CIFAR-10 (imbalanced) cells where U_N^hl slightly edges out U_N. Against standard SupCon (Khosla et al., NeurIPS 2020), U_N clearly wins on CIFAR-100 (0.3335 vs. 0.2919 F1), confirming the theoretical advantage in the extreme multi-class regime where the collision probability τ→0. However, SupCon remains competitive or superior on the imbalanced FashionMNIST and CIFAR-10 subsets, suggesting that the debiasing benefit of U_N is most pronounced when the number of classes is large relative to k. Overall, the empirical results validate the theoretical prediction that U_N's sample complexity of O(k) (independent of R) translates into better tail-class generalization in long-tailed and extreme multi-class scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Hieu & Ledent (2025), which first formulated a U-Statistic estimator for supervised contrastive representation learning under non-i.i.d. tuple dependencies (tuples constructed from a finite labeled pool). The prior work's analysis required all class-wise risk estimators to concentrate uniformly, yielding a sample complexity scaling with ρ_min^{-1} (the rarest class probability). This paper (i) refines that analysis to remove the ρ_min dependency entirely, achieving O(R) sample complexity regardless of class distribution, and (ii) introduces a fundamentally different debiased U-Statistic that decomposes the collision-free risk into collision-allowed and at-least-one-collision components, recovering the O(k) rate from the i.i.d. tuples regime (Arora 2019; Lei et al. 2023) in extreme multi-class settings where the class-collision probability τ is small.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical contribution is meaningful for the CRL community: it shows that the pessimistic ρ_min^{-1} scaling in prior supervised CRL generalization bounds was an artifact of the analysis rather than a fundamental limitation, and that a different estimator formulation can match the sample complexity of the idealized i.i.d. tuples regime. Empirically, the new estimator shows consistent improvements on tail classes in long-tailed distributions (e.g., ~14-point precision gap on FashionMNIST at k=7), suggesting practical relevance for extreme multi-class problems like fine-grained recognition or anomaly detection. Open questions include whether the R ≥ k assumption and the additive R-dependent terms in the bounds can be further tightened, and whether the debiased decomposition generalizes to other non-i.i.d. settings (e.g., domain adaptation or multi-modal CRL).
