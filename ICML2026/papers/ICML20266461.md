## Problem

When a neural network estimates age from a face photograph, it is unclear whether the transient intermediate representations it computes during inference constitute 'biometric data' under GDPR Article 4(14), BIPA, or the EU AI Act. No regulatory guidance addresses this question, and the empirical capability of age estimation models to actually identify individuals has never been systematically measured against the thresholds that existing biometric standards require.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266461/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each model receives a single RGB face image and produces a scalar age estimate (or facial attribute label) as its sole output; all intermediate activations are discarded after the forward pass. For the verification evaluation, embeddings are extracted from every intermediate layer via global average pooling and L2-normalization, then pairwise cosine similarity is computed to yield FNMR at fixed FMR operating points. The evaluation uses three standard benchmarks (LFW with 6,000 pairs, AgeDB-30 with 30-year age gaps, CFP-FP with frontal-to-profile pairs) rather than the models' native training data.

## Method

The paper evaluates 14 models spanning age estimation, facial attributes, face recognition (ArcFace ResNet-100 as baseline), and general-purpose vision (DINOv3, Perception Encoder, ImageNet ResNet-50) by extracting layer-by-layer embeddings, applying global average pooling and L2-normalization, and measuring FNMR@FMR on three face verification benchmarks. Primary results are reported at FMR=1% (100× more permissive than NIST/FIDO's 0.01% requirement) to ensure statistical reliability on LFW's 3,000 impostor pairs, with FNMR@FMR=0.01% also provided for regulatory comparison. A secondary experiment attaches a learned attention pooler to each frozen backbone's final layer and trains it with ArcFace loss on Glint360k, testing whether identity information is latent in the representations but inaccessible via simple pooling. Results show all age estimation models fall two orders of magnitude below the 5% FNMR threshold required by NIST SP 800-63-4 and FIDO, and even after attention probing, performance on harder benchmarks (AgeDB-30, CFP-FP) remains at 28–98% FNMR.

## Results

Across 14 models evaluated on three face verification benchmarks, age estimation models achieve FNMR of 27–95% at FMR=1% on LFW, compared to 0.23% for the ArcFace face-recognition baseline—a gap of two orders of magnitude. At the regulatory operating point (FMR=0.01%), age estimators reach 64–99% FNMR on LFW, far exceeding the 5% threshold required by NIST SP 800-63-4 and FIDO Alliance. On the harder AgeDB-30 and CFP-FP benchmarks, all non-face-recognition models saturate at 69–100% FNMR regardless of layer depth, confirming that age-related features are orthogonal to identity-discriminative features.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ArcFace (ResNet-100) serves as the face-recognition reference and dominates all other models by two orders of magnitude: 0.23% FNMR at FMR=1% on LFW versus 27–95% for age estimators. Among general-purpose vision models, Perception Encoder (20.8%) and DINOv3 (37.5%) outperform all age estimation models on LFW, yet still fall 80–160× short of ArcFace and saturate near 96–98% FNMR on AgeDB-30. The best age estimation model, the Commercial Age Estimator (Sumsub), achieves 26.8% FNMR on LFW at FMR=1%, but degrades to 97.2% on AgeDB-30 and 80.4% on CFP-FP, indicating its features capture age-related geometry rather than identity. After attention probing with identity-labeled training (Table 3), the Commercial Age Estimator improves to 2% FNMR on LFW but still reaches only 17% at the regulatory FMR=0.01% threshold and 67% on AgeDB-30—remaining an order of magnitude worse than ArcFace. The remaining age/attribute models (FairFace, Age+gender ViT, InsightFace, FaceLib, SSR-Net, Age Estimation PyTorch) all perform at or near chance (57–95% FNMR on LFW), with no meaningful improvement from probing on harder benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the face verification evaluation methodology (FNMR@FMR on LFW, AgeDB-30, CFP-FP) from the biometric recognition literature to answer a regulatory question that no prior ML work has addressed: whether age estimation models' intermediate representations constitute biometric data. It positions itself against the face recognition baseline (ArcFace/InsightFace) as the reference for identification capability, and uses the CoCa-style attention pooler as a secondary probe to test whether identity information is latent but inaccessible in frozen age-estimation features. Rather than building on a prior ML method, the paper carves new ground by systematically applying verification benchmarks to 14 age estimation and facial attribute models across CNN and ViT architectures, bridging the gap between biometric evaluation practice and data protection law.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first empirical evidence base for a regulatory question that has remained explicitly unresolved: the ICO's Yoti sandbox deemed the Article 4(14) capability question 'outside the scope,' and no enforcement action has addressed transient intermediate representations. The two-orders-of-magnitude gap and the AgeDB-30 orthogonality finding (age features are anti-correlated with identity features) give regulators a concrete, testable criterion for distinguishing classification from identification. Limitations include the bounded architecture families tested (a model built on a frozen face-recognition backbone would trivially retain identity), the use of public-figure benchmarks rather than diverse populations, and the fact that the paper addresses capability but not the broader 'reasonably foreseeable misuse' argument under the EU AI Act. Open questions include whether the transient-processing-versus-template-storage distinction should be codified in guidance, and how to handle the function-creep objection for proprietary models whose internal representations cannot be independently audited.
