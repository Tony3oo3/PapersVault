## Problem

Existing multimodal large language models, including proprietary (GPT-5.1, Gemini) and medical MLLMs (MedGemma, HuatuoGPT), produce ECG interpretations that are structurally complete but clinically incorrect, with diagnosis accuracy below 32% and pervasive hallucinations. Prior ECG-specialized MLLMs rely on prompt-based data generation from pretrained LLM knowledge, introducing factual medical errors, and suffer severe performance degradation and cross-modal inconsistency when either the ECG signal or image modality is missing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261981/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts three input channels: a 12-lead ECG time-series signal (processed through FeatureDB to extract 14 beat-wise physiological sequences per lead including RR intervals, P/PR/QRS/T/ST/QT measurements), a rendered ECG image (synthesized via ECG-imagekit), and an instruction text prompt. The output is a structured six-step protocol reasoning trace (five monograph-defined phases plus a final medical reasoning step) enclosed in a think block, followed by a brief summary narrative and a final diagnosis in an answer block. The architecture is modality-agnostic: it can accept signal-only, image-only, or both, with the <ecg> tag controlling time-series token injection independently of the <image> tag.

## Method

ECG-R1 combines three innovations. First, Protocol-Guided Instruction Data Generation extracts grounded physiological features via FeatureDB and feeds them into a five-phase protocol prompt derived from a clinical monograph, steering DeepSeek-V3.1-Terminus to produce 30K structured interpretation samples from MIMIC-IV-ECG that enforce explicit thresholds and differential exclusion. Second, a decoupled dual-encoder architecture (Qwen3-VL-8B for image, ECG-CoCa for signal) with independent projectors replaces GEM's coupled encoding, and Interleaved Modality Dropout (IMD) randomly drops one modality or swaps token-block order during SFT and RL, with theorems proving that minimizing the IMD mixture risk upper-bounds worst-environment risk and controls cross-modal TV discrepancy. Third, Reinforcement Learning with ECG Diagnostic Evidence Rewards (EDER) adds per-step evidence-coverage rewards (matching extracted key diagnostic phrases at each of the six reasoning steps) on top of format and Jaccard diagnosis-accuracy rewards, optimized via DAPO to prevent length collapse and encourage verifiable stepwise clinical reasoning.

## Results

On the ECG-Grounding test set (2,381 samples), ECG-R1 (RL) achieves a Diagnosis Accuracy of 80.29, outperforming the previous best ECG-specialized model GEM (74.70) by +5.59 points, and the best proprietary MLLM GPT-5.1-Instant (31.48) by nearly 50 points. ECG-R1 also leads on all six secondary metrics, with the largest gains over GEM in ECG Feature Grounding (+15.23), Evidence-Based Reasoning (+15.93), and Clinical Diagnostic Fidelity (+21.30). On ECG-Bench abnormality detection, ECG-R1 achieves new state-of-the-art accuracy on CSN (90.4%) and G12EC (84.5%), while remaining competitive with GEM on PTB-XL Super (81.7 vs 83.4 AUC) and CODE (91.4 vs 91.5 AUC).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ECG-R1 (RL) decisively outperforms GEM, the strongest prior ECG-specialized MLLM, on all seven metrics: Diagnosis Accuracy 80.29 vs 74.70 (+5.59), ECG Feature Grounding 80.57 vs 65.34 (+15.23), Evidence-Based Reasoning 79.08 vs 63.15 (+15.93), and Clinical Diagnostic Fidelity 84.20 vs 62.90 (+21.30). Against PULSE (66.13 DA), ECG-R1 gains +14.16 in Diagnosis Accuracy and +38.97 in ECG Feature Grounding. All non-ECG-specialized MLLMs—including GPT-5.1-Instant (31.48 DA), MedGemma-27B (25.23), and Qwen3-VL-8B (20.03)—fall far below ECG-R1, with the best proprietary model trailing by nearly 50 points in Diagnosis Accuracy. On ECG-Bench abnormality detection, ECG-R1 achieves SOTA on CSN (90.4% ACC) and G12EC (84.5% ACC), slightly trails GEM on PTB-XL Super AUC (81.7 vs 83.4) and CPSC 2018 AUC (74.9 vs 79.1), but matches or exceeds GEM on CODE AUC (91.4 vs 91.5) and F1 (86.7 vs 86.4). In cardiologist evaluation, ECG-R1 scores 4.34/5 on Analytical Accuracy vs GEM's 3.89/5, confirming fewer factual errors, and 4.38/5 on Overall Satisfaction vs GEM's 3.84/5.

## Relation to Prior Work

ECG-R1 directly departs from GEM (Lan et al., 2025), the first ECG omni-perception MLLM, by replacing its coupled time-series-to-image encoding with a decoupled dual-encoder architecture and introducing Interleaved Modality Dropout for robustness under modality missing. It also challenges the ECG-Grounding data generation paradigm (which relies on pure LLM prompting from extracted features) by proposing Protocol-Guided Instruction Data Generation grounded in a clinical monograph's five-phase diagnostic workflow. On the training side, ECG-R1 extends the reasoning-RL lineage of DeepSeek-R1 and R1-VL by adding stepwise ECG Diagnostic Evidence Rewards (EDER) that supervise intermediate reasoning steps rather than only final-answer correctness, and it builds on Qwen3-VL-8B and ECG-CoCa as its LLM and time-series encoder backbones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic quantitative evidence that severe hallucinations are pervasive across proprietary, open-source, and medical MLLMs on routine ECG interpretation, with even GPT-5.1 achieving only ~31% diagnosis accuracy while producing structurally complete but clinically incorrect analyses. This finding has direct safety implications for any deployment of MLLMs in clinical cardiology. Open questions remain around rare but clinically important conditions (RL data is concentrated on top-500 report patterns), reliable QT/QTc interval estimation, and the need for prospective multi-site validation before any real-world deployment. The protocol-guided data generation and IMD strategy may generalize to other medical imaging modalities where structured diagnostic protocols exist.
