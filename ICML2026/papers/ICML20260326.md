## Problem

Sample compressibility guarantees PAC-learnability of distribution families in the noise-free setting, but no general framework existed for extending these guarantees to stochastic perturbation regimes. Existing results on learning from noisy samples are problem-specific (e.g., simplices, Gaussian mixtures) and do not provide structural conditions under which learnability is preserved for arbitrary compressible classes. This paper identifies a small set of minimax-necessary and sufficient conditions under which sample-compressible families remain learnable from additively corrupted samples.

## Input / Output

The method consumes n i.i.d. noisy samples X̃_i = X_i + ζ_i (where ζ_i ~ G are i.i.d. noise vectors from a known symmetric product distribution G) drawn from an unknown f* ∈ F, together with the compression parameters (τ, t, m) and the noise CDF Φ_G. It produces an estimator f̂ ∈ F with an explicit ℓ₂ error bound of order ε · inf_{(α,ξ)∈P(F)} 24/√(B_G(α)(1−ξ)) and a corresponding TV bound under bounded-support or sub-Gaussian tail conditions. The sample complexity inflates the clean-sample bound N_clean(ε,δ) by an additive term Õ(d·τ(ε)/ε²)·log(1+σr), where the inflation depends on dimension, compression length, and noise scale.

## Method

The core technique is a perturbation quantization strategy that interfaces with the sample compression framework: each unknown noise vector ζ_i is approximated by a quantized surrogate from a finite grid I, generating |I|^{dτ(ε)} candidate denoised sample sequences. The Local Lipschitz Decodability assumption (Assumption 2.1) bounds the TV error introduced by residual quantization noise, yielding an augmented (τ, t + dτ·log|I|, m)-compression scheme for the noisy class F⋆G. To recover f* itself (not just f*⋆G), the paper invokes the Hausdorff-Young inequality to transfer the TV bound on f*⋆G to a pointwise Fourier-domain bound on f*−f̂ over low frequencies, then uses the Low-Frequency Property (Assumption 2.7) to control the high-frequency energy and obtain a global ℓ₂ bound. Both assumptions are shown minimax necessary via Le Cam's method, and the framework is instantiated for k-mixtures of uniform distributions over hyperrectangles, resolving a previously open sample-complexity problem.

## Results

The main result (Theorem 2.10) establishes that a sample-compressible family F with compression parameters (τ, t, m) and local Lipschitz constant r remains PAC-learnable from additive noise samples with sample complexity n ≥ N_clean(6ε, δ/2) + Õ(dτ(ε)/ε²)·log(1+σr), yielding an ℓ₂ error bound of ‖f̂−f*‖₂ ≤ ε·inf_{(α,ξ)∈P(F)} 24/√(B_G(α)(1−ξ)). For Gaussian noise N(0,σ²I_d), the error constant scales as 24√(Vol(B₂^d(1))·α^d·e^{(σα)²}/(1−ξ)), revealing an exponential dependence on noise variance (dimension blow-up). For the concrete application to k-mixtures of d-dimensional uniform distributions (Proposition 3.2), the sample complexity is O(d²k/ε²·log²(rdkσ/(εδ))), resolving a previously open problem.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or comparison tables. The paper positions itself as a generalization of prior problem-specific results: it extends the Fourier-based noisy learning framework of Saberi et al. (2023) for high-dimensional simplices to arbitrary sample-compressible distribution families, and builds on the sample compression learnability theorem of Ashtiani et al. (2018) by adding a perturbation quantization layer. The key theoretical contribution is showing that the two structural assumptions (Local Lipschitz Decodability and the Low-Frequency Property) are minimax-necessary (Claims 2.15, 2.17), meaning that without them, no algorithm can PAC-learn the family from noisy samples regardless of sample size. The paper does not claim matching lower bounds for the sample complexity, noting this remains open even in the noise-free regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sample compression framework of Ashtiani et al. (2018), which established that sample compressibility guarantees PAC-learnability in the noise-free regime, to the setting of additive independent noise. It generalizes the problem-specific Fourier-based noisy learning results of Saberi et al. (2023) for high-dimensional simplices into a unified framework applicable to any sample-compressible distribution family, introducing a perturbation quantization technique that interfaces naturally with compression schemes. The two structural assumptions (Local Lipschitz Decodability and the Low-Frequency Property) are shown to be minimax-necessary, going beyond prior work that only provided sufficient conditions for specific models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first general framework showing when sample-compressible families remain learnable under stochastic perturbation, resolving a gap where prior results were entirely problem-specific. The minimax impossibility results (e.g., a Gaussian family with unbounded variance becomes fundamentally unlearnable from noisy samples) clarify the precise structural boundary between learnable and unlearnable regimes under noise. Key open questions include: matching minimax lower bounds under sample compressibility alone, extending to efficient polynomial-time algorithms, and whether the two structural assumptions can be derived from one another. The exponential sample complexity blow-up with noise variance (dimension blow-up) is a significant practical limitation that the authors conjecture is unavoidable under TV distance.
