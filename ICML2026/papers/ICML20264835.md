## Problem

Existing methods to accelerate AdamW in LLM pre-training (curvature-based updates, matrix preconditioning, momentum mixing) require substantial modifications to the training pipeline and additional hyperparameter tuning, hindering practical adoption. The paper asks whether a minimal, plug-in transformation applied directly to the gradient can improve convergence without altering the base optimizer's internal logic or hyperparameters.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a raw stochastic gradient vector g ∈ R^d at each training step and produces a transformed gradient φ_p(g) = (sign(g_i)|g_i|^p)_i via a single elementwise sign-power operation with scalar exponent p > 0 (default p = 1.2). This powered gradient is then fed into an unmodified base optimizer (AdamW, Muon, Adagrad), which outputs updated model parameters. The only new hyperparameter is p, which the paper shows should be > 1 in the high-noise regime typical of LLM pre-training and < 1 for large-batch/low-noise settings.

## Method

GradPower applies the elementwise sign-power transformation φ_p(z) = |z|^p sign(z) to each gradient before it enters the base optimizer's moment estimation, requiring only a single line of code change. The key insight is that in the noise-dominated regime of LLM pre-training, p > 1 amplifies the slow 'river' (flat) directions that drive loss reduction while suppressing fast oscillations along sharp directions, effectively accelerating the dynamics that matter most. Theoretically, the paper proves that AdagradPower with p ∈ (0,1) in the low-noise regime achieves a convergence rate of O(log T / √T) in the (p+1)-norm, which is 2/(p+1) times faster than vanilla Adagrad, and that in the high-noise regime with p > 1, convergence is accelerated by a constant factor R^(p-1)/σ < 1. Empirically, AdamWPower (p = 1.2) consistently outperforms AdamW across LLaMA (66M–2B) and Qwen2MoE (0.5B–2B) models on C4 and OpenWebText under both cosine and warmup-stable-decay schedules, with the largest gains on MoE architectures, and integrates additively with Muon and Blockwise LR.

## Results

On LLaMA (2B) pre-trained on C4, AdamWPower (p=1.2) achieves an average downstream score of 48.26 vs. 47.72 for AdamW, with the largest gain on WinoGrande (59.43 vs. 56.83). Across dense LLaMA models (66M–2B) and MoE Qwen2MoE models (0.5B–2B), AdamWPower consistently achieves lower terminal loss than well-tuned AdamW under both cosine and warmup-stable-decay schedules. The absolute improvement on Qwen2MoE-2B (0.028) exceeds that on LLaMA-2B (0.022), and the wall-clock overhead is only 0.4% per step.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AdamW (the primary baseline), AdamWPower (p=1.2) achieves lower terminal loss in every configuration tested: LLaMA 66M/0.2B/0.4B/1B/2B on C4 and OpenWebText, Qwen2MoE 0.5B/1B/2B on C4, under both cosine and wsd schedulers. On downstream tasks, AdamWPower wins 5 out of 6 benchmarks (losing only ARC-C by 0.17 points) and improves the average by 0.54 points. Against Muon, MuonPower (p=1.2) outperforms the well-tuned Muon baseline in LLaMA pre-training, confirming GradPower's compatibility beyond the Adam family. Against AdamW with Blockwise LR (Wang et al., 2025), AdamWPower with Blockwise LR achieves a 0.045 loss improvement over AdamW with Blockwise LR alone, which is nearly the sum of the individual gains from AdamWPower (0.015) and Blockwise LR (0.03), indicating the two techniques are largely orthogonal and complementary.

## Relation to Prior Work

GradPower positions itself as a lightweight, plug-in alternative to the growing family of AdamW modifications that alter optimizer internals (momentum mixing, curvature injection, matrix preconditioning). Conceptually, it is closest to the Powerball method (Yuan et al., 2019), which also applies a sign-power transformation to gradients, but extends that idea from classical optimizers (GD, SGD) to modern adaptive optimizers in the LLM pre-training regime, and studies both p<1 and p>1 regimes. The theoretical analysis builds on the Adagrad convergence framework of Defossez et al. (2022), while the empirical motivation draws on the fast-slow dynamics / river-valley landscape literature (Wen et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2024) that identifies flat-direction progress as the key driver of loss reduction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical appeal is strong: a single line of code with 0.4% wall-clock overhead that consistently improves terminal loss across dense and MoE architectures, multiple scales, datasets, and LR schedulers without requiring any additional hyperparameter tuning. The theoretical result linking the optimal power p to the gradient noise regime (p*>1 in high-noise, p*<1 in low-noise) provides actionable guidance for practitioners choosing batch sizes. Key open questions remain: why MoE models and wsd schedules benefit disproportionately (the paper offers only a hypothesis about suppressing sharp-direction vibrations), whether a dynamic p schedule adapted to the evolving SNR during training could yield further gains, and whether the Adagrad-based convergence guarantees transfer directly to Adam's more complex update rule.
