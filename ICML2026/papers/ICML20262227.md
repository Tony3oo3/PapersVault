## Problem

Sparse autoencoders have successfully decomposed polysemantic language features into interpretable concepts, but applying them to the visual modality is obstructed by two barriers: training vision-specific SAEs requires difficult and ambiguous labeling of visual features, and fine-tuning existing LLM SAEs to incorporate visual information incurs prohibitive computational costs (e.g., ~20% of GPT-3's compute for Gemma Scope). The paper asks whether pre-existing, labeled language SAEs can be directly reused to interpret visual concepts without any new SAE training.

## Input / Output

The method takes 224×224 RGB images processed into 256 patch tokens by a frozen vision encoder (DINOv2, CLIP, or I-JEPA) along with a text prompt, and produces text descriptions, per-layer SAE concept activations on visual tokens, and steered text outputs after arithmetic interventions. Steering operates on specific spatial patch positions at the first 2–3 LLM layers (depending on architecture), with a fixed steering coefficient of 10, enabling localized modifications that preserve the surrounding scene context.

## Method

VISTA augments the standard LLaVA architecture (frozen vision encoder → trainable linear projector → frozen LLM) with a single additional training term: an SAE Reconstruction Loss that penalizes the MSE between projected visual tokens and their reconstruction by the LLM's pre-trained text SAEs at the first 5 layers. This forces the projector to map visual embeddings onto the sparse manifold defined by textual SAE features, achieving interpretability transfer without training or labeling any vision-specific SAEs. The projector is trained in two stages (595k Conceptual Captions pre-training, 665k LLaVA-1.5 fine-tuning) with both vision encoder and LLM frozen. Once aligned, fine-grained steering becomes a direct arithmetic operation: adding or subtracting SAE decoder directions at selected patch positions to remove or replace specific visual concepts while leaving the rest of the scene intact. The framework reveals that DINOv2's self-supervised local objective provides the spatial fidelity (SLA 0.927) necessary for reliable localized steering, whereas CLIP's contrastive global objective causes spatial confusion (SLA 0.201).

## Results

VISTA with DINOv2 achieves a Mean Score of 1.43 on object removal and 1.46 on object replacement (out of 2.0), representing a 35% improvement over the strongest vision-only baseline (VisionSAE, 1.06) on removal and a 47% improvement over PatchSAE (0.99) on replacement. The SAE-constrained alignment yields a threefold increase in LLM-verified matching rate compared to unconstrained training. DINOv2 achieves a Spatial Localization Accuracy of 0.927, substantially outperforming I-JEPA (0.702) and CLIP (0.201) on the Gemma-2-2B backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VISTA with DINOv2 decisively outperforms both vision-only SAE baselines on the steering task. Against VisionSAE (Pach et al., 2025), which trains SAEs on global CLIP tokens, VISTA improves the removal Mean Score from 1.06 to 1.43 (+35%) and the replacement Mean Score from 0.65 to 1.46 (+125%), with the gap driven by VisionSAE's inability to perform localized edits without hallucinating scene changes. Against PatchSAE (Lim et al., 2025), which trains SAEs on individual CLIP patches, VISTA improves removal from 0.65 to 1.43 (+120%) and replacement from 0.99 to 1.46 (+47%); PatchSAE's higher S1 rate (55 vs. 49 on removal) indicates it frequently introduces hallucinated details even when the target concept is correctly modified. The ablation without SAE constraints (same DINOv2 encoder, no reconstruction loss) collapses to 0.22/0.06, confirming that the SAE alignment is essential for steering to function at all. Across LLM backbones (Gemma-2-9B, LLaMA-3.1-8B), VISTA with DINOv2 maintains Mean Scores of 1.29–1.56, while CLIP-based VISTA remains below 0.62, reinforcing that spatial fidelity of the vision encoder is the binding constraint.

## Relation to Prior Work

VISTA sits at the intersection of two research threads: SAE-based mechanistic interpretability for language models (Bricken et al., 2023) and cross-modal alignment in LLaVA-style VLMs (Liu et al., 2023; Merullo et al., 2023). It departs from the dominant approach of training vision-specific SAEs (Pach et al., 2025; Lim et al., 2025) by instead constraining the visual projector to map tokens into an LLM's pre-existing, already-labeled text SAE manifold via a reconstruction loss. The paper also extends and partially challenges Venhoff et al. (2025), who observed 'delayed alignment' of visual tokens in deeper LLM layers, by showing this phenomenon is architecture-dependent and does not occur for LLaMA-3.1-8B without explicit SAE constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VISTA provides a low-cost, practical path to visual interpretability that avoids the prohibitive compute of training or fine-tuning vision SAEs and the ambiguity of labeling visual features, making it immediately applicable to any LLM with a published SAE (e.g., Gemma Scope, LLaMA Scope). The finding that DINOv2's self-supervised objective preserves patch-level spatial fidelity far better than CLIP's contrastive objective (0.927 vs. 0.201 SLA) is a practically important design consideration for anyone building fine-grained visual steering or attribution systems. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-LLaVA architectures (e.g., native multimodal models), whether SAE concept coverage limits interpretability for rare visual concepts, and whether the alignment enables the feature-circuit and hallucination-detection analyses the authors outline as future work.
