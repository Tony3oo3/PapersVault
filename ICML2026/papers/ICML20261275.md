## Problem

Existing multilingual medical benchmarks evaluate narrow aspects like diagnostic accuracy in English or a handful of languages, leaving the trustworthiness of language models in mid- and low-resource healthcare settings largely unexplored. No prior benchmark systematically assesses the full spectrum of trustworthiness dimensions—truthfulness, fairness, safety, robustness, and privacy—across diverse model families (proprietary, open-weight, medical) in a multilingual clinical context.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261275/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each evaluation sample pairs a clinical context passage (from MedlinePlus or FDA drug documents) with a question in one of 15 languages, formatted as open-ended QA, multiple-choice, or masked-token prediction. Adversarial variants inject perturbations (misspellings, code-switching, distraction text, abbreviation confusion), jailbreak prompts use persona-based or PAIR-optimized attacks, and privacy probes embed synthetic personal identifiers in clinical notes. Outputs are task-specific scores—accuracy for hallucination MCQs, cosine-similarity-based sycophancy/consistency scores, Refuse-to-Answer rates for OOD/jailbreak/disparagement, leak rates for privacy, and Perspective API toxicity scores—aggregated into per-model, per-language-tier (HR/MR/LR) rankings.

## Method

CLINIC is constructed via a two-step multilingual generation pipeline: an LLM first produces an English question from an English MedlinePlus passage, then a second prompt uses the English question plus both the English and target-language passages to generate a linguistically grounded question in the target language, avoiding direct translation artifacts. The benchmark spans 18 tasks across 5 trustworthiness dimensions and 6 healthcare subdomains, with 28,800 samples uniformly distributed (~1,920 per language) across 15 languages in three resource tiers. All samples are validated by 22 domain experts (Cohen's κ = 0.82 for trustworthiness alignment, 4.04/5 for multilingual quality). Evaluation employs GPT-4o as an LLM judge for open-ended tasks (validated against expert annotations at κ = 0.75), embedding-based similarity for sycophancy and consistency, the Perspective API for toxicity, and binary RtA/leak metrics for refusal and privacy tasks, enabling standardized comparison across 13 models from SLMs to proprietary systems.

## Results

Across 28,800 samples spanning 15 languages and 18 trustworthiness tasks, proprietary models (Gemini-2.5-Pro, GPT-4o-mini, Gemini-1.5-Flash) consistently lead, achieving 93–95% honesty scores in high-resource languages and 68–69% jailbreak refusal rates. Medical LLMs (OpenBioLLM-8B, UltraMedical, MMedLLama) underperform dramatically, scoring only 30–42% on honesty and 36–40% on jailbreak resistance. Performance degrades monotonically from high- to mid- to low-resource languages (HR > MR > LR) across all five dimensions, with the sharpest drops in honesty (e.g., Phi-4mini: 83.5% HR → 24.5% LR) and privacy leakage (QwQ-32B: 85.16% HR → 87.50% LR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates 13 models across three groups: proprietary (GPT-4o-mini, Gemini-1.5-Flash, Gemini-2.5-Pro), open-weight (LLaMA-3.2-3B, Qwen-2.1-5B, Phi-4mini, Qwen3-32B, DeepSeek-R1, DeepSeek-R1-LLaMA, QwQ-32B), and medical (OpenBioLLM-8B, UltraMedical, MMedLLama). Proprietary models dominate on safety-critical metrics: Gemini-2.5-Pro achieves 95.20% honesty (HR) vs. 40.75% for OpenBioLLM-8B, and 68.75% jailbreak refusal vs. 39.63% for the same medical model. Among open-weight models, DeepSeek-R1-LLaMA and QwQ-32B approach proprietary performance on honesty (94.50% and 93.12% HR) but lag on jailbreak resistance (40.79% and 43.64% HR vs. 68.75% for Gemini-2.5-Pro). Medical models consistently rank last across nearly all dimensions, suggesting that domain-specific fine-tuning does not compensate for weaker safety alignment and multilingual coverage. The HR > MR > LR degradation is universal, with low-resource languages exposing the largest gaps (e.g., Phi-4mini honesty drops from 83.5% to 24.5%, a 59-point collapse).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of three existing research threads: multilingual medical benchmarks (MedExpQA, MMedBench, XMedBench), general trustworthiness evaluation frameworks (TrustLLM, MultiTrust, CARES), and medical LLM development (MedPalm, HuatuoGPT, OpenBioLLM). Prior work has addressed these threads in isolation—CARES evaluates trustworthiness but only for English multimodal medical models, while multilingual medical benchmarks test accuracy without probing safety, fairness, or privacy. CLINIC is the first to unify all three, evaluating 13 model families across 15 languages on 18 trustworthiness tasks, using a two-step LLM-plus-expert generation pipeline grounded in MedlinePlus and FDA sources.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that even state-of-the-art proprietary models leak privacy information at rates of 46–87% and that medical domain models consistently underperform general-purpose models on nearly every trustworthiness dimension, challenging the assumption that domain-specific fine-tuning improves reliability. The systematic HR > MR > LR degradation (especially in honesty, fairness, and privacy) highlights a critical equity gap for global healthcare deployment. Open questions include whether the identified gaps can be closed via safety-aware fine-tuning or RLHF, whether the GPT-4o-as-judge dependency introduces systematic bias, and how findings generalize to multimodal clinical settings. The paper explicitly defers mitigation strategies to future work, leaving the field with a diagnostic tool but no prescribed remedy.
