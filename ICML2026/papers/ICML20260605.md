## Problem

Human ratings are inherently subjective and item-specific in their variability, yet quantifying this uncertainty typically requires expensive repeated measurements from multiple raters. This paper addresses the gap of estimating item-level rating uncertainty from a single observation by leveraging paired auxiliary objective data (e.g., neuroimaging) that carries information about the underlying item state, without requiring multiple human assessments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260605/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a paired observation: a 12-dimensional human rating vector (behavioral scores from ADI, SRS, ADOS, SCQ batteries) and a 116×116 resting-state fMRI functional connectivity matrix (parcellated into 116 regions via AAL, yielding 13,456 features). It produces three outputs: the posterior distribution over an item-specific uncertainty variable U (capturing rating fluctuation beyond content), a soft feature-selection mask over the auxiliary data identifying which connectivity features are informative versus noise, and the conditional predictive distribution p(x|y) for held-out ratings given only imaging. The uncertainty prior is data-conditioned, meaning the expected magnitude of rating noise varies with the specific auxiliary observation.

## Method

The framework is a Bayesian graphical model with three latent variables: a shared content vector Z (with a Gaussian mixture prior for heterogeneous subgroups), an uncertainty variable U with a zero-mean, data-conditioned Gaussian prior p(u|y) parameterized by a neural network, and a binary feature-selection mask M that gates each auxiliary feature between a content-dependent distribution and a background noise distribution. Amortized variational inference uses modality-specific encoders (ViT for imaging, MLP for ratings) to produce Gaussian posteriors over Z and U, while a cross-attention network over ViT features and Z produces the Bernoulli mask probabilities. The ELBO objective is augmented with a sparsity regularizer on the mask to prevent degenerate all-informative solutions, and the mixture prior on Z is updated via EM with moment matching. The key design choice is grounding U in the auxiliary data through the conditional prior, which prevents the uncertainty from absorbing systematic content that should be captured by the linear-Gaussian rating model x = Az + u.

## Results

On the ABIDE I/II validation set, the proposed method achieves near-perfect reconstruction of human ratings (R²_X = 0.976) with RMSE_X = 2.45, roughly half the error of the best multimodal baseline (MoPoE, RMSE_X = 5.02). For conditional prediction from imaging alone, it attains R²_X_pred = 0.561 with 90% predictive interval coverage of 0.891 and mean interval width of 10.47, which is 20–40% tighter than all baselines at comparable coverage. On the held-out ACE test set, the method maintains strong reconstruction (R²_X = 0.889) and the tightest intervals (MPIW_90 = 9.26), though Deep Ensembles slightly edges it on explained variance (0.589 vs. 0.501) at the cost of nearly double the interval width.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three multimodal generative baselines (MVAE, MMVAE, MoPoE), the proposed method dominates on every reconstruction and prediction metric: R²_X is 0.976 vs. 0.46–0.69, and RMSE_X is roughly halved. On the ACE test set, the gap widens dramatically—MoPoE's reconstruction collapses to R²_X ≈ 0 while the proposed method retains 0.889. Among the uncertainty-quantification baselines (Heteroscedastic Regression, Deep Ensemble, Evidential Regression), the proposed method achieves the best interval sharpness (MPIW_90 = 10.47 on validation, 9.26 on test) at near-nominal coverage, whereas Evidential Regression matches coverage (0.892) but with intervals 68% wider (17.65). The one notable exception is Deep Ensembles on the ACE test set, which slightly outperforms the proposed method on R²_X_pred (0.589 vs. 0.501) and PICP_90 (0.849 vs. 0.819), but at the cost of intervals nearly twice as wide (17.60 vs. 9.26) and a higher RMSE_X_pred (7.13 vs. 7.07). Overall, the proposed method wins on reconstruction quality, interval calibration, and cross-dataset generalization, with Deep Ensembles being the closest competitor on point-prediction accuracy in the out-of-distribution test setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes three research threads—aleatoric uncertainty estimation (heteroscedastic/evidential regression), multimodal generative modeling (MVAE, MMVAE, MoPoE), and crowd-sourced label uncertainty (Dawid-Skene)—into a single Bayesian graphical model that had not previously been unified. It departs from heteroscedastic and evidential regression by encoding uncertainty in the data-generating process rather than as a predicted output variance, and from multimodal VAEs by explicitly modeling label unreliability through a latent uncertainty variable with a data-conditioned prior. The feature-selection mask over auxiliary data, learned jointly with the content representation via amortized variational inference, is a novel architectural choice that distinguishes this work from both the uncertainty-estimation and multimodal-representation literatures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practically important gap: quantifying the reliability of a single human rating (e.g., a clinician's behavioral assessment) without the costly repeated measurements that classical inter-rater reliability methods require. The learned feature-selection mask provides interpretable, domain-meaningful outputs (midline hubs, cerebellum in autism fMRI), which could guide targeted data collection or model design. Key limitations include the assumption that rater inputs are unobserved or subjectively combined, and the absence of a direct comparison against empirical inter-rater uncertainty, which the authors flag as essential future work. Whether the single-instance formulation generalizes to settings where partial rater information is available remains an open question.
