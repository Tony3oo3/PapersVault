## Problem

Existing interactive world models condition on pixel-based histories (sliding windows or key-frame retrieval), which limits spatial memory to a few seconds of context and provides only partial, viewpoint-dependent information about the 3D environment. As rollouts extend, identifying relevant past evidence from an ever-growing bank of pixel observations becomes increasingly difficult, causing 3D consistency and temporal coherence to degrade sharply. The paper asks whether explicitly tracking a persistent 3D scene state can replace pixel-history conditioning to enable coherent, long-horizon world simulation.

## Input / Output

PERSIST is initialised with a single RGB frame (360×640×3) and optionally a 48³ voxel world frame (2138 semantic labels per voxel), then receives continuous user actions encoded as a 23-dimensional multi-hot vector of key presses and discretised mouse movements. At each timestep it autoregressively produces a 360×640 pixel frame, an updated 48³ latent 3D world state centred on the agent, and a 10-dimensional camera parameter vector (3D position, 6D rotation, FOV). The 3D world frame is encoded into 12×12×12×48 latent patches by a 3D-VAE, while pixel frames are encoded into 36×64×16 latent patches by a 2D-VAE, and the projected 3D-to-2D correspondence (w_2D) carries 192 channels per pixel position with depth-ordered voxel features.

## Method

PERSIST decomposes world simulation into three coupled components trained separately with rectified flow matching and diffusion forcing: (1) a world-frame denoiser W_θ (causal DiT with 3D spatial attention, absolute XYZ position embeddings, and cross-attention over pixel patches augmented with Plücker embeddings) that predicts the next latent 3D scene state; (2) a camera model C_θ (1D causal transformer) that predicts position and pitch/yaw/FOV residuals from past cameras, world frames, and actions; and (3) a pixel denoiser P_θ (causal DiT acting as a learned deferred shader) that denoises pixel latents conditioned on a depth-ordered stack of projected 3D features (w_2D) obtained via GPU-native depth-peeling rasterization. The key architectural insight is that the camera state acts as a spatial lookup key into the persistent world frame, making information retrieval independent of episode length and enforcing geometric consistency by construction. To mitigate exposure bias from the autoregressive loop, each denoiser is trained with diffusion forcing and a 10% random noise augmentation on cross-component inputs, allowing the three models to be assembled at inference without fine-tuning.

## Results

On the Luanti benchmark, PERSIST achieves FVD of 129/141/148 at 200/400/600 frames, substantially outperforming Oasis (409/687/875) and WorldMem (358/–/–), with generation quality remaining stable over extended horizons while pixel-history baselines degrade sharply. In the human study (28 participants, 800+ evaluations), PERSIST scores 2.6/5 overall versus 1.9 for Oasis and 1.5 for WorldMem, with the strongest gains in 3D consistency (2.5 vs 1.9/1.7) and temporal consistency (2.5 vs 1.8/1.5). The PERSIST+w0 variant (with explicit 3D initialization) further reduces FVD to 80/93/104 and raises the overall user rating to 3.0/5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PERSIST decisively outperforms both pixel-history baselines on all metrics. Against Oasis (sliding-window conditioning), PERSIST reduces FVD by 68% at 200 frames (129 vs 409) and by 83% at 600 frames (148 vs 875), while Oasis's FVD nearly doubles over the episode. Against WorldMem (key-frame retrieval), PERSIST achieves 129 vs 358 FVD at 200 frames, and WorldMem's quality degrades so rapidly that it cannot be evaluated beyond 200 frames. In the human study, PERSIST wins head-to-head comparisons against both baselines across all four rating dimensions (Visual Fidelity, 3D Consistency, Temporal Consistency, Overall), with the largest margin over WorldMem (2.6 vs 1.5 overall). A key qualitative finding is that pixel-history baselines produce irrecoverable artifacts by the 200th timestep, whereas PERSIST maintains stable rollouts for thousands of steps, with the 3D world-frame providing structural guidance that allows the pixel denoiser to self-correct from transient visual artifacts.

## Relation to Prior Work

PERSIST departs from the dominant paradigm of autoregressive video world models (Oasis, WorldMem, GameGen-X) that condition on pixel-based histories or retrieve key-frames from a growing memory bank, arguing that pixel observations are partial, redundant, and viewpoint-dependent carriers of spatial information. Instead, it draws on the architecture of traditional 3D game engines—maintaining a persistent, dynamically evolving 3D scene state and rendering observations from it—while implementing each component (world-frame prediction, camera tracking, world-to-pixel rendering) as a learned rectified-flow diffusion model with a causal DiT backbone. The rendering module specifically adopts the neural deferred shading paradigm (Thies et al., 2019) rather than NeRF-style ray marching, and exposure bias is mitigated via diffusion forcing (Chen et al., 2024). This positions PERSIST as a bridge between learned generative models and explicit 3D simulation, combining the flexibility of diffusion-based generation with the geometric consistency of a persistent spatial state.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicitly modelling a persistent 3D world state fundamentally changes the spatial-memory problem in world models: instead of an ever-growing retrieval task over pixel histories, the model performs a fixed-cost geometric lookup, enabling stable rollouts over thousands of steps where pixel-history baselines produce irrecoverable artifacts within 200 frames. This opens new capabilities—mid-episode 3D scene editing, explicit 3D initialisation, and modelling of off-screen dynamics—that are intractable with pixel-only representations. Key limitations include the requirement for ground-truth 3D supervision during training (restricting applicability to simulators or datasets with 3D annotations), a finite 3D region in memory, and the absence of real-time inference. The authors outline a clear roadmap: in-the-wild training via 2D-to-3D foundation models, end-to-end post-training to combat autoregressive drift, and unconstrained spatial memory via 3D memory banks—directions that will likely shape the next generation of interactive world models.
