## Problem

World models require action annotations that are scarce at scale, and existing latent action models have only been validated on narrow, task-aligned domains like video games or curated manipulation data. This paper tackles the unexplored challenge of training latent action world models on large-scale in-the-wild natural videos, where actions are far more diverse, environmental noise is prevalent, and no common embodiment exists across videos.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261250/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes video frames encoded by a frozen V-JEPA 2-L (or DINOv3) encoder into latent representations; the IDM receives pairs of consecutive representations (s_t, s_{t+1}) to infer 128-dimensional continuous latent actions, while the forward model predicts the next representation conditioned on past representations and the latent action. For downstream control, a lightweight controller maps real robot actions plus the previous frame's representation to latent actions, and planning takes an initial observation and goal observation to produce an action sequence via CEM. All prediction and planning operate in the encoder's latent space rather than pixel space, with a separate ViT-L decoder used only for visualization.

## Method

The core architecture jointly trains a ViT-L world model (with RoPE and frame-wise AdaLN-zero conditioning on latent actions) and an inverse dynamics model (two self-attention blocks on (t, t+1) frame pairs plus cross-attention with a learnable query) under an L1 prediction loss plus a latent action regularizer. The key contribution is a systematic study of three information-content regularizations—sparsity (L1 penalty with an L2 norm floor and a VICReg-inspired Variance-Covariance-Mean term), VAE-style noise injection (KL divergence to N(0,1)), and vector quantization—showing that constrained continuous latents (sparse or noisy) adapt to in-the-wild action complexity while discrete latents saturate near a deterministic baseline. The paper further demonstrates that learned latent actions are spatially localized and camera-relative (a consequence of lacking a common embodiment), yet remain transferable across videos and embodiments. A small cross-attention controller trained with L2 loss maps known actions and past representations to latent actions, enabling the in-the-wild-trained world model to solve DROID manipulation and RECON navigation planning tasks at performance comparable to action-annotated baselines.

## Results

On DROID robotic manipulation planning, the best latent action world model (noisy, high capacity) achieves a goal distance of Δxyz = 0.10 m, outperforming the action-annotated V-JEPA 2-AC baseline (0.15 m) while remaining below the domain-specific upper bound V-JEPA 2+WM (0.05 m). On RECON navigation, the same model achieves RPE = 0.40 and ATE = 1.40, beating the policy-based NoMaD baseline (RPE = 0.52, ATE = 1.93) but falling short of the purpose-built NWM world model (RPE = 0.35, ATE = 1.13). Across all settings, a moderate latent action capacity (neither maximally constrained nor unconstrained) yields the best downstream planning performance, confirming a U-shaped trade-off between information content and identifiability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On DROID planning, the proposed latent action world model (noisy, high capacity; Δxyz = 0.10 m) outperforms V-JEPA 2-AC (0.15 m), a model trained with explicit action labels on the same domain, demonstrating that latent actions learned purely from in-the-wild video can serve as an effective control interface. However, it remains below the V-JEPA 2+WM upper bound (0.05 m) from Terver et al. (2026), which is specifically designed for physical planning with action-annotated data. On RECON navigation, the proposed method (RPE = 0.40, ATE = 1.40) clearly beats the policy-based NoMaD baseline (RPE = 0.52, ATE = 1.93), confirming that world-model-based planning with latent actions surpasses learned policies. Against the domain-specific NWM world model (RPE = 0.35, ATE = 1.13), the proposed method is competitive but does not match its performance, as NWM is trained directly on navigation data with known actions. Notably, the paper also shows that adding just 10% DROID data to the in-the-wild pretraining further improves planning to Δxyz = 0.09 m, approaching the action-annotated upper bound.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the latent action model (LAM) line of work (Edwards et al., 2019; Rybkin et al., 2019; Bruce et al., 2024; Ye et al., 2025; Bu et al., 2025) from narrow, task-aligned domains (video games, tabletop manipulation, curated robot data) to large-scale in-the-wild natural videos (YouTubeTemporal-1B, HowTo100M). It builds on the V-JEPA 2 frozen-encoder paradigm for latent-space world modeling and the standard IDM + forward model joint training setup, but departs from the dominant vector quantization regularization by systematically comparing it against continuous sparsity (with VICReg-inspired VCM constraints) and VAE-like noise injection. The key methodological departure is the argument that in-the-wild video complexity demands flexible continuous latent actions rather than the discrete codebooks that prior LAM work relies on.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a world model trained exclusively on action-free in-the-wild videos can, via a lightweight controller, solve robotic manipulation and navigation planning tasks at performance comparable to models trained with explicit action labels—removing a critical annotation bottleneck for scaling world models. Open questions remain around direct sampling/planning in the latent action space (especially for sparse latents requiring MCMC), dynamic regularization that adapts to per-video complexity, and single-stage joint encoder/world-model training that could unlock better representations. The finding that learned latent actions are camera-relative rather than object-centric is both a limitation (no common embodiment) and a strength (enables cross-object motion transfer), and its implications for downstream VLA pipelines remain to be fully explored.
