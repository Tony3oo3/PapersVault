## Problem

Existing symbolic regression algorithms rank candidates by a trade-off between fitting accuracy and formula complexity, implicitly assuming that simpler formulas are structurally more rational. In practice, this framework permits accurate and compact but structurally irrational formulas—nested, ill-conditioned expressions that amplify rounding noise by orders of magnitude and are physically inexplicable. No scalable, parameter-free metric existed to evaluate the internal structural stability of a formula independent of its input-output mapping.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a symbolic formula represented as a computation tree (operators as internal nodes, variables/constants as leaves) together with a data distribution D (a set of input samples from the scientifically relevant domain). It produces a single scalar EIC score—the worst-case log₁₀ precision loss across all nodes in the tree—along with per-node variance amplification factors s_k(x) that localize structural instability. Computation requires only the formula structure and forward evaluations on D; no external hyperparameters, ground-truth labels, or symbolic differentiation engine are needed (a stochastic two-path estimation via Algorithm 2 suffices).

## Method

EIC models each formula as an information channel and quantifies how inherent multiplicative rounding noise (modeled as zero-mean, variance σ² at every operation node) is amplified as it propagates up the computation tree. The core recursive relation s_k²(x) = 1 + Σ_i κ²_{k,i}(x)·s_i²(x) decomposes total instability into locally injected noise (the constant 1) and child-propagated noise scaled by the squared partial relative condition number κ_{k,i} = (y_i/y_k)(∂e_k/∂y_i). EIC is then defined as max_k log₁₀(√E_D[s_k²(x)]), taking the worst-case node to prevent internal instabilities from being masked by insensitive downstream operations. The metric is parameter-free, scale-invariant under unit changes, and sensitive to algebraic rewriting (e.g., ln(exp(x)) scores higher than x). In practice, EIC is integrated as a penalty term in heuristic search fitness functions (steering GP/MCTS toward stable Pareto frontiers) or as a rejection-sampling threshold (EIC < 2) to filter pre-training corpora for generative SR models, improving sample efficiency by 2–4× and generalization R² by 22.4%.

## Results

On 133 SRBench white-box problems, EIC-enhanced GP and MCTS advance from Pareto Tier 2 to Tier 1, improving both accuracy (R²>0.99: 0.7288 and 0.7107 vs. 0.7215 and 0.6917 for vanilla) and reducing EIC by ~10% toward ground-truth levels (<1.0). For generative pretraining, EIC-guided rejection sampling (threshold θ=2) boosts sample efficiency by 357% (E2ESR), 199% (SNIP), and 287% (SR4MDL), while increasing final R² by 22.4% for E2ESR (0.55→0.68). A study with 108 human experts confirms EIC aligns with interpretability preferences in 69.8% of cases (p<10⁻³⁰), and EIC-filtered training distributions reduce JS/KL divergence to real physical formulas by 20–70%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EIC-GP and EIC-MCTS outperform their vanilla counterparts on both accuracy and EIC (e.g., MCTS: R² 0.7107 vs. 0.6917, EIC 1.052 vs. 1.185), advancing both to Pareto Tier 1 on white-box problems. Compared to PySR (EIC 2.586) and SR4MDL (EIC 1.580), EIC-enhanced methods achieve EIC values closer to ground-truth physical formulas (<1.0), while DSR and RSRM match low EIC but sacrifice accuracy (R² 0.34 and 0.25 vs. 0.71–0.73). For generative pretraining, EIC-filtered training dominates random pretraining across all three methods (E2ESR, SNIP, SR4MDL) in both sample efficiency and final R², and outperforms the PhyE2E LLM-based data construction baseline, which slightly reduces generalization due to limited formula diversity. AI Feynman 2, despite the highest complexity (113.2), maintains a moderate EIC (1.20), confirming that EIC captures structural rationality independently of formula length. LLM-generated formulas (LLM-SRBench Synth subset) exhibit substantially higher EIC than both SR-discovered and ground-truth formulas, indicating that LLMs hallucinate noise-amplifying substructures even when producing surface-plausible expressions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by repurposing concepts from numerical analysis (partial relative condition numbers, rounding-error propagation) and signal processing (noise figure, SNR degradation) to define a structural-stability criterion for symbolic formulas. It departs from the standard accuracy–complexity Pareto framework that underpins all major SR benchmarks (SRBench, SRBench++) and search algorithms (PySR, AI Feynman, DSR, RSRM), arguing that formula length and operator-weight metrics are blind to internal ill-conditioning. EIC is positioned as orthogonal to existing search strategies (GP, MCTS) and generative pretraining pipelines (E2ESR, SNIP, SR4MDL), serving as a plug-in third objective or data-filtering rule rather than a replacement for any of them.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete failure mode in AI-driven scientific discovery: SR algorithms routinely return formulas that are numerically accurate and compact yet structurally irrational, undermining trust and interpretability. By showing that a parameter-free, O(N)-computable stability metric can simultaneously improve Pareto frontiers in search and boost generative sample efficiency by 2–4×, EIC offers a practical, low-overhead addition to existing SR toolkits. Open questions remain: whether EIC generalizes to emergent or chaotic macroscopic dynamics, whether subformula-level (rather than max-node) analysis yields finer-grained diagnostics, and how EIC interacts with pretrained-model guidance in hybrid search pipelines. The reliance on the empirical data distribution as a proxy for the scientifically relevant domain is a acknowledged limitation that could matter for extrapolation.
