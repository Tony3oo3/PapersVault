## Problem

Self-improvement training for MLLMs, which iteratively fine-tunes models on their own generated reasoning traces, suffers from two interrelated failures rooted in using answer correctness as the sole quality signal: (1) data imbalance, where the model easily produces many correct solutions for simple queries but fails on hard ones, leading to over-training on easy samples; and (2) language prior bias, where the model over-relies on linguistic context and neglects visual cues, producing hallucinated reasoning traces that nonetheless arrive at correct answers and thus pass the correctness filter.

## Input / Output

Each training query consists of a system prompt, an RGB image (max 512×512 pixels), and a text instruction. The model generates a chain-of-thought reasoning trace followed by a final answer, both enclosed in  and <answer></answer> tags respectively, with a maximum output length of 2,048 tokens. During the self-improvement loop, K=10 candidate solutions are sampled per query at temperature 1.0, and the VAS filtering step (threshold τ=−0.5) removes low-quality correct solutions before they enter the training set, reducing the effective training data volume compared to baselines like ReST^EM.

## Method

VISTA introduces two complementary strategies into the iterative self-improvement loop. First, prefix resampling addresses data imbalance by exploiting the observation that errors in failed solutions typically occur late in the reasoning trace: it swaps the visual and instruction token order in the query, feeds the paraphrased context plus the original failed trace back into the model, and identifies the first 'critical token' where the actual token falls outside the model's Top-5 predictions under the perturbed context; that token is replaced with the Top-1 prediction, the trace is truncated, and J=3 new solutions are resampled from the corrected prefix. Second, the Vision-aware Attention Score (VAS) addresses language prior bias by extracting attention weights from the middle layer of the model, computing the normalized fraction of attention that output reasoning tokens allocate to visual tokens versus system and instruction tokens, and filtering out correct solutions whose VAS falls below a threshold, thereby removing hallucinated traces that over-rely on linguistic priors. The filtered dataset is then used for iterative SFT, DPO, or GRPO training, always re-initializing from the base model to prevent overfitting.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct, VISTA-SFT (3 iterations) achieves 65.87% average accuracy across SLAKE, VQA-Rad, and Geometry3K, a +13.66% gain over the SFT-Seed baseline (52.21%). On Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct, VISTA-SFT reaches 68.87% average (+6.67% over SFT-Seed). Across additional models (Qwen3-VL-2B, InternVL3-2B/8B) and tasks (ScienceQA, ChartQA), VISTA consistently outperforms all baselines by a clear margin, with up to +6.43% average gains in a single iteration. In DPO and GRPO post-training settings, VISTA also surpasses IRPO and ReST^EM-GRPO by +1.98% and +0.73% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VISTA outperforms all 10 baselines across every evaluated setting. Against the iterative SFT baselines, VISTA-SFT (Iter 3) beats ReST^EM by +3.31% (65.87 vs. 62.56) and STaR by +5.74% on Qwen2.5-VL-3B, with the gap widening on the harder Geometry3K task (37.27 vs. 32.28 and 30.78 respectively). R3V, which additionally leverages incorrect solutions for self-refine/self-select data, still trails VISTA by +4.70% overall on the 3B model. In the DPO setting, VISTA-DPO achieves 65.12% overall versus IRPO's 63.14% (+1.98%), and in GRPO training, VISTA-GRPO edges out ReST^EM-GRPO by +0.73%. For data collection strategies, prefix resampling outperforms both DART-Math's difficulty-aware sampling (Tong et al.) and Socratic-guided answer-conditioned sampling (Ding et al.) on both SLAKE and VQA-Rad. For data selection metrics, VAS substantially outperforms Answer PPL and DOLA-based Reasoning Depth, with the latter two even underperforming random selection on SLAKE. Finally, VISTA's zero-shot results surpass Self-Consistency (5-sample majority vote), and combining VISTA with SC yields a further +16.62% gain over SFT-Seed on SLAKE.

## Relation to Prior Work

VISTA extends the self-improvement training paradigm for MLLMs established by STaR and ReST^EM, which iteratively generate, filter, and fine-tune on self-produced reasoning traces. Its prefix resampling strategy is motivated by the critical-token concept from Lin et al. (2025) and the observation that errors in failed solutions cluster in later reasoning steps, but departs from prior difficulty-aware sampling (Tong et al., 2024) and answer-guided methods (Ding et al., 2025) by reusing partially correct prefixes rather than discarding failed attempts entirely. The Vision-aware Attention Score (VAS) is a single-pass simplification of the two-pass vision-aware head divergence metric from He et al. (2025), replacing the expensive image-removal comparison with a direct measurement of attention allocation to visual tokens in a middle layer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VISTA addresses two concrete failure modes of self-improvement in MLLMs—data imbalance on hard queries and language-prior-driven hallucinations—that limit the practical utility of iterative self-training. The framework's compatibility with SFT, DPO, and GRPO post-training paradigms makes it broadly applicable, and the VAS metric offers a cheap, interpretable quality signal for filtering self-generated data. However, experiments are limited to 3B–8B models, and the prefix resampling strategy may constrain trajectory diversity when critical tokens appear late, leaving open whether the approach scales to 30B+ or MoE architectures. A key open question is whether the attention-based hallucination signal generalizes to video or multi-image settings where visual token proportions differ substantially.
