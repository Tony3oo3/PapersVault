## Problem

Existing activation steering methods for LLM alignment apply fixed, layer-independent perturbations in an open-loop fashion, ignoring how interventions propagate through transformer layers and lacking true online error feedback. While control-theoretic formulations have been proposed, they either require offline training of steering policies, use reactive non-predictive interventions, or fail to exploit structural regularities in LLM dynamics that could enable efficient closed-loop control with minimal invasiveness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264185/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and a fixed-weight LLM, along with offline-computed contrastive prompt sets (e.g., toxic vs. benign) used to derive per-layer feature directions and a user-specified feature strength hyperparameter λ. During inference, it produces layer-wise additive activation perturbations applied at the final token position of each transformer block, yielding steered text generation. Because LQR gains are precomputed offline from layer-wise Jacobians, online execution requires only matrix-vector multiplications per layer per generation step, with no re-solving of the optimal control problem.

## Method

The paper's key empirical finding is that transformer block dynamics are locally linear: Jacobians at different reachable activations within the same layer exhibit strong subspace alignment, enabling the LLM to be modeled as a linear time-varying (LTV) system. Exploiting this, they linearize each transformer block around a nominal trajectory and adapt the classical Linear Quadratic Regulator to synthesize closed-loop steering controllers via Riccati recursions, computed offline and reused across autoregressive steps without recomputation. A novel Linear Feature Setpoint (LFS) signal adaptively scales the desired feature strength per layer based on activation norms, providing semantically meaningful tracking targets that enable disturbance rejection under linearization error. The resulting A-LQR controller is training-free, fully closed-loop (reacting to realized activations at each layer), and achieves 30–50× toxicity reduction while preserving model quality, outperforming open-loop baselines across eight model architectures from 1B to 70B parameters.

## Results

On toxicity mitigation (Real Toxicity Prompts, 5×1000 trials), A-LQR achieves 30–50× reduction in toxic outputs relative to base models (e.g., 0.18% on Gemma-2-2B vs. 4.16% original; 0.12% on Llama-3-8B vs. 5.14% original), while preserving MMLU scores within ~1–3 points and Dist-2 within 0.01 of the base model. On TruthfulQA, A-LQR improves the Truthfulness×Informativeness (T*I) score by 17–40% over base models (e.g., 76.28 on Qwen-2.5-14B vs. 53.17 original; 84.70 on Qwen-2.5-32B vs. 55.29 original) without degrading informativeness below 94%. For mechanistic jailbreaking (AdvBench), the A-LQR+ variant (all-token intervention) achieves 96–97% attack success rate on Qwen-2.5-3B/14B-Instruct, matching or exceeding the AAS baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

A-LQR consistently outperforms all six baselines on toxicity mitigation, achieving 2–5× lower toxic output rates than the best competing method across all four evaluated models (e.g., 0.12% vs. ActAdd's 0.56% on Llama-3-8B; 0.12% vs. ActAdd's 0.16% on Qwen-2.5-14B). ITI and PID-AcT achieve moderate toxicity reduction (≈8–10×) but degrade MMLU by 2–18 points (e.g., ActAdd drops Llama-3-8B MMLU from 66.04% to 47.76%), whereas A-LQR preserves MMLU within 1–3 points. On TruthfulQA, A-LQR achieves the highest T*I score on three of four models (Gemma-2-2B: 67.81 vs. ODESteer's 63.50; Qwen-2.5-14B: 76.28 vs. ODESteer's 71.56; Qwen-2.5-32B: 84.70 vs. S-PID's 78.52), while ODESteer is competitive on Llama-3-8B (62.36 vs. A-LQR's 63.63). The S-PID baseline, which shares A-LQR's LFS setpoint signal but uses a simpler PID controller, achieves comparable toxicity reduction on larger models (Qwen-2.5-32B: 0.28% vs. 0.22%) but induces large PPL spikes on smaller models (e.g., 191.95 on Llama-3.2-1B vs. A-LQR's 11.88), highlighting the value of the optimal-control formulation. For jailbreaking, A-LQR (last-token only) underperforms AAS on attack success rate (e.g., 85.6% vs. 76.0% on Qwen-2.5-3B-Instruct is actually A-LQR winning, but on Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct A-LQR gets 71.2% vs. AAS's 91.4%), while A-LQR+ (all-token) matches or exceeds AAS (96.2% vs. 76.0% on Qwen-2.5-3B; 97.1% vs. 77.9% on Qwen-2.5-14B).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the classical Linear Quadratic Regulator (Kalman et al., 1960) to LLM activation steering, building on the contrastive-vector framework of Turner et al. (2024b) for identifying semantic feature directions and the fixed-dimensional last-token intervention strategy of Karnik & Bansal (2025). It departs from prior control-theoretic steering methods (Kong et al., 2024; Cheng & Alonso, 2025; Nguyen et al., 2025) by exploiting a newly identified structural property—local linearity of transformer blocks—enabling predictive, closed-loop LQR control rather than the reactive or open-loop interventions used previously. The Linear Feature Setpoint (LFS) signal is a novel adaptive tracking target that replaces the fixed-magnitude perturbations used in most prior steering methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The local linearity finding (high Jacobian alignment across reachable activations within a layer) is of independent interest for mechanistic interpretability, suggesting transformer dynamics are more structured than previously assumed. The paper provides the first theoretical tracking-error guarantees for activation steering, and the 30–50× toxicity reduction (vs. 8–10× for baselines) across eight models demonstrates practical impact. Key open questions include automated tuning of the Q, R, and λ hyperparameters, reducing the memory cost of offline Jacobian computation (motivating low-rank compression), and extending to robust control formulations that handle larger linearization errors without degrading steering fidelity.
