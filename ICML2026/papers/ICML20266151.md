## Problem

Training 3D Gaussian Splatting at billion-primitive scale is fundamentally memory-bound: each Gaussian carries 59 float attributes plus gradients and Adam moments, so the aggregate parameter table exceeds even 24 GB GPU VRAM at only ~11M Gaussians, and prior host-offloading methods cap out near ~100M. The paper addresses how to scale single-GPU 3DGS training beyond this VRAM wall by exploiting the fact that each iteration activates only a small, spatially localized subset of Gaussians visible from the current camera batch.

## Input / Output

TideGS consumes multi-view RGB images with camera intrinsics/extrinsics and RGB-D observations (used for dense point-cloud initialization via backprojection), and produces a trained billion-scale 3DGS scene renderable in real time. Unlike standard 3DGS, the full parameter table resides on SSD and is never fully materialized in VRAM; only a capacity-bounded working set of spatial blocks is staged per iteration. Camera views are processed in a clustered TSP-ordered sequence rather than random shuffling to maximize temporal overlap between consecutive working sets.

## Method

TideGS virtualizes the 3DGS parameter table across an SSD-CPU-GPU hierarchy using three synergistic techniques. First, block-virtualized geometry Morton-sorts Gaussians into SSD-aligned blocks of 4096 primitives and uses per-block bounding spheres for conservative CPU-side frustum culling, so cross-tier traffic scales with the visible block set rather than total model size. Second, a hierarchical asynchronous pipeline organizes SSD storage as log-structured append-only segments with a per-block version index, maintains an LRU CPU cache with dirty-bit tracking for write-back, and overlaps SSD reads, PCIe transfers, and GPU rendering/backpropagation via dedicated I/O threads and double-buffered CUDA streams. Third, trajectory-adaptive differential streaming scores candidate blocks by next-step usefulness and recency under a camera-balanced Top-C budget, then transfers only the set-difference delta between consecutive resident sets, reducing PCIe volume to incremental working-set changes under smooth camera motion.

## Results

TideGS trains over 1 billion 3DGS primitives on a single 24 GB GPU, the first single-GPU method to do so. On MatrixCity at ~102M Gaussians, TideGS achieves 90.7 ms/iter with only 0.10 GB/iter PCIe traffic (4× less than CLM's 0.41 GB/iter) and 43.3% GPU utilization. At the ~1.1B scale, TideGS is the only evaluated single-GPU method that remains feasible, completing training at 525.6 ms/iter with 49.5% GPU utilization while CLM and Naive Offload both OOM. In the in-memory regime on Mip-NeRF 360, TideGS (Trajectory) reaches 3.50 img/s (within 11% of Native 3DGS's 3.95 img/s) with a PSNR gap of only 0.11 dB (28.92 vs. 29.03).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Native 3DGS (Kerbl et al., 2023), TideGS preserves reconstruction quality in the in-memory regime (PSNR 28.92 vs. 29.03, a 0.11 dB gap on Mip-NeRF 360) but extends the trainable scale from ~11.5M to over 1B Gaussians on the same 24 GB GPU. Against CLM (Zhao et al., 2026), the closest host-offloading baseline, TideGS reduces PCIe traffic by 4× at the ~102M scale (0.10 vs. 0.41 GB/iter) and cuts iteration time from 100.8 ms to 90.7 ms; at the ~1.1B scale, CLM fails with OOM (requiring ~45 GB for GPU-resident geometry) while TideGS trains successfully. Against the Naive Offload (ZeRO-Offload-inspired) baseline, TideGS avoids the OOM that Naive Offload hits at ~102M Gaussians, where per-iteration parameter residency alone consumes 12.13 GB for a 25M model. The paper also discusses Grendel-GS (Zhao et al., 2025) in the appendix as a 4-GPU distributed in-memory system that can match or exceed TideGS throughput with sufficient hardware, but at substantially higher infrastructure cost; TideGS targets the single-GPU operating point where such systems are infeasible.

## Relation to Prior Work

TideGS extends the host-offloading line of work for 3DGS training (ZeRO-Offload, CLM, GS-Scale) by pushing the memory hierarchy one level further down to SSD, where prior methods cap at ~100M Gaussians because they keep key geometry GPU-resident. Conceptually, it repurposes the sparse embedding-table caching paradigm (Wilkening et al., 2021) to the 3DGS setting, exploiting visibility-induced sparsity and camera-trajectory locality to treat VRAM as a working-set cache rather than a persistent parameter store. The trajectory-adaptive differential streaming (TSP-ordered views + set-difference block transfers) and Morton-ordered block virtualization are novel systems-level contributions that have no direct analogue in prior 3DGS offloading work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TideGS makes billion-scale 3DGS training feasible on a single commodity 24 GB GPU, lowering the hardware floor from multi-GPU clusters to a single workstation and shifting the bottleneck from VRAM capacity to SSD storage. This is practically significant for city-scale and aerial scene reconstruction where prior single-GPU methods saturate at 10–100M primitives. Key open questions include: how well the method degrades under unstructured image collections (where trajectory locality breaks down), whether optimizer-state cold restarts at high churn rates affect convergence quality on harder scenes, and whether the SSD-tier design can be combined with distributed multi-GPU training for even larger scenes.
