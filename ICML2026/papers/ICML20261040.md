## Problem

The stochastic linear bandits with parameter noise model—where the reward vector θ_t is sampled i.i.d. from a fixed unknown distribution—has received little theoretical attention compared to the additive noise and adversarial models. While the parameter noise model is trivially reducible to the adversarial model, it is unclear whether tighter, variance-dependent regret bounds are achievable, and no minimax-optimal algorithms or matching lower bounds existed for general action sets.

## Input / Output

At each timestep t, the learner selects an action a_t from a set A ⊆ R^d (either a finite set of size K or an ℓ_p unit ball) and receives a scalar reward X_t = a_t^T θ_t, where θ_t is drawn i.i.d. from an unknown distribution ν with mean θ* and covariance Σ. Rewards are bounded so that |a^T θ| ≤ 1 almost surely. The algorithm's output is the action sequence and a high-probability regret bound that depends on variance quantities: σ_max² (maximal action variance) for general sets, or σ_q² = (Σᵢ Σ_ii^{q/2})^{2/q} for ℓ_p balls, where q is the dual norm. The covariance matrix may be known or unknown, with the unknown case incurring an additional dT^{1/3} term.

## Method

The paper presents two algorithms. VASE (Algorithm 2) is a successive elimination algorithm for finite action sets that, in each phase, computes a G-optimal design, estimates per-action variances via a stopping-rule procedure, and uses these estimates both to reduce the number of exploration samples (proportional to σ²(a)·π(a) rather than uniformly) and as weights in a weighted least-squares estimator, preserving the same concentration error with fewer samples. VALEE (Algorithm 3) is a simple explore-exploit algorithm for ℓ_p unit balls (p ∈ (1,2]) that explores only the standard basis vectors using median-of-means estimation, adaptively doubling the exploration budget until the estimator's norm is small relative to the budget, then commits to the action â_i = sign(θ̂_i)|θ̂_i|^{q-1}/‖θ̂‖_q^{q-1}. The key insight enabling variance-dependent bounds is that in the parameter noise model, playing e_i for 1/ε² samples yields estimation error ε√(Σ_ii) rather than ε (as in additive noise), so the overall ℓ_q estimation error scales with σ_q² instead of d, yielding regret Õ(√(dTσ_q²))—optimal up to logarithmic factors and strictly better than the adversarial bound when variance is small.

## Results

For a general finite action set of size K, VASE (Algorithm 2) achieves regret $\widetilde{O}(d^2 + \sqrt{dT\log(K/\delta)\,\sigma_{\max}^2})$, which is minimax-optimal up to logarithmic factors. For $\ell_p$ unit balls with $p \in (1,2]$ and dual norm $q \geq 2$, the simple explore-exploit algorithm VALEE (Algorithm 3) achieves regret $\widetilde{O}(d + \sqrt{dT\,\sigma_q^2})$ when the covariance is known, matching the lower bound $\widetilde{\Omega}(\sqrt{dT\,\sigma_q^2})$ up to logarithmic factors. When the covariance is unknown, VALEE incurs an additional $d^{2/3+2/3q}\,T^{1/3}$ term. The key improvement over the additive noise model is that the regret depends on the variance quantity $\sigma_q^2$ (always at most 4) rather than the dimension $d$, yielding $\sqrt{dT}$ instead of $d\sqrt{T}$ for the $\ell_2$ unit ball.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's main comparison is against the additive noise model, where the minimax regret for the $\ell_2$ unit ball is $\Omega(d\sqrt{T})$ (Lattimore & Szepesvári, 2020; Rusmevichientong & Tsitsiklis, 2010). In the parameter noise model, VALEE achieves $\widetilde{O}(\sqrt{dT\,\sigma_q^2})$, which is strictly better whenever the reward variance $\sigma_q^2 < d$ — a significant improvement since $\sigma_q^2 \leq 4$ always. Against the adversarial model, where Bubeck et al. (2017) achieve $\widetilde{O}(\sqrt{dT})$ for $\ell_p$ balls with $p \leq 2$, the parameter noise model matches this bound when variance is constant but can do better when variance is small. The paper also contrasts with variance-dependent bounds in the additive noise model (Zhou et al., 2021; Zhao et al., 2023), which yield $\widetilde{O}(d\sqrt{T\,\sigma_{\max}^2})$ even on the $\ell_2$ ball — worse than the paper's $\widetilde{O}(\sqrt{dT\,\sigma_q^2})$ by a factor of $\sqrt{d}$. For $\ell_p$ balls with $p > 2$, VASE matches the adversarial lower bound of $\widetilde{\Omega}(d\sqrt{T\,\sigma_{\max}^2})$, confirming no improvement is possible in that regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the successive elimination framework of Lattimore & Szepesvári (2020, Algorithm 12) by incorporating variance-aware sampling and weighted least squares, yielding the VASE algorithm for general finite action sets. For ℓ_p unit balls, it adapts the simple explore-exploit paradigm from Slivkins (2024) into VALEE, which leverages the linear structure of parameter noise to achieve variance-dependent concentration bounds that are strictly tighter than additive-noise analysis. The lower bounds generalize the stochastic-adversary constructions of Bubeck et al. (2017) and Shamir (2014) to depend on the covariance matrix of the reward distribution rather than assuming constant variance, providing a unified variance-dependent minimax characterization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves the 'curious case of the unit ball' paradox for the parameter noise model: unlike the additive noise model where the ℓ₂ unit ball has minimax regret d√T (worse than the adversarial √(dT)), the parameter noise model achieves √(dTσ_q²), matching or beating the adversarial bound. This has practical implications for applications like online advertising where reward vectors are sampled from a population distribution. Open questions include closing the additive dT^{1/3} gap when the covariance is unknown, and finding an algorithm matching the d√(Tσ_q²) lower bound for ℓ_p balls with p>2.
