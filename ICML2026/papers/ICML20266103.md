## Problem

Existing mmWave-based human pose estimation methods either ignore Doppler cues entirely or fuse them symmetrically with spatial magnitude, causing non-human spectral responses (clutter, multipath, hardware artifacts) to be misinterpreted as limb motion and propagate into frame-to-frame jitter. The paper asks whether temporally stable HPE can be achieved under frame-wise inference by treating Doppler as a reliability-gated motion prompt rather than a symmetric feature channel.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266103/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each mmWave frame is a 3D tensor H_t ∈ R^(64×64×16) (range × angle × Doppler bins after resampling); the spatial magnitude is obtained by averaging |H_t| along the Doppler axis, while the full Doppler spectrum is retained per spatial cell. In multi-frame mode, a window of K=9 consecutive frames is consumed solely to aggregate Doppler prompts via confidence-weighted averaging, leaving the spatial backbone frame-local. The output is a set of 3D joint coordinates P_t ∈ R^(J×3) in a pelvis-centered coordinate system, with no post-hoc temporal smoothing applied.

## Method

PULSE decomposes each frame into a spatial magnitude stream (patch-encoded at 4×4 resolution into 256 tokens) and a Doppler signature stream (cell-level MLP embeddings at full 64×64 resolution, yielding 4096 tokens). A learned sigmoid gate g_{t,j} scores each Doppler token's motion relevance, and conditional cross-attention injects gated Doppler prompts into spatial tokens restricted to a 3×3 patch neighborhood to prevent cross-region spectral leakage. A per-token residual coefficient λ_i further modulates how much motion conditioning each spatial token receives, preserving spatial primacy for localization. Updated spatial tokens pass through 4-layer transformers before a lightweight MLP regression head predicts joint positions. The multi-frame extension aggregates Doppler tokens across K frames using confidence-weighted averaging before prompting, improving prompt reliability without altering the spatial pathway.

## Results

On HuPR, PULSE (1F) achieves the best MPJPE of 60.57 mm and MPJVE of 9.78 mm/frame, outperforming all single-frame baselines (HuPRModel: 65.37/14.70, MvDoppler: 69.71/13.11, RETR: 78.09/15.07) and even the multi-frame mmDiff (65.54/13.60). On XRF55, PULSE (1F) reduces MPJPE to 70.34 mm and MPJVE to 15.33 mm/frame, beating the strongest multi-frame baseline milliMamba (74.35/17.76) by 4.01 mm and 2.43 mm/frame respectively. On mmRadPose, PULSE (1F) reaches 68.83 mm MPJPE and 12.90 mm/frame MPJVE, with the multi-frame PULSE (KF) further improving to 67.56 mm and 11.7 mm/frame. Across all three datasets, PULSE consistently achieves the lowest AKV (5.1–7.1 mm/frame vs. 5.7–20.7 for baselines), indicating substantially smoother predicted trajectories without post-hoc smoothing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PULSE (1F) outperforms all five baselines on every dataset and every metric. Against the single-frame baselines, PULSE (1F) reduces MPJPE by 4.8–17.8 mm and MPJVE by 2.3–9.8 mm/frame compared to HuPRModel, MvDoppler, and RETR, with the largest gaps on XRF55 (e.g., 9.9 mm MPJPE improvement over MvDoppler). Against the multi-frame baselines, PULSE (1F) is competitive or superior despite using only one frame: on HuPR it beats mmDiff by 4.97 mm MPJPE and 3.82 mm/frame MPJVE, and beats milliMamba by 3.51 mm and 1.91 mm/frame. On XRF55, PULSE (1F) surpasses mmDiff by 8.72 mm MPJPE and 6.11 mm/frame MPJVE, and milliMamba by 4.01 mm and 2.43 mm/frame. The multi-frame PULSE (KF) further narrows the gap, achieving the best overall numbers on mmRadPose (67.56 mm MPJPE, 11.7 mm/frame MPJVE). Notably, PULSE also serves as a plug-in: when inserted into mmDiff and milliMamba backbones, it reduces MPJPE by 4.65–6.10 mm and MPJVE by 3.42–4.24 mm/frame across all three datasets, confirming that the controlled Doppler prompting mechanism is transferable beyond the authors' own architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

PULSE departs from the dominant paradigm in mmWave-based HPE where Doppler is either ignored (spatial-magnitude-only methods like HuPRModel), fused symmetrically with range-angle features (MvDoppler), or bypassed in favor of multi-frame temporal aggregation (mmDiff, milliMamba). Its conceptual lineage draws on FMCW radar signal processing (Richards et al., 2005) for the physical decomposition of a frame into spatial magnitude and Doppler signature, but repurposes the 'prompting' metaphor from conditional-attention literature to mean a one-way, confidence-gated modulation of spatial tokens by screened Doppler tokens. The locality-constrained cross-attention with per-token gating is architecturally novel within this domain, though each component (patch tokenization, cross-attention, sigmoid gating) is standard; the contribution lies in their asymmetric, role-differentiated composition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Temporal stability under frame-wise inference is a practical bottleneck for safety-critical mmWave monitoring (fall detection, rehabilitation tracking) where post-hoc smoothing is undesirable and privacy constraints preclude video. PULSE's plug-in demonstration (Table 3) suggests the screened-Doppler-prompting interface could be adopted by existing multi-frame backbones without architectural redesign, lowering the barrier to improved temporal consistency. Open questions include whether the implicit gate generalizes to 4D radar tensors with richer angular resolution, whether explicit clutter-rejection supervision becomes necessary in more adversarial environments, and whether the approach extends to egocentric or multi-radar configurations where Doppler semantics shift.
