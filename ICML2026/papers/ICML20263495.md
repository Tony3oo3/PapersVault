## Problem

Coreset selection has been extensively studied for classification but remains unexplored for object Re-identification, despite ReID datasets exhibiting severe temporal redundancy (e.g., MARS contains over 1M near-duplicate frames). Conventional CS methods assume shared train/test label spaces and select samples based on dataset-level difficulty criteria, both of which are ill-suited for ReID where identity spaces are disjoint and intra-class diversity—not sample difficulty—is the key factor for learning discriminative features.

## Input / Output

The method takes a ReID training dataset with identity labels, a fixed storage budget B (number of images), and a ViT-B/16 feature extractor trained for only a single epoch (not fully converged). It produces a pruned class set (retaining the top 1−α fraction of classes by diversity) and a coreset of at most B images. Unlike standard CS which preserves all classes, the output explicitly discards low-diversity classes, and the coreset is structured with more samples allocated to easier (lower-diversity) classes than harder ones.

## Method

The proposed CSOR framework is a two-stage procedure. Stage 1 (Diversity-driven Class Pruning) computes each class's intra-class diversity as the trace of its feature covariance matrix and removes the bottom α=20% of classes, reallocating the storage budget to remaining informative classes. Stage 2 (Coverage-Prioritized Sampling) processes the surviving classes in ascending diversity order (easy-to-hard, inspired by curriculum learning) and greedily selects samples that maximize the marginal gain of a submodular facility location function, which measures how well the selected subset covers the full class feature distribution. Sampling for each class terminates when the maximum marginal gain drops below a threshold τ=0.01, preventing redundant selections. This joint class-pruning-plus-coverage-sampling formulation is the first to treat coreset selection for ReID as a problem over both the sample set and the class set simultaneously.

## Results

On Market1501 with a 1,000-image budget (7.7% of data), the proposed CSOR method achieves 67.1% mAP and 83.8% Rank-1, reaching 82% of the full-dataset mAP (87.2%) while using only ~12% of the training data. On MSMT17-V2 (1,000 images), it attains 26.2% mAP / 50.2% R-1, and on CUHK03-NP (1,000 images), 43.1% mAP / 44.6% R-1. On the vehicle ReID dataset VeRi-776 (1,000 images), the method scores 41.9% mAP / 68.0% R-1, outperforming all baselines by a clear margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed CSOR method consistently outperforms all five baselines across every dataset and budget setting. AUM (Pleiss et al., 2020) is the strongest competitor at low budgets, achieving 57.4% mAP on Market1501 (1,000 images) versus CSOR's 67.1%, a gap of ~10 points; however, AUM's advantage shrinks at higher budgets where its aggressive class concentration (only 144 of 751 classes) limits inter-class diversity. EL2N (Paul et al., 2021) performs well on Market1501 (59.4% mAP) but degrades severely on MSMT17-V2 (3.9% mAP), revealing poor cross-dataset robustness. CCS (Zheng et al., 2023) is the closest baseline on CUHK03-NP (35.6% vs. 43.1% mAP) and VeRi-776 (35.2% vs. 41.9% mAP), but still trails CSOR by 7–8 mAP points. Forgetting (Toneva et al., 2019) and GraNd (Paul et al., 2021) are consistently outperformed by 10+ mAP points on Market1501 and by even larger margins on MSMT17-V2, where they struggle to capture intra-class diversity. Moderate (Xia et al., 2023) performs comparably to Random in most settings, confirming that classification-oriented difficulty-based criteria are poorly suited for ReID.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the established coreset selection literature (Forgetting, AUM, EL2N, GraNd, Moderate, CCS), which was developed for classification tasks where train and test label spaces overlap. The key conceptual departure is formulating CSOR as a joint optimization over both class selection and sample selection, motivated by the disjoint identity spaces in ReID. The sampling stage builds directly on the facility location function (Owen & Daskin, 1998) and its submodularity properties for greedy coverage maximization, while the class pruning stage introduces a diversity-score-based criterion (trace of within-class covariance) that has no direct analogue in prior CS work. The easy-to-hard budget allocation strategy draws on curriculum learning principles applied at the class level rather than the sample level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ReID datasets suffer from severe temporal redundancy (e.g., MARS contains over 1M images from 6 cameras with near-duplicate consecutive frames), making efficient data selection practically critical for storage and training costs. The finding that pruning ~20% of low-diversity classes improves performance—counterintuitively, removing data helps—provides a principled justification for class-level data curation in ReID. A key limitation is the reliance on a pre-trained feature extractor and annotated labels; extending CSOR to unsupervised or label-free settings remains open, as standard clustering produces noisy pseudo-labels for fine-grained ReID identities. The demonstrated applicability to continual learning and neural architecture search suggests broader utility beyond standalone ReID training.
