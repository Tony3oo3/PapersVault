## Problem

Generating valid link topologies for mobile ad hoc networks with directional antennas requires jointly satisfying sector occupancy, interference, connectivity, and half-duplex parity constraints, a combinatorial problem that is too slow for real-time reconfiguration under node mobility. Existing graph generative models lack mechanisms to enforce global structural coherence (e.g., matching edge counts and sector usage) while conditioning on node positions, and prior diffusion-based graph generators do not jointly predict topology and the bipartite transmit/receive parity needed for two-slot scheduling.

## Input / Output

The model takes 2D node coordinates (normalized by the fleet's global heading) and per-node yaw orientations as conditioning, plus optionally a previous edge set and parity for reconfiguration. It outputs a set of directional edges forming a connected bipartite graph and a binary parity vector assigning each node to a transmit or receive slot, with the hard constraint that edges only connect opposite-parity nodes and each node activates at most one link per antenna sector per slot. For partial-diffusion reconfiguration, the number of denoising steps scales with normalized node displacement, and the previous topology is injected as a guided-sampling prior.

## Method

NetDiff is a node-conditioned discrete denoising diffusion model (following the DiGress state-transition framework) that jointly denoises edges and parity over 50 steps using a 5-block graph transformer with 32-dimensional features. Its key architectural contribution is Absolute Cross-Attentive Modulation (ACAM) tokens: a small set of virtual tokens that read from node embeddings via unsoftmaxed cosine-similarity cross-attention (enabling graph-level 'counting' of edges, sectors, and clusters) and write back through FiLM multiplicative modulation, providing permutation-invariant global signals without interfering with node-node attention. Training uses a composite loss combining binary cross-entropy on edges and parity with domain-specific penalties for sector over-occupancy, acute-angle links, and same-parity edges. For network evolution under mobility, a partial-diffusion scheme starts from the previous topology at a diffusion time proportional to node displacement, requiring only 7–15 steps (3.3–5× faster) while preserving structural continuity with the prior topology.

## Results

NetDiff (ACAM) generates topologies with 22.42 links, 0.94 average link length, 3.52 link throughput, and 12.1% antenna saturation, closely matching the target (21.95 links, 0.85 length, 3.62 throughput, 9.7% saturation). In terms of instantaneous throughput upper bound, NetDiff achieves 78.92 Mbps on 16-node networks (vs. 79.33 target) and 322.40 Mbps on 32-node networks (vs. 340.52 target), reaching over 95% of target performance. A full 50-step diffusion pass takes ~450 ms on GPU, and partial diffusion for minor reconfigurations reduces this to 7–10 steps (3.3–5× faster).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the discrete diffusion graph transformer baseline (DDPM-GT, following DiGress), NetDiff (ACAM) improves link throughput from 3.18 to 3.52 and reduces antenna saturation from 17.6% to 12.1%, while also matching the target more closely in link count (22.42 vs. 23.72) and average link length (0.94 vs. 1.05). On the 32-node throughput benchmark, NetDiff achieves 322.40 Mbps versus 258.90 Mbps for DDPM-GT, a 24.5% improvement, and 78.92 vs. 75.43 Mbps on 16-node networks. The VAE-based baselines (GraphVAE, GT-VAE) are substantially outperformed: GraphVAE produces 75.90 links with 90% saturation and only 0.48 throughput, while GT-VAE reaches 2.06 throughput but still trails NetDiff by 41%. The classical heuristic (Huang et al., 2002) achieves only 52.89 Mbps (16-node) and 153.54 Mbps (32-node), roughly 33% and 52% below NetDiff respectively, and the omnidirectional baseline lags further at 47.24 and 71.63 Mbps. NetDiff's advantage over DDPM-GT is attributed to the ACAM tokens providing absolute, permutation-invariant global signals that help the model match graph-level counts (density, sector usage) that standard attention-based message passing struggles to capture.

## Relation to Prior Work

NetDiff extends the discrete denoising diffusion framework for graphs established by DiGress (Vignac et al., 2023a), adopting its state-transition-matrix noise schedule and stepwise 0-1 classification objective, while departing from it through the introduction of Absolute Cross-Attentive Modulation (ACAM) tokens that replace softmax-normalized cross-attention with unsoftmaxed dot-product aggregation to capture global graph cardinalities. The partial-diffusion reconfiguration mechanism builds on the classifier-guided sampling of Dhariwal & Nichol (2021), repurposing the previous topology as a soft guide rather than a learned classifier. The paper also positions itself against autoregressive, GAN, VAE, and RL-based graph generators, arguing that diffusion's parallel, permutation-invariant edge sampling is uniquely suited to node-conditioned topology generation with hard structural constraints, and extends the closely related TopoFormer (Marcoccia et al., 2025) by jointly predicting topology and transmit/receive parity rather than assigning parity a posteriori.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ACAM token mechanism—detaching a global aggregator from the main message-passing stream and using unsoftmaxed cross-attention for counting-like global descriptors—is a clean, generalizable architectural idea that could transfer to other graph-generation or graph-classification settings where global statistics (density, cluster counts, degree distributions) matter. Partial diffusion for temporal graph updates under bounded perturbation is broadly applicable to any setting where a graph evolves smoothly over time, and the 3–5× speedup with maintained constraint satisfaction is practically significant for real-time network reconfiguration. Open questions include whether ACAM tokens scale to much larger graphs (the paper caps at 32 nodes before requiring partitioning), whether the approach generalizes beyond the single wireless-network domain tested, and whether the expensive simulation-guided training data generation can be replaced by cheaper supervision. The reliance on proprietary antenna radiation patterns and the single-seed, single-domain evaluation limit the breadth of the empirical claims.
