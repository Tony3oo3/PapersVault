## Problem

Clinical LLM deployment has followed a 'scaling-first' paradigm, assuming that larger single models yield better medical reasoning. However, monolithic models face a three-way constraint: bounded knowledge limits effectiveness, absence of self-critique undermines reliability (hallucinations, adversarial fragility), and large memory/compute footprints hinder localized deployment in privacy-sensitive healthcare settings. This paper asks whether a group of small, specialized agents can collectively outperform a single giant model across all three dimensions simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The system consumes a clinical question or patient case vignette as text, which is then augmented by the knowledge agents querying authoritative sources (PubMed/Medline, CDC, FDA drug labels, clinical guidelines) to retrieve supporting evidence. The output is a structured consensus recommendation produced through multi-round deliberation: a final diagnosis with evidence-grounded justification, a safety/validity audit, and an adjudicated consensus, with early termination when no further substantive arguments are raised. The total parameter count of the 10-agent group (3B or 4B each) is comparable to or smaller than the 70B single-model baselines it outperforms.

## Method

SAG organizes ten small LLM agents (Llama-3.2-3B or Qwen3-4B) into four functional roles—Reasoning (A_R), Knowledge (A_K), Safety-check (A_S), and Synthesis & Judge (A_J)—that interact via a hybrid multi-agent debate: A_R and A_K engage in symmetric flat debate, while A_S and A_J perform hierarchical sequential review and final adjudication. The knowledge agents implement RAG over curated medical corpora to ground claims in external evidence rather than latent parameters. For domain adaptation, the paper applies two multi-agent RL strategies: GRPO, which samples multiple full SAG rollouts per case and updates agents using group-relative advantage with role-aligned reward terms (deduction for A_R, grounding for A_K, safety for A_S, coherent adjudication for A_J), and CTDE, which trains a centralized critic over the full debate state for credit assignment while each agent executes using only local inputs at deployment. The key architectural insight is that role specialization plus iterative critique-and-reconciliation substitutes for parameter growth, yielding higher correctness, lower hallucination, better safety calibration, and reduced memory footprint relative to a single 70B model.

## Results

On clinical correctness (top-1 accuracy), SAG with GRPO optimization achieves 90.3% on MedQA, 85.2% on MedMCQA, 84.0% on NEJM-MedQA, and 88.6% on GPQA-Bio (Llama backbone), while SAG with CTDE reaches 91.0%, 85.8%, 85.6%, and 87.3% (Qwen backbone). Even without any domain adaptation (pre-trained SAG), the system already surpasses the best single-model baselines (e.g., 84.6% vs. 81.5% on MedQA for Llama; 86.0% vs. 82.4% for Qwen). The cross-benchmark stability gap drops from 15.1–30.7 for single 70B/72B models to 5.4–7.0 for optimized SAG, indicating substantially more uniform performance across clinical task types.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAG decisively outperforms both clinical specialist baselines across all four correctness benchmarks. Against Meditron-70B (Chen et al., 2023), SAG (Pre-trained, Llama) leads by +49.7 pp on MedQA (84.6 vs. 34.9), +29.1 pp on MedMCQA (77.8 vs. 48.7), +36.4 pp on NEJM-MedQA (64.9 vs. 28.5), and +49.7 pp on GPQA-Bio (70.1 vs. 20.4). Against Me-LLaMA-70B (Xie et al., 2024), the margins are +26.6 pp on MedQA (84.6 vs. 58.0), +6.7 pp on MedMCQA (77.8 vs. 71.1), +20.7 pp on NEJM-MedQA (64.9 vs. 44.2), and +18.9 pp on GPQA-Bio (70.1 vs. 51.2). SAG also surpasses the strongest single-model baseline (Qwen-2.5-72B with DPO: 82.4/75.1/72.5/67.3) by +8.6 to +25.3 pp per benchmark, and its cross-benchmark gap (5.4–7.0) is roughly 2–4× tighter than the single-model gap (15.1–30.7), indicating that the multi-agent deliberation mechanism—not raw parameter scale—drives both accuracy and stability gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends multi-agent debate (MAD) frameworks (Liang et al., 2024; Smit et al., 2024) from general-purpose reasoning to a clinically structured architecture with specialized roles (reasoning, knowledge retrieval, safety auditing, adjudication), and integrates RAG for evidence grounding. It departs from the dominant 'scaling-first' paradigm in medical LLMs by showing that a group of small agents (3B/4B each, ~30–40B cumulative) can outperform single 70B+ models, drawing on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) and CTDE (Wen et al., 2021) for multi-agent optimization. The closest prior work, MedAgents (Tang et al., 2024), explored multi-agent collaboration for medical tasks but without the explicit safety/fairness/deployment-cost evaluation framework or the role-specific optimization objectives introduced here.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a concrete, empirically validated alternative to monolithic scaling for clinical AI, which is practically important for resource-constrained healthcare institutions that cannot deploy 70B+ models locally. The 2× latency penalty (58–64 s vs. 27–28 s) is a significant limitation that restricts SAG to offline or semi-urgent workflows (MDT preparation, second-opinion analysis) rather than real-time triage, and the paper acknowledges this trade-off. Open questions include whether the gains persist in production clinical environments with noisy EHR inputs, whether the specific 10-agent role allocation is optimal, and whether the approach extends to multimodal clinical data (imaging, lab panels) where single-model scaling may still hold advantages.
