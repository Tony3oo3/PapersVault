## Problem

Existing spatio-temporal forecasting methods perform fine-grained full-graph modeling that is highly susceptible to system noise and spurious correlations, particularly as spatial scale grows. While hierarchical approaches exist, they treat coarse-grained regional signals merely as auxiliary historical statistics rather than leveraging them as future-oriented structural guidance, leaving models unable to anticipate dynamic anomalies or stabilize long-horizon autoregressive rollouts.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a historical node-level tensor X_{t-L+1:t} ∈ R^{N×L×C} (L=12 lookback steps) alongside a pre-computed spectral clustering assignment matrix S ∈ {0,1}^{N×M} (M=0.2N regions) derived offline from a feature-driven affinity matrix. During training, it also receives future regional guidance Z_{t+1:t+P} ∈ R^{M×P×C} via teacher forcing; at inference this is replaced by a zero-mask reconstruction. Outputs are generated autoregressively in patches of length P: node-level predictions X̂_{t+1:t+P} ∈ R^{N×P×C} and regional predictions Ẑ_{t+P+1:t+2P} ∈ R^{M×P×C} with multi-quantile uncertainty estimates (τ ∈ {0.1, 0.5, 0.9}).

## Method

NEST constructs M latent regions via spectral clustering on a temporal affinity matrix built from raw feature sequences (not physical topology), yielding a low-pass-filtered macro representation whose SNR gain is theoretically bounded by cluster size and intra-cluster correlation (Theorem 1). A bidirectional cross-attention mechanism then couples N node-level historical tokens with M region-level future tokens: a top-down step lets nodes query regional futures, and a bottom-up step lets regions query enriched node features, reducing attention cost from O(N²d) to O(NMd). Training uses a multi-task objective combining Huber loss for node prediction, pinball loss for multi-quantile regional forecasting, and a masked guidance reconstruction loss, with scheduled sampling (decaying teacher-forcing probability) to bridge the training-inference gap. At inference, regional guidance is initialized from a zero-mask boundary decoder and recursively fed back, creating a nested autoregressive loop where evolving macro-trends continuously anchor micro-level predictions over extended horizons.

## Results

NEST achieves the best performance across all three LargeST traffic datasets (GBA, GLA, CA) and all prediction horizons (3, 6, 12 steps). On average, NEST improves MAE by 4.71%, RMSE by 4.41%, and MAPE by 9.34% over the strongest baseline (PatchSTG). The MAPE gains exceed 10% on GBA and CA, indicating superior handling of high-dimensional volatility. In long-horizon autoregressive rollout (up to 48 steps/12 hours), NEST maintains a growing advantage over PatchSTG, with the MAE gap expanding from 2.0 to 2.4 on GLA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NEST outperforms all 11 traffic baselines on every dataset and metric. Against the strongest baseline PatchSTG (KDD 2025), NEST reduces average MAE by 3.95–4.69%, RMSE by 3.95–4.17%, and MAPE by 11.78–11.88% on GBA and CA, and by 5.65%, 5.60%, and 6.14% respectively on GLA. Among earlier methods, NEST beats STID (CIKM 2022) by ~4.5% MAE, STWAVE (ICDE 2023) by ~4.5% MAE, and D2STGNN (VLDB 2022) by ~4.5% MAE on GBA; the gap widens against weaker baselines like RPMIXER (KDD 2024, ~32% MAE worse) and DSTAGNN (ICML 2022, ~27% MAE worse on GBA). On non-traffic domains, NEST surpasses Air-DualODE (ICLR 2025) on KnowAir, MAGE (NeurIPS 2025) on both KnowAir and UrbanEV, and iTransformer (ICLR 2024) on Electricity and Solar-Energy, confirming generality beyond traffic. In long-horizon autoregressive evaluation (48 steps), NEST maintains a consistent 2.0–2.4 MAE advantage over PatchSTG on GLA and CA, demonstrating superior error accumulation control.

## Relation to Prior Work

NEST builds on the hierarchical spatio-temporal modeling lineage (HGCN, HISTGNN, HIEST, HSTAN) and the spectral clustering framework of Ng et al. (2001), but departs from prior work in a key conceptual way: existing hierarchical methods treat coarse-grained regional signals as auxiliary *historical* statistics, whereas NEST explicitly predicts *future* macro-level states and feeds them as top-down guidance into fine-grained node forecasting. It also moves beyond full-graph dynamic modeling (DSTAGNN, PatchSTG) by replacing the N×N interaction space with a compact M-region representation (M≈0.2N), reducing cross-attention complexity from O(N²) to O(NM). The patch-based autoregressive formulation follows PatchSTG, but the nested dual-horizon encoding (past node context + future region guidance) and bidirectional cross-scale attention are new architectural elements.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical pain point in large-scale STF: full-graph models become noise-sensitive and computationally expensive as node counts grow into the thousands. The SNR theorem provides a clean theoretical justification for why semantic aggregation helps, and the 59.5% training speedup makes the approach attractive for deployment. However, several limitations remain open: the O(N²) affinity matrix construction is a one-time but non-trivial preprocessing cost; the global clustering assumes time-invariant spatial correlations, limiting adaptability to sudden topological shifts; and the autoregressive rollout, while stabilized by macro guidance, still introduces sequential latency that may be prohibitive for strict real-time applications at massive scale. Dynamic regionalization and more efficient boundary modeling are identified as promising future directions.
