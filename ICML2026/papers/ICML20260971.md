## Problem

State-of-the-art discrete neural samplers such as MDNS suffer from mode collapse at low temperatures, concentrating probability mass on a single discovered mode and failing to traverse high-energy barriers between modes. This prevents estimation of free energy landscapes and understanding of phase transitions in multimodal Boltzmann distributions, a problem that is especially acute in materials systems where each energy evaluation is computationally expensive and wasted samples on known minima are costly.

## Input / Output

The method takes an energy function E(x) (from cluster expansion or spin Hamiltonians), an inverse temperature β, and a problem-specific collective variable ξ(x) that projects discrete lattice configurations onto low-dimensional bins (magnetization for Ising, per-state occupation fractions for Potts, gold fraction for Cu-Au). It produces weighted discrete configurations requiring importance weights w_i = exp(V(ξ(x_i))) for unbiased observable estimation, plus a learned bias potential from which free energy profiles (potential of mean force) along the CVs are directly reconstructed. For exact-likelihood backbones (autoregressive, MDNS), likelihood-based reweighting is also available for two-point correlation functions.

## Method

MetaDNS alternates between an inner loop that trains a discrete neural sampler on a time-varying biased distribution π_V(x) ∝ exp(−β[E(x) + V_t(ξ(x))]) and an outer loop that deposits Gaussian-like hills in CV space at visited regions, scaled by a well-tempered factor exp(−V_{t−1}(s)/(γk_BT)) to prevent unbounded bias growth. At convergence the bias satisfies V*(s) ≈ −(1−1/γ)F(s) + c, enabling direct free energy reconstruction from the learned potential. The framework is backbone-agnostic, compatible with both CTMC-based masked diffusion (MDNS) and order-agnostic autoregressive models, and preserves asymptotically exact sampling through self-normalized importance reweighting. By generating independent configurations via a single forward pass per bias deposition step rather than correlated MCMC chains, MetaDNS achieves comparable or superior exploration with up to 2× fewer bias deposition steps than MCMC-based WT-MetaD on Potts and Cu-Au benchmarks.

## Results

On the L=16 Ising model at low temperature (β=0.6), MetaDNS achieves a ~5× lower up-spin concentration JS divergence (4.6×10⁻²) compared to MDNS (2.2×10⁻¹), while matching Swendsen-Wang ground truth in magnetization (0.974 vs 0.973) and two-point correlation (0.955 vs 0.954). On the 4×4×4 Cu-Au alloy at 500K, MetaDNS reduces both energy and composition JS divergences to 7.9×10⁻² and 8.5×10⁻² versus MDNS's 1.3×10⁻¹ for both, successfully capturing both Cu₃Au and CuAu ordered phases. For free energy convergence, MetaDNS reaches RMSE < 1 k_BT in 50k bias deposition steps on Potts (vs 94.5k for MCMC-based WT-MetaD) and 16k steps on Cu-Au (vs 33.8k), a ~2× reduction in required deposition steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MDNS (the primary neural sampler baseline), MetaDNS wins decisively on mode coverage at low temperatures: on Ising L=16 at β=0.6, MetaDNS achieves 4.6×10⁻² x↑ JS divergence versus MDNS's 2.2×10⁻¹ (~5× better), and on Potts L=16 at β=1.2, MetaDNS achieves 4.0×10⁻³ CV JS divergence versus MDNS's 3.2×10⁻¹ (~80× better). At high temperatures where mode collapse is absent, both methods perform comparably. Against MCMC-based WT-MetaD, MetaDNS achieves comparable free energy profiles with ~2× fewer bias deposition steps (50k vs 94.5k on Potts at low T; 16k vs 33.8k on Cu-Au at 500K), but wall-clock training time is longer for Potts (20h vs 1h on A100) due to neural network optimization overhead, while it is slightly shorter for Cu-Au (1.5h vs 1.75h) where sequential cluster expansion evaluations dominate. Inference is dramatically faster for MetaDNS: generating 10k samples takes <1 min via autoregressive unmasking versus ~30 min for Potts and ~40 min for Cu-Au with MCMC under the converged bias, yielding >30× and >40× inference speedups respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

MetaDNS integrates well-tempered metadynamics (WT-MetaD), a classical enhanced-sampling technique from molecular dynamics, into the training loop of discrete neural samplers such as MDNS (masked diffusion via stochastic optimal control) and autoregressive models. The closest prior work is Nam et al. (2026), which augments a continuous diffusion-based Schrödinger bridge sampler with an online WT-MetaD bias; MetaDNS transfers this idea to discrete configuration spaces where the sampler dynamics, path-measure structure, and reweighting require different technical treatment. Unlike prior discrete samplers whose exploration is governed by convergence to a fixed target or by annealing paths, MetaDNS introduces an explicit history-dependent bias in a low-dimensional collective-variable space that actively fills visited regions and forces barrier crossing, while preserving asymptotically exact sampling through importance reweighting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MetaDNS addresses a concrete and well-documented failure mode of state-of-the-art discrete neural samplers (mode collapse at low temperature) and enables free-energy landscape reconstruction in regions where standard samplers produce no samples, which is critical for understanding phase transitions in materials. The introduction of the Cu-Au binary alloy as a rigorous benchmark with cluster-expansion energies provides the ML community with a realistic testbed beyond toy spin models. Key open questions include automatic collective-variable discovery (current CVs are hand-crafted), scaling to larger lattices and higher-dimensional CVs, and formal convergence guarantees when the neural sampler is only an approximation to the biased target at each outer step.
