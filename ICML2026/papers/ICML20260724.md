## Problem

Existing test-time scaling methods for VLA models require additional training, external verifiers, and multiple forward passes, making them impractical for real-time robotic deployment. More fundamentally, they intervene only at action decoding while keeping visual representations fixed, which is insufficient under perceptual ambiguity where reconsidering how to perceive the scene is as important as deciding what action to take.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260724/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on autoregressive VLA backbones (OpenVLA, π0-FAST, SpatialVLA) that consume RGB visual observations and language instructions at each control step. It produces a sequence of K discretized action tokens (e.g., 7 tokens for OpenVLA encoding x, y, z, roll, pitch, yaw, gripper) and a modulated attention temperature γ_t applied to the vision encoder. A key design constraint is that visual attention at timestep t is modulated using the previous step's uncertainty deviation Δu_{t-1}, since current-step uncertainty is only available after action decoding completes, enabling single-pass inference without an extra forward pass.

## Method

SCALE defines a self-uncertainty score u_t^k at each token position as the difference between KL divergences from the predicted distribution to a one-hot reference on the top-1 token (full certainty) and a uniform reference (full ambiguity), yielding a bounded log-likelihood ratio that simultaneously captures distributional spread and top-1 decisiveness. This token-level uncertainty gates the action sampling temperature via a sigmoid (τ = T_0 · σ(u)), producing near-greedy decoding under confidence and diverse sampling under ambiguity. For visual perception, token-level uncertainties are averaged to a step-level value, compared against an exponential moving average to detect transitions in scene complexity, and converted into an attention temperature γ_t = κ^{tanh(Δu_{t-1})} that scales the vision encoder's self-attention across all layers. The two mechanisms form a closed-loop feedback where uncertainty at one timestep modulates action sampling while simultaneously adjusting visual attention for the next step, requiring no additional training, no external verifier, and only a single forward pass per control step.

## Results

On LIBERO with OpenVLA, SCALE achieves 81.5% average success rate, outperforming the strongest fixed decoding baseline (Top-p at 77.2%) by 4.3 points and the training-required TTS method MG-Select (70.8%) by 10.7 points. On LIBERO with π₀-FAST, SCALE reaches 93.0% vs. 91.2% for greedy decoding. On SIMPLER-WidowX, SCALE yields the largest gains: +14.6 points with π₀-FAST (34.4→49.0%) and +10.4 points with zero-shot SpatialVLA (31.3→41.7%). In real-world experiments, SCALE improves in-distribution success by +19.5 (OpenVLA) and +13.9 (π₀-FAST), and out-of-distribution success by +16.7 and +12.5 respectively, all while requiring only a single forward pass and no additional training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCALE outperforms all three training-required TTS methods: MG-Select (70.8% avg on LIBERO) by +10.7 points, TACO (60.0% on LIBERO-Long) by +3.3 points, and RoboMonkey (56.5% on LIBERO-Long) by +6.8 points, while requiring no additional training or multiple forward passes. Against fixed decoding strategies, SCALE consistently wins: temperature sampling (76.7%), top-k (76.7%), and top-p (77.2%) on LIBERO-OpenVLA all fall 4.3–4.8 points below SCALE's 81.5%, and on π₀-FAST the gap widens to 8.7 points (93.0 vs. 84.3 for temperature sampling). Among uncertainty metrics tested in the ablation (Table 7), SCALE's dual-reference self-uncertainty (63.3% on LIBERO-Long) surpasses Gini impurity (57.8%), p_max (56.0%), Entropy (55.4%), and Self-Certainty (53.8%), confirming that capturing both distributional spread and top-1 decisiveness is critical for VLA adaptive inference. Notably, naive stochastic decoding (temperature, top-k, top-p) actually degrades π₀-FAST performance below greedy decoding (91.2→84.3/88.1/86.2), whereas SCALE improves it to 93.0%, demonstrating that adaptive, uncertainty-conditioned sampling is essential rather than uniformly increasing stochasticity.

## Relation to Prior Work

SCALE extends the test-time scaling (TTS) line for VLA models (RoboMonkey, TACO, MG-Select) by replacing trained verifiers and multi-sample Best-of-N selection with a training-free, single-pass strategy that modulates both action decoding and visual attention. Conceptually, it builds on Self-Certainty (Kang et al., 2025) for LLMs but departs from it by introducing a dual-reference KL formulation (one-hot vs. uniform) that captures top-1 decisiveness in addition to distributional spread, which is critical for greedy-decoding VLAs. The joint perception-action modulation is motivated by Active Inference theory (Friston et al., 2016) and operationalizes the attention-temperature mechanism from Zou et al. (2024) in a closed-loop robotic setting, where prior TTS methods intervene only at action decoding while keeping visual representations fixed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SCALE offers a practically deployable inference strategy for VLA models: no additional training, no external verifier, single forward pass, and consistent gains across three architectures (OpenVLA, π0-FAST, SpatialVLA) in both simulation and real-world settings. The finding that naive stochastic decoding (temperature, top-k, top-p) often degrades performance—especially for π0-FAST—while adaptive, uncertainty-conditioned modulation helps, provides useful guidance for practitioners who might otherwise reach for fixed-temperature sampling. Open questions include whether the dual-reference uncertainty measure generalizes to non-autoregressive or continuous-action VLAs, whether the previous-step uncertainty proxy remains valid at very low control frequencies, and whether the approach extends to multi-robot or hierarchical planning settings where uncertainty dynamics are more complex.
