## Problem

Existing instruction data curation methods treat semantic redundancy and quality defects as a single coupled problem, applying coarse-grained global strategies (uniform selection or indiscriminate rewriting) that either discard semantically valuable samples with localized defects or introduce distributional shifts by modifying already high-quality data. The paper identifies that data quality is not binary but has a three-state structure—high-quality, fixable, and useless—that prior work fails to explicitly model.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an instruction-response dataset of (instruction, optional input context, response) triples, a pretrained sentence encoder (BGE-M3) for embedding computation, and a teacher LLM (Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct-AWQ) for diagnosis and remediation. It outputs a curated training set formed by union of untouched high-quality samples and diagnostically-guided remediated samples; because useless samples are discarded without replacement, the final set can be slightly smaller than the nominal data budget. Diagnostic scores are discrete integers (1–5) across four dimensions, and the triage uses fixed deterministic thresholds rather than learned classifiers.

## Method

Cure-SFT is a three-stage coarse-to-fine pipeline that explicitly decouples semantic redundancy from quality defects. Stage I performs stratified semantic-geometric sampling: K-means partitions embeddings into K=⌈√N⌉ clusters, then a k-center greedy algorithm selects M=⌊B/K⌉ samples per cluster to maximize local geometric diversity while preserving broad semantic coverage. Stage II applies a teacher model to produce a four-dimensional diagnostic score (instruction adherence, content richness, logical soundness, presentation quality) plus a natural-language defect description, then a deterministic triage rule assigns each sample to High Quality, Fixable, or Useless based on component-wise thresholds. Stage III performs two-phase targeted remediation on Fixable samples only: Phase 1 restores correctness (fixing factual errors, reasoning flaws, constraint violations) and Phase 2 enriches content and refines expression, with Phase 2 constrained to preserve Phase 1 corrections; only the response is modified, never the instruction. The diagnostic description and dimension-specific scores are encoded into a structured remediation guidance prompt that conditions the teacher model's generation, ensuring minimal intervention and controlled semantic drift.

## Results

Under a 10% data budget on Alpaca, Cure-SFT achieves an average Winning Score of 1.78 (vs. FULL), surpassing the strongest baselines NILE (1.69) and Selective-RT (1.63) by 0.09 and 0.15 respectively. On the higher-quality WizardLM dataset, Cure-SFT reaches 1.27 average Winning Score, outperforming NILE (1.23) and Selective-RT (1.22), while selection-based methods like IFD (0.67) and Deita (0.62) fall below Random (0.86). On AlpacaEval 2.0, Cure-SFT attains 7.66% Win rate on Alpaca and 7.24% on WizardLM, the highest among all methods on the Win metric for both datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Cure-SFT consistently outperforms all 10 baselines on average Winning Score across both Alpaca and WizardLM under a 10% budget. Against the strongest rewriting-based competitor NILE, Cure-SFT leads by 0.09 on Alpaca (1.78 vs. 1.69) and 0.04 on WizardLM (1.27 vs. 1.23), while also achieving the highest AlpacaEval Win rate on both datasets (7.66% vs. 7.31% and 7.24% vs. 7.14%). The unified Rewrite baseline achieves a higher LC Win on AlpacaEval (12.98 vs. 11.45), suggesting length inflation, but trails Cure-SFT by 0.24 in average Winning Score on Alpaca and 0.15 on WizardLM, indicating less stable fine-grained quality. Selection-based methods (IFD, Deita, AlpaGasus, T-SHIRT) show notable instability: on the higher-quality WizardLM dataset, IFD (0.67) and Deita (0.62) actually underperform Random sampling (0.86), whereas Cure-SFT maintains a clear margin. CoachLM, which trains a dedicated revision model, lags Cure-SFT by 0.67 on Alpaca and 0.34 on WizardLM in average Winning Score, highlighting the advantage of diagnostic-guided targeted remediation over learned holistic rewriting.

## Relation to Prior Work

Cure-SFT sits at the intersection of two established paradigms in instruction data curation: selection-based methods (AlpaGasus, IFD, Deita) that score and discard samples, and rewriting-based methods (Alpaca-GPT4, CoachLM) that uniformly regenerate responses. It builds on the k-center greedy algorithm (Sener & Savarese, 2018) for geometric diversity in embedding space and adopts the four-dimensional quality scoring framework from FLASK (Ye et al., 2024) for diagnostic triage. The key conceptual departure is the explicit disentangling of semantic redundancy from quality defects and the introduction of a three-state quality model (High Quality / Fixable / Useless) that replaces the binary usable/unusable assumption prevalent in prior work, enabling targeted remediation rather than wholesale discarding or rewriting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that a 10% data budget with diagnostic-guided curation can match or exceed full-data instruction tuning, which has direct cost implications for practitioners who cannot afford to train on every available sample. The three-state quality model is a useful conceptual reframing: the finding that the majority of samples in both Alpaca and WizardLM are 'Fixable' rather than simply 'bad' suggests that much of the value in instruction data is recoverable through targeted repair. Open questions include whether the approach scales to much larger corpora (the experiments use ~50K–64K samples), whether the 72B teacher model requirement is a practical bottleneck in resource-constrained settings, and whether the diagnostic-guided remediation strategy transfers to other fine-tuning paradigms beyond LoRA-based SFT.
