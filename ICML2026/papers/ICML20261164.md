## Problem

Standard confidence-based parallel decoding in Diffusion Language Models (DLMs) suffers from an inherent information-theoretic bottleneck: prioritizing high-confidence tokens reveals little information per step, forcing more decoding rounds to cover the fixed total information content of the output. The paper proves a lower bound formalizing this 'bits-to-rounds' tradeoff and identifies that existing block-based decoding further limits parallelism by processing blocks strictly sequentially.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (of arbitrary length) and a fixed target generation length (e.g., 512 tokens for MATH/GSM8K/HumanEval, 256 for MMLU-Pro) as input. The response window is initialized as a sequence of [M] mask tokens partitioned into contiguous blocks of fixed size (64 tokens), and the output is the fully unmasked generated text sequence. Decoding proceeds in iterative rounds where each round commits a subset of masked positions, with the total number of rounds being the key efficiency metric.

## Method

ETE (Explore-Then-Exploit) is a training-free decoding strategy built on two complementary mechanisms that maximize information decoded per round. First, fast block diffusion sampling replaces strict sequential block processing with budget-based progression (unlocking the next block after N rounds rather than full completion) and inter-block parallel decoding, expanding the feasible set of tokens that can be unmasked simultaneously. Second, when the decoding frontier becomes information-poor (average confidence below threshold γ), ETE triggers targeted exploration: it identifies medium-confidence candidate positions near an information level c_info*, performs batched look-ahead beam search with a shared KV cache to evaluate how each candidate reshapes the conditional distribution, and commits the hypothesis that induces the largest cascade of newly high-confidence downstream tokens. The theoretical foundation is a lower bound (Theorem 3.2) showing R ≥ max((−log p(x) − ε)/f, −log p(x)/log((n+1)/((1−f)n+1))), which ETE directly targets by amplifying the per-round information budget.

## Results

Across four main benchmarks (GSM8K, MATH, HumanEval, MMLU-Pro), ETE reduces the number of decoding steps by over 30% at comparable accuracy levels relative to the confidence-based baseline. In wall-clock time with KV caching, ETE achieves 50% higher throughput on GSM8K (accuracy > 0.75) and HumanEval, 30% on MATH, and up to 70% on MMLU-Pro. ETE also attains higher peak accuracy on GSM8K and MMLU-Pro. The information-theoretic lower bound (Theorem 3.2) is empirically validated: exploitation rounds scale linearly with sequence information content, with slopes decreasing monotonically as the confidence factor f decreases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares ETE against a single baseline: confidence-aware parallel decoding (Fast-DLLM, Wu et al., 2025) with a static confidence threshold, which the authors identified as yielding the strongest accuracy-speed Pareto frontier among prior methods. ETE consistently dominates this baseline on the accuracy-steps frontier across all four main benchmarks (GSM8K, MATH, HumanEval, MMLU-Pro), requiring over 30% fewer inference steps at nearly every accuracy level. In wall-clock time with KV caching, ETE achieves strictly better throughput: 50% higher on GSM8K (accuracy > 0.75) and HumanEval, 30% on MATH, and up to 70% on MMLU-Pro at comparable accuracy. ETE also reaches higher peak accuracy on GSM8K and MMLU-Pro. Additional evaluations on SVAMP and ARC-Challenge confirm the trend, with ETE saving nearly 50% steps in the high-accuracy regime. The fair-comparison variant (ETE Standard, with all hyperparameters fixed except the confidence threshold) still consistently outperforms the baseline, confirming the gains are not an artifact of the extra step-budget degree of freedom.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the block diffusion sampling framework of LLaDA (Nie et al., 2025) and the confidence-based parallel decoding with KV caching of Fast-DLLM (Wu et al., 2025), while drawing on the static-threshold exploitation step from Dimple (Yu et al., 2025). Its key conceptual departure is from the 'confident-token-first' paradigm that underlies all prior parallel decoding heuristics: the paper proves an information-theoretic lower bound showing that prioritizing high-confidence tokens is fundamentally suboptimal, then introduces Explore-Then-Exploit (ETE) to strategically decode high-entropy tokens that trigger cascades of confident predictions. The cross-block parallel decoding and budget-based progression mechanisms generalize the strictly sequential block-by-block schedule used in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The bits-to-rounds lower bound provides a clean theoretical explanation for why confidence-based decoding stalls, and ETE's training-free, modular design (compatible with existing KV caching) makes it directly deployable in current DLM inference stacks. The 30–70% throughput gains across four benchmarks and the identification of a hardware-dependent 'free lunch' regime for batched beam search have immediate practical relevance for serving DLMs at scale. Open questions include whether upper bounds can be established under structured-data assumptions, whether learned scoring heads can replace the look-ahead beam search, and how ETE's many hyperparameters (threshold, budget, beam size, trigger criterion) should be tuned under varying latency and memory constraints in production.
