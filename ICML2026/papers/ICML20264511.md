## Problem

Continuous Normalizing Flows and Conditional Flow Matching models are inherently nonlinear black boxes: sampling requires costly iterative ODE integration, and the intermediate generative dynamics are uninterpretable. Prior acceleration methods (Rectified Flow, MeanFlow, consistency distillation) either straighten paths or distill only endpoints, discarding the teacher's intermediate vector field. The paper asks whether a single fixed linear operator can globally linearize the full nonlinear generative trajectory while remaining faithful to the teacher's dynamics at every point, not merely at boundaries.

## Input / Output

For generation, the method takes a Gaussian noise sample x_0 ~ N(0,I) and produces a single image in one forward pass via a matrix exponential and decoder, with no iterative integration. For inversion and editing, it accepts a real image (32×32 RGB for FFHQ/CIFAR-10, 28×28 grayscale for MNIST), inverts it to noise space through the learned linear operator, and supports semantic perturbation in the 1024-dimensional Koopman latent space. The Koopman generator L (1026×1026 affine matrix) is itself an output, enabling Schur decomposition for spectral analysis, class-conditioned transfer functions, and mode-level editing that can be transferred back to the original CFM noise space.

## Method

The method lifts a pre-trained OT-CFM teacher into a Koopman observable space using a SongUNet encoder g_φ and decoder g_ψ^{-1}, with time-dependence handled by augmenting the state to [1, t, g(t,x)]^T and constraining the generator L to a block-affine structure that enforces ṫ=1 by design. Training uses four losses: phase and target losses match endpoints in Koopman and image space, a reconstruction loss fixes the gauge of the non-identifiable Koopman coordinates, and—critically—a consistency loss that enforces infinitesimal alignment between L·g and ∇g·v_t at every point along the trajectory. The paper proves (Proposition 3) that this consistency loss admits an unbiased, simulation-free estimator using conditional samples p_t(·|x_1) paired with the known marginal velocity v_t, avoiding the need to store full ODE trajectories. Sampling reduces to a single matrix exponential: x̂_1 = g_ψ^{-1}(exp(L)·g_φ(0,x_0)), and the Schur decomposition of L yields a coarse-to-fine mode hierarchy that supports spectral analysis, semantic direction discovery via CLIP coherence, and teacher-aligned inversion and editing unavailable in endpoint-only distillation.

## Results

At 1 NFE, the Koopman-CFM model with consistency achieves FID of 7.1 (MNIST), 10.1 (FFHQ), and 16.7 (CIFAR-10), competitive with OT-CFM at 25 NFE (11.6 FFHQ) while being 5.6× faster (37.2 ms vs 209 ms per image). The key differentiator is trajectory fidelity: the consistency-trained model achieves mean MSE of 0.5×10⁻⁵ between Koopman rollouts and CFM trajectories on FFHQ, versus 130×10⁻⁵ without consistency—a 260× improvement. The model without consistency achieves slightly better endpoint FID (7.5 FFHQ) but loses all trajectory and interpretability guarantees.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against OT-CFM, the Koopman method at 1 NFE dramatically outperforms the teacher at the same budget (FID 10.1 vs 149 on FFHQ, 16.7 vs 226 on CIFAR-10) and matches OT-CFM at 25 NFE (10.1 vs 11.6 FFHQ) while being 5.6× faster. Against Rectified Flow (1-step, FID 4.23 FFHQ) and MeanFlow (1-step, FID 3.34 FFHQ), the Koopman method has a 2–3× FID gap, but these baselines provide no trajectory fidelity or spectral interpretability. The paper's central claim is not raw FID superiority: the consistency-trained Koopman model uniquely achieves trajectory MSE of 0.5×10⁻⁵ (vs 130×10⁻⁵ without consistency), enabling mode decomposition, semantic editing with CLIP coherence up to 0.97, and class-conditioned spectral signatures—capabilities entirely absent in the straight-path and distillation baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Koopman operator theory (Koopman, 1931; Brunton et al., 2022) to the non-autonomous dynamics of Conditional Flow Matching, building directly on Berman et al. (2025) who first applied Koopman modeling to one-step distillation of diffusion models. The key departure is a trajectory consistency loss that enforces infinitesimal alignment with the teacher's vector field at every point along the generative path, rather than the boundary-only matching used in prior Koopman distillation or straight-path methods (Rectified Flow, MeanFlow). The non-autonomous adaptation via state-space augmentation with an affine lift is a technical contribution that makes the classical autonomous Koopman framework applicable to time-dependent flow matching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single linear operator can faithfully reproduce the full nonlinear trajectory of a CFM model, unlocking spectral analysis (coarse-to-fine mode hierarchy, class-conditional signatures) and principled semantic editing that are unavailable in black-box one-step samplers. However, experiments are limited to 32×32 images, the FID is competitive but not SOTA (and slightly worse than the no-consistency ablation), and the quality gap widens on more complex datasets, leaving open whether finite-dimensional Koopman representations exist for high-resolution or more complex flows. Scaling to latent-space DiTs and diffusion SDEs is identified as the most promising next step.
