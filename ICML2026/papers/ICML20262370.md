## Problem

Black-box forgery attacks on semantic watermarks have been demonstrated empirically, but the conditions under which such attacks succeed or fail remain theoretically uncharacterized. In particular, it is unclear how much security semantic watermarking systems can provide in practice, or how to design defenses that generalize beyond specific attack implementations.

## Input / Output

The method operates entirely in the latent space of a latent diffusion model (512×512 images). It takes a watermarked image and the reference watermarked latent z_T (recovered via the target model's DDIM inversion) as input, and produces two geometric deviation scores—global cosine similarity (G-Cos) and local SPD distance (L-SPD)—that serve as a scheme-agnostic forgery detection signal before any watermark verification is performed. For L-SPD, the latent is partitioned into a 16×16 spatial grid, SPD covariance matrices are computed per patch, and a top-k mean (k=5) aggregation identifies the most significant regional distortions.

## Method

The paper formalizes black-box forgery as a rate-distortion problem, proving that proxy-target model mismatch induces an irreducible distortion floor D_irr that fundamentally limits forgery fidelity regardless of the adversary's computational resources. This distortion is then characterized geometrically as two complementary structured deviations on the latent manifold: (1) global directional drift on the unit hypersphere, measured by Spherical Angular Distortion (SAD), and (2) local structural deformation on the SPD Riemannian manifold, measured by Local SPD Geometric Inconsistency (LGI) under the Affine-Invariant Riemannian Metric. The detection pipeline is training-free and scheme-agnostic: it computes G-Cos (cosine similarity between z_T and recovered z_T) and L-SPD (AIRM distance between local SPD covariance matrices) as a pre-verification step, flagging samples with anomalous geometric drift as potential forgeries before watermark extraction. Theoretical guarantees (Theorem 4.10) establish that forged and watermarked latents become asymptotically separable in the joint (SAD, LGI) space as latent dimension N grows.

## Results

Under guidance-based forgery attacks, the proposed G-Cos (global cosine similarity) and L-SPD (local SPD geometric inconsistency) metrics achieve near-perfect AUC detection across most cross-model proxy-target combinations, with G-Cos reaching 1.000 AUC for all six watermarking schemes (TR, RingID, HSTR, HSQR, GS, TAG) when the proxy and target differ (e.g., SD2.1 proxy → FLUX target). In the most challenging identical-model setting (SD3→SD3), performance degrades but remains meaningful: G-Cos achieves 0.943 AUC for TR and 0.933 for GS, while L-SPD maintains 1.000 and 0.993 respectively. Under optimization-based attacks, both metrics retain AUC above 0.950 across all target models and optimization step counts (20, 50, 100), with only marginal degradation as optimization steps increase (e.g., L-SPD for TR on SDXL drops from 0.997 at 20 steps to 0.990 at 100 steps).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against a separate external detection baseline; instead, it compares its two geometric metrics (G-Cos for global directional drift and L-SPD for local structural deformation) against each other and against a simple local cosine similarity baseline (grid size 16, mean aggregation). G-Cos consistently outperforms L-SPD in cross-model settings, achieving 1.000 AUC in nearly all cases, while L-SPD lags slightly (e.g., 0.832 for RingID under SD2.1→SD3). In the identical-model setting (SD3→SD3), L-SPD actually surpasses G-Cos for several schemes (e.g., 1.000 vs. 0.943 for TR), suggesting the two metrics capture complementary geometric signatures. The local cosine baseline achieves competitive performance (AUC = 1.000 for TR and GS under SDXL→SD2.1, and 0.954/0.976 in the identical-model setting), confirming that capturing geometric discrepancies is the key factor rather than the specific metric choice. The paper also contextualizes detection against attack success rates: when forgery TPR is high (e.g., 0.991 for TR with SD2.1→SD2.1), detection AUC drops to 0.955, whereas when forgery TPR is low (e.g., 0.161 for TR with FLUX→SD2.1), detection AUC reaches 1.000, validating the rate-distortion distortion floor theory.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the black-box forgery attack framework of Müller et al. (2025), which demonstrated empirically that proxy models can regenerate watermarked content, but departs from that work by providing the first theoretical formalization of forgery limits through a rate-distortion lens. The geometric detection mechanism draws on the Affine-Invariant Riemannian Metric (AIRM) from Pennec et al. (2006) for SPD manifold analysis, repurposing a tool from tensor computing to characterize local structural deformation in latent space. Unlike prior semantic watermarking schemes (Tree-Ring, Gaussian Shading, TAG) that focus on embedding and verification, this work introduces a scheme-agnostic pre-verification stage that operates purely on latent geometry, orthogonal to any specific watermark design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The irreducible distortion floor established by the rate-distortion analysis provides a principled upper bound on forgery fidelity, giving service providers a concrete security guarantee rather than relying on empirical attack evaluations. The detection method's zero-storage requirement (operating only on z_T and recovered latents) makes it deployable at scale without modifying existing watermarking pipelines. Key limitations include degraded (though still functional) performance in the identical-model setting, sensitivity to stochastic image distortions that corrupt latent coherence, and the open question of extending the framework to video-based semantic watermarks like VideoShield. The finding that even internal accumulated error (without any proxy mismatch) provides detectable geometric signal suggests the approach may generalize to other inversion-based verification scenarios.
