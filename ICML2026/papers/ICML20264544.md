## Problem

A fundamental question in understanding thinking LLMs is what they learn during post-training that their base counterparts lack, with prior work offering competing but untested hypotheses (new capabilities, better structuring, repurposed representations, or mere inference-time compute). No existing methodology directly decomposes the base-to-fine-tuned difference into interpretable, causally testable components. This paper addresses the gap by introducing constructive model diffing, which explicitly reconstructs the difference and measures how well the reconstruction recovers the thinking model's performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264544/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pairs of base and thinking models (nine pairs spanning 0.5B–32B parameters) along with sentence-level activations extracted from the thinking model's reasoning traces, where token activations are averaged within punctuation-delimited sentences to form the SAE training signal. It produces an interpretable reasoning taxonomy of 5–15 categories (titles, descriptions, exemplar sentences), a set of optimized category vectors in the base model's activation space, and a hybrid model that steers the base model at a sparse fraction of token positions (typically 5–28%). The final quantitative output is a gap-recovery percentage measuring what fraction of the base-to-thinking accuracy gap the hybrid closes on held-out benchmarks.

## Method

Constructive model diffing decomposes the base-to-thinking difference into two interpretable components: reasoning mechanisms (per-category steering vectors optimized in the base model) and reasoning heuristics (an SAE-based classifier from the thinking model that selects which mechanism to deploy at each position). The taxonomy is discovered unsupervised via Top-K SAEs with deliberately small dictionaries (5–50 features, k=3 sparsity) trained on sentence-level activations, effectively performing subspace clustering over reasoning functions. Category vectors are trained by teacher-forcing thinking-model rollouts through the base model, identifying token positions where the two models disagree, labelling each position with the top-activating SAE category, and jointly optimizing per-category vectors plus a small coefficient MLP to minimize cross-entropy on the thinking model's next token. At inference, the hybrid model generates with the base model, checks for disagreement at each step, and when disagreement occurs, applies the SAE-selected category vector scaled by the MLP-predicted coefficient. Across nine model pairs, RL-trained models recover ~76% of the base-to-thinking gap while SFT-distilled models recover only ~11%, indicating that RL primarily teaches heuristics for orchestrating pre-existing base-model mechanisms whereas SFT-distillation installs new ones.

## Results

Across nine base/thinking model pairs evaluated on GSM8K, MATH500, and a held-out Hendrycks-MATH subset, RL-trained Open-Reasoner-Zero models recover roughly 76% of the base-to-thinking accuracy gap on average (ranging from 49% to 107% across individual benchmarks and model sizes), while SFT-distilled DeepSeek-R1-Distill models recover far less, from negative values at 1.5B/8B up to ~15–20% at 14B/32B. The mixed QwQ-32B (SFT+RL) recovers ~20%, aligning with the distilled models rather than the RL-trained ones. Notably, the RL-trained hybrids achieve this recovery while steering only 5–12% of tokens per problem, whereas SFT-distilled hybrids steer 12–28%, ruling out token-level memorization as an explanation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external steering or diffing baselines; instead, it contrasts three training paradigms (RL, SFT-distillation, and mixed SFT+RL) under an identical constructive model diffing protocol. RL-trained Open-Reasoner-Zero models consistently outperform same-size SFT-distilled counterparts: at 32B, ORZ-32B recovers 49–94% of the gap versus 12–32% for R1-Distill-32B and 14–29% for QwQ-32B. At 1.5B, ORZ-1.5B recovers 71–88% while R1-Distill-1.5B recovers essentially nothing (−3 to +6%). The category-vector training loss corroborates this: ORZ-derived vectors converge to substantially lower cross-entropy than R1-derived vectors across all held-out sets, indicating the base model already contains the reasoning directions that RL exploits. Ablations confirm that both the learned category vectors and the SAE-based position/category selection are necessary — randomizing either drops recovery to 20–28% or below zero, respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the activation-steering literature (Turner et al., 2023; Venhoff et al., 2025) and Top-K SAE interpretability (Makhzani & Frey, 2014; Gao et al., 2025) into a unified 'constructive model diffing' framework that explicitly builds the base-to-fine-tuned difference from interpretable components rather than analyzing it post-hoc as crosscoders (Lindsey et al., 2024) do. It departs from prior model-diffing work by hypothesizing the diff operates at the level of reasoning mechanisms and their deployment heuristics, not individual linear features, and by measuring recovery of the fine-tuned model's performance as the validation criterion. The unsupervised SAE-based taxonomy discovery builds on the authors' prior steering-vector work (Venhoff et al., 2025) and concurrent SAE reasoning-feature analyses (Galichin et al., 2026; Troitskii et al., 2025), but adds the constructive reconstruction step that those works lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that RL-trained thinking models recover ~76% of the base-to-thinking gap via sparse steering (5–12% of tokens) while SFT-distilled models recover only ~11% provides a concrete, mechanistic distinction between what the two training paradigms teach: RL orchestrates pre-existing base-model mechanisms, while SFT installs new ones. This has direct practical implications for efficient reasoning-model development, suggesting that for RL-style training, the base model's latent capabilities matter more than the fine-tuning itself. Key limitations include the math-biased taxonomies (discovered on MMLU-Pro), the acknowledged alternative explanation that low SFT recovery may reflect category-vector optimization limitations rather than genuine mechanism modification, and the restriction to a single family of base models (Qwen2.5) plus one Llama variant, leaving open whether the RL-vs-SFT distinction generalizes across architectures and domains.
