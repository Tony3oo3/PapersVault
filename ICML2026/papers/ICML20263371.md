## Problem

Exploring the combinatorial peptide space (3.3×10³² sequences of length ≤25) for antimicrobial peptide discovery is intractable by brute force, and existing latent-space generative models rely on flat Euclidean metrics that ignore decoder-induced geometry, distorting distances and making exploration inefficient. Moreover, prior manifold-based approaches assume a fixed intrinsic dimensionality equal to the latent dimension, but the manifold hypothesis empirically fails for peptide data—the decoder Jacobian is rank-deficient, so the pullback metric is degenerate and the latent space is not a single Riemannian manifold.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained generative model decoder (HydrAMP or PepCVAE) that maps latent vectors to position-factorized peptide probability matrices in R^{L×A}, along with prototype or seed peptide sequences and a black-box property oracle (the APEX transformer MIC regressor predicting log₂ MIC across E. coli strains). It produces discrete peptide sequences at two levels: PoGS yields seed peptides from geodesic trajectories between prototype pairs, and LE-BO iteratively refines these into optimized candidates. The local enumeration step generates candidate sets as Cartesian products of per-position admissible residue sets derived from tangent-space mutation directions, and the final outputs are validated in vitro against 19 bacterial strains including 8 MDR isolates.

## Method

PepCompass decomposes the latent space into a Union of κ-Stable Riemannian Manifolds M^κ, where each point z is assigned a maximal-dimension affine submanifold W_z^κ constructed via truncated SVD of the decoder Jacobian, retaining only singular values exceeding κ to guarantee a positive-definite pullback metric with bounded condition number. For global exploration, PoGS optimizes a discrete energy functional combining ambient chord-length (kinetic), a property potential Φ (e.g., negative log MIC), and a latent regularizer, using ADAM to find low-potential seeds along geodesic-like curves between two prototypes. For local exploration, SORBES performs a provably convergent second-order Riemannian Brownian walk (with Christoffel-symbol corrections approximated via finite differences of the decoded curve) restricted to W_z^κ, while MUTANG reshapes the orthonormal basis of the ambient tangent space into L×A sensitivity maps and thresholds entries to enumerate discrete amino-acid substitutions. These are combined in LE-BO, which iteratively populates local neighborhoods via SORBES+MUTANG, fits a Gaussian process surrogate directly in peptide space, and selects the next evaluation by maximizing a GP acquisition function within a Levenshtein trust region, with ROBOT diversity filtering to encourage broad exploration.

## Results

LE-BO achieves the best minimal log₂ MIC across 5 of 6 prototype peptides (range 0.50–0.60), outperforming all 13 baselines except AdaLead on FL14 (0.51 vs. 0.60). In wet-lab validation, all 29 generated peptides (4 PoGS seeds + 25 LE-BO analogs) showed in vitro activity against at least one of 19 bacterial strains (100% success at 32 μg/ml; 82% at the more stringent 4 μg/ml threshold), including 8 multidrug-resistant isolates. PoGS with property-aware potential (λ=0.01) identifies 22.7±10.5 seeds and 12.6±4.5 wells per trajectory, roughly doubling the seed count over straight Euclidean interpolation (11.7±5.5 seeds, 2.5±1.9 wells).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LE-BO achieves the best log₂ MIC on 5 of 6 prototypes, with the sole exception being FL14 where AdaLead (0.51±0.38) narrowly edges out LE-BO (0.60±0.22). The closest overall competitors are AdaLead and DyNA-PPO, which reach log₂ MIC values in the 0.7–2.4 range, while LE-BO consistently stays below 0.60. Generative-model baselines (HydrAMP, PepCVAE, LaMBO-2) and evolutionary strategies (CMA-ES variants, Joker) are substantially outperformed, with log₂ MIC gaps of 1.0–5.0 units. The ablation showing Euclidean walk + mutations (0.65–0.87) confirms that the Riemannian geometry of SORBES provides a consistent ~0.1–0.2 log₂ MIC improvement over flat-space random walks. In wet-lab validation, PepCompass's 100% success rate at 32 μg/ml surpasses HydrAMP, AMP-Diffusion, CLaSS, and Joker, which all show lower fractions of active peptides at the same threshold.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Riemannian latent-space exploration paradigm (Arvanitidis et al., 2021a) by replacing the single-manifold assumption with a Union of κ-Stable Riemannian Manifolds, motivated by empirical evidence that the decoder Jacobian is rank-deficient for peptide generative models. The local exploration component builds directly on the second-order convergence theory of Schwarz et al. (2023) for Riemannian Brownian motion, extending it to non-compact manifolds via a spherical-cap compactification. The global exploration (PoGS) generalizes standard geodesic interpolation by augmenting it with a property potential, while the local optimization (LE-BO) integrates the ROBOT diversity scheme (Maus et al., 2023) with a novel tangent-space mutation enumeration (MUTANG) that reinterprets decoder Jacobian eigenvectors as biologically meaningful amino-acid substitutions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 100% in-vitro validation rate (29/29 peptides active, including against MDR strains) is unprecedented in the AMP design literature and demonstrates that geometry-informed exploration can dramatically outperform flat-Euclidean or purely generative approaches. Key limitations include the use of a Euclidean ambient metric (which is mutationally uniform but ignores biochemical substitution preferences) and the Cartesian-product assumption in MUTANG that neglects multi-site epistatic interactions. Open questions include whether biologically informed ambient metrics (sketched in Appendix D) further improve performance, whether the framework generalizes to other molecular sequence spaces (proteins, nucleic acids), and whether the κ-stable manifold construction scales to higher-dimensional latent spaces beyond the 64-dim HydrAMP model used here.
