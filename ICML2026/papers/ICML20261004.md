## Problem

Machine-learned XC functionals trained to reproduce reference energies and densities still do not consistently outperform traditional O(N⁴)-scaling hybrid functionals, because prior supervision only constrains the self-consistent fixed point and ignores the local response information (first and second functional derivatives) that is readily available from the reference hybrid functional. This paper asks whether supervising these derivatives on the space of admissible density matrices can close the accuracy gap while retaining O(N³) scaling.

## Input / Output

The method consumes electron densities represented as idempotent density matrices P = C_occ C_occ^T in a finite atom-centered Gaussian basis, parameterized on the Grassmannian manifold via O×V orbital rotation angles θ_ov. The loss is evaluated not only at the converged ground-state density but along the entire SCF trajectory with increasing weights on later iterations, and the gradient/Hessian terms are computed with respect to these tangent coordinates rather than directly with respect to the real-space density. Outputs include the learned XC energy and potential at each SCF step, the self-consistent density, and downstream TDDFT excitation energies via the Casida equations.

## Method

DI-Loss augments the standard energy + density loss with two derivative terms defined on the Grassmannian of admissible density matrices. The gradient loss supervises the O×V occupied-virtual block of the XC potential (computed in closed form as −2(CᵀΔV_xc C)_ia) along the SCF trajectory, constraining exactly the directions that move the density and drive the SCF update, while leaving density-invariant occupied-occupied and virtual-virtual blocks unconstrained. The Hessian loss supervises T=8 randomly sampled linear responses at the ground state via Hessian-vector products (O(OV) cost rather than O((OV)²) storage), with perturbation directions importance-weighted by 1/(ε_a−ε_i) to concentrate on low-gap transitions that dominate linear response and TDDFT excitation energies. Training is stabilized by a Metropolis-inspired accept-reject mechanism that adaptively rejects parameter updates causing anomalous loss changes, combined with the Muon optimizer and variance-preserving initialization. The approach is evaluated in a distillation setting where O(N³)-scaling semi-local architectures (NNmGGA, XCdiff, Skala-mGGA, EG-XC) are trained to reproduce B3LYP/def2-SVP targets, reducing total-energy MAE by 66% on average and improving TDDFT excitation-energy MAE by 24–35%.

## Results

In the B3LYP/def2-SVP distillation setting evaluated out-of-distribution on QM40, DI-Loss reduces the total-energy MAE by 66% on average across four architectures (NNmGGA: 4.2→2.9 mEh, XCdiff: 8.9→5.5 mEh, Skala-mGGA: 15.8→3.6 mEh, EG-XC: 15.9→3.1 mEh) relative to energy-plus-density supervision alone. The density-sensitive mean-field energy metric E_ρ improves from 1.2 to 0.8 mEh on average. In TDDFT, Hessian supervision reduces the mean excitation-energy MAE by 24–35% across molecule sizes (5–10 heavy atoms) for the best architecture (XCdiff), with the 5th excitation MAE dropping from 241 to 139 meV at 5 heavy atoms. Distilled ML-XC densities reduce B3LYP SCF iterations to ~50% of MINAO initialization (vs. ~65% for SCAN), yielding a 1.35× walltime speedup on a 35-heavy-atom molecule.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is between loss configurations (E+ρ vs. E+ρ+∇ vs. full DI-Loss) across four ML-XC architectures, with E+ρ serving as the baseline. DI-Loss wins on total-energy MAE for every architecture, with the largest gains for Skala-mGGA (77% reduction) and EG-XC (81% reduction), while NNmGGA and XCdiff show more modest 31% and 38% improvements respectively. For density metrics (L₂[ρ], dipole μ_ρ), DI-Loss does not improve uniformly: it slightly worsens L₂[ρ] on the three semi-local architectures but improves it by 8% on EG-XC, which the authors attribute to its non-local capacity absorbing the derivative signal. In TDDFT, adding only the gradient term actually raises excitation-energy MAE by 13–18% above the E+ρ baseline for XCdiff; the Hessian term is essential to reverse this and achieve the 24–35% reductions. For SCF acceleration, distilled ML-XC densities (RIC ≈ 50%) outperform the traditional SCAN semi-local initialization (RIC ≈ 65%) by roughly 15 percentage points in relative iteration count, though DI-Loss itself has only a marginal additional effect on RIC beyond what E+ρ distillation already provides.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the end-to-end differentiable SCF training framework established by Tamayo-Mendoza et al. (2018) and Li et al. (2021), which supervise energy and density along the SCF trajectory, by adding gradient and Hessian supervision defined on the Grassmannian manifold of admissible density matrices (Edelman et al., 1998). Unlike Kanungo et al. (2025), which constrains the XC potential through a single density-weighted scalar per sample, DI-Loss supervises the full O×V occupied-virtual block of the potential difference, capturing all density-moving directions. The Hessian supervision uses importance-weighted Hessian-vector products with gap-biased sampling that the authors connect to the OEP response function and the Casida matrix structure, providing a physically grounded curvature match without forming the full response operator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 66% average reduction in total-energy MAE and the 24–35% improvement in TDDFT excitation energies demonstrate that derivative supervision on the Grassmannian regularizes the learned functional beyond the fixed point, affecting SCF dynamics, orbital gaps, and linear response. The practical SCF acceleration (50% iteration reduction for B3LYP) offers a secondary use for distilled functionals as initializers. Key limitations include restriction to closed-shell organic molecules, applicability only in the distillation setting where reference derivatives are available, and an unresolved expressivity gap for O(N³) architectures capturing exact exchange. Open questions include extension to periodic systems, higher-fidelity targets (CCSD(T)), and whether the Hessian supervision overhead can be further reduced for larger basis sets.
