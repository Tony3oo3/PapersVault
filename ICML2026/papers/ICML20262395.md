## Problem

Latent/continuous chain-of-thought methods replace explicit textual rationales with internal hidden-state steps, but these intermediate computations are opaque and resist traditional interpretability tools such as step editing or ablation. This paper addresses the resulting gap by asking three causal questions that remain unexplored: which latent steps are causally necessary for correctness and when the answer first becomes decodable, how influence propagates across steps relative to explicit CoT, and whether intermediate trajectories retain competing answer modes or collapse early.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262395/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text problem (GSM8K, CommonsenseQA, or StrategyQA) and a latent trajectory of T=6 last-layer hidden states produced by a latent-reasoning model (Coconut or CODI). A single latent state is zeroed out and all downstream states are recomputed under fixed parameters; outputs include per-step flip rates (fraction of examples whose decoded answer changes), a T×T influence matrix W built from position-averaged teacher-forced KL shifts on gold-answer tokens, a superposition score measuring simultaneous support for competing binary answer modes, and a cumulative solved fraction tracking when correctness first becomes decodable under early-stop decoding.

## Method

The paper models latent CoT as a structural causal model where each latent step is a variable, and evaluates it through step-wise do-interventions that overwrite one hidden state while keeping the downstream transition mechanism intact. For influence estimation (RQ2), a single-step intervention at t is coupled with a teacher-forced readout at a downstream step s, and the token-averaged KL divergence between baseline and intervened distributions on the gold answer defines the directed propagation strength W_{t,s}, which is aggregated into an influence matrix and rendered as a sparsified principal graph. For superposition analysis (RQ3), stochastic rollouts are filtered to prompts yielding both Yes and No answers, and two complementary readouts—teacher-forced log-probability scoring and a lightweight linear probe trained on frozen latent states—track how competing modes persist or collapse across steps. The framework is instantiated on Coconut and CODI across GPT-2, Llama3-1B, and Qwen3-4B-Instruct backbones, with robustness checks across six intervention operators (zero, mean, Gaussian variants) and an alternative LM-head readout protocol to confirm that the observed non-local routing and commitment gap are not artifacts of a single operator or readout choice.

## Results

Step-wise zero-intervention flip rates on GSM8K reach 0.1–0.2+ for several backbones (Coconut and CODI), while CommonsenseQA flip rates remain mostly below ~0.1, indicating substantially higher decision volatility for arithmetic. On the influence-structure side, explicit CoT-SFT exhibits uniformly high locality (all ≥ 0.6) with low span, whereas latent Coconut/CODI models are markedly less local, have larger expected hop distances, and place substantially more normalized influence into late-step targets (late-in). Early-stop decoding shows that on CommonsenseQA the cumulative solved fraction S(k) saturates within the first few steps, while on GSM8K S(k) continues to increase through k=6, indicating that additional latent computation expands the set of decodable instances. For superposition, teacher-forced SS(t) is uniformly low across steps (early output-level commitment), while probe-based SS(t) remains substantially higher through intermediate steps with a clear drop only at the final step (late representational collapse).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three latent-reasoning paradigms (Coconut, CODI, Sim-CoT) against an explicit CoT-SFT baseline under a unified intervention and readout protocol. Coconut consistently shows higher flip rates than CODI under matched backbones, especially on GSM8K, and its influence graphs are dominated by early→late skip connections (e.g., step 1 directly to step 6), whereas CODI departs from a strict chain but exhibits less extreme long-range routing and greater cross-backbone variation. Both latent paradigms are markedly less local than CoT-SFT: CoT-SFT maintains near-chain topology (locality ≥ 0.6, low span) across all backbones, while Coconut/CODI show skip-dominant propagation with larger spans and higher late-in mass. Sim-CoT (Appendix D) occupies an intermediate position, showing more local connectivity than the strongest Coconut/CODI skip-heavy cases, consistent with its step-aligned supervision objective. On RQ3 superposition, CODI variants maintain higher probe-based superposition scores than Coconut variants across latent steps, aligning with CODI's less extreme long-range routing observed in RQ2.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the structural causal model (SCM) and do-intervention framework from Pearl (2000) and Robins & Greenland (1992) to a new domain: continuous latent reasoning steps, where the unit of analysis is a hidden-state vector rather than a text token or attention head. It extends the CoT faithfulness literature (Wang et al. 2023a; Yang et al. 2023; Yu et al. 2025), which applied text-level edits (deletion, shuffling) to explicit rationales, by defining an analogous intervention on latent states and measuring propagation via teacher-forced KL shifts. The mechanistic-interpretability toolkit of activation patching and causal tracing (Vig et al. 2020; Finlayson et al. 2021; Meng et al. 2022) provides the methodological template, but the paper's key departure is treating the latent-step sequence itself as the causal graph rather than individual model components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As latent/continuous CoT methods (Coconut, CODI, Sim-CoT) gain traction as alternatives to verbose explicit reasoning, this paper provides the first step-resolved causal diagnostic for understanding what those latent steps actually do. The finding that latent influence is non-local (skip-dominant) rather than chain-like, and that output-level commitment precedes representational commitment, directly informs training and decoding design: latent budgets should be allocated by functional role rather than treated as uniform extra depth. Key limitations include a fixed T=6 budget, single-step zero-overwrite interventions, and evaluation on relatively small backbones (≤4B), leaving open whether the observed routing structure persists at scale or with adaptive budgets.
