## Problem

On-policy RL methods like PPO rely on Gaussian actors with shallow MLPs, a parameterization choice that is often treated as a secondary design decision but is in fact a primary source of brittle optimization, high gradient variance, and sensitivity to hyperparameters. The paper asks whether replacing the Gaussian actor with a discretized categorical policy—turning the policy update into a cross-entropy-like objective—and pairing it with a regularized network architecture can systematically improve on-policy continuous control.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260282/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The actor receives either a low-dimensional state vector (MuJoCo locomotion, ManiSkill state-based tasks) or a raw RGB image encoded by a NatureCNN (ManiSkill vision-based tasks). It outputs a continuous action vector in [-1,1]^m, but this is produced by a factorized categorical distribution: the network emits d×K logits (d action dimensions, K=41 uniform bins per dimension), and the sampled action is the bin center selected per dimension. The critic architecture and all other training components remain identical to standard PPO, making the actor a true drop-in replacement.

## Method

RN-D replaces the standard diagonal-Gaussian actor with a factorized categorical policy where each action dimension is a softmax over K=41 uniformly spaced bins, converting the PPO surrogate into a weighted cross-entropy loss over action bins rather than a weighted squared-error regression. This parameterization is paired with a regularized actor network consisting of pre-LayerNorm residual MLP blocks (inverted-bottleneck FFN with 4× hidden expansion and ReLU), borrowed from Transformer and SimBa architectures. A theoretical analysis (Proposition 4.1) shows the categorical policy's gradient variance is bounded by mR²(1−1/K) regardless of exploration level, whereas the Gaussian policy's variance scales as R²/σ² and grows unboundedly as the standard deviation shrinks during training. A loss-swap ablation confirms that the categorical likelihood (cross-entropy) is the critical ingredient: using the same discretized representation but optimizing with a Gaussian log-likelihood (squared error) eliminates most of the performance gain. The approach is algorithm-agnostic, consistently improving PPO, TRPO, and SPO across locomotion and manipulation benchmarks with 1.3–1.9× sample-efficiency speedups and no meaningful runtime overhead.

## Results

RN-D (regularized network + discretized categorical actor) consistently achieves the best final performance across Gym locomotion (TD3-normalized return), ManiSkill state-based (success rate), and ManiSkill RGB-based (success rate) benchmarks under PPO, TRPO, and SPO. It reaches the best baseline's performance using roughly 1.3–1.9× fewer environment steps, and is the only variant that reliably solves the challenging StackCube task where other methods remain near-zero success. Wall-clock overhead is negligible: RN-D runs at 873.8 SPS (1.64 h) versus 854.4 SPS (1.65 h) for the standard MLP-C baseline across 5 Gym locomotion tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the off-policy TD3 baseline, RN-D outperforms on StackCube-v1 (rapidly reaching high success rate while TD3 fails to learn) and is competitive on Humanoid-v4, where TD3 initially improves quickly but RN-D continues to improve with more interaction. Against TD-MPC2, RN-D clearly wins on both tasks: TD-MPC2 makes little progress on Humanoid-v4 under the same wall-clock horizon and only improves much later on StackCube-v1, while also being considerably less computationally efficient. Within the on-policy family, RN-D beats the standard MLP-C Gaussian actor on all benchmarks, with the gap most pronounced on harder tasks (Ant, Humanoid, StackCube). The ablation comparing RN-D vs. RN-C isolates the benefit of the categorical (cross-entropy) objective over the Gaussian (squared-error) objective with the same regularized backbone, while MLP-D vs. MLP-C isolates the benefit of discretization with a standard MLP.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on two complementary lines: (1) discretized categorical policies for continuous control, initiated by Tang & Agrawal (2020) and extended by Zhu et al. (2024) with ordinal/unimodal parameterizations, and (2) regularized residual network architectures from supervised learning (SimBa, BroNet) that have been applied to critic networks in off-policy RL. The key departure is treating the actor as a first-class optimization object: the paper provides a gradient-variance analysis showing categorical policies have bounded variance independent of exploration scale, and a loss-geometry argument connecting the PPO actor update to weighted cross-entropy (analogous to Farebrother et al.'s cross-entropy-for-value-functions result), then pairs the simplest factorized categorical actor with a pre-LayerNorm residual MLP backbone. Unlike prior work that either discretizes actions without architectural changes or scales networks without changing the policy parameterization, RN-D combines both and isolates their complementary contributions via ablation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is a simple, algorithm-agnostic drop-in replacement (Gaussian → categorical actor + residual network) that consistently improves on-policy RL across PPO, TRPO, and SPO on both locomotion and manipulation benchmarks, with 1.3–1.9× sample-efficiency gains. The loss-swap ablation (categorical representation but Gaussian likelihood) is a useful diagnostic showing the cross-entropy objective itself, not merely discretization, drives most of the gain. Open questions include whether the benefit persists in actor-critic co-scaling regimes, how bin count interacts with task complexity at scale, and whether the approach transfers to off-policy or model-based settings. The paper's scope is limited to standard MuJoCo and ManiSkill benchmarks with moderate network sizes, so generalization to long-horizon, contact-rich, or multi-agent settings remains untested.
