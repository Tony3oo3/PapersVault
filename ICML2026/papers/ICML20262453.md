## Problem

Flow Matching theoretically guarantees constant-velocity straight-line trajectories, but in high-dimensional practice the MSE objective systematically underestimates velocity magnitude (the Velocity Deficit), causing generated samples to stall short of the data manifold (Integration Lag). Prior work focused on trajectory curvature (ReFlow, distillation) but overlooked this magnitude bias, and a naive global velocity boost is suboptimal because the contraction is harmful at the trajectory start (signal starvation) yet beneficial at the end (implicit denoising).

## Input / Output

The method operates on pre-trained flow matching models (SiT, MMDiT, U-Net) for class-conditional generation (ImageNet-1k at 256×256 and 512×512) and text-to-image generation (MS-COCO). SSC is a training-free, one-line inference-time modification to the ODE solver that scales the predicted velocity field by a time-dependent factor γ(t), requiring no retraining or architectural changes. MAFM modifies the training loss by adding a magnitude supervision term with a linearly decaying weight, applied during training of SiT or REPA-SiT backbones.

## Method

The paper proves via Jensen's Inequality that the MSE-optimal velocity field v*(x_t) = E[v_target | x_t] strictly underestimates kinetic energy because the squared norm is strictly convex and the target velocity has non-zero conditional variance under independent data coupling. The key insight is an asymmetry: at t→0 the missing signal component (α̇₀x₁) causes catastrophic integration lag, while at t→1 the missing noise component (σ̇₁x₀) acts as beneficial implicit denoising. Two solutions instantiate 'Initial Energy Injection': (1) MAFM augments the FM loss with λ(t)·(‖v_θ‖ − ‖x₁−x₀‖)² where λ(t)=λ₀(1−t) enforces magnitude constraints early but relaxes them late; (2) SSC applies a linearly annealed scale γ(t)=s_start·(1−t)+s_end·t (typically 1.1→1.0) to the velocity at inference, injecting kinetic energy at launch while preserving natural contraction at landing. On ImageNet-1k 256×256, SSC reduces FID from 13.68 to 7.58 at NFE=50, outperforming the 250-step baseline (FID 8.65) for a 5× speedup.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k 256×256, the training-free Scale Schedule Corrector (SSC) improves FID by 18.5%–23.7% across SiT model scales (e.g., 18.70→14.26 on SiT-XL/2), outperforming the training-based MAFM on FID while requiring zero retraining. On the fully converged SiT-XL/2 (7M steps), SSC reduces FID from 13.68 to 7.58 (44.6% improvement) at NFE=50, beating the 250-step baseline (FID 8.65) for a 5× speedup. On MS-COCO text-to-image generation, SSC reduces FID from 6.03 to 4.71 (~22% improvement) using the MMDiT+REPA backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the standard SiT Flow Matching baseline (Ma et al., ECCV 2024), SSC achieves consistent FID reductions of 18.5%–23.7% across all four model scales without any retraining, and notably outperforms the training-based MAFM on FID (e.g., 14.26 vs. 16.68 on SiT-XL/2), suggesting the integration lag is easier to correct at inference than to unlearn during training. Against REPA-enhanced SiT models (Yu et al., 2024), SSC reduces FID by 20.7%–28.4% at 512×512 resolution (e.g., 11.72→8.39 on SiT-XL/2) and by 15.6%–21.8% at 256×256, confirming the velocity deficit is scale-invariant. Against the MMDiT+REPA text-to-image baseline (Esser et al., ICML 2024) on MS-COCO, SSC reduces FID from 6.03 to 4.71 (~22%) at NFE=50, with consistent gains across multiple random seeds (mean 4.78 vs. 6.07, std 0.07). A notable finding is that SSC's FID gains come at a slight sFID cost relative to MAFM (e.g., 5.59 vs. 4.67 on SiT-XL/2), reflecting a structure-vs.-texture trade-off where inference-time correction excels at global geometry while training-time magnitude supervision better preserves local high-frequency detail.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Flow Matching framework (Lipman et al., 2023) and its scalable Transformer implementations (SiT, MMDiT), but departs from the dominant line of work on trajectory straightening (Rectified Flow, Consistency Models) and optimization balancing (Min-SNR, Logit-Normal). While those methods address trajectory curvature or gradient variance, this paper identifies an orthogonal and previously overlooked issue: a systematic velocity magnitude deficit arising from the MSE objective's averaging over crossing trajectories in high dimensions, formalized via Jensen's inequality. The proposed Scale Schedule Corrector (SSC) is positioned as complementary to, rather than a replacement for, classifier-free guidance, operating on the velocity field's kinetic energy rather than conditional strength.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: a one-line inference-time modification (SSC) yields a 5× sampling speedup with better FID than the 250-step baseline, with zero retraining cost, making it immediately adoptable across existing flow-matching pipelines. The conceptual insight that velocity contraction is harmful at the trajectory start (signal starvation) but beneficial at the end (implicit denoising) reframes how practitioners think about flow dynamics and opens questions about whether similar asymmetric corrections apply to other continuous generative paradigms. Limitations include dependence on high-dimensional orthogonality (potentially problematic in low-dimensional settings) and sensitivity in very high-CFG regimes, where the default injection scale may cause over-saturation.
