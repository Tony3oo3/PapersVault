## Problem

Vision-Language Models frequently generate self-reflective statements like 'let me check the figure again' during chain-of-thought reasoning, but it remains unknown whether these statements trigger genuine visual re-examination or are merely learned linguistic patterns without true visual grounding. This gap is critical for trustworthiness: if models claim to verify their reasoning against the image but do not actually re-attend to visual tokens, users in safety-critical domains may place unwarranted confidence in a verification process that is purely performative.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes a pair of visually similar but answer-divergent images (I_a, I_b) at identical resolutions, a question Q, and the model's prior reasoning chain R_a generated from I_a; the image is swapped mid-generation (full re-prefill with I_b) while R_a and a reflection prompt P are retained in the same assistant turn. Outputs include base accuracy (standard single-turn inference on I_b), probe accuracy (under the swap condition), performance degradation Δ = Acc_base − Acc_probe, and per-layer visual attention scores S_vis^(l)(t) averaged over image tokens across all heads at each decoding step. The VS-BENCH pairs are constrained to high CLIP similarity (0.95) and SSIM (0.86) with low LPIPS (0.14), ensuring the prior reasoning appears superficially plausible against the swapped image.

## Method

VISUALSWAP is a two-stage diagnostic protocol: Stage 1 generates reasoning R_a from (I_a, Q) via standard inference; Stage 2 replaces I_a with I_b, appends a reflection prompt P to R_a within the same assistant response, and continues generation, testing whether the model detects the visual discrepancy and revises its answer to A_b. VS-BENCH comprises 800 image pairs curated from MathVista, MathVerse, MathVision, and MMMU-Pro via a human-in-the-loop pipeline using Nano Banana Pro, where annotators identify answer-critical visual elements and generate targeted modifications that change the ground truth while preserving question validity. The mechanistic analysis reveals that self-reflective statements elicit only a marginal increase in visual token attention (Δ≈1.07 at layer 55 for Qwen3-VL-235B-Thinking), whereas an explicit multi-turn user instruction more than doubles this activation (Δ≈2.21), explaining why models 'say' they re-examine without actually 'looking.' A training-free causal intervention—scaling attention weights on image tokens by 2× during the reflection phase—recovers up to 18.2% probe accuracy, confirming that insufficient visual attention rather than capability loss drives the failure, and that the effect is most severe in Thinking models whose extended reasoning chains create stronger textual inertia.

## Results

Across 15 VLMs on VS-BENCH (800 image pairs from MathVista, MathVerse, MathVision, MMMU-Pro), all models fail to detect image swaps during self-reflection, with average performance degradation (Δ) ranging from 17.9% (Qwen3-VL-32B-Instruct) to 60.3% (ERNIE-4.5-VL-28B-A3B-Thinking). Thinking models are nearly 3× more vulnerable than their Instruct counterparts (e.g., Qwen3-VL-32B: 48.3% vs. 17.9% drop), and scaling provides no mitigation (Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B-Thinking drops 54.6% vs. 39.4% for the 8B variant). Attention analysis confirms the mechanism: self-reflective statements elicit only a marginal visual attention increase (Δ=1.07 at Layer 55 for 235B-Thinking), while explicit multi-turn user instructions more than double it (Δ=2.21), restoring accuracy from 34.1% to 85.4%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a diagnostic study rather than a method-vs-baseline comparison; the 'baselines' are the 15 VLMs being probed. The central finding is that no model family escapes the failure: Qwen3-VL, Kimi-VL, ERNIE-VL, and Qwen2.5-VL all show substantial degradation under VISUALSWAP. Within each family, Thinking variants consistently suffer 2–3× greater degradation than Instruct counterparts (e.g., ERNIE-4.5-VL: 60.3% vs. 34.3%; Qwen3-VL-32B: 48.3% vs. 17.9%), indicating that extended chain-of-thought amplifies textual inertia rather than improving visual grounding. Scaling within Qwen3-VL provides no relief: the 235B-A22B-Thinking model (Δ=54.6%) degrades more than the 8B-Thinking model (Δ=39.4%). The critical contrast is between self-generated reflection (Probe) and explicit multi-turn user instruction: the latter recovers Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B-Thinking from 34.1% to 85.4% (near its 88.8% baseline), proving the visual capability exists but is not autonomously engaged during continuous decoding. A 2× attention amplification on image tokens at the reflection point recovers +18.2% for the 8B-Thinking model, providing causal evidence that insufficient visual attention—not capability loss—drives the failure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new diagnostic ground at the intersection of VLM reasoning and interpretability. It builds on the recent wave of reasoning-enhanced VLMs (OpenVLThinker, VL-Rethinker, Qwen3-VL-Thinking) that elicit self-reflective chain-of-thought behaviors, and on existing visual reasoning benchmarks (MathVista, MathVerse, MathVision, MMMU-Pro) for data sourcing, but departs from all of them by asking a question none have addressed: whether self-reflective statements like 'let me check the figure again' trigger genuine visual re-attention or are merely learned linguistic patterns. The VISUALSWAP probing protocol and attention-level analysis are novel diagnostic tools that repurpose standard VLM inference to isolate the visual grounding component of reflection, rather than proposing a new architecture or training method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that thinking VLMs are nearly 3× more vulnerable to visual re-examination failure than their instruct counterparts directly challenges the assumption that extended reasoning improves reliability, with implications for safety-critical deployments (medical imaging, autonomous navigation) where users may trust a model's stated verification. The attention analysis and multi-turn recovery experiments establish that this is a failure of autonomous attentional control rather than visual capability, pointing toward concrete interventions (attention amplification, multi-turn verification in SFT/RL pipelines). Open questions include whether the failure persists in next-generation models with native visual attention mechanisms, whether the 2× attention amplification trick generalizes beyond Qwen3-VL, and how to train models to genuinely re-ground without external user intervention.
