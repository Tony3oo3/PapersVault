## Problem

Existing LLM-enhanced graph recommendation methods treat collaborative structural signals and LLM-derived semantic knowledge as separate or loosely coupled views, creating a supervisory gap where topological proximity and semantic relevance produce conflicting training signals. This conflict limits representation quality and introduces noise, particularly under data sparsity where structural neighborhoods diverge from semantic neighborhoods.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261387/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a bipartite user-item interaction graph, raw item attributes (title, release year), item poster images, and user historical interaction sequences. Item embeddings are constructed via element-wise additive fusion of a graph-convolution ID embedding, a Sentence-BERT-encoded LLM-augmented text embedding (Qwen-7B fills in missing attributes like director, region, language), and a CLIP-ViT image embedding. The output is a Top-K ranked item list per user, with the model also producing dense user and item embeddings that integrate all three modalities.

## Method

CCLRec introduces a structure-semantic consensus mining strategy that computes the intersection of a user's structural neighbors (derived from an item co-occurrence matrix G = R^T R with row-wise aggregation scores) and its semantically similar items (derived from mean-pooled LLM embeddings with cosine similarity), yielding high-confidence positive and negative pairs endorsed by both views. A consensus-driven contrastive loss then applies asymmetric weighting: λ₁ externally amplifies the log-likelihood of consensus positive pairs, while λ₂ internally strengthens the repulsion term for consensus negatives in the denominator. When the consensus set is empty, the loss gracefully degrades to unweighted dual-view joint supervision over the union of structural and semantic samples. The overall objective combines this contrastive loss with a standard BPR ranking loss, and the entire pipeline uses Qwen-7B offline for semantic attribute completion and user interest inference, with CLIP-ViT for visual features.

## Results

CCLRec achieves the best performance across all four metrics (Recall@10/20, NDCG@10/20) on both datasets. On the sparse Netflix dataset, CCLRec attains Recall@20 of 0.1154 and NDCG@20 of 0.0464, outperforming the strongest LLM-enhanced baseline CORONA (0.0938/0.0416) by approximately 23% in Recall@20. On the denser ML-1M dataset, CCLRec reaches Recall@20 of 0.2639 and NDCG@20 of 0.5105, surpassing CORONA (0.2565/0.5027) and LLMRec (0.2515/0.4970).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CCLRec outperforms all 10 baselines on every metric across both datasets. The closest competitor is CORONA (Chen et al., 2025), the most recent LLM-enhanced graph recommendation method: on Netflix, CCLRec improves Recall@20 by 2.16 percentage points (0.1154 vs. 0.0938, ~23% relative gain) and NDCG@20 by 0.48 pp; on ML-1M, the margins narrow to 0.74 pp in Recall@20 and 0.78 pp in NDCG@20. Among LLM-based methods, LLMRec (Wei et al., 2024) trails CCLRec by 3.25 pp in Netflix Recall@20 and 1.24 pp in ML-1M Recall@20. Multimodal baselines (LATTICE, MICRO, MMSSL) and KG-based CKE are consistently outperformed, with gaps of 4–7 pp in Recall@20 on Netflix. Classical GNN methods (NGCF, LightGCN) and CF baselines (MF-BPR, NFM) show the largest deficits, confirming that the consensus-driven contrastive mechanism provides substantial gains over purely structural or purely semantic approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

CCLRec sits at the intersection of GNN-based collaborative filtering (LightGCN, NGCF) and LLM-enhanced recommendation (LLMRec, CORONA), but departs from prior work by rejecting the common paradigm of treating graph structural signals and LLM-derived semantic knowledge as independent or loosely coupled views. Instead, it introduces a consensus mining strategy that computes the set intersection between structural co-occurrence neighbors and semantically similar items, then applies a weighted contrastive loss specifically on these dual-validated pairs. This builds on the multi-modal fusion framework of LLMRec (additive embedding combination with CLIP and Sentence-BERT) and the coarse-to-fine graph-LLM pipeline of CORONA, but replaces their independent optimization with an explicit agreement-based supervision mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The consensus-driven approach offers a principled way to resolve the supervisory conflict that arises when topological proximity and semantic relevance disagree, a problem that plagues most multi-view contrastive recommendation methods. However, the evaluation is limited to two movie-domain datasets (Netflix, ML-1M), and the consensus set is non-empty only 7% of the time on ML-1M, raising questions about robustness in domains with weaker structural-semantic alignment. The authors themselves flag the need for fine-grained alignment between LLM reasoning chains and graph propagation paths, as well as scalability to industrial-scale recommendation, as open directions.
