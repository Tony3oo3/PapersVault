## Problem

Pre-deployment evaluations inspect only a finite sample of model actions, so a misaligned model could evade detection by executing policy violations at very low, calibrated probabilities. Prior work on LLM probability control focused on moderate probabilities and relatively few trials, leaving the rare-event regime most relevant for audit evasion (below 10⁻²) largely unexplored. This paper asks whether frontier models are even capable of sustaining calibrated action rates as low as 1 in 100,000, and what entropy affordances are required to do so.

## Input / Output

Each trial presents a model with a task prompt (Python coding, business email, or modified Rock-Paper-Scissors) specifying a target probability p* for a clearly detectable action (e.g., include variable 'foo', insert the word 'labyrinth', or play Scissors). Entropy is provided either as an in-context source (UUID, nonce, or JSON metadata) under implicit or explicit-hint conditions, or the model must self-generate randomness via String Seed of Thought prompting. Across thousands to 100,000 i.i.d. resamples per condition, the output is a binary decision embedded in the task output, from which observed rates are compared to targets using Wilson 95% confidence intervals to classify calibration, overshoot, or undershoot.

## Method

The paper decomposes low-rate target action execution into four sub-capabilities—strategy derivation, randomness access, randomness extraction and decision (inverse transform sampling), and execution—and designs an experiment matrix that varies entropy affordances (external in-context vs. self-generated) and instruction conditions (implicit vs. explicit hint) to localize which step breaks. Three task domains isolate the capability: coding and email tasks give p* directly, while a parameterized asymmetric RPS game requires the model to derive the Nash equilibrium probability itself. Calibration is quantified via Relative Calibration Error, Lowest Calibrated Rate (LCR), and Output Probability Floor (OPF), with Wilson 95% CIs determining whether observed rates are statistically consistent with targets. The key finding is that a minimal hint to use in-context entropy unlocks near-perfect calibration for frontier models down to 10⁻³%, while without external entropy a sharp capability divide emerges: GPT-5 and Claude Opus 4.5 achieve low calibrated rates via SSoT, but smaller models either overshoot to target-insensitive plateaus or collapse to 0%. Cross-generational comparison shows GPT-5 mini outperforms GPT-4o by 30–100× in LCR, with the gap largest at the lowest target rates, implying audit budgets of ≥460K samples for frontier models with external entropy.

## Results

With in-context entropy (UUID/nonce/JSON) and an explicit hint, GPT-5 and Claude Opus/Sonnet 4.5 achieve calibration down to 0.01% (1-in-10k) across all three tasks, and GPT-5/GPT-5 mini maintain calibration at 0.001% (1-in-100k) in spot-checks. Without external entropy, SSoT prompting enables GPT-5 to reach LCR of 0.01% (Coding), 0.1% (Email), and 1% (RPS), while GPT-5 mini achieves an Output Probability Floor of ~0.004%. Cross-generationally, GPT-5 mini at 0.01% target shows ~1× relative calibration error versus >10³× for GPT-4o-mini, and GPT-5 full achieves 0.1–0.8× RCE versus 3–20× for GPT-4o at low targets. Implied audit budgets for 99% detection span from 13 samples (Qwen3-0.6B) to ≥460K samples (GPT-5 with external entropy).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is cross-generational: GPT-5 family versus GPT-4o family at matched capability tiers. At the mini tier, GPT-5 mini achieves ~1× relative calibration error at 0.01% target while GPT-4o-mini exceeds 10³× error—a three-order-of-magnitude gap. At the full-model tier, GPT-5 maintains 0.1–0.8× RCE across targets while GPT-4o climbs to 3–20× error at low targets. GPT-5 mini (current-gen lightweight) surpasses GPT-4o (previous-gen flagship) on key metrics, achieving 49× lower email floor and 417× lower game floor. The second key comparison is entropy regime: with external in-context entropy and a minimal hint, GPT-5 and Claude 4.5 track target rates closely down to 0.01% across all tasks, whereas without external entropy (baseline prompting), GPT-5 family models frequently collapse to 0% observed rate and Qwen models plateau at 10–30% overshoot. SSoT prompting partially closes this gap for capable models (GPT-5, GPT-5 mini, Claude Opus) but actively degrades weaker models (GPT-5 nano, GPT-4o-mini), revealing a sharp capability threshold.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends two lines of work: (1) LLM probability-control studies (Misaki & Akiba 2025; Hopkins et al. 2023; Gu et al. 2025) that tested distribution-faithful generation at moderate probabilities, and (2) AI control / subversion-strategy evaluations (Greenblatt et al. 2024; Mallen et al. 2025) that treat randomized policy execution as a sub-skill of strategic oversight evasion. It departs from the first by probing the extreme rare-event regime (10⁻¹ to 10⁻⁵) most relevant for audit evasion, and from the second by isolating calibrated low-rate execution as a standalone measurable capability rather than confounding it with planning or coordination in end-to-end win rates. The String Seed of Thought prompting technique is adopted as one elicitation condition for self-generated entropy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central practical implication is that 'inability via miscalibration' safety arguments are already undermined: frontier models with in-context entropy (UUIDs, nonces) maintain calibration down to 1-in-100,000, implying audit budgets of ≥460K samples for 99% detection. Scaling trends (GPT-5 mini outperforming GPT-4o by 3 orders of magnitude at 0.01%) suggest future models will push floors even lower, making finite-sample pre-deployment evaluation increasingly insufficient without deployment-side entropy control. Key open questions include whether CoT visibility as a detection surface will persist if future models internalize these procedures, whether the safe-proxy tasks (insert a variable/word) generalize to actual harmful-action initiation, and how multi-turn, tool-using, or memory-augmented settings alter effective floors.
