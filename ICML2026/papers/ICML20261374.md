## Problem

Experimentally measured mutation effects (e.g., deep mutational scanning) remain accurate but extremely sparse, limiting scalability of mutation effect models. While physics-based energy functions, protein language models, and inverse folding models can generate mutation signals at scale, they encode fundamentally different inductive biases and operate on incompatible semantic scales, so naively merging their outputs collapses informative cross-source disagreement into an artificial consensus. The field also lacks a systematic, structure-aligned PDB-wide augmentation resource that unifies these heterogeneous signals at the residue level.

## Input / Output

The student model receives a protein sequence and a target residue position, and outputs a 20-dimensional mutation preference score vector whose entries are used only for relative comparison among candidate substitutions at that position (not calibrated probabilities or physical energies). Training supervision comes from the MutAtlas dataset: 15.9M residue positions across 86,479 PDB chains, where each position carries aligned 20-dim mutation logits from FoldX (converted from ΔΔG energy differences), ESM2, and ESM-IF, yielding over 300M single-site mutation evaluations. All signals are restricted to the canonical 20-amino-acid alphabet and computed on the same structure-aligned residue sequence to guarantee positional correspondence across sources.

## Method

The paper proposes an unsupervised multi-source mutation preference distillation framework that trains an ESM2-650M student without any experimental mutation labels. The key mechanism operates on within-source pairwise preferences: for each residue position and amino-acid pair, source-normalized margins (divided by each source's mean absolute margin) provide scale-invariant supervision, and an agreement weight (fraction of sources supporting the same direction) modulates supervision strength via a soft-consensus logistic ranking loss. A disagreement-aware margin regularization term, weighted by the variance of source-normalized margins across sources, penalizes the student from becoming overconfident on pairs where reference sources strongly conflict. The final objective combines the agreement-weighted consensus loss with this disagreement penalty (λ=0.2 optimal), and the framework is validated on ProteinGym where it achieves the best zero-shot Spearman correlation (0.498) among all evaluated baselines and ablation variants.

## Results

On ProteinGym substitution benchmarks (201 datasets, macro-averaged Spearman rank correlation), the disagreement-aware distillation method (λ=0.2) achieves the best overall score of 0.498, outperforming the strongest sequence-only baseline VespaG (0.487) by 0.011 and the individual reference models ESM-IF (0.459) and FoldX (0.424) by 0.039 and 0.074 respectively. The method also leads on Binding (0.389), Expression (0.495), and Stability (0.625), while remaining competitive on Activity (0.467) and Fitness (0.414). Ablation shows a clear progression from hard consensus (0.468) → soft consensus (0.475) → disagreement-aware (0.498), confirming the value of graded agreement modeling and explicit cross-source disagreement regularization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The disagreement-aware method (0.498 overall) outperforms all five external baselines. Against the strongest sequence-only baseline VespaG (0.487), it gains +0.011 overall but loses on Activity (0.467 vs. 0.496) and Fitness (0.414 vs. 0.465), while winning on Binding (+0.026), Expression (+0.034), and Stability (+0.092). Against GEMME (0.480), the gain is +0.018 overall with similar per-task trade-offs. The individual reference models ESM-IF (0.459) and FoldX (0.424) are surpassed by +0.039 and +0.074 respectively, confirming that multi-source integration adds value beyond any single source. ESM2-650M (0.446), the student backbone before fine-tuning, improves by +0.052, demonstrating the benefit of the distillation objective. Notably, the ESM-IF-only ablation (0.480) falls short of the full method (0.498), indicating that physics-based FoldX signals contribute complementary information not captured by sequence- or structure-derived sources alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of protein mutation effect prediction (FoldX, ESM2, ESM-IF, ProteinMPNN) and multi-source learning (multi-teacher distillation, weak supervision, noisy-label learning). It departs from the standard multi-teacher assumption that heterogeneous sources provide noisy but compatible observations of a shared target; instead, the authors argue and empirically demonstrate that physics-based, sequence-based, and structure-conditioned models encode fundamentally different inductive biases operating on incompatible semantic scales. The disagreement-aware distillation objective—using agreement-weighted soft consensus plus variance-based margin regularization—is a novel formulation that repurposes pairwise preference learning from ranking literature into a protein-specific multi-source setting, while the PDB-wide mutation atlas is a new large-scale resource with no direct prior equivalent.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The PDB-wide atlas (15.9M residue positions, 86K chains, 300M+ single-site evaluations) is a practically important resource that exceeds the largest DMS collections by ~2 orders of magnitude in chain coverage, enabling systematic cross-model analysis at scale. The finding that cross-source disagreement is systematic and persists even at low-entropy positions challenges the implicit assumption in prior multi-source protein work that disagreement is merely noise to be averaged away. Open questions include whether the framework generalizes to more than three sources or to multi-site mutations, whether the PDB structural coverage introduces selection biases relative to natural sequence space, and whether the unsupervised approach can close the gap to fully supervised methods when experimental labels are available.
