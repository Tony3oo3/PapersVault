## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting from sparse views produces severe geometric distortions and incomplete reconstructions in under-observed regions. While recent diffusion-based methods attempt to inpaint or correct these artifacts, they fail to maintain faithful alignment with the input observations, especially under large viewpoint extrapolation, because they lack mechanisms to deeply exploit both the semantic content and 3D geometric structure of the reference views.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265549/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes K sparse-view RGB images with known camera poses and an initial low-quality 3DGS representation built from those views. During the iterative restoration loop, novel views are rendered along trajectories between two selected reference views at 480×720 resolution (49-frame sequences); the reference views are additionally processed by DINOv2 (resized to 350×518) and VGGT to extract 2D semantic and 3D geometric token sequences. The output is a progressively refined 3DGS representation whose rendered novel views are artifact-free and consistent with the input observations.

## Method

GSFixer builds a reference-guided video restoration model on top of CogVideoX-5B-I2V (a DiT-based video diffusion model), trained on paired artifact 3DGS renders and clean ground-truth frames. The key architectural contribution is injecting reference-view conditioning: DINOv2 extracts 2D semantic tokens and VGGT extracts 3D geometric tokens from the two reference views; these are projected to a common dimension, summed into fusion tokens, and injected into every DiT block via an added cross-attention layer where view tokens serve as queries and fusion tokens as keys/values. A reference-guided trajectory sampling strategy (RGT) alternates interpolation between reference views and sampling along a spherical path to balance angular coverage with restoration quality. The full pipeline iterates: render artifact novel views along RGT trajectories → restore them with the frozen diffusion model → add the fixed views to the 3DGS training set with a combined reconstruction loss (L1+SSIM on input views) and generative loss (L1+SSIM on fixed views, with annealed weight), progressively improving the 3DGS representation over two fix-and-distill cycles.

## Results

On the Mip-NeRF 360 dataset (out-of-domain), GSFixer achieves 15.61/17.27/18.63 dB PSNR under 3/6/9-view settings, outperforming the strongest generative baseline GenFusion (15.03/16.90/18.29 dB) and the NeRF-based ReconFusion (15.50/16.93/18.19 dB). On the in-domain DL3DV-Benchmark, GSFixer reaches 16.21 dB PSNR at 3 views, a 3.55 dB improvement over vanilla 3DGS (13.72 dB). On the proposed DL3DV-Res artifact restoration benchmark, GSFixer achieves 16.72 dB PSNR and 0.399 LPIPS, surpassing GenFusion by +2.16 dB PSNR and −0.087 LPIPS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GSFixer outperforms all 10 baselines across every dataset and view-count setting. Against the closest generative 3DGS baselines, GSFixer beats GenFusion by +0.58 dB PSNR (3-view) and +0.34 dB (9-view) on Mip-NeRF 360, and by +1.57 dB (3-view) on DL3DV-Benchmark; it surpasses Difix3D+ by a wider margin of +1.69 dB (3-view) on Mip-NeRF 360. Notably, GSFixer also exceeds the NeRF-based ReconFusion (15.50 dB) despite 3DGS being generally more prone to overfitting under sparse views. On the in-domain DL3DV-Benchmark, GSFixer leads GenFusion by +1.57 dB PSNR at 3 views and Difix3D+ by +1.14 dB. On the artifact restoration benchmark (DL3DV-Res), GSFixer improves over GenFusion by +2.16 dB PSNR and reduces LPIPS from 0.486 to 0.399, while also achieving higher VBench consistency scores (I2V_SC 0.9553 vs. 0.8916 for GenFusion), indicating that baselines actually degrade view consistency after their 'restoration.' Regularization-only methods (Zip-NeRF, FreeNeRF, SimpleNeRF, FSGS) and non-generative baselines (3DGS, 2DGS, ZeroNVS) trail GSFixer by 2–3 dB PSNR in the sparse 3-view regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

GSFixer extends the generative sparse-view NVS line initiated by ReconFusion and continued by 3DGS-Enhancer, Difix3D+, and GenFusion, which all use diffusion priors to correct artifacts in novel views. Its key departure is the explicit dual conditioning of a DiT-based video diffusion model (CogVideoX) on both 2D semantic tokens (DINOv2) and 3D geometric tokens (VGGT) extracted from reference views, rather than treating artifact and reference frames symmetrically or relying on image-level diffusion. The reference-guided trajectory sampling strategy further differentiates it by balancing angular coverage and per-frame restoration quality in the iterative fix-and-distill loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in sparse-view 3DGS pipelines where severe artifacts in unobserved regions degrade downstream applications (VR, autonomous driving, robotics). The introduction of DL3DV-Res fills a notable evaluation gap for 3DGS artifact restoration, and the finding that existing generative baselines (Difix3D+, GenFusion) actually *reduce* subject/background consistency scores relative to the original artifact frames highlights a previously underappreciated failure mode. Open questions include whether the 50-step denoising requirement can be reduced via single-step or few-step diffusion, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the DL3DV training distribution to truly unbounded or dynamic scenes.
