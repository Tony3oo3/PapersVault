## Problem

Existing analyses of diffusion model sampling bound the effect of score estimation error by crude terms of order T·ε₀², which obscure how step-size allocation across the time horizon affects final sample quality and fail to identify which regions of the reverse trajectory contribute most critically. This paper develops a non-asymptotic, stepwise analysis of how local score errors propagate and accumulate through the reverse dynamics, explaining the observed sensitivity of diffusion samplers to noise schedules.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261510/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The analysis takes as input a time-resolved score error profile (Lipschitz constants L_t and slice-wise L² bounds ε_t, both assumed non-increasing in t), a discretization schedule {h_j}, and the initial data distribution p_0. It produces a Wasserstein-2 bound between the terminal law of the perturbed sampler and the oracle sampler, structured as a sum over time steps where each local error ε_{τ_i} is weighted by a schedule-dependent amplification factor g_i that encodes the curvature of the forward marginals along the remaining reverse trajectory. The bound is explicitly decomposable into per-step contributions, enabling stepwise error density visualization and schedule comparison.

## Method

The core mechanism is a unified gain recursion built on a pathwise Hessian average H_k defined along the line segment connecting synchronously coupled perturbed and oracle trajectories. This yields a one-step error bound (Lemma 3.4) separating multiplicative amplification (governed by the operator norm of α_j I + β_j H_j + β_j L_{τ_j}) from additive injection of local score error. For Gaussian p_0, the Hessian is closed-form (H_k = −Σ_{τ_k}^{−1}), giving sharp explicit W₂ bounds; for Gaussian mixtures, two computable surrogate Hessians (mixture-weighted average and dominant-component) replace the intractable pathwise Hessian, with regime-adapted choices yielding O(δ) or O(e^{−κ}) correction terms in small- or large-separation settings. For general p_0, a distribution-agnostic bound uses the forward smoothing scale σ(τ) as a curvature envelope, which is refined under a globally Lipschitz-score assumption to avoid the worst-case 1/τ blow-up near the data end. The framework applies uniformly to both VP and VE diffusions and extends to probability-flow ODE discretizations with only modified amplification coefficients.

## Results

The paper's primary contributions are non-asymptotic Wasserstein error bounds (Theorems 3.5, 3.7, 3.12) that decompose terminal W₂ error into schedule-dependent amplification factors gᵢ and local score-error magnitudes ε_{τᵢ}. In the controlled 2D Gaussian-mixture experiment, the Uniform-logSNR schedule achieves the best sliced W₂ distance (0.545±0.182) and lowest predicted error accumulation (1.160±0.294), despite the Quadratic schedule having the smallest average score-estimation L₂ error (0.113±0.024). This demonstrates that final sampling quality is governed not merely by average score error but by how errors are distributed across time and amplified through the reverse dynamics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical analysis paper without direct method-to-method baselines; the empirical section compares four discretization schedules (Linear, Quadratic, Cosine, Uniform-logSNR) rather than competing samplers. The key finding is that the Quadratic schedule, despite achieving the lowest average score-estimation error (0.113), produces a worse final sliced W₂ (0.677) than Uniform-logSNR (0.545), because its step-size allocation concentrates larger steps in the data-end region where score errors are most amplified. The Cosine schedule performs worst on both predicted accumulation (2.480) and sliced W₂ (0.736), consistent with its larger average score error (0.175). The predicted accumulations from the theoretical bound (Eq. 18) rank the schedules in the same order as the empirical sliced W₂ values, validating the qualitative role of the propagation formula. The authors explicitly note these are controlled checks of qualitative trends, not claims of schedule optimality, and that practical performance depends on data distribution, training objective, and evaluation metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of non-asymptotic error analysis for diffusion model sampling (Chen et al. 2023; Benton et al. 2024; De Bortoli 2022), which has primarily focused on discretization error while treating score estimation error as a uniform L² bound of order T·ε₀². The key departure is a stepwise, time-resolved gain recursion built on pathwise Hessian averages that tracks how local score errors are amplified or damped along the reverse trajectory, yielding Wasserstein bounds that depend explicitly on the discretization schedule and the curvature of the forward marginals. The framework is instantiated for the exponential integrator (Zhang & Chen 2023) but applies to a broad class of first-order samplers, and covers both VP/VE SDEs and the probability-flow ODE.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The results provide a concrete theoretical mechanism explaining the empirically observed sensitivity of diffusion sampling to step-size allocation (Karras et al. 2022; Lu et al. 2022; Sabour et al. 2024): errors injected near the data end (small t) are amplified by the local curvature of the score field, while those near the noise end are damped. This justifies data-end-refining schedules and connects naturally to the log-SNR parameterization used in DPM-Solver. However, the experimental validation is limited to a 2D symmetric Gaussian mixture with four schedules, and the general-distribution bound (Theorem 3.12) is necessarily conservative due to the 1/σ²(τ) curvature blow-up as τ→0. Open questions include extending the analysis to the training stage (how noise schedules shape the time profile of ε_t and L_t), higher-order samplers, and singular data-manifold regimes where the score becomes unbounded.
