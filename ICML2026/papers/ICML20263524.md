## Problem

Autoregressive generative models for clinical time-series forecasting typically operate at a single fixed temporal resolution, yet different clinical endpoints exhibit vastly different temporal dynamics (e.g., serum potassium shifts in minutes while HbA1c evolves over weeks). The paper demonstrates that zero-shot forecast accuracy systematically varies with the chosen resolution and that no single resolution dominates across tasks and horizons, creating a fundamental mismatch between generative granularity and endpoint timescale.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263524/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a patient's irregularly sampled EHR history (diagnoses, procedures, medications, and decile-binned laboratory values) tokenized at multiple temporal resolutions, along with a task label identifying the target binary outcome and its prediction horizon H_k. It produces a single horizon risk probability per patient, estimated as the fraction of S=50 Monte Carlo rollouts in which the outcome occurs within H_k. The fused MoRGen predictor additionally requires a small labeled validation split to fit per-task/per-horizon convex mixture weights, making the final predictor a low-capacity supervised fusion over otherwise zero-shot generative experts.

## Method

MoRGen trains a pool of independently parameterized decoder-only autoregressive experts, each operating at a different fixed temporal resolution (minute, day, week, month, quarter, semi-annual). Fine-grained experts generate alternating time-gap and observation tokens at minute-level granularity, while coarse experts partition the timeline into fixed-length windows, encode each window as a binary vocabulary-presence vector, compress it into a discrete token via a pre-fitted Bernoulli mixture model (K components), and autoregressively generate the resulting window-token sequence. At inference, each expert produces a horizon risk via Monte Carlo rollouts, and MoRGen fuses these predictions as a convex combination whose weights are learned per dataset, task, and horizon by minimizing BCE on a validation split using the SLSQP solver. The key empirical insight driving the design is that coarser resolutions systematically improve calibration and sharpness (up to 90.5% of tasks) while finer resolutions preserve discriminative ranking, so the mixture can upweight whichever resolution best suits each endpoint-horizon pair.

## Results

Across three datasets (MIMIC-IV, Hospital A, EHRSHOT) and seven clinical tasks at both 30-day and 2-year horizons, MoRGen achieves statistically significant AUROC gains over the minute-level autoregressive baseline in all 42 task-horizon-dataset settings (100% win rate). The largest improvements appear on mortality: e.g., 30-day death AUROC rises from 70.27→89.06 on MIMIC, 69.13→81.67 on Hospital A, and 49.98→81.46 on EHRSHOT. Gains are especially pronounced in the smaller-data regime (EHRSHOT, 5,717 patients), where multiple tasks see 10–15 AUROC point improvements. MoRGen also improves calibration error for 85.7% of tasks at 30 days and 81.0% at 2 years, while increasing prediction sharpness (confidence) for the majority of tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is a fine-grained (minute-level) autoregressive generative forecaster that produces one token per minute of post-discharge time. MoRGen outperforms this baseline in all 42 evaluated task-horizon-dataset settings (100% win rate), with the most dramatic gains on mortality (up to ~31 AUROC points on EHRSHOT 30-day death). The paper also compares against ETHOS (Renc et al., 2024), a peer-reviewed autoregressive EHR trajectory generator. ETHOS is a strong comparator, beating the minute-level baseline in 30 of 42 settings, but MoRGen still outperforms ETHOS in 27 of 42 settings. Notably, adding ETHOS as an additional expert within the MoRGen mixture (ETHOS+MoRGen) further improves robustness, outperforming ETHOS alone in 40 of 42 settings, demonstrating that MoRGen functions as a general late-fusion framework not tied to a specific autoregressive backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the autoregressive EHR trajectory generation paradigm established by ETHOS and AReS, which use Monte Carlo rollouts from a fine-grained event-stream model for zero-shot risk forecasting. It departs from both the fine-grained event-stream line (ETHOS, AReS, Event Stream GPT) and the encounter/visit-level coarsening line (CEHR-BERT, Hi-BEHRT, TransformEHR) by explicitly sweeping fixed temporal resolutions (day through semi-annual) and fusing across them via a low-capacity convex mixture. The key conceptual departure is treating temporal resolution as a tunable, task-dependent hyperparameter rather than a fixed architectural choice, and showing that no single resolution dominates across heterogeneous clinical endpoints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that temporal resolution is task- and horizon-dependent, and that a simple convex fusion of resolution-specific experts yields consistent AUROC gains (especially dramatic for mortality, where baseline AUROC is near random at ~50), provides a practical and low-cost recipe for improving clinical risk forecasting without retraining a single monolithic model. Limitations include the reliance on labeled validation data for mixture weights (not fully zero-shot), the absence of censoring-aware evaluation, the use of binary presence vectors that discard within-window event counts, and restriction to the post-discharge setting. Open questions include whether per-patient adaptive mixture weights, hierarchical multi-timescale architectures, or diffusion-forcing-style generation could further improve long-horizon forecasting, and whether the approach transfers to inpatient settings where fine-grained temporal structure may be more critical.
