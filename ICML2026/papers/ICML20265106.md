## Problem

Retrieval-based model editing for medical LLMs faces two critical gaps: (1) textually similar but factually distinct medical entries cause representation overlap in the retrieval space, leading to inaccurate knowledge matching; and (2) existing medical editing benchmarks and methods are limited to single-edit settings, while real-world clinical updates (revised guidelines, new drug interactions) require simultaneous batch edits. Parameter-modifying locate-then-edit methods further degrade locality, which is unacceptable in safety-critical medical applications.

## Input / Output

At inference, a medical question (efficacy, generality, or locality query) is tokenized by a BERT-style encoder (RoBERTa or BioBERT) and projected through a shared MLP to produce a query vector. This vector is matched against stored key vectors (derived from subject-relation pairs only, excluding the object) in an external knowledge base; if the query-key dot product exceeds a learned prototype threshold, the corresponding continuous prompt (3 tokens for MedCF++, 8 for MedVersa) is prepended to the query embedding sequence before feeding the frozen LLM. If no threshold is exceeded, the model responds without any prompt, preserving original behavior.

## Method

MedREK introduces two components: (1) a shared query-key MLP with residual connections that encodes both incoming queries and knowledge keys into a unified representation space, deliberately constructing keys from only the (subject, relation) pair to avoid object-induced retrieval noise; and (2) an attention-based prompt encoder that projects a knowledge representation into T query vectors and a single key/value pair, then applies multi-head attention so each prompt token adaptively gates different features of the shared knowledge vector. Training combines an InfoNCE contrastive loss (pulling query representations toward their correct knowledge keys and away from a learned prototype) with a supervised editing loss that jointly optimizes efficacy (next-token accuracy on edited targets), generality (accuracy on rephrased prompts), and locality (KL divergence between original and edited model outputs on unrelated questions). A trainable knowledge prototype vector acts as a dynamic retrieval threshold, enabling the model to abstain from retrieval on unrelated queries and thereby preserving locality without any parameter modification to the base LLM.

## Results

On MedCF++ with Meditron-7B, MedREK achieves an average score of 89.33 in single-editing and 88.30 in 100-edit batch-editing, outperforming the strongest retrieval-based baseline RECIPE (84.11 and 71.83 respectively) by 5.2 and 16.5 points. On MedVersa, MedREK reaches 86.24 (single) and 85.96 (100-edit), again surpassing RECIPE (78.35 and 70.91). The key advantage is in Locality: MedREK maintains ~97–99% across all Locality sub-metrics even at 100 edits, while RECIPE drops to ~72–80% and parameter-modifying methods like MEMIT fall to ~77–88%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MedREK outperforms all seven baselines on the Average metric across both datasets and both editing settings. Against the closest retrieval-based competitor RECIPE, MedREK gains +5.2 Avg on MedCF++ single-edit (89.33 vs 84.11) and a dramatic +16.5 Avg on 100-edit batch (88.30 vs 71.83), driven primarily by Locality (97.68 vs 74.13) and Generality (79.88 vs 70.74). Against parameter-modifying MEMIT, MedREK wins on Generality (+13.5 in single, +13.5 in 100-edit) and Locality (+7.4 single, +13.8 batch) while MEMIT retains a marginal Efficacy edge in single-edit (79.06 vs 78.50). MedLaSA, the prior medical-specific method, shows severe Locality degradation (81.20 on MedCF++ single, absent from batch experiments entirely), confirming the paper's argument that locate-then-edit is unsuitable for medical batch updates. MEND and MALMEN are far behind on all metrics, with MALMEN's Locality collapsing to ~22–35% due to its meta-learning parameter updates. On MedVersa, MedREK's advantage over RECIPE is even larger in batch (85.96 vs 70.91 Avg), highlighting that the shared query-key MLP is critical when the knowledge space is broader and more diverse.

## Relation to Prior Work

MedREK builds directly on RECIPE (Chen et al., 2024b), a retrieval-based editing framework that stores knowledge as continuous prompts in an external memory, but replaces RECIPE's separate query and key encoders with a shared query-key MLP and swaps its FFN-based prompt generation for an attention-based prompt encoder. It departs from the dominant locate-then-edit paradigm (ROME, MEMIT, MedLaSA) that modifies model parameters, arguing that parameter edits harm locality in safety-critical medical settings. The paper also extends the medical knowledge editing line initiated by MedLaSA (Xu et al., 2024) by moving from single-edit to batch-edit evaluation and from parameter-based to retrieval-based strategies, while introducing MedVersa as a broader-coverage benchmark to support this shift.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first validated retrieval-based solution for batch knowledge editing in medical LLMs, addressing a practical gap where real-world clinical updates (revised guidelines, new drug interactions) inherently involve multiple simultaneous edits. The MedVersa benchmark fills a notable coverage gap over prior medical editing datasets, which were dominated by pharmacology. However, the evaluation is limited to two 7–8B medical LLMs and a relatively small benchmark, leaving open whether the approach scales to larger models or generalizes beyond the medical domain. The reliance on a BERT-style encoder for retrieval, while effective, introduces an architectural dependency that may complicate integration with decoder-only model families.
