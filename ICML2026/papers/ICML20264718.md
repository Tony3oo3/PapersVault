## Problem

Existing diffusion model unlearning methods rely on heuristic surrogate objectives (e.g., mapping forget-concept noise to a neutral target) that create a fundamental mismatch with the true unlearning goal, and they preserve utility only through surface-level constraints (parameter distance, image similarity) that ignore the intrinsic score-based generative dynamics, ultimately causing catastrophic forgetting on retained concepts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264718/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained Stable Diffusion checkpoint (SD v1.5 for object/style tasks, SD v1.4 for NSFW), a forget set of concepts, a retain set, and a task-specific discriminative classifier (ViT-Large for styles, ResNet-101 for objects, Qwen2.5-VL for NSFW). It fine-tunes only the attention layers of the UNet using 30-step DDIM trajectories with bfloat16 mixed precision, producing an unlearned model that suppresses target concepts while maintaining generation quality on all other prompts. Evaluation uses 100-step DDIM inference averaged over three seeds.

## Method

POSDA reformulates unlearning as a PPO-based reinforcement learning problem where the iterative denoising process is a finite-horizon MDP and the diffusion model is the policy. A dynamic anchor-based reward—constructed from a task-specific classifier's posterior probabilities over the top-k nearest semantic neighbors—directly quantifies the unlearning goal, replacing handcrafted surrogate objectives. The policy is updated via importance-weighted policy gradient with PPO clipping and prompt-wise advantage normalization. To prevent catastrophic forgetting, a score-level distribution alignment term minimizes the KL divergence between the unlearned and original model's score functions on retain concepts, computed as the difference in noise predictions (via Proposition 3.1) with gradients backpropagated through the full deterministic DDIM sampling trajectory. The two objectives are combined with a tunable regularization weight β.

## Results

On the UnlearnCanvas benchmark, POSDA achieves the highest average score (Avg.) across all six unlearning concepts: 97.77 (Van Gogh), 99.21 (Abstractionism), 98.67 (Sketch), 98.89 (Architectures), 97.78 (Dogs), and 93.59 (Human), outperforming the strongest baseline SAeUron by 2.9–35.6 points depending on the concept. On the I2P NSFW benchmark, POSDA attains the lowest Nudity Exposure Rate (NER-0.5 = 5.0%, NER-0.7 = 1.1%) and the highest CLIP score (26.73) while maintaining a competitive FID of 52.76, with only 63 total detected exposed body parts versus 443 for the original SD v1.4.

## Baselines & Comparisons

POSDA achieves the highest Avg. score on all six UnlearnCanvas concepts, with the largest margins over SAeUron on object tasks (e.g., +25.6 on Architectures, +34.5 on Human) where SAeUron's sparse autoencoder approach degrades severely. Among style unlearning baselines, SAeUron is the closest competitor (94.89–96.99 Avg.) but still trails POSDA by 2.9–3.0 points; EDiff and ESD are competitive on styles (91–92 Avg.) but collapse on objects (69–88 Avg.). FMN fails catastrophically on Sketch (UA = 0%) and yields the worst FID (103.39 on Van Gogh), while SEOT is inapplicable to the I2P NSFW benchmark. On the I2P NSFW task, POSDA (NER-0.5 = 5.0%, Total detections = 63) marginally outperforms EDiff (5.1%, 65) and substantially outperforms SalUn (6.9%, 83) and MACE (10.2%, 148), while also achieving the highest CLIP score (26.73 vs. 26.69 for EDiff and 25.48 for SalUn), indicating superior utility preservation alongside erasure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DDPO formulation (Black et al., 2024) that casts diffusion denoising as a finite-horizon MDP, repurposing the RL policy-gradient machinery for the unlearning objective rather than for reward maximization on general prompts. It departs from the dominant line of unlearning work (ESD, FMN, SEOT, MACE, etc.) that relies on handcrafted surrogate objectives—mapping forget-concept noise to a neutral target, editing cross-attention, or pruning neurons—by instead defining a task-specific classifier-based reward that directly quantifies erasure efficacy. The score-level distribution alignment component extends the score-matching literature (Song & Ermon, 2019) into a regularization role, replacing the surface-level parameter-distance or image-similarity constraints used by prior methods with a principled KL objective over noisy marginals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

POSDA provides a more principled and unified framework for concept unlearning, and its consistent SOTA across object, style, and NSFW benchmarks makes it a strong default choice for practitioners needing to erase specific concepts from Stable Diffusion. However, the method requires training a task-specific classifier as a reward model, adds RL sampling overhead (full DDIM rollouts per step), and is only validated on SD v1.4/v1.5—open questions include whether the approach transfers to larger architectures (SDXL, Flux), whether the classifier-based reward generalizes to open-ended or compositional concepts, and whether the unlearning is robust against adversarial recovery attacks (a concern the paper acknowledges but does not test). The reliance on a frozen reference policy and prompt-specific reward buffers also raises scalability questions for multi-concept or continual unlearning settings.
