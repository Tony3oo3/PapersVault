## Problem

Clustering functional data in the presence of phase variation is difficult because temporal misalignment obscures intrinsic shape differences. Existing approaches either decouple registration from clustering (causing error propagation and template bias) or impose restrictive parametric assumptions on warping functions (e.g., simple shifts or Dirichlet process priors), limiting scalability and expressiveness. No prior method provides a fully unsupervised, end-to-end deep learning framework that jointly learns smooth diffeomorphic warps and cluster assignments without distributional assumptions.

## Input / Output

The method takes univariate or multivariate functional curves sampled at T time points (x_i ∈ R^{d×T}) and produces three outputs: per-sample diffeomorphic warping functions γ_i(t) guaranteed strictly monotone by construction, soft cluster assignment probabilities p_ij over C clusters, and phase-aligned curves x̃_i(t) = x_i(γ_i(t)). The warping is computed via 1D linear interpolation of the ODE solution, and clustering operates on truncated Fourier coefficient vectors (K=10 basis functions) of the aligned curves, which filters high-frequency noise while preserving shape structure.

## Method

NeuralFLoC integrates three modules trained end-to-end: a 1D-CNN encoder maps raw curves to C-dimensional latent vectors that initialize cluster-specific Neural ODE flows; the ODE warper solves an IVP with a Softplus-activated MLP velocity field to produce smooth, strictly monotone warping functions, and the instance-specific warp is a convex mixture of cluster-specific flows weighted by current assignment probabilities; a Fourier-based soft-assignment layer computes cluster probabilities via a Student's t-kernel on aligned-curve coefficients, feeding back into the warping mixture. The joint objective combines a cluster-conditional SRVF registration loss (weighted Karcher mean per cluster, avoiding a single global template) with a self-paced KL clustering loss, optimized via Adam with adjoint-method gradients through the ODE (O(1) memory). The key insight is that making the warping a continuous diffeomorphic flow parameterized by Neural ODEs—rather than a discrete or parametric transform—guarantees invertibility by construction, enables stable joint optimization even when true warps are near-identity, and admits universal approximation and asymptotic consistency guarantees.

## Results

NeuralFLoC achieves the best registration (ATV) and clustering (ACC, NMI) across all five dataset configurations. On the Shapes dataset, it attains ATV=8.1, ACC=0.937, NMI=0.651; on Wave (d=3), ATV=8.5, ACC=0.992, NMI=0.942; and on Symbols (3 classes), ATV=1.6, ACC=0.951, NMI=0.835. The method also scales to ~70k samples (200× the original Symbols dataset) with near-identical performance (ATV=3.2, ACC=0.942, NMI=0.683), outperforming all baselines at scale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeuralFLoC outperforms all eight baseline families on every dataset and metric. Against the sequential SrvfRegNet pipelines, the gap is most dramatic on Shapes (ATV 8.1 vs. 23.2; ACC 0.937 vs. best 0.918 for K-Graph) and Wave(d=3) (ATV 8.5 vs. 13.5; ACC 0.992 vs. 0.971 for RandomNet), confirming that joint optimization avoids error propagation from a fixed registration step. Among the classical joint methods, SRC (Zeng et al., 2019) is the strongest competitor on Shapes (ATV 18.5, ACC 0.807) but still trails NeuralFLoC by 10.4 ATV points and 13 ACC points; BSRC (Wu & Hitchcock, 2016) and JPCCA (Gaffney & Smyth, 2004) are substantially weaker, with JPCCA particularly poor on Wave(d=1) (ATV 18.1 vs. 4.1) and Wave(d=3) (ATV 46.7 vs. 8.5). The deep clustering baselines FAE (Wu et al., 2024) and RandomNet (Li et al., 2024) fail to capture phase structure when paired with SrvfRegNet, yielding near-chance NMI on Shapes (0.000 and 0.182 respectively) versus 0.651 for NeuralFLoC. K-Graph (Boniol et al., 2025) is the closest sequential competitor on Shapes (ACC 0.918, NMI 0.632) but still lags by 1.9 ACC and 1.9 NMI points, and it fails entirely at the 70k-sample scale where NeuralFLoC maintains ACC=0.942.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate threads: Neural ODEs for continuous-time dynamics (Chen et al., 2018), the SRVF/Fisher-Rao geometric framework for elastic curve registration (Srivastava et al., 2011), and the self-paced soft-assignment loss from Deep Embedded Clustering (Xie et al., 2016). Prior joint registration-clustering methods (JPCCA, BSRC, SRC) rely on parametric warping assumptions or expensive Bayesian inference, while recent deep registration networks like SrvfRegNet remain decoupled from downstream clustering. NeuralFLoC departs from both by using a Neural ODE to parameterize diffeomorphic warps as a mixture of cluster-specific flows, making the registration geometry-aware (via SRVF) and the clustering shape-aware (via Fourier spectral domain) in a single differentiable pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first fully unsupervised, end-to-end deep learning framework for simultaneous functional registration and clustering, with theoretical guarantees (universal approximation and asymptotic consistency) that are rare in this area. The demonstrated robustness to missing data, irregular sampling, and noise, combined with linear scaling in sample size, makes it practically relevant for large-scale functional data (e.g., biomedical signals, motion capture). Open questions include performance on trajectories with sharp discontinuities or high-frequency volatility where smoothness assumptions break, the lack of uncertainty quantification for learned warps, and whether the framework extends naturally to non-stationary or multi-scale phase variation.
