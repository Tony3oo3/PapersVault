## Problem

Unsupervised domain transfer via distribution matching is fundamentally ill-posed: measure-preserving automorphisms (MPAs) preserve marginals while arbitrarily altering cross-domain correspondences, producing content-misaligned translations. Prior identifiability results (e.g., Diversified Distribution Matching) eliminate MPAs but require per-sample auxiliary labels or attributes for every sample, which is often unavailable or costly. This paper asks whether identifiability can be achieved with dramatically less supervision—specifically, a single paired anchor sample—by exploiting structural sparsity in the transfer map's Jacobian.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unpaired source samples x ~ p_x, unpaired target samples y ~ p_y, and at least one paired anchor (x^(l), y^(l)) with y^(l) = g*(x^(l)); in practice 1–10 anchors suffice for high-dimensional data. For image tasks, inputs are 32×32 MNIST digits or 128×128 Edges/Shoes images (the latter encoded to 16×16×4 latent vectors via a frozen Stable Diffusion VAE); for single-cell data, inputs are TF-IDF-processed count vectors. The output is a learned invertible transfer function g_θ that produces content-aligned translations in the target domain, evaluated via translation error (TE) or LPIPS against ground-truth paired targets.

## Method

The key theoretical insight reinterprets DT as a nonlinear unmixing problem in which source-domain samples act as visible latent components: under a Jacobian structural sparsity condition (each input coordinate influences only a small subset of output coordinates), Jacobian-sparsity minimization restricts solutions to a finite ambiguity class (permutation × component-wise invertible maps), distribution matching further constrains the remaining freedom to a finite set of one-dimensional MPAs, and a single randomly drawn anchor sample eliminates all non-trivial permutations and MPAs with probability one. Practically, the transfer map g_θ is trained end-to-end via a GAN adversarial loss for distribution matching, a simple L2 anchor regression loss, an invertibility regularizer (a reconstruction network f_α trained to invert g_θ), and a novel Jacobian sparsity regularizer. The sparsity regularizer avoids the O(D) backward passes of explicit Jacobian computation by using random sparse Gaussian probes z (with ‖z‖₀ = S ≪ D) and a two-forward-pass finite-difference approximation ‖(g_θ(x+δz)−g_θ(x))/δ‖₁, for which Proposition 4.1 provides tight sandwich bounds guaranteeing it closely approximates ‖J_g‖₀ when (S−1)(T−1) ≪ D. For image architectures, standard instance normalization is replaced with channel-only normalization to preserve the sparse Jacobian structure required by the theory.

## Results

On MNIST→rotated MNIST, the proposed method achieves the lowest translation error (TE = 0.20 ± 0.06) among all 12 baselines, substantially outperforming the next-best method DIMENSION (TE = 0.35 ± 0.09) and standard distribution-matching methods like CycleGAN (0.60) and CUT (0.81). On Edges→rotated Shoes, the proposed method attains LPIPS of 0.32 ± 0.07, competitive with DIMENSION (0.29 ± 0.06) which requires per-sample shoe-type labels, while all other baselines score 0.38 or worse. On the A549 single-cell RNA-seq→ATAC-seq task, the proposed method substantially outperforms the CM-AE baseline across all k in K-NN accuracy, with gains driven by the combination of anchor matching and Jacobian sparsity regularization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method decisively wins on the MNIST→rotated MNIST task, achieving TE of 0.20 ± 0.06 versus 0.35 ± 0.09 for DIMENSION (the only other method that addresses content misalignment) and 0.60–0.81 for all standard distribution-matching baselines (CycleGAN, CUT, UNIT, MUNIT, U-GAT-IT, StarGAN-v2). On Edges→rotated Shoes, the proposed method (LPIPS 0.32) is slightly behind DIMENSION (0.29) but ahead of all other baselines (best: ZeroDIM at 0.38); critically, DIMENSION requires per-sample shoe-type labels while the proposed method uses only 10 paired anchors. On the A549 single-cell task, the proposed method outperforms the CM-AE baseline (Yang et al., 2021) across all k in K-NN accuracy, and ablation confirms that the gain requires both the anchor loss and the Jacobian sparsity regularizer jointly. The 2D synthetic experiment further validates the theory: adding a single anchor reduces TE from 0.83 ± 0.30 (distribution matching alone) to 0.17 ± 0.08, confirming that one aligned pair suffices to eliminate MPA-induced misalignment under the sparsity condition.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate lines of work: diversified distribution matching (DDM) for domain transfer identifiability (Shrestha & Fu, 2024; 2025), which eliminates measure-preserving automorphisms by matching multiple conditional distributions but requires per-sample auxiliary labels, and sparse nonlinear unmixing (Zheng et al., 2022), which achieves identifiability of nonlinear ICA under Jacobian sparsity. The key conceptual departure is reinterpreting domain transfer as a nonlinear unmixing problem where source samples are 'visible' rather than latent, enabling the use of a single paired anchor to resolve the residual permutation and component-wise ambiguity that Jacobian sparsity alone leaves. The practical contribution of a masked finite-difference Jacobian sparsity regularizer extends the low-dimensional analytical regularizers from the unmixing literature to high-dimensional image and single-cell settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a single paired anchor (rather than per-sample labels) suffices for identifiability under Jacobian sparsity substantially lowers the supervision barrier for content-aligned domain transfer, which is practically significant in settings like single-cell biology where paired measurements across modalities are destructive to obtain. However, the sparsity assumption is strong and may not hold for all transfer tasks, and the gap between the theoretical single-anchor guarantee and the 10 anchors needed in practice for complex image data highlights that model mismatch and nonconvex optimization remain open challenges. Future work on identifiability without any paired data, alternative structural conditions beyond sparsity, and finite-sample robustness to noisy anchors are important directions the authors identify.
