## Problem

Current VLA models regress absolute action coordinates, violating the principle of General Covariance by conflating invariant task geometry with extrinsic nuisance variables (execution speed, reference frame, spatial scale). This entanglement fractures a single geometric intent into disjoint modes across a non-convex landscape, causing mode averaging (e.g., grasping empty space between objects) and temporal over-smoothing that destroys critical contact dynamics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263116/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB images (encoded via SigLIP ViT into unpooled patch embeddings) and a natural-language instruction, producing a 30-frame action chunk of dimension 7 (3D position, 3D rotation, 1D gripper) in a pose-normalized canonical frame rather than absolute world coordinates. The output is hierarchically structured: a discrete schema index (from a 1024-entry codebook) locks the geometric intent, and a continuous flow-matching head then generates the fine-grained trajectory conditioned on that intent, with affine parameters (translation, rotation, scale) regressed separately to re-ground the canonical shape into the physical workspace.

## Method

GAM enforces General Covariance through two orthogonal structural disentanglements: (1) an Arc-Length Parameterizer (ALP) that re-indexes trajectories by cumulative arc length instead of absolute time, making the loss invariant to execution speed and eliminating temporal mode averaging; and (2) a Schema-Affine Factorization (SAF) that analytically decomposes each trajectory into a canonical shape (invariant to SE(3) transforms) and equivariant affine parameters (centroid, principal-axis rotation, scale), projecting the problem onto a locally convex quotient space. The architecture pairs a Qwen2.5-VL-3B backbone (Planner) that predicts the discrete schema and affine parameters via cross-entropy and MSE losses, with a DiT-B flow-matching head (Executor) that generates the arc-length-aligned trajectory from Gaussian noise conditioned on the intent hidden state via AdaLN. Training is two-stage: first, an RVQ-VAE is unsupervisedly trained on SAF-extracted canonical shapes to build the geometric codebook; second, the full VLA is fine-tuned with a composite objective combining schema classification, flow matching, and a velocity regularization term that prevents collapse to stationary solutions.

## Results

On the LIBERO benchmark suite, GAM achieves the highest average success rate of 96.1%, outperforming the strongest baseline π₀ (94.1%) by +2.0% and BEAST (92.5%) by +3.6%. The advantage is most pronounced on LIBERO-Long (92.0% vs. 85.2% for π₀ and 86.4% for BEAST), where long-horizon consistency is critical. On SimplerEnv-WidowX under distribution shift, GAM reaches 56.3% overall success, surpassing the next-best SpatialVLA (42.7%) by +13.6%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GAM outperforms all seven baselines on the LIBERO average success rate. Against the strongest flow-matching baseline π₀ (94.1%), GAM gains +2.0% overall, with the largest margin on LIBERO-Long (+6.8%) where schema-based topological collapse prevents long-horizon drift. Against the tokenization-based BEAST (92.5%), GAM leads by +3.6% on average and +5.6% on Long. On SimplerEnv-WidowX, GAM (56.3%) substantially outperforms SpatialVLA (42.7%), OpenVLA-OFT (41.8%), RoboVLMs (37.5%), BEAST (37.5%), π₀-FAST (32.3%), Octo-Small (29.5%), π₀ (27.1%), and Octo-Base (16.0%), with the largest gap on Put Eggplant (87.5% vs. 79.2% for SpatialVLA) and Stack Block (41.7% vs. 29.2% for SpatialVLA). The only per-suite loss is on LIBERO-Object, where π₀ achieves 98.8% vs. GAM's 97.5%, a marginal 1.3% gap. The consistent dominance across both in-distribution (LIBERO) and out-of-distribution (SimplerEnv) settings supports the claim that geometric invariance via SAF and temporal invariance via ALP provide a stronger inductive bias than geometry-agnostic regression or standard tokenization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Calinon's (2020) concept of action schemas as locally convex basins of attraction in Riemannian space and Ijspeert et al.'s (2013) shape-speed separation principle from dynamical movement primitives, but unifies them under a single 'General Covariance' framework that prior VLA work (Octo, OpenVLA, π0, FAST, BEAST) has not explicitly enforced. Where existing methods treat robot actions as unstructured Euclidean vectors and rely on the network to implicitly learn invariances, GAM makes these invariances structural: arc-length reparameterization handles temporal diffeomorphisms, and SE(3) quotient-space projection (via Schema-Affine Factorization) handles spatial symmetries before any neural learning occurs. The RVQ-VAE tokenization is applied to canonical shapes rather than raw trajectories, and the flow-matching executor is conditioned on discrete schema tokens, creating a hierarchical plan-then-execute architecture distinct from flat diffusion or autoregressive decoders.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical justification (via two theorems on diffeomorphism invariance and quotient convexity) for why direct regression on absolute coordinates produces mode-averaging failures, and shows that enforcing these invariances structurally yields a +13.6% gap over the best baseline on domain-shifted SimplerEnv tasks. However, all experiments are simulation-only (LIBERO, SimplerEnv-WidowX), and the RSA correlation between latent and physical geometry is modest (ρ≈0.29), suggesting the learned representations are only partially aligned with the claimed invariant structure. Open questions include whether the fixed codebook (K=1024) scales to truly open-ended task distributions, how the framework handles multi-robot or bimanual settings with coupled SE(3) groups, and whether the analytical SAF normalization generalizes to non-rigid or deformable manipulation.
