## Problem

Existing multimodal jailbreak methods for Vision-Language Models treat text and image channels independently, rely on handcrafted templates that produce narrow attack distributions, and operate only in single-shot settings that are easily detected by frontier model guardrails. No prior approach models the joint distribution over paired text-image adversarial inputs, leaving cross-modal interactions that enable stealthy, coordinated attacks largely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262720/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a harmful behavior prompt (e.g., a one-sentence description of the target behavior), a large image corpus for CLIP-based distractor retrieval, and black-box API access to a target VLM. It produces a 3×3 composite image (900×990 px, each cell 300×330 px) containing seven semantically unrelated distractor images, one typographic rendering of the text prompt, and one Stable Diffusion 3–generated image, paired with a fixed benign wrapper prompt that instructs the VLM to interpret the grid. The attacker LLM outputs a latent (text prompt, image prompt) pair that is transformed into this composite before querying the VLM.

## Method

VERA-V recasts multimodal jailbreak generation as variational inference: a LoRA-fine-tuned Vicuna-7B attacker defines a variational distribution q_θ(x_t, x_v) over paired text and image prompts, trained to approximate the posterior over inputs that elicit harmful VLM responses. The ELBO objective is optimized via a REINFORCE gradient estimator where a judge model (HarmBench classifier) provides a scalar harmfulness score as a black-box proxy for the VLM likelihood, enabling multi-round feedback-driven refinement without access to target model internals. The composite image design coordinates three complementary channels—typographic rendering for explicit harmful cues, diffusion-based synthesis for implicit adversarial signals, and CLIP-retrieved distractors for attention fragmentation—so that the learned posterior captures cross-modal correlations rather than optimizing each modality in isolation. Early stopping triggers upon any successful jailbreak in a batch, and the KL regularization coefficient (β=0.4) balances exploitation of high-reward prompts against mode collapse, preserving diversity for scalable red-teaming.

## Results

On HarmBench, VERA-V achieves an average attack success rate (ASR) of 72.0% on Qwen2.5-VL-7B, 76.5% on InternVL3-8B, 60.5% on GPT-4o-mini, and 67.75% on GPT-4o, consistently outperforming all baselines. On the HADES dataset, VERA-V reaches 80.0% ASR on open-source models and 73.5% on GPT-4o. The headline result is a 53.75 percentage-point ASR improvement over the best baseline (CS-DJ, 14.0%) on GPT-4o for HarmBench, and a 52.5-point improvement on HADES (73.5% vs. 21.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VERA-V outperforms all four baselines across every target model and both benchmarks. Against CS-DJ (the strongest baseline), VERA-V gains +19.0 points on Qwen2.5-VL, +17.0 on InternVL3, +29.75 on GPT-4o-mini, and +53.75 on GPT-4o (HarmBench). FigStep and HADES, which rely on single-modality or fixed-template strategies, collapse to near-zero ASR on GPT-4o (0.0% and 4.0% respectively), while VERA-V maintains 67.75%, highlighting the critical role of joint cross-modal optimization. The text-only baseline AdvPrompter (34.5% on Qwen2.5-VL) confirms that attacking solely through the text channel is substantially less effective than VERA-V's coordinated text-image posterior. On the HADES dataset, VERA-V extends its lead, reaching 73.5% on GPT-4o versus 21.0% for CS-DJ, demonstrating consistent generalization across harmful-behavior categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

VERA-V directly extends VERA (Lochab et al., 2025), which first cast LLM jailbreak generation as variational inference over text prompts, to the multimodal setting where the attacker must jointly optimize over paired text-image inputs. It also builds on the compositional attack paradigm established by CS-DJ (typography + distractors) and HADES (typography + diffusion synthesis), but departs from these by replacing fixed, handcrafted templates with a learned joint posterior over prompt pairs, optimized via REINFORCE with judge feedback. The key conceptual departure is treating cross-modal interaction as a first-class object in the variational objective rather than composing single-modality attacks post hoc.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The distributional framing shifts VLM red-teaming from one-shot exploit discovery to scalable, diverse vulnerability mapping, which is critical for comprehensive safety evaluation of frontier models. The finding that joint cross-modal optimization substantially outperforms both text-only (AdvPrompter) and vision-only attacks underscores that current VLM safety alignment has a specific cross-modal blind spot that single-modality defenses cannot address. Open questions include whether the variational framing is strictly necessary over simpler reward-weighted fine-tuning, how the approach generalizes to next-generation VLMs with tighter multimodal safety, and whether the composite-image attack surface can be closed without degrading legitimate multimodal capability.
