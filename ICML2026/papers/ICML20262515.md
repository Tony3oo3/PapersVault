## Problem

Standard DPO treats all preference pairs uniformly, conflating two distinct learning objectives: constructing representations to comprehend the prompt (representation learning) and refining decision boundaries between similar responses (discrimination learning). No prior work has analyzed how these heterogeneous difficulty dimensions distribute across network depth during DPO training, nor has it exploited this structure to improve alignment quality.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard preference triplets (prompt, preferred response, rejected response) from datasets like UltraFeedback or HH-RLHF. Before training, it precomputes two per-sample difficulty scores—semantic complexity via Monte Carlo sampling from the reference policy and preference uncertainty from reward model margins—converting them to normalized percentile ranks. During training, the active sample set is dynamically defined by two independent pace parameters bounding the complexity-uncertainty plane, so the model sees a growing subset of the dataset whose composition shifts based on internal gradient states rather than a fixed schedule.

## Method

GDO-DPO is a closed-loop curriculum that independently regulates learning pace along semantic complexity and preference uncertainty using layer-specific gradient statistics. It partitions Transformer layers into a representation block (layers 0 through L_mid) and a discrimination block, then monitors two signals: a representation stability metric S_rep (EMA of the ratio of gradient energy in representation vs. discrimination layers) and a discrimination readiness metric A_disc (accuracy on held-out clear-preference samples at the current complexity level). The algorithm advances the semantic complexity boundary when S_rep drops below a threshold (indicating representations have stabilized) and the uncertainty boundary when A_disc exceeds a threshold (indicating the model can reliably discriminate clear preferences). This two-dimensional, state-aware progression prevents gradient interference—where ambiguous preference signals prematurely destabilize still-converging representations—while requiring only gradient norm extraction already computed during backpropagation, adding ~3% runtime overhead. The approach modifies only data presentation order and is compatible with any DPO variant (SimPO, IPO, ORPO, etc.).

## Results

On UltraFeedback with Llama-3-8B-Instruct, GDO-DPO achieves 8.41 MT-Bench, 32.5% AlpacaEval 2.0, and 21.4% Arena-Hard, outperforming DPO by +4.1% on AlpacaEval 2.0 and +2.9% on Arena-Hard. On Mistral-7B-v0.3, GDO-DPO reaches 8.15 MT-Bench, 27.4% AlpacaEval 2.0, and 19.1% Arena-Hard, improving over DPO by +3.3% and +2.8% respectively. Gains are largest on reasoning-intensive categories (Math +0.50, Reasoning +0.46, Coding +0.47 on MT-Bench) and on hallucination (TruthfulQA MC1: 42.8% vs. DPO's 39.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GDO-DPO outperforms all six preference-optimization baselines (DPO, IPO, SimPO, ORPO, CPO, SPPO) on every benchmark for both Llama-3-8B and Mistral-7B. Against the strongest baseline SimPO, GDO-DPO gains +2.7% on AlpacaEval 2.0 and +2.2% on Arena-Hard (Llama-3-8B), and +1.6% on both metrics (Mistral-7B). Against standard DPO, the margins are larger: +4.1% on AlpacaEval 2.0 and +2.9% on Arena-Hard for Llama-3-8B. The two scalar-curriculum baselines (Curr-Loss, Curr-PPL) provide only modest improvements over random sampling (e.g., Curr-Loss reaches 29.1% vs. DPO's 28.4% on AlpacaEval), confirming that single-dimension difficulty metrics fail to capture the two-axis structure. GDO-DPO also generalizes to HH-RLHF (31.2% AlpacaEval vs. DPO's 27.2%) and composes additively with SimPO (GDO-SimPO reaches 33.1% AlpacaEval, exceeding both GDO-DPO and SimPO individually).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of DPO-based preference optimization and curriculum learning, but departs from both lines in a specific way. Prior DPO variants (IPO, SimPO, ORPO, CPO, SPPO) modify the loss function while treating data consumption as uniform, and prior curriculum approaches in NLP rely on scalar difficulty metrics (perplexity, loss, sentence length) that conflate multiple difficulty sources. GDO-DPO instead uses layer-wise gradient statistics to define a bi-dimensional curriculum space (semantic complexity × preference uncertainty), grounding the design in Transformer interpretability findings about functional layer specialization. The 'gradient interference' failure mode—where premature exposure to ambiguous preferences destabilizes representation layers—connects the interpretability literature on feature distortion during fine-tuning (Kumar et al., 2022; Ren & Sutherland, 2025) to a concrete optimization pathology in DPO.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that a simple data-reordering strategy, requiring only 3% runtime overhead and compatible with any DPO loss variant, yields consistent 2–4% gains over strong baselines, with the largest improvements on reasoning-intensive tasks. The CKA analysis linking lower-layer representation preservation to reduced hallucination (TruthfulQA +3.3 points) provides a mechanistic explanation for why the curriculum helps beyond mere difficulty ordering. However, the theoretical foundation rests on a simplified linear model, all experiments are limited to 7–8B parameters, and the 15% preprocessing cost for semantic complexity estimation may be prohibitive at scale. Open questions include whether the gradient localization phenomenon persists at 70B+ scale, whether automated layer boundary detection can eliminate the L_mid hyperparameter, and how the framework adapts to online RLHF where the data distribution shifts during training.
