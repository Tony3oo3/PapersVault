## Problem

Neural operators for PDE solution learning are largely limited to problems with constant or weakly varying boundary conditions, yet in many applications BCs are the primary inputs driving system behavior and the solution operator is highly sensitive to them. Existing BC-handling methods are restricted to Cartesian geometries, fail on complex mixed-type BCs, or degrade when BCs vary substantially across samples. This paper addresses the gap by proposing a general framework that conditions any standard neural operator on arbitrary, highly variable, mixed-type boundary conditions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw boundary functions (Dirichlet, Neumann, and Robin values on up to eight randomly assigned segments), domain source functions, and geometric features (coordinates plus signed distance to boundary) defined on unstructured point clouds of ~16K nodes. The boundary functions are first merged into a unified Robin form with a principled shift-and-scale normalization that preserves physical units and balances magnitudes across BC types. Outputs are the full PDE solution field: a scalar field for Poisson problems, and vector displacement, Green-Lagrange strain, and Cauchy stress fields for elasticity and hyperelasticity problems, all evaluated at the same unstructured node locations.

## Method

The core contribution is an extender module that maps boundary data to latent pseudo-extensions defined over the entire spatial domain, converting the boundary-conditioning problem into a standard domain-to-domain operator learning task. Three extension strategies are explored: zero extensions (boundary values with interior zeros), harmonic extensions (solving a Laplace equation), and learned pseudo-extensions (LX), which use a stack of cross-attention blocks where boundary features attend into downsampled domain features with residual FF layers, trained end-to-end with the core operator. The extender operates in the downsampled latent space of the backbone architecture, keeping attention costs low. The framework is architecture-agnostic and is demonstrated with both the graph-based RIGNO and transformer-based GAOT backbones, requiring no per-dataset hyperparameter tuning. A key theoretical result shows that for linear problems the induced domain-to-domain operator is affine in the pseudo-extension, providing a principled justification for the extension-based decomposition.

## Results

Across 18 datasets (Poisson, linear elasticity, hyperelasticity on 6 geometries), learned pseudo-extensions (LX) with RIGNO reduce the average test error on elasticity/hyperelasticity problems from 30.6% (zero extension) to 11.0% median relative L² error. On the hyperelasticity (Rubber) Circle^H problem, LX(R) achieves 13.8% relative L² error on strain in 5.9 ms, and LX(G) achieves 13.3% in 3.7 ms, compared to 12.2% for FEM with 11.8K mesh nodes in 125 s (~20,000× speedup). For Poisson problems, harmonic extensions (HX) achieve the lowest errors, while zero extensions (0X) remain competitive and incur no additional cost. The framework requires no hyperparameter tuning across all 18 datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The only external baseline applicable to all 18 datasets is BENO (Wang et al., 2024), a graph-based framework that decomposes the problem into homogeneous-BC and homogeneous-PDE sub-problems with a transformer-based BC embedding. BENO produces significantly higher errors than even the simplest zero-extension approach across all datasets, with the gap widening on complex elasticity and hyperelasticity problems. The authors attribute BENO's underperformance to its reliance on a global BC encoding that erodes local spatial structure. On the elasticity/hyperelasticity benchmarks, LX(R) achieves 11.0% average median relative L² error versus BENO's substantially higher values (visible in Figure 2b), while HX and 0X also outperform BENO. For Poisson problems, BENO's errors are notably higher than the sub-1% errors reported in its original paper, highlighting the increased difficulty of the 18-dataset benchmark. The paper also shows that BENO fails to resolve the combined effect of varying BCs and domain source functions (Mixed+ configuration), whereas the proposed extended RIGNO and GAOT handle both simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the encode-process-decode neural operator paradigm (Sanchez-Gonzalez et al., 2020) by inserting a boundary-to-domain extender module that converts raw boundary functions into latent domain functions, making the problem amenable to any standard domain-to-domain operator. It departs conceptually from prior BC-handling approaches—PENN's local correction layers, BOON's kernel manipulation, and BENO's homogeneous/heterogeneous sub-problem decomposition—all of which operate at or near the boundary—by instead projecting BC information into the interior via learned pseudo-extensions. The extender is demonstrated as a plug-in for two distinct backbones (graph-based RIGNO and transformer-based GAOT), positioning it as an architecture-agnostic module rather than a new operator family.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 18-dataset benchmark with extreme per-sample BC variability (mixed types, component-wise assignments, up to 8 random segments) fills a critical gap: prior BC-conditioning methods were either inapplicable or produced consistently high errors on such settings, and no public benchmark existed to measure progress. The architecture-agnostic design and the finding that a single fixed hyperparameter set works across all datasets lower the barrier to adoption for the broader neural operator community. Key limitations include restriction to 2D elliptic problems (no 3D evaluation due to lack of comparable benchmarks) and the cross-attention cost scaling with boundary-node count. The strong transfer-learning results (84.6% relative error reduction across geometries, 67%→23% from linear to hyperelasticity) suggest a promising path toward pre-trained BC extenders for PDE foundation models.
