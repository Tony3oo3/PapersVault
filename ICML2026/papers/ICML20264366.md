## Problem

Pre-trained perception models (grounding, segmentation, 3D box estimation) degrade sharply in novel embodied environments due to domain gaps in viewpoint distribution, occlusion, and spatial semantics. The standard remedy—fine-tuning on downstream data—incurs catastrophic forgetting of prior knowledge and demands costly scene-specific annotations (pixel masks, 3D boxes, referring expressions). The paper asks whether perception can be adapted to new domains by changing how models are deployed (i.e., which viewpoints they observe) rather than modifying the models themselves.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a 512×512 RGB frame from a 1 m-height camera and a natural-language task instruction (e.g., 'segment the sofa near the table') that encodes both the task type and target object description; depth is consumed only by the 3D box estimator, not by the policy. The VLM policy outputs a structured JSON containing a chain-of-thought reasoning trace, a task-type routing label, an extracted object prompt, and a single discrete action from {move_forward, turn_left, turn_right, look_up, look_down, stop} (0.25 m / 10° steps), with a maximum horizon of 10 steps. The frozen perception modules then produce their respective outputs—2D bounding box with confidence, segmentation mask with combined detector+SAM confidence, or oriented 3D bounding box with geometric confidence—which feed back as scalar rewards.

## Method

Sea² freezes all perception modules (GroundingDINO, SAM, and a 3D box estimator) and instead trains a VLM (InternVL3.5-2B or Qwen3-VL-2B) as a low-level pose controller via a two-stage pipeline. Stage 1 (SFT) aligns the VLM with embodied spatial reasoning using trajectories from a deterministic heuristic (search → center → approach/stop), reducing RL exploration variance. Stage 2 (RL) refines the policy with GRPO, where the reward is a weighted combination of a format reward, a confidence reward (Δconfidence between consecutive steps from the frozen module), and a geometric reward (area ratio + center-alignment score), all computed without any downstream perceptual annotations. A task-type routing penalty (−1) enforces correct module selection, and the geometric term provides stable, module-agnostic spatial shaping that complements the noisier confidence signal. This decouples perception from control, enabling plug-and-play integration across diverse off-the-shelf perception backbones without retraining or scene-specific labels.

## Results

On ReplicaCAD, Sea² (Qwen3VL-2B backbone) achieves mAPavg of 0.8052 (+13.54%) for visual grounding, IoU of 0.6516 (+15.92%) for segmentation, and IoU of 0.3380 (+27.68%) for 3D box estimation, all relative to the static Pretrained Perception Module (PPM) baseline. On HM3D, the same method yields mAPavg +22.16%, segmentation IoU +12.49%, and 3D box IoU +9.31%. The method also surpasses the oracle Shortest Path baseline (which has ground-truth 3D coordinates) on all three tasks, e.g., 3D box IoU 0.3380 vs. 0.3259.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against prompt-based VLM controllers (InternVL3.5-2B and Qwen3VL-2B used without task-aligned training), Sea² achieves large and consistent gains: on ReplicaCAD, Ours (Qwen3VL) reaches mAPavg 0.8052 vs. 0.5615 for the prompt-only Qwen3VL-2B (+43.4% relative) and 0.6516 seg IoU vs. 0.4214 (+54.6%), demonstrating that naive prompting is insufficient without the two-stage SFT+RL pipeline. Against TTAOD-Foundation (Gao et al., 2026), a test-time adaptation method that updates detector parameters, Sea² outperforms on both ReplicaCAD (mAPavg 0.7825 vs. 0.7199) and HM3D (0.6202 vs. 0.5749), with especially large margins at mAP@0.75 (0.6975 vs. 0.6008 on ReplicaCAD). Critically, TTAOD-Foundation degrades source-domain COCO mAP from 0.5031 to 0.4228, while Sea² preserves it exactly at 0.5031 since no perception parameters are updated. Sea² also surpasses the oracle Shortest Path baseline (which knows ground-truth 3D target coordinates) on all three tasks, e.g., 3D box IoU 0.3380 vs. 0.3259, indicating that strategic viewpoint optimization beyond mere proximity is essential for high-quality perception.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the embodied active perception line (Yang et al. 2019; Kotar & Mottaghi 2022; Ding et al. 2023) by decoupling perception from control: all perception modules (GroundingDINO, SAM, 3D box estimator) remain frozen, and a VLM is repurposed as a low-level discrete pose controller trained via GRPO with perception-derived scalar rewards. Unlike prior active perception methods that either couple exploration to specific detector architectures or collect labeled data for retraining (Chaplot et al. 2021; Scarpellini et al. 2024), Sea² uses only confidence and geometric consistency signals from off-the-shelf modules, enabling plug-and-play compatibility. The GRPO training pipeline draws from the RLHF/GRPO lineage (Shao et al. 2024) but applies it to embodied control rather than language generation, and the two-stage SFT-then-RL design mirrors standard VLM post-training but with rule-based heuristic trajectories as the cold start.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core insight—that strategic viewpoint selection alone can recover 13–28% of domain-gap-induced performance loss without a single downstream label or parameter update—offers a practically attractive alternative to fine-tuning, which the paper demonstrates causes severe catastrophic forgetting (COCO mAP drops 38% after 2 epochs). The finding that a learned policy substantially outperforms an oracle shortest-path baseline (27.68% vs. 6.27% on 3D IoU) challenges the assumption that proximity to the target is sufficient for good perception. Key limitations include the frozen-module ceiling (cannot recover missing category knowledge), restriction to indoor scenes, and dependence on the perception module's confidence being at least partially calibrated; the authors suggest combining active viewpoint selection with parameter-efficient adaptation as a natural next step.
