## Problem

Inverse-designing microstructures with coupled cross-physics objectives (e.g., simultaneously targeting thermal conductivity, stiffness, and electrical conductivity) is intractable for existing paradigms: deep generative models produce physically hallucinated structures because they optimize statistical likelihood rather than physical validity, while traditional E algorithms lack the semantic reasoning to interpret ambiguous natural-language design intent. No prior framework grounds open-ended LLM semantic creativity within a rigorous closed-loop simulation validation process for multi-objective cross-physics design.

## Input / Output

The system accepts free-form natural-language specifications (e.g., 'design a copper microstructure with σ=3.8×10⁶ S/m, κ=26 W/(m·K), E=3 GPa, ν=0.25, vf=25%') and resolves them into quantifiable targets in a continuous mechanical conditioning space (E, G, ν). Output is a 64³-voxel 3D microstructure geometry generated by the MIND diffusion model, together with the optimized conditioning parameters and FEA-validated cross-physics properties (elastic, thermal, and mechanical fields under periodic boundary conditions). The design space is parameterized through latent mechanical conditions rather than direct geometric voxels, shifting the optimization from high-dimensional geometry to a lower-dimensional conditioning vector.

## Method

AutoMS adopts a two-layer hierarchical architecture that decouples semantic orchestration from numerical search. The Orchestration Layer uses a Manager agent to coordinate role-specialized agents—a Parser (leveraging GraphRAG) that maps semantic intent to parametric targets, a Generator (MIND diffusion model) that synthesizes geometries, a Simulator (JAX-FEM) that performs high-fidelity cross-physics validation, and a Reporter—while flagging physical infeasibility to counteract LLM hallucinations. The Optimization Layer executes Simulation-Aware Eolutionary Search (SAES), which constructs local gradient approximations from historical simulation data via Weighted Least Squares with spatial-proximity and temporal-freshness weighting, applies gradient-guided parameter updates with a physics-aware directional switch (boosting or reducing properties based on simulation trends), and uses NSGA-II Pareto-driven selection combined with adaptive scalarization-weight rebalancing to escape local optima when conflicting objectives stagnate. This closed-loop neuro-symbolic coordination achieves an 83.8% success rate across 17 diverse cross-physics tasks, nearly doubling NSGA-II (43.7%) and outperforming BayesOpt (65.0%) and CMA-ES (66.7%), while reducing wall-clock time by 23.3% versus ReAct-based LLM baselines through intelligent pruning of physically invalid search paths.

## Results

On a 17-task cross-physics microstructure design benchmark, AutoMS achieves a state-of-the-art 83.8% Success Rate (SR), nearly doubling NSGA-II (43.7%) and outperforming CMA-ES (66.7%) and BayesOpt (65.0%). The framework attains a Mean Relative Error (MRE) of 0.0140—over 30× lower than NSGA-II (0.4276)—and a Best Property Match (BPM) of 94.2%, indicating precise convergence onto target physical specifications. In terms of efficiency, AutoMS completes tasks in 2180.5 s on average, reducing wall-clock time by 23.3% compared to the ReAct baseline (2843.9 s) while requiring only 14.4 iterations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AutoMS decisively outperforms all four external baselines on the primary Success Rate metric: 83.8% vs. NSGA-II (43.7%), ReAct (53.3%), BayesOpt (65.0%), and CMA-ES (66.7%). On solution precision (MRE), AutoMS (0.0140) is an order of magnitude better than NSGA-II (0.4276) and roughly 1.4–1.7× better than the strongest numerical baselines BayesOpt (0.0194) and CMA-ES (0.0241). The multi-agent orchestration is validated by the ablation gap: removing role decomposition (S-SAES, 69.2% SR) or adding only chain-of-thought reasoning (C-SAES, 74.1% SR) both fall 9.7–14.6 points below the full AutoMS, confirming that the performance gain is not solely from the SAES numerical engine but from the hierarchical agent coordination. On efficiency, AutoMS is 2.5× faster than NSGA-II (2180.5 s vs. 5550.2 s) and 23.3% faster than ReAct, while the single-agent S-SAES variant is even faster (1743.0 s) but sacrifices 14.6% in SR, illustrating the speed–validity trade-off that multi-agent orchestration resolves.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MIND generative framework (Xue et al., 2025), originally designed for single-physics inverse microstructure design, into a cross-physics setting by wrapping it in a multi-agent LLM orchestration layer and a novel Simulation-Aware Evolutionary Search (SAES) mechanism. Conceptually, it departs from two prior paradigms: topology optimization (which struggles with non-differentiable, coupled-physics landscapes) and one-shot deep generative models (which produce physically hallucinated structures). The neuro-symbolic framing—LLMs as semantic navigators decomposing natural-language intent into parametric targets, while a closed-loop numerical engine handles gradient-guided search in conditioning space—distinguishes it from both prior multi-agent LLM systems (AutoGen, MetaAgent) that focus on workflow automation without physics grounding, and from prior evolutionary approaches (NSGA-II, CMA-ES) that lack semantic understanding of design intent.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and practically important gap: generative models for materials design produce visually plausible but physically invalid structures when faced with coupled cross-physics constraints, and traditional optimization methods cannot interpret ambiguous natural-language specifications. The 83.8% success rate (vs. 35.3% for one-shot generation and 43.7% for NSGA-II) on a 17-task benchmark, validated with 3D-printed prototypes, demonstrates that closed-loop simulation feedback can systematically eliminate physical hallucinations. Open questions include whether the approach scales to multiscale design (concurrent micro- and macro-optimization), whether explicit PDE constraints can be embedded in agent reasoning to reduce simulation cost (currently 69.2% of wall-clock time), and whether the self-designed 17-task benchmark generalizes to broader industrial design spaces with manufacturing constraints.
