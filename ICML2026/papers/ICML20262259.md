## Problem

Flow-based text-to-image models built on Transformer backbones produce overly similar images under a fixed prompt, limiting users' ability to explore diverse compositions. Existing diversity-enhancement methods alleviate this but incur substantial computational overhead through extra sampling steps, auxiliary optimization, or batch-wise interactions, and the mechanistic root cause of this homogeneity within internal representations remained unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262259/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

DAVE operates as a training-free, inference-time intervention that takes the same inputs as the base model (a text prompt and a random noise seed) and produces a generated image without modifying the sampling pipeline, scheduler, or architecture. The intervention is applied to intermediate hidden representations at selected Transformer blocks during the first 15–20% of denoising steps, requiring no additional forward passes, no batch-size constraints, and no feature-bank maintenance, so runtime and memory usage remain near-identical to the unmodified baseline.

## Method

DAVE (DC Attenuation for diVersity Enhancement) identifies that the zero-frequency spatial average (DC) component of intermediate Transformer representations rapidly converges across noise seeds in early denoising steps, creating a seed-invariant anchor that locks in global layout before structural variation can emerge. The method computes the spatial mean of hidden representations at target blocks and scales it by a coefficient α ∈ (0,1), effectively attenuating the DC component while preserving the seed-specific AC (spatial residual) content. A theoretical analysis formalizes why this early DC dominance arises from the synergy of spectral bias and the MSE flow-matching objective under low SNR, and proves via a Grönwall-type bound that once cross-seed DC separation is reduced to ε, later trajectory recovery is upper-bounded by ε·exp(L(1−t*)). The intervention is governed by three parameters—attenuation strength α, temporal cutoff τ, and target block pool L—selected via a dataset-agnostic criterion of cross-seed DC cosine similarity ≥ 0.99 with FDR correction, making DAVE robust to block choice and applicable across architectures (SD3, FLUX.1-dev, SANA1.5) and even distilled models without retraining.

## Results

On ImageNet with SD3.5, DAVE achieves FID 20.74, Recall 0.649, and Vendi Score 2.33, substantially improving over the original sampler (Recall 0.259, Vendi 1.71) while maintaining competitive CLIP alignment (0.290 vs 0.295). In the in-batch diversity setting on SD3.5 (Table 2), DAVE achieves FID 29.75, Recall 0.441, and Vendi 2.370, outperforming all interaction-based baselines (PG, SPELL, DiverseFlow, OSCAR) on both FID and diversity metrics. DAVE also generalizes to distilled models (SD3.5-Large-Turbo), improving Vendi from 1.383 to 1.643 while preserving CLIP score (0.205).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAVE consistently outperforms CADS on FID and Precision across all three models, with the gap most dramatic on Flux.1-dev (FID 25.31 vs 39.97) and SANA1.5 (FID 25.16 vs 68.13), where CADS's conditioning-perturbation strategy causes severe quality degradation. Against SPARKE, DAVE achieves substantially higher Recall (e.g., 0.649 vs 0.314 on SD3.5/ImageNet) and Vendi Score (2.33 vs 1.77) while maintaining comparable CLIP alignment. In the in-batch setting (Table 2), DAVE dominates all four interaction-based baselines: it achieves the best FID (29.75 vs 35.87–45.69), highest Recall (0.441 vs 0.204–0.255), and highest Vendi (2.370 vs 1.557–2.294) compared to PG, SPELL, DiverseFlow, and OSCAR, while incurring only linear O(TB) computational scaling versus their quadratic O(TB²) cost. DAVE's key advantage is delivering diversity gains through a single lightweight DC-attenuation operation with negligible overhead, rather than requiring batch-wise inter-sample interactions or auxiliary optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of diversity enhancement in T2I generation, which operates at the sampling level (condition perturbation in CADS, batch-wise repulsion in PG/SPELL/DiverseFlow, entropy-based guidance in SPARKE, orthogonal stochasticity in OSCAR). Instead, DAVE identifies a specific representational mechanism—early cross-seed convergence of the zero-frequency (DC) spatial average in Transformer hidden states—as the root cause of trajectory lock-in, and intervenes directly on that component via a single spatial-mean subtraction. Conceptually, it bridges the spectral-bias literature (Rahaman et al., 2019) and the internal-representation analysis line (C3, FREEU, attention-based editing) by pinpointing *where* and *why* diversity collapses, rather than merely steering trajectories at inference time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DAVE offers a practically attractive solution: a training-free, near-zero-overhead operation that works across SD3, FLUX.1-dev, SANA1.5, and distilled variants, while remaining compatible with CFG and batch-agnostic. The diagnostic insight that a single dominant DC mode (51% of energy) acts as a seed-invariant structural anchor is valuable for understanding flow-based generative models more broadly. Open questions include whether the observed block-dependent attribute shifts (color, size, texture) can be harnessed for controllable diversity, whether the approach extends to video or 3D generation, and whether the theoretical capacity bound (Theorem E.7) can be tightened to predict achievable diversity gains a priori.
