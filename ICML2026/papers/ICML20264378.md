## Problem

Existing Vision-Language Navigation models for UAVs follow a dead-reckoning paradigm that iteratively predicts the next waypoint from the previous estimate, inevitably accumulating positional errors over time (state drift). While prior work focuses on generating strong prior estimates, no explicit correction mechanism exists to calibrate these predictions against historical observations, causing progressive misalignment between the internal belief state and objective coordinates that degrades long-horizon trajectory prediction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264378/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each time step the model receives five synchronized RGB views (front, back, down, left, right), the UAV's current 3D coordinates, and a single global natural language instruction that remains fixed throughout the entire episode. The output is a 3D displacement vector in the body coordinate system representing the next waypoint. The MLLM backbone (EVA-CLIP visual encoder + Vicuna-7B language model) is frozen; only the visual projector, waypoint predictor, and LoRA adapters are trained, making the system lightweight to fine-tune on as little as 10% of the training trajectories.

## Method

NeuroKalman reframes continuous navigation as a recursive Bayesian state estimation problem, structurally decoupling it into a GRU-based Prediction step (modeling motion dynamics as the prior) and a memory-augmented Update step (an MLLM that encodes current observations plus retrieved historical anchors as the likelihood). The key mathematical insight is that attention-based retrieval over a visual memory bank is equivalent to Nadaraya-Watson Kernel Density Estimation, so the retrieved vector serves as a non-parametric likelihood that corrects the prior without gradient updates. A learnable sigmoid gating network approximates the Kalman Gain, dynamically weighting the prior and measurement based on a confidence score, so the system trusts external evidence when confident and falls back on smooth internal dynamics when ambiguous. The memory bank uses post-correction selective storage (only appending anchors when confidence exceeds 0.5), and an auxiliary L1 loss jointly supervises both the prior and measurement representations to prevent module collapse during fine-tuning.

## Results

On the TravelUAV benchmark (L1 assistant, Test-Seen, Full split), NeuroKalman achieves NE=71.56, SR=25.86%, OSR=58.73%, and SPL=22.43 using only 10% fine-tuning data, substantially outperforming TravelUAV-FT (NE=99.79, SR=17.56%) and the full-data TravelUAV baseline (NE=106.28, SR=16.10%). On the challenging Hard split (>250m), NeuroKalman reduces NE from 152.04 (TravelUAV) to 105.07 and improves OSR from 40.16% to 53.90%. On Test-Unseen-Object, NeuroKalman reaches SR=32.48% and OSR=60.82%, surpassing NavFoM (SR=29.83%, OSR=47.99%), and on Test-Unseen-Map it nearly doubles TravelUAV's SR (8.34% vs. 4.18%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeuroKalman consistently outperforms all four baselines across every metric and split. Against TravelUAV-FT (the strongest direct competitor, also fine-tuned on 10% data), NeuroKalman improves SR by 8.3 points (25.86% vs. 17.56%) and reduces NE by 28.23m on the L1 Full split, with an even larger gap on Hard trajectories (OSR: 53.90% vs. 36.85%). Against the full-data TravelUAV baseline, NeuroKalman trained on only 10% data still surpasses it on the L1 Hard split (NE: 105.07 vs. 152.04), demonstrating that the Kalman correction mechanism compensates for limited training data. Against OpenVLN, NeuroKalman leads by a wide margin on all metrics (e.g., SR: 25.86% vs. 14.39% on L1 Full). On the Test-Unseen-Object split, NeuroKalman edges out NavFoM (SR: 32.48% vs. 29.83%; OSR: 60.82% vs. 47.99%), though NavFoM retains a slight SPL advantage on the Hard subset due to its large-scale pretraining; on Test-Unseen-Map, NeuroKalman nearly doubles TravelUAV's SR (8.34% vs. 4.18%) and outperforms NavFoM on NE (100.32 vs. 125.10).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes continuous UAV vision-language navigation as a recursive Bayesian state estimation problem, drawing directly on the Kalman prediction-update cycle (Kalman, 1960; Särkkä & Svensson, 2023) and the Nadaraya-Watson kernel regression estimator (Nadaraya, 1964) as its mathematical backbone. It builds on the TravelUAV benchmark and its MLLM-based waypoint predictor (Wang et al., 2024b) but departs from the prevailing dead-reckoning paradigm by introducing an explicit likelihood correction step. The key conceptual bridge is the equivalence between attention-based memory retrieval and KDE over the visual feature manifold, which distinguishes it from prior Deep Bayesian Filtering approaches (Kloss et al., 2021; Revach et al., 2022) that rely on parametric likelihood models and from temporal context methods (SkyVLN, OpenFly, CityNavAgent) that treat memory as a passive buffer rather than probabilistic evidence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental and underappreciated failure mode in continuous navigation—state drift—and shows that a lightweight Bayesian correction mechanism can plateau error growth where parametric models exhibit unbounded accumulation. The data-efficiency result (10% fine-tuning outperforming full-data baselines) suggests that explicit error correction can substitute for scale in VLN, which has practical implications for deployment in GPS-denied environments. However, the evaluation is confined to a single benchmark (TravelUAV/AirSim) with a limited set of baselines, and the GRU-based prior is acknowledged as potentially insufficient for exceptionally long horizons. Open questions include whether the memory-bank approach generalizes to truly novel environments with no relevant historical anchors, and whether the framework extends to multi-agent or cooperative navigation settings.
