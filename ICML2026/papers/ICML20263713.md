## Problem

In federated class-incremental learning, exemplar replay methods face a fundamental trade-off: local-view selection (e.g., Re-Fed+) captures per-client dynamics but ignores cross-client collaborative features, while global-view selection (e.g., FedCBDR) accounts for cross-client distributions at the cost of privacy risks from exposing feature statistics and spectral information. No existing approach simultaneously achieves globally representative exemplar selection and strong privacy guarantees under strict federated constraints.

## Input / Output

Each client receives its local non-IID image partition (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet, 256×256 for DomainNet) plus k other clients' encoder parameters (backbone weights only, not full models) to construct multiple feature views. The method outputs a fixed-size exemplar buffer per task (e.g., 40–80 samples per client per task) with per-sample reweighting coefficients derived from cross-view selection frequency, which are then used to modulate the training loss during subsequent incremental tasks. The final model produces standard class predictions over the growing set of seen classes.

## Method

CLIF consists of two modules. The Cross-View Lewis Weight Fusion (CV-LWF) module passes each client's local samples through k different client backbones to form multiple feature views, computes ℓ_p Lewis weights under each view, and fuses them via pointwise maximum so the resulting score upper-bounds every view's Lewis weights; sampling with probabilities proportional to these fused scores then guarantees simultaneous ℓ_p subspace embedding preservation across all views (Theorem 1). The Frequency-Aware Weighted Training (FWT) module constructs a reweighted sampling matrix where each drawn row i receives weight (m_k·p_{k,i})^{−1/p}, so samples consistently selected across views receive amplified loss contributions during replay. Two deployment variants are provided: CLIF_R performs all view construction and sampling locally on each client (only downloading k encoder weights at the final round), while CLIF_F aggregates pseudo-features on the server for a global ranking. The theoretical analysis shows that with sample size m_k ≳ ε^{−4} T_k d^{max(p/2−1,0)} log²d · log(dT_k/ε), the reweighted exemplar-trained solution achieves bounded prediction error relative to the full-data optimum for all views simultaneously with probability ≥ 0.9.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (20 clients, 3 tasks, β=0.5), CLIF_F achieves 62.47% Top-1 accuracy, surpassing the strongest baseline FedCBDR (56.94%) by 5.5 points and Re-Fed+ (52.45%) by 10 points. On CIFAR-100 under the same setting, CLIF_R reaches 44.10% vs. Re-Fed+'s 42.79%, and on Tiny-ImageNet, CLIF_R attains 19.47% vs. Re-Fed+'s 18.88%. Across all datasets, heterogeneity levels (β=0.5/1.0), client counts (20/40), and task splits (3/5/5 and 5/10/10), both CLIF variants consistently outperform all eight baselines by 1–6% in Top-1 accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLIF dominates all eight baselines across every dataset and setting. Against the two strongest exemplar-replay baselines, CLIF_F outperforms FedCBDR by 5.5 points on CIFAR-10 (62.47 vs. 56.94) and by 1.1 points on CIFAR-100 (43.84 vs. 42.52) under 20 clients/β=0.5, while CLIF_R edges out Re-Fed+ by 1.3 points on CIFAR-100 (44.10 vs. 42.79) and 0.6 points on Tiny-ImageNet (19.47 vs. 18.88). The gap widens substantially under higher heterogeneity (β=0.5) and more tasks: on CIFAR-10 with 5 tasks and 40 clients, CLIF_F reaches 68.06% versus FedCBDR's 66.78% and Re-Fed+'s 61.92%. Regularization-based methods (FedEWC, FedLwf) and distillation-based methods (Target, LANDER) trail by 10–30 points, confirming that replay-based strategies are essential for FCIL. Notably, CLIF_R (local cross-view) sometimes surpasses FedCBDR (global feature sharing) on CIFAR-100, suggesting that multi-view Lewis weight fusion can match or exceed global-view selection without the associated privacy and communication costs.

## Relation to Prior Work

CLIF extends the Lewis-weight sampling framework of Huang et al. (ICLR 2024) from centralized multi-model active learning to the federated class-incremental setting, where multiple client backbones naturally provide diverse feature views. It positions itself between two existing exemplar-replay paradigms in FCIL: local-view methods like Re-Fed+ (Li et al., TPAMI 2025) that are privacy-safe but lack global awareness, and global-view methods like FedCBDR (Qi et al., NeurIPS 2026) that capture cross-client structure at the cost of exposing feature statistics. The key methodological departure is the pointwise-max fusion of per-view Lewis weights, which provably upper-bounds each view's true weights and thus preserves subspace structure for all client models simultaneously, combined with a frequency-aware reweighting of the replay loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and underexplored tension in FCIL: how to select representative exemplars that reflect global data geometry without incurring the privacy risks of sharing raw features or spectral statistics. The theoretical guarantee (Theorem 1) is a meaningful addition to a field that largely lacks formal analysis of replay-based selection, though the authors themselves note the sample-complexity bound is primarily qualitative given practical buffer sizes. Open questions include handling undertrained local models that may produce unreliable views, mitigating replay-induced heterogeneity in subsequent tasks, and whether the cross-view mechanism scales to highly dynamic or non-stationary class distributions. The modest 1–6% gains and the need to transfer client encoders (even if only at task boundaries) limit immediate deployment impact.
