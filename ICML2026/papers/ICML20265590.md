## Problem

Vision-language foundation models (VLMs) like CLIP provide language-grounded semantics but are perceptually coarse, while vision-only foundation models (VFMs) like DINO capture fine-grained geometry but lack semantic grounding. Existing fusion pipelines that combine them require non-trivial model access (intermediate features, dense mask queries) and are tightly coupled to specific tasks like segmentation, making them inapplicable to general image-level tasks or closed-source models. The paper asks whether heterogeneous foundation models can be made directly compatible at the representation level in a fully unsupervised, task-agnostic manner without updating any pretrained parameters.

## Input / Output

GPUA consumes frozen features from a VFM (DINOv3 ViT-B/16) and a VLM (CLIP ViT-B/16): for classification, the CLS token at 224×224 resolution; for segmentation, patch-level features where the top 1,024 patches per category are selected by VLM text-prototype similarity. The method produces a single orthogonal transformation matrix W (d_v × d_t) learned offline from unlabeled training-split features. At inference, W is applied as a fixed translation to image features, which are then matched against frozen text prototypes via cosine similarity (classification) or used for patch-level dense prediction (segmentation), with no test-time adaptation or prompt optimization.

## Method

GPUA recasts VFM-VLM compatibility as a cross-lingual translation problem, treating VFM features as a 'visual language' to be mapped into the VLM's semantic space via an orthogonal transformation W (W^T W = I) that preserves intrinsic geometry. The method uses a two-stage decoupled pipeline: Stage 1 (Unsupervised Correspondence Mining) learns a soft assignment matrix P by jointly optimizing a K-means-style geometric coherence term in VFM space (with learnable centroids C) and a semantic matching term in VLM space, solved via Sinkhorn-Knopp optimal transport with entropic regularization; Stage 2 (Geometry-Preserving Alignment) computes a closed-form orthogonal Procrustes solution from the mined correspondences, then refines W with a topology-aware hubness-suppression loss (THS) that up-weights margins for overly-central prototypes. The key design choice is sharing the same assignment matrix P across both spaces, which couples geometric and semantic structure in a single optimization, and the orthogonality constraint that prevents degenerate scaling or shearing while maintaining neighborhood geometry for stable nearest-prototype inference.

## Results

On zero-shot image classification across 11 benchmarks, GPUA achieves an average accuracy of 77.4%, improving over the CLIP baseline (65.2%) by +11.8% and over the strongest baseline COSMIC (73.3%) by +4.1%. The largest gains appear on domain-shift and fine-grained datasets: EuroSAT (+34.9% over CLIP), DTD (+14.7%), and FGVC-Aircraft (+5.5%). In the low-data variant (GPUA*, 16 unlabeled samples per class), average accuracy reaches 76.5%, still surpassing all prior unsupervised baselines. For open-vocabulary segmentation, GPUA as a plug-in module improves SC-CLIP from 20.1 to 21.3 mIoU on ADE20K and from 84.3 to 87.6 on VOC20, matching or exceeding dedicated VFM-based methods like Talk2DINO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPUA outperforms all 10 compared zero-shot classification baselines on average, with the largest margin over CLIP (+11.8%) and the strongest prior method COSMIC (+4.1%). Against test-time adaptation methods (ZERO, MTA, TDA, ZLaP, TIPPLE), GPUA leads by 4–12% on average, and notably does not require any inference-time adaptation. Against dual-prototype and memory-based methods (DPE, DMN, StatA), GPUA gains 2–8% on average, with particularly large advantages on EuroSAT (+20.9% over DPE) and DTD (+4.3% over DMN). In the 16-sample-per-class setting, GPUA* (76.5% avg) still exceeds COSMIC (73.3%) by 3.2%, demonstrating strong sample efficiency. For open-vocabulary segmentation, GPUA as a plug-in improves SC-CLIP by +1.2 mIoU on ADE20K and +3.3 on VOC20, and surpasses the dedicated VFM-based method Talk2DINO on all three benchmarks (e.g., 41.0 vs. 39.8 mIoU on C59), confirming that the alignment strategy itself—not merely stronger backbones—drives the gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

GPUA extends the unsupervised cross-lingual alignment framework (Lample et al., 2018; Grave et al., 2019) from word embeddings to vision-language feature spaces, building directly on LFA (Ouali et al., 2023), which first applied orthogonal Procrustes alignment to mitigate the modality gap within a single VLM. The key departure from prior multi-foundation-model fusion pipelines (Talk2DINO, CLIP-DINOiser, SCLIP) is that GPUA operates purely at the feature level with a decoupled two-stage strategy—unsupervised correspondence mining via Sinkhorn optimal transport followed by a closed-form orthogonal Procrustes mapping—rather than task-specific fusion architectures coupled to pixel-level prediction. This makes the alignment task-agnostic and compatible with frozen, potentially closed-source models, in contrast to prior methods that require intermediate feature access or end-to-end training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GPUA offers a practical, plug-and-play mechanism for making heterogeneous foundation models compatible without any parameter updates, which is particularly valuable for API-based or restricted deployment scenarios where model internals are inaccessible. The substantial gains (e.g., +34.9% on EuroSAT, +11.8% average over CLIP) and the strong low-data variant (16 samples/class) suggest that geometry-preserving alignment can extract significant discriminative signal from VFM features that VLMs alone miss. However, the method does not explicitly handle class imbalance, and the reliance on Sinkhorn scaling may limit scalability to very large correspondence matrices; extending the framework to video, 3D, or highly imbalanced open-vocabulary settings remains an open question.
