## Problem

Existing coreset selection methods for federated learning operate solely at the data level with a uniform retention ratio across all clients, which introduces 'fragmented clients' (clients with minimal data that degrade accuracy) and ignores client heterogeneity in contribution. No prior work performs client-level coreset selection that simultaneously reduces both computation and communication costs while accounting for differential client value.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261875/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes local client datasets (image classification tasks on Fashion-MNIST, CINIC-10, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100), the current global model, a total data retention ratio r, and a fragmentation threshold τ. During a pretraining phase (T_pre = 0.1T rounds), it computes GPSV scores using only last-layer gradients to avoid full-model gradient computation. The output is a two-level structure: a pruned client set N⁺ (eliminating fragmented clients) and, within each retained client, a data-level coreset of size B_i allocated proportionally to its GPSV score, yielding a final dataset D⁺ of size r|D|.

## Method

ShapCCS introduces a Gradient Projection Shapley Value (GPSV) that defines coalition utility as the projection length of the aggregated coalition gradient onto the global gradient direction, jointly encoding both directional alignment and magnitude information. A piecewise non-linear mapping ψ prevents the utility from being additive, thereby preserving client interaction effects and avoiding SV degeneration into individual marginal contributions. GPSV achieves O(1) per-coalition evaluation by exploiting projection additivity and Gray Code enumeration, enabling exact Shapley value computation without Monte Carlo sampling. At selection time, ShapCCS allocates the total budget B proportionally to normalized GPSV scores, iteratively excludes clients whose allocated budget falls below threshold τ (redistributing their budget to higher-scoring clients), and applies a pluggable data-level coreset selection method P within each retained client. A convergence analysis shows the non-vanishing bias term is bounded by the gradient client skew gap, which ShapCCS minimizes by preferentially retaining high-GPSV clients with well-aligned gradients.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with Dir(0.1) and r=70%, ShapCCS achieves 62.72% top-1 accuracy, surpassing the full-dataset baseline of 61.51%. The advantage grows as the retention ratio shrinks: at r=30% under Dir(0.1), ShapCCS reaches 55.26% versus 47.35% for the best baseline (S-FedAvg), a 7.91-point gap. Under IID with r=70%, ShapCCS attains 75.10%, again exceeding the full-dataset accuracy of 74.06%. Across all four datasets and three retention ratios, ShapCCS consistently outperforms every competing method, with the largest gains (up to 9.74%) observed under high heterogeneity and low budgets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ShapCCS outperforms all 11 baselines across every dataset, retention ratio, and data distribution setting. On CIFAR-10 Dir(0.1) at r=70%, ShapCCS (62.72%) leads the nearest competitor GCFL (58.71%) by 4.01 points and S-FedAvg (58.59%) by 4.13 points; the gap widens to 7.91 points over S-FedAvg at r=30%. Among data-level coreset methods, EL2N and Moderate are the strongest baselines but still trail ShapCCS by 4–5 points under Dir(0.1). The FL-specific baselines GCFL and FedCS underperform the centralized methods, with FedCS being the weakest overall (e.g., 50.63% vs. 58.42% for EL2N on CIFAR-10 Dir(0.1) r=70%). Notably, ShapCCS* (pure client-level selection without data-level pruning) is within 0.8 points of full ShapCCS, indicating that client-level selection alone already surpasses all data-level approaches. ShapCCS also exceeds full-dataset accuracy in multiple settings (marked †), a result no baseline achieves.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Shapley Value framework (Kuhn & Tucker, 1953) to federated learning by introducing GPSV, a gradient-projection-based utility that captures both directional alignment and magnitude of client updates, unlike prior gradient-based SVs (CGSV, CSSV) that only encode cosine similarity. It departs from existing FL coreset selection methods (GCFL, FedCS) that operate exclusively at the data level with uniform per-client retention ratios, instead proposing the first client-level coreset selection strategy that permanently prunes entire clients. The method integrates with Moderate (Xia et al., 2023) as a plug-in data-level selector and leverages GTG truncation (Liu et al., 2022) for scalable SV estimation at large client counts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ShapCCS simultaneously reduces both computational and communication overheads—a dual saving unattainable by data-level methods alone—making it practically relevant for bandwidth-constrained cross-device FL. The identification of 'fragmented clients' as actively harmful (not merely wasteful) provides a useful diagnostic for FL practitioners. Key limitations include reliance on the global gradient as an evaluation reference (vulnerable under extreme label noise) and a mandatory pretraining phase; the paper also leaves open the design of data-level selection strategies specifically suited to client-level coreset selection, and scalability beyond ~200 clients without Monte Carlo approximation.
