## Problem

Existing theoretical guarantees for GCN size transfer are confined to dense graphs or restricted sparsity regimes (e.g., Ω(log n/n)), yet real-world networks are inherently sparse and the downsampling process in size transfer produces subgraphs with fluctuating edge densities. This varying sparsity creates inconsistent topological profiles across source and target graphs that no prior framework can uniformly address, leaving a gap in transferability guarantees for the arbitrary sparsity levels encountered in practice.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264148/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a trained L-layer GCN with fixed parameters H, a source subgraph G_{m,n} and a target graph G_{M,N} drawn from potentially different sparsity subsequences of a generalized graphon sequence. Unlike the classical graphon normalization Φ(S_n/n, x_n, H), the stretched-form evaluation uses Φ(S_n/√(2e_n), x_n, H) where e_n is the realized edge count, reflecting the sparsity-aware scaling. The output is an explicit transfer error bound decomposed into density-dependent terms (scaling as 1/t_m^{k'} and 1/t_M^{k'}) and size-dependent terms (scaling as 1/√n and 1/n^{1/4}), along with the GCN node embeddings on the target graph.

## Method

The paper introduces the Generalized Graphon Convolutional Network (GWCN) as the non-zero limit of GCNs under arbitrary sparsity, built on the generalized graphon framework where sparse graphs are generated via double-sampling (first a sparse classical graphon W_m from a generalized graphon W_{R+}, then random graphs G_{m,n} from W_m). The key mechanism is stretching: scaling graphons by s = ||W||_1^{1/2} to amplify vanishing structural patterns in sparse regimes, yielding a double-convergent non-trivial limit W_{R+}^s. Transferability is then proven by bounding the GWCN output discrepancy between source and target via a triangle inequality over operator and signal differences, each decomposed along the two stages of double-sampling. The resulting bound (Theorem 5.1) shows transfer error vanishes as both truncation parameters t_m, t_M (controlling edge density) and graph sizes n, N grow, with an architectural amplification factor C(H) that scales with depth, width, and filter order.

## Results

The paper's primary result is Theorem 5.1, which establishes an explicit transfer error bound for GCNs across arbitrary sparsity, decomposing the error into density-dependent terms (governed by truncation parameters t_m, t_M) and size-dependent terms (governed by n, N). Empirically, on Cora (2708 nodes, edge density 0.00144), transfer error vanishes as subgraph size grows from 100 to 1600 nodes across all three sparsity schemes (f(n)=2/n, 0.2 log n/n, Θ(1)). In the synthetic geometric graph experiment, the proposed GWCN achieves steady convergence under extreme sparsity α(n)=1/n, whereas the baseline c-GCN (Keriven et al., 2020) completely stagnates, failing to reduce convergence error at all.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's main empirical baseline is the continuous GCN (c-GCN) from Keriven et al. (2020), which provides convergence guarantees only under relative sparsity Ω(log n/n). In the synthetic geometric graph experiment (Figure 6), the c-GCN's convergence error stagnates completely under the most extreme sparsity α(n)=1/n, while the proposed GWCN continues to reduce its transfer error steadily. Under the less extreme α(n)=log(n)/n regime, the GWCN also shows faster and more robust convergence. On real-world datasets (Cora, Pubmed, Ogbn-Arxiv), the paper does not directly compare against a competing GCN transfer method but instead validates its theoretical predictions: transfer error decreases monotonically with increasing subgraph size and decreasing edge density, and deeper/wider GCN architectures (3 layers, 64–256 hidden units) exhibit larger transfer errors due to architectural amplification, consistent with the C(H) factor in Theorem 5.1. The key differentiator is that prior frameworks (Keriven et al., Wang et al.) cannot guarantee vanishing transfer error under the sparsity levels tested (e.g., f(n)=2/n on Cora), whereas the GWCN bound holds for all k_n.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the generalized graphon framework (Borgs et al., 2018; Ji et al., 2024) — which uses stretching to produce non-trivial limits for sparse graph sequences — to the GCN transferability setting, building on the dense-graphon GCN analysis of Ruiz et al. (2020, 2023). It departs from prior sparse-graph GCN convergence results (Keriven et al., 2020, 2021; Wang et al., 2023, 2024) that are restricted to Ω(log n/n) sparsity, and from operator-based approaches (Le & Jegelka, 2023; Levie et al., 2021) that avoid explicit graph generation and thus cannot analyze edge density. The key conceptual move is introducing the GWCN as a non-zero limit object that is shared across subsequences with different sparsity, enabling a single transfer bound that covers arbitrary sparsity levels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical guarantee for GCN size transfer that holds across arbitrary sparsity with a vanishing error bound, filling a gap identified in Table 1 where all prior frameworks either restrict sparsity, lack explicit topological analysis, or fail to guarantee convergence. The decomposition of transfer error into size-dependent (n^{-1/2}, n^{-1/4}) and density-dependent (t_m^{-k'}) components gives practitioners a clear diagnostic: transfer improves with both larger graphs and lower edge density. Limitations include the Schwartz-space assumption on W_R+ (rapidly decaying connection probabilities), worst-case bounds that may be loose in practice, and analysis restricted to GCNs with fixed learned parameters — extension to other GNN architectures, adaptive sampling strategies, or tighter finite-sample bounds remains open.
