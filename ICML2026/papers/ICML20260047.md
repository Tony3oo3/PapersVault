## Problem

Existing LLM watermarking schemes operate on individual token identities, making them inherently vulnerable to semantic-invariant attacks such as paraphrasing and synonym substitution that alter surface tokens while preserving meaning. Prior semantic-aware methods rely on heuristic logit biases that sacrifice text quality, and existing theoretical frameworks characterize token-level trade-offs without incorporating robustness under meaning-preserving transformations. The paper asks whether a watermarking method can simultaneously achieve robustness to semantic-invariant attacks, distortion-free generation, and principled control over detection errors at low false-positive rates.

## Input / Output

During generation, PASA consumes the current NTP distribution from the LLM, a secret key, and the semantic cluster indices of the previous w tokens (default w=3) to seed a PRF that determines the auxiliary random variable; it outputs the next token sampled from a re-normalized within-cluster distribution. During detection, the method takes an observed token sequence, the shared secret key, the semantic mapping function f (K-means clusters over gte-Qwen2-7B-instruct embeddings, K=4 by default), and a lightweight surrogate language model (e.g., Llama-2-7B for Llama-2-13B generation) to approximate the NTP distribution and reconstruct the auxiliary sequence. The detection output is a binary decision based on an accumulated score of indicator functions 1{f(x_t)=ζ_t} across all tokens, compared against a threshold.

## Method

PASA elevates watermarking from the token vocabulary to a latent semantic embedding space by partitioning token embeddings into K disjoint semantic clusters via K-means, then anchoring shared randomness at the cluster level rather than the token level. The embedding uses a two-stage sampling strategy: Stage 1 constructs a truncated auxiliary distribution P_{ζ_t} where each cluster's probability is capped at the FA threshold α (default 0.4) with excess mass assigned to an overflow state ζ̃, and samples ζ_t via a PRF seeded by the secret key and the semantic history of the previous w tokens; Stage 2 samples the next token either within the selected cluster (re-normalized NTP) or across clusters weighted by overflow mass (when ζ_t=ζ̃), ensuring E_{ζ_t}[P_{X_t|x^{t-1},ζ_t}]=Q_t for distortion-free generation. The detector mirrors this procedure using a surrogate LM to approximate the NTP distribution, reconstructs the auxiliary distribution, replays the PRF to recover seeds, and accumulates the score 1{f(x_t)=ζ_t} across tokens. The design is grounded in a theoretical framework (Theorem 2) that characterizes the jointly optimal embedding-detection pair minimizing miss-detection error subject to worst-case false-alarm and distortion constraints, with the minimum MD error explicitly depending on the number of semantic clusters K, the FA threshold α, and the distortion budget ε.

## Results

Under DIPPER paraphrasing attacks (the paper's primary robustness evaluation), PASA achieves ROC-AUC of 0.9132 / 0.8993 / 0.8956 and TPR@1%FPR of 0.5900 / 0.5693 / 0.5377 at increasing word-order perturbation levels (Ord=0/20/80), substantially outperforming all token-level baselines. Under T5-based token replacement on Llama-2-13B, PASA reaches TPR@1%FPR of 0.9296 (T5-Large) and ROC-AUC of 0.9392 (T5-XXL), versus 0.7350 and 0.9196 for KGW. PASA also preserves text quality with a PPL of 11.44 (vs. 12.41 unwatermarked, 10.41 human) and achieves the lowest detection latency (0.27 s/sample) among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PASA dominates all five baselines under DIPPER paraphrasing, the attack most aligned with its design. At the strongest setting (Ord=80), PASA's TPR@1%FPR of 0.5377 exceeds KGW (0.3100) by 73%, AWTI (0.1350) by 300%, Exp-edit (0.1150) by 370%, and DAWA (0.0200) by 2600%. Under T5 token replacement (Table 1), PASA similarly outperforms KGW and DAWA on both Llama-2-13B and Mixtral-8×7B, though the gap narrows against Exp-edit and AWTI, which are specifically designed for edit robustness. On clean text, all methods achieve near-perfect detection (ROC-AUC ≥ 0.99), so differentiation is entirely under attack. In terms of text quality, PASA's PPL of 11.44 is close to the unwatermarked baseline (12.41) and far better than Exp-edit (23.40) and AWTI (19.77), while DAWA reports a lower PPL (8.41) but at the cost of near-zero robustness under paraphrasing. For detection latency, PASA (0.27 s) is faster than Exp-edit (2.41 s) and AWTI (10.52 s), though slower than KGW (0.04 s) which uses a simple count-based detector.

## Relation to Prior Work

PASA extends the distribution-adaptive watermarking framework of He et al. (2025, DAWA) by incorporating a formal semantic-invariant attack model into the optimization problem, yielding a jointly optimal embedding-detection pair that operates on semantic clusters rather than individual tokens. Conceptually, it bridges two previously disconnected lines of work: heuristic semantic-aware watermarking (SemStamp, k-SemStamp, CoheMark) that partition embedding space without theoretical guarantees, and token-level theoretical frameworks (DAWA, KGW) that lack robustness under meaning-preserving transformations. The key departure is anchoring shared randomness to K-means semantic clusters in a latent embedding space (via gte-Qwen2-7B-instruct), so that paraphrasing-induced token changes leave the cluster-level signal intact, while a two-stage sampling mechanism with an overflow state provably preserves the original NTP distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first watermarking scheme to simultaneously provide (i) a closed-form characterization of the minimum miss-detection error under semantic-invariant attacks, (ii) a distortion-free guarantee, and (iii) empirical robustness against strong paraphrasing (DIPPER Ord=80) where token-level baselines collapse (DAWA TPR@1%FPR drops to 0.02 vs. PASA's 0.54). Open questions include performance under very strong rewriting that alters both semantics and distributional structure, detection reliability on very short texts, and cross-family tokenizer compatibility, which limits deployment to same-family SLM pairs. The framework also suggests a practical multi-detector deployment strategy for source attribution across model families.
