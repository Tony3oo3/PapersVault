## Problem

Prior-data Fitted Networks (PFNs) enable zero-shot amortized causal effect estimation, but whether unified pre-training can achieve unbiased estimation across general interventional regimes remains unexplored. This paper identifies a fundamental theoretical limitation: a single observational SCM induces an exponentially large space of interventional distributions, so any finite pre-training set suffers from exponential prior uncoverage, leading to unavoidable posterior inconsistency and systematic estimation bias that worsens with the number of variables.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264750/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The PFN backbone (TabPFN, CausalPFN, or DoPFN) receives in-context tabular samples—both observational and interventional—along with the intervention specification (which variables are set to which values), and outputs a posterior predictive distribution over causal functionals such as ATE, CATE, or individual counterfactual predictions. The proposed fine-tuning strategies additionally consume samples drawn from one or more target interventional distributions: PWF uses samples from a single specified intervention, while MSF uses a mixture over K interventions selected via a greedy k-center criterion on the W-marginal TV metric.

## Method

The paper first proves (Theorems 1–3) that the empirical meta-prior learned by finite pre-training has uncovered mass δ ≥ 1 − N/(1+d)^D, which propagates through the posterior to yield a lower bound on estimation bias of ηδ − O(n^{-1/2}), establishing that fine-tuning is a fundamental necessity. To restore valid generalization, two strategies are proposed: (1) Point-Wise Interventional Fine-tuning (PWF), which micro-tunes the PFN on samples from a single target interventional distribution and guarantees local generalization within a TV-ball of radius ε around that distribution (Theorem 5); and (2) Meta-Sampling Fine-tuning (MSF), which casts interventional-space coverage as a budgeted k-center problem over W-marginals, selects K distributions via a greedy farthest-point algorithm (achieving a 2× approximation to the optimal coverage radius), and fine-tunes on the resulting mixture to guarantee uniform generalization over the entire interventional space (Theorem 6). Both strategies are applied on top of existing PFN backbones without architectural modification, and experiments on synthetic SCMs (linear, nonlinear, interaction) and the Lalonde/RealCause benchmark validate the theoretical predictions and the practical effectiveness of the fine-tuning protocols.

## Results

On uncovered interventional distributions, all pre-trained PFN baselines degrade substantially: CausalPFN's worst-case MAE exceeds 8.0 at |X|=10 in the Interaction setting, and errors on the Lalonde benchmark exceed 5. After applying the proposed MSF strategy, all three backbones show steady error reduction in both average and worst-case MAE across the full interventional space. Under the MSF protocol with k_interv=2 intervened variables, TabPFN achieves the lowest MAE (0.3087 Linear, 0.1050 Nonlinear, 0.9183 Interaction), while DoPFN is weakest in Linear (1.1244) and Interaction (2.0135) settings. Performance degrades gradually as k_interv increases from 2 to 7, but MSF maintains relatively stable accuracy with limited degradation across all SCM types.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates three PFN-based causal baselines—TabPFN, CausalPFN, and DoPFN—under both covered and uncovered interventional distributions. In the covered (in-distribution) setting, TabPFN achieves the lowest MSE (≈0.1 in Nonlinear), while CausalPFN and DoPFN show higher variance. In the uncovered setting, all three degrade sharply (errors >5 on Lalonde), confirming the theoretical prior-uncoverage risk. After applying the proposed MSF fine-tuning, TabPFN remains the strongest backbone across all SCM types (e.g., 0.3087 vs. 0.7134 vs. 1.1244 MAE for Linear at k=2), with DoPFN performing best among the weaker baselines in the Nonlinear setting (0.4012 vs. CausalPFN's 0.7472). CausalPFN and DoPFN exhibit larger gains from increasing the MSF budget K (from 5 to 11 on synthetic data), while TabPFN maintains the most stable error profile. The paper's key finding is that fine-tuning is necessary for all three baselines to recover valid causal estimation on uncovered interventions, with MSF providing uniform generalization guarantees across the full interventional space.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PFN-based amortized causal inference line (CausalPFN, DoPFN, TabPFN) from single-treatment settings to general interventional regimes where arbitrary subsets of SCM variables are intervened upon. While prior PFN causal models demonstrated zero-shot posterior inference for single treatments, this work identifies a fundamental theoretical gap: the interventional distribution space grows exponentially with the number of variables, making finite pre-training provably insufficient. The paper departs from the 'pre-train once, infer anywhere' paradigm by proving that fine-tuning is a necessary complement, and introduces two strategies (PWF for local, MSF for uniform generalization) that build on the k-center active learning literature to restore valid coverage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The exponential prior uncoverage theorem provides a clean, distribution-free lower bound showing that no finite pre-training budget can cover the interventional space of a D-variable SCM, which has direct implications for the design of causal foundation models. Practically, the PWF and MSF strategies offer a principled path to deploying PFN-based causal models on unseen interventions, with explicit generalization guarantees tied to TV-ball radius and coverage. Open questions include whether the theoretical bounds are tight in practice for realistic (continuous, high-dimensional) SCMs, how MSF scales when the interventional space is combinatorially large, and whether the approach extends to soft interventions or time-varying causal structures.
