## Problem

CLIP-based prompt tuning (e.g., CoOp) is highly sensitive to label noise: mislabeled samples that contradict the model's strong pre-trained prior generate disproportionately large cross-entropy gradients that aggressively overwrite the original knowledge, eventually degrading performance below the zero-shot baseline. Existing denoising approaches such as LogitNorm and LogitClip rely on static logit clamping or global scaling with handcrafted hyperparameters that are sensitive to specific noise rates and datasets, failing to adaptively distinguish noisy from informative samples.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (resolution varies by dataset, e.g., ~300×200 for Caltech101, 64×64 for EuroSAT, 320×240 midframes for UCF101) and a class-name text prompt with 16 learnable continuous tokens prepended. The frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 image encoder and text encoder produce embeddings whose inner products yield logits over C classes; the output is a single class label. Training uses the full training set (not few-shot) with instance-independent label noise (symmetric or pair-flip) at rates up to 90%.

## Method

DSPT replaces the standard cross-entropy loss −log(softmax(z)_{ỹ}) with a double-softmax formulation −log(softmax(softmax(z))_{ỹ}), applying a sequential probabilistic normalization before the final prediction. The resulting gradient ∂L/∂z_i = p_i[(q_i − δ_{ỹi}) + (p_{ỹ} − Σ_j p_j q_j)] is multiplicatively gated by p_i, bounding all per-logit gradients to ≤1 and inducing a self-adaptive saturation zone. Theorem 3.2 proves that for high-confidence predictions discordant with the noisy label (p_ŷ→1, ŷ≠ỹ), the total gradient magnitude converges to zero, effectively nullifying the disruptive surges from likely-mislabeled samples while preserving informative gradients from uncertain, potentially reliable samples. The loss is also bounded between log(1+(C−1)/e) and log(e+C−1) (Proposition 3.3), and generalization risk bounds are established under both symmetric and asymmetric noise (Theorems 3.4–3.5). The design is hyperparameter-free and drop-in, requiring no architectural changes or dataset-specific tuning.

## Results

On the average across 9 datasets (Caltech-101, Stanford Cars, Oxford Pets, Flowers-102, Food-101, FGVC Aircraft, DTD, EuroSAT, UCF-101), DSPT achieves 81.10% accuracy under 40% symmetric noise, 79.19% under 60%, and 74.22% under 80%, consistently outperforming the strongest baseline NLPrompt (80.56%, 77.43%, 68.82%) by 0.5–5.4 percentage points. Under pair-flip noise, DSPT reaches 81.97% (20%), 80.67% (30%), and 79.25% (40%), again leading NLPrompt (81.56%, 80.79%, 77.73%). Under extreme noise (90% sym / 80% pair on Caltech-101 and DTD), DSPT maintains 86.68% and 80.73% on Caltech-101, while CoOp collapses to 53.75% and 10.53% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DSPT outperforms CoOp by 3–16 percentage points across all noise settings, with the largest gap (16.45 pts) at 80% symmetric noise where CoOp degrades to 57.77% while DSPT holds at 74.22%. Against LogitNorm, DSPT leads by 5.8–10.8 points, as LogitNorm's static ℓ₂-normalization fails to adapt to per-sample gradient magnitudes. Compared to the closest competitor NLPrompt (which uses optimal-transport pseudo-labels and MAE loss), DSPT wins by 0.4–5.4 points on average, with the only near-tie being 30% pair-flip noise (80.67% vs. 80.79%, a 0.12 pt difference). In the extreme-noise ablation (Table 2), DSPT surpasses NLPrompt by 0.4–2.8 points on Caltech-101 and DTD under 90% sym / 80% pair noise, while CoOp, Smoothing, and LogitNorm all drop below 13% on pair-flip. Against other bounded-loss baselines (NCE, Bootstrap, LogitClip, GCE) in Table 3 and Table 5, DSPT leads by 2–5+ points on Caltech-101 and Oxford Pets, and on CIFAR-10 it achieves 77.77% at 50% sym noise versus 64.77% for GCE and 74.56% for LogitClip.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on CoOp (Zhou et al., 2022b) for CLIP-based prompt tuning, modifying its cross-entropy objective by inserting a second softmax normalization to create a double-softmax loss. It departs from existing noisy-label learning approaches (LogitNorm, LogitClip, NLPrompt, label smoothing) that rely on handcrafted hyperparameters, sample selection, or complex architectures, by reframing gradient vanishing—a traditionally undesirable training phenomenon—as an intrinsic noise-filtering mechanism. The work also extends the observation from Wu et al. (2023) that prompt tuning is inherently more noise-resistant than full fine-tuning, providing a principled gradient-dynamics explanation (the 'gradient surge paradox') and a minimal fix rather than a new training pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The hyperparameter-free, drop-in nature of DSPT makes it immediately practical for CLIP-based classification pipelines where labels are unreliable, avoiding the tuning burden of methods like LogitClip whose optimal threshold varies by more than an order of magnitude across datasets. However, the method is explicitly designed for confident pre-trained models and fails to transfer to training-from-scratch settings (CIFAR10 with ResNet34), limiting its generality. Open questions include whether the double-softmax mechanism extends to large vision-language models (LVLMs) for downstream noisy-label tasks, and whether self-adjustive smoothing parameters could close the small gap versus NLPrompt under moderate noise rates.
