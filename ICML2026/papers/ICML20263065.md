## Problem

Existing RL methods for few-step diffusion models require differentiable reward signals to backpropagate through the reward, excluding critical real-world signals such as human binary preferences, discrete object counts, and OCR-based text-rendering correctness. This paper addresses the open problem of reinforcing few-step generative models with arbitrary non-differentiable rewards without requiring additional ground-truth image data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263065/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt as input and produces a generated RGB image at 512×512 resolution using only 4 NFE (or 1 NFE for one-step models), trained via LoRA (rank 32) on top of pre-trained few-step backbones ranging from 0.6B (SD3.5-M) to 6B (Z-Image) parameters. Reward signals are obtained from non-differentiable evaluators (GenEval, OCR accuracy, ImageReward, HPSv3) applied to the final generated image, and the method requires no paired ground-truth images—only condition prompts and a reward function.

## Method

TDM-R1 decouples reinforcement into two jointly optimized components: a diffusion-parameterized surrogate reward and the few-step generator. The key insight is that TDM's deterministic (ODE) sampling trajectories yield unbiased per-step reward estimates via the conditional expectation r(x_t, c) = E_{p(x|x_t)}[r(x, c)], dramatically reducing reward variance compared to stochastic trajectories. The surrogate reward is trained via group-based Bradley-Terry preference optimization over noisy intermediate samples, using advantage-weighted positive/negative group partitions rather than simple pairwise comparisons. The generator is then optimized to maximize the surrogate reward while regularizing with a marginal-level reverse KL divergence against the teacher diffusion model (rather than the instance-level KL used in standard diffusion RL), which is compatible with few-step generation and avoids the blurriness caused by denoising-loss-based RL objectives. A dynamic EMA reference model stabilizes surrogate reward training, and the overall loop creates a GAN-like adversarial dynamic where the generator and surrogate reward co-adapt across iterations.

## Results

On GenEval, TDM-R1 with only 4 NFE achieves 0.92 overall score, surpassing the 80-NFE SD3.5-M base model (0.63) and the commercial SOTA GPT-4o (0.84), while approaching the 80-NFE standard diffusion RL methods Flow-GRPO (0.95) and DGPO (0.97). On visual text rendering, TDM-R1 reaches 0.95 OCR accuracy with 4 NFE, matching DGPO's 0.96 (80 NFE). On the 6B-parameter Z-Image model, TDM-R1 improves GenEval from 0.66 (100 NFE base) to 0.77 (4 NFE) and HPSv3 from 7.32 to 9.90, outperforming Z-Image-Turbo (4 NFE) on all metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TDM-R1 (4 NFE) dramatically outperforms its pre-RL few-step baseline TDM-SD3.5-M (4 NFE), improving GenEval from 0.61 to 0.92 (+31 points), and surpasses the 80-NFE SD3.5-M base model (0.63) by a wide margin. Against the commercial SOTA GPT-4o (0.84), TDM-R1 achieves a clear win at 0.92. Compared to the 80-NFE standard diffusion RL methods, TDM-R1 (0.92) trails Flow-GRPO (0.95) and DGPO (0.97) on GenEval, but does so with 20× fewer NFE (4 vs. 80) while maintaining superior out-of-domain image quality metrics (e.g., Aesthetic 5.42 vs. 5.25/5.31, DeQA 4.07 vs. 4.01/4.03). On visual text rendering, TDM-R1 (0.95 OCR) nearly matches DGPO (0.96) with 4 NFE vs. 80 NFE. On the 6B Z-Image model, TDM-R1 (0.77 GenEval, 9.90 HPSv3) outperforms both the 100-NFE Z-Image base (0.66, 7.32) and the 4-NFE Z-Image-Turbo (0.73, 9.13), demonstrating consistent gains at scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

TDM-R1 builds directly on Trajectory Distribution Matching (TDM) as its few-step backbone, leveraging TDM's deterministic sampling trajectories to enable unbiased per-step reward estimation. The surrogate reward module adapts the diffusion-parameterized reward formulation from Diffusion-DPO and the group-based preference optimization from DGPO, but reformulates them for noisy intermediate samples rather than clean images. The key departure from prior work is that existing RL methods for few-step models (Diff-Instruct++, Reward-Instruct) require differentiable rewards, while standard diffusion RL (Flow-GRPO, DGPO) produces blurry outputs when naively applied to few-step generation; TDM-R1 resolves both limitations by decoupling surrogate reward learning from generator optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TDM-R1 provides the first general-purpose RL post-training framework for few-step diffusion models that accepts arbitrary non-differentiable reward signals (human preferences, OCR accuracy, object counts), which are the most practically important reward types in real deployment. The finding that a 4-NFE model can surpass its 80-NFE base model by 29 points on GenEval, and that training on one verifiable metric synergistically improves an unrelated metric, suggests few-step models have substantial untapped capacity that RL can unlock. Open questions include the theoretical justification for the student-teacher reversal (4-step student outperforming 80-step surrogate reward model) and whether the Jensen's inequality relaxation in the surrogate reward objective can be tightened.
