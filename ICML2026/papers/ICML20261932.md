## Problem

Existing computational lithography methods either treat the multi-stage Layout→Mask→Resist Image→ADI pipeline as static point-to-point mappings or as goal-driven numerical optimization, failing to model the continuous physical evolution driven by process interventions across stages. This limits their ability to handle multi-stage optimization scenarios requiring continuous process adjustments, and makes them computationally expensive at scale. The paper introduces the first physics-informed World Model for computational lithography that unifies the pipeline as a decision-driven multi-step evolution system.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 512×512 grayscale IC layout image (6000 nm × 6000 nm field-of-view) and a 4-dimensional process parameter vector (source type, resist threshold, focus offset, exposure dose) as input, and sequentially produces mask, resist image, and ADI images along with latent intervention coefficient sequences at each stage. For inverse planning, a target ADI image is additionally provided, and the model optimizes intervention coefficients to drive the final state toward that target without requiring intermediate stage targets. All images are rasterized at 512×512 resolution from a 55 nm industrial dataset of 280K paired samples spanning 36 process configurations.

## Method

LithoDreamer comprises three components: (1) Space Prior Approximation (SPA), which estimates stage-specific orthonormal basis matrices from the top-k eigenvectors of the second-moment matrix of latent state variations between adjacent stages, constraining all interventions to physically consistent evolution directions; (2) a stochastic policy model (ViT + MLP) that predicts mean and standard deviation of intervention coefficient distributions via the reparameterization trick, then maps coefficients through the fixed SPA basis to yield feasible latent interventions; and (3) a DiT-based transition model with stage-aware MLP heads that predicts the next latent state conditioned on current state, intervention, and process parameters, using a gradient-norm-based state-gate to adaptively determine when within-stage evolution has converged. Training uses a contrastive variational optimization paradigm: a variational objective that minimizes terminal-state reconstruction over sampled intervention sequences (requiring no intermediate supervision), combined with a margin-based contrastive loss that pulls stage-matched interventions toward the target while pushing stage-mismatched projections away, enforcing stage-specific physical consistency.

## Results

On the in-domain 55 nm dataset, LithoDreamer achieves EPE of 1.58 nm (Mask), 0.96 nm (Resist Image), and 3.74 nm (ADI) for forward evolution, and 1.32 nm, 0.89 nm, and 3.28 nm for inverse planning, substantially outperforming all baselines. For OOD generalization, it maintains EPE of 1.06 nm (Resist Image) on unseen 55 nm process configurations and 1.28 nm on the cross-node 28 nm LithoSim dataset. LRC validation shows average deviations of only 1.3% (Pinch), 7.4% (Bridge), and 0.4% (EPE) from commercial simulation results, confirming manufacturing-rule consistency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LithoDreamer outperforms all six baselines on every metric across all three stages for both forward evolution and inverse planning. Among static lithography predictors, LMLitho (EPE 7.12/6.31/13.39 nm) and Unitho (8.69/8.62/5.54 nm) are the strongest, but they lack multi-step evolution and cannot model continuous process interventions; LithoDreamer reduces EPE by 4.5–11.7 nm over these. Among general world models, DriveDreamer-2 is the closest competitor on Mask (4.94 vs. 1.58 nm EPE) and ADI (5.58 vs. 3.74 nm), but it does not support multi-stage modeling, causing severe degradation on ADI mIoU (14.58% vs. 78.27%). DINO-WM and NWM perform worst overall, with EPE exceeding 17 nm on most stages, confirming that generic WM architectures without physics-informed constraints are ill-suited for multi-stage lithography. Notably, LithoDreamer is the only method that simultaneously supports both multi-step (within-stage) and multi-stage (cross-stage) evolution, which is critical for stable error propagation across the Layout→Mask→Resist Image→ADI pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the World Model paradigm—previously applied to autonomous driving (DriveDreamer-2, NWM) and embodied AI (DINO-WM)—for computational lithography, a domain where prior learning-based methods (LMLitho, Unitho, LithoNet) treat the Layout→Mask→Resist→ADI pipeline as static point-to-point mappings. The key conceptual departure is formulating lithography as a decision-driven multi-step evolution with continuous latent interventions, rather than a single forward prediction. The Space Prior Approximation (SPA) module, which estimates stage-specific orthonormal bases from latent state differences, is a novel physics-informed constraint not present in either the lithography or WM literatures, and the contrastive variational optimization paradigm addresses the specific challenge of unobserved intermediate interventions in lithography.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to unify the multi-stage lithography pipeline under a single world-model framework with interpretable, physics-constrained interventions, potentially enabling closed-loop process planning that static predictors cannot support. The publicly released 280K-sample 55 nm dataset and the demonstration of cross-node generalization (55 nm → 28 nm) provide a foundation for future research. However, the ADI EPE (3.74 nm) remains notably higher than the resist-image EPE (0.96 nm), suggesting the development-stage physics is still poorly captured, and the method's reliance on a frozen VAE encoder and fixed SPA bases may limit adaptability to substantially different process nodes or multi-patterning scenarios. Whether the contrastive variational paradigm generalizes beyond lithography to other multi-stage manufacturing or physical processes remains an open question.
