## Problem

AI-generated image detectors trained on known generators fail to generalize to unseen generators, and this gap persists even with large-scale training data. The paper identifies the root cause as insufficient coverage of the generative space: standard sampling explores only a narrow subset of the variations a diffusion model can produce, causing detectors to overfit to generator-specific artifacts rather than learning transferable discrimination cues.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260401/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the detector takes an RGB image (cropped to 224×224 for ResNet-50 or 336×336 for DINOv2-ViT-L, with multi-patch averaging for large images) and outputs a binary real/fake probability. During the PROBE training pipeline, the diffusion generator (SD 1.4 or SD 2.1) consumes a noise vector and a text prompt to synthesize 512×512 images, which are then fed to the detector as a critic signal. The final fine-tuning dataset mixes real COCO2014 images, standard diffusion samples, and boundary-induced fake samples in equal proportions per batch.

## Method

PROBE is a two-stage framework that uses the detector itself as a critic to steer a diffusion generator toward challenging regions of the generative space. In Stage 1, LoRA modules (rank 128) are applied to the U-Net attention layers, and the generator is optimized to minimize −log(1 − p(fake|x))—i.e., to produce images the detector misclassifies as real—while a VGG-19 perceptual loss (λ=1.0) constrains outputs to remain visually realistic. Deep Reward Tuning enables efficient gradient propagation by blocking backpropagation through most diffusion steps (K=5 of T=35), reducing memory cost. In Stage 2, the detector is fine-tuned on the resulting boundary-induced samples mixed with the original training data (50/50 batch weighting). A single probing round suffices, yielding +14.6% average bAcc for ResNet-50 and +6.5% for DINOv2, with diminishing returns from additional iterations.

## Results

PROBE improves average balanced accuracy (bAcc) by 14.6% for the ResNet-50 backbone (63.5% → 78.1%) and by 6.5% for the DINOv2-ViT-L backbone (87.4% → 93.9%) across seven benchmarks spanning in-house and in-the-wild settings. PROBE-DINOv2 achieves the highest bAcc on every benchmark, outperforming the second-best method (BFree, 89.3%) by 4.6% on average. On the GenImage SD 1.4 training group, PROBE-ResNet50 exceeds the second-best method (DRCT, 70.9%) by 7.2% on average, with particularly large gains on AIGI-Quality-Paradox (+22.9%) and Chameleon (+15.4%). PROBE also generalizes beyond diffusion-based generators, reaching 93.8% average bAcc on GAN and autoregressive models (Table 5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Within the GenImage SD 1.4 group, PROBE-ResNet50 (78.1% avg bAcc) outperforms all six compared methods, with the largest margins over CNNSpot (+14.7%), AIDE (+17.1%), and NPR (+16.2%), and a 7.2% advantage over the strongest baseline DRCT (70.9%). Notably, PROBE-ResNet50 achieves these gains without relying on pretrained vision-language models, unlike UnivFD, Effort, and AIDE which leverage CLIP features. In the Reconstruction Training Set group, PROBE-DINOv2 (93.9% avg) surpasses BFree (89.3%) by 4.6% and DDA (88.1%) by 5.8%, achieving the best score on all seven benchmarks. The paper further demonstrates that PROBE generalizes beyond diffusion-based generators: on GAN and autoregressive models (Table 5), PROBE-DINOv2 reaches 93.8% avg bAcc, outperforming BFree (90.1%) and the DINOv2 baseline (83.7%) by 3.7% and 10.1% respectively. A comparison with data augmentation strategies (Table 7) shows PROBE (+13.1% over baseline) substantially outperforms standard SD sampling (+4.6%), pixel-level PGD augmentation (+7.7%), and latent-level PGD augmentation (+7.3%), confirming that boundary exploration in the generative space is more effective than input-space or feature-space perturbations.

## Relation to Prior Work

PROBE sits at the intersection of AI-generated image detection and diffusion model editing. While prior data-centric approaches (BFree, DDA, DRCT) improve generalization by collecting more diverse training samples or aligning real-fake pairs, and adversarial augmentation methods (PGD in pixel or latent space) add local perturbations, PROBE instead treats the generator as a controllable exploration mechanism: it uses the detector as a critic to steer LoRA-adapted diffusion weights toward regions of the generative manifold where the detector is uncertain. The efficient optimization relies on Deep Reward Tuning (Wu et al., 2024) to avoid full backpropagation through the denoising chain, and the perceptual regularization draws on VGG-based feature matching (Johnson et al., 2016) to keep samples on the natural image manifold. Conceptually, the paper departs from the 'more data from more generators' paradigm by arguing that the binding constraint is coverage of the generative space, not data volume.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PROBE offers a practical, single-round training recipe that can be bolted onto any existing detector backbone (CNN or ViT) paired with any modifiable diffusion generator, yielding large generalization gains (14.6% avg bAcc for ResNet-50, 6.5% for DINOv2) across seven benchmarks without requiring new generator access at test time. The finding that boundary regions exposed by probing a single SD 1.4 model overlap substantially with failure modes of architecturally distinct generators (GANs, autoregressive NOVA) suggests a shared 'hard region' structure in the image space that is exploitable. Key limitations remain: the framework requires white-box access to a generator during training (ruling out black-box settings), and the BigGAN failure case shows that generators with fundamentally different spectral signatures (grid-like upsampling artifacts) fall outside the explored regions. Extending PROBE to black-box generators, video/3D generation, and multi-generator probing are natural next steps.
