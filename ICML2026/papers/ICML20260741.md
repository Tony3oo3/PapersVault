## Problem

Standard time-series forecasters follow a one-way history-to-target mapping that accumulates error and drifts over long horizons. Existing retrieval-augmented methods reuse target segments from similar training samples, creating label-neighbor dependence and diluting sample-specific future variations. The post-target continuation—the third segment of the natural training chain—remains largely unexploited as a structural prior for forecasting.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a multivariate look-back window X ∈ R^{L×C} and a pre-built train-only retrieval library of offset-aligned history–ratio pairs. It produces a forecast Ŷ ∈ R^{C×T} via an unchanged backbone, but the backbone's input is augmented with a continuation-style auxiliary sequence Z of the same shape as X, constructed online by retrieving and aggregating ratio-style transformations from the library. The two streams (X and Z) are fused at a single feature-level interface specific to each backbone architecture before prediction.

## Method

KUP-BI builds a non-parametric retrieval library from training chains by computing a ratio-style descriptor R = (F−H)⊘(H+ε·sign(H)) between each history H and its post-target continuation F, capturing relative evolution (amplitude rescaling, seasonal shifts) without any learnable summariser. At inference, the query window is last-step offset, channel-wise Pearson correlations are computed against all library keys, and Top-k candidates per channel are aggregated via temperature-controlled softmax weighting into a fused ratio matrix, which is robustly clipped (quantile-tanh) and applied to X to yield the auxiliary stream Z. Z is then fused with the backbone's intermediate features through a lightweight gated module (static or data-dependent) governed by a residual coefficient α that keeps the historical stream dominant. The entire construction is plug-and-play: the backbone architecture and training objective remain unchanged, and the library is reusable across different backbones without retraining.

## Results

KUP-BI consistently reduces MSE and MAE across all four backbones (DLinear, PatchTST, TimesNet, xPatch) on six public datasets (ETTh1, ETTh2, ETTm1, ETTm2, ILI, Exchange). The largest gains appear with DLinear: on ETTh2, MSE drops from 0.469 to 0.394 (Joint-tune, ~16% reduction), and on Exchange from 0.369 to 0.313 (~15% reduction). Even under the Plugin-only protocol (backbone hyperparameters frozen), KUP-BI yields stable improvements, e.g., DLinear on ETTh1 MSE 0.445→0.439 and xPatch on ETTh1 MSE 0.444→0.431. The added inference overhead is minimal, typically below 0.3 ms per batch for DLinear.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KUP-BI is evaluated as a plug-in on four backbone baselines: DLinear (MLP), PatchTST (Transformer), TimesNet (CNN), and xPatch (dual-stream MLP+CNN). It wins on nearly every dataset–backbone combination under the Joint-tune protocol. DLinear, having the least modeling capacity, benefits most: MSE improves by 16.0% on ETTh2 (0.469→0.394) and 15.2% on Exchange (0.369→0.313). TimesNet shows the second-largest relative gains, particularly on ILI where MSE drops from 2.438 to 2.114 (13.3% reduction), indicating that the continuation-style structural prior effectively mitigates long-horizon error accumulation on volatile series. PatchTST and xPatch, being stronger baselines, exhibit more moderate but still consistent improvements (e.g., xPatch on ETTh1: 0.444→0.409, 7.9% MSE reduction). Under the stricter Plugin-only protocol (backbone hyperparameters frozen), gains are smaller but remain positive in the majority of settings, confirming that KUP-BI does not require backbone retraining to be effective. The one notable exception is DLinear on ETTm1, where Plugin-only yields essentially no change (0.359→0.359), suggesting that on already well-modeled, low-variance series the auxiliary signal adds little.

## Relation to Prior Work

KUP-BI extends the retrieval-augmented time series forecasting line (RAFT, RAF, TS-RAG) by shifting the retrieval target from the supervised target segment to the post-target continuation — the third, previously unused segment of the natural 'history–target–post-target continuation' chain. Prior retrieval methods reuse target-level information from similar histories, which the authors argue creates shortcut learning and label-neighbor dependence; KUP-BI instead distills a ratio-style relative-change descriptor from post-target continuations, providing a weaker but more transferable structural prior. The framework is deliberately non-parametric in its continuation construction (closed-form ratio, Pearson-correlation retrieval, softmax aggregation) so it remains decoupled from the backbone and reusable across architectures, and it fuses the auxiliary stream via a lightweight gated module rather than concatenation or post-hoc correction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the post-target continuation — information that is structurally present in training trajectories but never exploited at inference — can serve as a useful inductive bias for long-horizon forecasting, and that this benefit is consistent across four architecturally diverse backbones (Transformer, MLP, CNN, hybrid) with minimal overhead. However, the gains are modest (typically 1–5% MSE reduction), the retrieval strategy does not handle phase shifts, and the authors acknowledge that backbone-specific tuning may be needed to fully unlock the method's potential, raising questions about direct applicability to large foundation models. Open directions include more sophisticated retrieval (phase-shift-aware matching), learnable encoders over the library, and whether the continuation-style signal generalizes to zero-shot or few-shot settings.
