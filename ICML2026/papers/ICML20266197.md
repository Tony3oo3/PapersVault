## Problem

The dominant LLM-as-a-Judge oversight paradigm uses probabilistic systems to supervise other probabilistic systems, inheriting their failure modes: hallucination echo chambers, vulnerability to persuasive manipulation by deceptive agents, and inability to enforce hard logical constraints. Formal verification offers deterministic guarantees, but the natural-language-to-formal-specification translation bottleneck has historically made it impractical for real-world agent trajectory oversight.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266197/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language user intent and an agent execution trajectory (a sequence of typed actions and observations, often in JSON with tool-call results) and produces a solver-backed binary safety verdict rather than a probabilistic score. Structured trajectory fields (numerical values, categorical labels, JSON schemas) are parsed deterministically without LLM involvement, while only residual semantic facts require localized LLM extraction with minimal context windows. The system also emits the synthesized Dafny specification and per-predicate violation explanations as structured feedback artifacts usable for iterative agent refinement.

## Method

FORMALJUDGE employs a bidirectional Formal-of-Thought architecture that confines all probabilistic reasoning to atomic yes/no fact extraction while delegating every logical composition step to deterministic solvers. The pipeline proceeds in four phases: (1) top-down decomposition of user intent into a set of binary, grounded, context-minimal atomic predicates; (2) context-aware extraction where deterministic parsing handles structured data and a localized LLM handles only semantic atoms with restricted context; (3) synthesis of a layered Dafny specification encoding atomic constants, derived predicates, and a conjunctive safety predicate; and (4) compilation through Boogie to Z3 for SMT-based verification, yielding a solver-checked verdict. This separation makes the final verdict follow from explicit predicates rather than holistic neural opinion, providing immunity to persuasive trajectory narratives and enabling weak-to-strong generalization where a 7B judge detects deception from 72B agents at over 90% accuracy.

## Results

FORMALJUDGE achieves the highest detection accuracy across all 21 benchmark–agent-model combinations in Table 1, with an average improvement of 16.6% over LLM-as-a-Judge baselines. Gains are largest on the Deceivers benchmark (up to +30.95 pp, e.g., 94.16% vs. 63.21% for Qwen-72B) and Agent-SafetyBench (up to +22.54 pp, e.g., 80.54% vs. 58.00% for Gemini-3.0-Pro). A 7B judge using FORMALJUDGE maintains >90% accuracy detecting deception from 72B agents, demonstrating weak-to-strong generalization. In iterative refinement, Dafny-guided feedback lifts Claude-4.5-Opus safety accuracy from 70.7% to 99.8% over three rounds, while all LLM-as-a-Judge baselines show no improvement or degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FORMALJUDGE outperforms all five LLM-as-a-Judge variants (Li et al., 2024a) across every benchmark and agent-model combination. The vanilla LLM baseline is the weakest, with FORMALJUDGE gaining +16.6% on average; the gap widens to +29–31 pp on the Deceivers benchmark where probabilistic judges are most vulnerable to fabricated agent outputs. LLM+CoT (Wei et al., 2022) does not consistently outperform the vanilla LLM—on Agent-SafetyBench it actually degrades accuracy for several agent models (e.g., −6.98 pp on Qwen-7B)—confirming that additional neural reasoning cannot compensate for the compositional-logic bottleneck. The strongest baseline, LLM+Python, narrows the gap on Agent-SafetyBench (e.g., 68.70% vs. 77.28% on GPT-4o) but still trails FORMALJUDGE by 8–22 pp, underscoring that executable-but-unverified scripts lack solver-backed guarantees. Structured information (LLM+Struct.) consistently beats prompting-only methods, validating that information presentation matters, yet the residual gap to FORMALJUDGE reveals that the core bottleneck is logical composition, not extraction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the neuro-symbolic line of work combining LLMs with symbolic solvers (Pan et al., 2023; He-Yueya et al., 2023) and recent demonstrations that LLMs can synthesize formal specifications (Ma et al., 2024; Wen et al., 2024) into a new architectural pattern for agent oversight. It departs from the dominant LLM-as-a-Judge paradigm by replacing holistic probabilistic judgment with a bidirectional Formal-of-Thought pipeline: LLMs act as specification compilers performing atomic fact extraction, while Dafny/Z3 handles all logical composition deterministically. The key conceptual departure is confining neural reasoning to binary yes/no atomic queries and delegating multi-constraint composition to SMT solving, rather than asking a single LLM call to simultaneously track and compose all constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The weak-to-strong generalization result (7B judge achieving >90% accuracy detecting deception from 72B agents) challenges the core assumption in scalable oversight that the supervisor must be at least as capable as the supervised agent, with direct practical implications for deployment cost. The iterative refinement finding—near-linear safety improvement (70.7%→99.8%) with Dafny feedback while LLM baselines plateau or degrade—suggests formal verification can serve as a precise diagnostic interface for agent self-correction. However, 80.6% of residual errors originate at the natural-language-to-formal interface, meaning the specification synthesis bottleneck remains the critical open problem; the paper provides conditional guarantees over explicit predicates rather than end-to-end guarantees over natural-language intent.
