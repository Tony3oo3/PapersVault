## Problem

Evolution Strategies, Optimization via Integration, and Consensus-Based Optimization have each been studied independently, and their connections—particularly between parametric distribution-update methods (ES/OVI) and nonparametric particle methods (CBO)—had not been formally investigated. This lack of a shared framework obscures which design choices (fitness aggregation, interaction scope) drive the behavioral differences between these optimizers and prevents principled combination of their strengths.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260016/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

All methods consume only scalar evaluations of a black-box objective F: R^d → R (no gradient access) over a population of N numerical vectors, with dimensionality ranging from 2D (BBOB) to ~1000D (Brax locomotion MLP weights) to 33D (model merging coefficients). Unimodal methods (ES, OVI, ES-OVI) output a single optimized parameter vector, while multimodal methods (cCBO, AdaPol, SchedPol) output multiple distinct optima, which is critical for the model merging application where some optima overfit a small evaluation subset while others generalize.

## Method

The paper introduces a Master Update (MU) equation in which all considered optimizers—ES, OVI, CBO, pCBO, cCBO, Consensus Hopping, and Diffusion Evolution—emerge as special cases parameterized by a fitness transformation Ψ (controlling sharpness preference), an interaction matrix K (controlling global vs. local vs. clustered consensus scope), transport coefficients (μ, λ), and noise scaling. Two key equivalences are established: CH ≡ OVI (identical particle trajectories) and DE ≈ pCBO with a time-dependent kernel. Leveraging these connections, the authors derive ES-OVI, which takes a convex combination of ES and OVI gradients with coefficient α ∈ [0,1] to explicitly trade off flat-minima robustness against sharp-minima performance, and AdaPol/SchedPol, which adaptively or on a fixed schedule interpolate between OVI's global-consensus resampling (λ=1) and cCBO's cluster-based local consensus (λ<1) to achieve multimodal optimization in high-dimensional settings.

## Results

The paper's main empirical results are presented as convergence curves rather than single headline numbers. On the BBOB benchmark (2D–20D), ES-OVI outperforms both ES and OVI on certain problems (e.g., 'Attractive Sector') while underperforming on others (e.g., 'GriewankRosebrock'), demonstrating the utility of the α interpolation knob. On Brax (~1000D locomotion), AdaPol tends to outperform other CBO variants, and OVI is competitive with CMA-ES; ES-OVI with α=0.75 performs well across tasks and achieves better robustness under action/observation noise than either pure ES or OVI. In the model merging experiment (33D, Mistral-7B), multimodal cCBO significantly outperforms unimodal OVI when initialized in a good region, while SchedPol/AdaPol remain competitive in both initialization regimes. Runtime overhead is modest: ES-OVI adds ~0.1s and AdaPol ~9.7s over 1000 iterations (population 256) relative to OpenAI-ES, which is negligible compared to function evaluation cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CMA-ES, OVI is competitive on Brax tasks (Figure 4), while CMA-ES scales poorly to D=5000 (149s vs. ~6s for other methods). Against ES (OpenAI-ES), ES-OVI with intermediate α can outperform pure ES on specific BBOB problems where ES's flat-minima preference causes convergence to suboptimal valleys, but ES retains an advantage in robustness under parameter perturbation. Against OVI, ES-OVI wins on problems where OVI's sharp-minima preference is detrimental, while OVI achieves better final performance on Brax when α is too low. Against CBO, AdaPol (which adaptively allocates particles between OVI and cCBO strategies) tends to perform better than standard CBO on Brax, and cCBO significantly outperforms OVI in model merging with good initialization. Against pCBO/cCBO, the paper shows pCBO struggles in dimensions ≥10 due to the curse of dimensionality, motivating cCBO's cluster-based approach; in model merging, cCBO with good init clearly beats OVI, while SchedPol bridges the gap when init is poor. Against DiffEvo, the paper proves DE is equivalent to pCBO with a time-dependent kernel and DDIM discretization; DE was excluded from Brax experiments due to inability to obtain satisfying results.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies parametric black-box optimizers (NES/ES, OVI) and nonparametric particle methods (CBO, pCBO, cCBO, CH, Diffusion Evolution) under a single Master Update equation, showing they differ primarily in fitness aggregation (expectation vs. log-sum-exp) and consensus scope (global vs. local vs. clustered). It departs from the Information Geometric Optimization framework (Ollivier et al., 2017), which covers parametric methods but excludes OVI and all particle-based approaches, and from prior ad-hoc combinations like SVGD+ES (Braun et al., 2025) or PSO+CMA-ES (Xu et al., 2019), by deriving hybrids (ES-OVI, AdaPol, SchedPol) directly from the structural connections rather than heuristically. Two key equivalences—CH ≡ OVI and DE ≈ pCBO with a time-dependent kernel—are shown for the first time, collapsing apparently distinct method families into a shared design space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified framework gives practitioners a principled 'knob' (the interpolation parameter α in ES-OVI, or the λ schedule in AdaPol/SchedPol) to trade off performance against robustness or to combine unimodal efficiency with multimodal exploration, which is demonstrated on Brax locomotion and LLM model merging under limited evaluation budgets. Key limitations include restriction to spherical Gaussian distributions (excluding CMA-ES and adaptive-covariance methods) and the introduction of new hyperparameters whose sensitivity varies. Open questions include extending the framework to full covariance adaptation, meta-learning the interaction matrix K_t, and whether the CH≡OVI equivalence yields new convergence guarantees for particle methods in high dimensions.
