## Problem

Existing methods for analyzing and manipulating LLM activations—PCA, sparse autoencoders, and linear steering—impose strong structural assumptions (linearity, sparsity) that cause interventions to push activations off-manifold, degrading output fluency. No prior work has trained a generative model of LLM activations that learns the activation distribution without such assumptions and can serve simultaneously as a steering prior and a feature extractor for interpretability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263059/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

GLP takes single-token residual stream activations from a fixed intermediate layer (e.g., Layer 7 of Llama1B, Layer 15 of Llama8B), standardized to zero mean and unit variance, and produces denoised activations via iterative flow-matching sampling. For on-manifold steering, the input is a steered activation (original + α·w) that is noised at t_start=0.5 and then denoised over 20 steps to project it back onto the learned manifold. For probing, the output is a set of 'meta-neurons'—scalar activations at each SwiGLU gate across all MLP blocks—extracted from a single forward pass at a chosen noise timestep. The model is unconditional, requiring no class labels or auxiliary conditioning.

## Method

GLP is a deep diffusion MLP (stack of SwiGLU blocks with residual connections, following Llama3 design) trained with a flow-matching objective: the forward process linearly interpolates between a real activation z_0 and Gaussian noise ε, and the network learns to predict the target velocity u = ε − z_0 at each timestep t, with t conditioned via multiplicative FiLM modulation of the SwiGLU gate. The key architectural choice is making the model width 2× the activation dimension with a 2× gated expansion, which the authors find critical for generation quality. The central insight is that the trained model itself—not just its generated samples—is the useful artifact: it acts as a prior for steering (an activation-space SDEdit that noises then denoises steered activations) and as an unsupervised feature encoder whose intermediate meta-neurons outperform SAE features and raw LLM neurons on 1-D probing. Across 0.5B–3.3B parameter models trained on 1B FineWeb activations, diffusion loss follows a smooth power law (L = 0.52 + 435.1·C^(−0.169)) that reliably predicts downstream steering and probing performance.

## Results

On 1-D probing across 113 binary tasks, GLP meta-neurons achieve 0.84 AUC on Llama1B and 0.87 AUC on Llama8B, outperforming the strongest baseline (raw MLP neurons at 0.79 and 0.82 respectively). For generation quality, the 3.3B GLP achieves a Frechet Distance of 0.53 on Llama1B activations (vs. 1.99 for SAE reconstruction), and GLP achieves a Delta LM Loss of 0.0513 vs. 0.1976 for SAE on Llama8B-Base. Diffusion loss follows a smooth power law L(C) = 0.52 + 435.1·C^(−0.169), and downstream steering and probing performance closely tracks this loss across 0.5B–3.3B parameter scales.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GLP outperforms SAEs across all evaluated metrics: Frechet Distance (0.53 vs. 1.99 on Llama1B), Delta LM Loss (0.0513 vs. 0.1976 on Llama8B-Base), and 1-D probing AUC (0.84 vs. 0.70 on Llama1B; 0.87 vs. 0.76 on Llama8B). Against raw LLM activations, GLP's meta-neurons beat raw layer outputs by 7 AUC points (0.84 vs. 0.77) and raw MLP neurons by 5 points (0.84 vs. 0.79) on Llama1B, and by 10 and 5 points respectively on Llama8B. Notably, the 1B-parameter GLP outperforms all raw activations from the 8B Llama model, suggesting GLP is a promising alternative to LLM scaling for interpretable representations. For steering, GLP post-processing expands the Pareto frontier over both SAE feature steering (Figure 5) and Persona Vectors (Figure 6), achieving higher concept scores at matched fluency. The paper also shows GLP's steering gains scale with compute, tracking the diffusion loss power law across 0.5B–3.3B checkpoints.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the meta-modeling literature (Schmidhuber 1992; Peebles et al. 2022; Wang et al. 2024), which has primarily focused on generatively modeling network weights, by instead modeling the distribution of residual stream activations. It builds directly on flow matching (Lipman et al., ICLR 2023) as its training objective and on SDEdit (Meng et al., ICLR 2022) as the conceptual template for its on-manifold steering procedure. Unlike SAEs and linear steering methods (DiffMean, Persona Vectors, Task Vectors) that impose sparsity or linearity assumptions, GLP learns the activation manifold without structural constraints, and unlike prior diffusion-on-activations work (Li et al. 2024; Zheng et al. 2025) that uses generated samples as conditioning signals, this paper leverages the trained meta-model itself as a prior and feature extractor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GLP provides a scalable, assumption-free alternative to SAEs and linear steering for LLM interpretability, with clean power-law scaling (irreducible error 0.52, α=0.169) that makes diffusion loss a reliable proxy for downstream utility. The finding that a 1B GLP's meta-neurons outperform raw activations from a 4× larger LLM on 1-D probing suggests generative modeling can create more disentangled representations than the source model alone. Key limitations include single-token, unconditional, single-layer modeling; open questions include whether multi-token and multi-layer extensions capture cross-position structure, whether conditioning on clean activations reduces information loss, and whether the scaling trends hold for larger source models beyond 8B.
