## Problem

Existing deep learning PPI predictors invest heavily in learning expressive protein representations but rely on generic aggregation heads (concatenation, dot products) that fail to encode the biological complementarity prior at interaction interfaces. The paper asks how to explicitly incorporate this complementarity prior—quantified by the L3 rule, where more length-3 paths between a protein pair indicate higher interaction likelihood—into a model-agnostic classification head that works even when test pairs are disconnected from the PPI network.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261245/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pair of protein embedding vectors (dimension d, produced by any frozen PPI predictor such as PIPR, GNN-PPI, or ESM2) and the PPI network topology (used only during pre-training to construct L3 pattern graphs). It outputs a single PPI likelihood score via graph-level classification of a generated prompt graph. Unusually, the pairwise prediction task is reformulated as a graph-level task: the two query embeddings plus K+1 learnable prompt nodes form a small graph of K+3 nodes and 2K+1 edges, whose edge weights are determined by a gating network's path-selection outputs.

## Method

L3-PPI operates in three stages: (1) pre-training a GIN-based surrogate on real L3 pattern graphs extracted from the PPI network to learn native pattern validity; (2) constructing an initial prompt pattern with K+1 shared learnable prompt nodes that form K candidate L3 paths between the two query proteins, then filtering paths via a Gumbel-Softmax gating network that assigns activation probabilities to each path; (3) feeding the resulting weighted prompt graph into the frozen surrogate for the final prediction. A path-number regularization loss (L_PN) enforces the L3 rule by penalizing configurations where positive pairs have too few activated paths or negative pairs have too many. Training uses a two-stage strategy—first optimizing prompt embeddings with all paths active, then jointly updating prompts and the gating network with the combined BCE + L_PN loss—making the module a lightweight, plug-and-play classification head that requires no modification to the underlying predictor.

## Results

On the interaction type prediction task (micro-F1), L3-PPI as a plug-and-play classification head improves all 8 baseline PPI predictors across 3 datasets (SHS27k, SHS148k, STRING) and 3 partitioning schemes (Random, DFS, BFS). The largest average gain is +3.88 micro-F1 over PIPR, with GNN-PPI+ reaching 87.42 on SHS27k (Random) and 95.29 on STRING (Random). Improvements are most pronounced under BFS and DFS partitioning (e.g., GNN-PPI+ gains +6.44 on SHS27k BFS, +1.26 on DFS), while Random partitioning shows smaller or occasionally negative deltas. On binary PPI prediction, GNN-PPI+ achieves the largest average gain of +6.02 micro-F1, with notable improvements on Yeast BFS (63.24→65.97) and SHS27k DFS (45.97→66.09).

## Baselines & Comparisons

L3-PPI consistently outperforms all 8 baselines in aggregate, with the largest average gains over PIPR (+3.88) and GNN-PPI (+3.56), both of which produce strong protein embeddings that the L3-PPI head can effectively exploit. The method shows its strongest advantage under BFS and DFS partitioning, where test proteins are disconnected from the training network: GNN-PPI+ gains +6.44 on SHS27k BFS and +1.26 on DFS, while PIPR+ gains +7.80 on SHS148k BFS. For weaker or already-strong baselines, gains are more modest: MAPE-PPI (the strongest baseline at 96.12 on STRING Random) sees only +1.10 average gain and slight degradation on Random splits, while SemiGNN-PPI shows mixed results (−2.36 on SHS27k Random but +8.24 on SHS27k DFS). The method also successfully integrates with protein pre-training models (ProstT5, ESM2-650M, Saprot, GearNet), achieving competitive or best-in-class scores in 3 of 9 ITP scenarios without modifying the backbone. Overall, L3-PPI wins on 27 of 36 ITP cells and 11 of 12 BPP cells, with the few losses concentrated on Random partitioning where the complementarity prior provides less discriminative signal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of graph prompt learning (GPL) and protein-protein interaction (PPI) prediction. It follows the 'prompt as graph' paradigm established by GPPt (Sun et al., 2022) and All-in-one (Sun et al., 2023), where learnable nodes are inserted into a graph structure to bridge pre-training and downstream tasks, but departs by constraining the generated graph to a specific biologically meaningful pattern (L3 paths) rather than learning arbitrary insert patterns. On the PPI side, it builds on the L3 rule from Kovács et al. (2019) and extends the empirical validation to additional path lengths and datasets, while addressing the practical limitation that handcrafted L3-path counts fail under disconnected test splits. The key conceptual departure from prior PPI predictors is treating the classification head as a first-class design problem, reformulating pairwise embedding aggregation as graph-level classification over a generated prompt pattern.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of L3-PPI as a lightweight classification head that can be attached to any frozen PPI predictor (sequence-based, structure-based, or protein language model) makes it broadly applicable without retraining expensive backbones. The biological interpretability of the generated L3 paths and the path-count regularization offer a transparent mechanism for understanding why a pair is predicted to interact, which is valuable in a domain where trust in predictions matters. Limitations include modest gains on Random partitioning, dependence on the quality of upstream representations (weak backbones see limited improvement), and the approach being validated only on PPI networks. Open questions include whether the L3-path-regularized prompt framework generalizes to other network-based prediction tasks (e.g., drug-target interaction, gene regulatory networks) and whether the optimal K value can be predicted from network properties rather than searched per dataset.
