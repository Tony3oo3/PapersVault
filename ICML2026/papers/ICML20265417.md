## Problem

LLM-based multi-agent systems incur high token costs because existing communication-structure optimization methods treat all activated edges as equally important, ignoring whether the information actually transmitted along an edge is adopted in the final decision. This lack of semantic-level credit assignment leads to inaccurate structure learning that preserves redundant or harmful communication while discarding critical reasoning paths.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265417/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a text query (e.g., a math word problem or multiple-choice question) and a set of five GPT-4o agents with domain-specific role prompts as input. It produces a final answer (numeric or text) along with a per-query pruned DAG communication graph whose edge probabilities have been tailored via virtual execution. Edge messages and the final aggregated output are encoded into 384-dimensional vectors via all-MiniLM-L6-v2, and the EPN consumes a 1920-dimensional concatenation of five such embeddings (source description, source history, query, target description, target history) to predict edge utilities without any LLM call.

## Method

AgentTailor introduces an Actor-Critic framework where the Actor parameterizes spatial and temporal edge activation probabilities via logits, an Edge Judge computes each edge's semantic utility as cosine similarity between its message embedding and the final aggregated output embedding, and a Critic (Edge Prediction Network) regresses relative contributions to enable virtual execution. A key design choice is the relative-contribution normalization: each edge's utility is subtracted from the average utility of its source node's incoming edges to eliminate positional bias, so that only genuinely incremental information receives positive credit. Training uses REINFORCE-style policy gradient with L1 sparsity regularization on the Actor, while the EPN is trained with MSE on Edge-Judge targets and then frozen once its loss drops below a threshold. Virtual execution samples edges under DAG constraints, predicts rewards via the locked EPN, and updates the Actor without LLM calls; at test time, K_v^(3) virtual tailoring steps refine edge probabilities per query before pruning the bottom p% of edges and running a single real execution.

## Results

AgentTailor achieves the best average accuracy of 91.36% across six diverse benchmarks (MMLU, GSM8K, AQuA, HumanEval, SVAMP, MultiArith), surpassing the strongest baseline AgentDropout (90.69%) by 0.67%. On general reasoning (MMLU: 86.14%) and algebraic reasoning (AQuA: 87.34%), AgentTailor leads all baselines by 1.90% and 3.25% respectively. In terms of efficiency, AgentTailor reduces total token consumption by 21.2%–61.6% compared to AgentDropout and 26.1%–70.9% compared to AgentPrune-R, while maintaining or improving accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AgentTailor outperforms all four baselines on average accuracy (91.36% vs. 90.69% AgentDropout, 89.41% AgentPrune-R, 87.43% GPTSwarm, and 81.65% AutoGen). It achieves the best per-dataset accuracy on five of six benchmarks, with the largest margins on AQuA (+3.25% over AgentDropout) and MMLU (+1.90% over AgentDropout). The sole exception is GSM8K, where AgentPrune-R (95.72%) and AgentDropout (95.63%) edge out AgentTailor (93.81%) by roughly 1.9–2.0%. Against the static AutoGen baseline, AgentTailor gains 9.71% on average, with the most dramatic improvement on HumanEval (+18.63%). Crucially, AgentTailor achieves these accuracy gains while consuming 21.2%–61.6% fewer total tokens than AgentDropout, demonstrating that semantic-aware edge judgment yields both higher quality and lower cost compared to purely structural or temporal topology optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

AgentTailor extends the spatio-temporal communication graph optimization line established by AgentPrune (Zhang et al., 2024) and AgentDropout (Wang et al., 2025b), which learn edge activation probabilities via policy gradients but treat all activated edges as equally important. The key departure is introducing semantic-level credit assignment: each edge's contribution is measured by cosine similarity between its message and the final aggregated output, normalized via a relative-contribution term to eliminate positional bias. The Edge Prediction Network (EPN) further departs from prior actor-critic formulations by serving as a semantic reward surrogate for virtual execution rather than a traditional state-value baseline, decoupling structure evaluation from costly LLM calls.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicitly modeling semantic edge contribution can reduce token consumption by 21.2%–61.6% while maintaining or slightly improving accuracy, offering a practical path toward scalable multi-agent systems. However, the EPN's moderate predictive fidelity (Pearson r ≈ 0.48) limits virtual execution to a coarse ranking signal, and the method's reliance on sentence-encoder similarity may not transfer to non-textual or highly structured domains. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger agent pools, whether richer adversarial models (multiple misleading agents, targeted edge attacks) preserve the robustness gains, and whether the moderate EPN correlation can be improved without sacrificing the lightweight design.
