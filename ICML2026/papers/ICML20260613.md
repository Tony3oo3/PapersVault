## Problem

Flow-matching policies for offline RL suffer from a critic-specific failure mode: value-guided policy improvement deforms the BC-flow's transport map, pushing latent samples toward weakly-supported, high-Q regions where the critic is unreliable. Standard pointwise flow-matching objectives are blind to this geometric deformation, and the resulting locally large Jacobians amplify critic errors and degrade the policy-improvement signal that downstream distilled actors depend on.

## Input / Output

The method consumes environment state vectors s from static offline datasets (D4RL Gym-MuJoCo, Adroit manipulation, OGBench navigation/manipulation) and produces continuous action vectors a of dimension d. The policy is a conditional flow-matching model u_θ(s, a, t) that deterministically transports a Gaussian noise sample a_0 ~ N(0,I) into an action a_1 over t ∈ [0,1], with the state s conditioning the entire trajectory. A distilled one-step actor is also trained to predict the final action directly, serving as the deployed policy.

## Method

PQL introduces two synergistic components on top of the FQL architecture. First, a Jacobian-based regularizer L_PDE = λ E[‖∇_a u_θ(s, a_t, t)‖_F²] penalizes the full Frobenius norm of the velocity field's Jacobian, constraining all local deformations (expansion, compression, shear, rotation) of the critic-guided transport map; this is estimated efficiently via Hutchinson's trace estimator using one or two random probe vectors and forward-mode JVPs, and is motivated by a Wasserstein stability bound (Theorem 4.1) showing that bounding the Jacobian norm controls the drift of the learned density path from a reference interpolation flow. Second, a Beta(α,α)-distributed timestep sampling strategy (α=3) replaces uniform sampling in the imitation loss, concentrating gradient updates on mid-trajectory segments where the trade-off between imitation fidelity and transport smoothness is most acute; this is justified by a variance-reduction argument (Proposition 4.2) showing the optimal sampling distribution is proportional to the instantaneous gradient norm. The PDE regularizer acts exclusively on the BC-flow branch, so λ does not create a trade-off with the critic-guided policy improvement term, which enters only through the one-step prediction a_1′.

## Results

On D4RL Gym-MuJoCo, PQL achieves the top or tied-for-top normalized score in 5 of 6 Medium-Expert and Medium-Replay settings, with headline scores of 112±3 on Hopper-Medium-Expert, 114±0.8 on Walker2d-Medium-Expert, and 84±1 on Walker2d-Medium-Replay. On Adroit manipulation tasks, PQL attains the best result in 11 of 12 settings, including 148±7 on Pen-Expert and 134±1 on Hammer-Expert. On OGBench, PQL achieves the top score in 8 of 10 evaluation settings, with the largest gains on long-horizon navigation (Antmaze-Large: 84±7 vs. FQL's 80±32) and dexterous manipulation (Cube-Single: 100±1, Puzzle-3x3: 19±3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PQL most dramatically outperforms the flow-based FQL (Park et al., 2025b), which collapses on Hopper (21±3 on ME vs. PQL's 112±3) and Walker2d (13±10 on ME vs. 114±0.8), suggesting that without geometric regularization the critic-guided flow becomes unstable. Against the strongest diffusion-based baselines, PQL wins on Hopper-ME (112±3 vs. SRPO's 100±14 and CNF's 109±5) and Walker2d-ME (114±0.8 vs. SRPO's 114±2 and CNF's 112±0.5), while trailing Flow (97±1) and CNF (96±0.3) on HalfCheetah-ME (90±1). On Adroit, PQL surpasses all flow-based variants (FAWAC, FBRAC, IFQL, FQL) by 5–30 points on expert datasets (e.g., Pen-Expert: 148±7 vs. FQL's 142±6; Hammer-Expert: 134±1 vs. FBRAC's 119±9) and also leads diffusion methods like IDQL (140±6) and SRPO (134±4) on Pen-Expert. On OGBench, PQL's advantage is largest in long-horizon navigation: Antmaze-Large (84±7 vs. FQL's 80±32 and FBRAC's 70±20) and Humanoid-Medium (30±10 vs. FBRAC's 25±6), while maintaining competitive scores on manipulation tasks (Cube-Single: 100±1 vs. FQL's 98±3). PQL does not uniformly dominate: CAC achieves higher scores on HalfCheetah-Medium (69±0.7 vs. 54±2) and HalfCheetah-MR (59±4 vs. 51±0.3), and Flow leads on HalfCheetah-ME, indicating that the regularization is most beneficial when the dataset contains high-quality trajectories that the critic can exploit.

## Relation to Prior Work

PQL extends FQL (Park et al., 2025b) by identifying a specific RL-specific failure mode: critic-guided policy improvement deforms the BC-flow's transport map, producing locally large Jacobians that are invisible to the pointwise flow-matching loss. The Jacobian regularizer itself is borrowed from neural-ODE regularization (Finlay et al., 2020), but the paper's conceptual contribution is diagnosing the continuity-equation violation as the mechanism of Q-induced instability and showing that effective integration requires a Beta-distributed timestep sampling strategy rather than the standard uniform sampling. The theoretical grounding via the Fokker-Planck/continuity equation and a Wasserstein stability bound (Theorem 4.1) positions the regularizer as a principled surrogate for transport regularity rather than an ad hoc penalty.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clear diagnostic for why flow-based offline RL policies can degrade under critic guidance and offers a minimal-overhead fix (two new hyperparameters, ~2 extra forward passes per update) that achieves SOTA across D4RL, Adroit, and OGBench. Open questions include whether the continuity-regularization perspective transfers to diffusion or score-based policy families, whether adaptive Beta schedules could further improve performance, and whether the Hutchinson estimator's linear scaling with action dimension becomes prohibitive in truly high-dimensional settings. The exponential dependence of the Wasserstein bound on the Jacobian norm J means the theoretical guarantee is motivational rather than tight, leaving a gap between the stability analysis and actual return optimality.
