## Problem

Mixed batching (MB) has become the default LLM inference scheduling strategy, yet the paper shows through controlled experiments that prefill-decode interference inflates MB's marginal cost above pure decode on bandwidth-constrained GPUs (crossover at ~20% decode ratio on RTX PRO 6000 vs. ~80% on H200). No prior work provides a principled, closed-form criterion for when exclusive batching (EB) should be preferred over MB, nor an adaptive scheduler that navigates this trade-off at runtime.

## Input / Output

The scheduler consumes a stream of LLM inference requests (each with random input length L and output length O), maintains sliding windows of recent output/input lengths to estimate the hazard rate h(t), mean input length μ_L, and mean output length μ_O online, and reads a one-shot per-hardware kernel-time profile to retrieve the mixed-batch marginal cost β_MB^e(r) at the current decode ratio. It outputs per-iteration scheduling decisions: the phase to execute (prefill or decode), the phase-switching threshold k* (number of idle slots that triggers a prefill phase), and the memory-safe batch size N* with a probabilistic OOM guarantee Pr(X_max > C) ≤ ε. The hybrid EB+ variant additionally outputs a mode selection (EB vs. MB) at each decision point.

## Method

The paper derives a closed-form crossover condition (Proposition 3.4) showing that EB outperforms MB when the marginal-cost gap β_MB^e − β_EB^w exceeds an amortized fixed-cost term that vanishes as O(1/N) at saturation. For the EB side, it models decode completions as a pure-death process under a hazard rate h(t), applies a fluid approximation to obtain the expected decode-phase duration, and solves the resulting throughput maximization to yield an optimal normalized switching threshold θ* = k*/N that depends only on the ratio p_0·α_p/α_d under CFR (Proposition 3.1), with a first-order IFR correction (Theorem 3.2) capturing real LLM workloads where longer-running requests become progressively more likely to complete. A memory-safe batch size N* is derived in closed form (Proposition 3.3) with a probabilistic OOM bound. The online controller EB(k̂*) estimates (p̂_0, η̂, μ̂_L) from sliding windows, solves for θ̂* and N̂* each cycle, and a KV-aware gate defers transitions when memory headroom is insufficient. The hybrid EB+ scheduler instantiates the crossover condition (Eq. 8) at runtime using EMA-smoothed occupancy N_obs and the empirically calibrated β_MB^e(r̂), switching between EB and MB without manual intervention and remaining robust under non-stationary traffic.

## Results

On the bandwidth-constrained L40S (0.864 TB/s), EB(k*) achieves 41.9% higher RPS than v1 (MB) on WildChat (14.70 vs. 10.36 RPS) with lower TTFT and TPOT. On the RTX PRO 6000 (1.792 TB/s) with Qwen3-8B, EB(k*) averages +7.9% RPS over v1 across four real-world workloads, peaking at +15.3% on ShareGPT. On the high-bandwidth H200 (4.8 TB/s), the gap narrows substantially: v1 matches or exceeds EB(k*) for Qwen3-30B-A3B (−2.9% average), confirming the bandwidth-driven crossover. The hybrid EB+ scheduler attains up to +36.4% throughput over v1 under non-stationary distribution shifts and +22.6% under concurrency shifts on the RTX PRO 6000.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two vLLM baselines: v0 (vLLM v0 exclusive-batching scheduler, equivalent to EB(k=1)) and v1 (vLLM v1 mixed-batching scheduler with decode-maximal chunked prefill). Against v0, EB(k*) consistently achieves higher throughput on both GPUs across all workloads, with the largest gaps on decode-heavy and balanced workloads (e.g., +15.3% RPS on ShareGPT, RTX PRO 6000, Qwen3-8B). Against v1 (the primary competitor), EB(k*) wins decisively on bandwidth-constrained hardware: +7.9% average RPS on RTX PRO 6000 with Qwen3-8B and +41.9% on L40S, while v1 retains its advantage on high-bandwidth H200 with the larger Qwen3-30B-A3B model (−2.9% average). The hybrid EB+ scheduler dynamically selects between EB and MB at runtime, matching or exceeding both baselines in every tested scenario: it recovers v1's low TTFT at light load (c=32) while capturing EB's throughput gains at high load (c=2048), and under non-stationary traffic it stays within ~1% of the better of v1 and EB(k*) in all four hardware×scenario cells.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on vLLM's PagedAttention infrastructure (Kwon et al., 2023) and positions itself against the three dominant LLM inference scheduling paradigms: exclusive batching (Orca, vLLM v0), mixed batching via chunked prefill (Sarathi, DeepSpeed-FastGen, now the default in vLLM v1/SGLang/TGI), and PD-disaggregated serving (DistServe, Splitwise, Mooncake). Its key departure is analytical: rather than treating MB as universally superior (as Sarathi and subsequent engines assume), it derives a closed-form crossover condition showing the EB-vs-MB optimum is governed by GPU memory bandwidth, model size, and workload composition. The fluid-approximation and hazard-rate modeling framework for deriving asymptotically optimal phase-switching thresholds is new to this scheduling context, extending prior Lagrangian analyses (Pang et al., 2025) with a tractable online controller.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that the industry-standard MB scheduler is suboptimal on bandwidth-constrained GPUs (up to 41.9% throughput loss) has immediate practical impact, particularly for deployments on export-restricted hardware where bandwidth budgets are tighter. The EB+ hybrid scheduler eliminates manual mode selection and adapts to non-stationary traffic, rivaling PD-disaggregation without extra hardware. Limitations include the single-GPU scope, first-order IFR correction, and the fluid approximation's bounded error at small batch sizes; open questions include multi-GPU generalization, SLO-aware switching, and whether FlashAttention kernels can be re-optimized for mixed batches on bandwidth-limited hardware to close the gap at the kernel level.
