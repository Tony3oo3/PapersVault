## Problem

Existing block-wise decoding methods for diffusion language models partition masked sequences into fixed-length blocks, which rigidly fragments semantic and syntactic constituents across block boundaries. This misalignment forces tightly coupled tokens to be decoded in separate blocks, increasing unmasking difficulty and degrading both generation quality and inference efficiency. No prior method adaptively aligns block boundaries with natural linguistic structure while simultaneously adjusting per-block decoding difficulty.

## Input / Output

The method operates on pretrained masked diffusion language models (LLaDA-8B-Instruct, LLaDA-1.5, Dream-v0-Base-7B) and takes a text prompt plus a fully masked token sequence of length L=512 as input, producing a generated text sequence. It is entirely training-free and works under three KV cache configurations (none, prefix, dual), with the adaptive partitioning and dynamic thresholding applied at inference time without modifying model weights.

## Method

Swordsman introduces two coupled mechanisms. First, entropy-driven adaptive block partitioning: at each decoding iteration, the model computes predictive entropy H_i at every remaining masked position, calculates entropy shifts ΔH_i = H_{i+1} − H_i between adjacent tokens, and places the next block boundary at the local maximum shift (subject to a minimum threshold τ_min=0.1 to prevent over-segmentation). This is grounded in the Entropy Reduction Hypothesis, which the paper formalizes to show that constituent boundaries exhibit large entropy shifts (log ρ where ρ = N_global/N_local ≫ 1) while intra-constituent shifts are bounded by a small δ. Second, dynamic parallel unmasking: instead of a static confidence threshold, Swordsman computes a per-block threshold τ_t that adapts at two levels — inter-block via a difficulty coefficient λ_k calibrated from historical block entropies, and intra-block via the ratio √(H̄_t/H̄_start) that progressively relaxes the threshold as entropy decays within a block. Together, these mechanisms align decoding blocks with semantic constituents and match parallelism to local uncertainty, improving both accuracy and throughput without any retraining.

## Results

On LLaDA-8B-Instruct with Dual KV cache, Swordsman achieves 81.50% accuracy on GSM8K, surpassing Fast-dLLM (75.21%) by +6.29% and AdaBlock (76.80%) by +4.70%, while also attaining the best MATH score (35.76%). On LLaDA-1.5, Swordsman reaches 84.00% GSM8K (None cache) and 43.90% HumanEval (Dual cache), the latter representing an +8.31% absolute gain over Fast-dLLM (35.59%). Inference speed is maintained or improved: under Dual cache, Swordsman delivers 73.74 TPS on LLaDA-8B-Instruct and 64.97 TPS on LLaDA-1.5, both exceeding Fast-dLLM's 72.12 and 61.30 TPS respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Fast-dLLM (fixed block size 32), Swordsman consistently wins on GSM8K across all three backbones: +6.29% on LLaDA-8B-Instruct (81.50 vs 75.21), +1.75% on Dream-v0-base-7B (76.50 vs 74.75), and +2.35% on LLaDA-1.5 (82.87 vs 80.52), while also improving MATH and HumanEval. The largest single-benchmark gain is +8.31% on HumanEval for LLaDA-1.5 (43.90 vs 35.59). Against D2F (discrete diffusion forcing), Swordsman trades a small speed advantage (D2F reaches 64.49 TPS with Prefix cache vs 58.68 for Swordsman) for substantially higher accuracy (81.50 vs 74.98 on GSM8K, 44.51 vs 36.59 on HumanEval for LLaDA-8B). Against AdaBlock (punctuation-based adaptive partitioning), Swordsman wins on GSM8K by +4.70% on LLaDA-8B-Instruct and +0.69% on LLaDA-1.5, and achieves a decisive +4.73% on HumanEval for LLaDA-1.5 (43.90 vs 39.17); AdaBlock's released implementation also suffers a critical MBPP failure (0.00%) on Dream-v0-base-7B under the unified evaluation protocol. On throughput, Swordsman edges out both Fast-dLLM and AdaBlock under Dual cache across all three models (e.g., 73.74 vs 72.12 vs 72.40 TPS on LLaDA-8B-Instruct).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the block-wise decoding paradigm for diffusion language models introduced by Fast-dLLM (Wu et al., 2025b), which established the combination of sequential inter-block denoising, parallel intra-block unmasking, and KV cache reuse. The key departure is replacing rigid fixed-length block partitioning with entropy-driven adaptive partitioning, grounded in the Entropy Reduction Hypothesis from psycholinguistics (Hale, 2006), to align block boundaries with semantic and syntactic constituents. It also supersedes the punctuation-based adaptive segmentation of AdaBlock (Lu et al., 2025) with a finer-grained, training-free mechanism that detects constituent boundaries via local maxima of entropy shifts between adjacent tokens, and introduces a two-level dynamic confidence threshold that prior block-wise methods lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Swordsman demonstrates that a simple, training-free decoding strategy can simultaneously improve both generation quality and inference speed for DLMs, addressing the core practical bottleneck that has limited adoption of diffusion language models relative to autoregressive ones. The entropy-driven partitioning is model-agnostic and requires no retraining or external parsers, making it broadly applicable across DLM architectures. However, the approach is validated only on block-wise DLMs (LLaDA, Dream), and the authors acknowledge potential need for dataset-specific hyperparameter tuning. Open questions include whether the method extends to semi-autoregressive or fully parallel DLMs, whether learned partitioning could replace the heuristic threshold, and whether the entropy-shift signal remains discriminative for very long or highly structured outputs.
