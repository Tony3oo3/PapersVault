## Problem

Black-box optimization benchmarks remain predominantly human-crafted, introducing expert bias, limiting diversity, and failing to keep pace with the rapid emergence of novel BBO tasks and learning-assisted optimizers that require large, diversified training distributions. Automating benchmark design to simultaneously ensure landscape fidelity to target tasks and strong algorithm-distinguishing power—without deep domain expertise or massive labor—remains an open challenge.

## Input / Output

EoB takes as input a target BBO task set (e.g., CoCo-BBOB, UAV path planning, or HPO instances) whose landscape features are extracted as 16-dimensional NeurELA vectors, a pool of 10 representative BBO algorithms for computing distinguishing scores, and an LLM (DeepSeek-v3.2). It outputs a Pareto front of executable Python programs, each encapsulated in a 'GeneratedFunction' class with vectorized NumPy operations, defaulting to 10-dimensional search spaces. The resulting suite of non-dominated programs collectively forms a complete benchmark balancing landscape similarity and algorithm-discrimination, and can be directly converted into autograd-compatible (PyTorch/JAX) functions for gradient-based training.

## Method

EoB formulates benchmark design as a bi-objective program evolution problem under the MOEA/D framework, simultaneously maximizing Landscape Similarity Indicator (LSI, based on NeurELA feature-space distance to target tasks) and Algorithm Distinguishing Capability (ADC, the standard deviation of min-max-normalized best-so-far values across 10 BBO algorithms). The key mechanism is a two-stage LLM-driven reproduction: a Reflection stage where the LLM compares neighborhood program pairs and performs three-fold causal analysis (successful pattern identification, beneficial gene preservation from the loser, and bi-objective tradeoff analysis) to produce structured JSON insights, followed by a Reproduction stage where the LLM synthesizes a new offspring program guided by those insights. Initialization injects one of seven landscape-construction knowledge types (e.g., asymmetric masking, polynomially warped periodicity, cross-dimensional operator interaction) to ensure population diversity, while PBI scalarization and Pareto-based selection maintain evolutionary pressure across the trade-off surface. This program-landscape co-evolution—where code-level edits are interpreted through their landscape-level consequences—distinguishes EoB from prior LLM-based heuristic search and enables interpretable, automated benchmark synthesis.

## Results

EoB-generated benchmarks achieve substantially higher performance consistency with real-world tasks than CoCo-BBOB: for UAV, EoB-UAV reaches 0.453 vs. CoCo-BBOB's 0.003 (a ~150× improvement), and for HPO, EoB-HPO reaches 0.761 vs. CoCo-BBOB's 0.694. When used as training distributions for the gradient-dependent optimizer GLHF, EoB-UAV achieves 0.848 test performance on UAV (vs. 0.838 for CoCo-BBOB) and EoB-HPO achieves 0.658 on HPO (vs. 0.514 for CoCo-BBOB). In terms of efficiency, EoB proxy benchmarks reduce per-step training time by 1–2 orders of magnitude (e.g., LDE on UAV drops from ~7 h to ~28 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is CoCo-BBOB (Hansen et al., 2010), the standard hand-crafted BBO benchmark. On UAV performance consistency, EoB-UAV (0.453) dramatically outperforms CoCo-BBOB (0.003), indicating that CoCo-BBOB's landscape is nearly uncorrelated with UAV task difficulty rankings, while EoB's bi-objective evolution produces benchmarks whose algorithm rankings closely mirror those on real UAV problems. On HPO, EoB-HPO (0.761) also beats CoCo-BBOB (0.694), though the gap is smaller since CoCo-BBOB already has moderate alignment with HPO. For the gradient-dependent GLHF optimizer, EoB-UAV (0.848) marginally exceeds CoCo-BBOB (0.838) on UAV, while EoB-HPO (0.658) substantially outperforms CoCo-BBOB (0.514) on HPO. EoB-BBOB (the generic benchmark without task-specific targeting) consistently falls between CoCo-BBOB and the task-specific EoB variants, confirming that the LSI objective steers the search toward task-relevant landscapes. Overall, EoB wins on all four scenario-metric combinations, with the largest gains on UAV where CoCo-BBOB is most misaligned.

## Relation to Prior Work

EoB builds on the MOEA/D decomposition framework (reference vectors, PBI scalarization, neighborhood-based selection) and repurposes it for LLM-driven program evolution rather than numerical optimization. It extends the LLM-based program evolution paradigm established by FunSearch, EoH, LLaMEA, and AlphaEvolve from heuristic/algorithm discovery to benchmark function design, and differentiates from the closest prior LLM-based benchmark generators (LLM-EBG, Skvorc et al.) by introducing a bi-objective formulation (landscape similarity + algorithm distinguishing capability) and validating the generated benchmarks as training distributions for learning-assisted optimizers. The landscape similarity objective leverages NeurELA's neural feature extraction, departing from traditional ELA feature sets used in prior instance-space approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EoB addresses a genuine bottleneck in the BBO community: the labor-intensive, expert-biased process of hand-crafting benchmarks that cannot keep pace with the rapid emergence of new optimization tasks and learning-assisted methods. The dramatic efficiency gains (orders of magnitude speedup over expensive real-world tasks for training learnable optimizers) and the finding that CoCo-BBOB—the field's de facto standard—shows near-zero performance consistency with realistic UAV tasks (0.003) while EoB-generated proxies achieve 0.453—suggest that the community's default evaluation paradigm may be misaligned with modern learning-based approaches. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to constrained, multi-objective, or dynamic optimization, whether it scales beyond 10D without quality degradation, and whether the results are robust across different LLM backbones and algorithm pools.
