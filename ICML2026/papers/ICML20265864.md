## Problem

Real-world assistive agents need to continuously infer and update a user's latent mental states (goals, beliefs) from sequential behavior, but existing approaches face a trilemma: model-based methods like Bayesian inverse planning are robust yet computationally prohibitive for real-time use, prompting-based LLMs are fast but lack uncertainty tracking, and learning-based methods require costly ground-truth mental state annotations that are unavailable in open-ended domains. No prior method combines the deliberative structure of model-based ToM with single-pass inference efficiency while eliminating the need for explicit mental state labels.

## Input / Output

The model receives a sequence of observed states and actions up to timestep t (rendered gridworld images or fused textual scene descriptions from VirtualHome) along with task context, and must output a probability distribution over N mental state hypotheses in a structured JSON format. In GridWorld, N=2 goal pairs with objects ordered by proximity to the human's starting position; in Household, N=10 goal particles each specifying a task type, a list of objects with counts, and a target location. These hypotheses feed an uncertainty-aware helping planner that selects assistive actions via probability-weighted expected return.

## Method

MindZero trains a multimodal LLM via Self-Supervised Reinforcement Learning (SSRL) using GRPO, where the reward is the ELBO: the probability-weighted log-likelihood of observed actions under each proposed mental state hypothesis plus the log mental-state prior, minus an entropy regularization term that prevents mode collapse. Action likelihood is estimated by a model-based planner (GridWorld) or a large LLM (Household), and the prior is either uniform or LLM-judged for commonsense plausibility, so no ground-truth mental state labels are needed. The model generates 32 rollout hypothesis sets per prompt during training, and the entropy bonus keeps the posterior diverse across competing goals. After training, the model internalizes the Bayesian inverse planning procedure and performs online mental reasoning in a single forward pass, achieving the robustness of model-based inference at a fraction of the computational cost.

## Results

On GridWorld QA, MindZero w/ Qwen3-VL-4B achieves 95.0% accuracy at only 3.6 TFLOPs, a 2.5× gain over its base model (37.7%) and surpassing the best proprietary baseline (Gemini-3-Pro, 83.7%). On Household QA, MindZero w/ Llama-3.2-3B reaches 77.8% accuracy at 4.4 TFLOPs, outperforming all open-weight baselines and approaching AutoToM w/ Gemini-3-Flash (80.2%, proprietary cost). On proactive assistance, MindZero w/ Qwen3-VL-8B achieves 24.5% speedup in GridWorld and MindZero w/ Qwen3-4B achieves 19.1% speedup in Household, while all base models and large proprietary models yield near-zero speedup in GridWorld.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Qwen3 base models (Yang et al., 2025), MindZero delivers a 2.1–2.5× accuracy gain on GridWorld QA (95.0% vs. 37.7% for Qwen3-VL-4B) and a 1.7–2.2× gain on Household QA (77.8% vs. 42.8% for Qwen3-4B), all at negligible additional inference cost. Against Llama-3 base models (Dubey et al., 2024), MindZero w/ Llama-3.2-3B improves Household QA accuracy from 34.8% to 77.8% and w/ Llama-3.1-8B from 41.3% to 76.2%, while also achieving 17.4% speedup in Household proactive assistance versus 1.7% for the base model. Against ThoughtTracing (Kim et al., 2025), MindZero w/ Llama-3.2-3B (77.8%, 4.4 TFLOPs) outperforms ThoughtTracing w/ Qwen3-235B-A22B (59.8%, 2097.9 TFLOPs) by 18 points at ~475× lower compute, and remains competitive with ThoughtTracing w/ Gemini-3-Flash (72.3%, proprietary) while using a 3B open-weight model. Against AutoToM (Zhang et al., 2025), MindZero w/ Llama-3.2-3B (77.8%, 4.4 TFLOPs) approaches AutoToM w/ Gemini-3-Flash (80.2%, proprietary) at a fraction of the cost, and clearly outperforms AutoToM with open-weight backends (e.g., 77.8% vs. 54.7% for AutoToM w/ Qwen3-4B). In proactive assistance, MindZero is the only method to achieve meaningful speedup in GridWorld (23–24.5%), where GPT-5.2 and Gemini-3-Flash yield 0% due to unstable goal predictions.

## Relation to Prior Work

MindZero sits at the intersection of Bayesian inverse planning (BIP) for Theory of Mind, variational inference (ELBO maximization), and GRPO-based reinforcement learning for language models. It departs from prior model-based ToM methods (BIP, ThoughtTracing, AutoToM) that perform explicit hypothesis search at test time, and from prior learning-based ToM methods that require ground-truth mental state annotations. The key conceptual move is to use self-supervised RL rewards—computed from action likelihood under proposed hypotheses—so that the model internalizes the BIP posterior into a single forward pass, eliminating both the annotation requirement and the test-time search cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that robust online mental reasoning can be learned as a self-supervised skill, bridging the long-standing gap between interpretable but slow model-based inference and fast but unreliable LLM prompting. The human study showing a 4B open-weight model statistically matching Gemini-3-Flash (p=0.24) is practically significant for deployable, cost-effective assistive agents. Open questions include extending to recursive multi-agent reasoning, handling longer input sequences without quadratic token growth, and validating in more open-ended real-world settings beyond VirtualHome and GridWorld.
