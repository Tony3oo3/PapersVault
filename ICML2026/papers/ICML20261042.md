## Problem

Existing provably robust Bayesian Optimization methods assume a bounded cumulative corruption budget (Σ|c_t| ≤ C), making them defenseless against even a single outlier of sufficient magnitude. This paper introduces a frequency-constrained adversary where corruptions are bounded only in the number of affected data points but may have infinite magnitude, and derives the first BO algorithms with provable sublinear regret under this stronger model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261042/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a black-box objective over a compact domain, a bounded kernel, and a corruption budget T_c (number of corrupted observations). In practice T_c is unknown and is estimated online by counting observations whose residuals fall outside the P-IMQ plateau. The output at each round is the next query point selected by maximizing a robust UCB built from the RCGP posterior mean and variance, with a confidence multiplier inflated by a factor Ψ(T_c) to account for the unobservable uncorrupted variance.

## Method

The core contribution is RCGP-UCB, which replaces the standard GP surrogate with a Robust Conjugate Gaussian Process whose posterior updates use a Plateau-Inverse Multi-Quadric (P-IMQ) weight function. Within a plateau of half-width L around a centering function g(x), observations are weighted identically to a standard GP (ensuring zero-cost robustness); outside the plateau, the weight decays as (1 + ((|y−g|−L)/c)²)^{-1/2}, bounding each corruption's influence regardless of its magnitude. Two variants are proposed: FC-RCGP-UCB fixes g≡0 and sets L from theoretical bounds, tolerating O(T^{1/4}) corruptions; A2-RCGP-UCB runs a fixed-center anchor model in parallel with an adaptive model centered at the anchor's posterior mean, breaking the adversary's feedback loop while recovering efficiency, at the cost of a weaker O(T^{1/7}) corruption tolerance. The acquisition function inflates the standard UCB multiplier by C_w√T_c and applies an observability penalty Ψ(T_c) to convert the unobservable uncorrupted variance into the observable RCGP variance.

## Results

The paper's headline theoretical result is that FC-RCGP-UCB and A2-RCGP-UCB achieve sublinear regret under frequency-constrained corruptions of possibly infinite magnitude, tolerating O(T^{1/4}) and O(T^{1/7}) corrupted points respectively, while matching GP-UCB's regret bound O(√(Tβ'_T γ_T)) in the corruption-free setting (zero-cost robustness). Empirically, across three benchmarks (Forrester 1D, CIFAR-10 ResNet hyperparameter tuning, and 36D Lunar-Lander-3), the RCGP-based algorithms achieve the lowest cumulative regret under adversarial corruption at frequency T_c = T^{1/3}, which exceeds the proven theoretical budgets. In the uncorrupted Forrester experiment, both RCGP-UCB variants produce cumulative regret curves virtually indistinguishable from GP-UCB, confirming the zero-cost property.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GP-UCB, both FC-RCGP-UCB and A2-RCGP-UCB match performance in the uncorrupted Forrester setting (validating Theorem 4.1) but substantially outperform it under adversarial corruption, where GP-UCB suffers the highest cumulative regret due to a single large outlier derailing its posterior. Against Student-t Process UCB, the RCGP-based methods achieve comparable or slightly better regret in the uncorrupted setting and clearly dominate under corruption, as the Student-t model's heavy-tailed likelihood is insufficient to handle infinite-magnitude corruptions. Against DiagnosticsGP (Martinez-Cantin et al., 2018), the RCGP methods generally win: in the corrupted Forrester experiment A2-RCGP-UCB maintains low regret while DiagnosticsGP's hard-thresholding occasionally over-discards genuine points; in the 36D Lunar-Lander-3 environment, DiagnosticsGP and Student-t actually underperform even GP-UCB due to overly conservative exploration in high dimensions, whereas RCGP-based methods still improve upon GP-UCB. The one caveat is that in individual Forrester runs with extremely large corruption magnitudes, DiagnosticsGP's complete exclusion of outliers can occasionally outperform A2-RCGP-UCB's soft down-weighting, at the cost of reduced efficiency in the uncorrupted setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the robust Bayesian optimization literature by replacing the standard bounded-cumulative-corruption model (where sum of |c_t| ≤ C) with a frequency-constrained adversary that permits corruptions of infinite magnitude. The surrogate model is the Robust Conjugate Gaussian Process (RCGP), which itself builds on generalized Bayesian inference (Bissiri et al., 2016; Knoblauch et al., 2019) to replace standard Bayes updates with robust alternatives while retaining conjugate closed forms. The acquisition function design follows the GP-UCB framework (Srinivas et al., 2010), and the P-IMQ plateau weight is a novel adaptation of the Inverse Multi-Quadric kernel specifically engineered to recover zero-cost robustness. The Anchor-Adapt algorithm (A2-RCGP-UCB) introduces a two-model architecture that resolves the tension between fixed-center robustness and adaptive-center efficiency, drawing on the adaptive centering recommendation from Laplante et al. (2025) while adding a provably stable anchor to prevent adversarial manipulation of the centering function.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide provable sublinear regret guarantees for BO under corruptions of possibly infinite magnitude, addressing a real-world vulnerability where a single catastrophic sensor failure or experimental crash can derail standard or previously-robust BO algorithms. The zero-cost robustness property—matching GP-UCB regret exactly when no corruptions are present—is a strong practical guarantee that removes the typical efficiency penalty associated with robust methods. Open questions include whether the conservative theoretical rates (T^{1/4} and T^{1/7}) can be tightened toward the empirically observed T^{1/3} tolerance, whether the corruption budget T_c can be estimated more reliably online, and whether the generalized Bayesian framework can yield further gains in other active learning or sequential decision-making settings where standard Bayes updates are fragile.
