## Problem

Standard tabular foundation models use a single linear projection per scalar cell, which the paper proves injects value variation through at most a one-dimensional channel, causing severe low-rank collapse in early layers and wasting the high-dimensional hidden space. Additionally, the conventional feature-attention → sample-attention ordering forces the first attention layer to operate on raw, unconditioned values and leaves sample-level computations disconnected from the final readout, further degrading the accuracy-efficiency trade-off.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a tabular table (N rows × D columns of numerical and categorical scalars) together with a small in-context training set, following the PFN-style paradigm where no gradient updates occur at inference. Each numerical cell is first z-score normalized per column, then expanded into a compact RBF feature vector before entering the transformer; categorical cells use standard entity embeddings. The output is a class probability distribution (classification) or a continuous prediction (regression), obtained via attention pooling over all feature tokens in the final layer rather than reading a single target token.

## Method

LimiX-2M combines two architectural changes into a 1.92M-parameter, 12-block transformer (d_model=96, H=6 heads). First, RaBEL replaces the linear scalar projection with a bank of M localized Gaussian RBF kernels per feature (centers at empirical quantiles, shared bandwidth), optionally augmented by an exponent gate that soft-assigns log-magnitude bins to produce scale-conditioned center/width scalars, raising shallow-layer effective rank by ~80% over the linear baseline. Second, the bidirectional block is reordered from F→S→N to S→N→F: sample-attention first aggregates cross-sample column statistics, an FFN compresses these signals, and feature-attention then models inter-feature relations on better-conditioned inputs; the final readout uses attention pooling so every attention computation contributes to the prediction. The model is pre-trained on synthetic data generated from hierarchical Structural Causal Models (random DAGs + functional mechanisms), and the paper provides a theoretical proof that affine scalar tokenization bounds the within-feature value subspace to rank ≤ 1 (pre-LN) and ≤ 2H (post-attention), independent of hidden width.

## Results

LimiX-2M (1.92M parameters) achieves the second-best aggregated rank across 11 tabular benchmarks, trailing only the larger LimiX-16M (16.52M). On OpenML-CC18 it attains a rank of 5.83 (vs. 8.56 for TabPFN-v2), on PFN-CLS 5.71 (vs. 7.03), and on TabArena-REG 4.54 (vs. 5.92). Inference on a 900×60 synthetic classification task takes 171.40 ms on GPU, roughly 2× faster than TabPFN-v2 and >10× faster than TabICL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LimiX-2M (1.92M) outperforms TabPFN-v2 (7.24M) on 9 of 11 benchmarks, with the largest gaps on OpenML-CC18 (5.83 vs 8.56) and TabZilla (6.58 vs 9.57); TabPFN-v2 edges ahead only on BCCO-REG (6.39 vs 6.52) and ties on TALENT-REG (7.02). Against TabICL (27.10M), LimiX-2M wins on every comparable benchmark, e.g. PFN-CLS 5.71 vs 8.79 and TabZilla 6.58 vs 10.57. Against Mitra (75.67M), LimiX-2M dominates across all 11 benchmarks, with margins as large as 10.35 on CTR23 (7.65 vs 18.00). Versus the AutoGluon ensemble, LimiX-2M wins on 9 of 11 benchmarks but concedes BCCO-REG (6.52 vs 5.13) and TabArena-REG (4.54 vs 3.46). Against XGBoost and CatBoost, LimiX-2M achieves a lower (better) rank on all 11 benchmarks, confirming that the 2M-parameter model surpasses strong tree ensembles without any ensemble overhead.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LimiX tabular foundation model family (Zhang et al., 2025) and the PFN-series synthetic pre-training paradigm (Hollmann et al., 2022; 2025) by identifying and fixing two specific architectural bottlenecks: the low-rank collapse induced by standard linear+ID scalar embeddings, and a suboptimal attention ordering (F→S→N) in bidirectional tabular transformers. The RaBEL embedding draws on classical RBF/kernel-method theory (Broomhead & Lowe, 1988; Park & Sandberg, 1991) to front-load localized nonlinearity into the tokenizer, while the S→N→F reordering is a minimal architectural change that routes all attention signals into the readout. The theoretical analysis (Proposition 3.1, Theorem B.1, Proposition B.2) formalizes why affine scalar tokenization inherently limits within-feature value sensitivity to a one-dimensional channel regardless of hidden width, providing a mechanistic explanation for the empirically observed rank collapse in TabPFN-v2 and similar models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that a 2M-parameter model can outperform 7M (TabPFN-v2) and 27M (TabICL) foundation models across 11 benchmarks while reducing both training and inference cost is practically significant for deployment-constrained settings. The low-rank collapse diagnosis and its formal proof give the field a concrete diagnostic tool (effective rank, value-sensitivity Jacobian) for identifying representational redundancy in tabular transformers, potentially guiding future architecture design beyond this specific fix. Open questions include whether RaBEL's gains persist under distribution shift and at larger scale, whether the exponent-gating mechanism generalizes to non-Gaussian value distributions, and whether the S→N→F ordering advantage holds when the number of features greatly exceeds the number of samples. The paper also notes that the full LimiX-16M still outperforms LimiX-2M, suggesting the compact variant trades some ceiling for efficiency.
