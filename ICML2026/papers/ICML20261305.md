## Problem

Existing discrete audio tokenizers rely on pretrained encoders, semantic distillation from self-supervised models, or heterogeneous CNN-based architectures, introducing fixed inductive biases that limit reconstruction fidelity and prevent unified scaling of model capacity, data, and quantization depth. The paper asks whether a simple, homogeneous, fully end-to-end learned tokenizer can scale gracefully and serve as a native discrete interface for autoregressive audio language models across speech, sound, and music.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261305/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The tokenizer consumes raw 24 kHz mono waveforms directly (no mel-spectrogram intermediates), patchifying them into fixed-dimensional vectors and progressively compressing via hierarchical patchify operations (sizes 240/2/2/2) to produce discrete RVQ tokens at 12.5 Hz with 32 quantization layers (codebook size 1024, latent dim 8). For TTS, the autoregressive model takes text tokens concatenated with a speaker prompt's transcription and audio tokens, and outputs RVQ token sequences at a user-specified depth K_infer, which the TAC decoder converts back to 24 kHz waveforms. Variable bitrate is achieved by truncating the active RVQ prefix at both training (quantizer dropout) and inference (selecting K_infer) without retraining.

## Method

TAC is a fully CNN-free architecture where both encoder and decoder are stacks of 68 causal Transformer blocks (hidden dims 768/768/768/1280, RoPE, 10 s sliding-window attention) with patchify/inverse-patchify stages for progressive temporal compression and expansion. Discretization uses 32-layer residual vector quantization with factorized codes (L2-normalized, latent dim 8) optimized via gradient descent with commitment and codebook losses, plus quantizer dropout for variable-bitrate support. All components—encoder, quantizer, decoder, multi-period and complex-STFT discriminators, and a 0.5B decoder-only LLM for audio-to-text semantic supervision—are jointly optimized end-to-end under a weighted combination of reconstruction (multi-scale mel), semantic (cross-entropy), commitment, codebook, adversarial, and feature-matching losses. For TTS, a Temporal Transformer (initialized from Qwen3-1.7B) models long-range temporal dependencies while a 4-block Depth Transformer predicts RVQ tokens autoregressively along the depth dimension; Progressive Sequence Dropout randomly truncates the active RVQ prefix during training, enabling a single model to synthesize at any bitrate by selecting the inference depth at test time.

## Results

TAC achieves state-of-the-art reconstruction across all evaluated bitrates (750–4000 bps) on speech, sound, and music. At 1000 bps, TAC reaches SIM 0.88/0.81 (EN/ZH) and PESQ-WB 2.87/2.43, outperforming XY-Tokenizer (SIM 0.85/0.79) and Mimi (SIM 0.74/0.59) at comparable bitrates. At 4000 bps, TAC attains SIM 0.97/0.93 and STFT distance 1.96/1.82 (audio/music), surpassing Mimi (SIM 0.94/0.83) and Encodec (SIM 0.86/0.75). For TTS, TAC-TTS achieves the highest speaker similarity among all compared open-source systems (EN SIM 73.1, ZH SIM 78.5 on Seed-TTS-Eval) while maintaining WER/CER below 2%/1.3%, and for ASR, TAC-ASR (1.7B) reaches EN WER 2.96 and ZH CER 3.44 without any auxiliary audio encoder.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On speech reconstruction, TAC consistently outperforms all compared tokenizers at matched or lower bitrates: at ~1000 bps it beats XY-Tokenizer by 0.03 SIM (EN) and 0.02 (ZH), and Mimi by 0.14/0.22 SIM; at ~2000 bps it surpasses Higgs Audio Tokenizer by 0.05/0.06 SIM and Qwen3 TTS Tokenizer by 0.01/0.01 SIM; at 4000 bps it leads Mimi by 0.03/0.10 SIM and Encodec by 0.11/0.18 SIM. On audio/music reconstruction, TAC at 4000 bps achieves the lowest STFT distance (1.96/1.82) and mel-loss (0.68/0.64), outperforming Mimi (2.34/2.21) and Encodec (2.33/2.17) at comparable or higher bitrates. For TTS on Seed-TTS-Eval, TAC-TTS achieves the best speaker similarity among all 17 compared systems (EN 73.1, ZH 78.5), surpassing the strongest discrete-AR baselines (HiggsAudio-v2: 67.7/74.0, FireRedTTS2: 66.5/73.6) by 5–6 points, and matching or exceeding top cascade/NAR systems (VoxCPM: 72.9/77.2, CosyVoice3: 72.0/78.1). For ASR, TAC-ASR (1.7B, no auxiliary encoder) achieves EN WER 2.96 and ZH CER 3.44, competitive with Whisper-Large-v3 (2.90/5.80) and Qwen2.5-Omni (2.37/2.56) while using a fraction of the parameters and no dedicated audio encoder. The paper also shows that end-to-end optimization is critical: partial (stage-wise) training plateaus early, whereas full end-to-end training yields sustained improvements with increased compute.

## Relation to Prior Work

TAC departs from the dominant CNN-based or hybrid CNN-Transformer codec lineage (SoundStream, EnCodec, DAC) and from tokenizers that rely on pretrained SSL encoders or semantic distillation (SpeechTokenizer, Mimi, XCodec2.0, Qwen3 TTS Tokenizer). It builds on Moshi's Temporal Transformer + Depth Transformer architecture for multi-stream autoregressive generation and on DAC's factorized vector quantization, but replaces all convolutional components with homogeneous causal Transformer blocks and eliminates all external pretrained encoders. The key conceptual departure is treating audio tokenization as a scaling problem solvable by a single end-to-end Transformer pipeline, analogous to how Transformers displaced CNNs in NLP, rather than as an architecture-engineering problem requiring handcrafted inductive biases and multi-stage training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TAC provides a unified, scalable discrete interface that could become a standard component in next-generation audio foundation models, supporting compression, understanding, and generation within one autoregressive framework. The demonstration that a purely autoregressive TTS system can surpass non-autoregressive and cascaded approaches challenges the prevailing assumption that NAR or hybrid architectures are necessary for competitive speech synthesis. Open questions include whether the per-frame latency penalty of the Transformer decoder (~106 ms vs. ~13 ms for Mimi) is acceptable for real-time streaming at scale, whether the scaling trends hold beyond the 1.2B parameter range tested, and whether the music and general-audio results (based on limited 3000-hour downstream training) will hold under larger-scale evaluation.
