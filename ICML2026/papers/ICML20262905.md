## Problem

Text-guided image-to-video (TI2V) generation models consistently fail to execute prompt-specified edits that require substantial changes to the reference image (object addition, deletion, or modification), a failure the authors term 'semantic negligence.' Through a pilot study, the paper identifies the root cause as 'visual dominance': the high-fidelity reference image induces severe attention dispersion (high entropy) in the video modality, suppressing text modality and preventing the model from incorporating new semantic information.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262905/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

AlignVid is a training-free plug-in that operates entirely within the attention layers of a pretrained diffusion-based video model (FramePack MM-DiT or Wan2.1 DiT), taking the same reference image and text prompt as the base model without any input-level modification. It produces a generated video (e.g., 832×480, 177 frames for FramePack; 800×480, 81 frames for Wan2.1) with improved prompt adherence. The method introduces negligible inference overhead (<0.1% runtime increase) and is evaluated on the newly introduced OmitI2V benchmark of 367 human-annotated image-text pairs spanning modification, addition, and deletion scenarios.

## Method

AlignVid comprises two components grounded in an energy-based analysis of attention. First, Attention Scaling Modulation (ASM) multiplies query or key embeddings by a scalar γ>1, which the paper proves is equivalent to inverse-temperature control on the row-wise softmax (Lemma 4.1), monotonically reducing attention entropy within the conditioning token block (Lemma 4.2) and sharpening focus onto high-logit, semantically relevant text tokens. Second, Guidance Scheduling (GS) gates ASM selectively: at the block level (BGS), only foreground-sensitive blocks (identified via PCA projection of attention maps + SAM2 segmentation, thresholded at foreground ratio >0.5) receive modulation; at the step level (SGS), modulation is restricted to early denoising steps (first 30%) where global semantic alignment is determined. The scaling target (Q vs. K) is architecture-aware: K is scaled in DiT-style cross-attention (Wan2.1) while either Q or K is scaled in MM-DiT self-attention (FramePack), with the optimal combination determined by ablation.

## Results

On the OmitI2V benchmark, AlignVid plugged into Wan2.1 achieves 77.20 (Modification), 79.54 (Addition), and 69.47 (Deletion) semantic alignment scores, improving over the Wan2.1 baseline by +4.85, +7.79, and +6.34 respectively, while also boosting Dynamic Degree from 46.02 to 47.04. On FramePack, the method yields +3.23 (Modification), +4.58 (Addition), and +2.07 (Deletion) with a notable Dynamic Degree jump from 20.05 to 28.53 (+8.48). Aesthetic quality drops are minimal (−0.37 to −1.49), and inference overhead is negligible (<0.1% runtime increase). The method generalizes to T2I (GenEval), image editing (ImgEdit), and T2V (VBench) tasks with consistent gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Wan2.1 (the strongest baseline on OmitI2V), AlignVid improves all three edit-type scores by 4.85–7.79 points and raises ViCLIP Addition from 20.59 to 23.30, while sacrificing only 1.49 aesthetic points. FramePack and FramePack-F1, the weakest baselines in semantic fidelity (58.14 and 58.50 on Deletion), see the largest relative gains from AlignVid (+2.07 and +2.56 on Deletion, +8.48 and +8.74 on Dynamic Degree), confirming the method's utility for models most affected by visual dominance. Skyreels-v2-I2V leads in Addition (76.64) but lags in Deletion (62.95); AlignVid on Wan2.1 surpasses it on all three edit types (77.20/79.54/69.47). EasyAnimate and HunyuanI2V trail all top methods across edit types and are not directly augmented by AlignVid in the paper. For generalization, OmniGen2 + AlignVid improves GenEval Position (0.55→0.60) and Color Attribution (0.62→0.70) but drops Counting (0.67→0.52), and on ImgEdit it gains on Add (+1.01), Remove (+0.50), and Style (+0.18) while slightly losing on Compose (−0.46).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the energy-based interpretation of attention established by Hong (2024), who showed that softmax attention corresponds to a gradient step minimizing an implicit energy function. While prior attention-manipulation methods for image/video editing (Prompt-to-Prompt, MasaCtrl, FATEZero, DiTCtrl) rely on external masks, cross-attention reweighting, or multi-prompt scheduling, AlignVid departs from this lineage by performing a minimal, training-free Q/K rescaling directly within the attention computation, grounded in the inverse-temperature equivalence. The paper also positions itself against existing I2V benchmarks (VBench, AIGCBench, EvalCrafter) that assess holistic video quality but do not isolate fine-grained edit compliance (addition, deletion, modification), motivating the new OmitI2V benchmark.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'visual dominance' diagnosis—where the reference image's high-entropy attention suppresses text tokens—provides a concrete, measurable explanation for a widely observed but poorly understood failure mode in TI2V models, and the OmitI2V benchmark (367 annotated samples) gives the field a targeted tool to track progress on edit-type prompts. The method's negligible overhead (+0.01–0.09% inference time) and architecture-agnostic plug-in design make it immediately deployable on existing production models. Open questions include whether the fixed γ=1.35 scaling factor remains optimal at larger model scales or for multi-object compositional edits, whether the slight aesthetic degradation can be eliminated without sacrificing semantic gains, and whether the attention-entropy proxy generalizes to more complex temporal reasoning tasks beyond single-edit compliance.
