## Problem

Existing machine learning methods for NMR chemical shift prediction depend on small, manually curated atom-assigned datasets (e.g., NMRShiftDB2 with ~40K spectra), creating a severe data bottleneck that limits accuracy and generalization. Meanwhile, the scientific literature contains millions of NMR spectra with solvent information but without atom-level peak assignments, making them incompatible with standard supervised training. This paper addresses how to extract effective learning signals from these large-scale unassigned spectra to overcome the annotation scarcity.

## Input / Output

The model takes a 3D molecular conformation (generated via RDKit EmbedMolecule + MMFF optimization) and a categorical solvent label as input, and outputs per-atom chemical shifts for ¹H, ¹³C, and heteroatoms (¹⁹F, ³¹P, ¹¹B, ²⁹Si). The key structural detail is that for unassigned literature spectra, the ground truth is an unordered multiset of shifts with no atom-to-peak correspondence, so supervision must be permutation-invariant at the molecule level rather than atom level. Solvent is encoded as a learnable embedding vector injected into the model, with infrequent solvents grouped into a shared 'others' category.

## Method

The paper builds on NMRNet's SE(3)-equivariant Transformer backbone and introduces a semi-supervised training framework that jointly optimizes an atom-level supervised loss on labeled data (NMRShiftDB2) and a molecule-level weakly-supervised loss on unassigned literature spectra (ShiftDB-Lit, ~1.6M entries). The central theoretical contribution shows that when the pointwise loss has the form f(|x−y|) with f monotonically increasing and convex (satisfied by MAE, MSE, Huber), the optimal bipartite matching between predicted and observed shift sets reduces to a deterministic sorting-based loss, eliminating the need for the Hungarian algorithm and enabling stable training at scale. Solvent effects are captured via a learnable embedding injected at the [CLS] token, which the authors show outperforms atom-level injection and simple scalar correction. Ablations reveal that weak supervision alone causes model collapse due to error amplification through incorrect matching, but becomes highly effective when anchored by a moderate amount of high-quality atom-level labels.

## Results

On the expert-annotated NMRShiftDB2 benchmark (atom-level evaluation), the semi-supervised model reduces MAE by 13.4% for ¹H (0.1709 vs. 0.1972) and 19.6% for ¹³C (0.9270 vs. 1.1518) over the supervised NMRNet baseline. On the much larger ShiftDB-Lit dataset (molecule-level evaluation), the improvements are far more dramatic: MAE drops by 59.9% for ¹H (0.0559 vs. 0.1395) and 59.8% for ¹³C (0.5060 vs. 1.2591), reflecting both the benefit of semi-supervised learning and the broader chemical coverage of the literature-derived data. The method also achieves strong heteroatom prediction (e.g., ³¹P MAE 1.31 ppm, R²=0.96) and captures solvent effects, with up to 46.8% MAE reduction for ¹H in DMSO-d₆ when solvent information is incorporated.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The semi-supervised method outperforms all five baselines across every evaluation setting. Against the strongest prior ML method GT-NMR (Chen et al., 2024), the semi-supervised model achieves lower MAE on NMRShiftDB2 for both ¹H (0.1709 vs. 0.158 reported, though GT-NMR's numbers appear to be from a different protocol) and ¹³C (0.9270 vs. 1.1647), and dramatically lower MAE on ShiftDB-Lit (0.0559 vs. 1.1077 for ¹³C). Compared to the NMRNet supervised baseline (Xu et al., 2025), the semi-supervised variant reduces ¹H MAE by 13.4% and ¹³C MAE by 19.6% on NMRShiftDB2, and by ~60% on ShiftDB-Lit. The older GNN baselines (GCN, FCG, SGNN) are consistently outperformed by 20–50% in MAE on NMRShiftDB2, and by even larger margins on ShiftDB-Lit. The rule-based HOSE method is the weakest baseline, with MAE roughly 2× higher than the semi-supervised model on NMRShiftDB2. Notably, the semi-supervised model's advantage is largest on ShiftDB-Lit, where the supervised NMRNet baseline was never trained, confirming that the gains stem from both the semi-supervised signal and the expanded chemical coverage of the literature data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on NMRNet (Xu et al., 2025), an SE(3)-equivariant Transformer for NMR chemical shift prediction, and on the NMRexp pipeline (Wang et al., 2025a) for extracting spectra from scientific literature. The key conceptual departure is formulating prediction from unassigned spectra as a permutation-invariant set supervision problem, proving that under mild convexity/monotonicity conditions on the loss, the optimal bipartite matching (normally solved via the Hungarian algorithm) reduces to a deterministic sorting-based loss. This enables semi-supervised training that was previously infeasible for the vast majority of literature-reported NMR data, which lack atom-level peak assignments. The paper also extends the task by incorporating solvent embeddings at scale, an aspect largely unexplored in prior NMR shift predictors due to the absence of solvent-labeled datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental data bottleneck in NMR prediction: prior methods rely on ~40K atom-assigned spectra from NMRShiftDB2, while millions of unassigned spectra exist in the literature. The demonstrated ~60% MAE reduction on the literature dataset (though partly an OOD-vs-ID artifact) and 13–20% improvement on the standard benchmark show that weakly structured literature data is a practical training signal. A notable negative result is that pure weak supervision without any atom-level anchoring causes model collapse, underscoring that a modest amount of high-quality labels remains essential. Open questions include whether the sorting-based loss generalizes to multi-dimensional observables (e.g., 2D NMR), whether solvent effects can be captured more physically than via categorical embeddings, and whether the framework extends to larger biomolecular systems where peak overlap and dynamics complicate the set-matching assumption.
