## Problem

Mimicry protection methods inject adversarial perturbations into images to prevent unauthorized style or subject imitation by text-to-image models, yet their robustness against perturbation removal attacks has never been systematically validated. This paper identifies that DDIM inversion inherently acts as a perturbation amplifier—nearly imperceptible protective perturbations induce disproportionately large reconstruction errors when images deviate from the learned semantic manifold—exposing a fundamental vulnerability in current protection strategies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263759/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single protected RGB image (512×512) as input and produces a purified image with protective perturbations removed. It operates in a black-box, data-free manner requiring no knowledge of the underlying protection algorithm or access to clean reference images. The purification residual is constrained by an ℓ∞ norm budget (default ε=24/255), ensuring the output remains perceptually indistinguishable from the input while pulling it back onto the diffusion model's learned manifold.

## Method

DIRP exploits the observation that DDIM inversion follows a deterministic denoising trajectory where reconstruction errors are governed by the Jacobian of the noise prediction network, causing off-manifold perturbations to accumulate and amplify along the trajectory. The method learns a purification residual δ by minimizing the single-step DDIM inversion reconstruction error (the discrepancy between the noise predictor's output at timestep t and at the inverted timestep t+1) under an ℓ∞ perceptual constraint. Three components enhance the optimization: Semantic Shortcut replaces the model's biased noise prediction with ground-truth Gaussian noise for distribution-aware supervision; Bidirectional Asynchronous Optimization jointly optimizes at symmetric timesteps t and T−t−1 to balance gradient magnitudes across the diffusion trajectory; and Latent Residual Refinement optionally applies VAE denoising to suppress residual high-frequency artifacts. The full pipeline uses 50 DDIM inversion timesteps and 500 optimization epochs by default, and generalizes across architectures (SD-v2.1, Flux.1-dev) and personalization paradigms (DreamBooth, LoRA, Textual Inversion).

## Results

On Textual Inversion fine-tuning (Table 1), DIRP achieves the best FID across all four degradation-based protections (Glaze: 174.64, Mist: 191.80, Anti-DreamBooth: 197.79, MetaCloak: 183.31) and the highest CLIP similarity (CS) for Mist (0.785), Anti-DreamBooth (0.787), and MetaCloak (0.783). For detection-based protections, DIRP reduces DIAGNOSIS watermark accuracy (WACC) from 99.90% (unpurified) to 1.80% and SIREN WACC from 49.70% to 0.10%, while all seven baselines retain WACC above 0.24% for DIAGNOSIS. The same trend holds on DreamBooth (Table 4) and LoRA (Table 5), where DIRP consistently achieves the lowest FID and near-zero WACC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIRP outperforms all seven baselines on FID for every degradation-based protection method; for example, on Mist it achieves FID 191.80 versus the next-best VA (211.45) and PDM-Pure (223.83), and on Anti-DreamBooth it reaches 197.79 versus PDM-Pure's 237.56. The most decisive separation appears on detection-based protections: DIRP drops DIAGNOSIS WACC to 1.80% and SIREN WACC to 0.10%, whereas every baseline retains WACC ≥ 0.24% for DIAGNOSIS (VA: 99.60%, NU: 99.80%, GrIDPure: 99.80%) and does not report SIREN WACC below 0.01%. Among baselines, VA and NU are the strongest on degradation-based FID/CS (e.g., NU achieves CS 0.822 on Glaze, slightly above DIRP's 0.812), but both fail catastrophically on watermark suppression. DA and CAT are the weakest overall, with DA actually worsening FID relative to the unpurified baseline on Glaze (231.26 vs. 196.66) and CAT showing the highest FID on Anti-DreamBooth (355.47). GrIDPure and PDM-Pure offer moderate FID improvements but leave DIAGNOSIS WACC near 100%, confirming that patch-wise or pixel-space purification cannot remove geometric watermark distortions. Overall, DIRP is the only method that simultaneously achieves top-tier generation quality and near-complete watermark removal across both protection paradigms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes DDIM inversion (Song et al., 2020), originally designed for deterministic image editing, as a purification signal for removing adversarial protective perturbations. It departs from prior perturbation removal attacks (VAE Attack, Diffusion Attack, IMPRESS, CAT, GrIDPure, PDM-Pure) that rely on stochastic reconstruction or VAE projection by exploiting the deterministic, manifold-constrained trajectory of DDIM inversion, which the authors show amplifies off-manifold perturbations through the Jacobian of the noise predictor. The theoretical grounding draws on the manifold hypothesis in diffusion models (Farghly et al., Chung et al., Mokady et al.), connecting the amplification effect to spectral norm inflation in high-curvature regions of the score field. Conceptually, the work bridges the mimicry protection literature (Glaze, Mist, Anti-DreamBooth, MetaCloak, DIAGNOSIS, SIREN) and the adversarial purification literature by identifying a previously overlooked structural vulnerability shared across all protection paradigms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that all six state-of-the-art mimicry protection methods—despite being optimized in a white-box (upper-bound) setting—are consistently broken by a single purification strategy exposes a fundamental robustness gap in current defenses that have been widely deployed (e.g., Glaze with millions of downloads). The particularly striking result is that prior attacks were essentially ineffective against detection-based protections (DIAGNOSIS WACC remained above 90% for all baselines), while DIRP reduces it below 2%, suggesting that the geometric distortion mechanism underlying detection-based methods is inherently fragile under deterministic inversion. Open questions include whether protections can be redesigned to be invariant to DDIM inversion trajectories, whether the amplification effect generalizes to newer architectures beyond U-Net-based latent diffusion, and whether the white-box assumption (perturbations optimized against the target model) is necessary for the attack to succeed in realistic black-box scenarios.
