## Problem

Existing path-specific counterfactual fairness approaches intervene on a single sensitive attribute along designated impermissible paths, but this is insufficient when confounding variables also carry ethically relevant information that influences the outcome. The paper demonstrates that single-sensitive path-specific fairness does not, in general, imply demographic parity, and asks how to construct a principled hierarchy of fairness notions that can selectively regulate multiple sensitive sources at the level of individual causal pathways.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265967/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method requires a fully specified structural causal model (DAG with structural equations), a partition of directed causal paths from sensitive variables to the outcome into admissible and impermissible sets, and for each impermissible path, a designation of which sensitive variable is the intervention target. For confounding sensitive variables, the reference value is not a fixed point but a distributional intervention drawn from P(Z | A = a′). The output is a counterfactual fair outcome Y^fair defined at the SCM semantics level; practical predictions are obtained as conditional expectations E[Y^fair | S, X] using a flexible base predictor (kNN or logistic regression in experiments).

## Method

The framework defines path-specific counterfactual semantics recursively in topological order: for each endogenous variable, parent inputs on impermissible paths are evaluated under reference (or distributional) values of the designated sensitive variable, while parent inputs on admissible paths retain factual values, yielding a nested counterfactual construction. This extends single-sensitive path-specific fairness to a multi-sensitive setting where different impermissible paths may require intervention at different sensitive variables, with confounders handled via distributional interventions Z* ~ P(Z | A = a′). The admissible path sets form a partially ordered set under inclusion, inducing a fairness hierarchy where moving to smaller admissible sets imposes stronger constraints. The key theoretical result (Theorem 3.4) shows that jointly intervening on the sensitive attribute A (fixed to a reference value) and all confounders Z (via distributional intervention) with an empty admissible path set guarantees demographic parity, overcoming the limitation of single-sensitive interventions. The framework operates at the counterfactual semantics level rather than through training-time regularization, so it can be combined with any base predictor without modifying its internal training procedure.

## Results

The multi-sensitive intervention Ŷ⁽²⁾, which enforces the strongest fairness constraint, achieves 0.757 overall accuracy while yielding identical group-wise probabilities (0.663 for both groups), satisfying demographic parity. ## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its framework against the Kusner-style counterfactual fairness baseline (Kusner et al., 2017), which uses residualized descendants and non-descendants of the sensitive attribute as predictors. On the synthetic benchmark, the Kusner baseline achieves a higher overall accuracy (0.840 vs. 0.757 for Ŷ⁽²⁾) but exhibits a substantial group-level gap in positive prediction probabilities (0.379 for A=0 vs. 0.600 for A=1, a gap of 0.221), failing to satisfy demographic parity. In contrast, the proposed multi-sensitive intervention Ŷ⁽²⁾ achieves exact demographic parity (0.663 for both groups) at the cost of 8.3 percentage points in overall accuracy. The path-specific variant Ŷ⁽³⁾ nearly matches the Kusner baseline in accuracy (0.838 vs. 0.840) while reducing the group gap to 0.138, demonstrating that selective path interventions can recover most predictive performance while still substantially narrowing disparity. On COMPAS, the proposed path-blocking approach (blocking A→Y directly) maintains accuracy at 0.656 versus 0.657 for the unconstrained baseline, indicating negligible performance loss for this particular causal structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the counterfactual fairness paradigm of Kusner et al. (2017) and the path-specific intervention semantics of Chiappa (2019) by introducing a multi-sensitive, path-dependent intervention framework grounded in the graphical hierarchy of interventions from Shpitser & Tchetgen Tchetgen (2016). The key conceptual departure is the recognition that in the presence of confounding, intervening on a single sensitive attribute along designated paths is insufficient to block all impermissible causal influences, motivating simultaneous interventions on multiple sensitive variables (including confounders) with path-dependent targets. The framework is positioned as a strict generalization: single-sensitive path-specific fairness and Kusner-style counterfactual fairness arise as special cases when the sensitive set is restricted or the admissible path set is empty.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled causal hierarchy of fairness notions that lets domain experts specify which causal channels of sensitive information should be preserved or suppressed, rather than imposing a single global constraint. The negative result (Proposition 3.2) that single-sensitive path-specific fairness does not guarantee demographic parity under confounding is a useful caution for practitioners relying on path-specific interventions. Key limitations include the dependence on a correctly specified SCM, the requirement that confounders be at least partially observed, and the absence of finite-sample estimation theory; open directions include scalable estimation, integration with representation learning, and extension to distributional criteria beyond demographic parity.
