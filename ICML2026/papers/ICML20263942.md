## Problem

Late-interaction retrievers like ColBERT store large multi-vector indices, and standard compression methods (PQ, OPQ) minimize average token reconstruction error. However, max-sim scoring depends on sparse, query-dependent 'winner' tokens, so uniform precision wastes budget on redundant tokens while small perturbations on winners can flip tight positive-vs-hard-negative margins. The paper asks: can we allocate effective representational fidelity within a document toward retrieval-critical tokens under a fixed bytes/token budget, and train the quantizer to preserve ranking structure rather than reconstruction fidelity?

## Input / Output

At query time, the system takes full-precision query token embeddings (128-dim, from a BERT-base encoder with projection head, up to 32 tokens) and scores them against a compressed document index storing only integer codes (M codes per token, K=256 entries per codebook, giving 2/4/8 bytes per token). Scoring proceeds via precomputed per-query lookup tables (m×M×K dot products, independent of corpus size) followed by integer-code max-sim aggregation, producing a ranked document list. The document context h(D) used to condition code selection is computed once at indexing time and discarded; the deployed index stores only integer token codes plus small per-document metadata (offsets, lengths).

## Method

CrossQ is an additive multi-codebook quantizer (M codebooks × K entries) whose per-token code selection is conditioned on a lightweight document context h(D) computed by a DeepSets-style permutation-invariant set encoder (two small MLPs with mean pooling). The context enables cross-token capacity allocation: a conditional MLP g([d_j; h(D)]) produces a token representation from which per-codebook logits are computed via learned projections and assignment keys, allowing the quantizer to assign higher effective fidelity to tokens more likely to become max-sim winners. Rather than minimizing reconstruction MSE, CrossQ is trained with ranking-aligned objectives: a listwise KL divergence between teacher and student score distributions over candidate sets, plus a pairwise hard-negative ranking loss with sharpness parameter γ that concentrates gradients on near-tie margin violations. Training uses temperature annealing (τ_q from 1.0 to 0.05) to progressively sharpen soft assignments toward the hard argmax codes used at deployment, with a straight-through estimator at the most aggressive 2 B/token budget. The key architectural distinction from prior conditional quantizers (QINCo2, routing methods) is that conditioning is cross-token (document-level) rather than per-token, and the training signal targets max-sim ranking preservation rather than reconstruction.

## Results

On MS MARCO dev, CrossQ achieves 0.369 MRR@10 at 2 B/token, 0.386 at 4 B/token, and 0.395 at 8 B/token, consistently outperforming all footprint-matched quantization baselines and the PLAID system reference. With light fine-tuning at 8 B/token, CrossQ reaches 0.399 MRR@10, retaining approximately 98% of the full-precision ColBERT teacher (0.408). On the nine-dataset BEIR subset at 4 B/token, CrossQ improves average nDCG@10 to 39.6 (vs. 38.7 for PLAID), and achieves 64× raw token-storage reduction (~61× including metadata, ~58× under conservative padding).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CrossQ outperforms all five baselines across every budget and metric. Against the strongest strictly footprint-matched quantization baseline (Token-wise Conditional RQ with ranking losses), CrossQ gains +0.010 MRR@10 at 2 B/token (0.369 vs. 0.359), +0.015 at 4 B/token (0.386 vs. 0.371), and +0.008 at 8 B/token (0.395 vs. 0.387). Against the PLAID system reference (candidate-matched, not retrained to each budget), CrossQ gains +0.012 MRR@10 at 2 B/token (0.369 vs. 0.357) and +0.005 at 4 B/token (0.386 vs. 0.381). Standard PQ and OPQ trail by 0.028 and 0.024 MRR@10 respectively at 2 B/token, confirming that reconstruction-optimized quantization is a weak proxy for max-sim ranking. On BEIR (9-dataset average nDCG@10 at 4 B/token), CrossQ leads PLAID by +0.9 points (39.6 vs. 38.7) and OPQ by +2.9 points (39.6 vs. 36.7), with the gap widening under domain shift. CrossQ also achieves the best p50 end-to-end latency (12.4 ms) among compressed methods at 4 B/token, versus 15.8 ms for PQ and 18.2 ms for OPQ.

## Relation to Prior Work

CrossQ sits at the intersection of late-interaction retrieval (ColBERT/ColBERTv2/PLAID) and learned vector quantization (PQ, OPQ, QINCo2). It departs from reconstruction-driven quantizers (PQ/OPQ) and token-local conditional quantizers (QINCo2) in two key ways: (i) it conditions code selection on a permutation-invariant document-level context (DeepSets-style) rather than token residuals or centroid distances, enabling cross-token fidelity allocation; and (ii) it trains with listwise score distillation and pairwise hard-negative ranking losses aligned to the max-sim operator, rather than MSE reconstruction. The additive (parallel) codebook structure, as opposed to the sequential residual structure of QINCo2, preserves the lookup-table scoring decomposition that makes late interaction efficient at query time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for memory-constrained deployment: 64× raw token-storage reduction while retaining ~98% of full-precision ColBERT MRR@10 at 8 B/token, enabling late-interaction retrieval in regimes where full-precision indices exceed available RAM. The paper's core empirical finding—that token reconstruction error correlates only weakly (Pearson r ≈ 0.33) with retrieval quality under max-sim—provides a principled argument against the default PQ/OPQ objective and motivates ranking-aligned compression more broadly. Limitations include specificity to ColBERT-style max-sim scoring (extension to dot-product or other operators requires adapted training signals), a modest ~1.3× indexing-time overhead, and the fact that gains, while consistent, are in the +0.010–0.012 MRR@10 range over strong baselines. Open questions include whether the document-context conditioning generalizes to longer documents or multi-document contexts, and whether the approach composes with learned routing (PLAID-style) for further latency reduction.
