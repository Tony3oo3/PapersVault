## Problem

Affine Maximizer Auctions (AMAs) guarantee dominant-strategy incentive compatibility and individual rationality by construction, but their VCG-style payment rule restricts each bidder's payment to a non-decreasing function of other bidders' valuations. Under negatively correlated valuations, the optimal mechanism requires payments that decrease in others' valuations, making AMAs unable to achieve optimal revenue—potentially performing arbitrarily poorly. This paper identifies this expressiveness gap and proposes a minimal modification to close it.

## Input / Output

The mechanism takes a bidding profile B = (b_1, ..., b_n) where each bidder reports valuations over m items (additive valuations in [0,1]). It produces an allocation matrix g ∈ [0,1]^{n×m} (same as the underlying AMA) and per-bidder payments p_i = p_i^AMA + p_i^Cor(V_{-i}), where the correlation-aware term p_i^Cor is a neural network conditioned only on other bidders' valuations, making it a constant from bidder i's perspective. A post-processing opt-out step can be applied to enforce strict ex-post IR by allowing any bidder with negative utility to receive zero allocation and pay zero.

## Method

CA-AMA augments the standard AMA payment with a correlation-aware term p_i^Cor(V_{-i}) that depends solely on other bidders' valuations, preserving DSIC by construction since it acts as a constant in bidder i's optimization. The optimal CA-AMA is formulated as an IR-constrained revenue maximization over AMA parameters (A, w, λ) and the p^Cor network. Training proceeds in two stages: a mutual training phase jointly optimizes AMA parameters (via softmax-approximated argmax) and p^Cor with a loss combining revenue and an adaptive IR-regret penalty, followed by a post-training phase that freezes AMA parameters and refines p^Cor using exact (non-softmax) AMA payments. Theoretical results establish continuity of the optimal p^Cor with respect to both AMA parameters and input valuations, and a uniform generalization bound on IR regret for 3-layer ReLU payment networks, supporting the feasibility of neural parameterization.

## Results

CA-AMA consistently achieves the highest revenue across all tested auction settings (2×2 through 5×5 items) under both Dirichlet Value Share and Linear Correlation Mixture distributions. Under the strongest correlation setting (Dirichlet α=0.5, 2×5), CA-AMA with ex-post IR achieves revenue 2.2694, outperforming Item-CAN (1.9685), Randomized AMA (1.8808), and VCG (0.6774) by 15%, 21%, and 235% respectively. The IR regret before post-processing remains below 0.005 in all settings, and the fraction of negative-utility samples is at most 3.9%. Training time is comparable to Randomized AMA (e.g., 27 min vs 26 min for 2×5 on A800 GPU).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CA-AMA outperforms all three baselines across every auction setting tested. Against Randomized AMA (AMenuNet, Duan et al. 2023), CA-AMA achieves 5–27% higher revenue, with the largest gaps under strong negative correlation (e.g., 21% improvement in Dirichlet α=0.5, 2×5; 27% in Linear Mixture α=0.8, 2×5 Sym). Against Item-CAN (Huo et al. 2025), which is specifically designed to exploit correlation information, CA-AMA still leads by 3–15%, confirming that the correlation-aware payment term provides additional expressiveness beyond score-based approaches. VCG, the classic baseline, is dominated by a wide margin (2–24× lower revenue), as expected given its inability to extract surplus under correlated valuations. Notably, CA-AMA's advantage grows with correlation strength: in the Dirichlet model, the gap over Randomized AMA widens from ~5% (α=2.0) to ~21% (α=0.5), and in the Linear Mixture model from ~15% (α=0.6) to ~27% (α=0.8), validating the theoretical prediction that CA-AMA's benefit is concentrated in correlated regimes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Affine Maximizer Auction (AMA) framework—originally generalized from VCG by Sandholm & Likhodedov (2015) and optimized via neural networks by LotteryAMA (Curry et al., 2023) and AMenuNet (Duan et al., 2023)—by adding a single correlation-aware payment term p_i^Cor(V_{-i}) that depends only on other bidders' valuations. This minimal modification preserves DSIC by construction while breaking the VCG-style constraint that a bidder's payment must be non-decreasing in others' valuations, directly addressing the failure mode identified by Feldman & Lavi (2021) and the Crémer-McLean intuition that full surplus extraction is possible under correlation. The two-stage training algorithm (mutual training with softmax approximation, then post-training with exact AMA payments) follows the differentiable-economics paradigm of AMenuNet but introduces an IR-regret penalty in place of the IC-regret used by RegretNet, exploiting the fact that DSIC is guaranteed structurally.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean theoretical result—that CA-AMA achieves optimal revenue in single-item auctions where deterministic AMA is arbitrarily bad, while CA-AMA = AMA for independent bidders—provides a sharp characterization of exactly when the correlation-aware term adds value. Practically, the mechanism achieves meaningful revenue gains (e.g., ~14% over Randomized AMA in the 2×2 Dirichlet α=0.5 setting) at comparable training cost, making it a drop-in upgrade for existing AMA-based pipelines. Open questions include extending the 'CA-AMA = AMA' equivalence to multi-item independent settings (acknowledged as a conjecture the authors could not prove), whether the two-option menu structure of CA-AMA (with ex-post opt-out) can be further generalized, and the ethical concerns around algorithmic price discrimination that the Impact Statement raises for deployment in ad auctions.
