## Problem

Existing machine unlearning methods operate by modifying model parameters, which is computationally expensive, unstable at scale, and often requires second-order information or multiple backward passes. No prior approach performs unlearning directly in the representation space using an information-theoretic objective, and the zero-shot setting (where only forget data is accessible) remains largely unsolved, with baselines either failing to forget or catastrophically destroying model utility.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260074/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen pre-trained encoder (ResNet-18, WideResNet-28-10, or ResNet-34) and learns a lightweight transformation f_φ over the penultimate-layer activations (a linear map in the standard setting, a 1-hidden-layer MLP in zero-shot). Given an input image x, the encoder produces z, and f_φ maps z to a transformed representation z' that feeds into the unchanged classifier head. In the standard setting both retain and forget data are available for optimization; in the zero-shot setting only forget data and basic metadata (per-class counts) are accessible, with class prototypes extracted from classifier weights via Neural Collapse.

## Method

Representation Unlearning learns a transformation f_φ in the representation space that enforces an information bottleneck: it maximizes mutual information between the transformed representation Z' and retain data while suppressing mutual information with forget data. Because these mutual-information terms are intractable, the authors derive variational upper bounds using Gaussian parameterizations, which reduce both objectives to closed-form mean-squared-error losses: the retention loss penalizes deviation of transformed retain representations from their originals, and the forget loss forces transformed forget representations to match the aggregate statistics of the full dataset. In the zero-shot variant, they replace data-driven sampling with Neural Collapse proxies (classifier weights as class-conditional centroids) and label-marginalized priors, eliminating the need for any retain data. The entire procedure optimizes only the lightweight f_φ via gradient descent with a single trade-off hyperparameter β, leaving the backbone encoder completely frozen, which yields orders-of-magnitude speed-ups (up to 757×) and minimal memory overhead compared to parameter-centric baselines.

## Results

On the class unlearning benchmark, Representation Unlearning (Rep. Unl.) achieves near-perfect forgetting (A_f ≤ 1.9%) while maintaining the highest retain accuracy across all three datasets: 93.5% on CIFAR-10, 73.4% on CIFAR-100, and 58.7% on Tiny ImageNet, with the lowest Test Cross-Entropy (0.36, 0.88, 1.80 respectively). In the zero-shot setting (no retain data access), Rep. Unl. ZS achieves 93.2% / 69.3% / 51.6% retain accuracy with A_f ≤ 4.0%, dramatically outperforming baselines that collapse (e.g., EMMN drops to 1.2% on CIFAR-100). The method also achieves up to 757× speed-up over full retraining on Tiny ImageNet and requires the lowest peak GPU memory (≈1,971 MB vs. 8,318 MB for retraining).

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the standard setting, SISA achieves perfect unlearning (0% A_f) but sacrifices significant utility (88.7% on CIFAR-10, 50.2% on Tiny ImageNet vs. Rep. Unl.'s 93.5% and 58.7%). SCRUB and NatMU perform competitively on CIFAR-10 but collapse on Tiny ImageNet (48.9% and 57.2% A_r respectively, vs. Rep. Unl.'s 58.7%), while AMUN and UNSIR fail to forget on complex datasets (A_f of 42.8% and 46.3% on Tiny ImageNet). Langevin Unlearning achieves near-perfect forgetting but catastrophically destroys utility (14.0% A_r on Tiny ImageNet). In the zero-shot setting, the gap widens dramatically: EMMN collapses to 1.2% A_r on CIFAR-100 and 0.5% on Tiny ImageNet, GKT is highly unstable (51.2±33% on CIFAR-100), and Boundary Shrinking fails to forget (20.7% A_f on CIFAR-100). Rep. Unl. ZS is the only zero-shot method that simultaneously achieves near-perfect forgetting (A_f ≤ 4.0%) and high retain accuracy (≥51.6%) across all three datasets, with the lowest Test CE in every zero-shot comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant parameter-centric paradigm in machine unlearning (SISA, SCRUB, UNSIR, Bad Teacher, AMUN, NatMU) by operating entirely in the model's representation space, learning a lightweight transformation f_φ rather than modifying network weights. Theoretically, it builds on the information bottleneck framework (Tishby et al., 2000) and variational mutual information bounds (Alemi et al., 2016; Poole et al., 2019) to derive tractable surrogates for the intractable MI objective. The zero-shot variant uniquely leverages the Neural Collapse hypothesis (Papyan et al., 2020) to substitute classifier weights for class-conditional feature centers, enabling unlearning without retain data access—a capability absent in prior zero-shot methods (GKT, EMMN, Boundary Shrinking) that rely on generative models or decision-boundary manipulation. The paper also distinguishes itself from concept erasure (LEACE, kernelized rate-distortion) by targeting holistic instance/class influence rather than shared attributes, and by using a trained transformation guided by information-theoretic bounds rather than closed-form projections.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a compelling efficiency story (up to 757× speedup, minimal GPU memory) that makes unlearning viable at scale, and the zero-shot variant addresses a practically important but underexplored regime where retain data is inaccessible. However, the method operates under a black-box threat model: the raw encoder output Z still encodes forget-set information, leaving it vulnerable to white-box recovery attacks, which limits its applicability in high-stakes privacy settings. The zero-shot variant's reliance on Neural Collapse introduces an intrinsic upper bound on retain utility that degrades gracefully but measurably as proxy alignment worsens (e.g., Tiny ImageNet NC4 = 0.94 vs. CIFAR-10 NC4 = 0.9996). Open questions include whether the same information-theoretic losses can be applied to fine-tune the full encoder (resolving the white-box limitation at higher compute cost) and whether the approach extends to LLM latent spaces, where representation geometry and collapse properties are less well-characterized.
