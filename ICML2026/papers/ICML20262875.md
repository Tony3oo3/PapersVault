## Problem

LLMs struggle with structural reasoning over serialized graph topologies, and existing solutions rely on costly GNN-LLM hybrids or fine-tuning that sacrifice generalizability. The paper identifies an unexamined internal mechanism: LLMs spontaneously reconstruct graph topology (evidenced by a 'sawtooth' attention pattern), but this latent structural understanding is actively suppressed by the attention sink, creating a representation bottleneck rooted in a conflict between semantic anisotropy and topology-aware local aggregation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a serialized graph as edge tuples (e.g., (u,v)) with a Source-Node Aggregation protocol that groups edges from the same source contiguously, plus a natural language query, and produces a standard text response. The intervention itself is entirely internal: during inference, it intercepts and modifies attention scores in identified heads/layers without altering model weights or the input/output interface. A key design choice is that the aggregation protocol is used only offline for head identification; the online sharpening remains effective even on non-aggregated (arbitrarily ordered) edge lists.

## Method

SLASH is a training-free, plug-and-play attention redistribution method with two phases. Offline, it identifies topology-aware heads via a two-stage pipeline: (1) matrix-based entropy filtering to isolate active heads in intermediate layers, and (2) structural feature extraction (top-k binarization + morphological closing) scored against a ground-truth token-level adjacency matrix, with Otsu's method setting automatic thresholds; it then calibrates a sharpening factor γ on a small disjoint dataset. Online, it applies a closed-form attention sharpening rule (Eq. 6) that scales the sink weight by γ and proportionally redistributes the recovered attention mass to all non-sink tokens. The theoretical contribution formalizes the bottleneck as a zero-sum competition between sink bias and structural aggregation under the global Softmax constraint, proving that the sink causes geometric contraction of node representations (Theorem 4.1) and quadratic decay of Dirichlet energy (Proposition 4.2), and that sharpening reverses both via a Topological Amplification Ratio ρ(λ,γ) = (1−γλ)/(1−λ).

## Results

On the GraphInstruct benchmark (9 graph reasoning tasks), SLASH improves average accuracy of general-purpose LLMs by 13–19 percentage points: Llama-3.2-3B from 0.085 to 0.214, Llama-3.1-8B from 0.282 to 0.352, Qwen3-8B from 0.174 to 0.367, and Qwen3-14B from 0.202 to 0.298. On MolecularNet (5 molecular property prediction tasks), gains are similarly large for general-purpose models (e.g., Qwen3-8B ClinTox: 0.018→0.855; Qwen3-4B HIV: 0.113→0.935), while the domain-specialized MolecularGPT shows negligible change (≤0.010). Fine-tuned GraphWiz models remain essentially unchanged (±0.005), confirming that SLASH's benefit is concentrated in models that have not already optimized structural attention through fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares SLASH-enhanced general-purpose LLMs against two categories of domain-specific baselines. On GraphInstruct, the fine-tuned GraphWiz models (Mistral-7B-RFT, LLaMA2-7B/13B-DPO) already achieve substantially higher average accuracy (0.544–0.642) than the vanilla general-purpose LLMs (0.085–0.282), and SLASH provides at most a 0.005 improvement over these fine-tuned baselines, confirming that fine-tuning has already optimized the structural attention that SLASH targets. On MolecularNet, the domain-specialized MolecularGPT (Liu et al., 2024) shows virtually no change under SLASH (e.g., BACE 0.650→0.650, BBBP 0.430→0.430), whereas SLASH dramatically boosts general-purpose models on the same tasks (e.g., Qwen3-8B ClinTox: 0.018→0.855, surpassing MolecularGPT's 0.200; Qwen3-4B HIV: 0.113→0.935, far exceeding MolecularGPT's 0.068). The key takeaway is that SLASH is most impactful for models that have not been fine-tuned on graph data, effectively closing much of the gap to domain-specific models on several tasks without any training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from both dominant paradigms for LLM-based graph learning—GNN+LLM hybrids and input-encoding strategies—by instead diagnosing an internal mechanistic failure. It repurposes the attention-sink literature (Xiao et al., 2024; Barbero et al., 2025), which was developed for topologically-flat text, to explain why LLMs underperform on serialized graphs: the sink's zero-sum competition with structural aggregation creates a representation bottleneck formalized via Dirichlet energy decay. The 'sawtooth' attention pattern revealing latent topology reconstruction is a new empirical observation not present in prior black-box treatments of LLM graph processing, and the training-free attention redistribution (SLASH) contrasts with both the architectural overhead of GNN adapters and the input-side focus of serialization research.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first mechanistic account of why LLMs struggle with graph topology, shifting the field's focus from 'how to feed graphs better' to 'how to fix what's already inside the model.' The training-free, plug-and-play nature makes it immediately applicable, though practical adoption is constrained by the need for full attention-map materialization (incompatible with FlashAttention) and the finding that gains vanish for domain-fine-tuned models. Open questions include whether the sharpening principle can be baked into pre-training, whether it extends to heterogeneous or dynamic graphs, and whether the anisotropy-vs-aggregation conflict generalizes beyond the specific serialization protocol studied.
