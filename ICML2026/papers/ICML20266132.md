## Problem

Neural operator methods on Riemannian manifolds with non-trivial topology face a fundamental tension: extrinsic spectral methods (FNO, DeepONet) are agnostic to cohomological structure and fail to preserve global invariants like circulation and net flux, while intrinsic geometric methods (GNO, MGN) suffer from over-smoothing and cannot efficiently capture high-frequency metric-dependent dynamics. No existing architecture jointly enforces exact preservation of harmonic (topological) degrees of freedom while resolving local geometric details governed by the Riemannian metric and material tensors, in a resolution-independent manner.

## Input / Output

The method consumes discrete differential form fields strictly typed by form degree on oriented simplicial complexes: 0-forms (scalars on vertices), 1-forms (oriented edge flows), and 2-forms (oriented face fluxes), together with source terms, boundary data, and material property tensors. The simplicial complex geometry (boundary matrices, Hodge star mass matrices) is preprocessed offline to yield a truncated Hodge Laplacian spectral basis, which is then fixed during training. Outputs are solution form fields of the same degree as the input, constrained to satisfy divergence-free conditions, flux conservation, and exact preservation of harmonic coefficients (Betti-number-determined) at every network layer.

## Method

HSD decomposes the solution operator into two orthogonally constrained branches via Hodge spectral duality. The Base branch operates in a truncated Hodge spectral subspace (harmonic + lowest-frequency modes), applying a gated MLP to spectral coefficients augmented with projected exterior-derivative and codifferential features, while hard diagonal projections freeze harmonic coefficients to exactly preserve cohomology classes. The Fiber branch lifts discrete forms to an auxiliary Euclidean grid via Whitney-form extension and kernel smoothing, applies FNO-style FFT spectral convolution to capture high-frequency metric-dominated dynamics, then projects the result back through (I − Π_base) so that only the Hodge-orthogonal complement is corrected. A lightweight commutator MLP couples spectral and geometric interaction features to compensate for the non-commutativity of topological and geometric operators, with near-zero initialization for stable training. The key architectural insight is that Hodge orthogonality provides an algebraic-level inductive bias: the two branches operate in strictly orthogonal subspaces, so the additive composition G_θ = G_base + G_fiber cannot introduce conservation-violating cross-talk between topological and geometric modes.

## Results

HSD achieves the best MSE across all three tasks: 1.08×10⁻² on External Aerodynamics (40% reduction vs. FNO-3D), 1.84×10⁻⁴ on Magnetostatics (36% reduction vs. DeepONet), and 3.56×10⁻⁴ on Toroidal Transport (36% reduction vs. FNO-3D). It also leads on all physics-consistency metrics (Enstrophy Fidelity, Energy Fidelity, Spectral Fidelity) and topological fidelity metrics (β₀ Score, IoU) across every task. On External Aerodynamics, HSD reaches 0.8423 Spectral Fidelity and 0.7658 Enstrophy Fidelity, substantially outperforming the next-best FNO-3D (0.7110 and 0.6638 respectively), confirming superior preservation of high-frequency vortex structures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HSD outperforms all five main baselines (GNO, MGN, DeepONet, Geo-FNO, FNO-3D) on every metric across all three tasks. FNO-3D is the strongest baseline overall, but HSD still reduces MSE by 40% (Ext. Aero), 36% (Magnetostatics), and 36% (Toroidal Transport) while also leading on Spectral Fidelity and topological metrics. DeepONet is competitive on Magnetostatics (MSE 2.89×10⁻⁴ vs. HSD's 1.84×10⁻⁴) but degrades sharply on External Aerodynamics (MSE 2.54×10⁻²) and Toroidal Transport (MSE 2.48×10⁻³), where its global fitting strategy fails to capture local geometric dynamics. GNO and Geo-FNO are consistently the weakest, with GNO showing particularly poor topological fidelity (S_β₀ = 0.2491 on Magnetostatics, 0.3795 on Toroidal Transport). In supplementary stress tests (Appendix Table 6), HSD also achieves the lowest relative L² error against attention-based operator architectures GNOT, ONO, and HAMLET on both Ellipsoid Aero and Torus Helmholtz benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the neural operator paradigm (FNO, DeepONet, GNO) from Euclidean domains to Riemannian manifolds with non-trivial topology by embedding discrete exterior calculus as an algebraic inductive bias. It departs from topological deep learning (simplicial neural networks, BSCNets), which targets classification or finite-step interpolation, by formulating a continuous operator-mapping problem with explicit Hodge-decomposition-based subspace splitting. The Lie-Trotter operator splitting framework from numerical ODE solvers is repurposed here as an architectural principle, decoupling topology-dominated (harmonic/low-frequency) and geometry-dominated (high-frequency) dynamics into complementary spectral and ambient-FFT branches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled neural operator architecture that exactly preserves cohomological invariants (Betti numbers, circulation, net flux) as hard algebraic constraints rather than soft loss terms, addressing a fundamental gap in applying neural operators to multiply-connected or high-genus domains. Key limitations include the requirement for fixed geometry (offline eigendecomposition per mesh), restriction to 3D manifolds, and inability to handle strong discontinuities like shocks. Open questions include extending to time-varying or non-isometrically deforming geometries via iso-spectral maps, and whether the spectral truncation dimension can be made adaptive per problem.
