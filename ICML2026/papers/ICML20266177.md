## Problem

Most self-supervised biosignal pretraining methods treat paired modalities (e.g., ECG and PPG) as exchangeable views of a shared latent state, using symmetric contrastive or reconstruction objectives. This ignores the directional, time-ordered physiological relationship—ECG initiates each heartbeat electrically while PPG records the delayed peripheral pulse—so learned representations may rely on within-modality interpolation rather than capturing cross-modal timing structure that is critical for cardiovascular health tasks.

## Input / Output

During pretraining, xMAE consumes synchronized 10-second PPG and ECG segments sampled at 100 Hz (1000 samples each). PPG is always fully visible, while ECG is masked with continuous temporal blocks covering 80–90% of the signal (curriculum schedule), and only the masked ECG positions are reconstructed. The transferable output is the PPG encoder's 256-dim token embeddings (25 tokens per segment, 2.85M parameters), which are frozen and probed with linear classifiers/regressors for 19 downstream tasks; the ECG encoder and cross-attention bridge are pretraining-only scaffolding discarded at inference.

## Method

xMAE reframes masked autoencoding as a structured inference problem under asymmetric temporal observability: PPG is always visible while ECG is masked with contiguous blocks, and a directional cross-attention layer uses ECG tokens (including learnable mask tokens) as queries and PPG tokens as keys/values, enforcing the physiological information flow from electrical activation to mechanical pulse. A curriculum masking schedule starts at 80% ECG masking and increases in 5% steps (up to 90%) whenever reconstruction loss improves by 10%, progressively suppressing within-ECG interpolation shortcuts and forcing reliance on PPG. The architecture comprises modality-specific convolutional patch encoders feeding independent Transformer encoders (2 blocks for PPG, 1 for ECG), a single cross-attention bridge, and a lightweight ECG decoder, trained with MSE loss restricted to masked ECG positions. This design makes precise temporal alignment a requirement for minimizing reconstruction error, implicitly encoding ECG-PPG timing structure (e.g., pulse transit time) into the PPG representation without explicit peak supervision.

## Results

On 19 downstream tasks across 6 datasets, xMAE (6.5M params, pretrained on 9.4k hours of paired ECG-PPG from MIMIC-III) outperforms all unimodal and multimodal baselines on 15 of 19 tasks via linear probing. Headline classification gains include Hypertension (lab) AUROC of 68.8 vs. 56.8 (Apple, best unimodal) and 56.3 (Apple-M, best multimodal), A1C detection at 65.1 vs. 52.5 (PaPaGei-P), and Ectopic Beats at 87.8 vs. 86.2 (LSM). On regression, xMAE achieves the lowest MAE for Systolic BP (free-living) at 11.60 mmHg and Diastolic BP (free-living) at 9.30 mmHg. Against open-source foundation models trained on 6–10× more data (PaPaGei: 57k hrs, AnyPPG: 200k hrs, Pulse-PPG: 55k hrs with 28.5M params), xMAE matches or exceeds performance on most tasks, suggesting the physiological inductive bias compensates for reduced data scale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

xMAE dominates the unimodal PPG baselines (MAE-1D, MSN, PaPaGei-P, Apple) across nearly all tasks, with the largest gap on Hypertension (lab): 68.8 vs. 56.8 (Apple) and 53.6 (MAE-1D), a 12-point AUROC improvement. Against multimodal baselines that also use ECG during pretraining (Apple-M, LSM, DINO, SimCLR), xMAE still leads on Hypertension (lab) by 12.5 points over Apple-M (68.8 vs. 56.3) and on A1C by 15.7 points over PaPaGei-P (65.1 vs. 52.5); it trails only on Platelets (68.6 vs. 69.2, Apple-M) and Sodium (61.7 vs. 63.1, MSN). In the open-weights comparison (Table 4), xMAE with 6.5M parameters and 9.4k pretraining hours matches or beats AnyPPG (5.8M, 200k hrs) on Ectopic Beats (87.8 vs. 89.3, close) and Systolic BP free-living (11.60 vs. 11.74), and outperforms Pulse-PPG (28.5M, 55k hrs) on Systolic BP (11.60 vs. 11.73) and Diastolic BP (9.30 vs. 9.36), demonstrating that the directional cross-modal inductive bias rivals the benefits of 6–20× more pretraining data or 4× more parameters. The ablation (Table 6) confirms that continuous masking, curriculum scheduling, and cross-reconstruction (vs. multi-reconstruction) each contribute, with cross-reconstruction alone yielding a 4.4-point gain on Hypertension (lab) over the multi-reconstruction variant.

## Relation to Prior Work

xMAE builds on the masked autoencoder paradigm (He et al., 2022) but departs fundamentally from prior multimodal biosignal pretraining (Apple-M, LSM, DINO, SimCLR) that treats ECG and PPG as exchangeable views. The key conceptual shift is reframing cross-modal reconstruction as an asymmetric inference problem: ECG is the upstream electrical signal and PPG is the delayed mechanical response, so the model uses directional cross-attention (ECG tokens query PPG tokens) rather than symmetric attention. This contrasts with prior PPG foundation models (PaPaGei, AnyPPG, Pulse-PPG) that are unimodal, and with ECG-to-PPG generation work that treats waveform fidelity as the end goal rather than a training-time scaffold for representation learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central practical finding is that injecting physiological temporal structure (directional cross-modal reconstruction with curriculum masking) into a 6.5M-parameter model pretrained on 9.4k hours of paired data yields representations competitive with or superior to models trained on 100k+ hours (AnyPPG, PaPaGei) or with 28.5M parameters (Pulse-PPG), suggesting domain inductive bias can substitute for data scale in biosignal pretraining. Open questions include the reliance on paired ECG-PPG data (limiting applicability where ECG is unavailable), the use of MSE loss which may miss fine-grained timing features like P-R intervals, and the limited interpretability of individual latent dimensions. The framework is positioned as generalizable to other temporally-ordered signal pairs (ECG-ballistocardiography, EMG-motion), but this remains untested.
