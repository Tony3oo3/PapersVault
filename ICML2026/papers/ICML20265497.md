## Problem

Existing short-term portfolio optimization methods address either transaction costs or portfolio sparsity, but rarely both simultaneously. The K-sparse transaction cost optimization (STCO) model that jointly minimizes negative return and transaction cost while enforcing a cardinality constraint is NP-hard, and its nonsmooth objective coupled with the simplex constraint makes it further intractable by standard convex or combinatorial solvers.

## Input / Output

At each time step t, the algorithm consumes the predicted relative price vector q_[t] (derived from historical prices via mean-reversion), the previous period's return-adjusted allocation ŵ_[t], a transaction cost rate γ, and a sparsity level K. It outputs a portfolio vector w_[t+1] on the m-dimensional simplex (non-negative, summing to 1) with at most K nonzero entries. The method operates fully online: only information up to period t is used, and the portfolio is rebalanced at every step, making it suitable for daily or hourly trading horizons.

## Method

The K-sparse constraint ‖w‖₀ ≤ K is bypassed by reformulating the problem as a nonsmooth DC (difference of convex) optimization using the vector K-norm ‖w‖_(K), with an exact penalty equivalence proven when the penalty parameter c exceeds 2√2 L (the Lipschitz constant of the smooth part). A Proximal Subgradient Algorithm (PSGA) is then designed: at each iteration it takes a subgradient of the vector K-norm and applies a proximal step of the convex part plus the simplex indicator. Since this proximal operator has no closed form, ADMM is embedded to compute it, yielding closed-form simplex projections and soft-thresholding updates per substep. A key theoretical contribution is a streamlined auxiliary function H(w,v) built from the Fenchel conjugate of the vector K-norm, which is shown to be semi-algebraic and satisfy the KL property with exponent 1/2, directly implying R-linear convergence of the entire PSGA sequence from any starting point.

## Results

On four real-market datasets (NYSE(O), NYSE(N), TSE, MSCI), the proposed STCO1 and STCO2 achieve the highest cumulative wealth among all transaction-cost-aware methods at 0.25% and 0.5% transaction cost rates. For example, on NYSE(O) at 0.5% cost, STCO1 reaches 6.54×10⁶ cumulative wealth versus 2.33×10⁶ for TCO1 and 1.52×10⁴ for TCO2. On TSE at 0% cost, STCO1 achieves 1216.92 versus 149 for TCO1. The paper also reports that STCO attains higher Mean Excess Return (MER) and α factor while yielding a smaller β factor (lower risk) compared to TCO and DENRPO baselines, confirming that sparsity reduces risk without sacrificing return.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SSPO and S1–S3 (sparse strategies ignoring transaction costs), STCO is dominated at 0% cost (e.g., SSPO reaches 1.06×10¹⁸ on NYSE(O) vs. STCO2's 7.59×10¹⁶) but decisively outperforms them once costs are introduced: at 0.5% on NYSE(N), SSPO drops to 0.00 while STCO2 retains 271.82. Against TCO1 and TCO2 (the primary transaction-cost-aware baselines from Li et al. 2017), STCO1/STCO2 consistently achieve higher cumulative wealth in most settings—for instance, on NYSE(O) at 0.25%, STCO1 (3.04×10¹¹) exceeds TCO1 (5.57×10⁹) by over 50×, and on TSE at 0%, STCO1 (1216.92) exceeds TCO1 (149) by 8×. Against the DENRPO doubly-regularized baselines (Yao & Zhang 2023), STCO2 matches or exceeds them on NYSE(N) at 0.5% (271.82 vs. 886.46 for DENRPO2-OLMAR, where DENRPO2-OLMAR actually leads) but STCO1 leads on TSE at 0.5% (1.49 vs. 0.91 for TCO1). The paper's risk analysis (MER, α, β factors) shows STCO achieves higher MER and α with lower |β| than both TCO and DENRPO, confirming the sparsity constraint reduces portfolio volatility while preserving return.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: transaction cost optimization (TCO) for online portfolio selection (Li et al., 2017) and sparse short-term portfolio optimization (SSPO) (Lai et al., 2018b; Luo et al., 2020). The key conceptual departure is the simultaneous enforcement of a K-sparse cardinality constraint and transaction cost minimization, which the authors note has been absent from prior literature. The DC reformulation of the K-sparse constraint is motivated by Gotoh et al. (2018), while the convergence analysis draws on the proximal DC algorithm framework of Liu et al. (2019), though with a simpler auxiliary function that yields a cleaner KL exponent of 1/2 and hence R-linear convergence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first unified model and algorithm that jointly handles transaction costs and sparsity in short-term portfolio selection, filling a gap where prior methods addressed only one of these practical concerns. The R-linear convergence guarantee for the entire sequence (not just subsequences) is a clean theoretical contribution that strengthens the practical reliability of the method. However, the approach is limited to short-term (hourly/daily) trading with mean-reversion-based predictions, and the authors themselves suggest that adaptive, data-driven sparsity via deep learning is a natural next step. The penalty parameter c must exceed a problem-dependent threshold (2√2 L), which in practice requires some tuning despite the reported insensitivity.
