## Problem

Existing spike encoding schemes for ANN-SNN conversion (rate coding, TTFS) severely underutilize the binary encoding space {0,1}^T, collapsing exponentially many spike sequences into a single value or restricting to single-spike codes. More expressive temporal encodings require complex time-varying dynamics that compromise the event-driven nature of SNNs and hinder practical deployment. The open problem is achieving maximal-capacity encoding under simple integrate-and-fire dynamics without sacrificing online (low-latency) computation.

## Input / Output

The method takes pretrained ANN weights (no retraining) and converts them to SNNs by setting each layer's spike magnitude θ to the learned QCFS scaling factor, with T = ⌈log L⌉ timesteps where L is the quantization level count. Network inputs are encoded via direct coding: real-valued activations of the first hidden layer are interpreted as constant currents fed to subsequent layers. The SNN produces the same outputs as the source ANN (class labels, detection boxes, sentiment scores) across diverse modalities including 32×32/224×224 RGB images, event streams, audio waveforms, and text sequences.

## Method

TWE treats a spike train as a temporal bit sequence, assigning each spike an exponentially decaying weight 2^(T−t) through a simple recursive integration o[t] = 2·u[t−1] + z[t], which exactly recovers the weighted sum after T steps. The paper identifies a fundamental temporal mismatch: since ANN activations are dominated by small values, spikes skew toward later timesteps, causing input current to lag behind the ideal firing schedule. Two complementary mechanisms resolve this: temporal relaxation delays firing decisions by T_r steps (scaling the threshold by 2^(T_r) to compensate for exponential amplification), and threshold relaxation lowers the firing threshold to αθ (optimal α = 0.5) while introducing negative spikes to prevent overshooting. Theoretical analysis proves the encoding error equals −u[T+T_r]/2^(T_r), the residual membrane potential converges to a uniform invariant measure on [−βθ, βθ], and the feasible input space volume grows as 2^(T_r) with delay.

## Results

TWE achieves negligible conversion loss (|ΔAcc.| ≤ 0.1%) with only 3–5 timesteps across all benchmarks. On CIFAR-10, TWE reaches 96.34% (ResNet-18) and 95.66% (VGG-16) with 3 steps, outperforming Fast-SNN (95.57%, 7 steps) and QCFS (95.92%, 16 steps). On ImageNet, TWE attains 81.59% (ViT-S/16) and 83.72% (ViT-L/16) with 5 steps, versus TTFSFormer's 81.38% requiring 4096 steps. Energy consumption on VGG-16/CIFAR-10 is 0.1602 mJ for TWE, roughly half that of rate coding (0.3247 mJ) and close to the TTFS lower bound (0.1085 mJ).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TWE consistently outperforms rate-coding baselines on both accuracy and latency: on CIFAR-10 it beats Fast-SNN (96.34% vs. 95.57%) and QCFS (96.34% vs. 95.92%) while using 3 timesteps instead of 7 and 16 respectively. Against TTFS-based methods, TWE achieves comparable or better accuracy with orders-of-magnitude fewer steps (e.g., 81.59% vs. 81.38% on ImageNet ViT-S/16 with 5 vs. 4096 timesteps). TWE also surpasses the differential coding method DCGS (81.59% vs. 81.11% on ViT-S/16) and burst-coding AdaFire (75.51% vs. 75.38% on ResNet-34) on ImageNet. Compared to the direct-training Ternary Spike baseline, TWE achieves higher accuracy on ImageNet (75.51% vs. 70.74%) with one fewer timestep, and on CIFAR-10 (96.34% vs. 95.80%) with one additional timestep. The only scenario where TWE does not strictly dominate is CIFAR-10 vs. Ternary Spike, where the latter uses 2 timesteps (vs. 3) but at a 0.54% accuracy cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of rate coding (the dominant ANN-SNN conversion paradigm) and temporal pattern coding (phase coding, TTFS), arguing that both underutilize the exponential encoding space {0,1}^T. The key conceptual departure is recognizing that the standard integrate-and-fire recursion o[t] = 2·u[t−1] + z[t] already implements binary-weighted spike decoding, eliminating the need for the time-varying thresholds or modulated spike magnitudes used in prior phase-coding and few-spike schemes (Rueckauer & Liu, 2021; Stöckl & Maass, 2021; Huang et al., 2025a). The paper further builds on the use of negative spikes (Hu et al., 2023; Li et al., 2022) but repurposes them as an error-correction mechanism within a threshold-relaxation framework, enabling online (low-latency) encoding where prior temporal schemes required offline, all-inputs-accumulated computation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TWE reduces the required timesteps for near-lossless ANN-SNN conversion from 7–1024+ to 3–5 across diverse architectures (CNNs, ViTs, YOLO, RoBERTa), with energy consumption approaching the TTFS theoretical lower bound while remaining fully online and event-driven. The rigorous theoretical guarantees (feasible-space volume scaling, uniform invariant measure, optimal α = 0.5) provide a principled design space rather than heuristic tuning. Open questions include whether the threshold-relaxation assumption (early input evidence predicts continued accumulation) holds for highly non-stationary or adversarial inputs, whether the condition θ ≥ Z/(1−β) can be relaxed without degrading the uniformity guarantee, and how TWE extends to recurrent SNNs or continuous-time neuromorphic hardware where the discrete ×2 recursion may not map directly.
