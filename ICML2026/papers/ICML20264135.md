## Problem

Existing certification methods for LLM outputs (semantic entropy, self-consistency, conformal prediction, information-lift certificates) treat responses as atomic units, forcing a binary accept-or-reject decision that cannot distinguish a slightly flawed summary from a completely incorrect one. For structured outputs like citation-grounded summaries, multi-hop reasoning chains, and tool-augmented responses, correctness is inherently compositional—a single hallucinated claim can invalidate an otherwise accurate response—yet no prior method provides formal risk bounds that decompose across individual claims while preserving correct content.

## Input / Output

CGRiC consumes an LLM structured output containing observable syntactic markers (citation spans, tool-call boundaries, or sentence-level factual assertions), the original query, retrieved passages, and any tool outputs. It produces a certification decision (accept, reject, or repair) accompanied by explicit per-claim risk bounds ε_i and verifier false-negative bounds η_i, and when repair is triggered, a localized corrected output that preserves all verified claims outside the repaired subgraph. Claims must be verifiable against an external source of truth independent of the generating model (retrieval+NLI, symbolic execution, or database checks), and the framework explicitly does not guarantee semantic completeness—bounds apply only to extracted claims, not to potentially missing ones.

## Method

CGRiC decomposes a structured output into a claim dependency DAG where nodes are atomic verifiable claims and directed edges encode logical dependencies (entailment, coreference, arithmetic chains, evidence reuse). For each claim, it computes an information lift (log-likelihood ratio between the full model and a systematically weakened skeleton model conditioned on local evidence only), then maps lift to a calibrated incorrectness bound ε_i via per-claim-type isotonic regression on ground-truth-labeled data. Per-claim certified failure probability is bounded by η_i·ε_i (verifier FNR × raw incorrectness), and these compose into an output-level guarantee via a union bound (always valid, no independence assumptions) or a tighter graph-structured product bound when a CI-score diagnostic validates conditional independence among sibling claims. When the composed risk exceeds the target δ, CGRiC triggers localized repair—expanded retrieval, tool re-execution, or minimal regeneration of only the highest-priority claim subgraph—rather than full abstention, with certificate validity preserved by recomputing lift and risk for repaired claims under the same calibration. The framework explicitly models extraction noise (missed and conflated claims via Clopper-Pearson bounds) and verifier imperfection, and a robustness extension certifies against the worst-case skeleton in a finite candidate family to handle baseline-model uncertainty.

## Results

At target risk δ=0.05, CGRiC (union bound) achieves certified-failure rates of 0.04–0.05 across all four primary benchmarks (HotpotQA, GovReport, GSM8K, ASQA) while reducing abstention by 31% relative to the closest atomic baseline EDFL (e.g., 0.29 vs. 0.42 on HotpotQA). The graph-structured bound further reduces abstention by 38% (0.26 on HotpotQA) when conditional independence is validated. The robust variant (Theorem 6) adds ~4pp abstention overhead but provides guarantees uniform over a family of skeleton models. A matched-component ablation confirms that ~8pp of the abstention reduction comes from the compositional risk framework itself, beyond confounding verifier strength and claim granularity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CGRiC outperforms all nine baselines on both certified-failure rate and abstention across all four primary datasets. Against the closest atomic lift-based baseline EDFL, CGRiC reduces abstention by 31% (union) to 38% (graph) while matching or exceeding its error rate (e.g., 0.05 vs. 0.07 on HotpotQA). Conformal prediction achieves comparable error but at substantially higher abstention (0.44 vs. 0.29 on HotpotQA), reflecting its inability to localize risk within structured outputs. Retrieval-augmented baselines (Self-RAG, CRAG, FacTool, CoVe) achieve lower error than atomic uncertainty methods (semantic entropy, self-consistency) but still abstain 4–13pp more than CGRiC and lack formal risk guarantees. The matched-component ablation (Table 4) isolates the compositional contribution: upgrading the verifier alone saves ~3pp abstention, adding per-claim granularity saves ~2pp, but the compositional risk composition and localized repair mechanism together account for the remaining ~8pp reduction—confirming the framework's value is not merely a stronger verifier or finer decomposition.

## Relation to Prior Work

CGRiC directly extends the information-lift certification framework of EDFL (Akter et al., 2025c) from atomic output-level decisions to compositional risk control over claim dependency graphs. It draws on selective prediction (semantic entropy, conformal prediction, self-consistency) and factual verification systems (FactScore, Self-RAG, CRAG, FacTool, CoVe) but departs from all of them by formalizing certification as a per-claim risk composition problem with explicit extraction-error and verifier-imperfection modeling. The graph-structured bound (Theorem 3) borrows conditional-independence reasoning from graphical models, while the localized repair mechanism converts the binary accept/reject paradigm into a targeted intervention loop—a design choice absent from prior atomic certification methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first formal risk guarantees for structured LLM outputs where correctness is compositional, addressing a gap that atomic certification methods (which force a binary accept/reject) cannot fill. The matched-component ablation convincingly isolates the compositional framework's contribution (~8pp abstention reduction) from confounding factors like verifier strength, strengthening the causal claim. Key limitations remain: the framework requires observable claim structure (inapplicable to latent reasoning models like o1), guarantees are conditional on verifier quality and extraction accuracy, and the 35–47% latency overhead may be prohibitive in some deployments. Open questions include whether the conditional-independence assumption can be replaced with a sharper finite-sample test, how the framework scales to book-length outputs beyond the hierarchical grouping heuristic, and whether calibration robustness can be improved without domain-specific re-annotation.
