## Problem

While prior work shows that factual recall in language models depends on the diversity of contexts in which facts appear during pretraining, these effects remain difficult to study systematically because large-scale synthetic setups (8-12 layer transformers, fixed templates) conflate statistical and factual learning and limit parameter sweeps. This paper asks: how does the level and structure of contextual diversity in pretraining data differentially affect factual recall, statistical pattern learning, and positional generalization, and where in the model architecture do the resulting optimization failures originate?

## Input / Output

Training sequences have length T=50 and are generated by inserting a source-target fact pair (from a disjoint vocabulary of K=100 pairs) into two designated positions of a background sequence drawn from one of N=10 Markov-chain templates (generic vocabulary V_D=3). At evaluation, the model receives the first T/2 tokens (containing the source token) as a prompt and auto-regressively generates the second half. Performance is scored on three axes—factual accuracy (correct target token appears), position accuracy (fact tokens only at the designated slot, generic tokens elsewhere), and statistical loss (KL divergence from the template's transition matrix)—each measured separately on in-distribution and out-of-distribution template-fact pairs.

## Method

The paper introduces a synthetic testbed that factorizes each sequence into a statistical stream (a mixture of N template distributions, each defined by a first-order Markov chain transition matrix and a placeholder position pair) and a factual stream (K atomic source-target pairs from a disjoint vocabulary), enabling independent control over two axes: diversity level (DIV, the fraction of templates in which each fact appears during training) and contextual structure (whether templates vary in statistics, positions, or both). Small 4-layer/4-head/32-dim decoder-only transformers are trained with standard next-token prediction, and generalization is probed via controlled completion prompts. Through systematic sweeps over DIV and structure, the paper shows that higher diversity delays in-distribution factual learning while low diversity causes out-of-distribution failures whose nature (factual, statistical, or both) depends on the contextual structure, and that the optimal diversity level shifts with training budget. Module-wise interventions (freezing or patching attention, embeddings, unembedding, and MLP from a high-diversity reference model) localize the optimization bottlenecks: statistical and positional failures trace to attention and embedding optimization, while factual-recall failures require joint optimization of the embedding and unembedding layers, with the unembedding layer specifically driving the high-diversity convergence slowdown.

## Results

On the 80M-parameter synthetic biography experiment, low-diversity training (DIV=0.1) yields only ~10% OOD factual recall, while freezing the embedding and unembedding layers from a high-diversity model improves OOD accuracy to ~65%. In the minimal 4-layer testbed, low diversity (DIV≤0.2) causes irreversible OOD factual recall failure that does not recover with additional training (except in the fixed-position MC10Pos1 setup), whereas high diversity (DIV=0.9) slows convergence but eventually achieves perfect ID and OOD performance on all three metrics (Loss_stat, Acc_pos, Acc_fact). Module interventions show that freezing the unembedding layer (U) from a high-diversity model eliminates the high-diversity convergence slowdown, and using a 10× larger learning rate for U (10⁻¹ vs. 10⁻³) makes convergence speed independent of diversity level.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper is a controlled synthetic testbed study rather than a method-vs-baseline comparison, so it does not benchmark against external methods. Instead, it compares three contextual structures (MC1Pos10, MC10Pos1, MC10Pos10) across diversity levels DIV∈{0.1,…,0.9} and eight module-intervention configurations. The key internal comparison is between low-diversity and high-diversity training: low diversity wins at short training budgets (3K steps) with intermediate DIV, while high diversity wins at long budgets (16K+ steps) but starts slower. Among interventions, freezing U alone improves Acc_fact from 47.59% to 60.03% at 10k steps, but combining U with E or Attn is necessary for strong factual recall (88.80% and 83.46% respectively), confirming that factual generalization requires jointly optimized features and unembedding. The 80M-parameter biography experiment validates these findings at scale, showing the same qualitative trade-offs and confirming that the E+U freezing intervention recovers most of the OOD gap (10%→65%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the synthetic-biography line of work initiated by Allen-Zhu & Li (2024) and Zucchet et al. (2025), which studied how paraphrastic diversity affects factual recall in language models. The key departure is the introduction of an explicit statistical stream (Markov-chain templates) alongside the factual stream, enabling joint study of statistical and factual generalization under fine-grained control over both diversity level and contextual structure—axes that were fixed or absent in prior setups. The minimal-scale design (4-layer/32-dim transformers) also departs from the 8–12 layer, 512–768 dim models used in prior work, enabling comprehensive parameter sweeps and module-level interventions that would be computationally infeasible at larger scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a low-cost, reproducible testbed for isolating how pretraining diversity shapes different facets of generalization (factual recall, positional structure, statistical patterns) and for localizing optimization bottlenecks to specific transformer modules. The finding that the unembedding layer drives the high-diversity convergence slowdown—and that a larger learning rate for U alone resolves it—offers a concrete, actionable insight for practitioners. Open questions include how these findings generalize to the entangled, long-tailed, and noisy distributions of real web-scale corpora, and whether the identified module-level bottlenecks persist at the scale of frontier models. The authors acknowledge that their minimalist setup abstracts away token ambiguity, non-atomic facts, and multi-role tokens, leaving a trade-off between analyzability and linguistic realism for future work.
