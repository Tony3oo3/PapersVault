## Problem

Noise-based certified machine unlearning faces a hard ceiling: the noise magnitude required to certify data erasure typically destroys model utility, particularly for large-scale deletion requests. While leveraging public data is a standard technique in differential privacy to relax this tension, its role in machine unlearning remained entirely unexplored, leaving practitioners without a mechanism to reduce the privacy-utility trade-off beyond increasing noise.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261819/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained model (obtained via T iterations of Projected Noisy Gradient Descent on a combined public + private dataset), a forget set (a subset of private data subject to unlearning), and a retain set (remaining private data plus all public data). It produces unlearned model weights after K fine-tuning PNGD iterations on the retain set, along with a certifiable Rényi divergence bound D_α(π_R^{T+K} ‖ π_U^K) quantifying how indistinguishable the unlearned model is from a model retrained from scratch without the forget set. The asymmetric structure is critical: only private data is subject to retraction, while public data remains permanently available and is never removed.

## Method

Asymmetric Langevin Unlearning (ALU) leverages public data as a stability anchor during Langevin unlearning, reducing the gradient sensitivity of each PNGD update by a factor of O(1/n_pub²) relative to the symmetric setting. This structural advantage means the learning and retraining weight distributions remain naturally close even when a constant fraction of private data is forgotten, so less noise is needed to certify the same unlearning guarantee. The theoretical core (Theorems 3.1–3.2) shows the Rényi divergence between unlearning and retraining distributions is bounded by the initial learning-retraining gap (shrunk by public data volume) times an exponential decay over unlearning iterations, enabling 'mass unlearning' of constant dataset fractions where symmetric methods become impractical. A complementary generalization bound (Theorem 4.1) explicitly quantifies the trade-off between noise reduction benefits and distribution mismatch penalties when public and private data come from different distributions, using the infinite Rényi divergence as a worst-case regret term.

## Results

On IMDB sentiment analysis (LSTM, 25,000 private points, K=15 unlearning iterations), ALU with 50,000 Amazon Reviews public points achieves 81.42% unlearned accuracy vs. 82.59% for standard LU without public data, a modest 1.17% utility drop. The U-LiRA membership inference attack shows that public data injection substantially reduces the adversary's confidence in distinguishing unlearned from retrained models, confirming improved unlearning efficacy. On DomainNet (24-class image classification), the Rényi divergence D_α(π_R^{T+K}‖π_U^K) decreases consistently with increasing public data volume (N=30,000 models, σ=0.01, α=2), and the initial distribution gap D_α(π_R^{T+1}‖π_U^1) shrinks with more public data, validating the theoretical O(1/n_pub²) sensitivity reduction. Under distribution mismatch (Infograph/Real pairing), the unlearning-retraining performance gap persists at ~10.5% regardless of public data volume, confirming the theoretical mismatch penalty.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is standard Langevin Unlearning (Chien et al., 2024a) in the symmetric setting (n_pub=0), which requires the full training process to satisfy differential privacy. ALU wins decisively on unlearning effectiveness: the variational Rényi divergence between unlearned and retrained weight distributions is substantially lower with public data injection, and U-LiRA membership inference confidence scores drop significantly, indicating the adversary cannot reliably distinguish unlearned from retrained models. On utility, ALU incurs a small cost: 1.17% accuracy drop on IMDB with well-aligned public data (Amazon Reviews) versus 2.19% with 40% label flipping (misaligned), compared to the symmetric baseline's 82.59% unlearned accuracy. The key trade-off is that public data reduces the required noise magnitude by a factor of (n_priv/(n_priv+n_pub))², enabling mass unlearning of constant dataset fractions where symmetric LU's noise requirement is independent of dataset size and becomes impractical. Under severe distribution mismatch (Infograph/Real on DomainNet), the mismatch penalty dominates and the ~10.5% performance gap persists regardless of public data volume, a limitation not present in the symmetric setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Langevin Unlearning (Chien et al., 2024a), which certifies approximate unlearning via Rényi divergence between weight distributions under noisy projected gradient descent, by introducing an asymmetric data model where a public corpus is permanently available alongside private data subject to deletion requests. The public-data-assisted privacy framework is well-established in differential privacy (Alon et al., 2019; Amid et al., 2022; Lowy et al., 2024) but had not been adapted to the unlearning setting; the only prior mixed-privacy unlearning work (Golatkar et al., 2021) required architectural modifications. For empirical evaluation, the paper draws on Birrell et al. (2021, 2023) for variational Rényi divergence estimation and Hayes et al. (2024) for the U-LiRA membership inference attack, positioning its contribution as a theoretical characterization of how data asymmetry reshapes the unlearning-utility trade-off rather than a new algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The O(1/n_pub²) reduction in unlearning cost provides a concrete, data-driven knob for practitioners to manage the noise-utility tension, enabling 'mass unlearning' of constant dataset fractions where symmetric methods become impractical. However, the practical impact is tempered by the intractability of Log-Sobolev constants for deep networks, the small-scale experimental validation (24-class DomainNet, IMDB sentiment), and the acknowledgment that the variational Rényi estimation is not a viable evaluation methodology. Open questions include extending the analysis to pretraining/fine-tuning regimes, replacing LSI with more tractable isoperimetric assumptions, and ensuring that public datasets themselves do not introduce secondary privacy leakage (Tramer et al., 2024).
