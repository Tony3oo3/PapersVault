## Problem

Existing time series explanation methods (masking, perturbation, gradient-based) are sensitive to distributional shifts and produce correlational rather than causal explanations, limiting their faithfulness and robustness in real-world deployment. No prior framework simultaneously addresses out-of-distribution generalization of explanations, causal grounding via counterfactuals, and compositional interpretability of learned concepts for black-box time series models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263523/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a time series instance x ∈ R^{D×T} (D features over T time steps) and query access to a black-box predictor f (no internal parameters or activations required), operating fully post-hoc and model-agnostically. It produces a sparse non-negative concept vector c ∈ R^d (d = r·D·T, overcomplete), an explanation-embedded reconstruction x̃ = g(c) that preserves the original label, and per-concept causal importance scores derived from approximate counterfactual interventions. The decoder g additionally outputs a decompositional ANOVA-like breakdown with Bernoulli sparsity masks, enabling visualization of individual concept effects and their pairwise/higher-order interactions.

## Method

TimeSAE is an end-to-end Sparse Autoencoder whose encoder and decoder are Block Temporal Convolutional Networks with Squeeze-and-Excitation blocks, using JumpReLU activation with learnable per-concept thresholds to prevent dead units and stabilize concept learning. Faithfulness is enforced through a contrastive InfoNCE loss (L_cf) that pulls together counterfactuals from the same high-CaCE intervention and pushes apart those from lower-CaCE interventions, grounded in the Causal Concept Effect framework. OOD robustness is achieved via a compositional consistency loss (L_cc) that samples novel concept combinations, decodes them, and penalizes encoder-decoder inversion error, ensuring the encoder inverts the decoder beyond the training support. A label-fidelity term ties the reconstruction to the black-box model's prediction, and the total objective is optimized jointly. The paper proves (Theorem 1) that under bounded approximation error, the SAE-based approximate counterfactuals preserve the true ordering of causal effects (order-faithfulness), and validates this empirically with Spearman ρ ≥ 0.89 across datasets.

## Results

TimeSAE achieves the best average faithfulness rank (1.7) across five datasets and three black-box model families (Transformer, PatchTS, DLinear), with standout scores of 2.15±0.080 on PAM and 2.12±0.072 on ETTH-1. On the OOD generalization benchmark (Table 1), TimeSAE consistently attains the highest AUPRC (e.g., 0.641 on ETTh1→ETTh2 vs. 0.628 for TimeX++) and the lowest distributional shift (KL 0.245, MMD 0.089), with a markedly smaller ID→OOD degradation than all baselines. The leave-one-out ablation confirms all four loss terms are necessary: removing L_cf drops AUPRC by 13.9%, removing L_cc drops faithfulness F_x by 24.1%, and removing L_label-fid. causes the largest degradation (38.2%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TimeSAE achieves the best average rank (1.7) among 11 methods, clearly outperforming gradient-based baselines (IG, Dynamask, WinIT) by a wide margin (e.g., 2.15 vs. 0.89 on PAM) and surpassing the strongest recent methods StartGrad (rank 2.4) and CounTS (rank 2.3). On PAM and ETTH-1/ETTH-2, TimeSAE leads all competitors by a substantial gap (e.g., 2.12 vs. 1.90 on ETTH-1, 2.09 vs. 1.70 on ETTH-2). However, CounTS slightly edges out TimeSAE on ECG (1.86 vs. 1.78) and EliteLJ (1.89 vs. 1.86), indicating that counterfactual-based methods remain competitive on smaller or more structured datasets. TimeX++ (Liu et al., 2024b) and TimeX (Queen et al., 2023) are consistently outperformed across all five datasets, confirming that the SAE-based concept decomposition with JumpReLU and the compositional consistency loss L_cc provide meaningful gains over information-bottleneck and self-supervised consistency approaches. In the OOD generalization setting (Table 1), TimeSAE exhibits the smallest ID→OOD degradation across all six shift scenarios (temporal resolution, cross-domain energy, weather→energy, HAR transfer, and athlete population shift), validating the compositional consistency mechanism as the key driver of robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

TimeSAE sits at the intersection of two research threads: sparse autoencoder-based concept learning (Ng, 2011; Rajamanoharan et al., 2024b with JumpReLU) and causal counterfactual explanation (Goyal et al., 2019 CaCE; Gat et al., 2023 approximated CFs). It departs from the dominant time-series explanation paradigm—masking or perturbing time steps (Dynamask, TimeX, TimeX++, StartGrad, ORTE)—by replacing sub-instance identification with an end-to-end SAE that decomposes inputs into sparse concept activations, supervised by a compositional consistency loss (Wiedemer et al., 2024) for OOD generalization and an InfoNCE contrastive loss for causal faithfulness. The paper also draws on neural functional ANOVA (Martens & Yau, 2020) and Concept Activation Regions (Crabbe & van der Schaar, 2022) for its decompositional decoder and concept alignment, but the key conceptual departure is framing time-series explanation as a causal, compositional concept-learning problem rather than a saliency-masking problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The order-faithfulness guarantee (Theorem 1) provides the first theoretical grounding for SAE-based approximate counterfactuals in time-series explanation, and the OOD robustness results (substantially smaller ID→OOD degradation than TimeX++ across six shift settings) address a practical gap that limits deployment of current explanation methods. The EliteLJ dataset opens a new evaluation axis in sports analytics. However, the method requires sufficiently large representative datasets and careful hyperparameter tuning (sparsity, dictionary size), and the paper acknowledges that faithfulness is only partially addressed—order preservation does not guarantee magnitude accuracy. Open questions include whether the learned concepts can support white-box model construction and whether the framework extends to explaining individual layers within foundation models like Chronos or TimeFM.
