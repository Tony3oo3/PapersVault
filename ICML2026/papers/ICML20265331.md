## Problem

Neural decoders for surface-code quantum error correction have shown accuracy gains over classical baselines like MWPM, but it remains unclear whether these gains stem from architectural sophistication or simply from larger training datasets. More critically, prior work has largely ignored the microsecond-scale latency constraint imposed by superconducting QEC cycles, leaving the practical deployability of neural decoders on FPGAs unverified.

## Input / Output

The decoder consumes a spatiotemporal syndrome volume s ∈ {0,1}^{r×(d²−1)} from r=d rounds of stabilizer measurements on a rotated surface code (d up to 9, i.e., 161 physical qubits), and outputs a binary logical-error label (or, for the GNN variant, per-detector error probabilities). Unlike hardware experiments that provide soft analog readout probabilities, the simulation-based input is strictly binary and sparse, which the Transformer architecture addresses via a convolutional tokenizer to avoid degenerate embeddings. The hard constraint is per-shot latency: the full inference must complete within one superconducting QEC cycle (~1 μs at 300 MHz, i.e., ≤300 clock cycles).

## Method

The paper unifies five representative neural decoder architectures (MLP, dilated 3D-CNN, TCN, Transformer, and GNN-based neural belief propagation) into a common benchmarking framework and systematically varies code distance (d=3–9) and training set size (10⁵–2.5×10⁷) to isolate the effects of data scale versus inductive bias. To address real-time feasibility, it develops an end-to-end compression pipeline that sequentially applies post-training quantization (PTQ), quantization-aware training (QAT) with the straight-through estimator, and unstructured magnitude pruning, targeting INT4 precision so that all arithmetic maps to LUT-based logic rather than scarce DSP slices on Xilinx UltraScale+ FPGAs. A key finding is that QAT is not an optional optimization but a prerequisite: temporal architectures (TCN, Transformer) suffer catastrophic accuracy loss under PTQ even at 8-bit, and only QAT recovers sub-MWPM fidelity at W4A4. The TCN emerges as the most hardware-efficient architecture, meeting the 1 μs deadline at d=9 on a VP1902 FPGA (314 estimated cycles, confirmed at 271 cycles via Vitis HLS synthesis), while the 3D-CNN's cubic parameter growth (32.5M at d=9) renders it impractical at larger distances.

## Results

On Google Sycamore hardware (d=3,5), the TCN decoder pretrained with a calibrated device-specific noise prior achieves 6.81% LER (d=3) and 11.59% LER (d=5) zero-shot, outperforming standard MWPM (8.01% and 14.38%) and rivaling or exceeding correlated MWPM (7.38% and 12.52%). In large-scale Stim simulations (p=0.005), all inductive-bias-aligned architectures (CNN, TCN, Transformer) converge to sub-MWPM LER once training data exceeds 10^7 samples, while a simple TCN-Small (~10^5 parameters) suffices for near-saturated fidelity at d≤9. After INT4 QAT and up to 70–80% unstructured pruning, the TCN decoder completes d=9 decoding in 314 estimated clock cycles (0.77 µs confirmed via Vitis HLS at 350 MHz) on a Xilinx VP1902 FPGA, well within the 1 µs real-time budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is MWPM via PyMatching v2 (Higgott & Gidney, 2025), evaluated in both standard (independent noise) and correlated (bias-aware) variants. On Sycamore d=3, the TCN decoder with a calibrated prior achieves 6.81% LER zero-shot, beating standard MWPM (8.01%) by 1.2 percentage points and correlated MWPM (7.38%) by 0.57 pp; fine-tuning further reduces LER to 6.70%. At d=5, the calibrated zero-shot TCN reaches 11.59%, surpassing standard MWPM (14.38%) by 2.79 pp and correlated MWPM (12.52%) by 0.93 pp. In simulation benchmarks (Fig. 3), all well-biased neural decoders trained on ≥10^7 samples consistently achieve lower LER than MWPM across d=3–9, with the gap widening at larger distances. The paper also shows that without a calibrated prior (uniform p=0.005 pretraining), the TCN decoder performs far worse than MWPM (34.42% at d=3), underscoring that the neural decoder's advantage over MWPM is contingent on accurate noise characterization rather than architecture alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies and re-implements five previously proposed neural decoder architectures (MLP, 3D-CNN, TCN, Transformer, GNN/Neural BP) under a common evaluation protocol, departing from prior work that typically introduced one architecture at a time to chase accuracy gains. The key conceptual departure is shifting the research question from 'which architecture decodes best?' to 'which architecture meets microsecond-scale FPGA latency while maintaining sub-MWPM accuracy?', introducing an end-to-end INT4 compression pipeline (PTQ → QAT → unstructured pruning) as a first-class design constraint rather than a post-hoc optimization. Validation on Google Sycamore hardware data (d=3,5) extends beyond the synthetic Stim simulations used in most prior neural decoder papers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that data scale (10^7 samples) dominates architectural complexity for near-term surface code decoding (d≤9) provides a clear, actionable design principle: invest in data generation rather than architectural novelty. The demonstration that INT4 QAT is a prerequisite (not an optimization) for temporal decoders on FPGAs, and that TCN is the sole architecture meeting the 1 µs budget at d=9, gives hardware teams a concrete target architecture. Open questions include whether these conclusions hold for larger code distances (d>9), continuous/streaming decoding with r≫d, non-surface-code families (e.g., qLDPC), and whether the data-first regime persists as physical error rates drop below the pseudo-threshold.
