## Problem

Panoramic video depth estimation is hindered by two obstacles: the absence of large-scale RGB-D panoramic video datasets and the unique geometric distortions of Equirectangular Projection (ERP), which cause non-uniform spatial information density and non-linear temporal dynamics that break the assumptions of perspective video depth models. Existing approaches either estimate depth per-frame without temporal consistency or rely on computationally expensive test-time optimization that struggles with dynamic scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264223/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a panoramic RGB video in Equirectangular Projection format and produces a temporally consistent single-channel depth sequence, predicting relative (affine-invariant) depth normalized to [0,1]. Training is performed at 640×320 resolution with 6-frame clips, while evaluation uses the standard panoramic resolution of 1024×512. Inference employs a 5-step denoising schedule with sliding-window processing to handle arbitrary-length videos, and the final depth is obtained by averaging the three VAE decoder output channels.

## Method

PVDepth extends DepthCrafter (a latent video diffusion model built on Stable Video Diffusion) with two geometry-aware adaptations for ERP. First, Progressive Sphere-aware Noise Initialization (PSNI) replaces i.i.d. Gaussian noise with a variance-preserving mixture of planar noise and sphere-projected noise (sampled in 3D and projected to ERP via trilinear interpolation), with the planar weight linearly annealed from 1 to 0.1 over 5K steps to curriculum-adapt the model to ERP's non-uniform information density. Second, Cube-rectified Temporal Modeling (CRTM) introduces an auxiliary branch that projects ERP latent features into a distortion-free cubemap representation, applies the shared temporal attention layer there (where motion trajectories are near-linear), projects the result back to ERP, and fuses it into the primary branch via a zero-initialized projection. Training follows a two-stage protocol: Stage I fine-tunes spatial layers with PSNI for 20K steps, then Stage II introduces CRTM and trains only the new projection layers for another 20K steps while freezing all spatial and original temporal weights.

## Results

On the PanoCARLA test set (197 clips, cross-scene evaluation), PVDepth achieves AbsRel of 0.211 / 0.205 / 0.181 and δ₁ of 0.789 / 0.787 / 0.818 at 2 / 10 / 20 fps settings, respectively, outperforming the strongest baseline DepthCrafter-FT (AbsRel 0.228 / 0.252 / 0.216) by 7–16% in relative error. On the real-world GenEx Real-World dataset, PVDepth attains the highest reprojection SSIM of 0.4194, surpassing ViPE (0.4107) and DepthCrafter (0.3290). PVDepth also exhibits stable performance across inference resolutions, improving AbsRel from 0.275 at 640×320 to 0.199 at 1024×512.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PVDepth outperforms all 9 baselines across all three FPS settings on PanoCARLA. Against the closest competitor, DepthCrafter fine-tuned on PanoCARLA, PVDepth reduces AbsRel by 7.5% (0.211 vs 0.228 at 2fps), 18.7% (0.205 vs 0.252 at 10fps), and 16.2% (0.181 vs 0.216 at 20fps), while improving δ₁ by 3.7–5.0 points. Image-based methods (DA-2, UniK3D, PanoDA, DepthAnywhere) suffer severe temporal inconsistency under video-level evaluation, with AbsRel values 2–6× higher than PVDepth despite competitive per-frame scores. Post-processing methods (Matrix-3D, ViPE) are limited by static-scene assumptions or optimization instability, yielding AbsRel 0.37–0.68 versus PVDepth's 0.18–0.21. On the real-world GenEx dataset, PVDepth achieves the highest reprojection SSIM (0.4194), narrowly beating ViPE (0.4107) and substantially outperforming DepthCrafter (0.3290) and DA-2 (0.3147), with more stable performance at larger temporal offsets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends DepthCrafter (a perspective video depth model built on Stable Video Diffusion) to the panoramic domain by introducing two geometry-aware adaptation mechanisms: Progressive Sphere-aware Noise Initialization (PSNI) and Cube-rectified Temporal Modeling (CRTM). It departs from the dominant 'predict-then-optimize' paradigm (Matrix-3D, ViPE) that enforces temporal consistency via test-time geometric alignment, instead pursuing an end-to-end generative approach. The work also bridges two previously separate lines of research—single-image panoramic depth estimation (DA-2, UniK3D, PanoDA) and perspective video depth estimation (DepthCrafter, Video Depth Anything, Depth Any Video)—by addressing the unique ERP-specific spatial and temporal challenges that prevent direct transfer between them.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PVDepth establishes the first end-to-end panoramic video depth estimation framework, and the PanoCARLA dataset (126.7K frames, 50+ km of outdoor roaming) fills a critical data gap that has blocked progress in this area. The PSNI and CRTM mechanisms offer a general recipe for adapting perspective diffusion models to non-uniform projection domains, potentially transferable to other geometric settings. Key limitations remain: training is restricted to outdoor synthetic scenes (limiting indoor generalization), reflective surfaces cause failures, and the ERP↔Cube projection adds ~28% FLOPs over the baseline. Open questions include whether the approach scales to real-world indoor panoramic video, how to handle strong specular reflections, and whether more efficient projection strategies can close the efficiency gap.
