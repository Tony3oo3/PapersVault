## Problem

Existing LLM ensemble methods apply a fixed set of base learners to all queries, creating problem-induced instability when task distributions shift. No prior work has systematically addressed both (i) dynamically selecting which subset of LLMs to invoke per query and (ii) learning to fuse potentially conflicting outputs from that dynamically composed subset, while keeping inference cost low by pruning redundant models.

## Input / Output

The system takes a text query (multiple-choice or open-ended) and a pool of N open-source LLMs (2B–70B parameters) as input. The Decider Agent consumes a state vector comprising the current pool membership, pool size, and K diversity metrics computed over a sliding window T of historical failures; the Fusion Agent consumes the probability distributions over answer choices produced by each selected model plus recent reward history. The output is a dynamically selected subset of m LLMs (m ≪ N) and a final fused answer, with the Decider Agent's branching policy producing independent Bernoulli inclusion decisions per model.

## Method

RL-Focal formulates LLM ensembling as a two-stage DEC-POMDP trained with MAPPO and a centralized critic. In Stage 1, a Decider Agent with a branching MLP policy simultaneously decides binary inclusion of each model, guided by a novel focal diversity metric that quantifies error correlation via the ratio of simultaneous-failure probability to individual-failure probability, plus a size-penalized reward that shrinks the pool. In Stage 2, a Fusion Agent with an MLP policy resolves conflicting probability vectors from the selected ensemble into a final decision. Training uses an offline warm-start (plurality voting as interim prediction for the Decider, then Fusion Agent training) followed by periodic online policy updates to adapt to distribution shifts. The focal diversity metric actively prunes the 2^N−N−1 possible ensemble combinations, and the branching architecture (separate final-layer heads per model) improves selection quality over a single-head policy by 8% on GPQA.

## Results

On five benchmarks (MMLU, GSM8K, BBH, MUSR, GPQA), RL-Focal achieves 83.59%, 93.89%, 65.00%, 55.25%, and 48.28% accuracy respectively, outperforming the best individual LLM in the 14-model pool by 1.10–8.48 percentage points. The largest gain is on BBH (+8.48% over Phi-4-14b) and GPQA (+5.97% over Qwen-2.5-72b). Compared to the closest RL-based ensemble baseline RLAE-MAPPO, RL-Focal matches or exceeds accuracy (e.g., 69.2% vs 69.1% on MMLU, 36.78% vs 35.3% on GPQA) while reducing inference cost by 2.3× on MMLU and 6.6× on GPQA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RL-Focal outperforms all eight compared baselines on the majority of metrics. Against RLAE-MAPPO (Fu et al., 2025), the closest RL-based competitor, RL-Focal achieves marginally higher accuracy (69.2% vs 69.1% MMLU; 36.78% vs 35.3% GPQA; 93.26% vs 92.5% GSM8k) while reducing inference cost by 2.3× (MMLU) and 6.6× (GPQA) due to dynamic model pruning. Against supervised ensemble methods—LLM-Blender/PAIRRANKER (Jiang et al., 2023), GAC (Yu et al., 2024), and DEEPEN (Huang et al., 2024)—RL-Focal leads by 5.4–11.4% on MMLU, 1.6–7.1% on GSM8k, and 2.6–4.2% on GPQA. Against RouteLLM (Ong et al., 2024), RL-Focal surpasses the best three-model routing combination by 8.28–11.62% on GPQA. On MMLU, RL-Focal (77.98%) beats DyLAN (70.5%), More Agents with 40 LLMs (60.05%), and LLM-TOPLA (72.77%), though it trails LLM-TOPLA by a narrow 0.17% on GSM8k (78.84% vs 79.01%), the only loss across all comparisons.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RL-based ensemble learning line (RLAE, Fu et al. 2025) by replacing the fixed-size ensemble with a dynamic, per-query subset selection problem, and by decomposing the ensemble into two sequential RL stages (Decider for model selection, Fusion for output aggregation) formulated as a DEC-POMDP. It builds on MAPPO (Yu et al., 2022) for multi-agent policy optimization and on action-branching architectures (Tavakoli et al., 2018) to handle the Decider's simultaneous binary selection actions. The focal diversity metric departs from standard agreement-based measures (Fleiss Kappa, Q-statistic) by conditioning on per-model failure patterns, drawing on the bias-variance-covariance decomposition of ensemble error (Krogh & Vedelsby, 1994; Brown et al., 2005).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the cost-accuracy tradeoff: RL-Focal matches or exceeds RLAE-MAPPO accuracy while reducing inference cost by 2×–9×, making dynamic LLM ensembles viable for latency-sensitive or budget-constrained deployments. The finding that a small, diversity-optimized subset (m ≪ N) consistently outperforms the full pool challenges the common assumption that 'more models = better ensemble' in the LLM setting. Open questions include scalability beyond ~14 models, applicability to truly open-ended generation (beyond MCQ and converted OEQ), robustness to rapidly shifting task distributions, and whether the two-stage RL framework can be replaced by a single end-to-end agent without the stability benefits of the centralized critic.
