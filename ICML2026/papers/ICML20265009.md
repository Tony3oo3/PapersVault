## Problem

Efficient diffusion model sampling relies on handcrafted timestep schedules that are suboptimal, solver-dependent, and cannot be optimized via gradients due to the discrete combinatorial nature of timestep selection. Additionally, a fundamental inconsistency exists between training (which fits the score function across all thousands of timesteps) and inference (which uses only a handful of selected steps), wasting model capacity on unused timesteps and degrading few-step generation quality.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained denoising network (NCSN++ within the EDM framework) and a candidate set of 128 timesteps (initialized via EDM heuristics) as input, along with Gaussian noise for sampling. It produces a binary mask selecting K timesteps (4–10 NFEs) from the candidate set, which is then used with a chosen ODE solver (DDIM or iPNDM) to generate 32×32 or 64×64 RGB images. The evaluation loss compares the distribution of samples generated under the masked schedule against those from the full schedule, using 50K images for FID computation.

## Method

The core idea is to reparameterize the discrete timestep selection problem as a Bernoulli mask m ~ Bern(s) with a sparsity constraint ||s||₁ ≤ K, transforming it into a continuous optimization problem solvable via policy gradient without backpropagation through the denoising network. The outer loop samples masks, evaluates generation quality via a distributional distance metric, and updates s using the score function ∇s ln p(m|s). To address the training-inference gap, the framework is extended into a bilevel optimization: the inner loop performs lightweight fine-tuning (1K steps) of the model on only the timesteps selected by the current outer-loop mask, while the outer loop continues optimizing the schedule based on the fine-tuned model's generation quality. Theoretical analysis establishes that the inner-loop model converges geometrically toward its per-mask optimum and that the outer objective is non-increasing under mild smoothness and boundedness assumptions.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with the iPNDM solver at NFE=4, PCS achieves FID 8.96 (PCS+ft: 8.75), substantially outperforming GITS (15.63), DMN (28.09), and LD3 (9.31). At NFE=10, PCS+ft reaches FID 2.14 on CIFAR-10 and 2.01 on AFHQv2. The bilevel fine-tuning variant (PCS+ft) consistently improves over PCS alone, with the largest gains at low NFEs (e.g., ImageNet iPNDM NFE=4: 14.97 → 11.75). Across all four datasets and both solvers (DDIM, iPNDM), PCS or PCS+ft achieves the best or near-best FID at every NFE from 4 to 10.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GITS (Chen et al., 2024), PCS wins decisively across all four datasets and both solvers; on CIFAR-10 iPNDM at NFE=4, PCS achieves FID 8.96 versus GITS's 15.63, and on ImageNet iPNDM NFE=4, 14.97 versus 17.56. Against DMN (Xue et al., 2024), the margin is even larger: on CIFAR-10 iPNDM NFE=4, PCS scores 8.96 versus DMN's 28.09, and on AFHQv2 iPNDM NFE=4, 10.15 versus 17.91. Against LD3 (Tong et al., 2024), PCS is the closest competitor and wins in most settings (e.g., CIFAR-10 iPNDM NFE=4: 8.96 vs. 9.31; ImageNet NFE=4: 14.97 vs. 15.69), though LD3 edges out PCS on AFHQv2 at NFE=4 (9.96 vs. 10.15). Compared to handcrafted schedules (uniform, LogSNR, EDM), PCS delivers dramatic improvements—on CIFAR-10 iPNDM NFE=4, FID drops from 58.16 (uniform) and 29.98 (EDM) to 8.96. The bilevel variant PCS+ft further narrows or eliminates the gap with LD3 in nearly all settings, confirming that training-inference alignment is critical under few-step sampling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on optimizing timestep schedules for diffusion model sampling (DMN, GITS, LD3) by recasting the discrete combinatorial selection problem as a continuous policy gradient optimization via Bernoulli reparameterization, drawing a conceptual parallel to mask-based model pruning. It departs from prior optimization-based schedulers by introducing a bilevel framework—borrowed from the LLM compression literature (Liao et al., 2026)—that jointly adapts the denoising network to the selected timesteps in an inner loop, addressing a training-inference mismatch that purely schedule-optimization methods ignore. Unlike RL-based timestep tuners (Xia et al., 2024) that predict step counts, PCS operates over a fixed candidate set and learns a sparse binary mask, and unlike distillation methods it requires no teacher-student retraining of the full network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of training-inference inconsistency and the demonstration that fine-tuning on selected timesteps outperforms full-timestep training (Table 1) challenges the default assumption that broader timestep coverage during training is always beneficial, suggesting that capacity should be concentrated where inference actually occurs. However, the practical impact is currently limited: experiments are confined to pixel-space NCSN++ models at 32×32 and 64×64 resolutions, and the bilevel fine-tuning adds non-trivial training overhead (1K inner-loop steps per outer iteration). Open questions include whether the approach transfers to latent diffusion models (e.g., Stable Diffusion), conditional/text-to-image generation, video, and whether the policy gradient formulation scales to the thousands of candidate timesteps typical of production models.
