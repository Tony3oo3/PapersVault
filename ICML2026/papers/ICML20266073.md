## Problem

Existing 'parameter-free' stochastic optimization methods still require unverifiable conditions on problem parameters—such as upper bounds on gradient noise or lower bounds on the initial distance to the optimum—making them only partially parameter-free. This paper introduces a rigorous formal distinction between partial and full parameter-freeness and targets the development of methods whose algorithmic inputs satisfy no unverifiable conditions related to true problem parameters, while still achieving near-optimal convergence rates.

## Input / Output

The method consumes stochastic gradient and function value oracles, an initial point x⁰, a total oracle budget T, and a confidence level δ. The user-specified constants (L_ε, F_ε, d_ε) are arbitrary positive values that need not satisfy any relationship to the true smoothness, suboptimality gap, or distance-to-optimum—this absence of constraints is precisely what distinguishes full from partial parameter-freeness. The output is a single solution point x^out selected via a model ensemble, accompanied by a high-probability convergence rate matching the optimally tuned rate up to logarithmic factors in 1/L_ε and 1/F_ε.

## Method

The GRASP framework combines three components: self-bounding analysis, non-uniform budget allocation, and model ensemble. The key insight of self-bounding is to compare the target convergence rate against a trivial benchmark (e.g., the initial point's suboptimality ‖∇ℓ(x⁰)‖·d₀) and solve for the parameter value where the target becomes vacuous, yielding a computable upper bound without any prior knowledge of the true parameter. For non-convex optimization (GRASP-NC), the method uses an initial gradient estimate ‖ĝ⁰‖ to construct self-bounded ranges for L_ℓ, F_ℓ, and Δ_ℓ, then grid-searches over SGD step sizes on a geometric sequence. For convex optimization (GRASP-C), it grid-searches over domain diameters for UNIXGRAD, offering two options: one for smooth acceleration and one for Hölder-smoothness universality (the latter requiring only a lower bound on the objective value). A non-uniform budget allocation (T_i ∝ 1/i) tightens the logarithmic overhead from log(d_max/d_ε) to log(d̄₀/d_ε), and a new gap-dependent ensemble lemma sharpens the ensemble error under interpolated variance, reducing it from Õ(σ_ℓ/√M) to Õ(√V₀/√M + (σ_ℓ+V₁)/M).

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18, GRASP-NC (non-convex) achieves a relative gradient-norm gap ρ of 0.11–0.23 versus the optimally tuned SGD, and GRASP-C (convex) achieves ρ of 0.08–0.16 versus tuned UNIXGRAD, confirming that the logarithmic overhead in the theoretical rate translates to only a 1.08×–1.23× practical multiplier. The initial sampling budget M can be reduced from T/4 to T/4096 with negligible degradation, indicating the theoretical requirement of T/4 initial samples is conservative. Theoretically, GRASP-NC matches the optimal tuned-SGD rate O(√(L_ℓ F_ℓ Δ_ℓ²/T) + (L_ℓ F_ℓ + Δ_ℓ²)/T) up to a log₊(T‖ĝ⁰‖/(L_ε F_ε)) factor, while GRASP-C achieves the accelerated rate O(L_ℓ d₀²/T²) up to log factors, all without requiring any prior knowledge of L_ℓ, F_ℓ, Δ_ℓ, or d₀.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against **Tuned SGD (Ghadimi & Lan, 2013)**, GRASP-NC matches the optimal convergence rate up to a logarithmic factor log₊(T‖ĝ⁰‖/(L_ε F_ε)), but eliminates the need to know L_ℓ, F_ℓ, and Δ_ℓ entirely — the user only supplies arbitrary small constants L_ε, F_ε. Against **Attia & Koren (2024)**, GRASP-NC achieves full parameter-freeness where the prior method is only partially free: Attia & Koren's grid search requires η_min and η_max as inputs, which in turn demand prior knowledge of problem parameters, whereas GRASP-NC derives its search range self-boundedly from the empirical gradient ‖ĝ⁰‖. In the convex setting, against **Kavis et al. (2019) UNIXGRAD**, GRASP-C removes the requirement for a known domain diameter D (i.e., a bound on d₀), achieving full freeness to d₀ while retaining the accelerated O(L_ℓ d₀²/T²) rate. Against **Kreisler et al. (2024) U-DOG**, GRASP-C (Theorem 4) is fully free to both L_ℓ and d₀ (U-DOG requires a lower bound r_ε ≤ d₀ and a noise upper-bound function Δ̂(·)), and additionally adapts to Hölder smoothness (ν ∈ [0,1]) whereas U-DOG only handles the separate smooth and non-smooth cases; however, GRASP-C incurs an additional ensemble error term ERR_N(T/N) that is absent in U-DOG's rate, and the log factors on the main convergence term are slightly larger (log₊²(d̄₀/d_ε) vs. log₊²(d₀/r_ε)·log²(1/δ)·log₊⁴(·)).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the grid search framework for parameter-free stochastic optimization established by Attia & Koren (2024) and Khaled & Jin (2024), which were 'partially parameter-free' because they required user-specified ranges for problem parameters. The key departure is the 'self-bounding analysis' technique, which derives computable search ranges by comparing the target convergence rate against a trivial benchmark rate, eliminating the need for any prior knowledge of parameter bounds. For the convex case, the method uses UNIXGRAD (Kavis et al., 2019) as a black-box base algorithm, and for the non-convex case, it builds on the high-probability SGD analysis and ensemble method from Attia & Koren (2024). The paper also introduces a sharper ensemble guarantee under a gap-dependent variance characterization (interpolation regime), improving upon the worst-case ensemble error of prior grid search methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first fully parameter-free methods (requiring no unverifiable conditions on problem parameters) for both non-convex and convex stochastic optimization, achieving near-optimal rates up to logarithmic factors. The self-bounding technique is general and could potentially extend to other optimization settings beyond those covered here. Open questions include whether the framework can be applied to other problem structures (e.g., composite objectives, constrained settings), whether more efficient ensemble methods can reduce the additive ensemble error inherent to grid search, and whether the theoretical requirement of T/4 initial sampling can be further reduced in practice without degrading guarantees.
