## Problem

Autoregressive LLMs decode tokens sequentially, making inference latency scale linearly with chain-of-thought length and impossible to reduce with additional compute. Existing adaptive parallel reasoning methods either degrade accuracy relative to strong sequential baselines, require non-trivial modifications to position embeddings, KV caches, or attention masks in the inference engine, or lack a principled RL formulation for credit assignment across parallel branches. ThreadWeaver targets the regime where abundant compute is available to shorten the critical reasoning path on long-CoT problems while maintaining accuracy on par with sequential thinking models.

## Input / Output

The model takes a standard text prompt (a math problem with a chat template) and produces a structured reasoning trajectory interleaving sequential segments with one or more fork-join parallel blocks delimited by <Parallel>, <Outlines>, and <Thread> control tokens, ending with a \boxed{} final answer. At inference, an external orchestrator state machine issues separate completion requests to a standard text-completion API (e.g., vLLM or SGLang) for each thread in parallel, stopping at </Thread>, then concatenates results and resumes sequential decoding—requiring zero modifications to the underlying engine. The model also supports a hybrid mode where the state machine is bypassed entirely, recovering ordinary autoregressive behavior for high-throughput serving.

## Method

ThreadWeaver introduces three coupled components. First, a two-stage data pipeline: GPT-5 rewrites 959 sequential Qwen3-8B trajectories into parallel fork-join format for SFT cold-start, followed by self-training on 17,491 model-generated trajectories filtered for format and answer correctness to close the distribution gap. Second, a trie-based training-inference co-design: all <context, completion> units that the inference state machine would issue are extracted, inserted into a token-level prefix tree, and traversed depth-first into a single packed training sequence with an ancestor-only attention mask and incremental positions, ensuring training exactly matches the per-request isolation at inference. Third, P-GRPO extends GRPO with (a) a parallel rollout via the state machine so the policy sees the same execution pattern at train and test time, (b) a trajectory-level scalar reward combining a correctness indicator and a clipped acceleration ratio (1 − longest_thread/total_tokens) awarded only on correct answers, and (c) simple mean-centering of advantages instead of standard-deviation normalization, which empirically prevents the model from sacrificing correctness for speed. The trajectory-level advantage is broadcast to all completion tokens across all threads, which the paper shows is the exact policy-gradient estimator under the chain-rule factorization of the parallel trajectory.

## Results

On six math reasoning benchmarks, ThreadWeaver (built on Qwen3-8B) matches the sequential GRPO baseline's average accuracy (71.9% vs. 72.2%) while reducing mean token latency from 15.1k to 13.2k tokens, yielding a 1.22× average speedup and up to 1.53× on Minerva Math. On AIME24 specifically, ThreadWeaver achieves 79.9% accuracy (vs. 78.3% for the sequential baseline) with a 1.14× speedup. Wall-clock measurements on 50 MATH500 problems confirm a 1.14× real-time speedup when distributing threads across four GPUs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the sequential GRPO baseline on Qwen3-8B, ThreadWeaver essentially ties on average accuracy (71.9% vs. 72.2%) while cutting mean token latency by ~13%, and actually surpasses the baseline on AIME24 (79.9% vs. 78.3%). Against Multiverse (Yang et al., 2025b), which is built on the much larger Qwen2.5-32B, ThreadWeaver achieves 79.9% vs. 53.8% accuracy on AIME24 and a higher self-parallelism speedup (1.25× vs. 1.18×) with an 85.2% activation ratio. Against Parallel-R1 (Zheng et al., 2025) on Qwen3-4B-Base, ThreadWeaver's 79.9% accuracy dwarfs Parallel-R1's best variant at 19.4%, with a far higher activation ratio (85.2% vs. 27.3%). The paper attributes these large gaps to ThreadWeaver operating in a long chain-of-thought regime on a thinking model, whereas Parallel-R1 produces significantly shorter CoTs that limit test-time scaling, and to the quality of model-aligned SFT data versus Parallel-R1's simpler GSM8K-based curriculum.

## Relation to Prior Work

ThreadWeaver extends the adaptive parallel reasoning paradigm introduced by Multiverse (Yang et al., 2025b) and formalized in PASTA and Parallel-R1, but departs from them in three key ways: it requires no modifications to the underlying inference engine (unlike Multiverse and PASTA which alter position embeddings or attention masks), it builds on thinking models with long CoT rather than instruct models, and it introduces P-GRPO—a parallelization-aware RL objective with thread-wise advantage broadcast and an acceleration reward. The RL foundation is GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), extended with a parallel rollout state machine and a trie-based training-inference co-design that eliminates the train-test gap. The two-stage data pipeline (GPT-5 rewriting followed by self-training with reward-based filtering) draws on STaR-style bootstrapping but is specifically designed to align parallel structure with the student model's native generative patterns.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ThreadWeaver is the first adaptive parallel reasoning framework to match the accuracy of comparably-sized sequential thinking models while reducing token latency, and its compatibility with off-the-shelf engines like vLLM and SGLang makes it deployable without custom inference infrastructure—a significant practical advantage over prior methods. However, the achieved speedups (1.14×–1.53×) are modest and bounded by Amdahl's law, depending heavily on problem decomposability; intrinsically sequential problems see little benefit. Open questions include whether deeper (multi-level) parallelism, better thread-level credit assignment, or application to non-math domains (code, science) could yield larger gains, and whether the approach generalizes to models without strong native sequential reasoning capabilities.
