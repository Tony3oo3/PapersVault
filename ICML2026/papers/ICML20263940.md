## Problem

Existing surrogate regret bounds for online structured prediction guarantee finite excess loss independent of the time horizon T, but these guarantees break down in non-stationary environments where every fixed comparator incurs surrogate loss growing linearly with T. The paper asks whether one can obtain cumulative target-loss bounds that depend on T only through data-dependent quantities (comparator surrogate loss and path length) rather than explicitly through T, thereby remaining meaningful under distribution shift.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263940/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each of T rounds, the learner receives a bounded input vector x_t (‖x_t‖₂ ≤ 1) and must produce a prediction ŷ_t from a potentially combinatorial prediction set (labels, rankings, etc.) before the ground-truth y_t is revealed. The learner maintains a linear estimator W_t in a bounded convex domain (Frobenius diameter D) that maps x_t to a score vector θ_t ∈ ℝ^d, which is then decoded via a randomized distribution π(θ_t) over the prediction set. The theoretical output is a bound on cumulative target loss of the form F_T + O(1 + P_T), where F_T is the cumulative surrogate loss of any chosen comparator sequence and P_T is its total path length in Frobenius norm.

## Method

The core technique synthesizes the dynamic regret analysis of online gradient descent (OGD) with the surrogate-gap exploitation strategy: a non-increasing learning rate η_t is constrained to a range whose upper bound involves the per-round surrogate-to-target loss gap, enabling cancellation of cumulative surrogate loss terms in the OGD regret decomposition. This yields a Polyak-style adaptive learning rate η_t = min{2(L_t(W_t) − E[ℓ(ŷ_t,y_t)])/‖G_t‖²_F, η_{t−1}} that automatically satisfies the required range and adapts to non-stationarity. For broader structured prediction settings where the prediction set differs from the label set (e.g., NDCG ranking), the paper introduces the convolutional Fenchel-Young loss and proves two new lemmas—a target-surrogate identity and a quadratic lower bound on the subtracted Fenchel-Young term—that replace the surrogate-gap and self-bounding assumptions. A matching lower bound via Yao's principle on binary classification with the smooth hinge loss confirms that the joint linear dependence on F_T and P_T is tight.

## Results

The main theoretical contribution is the "small-surrogate-loss + path-length" bound: for any comparator sequence U₁,…,U_T, the cumulative target loss is upper-bounded by F_T + O(1 + P_T), where F_T is the cumulative surrogate loss of the comparators and P_T is their path length (Theorem 3.4). A matching lower bound (Theorem 5.1) confirms this dependence on F_T and P_T is tight. Empirically, on synthetic (K=2, m=2) and MNIST (K=3, m=784) datasets over T=10,000 rounds with logistic loss, the proposed Polyak-style learning rate achieves the lowest cumulative 0-1 loss (number of mistakes) under high non-stationarity (100 segment-wise label flips), with its advantage over constant and AdaGrad rates growing as the number of flips increases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three OGD learning rates on the same online classification protocol: (i) Constant (η_t = α/M), the fixed rate used by Van der Hoeven (2020) and Sakaue et al. (2024); (ii) AdaGrad, a standard adaptive rate; and (iii) the proposed Polyak-style rate (Eq. 5). Under 1 label flip (nearly stationary), AdaGrad slightly outperforms Polyak-style, while constant is competitive. Under 10 flips (moderately non-stationary), the constant rate performs worst, AdaGrad is intermediate, and Polyak-style achieves the lowest cumulative mistakes on both synthetic and MNIST datasets. Under 100 flips (highly non-stationary), the gap widens substantially: Polyak-style clearly outperforms both alternatives, while the constant rate lags the most. The paper notes that AdaGrad does not necessarily satisfy the learning-rate condition (4) required by Theorem 3.4, so its competitive performance in the near-stationary regime is not theoretically guaranteed.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes two previously separate threads: the surrogate-gap technique for finite surrogate regret bounds in online classification/structured prediction (Van der Hoeven 2020; Sakaue et al. 2024) and the dynamic regret analysis of OGD with path-length comparators (Zinkevich 2003; Hall & Willett 2015). The key conceptual move is recognizing that a carefully constrained learning rate in OGD simultaneously controls the OCO regret term and cancels the cumulative surrogate loss, yielding a target-loss bound that depends on T only through the comparator's surrogate loss F_T and path length P_T. The paper further extends beyond the Ŷ=Y restriction of prior work by introducing new technical lemmas for the convolutional Fenchel-Young loss (Cao et al. 2025), enabling coverage of tasks like NDCG ranking where label and prediction sets differ.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result resolves a concrete gap: existing finite surrogate regret bounds become vacuous (Ω(T) target loss) in non-stationary environments, while the new F_T + O(1+P_T) bound remains meaningful when the environment changes only O(1) times. The Polyak-style learning rate is a practical contribution that empirically outperforms both constant and AdaGrad rates as non-stationarity increases. However, the work is restricted to full-information feedback; extending to bandit or delayed feedback settings—where prior surrogate regret bounds already depend explicitly on T—remains an open and challenging direction. The tight lower bound confirms the dependence on F_T and P_T cannot be improved, closing the theoretical picture for this formulation.
