## Problem

Extending context-based offline meta RL to multi-agent settings introduces two critical gaps: (1) decentralized agents must identify tasks from local trajectories that lack global information, leading to biased or unreliable task inference, and (2) role information—shared behavioral patterns across tasks that enable knowledge transfer—is not explicitly observable in the data, causing role collapse where agents converge to homogeneous behaviors. Prior single-agent meta RL methods and multi-agent multi-task baselines fail to address both challenges simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an offline multi-task dataset of decentralized trajectories (sequences of observations and actions per agent) from a DecPOMDP, where different tasks may involve varying numbers of allies and enemies. An LLM (GPT-5) processes textualized trajectory descriptions to generate discrete role labels, which serve as supervision for the role encoder. The system outputs decentralized actions per agent conditioned on 16-dimensional local task encodings and role encodings, with all encoders and policy networks built from Transformer architectures to handle variable-length agent observations.

## Method

D²TR learns three encoders in a staged procedure. First, a global task encoder f_G is trained on centralized (joint) trajectories using an InfoNCE contrastive loss plus a reconstruction decoder loss to maximize mutual information with the task identity; then an individual task encoder f_I is trained on decentralized trajectories with an additional mutual information knowledge distillation term that aligns local encodings toward the (stop-gradient) global encodings, providing a gap-controlled surrogate for the intractable local-to-task mutual information. Second, an LLM assigns semantic role labels to trajectories via either code generation (for tasks with explicit subgoals) or a pre-clustering → description → merging pipeline (for tasks without explicit subgoals), and a role encoder f_R is trained via InfoNCE contrastive learning on behavior-only contexts to capture individual-centric information. Finally, both task and role encodings are fed into an OMIGA-based actor-critic policy with a mixing network that decomposes a global Q-function into local Q-functions, enabling conservative offline policy learning that avoids the over-pessimism of purely value-based approaches on OOD observations.

## Results

D²TR achieves the best mean win rate across all 8 environments in both IID (0.75) and OOD (0.56) settings, outperforming the strongest meta-RL baseline UNICORN (0.72 IID, 0.48 OOD) and the strongest multi-task baseline HiSSD (0.62 IID, 0.47 OOD). In the most challenging CN Target environment, which demands precise task identification, D²TR reaches 0.74 IID win rate versus 0.53 for UNICORN, 0.52 for FOCAL, 0.17 for CSRO, and 0.26 for MG2L. In OOD settings, D²TR consistently leads across all environments, with the largest margins in CN Target (0.39 vs. 0.20 for UNICORN) and Stalker-Zealot (0.72 vs. 0.64 for UNICORN).

## Baselines & Comparisons

D²TR outperforms all seven baselines in overall mean win rate for both IID (0.75) and OOD (0.56). Against the strongest meta-RL baseline UNICORN (0.72 IID, 0.48 OOD), D²TR gains +0.03 IID and +0.08 OOD, with the largest margin in CN Target where UNICORN achieves only 0.53 IID vs. D²TR's 0.74. The multi-task baselines UPDeT (0.24 IID, 0.18 OOD) and ODIS (0.45 IID, 0.26 OOD) are substantially outperformed, confirming that the actor-critic backbone alone (Backbone: 0.69 IID, 0.51 OOD) already surpasses them. HiSSD (0.62 IID, 0.47 OOD) is competitive in Marine Hard (0.77 IID, 0.72 OOD) due to its fine-grained skill learning, but D²TR leads in all other environments. Among the context-based meta-RL baselines, CSRO (0.58 IID, 0.38 OOD) and FOCAL (0.59 IID, 0.40 OOD) suffer severe performance collapse in CN Target (0.17 and 0.52 IID respectively), while MG2L (0.69 IID, 0.51 OOD) shows that its global encoding strategy, effective online, fails to transfer to the offline decentralized setting. D²TR's advantage is most pronounced in OOD settings, where the role encoding provides transferable individual-centric knowledge that task encodings alone cannot capture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends context-based offline meta RL (FOCAL, CSRO, UNICORN) from single-agent to multi-agent settings, where the decentralized execution paradigm limits each agent's access to global information. It draws on knowledge distillation from POMDP literature to bridge the gap between centralized and decentralized task encodings via mutual information maximization (Theorem 4.1), and departs from prior role-based MARL work (ROMA, RODE, TRAMA) by using LLMs to extract semantic role labels from offline trajectories rather than performing online self-supervised role discovery. The policy backbone builds on OMIGA's implicit Q-learning framework, while the Transformer-based architecture follows the variable-length observation handling introduced by UPDeT and ODIS.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete gap in multi-agent offline meta RL: task encodings alone are insufficient for OOD generalization because they capture only team-centric objectives, while individual role information enables skill recombination across tasks. The LLM-assisted role labeling pipeline is a practical and extensible technique, though its effectiveness depends on label quality (as shown by the clustering-based ablation failing in OOD). Open questions include scalability to larger, more complex environments beyond SMACv2, the computational cost of the LLM labeling pipeline at scale, and whether the approach generalizes to truly open-ended tasks without any predefined subgoal structure. The reliance on GPT-5 for role labeling also raises reproducibility and cost concerns for the broader community.
