## Problem

Offline cooperative MARL datasets are typically mixtures of diverse cooperative behaviors, yielding highly multimodal joint behavior distributions. Existing policy-regularization methods decompose the joint policy into independent per-agent marginals, which the paper formalizes as a Combinatorial Mode Shift: each agent regresses to its own marginal, and the reconstructed joint policy spreads mass over exponentially many incoherent action combinations, causing severe out-of-distribution joint actions. No prior method provides a modality-coordinated regularization that respects inter-agent dependencies in the joint behavior distribution without requiring a full joint policy model or a centralized planner.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a fixed offline dataset of joint state-action transitions (from MPE or MaMuJoCo environments at various quality levels: expert, medium, random) and each agent's local observation. During training, agent i's diffusion score model takes as input the concatenated state and prefix actions a_{<i} from previously updated agents; during execution, all agents act concurrently from their own local observations only, producing deterministic continuous actions via DiLac policies. The sequential conditioning is strictly a training-time mechanism and imposes no ordering constraint at test time.

## Method

OMSD replaces the standard independent factorization μ(a|s)=∏ᵢμᵢ(aᵢ|s) with an exact chain-rule sequential decomposition μ(a|s)=∏ᵢμᵢ(aᵢ|s,a_{<i}), so each agent's KL-regularization references its conditional behavior distribution given prefix actions rather than its marginal. Per-agent conditional diffusion models (2-layer U-Net, 512 hidden units) are pretrained in parallel on the offline dataset to approximate the score ∇_{aᵢ}log μᵢ(aᵢ|s,a_{<i}) at low noise levels (t→0), following the SRPO distillation trick. The policy gradient for agent i combines the centralized IQL critic gradient ∇_{aᵢ}Q^{tot}(s,a) with the conditional score term (1/β)∇_{aᵢ}log μᵢ(aᵢ|s,a_{<i}), where prefix actions a_{<i}^{new} are sampled from the most recently updated policies of agents 1 through i−1 within the same iteration. This sequential update order is only used during training; execution remains fully decentralized and parallel, and the diffusion models serve solely as score estimators rather than action samplers, avoiding iterative denoising at inference time.

## Results

On the OMAR MPE benchmark (3 tasks × 3 dataset qualities), OMSD achieves an average normalized score of 126.7±11.4, outperforming the best baseline CFCQL (87.3±16.9) by 33.2%. Gains are largest on medium and random datasets where multimodal behavior is most pronounced, e.g., +70.6% on Predator-Prey Random and +107.5% on World Random. On the OMIGA MaMuJoCo benchmark (Table 3), OMSD achieves an average of 3400±90, a 73.9% improvement over the strongest baseline OMIGA (1954.74±313.48), with especially large gains on 3-Hopper (+329% to +403% across datasets). OMSD underperforms on a few expert-quality MaMuJoCo tasks (e.g., 2-Ant Good: −7.9%), where the pretrained critic provides limited improvement headroom.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OMSD outperforms all seven baselines on the MPE average (126.7 vs. CFCQL 87.3, DoF-P 75.6, MADiff-D 68.5, OMAR 64.7, MA-TD3+BC 57.8, MA-ICQ 55.6, MA-CQL 49.8), with the largest margins on medium and random datasets where multimodal behavior is most severe. Against the two diffusion-based baselines, OMSD beats MADiff-D on 8 of 9 MPE tasks and DoF-P on 7 of 9, with the exception being Coop. Nav. Expert where DoF-P edges ahead (126.3 vs. 102.3). On MaMuJoCo (OMAR datasets), OMSD wins on 8 of 9 tasks, losing only on 2-Ant Good (−7.9%) and 4-Ant Good (−7.7%), where the expert data provides limited room for improvement. On the OMIGA MaMuJoCo benchmark (Table 3), OMSD achieves a 73.9% average improvement over OMIGA, the strongest value-decomposition baseline, with gains ranging from +6.6% (2-Ant Expert) to +403% (3-Hopper Medium-Expert). The paper attributes OMSD's advantage over MADiff-D and DoF-P to using diffusion models purely as conditional score estimators for policy regularization rather than as trajectory planners or full policy generators, avoiding iterative denoising at execution time and compounding sampling errors on low-quality data.

## Relation to Prior Work

OMSD builds directly on the BRPO framework (Wu et al., 2019) for behavior-regularized offline policy optimization and the SRPO approach (Chen et al., 2024) of distilling score functions from pretrained diffusion models, extending both from single-agent to multi-agent settings. The key conceptual departure is from prior offline MARL methods (AlberDICE, DOM2, DoF) that assume independent factorization of the joint behavior policy μ(a|s)=∏μ_i(a_i|s); OMSD replaces this with a sequential conditional decomposition μ(a|s)=∏μ_i(a_i|s,a_<i), formalized through the Combinatorial Mode Shift (CMS) proposition showing that independent factorization induces TV distance → 1 as agent count grows. For the centralized critic, OMSD adopts IQL (Kostrikov et al., 2022) rather than value-decomposition methods (QMIX-style IGM), and uses diffusion models purely as conditional score estimators for policy-gradient regularization rather than as trajectory planners (MADiff) or full policy generators (DoF).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CMS analysis provides a clean theoretical explanation for a long-observed but poorly understood failure mode in offline MARL: independent per-agent regularization systematically misaligns joint policies when the dataset is multimodal, a condition that is the norm rather than the exception in practice. The large empirical gains on medium and random datasets (up to +107% on MPE World Random) demonstrate that the multimodality problem is not merely theoretical but a primary bottleneck for current methods. Key limitations include dependence on a pretrained centralized critic (weak critics cap improvement), validation only up to 6 agents in continuous control, and underperformance on some expert datasets where the behavior policy is already unimodal. Open questions include extending to discrete/hybrid action spaces, scaling to larger agent teams, and integrating with more robust value-based offline MARL critics to handle very low-quality data.
