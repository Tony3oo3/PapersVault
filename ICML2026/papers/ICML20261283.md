## Problem

Reconstructing high-resolution urban wind fields from sparse sensor observations on unstructured meshes remains challenging: classical reduced-order data assimilation loses explicit geometric and obstacle-induced structure, while supervised graph neural networks produce deterministic predictions that cannot incorporate new observations at inference time. No existing framework combines a learned, geometry-aware flow prior with a generative posterior reconstruction mechanism that generalizes to unseen mesh geometries, wind directions, and sensor layouts without retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261283/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 2D unstructured mesh (~3×10⁵ nodes, ~1.7×10⁶ edges) with fluid/wall/boundary node-type annotations, a global inflow wind direction angle (one of 360 discrete values), and sparse sensor readings encoded as a binary mask plus velocity values at selected nodes (0.1%–5% coverage). Each mesh is paired offline with a coarsened version (~5.5×10⁴ nodes) via optimal decimation that preserves building footprints. The output is a full 2D velocity field (Ux, Uy) on the original-resolution mesh, generated as a stochastic ensemble of physically plausible reconstructions rather than a single deterministic estimate, enabling uncertainty-aware inference.

## Method

GenDA is a multiscale graph-based score diffusion model (EDM formulation) whose denoiser is a hierarchical GNN operating on four edge sets (o2o, o2r, r2r, r2o) spanning the original and reduced meshes, enabling both local fine-scale corrections near obstacles and efficient long-range communication. The key insight is interpreting classifier-free guidance as tempered posterior sampling: sensor-dropout training (p_uc=0.1) yields an unconditional branch that learns a geometry-conditioned flow prior and a sensor-conditioned branch that injects observational constraints, and at inference the guidance weight γ blends them as D̃ = D_uncond + γ[D_cond − D_uncond], equivalent to sampling from p_γ(u|y,G) ∝ p(u|G)·p(y|u)^γ. This formulation avoids explicit linearization, adjoint solvers, or fixed reduced subspaces, and the multiscale hierarchy is most critical under sparse observations where long-range propagation of limited sensor information across complex urban geometry is needed.

## Results

On the MODELAIR Bristol RANS dataset (two held-out altitude slices, ~3×10⁵ nodes per mesh), GenDA achieves RRMSE of 0.310 / 0.190 / 0.136 and MAC of 0.938 / 0.977 / 0.988 at 300 / 3000 / 8000 observations, respectively, consistently outperforming all five baselines. The paper reports 25–57% RRMSE reduction and 23–33% SSIM improvement over the strongest competing methods across tested densities. At the sparsest setting (300 sensors, ~0.1% coverage), GenDA reduces RRMSE by 27% relative to MGN (0.426→0.310) and by 14% relative to the best reduced-order baseline LCSVD (0.360→0.310).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenDA outperforms all five baselines on RRMSE and MAC at every tested observation density (300, 3000, 8000). Against the deterministic GNN baselines, the gap is largest in the sparse regime: at 300 observations GenDA achieves RRMSE 0.310 versus 0.426 (MGN) and 0.412 (MS-MGN), a 27% and 25% reduction respectively, while also improving SSIM from ~0.66–0.68 to 0.834. Against the reduced-order DA baselines (LCSVD, POD-3DVar), GenDA wins on RRMSE at all densities (e.g., 0.136 vs. 0.354 at 8000 obs.) and on MAC (0.988 vs. ~0.92), though LCSVD and POD-3DVar remain competitive on SSIM at 300 observations (0.831/0.814 vs. GenDA's 0.834). The most informative comparison is against MultiMesh-VP, which shares the identical multiscale graph backbone but uses direct observation conditioning instead of classifier-free guidance: GenDA reduces RRMSE by 25% at 300 obs. (0.310 vs. 0.415), 26% at 3000 obs. (0.190 vs. 0.258), and 28% at 8000 obs. (0.136 vs. 0.189), isolating the contribution of the CFG-based posterior formulation. On held-out sensor interpolation (Table 3), GenDA similarly leads all baselines, reaching RRMSE 0.160 and MAC 0.982 at 8000 observations versus 0.222/0.969 for MultiMesh-VP.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the geometry-conditioned urban flow generation framework of Giral et al. (2025) into a full data assimilation setting by repurposing classifier-free guidance (CFG) as a learned posterior reconstruction operator: the unconditional branch encodes a geometry-aware flow prior, while the sensor-conditioned branch injects observational constraints, with the guidance weight γ acting as an inverse temperature on the likelihood. The multiscale graph backbone is inherited from MeshGraphNets and Multiscale MeshGraphNets, and the diffusion training/sampling follows the EDM formulation and GenCast norm-conditioning scheme. Conceptually, the paper departs from classical reduced-order DA (POD-3DVar, SVD-based assimilation) by avoiding fixed linear subspaces and adjoint solvers, and from supervised GNN surrogates by enabling inference-time adaptation to new sensor layouts and unseen geometries without retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a scalable, retraining-free path to generative data assimilation on unstructured meshes, producing uncertainty-aware ensembles rather than deterministic point estimates—valuable for real-time urban air-quality monitoring where sensor networks are sparse and heterogeneous. However, validation is limited to steady 2D RANS slices from a single Bristol neighborhood, with no enforcement of PDE residuals, mass conservation, or boundary conditions as hard constraints. Key open questions include extension to 3D volumetric domains, temporal forecasting with streaming sensors, joint learning across altitudes, and integration of physics-based constraints within the generative model to improve physical consistency beyond what the learned prior captures.
