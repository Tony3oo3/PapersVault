## Problem

Architectural role tags (e.g., <user>, <tool>, <assistant>) are the foundational defense against prompt injection, yet attacks routinely succeed at near-100% rates against models with near-perfect safety benchmarks. Prior behavioral studies showed role boundaries fail to constrain output but could not distinguish whether models fail to *perceive* the correct role or perceive it correctly yet ignore the hierarchy. This paper identifies the root cause as a representational failure: models encode stylistic cues and architectural tags onto the same latent feature, so when they conflict, attacker-controllable style dominates perception.

## Input / Output

The role probes consume token-level hidden states extracted from a target LLM at a chosen layer, trained on non-instruct text sequences (from C4 and Dolma3) where identical content is wrapped in each of five role tags, forcing the probe to learn only the tag-induced geometric signature. Output is a per-token probability distribution over roles (CoTness, Userness, Assistantness, Toolness, Systemness), which is then aggregated over injected text spans to produce a single confusion score. This score predicts attack success before any output token is generated, with a near-monotonic dose-response relationship (e.g., lowest CoTness quantile: 9% ASR; highest: 90%).

## Method

The paper introduces role probes: multinomial logistic regression classifiers trained on hidden states where the only varying signal across classes is the enclosing role tag (content held constant via non-instruct pretraining text). This design ensures probes capture the model's internal tag geometry rather than stylistic confounds. The key empirical finding is that these tag-trained probes fire strongly on untagged text that merely *sounds* like a role (e.g., CoT-style text without  tags still registers at 83% CoTness), revealing that style and tags write to the same latent direction. The paper then introduces CoT Forgery, a zero-shot black-box attack that injects fabricated chain-of-thought reasoning into user or tool channels; the model mistakes the forgery for its own reasoning, achieving 60% ASR on StrongREJECT against models with near-zero baselines. Ablations confirm style is causal (destyling collapses ASR from 61% to 10%), and the probes' confusion scores predict attack success across both CoT Forgery and standard agent prompt injections (1,000 exfiltration attempts, 2% to 70% ASR across quantiles).

## Results

CoT Forgery achieves 60% average attack success rate (ASR) on StrongREJECT across six frontier models, compared to near-zero baselines (0–4% ASR) for raw harmful prompts and standard jailbreaks. In the agent hijacking setting, CoT Forgery raises ASR from 0–2% (standard prompt injection) to 56–70% across all models. Role confusion, measured by role probes, predicts attack success before any token is generated: the lowest-confusion quantile succeeds 9% of the time while the highest succeeds 90% (CoT Forgery), and 2% vs. 70% for standard agent injections. Destyling the forged CoT collapses ASR from 61% to 10%, confirming style as the causal driver.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against competing defense or attack methods in the traditional sense; instead it benchmarks CoT Forgery against the models' own safety baselines. All six tested models (gpt-oss-20b, gpt-oss-120b, o4-mini, GPT-5 nano/mini/full) report near-perfect safety scores on standard jailbreak benchmarks, yet CoT Forgery breaks these defenses with 17–80%+ ASR depending on model. In the agent setting, standard prompt injection (a fake user command in tool output) largely fails at 0–2% ASR, while CoT Forgery (the same command plus forged reasoning) achieves 56–70%, a 28–35× improvement. The style ablation serves as the critical internal comparison: removing CoT-style markers while preserving the identical argument drops ASR from 61% to 10%, isolating style as the causal mechanism rather than argument content. Cross-model validation on four architectures (gpt-oss-20b, gpt-oss-120b, Nemotron-3, Qwen3-30B-A3B) confirms that user-style text retains 76–88% Userness even when wrapped in <tool> tags, with Toolness never exceeding 20%, demonstrating the vulnerability is architecture-independent.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the linear probing methodology (Alain & Bengio, 2018) and the instruction hierarchy framework (Wallace et al., 2024), but departs from prior work by moving from behavioral evidence to representational mechanism. Prior studies (Zhang et al., 2025; Geng et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2025b) demonstrated that role boundaries fail behaviorally—swapping instructions between roles leaves output unchanged—but could not distinguish whether the model misperceives roles or perceives them correctly yet ignores the hierarchy. This paper resolves that ambiguity by showing, via controlled probes trained exclusively on tag geometry, that style and tags converge onto the same latent feature, making the failure perceptual rather than one of obedience. The CoT Forgery attack is positioned as a diagnostic instrument that succeeds only if perception fails, complementing the broader prompt injection literature (Willison, 2022; Abdelnabi et al., 2023) with a mechanistic account.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that architectural role tags—the foundational security boundary of LLMs—are effectively invisible in latent space reframes prompt injection from a pattern-matching problem to a representational geometry problem, implying that current defenses (which memorize attack patterns) are structurally insufficient. Role probes offer a concrete diagnostic tool for evaluating whether proposed defenses reshape latent geometry or merely add heuristics. Key limitations include testing only 20–120B models and assuming roles occupy directional subspaces (validated via downstream prediction but not proven). Open questions include whether genuine role perception is achievable through training or architecture changes, whether the framework unifies other attack families, and whether role probes can serve as real-time injection detectors by flagging discrepancies between parsed and perceived roles.
