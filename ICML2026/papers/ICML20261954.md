## Problem

Existing adversarial attacks on cooperative MARL under explicit attack budgets are inefficient: planner-based methods (Wolfpack) and population-based search (EGA) require repeated attacks or extensive search to achieve strong disruption, and flat adversarial policies struggle to jointly optimize attack timing, victim selection, and action replacement over a vast combinatorial space. This results in weak adversarial training signals and only modest robustness improvements when only a small number of critical timesteps are compromised.

## Input / Output

The adversary consumes joint agent observations (each agent's local observation combined with a mean-aggregated global context via a shared MLP encoder) and the remaining episode-level budget B_t as input. It outputs a victim set (up to K agents selected sequentially without replacement per timestep) and per-agent adversarial replacement actions, subject to the hard constraint that at most B timesteps per episode may be attacked. The full BHEA-AT framework additionally produces a robustified victim policy trained against a pool of adversary snapshots sampled with rank-based recency weighting.

## Method

BHEA factorizes the adversarial policy into a victim selector π_sel (deciding when to attack and which agents to hijack) and an action attacker π_act (determining replacement actions), each optimized independently via PPO with separate value functions and GAE. The selector is parameterized as an (N+1)-class categorical policy over agents plus a no-op outcome, with action masking enforcing budget and capacity constraints; for K>1, victims are selected autoregressively with updated feasible sets. This hierarchical decomposition decouples the high-level timing/coordination decision from low-level action manipulation, reducing gradient variance and enabling more effective credit assignment than a flat joint policy. BHEA-AT alternates adversary evolution (PPO updates against a fixed victim) with victim robustification (training against adversaries sampled from a capped pool using a rank-based recency distribution), requiring no planning or population search and reducing training time by 35–60% compared to ROMANCE and WALL.

## Results

Under the paper's own BHEA attack (the most stringent stress test), BHEA-AT maintains 95.1–99.5% win rate across all six SMAC scenarios, while ROMANCE drops to 36.2–85.1% and WALL to 26.2–69.2%. Under the Wolfpack attack, BHEA-AT achieves 91.3–99.2% win rate, outperforming ROMANCE (81.3–95.1%) and WALL (81.2–99.1%). In terms of training efficiency, BHEA-AT requires only 16–18 hours per scenario, compared to 25–30 hours for ROMANCE and 43–47 hours for WALL, while achieving stronger robustness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ROMANCE (Yuan et al., 2023), BHEA-AT achieves substantially higher win rates under the BHEA attack: 99.2% vs 80.2% on MMM, 95.1% vs 85.1% on 2s3z, and 93.1% vs 45.1% on 8m. Under the Wolfpack attack, BHEA-AT also leads (e.g., 98.3% vs 91.2% on MMM, 92.1% vs 81.3% on 8m). Against WALL (Lee et al., 2025), the gap is even more pronounced under BHEA attack: 99.2% vs 63.1% on MMM and 95.1% vs 45.2% on 2s3z. In terms of training cost, BHEA-AT reduces wall-clock time by approximately 35–40% relative to ROMANCE and by more than 60% relative to WALL (16–18 h vs 25–30 h vs 43–47 h per scenario), while simultaneously achieving stronger robustness. On MPE benchmarks (Appendix H), BHEA-AT similarly outperforms WALL across all simple_spread and simple_tag configurations under both natural and adversarial settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the LPA-Dec-POMDP formulation introduced by Yuan et al. (2023, ROMANCE/EGA), which first formalized a budget-constrained action-hijacking adversary in CMARL. It departs from the two dominant attack paradigms—planner-based (Wolfpack, Lee et al. 2025) and population-based evolutionary search (EGA/ROMANCE)—by replacing explicit procedural planning or population diversity with a single end-to-end hierarchical policy optimized via PPO. The key architectural departure is the factorization of the adversarial decision into a budget-aware victim selector (when/which) and an independent action attacker (how), which the authors show is strictly equivalent in expressivity to a flat policy for K=1 yet substantially easier to optimize. The adversarial training loop (BHEA-AT) follows the alternating attacker-victim paradigm common to ROMANCE and WALL but simplifies the pool mechanism to a rank-based recency sampler.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is twofold: BHEA provides a much stronger stress-test for cooperative policies under realistic limited-budget threats (dropping Vanilla QMIX to ~10% win rate on several SMAC maps versus ~40–50% for Wolfpack under the same budget), and BHEA-AT achieves comparable or better robustness at 35–60% lower training cost than existing adversarial training frameworks. This makes robust CMARL training more accessible and scalable. Key limitations include evaluation only on small-team SMAC and MPE benchmarks, a strong full-observability attacker assumption, and simulation-only settings that do not capture real-world noise or physical constraints. Open questions include scalability to larger agent populations, adaptive or multi-round attack budgets, and transfer to continuous-action or safety-critical deployment domains.
