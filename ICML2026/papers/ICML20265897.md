## Problem

Existing decentralized minimax optimization algorithms communicate full-precision variables and gradients, creating a severe communication bottleneck for large-scale models. While the error feedback mechanism has proven effective for reducing compression error in decentralized minimization, it has never been applied to decentralized minimax problems because the interaction between primal and dual variables introduces circular dependencies among consensus errors and compression errors that make convergence analysis intractable.

## Input / Output

Each of K workers holds a local partition of the training data and communicates only compressed differences (via a Top-K sparsification operator with ratio K=10%) with its neighbors over a peer-to-peer graph (ring, torus, or random). The algorithm maintains a local copy of the compression residual for every neighbor, so the communicated payload is the compressed difference between the full-precision intermediate value and the accumulated residual rather than the variable itself. The output is an ε-accuracy minimax solution satisfying (1/T)Σ E[‖∇F(x̄_t)‖²] ≤ O(ε²), with iteration complexity T = O(κ⁴/(K·δ³·(1−λ)⁴·ε³)).

## Method

CE-DSGDAR combines STORM momentum-based gradient estimators with a gradient-tracking communication strategy, applying error feedback to both the primal/dual variables and their gradient trackers. The key algorithmic design insight is an asymmetric balance coefficient: the variable update uses α_x·η (scaling with the learning rate) to balance the full-precision local step against the compression residual, while the gradient tracker update uses α_u (independent of η), mirroring the structure of the full-precision DM-HSGD algorithm. For convergence analysis, the authors construct a 12-term potential function unifying gradient estimation error, consensus error, and compression error, then resolve the circular dependence among coefficients (e.g., c₅ depends on c₇ and c₉, c₇ depends on c₅ and c₉, c₉ depends on c₅ and c₇) by expressing some coefficients in terms of others and fixing the independent terms first. This yields the first theoretical guarantee for a communication-efficient decentralized minimax algorithm, with explicit dependence on the compression contraction constant δ and the spectral gap (1−λ) of the communication graph.

## Results

On AUC maximization (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, STL-10, CATvsDOG) and fair classification (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, STL-10), CE-DSGDAR with Top-10% sparsification achieves nearly identical test AUC scores and test accuracy as full-precision baselines (DSGDA, DM-HSGD, DSGDAM) across all datasets, while substantially reducing communicated megabytes. The algorithm uses 8 workers on a LINE graph with batch size 32 per worker. Ablation studies confirm robustness to communication topology (ring, torus, random), compression ratio (Top-5% vs Top-10%), and hyperparameter choices (α_x, α_y, α_u, α_v). On the a9a robust logistic regression task (Appendix A), CE-DSGDAR similarly matches the loss function trajectory of full-precision methods including DGDA-VR at lower communication cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares CE-DSGDAR against three full-precision decentralized minimax baselines: DSGDA (Tsaknakis et al., 2020), DM-HSGD (Xian et al., 2021), and DSGDAM (a moving-average variant of DM-HSGD). Across all four AUC maximization datasets and three fair classification datasets, CE-DSGDAR achieves 'almost the same' AUC score and test accuracy as all three full-precision baselines, while incurring significantly lower communication costs (measured in megabytes) due to Top-10% sparsification. The paper does not report specific numerical gaps but the figures show CE-DSGDAR tracking the full-precision curves closely in the epoch-vs-metric plots while being well-separated in the MB-vs-metric plots. In the additional a9a experiment (Appendix A), CE-DSGDAR also matches DGDA-VR (Zhang et al., 2024a), a variance-reduced method requiring full gradient computation, on loss function value at reduced communication cost. The key takeaway is that compression does not degrade convergence quality but yields substantial communication savings, confirming the theoretical prediction that the error feedback mechanism preserves the convergence rate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the error feedback mechanism—previously applied to decentralized minimization (Koloskova et al., 2020; Zhao et al., 2022) and centralized minimax (Zhang et al., 2023b)—to the decentralized stochastic minimax setting, which had no prior theoretical treatment. It builds directly on the gradient-tracking structure of DM-HSGD (Xian et al., 2021) and the STORM momentum estimator (Cutkosky & Orabona, 2019), adding compressed communication to both variables and gradient trackers. The key conceptual departure is the novel balance-coefficient design: the variable update coefficient scales with the learning rate η while the gradient tracker coefficient does not, a distinction absent from prior minimization-focused error feedback methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide convergence guarantees for communication-efficient decentralized minimax optimization, filling a gap between the full-precision decentralized minimax literature and the compressed-communication minimization literature. The convergence rate reveals an explicit O(δ^{-3}(1-λ)^{-4}) dependence, showing that compression introduces a high-order penalty on the spectral gap compared to the O((1-λ)^{-2}) of full-precision methods—a tradeoff practitioners must weigh. Open questions include whether the high-order spectral gap dependence can be mitigated, whether the approach extends to non-strongly-concave or non-smooth minimax problems, and whether the η-dependent balance coefficient design generalizes to other compression operators beyond Top-K.
