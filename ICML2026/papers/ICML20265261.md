## Problem

In synchronous RL training for LLMs, the rollout phase is dominated by long-tail bubbles where faster data-parallel ranks idle while waiting for stragglers, wasting over 60% of GPU time. Existing mitigations either break strict synchrony (async RL, partial rollout) or rely on cross-epoch response similarity, which suffers a cold-start problem on large-scale datasets where a single epoch spans days to weeks. The paper asks: can we exploit these inevitable bubbles to accelerate rollouts while preserving the mathematical exactness of synchronous RL?

## Input / Output

The method consumes a batch of prompts (64 prompts × 16 samples per prompt = 1024 total sequences) along with the current policy model weights, and additionally leverages partial rollouts pre-generated during the previous step's inter-GPU idle windows. These pre-generated sequences (up to 2/3 of full response length) are indexed into per-prompt suffix trees sharded across rollout workers. The output is a set of complete rollout responses whose token distribution is mathematically identical to standard autoregressive sampling, with decoding steps reduced by ~50% and rollout throughput improved by up to 1.8×.

## Method

BubbleSpec converts inter-GPU rollout bubbles into a cross-step pipelining mechanism: when a DP rank finishes its current batch early, it proactively pre-generates 16 responses per prompt for the next step, which are then organized into suffix trees for model-free draft retrieval. At each decoding step, a fixed-length draft block (4 tokens) is retrieved via prefix matching against the suffix tree, and verified sequentially under the current policy using rejection sampling with a degenerate proposal (accept with probability p_t(x̃_t)), guaranteeing exact distribution preservation without any separate draft model. A key systems contribution is a unified attention operator that processes both normal decode queries and short speculative verification queries in a single CUDA kernel launch, eliminating the ~3× latency overhead of batch-split prefill/decode attention that would otherwise offset the step-reduction gains. The approach requires no warm-up epoch, no cross-epoch policy similarity assumption, and is algorithm-agnostic (validated on SAPO, GRPO, GSPO), making it a plug-and-play module for any synchronous RL framework.

## Results

BubbleSpec reduces average decoding steps by 48.9%–56.8% and maximum decoding steps by 43.8%–59.6% across Qwen3-1.7B, Qwen3-4B, and Qwen2.5-VL-7B, translating to rollout time reductions of 30.0%–45.2% and throughput improvements of 1.4×–1.8×. The average acceptance length is ~2 tokens with a draft length of ~3.8 tokens and acceptance rates of 21.7%–29.9%. Accuracy after RL training is preserved: on Qwen2.5-VL-7B, BubbleSpec achieves 79.58 on AIME24 and 51.41 on Geo3K (OOD), comparable to Verl's 80.72 and 48.25, confirming lossless speculative decoding.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the vanilla Verl baseline, BubbleSpec consistently wins on both rollout time and decoding steps across all three model sizes, with the largest gains on the smaller Qwen3-1.7B (45.2% rollout time reduction) and diminishing but still substantial gains on Qwen2.5-VL-7B (30.0% reduction), attributed to higher computational intensity at larger model sizes. Against RollPacker (a synchronous batch-reordering method), BubbleSpec achieves a significantly lower average rollout time of 397 s versus 655 s on Qwen2.5-VL-7B; RollPacker's tail rounds far exceed BubbleSpec's per-step time, and its gains depend heavily on the sparsity of long responses, whereas BubbleSpec maintains consistent speedup even when bubble capacity is limited. On accuracy, BubbleSpec matches Verl within noise (e.g., 79.58 vs. 80.72 on AIME24; 51.41 vs. 48.25 on Geo3K OOD), confirming that the speculative decoding is mathematically lossless and does not degrade RL training quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

BubbleSpec sits at the intersection of speculative decoding for LLM inference and efficient RL training systems. It builds directly on the rejection-sampling-based speculative decoding framework (Leviathan et al., 2023) and the suffix-tree pattern matching of model-free suffix decoding (Oliaro et al., 2025), but departs from prior RL-specific speculative methods (Rhyme-RL, SpecRL) that rely on cross-epoch response similarity and require a warm-up epoch. The key conceptual departure is reframing inter-GPU rollout bubbles—from a waste to be eliminated (as in asynchronous RL or RollPacker's batch reordering)—into a computational resource for pre-generating next-step drafts, while the unified attention operator addresses a previously underappreciated source of per-step latency inflation in speculative verification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BubbleSpec addresses a practical bottleneck (rollout phase consuming >70% of RL training time) with a plug-and-play solution that requires no algorithmic modifications and provides immediate speedup from step one, making it particularly relevant for large-scale RL training where epoch-based history is unavailable or stale. The strict synchronous guarantee (mathematically identical output distribution) is important given recent evidence that off-policy or asynchronous rollouts destabilize training. Limitations include diminishing speedup at larger model sizes (computation-bound regime) and dependence on sufficient inter-GPU bubble time; open questions include multi-node scaling, whether intra-GPU bubble exploitation can be stabilized via memory-bandwidth isolation, and how draft quality evolves as the policy diverges significantly within a single epoch.
