## Problem

LLMs degrade sharply on long-horizon reasoning tasks where errors compound across dependent steps, yet RL training for reasoning is bottlenecked by the scarcity of verifiable long-horizon data. This paper asks whether long-horizon reasoning capabilities can be bootstrapped by scaling RL using only abundant, existing short-horizon training data, without new annotations or step-level supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261830/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a single prompt containing h chained sub-problems in sequence, where each sub-problem's numeric input is derived from the previous sub-problem's answer via a lightweight adapter (identity, scaling, or unit conversion). The model produces a chain-of-thought trace solving all sub-problems sequentially, ending with a final numerical answer between <answer> tags. Supervision is outcome-only: the reward signal depends solely on whether the final answer y_h is correct, with no intermediate step labels. Max completion length scales with horizon (768 tokens for h=1 up to 1536 for h≥4).

## Method

The method has two components: (1) a chained problem construction pipeline that composes atomic verifiable problems (e.g., GSM8K) into dependency chains of arbitrary length h using deterministic adapters, with two chaining strategies—transformation chaining (rewriting parameters symbolically so answers stay fixed) and substitution chaining (recomputing downstream answers); and (2) a stagewise curriculum RL training loop using Dr. GRPO with outcome-only rewards, where the model is trained sequentially on horizons h=1,2,…,H_max, carrying parameters forward between stages. The key insight is that long-horizon accuracy depends on horizon-specific reliability factors σ_j (state tracking, intermediate value management) beyond atomic step accuracy p, and that curriculum training first raises p at short horizons where signal-to-noise is high, then improves σ_j at longer horizons. Theoretically, this yields an exponential reduction in sample complexity over direct full-horizon training, matching the scaling of dense per-step rewards while requiring only a single terminal reward.

## Results

On in-domain composed GSM8K problems, curriculum RL training up to horizon 5 (h1) achieves 58.51% accuracy at L-2, 36.39% at L-3, 18.77% at L-4, and 9.82% at L-5, representing 66.9%, 81.3%, 180.1%, and 175.1% relative improvements over the Instruct model baseline. On harder out-of-domain benchmarks, the same training yields a 106.3% improvement on AIME 2024 (5.10→10.52), 70.6% on AIME 2025 (1.77→3.02), 20.7% on GSM-Symbolic P2 (43.08→52.00), and 7.8% on MATH-500 (64.20→69.20). Long-context benchmarks also improve: LongBench-v2 rises from 35.00% to 37.90% and Hash-hop from 15.98% to 18.73%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares h1 (curriculum RL on composed GSM8K) against five baselines: the untrained Instruct model, three equal-compute RL variants (Only-L1, Uniform-Mix, Only-Long), and Tree-of-Thought (inference-time search). Curriculum training decisively outperforms all baselines at every horizon ≥ 2: at L-4 it reaches 18.77% versus 6.70% (Instruct), 6.70% (Only-L1), 0.54% (Uniform-Mix), 3.22% (Only-Long), and 2.00% (Tree-of-Thought). The Only-L1 and Uniform-Mix baselines show essentially no long-horizon gains (or even degrade), confirming that standard RLVR on single-step data cannot bootstrap multi-step capabilities. Tree-of-Thought, which increases inference-time compute, also fails to close the gap (39.40% at L-2 vs. 58.51% for h1), indicating the improvement comes from learned capabilities rather than search. On out-of-domain benchmarks (Table 2), h1 also beats standard GSM8K RLVR on every metric, with the largest margin on AIME 2024 (10.52 vs. 6.88) and GSM-Symbolic P2 (52.00 vs. 47.60).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the RLVR paradigm popularized by DeepSeek-R1 and OpenAI o1, using Dr. GRPO (Liu et al., 2025c) as the core optimization algorithm, but departs from standard RLVR by addressing the saturation problem identified by Cui et al. (2025) and Wu et al. (2025) through compositional data construction and a stagewise curriculum over horizon lengths. It positions itself against approaches requiring step-level labels, process reward models, or inference-time search (e.g., Tree-of-Thoughts), instead training the model to internalize long-horizon reasoning paths with outcome-only rewards. The theoretical analysis connects to the length generalization literature (Dubois et al., 2019; Anil et al., 2022) and adaptive difficulty scheduling (Shi et al., 2025; Parashar et al., 2025), but uniquely provides a sample-complexity proof showing curriculum RL achieves polynomial scaling where direct full-horizon training is exponential.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, annotation-free recipe for scaling RL training on long-horizon tasks by composing existing short-horizon problems, which is directly applicable to real-world settings where long-horizon data is scarce and expensive. The demonstration that curriculum RL teaches genuinely new capabilities (evidenced by pass@128 exceeding the base model) challenges the influential finding of Yue et al. (2025) that RLVR is bounded by base-model capabilities, suggesting the bound is specific to non-compositional training data. Limitations include evaluation primarily on 3B models, reliance on serial (not DAG) compositions, and training on 6th-grade-level GSM8K; open questions include whether the approach scales to frontier models, more complex dependency structures, and non-mathematical atomic tasks.
