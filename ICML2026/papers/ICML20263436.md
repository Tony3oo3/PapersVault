## Problem

Standard Expert Parallelism (EP) assumes approximately balanced token routing across GPUs, but well-trained MoE models naturally exhibit imbalanced routing as experts specialize in domains. Under extreme imbalance, EP concentrates a disproportionate number of tokens on a few devices, causing compute-bound latency spikes and out-of-memory failures during post-training or inference, where parameter-altering load-balancing losses are inapplicable. No existing system-level mechanism redistributes both tokens and expert weights dynamically while preserving exact MoE computation.

## Input / Output

Per device, LLEP receives K-repeated token hidden representations (B_p × K × D), router indices (B_p × K), gating scores (B_p × K), and local expert weight matrices (D × H for each of M = N/P experts). It produces the standard MoE layer output (B_p × H), the per-token sum of gated expert FFN results. Unusually, the method also computes a per-batch global routing plan and a peer-to-peer weight transfer plan, meaning expert weight matrices are dynamically shipped between devices at runtime rather than remaining statically sharded.

## Method

LLEP first computes global per-expert loads across all EP ranks; if the imbalance ratio max(l)/mean(l) falls below threshold λ, it falls back to standard EP. Otherwise, it runs a Least-Loaded Assignment (LLA) algorithm that processes experts in decreasing load order: each expert's tokens are assigned to its native GPU up to a capacity threshold m_α = α·total_load/P, with excess tokens spilled to the least-loaded available GPUs (respecting a minimum-tokens-per-GEMM constraint m to avoid inefficient tiny transfers). A corresponding weight transfer plan is constructed so that any foreign expert whose tokens are assigned to a device is P2P-transferred there. The dispatch-compute-combine phase then executes All-to-All for token routing plus P2P for weight transfers, followed by grouped GEMMs for both native and foreign experts. LLEP supports full backward-pass gradient propagation, making it applicable to both training and inference without altering model behavior.

## Results

On a 128-expert MoE layer (4 active, D=H=2048) under 8-way EP, LLEP achieves up to 5× speedup over standard EP when 95% of tokens concentrate into a single expert, while maintaining near-identical throughput under balanced routing. Peak memory per GPU is reduced by up to 4× (5× for gpt-oss-120b configuration). End-to-end full-model throughput improves by 2.2× for gpt-oss-20b and 1.9× for gpt-oss-120b on Nemotron-Math data. In training (gpt-oss-20b, Zero-3 + CPU offloading), LLEP achieves 1.25× wall-clock speedup with comparable AIME'25 accuracy; in inference prefill on 32 GPUs, LLEP reaches 1.94× throughput versus 1.45× for EPLB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is Standard Expert Parallelism (EP, Shoeybi et al., 2019), which statically assigns each expert to one GPU and relies on All-to-All dispatch/combine. LLEP outperforms EP in every imbalanced scenario tested: up to 5–6× speedup and 4–5× peak-memory reduction at the MoE-layer level, translating to 1.9–2.2× end-to-end throughput gains on full gpt-oss models. Under perfectly balanced routing, LLEP matches EP throughput (within noise) thanks to its adaptive λ threshold that falls back to standard EP. A secondary comparison is EPLB (Liu et al., 2024), which replicates hot experts across devices for inference; LLEP surpasses EPLB in prefill throughput (1.94× vs. 1.45× over EP) and additionally supports training and backward-pass gradient propagation, whereas EPLB is inference-only and still susceptible to OOM under extreme imbalance. Unlike prior load-balancing approaches that modify model parameters (auxiliary losses, routing biases), LLEP preserves exact MoE computation, making it applicable to post-training and inference where parameter alteration is prohibited.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the standard Expert Parallelism (EP) dispatch-combine procedure from Megatron-LM (Shoeybi et al., 2019) by adding dynamic, load-aware re-routing of both tokens and expert weights across devices. It departs from prior load-balancing approaches that operate at the model level (auxiliary losses, routing biases) or replicate experts statically (EPLB, Liu et al. 2024), instead treating routing imbalance as a system-level scheduling problem solvable via a greedy least-loaded assignment. The method is positioned as an exact-computation algorithm that preserves MoE routing semantics, contrasting with approaches that alter model behavior for efficiency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LLEP addresses a practical failure mode (OOM crashes, severe latency spikes) that affects any MoE deployment under domain-specific post-training or inference, where load-balancing losses are inapplicable. The up-to-5× speedup and 4× peak-memory reduction, combined with the exact-computation guarantee, make it a drop-in replacement for standard EP in production MoE pipelines. Open questions include hardware-specific hyperparameter tuning (α, m, λ), multi-node extensions to limit inter-node P2P transfers, and kernel-level fusion of the All-to-All and weight-transfer operations to further reduce communication overhead.
