## Problem

Existing visual token sparsification methods for MLLMs rely on a static assumption of uniform spatial redundancy, which holds for natural images but breaks down for spatially sparse structured inputs like charts and documents where critical high-frequency information is localized and fragile. A one-size-fits-all sparsifier cannot simultaneously achieve aggressive compression on redundancy-dominated inputs while preserving the structural skeleton of sparse symbolic data, leading to disproportionate semantic degradation under high compression ratios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261221/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a high-resolution image (processed at dynamic resolution with MinPix=256×28×28, MaxPix=2048×28×28 by Qwen2.5-VL's vision encoder) and a text instruction, producing a sparsified visual token sequence of cardinality K=⌊ρN⌋ (with ρ∈{0.3, 0.2, 0.1}) that conditions autoregressive text generation. The routing decision is made from raw pixel-level gradient statistics before any token-level processing, and the sparsified tokens are either a direct subset (RSB) or a fused representation with positional encodings (SFB), both fed to the LLM alongside the text embeddings.

## Method

AD-BTS introduces a parameter-free Gradient-based Routing Gate that computes a structural flatness score (proportion of pixels with negligible L1 gradient magnitude) to bifurcate processing into two branches. For redundancy-dominated inputs (flatness ≤ δ=0.4), the Redundancy Selection Branch freezes all LoRA adapters and performs query-aware hard token selection via a differentiable Top-K mechanism that uses implicit differentiation with a bisection solver to find a dynamic bias enforcing the cardinality constraint. For spatially sparse structured inputs (flatness > δ), the Structural Fusion Branch activates conditional LoRA adapters (rank 256, ~85M total trainable parameters) and fuses the full feature map as context into the selected anchor tokens via multi-head cross-attention with learnable positional encodings, preserving structural topology. The entire framework is trained end-to-end with a cross-entropy language modeling loss plus a binary cross-entropy budget regularization term that drives the soft mask toward the discrete selection, requiring only 1.2% additional parameters over the frozen backbone.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-VL-7B, AD-BTS achieves 98.72% normalized average score at 30% token retention, surpassing the strongest training-based baseline VisionSelector (96.90%) and the best training-free method VisionZip (93.57%). Under extreme 10% retention, AD-BTS maintains 88.43% normalized accuracy, outperforming VisionSelector (87.36%), FastV (75.35%), and DART (68.32%). On the structure-sensitive ChartQA benchmark at 20% retention, AD-BTS scores 80.2 (relaxed accuracy) versus 68.12 for VisionSelector and 52.56 for VisionZip. On video benchmarks, AD-BTS reduces peak GPU memory to 16.76 GB (35% below the 25.97 GB baseline) and prefill latency to 746 ms (47% reduction) while matching or exceeding baseline accuracy on MVBench (67.03 vs. 68.10) and SEEDBench (60.37 vs. 60.48).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AD-BTS outperforms all seven baselines across every compression regime on the normalized average score. The closest competitor is VisionSelector (Zhu et al., 2025), the only other end-to-end learnable method; AD-BTS leads by 1.82 points at 30% retention (98.72% vs. 96.90%) and by 1.07 points at 10% (88.43% vs. 87.36%), with the gap widening most on ChartQA (83.68 vs. 73.04 at 30%; 68.24 vs. 61.60 at 10%). Among training-free methods, VisionZip (Yang et al., 2024) is the strongest at 30% (93.57%) but degrades sharply to 72.73% at 10%, whereas AD-BTS retains 88.43%. FastV (Chen et al., 2024) and DivPrune (Alvar et al., 2025) show moderate performance at 30% (91.56% and 92.27%) but collapse to 75.35% and 75.61% respectively at 10%, confirming their vulnerability to structural sparsity. DART (Wen et al., 2025) and Prumerge+ (Shang et al., 2024) are the weakest baselines, scoring only 68.32% and 71.48% at 10% retention. The Dynamic (Rao et al., 2021) baseline, despite its adaptive gating, underperforms all other methods at every compression level (88.75% at 30%, 79.77% at 10%), suggesting that layer-wise adaptivity without input-aware routing is insufficient for structure-sensitive inputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the visual token sparsification line of work (FastV, DART, VisionZip, DivPrune, Prumerge+) by challenging their shared assumption of uniform spatial redundancy. Rather than applying a single static operator (pruning or merging) to all inputs, AD-BTS introduces a density-aware routing mechanism that decouples processing into a frozen selection branch for redundant inputs and a structure-preserving fusion branch (with conditional LoRA) for spatially sparse structured inputs like charts. The differentiable top-k selection via implicit differentiation and the Dynamic Adapter Modulation (gating LoRA on input type) are technical departures from prior work that either activates adapters persistently or operates independently of token compression.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial for deploying high-resolution MLLMs: a 1.8× prefill speedup at 20% token retention with only ~1.3% accuracy loss on the average, and a 12.1% gain on ChartQA over the strongest baseline. The key open question is whether the global flatness-based routing generalizes to region-level granularity, which the authors identify as the natural next step. Limitations include reliance on a single threshold δ, evaluation primarily on Qwen2.5-VL, and the fact that the SFB's LoRA adapters add ~85M parameters (1.2%) that must be stored alongside the frozen backbone.
