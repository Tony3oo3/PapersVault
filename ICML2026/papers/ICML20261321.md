## Problem

Certified robustness against data poisoning for graph neural networks remains largely underexplored, and the partition-and-aggregate paradigm that yields strong poisoning certificates for images fails on graphs due to inherent label and structure sparsity. Naive partitioning leaves each subgraph with too few labeled nodes (often fewer than the number of classes) and too few edges for GNNs to learn meaningful representations, resulting in vacuous robustness guarantees and poor clean accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method operates in a semi-supervised transductive node classification setting: it takes an attributed undirected graph G = (E, X, y) where only a fraction of nodes carry labels (e.g., 30% training labels), and a test node index v. It outputs a majority-vote class prediction c* for node v together with a certified robustness margin r_m, which is the maximum number of label flips, edge modifications, or combined perturbations the prediction provably withstands. The partitioning is deterministic via an MD5 hash over node features, and the robust margin is computed per-node from the base classifier vote counts.

## Method

ST-GPA generalizes Deep Partition Aggregation to non-i.i.d. structured data (Theorem 3.1) and then overcomes the sparsity bottleneck by enriching each partition with synthetic data before training base classifiers. For label poisoning, it applies co-training via label propagation (iterating S^(i+1) = αÃS^(i) + (1−α)Y) followed by self-training (selecting highest-confidence GNN softmax predictions as pseudo-labels), controlled by a selectiveness hyperparameter t. For structure poisoning, it generates pseudo-edges by training a GNN on the sparse partition, then iteratively adding edges between node pairs with the highest combined same-class confidence, scaled by a multiplier ε relative to the original edge count. An efficient global/local max-heap algorithm (Algorithm 3) selects top-scoring edge candidates in near-linear time rather than O(kn²). The framework is architecture-agnostic (validated on GCN, GAT, APPNP, and NodeFormer) and supports joint label-structure certification by partitioning both labels and edges and applying all three semi-supervised steps in sequence.

## Results

ST-GPA restores ensemble clean accuracy to approximately 70–80% for label flipping (k=80), ~76% for structure perturbation (k=800), and ~67% for joint label+structure poisoning (k=200), compared to near-random or below-MLP performance with naive partitioning. On Cora-ML (binary) under label poisoning, ST-GPA achieves 66.5% certified accuracy at 5% perturbation and 36.2% at 10%, outperforming the exact MILP-based LabelCert at 10% (32.6%) while matching it at 5% (68.7%). For structure perturbation with k=800 partitions, ST-GPA's link prediction method consistently exceeds the infinitely robust MLP baseline across Cora-ML, CiteSeer, and Cora, whereas the competing PGNNCert falls below it on all three datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LabelCert (Sabanayagam et al., 2025), ST-GPA is slightly behind at 5% perturbation (66.5% vs. 68.7%) but clearly ahead at 10% (36.2% vs. 32.6%) on Cora-ML binary; however, LabelCert is restricted to binary classification with only 20–40 total labels via an MILP solver, whereas ST-GPA handles multi-class settings (7+ classes) and scales to graphs with thousands of nodes. Against PGNNCert (Li et al., 2025), the comparison is stark: PGNNCert's certified accuracy falls below the infinitely robust MLP baseline on all three tested datasets (Cora-ML, CiteSeer, Cora), rendering its structure certificates vacuous, while ST-GPA's link prediction method produces certified accuracy meaningfully above the MLP baseline. The paper notes that PGNNCert also does not provide any certificate against label flipping, a gap that ST-GPA fills. Overall, ST-GPA is the first method to deliver non-vacuous, effective certified robustness against both label and structure poisoning simultaneously for GNN node classification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the Deep Partition Aggregation (DPA) paradigm from Levine & Feizi (2021), originally designed for i.i.d. image data, to non-i.i.d. graph-structured data by formulating a hash-based partitioning scheme over edges and labels (Theorem 3.1). It then identifies a fundamental failure mode: the label and structure sparsity inherent in common graph datasets renders naive partitioning ineffective, with base classifiers degrading to MLP-level performance. To overcome this, the paper introduces semi-supervised enrichment of each partition—using label propagation (Zhu & Ghahramani, 2003) for co-training, GNN self-training for pseudo-labels, and confidence-based link prediction for pseudo-edges—before training the ensemble. This positions the work as the first to produce non-vacuous, effective certificates against both label and structure poisoning for GNNs in node classification, departing from prior attempts (LabelCert, PGNNCert) that were either limited to infinite-width networks, produced certificates below the MLP baseline, or could not scale beyond toy datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that semi-supervised learning is not merely helpful but essential for any partition-based poisoning certification on graphs, providing a clear recipe (co-train → self-train → link-predict) that practitioners can adopt. Key limitations include a fundamental reliance on homophily (certificates degrade below ~60% homophily) and the computational cost of link prediction on large or dense graphs, which currently prevents scaling structure certificates to datasets like ogbn-arxiv. Open questions include developing adaptive poisoning attacks tailored to partition-ensemble methods to assess certificate tightness, extending the framework to heterophilic graphs, and whether the self-training-based partition enrichment idea transfers to other structured-data domains beyond graphs.
