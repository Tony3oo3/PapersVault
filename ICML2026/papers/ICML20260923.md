## Problem

Data-driven neural PDE surrogates for the strongly nonlinear Grad-Shafranov equation (GSE) produce non-physical flux topology under out-of-distribution extrapolation, while physics-only (residual-only) training can converge to incorrect solution branches. No prior work systematically connects architecture choice, training paradigm, and OOD failure mechanism under multi-parameter distribution shifts for fixed-boundary tokamak equilibria, leaving a gap between simulation-trained accuracy and deployment reliability in safety-critical fusion workflows.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 10-dimensional parameter vector encoding D-shaped boundary geometry (R₀, a, κ, δ), global operating quantities (plasma current I_p, pressure/current partition β₀), and GAQ source-profile shape exponents (n_p, m_p, n_f, m_f), and produces the poloidal flux field ψ(R,Z) on a uniform 101×161 rectangular grid in the (R,Z) plane. The input vector is consumed by a low-dimensional MLP BranchNet, while spatial coordinates are processed independently by the TrunkNet; the two are fused via element-wise modulation before the Regressor outputs the scalar field. A homogeneous Dirichlet condition (ψ=0) is imposed on the plasma boundary defined by the Miller parameterization, and the computational domain is set to R∈[R₀−a, R₀+a], Z∈[−κa, κa] to contain the D-shaped boundary for all parameter combinations.

## Method

The core architecture, TKNO, follows a DeepONet-style Branch–Trunk–Regressor factorization where the TrunkNet is a Transformer with Galerkin (linear) attention that computes a global feature-coupling matrix C^(h) = (K^(h))ᵀV^(h)/N over all spatial tokens, providing nonlocal elliptic mixing motivated by the Green-function integral form of the fixed-boundary GSE, and the Regressor is a KAN with learnable 1D B-spline nonlinearities to capture the compact but highly nonlinear source-response map. Training uses a physics-anchored composite loss combining sparse or full-field data anchors (calibrating toward the physically relevant solution branch) with PDE-residual, boundary-condition, and plasma-current constraints (regularizing the field away from labeled points); the PDE residual is computed via finite differences matching the ground-truth FDM+SOR solver discretization rather than automatic differentiation, ensuring compatibility with all trunk architectures. The key empirical finding is that this combination reduces worst-tail OOD errors (p99 from 53.7% to 44.1% on joint shifts) and limits high-frequency error amplification to 7.5–8.1× versus 78–116× for data-only, while maintaining millisecond-level inference suitable for real-time tokamak control.

## Results

On the pilot 4D IID test set, TKNO (Transformer trunk + KAN regressor) achieves the best mean L₂(ψ) error of 0.25% and max L₂(ψ) of 0.42%, with the lowest PDE residual (MAE 1.23) among all tested architectures. Under full 10D multi-parameter OOD evaluation, Physics-anchored TKNO reduces the joint-OOD p₉₉ error from 53.7% (Data-only) to 44.1% and the OOD/IID growth ratio from 43.3× to 19.5×, with the strongest tail reduction on shape-driven shifts (p₉₉: 52.3% → 33.2%). On EXL-50U discharge inputs, the model agrees with the operational equilibrium solver at RMSE 1.27%, R² = 99.78%, and magnetic-axis error 5.8 mm, with millisecond-level inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TKNO outperforms all three cross-family baselines on every reported metric: it achieves 0.25% mean L₂(ψ) versus 0.31% (ONO), 0.33% (Transolver), and 0.74% (Flow Matching), and 0.42% max L₂(ψ) versus 0.48%, 0.51%, and 1.40% respectively. TKNO also records the lowest PDE residual (MAE 1.23) compared to ONO (1.61) and Transolver (2.46), indicating superior differential-structure recovery. Flow Matching, while competitive in mean error, requires 32 iterative Heun steps (~100 ms) and uses 3× more parameters (0.91 M vs. 0.29 M), making it unsuitable for millisecond-level tokamak control. Among the trunk variants with a fixed KAN regressor, the Transformer trunk (T-K) is the only architecture simultaneously best in mean flux accuracy, worst-case flux accuracy, and PDE residual, whereas the CNN trunk (C-K) achieves competitive pointwise error (0.30%) but a much larger PDE residual (5.23), confirming that local interpolation alone does not recover the elliptic differential structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DeepONet Branch-Trunk operator-learning paradigm (Lu et al., 2021) and the physics-informed neural operator (PINO) framework (Li et al., 2024), instantiating the trunk with Galerkin Transformer attention (Cao, 2021) and the regressor with KAN splines (Liu et al., 2025), motivated by the Green-function integral structure of the fixed-boundary GSE. It departs from prior GSE neural solvers (Jang et al., 2024; Rizqan et al., 2025; Zhou & Zhu, 2025; Wang et al., 2024), which largely target IID accuracy with simplified or linear source profiles, by introducing a 'physics-anchored' training paradigm that combines sparse data anchors with PDE/BC/current constraints specifically to address the branch-selection problem in strongly nonlinear elliptic PDEs. The OOD evaluation protocol—joint multi-parameter and group-isolated distribution shifts—extends the single-regime testing common in prior neural PDE surrogate work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical deployment on EXL-50U with millisecond-level inference (8800× speedup over the numerical solver) and close agreement with the operational equilibrium solver (RMSE 1.27%) demonstrates a viable path toward real-time AI surrogates in safety-critical fusion workflows. The systematic OOD analysis reveals that data-only surrogates develop severe error tails (p99 up to 53.7%) while physics-only training can converge to wrong solution branches (~12% branch-scale mismatch), and that the physics-anchored combination mitigates both failure modes—insights transferable to other strongly nonlinear PDE surrogates. Key limitations include the fixed-boundary assumption (free-boundary coupling to vacuum/coil circuits is needed for closed-loop control), the GAQ profile-family restriction, and the static-equilibrium scope; extending to time-dependent, free-boundary, and multi-device settings remains open.
