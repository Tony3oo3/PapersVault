## Problem

The precise sample complexity for learning an ε-approximation of the optimal linear contract in the offline setting remained unresolved. Previous upper bounds from Dutting et al. (2025b) either carried an extra log(1/ε) factor or depended on the number of actions n, while a matching lower bound of Ω(ln(1/δ)/ε²) was already known, leaving a gap in the large-n regime. This paper closes that gap by proving the optimal O(ln(1/δ)/ε²) bound, independent of n and m.

## Input / Output

The principal receives only oracle access to the empirical utility u_p(S, ·) over a sample S of s i.i.d. agent types, without seeing individual types or their action sets. The known reward vector r ∈ [0,1]^m parameterizes the linear contract class C_linear = {αr : α ∈ [0,1]}. The output is a single scalar α̂ ∈ [0,1] (the EUM maximizer over a grid of O(1/ε) points), accompanied by a uniform convergence guarantee: |u_p(D, α) − u_p(S, α)| ≤ ε for all α simultaneously, with the sample complexity independent of the number of actions n and outcomes m.

## Method

The key insight is to bypass the pseudo-dimension machinery of Dutting et al. (2025b) and instead construct an explicit L₂ cover of the linear contract space of size O(1/ν²) by exploiting the monotonicity of the principal's expected reward r_p(θ, α) in α (a result of Dutting et al., 2021a). The cover is built by (i) placing O(1/ν) equally spaced grid points on the x-axis and (ii) discretizing the y-axis of the empirical reward into O(1/ν) intervals and taking one point from each pullback interval, which are guaranteed to be intervals by monotonicity. This cover is plugged into a Rademacher-complexity chaining bound (Rebeschini, 2021), yielding a generalization error of O(1/√s) for the entire class. Combined with McDiarmid's inequality for concentration, this gives the uniform convergence guarantee with s = O(ln(1/δ)/ε²) samples. The EUM algorithm then achieves the same sample complexity by querying the oracle O(1/ε) times over a discretization D_{ε/4} of [0,1], using the monotonicity lemma to argue that the nearest grid point to the right of an ε/4-optimal contract is itself near-optimal.

## Results

The main result (Theorem 1.1) establishes that for any unknown distribution over agent types, the empirical utility of every linear contract α ∈ [0,1] is within ε of its expected utility with probability at least 1−δ, using only s ≥ 3456·ln(4/δ)/ε² samples. As a corollary (Theorem 1.2), the Empirical Utility Maximization (EUM) algorithm returns an ε-approximation of the optimal linear contract with probability 1−δ using s ≥ 6912·ln(4/δ)/ε² samples and O(1/ε) oracle queries. This sample complexity is independent of the number of actions n and outcomes m, and matches the lower bound Ω(ln(1/δ)/ε²) from Dutting et al. (2025b, Theorem 5.9) up to constant factors, proving optimality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper improves upon two upper bounds from Dutting et al. (2025b) for learning the best linear contract. The first previous bound, O((ln(1/ε) + ln(1/δ))/ε²), carries an extra ln(1/ε) factor and only guarantees approximation over a discretization of the contract space (multiples of ε), not uniform convergence over all linear contracts. The second previous bound, O((ln(n) + ln(1/δ))/ε²), depends on the number of actions n, which is assumed unknown to the principal in this paper's setting. The new bound O(ln(1/δ)/ε²) eliminates both the ln(1/ε) and ln(n) dependencies, and moreover provides the stronger uniform convergence guarantee (all contracts simultaneously) rather than just learning the best contract. Combined with the lower bound Ω(ln(1/δ)/ε²) from Dutting et al. (2025b, Theorem 5.9), this closes the gap and proves the sample complexity is tight up to constants, with no gap between uniform convergence and best-contract learning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the offline contract-learning framework of Dutting et al. (2025b), which established a pseudo-dimension-based general theory and left the exact sample complexity for linear contracts open. The key departure is methodological: rather than bounding the pseudo-dimension of the contract class (which introduced either a ln(1/ε) or ln(n) factor), the authors exploit the monotonicity of the principal's expected reward under linear contracts (from Dutting et al., 2021a) to construct a direct L₂ cover, then apply a Rademacher-complexity chaining argument (Rebeschini, 2021) to obtain the optimal O(ln(1/δ)/ε²) bound. The analysis also works under a weaker oracle-access assumption than the full-type-information setting of Dutting et al. (2025b).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result closes the gap between upper and lower bounds for learning linear contracts, establishing that the simple EUM algorithm is sample-optimal and that uniform convergence over the entire class of linear contracts costs no more (up to constants) than learning a single contract. The sample complexity being independent of the number of actions n and outcomes m is practically significant, as these quantities can be large and are assumed unknown to the principal. Open questions include whether similar direct-cover arguments can yield optimal bounds for richer contract classes (e.g., piecewise-linear or menu-based contracts), and whether a genuine sample-complexity gap between uniform convergence and best-contract learning exists for more complex classes.
