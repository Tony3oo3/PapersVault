## Problem

Partially Relevant Video Retrieval (PRVR) retrieves untrimmed videos from brief, partially relevant text queries, but existing methods treat all queries uniformly and rely on sparse MIL supervision that only supervises the single best-matching clip. This ignores the fine-grained heterogeneity of real-world queries (precise, polysemous, under-determined) and leaves the model vulnerable to spurious local noise, resulting in unreliable retrieval when semantic ambiguity is present.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262471/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text query (encoded as 1024-d RoBERTa features) and an untrimmed video pre-extracted into 128 frames and 32 clips (I3D or ResNet152+I3D features), processed through a dual-branch Gaussian attention encoder with latent dimension d=384. It produces a ranked video list via combined frame-scale and clip-scale cosine similarities, along with per-query evidential uncertainty estimates and a three-way query type label (precise, polysemous, or under-determined) derived from Dirichlet-based belief masses and entropy.

## Method

Holmes is a hierarchical evidential learning framework operating at two levels. At the inter-video level, cross-modal similarities are converted to Dirichlet evidence, and a three-fold principle (epistemic uncertainty, label consistency, aleatoric uncertainty) categorizes queries into precise, polysemous, and under-determined subsets; polysemous queries receive soft label calibration using model-estimated similarities, while dual-scale (frame+clip) evidential opinions are fused via Dempster-Shafer combination. At the intra-video level, Flexible Optimal Transport with an adaptive dustbin bucket (set to the bottom 30th percentile of similarity scores) establishes soft one-to-many query-clip assignments, providing denser temporal supervision than standard MIL and filtering irrelevant clips. Training follows a two-stage scheme: a 20-epoch warm-up with base retrieval losses and inter-video evidential loss without label recalibration, followed by joint optimization of all objectives including intra-video evidential loss and query-adaptive label calibration.

## Results

Holmes achieves state-of-the-art performance across all three PRVR benchmarks. On ActivityNet Captions, it reaches R@1=9.3% and SumR=156.8; on Charades-STA, R@1=2.3% and SumR=80.6; and on TVR, R@1=18.4% and SumR=198.6. These results surpass the strongest prior PRVR method DreamPRVR by 0.7 and 0.6 in SumR on ActivityNet Captions and Charades-STA, respectively, and by 5.5 on TVR. Holmes also outperforms the previous best TVR result (MGAKD, SumR=190.5) by 8.1 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Holmes outperforms all 21 compared baselines across the three PRVR benchmarks. Against the closest competitor DreamPRVR (Li et al., 2026), Holmes gains +0.7 SumR on ActivityNet Captions (156.8 vs. 156.1), +0.6 on Charades-STA (80.6 vs. 80.0), and +5.5 on TVR (198.6 vs. 193.1). On TVR specifically, Holmes surpasses MGAKD (Zhang et al., 2025c) by 8.1 SumR and GMMFormerV2 (Wang et al., 2024a) by 9.5. The gap over earlier PRVR methods is more pronounced: Holmes improves over GMMFormer (Wang et al., 2024b) by 10.8 SumR on ActivityNet and 22.0 on TVR, and over the original MS-SL (Dong et al., 2022a) by 16.7 and 26.2 respectively. Holmes also dominates T2VR baselines (e.g., Cap4Video, SumR=130.2 on ActivityNet) and VCMR baselines (e.g., CONQUER, SumR=133.1), confirming that PRVR-specific modeling with explicit uncertainty quantification yields substantially better retrieval than task-agnostic approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

Holmes builds directly on the MS-SL (Dong et al., 2022a) PRVR formulation—adopting its dual-branch frame/clip architecture, similarity computation, and base training losses—while introducing a hierarchical evidential learning layer grounded in the Dirichlet-based framework of Sensoy et al. (2018). Conceptually, it departs from prior PRVR methods (GMMFormer, ARL, MSC-PRVR) that either ignore query heterogeneity or handle ambiguity only implicitly via regularization, by proposing an explicit three-fold diagnostic (epistemic uncertainty, label consistency, aleatoric uncertainty) for fine-grained query categorization. The intra-video Flexible Optimal Transport with a dustbin bucket repurposes the SuperGlue (Sarlin et al., 2020) mechanism from feature matching into a temporal alignment context, addressing the sparse MIL supervision problem that prior PRVR works have not tackled directly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, interpretable lens for understanding why PRVR is fundamentally harder than T2VR—linking inter-video semantic ambiguity to intra-video supervisory sparsity—and offers a modular framework (query identification → label calibration → dense temporal alignment) that other retrieval settings could adopt. However, the method relies on frozen pretrained features (I3D/ResNet) and requires full-video access, limiting its applicability to streaming or end-to-end scenarios with modern encoders like CLIP. Open questions include whether the evidential framework generalizes to other partially-supervised multimodal tasks, and whether the three-fold query identification remains stable under distribution shift or adversarial queries.
