## Problem

Optimal transport-based matching mechanisms (school admissions, dating apps, kidney allocation) can produce highly homogeneous pairings that systematically disadvantage certain social groups, yet no prior work has defined or enforced group-level fairness constraints directly on the transport plan. Existing fairness notions from supervised/unsupervised learning do not naturally extend to matching, where the coupling itself—not a prediction or representation—is the object of concern.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260295/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two finite populations with features and discrete sensitive attributes (e.g., students with location and social status; schools with location and prestige), a ground cost matrix C ∈ R^{n×m}, a fairness target matrix F ∈ R^{K_s×K_w} that must be a valid coupling between the group marginals p and q, and an entropic regularization parameter ε. It outputs either a probabilistic transport plan Π ∈ R^{n×m} satisfying exact or approximate group-level mass constraints, or a reusable learned cost function c_θ (Mahalanobis or neural) that can be applied to new samples via standard entropic OT without retraining.

## Method

The core algorithm, FairSinkhorn, modifies the standard Sinkhorn iteration by adding a group-fairness projection step that updates a matrix L^{(t+1)} = F ⊘ Φ(u,v) at each iteration, exploiting the fact that fairness constraints are linear in the transport plan and thus enter the dual as an elementwise multiplier on the kernel. For relaxed fairness, two strategies are proposed: (i) a convex penalized OT objective min_Π Tr[Π^T C] + ε KL(Π) + λL_F(Π) solved via generalized conditional gradient, for which the paper proves an O(n^{-1/2}) sample complexity bound matching standard entropic OT; and (ii) a bilevel optimization that learns a parameterized cost c_θ (Mahalanobis or neural) whose induced entropic OT plan minimizes fairness violation, with a uniform deviation bound over the cost family guaranteeing fairness generalization to unseen samples.

## Results

The paper evaluates its methods on synthetic (Gaussian, Circles) and semi-synthetic (Kaggle Dating App, 7 income groups) datasets, reporting results as cost–fairness trade-off curves rather than single-point metrics. On the Gaussian dataset, penalized OT, Mahalanobis cost learning, and MLP cost learning achieve comparable trade-offs, while on the Circles dataset penalized OT dominates, reaching any fairness level at lower transport cost; the Mahalanobis cost learning variant cannot reduce fairness loss below 10⁻², whereas the MLP variant can. On the dating app dataset (high-dimensional, 7×7 group structure), both relaxed approaches yield nearly identical convex trade-off curves. For inference on new samples, cost learning is substantially faster than penalized OT (which must re-solve the full penalized problem per batch), and fairness on unseen data remains close to training-time levels, with a smaller generalization gap for Mahalanobis than for MLP costs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is vanilla entropic OT (standard Sinkhorn algorithm) with varying entropic regularization ε. Vanilla OT consistently achieves low transport cost but large fairness violations, and increasing ε alone fails to reach the prescribed fairness target on any dataset, confirming the necessity of the proposed fairness-aware formulations. FairSinkhorn enforces perfect fairness (zero deviation from target F) but at a substantially higher transport cost, establishing the upper bound of the trade-off. Penalized OT smoothly interpolates between these two extremes by tuning the penalty λ, and on the Circles dataset it strictly dominates both cost-learning variants in the cost–fairness plane. Cost learning (Mahalanobis and MLP) matches penalized OT on the Gaussian dataset but underperforms on Circles due to the restricted parametric cost family; however, it offers the practical advantage of a reusable cost function that enables fast inference on new samples without re-solving the penalized problem. On the semi-synthetic dating app dataset, penalized OT and cost learning produce nearly identical trade-off curves, suggesting the two approaches are complementary rather than one dominating the other.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the entropic optimal transport framework (built on the Sinkhorn algorithm and its sample complexity theory from Genevay et al., 2019, and Rigollet & Stromme, 2025) by introducing group-level mass constraints on the transport plan. It departs from prior fairness-in-matching work, which has focused on individual fairness in Gale-Shapley mechanisms or participation constraints in kidney exchange, by formulating fairness as explicit inter-group coupling targets. It also distinguishes itself from the OT-for-fairness literature (Wasserstein barycenters for fair prediction) by studying the fairness of the transport plan itself rather than using OT as an auxiliary tool. The penalized approach leverages the generalized conditional gradient framework of Rakotomamonjy et al. (2015), while the cost-learning strategy draws on bilevel optimization and inverse OT literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, flexible framework for regulators and market designers to enforce group fairness in matching mechanisms (school admissions, dating apps, hiring), with two complementary algorithms offering different trade-offs between flexibility and reusability. The theoretical guarantees—matching the O(n^{-1/2}) sample complexity of vanilla entropic OT and providing uniform generalization bounds for learned costs—give practitioners confidence in finite-sample performance. Key limitations include the restriction to group fairness (individual fairness is left open), the absence of analysis on how matchings affect downstream individual outcomes, and the acknowledgment that fairness target design remains a political decision. The exponential dependence on ε^{-1} in the cost-learning generalization bound, while unavoidable, may limit practical applicability at small regularization values.
