## Problem

Existing fair federated learning methods face a fundamental bottleneck: they either sacrifice global model accuracy to reduce cross-client performance variance, or they achieve sub-optimal fairness because they lack explicit mechanisms to constrain performance variance. No prior method simultaneously optimizes both fairness and global accuracy without compromise, and the role of entropy in achieving fairness (as opposed to merely uniform resource allocation) remains underexplored.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard supervised classification data: RGB images (32×32 for MNIST/Fashion-MNIST/CIFAR-10, 32×32 for CIFAR-100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet) and text sequences (Reddit for language modeling). Beyond the usual model parameters, the server additionally requires each participating client's scalar loss F_i(x_t) and one-step gradient ∇F_i(x_t) to compute entropy-based aggregation weights and the fairness-aware alignment gradient. The output is a single global model whose cross-client test-loss variance is minimized while maintaining high global accuracy.

## Method

FedEBA+ combines two mechanisms derived from a constrained maximum entropy formulation. First, Entropy-Based Aggregation (EBA) solves max H(p) subject to Σp_i=1 and Σp_i F_i(x)=f̃(x), yielding closed-form weights p_i = exp[F_i(x)/τ] / Σ_j exp[F_j(x)/τ] that upweight underperforming clients; the temperature τ is dynamically clamped to prevent weight collapse. Second, step-wise alignment blends each client's local stochastic gradient with a server-computed fair gradient: h_{t,k}^i = (1−α)∇F_i(x_{t,k}^i; ξ_i) + α g̃^t, where g̃^t is the entropy-weighted average of client gradients at the global model, preventing local drift from diverging from the fairness-aware direction. A practical variant, Prac-FedEBA+, approximates the fair gradient from the average of K local updates, matching FedAvg's single-round communication cost. Theoretical analysis provides non-convex convergence guarantees and proves that FedEBA+ achieves no larger cross-client test-loss variance than FedAvg under both generalized linear regression and strongly convex settings.

## Results

On FashionMNIST, FedEBA+ achieves 87.50% global accuracy with a cross-client variance of 43.41 (C_V = 2.02), outperforming FedAvg (86.49% acc, 62.44 var) and the best fairness baseline q-FFL (86.57% acc, 54.91 var). On CIFAR-10, FedEBA+ reaches 72.75% global accuracy with variance 68.71 (C_V = 1.06), improving over FedAvg by ~5% in accuracy while reducing variance by ~34%. On CIFAR-100 and Tiny-ImageNet, FedEBA+ achieves the best C_V (0.17 and 4.59 respectively), with variance reductions of 36% and 29% over FedAvg while maintaining or exceeding global accuracy. The communication-efficient variant Prac-FedEBA+ matches FedAvg's communication cost while still achieving the second-best C_V on three of four datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedEBA+ dominates all 11 baselines on the C_V (accuracy-fairness trade-off) metric across all four main datasets. Against FedAvg, FedEBA+ improves global accuracy by ~1% on FashionMNIST and ~5% on CIFAR-10 while simultaneously reducing variance by 30% and 34% respectively. Among fairness-specific baselines, q-FFL (the strongest variance reducer) still trails FedEBA+ by 11.5% in variance on FashionMNIST and 10% on CIFAR-10, while also sacrificing ~1% global accuracy. FOCUS shows a catastrophic failure on CIFAR-10 (variance 455.14, worst-5% accuracy 9.54%), indicating its agent-awareness clustering breaks down under high heterogeneity. AAggFF, the closest conceptual competitor (sequential decision-making for aggregation), is outperformed by FedEBA+ on both accuracy and variance on every dataset, with the gap widening on CIFAR-10 (72.75% vs 68.07% accuracy). The practical variant Prac-FedEBA+ achieves the second-best C_V on FashionMNIST (1.87) and CIFAR-10 (0.94) at FedAvg-level communication cost, confirming that the alignment step can be approximated without extra rounds.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedEBA+ extends the Maximum Entropy Principle from statistical mechanics (Jaynes, 1957) and information-theoretic resource allocation (Johansson & Sternad, 2005) into the federated learning setting, but with a critical departure: rather than unconstrained entropy maximization (which yields uniform FedAvg weights), it solves a *constrained* maximum entropy problem where the fairness constraint ties aggregation to client losses, producing closed-form exponential weights that upweight underperforming clients. This positions it alongside prior fair FL reweighting methods (q-FFL, AFL, AAggFF) but distinguishes itself through a principled constrained-optimization derivation and an additional step-wise gradient alignment mechanism that has no direct analogue in the prior fair FL literature. The robustness component borrows Local Self-Regularization (Jiang et al., 2022) for noisy-label resilience.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent bottleneck in fair FL: most methods that reduce cross-client performance variance do so at the cost of global accuracy, and FedEBA+ claims to break this trade-off with consistent gains on both metrics across five datasets and four architectures. The practical variant (Prac-FedEBA+) matching FedAvg's communication cost makes the approach deployable in bandwidth-constrained settings. Open questions include extension to model-heterogeneous FL (the gradient alignment step assumes homogeneous architectures), whether the entropy-based weighting generalizes to vertical or split FL, and how the method behaves under extreme client non-participation or adversarial data poisoning.
