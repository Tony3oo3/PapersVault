## Problem

Long-horizon, multi-turn information-seeking tasks suffer from sparse, trajectory-level rewards that provide no granular credit assignment for individual intermediate actions. Standard RL approaches (e.g., GRPO) assign a uniform advantage across all turns, obscuring which specific questions drove progress versus which were uninformative. This paper asks whether an agent's own internal belief updates can serve as a dense, intrinsic reward signal that resolves this credit-assignment gap without requiring a separately trained critic or process reward model.

## Input / Output

At each turn, the agent receives the accumulated interaction history (prior questions and environment responses) and generates a yes/no question as its action; the environment (a user simulator LLM) returns a response. The belief signal is elicited post-hoc by prompting the model with a fixed template (e.g., 'Is the secret <target>?') and reading the log-probability assigned to the target concept tokens conditioned on the full history. The per-turn reward combines the binary trajectory outcome, the ReLU-clipped Δ Belief (log-ratio of consecutive beliefs), and efficiency penalties (repetition, invalid format, turn count), all scaled by a hyperparameter λ=0.1 to balance magnitudes.

## Method

Δ Belief-RL computes a dense per-turn reward by measuring the log-ratio of the agent's probability assigned to the target concept before and after each interaction (Δ Belief_t = log b_t − log b_{t-1}), clipped at zero via ReLU to reward only belief-increasing actions. This intrinsic signal is combined with the sparse end-of-game outcome reward and efficiency penalties into a per-turn reward r_t. The key architectural modification is turn-wise GRPO: instead of applying a single trajectory-level advantage to all tokens, the method computes group-relative advantages independently at each turn (normalizing r_t across G=16 sampled trajectories per turn), assigning the resulting advantage to all tokens generated in that turn. Training uses LoRA (rank 64) on Qwen3-1.7B/4B as the questioner with Qwen3-14B as the user simulator on the 20 Questions environment, starting from an SFT checkpoint. The approach requires no separate critic, process reward model, or oracle access at inference time—the belief is computed post-hoc on stored trajectories, and a custom attention mask (inspired by TreeAttention) computes all per-turn Δ Belief values in a single forward pass, adding only 10–15% wall-clock overhead.

## Results

On the 20 Questions test set, CIA (Δ Belief-RL) achieves 24.80% Mean@8 and 53.10% Pass@8 at 1.7B scale, and 33.72% Mean@8 and 63.97% Pass@8 at 4B scale, outperforming the SFT baseline by 14.83% and 20.38% respectively. Notably, CIA-1.7B (24.80%) surpasses DeepSeek-V3.2 (670B parameters) at 14.35% Mean@8, and CIA-4B (33.72%) exceeds Qwen3-235B-Instruct (8.83%) by 24.89%. Performance continues to scale beyond the 20-turn training horizon, with CIA achieving 26% higher absolute success rate than STARPO at 50 turns on the 4B model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against STARPO (Wang et al., 2025), the primary multi-turn GRPO baseline, CIA wins decisively across all settings: +8.26% Mean@8 at 1.7B and +9.36% at 4B on the 20Qs test set, with consistent advantages on Pass@8, pass@k up to k=128, training efficiency (fewer turns and less redundancy), and OOD generalization to Guess My City, Murder Mystery, Customer Service, and User Personalization. CIA also outperforms STARPO on test-time interaction scaling, maintaining a 26% absolute gap at 50 turns for the 4B model. Against IGPO (Wang et al., 2026), which uses discounted future information gain for credit assignment, CIA achieves consistently higher training success rates on 20 Questions; the paper attributes this to IGPO's mixing of local signal with future rewards and its trajectory-level z-normalization distorting the local directionality of the belief-update signal, whereas Δ Belief-RL preserves per-turn local credit assignment with ReLU shaping.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-turn GRPO framework (Zeng et al., 2025) by replacing trajectory-level advantages with turn-wise advantages, and introduces a dense intrinsic reward computed from the agent's own log-probability shift toward the target concept. It departs from prior intrinsic-reward approaches (NOVER, PACR, RLP) that operate at the sequence level by measuring belief updates at the interaction level, and from process reward models that require expensive step-level supervision. The method is positioned against STARPO (Wang et al., 2025) as the primary multi-turn RL baseline and compared against IGPO (Wang et al., 2026), which uses discounted future information gain rather than local per-turn attribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core practical contribution is a near-free dense credit assignment signal (a single log-probability lookup per turn) that eliminates the need for a separate critic or process reward model, with only 10–15% computational overhead. The finding that a 4B model trained with this signal outperforms a 670B general-purpose model by ~20% on information-seeking tasks suggests that specialized RL training can compensate for scale in narrow interactive settings. Key limitations include the requirement for a verifiable target at reward-computation time and potential restriction of output diversity when optimizing against a single reference; extending the method to fully open-ended domains without any verifiable signal remains an open problem.
