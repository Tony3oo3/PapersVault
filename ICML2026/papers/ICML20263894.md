## Problem

Posterior sampling with discrete diffusion models is severely limited: derivative-free guidance produces sparse, weak signals; continuous relaxations like Gumbel-Softmax restrict applicability to tokens with continuous embeddings; and split Gibbs samplers incur exponential complexity in sequence length. No existing method enables efficient, training-free posterior sampling in purely discrete token spaces (e.g., binary LFQ embeddings) while maintaining competitive quality with continuous diffusion baselines.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263894/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For inverse problems, the method takes a degraded image (e.g., 4× downsampled, Gaussian-blurred, or partially masked) at 256×256 (or 1024×1024 for the large variant APS-L) and produces a restored image, operating entirely on discrete token sequences from an LFQ tokenizer rather than continuous latents. For stylization, it accepts a single reference style image plus a text prompt and outputs a stylized image. For question answering, it takes a text prompt and produces a text answer by guiding a 7B-parameter diffusion language model. All vision tasks are training-free, reusing a pretrained MMaDA denoiser without task-specific retraining.

## Method

APS introduces two algorithmic innovations built on a variational upper bound (Theorem 3.3) that reuses a pretrained discrete diffusion denoiser without retraining. First, **quantized expectation** replaces the intractable O(K^L) measurement-likelihood computation with a differentiable surrogate: it computes the expected codebook embedding as a probability-weighted sum, quantizes it via the LFQ sign function, and applies the straight-through estimator to backpropagate measurement gradients through all entries of the conditional probability table, reducing cost to O(KL). Second, **anchored remasking** adaptively decodes high-confidence 'anchor' tokens early by thresholding per-token confidence under the joint posterior x_φ(z_t) (which encodes cross-token relations), rather than using independent per-token confidence as in standard masked diffusion samplers. At each reverse step, the logits are optimized for a small number of inner steps (saturating around 50–100), backpropagating only through the lightweight VQ-VAE decoder rather than the 8B-parameter backbone, keeping computational overhead minimal.

## Results

On FFHQ super-resolution (4×), APS achieves LPIPS 0.234 and PSNR 27.50, outperforming G2D2 (0.271 / 26.93) by 13.65% in LPIPS and 2.11% in PSNR; the larger variant APS-L further reaches LPIPS 0.186 and PSNR 28.83. On ImageNet, APS-L delivers up to 35.82% LPIPS reduction and 10.94% PSNR gain over G2D2 across SR and Gaussian deblurring. APS also generalizes to nonlinear tasks (HDR, nonlinear blur) and enables training-free stylization (ImageReward 0.63, CLIP-T 0.34) and a 21.99% improvement in diffusion language model question answering over Dream-7B-Instruct.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among discrete diffusion baselines, APS consistently outperforms G2D2 on all four FFHQ/ImageNet × SR/Deblur settings (e.g., FFHQ SR: LPIPS 0.234 vs 0.271, PSNR 27.50 vs 26.93) and also beats SGDD and SVDD-PM where they report results. Against continuous pixel-space baselines (DPS, DDRM, DiffPIR), APS matches or exceeds them on PSNR while achieving lower LPIPS; notably, APS-L surpasses DiffPIR on FFHQ SR (LPIPS 0.186 vs 0.260) and on ImageNet Deblur (PSNR 26.35 vs 22.80). Versus latent-space continuous methods (PSLD, ReSample), APS-L achieves superior LPIPS on ImageNet SR (0.224 vs 0.332) and Deblur (0.282 vs 0.365), though PSLD retains a PSNR edge on FFHQ inpainting. In the controlled same-prior experiment (Table 7, both using VQ-Diffusion), APS improves over G2D2 by +1.34 PSNR, +0.039 SSIM, and −0.039 LPIPS, confirming gains come from algorithmic design rather than a stronger backbone. For stylization, APS (ImageReward 0.63, CLIP-T 0.34) surpasses the base MMaDA model and beats IP-Adapter and StyleAligned on ImageReward, while for question answering, Dream+APS achieves 4.5775 oracle-judge score vs Dream's 3.7523 (+21.99%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the posterior sampling framework from continuous diffusion (DPS, DDRM, PSLD) to masked discrete diffusion, where the non-differentiability of token space blocks standard gradient-based guidance. It departs from prior discrete approaches—G2D2's Gumbel-Softmax relaxation (limited to continuous embeddings) and SGDD's split Gibbs sampler (exponential complexity)—by introducing quantized expectation, which computes a differentiable expected embedding in continuous codebook space and applies LFQ with a straight-through estimator to recover gradient-like signals in purely discrete (binary) token space. The anchored remasking strategy adapts the anchoring concept from ADLM (Rout et al., 2025a) from a training-time objective to a training-free inference-time decoding rule, leveraging the joint posterior rather than per-token confidence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

APS is the first training-free posterior sampler for masked discrete diffusion with purely discrete embeddings, enabling direct reuse of large pretrained models (e.g., MMaDA, Dream-7B) without task-specific retraining—a significant practical advantage over continuous diffusion methods that require backpropagation through multi-billion-parameter denoisers. The generalization from vision inverse problems to diffusion language model guidance (21.99% QA improvement) suggests the framework could extend to next-generation multimodal discrete diffusion models. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher resolutions beyond 1024×1024, how it interacts with advanced tokenizers (e.g., index backpropagation quantization), and whether the quantized expectation surrogate can be made asymptotically exact rather than a variational approximation.
