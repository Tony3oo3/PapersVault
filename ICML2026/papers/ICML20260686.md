## Problem

Training a single generalist diffusion model that achieves Pareto-optimal performance across multiple target distributions requires substantially more paired data than training individual specialists, because the generalist class must have much larger capacity. In practice, paired samples (expert demonstrations, curated text-image pairs) are scarce while condition data (text prompts, robot states, masked images) are abundant, creating a semi-supervised regime where the statistical cost of the generalist becomes prohibitive. This paper asks whether weakly supervised data can reduce the labeled-sample complexity of multi-objective diffusion model learning.

## Input / Output

The method takes limited paired (x, y) samples per objective (e.g., ~900 robot trajectories or 10k labeled images) plus abundant unlabeled condition samples (e.g., 5000 initial states or 18k unlabeled images), and produces a single generalist diffusion model that simultaneously approximates K target conditional distributions. In the robotics setting, inputs are 256×256 RGB observations and 9-dimensional joint-position vectors; in image restoration, inputs are 64×64 masked CelebA-HQ faces with three distinct mask families. The generalist is substantially larger than each specialist (293M vs. 85M parameters in robotics, 1.16B vs. 290M in image restoration), realizing the capacity gap C_F ≫ C_H that drives the sample-efficiency gain.

## Method

The paper proposes a two-stage semi-supervised training pipeline: first, lightweight specialist diffusion models are trained per objective from limited paired data via denoising score matching; second, the specialists generate pseudo-paired samples on abundant unlabeled conditions, and a larger generalist is trained on the union of labeled and pseudo-labeled data using a scalarized multi-objective loss. The key theoretical contribution is nonasymptotic generalization bounds (in score matching loss and total variation distance) showing that the required number of paired samples scales with the covering number of the specialist classes rather than the generalist class, yielding a labeled-sample complexity of Õ(KC_H/ε²) versus Õ(KC_F/ε²) for fully supervised training. For linear scalarizations with convex function classes, a sharper rate is obtained via a refined analysis. The framework is further extended to diffusion policies in sequential decision making, where on-policy rollouts introduce distribution shift between expert-generated and learned-policy data, and the paper establishes the first theoretical guarantee on the sub-optimality gap of diffusion policies in imitation learning.

## Results

On robotic manipulation (StackCube and PickCube in RoboVerse), the semi-supervised MOL method improves closed-loop success rates over the labeled-only baseline in every setting, with the largest gains under out-of-distribution domain randomization: +16 pp at Level 2 and +14 pp at Level 3 on StackCube, and +44 pp at Level 0 and +16 pp at Level 3 on PickCube. On CelebA-HQ face inpainting (64×64), the method improves PSNR by +0.62 to +1.41 dB, SSIM by +0.040 to +0.054, and reduces LPIPS by 0.006 to 0.014 across three mask families (alternating lines, face-region, wide-stroke). The theoretical contribution establishes that labeled-sample complexity scales with specialist model complexity C_H rather than generalist complexity C_F, yielding a sample-efficiency gain of C_F/C_H when the generalist is substantially larger.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the proposed two-stage semi-supervised MOL pipeline against a single labeled-only MOL baseline that uses the identical generalist architecture but is trained exclusively on the limited paired (labeled) data, without specialist-generated pseudo-samples. The semi-supervised method wins in every reported setting across both domains. In robotic manipulation, the advantage is most pronounced under distribution shift: on StackCube, success jumps from 24% to 40% at Level 2 (OOD) and from 8% to 22% at Level 3 (OOD); on PickCube, the gain reaches +44 pp at Level 0 (69% vs. 25%) and +16 pp at Level 3 (42% vs. 26%). In image inpainting on CelebA-HQ, the semi-supervised method consistently outperforms the labeled-only baseline on all three metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) across all three mask families, with the largest PSNR gain of +1.41 dB on the alternating-lines mask. The consistent pattern across qualitatively different tasks (robotic control vs. image restoration) and evaluation protocols (closed-loop success rate vs. pixel-level reconstruction metrics) supports the theoretical prediction that specialist-generated pseudo-data is most beneficial when the generalist class is much larger than the specialist classes (C_F ≫ C_H).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the semi-supervised multi-objective learning framework of Wegel et al. (2025) from prediction tasks with Bregman losses to conditional diffusion models with score matching objectives, and further to diffusion policies in sequential decision making. It builds on the score approximation and distribution recovery theory for diffusion models (Oko et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2023a; Wibisono et al., 2024) and the diffusion policy architecture of Chi et al. (2025), while drawing on the specialist-to-generalist distillation paradigm from robot foundation models (Xu et al., 2024; Xiao et al., 2025). The key departure is formalizing the 'specialist-to-generalist' pipeline within a Pareto-optimality framework and proving that labeled-sample complexity scales with specialist rather than generalist complexity—a property previously established only for prediction losses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical guarantee on the sub-optimality gap of diffusion policies in imitation learning, and the first statistical theory for semi-supervised multi-objective learning of generative models. The practical implication is clear: when the generalist class is much larger than specialist classes (as in vision-language-action models vs. single-task policies), the two-stage pipeline provably reduces labeled-data requirements. Open questions include extending guarantees beyond the realizability assumption, addressing OOD generalization to unseen tasks, closing the gap between the score-matching rate and the induced total-variation rate, and extending the framework to other generative paradigms such as large language models.
