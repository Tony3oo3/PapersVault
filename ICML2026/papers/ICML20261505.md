## Problem

Graph foundation models struggle to generalize across domains because graphs lack a shared geometric reference: existing approaches either enforce fixed feature dimensionality (aligning features but not topology) or discretize graphs into tokens that violate permutation invariance. This paper asks whether graph topology itself can serve as the primary transferable signal, providing a unified coordinate system that accommodates heterogeneous structures without dataset-specific preprocessing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261505/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a graph's adjacency matrix A ∈ R^(N×N) and node feature matrix X ∈ R^(N×F) as input, where F is arbitrary and may differ across datasets—no padding, projection, or dimensionality reduction is required. The graph is converted to a metric measure space with a degree-based probability measure. The output is a unified embedding z(G) ∈ R^(K+r+MF) formed by concatenating K-dimensional structural coordinates, an r-dimensional MLP decoder output, and the vectorized structure-projected feature matrix, which is then fed to a frozen Prototypical Network for classification.

## Method

SCGFM learns K geometric bases, each a fixed-size M-node pseudo-metric matrix B_k ∈ [0,1]^(M×M), that collectively form a shared structural coordinate system over the space of metric measure spaces. During pre-training, each graph is aligned to the bases via Sliced Gromov-Wasserstein (SGW) distance, reducing the O(N³) exact GW cost to O(N log N); structural coordinates w are computed as a softmax over negative GW distances, and a linear surrogate B̃(G) = Σ w_k B_k approximates the GW barycenter for end-to-end reconstruction. The training objective combines GW reconstruction loss, graph-level statistics reconstruction (degree/clustering histograms, motif counts), and a pairwise diversity regularizer to prevent base collapse. At inference, the OT transport plans from the GW computation are reused to project node features onto the bases (structure-aware re-encoding), yielding a feature representation that is agnostic to the original feature dimensionality while remaining anchored to graph topology.

## Results

On 5-shot graph classification across 6 datasets from 3 domains, SCGFM achieves a mean accuracy of 57.82% (excluding COLORS-3), outperforming the strongest GFM baseline RiemannGFM by 4.08%. On the particularly challenging COLORS-3 benchmark (11-way, large structural shifts), most baselines degrade to near chance-level (~9%), while SCGFM remains substantially more robust. On 5-shot node classification, SCGFM reaches ~84.17% on Reddit and ~70.54% on Cora, exhibiting near source-independent behavior regardless of pre-training domain. On graph retrieval (frozen embeddings), SCGFM achieves P@10=0.784 and kNN@10=0.835 on MUTAG, surpassing GIN (0.736/0.798), GCN (0.669/0.739), and GAT (0.654/0.734).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCGFM consistently outperforms all 14 baselines across graph and node classification. Against the strongest GFM competitor RiemannGFM, SCGFM gains +4.08% mean accuracy on graph classification (57.82% vs. 53.74%, excluding COLORS-3) and maintains a clear advantage on the structurally challenging COLORS-3 where RiemannGFM drops to ~9%. In the dedicated cross-domain GFM comparison (Table 13), SCGFM achieves 68.32% on BZR→PROTEINS, surpassing the best native baseline MDGFM (57.06%) by 11.26% and the best aligned baseline MDGFM (54.04%) by 14.28%. On node classification, SCGFM's near source-independent behavior (~84.17% on Reddit regardless of pre-training domain) contrasts sharply with baselines like GCN, GIN, and GAT, which show significant domain-dependent variance. On graph retrieval, SCGFM leads all GNN baselines by 4.8–13.0 points on P@10 across MUTAG and COX2. The self-supervised baselines (DGI, GraphCL, GraphMAE, GraphACL, S2GAE) and prompting-based GFM (GCOPE) are uniformly outperformed, confirming that structure-centric geometric alignment provides stronger cross-domain transfer than feature-space or prompt-based strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

SCGFM departs from the dominant feature-centric and prompt-based paradigms in graph foundation models (OFA, GIT, UniGraph, SAMGPT) by treating graph topology as the primary transferable signal rather than node features or linguistic interfaces. It builds directly on the Sliced Gromov-Wasserstein framework (Vayer et al., 2019) for scalable metric alignment and on Memoli's (2011) GW distance as the geometric backbone, but introduces a novel architectural idea: learnable geometric bases that form a shared structural coordinate system, analogous to a dictionary in a compressed sensing sense but operating in mm-space rather than feature space. This distinguishes it from prior prototype/dictionary learning (Zeng et al., 2023; Vincent-Cuaz et al., 2021) which operates in feature space, and from graph tokenization (Chen et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2025a) which imposes artificial orderings on permutation-invariant structures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a principled geometric alternative to the ad-hoc feature-alignment strategies (padding, projection, adapters) that currently underpin most GFMs, and the O(N log N) SGW-based computation enables scalability to 5M nodes where all compared baselines fail with OOM errors. The interpretability of learned bases—showing domain-specific vs. universal structural motifs without supervision—provides a diagnostic tool for understanding what structure is transferable. However, the SGW approximation retains only ρ≈0.76 correlation with exact GW, the total-boundedness assumption is unverified for truly heterogeneous corpora, and the performance gains over RiemannGFM (~4%) are modest, leaving open whether the geometric coordinate system scales to the very large, multi-domain pretraining regimes that define the GFM paradigm.
