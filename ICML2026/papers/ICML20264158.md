## Problem

Probabilistic circuits (PCs) enforce data-independent mixture weights at sum nodes, preventing them from adapting routing to the local geometry of the data manifold. While Voronoi tessellations offer a natural mechanism for input-dependent, geometry-aware routing, naively inserting them into PCs breaks tractable inference because oblique Voronoi cell boundaries couple variables that the circuit's product nodes require to be independent. The paper formalizes this incompatibility and develops two complementary strategies to resolve it.

## Input / Output

The method consumes continuous data points x ∈ R^D along with learned centroids (initialized via k-means) and PC parameters (Gaussian leaves, Tucker sum-product layers). During training, a soft temperature-scaled gate w_k(u;α) = exp(−α‖u−c_k‖²)/Σ_j exp(−α‖u−c_j‖²) replaces hard Voronoi indicators to enable backpropagation; at test time, hard Voronoi assignments are restored. For general VT-PCs, the output is certified lower and upper bounds on the partition function, marginals, and conditionals (via axis-aligned box approximations of Voronoi cells); for HFV-PCs, the output is the exact log-likelihood computed in O(|C|K^m) time.

## Method

The paper introduces two geometry-aware PC constructions. First, general Voronoi-Tessellated PCs (VT-PCs) place unconstrained Voronoi cells at sum nodes and recover reliability through a certified approximate inference framework: each Voronoi cell is enclosed by an axis-aligned outer box (via LP) and contains a conservative inner box (radius δ_k/(2√d)), and an anytime refinement algorithm (Algorithm 1) recursively bisects boundary boxes to monotonically tighten bounds at rate O(2^{−t/d}). Second, Hierarchical Factorized Voronoi PCs (HFV-PCs) enforce geometric alignment by factorizing the Voronoi tessellation as a Cartesian product of lower-dimensional cells that mirror the circuit's vtree decomposition, so that Fubini's theorem restores exact tractable inference in O(|C|K^m) time. Both variants are trained via maximum likelihood with a soft-to-hard temperature annealing schedule; Theorem 3.16 proves the soft gate converges to the hard Voronoi indicator at an exponential rate governed by the geometric margin γ(u) = min_{j≠k*}(‖u−c_j‖²−‖u−c_{k*}‖²), ensuring stable optimization while recovering exact inference guarantees at test time.

## Results

On UCI tabular benchmarks, VT-HCLT certified lower bounds exceed the exact HCLT baseline on power (5.10 vs. 0.55), gas (10.57 vs. 7.22), and hepmass (−19.10 vs. −20.36), while VT-EinsumNet lower bounds beat EinsumNet on power (1.73 vs. 0.56) and gas (7.13 vs. 5.85). HFV variants essentially match their baselines (e.g., HFV-EinsumNet: 0.56/5.86/−22.32/−32.18 vs. EinsumNet: 0.56/5.85/−22.35/−32.24), confirming that the alignment constraint for exact tractability limits expressivity in shallow circuits. On the 8 synthetic 2D/3D geometric datasets, VT certified lower bounds consistently exceed baseline exact log-likelihoods, while HFV remains comparable to baselines. The gap between certified lower and upper bounds widens with dimensionality (e.g., on miniboone 43D, VT-HCLT spans −26.46 to −8.95), consistent with the O(2^{−t/d}) convergence rate in Theorem 3.9.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares two baseline PC architectures—EinsumNet (random binary tree) and HCLT (Hidden Chow-Liu Tree)—against their Voronoi-tessellated (VT) and Hierarchical Factorized Voronoi (HFV) variants. VT-HCLT certified lower bounds outperform the HCLT baseline on 3 of 4 UCI datasets: power (5.10 vs. 0.55, +4.55), gas (10.57 vs. 7.22, +3.35), and hepmass (−19.10 vs. −20.36, +1.26), but underperform on miniboone (−26.46 vs. −25.43, −1.03). VT-EinsumNet lower bounds beat EinsumNet on power (1.73 vs. 0.56) and gas (7.13 vs. 5.85) but trail on hepmass (−22.98 vs. −22.35) and miniboone (−32.82 vs. −32.24). HFV variants essentially tie their baselines across all four datasets (differences ≤ 0.06), confirming that the geometric alignment constraint required for exact tractability does not yield additional expressivity in the shallow-circuit regime tested. On the 8 synthetic geometric datasets, VT lower bounds consistently exceed baseline exact log-likelihoods, while HFV matches baselines, suggesting the geometry-aware routing benefit is most pronounced when the data has strong local structure and the circuit is deep enough to exploit it.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the probabilistic circuit (PC) framework by replacing data-independent mixture weights with Voronoi-tessellation-based geometric gating, drawing on Voronoi density estimation (Polianskii et al., 2022; Marchetti et al., 2023) and differentiable tessellation in normalizing flows (Chen et al., 2022). It departs from prior input-dependent routing in PCs (CSPNs, SPQNs, PNCs) by encoding geometry directly in the modeled input space rather than conditioning on external features or imposing variable orderings. The base architectures (EinsumNet, HCLTs) are standard tensorized PCs, and the paper's core contribution is the theoretical formalization of why Voronoi geometry breaks tractability and two principled remedies: certified box-based approximate inference and hierarchical factorized Voronoi (HFV) gating that restores exact inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first formal incompatibility result between Voronoi routing and PC tractability, along with two complementary solutions that give practitioners a clear expressivity-tractability tradeoff: certified bounds for full geometric expressivity, or exact inference under factorized geometry. A key limitation is the curse of dimensionality in the certified bounds (gap scales as O(2^{-t/d})), making the approximate inference framework impractical beyond moderate dimensions, while HFV sacrifices expressivity for exactness. Open questions include whether learned embeddings can recover more geometric structure within HFV, whether tighter certification via adaptive decompositions or approximate Voronoi diagrams can close the bound gap, and whether the geometric interpretability enables practical continual learning or anomaly detection applications.
