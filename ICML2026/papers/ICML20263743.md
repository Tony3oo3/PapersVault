## Problem

Existing causal discovery methods for Poisson branching structural causal models (PB-SCM) assume causal sufficiency, meaning all common causes are observed. This assumption fails in real-world count data where latent confounders (e.g., unobserved user intent in advertising) are ubiquitous, and no prior work has established how to identify causal structures in the Poisson branching framework when such confounders are present.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263743/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes m i.i.d. samples of multi-dimensional non-negative integer-valued (Poisson) count data and produces a Partial Parental Acyclic Directed Mixed Graph (PPADMG) over the observed variables, where edges carry directed, bi-directed, or circle endpoints to encode direct causation, latent confounding, and orientation uncertainty respectively. The algorithm operates by computing higher-order partial derivatives of the empirical local PGF (obtained by setting non-target variables' arguments to zero), testing whether specific monomial coefficients vanish, and applying a three-phase procedure: skeleton learning, latent confounding detection, and triple-based orientation.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a Trie-based graphical representation that maps every directed path structure in the causal DAG to a unique monomial in the log-PGF expansion. The Monomial Representation Theorem (Theorem 3.5) establishes that the log-PGF decomposes as a sum over all Pruned Tries (subtrees obtained by cutting edges from the full path tree rooted at each node), where each Pruned Trie contributes a monomial (product of vertex variables) with a coefficient determined by the thinning probabilities along active and cut edges. By leveraging local PGFs to isolate 3-variable substructures, the paper proves that the set of 29 monomial equivalence classes over three observed variables is completely identifiable via specific monomial presence/absence conditions (Theorems 3.7–3.13), covering skeleton detection, confounded edge identification, collider orientation, and bi-directed edge recovery. The practical algorithm (Algorithm 1) tests for these monomials by computing second-, third-, and higher-order mixed partial derivatives of the empirical log-PGF at zero, using threshold or bootstrap tests to determine significance.

## Results

On four synthetic latent-confounding scenarios (Cases 1–4), the proposed LC-PB-SCM method achieves F1 scores of 1.00, 0.72, 0.84, and 0.87, consistently outperforming all baselines. The method achieves perfect recovery (F1 = 1.00) on Case 1 and maintains F1 ≥ 0.72 across all cases, while the strongest baseline (FCI) scores 0.00 on three of four cases. On the real-world Shopping Mall Paid Search Campaign dataset, the method correctly identifies the causal direction Clicks → Conversions in the presence of a latent confounder (Impressions).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed LC-PB-SCM method outperforms all four baselines across all four synthetic cases. FCI, a constraint-based method, fails completely on Cases 1, 3, and 4 (F1 = 0.00) and only achieves 0.53 on Case 2, as it cannot exploit the parametric branching structure. PB-SCM, the closest prior method that models branching but assumes causal sufficiency, scores between 0.41 and 0.47, consistently underperforming the proposed method by 0.25–0.53 F1 points. GES (score-range 0.33–0.55) and OCD (score-range 0.33–0.66) both struggle with the latent confounding and branching dynamics, with OCD performing best on Case 3 (0.66) but still trailing the proposed method (0.84). The largest margin of improvement is on Case 1, where the proposed method achieves perfect F1 = 1.00 versus 0.55 for the best baseline (GES).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PB-SCM framework (Xiang et al., NeurIPS 2024; Qiao et al., AAAI 2024), which established identifiability of Poisson branching causal structures under the restrictive assumption of causal sufficiency, by incorporating latent confounders. It departs from the broader latent-confounding literature (FCI, GIN, rank-based methods) by exploiting the unique algebraic signature of the binomial thinning operator in the Probability Generating Function, rather than relying on covariance rank conditions or conditional independence tests. The novel Trie representation bridges graph topology and PGF monomial structure, a connection absent from both the prior PB-SCM work and the discrete latent variable tensor-rank approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The completeness theorem (29 monomial equivalence classes for 3-node structures) provides a rare finite, verifiable identifiability guarantee in a setting where latent confounding typically renders causal direction unidentifiable. However, the scope is limited: only exogenous (root) latent confounders are handled, intermediate latent mediators are excluded via a compositional equivalence argument, and identifiability is proven only for local 3-variable substructures. Extending to larger substructures, handling interactive latent effects, and scaling the empirical PGF estimation to high-dimensional count data remain open challenges that will determine the method's practical reach beyond the demonstrated advertising and sports applications.
