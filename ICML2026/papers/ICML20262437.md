## Problem

Existing manifold clustering methods treat manifold construction and label assignment as loosely coupled stages, producing inconsistent structures, while anchor-based scalable alternatives often lack explicit balance constraints and collapse into degenerate partitions on skewed data. No unified framework simultaneously enforces manifold-label consistency, operates at anchor-level complexity, and provides a theoretically grounded balance mechanism.

## Input / Output

The method takes a data matrix X ∈ R^{n×d} and a target number of clusters K, then selects m anchors (m ≪ n) via the VDA strategy to form a compact representation. Clustering is performed entirely in the m×m anchor space rather than the n×n sample space; final sample labels are recovered by multiplying the anchor graph M with the anchor label matrix Z and taking argmax per row. The anchor-induced distance matrix W is constructed through a nonlinear sigmoid-like transformation of the structural association matrix B = M^T Δ^{-1} M, avoiding any direct sample-to-sample distance computation.

## Method

LMBC extends manifold K-means to the anchor space by treating K-means as a special case of manifold learning, where the label matrix Z directly induces the manifold structure S = QQ^T (with Q = ZP^{-1/2}), ensuring that anchors on the same manifold surface share labels. For balance, the paper proves (Theorem 3.1) via Jensen's inequality that maximizing the Schatten-p norm ‖Z‖_sp^p over the simplex-constrained label matrix forces equal cluster sizes m/K, a novel use since Schatten-p is conventionally minimized. The optimization alternates between updating Z via a first-order Taylor linearization of the Schatten-p term (reducing each row update to an argmin over K entries) and updating the gradient F = pUΣ^{-1}|Σ|^pV^T via SVD, with an incremental caching strategy that brings per-iteration cost to O(mK). The overall time complexity is O((mK² + mKt)T + m²K) and space is O(m²), both depending only on the number of anchors rather than samples.

## Results

On the four large-scale datasets, LMBC achieves the best ACC across all: 0.8594 on Pendigits, 0.9267 on CIFAR-10, 0.7964 on MNIST, and 0.4230 on Covtype. On the four smaller datasets (Table 2), LMBC also leads on every metric, with particularly strong gains on gisette (ACC 0.9679 vs. 0.9421 for ERCAN) and USPS (ACC 0.8560 vs. 0.7895 for FCAG). Several sample-level baselines (KKM, K-sum, ERCAN, RKM) fail entirely on Covtype with out-of-memory errors, while LMBC completes within its O(m²) anchor-space budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LMBC outperforms all 11 baselines on every dataset where a comparison is possible. Against sample-level methods, the gap is largest on high-dimensional data: on gisette, LMBC reaches ACC 0.9679 versus 0.9421 (ERCAN) and 0.5017 (LCSOG), while KKM and K-sum collapse to near-chance (≈0.50). Among anchor-based competitors, LMBC beats FCAG by 2.2 ACC points on MNIST (0.7964 vs. 0.6481) and by 5.6 points on Covtype (0.4230 vs. 0.2195), and surpasses ACLR by 4.7 points on MNIST (0.7964 vs. 0.7490). The closest competitor overall is L-Isomap on CIFAR-10 (0.8966 vs. 0.9267, a 3.0-point gap), but L-Isomap cannot run on Covtype at all. On the naturally imbalanced Covtype dataset, LMBC still leads (ACC 0.4230) without forcing artificial balance, whereas FCAG and FCALS-D drop to 0.2195 and 0.3660 respectively. The ablation (Table 4) confirms that removing the Schatten-p balance term, the anchor-induced distance, or the manifold consistency term each degrades performance, with the anchor-induced distance contributing the largest single drop (e.g., gisette ACC falls from 0.9679 to 0.6080 without it).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate threads: manifold K-means (building on Gao et al. 2025's observation that K-means is a special case of manifold learning), anchor-based scalable clustering (using the structural association matrix formulation from Ma et al. 2025 / ACLR), and balanced clustering regularization. The key conceptual departure is inverting the usual relationship: rather than learning a manifold and then clustering on it, the method uses cluster labels to *guide* manifold construction, ensuring structural-label consistency. Additionally, it repurposes the Schatten-p norm in the opposite direction from its conventional use (maximization for balance rather than minimization for denoising), supported by a Jensen-inequality-based proof.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a theoretically grounded, single-framework answer to a practical gap: anchor-based clustering methods that are scalable but lack explicit balance guarantees, and balanced clustering methods that are sample-level and not integrated with manifold-aware representations. The soft balance mechanism via Schatten-p maximization is elegant and avoids hard constraints, and the synthetic experiments show it adapts to both balanced and imbalanced data naturally. However, the method still requires knowing K in advance, the Covtype results (ACC ≈ 0.42) suggest limited robustness to extreme real-world imbalance, and the anchor ratio sensitivity (0.24–0.9 across datasets) indicates non-trivial hyperparameter tuning in practice. Open questions include whether the label-guided manifold construction extends to multi-view or semi-supervised settings, and whether the O(m²) space cost remains prohibitive for very large anchor counts.
