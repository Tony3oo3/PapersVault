## Problem

Diffusion models generate realistic images but often fail at precise text-image alignment, producing errors like overcounting and object repetition. While SoftREPA improves alignment by optimizing soft text tokens via contrastive learning, its contrastive objective excessively penalizes negative pairs, forcing soft tokens into off-manifold regions and causing characteristic failure modes. The paper addresses this instability by replacing unbounded contrastive pushing with bounded, score-level preference guidance that is reward-free and derived entirely from the diffusion model's internal log-likelihood.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262316/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

For text-to-image generation, the method takes a text prompt and produces a 512×512 (SD1.5/SDXL) or 1024×1024 (SD3) RGB image; for image editing, it takes a source image plus a text prompt describing the desired modification and outputs the edited image. During training, the method consumes paired text-image data where positive pairs are the ground-truth caption-image combinations and negative pairs are in-batch mismatched captions, with two separate learnable soft token vectors (ψ⁺ and ψ⁻) appended to the text conditioning. The method is applied as a lightweight post-training step on frozen diffusion backbones, optimizing only the soft tokens.

## Method

AGSM formulates text-image alignment as a Plackett-Luce (PL) preference model over multiple candidate captions, generalizing the pairwise Bradley-Terry model used in Diffusion-DPO. An implicit alignment reward is defined as the expected conditional log-likelihood of the DDPM reverse transition under each text condition, requiring no external reward model. This reward tilts the target conditional distribution into positive and negative branches (p_t⁺ and p_t⁻), analogous to classifier-free guidance and negative prompting respectively. A dual-token scheme assigns separate soft tokens ψ⁺ and ψ⁻ to the positive and negative branches, so the score-matching objective explicitly guides the denoising target in bounded directions for both aligned and mismatched pairs rather than merely maximizing the loss on negatives. This prevents the unbounded divergence of SoftREPA's contrastive objective while preserving generative fidelity, and the approach is complementary to existing RL-based diffusion post-training methods.

## Results

On COCO-val5K text-to-image generation, AGSM achieves ImageReward of 34.50 (SD1.5), 84.22 (SDXL), and 103.3 (SD3), with FID of 25.94, 24.83, and 34.08 respectively—substantially lower FID than SoftREPA (29.25, 26.42, 36.21) while maintaining competitive CLIP alignment scores (27.23, 26.86, 27.00). On GenEval, the method achieves over 35% improvement in counting accuracy compared to SoftREPA, directly addressing its characteristic overcounting and repetition failure modes. The method is applicable across SD1.5, SDXL, and SD3 backbones and is complementary to RL-based post-training methods such as Diffusion-DPO and DSPO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SoftREPA (the primary baseline), AGSM trades a modest ImageReward reduction (e.g., 34.50 vs. 40.02 on SD1.5; 103.3 vs. 108.5 on SD3) for substantially better image fidelity (FID 25.94 vs. 29.25 on SD1.5; 34.08 vs. 36.21 on SD3) and slightly higher CLIP alignment scores across all three backbones. On GenEval, AGSM achieves over 35% improvement in counting accuracy over SoftREPA, directly mitigating its overcounting and object-repetition failure modes caused by unbounded contrastive pushing of negative pairs. Compared to the unmodified base models (SD1.5, SDXL, SD3), AGSM yields large gains in ImageReward (e.g., +16.8 on SD1.5, +9.2 on SDXL, +9.0 on SD3) while keeping FID close to or below the base level. Against RL-based post-training methods (Diffusion-DPO, DSPO, SPO, InPO), the paper positions AGSM as complementary rather than directly competitive: it operates on soft tokens within the score-matching objective and can be stacked on top of preference-optimized checkpoints. For image editing (Table 10), AGSM-based editing matches or slightly trails FlowEdit and FlowAlign on CLIP and LPIPS but achieves the best SSIM (86.44) and lowest LPIPS (6.89) among the flow-based baselines on the SD3 backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends SoftREPA (Lee et al., 2025a), which optimizes lightweight soft text tokens via a contrastive score-matching objective to improve text-image alignment in diffusion models. The key departure is identifying that SoftREPA's contrastive formulation causes unbounded divergence of negative pairs off the diffusion manifold, and replacing it with a Plackett-Luce preference formulation that assigns explicit, bounded score-level guidance to both positive and negative pairs using separate soft tokens (ψ⁺, ψ⁻). The score-level preference modeling draws on DSPO (Zhu et al., 2025) and Diffusion-DPO (Wallace et al., 2024), but the paper's contribution is making this reward-free by deriving alignment preferences from the model's own reverse-transition log-likelihood rather than external reward models or human annotations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled diagnosis of why contrastive soft-token training produces characteristic failure modes (overcounting, object repetition) and offers a targeted fix that is lightweight, model-agnostic (SD1.5, SDXL, SD3), and complementary to RL-based post-training. The 35% improvement in GenEval counting accuracy over SoftREPA while matching its overall performance is practically meaningful for deployment. Open questions include whether the dual-token scheme generalizes to multi-modal or video generation, whether the PL formulation's boundedness guarantees hold under longer training or larger batch sizes, and how the method interacts with emerging flow-matching backbones beyond the tested SD3.
