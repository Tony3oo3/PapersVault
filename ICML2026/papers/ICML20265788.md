## Problem

Standard diffusion policies model multimodal robot behaviors but rely on high-dimensional sampling noise as their only control interface, making behavior steering under constraint-induced shifts ill-conditioned and unreliable. When environmental changes invalidate training modes, existing methods either require full retraining or perform unstable optimization in unstructured noise spaces, failing to select or synthesize feasible strategies from a single new demonstration.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265788/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The policy takes robot observation context c_t and a low-dimensional continuous behavior latent z (d_z ≪ H·d_a) as conditioning inputs, producing action chunks of horizon H executed in receding-horizon fashion. At test time, a single target demonstration τ̃ is consumed by the frozen encoder to produce a semantic warm-start z_0, which is then refined via a few gradient steps on a diffusion-consistent fitting loss—no policy weights are updated. The behavior latent is learned from full trajectory traces (e.g., proprioceptive positional states) rather than raw observations, and the encoder outputs a Gaussian posterior whose mean serves as the deterministic control code.

## Method

PDP learns a geometry-aligned behavior manifold by training a trajectory encoder-decoder (VAE) with an additional soft-DTW geometry loss that enforces linear correspondence between Euclidean distances in latent space and physical trajectory similarity, making z a navigable coordinate chart where small displacements induce predictable behavior changes. The diffusion denoiser is conditioned on z via FiLM-style global modulation (layer-wise affine transformations h←γ(z)⊙h+β(z)) rather than input concatenation, which ablations show is critical for the denoiser to actually track the latent signal. Training alternates between embedding refinement (reconstruction + KL + geometry losses) and denoiser optimization with a stop-gradient on z to prevent the diffusion loss from distorting the metric structure. At deployment, adaptation reduces to optimizing only z via gradient descent on a diffusion-consistent fitting objective initialized from the encoder's semantic warm-start, enabling both selection of surviving training modes and interpolation to synthesize novel behaviors absent from the training data.

## Results

On constraint-shifted manipulation scenes (the paper's primary evaluation), PDP achieves a 92.0% overall average success rate across four representative domains, compared to 64.6% for the strongest baseline (VQ-BeT) and 21.5% for standard Diffusion Policy. In the zero-mode-feasible setting (Scene 3), where no training mode remains valid and a single new demonstration must be adapted to, PDP is the only method that consistently succeeds across all domains (e.g., 100% on CLOSEDRAWER, 91.7% on MEATOFFGRILL), while all baselines drop below 25%. On the real-robot OPENDRAWER task, PDP achieves 5/5 success in all four scenes, whereas DP degrades from 5/5 to 1/5 under constraint shifts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PDP decisively outperforms all eight baselines on constraint-shifted scenes, with a 92.0% overall average versus 64.6% for the closest competitor (VQ-BeT) and 21.5% for standard Diffusion Policy (DP). The gap is most dramatic in the zero-mode-feasible setting (Scene 3), where PDP remains the only method to consistently succeed across domains (82.5–100%), while DP drops to 2.5–40%, BC to 0–22.5%, and IBC/ADPro to 0–0.8%. VQ-BeT is competitive on CLOSEDRAWER (100% in all three scenes) and PLACEBLOCK (76–92%), but collapses to 0% on MEATOFFGRILL and drops to 70% on PICKUPCUP Scene 3. BC, which is allowed to fine-tune its weights on the new demonstration, achieves moderate success in existing-mode scenes (e.g., 80% on MEATOFFGRILL Scene 1) but fails in the novel-behavior regime, confirming that local parameter adaptation alone cannot synthesize unseen strategies. On the original (unshifted) scenes, PDP matches or exceeds VQ-BeT and Diff-ES (e.g., 90% vs. 79.2% on PICKUPCUP, 97.5% vs. 77.5% on PLACEBLOCK), showing that the behavior latent does not sacrifice multimodal fidelity. On the real-robot task, PDP maintains 5/5 success across all scenes while DP degrades to 1/5 (20%) under the hardest constraint shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

PDP extends the Diffusion Policy framework (Chi et al., 2023) by replacing its reliance on stochastic sampling noise for multimodality with an explicit, continuous behavior latent z learned via a VAE whose geometry is aligned to physical trajectory similarity through a soft-DTW loss. The conditioning mechanism draws on FiLM-style global modulation (Perez et al., 2018) to deeply integrate the latent into the denoiser, while the test-time adaptation procedure (gradient descent on z with a semantic warm-start) departs from prior noise-space optimization approaches (Wagenmaker et al., 2025; Li et al., 2025c) by operating in a low-dimensional, well-conditioned space. Conceptually, the work bridges classical parameterized-skill learning in RL (Konidaris & Barto, 2009; Dalal et al., 2021) with modern generative diffusion policies, and contrasts with discrete-bottleneck alternatives like VQ-BeT (Lee et al., 2024) that restrict control to a finite behavior set.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important deployment gap: constraint-induced behavior shifts where environmental changes invalidate most training modes, a failure mode underrepresented in standard observation-shift evaluations. The zero-mode-feasible setting (Scene 3) is particularly valuable, showing that a structured latent space enables synthesis of behaviors absent from training data from a single demonstration—something weight-updating BC cannot achieve. Key limitations include the requirement for a successful target demonstration at adaptation time and the assumption that the new behavior lies within or near the learned manifold; scalability to truly out-of-distribution constraints or tasks with continuous (rather than discrete) mode structure remains open. The benchmark suite, while well-designed, is constructed with explicitly discrete modes, so generalization to more natural, continuously-varying multimodality is untested.
