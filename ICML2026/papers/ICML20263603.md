## Problem

Existing minority sampling methods define rarity relative to the generative model's own learned density, confining 'minority' to model-specific notions tied to a particular training set. This generator-centric perspective cannot capture semantically meaningful atypicality grounded in real-world priors, such as instances that are genuinely rare in the broader world rather than merely uncommon in a narrow training distribution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263603/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method is entirely training-free and operates at inference time on a pretrained diffusion model (e.g., ADM, Stable Diffusion 1.5, SDXL-Lightning) paired with a pretrained JEPA encoder (DINOv2-ViT-S/14). It accepts random noise as input, optionally conditioned on class labels or text prompts, and produces atypical RGB images (64×64 for CelebA, 256×256 for ImageNet, 512×512 for SD). Notably, the JEPA encoder is condition-agnostic—it never sees the conditioning signal—so the guidance is applied without modifying either the diffusion model or the encoder.

## Method

JEPA guidance steers diffusion sampling toward low-density regions under a world prior by using the gradient of JEPA-SCORE (the log-sum of singular values of the JEPA encoder's Jacobian) as a guidance term added to each reverse-diffusion step. To make this computationally tractable, the paper approximates the Jacobian's singular values via randomized SVD with a theoretical error bound (Proposition 4.1), and applies the Envelope Theorem to avoid backpropagating through the SVD optimization, reducing memory and compute. Deferred guidance delays the start of JEPA guidance to an intermediate timestep (τT, typically 0.8T) to avoid the domain gap between noisy denoised estimates and clean JEPA inputs, and to allow conditional models to incorporate conditioning before guidance begins. The guidance is applied intermittently (every N steps) with a variance-scaled or constant step size, yielding a bilevel optimization where the inner level estimates representation-level density and the outer level guides sampling toward world-level atypicality.

## Results

On CelebA 64×64 (unconditional), JEPA guidance achieves cFID 8.50 and JEPA-SCORE −300.79, substantially outperforming ADM (cFID 12.11, JEPA −221.67) and all generator-centric minority baselines (cFID 58–67, JEPA −139 to −203). On ImageNet 256×256 (class-conditional), it reaches cFID 18.33 and JEPA-SCORE −241.62 versus ADM's 26.44 and −102.01. On text-to-image (SDv1.5), JEPA guidance attains the lowest JEPA-SCORE (−355.40) while maintaining competitive CLIPScore (31.46) and PickScore (21.50), and on SDXL-Lightning it achieves JEPA-SCORE −337.88 with CLIPScore 31.52.

## Baselines & Comparisons

JEPA guidance decisively outperforms all generator-centric minority samplers (Sehwag et al., MG, SGMS, BnS) on both CelebA and ImageNet, achieving the best cFID, sFID, and JEPA-SCORE while also matching or exceeding ADM on fidelity metrics. Notably, existing minority methods like SGMS actually underperform standard ADM sampling on JEPA-SCORE (−171.85 vs. −221.67 on CelebA), confirming that generator-centric approaches cannot produce world-centric minorities. On text-to-image (SDv1.5), JEPA guidance achieves the lowest JEPA-SCORE (−355.40) among all methods, surpassing MinorityPrompt (−322.33) and CADS (−311.23), while maintaining competitive CLIPScore (31.46 vs. MinorityPrompt's 31.56) and the highest PickScore (21.50). On SDXL-Lightning, it achieves JEPA-SCORE −337.88 versus DDIM's −283.04, with comparable CLIPScore (31.52 vs. 31.57) and PickScore (22.63 vs. 22.68). In the downstream data-augmentation task, 30K JEPA-guided samples yield the best F1 (0.775) and Recall (0.731) for CelebA attribute classification, outperforming 50K samples from SGMS or BnS.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the minority sampling literature (Sehwag et al., MG, SGMS, BnS, MinorityPrompt) by redefining the reference distribution: instead of targeting low-density regions under the generative model's own learned prior, it targets low-density regions under a world prior induced by a JEPA encoder (e.g., DINOv2). The guidance mechanism builds directly on the score-based diffusion guidance framework (Song et al., 2020b) and the JEPA-SCORE density estimator (Balestriero et al., 2025), which showed that JEPA Jacobian singular values correlate with data density. The computational tractability is achieved through randomized SVD (Halko et al., 2011) combined with the envelope theorem to avoid backpropagating through the SVD, and a deferred-guidance strategy to bridge the domain gap between noisy diffusion latents and clean JEPA inputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The shift from generator-centric to world-centric minority sampling addresses a fundamental limitation: samples rare under a specific training distribution need not be rare in the real world, and the paper demonstrates this concretely (e.g., generator-minorities are semantically dispersed while world-minorities concentrate on genuinely atypical categories like ostriches and stealth aircraft). The approach is entirely training-free, condition-agnostic, and integrates at inference time with off-the-shelf models, making it broadly applicable. Open questions include whether other world models (beyond JEPAs) yield complementary or superior atypicality signals, how to combine multiple priors, and how to further reduce the Jacobian computation overhead for larger models. The paper also notes a dual-use concern: reversing the guidance direction could amplify existing biases in the world prior.
