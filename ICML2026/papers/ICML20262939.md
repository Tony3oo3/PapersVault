## Problem

Mobile agents require hybrid-capabilities reasoning spanning screen summary, subtask planning, action decision, and action function, but existing dense models cannot decouple and balance these capabilities, while standard MoE architectures use input-oriented activation that is fundamentally incompatible with the output-oriented activation needed to align expert selection with the reasoning stage of each generated token.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262939/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a user instruction, a current screen screenshot, and the action history, CoME generates a structured four-stage reasoning trajectory (screen summary → subtask plan → action decision → action function) as free-form text tokens, from which the final action (type and parameters such as click coordinates or typed text) is parsed. The key structural constraint is that each output token is tagged with the reasoning stage it belongs to, and the channel router must select the corresponding expert channel for that token, making the output generation process stage-aware rather than input-token-dependent.

## Method

CoME extends each transformer layer's FFN into four specialized experts (one per reasoning stage) while sharing a single Self-Attention module, and introduces a channel router that, after the final layer, projects the flattened multi-channel hidden states into per-token expert logits and fuses the channels via softmax-weighted summation to produce the final hidden state for LM-Head decoding. Training follows a three-stage progressive curriculum: Expert-FT fine-tunes each expert's FFN on capability-specific data to decouple skills; Router-FT trains only the channel router with cross-entropy and a norm-regularization loss against per-token expert labels; CoT-FT jointly optimizes the full model with SFT loss plus router-norm regularization to enable balanced multi-expert collaboration. To mitigate error propagation across reasoning stages, Info-DPO trains reward models that predict the final action conditioned on progressively longer prefixes of the trajectory, computes per-stage information gain as the log-likelihood ratio between consecutive reward models, and uses this process-level signal (combined with action-accuracy reward) to select DPO preference pairs that reinforce trajectories where every intermediate step contributes positively to the final prediction.

## Results

On the AITZ benchmark, CoME (5B activated parameters) achieves 66.98% overall action match accuracy, outperforming the strongest dense 7B baseline (UITars, 65.41%) by +1.57% and the best MoE baseline (AriaUI, 63.56%) by +3.42%. On AMEX, CoME reaches 72.61% overall accuracy, surpassing the best dense model (SphAgent, 70.71%) by +1.90% and the best MoE model (Qwen2VL-MoE, 64.56%) by +8.05%. On out-of-distribution Android Control splits, CoME achieves 66.12% average, exceeding OS-Atlas (7B) by +2.08 points with fewer parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoME outperforms all 13 baselines on both AITZ and AMEX. Against dense agents, CoME (5B) beats the strongest 7B model UITars (65.41% on AITZ) by +1.57% and OS-Atlas (65.11%) by +1.87%, while also surpassing 2B models (UITars 2B: 55.63%, ShowUI: 49.55%) by over 11%. Against MoE baselines, CoME leads AriaUI (3.9B, 63.56%) by +3.42% and Qwen2VL-MoE (3B, 60.69%) by +6.29% on AITZ, with an even larger margin on AMEX (+8.05% over Qwen2VL-MoE). Notably, CoME achieves the highest CLICK accuracy (65.22%) among all methods, a particularly challenging action type that requires precise hybrid-capabilities reasoning. On the OOD Android Control benchmark, CoME (5B) surpasses OS-Atlas (7B) by +2.08 points average, demonstrating stronger generalization. The paper also shows CoME uses less GPU memory (11.69 GB inference vs. 16.52 GB for Qwen2VL-7B) while achieving higher accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from standard Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures, which use input-oriented activation to route input tokens to experts, by proposing output-oriented activation that selects which expert's hidden state generates each output token based on the current reasoning stage. It builds on the chain-of-action-thought formulation from AITZ (Zhang et al., 2024), which decomposes GUI agent reasoning into screen summary, subtask plan, action decision, and action function stages, and on the growing line of dense mobile agents (UITars, OS-Atlas, UGround) and sparse MoE mobile agents (AriaUI, MolmoE, DeepSeekVL2). The progressive training curriculum (Expert-FT → Router-FT → CoT-FT) and the InfoGain-Driven DPO objective are novel contributions that repurpose standard SFT and DPO losses with process-level information-gain rewards to address error propagation in multi-stage reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The output-oriented activation mechanism offers a principled way to align expert specialization with reasoning stages in multi-capability agent systems, a design choice that could generalize beyond mobile GUI agents to any autoregressive task requiring stage-specific capabilities. The finding that a smaller (7B) reward model produces more discriminative InfoGain signals than a larger (72B) one is a practical insight for process-reward design in DPO. However, the performance gains over strong baselines remain modest (+1.73% over dense, +5.72% over MoE), and the approach is validated only on two mobile GUI benchmarks with a fixed 4-expert decomposition; open questions include whether the architecture scales to more heterogeneous capability sets, whether the progressive training recipe transfers to other multi-stage reasoning domains, and whether the output-oriented activation advantage persists at larger model scales where dense models may close the gap.
