## Problem

High-dimensional ERM analyses under non-Gaussian data designs typically rely on implicit Gaussian universality arguments that replace the data or test score by a moment-matched Gaussian proxy, without characterizing when such replacements are valid. This paper makes the universality step explicit and provides a precise criterion for when Gaussian score and performance universality hold versus when they break down, a distinction that prior frameworks (RMT, AMP, CGMT) leave implicit or assume away.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes a training set of n samples (x_i, y_i) in R^p × Y with p = Θ(n), a convex twice-differentiable loss L_y, and a convex strongly-convex regularizer ρ, under a samplewise Poincaré concentration assumption on the joint distribution of (x, y). It produces a deterministic saddle-point tuple (μ*, α*, κ*, β*, ν*) obtained from a finite-dimensional min-max objective J, from which the asymptotic mean μ_θ̂ ≈ μ*, the fluctuation scale tr(C_x C_θ̂) ≈ α*², and the full test-score law x^T θ̂ ≈ x^T μ* + α* z are read off. The key structural output is the decomposition separating the (generally non-Gaussian) informative projection x^T μ* from an independent centered Gaussian of variance α*².

## Method

The paper heuristically extends the Convex Gaussian Min-Max Theorem to concentrated non-Gaussian column designs (Claim 4.1), then decomposes the ERM solution as θ̂ = μ_θ̂ + θ₀ and analyzes the mean and fluctuation separately. A quadratic universality result (Theorem 3.3) shows that any smooth strongly-convex regularizer satisfying a weak-separability condition on its third-derivative tensor can be replaced, for the first two moments of the test score, by a quadratic surrogate depending only on H_ρ = ∇²ρ(0). The resulting deterministic min-max objective J(μ, α, κ; β, ν) involves the Moreau envelope of the loss evaluated at μ^T x + α z, and its saddle point (μ*, α*) is equivalently characterized by a four-equation fixed-point system (Theorem 4.4) that recovers classical RMT/AMP equations in the squared-loss linear-model case. Under a minimizer-universality assumption (Assumption 4), the test score is fully characterized as x^T μ* + α* z, yielding the sharp criterion that the score is Gaussian if and only if the informative projection x^T μ* is Gaussian (Theorem 6.1). For signal-plus-Gaussian-noise models, a covariance-adjusted confinement theorem (Theorem 7.1) shows μ* lies in (H_ρ + ν*C_N)^{-1}F, isolating the structured component x_I^T μ* as the sole source of score non-Gaussianity.

## Results

The paper's main results are theoretical: Theorem 4.3 provides a deterministic min-max/saddle-point characterization of the ERM estimator's asymptotic mean μ* and fluctuation scale α*, while Theorem 6.1 establishes that the test score x⊤θ̂ is asymptotically Gaussian if and only if the informative projection x⊤μ* is Gaussian. Empirically, in a teacher-student model with bimodal features (p=30, n=50), performance universality holds at the squared-loss endpoint (η=0) despite a clearly non-Gaussian score distribution, but the moment-matched Gaussian surrogate fails to predict empirical generalization error for any η>0 (elastic loss). On MNIST (Appendix G, Figure 2), Gaussian score universality predictions visibly fail to match empirical decision-score histograms and generalization performance, confirming the theoretical breakdown characterization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper, so 'baselines' are prior analytical frameworks rather than competing algorithms. The paper's fixed-point system (Theorem 4.4) recovers the two-equation RMT system of Karoui et al. (2013) and Bean et al. (2013) in the squared-loss/linear-model special case, and generalizes the AMP state-evolution equations of Donoho & Montanari (2016) to arbitrary convex losses and non-Gaussian designs. Relative to the CGMT-based analyses of Thrampoulidis et al. (2014, 2015) and recent extensions (Akhtiamov et al. 2024; Bosch & Panahi 2025), the key novelty is the explicit decomposition θ̂ = μ_θ̂ + θ₀ that separates the deterministic mean from fluctuations, enabling the score universality characterization (Theorem 6.1) that prior CGMT approaches do not provide. Compared to the score-universality assumption in Montanari & Saeed (2022) and Mallory et al. (2025), which requires the estimator direction to lie in a 'unavoidable' set S, this paper identifies the precise condition (x⊤μ* must be Gaussian) and demonstrates concrete breakdowns (elastic loss with bimodal features, MNIST) where that assumption fails. The covariance-adjusted confinement result (Theorem 7.1) extends the geometric analysis of Mai & Liao (2025) by showing that anisotropic residual noise shifts the relevant subspace from F to (H_ρ + ν*C_N)⁻¹F.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate theoretical toolkits for high-dimensional ERM—random matrix theory (Karoui et al., 2013; Bean et al., 2013), approximate message passing (Donoho & Montanari, 2016; Montanari & Saeed, 2022), and the Convex Gaussian Min-Max Theorem (Gordon, 1988; Thrampoulidis et al., 2014, 2015)—under a single conditional CGMT framework extended to concentrated non-Gaussian designs. Its key departure from prior work is making the Gaussian universality step explicit: rather than assuming or proving universality under restrictive conditions, the paper derives a deterministic saddle-point characterization (Theorem 4.3) that precisely identifies when the score, performance, or minimizer is Gaussian and when it is not. The fixed-point equations recover the classical RMT system for squared loss (Karoui et al., 2013) and generalize the AMP state-evolution structure to arbitrary convex losses and non-Gaussian covariates, while the covariance-adjusted confinement result (Theorem 7.1) extends Dobriban & Wager's (2018) ridge analysis to anisotropic noise settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first general framework that delineates the boundary of Gaussian universality for ERM, distinguishing three previously conflated notions (performance, score, and minimizer universality) and showing that performance can be universal even when the score is provably non-Gaussian. This resolves a long-standing ambiguity in the literature where universality was either assumed (Montanari & Saeed, 2022; Mallory et al., 2025) or shown to fail in isolated examples (Pesce et al., 2023; Mai & Liao, 2025) without a unifying criterion. Key limitations include the conjectural nature of the non-Gaussian CGMT extension (Claim 4.1) and the unproved minimizer universality assumption (Assumption 4), both of which restrict the results to conditional statements. Open questions include a systematic study of the reduced fixed-point system for random-feature models with low-dimensional latent variables, and a rigorous proof of Assumption 4 via Chatterjee-type normal approximation (noted as under preparation).
