## Problem

Computing the differential entropy of distributions known only up to a normalization constant is a fundamental challenge in information theory, energy-based modeling, and maximum-entropy reinforcement learning. While P-SVGD (Messaoud et al., 2024) introduced a Stein variational approach for this setting, it fails to scale to high dimensions due to incorrect local invertibility assumptions, an omitted trace-of-Hessian term that is only valid asymptotically, and a particle-truncation heuristic that induces mode collapse. This paper traces these failures to specific algorithmic flaws and provides principled, provably correct fixes.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an unnormalized density p̄(x) (typically a differentiable deep network), a parameterized initial Gaussian q₀, and M interacting particles, and produces a closed-form differential entropy estimate via the change-of-variables formula applied over L_c adaptive SVGD steps. Unlike sample-based entropy estimators, the output entropy is computed analytically from the learned variational distribution q_θ^L rather than by plugging samples into a density estimator, and the number of integration steps L_c is determined dynamically by a Kernelized Stein Discrepancy convergence check rather than fixed a priori.

## Method

MET-SVGD unifies two informal step-size constraints into a single global invertibility bound derived from Banach's fixed-point theorem (Corr. 3.3), then learns step-wise, dimension-wise RBF kernel bandwidths and step sizes end-to-end via reverse KL-divergence minimization, eliminating manual hyperparameter tuning. It restores the trace-of-Hessian term omitted in P-SVGD using Hutchinson's stochastic trace estimator combined with the double-differentiation trick, which is critical for correctness in the finite-particle regime and for scaling beyond 20 dimensions. A Metropolis-Hastings accept-reject step with a Rademacher auxiliary variable (analogous to momentum-flip in HMC) replaces the particle-truncation heuristic, yielding a reversible proposal and asymptotic convergence to the target independent of particle count. The number of SVGD steps is adapted per target using an efficiently computable Kernelized Stein Discrepancy that reuses precomputed gradient and trace quantities from the entropy derivation, adding negligible overhead.

## Results

On entropy estimation benchmarks (Gaussian and GMM targets up to 1000-D), MET-SVGD achieves up to 12× lower entropy estimation error than P-SVGD and 16× lower error than existing SVGD baselines (S-SVGD, GSVGD, β-SVGD). On CIFAR-10 energy-based image generation, MET-SVGD improves FID by 80.4% over P-SVGD and yields 64× higher training stability. In maximum-entropy reinforcement learning (Walker2d-v2, Humanoid-v2), MET-SVGD achieves up to 16% higher returns than P-SVGD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MET-SVGD consistently outperforms its direct predecessor P-SVGD (Messaoud et al., 2024) across all three tasks: 12× lower entropy error, 80.4% better FID, and 16% higher RL returns. Against SVGD scalability baselines—S-SVGD (Gong et al., 2021), GSVGD (Liu et al., 2022b), and β-SVGD (Sun & Richtárik, 2022)—MET-SVGD achieves up to 16× lower entropy estimation error in high dimensions (up to 1000-D), where these methods struggle beyond 20-D. On CIFAR-10 EBM training, MET-SVGD outperforms Glow-NF (Kingma & Dhariwal, 2018) and LD with contrastive divergence (Welling & Teh, 2011) in both FID and training stability, though the paper notes that adding the MH step to the EBM setting can cause divergence due to the instability of the ELBO objective. In MaxEnt RL, MET-SVGD surpasses SAC (Haarnoja et al., 2018) and SAC-NF (Papamakarios et al., 2017), with the latter exhibiting strong mode collapse and early saturation that limits exploration. The gains are most pronounced when increasing particle count from 10 to 64, and ablations confirm that the learned kernel bandwidth (C2), restored trace-of-Hessian correction (C4), and MH step (C5) each contribute substantially to the overall improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is a direct and pointed correction of P-SVGD (Messaoud et al., 2024), which itself extended SVGD (Liu & Wang, 2016) into a variational inference framework for entropy estimation from unnormalized densities. MET-SVGD refutes P-SVGD's central diagnosis—that kernel-bandwidth sensitivity stems from invertibility violations—by proving global invertibility always holds under a Banach fixed-point condition, and instead attributes the failure to cumulative trace-term noise and an omitted trace-of-Hessian term. The method also draws on invertible residual networks (Behrmann et al., 2019) for the global invertibility condition, Metropolis-Hastings for convergence guarantees, and Hutchinson's estimator for the restored Hessian trace, positioning MET-SVGD as a residual flow with an adaptive number of layers and full-rank Jacobian.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By fixing P-SVGD's algorithmic flaws with principled replacements (unified step-size bound, learned kernel bandwidths, MH correction, restored trace term), MET-SVGD scales SVGD-based entropy estimation to 1000 dimensions and provides asymptotic convergence guarantees independent of particle count—capabilities that are directly relevant to energy-based model training, maximum-entropy RL, and scientific applications like molecular design. A notable open question is the interaction between MH rejection dynamics and the instability of contrastive-divergence/ELBO losses in EBM training, where the MH variant actually diverges in practice. The paper also leaves the finite-particle convergence rate of MH-augmented SVGD as future work, and the practical overhead of the Banach fixed-point inverse computation for the reverse SVGD update remains a consideration at scale.
