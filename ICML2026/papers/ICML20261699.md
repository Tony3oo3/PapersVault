## Problem

Chain-of-Thought reasoning in LLMs becomes brittle over long horizons: irrelevant context persists, small early deviations compound into inference drift, and late-stage corrections destabilize the trajectory. The KV cache provides implicit memory but does not implement controlled forgetting or risk-triggered, calibrated corrections when the model is uncertain. The paper asks whether autoregressive decoding can be stabilized as a perturbed dynamical system with explicit dissipative dynamics and bounded, selective interventions.

## Input / Output

TiF operates on the base Transformer's post-norm readout (the normalized hidden vector after the final LayerNorm, before the LM head), which is projected to a compact controller feature φ_t. It produces a stabilized readout h̃_t = h_t + α_t(r_t ⊙ m_t) from which logits are computed, yielding the next token and a generated reasoning chain. The persistent controller state s_t (dimension d_s ≪ d_h) is carried across decoding steps and updated via a continuous-time ODE integration over inner-time τ∈[0,1] at each token, making the process a hybrid discrete-continuous system rather than a purely token-by-token autoregressive loop.

## Method

TiF augments a pretrained Transformer with a lightweight Neural ODE controller whose thought state evolves via the dissipative dynamics ṡ = −γs + g_θ(s, φ_t), where the linear contraction term −γs implements controlled forgetting and the MLP-driven term g_θ accumulates task-relevant evidence. The controller output is injected as a post-norm residual with two gating mechanisms: a demand-supply impact gate α_t = α_0·H_t·C_t (multiplying base-model entropy by a tanh-bounded state-energy capacity score) that makes intervention risk-triggered and near-identity when the model is confident, and a direction gate r_t = tanh(W_r[φ_t; s_t]) that provides elementwise-bounded, direction-specific steering. The paper establishes well-posedness, dissipativity (an absorbing set for the controller state under γ > L_s), and incremental stability (exponential forgetting of state perturbations) of the controlled dynamics, implying bounded interventions over arbitrarily long contexts. Training follows a two-stage schedule—freeze the base LLM and warm up the ODE controller for 2 epochs, then jointly fine-tune both—using a compute-lean fixed-step Euler solver (N=1) by default, adding less than 0.2% parameter overhead to the base model.

## Results

On GSM8K with Llama-3.2-3B, TiF achieves 50.49% greedy@1 accuracy (+5.76 over SFT) and 58.38% maj@8 (+5.08), while on MATH500 it reaches 11.00% greedy@1 (+2.00) and 15.20% maj@8 (+1.00). Gains are scale-dependent: smaller models benefit most (Llama-3.2-3B: +5.76; Qwen2.5-3B: +2.66; Qwen3-4B: +0.53; Llama-3.1-8B: +0.45 on GSM8K greedy@1). The relative improvement concentrates on long reasoning chains, peaking at +25.3% on GSM8K problems requiring ≥5 steps, consistent with the incremental stability guarantee. On robustness benchmarks, TiF improves over SFT on GSM-IC-4K (+1.42%) and GSM-DC-with-IC Hard (+0.77%), and attains best accuracy in 6/7 GSM-Plus perturbation categories.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TiF outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric in Table 2. The strongest competitor is Masked Thought (MFT), which TiF surpasses by 0.91 points on GSM8K greedy@1 (50.49 vs. 49.58) and 0.61 on maj@8 (58.38 vs. 57.77), but by a much larger margin on MATH500: +3.80 greedy@1 (11.00 vs. 7.20) and +4.40 maj@8 (15.20 vs. 10.80), indicating TiF's dissipative dynamics are particularly effective on harder, longer-horizon math problems. NEFTune provides only marginal gains over SFT (+0.76 on GSM8K greedy@1) and actually degrades MATH500 performance (8.20 vs. 9.00), while both Pause Token variants (Prefix and Dense) fail to improve over SFT on GSM8K and degrade MATH500, confirming that discrete token padding without persistent memory cannot extend reasoning depth. RE-Control, a test-time representation editing method, underperforms SFT on GSM8K (40.18 vs. 44.73) and is outperformed by TiF by 10.31 points on GSM8K greedy@1, though it does edge out TiF on MATH500 greedy@1 by 1.00 point (10.00 vs. 11.00 is actually TiF winning; RE-Control is 10.00 vs TiF 11.00). Overall, TiF's explicit continuous-time stabilization yields consistent wins across both mathematical reasoning and robustness evaluations.

## Relation to Prior Work

TiF positions itself at the intersection of Neural ODEs (Chen et al., 2018) and autoregressive LLM decoding, wrapping a pretrained Transformer with a lightweight continuous-time controller rather than modifying the backbone. It departs from state-space sequence models (Mamba, S4, Gated DeltaNet) that replace or augment attention for efficient recurrence, instead treating the Transformer as a fixed base and adding an external dissipative stabilization layer. Among training-time interventions for reasoning robustness (MFT, NEFTune, Pause tokens, RE-Control), TiF is distinct in imposing an explicit dynamical structure (dissipation + contraction) on a persistent latent state, grounded in nonlinear control theory (contraction analysis, ISS) rather than regularization or masking heuristics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical template (well-posedness, dissipativity, incremental stability) for reasoning about LLM decoding as a dynamical system, which could inform future stabilization or control-based decoding methods. However, empirical gains are modest and scale-dependent: the largest improvements appear on smaller models (Llama-3.2-3B: +5.76 greedy@1) while Llama-3.1-8B shows near-zero gains, and robustness improvements under distractors are small (+0.77% on the hardest split). Open questions include whether entropy-based gating can detect confidently-wrong predictions, whether the per-token ODE overhead is acceptable at scale, and whether the dissipative controller generalizes to non-mathematical or multimodal reasoning without task-specific fine-tuning.
