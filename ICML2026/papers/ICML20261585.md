## Problem

Transformer-based forecasters achieve high accuracy in long-term time series forecasting but incur quadratic O(L²) attention cost, while efficient linear/MLP models run in O(L) yet lack expressivity for nonlinear dynamics. Existing time-series knowledge distillation methods inherit the computer-vision paradigm of point-wise prediction matching, which empirically causes negative transfer when teacher predictions carry aleatoric uncertainty (phase shifts, distribution drifts). The paper asks how to transfer stable structural knowledge from heavy teachers to lightweight students without blindly mimicking potentially unreliable point predictions.

## Input / Output

The student LMP-Net consumes a historical multivariate time series X ∈ R^{L×C} (look-back L=96, C variates) and outputs a future forecast Ŷ ∈ R^{H×C} for horizons H ∈ {96, 192, 336, 720}. A key architectural constraint is the token-point granularity mismatch: the teacher (e.g., PatchTST) exposes patch/token-level features of dimension P, while the student operates on point-wise variate representations, making direct feature matching ill-posed. The distillation pipeline is training-only; at inference, only the lightweight student (as few as 0.18M parameters) is retained.

## Method

Dynamic Structural Distillation (DSD) combines three mechanisms. First, LMP-Net embeds an Expand-Evolve-Contract pathway (inspired by Cover's theorem) that lifts the seasonal component into a higher-dimensional latent manifold (D ≥ L), evolves it with a lightweight MLP, and contracts back to the forecast horizon, adding expressivity while preserving near-linear inference. Second, Dual Manifold Alignment bridges the token-point mismatch at two levels: Macro-SPKD minimizes Frobenius distance between teacher and student Gram matrices to preserve inter-sample relational topology, and Micro-OT computes an entropy-regularized Sinkhorn distance between K student latent tokens and P teacher patch tokens to establish soft local geometric correspondence. Third, Regime-Aware Adaptive Distillation (RAAD) modulates prediction-level supervision via a dataset-level regime prior λ_KD (selected by validation sweep) and an instance-level confidence gate g_{i,t} = exp(−γ·e_{i,t}) that down-weights distillation where the teacher deviates from ground truth, thereby suppressing negative transfer in structure-dominant regimes while retaining useful prediction guidance in prediction-dominant regimes.

## Results

On the main multivariate LTSF benchmark (averaged over H∈{96,192,336,720}), LMP-Net distilled with DSD achieves the best MSE on 6 of 8 datasets: ETTh1 (0.430), ETTh2 (0.367), ETTm1 (0.379), ETTm2 (0.278), Electricity (0.179), and Exchange (0.358). On Weather it ties TimeMixer for second-best MSE (0.246, behind Pathformer's 0.244), and on Traffic it achieves 0.479 MSE, second only to iTransformer (0.428). In terms of efficiency, LMP-Net uses only 0.18M parameters and 0.08G MACs on ETTh1, delivering 96,398 samples/s throughput—a 15.7× speedup over PatchTST—while matching or exceeding Transformer-level accuracy on most benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LMP-Net+DSD outperforms all seven baselines on 6 of 8 datasets (ETTh1, ETTh2, ETTm1, ETTm2, Electricity, Exchange), with the largest margins over Crossformer (e.g., 0.358 vs. 0.940 MSE on Exchange) and DLinear (e.g., 0.278 vs. 0.361 on ETTm2). Against the strongest Transformer baselines, it beats PatchTST on all 8 datasets (e.g., 0.430 vs. 0.441 on ETTh1, 0.179 vs. 0.204 on Electricity) and iTransformer on 7 of 8, losing only on Traffic (0.479 vs. 0.428). Among efficient baselines, it dominates DLinear and PatchMLP across all datasets and matches or beats TimeMixer everywhere (tying on Weather at 0.246, winning by 0.035 on Traffic). The sole dataset where LMP-Net does not achieve the best MSE is Traffic, where iTransformer's 0.428 MSE remains substantially ahead, likely due to the 862-variate cross-channel coupling that the channel-independent student cannot fully capture. On Weather, Pathformer edges out LMP-Net by 0.002 MSE (0.244 vs. 0.246), making it the closest competitive margin in the paper.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DLinear decomposition-reconstruction pipeline by inserting an Expand-Evolve-Contract manifold expansion pathway on the seasonal branch, inspired by Cover's theorem, to boost expressivity while retaining linear-time inference. For the distillation side, it departs from the dominant output-matching paradigm in time-series KD (e.g., KD-Informer) by importing structural distillation ideas from computer vision—specifically SPKD for inter-sample topology preservation and entropic Optimal Transport (Sinkhorn) for cross-granularity geometric alignment between token-level teacher features and point-level student representations. The Regime-Aware Adaptive Distillation mechanism, which gates prediction-level supervision via a dataset-level prior and instance-level confidence, represents a novel departure from prior KD methods that uniformly trust teacher outputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value lies in the demonstrated regime-dependent nature of distillation: naive prediction-level KD can actively degrade the student on structure-dominant datasets (e.g., Electricity) while helping on prediction-dominant ones (e.g., Traffic), a finding supported by validation sweeps, teacher win-rate analysis, and reliability-bin diagnostics. The framework achieves a favorable accuracy-efficiency trade-off (0.18M params, 96K samples/s on ETTh1) while remaining teacher-agnostic across five architectures. Limitations include the channel-independent student struggling with high-dimensional cross-variable coupling (Traffic), the training-time overhead of the frozen teacher forward pass, and the fact that Micro-OT degenerates to macro-only alignment when the teacher lacks explicit token granularity. Open questions include whether the regime prior can be learned end-to-end rather than selected via validation sweep, and whether the approach extends to probabilistic forecasting or anomaly detection.
