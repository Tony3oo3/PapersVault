## Problem

In multi-agent reinforcement learning, parameter sharing improves sample efficiency but provably yields suboptimal policies in heterogeneous settings because agents are forced to condition on overlapping observation spaces. Existing remedies—clustering, agent-specific masks, or ID concatenation—rely on environment-specific priors and fail when the team exhibits multi-modal policies. The paper identifies observation-space overlap as the root cause and asks whether a shared policy can achieve the same asymptotic optimality as fully heterogeneous policies.

## Input / Output

Each agent receives a local observation vector (zero-padded to a uniform dimension across agents) and a 10-dimensional role embedding z_i learned by a self-supervised VAE. The role embedding is mapped through a shared supernetwork into a projection matrix P_i ∈ R^{d_s × d'}, which transforms the observation into a divided subspace h_i = (P_i·o_i, o_i). The shared diffusion policy outputs actions via a K-step denoising process: continuous actions are scaled to [-1,1] via tanh with optional action selection (sampling K candidates and picking max Q), while discrete actions are sampled from a Gumbel-softmax over m padded action logits. A centralized distributional-Q critic (b-bin) or direct-prediction Q-network provides value estimates for policy improvement.

## Method

Dspic guarantees zero policy deviation from the heterogeneous optimum (Theorem 4.3) by constructing a Complete Division (CD) of the observation space: a VAE pre-trained to predict next-state and reward from agent ID produces discriminative role embeddings, which a shared matrix encoder g_ψ maps to a set of mutually orthogonal projection matrices {P_i} (enforced by a weighted orthogonality loss). Each agent's observation is projected into a disjoint subspace, so the single shared diffusion policy (DDPM) conditions on non-overlapping inputs, eliminating the interference that causes suboptimality. Policy improvement proceeds sequentially over a random agent permutation, updating each agent's diffusion policy via a KL-minimization objective against a Boltzmann target derived from the multi-agent soft Q-function, with a Conservative Temperature Adaptation (CTA) mechanism that clips the entropy loss to prevent over-exploration collapse. The full algorithm inherits monotonic improvement and convergence to a QRE (Theorem 4.7) while sharing all policy parameters, and uses a distributional-Q critic with twin networks for stable value estimation.

## Results

Dspic achieves superior sample efficiency and asymptotic performance across all four benchmarks (MaMuJoCo, SMAC, SMACv2, LBF) compared to 7 baselines. In the only tabular numerical comparison (Table 2), Dspic scores 19.04±0.65 on 6h_vs_8z, 19.51±0.20 on MMM2, 15.06±1.63 on terran_5_vs_5, and 0.97±0.03 on LBF2p3f, outperforming ROMA and RODE on all four tasks. Training overhead is moderate: 1.58× HASAC wall-clock time overall (ranging from 1.26× on LBF to 1.98× on MaMuJoCo), while inference overhead is only 1.14×. The method scales from 2 to 10 agents and handles both continuous (MaMuJoCo) and discrete (SMAC, LBF) action spaces.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Dspic outperforms MAPPO (shared-policy PPO) across nearly all tasks, as MAPPO's uniform parameter sharing leads to suboptimal equilibria in heterogeneous settings; the sole exception is 6h_vs_8z where agents are homogeneous. Against HAPPO (heterogeneous PPO), Dspic wins in most scenarios, with HAPPO degrading notably on MMM2 where its purely heterogeneous architecture prevents knowledge sharing. Compared to HASAC (the strongest MaxEnt baseline), Dspic achieves better sample efficiency in MaMuJoCo and SMAC while HASAC has a slight edge on the simpler LBF task where intensive exploration is less critical. Against Kaleidoscope (learnable masks), Dspic surpasses all policy-based baselines in terran_5_vs_5, though Kaleidoscope retains an advantage there due to its QMIX-based value decomposition. Dspic also outperforms MAT-Dec (Transformer-based sequence modeling) and MASAC-Diff (diffusion shared policy without CD), with the latter showing lower stability in Walker2d 6x1 and Ant 4x2. In the role-based comparison (Table 2), Dspic beats ROMA by 4+ points on 6h_vs_8z and LBF2p3f, and dramatically outperforms RODE on terran_5_vs_5 (15.06 vs. 5.64).

## Relation to Prior Work

Dspic sits at the intersection of two recent lines of work: HASAC's maximum-entropy heterogeneous-agent soft policy iteration (which guarantees QRE convergence via sequential updates) and DIME's formulation of diffusion models as MaxEnt policies (providing the entropy lower bound and KL-minimization training objective). The key conceptual departure is the Complete Division (CD) of the observation space via role-conditioned orthogonal projections, which the authors prove eliminates the policy deviation inherent in naive parameter sharing (Theorem 4.3). This positions Dspic between the homogeneous extreme (MAPPO's uniform sharing) and the heterogeneous extreme (HASAC's fully separate policies), drawing architectural inspiration from SePS's VAE-based role embeddings and ROMA's hypernetwork design while replacing their soft constraints with a hard spatial partition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CD framework provides a clean theoretical resolution to a long-standing tension in MARL: how to retain the sample efficiency of parameter sharing without sacrificing the optimality of heterogeneous policies. The proof that zero policy deviation is achievable under CD is a satisfying result, and the empirical validation across four diverse benchmarks (continuous control, discrete micromanagement, and foraging) strengthens its credibility. However, the method still operates in cyber space, the orthogonal construction leaves non-empty indivisible regions (null-space intersections), and scalability beyond 10 agents remains untested. The authors themselves flag real-world embodied multi-agent tasks and reducing indivisible regions as important future directions, and the reliance on a pre-trained VAE for role extraction adds a warmup dependency that may not transfer cleanly to non-stationary or open-world settings.
