## Problem

Existing Thinking with Images (TWI) methods assume that both the visual cue mining stage and the answer reasoning stage in interleaved image-text CoT are faultless, but in practice errors in either stage propagate and significantly degrade performance. This paper identifies and formalizes this under-explored issue as Noisy Thinking (NT) and shows that standard test-time scaling methods designed for pure-text CoT are intractable for the interleaved multimodal setting because visual signals are continuous, unsegmented, and their uncertainty does not directly align with cue relevance.

## Input / Output

The method takes a multimodal question (text) paired with an initial RGB image and produces a single final text answer. It operates by sampling up to 32 interleaved thinking traces, each containing multiple turns of textual CoT and tool-invoked visual cues (e.g., zoomed-in crops), and works under both online (sequential generation with early stopping at a consensus threshold β=0.9) and offline (all traces pre-generated, then post-hoc filtered) settings. The filtering ratio α and temperature τ are the only tunable hyperparameters, with α=0.4 and τ varying by model variant (0.1 for Thinking, 1.0 for Instruct).

## Method

RTWI proposes a text-centric reliability estimation that avoids direct visual uncertainty modeling by using the Top-k highest-entropy tokens in the textual CoT as a unified proxy for both visual cue quality (mining stage) and reasoning quality (reasoning stage), justified by the observation that tool-invocation tokens carry disproportionately high entropy. A dual-stage filtering module then discards traces whose stage-wise reliability falls below self-adaptive percentile thresholds derived from warm-up traces, preventing noisy traces from contaminating the answer pool. Finally, a reliable voting module replaces uniform majority voting with confidence-weighted aggregation, where each trace's weight is determined by two complementary principles: the trace-wise reliability (sum of both stage reliabilities) and the reliability leap (the non-negative improvement from mining to reasoning stage), combined as C_t = exp(Δ_t / (|w_t|·τ)). This design prioritizes traces that are both consistently reliable across stages and exhibit a confidence boost after acquiring visual cues, mirroring how correct cues boost human reasoning confidence.

## Results

On the primary high-resolution benchmarks (online setting, Qwen3-VL-8B-Thinking), RTWI achieves 85.2% accuracy on Vstar Bench (Attr), 81.6% on Vstar Bench (Spatial), 91.3% on HR-Bench 4K (FSP), and 65.8% on HR-Bench 4K (FCP), while saving 50–62% of tokens (TSR). On reasoning benchmarks, RTWI reaches 23.2% on MathVision and 61.7% on LogicVista (Thinking variant), substantially outperforming the strongest baseline DeepConf (21.2% and 51.8% respectively). The Instruct variant similarly leads, e.g., 94.8% on Vstar Bench (Attr) and 78.3% on HR-Bench 4K (FCP).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RTWI outperforms all eight baselines across every benchmark and both model variants. Against the TWI-specific baselines, RTWI surpasses Thyme by 1.7–4.0 points on Vstar Bench and by 2.8 points on HR-Bench 4K FCP, and exceeds DeepEyes by 3.9 points on HR-Bench 4K FCP (65.8 vs. 59.0). Among TTS baselines, the largest margins appear on LogicVista where RTWI (61.7%) beats DeepConf (51.8%) by nearly 10 points and Self-Cer. (49.3%) by 12.4 points, and on Vstar Bench Spatial where RTWI (81.6%) leads CISC (69.7%) by 11.9 points. On the high-resolution perception tasks, RTWI consistently gains 2–5 points over the strongest TTS baselines (DeepConf, Self-Cer.) while also achieving the highest token saving ratios (50–62% TSR vs. 30–52% for competitors), demonstrating that reliability-aware filtering both improves accuracy and reduces unnecessary computation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Test-Time Scaling (TTS) line of work—self-consistency, early stopping, and confidence-weighted voting—from the text-only 'reasoning with language' paradigm to the more complex 'thinking with images' (TWI) paradigm with interleaved image-text CoT. It builds on token-entropy-based uncertainty estimation (Farquhar et al., 2024; Taubenfeld et al., 2025) but introduces a key conceptual departure: using the textual CoT that drives tool invocations as a text-centric proxy for visual cue reliability, thereby sidestepping the substantially harder problem of modeling visual uncertainty directly. The paper positions itself as orthogonal to training-based TWI improvements like DRIM, offering a plug-and-play inference-time solution applicable to any TWI model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of Noisy Thinking as a distinct, practical failure mode in TWI systems fills a gap: prior TWI methods implicitly assumed faultless cue mining and reasoning, yet real-world deployments inevitably produce noisy traces that propagate errors. The plug-and-play nature (no retraining, works with any TWI backbone) and the simultaneous accuracy-efficiency gains (e.g., +5.2 ACC with 61% token saving on Vstar Attr) make this immediately deployable. Open questions include whether the text-centric reliability proxy generalizes beyond simple zoom-in tools to more complex visual manipulations (cropping, counting, spatial reasoning), whether it extends to video or multi-image inputs, and whether the approach remains effective as TWI models grow more capable and produce fewer noisy traces.
