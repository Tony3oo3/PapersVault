## Problem

Existing B-rep CAD classification methods (UV-Net, AAGNet, DFGAT, etc.) model topological structure via graph neural networks but do not explicitly encode 3D geometric symmetries, causing them to learn pose-dependent features that degrade sharply under rotations and reflections. EquiCAD addresses this by introducing a group-theoretic equivariant encoding that assigns each B-rep geometric attribute to its correct irreducible representation based on physical parity, then combines this symmetry-preserving representation with hierarchical graph reasoning for robust shape classification.

## Input / Output

Input is a B-rep CAD model parsed into a face-edge graph where nodes are parametric surfaces and edges are shared boundary curves. Surfaces are sampled on 16×16 UV grids yielding 7-channel tensors (3D coordinates, surface normals, binary trimming mask), and curves are uniformly sampled to 64 points yielding 6-channel tensors (coordinates, tangents); all geometry is bounding-box normalized. The graph topology follows B-rep incidence (an edge exists only when two faces share an explicit boundary curve), not geometric continuity. Output is a single shape class label for multi-class benchmarks or a binary feature-presence score (AUROC) for the 24-task Features benchmark.

## Method

EquiCAD uses a dual-path encoder per geometric entity: an SO(3)/O(3)-equivariant branch that types inputs by physical parity (coordinates and tangents as polar vectors 1o, normals as axial vectors 1e, trimming masks as scalars 0e), processes them through equivariant linear and tensor-product layers following Clebsch-Gordan decomposition, and pools scalar (0e) outputs to invariant embeddings; and an auxiliary CNN branch that operates on invariantized features (Euclidean norms of non-scalar irrep copies) to capture high-capacity local UV-space patterns. The two invariant embeddings are fused by element-wise summation, then propagated through a 3-layer edge-conditioned message-passing GNN (DGCNN-style) with residual connections. A jumping-knowledge readout aggregates node features across all layers via permutation-invariant pooling to produce the final graph-level embedding, optimized with cross-entropy loss. The key differentiator is the rigorous parity-aware irrep typing that ensures normals transform as pseudo-vectors under reflections, which ablations confirm is critical for maintaining physical consistency and robustness.

## Results

On the three multi-class benchmarks, EquiCAD achieves 97.64% accuracy on SolidLetters, 91.66% on Parts, and 99.87% on Machining Feature, outperforming all 8 baselines on every dataset while using only 1.36M parameters. On the newly introduced Features dataset (16 challenging binary feature-recognition tasks), EquiCAD attains a mean AUROC of 0.710, surpassing the strongest baseline UV-Net (0.613) by nearly 10 points, with particularly large gains on triangular passages (0.998 vs. 0.859) and rectangular pockets (1.000 vs. 0.839). Under rigid transformations on Parts, EquiCAD maintains 100% prediction consistency under both rotations and reflections, whereas DFGAT drops to 37.85% rotational and 21.09% reflectional consistency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EquiCAD outperforms all 8 baselines on all three multi-class benchmarks. Against the strongest point-based method, PointNet++ (97.23% / 88.62% / 91.38%), EquiCAD gains +0.41%, +3.04%, and +8.49% on SolidLetters, Parts, and Machining Feature respectively. Among CAD-oriented GNNs, the closest competitor is DTGBrepGen (95.54% / 90.59% / 98.73%), which EquiCAD surpasses by +2.10%, +1.07%, and +1.14%; notably, DTGBrepGen uses 25× more parameters (34.26M vs. 1.36M). UV-Net, the prior B-rep SOTA, is beaten by +0.68% on SolidLetters, +4.86% on Parts, and +0.13% on Machining Feature. On the fine-grained Features benchmark, EquiCAD leads every one of the 16 reported tasks, with the largest margins over UV-Net on triangular passages (+0.139 AUROC) and rectangular pockets (+0.161 AUROC). The robustness comparison (Table 4) is stark: EquiCAD retains 100% prediction consistency under 60°–300° rotations and reflections, while DFGAT's accuracy collapses from 90.30% to 36.15% (rotations) and 19.34% (reflections), confirming that standard GNNs learn pose-dependent features.

## Relation to Prior Work

EquiCAD sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: B-rep graph neural networks (UV-Net, AAGNet, DFGAT, DTGBrepGen) that model CAD topology but ignore 3D symmetries, and O(3)-equivariant architectures (e3nn, Vector Neurons, Tensor Field Networks) that enforce symmetry but have been applied only to point clouds, voxels, or spherical signals. The paper's key departure is a principled physical-parity typing of B-rep entities—assigning coordinates and tangents as polar vectors (1o) and surface normals as axial vectors (1e)—which, to the authors' knowledge, had not been formalized for CAD boundary representations. The graph aggregation module reuses the MPNN edge-conditioned convolution from DGCNN and the jumping-knowledge readout, so the novelty is concentrated in the equivariant encoding layer and its integration with the B-rep face-edge graph.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that symmetry-aware encoding is not merely a theoretical nicety but yields a dramatic practical advantage: EquiCAD maintains 100% prediction consistency under arbitrary rotations and reflections on Parts, while the strongest non-equivariant baseline (DFGAT) drops to ~38% consistency, a gap that would be unacceptable in manufacturing inspection pipelines. The newly constructed Features benchmark (24 binary tasks from BrepMFR) fills a gap in fine-grained CAD feature-recognition evaluation that prior multi-class benchmarks do not address. Open questions include whether the framework extends to non-manifold or open-shell B-reps, whether the physical-parity typing generalizes to other geometric entities (e.g., curvature tensors), and whether the approach transfers to generative or segmentation tasks on CAD data.
