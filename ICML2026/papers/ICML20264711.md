## Problem

Existing open-vocabulary 3D affordance grounding methods align interaction images or textual instructions with 3D geometry but overlook the intrinsic hierarchical relationship between local object-region attributes and affordances. This causes models to rely on coarse visual similarity and collapse unseen affordances into frequently observed ones, limiting generalization. The paper argues that affordances emerge from specific attribute compositions (e.g., curved shape + appropriate thickness → grasping) rather than global object appearance, and that this attribute-to-affordance hierarchy must be explicitly modeled.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud (2048 points), a 224×224 RGB interaction image, and five textual prompts (object geometric knowledge, affordance intention knowledge, and a counterfactual attribute description) generated by a multimodal LLM from the interaction image. The image is encoded by DINO-ViT-S into 512×14×14 features, the point cloud by PointNet++ into 512×2048 features, and the prompts by RoBERTa into 512-dim vectors. The output is a point-wise 3D affordance heatmap of shape N×1, supervised by Focal and Dice losses against ground-truth point-level annotations.

## Method

The AAH framework first constructs a hypergraph over DINO-ViT image features using α-space hyperedges (distance threshold ε=3) and applies hypergraph convolution with residual connections to capture local region relationships. Counterfactual attribute prompts, generated by an MLLM to perturb factual attribute descriptions, are encoded by RoBERTa and fused with image features via Conv1d layers. All fused image, text, and point-cloud features are then projected into a Lorentz-model hyperbolic space via the exponential map at the origin, where three geometric losses operate: a symmetric positive contrastive loss for cross-modal instance alignment, an attribute-affordance ordering loss using nonsymmetric entailment cones (half-aperture contracting with radius) to enforce the attribute→affordance→3D-region partial order, and a counterfactual exclusion loss that penalizes the true affordance embedding for falling inside the counterfactual attribute's cone. A decoder fuses the hyperbolic-informed features with point-cloud geometry through 1×1 convolutions and Feature Propagation upsampling, producing the final sigmoid affordance heatmap.

## Results

On PIADv2, AAH achieves state-of-the-art performance across all three evaluation settings. In the Seen setting, it reaches 93.29% AUC and 38.98% aIOU, outperforming the strongest baseline GREAT (91.99% AUC, 38.03% aIOU). The advantage is most pronounced in the Unseen Affordance setting, where AAH attains 70.90% AUC and 13.26% aIOU versus GREAT's 69.81% AUC and 12.05% aIOU, with a MAE reduction from 0.127 to 0.113. The method also improves SIM from 0.290 to 0.320 in the Unseen Affordance split, indicating better structural alignment of predicted affordance heatmaps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AAH outperforms all five baselines on every metric across all three PIADv2 splits. Against the strongest competitor GREAT (CVPR 2025), AAH gains +1.30 AUC in Seen, +1.44 AUC in Unseen Object, and +1.09 AUC in Unseen Affordance, with the largest relative improvement in aIOU for Unseen Affordance (+1.21 points, from 12.05 to 13.26). Compared to LASO (CVPR 2024), the gap widens substantially in the Unseen Affordance setting: AAH achieves 70.90% AUC versus LASO's 64.07% (+6.83), reflecting the benefit of explicit hierarchical attribute-affordance modeling over text-point alignment alone. IAG (ICCV 2023) and the cross-modal baselines XMF (NeurIPS 2022) and FRCNN (Remote Sensing 2023) trail further behind, with IAG reaching only 62.29% AUC and FRCNN 59.08% AUC in the Unseen Affordance split. The consistent margin across Seen, Unseen Object, and Unseen Affordance settings indicates that the hyperbolic hierarchical structure and counterfactual exclusion loss contribute most to generalization rather than in-distribution accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the open-vocabulary 3D affordance grounding (OVAG) line initiated by IAG, LASO, and GREAT, which align 2D interaction images or textual instructions with 3D point cloud representations. It departs from these methods by arguing that affordances emerge from specific local attribute compositions rather than global object appearance, and introduces a hypergraph-to-hyperbolic-space pipeline that explicitly encodes the attribute→affordance→region hierarchy. The hyperbolic embedding draws on the broader literature on hyperbolic representation learning (Nickel & Kiela; Ganea et al.; Liu et al.), but applies it here to region-level affordance semantics rather than to general hierarchical classification or graph structure. Counterfactual prompting, borrowed from causal-reasoning ideas in NLP, is repurposed to stress-test which attributes are truly necessary for an affordance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a real generalization bottleneck in OVAG: models trained on seen affordances collapse unseen ones into frequent visual patterns because they never model the attribute-level causal chain. The hierarchical hyperbolic formulation offers a principled geometric mechanism for this, and the consistent (if moderate) gains across Seen/Unseen-Object/Unseen-Affordance splits on PIADv2 support the claim. Key limitations remain: reliance on manually designed counterfactual prompts, extra geometric computation that may hinder scalability to very large point clouds, and sensitivity to prompt quality (though noise-robustness experiments mitigate this concern). Open questions include whether counterfactual conditions can be generated automatically and whether the hyperbolic hierarchy generalizes beyond the single PIADv2 benchmark.
