## Problem

AET-based adversarial attacks (e.g., DRA, SA-AET) improve transferability by interpolating within a simplex of historical adversarial samples, but they rely on finite random sampling to approximate the distribution of effective perturbations, which is unstable and inefficient under limited query budgets. No prior method explicitly models or optimizes the perturbation distribution itself, leaving a gap between the heuristic sampling strategy and the underlying distributional structure that governs gradient aggregation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265573/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a clean RGB image and its paired text caption from Flickr30K or MSCOCO, with the image constrained to an ℓ∞ perturbation ball of radius 8/255 and the text limited to a single-word substitution budget. It produces an adversarial image (generated via 10-step PGD with distributional mixing over the AET simplex) and an adversarial text (generated via BERT-attack conditioned on the final adversarial image). The adversarial pair is then evaluated for transferability across unseen target VLMs (ALBEF, TCL, CLIP variants, BLIP, Qwen3-VL) on both retrieval and captioning tasks.

## Method

DDGA replaces heuristic simplex sampling with a learnable Dirichlet distribution Dir(α) over the AET mixing weights, where α is optimized via policy gradient (REINFORCE) with the cross-entropy loss of the mixed input as reward. A key theoretical result (Theorem 3.2) shows that, to second order, the expected gradient over any perturbation distribution equals the gradient evaluated at the distribution's mean, so optimizing the Dirichlet parameters directly optimizes the effective attack direction without needing to sample and select among candidates. The expected perturbation (Dirichlet mean) drives the primary PGD update with multi-scale augmentation, while the closed-form Dirichlet covariance is exploited to construct gradient-orthogonal perturbations that inject structured diversity into the search. Adversarial text is generated post-hoc via BERT-attack on the final adversarial image, completing the multimodal attack.

## Results

On Flickr30K ITR (CLIP_ViT source), DDGA achieves 41.19% / 53.48% ASR (TR/IR) on ALBEF and 41.83% / 54.02% on TCL, outperforming the strongest baseline SA-AET by +4.59 / +3.04 and +2.63 / +2.92 points respectively. On the larger MSCOCO benchmark, gains are more pronounced: DDGA reaches 66.46% / 72.53% on ALBEF, surpassing SA-AET by +8.82 / +5.65 points. For cross-task transfer to image captioning (BLIP), DDGA reduces CIDEr from 43.90 (clean) to 28.83 on Flickr30K, the largest degradation among all methods. Against the robustly defended TeCoA model, DDGA achieves 75.51% TR R@1, exceeding SA-AET by +6.53 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DDGA consistently outperforms all four baselines across nearly all source–target pairs. Against SGA (Lu et al., ICCV 2023), the improvement is substantial: on Flickr30K with CLIP_ViT source, DDGA gains +18.77 / +18.89 points over SGA on ALBEF (TR/IR), reflecting the advantage of distributional optimization over simple multi-scale gradient aggregation. Against DRA (Gao et al., ECCV 2024), DDGA improves ALBEF TR from 27.84% to 41.19% (+13.35 points), demonstrating that learned Dirichlet mixing is far more effective than uniform random sampling within the AET simplex. Against SA-AET (Jia et al., TPAMI 2025), the closest competitor, DDGA gains +4.59 / +3.04 points on Flickr30K ALBEF and +8.82 / +5.65 points on MSCOCO ALBEF, with the gap widening on the more diverse MSCOCO dataset. Against Co-Attack (Zhang et al., ACM MM 2022), a white-box cross-modal method, DDGA achieves 41.19% vs. 8.55% on ALBEF TR, though this comparison is less meaningful given the white-box vs. black-box setting difference. Notably, DDGA also maintains its advantage against the adversarially trained TeCoA defense (75.51% vs. 68.98% TR R@1), suggesting the learned perturbation distribution produces directions that are harder for robust training to neutralize.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Adversarial Evolution Triangle (AET) line of work initiated by DRA (Gao et al., ECCV 2024) and SA-AET (Jia et al., TPAMI 2025), which generate adversarial perturbations via finite random sampling within a simplex spanned by clean and historical adversarial examples. The key departure is replacing this heuristic sampling with an explicit Dirichlet distribution over the simplex mixing weights, optimized via policy gradient, motivated by a second-order Taylor analysis (Theorem 3.2) showing that gradient aggregation is governed by the mean perturbation rather than individual samples. The method also draws on SGA's (Lu et al., ICCV 2023) augmentation-based gradient aggregation and incorporates BERT-attack for the text modality, while the covariance-guided orthogonal perturbation is a novel exploitation of the Dirichlet's closed-form second-order structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, distribution-aware alternative to the ad-hoc sampling strategies in AET-based attacks, with a clean theoretical justification that the expected perturbation suffices for gradient aggregation to second order. The consistent improvements across multiple VLM architectures, datasets, and tasks (retrieval, captioning, MLLM transfer) suggest the learned perturbation distribution captures more transferable directions than finite sampling. However, the computational overhead of the policy gradient update, the fixed 3-point simplex (M=3), and the still-separate text attack (BERT-attack) limit practical adoption; extending to jointly optimized multimodal perturbations and larger simplices remains open.
