## Problem

Existing AI-generated video detection methods operate exclusively on fully decoded RGB frames, incurring prohibitive computational costs while decoder loop filters (deblocking, interpolation) smooth out the very high-frequency generative artifacts they seek to detect. Critically, these methods discard rich forensic signals—motion vectors and prediction residuals—that are natively available in the compressed bitstream at zero additional cost. No prior work had systematically explored AI-generated video detection in the compressed domain.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266274/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

STREAM consumes raw H.264/MPEG-4 compressed bitstreams without full decoding, extracting three modalities directly: I-frames (decoded only for keyframes), per-macroblock motion vectors, and prediction residuals. The output is a video-level binary probability (AI-generated vs. real) produced by a softmax over a fully connected head. Inference processes 1,000 standardized clips in 16 seconds on a single RTX 4090, compared to 40–188 seconds for pixel-domain baselines, because the sparse compressed representations bypass dense frame-level backbone processing.

## Method

STREAM is a two-stage compressed-domain framework. Stage 1 (Motion-Guided Feature Fusion) encodes I-frames and residuals with independent lightweight CNNs (ResNet-152 and ResNet-18 respectively), uses motion vectors to perform differentiable warping that spatially aligns I-frame features to each temporal step, and applies a pixel-level sigmoid gate to adaptively blend residual artifacts with aligned texture context. Stage 2 (Temporal Inconsistency Mining) pools each fused frame feature via global average pooling into a temporal sequence, processes it through a Multi-Scale Temporal Convolutional Network with progressively dilated 1D convolutions to capture both short-term jitter and long-range incoherence, then aggregates via temporal average pooling and a fully connected layer for binary classification. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with binary cross-entropy loss, and the key insight is that compressed-domain residuals preserve high-frequency generative artifacts that are irreversibly attenuated by decoder loop filters, while motion vectors natively encode non-physical temporal irregularities without the cost of optical flow estimation.

## Results

STREAM achieves a mean mAP of 0.965 across five benchmarks (VidProM, GenVideo, GVF, VideoPhy, AIGVDet-D), surpassing the second-best baseline D3 (0.943) by 0.022. Per-dataset mAP ranges from 0.924 (GVF) to 0.999 (VidProM). Inference speed is 16 s per 1,000 videos, a 2.5× speedup over D3 (40 s) and an 11.75× speedup over the slowest baseline NPR (188 s), while simultaneously delivering the highest accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STREAM outperforms all six pixel-domain baselines on every dataset. Against the strongest competitor D3 (ICCV'25), STREAM gains +0.023 mAP on VidProM (0.999 vs 0.976), +0.017 on GenVideo, +0.029 on GVF (0.924 vs 0.895), +0.016 on VideoPhy, and +0.026 on AIGVDet-D, while running 2.5× faster (16 s vs 40 s per 1,000 videos). Against AIGVDet (PRCV'24), the gap is +0.046 average mAP with a 4.6× speed advantage. DeMamba, NPR, STIL, and TALL trail by 0.08–0.29 mAP and are 5–12× slower. In the appendix comparison with NSG-VD (NeurIPS'25), STREAM achieves 0.9204 average accuracy vs 0.9146 while running ~9× faster (39 s vs 360 s per 1,000 videos). No baseline matches or exceeds STREAM on any reported metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant pixel-domain paradigm for AI-generated video detection (D3, DeMamba, NPR, AIGVDet) by operating directly on native MPEG-4/H.264 compressed bitstreams, a domain previously explored only for semantic video understanding tasks (CoViAR, CVOS, MMNet). It repurposes the 'free-to-use' compressed-domain representations—motion vectors, prediction residuals, and I-frames—that prior video coding literature used for efficient feature propagation, but redirects them toward forensic artifact mining rather than semantic recognition. The network architecture itself (ResNet encoders + MS-TCN) is standard; the novelty lies entirely in the input representation and the motion-guided alignment/gated fusion mechanism designed to handle the heterogeneous, spatially misaligned nature of compressed-domain features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper opens a genuinely new research direction for AIGV detection by showing that the compressed bitstream preserves forensic signals (high-frequency residuals, non-physical motion patterns) that are irreversibly attenuated by decoder loop filters, and that exploiting these signals yields both higher accuracy and 2.5× faster inference than the best pixel-domain baselines. Key open questions include: whether the approach extends to newer codecs (HEVC, AV1) with different macroblock structures, how it handles manually spliced or edited videos that break motion-vector continuity, and whether the sensitivity to occlusion (50% watermark causes −13.2% mAP) can be mitigated. For practitioners deploying real-time deepfake video screening, the efficiency gain is practically significant, though the current H.264/MPEG-4-only support limits immediate adoption in diverse streaming ecosystems.
