## Problem

Sampling from unnormalized multimodal distributions with limited density evaluations remains a fundamental challenge. Parallel Tempering provides robust global exploration but degrades when reference-target overlap is poor, while diffusion-based samplers offer continuous transport at the cost of expensive neural network training. The paper asks whether these two paradigms can be combined into a single training-free framework that achieves a superior trade-off between sample quality and density evaluation cost.

## Input / Output

The method requires only the unnormalized target density π̃(x) and its gradient, plus a tractable reference distribution (e.g., a Gaussian); no pre-existing target samples or trained networks are needed. The score of the conditional intermediate distribution is computed in closed form via the change-of-variables formula, so each SDE step costs one target density evaluation and one gradient evaluation. Computational cost is governed by hyperparameters: the number of PT steps K (Stage 1), SDE integration steps N, and MCMC corrector steps M per integration step (Stage 2).

## Method

CDS introduces Conditional Interpolants, a class of stochastic processes obtained by conditioning the interpolant map on a fixed reference point z, which yields a conditional probability path from a Dirac mass at z to the target. This conditioning produces a closed-form SDE whose drift depends only on the target score (computed analytically via change of variables) and the interpolant velocity field, eliminating any neural approximation. The two-stage procedure first uses Parallel Tempering to sample from the conditional initialization distribution ν_{t₀|z} for small t₀, where the distribution concentrates near z and thus overlaps well with the reference; it then transports samples to the target by integrating the closed-form SDE from t₀ to 1. A key theoretical result (Proposition 3.6, Corollary D.6) shows that the Wasserstein-1 sampling error in Stage 1 scales linearly with t₀ for the linear interpolant, so the initialization cost vanishes as t₀→0. The SDE integration provides continuous score-guided refinement that corrects Stage-1 approximation errors, empirically outperforming a simple inverse-interpolation-map baseline.

## Results

Across 8 target distributions spanning 4 tasks (Gaussian Mixture, Lennard-Jones, Alanine Dipeptide, Bayesian Neural Network), CDS achieves a mean Hypervolume Ratio (HVR) of 0.9863, outperforming the strongest competitor NRPT (0.9827) and all other baselines. On the highest-dimensional BNN task (D=550), CDS significantly outperforms all samplers. On the ALDP molecular dynamics benchmark (D=66), only CDS and NRPT successfully recover both conformational modes under a fixed budget of 2×10⁵ density evaluations, while MALA and HMC degrade severely.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CDS outperforms all five main baselines in aggregate HVR, with the closest competitor being NRPT (0.9827 vs. 0.9863). On the Gaussian Mixture tasks, CDS and DiGS both outperform all methods in low dimensions (D=2), but DiGS deteriorates sharply in 16D due to its Metropolis-within-Gibbs mechanism, while CDS maintains its efficiency. On Lennard-Jones, local samplers (MALA, HMC) are competitive since the target is less multimodal, and CDS performs on par with NRPT, surpassing it only in the high-density-evaluation budget regime. On Alanine Dipeptide, CDS clearly separates from MALA and HMC (which degrade severely) and rivals NRPT for the best results, followed by OASMC and DiGS. On the 550-dimensional BNN posterior, CDS significantly outperforms all competing samplers, demonstrating superior exploration of complex multimodal posteriors. Additional comparisons against NUTS, SVGD, and MAMS in the appendix confirm CDS's consistent advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate paradigms for sampling from unnormalized distributions: annealing-based MCMC (specifically Non-Reversible Parallel Tempering, Syed et al. 2022) and the Stochastic Interpolant framework (Albergo et al. 2025; Song et al. 2021; Lipman et al. 2023). The key conceptual departure is the introduction of Conditional Interpolants, which condition on a reference point z to obtain a conditional probability path whose score admits a closed-form expression via the change-of-variables formula, thereby eliminating the need for neural network training that plagues existing diffusion-based samplers (Neural Diffusion Samplers, RDMC, DiGS). Unlike prior non-amortized diffusion methods (DiGS, RDMC) that follow marginal probability paths with intractable scores, CDS follows a conditional path where the drift is exactly computable, and unlike standard PT that directly targets the full target, CDS uses PT only to sample a near-reference initialization distribution before transporting via the closed-form SDE.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CDS offers a training-free alternative to neural diffusion samplers that achieves competitive or superior sample quality with fewer density evaluations, which is critical in settings where each evaluation is expensive (molecular force fields, large neural networks). The theoretical guarantee that initialization cost vanishes as t₀→0 provides a principled basis for the two-stage design. However, the paper identifies two open limitations: the linear interpolant causes numerical instabilities for densities with singularities (LJ, ALDP), and the choice of t₀ involves a delicate trade-off between reference overlap and replica degeneracy. A promising direction highlighted by the authors is designing geometry-aware interpolants that adapt to the target's structure, which could resolve both limitations without abandoning the CDS framework.
