## Problem

Text diffusion models (TDMs) offer faster, more flexible generation than autoregressive LLMs, but their safety remains largely unaddressed. Existing safety techniques—post-hoc filtering, rejection sampling, decoding constraints—are designed around autoregressive decoding and do not translate to the iterative, parallel denoising process of TDMs. TDM-specific jailbreak attacks (DIJA, PAD) exploit the parallel denoising mechanism to achieve near-100% attack success rates, underscoring the need for defence mechanisms native to the diffusion paradigm rather than repurposed from AR models.

## Input / Output

Given a trained text diffusion model (MDLM or LLaDA), a conditioning prompt that can sit at any arbitrary position in the sequence (not just the prefix), and a finite negation set of N≈500–1000 unsafe reference sequences, SAD produces text steered away from unsafe regions during generation. Prompt tokens are clamped and never masked; SAD acts only on continuation tokens. The negation set is prompt-agnostic—evaluated with an empty prompt—so a single set works universally across all test prompts, including adversarial jailbreak prompts whose distribution cannot be anticipated.

## Method

SAD adapts the safe denoiser identity (Theorem 2.1) from continuous image diffusion to discrete masked text diffusion. At each reverse denoising step within a critical early timestep window C, the safe denoiser is computed as: data denoiser + β*(x_t)·(data denoiser − unsafe denoiser), where the unsafe denoiser is approximated by a Monte Carlo weighted average over the negation set using the categorical forward kernel (reducing to counting matching and masked positions in O(L) per reference), and β* is estimated from the same kernel scaled by a hyperparameter η. The correction is applied in log-probability space to preserve valid categorical distributions, and a relaxed kernel (placing small mass on token mismatches) prevents the reference weights from zeroing out within a few reverse steps. The entire procedure is training-free, requiring no modification to the underlying diffusion model, with the primary per-step cost of O(NL) being small relative to the base model's forward pass.

## Results

On hazardous generation (Table 1), SAD reduces the Llama-Guard unsafe rate from 38.4% to 32.6% (−5.8 pp) on MDLM and from 19.8% to 14.8% (−5.0 pp) on LLaDA-8B-Base, with BERTScore on benign prompts essentially unchanged (Δ ≤ 0.001). Under the PAD jailbreak attack (Table 2), SAD cuts HarmBench ASR from 43.6% to 29.0% on LLaDA-1.5 (−14.6 pp) and from 45.2% to 28.6% when composed with DiffuGuard (−16.6 pp). On memorization, increasing η to 128 reduces 10-gram fuzzy overlap below the 18.59% baseline while BERTScore drops only from 0.515 to 0.511. Throughput overhead is modest: LLaDA throughput drops from 0.382 to 0.313 seq/s with a 64-step active window, and scaling the negation set from 100 to 5000 references changes throughput by ≤ 2.3%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FK Steering (Singhal et al., 2025), SAD matches the unsafe rate (≈32% vs. ≈33% on MDLM) at roughly 29× lower wall-clock time and lower perplexity (34.6 vs. 42.6), making it far more practical for deployment. Against PPL filtering (Alon & Kamfonas, 2023), PPL already suppresses zero-shot ASR to near-zero, leaving little headroom; however, under the PAD attack where PPL only reduces ASR from 43.6% to 39.8% (LLaDA-1.5), SAD adds a further 9.2 pp reduction to reach 30.6%. Against Self-reminder (Xie et al., 2023), the pattern is similar: self-reminder is strong on zero-shot but weak on diffusion-native attacks, and SAD contributes modest additional gains (e.g., −3.8 pp under PAD on LLaDA-1.5). Against DiffuGuard (Li et al., 2026), the most directly comparable diffusion-time defence, SAD is complementary rather than redundant: under PAD, DiffuGuard alone achieves 45.2% ASR on LLaDA-1.5, while DiffuGuard+SAD reaches 28.6% (−16.6 pp additional), and under DIJA on Dream-Instruct the combination drops ASR from 6.4% to 3.1%. Overall, SAD wins or ties on every diffusion-native attack setting and is most impactful when the attack exploits early trajectory commitment during parallel denoising (PAD), while offering diminishing but still positive returns when other defences already drive ASR near zero.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the training-free safe denoiser of Kim et al. (2025), originally derived for continuous image diffusion, to the discrete masked-token setting of text diffusion models (MDLM, LLaDA). The key technical contribution is the categorical kernel factorization (Eq. 7) that replaces the Gaussian forward kernel, enabling the exact safe-denoiser identity (Thm. 2.1) to be instantiated with a finite negation set of unsafe text sequences. Unlike concurrent inference-time steering methods for masked diffusion LMs (Lee & Han 2026, An & Han 2026) that rely on contrastive directions or per-token attribute scores, SAD specifies the steering signal as a corpus of sequences to avoid, inheriting the form of the exact safe-denoiser identity rather than a learned scorer. It also departs from heuristic diffusion-time defences like DiffuGuard (Li et al., 2026) by being derived from a provable identity, and from particle-based methods like FK Steering (Singhal et al., 2025) by operating on a single trajectory with O(NL) overhead rather than k parallel trajectories.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As text diffusion models (LLaDA, Dream, etc.) gain traction as alternatives to AR LLMs, the absence of principled, training-free safety mechanisms is a critical deployment gap; SAD fills this by showing that a small negation set (N≈500–1000) steered through the denoising trajectory can reduce hazardous generation by 5–16 points and substantially improve jailbreak robustness (especially against PAD) without retraining. The method is composable with existing defences (DiffuGuard, PPL filtering, self-reminders), making it a practical drop-in safeguard. Key limitations remain: SAD is an empirical approximation rather than a safety certificate, its effectiveness depends on negation-set coverage of the target unsafe modes, and the dramatic refusal-rate shifts observed (e.g., 87%→7% on LLaDA-Instruct) raise open questions about whether the model is genuinely producing safer content or merely changing output style in ways that evade classifiers. Future work on two-sided guidance, held-out reference construction, and human-validated evaluation would strengthen the safety guarantees.
