## Problem

Causal machine learning methods are typically evaluated on a small number of hand-crafted or semi-synthetic datasets, leading to brittle, non-generalizable conclusions that are sensitive to hidden modeling assumptions. There is no principled, transparent, and diverse synthetic benchmark generator that systematically covers all three levels of the Pearl Causal Hierarchy (observation, intervention, counterfactual) with explicit, controllable assumptions and coverage guarantees.

## Input / Output

The generator takes a Space of Interest (SoI) specification—a declarative tuple defining classes of SCMs (graph size, edge density, hidden variable proportion, mechanism family, noise distribution), queries (type, count, specific values), and data (sample size)—along with a random seed. It produces a complete causal dataset: a structural causal model with its latent-projected causal graph, an observational dataset of forward-sampled data points, a causal query (ATE, CATE, or Ctf-TE) with realizable variable values drawn from a large auxiliary sample, and the analytically computed ground-truth query value. The SoI can be configured to enforce or deliberately violate identification assumptions, enabling both in-regime accuracy testing and controlled robustness evaluation.

## Method

CausalProfiler samples causal datasets through a three-stage pipeline: (1) it generates a random DAG with a target expected degree, designates a proportion of nodes as hidden, applies Verma's latent projection to expose only the observed structure, and assigns causal mechanisms—either regional discrete mechanisms (tabular mappings from parent configurations to outputs, conditioned on R noise regions of the exogenous space, enabling controllable stochasticity and non-linearity) or parametric continuous families (linear, neural networks); (2) it forward-samples observational data in topological order and samples queries with realizable values from a large auxiliary dataset; (3) it computes ground-truth query values via do-operations for interventional queries and the three-step abduction-action-prediction procedure for counterfactual queries. A key theoretical contribution is a coverage guarantee (Proposition 5.1) showing that with sufficiently expressive regional discrete mechanisms, every causal dataset in the SoI has strictly positive probability of being generated, ensuring L3-expressivity. The framework also includes an assumption-analysis module to quantify emergent properties (linearity, monotonicity, etc.) and an analysis of the induced sampling distribution to support bias-aware interpretation of results.

## Results

On the ATE estimation experiments (Table 1), method rankings shift dramatically across SoIs: DCM achieves the lowest mean error on Linear-Medium (0.1530) but degrades to the worst performer on NN-Medium (0.0276), while VACA reverses to the best on NN-Medium (0.0090) and NN-Large (0.0090). Under low-data conditions (50 samples), NCM is most stable (mean error 0.0097) while DCM's error nearly triples (0.0267 → 0.0777). On discrete counterfactual experiments (Table 2), DCM achieves lower mean error than CausalNF across all three settings (e.g., 0.1974 vs. 0.5233 on D2-Reject), but CausalNF is 15–21× faster and avoids the worst-case errors (max 1.0 vs. 2.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DCM (Chao et al., 2024) achieves the lowest mean ATE error on Linear-Medium (0.1530) but becomes the worst method on NN-Medium (0.0276 vs. VACA's 0.0090) and degrades sharply under low data (0.0777 vs. NCM's 0.0097), revealing sensitivity to mechanism functional form and sample size. VACA (Sánchez-Martin et al., 2022) is the best performer on NN-Medium and NN-Large (mean error 0.0090) with the lowest max error, but suffers a 53.4% failure rate on Linear-Medium due to numerical instabilities. NCM (Xia et al., 2023) is the most robust under data scarcity (0.0097 on NN-Large-LowData) and maintains stable performance across all SoIs, though it is never the outright best in any single setting. CausalNF (Javaloy et al., 2023) is competitive but never the top performer; on discrete counterfactual tasks it is 15–21× faster than DCM and avoids the worst-case errors (max 1.0 vs. 2.0), making it attractive when runtime and tail risk matter more than average accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

CausalProfiler unifies and extends several partially-overlapping lines of work: causal discovery benchmark generators (Kalainathan et al., 2020; Gupta et al., 2023; Rudolph et al., 2023) that sample SCMs but lack ground truth for interventional/counterfactual queries; query estimation frameworks (Sharma & Kiciman, 2020; Textor et al., 2017) that require manual SCM specification; and the regional discrete mechanism concept inspired by Regional Canonical Models (Xia et al., 2023) and canonical SCMs (Zhang et al., 2022). The key departure is integrating random SCM sampling, automatic query ground-truth computation via do-calculus and the three-step counterfactual procedure, and a formal coverage guarantee (Proposition 5.1) into a single framework parameterized by a Space of Interest, rather than relying on hand-crafted or semi-synthetic datasets like ACIC2016 or Syntren.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This addresses a widely acknowledged gap in Causal ML: the absence of a principled, transparent, and diverse synthetic evaluation paradigm that covers all three levels of the Pearl Causal Hierarchy. The experiments demonstrate that method rankings can reverse entirely depending on the SoI (e.g., DCM best on linear SCMs but worst on small-NN SCMs; VACA's failure rate drops from 53% to 5%), underscoring that conclusions drawn from a handful of fixed datasets are unreliable. Key limitations include the non-uniformity of the induced dataset distribution (the coverage guarantee ensures positive probability but not uniformity), the restriction to tabular data, and the inability to replace real-world validation. Open directions include stratified sampling to reduce distributional bias, automated SoI exploration, and bridging the simulation-to-real gap via data-driven SoI specification.
