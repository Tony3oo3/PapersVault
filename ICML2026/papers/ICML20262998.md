## Problem

Existing adapter-based PEFT methods for vision models suffer from two unresolved limitations on complex dense prediction tasks: (1) input-agnostic low-rank modeling, where fixed adapter weights cannot adapt to input variations, weakening spatial dependency capture; and (2) feature redundancy, where isolated per-layer adapters produce highly similar representations across network layers. These gaps limit PEFT methods' ability to match full fine-tuning performance on tasks like object detection, instance segmentation, and panoptic segmentation.

## Input / Output

ParaX operates on standard RGB images fed into frozen pre-trained hierarchical backbones (Swin, ConvNeXt, ViT, VMamba) at task-specific resolutions (e.g., 1280×800 for COCO detection, 224×224 for classification). Outputs are task-dependent: per-pixel class maps (UperNet), bounding boxes plus instance masks (Mask R-CNN), panoptic segmentation (Panoptic FPN), or pose keypoints (HRFormer head). The method requires fewer than 4–8% of the trainable parameters of full fine-tuning while the backbone weights remain frozen throughout training.

## Method

ParaX constructs a large shared expert center per network stage containing a bank of trainable parameter matrices (pairs for channel down/up-projections, triplets for multi-kernel depthwise convolutions). A lightweight router (global average pooling → 16-channel bottleneck → two parallel linear layers with softmax) generates input-dependent gating vectors that dynamically aggregate parameter matrices from the shared center to form the effective adapter weights for each module. The critical architectural choice is sharing the same expert center across all layers within a stage: because each expert's gradient accumulates contributions from every layer that routes to it, updating one expert immediately modulates weight matrices in all other layers, establishing implicit cross-layer interaction without explicit feature connections. For spatial modeling, ParaX applies sequentially stacked dynamic depthwise convolutions (kernel sizes 3, 5, 7) with residual connections and a spatially-varying aggregation module that recalibrates multi-scale features via 1×1 convolution and softmax attention maps.

## Results

On ADE20K semantic segmentation with Swin-L, ParaX achieves 52.0% mIoU, surpassing full fine-tuning (51.2%) by 0.8% while using only 7.3M trainable parameters (<4% of the 195M in full fine-tuning). On COCO2017 object detection with ConvNeXt-L, ParaX reaches 49.5% AP^b / 44.8% AP^m, exceeding full fine-tuning by 1.4%/1.6% and Mona by 0.6%/0.4%. On panoptic segmentation (COCO2017, Swin-B), ParaX attains 48.8% PQ, improving over AdaptFormer by 1.7% and Mona by 0.7%. For image classification, ParaX achieves 87.4% mean top-1 accuracy across CIFAR-100, SVHN, Food-101, and ImageNet-R, outperforming Mona (86.7%) and full fine-tuning (84.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ParaX consistently outperforms all 12 baselines across every evaluated task. Against the strongest adapter baseline Mona, ParaX gains 0.4–0.5% mIoU on ADE20K (Swin-B/L), 0.6%/0.4% AP^b/AP^m on COCO2017 (ConvNeXt-L), 0.7% PQ on panoptic segmentation (Swin-B), and 1.6% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-R. Against full fine-tuning, ParaX achieves comparable or superior performance with <4% of the parameters: it beats full fine-tuning by 0.8% mIoU on ADE20K (Swin-L) and by 1.4%/1.6% AP^b/AP^m on COCO2017 (ConvNeXt-L). Compared to LoRA, ParaX improves by 7.2% AP^b on COCO2017 (Swin-B) and 2.2% mIoU on ADE20K (Swin-B). Against AdaptFormer, ParaX gains 1.7% PQ on panoptic segmentation (Swin-B) and 3.4% AP^b on COCO2017 (Swin-B). The margin over VPT is the largest (e.g., 6.7% AP^b on COCO2017 Swin-B), reflecting VPT's input-agnostic prompt design. On the domain-shifted ImageNet-R benchmark, ParaX's advantage over Mona widens to 1.6%, suggesting the dynamic routing mechanism is particularly effective under distribution shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

ParaX extends the adapter-based PEFT lineage (LoRA, AdaptFormer, Mona) by replacing each layer's fixed low-rank parameter matrices with a shared expert center of trainable matrices that are dynamically combined via a lightweight router. This borrows the gating concept from mixture-of-experts architectures but applies it to parameter matrices rather than sub-network experts, and crucially shares the expert pool across multiple network layers to enable implicit cross-layer interaction. The paper explicitly departs from prior MoE-style PEFT methods (LoRand, MLAE) by introducing input-dependent routing and cross-layer parameter sharing, addressing what it identifies as two key deficiencies: input-agnostic modeling and redundant cross-layer representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ParaX demonstrates that a simple shared-expert-center design with dynamic routing can match or exceed full fine-tuning on dense prediction tasks (segmentation, detection, panoptic segmentation) while using fewer than 4% of trainable parameters, offering a practical path to multi-task deployment with minimal storage overhead. The implicit cross-layer interaction mechanism—where updating one expert immediately affects all layers that reference it—provides a theoretically grounded explanation for the observed feature diversity gains. Limitations include the inability to merge adapters into the backbone at inference, a ~15% training latency increase from multi-kernel convolutions, and lack of evaluation on very large models (e.g., DINOv3-ViT-7B). Open directions include extending the approach to LLMs and multimodal LLMs, exploring sparse expert activation, and reducing the remaining gap to full fine-tuning on the most complex tasks like panoptic segmentation.
