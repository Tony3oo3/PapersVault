## Problem

Recovering surface normals from single-view polarization images in complex media such as ice is severely degraded by internal light transport effects (birefringence, multiple scattering, anisotropic propagation) that violate the ideal Fresnel reflection assumptions underlying both classical and existing deep-learning SfP methods. No prior work has systematically addressed this problem for ice, and no real-world polarization dataset for ice objects existed to benchmark learning-based approaches.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single-view 4-channel polarization image (analyzer angles 0°, 45°, 90°, 135°; native 2448×2048, resized to 512×512 for training) and produces a 3-channel surface normal map in the camera coordinate system. Beyond the raw polarization input, the network also consumes three physics-based candidate normal maps derived from the Fresnel model (accounting for diffuse/specular ambiguity) and a pixel-wise structure-aware consistency prior computed from AoLP autocorrelation and wavelet high-frequency energy, all fused within the dual-branch architecture.

## Method

IceSfP is a dual-branch CNN with an EPSANet50 backbone: one branch encodes raw polarization features while the other encodes physics-based normal priors concatenated with a structure-aware consistency prior. The consistency prior is constructed by computing a DoLP-weighted double-angle AoLP autocorrelation over 3×3 neighborhoods, extracting a direction-dependent correlation decay scale along four principal axes, and combining it with a two-level Haar SWT high-frequency energy measure to yield a pixel-wise reliability map. At the deepest feature scale, a Cross-modal Reliability Attention (CRA) module uses cross-attention to selectively weight reliable physics priors against raw polarization features, while Multi-scale Feature Fusion modules at intermediate resolutions align and merge both branches via skip connections. A SPADE module in the decoder re-injects local spatial details from the raw polarization input. Training is supervised end-to-end with a cosine similarity loss plus an AoLP loss weighted by the consistency prior (λ=0.05), using AdamW with cosine annealing over 150 epochs on 8× A100 GPUs.

## Results

On the newly introduced IceSfP dataset, the proposed IceSfP method achieves a mean angular error (MAE) of 16.01°, a median angular error (MedAE) of 13.93°, and angular accuracies of 41.92% (<11.25°), 79.58% (<22.5°), and 89.21% (<30°). This represents a 2.74° MAE improvement over the second-best method (TransSfP at 18.75°). On the external DeepSfP and TransSfP benchmarks, the method also achieves the best MAE (16.33° and 15.59° respectively), outperforming the next-best baselines by 1.17° and 0.46°.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed IceSfP method outperforms all five baselines across every metric on the IceSfP dataset. Against the closest competitor TransSfP (18.75° MAE), IceSfP improves by 2.74°, with the gap widening to 3.58° over Attention U²Net (19.59°) and 4.12° over SPW (20.13°). DeepSfP (18.76°) is nearly tied with TransSfP but still trails IceSfP by 2.75°; the paper attributes this to DeepSfP's direct feature-level fusion of unreliable physics priors in complex media. The physics-based method of Mahmoud et al. (62.32° MAE) is catastrophically outperformed, confirming that analytic Fresnel inversion breaks down under ice's internal scattering and birefringence. On the external DeepSfP benchmark, IceSfP leads SPW (17.50°) by 1.17°, and on TransSfP it edges out Attention U²Net (16.05°) by 0.46°, demonstrating generalization beyond ice-specific conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the deep-learning-based Shape from Polarization (SfP) line initiated by Ba et al. (DeepSfP, ECCV 2020) and subsequent works targeting transparent objects (TransSfP), uncontrolled scenes (SPW), and underwater scattering (Attention U²Net) to a new and more challenging medium: ice, where volumetric scattering, birefringence, and multipath propagation severely violate the surface-reflection assumptions underlying all prior SfP methods. Rather than simply applying an existing network to a new domain, the authors introduce a structure-aware polarization consistency prior built from AoLP autocorrelation and stationary wavelet transform, which is then integrated into a dual-branch architecture via a Cross-modal Reliability Attention (CRA) module that adaptively gates physics-based normal priors. The paper also contributes the first real-world ice SfP dataset, filling a gap that no prior benchmark addressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work establishes the first systematic benchmark and method for single-view SfP in ice, a practically important medium for environmental monitoring, road icing detection, and polar research where existing active or passive 3D imaging methods fail. The consistency-prior-plus-CRA paradigm offers a generalizable recipe for integrating unreliable physics-based cues into learning-based pipelines, potentially applicable to other translucent or highly scattering media. However, the method is validated only on ice under controlled indoor lighting, does not explicitly model uncertainty in the Fresnel inversion, and its generalization to other complex media or uncontrolled outdoor conditions remains untested, leaving these as important open questions.
