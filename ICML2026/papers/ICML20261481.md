## Problem

Existing skill discovery and hierarchical structure learning methods in reinforcement learning rely on action labels, reward signals, or handcrafted annotations, limiting their applicability to abundant unlabelled video data. Moreover, most prior approaches produce flat segmentations rather than deep, compositional hierarchies that capture how low-level behaviours compose into higher-level subroutines. This paper asks whether both skill segmentation and multi-level hierarchical structure can be recovered purely from observational trajectories without any supervisory signal.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabelled observation trajectories as raw RGB frames (274×274×3 top-down in Craftax; 640×360×3 first-person in Minecraft), reduced to feature vectors via PCA (650 components) or MineCLIP (512-dim embeddings), along with a single hyperparameter K specifying the maximum number of skills. It produces per-frame discrete skill indices, a context-free grammar whose terminals are atomic skills and whose non-terminals are discovered subroutines, and a hierarchical task decomposition tree per episode. Notably, the grammar is induced over a concatenated corpus of all episodes separated by boundary tokens, enabling cross-episode subroutine reuse, and the entire discovery pipeline requires no action, reward, or interaction data.

## Method

HiSD is a two-stage pipeline that decouples structure discovery from policy execution. Stage 1 (segmentation) applies ASOT, a temporally consistent unbalanced optimal transport formulation with a Gromov-Wasserstein temporal regularity term that enforces local label smoothness over a configurable radius, yielding discrete skill indices per frame via argmax over the transport plan. Stage 2 (structure induction) collapses contiguous same-label frames into atomic terminal symbols (removing duration variance), concatenates all episode sequences with a unique boundary token, and runs a modified Sequitur algorithm that forbids the boundary token from appearing in any production rule, thereby preventing cross-episode merging and ensuring learned non-terminals capture intra-episode subroutines. The resulting grammar's derivation tree provides the hierarchical decomposition, with internal nodes representing reusable composite skills. For downstream RL deployment, each terminal skill is grounded as a temporal option with a BC-trained policy and PU-learned initiation/termination conditions, while non-terminal nodes execute child options sequentially; a high-level Maskable PPO agent selects among valid options using the learned initiation sets as action masks.

## Results

On the six Craftax and Minecraft tasks, HiSD achieves the highest average mIoU on 5 of 6 tasks, reaching 66% on Stone Pickaxe Static (vs. 30% OMPN, 45% CompILE) and 38% on Minecraft Mapped (vs. 14% OMPN, 6% CompILE). On the simplest task (WSWS Random), both baselines slightly outperform HiSD (75–76% vs. 63% Avg. mIoU). On hierarchy quality, HiSD produces dramatically more consistent decompositions: 9 unique trees vs. 499 for OMPN on WSWS Random, and 47 vs. 500 on Stone Pickaxe Random, while also yielding smaller, more interpretable trees (e.g., 12.84 avg. size vs. 15.54 for OMPN).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HiSD is compared against CompILE (Kipf et al., 2019) and OMPN (Lu et al., 2021), both of which require action labels, ground-truth sub-task orderings, and additional structural priors (segment counts or hierarchy depth), whereas HiSD requires only the maximum skill count K. On skill segmentation (Avg. mIoU), HiSD outperforms both baselines on 5 of 6 tasks, with the largest margins on Minecraft: 31% vs. 14% (OMPN) and 6% (CompILE) on the All task, and 38% vs. 14% and 6% on Mapped. HiSD loses only on the simplest Craftax task (WSWS Random, 2 skills), where both baselines reach ~75–76% mIoU versus HiSD's 63%. On hierarchy quality, HiSD produces far more consistent and compact trees: 9 unique trees matching ground truth on WSWS Random versus 499 for OMPN, and 47 vs. 500 on Stone Pickaxe Random, while OMPN's trees are larger (15.54 vs. 12.84 avg. size) with higher branching factors (5.10 vs. 2.03 max). In downstream RL, the HiSD hierarchy reaches near-optimal reward (~0.94) by N=250 action-labelled episodes, outperforming both the OMPN hierarchy (which fails to converge due to excessive branching) and the flat CompILE skills-only agent (~0.7 reward).

## Relation to Prior Work

HiSD combines two established components—ASOT (Xu & Gould, CVPR 2024) for temporally consistent unsupervised action segmentation via unbalanced optimal transport, and Sequitur (Nevill-Manning & Witten, 1997) for linear-time grammar induction—into a unified pipeline that had not previously been applied to skill hierarchy discovery. The key conceptual departure from prior skill-discovery work (CompILE, OMPN, NPBRS) and hierarchy-learning work (HTN-Maker, Circuit-HTN, Clique-Chain HTN) is the removal of all action labels, reward signals, and online interaction: HiSD operates purely on pre-extracted visual features from unlabelled trajectories. This positions the paper at the intersection of temporal action segmentation and symbolic structure induction, bridging continuous perceptual processing with discrete compositional planning in a way that prior methods, which typically produce flat segmentations or require structured symbolic input, do not.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for action supervision and manual annotation during structure discovery, HiSD opens a path toward scaling skill and hierarchy learning to the abundant unlabelled video data available in domains like Minecraft, where collecting action-labelled demonstrations is expensive. The demonstrated utility in downstream RL (hierarchy agents outperforming flat-skill and primitive-action baselines) validates that the discovered structure is not merely descriptive but functionally useful for credit assignment. Key open questions remain: the deterministic Sequitur cannot absorb segmentation noise (producing 500 unique trees in Minecraft 'All'), the method requires a fixed skill budget K, and the single-label-per-timestep assumption limits applicability to concurrent behaviours. Probabilistic grammars (PCFGs, fragment grammars) and end-to-end representation learning are natural next steps.
