## Problem

While function vectors (FVs) are established as causal task representations in in-context learning, the specific mechanism by which individual few-shot examples compose into a single FV, and how cross-example contextualization modulates this composition, remained unclear. Prior circuit-level work identified a two-stage contextualize-then-aggregate process but left the aggregation details and the causal role of contextualization underspecified, making it unknown how the FV is actually built from per-example signals.

## Input / Output

The method takes n-shot ICL prompts (n=3/5/10) on discrete mapping tasks—both normal (deterministic rule) and ambiguous (multiple compatible mappings) variants—and processes them through pretrained LLMs (Gemma-2 2B/9B/27B, Llama-3.2 1B/3B, Llama-3.1 8B). It produces per-example sub-FV decomposition weights, attention reweighting profiles comparing contextualized versus uncontextualized computation, and a Shapley-value attribution of contextualization gains to Query-Key routing versus Value updates. FV quality is quantified via injection accuracy: the per-prompt FV is injected into 0-shot prompts at various layers and scaling factors, and the maximum accuracy across the sweep serves as the scalar quality score.

## Method

The core approach combines three complementary intervention techniques. First, per-example sub-FVs are extracted by masking attention so the last token attends only to a single example, then a global OLS regression with weights shared across a batch of prompts reconstructs the full FV, establishing that n-shot FVs are near-linear superpositions of example-level contributions. Second, an attention edge ablation creates an 'uncontextualized' model that zeros all cross-component attention while preserving intra-example edges and aggregation edges into the last token, providing a clean counterfactual to isolate contextualization's causal contribution. Third, a 2×2 factorial intervention independently toggles contextualization in the Query-Key and Value pathways of FV heads, with a symmetric Shapley decomposition attributing the overall contextualization gain to each pathway. The paper introduces ambiguous prompt datasets (where some examples are compatible with multiple candidate mappings) as stress tests to expose content-dependent attention shifts beyond recency bias, and uses symmetric Key patching and Q patching with token-replacement corruption to identify whether examples or the ICL query drive the QK alignment that routes attention toward informative demonstrations.

## Results

The n-shot function vector (FV) is well-approximated by a linear superposition of per-example sub-FVs, achieving mean cosine similarity ≥ 0.925 and mean R² ≥ 0.875 in both contextualized and uncontextualized settings across all models and tasks. The OLS-reconstructed FV recovers most of the full FV's causal effect, with injection accuracy ratios (OLS/Full-FV) typically between 0.88 and 1.12. In the Shapley decomposition of contextualization gains, the Query-Key pathway (φ_QK) is consistently larger and more reliably positive than the Value pathway (φ_V), indicating that contextualization improves FV quality primarily by reshaping attention routing toward informative examples rather than by modifying per-example Value representations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a mechanistic interpretability paper without traditional method-vs-baseline comparisons; instead, it contrasts controlled ablation settings to isolate causal contributions. The key contrast is between the contextualized (intact) model and the uncontextualized ablation (cross-component attention zeroed): the linear superposition holds in both settings, but the fitted mixture weights shift substantially, revealing that contextualization reweights example contributions. The 2×2 QK/V factorial decomposition shows that contextualizing QK yields a larger and more consistent positive Shapley contribution to FV injection accuracy than contextualizing V, particularly on ambiguous tasks where entropy reduction (−0.08 to −0.15) sharpens attention toward unambiguous examples. On normal tasks, contextualization primarily flattens the recency-biased attention profile (centroid shift ΔC ≈ −0.4 to −0.6 at 10-shot) without reducing entropy (Ĥ > 0.95), whereas on ambiguous tasks it actively concentrates attention on informative demonstrations. In ambiguous settings, corrupting the examples (rather than the query) causes larger drops in FV injection accuracy, confirming that QK alignment is predominantly example-led rather than query-driven.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the function vector (FV) framework established by Todd et al. (2024) and Hendel et al. (2023) from dataset-level averaged task representations to per-prompt FVs, asking how individual few-shot examples compose into a single task direction within one prompt. It builds directly on the two-stage contextualize-then-aggregate circuit identified by Bakalova et al. (2025), whose edge-ablation methodology is adopted here, but goes further by causally decomposing the contextualization step into Query-Key routing versus Value-mediated updates via a 2×2 Shapley-value intervention. The paper also positions its findings against retrieval-based ICL accounts (Min et al., 2022; Yu & Ananiadou, 2024) and induction-circuit perspectives (Olsson et al., 2022), arguing that additive superposition and attention reweighting are complementary facets of the same FV-formation pathway rather than competing explanations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first prompt-level, intervention-based causal account of how few-shot demonstrations form a function vector, unifying the previously separate perspectives of additive task superposition and context-dependent attention reweighting into a single mechanistic picture. The clean Shapley decomposition showing that QK alignment (not Value updates) is the primary causal conduit through which contextualization improves FV quality—especially under ambiguity—gives the interpretability community a testable, bounded model of FV formation. Key limitations include the restriction to discrete mapping toy tasks (no long-horizon or compositional reasoning), the dependence on the FV-head localization procedure, and the still-unclear conditions under which Value contextualization helps versus harms. Extending this framework to richer task families and formalizing 'ambiguity' as a measurable quantity are natural next steps.
