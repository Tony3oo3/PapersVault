## Problem

Certified decentralized unlearning (DU) must remove a client's influence from a collaboratively trained consensus model without retraining, but existing methods rely on static problem-specific hyperparameters, convexity assumptions, or fixed topologies, limiting their general applicability. The core technical gap is that sensitivity bounds in decentralized settings fail to capture how local SGD updates and peer-to-peer mixing dynamically propagate a client's contribution to the consensus model over the training trajectory.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the full historical model trajectory H (a sequence of consensus checkpoints across all training and retraining phases), the index of the requesting client c, privacy parameters (ε, δ), and a noise scale σ. It outputs a perturbed-and-retrained unlearned model along with a certified (ε,δ)-unlearning guarantee. The sensitivity bound Υ(t,c) is computed incrementally via an O(t) recurrence over the trajectory, and the framework naturally extends to sequential requests by maintaining and updating H across multiple unlearning episodes, with per-request storage overhead of O(|H_u|·d) rather than per-client local model caching.

## Method

TRACE-DU derives a traceable sensitivity bound by combining two components: (1) the SGD stability factor G(f_i, γ) from Hardt et al. (2016), which bounds how local update divergence contracts or expands per step (non-expansive for convex, contractive for strongly convex, bounded by 1+γL for non-convex), and (2) a trajectory-based client contribution proxy Ω_c(𝓘, x̄^p) = (1/(N-1))‖x_c^p − x̄^p‖₂ that measures the deviation between the target client's local model and the consensus model at each round. These are combined into Υ(t,c) = Σ_p G(f_𝓘,γ)^((t-p-1)K) · Ω_c(𝓘, x̄^p), which is updated incrementally during training. The framework then selects the latest checkpoint U where Υ(U,c) falls below a Gaussian-mechanism-derived threshold, perturbs x̄^U with calibrated noise N(0, σ(U,c)²I_d), and continues decentralized training for T_r rounds. A high-probability version replaces Ω_c with a bound from consensus error analysis (Lemma 4.3), and the sequential extension TRACE-SDU tracks the evolving trajectory H_u to handle arbitrary numbers of sequential deletion requests without convexity assumptions.

## Results

TRACE-DU achieves unlearning times of 0.02 s (MNIST), 0.11 s (Fashion-MNIST), and 2.21 s (CIFAR-10), which are 2–3 orders of magnitude faster than PDUDT (6.23 s, 11.23 s, 18.02 s) and CDU (4.91 s, 8.92 s, 20.84 s). On MIA precision (where 50% = random guessing), TRACE-DU reaches 51.74% (MNIST), 49.22% (F-MNIST), and 48.59% (CIFAR-10), consistently near the random-guessing baseline. In sequential unlearning (Seq-DU), TRACE-SDU maintains superior retain accuracy across 10 or 5 sequential requests while all baselines suffer progressive performance degradation. The framework also achieves the smallest forget-accuracy gap to the retrain gold standard across all three primary datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Retrain (D-PSGD), TRACE-DU is roughly 150,000× faster on MNIST (0.02 s vs. 3136 s) and ~5,500× faster on CIFAR-10 (2.21 s vs. 12290 s), while achieving comparable or better MIA precision (51.74% vs. 56.31% on MNIST). Against PDUDT, TRACE-DU is ~300× faster on MNIST and ~8× faster on CIFAR-10, with lower MIA precision on MNIST (51.74% vs. 54.32%) and CIFAR-10 (48.59% vs. 55.19%), though PDUDT edges ahead on F-MNIST MIA (49.60% vs. 49.22%). Against CDU, TRACE-DU is ~245× faster on MNIST and ~9× faster on CIFAR-10, with better MIA on MNIST (51.74% vs. 54.37%) and CIFAR-10 (48.59% vs. 50.12%), while CDU is marginally better on F-MNIST MIA (48.79% vs. 49.22%). On retain accuracy (model utility), TRACE-DU/TRACE-SDU consistently outperforms all three baselines across both Single-DU and Seq-DU scenarios, with the advantage growing under frequent sequential unloading where baselines fail to recover utility within the same training budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the certified unlearning framework of Sekhari et al. (2021) (sensitivity bounding + Gaussian mechanism for (ε,δ)-unlearning) and the SGD stability analysis of Hardt et al. (2016), transplanting both into the decentralized setting for the first time. It departs from prior DU methods (PDUDT, CDU, RR-DU) by replacing static, problem-specific sensitivity bounds with a trajectory-based proxy (local model deviation from consensus) that is traceable from standard training dynamics, and by removing the convexity assumption that limits CDU and PDUDT. The high-probability consensus error bound from Armacki and Sayed (2026) provides the technical backbone for making the sensitivity bound computable without explicit access to the unlearned model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TRACE-DU is the first certified DU framework that simultaneously handles non-convex losses, dynamic topologies, and sequential unlearning requests, filling a gap that no prior method covers (see Table 1). The dramatic efficiency gain (0.02s vs. 6.23s unlearning time on MNIST) and the robustness under frequent sequential requests (where baselines degrade in utility) make it practically attractive for real-world decentralized systems. Open questions include scalability to very large networks (experiments use 50 clients), the tightness of the high-probability sensitivity bounds in practice, and whether the framework extends to asynchronous or partially synchronous training protocols. The storage overhead of maintaining historical model trajectories (O(|H_u|·d)) remains a concern for very long training runs with many unlearning requests.
