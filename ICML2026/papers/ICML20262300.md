## Problem

ReLU networks possess well-documented rescaling symmetries where different parameter values implement identical functions, yet existing methods to exploit this invariance (G-SGD, Path-SGD, Equinormalization) rely on ad hoc heuristics rather than principled geometric criteria. The paper asks whether a rescaling strategy can be derived from the geometry of the path-lifting representation to provably improve the conditioning of training dynamics in lifted space.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a ReLU network's parameter vector θ ∈ ℝᵖ and its architecture encoded as a directed acyclic graph, and produces rescaled parameters θ' = Dθ where D is a diagonal matrix with per-neuron rescaling factors λ_h = exp(u_h). Crucially, the computation only requires the diagonal of G = ∂Φ(θ)ᵀ∂Φ(θ), obtainable via a single backward pass, and the optimization is over H hidden-neuron variables (typically H ≪ p). The rescaling is function-preserving: f_{θ'} = f_θ exactly, so it can be applied at initialization or as a teleportation step during training without altering the network's output.

## Method

PathCond is motivated by the observation that gradient flow in parameter space induces a preconditioned flow in the path-lifting space with the path-kernel P_θ = ∂Φ(θ)∂Φ(θ)ᵀ as preconditioner; the goal is to choose a rescaling D so that P_{Dθ} ≈ I, mimicking idealized gradient descent in lifted space. The alignment criterion uses the logdet Bregman divergence d(αG_{Dθ}‖I_p), which upper-bounds the condition number and avoids ever forming the combinatorially large q×q path-kernel (q ~ 10⁵³ for ResNet-18). By exploiting the spectral-divergence identity d(AAᵀ‖I_q) = d(AᵀA‖I_p) and the structure of the rescaling group, the problem reduces to minimizing a convex function F(u) over H neuron variables that depends only on diag(G). This is solved via alternating minimization with closed-form coordinate updates: each neuron's optimal rescaling is the positive root of a quadratic polynomial, yielding an O(n_iter · p) algorithm that converges in fewer than 10 iterations and runs in at most one training epoch of wall-clock time.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with fully connected networks (2–8 hidden layers), PathCond reaches 99% training accuracy up to 1.5× faster than the standard Kaiming-initialized baseline, and a 2.5M-parameter PathCond model matches the performance of a 3.25M-parameter baseline. On the CIFAR-NV fully convolutional architecture, PathCond reaches 80% best test accuracy while both the baseline and ENorm plateau at 77%, with near-optimal training performance achieved approximately 20 epochs earlier. The one-time rescaling cost at initialization is at most comparable to a single training epoch (22%–103% of one epoch across tested architectures), making the overhead negligible over a typical 100+ epoch run.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares PathCond against the Equinormalization (ENorm) heuristic of Stock et al. (2019), which performs rescaling at every iteration to minimize a weighted ℓ₂,₂ norm of layer weights. On CIFAR-10 MLPs, ENorm does not yield any improvement over the standard baseline, whereas PathCond consistently accelerates training by up to 1.5× across all depths tested. On the CIFAR-NV convolutional architecture, both the baseline and ENorm plateau at 77% test accuracy, while PathCond reaches 80%, demonstrating that the geometrically motivated rescaling criterion also improves generalization. A key practical advantage is that PathCond is entirely parameter-free, while ENorm requires tuning of the norm exponents (p, q) and per-layer weights. On the MNIST autoencoder task, PathCond achieves lower final training loss than the baseline across all compression factors, with the gap widening for more unbalanced architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the path-lifting framework (Neyshabur et al., 2015; Stock & Gribonval, 2023) and the rescaling symmetry group of ReLU networks, adopting the teleportation-based algorithmic structure of Zhao et al. (2022). It departs from prior rescaling methods (ENorm, Path-SGD, G-SGD) that rely on heuristic criteria such as norm minimization or path normalization, instead introducing a geometrically principled criterion: aligning the path-kernel P_θ with the identity via a Bregman logdet divergence (Kulis et al., 2009). The key technical contribution is showing this alignment can be computed without ever forming the combinatorially-sized path-kernel, reducing the problem to a convex optimization over H neuron-level variables solvable by block coordinate descent.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PathCond provides the first parameter-free, geometrically motivated rescaling criterion for ReLU networks, removing the hyperparameter tuning burden of ENorm and offering a clear theoretical interpretation (conditioning the lifted-space metric). The characterization of favorable regimes (varying-width architectures, small-variance initialization) gives practitioners actionable guidance on when to expect gains. However, empirical validation is limited to CIFAR-10 and MNIST with modest network sizes; scaling to large-scale models (e.g., LLMs, ImageNet-scale vision) and integration with adaptive optimizers (Adam, Muon) remain open. The paper also shows approximate applicability to smooth activations (GELU, SiLU), opening a path toward transformers but without formal guarantees.
