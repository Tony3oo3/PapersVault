## Problem

Existing Recurrent Reasoning Models (RRMs) such as HRM and TRM handle symbol symmetries only implicitly through costly data augmentation, which increases sample complexity and prevents extrapolation to unseen symbols (e.g., larger Sudoku grids requiring new digits). This paper asks whether enforcing permutation equivariance over symbols at the architectural level can eliminate this reliance on augmentation while improving generalization and parameter efficiency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261153/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a structured puzzle as a tuple of symbols from a finite alphabet C (e.g., digits plus a mask for Sudoku, up to 10 colors on a 30×30 grid for ARC-AGI), optionally augmented with few-shot examples and a task-type identifier. Unlike vanilla RRMs that produce a D×I embedding matrix, SE-RRM constructs a D×I×K tensor by assigning a shared embedding vector to all usual symbols at every position (zero where absent), with distinct embeddings reserved only for special tokens like masks. The output is a single logit per (position, symbol) pair obtained by linearly projecting the feature dimension, from which a categorical cross-entropy loss is computed independently at each position.

## Method

SE-RRM introduces a third symbol dimension to the recurrent state and replaces the single position-attention layer per block with two axial-attention layers: one operating along the position dimension (T^{D,I}) and one along the symbol dimension (T^{D,K}), followed by a position-wise SwiGLU MLP and RMSNorm. The critical design choice is a shared embedding vector for all usual symbols across all positions, which—combined with the symbol-dimension attention—guarantees permutation equivariance under any symbol relabeling (Proposition 2.3). Training follows the same fixed-point iteration with deep supervision as vanilla RRMs, but replaces Q-learning-based halting with a simpler stochastic termination (probability p per step). With only 2M parameters (vs. 7M for TRM and 27M for HRM), the architecture enables zero-shot extrapolation to unseen symbol sets (e.g., 4×4 and 16×16 Sudoku trained only on 9×9) and reduces ARC-AGI augmentation from ~1000 to 8 dihedral transforms per sample.

## Results

On 9×9 Sudoku, SE-RRM achieves 93.73% fully solved rate (FSR) and 97.58% grid-point accuracy (GPA), substantially outperforming TRM (71.94% FSR) and HRM (63.53% FSR) with only 2M parameters. SE-RRM uniquely generalizes to 4×4 mini Sudoku (95.46% FSR) where both HRM and TRM drop to 0%, and to 16×16 and 25×25 grids where the other RRMs cannot operate at all due to unseen symbols. On ARC-AGI-1, SE-RRM achieves 45.3% pass@2 (vs. 44.6% TRM, 40.3% HRM) using only 8 dihedral augmentations instead of ~1000 color permutations, and on Maze it reaches 88.8% FSR (vs. 85.3% TRM, 74.5% HRM).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against HRM (27M params), SE-RRM (2M params) wins decisively on every task: 9×9 Sudoku FSR 93.73% vs. 63.53%, 4×4 Sudoku 95.46% vs. 0%, ARC-AGI-1 pass@2 45.3% vs. 40.3%, and Maze FSR 88.8% vs. 74.5%. Against TRM (7M params), SE-RRM leads on 9×9 Sudoku (93.73% vs. 71.94% FSR), 4×4 Sudoku (95.46% vs. 0%), ARC-AGI-1 (45.3% vs. 44.6%), and Maze (88.8% vs. 85.3%), but trails slightly on ARC-AGI-2 (7.1% vs. 7.8% pass@2). Against GPT-OSS-20B, SE-RRM dominates on 9×9 Sudoku (93.73% vs. 21.67% FSR) and is the only model that can even attempt 16×16/25×25 grids, though GPT-OSS-20B achieves a perfect 100% on the trivial 4×4 puzzles. The ablation in Table A5 confirms that the equivariance mechanism (shared symbol embedding + axial attention over the symbol dimension) is the critical factor: matching all other hyperparameters to TRM without equivariance yields only 32.3% on ARC-AGI-1, while adding equivariance recovers 45.3%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Recurrent Reasoning Model (RRM) family—specifically HRM (Wang et al., 2025) and TRM (Jolicoeur-Martineau, 2025)—by introducing a third tensor dimension for symbols and applying axial attention (one self-attention layer over positions, another over symbols) to enforce permutation equivariance under symbol relabeling. The architectural mechanism draws on axial-attention transformers (Ho et al., 2019; Rao et al., 2021) and the broader group-equivariant network literature (Cohen & Welling, 2016), but the key departure from prior RRMs is replacing symbol-specific learned embeddings with a single shared embedding vector for all 'usual' symbols, making equivariance a structural guarantee rather than something approximated through data augmentation. The fixed-point iteration, deep supervision, and recurrent block structure are inherited directly from HRM/TRM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By encoding symbol symmetry architecturally, SE-RRM reduces ARC-AGI training augmentation from ~1000 to 8 per sample and enables zero-shot generalization to smaller (4×4) and larger (16×16, 25×25) Sudoku grids where HRM and TRM completely fail due to their symbol-specific embeddings. The 2M-parameter model outperforming 7M (TRM) and 27M (HRM) counterparts suggests that explicit symmetry encoding is a more parameter-efficient inductive bias than brute-force augmentation. Open questions include whether the partial generalization to larger grids (nonzero GPA but 0% FSR on 16×16/25×25) can be improved with more training or architectural changes, and whether the approach scales to domains where K ≫ I, which the authors note becomes computationally prohibitive.
