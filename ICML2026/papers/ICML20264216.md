## Problem

Existing multi-turn jailbreak benchmarks are limited in both diversity and scale: they cover few unique harmful intents (at most ~1,078 after deduplication) and rely heavily on template repetition or small human-annotated sets, making them insufficient to capture fine-grained LLM vulnerabilities under realistic conversational adversarial settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264216/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a single harmful intent q (a short text description of a risky request) and produces a multi-turn adversarial prompt q_adv consisting of 2–6 user-only turns designed to gradually elicit a harmful response from a victim LLM. Evaluation outputs are attack success rates (ASR@1, @5, @10) computed across five victim models (DeepSeek-R1-7B, Qwen3-8B, LLaMA3.1-8B, Gemini-2.5-flash-lite, GPT-4.1-mini) and multiple judges (LLaMA Guard, GPT-4o-mini, StrongREJECT), with the final benchmark comprising 10,389 verified multi-turn adversarial prompts spanning 2,665 unique intents across 26 fine-grained safety categories.

## Method

MultiBreak is constructed via a pool-based active learning loop with three stages. First, a diverse intent pool is initialized by consolidating 5 multi-turn and 9 single-turn datasets, deduplicating with Qwen3-0.6B embeddings (threshold 0.7), and removing false-harmful samples via closed-source victim evaluation. Second, an iterative active learning cycle fine-tunes three attack generators (LLaMA3-8B and Qwen2.5-7B with full-parameter SFT; DeepSeek-Distill-Qwen-14B with LoRA) on verified successful jailbreaks, using a composite acquisition function that partitions generated prompts into ACCEPT (ASR ≥ 0.75 and faithfulness ≥ 0.5), REWRITE (high uncertainty σ ≥ 0.2 across victim-judge pairs but below ASR threshold), and DISCARD. Third, a Qwen2.5-7B rewriter (not fine-tuned, to avoid the refusal/hallucination failure mode of directly exposing harmful intents) rewrites REWRITE samples to clarify ambiguous phrasing and strengthen persuasion, converting borderline cases into reliable jailbreaks that are then fed back into the training set for the next generator retraining iteration.

## Results

MultiBreak achieves the highest ASR across nearly all victim-model and judge combinations. On DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B (LLaMA Guard judge), MultiBreak reaches ASR@1 of 0.833, exceeding the best baseline MHJ (0.293) by 54.0%. On GPT-4.1-mini (GPT-4o-mini judge), MultiBreak achieves ASR@1 of 0.804 versus MHJ's 0.701, a 34.6% gain. At ASR@10, MultiBreak surpasses 88% under LLaMA Guard and 78% under GPT-4o-mini across all five victim models, confirming both effectiveness and efficiency of the curated attacks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MultiBreak outperforms all four multi-turn baselines (CoSafe, MHJ, SafeDial, RedQueen) on the vast majority of victim-model and judge settings. The largest margin is against MHJ on DeepSeek-7B under LLaMA Guard (0.833 vs. 0.293, +54%), while on GPT-4.1-mini under GPT-4o-mini the gap over MHJ narrows to +10.3 percentage points (0.804 vs. 0.701). Against CoSafe and SafeDial, MultiBreak leads by 40–70+ percentage points on open-source models, though the gap shrinks on closed-source models where CoSafe and SafeDial benefit from GPT-4o-mini's lenient judging. Against RedQueen (template-based), MultiBreak dominates across all settings, with RedQueen's ASR@1 dropping to 0.029–0.185 on open-source models. When compared to adaptive attack methods (X-Teaming, ActorAttack) under identical single-attempt budgets, MultiBreak still achieves higher ASR@1 (0.710 vs. 0.499 and 0.380 on GPT-4.1-mini), and against state-of-the-art defenses (X-Boundary, NBF-LLM), MultiBreak retains ASR@1 of 0.621–0.710, indicating that current defenses remain insufficient against diverse multi-turn attacks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-turn jailbreak benchmarking line (CoSafe, MHJ, SafeDialBench, RedQueen) by reframing dataset construction as a pool-based active learning problem, building on the active learning framework of Tamkin et al. (2022) for iterative data enrichment. It departs from prior approaches that rely on template repetition (RedQueen), human red-teaming (MHJ), or closed-source model prompting (CoSafe, SafeDialBench) by using an uncertainty-guided acquisition function to iteratively fine-tune open-source attack generators. The conceptual contribution is the composite acquisition criterion balancing exploitation (high ASR), exploration (high judge disagreement), and faithfulness (embedding alignment), which has no direct analogue in prior multi-turn jailbreak curation pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MultiBreak (10,389 prompts, 2,665 intents, 26 fine-grained categories) is likely to become a standard evaluation resource given its scale and diversity, and the active learning pipeline is practically scalable to new victim models and intents. The finding that categories appearing benign under single-turn evaluation can exhibit up to 44.8% higher ASR in multi-turn settings challenges the sufficiency of single-turn safety benchmarks. Open questions include the substantial judge disagreement observed (near-zero Cohen's κ for GPT-4o-mini on DeepSeek/Qwen3), the uneven suppression of categories by current defenses, and whether pre-scripted (non-interactive) conversations fully capture the dynamics of adaptive multi-turn attacks.
