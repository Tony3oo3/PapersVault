## Problem

Existing federated LLM alignment methods (FedDPO, FedBiscuit) enforce a single monolithic reward model that inevitably averages out inherently conflicting user preferences such as helpfulness versus harmlessness. While Variational Preference Learning (VPL) offers a latent-variable pathway to personalization, naively transplanting it into federated settings triggers posterior collapse: each client's sparse, heterogeneous local data cannot support stable posterior estimation against a generic standard Gaussian prior, causing the latent variable to degenerate and rendering personalization ineffective.

## Input / Output

Each client holds a local set of pairwise preference triples (s_A, s_B, y) over frozen-LLM candidate responses; the method extracts a difference embedding Δh = h_chosen − h_rejected from response-region hidden states (via answer-token markers) rather than consuming raw text, then feeds this through a feature extractor and variational encoder to produce a 32-dimensional latent preference vector z. Stage 1 outputs a binary preference selector conditioned on z; Stage 2 consumes z as a conditioning signal injected into the policy's input embeddings (via a Z-TO-EMBEDDING module) to generate aligned text responses scored by the frozen selector under DPO.

## Method

FedVPA-GP stabilizes federated variational inference with two coupled mechanisms. First, a Federated Mixture Prior replaces the fixed N(0,I) prior with a weighted sum of peer clients' posterior Gaussians, where the mixture weights are learned per-client via Gumbel-Softmax relaxation over local trainable logits (excluded from federated averaging), allowing each client to upweight distributionally compatible peers and filter conflicting noise. Second, an Orthogonal Loss combines a pull term that attracts each client's latent z toward a server-assigned prototype (via balanced k-means on collected client means) with a Frobenius-norm orthonormality constraint on the prototype matrix, explicitly separating conflicting preference modes in the latent space. Training follows a two-stage strategy: Stage 1 performs federated selector training minimizing a negative ELBO (reconstruction + KL against the mixture prior) plus the orthogonal loss, with variance capping and optional base-logit dropout to further guard against collapse; Stage 2 runs centralized DPO on the server, using the converged selector as a reward model to fine-tune the policy conditioned on inferred client context z, thereby avoiding the communication cost of federated generation.

## Results

On HH-RLHF with Qwen-2 0.5B (10 clients), FedVPA-GP achieves 66.45% helpfulness and 89.21% harmlessness GPT-4 win-rate, outperforming the best monolithic baseline (FedBiscuit: 48.85%/75.12%) by ~17.6 and ~14.1 points respectively. With Gemma-2B (10 clients), the gains are even larger: 73.21% helpfulness and 96.34% harmlessness vs. FedBiscuit's 51.65%/82.45%. Performance degrades gracefully as client count increases from 10 to 100, with FedVPA-GP maintaining a consistent 10–15 point advantage over monolithic baselines on both axes, while the naive FedVPL baseline drops more sharply under increased data sparsity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedVPA-GP consistently outperforms both monolithic baselines (FedDPO and FedBiscuit) on both helpfulness and harmlessness across all client counts and both base models. Against FedDPO (Ye et al., 2024), the gap is largest on helpfulness: e.g., 66.45% vs. 48.12% (Qwen-2, 10 clients) and 73.21% vs. 52.34% (Gemma-2B, 10 clients), reflecting FedDPO's inability to model conflicting preferences. Against FedBiscuit (Wu et al., 2024), FedVPA-GP gains ~17–22 points on helpfulness and ~12–14 points on harmlessness (Qwen-2, 10 clients), with similar margins at larger scales. The naive FedVPL baseline (standard Gaussian prior, no orthogonal loss) narrows the gap but still trails FedVPA-GP by 4–7 points, confirming that both the Federated Mixture Prior and the Orthogonal Loss contribute meaningfully. On unseen-client generalization (Table 2), FedVPA-GP retains 63.16%/91.23% win-rates versus 65.28%/94.25% on seen clients, while FedVPL drops sharply (49.25%/75.21% unseen), indicating that the mixture prior and orthogonal structure enable transfer to novel users.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Variational Preference Learning (VPL; Poddar et al., 2024), which models user intent as a continuous latent variable in centralized settings, to the federated learning regime where local data sparsity and heterogeneity cause posterior collapse. It adopts the two-stage binary-selector-then-DPO pipeline from FedBiscuit (Wu et al., 2024) but replaces the monolithic reward with a latent-conditioned one, and repurposes the orthonormal-prototype mechanism from CLOP (Li et al., 2024, originally for contrastive learning) as an orthogonal loss to enforce semantic separation of preference modes. The key architectural departure from both FedDPO and FedBiscuit is the introduction of a Federated Mixture Prior with Gumbel-Softmax-weighted peer posteriors, which provides a dynamic, distribution-aware regularization target that a fixed Gaussian prior cannot.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and underexplored gap: how to perform variational personalization when each client sees only a fragmented, single-mode slice of the global preference distribution. The demonstrated ability to generalize to unseen clients via inference alone (without parameter updates) is practically valuable for deployment. However, the evaluation is confined to a single dataset (HH-RLHF) with only two binary preference axes and small models (0.5B–2B), leaving open whether the mixture prior and orthogonal loss scale to richer, multi-dimensional preference spaces or larger LLMs. The framework's lightweight overhead (~0.9M params, 256 B/round) makes it deployable, but the binary-axis setting limits immediate applicability to the full spectrum of human values.
