## Problem

Current video generation models (VGMs) struggle with visual reasoning tasks such as spatial planning, despite emerging Chain-of-Frame (CoF) capabilities. The paper attributes this to two gaps: VGMs are trained on general-purpose video corpora emphasizing perceptual fidelity over reasoning, and they generate videos in a single pass without mechanisms to explore alternative trajectories or revise intermediate errors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263531/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a textual task description and an initial conditioning image that has been augmented with text-image alignment primitives (object reference labels with arrows and directional reasoning hints) to ground linguistic signals into pixel space. At inference, the conditioning image is further perturbed across branches by varying font sizes, colors, and positions of these visual primitives. The output is a temporally concatenated video trajectory (sequence of clips) representing the optimal reasoning path, selected from a forest of candidate branches based on average constraint-satisfaction rating.

## Method

ReaForest combines three components: (1) ReaGen-27k, a 27,360-instance dataset of spatial planning tasks (maze, Sokoban, trapfield) with text-image alignment that activates latent reasoning in Wan2.2-TI2V-5B via LoRA fine-tuning; (2) ReaTTS, a test-time scaling framework that grows a forest of reasoning branches through iterative next-clip generation, using reflective rectification (an LLM rewrites generation conditions when all branches are pruned, enabling recovery from systematic failures) and entropy-aware budget reallocation (a temperature-scaled softmax with adaptive temperature modulated by both step-wise exponential annealing and normalized forest entropy to balance exploration and exploitation); (3) hierarchical constraint verification by an MLLM that decomposes evaluation into hard constraints (immediate pruning), soft constraints (incremental rating for budget allocation), and terminal constraints (goal achievement detection), providing structured step-wise feedback to guide the search.

## Results

ReaForest achieves 88.4% Exact Match (EM) and 100.0% Success Rate (SR) on VRBench, and 56.7% Task Completeness (TC) on MMGR, representing improvements of +88.4 and +24.5 percentage points over the base model Wan2.2-TI2V-5B (0.0% EM, 32.2% TC). The method reaches near-perfect performance on maze navigation variants (regular, irregular, 3D) and trapfield traversal, while also showing significant gains on Sokoban puzzles and indoor navigation. ReaForest substantially outperforms the strongest closed-source VGM (Sora-2: 1.4% EM) and the strongest MLLM baseline (Qwen2.5-VL-7B +SFT: 30.5% EM) by large margins across all six spatial planning tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReaForest dominates all baselines by wide margins on the primary metrics. Against MLLMs, it surpasses Gemini-2.5-Pro (15.9% EM) by 72.5 points, GPT-5-high (17.6% EM) by 70.8 points, and even the fine-tuned Qwen2.5-VL-7B +SFT (30.5% EM) by 57.9 points on VRBench EM, validating the superiority of Chain-of-Frame over Chain-of-Thought reasoning for vision-centric spatial planning. Among closed-source VGMs, ReaForest outperforms Sora-2 (1.4% EM, 38.1% TC) by 87.0 and 18.6 points respectively, and Veo-3.1-pro (1.1% EM) by 87.3 points. Against open-source VGMs, it exceeds Wan-R1 (37.8% EM) by 50.6 points and the base Wan2.2-TI2V-5B (0.0% EM) by 88.4 points. The consistent gap across all model categories—text-centric MLLMs, proprietary VGMs, and open-source VGMs—indicates that the gains stem from the ReaForest framework (reasoning activation + test-time scaling + hierarchical verification) rather than from any single architectural advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Chain-of-Frame (CoF) reasoning paradigm introduced by Wiedemer et al., which observed that video generation models can produce sequential frames serving as visual reasoning steps, and on the Wan2.2-TI2V-5B DiT-based video generation architecture as the base model. It departs from prior test-time scaling methods for video generation (Best-of-N sampling, tree-based search with hard top-k pruning) by introducing a forest-structured search with entropy-aware soft budget allocation and reflective rectification for failure recovery. The training-time component (ReaGen-27k with text-image alignment) is novel in explicitly grounding linguistic reasoning signals into visual pixel space, addressing a gap the authors identify in general-purpose video corpora that emphasize perceptual fidelity over reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that VGMs can be transformed into effective visual reasoners for spatial planning through a combination of targeted training activation and structured test-time scaling, achieving 88.4% EM on VRBench from a 0.0% base model. The CoF-vs-CoT comparison (88.4% vs. 30.5% for fine-tuned Qwen2.5-VL-7B on identical data) provides evidence that generating visual frames is fundamentally better suited than text-based reasoning for tracking evolving visual states over long horizons. Limitations include reliance on a relatively weak open-source base model (Wan2.2-TI2V-5B), restriction to synthetic spatial planning tasks, and the need for an external MLLM verifier. Open questions include whether the framework transfers to real-world dynamic environments (robotic navigation, autonomous driving) and whether more powerful base models would close the remaining gaps on visually ambiguous scenarios.
