## Problem

In financial time-series forecasting, the training label is conventionally set to the exact inference target horizon, implicitly assuming that closer alignment always improves generalization. This paper identifies the Label Horizon Paradox: the optimal supervision signal often resides at an intermediate horizon that better balances signal realization against noise accumulation, yet this temporal dimension of the label has never been systematically studied or optimized.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264214/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes minute-level multivariate market microstructure (7 variables: OHLC, amount, volume, transaction count) divided into 15-minute patches, from which statistical descriptors (moments, correlations, intensity ratios) are extracted to form a fixed-dimensional feature tensor per stock. During training, the model is supervised by a weighted composite of intermediate-horizon cumulative returns (one per minute within the prediction window), while at inference it produces a single cross-sectional score vector for all N stocks evaluated against the fixed target-horizon return. The method additionally outputs a learned softmax weight vector λ over all candidate horizons, identifying the optimal supervision point.

## Method

The core contribution is a bi-level optimization framework that treats the label horizon as a learnable parameter rather than a fixed choice. The inner loop updates model parameters θ on a support set using a weighted combination of losses against all candidate horizon labels, while the outer loop evaluates the resulting model on a query set against the true target horizon and updates the horizon weights λ via gradient descent, regularized by an entropy term to prevent collapse onto a single horizon. Training proceeds in two phases: a 3-epoch warm-up with standardized mean-field supervision to establish stable feature representations, followed by bi-level iterations where a single inner-loop gradient step (M=1) suffices due to the pre-trained initialization. The theoretical grounding uses a time-varying APT linear factor model showing that log-performance decomposes into an information-gain term 2ln α(δ) and a noise-penalty term ln[α(δ)²+K(δ+δ₀)], with the optimal horizon δ* occurring where the relative signal growth rate equals the inverse noise threshold 1/(2(δ+δ₀)).

## Results

On the CSI 500 mid-cap index, the bi-level framework lifts IC (×10) from 0.845 to 1.029 for LSTM (+21.8%) and from 0.862 to 1.079 for GRU (+25.2%), while Sharpe ratios improve from 3.534 to 3.660 and 3.398 to 3.458 respectively. Gains are consistent across all ten architectures and all three A-share indices (CSI 300, 500, 1000), with the largest relative improvements on mid-cap stocks and the smallest on large-cap CSI 300 (e.g., LSTM IC 0.637→0.720, +13.0%). The method also generalizes to the US S&P 500 and maintains resilience during the 2024 market crash stress test.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The bi-level framework is applied as a training-time modification on top of each of ten backbone architectures, so the comparison is always 'same architecture, standard label' vs. 'same architecture, adaptive label.' The method wins on every single architecture–dataset combination across all six reported metrics (IC, ICIR, RankIC, RankICIR, Top Return, Sharpe). The largest relative IC gains appear on CSI 500: GRU improves from 0.862 to 1.079 (+25.2%), Mamba from 0.917 to 1.141 (+24.4%), and DLinear from 0.839 to 0.986 (+17.5%). On CSI 300, gains are more modest but still universal (e.g., GRU IC 0.586→0.730, +24.6%; DLinear 0.537→0.684, +27.4%). On CSI 1000, where baseline ICs are already high, improvements narrow to roughly 5–10% (e.g., LSTM 1.275→1.372, +7.6%; Mamba 1.328→1.376, +3.6%). Sharpe ratio gains are particularly notable for SSM-based models on CSI 500 (Mamba 3.452→3.854, +11.6%) and CSI 1000 (Bi-Mamba+ 3.622→4.409, +21.7%), suggesting the adaptive label also improves risk-adjusted portfolio performance beyond raw correlation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes bi-level optimization—previously applied to hyperparameter tuning (Franceschi et al., 2018) and noisy sample re-weighting (Ren et al., 2018; Shu et al., 2019)—for a fundamentally different axis: selecting the optimal temporal horizon of the supervision label rather than filtering or weighting training samples. The theoretical grounding extends static Arbitrage Pricing Theory into a time-varying framework with explicit signal-realization and noise-accumulation dynamics, building on the underreaction/momentum literature (Hong & Stein, 1999; Shleifer, 2000). The paper explicitly distinguishes itself from label smoothing (Müller et al., 2019), which operates on the numerical distribution of a fixed target, and from noisy-label learning (Zhang & Sabuncu, 2018), which operates on sample quality rather than label timing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that the canonical 'train on the target' convention is often suboptimal in high-noise financial forecasting, the paper opens a label-centric research direction that is orthogonal to the dominant data-centric and model-centric streams. The signal-noise trade-off framework provides a principled, testable mechanism (validated via the multiplicative IC decomposition in Corollary 3.3) that could inform label design in other noisy, non-stationary forecasting domains. Key limitations include the theoretical analysis being restricted to linear estimators with a fixed signal direction, and the bi-level optimization adding non-trivial implementation complexity; open questions include whether the paradox persists under horizon-dependent factor rotations, longer multi-day horizons, or in the presence of regime shifts beyond the 2024 stress test.
