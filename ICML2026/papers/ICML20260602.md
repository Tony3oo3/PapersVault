## Problem

4D reconstruction from real-world depth-sensor observations is fundamentally difficult because individual frames are partial, unordered, and lack explicit temporal correspondences, making it impossible to distinguish static structure from dynamic objects or to naively fuse frames. Existing point-based methods are restricted to single objects, assume near-complete inputs, or require explicit correspondences, while image-based approaches depend on rich appearance cues unavailable in geometry-only sensing. This paper tackles the underexplored problem of global, correspondence-free 4D reconstruction from partial point cloud sequences at both object and scene level.

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of S partial, unordered point clouds (8,192 points per frame by default, 8 frames in training and 16 in evaluation) with no point-to-point correspondence across time and no image or mesh supervision. It outputs a complete, temporally consistent 4D point cloud sequence of 50,000 points per frame, generated at any desired density via ODE integration. Notably, the model requires no watertight meshes, canonical templates, or motion labels during training—only the global point cloud at each time step serves as supervision.

## Method

DynaTok encodes each partial point cloud into M=512 latent tokens via FPS-anchored cross-attention with Fourier-embedded coordinates, then aggregates them across time using a Transformer with alternating per-frame self-attention and cross-frame global attention augmented by 3D rotary positional embeddings. A key architectural choice is the residual token decomposition Z_s = G + H_s, where G (frame-1 tokens) captures persistent geometry and H_s encodes time-varying motion, achieving explicit geometry-motion decoupling within a single unified model rather than separate shape and deformation networks. A conditional flow-matching decoder (3-layer Transformer) then reconstructs the complete point cloud at each time step by integrating a learned ODE from a uniform cube prior, trained solely with a velocity-matching loss. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end from scratch on 8×H100 GPUs for 250k iterations, using only 19M parameters and ~6.6k GFLOPs per 16-frame scene.

## Results

On DT4D-A (object-level), DynaTok achieves Accuracy 0.023, Completeness 0.021, and Normal Consistency 0.914 under Unseen Motion, and 0.027 / 0.026 / 0.877 under Unseen Individual, substantially outperforming the closest 4D baseline Motion2VecSets (0.055 / 0.060 / 0.856). On Kubric (scene-level), DynaTok reaches Accuracy 0.008, Completeness 0.010, and NC 0.835 for foreground-only, and 0.010 / 0.014 / 0.888 for full foreground+background scenes. The model uses only 19M parameters and ~6.6k GFLOPs per 16-frame scene, roughly 12× fewer FLOPs than Motion2VecSets and 560× fewer than TRELLIS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Motion2VecSets (Cao et al., 2024), the closest 4D baseline, DynaTok wins on every metric: on DT4D Unseen Motion it reduces Completeness from 0.060 to 0.021 (2.9×) and improves NC from 0.856 to 0.914; on Kubric foreground it cuts Accuracy from 0.061 to 0.008 and NC from 0.545 to 0.835, and Motion2VecSets cannot be evaluated on full scenes due to its fixed 512-point input. Against the per-frame 3D baselines (Shape2VecSet, TripoSG, TRELLIS), DynaTok achieves comparable or slightly better Accuracy but dramatically lower Completeness (e.g., 0.021 vs. 0.174–0.190 on DT4D), confirming that temporal aggregation is essential for recovering unobserved geometry. On Kubric FG+BG, DynaTok (0.010 / 0.014 / 0.888) edges out TRELLIS (0.011 / 0.025 / 0.874) on all three metrics, while TripoSG and Shape2VecSet show larger gaps in Completeness and NC. Overall, DynaTok is the only method that simultaneously handles partial, correspondence-free inputs at both object and scene level without requiring watertight mesh supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

DynaTok builds on the latent-token paradigm from 3D shape autoencoders (TripoSG, 3DShape2VecSet) and the flow-matching generative framework (Lipman et al., 2023; Chang et al., 2024), repurposing them for a temporal setting. It departs from the closest prior 4D point-based method, Motion2VecSets (Cao et al., 2024), in three key ways: it handles partial and correspondence-free inputs rather than fixed-size complete point clouds, it operates at scene level with multiple objects rather than a single deformable object, and it achieves geometry-motion decoupling via a simple residual token formulation within a single unified model rather than two separate networks. The spatiotemporal alignment module draws on the interleaved attention architecture of VGGT (Wang et al., 2025a), extended with 3D rotary positional embeddings to encode spatial relationships among token queries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical sensing gap: depth cameras and LiDAR produce partial, unordered, correspondence-free point clouds, yet most 4D reconstruction methods assume dense images or complete geometry. The 19M-parameter model outperforming 423M-parameter Motion2VecSets and 1.6B-parameter TRELLIS while requiring only point-cloud supervision (no meshes, no correspondences) makes the approach attractive for real-time robotic and AR pipelines. Limitations include reliance on rigid/near-rigid dynamics from Kubric training, short sequence lengths (16 frames at evaluation), and the zero-shot in-the-wild results still showing visible artifacts on highly deformable objects. Open questions include scaling to longer temporal horizons, integrating richer dynamics (soft bodies, fluids), and whether the latent-token aggregation strategy transfers to other sensing modalities like sparse LiDAR sweeps.
