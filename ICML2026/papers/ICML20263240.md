## Problem

Deploying guardrails for custom, task-specific policies remains challenging: generic safety models cannot adapt to novel policies without costly retraining, LLM-as-a-Judge prompting suffers from inconsistent boundary-case performance and high inference latency, and training dedicated classifiers demands substantial labeled data that is expensive to obtain. The paper tackles the open question of how to train an accurate, task-specific guardrail model purely from synthetic data, given only a natural language policy description and a small set of unlabeled examples.

## Input / Output

The BARRED pipeline takes a natural language policy/criterion description and a small set of unlabeled seed examples (e.g., 10 random transcripts or 20 sampled instances) and produces a synthetic training corpus of (input block, binary label, reasoning trace) tuples. The resulting fine-tuned classifier accepts heterogeneous text inputs—multi-turn customer service dialogues, structured agent plans with tool specifications, or Q&A paragraphs—and outputs a single binary character (0 or 1) indicating policy compliance or violation, with no explanation.

## Method

BARRED generates training data through a four-stage pipeline: (1) dimension decomposition, where the task domain is decomposed into task-relevant dimensions and Verbalized Sampling elicits diverse instantiations per dimension to avoid mode collapse; (2) boundary-case generation, where a dimension, instantiation, and target label are uniformly sampled and the generator produces a challenging sample near the decision boundary with a reasoning trace; (3) asymmetric multi-agent debate validation, where a rigid Advocate agent defends the proposed label against a panel of independent Judges who deliberate over up to T rounds, with the sample accepted only upon unanimous Judge consensus on the target label; and (4) iterative refinement, where rejected samples receive structured feedback from dissenting Judges and are regenerated. The asymmetric design—steadfast Advocate versus deliberating Judges—stress-tests label faithfulness by forcing the Advocate's reasoning to withstand adversarial scrutiny, and ablations show this debate mechanism substantially outperforms both no verification and single-agent self-refine. Compact models (GPT-4.1-nano, Qwen2.5 via LoRA) fine-tuned on the resulting synthetic data consistently outperform frontier LLMs and dedicated guardrail models across four diverse tasks.

## Results

Across four guardrail tasks (Repetition, Privacy, Plan Verification, Health Compliance), the BARRED fine-tuned GPT-4.1-nano achieves the highest or near-highest accuracy on both human-annotated and synthetic test sets, reaching 0.96/0.93 (Repetition), 0.87/0.98 (Privacy), 0.93/0.98 (Plan), and 0.89/0.99 (Health). Even the smallest fine-tuned model (Qwen2.5-3B) outperforms the 20B OSS-Safeguard guardrail on three of four tasks. The BARRED fine-tuned Qwen2.5-14B surpasses the reasoning model GPT-5-mini on Repetition (0.93 vs. 0.94 human, 0.91 vs. 0.90 synth) and Health (0.85 vs. 0.73 human), while matching or exceeding it on Plan and Privacy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Qwen2.5-14B used as a zero-shot LLM-as-a-Judge (0.48–0.60 accuracy), the BARRED fine-tuned Qwen2.5-14B improves by 45–52 points on human test sets, demonstrating that task-specific synthetic training dramatically outperforms prompting the same model. Against OSS-Safeguard-20B, a dedicated 20B guardrail model, the BARRED fine-tuned 3B model (ft-Qwen2.5-3B) wins on Repetition (0.92 vs. 0.89), Health (0.79 vs. 0.75), and Plan (0.87 vs. 0.87, tie), while the 14B variant wins on all four tasks (e.g., Health 0.85 vs. 0.75). Against Glider, a general-purpose 3.8B evaluation model, the BARRED fine-tuned models dominate by 25–35 points across all tasks (e.g., ft-4.1-nano achieves 0.96 vs. 0.57 on Repetition). Notably, the BARRED fine-tuned GPT-4.1-nano also surpasses the larger reasoning model GPT-5-mini on Health (0.89 vs. 0.73 human) and Plan (0.93 vs. 0.92 human), showing that compact task-specific training can exceed frontier-scale general-purpose inference.

## Relation to Prior Work

BARRED sits at the intersection of three research threads: verbalized sampling for diverse generation (Zhang et al., 2025b), multi-agent debate for improving LLM outputs (Du et al., 2023), and boundary-case synthesis for classifier robustness (Levi et al., 2024). The key conceptual departure is the *asymmetric* debate design—a rigid Advocate that never changes its label position stress-tests samples against deliberating Judges, inverting the symmetric 'society of minds' paradigm where all agents are free to update. Unlike prior guardrail training (LlamaGuard, WildGuard, DynaGuard) that relies on curated human annotations or mixes synthetic data with existing safety corpora, BARRED trains a task-specific classifier entirely from synthetic data given only a natural-language policy and unlabeled seeds, positioning itself as a solution to the static-vs-dynamic guardrail trade-off.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical path to deploying accurate, low-latency custom guardrails without the costly human annotation pipeline that currently gates policy-specific safety deployment. The finding that 3B-parameter models fine-tuned on ~1,000 synthetic samples match or exceed frontier LLMs (GPT-4.1, GPT-5-mini) on these tasks suggests that task-specific synthetic training can substitute for scale in narrow classification settings. Open questions include generalization to multi-label or hierarchical policies, transfer of synthetic data across related tasks, and whether the multi-LLM-call generation cost remains prohibitive for organizations with tight budgets. The evaluation is limited to four binary tasks across three domains, so broader applicability remains to be demonstrated.
