## Problem

Whether CNNs recognize objects by shape or texture remains unresolved, with conflicting reports of texture bias (Geirhos et al., 2018) and shape bias (Burgert et al., 2025) in the literature. The paper argues these contradictions stem from methodological confounds—conflating local contour sensitivity with genuine global shape understanding—and that texture bias is fundamentally rooted in the local receptive field and pooling inductive biases of convolutional architectures, not in data or optimization choices.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266432/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 28×28 grayscale images containing a single geometric object (circle or square) rendered with a specific texture (grating or dotted), with objects placed at random positions and scales to prevent trivial spatial shortcuts. The output is a binary class label. The critical design feature is that shape and texture are deterministically paired during training, then independently manipulated at test time across four disentangled conditions (aligned, cue-conflict with reversed pairing, texture-only with shape removed, and shape-only with texture removed), enabling direct attribution of classification decisions to specific cues.

## Method

The paper constructs a minimal synthetic dataset inspired by the Navon paradigm where shape and texture are parametrically independent, then trains CNN, ViT, MLP, and SPARSE_CNN (top-k activation sparsity) from scratch under identical protocols (Adam, lr=1e-3, 10 epochs, 10 runs). Models are evaluated under four test conditions that directly compare cue-conflict and cue-suppression paradigms within a single unified framework, demonstrating that both paradigms yield consistent evidence of texture dominance and resolving apparent contradictions in prior work. The SPARSE_CNN shows that architectural sparsity alone can shift cue reliance toward shape on the synthetic task, but fails to generalize to texturized MNIST and FashionMNIST, confirming that suppressing local activations is not equivalent to building global shape representations. The paper further proposes a three-level shape processing ladder (shape bias score β_shape, configural shape score CSS, contour integration score CIS) as a falsifiable benchmark, and identifies three architectural directions—group-equivariant networks, capsule networks, and recurrent/feedback architectures—paired with training interventions (shape-contrastive loss, cue availability curriculum) to close the gap with human-like shape processing.

## Results

On the synthetic shape-texture discrimination task (28×28, circle vs. square), standard CNNs and ViTs achieve ~100% accuracy in the Shape+Texture and Texture-Only conditions but drop to ~chance on Shape-Only and ~10% on Cue Conflict, confirming a strong texture bias. CapsuleNet substantially improves Shape-Only accuracy to 91.2% and Cue Conflict to 12.8%, while SPARSE_CNN (Top-K activation sparsity) shows qualitatively higher cue-conflict and shape-only accuracy than standard CNNs on the synthetic dataset. On MNIST and FashionMNIST with the same texturization protocol, the qualitative trends replicate, though SPARSE_CNN no longer yields improvements, indicating its benefit is dataset-specific.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SPARSE_CNN (Li et al., NeurIPS 2023), the paper shows that Top-K activation sparsity does shift cue reliance toward shape on the synthetic dataset—yielding higher cue-conflict and shape-only accuracy than a standard CNN—yet this advantage disappears on MNIST and FashionMNIST, where SPARSE_CNN provides no measurable improvement. The authors frame SPARSE_CNN as a proof-of-concept lower bound demonstrating that purely architectural changes can alter cue weighting, not as a general solution. Against CapsuleNet (Sabour et al., NeurIPS 2017), the comparison is more favorable: CapsuleNet achieves 91.2% Shape-Only accuracy versus ~chance for standard CNNs and ViTs, and 12.8% Cue Conflict versus ~10%, providing concrete evidence that part-whole encoding via dynamic routing meaningfully improves shape exploitation. However, CapsuleNet still does not reach the ~100% Cue Conflict accuracy that a truly shape-dominant model would, confirming that even the most shape-favorable architecture tested remains partially texture-biased under cue competition.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes and re-examines the shape-versus-texture debate initiated by Geirhos et al. (2018a) and Baker et al. (2018), and directly engages with the more recent cue-suppression paradigm of Burgert et al. (2025) that has been interpreted as evidence for shape bias. Rather than introducing a new method, it unifies these seemingly contradictory paradigms under a single controlled experimental framework using minimal synthetic stimuli, arguing the apparent disagreement is a methodological artifact of cue leakage and conflation of local contour sensitivity with global shape integration. It also repurposes the SPARSE_CNN architecture of Li et al. (2023) as a proof-of-concept that architectural inductive biases alone can shift cue reliance, while explicitly noting its failure to generalize beyond the synthetic task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reframes an ongoing and somewhat polarized debate by showing that cue-conflict and cue-suppression paradigms yield consistent conclusions when confounds are controlled, and by proposing a concrete three-level 'shape processing ladder' (β_shape, CSS, CIS) as a falsifiable benchmark for future architectures. Its central claim—that texture bias is rooted in convolutional inductive biases (local receptive fields, pooling) rather than data or optimization—carries direct implications for robustness, safety, and OOD generalization in deployed vision systems. However, the empirical evidence rests on very simple 28×28 binary classification tasks with small models, and the proposed architectural directions (equivariant networks, capsule nets, recurrent feedback) are outlined but not implemented or validated, leaving the core question of whether any existing architecture can achieve human-like global shape integration genuinely open.
