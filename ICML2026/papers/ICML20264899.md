## Problem

Extending Mixture-of-Experts architectures to continual multimodal learning faces two unresolved challenges: existing methods add a fixed number of experts to every layer for each new task (wasting parameters when tasks overlap), and updating the shared router causes catastrophic forgetting of prior tasks' routing strategies. Additionally, practical deployment requires task-agnostic inference where task labels are unavailable at test time.

## Input / Output

The model accepts an RGB image (336×336, processed by a frozen CLIP visual encoder) paired with a text instruction, and generates a free-form text answer via next-token prediction. A key operational constraint is that during inference the task identity is unknown: the system must automatically determine which task-specific router and expert set to activate based solely on the input's hidden representation, without any explicit task identifier or replay data.

## Method

CoPE builds on LLaVA-1.5 with LoRA-based MoE experts (rank 64) in each Transformer FFN layer and introduces two mechanisms. First, Probe-Guided Knowledge Extension (PGKE) performs a two-stage expansion: temporary probe experts are added to all layers and briefly trained, then per-layer activation frequencies are compared against a threshold (mean − 0.8·std); only layers where probes exceed the threshold receive permanent new experts, which are initialized from the most-activated existing expert, while all prior experts remain frozen. Second, the Probabilistic Task Locator (PTL) fits a lightweight VAE to each task's hidden-representation distribution and stores the resulting statistics in a Primitive Bank; at inference, reconstruction probabilities are z-score normalized against all stored primitives, and the highest-scoring task's frozen router is activated, enabling replay-free, task-agnostic routing. The total loss combines cross-entropy, KL divergence, reconstruction, and load-balancing terms.

## Results

On the CoIN benchmark (8 VQA tasks), CoPE achieves a mean accuracy of 59.23% in the 'Last' setting with a Backward Transfer (BWT) score of −3.58, substantially outperforming the next-best method SEFE (58.57%, BWT −10.45) and all other baselines (BWT ranging from −17.01 to −20.90). In the 'Immediate' setting, CoPE reaches 62.81% mean accuracy, competitive with EWC (62.44%) and MoELoRA (62.10%). The method uses only 43.96M trainable parameters per task, roughly 30% fewer than the 62M used by MoELoRA, EWC, and LWF, while achieving dramatically better anti-forgetting performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the critical 'Last' setting (after all 8 tasks), CoPE achieves a BWT of −3.58, a 6.87-point improvement over the next-best method SEFE (−10.45) and a 13.4–17.3-point improvement over all other baselines (LoRA −17.01, MoELoRA −17.86, EWC −20.90, LWF −18.31, O-LoRA −17.43, LoTA −17.14). On mean accuracy in the Last setting, CoPE (59.23%) leads SEFE (58.57%) by 0.66 points and O-LoRA (51.87%) by 7.36 points. In the 'Immediate' setting, CoPE (62.81%) slightly edges out EWC (62.44%) and MoELoRA (62.10%), while using ~30% fewer trainable parameters (43.96M vs. 62M). Compared to MoExtend (62.06% Immediate), CoPE achieves higher accuracy with adaptive expert allocation (16–27 experts per task vs. a fixed 23), saving parameters on simpler tasks like VizWiz (16 vs. 23 experts). The largest single-task gain is on ImageNet in the Last setting, where CoPE reaches 94.50% versus 30.36–83.10% for other methods, demonstrating that the probe-guided expansion and per-task router isolation effectively preserve domain-specific knowledge.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoPE extends the MoE-based continual learning line initiated by LEMoE (Wang & Li, 2024) and MoExtend (Zhong et al., 2024), which add fixed experts to all layers per task, by introducing a probe-guided mechanism that dynamically selects which layers actually need new capacity. It departs from the single-shared-router paradigm (which causes catastrophic forgetting in the gating network) by assigning each task a dedicated frozen router and using a VAE-based Probabilistic Task Locator for task-agnostic inference, drawing on variational anomaly detection (An & Cho, 2015; Pu et al., 2016). The combination of LoRA-parameterized experts with adaptive per-layer expansion and distribution-matching routing represents a new architectural synthesis not present in prior MoE-CL or PEFT-CL work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses two practical bottlenecks in continual MLLM adaptation: uncontrolled parameter growth (saving ~30% parameters vs. every-layer expansion) and router-level forgetting (BWT of −3.58 vs. −17 to −21 for baselines), all without replay data or task IDs at inference. Key limitations include restricting expert expansion to the language model (not the visual encoder), empirically set hyperparameters (α, N_s), and linear storage growth of the Primitive Bank with task count. Open questions include whether the probe-guided mechanism extends to the visual module, whether N_s can be made end-to-end learnable, and how the approach scales to dozens or hundreds of tasks where the VAE-based locator's confusion among similar tasks (GQA/VQAv2/TextVQA) may become more severe.
