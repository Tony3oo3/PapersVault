## Problem

Existing neural logistic bandit algorithms face a fundamental trade-off: variance-aware methods (e.g., Faury et al., 2020) achieve favorable κ-dependence but incur direct dependence on the ambient feature dimension d, which is prohibitively large for overparameterized neural networks, while data-adaptive methods (e.g., Verma et al., 2025) scale with the effective dimension d̃ but retain a pessimistic worst-case variance bound, yielding suboptimal κ-dependence. No prior approach simultaneously achieves both variance-adaptive and data-adaptive regret guarantees for neural logistic bandits.

## Input / Output

Each round presents K context vectors from a feasible set in R^d (unit norm, with a symmetry constraint [x]_j = [x]_{j+d/2} for analytical convenience), and the agent receives a binary Bernoulli reward governed by an unknown latent function h(x) through a sigmoid link. The neural network is a fully connected ReLU architecture of width m and depth L, initialized via a structured random scheme that ensures f(x;θ₀)=0 at initialization. The output is the selected action (argmax of the UCB score) and updated network parameters obtained via gradient descent on a regularized negative log-likelihood loss with an adaptively updated regularization parameter λ_t that grows with the observed data.

## Method

The central technical contribution is a new Bernstein-type tail inequality for self-normalized vector-valued martingales (Theorem 3.1) that is simultaneously variance-adaptive and data-adaptive. The key insight is to track the growth of the self-normalized error ‖s_t‖_{H_t^{-1}} via a one-dimensional martingale rather than a d-dimensional one, combined with Freedman's inequality, which bypasses the explicit d-dependence that plagued prior analyses (Faury et al., 2020). The design matrix H_t incorporates non-uniform per-round variances σ_i² = μ̇(x_i^⊤θ*), dropping the worst-case κ factor. Building on this, NeuralLog-UCB-1 achieves regret Õ(d̃√(κT)) with an adaptive regularization schedule, while NeuralLog-UCB-2 further replaces the true reward variance in the design matrix with a neural-network-estimated variance μ̇(f(x_i;θ_i)), yielding the tighter bound Õ(d̃√(T/κ*)) that matches the best-known κ-dependence without any direct dependence on d.

## Results

The paper's primary contribution is theoretical: NeuralLog-UCB-1 achieves a regret bound of Õ(d̃√(κT)) and NeuralLog-UCB-2 achieves Õ(d̃√(T/κ*)), where d̃ is a data-adaptive effective dimension and κ* is a problem-dependent variance parameter. Empirically, on synthetic nonlinear reward functions (d=20, K=5, T=2000) and real-world datasets (MNIST, Mushroom, Shuttle), NeuralLog-UCB-2 (algorithm 5) consistently achieves the lowest cumulative regret across all settings, including varying effective dimensions (low: 10 vectors, medium: 50, high: 10000). The linear-based baselines (Logistic-UCB-1 and ada-OFU-ECOLog) exhibit the worst performance due to their inability to capture nonlinear reward structures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NCBF-UCB (Verma et al., 2025), NeuralLog-UCB-1 improves the neural regret from Õ(κd̃√T) to Õ(d̃√(κT)), reducing the dependence on κ from linear to square-root, while NeuralLog-UCB-2 further improves to Õ(d̃√(T/κ*)), matching the best-known κ* dependence. Against Logistic-UCB-1 (Faury et al., 2020), whose direct neural extension would scale with the full parameter count p rather than the effective dimension d̃, both proposed algorithms replace p with d̃, yielding substantially tighter bounds for overparameterized networks. Against ada-OFU-ECOLog (Faury et al., 2022), NeuralLog-UCB-2 achieves the same optimal Õ(√T/κ*) dependence on the variance parameter but with d̃ instead of the ambient dimension d (or p in the neural setting). Empirically, NeuralLog-UCB-2 outperforms all three baselines on every tested dataset and effective-dimension setting, with the linear-based methods (Logistic-UCB-1, ada-OFU-ECOLog) showing the largest regret gaps due to their linear reward assumption failing on the nonlinear synthetic functions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: variance-adaptive logistic bandit analysis (Faury et al. 2020; Abeille et al. 2021) that achieves tight κ-dependence but incurs explicit dependence on ambient dimension d, and neural bandit frameworks (Zhou et al. 2020; Verma et al. 2025) that handle nonlinear rewards via NTK linearization but previously relied on worst-case variance estimates. The key departure is a new Bernstein-type self-normalized martingale inequality built on Freedman's inequality applied to a one-dimensional martingale tracking the self-normalized error, which simultaneously achieves data-adaptivity (effective dimension d̃) and variance-adaptivity (no κ factor) — a combination that was previously incompatible. The NTK linearization and initialization scheme follow Zhou et al. (2020), while the variance-adaptive design matrix structure builds on Faury et al. (2020).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper delivers the tightest known regret bounds for neural logistic bandits, matching the best κ-dependency from linear logistic bandit theory while avoiding the ambient dimension penalty that makes direct extensions to overparameterized networks impractical. The new tail inequality is a general tool that extends naturally to dueling bandits and could inform other settings requiring simultaneous variance- and data-adaptive concentration. A notable open question is the persistent dependence on the norm parameter S in the leading term; recent linear bandit work has eliminated this but at the cost of projection steps or nonconvex subroutines that are undesirable in the neural setting. Practical limitations include the requirement for very large network widths (polynomial in T, K, L) and the use of diagonal approximations for the design matrix in experiments.
