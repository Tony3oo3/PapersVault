## Problem

Current multimodal LLMs rely predominantly on text-only safety guardrails, leaving them vulnerable to attacks where harmful content is embedded in complex audio compositions (overlapping speech, background non-speech audio, multi-speaker dialogue) while the accompanying text prompt appears innocuous. Existing audio jailbreaking methods depend on adversarial perturbation optimization or white-box access, failing to capture the realistic black-box threat of naturally occurring speech-audio mixtures that are fully intelligible to humans.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265461/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark feeds the target model a composed audio stream (one of three types: speech-speech overlap, speech-audio overlap, or multi-speaker dialogue) paired with a text prompt that implicitly references the harmful audio content without containing explicit unsafe information. For SSO and SAO, the model must classify whether the audio is safe to play (a yes/no permission question); for MSD, the model must answer an open-ended question that triggers engagement with the harmful dialogue. SALMONN-Guard outputs either a binary safety label (harmful/harmless) for standalone audio or a refusal string for joint text-audio queries, intercepting harmful requests before they reach the downstream LLM.

## Method

SACRED-Bench constructs attacks via three composition mechanisms: (a) SSO cross-fades a ChatTTS-synthesized harmful instruction with a GPT-4o-generated benign carrier, adjusting volume, speed, and overlap duration to acoustically conceal the payload; (b) SAO overlays harmful non-speech audio (extracted from adult/violent online content) as an audible background under clean VoiceBank-DEMAND speech; (c) MSD uses GPT-4o to rephrase harmful instructions into a two-speaker ChatTTS dialogue, paired with a semantically neutral text query that acts as a referential trigger. SALMONN-Guard is built on Qwen2.5-Omni-7B and fine-tuned via LoRA (rank 32) applied jointly to the LLM backbone, audio encoder, and aligner module using a two-stage SFT curriculum: 3 epochs on the full 10k-sample corpus followed by 5 epochs exclusively on the MSD subset. This end-to-end multimodal guard jointly reasons over speech, non-speech audio, and text, reducing the overall attack success rate from 66.75% (Gemini 2.5 Pro) to 11.32% while maintaining 0% false alarm rate on harmless controls.

## Results

On SACRED-Bench, SALMONN-Guard achieves an overall Attack Success Rate (ASR) of 11.32%, dramatically outperforming the best proprietary model Gemini 2.5 Pro (66.75%) and the best open-source model Qwen3-Omni-30B (77.95%). The most vulnerable attack type is Speech-Audio Overlap (SAO), where Gemini 2.5 Pro suffers 88.56% ASR but SALMONN-Guard reduces this to just 5.16%. On the harmless control set (HCS), SALMONN-Guard achieves 0% false alarm rate, confirming that its strong safety performance is not due to over-refusal. The overall behavior error (OBE), which averages ASR and FAR, drops from 63.15% (Gemini 2.5 Pro) to 7.74% (SALMONN-Guard).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SALMONN-Guard outperforms all nine baselines by a wide margin on every metric. Against the strongest proprietary model Gemini 2.5 Pro (66.75% overall ASR), SALMONN-Guard reduces ASR to 11.32%, a 55.4-point improvement; the gap is most dramatic on SAO (5.16% vs. 88.56%) and MSD (14.08% vs. 63.93%). Among open-source models, even the best performer Qwen3-Omni-30B (77.95% overall ASR) is surpassed by 66.6 points, while weaker models like Qwen2-Audio-7B (98.16%) and Qwen2.5-Omni-7B (92.83%) are nearly defenseless. GPT-4o (70.05%) and Gemini 1.5 Pro (85.12%) show similar vulnerability patterns, with SAO being the most exploited attack vector across all models. SALMONN-Guard also generalizes to unseen attack types: on JALMBench's Speech Insertion and Speech Editing subsets, it achieves 0.00% and 3.00% ASR respectively, compared to 13.41%/23.00% for Gemini 2.5 Pro, despite being trained exclusively on SACRED-Bench data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant perturbation-based audio jailbreaking paradigm (noise injection, speed manipulation, adversarial optimization) by instead exploiting realistic speech-audio compositions—overlapping speech, non-speech audio mixtures, and multi-speaker dialogue—as black-box attacks that require no gradient access or iterative optimization. Conceptually, it extends the visual safety leakage insight from VLSBench (Hu et al., 2025) to the audio domain, showing that text-only guardrails fail when harmful content is carried in the audio modality and referenced only indirectly in text. On the defense side, it introduces the first guard model that jointly processes speech, non-speech audio, and text (SALMONN-Guard), in contrast to prior text-only classifiers like LlamaGuard or cascaded ASR-plus-filter pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 66% attack success rate on Gemini 2.5 Pro—a model with fully enabled safety guardrails—exposes a critical and previously underquantified gap in deployed multimodal LLM safety: text-centric guardrails are structurally blind to harmful content carried in non-speech audio or distributed across multi-speaker conversations. SALMONN-Guard's reduction to ~20% ASR and its generalization to unseen attack types (0% on Speech Insertion, 3% on Speech Editing) demonstrate that a lightweight, audio-aware guard layer is both feasible and broadly effective, but the residual 20% and the reliance on a single 7B backbone leave open questions about scalability, multilingual coverage, and robustness to more sophisticated compositional attacks. The benchmark itself (30h training, 7h test) provides the community with a reproducible, black-box evaluation tool that prior perturbation-based benchmarks could not offer.
