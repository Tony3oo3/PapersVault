## Problem

Adversarial robustness research for language models is fragmented across applications (jailbreaks, corpus poisoning, reward hacking) and attack types, obscuring shared vulnerabilities and encouraging defenses that overfit to single attack recipes. Current adversarial training formulations, particularly those targeting GCG/HotFlip-style attacks, are short-sighted and fail to generalize across diverse threat classes. The paper asks whether a unified, content-agnostic framework can expose these gaps and yield training methods that confer broad, transferable robustness.

## Input / Output

All three model types (dense retrievers, pointwise rerankers, and reward models) consume a context (query or prompt) paired with candidate text (passage or response) and produce a single scalar score via a softmax cross-entropy objective. The adversarial training pipeline additionally generates perturbed variants of each candidate—via string edits, gradient-guided token swaps, continuous embedding perturbations, or inserted sentences—and applies a squared hinge penalty to discourage score increases on those variants. For the RLHF component, the adversarially trained reward model scores sampled policy responses during REINFORCE++ training, ultimately producing an aligned Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct policy model evaluated via LLM-judge pairwise comparisons.

## Method

The paper introduces a unified adversarial training framework for text scoring models that combines four complementary perturbation signals: (1) Rudimentary training with single-step string-level edits (character/word insertions, deletions, swaps), (2) HotFlip training with gradient-guided token swaps, (3) PGD training with one-step continuous perturbations in token embedding space, and (4) Content Injection training that inserts unrelated sentences or the query itself into candidate texts. Each discrete perturbation is penalized via a squared hinge loss that discourages the adversarial variant from outscoring its clean counterpart (or, for query injection, from outscoring the relevant passage), while PGD adds a cross-entropy term under the perturbed embeddings. The Combined strategy randomly samples one discrete perturbation per text per batch (to reduce compute) alongside the always-on PGD term, using medium-to-high strength hyperparameters tuned on a dev set. A key empirical finding is that robustness transfer between attack families is limited—PGD and HotFlip training do not reliably protect against content injection, and vice versa—making the combination of complementary signals necessary for broad robustness. The framework is validated across E5-BERT retrievers, Qwen3-0.6B rerankers, and Llama-3.2-3B/3.1-8B reward models, with RLHF experiments showing that adversarially trained reward models reduce reward hacking (lower KL drift) and produce policies preferred by LLM judges.

## Results

At medium adversarial training strength, the combined training strategy (Rudimentary + HotFlip + PGD + Injection) achieves the strongest overall robustness across all three model types: for rerankers, it reduces sentence injection ASR from 21.1% (base) to 0.32% and query injection ASR from 3.08% to 0.03%, while for reward models it cuts rudimentary ASR from 93.3% to 62.7% and HotFlip ASR from 95.3% to 64.7%. Effectiveness is maintained or improved: the combined retriever achieves 57.7 average NDCG@10 (vs. 57.0 base) and the combined reranker reaches 62.4 (vs. 61.5 base). In RLHF, policies trained with combined adversarially trained reward models are significantly preferred over base-reward-model policies by an LLM judge (e.g., 46.5% W vs. 11.0% T at β=0.01) while exhibiting lower KL divergence, indicating reduced reward hacking.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares a base (no adversarial training) model against five individual adversarial training methods (Rudimentary, HotFlip, PGD, Injection, Paraphrasing) and a Combined strategy, all at medium strength. No single method dominates all attacks: HotFlip-trained rerankers are most robust to HotFlip attacks (88.3% ASR vs. 94.5% for Combined), and injection-trained retrievers/rerankers slightly outperform Combined on sentence injection. However, Combined training consistently achieves the best or near-best robustness across all attack types simultaneously, while also maintaining or improving task effectiveness (e.g., reranker avg. NDCG@10 of 62.4 vs. 61.5 base). Paraphrasing training provides the weakest robustness gains and is excluded from the Combined strategy. In the RLHF setting, policies trained with Combined adversarially trained reward models are significantly preferred over base-reward-model policies (46.5% win rate vs. 11.0% tie at β=0.01, p<0.05), with lower KL divergence indicating reduced reward hacking, while medium- and high-strength Combined reward models perform comparably in downstream policy quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adversarial training literature from text classification (Miyato et al., 2017; Madry et al., 2018) and generative LLMs (Zou et al., 2023; Mazeika et al., 2024) to text scoring models—dense retrievers, rerankers, and reward models—under a unified score-manipulation threat model. It departs from the GCG/HotFlip-centric robustness paradigm by showing that gradient-guided adversarial training does not reliably transfer to content injection attacks, and by introducing the first adversarial training formulation against content injection for text scoring models (building on Tamber & Lin, 2025). The combined training strategy, which integrates PGD, HotFlip, rudimentary perturbation, and injection losses via a squared hinge penalty, is a new loss formulation that targets multiple attack families simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unification of adversarial robustness across retrievers, rerankers, and reward models under a single testable failure criterion (ranking errors) provides a principled framework that the fragmented literature lacks, and the finding that robustness does not transfer well between perturbation and injection attacks challenges the assumption that gradient-based adversarial training yields broad protection. The practical demonstration that adversarially trained reward models reduce reward hacking during RLHF and produce better-aligned policies is directly relevant to the growing deployment of RLHF systems. Key limitations include the restriction to models up to 8B parameters, the absence of generative LLM evaluation, and incomplete attack coverage; open questions include whether the combined training approach scales to frontier models and whether the text scoring lens can be extended to open-ended generation via DPO-style constraints.
