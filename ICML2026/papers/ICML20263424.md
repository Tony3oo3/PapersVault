## Problem

Standard local message passing in mesh-based GNNs suffers from oversquashing and underreaching, failing to propagate information across the dozens of hops needed for shocks, wakes, and stress concentrations on industrial meshes. Existing long-range augmentation methods (e.g., MGN Tree) add edges via static geometric heuristics that treat the domain uniformly, wasting a limited edge budget on well-predicted regions while under-serving physically difficult zones. No prior approach uses a learned a posteriori error signal to decide where and how to modify graph connectivity during training.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unstructured mesh graphs (triangular or quadrilateral, ~5K–37K nodes) with node features (coordinates, node type, boundary distance, and problem-specific input scalars) and edge features (relative displacement, Gaussian distance kernel). It produces two outputs: the PDE solution field (e.g., displacements, stresses, pressure, velocity, Mach) on every mesh node, and a scalar node-wise error indicator. Unusually, the error head always operates on the fixed base mesh graph while the field head operates on a periodically rebuilt augmented graph, and the augmentation edge set is reset to empty and regenerated from scratch every T epochs rather than accumulated.

## Method

MiSe-GNN is a dual-head GNN with a shared encoder-processor and two decoders: a field head predicting the PDE solution and an error head predicting a node-wise a posteriori error on the fixed base graph. Every T epochs, the predicted error map is fed into an adaptive binary-tree algorithm that recursively partitions the domain along the axis of maximum geometric variance, selecting the highest-error node in each subdomain as the split pivot; from the resulting hierarchy, sparse multiscale augmentation edges are extracted (level-1 edges link high-error nodes to their parent pivot, higher-level edges connect pivots to k-hop ancestors). The field head is then trained on the augmented graph while the error head remains supervised on the base graph, with gradients from the error loss flowing through the shared trunk but not through the detached error target. This cyclic, error-driven rewiring concentrates long-range message-passing capacity in physically challenging regions (shocks, wakes, stress concentrations) while keeping the graph sparse elsewhere, functioning as a graph-space analogue of adaptive mesh refinement without modifying the physical discretization.

## Results

On the 2D_MultiScHypEl dataset (10 MP / hidden size 32), MiSe-GNN achieves a macro-average RRMSE reduction of 35.1% over MGN and 20.6% over MGN Tree, with the largest gains on displacement fields (up to ~56%). On 2D_profile, MiSe-GNN reduces RRMSE by 25.9%–53.5% (avg. 37.7%) vs. MGN and 21.7%–32.6% (avg. 25.7%) vs. MGN Tree. On the PLAID leaderboards, MiSe-GNN attains the best Total Error on both 2D_MultiScHypEl (0.0209) and 2D_profile (0.0285), outperforming FNO, Vi-Transformer, MARIO, MMGP, and Augur. On VKI-LS59, the turbulent-viscosity field (nut) sees a 60.6% RRMSE reduction vs. MGN, and on Tensile2d the U1 displacement error drops by 94.6% vs. MGN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MiSe-GNN consistently outperforms MGN (Pfaff et al., 2021) across all four datasets, with average RRMSE reductions of 35.1% (2D_MultiScHypEl), 37.7% (2D_profile), 48.3% (VKI-LS59), and >90% (Tensile2d). Against MGN Tree (Ripken et al., 2023), which adds static hierarchical edges, MiSe-GNN still wins by 20.6% (2D_MultiScHypEl) and 25.7% (2D_profile), confirming that error-guided selective augmentation beats uniform geometric rewiring. On the PLAID leaderboards, MiSe-GNN ranks #1 in Total Error on both 2D_MultiScHypEl (0.0209 vs. Augur 0.0221, FNO 0.0302) and 2D_profile (0.0285 vs. MARIO 0.0307, Vi-Transformer 0.0309), though MARIO leads on the Pressure field in 2D_profile (0.0156 vs. 0.0212) due to its latent neural-field architecture better enforcing thermodynamic closure. FNO (Li et al., 2021) and Vi-Transformer (Dosovitskiy et al., 2021) are substantially outperformed on all fields. MMGP (Casenave et al., 2024) is also surpassed on 2D_profile (Total Error 0.0285 vs. 0.0365). Notably, when MGN and MGN Tree are equipped with the same geometric encodings (DID+Sinus+Sph), MiSe-GNN retains a 17–21% advantage, isolating the gain to error-guided connectivity rather than feature engineering.

## Relation to Prior Work

MiSe-GNN builds directly on the MeshGraphNets (MGN) encoder-processor-decoder architecture for mesh-based PDE surrogates and on MGN Tree's hierarchical multiscale rewiring, but departs from both by making edge augmentation error-driven and periodically rebuilt rather than static and geometry-driven. The paper positions itself against a growing body of work on dynamic graph structure (EvoMesh, Adaptive Message Passing, spectrum-preserving sparsification) and on computational graph topology analysis (Vitvitskyi et al.), but distinguishes its contribution as the first to use a learned pre-intervention a posteriori error signal to adapt message-passing connectivity on a fixed mesh without modifying the physical discretization. The cyclic training scheme—where the error head is always supervised on the base graph while the field head trains on the augmented graph—creates a clean separation between diagnosis and correction that prior hierarchical rewiring methods lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a transparent, interpretable mechanism for allocating long-range message-passing capacity in mesh GNNs, with the induced augmentation edges directly visualizable as a graph-space analogue of adaptive mesh refinement. Consistent 20–60% RRMSE reductions across four industrial CFD/CSD benchmarks, validated by equal-budget controls against random and static hierarchical alternatives, establish error-guided augmentation as a robust design principle rather than a dataset-specific trick. Key open questions include scaling the adaptive tree to large 3D meshes, robustness under distribution shift (where miscalibrated error maps could degrade augmentation), and whether directional/anisotropic rewiring could further exploit the error signal. The two-forward-pass inference cost and dependence on error-head calibration remain practical barriers to deployment in time-critical engineering pipelines.
