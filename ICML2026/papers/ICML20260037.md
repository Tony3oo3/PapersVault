## Problem

Standard preference optimization methods (DPO, IPO, RLHF) collapse all human preferences into a single scalar reward, obscuring trade-offs between conflicting objectives like helpfulness and harmlessness. Existing multi-objective approaches either require inference-time user preference vectors or rely on heuristic scalarization that provably fails to reach the Pareto front. The paper asks: can we learn a single offline policy from pairwise preference data that approximates the Pareto front without scalarization or inference-time probing?

## Input / Output

MOPO consumes an offline dataset of pairwise preference triplets (context x, action pair y/y', and K-dimensional preference labels indicating which action is preferred under each objective) and produces a single aligned policy π* parameterized by a language model. No pointwise reward models are required during training—only the preference labels. The policy is extracted via importance-weighted behavioral cloning from the optimal importance sampling ratio, and constraint thresholds for secondary objectives are set adaptively from previous optimization iterates rather than specified manually. Experiments use 7B-scale models (Zephyr-7b, Llama-3.1-8B, Mistral-7B, phi-1.5) fine-tuned with LoRA.

## Method

MOPO casts multi-objective alignment as a concave constrained optimization problem (COP): maximize the primary objective's expected preference minus a KL penalty, subject to lower-bound constraints on secondary objectives' expected preferences. The Lagrangian dual yields a closed-form importance sampling ratio ρ*(y) = exp(τ⁻¹[p(y≻y') + λᵀq(y≻y')] − 1), enabling simple iterative updates. A key robustness innovation is constraining the *lower bound* of preference estimates (via an adversarial distribution π_k within an ε-KL ball of the reference) rather than the raw estimates, protecting against finite-sample estimation error in the constrained objectives. Policy extraction uses importance-weighted behavioral cloning (maximizing E_{y~π*}[log π(y)]) since the normalization constant is intractable for large models. The practical algorithm alternates gradient updates on χ (lower-bound parameters), λ (Lagrange multipliers), and ψ (policy parameters), with a lagged reference policy updated every t₀ steps and an adaptive constraint schedule b = βᵀG(ρ^(t−t₀)) that eliminates the need for manual threshold tuning.

## Results

On the Helpful Assistant task (HH-RLHF) with Zephyr-7b-beta, MOPO-Lag achieves normalized rewards of (0.53, 0.51) on helpful–harmless and (0.52, 0.53) on humour–harmless, Pareto-dominating all baselines. On the Reddit TL;DR summarization task, MOPO-Lag reaches (0.60, 0.55) on no-hallucinate–faithful, the highest combined score across all methods. In the three-objective setting (helpful, humour, harmless), MOPO-Lag attains (0.39, 0.22, 0.17), the most balanced trade-off, versus PARM's (0.31, 0.17, 0.23) and RiC's (0.25, 0.15, 0.11). MOPO's advantage is consistent across model scales from phi-1.5 (1.3B) to Llama-3.1-8B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MOPO-Lag Pareto-dominates RiC on all four task pairs for Zephyr-7b-beta, e.g., improving helpful from 0.43 to 0.53 and no-hallucinate from 0.50 to 0.60. Against PARM (the strongest baseline), MOPO-Lag wins on helpful–harmless (0.53 vs 0.45), humour–harmless (0.52 vs 0.48), and no-hallucinate–faithful (0.60 vs 0.56), while PARM narrowly edges on prefl–faithful (0.52 vs 0.50). MODPO and DPO on D_J are substantially outperformed across all objectives, with gaps of 0.10–0.20 in normalized reward. In the three-objective experiment (Table 2), MOPO-Lag achieves the highest helpful (0.39) and humour (0.22) scores, while PARM retains a slight advantage on harmless (0.23 vs 0.17), confirming that MOPO provides the most balanced multi-objective trade-off. The advantage of MOPO is consistent across all five base models tested (phi-1.5, OpenChat-v3.5, Llama-3.1-8B, Mistral-7b-v0.2, Zephyr-7b-beta), indicating the method generalizes across model scales.

## Relation to Prior Work

MOPO extends the KL-regularized preference optimization framework of DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) from a single scalar objective to a multi-objective constrained setting, borrowing the primary-maximize/secondary-constrain paradigm from constrained multi-objective RL (Huang et al., 2022). It departs from scalarization-based approaches (MODPO, Rewarded Soups) and inference-time conditioning methods (RiC, PARM, DPA) by operating entirely offline on pairwise preferences without requiring user preference vectors at deployment. The key technical departure is formulating the problem in terms of preference probabilities (via the Bradley-Terry model) rather than pointwise rewards, and solving the resulting concave constrained optimization through a Lagrangian dual with importance sampling ratios and importance-weighted behavioral cloning for policy extraction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MOPO provides a principled, offline framework for multi-objective LLM alignment that avoids the need for inference-time user preference inputs and achieves Pareto-optimal solutions, addressing a practical gap left by both scalarization and decoding-time methods. The paper explicitly identifies the lack of rigorous theoretical analysis (convergence rates, sample complexity) as a key open direction, and the constraint threshold initialization remains heuristic in practice (adaptive schedule rather than the theoretically sufficient but expensive condition in Proposition A.3.3). A broader open question is whether the primary/secondary objective decomposition generalizes naturally to settings where no objective is clearly dominant, and whether the framework extends to more than three objectives without combinatorial explosion in constraint management.
