## Problem

RL-based image editing agents typically rely on dense proxy rewards (e.g., small incremental quality-score gains) to compensate for sparse human feedback, but this biases policy gradients toward numerically measurable yet perceptually negligible edits rather than meaningful visual improvements. The paper addresses how to train editing agents under genuinely sparse binary feedback without collapsing into optimizing proxy artifacts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265242/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives an RGB image and a high-level text editing instruction, and produces a short sequence (at most T=2 steps) of discrete tool selections paired with continuous parameter values from a 10-tool retouching toolbox (e.g., curve, color, brightness, HSL). Each action is applied deterministically through a retouch engine to update the image, and the final output is the retouched image. The binary reward is assigned only when the quality metric improvement exceeds a minimum margin δ, filtering out trivial local fluctuations.

## Method

B-Spar is a reward-centric RL framework built on Qwen3-VL-2B (LoRA fine-tuned) with three coupled mechanisms. First, Prior-Guided Trajectory Sampling leverages the MLLM's domain prior to bias exploration toward candidate trajectories likely to satisfy the strict binary reward, collecting a logged buffer without parameter updates. Second, Bayesian Reward Modeling trains a Bayesian Neural Network on RGB histogram descriptors to estimate success likelihood, regularizes with a Beta prior, generates candidate actions via Gaussian perturbations around observed successes, and densifies the sparse binary signal into a smooth reward landscape using product-of-Gaussians kernels in both action and image-descriptor space. Third, Anchor-Regularized Policy Optimization (AQPO) conditions the policy on a sampled anchor action as a [HINT] token while applying a Bernoulli dropout mask to prevent over-reliance, then optimizes via GRPO to maximize the expected densified reward. Training proceeds in two stages: supervised fine-tuning on ~500K domain-knowledge samples, followed by ~42 hours of GRPO-based online RL on 12K samples with vLLM rollouts.

## Results

On the MIT-FiveK test set (800 samples), B-Spar achieves the best scores on all no-reference quality metrics: Artimuse 61.37, PQ 8.84, and HyperIQA 69.02, as well as the best full-reference perceptual metrics LPIPS 1.03 (lower is better) and SSIM 67.88. The LPIPS score represents a ~33.5% relative improvement over the second-best method (PhotoArt, 1.55) and over 95% improvement over AIGC baselines such as MagicBrush (20.91). B-Spar also achieves the lowest latency (1.86 s) with only 2.01B parameters, making it ~42× faster than JarvisArt (77.78 s) and ~85× faster than Step1XEdit (158.65 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

B-Spar outperforms all seven baselines on the key perceptual metrics. Against AIGC-based methods, it achieves over 95% lower LPIPS (1.03 vs. 20.91 for MagicBrush, 43.69 for Step1XEdit) and leads Artimuse by 8.45 points over MagicBrush (61.37 vs. 52.92). Against the strongest prompt-based baseline PhotoArt (175B+ params), B-Spar improves LPIPS by ~33.5% (1.03 vs. 1.55) and Artimuse by 3.12 points (61.37 vs. 58.25), while using 175B+ vs. 2.01B parameters and 35.92 s vs. 1.86 s latency. Against the training-based JarvisArt, B-Spar improves LPIPS by ~87% (1.03 vs. 7.80) and Artimuse by 1.06 points, with 42× lower latency. The only metric where B-Spar trails is PSNR (16.34 vs. 20.75 for PhotoArt), which the authors attribute to prioritizing perceptual realism over strict pixel-level alignment. On the MMArt-PPR10k dataset, B-Spar also leads all baselines on Artimuse (61.22 vs. 59.13 for JarvisArt, 56.54 for InstructPix2Pix), confirming cross-dataset generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of agentic MLLM image editing (JarvisArt, PhotoArtAgent, MonetGPT) and sparse-reward RL, departing from the prevailing practice of using dense proxy rewards (e.g., incremental IQA score gains) to stabilize policy optimization. Rather than training a learned reward model to densify signals (as in RLHF-style approaches) or relying on rule-based verifiable rewards (RLVR), B-Spar repurposes Bayesian inference—specifically a BNN with a Beta prior and Gaussian kernel diffusion—to convert sparse binary feedback into a smooth, uncertainty-regularized reward landscape. The anchor-regularized policy optimization (AQPO) with signal dropout is a novel training strategy that has no direct analogue in prior agentic editing work, and the prior-guided trajectory sampling leverages the MLLM's domain knowledge as an exploration bias rather than a policy prior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and underappreciated failure mode in RL-based image editing: dense proxy rewards systematically bias policy gradients toward perceptually negligible edits, a problem that becomes acute in high-dimensional tool-parameter spaces. The 33.5% LPIPS improvement and 41× latency reduction over agent-based baselines are practically significant for real-time deployment, and the finding that a 2B model outperforms 175B+ prompt-based systems on this task suggests that targeted RL training can substitute for raw model scale in narrow tool-use settings. Key limitations include the restriction to global retouching (10 fixed tools, 2-step horizon) rather than open-ended semantic editing, and the reliance on a single IQA metric (HyperIQA) for the binary reward threshold, which may not capture all dimensions of perceptual quality. Open questions include whether the Bayesian densification approach generalizes to longer-horizon, multi-tool editing pipelines and to tasks where the reward signal is inherently multi-dimensional rather than scalar.
