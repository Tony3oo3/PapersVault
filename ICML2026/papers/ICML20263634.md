## Problem

Existing LMM-based image quality assessment methods support only partial perception dimensions—either text-level quality reasoning or pixel-level grounding—but lack a unified framework for multi-granularity quality understanding. This limitation stems from the absence of a coherent task-and-data formulation spanning global to pixel-level analysis, and from grounding approaches that introduce special tokens (e.g., <seg>) which degrade instruction-following and reasoning capabilities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263634/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts an RGB image paired with a free-form text question and produces task-dependent outputs: natural-language quality descriptions (global or region-level), pixel-level segmentation masks for grounding sub-tasks, and distortion type labels with optional perceptual effect descriptions for referring. For grounding, the LMM's textual output contains positional terms (left/right, top/bottom) that are implicitly converted to a single point prompt via softmax over token logits; this point is then consumed by a frozen SAM to generate the final mask. If the textual answer indicates the target region spans the entire image, grounding is skipped and the full image is used as the mask.

## Method

IQA-Spider employs a two-stage training design on a hybrid dataset (IQA-Spider-33K + Q-Instruct + DQ-495K) using LoRA for the LLM and full-tuning for the visual encoder and projector. Stage 1 performs standard next-token-prediction instruction tuning to equip the LMM with fine-grained text-level reasoning across four task paradigms (global description, local description, grounding, and referring). Stage 2 introduces a training-free text-to-point grounding paradigm: the LMM's native logits for positional terms are passed through a closed-set softmax to yield probability weights, which are combined via weighted average to produce normalized (x, y) coordinates scaled to image dimensions. These coordinates serve as point prompts for a frozen SAM segmentation head, requiring no architectural modification, no grounding-specific supervision, and no fine-tuning of the segmentation module. This design preserves the LMM's original language modeling space and instruction-following capability while enabling pixel-level quality grounding in a plug-and-play manner.

## Results

On the proposed IQA-Spider-33K benchmark, the best model (Qwen3-VL backbone) achieves GPT-4V scores of 7.12/10 (Global Description), 7.10/10 (Local Description), 2.41/5 (Grounding), and accuracy of 0.594 (Ref-short) and 0.484 (Ref-long), substantially outperforming all baselines. On the pixel-level grounding sub-tasks (Table 3), the Qwen3-VL variant reaches an average mIoU of 0.408, compared to 0.264 for Q-Instruct and 0.190 for Q-Ground. On Q-Bench-A1 (multiple-choice low-level vision QA), the model attains 74.45% accuracy without any task-specific training, and on KADID-10K scoring it achieves SRCC/PLCC of 0.815/0.783 (Phi-3.5-Vision variant).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against 16 baselines spanning IQA-specific models (Q-Instruct, Co-Instruct, DepictQA-Wild, Q-Ground), general LMMs (Phi-3.5-Vision, Qwen2.5-VL, Qwen3-VL, LLaVA, InternLM-XComposer, mPLUG-Owl2), and grounding models (LISA, Sa2VA, PixelLM). On the primary IQA-Spider-33K benchmark, the proposed method wins on all five tasks across all three backbone variants; the largest margins are on grounding (2.41 vs. 0.93 for Q-Instruct, a 2.6× improvement) and referring (0.594 vs. 0.116 for Q-Instruct, a 5.1× improvement). On Q-Bench-A1, the Qwen3-VL variant reaches 74.45% accuracy, surpassing Q-Instruct (67.56%) and InternLM-XComposer-VL (65.35%). On Q-Ground-Test (mIoU), the method achieves 0.338 (Qwen3-VL) without any grounding-specific fine-tuning, outperforming Q-Ground (0.271), PixelLM (0.252), and LISA (0.227), all of which were fine-tuned on Q-Ground-100K. On KADID-10K scoring, the Phi-3.5-Vision variant attains SRCC 0.815 / PLCC 0.783, exceeding Q-Instruct (0.698/0.676), CLIP-IQA+ (0.654/0.653), MUSIQ (0.556/0.575), and ManIQA (0.465/0.499). The only notable exception is that the base Qwen2.5-VL model scores 1.25 on grounding (vs. 0.93 for Q-Instruct), suggesting that general LMMs retain some implicit spatial awareness, but the proposed method still improves this to 2.13.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of LMM-based image quality assessment (Q-Instruct, DepictQA-Wild, Q-Align) and LMM-based visual grounding (LISA, Sa2VA, GLaMM), but departs from both lines in a key way. Prior grounding approaches tightly couple language generation to pixel-level segmentation via special tokens (e.g., <seg>), which the authors argue degrades instruction-following and reasoning. Instead, IQA-Spider introduces a training-free text-to-point paradigm that maps native positional-term logits (left/right/top/bottom) to SAM point prompts, avoiding any architectural modification or grounding-specific fine-tuning. The four-task formulation (global description, local description, grounding with three sub-tasks, and referring) unifies capabilities that prior IQA works addressed in isolation, with Q-Ground being the closest prior effort but limited to a narrow distortion taxonomy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified multi-granularity benchmark (IQA-Spider-33K) and the training-free grounding paradigm offer a practical path toward explainable, region-aware quality assessment without the cost of grounding-specific supervision. The finding that a simple 4-term positional mapping outperforms a jointly-trained multimodal encoder (EVF-SAM) suggests that explicit grounding tokens may be unnecessary for quality-specific localization. However, the grounding mIoU scores remain modest (0.31–0.44), the positional vocabulary is limited to four coarse terms, and evaluation relies heavily on GPT-4V scoring, leaving open questions about spatial precision, scalability to video or 3D content, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the curated distortion space.
