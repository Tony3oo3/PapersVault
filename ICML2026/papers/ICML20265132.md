## Problem

Parameter isolation-based continual learning methods inevitably enlarge model capacity as tasks accumulate, yet they still perform full-network propagation where masked, unused parameters participate in every forward and backward pass, causing per-task computational cost to grow linearly with the number of tasks. This capacity-driven efficiency bottleneck has been largely overlooked, especially in the graph domain where over-squashing and large adjacency matrices amplify the cost. The paper asks whether subnetwork construction can be made structurally decoupled from the full network so that training and inference scale independently of total model capacity.

## Input / Output

The method takes a graph G = (V, E, X) with node features X ∈ R^{N×F} and a task identity t, and produces class predictions (node-level or graph-level) along with a task-specific subnetwork defined by two index sets: a dendritic branch mask M_dm and a column-selection set I. The subnetwork is stored as sparse indices rather than a dense binary mask, enabling memory-efficient reconstruction. For node tasks, the task-specific context is a one-hop aggregation Â_t X_t; for graph tasks, it is the Rayleigh Quotient distribution diag((X_i^T L_i X_i)/(X_i^T X_i)) per graph, which captures size-invariant spectral patterns.

## Method

CAGNN wraps an over-parameterized GCN with a Context-aware Hierarchical Masking (CHM) module inspired by dendritic neurons. In Stage 1 (dendritic masking), self-attention scores over the task context select one branch per input–intermediate position from B candidate branches via Gumbel-Softmax with straight-through estimation, compressing W_0 ∈ R^{F×D×B} into a dense W_global ∈ R^{F×D}. In Stage 2 (low-rank masking), a second self-attention score ranks columns and selects top-r to form low-rank factors P ∈ R^{F_A×r} and Q ∈ R^{F_B×r}, yielding the forward weight W̃ = PQ^T. After a brief warm-up (e.g., 10% of epochs), the mask is frozen and the model trains and infers as a compact dense GCN on the decoupled subspace, excluding all unused parameters from propagation. A Fisher information regularization term, applied only during warm-up for t > 1, biases CHM toward reusing parameters with low Fisher values (less critical for prior tasks), promoting effective cross-task reuse without degrading performance. The theoretical analysis shows that low-rank masking achieves a smaller irreducible reuse bias than neuron-wise masking when the bias matrix is well-captured by a low-rank subspace, and the complexity analysis confirms that CAGNN's per-epoch cost depends on the selected rank r rather than the full hidden dimension, making it capacity-agnostic.

## Results

CAGNN achieves zero forgetting (AF = 0.0) across all 8 datasets while attaining the best or near-best Average Performance (AP) among parameter-isolation methods: 94.68% on Cora, 97.32% on CoraFull (35 tasks), 91.55% on OGB-Arxiv, 96.50% on MNIST, and 81.13% on CIFAR-10. In terms of efficiency, CAGNN at 0.99 sparsity runs ~3.6× faster than WSN per epoch (197 ms vs. 787 ms on OGB-Arxiv) and exhibits substantially slower memory growth over 140 tasks (6,296 MB vs. 8,980 MB for WSN). The GAT-backed variant (CAGAT) further improves AP on node-classification datasets (e.g., 95.28% on Cora, 97.80% on CoraFull) while maintaining AF = 0.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest competitor WSN (element-wise winning subnetworks), CAGCN achieves higher AP on 7 of 8 datasets (e.g., +0.62 on Cora, +1.27 on OGB-Arxiv, +1.11 on CIFAR-10, +2.55 on Aromaticity) while losing only on Amazon Computers (98.01 vs. 98.24); critically, CAGNN is ~3.6× faster per epoch and grows memory far more slowly over 140 tasks. Against PackNet and Piggyback (neuron-wise / learned element-wise masks), CAGNN wins on 7 and 6 of 8 datasets respectively, with the largest margins on graph-classification tasks (MNIST: 96.50 vs. 96.05/83.12; CIFAR-10: 81.13 vs. 80.17/76.17). Against TAAM (frozen backbone + task-specific modulators), CAGNN wins on 5 of 8 datasets, with particularly large gains on OGB-Arxiv (+5.28) and CIFAR-10 (+10.14), but trails on Citeseer (−0.36), Computers (−0.63), and Aromaticity (−3.81). Against HAT (hard attention), CAGNN dominates on all node-classification datasets and MNIST/CIFAR-10, but HAT retains a small edge on Aromaticity (54.28 vs. 49.70). All regularization- and replay-based baselines (LwF, EWC, TWP, ERGNN) suffer non-zero forgetting (AF > 0) and are consistently outperformed by CAGNN's forget-free parameter isolation.

## Relation to Prior Work

CAGNN extends the parameter-isolation paradigm for continual graph learning by repurposing the dendritic neuron model (Wu et al., 2018) as a structured masking mechanism, replacing the irregular element-wise masks of WSN and Piggyback with a two-stage hierarchical scheme (dendritic + low-rank) that is inherently compressible. It departs from prior methods' reliance on full-network propagation by decoupling task-specific subnetworks from the shared parameter space after a brief warm-up, and incorporates Fisher information regularization (Kirkpatrick et al., 2017) to guide parameter reuse during subnetwork construction. The paper explicitly positions itself as the first to study capacity-driven efficiency degradation in parameter isolation, filling a gap that prior work (PackNet, HAT, WSN, TAAM) addressed only in terms of accuracy and forgetting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupled propagation design offers a practical path to scaling parameter-isolation continual learning to longer task streams without the linearly growing computational overhead that currently limits the paradigm. The theoretical result (Theorem 3.2) that low-rank masking achieves smaller reuse-induced bias than neuron-wise masking under the same parameter budget provides a principled justification for the architectural choice and suggests broader applicability beyond graphs. Open questions include whether the approach extends to class-incremental or domain-incremental settings where task identity is not available at inference, how it behaves on much larger graphs (beyond OGB-Arxiv's ~170K nodes), and whether the warm-up phase can be further shortened or eliminated. The method's reliance on task-specific context for mask construction also raises questions about performance when contextual signals are weak or misleading.
