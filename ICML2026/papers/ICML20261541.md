## Problem

Existing text-guided segmentation methods treat the task as a single forward pass, where the model directly predicts pixel prompts to a segmentation model in one shot. This limits the model's ability to verify its initial localization, refocus on ambiguous regions, or iteratively refine masks when the first hypothesis is wrong. Prior RL-based approaches further over-emphasize coarse overlap or prompt-level accuracy while providing limited supervision on the reasoning process itself.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261541/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a natural-language description (either an explicit referring expression or a complex reasoning query) and produces a pixel-level segmentation mask. Internally, the agent emits structured JSON tool calls containing pixel-space coordinates (point_coords, bbox_2d) that are fed to a frozen SAM2-large segmentor; the final answer is a JSON array of one or more mask hypotheses. The agent operates within a maximum of 8 tool-call turns with a 32K-token prompt budget, and all coordinates are anchored to the original image's pixel space regardless of zoom or rotation operations.

## Method

RSAgent reformulates text-guided segmentation as a finite-horizon MDP in which a Qwen2.5-VL-7B policy interleaves textual reasoning with structured tool invocations (image zoom-in, rotation, and SAM2 segmentation) over multiple turns, observing visual feedback (mask overlays, zoomed crops) in a history pool to iteratively re-localize and refine. Training proceeds in two stages: (1) cold-start SFT on ~5K synthetic multi-turn trajectories generated by a data pipeline that uses Gemini-2.5-Pro/o3 for problem generation and Qwen2.5-VL-72B for trajectory synthesis, filtered by IoU ≥ 0.9 and ≤ 6 turns; and (2) agentic RL via GRPO on ~10K examples with a fine-grained composite reward combining final-answer quality (matched mask IoU + bounding-box IoU), step-wise process shaping (IoU improvement, tool-call costs, point sparsity/novelty), and format compliance. The key architectural insight is decoupling the MLLM's reasoning from a frozen segmentation toolbox, so the model can perform visual reflection—revisiting regions it initially overlooked and correcting erroneous hypotheses—rather than committing to a single mask in one pass.

## Results

RSAgent achieves state-of-the-art performance on both RES and ReasonSeg benchmarks. On RefCOCOg, it reaches 81.8% cIoU (test) and 81.3% cIoU (val), surpassing the previous best MLLM-based method RICE (80.4%) by 1.4 points. On ReasonSeg test, RSAgent attains 66.5% gIoU and 57.9% cIoU, improving over Seg-Zero (57.5% gIoU) by 9.0 points and over SAM-R1 (60.2% gIoU) by 6.3 points. On RefCOCO and RefCOCO+, RSAgent also leads with 83.1% and 83.0% cIoU respectively on testA splits.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RSAgent outperforms all single-pass MLLM segmentation baselines across every RES split: it leads RICE (80.4% cIoU on RefCOCOg test) by 1.4 points, SAM4MLLM (76.4%) by 5.4 points, and LISA (70.6%) by 11.2 points. Against CoT-guided RL methods, RSAgent surpasses Seg-Zero (72.6% on RefCOCOg val) by 8.7 points and Seg-R1 (71.4% on RefCOCOg test) by 10.4 points. On ReasonSeg test, RSAgent (66.5% gIoU) beats SAM-R1 (60.2%) by 6.3 points, Seg-Zero (57.5%) by 9.0 points, and the training-free SAM3 Agent (62.6%) by 3.9 points. Notably, even RSAgent-single (restricted to one forward pass) achieves 76.0% cIoU on RefCOCOg test, outperforming all prior single-pass MLLM methods, indicating that the cold-start SFT and RL training independently strengthen the base model's segmentation reasoning. The multi-turn agentic formulation provides an additional 5.8-point gain over RSAgent-single on RefCOCOg test, confirming that iterative tool invocation is the primary source of the performance advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

RSAgent extends the ReAct reason-and-act paradigm (Yao et al., 2022) from text-only reasoning to pixel-level segmentation, coupling a frozen SAM2 segmentor as an external tool within a multi-turn MDP. It departs from the dominant single-pass MLLM segmentation line (LISA, Seg-Zero, SAM-R1) where the model emits pixel prompts in one forward pass and receives only an outcome-based reward; instead, RSAgent interleaves textual reasoning with iterative tool invocations (zoom, rotate, segment) and uses a fine-grained process reward that shapes each step's IoU improvement. The RL training leverages GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) as in recent reasoning-focused LLM work, but applies it to a long-horizon, tool-augmented visual policy rather than a single generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The controlled comparison (Table 8) convincingly shows that the multi-turn agentic formulation provides a material benefit beyond what the same synthetic data and RL recipe yield in a single-pass setting, supporting the broader claim that iterative observe-reason-act interaction is a stronger pathway for pixel-level tasks than one-shot prediction. Key open questions include the efficiency cost of multi-turn inference (up to 8 tool calls), the ceiling imposed by the frozen SAM2 backbone on fine boundaries and extreme occlusion, and whether the approach generalizes to video, 3D, or embodied settings. The process-reward design (IoU-improvement shaping, tool-call costs, point sparsity) is a reusable recipe that could transfer to other agentic perception tasks.
