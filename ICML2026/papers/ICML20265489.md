## Problem

Vision Foundation Models achieve strong zero-shot and out-of-distribution performance on satellite imagery, but this perceptual robustness may arise from visual correlations rather than physically meaningful latent structure. Standard evaluation protocols—average-case accuracy and perception-driven OOD shifts such as cross-agency splits—cannot detect when representations collapse along critical physical axes in extreme regimes, creating a gap between perceptual correctness and scientific utility that the authors term the Perception-Physics Paradox.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265489/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 224×224-pixel infrared brightness temperature crops (GridSat-B1, ~8 km spatial resolution) centered on reported storm locations at 3-hour temporal resolution, paired with physical state labels (minimum central pressure Pc and maximum sustained wind Vm) from IBTrACS. The probing framework produces three alignment residuals—static fidelity error ξ_stat, dynamic coherence gap ξ_dyn, and manifold consistency violation ψ_con—each computed via restricted linear readouts (ridge regression) on frozen VFM CLS tokens, stratified by intensity regime (moderate: Pc ≥ 980 hPa; intense: Pc < 980 hPa) to test uniformity across the regime family.

## Method

The paper formalizes scientific alignment through structural isomorphism, requiring that a learned representation z = g(x) identifies the physical state y up to an injective linear reparameterization z = Ay + ε_μ(y) with uniformly bounded residuals and Jacobian norms across all regimes. This definition induces a hierarchy of three falsifiable necessary conditions—static fidelity (linear recoverability of state), dynamic coherence (alignment of latent and physical time derivatives via finite differences), and manifold consistency (preservation of known pressure-wind couplings from gradient wind balance)—each operationalized as a probe with a restricted linear surrogate class. To test these probes, the authors construct TC-BENCH, a versioned global tropical cyclone benchmark (1980–2024, 3,928 cyclones across 7 agencies) with a fully reproducible pipeline integrating IBTrACS v4r01 metadata and GridSat-B1 satellite imagery. Applying the probes to frozen VFMs (DINOv2, DINOv3, CLIP, SigLIP, SigLIP2, MAE) reveals systematic latent collapse in intense regimes: effective dimensionality drops ~60%, feature spread contracts, and physically distinct storms are compressed into a lower-rank latent subspace, while a supervised pixel-space baseline confirms the signal remains statistically present in the imagery.

## Results

Across all evaluated VFMs (DINOv2, DINOv3, CLIP, SigLIP, SigLIP2, MAE), static fidelity error ξ_stat degrades sharply in the intense regime (P_c < 980 hPa) relative to the moderate regime, with a consistent gap observed across all model families. Dynamic coherence gap ξ_dyn increases with intensity and spikes in catastrophic regimes (P_c < 920 hPa). Manifold consistency violation ψ_con rises from ~20% in the moderate regime to ~55% in the intense regime. A supervised ResNet-18 pixel baseline achieves normalized MAE of 0.4 (median 0.3) in the intense regime, outperforming all frozen VFM linear probes, confirming the signal remains recoverable and the failure is representational rather than task-intrinsic.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All six frozen VFMs (DINOv2, DINOv3, CLIP, SigLIP, SigLIP2, MAE) exhibit the same qualitative failure: stable performance in the moderate regime but sharp degradation in the intense regime, indicating a systematic representational collapse rather than a model-specific artifact. DINOv3-base performs best under the linear probe protocol but still shows ~60% reduction in effective dimensionality and diminished feature spread in intense pressure bins. Video-based models (VideoMAE, V-JEPA2, X-CLIP) improve average prediction error over static-image models but do not eliminate the moderate-to-intense degradation, ruling out static pretraining as the sole cause. Nonlinear probes (MLP, Transformer) on DINOv3-base features confirm the gap persists, showing the failure is not a linear-readout artifact. The supervised ResNet-18 pixel baseline outperforms all frozen VFM probes in the intense regime (normalized MAE 0.4 vs. higher VFM errors), demonstrating that pressure signal remains statistically present in the imagery and that the VFM representations themselves are the bottleneck.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of causal representation learning (CRL), OOD generalization, and scientific ML. It departs from CRL by replacing the requirement for coordinate-wise causal identifiability with a weaker, more testable condition—structural isomorphism (unique identification up to a linear reparameterization)—and from standard OOD evaluation by arguing that perception-based distribution shifts (e.g., cross-agency) are insufficient proxies for physical invariance. The probing methodology extends classical linear probing (Alain & Bengio, 2016) into a structured hierarchy of physically motivated diagnostics (static fidelity, dynamic coherence, manifold consistency) derived from a formal definition, while the TC-BENCH dataset builds on IBTrACS and GridSat-B1 to address reproducibility gaps in prior cyclone benchmarks like Digital Typhoon.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete, measurable failure mode—regime-dependent latent collapse under visual saturation—that is invisible to standard average-case and OOD metrics, which is critical for high-stakes scientific applications where models must resolve fine-grained physical variation in extreme regimes. The structural isomorphism framework provides a falsifiable, hierarchical set of necessary conditions that can be applied beyond tropical cyclones to any setting where observations become locally insensitive to physically important variation (e.g., wildfire monitoring). Key limitations include that the conditions are necessary but not sufficient for scientific alignment, the analysis is restricted to linear probes and a single physical system, and the framework does not yet prescribe how to train aligned representations. Open questions include whether alignment-aware objectives (temporal consistency, physical constraints, multimodal supervision) can close the gap, and whether the collapse mechanism generalizes to other scientific domains.
