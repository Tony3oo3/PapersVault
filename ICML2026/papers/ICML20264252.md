## Problem

Reinforcement learning-based MLLMs for visual grounding face three critical failures in camouflaged object detection: non-differentiable multi-object matching (Hungarian/greedy) blocks gradient flow in multi-instance scenes, low-quality or ambiguous samples inject harmful gradients, and purely geometric IoU rewards cannot distinguish true camouflaged targets from texture-similar background distractors (semantic drift). Existing RL methods like VLM-R1 and Seg-R1 address none of these issues, leading to poor localization on COD benchmarks.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a natural-language text query as input to Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct, which generates bounding boxes in [x1, y1, x2, y2] coordinate format. During inference, SAM3 converts the predicted boxes into pixel-level segmentation masks for evaluation. A frozen DINOv2-ViT-G/14-reg network extracts dense patch-token features (downsampled by patch size p) used exclusively during training to compute the semantic contrastive reward via RoI Align and global average pooling over predicted, ground-truth, and expanded-background regions.

## Method

PMSPO designs a three-component reward function within the GRPO framework: (1) a Sinkhorn optimal-transport assignment matrix replaces binary Hungarian matching, yielding soft weights P* that enable differentiable F-β IoU rewards with stable gradients for multi-object scenes; (2) Positive Learning Gain Filtering (PLGF) runs one preliminary GRPO round and retains only samples whose reward improves by more than threshold δ, removing ambiguous or already-solved instances; (3) a DINOv2-based semantic contrastive reward computes cos(f_pred, f_gt) − λ·cos(f_pred, f_bg) to penalize predictions that capture background rather than target content. These rewards are integrated through a three-stage curriculum (0–15% format/greedy, 15–40% Sinkhorn IoU, 40–100% sharpened IoU × multiplicative contrastive modulation) that progressively increases task complexity, with the contrastive weight α linearly ramping from 0.1 to 0.25 in the final stage.

## Results

PMSPO (built on Qwen2.5-VL-3B) achieves state-of-the-art performance among all reinforcement learning methods across all four metrics on three COD benchmarks. On COD10K, it reaches MAE 0.024, Fβ 0.852, Eφ 0.935, and Sα 0.879; on CAMO, MAE 0.057, Fβ 0.834, Eφ 0.885, Sα 0.833; and on CHAMELEON, MAE 0.036, Fβ 0.875, Eφ 0.921, Sα 0.878. Notably, PMSPO outperforms Seg-R1-7B (a 7B model) by 22.6% MAE reduction on COD10K while using less than half the parameters, and reduces MAE by 85.0% compared to the same 3B base model used zero-shot.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PMSPO outperforms all 10 baselines across every metric on all three benchmarks. Against general MLLMs, PMSPO (3B) surpasses Qwen2.5-VL-7B by 57.1% MAE reduction on COD10K (0.024 vs. 0.056), demonstrating that reward-driven fine-tuning of a smaller model far exceeds simple parameter scaling. Among COD-specific prompt-based methods, PMSPO beats the strongest baseline LiP by 29.4% MAE reduction on COD10K (0.024 vs. 0.034) and by 3.2 Fβ points on CHAMELEON (0.875 vs. 0.820). Among reinforcement learning methods, PMSPO dominates VLM-R1 (MAE 0.024 vs. 0.061 on COD10K) and Visual-RFT (MAE 0.024 vs. 0.058), and critically outperforms Seg-R1-7B—which uses more than twice the parameters—by 22.6% MAE reduction on COD10K (0.024 vs. 0.031) and 3.9 Fβ points on CHAMELEON (0.875 vs. 0.836), confirming that reward design matters more than model scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GRPO-based reinforcement learning paradigm for visual grounding (VLM-R1, Seg-R1, Visual-RFT) to the specific challenges of camouflaged object detection, where prior RL methods fail due to non-differentiable matching, unfiltered low-quality samples, and purely geometric rewards that cannot distinguish semantically confusing backgrounds. It departs from the training-free MLLM-for-COD line (GenSAM, ProMaC, VLCO, LiP) by introducing reward-driven parameter optimization rather than prompt engineering, and introduces Sinkhorn optimal transport into MLLM-based multi-object matching for the first time, replacing the binary Hungarian or greedy assignments used in prior detection RL work. The semantic contrastive reward leverages DINOv2 features in a way that is novel to the COD-RL setting, while the curriculum reward framework draws on established curriculum learning principles applied here to reward component scheduling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a 3B model with carefully designed rewards outperforms a 7B RL baseline (Seg-R1-7B) by 22.6% MAE on COD10K suggests that reward engineering can compensate for model scale in domain-specific visual tasks, which has practical implications for resource-constrained deployment. The semantic drift problem is well-characterized and the DINOv2-based contrastive reward offers a generalizable mechanism for any detection task where geometric overlap is insufficient. Open questions include whether the Sinkhorn matching advantage persists in high-density multi-object scenes, whether the single-epoch training finding generalizes beyond COD, and whether the framework's reliance on external frozen components (DINOv2, SAM3) limits end-to-end adaptability to new camouflage domains.
