## Problem

Existing dataset distillation methods optimize synthetic sensor data as independent discrete sample values, which is fundamentally misaligned with the continuous-time physical processes that generate multivariate sensor signals. This sample-wise parameterization produces high-frequency non-physical artifacts, overfits to specific architectures, and prevents reuse across different sampling rates. The paper asks whether shifting optimization to the underlying continuous trajectory space can resolve these failure modes while enabling extreme compression.

## Input / Output

The method takes real multivariate sensor datasets (N temporal samples × S channels, e.g., 2000×30 WiFi CSI at 1000 Hz or 128×9 smartphone IMU at 50 Hz) with class labels and produces a compact set of continuous trajectory parameters per synthetic sample: a spatial basis V_space ∈ R^{S×R}, M piecewise Chebyshev coefficient matrices C_m ∈ R^{R×(D+1)}, and singular values σ. Unlike fixed-tensor outputs from prior DD methods, these parameters define an analytically evaluable continuous function, enabling zero-shot resampling at arbitrary target resolutions without redistillation. The per-sample storage is SR + RM(D+1) floats, yielding compression ratios up to 133× on high-rate signals.

## Method

CHESS constrains synthesis to a physically meaningful function manifold through three coupled components: (1) low-rank SVD factorization X^syn = U_time Σ V_space^T enforces cross-channel spatial coherence by restricting trajectories to a shared R-dimensional subspace; (2) piecewise Chebyshev polynomial fitting parameterizes each temporal basis vector as R continuous functions approximated by degree-D Chebyshev polynomials over M segments, providing explicit derivative bounds via Markov Brothers' inequality that suppress high-frequency spikes; (3) analytical resampling re-evaluates the fixed Chebyshev basis at new timestamps to generate data at arbitrary resolutions. Training uses a distribution matching objective with a pretrain-then-align strategy, backpropagating gradients through the differentiable reconstruction module to optimize the latent manifold parameters (V_space, {C_m}, σ) rather than raw signal values. Initialization projects real samples onto the manifold via truncated SVD followed by segment-wise least-squares fitting onto the Chebyshev basis, ensuring optimization starts from a physically meaningful point.

## Results

CHESS achieves state-of-the-art accuracy across all six sensor datasets under the dataset distillation protocol, with an average improvement of approximately 6.4% over the strongest baseline. On the high-sampling-rate WiFi CSI datasets (ActR, MeR, FacT), CHESS achieves a per-sample compression ratio of 133.3× while maintaining superior accuracy (e.g., 71.0% on ActR at SPC=5 vs. 59.8% for DC). On lower-rate datasets (PAMAP2, UCI-HAR), compression ratios of 6.7–6.9× are achieved with consistent accuracy gains. CHESS also demonstrates zero-shot resolution adaptation, improving from 62.3% (DANCE) to 75.8% on 2000-step upsampling of MeR without redistillation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CHESS outperforms all seven baselines on the majority of dataset–SPC configurations. Against the coreset selection methods (K-Means, Herding, K-Center), CHESS leads by large margins (e.g., 71.0% vs. 53.8% for K-Means on ActR SPC=5), confirming that learned synthesis substantially exceeds subsampling. Among dataset distillation baselines, CHESS most dramatically surpasses DC (gradient matching) and MTT (trajectory matching), which suffer from architecture-specific artifacts; on ActR SPC=5, CHESS leads DC by 11.2%. Against the closest competitors DANCE and NCFM (both sharing the same distribution-matching objective), CHESS gains 3–11% on the WiFi CSI datasets (e.g., 77.1% vs. 66.7% for NCFM on MeR SPC=5) and 1.5–3.3% on PAMAP2 and UCI-HAR. The only marginal exception is UCI-HAR SPC=5, where DC (79.5%) slightly edges CHESS (80.8%) within one standard deviation, and NTU-Fi SPC=5 where NCFM (92.4%) is close to CHESS (94.4%). Cross-architecture evaluation further shows CHESS maintains stability on ShuffleNet and MobileNet where DANCE and NCFM drop below 50% on MeR, while coreset methods show no meaningful generalization advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

CHESS adopts the distribution matching (DM) optimization objective from DANCE (Zhang et al., 2024) and NCFM (Wang et al., 2025), but fundamentally departs from the discrete sample-wise parameterization that all prior dataset distillation methods (DC, MTT, DANCE, NCFM) share. Instead of treating synthetic data as independent pixel/sample values, CHESS reparameterizes the data space itself as a continuous trajectory manifold defined by low-rank SVD spatial coherence and piecewise Chebyshev polynomial temporal fitting, drawing on classical approximation theory (Stone-Weierstrass, Markov Brothers' inequality). This positions the work as the first to inject explicit continuous-time dynamical system (CTDS) physical priors into the dataset distillation framework, distinguishing it from prior parameterization approaches (decoder-based, dictionary-based, INR-based) that reduce dimensionality without encoding physical structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-shot resolution adaptation capability—distilling once at one sampling rate and deploying at arbitrary others via analytical Chebyshev resampling—is a genuinely new property that no prior distillation method offers, with direct practical value for heterogeneous edge sensing deployments. However, the approach is explicitly scoped to multivariate CTDS signals with strong inter-channel spatial correlations (WiFi CSI, IMU), and the authors acknowledge it may not generalize to unstructured multivariate time series (financial, server logs) or to non-classification tasks like forecasting. The 133× compression, while impressive, largely exploits temporal redundancy in high-sampling-rate signals and drops to ~7× on lower-rate datasets, tempering the generalizability of the compression claim. Extending the function-first paradigm to regression, forecasting, and weaker-correlation settings remains an open direction.
