## Problem

The Probabilistic Bisection Algorithm (PBA) is widely used for stochastic root-finding and boundary detection, yet its convergence rate for a fixed, unknown ground truth has remained unproven. Prior analyses either discretize the search space or model the truth as a random variable, and the standard supermartingale argument that guarantees monotone error reduction breaks down for the original PBA because accuracy improvement depends critically on where each query falls. This paper resolves the gap by directly characterizing the oscillatory query dynamics of PBA.

## Input / Output

At each round the algorithm selects a query point as the posterior median over [0,1]^d and receives a binary label that is flipped independently with probability p ∈ (0, 1/2). In 1D the output is the final query θ̂_n = X_{n+1}; in d ≥ 2 dimensions the output is a piecewise-polynomial boundary estimate ĝ_n constructed by interpolating 1D PBA estimates along vertical grid lines (Hölder case) or by linear regression over PBA-estimated edge intersections (linear case). The noise level p is assumed known and enters the Bayesian update rule explicitly.

## Method

The key insight is that PBA queries oscillate around the truth with steadily shrinking amplitude, and the paper formalizes this via two new propositions. Proposition 1 shows that the ratio M_i = a_i^{(3)}/a_i^{(2)} (posterior mass in the far interval over the near interval) decays exponentially by proving ln(M_i) is a supermartingale whose decrease is triggered whenever a query crosses the truth. Proposition 2 establishes that such crossings occur linearly often (E[T] ≥ ηn) via a stopping-time argument on the posterior probability a_i^{(1)} crossing 1/2. Combining these with Azuma-Hoeffding yields E|θ̂_n − θ*| ≤ 3e^{−Cn}, matching the noiseless binary-search rate. For high dimensions, the 1D result is lifted by partitioning the base domain into dyadic cells, running PBA on each vertical line, and interpolating with tensor-product polynomials (Hölder case) or by edge-detection hypothesis testing followed by PBA along detected edges and linear reconstruction (linear case, achieving exponential rate).

## Results

The paper establishes that PBA achieves exponential convergence in 1D: E|θ̂_n − θ*| ≤ 3e^{−Cn} (Theorem 1), where C > 0 depends only on the noise level p. In the high-dimensional Hölder-smooth setting (Theorem 2), the L₁ error satisfies E‖ĝ_n − g*‖₁ ≤ O((log n / n)^{α/(d−1)}), which is nearly minimax optimal. For linear decision boundaries (Theorem 3), exponential convergence E‖ĝ_n − g*‖₁ ≤ C₁ exp(−cn) is recovered. Simulation experiments (Figures 1–5) confirm exponential decay in log-plots for 1D (θ* = 1/3, p ∈ {0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.4}) and polynomial decay for Lipschitz boundaries, with larger p yielding slower convergence as predicted.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper that does not benchmark PBA against competing algorithms; the `compares_to` list is empty. The paper's contribution is to close a long-standing gap: prior analyses by Burnashev & Zigangirov (1974) and Waeber et al. (2013) established exponential convergence only under a discretized query grid or a Bayesian (random-truth) model, respectively, neither of which applies to the original PBA with a fixed, unknown ground truth. The authors show that the key supermartingale argument used in those prior works (Eq. 3 in the paper) breaks down for the original PBA because accuracy improvement depends critically on query location. Instead, they introduce a finer-grained analysis of query oscillation dynamics (Propositions 1 and 2) to prove the same exponential rate holds for the original algorithm. The high-dimensional extension (Theorem 2) matches the minimax lower bound of Castro & Nowak (2008) up to logarithmic factors, and Theorem 3 provides the first exponential-rate guarantee for learning linear boundaries via PBA-based methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Horstein's (1963) Probabilistic Bisection Algorithm, which extends classical binary search to noisy binary responses via Bayesian posterior updates. Prior theoretical analyses either discretized the search space (Burnashev & Zigangirov, 1974) or modeled the ground truth as a continuous random variable (Waeber et al., 2013), both of which rely on a simplifying property—that a certain error-bounding quantity decreases in expectation regardless of query location. The key departure here is that this monotone-decrease argument provably fails for the original PBA with a fixed, unknown truth on a continuous domain, necessitating an entirely new analytical framework centered on tracking the oscillatory dynamics of PBA queries and their crossing times around the truth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This resolves a long-standing open question by proving that PBA achieves the optimal exponential convergence rate (matching noiseless binary search) for any fixed ground truth, bridging the gap between its strong empirical performance and theoretical understanding. The developed tools for characterizing query oscillation and posterior concentration are of independent interest for analyzing other adaptive algorithms on continuous domains. Open questions include whether PBA retains exponential convergence when the actual noise level exceeds the assumed one, and the practical challenge of numerically computing the posterior median at each step. The high-dimensional extension to Hölder-smooth boundaries achieves near-minimax optimal rates, with the special case of linear boundaries recovering exponential convergence—a result the authors note is the first of its kind.
