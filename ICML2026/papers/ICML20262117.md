## Problem

Generative molecular design methods that guarantee synthesizability through hard-coded reaction templates and building blocks (reaction-based MDPs) suffer from combinatorial action spaces, fixed constraint definitions, and an inability to leverage rich chemical priors from large-scale SMILES corpora. Conversely, sequence-based SMILES generation is flexible and well-suited to pretrained language models but offers no synthesizability guarantee. The paper asks whether synthesizability can be induced as a soft, distributional constraint on a sequence-based GFlowNet without sacrificing optimization performance or modeling flexibility.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained SMILES language model (GP-MolFormer) as a chemical prior, a task-specific scalar reward (e.g., proxy binding affinity for sEH, or a weighted Vina+QED score for structure-based design), and a synthesizability oracle defined by a set of reaction templates and building blocks (evaluated via heuristic retrosynthesis or AiZynthFinder). It outputs SMILES strings sampled from a posterior that is proportional to reward × prior × synthesizability indicator, with the synthesizability constraint enforced softly through training rather than through the generation process itself. The sequence-based MDP operates over a fixed SMILES token vocabulary, and the model can be rapidly re-aligned to new constraint sets (e.g., fewer reactions, added Lipinski/BRENK filters) with only ~100 additional training steps.

## Method

S3-GFN initializes a GFlowNet forward policy from a pretrained SMILES prior and performs post-training via Relative Trajectory Balance (RTB), but splits the training into two phases to decouple reward optimization from constraint enforcement. During on-policy updates, RTB is applied exclusively to synthesizable (positive) trajectories, reweighting probability mass within the feasible region. During replay updates, a contrastive auxiliary loss is added: it computes a softmax-based penalty on sequence-level log-probabilities that suppresses the relative likelihood of negative (unsynthesizable) trajectories drawn from a separate replay buffer compared to positive ones. This auxiliary loss operates on relative scores rather than modifying the scalar reward, so it avoids the optimization conflicts and shared-prefix penalty propagation observed with reward shaping. The off-policy replay framework also supports external augmentation—genetic search to discover high-reward positives and local graph mutations to generate informative negatives—enabling the model to maintain a stable, balanced batch composition even as the posterior concentrates on synthesizable molecules.

## Results

On the sEH proxy task, S3-GFN achieves a 94.5% positive (synthesizable) ratio with a mean sEH score of 1.009 and Pos. Top-100 sEH of 1.043, outperforming SynFlowNet (0.907 sEH, 0.989 Pos. Top-100) while operating on a simpler sequence-based MDP. On five LIT-PCBA targets, S3-GFN achieves the best average Vina docking scores (e.g., −12.32 kcal/mol on ADRB2 vs. −11.45 for RxnFlow) while simultaneously attaining 96–100% AiZynthFinder success rates, compared to 47–71% for reaction-based baselines. In the sample-limited PMO benchmark (10K oracle budget), S3-GFN with genetic exploration reaches AUC Top-10 of 0.905 on GSK3β and 0.979 on DRD2, the best among all GFN-based methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

S3-GFN outperforms all three reaction-based GFlowNet baselines (SynFlowNet, RGFN, RxnFlow) on both Vina docking scores and AiZynthFinder success rates across all five LIT-PCBA targets; for example, on ADRB2 it improves Vina from −11.45 (RxnFlow) to −12.32 while raising AiZynthFinder from 60% to 100%. Against the reward-shaping baseline (RTB+RS), S3-GFN achieves comparable sEH scores (1.009 vs. 1.003) but a higher positive ratio (0.945 vs. 0.987) and, critically, preserves higher uniqueness (929 vs. 770) and Pos. Top-100 diversity under constraint changes, because the contrastive auxiliary loss avoids penalizing shared substructures. In the sample-limited PMO benchmark, S3-GFN with genetic exploration (AUC 0.905 on GSK3β, 0.979 on DRD2) surpasses both RTB+RS (0.502, 0.783) and REINVENT+RS (0.830, 0.964), and matches or exceeds non-GFN baselines Graph GA-ReaSyn (0.889, 0.977) and SynGA (0.866, 0.976). Compared to the SMILES-based Saturn method, S3-GFN achieves a higher positive ratio (0.945 vs. 0.885), higher mean sEH (1.009 vs. 0.906), and substantially higher diversity (0.764 vs. 0.390). The fragment-based FragGFN baseline achieves 0% positive ratio, confirming that sequence-based generation without synthesizability constraints fails to produce valid synthetic routes.

## Relation to Prior Work

S3-GFN extends the GFlowNet post-training framework (RTB, Venkatraman et al. 2024; Pandey et al. 2025) by adding a contrastive auxiliary loss that explicitly suppresses probability mass outside a synthesizable region, decoupling constraint enforcement from scalar reward optimization. It departs from the dominant reaction-based MDP approach (SynFlowNet, RGFN, RxnFlow) that hard-codes synthesizability through predefined reaction templates and building blocks, instead operating on a simpler sequence-based SMILES MDP initialized from a pretrained chemical language model (GP-MolFormer). The off-policy replay-buffer mechanism builds on genetic-guided GFlowNets (Kim et al., 2024a), but repurposes it to maintain separate positive/negative buffers for contrastive training rather than reward-prioritized exploration alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that soft distributional regularization on a sequence-based MDP can match or exceed hard-constrained reaction-based GFlowNets on external synthesizability metrics (AiZynthFinder ≥95% vs. ~72% for reaction-based methods), suggesting that the rigid coupling between generation-time reaction rules and external validation is a fundamental limitation of the reaction-based paradigm. The finding that reward shaping catastrophically degrades GFlowNet performance (RTB+RS AUC 0.502) while working acceptably under REINVENT (AUC 0.830) highlights an important and previously unexplored interaction between the TB/RTB objective and penalty-based constraints. However, all validation remains in silico; wet-lab synthesis of generated molecules is an open question, and the method's reliance on a retrosynthesis-based synthesizability oracle limits its applicability to settings where such oracles are available and reliable.
