## Problem

Conventional LLM scaling via more parameters and data is hitting diminishing returns due to data scarcity and communication overheads, while existing compute-scaling alternatives—vertical scaling (deepening via parameter reuse) and test-time CoT—suffer from training instability, discrete-token constraints, or dependence on specialized data. The paper asks whether one can scale computation during pretraining by teaching the model to generate an intermediate latent thought in continuous space before each token, effectively adding a per-token 'thinking' step without deepening the architecture.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a standard text sequence as token embeddings and, at inference, produces two outputs per position: a latent thought (the last-layer hidden state, a continuous vector of dimension d_model) and the predicted next token. The latent thought is fed back as the next-step input embedding, preserving the original token's positional encoding so the context window is not halved. At training, the interleaved sequence of [token, latent, token, latent, …] doubles the effective sequence length, and the cross-entropy loss is computed only at the latent positions to predict the subsequent real token.

## Method

PonderLM-2 introduces a horizontal-scaling mechanism: after computing the last hidden state at each position, that state is fed back as the input embedding for the next step, so the model performs two forward passes per token (one to produce the latent thought, one to predict the token). Because this creates a strict left-to-right dependency (h₁→h₂→…→h_T) that is infeasible to train sequentially, the authors approximate the autoregressive hidden states via Jacobi fixed-point iteration: they interleave original embeddings with the previous iteration's hidden states, run a single parallel forward pass, and repeat for K rounds (randomly sampled from {2,3} to prevent depth overfitting). Causality guarantees convergence in at most T steps, and the Banach contraction analysis (empirically L≈0.345) shows exponential decay to the sequential-inference solution within ~4–6 iterations. The approach requires no specialized data, no RL, and no architectural changes beyond the feedback loop, and it generalizes to chaining multiple latent thoughts per token for further gains.

## Results

On the LLaMA-1.4B testbed (26B tokens), PonderLM-2-LLaMA-1.4B achieves Pile PPL 7.89, Lambada OpenAI PPL 7.39, and average downstream accuracy of 52.3% (0-shot) / 51.9% (5-shot) at 2× inference FLOPs, surpassing the 2.8B-parameter oracle (PPL 8.23, avg acc 49.8%) and all compute-scaling baselines at both 2× and 4× budgets. On the Pythia suite (300B Pile tokens), PonderLM-2-Pythia-1.4B reaches 58.5% average 0-shot accuracy, exceeding Pythia-2.8B (57.3%) by +1.2 points despite having half the parameters. The 1.26B variant matches Pythia-2.8B with 55% fewer parameters, and the 1.4B variant converges to Pythia-1.4B's final loss using 62% fewer training tokens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the official Pythia suite, PonderLM-2-Pythia-1.4B (300B tokens) outperforms Pythia-2.8B on all nine downstream tasks (58.5% vs. 57.3% avg 0-shot acc), and PonderLM-2-Pythia-410M exceeds Pythia-1B and Bloom-1.7B. Against OPT-1.3B and Bloom-1.7B at comparable sizes, PonderLM-2-Pythia-1.4B leads by +4.9 and +8.3 points in average 0-shot accuracy respectively. Against PonderLM (the prior vertical-scaling method), PonderLM-2-LLaMA-1.4B at 2× inference FLOPs beats Pondering LLaMA-1.4B even at 7 pondering steps (8× FLOPs) on Pile PPL (7.89 vs. 8.01) and average accuracy (52.3% vs. 51.4%). Against Looped Transformer and Pause Token at matched 2× FLOPs, PonderLM-2 leads by 2.5 and 3.8 points in average 0-shot accuracy, and maintains its advantage even when those baselines are given 4× FLOPs. Against TinyLLaMA-1.1B (trained on 3T tokens, 10× more data), PonderLM-2-Pythia-1.4B (300B tokens) achieves higher average accuracy (58.5% vs. 55.4% 0-shot), demonstrating superior data efficiency. The 2.8B-parameter LLaMA oracle, which doubles model depth to match inference FLOPs, is outperformed on every metric by PonderLM-2-LLaMA-1.4B.

## Relation to Prior Work

PonderLM-2 repositions the latent-reasoning idea from PonderLM (Zeng et al., 2025) from vertical scaling (deepening the network via a probability-weighted 'pondering embedding') to horizontal scaling (extending the generation process per token by feeding the last hidden state back as the next input). It also adapts Coconut's (Hao et al., 2024) continuous-space thought mechanism from fine-tuning on CoT data to pretraining on a general corpus, making the latent thought a per-token operation rather than a per-question one. The key technical departure from prior iterative methods (Looped Transformers, Pause Tokens, Quiet-STaR) is the use of Jacobi fixed-point iteration to parallelize what would otherwise be a strictly sequential autoregressive unrolling, enabling standard data-parallel training without the instability issues of recurrent depth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central claim—that one latent thought per token lets a 1.4B model match a 2.8B model at equal inference FLOPs—introduces a new 'horizontal' scaling axis orthogonal to parameter count and data volume, potentially alleviating data-scarcity and communication-overhead bottlenecks. The plug-and-play continual-pretraining results on LLaMA-3-3B (with only ~1.1× memory overhead) suggest the method can retrofit existing foundation models without retraining from scratch. Open questions include whether the ~8× training-FLOPs overhead (from Jacobi iterations on doubled-length sequences) remains acceptable at frontier scale, whether the 2× KV-cache growth becomes prohibitive for long-context serving, and whether chaining many latent thoughts (the 'latent CoT' direction) yields diminishing returns or continues to scale favorably.
