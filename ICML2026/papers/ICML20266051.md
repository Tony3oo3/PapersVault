## Problem

As LLMs and MLLMs become formal gatekeepers in conference peer review (AAAI, ICML, NeurIPS), their robustness to adversarial manipulation remains unexplored. Existing robustness studies are overwhelmingly text-only, ignoring the visual modality where core methodology and results reside; the threat is also distinct from standard jailbreaking, targeting domain-specific score inflation rather than general safety violations; and no practical defenses exist for detecting subtle, domain-specific manipulations buried in lengthy multimodal documents.

## Input / Output

The reviewer MLLM receives a system review prompt, the full anonymized paper text, and K extracted key figures (method diagrams, result charts), and produces a structured review with tag-level judgments (aspect tags like originality/soundness paired with positive/negative sentiment) plus numerical scores on 8 standardized aspects (Overall, Substance, Appropriateness, Meaningful Comparison, Soundness, Originality, Clarity, Impact), each on a 1–10 scale. Attack evaluation reports the aggregate score shift as the sum of changes across all 8 aspects (theoretical range ±80). For visual attacks, input figures are resized to match each vision encoder's native resolution (e.g., 336×336 for LLaVA); the chunk-based defense further segments text into sentence-boundary-preserving chunks and treats each figure as a standalone visual chunk for retrieval.

## Method

PaperGuard unifies three attack families under a single score-inflation objective: black-box prompt injections (12 variants spanning direct commands, role-play, oblique framing, and multi-step sequences), white-box text perturbations via GCG optimization on a surrogate model targeting the structured-review format prefix, and white-box image perturbations via an AutoAttack-style ensemble (PGD ℓ∞, APGD ℓ∞, C&W ℓ₂) that finds imperceptible pixel-level noise maximizing review scores. The proposed defense, Chunk-based Embedding Search, segments the paper into text and figure chunks, embeds them with the multimodal E5-V model into a shared space, retrieves top-k nearest neighbors against a reference database of known attack patterns (both textual instructions and visual templates), and then invokes a verifier LLM on each (reference, chunk) pair to confirm active malicious intent rather than benign discussion. This retrieve-then-verify pipeline achieves 95% detection accuracy with 0% false positive rate on text and 93.5% accuracy with 0.10% FPR on visual attacks, substantially outperforming global BERT classifiers (0% recall), LLM-as-Judge (100% FPR), and moderation APIs (0% recall), and generalizes to 100% recall on 17 real-world hidden prompt injections found in arXiv papers.

## Results

Black-box prompt injection achieves up to 80% Attack Success Rate (ASR) on Claude-sonnet-4.5 with a mean score inflation of +14.14 points, while GPT-4o and GPT-4.1 show ASRs of 72% and 68% respectively. White-box visual attacks (PGD, APGD, C&W) inflate review scores by 9.17–14.11 points across MLLMs, with APGD(ℓ∞) being the most potent (14.11 on Janus-Pro-7B). For defense, the proposed Chunk-based Embedding Search achieves 95.0% accuracy and 92.86% recall at 0.0% FPR on text attacks, and 93.5% accuracy / 90.32% recall at 0.10% FPR on visual attacks, substantially outperforming all baselines. On 17 real-world arXiv papers with confirmed hidden injections, the defense reaches 100% recall at 0% FPR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among defense strategies, the proposed Chunk-based Embedding Search dominates all baselines: it achieves 95.0% accuracy and 92.86% recall at 0.0% FPR on text attacks, versus the BERT sliding-window classifier (38.5% Acc, 0% Recall, 35% FPR) and the global Embedding Classifier (64.5% Acc, 0% Recall, 12% FPR), both of which completely fail to detect localized injections in long documents. LLM-as-Judge reaches high recall (90%) but suffers a catastrophic 100% FPR on text and 1% FPR on visual settings, making it impractical for real deployment where false accusations against legitimate authors are unacceptable. On visual attacks, Chunk-based retrieval (93.5% Acc, 90.32% Recall, 0.10% FPR) outperforms the Embedding Classifier (72.5% Acc, 0% Recall) and LLM-as-Judge (35.6% Acc, 90% Recall, 1% FPR). Against real-world hidden injections (17 confirmed arXiv papers), EmbSearch detects all 17 (100% recall, 0% FPR) while GPT-4o LLM-as-Judge catches only 11/17 (64.7%) and the Moderation API catches none (0/17). For white-box GCG strings, EmbSearch still leads with 95% recall at 2% FPR, outperforming even a GCG-aware LLM-as-Judge (75% recall, 8% FPR).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adversarial robustness evaluation paradigm (well-established for text classification and image classification) to the domain of AI-assisted scientific peer review, and further extends it from text-only to multimodal (text + figures). It builds on the LLM peer-review automation literature (Zhou et al., Du et al., MMReview) which evaluates review quality under benign inputs, and on the text-only robustness work of Lin et al. (2025, 'Breaking the Reviewer'), but departs from both by introducing a domain-specific threat model (score inflation rather than safety jailbreak) and by incorporating white-box visual perturbations (PGD, APGD, C&W) as a first-class attack vector. The defense side repurposes retrieval-augmented generation ideas (chunk-based embedding search with E5-V) into a 'needle-in-a-haystack' detection pipeline, contrasting with the global-classification and LLM-as-Judge approaches that prior work would naturally reach for.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

With AAAI, ICML, and NeurIPS now formally integrating AI-generated reviews into their initial screening, the demonstrated 80% ASR on Claude-sonnet-4.5 and the +14-point visual-attack inflation represent an immediate, deployable threat to scholarly integrity. The finding that imperceptible figure perturbations alone can skew reviews—without any text modification—means that text-only moderation pipelines currently in use are structurally insufficient. Open questions include whether the chunk-based defense generalizes to fully adversarial (adaptive) attacks that co-optimize against the retrieval database, whether attacks survive full PDF rasterization/OCR pipelines, and whether the score-inflation objective transfers to other high-stakes LLM evaluation settings (e.g., automated grading, hiring). The paper's practical defense (near-zero FPR, 95% recall) is a concrete starting point, but the arms-race dynamic between adaptive attackers and pattern-matching defenders remains unresolved.
