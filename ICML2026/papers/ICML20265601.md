## Problem

Existing EEG foundation models rely on waveform reconstruction or autoregressive objectives that are sensitive to high-frequency noise and stochastic fluctuations rather than consistent neurophysiological structure. While discrete tokenization has been explored for EEG, prior methods use discrete codes as encoder inputs or reconstruction targets rather than as abstract supervision signals for latent-space prediction, leaving a gap in how to combine noise-robust categorical constraints with fine-grained continuous representation learning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw multi-channel EEG recordings resampled to 200 Hz, bandpass-filtered (0.5–45 Hz), and spherically interpolated to a canonical 21-channel 10-20 montage, then segmented into non-overlapping 1-second patches (200 samples) yielding a sequence of C×N patch tokens. The pre-trained encoder outputs continuous 768-dimensional patch-level representations, while the frozen Stage-A tokenizer separately produces discrete code indices from an 8192-entry codebook. Critically, discrete codes never enter the encoder pathway—they serve exclusively as supervision targets, so the encoder always operates on continuous inputs while being regularized by noise-filtered categorical constraints.

## Method

PATCHCODE is a two-stage decoupled framework. Stage A trains a Region-Aware Tokenizer (6-layer Transformer + VQ codebook of 8192×256 entries) supervised by frequency-domain reconstruction (FFT amplitude and phase of the first 50 bins), region-wise contrastive regularization, and codebook usage entropy; after training the tokenizer is frozen and its decoder discarded. Stage B employs a Student-Teacher architecture with Region-Bias Transformer encoders (12 layers, 768-dim) under JEPA-style structured block masking (40% ratio, region-guided), where the Student processes masked continuous patches and is optimized through three complementary pathways: Patch-to-Patch (MSE against EMA Teacher's continuous representations), Patch-to-Code (cross-entropy predicting frozen tokenizer code indices), and Code-to-Patch (MSE predicting Student representations from frozen code embeddings, allowing gradients to flow back into the Student encoder). A learnable R×R region-bias table modulates attention logits to encode inter-region interaction priors, and the EMA Teacher (momentum 0.996→0.9999) provides stable, slowly-evolving targets that prevent representational collapse.

## Results

On SEED-V emotion recognition, PATCHCODE (Large) achieves 43.46% balanced accuracy, outperforming the strongest baseline CSBrain (41.97%) by 1.49 points. On BCI-IV-2a motor imagery, PATCHCODE (Large) reaches 58.42% balanced accuracy (vs. CSBrain 56.57%), and on HMC sleep staging it attains 75.56% balanced accuracy with Cohen's κ = 0.705. For CHB-MIT seizure detection, PATCHCODE achieves 90.85% AUC-ROC and 76.20% balanced accuracy, though CSBrain retains a lead on AUC-PR (0.516 vs. 0.391). With only 25% of labeled data, PATCHCODE matches or exceeds all baselines trained with full data on both SEED-V and BCI-IV-2a.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PATCHCODE (Large) outperforms all five compared baselines on balanced accuracy across all four main tasks. Against the supervised baselines EEGNet and EEGConformer, the gap is substantial: on SEED-V, PATCHCODE achieves 43.46% vs. 29.61% (EEGNet) and 35.37% (EEGConformer), and on HMC sleep staging 75.56% vs. 65.34% and 71.49% respectively. Among EEG foundation models, PATCHCODE surpasses BIOT (38.37% → 43.46% on SEED-V), LaBraM-Base (39.76% → 43.46%), and CBraMod (40.91% → 43.46%) on emotion recognition, with similar margins on motor imagery and sleep staging. The closest competitor is CSBrain, which PATCHCODE beats on balanced accuracy for SEED-V (+1.49%), BCI-IV-2a (+1.85%), HMC (+2.11%), and CHB-MIT (+3.58%), though CSBrain retains a lead on Cohen's κ for SEED-V (0.2785 vs. 0.2594) and AUC-PR for CHB-MIT (0.5164 vs. 0.3910). The data-efficiency results further show PATCHCODE with 25% labels (38.9% on SEED-V, 52.1% on BCI-IV-2a) exceeding CSBrain trained with 100% labels (42.0% and 56.6% respectively) on BCI-IV-2a and matching it on SEED-V.

## Relation to Prior Work

PATCHCODE sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) JEPA-style latent prediction (LeCun et al., 2022; Assran et al., 2023), which avoids waveform-level reconstruction by predicting continuous embeddings of masked regions; (2) discrete-token SSL from speech and vision (HuBERT, BEiT, wav2vec 2.0), where frozen quantized codes serve as noise-robust supervision targets; and (3) EEG foundation models (LaBraM, BIOT, CBraMod, CSBrain) that have predominantly relied on masked autoencoding or contrastive objectives. The key departure is that, unlike LaBraM which feeds discrete tokens into the encoder as BERT-style inputs, or reconstruction-based methods that recover raw waveforms, PATCHCODE keeps the encoder input fully continuous while using a frozen spectral tokenizer's discrete codes purely as classification targets—adapting the HuBERT/BEiT supervision paradigm to EEG's low-SNR, cross-subject-variability regime for the first time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical relevance is strongest in clinical and BCI settings where labeled EEG data is scarce: matching full-data baselines with only 25% of labels could meaningfully reduce annotation costs. The two-stage decoupled design, however, introduces an extra pre-training cost (100 epochs for the tokenizer) and relies on canonical 21-channel interpolation that may degrade performance for non-standard montages. Open questions include whether the discrete supervision advantage persists at larger scale (beyond 37M parameters), whether dynamic region partitioning based on functional connectivity could replace the fixed 10-20 anatomy, and whether multi-modal pre-training (EEG + MEG/fMRI) can further improve cross-subject transfer. The paper also acknowledges the need for prospective clinical validation before any real-world deployment.
