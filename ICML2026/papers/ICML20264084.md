## Problem

Existing forgery detection methods are confined to uni-modal or bi-modal settings, unable to process the interleaved text, image, and video content prevalent in real-world social media misinformation. Moreover, when unifying detection (binary classification) and grounding (fine-grained localization) into a single multi-task RL framework, a 'difficulty bias' emerges: the simpler classification objective dominates gradient updates, causing the more challenging localization tasks to stagnate or even degrade.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264084/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts interleaved social-media content comprising text passages, RGB images, video clips, and image-text pairs, and produces a structured output containing a chain-of-thought reasoning block followed by a final answer that includes a binary Real/Fake verdict plus, when applicable, a spatial bounding box (image tampering), a semantic text span (text tampering), or a temporal interval (video tampering). The backbone is Qwen3VL-8B fine-tuned with LoRA, and the output is constrained to a strict <think>...<answer>...</answer> format enforced by a format-compliance reward during RL.

## Method

OmniVL-Guard combines two core designs. First, a Self-Evolving CoT Generation pipeline iteratively (3 rounds) uses the current policy to generate reasoning paths on unlabeled data, filters them via ground-truth accuracy and SOTA-MLLM verification, and retrains from the base model each round to avoid distributional drift; a Multi-Agent Collaborative Hard-CoT Synthesis step then covers long-tail failure cases by having one MLLM generate GT-conditioned CoT, a second 'Refiner' rewrite it to remove hindsight bias, and a third judge validate the result. Second, ARSPO (Adaptive Reward Shaping Policy Optimization), built on SAPO, addresses the difficulty bias through two mechanisms: (1) task-specific reward mapping functions—identity for classification and a convex exponential g(x)=e^{ax} (a=3) for the three localization tasks—that amplify the gradient contribution of high-quality responses in regions where task-difficulty sensitivity H'_k is near zero; and (2) a Dynamic Coefficient Adjustment algorithm that periodically monitors per-task learning momentum and total relative gain, then boosts laggard tasks, rescues regressing tasks, and decays mastered tasks to maintain balanced multi-task optimization. The full pipeline trains via SFT cold-start on the 73k-sample FSFR_sft dataset followed by RL on the 110k-sample FSFR_rl dataset.

## Results

On the in-domain FSFR test set (~700k samples), OmniVL-Guard achieves 96.20% / 93.12% / 98.58% / 75.52% binary classification accuracy on Text / Image / Video / Text-Image modalities, and 54.26 IoU / 63.78 F1 / 59.22 tIoU on image / text / video tampering localization, respectively. The most striking gains are in fine-grained grounding: +37.79 tIoU over the best video localization baseline and +22.92 F1 over the best text localization baseline. On out-of-domain zero-shot benchmarks, OmniVL-Guard reaches 79.38% on MM-FakeBench (Text-Image) and 63.55% on FakeSV (Text-Video), outperforming all domain-specific methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OmniVL-Guard outperforms both general-purpose MLLMs (Qwen3VL-235B, Llama3.2-90B) by large margins on all localization tasks (e.g., +37.79 tIoU over Qwen3VL on video localization) while also surpassing them on binary classification. Against domain-specific image-text methods, it beats HAMMER (75.52 vs 71.23 ACC), FKA-Owl (75.52 vs 72.08), and AMD (75.52 vs 65.34) on Text-Image classification, and dominates on grounding (54.26 IoU vs HAMMER's 48.53; 63.78 F1 vs HAMMER's 40.86). For unimodal baselines, OmniVL-Guard exceeds Fake-VLM (93.12 vs 90.39) and D3 (93.12 vs 88.46) on image classification, and Video-R1 (98.58 vs 96.44) on video classification, though it trails FakeSV-VLM by a narrow 0.23 points on video binary classification (98.58 vs 98.81). On text, it surpasses BERT (96.20 vs 61.45) and LUKE (96.20 vs 63.72) by a wide margin. On out-of-domain zero-shot evaluation, OmniVL-Guard leads all baselines on MM-FakeBench (79.38 vs FKA-Owl's 62.32) and FakeSV (63.55 vs Video-R1's 55.32), confirming that RL-enhanced reasoning transfers to unseen forgery types.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DeepSeek-R1/GRPO reinforcement learning paradigm from text-only reasoning to multimodal forensic detection, building directly on SAPO as the base policy optimization algorithm. It departs from prior forgery detection work (HAMMER, Fake-VLM, D3, Video-R1) which is confined to single-modality or image-text pairs, by unifying text, image, video, and image-text detection and grounding in one VLM framework. The key methodological departure is the theoretical analysis of multi-task gradient dynamics (the 'Matthew Effect'), which motivates ARSPO's nonlinear reward mapping and dynamic coefficient adjustment—mechanisms absent from standard GRPO/SAPO formulations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first unified framework handling all major social-media modalities (text, image, video, image-text) for both forgery classification and fine-grained manipulation grounding, addressing a real gap in the misinformation defense landscape. The theoretical decomposition of why simpler tasks dominate multi-task RL gradients, and the demonstration that convex reward mapping helps even in single-task settings, offers a generalizable principle for multi-task RL beyond forensics. Open questions include whether the approach scales to additional modalities (audio, 3D), whether the 3-iteration self-evolution loop is optimal for other domains, and whether the 700k-sample test set adequately captures the diversity of real-world manipulation techniques.
