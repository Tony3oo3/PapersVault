## Problem

Existing CoT-based VLA models face two fundamental limitations: explicit textual CoT generates long autoregressive token sequences that inflate inference latency to below 5 Hz (unacceptable for real-time control), and both textual and visual CoT rely on discrete token representations that mismatch the inherently continuous perception and action spaces of robotics. No prior method internalizes both textual and future-oriented visual CoT into continuous latent representations that directly condition action generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260182/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes multi-view RGB images (three RGB-D cameras on the real-robot platform) and a natural-language instruction, encoded by Qwen3-VL's image encoder into visual tokens. At inference time, no explicit CoT tokens are generated; instead, a single compact latent reasoning token per step and a predicted future visual latent (via an <img_next> token) condition a 16-layer Diffusion Transformer that outputs a continuous action trajectory. The action horizon is task-dependent: 8 steps for LIBERO, 16 for Bridge/SimplerEnv, and 25 for real-robot tasks, with inference latency of 135 ms per rollout on an A100 GPU.

## Method

LaRA-VLA builds on Qwen3-VL as the backbone VLM and introduces a three-stage curriculum that progressively internalizes explicit multimodal CoT into continuous latent space. Stage I fine-tunes the VLM with explicit textual CoT supervision (negative log-likelihood), L1 visual latent prediction loss (stabilized by an EMA target encoder), and discrete action tokens via the FAST tokenizer with inverse-dynamics grounding. Stage II replaces discrete CoT tokens with learnable latent representations on a curriculum schedule, using visual prediction as implicit supervision while annealing the explicit CoT loss to zero. Stage III removes autoregressive action decoding entirely and trains a 16-layer Diffusion Transformer action expert with a flow-matching objective, conditioned on the multi-modal latent context (current visual features, text latent, and predicted future visual latent). A tailored attention mechanism enforces causal and bidirectional information flow across text, current-image, future-image, and action tokens, and the visual goal latents serve as implicit multimodal supervision that regularizes the textual latent reasoning, yielding up to 90% inference speedup over explicit CoT baselines.

## Results

On LIBERO, LaRA-VLA achieves the best average success rate of 97.9%, with standout scores of 99.8% on the Object suite and 96.6% on the Long suite, surpassing the strongest No-CoT baseline (OpenVLA-OFT, 97.1%) and the best textual CoT method (DeepThinkVLA, 97.0%). On SimplerEnv-WidowX, LaRA-VLA attains 68.8% average success rate, outperforming the best visual CoT baseline (UD-VLA, 62.5%) and the best No-CoT method (CogACT, 51.3%). Inference latency is only 135 ms per rollout on an A100 GPU, reducing inference time by up to 90% compared to explicit CoT-based approaches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On LIBERO, LaRA-VLA (97.9% avg) outperforms all 13 baselines across four CoT paradigms. It edges out the closest competitors—OpenVLA-OFT (97.1%) and DeepThinkVLA (97.0%)—by 0.8 and 0.9 points respectively, while achieving a decisive 8.2-point margin over the only other latent CoT method, Fast-ThinkAct (89.7%). On SimplerEnv-WidowX, LaRA-VLA (68.8%) leads UD-VLA (62.5%) by 6.3 points and CogACT (51.3%) by 17.5 points, with particularly large gains on Put Spoon (95.8% vs. 71.7% for CogACT). In real-robot experiments, LaRA-VLA substantially outperforms ACT (0% on all four tasks) and ECoT, and surpasses GR00T N1.5 overall, with the largest gap on the tightly coupled 'Find the Block and Place It in the Basket' task (33.3% vs. 16.7%). Inference efficiency is a key differentiator: LaRA-VLA runs at 135 ms per rollout, up to 90% faster than explicit CoT methods like ECoT, and also faster than Fast-ThinkAct, which also uses latent reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

LaRA-VLA extends the latent chain-of-thought paradigm from language-only settings (Coconut, SIM-CoT, CoDi) to embodied VLA models, adapting continuous latent reasoning to the joint perception-action space of robotics. It departs from Fast-ThinkAct, which only latentizes textual CoT while retaining discrete visual traces, by internalizing both textual and future-oriented visual CoT into continuous latents, with visual goal latents providing implicit multimodal supervision for textual reasoning. The action generation pipeline builds on FAST's recursive action tokenization for Stages I–II and introduces a 16-layer Diffusion Transformer action expert with flow matching for Stage III, while the EMA-stabilized visual latent prediction follows the VJepa-style target network design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 90% inference latency reduction (135 ms per rollout) addresses a critical bottleneck for real-time robotic control, where explicit CoT methods operate at 1–5 Hz. The robustness gap under visual perturbations (e.g., 56.3% vs. 13.5% on SimplerEnv under high-severity Gaussian blur) suggests latent representations may be inherently more stable than discrete token-based reasoning, though the single-latent-token-per-step constraint limits expressiveness and the mixed real-world results (e.g., 41.6% vs. 50.0% on Put All Objects vs. GR00T N1.5) indicate the approach has not yet fully closed the gap on all long-horizon tasks. Open questions include whether multi-token latent reasoning can scale without collapse, whether the curriculum training overhead can be reduced, and whether the continuous-latent advantage persists in more complex multi-robot or multi-task settings.
