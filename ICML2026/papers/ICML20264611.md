## Problem

Vision-language models remain highly vulnerable to multimodal jailbreak attacks because visual inputs dilute the risk signals that the LLM backbone inherently recognizes in text. Existing defenses either require costly safety fine-tuning prone to catastrophic forgetting or apply aggressive, global token manipulations that significantly degrade the model's utility on standard visual understanding tasks. The paper identifies the root cause as a token-level 'Semantic Gap' where visual tokens carrying harmful content fail to project into the LLM's unsafe semantic space and progressively attenuate across transformer layers.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard image or video inputs alongside a text prompt, operating entirely during the prefill stage before any autoregressive decoding begins. It extracts the visual token hidden representations (H_v ∈ R^{L_v × d}) and computes a token-level cosine similarity matrix against a pre-defined Unsafe Prototype Subspace, then modifies only a sparse subset (typically 0.5%–1%) of visual tokens whose similarity exceeds a threshold τ. The output is a standard text response, but with the LLM's latent safety mechanisms activated so that harmful requests are refused while benign queries proceed normally.

## Method

RAI is a training-free, one-time injection framework that bridges the visual-textual semantic gap at the initial LLM layer (Layer 0). It first constructs an Unsafe Prototype Subspace by extracting embedding vectors for K=9 representative risk keywords (e.g., violence, illegal, sexual, hateful, cybercrime, misinfo, fraud, self-harm, weapons) directly from the model's own token embedding matrix. Each visual token is then scored via cosine similarity against this subspace, and only tokens exceeding a threshold τ are selected for calibration. The selected tokens undergo a sparse additive modulation h'_v = h_v + Σ_{k∈K_v} s_{v,k} · u_k/‖u_k‖, injecting risk-aligned semantic directions proportional to each token's similarity with the corresponding risk category. Because the intervention is strictly additive, confined to a minimal token subset, and executed once at the input layer, it amplifies safety-critical signals before they attenuate while preserving the global visual semantic structure needed for cross-modal reasoning.

## Results

On MM-SafetyBench, RAI achieves a perfect 0.00% average ASR on Qwen3-VL-8B, reducing it from 7.47% (Original) and 2.24% (ShiftDC). On the most vulnerable model, LLaVA-1.6-7B, RAI drops ASR from 49.42% to 3.62%, outperforming ShiftDC (14.83%) by a factor of four. On DeepSeek-VL-7B, RAI achieves 4.32% ASR versus ShiftDC's 8.48%. Utility is preserved: MME scores remain within 1 point of the original (e.g., 610.64 vs. 611.78 on Qwen2.5-VL), and MM-Vet drops by ≤0.4 points, while inference overhead is only +13.2% versus +54.0% for ShiftDC and +117.9% for CoCA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAI outperforms all four baselines on ASR across every model and benchmark. Against the strongest baseline ShiftDC (Zou et al., 2025), RAI achieves roughly 2× lower ASR on DeepSeek-VL (4.32% vs. 8.48%) and ~4× lower on LLaVA-1.6 (3.62% vs. 14.83%), while matching or exceeding it on Qwen3-VL (0.00% vs. 2.24%). AdaShield (Wang et al., 2024), a prompt-based method, is consistently outperformed by a wide margin (e.g., 3.62% vs. 22.24% on LLaVA-1.6). CoCA (Gao et al., 2024) and ECSO (Gou et al., 2024) provide only marginal improvements over the original models (e.g., CoCA reduces LLaVA-1.6 ASR from 49.42% to 44.48%), indicating that logits adjustment and image-to-text conversion are insufficient against sophisticated multimodal jailbreaks. On utility, RAI also edges out ShiftDC on MM-Vet (59.9 vs. 57.2 on Qwen3-VL) and maintains MME scores closest to the original, whereas CoCA and ECSO show larger utility regressions. Inference latency further favors RAI: +13.2% overhead versus +54.0% (ShiftDC) and +117.9% (CoCA).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the training-free VLM defense literature, departing from both prompt-based methods (AdaShield, ECSO) that operate at the input level and representation-level methods (ShiftDC) that apply coarse, image-level calibration. Its key conceptual departure is the identification of a token-level 'Semantic Gap'—where visual tokens progressively lose alignment with the LLM's unsafe semantic space across layers—and the use of the model's own language embeddings to construct an Unsafe Prototype Subspace for targeted, sparse injection at Layer 0. Unlike SafePTR's subtractive pruning paradigm, RAI is purely additive and confined to a minimal subset of risk-bearing tokens, preserving global visual semantics. The approach draws on activation steering ideas from LLM safety but repurposes them for cross-modal risk signal restoration rather than global direction control.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RAI offers a practically attractive safety mechanism: training-free, low-overhead (13.2% vs. 54% for ShiftDC), and near-lossless on utility benchmarks, making it deployable without retraining pipelines. The empirical diagnosis of progressive risk-signal attenuation across layers provides a useful mechanistic explanation for why VLMs fail at safety that could inform future projector or alignment design. Open questions include robustness to novel attack categories outside the 9 predefined risk tokens, applicability to models with weaker text-based safety alignment, and whether the sparse gating threshold generalizes across architectures without per-model tuning. The perfect 0.00% ASR on Qwen3-VL, while impressive, may partly reflect that model's already-strong baseline safety, leaving the method's ceiling on weaker models less clear.
