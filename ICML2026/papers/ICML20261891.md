## Problem

Existing self-evolving training frameworks for Large Multimodal Models rely on heuristic signals (e.g., perplexity, entropy) rather than explicit failure attribution, and depend on static image sets that limit visual diversity. This causes the evolutionary process to pursue superficial complexity instead of addressing genuine capability gaps, leading to unstable training dynamics, distribution drift, and performance plateau or regression on long-tail tasks such as visual math and OCR.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261891/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The trained model takes a single RGB image or a multi-image composition together with a text question and produces a text answer. During data generation, the Image Selector agent retrieves images from an external pool via the Serper API (top-3 per search) and applies editing/composition through Qwen-Image-Edit, so visual inputs are not constrained to a fixed dataset. Generated answers must be verifiable (e.g., numeric, multiple-choice with explicit options, or structured format) to support GRPO's binary reward signal, and a Validation agent enforces category consistency, solvability, answer verifiability, and format compliance before a sample enters the training set.

## Method

DPE is a closed-loop iterative training framework with three operators per round: a diagnostic agent samples 200 instances across 12 capability dimensions (geometry, charts, formulas, OCR, etc.), computes per-category accuracy, attributes failures to recurring error patterns, and outputs a category proportion vector plus actionable generation instructions; a four-agent questioner system (Planner, Image Selector, Question Generator, Validation) converts this report into a training set with hard category-quota constraints and quality gating, sourcing and editing images from external pools to expand visual coverage; and the model is updated via GRPO with group-normalized advantages and difficulty-aware filtering that retains only moderately difficult samples (pass rate near 0.5) to maximize per-example learning signal. The key distinction from prior self-evolution methods is that diagnosis explicitly steers both the data mixture and the question direction toward identified weaknesses, rather than relying on static distributions or generic quality metrics, and the image retrieval/editing pipeline breaks the visual diversity bottleneck inherent in fixed image sets.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct, DPE achieves a 59.29 average across 11 benchmarks after three iterations, outperforming VisPlay (58.33) and the base model (57.29). On the stronger Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct backbone, DPE reaches 68.04 average (vs. 65.64 base), with standout scores of 69.11 on MMMU, 72.13 on MMStar, 53.88 on MathVision, 76.2 on MathVista, and 74.13 on HallusionBench. The 8B DPE model attains a 64.39 average, surpassing the 72B-parameter Qwen2.5-VL (61.9) and GPT-4o (56.1) on the same benchmark subset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against VisPlay (He et al., 2025), DPE wins on 9 of 11 benchmarks for Qwen2.5-VL-7B, with the largest gap on CharXiv_RQ (40.91 vs. 37.00, +3.91) and HallusionBench (69.19 vs. 68.24, +0.95); VisPlay shows notable oscillation (e.g., MMMU drops to 49.3 at Iter2) while DPE maintains a monotonic upward trend. Against Qwen2.5-VL-72B (Bai et al., 2025b), the 8B DPE model surpasses the 72B model on MathVision (53.88 vs. 38.1, +15.78) and MathVista (76.2 vs. 74.8, +1.4), and achieves a higher overall average (64.39 vs. 61.9), though it trails on MMMU (69.11 vs. 70.2) and CharXiv (48.1 vs. 49.7). Against static Vision-R1 training (Li et al., 2025a) with 47K samples, DPE using only ~3K iteratively generated samples outperforms on all four reported benchmarks: MMMU (56.44 vs. 54.8), HallusionBench (69.0 vs. 67.6), MathVista (69.5 vs. 68.8), and RealWorldQA (70.5 vs. 69.9), demonstrating that targeted diagnostic-driven data is more effective than 15× more static data.

## Relation to Prior Work

DPE extends the self-evolving multimodal training line (VisPlay, M-STAR, EvoLMM, IREASONER) by replacing their heuristic, 'blind' data selection with an explicit diagnostic loop that performs failure attribution across 12 capability dimensions and converts the result into category-quota constraints for the next generation round. It also departs from prior self-evolving methods that rewrite text over a fixed image set by introducing a multi-agent questioner system with external image retrieval (Serper) and editing (Qwen-Image-Edit), thereby decoupling visual diversity from the seed dataset. The RL optimization stage uses GRPO with verifiable rewards, consistent with the broader RLVR trend in LMM post-training (VLM-R1, Vision-SR1, SRPO), but the key architectural novelty is the closed-loop coupling between diagnosis, targeted generation, and policy update.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that ~3K diagnostically targeted samples can match or exceed 47K static training samples, suggesting that data distribution alignment matters more than volume for long-tail multimodal reasoning. The ablation showing that removing the diagnostic module causes performance to plateau or regress (e.g., CharXiv oscillating around baseline vs. steady 4-point gain) provides concrete evidence that explicit failure attribution is necessary for stable iterative improvement. Open questions include whether the 12-category diagnostic space generalizes to new task domains, whether gains persist beyond three iterations, and the computational cost of running four frontier LLM agents per generation round. The framework's reliance on external image search also raises reproducibility and licensing considerations that the paper does not fully address.
