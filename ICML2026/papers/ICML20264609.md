## Problem

Existing semi-supervised continual learning methods enforce dense pseudo-label consistency and indiscriminate distillation across all feature dimensions, which amplifies pseudo-label noise into feature drift, while their class-agnostic regularization fails to separate novel classes from confusable base classes, causing boundary erosion. The paper addresses the co-occurrence of both issues under class-incremental settings by grounding continual updates on reliable discrete anchors that remain stable under noisy pseudo-labels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264609/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each task t, the method receives a small labeled set (e.g., 1–25% of per-class samples) and a large unlabeled stream of RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR, 256×256 for ImageNet-100), processed through a ResNet backbone with a 512-dim projection head. A frozen teacher from the previous task and a replay buffer of exemplars are also consumed. The output is a class label over the expanding class set, with the student model updated to balance base-class retention and novel-class acquisition.

## Method

DiL introduces two coupled modules. Discrete Contrastive Distillation (DCD) computes per-class importance masks from activation magnitude and frequency over labeled samples, selects top-K channels to form sparse discrete anchor prototypes, then performs partitioned KL distillation (separate weights for base vs. novel class sub-distributions) on a leaky-masked feature representation, suppressing noisy gradients from uninformative channels. Class-Aware Channel-Chunked Encoding (CACE) partitions the 512-dim feature space into B disjoint chunks, assigns each class a k-chunk support set, and for novel classes selects the chunk combination that minimizes overlap with the most confusable base class (identified via cosine similarity of masked prototypes), then supervises features toward Gaussian-noise anchors on the active chunks. The total loss combines standard supervised cross-entropy, FixMatch-style pseudo-label consistency, replay distillation, DCD, and CACE terms, all optimized jointly per task.

## Results

On the CIFAR-100-20 benchmark (10 novel classes per task, 20 labels per class), DiL (iCaRL&Fix) achieves 56.16% average and 45.55% last-task accuracy, surpassing the prior SOTA USP (53.20%/41.30%) by 2.96%/4.25% and DSGD (52.80%/35.47%) by 3.36%/10.08%. On the 20-task ImageNet-100 protocol with only 1% labels, DiL (DER&Fix) reaches 56.07% average accuracy versus 41.22% for DER&Fix+USP, a 14.85% gain. On CUB-200 (11-task few-shot), DiL narrows the base-novel accuracy gap to 3.18% (vs. >10% for USP and UaD-CIE) and achieves 68.22% average novel-class accuracy, well above USP's 55.41%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiL consistently outperforms the primary SOTA competitor USP across all four datasets: on CIFAR100-20 it gains +2.96% Avg / +4.25% Last, on CIFAR100-80 it gains +7.78% Avg / +8.12% Last, and on ImageNet100-100 (10-task) it gains +18.06% Avg / +14.38% Last (iCaRL&Fix). Against DSGD, the gap widens substantially on longer-horizon benchmarks (e.g., +8.45% Last on CIFAR100-80, +12.13% Last on CIFAR100-125), confirming that selective channel distillation and confusion-aware encoding become increasingly valuable as task sequences grow. On CUB-200, DiL improves novel-class average accuracy from 55.41% (USP) and 44.42% (UaD-CIE) to 68.22%, while maintaining competitive base-class accuracy (71.40% vs. 72.03% for UaD-CIE). DiL slightly trails USP on CIFAR10-30 with the DER&Fix backbone (81.39 vs. 81.43 Avg) but leads on all other settings. Older baselines (iCaRL, DER, CCIC, ORDisCo, NNCSL) are substantially outperformed, with gaps exceeding 20% in Avg accuracy on CIFAR-100 settings, underscoring the difficulty of the SSCL protocol.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiL builds on the FixMatch semi-supervised pipeline (weak-strong augmentation with confidence-thresholded pseudo-labels) and the teacher-student continual learning paradigm, but departs from prior SSCL methods that enforce dense full-dimensional distillation or indiscriminate geometric regularization. It draws conceptually from noisy-label learning (selective reliable-sample usage à la RankMatch/OT-Filter), ECOC-style discrete codeword assignment, and prototype anchoring, unifying these into a single framework where channel-selected distillation (DCD) suppresses pseudo-label noise propagation and confusion-aware block-sparse encoding (CACE) proactively isolates novel classes from confusable base classes. The key positioning is that prior work addresses either feature stability or geometric separation in isolation, whereas DiL couples both through shared discrete anchor prototypes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the two dominant failure modes in SSCL—feature drift from noisy pseudo-labels and boundary erosion from class confusion—can be simultaneously mitigated without additional parameters or inference cost, with particularly large gains under extreme label scarcity (14.85% Avg improvement over USP on 20-task ImageNet-100 at 1% labels). The reduced base-novel accuracy gap on CUB-200 (3.18% vs. >10% for baselines) suggests the method addresses a practical imbalance that limits real-world deployment. Open questions include whether the fixed channel-selection and block-assignment strategies remain effective over very long task sequences or under non-stationary data distributions, which the authors acknowledge as future work.
