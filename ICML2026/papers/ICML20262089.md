## Problem

Continuous diffusion language models denoise an entire sequence jointly in a continuous latent space and never maintain an explicit discrete prefix, so the core primitive of autoregressive constrained decoding—masking next-token choices that make a prefix uncompletable—does not transfer. Existing steering methods for diffusion either target specialized discrete masking variants or require training a separate classifier per constraint, leaving a gap for training-free, principled enforcement of formal syntactic constraints (e.g., regular expressions) on continuous diffusion models.

## Input / Output

The method takes a regular expression (converted to a DFA and then aligned to the model's tokenizer vocabulary via the Align procedure) and a fixed sequence length (64 tokens) as inputs, and produces a single text sequence decoded by argmax from the final unigram distribution after all denoising timesteps complete. Unlike discrete diffusion models that decode at every step, PLAID/DIFFINITY operates entirely in a 32-dimensional word2vec latent space throughout denoising and only maps to discrete tokens once at the end. The regex is consumed analytically through dynamic programming over weighted transition matrices rather than by filtering individual token choices.

## Method

DIFFINITY modifies the DDPM reverse denoising step by adding a guidance term γσ²_t ∇_{x_t} log E_{s~Dec(x_t)}[s ∈ L], where the expectation is computed analytically via dynamic programming over the vocabulary-aligned DFA: at each position k, a transition matrix M_k is built from the unigram distribution Dec(x_t) and the automaton's transitions, and the expected acceptance probability is p_0·M_1·…·M_l read off the accepting state. A key technical component is the Align(A, V) procedure, which factorizes the character-level DFA into character transitions, then augments it with multi-character token transitions whenever the underlying character path exists, ensuring all valid tokenizations of a string are counted. This replaces the learned classifier in standard classifier guidance with an exact, training-free computation, so the sampler approximates the base model conditioned on syntactic validity without any auxiliary training. The gradient is backpropagated through the PLAID transformer decoder to obtain updates in the word2vec latent space, and the guidance scale γ (set to 2.5) controls the strength of constraint enforcement.

## Results

DIFFINITY achieves an average constraint satisfaction rate of 92.9% across 110 natural-language regex benchmarks (ranging from 85.5% on Between-n to 96.8% on Suffix) and 68.4% on 70 JSON-schema regexes, with pass@10 rates of 95–100% and 91.4% respectively. The perplexity cost of constrained generation is minimal: DIFFINITY's average perplexity is 60.0 versus 61.6 for unconstrained PLAID, and fluency is 35.8 versus 46.0 unconstrained. On the JSON benchmark, DIFFINITY (68.4% Sat.) is mildly outperformed by GPT2-Large+Guidance (77.0%) but achieves comparable pass@10 (91.4% vs. 98.6%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GPT2-Large+Guidance (autoregressive constrained decoding), DIFFINITY wins decisively on constraint satisfaction (92.9% vs. 20.5% average) and perplexity (60.0 vs. 124.4 average), while matching or exceeding fluency (35.8 vs. 37.2); GPT2 models suffer from the 'infinite repetition trap' under finite token budgets. Against LLaDA+DINGO (discrete diffusion + constrained decoding), DIFFINITY trades a small satisfaction gap (92.9% vs. ~100%) for a massive quality advantage: perplexity drops from 222.8 to 60.0 and fluency from 3.6 to 35.8, indicating DINGO severely distorts the base model distribution. Against PLAID's native token-level guidance, DIFFINITY achieves 92.9% satisfaction versus 59.4% and produces lower minimum-perplexity samples (10.4 vs. 25.7). Against trained classifier guidance (three variants), DIFFINITY is overwhelmingly superior: classifier guidance achieves only 2.27% satisfaction on natural-language benchmarks and 0% on JSON, despite classifiers reaching AUC 0.74–0.81, because the learned proxy score does not align with the binary constraint-satisfaction metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is a training-free instantiation of classifier guidance (Dhariwal & Nichol, 2021; Song et al., 2020) for continuous diffusion language models, replacing the learned classifier with an analytical dynamic-programming computation over a DFA. It builds directly on PLAID (Gulrajani & Hashimoto, 2023) as the base model and modifies its DDPM denoising equation (Ho et al., 2020) with a gradient term derived from the expected acceptance probability. The key departure from prior constrained-decoding work (GCD for autoregressive models, DINGO for discrete masking diffusion) is that the method operates on continuous latent representations without requiring an explicit discrete prefix, and introduces a tokenizer-automaton alignment step to bridge character-level regex definitions with token-level model vocabularies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to steer continuous diffusion models to satisfy arbitrary regular-expression constraints without training auxiliary classifiers, and it demonstrates that the resulting sampler preserves the base model's distribution far better than autoregressive constrained decoding (which suffers from the infinite repetition trap and distribution distortion). The counterintuitive finding that stronger guidance *improves* perplexity suggests that constraining the diffusion trajectory to stay near the target language manifold suppresses unstable denoising paths, a mechanism that could generalize to other structured-generation settings. Key limitations include significant computational overhead (2–3× latency, memory pressure from large transition matrices), evaluation only on the small PLAID model, and support only for regular (not context-free) constraints; scaling to larger continuous diffusion models and developing memory-efficient automaton representations remain open.
