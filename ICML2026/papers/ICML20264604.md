## Problem

Unified discrete VLA frameworks that treat actions as tokens within a VLM have consistently underperformed decoupled continuous designs (diffusion or flow policy heads) due to compounding errors from single-step autoregressive action generation and slow inference. This gap has reinforced the assumption that continuous action modeling is essential for high-performance manipulation, despite the architectural simplicity and optimization ecosystem that a unified next-token prediction framework would provide.

## Input / Output

The model consumes interleaved sequences of variable-resolution RGB images (processed by Qwen2.5-VL's ViT with 28×2 spatial compression), natural language instructions, 7-DoF proprioceptive states (XYZ, RPY, gripper), and past action tokens, with a configurable history window (default 5 timesteps). The primary output is a chunk of 10 future actions, each discretized into 7 tokens (one per DoF) from a 256-bin vocabulary; auxiliary outputs include VQA text responses, bounding box coordinates, and 2D end-effector point traces for co-training tasks.

## Method

RoboOmni's core contribution is Multi-Token Action Prediction (MTAP), which replaces sequential autoregressive action decoding with H parallel projection heads (lightweight MLPs) that each map the shared final-layer hidden state to a distinct action step, enabling a full action chunk to be generated in a single forward pass. For Bin tokenization, MTAP acts as a temporal compressor trading marginal parameter overhead for a ~9× inference speedup (107→12.1 ms/action); for FAST tokenization, it serves as an auxiliary multi-token prediction objective to aid backbone convergence on frequency-domain tokens. The architecture preserves the native causal attention of the Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone for the interleaved history, while MTAP's parallel heads operate only on the action prediction step, avoiding any modification to the VLM's attention structure. Training employs sequence packing across trajectories (with cross-trajectory visibility to encourage context-dependent inference), Classifier-Free Guidance (20% instruction dropout), and multi-modal co-training on VQA, visual grounding, trace prediction, and video captioning tasks alongside the primary action prediction objective.

## Results

On the CALVIN benchmark (Train: ABCD, Eval: D), RoboOmni(FAST) achieves a 94.4% average success rate across 1–5 consecutive tasks, with an 88.1% 5-task success rate and a task length of 4.73, establishing a new state-of-the-art for unified discrete-token VLA models. On SimplerEnv (Google Robot, Visual Matching), RoboOmni(FAST) reaches an 86.8% average success rate across three tasks, surpassing the strongest continuous baseline SpatialVLA (70.0%) by over 16 percentage points. In real-world experiments on a Kinova Gen-3 arm, RoboOmni achieves a 91% average success rate across four generalization settings, including 91% on unseen objects where the next-best baseline (π₀-FAST) drops to 61%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RoboOmni(FAST) outperforms the strongest unified discrete baseline π₀-FAST by 7.8 percentage points on CALVIN 5-task success (88.1% vs. 80.3%) using the identical FAST tokenizer and data mixture, isolating the gain to architectural design (MTAP + interleaved history + co-training). Against the decoupled continuous baseline RoboVLMs, RoboOmni(FAST) leads by 5.5 points (88.1% vs. 82.6%), directly challenging the assumption that continuous action heads are necessary for top manipulation performance. Compared to the single-frame OpenVLA, RoboOmni(Bin) improves 5-task success from 34.6% to 83.4% (+48.8 pp), demonstrating the critical role of historical context and MTAP action chunking. On SimplerEnv, RoboOmni(FAST) (86.8%) surpasses SpatialVLA (70.0%) by 16.8 pp and π₀-FAST (61.9%) by 24.9 pp, indicating superior real-to-sim visual robustness. In real-world experiments, RoboOmni maintains 91% success on unseen objects while π₀-FAST drops to 61% and RoboVLMs to 55%, highlighting the generalization advantage of the unified co-training paradigm.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the unified discrete VLA line (RT-2, OpenVLA) and the decoupled continuous line (π₀, RoboFlamingo, RoboVLMs), but departs from both by arguing that the compounding-error and speed limitations of prior unified approaches stem from single-step autoregressive action decoding rather than from discreteness itself. The key technical departure is Multi-Token Action Prediction (MTAP), adapted from multi-token prediction in LLM training (Gloeckle et al., 2024; DeepSeek-V3), which enables parallel action-chunk decoding without modifying the VLM's causal attention. It also integrates VLM-native training optimizations—sequence packing, classifier-free guidance, and multi-modal co-training—that prior VLA systems typically forgo when adding decoupled policy heads.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a properly optimized discrete unified framework can outperform continuous decoupled baselines (π₀, RoboVLMs) across CALVIN, SimplerEnv, and real-robot settings, the paper challenges the field's working assumption that continuous action spaces are necessary for high-performance manipulation. The 27× inference speedup over OpenVLA and 6.6× over RoboFlamingo make long-context multi-modal policies practically deployable at real-time rates. Open questions include whether the advantage persists at larger scales (100B+ backbones), across more diverse embodiments, and in safety-critical unstructured environments where the paper itself cautions that benchmark success does not guarantee safe operation.
