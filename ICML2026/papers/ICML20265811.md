## Problem

Existing knowledge distillation methods for LLMs are designed for supervised fine-tuning and rely on fixed teacher traces or teacher-student KL regularization. When integrated into RL post-training, these approaches suffer from distribution mismatch (teacher supervision misaligned with the student's evolving rollout distribution) and objective interference (the KL regularizer competes with reward maximization, requiring fragile hyperparameter balancing). The paper asks how to incorporate teacher guidance into RL in a way that is selective, advantage-aware, and stable.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt x and produces a chain-of-thought reasoning response y generated autoregressively by the student policy. During training, the student samples a group of G responses per prompt (group size 8), and the teacher model is queried only to compute token-level log-probabilities on these student rollouts (not to generate its own trajectories). Context lengths range from 2K to 30K tokens depending on the task, with student models from 0.6B to 8B parameters and teachers up to 32B. At inference time, no teacher is required.

## Method

RLAD introduces Trust Region Ratio Distillation (TRRD), which replaces the standard GRPO importance ratio π_θS/π_θS,old with a geometric interpolation (π_θS/π_θS,old)^α · (π_θS/π_θT)^(1−α), effectively centering the PPO-style trust region on a teacher–old-student mixture distribution rather than the old student alone. This makes teacher influence advantage-modulated: under positive advantage, high teacher probability allows larger policy increases before clipping saturates, while under negative advantage, high teacher probability prevents aggressive suppression of teacher-preferred tokens. The GRPO-style clipping on the TRRD ratio bounds the log-probability shift relative to the mixture anchor, yielding stable, trust-region-constrained updates. Equivalently, TRRD optimizes a convex combination of KL to the old student and KL to the teacher, but only through the advantage-weighted ratio rather than as a separate loss term, eliminating the multi-objective trade-off that plagues KDRL. The mixing coefficient α (fixed at 0.5) interpolates between pure GRPO (α=1) and a DPO-like teacher-referenced objective (α→0).

## Results

On logical reasoning (K&K Logistics, 8K context), RLAD improves average accuracy from 0.76 to 0.94 for Qwen3-0.6B (+18%) and from 0.95 to 0.99 for Qwen3-1.7B (+4%) over GRPO, while also outperforming KDRL (0.92 and 0.96 respectively). On long-context math reasoning (30K, base models), RLAD raises the average across five benchmarks by +2.5 for Qwen3-1.7B-Base (39.0 vs. 36.5 GRPO) and +5.5 for Qwen3-8B-Base (66.5 vs. 61.0 GRPO), with the largest gains on AIME25-Pass@32 (48.5→66.4 for 8B-Base). For post-trained models at 8K, RLAD achieves +6.6 average improvement for Qwen3-1.7B (57.8 vs. 51.2) and +2.6 for Qwen2.5-1.5B-DS (59.1 vs. 56.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RLAD consistently outperforms GRPO across all settings: on logical reasoning it gains +18% (0.6B) and +4% (1.7B) average accuracy at 8K, and on math reasoning it gains +2.5 to +6.6 in average score depending on model size and context length. Against KDRL (explicit KL-based on-policy distillation), RLAD wins on average accuracy in nearly all configurations (e.g., 0.94 vs. 0.92 for Qwen3-0.6B on K&K; 66.5 vs. 64.9 for Qwen3-8B-Base on math), with the largest margins on the hardest subsets (PPL7/PPL8 in logic; AIME24/25 in math). A notable distinction is that KDRL tends to produce larger relative gains in Pass@32 than Pass@1, suggesting its improvements are driven by teacher imitation rather than reward-driven policy optimization, whereas RLAD yields stronger Pass@1 gains and more stable training dynamics. On the weak-teacher ablation (Qwen3-0.6B teacher for Qwen3-1.7B student), RLAD stays close to GRPO (0.946 vs. 0.950 avg) while KDRL degrades more noticeably (0.910), confirming that TRRD's trust-region design prevents a weaker teacher from harming the student.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GRPO/PPO trust-region policy optimization framework (Shao et al., 2024; Schulman et al., 2017) by embedding a teacher policy directly into the importance-sampling ratio, replacing the separate KL distillation term used by KDRL (Xu et al., 2025). Conceptually, it departs from the 'RL objective + auxiliary distillation loss' paradigm by folding teacher guidance into the trust-region anchor itself, so that imitation is advantage-gated and clipping-bounded rather than an independent gradient. The interpolation between GRPO (α=1) and a DPO-like teacher-referenced objective (α→0) provides a unifying view that connects RL post-training and preference optimization under a single ratio-based update.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~12% training overhead (no teacher at inference) makes RLAD practically deployable for distilling reasoning capabilities into small models, and the weak-teacher robustness result (RLAD degrades gracefully while KDRL does not) addresses a real deployment concern. A key open question is whether TRRD-style distillation can be extended to closed-source or API-only teachers that do not expose token-level logits, which the authors identify as a current limitation. The observation that explicit-KL distillation in RL primarily drives imitation (Pass@32) rather than reward-aligned improvement (Pass@1) suggests that the field may need to rethink how 'distillation' and 'RL' interact, rather than simply summing their losses.
