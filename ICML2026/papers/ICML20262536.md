## Problem

Existing spatial-spectral fusion (SSF) methods learn fusion mappings in the coordinate/pixel domain via convolutions and attentions, tying the learned mapping to a specific spatial resolution and offering no explicit control over which frequency components are amplified. This causes poor generalization when input resolutions vary and allows high-frequency spatial energy to leak into the spectral dimension, producing severe spectral distortion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262536/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution hyperspectral image (LR-HSI, e.g., 16×16×31) and a high-resolution multispectral image (HR-MSI, e.g., 64×64×3) as input, bicubically upsamples the LR-HSI to match the HR-MSI spatial size, and concatenates them channel-wise before encoding. The output is a high-resolution hyperspectral image (HR-HSI, e.g., 64×64×31) produced as a residual added to the bicubically upsampled LR-HSI. The same trained model generalizes across scaling factors (×4, ×8, ×16, ×32) without retraining, which is the key practical advantage over coordinate-tied baselines.

## Method

LSO decouples coordinate information from learned features by performing fusion in a compact latent space. An EDSR encoder extracts deep features from the concatenated inputs, which are then projected into N learned latent tokens (spectral prompts) via cross-attention, creating a resolution-agnostic bottleneck. A hierarchical patch-based architecture processes these tokens at K spatial scales with shared parameters within each scale, injecting multi-scale cues. The latent fusion operator is parameterized by a Trigonometric Basis Solver: a finite expansion of sin(qz) and cos(qz) terms with learnable coefficients plus a residual connection, reducing the mapping to a family of 1D scalar functions per channel. This structured parameterization provides an explicit capacity-stability trade-off governed by the number of basis functions Q, with a proven O(1/Q) approximation rate under Lipschitz targets, and naturally prevents uncontrolled high-frequency leakage into the spectral dimension.

## Results

On the CAVE dataset, LSO achieves PSNR of 50.728 dB, SAM of 2.381°, and ERGAS of 1.170 at ×4 scaling, and PSNR of 48.118 dB, SAM of 2.961°, and ERGAS of 1.675 at ×8 scaling, outperforming all seven baselines on every metric. On the larger Harvard dataset, LSO reaches PSNR of 49.182 dB (×4) and 47.957 dB (×8), again leading all competitors. The method uses only 1.94M parameters and 222.85G FLOPs, making it the most parameter-efficient among the compared deep methods while delivering the best reconstruction quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LSO outperforms all seven baselines on every metric (PSNR, SAM, ERGAS) across both CAVE and Harvard at both ×4 and ×8 scaling. On CAVE×4, the closest competitor is SpecSolver (PSNR 50.568), which LSO surpasses by 0.16 dB in PSNR and 0.108° in SAM; on CAVE×8, LSO leads Otias (the next-best PSNR at 47.769) by 0.35 dB. On Harvard×4, LSO beats DCT (49.068 PSNR) by 0.114 dB, and on Harvard×8 it edges out Otias (47.848 PSNR) by 0.11 dB. Paired two-sided t-tests across 10 independent runs confirm that most improvements are statistically significant at p < 0.01, and a Bonferroni-corrected family-wise threshold of α/7 ≈ 0.0071 is also exceeded. Notably, LSO achieves these gains with the fewest parameters (1.94M) among all deep methods, roughly 40–75% fewer than competitors like DCT (8.15M) or MIMO (4.98M), while also requiring fewer FLOPs than DCT, LFormer, FeINFN, SpecSolver, and Otias.

## Relation to Prior Work

LSO carves new ground at the intersection of latent-space learning and spectral methods for spatial-spectral fusion. While recent SSF methods (FeINFN, Otias, SpecSolver) have moved toward learning continuous mappings via implicit neural representations or neural operators, they still parameterize the fusion in the coordinate domain, tying the model to a specific spatial resolution. LSO departs from this by projecting high-dimensional observations into a compact latent token space via cross-attention with learned spectral prompts, then parameterizing the latent fusion operator as a trigonometric basis expansion — a structured, frequency-controllable alternative to the generic CNN/Transformer/Mamba backbones used in prior work. The hierarchical patch-based architecture draws on multi-scale reasoning common in SSF but is integrated with the latent projection in a way not seen in prior SSF pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is cross-scale transferability: a model trained at one resolution generalizes to 4×, 16×, and 32× without retraining, which is valuable for real-world systems where sensor pairings vary. The trigonometric basis solver provides an explicit capacity-stability knob (number of basis functions) and a theoretical justification (Remark 3.1) for why direct spectral approximation in the discretized coordinate space is intractable, grounding the design choice in approximation theory. However, the evaluation remains limited to simulated degradation protocols on two datasets, and real-world assessment is purely qualitative due to the absence of paired ground-truth HR-HSIs, leaving open whether the spectral fidelity gains hold under genuine sensor noise and calibration drift.
