## Problem

The language generation in the limit framework of Kleinberg & Mullainathan (2024) provides no feedback to the generator, leading to counterintuitive pathologies: generability is not preserved under finite unions and is brittle to even a single corrupted example. This paper asks whether incorporating feedback—either passive mistake indicators or active membership queries—restores well-behaved structure, and characterizes exactly which language collections become generable under each feedback model.

## Input / Output

At each round the generator observes an ordered history of adversarially enumerated examples from the target language K, plus a feedback signal: in the mistake model a single bit indicating whether the previous output was valid, and in the query model a bit answering an adaptively chosen membership query. The generator must output either a single string (element-based) or an infinite set of strings (set-based) that are all valid members of K not yet seen. A key structural difference is that in the query model the generator selects which string to test before committing to its output, while in the mistake model feedback arrives only after the output is fixed.

## Method

The central contribution is a characterization via countable inner-covers: a collection L is generable with mistake feedback (by either element- or set-based generators) if and only if it admits a countable family of infinite sets each contained in some language of L. The same characterization holds for set-based query-feedback generation, though element-based query generation is strictly more powerful (a separation is constructed using a block-partitioned family of languages). The proof technique rests on three tools: (1) a diagonalization argument establishing finite locking histories for any successful generator, (2) a self-simulation procedure that converts element-based generators into set-based ones by recursively feeding the generator's own outputs back as input, and (3) a finite-expansion subroutine (from Mehrotra et al., 2025) that absorbs arbitrary finite corruption in the feedback transcript by replacing each candidate set with all its finite perturbations, preserving countability.

## Results

The paper's main contribution is a complete characterization: a language collection is generable with mistake feedback (element- or set-based) if and only if it admits a countable inner-cover (Theorem 3.1, 3.2). The same countable-inner-cover condition characterizes set-based generation with query feedback (Theorem 3.4), establishing that the two feedback models have identical expressive power for set-based generation. A separation is shown for element-based query feedback: there exists a collection generable by an element-based generator with queries but not by any set-based generator (Theorem 3.3). Additional implications include closure under countable unions, generation with zero adversarial examples, and robustness to arbitrary example contamination plus finite feedback corruption.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four feedback/generation configurations rather than empirical baselines. Under mistake feedback, element-based and set-based generators are provably equivalent (Theorem 3.2), both characterized by countable inner-covers. Under query feedback, a strict separation emerges: element-based generation is strictly more powerful than set-based generation (Theorem 3.3), though set-based query generation still coincides with mistake-feedback generation in power (Theorem 3.4). Compared to the no-feedback model of Kleinberg & Mullainathan (2024), the feedback models gain closure under countable unions (Corollary 3.6), eliminate the need for adversarial examples entirely (Corollary 3.7), and tolerate arbitrary example contamination plus finite feedback corruption (Corollary 3.8) — properties that fail even under a single corrupted example in the no-feedback setting (Bai et al., 2026). The countable-inner-cover condition also provides a 'sandwich' for the open no-feedback characterization: finite inner-covers are sufficient and countable inner-covers are necessary, but neither alone is sufficient (Theorem 3.10).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the language generation in the limit framework of Kleinberg & Mullainathan (2024) by incorporating two feedback mechanisms: mistake feedback (inspired by Littlestone's online learning model) and membership-query feedback (introduced by Charikar & Pabbaraju, 2025). The key technical tool is the 'finite expansion' subroutine of Mehrotra et al. (2025), which is repurposed here to handle corrupted feedback transcripts. The paper departs from prior work by introducing countable inner-covers as a unifying combinatorial characterization, showing that both feedback models (for set-based generation) are characterized by the same condition, and by establishing that the adversary's examples become entirely unnecessary under feedback.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean characterization via countable inner-covers resolves several open questions: it explains why generation with feedback is closed under countable unions (unlike the no-feedback model), shows robustness to arbitrary example contamination and finite feedback corruption, and reveals that element-based and set-based generation coincide under mistake feedback but separate under query feedback. The 'sandwich' result for the no-feedback model (finite inner-cover sufficient, countable necessary, but neither characterizes) identifies a precise gap that remains an open problem. Open questions include characterizing element-based query generation, tolerating vanishing (rather than finite) feedback corruption, and whether breadth constraints are achievable with feedback.
