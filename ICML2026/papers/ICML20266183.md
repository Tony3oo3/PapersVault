## Problem

Prior FHE-based ML training work provides no theoretical convergence guarantees for approximate gradient descent, where activation and loss functions must be replaced by polynomial approximations for FHE compatibility. Standard DP-GD's per-sample gradient clipping is computationally prohibitive under FHE (requiring ~24 multiplicative depth per iteration versus ~8 for standard GD), and polynomial approximations can silently break DP guarantees when inputs fall outside their approximation intervals, which prior work selected only heuristically.

## Input / Output

The client encrypts its dataset (features normalized to [-1,1]^m, binary labels) and pre-sampled Gaussian noise under an FHE public key, sending both to the server in a single round. The server performs homomorphic training entirely on ciphertexts and returns the final encrypted model weights, which the client decrypts to obtain a differentially private model. In the multi-client setting, each client encrypts under its own public key and a single round of joint decryption among clients yields the shared model.

## Method

The paper's core algorithm (Algorithm 3) replaces DP-GD's expensive per-sample gradient clipping with a barrier function B_κ(Θ − ‖w‖²) added to the objective, which provably bounds the iterate norm ‖w_i‖ at every step. This bound fixes the polynomial approximation interval for activation and loss derivatives a priori, enabling rigorous sensitivity analysis and correct noise calibration without any clipping operations. The barrier's derivative 1/x is itself polynomially approximated, and all non-polynomial components (sigmoid, loss derivatives) are replaced by minimax or least-squares polynomials of chosen degree. Theoretically, the paper proves convergence of approximate GD (Theorem 3.3) and DP-approximate GD (Theorem 4.1) under bounded gradient distance ζ, and establishes (ε,δ)-DP for the full no-clipping algorithm (Theorem 5.1) via zCDP composition. Empirically, this reduces per-iteration multiplicative depth from 24 to 9 (a 2.5×–5× speedup) while matching DP-GD accuracy within 0.01% on MNIST and Credit datasets.

## Results

Under FHE with ε=1, δ=10⁻⁵, the proposed no-clipping algorithm (Algo 5) achieves 93.99% accuracy and 98.22% AUC on MNIST, matching DP-SGD (93.62%, 98.21%) while reducing per-iteration runtime from 600.1 s to 148.2 s (10 threads, RingDim=32768) — a ~4× speedup. On the Credit dataset, accuracy is 77.99% vs. 78.00% for DP-SGD, with runtime dropping from 446.1 s to 132.2 s. At the higher security level (RingDim=131072), the speedup over DP-SGD grows to ~14× (566.7 s vs. 7996.8 s per iteration on MNIST). Multiplicative depth per iteration drops from 24 (DP-SGD) to 9 (NoClip), a 2.5× reduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard DP-SGD (per-sample gradient clipping under FHE), the no-clipping method matches accuracy within 0.01–0.33% on both MNIST and Credit while achieving a 3–14× reduction in per-iteration runtime, depending on ring dimension and thread count. Against Output-SGD (output perturbation, Wu et al. 2017), the no-clipping method delivers substantially higher accuracy — 93.99% vs. 74.18% on MNIST and 77.99% vs. 76.76% on Credit — at essentially the same runtime (148.2 s vs. 147.5 s per iteration on MNIST, 10 threads). The paper also reports that relative to a plaintext DP-GD baseline, the FHE-trained no-clipping model incurs at most a 1.82% accuracy drop and 0.78% AUC drop, confirming that the FHE overhead is negligible in utility terms. The key practical advantage is that removing gradient clipping reduces multiplicative depth from 24 to 9 per iteration, which is the primary driver of FHE computational cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two largely separate literatures: FHE-based secure ML training (Gilad-Bachrach et al. 2016; Kim et al. 2018; Han et al. 2018; Mihara et al. 2020), which has been purely empirical with no convergence guarantees, and differentially private training (Abadi et al. 2016), which assumes plaintext computation. The key departure is from standard DP-GD, which relies on per-sample gradient clipping to bound sensitivity; under FHE, clipping requires expensive homomorphic comparisons, square roots, and divisions (circuit depth ~24/iteration). The authors replace clipping with a logarithmic barrier function added to the objective, which provably bounds iterate norms and thus fixes the polynomial approximation interval a priori, reducing depth to ~9. Prior FHE+DP work (Phong et al. 2018; Aziz et al. 2023; Sébert et al. 2023) encrypts only model updates in interactive federated settings, whereas this paper encrypts the full training dataset for non-interactive outsourcing to an untrusted server.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide end-to-end theoretical guarantees (convergence + differential privacy) for ML training under FHE with polynomial approximations, filling a gap where prior FHE-ML papers offered only empirical results. The finding that naively substituting polynomial approximations into DP-GD can silently break DP guarantees (because gradient sensitivity becomes unbounded outside the approximation interval) is an important caution for the field. The practical 2.5× reduction in multiplicative depth makes FHE-based private training substantially more feasible. However, the analysis is currently restricted to smooth, convex objectives (e.g., logistic regression), and extending the barrier-function technique to general deep neural networks with non-convex losses remains an open problem that limits immediate applicability to modern architectures.
