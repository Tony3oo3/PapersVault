## Problem

Existing KV cache eviction methods rank tokens heuristically using accumulated attention weights, overlooking the contribution of value states and attention outputs to final model predictions. This leads to suboptimal eviction decisions that may retain tokens with negligible output impact or discard tokens whose importance is not evident from attention weights alone. The paper asks whether a principled, output-aware saliency measure can be derived in closed form to replace these heuristics and improve token selection.

## Input / Output

The method operates on per-layer, per-head KV cache matrices (K, V), attention weight matrices (A), pre-softmax logits (Z), and attention output vectors (O) within a configurable perturbation window of query positions (e.g., 64 tokens in prefill, 1 in decoding). It produces per-token saliency scores that serve as plug-and-play replacements for the attention-based scores in existing eviction pipelines (H2O, TOVA, SnapKV, AdaKV), supporting both static one-shot eviction during prefill and dynamic per-step eviction during decoding. For GQA models, per-query-head scores are aggregated over the associated KV head group to yield a single per-KV-head score, avoiding the redundant cache retention required by prior methods like SnapKV.

## Method

OBCache formulates KV cache eviction as a layer-wise structured pruning problem grounded in Optimal Brain Damage theory, where each token's saliency is the second-order Taylor approximation of the perturbation in historical attention outputs induced by removing that token's KV pair. Three closed-form scores are derived for three pruning units: isolated value (squared attention-weight column norm scaled by value norm), isolated key (attention-weight and logit-weighted squared deviation between the value vector and each attention output), and joint key-value (sum of the first two plus a cross-term capturing key-value interaction). The key insight is that the true eviction error—impact on future, unobservable attention outputs—can be effectively proxied by perturbations in recent historical outputs, making the scores computable at inference time without access to future states. Existing attention-based eviction scores (H2O, TOVA, SnapKV) are shown to emerge as special cases when the objective is relaxed to attention-matrix perturbation and the pruning unit is reduced to a single attention column, positioning OBCache as a strict generalization that adds output-aware signals beyond raw attention statistics.

## Results

On RULER-4K (LLaMA-3.1-8B), integrating OBCACHE-K into H2O yields a +10.1-point average accuracy gain (57.5→67.6, Q-Aware) and +7.2 points (31.7→38.9, Q-Agnostic) over the original attention-only score. The strongest baseline AdaKV benefits most under query-agnostic compression, where OBCACHE-K improves average accuracy from 43.0 to 55.0 (+12.0 points) at 4K context. On PG19 dynamic decoding (1024-token budget), all OBCACHE variants consistently outperform H2O across all context lengths from 1K to 32K tokens, with OBCACHE-K achieving the lowest perplexity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OBCACHE-K consistently outperforms all four attention-based baselines (H2O, TOVA, SnapKV, AdaKV) across every compression ratio, context length, and query-aware/agnostic setting on RULER, with the largest gains observed for H2O (+10 pts on 4K Q-Aware) and AdaKV (+12 pts on 4K Q-Agnostic). Against the value-aware baselines VATP and CriticalKV (Figure 4, RULER-4K with AdaKV), OBCACHE-K and OBCACHE-V&K achieve the strongest performance-compression trade-offs, while OBCACHE-V (equivalent to VATP's ℓ₂ variant) outperforms both VATP and CriticalKV at higher compression ratios. On PG19 dynamic decoding, all OBCACHE variants beat both the StreamingLLM (Sink) baseline and H2O across all sequence lengths, confirming that output-aware scoring improves long-range dependency preservation during generation. OBCACHE-K generally matches or slightly exceeds OBCACHE-V&K, suggesting the key-pruning term captures most of the output sensitivity without the additional cross-term computation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the Optimal Brain Damage (OBD) framework—originally designed for pruning static network weights—into the dynamic KV cache eviction setting, treating cached key and value vectors as pruning variables. It shows that existing attention-based eviction scores (H2O, TOVA, SnapKV) emerge as special cases when the pruning objective is restricted to the attention matrix and the pruning unit is a single attention column, while value-aware scores (VATP, CriticalKV) correspond to the isolated-value sub-case. The key departure is deriving closed-form key-pruning and joint key-value pruning scores via second-order Taylor approximation, which incorporate attention logits, value states, and attention outputs—signals absent from all prior heuristic methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing a principled, plug-and-play scoring mechanism that consistently improves every tested eviction baseline (H2O, TOVA, SnapKV, AdaKV) across both prefill and decoding stages, OBCache offers a drop-in upgrade for practitioners already using score-based KV cache compression. The theoretical unification clarifies why attention-only heuristics fall short and points toward more informed eviction decisions. Open questions remain around the structural bias toward earlier tokens (mitigated only by a static recent window), the choice of perturbation window during decoding, and whether the framework extends naturally to channel-wise pruning or cache merging via relaxed OBD/OBS approximations. The paper also notes it has not been evaluated on multi-round agentic or reasoning-heavy decoding tasks where dynamic eviction may play a larger role.
