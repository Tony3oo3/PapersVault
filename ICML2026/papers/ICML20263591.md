## Problem

Existing sleep analysis models are task-specific and anchored to localized 30-second epoch features, failing to capture the global macro-architecture of a full night's sleep (sleep cycles, stage distributions across hours). This limits their utility for subject-level clinical diagnosis and long-term health prediction, where macro-structural dynamics are established biomarkers. Additionally, reliance on expert-scored labels introduces inter-scorer variability and annotation noise that hinders scalability.

## Input / Output

The model consumes full-night PSG recordings across six modalities (EEG, EOG, ECG, EMG, respiratory, SpO2) resampled to 100 Hz with modality-specific bandpass filtering and z-score normalization (except SpO2). The Micro-Encoder processes 60-second windows (120 patches of 500 ms each), while the Macro-Encoder ingests the complete sequence of epoch-level embeddings (up to 1,080 epochs / 540 minutes per subject). Outputs are two-tiered: epoch-level micro-embeddings for second-scale segmentation and subject-level macro-embeddings for full-night characterization, which feed linear probes or LSTM heads for sleep staging, per-second SDB segmentation, Cox PH disease risk scores, and demographic regression.

## Method

SleepMaMi employs a hierarchical dual-encoder: a Micro-Encoder with modality-private Transformer encoders and a shared Mixture-of-Experts Transformer (with patch merging for sequence-length reduction) trained via a hybrid objective combining masked autoencoding (with frequency-adaptive masking: 500 ms for high-frequency signals, 2 s for low-frequency) and multi-modal contrastive learning with KoLeo regularization; and a Macro-Encoder using bi-directional Mamba (Selective State Space) layers for linear-complexity long-sequence modeling. The Macro-Encoder is trained with Demographic-Guided Contrastive Learning (DGCL), a soft-target contrastive objective that partitions the full night into 90-minute ultradian-cycle intervals and computes weighted similarities between subject pairs based on z-scored age/BMI distance plus a sex penalty, pulling demographically similar subjects together in latent space without requiring any manual sleep-stage annotations. This metadata-driven pretraining circumvents inter-scorer variability while encoding normative and pathological macro-structural trajectories, and the bi-directional Mamba choice addresses variable recording durations by learning architecture from both sleep onset and termination.

## Results

On sleep stage classification (the primary task), SleepMaMi achieves 81.9% accuracy and 74.1% Macro-F1 on SHHS1, 80.7% accuracy and 71.2% Macro-F1 on the held-out CFS dataset, and 79.1% accuracy and 65.2% Macro-F1 on SOF, outperforming all baselines across all four evaluation datasets. For SDB segmentation, SleepMaMi reaches 60.6% Macro-F1 on SHHS1 and 78.0% on KISS, substantially exceeding SleepFM-Disease (39.4% and 65.1% respectively). On disease prediction (SHHS1), SleepMaMi achieves C-Index values of 0.778 (Angina), 0.793 (CHF), and 0.776 (CHD death), matching or exceeding SleepFM-Disease across six cardiovascular outcomes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SleepMaMi outperforms all four foundation-model baselines on sleep staging across every dataset. Against the strongest time-series baseline MOMENT-Base, SleepMaMi gains +2.5% accuracy on SHHS1 (81.9 vs 79.4), +9.3% on CFS (80.7 vs 71.4), and +0.1% on SOF (79.1 vs 79.0), with the largest Macro-F1 margins on CFS (+18.8 points). Against the EEG-only LaBraM, SleepMaMi leads by 11.7% accuracy on SHHS1 and 21.6% on CFS, confirming the benefit of multi-modal input. Against the closest sleep-specific competitor SleepFM-Disease, SleepMaMi achieves +12.2% accuracy on SHHS1 (81.9 vs 69.7) and +9.6% on CFS (80.7 vs 71.1). On SDB segmentation, SleepMaMi's Macro-F1 advantage over SleepFM-Disease is most pronounced on SHHS1 (60.6 vs 39.4, +21.2 points) and KISS (78.0 vs 65.1, +12.9 points), attributed to the MAE reconstruction objective capturing fine-grained respiratory morphology. On disease prediction, SleepMaMi is broadly comparable to SleepFM-Disease, with a notable +0.146 C-Index gain on Angina (0.778 vs 0.632) but a slight −0.003 deficit on CVD death (0.788 vs 0.791), indicating parity on this subject-level task.

## Relation to Prior Work

SleepMaMi extends the sleep foundation model lineage initiated by SleepFM (Thapa et al., 2024/2026) by introducing a hierarchical dual-encoder that explicitly models both micro-structure (via a private-shared Transformer with MoE, trained with MAE + multi-modal CL) and macro-structure (via a bi-directional Mamba encoder trained with a novel Demographic-Guided Contrastive Learning objective). The micro-encoder builds directly on MAE (He et al., 2022) and contrastive learning (Oord et al., 2018; Chen et al., 2020), while the macro-encoder departs from prior sleep FMs by replacing epoch-local processing with full-night sequence modeling using Mamba (Gu & Dao, 2024) and using demographic metadata as soft supervisory signals rather than relying on manual sleep-stage annotations. The key conceptual departure from SleepFM and contemporaries (SleepGPT, Sleep2Vec, OSF) is the explicit unification of second-scale signal morphology with hour-scale sleep architecture within a single representation, rather than anchoring exclusively to 30-second epochs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a fundamental gap in sleep AI: prior foundation models capture only localized micro-structure, ignoring the clinically critical macro-architecture (sleep cycles, stage distributions) that serves as a biomarker for long-term health outcomes. The label-efficient few-shot results (single-sample F1 of 44–57%) and annotation-free pretraining (DGCL) make the model practical for resource-constrained clinical settings and rare disorder research where massive scored data is unavailable. Open questions include whether the empirically designed demographic distance function in DGCL can be made learnable, how the model generalizes to populations underrepresented in the pretraining corpus (e.g., pediatric, non-Western cohorts), and whether the demographic metadata used in DGCL could inadvertently encode or amplify clinical biases despite the authors' subgroup analysis showing robustness.
