## Problem

In knowledge-intensive domains like medicine, intermediate reasoning steps cannot be locally verified the way math or code can, so subtle errors propagate undetected through reasoning traces. Prior process reward models (PRMs), including retrieval-augmented variants like Med-PRM, score completed trajectories post hoc, which prevents them from steering generation in real time or enabling search-based decoding that ranks and prunes candidates at every step.

## Input / Output

The system takes a medical question, a frozen autoregressive policy model (e.g., Qwen3-4B-Instruct), and a fixed knowledge base of medical documents (textbooks, StatPearls, clinical guidelines, rare-disease corpus). At each reasoning step, the PRA consumes the partial trace plus optionally retrieved documents and emits two binary scores: a reasoning reward (step correctness) and a search action (whether retrieval was needed). The final output is the highest-scoring complete trace's answer from beam search, with the policy model's parameters and input context never modified.

## Method

PRA is a single transformer (fine-tuned Qwen3-4B-Instruct) with two token-level readout heads sharing parameters: an action controller that decides per-step whether to invoke retrieval, and a reward scorer that assigns a binary correctness label to the most recent step conditioned on the partial trace and retrieved evidence. Training uses supervised labels generated by a Qwen3-235B-Instruct teacher, with search labels derived from a margin-shift criterion measuring how much retrieved documents change the teacher's posterior. At inference, PRA operates inside beam search (B=4, branching factor b=16): the frozen policy extends each beam trace with b candidates, PRA scores all B×b candidates, and the top-B by cumulative reward are retained. The critical design choice is applying rewards online during generation rather than post hoc, which ablations show accounts for the majority of the performance gain over outcome-level or post-hoc process-level scoring.

## Results

Using Qwen3-4B-Instruct as the frozen policy, PRA achieves 81.9% accuracy on MedQA (in-distribution), a new state-of-the-art at the 4B scale, and 72.1% average accuracy across seven medical reasoning benchmarks. This represents a 5.2-point improvement over the strongest baseline (RAG + Self-Consistency at 66.9% average). PRA also generalizes to unseen policy models from 0.5B to 8B parameters, improving MedQA accuracy by up to 25.7 points (e.g., Qwen2.5-0.5B: 28.4% → 54.1%) without any policy retraining.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PRA (81.9% MedQA, 72.1% avg) outperforms all baselines across every benchmark. Against the RAG baseline (Lewis et al., 2020), PRA gains +9.7 points on MedQA (81.9 vs. 72.2) and +5.9 on average (72.1 vs. 66.2). The strongest baseline, RAG + Self-Consistency (66.9% avg), is surpassed by PRA by 5.2 points on average, with the largest gaps on Medbullets (+7.5), GPQA (+10.7), and Lancet (+9.9). Notably, Self-Consistency actually degrades performance on GPQA and Lancet when applied to CoT or RAG (e.g., CoT+SC drops from 60.8 to 51.8 on GPQA), whereas PRA maintains stable gains on these harder benchmarks. On the in-distribution MedQA benchmark, PRA's 81.9% exceeds CoT+SC (74.8%) by 7.1 points and RAG+SC (76.7%) by 5.2 points, establishing a new SOTA at the 4B parameter scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the process reward model (PRM) paradigm from Lightman et al. (2023) and the retrieval-augmented medical PRM of Med-PRM (Yun et al., 2025) by making step-wise reward evaluation online rather than post hoc. The key architectural departure is decoupling retrieval and verification entirely from the frozen policy: the policy never sees retrieved documents, and the reward agent (PRA) handles both evidence acquisition and step scoring within a beam-search loop. This contrasts with standard RAG, which injects documents into the policy context, and with prior PRMs that score only completed trajectories, preventing mid-generation correction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupling of domain-specific reward from the policy model enables a practical deployment paradigm where new or updated backbones can be swapped in without retraining, reducing the cost of keeping medical AI aligned with evolving guidelines and literature. The cross-model generalization results (up to 25.7% absolute gains on 0.5B–3B models) suggest substantial latent reasoning capacity in small models that standard decoding fails to exploit. Open questions include whether the always-search assumption generalizes to domains where retrieval is less informative, how PRA scales to much longer reasoning traces, and whether the framework transfers cleanly to non-medical knowledge-intensive tasks without retraining the reward agent.
