## Problem

Existing linear attention and SSM architectures treat the query as a passive readout mechanism, restricting state evolution to key-value interactions. The paper shows that query-conditioned readout S_{t-1} q_t actually encodes a structured value aggregation over accumulated memory that is complementary to key-based retrieval, yet this signal is never fed back into state correction. This leaves a corrective signal unexploited that could improve memory dynamics without increasing computational cost.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a token sequence and produces next-token predictions via a standard autoregressive language modeling objective. Internally, the recurrent state S_t ∈ R^{d_v × d_k} is updated per timestep using projected keys, values, and queries, with the query serving a dual role: it both reads out the output o_t = S_t q_t and contributes a corrective term to the state update. For hardware-efficient training, sequences are partitioned into chunks of length C, enabling parallel intra-chunk computation with a recurrent dependency across chunks.

## Method

Q-Delta modifies the gated delta-rule state update by adding a query-feedback term: S_t = α_t S_{t-1}(I − β_t(k_t k_t^T + λ_t q_t k_t^T)) + β_t v_t k_t^T, where λ_t is a learnable per-head coefficient (via sigmoid projection) that modulates the influence of the query-conditioned prediction ô_t = S_{t-1} q_t in the correction signal. The paper proves one-step contraction of the mixed key-query prediction error under the condition β_t a_t ∈ (0,2) and establishes global geometric tracking with a bound proportional to residual drift. A chunkwise-parallel formulation is derived using an extended WY representation and UT transform, yielding a Triton kernel that matches delta-rule throughput. Empirically, Q-Delta outperforms GatedDeltaNet, Mamba2, and other linear RNN baselines on language modeling perplexity, zero-shot commonsense reasoning, and long-context retrieval at both 340M and 1.3B scales.

## Results

At the 340M scale (15B tokens), Q-Delta achieves the best average zero-shot accuracy of 47.24% across 8 commonsense reasoning tasks, outperforming Mamba (46.64%), Mamba2 (46.27%), GatedDeltaNet (46.01%), RetNet (44.55%), and DeltaNet (43.82%), while maintaining competitive perplexity (WikiText 26.89, LAMBADA 32.67). At the 1.3B scale (30B tokens), Q-Delta extends this lead to 53.47% average accuracy, with the best WikiText perplexity (19.21) and LAMBADA perplexity (15.19). On the synthetic S-NIAH retrieval benchmark (1.3B), Q-Delta achieves 90.02% average accuracy, substantially ahead of GatedDeltaNet (83.51%) and DeltaNet (81.29%), with near-perfect pass-key retrieval (100%) and strong performance on harder UUID-in-haystack tasks (48.0% at 4K context vs. 21.4% for GatedDeltaNet).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Q-Delta consistently outperforms all five baselines on average zero-shot accuracy at both model scales: at 340M it leads Mamba (46.64%) by 0.60 points and GatedDeltaNet (46.01%) by 1.23 points; at 1.3B it leads GatedDeltaNet (52.77%) by 0.70 points and DeltaNet (52.53%) by 0.94 points. On the synthetic S-NIAH retrieval benchmark (1.3B), Q-Delta's 90.02% average is a substantial 6.5-point improvement over GatedDeltaNet (83.51%) and 8.7 points over DeltaNet (81.29%), with the largest gains on the hardest UUID-in-haystack task at 4K context (48.0% vs. 21.4% for GatedDeltaNet). On real-world recall-intensive tasks (340M), Q-Delta wins on SWDE (31.6 vs. 29.1), FDA (22.0 vs. 18.9), and NQ (18.4 vs. 18.2), but trails GatedDeltaNet on SQD (52.3 vs. 56.0) and Drop (33.2 vs. 34.2), and trails Mamba on TQA (48.6 vs. 55.2). RetNet is the weakest baseline across all settings, particularly on long-context retrieval where its S-NIAH average drops to 38.09% at 4K. Throughput comparisons show Q-Delta matches delta-based baselines (DeltaNet, GatedDeltaNet) closely, while Mamba2 exhibits noticeably lower throughput at shorter sequence lengths.

## Relation to Prior Work

Q-Delta extends the gated delta-rule lineage (DeltaNet → GatedDeltaNet) by adding a query-conditioned correction term λ_t q_t k_t^T to the state transition, while retaining the same online-learning interpretation established by Longhorn. The chunkwise-parallel formulation directly generalizes GatedDeltaNet's WY representation and UT transform to the mixed key-query input x_t = k_t + λ_t q_t. The key conceptual departure from all prior linear RNNs (RetNet, Mamba/Mamba2, DeltaNet, GatedDeltaNet) is the rejection of the assumption that the query plays no role in shaping state dynamics, treating it instead as a source of complementary corrective signal alongside the key.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled justification for incorporating query feedback into recurrent state updates, supported by a stability analysis (one-step contraction and global geometric tracking) and empirical complementarity evidence showing that key- and query-based readouts occupy largely decorrelated directions in value space. The chunkwise-parallel Triton implementation makes the method practical at competitive throughput. Open questions include whether the benefit persists at larger scales (beyond 1.3B), whether the query-feedback mechanism interacts differently with multi-head or multi-layer architectures, and whether the modest mean alignment (β_t·a_t ≈ 0.043) limits the practical magnitude of the correction in deeper models.
