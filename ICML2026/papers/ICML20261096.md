## Problem

Existing privacy analyses of k-out-of-t random allocation (used in DP-SGD and communication-efficient private aggregation) produce bounds that are either loose due to approximation steps or expressed in hockey-stick/Rényi divergence parameters that introduce overheads during composition. No method existed to compute the full privacy loss distribution (PLD) of random allocation efficiently, preventing tight, lossless privacy accounting for complex nested-sampling algorithms like PREAMBLE.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261096/cropped_page4_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a discrete PLD realization (a PMF over privacy loss values) for a base mechanism such as the Gaussian mechanism, along with sampling parameters (t total steps, k allocations per element) and accuracy parameters (α controlling the shift in privacy loss, β controlling the probability mass assigned to unbounded loss). It outputs a discrete PLD realization that (α,β)-tightly stochastically dominates the true PLD of the random allocation scheme, from which (ε,δ) parameters and full privacy profiles can be extracted. Because the output is itself a PLD realization, it composes losslessly with other PLD-based operations (composition, further subsampling), unlike prior Rényi or hockey-stick bounds.

## Method

The key insight is that the PLD of 1-out-of-t random allocation equals the logarithm of a sum of exponentiated PLDs (exp-PLDs) of the dominating distribution pair, reducing the problem to t-wise convolutions on exp-PLDs rather than on PLDs directly. The algorithm discretizes exp-PLDs on a geometric (multiplicative) grid to avoid the enormous dynamic range that makes additive-grid FFT impractical, then computes the t-wise convolution via exponentiation by squaring in O(log t) steps. For the general k-out-of-t case, a tighter reduction decomposes the scheme into compositions of 1-out-of-⌊t/k⌋ and 1-out-of-⌈t/k⌉ allocations. The paper also formalizes PLD realization as a domination-preserving representation, enabling direct PLD-based computation of Poisson subsampling (Theorem 3.2) and random allocation (Theorem 4.3) as first-class accounting operations, not just composition.

## Results

The paper provides an efficient algorithm for computing the privacy loss distribution (PLD) of k-out-of-t random allocation with runtime O(log³(t)·log(t/β)/(σ²α²)) for the Gaussian mechanism (Theorem 4.3). The resulting privacy bounds are never worse than Poisson subsampling with rate 1/t, and in most practical parameter regimes are slightly better (Figures 1–2). For the PREAMBLE DP-SGD setting (n=6×10⁵, d=2²⁰, C=2¹⁵, E=10), the PLD-based accounting significantly reduces the required noise σ to achieve (ε=1, δ=10⁻⁶)-DP compared to RDP-based bounds (Figure 3). In the synthetic privacy-utility experiment, random allocation achieves higher estimation accuracy than Poisson sampling for all privacy settings considered (Figure 9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the analytic bounds of Feldman & Shenfeld (2025), the proposed PLD-based method is significantly tighter across all tested parameter regimes (Figures 1–2); their bounds typically show a ~20% increase in ε over Poisson, while the new method matches or beats Poisson. Against the RDP-based bounds of Dong et al. (2025) and Schuchardt & Kalinin (2026), the improvement is most pronounced in multi-epoch composition settings: for PREAMBLE (Figure 3), the PLD accounting yields substantially lower required noise σ than RDP-based accounting, which suffers from lossy RDP-to-(ε,δ) conversion. Against the Monte Carlo simulations of Chua et al. (2024a), the new method is nearly indistinguishable from their lower bound, closely matches the MC mean estimate where statistically stable, and is tighter than the MC high-probability bound (Figure 2). Overall, the proposed method dominates all prior bounds and is never worse than Poisson subsampling, establishing random allocation as a strictly better sampling scheme for DP-SGD in terms of the privacy-utility trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PLD-based numerical accounting framework (Gopi et al., 2021; Zhu et al., 2022) from composition to the random allocation sampling scheme, building directly on the reduction and dominating-pair characterization from Feldman & Shenfeld (2025). Prior analyses of random allocation (Feldman & Shenfeld, 2025; Dong et al., 2025; Schuchardt & Kalinin, 2026) produced hockey-stick or Rényi divergence bounds that introduce overheads under composition; this paper instead computes the PLD directly by representing it as a log-sum-exp of exponentiated PLDs and performing convolutions on exp-PLDs. The subsampling PLD transformation (Theorem 3.2) is derived in the spirit of the hockey-stick transformation of Balle et al. (2018), and the PLD realization concept generalizes the discrete PLD representations used in existing accounting libraries (Google, Microsoft, Meta) to operations beyond composition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method that provides tight, composition-compatible privacy parameters for random allocation, enabling accurate accounting for complex nested-sampling schemes like PREAMBLE where prior RDP-based bounds were lossy. The result that random allocation matches or beats Poisson subsampling for the Gaussian mechanism (contradicting the up-to-20% gap suggested by prior analytical bounds) has direct practical implications for DP-SGD and communication-efficient federated learning. Open questions include a full characterization of the Poisson-vs-random-allocation relationship for non-Gaussian mechanisms (the Laplace case shows a more complex, regime-dependent picture) and whether the PLD-realization framework extends to other structured sampling schemes beyond random allocation and Poisson subsampling.
