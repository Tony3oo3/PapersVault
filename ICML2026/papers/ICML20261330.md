## Problem

Multi-token prediction (MTP) for byte-level LLMs must balance expressiveness (capturing dependencies between future bytes) and latency (sampling the window in parallel). Existing MTP methods either assume full independence between future tokens—producing high-probability 'byte-salad' continuations—or retain autoregressive generation within the window, increasing latency. No prior work provides a systematic framework to navigate this expressiveness-latency spectrum while guaranteeing that generation quality matches the target autoregressive LLM.

## Input / Output

The method takes a byte-level text context (up to 8192 bytes) processed by an LLM backbone (EvaByte 6.5B or Llama 3.2 3B Byte, with vocabularies of 320 and 268 respectively) to produce a d-dimensional hidden state, which is then mapped via a neural head to circuit parameters. The output is a window of n future bytes (n=8 or 16) sampled from the probabilistic circuit in a single feed-forward pass, followed by speculative decoding that accepts a prefix of these tokens guaranteed to match the target AR LLM exactly (greedy) or in expectation (sampling). The MTP window uses overlapping positions so speculative decoding can begin from any generation step.

## Method

MTPC parameterises the joint distribution over n future bytes as a probabilistic circuit (PC)—a tractable computational graph of sum, product, and input units—offering four architectures that form an expressiveness hierarchy: fully factorised (FF), canonical polyadic mixture (CP), hidden Markov model (HMM), and binary tree factorisation (BTree). The PC is conditioned on the LLM hidden state via a neural head that outputs categorical input-unit parameters (one unembedding tensor per window position and mixture component) and softmax-parameterised sum-unit weights (mixture coefficients or transition matrices). For self-speculative decoding, the draft model shares the LLM backbone with the verifier but optionally adds LoRA adapters to the last k layers, creating a second axis to trade off expressiveness (draft-verifier divergence) against latency (extra forward passes). The BTree architecture achieves the best throughput by enabling fully parallel sampling of both latent variables and tokens, unlike the autoregressive HMM. Training uses instruction fine-tuning on Tulu 3 with negative log-likelihood loss and exponential discounting across window positions, initialised from the pretrained MTP head to smoothly transition from FF to more expressive circuits.

## Results

MTPC achieves a 5.15× throughput speedup over autoregressive generation for EvaByte 6.5B and 2.24× for Llama 3.2 3B Byte, while also providing a 1.17× speedup over MTP with independence assumptions (FF). On EvaByte with n=8 and speculative sampling, MTPC-CP (r=32) reaches μ_acc=5.88 and 191.0 tok/s, compared to the FF baseline's μ_acc=5.19 and 176.3 tok/s. The optimal configuration for EvaByte is MTPC-BTree with n=16 and 1 LoRA layer, while Llama benefits most from 2 LoRA layers. All speedups are obtained with guaranteed lossless generation quality (exact match for greedy decoding, match in expectation for sampling).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is EvaByte-MTP (FF), the fully factorised multi-token prediction head that assumes independence between future bytes. MTPC-CP with r=32 improves mean accepted tokens from 5.19 to 5.88 (+0.69) and throughput from 176.3 to 191.0 tok/s (+8.3%) while adding only ~0.0015s of latency per cycle. Against autoregressive (STP) generation, MTPC-BTree with n=16 and 1 LoRA layer achieves a 5.15× wall-time speedup for EvaByte and 2.24× for Llama 3.2 3B Byte. Both models also achieve a consistent 1.17× speedup over the best FF MTP configuration, confirming that relaxing the independence assumption is the key driver of gains under speculative sampling. Notably, MTPC-HMM achieves the highest acceptance rates but is outperformed in throughput by MTPC-BTree due to its autoregressive (sequential) latency, while MTPC-FF surprisingly matches or exceeds expressive circuits under greedy decoding for EvaByte (which was pretrained with the independence assumption).

## Relation to Prior Work

MTPC unifies and extends the spectrum of multi-token prediction parameterisations—fully factorised (Medusa, EvaByte), CP tensor decomposition (Basharin et al., 2025), and shallow autoregressive heads (Hydra, EAGLE)—under a single probabilistic circuit framework, adding novel HMM and binary-tree factorisation architectures that were previously unexplored for MTP. It departs from prior MTP work by treating the dependency structure over future tokens as an explicit, selectable architectural choice rather than a fixed assumption, and by introducing partial LoRA layer sharing between draft and verifier as a second, orthogonal axis of the expressiveness-latency trade-off. The self-speculative decoding algorithm is adapted from Leviathan et al. (2022) and Medusa's shared-backbone design, with a key modification that sacrifices the 'free' bonus token to halve the number of LLM forward passes per cycle.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Byte-level LLMs are increasingly attractive for their tokenizer-free generality but remain prohibitively slow; MTPC's 5.15× speedup over autoregressive generation for EvaByte (while guaranteeing identical output quality via speculative decoding) makes them substantially more deployable. The PC framework provides a principled, extensible way to navigate the expressiveness-latency spectrum, and the finding that the optimal operating point is often not the most expressive architecture (e.g., BTree over HMM, 1 LoRA layer over 4) gives practitioners concrete guidance. Open questions include extending MTPC to subword-level LLMs (constrained by a logits memory bottleneck scaling as s×n×v×r), multi-lingual generation, and fair comparison to optimised shallow-AR methods like EAGLE on byte-level models, which the authors acknowledge as non-trivial and leave for future work.
