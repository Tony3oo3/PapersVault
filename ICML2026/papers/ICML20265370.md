## Problem

Looped Language Models (LoopLMs) that perform latent reasoning via depth recurrence exhibit unreliable test-time scaling: accuracy peaks at a certain iteration depth and then collapses with further recurrence. The paper diagnoses this as a fundamental trade-off between effectiveness (information flow through residual connections) and stability (bounded latent trajectories) in the recurrent dynamics, showing that common remedies like auxiliary layers, L2 regularization, or random loop sampling alone cannot resolve the deadlock.

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt (e.g., a math word problem) and a user-specified recurrent iteration count t as input, and produces a text answer via standard autoregressive decoding. The iteration count is the test-time compute knob: the same set of parameters is applied t times over the latent state, so increasing t at inference costs only additional forward passes without expanding the context window or parameter count. For the arithmetic diagnostic experiments, inputs are 4-digit addition strings over a small digit/operator vocabulary; for the main evaluation, inputs are natural-language math problems from GSM8K, MATH500, ASDiv, SVAMP, and AMC23.

## Method

STARS (STAbility-driven Recurrent Scaling) is a training framework that enforces asymptotic stability of the latent trajectory by regularizing the spectral radius of the Jacobian of the recurrent map, grounded in the Lyapunov Linearization Theorem (a fixed point is locally stable iff the spectral radius of its Jacobian is < 1). To avoid the infeasible cost of full eigenvalue computation on high-dimensional Jacobians, STARS estimates the spectral radius via a single-step power iteration using Jacobian-vector products (JVP) in PyTorch, yielding a lightweight per-sample loss L_JSRR = ‖Jv‖². This JSRR loss is combined with the standard cross-entropy SFT loss under a random loop sampling strategy that draws the iteration depth t from a log-normal distribution each batch, ensuring the spectral radius constraint is applied across diverse states along the trajectory rather than at a single fixed depth. The final objective is L_STARS = E_{t~P}[(1−λ)·L_SFT^(t) + λ·L_JSRR^(t)], which simultaneously maximizes task effectiveness and drives the latent dynamics toward stable fixed points, enabling reliable performance as test-time recurrence depth increases.

## Results

On the multi-digit addition task, STARS achieves 100% accuracy at all recurrent depths (up to 100+ iterations), demonstrating fully reliable test-time scaling. On mathematical reasoning benchmarks, Ouro-1.4B-STARS peaks at 74.18% average accuracy at 4 recurrent steps (GSM8K: 81.96%, MATH500: 67.40%, AMC23: 52.50%), outperforming the SFT baseline's peak of 70.46%. Critically, when scaling to 8 recurrent steps, STARS retains 65.55% average accuracy versus only 52.97% for SFT and 55.09% for base Ouro-1.4B, showing a substantially slower degradation curve.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the base Ouro-1.4B model, STARS improves peak average accuracy from 67.41% to 74.18% at 4 recurrent steps and reduces the 4-to-8-step degradation from 12.32 pp to 8.63 pp. Compared to the Ouro-1.4B-SFT baseline, STARS achieves a higher peak (74.18% vs. 70.46%) and a dramatically smaller collapse at 8 steps (65.55% vs. 52.97%), a 12.58 pp advantage. Against the retrofitted-recurrence models (Recurrent-Llama-3.2 and Recurrent-OLMo-2-0425 from McLeish et al.), STARS outperforms by a wide margin (74.18% vs. 18.79% and 21.57% average), indicating that the instability is not inherent to recurrence but to training design. Among standard non-looped LLMs, STARS at 4 steps surpasses Qwen3-4B (80.36% vs. 81.96% on GSM8K) and Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct (74.91% vs. 81.96%) on GSM8K, and exceeds all listed standard models on MATH500 and AMC23, while trailing Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct and Qwen3-1.7B on SVAMP (84.33% vs. 87.00%/89.00%). Overall, STARS is the only method that simultaneously achieves the highest peak performance and the most graceful degradation as recurrent depth increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LoopLM line of work (Ouro, Huginn, Universal Transformer) by diagnosing their unreliable test-time scaling through a dynamical systems lens, identifying a fundamental stability–effectiveness trade-off rooted in normalization placement. It departs from DEQ's Frobenius-norm Jacobian regularization by instead constraining the spectral radius via a single-step power iteration with JVPs, and couples this with random loop sampling (previously used in Huginn but not systematically analyzed) to enforce global trajectory stability. The theoretical grounding in the Lyapunov Linearization Theorem provides a principled justification absent from prior LoopLM training recipes, which relied on ad-hoc architectural choices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LoopLMs promise test-time scaling without expanding context length, making them attractive for efficient reasoning; STARS offers the first principled training framework that addresses their core instability, reducing GSM8K degradation from 20.47% to 8.26% at 8 recurrent steps while also improving peak accuracy. However, evaluation is confined to mathematical reasoning, performance does not improve monotonically with depth, and training cost is roughly 2× standard SFT, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to commonsense or strategic planning tasks. A key open question is whether fully reliable, monotonically improving test-time scaling is achievable for the hardest problems, or whether the stability constraint inherently caps the depth of useful reasoning.
