## Problem

Long-horizon LLM-RL training frequently suffers from sudden gradient norm surges and training collapse, driven by high gradient variance that scales with trajectory length and reward sparsity. Existing group-mean baselines (GRPO, RLOO) treat all sequences as equally informative, while the Optimal Global Baseline ignores token-level heterogeneity and is inherently acausal since total sequence energy is unknown during intermediate generation steps. No prior method provides a causal, token-level baseline that accounts for the accumulated stochasticity of each token's gradient contribution.

## Input / Output

Given a prompt and a group of N generated responses with rule-based reward signals, the method computes per-token baselines by weighting each sequence's reward-to-go by its cumulative logit-gradient norm (realized energy) up to that token position. The Logit-Gradient Proxy requires only forward-pass token probabilities—no additional backward passes—making the per-token baseline computation essentially free. The output is a set of N per-token advantage sequences (one per response) that replace the single scalar advantage used by group-mean baselines, enabling the policy to downweight noisy tokens and upweight informative ones within the same update.

## Method

The paper derives the Optimal Token Baseline (OTB) by minimizing the gradient variance of the causal policy gradient estimator, showing that the optimal per-token baseline is the energy-weighted centroid of the reward-to-go, where the weight is the realized energy W_t = Σ_{j≤t} ||s_j||² (the cumulative squared gradient norm up to step t). To make this tractable, the authors invoke the Gradient Consistency assumption (positive cross-temporal gradient correlation in Transformers) to consolidate cross-temporal score correlations into cumulative energy weighting, and introduce the Logit-Gradient Proxy ||e_{y_t} − π_t||² = 1 − 2π(y_t) + ||π_t||², which approximates the true parameter gradient norm using only forward-pass probabilities with zero extra backward passes. The practical OTB is then computed as B̂_t = Σ_i G_t^(i)·Ŵ_t^(i) / Σ_i Ŵ_t^(i) over the group, where Ŵ_t is the cumulative logit-gradient proxy. The paper proves unbiasedness, shows OTB strictly dominates the static OGB in variance, and demonstrates that the proxy is mathematically justified via the RMSNorm structure of modern LLMs, where the hidden-state norm is approximately constant, making the logit gradient norm proportional to the true parameter gradient norm.

## Results

On Single-Turn Reasoning (Qwen3-8B-Base), OTB achieves 30.31 on AIME25, 37.29 on AIME24, 85.08 on AMC23, and 93.43 on MATH500, outperforming all baselines. On Multi-Turn TIR (Qwen2.5-7B), OTB reaches 28.13 on AIME25, 41.46 on AIME24, 79.45 on AMC23, and 84.69 on MATH500. A key efficiency result: OTB matches the performance of group size N=32 with only N=4, reducing total token consumption by 66.03% (Single-Turn) and 68.32% (TIR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OTB outperforms GRPO by 5.0 points on AIME25 and 9.7 on AIME24 in Single-Turn, and by 9.6 on AIME25 and 13.9 on AIME24 in TIR, where GRPO suffers severe training collapse. Against RLOO, OTB leads by 5.9 (AIME25) and 12.3 (AIME24) in TIR, with RLOO showing the weakest performance overall. OPO (which uses sequence length as a gradient proxy) is the closest competitor in Single-Turn (27.29 vs 30.31 on AIME25), but OTB extends its lead to 7.3 points on AIME24 in TIR, where OPO's length-based approximation fails to capture token-level heterogeneity. OGB achieves 30.10 on AIME25 in Single-Turn (narrowly behind OTB's 30.31) but drops to 21.15 in TIR, confirming that its acausal, sequence-level weighting cannot track dynamic token-level energy shifts. In TIR, OTB surpasses SimpleTIR (which filters invalid trajectories) by 1.46 on AIME25 and 3.55 on AIME24, demonstrating that variance reduction alone suffices where trajectory filtering is insufficient. For stability-focused comparisons, OTB maintains stable gradient norms where both TIS and MIS (importance-sampling-based methods) eventually collapse in TIR, indicating that gradient variance—not just policy mismatch—drives training instability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Optimal Global Baseline (OGB) lineage from Dayan (1991), Greensmith et al. (2004), and Weaver & Tao (2013), which weights rewards by total trajectory gradient magnitude, by projecting the variance-minimization objective into a causal, token-level form. It departs from OGB in two critical ways: (1) replacing the acausal total energy with cumulative 'realized energy' available at each generation step, and (2) introducing the Logit-Gradient Proxy to approximate gradient norms from forward-pass probabilities alone, eliminating the need for per-token backward passes. The method also subsumes group-mean baselines (GRPO, RLOO) and the length-based OPO as special cases, positioning itself as the most general member of the optimal-baseline family for LLM-RL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Training collapse in long-horizon LLM-RL is a major practical bottleneck, and OTB's ability to match N=32 performance with only N=4 (reducing token consumption by >65%) directly addresses the sample-efficiency cost of on-policy RL. The Logit-Gradient Proxy is a 'free lunch' requiring zero additional computation, making the method easy to adopt in existing RL frameworks. Open questions include generalization beyond math reasoning to open-ended tasks (e.g., search-augmented agents), the validity of the Gradient Consistency assumption in non-Transformer architectures, and whether the Future Energy Homogeneity assumption (Appendix C.4) holds under highly heterogeneous multi-turn interactions.
