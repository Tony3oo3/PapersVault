## Problem

A recent empirical study showed that synthetic noise from simple distributions (e.g., Gaussian) can serve as a surrogate source domain in semi-supervised transfer learning, but it lacked a theoretical explanation for why noise improves generalization and omitted standard benchmarks such as CIFAR-10/100 and ImageNet-1K. This paper formalizes the setting as Semi-Supervised Noise Adaptation (SSNA) and provides both a generalization bound and extensive empirical validation on standard datasets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265491/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes three inputs: a small set of labeled target samples (e.g., 4 per class for CIFAR-10, 100 per class for ImageNet-1K), a large pool of unlabeled target samples, and synthetic noise vectors drawn from class-specific Gaussian distributions in a 1024-dimensional space (with identity covariance). Noise class indices are assigned one-to-one to target class indices without any semantic correspondence. The output is a standard class label prediction on held-out test target samples, evaluated by Top-1 and Top-5 accuracy.

## Method

NAF projects target samples through a representation extractor g_t and noise through a noise projector g_n into a shared representation space Z, then jointly minimizes three losses: the target empirical risk L_t, the noise empirical risk L_n, and a distributional discrepancy term L_{n,t} (instantiated as Negative Domain Similarity, which negates the average cosine similarity between global and class-wise means of the two domains). A generalization bound (Theorem 4.1) shows that the target error is controlled by the sum of both empirical errors plus the H-divergence in Z, motivating this joint optimization. Unlabeled target samples receive iteratively updated pseudo-labels from a shared classifier to estimate class means for the alignment loss. The key mechanism is that classifying noise into distinct class indices induces a discriminative cluster structure in Z, which then guides target representations to form more separable clusters through distributional alignment, effectively tightening the generalization bound.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with 4 labeled samples per class, NAF achieves 67.90% top-1 accuracy with ResNet-18, outperforming the ERM baseline (55.55%) by 12.35%. With ResNet-50, the gap widens to 15.15% (73.98% vs. 58.83%). On the large-scale ImageNet-1K (1000 classes, 100 labeled per class), NAF reaches 37.10% versus ERM's 36.11% (+0.99%), and on the text dataset AG News-4, NAF achieves 82.82% versus ERM's 78.64% (+4.18%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NAF consistently outperforms the ERM supervised baseline across all 10 evaluated datasets, with the largest gains on CIFAR-10 (+12.35% ResNet-18, +15.15% ResNet-50) and CIFAR-100 (+7.61%). As a plug-in to seven SOTA SSL methods, NAF yields consistent improvements: UDA+NAF gains +20.99% average over UDA on CIFAR-10, FixMatch+NAF gains +9.62%, and even the strongest baseline DST+NAF still improves by +0.84%. On fine-grained datasets (CUB-200, Oxford Flowers-102, Stanford Cars-196), NAF beats ERM by 8.94%, 5.51%, and 7.74% respectively. In contrastive learning comparisons on CIFAR-100, ERM+NAF (49.98%) substantially outperforms both CLIP-inspired contrastive learning (44.15%) and SupCon (44.02%). The only scenario where NAF underperforms is when the noise domain loses its discriminative structure (single-point noise), where it drops below ERM, confirming that class-discriminative structure in the noise is essential for positive transfer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper formalizes the Semi-Supervised Noise Adaptation (SSNA) problem, building directly on the empirical observation by Yao et al. (2025) that Gaussian noise can serve as a surrogate source domain in semi-supervised transfer learning. Theoretically, it extends the semi-supervised domain adaptation framework of Ben-David et al. (2010) by deriving a generalization bound that explicitly accounts for a domain-agnostic noise source, incorporating insights from Li et al. (2021). Conceptually, it departs from standard transfer learning by relaxing the assumption that the source domain must contain semantically meaningful samples, and distinguishes itself from data augmentation by operating at the domain level (aligning a synthetic noise distribution with the target in a shared representation space) rather than the data level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that NAF eliminates the need for real out-of-domain source data, addressing privacy, copyright, and availability constraints that often hinder transfer learning. Its plug-in compatibility with seven SOTA SSL methods (UDA, FixMatch, FlexMatch, etc.) suggests broad applicability without architectural changes. Key limitations include heuristic hyperparameter selection (α, β) and evaluation restricted to standard benchmarks; extending to realistic deployment scenarios (e.g., recommendation systems, medical imaging) and developing adaptive strategies for the trade-off parameters remain open. The theoretical bound also assumes a fixed one-to-one class mapping between noise and target, which may not hold in open-set or hierarchical settings.
