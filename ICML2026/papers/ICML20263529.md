## Problem

Small language models (≤3B parameters) significantly underperform on multi-step mathematical reasoning compared to larger models, and existing remedies like knowledge distillation are computationally expensive and suffer from distribution mismatch (the 'learnability gap'). The paper identifies that stronger models produce fewer reasoning steps with higher per-step information density ('Dense Reasoning') and asks whether this structural property can be transferred to small models at inference time without parameter updates or large-scale teacher data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263529/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference time, the method takes a math problem query and a pre-computed steering vector (a single d-dimensional vector derived from 50 contrastive dense/sparse pairs of the target model's own outputs). The steering vector is injected additively into the residual stream at a fixed middle layer (e.g., L17) at every decoding step, scaled by a scalar λ. The output is an autoregressively generated reasoning trace with fewer but denser intermediate steps, terminated by a boxed final numerical answer. Notably, the steering vector is model-specific and layer-specific, requiring a one-time calibration pass but no further training.

## Method

DenseSteer is a training-free, inference-time activation-steering framework built on three components. First, Dense-Rewriting uses GPT-5.1 to conservatively merge adjacent steps in the SLM's own generated traces, producing dense variants that preserve semantic content and style while increasing reasoning density (ρ = tokens/steps); these form contrastive pairs with the original sparse traces. Second, the steering vector v_ℓ is extracted as the mean difference of final-token hidden states between dense and sparse variants at a chosen layer ℓ, capturing a direction in activation space that separates dense from sparse reasoning. Third, during inference, λ·v_ℓ is added to the residual stream at a middle layer (L16–L17) at each decoding step, biasing generation toward denser reasoning without modifying parameters. The critical design choice is using the target model's own rewritten outputs as positive anchors rather than external model traces, which keeps token-level NLL low and avoids the distribution shift that plagues naive cross-model steering or knowledge distillation.

## Results

On Qwen-2.5-3B-Instruct, DenseSteer achieves 84.8% on GSM8K, 64.6% on MATH-500, 42.5% on AMC, and 10.0% on AIME, yielding a sample-weighted average of 62.5% across five benchmarks—the best overall among all methods tested. On Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct, DenseSteer reaches 79.7% GSM8K and 25.0% AMC, with an average of 52.7%, remaining competitive with the strongest distillation baseline (Mix-Long, 54.2%). The method requires only 50 contrastive pairs and operates entirely at inference time with no parameter updates, while maintaining or reducing token-level NLL relative to the unsteered baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the distillation baselines from Li et al. (2025) (Short CoT, Long CoT, Mix-Long, Mix-Large), DenseSteer achieves the best average on Qwen-2.5-3B-Instruct (62.5% vs. 61.3% for Mix-Large), winning on GSM8K (+1.1), MATH-500 (+3.0), and AIME (+3.3), though Mix-Large edges it on Olympiad-Bench (21.0 vs. 20.7). On Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct, the advantage narrows: Mix-Long retains the best average (54.2% vs. 52.7% for DenseSteer), primarily due to stronger MATH-500 (51.6 vs. 43.4) and Olympiad-Bench (12.3 vs. 10.5) scores, while DenseSteer matches or exceeds distillation on GSM8K and AMC. Against SEAL (Chen et al., 2025a), DenseSteer clearly wins on both GSM8K (84.8% vs. 83.8%) and MATH-500 (64.6% vs. 61.2%) on Qwen-2.5-3B-Instruct, indicating that steering toward dense reasoning is more effective than SEAL's approach of steering away from redundant reflection patterns. Overall, DenseSteer's key advantage is data efficiency: it uses only 50 contrastive pairs at inference time versus the >2,000 teacher-generated samples and full fine-tuning required by the distillation baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the activation-steering paradigm (Panickssery et al., 2024; Turner et al., 2024) from style/safety control to reasoning-structure transfer, and positions itself as a training-free alternative to knowledge distillation for small language models. It departs from prior steering work (e.g., SEAL, which steers away from redundant reflections) by introducing a positive-direction signal derived from the target model's own outputs rewritten into a denser format, using token-level NLL as an explicit distributional-compatibility criterion. The 'Dense Reasoning' framing—fewer steps with higher per-step information density—connects to mechanistic interpretability observations about intermediate-layer representations but is operationalized here as a contrastive steering objective rather than a circuit-level analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DenseSteer offers a practically attractive recipe (50 contrastive pairs, no training, single-layer injection) for practitioners who cannot afford distillation pipelines, and the NLL-based sample-selection criterion provides a general principle for constructing in-distribution steering signals. However, the gains are modest (≈1–2 points on GSM8K for 3B models, near-zero on Llama-3.2-3B), the method cannot inject missing knowledge, and validation is concentrated on mathematical reasoning with only brief transfer checks to LogiQA and MMLU/BBH/HotpotQA. Open questions include whether the middle-layer control point generalizes across architectures, whether the approach scales to 7B+ models where the reasoning gap narrows, and whether denser reasoning is a cause or merely a correlate of accuracy.
