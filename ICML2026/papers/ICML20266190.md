## Problem

Standard GRPO-style RL post-training for LLM reasoning applies uniform sampling and near-uniform weighting across all questions, regardless of difficulty or the model's current competence. The paper identifies three recurring training dynamics—confidence inflation, advantage contraction, and hierarchical convergence—that expose how this uniformity wastes compute on already-saturated easy questions while leaving genuinely hard questions discovery-limited, with the probability of observing even one correct rollout vanishingly small under finite group sizes.

## Input / Output

The method consumes text question-answer pairs from a training set (e.g., MATH) and, for each question, samples G rollouts from the current policy to obtain binary accuracy rewards on the final answer. From these rollouts it computes two per-question scalars—group confidence (geometric mean token probability) and group error rate (fraction of incorrect rollouts)—which together determine a bounded value in [0,1] used to reweight gradients and guide resampling. The output is an updated policy that generates reasoning trajectories ending in a final answer, evaluated by exact-match accuracy on held-out benchmarks.

## Method

CoDaPO introduces a data-centric compute-allocation layer on top of GRPO with three components. CoDaWeighting assigns each question a value v_q = c_q · (1 − 4(d_q − 1/2)²), where the linear confidence term c_q rewards coherent reasoning and the U-shaped difficulty term creates a 'learnable band' that suppresses both nearly-solved (d≈0) and discovery-limited (d≈1) questions. CoDaSampling then resamples the top-K highest-value questions with replacement within each mini-batch, generating fresh rollouts to increase the probability of discovering at least one correct trajectory for hard questions. CoDaLearning applies a value-weighted GRPO objective with token-level micro-averaging (removing implicit length penalties) and no KL-to-reference regularization, executed in two stages: a broad update over the full batch followed by a focused update over the resampled high-value subset. The bounded value v_q acts as a multiplicative gate on per-token gradient weights, effectively annealing updates on saturated questions while amplifying signal on learnable ones.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B, CoDaPO achieves 41.30% average accuracy across seven math benchmarks (MATH500, AIME 2024/2025, AMC 2023, OlympiadBench, Minerva, GSM8K), outperforming the strongest baseline GPG (39.77%) and GRPO (39.08%) by 1.53 and 2.22 percentage points respectively. The gains scale with model size: on Qwen2.5-Math-7B, CoDaPO reaches 46.67% average vs. 45.03% for DAPO, and on Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct it achieves 15.05% vs. 14.40% for DAPO. CoDaPO also generalizes beyond math, improving MMLU (44.81% vs. 43.46% for GRPO), GPQA (24.45% vs. 19.69%), and HumanEval (50.61% vs. 34.76%) on Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B, and boosting coding pass rates from 50.09% to 53.85% on TACO/HumanEval/LiveCodeBench.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoDaPO consistently outperforms all four RL baselines (GRPO, DAPO, Dr.GRPO, GPG) across all three model backbones and all seven math benchmarks. On Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B, CoDaPO's 41.30% average surpasses the closest competitor GPG (39.77%) by 1.53 pp and GRPO (39.08%) by 2.22 pp, with the largest single-benchmark gain on OlympiadBench (36.16% vs. 32.87% for DAPO, +3.29 pp). On Qwen2.5-Math-7B, CoDaPO leads DAPO (the strongest baseline at 45.03%) by 1.64 pp, with notable advantages on AIME 2024 (30.49% vs. 29.77%) and AMC 2023 (63.50% vs. 59.16%). On Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct, CoDaPO achieves 15.05% vs. DAPO's 14.40%, confirming backbone-agnostic gains. Cross-domain evaluation further shows CoDaPO beating GRPO on MMLU (+1.35 pp), GPQA (+4.76 pp), and HumanEval (+15.85 pp), and on coding tasks (TACO +1.74 pp, HumanEval +4.27 pp, LiveCodeBench +5.25 pp), indicating that the compute-allocation strategy transfers beyond the math training domain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GRPO and its recent variants (DAPO, Dr.GRPO, GPG), which all modify the RL objective (clipping, normalization, KL terms) but treat all questions uniformly in sampling and weighting. CoDaPO departs from this objective-centric lineage by taking a data-centric, compute-allocation perspective: it diagnoses three structural pathologies in GRPO training (confidence inflation, advantage contraction, hierarchical convergence) and addresses them through per-question value weighting and value-guided resampling rather than through a new surrogate loss. The paper explicitly contrasts itself with curriculum learning, arguing that its non-monotonic, dynamics-driven weighting (suppressing both very easy and very hard items) is fundamentally different from static difficulty scheduling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The formal characterization of GRPO's training dynamics—linking asymmetric clipping to entropy collapse, group normalization to advantage contraction, and finite sampling to hierarchical convergence—provides a useful diagnostic lens for practitioners tuning RL post-training pipelines. The compute-allocation framing (concentrating trials on a 'learnable band') is a practical and general principle that could inform sampling strategies beyond this specific method. However, CoDaPO does not resolve the underlying confidence inflation or raise the theoretical ceiling of RL; it reallocates a fixed budget more efficiently. Open questions include whether the U-shaped difficulty prior and linear confidence weighting are optimal, whether the two-stage update adds meaningful benefit over simpler reweighting, and whether the approach transfers to settings without verifiable rewards.
