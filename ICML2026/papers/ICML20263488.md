## Problem

Existing uncertainty quantification methods for long-form LLM generation either assign a single confidence score to an entire paragraph or decompose paragraphs into atomic facts and treat them as independent units, ignoring the structural dependencies among facts within a paragraph. This 'bag-of-facts' approach discards the logical flow, hierarchical relationships, and latent semantic interdependencies that characterize long-form text, leading to coarse and potentially misleading uncertainty estimates that fail to capture organizational variation across generations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263488/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a text query q and N=20 independently sampled paragraph-length responses from an LLM, GAUSS designates one paragraph as the anchor and the remaining N-1 as references. Each paragraph is decomposed into atomic facts (via a prompted decomposition model), embedded with a sentence encoder (mpnet-base-v2, 768-d), and organized into a semantic graph. The primary output is a scalar paragraph-level uncertainty score U(q) computed as the mean fused Gromov-Wasserstein alignment cost between the anchor graph and all reference graphs; a GAUSS-atomic variant additionally yields per-fact uncertainty scores by examining the optimal coupling matrix entries for each anchor node.

## Method

GAUSS represents each generated paragraph as a semantic graph whose nodes are atomic facts (with sentence-embedding features) and whose structure matrix encodes pairwise cosine distances between fact embeddings, capturing inter-fact semantic dependencies. Uncertainty is quantified via the fused Gromov-Wasserstein distance between the anchor graph and each reference graph, where the semantic cost combines local cosine similarity with a global entailment gate (Qwen2-32B-Instruct) that suppresses alignment when the reference paragraph lacks factual support, and the structural cost penalizes mismatches in pairwise relational patterns across the two graphs. The trade-off parameter α (set to 0.5) balances these two cost components. Theoretically, the paper proves Lipschitz continuity of both the alignment distance and the resulting uncertainty measure under semantic and structural perturbations, and derives exponential convergence bounds via McDiarmid's inequality showing that reliable estimates require only modest sample sizes, with convergence rate governed by the LLM's intrinsic graph-generation inconsistency factor D.

## Results

GAUSS achieves the strongest negative Spearman correlation (SC) with factuality across all five LLMs and three datasets. For example, on llama3-8b-instruct/Bios, GAUSS reaches SC = −0.707 and PC = −0.708, outperforming the next-best baseline (Dis-Rating, SC = −0.650). On WildHallu with qwen2-7b-instruct, GAUSS attains SC = −0.707 and PC = −0.715, while the best competing method (Dis-Single) only reaches SC = −0.655. GAUSS also maintains competitive calibration (UCCE/QCCE), often matching or exceeding the best baseline by 0.01–0.05 in absolute error.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GAUSS consistently outperforms all five baselines on Spearman and Pearson correlation across the majority of model–dataset pairs. Against LUQ [35], the gap is largest: LUQ frequently yields near-zero or positive SC (e.g., +0.094 on falcon-7b/Bios), while GAUSS achieves strongly negative SC (−0.412 to −0.707), indicating LUQ's semantic-only entailment averaging fails to capture structural divergence. Against Gen-Binary [34], GAUSS wins on Bios by 0.05–0.11 in SC (e.g., −0.707 vs. −0.599 on llama3-8b) and on WildHallu by 0.02–0.07 (e.g., −0.707 vs. −0.641 on qwen2-7b); Gen-Binary is closest on LongFact/falcon-7b (−0.644 vs. −0.656). Against Centrality [16], GAUSS leads by 0.06–0.20 in SC on Bios and WildHallu (e.g., −0.707 vs. −0.507 on qwen2-7b/WildHallu), though Centrality occasionally achieves lower UCCE (e.g., 0.042 vs. 0.144 on mistral-7b/Bios), suggesting it calibrates well in some settings despite weaker correlation. The single-sample baselines Dis-Rating and Dis-Single [34] are substantially weaker, often showing positive or near-zero SC on Bios, confirming that internal LLM confidence signals are unreliable for long-form uncertainty.

## Relation to Prior Work

GAUSS builds directly on the fused Gromov-Wasserstein (FGW) distance from Vayer et al. (ICML 2019) as its core graph-alignment mechanism, repurposing it from general structured-data matching to paragraph-level uncertainty quantification. It departs from prior long-form UQ methods (LUQ, Gen-Binary, Dis-Rating/Dis-Single) that treat atomic facts as independent units scored via entailment or LLM confidence, and from the bipartite-graph centrality approach of Jiang et al. [16], by constructing a separate per-paragraph semantic graph whose edges encode inter-fact dependencies and whose alignment cost captures both structural and semantic divergence. The theoretical apparatus (Lipschitz continuity of the FGW distance, McDiarmid-based concentration) is borrowed from optimal transport and concentration-of-measure literature but applied here for the first time to long-form generation uncertainty.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GAUSS provides the first long-form UQ framework with formal robustness and convergence guarantees, giving practitioners a principled way to bound how many samples are needed for a stable uncertainty estimate and how sensitive the score is to embedding or decomposition noise. The consistent outperformance across five LLMs and five datasets validates that structural coherence is a genuine, previously underexploited source of uncertainty beyond semantic agreement. Key open questions remain: the method does not disentangle epistemic from aleatoric uncertainty, it inherits errors from auxiliary fact-extraction and entailment models (limiting applicability to subjective or contested claims), and it has not been tested on very long multi-paragraph documents or in settings where the 'correct' structure is highly variable by design.
