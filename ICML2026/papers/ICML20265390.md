## Problem

Existing safety mechanisms for diffusion models are designed for U-Net architectures or text-to-image synthesis only, leaving multimodal diffusion transformers (MM-DiTs) like FLUX.1 vulnerable in instruction-driven image-to-image editing, where users can bypass text-centric safeguards by supplying unsafe reference images. No unified, training-free method exists that simultaneously mitigates unsafe content generation in both T2I and I2I tasks within the MM-DiT framework while preserving generation quality and editing fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265390/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For T2I, the method takes a text prompt and produces a 1024×1024 safe image; for I2I, it takes a reference image plus a text editing instruction and produces a safe edited image. The method additionally requires a small set of pre-collected unsafe anchors (D-dimensional patch embeddings extracted from MM-Attn outputs at the final diffusion timestep on a few unsafe images, typically 2 per concept), which are computed offline and reused at inference. The intervention operates entirely at inference time on the output image token patches, adding only ~3 MB of VRAM and ~4 s of latency over the base model.

## Method

UVR is grounded in an analysis of multimodal attention (MM-Attn) dynamics that identifies a task-independent semantic start-up stage (first few diffusion steps) where unsafe semantics rapidly emerge in output image patches, followed by task-specific amplification (T2I) or prolonged reference interference (I2I). The method has two components: (1) Visual Safety Localization, which computes similarity between current output patch embeddings and the offline unsafe anchors to produce a binary spatial mask, refined via connected-component selection and morphological dilation into a core mask M̃ and an expanded mask M̂; (2) Targeted Safety Regulation, which adaptively modulates attention scores—applying strong suppression (λ̲) to inter-group flows for core unsafe tokens, milder suppression (λ̄) for the expanded context, and enhancement (λ_o > 1) for benign O^img→O^img flows—while injecting Gaussian noise into core unsafe tokens only during the early start-up stage (t ≤ t₀). This restricts unsafe information from being injected into and propagated through output patches without modifying model parameters, achieving 91% and 77% erasure rates on T2I and I2I respectively.

## Results

On T2I nudity erasure, UVR achieves the lowest unsafe count of 40/854 (95.3% erasure) on I2P and 97/1039 (90.7% erasure) on the adversarial Unsafe-1K set, while maintaining CLIP score of 31.32% (matching FLUX.1-dev's 31.31%) and FID of 76.76. On I2I editing, UVR reduces unsafe outputs to 46/854 on I2P and 151/1039 on Unsafe-1K (vs. 175 and 402 for unmodified FLUX.1-kontext), while achieving the highest VQA score of 88.42% on unsafe-reference editing. The method is training-free, adds only ~3 MB VRAM, and runs at 25s/sample (unsafe) vs. 31s for SLD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UVR outperforms all five baselines on erasure effectiveness across both T2I and I2I settings. On T2I I2P, UVR produces 40 unsafe images vs. 46 (SLD, closest competitor), 48 (ESD), 53 (UCE), 81 (DES), and 86 (EraseAnything); on the harder adversarial Unsafe-1K set, the gap widens dramatically to 97 vs. 193 (ESD), 220 (SLD), 239 (UCE), 242 (DES), and 265 (EA). For I2I editing with unsafe references, UVR achieves 46 unsafe on I2P and 151 on Unsafe-1K, substantially beating the best baselines (DES/ESD/SLD at 69 and 319 respectively) and the unmodified FLUX.1-kontext (175 and 402). In terms of generation quality, UVR matches or slightly exceeds the unmodified model on CLIP (31.32% vs. 31.31% for T2I) and achieves the highest VQA score (88.42%) on I2I editing, while SLD and UCE show comparable CLIP but weaker erasure. The key differentiator is that UVR is the only method that simultaneously achieves strong erasure in both T2I and I2I without fine-tuning, whereas baselines like EraseAnything and UCE degrade significantly on I2I (97 and 115 unsafe on I2P respectively).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the concept-erasure literature for diffusion models (ESD, SLD, UCE, DES, EraseAnything) from U-Net cross-attention and T2I-only settings to the multimodal attention (MM-Attn) architecture of FLUX-series DiTs, covering both text-to-image synthesis and instruction-driven image-to-image editing under a single framework. It builds on ConceptAttention (Helbling et al., 2025) for patch-level localization via attention-output similarity and on the information-bottleneck mechanism of Schulz et al. (2020) for noise-based feature restriction, but repurposes both for safety regulation rather than interpretability or attribution. The key conceptual departure is the output-centric, task-agnostic intervention on O^img patches, as opposed to prior text-centric erasure that manipulates prompt embeddings or cross-attention projections, which the authors show is bypassable in I2I editing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UVR addresses a practical gap: FLUX.1-kontext and similar MM-DiTs remain vulnerable to unsafe I2I editing via harmful reference images, and no prior training-free method handles both T2I and I2I in this architecture. The attention-dynamics analysis (task-independent start-up stage, task-specific amplification/interference) provides an interpretable basis for *when* and *where* to intervene, which could inform future safety designs beyond FLUX. Limitations include reliance on Grounded-SAM for offline anchor collection (less reliable for abstract concepts), context-insensitive intervention that may over-suppress legitimate content (e.g., medical or artistic nudity), and evaluation primarily on the FLUX family. Open questions include adaptive, content-aware intervention schedules and whether the mechanism transfers to non-FLUX MM-DiTs with different block structures.
