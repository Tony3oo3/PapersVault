## Problem

Deep ensembles remain the gold standard for epistemic uncertainty quantification, but their computational cost scales linearly with ensemble size, making them impractical for billion-parameter foundation models. Existing implicit ensemble methods like LoRA-Ensemble reduce overhead but still require learning new low-rank parameters per member. The paper asks whether the intrinsic structure of pretrained weights—specifically their singular value decomposition—can be exploited to create diverse ensemble members with near-zero additional parameters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264514/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images (224×224) or text sequences (up to 512 tokens) fed into pretrained transformer backbones (DINOv1/v2 ViT-S, BERT-base, LLaMA-2-7B). At inference, each of the M ensemble members performs a full forward pass through the shared backbone with its own singular-value-scaled weight matrices, producing M probability distributions that are averaged into a single calibrated prediction. The only trainable parameters are per-member singular value vectors and bias vectors, adding less than 1% overhead relative to the base model.

## Method

SVE decomposes each target linear projection matrix via SVD, freezes the left and right singular vectors as a shared 'knowledge basis,' and instantiates M trainable copies of the singular value diagonal (plus per-member bias vectors). Each member's singular values are initialized with a small multiplicative Gaussian perturbation (σ_init ≈ 0.01) around the pretrained values, and diversity emerges during joint training as stochastic mini-batch sampling drives members toward distinct rescalings of the same orthogonal directions. All M members are trained simultaneously with an average cross-entropy loss, and non-negativity of singular values is enforced via clamping. The key efficiency gain is that SVE adds only min(m,n) parameters per weight matrix per member (versus O((m+n)r) for LoRA-based ensembles), yielding roughly 10× fewer trainable parameters than LoRA-Ensemble while matching or exceeding its calibration quality.

## Results

On Flowers102 (DINO ViT-S/16, M=4), SV-Ensemble achieves 95.4% accuracy, 1.0% ECE, 0.18 NLL, and 0.07 Brier score, outperforming all 8 baselines on every metric. On Oxford Pets with only ~200 training iterations, SV-Ensemble reaches 90.1% accuracy and 2.2% ECE, while Deep Ensemble (89.2%, 13.3% ECE) and Batch Ensemble (70.5%, 48.7% ECE) fail to calibrate. On ARC-Easy with LLaMA-2-7B (M=16), SV-Ensemble achieves 85.8% accuracy and 3.8% ECE, matching LoRA-Ensemble's accuracy (86.0%) while halving its ECE (9.0%) and using an order of magnitude fewer parameters (1.0% vs 10.3% overhead).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SV-Ensemble outperforms Deep Ensemble on Flowers102 (95.4% vs 91.5% acc), DTD (79.5% vs 65.4% acc), and Oxford Pets (90.1% vs 89.2% acc, 2.2% vs 13.3% ECE), while losing to it on CIFAR-100 accuracy (85.6% vs 85.4% is actually a near-tie; Deep Ensemble gets 85.4%). Against LoRA-Ensemble, SV-Ensemble wins on Flowers102 (95.4% vs 94.6% acc), DTD (79.5% vs 78.4% acc), Oxford Pets (90.1% vs 86.1% acc, 2.2% vs 9.0% ECE), SST-2 ECE (2.8% vs 3.8%), and ARC-Easy ECE (3.8% vs 9.0%), but loses on CIFAR-100 (85.6% vs 88.2% acc, 2.1% vs 1.1% ECE). MC Dropout and EDL are decisively outperformed across all datasets, with MC Dropout dropping to 25.9% accuracy on Oxford Pets. Batch Ensemble catastrophically fails on Oxford Pets (48.7% ECE) and underperforms SV-Ensemble on all other vision tasks. On ARC-Easy, SV-Ensemble (3.8% ECE) approaches BLoB N=10 (3.6% ECE) and C-LoRA (4.3% ECE) in calibration while matching or exceeding their accuracy (85.8% vs 85.5% and 84.4%), and substantially outperforms Bayes-LoRA LA (5.4% ECE) and Checkpoint Ensemble (9.8% ECE). Overall, SV-Ensemble achieves the best or second-best result in nearly every setting while adding only ~1% parameter overhead versus 10.3% for LoRA-Ensemble and 700% for Deep Ensemble.

## Relation to Prior Work

SVE directly extends Singular Value Fine-tuning (SVF; Sun et al., 2022) and its large-model variant SVFit (Sun et al., 2024), which established that pretrained weight matrices can be decomposed via SVD and adapted by training only singular values while freezing singular vectors. The conceptual leap is repurposing this single-model adaptation mechanism for ensemble construction: by instantiating per-member singular values over a shared singular-vector basis, SVE creates functionally diverse models without learning new weight-space directions. This positions SVE as a more parameter-efficient alternative to LoRA-Ensemble (Mühlematter et al., 2026), which trains full low-rank matrices per member, and to explicit Deep Ensembles (Lakshminarayanan et al., 2017), which duplicate entire model weights. The method also draws on the broader implicit-ensemble literature (BatchEnsemble, MIMO, FiLM-Ensemble) and the interpretability finding that transformer singular vectors encode semantically meaningful knowledge directions (Millidge & Black, 2022; Elhage et al., 2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SVE makes principled epistemic uncertainty estimation accessible in resource-constrained settings by reducing parameter overhead to under 1% of the base model (vs. 700% for deep ensembles and ~10% for LoRA-Ensemble), validated across 22M–7B parameter backbones in both vision and NLP. A notable finding is that SVE's advantage grows with backbone pretraining quality—performing worst with random initialization and best with DINOv2—confirming that the method's effectiveness is contingent on the singular vectors encoding meaningful knowledge directions. Key limitations include inference FLOPs remaining comparable to deep ensembles (one forward pass per member), non-trivial integration with quantized backbones, and the risk of inheriting backbone biases since only existing directions are rescaled. Open directions include distilling the implicit ensemble into a single calibrated network and hybridizing SVE with LoRA-Ensemble to combine calibration efficiency with additional expressivity.
