## Problem

Vision-language models exhibit a paradoxical 'spatial intelligence gap': they achieve Olympiad-level symbolic logic yet fail on elementary tasks like counting stacked blocks under occlusion. Diagnostic probing reveals the bottleneck is neither the visual encoder (which extracts sufficient geometric features) nor the reasoning engine (which is intrinsically capable), but the absence of a stable view-consistent intermediate representation that bridges egocentric perception and logical reasoning, causing spatial hallucinations and reasoning drift.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265732/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single egocentric 2D RGB image and a natural-language spatial query (e.g., 'How many blocks are there?') and produces a two-part output: first, a structured orthographic three-view description (front, left, top) encoded as JSON with view-specific spatial primitives (block coordinates with stacking cues for counting; ordered perceptual sequences for object reasoning), and second, a final spatial answer (integer count or discrete 8-direction label) preceded by a view-grounded reasoning trace that integrates evidence in a fixed front→left→top order written in first-person mental simulation style.

## Method

3ViewSense implements a 'Simulate-and-Reason' pipeline that decomposes spatial reasoning into two capability-oriented stages. Stage I (Orthographic Mental Simulation) is trained via SFT to induce canonical front/left/top orthographic views from a single egocentric image, using programmatically extracted annotations from synthetic data with a proven bijective uniqueness condition ensuring each 3D block configuration maps to a unique set of projections. Stage II (View-Grounded Reasoning) is trained via SFT to condition on the inferred views and produce natural-language reasoning traces (generated by a teacher model and filtered for answer correctness) followed by the final answer. Starting from the Stage II checkpoint, GRPO-based reinforcement learning with math-verifiable rewards (strict exact-match or slack partial-credit for counting/direction) further refines correctness while preserving the view-grounded reasoning behavior. The key architectural insight is that introducing the orthographic views as an explicit intermediate representation transforms ill-posed 3D spatial reasoning into tractable pattern recognition on structured 2D planes, reducing geometric ambiguity without requiring external 3D tools or auxiliary modalities.

## Results

On the in-domain OrthoMind-3D benchmark, 3ViewSense-4B-rl-strict achieves 95.0% on Block Counting, 88.2% on Block Counting (Attr.), 98.7% on Object Counting, and 93.3% on Object Positioning, substantially outperforming all open-source and specialized baselines. On the out-of-domain split, the slack-reward variant reaches 38.7% on Block Counting (↑82.5% over the Qwen3-VL-4B base) and 76.1% on Object Positioning (↑63.0%). Gains transfer to external benchmarks, with MindCube-Tiny improving from 27.2% to 38.9% (+43.0% relative), SPBench-SI from 22.2% to 25.4% (+14.4%), and ViewSpatial from 35.5% to 37.1% (+4.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

3ViewSense-4B-rl-strict dominates all open-source and specialized baselines on every in-domain task, achieving 95.0% on Block Counting versus 10.6% (Qwen3-VL-8B) and 10.4% (SpaceOm-4B), and 93.3% on Object Positioning versus 47.6% (Qwen3-VL-8B) and 17.6% (SpaceOm-4B). Even the SFT-only variant (33.4% Block Count, 91.0% Object Position) already surpasses all non-proprietary baselines by large margins. Against proprietary models, 3ViewSense-4B-rl-strict matches or exceeds Gemini-3-pro on Block Counting (95.0% vs. 13.8%) and Object Counting (98.7% vs. 83.3%), though Gemini-3-pro retains a slight edge on Object Position (Attr.) (93.6% vs. 93.2%). On out-of-domain and external benchmarks, 3ViewSense-4B-rl-slack outperforms the Qwen3-VL-4B base by 82.5% relative on OOD Block Counting and 43.0% on MindCube-Tiny, while also surpassing SpatialLadder-3B (38.5% MindCube-Tiny) and SpaceOm-4B (35.5% MindCube-Tiny). The ablation in Table 4 further shows that the view-guided reasoning process (not just the training data) drives generalization: Direct QA collapses to 1.3% on SPBench-SI and 7.2% on ViewSpatial, whereas 3ViewSense reasoning retains 21.2% and 34.1% respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by repurposing the engineering-drawing convention of orthographic projections (front/left/top) as an explicit intermediate representation for VLM spatial reasoning, departing from the three dominant paradigms it surveys: auxiliary 3D modalities/tools, unstructured RL or curriculum training, and implicit mental-imagery models (MindCube, APC). Its two-stage Simulate-and-Reason pipeline (Orthographic Mental Simulation → View-Grounded Reasoning, refined with GRPO) is conceptually closest to the mental-modeling line but replaces free-form 3D hallucination with structured, geometrically constrained 2D projections. The accompanying OrthoMind-3D benchmark is positioned as filling a diagnostic gap left by existing spatial benchmarks (MindCube, CV-Bench, SPBench, OmniSpatial, ViewSpatial), which the authors argue lack the granularity to isolate 2D-to-3D projection and mental-rotation failure modes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The diagnostic finding that a lightweight MLP probe on frozen visual features outperforms the full VLM on block counting, and that injecting explicit three-view text descriptions yields large gains (up to +217% for Gemini-3-pro), reframes the spatial-intelligence gap as an interface/representation problem rather than a perception or reasoning-capacity problem—a useful reframe for the field. However, OOD generalization is mixed (Object Count drops ~36% under SFT), three fixed orthographic views cannot capture support relations, dynamics, or affordances, and the in-domain results (95% on block counting) benefit from the simplicity of the synthetic task. Open questions include whether view-induction uncertainty can be estimated adaptively, whether the approach scales to open-world scenes with richer physical priors, and whether the view-grounded reasoning behavior survives integration into larger general-purpose multimodal models without catastrophic forgetting.
