## Problem

Prior compositional generalization in offline GCRL relies on trajectory stitching over temporally contiguous segments, which cannot reuse the same task-relevant transformation across varying task-irrelevant contexts. The paper identifies that successful analogy transduction—transplanting task-endogenous analogies into novel contexts—requires out-of-combination (OOC) extrapolation, where both the context and the analogy are individually present in data but their pairing is unseen, a capability that prior methods lack.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a current state vector s and a goal state vector g from manipulation environments (robotic arm, drawer, window, buttons, cubes). At inference, the high-level policy outputs a k-step dual analogy vector α^∨(s_t, s_{t+k}) = φ(s_{t+k}) − φ(s_t) as a sub-goal displacement, and the low-level policy outputs a primitive action conditioned on both the current state and that proposed analogy. The value function and both policies factor their computation into separate anchor (state) and displacement (analogy) encoders joined by a low-rank bilinear product, rather than processing the raw state-goal pair jointly.

## Method

CTA introduces a temporal distance difference field α(s,g)(x) = d*(x,g) − d*(x,s) as a theoretically grounded analogy representation, proven under a goal-conditioned endogenous BCMP to be invariant to task-exogenous contexts and sufficient for optimal goal-reaching. In practice, this field is approximated by the dual analogy α^∨(s,g) = φ(g) − φ(s), learned via goal-conditioned IQL with an inner-product temporal distance. The architecture is a hierarchical IQL where the value function V(s,g) = Ω₁(s)·Ω₂(α^∨(s,g)) and both the high-level (analogy-proposing) and low-level (action-executing) Gaussian policies use bilinear transduction with a shared bottleneck dimension b ≪ d, enforcing a low-rank structure that enables OOC extrapolation to unseen analogy-context combinations. Training uses an action-free IQL loss for the value function and advantage-weighted regression for both policies, with the high-level policy trained on k-step sub-trajectories to expand the pool of reusable analogies.

## Results

On the OGBench manipulation suite (8 environments, 8 seeds), CTA achieves an average success rate of 56.3%, improving by approximately 42% over the strongest baseline HIQL√ (39.3%). Gains are most pronounced on puzzle environments where compositional generalization is critical: CTA scores 84% on puzzle-4x4 versus 34% for the best baseline (GCIQL√), a ~2.5× improvement. CTA achieves the best or near-best result on 6 of 8 tasks, with the largest margins on puzzle-4x4 (+50 pts), puzzle-4x5 (+7 pts), and puzzle-4x6 (+6 pts). In a direct OOC case study (held-out context-task pairs), CTA achieves 94% direct success on scene and 100% on puzzle-4x4, versus 86–87% for HIQL√+α√ which uses the same dual analogy but lacks OOC extrapolation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CTA substantially outperforms all non-representation baselines: GCBC (1.9% avg), QRL (1.5%), and CRL (7.5%) are far below CTA's 56.3%, confirming that explicit state-goal representations are essential for compositional generalization in these environments. Among value-learning baselines, GCIQL (38.6%) and HIQL (11.0%) are also clearly outperformed. The most informative comparison is against the dual-representation variants: CTA (56.3%) beats HIQL√ (39.3%) by ~43% and GCIQL√ (34.1%) by ~65%, showing that the advantage comes from analogy transduction with OOC extrapolation rather than from the dual goal representation alone. Critically, HIQL√+α√ (39.6%), which replaces the dual goal representation with the same dual analogy α√(s,g) but lacks bilinear transduction for OOC extrapolation, performs nearly identically to HIQL√ (39.3%), isolating the OOC extrapolation mechanism as the source of CTA's gains. On the puzzle-4x4 environment specifically, CTA (84%) achieves roughly 2.5× the score of the best baseline (GCIQL√ at 34%), and in the direct OOC case study CTA reaches 100% direct success versus 95% for HIQL√+α√, confirming that CTA directly solves unseen context-task combinations rather than relying on indirect in-distribution detours.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends offline goal-conditioned RL (building on IQL and HIQL) by introducing a new axis of compositional generalization—analogy transduction—that complements the dominant trajectory-stitching paradigm. The analogy representation (temporal distance difference field) draws on dual goal representations (Park et al., 2026) and distance-difference geometry, while the OOC extrapolation mechanism borrows bilinear transduction (Netanyahu et al., 2023) and grounds the theory in a goal-conditioned variant of the BCMP framework (Du et al., 2019). The closest prior work is goal-conditioned bisimulation analogies (Hansen-Estruch et al., 2022), but CTA departs by using optimal temporal distances rather than on-policy bisimulation metrics, avoiding bootstrapping and reward-matching pathologies that limit offline applicability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete gap in offline GCRL—trajectory stitching cannot reuse the same task transformation across varying contexts—and provides both a theoretical characterization (GCE-BCMP + Proposition 4.4) and a practical method (CTA) that closes it, yielding ~42% average improvement over the strongest baseline on OGBench. Key limitations include Assumption 4.3 (task-block coordinate consistency), which may be violated in realistic environments, and the gap between the theoretically invariant distance-difference field and its learned dual-analogy surrogate, for which invariance is not guaranteed. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to non-manipulation domains (e.g., navigation, language-grounded tasks) and how to relax the endogenous/exogenous separability assumption.
