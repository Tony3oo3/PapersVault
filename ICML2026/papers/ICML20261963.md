## Problem

Text-to-image models are trained predominantly on short prompts (10–20 tokens) and evaluated with coarse metrics, yet professional workflows require long, detail-rich prompts (200+ tokens) with high constraint density, structured compositionality, and strict attribute-to-entity binding. Existing long-prompt benchmarks remain shorter, simpler, and rely on weak diagnostic metrics (e.g., CLIPScore), leaving a gap in understanding how models handle fine-grained compositional failures under detail overload.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261963/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes 4,116 expert-validated prompts averaging 284.89 tokens, each specifying multiple entities with nested modifiers, spatial positions, and inter-object relationships. Generated images (512×512 for SD1.5-family, 1024×1024 for others) are scored via MLLM-based binary judgments across four dimensions: Character Attributes (object/animal/person), Character Locations (nine-region spatial partitioning), Scene Attributes (background/lighting/style), and Entity Relationships (spatial/interactive). A mini-benchmark of 800 prompts and a lightweight 3B evaluator are provided for resource-constrained settings.

## Method

DETAILMASTER combines an automated data construction pipeline with a multi-stage evaluation workflow. The construction pipeline starts from human-annotated captions (DOCCI, Localized Narratives), then applies MLLM-based character identification, dual-model localization (MLLM + YOLOE-11L with IoU/BLIP arbitration), hierarchical class-specific attribute extraction with visual grounding via cropped sub-images, and LLM-based prompt refinement to produce detail-rich prompts. The evaluation pipeline uses dual-model character presence detection (MLLM + YOLOE), then performs per-attribute binary MLLM judgments on cropped or highlighted regions for each of the four dimensions. A 2×2 ablation on a modified SD-XL (with T5 encoder and Timestep-Aware Semantic Connector) disentangles the effects of expanded token limits (77 vs. 512) from long-prompt training, showing that long-prompt training yields greater gains than simply increasing context capacity, and that the two factors are synergistic.

## Results

On DETAILMASTER (4,116 prompts, avg. 284.89 tokens), GPT Image-1 achieves the highest scores: 59.41% (Object), 48.04% (Animal), 40.40% (Person), 53.92% (Character Locations), and 63.07% (Entity Relationships), while Scene Attributes are near-saturated at 97.50–98.85%. Even the best proprietary model plateaus around 50% on the three hardest compositional dimensions, revealing a substantial gap between scene-level fidelity and fine-grained attribute/relationship adherence. Among open-source models, Qwen-Image leads with 51.01% (Object), 49.11% (Animal), 46.98% (Char. Loc.), and 60.04% (Entity Rel.), closely followed by FLUX.1-dev (51.47% Object, 47.49% Entity Rel.). A controlled 2×2 ablation on SD-XL shows that long-prompt training with a 77-token limit (30.50% Object) outperforms short-prompt training with a 512-token limit (25.37% Object), and the combination of both yields the best result (30.50% → 30.50% Object, 13.07% Entity Rel.), confirming a synergistic effect.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT Image-1 outperforms Gemini 2.0 Flash across all dimensions, with the largest margins on Object attributes (+3.97 pp), Character Locations (+9.18 pp), and Entity Relationships (+12.29 pp), establishing the current upper bound for long-prompt compositional generation. Among open-source models, Qwen-Image surpasses FLUX.1-dev on Person attributes (+4.39 pp), Character Locations (+5.41 pp), and Entity Relationships (+12.55 pp), while FLUX.1-dev edges ahead on Object attributes (+0.46 pp) and Animal attributes (−3.28 pp for Qwen-Image). Long-prompt optimized models built on SD1.5/SD-XL (ELLA, LongAlign, ParaDiffusion) improve over their respective baselines but remain far below modern T5-based models: ParaDiffusion (best in group, 35.25% Object) still trails FLUX.1-dev by 16.22 pp on Object and 21.99 pp on Entity Relationships. The ablation study confirms that long-prompt training with a 77-token limit (30.50% Object, 13.07% Entity Rel.) outperforms simply expanding to 512 tokens with short-prompt training (25.37% Object, 10.12% Entity Rel.), and the combined 512-token + long-prompt configuration achieves the best ablation result (30.50% Object, 13.07% Entity Rel.), demonstrating that both factors are necessary and synergistic. Across all 19 models, a consistent negative correlation exists between prompt token length and generation accuracy, with Character Locations being the most failure-prone dimension due to conflicts with real-world positional priors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of T2I evaluation benchmarks and long-prompt generation research. It extends the compositional T2I evaluation line (HRS-Comp, T2I-CompBench, TIFA, Gecko) from short prompts to the long-prompt regime, while providing the first fair, fine-grained benchmark for the long-prompt optimization methods (ELLA, LongAlign, LLM4GEN, ParaDiffusion, LLM Blueprint) that previously lacked a common evaluation standard. The data construction pipeline repurposes existing human-annotated captioning datasets (DOCCI, Localized Narratives) and augments them via LLM/MLLM-based attribute extraction, departing from prior long-prompt benchmarks that rely on LLM-generated or synthetic prompts. The ablation study introduces a controlled 2×2 design (token limit × training prompt length) that no prior T2I work has systematically isolated.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that even frontier proprietary models (GPT Image-1, Gemini 2.0 Flash) plateau around 50% on spatial and relational dimensions, exposing a fundamental gap between semantic understanding and compositional rendering that the field has not yet addressed. The finding that long-prompt training with a 77-token limit outperforms short-prompt training with a 512-token limit redirects attention from context-window expansion toward training data and curriculum design. Open questions the paper identifies include structure-aware prompt parsing (modeling syntactic graphs rather than flat token sequences), object-centric latent representations to prevent attribute leakage, and curriculum-based training from simple to complex prompts — all of which remain unsolved and likely require architectural innovations beyond current diffusion or flow-matching paradigms.
