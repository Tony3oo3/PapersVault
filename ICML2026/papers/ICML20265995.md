## Problem

Transferring pre-trained models to new human sensing modalities (e.g., from image to mmWave radar or IMU) with only a few labeled sensor samples remains unsolved: existing few-shot and cross-domain methods either require large-scale same-modality source data, depend on shared semantic spaces or paired cross-modal data, or suffer severe negative transfer from modality shift. Moreover, these methods neglect the resource constraints of edge devices, making them impractical for real-world deployment on wearables and smartphones.

## Input / Output

XTransfer takes publicly available pre-trained source models (e.g., ResNet18 on miniImageNet, ResNet18-1D on Newsgroup or VoxCeleb) from any modality and a small set of labeled target sensor data (3–10 shots per class, no unlabeled target data required). A trainable connector (adaptive convolutional Pre-header, bilinear Resizer, and an encoder-decoder pair) handles arbitrary shape mismatches between heterogeneous source layers and sensor inputs. The output is a restructured compact model composed of selectively recombined source layers with PCA-pruned channels, deployable on edge devices (smartwatch, smartphone, Raspberry Pi) to produce class labels or regression values (e.g., blood pressure).

## Method

XTransfer combines two core mechanisms. First, the Splice-Repair-Removal (SRR) pipeline performs layer-wise model repairing: a generative transfer module (adversarially trained connector) aligns target-modality latent features to frozen source-layer anchors in a PCA-reduced orthogonal feature space, minimizing Mean Magnitude of Channels (MMC) shifts via an anchor-based repair loss with Hungarian-algorithm class pairing; PCA-derived component weights then guide channel removal to discard redundant channels. Second, the Layer-Wise Search (LWS) control performs an NAS-inspired, resource-constrained search over source layers, using a Silhouette-score-based value function to select and incrementally recombine only useful repaired layers while discarding others. A pre-search check estimates repair rates via an exponential growth model to bypass unnecessary SRR calls, and a dynamic search range mechanism adaptively widens or narrows the candidate pool to balance accuracy and efficiency in multi-source settings with large candidate pools.

## Results

Under cross-modality FSL settings (5-shot, LOOCV), Our-Single (ResNet18 on miniImageNet) achieves 71.8% on HHAR, 78.4% on WESAD, 87.0% on Writing, and 55.6% on Emotion, reaching or surpassing the MetaSense oracle (69.0%, 64.0%, 83.3%, 56.3%) on most datasets. Our-Multi (Multi-ResNet18 on 5 image datasets) further improves to 74.3% on HHAR and 86.1% on Writing. In terms of accuracy-to-resource (ATR) efficiency, Our-Single achieves ATR values of 1.42–2.76, surpassing single-source baselines by 1.6–29× and multi-source baselines (MDDA, MCW) by 16.6–98×.

## Baselines & Comparisons

XTransfer (both Our-Single and Our-Multi) outperforms all single-source baselines (ProtoNet, DAPN, MAML, SemiCMT, GPT2) by 15–45 percentage points in 5-shot accuracy across most target datasets. Against the multi-source baselines MDDA and MCW, the gap is even more dramatic: on HHAR 5-shot, Our-Single reaches 71.8% versus MDDA's 17.7% and MCW's 25.4%. Compared to the MetaSense oracle (trained from scratch with sufficient target data), XTransfer matches or exceeds oracle accuracy on WESAD (78.4% vs. 64.0%), Writing (87.0% vs. 83.3%), and ChestX (30.4% vs. 28.1%) in 5-shot, while remaining slightly below on HHAR (71.8% vs. 69.0%—actually above) and Emotion (55.6% vs. 56.3%). In ATR (accuracy-to-resource ratio), Our-Single achieves 1.42–2.76 versus 0.02–0.08 for MDDA and 0.04–0.07 for MCW, representing a 16.6–98× efficiency improvement. The ablation (SRR without LWS) confirms that the full pipeline is necessary: SRR alone still beats baselines on HHAR/WESAD/Gesture but falls below oracle overall, while adding LWS closes the gap and substantially boosts ATR.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of few-shot learning, cross-modal transfer, and neural architecture search, but does not build directly on a single prior method. It departs from same-modality FSL (MetaSense, ProtoNet, MAML) and cross-domain FSL (DAPN, MDDA, MCW) by eliminating the requirement for same-modality source models, and from cross-modal methods (SemiCMT, ImageBind) by removing the need for paired data or shared semantic spaces. The SRR pipeline repurposes GAN-style adversarial training for a lightweight connector, PCA for dimensionality reduction in an anchor space, and Hungarian-algorithm class pairing, while the LWS control borrows NAS-inspired search over a layer-wise action space. The combination of layer-wise latent feature repair with resource-constrained layer recombination under a cross-modality few-shot setting is the paper's core conceptual contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By enabling reuse of publicly available pre-trained models (e.g., from PyTorch Hub) with only a handful of labeled sensor samples, XTransfer substantially lowers the barrier to deploying human sensing applications on resource-constrained edge devices, reducing both data collection costs and training compute. The ATR improvements of up to 98× over multi-source baselines are practically significant for edge deployment. Open questions include: how to automatically select the optimal source model for a given target task, whether the approach generalizes to transformer-based or foundation-model backbones, and whether the PCA anchor space (2 components) remains effective as modality gap widens further. The method's multi-component pipeline (SRR + LWS + dynamic search range) also raises questions about robustness and ease of tuning in new deployment scenarios.
