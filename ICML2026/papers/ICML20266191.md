## Problem

Existing visual mechanistic interpretability methods face a fundamental tradeoff: activation maximization produces high-frequency artifacts that deviate from the natural image distribution, while dataset retrieval yields unstable, boundary-biased explanations, and prompt-guided diffusion introduces an information bottleneck that limits faithfulness to internal features. No prior framework provides a principled way to simultaneously balance mechanistic faithfulness (activating the target feature) and perceptual interpretability (staying close to natural images).

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained SAE (transcoder) feature activation as an intrinsic score s(x), a pre-trained text-conditioned diffusion prior p_θ (e.g., Stable Diffusion), and a guidance strength β calibrated to a target expected activation level. It produces a set of generated RGB images that simultaneously exhibit high SAE feature activation and remain close to the natural image distribution. The sampling is training-free: both the diffusion model and the SAE are pre-trained, and β is determined by one-dimensional root-finding to match a desired faithfulness level.

## Method

The paper reformulates feature interpretation as a KL-minimal distributional inference problem: find the distribution q that minimizes KL(q‖p) subject to a soft faithfulness constraint E_q[s(X)] ≥ m. The unique solution is an exponentially tilted distribution q_β(x) ∝ p_θ(x) exp(β s(x)), which the authors call the KL-minimal soft-constraint (KLSC) principle. To sample from this tilted distribution, they propose EnergyDPS, which reinterprets the SAE feature activation as an energy function and replaces the standard measurement log-likelihood guidance in diffusion posterior sampling (DPS) with energy guidance ∇_{x_i} s(x̂_0) injected at each reverse diffusion step. Unlike proxy-guided diffusion methods that optimize a text prompt embedding, EnergyDPS directly samples from the full probability space without a prompt-family bottleneck, avoiding the information loss inherent in cross-modal text-to-image steering. The approach is validated on DINOv3 ResNeXt-Large transcoder features, showing lower KL divergence from the natural prior and higher blinded interpretability scores than MACO and DiffExplainer baselines.

## Results

On DINOv3 SAE features, EnergyDPS achieves the best QualiCLIP score of 0.5791 on 100 random features, substantially outperforming MACO (0.4228) and DiffExplainer (0.3259), while also attaining the highest NRQM (6.7821 vs. 4.4869 for MACO and 6.7460 for DiffExplainer). In a blinded interpretability study with human and AI raters, EnergyDPS scores 2.92 (human), 2.90 (GPT), and 3.09 (Gemini) on a 1–4 scale, exceeding MACO (1.92/2.09/2.57) and DiffExplainer (1.95/2.14/1.90) by roughly one full point across all raters, and yields the lowest weighted description variance (0.2505), indicating more consistent semantic convergence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EnergyDPS consistently outperforms both baselines on the primary interpretability metric QualiCLIP: on 100 random DINOv3 features it reaches 0.5791 versus 0.4228 for MACO (activation maximization) and 0.3259 for DiffExplainer (prompt-guided diffusion), a gap of roughly 0.16 and 0.25 points respectively. On NRQM (no-reference image quality), EnergyDPS (6.7821) surpasses MACO (4.4869) by a wide margin and edges out DiffExplainer (6.7460) slightly. In the blinded interpretability study, EnergyDPS leads all three rater types by approximately one point on the 1–4 scale (e.g., 2.92 human vs. 1.92 MACO and 1.95 DiffExplainer) and achieves the lowest weighted description variance (0.2505), indicating that its generated image sets convey a clearer, more consistent shared semantic concept. MACO produces high-frequency artifacts that reduce perceptual interpretability, while DiffExplainer's reliance on text prompts introduces an information bottleneck that limits its ability to capture fine-grained or non-verbal SAE feature semantics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes visual mechanistic interpretability as a KL-minimal distributional inference problem, departing from the sample-wise paradigm (top-K retrieval, activation maximization with regularization, prompt-guided diffusion) that dominates prior work. It builds directly on diffusion posterior sampling (Chung et al., 2023) by replacing measurement log-likelihood guidance with an intrinsic SAE feature activation as an energy function, and on the SAE/transcoder literature for defining monosemantic features. The key conceptual departure is the KLSC (KL-minimal soft-constraint) principle, which replaces hard thresholding with exponential tilting and provably avoids the boundary bias, threshold instability, and interpretability gap that the paper formally identifies in prior paradigms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The distributional view provides a unifying theoretical lens that explains *why* existing visual MI methods fail (support discontinuity, finite-pool winner-takes-most, prompt information bottleneck), giving practitioners principled criteria for evaluating interpretability pipelines. EnergyDPS offers a practical, training-free sampler that avoids the text-prompt bottleneck, which is particularly relevant as SAE features in vision models are often fine-grained and non-verbal. Key limitations include the computational cost of diffusion sampling (many reverse steps), the assumption that the natural image distribution is a minimally lossy reference space, and the gap between natural-image plausibility and truly human-understandable concepts. Open questions include whether fewer-step samplers or distillation can match the quality, and how the framework extends to non-visual modalities where the reference prior must be redefined.
