## Problem

Existing deepfake detectors treat real and synthetic images as independent instances in a binary classification framework, overlooking the structural property that synthetic images are implicitly paired with real images sharing the same semantic source. This causes models to overfit to generator-specific low-level artifacts rather than capturing generation-induced relational discrepancies, leading to poor generalization to unseen manipulation methods.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265225/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB face images as input and produces a real/fake probability score via a lightweight binary classifier on CLIP ViT-L/14 features. A key operational detail is that the model consumes semantically aligned real-fake pairs rather than independent samples: for each synthetic image, a corresponding real image is retrieved via nearest-neighbor search in the L2-normalized CLIP embedding space. At inference, video-level predictions are obtained by averaging frame-level confidence scores, following the DeepfakeBench protocol.

## Method

BPL consists of three components: (1) Real-Fake Pairing, which infers latent semantic correspondences between real and synthetic images by cross-distribution retrieval in the CLIP embedding space (or uses explicit source indices when available); (2) Pair-aware Discrepancy Learning, a contrastive objective that maximizes the L2 distance between paired real-fake features, augmented with a discrepancy inversion mechanism that flips the loss sign once the pair distance exceeds a threshold m, preventing over-separation and overfitting; (3) Bias-only Optimization, which freezes all CLIP ViT-L/14 projection weights and updates only bias parameters, preserving the pretrained semantic manifold while allowing lightweight activation re-centering for generation-induced distributional shifts. The total loss combines the discrepancy-driven objective (weighted by λ) with a standard binary cross-entropy classification loss.

## Results

On the DF40 cross-manipulation benchmark (trained on Celeb-DF c23), BPL achieves an average AUC of 88.86%, surpassing the strongest baseline Effort (86.29%) by 2.57% and the best traditional artifact-based method SBI (60.05%) by 28.81%. For cross-dataset generalization, BPL reaches 93.1% AUC on FaceForensics++, 83.8% on DFDC, and 86.6% on DFDCP, outperforming Effort by +3.6%, +13.1%, and +5.8% respectively. Under extreme data scarcity (10% training data), BPL degrades by only 1.35% in average AP versus 4.26% for Effort, while still outperforming Effort trained on 100% of the data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BPL outperforms all nine baselines on average AUC across the DF40 cross-manipulation benchmark. Against the strongest VLM-based competitor Effort (ICML 2025), BPL gains +2.57% average AUC (88.86% vs. 86.29%), with the largest per-method margins on MobSwap (+5.75%) and DFLab (+6.04%). Traditional artifact-based methods (F3Net, SPSL, SRM, CORE, RECCE, SBI) all fall in the 48–60% AUC range, with BPL exceeding the best among them (SBI at 60.05%) by nearly 29 points. UCF (64.38%) and TALL (52.90%) represent intermediate VLM-based and layout-based approaches, both substantially outperformed. On cross-dataset evaluation, BPL's advantage over Effort widens dramatically on DFDC (+13.1% AUC) and DFDCP (+5.8%), while on FaceForensics++ the gap is +3.6%. In the PEFT comparison (Table 7), BPL also surpasses LoRA (r=1/4/8) and LayerNorm Tuning by 12–34% average AUC, confirming that bias-only adaptation is more effective than low-rank or normalization-based parameter-efficient strategies for this task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VLM-based deepfake detection paradigm (CLIP backbones as in Effort, UCF, AIDE) by reframing the problem from independent-sample binary classification to pair-wise discrepancy learning over semantically aligned real-fake pairs. It draws on contrastive deepfake detection (Larue et al., Hong et al.) for the margin-based pair objective, and on paired-data approaches (AlignedForensics, DDA) for the structural motivation, but differs by constructing pairs via CLIP cross-distribution retrieval rather than generator-specific reconstruction. The bias-only fine-tuning strategy departs from both full fine-tuning and SVD-based subspace decomposition (Effort), arguing that additive activation shifts suffice for forgery adaptation without introducing new representational directions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that freezing all projection weights and updating only bias terms yields a +33% AUC improvement over full fine-tuning challenges the prevailing PEFT assumption that learning new feature-space directions is necessary for domain adaptation, and offers a practically attractive, low-capacity alternative. The pair-aware formulation provides a principled lens for isolating generation-induced artifacts from semantic variance, potentially transferable to other detection tasks (e.g., text-to-image forensics). However, evaluation is concentrated on face-manipulation benchmarks within the Celeb-DF domain, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to non-face generative content, video-level temporal cues, or the rapidly evolving landscape of diffusion-based synthesis.
