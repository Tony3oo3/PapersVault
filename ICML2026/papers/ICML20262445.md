## Problem

Pre-Norm Transformers offer stable gradient propagation but suffer from unbounded hidden-state magnitude growth that dilutes deeper layer contributions, while Post-Norm variants achieve stronger representational capacity but exhibit severe training instability at scale. Prior hybrid attempts to combine both paradigms within a single residual stream lack robustness outside carefully tuned settings, reflecting a structural tension that cannot be resolved by simply mixing normalization operations along one path.

## Input / Output

SiameseNorm is a drop-in architectural replacement for the standard Transformer residual block, requiring no architecture-specific tuning. For language pretraining it consumes text token sequences (up to 2048 tokens) and produces next-token distributions; for vision it takes ImageNet images and outputs class labels; for diffusion it accepts noisy latents with text prompts and outputs denoised latents. The only additional parameters are lightweight LN layers, increasing total parameter count and FLOPs by under 0.1%.

## Method

SiameseNorm maintains two coupled residual streams sharing identical computation modules: an X-stream that applies LN after each residual update (Post-Norm-like, keeping magnitude bounded) and a Y-stream that accumulates updates without main-path normalization (Pre-Norm-like, preserving an identity gradient route). At each layer, the shared residual block F_i receives input X_i + LN(Y_i), and its output O_i is added to both streams, with the X-stream additionally normalized. Two auxiliary mechanisms ensure compatibility with standard Pre-Norm training recipes: a Normalized Input LN on the fused representation before F_i, and Depth-wise Scaling that attenuates the update injected into the bounded X-stream by 1/√(l+1). Gradient analysis shows the block Jacobian's diagonal blocks recover the Pre-Norm and Post-Norm transition matrices respectively, while off-diagonal coupling terms allow each residual block to receive optimization signals from both pathways simultaneously.

## Results

On 1.3B dense language models trained on 100B tokens, SiameseNorm achieves the best average PPL of 10.43 at LR=1×10⁻³ and 10.48 at LR=2×10⁻³, outperforming Pre-Norm (10.84 and 10.89) by 0.41 and 0.41 PPL respectively. At the aggressive LR=2×10⁻³, SiameseNorm reaches 39.6% on Arithmetic (vs. 28.1% for Pre-Norm), a 41% relative improvement. On the 15A2B MoE model, SiameseNorm reduces PPL from 7.92 to 7.76 and improves average downstream score from 61.26 to 63.07. Across all learning-rate regimes, SiameseNorm is the only method that maintains stable convergence while achieving the best PPL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SiameseNorm outperforms Pre-Norm (the strongest stable baseline) by 0.37–0.41 PPL across all learning-rate settings, with the gap widening at higher LRs (0.41 at LR=2×10⁻³) and longer training (0.25 PPL at 350B tokens). Post-Norm, Deep-Norm, SpanNorm, and HybridNorm all diverge at LR≥1×10⁻³, making them non-competitive in the aggressive regimes where SiameseNorm excels; at the conservative LR=4×10⁻⁴, SiameseNorm still beats HybridNorm (10.57 vs. 10.91 PPL) and SpanNorm (10.57 vs. 11.00). ResiDual, the closest multi-path competitor, shows loss spikes at LR≥1×10⁻³ and is beaten by 0.21–0.79 PPL. Hyper-Connections-2×DHC is the strongest non-diverging competitor at LR=1×10⁻³ (10.73 vs. 10.43 PPL) but still trails SiameseNorm by 0.30 PPL and 0.33 Avg Score. On the 15A2B MoE model, SiameseNorm improves over Pre-Norm by 0.16 PPL and 1.81 Avg Score, with the largest gain on Arithmetic (58.2 vs. 48.6). Peri-LN, a Pre-Norm variant, is consistently beaten by 0.28–0.41 PPL across all settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

SiameseNorm builds directly on the Transformer residual block (Vaswani et al., 2017) and borrows the depth-wise residual scaling from DeepNorm (Wang et al., 2024) as an auxiliary mechanism, while using HybridNorm (Zhuo et al., 2025) as its Post-Norm-style sub-stream. Its key departure from prior hybrid approaches (Mix-LN, HybridNorm, SpanNorm, ResiDual, Hyper-Connections) is the identification of a structural tension: within a single residual stream, preserving Pre-Norm's identity-gradient path and applying Post-Norm's main-path normalization are fundamentally incompatible. Rather than alternating or mixing normalization across layers or submodules, SiameseNorm structurally decouples the two roles into parallel coupled streams that share all computation modules, a topology that no prior single-stream hybrid achieves.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Pre-Norm vs. Post-Norm trade-off has constrained Transformer architecture design for years, with Post-Norm's superior representational capacity remaining inaccessible under aggressive training regimes. SiameseNorm offers a drop-in architectural replacement with under 0.1% parameter overhead that is compatible with standard Pre-Norm training recipes, validated across dense LLMs (400M–1.3B), MoE (15B), ViT, and DiT. The primary limitation is that the analysis is empirical rather than providing convergence guarantees, and the two-stream design introduces additional architectural complexity that may complicate further modifications. Open questions include whether the paradigm extends to even deeper models (100+ layers), whether the Post-Norm stream's dominance in output (42.6% vs. 16.2% in Logit Lens) implies the Pre-Norm stream could be further simplified, and how the approach interacts with emerging normalization-free designs like DyT.
