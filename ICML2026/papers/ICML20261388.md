## Problem

Existing backdoor attacks on VLMs rely on dirty-label data poisoning that modifies text labels alongside visual triggers, creating detectable image-text mismatches. Clean-label backdoor attacks, which preserve original labels and only modify images, have only been explored for traditional image/video classification and fail on VLMs because surrogate-model-based feature approximation cannot encode the rich semantic content required for captioning and VQA tasks.

## Input / Output

The method takes a clean image, a triggered image (clean image plus a pre-computed universal adversarial perturbation), the triggered image's text description, and a binary GradCAM-guided mask as inputs, and produces a single poisoned image where modifications are confined to the masked region. The poisoned image must retain the exact same text label as the clean image (the clean-label constraint) while embedding trigger features that cause the VLM to output a semantic substitution (e.g., 'people' → 'dog'). The UAP is generated once via projected gradient ascent on CLIP and reused across all samples, and the diffusion model (SD v1.4) performs generation only within the mask, leaving unmasked pixels unchanged.

## Method

CBV generates poisoned images by modifying the denoising score function during the reverse diffusion process to incorporate multimodal guidance from both the triggered image and its text description. At each reverse step, the original score s_θ(x_t, t) is augmented with two gradient terms: an image-level similarity gradient ∇S_I(φ(x̃_t), φ(x_trig)) weighted by λ_I and a text-level similarity gradient ∇S_T(φ(x̃_t), ψ(y_trig)) weighted by λ_T, both projected onto the GradCAM mask to restrict modifications to semantically important regions. A theoretical derivation via Bayes' theorem shows that the conditional score ∇log p(x_{t-1}|x_t, y_trig) decomposes into the unconditional score minus the trigger-likelihood gradient, justifying the score modification as an exact (up to a Gaussian approximation) conditional denoising step. This mechanism incrementally embeds triggered image features into the generated sample while preserving visual naturalness, achieving >80% ASR across four VLMs with computation time (~29s) an order of magnitude lower than iterative optimization-based baselines.

## Results

On MSCOCO image captioning, CBV achieves ASR of 85.77% (LLaVA-v1.5-7B), 83.83% (MiniGPT-v2), 84.12% (Qwen-2.5-VL-7B), and 87.71% (InstructBLIP-7B), while preserving CIDEr scores within ~10 points of clean baselines (e.g., 121.08 vs. 131.20 for LLaVA). On VQA v2, CBV reaches ASR of 83.58–85.87% across the four VLMs with VQA accuracy dropping by only 1–4 points relative to clean models. These ASR values are comparable to the strongest dirty-label VLM backdoor attacks (TrojVLM: 94.60%, VL-Trojan: 94.93%) yet exceed all prior clean-label methods by a factor of 7–12×.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the five dirty-label VLM backdoor baselines, CBV's ASR (83.6–87.7%) is within 5–10 points of the strongest methods (TrojVLM 94.6%, VL-Trojan 94.9%, BadToken 93.3%) while being the only clean-label approach, and it substantially outperforms Shadowcast (55.9–57.8% ASR) by roughly 30 points. Against the five prior clean-label methods (CBAVR, Narcissus, HTBA, SAA, IP), CBV achieves 7–12× higher ASR (e.g., 85.77% vs. 11.54% for Narcissus on LLaVA), a gap the authors attribute to diffusion-model-generated poisoned images encoding richer trigger semantics than surrogate-model approximations. On normal functionality, CBV's BLEU@4 and CIDEr scores remain within 1–10 points of clean baselines, on par with or slightly better than most dirty-label methods. CBV also runs in 28.7 s per sample versus 185–218 s for the optimization-based clean-label baselines (HTBA, SAA, IP, Narcissus), a ~7× speedup. The paper further shows CBV is the only method satisfying all four properties simultaneously: trigger stealthiness, clean-label constraint, no training manipulation, and targeting VLMs (Table 1).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two previously separate lines of work: clean-label backdoor attacks (CBAVR, Narcissus, HTBA, SAA, IP), which were limited to image/video classification and relied on surrogate-model feature-space approximation, and VLM backdoor attacks (TrojVLM, BadToken, MABA, VL-Trojan, Shadowcast), which all required dirty-label text modification. CBV departs from both by using a diffusion model's score-matching reverse process as the generation mechanism, borrowing the guided-score idea from Guo et al. (2025b) for targeted adversarial example synthesis, and applying it for the first time to produce clean-label poisoned samples for VLMs. The UAP trigger design draws on Moosavi-Dezfooli et al. (2017), and the GradCAM-guided mask localizes modifications using standard saliency tools, but the combination of these components within a diffusion-based score-matching pipeline is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Clean-label backdoor attacks on VLMs are significantly harder to detect than dirty-label variants because no text labels are altered, yet this paper shows they can achieve ~85% ASR—nearly matching dirty-label methods—while producing visually natural poisoned images. This raises the practical threat level for VLMs deployed in safety-critical settings (medical imaging, autonomous driving, content moderation), where data provenance is hard to audit. A notable open question is the near-total failure of existing defenses (STRIP, Neural Cleanse, TIJO, Fine-pruning), suggesting the field lacks effective countermeasures for semantically integrated, diffusion-generated triggers. Future work should explore whether the attack generalizes to video VLMs, multi-turn dialogue, or models with stronger data-augmentation pipelines.
