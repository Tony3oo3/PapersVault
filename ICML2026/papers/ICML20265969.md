## Problem

Post-training alignment (RLHF, DPO) causes mode collapse in LLMs, severely reducing output diversity. Prior work attributed this to algorithmic limitations such as inadequate reward models or majority-favoring optimization, but this paper identifies a more fundamental, pervasive data-level driver: typicality bias in preference data, whereby human annotators systematically favor familiar, fluent, and predictable text. This means that even with a perfect reward model and optimization process, inherent bias within preference datasets will still drive mode collapse across the majority of alignment methods.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard text prompt (e.g., a creative writing topic, a dialogue persona, or a math problem specification) and reformulates it into a distribution-level query that explicitly requests k candidate responses paired with their verbalized probabilities (e.g., 'Generate 5 jokes about coffee and their corresponding probabilities'). The output is a structured list of (response, probability) tuples, from which downstream tasks can sample via probability-weighted or uniform selection. The method is training-free, model-agnostic, and requires no logit access, operating purely through prompt engineering at inference time.

## Method

Verbalized Sampling (VS) is a training-free prompting strategy that mitigates mode collapse by shifting the semantic target of the prompt from a single instance to a probability distribution over responses. The theoretical foundation shows that typicality bias (formalized as α > 0 in the Bradley-Terry reward decomposition) induces a power-transform sharpening of the reference policy with exponent γ = 1 + α/β > 1, which under flat rewards compresses probability mass toward the base model's mode for instance prompts. The key insight is that for distribution-level prompts, the representativeness heuristic (from cognitive psychology) makes diverse, high-entropy samples more 'typical' than repetitive ones, so the same sharpening mechanism amplifies the reward gap in favor of representative distributions, recovering pre-training diversity. Three variants are proposed: VS-Standard (single-call distribution query), VS-CoT (with chain-of-thought reasoning before generating the distribution), and VS-Multi (multi-turn distribution elicitation), all of which allow diversity tuning via probability thresholds in the prompt without altering decoding parameters.

## Results

On creative writing tasks (poem, story, joke), VS-Standard increases semantic diversity by 1.6–2.1× over direct prompting, and human evaluation shows a 25.7% improvement in diversity scores (e.g., 2.39 vs. 1.90 on a 4-point scale for poems). VS recovers 66.8% of the base model's diversity after alignment. On synthetic data generation for math, VS-Multi achieves 37.5% average downstream accuracy across MATH500, OlympiadBench, and Minerva Math, outperforming the no-synthetic-data baseline (32.8%) and all prompting baselines (Direct 30.6%, CoT 33.7%, Sequence 34.3%, Multi-Turn 33.2%). In dialogue simulation, GPT-4.1 with VS matches a dedicated fine-tuned Llama-3.1-8B persuadee simulator on donation amount distribution alignment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares Verbalized Sampling against four prompting baselines under a fixed computation budget of N total responses: Direct (single instance per call), CoT (chain-of-thought then single instance), Sequence (list of k items per call), and Multi-Turn (one item per turn across N turns, from West & Potts 2025a). VS-Standard consistently and significantly outperforms all four baselines on semantic diversity across poem, story, and joke generation (one-tailed t-tests, p < 0.001). On synthetic data generation, VS-Multi achieves 37.5% average downstream math accuracy, beating Multi-Turn (33.2%) by 4.3 points and Sequence (34.3%) by 3.2 points, while Direct prompting actually degrades below the no-synthetic-data baseline (30.6% vs. 32.8%). In dialogue simulation, VS with GPT-4.1 matches the donation-amount distribution of a fine-tuned Llama-3.1-8B model, and DeepSeek-R1 with VS even surpasses it on median donation alignment. The paper also notes an emergent scaling trend: larger models (GPT-4.1, Gemini-2.5-Pro) gain 1.5–2× more diversity from VS than smaller models (GPT-4.1-Mini, Gemini-2.5-Flash), suggesting the method's benefits grow with model capability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant algorithmic framing of mode collapse (inadequate reward models, KL-regularized optimization amplifying majority responses, SFT overfitting) by identifying a data-level cognitive cause: typicality bias in human preference annotations, grounded in well-established findings from cognitive psychology (mere-exposure effect, processing fluency, representativeness heuristic). For solutions, it contrasts with training interventions, decoding strategies (μ-sampling, min-p), and handcrafted prompting by proposing a principled, training-free prompting method whose theoretical justification follows directly from the identified cause. The paper also extends prior work on LLMs verbalizing distributions (Tian et al. 2023, Xiong et al. 2024) by providing the first theoretical account of why distribution-level queries specifically recover diversity, rather than merely observing the effect empirically.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that aligned models retain substantial inherent diversity (VS recovers ~67% of base-model diversity) and that a simple inference-time prompt can partially undo alignment-induced collapse has broad practical implications for creative generation, social simulation, and RL exploration. The emergent trend that more capable models benefit more from VS suggests the technique will grow in value as models scale, but also introduces a dependency on model capability that limits applicability to smaller or less capable systems. Open questions include whether typicality bias can be mitigated at the reward-model level (complementing the inference-time fix), how VS interacts with RL training loops for action-space exploration, and whether the probability verbalization remains reliable as models evolve. The computational overhead of generating k candidates per call remains a practical constraint for latency-sensitive deployments.
