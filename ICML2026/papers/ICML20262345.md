## Problem

Fully quantizing Large Reasoning Models to 4-bit floating-point (W4A4KV4) causes severe reasoning degradation that existing PTQ and QAT pipelines fail to recover, even with increased training budgets. The paper identifies that FP4 failures concentrate on low-entropy tokens—precise symbolic commitments like digits and operators—where quantization noise inflates the probability of sampling non-top-1 alternatives, and these small errors cascade into complete reasoning failures.

## Input / Output

The method takes a BF16 LRM checkpoint (R1-Qwen-14B or R1-Llama-8B), a reasoning trace dataset (OpenThoughts3 Math subset), and a small calibration set (Wikitext-2, 256 samples × 512 tokens) as inputs, and produces an FP4-quantized model with calibrated RoPE-consistent scaling and shift vectors folded into projection weights. A key structural constraint is that Stage-2 QAT must reuse the exact same reasoning traces from Stage-1 BF16 fine-tuning (D_TAQ ⊆ D_FT), and the KV cache is quantized in E1M2 format rather than the default E2M1 to reduce training loss. At inference, the model accepts standard text prompts and generates chain-of-thought reasoning traces with final answers under NVFP4 or MXFP4 arithmetic.

## Method

ReQAT combines three components targeting the identified low-entropy failure mode. (i) Trace-Aligned QAT (TAQ) is a two-stage procedure where Stage-1 performs standard BF16 fine-tuning and Stage-2 applies QAT on the same reasoning traces, so quantization-aware updates repeatedly act on the same high-confidence symbolic commitments; gradient analysis shows this reallocates learning signal toward low-entropy token positions. (ii) Selective Entropy Minimization (SEM) adds an auxiliary loss L_SEM = L_SFT + λ·(1/T)Σ w_t·H_t that selectively sharpens the predictive distribution at low-entropy positions using a soft weight w_t = max(0, 1−(H_t−H_min)/(τ−H_min+ε)) with τ set to the 75th percentile of minibatch entropy. (iii) Q-FIT jointly calibrates pre-RoPE paired channel scaling (s, folded into projection weights) and post-RoPE key shifting (m, applied at inference) by grid-searching (α_s, α_m) ∈ [0,1] to minimize MSE between BF16 and KV4 attention outputs, adapting the transformation mix to layer-specific key-cache outlier patterns.

## Results

On R1-Qwen-14B, ReQAT (full TQS) achieves 68.02% AIME-120 accuracy under MXFP4 W4A16 at 350M fine-tuning tokens, surpassing the BF16 full fine-tuning baseline of 65.46% by 2.56 points. Under the most aggressive NVFP4 W4A4KV4 setting, ReQAT (TQ) reaches 65.94% AIME accuracy, exceeding both the BF16 baseline (56.83%) and BF16 FT (65.46%) without additional training budget. End-to-end throughput on NVIDIA Blackwell hardware shows up to 3.93× speedup on DGX Spark and 3.13× on B200 relative to BF16, with Q-FIT introducing only 4–5% overhead over native NVFP4.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReQAT decisively outperforms all five baselines across every quantization setting. Against AWQ (PTQ, W4A16), which achieves 51.46–53.02% AIME, ReQAT reaches 68.02% under MXFP4 W4A16 — a gap of over 15 points. Against QuaRot (PTQ, W4A4KV4, 40.42%) and FlatQuant (PTQ, W4A4KV4, 49.16%), ReQAT under NVFP4 W4A4KV4 achieves 65.63%, exceeding both by 16.5 and 16.5 points respectively. Against ParetoQ (QAT), which plateaus at 62.29% (W4A16), 58.03% (W4A4), and 58.86% (W4A4KV4), ReQAT surpasses it by 5.7, 7.9, and 6.8 points at matched budgets. Against NVIDIA's QAD (54.29% under MXFP4 W4A16), ReQAT leads by 13.7 points. Notably, ReQAT is the only method that exceeds the BF16 full fine-tuning accuracy (65.46%) under any FP4 configuration, while all baselines remain at or below the BF16 baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends standard QAT for LLMs (ParetoQ, QAD) by identifying a specific failure mode—quantization-induced sampling errors at low-entropy tokens—and designing all three components (TAQ, SEM, Q-FIT) around that insight. For KV cache quantization, it departs from prior work that applies either pre-RoPE scaling (QServe) or post-RoPE shifting (SageAttention) in isolation, instead jointly calibrating both via a lightweight grid search (Q-FIT). The entropy-based diagnostic builds on Wang et al. (2025a), who used token entropy to analyze RL training dynamics in LRMs, but repurposes it here as a training signal for quantization-aware optimization rather than an analysis tool.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ReQAT provides the first practical recipe to deploy reasoning models at full W4A4KV4 precision while matching or marginally exceeding BF16 fine-tuning accuracy, with 3–4× throughput on production Blackwell hardware—removing a major barrier to serving LRMs at scale. The entropy insight (that FP4 failures concentrate on confident symbolic tokens, not uncertain transitions) is a reusable diagnostic that could inform other low-precision training strategies. Key limitations include dependence on SFT data quality (weak traces limit TAQ gains), the small margin over BF16 FT (~0.5 points on AIME), and untested generalization to RL-based or distillation-based supervision paradigms.
