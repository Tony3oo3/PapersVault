## Problem

Existing image-to-image (I2I) evaluations focus on instruction following and output aesthetics or perceptual quality, but largely ignore whether the output preserves the semantic content and spatial structure of the input. Mask-based consistency checks fail when edits induce legitimate global changes (e.g., new shadows), and general-purpose MLLMs remain insensitive to fine-grained pixel-level misalignment, producing incorrect fidelity judgments even when semantic content appears consistent.

## Input / Output

The model receives two RGB images (input before processing and output after processing, each with max side length ≤1344 px) together with a free-text I2I instruction prompt. It produces structured JSON in two formats: a concise Format 1 with a binary verdict (Yes/No/NULL) and a list of error-type labels per dimension (e.g., ["add","replace","remove"] for semantic, ["misalignment","repainting"] for structure, ["noise","blur","color cast","exposure degradation","artifact"] for low-level), and a detailed Format 2 that adds a chain-of-thought preservation analysis followed per-error-type descriptions. The three dimensions are evaluated independently, and the Low-level dimension can return NULL when the instruction explicitly requests that type of processing (e.g., denoising).

## Method

StableI2I fine-tunes Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct through a multi-stage pipeline: (1) SFT on Binary & Type QA, Multiple-choice QA, and Free-form Descriptive data to establish task format and preserve visual perception; (2) GRPO reinforcement learning with custom set-overlap rewards for multiple-choice and binary-answer tasks to improve generalization; (3) the RL-trained model annotates large-scale unlabeled I2I data, which GPT-5 filters before being converted into Open-ended QA with CoT rationales; (4) full fine-tuning on the combined original and augmented data. A key data-construction insight is an error-amplification pipeline that deliberately corrupts semantic descriptions fed to restoration models, forcing them to produce samples with controlled semantic drift and thereby mitigating the long-tail scarcity of subtle consistency violations. To strengthen the ViT encoder's weak pixel-level alignment, the authors introduce Texture-Aware Enhancement Data (near-identical 95–98% crops of the same image) and an Image Identity task, while Multiple-choice QA converts open-ended error descriptions into deterministic choice questions that are amenable to structured RL rewards.

## Results

On StableI2I-Bench, StableI2I achieves 89.10% Binary Accuracy and 83.00% Strict Accuracy (averaged across Structure, Semantic, and Low-level dimensions), outperforming the strongest general-purpose MLLM, Gemini-3-pro (82.28% Binary / 67.15% Strict), by 6.8 and 15.9 points respectively. The advantage is most pronounced on the Structure Level, where StableI2I reaches 85.40% Binary Accuracy versus 71.52% for Gemini-3-pro, reflecting its enhanced pixel-level alignment capability. When evaluated under the simplified StableI2I template (removing benchmark-specific instructional priors), general MLLMs degrade substantially while StableI2I maintains its scores, confirming the gap is intrinsic rather than prompt-driven.

## Baselines & Comparisons

StableI2I outperforms all 15 compared MLLMs across every fidelity dimension on StableI2I-Bench. The largest margin is on Structure Level, where StableI2I (85.40% Binary) exceeds the best MLLM Gemini-3-pro (71.52%) by 13.9 points, and GPT-4o (59.90%) by 25.5 points, highlighting that pixel-level structural alignment remains a critical weakness of general-purpose models. On Semantic Level, the gap narrows (82.80% vs. 83.61% for Gemini-3-pro), as most MLLMs handle high-level content preservation reasonably well. On Low-level Appearance, StableI2I achieves near-perfect 99.10% Binary Accuracy, surpassing GPT-4o (94.70%) and GPT-5 (93.20%). Among open-source models, Qwen3VL-32B-Instruct is the strongest (71.50% Binary Avg) but still trails StableI2I by 17.6 points. In the secondary evaluation (Table 2), StableI2I is used as a scoring tool to assess I2I generative models: Bagel leads with 0.8838 average on ImgEdit-Bench, while Flux.1-dev and GPT-Image-1 suffer catastrophic Structure scores (0.0123 and 0.1342 respectively), revealing severe content repainting that conventional IQA metrics miss. In a direct head-to-head with ImgEdit-Judge on human preference pairs, StableI2I achieves 80% accuracy on ImgEdit-Bench versus 30% for ImgEdit-Judge, and 76% versus 52% on GEdit-Bench.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two dominant lines of I2I evaluation: (1) reference-free IQA metrics (CLIP-IQA, MANIQA, MUSIQ) that score absolute perceptual quality or aesthetics without conditioning on the input image, and (2) MLLM-based judges (ImgEdit-Judge, prompt-engineered GPT-4o/Claude) that assess instruction following and high-level semantic consistency but lack pixel-level structural sensitivity. StableI2I carves new ground by unifying semantic, structural, and low-level appearance fidelity into a single instruction-conditioned assessment, and by training a compact 8B MLLM (Qwen3-VL-8B) with SFT + GRPO reinforcement learning specifically for this multi-dimensional fidelity task, rather than relying on prompt engineering or distillation from a larger closed-source model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical gap in high-stakes I2I applications (medical imaging, remote sensing) where unintended content drift can have serious consequences, and the StableI2I-Bench provides the first systematic benchmark for measuring whether MLLMs can actually detect such drift. A key open question is whether the structure-level consistency gap (where even GPT-5 and Gemini-3-pro score below 72% binary accuracy) reflects a fundamental limitation of current MLLM architectures in pixel-level alignment, or whether it can be closed with larger models or better training data. The paper acknowledges limitations on style transfer, object extraction, and human pose editing, suggesting the current formulation conflates intentional global changes with unintended repainting in some task categories.
