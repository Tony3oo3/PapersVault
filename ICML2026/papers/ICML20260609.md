## Problem

Reinforcement learning methods adapted to masked diffusion LLMs (dLLMs) rely on sequence-level marginal likelihoods, which are intractable because a single terminal sequence can be produced through exponentially many unmasking trajectories. This forces existing approaches to use biased likelihood surrogates or high-variance estimators. The paper asks whether dLLM post-training can be formulated entirely in terms of tractable state-level quantities—local unmasking posteriors on partially masked states—bypassing likelihood evaluation altogether.

## Input / Output

DTM takes a pretrained dLLM (LLaDA-8B-Instruct, 8B parameters), a scalar reward function over complete sequences, and text prompts as input, and produces a fine-tuned dLLM that generates text sequences of configurable length (256 or 512 tokens). Training operates on intermediate partially-masked states constructed via a semi-autoregressive (SAR) interpolant with block size 32, where only masked positions within the active block are eligible for updates. A replay buffer recycles terminal rollouts and their rewards across many training steps, amortizing the cost of expensive online generation.

## Method

DTM recasts dLLM fine-tuning as progressive reward tilting: rather than matching the full tilted target ρ₁(x)∝ρ₁(x)e^{Ar(x)} in one step, it anneals a tilt parameter a from 0 to A in small increments h, training the model to match ρ₁,ₐ₊ₕ from ρ₁,ₐ. The key derivation uses the Esscher transform to express the intractable tilted unmasking posterior πₐ₊ₕ as a conditional importance-weighted expectation under the accessible base measure Pₐ, yielding a weighted cross-entropy objective (c-DTM) whose unique minimizer is exactly πₐ₊ₕ. A control variate T_c (parameterized by c, typically c=1) replaces the raw one-hot target with a lower-variance random simplex while preserving the same weighted conditional mean, provably reducing gradient variance for small h. The objective is aligned with semi-autoregressive decoding by restricting the interpolant and summation to the active block, and a KL bound ties the training loss to the terminal distribution divergence, guaranteeing that minimizing the loss controls the induced terminal marginal.

## Results

Fine-tuning LLaDA-8B-Instruct with DTM yields 99.2% accuracy on Sudoku (3-shot, length 256) and 81.3% on Countdown (0-shot, length 256), substantially outperforming the strongest prior baseline SPG (94.0% and 70.7% respectively). On mathematical reasoning, DTM achieves 40.2% on MATH500 and 83.2% on GSM8K at length 512, remaining competitive but trailing SPG (41.8% and 84.5%). The paper also demonstrates that DTM converges faster than SPG on Sudoku under identical 8×H100 wallclock budgets, and that the control variate (c=1) combined with a moderate annealing step (h=6 for Countdown) prevents mode collapse while preserving solution diversity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DTM decisively outperforms all six baselines on Sudoku, reaching 99.2% (length 256) versus SPG's 94.0%, UniGRPO's 67.0%, and the base model's 27.7%—a gap of over 5 points over the strongest prior method. On Countdown, DTM also leads all baselines with 81.3% at length 256, exceeding SPG (70.7%) by roughly 11 points and WD1 (52.3%) by nearly 29 points. On MATH500, DTM (36.0/40.2) trails SPG (40.0/41.8) by about 4 points at both generation lengths, though it matches D1 and UniGRPO at length 256. On GSM8K, DTM (81.6/83.2) is competitive with D1 (80.6/81.3) and WD1 (81.5/80.3) but falls short of SPG (86.1/84.5) and UniGRPO (82.5/82.7). The authors attribute the MATH500/GSM8K gap to DTM's state-level local posterior training being less directly aligned with long-horizon coherent reasoning, while noting that DTM's gains grow with additional decoding budget (e.g., MATH500 rises from 36.0 to 40.2 when generation length doubles). In wallclock comparison on Sudoku, DTM reaches a higher reward than SPG in less time on 8 H100 GPUs.

## Relation to Prior Work

DTM extends the continuous-space Tilt Matching framework (Potaptchik et al., 2025) to discrete masked diffusion language models, translating the incremental Esscher-tilting philosophy into a cross-entropy objective over local unmasking posteriors. It departs fundamentally from the dominant line of RL-based dLLM fine-tuning (d1, WD1, UniGRPO, SPG, LLaDA-1.5), all of which require sequence-level marginal likelihoods that are intractable for masked diffusion due to the exponential number of unmasking trajectories. By recasting fine-tuning as state-level posterior matching under reward tilting, DTM bypasses likelihood evaluation entirely while inheriting the CTMC structure and discrete stochastic interpolant machinery from Kim et al. (2025a) and Gat et al. (2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DTM provides a principled, likelihood-free alternative to RL post-training for dLLMs with a provably correct minimizer and a KL bound on the terminal distribution, addressing a structural intractability that has constrained the field. The SAR-aligned variant and replay buffer offer practical efficiency gains, and the maze-planning ablations give clear guidance on annealing step size and control variate selection to avoid mode collapse. However, DTM remains less competitive than SPG on open-ended math reasoning (MATH500, GSM8K), suggesting that local posterior matching may not fully capture the demands of long-horizon coherent reasoning—an open question for whether state-level objectives can be augmented to close this gap. Validation is currently limited to a single 8B model family, leaving open whether the approach generalizes across architectures and scales.
