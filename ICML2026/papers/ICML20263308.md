## Problem

Optimal LLM data mixing requires partitioning web-scale corpora into semantically distinct and balanced clusters, but existing approaches are fundamentally misaligned: human-defined taxonomies suffer from ontological mismatch with model-relevant semantics, while Euclidean clustering (e.g., K-Means) fails on anisotropic neural embeddings that reside on a hypersphere, causing dominant clusters to swallow the semantic long-tail (cluster collapse). No prior method simultaneously respects the Riemannian geometry of embeddings and enforces balanced cluster masses needed for diverse data mixtures.

## Input / Output

GEM consumes ℓ2-normalized text embeddings (e.g., from BGE or RoBERTa) from a CommonCrawl/RefinedWeb-filtered corpus, operating first on a representative seed subset (default 3B tokens) for the teacher phase. It produces soft cluster responsibilities, vMF mixture parameters (mean directions μ_k and concentrations κ_k, typically κ≈900), and an interpretable 24-topic taxonomy with LLM-generated labels and descriptions. For web-scale deployment, the learned partition is distilled into a FastText classifier that assigns topic probabilities to every raw document in the full corpus, yielding data mixing coordinates compatible with frameworks like DoReMi, RegMix, and Perf.

## Method

GEM formulates semantic partitioning as an entropy-regularized variational objective over a Mixture of von Mises-Fisher distributions on S^{d-1}, augmented with a mixing-balance regularizer that penalizes deviation of empirical cluster masses π(Γ) from uniformity. The key design choice is decoupling the generative prior (fixed α_k = 1/K) from the empirical mass, so the balance term acts on actual assignments rather than learned weights, preventing the rich-get-richer feedback of standard EM. Inference uses a Minorize-Maximize scheme: the E-step maximizes a quadratic minorizer of the concave regularizer (guaranteeing monotonic ascent of the full objective), while the M-step applies closed-form spherical updates for μ_k (normalized weighted resultant) and κ_k (high-dimensional approximation). For scalability, a Teacher-Student pipeline distills the converged GEM partition into a lightweight FastText classifier using GIS-scored pseudo-labels, and the Geometric Influence Score (combining assignment certainty, directional coherence, and cluster-restricted local density) selects representative samples for LLM-based taxonomy generation.

## Results

Across three data-mixing frameworks (DoReMi, Perf, RegMix) with 1.1B-parameter LLaMA models trained on 25B tokens, GEM achieves the best average downstream accuracy in all settings: 43.95% under DoReMi, 44.79% under Perf, and 41.45% under RegMix. The largest gain is under DoReMi, where GEM surpasses the strongest baseline (WebOrganizer Format, 42.79%) by 1.16 points on average, with particularly strong improvements on Commonsense Reasoning (+1.76 pts) and Logic & Linguistics (+1.76 pts). Under Perf, GEM leads by 0.54 points on average, and under RegMix by 0.68 points. The ablation (Figure 6) further shows that the full GEM pipeline (42.1% avg) outperforms K-Means (38.5%), Spherical K-Means (40.6%), and vanilla vMF without the balance regularizer (41.3%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GEM consistently outperforms all three baseline families across all three mixing frameworks. Against K-Means (MacQueen, 1967), GEM leads by 3.0–4.0 points on average (e.g., 43.95 vs. 39.94 under DoReMi), confirming that Euclidean clustering is fundamentally mismatched to anisotropic neural embeddings. Against TOS (Peng et al., 2025), which uses a human-curated hierarchical taxonomy, GEM gains 3.7–4.3 points on average, demonstrating that human-defined ontologies introduce misalignment relative to the model's latent semantic geometry. The strongest comparison is against WebOrganizer (Wettig et al., 2025), an LLM-annotated taxonomy: GEM wins by 1.16 points under DoReMi (43.95 vs. 42.79), 0.54 under Perf (44.79 vs. 44.25), and 0.68 under RegMix (41.45 vs. 40.77). The gap is largest on Commonsense Reasoning and Logic & Linguistics, suggesting GEM's balanced partitions better preserve the long-tail semantic diversity needed for reasoning tasks. Notably, GEM also achieves higher predictability in RegMix's Spearman correlation (Figure 4), indicating its taxonomy yields a better-conditioned mixing simplex than any baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

GEM builds directly on the von Mises-Fisher mixture model (Banerjee et al., 2005) as its generative backbone, but departs from standard EM by fixing the generative prior to uniform and introducing a mixing-balance regularizer on the empirical cluster mass, optimized via a provable Minorize-Maximize algorithm rather than the usual E/M alternation. Conceptually, it positions itself against two existing paradigms for pre-training data categorization: taxonomy-based methods (WebOrganizer, TnT-LLM) that impose human ontologies, and Euclidean unsupervised clustering (K-Means, HDBSCAN) that mismatches the anisotropic geometry of modern neural embeddings. The paper also connects to the data-mixing literature (DoReMi, RegMix, DoGE) by arguing that these reweighting algorithms are upper-bounded by the quality of the underlying partition, and that a geometry-aware clustering step is a prerequisite rather than an afterthought.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled geometric framework that makes data-mixing coordinates more predictable and less entangled, which could simplify the search for optimal mixtures at scale. However, the evaluation is limited to 1.1B-parameter models trained on 25B tokens, so the scaling behavior to multi-trillion-token regimes and larger architectures remains unvalidated. Open questions include whether the balance regularizer and vMF formulation retain their advantage when the embedding space itself is co-trained with the LLM, and whether the taxonomy can be jointly optimized with downstream objectives in a bi-level fashion. The teacher-student distillation to FastText is practical but introduces a fidelity gap that is only partially characterized.
