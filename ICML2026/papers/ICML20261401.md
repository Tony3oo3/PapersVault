## Problem

Production-ready human video generation requires digital actors to maintain strictly consistent full-body identity across dynamic shots, viewpoints, and motions, yet existing methods either overemphasize facial cues while neglecting body-level consistency (causing a 'floating head effect') or induce pose-locking where the reference pose constrains all generated motion into static copy-paste artifacts. A critical data gap compounds this: no large-scale dataset provides diverse multi-view identity references for humans in unconstrained, in-the-wild environments.

## Input / Output

The method accepts a free-form text prompt plus a variable number (0–5) of reference facial images and body images captured from arbitrary viewpoints, environments, and poses—no canonical three-view set is required. It produces a 93-frame video (256p or 480p) encoded in a VAE latent space, where the generated subject must simultaneously follow the prompt's motion and scene description while preserving full-body identity fidelity across viewpoint transitions. The framework is built on a 5B-parameter DiT backbone (LongCat-Video) fine-tuned with rank-128 LoRA (0.29B trainable parameters).

## Method

WILDACTOR injects multi-view identity cues into a Rectified Flow video DiT via two architectural mechanisms. First, Asymmetric Identity-Preserving Attention (AIPA) enforces unidirectional information flow: reference tokens undergo self-attention to form a unified identity representation, then video tokens (as Queries) attend to both video and reference Keys/Values, while reference tokens remain isolated from noisy video latents; lightweight LoRA adapters are applied exclusively to reference token projections to avoid corrupting the frozen backbone. Second, Identity-Aware 3D RoPE (I-RoPE) assigns reference tokens fixed temporal offsets (T+4 for face, T+128 for body) and shifted spatial coordinates beyond the video's (H_max, W_max) grid, preventing positional ambiguity between motion and static appearance. During training, a Viewpoint-Adaptive Monte Carlo Sampling strategy dynamically suppresses candidate references within an angular neighborhood (δ=π/6) of already-sampled views (decay γ=0.5), biasing the model toward complementary viewpoint coverage and mitigating the severe frontal-view bias in raw data.

## Results

On Actor-Bench (Contextual Generalization), WILDACTOR achieves the highest Body Consistency score of 0.952, surpassing the strongest commercial baseline Vidu Q2 (0.905) and Kling 1.6 (0.885), while also attaining the best VLM-Level Semantic Alignment of 0.920. In the Sequential Narrative setting, WILDACTOR (T2V→I2V w/Ref) scores 0.925 Body Consistency and 0.893 VLM-Level alignment, substantially outperforming the no-reference autoregressive baseline (0.450 / 0.613). WILDACTOR uses only 5B parameters (0.29B trainable via LoRA) compared to 14B for open-source baselines VACE and Stand-In, yet outperforms them on every metric.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against open-source baselines, WILDACTOR dominates: it outperforms VACE by +0.074 Face ID, +0.370 Body Consistency, and +0.253 VLM-Level alignment, and beats Stand-In by +0.049 Face ID, +0.536 Body Consistency, and +0.320 VLM-Level, all while using roughly half the parameters (5B vs. 14B). Against the strongest commercial model Vidu Q2, WILDACTOR wins decisively on Body Consistency (0.952 vs. 0.905) and VLM-Level Semantic Alignment (0.920 vs. 0.880), but trails slightly on Face Identity (0.559 vs. 0.565) and Feature-Level alignment (0.238 vs. 0.241), which the authors attribute to Vidu Q2's aggressive appearance copying favoring facial similarity over structural flexibility. Against Kling 1.6, WILDACTOR leads on Body Consistency (+0.067) and VLM-Level (+0.053), with Face ID essentially tied (0.559 vs. 0.558). In the Sequential Narrative setting, WILDACTOR's autoregressive pipeline with reference conditioning achieves 0.925 Body Consistency versus 0.450 for the no-reference baseline, demonstrating that persistent identity references are critical for long-form coherence.

## Relation to Prior Work

WILDACTOR fine-tunes the LongCat-Video 5B DiT backbone with Rectified Flow, positioning itself within the recent shift from U-Net to DiT-based video generation. It departs from prior identity-preserving methods (VACE, Stand-In, Phantom) that either overemphasize facial cues via face-recognition encoders or naively concatenate reference images, both of which cause pose-locking or 'floating head' artifacts. The Asymmetric Identity-Preserving Attention mechanism is a conceptual departure from bidirectional cross-attention, enforcing unidirectional information flow from reference to video tokens, while the Viewpoint-Adaptive Monte Carlo Sampling strategy addresses a data-level gap that prior in-the-wild datasets (OpenS2V-Nexus, HumanVid) leave unaddressed due to severe frontal-view bias.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete production bottleneck—maintaining full-body identity across viewpoint changes in generated video—that prior methods handle poorly, and the 1.6M-video / 18M-image Actor-18M dataset provides a much-needed training resource for view-invariant human representations without expensive studio captures. The AIPA mechanism is a lightweight, plug-and-play architectural pattern (reference-only LoRA + asymmetric attention) that could transfer to other conditional generation settings. Key limitations include single-person scope, reliance on generative augmentation (Qwen-Image-Edit, Nano-Banana) for viewpoint diversity which may introduce subtle identity distortions, and evaluation on a relatively small 75-subject benchmark. Extending to multi-person scenes with disentangled identity features remains an open challenge the authors explicitly flag.
