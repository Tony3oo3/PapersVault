## Problem

Existing class-incremental semantic segmentation methods effectively stabilize old-class representations but leave new-class performance limited, particularly as incremental steps grow longer. This paper identifies the bottleneck as low-margin regions—pixels where the ground-truth logit is close to the top competing logit—and provides a theoretical diagnosis showing these regions exhibit high second-order sensitivity along the margin direction and a small stability radius, making them ill-conditioned for optimization under incremental updates.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images at 512×512 resolution and produces pixel-wise segmentation masks over an expanding class vocabulary. In the incremental setting, at each step only labels for newly introduced classes are available; pixels belonging to previously learned or future classes are treated as background, and pseudo-labels approximate old-class supervision. The model uses a ViT-B/16 encoder (pretrained on ImageNet) with a two-layer transformer decoder, and the same optimization framework is also instantiated on ResNet-101/DeepLab-v3 and SegFormer backbones.

## Method

LDKA targets low-margin optimization through three complementary strategies. Progressive Margin Learning (PML) defines a Robust Progress Index combining margin magnitude and second-order sensitivity, ranks pixels by this index within each batch, and applies a progress-aware exponent to the empirical CDF so that optimization budget shifts from high-margin to low-margin regions as training progresses. Smooth Knowledge Distillation (SKD) extends standard feature-level distillation with a directional second-order sensitivity damping term (computed via double backpropagation on the distillation discrepancy gradient) and a perturbation stabilization loss that injects a normalized gradient-direction perturbation into the pre-decoder feature and penalizes the resulting response variation. Misclassification-Aware Decoupling (MAD) constructs a per-epoch competition matrix measuring pairwise misclassification rates and uses it to weight a decoupling loss that pushes apart class tokens (or classifier weights in CNN variants) of frequently confused class pairs.

## Results

On Pascal VOC 2012, LDKA (ViT-B/16) achieves the best overall mIoU across all five incremental settings, reaching 82.4 (15-5), 82.5 (19-1), 80.1 (15-1), 75.3 (2-2), and 76.7 (10-1), consistently outperforming the strongest baseline MBS by 1.2–2.7 mIoU. On ADE20K, LDKA achieves 46.3 (100-50), 46.9 (50-50), 44.9 (100-10), and 40.1 (100-5) overall mIoU, approaching the joint-training upper bound (49.5, 49.5, 49.3, 49.3) with gaps of only 3.2–9.2 mIoU. New-class mIoU improvements over MBS range from 2.3 to 4.3 on VOC and 2.5 to 3.3 on ADE20K, while old-class performance is preserved or slightly improved.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LDKA outperforms all 24 compared baselines on overall mIoU across all nine incremental configurations. On Pascal VOC 15-1 (the most commonly cited setting), LDKA achieves 80.1 overall mIoU, surpassing MBS (78.0, +2.1), CoGaMiD (77.8, +2.3), CoinSeg (75.5, +4.6), and SSUL (67.5, +12.6). On the longer-horizon 2-2 (10 steps) and 10-1 (11 steps) settings, LDKA gains 2.7 and 0.1 mIoU over MBS respectively, with the largest advantage over older methods like MIB (31.0 → 75.3 on 2-2). On ADE20K, LDKA leads MBS by 0.8 mIoU on 100-50 (46.3 vs 45.5) and by 1.8 on 50-50 (46.9 vs 45.1), while on the more challenging 100-5 setting it improves by 2.0 (40.1 vs 38.1). Notably, LDKA's new-class mIoU on VOC 15-1 (69.9) exceeds MBS (65.6) by 4.3 points, and on ADE20K 100-50 (40.7) exceeds MBS (37.5) by 3.2 points, confirming that the gains are driven primarily by improved plasticity on new classes rather than at the expense of old-class stability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the regularization-based paradigm of class-incremental semantic segmentation (CISS), which has been dominated by knowledge distillation, contrastive learning, and replay strategies aimed at stabilizing old-class representations. It departs from this line by identifying a specific optimization bottleneck—low-margin regions where the top-1 and top-2 logits are close—and providing a formal theoretical diagnosis (high second-order sensitivity along the margin direction, small stability radius) that prior CISS work had not articulated. The proposed LDKA framework repurposes knowledge distillation by adding directional second-order sensitivity damping and perturbation stabilization (SKD), introduces a margin-driven progressive weighting scheme explicitly distinguished from curriculum learning (PML), and uses a competition matrix to guide class-token decoupling (MAD), collectively forming an allocation-stabilization-decoupling design that targets the identified bottleneck rather than generic forgetting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By isolating low-margin regions as the optimization bottleneck and grounding the analysis in second-order sensitivity and stability radius, the paper offers a principled explanation for why new-class performance plateaus in long-horizon CISS, complementing the more common 'forgetting' narrative. The near-joint-training performance on ADE20K (46.3 vs. 49.5 mIoU) and consistent gains across 9+ incremental configurations, validated on ViT, ResNet-101, and SegFormer backbones, suggest the optimization design is broadly applicable. Open questions include whether the theoretical analysis extends beyond softmax cross-entropy to other loss formulations, how the method behaves under extreme class counts (e.g., 200+), and whether the competition matrix in MAD remains informative when class similarity is very high or very low.
