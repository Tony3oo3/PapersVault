## Problem

Grokking—the abrupt transition from memorization to generalization long after overfitting—remains mechanistically unexplained. Prior work has offered post-hoc circuit interpretations or temporal acceleration heuristics, but has not identified the internal structural dynamics that drive the phase transition between memorization and grokking, nor provided a principled way to actively catalyze that transition.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264407/cropped_page1_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

For modular arithmetic, the model receives a token sequence {a, op, b, %, P, =} where a, b ∈ [0, P−1] and predicts the single result token c = (a op b) mod P as a P-class classification. For tense-inflection, the input is a past-tense English sentence plus a tense marker (PAST/PRESENT), and the output is the correctly inflected sentence. The R2G loss itself operates entirely on intermediate Value vectors within attention layers, requiring no modification to the input format or output head.

## Method

The paper identifies that grokking corresponds to a geometric reorganization of Value (V) vectors in Transformer attention layers: representations shift from collapsed clusters (memorization) to a circular/hyperspherical topology where numerically adjacent tokens are maximally separated. Building on this, the R2G (Repel-to-Grokking) loss maximizes the Euclidean distance between randomly permuted pairs of V vectors using a reciprocal formulation (1/Σ‖vᵢ−vᵢ′‖₂), which provides adaptive gradient scaling—strong repulsion when representations are collapsed and weak repulsion once structure has formed. A radial-angular decomposition of the cross-entropy gradient shows that angular updates are suppressed by a 1/r factor relative to radial updates, explaining why standard training stalls in memorization and why angular reorganization is the primary driver of grokking. The loss is plug-and-play (no new parameters, no extra data) and is validated across modular addition/multiplication at multiple moduli and a tense-inflection task, outperforming Grokfast and NeuralGrok under identical budgets.

## Results

On the primary P=500 modular addition task (26,650 samples), R2G loss achieves a 73% grokking rate, substantially outperforming Grokfast (60%) and NeuralGrok (27%). R2G also converges fastest among successfully grokking runs. Across other tasks, R2G lifts grokking rates by 80% (P=499 addition), 40% (P=113 addition), 67% (P=113 multiplication), 80% (2-layer Transformer), and 67% (Tense-Inflection), consistently converting memorization and semi-grokking runs into full grokking.

## Baselines & Comparisons

R2G outperforms both temporal-acceleration baselines on the shared P=500 modular addition benchmark: it achieves a 73% grokking rate versus 60% for Grokfast (Lee et al., 2024) and 27% for NeuralGrok (Xu et al., 2025), a 13-point and 46-point margin respectively. Beyond the grokking rate, R2G also converges fastest among successfully grokking runs, indicating an advantage along both the phase axis and the temporal axis. Grokfast amplifies slow low-frequency gradients (α=0.3), while NeuralGrok trains an auxiliary amplifier to re-weight gradients (c_norm=0.2, inner-loop steps=2); both operate purely on the temporal axis without reshaping representation geometry. R2G's structural intervention additionally rescues 6% of previously failed runs into grokking—a capability neither baseline demonstrates—by actively enforcing pairwise repulsion in the Value-vector manifold rather than merely accelerating existing gradient dynamics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of two existing grokking research threads: mechanistic interpretability (Nanda et al., Zhong et al., Varma et al.) which provides post-hoc explanations of learned circuits, and temporal acceleration methods (Grokfast, NeuralGrok, weight-norm constraints) that reduce the time-to-grokking without addressing the structural cause. The key departure is reframing grokking as a geometric phase transition in the Value-vector manifold and proposing an active structural intervention (R2G loss) that enforces pairwise repulsion, thereby bridging the gap between correlational observation and causal manipulation. The radial-angular decomposition of softmax gradients provides a theoretical lens absent from prior acceleration work, which operates purely on the temporal axis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of angular reorganization (rather than norm growth) as the primary driver of grokking offers a testable mechanistic hypothesis that could guide future representation-learning research beyond algorithmic tasks. The R2G loss is remarkably simple (no new parameters, no extra data) and generalizes across operations, architectures, and a linguistic task, making it a practical tool for grokking research. However, the empirical scope remains narrow—primarily small Transformers on modular arithmetic—and the OLMo-2-1B analysis is explicitly preliminary, leaving open whether geometric reorganization is a general principle in large-scale pretraining or an artifact of small algorithmic settings. Adaptive selection of the α hyperparameter and extension to non-grokking generalization regimes are noted as important future directions.
