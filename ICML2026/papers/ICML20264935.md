## Problem

In single-stream autoregressive LLMs, every generated token simultaneously updates internal state and becomes an irreversible public commitment, creating a 'silence tax' where deliberation delays all visible content. Existing approaches either fix disclosure timing with templates/heuristics or naively reward earlier output, which incentivizes unsupported filler. What is missing is a mechanism that makes disclosure an explicit, learnable decision variable tied to a concrete support condition—namely, that disclosed text must be entailed by the reasoning prefix available at that point.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard text prompt x and produces a single autoregressive token sequence annotated with lightweight channel tags (R for private reasoning, A for user-visible disclosure). Both think and speak tokens remain in the same context—there is no second model or hidden channel—so the speak stream is a monotonically growing prefix of the final answer that the model chooses to reveal progressively. The interleaved format is realized purely through tag tokens predicted by the same model, requiring no architectural modification to the base LLM.

## Method

SxS Interleaved Reasoning frames disclosure timing as a sequential decision problem (think vs. speak) within standard autoregressive decoding. For SFT data construction, the paper segments standard (x, r, a) triples into blocks and computes entailment-aligned boundaries ℓ_k via an LLM-based entailment checker, emitting answer increments only when the reasoning prefix newly supports them (Algorithm 1). Training proceeds in two stages: SFT teaches the dual-action tag semantics on ~330k interleaved trajectories, then GRPO-based RL with outcome-only rewards recovers task accuracy under the distribution shift induced by the interleaved format. An optional correctness-preserving reward shaping step, formulated as a convex quadratic program, can further control interleaving granularity without compromising the correctness signal. The approach is evaluated across MoE (Qwen3-30B-A3B) and dense (Qwen3-4B) architectures on AIME25, GPQA-Diamond, LiveCodeBench, and KOR-Bench, showing improved accuracy–content-latency Pareto trade-offs without architectural changes.

## Results

On AIME25, SxS RL Final achieves 80.0% accuracy on Qwen3-4B (vs. 73.8% for Standard CoT RL Final) and 79.2% on Qwen3-30B-A3B (vs. 80.6%), while substantially reducing Average Inter-Response Wait (AIRW) from 21,316 to 8,519 tokens on the 4B model. On GPQA-Diamond, SxS RL Final reaches 49.3% on Qwen3-4B versus only 19.0% for Standard CoT RL Final, demonstrating that the interleaved format prevents the catastrophic forgetting observed under math-focused RL. The SFT-only stage incurs a temporary accuracy dip (e.g., 38.3% on 4B AIME25), which RL recovers to near or above baseline levels while preserving the interleaved disclosure structure.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares SxS against three baselines: the unmodified Base Model, Standard CoT after SFT, and Standard CoT after full RL. On AIME25, SxS RL Final outperforms Standard CoT RL Final by 6.2 points on Qwen3-4B (80.0% vs. 73.8%) while cutting AIRW by 60% (8,519 vs. 21,316 tokens); on Qwen3-30B-A3B it is within 1.4 points (79.2% vs. 80.6%) with a 17% AIRW reduction. On GPQA-Diamond, the advantage is dramatic: SxS RL Final achieves 49.3% on Qwen3-4B versus 19.0% for Standard CoT RL Final, indicating that the dual-channel format mitigates the catastrophic forgetting caused by math-focused RL. On LiveCodeBench and KOR-Bench (Table 2), SxS RL Final matches or slightly exceeds Standard CoT RL Final in accuracy (e.g., 39.62 vs. 39.34 pass@1 on LCB for 4B; 32.96 vs. 19.52 overall on KOR-Bench for 4B) while consistently reducing AIRW. The SFT-only SxS models achieve the lowest latency (AIRW ~3,500–5,000) but suffer a 20–35 point accuracy drop, confirming that RL is necessary to recover task performance under the interleaved format.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the tagged-interleaving paradigm introduced by Xie et al. (2025) and the broader CoT reasoning framework (Wei et al., 2022) by reframing disclosure timing as a learned sequential decision problem rather than a fixed template or heuristic. It builds on GRPO (as used in Kimi K1.5) for the RL stage but applies it to a novel dual-channel rollout format where the model alternates between private reasoning (think) and public disclosure (speak) tokens. The key conceptual departure from prior interleaving work is the entailment-aligned supervision: answer prefixes are only unlocked when the reasoning prefix provably supports them, preventing the filler and premature-commitment failure modes that plague naive early-streaming incentives. Unlike pipelined architectures (Woo et al., 2025) or specialized streaming mechanisms (Tong et al., 2025), SxS requires no second model, separate hidden state, or architectural modification—visibility is simply a controllable attribute within standard autoregressive decoding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that pacing can be learned as a stable behavioral policy in standard autoregressive decoding, offering a practical lever for improving user-perceived responsiveness of reasoning models without sacrificing accuracy. The 'dip-and-recover' training dynamic (SFT causes a large accuracy drop, RL recovers it) is an important practical finding for anyone attempting format-shifting post-training. The OOD robustness observation—SxS retaining substantially higher GPQA-Diamond accuracy than Standard CoT after math-focused RL (49.3% vs. 19.0% on 4B)—suggests that coupling intermediate reasoning to visible commitments may reduce reward-hacking rationales, though the authors acknowledge this mechanism is not directly measured. Open questions include whether the entailment checker can be replaced by a lightweight NLI model at scale, whether the approach generalizes beyond math/science to open-ended generation, and whether the token-level latency proxies (ARI, ABO, AIRW) correlate well with actual user-perceived waiting in deployed systems.
