## Problem

Standard softmax attention enforces a unit-sum constraint on attention weights, forcing the model to distribute a fixed total mass across tokens even when no token warrants strong attention. This induces overly concentrated, winner-takes-all distributions (notably on first tokens) and limits the model's ability to uniformly reduce attention magnitude. Prior remedies such as attention sinks and gated attention provide only input-agnostic or post-hoc control, leaving the relative allocation among tokens largely unchanged.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the standard query, key, and value projections along with the current hidden state X. It produces an attention output in which the softmax-normalized weight vector is multiplied by a per-query scalar scale α(X) and offset by a uniform bias β(X) broadcast across all key positions. The scale is a scalar (or 1D vector) computed from X via a linear projection and a custom linear-clipping activation, while the bias is derived from the deviation of α from its exponential moving average, divided by the key sequence length N to keep total bias contribution length-invariant.

## Method

Affine-Scaled Attention applies an input-dependent affine transformation to the softmax-normalized attention weights: Attn_affine = [α(X)·softmax(QK^T/√dk) + β(X)]V. The scaling factor α(X) = φ(W_α X) is computed from hidden states through a learnable linear gate and a custom piecewise-linear clipping activation (linear in [-5,5], saturated outside), which prevents the collapse to constant values that sigmoid saturation would cause. The bias β(X) = (α_ma − α(X))/N is coupled to the scale via an exponential moving average α_ma (momentum ρ=0.9), so that when the model sharpens attention (α increases), the bias decreases, and vice versa, stabilizing overall attention magnitude. This design relaxes the unit-sum constraint in a context-sensitive way, enabling per-query and per-head modulation of attention mass. The method adds negligible parameter overhead (0.4M–2.1M for 0.5B–3B models), is orthogonal to Gated Attention, and can be integrated into FlashAttention kernels since α and β are scalar gating values applied before or after the SDPA computation.

## Results

Across 0.5B, 1B, and 3B student models trained via knowledge distillation on 20B FineWebEdu tokens, Affine-Scaled Attention achieves the best average commonsense reasoning accuracy at every scale: 51.3% (0.5B), 57.2% (1B), and 62.4% (3B), compared to 50.6/56.7/60.8% for the standard softmax baseline. C4 perplexity is also consistently lower (40.8 vs. 44.9 for the 3B model), and the method adds only 0.4M–2.1M parameters. Gains are most pronounced on reasoning-oriented benchmarks at larger scale, e.g., ARC-c improves from 38.4% to 41.5% and BoolQ from 63.6% to 65.1% at 3B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the standard softmax baseline, Affine-Scaled Attention wins on average accuracy at every model size (51.3 vs. 50.6 at 0.5B; 57.2 vs. 56.7 at 1B; 62.4 vs. 60.8 at 3B) and on C4 perplexity (e.g., 40.8 vs. 44.9 at 3B), with the largest gaps on ARC-c (+3.1 pts) and BoolQ (+1.5 pts) at 3B. Against the attention-sink baseline (learnable denominator from GPT-OSS), Affine-Scaled Attention also wins on average accuracy at all three scales and substantially on C4 PPL (70.6 vs. 81.5 at 0.5B; 40.8 vs. 48.5 at 3B), indicating that the input-dependent scaling provides more effective reweighting than a fixed or learnable scalar sink. Against Gated Attention (post-SDPA sigmoid gate), combining Affine-Scaled Attention with gating yields further gains over gating alone (58.0 vs. 57.1 avg at 1B; 63.1 vs. 61.6 at 3B), confirming the two mechanisms are complementary and that Affine-Scaled Attention's per-head, input-adaptive modulation is orthogonal to post-attention gating.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on relaxing the unit-sum constraint of softmax attention, which includes off-by-one attention (Miller, 2023), the learnable attention sink in GPT-OSS (Agarwal et al., 2025), and Gated Attention (Qiu et al., 2025). Unlike prior approaches that modify the softmax denominator (off-by-one, sink) or apply a post-attention multiplicative gate (Gated Attention), Affine-Scaled Attention applies an input-dependent affine transformation—scaling plus bias—directly to the softmax-normalized weights. This positions it as a distinct mechanism: it is input-adaptive (unlike the input-agnostic sink), operates on the weight distribution itself rather than the raw logits or the post-attention output, and couples the scale and bias through a moving-average reference to maintain stability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a minimal-parameter (0.4M–2.1M) modification that is compatible with FlashAttention and complementary to Gated Attention, making it a practical drop-in improvement for transformer pretraining pipelines. However, the evaluation is limited to moderate-scale models (0.5B–3B) trained via knowledge distillation on 20B tokens, leaving open whether the gains persist at frontier scale or under standard cross-entropy pretraining. A key open question is whether the input-adaptive scaling provides genuine representational benefits beyond what a well-tuned fixed sink or gate can achieve, and whether the moving-average bias mechanism generalizes across architectures and training regimes.
