## Problem

Existing convergence analyses of Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) either assume convexity, require the perturbation radius ρ to be vanishingly small, or show under PL conditions that constant ρ=O(1) leaves a non-vanishing optimization error. This leaves unanswered a fundamental question: for a general non-convex loss with large, constant ρ, how does SAM actually behave, and how far is the practical one-step SAM update from the ideal minimax objective that motivates it?

## Input / Output

The method takes a twice-differentiable loss l(θ), a perturbation radius ρ, a learning rate η, and a number of inner adversarial iterations M, and produces optimized model parameters θ. Unusually, the perturbation Δ is treated as a latent state variable coupled to θ through a two-timescale dynamical system rather than being solved in closed form at each step. The theoretical output includes a convergence set Ω whose radius scales with ρ and the local Hessian spectral norm σ₁(∇²l), providing a curvature-dependent characterization of where the trajectory settles rather than a single point.

## Method

The paper reformulates SAM as the bilevel minimax problem min_θ max_{‖Δ‖₂≤ρ} l(θ+Δ) and derives a coupled two-timescale ODE where θ descends the loss at the perturbed point while Δ chases the worst-case direction on the ρ-sphere via a projected fixed-point flow. A Lyapunov function combining the loss value and the perturbation tracking error certifies global descent without convexity, yielding a convergence set Ω governed by the ratio β/α (inner-to-outer timescale) and local curvature. Discretizing this ODE with M inner steps before each outer update produces Multi-step SAM (MSAM), which recovers classical SAM at M=1 and admits a uniform-in-time error bound of the form C₁η + C₂κ^M, where κ=ρB₂/c is the contraction ratio of the inner fixed-point map. The analysis further reveals that the SAM update direction is mathematically equivalent to the gradient of a Penalized Gradient Norm objective, connecting the minimax formulation to bilevel implicit differentiation.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (ResNet20), MSAM achieves 93.3–93.7% test accuracy across ρ ∈ {0.025, 0.05, 0.10}, consistently outperforming SGD (93.2%) by +0.2–0.5%. On CIFAR-100, MSAM (M=5) surpasses standard SAM on ResNet56 at ρ=0.10 (74.8% vs 74.2%), while matching or exceeding SAM on ResNet20 (71.4% vs 71.3%). On Imagenette2 (ResNet-50), Periodic MSAM achieves 76.87% Top-1 accuracy, a +1.86% improvement over standard SAM (75.01%), with only ~20% additional wall-clock time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares MSAM against standard SAM (M=1) and SGD across CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and Imagenette2. MSAM consistently matches or slightly exceeds standard SAM on CIFAR-10 (e.g., 93.7% vs 93.7% for ResNet20 at ρ=0.10), confirming that multiple inner steps preserve SAM's stationary-point approximation without degrading performance. On CIFAR-100, MSAM (M=5) clearly beats SAM on ResNet56 at ρ=0.10 (74.8% vs 74.2%), suggesting that deeper networks with stronger non-convexity benefit more from accurate inner-loop resolution. On Imagenette2, Periodic MSAM (switching to M=5, ρ=0.2 in the last 20% of training) achieves 76.87% vs SAM's 75.01%, a substantial +1.86% gain. In the large-ρ regime on synthetic matrix completion problems, standard SAM fails to converge (loss ~1.5–2.5) while MSAM (M=5 or 10) recovers losses comparable to GD (~1e-5 to 1e-4), validating the theoretical prediction that insufficient inner accuracy causes SAM to diverge when ρ is large.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of SAM convergence analysis (Andriushchenko & Flammarion, 2022; Oikonomou & Loizou, 2025; Si & Yun, 2023) by removing the small-ρ and PL-condition assumptions that prior work required, instead deriving a two-timescale ODE for the coupled (θ, Δ) dynamics and proving convergence via Lyapunov analysis without convexity. It draws on bilevel optimization theory (Ji et al., 2021; Kwon et al., 2023) for the KKT structure and on the ODE/Lyapunov framework for optimization (Su et al., 2014; Sanz Serna & Zygalakis, 2021) for the stability certificate. The resulting MSAM algorithm is a discretization of this ODE, and the paper shows its first-order Taylor expansion recovers the Penalized Gradient Norm objective of Zhao et al. (2022), providing a theoretical bridge between SAM and heuristic curvature-regularization methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first analysis that gives a principled, curvature-dependent convergence guarantee for SAM with large constant ρ in the non-convex setting, answering a question explicitly flagged as open by prior work. The key practical insight—that increasing inner steps M improves tracking fidelity but simultaneously degrades convergence precision due to the β/α trade-off—provides a concrete knob for practitioners tuning SAM, and the Periodic MSAM strategy (switching to large-ρ multi-step exploration only in late training) offers a low-overhead recipe. Open questions include extending the analysis to the stochastic setting, characterizing the optimal ρ schedule as a function of landscape geometry, and understanding whether the dynamic-equilibrium interpretation of SAM's oscillation translates to measurable generalization gains beyond the modest CIFAR improvements shown here.
