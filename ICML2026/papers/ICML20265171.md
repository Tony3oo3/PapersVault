## Problem

Reasoning LLMs frequently overthink on ill-posed or ambiguous queries, generating long, wasteful reasoning traces without abstaining. Existing mitigation strategies—prompting, hidden-state probing, and logit-based entropy thresholds—lack formal false-positive-rate guarantees, are brittle under distribution shift between calibration and test queries, or require access to internal model activations incompatible with black-box APIs. The paper asks whether a statistically principled, interpretable, and black-box-compatible stopping rule can halt reasoning on ill-posed queries while controlling premature halts on well-posed ones.

## Input / Output

At test time the method consumes only the streaming reasoning trace (a token sequence) and a pre-extracted set of 102 uncertainty keywords, checking for keyword arrivals at fixed intervals of B=250 tokens. Calibration requires only well-posed reasoning traces from the target model—no negative examples, no logits, no hidden states—making it fully compatible with proprietary LLM APIs. The output is a binary continue/halt decision at each checkpoint, governed by a single calibrated threshold τ* (for Maxwise) or a sequential Z-statistic with Sidák-corrected significance (for Renewal).

## Method

The approach has two stages. First, a semi-supervised keyword extraction pipeline trains random-forest classifiers on k-gram bag-of-words features from 800 paired well-posed/ill-posed GSM8K traces, then intersects high-importance features across cross-validation folds and filters them into three interpretable semantic categories (Impossibility, Speculation, Insufficiency) via rule-based lexical matching. Second, two stopping rules are calibrated on well-posed traces only: (i) a Renewal-process rule that models inter-arrival times of uncertainty keywords, exploits the asymptotic normality of the renewal counting process N_t, and applies Sidák-corrected sequential testing at each bin boundary; and (ii) a Maxwise conformal rule that computes the maximum uncertainty-density score (keyword count / prefix length) over calibration traces and sets the stopping threshold at the (1−α)(1+1/n) quantile, yielding a finite-sample FPR guarantee via conformal prediction. The key insight is that uncertainty keywords form a point process whose arrival rate elevates on ill-posed queries, and this text-level signal is more robust to distribution shift than trace length or logit-derived uncertainty measures.

## Results

Across four math reasoning benchmarks (GSM-MC, UMWP, MiP, MMLU), the proposed Renewal rule achieves FPR of 0.75–3.75% with detection power of 46.8–71.2%, while Maxwise achieves FPR of 2.63–5.52% with power of 48.7–75.3%, both at a target FPR of 5%. On scientific reasoning (GPQA, HLE), Maxwise maintains FPR of 2.56–5.96% with power of 24.6–32.4%. Against an oracle upper bound, the methods recover approximately 82–85% of achievable detection power on math benchmarks and 47–58% on scientific benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the prompting baselines (Confidence, Criticism), both proposed methods dominate on power: Maxwise achieves 48.7–75.3% detection power versus 0.5–15.5% for prompting, while maintaining FPR within the 5% target. The length-based baseline suffers severe FPR inflation under distribution shift (20.67% on MiP, 12.91% on MMLU) and low power on UMWP (8.59%), whereas Renewal keeps FPR at 1.12% and 0.75% on those same datasets with power of 71.15% and 66.42%. The logit-based DEER and Entropy baselines, despite sharing the same conformal calibration framework, exhibit substantially higher FPR on out-of-distribution benchmarks (DEER: 16.67% on MiP, 20.18% on MMLU; Entropy: 19.39% on MiP) compared to Maxwise (3.21% and 2.63%), demonstrating the brittleness of logit-derived uncertainty signals under distribution shift. The linear probing baseline (Liu et al., 2025a) achieves competitive power on in-distribution GSM-MC but inflates FPR to 30–40% on MiP and MMLU, while Renewal and Maxwise maintain FPR below 4% on all benchmarks. Overall, the proposed keyword-arrival-based methods consistently outperform all baselines on the joint FPR–power tradeoff, with Renewal favoring FPR control and Maxwise favoring detection power and token savings (63–70% on ill-posed queries).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the conformal prediction framework (Vovk et al., 2005) to provide finite-sample false-positive guarantees for a nonparametric stopping rule, and on renewal process theory (Grimmett and Stirzaker, 2001) to construct a parametric sequential test for a second rule. It departs from prior early-stopping approaches for reasoning LLMs—prompting-based abstention (Ma et al., 2024; Huang et al., 2025), hidden-state probing (Liu et al., 2025a; Zhang et al., 2025), and logit-derived uncertainty scores (Yang et al., 2025b; Yong et al., 2025)—by operating solely on observable uncertainty keywords in the generated text, requiring no access to internal activations, no negative calibration examples, and no fine-tuning. The semi-supervised keyword extraction pipeline (random forest on k-gram features with interpretability filtering) is a novel repurposing of classical feature selection for the specific goal of building an interpretable stopping signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that the proposed stopping rules are compatible with proprietary black-box LLM APIs and provide interpretable, statistically controlled early stopping without requiring curated negative examples or model internals—addressing a key deployment gap left by probing and logit-based methods. A key limitation is that detection power is fundamentally bounded by how much ill-posedness signal the model actually verbalizes in its trace; the oracle analysis (82–85% recovery on math) quantifies this ceiling. Open questions include adaptive bin-size scheduling to handle short traces, the design of downstream policies post-halt (abstain vs. clarify vs. summarize), and whether the keyword-based signal remains discriminative for models that do not verbalize uncertainty as explicitly. The finding that simple keyword-arrival statistics outperform logit-based and probing-based signals under distribution shift also raises broader questions about the robustness of internal-state uncertainty proxies.
