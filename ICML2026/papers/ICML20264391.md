## Problem

Existing multimodal CoT methods either rely on verbose textual reasoning that causes 'textual context dominance' (overshadowing visual signals) or invoke external visual tools with unstable invocation and high latency. Pure latent-space reasoning avoids these issues but suffers from semantic drift over long horizons and lacks interpretable checkpoints for diagnosing failures. No prior framework provides a mechanism to dynamically retrieve fine-grained visual evidence conditioned on the current reasoning state while preserving both interpretability and computational efficiency.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image (encoded into a set of visual feature patches Z) and a free-form text query Q₀ as input. Rather than producing a single monolithic CoT chain, the output is generated through a variable-length trajectory of explicit textual sub-states (Q₀ → Q₁ → … → Q_T) that progressively simplify the query, with a confidence-based gating score s_t determining when to terminate recursion and decode the final text answer. The number of explicit steps T and latent iterations N per step are adaptive, allowing early exit on simpler queries.

## Method

LDPVR formalizes visual reasoning as a Markov Chain of Recursive State Simplification (MCRSS), where each transition Q_t → Q_{t+1} is mediated by a Latent Transition Kernel implementing a dual-phase Interleaved Latent Grounding mechanism: Phase 1 anchors the current textual state onto the visual manifold via query-centric cross-attention to derive a semantic intent, and Phase 2 executes N iterative latent probes that extract top-k salient visual patches to resolve ambiguities before projecting back to the next explicit state. A simplification head predicts both the next state and an answerability confidence score, with a gating threshold τ enabling dynamic early termination. Training follows a three-stage curriculum: (1) SFT on explicit simplification trajectories to establish decomposition logic, (2) latent-text distillation with a semantic matching loss plus contrastive term to prevent latent collapse, and (3) GRPO reinforcement learning with a composite reward balancing correctness against iteration count to incentivize efficient latent shortcuts. The paper provides an information-theoretic proof that, under the condition that newly grounded visual features provide mutual information exceeding compression loss, the predictive entropy of the target answer strictly decreases at each Markovian step.

## Results

LDPVR achieves consistent improvements across all four backbone architectures and six benchmarks. The most notable gains appear on reasoning-intensive tasks: on MathVista, LDPVR boosts R1-OneVision-7B from 51.8% (No-CoT) to 58.7%, surpassing the strongest baseline SCAFFOLD (55.9%) by 2.8 points. On visual perception, LDPVR improves VLAA-Thinking-7B on HallusionBench from 63.1% to 68.2% (+5.1% over No-CoT, +2.1% over SCAFFOLD) and on MMVP from 67.4% to 72.4% (+5.0% over No-CoT). Across all 24 backbone-benchmark combinations, LDPVR outperforms every baseline, with average gains of 2.1–6.9% over No-CoT and 1.1–4.2% over SCAFFOLD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LDPVR outperforms all five baselines across every backbone and benchmark. Against the strongest explicit-reasoning baseline SCAFFOLD (Lei et al., 2025), LDPVR gains 1.1–4.2% on cross-modal and perception tasks and up to 2.8% on MathVista (R1-OneVision: 58.7 vs. 55.9). Against CCOT (Cheng & Van Durme, 2024), the margin is larger, reaching 5.2% on MMVP with R1-OneVision (72.4 vs. 69.1) and 4.0% on MM-Math with Qwen2.5-VL (33.3 vs. 30.2). Multimodal CoT (Zhang et al., 2023) is the weakest baseline, with LDPVR exceeding it by 2.3–6.9% depending on backbone and task. In the appendix comparison against pure latent-space methods (Table 4), LDPVR beats PixelReasoner (Wang et al., 2025) by 0.5–2.5% and LVR (Li et al., 2025) by 0.3–3.3% on average, while also surpassing Vthinker and Monet on most benchmarks, confirming that the interleaved latent grounding mechanism provides advantages over both explicit scaffolding and purely latent approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves a middle path between two established multimodal reasoning paradigms—explicit textual CoT (Multimodal-CoT, CCOT, SCAFFOLD) and tool-augmented 'thinking with images'—as well as purely latent-space approaches (LVR, Vthinker, Monet). Its key conceptual departure is reframing the latent space not as a passive memory buffer or black-box repository but as an active Markovian transition kernel that drives the evolution of explicit textual states, formalized as a Markov Chain of Recursive State Simplification (MCRSS). The three-stage training curriculum (SFT → latent-text distillation → GRPO) repurposes standard techniques in a specific sequence to bridge explicit reasoning logic with efficient latent inference, while the interleaved latent grounding mechanism (semantic anchoring + progressive evidence retrieval) is the architectural novelty that distinguishes it from prior latent approaches that rely on auxiliary image supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical tension in multimodal reasoning: explicit CoT is interpretable but verbose and prone to textual context dominance, while pure latent reasoning is efficient but opaque and susceptible to semantic drift. LDPVR's gating mechanism for dynamic early termination offers a concrete efficiency lever, and the Pareto-frontier positioning (top accuracy at near-No-CoT latency) is practically relevant for deployment. However, the theoretical guarantee of monotonic entropy decrease is conditional on the latent phase extracting more novel information than is lost in text compression—a condition that is difficult to verify empirically. Open questions include whether the framework scales beyond 7B backbones, whether the MCRSS formulation generalizes to video or multi-image reasoning, and whether the modest 1–7% gains over strong baselines (SCAFFOLD, Monet) persist on harder, longer-horizon tasks.
