## Problem

Dataset pruning for 3D data remains largely unexplored, and the inherent long-tail class distributions in 3D benchmarks (e.g., 153× ratio in ShapeNet55) create a fundamental conflict between overall accuracy (OA) and mean class accuracy (mAcc) as evaluation targets. No prior work has provided a principled framework that simultaneously respects both priors while enabling flexible preference steering, leaving practitioners to make premature trade-offs between the two metrics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266052/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a full 3D dataset (point clouds or meshes), a pruning budget B specifying the target subset size, and a steering parameter K ∈ [0,1] that controls the OA–mAcc preference. A teacher model is first trained on the full dataset to produce calibrated soft labels and embedding geometry; the pruned subset is then selected from the teacher's embedding space. The output is a compact coreset of 3D samples plus a student model trained on that subset via knowledge distillation, evaluated under both the natural (empirical) and uniform class priors.

## Method

3D-Pruner is grounded in a quadrature-approximation formulation that decomposes pruning error into representation error (within-class fidelity) and prior-mismatch bias (distribution shift from the subset). To resolve prior-mismatch bias, the method decouples structural likelihood from class priors via knowledge distillation: the teacher's classifier head is retrained with a class-balanced objective to calibrate soft labels, and Relational Knowledge Distillation (RKD) transfers pairwise distances and triplet angles from the teacher embedding to preserve manifold geometry under sparsity. To minimize representation error, the method selects samples using embedding-geometric signals (FL-RBF) rather than classifier-derived scalars (Loss, EL2N), which are shown to be strongly correlated with class frequency and thus unreliable under imbalance. Seeded Global Selection (SGS) wraps the base selector by first allocating a per-class safety floor of b = ⌊KB/|C|⌋ samples to guarantee coverage of the shared high-return error regime, then filling the remaining budget with global geometric selection; K directly interpolates between stratified seeding (mAcc-favoring) and pure global selection (OA-favoring), providing a single-knob preference control.

## Results

On ShapeNet55 (m=550, ~1.3% of data), 3D-Pruner with FL-RBF(K=0.4) achieves 84.95% OA and 74.89% mAcc, improving mAcc by +6.53 points over the unsteered FL-RBF baseline (68.36%) while retaining competitive OA (−0.57). On ModelNet40 (m=400), it reaches 88.33% OA / 83.96% mAcc, and on ScanObjectNN (m=150), 57.84% OA / 53.47% mAcc. Across all three datasets and multiple budgets, the method consistently dominates both OA and mAcc simultaneously, with the steering parameter K providing a controllable trade-off (e.g., K=0.6 on ShapeNet55 pushes mAcc to 76.49% at a cost of −1.25 OA).

## Baselines & Comparisons

3D-Pruner (FL-RBF with SGS) outperforms all eight baseline families on mAcc across every dataset and budget, with the largest gains on the most imbalanced dataset ShapeNet55 where mAcc improvements reach +6.5 to +8.1 points over the unsteered FL-RBF. Against the strongest stratified baselines (NUCS-EL2N, CCS-CP-EL2N, Balance-EL2N), the method wins on both OA and mAcc simultaneously: on ShapeNet55 (m=550), NUCS-EL2N achieves 78.17/71.89 while 3D-Pruner reaches 84.95/74.89. Scalar-signal methods (Loss, EL2N, Entropy) are dramatically weaker, with EL2N on ShapeNet55 collapsing to 52.23 OA / 19.45 mAcc due to extreme class-frequency bias (40× imbalance ratio in selection). DRoP, despite its distributionally-robust formulation, is the weakest stratified method (45.50/54.73 on ShapeNet55), suggesting its difficulty-based allocation is unreliable under strong 3D inductive bias. Among embedding-based global selectors, FL-RBF already dominates Herding and K-Center on OA, and SGS further lifts mAcc by 6–8 points without sacrificing OA, confirming that the safety-floor mechanism is the key differentiator.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the well-established 2D dataset pruning literature (submodular selection via FL-RBF, k-center core-sets, herding, gradient matching) to the 3D modality, where long-tail class distributions in both train and test splits create a novel OA-vs-mAcc dilemma absent in balanced 2D benchmarks. It departs from prior imbalance-aware pruning methods (DRoP, NUCS, CCS-CP) that optimize a single fixed target criterion by explicitly formulating pruning as a quadrature approximation of population risk, decomposing error into representation error and prior-mismatch bias, and introducing a steering parameter to navigate between the two evaluation priors. The post-pruning training leverages knowledge distillation (calibrated soft labels and relational KD) in a way that exploits KD's structural invariance to reweighting—a property the authors argue is typically overlooked in the dataset distillation literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first principled treatment of 3D dataset pruning, this work provides a theoretical framework (quadrature decomposition) and practical guidelines (embedding geometry over scalar signals, safety floors, KD-based distillation) that should inform future 3D data-efficient training. The finding that complex imbalance-aware allocators can be outperformed by simple stratified sampling on ShapeNet55 challenges the assumption that more sophisticated difficulty estimation always helps under strong class imbalance. Open questions include whether the steering mechanism and geometric selection principles scale to much larger 3D datasets (e.g., ScanNet-scale), whether they transfer to 3D detection/segmentation tasks, and how the framework behaves when the test distribution differs substantially from the training distribution.
