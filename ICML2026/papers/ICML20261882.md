## Problem

LLMs struggle to dynamically adapt to diverse conditions (task, domain, persona, style, sentiment) without degrading their general capabilities. Finetuning causes catastrophic forgetting, in-context learning suffers from high variance and context-length limitations, and gradient-based meta-learning is computationally prohibitive at LLM scale. The paper seeks a unified, parameter-efficient mechanism that lets a frozen LLM adapt its internal computation to arbitrary textual conditions without task-specific retraining.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text input x and a textual condition z (e.g., a persona profile, domain label, style descriptor, or task description). The condition z is inserted into a template prompt, tokenized using the LLM's own embedding layer (no separate encoder), and fed into a lightweight hypernetwork that produces per-layer β vectors of dimension C (the FFN intermediate size, 14336 for Llama-3.1-8B). The output is a standard conditioned text response generated by the modified LLM, with the hypernetwork's β output cached after the first forward pass so subsequent decoding tokens incur negligible overhead.

## Method

MeGan replaces the fixed SiLU activation in each SwiGLU FFN block with a parameterized Swish_β, creating a β-SwiGLU block where β dynamically controls the nonlinearity slope of the gating function. A lightweight hypernetwork (cross-attention with reduced dimension R, plus a one-layer bias-free MLP with tanh) maps the embedded textual condition and a layer-position embedding to a per-layer β vector, effectively converting self-gating into condition-driven meta-gating. The base LLM parameters remain frozen; only the hypernetwork parameters (3.4M–13.6M depending on R) are trained via supervised fine-tuning with cross-entropy loss plus an L2 regularization term that anchors β near 1. The bottleneck architecture (D→R→C) enforces information compression, extracting key conditional patterns while reducing trainable parameters below comparable hypernetwork baselines like Text-to-LoRA.

## Results

On the multi-condition benchmark (Table 4), MeGan achieves the best R-L and B-2 scores across all five condition types (persona, domain, style, sentiment). The most dramatic gain is on AdaptSum (domain), where MeGan reaches R-L=41.85 and B-2=26.88, nearly doubling the next-best SFT (20.99/8.80) and Text-to-LoRA (20.02/7.98). On CrossFit&UnifiedQA (Table 5), MeGan outperforms all baselines across all seven transfer settings, e.g., 73.8/84.4 on non-NLI→NLI vs. 52.4/65.0 for MAML-en-LLM. On the 10 unseen SNI benchmarks (Table 6), MeGan achieves the highest average score of 77.5, leading on 7 of 10 benchmarks (ArcC 85.3, ArcE 94.2, HS 77.5, OQA 84.2, PIQA 85.8, WG 67.5, BQ 83.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MeGan consistently outperforms all baselines across the five condition types. Against the closest hypernetwork competitor Text-to-LoRA, MeGan wins decisively on AdaptSum (R-L 41.85 vs. 20.02, a 2.1× improvement) and GYAFC (R-L 29.37 vs. 22.95), while Text-to-LoRA retains a narrow edge on Persona-Chat B-2 (13.70 vs. 10.15) and MIC D-2 (0.42 vs. 0.17). Against standard fine-tuning baselines (SFT, LoRA), MeGan's advantage is largest on domain and sentiment conditions: on SST, MeGan achieves R-L=22.76 vs. SFT's 12.77 and LoRA's 13.31, more than doubling their scores. On CrossFit&UnifiedQA task transfer, MeGan surpasses MAML-en-LLM by 15–22 points on unseen-task accuracy (e.g., 84.4 vs. 65.0 on non-NLI→NLI) and Text-to-LoRA by 7–34 points depending on the setting. On the 10 SNI general benchmarks, MeGan leads the average (77.5 vs. 76.9 for Text-to-LoRA, 76.5 for LoRA) but trails Text-to-LoRA on GSM8K (74.1 vs. 79.1) and HumanEval pass@1 (57.9 vs. 64.6), suggesting the meta-gating mechanism is less effective for math and code generation. Prompting baselines (ICL, CoT, Plan-and-Solve, Metacognitive Prompting) are uniformly outperformed, with MeGan achieving 2–3× higher R-L on style and sentiment tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the hypernetwork paradigm (Ha et al., 2017) and the closest prior work, Text-to-LoRA (Charakorn et al., 2025), which uses a text-conditioned hypernetwork to generate LoRA weight matrices. The key departure is that MeGan generates β values that modulate the nonlinearity of the FFN's Swish activation (β-SwiGLU) rather than modifying linear weight matrices, drawing an explicit analogy to neuromodulators controlling synaptic gain in neuroscience. This positions the method as a 'meta-gating' mechanism distinct from both adapter-based approaches (which alter the latent directly) and in-context meta-learning methods (which rely on prompt engineering), and it unifies adaptation across diverse condition types (task, domain, persona, style, sentiment) under a single lightweight architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MeGan offers a practically attractive recipe for condition-adaptive LLMs: freeze the backbone, train only 3.4M–13.6M hypernetwork parameters, and obtain zero-shot adaptation to unseen tasks or condition types with minimal inference overhead. The consistent outperformance over LoRA, SFT, and Text-to-LoRA across six condition types and multiple benchmarks suggests that modulating activation nonlinearity is a sufficient and underexplored control channel for LLM adaptation. Open questions include whether the mechanism generalizes to non-textual conditions (e.g., images, audio), whether it can acquire genuinely new knowledge (as opposed to reweighting existing capabilities), and how performance scales to much larger backbones or longer contexts. The authors also note that vague or incorrect condition inputs degrade performance, highlighting a robustness gap.
