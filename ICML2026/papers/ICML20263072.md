## Problem

Existing self-consistency methods for LLM reasoning suffer from two critical inefficiencies: they evaluate output consistency monolithically (all-or-nothing), failing to exploit partially correct answers across samples, and they rely on static stopping criteria that cannot adapt to varying task difficulties or heterogeneous LLM capabilities, resulting in either insufficient sampling for hard problems or wasteful oversampling for easy ones.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263072/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text question, the target LLM, and a pre-sampled historical performance database (100 question-answer tuples collected via Adaptive Consistency) as inputs. For structured tasks, the output is a fully resolved JSON object where each schema field converges independently; for unstructured tasks, the output is a final answer selected by majority vote over reasoning samples that are consistent with converged semantic anchors. The LLM itself serves as both the anchor generator and the difficulty classifier, avoiding dependence on a stronger proprietary model.

## Method

TMAFC introduces two complementary mechanisms. Fractal-Consistency (FC) decomposes the output space into independently evaluable atomic units—explicit schema fields for structured tasks or LLM-generated semantic anchors for unstructured tasks—and applies the Beta stopping criterion per component, removing converged components from subsequent prompts so the LLM focuses only on unresolved parts; for unstructured tasks, after all anchors converge, samples violating the anchor consensus are filtered before a final majority vote. Adaptive Stopping Criteria Calibration (ASCC) replaces AC's fixed (α₀=1, β₀=1) prior with task- and model-aware parameters: an LLM-based classifier (using 7 linguistic/logical features and few-shot historical examples) assigns each question to one of K=3 difficulty levels, and MLE over the historical Beta-binomial likelihood for that category (solved via L-BFGS-B with box constraints) yields calibrated (α₀, β₀) that increase β₀ for harder tasks or weaker models, automatically demanding more samples to ensure reliable consensus.

## Results

On structured tasks (NER, RE, EE) with GPT-4, TMAFC reduces sampling cost by up to 78.5% (RE: 21.48 vs. 100 for SC) and 55% (vs. AC) while improving accuracy by 0.4%–1.5% (e.g., EE: 43.6% vs. 41.8% for AC). On unstructured tasks (GSM-hard, MathP-hard, Penguins, Disambiguation_QA), TMAFC cuts sampling cost by up to 60.5% (Symbolic: 19.77 vs. 50 for SC) and 38% (vs. AC) with a ~0.8% accuracy gain. Across four LLMs (GPT-4, GPT-3.5-Turbo, Qwen3-14B, Llama3-8B-Instruct) on hybrid tasks, TMAFC achieves an average 32% reduction in sample cost versus AC while maintaining or slightly improving accuracy (e.g., +0.75% on GPT-4 Hard tasks).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TMAFC consistently outperforms all three baselines on sampling cost while matching or exceeding accuracy. Against Self-Consistency (SC, Wang et al. 2023), TMAFC reduces sampling cost by 67–79% on structured tasks (e.g., NER: 16.44 vs. 50) and 58–60% on unstructured tasks (e.g., Symbolic: 19.77 vs. 50), while improving accuracy by up to 1.5%. Against Early-Stopping Self-Consistency (ESC, Li et al. 2024), TMAFC further reduces cost by 40–60% (e.g., RE: 21.48 vs. 65.8) with equal or better accuracy, as ESC's window-based stopping cannot exploit component-level convergence. Against Adaptive Consistency (AC, Aggarwal et al. 2023), the primary SOTA baseline, TMAFC achieves the largest relative gains: up to 55% cost reduction on structured tasks and 38% on unstructured tasks, with small accuracy improvements (+0.4% to +1.5%), demonstrating that both Fractal-Consistency and ASCC contribute complementary efficiency gains. Notably, TMAFC's advantage over AC grows with model weakness (Llama3-8B: −9.1% avg cost) and task difficulty (Hard: −15.8% for GPT-4), confirming the adaptive calibration mechanism works as intended.

## Relation to Prior Work

TMAFC extends the self-consistency paradigm (Wang et al., ICLR 2023) and its early-stopping variants—Adaptive Consistency (Aggarwal et al., EMNLP 2023) and Early-Stopping Self-Consistency (Li et al., ICLR 2024)—by attacking two limitations those methods share: monolithic whole-output evaluation and static stopping parameters. The Fractal-Consistency mechanism departs from prior work by decomposing outputs into atomic components (schema fields for structured tasks, LLM-generated anchors for unstructured tasks) and applying the Beta stopping criterion independently to each, enabling cross-sample recombination of partial correctness. The ASCC module further departs from AC's fixed non-informative prior (α₀=1, β₀=1) by calibrating the Beta parameters via MLE over per-difficulty-category historical data, coupled with an LLM-based difficulty classifier that operates in a dual online/offline loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for anyone deploying self-consistency at scale: reducing sampling cost by up to 55% (structured) and 38% (unstructured) while maintaining or slightly improving accuracy directly lowers inference spend without architectural changes to the base LLM. However, the approach introduces non-trivial overhead—anchor generation accuracy drops to ~55% on weaker models, and the LLM-based difficulty classifier adds ~8–24% token cost—so the net savings depend on task difficulty distribution and model capability. Open questions include how anchor quality degrades on genuinely novel or multi-step compositional problems, whether the MLE-based calibration remains stable under non-stationary task distributions, and whether the component-wise stopping criterion generalizes to open-ended generation tasks where 'atomic units' are harder to define.
