## Problem

Cross-validation confidence intervals for model comparison rely on a relative loss stability condition r(h_n)=o(1), but it was unknown whether individually stable algorithms could violate this condition when compared against each other. This paper proves that even simple, individually stable estimators (Lasso, soft-thresholding) generate relatively unstable comparisons in a standard linear model, rendering standard CV confidence intervals asymptotically invalid with severe undercoverage.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes i.i.d. data from an intercept-free linear model with Gaussian features and noise, two prediction algorithms (e.g., ST(λ_n) vs. ST(λ_n+δ_n) or Lasso with slightly different penalties), and a k-fold CV structure. It produces the relative stability measure r(h_n), the loss stability γ(h_n), the variance σ²(h_n), and CV confidence intervals for the test error difference R_n. A key structural detail is that the comparison loss h_n^diff is the difference of two squared-error losses, and the critical regime is when both algorithms are consistent (so σ²(h_n^diff)→0), making the relative condition far harder to satisfy than the absolute one.

## Method

The paper introduces relative loss stability r(h_n)=n·γ(h_n)/σ²(h_n) as the operative condition for the CV central limit theorem and consistent variance estimation, then proves that ST and Lasso comparisons violate it with r(h_n)=Ω(√n)≠o(1) whenever the true parameter β* has at least one zero coefficient. The proof proceeds by first establishing a Lasso–ST proximity bound (Lemma 2.3) via an optimizer comparison argument, then analyzing the ST comparison directly: σ²(h_n^diff) scales as 1/n² while γ(h_n^diff) is only O(1/(n²√n)), so their ratio diverges. The instability is traced to the non-smoothness of soft-thresholding at zero coefficients, where a single data point change can flip a coefficient between zero and nonzero, creating persistent variance in the loss difference. The paper also shows that when β* is fully dense (no zeros), ST comparisons become relatively stable, and provides an alternative conservative CI construction (Proposition 6.1) that combines individual algorithm intervals to guarantee valid comparison coverage without requiring relative stability of the difference.

## Results

The paper proves that for the ST(λ_n) vs ST(λ_n+δ_n) comparison under the linear model with sparse β*, σ²(h_n^diff) = O(1/n²) while γ(h_n^diff) = Ω(1/(n²√n)), yielding relative instability r(h_n^diff) = Ω(√n) ≠ o(1). In contrast, the single-algorithm assessment of ST is relatively stable with r(h_n^sing) ~ C/(2τ⁴)·(1/n) = o(1). Identical instability results hold for Lasso comparisons (Theorem 3.3). In simulations (β*=(3,1,-5,3,0,0,0,0,0,0), τ=10, k=10, n up to 90,000), the within-fold variance estimator underestimates the targeted variance of √(nk/(k−1))(R̂_n − R_n), causing severe undercoverage of CV confidence intervals for the comparison, while coverage is accurate for single-algorithm assessment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper without traditional method-vs-method benchmarks, so 'baselines' refer to alternative algorithms and settings used to contextualize the instability result. The key comparison is between single-algorithm assessment and model comparison: ST and Lasso are individually relatively stable (r(h_n^sing)=o(1)) yet their pairwise comparison is relatively unstable (r(h_n^diff)=Ω(√n)), a dichotomy that does not occur for ridge regression, which satisfies relative stability in both settings (γ scales as 1/n² for single and 1/n⁴ for comparison, both faster than the corresponding σ² rates). The paper also contrasts sparse β* (‖β*‖₀<p) with dense β* (‖β*‖₀=p): a single zero coefficient in β* is sufficient to trigger instability for ST and Lasso comparisons, while fully dense β* restores relative stability. The Lasso-ST proximity bound (Lemma 2.3) shows the two estimators are within O(λ_n√p/(nμ_n)) of each other, allowing all ST results to transfer directly to Lasso without separate proofs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CV inference framework of Bayle et al. (2020) and Austern & Zhou (2020), which established central limit theorems and consistent variance estimators under loss stability conditions (Kumar et al., 2013). The key departure is that prior work treated stability as a property of a single algorithm, while this paper introduces and analyzes *relative* loss stability for algorithm comparisons, showing that the asymptotic variance in the CLT can vanish when comparing two consistent algorithms, invalidating the standard inference even when each algorithm is individually stable. The paper also positions itself against Luo & Barber (2024), who study stability for expected test error rather than the realized test error targeted here, and against Bates et al. (2024), who observe slow convergence for single-algorithm CV coverage but do not address the comparison setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Since CV is the dominant tool for model comparison in practice, the finding that standard CV confidence intervals can severely undercover for comparisons of individually stable algorithms (Lasso, soft-thresholding) is a practically important warning: practitioners may be drawing invalid conclusions about model improvements. The paper provides a concrete diagnostic (the relative stability ratio r(h_n)) and an alternative, possibly conservative, CI construction (Proposition 6.1) that requires only individual stability. Key limitations include the restriction to Lasso/ST in a linear Gaussian model, the lack of broadly verifiable conditions for relative stability, and the open question of whether relative instability always implies CV invalidity. Future work should establish general conditions under which comparisons are or are not relatively stable and develop tighter inference procedures that exploit correlation structure between the two algorithms.
