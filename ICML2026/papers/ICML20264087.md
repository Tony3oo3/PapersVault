## Problem

Existing online resource-allocation frameworks each capture only one layer of a natural two-layer decision: Bandits with Knapsacks (BwK) handles configuration selection but commits resources immediately upon pulling an arm (sample-and-commit), while Online Linear Programming (OLP) handles observe-then-decide admission but assumes a fixed request distribution with no configuration choice. Moreover, the natural fixed-configuration oracle fails to upper-bound feasible online policies when resources are heterogeneous, because switching among configurations can exploit complementary budgets. The paper unifies both layers into a single select-observe-admit protocol and constructs a valid switching-aware benchmark.

## Input / Output

At each of T periods the algorithm receives a reward-resource pair (r_t, a_t) ∈ R_+ × R_+^d drawn from the distribution D_{θ_t} induced by the chosen configuration, and must output an irrevocable admission decision x_t ∈ {0,1} subject to the pathwise budget constraint Σ a_t x_t ≤ B. The algorithm internally maintains per-configuration sample sets S_θ (storing all observed pairs regardless of admission for unbiased estimation), a mixture distribution w_t over the K configurations, and a global dual price vector p_t. The saddle-point LP is re-solved only O(K log T) times over the horizon via a doubling schedule, keeping per-round computation tractable.

## Method

The paper introduces a switching-aware fluid oracle whose value V^mix(b) is characterized as a max-min problem: max over a mixture w on the simplex, min over a price p of ⟨p,b⟩ + Σ w_θ g_θ(p), where g_θ(p) is the expected surplus under configuration θ. Saddle points of this max-min are characterized by three conditions—envelope support (w randomizes only among configurations attaining the maximum surplus at p*), feasibility (induced threshold consumption ≤ b), and complementarity (price times slack = 0)—linking the dual price to a threshold admission rule x_θ(r,a) = 1{r > ⟨p*,a⟩}. The SP-UCB-OLP algorithm solves an optimistic version of this saddle point at each round: it replaces population surpluses with empirical estimates plus UCB confidence radii β_{α,θ}(t) = α√((d log t + log(KT/δ))/N_θ), solves the resulting finite LP to obtain (w_t, p_t), samples θ_t ~ w_t, and admits the request if r_t > ⟨p_t, a_t⟩ and the remaining budget permits. A conservative per-period budget b^safe = (1−ε)b with ε = √(log T/T) ensures pathwise feasibility with high probability. The algorithm achieves Õ(α√(KT)) switching-aware regret under stationarity, boundedness, and no-tie assumptions.

## Results

SP-UCB-OLP achieves $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{KT})$ regret against the switching-aware fluid oracle (Theorem 5), empirically confirmed by Regret/$\sqrt{T}$ remaining approximately constant at ≈1.6 across horizons T ∈ {100, 200, 500, 1000, 2000} on a K=5 Gaussian scenario. On real Alibaba cluster traces (T=5,000, d=2, K=3), minimal exploration (α=0.01) yields 97.4% ± 0.6% competitive ratio, approaching the Oracle's 99.95%, while Greedy (α=0) drops to 84.8% ± 14.2% with 10/50 seeds below 70%. On the complementarity-gap scenario S4, all learning algorithms achieve CR^mix ≤ 1 (validating the oracle as an upper bound) while exceeding the fixed-configuration oracle (CR* > 1), confirming the necessity of the switching-aware benchmark.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares SP-UCB-OLP against four baselines: Greedy (α=0, pure exploitation), Random (uniform configuration selection), Oracle (true optimal mixture and price), and OneHot (single-configuration commitment). On Alibaba traces, SP-UCB-OLP with α=0.01 achieves 97.4% ± 0.6% competitive ratio, dramatically outperforming Greedy (84.8% ± 14.2%) with a 24× reduction in variance, while approaching Oracle (99.95%) and far exceeding Random (61.1%). On the complementarity scenario S4, Greedy and Random actually outperform SP-UCB-OLP on CR^mix (0.96 and 0.99 vs. 0.91), but all three exceed the fixed oracle (CR* > 1), while OneHot (CR* ≈ 1.01) cannot exploit resource complementarity at all. The key finding is that even minimal exploration (α=0.01) prevents regime lock-in, making SP-UCB-OLP robust where pure exploitation fails.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two established online optimization frameworks—Bandits with Knapsacks (BwK, Badanidiyuru et al. 2018) for configuration selection under budget constraints, and Online Linear Programming (OLP, Agrawal et al. 2014; Li & Ye 2022) for observation-based threshold admission—into a single two-layer 'select-observe-admit' protocol. The key departure from both is the introduction of a switching-aware fluid oracle that accounts for the value of mixing configurations over time, replacing the naive fixed-configuration oracle that provably fails as an upper bound under heterogeneous resources. The algorithmic contribution (SP-UCB-OLP) combines UCB-style exploration over configurations with dual-price threshold admission, solving an optimistic saddle-point problem at each round via a finite LP.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The two-layer structure directly models practical systems in LLM serving, GPU cluster scheduling, and revenue management where configuration choice and admission are coupled sequential decisions. The switching-aware oracle provides a theoretically valid benchmark (unlike the fixed-configuration oracle), and the Õ(√(KT)) regret guarantee gives practitioners a principled algorithm with sublinear loss. Limitations include the i.i.d. stationarity assumption, bounded reward-resource pairs, and the absence of contextual or non-stationary settings; the Alibaba trace validation, while encouraging, uses synthetic reward coefficients rather than native revenue signals. Open questions include extending to non-stationary arrivals, contextual configurations, and delayed feedback.
