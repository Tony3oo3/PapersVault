## Problem

Simulating deformable objects under robot manipulation in a real-to-sim setting remains unsolved: physics-based simulators require predefined material parameters that are intractable to infer from visual data, while neural dynamics methods reproduce observed trajectories without robot-conditioned control or long-horizon stability. Neither paradigm supports action-controllable, interaction-consistent simulation of complex soft-body tasks like cloth folding under partial occlusion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261746/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes synchronized multi-view RGB frames (640×480, 30 FPS from three cameras) and per-frame robot joint states from an ARX-Lift arm, along with a fixed initial Gaussian splat representation G₀ reconstructed via Pi3 + 3DGS. It produces a temporally rolled-out sequence of Gaussian splat states (position, anisotropic covariance, mass, physical attributes) that are rendered under known camera poses to yield predicted RGB images. The simulation is open-loop and conditioned on the full joint trajectory; the initial splat configuration is fixed, precluding the random reinitialization tricks available to particle-based simulators.

## Method

SoMA represents the deformable object as a three-level hierarchical graph of Gaussian splats and models dynamics as explicit force-driven updates rather than state-only regression. Environmental forces (gravity plus proximity-based support) and robot-induced forces (predicted by a graph neural network over an interaction graph between robot control points and splats, conditioned on gripper state) are aggregated bottom-up and fed into a hierarchical mesh graph network that predicts per-node velocity and rotation, propagated top-down through learned affine transforms. A robot-conditioned R2S mapping (similarity transform recovered from a wrist-mounted camera) anchors the reconstructed scene to the robot's joint-space frame so that forward-kinematics outputs directly drive the interaction graph. Training uses a two-stage multi-resolution temporal schedule (coarse stride k·dt then fine dt) combined with a blended loss: occlusion-aware masked image reconstruction (L2 + D-SSIM) for visible regions and a mass-conservation regularization across hierarchy levels to regularize occluded splats, yielding stable long-horizon rollouts without predefined physical models.

## Results

On the SoMA real-world manipulation dataset (rope, cloth, doll), SoMA achieves the best performance across all metrics on both resimulation and generalization. On resimulation, SoMA reaches PSNR 33.51, SSIM 0.971, LPIPS 0.055, Abs Rel 0.089, and RMSE 0.124, outperforming PhysTwin (PSNR 28.77) and GausSim (PSNR 31.69). On generalization to unseen manipulations, SoMA attains PSNR 32.89 and LPIPS 0.062, a substantial margin over PhysTwin (PSNR 26.54) and GausSim (PSNR 31.29). On the more challenging T-shirt folding task, SoMA achieves PSNR 27.57 and SSIM 0.896 versus PhysTwin's 22.85 and 0.842, demonstrating stable long-horizon simulation under complex self-contact.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SoMA is compared against two baselines: PhysTwin (a differentiable physics-based simulator) and GausSim (a state-based neural GS simulator). SoMA wins on every metric in both resimulation and generalization settings. Against PhysTwin, SoMA gains +4.74 dB PSNR on resimulation (33.51 vs. 28.77) and +6.35 dB on generalization (32.89 vs. 26.54), with LPIPS reduced from 0.086 to 0.055 (resim) and 0.092 to 0.062 (gen); PhysTwin degrades notably under occlusion-heavy robot manipulation due to its reliance on explicit point tracking. Against GausSim, SoMA improves PSNR by +1.82 dB (resim) and +1.60 dB (gen), while reducing LPIPS from 0.092 to 0.055 (resim) and 0.127 to 0.062 (gen); GausSim, being purely state-based without action conditioning, exhibits increasing deviation in later timesteps and fails to generalize to unseen actions. On the T-shirt folding task, SoMA outperforms PhysTwin by +4.72 dB PSNR (27.57 vs. 22.85) and reduces LPIPS from 0.198 to 0.128, confirming its advantage in long-horizon, self-contact-heavy scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

SoMA builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) for scene representation and on the hierarchical graph dynamics architecture of GausSim (Shao et al., 2025) for multi-scale state propagation. However, it departs from both physics-based simulators (PhysTwin, differentiable simulators) by eliminating predefined physical models, and from state-based neural simulators (GausSim, GS-Dynamics) by introducing explicit force-driven dynamics with robot-action conditioning. The key conceptual departure is the robot-conditioned R2S mapping that anchors learned GS dynamics to joint-space actions, combined with a blended supervision scheme (occlusion-aware image loss + physics consistency regularization) that addresses the partial observability inherent in manipulation scenes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SoMA provides a practical pathway to scalable data synthesis for soft-body manipulation without manual physics parameterization, which is a significant bottleneck for embodied learning. The demonstrated ability to stably simulate long-horizon T-shirt folding with self-contacts, while maintaining 20% improvement over baselines, suggests the approach could reduce real-robot data collection costs. However, the evaluation is limited to four object categories on a single robot platform with 13K splats, and the paper itself acknowledges that broader validation across diverse geometries, materials, and interaction patterns is needed. Open questions include whether the force-driven formulation scales to more complex multi-object scenes and whether the hierarchical GS representation can handle objects with significantly different topologies.
