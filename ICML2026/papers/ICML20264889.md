## Problem

Variational estimation of Amari α-divergences requires a critic whose expressiveness, optimization stability, and computational cost are all simultaneously controlled. Unconstrained neural critics suffer from high-variance objectives and unstable training, while exact RKHS kernel methods provide principled capacity control but incur O(n³) Gram-matrix complexity that precludes large-scale use. No existing framework reconciles these tensions with a unified non-asymptotic error analysis covering approximation, discretization, sampling, and optimization effects.

## Input / Output

The estimator consumes m i.i.d. samples from distribution P and n i.i.d. samples from distribution Q (both on a compact domain in R^d) and outputs a single scalar estimate of D_A^{(α)}(P‖Q). In the GAN application, the same smoothed Amari objective is used as the discriminator loss: the discriminator receives real images (CelebA 64×64 or FFHQ 256×256) and generated images, and the generator receives latent noise vectors z to produce images. The divergence estimator operates on arbitrary numerical data, while the GAN variant processes RGB images at standard resolutions.

## Method

The critic is constrained to an RKHS ball of radius R (Ivanov constraint) and approximated by a bandlimited Random Fourier Feature map ψ(x) ∈ R^D, reducing the infinite-dimensional variational problem to a finite-dimensional linear objective f_w(x) = w^T ψ(x) with ‖w‖₂ as a norm proxy. A smoothed surrogate φ_{p,ε}(u) = (u²+ε²)^{p/2} replaces |u|^p to ensure differentiability when exponents a or b fall below 1, and the objective is optimized via Armijo-SGD whose local linear convergence is guaranteed by a PL condition induced by statistical non-degeneracy (a Fisher-information-like curvature in the feature covariance). A scale-invariant post-processing step fixes the learned direction ŵ and analytically re-optimizes the scalar amplitude t to remove regularization bias before computing the final divergence. The theory provides a four-term error decomposition (RKHS approximation O(R^{-γ}), RFF discretization O(RD^{-1/2}), statistical O(N^{-1/2}), optimization residual) under a spectral source condition, with explicit schedules coupling (R, W, D, ε, λ). For GANs, fixed Spatial RFF positional embeddings are injected into the CNN discriminator bottleneck with zero-initialized fusion weights, adding a controlled high-frequency spectral component without disrupting early training.

## Results

On CelebA, injecting Spatial RFF with bandwidth σ=5 into the CNN discriminator reduces FID from 240.47 (σ=0 baseline) to 27.23, KID from 22.59×10⁻² to 2.15×10⁻², and SWD from 143.92 to 120.78, while increasing high-frequency energy from 24.89 to 32.57. On synthetic divergence estimation across d∈{2,5,10} and n up to 5×10⁶, the RFF-Estimator achieves the lowest mean estimation error among all four critic variants, maintaining stable performance at d=10 where the unconstrained Basic(NN) diverges. The RFF estimator also exhibits a consistent error profile in low-sample regimes where unconstrained neural baselines show instability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four critic architectures on synthetic divergence estimation: Basic(NN) (standard MLP), Adaptive(NN) (MLP with width scaling with n), RFF-Estimator (linear critic over RFF), and RKHS(NN) (neural critic with explicit smoothness regularization). The RFF-Estimator achieves the lowest mean error overall, effectively outperforming the RKHS(NN) baseline and substantially outperforming both unconstrained MLP variants, particularly at d=10 where Basic(NN) diverges. On CelebA, the Spatial-RFF discriminator (σ=5) dramatically outperforms the no-RFF baseline (σ=0): FID drops by ~89% (27.23 vs 240.47) and KID by ~90% (2.15 vs 22.59 ×10⁻²), while high-frequency energy increases by ~31%. The σ=2 setting achieves the best SWD (127.23 vs 120.78 for σ=5), suggesting a slight trade-off between SWD and FID/KID at the highest bandwidth. The paper also reports a rebuttal validation on FFHQ-256 with an R3GAN backbone, where the RFF-augmented variant substantially reduces saturation artifacts compared to the baseline, though no quantitative table is provided for this setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RKHS-constrained critic framework for divergence estimation (Ghimire et al., 2021; Nguyen et al., 2010) from KL to the full Amari α-family, while replacing the cubic-complexity exact kernel implementation with bandlimited Random Fourier Features (Rahimi & Recht, 2007; Avron et al., 2017). The variational power-type objective J(f)=E_P[|f|^a]−E_Q[|f|^b] follows the f-GAN variational minimization paradigm (Nowozin et al., 2016), but the key departure is the unified four-term error decomposition (RKHS approximation, RFF discretization, sampling, optimization) with explicit spectral coupling between the RKHS radius R, bandwidth W(R), and feature budget D. The local PL geometry result—that statistical non-degeneracy of the data distribution induces intrinsic curvature in the RFF parameter space, independent of the Tikhonov regularizer—goes beyond prior analyses that treat optimization error as an external nuisance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a principled, linear-time alternative to exact kernel methods for divergence estimation with non-asymptotic guarantees, and the explicit (R, W, D, ε, λ) schedules give practitioners concrete hyperparameter guidance rather than ad-hoc tuning. The GAN application (Spatial RFF injection into the discriminator) demonstrates a practical recipe for improving high-frequency capture under non-standard objectives, though the experiments are limited to CelebA and FFHQ-256 with a basic CNN backbone, and the 'stability' claim is strictly critic-side. Open questions include whether the spectral coupling and PL geometry results extend to non-shift-invariant kernels, data-adaptive bandwidths, and larger-scale generative models where the linear RFF critic may be insufficiently expressive.
