## Problem

Automated multi-agent system (MAS) design methods such as ADAS, AFlow, and GPTSwarm rely on singular fixed optimization strategies, templated agent structures, and ignore task difficulty gradients, limiting cross-domain generalization. The paper addresses the fundamental tension between the inefficiency of purely evolutionary search and the rigidity of manual design, asking how to build a framework that simultaneously evolves workflow topology, agent roles, and search strategies in a task-adaptive manner.

## Input / Output

EvoMAS takes a task distribution T (partitioned into n ordered difficulty subsets via LLM-as-a-Judge) and an initial evolutionary rule set R as input, along with a population of candidate workflow graphs initialized via RAG retrieval from a knowledge-rich resource library. The output is an evolved sparse cyclic directed graph G* whose nodes are specialized agents (Planner, Critic, Actor, Custom) with role-specific prompts, tools, and interaction edges, plus an updated rule set and strategy distribution. Workflows are serialized as Python MultiAgentSystem classes, and selection is performed by an LLM evaluating candidates on performance, structural plausibility, and rule conformance rather than scalar fitness alone.

## Method

EvoMAS models workflow evolution as a non-homogeneous Markov process over state (population, rules, strategies), cycling through variation, LLM-based preference-guided selection, and periodic Cyber Creator reflection. Variation applies seven operators—three exploration strategies (diversity expansion via symmetric-difference perturbation, conceptual recombination via subgraph crossover, cross-domain hybridization via structural projection) and three exploitation strategies (pseudo-gradient prompt refinement, elite-consensus synthesis, role specialization mapping)—plus a custom operator for domain-specific transformations. Every K generations, the Cyber Creator meta-controller performs bi-level optimization: it prunes and induces rules to reshape the feasible manifold Ω_R, and re-weights the strategy distribution via Multiplicative Weights Update based on each operator's marginal fitness gain. A curriculum schedule evolves workflows sequentially from easy to hard tasks with a cumulative-performance stability constraint preventing catastrophic forgetting, while a Graph-RAG-based Evolution Resource Library (rule pool + gene pool) accumulates and transfers structural knowledge across stages.

## Results

EvoMAS achieves an average accuracy of 80.06% across six benchmarks (GSM8K, MATH, HumanEval, MBPP, HotpotQA, ALFWorld), setting SOTA on five of six and outperforming the previous best method AFlow by 3.78% on average. Gains are most pronounced on MATH (60.33% vs. AFlow's 55.37%, +4.96%) and HotpotQA (77.90% vs. 73.80%, +4.10%). On the GAIA embodied-intelligence benchmark, EvoMAS leads all difficulty levels with a 22.59% average, surpassing the second-best MaAS by 1.90%. In terms of deployment cost, EvoMAS achieves the lowest TCO@100 ($153.72) among all compared methods, roughly 3× cheaper than AFlow with GPT-4o.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EvoMAS outperforms all six compared baselines on the six-benchmark average. Against the strongest automated baseline AFlow (76.28%), EvoMAS gains 3.78% overall, with the largest per-task margins on MATH (+4.96%) and HotpotQA (+4.10%), while also surpassing AFlow on ALFWorld by 8.12% (67.28% vs. 59.16%). Against AgentSquare (74.16%), the second-strongest automated method, EvoMAS leads by 5.90%, primarily driven by gains on MATH (+12.07%) and HotpotQA (+6.20%). Among manually designed systems, EvoMAS exceeds AgentVerse (69.92%) by 10.14% and MultiPersona (68.10%) by 11.96%. The gap over single-agent baselines (IO, CoT) is even larger at 12–13%, confirming that the evolved multi-agent structure provides substantial value beyond prompt-level improvements. On the GAIA benchmark specifically, EvoMAS (22.59%) beats MaAS (20.69%) by 1.90%, with the widest margin on the hardest Level-3 tasks (8.61% vs. 6.25%), indicating stronger complex-reasoning and tool-use capabilities.

## Relation to Prior Work

EvoMAS extends the automated MAS design line initiated by ADAS, AFlow, GPTSwarm, and AgentSquare, which each employ a single fixed optimization strategy (MCTS, evolutionary search, RL, or modular search) over a template-constrained design space. It departs from these by introducing a repertoire of six biologically-inspired operators plus a custom strategy, a meta-level Cyber Creator that adaptively rewrites evolutionary rules and strategy weights via heuristics-in-the-loop reflection, and a curriculum schedule that evolves workflows from simple to complex tasks. The graph-based representation and evolutionary resource library (built on LightRAG) draw from prior work on agentic workflow formalization and retrieval-augmented generation, but the combination of dynamic strategy adaptation, role-level evolution, and meta-optimization is novel in this context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical bottleneck—manual MAS design is labor-intensive and rigid—by demonstrating that automated evolution can produce specialized, cost-efficient multi-agent workflows across heterogeneous domains (math, code, embodied, tool-use) with a total training cost of ~$2.22. The counterintuitive finding that the Cyber Creator's reflective overhead actually reduces total deployment cost by ~18% (by pruning invalid structures early) offers a concrete principle for resource-aware agent system design. Key limitations include dependence on a strong optimizer LLM (Claude-3.5-sonnet) for structural variation, potential bias from LLM-as-Judge selection, and untested scalability to substantially larger agent populations or open-ended domains beyond the seven benchmarks evaluated.
