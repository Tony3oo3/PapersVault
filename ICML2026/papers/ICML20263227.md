## Problem

Despite a recent revival of encoder-only model pre-training with modern architectures and techniques, there has been a conspicuous lack of large-scale multilingual encoder models. XLM-R (2019) remains the de facto state-of-the-art for massively multilingual NLU, and prior modern encoder work (ModernBERT, Ettin, EuroBERT) has not scaled beyond 15 languages. Additionally, including low-resource languages in a fixed data mixture forces a tradeoff between upsampled repetition and dilution, limiting performance on the 1700+ languages with very little training data.

## Input / Output

MMBERT accepts multilingual text sequences up to 8192 tokens (extended from 1024 during mid-training), tokenized with the Gemma 2 tokenizer (256K vocabulary) covering 1833 languages and 1895 language/script variants. It produces bidirectional sentence and token-level embeddings suitable for downstream NLU tasks (classification, QA, NLI) and retrieval (text-to-text, text-to-code, code-to-code). Unlike prior multilingual encoders capped at 512 tokens, MMBERT supports long-context inference at up to 8192 tokens while remaining 2–4× faster than XLM-R or mGTE at equivalent sequence lengths.

## Method

MMBERT uses the ModernBERT architecture (22 layers, sliding-window attention with window 128, global attention every 3 layers, RoPE) with the Gemma 2 tokenizer, trained via masked language modeling on 3T tokens across three phases: pre-training (2.3T tokens, 60 languages, 30% mask rate), mid-training/context extension (600B tokens, 110 languages, 15% mask rate, sequence length extended to 8192), and decay (100B tokens, 1833 languages, 5% mask rate). The key novelty is annealed language learning: low-resource languages are introduced only during the decay phase, with inverse-temperature sampling (τ = 0.7 → 0.5 → 0.3) progressively shifting the language distribution from high-resource-biased toward uniform, allowing rapid acquisition of new languages on minimal data. An inverse masking schedule (30% → 15% → 5%) progressively reduces the mask rate across phases, and three decay variants (English-focused, 110-language, 1833-language) are combined via TIES-merging to produce the final model. The small variant (140M params) is initialized from the base model via strided weight sampling.

## Results

MMBERT base achieves 86.3 average on English GLUE (vs. XLM-R's 83.3 and mGTE's 84.0), 72.8 average on XTREME multilingual NLU (vs. XLM-R's 70.4), and 54.1 average on MTEB v2 multilingual retrieval (vs. XLM-R's 52.4 and mGTE's 52.7). MMBERT small (140M params) outperforms all other small encoders, reaching 84.7 on GLUE and 50.7 on MTEB v2 multilingual. On low-resource Faroese QA (FoQA), MMBERT base scores 69.8 F1, outperforming Google's Gemini 2.5 Pro (63.8) and OpenAI's o3 (61.5) by 6 and 8.3 points respectively, using only 100B tokens of decay-phase training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MMBERT base outperforms XLM-R across all major benchmarks: GLUE 86.3 vs. 83.3, XTREME 72.8 vs. 70.4, MTEB v2 multilingual 54.1 vs. 52.4, and CoIR code retrieval 42.2 vs. 33.6. Against mGTE base, MMBERT wins on GLUE (86.3 vs. 84.0), XTREME (72.8 vs. 71.1), MTEB multilingual (54.1 vs. 52.7), and CoIR (42.2 vs. 38.9). MMBERT small (140M) significantly outperforms both mDistilBERT (77.5) and Multilingual MiniLM (78.3) on GLUE, reaching 84.7, and also surpasses XLM-R base on GLUE. On EuroBERT's in-distribution languages (Table 9), MMBERT base still wins (e.g., 74.5 vs. 66.8 on Arabic XNLI), though EuroBERT 210m edges out MMBERT on CoIR (45.3 vs. 42.2) due to its use of the non-public Stack v2 dataset. MMBERT base approaches but does not fully match ModernBERT base on English-only tasks (86.3 vs. 87.4 GLUE; 53.9 vs. 53.8 MTEB), despite training on a majority of non-English data. Finally, MMBERT small outperforms the similarly-sized Gemma 3 270M decoder on XNLI (73.6 vs. 69.0) and GLUE (84.7 vs. 82.9), reinforcing the encoder advantage for NLU tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

MMBERT directly builds on the ModernBERT architecture (Warner et al., 2024) and the Ettin training recipe (Weller et al., 2025), adapting them to a massively multilingual setting by swapping in the Gemma 2 tokenizer and multilingual data sources (FineWeb2, DCLM, Dolmino). It departs from prior multilingual encoders (XLM-R, mGTE, EuroBERT) in two key ways: (1) an inverse masking schedule that progressively lowers the mask rate across training phases, and (2) 'annealed language learning,' which introduces 1700+ low-resource languages only during the final decay phase rather than throughout training. The model merging via TIES-merging across three decay-phase variants is also a novel training procedure not seen in prior encoder work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MMBERT is the first massively multilingual encoder to meaningfully surpass XLM-R, which has held the SoTA position for over six years, and it does so with half the training tokens while being 2–4× faster at inference. The annealed language learning technique offers a practical recipe for multilingual pre-training that avoids the upsample-or-dilute tradeoff, and the finding that a 307M encoder outperforms frontier decoders (o3, Gemini 2.5 Pro) on low-resource QA underscores the continued value of encoder architectures for NLU. Limitations include NER/POS weaknesses from tokenization choices, dependence on upstream data quality for 1800+ languages, and the fact that the English-heavy data mix (up to 34%) may not be optimal for all multilingual settings. Open questions include whether the annealing schedule generalizes to even larger language counts or different decay durations, and whether the structured-prediction gap (POS, NER) can be closed without architectural changes.
