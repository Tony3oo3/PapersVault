## Problem

Existing model merging techniques based on linear parameter arithmetic or optimization fail on Mixture-of-Experts architectures because the non-linear softmax and discrete Top-k routing mechanisms are highly sensitive to small parameter perturbations, causing a failure mode termed routing breakdown where the merged router misdispatches tokens to incorrect experts. No prior method specifically addresses the calibration of router behavior during MoE merging, leaving a gap between effective dense-layer merging and the preservation of expert selection logic.

## Input / Output

The method takes task-specific source MoE models, an uncalibrated merged model produced by any existing merging strategy (Task Arithmetic, TIES, DARE, WUDI, etc.), and per-task calibration prompts (filtered correct source responses yield best results, though prompts alone or even general pre-training data still provide gains). It produces a calibrated merged MoE model with corrected router weights for each MoE layer, while expert and dense-layer parameters remain unchanged. The calibration is layer-by-layer and on-the-fly: input features for layer l are generated by forwarding the partially calibrated model through layers 0 to l−1, and the method converges with as few as 40% of available calibration samples.

## Method

HARC formulates router calibration as minimizing the expected KL divergence between source and merged routing distributions, then derives a quadratic approximation via a second-order Taylor expansion of the log-partition function, yielding a Hessian-weighted least-squares objective with a closed-form solution. The precision matrix for each task is the expectation of the Kronecker product between the softmax Hessian (diag(r) − rrᵀ) and the input covariance, which captures both per-expert importance (diagonal) and competitive coupling between experts (off-diagonal). To avoid the prohibitive O(K²d²) cost of materializing this matrix, a matrix-free conjugate gradient solver exploits Kronecker-vectorization identities to compute the required matrix-vector products in O(Kd) time per token. A diagonal regularization term (interpolating between the full and diagonal precision matrix via a damping factor α) stabilizes the system against rank-deficiency from Top-k sparsity, and the entire procedure is training-free, requiring only forward passes and iterative linear solves.

## Results

On OLMoE-1B-7B-0125 merging (math + code), HARC consistently improves all six merging baselines, with the largest gain under WUDI-Merging: overall score rises from 38.08 to 38.96 (+0.88), the best merged result in the table. Code generation is the most routing-sensitive domain; for example, Weight Averaging drops HumanEval+ from 33.50 (individual) to 20.27, while HARC partially recovers it to 20.73. Across all baselines, HARC yields gains ranging from +0.17 (Fisher Merging) to +0.88 (WUDI), and the best merged model (WUDI+HARC, 38.96) closes the gap to the individual-model upper bound (40.05) to within 1.09 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HARC is evaluated as a plug-in post-processing step on top of six merging baselines: Weight Averaging, TIES-Merging, DARE, WUDI-Merging, Fisher Merging, and RegMean. It wins on every baseline, with the largest absolute gain under WUDI-Merging (+0.88 overall, 38.08→38.96) and the smallest under Fisher Merging (+0.17, 35.03→35.20). The improvement is most pronounced on code generation, where routing breakdown is most severe: DARE+HARC lifts HumanEval+ from 29.57 to 31.36 and MBPP+ from 36.77 to 36.82, while WUDI+HARC raises MBPP+ from 36.72 to 38.19. On math benchmarks, gains are smaller but consistent (e.g., WUDI+HARC improves GSM8K from 69.45 to 70.06 and MATH500 from 17.43 to 17.96). Notably, HARC does not always improve every individual sub-metric (e.g., Fisher+HARC slightly drops GSM8K from 67.23 to 66.87), but the overall score improves in all six cases, confirming that the router calibration is complementary to the expert-merging strategy rather than a replacement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the model merging literature from dense architectures to Mixture-of-Experts, identifying that the Linear Mode Connectivity assumption underlying methods like Task Arithmetic, TIES-Merging, and WUDI breaks down at the router due to softmax and Top-k non-linearities. Methodologically, HARC builds on Fisher Merging's precision-matrix weighting and RegMean's diagonal interpolation strategy, but departs by retaining the full off-diagonal Hessian structure (capturing softmax-induced expert competition) and applying it specifically to the routing sub-module rather than all parameters. The approach is positioned as a model-agnostic post-processing calibration step that composes with any existing merging baseline, rather than a standalone merging method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of routing breakdown as a distinct, quantifiable failure mode (50% per-layer expert mismatch compounding to 99.98% divergence across depth) gives MoE practitioners a concrete diagnostic and a lightweight fix that requires no retraining. The finding that post-merge SFT actually increases routing KL divergence—rather than correcting the router—challenges the assumption that fine-tuning naturally repairs structural damage from merging, and positions HARC as a complementary structural repair step. Open questions include whether the quadratic approximation holds under more aggressive merging (larger task vectors, more than 3 models), whether the approach generalizes to learned or hierarchical routing beyond Top-k, and whether the modest absolute gains (0.5–0.9 points) scale proportionally in higher-stakes or multi-domain settings.
