## Problem

The weak dual formulation of entropic optimal transport (EOT) with quadratic cost contains an intractable log-partition term that blocks efficient optimization. Existing solvers resolve this by either requiring costly MCMC sampling at every training iteration (EgNOT) or by restricting the transport plan to a narrow parametric family such as Gaussian mixtures (LightSB). No prior method achieves both simulation-free training and unrestricted parameterization of the conditional transport plan.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unpaired i.i.d. samples from a source distribution p_0 and a target distribution p_1, which in practice are 512-dimensional ALAE latent codes extracted from 1024×1024 FFHQ face images, or PCA-projected single-cell data (50–1000 dimensions). At inference, a single source sample x_0 is encoded, and Langevin dynamics (10–1000 steps) sample from the learned conditional π*(x_1|x_0); the sampled latent is then decoded back to pixel space to yield the translated image. The auxiliary normalizer network ξ_ψ is discarded after training—only the potential f_θ is retained for generation.

## Method

The key insight is an exact variational reformulation of the intractable log-partition log Z(f, x_0) as a tractable minimization over an auxiliary log-normalizer ξ(x_0), derived from the pointwise inequality log E[exp(A)] ≤ ξ + E[exp(A − ξ)] − 1. Substituting this bound into the weak dual EOT objective yields a fully differentiable loss L(f, ξ) that requires only i.i.d. Gaussian noise samples z ~ N(0, I) to estimate expectations—no sampling from the model distribution itself. Both the potential f_θ and the auxiliary normalizer ξ_ψ are parameterized by 4-layer MLPs (256 hidden units, SiLU activations) and trained jointly via AdamW in a single optimization loop, with no alternating or adversarial steps. At inference, Langevin dynamics target the unnormalized density exp((f_θ(x_1) − ‖x_1 − x_0‖²/2)/ε) to generate transport samples. The paper further provides finite-sample generalization bounds (estimation error decaying as O(N^{-1/2})) and proves vanishing approximation error under universal function approximation, establishing that the learned plan converges to the true EOT plan in KL divergence.

## Results

On the ALAE latent-space image translation benchmark (FFHQ, ε=1.0), VarEOT achieves FID of 9.71 (M→F), 10.28 (F→M), 16.83 (A→C), and 16.70 (C→A) with 1000 Langevin steps, substantially outperforming LightSB (22.63, 20.97, 24.43, 24.87) and EgNOT (17.58, 26.21, 28.45, 23.68). On the MSCI single-cell dataset (DIM=1000), VarEOT matches the Langevin-based EgNOT solver in energy distance (1.47 vs. 1.46) while training 5–15× faster on less powerful hardware (GTX 1080 vs. V100). Wall-clock training for the M→F task takes 131 s for VarEOT (K=128) versus 1692 s for EgNOT, with VarEOT achieving a lower FID (11.43 vs. 17.58).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LightSB (Korotin et al., 2024), VarEOT achieves substantially lower FID across all four translation tasks at ε=1.0 (e.g., 9.71 vs. 22.63 on M→F) and lower LPIPS, while also training faster (131 s vs. 191 s on M→F); the gap widens at smaller ε where LightSB's Gaussian-mixture restriction becomes more limiting. Against EgNOT (Mokrov et al., 2024), VarEOT wins on FID at ε=1.0 (9.71 vs. 17.58 on M→F) and at ε=0.5 (9.77 vs. 14.07), while training ~13× faster (131 s vs. 1692 s) due to its simulation-free objective; at ε=0.1, EgNOT with NFE=1000 slightly edges VarEOT on FID (7.63 vs. 12.74) but VarEOT still matches it on LPIPS. On the MSCI single-cell benchmark, VarEOT is competitive with the Langevin-based EgNOT solver (energy distance 1.47 vs. 1.46 at DIM=1000) and the Minimax solver of Gushchin et al. (2023) (2.31 vs. 2.24 at DIM=100), while being 5–15× faster than both. The discrete Sinkhorn baseline (Cuturi, 2013) and the IPF solver (Vargas et al., 2021) are only compared on MSCI; VarEOT matches or exceeds them in energy distance at all PCA dimensions while operating in the continuous OT setting. Overall, VarEOT consistently wins or ties on quality while offering the only combination of simulation-free training and unrestricted (non-Gaussian-mixture) parameterization among the compared solvers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the weak dual formulation of entropic optimal transport introduced by EgNOT (Mokrov et al., 2024) and the simulation-free but parametrically restricted LightSB (Korotin et al., 2024). The key conceptual departure is replacing the intractable log-partition term with an exact variational minimization over an auxiliary lognormalizer ξ, a technique borrowed from variational inference (the log u ≤ u − 1 bound) but applied here to yield a tight, simulation-free dual objective without restricting the transport plan to Gaussian mixtures. The method also positions itself against Schrödinger bridge solvers and Sinkhorn-based dual EOT, arguing that the weak dual framework with a single potential is more natural for conditional transport recovery.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VarEOT removes the primary practical barrier to using weak dual EOT solvers—MCMC sampling during training—while retaining full expressiveness, making entropic transport more accessible for domain translation and single-cell biology applications. The finite-sample generalization bounds and vanishing approximation error under universal function approximation provide theoretical grounding that prior simulation-free EOT methods lacked. Open questions include extending the framework beyond quadratic cost, addressing the numerical instability of the exponential terms at small ε, and whether the auxiliary network ξ can be eliminated or simplified further. Langevin sampling at inference remains a bottleneck for high-quality generation, limiting the method's practical speed advantage in latency-sensitive settings.
