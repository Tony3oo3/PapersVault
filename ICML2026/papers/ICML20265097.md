## Problem

Vision-Text Compression (VTC) renders long text into dense images for VLMs, achieving high OCR fidelity but a severe reasoning gap: models that reason robustly on native text fail on visually compressed equivalents, particularly in long-range retrieval and multi-step deduction. Standard post-training (SFT, RLVR) applied directly to visual prompts yields only marginal gains compared to their textual counterparts—a phenomenon the authors term post-training transfer failure. The paper asks how to effectively transfer reasoning capabilities from the well-trained text channel to the weaker visual channel without altering the fixed VTC rendering pipeline.

## Input / Output

For each reasoning instance, the method constructs two semantically equivalent prompts: a standard text token sequence and its text-as-image rendering (A4 page at 72 DPI, 9pt Verdana, 10pt line height, black on white, yielding 2.5–4× compression with <1% character error rate). The VLM (Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct) consumes the visual prompt through its vision encoder as a dense pixel grid and the text prompt through the standard token pathway, both feeding the same autoregressive LLM backbone. The model outputs a chain-of-thought reasoning trace followed by a final answer, evaluated by exact match.

## Method

CoRe (Coordinated Reasoning) introduces a step-wise KL divergence alignment loss that matches the visual branch's next-token probability distribution to the text branch's distribution at every position along a reasoning trace, with stop-gradient on the text branch to treat it as a dynamic reference anchor. In SFT, alignment targets are ground-truth CoT traces; in RL (GRPO), reward-verified rollouts sampled from the text policy serve as self-generated targets, enabling self-imitation without additional human annotation. The total loss combines the standard task objective (next-token prediction or GRPO) with a λ-weighted CoRe term, and the text branch co-evolves during training so its improving policy naturally raises the alignment ceiling. Theoretically, Pinsker's inequality shows that minimizing the cross-view KL directly tightens an upper bound on visual reasoning error, decomposing it into text-branch error plus a distributional gap term. This process-level supervision is richer than standard SFT because it transfers the entire decision-making strategy—including the model's uncertainty and preference structure over plausible continuations—rather than merely enforcing probability mass on the correct token.

## Results

On mathematical reasoning (Table 1), CoRe's full pipeline (CoRe-RL → Visual SFT → Visual GRPO) achieves a visual-prompt average of 42.97% across MATH-500, GSM8K, AMC, AIME 2024, and AIME 2025, recovering ~70% of the gap to the text upper bound (63.80%), compared to 29.44% for standard Visual GRPO directly on the base model. On long-context memory (Table 2), CoRe-RL jumps HotpotQA from 7.0% (zero-shot) to 23.0% and 2WikiMultihopQA from 10.0% to 35.0%, versus only 9.0% and 13.0% for standard Visual GRPO. On tabular retrieval (Table 3), CoRe-RL boosts InfoTabs from 31.1% to 70.2% and TabFact from 24.8% to 61.3%, while standard Visual GRPO reaches 61.3% and 52.0% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares CoRe against three standard visual post-training baselines: (1) Visual SFT + Visual GRPO (24.00% avg), (2) Visual GRPO directly on the base model (29.44% avg), and (3) the text-prompt upper bound via Text GRPO (63.54% avg). CoRe's full pipeline (42.97% visual avg) outperforms the strongest visual baseline (Visual GRPO on Base) by +13.5 points on the math average, and by +14.9 points on GSM8K (82.19% vs. 67.31%) and +8.4 points on MATH-500 (62.80% vs. 41.80%). On long-context tasks, CoRe-RL dominates standard Visual GRPO by large margins (e.g., +14 pts on HotpotQA, +22 pts on 2WikiMultihopQA), whereas standard GRPO yields only +2 pts over zero-shot. On tabular QA, CoRe-RL beats Visual GRPO on all five benchmarks (e.g., +8.9 pts on InfoTabs, +9.3 pts on TabFact), and notably surpasses even the text GRPO baseline on InfoTabs (70.18% vs. 45.92%) and TabFact (61.31% vs. 31.53%), suggesting the process-level alignment signal is more effective than sparse outcome rewards for structured data. The remaining gap to the text upper bound (~21 pts on math avg) is attributed to hierarchical-table localization errors and long-range dependency tracking failures in the visual modality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the VTC rendering protocol from Glyph (Cheng et al., 2025) and the GRPO reinforcement learning framework (Shao et al., 2024), but departs from both by identifying a specific failure mode—post-training transfer failure—where standard visual SFT/RL yields only marginal gains despite identical semantic content. Methodologically, CoRe is a cross-modal step-wise KL alignment loss closely related to knowledge distillation (Hinton et al., 2015), but applied in a novel setting: the 'teacher' is not a separate model but the same checkpoint's text-conditioned policy, creating a self-imitation loop. The paper positions itself distinctly from prior cross-modal consistency work (Wu et al., 2025; Vision-SR1) by targeting pure-visual VTC reasoning rather than mixed-input VQA or reward design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that the VTC reasoning gap is primarily an alignment problem rather than a capacity problem reframes how practitioners should approach post-training for visually compressed inputs—a simple plug-in KL term recovers up to 70% of the performance gap without architectural changes. However, the results are confined to a high-fidelity compression regime (CER < 1%), a single model family (Qwen3-VL-8B with cross-validation on Qwen2.5-VL-7B), and text-heavy tasks; the extreme-compression frontier, dynamic rendering strategies, and non-text visual semantics remain unexplored. The dependence on a stronger text anchor also means CoRe cannot create reasoning capabilities absent in the textual policy, capping its utility for tasks where the model is weak in both modalities.
