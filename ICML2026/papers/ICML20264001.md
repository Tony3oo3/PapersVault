## Problem

The Euler-Maruyama discretization used in BSDE-based PDE solvers introduces a systematic bias in the training loss that degrades optimization accuracy. The known fix—switching to a Stratonovich Heun scheme—eliminates the bias but reintroduces explicit Hessian (second-order derivative) evaluations, making it 40× slower than the baseline EM-BSDE and offering no clear advantage over FS-PINNs. The paper asks whether one can remove the discretization bias while preserving the second-order-free, low-cost structure of the original EM update.

## Input / Output

The method consumes PDE coefficients (drift μ, diffusion σ, nonlinear source φ), a terminal condition g(x), and a prescribed initial state x₀, and produces a neural-network approximation u_θ(t,x) whose primary target is the initial-time value u(0, x₀). Rather than sampling a dense (t,x) grid as in collocation PINNs, the method rolls out forward SDE trajectories from x₀ and enforces self-consistency along those paths, so the network is only queried at points visited by the stochastic dynamics. All derivatives are computed via reverse-mode automatic differentiation, and the method requires only first-order spatial gradients (no Hessian) at each training step.

## Method

The core contribution is the Un-EM-BSDE loss, which replaces the standard squared one-step EM error with the product of two independently sampled shot-averaged one-step errors: ℓ_UEM = E[Shot_{M₁}[err_EM] × Shot_{M₂}[err_EM]]. Because the two factors use disjoint Brownian increments, the expectation factorizes as E[err]·E[err], and each factor's expectation equals the PDE residual plus O(Δt^{1/2}), so the product yields (PDE residual)² + O(Δt^{1/2})—the same leading term as the continuous-time objective but without the ½Tr[(σᵀ∇²u σ)²] bias term that plagues the squared EM error. This sample-splitting construction is second-order-free (only ∇u is needed, never ∇²u), costs roughly 1.8× the baseline EM-BSDE training time, and provably has estimator variance no larger than standard EM-BSDE under mild conditions (M₁=1, M₂=2 suffices). The paper further shows the same debiasing wrapper can be plugged into other biased one-step losses (e.g., Shotgun), reducing their error by ~2.7× at moderate overhead.

## Results

Across five benchmark PDEs (HJB, BSB, AC, BZ, PIDE) under both soft and hard terminal constraints, Un-EM-BSDE (M₁=M₂=5) is consistently in the top two methods by relative L₂ error (RL2). Under hard constraints, it achieves RL2 of 0.0034×10⁻² on AC, 0.0120×10⁻² on BSB, and 0.2279×10⁻² on BZ, matching or exceeding the unbiased Heun-BSDE and FS-PINNs baselines. Crucially, it does so with a training time of only 1.79× the EM-BSDE baseline, compared to 42.91× for Heun-BSDE and 32.07× for FS-PINNs, because it requires no Hessian evaluations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against EM-BSDE (Raissi, 2024), Un-EM-BSDE consistently improves accuracy by removing discretization bias: e.g., on BSB hard-constraint, RL2 drops from 0.3456 to 0.0120 (×29 reduction), and on AC hard-constraint from 0.0400 to 0.0034 (×12 reduction). Against Shotgun (Xu & Zhang, 2025), the improvement is dramatic where Shotgun is unstable (BSB soft: 39.99 vs 0.0814; BZ soft: 86.53 vs 5.18), though Shotgun occasionally wins on hard-constraint HJB (0.2036 vs 0.1444). Against Heun-BSDE (Park & Tu, 2025), Un-EM-BSDE achieves comparable or better accuracy (e.g., AC hard: 0.0034 vs 0.0912; BZ soft: 5.18 vs 6.17) while being ~24× faster in training time (1.79× vs 42.91× EM-BSDE), since Heun-BSDE requires two Hessian evaluations per step. Against FS-PINNs (Park & Tu, 2025), FS-PINNs often achieves slightly lower RL2 (e.g., BSB hard: 0.0048 vs 0.0120; PIDE hard: 0.0137 vs 0.0226), but at 32.07× training cost versus 1.79× for Un-EM-BSDE, and FS-PINNs still requires one Hessian per residual evaluation. The key practical advantage is that Un-EM-BSDE is the only method that is simultaneously unbiased, second-order-free (no Hessian), and near-EM-BSDE speed, making it the most scalable option for high-dimensional PDEs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three recent threads in high-dimensional PDE solving: the EM-BSDE method (Raissi, 2024), which is computationally efficient but carries a discretization-induced bias in its self-consistency loss; the Heun-BSDE method (Park & Tu, 2025), which eliminates that bias via Stratonovich-Heun integration but requires expensive Hessian evaluations; and the Shotgun method (Xu & Zhang, 2025), which reduces bias by a 1/M factor through multi-shot averaging but does not remove it entirely. The proposed Un-EM-BSDE loss departs from all three by applying a sample-splitting principle—forming the loss as a product of two independently drawn one-step errors—to achieve unbiasedness while retaining the second-order-free structure of the original EM update. The authors also demonstrate that the same debiasing wrapper can be plugged into other biased one-step objectives, as shown with the Shotgun loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that Un-EM-BSDE achieves accuracy comparable to Heun-BSDE and FS-PINNs at roughly 1.79× the training cost of EM-BSDE, versus 42.91× for Heun-BSDE, making unbiased BSDE training viable in high dimensions where Hessian computations become prohibitive. The general debiasing wrapper (demonstrated on Shotgun) suggests a reusable recipe for correcting discretization bias in other trajectory-based solvers. However, the theoretical guarantees rely on boundedness and regularity assumptions that do not fully cover coupled FBSDEs or PIDEs, and the method does not consistently outperform FS-PINNs, which remains the strongest unbiased baseline in several settings. Extending the analysis to adaptive time-stepping and less regular problem classes is identified as important future work.
