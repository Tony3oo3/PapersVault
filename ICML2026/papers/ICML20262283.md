## Problem

Large language models remain unable to natively reason over time-series data, a critical gap for medical applications where diagnosis and monitoring depend on temporal patterns. Prior approaches either tokenize time-series as text (yielding poor performance), attach classification heads (losing free-form generation), or use soft prompting (which scales poorly in memory with sequence length and number of series). No existing method treats time-series as a first-class modality while preserving natural-language reasoning and scaling to long or multivariate inputs.

## Input / Output

The model accepts raw multivariate time-series (e.g., 3-axis accelerometer at 50 Hz, single-channel EEG at 100 Hz, or 12-lead ECG at 100 Hz) alongside natural-language prompts. Each series is normalized to [-1, 1], with its original mean, standard deviation, and sampling rate appended as text to preserve scale context; multiple series are interleaved with per-sensor textual descriptions. Output is a structured autoregressive generation: a free-form chain-of-thought rationale followed by a final answer in the template '<reasoning> Answer: <final answer>', enabling both interpretability and extractable predictions.

## Method

OpenTSLM introduces two architectures that extend pretrained LLMs (Llama-3.2, Gemma-3) with time-series as a native modality. OpenTSLM-SoftPrompt encodes patches via a PatchTST-style 1D-convolution encoder, projects them into the LLM hidden space through a TransformerEncoder + MLP, and interleaves the resulting tokens with text tokens in a single sequence, fine-tuned via LoRA. OpenTSLM-Flamingo instead compresses patches into a fixed-size latent via a Perceiver Resampler and injects it through gated cross-attention layers inserted every N transformer blocks, triggered by special <TS> tokens, so the LLM backbone remains frozen. Training follows a two-stage curriculum: stage 1 warms up the encoder on synthetic TSQA and M4-captioning data, then stage 2 fine-tunes on three new CoT datasets (HAR-CoT, Sleep-CoT, ECG-QA-CoT) using an autoregressive language-modeling loss. The key architectural insight is that cross-attention keeps VRAM near-constant regardless of sequence length or number of series, whereas soft-prompting memory grows with both, making the Flamingo variant practical for long or multivariate inputs such as 12-lead ECGs. Pretrained time-series foundation models (e.g., Chronos-2) can be plugged in as frozen encoders with only a projection layer trained, yielding substantial accuracy gains.

## Results

OpenTSLM-SoftPrompt (Llama3.2-1B) achieves the best overall performance, reaching 97.50 F1 on TSQA, 65.44 F1 on HAR-CoT, and 69.88 F1 on Sleep-CoT. OpenTSLM-Flamingo (Llama3.2-3B) leads on ECG-QA-CoT with 40.25 F1, substantially outperforming GPT-4o with plots (24.95 F1) and all tokenized baselines (which hit OOM on 12-lead ECG). Across all four datasets, OpenTSLM models outperform GPT-4o (text-only: 45.32/2.95/15.47/18.19 F1) and the strongest tokenized-finetuned baseline (84.54/60.44/9.05/OOM F1). With pretrained Chronos-2 encoder, OpenTSLM-Flamingo further improves, e.g., TSQA F1 rises from 77.86 to 99.69 for Gemma3-270M.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against tokenized time-series baselines (Gruver et al., 2023), OpenTSLM dominates across all datasets: zero-shot tokenized Llama3.2-3B scores only 16.24 F1 on TSQA and 0.00 on HAR/ECG, while even finetuned tokenized Llama3.2-3B reaches at most 84.54 F1 on TSQA and 60.44 on HAR-CoT, trailing OpenTSLM-SoftPrompt by 13 and 5 F1 points respectively; on ECG-QA-CoT, tokenized finetuning is infeasible (OOM at >100 GB VRAM for 80k tokens). Against ITFormer (Wang et al., 2025b), OpenTSLM-SoftPrompt Llama3.2-1B wins on HAR-CoT (65.44 vs. 55.41 F1) and Sleep-CoT (69.88 vs. 42.38 F1), while OpenTSLM-Flamingo Llama3.2-3B edges out ITFormer on ECG-QA-CoT (40.25 vs. 37.52 F1). InstructTime (Cheng et al., 2025) is outperformed on all three CoT datasets: HAR-CoT 65.44 vs. 61.73, Sleep-CoT 69.88 vs. 42.57, and ECG-QA-CoT 40.25 vs. 20.71 F1. ChatTS (Xie et al., 2025) trails OpenTSLM by 8–28 F1 points across HAR-CoT (65.44 vs. 57.63), Sleep-CoT (69.88 vs. 41.31), and ECG-QA-CoT (40.25 vs. 8.91). ChatTime (Wang et al., 2025a) is the weakest comparator, achieving 41.11 F1 on HAR-CoT, 19.09 on Sleep-CoT, and running out of memory on ECG-QA-CoT.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the Flamingo/OpenFlamingo cross-attention architecture (originally for vision-language) to treat time-series as a native modality in LLMs, alongside a soft-prompting variant that concatenates learned time-series tokens with text tokens. It builds on PatchTST for the time-series encoder, LoRA for efficient LLM fine-tuning, and pretrained TSFMs like Chronos-2 as frozen encoders. The key departure from prior work (ITFormer, ChatTS, InstructTime, MedualTime, Time2Lang) is twofold: (1) time-series are integrated as a distinct modality rather than tokenized text or fed through a classification head, preserving free-form text generation and CoT reasoning; and (2) the paper systematically compares soft prompting vs. cross-attention, showing the latter scales far better in memory for long or multivariate series.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: open-source 270M–3B parameter models outperform GPT-4o (~200B) on time-series reasoning tasks at a fraction of the compute, enabling on-device deployment for digital health applications. The memory-scaling finding (cross-attention stays near-constant while soft prompting grows with sequence length and number of series) provides clear architectural guidance for practitioners. Open questions include whether the Perceiver Resampler's fixed-size latent bottleneck limits performance on very long sequences, whether CoT rationales generated by GPT-4o from plots introduce circularity (since GPT-4o performs poorly on those same plots), and how well these models generalize out-of-distribution to unseen sensor types or clinical domains.
