## Problem

Standard diffusion models are trained with local denoising objectives at individual time steps that do not enforce consistency between predictions along the denoising trajectory. This cross-time mismatch accumulates over the sampling path and becomes particularly detrimental for few-step samplers, where local errors have limited opportunity to average out. The paper asks whether a principled training-time regularizer can enforce multi-step temporal consistency without redesigning the generator architecture.

## Input / Output

The method operates as a drop-in training regularizer on top of existing diffusion pipelines (EDM or consistency training). At each training step, it consumes a clean data sample x_0, a noisy sample x_t at a sampled noise level, and a second noisy sample x_{t'} at a paired noise level (offset by span k and stride Δ), and produces a scalar TD loss that penalizes the mismatch between the true posterior-mean drift and the model's predicted drift over that interval. The final model still takes random noise (optionally conditioned on a class label) and outputs a generated image at 32×32 or 64×64 resolution, sampled via a probability-flow ODE with the Heun integrator.

## Method

The paper reformulates the diffusion denoising process as a finite-horizon Markov Reward Process traversed backward in time, where the reward at each transition is the difference of true posterior means (the diffusion drift) and the value function equals the displacement of the posterior mean from the clean data. Applying temporal difference learning yields a TD error that decomposes into the true one-step diffusion drift minus the model's predicted one-step drift, so minimizing the squared TD loss aligns the model's internal temporal dynamics with the known forward corruption process. A unified two-time posterior mean (linear in x_0 and x_t with family-specific coefficients A_{t,τ} and κ_{t,τ}) allows the same TD objective to apply to both discrete-time (DDPM/DDIM) and continuous-time (VP/VE/EDM/CM) formulations. To stabilize optimization across heterogeneous time pairs, the authors derive a principled pairwise reweighting w_TD(t,t') by bounding the TD error in terms of the normalized raw-network errors, yielding a weight inversely proportional to the sum of squared rescaling factors. The final training objective combines the weighted TD loss with the base EDM or CT loss via a mixing coefficient λ, and an EMA target network provides the bootstrap estimate for the next-state value.

## Results

On the primary TD+EDM comparison (Table 2), the method matches or beats the EDM baseline in the 12–18 step regime across all three datasets: on FFHQ (64×64), FID-50k improves from 4.499→4.400 (12 steps), 3.695→3.564 (15 steps), and 3.370→3.246 (18 steps). For one-step consistency training (Table 3), TD+CT achieves a substantial FID-50k reduction on FFHQ from 19.45 to 15.93 (−3.52) and a smaller gain on AFHQv2 from 12.97 to 12.87. The advantage is most pronounced at low NFE, consistent with the paper's motivation of reducing cross-time inconsistency accumulation in few-step samplers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the EDM baseline (Karras et al., 2022), TD+EDM wins on 10 of 12 dataset-step combinations, with the largest margins on FFHQ (up to −0.124 FID at 18 steps) and CIFAR-10 (−0.081 at 15 steps); it loses narrowly on CIFAR-10 at 9 steps (+0.096) and AFHQv2 at 12 steps (+0.048). Against the CT baseline (Song et al., 2023), TD+CT wins on both datasets at one-step sampling: FFHQ improves by 3.52 FID (19.45→15.93) and AFHQv2 by 0.10 FID (12.97→12.87). The FFHQ one-step gain is particularly notable as it represents an ~18% relative FID reduction. Across multi-step evaluation (1–8 steps, Figure 3), TD+CT maintains a predominantly negative ΔFID relative to CT throughout training, indicating the consistency benefit generalizes beyond the single-step setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes temporal difference (TD) learning from reinforcement learning as a training regularizer for diffusion models, building directly on the MRP formulation for supervised learning from Pan et al. (2025) and the unified two-time posterior mean structure shared by DDPM, DDIM, VP/VE-SDE, EDM, and consistency models. It departs from prior RL-for-diffusion work (DDPO, DPOK, Adjoint Matching) that applies policy-gradient or optimal-control methods to optimize external rewards, instead performing policy evaluation over the denoising trajectory itself to enforce cross-time consistency of posterior-mean drifts. Unlike consistency models that require reconstructions to agree across time, or shortcut/MeanFlow models that redesign the generator for few-step inference, the TD objective is a drop-in regularizer that preserves the base EDM or CT training pipeline while adding a drift-matching penalty.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The TD objective addresses a practical bottleneck—degraded sample quality under few-step sampling—without requiring architectural changes or new samplers, making it immediately applicable to existing EDM and CT training pipelines. The principled pairwise weighting scheme (Eq. 30/34) is a non-trivial design choice whose necessity is confirmed by ablation, and the unified formulation across discrete and continuous time provides a clean theoretical bridge. However, validation is limited to 32×32 and 64×64 resolutions with a probability-flow ODE solver, and the ~1.5× compute overhead for TD+EDM (mitigated in TD+CT via the existing EMA teacher) constrains immediate adoption at scale. Open questions include whether the gains persist at higher resolutions, with SDE samplers, or when combined with distillation and solver-aware training.
