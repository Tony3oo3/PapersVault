## Problem

Prior work on fair transit stop placement was restricted to the line metric, failing to capture the complexity of real-world transportation networks. This paper asks whether fair clustering algorithms can guarantee proportionally representative stop placements in general metric spaces, and whether exploiting the agent-level structure (each agent has a source-destination pair rather than a single point) can yield stronger fairness guarantees than any clustering-based approach.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260149/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a TrSP instance consisting of n agents each defined by a source-destination pair (a_i, b_i), a set of m candidate stop locations, two distance metrics (walking metric d and transit metric d' both satisfying the triangle inequality), and a budget k. The output is a feasible subset of at most k candidate stops. Unlike standard clustering where each agent is a single point, here each agent contributes two endpoints to the induced clustering instance, and the agent cost function combines walking distances to/from stops with a transit cost between the two stops used, allowing the agent to also opt to walk directly if cheaper.

## Method

The paper establishes a metatheorem reducing TrSP to fair centroid clustering: any ρ-Proportional Fairness clustering solution yields a (2,ρ)-core TrSP solution, giving GC-TrSP a tight (2, 1+√2)-core guarantee. To surpass the 3-JR barrier inherent to all clustering algorithms, the Expanding Cost Algorithm (ECA) grows cost balls centered on pairs of stops rather than single stops, directly optimizing agent-level costs and achieving a tight (1+√2)≈2.414-JR approximation that holds under arbitrary transit cost functions. Since ECA provides no bounded core guarantee while GC-TrSP does, the paper introduces a λ-Hybrid algorithm that interpolates between the two via a tunable parameter, enabling a continuous trade-off between JR and core approximation quality. The paper also proves that JR is not guaranteed to exist in general metric spaces (lower bound of (1+√3)/2≈1.366) and that computing a minimum-total-cost solution is NP-hard.

## Results

Theoretically, ECA achieves a tight (1+√2) ≈ 2.414-JR guarantee under arbitrary transit cost functions, improving on GC-TrSP's tight (2+√5) ≈ 4.236-JR. GC-TrSP achieves a tight (2, 1+√2)-core guarantee, while ECA provably fails any (γ,ρ)-core for γ,ρ ≥ 1. The paper establishes a lower bound of (1+√3)/2 ≈ 1.366 on JR approximability for any algorithm, and shows no clustering algorithm can guarantee better than 3-JR. Empirically on the Helena Capital Transit dataset (40 agents), GC-TrSP attains average core approximation ratios of 1.007–1.155 across k ∈ {5,10,15,20}, consistently outperforming ECA (1.072–1.454) on core, while both algorithms achieve JR ratios close to 1 in practice.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between its two main algorithms: GC-TrSP (a clustering-based approach) and ECA (a novel cost-based approach). ECA decisively outperforms GC-TrSP on JR approximation (2.414 vs. 4.236), and this advantage is robust to arbitrary transit cost functions, whereas GC-TrSP's JR guarantee degrades unboundedly under non-zero transit costs (Remark 3.7). Conversely, GC-TrSP provides a tight (2, 1+√2)-core guarantee while ECA provably fails any bounded core approximation (Proposition 4.2), making the two algorithms complementary. The λ-Hybrid algorithm interpolates between them via a tunable parameter λ, trading off JR for core guarantees. Against the theoretical lower bounds, ECA's 2.414-JR is the best achievable by any algorithm (the general lower bound is 1.366), and it surpasses the 3-JR barrier that no clustering algorithm can cross (Proposition 3.6). Empirically, GC-TrSP consistently achieves lower core approximation ratios than ECA across all tested k values and transit cost scalings on the Helena dataset, though ECA's ratios remain close to 1 in most instances.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the fair transit stop placement (TrSP) problem from the line metric (Bullinger et al., 2025) to general metric spaces, and draws a structural correspondence between TrSP and fair centroid clustering (Chen et al., 2019). The key conceptual departure is showing that the natural reduction to clustering has a fundamental ceiling: no clustering algorithm can achieve better than 3-JR, while the paper's novel Expanding Cost Algorithm (ECA), which operates on stop pairs and agent-level costs rather than endpoint distances, breaks this barrier to achieve a tight 2.414-JR under arbitrary transit cost functions. The λ-Hybrid algorithm then interpolates between the clustering-based GC-TrSP (strong core guarantee, weaker JR) and ECA (strong JR, no core guarantee), offering a tunable trade-off.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that JR is not guaranteed to exist in general metric spaces (lower bound ≈1.366), a significant negative result that contrasts with the line setting where JR always exists. The NP-hardness of cost minimization in general metric spaces (vs. polynomial time on the line) further underscores the increased complexity. A key open question is whether constant-factor core approximations can be achieved under broader classes of transit cost metrics beyond null transit costs, since the current core guarantees rely on the clustering reduction. The empirical evaluation on Helena Capital Transit data suggests practical approximation ratios are close to 1, but the worst-case guarantees remain the primary contribution.
