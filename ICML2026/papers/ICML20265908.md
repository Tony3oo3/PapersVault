## Problem

The Bellman equation, the central pillar of RL, becomes only approximately valid under non-Markovian dynamics (long-range dependencies, partial observability, memory effects), yet existing work offers limited theoretical treatment of what dynamics are mathematically capturable by the Bellman framework and how to obtain optimal Markov approximations. This paper asks whether the structural mismatch between TD errors and the Bellman framework can be characterized in a way that yields both a principled diagnostic of non-Markovian inconsistency and a new algorithmic correction.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RL transitions (s, a, r, s') sampled from a replay buffer, processed by two neural networks sharing the same backbone: a value/Q-network V_θ and a scalar potential network U_φ. Beyond the standard value function and policy outputs, HFPS produces a topological residual δ_V^res whose norm quantifies Bellman non-integrability and can serve as an online mismatch diagnostic for gating updates or detecting distribution shift. The potential network's output is not a value estimate but a state-level potential whose discounted difference u(s') − γu(s) approximates the integrable component of the TD signal.

## Method

The paper casts TD errors as 1-cochains in a Hilbert space C¹ of transition functions, with a discounted de Rham differential d mapping state potentials to transition signals via (du)(s,a,s') = u(s') − γu(s). Under this formulation, Markov dynamics correspond to topological integrability (the TD error lies in the exact subspace im(d)), and any TD error admits an orthogonal Hodge-type decomposition into an integrable component du*_V and a topological residual δ_V^res, where the optimal potential u*_V is characterized by the Poisson equation Δ₀u*_V = d*δ_V. The HFPS algorithm instantiates this with two networks: U_φ is trained via an inner loop to minimize the empirical projection loss ‖δ_θ − dU_φ‖², and V_θ is then updated using only the projected integrable component rather than the raw TD error. In practice, stability is maintained through TD-error clipping, residual-adaptive weighting that blends integrable and raw TD based on projection quality, and norm-preserving rescaling of the effective update direction. Theoretical guarantees include Lipschitz stability of the decomposition under perturbations, sensitivity bounds on the discount factor, and a bias bound showing the HFPS update deviates from standard TD by at most B‖δ_V^res‖_{C¹}.

## Results

On the Nonmarkov regime (hidden actuator memory), HFPS achieves the best AUC@T across all five control tasks: 107.6 on LunarLander, −82.7 on Acrobot, −556.9 on Pendulum, −839.7 on PointMass, and 184.7 on BipedalWalker (×10⁶ scale). The largest margins over the strongest baseline appear on PointMass (HFPS −839.7 vs. DRQN −1063.0, a ~21% improvement) and BipedalWalker (HFPS 184.7 vs. PPO 78.5, more than 2×). On Final@T, HFPS is competitive, winning on Pendulum (−106.2 vs. DRQN −235.8) and PointMass (−207.1 vs. ADRQN −256.4), while DRQN edges it on LunarLander (169.1 vs. 145.6) and ADRQN on BipedalWalker (151.7 vs. 146.0). Wall-clock overhead is modest at ~1.27× compared to DQN on CartPole.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HFPS outperforms all eight baselines on AUC@T (sample efficiency) across every Nonmarkov task. The most dramatic wins are on PointMass, where HFPS (−839.7) beats the best baseline DRQN (−1063.0) by ~21%, and on BipedalWalker, where HFPS (184.7) more than doubles PPO (78.5) and DRQN (23.6). Among value-based baselines, DQN, Double DQN, and Dueling DQN all suffer severe degradation under the hidden-memory regime (e.g., DQN drops to −2672.2 on PointMass), while HFPS remains stable. Recurrent baselines (DRQN, ADRQN, R2D2) are the strongest competitors, particularly on LunarLander where DRQN achieves a higher Final@T (169.1 vs. 145.6) and on BipedalWalker where ADRQN edges HFPS on Final@T (151.7 vs. 146.0), but they still trail HFPS on AUC@T in every task. Actor-critic methods (A2C, PPO) perform worst overall, with A2C collapsing to −500.0 on Acrobot and −9536.1 on PointMass, suggesting that the hidden actuator memory is especially detrimental to policy-gradient approaches without explicit memory modeling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the Hodge decomposition from discrete differential geometry and combinatorial Hodge theory (Desbrun et al., 2006; Jiang et al., 2011; Lim, 2020) into the RL setting by reinterpreting TD errors as 1-cochains on the state-transition space and Bellman integrability as exactness of the discounted coboundary operator. It departs from the dominant non-Markovian RL literature—which relies on memory augmentation, recurrent architectures (DRQN, R2D2, ADRQN), or finite-state controllers—by offering a Hilbert-space projection principle that filters TD signals rather than expanding the state representation. The method is built directly on classical TD semi-gradient updates (Sutton, 1988) and standard DQN/PPO training loops, with the topological projection inserted as a lightweight inner-loop step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The topological residual provides a principled, interpretable diagnostic of Bellman mismatch that is absent from standard TD learning, and the HodgeFlow projection is modular enough to plug into PPO/GAE or any off-policy TD algorithm with only ~1.27× wall-clock overhead. Key limitations include the gap between population-level orthogonality guarantees and the empirical mini-batch projection actually computed, the additional potential network and inner-loop SGD steps, and the fact that HFPS is a Bellman-compatible filter rather than a replacement for belief-state estimation in strongly non-Markovian settings. Open questions include whether the cochain perspective generalizes to multi-step TD, off-policy importance-weighted corrections, or continuous-action settings, and whether the residual can guide adaptive representation learning.
