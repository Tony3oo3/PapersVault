## Problem

Standard policy gradient methods like GRPO compute advantages using a single global baseline, implicitly assuming all trajectories are comparable. For LLM search agents, trajectories are structurally heterogeneous—varying in the number, placement, and outcomes of search calls—which makes global comparisons an 'apples-to-oranges' mismatch. The paper formalizes this as cross-stratum bias, showing it systematically penalizes trajectories from lower-reward strata (e.g., newly discovered complex multi-search strategies), distorting credit assignment and suppressing exploration of effective search policies.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a text question and interacts with a search engine (E5 retriever over 2018 Wikipedia for QA; Google search plus a sandboxed Python executor for deep-research) over up to 4–5 multi-turn interactions, each turn producing generated tokens and optionally a search query that retrieves top-3 passages. The final output is a text answer, scored by Exact Match (QA) or task completion (GAIA). Trajectories within a batch are partitioned by the number of tool calls issued, which serves as the stratification key for advantage computation.

## Method

Stratified GRPO replaces GRPO's global advantage normalization with Stratified Advantage Normalization (SAN), which partitions a batch of trajectories into homogeneous strata by search-call count and computes each trajectory's advantage as its reward minus the stratum mean, divided by the stratum standard deviation. The paper proves SAN is conditionally unbiased with unit variance within every stratum while preserving the global zero-mean and unit-variance properties of standard normalization, and that the population SAN gradient decomposes into a weighted sum of true within-stratum policy gradients. To handle finite-sample noise when some strata are small, a Blended Advantage linearly interpolates between SAN and the global estimator via a coefficient α (default 0.6–0.8). The full algorithm (Algorithm 1) computes both global and per-stratum statistics per prompt, blends the two advantages, and uses the result in the standard GRPO policy-gradient update.

## Results

On seven factual QA benchmarks, Stratified GRPO achieves 41.6 average EM on Qwen2.5-3B-Base (vs. GRPO's 33.3, +8.3 points) and 40.5 on Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct (vs. GRPO's 29.2, +11.3 points), with the largest gains on multi-hop tasks (up to +14.5 over the strongest baseline). On the GAIA deep-research benchmark, Stratified GRPO reaches 49.5 average (vs. GRPO's 36.9, +12.6 points), including a 2× relative improvement on the hardest Level-3 tasks (33.3 vs. 16.7). The method also combines additively with the stronger DAPO recipe, lifting the average from 35.5 (DAPO alone) to 43.8.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Stratified GRPO outperforms the primary RL baseline GRPO by +8.3 points (Base) and +11.3 points (Instruct) on average across seven QA benchmarks, with the gap widening to +14.5 on multi-hop tasks where structural heterogeneity is most pronounced. Among non-RL baselines, it surpasses RAG (27.0 avg), Search-o1 (25.5), and IRCoT (18.1) by large margins, and also beats the strongest RL baselines Search-R1 (30.3/32.5) and ReSearch (31.9/33.1). On the GAIA deep-research benchmark, Stratified GRPO (49.5) exceeds all tool-integrated reasoning agents (ReAct 23.3, WebThinker 22.3, Search-o1 21.4) and all large models without tools (DeepSeek-R1-671B 25.2, GPT-4o 17.5), and doubles GRPO's Level-3 score (33.3 vs. 16.7). The method is also compatible with the stronger DAPO recipe: adding Stratified GRPO to DAPO lifts the average from 35.5 to 43.8, confirming that the stratification correction addresses a complementary normalization issue rather than overlapping with DAPO's improvements.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) to the specific setting of LLM search agents, building directly on the RL formulation and experimental setup of Search-R1 (Jin et al., 2025). The key conceptual departure is identifying 'cross-stratum bias' — a systematic distortion in advantage estimation that arises when structurally heterogeneous trajectories (differing in tool-call count) are normalized against a single global baseline. The proposed Stratified Advantage Normalization (SAN) partitions trajectories by structural properties and normalizes locally, with a theoretical decomposition (Proposition 3.3) showing exactly how the global estimator's bias decomposes into a rescaled SAN term plus a stratum-specific offset. A blended estimator further bridges the population-level theory with finite-sample practice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean mechanistic explanation for why standard GRPO stagnates on complex multi-search strategies (cross-stratum bias suppresses gradients for lower-reward strata), and a simple fix that yields substantial gains (up to 12.6 points on GAIA, 14.5 on multi-hop QA). The technique is lightweight and could generalize to any RL setting with heterogeneous trajectory structures beyond search agents. Limitations include the reliance on a single hand-designed stratifier (tool-call count), population-level theoretical guarantees that don't directly cover finite-sample behavior, and evaluation limited to relatively small models (3B–8B). Open questions include whether richer or learned stratification criteria could further improve performance, and whether the approach transfers to other tool-use or multi-modal agent settings.
