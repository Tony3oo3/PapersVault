## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models achieve >80% accuracy on single-object tasks but drop below 50% on multi-object compositional generation (counting, attribute binding, spatial relations), yet the underlying data-related causes remain unclear. Prior compositional generalization studies focus on single-object settings with continuous attributes, leaving open how discrete multi-object compositions are affected by data imbalance, dataset size, and held-out concept combinations.

## Input / Output

The model takes a one-hot condition vector encoding discrete concepts (object count 1–10, sphere/cube color pairs from 10 colors, or angular spatial relation in 10 bins of 18°) and produces a 128×128 RGB image of a multi-object scene. For compositional generalization, two concept vectors are jointly encoded as separate tokens and injected via cross-attention. Evaluation is performed by task-specific discriminative classifiers (CNN for counting, ResNet for attribution and spatial relations) trained on extended MOSAIC data, with joint accuracy additionally requiring a 10-class color classifier.

## Method

The paper introduces MOSAIC, a simulation-based diagnostic dataset (built on COMFORT/Blender) that explicitly factorizes three multi-object compositional concepts—Attribution, Counting, and Spatial Relations—each with controlled variants (Base, Complex, Grid) to isolate scene complexity from concept difficulty. Two ~90M-parameter diffusion backbones (U-Net with score-matching and DiT with flow-matching) are trained from scratch with a frozen SD2 VAE and a lightweight one-hot condition encoder, jointly optimized for 20k steps. The experimental design systematically varies dataset size (2k–100k), distribution (uniform vs. skewed inspired by LAION-2B frequencies), and the number of held-out concept-pair diagonals (0–8) to probe concept generalization (RQ1) and compositional generalization (RQ2). For realistic validation, SD3-medium is fine-tuned with LoRA on the SPEC benchmark, and a less-controlled MOSAIC OBJECTS variant with occlusions and varied object categories tests whether findings persist under distribution shift.

## Results

On the MOSAIC diagnostic benchmark, Attribution and Spatial Relations achieve >90% accuracy across all dataset sizes (2k–100k) and both uniform/skewed distributions for both U-Net and DiT backbones. Counting, however, exhibits a distinct failure mode: near-perfect accuracy at 2k samples drops sharply at 10k and 50k (peaking early in training then degrading), and only recovers at 100k, largely independent of data skewness. Introducing a radial grid layout substantially improves counting accuracy across all scales. For compositional generalization, accuracy on unseen compositions degrades monotonically as more concept-pair diagonals are held out, with a consistent difficulty ordering of Attribution < Counting < Spatial Relations; Spatial Relations show the most severe and unstructured confusion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a diagnostic/empirical study rather than a method-comparison paper, so there are no external method baselines. The internal comparisons are: (1) U-Net (score-matching, ~90M params) vs. DiT (flow-matching, ~90M params) — DiT shows improved robustness under limited data, particularly for Counting at 10k, but both architectures exhibit the same qualitative failure patterns. (2) Base vs. Complex scene settings — increasing scene complexity (more objects, distractors) degrades Attribution and Spatial Relations accuracy at 10k, confirming that scene complexity, not concept imbalance, is the dominant factor. (3) Base vs. Grid layout for Counting — the grid constraint dramatically improves counting accuracy at all dataset sizes, showing that spatial inductive bias mitigates the counting failure. (4) Fine-tuning SD3-medium with LoRA on SPEC — spatial relation accuracy improves steadily with training steps while counting accuracy deteriorates, consistent with the controlled MOSAIC findings. The paper does not propose a new generation method, so no head-to-head method comparisons are reported.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the COMFORT simulation pipeline (Zhang et al., 2024) for 3D scene rendering and on the diagonal leave-out scheme from Uselis et al. (2025) for controlling unseen compositions, but repurposes both into a new diagnostic framework (MOSAIC) specifically designed to isolate multi-object compositional concepts (attribution, counting, spatial relations). It departs from prior compositional generalization studies in diffusion models (Okawa et al., 2023; Park et al., 2024), which focused on single-object settings with continuous attributes, by studying discrete multi-object compositions under controlled data distributions. Unlike prior analyses of multi-object failures that attribute them to text encoders or frequency effects without systematic data control, this work trains vanilla U-Net and DiT diffusion models from scratch on MOSAIC to enable causal analysis of data properties.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies scene complexity (not concept imbalance) as the dominant factor in multi-object generation failures and reveals that counting exhibits a unique early-peaking-then-degrading learning dynamic where training loss continues to decrease while task accuracy collapses—a misalignment between optimization and generation quality. The compositional generalization difficulty ordering (Attribution < Counting < Spatial Relations) and its persistence under realistic fine-tuning of SD3 provide concrete guidance for where inductive biases (e.g., layout conditioning, spatial priors) are most needed. Open questions include whether stronger architectural inductive biases can close the compositional gap, how these findings scale to open-vocabulary settings, and whether the counting degradation observed during fine-tuning reflects a fundamental limitation of the denoising objective for numerosity.
