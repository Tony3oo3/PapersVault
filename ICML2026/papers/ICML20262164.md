## Problem

Predictive memory in online video models often exacerbates drift under occlusion or rapid motion because inaccurate predictions are unconditionally committed to the internal state, contaminating it and causing irreversible identity loss. The paper identifies this as a reliability mismatch: existing approaches treat predicted latent states as equally trustworthy as observation-driven features, even under severe uncertainty, and lack an explicit mechanism to regulate when predictive dynamics should influence perception.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262164/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes sequential video frames at 224×224 resolution (14×14 feature maps) along with an initial target prompt, operating over a sliding tracklet of 8 past frames. It produces per-frame segmentation masks at 64×64 decoder resolution and object bounding boxes. A key design detail is that the predictive prompt is injected as a dense spatial prompt into the SAM2 decoder only when a binary reliability gate is active, and its spatial alignment adapts between the previous frame and the last reliably observed frame depending on current observation confidence.

## Method

RPM augments a frozen SAM2.1 backbone with a 24-layer Mamba2 state-space model that generates one-step-ahead predictive latents from a short 8-frame tracklet, aggregated via softmax-weighted temporal pooling into a single predictive prior. A reliability signal combines three complementary cues—prediction-observation feature consistency (cosine distance in a target-centered crop), decoder-derived IoU confidence, and objectness—to produce a binary gate that activates predictive prompting only when observation confidence is low AND the prediction remains consistent with recent evidence. When active, the predictive prior is transformed into a decoder-aligned dense prompt and added to the observation features; when inactive, inference falls back to standard observation-driven SAM2 updates. Spatial anchoring of the predictive prompt also adapts: it uses the previous frame when objectness is reliable, reverting to the last confidently observed frame otherwise to prevent drift-crop feedback. Training freezes all SAM2.1 parameters and optimizes only the RPM module with a dynamics consistency loss (MSE between predicted latent and next-frame observation feature) plus a mask prediction loss (BCE + Dice), with no supervision on the reliability gate itself.

## Results

On LaSOT, RPM achieves 74.5 AUC, 82.9 Pnorm, and 80.7 P, outperforming the SAM2.1 baseline (66.0 AUC) by 8.5 points and the strongest SAM2-based tracker HiM2SAM (73.4 AUC) by 1.1 points. On LaSOT_ext, RPM reaches 60.9 AUC and 71.4 P, surpassing HiM2SAM (59.3 AUC) by 1.6 points and DAM4SAM (58.6 AUC) by 2.3 points. On GOT-10k, RPM attains 79.7 AO, matching the best supervised tracker SUTrack (79.3 AO) while operating zero-shot. The reliability-guided fusion adds only 0.82% latency overhead versus 22.2% for unconditional predictive prompting, with the predictive gate activating on just 3.71% of LaSOT frames.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among zero-shot SAM2-based methods, RPM outperforms all four baselines across every benchmark. On LaSOT, RPM (74.5 AUC) surpasses HiM2SAM (73.4) by 1.1, DAM4SAM (73.3) by 1.2, SAMURAI (70.7) by 3.8, and the SAM2.1 backbone (66.0) by 8.5. The gains are most pronounced on LaSOT_ext, where long sequences amplify drift: RPM leads HiM2SAM by 1.6 AUC (60.9 vs. 59.3) and DAM4SAM by 2.3 AUC (60.9 vs. 58.6). On GOT-10k, RPM (79.7 AO) edges out SAMURAI (79.6) and SAM2.1 (77.9), and is competitive with the best supervised tracker SUTrack (79.3 AO) despite operating zero-shot. In the occlusion-stratified analysis (Table 5), the advantage grows with occlusion duration: on long occlusions (>20 frames), RPM achieves 0.421 mean IoU versus 0.384 for HiM2SAM and 0.311 for SAMURAI. For video object segmentation generalization (Table 6), RPM improves J&F over SAM2.1 on all seven benchmarks, with the largest gains on SA-V val (+1.5) and LVOS v1 val (+1.9), confirming the reliability-guided mechanism transfers beyond tracking without architectural changes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of memory-based video foundation models (SAM2 lineage), state-space sequence modeling (Mamba2), and classical reliability-aware state estimation (Kalman filtering). It departs from prior SAM2-based trackers (SAMURAI, DAM4SAM, HiM2SAM) that are purely observation-driven and cannot extrapolate during occlusion, and from prior predictive approaches (DreamTrack, world-model-based trackers) that inject predicted latents unconditionally into memory. The key conceptual departure is identifying a 'reliability mismatch'—showing that unregulated predictive memory actively degrades tracking by contaminating the memory state—and proposing a binary, reliability-gated mechanism that treats predictions as conditional priors rather than permanent memory writes. The use of Mamba2 as a lightweight predictive dynamics backbone over short tracklets, combined with a deterministic (non-learned) gating rule, distinguishes the approach from both learned soft-fusion methods and multi-modal future prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that predictive memory is beneficial only when its reliability is explicitly modeled, and that unconditional prediction injection can underperform static memory under occlusion—provides a general design principle for integrating world-model reasoning into video foundation architectures. The ablation showing that binary suppression outperforms learned soft-gating (74.5 vs. 72.3 AUC) challenges the common assumption that continuous, differentiable blending is always preferable, suggesting that hard gating may be necessary to prevent residual error compounding in recursive memory systems. Open questions include whether the one-step prediction horizon and fixed global thresholds generalize to substantially different observation statistics (e.g., 3D tracking, multi-object settings), and whether the reliability signal can be made fully learned without reintroducing the soft-gating failure mode. The near-zero computational overhead (3.7% activation rate, +0.82% latency) makes the approach practically deployable.
