## Problem

Existing causal root cause analysis (RCA) methods for microservice systems rely on constraint-based independence tests that lose statistical power as conditioning sets grow, making them sample-inefficient when only a handful of anomalous samples are available. Moreover, prior Bayesian causal discovery approaches require enumerating super-exponentially many DAGs or making parametric assumptions, and no nonparametric RCA method had previously provided statistical consistency guarantees. BRCD addresses both gaps by combining a partial causal structure (CPDAG) with efficient polynomial-time DAG sampling to perform principled Bayesian inference over root causes.

## Input / Output

BRCD consumes two datasets of monitoring metrics (e.g., latency, CPU, memory) collected from N microservices: a pre-failure observational set D_obs and a post-failure interventional set D_int, along with a CPDAG learned from D_obs via an observational causal discovery algorithm (e.g., BOSS) and a prior over root cause sets. The failure is encoded as a binary proxy variable F pointing to the true root cause, so the combined data is modeled as a soft intervention. The output is a full posterior distribution p(R|D) over all candidate root cause sets, from which a ranked list of the most probable anomalous metrics is extracted; the method also supports iterative posterior updates as additional anomalous samples arrive (anytime property).

## Method

BRCD's key insight is that all DAGs within the same interventional Markov equivalence class (I-MEC) induce identical interventional likelihoods, so the posterior can be updated by sampling one representative DAG per I-MEC rather than enumerating all DAGs. For each candidate root cause set R, the algorithm orients all possible edge-cut configurations between R and V\R on the given CPDAG, closes under Meek rules to obtain I-CPDAGs, then draws a uniform DAG from each I-CPDAG using the polynomial-time clique-picking sampler of Wienobst et al. (2023) and evaluates the joint likelihood under the augmented graph G_aug (G with F→R added). The posterior over root causes is computed by summing weighted likelihoods across all I-CPDAGs, with weights proportional to I-MEC sizes. The paper proves generic identifiability of root causes under interventional faithfulness (Lemma 4.1), posterior consistency (Theorem 4.3), and an exponential finite-sample bound that remains valid under an ε-vanishing plug-in approximation (Theorem 4.4). CPDAG uncertainty is handled by bootstrapping multiple observational causal discovery runs and marginalizing over the resulting CPDAG candidates.

## Results

On the Petshop benchmark (only 5 anomalous samples per scenario), BRCD achieves 0.40 average top-1 accuracy, outperforming the strongest constraint-based baseline RCD (0.30) and RCG (0.13); with bootstrapping (BRCD-B100) it reaches 0.47 top-1 and 0.73 top-5. On synthetic DAGs up to 1000 nodes, BRCD consistently attains the highest top-l accuracy across all graph sizes and scales more efficiently than RCD and RCG (which fails to finish within the 3-minute limit at 1000 nodes). For multiple root causes on 50-node DAGs, BRCD matches RCD at 0.90 accuracy while outperforming RCG (0.80), and in the presence of latent confounders BRCD achieves 0.60 top-1 vs. RCD's 0.50 and RCG's 0.36.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BRCD outperforms all nine baselines on the Petshop dataset in top-1 accuracy (0.40 vs. RCD 0.30, BARO 0.23, IDI 0.19, SimpleRCA 0.18, ShapleyIQ 0.15, RCG 0.13, SO 0.01, ST 0.00, Cholesky 0.00, MicroDig 0.00), with the gap widening at top-3 and top-5. On synthetic benchmarks, BRCD consistently achieves the highest top-l accuracy across graph sizes from 10 to 1000 nodes, while RCG fails to complete within the time limit at 1000 nodes. For multiple root causes, BRCD ties RCD at 0.90 accuracy but clearly beats RCG (0.80), attributed to RCG's sample-inefficient conditional mutual information computation. In the latent-confounder setting, BRCD (0.60 top-1) outperforms both RCD (0.50) and RCG (0.36), benefiting from not relying on conditional independence tests. On Online Boutique and Sockshop, BRCD slightly underperforms SimpleRCA and BARO (which use marginal-distribution deviation ranking) but outperforms all other baselines, suggesting that distribution-shift-based methods have an advantage on those particular datasets while BRCD generalizes better to Petshop's near-constant variables and missing values.

## Relation to Prior Work

BRCD sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) constraint-based RCA methods like RCD (Ikram et al., 2022) and RCG (Ikram et al., 2025) that model failures as interventions but suffer from sample-inefficient conditional independence tests; (2) Bayesian causal discovery, which was previously intractable due to the super-exponential DAG space but became feasible via Wienobst et al.'s (2023) polynomial-time MEC sampling; and (3) the I-MEC framework (Yang et al., 2018; Kocaoglu et al., 2019) that groups DAGs sharing the same interventional likelihood. The key departure is that BRCD is the first to exploit I-MEC grouping for RCA—scoring one sampled DAG per I-MEC rather than enumerating all DAGs—thereby obtaining the first posterior consistency guarantees for nonparametric root cause discovery without parametric assumptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: microservice outages cost millions per hour, and anomalous samples are inherently scarce (Petshop has only 5 per incident), making sample efficiency a first-order concern that constraint-based methods handle poorly. The theoretical contribution—identifiability under extended faithfulness and an exponential finite-sample posterior bound robust to ε-approximate plug-in estimators—provides a principled foundation that prior RCA methods lacked. Limitations include reliance on a pre-failure CPDAG (whose quality depends on the observational discovery algorithm), a bound that is qualitative rather than tight in practice, and moderate (not dominant) empirical gains over RCD on Petshop. Open questions include tighter sample-complexity bounds, integration of informative priors from domain knowledge or other RCA signals, and extension to settings with latent confounders or cyclic dependencies.
