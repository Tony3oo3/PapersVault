## Problem

Optimization-based diffusion inverse-problem methods (e.g., DiffPIR, DCDP, DMPlug) alternate denoising and data-consistency steps, but it remains unclear whether their iterative procedure converges to the global MAP or is consistent with posterior sampling (DPS). This paper shows that these methods implicitly solve local MAP subproblems along the diffusion trajectory, which are neither equivalent to global MAP nor to posterior sampling, and formalizes this perspective as a unified framework.

## Input / Output

Given a noisy measurement y ∈ R^m, a forward operator H(·) (linear, nonlinear, or non-differentiable), and a pretrained diffusion model, LMAPS produces a single reconstructed image or signal x0 as a point estimate rather than a posterior sample. For image restoration, inputs are 256×256 RGB images on FFHQ or ImageNet with Gaussian noise σy=0.05; for scientific tasks, inputs include multi-receiver scattering data, k-space MRI measurements, and interferometric visibilities. The method requires K gradient steps per diffusion timestep (typically 20–200) to solve each local MAP subproblem.

## Method

LMAPS replaces the conditional mean E[x0|xt,y] used in DPS with the conditional mode argmax p(x0|xt,y) at each reverse diffusion step, then applies the standard DDIM transition. The local MAP objective is solved via gradient descent using a Gaussian approximation p(x0|xt) ≈ N(m_{0|t}, Σ_{0|t}), where the covariance is approximated as isotropic Σ_{0|t} ≈ (1/SNR)·I, justified by asymptotic analysis showing anisotropy is higher-order as t→0. A key reformulation rewrites the objective as a convex combination of a prior-fidelity term and a data-consistency term with weights μt = σt²/(σt²+k1²) that automatically anneal from measurement-driven to prior-driven over the diffusion schedule, improving numerical stability. For linear inverse problems, the local MAP reduces exactly to the Tweedie Moment Projected Diffusion (TMPD) update, unifying DPS and optimization-based methods under one framework. The tunable parameter k2 controls the strength of measurement conditioning and thus the degree of mode-seeking in the reconstruction.

## Results

LMAPS achieves the best PSNR, SSIM, or LPIPS in 43 out of 60 cases across 10 image restoration tasks on FFHQ and ImageNet (256×256). On FFHQ, headline PSNR values include 30.74 (SR 4×), 34.51 (Gaussian Deblurring), 32.62 (Motion Deblurring), and 31.56 (Phase Retrieval), consistently outperforming DAPS, DMAP, and SITCOM. On scientific inverse problems (InverseBench), LMAPS attains the highest PSNR across all 10 settings, with gains exceeding 1.5 dB on three Linear Inverse Scattering tasks (e.g., 38.07 vs. 36.56 PSNR at NR=360). LMAPS also matches or exceeds SITCOM's quality while requiring substantially fewer NFEs (200 vs. 600).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LMAPS outperforms all 18 baselines on the majority of distortion metrics (PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS). Against the closest competitor DMAP (Xu et al., 2025), LMAPS gains 2.2 dB on SR (30.74 vs. 28.56), 2.6 dB on box inpainting (25.02 vs. 22.37), 2.1 dB on Gaussian deblurring (34.51 vs. 32.43), and 5.1 dB on motion deblurring (32.62 vs. 27.52) on FFHQ; however, DMAP achieves lower FID on SR (44.78 vs. 80.63), reflecting a fidelity-vs-diversity trade-off. Compared to DAPS (Zhang et al., 2025a), LMAPS improves PSNR by 1.7 dB on Gaussian deblurring and 3.0 dB on motion deblurring while using fewer NFEs (200 vs. 200 with 100 Langevin steps per step). Against SITCOM (Alkhouri et al., 2024), LMAPS matches or exceeds PSNR (34.51 vs. 33.96 on deblurring) with 200 NFEs versus SITCOM's 600, and achieves lower LPIPS (0.066 vs. 0.082). On scientific inverse problems, LMAPS surpasses RED-diff (Mardani et al., 2023) by 1.5 dB on LIS (38.07 vs. 36.56 at NR=360), outperforms DAPS by 3.4 dB on LIS and 1.3 dB on CS-MRI 4× noiseless, and leads PnP-DM (Wu et al., 2024) by 0.7 dB on Black Hole imaging at 100% observation. LMAPS also dominates DPS (Chung et al., 2022), DDNM (Wang et al., 2022), DDRM (Kawar et al., 2022), IIGDM (Song et al., 2023b), LGD (Song et al., 2023c), DiffPIR (Zhu et al., 2023), DCDP (Li et al., 2024), DMPlug (Wang et al., 2024), MGDM (Janati et al., 2025), MAP-GA (Gutha et al., 2025), MMPS (Rozet et al., 2024), and MCG-diff (Cardoso et al., 2023) across nearly all tasks and metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

LMAPS is most directly built on DMAP (Xu et al., 2025), which also frames diffusion inverse-problem solvers through a local MAP lens, but LMAPS poses the local MAP problem in clean-signal space rather than latent space, making the data-consistency term p(y|x₀) explicit and the connection to optimization-based methods (Resample, DiffPIR, DCDP, DMPlug) transparent. The algorithmic backbone inherits the DDIM transition from DPS (Chung et al., 2022) and the decoupled annealing step from DAPS (Zhang et al., 2025a), while the isotropic covariance approximation Σ₀|ₜ ≈ (1/SNR)·I is derived from the TMPD moment-projection covariance (Boys et al., 2023), of which LMAPS recovers TMPD as a special case for linear problems. The key conceptual departure is the unification: LMAPS shows that DPS, DMAP, TMPD, and heuristic optimization-based solvers are all instances of the same local MAP subproblem, differing only in how the conditional prior p(x₀|xₜ) is approximated and whether the mean or mode is used.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a much-needed probabilistic interpretation for a large family of optimization-based diffusion inverse-problem methods that previously lacked a principled objective, and the isotropic covariance approximation is well-justified by an asymptotic argument that holds for both Gaussian and general smooth priors. Empirically, LMAPS achieves the best results in 43/60 PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS cases across 10 image restoration tasks and 3 scientific problems while being more efficient than DAPS, making it a strong practical default. The main open question the authors flag is that local MAP does not converge to global MAP, and efficiently solving the global MAP with diffusion priors remains unsolved—this gap between local and global objectives is likely to be a recurring theme in future work. A limitation is that the isotropic covariance, while asymptotically justified, may be suboptimal at intermediate noise levels where anisotropic prior curvature still matters.
