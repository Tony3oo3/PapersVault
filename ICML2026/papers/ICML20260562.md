## Problem

LLM-as-a-judge evaluations introduce systematic measurement error, and two separate methodological traditions—direct misclassification correction (Rogan-Gladen) and surrogate-outcome calibration (PPI, PPI++)—have developed in parallel without a unified statistical comparison. This paper asks: under a common semiparametric model, what is the smallest achievable asymptotic variance for estimating the true mean, and which of these estimators attain it?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260562/cropped_page6_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a large test set (size n) with only LLM judge labels Ŷ_i ∈ {0,1} (or ordinal/continuous scores) and a smaller calibration set (size m) with paired (Y_i, Ŷ_i) observations, where the split follows an MCAR mechanism with n/m → γ₁. It produces a point estimate of the true mean θ = E[Y] (e.g., human win rate), a confidence interval with valid asymptotic coverage, and an asymptotic variance estimate. A key practical constraint is that the calibration set can be as small as 1% of the total data, and the method must remain valid when the labeling rule is MAR (conditioned on observed covariates) rather than strictly MCAR.

## Method

The paper derives the efficient influence function (EIF) for θ = E[Y] under the observed-data model (Y, Ŷ, R) with R indicating calibration membership, yielding the one-step estimator θ̂{EIF} = (1/N)Σ μ̂(Ŷ_i) + (1/m)Σ(Y_j − μ̂(Ŷ_j)), where μ̂(·) estimates the conditional mean E[Y|Ŷ] from the calibration set. The central theoretical result is that in the binary case, μ(Ŷ) is necessarily linear in Ŷ, so optimally tuned PPI++ (with λ* = γ₁Cov(Y,Ŷ)/((1+γ₁)Var(Ŷ))) is asymptotically equivalent to the EIF estimator and to the joint MLE, while standard PPI (which implicitly uses μ(Ŷ)=Ŷ) and Rogan-Gladen are strictly less efficient. For non-binary or ordinal surrogates, the EIF framework is unchanged but requires flexible estimation of E[Y|Ŷ] (e.g., GAMs, splines, or per-category means) to attain the efficiency bound, since the linear PPI++ family is no longer rich enough. The paper further extends the EIF to MAR labeling by replacing the constant P(R=1) with a covariate-dependent π(W) and μ(Ŷ) with μ(W), and proves that PPI is always at least as efficient as Rogan-Gladen (Proposition 3.3) with equality only under perfect classification.

## Results

On the arena-human-preference-140k dataset (three model pairs, four LLM judges, 10% calibration), EIF, PPI++, and Joint MLE all achieve near-nominal 90% coverage with mean CI widths of 0.27–0.30, while PPI overcovers (93–96%) with wider intervals (0.35–0.44) and Rogan-Gladen is highly conservative (98–99% coverage) with very wide intervals (0.78–0.90). In binary simulations, EIF and PPI++ produce nearly identical intervals that are 35–55% narrower than PPI and up to 10× narrower than Rogan-Gladen at low judge accuracy (q₀=q₁=0.6). The naive estimator collapses to 0% coverage when judge bias is substantial (e.g., GPT-4o-mini on Gemini 2.5 Pro, both judges on Qwen3-235B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Rogan-Gladen, the EIF/PPI++/MLE estimators are dramatically more efficient: on real data, RG intervals are 0.78–0.90 wide versus 0.27–0.30 for the efficient methods (roughly 3× wider), and in simulations the gap widens to 10× at low judge accuracy (q₀=q₁=0.6) due to the 1/(q₀+q₁−1)² amplification in RG's variance. Against PPI, the paper proves PPI is suboptimal (it corresponds to the special case μ(Ŷ)=Ŷ in the EIF framework) and empirically confirms PPI++/EIF intervals are 35–55% narrower; PPI also overcovers (93–96% vs. the 90% target). Against PPI++, the paper's key theoretical result is that in the binary setting, optimally tuned PPI++ is asymptotically equivalent to the EIF estimator (and to the MLE), so the three methods produce nearly identical coverage and width in all experiments. The naive estimator (no correction) is the clear loser: it achieves 0% coverage in several judge-dataset combinations where systematic bias is large, making it unreliable for any practical evaluation pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate methodological traditions—measurement-error correction (Rogan-Gladen, confusion-matrix inversion) from epidemiology and prediction-powered inference (PPI, PPI++) from modern ML—under a single semiparametric efficiency framework. The key conceptual move is recognizing that all these estimators are different approximations to the conditional mean E[Y|Ŷ]: PPI uses the identity map, PPI++ uses a one-parameter linear map, and the EIF estimator uses the true (or flexibly estimated) conditional expectation. The paper builds directly on the missing-data EIF theory of Robins & Rotnitzky (1995) and the recent RePPI/generalized PPI line (Ji et al., 2025; Gronsbell et al., 2024; Zou et al., 2026), but provides the explicit comparison to direct misclassification correction that those works do not offer, and proves the binary-case equivalence between optimally tuned PPI++, the EIF estimator, and the joint MLE.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides actionable guidance for the rapidly growing LLM-as-a-judge evaluation ecosystem: in the binary setting, PPI++/EIF/MLE are all asymptotically optimal and produce CIs 3–15× narrower than Rogan-Gladen, while standard PPI remains a reasonable but suboptimal fallback. The practical finding that RG can collapse to 0% coverage under judge-confidence-based sampling (MAR(Ŷ)) while EIF remains valid after a simple probability adjustment is important for real-world audit designs. Open questions include instance-dependent misclassification (error rates varying with prompt difficulty), multi-judge ensembling with distinct error profiles, and transportability under distribution shift, where the paper notes RG-style correction may actually be more robust if the confusion matrix is stable while the full calibration relation is not.
