## Problem

Existing LLM science benchmarks focus on perceptual or knowledge-based tasks (QA, property prediction) but largely ignore the modelling tasks that are a fundamental starting point for real scientific research. In materials science, constructing and manipulating atomic structures through sequences of spatial operations is one of the most creative and least automated steps, yet no benchmark exists to evaluate whether LLMs can faithfully execute such structured modifications in continuous space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263103/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each test sample provides a CIF crystal structure file (standardized to pymatgen/Materials Project format) paired with a natural-language action prompt specifying a structural modification (e.g., 'Move the atom at index 3 by [0.5, -1.2, 0.3] angstrom'). The model must return the modified CIF enclosed in <cif> tags. Evaluation proceeds through a three-step validation pipeline: format validation, composition verification (correct elements and atom counts), and symmetry-aware structural matching via pymatgen's StructureMatcher with a 0.5 Å site tolerance. The primary metrics are success rate (fraction passing all checks) and mean max_dist (maximum pairwise atomic displacement after optimal alignment, averaged over valid outputs).

## Method

The core contribution is the AtomWorld data generator, which automatically produces unlimited (input structure, action prompt, target structure) triples by sampling structures from a pool, randomizing action parameters (atom indices, displacements, angles, radii), applying the instantiated action via pymatgen, and generating an aligned natural-language prompt. The AtomMotor-2K instantiation instantiates this with 2500 questions spanning 10 fundamental actions under 4 modelling categories (point defect/doping, surface generation, structure perturbation, supercell creation) using 250 structures from the Materials Project. The paper evaluates 10+ LLMs (frontier closed and open-source) without fine-tuning, and supplements the main benchmark with diagnostic probes—pure coordinate tests, CIF-Repair, CIF-Gen, Chemical Competence Score, and StructProp—that decompose spatial reasoning, format literacy, and property-oriented understanding into separable levels. A preliminary tool-augmented agent (DeepSeek + pymatgen coding) is also tested to probe whether external tools can close the spatial-reasoning gap.

## Results

On AtomMotor-2K (2500 questions across 10 structural actions), Claude Opus 4.6 achieves the highest overall success rate, while success rates drop sharply for spatially complex operations—falling below 12% for rotate_around across all models. Easy actions (change, remove, swap, add) are near-perfect for top models, whereas moderate actions (move, move_towards, insert_between) show 40–80% success, and hard actions (delete_below, rotate_around) remain below 20% for most models. On complementary tasks, Gemini 2.5 Pro leads CIF-Repair (100%) and CIF-Gen (95%), while GPT-o3 achieves 100% on CIF-Gen; StructProp success rates are generally below 50% (random-guess level) except for Gemini 2.5 Pro (80% band gap, 60% elastic).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a benchmark paper, so the 'baselines' are the various LLMs being evaluated. Claude Opus 4.6 and GPT-5.4 lead on AtomMotor-2K success rates, with Claude Opus 4.6 showing the lowest mean max_dist across most actions (e.g., 0.04 Å on insert_between vs. 0.19 Å for Llama-3 70B). Qwen3-32B consistently outperforms the larger Llama-3 70B across nearly all tasks, indicating that architectural and training choices matter as much as parameter count. On the pure coordinate tests, both Gemini 2.5 Pro and DeepSeek-V3.2 achieve near-zero max_dist for move (0.000 Å) but degrade catastrophically on rotate_around (16.17 Å and 14.06 Å respectively), confirming that nonlinear spatial transformations are the core bottleneck. Tool-augmented DeepSeek (with RAG over pymatgen) improves rotate_around from 6.8% to 18% success and insert_between from 45.6% to 83%, but gains remain limited for the hardest operations. Domain-specialized models (NatureLM, LLaMat) scored 0% on the first 16 tasks of each category and were excluded from full evaluation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a new evaluation axis for LLMs in materials science: structure *modification* as a verifiable execution task, distinct from the prior focus on structure generation (CrystaLLM, MatterGPT, AtomGPT) or knowledge-based QA (LLM4MatBench, MaCBench). It repurposes existing infrastructure—pymatgen's StructureMatcher for evaluation, the Materials Project for structure sourcing, and Robocrystallographer for description generation—into a systematic benchmark pipeline that had not previously been assembled. The conceptual framing of structure modelling as a 'motor skills' problem, analogous to embodied agent action execution, departs from the perceptual/cognitive framing that dominates current science benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AtomWorld provides the first verifiable, quantitative metrics for LLM spatial reasoning on atomic structures, filling a gap the authors identify as critical before LLMs can be deployed as autonomous scientific agents. The finding that even the best model (Claude Opus 4.6) drops below 12% success on rotation tasks, and that tool augmentation yields only marginal gains for complex operations, sets a clear ceiling on current capabilities and motivates future work on multimodal inputs, diffusion-based LLMs, and RL with structural rewards. Open questions include whether text-only CIF representation is fundamentally limiting, whether the benchmark's difficulty will erode as models improve, and how agentic action-observation loops might close the gap between copilot and autonomous agent performance.
