## Problem

In coupled spatiotemporal forecasting (e.g., ocean-atmosphere systems), errors from each autoregressive simulator propagate into the other and are amplified in a vicious cycle the authors term Reciprocal Error Amplification (REA), causing rapid collapse of long-range predictions. Existing remedies—end-to-end fine-tuning of the full coupled system or static statistical bias correction—either destroy learned physical priors through catastrophic forgetting or fail to capture the dynamic, state-dependent error patterns inherent to coupled interactions.

## Input / Output

The framework operates on 1° spatial resolution, 24-hour temporal resolution data: the atmosphere engine consumes 69 ERA5 variables plus sea surface temperature as a boundary condition, the ocean engine consumes 93 GLORYS12 variables plus 8 atmosphere surface variables (current and next-step), and the correction agent receives the combined 162 predicted variables to output 162 corrected variables. In the extended three-sphere case, 4 soil-temperature levels from ERA5 are added, bringing the total to 166 variables. All inputs are normalized per-variable (with climatological-mean subtraction for periodic ocean fields), and the corrected output at each step is fed back autoregressively as the next step's input.

## Method

PnP-Corrector decouples physical simulation from error correction: pre-trained physics engines (any architecture) are frozen, and a lightweight correction agent is trained in an autoregressive predict-then-correct loop where it sees the biased coupled output and learns to map it back toward ground truth, thereby internalizing the dynamic, state-dependent error structure of the full system. The correction agent is instantiated as DSLCast, a convolutional encoder-decoder (16 encoder blocks, 8 decoder blocks) whose two key building blocks are the Axially-Gated Block (parallel depthwise-separable 1×k and k×1 convolutions with a learned sigmoid gate) and the Differentiable Semi-Lagrangian Advection Block, which predicts a bounded flow field via tanh, performs backward-tracing to construct a sampling grid, and warps features through differentiable bilinear interpolation to explicitly model advection. A theoretical analysis in the appendix shows that if the correction agent's Lipschitz constant times the simulator's Lipschitz constant is less than one (i.e., the composite map is a contraction), the cumulative error is uniformly bounded by ε_corr/(1−λ) rather than growing exponentially as L_F^T, formally guaranteeing long-term stability.

## Results

On the 300-day global ocean-atmosphere coupled forecast (162 variables, 60 ICs), PnP-Corrector reduces RMSE by 20.60% for ConvLSTM (1.819→1.444), 23.75% for SimVP (1.367→1.042), 22.24% for GraphCast (1.699→1.321), and 12.94% for CirT (2.297→2.000). The improvement grows with lead time for GraphCast, from 2.02% at 25 days to 25.01% at 180 days, confirming that the framework specifically targets long-range error accumulation. For the Ola baseline, which diverges to RMSE >100 beyond 120 days, PnP-Corrector stabilizes the forecast to RMSE ≈46 at 300 days. The proposed DSLCast backbone achieves the lowest absolute RMSE (0.979 at 300 days) with only 34.76M parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PnP-Corrector is applied as a plug-and-play module on top of five diverse baselines, and it improves every one of them. Against GraphCast (the strongest uncoupled weather model), the correction yields a modest 2% RMSE gain at 25 days but grows to 22–25% at 180–300 days, demonstrating that the benefit scales with the severity of Reciprocal Error Amplification. Against SimVP and ConvLSTM (general-purpose spatiotemporal models), the gains are larger and more uniform (20–50% RMSE reduction), since these weaker baselines accumulate more error in the coupled loop. Against Ola (a coupled ocean-atmosphere model), the improvement is dramatic: Ola's forecast diverges to RMSE >100 beyond 120 days, while Ola+PnP stabilizes to RMSE ≈46 at 300 days. Against CirT (a geometry-inspired transformer), the gain is the smallest (9–13% RMSE), consistent with CirT's stronger inherent stability. The proposed DSLCast backbone achieves the best absolute accuracy (0.979 RMSE at 300 days) but shows the smallest relative PnP gain (2.3%), suggesting that a stronger base model leaves less systematic bias for the corrector to fix.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the coupled Earth-system forecasting line initiated by Ola (Wang et al., 2024) and Cresswell-Clay et al. (2025), which demonstrated that naive coupling of independent atmosphere and ocean models leads to rapid drift. It departs from prior error-mitigation strategies—end-to-end fine-tuning (which causes catastrophic forgetting) and static statistical bias correction (which cannot capture dynamic, state-dependent errors)—by introducing a decoupled paradigm: freeze pre-trained physics engines and train only a lightweight correction agent. The DSLCast architecture itself draws on axially-gated convolutions (reminiscent of SimVP) and adds a differentiable semi-Lagrangian advection block as a physics-inspired inductive bias, positioning it between pure CNN predictors and operator-based models like GraphCast.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Stable 300-day coupled ocean-atmosphere forecasting remains a critical unsolved problem for climate prediction (e.g., ENSO), and the model-agnostic PnP-Corrector offers a practical path to retrofit long-term stability onto existing SOTA models without retraining them. The main limitation acknowledged is the absence of uncertainty quantification—corrections are deterministic, which is insufficient for risk assessment in operational forecasting. Open questions include whether the contraction-mapping assumption (λ < 1) holds robustly across all regimes, how the framework scales to more than three coupled spheres, and whether the correction agent can be made probabilistic. The relatively modest gains for already-strong baselines (DSLCast: ~1–2% RMSE improvement) suggest diminishing returns when the base model is well-calibrated.
