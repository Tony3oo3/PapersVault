## Problem

Modern LLM agent benchmarks contain pervasive hidden validity flaws arising from interactions among user instructions, environments, tools, ground-truth trajectories, and evaluation protocols, yet no scalable automated method exists to systematically diagnose these component-level issues. Manual verification does not scale to the multi-component complexity of agent benchmarks, and existing automated approaches for static datasets cannot capture the semantic and contextual failures that emerge from cross-component interactions.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes a standardized task representation T = ⟨U, E, G, V⟩ constructed by mapping each benchmark's native fields (user prompts, tool schemas, reference trajectories, evaluation rules) into four fixed components. It produces a structured judgment φ(T) → (y, c, r): a binary flaw indicator, a taxonomy category localizing the issue to a specific component, and a natural-language rationale. Downstream, validated issues yield cleaned benchmark variants with flawed tasks removed or repaired, released as part of AgentSuite.

## Method

COBA decomposes agent benchmark auditing into two sequential stages. Stage 1 applies deterministic rule-based detectors (schema validators, role-confusion checks) to filter structurally invalid tasks at high precision. Stage 2 employs an LLM judge (default: Gemini-2.5-Pro-Thinking) conditioned on a component-based issue taxonomy derived bottom-up from expert annotation of six benchmarks; the judge reasons over the full task context but is constrained to classify issues within the fixed four-component ontology (User, Environment, Ground Truth, Evaluation), reducing free-form interpretation. An optional adversarial rebuttal stage then reviews initially flagged cases under three adjudication principles—workaround allowance, semantic equivalence, and parameter derivability—to overturn false positives. The key design insight is that localizing diagnosis to components, rather than assessing task validity holistically, aligns with the empirical observation that benchmark flaws are distributed across all four components and enables modular, benchmark-agnostic detection.

## Results

COBA achieves F1 scores of 0.791–0.874 with expert human judgments across six agent benchmarks (ACEBench 0.874, BFCL V3 0.831, CFB 0.791, τ-Bench 0.796, τ²-Bench 0.828), with post-hoc validation accuracy exceeding 72.1% at full scale. The pipeline identifies validated issues in 11.8%–24.2% of tasks across the six benchmarks (66.7% for DrafterBench, dominated by deterministic errors). On ACEBench, removing validated issues shifts 60% of model rankings (Kendall's τ = 0.871 vs. 0.960 under random removal), and the taxonomy-guided prompt outperforms a generic critique prompt in 72.8%–73.3% of disagreement cases while the generic prompt over-flags by 1.7×.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary external comparison is against the manual verification of τ²-Bench reported in SABER (Cuadron et al., 2025). On the Airline and Retail domains, COBA recovers 11 of the 15 objective issues identified through manual verification and additionally surfaces 14 validated objective issues within the same scope that were missed, plus role-confusion issues in Telecom and 'do-nothing' evaluation loopholes outside the manual scope. Internally, the taxonomy-guided prompt is compared against a generic critique prompt: in 114 (ACEBench) and 202 (CFB) disagreement cases, the taxonomy-guided judgment is correct 72.8% and 73.3% of the time, while the generic prompt flags 1.7× more tasks as problematic, indicating systematic over-flagging. A random-removal baseline confirms that the observed ranking shifts (pairwise flip rate 5.75%) are substantially larger than random task removal (1.50%, 95% CI [0.46, 2.65]), validating that the effects are driven by genuine benchmark flaws rather than task count reduction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of benchmark quality assessment (Northcutt et al., 2021; Truong et al., 2026; Vendrow et al., 2025) from static, single-component datasets to the multi-component interactive structure of agent benchmarks. Where prior work relied on manual audits, domain-specific heuristics, or generic LLM-as-judge checks that struggle with cross-component interactions, COBA introduces a standardized four-component decomposition (User, Environment, Ground Truth, Evaluation) as the organizing principle for both a recurring issue taxonomy and a hybrid rule-based + taxonomy-guided LLM detection pipeline. It also departs from concurrent manual-verification efforts on τ²-bench (Cuadron et al., 2025) by demonstrating that automated component-level auditing can surface issues that expert manual inspection misses due to the combinatorial complexity of agent benchmark interactions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that benchmark flaws affect ~27% of tasks on average and that removing them reshuffles 60% of model rankings in ACEBench (far beyond a random-removal baseline) suggests that current agent leaderboards may be substantially unreliable, with implications for model selection and research direction. The component-based taxonomy and COBA pipeline offer a scalable, reusable auditing framework that generalizes to unseen benchmarks with minimal adaptation, lowering the barrier to maintaining benchmark quality as agent evaluation proliferates. Open questions include whether the taxonomy will need significant revision for new benchmark paradigms (e.g., multi-agent, embodied), whether issue-guided repair can be fully automated rather than relying on manual correction, and how to handle the inherent tension between task realism (ambiguity, open-endedness) and evaluability that the paper identifies but does not resolve.
