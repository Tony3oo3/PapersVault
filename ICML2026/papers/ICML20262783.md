## Problem

Existing gray-box LVLM attacks require the attacker to select a specific target image and perform only basic visual feature alignment, ignoring the rich multi-view correspondence between detailed visual content and target semantics. This paper introduces a more natural gray-box setting where the attacker specifies a target textual semantic and proposes a multi-level adversarial alignment that jointly matches coarse global features and fine-grained visual-textual details.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262783/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a clean RGB image, an attacker-chosen target text semantic, and access to the LVLM's vision encoder (plus its corresponding text transformer) as input, and produces a perturbed adversarial image x^adv = x + δ constrained to an l∞ ball of radius ε (4/255 for gray-box, 16/255 for transfer). Unlike prior gray-box attacks that require a target image, here the target is purely textual; an LLM expands it into N=30 fine-grained descriptions, and M=20 augmented views (crops, flips, resizes) of the adversarial image are generated to model local visual content. The SVD projection uses the top K=256 principal components of the combined target text features.

## Method

The attack operates in two alignment stages optimized jointly via PGD on the vision encoder. First, a coarse global alignment projects the adversarial image embedding onto an SVD-structured subspace spanned by the top-K principal components of the concatenated target and fine-grained text features, eliminating non-target distortions; the paper proves this projection strictly increases cosine similarity with the target. Second, a fine-grained multi-context alignment models M augmented image views and N LLM-generated text descriptions as discrete distributions with entropy-adaptive importance weights, then solves an optimal transport problem (via Sinkhorn) whose cost matrix uses the projected cosine similarity. The paper proves the projected OT cost is element-wise no larger than the unprojected cost, guaranteeing a lower transport distance. The two losses (global projected cosine similarity and OT transport cost) are backpropagated through the vision encoder to update the perturbation.

## Results

On the gray-box targeted attack setting (ε=4/255, 100 PGD steps), the proposed method achieves 86.5% ASR and 0.82 AvgSim on COCO with Qwen2.5-VL-3B, outperforming the best baseline (AttackVLM at 74.8% ASR) by over 11 points. Across all six open-source LVLMs and four tasks, the method consistently leads by 8–15 ASR points. In the CLIP-based transfer attack setting (ε=16/255, 300 steps), the method reaches 81.2% ASR on GPT-4.1 and 78.4% on GPT-4o, surpassing the strongest baseline FOA-Attack (77.3% and 75.1% respectively) while also achieving 67.3% ASR on Gemini-2.0 versus 53.4% for FOA-Attack.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper outperforms all 11 baselines across both gray-box targeted and CLIP-based transfer settings. Among gray-box targeted baselines, AttackVLM (Zhao et al., 2024) is the strongest competitor, reaching 74.8% ASR on COCO/Qwen2.5-VL-3B, while the proposed method achieves 86.5%—a 11.7-point gap. VEAttack (Mei et al., 2025) and VT-Attack (Wang et al., 2024b) trail further behind at 72.5% and 69.2% respectively on the same setting. MIX-Attack (Tu et al., 2023) is the weakest gray-box baseline at 63.4%. In the transfer attack setting, FOA-Attack (Jia et al., 2025) is the closest competitor, achieving 75.1% ASR on GPT-4o versus 78.4% for the proposed method; M-Attack (Li et al., 2025) reaches 60.3% on GPT-4o. The remaining transfer baselines—AnyAttack (Zhang et al., 2024), AdvDiffVLM (Guo et al., 2024a), and SSA-CWA (Dong et al., 2023)—all fall below 45% ASR on GPT-4o, confirming a substantial performance gap. For efficiency, the proposed method (3.48T Flops, 7.2h) is comparable to VEAttack (2.59T, 5.3h) but far cheaper than white-box APGD (9.93T, 25.5h) and Ensemble (9.93T, 41.9h) attacks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the gray-box LVLM attack line (AttackVLM, VT-Attack, VEAttack) by replacing the image-target formulation with a text-target one, arguing that an attacker naturally specifies a textual semantic rather than selecting a target image. The method builds on optimal transport (Peyré & Cuturi; Sinkhorn) for fine-grained cross-modal alignment and introduces an SVD-structured subspace projection as a theoretically grounded mechanism for global feature alignment, a component absent from prior gray-box attacks that rely on simple cosine-similarity matching. The LLM-based expansion of target semantics into fine-grained descriptions draws on prompt-augmentation work (Pratt et al., Roth et al.) but repurposes it for adversarial guidance rather than inference-time classification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The text-targeted gray-box formulation is more practical and flexible than image-targeted alternatives, and the demonstrated transferability to production closed-source models (GPT-4o, Claude, Gemini) via a single CLIP surrogate underscores a real deployment risk for LVLM applications. The theoretical guarantee that SVD projection monotonically improves cosine similarity and reduces OT cost provides a clean justification for the design, but the approach still requires vision-encoder access and an LLM call for semantic expansion, limiting its applicability in fully black-box scenarios. Open questions include whether the alignment-based perturbation generalizes to physical-world attacks and whether targeted defenses (e.g., feature-space regularization in the vision encoder) can neutralize the SVD-subspace constraint.
