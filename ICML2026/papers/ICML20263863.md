## Problem

Masked diffusion language models degrade sharply when generating multiple tokens in parallel, because token-level cross-entropy training objectives only model marginal distributions while parallel decoding implicitly assumes conditional independence across positions. This mismatch between marginal optimality during training and joint sequence consistency during multi-token generation produces outputs that are locally plausible but globally incoherent, a failure mode that persists even with valid and diverse training data.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and produces a generated text sequence (up to 512 tokens) through a two-phase pipeline: mask diffusion first unmasking all positions in parallel to yield a complete coarse draft, followed by edit diffusion that applies localized replacement, deletion, and insertion operations. The total diffusion step budget is fixed and split between the two phases (e.g., 48 mask + 16 edit steps out of 64 total), and the edit phase terminates early when the model predicts an empty edit action, making the effective number of refinement steps adaptive per sample.

## Method

ME-DLM augments standard masked diffusion with a lightweight edit-based refinement stage. After parallel unmasking produces a complete draft, the model iteratively predicts token-wise edit pairs (c_i, n_i) — where c_i is a replacement token or [DEL] and n_i is a candidate insertion — conditioned on the full current sequence, then applies them through a deterministic left-to-right edit operator that couples decisions across positions. Training supervision is derived from the minimal edit script (via edit distance) between the model's own generated draft and the ground-truth target, providing a deterministic signal under a fixed canonicalization scheme that enforces the smallest possible modification set. A curriculum over diffusion depth m gradually increases the number of edit steps the model must learn to perform, and the model is trained on its own generation errors to align training with the inference-time distribution. Although edit predictions are factorized per token, the shared conditioning on the full sequence and the deterministic application operator induce effective sequence-level dependencies, enabling targeted correction of globally inconsistent outputs without altering the underlying diffusion generation process.

## Results

At the most aggressive parallel decoding budget (1/8, i.e., 64 total diffusion steps), ME-DLM Stage-3 achieves 25.0 on HumanEval and 63.8 on GSM8K, yielding gains of +11.6 and +33.6 points respectively over the Stage-2 baseline. The average improvement over Stage-2 grows monotonically as the budget shrinks: from +4.3 at 1/1 to +13.3 at 1/8, confirming that edit-based refinement is most effective under high-parallelism, low-step regimes. On general benchmarks (MMLU, ARC-C, TruthfulQA), Stage-3 improves MMLU from 21.7 to 56.2 and ARC-C from 26.0 to 69.2 at the 1/8 budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LLaDA-Instruct (the base masked diffusion model), ME-DLM Stage-3 wins across every budget and dataset: at 1/8 the average score rises from 20.9 to 32.6 (+11.7), and on GSM8K from 50.3 to 63.8. Against Soft Mask, ME-DLM Stage-3 leads by 3.3 points on GSM8K at 1/2 (82.7 vs 79.4) and by 14.3 points at 1/4 (76.6 vs 62.3), with the gap widening as parallelism increases. Against EvoToken, the advantage is smaller but consistent: +0.9 on GSM8K at 1/2 (82.7 vs 81.8) and +4.3 at 1/4 (76.6 vs 72.3). Notably, both Soft Mask and EvoToken report no code-generation results, so the comparison on HumanEval/MBPP is only against LLaDA-Instruct, where ME-DLM Stage-3 leads by 12.8 points at 1/8 (25.0 vs 12.2). The consistent widening of the performance gap as the budget decreases indicates that edit-based refinement becomes increasingly critical under aggressive parallel decoding, where marginal token predictions alone produce globally inconsistent sequences.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the masked diffusion language model (MDLM) paradigm established by Sahoo et al. (2024) and scaled by LLaDA (Nie et al., 2025), which trains with token-level denoising objectives and decodes via iterative parallel unmasking. It departs from prior parallel-decoding improvements—soft masks (Hersche et al., 2025), progressive token evolution (Zhong et al., 2026), and remasking strategies—by reframing the post-unmasking phase as an explicit edit-based refinement process supervised by edit distance, rather than modifying the masking or unmasking mechanism itself. The approach is conceptually related to edit-based sequence-to-sequence models (Gu et al., 2019; Reid et al., 2022) and coarse-to-refine generation (speculative decoding, Medusa), but is the first to integrate minimal-edit correction as a native diffusion step conditioned on the full generated sequence under a fixed step budget.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a concrete failure mode of parallel diffusion decoding—the gap between marginal token accuracy and joint sequence validity—that has been empirically observed but not previously formalized or corrected with a lightweight, training-compatible mechanism. The substantial gains at aggressive parallelism (1/8 budget) with negligible inference overhead make this practically relevant for latency-sensitive deployment of diffusion LMs. Open questions include whether the edit-distance supervision can be replaced by learned or training-free consistency checks, whether the approach transfers to other diffusion architectures beyond LLaDA, and whether the three-stage training recipe can be simplified. A limitation is that the method still requires an additional training stage and a fixed step-allocation heuristic between mask and edit phases.
