## Problem

Existing anchor-based multi-view clustering methods rely on a single static anchor set, decoupling anchor selection from graph learning and assuming a fixed representation granularity. This overlooks the hierarchical abstraction process by which data is progressively organized from fine-grained local details to coarse-grained semantic concepts, and produces opaque, non-inspectable clustering decisions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261643/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes M multi-view feature matrices X_t ∈ R^{d_t×n} (with varying dimensions d_t per view), a target number of clusters k, a hierarchy depth L (typically 1–4), and per-layer anchor counts {c_l} satisfying c_1 > c_2 > … > c_L ≥ k. It outputs cluster labels via k-means on the left singular vectors of the final n × c_L consensus bipartite graph, along with the full hierarchy of learned anchor matrices V_t^(l) and cross-layer assignment graphs U^(l). Crucially, all anchors at every level remain in the original feature space rather than being projected into a learned latent space, making the abstraction path explicitly inspectable.

## Method

HAG-MVC organizes multi-view data into an L-level pyramid of anchor sets with decreasing cardinality. The first layer constructs a shared sample-to-anchor bipartite graph across all views, while each subsequent layer learns view-specific anchors and an anchor-to-anchor graph connecting the (l−1)-th to the l-th layer, encoding higher-level structural dependencies. Anchors and graphs are jointly refined through an alternating optimization scheme: assignment matrices U^(l) are updated via column-wise quadratic programming under non-negativity and column-stochasticity constraints, while anchor matrices V_t^(l) are updated in closed form via least-squares. The final consensus graph U = ∏U^(l)ᵀ is factorized by SVD, and k-means on the resulting embedding yields cluster labels. This multi-level co-evolution mechanism, combined with the constraint that all anchors reside in the original feature space, provides both hierarchical semantic abstraction and inherent interpretability at linear O(n) computational cost.

## Results

HAG-MVC achieves the best ACC on all 12 benchmark datasets, with improvements of 0.13%–15.24% over the second-best method. Notable gains include +9.60% on PIE (0.6028 vs. 0.5500), +15.24% on Reuters (0.6035 vs. 0.4623), and +11.03% on YoutubeFace (0.2858 vs. 0.2574). On NMI, the method also leads on all 12 datasets, with the largest margin on ForestTypes (0.6036 vs. 0.5476) and PIE (0.8048 vs. 0.7636). The method maintains linear O(n) time complexity, scaling to 101K-sample YoutubeFace where several baselines fail (OOM).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HAG-MVC outperforms all 11 baselines on ACC across every dataset where comparisons are possible. Against the strongest single-layer anchor methods (FMVACC, FastMICE, UDBGL), the gains are most pronounced on high-dimensional or large-scale data: +15.24% on Reuters (0.6035 vs. 0.4623 MVCAGAF) and +11.03% on YoutubeFace (0.2858 vs. 0.2574 HBG-MVSC). Against the closest competitor on medium-scale data, FastMICE leads on MFeat (0.9319 vs. 0.8640) and COIL100 (0.7811 vs. 0.7801, a near-tie), while Orth-NTF is the runner-up on HW (0.9318 vs. 0.9220) and PIE (0.6028 vs. 0.5500). The hierarchical co-evolution design proves most critical on datasets with complex multi-view structure (PIE, Reuters, YTF), where static single-layer anchors fail to capture cross-view consensus. Several baselines (SMVSC, UDBGL, Orth-NTF, MVSC-HFD) cannot run on Reuters or YoutubeFace due to memory constraints, while HAG-MVC handles all 12 datasets within linear O(n) complexity.

## Relation to Prior Work

HAG-MVC extends the hierarchical anchor graph concept originally introduced for semi-supervised learning (Wang et al., 2017) and later adapted to unsupervised single-view clustering (Yang et al., 2020) into the multi-view setting with a key departure: instead of fixing anchors via k-means at each layer (as in HBG-MVSC, Zhou et al., 2025), it jointly co-evolves anchors and inter-layer graphs through alternating optimization. The SVD-based spectral clustering step inherits directly from the anchor-graph framework of Kang et al. (2020) and Wang et al. (2024), while the multi-view fusion is handled natively within the hierarchical objective rather than through post-hoc graph fusion. The paper thus carves a middle path between static single-layer anchor methods and opaque deep clustering, positioning hierarchical co-evolution as a transparent alternative.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The interpretability guarantee—keeping all anchors in the original feature space so that the abstraction pyramid is explicitly inspectable—addresses a real gap for trustworthy AI applications where black-box deep clustering is unacceptable. The linear O(n) scalability demonstrated on datasets up to 101K samples (YoutubeFace) makes the approach practically viable for large-scale multi-view settings where full-graph methods are infeasible. However, the fixed hierarchy depth (L≤4) and the reliance on k-means initialization for the first-layer anchors remain limitations; adaptive depth selection and data-driven anchor initialization are acknowledged as open challenges that could further strengthen the framework.
