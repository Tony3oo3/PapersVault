## Problem

Simulating long-time Hamiltonian dynamics is bottlenecked by the small timesteps required for numerical stability, and existing approaches to learn large-timestep integrators depend on expensive reference trajectories or teacher models for training. This paper asks whether accurate large-timestep Hamiltonian flow maps can be learned directly from single-time phase-space samples with instantaneous force labels, without any trajectory generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263876/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes molecular positions x ∈ ℝ^{dN}, momenta p ∈ ℝ^{dN}, a continuous timestep Δt ∈ [0, Δt_max], and atom types/masses, and outputs mean velocities v̄ and mean forces f̄ over the interval. Since standard MLFF datasets provide only (x, f) pairs, momenta are sampled independently from Maxwell-Boltzmann distributions at varying temperatures during training. The predicted mean displacement field, when multiplied by Δt, yields the full phase-space update (x_{t+Δt}, p_{t+Δt}), and at Δt = 0 the force component reduces to a standard MLFF force prediction.

## Method

The core contribution is a trajectory-free consistency condition derived by adapting the Mean Flow formalism to deterministic Hamiltonian dynamics. They define a startpoint-conditioned mean displacement field ū(x, p, Δt) = (1/Δt)∫[v_τ, f_τ]dτ, then differentiate to obtain an integral-free identity: ū = [v, f] + Δt[(v·∂_x)ū + (f·∂_p)ū − ∂_{Δt}ū], which any valid mean displacement field must satisfy. Training minimizes the squared error between the network prediction and this self-consistent target, computed efficiently via a single Jacobian-vector product (JVP) with stop-gradient on the target, adding only ~30% overhead over standard MLFF training. The architecture is a Euclidean transformer (Frank et al., 2025) with a shared backbone for velocity and force heads, and inference applies three lightweight filters—random rotation for approximate equivariance, center-of-mass drift removal, and a coupled closed-form correction for simultaneous energy and angular momentum conservation.

## Results

On the MD17 NVT benchmark (300 ps, Langevin thermostat), the HFM at Δt=0.5 fs matches or slightly beats the MLFF+VV baseline on interatomic distance MAE (e.g., Aspirin: 0.026 vs. 0.030; Salicylic Acid: 0.035 vs. 0.035), while maintaining reasonable accuracy up to Δt=9 fs (Aspirin: 0.046; Naphthalene: 0.063). On the gravitational 100-body system, the HFM requires 4× fewer integration steps than Velocity Verlet to reach the same MSE. For coarse-grained Chignolin, the HFM achieves a >20× speedup (Δt=45 fs vs. 0.5 fs reference) while recovering correct transition rates, and for alanine dipeptide it produces accurate free energy surfaces at Δt=12 fs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is a standard SO(3)-equivariant MLFF integrated with Velocity Verlet at Δt=0.5 fs. At matched timesteps (0.5 fs), the HFM achieves comparable or slightly better distance-distribution MAE on 3 of 4 MD17 molecules (Aspirin: 0.026 vs. 0.030; Salicylic Acid: 0.035 vs. 0.035; Naphthalene: 0.044 vs. 0.043), while the key advantage emerges at larger timesteps where the HFM remains stable up to 9–12 fs and the VV baseline becomes unreliable beyond ~2–4 fs. Against ScoreMD (Plainer et al., 2025), a diffusion-based generative approach, the HFM clearly wins on distributional fidelity: when trained on biased paracetamol data missing a conformational mode, the HFM recovers the full reference free-energy landscape during NVT rollouts, whereas ScoreMD reproduces the biased training distribution in both iid-sampling and simulation modes. On the gravitational N-body benchmark, the HFM outperforms VV integration of the ground-truth potential by a factor of 4 in the number of steps needed to reach equivalent MSE, with VV diverging at coarse discretizations while the HFM remains physically consistent.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the Mean Flow consistency framework (Geng et al., 2025a) and self-distillation formalism (Boffi et al., 2025a) from stochastic generative modeling to deterministic Hamiltonian phase-space dynamics. It departs from prior large-timestep MD methods (FlashMD, Thiemann et al., TimeWarp) that require expensive reference trajectories for training, by deriving a trajectory-free consistency condition that only needs instantaneous force labels. The approach also contrasts with generative equilibrium-sampling methods (ScoreMD, Boltzmann generators) by remaining purely deterministic and preserving fine-grained kinetic information rather than targeting stationary distributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for trajectory generation, this method can be trained directly on widely available MLFF datasets at comparable cost to standard force field training, removing a major practical bottleneck for large-timestep MD. The demonstrated 20× speedup on coarse-grained proteins and stable 12 fs rollouts on alanine dipeptide are practically significant for drug discovery and biophysics. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-separable Hamiltonians, how to push timesteps further in strongly chaotic regimes without instability, and whether the approximate conservation (requiring inference filters) can be made exact through architectural constraints.
