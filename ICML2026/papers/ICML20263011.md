## Problem

Stereo inpainting of disocclusions in DIBR-based monocular-to-stereo conversion is the critical bottleneck, but training data is either scarce (real stereo pairs with error-prone stereo matching) or suffers from Sim-to-Real domain gaps (synthetic renders). No ready-to-use dataset with geometrically consistent disocclusion masks exists, and naive cycle-consistency self-supervision is computationally infeasible due to non-differentiable warping and multiple sequential model inferences per training step.

## Input / Output

The inpainting network receives a 4-channel input (masked RGB with disoccluded pixels zeroed out, concatenated with the binary disocclusion mask) and outputs a 3-channel RGB completion of the stereoscopic target view. Disocclusion masks are precomputed offline via GRT from a single disparity estimate (Depth Anything V2-Large, rescaled to [0, αW] with α=0.1 for training, α=0.06 for evaluation) and reused across training epochs. Image models operate at 256×256 (training) and 512×512 (evaluation); video models process local sequences of length 10–20 at 432×240.

## Method

The core contribution is the Geometric Reciprocity Theorem (GRT), which proves that under nearest-neighbor DIBR warping, the disocclusion mask M_dis^{L→R} for synthesizing a target view equals the mask of pixels lost when warping back from target to source (M_lost^{R→L}). The proof eliminates three computational steps—left-view inpainting, left-view disparity re-estimation, and explicit warping—by showing that inpainted content is geometrically irrelevant, transferred disparity suffices for non-disoccluded pixels, and round-trip warping returns valid pixels to their exact origins. This reduces mask computation to two analytical checks (boundary violation and depth-buffer occlusion) on the disparity map alone, enabling any monocular image to serve as its own ground truth with train-inference consistent masks. The inpainting backbones are LaMa (FFC-based CNN, 27M params) for images and ProPainter (recurrent flow completion + sparse video transformer) for video, fine-tuned on GRT-derived triplets from ImageNet-GRT (~1.28M images) and Kinetics-GRT (~240K videos) with L1 + perceptual/adversarial losses.

## Results

On DAVIS-GRT (3,455 frames), Ours-Image achieves 35.52 PSNR / 0.9800 SSIM / 0.0129 LPIPS, and Ours-Video achieves 34.06 PSNR / 0.9733 SSIM / 0.0210 LPIPS with 0.9770 CTC, both substantially outperforming all baselines. Inference is 0.05 s/frame (image) and 0.24 s/frame (video), making it 208× faster than StereoDiffusion and 12.5× faster than SVG. On the full-pipeline Inria 3DMovie benchmark, Ours-Video reaches SIoU 0.2516 and MEt3R 0.0973, indicating markedly better geometric consistency and viewing comfort than prior video methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three image baselines (Mono2Stereo, ZeroStereo, StereoDiffusion) and two video baselines (SVG, StereoCrafter). Ours-Image wins all image baselines by a large margin: +5.75 dB PSNR over StereoDiffusion (29.77→35.52), +7.02 dB over Mono2Stereo, and is 208× faster than StereoDiffusion (0.05 s vs 10.4 s per frame). For video, Ours-Video surpasses StereoCrafter by +5.11 dB PSNR (28.95→34.06) and SVG by +6.73 dB, while also achieving the best CTC (0.9770) and running 12.5× faster than SVG. On the full-pipeline Inria 3DMovie evaluation, Ours-Video improves SIoU from 0.2201 (StereoCrafter) to 0.2516 and nearly halves MEt3R from 0.1961 to 0.0973, confirming superior stereoscopic viewing comfort. The paper further shows that GRT fine-tuning benefits diverse backbones (LaMa, SD1.5/2.1/XL, ProPainter, StereoCrafter), with even purpose-built StereoCrafter gaining +1.90 dB PSNR after GRT fine-tuning, validating the data-quality advantage over existing stereo-pair collection strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DIBR pipeline for monocular-to-stereo conversion (depth estimation → warping → inpainting) and the cycle-consistency paradigm from CycleGAN and temporal cycle-consistency learning, but departs fundamentally by proving that the cycle is analytically redundant under nearest-neighbor warping. Where prior training-based methods (StereoCrafter, ReStereo, M2SVid) rely on error-prone stereo-pair alignment or domain-gapped synthetic data, and training-free methods (StereoDiffusion, SVG) lack stereo-specific geometric priors, the Geometric Reciprocity Theorem enables self-supervised training from arbitrary monocular videos with train-inference mask consistency. The inpainting backbones (LaMa, ProPainter) and depth estimator (Depth Anything V2) are adopted from prior work; the novelty is entirely in the data construction principle and the theoretical result that eliminates the need to execute the cycle.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GRT resolves the central data bottleneck in stereo inpainting by showing that inference-identical disocclusion masks can be computed from a single depth estimate, making high-quality training data as scalable as monocular video itself. The released datasets (ImageNet-GRT, Kinetics-GRT, DAVIS-GRT) and the architecture-agnostic fine-tuning recipe (validated on LaMa, SD1.5/2.1/XL, ProPainter, StereoCrafter) provide a practical path for the community. Key limitations remain: the theorem assumes rectified stereo geometry and nearest-neighbor warping, and mask quality is bounded by depth-estimation accuracy, which degrades on transparent, reflective, or highly textured surfaces. Open questions include whether the renderer-level extension to bilinear warping can close the small train-test gap, and whether GRT generalizes to non-rectified or multi-view (beyond stereo) settings.
