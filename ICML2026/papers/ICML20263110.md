## Problem

Autoregressive (AR) video diffusion models suffer from quadratic attention complexity that dominates runtime, especially in later chunks where the KV cache grows linearly. Existing sparse attention methods designed for bidirectional video diffusion models degrade significantly when applied to AR models because they ignore the heterogeneous error accumulation across chunks and discard informative historical context. This paper presents the first sparse attention solution specifically tailored to the chunk-by-chunk AR generation paradigm.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263110/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and produces a generated video clip (5s or 15s at 16 FPS, 512×768 resolution) by accelerating the inference of existing AR video diffusion models (Self Forcing, LongLive, Infinite-Forcing). Generation proceeds chunk-by-chunk with 3 latent frames per chunk, where each chunk is denoised in a few steps conditioned on the KV cache of all prior clean chunks. The method is plug-and-play: it replaces the dense attention kernel with a sparse one and optionally adds FP8 linear layers, fused kernels, and LightVAE for further speedup.

## Method

LIGHT FORCING combines two complementary mechanisms. Chunk-Aware Growth (CAG) allocates the sparsity budget across chunks using a theoretical TV error bound: earlier chunks receive lower sparsity (denser attention) to preserve the visual anchor, while later chunks tolerate progressively higher sparsity since they inherit structured priors from preceding chunks; the per-chunk ratio is s_i = s_base − α_i·β with α_i ∝ 1/√T. Hierarchical Sparse Attention (HSA) performs coarse-to-fine mask selection: first, framewise-compressed keys retrieve a small set of relevant historical frames per query block, then blockwise-compressed keys select critical blocks within those frames, yielding a two-level (frame + block) sparse mask that maintains fixed computational complexity independent of total historical length while preserving long-range temporal context. Together, CAG governs macro-level sparsity allocation and HSA determines fine-grained which historical information each block attends to, achieving 1.3× attention speedup and 2–3× end-to-end speedup while matching or exceeding dense-attention quality (84.5 vs. 84.1 on VBench).

## Results

On Self Forcing 1.3B (fps=16), LIGHT FORCING achieves a VBench Total Score of 84.5 with a 1.30× end-to-end speedup (7.39s latency), surpassing the dense FlashAttention2 baseline (84.1, 9.61s) and all sparse baselines. On LongLive 1.3B, it reaches 83.9 Total Score with 1.19× speedup (8.81s), again exceeding dense attention (83.2, 10.47s). Combined with FP8 linear layers, kernel fusion, and LightVAE, the full deployment stack achieves 2.0–3.0× end-to-end speedup, reaching 27.4 FPS on RTX 5090 and 33.9 FPS on H100 for real-time video generation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LIGHT FORCING outperforms all six sparse attention baselines on Total Score for both Self Forcing and LongLive, and notably surpasses the dense FlashAttention2 baseline (84.5 vs. 84.1 on Self Forcing; 83.9 vs. 83.2 on LongLive), demonstrating that properly designed sparsity can be lossless. Among sparse methods, STA (83.6) and VMoBA (83.6) are the closest competitors on Self Forcing but still trail by 0.9 points; Radial Attention suffers severe quality collapse (73.7 Total Score, 45.8 Aesthetic Quality) due to its energy-decay pattern being ill-suited to AR chunk generation. SVG2 is the only method that is actually slower than dense attention (0.45× speedup) due to repeated K-means re-initialization as the KV cache grows. On LongLive, the gap widens dramatically: VMoBA drops to 71.3 Total Score (Subject Consistency collapses to 58.3) while LIGHT FORCING maintains 83.9. In the efficient deployment comparison (Table 8), LIGHT FORCING also beats feature-caching (TeaCache) and token-reduction (DyCoke) methods on all reported VBench metrics at comparable speedup (1.30×).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the autoregressive video diffusion paradigm established by Self Forcing, LongLive, and Infinite-Forcing, which generate video chunk-by-chunk using few-step denoising conditioned on a growing KV cache. It departs from the large body of sparse attention work (STA, Radial, SVG2, VMoBA, SLA) designed for bidirectional video diffusion models, identifying that these chunk-agnostic policies fail on AR models due to progressive error accumulation and insufficient historical context utilization. The two proposed mechanisms—Chunk-Aware Growth (grounded in diffusion sampling error bounds from Li et al., 2023) and Hierarchical Sparse Attention (coarse-to-fine frame-then-block selection)—are specifically tailored to the AR generation structure, representing the first sparse attention solution purpose-built for this setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LIGHT FORCING enables real-time video generation (27.4 FPS) on a consumer-grade GPU (RTX 5090) for the first time, which has direct implications for interactive applications like game simulation and robot learning. The finding that properly structured sparsity can match or slightly exceed dense attention quality suggests substantial redundancy in current AR video diffusion attention, opening the door to more aggressive compression. Key limitations include evaluation only on 1.3B models (scaling to 14B remains open), residual artifacts in a small fraction of long-video samples, and the fact that the core sparse-attention speedup is modest (~1.3×) with the larger 2–3× gains coming from standard deployment techniques (FP8, kernel fusion, LightVAE).
