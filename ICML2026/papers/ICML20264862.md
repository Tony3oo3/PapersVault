## Problem

Current LLM architectures are fundamentally memory-based, predicting the next token by retrieving from stored context (System 1) without a dedicated mechanism for goal-directed, multi-step planning (System 2). While reinforcement learning and test-time training partially address this gap, they remain external to the model's forward computation, leaving planning decoupled from the core inference mechanism. The paper asks whether optimal control can be internalized as a native architectural component that enables planning before prediction.

## Input / Output

The TTC layer receives a context-encoded latent state h_0 (projected from attention outputs) and produces the first-step optimal control action u_1* as the next-token representation, which is added back to the residual stream. LQR parameters (A_t, B_t, Q_t, R_t) are not fixed but synthesized on-the-fly from h_0 via time-modulated power series with exponential decay coefficients, making the planning problem context-adaptive. The planning horizon T is a free parameter that can be extended at test time (trained up to T=32, evaluated up to T=64) without retraining, enabling test-time compute scaling.

## Method

The TTC layer formulates next-token prediction as a finite-horizon Linear-Quadratic Regulator (LQR) over latent states, where the first-step optimal action u_1* is decoded as the token representation. To make this tractable at scale, the paper derives a symplectic iteration solver (Theorem 3.3) that replaces the sequential Riccati recursion's O(T) dense matrix inversions with a single inversion plus a cumulative product of symplectic matrices whose per-step inversions are fully parallelizable, achieving >10× throughput over classical solvers. Backpropagation through the TTC layer is handled by solving a dual LQR (zero initial state, affine cost from upstream gradient) and combining primal/dual KKT solutions, yielding closed-form gradients for all LQR parameters. In the hybrid TTC-Net architecture, TTC layers are inserted between attention and MLP every 8 transformer blocks, with A_t and R_t parameterized as diagonal matrices to reduce inversions to O(1), and the entire solver fused into a single CUDA kernel. The model is trained via supervised fine-tuning on pretrained LLMs (Llama-3-7B) with zero-initialized output projections, treating the TTC layers as adapters that learn latent world-modeling dynamics and reward structures through imitation.

## Results

On MATH-500, TTC-Net (inserted as an adapter into Llama-3-Instruct-7B) achieves 52.80% Acc@8, a +27.8 percentage-point improvement over the base model (25.00%) and +6.0 points over full fine-tuning without new modules (46.80%). On the harder AIME benchmarks, TTC-Net achieves 20.00% Pass@8 on both AIME 2024 and AIME 2025, roughly 3× the best memory-based adapter baseline (RetNet at 13.33% on AIME24) and 3× full fine-tuning (6.67% on AIME24), while the base model scores 0%. On AMC, TTC-Net reaches 54.22% Pass@8 versus 46.98% for full fine-tuning and 44.58% for the best SSM adapter (+Mamba).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TTC-Net outperforms all seven adapter baselines and full fine-tuning across every benchmark. On MATH-500, the closest competitor is +GDN (47.80%), trailing TTC-Net by 5.0 points; +MesaNet (47.40%) and +Attention (47.00%) follow. On AIME 2024 Pass@8, TTC-Net (20.00%) roughly triples the best memory-based adapter (+RetNet at 13.33%) and triples full fine-tuning (6.67%), while +Mamba, +GDN, and +Attention all score only 3.33%. On AIME 2025, TTC-Net is the only method to achieve non-trivial Pass@8 (20.00%), with +Attention reaching 6.67% and all other adapters at 0–3.33%. On the Sudoku task (Table 1), TTC-Net also leads all sequential-modeling baselines (Transformer, Mamba, Mamba2, GDN, Samba) by 2.8–6.7 points in single-step board accuracy and 2.7–3.1 points in multi-step board accuracy, confirming gains beyond math reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant memory-based paradigm (attention, SSMs, linear RNNs) that the authors frame as 'System 1' associative retrieval, and from test-time training architectures (Titans, Atlas, MesaNet) that interpret inference as self-supervised regression over past context. Instead, it draws on differentiable optimization layers (OptNet, Differentiable MPC) and classical LQR theory to internalize goal-directed planning as an architectural component, while leveraging the symplectic structure of LQR dynamics to derive a hardware-efficient solver that replaces sequential Riccati recursion with parallelizable matrix products. The work also positions itself against RL-based reasoning (DeepSeek-R1, RL pretraining) by arguing that reward-driven optimization remains decoupled from the forward computation, whereas TTC embeds a value function and world model directly within each layer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a unifying reframing of test-time learning as structured decision-making rather than memorization or parameter adaptation, integrating world modeling, model-based RL, and long-horizon planning into a single architectural primitive that can be inserted as a lightweight adapter into pretrained LLMs. The 10× throughput gain from the symplectic-iteration CUDA solver addresses a key scalability bottleneck that has kept optimal control out of large-scale sequence models. Open questions remain: how multiple TTC layers interact in deep stacks, whether more expressive (non-linear) dynamics and reward models improve reasoning, and whether the gains observed on a 7B backbone persist at larger scales or across all training stages. The paper also acknowledges that the theoretical analysis of a single TTC layer does not yet extend to the emergent behavior of stacked layers.
