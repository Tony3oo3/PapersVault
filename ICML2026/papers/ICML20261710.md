## Problem

Existing sample-efficient RL algorithms for low-rank MDPs rely on policy planning or constrained planning oracles that are computationally intractable (NP-hard or requiring exponentially many supervised learning calls). This paper asks whether one can design an algorithm that is simultaneously sample-efficient and computationally efficient, relying only on supervised learning as the basic computational primitive, and establishes a formal oracle-complexity hierarchy showing that policy evaluation is the weakest sufficient oracle.

## Input / Output

The algorithm operates in an episodic low-rank MDP with continuous state space, finite action space, and horizon H, where the transition kernel admits a low-rank factorization T(s'|s,a) = ⟨φ(s,a), μ(s')⟩ with unknown latent features. At each iteration it consumes H exploratory trajectories (with uniform random actions at the target step) and produces an ε-optimal policy as a uniform mixture over all K iterates. The sampling oracle ρ must satisfy a coverage condition relative to the optimal policy's occupancy measure, and the model class Θ = Φ × �si is assumed finite for concentration arguments.

## Method

Opt-AC is an optimistic actor-critic algorithm whose entire computation reduces to supervised learning oracle calls. Each iteration performs: (i) approximate maximum-likelihood estimation of the low-rank transition model (one SL call), (ii) construction of an exploration bonus b̂ = 3H·min{α‖φ̂(s,a)‖_{Λ̂⁻¹}, 1} from the empirical Gram matrix, (iii) policy evaluation of the optimistic Q-function under the augmented reward r + b̂ (one PE call, reducible to a single convex smooth regression), and (iv) a KL-regularized mirror-descent policy update with closed-form softmax solution. The key theoretical insight is that the bonus coefficient α = Õ(√|A|) (rather than Õ(√(d²+|A|)) used in prior work) combined with direct control of the empirical Gram matrix yields a d³ dependence instead of d⁴, improving sample complexity by a factor of d over RAFFLE. The algorithm achieves Õ(H⁵d³|A|²log|Θ|/ε²) sample complexity with SL^{2K}(1/K) computational complexity.

## Results

On six MuJoCo continuous-control benchmarks, cRFFSAC+Bonus achieves the best final return on HalfCheetah (6454±479 vs. SAC's 4994±99) and Reacher (−3.86±0.18 vs. −4.81±0.19), while cRFFSAC (without bonus) leads on Ant (2701±663 vs. 2081±465) and Hopper (1449±598 vs. 1316±740). On Pendulum and InvertedPendulum, all three methods are statistically indistinguishable. The theoretical contribution is a sample complexity of Õ(H⁵d³|A|² log|Θ|/ε²), improving over the prior best (RAFFLE) by a factor of d, while requiring only a policy-evaluation oracle implementable via supervised learning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole empirical baseline is Soft Actor-Critic (SAC), a strong off-policy continuous-control method. cRFFSAC (the actor-critic variant without an explicit exploration bonus) outperforms SAC on Ant by ~30% (2701 vs. 2081) and on Hopper by ~10% (1449 vs. 1316), while matching SAC on Pendulum and InvertedPendulum. The full cRFFSAC+Bonus variant further improves on HalfCheetah (+29% over SAC: 6454 vs. 4994) and Reacher (+20%: −3.86 vs. −4.81), confirming that the optimism bonus derived from the learned transition model aids exploration in sparse-reward settings. However, the bonus does not uniformly help: on Ant, cRFFSAC+Bonus (1910) underperforms both cRFFSAC (2701) and SAC (2081), suggesting the bonus magnitude may be environment-sensitive. Overall, the paper demonstrates that a policy-evaluation-only actor-critic design can match or exceed a well-tuned SAC baseline across most tasks, supporting the practical viability of avoiding planning or constrained-planning oracles.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the low-rank MDP framework introduced by Agarwal et al. (2020) and the random Fourier feature construction of Rahimi & Recht (2007), but departs fundamentally from prior algorithms (FLAMBE, REP-UCB, RAFFLE, SpanRL, MOFFLE) that all rely on policy planning or constrained planning oracles. The key conceptual departure is establishing a strict oracle-complexity hierarchy—policy evaluation < policy planning < constrained planning—under low-rank MDPs, showing that the weakest oracle (policy evaluation, reducible to a single supervised learning call) suffices for provably efficient RL. The proposed Opt-AC algorithm replaces the UCB-style backward planning loop with a KL-regularized mirror-descent actor update, making every computational step implementable via standard regression.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work bridges a long-standing gap between sample-complexity guarantees and computational feasibility in RL with function approximation, showing that the computational barrier can be broken by restricting to the weakest reasonable oracle. The cRFF construction (Theorem 6.1) provides a concrete, verifiable pathway from smooth transition kernels to approximate low-rank representations, grounding the theory in practical environments. Open questions include whether oracle lower bounds can be established to confirm the hierarchy is tight, whether the d^3 sample complexity is minimax-optimal, and whether the approach extends to partially observable or continuous-action settings. The empirical validation, while competitive with SAC, is limited to six MuJoCo tasks with four seeds, leaving open whether the theoretical advantages translate to larger-scale or more complex environments.
