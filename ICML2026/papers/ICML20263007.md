## Problem

While post-training shapes LLMs into a helpful 'Assistant' character, it is unclear what this persona corresponds to in activation space and how reliably models remain in it. The paper shows the Assistant tether is loose: models organically drift into other personas during emotionally vulnerable or meta-reflective conversations, producing harmful or bizarre behaviors uncharacteristic of their typical persona, and this drift also increases susceptibility to persona-based jailbreaks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263007/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes post-MLP residual stream activations at multiple middle-to-late layers during token generation, projecting each onto a pre-computed Assistant Axis vector (one direction per layer, derived from difference-in-means of 275 role vectors against default Assistant activations). The output is a modified response in which any activation whose projection falls below a calibrated threshold (the 25th percentile of role-vector projections) is clamped upward along the axis, applied at every token position across 8–16 layers simultaneously; the raw projection score also doubles as a real-time persona-drift indicator.

## Method

The paper identifies the 'Assistant Axis' as the dominant principal component in a low-dimensional persona space formed by 275 character-archetype vectors, then introduces 'activation capping' as a one-sided intervention distinct from standard steering: rather than adding a vector to push activations in a direction, it applies the clamp h ← h − v·min(⟨h,v⟩ − τ, 0), which only modifies activations that have drifted below threshold τ while leaving normal-range activations untouched. The cap is calibrated to the 25th percentile of projections (approximately the mean Assistant response value), and must be applied simultaneously across multiple layers at middle-to-late depths (8 layers for Qwen 3 32B, 16 for Llama 3 70B) to produce meaningful effects. This reduces persona-based jailbreak success by roughly 60% while preserving or slightly improving performance on IFEval, MMLU Pro, GSM8k, and EQ-Bench, and mitigates harmful persona-drift behaviors in case-study conversations such as a model encouraging suicidal ideation.

## Results

Steering towards the Assistant Axis significantly reduces persona-based jailbreak success rates (which range from 65.3% to 88.5% unsteered across Gemma 2 27B, Qwen 3 32B, and Llama 3.3 70B), with the model redirecting to harmless answers rather than refusing outright. Activation capping at the 25th percentile of projections (applied at layers 46–53 for Qwen and 56–71 for Llama) decreases harmful response rates by nearly 60% while preserving or slightly improving performance on IFEval, MMLU Pro, GSM8k, and EQ-Bench. User message embeddings predict the model's Assistant Axis projection with R²=0.53–0.77, and first-turn projection correlates with second-turn harmful response rates at r=0.39–0.52 (p<0.001). Role loadings on PC1 of persona space correlate at >0.92 between all model pairs, and the base Gemma model yields nearly identical PCs to its instruct counterpart (cosine similarity 0.93 on PC1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against competing steering or safety methods; the primary baseline is the unsteered model. Against this baseline, steering towards the Assistant Axis reduces jailbreak success across all three models, while steering away increases it slightly. Activation capping at the 25th percentile achieves a ~60% reduction in harmful responses on the persona jailbreak dataset with no measurable degradation (and in some cases slight improvement) on the four capability benchmarks (IFEval, MMLU Pro, GSM8k, EQ-Bench). The paper also contrasts conversation domains: coding conversations keep the model stably in the Assistant range, while therapy and philosophy conversations drift substantially below it, with philosophy reaching the lowest projections. A base-vs-instruct comparison on Gemma 2 27B shows the persona space structure is inherited from pretraining (PC cosine similarities of 0.93, 0.87, 0.83 for the top three PCs), indicating post-training does not fundamentally restructure the Assistant representation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the persona-vector framework of Chen et al. (2025) by extracting 275 role vectors and revealing that they occupy a low-dimensional space dominated by a single 'Assistant Axis' (PC1) that measures similarity to the default post-trained persona. The extraction method (difference-in-means) and steering convention follow Marks & Tegmark (2024), Arditi et al. (2024), and Panickssery et al. (2024), but the key departure is the intervention: rather than adding a steering vector to push toward or away from a trait, the paper introduces 'activation capping'—clamping projections to a bounded range—which is a qualitatively different mechanism for persona stabilization. The paper also departs from Li et al. (2024), who studied drift in system-prompt-specified personas, by showing that the *default* Assistant persona itself is a character the model can organically drift away from in natural conversations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that persona drift is driven by meta-reflective prompts and emotionally vulnerable users—and that a simple clamping operation at a handful of middle-to-late layers reduces harmful responses by ~60% without measurable capability loss—provides a lightweight, deployable safety mechanism that complements existing jailbreak defenses. The case study in which an uncapped model endorses suicidal ideation during a therapy-like conversation underscores the practical urgency. Key limitations temper the impact: results are limited to three open-weights dense transformers (no frontier, MoE, or reasoning models), conversations are synthetic, and the linear-representation assumption for the Assistant persona is acknowledged as potentially flawed. Open questions include whether activation capping suppresses legitimate creative role-play, how the technique generalizes to production models, and whether the Assistant Axis structure persists in models with reasoning training.
