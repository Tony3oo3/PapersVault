## Problem

Streaming video LLMs that maintain bounded memory via token merging or discarding suffer cumulative information loss over long durations, often underperforming offline models on videos exceeding 30 minutes. No prior streaming video LLM has used test-time training to continuously consolidate multimodal representations into parameter-based long-term memory, and no existing benchmark explicitly evaluates whether a model can learn procedural knowledge from videos observed hours earlier and reuse it for real-time decision-making.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264293/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a continuous audio-visual video stream at 1 FPS and 360p resolution of unknown length, with audio encoded by Whisper-Large-v3 and visual frames projected to text space via a modality aligner. A text query arrives at an arbitrary timestamp during the stream, and the model must produce a text answer using only a fixed-size memory (16k tokens by default, up to 128k on 80GB GPUs) regardless of how long the stream has been running. Audio tokens bypass the TTT_MEM layer and are fed directly into the token discarding stage since they are roughly 1/75 the volume of visual tokens.

## Method

The core mechanism is the TTT_MEM layer, an MLP with fast weights updated via one step of gradient descent at each incoming mini-batch of 12–16 frames, using a combined multi-view reconstruction loss and a novel long-span prediction loss that forces the fast weights to remain predictive of inputs from T mini-batches earlier, acting as a temporal consistency regularizer for long-range retention. Incoming memory tokens are concatenated with the existing fixed-size memory buffer, and K tokens with the highest cosine similarity to their neighbors are discarded to maintain constant memory. Training follows a two-stage scheme: a cold-start stage jointly optimizes TTT_MEM projection matrices and LoRA adapters on medium-length contexts, followed by a scale-up stage that freezes TTT_MEM parameters while expanding context length and memory capacity. At inference, a modality-aware prompt-dependent reading mechanism iteratively prefills memory chunks into the Transformer's KV cache, computing per-position attention scores from the prompt to select only the top-m most relevant multimodal KV pairs per layer, reducing the effective context to an average of M tokens while preserving non-multimodal token positions.

## Results

Under a fixed 16k memory token budget (~24 GB GPU memory), video-SALMONN S with the modality-aware reading mechanism achieves 76.9% on Video-MME, 55.6% on LVBench, 58.9% on VideoEvalPro, and 46.7% on ELViM, setting new state-of-the-art across all benchmarks. The headline result is a 14.2% absolute accuracy improvement over the strongest non-streaming baseline (video-SALMONN2+) on ELViM, and 3–7% gains over streaming baselines on long-video benchmarks (LVBench, VideoEvalPro). On StreamingBench, where long-term memory is less critical, video-SALMONN S remains competitive (67.1%), confirming that the TTT_MEM memory mechanism does not sacrifice real-time perception.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the non-streaming baselines, video-SALMONN S (w/ reading) outperforms Qwen3-VL 8B by +7.2% on Video-MME, +8.2% on LVBench, and +18.6% on ELViM, and beats video-SALMONN2+ by +2.9% on LVBench, +5.4% on VideoEvalPro, and +14.2% on ELViM. Among streaming baselines, it surpasses Similarity Merging (MovieChat-style) by +4.0% on LVBench and +7.1% on VideoEvalPro, and PEMF (StreamForest) by +6.1% on LVBench and +8.5% on VideoEvalPro, with the gap widening on ELViM (+8.2% and +8.5% respectively). Compared to the KV-cache compression method AdaReTaKe, video-SALMONN S gains +1.3% on LVBench, +4.4% on VideoEvalPro, and +5.2% on ELViM. On StreamingBench, where long-term memory is not the bottleneck, all methods perform within ~1% of each other, and video-SALMONN S ties PEMF at 67.1%, confirming that the TTT_MEM mechanism does not degrade short-horizon real-time perception.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the test-time training (TTT) formulations of Sun et al. (2024) and Dalal et al. (2025) by repurposing fast-weight updates as a streaming long-term memory mechanism for video understanding, adding a long-span prediction objective to strengthen retention over distant timestamps. It departs from the dominant streaming video paradigm of token merging or discarding (MovieChat, VideoLLM-online, StreamForest/PEMF) by encoding history into model parameters rather than maintaining an explicit token buffer, and from KV-cache compression methods (ReTaKe, AdaReTaKe) by introducing a modality-aware, prompt-dependent reading mechanism that selectively retrieves relevant memory. The two-stage training scheme (cold-start with medium contexts, then scale-up with frozen TTT parameters) is a practical contribution not present in prior TTT work, which typically trains at a fixed sequence length.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ELViM benchmark fills a genuine gap: no prior evaluation explicitly tests whether a streaming video model can consolidate procedural knowledge from hours-earlier observations and apply it in real time, a capability essential for future video-based agents. The finding that a streaming model with TTT memory outperforms non-streaming baselines by 15% on ELViM challenges the assumption that offline full-video access is necessary for long-horizon reasoning, and the 25% memory-token efficiency over merging suggests parameter-based memory may scale more favorably than explicit token buffers. Open questions include whether TTT memory can be inspected, edited, or selectively forgotten (privacy/safety), whether the approach generalizes beyond 3-hour videos, and whether the long-span prediction objective transfers to other modalities or tasks.
