## Problem

Existing benchmarks evaluate media authenticity (real vs. AI-generated) and content veracity (factual vs. non-factual) in isolation, overlooking how real-world deception jointly exploits both dimensions across multiple modalities. No prior benchmark unifies these two axes across text, audio, image, and video within realistic presentation formats that reflect how multimodal misinformation is actually composed and delivered.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261210/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each instance is a coherent multimodal webpage (desktop or mobile layout) bundling text, audio, image, and video components drawn from the same semantic root but potentially from different quadrants of the content×media matrix (e.g., factual natural text paired with non-factual AI-generated video). Outputs include per-modality authenticity labels, a global factuality verdict (Supported/Refuted/NEI), three cross-modal consistency labels (T-I, T-A, T-V), and over 16K rationale-level annotations organized by modality and dimension. The OmniCheck framework additionally produces structured, evidence-backed diagnostic reports on demand.

## Method

FakeWorld constructs 3,153 balanced instances via controlled factual perturbation (modifying 1–2 proper nouns across seven categories), multimodal synthesis with 35 SOTA generative models, cross-quadrant sampling, and rendering into realistic news/encyclopedic layouts. OmniCheck is a three-stage agentic pipeline: (1) a Decomposer partitions input into modality-specific streams and decouples the media and content axes for parallel execution; (2) Authenticity Verification combines retrieval-augmented fact checking (iterative search with budget constraints), MLLM-based cross-modal consistency classification, and modality-specific LoRA adapters (rank-16 on Qwen2.5-Omni-3B) for high-precision media detection; (3) an Explainer aggregates all outputs into a Context Pack and generates dimension-aware diagnostic reports guided by structured explainability templates. A key preliminary finding motivates the design: MLLMs contain an inherent perceptual discriminator in the visual encoder that is selectively activated by task intent, meaning zero-shot detection is grounded in perceptual representations rather than post-hoc reasoning alone.

## Results

On FakeWorld 1.0, Gemini 3 Pro achieves the highest overall media authenticity accuracy (78.99%) and best factual verification (87.65%), while Qwen3-VL-235B-Thinking leads cross-modal consistency (75.01%) and ranks second in overall media detection (71.52%). The OmniCheck framework with a LoRA-adapted Qwen2.5-Omni-3B detector achieves 87.16% overall media accuracy and 78.66% content veracity, substantially surpassing all zero-shot MLLM baselines. Explainability scores (LLM-judged Completeness/Detail) peak at 4.01/4.17 for OmniCheck, compared to 2.99/2.95 for the best zero-shot model (Claude Sonnet 4.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among zero-shot closed-source MLLMs, Gemini 3 Pro dominates media authenticity (78.99% overall) and fact verification (87.65%), outperforming GPT-5.2 (65.14% media, 81.86% fact) and Claude Sonnet 4.5 (63.71% media, 82.68% fact). GPT-5.2 is the strongest on text-specific detection (68.47% acc vs. 51.60% for Gemini), while Qwen3-VL-Plus leads video detection (90.96% acc). Among open-source models, Qwen3-VL-235B-Thinking is the strongest, achieving 71.52% media accuracy and the best cross-modal consistency (75.01%), narrowing the gap to closed-source systems. GLM-4.6v and Seed-1.8 trail significantly (53.43% and 63.60% media accuracy, respectively). The OmniCheck framework with LoRA fine-tuning on Qwen2.5-Omni-3B dramatically outperforms all zero-shot baselines, jumping from 30.48% to 87.16% media accuracy and from 0.81% to 87.62% fact accuracy, demonstrating that modular task decomposition plus task-adaptive training is far more effective than monolithic zero-shot reasoning for high-fidelity multimodal deception detection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by unifying two previously isolated research threads—media authenticity detection (FakeBench, FakeClue, LOKI, Ivy-Fake) and content veracity verification (MMFakeBench, Forensics-Bench)—into a single omni-modal evaluation framework spanning text, audio, image, and video simultaneously. No prior benchmark jointly instantiates both axes within realistic webpage or mobile-social presentation layouts; existing work either detects synthetic media in isolation or verifies factual claims without cross-modal media forensics. The OmniCheck agentic framework extends the modular decomposition idea from MMD-Agent but generalizes it to all four modalities and both authenticity dimensions, with LoRA-based modality-specific routing for efficient detection. The preliminary manifold analysis of MLLM visual encoders, showing task-gated perceptual discriminators, departs from the prevailing assumption that zero-shot detection is purely instruction-following.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FakeWorld 1.0 is likely to become a standard stress-test benchmark for multimodal verification systems, given its first-of-its-kind coverage of all four modalities with both authenticity axes in realistic presentation formats and 16K+ rationale-level annotations. The empirical findings—that even frontier MLLMs exhibit strong detection bias toward 'natural' and 'consistent' predictions, and that explicit reasoning (thinking) only helps at scale (>32B) for media detection—provide actionable guidance for system designers. The paper explicitly limits itself to single semantic events and leaves multi-event scenarios, harder benchmark variants, and full end-to-end agent evaluation (e.g., inspecting live HTML pages) to future work, which are significant open questions for deploying such systems in practice.
