## Problem

Existing safety attribution methods for LLMs rely on heuristic, domain-specific metrics (e.g., causal mediation scores, greedy head selection) that are computationally prohibitive, unstable, and tend to isolate disjoint components rather than functionally complete circuits. This limits the ability to universally localize and manipulate the internal mechanisms responsible for safety behaviors such as backdoor activation or alignment refusal.

## Input / Output

SafeSeek consumes an LLM's computational graph parameterized by θ, a small safety dataset (100 samples for mask training), and text inputs (clean or trigger-poisoned), then produces strictly binary circuit masks at user-selected granularities (individual weights, generalized neurons, attention heads, or full layers). In the backdoor scenario, it outputs two complementary subgraphs—a dense clean model and a sparse backdoor circuit—via dual-mask optimization, while in safety alignment it yields a sparse safety circuit and a dense unsafe subgraph. The final SaCirT step takes these masks and produces a fine-tuned model where only the identified circuit parameters are updated.

## Method

SafeSeek reformulates circuit discovery as a gradient-based optimization over learnable latent variables z_u, where each unit u receives a sigmoid mask m_u = sigmoid(z_u) that is strictly binarized during the forward pass via the Straight-Through Estimator (STE) while gradients flow through the continuous path in the backward pass. The objective minimizes KL divergence between the full model and the masked subgraph (faithfulness) plus an L1 sparsity penalty, with the STE ensuring training-inference consistency that ablations show is critical (without it, general capabilities collapse to 0%). For backdoor attribution, a Dual-Mask Optimization (DMO) simultaneously optimizes two mask sets with a sparsity loss that penalizes overlap between the clean and backdoor subgraphs while encouraging a dense clean model and a sparse malicious circuit. The framework supports mixed-granularity attribution (e.g., attention heads for MHA + neurons for MLPs) and extends to Safety Circuit Tuning (SaCirT), which freezes all non-circuit parameters and exclusively fine-tunes the identified safety subnetwork on a safety dataset.

## Results

In the backdoor scenario, SafeSeek identifies a circuit with only 0.42% sparsity whose ablation reduces the Attack Success Rate (ASR) from 100% to 0.4% (OOD) while retaining >99% general utility (GSM8k: 72.5→72.0, MMLU: 68.8→67.2). In the safety alignment scenario, removing the identified circuit (3.03% heads, 0.79% neurons) causes ASR to spike from 0.8% to 96.9% (ID) and 7.9% to 81.5% (OOD), confirming its causal role. Safety Circuit Tuning (SaCirT) applied to these circuits maintains 96.5% safety retention during helpfulness fine-tuning, outperforming LoRA which drops to 89.3% harmless rate on AdvBench. Against baselines, SafeSeek achieves 96.1% ASR(ID) at 0.32% sparsity in 22 minutes, striking the best trade-off between attribution effectiveness, sparsity, and utility preservation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SafeSeek is compared against two baselines: SHIPS (Zhou et al., 2024b), a greedy attention-head attribution method, and SN-Tune (Zhao et al., 2025), a safety-specific neuron fine-tuning approach. Against SHIPS, SafeSeek dramatically outperforms on attribution effectiveness (ASR 96.1% vs. 73.9% ID; 96.6% vs. 74.6% OOD) and is ~9× faster (22 min vs. 3h 32min), though SHIPS preserves slightly more general utility (GSM8K 62.5% vs. 55.3%; MMLU 69.3% vs. 64.2%). Against SN-Tune, SafeSeek achieves comparable ASR (96.1% vs. 98.4% ID) but with 13× greater sparsity (0.32% vs. 4.34%), resulting in substantially less utility degradation (GSM8K 55.3% vs. 49.7%; MMLU 64.2% vs. 32.8%; HellaSwag 52.7% vs. 47.7%). SN-Tune is marginally faster (13 min vs. 22 min) and uses less GPU memory (15 GB vs. 24 GB), but at the cost of severe general capability collapse. Overall, SafeSeek achieves the best balance: near-maximal attribution effectiveness with extreme sparsity and minimal utility loss.

## Relation to Prior Work

SafeSeek builds on the circuit discovery formulation from ACDC (Conmy et al., 2023) and the learnable-mask paradigm from Edge Pruning (Bhaskar et al., 2024), but departs fundamentally from prior safety attribution methods (SHIPS, SN-Tune, safety-neuron discovery) that rely on heuristic greedy search or domain-specific causal metrics to isolate individual heads or neurons. The key conceptual shift is reformulating safety circuit discovery as a differentiable optimization over multi-granular binary masks via the Straight-Through Estimator, unifying backdoor and alignment scenarios under a single Dual-Mask Optimization objective. Additionally, the paper extends the interpretability-to-control pipeline by introducing SaCirT, which leverages the identified sparse circuits for parameter-efficient safety fine-tuning—a practical step absent from prior attribution-only frameworks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that both backdoor mechanisms and safety alignment are governed by extremely sparse (<1%) circuits structurally orthogonal to general utility provides a mechanistic explanation for why targeted interventions can be so surgical, and offers a practical tool (SaCirT) for backdoor eradication and alignment-tax mitigation with minimal parameter updates. Key limitations include the restriction to models up to 32B parameters (scalability to frontier >100B models is untested), the assumption that safety mechanisms are inherently sparse (which may not hold for denser capabilities like complex reasoning), and the acknowledged dual-use risk that the same optimization could be weaponized to surgically remove safety safeguards. Open questions include whether the identified circuits are truly minimal or contain redundancies, and whether the framework generalizes to safety properties that are more diffusely encoded.
