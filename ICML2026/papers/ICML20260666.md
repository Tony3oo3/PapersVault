## Problem

Existing model merging methods treat merging coefficients as fixed hyperparameters or uniform values, producing unified models whose quality is misaligned with the downstream adapter training that follows. The paper formalizes this misalignment (Theorem 3.1): optimizing the unified model's static performance does not minimize the post-adaptation loss, because adapters can only correct errors within their constrained update subspace, leaving orthogonal components irreducible. The key question is how to learn a unified model initialization that is specifically tailored to maximize the effectiveness of subsequent lightweight adapter training.

## Input / Output

The method takes K fully finetuned models (sharing a common pretrained backbone) and unlabeled data from each task's validation set. For vision experiments, inputs are 224×224 RGB images across 8 classification tasks; for NLP, tokenized text sequences across 7 GLUE tasks. The output is a single unified backbone (parameter count comparable to one finetuned model) plus K tiny residual-MLP adapters (99,136 parameters each, rank 64), which together produce class labels at inference. No labeled data is required during the merging or adapter-training stages.

## Method

MetaMerging formulates merging coefficient optimization as a bilevel meta-learning problem inspired by MAML. In the inner loop, it performs one or few gradient steps on lightweight task-specific adapters (residual MLPs: h − Linear(ReLU(Linear(h)))) using an L2 alignment loss between the finetuned model's features and the adapted unified model's features. In the outer loop, it meta-updates the K scalar merging coefficients λ via the gradient of the post-adaptation alignment loss on a held-out meta-test batch, so that the learned θ_uni = θ_pre + Σ λ_k V_k is an initialization from which small adapter updates yield large task-specific gains. A regret-based analysis (Theorem 3.2) proves that this meta-objective reduces the dominant term in the adapter training regret bound. For large models (ViT-L/14, GPT-2), a FOMAML-style first-order approximation avoids expensive second-order derivatives, and the entire procedure converges in roughly 1–2 hours on a single GPU since only K scalars are optimized.

## Results

On eight image classification tasks with ViT-B/32, MetaMerging achieves 87.2% average accuracy, surpassing the strongest baselines Surgery (86.1%), Pareto Merging (85.9%), and DOGE (85.9%) while approaching the individual finetuned model upper bound of 90.5%. With the larger ViT-L/14 backbone, MetaMerging reaches 93.4% average accuracy, outperforming WUDI-Merging and DOGE (both 92.6%) and Surgery (92.3%). On seven GLUE text classification tasks with GPT-2, MetaMerging achieves 76.9% average accuracy, a substantial 6.9–8.1% improvement over the best classical merging baselines (Task Arithmetic and Ties-Merging at 70.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MetaMerging outperforms all 14 baselines on average accuracy across both vision model scales and the language model. On ViT-B/32, it beats the strongest training-based baselines Surgery (86.1%), Pareto Merging (85.9%), and DOGE (85.9%) by 1.1–1.3%, and surpasses training-free methods like Ties-Merging (73.6%) and Task Arithmetic (70.1%) by 13.6–17.1%. On ViT-L/14, the gap narrows slightly (MetaMerging 93.4% vs. WUDI-Merging/DOGE at 92.6%), but MetaMerging still leads all methods. On GPT-2 text classification, the advantage is most pronounced: MetaMerging (76.9%) exceeds Task Arithmetic and Ties-Merging (70.0%) by 6.9% and RegMean (68.8%) by 8.1%, while classical methods like Weight Averaging (56.1%) and Fisher Merging (58.7%) lag far behind. Notably, MetaMerging also surpasses multi-task learning (88.9% on ViT-B/32) by 1.7% while requiring only 1h 23m of training versus 15h 53m, and its final model retains only ~89M parameters (one unified backbone + K tiny adapters) compared to WEMoE's 574M parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of model merging and meta-learning. It inherits the task-vector formulation from Task Arithmetic (Ilharco et al., 2023) and the merging-and-training paradigm with lightweight adapters from Surgery (Yang et al., 2024b), but departs from both by treating the merging coefficients as learnable parameters optimized via a MAML-style bilevel loop (Finn et al., 2017) rather than fixed hyperparameters or uniform values. The key conceptual departure is formalized in Theorem 3.1, which proves that the coefficient vector minimizing static merged-model performance is generally different from the one minimizing post-adaptation performance, thereby justifying the meta-learning objective as the correct optimization target.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, theoretically grounded explanation for why naively tuning merging coefficients (or using uniform averaging) is suboptimal when downstream adapters are involved, and offers a lightweight fix that requires optimizing only K scalar values. The finding that the best merged model before adaptation is not the best after adaptation (MetaMerging w/o adapters scores 67.8% vs. AdaMerging's 80.1%, yet MetaMerging w/ adapters reaches 87.2%) challenges the common practice of evaluating merged models in isolation. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very large LLMs with many tasks, whether the single-step inner-loop approximation (FOMAML) remains sufficient for deeper adapter architectures, and how the method interacts with more complex merging structures like MoE-based approaches.
