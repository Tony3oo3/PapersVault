## Problem

MARS improves upon MVR by scaling the momentum correction term with a coefficient γ, and empirically outperforms MVR (γ=1) in LLM training, yet no existing theory explains why this scaling helps. Prior analyses either require infeasible time-varying γ values or yield the same O(1/T^{1/3}) rate for both MARS and MVR, failing to distinguish their convergence behavior.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264153/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each MARS iteration consumes two stochastic gradients evaluated at the current and previous iterates with the same data sample (∇f_ξ(x_t) and ∇f_ξ(x_{t-1})), along with the previous gradient estimate g_{t-1}, to produce an updated parameter vector x_{t+1} and a new gradient estimate g_t. The correction term Δ_t = ∇f_ξ(x_t) − ∇f_ξ(x_{t-1}) is scaled by a fixed γ ≥ 0 before being folded into the momentum update, distinguishing MARS from both MVR (γ=1) and plain SGD with momentum (γ=0).

## Method

The paper introduces γ-similarity, a condition measuring how well γ(∇f_ξ(x)−∇f_ξ(y)) tracks the full gradient difference ∇f(x)−∇f(y) in squared norm, which recovers standard similarity at γ=1 and L-smoothness at γ=0. A key lemma upper-bounds the γ-similarity constant δ_γ by γ²δ² + (γ−1)²L², whose minimizer γ* = L²/(δ²+L²) yields a constant strictly smaller than both δ and L. Using this refined condition, the authors derive a gradient complexity bound for MARS that explicitly depends on γ and δ_γ, proving that an appropriately chosen γ ∈ [0,1] gives strictly lower complexity than MVR. Experiments on CIFAR-10 (probe of local correction scale) and 124M-parameter Llama-style pretraining on SlimPajama confirm that small γ values (e.g., 0.01–0.1) improve token efficiency over both MVR and AdamW.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (8.1M-param CNN, 20 epochs), the fixed-γ sweep shows that MARS with γ=0.25 achieves the best final training loss of 0.0854, substantially outperforming the MVR setting γ=1 (loss 0.1415) and SGD with momentum γ=0 (loss 0.1925). In the 124M-parameter LLM pretraining on SlimPajama (~2.10B tokens, Chinchilla-optimal budget), MARS-AdamW with small γ values (0.01–0.1) achieves lower validation loss than AdamW in the late training stage, while the classic MVR setting γ=1 exhibits pronounced instability under fixed hyperparameters. Theoretically, the paper proves that MARS with an optimally chosen γ∈[0,1] attains a strictly lower gradient complexity bound than MVR, with the speedup governed by the ratio Lσ/(εδ).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares MARS (with various γ) against two baselines: AdamW and MVR (γ=1). In the 124M LLM pretraining experiment, MARS-AdamW with small γ values (0.01, 0.025, 0.04, 0.1) consistently achieves lower validation loss than AdamW in the late training stage, while the MVR setting γ=1 shows pronounced instability and requires dedicated hyperparameter tuning to be competitive. On CIFAR-10, MARS with γ=0.25 reduces final training loss by 40% relative to MVR (0.0854 vs. 0.1415) under identical hyperparameters, confirming that the optimal correction scale is well below the MVR value. The theoretical analysis (Corollary 2) further establishes that, under the condition B < Aδ, the MARS complexity bound at the optimal γ⋆ is strictly no larger than the MVR bound, providing a provable advantage that the empirical results corroborate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper provides the missing theoretical justification for MARS (Yuan et al., 2025), which extends MVR (Cutkosky & Orabona, 2019) by scaling the stochastic gradient-difference correction term with a coefficient γ. While Yuan et al. showed MARS has lower estimator variance than MVR, their convergence analysis required infeasible time-varying γ_t and did not yield an explicit complexity advantage. Here, the authors introduce γ-similarity—a condition that unifies standard similarity (γ=1) and smoothness (γ=0)—to prove that a fixed optimal γ∈[0,1] gives MARS a strictly lower gradient complexity bound than MVR, resolving the gap between MARS's empirical superiority and its prior theoretical treatment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The γ-similarity framework offers a clean, reusable lens for analyzing any scaled-correction momentum variant, and the proof that MARS provably beats MVR closes a notable gap in the stochastic optimization literature. However, the theoretical advantage is established through surrogate upper bounds on δ_γ (via Lemma 1) rather than tight characterizations, and the empirical validation is limited to a 124M-parameter model on a single dataset with fixed hyperparameters across γ values. Open questions include whether the optimal γ can be estimated or adapted online during training, whether the advantage persists at billion-parameter scale, and whether the analysis extends cleanly to adaptive optimizers like AdamW beyond the heuristic MARS-AdamW variant tested here.
