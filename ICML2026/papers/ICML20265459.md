## Problem

Accelerated dual methods for distributed optimization achieve optimal communication complexity but require exact or O(ε²)-accurate dual gradients, which in the stochastic setting demands O(ε⁻²) local steps — often impractical given communication schedules and client compute budgets. The paper asks whether such methods can converge for an arbitrary number of local steps while still attaining optimal communication complexity when local computation is sufficient, and whether the sensitivity of accelerated methods to gradient inexactness can be overcome through appropriate parameter tuning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes M clients' unbiased stochastic gradient estimates, a symmetric doubly-stochastic network weight matrix W (with spectral gap p = 1 − σ₂), and initial primal-dual variables; the K inner SGD steps require zero inter-agent communication, with communication occurring only at the outer dual update. It outputs an ε-optimal consensus solution (the average of local variables) together with a polynomial bound on consensus violation, and the communication complexity scales as Õ(κ^(1/2) p^(−1/2)) when K = Θ̃(ε⁻¹), matching the known lower bound up to logarithmic factors.

## Method

Local ADA reformulates the distributed problem as a linearly constrained optimization via U = (I−W)^(1/2), then alternates K steps of stochastic gradient descent on the primal variable (inner loop, entirely local) with an accelerated gradient ascent step on the dual variable (outer loop, one communication round). The central technical contribution is a new analysis framework for accelerated gradient methods under δ-inexact oracles, which reveals a fundamental trade-off: smaller momentum and step sizes reduce the inexactness bias but slow the contraction rate, and this trade-off is exploited to prove convergence for any K. When K is large enough (Θ̃(ε⁻¹) in the stochastic case), the contraction is dominated by the momentum-driven rate, yielding the near-optimal Õ(κ^(1/2) p^(−1/2)) communication complexity. A byproduct of the refined inexact-AGD bound is the first algorithm achieving simultaneously near-optimal Õ(ε^(−1/2)) gradient complexity and optimal O(δ²/ε²) reproducibility under biased gradients, disproving a conjecture from prior work.

## Results

Local ADA achieves a communication complexity of $\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(\kappa^{1/2} p^{-1/2})$ for strongly convex stochastic decentralized optimization, matching the known lower bound $\widetilde{\Omega}(\kappa^{1/2} p^{-1/2})$ up to logarithmic factors. Crucially, this rate is attained with an arbitrary number of local SGD steps K, and only $\widetilde{\Theta}(\epsilon^{-1})$ local steps are needed to reach the optimal communication complexity. In experiments on YearPredictionMSD (ridge/elastic net regression, 20 clients, 4×5 grid) and w8a (logistic regression, 30 clients, three topologies), Local ADA consistently reaches the lowest final objective gap among all compared methods, with increasing K (20→200) further reducing communication rounds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Local ADA strictly dominates LED (Alghunaim, 2024) in communication complexity: $\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(\kappa^{1/2} p^{-1/2})$ vs. $\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(\kappa^2 p^{-1})$, while both support arbitrary local steps. Against Distributed FGM (Uribe et al., 2020), Local ADA matches the same $\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(\kappa^{1/2} p^{-1/2})$ rate but removes the critical requirement that K = $\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-2})$ local steps be used, guaranteeing convergence for any K. Compared to Stochastic GT (Koloskova et al., 2021) and Local-DSGD (Koloskova et al., 2020), Local ADA improves the dependence on the condition number from $\kappa$ to $\kappa^{1/2}$ and on the spectral gap from $p^{-1}$ to $p^{-1/2}$. D-MSAG (Fallah et al., 2022) achieves a comparable leading term but adds an extra $C_0\sqrt{\kappa}\,p^{-1/2}(M\epsilon)^{-1/4}$ factor and does not support local steps. In the empirical experiments, Local ADA outperforms LED, Decentralized Scaffnew, and Local-DSGD on both YearPredictionMSD and w8a across all tested topologies, reaching the lowest final objective gap and converging faster in the early phase on ridge regression.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the accelerated dual method lineage from Scaman et al. (2017) and Uribe et al. (2020), which achieved optimal communication complexity in distributed optimization but required exact or O(ε²)-accurate primal solves to approximate dual gradients. The key departure is reframing the partial Lagrangian gradient (after only K local SGD steps) as an inexact gradient oracle for the dual function, then developing a new inexact-AGD analysis that shows convergence for arbitrary K by trading momentum/step-size against bias. This positions Local ADA between the suboptimal-complexity local-step methods (LED, Local-DSGD) and the exact-solve dual methods, closing the gap that neither family could fill in the stochastic setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that accelerated dual methods converge with arbitrary local steps and still achieve the optimal O(√κ · p^{-1/2}) communication complexity resolves a natural open question and provides a practical recipe for reducing communication in federated and decentralized training. A notable byproduct is the first algorithm simultaneously achieving near-optimal gradient complexity and reproducibility under biased gradients, disproving a conjecture from Ahn et al. (2022) and Zhang et al. (2024) that these two goals were incompatible. Limitations include the strongly convex assumption in the main analysis (non-convex extension is deferred to a Catalyst wrapper) and empirical validation on only two tabular datasets with modest client counts, leaving open whether the theoretical gains persist at scale with deep networks.
