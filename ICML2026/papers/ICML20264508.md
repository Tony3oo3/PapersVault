## Problem

Existing one-shot pruning methods like SparseGPT and Wanda are designed for Transformer linear and attention layers and cannot handle Mamba's time-shared state-transition matrix, its zero-order-hold discretization, or the highly heterogeneous pruning sensitivity across FFN submodules. No principled second-order (OBS-based) pruning framework existed for selective state-space architectures, leaving Mamba-based LLMs without an efficient training-free compression pathway.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pretrained Mamba checkpoint (130M–2.8B for Mamba-1, 370M–8B for Mamba-2) and 128 calibration segments of 2048 tokens from WikiText-2. During a single forward pass, it collects hidden-state tensors h_{1:L} at every timestep and the discretized ΔA values to compute per-element Hessian statistics. The output is a binary mask applied to the A_log matrix (D×N) and to FFN weight matrices, producing a sparse model with a user-specified global sparsity ratio (e.g., 50%) without any fine-tuning.

## Method

SparseSSM extends the Optimal Brain Surgeon framework to SSMs by exploiting the diagonal structure of A_log: Theorem 3.1 shows that the per-timestep OBS saliency of parameter ΔA_{t,d,n} reduces to ΔA_{t,d,n}² · h_{t-1,d,n}², requiring only the hidden state from the previous step rather than a full Hessian. Because A_log is time-shared, the method aggregates per-timestep masks using a prefix-biased power-law weight w_t ∝ (t+1)^{-μ} that privileges early timesteps where most state information is accumulated. For the FFN branches, a sensitivity-aware allocation assigns lower sparsity to in_proj and out_proj modules (identified via Hessian-trace ranking) while distributing the remaining budget uniformly, ensuring the global sparsity constraint is met exactly. The framework extends naturally to N:M semi-structured and column-wise structured sparsity on A_log, yielding measurable wall-clock speedups (up to 2.23×) and memory reductions without specialized kernels.

## Results

At 50% unstructured sparsity on the SSM A_log matrix, SparseSSM achieves near-dense perplexity across all Mamba scales: 15.04 WikiText-2 (vs. 14.32 dense) on Mamba-370M, 10.63 (vs. 9.45 dense) on Mamba-2.8B, and 21.26 (vs. 20.60 dense) on Mamba-130M. Average zero-shot accuracy degrades by less than 0.5 points on Mamba-370M (47.47 vs. 47.49 dense) and actually exceeds dense on Mamba-2.8B (53.58 vs. 56.91 is a drop, but the gap is minimal relative to baselines). Structured pruning at 50% sparsity yields a 1.72× speedup on the selective scan operator and up to 2.23× end-to-end speedup on Mamba-2.8B, with only ~1 perplexity point degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SparseSSM dominates all four baselines by a wide margin on perplexity at 50% sparsity. On Mamba-370M, SparseSSM achieves 15.04 WikiText-2 perplexity versus 40.91 for Wanda, 291.2 for magnitude pruning (MP), 334.5 for Mamba-Shedder, and 2696 for SparseGPT — a gap of 2.7× to 179× over the next-best method. The advantage is even more dramatic on smaller models: on Mamba-130M, SparseGPT's perplexity explodes to 2.4×10⁷ while SparseSSM stays at 21.26 (near the dense 20.60). On zero-shot accuracy, SparseSSM leads by ~5.4 points on average over the best baseline (SparseGPT) on Mamba-370M (47.47 vs. 44.56), and the gap widens on smaller models where baselines degrade more severely. Mamba-Shedder, the only baseline specifically designed for Mamba, consistently underperforms SparseSSM by 5–10 accuracy points and produces 20–100× higher perplexity, confirming that the time-shared, discretized SSM structure requires the dedicated Hessian-based treatment introduced here.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Optimal Brain Surgeon (OBS) pruning lineage—originally formalized by Hassibi & Stork (1992) and scaled to LLMs by SparseGPT (Frantar & Alistarh, 2023)—to selective state-space models, where the time-shared, discretized state-transition matrix A_log breaks the assumptions underlying existing layer-wise pruning. It departs from prior Mamba pruning work (Mamba-Shedder, Ghattas et al., Shihab et al.) by providing a rigorous second-order theoretical justification (Theorem 3.1) rather than heuristic or iterative schedules, and borrows the sensitivity-aware sparsity allocation from Shao et al. (2024) to handle heterogeneous FFN module robustness. The power-law temporal weighting for merging per-timestep saliency masks is a novel aggregation strategy not present in any prior SSM or Transformer pruning method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As Mamba-based LLMs gain traction as efficient alternatives to Transformers, this work establishes that they can be compressed as effectively as their Transformer counterparts via principled, training-free pruning—removing a key barrier to deployment on resource-constrained hardware. The dramatic failure of SparseGPT on SSM modules (perplexity exploding by orders of magnitude) highlights that architecture-specific pruning is not optional but necessary, a finding that generalizes to any model with time-shared or recurrent parameters. Open questions include extending structured pruning beyond the SSM module to the full Mamba architecture, adapting the temporal weighting to other time-varying architectures (e.g., state-evolving multimodal models), and investigating whether the method transfers to quantization or other compression paradigms for SSMs.
