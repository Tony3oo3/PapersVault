## Problem

Existing diffusion-based LID estimators (FLIPD, LIDL) fail in high-dimensional, high-codimension settings because they indiscriminately sum curvature across all dimensions, allowing massive normal-direction eigenvalues to overwhelm the tangent signal. kNN-based methods similarly degrade due to the curse of dimensionality. The paper addresses this by asking how to selectively count only tangent (zero-curvature) directions while suppressing normal-direction noise.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263516/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single high-dimensional data point x, a pre-trained score model s_θ(x,t) (typically a Conv2D UNet for 32×32 images or 512×512 latent-space images, or an MLP for D=3), and a noise scale t selected within a diagnostically identified 'safe zone.' It outputs a scalar LID estimate computed via K=8 Rademacher probes and m=5 Lanczos steps, requiring only 40 Hessian-vector products total. For 512×512 images, estimation is performed in the VAE latent space (D≈16,000) rather than pixel space to preserve topological structure.

## Method

LHSD exploits the tangent-normal spectral separation of the log-density Hessian: for small noise σ(t), tangent eigenvalues are O(1) while normal eigenvalues scale as 1/σ(t)², creating a spectral gap. A Hill-type filter f(λ)=1/(1+(|λ|/κ(t))^p) with time-varying cutoff κ(t)=c/σ(t)² maps tangent eigenvalues to ≈1 and normal eigenvalues to ≈0, so tr(f(H)) approximates the intrinsic dimension d. To avoid the O(D³) cost of full eigendecomposition, the trace is estimated via Stochastic Lanczos Quadrature (SLQ), which compresses the Hessian into an m×m tridiagonal matrix using only Hessian-vector products computed by backpropagation through the score network, yielding O(D) total complexity. A transition-mass diagnostic M(t) enables verifiable selection of the noise scale t by identifying the spectral valley where the filter cutoff sits safely between the two eigenvalue clusters.

## Results

On the high-dimensional manifold mixture benchmark (D up to 3072), LHSD (m=5) achieves a mean absolute error (MAE) of 6.6 across all settings, approximately 1/46 that of the second-best method FLIPD (MAE 307.4). On the most challenging setting (F^{10+80+200} ⊂ R^{3072}), LHSD reduces MAE from 1240.89 (FLIPD) to 5.19, a ~239× improvement. On the IDR benchmark (D=784), LHSD (m=2) achieves an average MAE of 0.425, outperforming FLIPD (0.702) while remaining stable under the high-dimensional embedding that causes other methods to degrade dramatically.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LHSD decisively outperforms all five baselines across every dimension and embedding setting. Against the strongest diffusion-based baseline FLIPD, LHSD (m=5) achieves a 46× lower average MAE (6.6 vs. 307.4), with the gap widening dramatically in high co-dimension settings: on F^{10+80+200} ⊂ R^{3072}, FLIPD's MAE explodes to 1240.89 while LHSD stays at 5.19. The other diffusion-based methods (LIDL, NB) fail catastrophically at D≥1024, with MAE values exceeding 2000, confirming the paper's analysis that indiscriminate summation over normal directions overwhelms the tangent signal. kNN-based methods (ESS, LPCA) also collapse at D≥1024 (MAE ≈ 825–891), demonstrating the curse of dimensionality in neighborhood search. On the lower-dimensional IDR benchmark (D=784), LHSD (m=2) still leads with MAE 0.425 vs. FLIPD's 0.702, and uniquely maintains stability when manifolds are embedded into Fashion-MNIST space where FLIPD degrades by 4× (Funnel: 0.363→1.606) while LHSD degrades only 2× (0.323→0.661).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of diffusion-based LID estimation (LIDL, FLIPD, NB) by replacing their trace-based or rank-based formulations with a Hessian spectral filtering approach. It draws on the tangent-normal spectral decomposition insight from Genovese et al. (2014) and Ventura et al. (2025), which showed that the log-density Hessian naturally separates into near-zero tangent and large normal eigenvalues, and operationalizes this via a Hill-type filter combined with Stochastic Lanczos Quadrature (Ubaru et al., 2017) for O(D) computation. The key departure from FLIPD and LIDL is that instead of summing score divergences or score norms indiscriminately across all D dimensions, LHSD selectively counts only the zero-curvature tangent directions, explicitly suppressing the normal-direction noise that causes existing methods to fail in high co-dimension settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LHSD provides the first diffusion-based LID estimator that scales linearly with ambient dimension and remains accurate in the high co-dimension regime (d ≪ D) typical of image data, where all prior diffusion-based methods degrade catastrophically. The verifiable hyperparameter selection via transition mass diagnostics addresses a practical gap: practitioners can visually confirm the filter sits in the spectral gap rather than relying on blind tuning. A key open question is whether loss convergence of the score model guarantees accurate recovery of the true Hessian spectral geometry on complex real-world data, and whether the method extends to settings where the manifold hypothesis is only approximately satisfied. The memorization detection application (AUROC 99.87% while controlling for image complexity) demonstrates a concrete downstream use case that could inform safety auditing of generative models.
