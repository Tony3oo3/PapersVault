## Problem

Detecting whether a text-to-image diffusion model was fine-tuned on a specific image is critical for copyright enforcement, but existing non-intrusive methods (CGI-DM, FineXtract) all require access to the pre-fine-tuned model as a reference, which a malicious infringer will never provide. Intrusive watermarking methods, meanwhile, cannot protect images already in public circulation and degrade image quality. This paper addresses the gap by proposing the first detection method that requires neither image modification nor the pre-fine-tuned model.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image to verify, white-box or gray-box access to the suspect fine-tuned model, and the fine-tuned text condition (e.g., 'a photo of sks dog'). It performs DDIM inversion followed by reconstruction at five intermediate stochasticity ratios (η ∈ {0.35, 0.4, 0.5, 0.6, 0.65}), producing five reconstructed images per input. The final output is an average SSCD similarity score compared against a fixed threshold to yield a binary infringement determination. The entire pipeline requires only six sampling steps and ~4 GB VRAM, making it highly efficient.

## Method

RECOVER exploits a property termed 'inversion robustness': because a diffusion model is trained to denoise fine-tuning images across all noise levels, those images exhibit significantly more stable reconstruction under stochastic perturbations than non-fine-tuning images. The verification pipeline inverts the target image via DDIM to obtain its latent code z_T, then reconstructs it at multiple intermediate stochasticity ratios η ∈ [0.3, 0.7], where the gap in reconstruction quality between fine-tuning and non-fine-tuning images is maximized (theoretically justified by boundary conditions: η→0 yields deterministic inversion where all images reconstruct well, while η→1 yields fully stochastic generation where identity is lost). The similarity between each reconstruction and the original is measured via SSCD, and the average score serves as the detection signal. A key differentiator from prior work is that only the suspect model is needed—no pre-fine-tuned model, no gradient access, and no image modification—making it applicable to already-released images and realistic legal scenarios where the infringer controls which model is presented.

## Results

On the DreamBooth dataset (subject-driven generation) with LoRA fine-tuning on Stable Diffusion v1.5, RECOVER achieves 89.87% ACC and 94.23% AUC, outperforming all baselines. On the WikiArt dataset (style-driven generation) under the same settings, RECOVER reaches 84.50% ACC and 90.58% AUC. Across all four Stable Diffusion variants tested (v1.4, v1.5, v2.0, v2.1), RECOVER consistently achieves the highest ACC and AUC among pre-fine-tuned-model-free methods, with ACC ranging from 84.50% to 89.87% and AUC from 86.60% to 94.23%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RECOVER outperforms all four baselines across every base model and both datasets. Against the two pre-fine-tuned-model-dependent methods, RECOVER (89.87% ACC, 94.23% AUC on SD v1.5 subject-driven) exceeds CGI-DM (86.71%, 93.81%) by ~3.2% ACC and FineXtract (73.42%, 78.89%) by ~16.5% ACC, while critically not requiring access to the pre-fine-tuned model. Against the two pre-fine-tuned-model-free baselines, the advantage is even more pronounced: RECOVER surpasses Text2img (67.62%, 73.78%) by 22.3% ACC and Img2img (65.82%, 68.30%) by 24.1% ACC on SD v1.5 subject-driven generation. On style-driven generation (WikiArt), RECOVER (84.50%, 90.58%) similarly dominates Text2img (65.00%, 63.70%) and Img2img (75.00%, 76.40%). The paper further demonstrates that when the pre-fine-tuned model is maliciously claimed (a realistic legal scenario), CGI-DM and FineXtract degrade to below Text2img performance, while RECOVER remains unaffected since it never requires that model.

## Relation to Prior Work

RECOVER builds directly on DDIM inversion and generation (Song et al., 2021a), repurposing the inversion-reconstruction pipeline as a detection signal rather than a generation tool. It departs from the two main lines of prior work on unauthorized data usage detection: intrusive methods (watermarking, adversarial perturbations) that modify images before release, and non-intrusive methods (CGI-DM, FineXtract) that require access to the pre-fine-tuned model to compute gradient or noise discrepancies. The key conceptual departure is eliminating the pre-fine-tuned model entirely, instead exploiting the intrinsic inversion robustness that fine-tuning imparts to the suspect model's denoising trajectory. The paper also distinguishes itself from membership inference by providing visual reconstruction evidence rather than a binary classification output, which is more interpretable for legal proceedings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a critical practical gap: in real-world copyright disputes, the pre-fine-tuned model is typically unavailable or can be maliciously substituted, rendering existing non-intrusive methods ineffective. RECOVER's efficiency (6 sampling steps, 4 GB VRAM) and architecture-agnostic applicability (U-Net, DiT, flow matching) make it a practical auditing tool for platforms and legal proceedings. Limitations include the requirement for white-box or gray-box access to the suspect model, the absence of a full multi-step theoretical bound (only single-step analysis is provided), and inapplicability to strict black-box settings. Open questions include whether the inversion robustness signal degrades under more sophisticated adaptive attacks and whether the method extends to video or 3D generation models.
