## Problem

Standard GFlowNets for discrete amortized inference rely on a Markovian forward policy that conditions only on the current state, which provably limits the expressivity of the learnable distribution class and causes state aliasing—where distinct states become representationally indistinguishable to the policy network. This leads to slow convergence and poor fit to targets with sharp or multi-modal structure, particularly in compositional spaces like graphs and sequences.

## Input / Output

The method takes an unnormalized reward function R over terminal states of a pointed DAG (the compositional state graph), a state embedding function ψ, and an initial latent state W₀. It produces a path-dependent forward policy p_F(s'|s, W) and a learned vector field φ governing the latent dynamics W_t = W_{t-1} + φ(s_t, W_{t-1}), from which samples are drawn by iteratively applying the policy until a terminal state is reached. The lifted state space is S × Ω (with Ω = ℝ^d), so the policy's input at each step includes both the current graph/sequence/set state and the accumulated latent representation of the entire trajectory so far.

## Method

The paper lifts the GFlowNet's MDP by augmenting each state s_t with a latent variable W_t that evolves via a deterministic, state-conditional difference equation, making the forward policy depend on the full trajectory history rather than just the current state. The latent dynamics is implemented as a modified Self-Referential Weight Matrix (SRWM) with an ergodic rotation matrix R that enforces diversity across the rollout, reducing aliasing effects. Training jointly optimizes the policy and the vector field φ by minimizing standard GFlowNet objectives—trajectory balance (TB), subtrajectory balance (SubTB), or contrastive balance (CB)—extended to the lifted DAG, where the backward policy can be chosen Markovian or path-dependent. The paper proves that this lifting strictly enlarges the space of representable distributions for linear, GNN-based, and ReLU-network policies (Propositions 3.1–3.2), and that a Markovian backward policy at equilibrium induces a Markovian forward policy (Proposition 4.8), providing a principled design choice.

## Results

The paper evaluates goodness-of-fit using FCS (Flow-Consistency in Subgraphs, i.e., average TV distance on tractable subsets; lower is better) across four benchmark tasks. On sequence design, the path-dependent model reduces FCS from 0.084 to 0.011 (Syn-8), 0.165 to 0.024 (Syn-16), and 0.243 to 0.055 (Syn-32), representing 3–7× improvements over the Markovian baseline. On biological sequence design, FCS drops from 0.165 to 0.135 (SIX6) and 0.175 to 0.130 (PHO4). On set generation (S=64), FCS improves from 0.091 to 0.053 (K=16) and 0.105 to 0.064 (K=24).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the standard Markovian GFlowNet (Bengio et al., 2021), which uses a state-only forward policy without any latent dynamical system. The path-dependent model wins on every reported task and metric. The advantage is most pronounced on synthetic sequence design, where FCS improves by a factor of 3–7× (e.g., 0.243 → 0.055 on Syn-32), and moderate but consistent on real biological sequence design (SIX6: 0.165 → 0.135; PHO4: 0.175 → 0.130). On set generation with S=64, the path-dependent model also achieves lower FCS for both K=16 and K=24. The paper additionally shows (Figure 5, Section 3.3) that the path-dependent model converges roughly 100× faster in separating aliased states in the LINES environment, and (Figure 7) that it navigates the sparse grid-world target more effectively than the Markovian counterpart, which struggles to explore the space.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GFlowNet framework (Bengio et al., 2021; 2023), which casts discrete amortized sampling as learning a Markovian MDP over a pointed DAG, by lifting the state space with a learnable latent dynamical system that makes the policy path-dependent. The lifting concept is directly inspired by Hamiltonian Monte Carlo's momentum augmentation for continuous sampling, while the specific recurrent architecture draws on Fast Weight Programmers and Self-Referential Weight Matrices (Schmidhuber, 1993; Irie et al., 2022; Schlag et al., 2021). The paper also builds on recent GNN expressivity analyses (Silva et al., 2025a; Kim et al., 2025a) to prove that path-dependence strictly increases the hypothesis class for graph-structured samplers, and extends the standard GFlowNet learning objectives (TB, SubTB, CB) to the lifted setting with matching correctness guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and provably demonstrates a fundamental expressivity bottleneck in Markovian GFlowNets: state aliasing can make certain target distributions entirely unlearnable regardless of network capacity, a limitation that path-dependence resolves. This reframes the GFlowNet design space and suggests that partially observable MDP techniques are more broadly applicable to discrete amortized sampling than previously recognized. Key open questions include whether stochastic latent dynamics (which make the lifted state space uncountable) can be rigorously formulated and trained, and whether the SRWM architecture scales to larger, more complex compositional tasks beyond the relatively small benchmarks evaluated here.
