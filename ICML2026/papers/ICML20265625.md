## Problem

Existing physics benchmarks for evaluating (M)LLMs suffer from outdated Olympiad coverage (stopping at 2021), limited or absent multimodal content, coarse answer-only evaluation, and no mechanism for direct comparison with human contestants. HiPhO addresses all four gaps by compiling the 13 most recent high school physics Olympiads (2024–2025) and introducing a human-aligned, medal-based evaluation protocol that places model scores on the same scale as real Olympiad participants.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265625/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each problem is presented as a structured JSON record containing the problem stem, optional sub-questions, a figure path (one of four modality types: text-only, text+illustration, text+variable figure, text+data figure), and an official step-level rubric. MLLMs receive both text and figures; LLMs receive text only. The evaluation produces a per-problem score (the maximum of answer-level and step-level scores), a total exam score summed across all problems, and a medal assignment (gold/silver/bronze) by comparing the exam score against official human medal cutoffs. Each problem is scored over 8 independent inference runs at temperature 0.6, with the mean used as the final score.

## Method

The core contribution is a dual-level grading pipeline that mirrors human Olympiad examiners. At the answer level, a rule-based math-verifier first checks numeric/symbolic equivalence, and mismatches are adjudicated by Gemini-2.5-Flash to handle alternative expressions. At the step level, the model's full solution is scored against each criterion in the official rubric by Gemini-2.5-Flash, which returns a partial-credit score (e.g., 1.0, 0.5, 0.0) per criterion; for exams with multiple official marking schemes, the maximum across schemes is taken. The final per-problem score is the maximum of the two levels, preserving full credit for correct answers while rewarding valid intermediate reasoning. Medal thresholds are set to the minimum theoretical score among official medalists at each level, enabling direct model-to-human comparison. A partial-prompt completion test (ROUGE-L and answer accuracy on the first 60% of each problem) confirms minimal data leakage across 8 representative models.

## Results

On the HiPhO benchmark (13 physics Olympiads, 2024–2025), the evaluation metric is total exam score mapped to gold/silver/bronze medal thresholds. Gemini-3-Pro achieves the highest medal count with 13 gold medals (e.g., 25.2/29.4 on IPhO-2025), while GPT-5.2, Gemini-2.5-Pro, P1-235B-A22B, and GPT-OSS-120B (high) each earn 12 golds. Even the best model (25.2) trails the top human contestant (29.2) on IPhO-2025, and most open-source MLLMs remain at or below the bronze threshold. Across modality types, performance drops monotonically from Text-Only (84% for GPT-5) to Text+Data Figure (68%), and Optics is the hardest physics field (all models <66%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among closed-source MLLMs, Gemini-3-Pro leads with 13/13 gold medals, narrowly ahead of GPT-5.2 and Gemini-2.5-Pro (12 golds each); GPT-5 and o3 follow with 11 golds, while Grok-4 (10), Claude-4-Sonnet-Thinking (8), and o4-mini (6) trail further behind. A striking finding is that open-source LLMs rival or surpass several closed-source MLLMs: P1-235B-A22B and GPT-OSS-120B (high) each earn 12 golds (matching GPT-5.2 and Gemini-2.5-Pro), and DeepSeek-V3.2-Thinking (11 golds) outperforms Grok-4, Claude-4-Sonnet-Thinking, and o4-mini. However, open-source MLLMs lag substantially behind their LLM counterparts of similar scale—Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B-Thinking (10 golds) underperforms Qwen3-235B-A22B-Thinking (10 golds) and P1-235B-A22B (12 golds), suggesting multimodal training trades off physics reasoning accuracy. Even the strongest model (Gemini-3-Pro, 25.2/29.4 on IPhO-2025) falls well short of the top human score (29.2/30), and on harder Olympiads like EuPhO-2025 the gap widens (best model 21.0 vs. top human 27.0). Scaling within model families consistently helps: Qwen3 improves from 6 golds (30B) to 10 golds (235B), and Qwen3-VL from 8 to 10 golds over the same range.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PHYSICS evaluation framework (Zheng et al., 2026) by adding step-level rubric-based grading and medal-threshold scoring, while departing from prior physics benchmarks (OlympiadBench, OlympicArena, SeePhys, PhysReason) that suffer from outdated coverage, limited modality, and answer-only evaluation. Conceptually, it bridges the gap between mathematical Olympiad benchmarks (where models like GPT-5.2 have reached 100% on AIME) and physics Olympiad evaluation, which had lacked systematic, up-to-date, human-aligned assessment. The evaluation pipeline reuses math-verify for answer checking and introduces Gemini-2.5-Flash as a step-level grader validated against human experts, a design choice motivated by showing GPT-4o systematically underestimates model performance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HiPhO is the first benchmark to enable direct, medal-standard comparison between (M)LLMs and human Olympiad contestants, revealing that even the strongest closed-source MLLMs (Gemini-3-Pro, GPT-5.2) remain well below top human scores on the hardest exams (IPhO, APhO, EuPhO). The finding that open-source text-only LLMs outperform open-source MLLMs on physics Olympiads challenges the assumption that multimodal perception should aid physics reasoning, pointing to a trade-off in current multimodal training. Open questions include how to evaluate experimental physics (embodied reasoning, diagram generation), whether the gap to top humans will close with scaling, and how to build a reliable open-source grader to reduce dependence on proprietary models for evaluation.
