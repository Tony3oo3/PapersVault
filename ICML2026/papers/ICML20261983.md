## Problem

Existing incomplete multi-view clustering methods assume relatively balanced missingness across views, but real-world data often exhibits severe imbalance in observation rates (e.g., medical imaging at 20% vs. blood tests at 100%). This unbalanced pattern creates observation bias that (1) prevents cross-view associations learned from scarce complete samples from generalizing to incomplete ones, and (2) causes clustering predictions to spuriously depend on low-missing-rate views rather than intrinsic data structure.

## Input / Output

The method takes a multi-view dataset with N samples and V views where each view has a distinct missing rate (e.g., for 4 views at AMR=0.5: 50%, 37.5%, 12.5%, 0% missing), along with the binary observation mask matrix M ∈ {0,1}^{N×V}. It produces K cluster assignments via k-means on a fused representation that concatenates observed view encodings with meta-generated representations for missing views. The meta-generator outputs a conditional Gaussian (mean and diagonal variance) per missing view, with the mean used deterministically at inference.

## Method

CIMLN integrates three modules: (1) per-view encoder-decoder autoencoders projecting each view into a shared latent space; (2) a context-aware meta-generation module that formulates missing-view recovery as a meta-learning task, using complete samples as a support set and encoding global cross-view statistics via expected Hadamard products (context vectors) to condition a Gaussian generator, enabling rapid adaptation from scarce paired data; and (3) a causal-invariant structure learning module that models observation masks as confounders in a structural causal model, constructs counterfactual scenarios by randomly masking available views with probability β, and enforces KL-divergence consistency between factual and counterfactual clustering assignments to eliminate spurious dependence on observation patterns. Training proceeds in two stages: first optimizing reconstruction loss for the autoencoders, then jointly training all modules with L = L_rec + λ₁·L_meta + λ₂·L_cau. The paper provides theoretical guarantees including a Rademacher-complexity-based generalization bound for the meta-generator and a Pinsker-inequality-based proof that bounded causal-invariance loss implies ε-invariant cluster assignments across observation patterns.

## Results

CIMLN achieves the best ACC on all eight benchmark datasets under unbalanced missingness (AMR=0.5), with margins of 0.21–4.62 percentage points over the second-best method. The largest gains appear on bias-sensitive datasets: CiteSeer (56.55% ACC, +4.62 over FIMCFG), YouTubeFace20 (76.74% ACC, +2.32), and FashionMNIST (56.33% ACC, +0.53). On NMI, CIMLN leads on 7/8 datasets (losing only 0.21 on Reuters to FIMCFG), and on ARI it leads on all 8, with particularly large margins on CiteSeer (+4.71) and FashionMNIST (+6.39).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CIMLN outperforms all 12 baselines on ACC across every dataset, with the closest competitor being FIMCFG (federated graph-guided method, ICML 2025), which ranks second on 5 of 8 datasets. The largest absolute gains are on CiteSeer (+4.62 ACC over FIMCFG, +4.71 ARI) and FashionMNIST (+6.39 ARI over DVIMC), indicating that the causal-invariant counterfactual mechanism is most beneficial when observation bias is severe. PMIMC (prototype matching, IEEE TIP 2025) is the strongest recovery-based competitor, placing second on Caltech (83.64 vs 83.93 ACC) and Scene-15 (40.58 vs 41.29 ACC). On NMI, CIMLN is narrowly beaten by FIMCFG on Reuters (23.88 vs 24.09, a 0.21 gap), the only metric-dataset combination where CIMLN does not lead. Older GAN-based (GP-MVC) and contrastive (DCP, SURE) methods trail by 10–30+ points, confirming that unbalanced missingness is far more challenging than the balanced settings they were designed for. The consistent superiority across both recovery-based (CPMN, ESFMC) and recovery-free (DVIMC, APADC) paradigms suggests that jointly addressing view recovery quality and observation-pattern invariance is necessary rather than sufficient in isolation.

## Relation to Prior Work

CIMLN repurposes two established paradigms—meta-learning and causal inference—into a unified framework for a specific gap in incomplete multi-view clustering (IMVC). Prior IMVC methods (GP-MVC, DCP, COMPLETER, DIVIDE, etc.) assume balanced or random missingness and focus on recovery or contrastive alignment, while causal multi-view clustering work (CausalMVC, CauMVC) addresses spurious correlations but does not model observation masks as confounders under unbalanced missingness. The paper's key conceptual move is treating the observation mask as a confounding variable in a structural causal model and using counterfactual masking to enforce invariance, while simultaneously framing missing-view generation as a meta-learning adaptation task conditioned on global second-order statistics. No prior work combines these two mechanisms for the unbalanced IMVC setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a practically important and previously under-addressed failure mode: under unbalanced missingness, standard IMVC methods develop spurious dependence on low-missing-rate views, degrading both recovery quality and clustering fairness. The theoretical guarantees (Rademacher-based generalization bound for the meta-generator and a Pinsker-based invariance bound for the causal loss) provide rigorous justification for the design choices. Open questions include how the method behaves when the complete-sample set |S_c| becomes very small (extreme unbalancedness), whether the simplified binary-mask causal model captures real-world confounding adequately, and whether the random-masking intervention strategy generalizes to structured or adversarial missingness patterns. The approach is also limited to a fixed AMR=0.5 protocol, leaving open how performance scales across the full missingness spectrum.
