## Problem

Semi-supervised referring expression segmentation (SS-RES) relies on pseudo-labeling to exploit unlabeled image-text pairs, but fixed confidence thresholds are brittle under the referential ambiguity, occlusion, and long-range reasoning inherent to RES. External MLLM priors and the segmentor's own predictions exhibit systematic, sample-dependent disagreements that no static threshold can resolve, causing either discarded informative samples or noisy supervision that rapidly contaminates training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260409/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (resized to 480×480) paired with a free-form natural language referring expression and produces a pixel-level binary foreground probability map. During training, a frozen MLLM (Qwen2.5-VL) and SAM2 generate offline semantic-spatial priors for unlabeled pairs, but at inference the system relies solely on the trained segmentation network with no external model calls. The weak-to-strong augmentation paradigm applies intensity-based image perturbations and EDA-style text edits (synonym replacement, insertion, swap, deletion) to construct consistency supervision, with directional words swapped to preserve language-image alignment under geometric transforms.

## Method

L2L formulates pseudo-label construction as a learnable decision-making process with three co-evolving components. SPM fuses the MLLM/SAM2 soft prior with the model's weak-view prediction in logit space using a pixel-adaptive weight that accounts for per-pixel certainty (margin-based score) and image-level agreement, producing a calibrated prior that is iteratively refined as the model improves. SESM injects this prior into a 4-stage Swin Transformer encoder via stage-adaptive gated modulation—local linear projection in shallow stages (S1–S2) and cross-attention in deep stages (S3–S4)—with learnable gating factors that dynamically balance semantic (text) and structural (spatial) guidance at each level. RPLE casts pseudo-label acceptance as a DDPG actor-critic problem: a 6-dimensional state (confidence mean/std, class proportions, agreement score) drives an actor that outputs adaptive foreground/background thresholds, while a critic scores the selected pseudo-labels via a multi-perspective reward combining separability gain, consistency gain, and coverage gain, enabling sample-adaptive thresholding that dynamically balances precision and coverage throughout training without manual tuning.

## Results

On RefCOCO (5% labeled), L2L achieves 67.30/71.46/62.60 Overall IoU (val/testA/testB), outperforming the strongest SS-RES baseline SemiRES (61.31/66.64/55.94) by +6.0/+4.8/+6.7 IoU. On RefCOCO+ (5%), it reaches 57.44/63.60/50.43, and on RefCOCOg (5%) it reaches 59.71/61.11 (val-u/test-u). Under the extreme 0.1% label budget, L2L (65.08/68.53/59.39 on RefCOCO) surpasses the strongest zero-shot baseline SAM 3 Agent (59.4/64.3/55.0) by over 5 IoU on val, demonstrating that even a handful of labeled samples combined with MLLM priors can exceed fully zero-shot performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among SS-RES baselines, L2L consistently outperforms all competitors across every dataset and label budget. The closest SS-RES baseline, SemiRES (which uses SAM for pseudo-label refinement), is surpassed by +6.0 IoU on RefCOCO val and +12.1 IoU on RefCOCO+ testB at 5% labeled; RESMatch, FixMatch, CCVC, and AugSeg trail by even larger margins (10–35 IoU). Against the paper's own ablation baselines, the naive linear-fusion MLLM baseline (w/ MLLM, fixed 0.5 mixing) is improved by +3.2 IoU on RefCOCO val, confirming that the reinforced selection and uncertainty-aware calibration add value beyond simply blending priors. In the near-zero-shot comparison (0.1% labeled, Table 2), L2L beats all zero-shot methods except SAM 3 Agent on RefCOCOg (51.01 vs 57.2 val-u), while surpassing SAM 3 Agent on RefCOCO (65.08 vs 59.4 val) and RefCOCO+ (55.97 vs 51.4 val). It also outperforms other zero-shot methods such as HybridGL (49.5 val), VLM-VG (49.9 val), and LGD+DINO (49.5 val) by 15+ IoU on RefCOCO, indicating that even minimal supervision combined with MLLM priors substantially exceeds purely zero-shot grounding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the semi-supervised RES (SS-RES) line initiated by RESMatch (consistency regularization with fixed-threshold pseudo-labeling) and SemiRES (SAM-guided pseudo-label refinement) by replacing static confidence thresholds with a reinforcement-learning-driven, sample-adaptive pseudo-label selection process (DDPG actor-critic). It further departs from prior SS-RES work by introducing a frozen MLLM (Qwen2.5-VL) + SAM2 pipeline to generate semantic-spatial priors, which are uncertainty-calibrated via logit-space fusion (SPM) and injected into a hierarchical Swin Transformer encoder through stage-adaptive gated modulation (SESM). The overall framework is a closed-loop self-evolving system where the segmentor and pseudo-label quality co-improve, building on the weak-to-strong consistency paradigm of FixMatch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a core practical bottleneck in SS-RES: fixed-threshold pseudo-labeling is brittle under the referential ambiguity and occlusion inherent to referring expressions, and the RL-based adaptive selection offers a principled alternative that dynamically balances precision and coverage. However, ablations reveal that the MLLM+SAM2 prior contributes the bulk of the performance gain (baseline w/ MLLM already reaches 64.07 val IoU vs. 67.30 for the full method), leaving the RL and SESM components with a modest ~3-point marginal contribution. Open questions include whether the RL pseudo-label selection generalizes beyond RES to other semi-supervised segmentation tasks, whether the offline MLLM dependency is practical at scale, and how the method handles compositional reasoning failures (spatial/ordinal/counting constraints) that the authors acknowledge as persistent limitations.
