## Problem

Existing LLM-based optimization modeling methods rely on code-execution error signals for self-correction, which cannot detect structural flaws (e.g., missing flow-balance constraints) or logically infeasible solutions that a solver still executes successfully. This limits modeling accuracy because the underlying mathematical formulation can remain fundamentally incorrect even when the solver code runs without failure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262922/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a natural-language OR problem description as input and produces a parameterized optimization model (LaTeX formulation with symbolic parameters), executable Gurobi solver code, and the optimal solution. The formulation is scale-agnostic: problem dimensions are represented by symbolic parameters (e.g., n_warehouses, n_customers) rather than hardcoded values, so the same model logic applies whether the instance has 20 or 20,000 entities. The framework is plug-and-play and can be layered on top of any existing prompt-based or fine-tuned OR LLM to verify and refine its output.

## Method

Opt-Verifier introduces a dual-side verification mechanism built on a multi-agent pipeline with a fixed, directed workflow (no management agent). First, a structure distillation agent extracts a multi-level modeling structure (high-level problem type, medium-level variant, low-level specific constraints) from the problem description, which is then used to guide a formulation agent in generating the initial model. Structure-side verification back-translates the generated model into an abstract structure and compares it against the original structure (inspired by dual learning in machine translation) to detect omissions or mismatches. Solution-side verification interprets the solver's numerical output in natural language and critiques that interpretation for logical or mathematical violations invisible to code-execution checks. A refinement agent then corrects the model using the combined feedback. All agents are implemented with GPT-4o-mini, and the fixed workflow avoids the redundant agent calls that plague management-agent-based multi-agent baselines, yielding lower token usage and faster inference.

## Results

Opt-Verifier achieves a micro-average solving accuracy (SA) of 64.3% across five benchmarks (NL4Opt, Mamo ComplexLP, ComplexOR, IndustryOR, OptMATH), representing an approximately 20% absolute improvement over the Standard prompting baseline (32.7%) and a 13.7-point gain over the strongest prompt-based method OptiMUS (50.6%). It also outperforms the strongest reasoning LLMs—OpenAI-o1 (58.1%) and DeepSeek-R1 (56.7%)—despite using the much weaker GPT-4o-mini as its backbone. On the most challenging benchmark, IndustryOR, Opt-Verifier reaches 45.0% SA versus 36.0% for OpenAI-o1 and 38.0% for ORLM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Opt-Verifier (GPT-4o-mini backbone) wins on every benchmark where a direct comparison is available. Against prompt-based baselines, it improves over OptiMUS by 13.7 points in micro-average SA (64.3 vs. 50.6), over CoE by 17.7 points (64.3 vs. 46.6), and over CoT by 27.1 points (64.3 vs. 37.2). Against fine-tuned OR LLMs, it surpasses LLMOPT (47.7) by 16.6 points and ORLM (41.3) by 23.0 points in micro-average; on NL4Opt specifically, Opt-Verifier (96.5) edges out SIRL (96.3) and OptMATH (95.9). Against reasoning LLMs, Opt-Verifier beats OpenAI-o1 (58.1) by 6.2 points and DeepSeek-R1 (56.7) by 7.6 points in micro-average, despite using a substantially weaker base model. In the plug-and-play experiments (Table 3), Opt-Verifier further boosts OptiMUS from 50.6 to 64.3 micro-average and ORLM from 41.3 to 56.9, confirming its generality as a verification layer. On the cleaned ReSocratic benchmark (Appendix A.1), Opt-Verifier reaches 89.6% SA versus 48.4% for Standard and 87.6% for OptiMUS.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the LLM-based optimization modeling literature, departing from prior prompt-based multi-agent frameworks (CoE, OptiMUS) that rely solely on code-execution errors for self-correction, and from fine-tuned OR LLMs (ORLM, LLMOPT, OptMATH, SIRL) that improve generation but lack structural verification. The key conceptual departure is the dual-side verification mechanism: structure-side verification borrows the back-translation idea from dual learning in machine translation (He et al., 2016), while solution-side verification leverages LLM common-sense reasoning to check logical coherence of numeric solutions. The multi-level modeling structure (high/medium/low) formalizes the coarse-to-fine analysis that human OR experts perform, providing a structured reference that prior methods lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design means Opt-Verifier can enhance any existing OR LLM or prompt-based pipeline without retraining, making it broadly applicable across the growing ecosystem of optimization modeling tools. A notable limitation is that verification precision and recall degrade on hard problems (68% recall for structure verification), suggesting the framework still struggles with problems that fall outside conventional OR classifications. Open questions include whether the verification agents can be replaced by smaller or specialized models to reduce cost, and whether the approach generalizes to stochastic or multi-stage optimization problems beyond the deterministic LP/MILP setting tested here.
