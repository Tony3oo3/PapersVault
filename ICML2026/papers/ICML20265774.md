## Problem

LLMs struggle to follow precise length constraints because they lack an internal representation of length — a 'cognition-action gap' where the model cannot independently track or plan output length. Existing approaches either impose external length markers (incurring inference overhead and poor generalization) or treat length as a scalar RL reward (entangling length with semantic features in the representation space), both of which fail to establish the missing cognitive basis for length control.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language instruction containing an explicit word-count constraint (e.g., 'write exactly 200 words about X') and produces a text response matching that target length. Length constraints span 10 to 4,000 words, and word counting is bilingual: Chinese characters are each counted as one word while English uses whitespace-delimited tokenization. During training, the Hindsight Awareness component additionally consumes the model's own generated output concatenated with a counting prompt, producing a structured XML-formatted word count as the supervised target.

## Method

LARFT unifies three components: (1) Length-Oriented RL via GRPO with a piecewise-linear reward R_len = max(0, 1 − |L(τ)−c|/c) that provides dense, bounded length signals; (2) Hindsight Length Awareness, which repurposes on-policy rollouts by concatenating each generated trajectory with a counting instruction ('Count how many words in the text above?') and training a supervised negative-log-likelihood loss on the actual word count, thereby decoupling length from semantics; (3) a Unified Optimization Mechanism that combines both losses as L_GRPO + λ_t · L_aware, where λ_t follows a cosine annealing schedule from λ_max to 0, establishing a coarse-to-fine curriculum that prioritizes length cognition early and shifts to precise generation control later. The key insight is that converting the model's own failed generations into valid counting supervision creates a positive feedback loop: enhanced length awareness improves generation, and improved generation provides better awareness training data.

## Results

LARFT achieves state-of-the-art length instruction following across four base models (Qwen2.5-3B/7B, Llama-3.2-3B, Llama-3.1-8B), with an average improvement of +20.92 points over untrained models across LIFEBench, LongBench, and Lenctrl-Bench. On Qwen2.5-7B, LARFT attains LIFEBench LS=80.57 (vs. 70.94 for pure RL), LongBench S_l=96.75, and Lenctrl-Bench MAE=7.00, while incurring only a −1.45-point average drop on four general benchmarks (MMLU, GSM8K, IFEval, GPQA). The method surpasses the strongest RL baseline by 4.59 points on average and maintains or improves generation quality (S_q, ROUGE-L) relative to base models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LARFT decisively outperforms both tailored length-following methods: Ruler (Li et al., 2024, meta-token intervals) and PositionID (Wang et al., 2024, explicit word-count injection) suffer severe degradation on short-length constraints (MAE 53–86 vs. LARFT's 5–8) and achieve LIFEBench LS of only 27–34, roughly half of LARFT's 66–81. Against the strongest RL baseline (pure GRPO with length reward), LARFT gains +4.59 points average LS across four models (e.g., 80.57 vs. 70.94 on Qwen2.5-7B) while also improving LongBench S_l by up to 4.36 points and maintaining higher IFEval scores (81.53 vs. 79.74 on Qwen2.5-7B). In the extended comparison (Table 14), LARFT surpasses Hansel (Song et al., 2025) by 11.67 LS points (80.57 vs. 68.90) and MarkerGen (Yuan et al., 2025a) by 14.77 points (80.57 vs. 65.80) on Qwen2.5-7B. Notably, SFT-based paradigms (SFT, PositionID, Ruler) all exhibit >15-point MAE increases on Lenctrl-Bench, confirming that supervised fine-tuning alone cannot achieve precise length control, whereas LARFT's RL+awareness combination resolves this failure mode.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two established paradigms for length instruction following: external length-marker methods (Ruler, PositionID, MarkerGen) that inject rigid length tokens, and pure RL-based policy optimization that treats length as a scalar reward. Its key conceptual departure is the 'cognition-action gap' framing, arguing that both paradigms fail because they never establish an internal length representation. The Hindsight Length Awareness mechanism repurposes the Hindsight Experience Replay idea (Andrychowicz et al., 2017) from robotics to convert failed on-policy rollouts into supervised counting tasks, while the RL component uses GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) within the RLVR paradigm. The unified cosine-annealed joint loss is a straightforward scheduling choice, but the specific combination of on-policy hindsight counting with policy optimization is the paper's methodological contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Length instruction following is a practical bottleneck for real-world LLM applications (creative writing, report generation, token-budget-constrained inference), and the paper demonstrates that the gap is substantial and addressable without sacrificing general capabilities. The finding that SFT-based length-control methods actively degrade short-length following (MAE +15 points) while the off-policy counting variant also hurts performance challenges the assumption that more supervised length data is always beneficial. Open questions include whether the cognition-action gap framework generalizes to other instruction dimensions (format, style, structure), whether the approach scales beyond 4,000-word constraints, and whether the linear-probing evidence of length representations translates to more complex multi-constraint settings.
