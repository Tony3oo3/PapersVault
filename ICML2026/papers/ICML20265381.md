## Problem

Unified visual tokenization faces a fundamental zero-sum trade-off: pixel-level reconstruction gradients unfold the latent manifold to capture high-frequency detail, while semantic alignment gradients collapse it for conceptual invariance, causing destructive interference that prevents any single tokenizer from excelling at both generation and understanding. Existing unified approaches (UniTok, TokenFlow, UniLIP) merely balance these conflicting objectives with weighted sums, leaving mid-frequency structural information under-modeled and forcing a choice between generative fidelity and semantic robustness.

## Input / Output

The tokenizer consumes 224×224 RGB images and produces 256 continuous visual tokens (learnable queries) in a compact latent space, decoded back to 256×256 pixels via a DC-AE pixel decoder. When integrated into the full UMM, the system additionally accepts text prompts and reference images with editing instructions, producing generated or edited images through a SANA Diffusion Transformer conditioned on dual-pathway features (dense multimodal hidden states plus compact query embeddings) from a frozen InternVL3 MLLM backbone. The 6-layer Synergistic Block connector bridges the MLLM's semantic space to the DiT's generation space, with the entire pipeline trained on ~36M image-text pairs from BLIP3-o, CC12M, and JourneyDB.

## Method

MUSE proposes the Gradient Orthogonality Hypothesis, empirically verified across random, CLIP, MAE, and DINOv2 backbones: semantic gradients naturally concentrate in W_V while structural gradients cluster in W_Q,K, making these objectives reside in orthogonal parameter subspaces. The Synergistic Block physically enforces this by splitting self-attention into a Topology Stream (W_Q, W_K producing the adjacency matrix A, optimized solely by a KL-divergence Structural Topology Alignment loss against DINOv3 teacher attention maps) and a Semantic Stream (W_V producing feature values, optimized by an NCE-based Active Semantic Anchoring loss against text embeddings), with stop-gradient operators preventing cross-contamination. Training follows a three-stage curriculum: topology warmup (frozen encoder, structural alignment only), semantic injection (anchoring values while preserving learned topology), and synergistic tuning (end-to-end with adversarial reconstruction). This decoupling transforms gradient conflict (cos θ_g ≈ −0.15 in naive baselines) into positive synergy (cos θ_g ≈ +0.04), enabling MUSE to match specialist generation quality (gFID 3.08) while surpassing its own teacher InternViT-300M in linear probing (85.2% vs. 82.5%).

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, MUSE achieves gFID 3.08 and rFID 0.62 for generation, while simultaneously reaching 85.2% linear probing accuracy—surpassing its own teacher InternViT-300M (82.5%)—and 76.1% zero-shot accuracy. On ADE-20K, MUSE attains mIoU 46.5% and segmentation accuracy 72.8%, far exceeding all unified baselines (mIoU 15.4–36.8%). In the controlled UMM comparison (Table 3), MUSE-3B outperforms UniLIP-3B on every metric: +0.8 MMB, +1.1 MMMU, +1.8 MMVP, +0.03 GenEval Position, and +0.14 ImgEdit Overall, under identical backbone and data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary controlled baseline UniLIP (identical backbone, data, and training protocol), MUSE wins on every metric: gFID drops from 5.73 to 3.08, linear probing jumps from 76.2% to 85.2%, and mIoU surges from 15.4% to 46.5%, demonstrating that the gradient decoupling eliminates the zero-sum trade-off. Compared to VTP-L-d64, which achieves the best prior gFID (3.01), MUSE matches generation quality (3.08) while gaining +4.7 points in linear probing (85.2 vs. 80.5) and +9.7 mIoU (46.5 vs. 36.8), showing that VTP's 'high-fidelity trap' erodes semantics. MUSE also outperforms its own teacher InternViT-300M on linear probing (85.2% vs. 82.5%), a result the authors attribute to structurally aligned reconstruction actively refining semantic perception. Against generation specialists, MUSE matches VA-VAE-d32's gFID (3.08 vs. 4.56) while adding full understanding capability (76.1% zero-shot vs. none). In the UMM setting (Table 3), MUSE-3B surpasses UniLIP-3B by +0.8 MMB, +1.1 MMMU, +1.8 MMVP, and +0.14 ImgEdit Overall, and exceeds the generation specialist FLUX.1-dev on WISE (0.65 vs. 0.50) while matching its GenEval Position score (0.89 vs. 0.68). The only metric where MUSE trails a specialist is DINOv3-L's mIoU (46.5 vs. 53.1) and linear probing (85.2 vs. 86.4), but MUSE uniquely provides decodable tokens for generation, which DINOv3 cannot.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unified visual tokenizer line (UniTok, TokenFlow, UniLIP, VTP) by reframing the reconstruction-vs-understanding trade-off as a geometric problem of Manifold Misalignment, where pixel and semantic gradients destructively interfere in shared parameter subspaces. It builds on UniLIP's dual-condition UMM architecture and training protocol for evaluation, and on DINOv3's emergent attention segmentation as a structural prior. The key departure is the Gradient Orthogonality Hypothesis: rather than balancing competing losses with weighted sums, MUSE physically routes structural gradients to W_Q,W_K and semantic gradients to W_V via a Synergistic Block, converting interference into orthogonal optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

If the topological orthogonality principle generalizes beyond ViT attention, it offers a lightweight architectural recipe (1.03× params vs. 2× for two-stream) for resolving multi-objective conflicts in any Transformer-based representation learner. The claim that structurally aligned reconstruction can *improve* semantic perception (beating the teacher in linear probing) challenges the common assumption that reconstruction loss dilutes discriminative features. However, the Gradient Orthogonality Hypothesis is tied to the Q/K/V formulation and unverified on non-attention architectures, and the method currently depends on a dense DINOv3 structural teacher; extending to CNNs, hybrid backbones, or lighter structural priors remains open.
