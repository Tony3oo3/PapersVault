## Problem

Existing multimodal unlearning methods evaluate effectiveness solely by output deviation from ground truth, overlooking whether the unlearned model produces coherent, context-aware refusals or instead generates hallucinated, repetitive, and degenerated text. Additionally, a persistent trade-off exists between unlearning quality and model utility: gradient-ascent methods over-suppress the forget set causing global degradation, while utility-preserving methods exhibit incomplete unlearning. The paper addresses both gaps by introducing generation quality as a primary evaluation criterion and proposing a controllable framework that learns a fine-grained refusal boundary.

## Input / Output

The method consumes image-text pairs (an RGB image paired with a natural-language query) drawn from fictional character profiles in MLLMU-Bench or character-based queries in CLEAR. The output is a free-form text response that must either be a contextually appropriate refusal (e.g., 'the answer cannot be inferred from the image alone') for forget-set inputs or a correct, informative answer for retain-set inputs. The critical behavioral requirement is that the model dynamically switches between these two response modes based on a learned boundary, rather than applying a uniform refusal or a uniform answer strategy.

## Method

ASRU is a two-stage framework. Stage 1 (Refusal Steering) constructs a refusal direction as the difference between mean residual-stream activations on a target set (unseen images with privacy queries) and the forget set at a selected layer L*, then induces initial refusal behavior by updating only the down-projection matrix of that layer via a closed-form ridge-regression solution, leaving all other parameters frozen. Stage 2 (Refusal Boundary Optimization) introduces a boundary set of retain samples highly similar to the forget set and applies GRPO with a verifiable rule-based reward: the reward gives 1.0 for valid refusals on forget inputs and correct answers on boundary inputs, 0.0 for ground-truth leakage or over-refusal, and intermediate scores for partial matches. Group-relative advantages computed over G rollouts per paired (forget, boundary) input drive policy updates with clipped ratios and KL regularization, sharpening the refusal boundary into a generalizable, context-aware decision surface.

## Results

On MLLMU-Bench (5% forget, Qwen3-VL-8B), ASRU reduces forget-set classification accuracy to 30.83% (vs. 43.20% for the best baseline MMUnlearner) and forget-set ROUGE-L to 0.398 (vs. 0.537 for GA_Diff), while achieving a 5.8× average improvement in generation quality (CR: 2.98, Forgetfulness: 3.04 vs. best baseline 0.68/0.72). ASRU uses only 27.3% of the supervised retain set, yet retains or even exceeds vanilla model utility on the retain set (53.74% vs. 51.59% classification accuracy). Across all three architectures (Qwen3-VL-4B, Qwen3-VL-8B, LLaVA-1.5-7B), ASRU achieves an average 24.6% gain in unlearning effectiveness over the strongest baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ASRU outperforms all five baselines on both forget quality and generation quality across all three model architectures. On Qwen3-VL-8B (5% forget), ASRU reduces forget-set classification accuracy to 30.83%, beating the strongest forget baseline MMUnlearner (43.20%) by 12.4 points, and forget-set ROUGE-L to 0.398 vs. GA_Diff's 0.537. The generation quality gap is the most dramatic: ASRU scores 2.98/3.04 on Contextual Refusal/Forgetfulness, compared to the best baseline GA_Diff at 0.68/0.72 — a roughly 4–5× margin. In terms of retain utility, ASRU matches or slightly exceeds the vanilla model (53.74% vs. 51.59% retain classification accuracy on Qwen3-VL-8B), whereas GA, NPO, and MMUnlearner all degrade retain performance by 4–8 points. Notably, ASRU achieves these results using only 27.3% of the supervised retain set, while GA_Diff, KL_Min, and MMUnlearner require 100%. On the CLEAR dataset, ASRU also leads with 45.21% forget classification accuracy (vs. 50.00% for NPO, the next best) while maintaining 70.03% real-world accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

ASRU sits at the intersection of two previously separate lines of work: activation steering for representation-level control (Jorgensen et al., 2023; Shen et al., 2025) and GRPO-based policy optimization for LLM alignment (Shao et al., 2024). Prior multimodal unlearning methods (GA, NPO, MMUnlearner) operate purely at the output level—maximizing loss on the forget set or applying preference optimization—without explicitly modeling the *form* of the model's response. ASRU departs from this by treating the refusal behavior itself as a learnable representation-level property (via a closed-form down-projection update) and then refining the decision boundary with a verifiable reward, a two-stage decomposition not seen in prior unlearning work. The paper also introduces generation quality (contextual refusal coherence and absence of hallucination) as a first-class optimization and evaluation objective, which prior multimodal unlearning benchmarks like MLLMU-Bench did not measure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By elevating generation quality to a core unlearning objective, the paper addresses a practical safety gap: an unlearned model that hallucinates plausible-but-wrong answers (e.g., fabricated medical advice after record removal) is arguably more dangerous than one that simply refuses. The two-stage design—cheap representation-level initialization followed by targeted RL—offers a data-efficient recipe (only 27.3% of retain supervision needed) that could be adopted by other unlearning pipelines. Limitations include restriction to image-text pairs, reliance on a GPT-based judge for generation quality (mitigated by human ICC validation), and evaluation on only two benchmarks; extension to video, audio, and larger model families remains open. A key open question is whether the boundary-set construction strategy generalizes to open-world unlearning requests where 'similar-but-retain' samples are not readily available.
