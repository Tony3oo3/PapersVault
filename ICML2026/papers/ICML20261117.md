## Problem

SAM3D enables open-world, mask-conditioned single-view 3D reconstruction but suffers from prohibitive inference latency (462s per scene) that blocks interactive deployment. The paper identifies that generic acceleration strategies—uniform step skipping or random token pruning—are brittle for SAM3D because the pipeline exhibits multi-level heterogeneity: shape tokens evolve smoothly while layout tokens are volatile, texture refinement is spatially sparse, and geometric spectral content varies dramatically across object instances. This is the first systematic profiling of SAM3D's inference dynamics and the first training-free framework that exploits these heterogeneities simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and a binary object mask as input, encoded via a pretrained DINOv2 vision encoder into visual tokens. It produces an explicit 3D mesh with texture and global layout parameters (rotation, translation, scale) per object. The pipeline is two-stage coarse-to-fine: a Sparse Structure Generator (5000 tokens, 25 denoising steps) predicts a voxel-like coarse structure and layout, then a Sparse Latent Generator (26892 tokens, 25 steps) refines fine geometry and appearance, followed by a mesh decoder (26335 tokens) and a 3D Gaussian Splatting decoder. Fast-SAM3D is purely an inference-time acceleration layer requiring no retraining of the underlying SAM3D backbone.

## Method

Fast-SAM3D integrates three plug-and-play, heterogeneity-aware modules. (1) Modality-Aware Step Caching (MASC) in the SS Generator disentangles update rules: shape tokens are extrapolated via 1st-order Taylor expansion from a finite-difference gradient, while volatile layout tokens use momentum-anchored smoothing that blends linear extrapolation with the last full-backbone anchor (β=0.5) to suppress pose drift. (2) Joint Spatiotemporal Token Carving in the SLaT Generator computes a unified importance score combining temporal saliency (L2 update magnitude and abruptness) with spatial saliency (FFT-based high-frequency energy), retaining only the top-10% active tokens per step; a curvature-aware switching mechanism triggers full backbone evaluation only when accumulated relative change exceeds a threshold (ε=1.5). (3) Spectral-Aware Token Aggregation in the mesh decoder computes a joint 2D-mask/3D-voxel High-Frequency Energy Ratio to select an instance-adaptive downsampling factor (S ∈ {1.25, 1.50, 2.00}), then quantizes token coordinates into voxel-aligned bins and max-pools features, reducing token count by ~S³ while preserving salient local geometry. Together these modules achieve 2.67× object-level speedup with negligible or even improved geometric fidelity.

## Results

Fast-SAM3D achieves a 2.67× object-level speedup (31.04s → 11.60s) and 2.01× scene-level speedup (462.3s → 229.7s) over the original SAM3D, while simultaneously improving geometric fidelity: F1@0.05 rises from 92.34 to 92.59, vIoU from 0.543 to 0.552, and ICP-rot drops from 19.32° to 17.71°. The method reduces FLOPs from 639.59T to 201.78T (3.2× reduction). On the ADT scene-layout benchmark, Fast-SAM3D matches SAM3D's 3D-IoU (0.401 vs 0.403) while halving inference time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Fast-SAM3D dominates all five baselines on the speed–quality Pareto frontier. Against the step-caching baselines TaylorSeer (1.35×) and EasyCache (1.34×), Fast-SAM3D nearly doubles the object-level speedup (2.67×) while recovering the quality they sacrifice: F1 improves from 90.95/87.06 to 92.59, and vIoU from 0.504/0.432 to 0.552. Fast3Dcache, which relies on multi-view redundancy, is essentially ineffective in the single-view setting (1.03× speedup, 3D-IoU collapses to 0.051), confirming that its design does not transfer. The naive Random Drop baseline suffers catastrophic structural failure (3D-IoU 0.094, F1 83.52), demonstrating that 3D token sparsity cannot be exploited without saliency-aware selection. Uniform Merge achieves a 2.01× object speedup but at the cost of F1 (91.48) and 3D-IoU (0.367), whereas Fast-SAM3D's spectral-aware aggregation preserves F1 at 92.59 and 3D-IoU at 0.375. Notably, Fast-SAM3D slightly exceeds the unaccelerated SAM3D on F1 and vIoU, which the authors attribute to the saliency mechanism acting as a spatial denoising filter that prunes low-confidence noisy tokens.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SAM3D's two-stage coarse-to-fine diffusion pipeline (Sparse Structure Generator → Sparse Latent Generator → Mesh Decoder) and positions itself as a training-free, system-level acceleration layer rather than a new reconstruction model. It departs from generic diffusion acceleration techniques (uniform step skipping à la TaylorSeer/EasyCache, random token pruning, multi-view token caching à la Fast3Dcache) by arguing that their failure in 3D stems from a 'multi-level heterogeneity'—kinematic distinctiveness between shape and layout tokens, intrinsic spatial sparsity of texture refinement, and instance-level spectral variance—that 2D-oriented methods ignore. The three proposed modules (modality-aware step caching, spatiotemporal token carving, spectral-aware token aggregation) are each targeted at one of these heterogeneity axes, making the framework a model-aware composition of known primitives (Taylor extrapolation, FFT-based saliency, coordinate quantization with max-pooling) rather than a new architectural primitive.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

A 2.67× end-to-end speedup with negligible (and in some metrics slightly positive) fidelity loss is practically significant for interactive single-view 3D deployment, where SAM3D's ~31 s/object latency is a hard barrier. The heterogeneity-aware design principle is demonstrated to transfer to TRELLIS (2.26× speedup), suggesting a reusable recipe for accelerating any multi-stage 3D diffusion pipeline, though the evidence base is limited to two backbones. Open questions include whether the spectral complexity proxy generalizes to open-world scenes with many overlapping objects, whether the discrete three-level downsampling schedule can be made continuous, and whether the training-free constraint is ultimately limiting compared to a distilled few-step variant. The paper does not address peak-memory reduction beyond the mesh stage, and the saliency carving's 'denoising' benefit (F1 improvement) is observed on a single dataset configuration, leaving its robustness under distribution shift untested.
