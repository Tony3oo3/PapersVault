## Problem

Standard PPO for LLM reasoning relies on a scalar critic that compresses the full return distribution into a single expectation, discarding variance and multimodality that are critical for accurate credit assignment in sparse-reward reasoning tasks. While Distributional RL could recover this information, existing methods are either computationally intractable over LLM vocabularies (argmax over actions) or depend on off-policy replay buffers that are memory-prohibitive and destabilizing for on-policy LLM fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and an LLM backbone (Qwen2.5-Math-7B, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, or Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) as input. The actor generates a free-form text response (up to 3072–16384 tokens depending on the task). The critic, which shares the actor's backbone architecture and initialization, outputs an ordered set of N=51 quantiles representing the full return distribution at each state; a scalar value for policy optimization is then extracted as the arithmetic mean of these quantiles. The distributional head uses a center-delta parameterization (one base quantile plus N−1 non-negative deltas) to enforce monotonicity by construction.

## Method

DisPPO replaces PPO's scalar critic with a non-parametric quantile-regression critic while keeping the actor as standard PPO. The key mechanism is Distributional GAE (D-GAE), which extends the scalar GAE recursion element-wise to each of the N quantile levels, producing per-quantile multi-step regression targets that approximate the distributional λ-Bellman operator. The critic is trained by minimizing the asymmetric quantile Huber loss between predicted quantiles and D-GAE targets, which is equivalent to a 1-Wasserstein projection onto the N-quantile manifold. The actor update is unchanged: scalar advantages are computed from the mean of the predicted quantiles and fed into the standard PPO clipped surrogate objective. Theoretically, the paper proves that the composite update operator (distributional λ-Bellman followed by quantile projection) is a contraction mapping under the Wasserstein metric with factor γ(1−λ)/(1−γλ), guaranteeing convergence to a unique fixed point.

## Results

On mathematical reasoning (Qwen2.5-Math-7B, avg@k), DisPPO achieves 45.0 average across six benchmarks, improving over PPO (43.1) by +1.9 and over DAPO (42.2) by +2.8. On pass@k, DisPPO reaches 71.2 average vs. PPO's 67.3 (+3.9), with the largest gain on AIME24 (+13.4). On SQL code generation (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct), DisPPO improves greedy accuracy over PPO by +2.1 (in-domain Bird) and +4.2 (out-of-domain Spider). In standard RLHF (LLaMA-3-8B), DisPPO achieves 30.14 on AlpacaEval 2 vs. PPO's 20.77 (+9.37).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DisPPO consistently outperforms all four baselines across every evaluated domain. Against GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), DisPPO leads by +4.5 on math avg@k (45.0 vs. 40.5) and +4.5 on SQL avg (78.9 vs. 74.4). Against DAPO (Yu et al., 2025), DisPPO gains +2.8 on math avg@k and +1.7 on SQL avg, with the advantage widening on out-of-domain Spider (+4.2 greedy over PPO, +4.4 over DAPO). Against standard PPO (Schulman et al., 2017), DisPPO improves by +1.9 on math avg@k, +3.9 on math pass@k, +2.6 on SQL avg, and +1.1 on multi-turn tool-augmented reasoning avg@64. Against VAPO (Yue et al., 2025), a value-regularized PPO variant, DisPPO leads by +1.7 on three-seed math avg (45.3 vs. 43.6) and by +9.37 on AlpacaEval 2 in the RLHF setting, confirming that the distributional critic's gains are not merely an artifact of value regularization.

## Relation to Prior Work

DisPPO sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: distributional RL (Bellemare et al., 2017; Dabney et al., 2018b) and on-policy actor-critic methods for LLMs (PPO, GAE). The key departure from prior distributional RL is the move from off-policy, value-based settings (QR-DQN, D4PG) to an on-policy, policy-gradient-compatible framework, motivated by the memory and stability constraints of LLM training. The paper also distinguishes itself from risk-sensitive distributional policy gradients (WCPG, SDPG) by retaining the standard expected-return objective, and from parametric distributional critics (GMAC) by using non-parametric quantile regression. The theoretical contribution extends the known γ-contraction of the distributional Bellman operator to the composite λ-Bellman-plus-quantile-projection operator under the Wasserstein metric.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that a distributional critic can be dropped into any existing PPO pipeline for LLMs with only ~4% training overhead and no inference cost, making it an accessible upgrade for teams already using actor-critic RL for reasoning. The mechanistic finding that distributional supervision yields more accurate mean value estimates—particularly in the boundary regions where most reasoning states reside—provides a concrete explanation for why the method works beyond just 'more parameters.' Open questions include whether the advantage persists at scale (the paper only evaluates up to 8B models), whether the learned return distribution can be exploited for risk-sensitive or uncertainty-aware decoding, and how the method interacts with the emerging trend toward value-model-free approaches (GRPO, DAPO) that are gaining traction for long-CoT settings.
