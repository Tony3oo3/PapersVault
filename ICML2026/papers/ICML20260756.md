## Problem

A dominant assumption in representation learning holds that globally well-distributed embeddings (high isotropy, uniformity, participation ratio) are a reliable proxy for representational competence. This paper tests whether that geometric regularity actually predicts compositional binding—the ability to encode how visual elements are arranged rather than merely which elements are present—and finds it does not, with standard geometric metrics showing near-zero correlation (r ≤ 0.11) with binding accuracy across 26 vision encoders.

## Input / Output

The diagnostic takes a frozen pretrained vision encoder (CNN or ViT, 26 models spanning contrastive, self-distillation, masked-prediction, variance-decorrelation, vision-language, and supervised families) and a set of 100 probe images (ImageNet validation images or Gaussian noise with μ=0.45, σ=0.225, clipped to [0,1]). It produces a scalar Jacobian Effective Rank (JER) per model via 32 random orthonormal Jacobian-vector products with 5 power iterations, alongside geometric metrics (Global Participation Ratio, Global Isotropy, Local Isotropy) computed on 1,000 ImageNet embeddings. Downstream evaluation uses a synthetic 224×224 attribute-binding task with 3 shapes and 6 colors where query and target share zero colors, forcing reliance on shape-position structure over 500 four-way trials (chance = 25%).

## Method

The paper introduces Jacobian Effective Rank (JER), which applies the Participation Ratio formula to the singular values of the encoder's input-output Jacobian J(x) = ∂f/∂x, estimated via randomized range-finding (Halko et al., 2011) to avoid explicitly forming the full matrix. JER captures how many independent input directions the encoder is locally sensitive to, in contrast to geometric metrics that summarize the covariance spectrum of the embedding distribution. The key empirical finding is that JER correlates strongly with compositional binding (r = 0.69, p < 0.001) while all geometric metrics are uncorrelated (r ≤ 0.11, p > 0.59), and this holds on both natural images and stimulus-agnostic Gaussian noise. A theoretical account (Section 6) explains the disparity: variance-decorrelation objectives (Barlow Twins, VICReg) constrain J(x) through second-order augmentation covariance J(x)Σ_δ J(x)ᵀ, directly penalizing correlated row sensitivities and preserving high effective rank, whereas contrastive, clustering, masked-prediction, and vision-language objectives constrain J(x) only through the rank-deficient gradient projection (∂L/∂z)ᵀ J(x), leaving orthogonal sensitivity directions unconstrained and allowing rank collapse. A bivariate model combining JER with a Same/Different structural discrimination probe explains 78% of binding variance (R² = 0.78), confirming that compositional capability depends on both intrinsic functional complexity and specific invariance properties.

## Results

Across 26 vision encoders, standard geometric metrics (Global Participation Ratio r=0.10, Global Isotropy r=0.11, Local Isotropy r=0.02) show no significant correlation with compositional binding accuracy, while Jacobian Effective Rank (JER) achieves r=0.69 (p=0.0001). Combining JER with a Same/Different discriminability probe explains 78% of variance in binding performance (R²=0.78; LOO-CV R²=0.71). On the SugarCrepe natural-image benchmark (13 CLIP variants), JER retains a significant partial correlation (r=0.70, p=0.017) after controlling for ImageNet zero-shot accuracy and architecture, whereas ImageNet accuracy does not (r=0.34, p=0.301).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is between geometric metrics (isotropy, participation ratio) and the proposed JER as predictors of compositional binding. All three geometric baselines fail: G.PR (r=0.10, p=0.64), G.Iso (r=0.11, p=0.59), and L.Iso (r=0.02, p=0.93) are statistically indistinguishable from zero, while JER achieves r=0.69 (p=0.0001). Among encoder families, variance-decorrelation objectives (Barlow Twins 44.6%, VICReg 43.6%) substantially outperform contrastive (MoCo v3 22.4%), vision-language (CLIP 12.6–13.8%), and supervised (ConvNeXt 14.6–16.0%) encoders on binding, and this ordering aligns with JER rather than with any geometric metric. On SugarCrepe, JER retains a significant partial correlation (r=0.70) after controlling for ImageNet accuracy and architecture, while ImageNet accuracy loses significance (r=0.34, p=0.301) under the same controls. Alternative geometric readouts (kNN, local PCA) consistently degrade binding accuracy relative to plain cosine similarity across all models, confirming that the binding gap is intrinsic to the pretraining objective rather than recoverable through post-hoc geometric manipulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant geometric-evaluation paradigm in representation learning (isotropy, participation ratio, alignment/uniformity) by showing these metrics are orthogonal to compositional binding capability. It repurposes the input-output Jacobian—a standard tool from optimization theory—as a representation diagnostic (Jacobian Effective Rank), bridging the gap between static distributional statistics and local functional sensitivity. The theoretical account in Section 6 connects to the objective-design literature (Barlow Twins, VICReg, CLIP, DINO, MAE) by showing which Jacobian functionals each loss family exposes, but does not build on a specific prior method; rather, it carves new analytical ground by formalizing why geometry and functional sensitivity are decoupled under existing objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that widely adopted geometric diagnostics (isotropy, participation ratio) exhibit near-zero correlation with compositional binding challenges a foundational assumption in representation learning and suggests that current evaluation protocols may be systematically blind to structural capabilities. The JER diagnostic is cheap (5–10 min per model) and provides a practical complementary axis for model selection. Key open questions include whether training objectives can be designed to explicitly preserve functional rank, whether the synthetic-binding finding generalizes to richer compositional tasks (spatial reasoning, multi-object relations), and whether the 26-model suite is sufficient for fine-grained objective-family claims. The natural-image validation is limited to CLIP variants, leaving the broader generalizability of the JER-binding relationship partially untested.
