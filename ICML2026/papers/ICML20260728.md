## Problem

Prior hallucination-mitigation work treats each model as an isolated instance, overlooking whether specific attention heads that encode context-faithful information persist when a base LLM is fine-tuned into instruction-tuned or multimodal variants. This paper asks whether a fundamental behavioral link exists between foundational LLMs and their downstream descendants at the level of individual attention heads, and whether such a link can be exploited for family-level truthfulness enhancement.

## Input / Output

For probing, the method consumes structured triples of (context, question, answer) where context is either textual world knowledge (LLMs) or an RGB image (MLLMs), and answer is labeled truthful or hallucinated; activations are extracted at the final answer token position. The soft-gating mechanism operates at inference time on the residual stream of any model in the lineage, requiring no architectural modification. Downstream evaluation produces binary truthfulness judgments (HaluEval), object-presence yes/no labels (POPE), and free-form image captions scored for hallucinated objects (CHAIR).

## Method

The paper first computes a per-head Truth Score by training a lightweight binary linear probe on each attention head's output at the final answer token, using 5-fold cross-validation on balanced truthful/hallucinated pairs. The key empirical finding is that these scores are strongly correlated within model families (Pearson r ≈ 0.77–0.98 single-dataset, 0.51–0.64 cross-dataset) but near-zero across unrelated families, a pattern explained by minimal within-family weight drift (Frobenius norm ≈ 0.03) versus large cross-family divergence (≈ 1.01). Building on this, TruthProbe applies a soft gate g_l^h = 1 + λ·norm(S) to each head's output before the residual addition, amplifying context-truthful heads while preserving all heads' contributions. Crucially, Truth Scores derived from a base LLM transfer directly as a plug-and-play gate to its fine-tuned LLM and MLLM descendants, eliminating the need to re-probe each variant and yielding consistent gains on HaluEval, POPE, and CHAIR.

## Results

On HaluEval, TruthProbe substantially improves F1 for both LLMs: Vicuna-7B goes from 13.37 to 29.15, and Qwen2.5 from 36.69 to 46.54. On POPE (object-presence QA), base-LLM-derived Truth Scores transfer effectively to MLLMs, e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-Omni accuracy improves from 85.1 to 87.3 (TruthProbe_LLM) and LLaVA-NeXT from 87.7 to 88.3. On CHAIR (image captioning hallucination), LLaVA-1.5 CHAIR_I drops from 6.99 to 5.36 and LLaVA-NeXT from 6.91 to 4.94 with TruthProbe_LLM. The most dramatic gain appears on fine-tuned LLMs: Vicuna-7B (instruction-tuned) F1 jumps from 13.37 to 57.17 when using LLaMA2-7B base-LLM Truth Scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary external comparison is against ITI (Li et al., 2023c) on TruthfulQA generation (Table 12). ITI achieves 57.64% truthfulness, slightly edging out TruthProbe_BaseLLM (56.91%) and TruthProbe_FTLLM (55.38%), so ITI wins on the truthfulness metric. However, TruthProbe_FTLLM achieves the highest informativeness at 29.02% versus ITI's 27.84%, indicating a better truthfulness–informativeness trade-off. Against vanilla (unmodified) models, TruthProbe consistently improves across all benchmarks: on HaluEval, F1 gains range from +9.8 (Vicuna-7B) to +43.8 (Vicuna-7B instruction-tuned with base-LLM scores); on POPE, recall improves by 1–3 points; and on CHAIR, hallucination rates drop by 1.5–2.3 points. The key finding is that TruthProbe_LLM (using base-LLM scores) performs comparably to TruthProbe_MLLM (using directly probed MLLM scores), validating the inheritance hypothesis and enabling a 'probe-once, reuse-within-family' paradigm.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the linear-probing framework from ITI (Li et al., 2023c), which identified truthfulness-related heads within a single model, into a cross-model, lineage-aware setting. While prior hallucination-mitigation work (attention recalibration, over-trust penalties, DPO-based alignment) treats each model as an isolated instance, this paper discovers that head-level context-truthfulness is inherited within model families and leverages this to transfer base-LLM Truth Scores as a plug-and-play soft gate to fine-tuned LLM and MLLM descendants. The soft-gating mechanism departs from ITI's hard activation-shifting intervention by preserving all head contributions while modulating their relative influence, and the mechanistic analysis via Frobenius-norm weight drift connects the inheritance phenomenon to the known low-rank, localized nature of fine-tuning updates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'probe once, reuse within family' paradigm offers a practical system-level benefit: a single base-LLM probing run can guide truthfulness enhancement across multiple downstream variants without repeating the full activation-extraction and probe-training pipeline, which is substantially cheaper for LLMs than MLLMs (5.6×–21.8× fewer TFLOPs). However, TruthProbe does not eliminate hallucinations, and the practical gains on MLLM benchmarks (POPE, CHAIR) are modest (1–3 points), limiting its standalone impact. Open questions include whether cross-family transfer can be improved beyond the partial gains from Procrustes alignment (Pearson 0.10 → 0.31), whether the inheritance property holds for larger or architecturally different model families, and whether the approach generalizes to domains where probing data is scarce beyond the single medical-domain experiment reported.
