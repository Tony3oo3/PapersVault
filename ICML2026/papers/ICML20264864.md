## Problem

Existing fMRI foundation models rely on predefined parcellations that project high-dimensional 4D volumes into low-dimensional regional summaries, discarding fine-grained voxel-level signals and introducing systematic atlas-dependent biases that hinder generalization and fair benchmarking. No scalable, atlas-free framework existed that could operate directly on voxel-level fMRI while remaining computationally tractable for large-scale self-supervised pretraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264864/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes raw 4D fMRI volumes resampled to 96×96×96 at 2 mm isotropic resolution with temporal interpolation to a uniform 0.72 s TR, globally Z-scored. A dynamic patching mechanism adaptively tokenizes each volume into approximately 4,300 tokens (reduced from ~14,000 under uniform partitioning) by pruning background and varying patch granularity. The encoder outputs a CLS token serving as the sequence-level brain representation, which is paired with a one-layer MLP head for downstream classification, regression, or CLIP embedding alignment.

## Method

Omni-fMRI replaces fixed parcellation with a dynamic patching strategy guided by a spatiotemporal complexity score (time-aggregated intensity variance computed via 3D average pooling): background patches are pruned, low-complexity regions receive a single coarse token, and high-complexity regions are recursively subdivided into fine base-size patches. A dual-path multi-scale embedding projects heterogeneous patches into a shared latent space—fine patches via direct 3D convolution, coarse patches via a residual branch with a Zero-initialized MLP that gradually incorporates high-frequency structural detail. The self-supervised objective is a scale-aware masked autoencoder that injects learnable scale embeddings into the decoder and employs a bank of scale-specific reconstruction heads, normalizing the loss by both token frequency and patch volume to prevent large patches from dominating optimization. This design enables a standard ViT with global self-attention (12 layers, 768-dim, 157M parameters) rather than hierarchical shifted-window architectures, capturing long-range functional dependencies while training in ~32 hours on 4 A10G GPUs across 49,497 fMRI sessions.

## Results

Omni-fMRI achieves state-of-the-art performance across all 10 downstream tasks spanning 9 datasets. On demographic tasks, it attains R=0.959 on NKI age regression (vs. 0.946 for SwiFT and 0.835 for NeuroSTORM), 95.54% accuracy on HCP sex classification, and 92.05% on NKI education classification. On clinical tasks, it reaches 72.57% accuracy on PPMI Parkinson's diagnosis and 88.77% on ADNI Alzheimer's classification, outperforming the strongest baselines by 4–14 percentage points. In the few-shot HCP 23-way task setting (10% data), Omni-fMRI sustains 45.29% accuracy while BrainMASS drops to 23.12% and BrainGNN to 7.38%, demonstrating exceptional data efficiency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Omni-fMRI outperforms all seven baselines on every reported task. Against the closest voxel-level competitor NeuroSTORM (Mamba + shifted windows), Omni-fMRI gains 1.4 points on HCP sex classification (95.54% vs. 94.17%), 4.3 points on PPMI PD diagnosis (72.57% vs. 68.25%), 4.5 points on ADNI-AD (88.77% vs. 84.26%), and 2.2 points on NKI age regression (R=0.959 vs. 0.835). SwiFT (Swin 4D fMRI Transformer) is competitive on NKI age (R=0.946) but trails Omni-fMRI by 4–5 points on clinical tasks. ROI-based models (Brain-LM, Brain-JEPA, BrainMASS) are consistently weaker: BrainMASS, the strongest ROI baseline, lags by 11 points on PPMI and 14 points on ADNI-AD. In the few-shot HCP task setting, Omni-fMRI's advantage is most dramatic—maintaining 45.29% at 10% data while BrainMASS collapses to 23.12% and BrainGNN to 7.38%. On image retrieval (NSD), Omni-fMRI doubles NeuroSTORM's Top-1 accuracy (6.93% vs. 3.00%) and quadruples SwiFT's (1.65%). Notably, linear probing with Omni-fMRI even surpasses NeuroSTORM's fully fine-tuned results on ADNI-AD (84.26% LP vs. 71.64% fine-tuned), indicating intrinsically more linearly separable representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-supervised fMRI foundation model line (Brain-LM, Brain-JEPA, BrainMASS, NeuroSTORM) by replacing both ROI-parcellation inputs and hierarchical shifted-window backbones with a standard ViT encoder operating on dynamically tokenized voxel-level signals. The dynamic patching mechanism is adapted from adaptive patch tokenization in computer vision (Choudhury et al., 2025; Havtorn et al., 2023), and the self-supervised objective builds directly on the MAE framework (He et al., 2022), modified with scale-aware reconstruction heads to handle heterogeneous patch sizes. The key conceptual departure is that by pruning background and coarsening low-complexity regions, the token count drops from ~14K to ~4.3K, making global self-attention tractable without the inductive biases of windowed or hierarchical architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating atlas dependency, Omni-fMRI addresses a fundamental representational bottleneck in fMRI analysis: predefined parcellations discard voxel-level information and introduce inter-subject misalignment that no single atlas can resolve. The comprehensive 11-dataset, 16-task benchmark with released experiment logs and exact test IDs directly targets the reproducibility crisis in fMRI evaluation. Open questions remain: the complexity-based patching is heuristic (variance thresholding) rather than learned, the image retrieval evaluation relies on GLM-estimated responses rather than raw BOLD, and whether the dynamic patching strategy generalizes to task-specific information distributions without re-tuning the threshold. The 32-hour pretraining of a 157M model on 4 A10G GPUs (vs. ~13 days for NeuroSTORM's 7.7M model on 4 A6000s) is a practically significant efficiency gain that may enable larger-scale fMRI foundation models.
