## Problem

Existing 2D video world models entangle camera motion with scene dynamics in the image plane, causing geometric inconsistencies (morphing, vanishing objects) over long rollouts, while prior 3D world models either require expensive sensor data (LiDAR, occupancy grids) or fail to explicitly separate ego-motion from environmental dynamics. No prior approach maintains a persistent 3D latent representation into the future from monocular observations alone, leaving a gap between retrospective 3D reconstruction and prospective world modeling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263351/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of N=4 monocular RGB context images (e.g., 512×336 at inference) and autoregressively forecasts future 3D scene tokens that are decoded via CUT3R's pre-trained heads into per-frame depth maps and ego-camera poses, rolled out up to 2 seconds (or 5 timesteps) ahead. Because the model operates entirely in CUT3R's latent space, it inherits CUT3R's decoder and heads for producing multi-view-consistent depth and camera intrinsics without any dataset-specific output layers, enabling zero-shot transfer across domains.

## Method

FR3D learns to propagate scene states and agent poses within the frozen latent space of CUT3R via a teacher-student distillation strategy, using Smooth L1 loss on latent tokens rather than pixel-level supervision. The architecture employs two separate Masked Transformers—a Pose Masked Transformer (M_z, 4 layers) forecasting ego-camera pose tokens and a Spatial Masked Transformer (M_F, 12 layers) forecasting spatial scene tokens—coupled by four bidirectional cross-attention layers that enforce interdependence between pose and depth prediction. This disentanglement resolves the fundamental ambiguity between self-motion and world-motion: the pose stream extrapolates ego-trajectory while the spatial stream captures 3D scene evolution, with each constraining the other. Training uses an autoregressive sliding-window paradigm where student-predicted tokens progressively replace teacher tokens in the context, improving robustness to compounding errors and aligning training with inference-time rollout.

## Results

On zero-shot depth estimation, FR3D achieves AbsR of 0.178 and δ₁ of 0.758 on KITTI at T+2.0s, and AbsR of 0.229 and δ₁ of 0.660 on nuScenes at T+2.5s, outperforming all baselines at every evaluated horizon. For pose estimation, FR3D reaches ATE of 0.403 and RPE_t of 0.306 on KITTI at T+2.0s, substantially outperforming CUT3R-Foresight+M_z (ATE 0.626, RPE_t 0.482). The oracle CUT3R (with access to future frames) remains the upper bound, e.g., AbsR 0.086 on KITTI at T+2.0s, but FR3D closes the gap considerably compared to prior forecasting methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FR3D consistently outperforms DINO-Foresight across all time horizons on both KITTI and nuScenes; the gap widens with longer prediction horizons (e.g., on nuScenes at T+2.5s, FR3D achieves AbsR 0.229 vs. DINO-Foresight's 0.283, a 19% relative improvement). Against CUT3R-Foresight (which replaces DINOv2 features with CUT3R tokens but lacks pose disentanglement), FR3D also wins at every horizon, with the largest gains at T+2.0s on KITTI (AbsR 0.178 vs. 0.200) and T+2.5s on nuScenes (AbsR 0.229 vs. 0.249). For pose estimation (Table 2), FR3D substantially outperforms CUT3R-Foresight+M_z, achieving ATE 0.403 vs. 0.626 on KITTI at T+2.0s and ATE 0.437 vs. 0.812 on nuScenes at T+2.5s, demonstrating the benefit of joint pose-spatial forecasting with cross-attention. FR3D remains below the CUT3R oracle (which has access to future frames), but the gap is far smaller than for any baseline, particularly at longer horizons where baselines degrade rapidly while FR3D maintains stable predictions.

## Relation to Prior Work

FR3D sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: feed-forward 3D reconstruction (CUT3R, DUSt3R, Spann3R) and latent-space world modeling (DINO-Foresight, DINO-WM, VFMF). It builds directly on CUT3R's persistent-state architecture as a frozen teacher and adapts DINO-Foresight's masked-transformer forecasting formulation, but departs from both by operating in a 3D reconstruction model's latent space rather than 2D image-feature space, and by explicitly separating ego-camera pose prediction from spatial scene-token prediction via two cross-attending masked transformers. This contrasts with prior 3D world models (OccWorld, Copilot4D, DiST-4D) that rely on occupancy grids or LiDAR and do not disentangle self-motion from scene dynamics, and with 2D video world models (GAIA-1, Vista, Cosmos) that conflate camera trajectory with scene evolution in the image plane.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a data-efficient, single-dataset-trained model can achieve competitive zero-shot future 3D reconstruction by inheriting geometric priors from a foundation model, offering a practical alternative to the extreme-scale training (22M GPU hours) required by current 2D world models. The disentanglement of ego-motion from world-motion provides a principled mechanism for maintaining geometric consistency over multi-second rollouts, which is critical for safety-critical applications like autonomous driving. However, open questions remain: scale drift accumulates over rollouts, the model exhibits a longitudinal-motion bias that degrades lateral-motion prediction, and the approach has only been validated on driving and one indoor dataset, leaving its applicability to highly dynamic or unstructured environments untested. Integrating uncertainty to model multi-modal futures and extending to diverse motion patterns are natural next steps.
