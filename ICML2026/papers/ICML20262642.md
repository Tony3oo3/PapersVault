## Problem

Existing MLLM-based video misinformation detectors rely on single-pass inference and over-trust internally generated assumptions, leading to cross-modal hallucinations when critical evidence is sparse or fragmented. They lack explicit mechanisms for uncertainty awareness, targeted external evidence acquisition, and iterative refinement of verification judgments, making them unreliable in ambiguous real-world scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262642/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each sample is a triplet of news video content, a speech-to-text transcript from the audio stream, and textual metadata (titles, keywords); for models without native video support, textual descriptions substitute for the video. The output is a structured misinformation verdict (fake/real) enclosed in <answer> tags, preceded by a full reasoning trace in <think> tags that includes intermediate uncertainty assessments, tool-invocation decisions, and evidence-grounded reflections. The maximum prompt length is 16,384 tokens and the maximum response length is 768 tokens.

## Method

FactGuard formulates verification as a two-stage agentic process on Qwen2.5-VL-7B: Stage 1 performs initial chain-of-thought reasoning to assess ambiguity and decide whether to invoke external tools (FactProbe for web-based knowledge retrieval or ClipScout for targeted video-clip temporal inspection), and Stage 2 refines the judgment by incorporating the retrieved evidence. Training uses a two-stage pipeline: first, agentic Chain-of-Thought supervised fine-tuning on trajectories generated by a Qwen2.5-VL-72B teacher to inject structured reasoning and tool-invocation behaviors; second, decision-aware reinforcement learning via GRPO (8 rollouts, β=0.04) with a composite reward that jointly models decision correctness, output format validity, asymmetric error costs (separate penalties α for false positives and γ for false negatives), and a gated tool-usage reward that incentivizes tool invocation only when it leads to correct verification. This design explicitly calibrates tool usage and risk sensitivity rather than optimizing raw label accuracy alone.

## Results

FactGuard achieves state-of-the-art performance across all three benchmarks: 79.3% accuracy and 81.4% F1 on FakeSV, 75.3% accuracy and 75.2% F1 on FakeTT, and 83.0% accuracy and 83.9% F1 on FakeVV. Compared to the strongest task-aligned reasoning baseline Fact-R1, FactGuard improves accuracy by +3.7 (FakeSV), +0.9 (FakeTT), and +1.8 (FakeVV). Against the best zero-shot MLLM (GPT-5), FactGuard leads by +10.9, +17.1, and +25.5 accuracy points on FakeSV, FakeTT, and FakeVV respectively, demonstrating that agentic tool-augmented verification substantially outperforms single-pass multimodal reasoning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FactGuard outperforms all 16 baselines across all three datasets in both accuracy and F1. Against the strongest discriminative model FakingRec, FactGuard gains +9.8, +4.3, and +10.9 accuracy on FakeSV, FakeTT, and FakeVV respectively, showing that agentic reasoning with external evidence acquisition far exceeds task-specific multimodal fusion. Among zero-shot MLLMs, GPT-5 is the strongest (68.4/58.2/57.5 Acc), yet FactGuard still leads by 10.9–25.5 points, indicating that even the most capable general-purpose models suffer from cross-modal hallucination in ambiguous verification scenarios without adaptive evidence acquisition. The closest competitor is Fact-R1, the only other task-aligned reasoning model, which FactGuard surpasses by +3.7 (FakeSV), +0.9 (FakeTT), and +1.8 (FakeVV) in accuracy; the smaller margin on FakeTT suggests that when misinformation cues are more visually salient, the advantage of external tool invocation diminishes. Notably, all zero-shot MLLMs (GPT-4o, GPT-5, Gemini, DeepSeek-R1, Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL, QVQ) perform substantially below the discriminative baselines on FakeVV (34–55% F1 vs. 71–72% for SV-FEND/FakingRec), highlighting that video misinformation detection remains a particularly challenging task for general-purpose multimodal models without domain-specific training.

## Relation to Prior Work

FactGuard extends the single-pass verification paradigm of Fact-R1 (which itself builds on DeepSeek-R1's GRPO algorithm) by reformulating video misinformation detection as an agentic, iterative decision-making process with selective external tool invocation. While prior MLLM-based approaches (GPT-4o, Gemini, Qwen2.5-VL) and task-aligned reasoning models (Fact-R1) rely on fixed-depth inference and internal parametric knowledge, FactGuard introduces an uncertainty-aware two-stage pipeline where the model explicitly assesses ambiguity and conditionally calls FactProbe (web retrieval) or ClipScout (targeted frame inspection) before refining its verdict. The two-stage training strategy—agentic Chain-of-Thought SFT followed by decision-aware GRPO with structured rewards for tool usage and asymmetric error costs—departs from Fact-R1's RL-only approach and draws on the broader tool-augmented agent literature (LLaVA-Plus, PyVision) that had not previously been applied to video misinformation detection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a concrete failure mode of current MLLM-based detectors—cross-modal hallucination in ambiguous cases—by grounding verification in externally acquired evidence rather than internal assumptions, which is practically important as video misinformation on platforms like TikTok outpaces manual fact-checking. The cost-sensitive risk modeling (adjustable α:γ ratio) offers a principled mechanism for tuning precision-recall trade-offs to deployment context (content moderation vs. early warning), a feature absent from prior discriminative or RL-based detectors. Key limitations include the 2.5× latency overhead over single-pass baselines (driven largely by API-based web retrieval), reliance on a 7B student model distilled from a 72B teacher, and evaluation on only three short-video benchmarks; open questions include whether the agentic loop scales to longer-form or multi-claim videos, whether locally hosted knowledge bases can close the latency gap without sacrificing retrieval quality, and whether the approach generalizes to misinformation types beyond the tested social-media short-video domain.
