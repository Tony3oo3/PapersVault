## Problem

Controllable diffusion generation relies on a patchwork of seemingly disconnected heuristics—classifier-free guidance, LoRA adapters, DDPO, DPOK, reward-weighted losses—without a unified theoretical framework explaining how and why they work. The paper asks whether reverse diffusion sampling can be cast as a single principled control problem that subsumes these approaches and yields both new algorithms and a principled model architecture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265413/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (conditioned on c) and produces a generated RGB image via the Stable Diffusion v1.4 latent diffusion pipeline, with the text encoder and VAE kept frozen. The key architectural detail is that the learned side network s_θ receives the pretrained reverse mean μ₀(x_t, c, t) as its spatial input rather than the raw noisy latent x_t, making it a gray-box module that only needs access to intermediate denoising outputs. At inference, a guidance strength λ_model ≥ 1 scales the side-network correction, allowing tunable control over how far the output departs from the pretrained distribution.

## Method

DiffCon casts reverse diffusion sampling as state-only stochastic control within a generalized linearly-solvable MDP, where the controller reweights the pretrained reverse-time Gaussian transition kernels under an f-divergence penalty (recovering KL-regularized LS-MDP as a special case). From the resulting optimality conditions, the paper derives two practical RL fine-tuning objectives: (i) an f-divergence-regularized policy gradient with a PPO-style clipped update, and (ii) a reward-weighted score-matching loss whose weighting function is determined by the chosen f-divergence (exponential for KL, polynomial for α-divergence) and which preserves the same minimizer as the intractable optimal score-matching objective. The LS-MDP analysis further implies that the optimal score decomposes as a fixed pretrained baseline ε₀ plus a lightweight control correction, motivating a side-network parameterization that uses cross-attention blocks over random Fourier features of μ₀ to produce two outputs—a scalar z (modulating the pretrained score) and a vector h (adding a correction)—all while keeping the backbone frozen for gray-box compatibility.

## Results

On Stable Diffusion v1.4 with HPS-v2 win rate against the pretrained model, DiffCon (gray-box, 12M params) achieves 0.667 (SFT), 0.682 (RWL), and 0.696 (PPO), surpassing the white-box LoRA baseline (17M params) on SFT (0.577) and RWL (0.611). For PPO, the white-box variants DiffCon-J and DiffCon-S reach 0.935 and 0.932 respectively, exceeding LoRA's 0.905. The naive side-network baseline (DiffCon-Naive) with the same parameter count drops to 0.566/0.506/0.520, confirming the importance of the LS-MDP-motivated score decomposition.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LoRA (Hu et al., 2022), the gray-box DiffCon with fewer parameters (12M vs. 17M) wins on SFT by ~9 percentage points (0.667 vs. 0.577) and on RWL by ~7 points (0.682 vs. 0.611), but loses on PPO (0.696 vs. 0.905); the white-box DiffCon-J/S variants recover and surpass LoRA on PPO (0.935/0.932 vs. 0.905). Against DPOK's reward-weighted loss (Fan et al., 2023, Algorithm 2), the paper's polynomial weighting (Eq. 21) is compared in Figure 8 and shown to yield better HPS-v2 win-rate curves during RWL training. The DiffCon-Naive ablation (same architecture but without the z/h decomposition) confirms that the LS-MDP-derived parameterization is critical: it underperforms DiffCon by 10+ points on SFT and is near-random on RWL/PPO.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiffCon reframes diffusion fine-tuning through the linearly-solvable MDP (LS-MDP) lens of Todorov (2006), departing from the standard MDP formulation used by DDPO and DPOK where the next noisy state is treated as an explicit action. Rather than introducing a separate action variable, the LS-MDP view directly modulates the reverse-time transition kernel via exponential tilting, with an f-divergence regularizer (recovering KL-regularized control as a special case). This connects to the recent line of work casting diffusion fine-tuning as entropy/f-divergence-regularized stochastic control (Tang & Zhou, 2024; Uehara et al., 2024b; Han et al., 2024), but DiffCon's contribution is the explicit derivation of practical RL updates (policy gradient, PPO, reward-weighted regression) and a principled score decomposition (pretrained baseline + lightweight control correction) that motivates a gray-box side-network parameterization, in contrast to white-box adapters like LoRA or guidance-style modules like ControlNet.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The LS-MDP framework provides a unified theoretical language that explains why a patchwork of previously disconnected heuristics (classifier-free guidance, reward-weighted score matching, KL-regularized policy gradients) are all instances of the same regularized control problem, which should help the community design and analyze new fine-tuning objectives more systematically. The gray-box parameterization is practically significant for proprietary or API-only diffusion models where internal weights are inaccessible. However, experiments are limited to Stable Diffusion v1.4 with a single reward model (HPS-v2), and the authors themselves note that extending to personalization, safety alignment, and transfer learning remains open. Whether the principled z/h decomposition generalizes to larger models (e.g., SD3, Flux) or different reward structures is untested.
