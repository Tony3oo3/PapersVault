## Problem

Two recent experimental studies of the volume hypothesis produced contradictory conclusions: Guess-and-Check random sampling yielded poor generalization relative to SGD (Peleg & Hein, 2024), while molecular dynamics density estimates supported the hypothesis (Yang et al., 2026). The paper identifies that these experiments operated in different data-size regimes (≤32 vs. large training sets) and asks whether the generalization advantage of gradient-based optimization over random sampling is data-size dependent.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263541/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes binary-weight ({±1}) CNN architectures (Base, Deeper, Wider; 76K–152K parameters) and balanced 10-class subsets of MNIST or Fashion-MNIST at training sizes D ∈ {30, 300, 600}, with full 10,000-sample test sets for evaluation. It produces the joint density of states log g(A, Q) over training accuracy A (restricted to A ∈ {D−5, …, D}) and test accuracy Q (binned in groups of 10, over a 10%-accuracy window), from which the maximum-density generalization accuracy Q* = argmax_Q g(A=D, Q) is extracted and compared to GD/SGD baselines.

## Method

The core technique is the Replica Exchange Wang-Landau (REWL) algorithm adapted to binary neural networks: 4–6 independent random walkers perform Metropolis-Hastings walks in {±1}^N parameter space, each confined to an overlapping (A, Q) sub-region, with periodic exchange proposals between walkers to ensure ergodicity. At each step, a proposal flips p binary weights (tuned to 3–9 for ~40% acceptance), the full train and test accuracies are evaluated on GPU, and the log-density is updated by a modification factor f that is reduced geometrically (f ← √f) whenever the visit histogram satisfies a flatness criterion, until log f ≤ 2⁻¹⁷. Walkers' log-densities are aggregated by rigid vertical shifts minimizing overlap discrepancies, yielding a single combined density curve. The resulting Q* is compared against networks trained via BinaryConnect with Adam (GD and mini-batch SGD), revealing that the SGD-over-random gap shrinks monotonically with D across all architectures and datasets.

## Results

Using the Replica Exchange Wang-Landau (REWL) algorithm to estimate the joint density of states g(A,Q) over training and test accuracies in binary networks, the paper shows that the generalization gap between random sampling (argmax of the density at zero training error) and gradient-based training diminishes as training data size grows. On MNIST with the Base model, the GD gap shrinks from 30.2% at D=30 (Random 28.5% vs. GD 58.7%) to 13.5% at D=600 (Random 75.0% vs. GD 88.5%). A similar trend holds across all three architectures (Base, Deeper, Wider) and both datasets (MNIST, Fashion-MNIST), with the density curves also sharpening (increasing curvature) as D increases, indicating concentration of probability mass around a narrower range of test accuracies.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the REWL-estimated random-sampling generalization (the argmax of the density at zero training error) against GD and SGD trained via BinaryConnect with Adam. In every configuration, GD/SGD outperforms random sampling, but the margin shrinks substantially with more data: on MNIST-Base the GD gap drops from 30.2 pp (D=30) to 13.5 pp (D=600), and on Fashion-MNIST-Deeper it drops from 21.2 pp to 5.4 pp. This data-size-dependent narrowing reconciles the apparently contradictory findings of Peleg & Hein (2024), who observed large gaps in the low-sample regime (≤32 samples, binary classification), and Yang et al. (2026), who found near-parity in the high-sample regime using molecular dynamics density estimates. Wider networks consistently achieve higher random-sampling accuracy than Base or Deeper models (e.g., 77.1% vs. 75.0% vs. 72.9% on MNIST-600), a trend not observed in Peleg & Hein's Guess-and-Check experiments. The density curves also sharpen with increasing D across all architectures, confirming that the volume of well-generalizing interpolating solutions becomes more concentrated as data constraints tighten.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies the Replica Exchange Wang-Landau (REWL) algorithm from statistical physics to estimate the joint density of states over training and test accuracies in binary neural networks, extending prior uses of Wang-Landau in ML (Mele et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2023) from single-quantity densities to joint (train, test) accuracy distributions. It directly addresses a contradiction between Peleg & Hein (2024), whose Guess-and-Check random sampling showed poor generalization, and Yang et al. (2026), whose molecular dynamics density estimates supported the volume hypothesis. The paper positions itself as bridging these two results by probing intermediate data-size regimes (D = 30, 300, 600) that neither prior study covered, and situates its findings within the broader simplicity-bias and architectural-bias literature (Chiang et al., 2022; Pérez et al., 2019; Berchenko, 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean, data-size-dependent reconciliation of two seemingly contradictory experimental results on the volume hypothesis, suggesting that optimization-induced bias dominates in small-data regimes while architectural volume effects emerge as data grow. However, the scope is limited to binary-weight networks and datasets of at most 600 samples on MNIST/Fashion-MNIST, leaving open whether the observed transition persists for continuous weights, larger architectures, or more complex datasets. A key open question is whether the sharpening of the density of states with data size reflects a fundamental geometric property of overparameterized models or is an artifact of the binary parameterization and small scale studied here.
