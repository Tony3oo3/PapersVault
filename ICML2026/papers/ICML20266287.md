## Problem

Most graph anomaly detection methods assume homophily, where connected nodes share similar labels, but real-world graphs often exhibit heterophily that causes oversmoothing and disperses anomaly signals across spectral frequencies. Prior work addresses heterophily through structure modification, spectral filtering on the original graph, or manifold learning in isolation, but no method explicitly models heterophily jointly in both spectral and geometric (manifold) domains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266287/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an undirected graph G=(V,E,X) with binary adjacency matrix A∈{0,1}^{N×N} and node attribute matrix X∈R^{N×d}, and produces a per-node anomaly probability via a sigmoid output. Training is semi-supervised with a 40/20/40 train/validation/test split, and the edge heterophily predictor is jointly trained with the node classifier using a combined binary cross-entropy loss. The decision threshold for F1-Macro and G-Mean is selected on the validation set to maximize F1-Macro.

## Method

HSMAD consists of two heterophily-aware modules. The Heterophily-Weighted Spectral Filtering (HWSF) module predicts per-edge heterophily scores via an MLP on absolute node-attribute differences, down-weights heterophilic edges to construct a weighted Laplacian, splits edges into low- and high-heterophily branches via a quantile threshold, and applies a multi-scale Beta wavelet filter bank (C+1 filters parameterized by p+q=C) to each branch, fusing the resulting spectral representations through a learnable gate. The Heterophily-Routed Manifold Update (HRMU) module routes neighbor aggregation messages to a positive-curvature (spherical) channel for homophilic edges and a negative-curvature (hyperbolic) channel for heterophilic edges, performing κ-addition and exponential/logarithmic maps in each manifold with learnable curvature parameters, then fusing the two channels via a gating coefficient. The key insight is that down-weighting heterophilic edges concentrates spectral energy in fewer eigenmodes (reducing spectral entropy and increasing the Gini coefficient), while curvature-adaptive message routing preserves both local homophilic and heterophilic structural signals that Euclidean aggregation would blur.

## Results

HSMAD achieves an average F1-Macro of 0.8953 and average AUROC of 0.9549 across six real-world GAD datasets, outperforming the best baseline (CGADM, 0.8721 F1-Macro) by 2.66% in relative terms. It attains the best F1-Macro on all six datasets and the best AUROC on five of six (trailing PMP and CGADM only on the large-scale T-Social dataset). The average AUPRC is 0.8609 and average G-Mean is 0.8795, both also state-of-the-art.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HSMAD outperforms all 17 baselines on average F1-Macro, with the closest competitor being CGADM (0.8721 vs. 0.8953, a 2.66% relative gain). On AUROC, HSMAD leads on five of six datasets; the sole exception is T-Social, where PMP (0.9946) and CGADM (0.9932) slightly edge out HSMAD (0.9888), attributed to T-Social's low average degree (12.64) limiting heterophily signal availability. Among heterophily-aware spectral methods, HSMAD surpasses BWGNN (0.8411 F1) and SEC-GFD (0.8376 F1) by 5+ points in average F1-Macro. Manifold-based baselines CurvGAD (0.7914 F1) and SpaceGNN (0.8462 F1) are also clearly outperformed, with CurvGAD and SpaceGNN additionally failing (OOM/OOT) on T-Social. Classical GNNs (GCN, GAT, GraphSAGE) and structure-modification methods (GHRN, SparseGAD, NRGL) show substantially lower performance, with SparseGAD and NRGL also OOM on T-Social.

## Relation to Prior Work

HSMAD sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: spectral graph filtering (building directly on BWGNN's Beta wavelet filter bank) and constant-curvature manifold learning (adopting the κ-geometry framework from Bachmann et al. and Ganea et al.). Its key departure is that prior heterophily-aware GAD methods (GHRN, SparseGAD, SplitGNN) modify graph topology or operate purely in Euclidean space, while prior manifold-based GAD methods (CurvGAD, SpaceGNN) ignore heterophily entirely. HSMAD is the first to explicitly route heterophily information into both the spectral domain (via heterophily-weighted Laplacian reconstruction) and the geometric domain (via curvature-routed message passing), unifying these two previously separate axes of heterophily handling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled mechanism for preserving anomaly signals that are dispersed by heterophilic edges, with the spectral energy concentration observation offering a concrete diagnostic for when heterophily-aware weighting helps. However, the computational overhead from edge-level feature prediction and manifold operations is non-trivial (Table 6 shows HSMAD uses ~5.4 GB training memory on Yelp vs ~1.2 GB for DSGAD), and the method underperforms on sparse graphs like T-Social where neighborhood signals are limited. Open questions include whether the two-channel curvature routing can be made more efficient, whether the approach extends to dynamic or heterogeneous graphs, and whether the heterophily-weighting benefit generalizes beyond the six GAD-Bench datasets.
