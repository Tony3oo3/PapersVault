## Problem

Offline RL critics frequently suffer value collapse when bootstrapped TD updates amplify their own errors, but prior work treats the optimizer as a black box, attributing instability to architecture or loss design. This paper asks whether optimizer dynamics themselves can directly trigger or suppress collapse, and derives the first necessary-and-sufficient spectral condition for TD error stability under Adam specifically, rather than SGD.

## Input / Output

At each step the method consumes the task gradient g_t (the TD loss gradient), the current layer-wise weight matrices W_b for a selected set of constrained blocks, and Adam's running moment estimates (m_t, v_t). It outputs updated critic parameters ω_{t+1} = ω_t − η(u_t + δ_t), where u_t is the standard Adam direction and δ_t is a per-layer orthogonality drift whose magnitude is ratio-clipped to κ‖u_t‖ and whose alignment with the task gradient is bounded by a budget τ. The correction is computed deterministically from the weight matrices (not from the gradient), so it never enters Adam's moment recursion.

## Method

The paper models frozen linearized TD-error dynamics under Adam as a second-order linear recurrence and proves that stability holds if and only if the spectral radius of an explicit augmented update matrix A(η) is strictly below one. A tractable sufficient condition (Proposition 5.1) separates this into a bootstrapped scale term γ‖Φ‖₂‖Φ_*‖₂ (controlled by input normalization and spectral norm constraints) and a geometric term ‖ΦᵀΦ − I‖₂ (controlled by near-orthogonality of the parameter-induced Jacobian dictionary). AdamO enforces the geometric term by applying a decoupled, budgeted orthogonality correction δ_t = κ(‖u_t‖_F / ‖r_t‖_F + ε_r) r_t on selected weight blocks, scaled down by a per-layer constraint ⟨g_t, δ_t⟩_F ≥ −τ(⟨g_t, u_t⟩_F)_+ so the correction never exceeds a fraction τ of the Adam descent. Crucially, the orthogonality signal is applied as an additive drift after the Adam step rather than mixed into the gradient, preventing contamination of Adam's second-moment estimates that would otherwise distort all future task updates. In the conflict-free regime (τ = 0), the method provably never increases next-step task loss relative to Adam under standard smoothness, while a positive τ yields an explicit worst-case degradation bound.

## Results

On D4RL benchmarks, AdamO consistently outperforms standard Adam across six offline RL algorithms (TD3+BC, IQL, ReBRAC, ACTIVE, PARS, SQOG). The most dramatic gains appear in TD3+BC: Locomotion average improves from 29.6 to 66.1 (+123%), AntMaze average from 15.0 to 31.8 (+112%), and Walker2d-medium-expert from 22.4 to 98.5 (+340%). For IQL, improvements are more moderate but consistent: Locomotion average 47.3→53.6 (+13%), AntMaze average 48.1→54.6 (+14%). In the Adroit domain, TD3+BC with AdamO recovers Pen-human from −4.1 to 83.1 and Pen-cloned from 5.6 to 82.4, tasks where standard Adam completely fails.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard Adam, AdamO wins on the vast majority of tasks across all six algorithm backbones, with the largest margins in collapse-prone settings (AntMaze-large/ultra, Adroit-human, Locomotion-10k). Compared to the stability-focused optimizer Kron (Castanyer et al., 2025), AdamO achieves 66.1 vs. 58.0 on Locomotion and 93.4 vs. 67.6 on the Pen subset with TD3+BC, while also showing tighter variance (e.g., 92.5±1.2 vs. 72.2±13.1 on AntMaze-umaze). Against TRAC (Muppidi et al., 2024) and C-AdamW (Liang et al., 2025b), AdamO delivers higher average scores with comparable or lower wall-clock cost (172 min vs. 144 min for Adam). Against architectural stabilizers like LayerNorm and Spectral Normalization (Table 3), AdamO matches or exceeds them on most tasks (e.g., 98.5 vs. 95.9 on Walker2d-me with TD3+BC) while being algorithm-agnostic. Against explicit rank regularization DR3 (Kumar et al., 2022), AdamO achieves better scores on nearly all Locomotion and AntMaze tasks without additional loss terms. The only consistent losses are minor (1–6%) on a few already-stable Adroit tasks (e.g., ReBRAC Pen-human 105.4→102.5) and PARS AntMaze-umaze (97.3→93.5), suggesting AdamO's correction is most beneficial precisely where collapse risk is highest.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of work on offline RL value collapse, which has focused on architectural interventions (normalization layers), auxiliary loss regularization (DR3, C4), or heuristic optimizer modifications (state resetting, TRAC, Kron). Instead, it treats the optimizer's internal dynamics as the primary locus of instability, deriving the first necessary-and-sufficient spectral stability condition specifically for Adam-based semi-gradient TD updates (as opposed to prior analyses that assumed SGD). The orthogonality penalty formulation is borrowed from soft-orthogonality regularization (Brock et al., 2017; Bansal et al., 2018), but the key departure is enforcing it as a decoupled optimizer-level drift rather than a loss term, motivated by the observation that mixing the orthogonality gradient into Adam's moment estimates corrupts future task updates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AdamO provides a practical, drop-in optimizer replacement that suppresses value collapse across six offline RL algorithms and four D4RL domains without modifying the algorithm's objective or architecture, which is valuable for practitioners who want stability without re-deriving their method. The theoretical contribution—a Hurwitz condition on the TD dynamics operator S that cleanly separates a bootstrapped scale term (controlled by normalization) from a geometric term (controlled by parameter orthogonality)—gives a principled diagnostic for when collapse will occur. Key limitations include the reliance on a local linearized 'frozen terminal' approximation, the acknowledged tension between the infinitesimal step-size regime where stability is guaranteed and practical training budgets, and the fact that the spectral condition is verified empirically rather than enforced in closed form during training.
