## Problem

While memorization in diffusion models has been studied empirically, no theoretical account exists for where and why membership signals concentrate in Flow Matching's deterministic linear interpolation path. Standard training metrics (loss curves) remain stable even as the model silently encodes exploitable asymmetries between training and held-out data, and existing diffusion-based MIA methods rely on iterative denoising structures that do not transfer to Flow Matching. This paper asks: at which point along the interpolation path does the membership signal peak, and can this location be predicted from data statistics alone?

## Input / Output

The detection protocol takes a trained Flow Matching model v_θ and a query sample x_1 (audio latents of shape 64×50 from Music2Latent or 64-channel sequences from Stable Audio VAE; image latents of shape 4×32×32 from SD VAE), samples K=100 independent noise vectors x_0 ~ N(0, Σ_0), and for each λ ∈ {0, 0.1, …, 1.0} computes the reconstruction MSE after averaging over noise realizations. This yields an 11-dimensional λ-resolved error profile per sample. For the MIA, these profiles serve as features to a small MLP that outputs a binary member/non-member classification; the attack requires only forward passes through the model with no gradient computation or weight access.

## Method

The paper decomposes the Flow Matching training loss into approximation error, irreducible variance, and a cross-correlation term G_n^train(λ) that measures the correlation between the model's deviation from the optimal predictor and sample-specific residuals—identifying this term as the membership signal. Under Gaussian isotropic assumptions, the expected train-test gap is shown to be proportional to the irreducible variance σ_irr²(λ), which follows a bell-shaped curve over λ and peaks at a closed-form location λ_F* = tr(Σ_0² + Σ_0Σ_1) / tr((Σ_0+Σ_1)²) determined solely by the noise and data covariances. The mechanism is a competition between learning the population nonlinear residual r and fitting sample-specific noise ε_i: at λ_F* the linear predictor vanishes, forcing the model to explain the full nonlinear target with features that are statistically indistinguishable between the two components. The signal accumulates throughout training while validation loss decreases (temporal compensation), making it invisible to standard early-stopping diagnostics. As a proof of concept, the 11-dimensional λ-resolved error profile is fed to an MLP classifier for MIA, achieving 0.91 AUC on MAESTRO v3 versus 0.72 and 0.83 for SecMI and PIA adapted to Flow Matching.

## Results

On MAESTRO v3, the proposed λ-resolved MIA achieves 0.91 AUC (56.7% TPR@5%FPR), substantially outperforming the NaiveRF baseline (0.67 AUC), SecMI adapted to Flow Matching (0.72 AUC), and PIA adapted to Flow Matching (0.83 AUC). The advantage persists across all four datasets: MTG-Jamendo (0.72 vs. 0.64 PIA), FMA Large (0.67 vs. 0.61 PIA), and CelebA (0.65 vs. 0.62 PIA). The bell-shaped train-test gap is universal across all configurations tested (7 ablation axes), and the closed-form peak prediction λ*_F matches the observed peak whenever Gaussian isotropic latent assumptions hold (e.g., λ_obs ∈ [0.5, 0.6] vs. λ*_F = 0.52 on MAESTRO v3 with Music2Latent).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its λ-resolved reconstruction-error MIA against two baselines adapted from the diffusion-model literature: SecMI (Duan et al., 2023, ICML) and PIA (Kong et al., 2023, ICCV), both of which rely on iterative denoising structure and must be modified to apply to Flow Matching. The proposed method wins on all four datasets against both baselines. On MAESTRO v3, the gap is largest: 0.91 AUC vs. 0.83 (PIA) and 0.72 (SecMI), with TPR@5%FPR more than 1.5× higher than PIA (56.7% vs. 36.5%). On MTG-Jamendo and FMA Large, the advantage narrows to 0.08 and 0.06 AUC over PIA respectively, but remains consistent. On CelebA (where Gaussian isotropic assumptions are violated), the method still edges out PIA (0.65 vs. 0.62) and clearly beats SecMI (0.65 vs. 0.56), confirming the λ-resolved profile carries signal even when the closed-form peak prediction fails. The NaiveRF baseline (single-point reconstruction error at λ*) is consistently the weakest, validating that the full λ-resolved structure is essential rather than just the peak location.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the trajectory-dependent memorization signals observed in diffusion models (Matsumoto et al., 2023) to the Flow Matching / Rectified Flow framework, but with a key departure: rather than empirically discovering where signals concentrate, it derives a closed-form expression for the peak location λ*_F from the data covariances alone. The theoretical analysis builds on the linear interpolation structure of Flow Matching (Lipman et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2023) and the loss decomposition framework, while the MIA component adapts and compares against diffusion-specific attacks (SecMI, PIA) that do not transfer directly to the deterministic interpolation path. The paper positions itself as providing the missing theoretical grounding for why intermediate timesteps carry memorization signals, a question left open by prior empirical work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that the membership signal peaks at a computable, architecture-independent location governed solely by data geometry has direct practical implications: practitioners can locate the vulnerability on a small proxy model and transfer the knowledge to deployed systems (FLUX, Stable Audio) without retraining, and can concentrate privacy-preserving mechanisms near λ*_F rather than applying uniform regularization. The paper also reveals a fundamental trade-off between training efficiency and privacy, since schedulers that concentrate sampling near λ*_F (improving convergence, as Esser et al. observed) simultaneously amplify membership leakage. Key limitations include the Gaussian isotropic requirement for the closed-form prediction (violated by image VAEs), white-box-only MIA, unconditional generation only, and scale capped at 880M parameters, leaving the interaction of capacity and dataset size at deployed scale open.
