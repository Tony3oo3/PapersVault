## Problem

Annealed Langevin Dynamics (ALD) empirically improves multimodal sampling over classical Langevin, but existing theory only provides polynomial-in-dimension complexity bounds that still deteriorate as dimension grows. The paper addresses the stronger question of whether ALD can achieve dimension-uniform sampling guarantees—i.e., a fixed time horizon that works across all finite-dimensional truncations of a single infinite-dimensional multimodal target—and under what spectral conditions this stability persists when the score or initialization is misspecified.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a sequence of finite-dimensional truncations (ρ_★^d)_{d≥1} of a fixed infinite-dimensional Gaussian mixture target, where each truncation adds coordinates in a shared orthonormal basis. Inputs include the mixture parameters (weights, means, diagonal component covariances σ_ij), a smoothing covariance spectrum (λ_j) that must be summable, and a preconditioner spectrum (γ_j). The output is the ALD output law at final time T, with an explicit KL divergence bound B_ann^d(T) ≤ ε achieved at T = ε^{-1} K_d, where K_d is a coordinatewise sum over j of (λ_j/γ_j) log(1+λ_j/σ_ij). The key structural requirement is that the target family represents successive refinements of one target, not independent high-dimensional problems.

## Method

The core technique is a path-matching argument: a reference diffusion is constructed whose law follows the annealing path exactly (via a correction field v_t^d solving a Fokker-Planck equation), and Girsanov's formula bounds the KL divergence between the ALD path law and this reference by a path-matching energy. For Gaussian mixtures with diagonal covariances, this energy is explicitly computable, yielding the key quantity K_d = (1/16) Σ_i w_i Σ_j (λ_j/γ_j) log(1+λ_j/σ_ij), so that T = ε^{-1}K_d guarantees KL accuracy ε. Dimension-uniformity reduces to sup_d K_d < ∞, enforceable by choosing decaying smoothing and preconditioner spectra compatible with the component covariance spectra (e.g., λ_j = 40·j^{-2.7}, γ_j = j^{-1.5}). In the perturbative regime with misspecified mixture scores, the preconditioner plays a dual role: larger γ_j coefficients reduce the annealing bias, while sufficient spectral decay of γ_j prevents coordinatewise score errors from accumulating across high-frequency coordinates, with explicit summability conditions linking (γ_j), (λ_j), and the perturbation magnitudes (Δσ_ij, Δm_ij).

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 3.1) establishes that KL(ρ⋆^d ‖ ρ_T^ALD,d) ≤ ε whenever T ≥ ε⁻¹·K_d, where K_d = (1/16)·Σ_i w_i·Σ_j (λ_j/γ_j)·log(1+λ_j/σ_ij). Dimension-uniform control (sup_d K_d < ∞) is guaranteed by the spectral summability condition Σ_i w_i·Σ_j λ_j²/(γ_j·σ_ij) < ∞, which can be enforced through compatible choices of the smoothing and preconditioner spectra. Numerically, with the tailored design γ_j = j^{-1.5} and λ_j = 40·j^{-2.7}, the empirical KL remains uniformly bounded as d grows to 65, whereas the flat-spectrum choice (Γ=I, C=40I) shows clear deterioration. In the perturbative regime with covariance perturbation Δσ_ij = 0.05·j^{-3}, the admissible preconditioner Γ=Diag(j^{-3.5}) maintains uniform KL control up to d=75, while the non-admissible Γ=Diag(j^{-1}) exhibits growing error.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against competing sampling algorithms; instead, it compares spectral design choices within the ALD framework. In the exact-score setting (Figure 3), the tailored spectral design (Γ=Diag(j^{-1.5}), C=Diag(40·j^{-2.7})) keeps the annealing-induced KL uniformly small across dimensions d ∈ {1,…,65}, while the flat-spectrum baseline (Γ=I, C=40I) shows KL growing with d, confirming that condition (6) captures a genuine stability constraint rather than a proof artifact. In the perturbative setting (Figure 4), the preconditioner Γ=Diag(j^{-3.5}) satisfies the sufficient summability conditions and maintains dimension-uniform KL control under a covariance-perturbed score (Δσ_ij = 0.05·j^{-3}), whereas Γ=Diag(j^{-1}) violates the component-score summability condition and the KL increases with dimension. The comparison with Guo et al. The flat-spectrum ALD in Figure 1 also exceeds the 20,000-step cap for d > 10, while the tailored design reaches the target accuracy ε=0.3 with no visible deterioration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dimension-robustness analysis framework of Hairer et al. (2007) from standard Langevin diffusion to annealed Langevin dynamics (ALD), a setting where prior theory (Guo et al., 2025) only provided polynomial-in-dimension KL bounds. The ALD framework itself is inherited from score-based generative modeling (Song & Ermon, 2019), and the path-matching energy technique used to bound annealing-induced bias follows the Girsanov-based approach of Guo et al. (2025), adapted here to an infinite-dimensional Hilbert-space setting. The key conceptual departure is the identification of preconditioner spectral decay as the mechanism that converts polynomial-in-d ALD guarantees into dimension-uniform ones, a role that has no analogue in the fixed-dimension ALD literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first rigorous demonstration that ALD can sample multimodal targets with dimension-uniform error control, closing a gap that left practitioners without theoretical justification for ALD's empirical success in infinite-dimensional settings (e.g., Bayesian nonlinear inverse problems). The practical takeaway—that preconditioner spectrum must decay sufficiently fast to prevent coordinatewise error accumulation while remaining large enough to reduce annealing bias—provides a concrete design principle for infinite-dimensional samplers. Limitations include the restriction to Gaussian mixtures with co-diagonalizable covariances, and the open question of whether these guarantees survive Euler-Maruyama discretization (addressed in a companion paper) or extend to non-Gaussian multimodal targets.
