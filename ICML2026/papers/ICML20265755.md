## Problem

In PD-disaggregated LLM serving, decode instances are memory-bound and suffer persistent load imbalance because output lengths are unknown at dispatch time, forcing stateless routing (round-robin, least-request) that ignores per-request KV cache demand. Existing length-aware scheduling is confined to intra-instance reordering and cannot guide cross-instance placement, while on-critical-path prediction adds unacceptable latency overhead. The paper tackles how to obtain reliable, low-overhead output-length estimates and use them for safe, memory-aware cross-decoder scheduling.

## Input / Output

The system consumes a text prompt (forwarded to the cloud prefiller) and the same prompt on the request-origin device for speculative prediction, plus periodic runtime state reports from each decoder (free KV blocks, reserved blocks, effective blocks, queue length). It produces a conformal-calibrated upper bound on output length (with 1−α coverage guarantee, α=0.1 over a 500-request sliding window), a decoder routing decision selecting the memory-feasible decoder with the shortest waiting queue, and an SJF-ordered ready queue within each decoder. The prediction runs on-device (Jetson Orin NX) with a LoRA-adapted Vicuna-7B or Vicuna-160M, fully overlapped with cloud prefill so it adds no critical-path latency.

## Method

MAPS integrates three coordinated mechanisms. First, a device-assisted speculative predictor (SP) — a lightweight LLM LoRA-finetuned on multi-model, multi-temperature maximum output lengths — estimates output length on the request-origin device in parallel with cloud-side prefill, hiding prediction latency. Second, an uncertainty-aware calibration module (UAC) applies one-sided conformal prediction over a sliding window of recent prediction errors to convert the point estimate into a coverage-guaranteed upper bound, bounding underestimation risk to α and preventing unsafe admission. Third, a hierarchical global-local scheduler uses the calibrated bound to compute per-request KV block demand, filters decoders by memory feasibility, routes to the feasible decoder with minimum queue length (global), and reorders the local ready queue by predicted length in SJF order to mitigate head-of-line blocking (local). The key architectural insight is that device-side prediction overlaps with prefill to eliminate latency overhead, while conformal calibration decouples scheduling safety from prediction accuracy, allowing even a 160M-parameter predictor to yield near-oracle scheduling performance.

## Results

Across two real-world workloads (ShareGPT and BurstGPT) and two LLMs (LLaMA3.1-8B, Qwen2.5-3B), MAPS reduces mean end-to-end latency by 42.6% and P99 tail latency by up to 84.8% compared to vLLM, SGLang, and Llumnix. On the ShareGPT workload, P99 E2E latency is reduced by up to 70.3%; on the bursty BurstGPT workload, the average P99 reduction reaches 36.8%. For output-length prediction, SP-7B achieves MAE of 56 tokens and Acc-100 of 85%, outperforming PO-IT (MAE 63, Acc-100 81%) and S3 (MAE 71, Acc-100 64%). The uncertainty-aware calibration module reduces underestimation probability from 64% (raw SP) to 9% (SP+UAC).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three scheduling baselines, MAPS consistently wins on all latency metrics: it reduces P99 E2E latency by up to 70.3% over vLLM and SGLang on ShareGPT, and by up to 36.8% on BurstGPT. Compared to Llumnix, MAPS achieves lower P99 E2E latency and dramatically lower max inter-token latency (Llumnix suffers extreme token-level stalls from request migration), though Llumnix marginally edges out MAPS on mean TTFT by dispatching to the most idle decoder. On the prediction side, SP-7B outperforms PO-IT by 11.1% in MAE (56 vs. 63) and S3 by 21.1% (56 vs. 71), with corresponding gains in Acc-50 and Acc-100. Even the lightweight SP-160M variant, despite substantially higher MAE (92.2), still beats all three scheduling baselines by 21–57% in P99 E2E latency, demonstrating that the calibration and scheduling layers compensate for weaker predictors. MAPS also approaches an Oracle scheduler (ground-truth lengths) within 19.8% relative P99 gap, suggesting limited headroom from further predictor improvements.

## Relation to Prior Work

MAPS builds on the PD-disaggregation architecture popularized by DistServe and the PagedAttention-based KV cache management from vLLM, extending them with a predictive scheduling layer that prior systems lacked. It departs from intra-instance length-aware schedulers (PO-IT, Ranking) by enabling cross-instance, memory-feasible routing, and from reactive reschedulers (Llumnix) by making scheduling decisions proactively at dispatch time. The use of conformal prediction to convert point length estimates into coverage-guaranteed upper bounds is a novel application of distribution-free uncertainty quantification to LLM serving, and the device-assisted speculative prediction that overlaps with cloud prefilling repurposes edge inference for a scheduling-support role rather than an execution role.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 42.6% average and up to 84.8% tail latency reductions address a concrete production pain point in PD-disaggregated LLM serving, where stateless RR routing causes persistent KV-cache imbalance under heavy-tailed output distributions. The finding that even a degraded 160M-parameter predictor still outperforms all baselines suggests the scheduling framework is robust to prediction quality, lowering the barrier to deployment on constrained edge hardware. Key open questions include scaling beyond two decoders (the evaluation uses only six GPUs), handling chain-of-thought workloads where intermediate token counts are highly variable, and adapting the predictor to substantially different domains (code generation, agentic traces) without retraining.
