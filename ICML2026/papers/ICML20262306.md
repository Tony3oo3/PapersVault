## Problem

Logit Lens, the dominant parameter-based method for interpreting SAE features, suffers from two fundamental gaps: it captures only the direct effect of a feature on output logits while ignoring indirect, module-mediated effects that arise when downstream layers consume the perturbed residual stream, and it uses only decoder (value) features, leaving the input-side causality (which inputs activate a feature) uncharacterized. As a result, many features—especially in earlier layers—yield diffuse or uninterpretable token signatures under Logit Lens.

## Input / Output

Query Lens takes an SAE's key feature vector (encoder column) or value feature vector (decoder column) at a given layer, along with the model's weights and a small sampled corpus (1024×32 tokens from The Pile) to compute the stream transition via Jacobian-vector products. It produces two top-k token score vectors (k=25): a key-side score identifying which input tokens most increase the feature's activation, and a value-side score identifying which output tokens the feature promotes. The backward readout uses a centered-normalized embedding matrix that encodes token-substitution effects rather than raw embedding directions, making the input-side interpretation relative to the current token.

## Method

Query Lens decomposes a feature's causal role into forward dynamics (value feature transported through the product of downstream identity-plus-Jacobian factors, then read out via the unembedding matrix) and backward dynamics (key feature transported through upstream Jacobians, then read out via the centered-normalized embedding matrix). The full stream transition—capturing all indirect, module-mediated paths—is computed efficiently as a single JVP or VJP pass through the relevant layers rather than by explicitly forming the d_m×d_m Jacobian, with in-place accumulation across O(L) factor applications. The paper further proposes the Subspace Channel Hypothesis, verified by learning LoRA-style low-rank maps from value features to per-module Jacobian-vector-product responses and showing that maps sharing a destination layer have significantly higher column-space overlap than those with different destinations, indicating that each downstream module reads features through its own layer-specific low-dimensional subspace.

## Results

Across four model/SAE configurations (GPT-2 Small, Gemma-3-270M, Gemma-3-1B, Qwen-3-1.7B), QL_KEY achieves the highest Input Score (I(T)) and QL_VALUE achieves the highest Output Score (O(T)) in every setting. On GPT-2 Small, QL_KEY reaches 39.32% input score (vs. 31.47% for the best ablation baseline TC) and QL_VALUE reaches 15.24% output score (vs. 13.61% for TC). The relative gain of Query Lens over Logit Lens widens for features in earlier layers, where indirect effects are larger. On interpretability scoring (0–10), QL_KEY and QL_VALUE consistently outperform their Logit Lens counterparts (e.g., 4.02 vs. 3.03 and 5.95 vs. 4.87 on GPT-2 Small).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Query Lens is compared against four baselines: Logit Lens (LL_KEY, LL_VALUE), Tuned Lens (TL), Zero-Out (ZO), and Token Change (TC). On the input side, QL_KEY dominates all baselines by a wide margin—e.g., 39.32% vs. 31.47% (TC) and 11.74% (LL_VALUE) on GPT-2 Small, and 21.82% vs. 10.03% (TC) on Gemma-3-270M. On the output side, QL_VALUE leads in every configuration, e.g., 15.24% vs. 13.61% (TC) on GPT-2 Small and 10.63% vs. 9.74% (TC) on Gemma-3-270M. Tuned Lens underperforms both Logit Lens and Query Lens, which the authors attribute to a distributional mismatch between the learned affine map (trained on dense residual vectors) and individual SAE feature vectors. TC is the strongest ablation-style baseline but remains sensitive to the clamp value choice, and its advantage over QL_VALUE narrows or disappears at most clamp settings. The paper also reports that QL's relative gain over LL widens for earlier-layer features, confirming that the indirect effects captured by the full stream transition are the primary source of improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

Query Lens extends Logit Lens (Nostalgebraist, 2020) by replacing its identity-only stream transition with the full product of downstream Jacobians, thereby capturing indirect, module-mediated effects that Logit Lens structurally ignores. It also repurposes the key-value memory view of MLPs (Geva et al., 2021) to unify input-side and output-side feature interpretation under a single Jacobian-based framework, closing a gap where prior work treated these as separate methodological tracks (activation-based for inputs, parameter-based for outputs). The Subspace Channel Hypothesis further extends the layer-communication literature (Elhage et al., 2021; Merullo et al., 2024) from attention-head virtual weights to full residual blocks, proposing that each downstream module reads features through its own low-rank subspace.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Query Lens provides a more faithful and complete tool for SAE feature interpretation, with the key practical implication that features previously deemed uninterpretable under Logit Lens often yield coherent token signatures once indirect effects are accounted for. The Subspace Channel Hypothesis opens a path toward targeted model editing by identifying which subspaces each layer uses to read features. Key open questions include the fidelity-variance tradeoff (full transitions are noisier near the output endpoint, making a static choice suboptimal), the functional role of the query-response structure, and whether per-feature adaptive fidelity selection can close the remaining gap to oracle performance.
