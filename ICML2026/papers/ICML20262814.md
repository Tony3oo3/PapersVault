## Problem

Existing correction-based methods for noisy correspondence in cross-modal retrieval rely on a 'Discrete Selection' paradigm—picking a single substitute proxy from the finite dataset—which suffers from Single-Point Fragility (the chosen neighbor may itself be noisy) and Discretization Error (forcing continuous semantic truths onto imperfect discrete samples). This paper argues that the semantic truth of a noisy sample is not a discrete point to be found but a continuous prototype best synthesized from the collective consensus of its local intra-modal neighborhood.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-extracted 2048-d image features (Faster R-CNN bottom-up attention) and Bi-GRU text embeddings, projected into a shared 1024-d space via learnable dual-encoder networks (GPO backbone). At inference, all auxiliary modules (Cross-Model Memory Bank, Graph Refiner) are discarded, and the model operates as a standard dual-encoder producing cosine-similarity scores for bidirectional retrieval ranking. Training uses a co-training setup with two peer networks that interactively rectify each other's noisy samples via the peer's frozen memory queue.

## Method

IN²R shifts from discrete proxy selection to continuous prototype synthesis via a Graph-Guided Rectification mechanism. After a warm-up phase with Symmetric Cross Entropy, a GMM-based partition separates clean from noisy samples each epoch; clean pairs are trained with inter-modal triplet loss plus intra-modal geometric consistency (random-dropout augmentation) to stabilize the feature manifold. For noisy pairs, the framework retrieves Top-K intra-modal neighbors from the peer network's dynamic Cross-Model Memory (elitist FIFO queue with gradient detachment), then applies a learnable Graph Refiner—a single-layer Multi-Head Self-Attention encoder—that performs relational reasoning over the neighborhood to suppress outliers and synthesize a continuous soft prototype. This prototype is converted into a calibrated soft target distribution and used with the robust SCE loss to supervise the noisy sample, effectively replacing 'imitation of a proxy' with 'alignment with a synthesized truth.' The cross-model decoupling (each network queries only the peer's memory) prevents confirmation bias, and the entire rectification pipeline is bidirectional and symmetric across modalities.

## Results

On Flickr30K, IN²R achieves rSum of 511.6 at 20% noise, 504.1 at 40%, 495.2 at 60%, and 458.8 at 80%, consistently outperforming all baselines. On MS-COCO, it reaches 527.3 (20%), 523.2 (40%), 518.0 (60%), and 501.3 (80%) rSum. On the real-world CC152K dataset, IN²R attains a state-of-the-art rSum of 380.8 with Text Retrieval R@1 of 45.2%. The advantage grows substantially with noise: at 80% noise, IN²R surpasses the next-best method (PCSR) by 21.3 points on Flickr30K and 7.6 points on MS-COCO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IN²R outperforms all eight baselines on Flickr30K across every noise level (20%–80%), with the largest margin at 80% noise where it leads PCSR by 21.3 rSum points (458.8 vs. 437.5) and NCR by 437.8 points (NCR collapses to 21.0). On MS-COCO, IN²R wins at 20% (527.3 vs. ESC's 526.8), 60% (518.0 vs. SPS's 517.2), and 80% (501.3 vs. PCSR's 493.7) noise, though it narrowly trails ESC by 0.7 points at 40% noise (523.2 vs. 523.9). On the real-world CC152K benchmark, IN²R achieves the best rSum of 380.8, surpassing SPS (376.3) by 4.5 points and PCSR (375.3) by 5.5 points, while also posting the highest Text Retrieval R@1 (45.2% vs. PCSR's 43.7%). The correction-based baselines (L2RM, CREAM, PCSR) and consistency-based methods (BiCro, ESC, GSC) all degrade sharply at 80% noise, whereas IN²R's graph-guided continuous rectification maintains stable performance, confirming the robustness advantage of synthesizing soft prototypes over discrete proxy selection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the correction-based paradigm for noisy correspondence learning (NCL) in cross-modal retrieval, building directly on L2RM's Symmetric Cross Entropy loss and the broader line of neighbor-retrieval methods (NAC, PCSR, SPS). Its key conceptual departure is reframing the rectification problem: rather than the 'Discrete Selection' paradigm that seeks a single substitute proxy from the finite dataset, IN²R synthesizes a continuous soft prototype via a learnable Graph Refiner (MHSA-based) over intra-modal neighbors retrieved from a cross-model memory bank. The cross-model co-training architecture with decoupled memory queues draws from peer-teacher ideas in semi-supervised learning, while the intra-modal geometric constraints follow ConVSE's dropout-augmentation strategy for manifold stabilization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paradigm shift from discrete proxy selection to continuous prototype synthesis offers a principled way to avoid both single-point fragility and discretization error, and the method's robustness at 80% noise (where NCR collapses to 21.0 rSum while IN²R maintains 458.8) demonstrates practical value for web-scale datasets with heavy corruption. The zero inference overhead (all auxiliary modules are training-only) makes it deployable as a drop-in training strategy for standard dual-encoder retrieval systems. Open questions include whether the local-consensus mechanism amplifies distributional biases in the training data (acknowledged in the Impact Statement), how the method scales to multi-modal settings beyond image-text, and whether the GMM-based clean/noisy partition remains reliable under adversarial or structured noise patterns.
