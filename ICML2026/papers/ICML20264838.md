## Problem

Diffusion-based talking head generation achieves superior visual quality and lip-sync accuracy but suffers from two critical limitations: extremely slow inference (tens to hundreds of seconds per 5-second clip) and a non-autoregressive paradigm that forces users to wait for the entire video before any frame is available. No existing method combines end-to-end diffusion quality with real-time, chunk-by-chunk streaming generation on a single GPU.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264838/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference image (512×512) and a 16 kHz monochannel speech waveform as input, producing a 512×512, 25 fps talking head video generated in streaming chunks of 4 latent frames (32 pixel frames). Speech is compressed via a Whisper-tiny encoder followed by a SpeechAE module into temporally aligned latent codes, while video is compressed through a temporal VAE at a 32×32×8 spatio-temporal ratio (1:8192 pixels-to-tokens). Inference uses 8-step Flow Matching sampling with Joint CFG (α=6.0), achieving ~4.4 s runtime for a 121-frame clip on a single A100 GPU.

## Method

REST builds a Streaming A2V-DiT backbone (28 transformer blocks) operating in a compact video latent space, where each block integrates three attention modules: a Self-Attention with the proposed ID-Context Cache, a 3D Full-Attention for text conditioning, and a frame-level 2D Cross-Attention for audio conditioning. The ID-Context Cache enables semi-autoregressive streaming by concatenating a persistent ID-Sink (reference-image KV anchor) with a Context-Cache (previous chunk's KV) into the self-attention key/value sequence, preserving identity fidelity and temporal coherence across chunks. Training uses a two-stage Asynchronous Streaming Distillation (ASD) scheme: a non-streaming teacher is first trained with a chunk-wise asynchronous noise schedule to establish a quality upper bound, then a streaming student is trained under the teacher's supervision via an InfoNCE contrastive loss (maximizing frame-wise mutual information between teacher and student velocity flows) plus a second-order smoothness matching loss (aligning discrete jerk between the two), formalized with theoretical proofs of MI invariance under Flow Matching and spectral high-pass filtering properties of the smoothness term.

## Results

On HDTF, REST achieves the best FID (14.597), FVD (219.870), and E-FID (0.931), with near-best lip-sync (Sync-C 8.335, Sync-D 6.701), while running in just 4.416 s per 121-frame video — roughly 3× faster than the next-fastest method (AniTalker, 13.577 s) and ~200× faster than FantasyTalking (896.089 s). On MEAD, REST again leads in FVD (237.521), E-FID (1.064), Sync-C (7.632), and Sync-D (7.573), with FID (46.540) competitive with Sonic (47.070) and Ditto (45.403). REST is the only method supporting streaming inference, and long-form stability tests show metric fluctuations bounded within 1.5% over 70 s of continuous generation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

REST dominates on inference speed, completing a 121-frame video in 4.416 s versus 13.577 s (AniTalker), 17.974 s (Ditto), 83.584 s (Sonic), 124.105 s (EchoMimic), 212.002 s (Hallo), and 896.089 s (FantasyTalking) — a 3–200× speedup over all baselines. On HDTF, REST achieves the best FID (14.597 vs. Ditto's 15.440 and Sonic's 16.894) and FVD (219.870 vs. Sonic's 245.416), while Sonic edges out REST on lip-sync (Sync-C 8.525 vs. 8.335; Sync-D 6.576 vs. 6.701). On MEAD, REST wins FVD (237.521 vs. Sonic's 218.308 — actually Sonic is better here at 218.308), E-FID (1.064 vs. Sonic's 1.434), Sync-C (7.632 vs. Sonic's 7.501), and Sync-D (7.573 vs. Sonic's 7.831), but trails Ditto (45.403) and Sonic (47.070) slightly on FID (46.540). Motion-space methods (Ditto, AniTalker) are faster than end-to-end baselines but suffer substantially worse FVD and E-FID, confirming the quality ceiling of two-stage pipelines. REST is the only method that supports streaming inference, a capability none of the seven baselines offer.

## Relation to Prior Work

REST sits at the intersection of two previously incompatible paradigms in talking head generation: end-to-end diffusion models (Sonic, EchoMimic, Hallo, FantasyTalking) that deliver high visual fidelity but require 76–896 s per 5 s clip, and autoregressive/motion-space methods (AniTalker, Ditto, Artalk) that support streaming but are bottlenecked by low-dimensional motion representations. The paper inherits its compact video latent space from LTX-Video's temporal VAE, its speech encoding pipeline (SpeechAE) and Joint CFG from READ, and its generative backbone from Flow Matching with a DiT architecture. The key departure is the ID-Context Cache mechanism, which repurposes key-value caching from LLM-style autoregressive inference into a diffusion transformer to enable semi-autoregressive chunk-by-chunk generation, and the Asynchronous Streaming Distillation scheme that transfers global temporal priors from a non-streaming teacher to a streaming student via contrastive and smoothness-matching objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

REST is the first end-to-end diffusion-based THG system to achieve near-real-time streaming (4.4 s for a 4.84 s video on a single A100) while matching or exceeding the quality of much slower non-streaming baselines, opening the door to interactive applications (virtual avatars, real-time video conferencing, live content production) that were previously only accessible to lower-quality two-stage AR methods. The ID-Context Cache and ASD strategies are architecturally general and could transfer to other streaming video generation tasks beyond talking heads. Open questions include scalability to higher resolutions (beyond 512×512), multi-person or full-body scenarios, and whether the 8-step sampling budget can be further reduced without quality loss; the paper's 70 s stability test, while reassuring, does not yet cover the multi-minute durations needed for practical deployment.
