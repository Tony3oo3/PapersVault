## Problem

In federated learning, global class imbalance and cross-client data heterogeneity coexist, causing the aggregated model's decision boundaries to be biased toward majority classes. Existing data-level methods (e.g., SMOTE) require global class prior knowledge to identify minority classes, which is inaccessible under FL privacy constraints, leaving data-level approaches underdeveloped—especially under extreme conditions where minority classes are entirely absent from many clients.

## Input / Output

Each client receives the global model parameters at a designated round T_relabel and runs a single forward pass over its local image dataset to obtain a posterior probability matrix. The method then produces a re-labeled version of the local dataset (features unchanged, only labels modified) that is reused for all subsequent local training rounds. The re-labeling is one-shot: no additional communication rounds or parameter uploads are needed, and the computational cost is approximately one-third of a single training round's FLOPs.

## Method

FedReLa is a model-agnostic, data-level plug-in that corrects biased global decision boundaries by asymmetrically re-labeling local majority-class samples into minority classes. At round T_relabel, each client computes posterior probabilities via the global model, applies class-wise z-score standardization to calibrate systematically underestimated minority-class posteriors, and then uses tanh normalization with a client-specific quantile threshold (controlled by hyperparameter τ) and a class-prior reweighting vector to produce re-labeling probabilities in [0,1]. The re-labeling is strictly one-directional (majority→minority only), with probability proportional to the degree of feature-space intrusion, preserving minority-class sample integrity. Theoretically, this shifts the Bayesian decision boundary back toward the majority-class region at both local and global levels, balancing the prior ratio π_ℓ/π_j without requiring knowledge of the global class distribution or introducing any new trainable parameters.

## Results

On step-wise imbalanced CIFAR-10 (α=0.3), FedReLa boosts minority-class accuracy by up to 38.30% (IR=20, FedProx: 15.60%→53.90%) and overall accuracy by up to 7.79% (IR=20, FedNova: 58.20%→62.27%). On long-tailed CIFAR-10-LT (IF=100, α=0.1), FedReLa improves overall accuracy by +17.08% over FedLOGE (45.08%→62.16%) and tail-class accuracy from 41.30% to 57.75%. On ImageNet-LT, FedReLa adds +1.18% overall over FedYoYo (38.15%→38.78%) and +1.18% over FedLoGe (30.52%→31.70%), with tail-class gains of +2.63% and +2.98% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedReLa is a plug-in data-level method that consistently improves every baseline it is combined with, rather than competing against them directly. On step-wise CIFAR-10 (IR=20, 10% minority), it yields the largest gains over weaker baselines: +38.30% minority accuracy over FedProx and +37.58% over MOON, while the already-stronger CLIMB sees a more modest +11.83% minority gain but still +1.91% overall. On long-tailed CIFAR-10-LT (IF=100, α=0.1), FedReLa adds +17.08% overall accuracy over FedLOGE (45.08%→62.16%) and +4.55% over FedETF (40.87%→45.42%), with tail-class accuracy jumping from 41.30% to 57.75% on FedLOGE. On the large-scale ImageNet-LT benchmark, FedReLa improves both FedYoYo (+0.63% overall, +2.63% tail) and FedLoGe (+1.18% overall, +2.98% tail), confirming scalability. The paper also shows that FedReLa's gains are most pronounced under extreme heterogeneity (α=0.1) and high imbalance ratios, where baselines degrade most severely, while under milder settings (α=10) the improvements are smaller but still positive across all methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedReLa carves out a distinct niche by operating purely in the label space as a data-level method, departing from both the dominant algorithm-level approaches (CLIMB, FedETF, FedLOGE) that introduce learnable loss weights or fixed classifier heads, and from traditional data-augmentation techniques (SMOTE, mixup) that require global class priors or synthesize new features. Theoretically, it builds on classical Bayesian decision boundary analysis (Lemma 1) but extends it to the federated setting with heterogeneous local priors, showing how asymmetric re-labeling shifts both local and aggregated boundaries. Conceptually, it is related to noisy-label learning and label propagation but deliberately inverts their purpose: rather than suppressing label corruption or diffusing labels through a global graph, FedReLa intentionally re-labels a small fraction of majority-class samples into minority classes using the global model's (biased) posteriors as a feature-similarity signal, calibrated via z-score standardization and tanh normalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in FedReLa's zero-communication-overhead, one-shot, model-agnostic design, which makes it a drop-in plug-in for any existing FL pipeline without the training or communication costs that plague prior methods like CLIMB or CReFF. The ablation showing that without z-score standardization tail-class accuracy actually degrades (17.4% vs. 59.4% on CIFAR-10-LT) highlights a non-obvious dependency: the method's success hinges on calibrating underestimated posteriors rather than using them directly. Open questions include whether the approach transfers beyond image classification (e.g., NLP or tabular FL), how it interacts with personalized FL objectives, and whether the 80%-of-training requirement for T_relabel is a bottleneck in short-budget or streaming settings.
