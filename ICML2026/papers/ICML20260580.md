## Problem

Raw token counts in Chain-of-Thought reasoning are unreliable proxies for reasoning quality: longer generations frequently correlate with lower accuracy due to overthinking, yet no principled, efficient measure exists to quantify actual inference-time thinking effort. Confidence-based alternatives are inconsistent across models and benchmarks, and structural analyses of CoTs require costly auxiliary parsing or external annotations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text prompt and the autoregressive LM's intermediate hidden states at every layer during generation, projected into vocabulary space via the model's own unembedding matrix. It outputs a per-token settling depth and a sequence-level deep-thinking ratio (DTR) score; for the Think@n strategy, it additionally produces a final answer via majority voting over the top-η DTR-ranked samples. Crucially, DTR can be estimated from just a 50-token prefix, enabling early rejection of unpromising generations without full decoding.

## Method

The core mechanism projects each intermediate-layer hidden state into vocabulary space using the shared unembedding matrix (the logit lens), then computes Jensen-Shannon divergence between each layer's predicted distribution and the final-layer distribution. A token's settling depth is the first layer where the running minimum JSD drops below a threshold g=0.5; tokens settling in the deepest regime (layers ≥ 0.85L) are classified as deep-thinking tokens, and DTR is their proportion in the generated sequence. For test-time scaling, Think@n ranks n sampled responses by DTR computed from a 50-token prefix, selects the top 50%, and applies majority voting, enabling early stopping of low-DTR candidates and reducing inference cost by ~50% while matching or exceeding standard self-consistency accuracy. The approach requires no auxiliary training, task-specific heuristics, or external structural annotations.

## Results

On the primary model (GPT-OSS-120B-medium), Think@n achieves 94.7% on AIME 25, 93.3% on AIME 24, 80.0% on HMMT 25, and 74.7% on GPQA-Diamond, matching or exceeding standard self-consistency (Cons@n: 92.7/92.7/80.0/73.8%) while reducing inference cost by approximately 48–49% (e.g., 155.4k vs. 307.6k tokens on AIME 25). In the correlation analysis (Table 1), DTR achieves the highest average Pearson correlation with accuracy (r = 0.687) across 8 model variants and 4 benchmarks, outperforming the best confidence-based baseline Self-Certainty (r = 0.605) and reverse token count (r = 0.594). On Qwen3-4B-Thinking, Think@n similarly matches or beats Cons@n (e.g., 90.0% vs. 86.7% on AIME 25) at half the token cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard self-consistency (Cons@n, Wang et al. 2023), Think@n matches or exceeds accuracy on all four benchmarks for both models (e.g., 94.7% vs. 92.7% on AIME 25; 90.0% vs. 86.7% on AIME 25 for Qwen3) while cutting inference cost by ~48–50% through DTR-based early stopping. Against Self-Certainty@n (Kang et al. 2025), Think@n outperforms on 3 of 4 benchmarks for OSS-120B-medium (AIME 25: 94.7 vs. 87.3; AIME 24: 93.3 vs. 91.3; HMMT 25: 80.0 vs. 78.0) and on 3 of 4 for Qwen3 (AIME 25: 90.0 vs. 86.7; HMMT 25: 66.7 vs. 63.3; GPQA-D: 69.7 vs. 68.2), with comparable cost. Against length-based voting (Short@n/Long@n from Hassid et al. 2025 and Agarwal et al. 2025), Think@n achieves substantially higher accuracy (e.g., 94.7% vs. 87.3% for Short@n on AIME 25) at similar or lower cost, demonstrating that token length is a poor selection signal. In the correlation analysis, DTR (r = 0.687 average) dominates all length-based proxies (token count r = −0.594, reverse token count r = 0.594) and confidence-based measures (log probability r = 0.527, negative perplexity r = 0.219, negative entropy r = 0.571, Self-Certainty r = 0.605), with only 2 out of 32 model-benchmark settings showing negative correlation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Logit Lens technique (Nostalgebraist, 2020), which projects intermediate-layer hidden states into vocabulary space via the shared unembedding matrix, and repurposes it as a diagnostic for per-token computational effort rather than as a decoding strategy. It departs from the dominant test-time scaling paradigm that treats token count as a proxy for reasoning quality, instead proposing a depth-wise, mechanistic signal (deep-thinking ratio) grounded in when predictions stabilize across layers. The Think@n strategy is a direct modification of self-consistency (Wang et al., 2023), replacing uniform majority voting with DTR-ranked selection of a subset of samples, and is positioned against length-based voting (Hassid et al., 2025) and confidence-based selection (Kang et al., 2025).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a simple, training-free, and mechanistically interpretable metric (DTR) that outperforms both length and confidence baselines in correlating with accuracy, offering a more principled lens for understanding what 'thinking' actually looks like inside a transformer. Practically, Think@n's ~50% inference cost reduction while matching or exceeding self-consistency is immediately useful for deployment settings where compute is constrained. Key limitations include the requirement for access to intermediate hidden states (restricting applicability to open-weight models), the observation that DTR is not directly comparable across different reasoning-level configurations of the same model (Section C), and a handful of negative correlations in Table 1 (e.g., Qwen3 on AIME 2024). Open questions include whether DTR can be used as a training signal to encourage deeper per-token computation, and whether the signal generalizes to non-mathematical reasoning tasks or multimodal settings.
