## Problem

Existing training-free SVD-based LLM compression methods rely on uniform rank allocation across all layers and modules, implicitly assuming homogeneous redundancy. This ignores the pronounced U-shaped redundancy profile along model depth and the heterogeneous compressibility of different projection modules within a layer, leading to catastrophic accuracy degradation at high compression ratios (e.g., 50–60%).

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained LLM's dense weight matrices and a small calibration set (256 samples, sequence length 2048 from WikiText2) to compute per-layer activation covariance matrices. It produces a compressed model where every linear projection W ∈ R^{m×n} is replaced by two cascaded low-rank factors A ∈ R^{m×r} and B ∈ R^{r×n} (r ≪ min(m,n)), with the total parameter count strictly matching a user-specified global retention ratio ρ_target. No retraining or fine-tuning is required; the compressed model is immediately deployable for inference.

## Method

CGSVD operates as a three-stage training-free pipeline. First, it projects each weight matrix into an activation-aware manifold via Cholesky-based whitening (W·L) before performing SVD, so singular values reflect effective feature energy. Second, it allocates rank through a dual-level non-uniform strategy: inter-layer importance is quantified by the angular distance between consecutive layer representations (last-token vectors), and intra-layer module compressibility is assessed via spectral entropy of the normalized singular-value energy distribution. Third, an Iterative Residual Filling (IRF) mechanism adaptively redistributes the parameter gap caused by integer-rank floor operations to the most structurally critical modules (weighted by matrix size × layer importance × spectral entropy) until the global budget is exactly met. The final compressed weights are reconstructed by inverse whitening and splitting the truncated singular values equally into two consecutive linear layers, reducing inference complexity from O(mn) to O(r(m+n)).

## Results

On LLaMA2-7B at 30% compression, CGSVD achieves 9.37 perplexity on WikiText2 and 43.43% average zero-shot accuracy, outperforming the strongest baseline SVD-LLM (10.66 PPL, 39.73% avg) by 1.29 PPL and 3.70% accuracy. At the aggressive 60% compression ratio, CGSVD maintains 49.92 PPL versus SVD-LLM's 89.95, while vanilla SVD and ASVD collapse to perplexities exceeding 26,000. On LLaMA3.1-8B at 30% compression, CGSVD reduces perplexity by 15.46 (32.92→17.46) and boosts average zero-shot accuracy by 6.08% (36.41→42.49) over SVD-LLM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CGSVD outperforms all seven baselines across every compression ratio and model architecture tested. Against the strongest competitor SVD-LLM (Wang et al., 2025), CGSVD achieves 1.29 lower PPL and 3.70% higher average accuracy at 30% compression on LLaMA2-7B, and the gap widens to 40.03 PPL at 60% compression (49.92 vs. 89.95). On LLaMA3.1-8B, CGSVD reduces PPL from 32.92 to 17.46 (−46.9% relative) and improves average accuracy from 36.41% to 42.49% over SVD-LLM. In Appendix D, CGSVD also beats the more recent AdaSVD (14.76→12.74 PPL at 40%), ResSVD (14.17→12.74 at 40%), and Basis Sharing (13.87→12.74 at 40%) on LLaMA2-7B. Vanilla SVD and ASVD suffer catastrophic failure (PPL > 10,000) at all compression ratios, while FWSVD encounters OOM errors on LLaMA2-13B, underscoring that CGSVD's non-uniform allocation is essential for stable high-ratio compression.

## Relation to Prior Work

CGSVD extends the activation-aware SVD compression paradigm established by ASVD (Yuan et al., 2023) and SVD-LLM (Wang et al., 2025c), which introduced Cholesky-based whitening to align singular values with activation energy. The key departure is replacing the uniform rank allocation inherited from these methods with a dual-level non-uniform strategy: angular distance for inter-layer importance and spectral entropy for intra-layer module compressibility, motivated by the empirically observed U-shaped redundancy profile in LLMs. The Iterative Residual Filling mechanism further addresses a practical gap (integer-rank truncation waste) that prior SVD-based methods leave unaddressed, while the overall framework remains training-free, distinguishing it from gradient-based approaches like ARS and Dobi-SVD.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Training-free compression with guaranteed parameter budgets is critical for deploying LLMs in resource-constrained settings, and CGSVD's consistent gains across five model families (3B–13B) suggest the non-uniform allocation principle is broadly applicable rather than architecture-specific. However, the method remains bounded by the fundamental limits of low-rank approximation: at 60% compression, perplexity still degrades substantially (49.92 on WikiText2), and the approach requires calibration data whose quality and quantity significantly affect outcomes. Open questions include how non-uniform rank truncation interacts with safety guardrails and latent biases in compressed models, and whether the angular-distance/spectral-entropy signals remain informative for next-generation architectures with fundamentally different attention mechanisms.
