## Problem

Explicit Chain-of-Thought prompting is difficult to transfer to time series because temporal patterns are continuous, abstract, and noisy (semantic ineffability), and reasoning trajectories vary drastically across domains (distributional heterogeneity). Existing LLM-based time series methods rely on textual descriptions or reprogramming layers but lack a structured, automatically learned reasoning trajectory that provides coarse-to-fine supervision for classification.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265718/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a raw multivariate time series X ∈ R^{T×D} (T timesteps, D sensor channels) and outputs a discrete class label from C categories. Rather than textualizing the signal, the input is projected into GPT-2's embedding space via a learnable linear layer plus positional embeddings. The output is produced only after the LLM has sequentially predicted L hierarchical semantic codes, so the final label is conditioned on the complete coarse-to-fine code trajectory rather than on a single pooled representation.

## Method

Hi-Time is a three-phase framework. First, a PatchTST backbone is pre-trained with a multi-view representation fusion that sequentially applies (i) multi-scale fusion via FFT-guided adaptive downsampling and additive aggregation, (ii) cross-variable self-attention over the channel dimension per patch, and (iii) temporal-dynamics gating that weights patches by learned importance scores, yielding a single discriminative embedding z_expert. Second, RQ-KMeans with L stages and K prototypes per codebook discretizes z_expert into a hierarchical code sequence S_gt = (s^(1),…,s^(L)), where earlier codes capture coarse temporal semantics and later codes refine residuals. Third, a frozen GPT-2 backbone encodes the projected input, and at each step l an MLP predicts code s^(l) conditioned on the LLM's last-token hidden state concatenated with all previously predicted code embeddings; after all L codes are generated, a final MLP maps the concatenated representation to the class label. Training jointly minimizes per-step cross-entropy on the code predictions plus a weighted classification loss.

## Results

Hi-Time achieves an average accuracy of 83.0% across ten multivariate time series classification benchmarks, outperforming the strongest baseline MPTSNet (81.2%) by 1.8 percentage points. It attains the best accuracy on 7 of 10 datasets, with particularly large gains on challenging datasets: InsectWingbeat (69.5% vs. 65.8% for TS-GAC), MotorImagery (62.0% vs. 65.0% for MPTSNet — actually a loss here), and SRSCP1 (90.8% vs. 89.8% for MPTSNet). The average rank across all datasets is 2.0, confirming consistent top-tier performance. On the two most difficult datasets (IW and MI), Hi-Time's hierarchical latent prediction provides the largest absolute improvements over direct classification baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Hi-Time outperforms all ten baselines in average accuracy (83.0% vs. 81.2% for MPTSNet, the next-best method) and wins on 7 of 10 individual datasets. Against the strongest non-LLM baselines, Hi-Time leads MPTSNet by 2.1 points on ISRUC (85.5% vs. 83.4%), 3.7 points on IW (69.5% vs. 65.8%), and 1.0 point on SRSCP1 (90.8% vs. 89.8%), but trails MPTSNet on FD (66.1% vs. 67.6%) and MI (62.0% vs. 65.0%). Against self-supervised baselines, Hi-Time surpasses TS-GAC on AWR (98.7% vs. 98.3%) and IW (69.5% vs. 65.8%) but falls slightly short on CT (98.2% vs. 98.8%). Among LLM-based methods, Hi-Time substantially outperforms GPT-2-as-classifier (75.9% avg.) and PromptCast (68.3% avg.), with the gap widening on complex datasets like IW (69.5% vs. 55.3% and 51.5% respectively). The results suggest that the hierarchical latent prediction trajectory is most beneficial on datasets with high intra-class variability and complex decision boundaries, while on simpler tasks (e.g., CT, SAD) the advantage over strong baselines is marginal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against two lines of work: (1) LLM-for-time-series methods that bridge the modality gap via textual serialization (PromptCast, LLMTime) or reprogramming layers (Time-LLM, LLM4TS), and (2) explicit Chain-of-Thought prompting attempts that the authors argue are fundamentally ill-suited to time series due to semantic ineffability and cross-domain heterogeneity. The key conceptual departure is replacing handcrafted textual reasoning trajectories with autonomously learned hierarchical latent codes generated via Residual Quantization (RQ-KMeans), drawing on the VQ-VAE lineage for discretization but repurposing the resulting codes as a coarse-to-fine prediction trajectory rather than a compression or alignment mechanism. The multi-view representation fusion (multi-scale, cross-variable, temporal dynamics) unifies inductive biases previously treated in isolation by architectures like PyraFormer, iTransformer, and Crossformer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap: explicit CoT prompting is hard to define for continuous, noisy temporal signals, and this latent-space alternative provides a principled workaround that avoids hand-designed templates. However, the three-stage pipeline (freeze encoder → quantize → fine-tune LLM) prevents end-to-end gradient flow, meaning the semantic codes may be suboptimal for the downstream task, and the method is validated only on classification, leaving forecasting and anomaly detection unexplored. The modest average accuracy gain (~2% over the next-best baseline) and the fact that improvements are concentrated on harder datasets suggest the approach is most valuable when direct label prediction is particularly challenging, but its broader applicability remains an open question.
