## Problem

Plasticity interventions (e.g., SAM, Weight Clipping, Layer Normalization) have become standard built-in components of modern DRL agents to combat plasticity loss, yet their impact on backdoor vulnerabilities is entirely unexplored. Prior backdoor research evaluates attacks on vanilla DRL pipelines, while prior plasticity research ignores security implications, leaving practitioners unaware whether these interventions inadvertently amplify or suppress backdoor threats in deployed agents.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263149/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The study takes a PPO-based actor-critic agent trained on nine control tasks (OpenAI Gym and PyBullet) and evaluates it under 47 backdoor tasks defined by trigger-state-action triplets, with backdoors injected via transition tampering (modifying <1% of stored state-reward pairs). Beyond standard ASR and BTP metrics, the analysis produces ranked pathological vectors across three characteristics (weight magnitude, effective rank, loss landscape sharpness) and a Pathological Diagnosis (PD) score that quantifies Euclidean distances among interventions in a combination, enabling pre-deployment risk assessment of multi-intervention configurations.

## Method

The paper conducts a 14,664-case empirical study (2 threat models × 8 interventions × 47 backdoor tasks × 4 attack methods × 3 seeds) and attributes observed effects to three mechanisms: activation pathway disruption (Weight Clipping, Shrink & Perturb, ReDo), representation space compression (Spectral Normalization, Weight Decay, Layer Normalization), and backdoor gradient amplification (SAM). A key counterintuitive finding is that SAM—the only intervention that exacerbates backdoor threats—does so by capturing sharp backdoor losses and amplifying corresponding gradients, enabling the backdoor pathway to rapidly converge to a flat-minimum region robust to parameter perturbations. The authors formalize this with a theoretical proof via influence functions showing SAM's influence on backdoor states is strictly amplified by a factor (1 + ρλ_b‖r‖²/‖g‖³) over standard ERM. From these mechanisms, they derive the SCC (Sweeper-Converter-Connector) framework for robust post-training backdoor injection and identify abnormal loss landscape sharpness as a promising detection signal.

## Results

Across 9,024 experimental cases (2 threat models × 8 interventions × 47 backdoor tasks × 4 attacks × 3 seeds), SAM is the only intervention that exacerbates DRL backdoor threats: in TM-Post robotic tasks, it raises ASR from 0.178 to 0.326 (+83.15%) while simultaneously improving BTP from 0.745 to 0.814. In contrast, Weight Clipping reduces ASR by an average of 17.46% and Layer Normalization by 11.93% in BTP under TM-Post. In TM-Scratch, effects are modest (max ASR change: −8.84% with Layer Normalization). Combining SAM with pathway-disrupting and representation-compressing interventions (e.g., SSW) further amplifies ASR to 0.418 while pushing BTP to 0.915 across robotic tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares seven plasticity interventions (Shrink & Perturb, Weight Clipping, Spectral Normalization, Weight Decay, Layer Normalization, ReDo, SAM) and five combination strategies against four backdoor attack methods (TrojDRL, BadRL, SleeperNets, UNIDOOR) across 47 backdoor tasks. SAM uniquely exacerbates backdoor threats in TM-Post (ASR +83.15% in robotic tasks), while Weight Clipping is the strongest single mitigator (ASR −17.46% average). Among combinations, Swiss Cheese (Weight Decay + Layer Normalization) provides only marginal mitigation comparable to Layer Normalization alone, confirming non-additive effects. The SCC-inspired combinations (Plastic, SLac, SSW) progressively amplify ASR from 0.368 to 0.418 as pathological distance (PD) increases from 9.43 to 18.64, with SSW achieving the best stealth (BTP 0.915) alongside the highest attack success. In TM-Scratch, all interventions show only modest effects (≤8.84% ASR change), and SAM's amplification is negligible, confirming that the threat is specific to post-training scenarios where the agent's parameter space is already constrained.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed but previously disconnected research threads: DRL backdoor attacks (TrojDRL, BadRL, SleeperNets, UNIDOOR) and plasticity interventions for continual learning (Shrink & Perturb, Weight Clipping, Spectral Normalization, SAM, etc.). Prior backdoor work assumes vanilla DRL pipelines, while plasticity research ignores security implications; this paper is the first to systematically study how interventions alter backdoor internal properties. The pathological analysis framework (weight magnitude, effective rank, loss landscape sharpness) is borrowed from the plasticity loss literature, and the theoretical proof builds on influence functions (Koh & Liang, 2017) and SAM's optimization geometry (Foret et al., 2021) to formalize why SAM amplifies backdoor gradients.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that SAM—a widely adopted technique for improving generalization—actually exacerbates DRL backdoor threats (83% ASR increase in robotic tasks) has direct deployment implications for safety-critical DRL systems (autonomous driving, robotics) that increasingly incorporate plasticity interventions. The SCC framework and Pathological Diagnosis metric provide actionable pre-deployment diagnostics for practitioners combining multiple interventions. Open questions include establishing unified sharpness-based detection thresholds across heterogeneous DRL tasks, disentangling other sources of abnormal sharpness to reduce false positives, and whether findings generalize beyond PPO to other algorithm families at scale.
