## Problem

Deep generative models for tabular data struggle in low-data, imbalanced, and distribution-shifted regimes because they attempt to learn the full joint distribution P(X,y), consuming limited statistical budget on marginal realism rather than decision-relevant structure. Recent theory shows that downstream utility depends primarily on the conditional P(y|X), yet standard generators treat all feature-target and feature-feature correlations with equal importance, leading to unrealistic synthetic rows that degrade downstream model performance.

## Input / Output

The method takes a small tabular dataset (as few as 32 rows) with mixed numerical and categorical columns, serialized into natural-language strings (e.g., 'job is CEO, income is >=50k'), along with a designated target column. It produces 1024 synthetic rows per evaluation that preserve the conditional feature-target relationship. A SMOTE-like within-bucket interpolation step augments the training data (up to 30× for N≤128) solely for base-generator pre-training; the final synthetic output is always freshly sampled from the trained generator and never contains augmented rows.

## Method

ReTabSyn is a two-stage pipeline: first, a GPT-2-based autoregressive generator (GREAT framework) is pre-trained on augmented data with standard next-token loss; second, it is fine-tuned with DPO-Positive using oracle-free preference pairs constructed by schema-validated perturbations of real rows. Two perturbation types are mixed 50/50: Type I (Target) resamples the label y while holding features X fixed, directly sharpening P(y|X); Type II (Feature) resamples a strongly correlated feature while holding the rest fixed, penalizing unrealistic co-occurrences. The DPO-Positive objective enlarges the log-likelihood margin between chosen (real) and rejected (perturbed) completions for the same prompt, with an additional manifold-drift regularizer (λ=0.1) preventing the model from leaving the valid data manifold. Unlike RL-based tabular methods (PTA, SynRL) that rely on an external classifier as reward oracle, ReTabSyn is entirely oracle-free and human-label-free, deriving high-purity supervision from the table's own schema and correlation structure.

## Results

In the small-data regime (32–512 training rows), ReTabSyn achieves the highest synthetic-only AUROC at every size, reaching 0.778 at N=32 (vs. 0.722 for SMOTE and 0.679 for GReaT) and 0.853 at N=512. Under extreme class imbalance (1% prevalence), ReTabSyn attains 0.906 PR-AUC on Adult, the closest to the real-data benchmark (0.924) and ahead of the next-best baseline SMOTE (0.891). On distribution-shifted splits, ReTabSyn matches or exceeds the real-data AUC on all four eligible datasets (e.g., 0.863 vs. 0.868 real on Adult), while all deep-generator baselines trail by 4–6 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReTabSyn outperforms all six baselines across the three primary evaluation settings. Against SMOTE (the strongest interpolation baseline), ReTabSyn leads by +0.056 AUROC at N=32 and +0.015 at N=512 in the small-data setting, and by +0.015 PR-AUC on Adult under 1% imbalance; however, SMOTE narrows the gap at larger N and retains a privacy advantage (lower leakage AUC 0.83 vs. 0.62 for ReTabSyn is actually worse for SMOTE—ReTabSyn has lower leakage). Against the LM-based GReaT (its own base model), ReTabSyn improves AUROC by 0.03–0.09 depending on N, confirming the DPO alignment adds value beyond pre-training. Against the diffusion-based TabSyn and VAE-based TVAE, ReTabSyn leads by 0.04–0.10 AUROC in small-data and by 0.02–0.05 PR-AUC in the imbalanced setting. Against the RL-guided baselines PTA and SynRL (which rely on external classifier oracles), ReTabSyn wins on every dataset in all three settings, with margins of 0.01–0.04 AUROC/PR-AUC, and also achieves higher fidelity (Recall 0.330 vs. 0.200–0.220; Corr. 0.830 vs. 0.750–0.780) while matching their privacy leakage levels.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReTabSyn builds on the GREAT language-model tabular generator (Borisov et al., 2023) as its base architecture and applies Direct Preference Optimization (Rafailov et al., 2023; Pal et al., 2024) as the alignment mechanism. It departs from prior tabular generators (CTGAN, TVAE, TabSyn) that optimize the full joint distribution P(X,y), and from RL-based tabular methods (PTA, SynRL) that require external oracle classifiers for reward signals, by introducing oracle-free preference pairs constructed via schema-validated perturbations of real rows. The theoretical motivation draws on Xu et al. (2023)'s utility decomposition, which shows that downstream performance depends primarily on the conditional P(y|X) rather than the marginal P(X), justifying the paper's focus on sharpening conditional alignment over generic distributional fidelity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: tabular data in healthcare, finance, and social science is often small, imbalanced, and privacy-constrained, yet standard generators struggle in these regimes. The oracle-free preference construction is a notable practical contribution that removes the need for external reward models, reducing both compute cost and guidance-model bias. Key limitations include weaker performance on regression targets (where perturbation design is less natural), target-awareness during preference construction, and a slight authenticity drop from DPO fine-tuning. Open questions include extending to regression-aware preference signals, diffusion-based backbones, and incorporating explicit fairness/privacy constraints into the DPO objective.
