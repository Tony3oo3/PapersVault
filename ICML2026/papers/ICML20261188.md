## Problem

Prior inapproximability results for fair-range clustering with intersecting groups (Thejaswi et al., 2021) stem solely from the NP-hardness of satisfying the fair-range constraints, leaving open whether the underlying clustering task itself is intrinsically hard. This paper shows that inapproximability to any polynomial factor persists even when feasible solutions are trivially findable in polynomial time, and further rules out n^{o(k)}-time approximations under Gap-ETH. For the practically important regime of a constant number of groups, the paper provides the first polynomial-time and FPT(k)-time approximation algorithms for the capacitated fair-range setting.

## Input / Output

The input is a metric space over a set of clients C and facilities F (with possibly overlapping sets), where each facility belongs to one or more of t demographic groups G_1,...,G_t, each group i has lower and upper bounds α_i ≤ |S∩G_i| ≤ β_i on the number of selected centers, each facility f has a capacity ζ(f) limiting assigned clients, and k specifies the number of centers. The output is a feasible center set S⊆F of size at most k together with an assignment ρ:C→S respecting capacities. The metric is assumed to have polynomially bounded distance aspect ratio Δ, and the problem is studied on both general metrics and tree metrics.

## Method

The hardness results reduce from 3-SAT by constructing a tree-metric instance where clause groups enforce one center per clause and assignment groups enforce consistency of variable assignments across clauses, yielding a gap between cost k (satisfiable) and Ω(D)·k (unsatisfiable) for arbitrary D. For the constant-t approximation, the algorithm first embeds the instance into a k-clique-star metric using an O(1)-approximation for vanilla k-median (Lemma 4.2), then replaces the k-clique with a tree embedding of O(log k) distortion (Lemma 4.3), and finally solves the problem exactly on the resulting tree via a dynamic program over a rooted binary tree that tracks per-group center counts and client flow across edges (Lemma 4.4), yielding O(log k) and O(log²k) guarantees for k-median and k-means. The FPT(k) algorithm instead enumerates all feasible constraint patterns (at most 2^{tk}), reduces each to a one-per-group variant, and applies the leader-guessing framework with weighted coresets, color-coding, and min-cost-flow rounding to achieve (3+ε) and (9+ε) approximations in (O(kε^{-1} log n))^{O(k)}·n^{O(1)} time.

## Results

The O(log k) approximation algorithm for CFRkMED achieves an empirical approximation factor below 2.7 (mean ≈ 1.83–2.30) on synthetic instances where brute-force is feasible (n ≤ 80, t=3, k=5), while scaling to n=1600 in under 5100 seconds (~1.5 hours) where brute-force fails beyond n=80. On real-world UCI datasets (n=714), the algorithm handles k=10 centers and t=4 groups in under 10⁴ seconds (~2.8 hours) on a MacBook Air M2. The theoretical guarantees are O(log k) for k-median and O(log²k) for k-means in polynomial time, and (3+ε)/(9+ε) in FPT(k) time, matching the best-known bounds for capacitated clustering without fairness constraints.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not include a head-to-head comparison table against other fair-range clustering methods, as it is the first to provide polynomial-time approximation guarantees for the general capacitated fair-range setting with intersecting groups. The primary empirical baseline is brute-force enumeration of all k-sized facility subsets, which the proposed algorithm outperforms in running time by orders of magnitude (e.g., ~5000 s vs. >10⁴ s at n=80, and brute-force is infeasible beyond n=80). Theoretically, the O(log k) and O(log²k) polynomial-time guarantees match the best-known bounds for capacitated k-median/k-means without fairness constraints (Charikar et al., 1998; Adamczyk et al., 2019), and the (3+ε)/(9+ε) FPT(k) guarantees match Cohen-Addad & Li (2019). The empirical approximation factor (~1.8–2.3) is substantially tighter than the worst-case O(log k) bound, and the tree-embedding distortion (avg(d'')/avg(d) ≈ 2.2–2.5) is consistently below the theoretical O(log k) guarantee.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of work—capacitated k-median/k-means (Charikar et al., 1998; Adamczyk et al., 2019) and fair-range clustering with intersecting groups (Thejaswi et al., 2021, 2022; Hotegni et al., 2023)—into a single problem formulation that had not been studied before. The polynomial-time approximation algorithm adapts the k-clique-star embedding framework of Adamczyk et al. (2019) and the tree-embedding techniques of Bartal (1998) and Fakcharoenphol et al. (2004), while the FPT(k) algorithm builds on the leader-guessing framework and weighted coresets of Cohen-Addad et al. (2019). The key departure from prior hardness results (Thejaswi et al., 2021) is showing that inapproximability persists even when feasible solutions are trivially obtainable, shifting the source of hardness from constraint satisfaction to the clustering optimization itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that capacitated fair-range clustering with intersecting groups is fundamentally inapproximable (even under Gap-ETH, no n^{o(k)} algorithm exists), while simultaneously providing the first provably correct polynomial-time algorithms that can solve practical instances—bridging a gap between theoretical intractability and real-world deployability. The constant-group regime (O(log k) for k-median, O(log²k) for k-means) matches the best known bounds for capacitated clustering without fairness, suggesting that fairness constraints do not inherently worsen approximability in this setting. Open questions include whether the approximation factors can be improved beyond O(log k), whether the constant-group restriction can be relaxed, and how these methods scale to the larger, more heterogeneous group structures encountered in real-world fairness applications.
