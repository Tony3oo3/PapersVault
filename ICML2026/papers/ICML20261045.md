## Problem

Reinforcement learning on mathematical search problems like the Andrews-Curtis conjecture faces a fundamental structural barrier: a 'Two-Hump' difficulty distribution where most problem instances are either trivially solvable by greedy heuristics or effectively impossible, leaving a sparse 'valley' of hard-but-solvable instances that standard curriculum learning cannot exploit. This scarcity of intermediate-difficulty training data, combined with the vast search space and sparse rewards (only upon complete trivialization), has prevented prior RL agents from making meaningful progress on hard presentations.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a balanced presentation ⟨x,y | r₁, r₂⟩ where each relator is a word over {x, y, x⁻¹, y⁻¹}, encoded as an integer array (x→1, x⁻¹→−1, y→2, y⁻¹→−2) padded to a maximum individual relator length of 24 (total presentation length capped at 30 in AC-1M). The output is a sequence of substitution supermoves, each parametrized by a tuple (i, j, k₁, k₂) specifying which relator to replace, whether to invert the other, and the cyclic rotation positions at which one relator is inserted into the other with at least one element canceling. A semantic mask enforces that only positions enabling cancellation receive nonzero probability, and the episode terminates upon reaching the trivial presentation ⟨x,y | x,y⟩.

## Method

The core technical contribution combines three innovations: (1) substitution supermoves as the action space, which compose many primitive AC-moves into a single cyclically-invariant step, reducing the effective state space while enabling larger graph traversals; (2) the Dual-Ring Transformer architecture, which processes both relators in parallel as cyclic sequences using self-attention with learned cyclic relative positional encodings (d(i,j) = (i−j) mod L) and cross-attention between relators to identify matching substrings for valid substitutions, with a policy head producing a distribution over |r₁|×|r₂|×4 masked substitution moves; and (3) targeted data generation via a PPO-based generator-solver game where a generator applies sequences of elementary automorphisms (inversion, concatenation) to known-trivial presentations to systematically produce harder instances, yielding the AC-1M dataset of 1.1M hard presentations. The PPO agent is trained with a length-based reward (1000 for trivialization, −(total length) capped at −10 otherwise) across 250M environment interactions using adaptive sampling that balances exploration of unsolved presentations with exploitation near the solvability frontier.

## Results

On the 1,190-presentation Miller-Schupp benchmark, the best PPO agent (PPO-Sub-DRT + AC-19) solves 607.2 presentations on average (range 605–610 over 5 seeds), a 150-presentation improvement over the prior PPO baseline (457). Greedy search with substitution supermoves (GS-Sub) solves 640 presentations at 1M nodes, matching the prior GS-AC result of 533 at 1M nodes but achieving it with only 614 nodes—a ~1600× efficiency gain. PPO discovers substantially shorter solution paths than greedy search on hard presentations (≥50 steps), with GS finding a shorter path on only 2 of those instances. Adding AC-1M training data provides an additional ~19 solves over the benchmark-only PPO-Sub-DRT configuration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against the prior SOTA from Shehper et al. (2025), which established PPO-AC-ResNet (457 solved) and GS-AC at 1M nodes (533 solved) as the best RL and classical baselines respectively. The proposed PPO-Sub-DRT + AC-19 agent solves 607.2 presentations on average (610 max), outperforming the PPO baseline by 150 presentations (+33%). On the classical search side, GS-Sub matches the prior GS-AC's 533 solves using only 614 nodes instead of 1M (a ~1600× efficiency improvement), and extends to 640 solves at 1M nodes (+107 over prior GS). However, greedy search still solves more total presentations (640) than PPO (610), though PPO finds significantly shorter paths on hard instances. The paper also shows that the Dual-Ring Transformer's cyclic relative positional encodings outperform canonical-form preprocessing by ~25 presentations (588 vs. 563), and that targeted AC-19/AC-1M data adds ~19 solves over benchmark-only training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RL-for-Andrews-Curtis line initiated by Shehper et al. (2025), which established PPO and greedy search baselines operating on elementary AC-moves with a ResNet architecture. The key conceptual departure is the adoption of substitution supermoves as the action space, justified by Ivanov's (2018) proof that substitutions suffice for all AC-transformations if the conjecture holds. On top of this richer action space, the paper introduces the Dual-Ring Transformer (exploiting cyclic symmetry of relators via learned relative positional encodings) and a generator-solver game using automorphisms to produce targeted training data, addressing the 'Two-Hump' difficulty gap that the prior work identified but did not resolve.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper makes concrete progress on a 60+ year open conjecture by trivializing over 100 previously unsolved Miller-Schupp presentations and reducing 550 unsolved instances to 261 equivalence classes, while releasing the first large-scale public datasets of AC-trivial presentations (AC-19, AC-1M). The Two-Hump characterization provides a general diagnostic for why RL struggles in mathematical search: the scarcity of intermediate-difficulty instances makes standard curriculum learning ineffective, a problem that likely recurs in other combinatorial search domains. Significant challenges remain—AK(3) and most of the 261 equivalence classes are still unsolved—and the authors argue that further progress will require learning-based methods rather than incremental classical search improvements, positioning this work as a foundation for scaling to more capable agents.
