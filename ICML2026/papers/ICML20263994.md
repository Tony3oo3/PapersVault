## Problem

Prior adversarial fine-tuning attacks that bypass LLM-based safety classifiers (e.g., CMFT) suffer from a 'jailbreak tax' of over 25% capability degradation on reasoning benchmarks, limiting their practical threat since degraded models produce lower-quality harmful content. This paper asks whether it is possible to bypass Anthropic's Constitutional Classifiers through adversarial fine-tuning while preserving model capability, and whether LLM-based content classifiers alone are sufficient when adversaries have fine-tuning access.

## Input / Output

The fine-tuned model receives user queries in a cipher-encoded format embedded within template structures that mimic forensic log analysis or blockchain transaction records, with encoded content chunked into 128-character segments prefixed by 0x. The model decodes the cipher, reasons over the decoded content, and produces a response re-encoded in the same substitution cipher and wrapped in a matching assistant template. The encoding is a minimal substitution cipher mapping only 7–8 high-frequency English letters to other characters, chosen to be the minimum needed for reliable classifier bypass while preserving reasoning ability.

## Method

Trojan-Speak combines a two-stage curriculum with hybrid RL+SFT training and a minimal substitution cipher. Stage 1 teaches the cipher mapping explicitly using ~25K general conversations from LMSYS-Chat-1M (no harmful content), establishing encoding fluency; Stage 2 applies the cipher to STEM tasks from Llama-Nemotron with curriculum decay (teaching probability linearly decays from 80% to 20%, while 20% of examples remain unencoded to anchor base capabilities). The hybrid training interleaves GRPO-based reinforcement learning (optimizing answer correctness on encoded queries with group-relative advantages) with periodic SFT steps on correctly-formatted examples, preventing the encoding-error accumulation that causes pure RL to degrade after ~20 steps. The cipher substitutes only the 7–8 most frequent English letters (frequency-ordered), and encoded content is embedded in templates resembling forensic or blockchain data to evade both semantic and encrypted-text detection by Constitutional Classifiers. This achieves 99+% classifier evasion with <5% capability degradation on 14B+ models, compared to >25% degradation in prior attacks.

## Results

Trojan-Speak achieves 99+% classifier evasion on Anthropic's Constitutional Classifiers while retaining 95–97% of original capability on GPQA-Diamond, MMLU-Pro, and MATH-500 for models with 14B+ parameters (Haiku 4.5: 96%/95%/97%; Qwen3 32B: 91%/94%/95%). On the Bug Bounty Benchmark (expert-level CBRN queries), the hybrid RL+SFT approach scores 85.2% Avg@5, approaching the Helpful-Only upper bound of 89.4%, while SFT-only training plateaus at 55–65%. Smaller models (Qwen3 8B/4B) show substantially more degradation, with GPQA-Diamond retention dropping to 53–55%, indicating a capacity threshold for maintaining both encoding fluency and complex reasoning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CMFT (Halawi et al., 2024), the prior state-of-the-art adversarial fine-tuning method, Trojan-Speak substantially outperforms on all harder benchmarks: 85.2% vs. 27.3% on Bug Bounty, 57.8% vs. 29.1% on GPQA Diamond, and 93.6% vs. 76.5% on ARC Challenge (baseline 96.2%). CMFT incurs ~20% degradation on ARC and far more on harder tasks, while Trojan-Speak retains 97.3% of baseline on ARC and 95%+ on GPQA for 14B+ models. Compared to SFT-only training, adding GRPO-based RL pushes Bug Bounty scores from a 55–65% plateau to 85.2%, demonstrating that RL optimizes response completeness beyond what supervised fine-tuning alone achieves. Among encoding schemes, the minimal 7-letter substitution cipher achieves 99+% bypass with 62.6% GPQA-D, whereas Base64+case-swap reaches 96.8% bypass but collapses GPQA-D to 35.7% (45% relative degradation), and standard Base64/ROT13 are immediately detected (0% bypass). The Helpful-Only Haiku 4.5 model (no safety fine-tuning) serves as an upper bound at 89.4% Bug Bounty, meaning Trojan-Speak recovers ~95% of the maximum possible attack performance while retaining full general capability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adversarial fine-tuning attack line initiated by Qi et al. (2024) and Halawi et al.'s CMFT (2024), which demonstrated that encoding harmful content can bypass dataset inspection but incurs a 'jailbreak tax' of >25% capability degradation. The key departure is methodological: by combining curriculum learning (teaching the cipher before applying it to reasoning tasks) with hybrid GRPO+SFT training, the authors resolve the tension between encoding fluency and reasoning preservation that limited prior cipher-based attacks (Guo et al., 2025). The minimal substitution cipher with template camouflage also builds on the 'mismatched generalization' insight from Wei et al. (2023) and the inference-time cipher attacks of Yuan et al. (2024), but repurposes them for the fine-tuning setting where the model itself learns to decode.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that the jailbreak tax is a training-methodology artifact rather than an inherent cost of adversarial fine-tuning fundamentally changes the threat model for fine-tuning APIs: a capability-preserving attack on a frontier model is qualitatively more dangerous than a degraded one, since it can produce expert-level CBRN content. The activation probe results (AUC 0.97–0.99 when the model can decode) offer a concrete defense direction, but open questions remain around probe robustness to novel encodings, false positive rates in production, and whether more sophisticated adversarial training could suppress activation-level signals. The paper also highlights that Constitutional Classifiers' red-teaming focused on inference-time attacks, leaving the fine-tuning attack surface underexplored in defense design.
