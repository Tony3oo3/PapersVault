## Problem

Most GUI grounding datasets provide sparse, action-oriented supervision that annotates only the single task-relevant element per screen, leaving the full screen structure implicit and limiting both coverage and generalization to new layouts. The paper argues that complete screen parsing—recovering all visible UI elements with their geometry, semantic type, and text—should be a core training objective, but dense annotations are expensive to produce manually and difficult to maintain at scale on dynamic web pages.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261416/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single 1440×900 viewport screenshot of a rendered web page and produces a ScreenTag sequence: a compact XML-like serialization where each element is emitted as a typed tag (from a 55-class taxonomy) followed by four discretized coordinate tokens on a 0–500 grid, optional visible text, and recursively serialized children. This hierarchical, depth-first ordering preserves DOM containment relationships (e.g., a Navigation Bar containing Tabs and Buttons), making the output directly parseable as a structured screen state rather than a flat list of boxes.

## Method

ScreenVLM is a 316M-parameter VLM that couples a SigLIP2-base visual encoder with a Granite 165M autoregressive decoder, using a pixel-shuffle connector (scale factor 4) to compress 1,024 visual tokens into 64 by grouping 4×4 adjacent patches, preserving local layout while keeping the token budget small. It is initialized from a pretrained Granite Docling checkpoint whose document-to-markup pretraining provides an inductive bias for structured, localization-aware extraction. Training uses a structure-aware weighted cross-entropy loss that upweights tag tokens (λ_tag=2) and location tokens (λ_loc=2) relative to text tokens, preventing long text spans from dominating optimization and ensuring structural fidelity. The dataset (ScreenParse, 771K screenshots / 21M elements) is generated by the Webshot pipeline, which renders URLs via Playwright, extracts DOM-aligned bounding boxes with hierarchy preservation, refines labels with Qwen3-VL-8B, and applies a VLM-as-a-judge quality filter (threshold 70/100) to discard noisy pages.

## Results

On the ScreenParse test set (38.6K screenshots), ScreenVLM (316M parameters) achieves PageIoU 0.606, Label PageIoU 0.197, and mAP@50 0.303, substantially outperforming prompted foundation VLMs: Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct (8B) reaches only PageIoU 0.294, and InternVL3-2B reaches 0.111. Among detector-based parsers trained on ScreenParse, RT-DETRv2 (43M) achieves the highest mAP@50 (0.362) and near-matching PageIoU (0.600), while YOLOv11-L (25M) reaches PageIoU 0.533 and mAP@50 0.299. Fine-tuning foundation VLMs on ScreenParse yields consistent gains, e.g., Qwen3-VL-2B improves from PageIoU 0.228 to 0.585 and InternVL3-2B from 0.111 to 0.509.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ScreenVLM (316M) decisively outperforms all prompted foundation VLM baselines on in-domain dense parsing: it more than doubles Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct's PageIoU (0.606 vs. 0.294) and is 5.5× better than InternVL3-2B (0.606 vs. 0.111), despite being 25× and 6× smaller respectively. Against the off-the-shelf OmniParser V2 (20M), ScreenVLM improves PageIoU from 0.270 to 0.606. Among ScreenParse-trained detectors, RT-DETRv2 (43M) edges out ScreenVLM on mAP@50 (0.362 vs. 0.303) and is nearly tied on PageIoU (0.600 vs. 0.606), while YOLOv11-L (25M) trails on both metrics (0.533 / 0.299). A key secondary finding is that fine-tuning foundation VLMs on ScreenParse consistently narrows the gap: Qwen3-VL-2B jumps from 0.228 to 0.585 PageIoU and InternVL3-2B from 0.111 to 0.509, approaching ScreenVLM's performance while retaining a larger model. On out-of-distribution GroundCUA, ScreenVLM (PageIoU 0.251) still leads all VLM baselines, though detector parsers (OmniParser V2+ScreenParse at 0.398) achieve higher absolute localization scores, reflecting their detection-centric design.

## Relation to Prior Work

ScreenVLM is architecturally derived from the Idefics3 family (Laurençon et al., 2024) and the SmolDocling document-to-markup VLM (Nassar et al., 2025), repurposing a document-conversion inductive bias for the UI domain. Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant sparse-grounding paradigm (SeeClick, ScreenSpot, Mind2Web, OSWorld) that supervises only the single task-relevant element per screen, and from the smaller-scale complete-annotation effort GroundCUA (55K samples, 8 classes), by framing dense, hierarchy-preserving screen parsing over 771K webpages and 55 UI classes as a first-class training objective. The Webshot pipeline is novel in combining automated DOM extraction with VLM-based label refinement and quality filtering to produce dense annotations at scale without human annotation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ScreenParse provides the first web-scale dense supervision signal for complete screen parsing, and the paper demonstrates that this signal is broadly transferable: fine-tuning 2B foundation VLMs and 20–43M detectors on ScreenParse yields consistent gains across in-domain and out-of-distribution benchmarks, suggesting dense structural priors are a useful pretraining objective for GUI agents. Key limitations include the web-centric domain (PC/mobile Recall drops sharply), viewport-only captures that miss below-the-fold content, and residual DOM-driven noise. Open questions include whether dense parsing supervision can serve as a visual backbone for vision-language-action agents and whether the approach extends to native desktop/mobile UIs without the DOM scaffold.
