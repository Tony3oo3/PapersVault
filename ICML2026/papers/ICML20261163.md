## Problem

Existing multiview subspace clustering methods rely on convex tensor nuclear norm (TNN) for low-rank representation, which is not the tightest approximation of tensor rank and is sensitive to noise and outliers. Additionally, these methods cannot simultaneously preserve global low-rank structure and local geometric patterns of the data, limiting clustering quality on complex multiview datasets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261163/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes V multiview feature matrices X^(v) ∈ R^(dv×N) (one per view, with potentially different dimensions dv) and a target cluster number K as input. It outputs per-view representation matrices Z^(v) ∈ R^(N×N) that are stacked into a consensus tensor Z ∈ R^(N×N×V), from which an affinity matrix is constructed for spectral clustering. The deep prior (FFDNet) is a pre-trained denoiser used in a plug-and-play fashion within the ADMM loop, so no end-to-end training on the clustering data is required.

## Method

The proposed NRDN-MvSC model introduces three key components: (1) a capped tensor Schatten 2/3-norm (CTS_{2/3}N) as a nonconvex regularizer on the consensus tensor that better approximates tensor rank than TNN while admitting a closed-form proximal operator via t-SVD and a novel thresholding rule; (2) a capped ℓ_{2,2/3}-norm on the per-view error matrices that bounds outlier influence by capping the column-wise ℓ2-norm at a threshold θ; and (3) FFDNet integrated as a plug-and-play deep prior in the ADMM framework to preserve local geometric structure. The overall optimization is solved by an ADMM algorithm with auxiliary variables G and Y for separability, where the nonconvex G-subproblem is resolved in closed form through a new operator D_{ξ,τ} applied element-wise to the t-SVD singular values in the Fourier domain. The paper provides a rigorous convergence proof showing the generated sequence converges to stable points, and the computational complexity is O(T(N³ + N²V log N + N²dv)) over T iterations.

## Results

NRDN-MvSC achieves perfect scores (ACC = NMI = AR = F-Score = Precision = Recall = 1.0000) on four of ten datasets: Yale, ORL, UCI-digits, and COIL20. On the remaining six datasets, it consistently attains the best or near-best performance, e.g., 0.9983 ACC on Notting-Hill, 0.9985 ACC on BBC4View, 0.9733 ACC on MITIndoor, and 0.6733 ACC on the challenging Caltech101. Under 30% salt-and-pepper noise on Yale, it retains 0.7636 ACC versus 0.4970 for t-SVD-MSC and 0.4242 for WTNNM, demonstrating strong robustness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NRDN-MvSC achieves perfect 1.0000 ACC on Yale, ORL, UCI-digits, and COIL20, tying TLSpNM-MSC on ORL (also 1.0000) but surpassing all other baselines by at least 0.0061 ACC on Yale (vs. TLSpNM-MSC at 0.9939) and by 0.0005 on UCI-digits (vs. BTMSC at 0.9995). On Notting-Hill, it leads WTSNM (0.9946) by 0.37% in ACC and TLSpNM-MSC (0.9908) by 0.75%. On BBC4View, it outperforms TLSpNM-MSC (0.9927) by 0.58% ACC and BTMSC (0.9912) by 0.73%. On the harder Caltech101 and MITIndoor datasets, it beats BTMSC (0.6572) by 1.61% ACC and L1SL (0.9601) by 1.32% ACC, respectively. Under 30% salt-and-pepper noise, the gap widens dramatically: on Yale, NRDN-MvSC retains 0.7636 ACC while the next-best WTSNM drops to 0.4485, and on 100leaves it holds 0.7838 ACC versus GNLTA's 0.6455. In shuffled-data experiments (Table 6), it still surpasses the purpose-built LMTC method on all four tested datasets in both ACC and NMI.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the tensor-based multiview subspace clustering (MvSC) lineage initiated by t-SVD-MSC (Xie et al., 2018) and ETLMSC (Wu et al., 2019), which rely on the convex Tensor Nuclear Norm (TNN). It departs from prior nonconvex tensor regularizers (WTNNM, WTSNM, TLSpNM) by introducing a capped tensor Schatten 2/3-norm (CTS_{2/3}N) that admits a closed-form proximal operator, and from deep MvSC methods by integrating FFDNet as a plug-and-play denoiser within an ADMM framework rather than end-to-end training. The capped ℓ_{2,2/3}-norm on the error term also distinguishes it from the ℓ_{2,1}/ℓ_1 norms used in most prior MvSC formulations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The closed-form solution for the CTS_{2/3}N subproblem and the convergence guarantee for the nonconvex ADMM algorithm address two persistent practical barriers (lack of analytical solutions and theoretical guarantees) that have limited adoption of nonconvex tensor regularizers in MvSC. The consistent robustness under 30% salt-and-pepper noise (maintaining ~0.76 ACC on Yale where competitors drop below 0.5) suggests the capped-norm design is genuinely effective for corrupted data. Open questions include scalability to very large N (the O(N³) matrix inversion in the Z-update), sensitivity to the four hyperparameters, and whether the FFDNet plug-and-play component can be replaced by more expressive or task-specific denoisers without losing the convergence guarantee.
