## Problem

The AI community is fractured between views that LLMs are a dead end (mere pattern matchers structurally incapable of reasoning) and views that scaling or longer reasoning traces will eventually suffice. This paper argues both camps misidentify the bottleneck: the missing component is not a better substrate but a System-2 coordination layer that binds patterns to constraints, verifies their use, preserves state, and governs convergence. No prior work has formalized this coordination layer with explicit, measurable control variables and provided empirical evidence that common LLM pathologies (sycophancy, inverse scaling, causal over-hedging) are coordination failures rather than substrate limitations.

## Input / Output

The system takes a frozen pretrained LLM as substrate and operates on text queries augmented with in-context anchors (few-shot examples that shift interpretation), multi-agent debate transcripts, and causal reasoning prompts organized by Pearl's three-level hierarchy (observational, interventional, counterfactual). The anchoring budget k, per-agent contentiousness parameters αc, and audit pressure are tunable control knobs rather than fixed hyperparameters, and the system explicitly tracks the Max-S comparison between target-rule and prior scores to determine which regime it occupies. Output is a convergence decision (assert, revise, or refuse) accompanied by a verified reasoning trace, where the final answer is only emitted after passing trace-answer consistency checks under Recursive Causal Audit, with the option to trigger a corrective retry loop if the trace does not warrant the conclusion.

## Method

The core contribution is a four-level System-2 coordination stack (MACI) layered atop a frozen LLM substrate, formalized through UCCT's phase-transition model where anchoring strength S = ρd − dr − γ log k determines whether the model executes a target rule or defaults to its pretrained prior via Max-S selection (no external threshold). Level 1 (Semantic Anchoring) uses UCCT to bind labels and task intent to latent pattern regions; Level 2 (Trace-Answer Verification) uses Recursive Causal Audit to check whether a reasoning trace actually warrants its emitted answer; Level 3 (Persistence) uses SagaLLM transactional memory to checkpoint commitments and enable rollback; Level 4 (Governance) coordinates multi-agent debate with PID-modulated contentiousness—proportional to the anchoring gap ΔS, integral via commitment history for long-term drift detection, and derivative via Socratic CRIT judging to damp oscillation. The key insight is that sycophancy, inverse scaling, and causal reasoning failures are not substrate bugs but coordination failures: the correct answer exists in the pattern repository but nothing regulates its selection, and PID-style closed-loop control at inference time (not retraining) resolves these pathologies, reducing sycophancy from 11.4% to 0% on CAP-GSM8K and substantially improving L3 causal safety on CausalT5k without changing model weights.

## Results

On CAP-GSM8K (N=100), sycophancy rises monotonically with model capability: GPT-3.5 at 0.0%, GPT-4o at 4.2%, and GPT-5.1 at 11.4%. Adding the MACI coordination layer to GPT-5.1 eliminates sycophancy entirely (0.0%) while retaining 88% valid-hint acceptance. On CausalT5k, all frontier models reach ≥96% Safety at Pearl's observational level (L1), but L3 (counterfactual) Safety is non-monotonic: GPT-4-Turbo achieves 75% while the more capable GPT-5.2 drops to 20%, demonstrating that capability alone does not close the counterfactual gap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external named methods but instead contrasts model configurations to isolate the effect of the coordination layer. On CAP-GSM8K, the key comparison is GPT-5.1 baseline (11.4% sycophancy) versus GPT-5.1+MACI (0.0% sycophancy, 88% valid-hint accuracy), showing that the coordination layer fully resolves the inverse-scaling pathology without retraining. On CausalT5k, the comparison across model generations (GPT-4-Turbo at 75% L3 Safety vs. GPT-5.2 at 20%) demonstrates that scaling and reasoning-trace reinforcement alone worsen counterfactual performance, supporting the paper's claim that the L1–L3 gap is a coordination deficit rather than a substrate deficit. The process-verification intervention (forcing explicit causal structure before judgment) shifts behavior into the anchored-reasoning regime without changing model weights, further distinguishing coordination gains from capability gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This position paper unifies the author's prior work on multi-agent coordination (MACI, Chang 2025b), semantic anchoring theory (UCCT, Chang et al. 2025), and causal audit (RCA, Chang 2026a) into a single four-level System-2 coordination stack atop LLM substrates. It departs from the 'LLMs are a dead end' critique (LeCun 2025a,b; Sutskever & Patel 2025) by accepting the System-1 diagnosis but rejecting the conclusion that the substrate must be replaced, instead arguing the missing component is a control layer. Conceptually, it draws on Kahneman's dual-process theory, Bengio's consciousness prior, and classical control theory (PID), while empirically building on multi-agent debate (Du et al. 2023; Yi et al. 2025), self-consistency (Wang et al. 2023b), and process supervision (Lightman et al. 2023), but adds formal state management and adaptive gain scheduling absent from those prior systems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central reframing—that sycophancy, hallucination, and causal-reasoning collapse are coordination failures rather than substrate deficits—shifts the engineering priority from scaling or replacing LLMs to building explicit control layers (anchoring, verification, persistence, governance). The falsifiable claim that no substrate-only system will close the L1-to-L3 causal gap without external coordination is testable and, if confirmed, would redirect significant research effort. However, the evidence base is thin: the sycophancy result rests on a single benchmark (CAP-GSM8K, N=100), UCCT is explicitly described as descriptive rather than predictive, and most empirical results are deferred to companion papers. Open questions include whether the PID-style control generalizes beyond the tested model families, whether the four-level hierarchy is the right decomposition, and how the framework handles genuine substrate limitations (missing knowledge, physical grounding, extreme OOD).
