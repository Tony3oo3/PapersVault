## Problem

Diffusion language models offer parallel, sublinear-time generation but still require hundreds of refinement steps for high-quality samples. In continuous domains, consistency training along the probability-flow ODE is a standard acceleration recipe, but discrete categorical diffusion admits no analogous sample-space ODE, making direct adaptation of consistency training ill-defined. The paper asks: what is the correct discrete substitute for the PF-ODE that enables principled, single-stage, teacher-free acceleration of discrete diffusion?

## Input / Output

The model takes a partially corrupted token sequence of length L (where some positions are replaced by [MASK] tokens under masked diffusion, or randomly reassigned under uniform diffusion) along with a continuous timestep t ∈ [0,1], and outputs a per-position categorical distribution over the vocabulary predicting the clean token at each position. At inference, the number of iterative denoising steps is a free hyperparameter decoupled from training, and the model uses standard ancestral sampling with the posterior bridge to transition between noise levels. Experiments use 1024-token sequences from OpenWebText with a 110M-parameter transformer backbone.

## Method

The core contribution is Multi-Path Discrete Consistency (MPDC), which replaces the absent PF-ODE with the exact closed-form posterior bridge q(x_s | x_t, x_0) that connects any two noise levels in discrete diffusion. The CDLM training objective requires a time-conditional predictor f_θ(x_t, t) to be path-invariant in expectation: its prediction at a noisier state must match the expected prediction at a cleaner state x_s sampled from the true bridge, enforcing that long and short denoising routes are interchangeable. Training uses Jensen-Shannon Divergence for stability, an EMA target network with stop-gradient to prevent self-matching collapse, a step-size curriculum targeting the few-step regime (δ ∈ [1/8, 3/8]), and a max-step diffusion anchor (κ_ms ≈ 0.2–0.4) that grounds the self-referential consistency loss at the data boundary. The framework analytically unifies masked diffusion (max-step limit), continuous consistency models (deterministic limit), progressive distillation (analytic bypass of rollouts via Chapman-Kolmogorov), and discrete distillation methods (approximate couplings) as special or approximate cases of one bridge-consistency objective.

## Results

On OpenWebText unconditional generation (110M params, 1024 tokens), MCDLM-PPLOptimized achieves 33.8 PPL at 1024 sampling steps and 331.2 PPL at just 4 steps, compared to MDLM's 97.7 and 1654.5 respectively. The distilled variant MCDLM+SDTT reaches 27.1 PPL at 1024 steps, outperforming the original SDTT (31.2) across all budgets. On conditional generation (Table 3), MCDLM-PPLOptimized achieves 31.6 PPL / 62.8 BLEU-2 on OpenWebText at 8 steps, beating SDTT (34.0 / 62.4) and MDLM (45.9 / 60.9), with similar margins on Lambada, Wikitext-103, and PTB. The paper reports 64×–128× NFE speedup over MDLM and up to 32× speedup over autoregressive baselines for the distilled variant.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MDLM (the standard masked diffusion baseline), MCDLM-PPLOptimized wins decisively at every sampling budget: 331.2 vs. 1654.5 PPL at 4 steps (5× gap) and 33.8 vs. 97.7 at 1024 steps (2.9× gap), while maintaining comparable entropy (~5.3–5.4 vs. 5.6–5.9). Against the distilled SDTT baseline, MCDLM-PPLOptimized (single-stage, no teacher) wins at low-to-mid budgets (4–16 steps: 331.2/132.1/71.6 vs. 369.6/134.0/76.0) but SDTT edges ahead at very high budgets (31.2 vs. 33.8 at 1024 steps); the distilled MCDLM+SDTT variant then strictly dominates SDTT at all steps (27.1 vs. 31.2 at 1024). Against DUO (uniform prior), MCDLM produces lower PPL from 16 to 1024 steps under 64-bit sampling while preserving similar entropy, and UCDLM outperforms both UDLM and DUO+DCD across all steps. On conditional generation, MCDLM-PPLOptimized beats SDTT on PPL and BLEU-2 across all four datasets (e.g., 31.6/62.8 vs. 34.0/62.4 on OpenWebText at 8 steps), while DUO-DCD's greedy sampler produces near-random outputs that fail to preserve input conditions (BLEU-2 drops to ~19–32), making it non-competitive for conditional tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the consistency-model principle from continuous diffusion (Song et al., 2023) to discrete token sequences by replacing the absent Probability Flow ODE with the exact posterior bridge already available in closed form from the discrete diffusion formalism (Austin et al., 2021). It builds directly on MDLM (Sahoo et al., 2024) for the masked-corruption setting and shows that MDLM's NELBO, continuous consistency training, progressive distillation, shortcut models, and recent discrete distillation methods (SDTT, DUO-DCD) all emerge as special cases or empirical approximations of a single Multi-Path Discrete Consistency (MPDC) objective. The key departure from prior discrete acceleration work is that CDLM is single-stage and teacher-free: supervision comes from the analytic bridge rather than from a pretrained teacher's rollout or a continuous-to-discrete projection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CDLM provides a principled, single-stage recipe for few-step discrete generation that matches or beats multi-stage distilled baselines at 110M scale while preserving healthier sample entropy, challenging the assumption that a teacher model is necessary for efficient discrete diffusion. The unifying view (MDLM, consistency, distillation as limits of one objective) gives the field a common language for comparing and combining acceleration techniques. Limitations include the explicit targeting of the few-step (4–8) rather than one-step regime due to the token-factorization barrier, evaluation at 110M parameters on OpenWebText, and the fact that the largest gains are in the low-step regime where absolute PPLs remain higher than AR. Open questions include scaling to billion-parameter models, extending to other discrete domains (biological sequences, graphs, code), and whether the JSD-based training-from-scratch stability advantage persists at larger scale.
