## Problem

Most existing LLM unlearning methods require access to the original training corpus for both target unlearning data and distribution-matched retention data, which is often unavailable in deployed settings due to privacy, licensing, or governance constraints. This creates a fundamental tension: effective unlearning demands corpus-scale supervision, yet re-exposing sensitive data during unlearning introduces additional privacy risk. The paper addresses original-corpus-free selective unlearning—removing target knowledge using only minimal synthetic data while preserving non-target utility.

## Input / Output

The method takes a target anchor string (e.g., an entity name), a small set of safe reference prompts (as few as 10) that elicit refusal/uncertain behavior, and generates ~20–30 synthetic prompts by inserting the anchor into diverse contextual templates (biographical, instructional, comparative, etc.). It produces modified LLM weights that yield refusal/uncertain-style responses on target prompts. Uniquely, the intervention is localized: alignment losses are applied only within a short token window (±8 tokens) around each anchor occurrence in the prompt, while all other positions are stabilized via teacher distillation.

## Method

GU operates on prompt-conditioned hidden states rather than output-level behavior. It first distills a low-rank safe-behavior subspace (rank-k PCA, default k=4) from hidden states of safe reference prompts at selected late layers. It then fine-tunes the model so that hidden states in a local window around anchor tokens are aligned to this subspace via two complementary losses: a centroid-pull loss (L2 distance to the safe-reference mean) and a fold-back confinement loss that penalizes normalized out-of-subspace energy by reflecting the mean-shifted state across the safe subspace and minimizing cosine dissimilarity. A frozen teacher model provides KL-divergence stabilization on non-window tokens of target prompts and on a separate synthetic retain pool (confusable and unrelated names) to limit collateral drift. The full objective combines the core geometric alignment and retain-side stabilization with equal weight, requiring no access to the original pretraining corpus.

## Results

On the ToFU benchmark (Forget-10), GU achieves the best model utility (M.U.) among all non-collapsing methods: 0.577 on LLaMA3.2-1B (only −0.021 drop) and 0.598 on LLaMA2-7B (−0.025 drop), while simultaneously attaining the lowest Forget ROUGE (F.R.) of 0.06 and 0.058 respectively, indicating strong target-answer suppression. On LLaMA2-7B, GU's PrivLeak score of −1.5 is closest to the ideal value of 0, indicating near-chance membership-inference distinguishability. Crucially, GU achieves these results without access to the original training corpus, requiring only ~30 anchor-conditioned synthetic prompts per target entity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GU wins on the critical utility-preservation axis: it achieves M.U. of 0.577 (1B) and 0.598 (7B), outperforming GradDiff (0.436/0.617), NPO (0.418/0.540), SimNPO (0.423/0.553), and UN-DIAL (0.560/0.518), while GA and RMU collapse utility to near zero (0.000 and 0.030). On target suppression (F.R.), GU achieves 0.060 (1B) and 0.058 (7B), substantially lower than GradDiff (0.347/0.005), NPO (0.248/0.500), SimNPO (0.398/0.432), UN-DIAL (0.295/0.276), and RMU (0.358/0.097), though GA achieves lower F.R. at the cost of total utility collapse. On privacy (PrivLeak closest to 0), GU's −1.50 on LLaMA2-7B is the best among all methods, far superior to UN-DIAL (−94.99) and NPO (−90.64). In the matched source-free comparison (Table 4), where all baselines are given the same synthetic data, GU still achieves the best E.S.–M.U. trade-off (E.S. 0.172, M.U. 0.577), confirming that the geometric objective—not just synthetic data construction—drives the gains. Against LUNAR (Shen et al., 2025) under the same synthetic-data protocol, GU achieves lower E.S. (0.114 vs. 0.174) and substantially higher M.U. (0.598 vs. 0.312) on LLaMA2-7B.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant gradient-based and preference-optimization unlearning pipelines (GA, GradDiff, NPO, SimNPO) that require access to the original training corpus, and from representation-level methods (RMU, LUNAR) that still depend on target-specific training data. It repurposes two well-established ideas—low-rank linear subspaces for behavioral attributes (Hernandez & Andreas, 2021; Park et al., 2024) and activation steering (Turner et al., 2023; Arditi et al., 2024)—into a source-free unlearning objective that operates on prompt-conditioned hidden states via a centroid-pull and fold-back confinement loss. The evaluation is grounded in the OpenUnlearning framework, and the method is positioned as a complement to, rather than a replacement for, output-level refusal tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical deployment gap: most unlearning methods re-expose sensitive training data, creating a compliance and privacy risk that the source-free constraint eliminates. The matched synthetic-data comparison (Table 4) cleanly isolates the geometric objective as the critical differentiator, showing that synthetic data construction alone is insufficient. Key limitations include dependence on reliable training-time anchors (aliases and misspellings degrade performance substantially), no certification of complete representational erasure under white-box audits, and potential under-coverage of rare or multilingual query contexts. Open questions include whether the approach extends to diffuse topic-level unlearning (beyond entity anchors) and whether stronger relearning resistance can be achieved without sacrificing utility.
