## Problem

Large Reasoning Models suffer from 'Cognitive Drift'—deviating from Standard Operating Procedures during complex, multi-step specialized tasks—because existing retrieval-augmented methods deliver SOPs as passive, static context in the prompt, which lacks enforceability over long reasoning trajectories. The paper identifies a fundamental integration gap: retrieved procedural guidance competes with other tokens for attention and is easily ignored or hallucinated past by the model, rather than being structurally enforced at each reasoning step.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a natural-language user query and a pre-compiled Expert Procedure Graph (EPG)—a directed acyclic graph whose nodes are tuples of an action directive (text instruction) and a knowledge container (static annotation or RAG-retrievable evidence), and whose edges encode conditional routing logic. The EPG uses a 'Chain-of-Trees' topology: a sequential chain of analytical stages, each containing a decision sub-tree for branching. Output is a segmented reasoning chain (with explicit <analysis> boundary tags at each procedural step) followed by a final answer; the reasoning chain is physically interrupted and resumed at each step boundary rather than generated in one continuous pass.

## Method

TG-RAG introduces two coupled components. First, the Expert Procedure Graph (EPG) formalizes unstructured SOPs into a structured DAG with a Chain-of-Trees topology (sequential macro-stages containing conditional micro-sub-trees), decoupling invariant procedural logic (edges and action directives) from mutable domain knowledge (knowledge containers that can hot-swap via external RAG). Second, the Interrupt-Retrieve-Generate (IRG) mechanism physically pauses generation at each </analysis> tag using inference-engine sequence interruption (e.g., vLLM), performs state-dependent semantic routing to select the next EPG node, and applies Dual-Pathway Injection: the action directive is appended directly into the active reasoning stream (r' = [r, g_v]) as a hard procedural constraint, while the knowledge container is injected into the input context (x' = [x ⊕ C_v]) as informational grounding. This transforms guidance from a passive prompt-level suggestion into an active, step-by-step state transition that reanchors the reasoning trajectory at every decision juncture, with an Open Branch fallback for out-of-distribution cases.

## Results

On specialized expert-driven domains, TG-RAG achieves the highest accuracy across all three backbone models. With Qwen3-32B on the Finance task, TG-RAG reaches 76.4% accuracy, surpassing the strongest RAT baseline RATT (71.2%) by 5.2 points and Self-RAG (64.1%) by 12.3 points. On R1-Distill-14B Medical, TG-RAG attains 90.4% versus IR-CoT's 86.4% and Naive RAG's 82.4%. On general benchmarks, TG-RAG remains competitive: it achieves 99.1% on GSM8K (Qwen3-32B) and 96.3% on StrategyQA (Qwen3-32B), though Few-CoT slightly edges it on R1-Distill-32B GSM8K (97.2% vs. 96.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TG-RAG consistently outperforms all seven baselines on the three specialized-domain tasks (Finance, Medical, Astronomy) across all three backbone models. Against the strongest non-retrieval baseline CoT-SC, TG-RAG gains 2.3–10.0 points (e.g., 81.4% vs. 73.4% on R1-Distill-32B Finance; 83.2% vs. 71.2% on R1-Distill-14B Astronomy), though CoT-SC narrowly edges TG-RAG on Qwen3-32B Astronomy (94.4% vs. 93.2%, within overlapping confidence intervals). Among RAG baselines, TG-RAG surpasses Self-RAG by 5.2–12.3 points and Naive RAG by 4.1–14.0 points on specialized tasks. Against RAT methods, TG-RAG leads RATT by 2.3–5.2 points and IR-CoT by 3.3–5.2 points on specialized domains, with the largest margins in Finance (e.g., 76.4% vs. 71.2% RATT on Qwen3-32B). On general benchmarks, TG-RAG wins on StrategyQA across all models (up to +2.3 points over RATT) and on GSM8K with Qwen3-32B (99.1%) and R1-Distill-14B (91.3%), but Few-CoT slightly outperforms it on R1-Distill-32B GSM8K (97.2% vs. 96.1%), suggesting the EPG's procedural structure offers less advantage for simpler mathematical tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

TG-RAG extends the Retrieval-Augmented Thought (RAT) line of work (IR-CoT, RATT) and the Buffer of Thoughts (BoT) paradigm by shifting retrieval from a passive, prompt-level context augmentation to an active, inference-engine-level intervention. The key conceptual departure is the Interrupt-Retrieve-Generate mechanism, which physically pauses generation at structural boundaries and injects step-specific directives directly into the reasoning stream, rather than relying on the model's instruction-following to holistically execute a retrieved plan. The Expert Procedure Graph formalizes SOPs into a Chain-of-Trees topology, drawing on decision-tree structure for conditional branching, and the Dual-Pathway Injection strategy separates procedural control signals (injected into the reasoning sequence) from factual knowledge (injected into the input context), a design not present in prior RAG or RAT methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical gap in safety-critical domains (finance, medicine) where even minor procedural deviations can have severe consequences, and it does so in a training-free manner compatible across diverse LRMs. The semi-automated EPG construction pipeline (~30 minutes of expert verification per domain) and the hot-swappable Knowledge Container design make the system maintainable as domain knowledge evolves. Open questions include how the approach scales to SOPs with very deep or highly interconnected branching, whether the Open Branch fallback (<1% activation) remains sufficient under distributional shift, and whether the latency overhead (mitigated by lightweight routing) is acceptable in real-time clinical or financial settings. The authors explicitly note this is a research exploration requiring human oversight, not a deployment-ready solution.
