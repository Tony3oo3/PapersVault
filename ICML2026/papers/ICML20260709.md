## Problem

Privacy amplification by iteration (PABI) yields convergent DP bounds for first-order methods like DP-SGD, but the standard shifted-divergence analysis breaks for zeroth-order methods because their noise is anisotropic (scalar Gaussian along a random direction) rather than isotropic, and the global Lipschitz property no longer holds almost surely. Prior DP zeroth-order work (DPZero, Tang et al.) relies solely on the composition theorem, giving privacy cost that grows linearly with the number of training steps T. This paper establishes the first convergent hidden-state DP bound for zeroth-order optimization and reveals that the update dimension K improves privacy under hidden-state analysis—a benefit invisible to composition-based accounting.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes only forward-pass loss evaluations ℓ_i(w) on a training dataset D = {x_i}_{i=1}^n (no gradient computation), along with model weights w_t ∈ R^d constrained to an ℓ2 ball of radius R. At each step it samples K orthonormal directions from the Stiefel manifold V_K(R^d) and evaluates the loss at w_t ± ξu_{t,k} for each direction. The output is the final iterate w_T accompanied by a (ε, δ)-DP guarantee where ε is independent of T (convergent) for smooth strongly convex losses, expressed as O(√(Δ² log(1/δ)/(n²σ²) · min(T, Rn√d/(KΔ)))).

## Method

The paper proposes Noisy-ZOGD, a hybrid noise mechanism that injects both directional Gaussian noise (scalar G_{t,k}^{(1)} along K orthonormal update directions u_{t,k}) and isotropic Gaussian noise (G_t^{(2)} over all d coordinates), with a tunable mixing parameter β_t controlling the split. The key technical contribution is a novel coupling analysis that constructs an auxiliary process W̃_T 'in the middle' of two adjacent training processes W_T and W'_T, decomposing the privacy proof into a TV distance bound (capturing the probability δ_f that a high-probability Lipschitz event fails, controlled via beta-distribution concentration) and a Rényi divergence bound on W̃_T vs. W'_T (from the shifted Gaussian mechanism). This bypasses the shifted-divergence framework entirely, which is inapplicable because the zeroth-order update map is only generalized Lipschitz with high probability rather than globally. The analysis further shows that using orthonormal directions (rather than i.i.d. random directions) is essential for tight concentration, and that increasing K reduces privacy loss under fixed utility constraints—enabling a new privacy-computational-complexity tradeoff for DP zeroth-order optimization.

## Results

The paper establishes the first convergent hidden-state DP bound for zeroth-order optimization. For smooth strongly convex losses, the privacy cost is O(√(Δ²log(1/δ)/(n²σ²) · min(T, Rn√d/(KΔ)))), which saturates as T→∞, in contrast to the standard composition bound O(√(Δ²Tlog(1/δ)/(n²σ²))) that grows linearly with T. A key finding is that under hidden-state analysis, the privacy loss decays with the update dimension K (inversely proportional in the convergent regime), whereas standard composition shows no privacy benefit from K>1 when β_t=0. The general (non-convex) case yields a numerical privacy accountant via a constrained optimization over τ, β_t, and a_t.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its hidden-state analysis against three baselines: (1) standard composition (Theorem 3.1), which gives O(√(Δ²Tlog(1/δ)/(n²σ²))) and grows unboundedly with T; (2) output perturbation, which gives O(√(R²log(1/δ)/σ²)) independent of T but is generally weaker for practical T; and (3) prior DP zeroth-order methods (DPZero by Zhang et al. 2024a, Tang et al. 2024) that rely solely on composition. The proposed method strictly dominates composition in the convergent regime (T > Rn√d/(KΔ)), where the privacy cost plateaus while composition continues to grow. Additionally, the paper reveals that using K>1 orthonormal update directions improves privacy under hidden-state analysis—a benefit invisible to composition-based accounting, where K>1 with isotropic noise (β_t=0) actually worsens the bound by a factor of d/K. The hybrid noise mechanism (β_t∈(0,1)) further improves the privacy-utility tradeoff compared to the pure directional (β_t=1) or pure isotropic (β_t=0) choices used in prior work.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Privacy Amplification by Iteration (PABI) framework—well-developed for first-order methods like DP-SGD (Feldman et al., 2018; Altschuler & Talwar, 2022; Chien & Li, 2025)—to zeroth-order optimization, where the anisotropic (scalar-along-direction) noise structure breaks the standard shifted-Rényi-divergence analysis. It builds on the DP zeroth-order algorithms of Zhang et al. (2024a, DPZero) and Tang et al. (2024), which rely on composition-based privacy accounting, by introducing a hybrid noise mechanism and a novel auxiliary-process coupling that sidesteps the global Lipschitz barrier entirely. The Wasserstein distance tracking (Lemma 3.7) and coupling construction follow the hidden-state analysis paradigm of Chien & Li (2025) and Wei et al. (2024), but the specific construction is tailored to the zeroth-order update geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first convergent (T-independent) DP guarantee for zeroth-order optimization under the hidden-state model, revealing that the update dimension K actually *reduces* privacy loss under fixed utility constraints—a benefit invisible to standard composition analysis. The finding that orthonormal (Stiefel-manifold) directions improve privacy over i.i.d. directions provides a concrete, previously unrecognized design principle for DP zeroth-order algorithms. However, the results require smoothness (and for closed-form bounds, strong convexity) assumptions, and the authors explicitly note a theory-practice gap: the bounds are not yet directly applicable to LLM fine-tuning, and it remains unclear what parameter regimes yield ε ≤ 1 with reasonable utility. Open questions include extending to non-smooth losses, shuffled cyclic minibatches, and bridging the gap to practical LLM training.
