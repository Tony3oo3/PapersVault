## Problem

Existing conformal KGQA methods suffer from two critical failures: hop-level calibration introduces sequential dependencies that violate the exchangeability assumption required for valid coverage guarantees, and weak local-similarity-based scoring yields excessively large prediction sets that lack practical utility. No prior method simultaneously provides statistically valid coverage and compact, discriminative answer sets for multi-hop knowledge graph reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262774/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a natural language query and a knowledge graph, CPR first uses an LLM (Qwen3-8B, zero-temperature) to generate up to 4 candidate relation chains as soft hints, then performs bounded tree expansion (TreeG) over the KG to retrieve candidate reasoning paths. The output is a set-valued answer prediction — the terminal entities of all retrieved paths whose RCVNet scores fall below a conformal threshold — accompanied by a finite-sample guarantee that the set contains at least one correct answer with probability ≥ 1−α at a user-specified risk level α.

## Method

CPR combines two key innovations. First, a Residual Conformal Value Network (RCVNet) learns discriminative path-level nonconformity scores: during training, PUCT-guided exploration collects positive-negative path pairs (correct paths vs. hop-wise deviations) and accumulates Beta-Bernoulli relation priors, which together with semantic similarities form a 3-dim scalar input; RCVNet is a 3-layer residual MLP with FiLM conditioning on concatenated query/relation/path embeddings, trained via a Softplus pairwise ranking loss. Second, Query-Exchangeable Conformal Prediction (QE-CP) performs calibration at the query level rather than the hop level: the nonconformity score S(q) is the minimum RCVNet score among paths terminating at correct answers, and because individual queries are i.i.d. and S(q) is a deterministic function of each query, exchangeability is preserved, yielding a valid split-conformal threshold. At inference, TreeG retrieves candidate paths using hint-augmented RCVNet scores, and the prediction set comprises all paths scoring below the calibrated threshold, guaranteeing P(answer set ∩ ground truth ≠ ∅) ≥ 1−α.

## Results

On WebQSP at α=0.5, CPR achieves 61.4% ECR with APSS 7.14 and Coverage Efficiency 8.60%, outperforming UaG (51.8% ECR, APSS 14.29) by ~10 points in coverage while halving prediction set size. On the more challenging CWQ dataset, CPR is the only method that satisfies coverage guarantees at α≤0.5, achieving 58.8% ECR with APSS 11.40 at α=0.5, whereas UaG, CLM, and LoFreeCP all fail. On PQ and PQL (full-KG reasoning without subgraph extraction), CPR achieves 55.1% ECR (APSS 2.95) and 53.1% ECR (APSS 2.65) respectively at α=0.5, consistently exceeding baselines in Coverage Efficiency across all risk levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CPR consistently outperforms all three baselines across every dataset and risk level where comparisons are valid. Against UaG (Ni et al., 2025), the most direct KGQA conformal baseline, CPR improves ECR by 9.6 points on WebQSP (61.4% vs 51.8% at α=0.5) while reducing APSS by 50% (7.14 vs 14.29); on CWQ, UaG fails to meet coverage for α≤0.5 entirely, whereas CPR achieves 58.8% ECR at α=0.5. Against CLM (Quach et al., 2024) and LoFreeCP (Su et al., 2024), both LLM-based conformal methods, CPR achieves substantially higher ECR (e.g., 61.4% vs 51.0%/50.1% on WebQSP) and smaller prediction sets, while CLM and LoFreeCP fail coverage guarantees on CWQ for α≤0.6 and on PQ/PQL for α≤0.6. The gap is most dramatic on CWQ at α=0.6, where CPR's Coverage Efficiency of 6.01% exceeds UaG's 0.35% by over 15×, reflecting UaG's prediction set explosion (APSS 120.20 vs CPR's 8.35). On PQ and PQL, CPR's advantage over UaG is narrower in ECR (55.1% vs 54.6%; 53.1% vs 52.1%) but consistent in Coverage Efficiency, confirming that path-level calibration and RCVNet scoring yield tighter, more reliable prediction sets even in dense full-KG settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends split conformal prediction (Shafer & Vovk, 2008) to multi-hop KGQA by shifting the calibration unit from the hop level (as in UaG, Ni et al. 2025) to the query level, arguing that hop-level sequential dependencies fundamentally violate the exchangeability assumption underlying conformal guarantees. For discriminative scoring, it repurposes PUCT (Silver et al., 2018) not as an inference-time search strategy but as an offline trajectory collector that generates positive-negative path pairs for training a lightweight residual network (RCVNet). The approach departs from prior conformal KG methods (CF-GNN, UnKGCP) that operate on atomic node-level assertions, and from LLM conformal baselines (CLM, LoFreeCP) that treat reasoning as a black box, by defining nonconformity scores over complete reasoning paths while preserving query-level exchangeability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine theoretical gap: prior conformal KGQA methods (notably UaG) provide coverage guarantees that are formally violated by their own hop-level calibration, undermining their reliability in high-stakes settings. The clean exchangeability argument and the practical result (45% ECR gain, 52% APSS reduction) make this a meaningful step toward trustworthy multi-hop reasoning. Limitations include dependence on KG quality, the relatively small ablation gain from PUCT (0.4% ECR on WebQSP), and the fact that LLM-generated hints, while helpful for compactness, are not essential for validity. Open questions include whether the approach extends to dynamic or uncertain KGs, how it behaves under distribution shift between calibration and test queries, and whether the path-level calibration unit generalizes to other structured reasoning tasks beyond KGQA.
