## Problem

Existing cross-modal remote sensing translation methods treat each modality pair as an independent task, requiring O(N²) direction-specific models that fragment supervision and fail to generalize to unseen modality combinations. This is compounded by the scarcity of large-scale paired data spanning multiple heterogeneous sensors, leaving the community without both the data infrastructure and the modeling abstraction needed for scalable any-to-any translation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260664/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a single remote sensing image in any of five modalities (RGB 256×256×3, SAR 256×256×1, NIR 256×256×1, PAN 512×512×1, MS 128×128×6) and produces the corresponding image in any target modality. All modalities are first projected into a shared 4×64×64 latent space by modality-specific VAEs, and the final output is generated via a single-pass feed-forward trajectory (backbone prediction → adapter correction → decoder), avoiding iterative denoising at inference.

## Method

Any2Any decouples modality-specific representation learning from shared semantic mapping in a three-stage pipeline: (i) N independent VAEs project heterogeneous sensor observations into a geometrically aligned latent manifold Z; (ii) a shared Diffusion Transformer (DiT) backbone performs x₀-prediction—directly regressing the clean target latent from the concatenation of noisy target and source latents—conditioned via AdaLN on timestep and source/target modality embeddings; (iii) lightweight zero-initialized residual adapters, one per target modality, correct systematic distribution mismatches between the backbone output and the target decoder's effective manifold. The key insight is the Latent Anchor mechanism: paired supervision from RST-1M collapses the conditional target distribution to a Dirac delta, transforming intractable cross-modal generation into stable supervised regression. Training uses a stop-gradient operator to isolate adapter optimization from the backbone, and inference reduces to a single O(1) forward pass regardless of the modality pair.

## Results

Any2Any-L achieves state-of-the-art PSNR and RMSE across all 14 translation tasks on RST-1M. For example, on SAR→RGB it reaches 25.20 PSNR / 16.85 RMSE (vs. BBDM 19.50 / 31.02 and Pix2PixHD 18.48 / 35.81), and on MS→RGB it attains 33.22 PSNR / 6.45 RMSE (vs. BBDM 26.39 / 12.76). On SSIM, Any2Any-L leads in most directions, though BBDM retains a slight edge on two tasks (SAR→RGB: 0.66 vs. 0.62; MS→RGB: 0.91 vs. 0.81) due to its smoother-texture diffusion prior. The model also produces semantically reasonable zero-shot translations for six unseen modality pairs (e.g., SAR↔PAN, PAN↔MS, NIR↔PAN) that had no paired training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Any2Any-L dominates all five baselines on PSNR and RMSE across every one of the 14 translation directions. The largest margins appear on SAR→RGB (25.20 vs. 19.50 PSNR over BBDM, a 5.7 dB gap) and MS→RGB (33.22 vs. 26.39, a 6.8 dB gap), where the unified latent-space formulation and x₀-prediction re-parameterization provide the most benefit. On SSIM, Any2Any-L wins in 12 of 14 tasks; BBDM retains a narrow SSIM edge on SAR→RGB (0.66 vs. 0.62) and MS→RGB (0.91 vs. 0.81), which the authors attribute to BBDM's Brownian-bridge prior favoring smoother textures at the expense of radiometric accuracy. Pix2Pix and ControlNet are consistently the weakest baselines (often below 15 PSNR), while Pix2PixHD and LBM occupy the middle range. Notably, each baseline was trained as 14 separate one-way models, whereas Any2Any uses a single unified network, reducing model count from O(N²) to O(1) while still outperforming the per-pair specialists.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant pairwise paradigm in remote sensing cross-modal translation (SAR→optical GANs, conditional diffusion models like RLIDM and S3OIL) by formulating a unified Any-to-Any task where a single shared DiT backbone handles all modality pairs simultaneously. Conceptually, it repurposes the latent diffusion architecture (modality-specific VAEs + shared denoising backbone) from text-to-image generation, replacing text conditioning with AdaLN-based modality embeddings and adding lightweight residual adapters for per-target manifold calibration. The x0-prediction reparameterization, borrowed from the diffusion literature, is motivated here by the structural instability of ε-prediction under extreme cross-sensor physical discrepancies. The RST-1M dataset itself is constructed by aggregating five existing public datasets (SEN1-2, SEN12MS, CACo, SpaceNet-3/5) using RGB as a pivot modality to ensure global connectivity, rather than acquiring new co-registered multi-sensor data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reduction from O(N²) direction-specific translators to a single O(1) unified model is practically significant for multi-sensor Earth observation systems where sensor diversity is growing. RST-1M, as the first million-scale multi-modal remote sensing benchmark with seven connected modality pairs, fills a critical data gap that previously prevented systematic evaluation of cross-modal generalization. However, the zero-shot generalization to unseen modality pairs (SAR-PAN, PAN-MS, NIR-PAN) is demonstrated only qualitatively without quantitative metrics, leaving open whether the semantic transfer is truly robust or merely plausible. Future work should address whether the framework extends to genuinely unpaired settings, temporal consistency across multi-temporal observations, and scalability beyond five modalities to hyperspectral or LiDAR sensors.
