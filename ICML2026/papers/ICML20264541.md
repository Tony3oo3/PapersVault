## Problem

Existing uncertainty quantification methods for LLM hallucination detection are either computationally expensive (sampling-based approaches incur 400–800% overhead), require costly task-specific supervision (confidence regressors), or underperform on long-form generation (simple probability/entropy scores). There is a lack of a single UQ method that is simultaneously unsupervised, efficient, and robust across diverse generation tasks and model architectures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264541/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a white-box LLM's greedy generation of a text prompt, the method consumes the per-token attention weights to the immediately preceding token (a_{i,i-1}^{l,h}) from a fixed subset of middle layers and the token-level conditional probabilities (or entropy for instruction-tuned models). It outputs a single scalar sequence-level uncertainty score computed in one forward pass, which can be thresholded to reject the most uncertain predictions for selective generation. No additional sampling, auxiliary models, or labeled data are required.

## Method

RAUQ identifies 'uncertainty-aware' attention heads per layer by selecting the head with the highest average attention weight to the preceding token across the generation, exploiting the empirical observation that these heads show a systematic attention drop at hallucinated positions. Token-level confidence is then computed recurrently as c_l(y_i) = α·s_i + (1−α)·a_{i,i-1}^{l,h_l}·c_l(y_{i-1}), where s_i is the token probability (or entropy), propagating uncertainty from prior generation steps to capture conditional dependencies. Token scores are aggregated via the mean of log-confidence (length-normalized), and layer-wise uncertainty scores are combined by taking the maximum across a fixed range of middle layers. The entire pipeline requires only one hyperparameter α (0.2 for base models, 0.9 for instruction-tuned models) and adds less than 1% inference overhead.

## Results

RAUQ achieves a mean Prediction Rejection Ratio (PRR) of 0.384 across 12 datasets and 4 base LLMs, outperforming the second-best method (Perplexity, 0.357) by 0.027 PRR on average. The advantage is most pronounced on QA (e.g., 0.396 vs. 0.347 for Llama-3.1 8B) and MT (e.g., 0.473 vs. 0.422 for Gemma-2 9B), where RAUQ leads by 0.05+ PRR over the next-best baseline. Critically, RAUQ adds only 0.3% inference overhead (1.17 s per instance) compared to 400–800% for sampling-based methods like Semantic Entropy and SAR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAUQ (mean PRR 0.384) outperforms all 15 unsupervised baselines on average. The closest competitor is Perplexity (0.357), which RAUQ surpasses by 0.027 PRR; the gap widens to 0.051 on Gemma-2 9B MT (0.473 vs. 0.422 for Simple Focus) and 0.049 on Llama-3.1 8B QA (0.396 vs. 0.347). Sampling-based methods—Semantic Entropy (0.240), SAR (0.294), and DegMat (0.241)—trail RAUQ by 0.09–0.14 PRR while incurring 400–800% inference overhead versus RAUQ's 0.3%. Attention-based baselines perform poorly: Attention Score (Sriramanan et al., 2024) scores only 0.106, and Focus (Zhang et al., 2023) reaches 0.317, both substantially below RAUQ. Against supervised methods (Factoscope, SAPLMA), RAUQ slightly underperforms on their in-domain data but greatly outperforms them on average in out-of-domain settings, confirming its generalization advantage. The paper further demonstrates that RAUQ's components (head selection, excluding prompt tokens) improve the Attention Score method by 0.075 PRR when integrated, validating the transferability of the design choices.

## Relation to Prior Work

RAUQ builds on the recurrent uncertainty propagation framework introduced by Zhang et al. (2023, Focus) and Vazhentsev et al. (2025a, TAD), which established that confidence at generation step i must account for conditional dependencies from prior steps. It departs from prior attention-based UQ methods in two key ways: rather than using attention as a secondary signal (Zhang et al., 2023; Lin et al., 2024a) or averaging indiscriminately across all heads (Sriramanan et al., 2024), RAUQ identifies a small subset of 'uncertainty-aware' heads per layer via their high average attention to the immediately preceding token, and uses only that single head's signal. Unlike supervised approaches (SAT Probe, Lookback Lens, TAD) that require task-specific annotations, RAUQ is fully unsupervised and plug-and-play, and unlike sampling-based methods (Semantic Entropy, SAR, DegMat) it operates in a single forward pass.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The <1% computational overhead and absence of any task-specific tuning make RAUQ one of the first UQ methods practical for real-time deployment in production LLM pipelines, addressing a key gap where prior SOTA methods required 400–800% overhead from repeated sampling. The consistent SOTA performance across nine LLMs of varying sizes (360M to 70B) and twelve datasets spanning QA, summarization, and translation demonstrates strong generalization. However, the method is restricted to white-box LLMs with accessible attention weights, and the authors acknowledge open questions around extension to black-box models, calibration in deployment settings, and applicability to error types beyond factual hallucinations.
