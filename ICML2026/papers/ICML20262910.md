## Problem

Current KV cache compression evaluations focus almost exclusively on retrieval-oriented long-context tasks (e.g., Needle-in-a-Haystack), masking severe degradation in High-Density Reasoning where nearly every token in a Chain-of-Thought few-shot prompt is reasoning-critical. No systematic benchmark exists to assess compression impact across fundamental capabilities such as arithmetic reasoning, world knowledge, commonsense, code generation, and safety, and existing token-level pruning methods fragment few-shot examples into incoherent pieces, causing catastrophic reasoning failure under aggressive budgets.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a structured text prompt containing n few-shot examples (shots) and the per-layer KV cache vectors (k_i^l, v_i^l ∈ R^d for each token i at layer l). It produces a compressed KV cache with two structurally distinct components: a prefill portion that retains complete shot examples as indivisible units (compressed once at the start, then frozen), and a decoding portion that is dynamically re-pruned at every generation step. Compression is applied independently per transformer layer with separate budgets r_p (prefill) and r_d (decoding), and the final cache is the per-layer union of the two compressed portions.

## Method

ShotKV explicitly separates prefill and decoding compression to address their distinct sensitivities identified by KVFundaBench. In the prefill phase, it computes a shot-level importance score (Eq. 3) by averaging accumulated attention across all heads within each few-shot example, then selects top-K shots as atomic units to preserve (Eqs. 4–6), ensuring the semantic coherence of reasoning chains is maintained. In the decoding phase, it applies dynamic token-level pruning (Eqs. 7–8) using attention scores from the current generation step to retain the most influential context tokens as the cache grows. The key differentiator from prior token-level methods (H2O, SnapKV) is treating shots as indivisible semantic units rather than scoring individual tokens, which the benchmark analysis shows is essential for preserving Chain-of-Thought structure. The approach is intentionally lightweight—no new scoring primitive is introduced—and its contribution is validating that this targeted combination of shot-aware prefill preservation and dynamic decoding compression recovers the reasoning performance lost by token-level methods under aggressive budgets.

## Results

On LG-GSM8K (long-context generation, ~4k output tokens), ShotKV achieves 47.33% accuracy at 40% compression, surpassing the FullKV baseline (46.00%) and outperforming StreamingLLM (39.50%), H2O (32.66%), and PyramidInfer (38.33%). On many-shot arithmetic reasoning (50-shot GSM8K, >4K prompt), ShotKV maintains 80.37% accuracy at an aggressive 10% compression ratio, compared to FullKV's 82.35% and H2O's 51.27%. ShotKV also delivers an 11.3% end-to-end latency reduction (162.78s vs 183.42s) at 8192-input/4096-output sequence length, with a 13.1% throughput gain.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ShotKV consistently outperforms all six baselines on many-shot arithmetic reasoning at aggressive compression: at 10% ratio it achieves 80.37% vs. H2O (51.27%, +29.1 pts), SnapKV (68.27%, +12.1 pts), PyramidKV (70.37%, +10.0 pts), StreamingLLM (74.32%, +6.1 pts), and ChunkKV (79.32%, +1.1 pts). On LG-GSM8K at 40% compression, ShotKV (47.33%) beats StreamingLLM (39.50%, +7.8 pts), PyramidInfer (38.33%, +9.0 pts), and H2O (32.66%, +14.7 pts), and even exceeds the uncompressed FullKV baseline (46.00%). The gap over the Random Shot baseline (51.34% at 10%) confirms that attention-based shot selection, rather than arbitrary retention, drives the gains. On the non-ICL HotpotQA generalization test at 10% compression, ShotKV (43.60%) is competitive with PyramidKV (43.80%) and SnapKV (43.36%) but trails FullKV (45.55%) by ~2 pts, indicating the method transfers to document QA via sentence-as-shot adaptation with a small residual cost. In terms of efficiency, ShotKV achieves a 7.2% latency reduction at 4096/4096 and 11.3% at 8192/4096, comparable to ChunkKV (8.6%) and SnapKV (6.9%) at the shorter setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

ShotKV is explicitly positioned as a lightweight, task-motivated combination of two prior design choices rather than a new algorithmic primitive: shot-aware semantic-unit preservation in prefill (sharpening ChunkKV's chunk-level retention with explicit few-shot boundaries) and prefill/decoding phase separation (adopted from SCOPE). The broader contribution is KVFundaBench, which departs from the dominant retrieval-oriented evaluation paradigm (NIAH, LongBench, RULER) by systematically probing fundamental capabilities—arithmetic reasoning, commonsense, code generation, safety, and world knowledge—under matched compression budgets. The paper's conceptual departure is the 'High-Density Reasoning' framing, arguing that token-level compression methods (H2O, SnapKV, StreamingLLM) fragment CoT chains in ways invisible to retrieval benchmarks, and that semantic-unit integrity is the operative variable rather than fine-grained token selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark fills a genuine evaluation gap: practitioners deploying compressed KV caches for reasoning-heavy workloads (math, code, safety-critical generation) have had no systematic way to detect the sharp degradation that retrieval benchmarks miss. The finding that instruction-tuned models are paradoxically *more* vulnerable to aggressive compression than base or reasoning-tuned models has practical implications for which model variants to deploy under memory constraints. Key limitations include dependence on open-weight access to the KV cache, heuristic (non-principled) attention-based scoring, and prompt-structure dependence that limits applicability to fully zero-shot or unstructured long-document settings. Open questions include combining ShotKV with KV quantization, extending to proprietary API models, and whether the semantic-unit principle generalizes beyond few-shot exemplars to other structured prompt components.
