## Problem

Existing RL frameworks for LLM reasoning rely on the policy to independently explore long reasoning chains under sparse, outcome-based verifiable rewards, leading to sample-inefficient optimization and unstable training dynamics. While test-time scaling methods (self-consistency, Tree-of-Thoughts, Mixture-of-Agents) improve solution quality at inference, they do not enhance the underlying policy capacity and incur substantial overhead. The paper asks whether external expert guidance can be integrated as a learnable, on-demand action during RL training that densifies the reward signal and is ultimately internalized so the model reasons autonomously at evaluation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text problem statement (e.g., a competition math question) as input and produces a multi-step reasoning trajectory ending in a final answer extracted by a predefined rule. During training, the policy's action space is augmented with a structured consult-expert action: the model emits JSON-formatted agent_calls specifying expert IDs and query strings, and receives agent_returns containing expert reasoning and answers, which are appended to the trajectory history. At test time, the consult action is disabled entirely, and the model outputs a single reasoning chain with a final answer in the format 'Answer: xxx', scored by F1 against ground truth.

## Method

EAPO augments the policy's action space with a dedicated consult-expert action, optimized jointly with standard reasoning tokens under a single end-to-end verifiable reward (F1 score). Up to K parallel expert replicas (e.g., three QwQ-32B instances) respond to structured queries dispatched by the policy, and three rollout paradigms emerge naturally: self-resolution for easy problems, direct multi-expert consultation for hard ones, and task decomposition with targeted sub-queries for complex ones. A curriculum-like annealing schedule (ρ_s = s^{-1}) progressively reduces the probability that expert responses are written into the trajectory history, forcing the policy to internalize consulted reasoning strategies and reducing consultation frequency over training. The episode turn budget is simultaneously shrunk toward the evaluation budget, further promoting independence. This design densifies the reward signal early (by increasing the proportion of partially correct trajectories) while guaranteeing that the final policy requires no external assistance at test time.

## Results

On the three mathematical reasoning benchmarks (AIME 2024, AIME 2025, AIMO 2025), EAPO achieves an average Pass@32 of 64.07, outperforming the strongest baseline (Self-Exploratory RL at 59.16) by 4.91 points and the base model (42.53) by 21.54 points. EAPO also attains the lowest average variance (0.0643) among all online RL methods, indicating more stable reasoning. On non-mathematical benchmarks (HumanEval, HLE, GPQA, MMLU, EvalPlus, HotpotQA, SimpleQA), EAPO trained exclusively on math data achieves an average of 49.25, surpassing Self-Exploratory RL (47.15) and the base model (41.59).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EAPO outperforms all five baselines across all three math benchmarks. Against the DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B base model (zero-shot), EAPO gains 21.54 points in average Pass@32 (64.07 vs. 42.53). Compared to LeaP (Luo et al., 2025), a collaborative inference framework, EAPO improves by 16.99 points on average, with the largest gap on AIMO 2025 (64.17 vs. 40.31). Full-parameter and LoRA distillation baselines trail EAPO by 28.76 and 20.83 points respectively, confirming that static SFT cannot match the dynamic exploration enabled by RL with expert consultation. The closest competitor is Self-Exploratory RL (DAPO), which EAPO surpasses by 4.91 points on average (64.07 vs. 59.16), with the most pronounced gain on AIMO 2025 (64.17 vs. 55.22, a 8.95-point improvement). EAPO also achieves lower variance than Self-Exploratory RL (0.0643 vs. 0.0727), indicating more consistent reasoning across sampled trajectories.

## Relation to Prior Work

EAPO sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) RL with verifiable rewards for LLM reasoning (DAPO, GRPO-style outcome supervision), (2) expert-guided distillation and teacher-student RL where a stronger model provides supervision, and (3) hierarchical RL with manager-executor decompositions. The key departure from prior expert-guided methods is that expert consultation is formulated as a learnable action within the policy's own action space, optimized end-to-end under the same RL objective, and annealed to zero via a curriculum schedule (ρ_s = s^{-1}). Unlike test-time scaling approaches (self-consistency, Tree-of-Thoughts, LeaP) that improve inference without changing the model, EAPO internalizes expert knowledge into the policy weights so that the model reasons independently at evaluation time. The paper also contrasts with offline distillation (full-parameter or LoRA SFT from expert trajectories), showing that on-policy, adaptive consultation outperforms static trajectory regression.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~5-point average gain over self-exploratory RL on AIME/AIMO benchmarks, combined with consistent cross-domain transfer to code, science, and QA tasks from math-only training, suggests that the internalized reasoning behaviors (summarization, judgment, multi-perspective integration) are broadly useful rather than domain-specific. The ablation showing that temporal allocation of expert access matters more than total consultation budget (annealed schedule beats budget-matched fixed rate by 4.4 points) is a practically useful design principle for curriculum-based RL. Open questions include whether the approach scales to much larger policy models (only 7B/14B tested), whether the benefit persists when the expert is weaker than the policy, and how EAPO interacts with other recent RL techniques like process-reward modeling or multi-turn RL. The homogeneous-expert finding (no gain when expert = policy) also raises the question of whether self-play variants could substitute for external experts.
