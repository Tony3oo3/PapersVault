## Problem

High-quality training problems remain a key bottleneck for scaling LLM reasoning: human-curated datasets are costly and limited, while existing synthetic corpora are often too easy or narrow in domain coverage. Prior rationale-based synthesis (PromptCoT) improved difficulty but relied on hand-crafted instructions and was confined to mathematics, leaving no principled, domain-agnostic, and learnable framework for scalable prompt generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266065/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of foundational concepts (e.g., number-theory properties, algorithmic patterns) extracted from seed problems as its conditioning input, and produces synthetic prompts paired with automatically verifiable signals—boxed final answers for mathematics (via majority voting over 8 generations) or 3–4 unit tests for programming (generated by Qwen3-32B). The full pipeline outputs concept-rationale-prompt triples, where rationales serve as latent explanatory bridges that are not part of the final training data but drive the quality of the generated prompts.

## Method

PROMPTSCALE formulates prompt synthesis as a latent-variable model p(x|c) = Σ_z p(x|z,c) p(z|c) and optimizes it via an EM loop. In the E-step, a rationale generation model q_φ(z|c,x) is updated by reinforcement learning with reward R(c,x,z) = log p_θ(x|z,c) + log p_θ(z|c) (the joint log-likelihood), sampling eight candidate rationales and fine-tuning on the highest-reward one. In the M-step, the prompt generation model p_θ(z,x|c) is trained to maximize the expected joint log-likelihood under the current posterior, using rationales decoded from q_φ. Both models are initialized from Qwen2.5-32B-Base via cold-start annotations from four teacher models on ~15K seed problems, then alternately refined until convergence. The resulting prompts support two post-training regimes: self-play via DPO with binary verifiable rewards for strong models, and SFT on teacher-distilled reasoning traces for weaker models.

## Results

Under the self-play setting with Qwen3-30B-A3B-Thinking-2507, PROMPTSCALE achieves 92.1 (AIME 24), 89.8 (AIME 25), 76.7 (HMMT Feb 25), 74.2 (LiveCodeBench v5), 71.0 (LiveCodeBench v6), and 2079 Elo (Codeforces), setting new state-of-the-art at the 30B scale across all six benchmarks. In the SFT setting, training Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct solely on PROMPTSCALE-synthesized prompts (4.77M examples) yields 73.1 (AIME 24), 65.6 (AIME 25), 46.5 (HMMT Feb 25), 53.4 (LiveCodeBench v5), 48.9 (LiveCodeBench v6), and 1815 Elo (Codeforces), surpassing all human-curated and hybrid baselines. The method also demonstrates strong data efficiency, using only ~90% of math prompts and ~10% of code prompts compared to OpenMathReasoning and OpenCodeReasoning in the 4B self-play setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PROMPTSCALE outperforms all seven baselines across all six benchmarks in both self-play and SFT settings. Against the closest competitor PromptCoT (the direct predecessor), PROMPTSCALE gains +5.3 on HMMT Feb 25 (76.7 vs. 72.0), +6.4 on LiveCodeBench v5 (74.2 vs. 69.7), +3.2 on LiveCodeBench v6 (71.0 vs. 67.8), and +28 Elo on Codeforces (2079 vs. 2051) in the 30B self-play setting. Among the open-source corpora, OpenMathReasoning is the strongest math baseline (87.9 AIME 24) but is far weaker on code (65.7 LCB v5), while OpenCodeReasoning leads on code (70.8 LCB v5) but trails on math; PROMPTSCALE dominates both. In the SFT setting, PROMPTSCALE (100% synthetic prompts) beats OpenMathReasoning (human, 4.92M) by +7.7 on AIME 25 (65.6 vs. 58.1) and +39.2 on LCB v5 (53.4 vs. 9.7), and beats OpenThoughts3 (hybrid, 1.20M) by +13.3 on AIME 24 (73.1 vs. 59.8) and +6.4 on LCB v5 (53.4 vs. 47.0). The base models (Qwen3-30B-A3B and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) show consistent but modest pre-training performance, confirming that the gains come from the synthesized training data rather than the base architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends PromptCoT (Zhao et al., 2025a), which introduced the concept-rationale-prompt paradigm for synthesizing olympiad-level math problems, by replacing its hand-crafted instruction heuristics with a principled EM optimization loop that jointly refines rationale generation and prompt construction. The key conceptual departure is making the pipeline fully learnable and domain-agnostic (covering both mathematics and programming), rather than relying on domain-specific prompt engineering confined to a single field. In the broader landscape of task synthesis for LLM reasoning, PROMPTSCALE positions itself as a scalable alternative to the many heuristic or single-pass generation pipelines (Self-Instruct, Evol-Instruct, key-point-driven synthesis) that have dominated prior work, and it introduces a self-play post-training regime that decouples model improvement from the availability of ever-stronger teacher models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that 100% synthetic prompts (4.77M examples) can match or exceed human-curated and hybrid datasets across six challenging benchmarks is a strong signal that scalable prompt synthesis can replace costly manual curation as the primary data source for reasoning LLMs. The self-play regime is particularly impactful because it removes the dependency on proprietary or prohibitively expensive teacher models, enabling autonomous improvement through verifiable feedback alone. Open questions include whether the EM framework generalizes to agentic or multi-step tool-use tasks beyond math and code, whether the rationale quality plateaus at scale, and whether the approach can be extended to domains where automatic verification is less straightforward. The released corpus and open-source pipeline also provide a practical foundation for the community to build upon.
