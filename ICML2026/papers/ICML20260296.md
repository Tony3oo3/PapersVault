## Problem

Existing diffusion-based samplers that use least-squares matching objectives face critical trade-offs: Adjoint Sampling (AS) requires memoryless priors (e.g., Dirac delta), Adjoint Schrödinger Bridge Sampler (ASBS) relies on an alternating optimization between the drift and a terminal potential that is empirically unstable, and importance-sampling-based methods deteriorate in high dimensions. The paper addresses the gap of a single, scalable, and stable training objective that accommodates arbitrary prior distributions and reference processes without alternating schemes or importance weights.

## Input / Output

The method requires only pointwise evaluation of the unnormalized target density ρ_target (and its gradient) plus a tractable prior distribution such as a Gaussian—no samples from the target are needed. The output is a neural network parameterizing a Markovian drift field u(x,t) that defines a stochastic transport map: integrating the SDE dX_t = σ(t)u(X_t,t)dt + σ(t)dB_t from the prior yields samples from the target. The algorithm operates on continuous d-dimensional data and scales to d=2500 for synthetic targets and d=165 for molecular benchmarks.

## Method

BMS formulates diffusion-based sampling as a generalized fixed-point iteration grounded in Nelson's relation, unifying AS and ASBS as special cases. The key theoretical contribution is a generalized target score identity (Proposition 2.6) that expresses the marginal score as a convex combination of coupling scores, which become tractable when the coupling factorizes as p_prior ⊗ p_target (independent coupling). Each iteration alternates between: (1) simulating the forward SDE with the current drift, (2) resampling prior endpoints to form an independent coupling, (3) sampling intermediate states from a Brownian bridge conditioned on those endpoints, and (4) regressing the Markovian drift on the path-dependent target ξ via a least-squares objective. A damped variant (u_{i+1} = αΦ(u_i) + (1−α)u_i) regularizes updates in L², reducing variance and mitigating mode collapse, which is critical for stability in high-dimensional and multimodal settings.

## Results

On n-body particle systems, BMS achieves the best Wasserstein-2 distance across all dimensions: 0.38±0.01 on DW-4 (d=8), 1.54±0.00 on LJ-13 (d=39), and 3.80±0.01 on LJ-55 (d=165), with the largest margin over the next-best method (PDNS) appearing at d=165 where BMS achieves E(·)W2=2.36±0.45 versus PDNS's 21.97±3.14. On molecular benchmarks, damped BMS (η=10) on Alanine Dipeptide (d=66) achieves D_JS=0.068±0.009, RMSE_ΔF=0.079±0.000, and TICA-W2=0.900±0.066, all state-of-the-art. On Alanine Tetrapeptide (d=126), damped BMS (η=50) achieves D_JS=0.228±0.011 and RMSE_ΔF=0.396±0.012, while undamped BMS and ASBS diverge during training. On high-dimensional GMMs (up to d=2500), BMS maintains stable training and mode coverage where ASBS degrades significantly.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BMS outperforms all eight baselines on every n-body system tested. The most dramatic gains appear at the highest dimension (LJ-55, d=165), where BMS achieves E(·)W2=2.36±0.45 compared to PDNS (21.97±3.14), ASBS (28.10±8.15), and AS (58.04±20.98) — roughly a 9× improvement over the next-best method. On the molecular benchmark Alanine Dipeptide (d=66), damped BMS (η=10) achieves D_JS=0.068 and RMSE_ΔF=0.079, beating damped ASBS (η=10) which scores 0.111 and 0.128 respectively. On Alanine Tetrapeptide (d=126), undamped ASBS and BMS both diverge during training (marked with †), while damped BMS (η=50) achieves D_JS=0.228 and RMSE_ΔF=0.396, slightly outperforming damped ASBS (η=50) at 0.230 and 0.457. The paper also demonstrates that BMS scales to GMMs with d=2500 dimensions while ASBS exhibits clear performance degradation beyond d≈50, highlighting BMS's superior stability in high-dimensional settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies Adjoint Sampling (AS, Havens et al. 2025) and the Adjoint Schrödinger Bridge Sampler (ASBS, Liu et al. 2025) as special cases of a generalized fixed-point iteration grounded in Nelson's relation and a novel generalized target score identity. The key departure is the introduction of independent couplings (Π₀,T* = p_prior ⊗ p_target), which yield a tractable path-dependent drift without requiring the memorylessness condition that restricts AS to deterministic priors, nor the alternating optimization between drift and corrector potential that makes ASBS unstable. The damped fixed-point iteration draws on proximal point and trust region ideas recently applied to diffusion training (Guo et al. 2025a; Blessing et al. 2025a), but is here derived as a variational regularization of the Markovianization step rather than an ad hoc stabilization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BMS enables diffusion-based sampling to scale to dimensions (d=2500 for GMMs, d=165 for LJ-55) that were previously intractable for matching-based samplers, while maintaining mode diversity through its inherent forward-KL (mass-covering) objective. The critical role of damping—without which both ASBS and BMS diverge on molecular benchmarks—suggests that stability, not expressivity, is the binding constraint for high-dimensional sampling. Open questions include convergence guarantees for the fixed-point iteration, adaptive damping schedules, and whether the score-function blindness to isolated modes (Remark 2.12) can be mitigated by incorporating direct density information into the loss.
