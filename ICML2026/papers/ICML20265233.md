## Problem

Monolithic VLA policies optimize a single set of parameters for both coarse relocation and contact-critical manipulation, causing gradient interference where the dominant move-phase signal drowns out the learning of fine-grained operate-phase dynamics. While prior work addresses trajectory heterogeneity through RL credit assignment or temporal scaling, none explicitly disentangles these two behavioral regimes under imitation-based supervision with a structural inductive bias.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265233/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a language instruction, multi-view RGB observations, and a 14-DoF dual-arm proprioceptive state at each control step, producing an action chunk of horizon H with 14 dimensions (7 per arm, including gripper). Actions are z-score normalized and modeled as Gaussian distributions via conditional flow matching; the gripper dimension does not strictly follow this assumption. Phase labels for training are auto-generated by an MLLM (Seed 1.6 Vision) operating on 5 fps video capped at 64 frames, with iterative validation and self-correction to enforce structural constraints.

## Method

Move-Then-Operate introduces a hard-routing dual-expert architecture on top of the π0 VLA backbone: a shared VLM encoder produces dense hidden states, a lightweight MLP router (operating on masked global average-pooled features) predicts a binary phase label (MOVE or OPERATE), and one of two CFM experts with disjoint LoRA parameters generates the action via ODE integration. During training, teacher-forcing with ground-truth phase labels ensures each expert receives gradients only for its own phase, orthogonalizing parameter updates and preventing the move-phase's larger-magnitude actions from dominating the operate-phase's learning signal. The phase annotation pipeline leverages an MLLM with subtask decomposition, velocity cues, and topological constraints (at most 2 phases per subtask, strict chronological ordering), refined through an iterative validator-feedback loop. This structural decoupling yields a +24.1% absolute success-rate improvement over monolithic π0 on RoboTwin2 while matching models trained on 10× more data and converging 40% faster.

## Results

On the RoboTwin2 benchmark (8 tasks, 50 demonstrations each), Move-Then-Operate achieves an average success rate of 68.9%, outperforming the monolithic π0 baseline (44.75%) by +24.1% absolute. The method also reaches peak performance in 40% fewer training steps and rivals baselines trained on 10× more data. Gains are most pronounced on contact-critical tasks: Click Bell improves from 44% (π0) to 99%, and Press Stapler from 62% to 70%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the monolithic π0 baseline (Black et al., 2024), Move-Then-Operate wins on all 8 tasks, with the largest margins on Click Bell (+55 pp) and Place Cans Plasticbox (+45 pp), yielding a +24.1 pp average improvement. Against RDT-1B (Liu et al., ICLR 2025), the method dominates across the board (68.88% vs. 35.63% average), with particularly large gaps on Place Cans Plasticbox (+15 pp) and Press Stapler (+39 pp). In the data-efficiency comparison (Table 2), where π0.5* and GO-1* are trained on 10× more demonstrations, Move-Then-Operate wins on contact-rich tasks (Press Stapler: 93% vs. 80%/66%; Click Bell: 99% vs. 75%/98%) but trails on visually diverse tasks like Place Cans Plasticbox (16% vs. 40%/68%), indicating that architectural specialization compensates for data scarcity in precision manipulation but not in broad visual generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the π0 vision-language-action flow model by replacing its monolithic action head with a dual-expert Conditional Flow Matching architecture, where a lightweight router selects between a Move expert and an Operate expert per action chunk. It departs from prior MoE-for-VLA work (AdaMoE, MoEDP, VER, FedVLA) that routes on task or modality heterogeneity, instead routing on the temporal phase of manipulation grounded in classical motor-control theory (Fitts' law, Woodworth's two-phase model). The MLLM-based auto-labeling pipeline for phase segmentation is a novel data-engineering contribution that has no direct precedent in the VLA literature, where phase annotations are typically absent or manually curated.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a structurally decoupled two-expert policy matches or exceeds models trained on 10× more data on contact-rich tasks suggests that architectural specialization can substitute for data scale in precision manipulation, a practically important finding for labs with limited demonstration budgets. However, all evaluations are on the simulated RoboTwin2 benchmark with 8 tasks, and the paper acknowledges degradation on visually diverse tasks where the data gap re-emerges. Open questions include whether the two-phase decomposition generalizes to tasks with more than two behavioral regimes (e.g., multi-contact assembly), whether the MLLM labeling pipeline transfers to real-robot data without re-tuning, and whether the approach extends beyond dual-arm settings.
