## Problem

Linear stochastic bandits have traditionally optimized regret minimization in isolation, overlooking the equally critical need for accurate parameter estimation (e.g., Average Treatment Effect in clinical trials) and safety constraint satisfaction. No prior work has characterized the fundamental Pareto-optimal frontier among these three competing objectives—regret, statistical inference, and safety—under a unified theoretical framework, leaving practitioners without guidance on the theoretical cost of precision.

## Input / Output

At each time step the algorithm selects one of K arms, each characterized by a d-dimensional feature vector x_a, and observes a scalar reward y_t = ⟨x_{a_t}, θ*⟩ + η_t (σ-subGaussian noise) plus a stochastic cost Z_t(a) with unknown mean c_a. The safety constraint requires the cumulative expected cost E[Σ Z_t(a)π_t(a)] ≤ 0 at all times. The algorithm outputs a time-varying arm-selection policy π_t and, at the end of Phase I, an OLS-based parameter estimate θ̂_stat; the hyperparameter β ∈ [1/2,1) controls the Phase I duration T_1 = ⌊T^β⌋, directly tuning the regret–estimation trade-off.

## Method

SERMiSC is a two-phase algorithm whose design is motivated by the decomposition π = (1−κ)π* + κπ′, where π* is the optimal safe policy and π′ is an exploratory policy ensuring full-rank design. Phase I (Safe Statistical Estimation) uses a hybrid policy π_t(i) = q_t·π′(i) + (1−q_t)·Gibbs(Ĉ_{t−1}(i)) that blends uniform exploration with a cost-aware Gibbs distribution built from inverse-propensity-weighted cost estimates, achieving O(1/√T_1) estimation error with Õ(1) safety violation. Phase II (Safe Regret Minimization) employs a pessimistic-optimistic arm-selection rule a_t = argmax_i [r̂_t(i) − Z_t(i)Q(t)/V_t], where the virtual queue Q(t) tracks cumulative safety violation and is analyzed via Lyapunov drift to yield Õ(√T_2/δ) regret and O(1) safety cost. The Pareto optimality is established by a minimax lower bound derived through a three-step information-theoretic argument: constructing two hard environments with rank-deficient design under π*, reducing the estimation error to a KL-divergence upper bound via Pinsker's inequality, and solving the resulting linear program via Lagrangian duality to obtain Ω(1/√(δ_T + CT^β)), which SERMiSC matches up to logarithmic factors.

## Results

SERMiSC achieves a Pareto-optimal trade-off with cumulative regret Õ(T^β), near-constant safety violation Õ(1), and statistical estimation error O(T^{-β/2}), where β ∈ [1/2, 1) controls the Phase I duration T₁ = ⌊T^β⌋. These bounds match the minimax lower bound of Ω(1/√(δ_T + CT^β)) established in Theorem 3.1 up to logarithmic factors. In simulations (T=1000, K=10, d=8), SERMiSC with T₁=100 achieves regret and safety performance comparable to PO while significantly outperforming it in estimation error, and avoids the divergent safety violations exhibited by OFUL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks SERMiSC (T₁=100) against two baselines: OFUL (Abbasi-Yadkori et al., 2011) and PO (Liu et al., 2021). OFUL, a standard unconstrained linear bandit algorithm, incurs divergent safety violations over the horizon, making it infeasible in safety-critical settings. PO, a pessimistic-optimistic algorithm designed for safety-constrained linear bandits, achieves regret and safety performance comparable to SERMiSC, confirming that both methods handle the safety constraint effectively. However, SERMiSC significantly outperforms PO in statistical estimation error, demonstrating the benefit of its dedicated Phase I exploration for parameter recovery. The key differentiator is that PO was not designed with inference as an objective, so its exploitation-focused arm selection provides insufficient geometric coverage of the parameter space, whereas SERMiSC's two-phase structure explicitly allocates budget to ensure full-rank design matrix coverage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of research: the regret-inference Pareto trade-off (pioneered by Simchi-Levi & Wang, 2025, and extended to non-stationary, network, and high-dimensional settings) and safety-constrained linear bandits (Liu et al., 2021; Ma et al., 2024). The key departure is that prior safety-aware bandit work entirely neglects statistical inference, while prior regret-inference work ignores safety constraints; SERMiSC is the first to address all three objectives simultaneously. Algorithmically, Phase II directly repurposes the pessimistic-optimistic virtual-queue mechanism and Lyapunov drift analysis from Liu et al. (2021), while Phase I introduces a Gibbs-based safety-aware exploration policy with inverse propensity weighting to handle unknown costs during the estimation phase.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first minimax lower bound characterizing the Pareto frontier among regret, estimation error, and safety violation in linear bandits, and shows the proposed two-phase algorithm matches this bound up to logarithmic factors while maintaining near-constant safety risk. This is practically relevant for high-stakes sequential decision-making (clinical trials, algorithmic trading) where parameter estimation is needed for generalization beyond the observed cohort. Key limitations include the linear reward model assumption, the fixed action set, and the two-phase structure that incurs linear regret during Phase I; the authors identify designing a fully adaptive interleaved algorithm and extending to nonlinear or misspecified models as natural next steps.
