## Problem

Existing video anomaly detection methods require annotated target-domain video data for training, which is scarce, expensive to collect, and sometimes unavailable due to privacy policies or domain-specific data gaps. This paper tackles the vision-free VAD setting: can a VAD model be trained entirely without any video examples, relying solely on textual semantics of anomaly events?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265873/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes LLM-generated text descriptions (4 sequential sentences per event, repeated to 16 time steps and zero-padded to 256 dimensions) encoded by a frozen CLIP text encoder. At inference, it takes raw video frames encoded by the frozen CLIP image encoder (ViT-B/16) and produces per-frame anomaly scores in [0,1] along with multi-class anomaly type predictions. The critical design constraint is that the temporal predictor trained on text embeddings must accept video frame embeddings at test time without any fine-tuning, relying entirely on CLIP's shared embedding space for cross-modal transfer.

## Method

The core idea is to treat LLM-generated temporally structured text descriptions as surrogate video sequences for training a VAD model. DeepSeek-V3 generates 4-sentence event timelines (beginning → development → climax → resolution) for each anomaly category, which are encoded by a frozen CLIP text encoder. A novel Event Evolution Causal Attention (EC-Attn) module models temporal dependencies via two complementary components: Event-Focus Causal Attention (EFC-Attn) applies windowed causal self-attention to capture short-range action details, while Event-Context Causal Attention (ECC-Attn) performs global attention with a learnable distance-aware causal bias to model long-range event evolution. A hierarchical anomaly-aware branch combines a binary anomaly head and a multi-class event head, trained with a multiple-instance learning objective using top-k temporal aggregation. At inference, the frozen CLIP image encoder extracts video frame embeddings that directly replace text embeddings in the trained predictor, leveraging CLIP's contrastive alignment to bridge the text-to-video modality gap without any additional training.

## Results

On XD-Violence, TD-VAD achieves 75.83% AP and 89.50% AUC, surpassing the strongest weakly-supervised baseline (MSL, 75.53% AP) and the best unsupervised method (RareAnom, 68.33% AUC) by large margins. On UCF-Crime, it reaches 80.82% AUC, outperforming the best weakly-supervised method (GCL, 79.84%) and the best unsupervised method (DyANNet, 79.76%). Compared to LAVAD (13B params, 1.26 FPS), TD-VAD (0.31B params) improves AP from 62.01 to 75.83 while achieving a ~145× speedup to 183 FPS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TD-VAD outperforms all compared baselines on both datasets without using any target-domain video data for training. On XD-Violence, it exceeds the best weakly-supervised method MSL (75.53% AP) by 0.30% AP and the best unsupervised method RareAnom (68.33% AUC) by 21.17% AUC. On UCF-Crime, it surpasses the strongest weakly-supervised baseline GCL (79.84% AUC) by 0.98% and the best unsupervised method DyANNet (79.76% AUC) by 1.06%. Against the closest language-driven competitor LAVAD (Zanella et al. 2024), TD-VAD improves AP from 62.01 to 75.83 and AUC from 85.36 to 89.50 on XD-Violence, while reducing model size from 13B to 0.31B parameters and increasing inference speed from 1.26 to 183 FPS (~145×). The method also beats the raw CLIP zero-shot baseline by 37.62% AP and 51.29% AUC on XD-Violence, demonstrating that the learned temporal predictor adds substantial value over direct CLIP scoring.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs fundamentally from all prior VAD paradigms (weakly-supervised, one-class, unsupervised) that require target-domain video data for training, and from inference-time LLM/VLM scoring approaches like LAVAD that still process video frames through large models at test time. It repurposes CLIP's frozen cross-modal alignment space as a bridge: LLM-generated temporally structured text descriptions serve as training surrogates for video sequences, and the same predictor is applied to CLIP image embeddings at inference. The Event Evolution Causal Attention module (combining windowed local and global causal attention) is a new architectural contribution for modeling temporal logic in text-as-video sequences, building on standard transformer attention but with a dual-granularity causal design tailored to event evolution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The vision-free training paradigm addresses a genuine practical bottleneck—data privacy constraints, the rarity and cost of annotating anomaly videos, and the inability to collect target-domain footage in sensitive settings. The 145× speedup over LAVAD (183 FPS vs. 1.26 FPS) with fewer parameters (0.31B vs. 13B) makes deployment in real-time surveillance far more feasible. However, open questions remain: cross-dataset generalization is weak (UCF-trained model drops from 89.50 to 59.53 AUC on XD), fine-grained categories with overlapping semantics (Abuse at 61.47, Shooting at 72.68 AUC) remain challenging, and the bounded cross-modal distortion assumption may not hold for novel or out-of-distribution anomaly types not well-represented in the generated text corpus.
