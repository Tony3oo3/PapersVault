## Problem

Existing streaming video benchmarks rely on multiple-choice formats, short clips, or incomplete temporal coverage, failing to capture how real mobile assistants must track continuous audio-visual streams and decide *when* to respond. No prior benchmark unifies on-screen (device tutorials, app recordings) and off-screen (vlogs, egocentric) mobile scenarios with open-ended questions spanning Backward, Instant, and Forward temporal scopes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263015/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a 2 FPS video stream and synchronized audio updated every 1 second, with access limited to a 60-second sliding visual window plus the full text dialogue history. A single text query is injected at a fixed questioning timestamp; at each subsequent 1-second tick the model must output either the exact token 'Silent' or an open-ended natural-language answer. For Forward questions the valid response window is a 2-second interval starting at a ground-truth 'proactive timestamp' (when evidence first appears), while Backward and Instant questions require an answer exactly at the questioning timestamp.

## Method

PhoStream is built via an Automated Generative Pipeline in which Gemini 3 Pro generates timestamped QA candidates from 2 FPS video segments, then an automated checker re-evaluates each pair using only content up to a strict cutoff timestamp (question time for Backward/Instant, proactive time for Forward) to prevent future leakage; 10 human experts perform two rounds of verification on the surviving candidates. Evaluation uses an Online Inference Pipeline that issues each query once and lets the model autonomously decide response timing, scoring Early Response and No Response as 0 and valid in-window answers via an LLM-as-a-Judge rubric (0–5, scaled to 0–100). The key design insight is that this unified single-query protocol exposes a temporal asymmetry: models score above 80 on Instant/Backward tasks but collapse on Forward tasks (e.g., Gemini 3 Pro drops to 16.40), revealing that the dominant failure mode is premature answering rather than perceptual inability.

## Results

On PhoStream (5,572 open-ended QA pairs, 578 videos, avg. 13.3 min), a stark temporal asymmetry emerges: proprietary models score 71–82 on Instant and Backward tasks but collapse to 16–44 on Forward tasks. Doubao-Seed-1.6 achieves the best Overall score (56.01) and best Forward score (44.26) with the lowest Early Response rate (29.76%), while Gemini 3 Pro leads on Instant (80.83) and Backward (82.19) yet scores only 16.40 on Forward with a 79.12% ER rate. Open-source omni models (Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B) show near-perfect Instant/Backward scores (~77) but a catastrophic 97.89% ER rate on Forward, yielding a Forward score of just 1.26. Purpose-built streaming models (VideoLLM-online, Dispider, MMDuet2) trail all general MLLMs, with Overall scores of 12.34, 23.50, and 4.96 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among proprietary models, Doubao-Seed-1.6 dominates the Forward task (44.26 vs. Gemini 3 Pro's 16.40 and Doubao-Seed-1.8's 33.38) with the lowest ER rate (29.76%) and highest PC rate (56.85%), making it the only model that reliably waits for evidence before answering. Gemini 3 Pro leads on Instant (80.83) and Backward (82.19) but its 79.12% ER rate on Forward questions reveals severe impatience, dropping its Overall to 48.70. Among open-source omni models, Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B matches Gemini 3 Pro on Instant/Backward (~77) yet achieves a Forward score of only 1.26 with a 97.89% ER rate—the worst of all models—indicating it almost never waits. Purpose-built streaming models underperform all general MLLMs: VideoLLM-online-8B (Overall 12.34) produces continuous narration rather than deferred answers (99.54% ER), MMDuet2 (Overall 4.96) fails to trigger responses at all (59.21% NR), and Dispider (Overall 23.50) sits in between. The audio ablation (Table 4) further shows that adding audio improves Instant/Backward by +3 to +9 points but slightly worsens Forward scores, suggesting audio cues encourage premature confidence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of streaming video QA benchmarks (StreamingBench, OVO-Bench, OmniMMI, ProactiveVideoQA) by introducing the first mobile-centric formulation that unifies on-screen (device tutorials, app recordings) and off-screen (vlogs, egocentric) scenarios under a single open-ended QA protocol. It departs from prior work in three key ways: replacing multiple-choice with open-ended responses judged by LLM-as-a-Judge, emphasizing Forward (proactive) questions as ~50% of the dataset rather than treating them as a minor subset, and using substantially longer videos (13.3 min average vs. 2.1–9.7 min in prior benchmarks) to stress long-horizon context tracking. The Online Inference Pipeline, where the model autonomously decides when to respond within a 1-second update loop, builds on the streaming architectures of VideoLLM-online, Dispider, and MMDuet2 but reframes evaluation around response timing rather than per-frame accuracy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a concrete and actionable failure mode—Early Response bias—that is orthogonal to perception quality: models that score 80+ on Instant/Backward tasks collapse to single digits on Forward tasks because they answer before evidence appears, not because they cannot perceive it. This reframes the open problem for mobile assistants from 'what to say' to 'when to speak,' a distinction that current training objectives (next-token prediction, multi-turn RL for silence) have not adequately addressed. The audio ablation adds a nuanced trade-off: richer multimodal signals improve comprehension but make models more overconfident in responding early, suggesting that modality integration and response gating must be co-designed. Open questions include whether longer training with explicit temporal-reward signals, or architectural changes (e.g., a dedicated 'wait' decision head), can close the Forward gap without degrading Instant/Backward performance.
