## Problem

Formal theorem proving (FTP) in Lean 4 can verify known propositions but cannot constructively find unknown terms satisfying given conditions, while informal LLM reasoning produces answers without process-level verifiability. Existing neuro-symbolic pipelines (e.g., DTV, PutnamBench) only perform post-hoc verification of externally derived answers, leaving the derivation process itself unverified and disconnected from the formal kernel. This paper addresses the gap by unifying answer discovery and proof construction within a single formal environment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262974/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The input is a Lean 4 formalization structured as a quadruple (V, a, Φ, Ψ): independent variables with types, a queried metavariable, a set of hypotheses, and a set of conclusions, written via a custom DSL (problem ... find ... s.t. ... := solve). In FPS, the output is an answer term of type T_a plus a proof term establishing P(a); in D-FPS, the output is an answer proposition A in Prop plus a forward completeness proof (Ψ → A) and a backward soundness proof (A → Ψ). Metaprograms extract the answer and proofs as functions of the free variables, and RPE (a restricted tactic set including norm_num, ring_nf, rfl, aesop, rw_search) checks semantic equivalence between the produced answer and a human-annotated ground truth, rejecting tautological or unsimplified terms.

## Method

FPS formulates problem-solving as constructing an inhabitant of a Π-type wrapping a Σ-type in Lean 4, where the solve tactic initializes two coupled subgoals: a Proof goal (prove the conclusions with the answer instantiated) and an Answer metavariable goal (construct the answer term), guaranteeing soundness by Theorem 3.5. D-FPS shifts the paradigm from search-and-instantiate to derive-and-assign by using propositions (rather than terms) as answers and decoupling the process into a forward phase (derive the answer proposition from hypotheses and conclusions, ensuring completeness) and a backward phase (verify the derived proposition implies the conclusions, ensuring soundness), with Theorems 3.6–3.7 establishing expressiveness, completeness, and soundness for find-all problems. RPE (Restricted Propositional Equivalence) operationalizes human-aligned correctness as provability within a fixed restricted tactic set, achieving 100% precision and 0.9732 Cohen's kappa on xVerify, thereby preventing tautological shortcuts that full propositional equality would permit. Three benchmarks (FormalMath500, MiniF2F-Solving, PutnamBench-Solving; 1,086 problems total) are constructed via a three-phase expert-in-the-loop pipeline with well-posedness and soundness checks, and experiments across FTP models and generalist LLMs reveal a significant 'alignment tax': Solved% is consistently far below Proven%, with 89.7% of typechecked-but-unsolved cases being tautological answers.

## Results

On FPS (Table 1), the best method Goedel-Prover-V2-8B achieves 80.19% Proven% but only 59.17% Solved% on FormalMath500, 86.22% vs. 65.87% on MiniF2F-Solving, and 2.88% vs. 1.03% on PutnamBench-Solving, revealing a consistent 'alignment tax' of 15–21 percentage points between formal validity and human-aligned correctness. On D-FPS (Table 2), Hybrid CoT with DeepSeek-V3.2 reaches 48.58% Solved% on FormalMath500 and 57.33% on MiniF2F-Solving, with Typecheck% nearly matching Solved% (e.g., 50.65% vs. 48.58%), confirming D-FPS's effectiveness in suppressing tautological shortcuts. RPE achieves 100% precision, 97.18% recall, and 0.9732 Cohen's kappa on 300 xVerify examples, validating its alignment with human judgment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Goedel-Prover-V2-8B dominates all three benchmarks on both Typecheck% and Solved%, outperforming the next-best whole-proof method DeepSeek-Prover-V2 (CoT) by 25.5 pp in Solved% on FormalMath500 (59.17% vs. 33.68%) and 43.5 pp on MiniF2F-Solving (65.87% vs. 22.40%). Kimina-Prover is the strongest runner-up on FormalMath500 (30.32% Solved%) but trails Goedel-Prover-V2-8B by ~29 pp. Proof-search methods (InternLM2.5-StepProver, LeanSTaR) achieve moderate Typecheck% (40–44%) but suffer large alignment gaps, yielding only 25–29% Solved%. Prompt-based generalist LLMs (DeepSeek-V3.2, Sonnet-4.5) and DSP+ are substantially weaker, with Solved% below 17% on FormalMath500 and near-zero on PutnamBench-Solving. Notably, several whole-proof models (DeepSeek-Prover-V1.5, Goedel-Prover, STP) show a dramatic Typecheck% collapse relative to Proven% (e.g., Goedel-Prover: 63.02% Proven% but only 0.36% Typecheck% on MiniF2F-Solving), indicating they produce proofs that are formally valid for the ground-truth but fail to construct a valid answer term when the answer is left as a metavariable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends formal theorem proving (FTP) in Lean 4 from verifying known propositions to constructive problem-solving, where the goal is to discover an unknown term satisfying given constraints. It departs from the post-hoc verification paradigm of DTV and PutnamBench (where an LLM informally generates an answer that is then formally checked) by unifying answer derivation and proof construction within a single dependent-type formulation (a Π-type wrapping a Σ-type). The RPE evaluation metric builds on the observation that existing answer-checking methods (exact match, SymPy, relaxed matching) lack expressiveness for complex mathematical objects, replacing them with a restricted formal-verification-based equivalence check.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The quantified 'alignment tax'—that models proving 80%+ of problems can only solve ~59% when required to construct human-aligned answers—provides a concrete diagnostic for why existing FTP models cannot be directly repurposed for problem-solving, motivating dedicated training data and architectures. The three benchmarks (1,000+ problems spanning grade-school to undergraduate level) fill a clear gap between informal math benchmarks (outcome-level only) and formal FTP benchmarks (process-level for proving only). However, the near-zero performance on PutnamBench-Solving (<2% for all methods) and the reliance on prompting for D-FPS (due to undertraining) suggest the field is still far from solving these tasks, and the practical impact is currently limited to the formal-reasoning research community working in Lean 4.
