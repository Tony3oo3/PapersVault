## Problem

Language models are highly sensitive to single-token changes at generation points where the predicted distribution is spread across multiple tokens, yet this sensitivity is typically attributed to exponential divergence from compounding error. The paper asks whether the geometry of these 'blurry' points encodes a structured, semantically meaningful relationship to the model's internal state that is invisible to scalar uncertainty measures like entropy or variance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263329/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes the last hidden activation z_t (restricted to S^{n-1} via RMSNorm), the top two probability-weighted token embeddings (p1·v1, p2·v2), and the residual-stream activation y at a chosen probe layer. It produces an so(n)-valued 1-form (connection), a holonomy operator H computed via a clover-shaped parallel-transport loop, and the oriented uncertainty vector q = Hy − y ≈ hy. The final semantic readout is the absolute cosine similarity (coupling) between q and 737 world vectors extracted by linear probes on chess positions, which carries an intrinsic orientation absent from scalar uncertainty metrics.

## Method

The paper constructs a three-blade A(z_t) = 4 z_t ∧ (p1 v1) ∧ (p2 v2) whose Frobenius norm measures the volume of the parallelepiped spanned by the hidden state and the two competing token embeddings, capturing the extent of blurring. Contracting with a tangent vector μ yields an so(n)-valued 1-form A_μ(z_t) = 4p1p2(−(μ·v1)(z_t∧v2) + (μ·v2)(z_t∧v1)), where the 4p1p2 prefactor acts as a 'probability charge' that vanishes at confident generation points. The holonomy operator H is computed by parallel-transporting y around a clover of four small squares (side length ε) in the tangent plane at z_t, and the curvature tensor h ≈ −ε²(∂_μ A_ν − ∂_ν A_μ − [A_ν, A_μ]) emerges at O(ε²) via Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff. The oriented uncertainty vector q = Hy − y ≈ hy is then tested for semantic coupling to world vectors, with PCA revealing clustering by chess board region (king's side vs. queen's side vs. centre) and coupling strength monotonically tracking piece importance (Spearman ρ = 0.90). Ablations with random SO(n) matrices and simple rotations onto v1 or v2 confirm that the observed structure is specific to the blurring geometry rather than arbitrary rotations.

## Results

The curvature of the blurring geometry (q = Hy − y) couples to chess world vectors with a Spearman's ρ = 0.90 (p = 0.037) correlation between coupling strength and piece importance (pawns < knights < bishops < rooks/queens). Two-dimensional PCA of q vectors reveals semantically structured 'ears' separating king's-side, queen's-side, and centre world vectors. Ablations confirm specificity: a scale-matched random SO(n) matrix captures only 34.8% of variance in two principal directions versus 53.9% for the true holonomy, and simple rotations of z_t onto v₁ or v₂ capture only 31.4%. Results generalise to the Mistral-Small-3.1-24B model (ρ = −0.90, p = 0.037), with the sign reversal in flow direction between models noted as a potentially interpretable architectural difference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external methods but instead ablates its own construction to verify that the observed semantic structure is specific to the blurring geometry. A random SO(n) matrix matched to the Frobenius norm of the true holonomy operators produces no directional PCA structure and captures only 34.8% of dataset variance in two principal directions, compared to 53.9% for the true holonomy. Simpler rotations that align z_t directly onto v₁ or v₂ (the top two token embeddings) perform even worse at 31.4% variance, confirming that it is the tension between competing output vectors—not a single rotation—that drives the structure. Computing Hy directly instead of the curvature q = Hy − y yields no semantically meaningful coupling, consistent with the theoretical expectation that only the curvature (not the holonomy itself) is the physical observable. The Mistral model serves as a held-out generalisability check rather than a baseline, confirming the PCA structure and piece-importance correlation transfer across model lineages.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of LLM interpretability (linear probes for world models, following OthelloGPT and Nanda et al.) and differential geometry (gauge theory, parallel transport, and holonomy from fiber bundle mathematics). It departs from prior scalar uncertainty measures (entropy, variance, semantic uncertainty à la Kuhn et al.) by constructing an oriented, so(n)-valued 1-form from the wedge product of the hidden state with probability-weighted token embeddings, then extracting a curvature tensor via a clover-loop holonomy. The sensitivity of autoregressive models to single-token perturbations is well documented (Bhargava et al., Lu et al., Holtzman et al.), but this paper reframes that sensitivity as a geometric property of the token embedding space rather than a dynamical instability, and shows the resulting curvature is semantically structured.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central claim—that a purely geometric object derived from token embeddings (no learned parameters) couples to the model's internal world model in a way that respects board-region structure and piece importance—suggests that the discrete output geometry of LLMs is not an arbitrary interface but a faithful encoding of internal representational structure. However, the evidence is confined to a single reasoning domain (chess) and two model families, with modest statistical significance (Spearman ρ = 0.90, p = 0.037), and the PCA-based semantic clustering, while visually compelling, lacks a formal hypothesis test. Open questions include whether the framework generalizes to non-chess reasoning tasks, whether the flow-direction difference between Qwen and Mistral reflects a meaningful architectural or training distinction, and whether higher-order holonomy terms (O(ε⁴)) carry additional semantic information.
