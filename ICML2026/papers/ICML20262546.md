## Problem

Existing sentence-level watermarking methods (e.g., SemStamp) rely on prefix-based designs where each sentence's watermark signal is conditioned on its immediately preceding context. Strong paraphrasers like DIPPER and GPT-3.5 frequently induce structural perturbations—sentence splitting and merging—that alter these contexts, causing a cascading loss of watermark signals across multiple sentences and dropping detection rates from ~91% to ~22% TPR@5%.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262546/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During generation, the method takes a text context (prompt plus previously generated sentences) and a global secret bit sequence partitioned into blocks of M bits, producing a watermarked text where each sentence's semantic embedding encodes the corresponding bit block. During detection, the input is a potentially watermarked text of N sentences; the method produces a single watermark score (z-score) computed as the maximum over 2N restructured text variants and variable-length secret bit sequence candidates (ranging from ⌈αN′⌉ to ⌈βN′⌉ blocks), where α=0.5 and β=1.5 by default. The bit signals per sentence are extracted via inner products between the sentence embedding (all-mpnet-base-v2) and M pre-defined orthonormal secret vectors.

## Method

AliMark reformulates sentence-level watermarking as a global bit sequence encoding and alignment problem, decoupling detection from prefix context. During embedding, the LLM generates Q=64 candidate next sentences and selects one whose M=8-bit signal (determined by the sign of inner products with secret orthonormal vectors) matches the current block of a global secret bit sequence, so the entire text encodes a sequential bit pattern. During detection, a Re-Structurer (RS) module generates N−1 re-merge and N re-split variants to partially recover the original sentence structure, and an Adaptive Bit Sequence Alignment (ABSA) module aligns each variant's extracted bit sequence against secret bit sequence candidates of varying lengths using Block Edit Rate (BER)—a block-level adaptation of Levenshtein distance where deletion/insertion costs equal M and substitution cost is the Hamming distance between blocks. The final watermark score is the maximum z-score across all RS candidates and all alignment lengths, making the detection robust to sentence merges, splits, insertions, and deletions that would catastrophically break prefix-based methods.

## Results

Using OPT-1.3B as the backbone on Booksum, AliMark achieves TPR@5% of 66.6% under GPT-3.5 paraphrasing and 61.6% under DIPPER, roughly doubling the best sentence-level baseline PMark (33.0% and 30.4%, respectively). Under weaker paraphrasers (Pegasus, Parrot), AliMark maintains TPR@5% above 95%, while token-level methods (KGW, SynthID, SIR) collapse to near-zero detection. On C4 with the same backbone, AliMark reaches 51.6% TPR@5% under GPT-3.5 versus 28.2% for PMark. Similar trends hold with Qwen3-1.7B (Table 2), where AliMark achieves 53.6% TPR@5% under GPT-3.5 on Booksum.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AliMark outperforms all eight baselines across nearly every setting. Against the strongest sentence-level baseline PMark, AliMark gains approximately 33 percentage points in TPR@5% under GPT-3.5 on Booksum (66.6% vs. 33.0%) and 31 points under DIPPER (61.6% vs. 30.4%), with similar margins on C4 (51.6% vs. 28.2% under GPT-3.5). The other sentence-level baselines—SemStamp, k-SemStamp, and SimMark—degrade more severely under strong paraphrasers, dropping to 16–22% TPR@5% under GPT-3.5 on Booksum. Token-level methods (KGW, EXP, SynthID, SIR) are essentially broken under any paraphrasing attack, with TPR@5% falling below 5% for KGW, SynthID, and SIR under GPT-3.5, confirming that surface-level statistical signals cannot survive semantic rewriting. Under weaker sentence-to-sentence paraphrasers (Pegasus, Parrot), the gap narrows but AliMark still leads by 9–11 points over PMark in TPR@5%.

## Relation to Prior Work

AliMark draws a direct conceptual line from EXP (Kuditipudi et al., TMLR 2024), which reformulated token-level watermark detection as a sequence alignment problem to handle insertions and deletions, and transplants this idea to the sentence level. It also inherits the semantic-embedding-to-discrete-values mechanism from SemStamp (Hou et al., NAACL 2024) but departs fundamentally from the prefix-based conditioning: instead of each sentence's watermark depending on its immediate predecessor, AliMark encodes a global secret bit sequence across all sentences, making detection context-independent. The Block Edit Rate metric is a block-level generalization of Levenshtein distance, and the Re-Structurer module is a novel text-level preprocessing step with no direct analogue in prior watermarking work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides compelling empirical evidence that structural perturbations (sentence splitting and merging) are not edge cases but common artifacts of strong paraphrasers, and that they cause cascading watermark signal loss in all existing prefix-based sentence-level methods. This reframes the robustness problem for sentence-level watermarking from purely semantic to also structural. Key open questions include whether learned or multi-step restructuring can handle more complex perturbations without inflating false positives (the paper shows both alternatives underperform), and whether the approach scales efficiently to very long documents where the O(N²) alignment cost becomes prohibitive. The single-step RS design is a pragmatic but acknowledged limitation that caps achievable robustness.
