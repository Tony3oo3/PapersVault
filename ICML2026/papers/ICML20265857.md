## Problem

Visual Autoregressive (VAR) models incur substantial computational cost from their massive token counts, and existing training-free token reduction methods suffer from three key limitations: heuristic stage partition based on frequency analysis, non-adaptive fixed reduction schedules shared across all instances, and limited acceleration scope restricted to later scales. The paper argues these limitations stem from treating scales and layers as isolated units while neglecting the continuous variation in model predictions, and proposes entropy as a principled measure to capture this continuous modeling dynamics.

## Input / Output

NOVA is a training-free acceleration wrapper applied to existing VAR backbones (Infinity-2B, Infinity-8B, HART-0.7B, VAR-d30, VAR-d36). For text-to-image generation it consumes a text prompt and produces a 1024×1024 RGB image; for class-conditional generation it takes an ImageNet class label and outputs 256×256 or 512×512 images. The method operates entirely at inference time, modifying only which tokens are computed at each scale and layer during the standard coarse-to-fine autoregressive decoding, with no architectural changes or retraining required.

## Method

NOVA performs dual-linkage adaptive token reduction guided by entropy analysis through three components. First, Adaptive Acceleration Activation online identifies the inflection point of scale-level entropy growth (using a moving-averaged growth rate compared against an early-scale baseline) to determine the earliest safe scale for pruning, avoiding both premature quality loss and delayed acceleration. Second, Dual-Linkage Acceleration computes a distinct reduction ratio per scale via a sigmoid-based progressive function refined by the entropy growth rate, then further adjusts the ratio per layer based on the relative entropy deviation of that layer from the mean of all preceding layers, allocating more computation to high-entropy layers. Third, Residual Cache Reuse reconstructs pruned token outputs by interpolating the layer-wise residual (output minus input) from the prior scale, preserving fine-grained structural cues without redundant computation. Entropy is computed directly from the predictive token distributions already produced during decoding, adding only O(N_t·|V|) overhead versus O(N_t²·d) for self-attention.

## Results

On Infinity-2B, NOVA achieves a 2.89× speedup (2.43s → 0.84s) with GenEval Overall of 0.72, matching the unaccelerated baseline (0.73) and outperforming all other acceleration methods. On Infinity-8B, NOVA reduces latency from 1.51s to 0.75s (1.33× speedup) while achieving an ImageReward score of 1.035, surpassing the original Infinity-8B (1.030). On HART-0.7B, NOVA delivers 1.62× speedup with GenEval Overall of 0.51, equal to the unaccelerated baseline. Entropy computation overhead is negligible at 0.9% of total inference latency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NOVA consistently outperforms all compared acceleration methods in speed while matching or exceeding generation quality. On Infinity-2B, NOVA achieves 2.89× speedup versus FastVAR (2.45×), SkipVAR (2.23×), and SparseVAR (1.66×), while maintaining GenEval Overall of 0.72 (matching SparseVAR and SkipVAR, exceeding FastVAR's 0.70). On HART-0.7B, NOVA reaches 1.62× speedup with GenEval 0.51, surpassing FastVAR (1.30×, 0.50) and SparseVAR (1.38×, 0.50). Against ToMe on HART-0.7B, NOVA achieves both higher speedup (1.62× vs. 1.36×) and better GenEval (0.51 vs. 0.46). Notably, on Infinity-8B, NOVA's ImageReward score of 1.035 exceeds the unaccelerated baseline (1.030), while FastVAR (1.028) and SkipVAR (1.032) fall short or match.

## Relation to Prior Work

NOVA builds directly on VAR's next-scale prediction paradigm (Tian et al., 2024) and positions itself as a training-free acceleration layer atop existing VAR backbones (Infinity, HART). It departs from prior token-reduction methods (FastVAR, SparseVAR, SkipVAR) that rely on heuristic frequency-based stage partitioning and fixed, instance-independent reduction schedules, instead using predictive entropy as a principled, instance-adaptive criterion for both when to activate acceleration and how aggressively to prune at each scale and layer. The dual-linkage design (scale-linkage + layer-linkage) and residual cache reuse represent a conceptual shift from treating scales and layers as isolated units toward modeling the continuous uncertainty-reduction dynamics inherent in coarse-to-fine generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NOVA offers a practical, plug-and-play acceleration path for VAR models that achieves near-lossless quality at 2.89× speedup without retraining, which is significant given VAR's growing adoption for high-resolution text-to-image generation. The instance-adaptive activation (entropy inflection point detection) addresses a real limitation of fixed schedules, and the finding that entropy outperforms both attention-weight and MSE-based token selection provides a useful design principle. However, NOVA does not optimize KV cache memory (a major bottleneck at high resolution), preserves early scales entirely, and still involves heuristic threshold choices (α, τ, λ), leaving open whether fully learned or theoretically optimal schedules could further close the gap. Extending entropy-guided adaptive pruning to other autoregressive or diffusion-based generation paradigms remains an open direction.
