## Problem

LLM agents re-derive solutions from scratch even in recurring scenarios because existing memory-augmented methods store passive episodic narratives (trajectories, reflections, graphs) that still require inference-heavy re-reasoning at decision time. The paper tackles three coupled obstacles: converting passive experience into executable procedural units (executability), ensuring those units are reliably reusable with consistent performance gains (reusability), and optimizing them without parameter updates that risk catastrophic forgetting (non-parametric optimization).

## Input / Output

At decision time, the agent receives a natural-language state and a fixed-capacity pool of Skills, where each Skill is a structured tuple of activation condition, ordered execution procedure, and termination condition—all expressed in natural language. The frozen LLM generates a single primitive action conditioned on the state and the active Skill. During the learning phase, the system consumes batches of interaction trajectories (state-action-reward sequences) to extract semantic gradients and evolve the Skill pool, which is the sole learned artifact; no model weights are updated.

## Method

Skill-Pro formalizes agent decision-making as a Skill-MDP where a selection policy picks a Skill from a pool and a frozen LLM executes primitive actions under that Skill's procedure. The core evolution mechanism, Non-Parametric PPO, operates in two stages: (1) Semantic Gradients—per-trajectory hindsight attribution produces structured natural-language refinement directions for each Skill component (activation, execution, termination), which are batch-aggregated and applied via an LLM-driven update to generate candidate Skills; (2) PPO Gate—each candidate is verified by computing importance ratios between the candidate and behavior Skill under the frozen LLM's token-level log-probabilities, then evaluating a clipped surrogate objective to enforce a trust-region constraint, with best-of-Nc selection admitting only candidates with positive PPO Gate scores. A score-based maintenance loop tracks cumulative advantage per Skill online and prunes negative-score or semantically redundant entries to sustain a compact, high-quality pool under fixed capacity K.

## Results

On ALFWorld, Skill-Pro achieves 0.900 success rate on Train and 0.909 on OOD, outperforming the best baseline AWM (0.700/0.900). On Mastermind-v0, Skill-Pro reaches 0.606 average return versus G-Memory's 0.577, and maintains gains on Hard (0.463 vs 0.406) and Extreme (0.333 vs 0.356, a narrow loss). In cross-agent transfer, Skill-Pro leads on all three backbones (Gemma-3-4B: 0.444, Qwen3-32B: 0.615, LLaMA-3.3-70B: 0.647). Critically, Skill-Pro stores only 816 total tokens (vs. 40K–392K for baselines) while achieving 0.925 in-domain reuse rate, compared to 0.02–0.35 for all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Skill-Pro outperforms all seven baselines on ALFWorld Train (0.900 vs. AWM's 0.700, the next best) and leads on ALFWorld OOD (0.909 vs. AWM's 0.900). On Mastermind-v0, Skill-Pro (0.606) beats G-Memory (0.577), ReAct (0.557), and RAG (0.521), with the gap widening on Hard (0.463 vs. G-Memory 0.406). The sole loss is on Mastermind-Extreme, where G-Memory edges ahead (0.356 vs. 0.333), though the margin is small. In cross-agent transfer, Skill-Pro dominates all three backbones, most notably on Qwen3-32B (0.615 vs. AWM 0.592) and LLaMA-3.3-70B (0.647 vs. State 0.613). On reuse rate (Table 1), Skill-Pro's 0.925 in-domain rate dwarfs RAG (0.349), Expel (0.285), and G-Memory (0.091), while its 816-token storage is 50–480× smaller than baselines ranging from 40K (G-Memory) to 392K (AWM) tokens.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes PPO's trust-region principle (Schulman et al., 2017) in a fundamentally non-parametric setting, replacing gradient-based weight updates with LLM-driven Skill refinement guided by semantic gradients. It departs from the dominant memory-augmented agent paradigm (RAG, Expel, A-MEM, AWM, G-Memory), which the authors characterize as episodic memory that forces agents to re-derive solutions from retrieved narratives, by formalizing procedural memory through the Skill-MDP. The semantic gradient mechanism draws on TextGrad's text-as-gradient idea but adapts it from static variable optimization to sequential decision-making with hindsight attribution over trajectories.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 816-token procedural memory outperforming 100K–400K-token episodic memories on both reuse rate and task performance is a compelling efficiency result that suggests procedural abstraction is fundamentally more compact than episodic storage for LLM agents. Cross-agent transfer (skills learned with Gemma-2-9B reused by Qwen3-32B and LLaMA-3.3-70B) hints at a model-agnostic procedural knowledge layer. Key open questions include whether explicit natural-language Skills can scale to more complex, open-ended environments, whether the PPO Gate's log-probability requirement limits practical deployment with opaque APIs, and whether the framework can transition toward implicit procedural representations as the authors themselves note.
