## Problem

Existing sepsis prediction models capture either temporal dynamics (LSTMs, Transformers) or structural patient relationships (GNNs) but cannot integrate both while producing clinically interpretable explanations grounded in disease mechanisms. Clinicians need predictions that connect specific clinical indicators to pathophysiological processes in ways that align with medical reasoning, rather than opaque feature attributions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265583/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 199-dimensional structured EHR features per 24-hour sliding window (12h stride), comprising vital signs with five summary statistics, laboratory values, top-50 ICD-10 diagnosis codes, and 19 ATC medication categories. It produces a patient-level sepsis probability, per-window risk scores, interpretable deterioration attention weights identifying critical windows, and a 100–200 word evidence-conditioned clinical reasoning text generated jointly with the prediction. For sepsis-positive patients, observation is censored at onset time to prevent temporal leakage.

## Method

PathwayLLM is a three-stage architecture. Stage 1 encodes each window through four specialized encoders—a temporal MLP, a trajectory MLP over diagnosis history, a 2-layer R-GCN over a heterogeneous patient-diagnosis-medication graph, and a pathway encoder that modulates a global prior with a 735-edge binary activation vector from a PC-algorithm-discovered dependency DAG—then fuses them via cross-modal attention into a 256-dim representation. Stage 2 injects these representations into a frozen 8B LLM (Qwen3-8B) via four modality-specific soft tokens prepended to a structured clinical prompt and layer-wise injection at eight intermediate depths, with LoRA (rank 16) adapting only Q/V projections (29.9M total trainable parameters); the LLM jointly learns risk classification and explanation generation supervised by Qwen3-Max synthetic reasoning texts. Stage 3 aggregates window embeddings through a causal 2-layer Clinical Trajectory LSTM with Deterioration Attention (combining learned importance scores with temporal position weighting) to produce the final patient-level risk score. Training uses a two-phase strategy: Phase 1 trains Stages 1–2 with window-level labels and language modeling loss, then Phase 2 freezes those stages and trains Stage 3 with patient-level labels.

## Results

On the MIMIC-IV test set (2,312 patients, 8.48% sepsis prevalence), PathwayLLM with Qwen3-8B achieves patient-level AUROC 0.891 (95% CI: 0.872–0.910) and AUPRC 0.724 (95% CI: 0.687–0.761), representing absolute improvements of +0.070 AUROC and +0.196 AUPRC over the strongest baseline (COMPOSER). External validation on eICU (12,847 patients, 143 hospitals) yields AUROC 0.842 zero-shot and 0.867 after light fine-tuning of Stage 3 on 20% of eICU data. At the window level, Stages 1–2 alone achieve AUROC 0.843, and the full CT-LSTM aggregation lifts patient-level performance to 0.891/0.724, confirming gains from both strong window representations and trajectory modeling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PathwayLLM (Qwen) outperforms all 11 baselines on both AUROC and AUPRC. The largest gap is against the strongest baseline COMPOSER (Shashikumar et al., 2021), where PathwayLLM gains +0.070 AUROC (0.891 vs 0.821) and +0.196 AUPRC (0.724 vs 0.528), a 37% relative AUPRC improvement that is particularly meaningful given the 8.45% class imbalance. Among time-series baselines, DuETT (0.818/0.519), Raindrop (0.812/0.502), and mTAND (0.801/0.478) all trail PathwayLLM by 7–9 AUROC points, while Transformer (0.794/0.458) and TCN (0.781/0.423) lag further behind. EHR-specialized pretrained models (CEHR-BERT 0.805, Med-BERT 0.802, NYUTron 0.814) and the graph-based GAT (0.786) and GRASP (0.793) baselines are all surpassed by 7–9 AUROC points. On eICU zero-shot transfer, PathwayLLM (0.842) also exceeds COMPOSER (0.756) by 8.6 AUROC points, confirming that the multi-modal encoding and LLM integration generalize across institutions and coding systems.

## Relation to Prior Work

PathwayLLM sits at the intersection of three established threads in clinical ML: time-series sequence modeling (LSTM/Transformer), heterogeneous graph encoding of EHR (R-GCN over patient-diagnosis-medication networks), and LLM-based clinical reasoning (NYUTron, GRASP, KARE). Its conceptual departure is the explicit integration of all three into a single three-stage pipeline, with the addition of a fourth modality—statistical dependency pathways discovered via the PC algorithm—that provides mechanistic, structure-grounded signals absent from purely correlational GNN or time-series approaches. The strongest prior baseline, COMPOSER, addresses sepsis prediction with conformal abstention but lacks graph-structured population knowledge and LLM-generated explanations; PathwayLLM positions itself as addressing the interpretability gap that purely discriminative models leave open.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 37% relative AUPRC gain over COMPOSER and the clinician-validated explanation quality (4.2/5 coherence, 89% risk-factor accuracy) make this one of the more clinically credible sepsis-prediction systems reported to date, particularly for the suspected-infection risk-stratification setting. However, several open questions limit near-term deployment: no prospective multi-site validation, no fairness analysis across demographic subgroups, inability to distinguish infectious from sterile SIRS (a 11% over-attribution failure mode), and reliance on synthetic reasoning supervision bounded by the teacher model's accuracy. The framework also excludes unstructured data (clinical notes, imaging), and the 6% hallucinated-specificity rate in generated explanations raises trust concerns for real clinical use.
