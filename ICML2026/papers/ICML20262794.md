## Problem

Equation Learner (EQL), a gradient-based symbolic regression method, frequently activates redundant operators as network complexity grows, producing over-complex expressions and failing to recover exact ground-truth formulas. The paper identifies a specific mechanism: operators that do not vanish at zero (e.g., exp(0)=1) induce non-zero gradients on paths whose connection weights are already zero, preventing the ideal sparse expression from acting as a stable training attractor and biasing optimization toward unnecessarily complex structures.

## Input / Output

The method consumes tabular data points (x, y) with x ∈ R^d, sampled over specified ranges with Gaussian noise at σ ∈ {0.1, 0.01, 0.001}. It outputs a compact symbolic mathematical expression (e.g., polynomial, trigonometric, or mixed form) along with numerical predictions ŷ. The recovered expression is algebraically simplified after extraction to absorb offset terms introduced by zero-point transformations, and exact recovery is judged by algebraic equivalence rather than syntactic match.

## Method

EQL-Z combines three complementary mechanisms. First, a zero-point constraint enforces Λ(0)=0 for all symbolic operators via output shifts (e.g., exp(x)→exp(x)−1, cos(x)→cos(x)−1) or input shifts (e.g., log(x)→log(x+1)), which provably eliminates residual gradients on silent paths so that zero-weight, zero-input connections remain stationary. Second, a small-to-large structure search grows the network from a compact seed through local width/depth edits, accepting each expansion only when a complexity-penalized validation score (R²_val minus a normalized operator-count penalty) improves, with stage-gated operator families and family-adaptive sampling to focus search on effective operator subspaces. Third, after the final architecture is selected, BFGS quasi-Newton optimization refines the continuous coefficients of the extracted analytic expression, acting as a last-mile numerical correction that tightens R² without altering the symbolic form.

## Results

On the 12 standard Nguyen benchmarks, EQL-Z achieves a 98.3% average exact equation recovery rate (100% on 11/12 functions, 80% on Nguyen-5), compared to 0% for vanilla EQL under the same 20-run protocol. On real-world scientific tasks (Oscillation 1/2, E. coli growth, Stress-Strain), EQL-Z reduces ID/OOD NMSE by 1–3 orders of magnitude over vanilla EQL (e.g., Oscillation 1 ID NMSE: 0.0032 vs. 2.5078). On SRBench black-box datasets, EQL-Z reduces average expression complexity by over 80% relative to EQL while maintaining competitive R² and tolerance-based accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EQL-Z decisively outperforms vanilla EQL across all evaluation settings: 98.3% vs. 0% exact recovery on Nguyen benchmarks, and 1–3 orders of magnitude lower ID/OOD NMSE on real-world tasks (e.g., Stress-Strain OOD: 0.1597 vs. 1.0349). Against the strongest non-LLM symbolic regression baselines, EQL-Z leads ParFam (88.8%), SPL (93.5%), and NGGP (90.3%) on average Nguyen recovery, with the largest margin over GP (49.3%). On the four real-world scientific tasks, EQL-Z is competitive with DSR, E2E, and PySR, often matching or exceeding them (e.g., Oscillation 2 OOD NMSE 0.0036 vs. DSR's 0.1945), though it trails LLM-SR (Mixtral/GPT-3.5) which leverages large language model priors for equation discovery. On SRBench black-box datasets under a 10-minute budget, EQL-Z achieves comparable R² to advanced GP-based methods while producing expressions over 80% shorter in complexity than EQL.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Equation Learner (EQL) paradigm (Martius & Lampert, 2016; Sahoo et al., 2018) by identifying a specific gradient-level failure mode—residual gradients on silent operator paths caused by non-zero operator responses at the origin—that prior EQL variants (iEQL, OccamNet, CONSOLE, DYSYMNET) addressed only indirectly through connection sparsity or pruning. EQL-Z departs from this lineage by intervening at the operator-definition level (zero-point consistent transformations) and at the architecture-search level (small-to-large growth), rather than relying solely on regularization to suppress redundant terms. The BFGS fine-tuning step borrows from classical numerical optimization but is applied to the extracted analytic expression rather than the full network, distinguishing it from standard EQL training loops.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-point constraint is a simple, generalizable design principle for any differentiable symbolic operator library, and its identification explains why EQL-family models systematically fail at exact recovery despite sufficient representational capacity. However, EQL-Z still lags behind LLM-SR methods on real-world scientific tasks, and the small-to-large search introduces non-trivial computational overhead (10 min to 6 hours per task) with heuristic stage-unlocking rules that limit scalability to larger operator libraries or higher-dimensional inputs. Open questions include whether the zero-point principle extends cleanly to all singular operators (e.g., division, higher-order rational functions) and whether combining EQL-Z's continuous optimization with LLM-based structural priors could close the remaining gap to LLM-SR.
