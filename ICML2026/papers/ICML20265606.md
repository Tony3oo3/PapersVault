## Problem

Existing dynamics-learning methods (Neural ODEs, continuous normalizing flows) parameterize vector fields in flat ambient space, ignoring the intrinsic curvature of the data manifold. This geometric agnosticism produces two failure modes: smoothing drift, where conflicting local velocities average into off-manifold directions, and rollout drift, where discrete integration steps push trajectories off the manifold surface. The paper asks whether a unified, differentiable geometric constraint can simultaneously prevent both failures across diverse domains without requiring a priori knowledge of the manifold.

## Input / Output

For ODE discovery, the method consumes sparse, non-uniformly sampled state vectors (e.g., 2D positions on a circle with angular gaps of 120°–240°) and outputs a sparse closed-form vector field recovered via post-hoc SINDy regression on the learned tangent velocities. For single-cell genomics, it takes unspliced/spliced mRNA count matrices (top 2000 highly variable genes, PCA-reduced to 30 dims) and produces per-cell velocity vectors constrained to a kinetic subspace. For video detection, it ingests 10-frame clips projected into a frozen Stable Diffusion VAE latent space and outputs a pixel-wise normalized second-order tangent-projection residual used as a detection score, requiring no task-specific training of the geometric module itself.

## Method

GFG employs a dual-stream architecture that disentangles static state identity from dynamic evolution. The State Stream is a VQ-VAE whose decoder G_θ defines the learned data manifold; the Dynamics Stream encodes evolution trends into a weighted combination of L discrete velocity primitives from a learnable codebook, preventing the model from regressing to an off-manifold mean. The key mechanism is Neural Tangent Projection: rather than training a separate unconstrained velocity decoder, the method computes the Jacobian-Vector Product (JVP) of the state decoder along the latent velocity direction, yielding a tangent velocity v_tan = J_θ(z_s) z_v that lies in the tangent space by construction, eliminating off-manifold drift without explicit coordinate charts. Training combines a standard VQ-VAE reconstruction loss with a domain-specific physics consistency term (latent-space ODE rollout for dynamics discovery, nonnegative-ridge kinetic projection residual for RNA velocity, or second-order tangent-flow residual for video), jointly optimizing the manifold geometry and the dynamics. The theoretical analysis (Theorem 3.2) shows that tangent projection strictly reduces estimation error when the true dynamics are tangential, and that the projection residual provides a principled manifold-alignment signal during training.

## Results

On the ODE discovery benchmark (2D circular motion with sparse non-uniform sampling), GFG achieves a one-step rollout MSE of 0.0003 and a valid prediction time (VPT) of 26.1, compared to MSE 1.29–38.35 and VPT 3.8 for all baselines, and is the only method that recovers the correct equation coefficients. On four scRNA-seq datasets, GFG dominates all three geometric metrics (VeloCoh, ICCoh, CBDir), e.g., on Mouse Brain it reaches VeloCoh 0.864, ICCoh 0.736, and CBDir 0.657 versus the best baselines of 0.765, 0.504, and 0.515. For AI-generated video detection, GFG alone attains 93.4% AUROC, and when fused with NSG-VD the combined system reaches 96.3% AUROC and 93.2% Recall across ten unseen generators.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In ODE discovery, GFG outperforms SINDy (MSE 1.29, VPT 3.8), IRK-SINDy (MSE 1.29, VPT 3.8), MNN (MSE 4.85), and S-MNN (MSE 38.35) by orders of magnitude, achieving MSE 0.0003 and VPT 26.1 while being the only method to recover the correct equation coefficients. In RNA velocity inference, GFG beats all five baselines (scVelo, veloVI, DeepVelo, TIvelo, Graphvelo) on every metric across all four datasets; the largest gains appear on Mouse Brain (CBDir 0.657 vs. 0.515 for TIvelo) and Retina (VeloCoh 0.998 vs. 0.922 for scVelo), while Graphvelo remains the closest competitor on Erythroid Lineage (CBDir 0.569 vs. 0.746). For AI-generated video detection, GFG alone (AUROC 93.4, Recall 73.6) underperforms NSG-VD (AUROC 96.1, Recall 88.0) but substantially outperforms STIL (AUROC 93.5, Recall 27.0), NPR (AUROC 93.0, Recall 57.4), TALL (AUROC 95.7, Recall 60.8), and DeMamba (AUROC 93.9, Recall 72.0); the GFG+NSG-VD fusion achieves the best overall AUROC of 96.3 and Recall of 93.2, confirming GFG as a complementary geometric signal rather than a standalone replacement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate lines of work—sparse ODE discovery (SINDy, MNN), single-cell RNA velocity inference (scVelo, DeepVelo, Graphvelo), and AI-generated video detection (NSG-VD, DeMamba)—under a single geometric constraint: projecting learned dynamics onto the tangent bundle of a VQ-VAE-learned manifold via matrix-free Jacobian-Vector Products. It departs from prior geometric deep learning approaches (Neural Manifold ODEs, Riemannian Flow Matching) that assume known or analytically specified manifold geometry, instead learning the manifold end-to-end and using the decoder's Jacobian as the tangent-space operator. For the video detection component, it explicitly builds on NSG-VD's Deep MMD scoring framework, adding a complementary tangent-consistency feature stream rather than replacing the existing pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The JVP-based tangent projection is a clean, differentiable, and computationally efficient mechanism that could be adopted as a plug-and-play constraint in other manifold-constrained learning settings, and the paper's demonstration that the same projection residual serves as both a training signal and a training-free verification metric is a useful conceptual unification. However, the ODE discovery results, while dramatic, are on a single 2D circular-motion system where baselines are known to fail; the video detection contribution is modest (GFG alone underperforms NSG-VD, and the combined system yields only marginal gains); and the framework's assumptions (local differentiability, tangential dominance) limit applicability to systems with abrupt transitions or strong off-manifold effects. Open questions include whether the approach scales to high-dimensional PDE discovery, whether the velocity codebook size and manifold dimensionality interact in non-trivial ways at scale, and whether the tangent-consistency signal remains discriminative for next-generation generators that may better preserve geometric structure.
