## Problem

Real-world multi-view data frequently suffers from both missing views and unknown cross-view sample correspondences, yet existing methods handle these as separate problems with distinct pipelines, causing error propagation and suboptimal fusion. Diffusion-based imputation is too slow for practical deployment, and alignment methods typically require expensive correspondence supervision or scale poorly with dataset size.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266168/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes heterogeneous multi-view feature vectors (dimensions ranging from 6 to 7,950 per view depending on the dataset) along with a binary availability mask indicating which views are observed per sample. In the unaligned setting, cross-view index correspondences are unknown and the method must infer structure without them. Outputs include soft cluster assignments (via DEC-style sharpened targets), imputed latent representations for missing views (generated by integrating a learned ODE with 10 Euler steps), and a shared 128-dimensional latent space where all views are projected for joint fusion.

## Method

OPTION integrates three components into a single end-to-end objective: (1) conditional flow matching with OT-guided affine paths (using a small endpoint regularizer σ_min) to learn deterministic transport from Gaussian noise to data, requiring only 10 ODE integration steps at inference versus 100+ for diffusion; (2) a Gromov-Wasserstein-inspired structural regularizer that computes RBF kernel matrices within mini-batches for each view and penalizes Frobenius-norm discrepancies between kernel matrices across view pairs, aligning intra-view geometric topology without solving hard correspondences; and (3) DEC-style soft clustering with learnable centroids refreshed by K-Means every 5 epochs. The conditioning mechanism adapts to the scenario: in aligned settings it uses consensus representations from available views, while in unaligned settings it conditions each view independently on its assigned cluster prototype. An optional InfoNCE contrastive loss is activated only when sample correspondences are known, providing instance-level consistency for aligned data while the GW term handles structure-level consistency for unaligned data.

## Results

On the incomplete multi-view clustering task, OPTION achieves 86.1% ACC on COIL-20 and 74.7% ACC on CUB at 70% view missingness, outperforming the strongest diffusion-based baseline DCG (34.4% and 18.2% respectively) by over 50 percentage points. OPTION exhibits remarkable stability across missing rates, with only a 2.6% ACC drop on COIL-20 from 0% to 70% missingness, whereas DCG drops by 28.3%. On the unaligned task, OPTION maintains 46.3% ACC on BDGP at 60% shuffle rate versus MFLVC's random-guess 20.0% and MRG-UMC's 36.6%. Inference latency is 3.28 ms per 256-sample batch (10 ODE steps), yielding an average 8.1× speedup over DCG's 25.95 ms (100 diffusion steps).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OPTION outperforms all 10 baselines across both incomplete and unaligned scenarios on every dataset. The strongest competitor is DCG (Zhang et al., AAAI 2025), the diffusion-based method, which is competitive at 0–30% missingness on COIL-20 (62.7% vs. OPTION's 88.7%) but collapses at 50–70% missingness (47.7%→34.4% vs. OPTION's 87.6%→86.1%); OPTION also runs 8.1× faster. SURE (Yang et al., TPAMI 2022) is the best non-generative baseline at low missing rates (75.2% on COIL-20 at 0%) but degrades to 41.5% at 50% and 35.0% at 70%, while OPTION stays above 86%. On the unaligned task, MRG-UMC (Xin et al., TNNLS 2025) is the strongest dedicated unaligned method (61.3% on COIL-20 at 0% shuffle) but drops to 33.3% at 60% shuffle, whereas OPTION retains 51.5%; PROTOCOL (Xue et al., ICML 2025) shows unusual stability on CUB unaligned (55.7% at 60% shuffle) yet still trails OPTION (53.0%→55.7% vs. 53.0%). MFLVC (Xu et al., CVPR 2022) performs at or near random-guess level (20.0% ACC on BDGP) across all conditions, and DealMVC, FreeCSL, and CANDY consistently rank in the bottom tier. ROLL (Sun et al., CVPR 2025) is competitive on COIL-20 at 0% (80.0%) but degrades sharply under both missingness and misalignment, falling below OPTION by 10–20 points at moderate stress levels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on conditional flow matching (Lipman et al., 2022) as its generative backbone, repurposing the OT-guided affine path formulation for missing-view imputation in multi-view clustering rather than for general-purpose image generation. It departs from the diffusion-based IMVC line (e.g., DCG) by replacing iterative stochastic denoising with a deterministic 10-step ODE, and from the permutation-learning UMVC line (PVC, MRG-UMC, CANDY) by substituting hard correspondence assignment with a Gromov-Wasserstein-inspired batch-level structural regularizer. Conceptually, the paper's main positioning move is unifying imputation, alignment, and clustering into a single end-to-end objective, whereas prior work treats incomplete and unaligned MVC as separate problems solved by distinct pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 8.1× measured inference speedup over the diffusion-based baseline and the near-flat performance curve under increasing missingness (2.6% decline vs. 28.3% for DCG on COIL-20) make this practically relevant for real-time or large-scale multi-view deployment. However, the ablation reveals that the two headline novel components (flow matching and GW alignment) each contribute only ~1% ACC, while standard contrastive and DEC clustering losses contribute 11–12%, suggesting the unified optimization framework matters more than any individual mechanism. The paper explicitly leaves the combined severe-missingness-plus-misalignment setting as future work, and the unaligned protocol shuffles only one view, so the method's robustness to more realistic multi-view misalignment remains untested.
