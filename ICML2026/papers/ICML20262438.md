## Problem

Existing visually grounded reasoning methods constrain LVLMs to rigidly align intermediate visual rationales with geometric priors from detection experts (e.g., GroundingDINO), but these priors are optimized for localization precision rather than reasoning utility. The paper demonstrates that the most geometrically precise annotation is paradoxically the least helpful for downstream reasoning, revealing a fundamental mismatch between expert design principles and the context-rich perceptual behaviors that LVLMs actually need.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image and a text instruction as input and produces a two-stage output: first a structured Perceptual Flow consisting of a planning state (a language decomposition enclosed in <analyze> tags) followed by a sequence of perceptual states, each pairing an absolute-coordinate RoI (4-tuple) with a descriptive caption enclosed in <localize> tags; then a grounded text answer generated by self-conditioning on the flow and the corresponding cropped visual features. The flow length K is variable per instance, and RoIs are represented in normalized [0,1]⁴ coordinates during training.

## Method

PFlowNet decouples perception from reasoning via a self-conditioned autoregressive generation: it first samples a perceptual flow Z from a learned variational distribution p_θ(Z|X), then generates the answer Y conditioned on both the flow and the RoI-cropped image features. Training proceeds in two stages—SFT on synthetic flows (generated by teacher models like Gemini-3 and GPT-4o with randomly expanded RoIs to mitigate expert bias) followed by Variational Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT) that minimizes a Sub-Trajectory Balance objective with a multi-dimensional reward combining contrastive caption quality (KL divergence between policy captions and ideal vs. noisy caption distributions) and reasoning efficacy (conditional log-likelihood improvement for the target answer). A vicinal geometric shaping term, based on symmetrized Chamfer-IoU distance, softly penalizes trajectories that fall outside an ε-neighborhood of expert RoIs rather than enforcing strict alignment, enabling reasoning-oriented exploration while preserving visual validity. The paper provides a provable TV-distance bound showing that with proper calibration of shaping intensity λ and radius ε, PFlowNet strictly tightens the approximation guarantee over both standard MLE (λ→0) and expert-guided RLVR (λ→∞).

## Results

PFlowNet (built on Qwen3-VL-8B) achieves SOTA on V* Bench (90.6%), MME-RealWorld-Lite (67.0%), and TreeBench (55.3% overall perception), surpassing the base model by 13.1%, 18.4%, and 10.4% respectively. On fine-grained tasks, it reaches 80.4% on HR-Bench 4K, 76.9% on HR-Bench 8K, and 95.1%/61.8% on ScreenSpot v2/Pro. The method sets SOTA on 89% of sub-tasks across all evaluated benchmarks, with particularly large gains on reasoning-heavy subsets (e.g., +18.4% on TreeBench reasoning, +30.7% on MME-RW Auto.Driv. reasoning).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PFlowNet outperforms all visually grounded reasoning baselines across every benchmark. Against the strongest VGR competitor TreeVGR, PFlowNet leads by 5.3% on TreeBench perception (55.3 vs. 50.4), 11.3% on MME-RW reasoning (70.0 vs. 51.3), and 3.2% on V* Bench (90.6 vs. 87.4). Among agentic frameworks, PFlowNet surpasses DeepEyesV2 by 14.3% on MME-RW perception (67.0 vs. 52.4) and Thyme by 22.6% on MME-RW reasoning (70.0 vs. 48.0), while also achieving lower inference latency due to its single-pass flow-guided design. On fine-grained tasks, PFlowNet (8B) matches or exceeds the larger Qwen3-VL-32B on V* Bench (90.6 vs. 87.4) and HR-Bench 4K (80.4 vs. 82.1, within 1.7%), and leads all GUI grounding models on ScreenSpot Pro (61.8 vs. 60.9 for SeedVL-1.5). The contrastive reward design is critical: ablation shows removing the P⁻ term drops V* Bench from 90.6% to 88.1%, and removing the perceptual flow entirely causes a 6.2% drop on TreeBench, confirming the flow serves as a semantic anchor beyond mere localization.

## Relation to Prior Work

PFlowNet builds directly on the Sub-Trajectory Balance (SubTB) objective from GFlowNets (Madan et al., 2023), repurposing it as a hierarchical variational loss for optimizing perceptual trajectories in LVLMs. It departs from the dominant grounded RLVR paradigm (TreeVGR, Pixel-Reasoner, ViGoRL) that enforces rigid IoU alignment with visual expert priors, and from agentic frameworks (DeepEyes, Thyme) that rely on multi-turn tool executions. The key conceptual departure is replacing pointwise geometric matching with a self-parameterized variational distribution over perceptual flows, regularized by a vicinal geometric shaping term inspired by Vicinal Risk Minimization rather than strict KL constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central empirical finding—that the most geometrically precise expert annotation is not the most useful for reasoning—challenges a core assumption in grounded RLVR and motivates a fundamentally different training objective. The theoretical analysis (TV distance bounds with provable improvement over both MLE and expert-guided RLVR) provides rare formal grounding for a practical LVLM training recipe. Open questions include whether the variational approach generalizes to multi-image or video reasoning, whether the 8B-matches-32B gap persists on truly novel task distributions, and whether the contrastive caption reward can be replaced by lighter-weight alternatives to reduce training cost.
