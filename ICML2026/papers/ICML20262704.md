## Problem

Standard off-policy RL policies produce high-frequency oscillatory control signals that are unsafe for physical actuators, yet existing remedies either constrain the policy architecture (Lipschitz bounds, ODE filters) or expand the action space to R^{hd} via explicit action chunking, which causes optimization instability and breaks compatibility with step-wise interfaces and point-wise safety overrides. The paper asks whether the stabilizing benefits of temporal abstraction can be captured implicitly—achieving smooth, temporally coherent execution while retaining a standard d-dimensional policy output and reactive step-wise reactivity.

## Input / Output

The policy consumes either a low-dimensional state vector (e.g., speed, acceleration, SOC, fuel-cell power for the FCEV task; joint positions/velocities for DMC) or a semantic-segmentation image (CARLA driving) and outputs a single d-dimensional continuous reference action. This reference action is not sent directly to the environment; instead, a deterministic execution operator expands it into h step-wise executed actions (u_{t+k} = w_k · a_t) that are applied one per environment step. The policy output dimension remains d throughout, so the interface is identical to vanilla actor-critic methods, and the executed action stream still supports instantaneous point-wise safety interventions from an expert.

## Method

DWS couples two synchronized windows sharing a common horizon h (default 3). The Execution Window applies a deterministic modulation profile—either Zero-Order Hold (w_k = 1, capping control frequency at 1/h) or Dissipative Decay (w_k = 1 − k/h, gradually attenuating magnitude)—to transform each boundary reference action into a locally coherent h-step trajectory, structurally suppressing intra-window jitter independent of policy stochasticity. The Value Window maintains an ordered on-policy buffer of executed transitions and constructs h-step windowed TD targets G_t^{(h)} with a terminal mask, then trains the critic via a Window-Step Mixed Bellman Error that interpolates between the standard one-step target and the windowed target using a validity gate z_t, thereby aligning value propagation with the execution commitment and mitigating critic myopia. A lightweight actor-side regularizer penalizes first-order action differences between temporally adjacent states sampled from the window buffer, softening boundary discontinuities. The entire framework is a plug-and-play wrapper: the original actor-critic objectives, target-network machinery, and replay pipeline are unchanged, and the design preserves full compatibility with expert-guided RL because the executed action stream remains point-wise overridable.

## Results

On the safety-critical Lane-Change Overtaking (LCO) task in CARLA, DWS achieves a 100% success rate with 0% collisions and 0% boundary violations, while all baselines fail (best baseline LipsNet++ reaches only 20% success). DWS reduces steering actuation jitter (ActSmooth) by 88.7% and yaw rate oscillation by 78.5% compared to LipsNet++. On the DeepMind Control Suite, DWS-SAC achieves near-optimal returns (975.35 on Reacher-Hard, 974.75 on Ball-in-Cup) while reducing AFR by over 80% versus vanilla baselines. On the FCEV energy management task, DWS attains the best reward (−1393.15) and lowest operational cost (157.04 RMB), improving reward by 56.7% over the strongest baseline (ActionChunk).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DWS dominates all seven baselines across every evaluated domain. Against the vanilla actor-critic backbones (TD3 and SAC), DWS achieves comparable or higher task returns while reducing AFR by 80%+ on precision tasks (e.g., 0.10 vs 0.56 on Reacher-Hard). Against the smooth-control baselines L2C2, LipsNet++, and SmODE, DWS wins decisively on task performance: LipsNet++ collapses to 0.40 reward on Reacher-Hard and only 20% success on LCO, while SmODE and L2C2 suffer SOC drift in the EMS task; DWS matches or exceeds their smoothness metrics while maintaining full task competence. Against the explicit action chunking baseline (ActionChunk, following Q-chunking), DWS avoids the dimensionality explosion that causes ActionChunk to fail entirely on Ball-in-Cup (3.12 reward) and Cart-pole (0.01 reward), and achieves 100% vs 0% success on LCO. Against the expert-guided backbone HG-TD3, DWS improves LCO success from 0% to 100% and AEB reward from 362.42 to 390.70 while reducing JerkRMS by 50.9%, demonstrating that the dual-window design synergizes with expert intervention rather than conflicting with it.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by reframing the smooth continuous control problem through the lens of implicit action chunking, departing from both regularization-based smoothers (Mysore, Shen) and architectural approaches (L2C2, LipsNet++, SmODE) that remain bound to step-wise decision making. It explicitly contrasts with the explicit action chunking paradigm (Q-Chunking, Decoupled Q-Chunking, RoboAgent, π0), which expands the policy output to R^{hd} and executes open-loop trajectory segments. DWS's key conceptual departure is achieving temporal coherence through a deterministic execution operator (zero-order hold or dissipative decay) applied to a standard d-dimensional action, coupled with a synchronized Value Window that aligns critic Bellman backups to the execution horizon—something absent in both prior smoothing methods and explicit chunking approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DWS offers a plug-and-play module that preserves standard actor-critic interfaces and experience replay while delivering execution-level smoothness guarantees, making it directly applicable to safety-critical deployment where instantaneous point-wise safety overrides (expert-guided RL) must coexist with temporally coherent control. The fixed execution horizon introduces a structural inductive bias that may limit adaptability under abrupt within-window disturbances, and the paper acknowledges that state-dependent or learnable execution profiles and adaptive window lengths are natural next steps. Extending the framework to offline RL for large-scale pre-training is identified as a promising but unexplored direction.
