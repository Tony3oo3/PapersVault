## Problem

Existing audio deepfake detection methods predominantly rely on local or pairwise interactions (convolutions, attention, binary graph edges), overlooking high-order interactions (HOIs) where discriminative artifacts emerge only from the joint consideration of multiple temporal-spectral cues. The paper uses O-information as a diagnostic tool to argue that generalizable spoofing artifacts contain synergistic (non-redundant) high-order structures that pairwise mechanisms cannot explicitly model, motivating a shift toward hypergraph-based relational representation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264969/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes raw audio waveforms padded or truncated to 4 seconds (64,000 samples) and produces a binary bona-fide/spoof classification score. The waveform is first encoded by a pretrained XLS-R (300M) SSL frontend together with a RawNet2 residual encoder, then aggregated along spectral and temporal dimensions to form two node sets (42 spectral and 66 temporal nodes) that serve as the vertex set for hypergraph construction. Evaluation is reported as EER and F1 across 13 cross-scenario test sets, with the model trained exclusively on ASVspoof 2019 LA.

## Method

HyperPotter replaces the pairwise graph attention layers in Wav2Vec2-AASIST with a memory-enhanced Hypergraph Attention GNN (HAGNN) that constructs soft hyperedges via Fuzzy C-Means clustering over node features, enabling multi-node relational message passing. A relational artifact amplification operator fuses a structural term (shared hyperedge participation, UU^T) with a feature-similarity term (X'X'^T/√D), then applies an attention-driven aggregate-then-project-back update to emphasize the most informative synergistic artifacts. A class-aware prototype bank (positive, negative, and global centroids) is maintained via EMA updates with a two-stage alignment strategy (soft alignment for early epochs, slot alignment later) to initialize FCM centroids, replacing random initialization after a 5-epoch warm-start. Four heterogeneous HAGNN layers process spectral and temporal graphs jointly, and the entire framework adds only 0.03M parameters over the baseline while achieving an average relative EER reduction of 12.68% across 13 test sets.

## Results

HyperPotter (317.87M params, only +0.03M over baseline) achieves the best EER on 8 of 13 test sets, including In-the-Wild (5.72%), ASVspoof 2021 DF (1.78%), FoR (3.89%), Codecfake (34.47%), ADD2022 Track 3 (11.31%), ADD2023 R1 (21.49%), LibriVoc (2.55%), and SONAR (27.71%). It improves over the retrained Wav2Vec2-AASIST baseline on 11 of 13 sets, yielding an average relative EER reduction of 12.68% across all sets and 22.15% on the improved subsets. Performance degrades on the two codec-heavy sets (ASVspoof 2021 LA: 3.94% vs 2.48%; ASVspoof 5: 16.04% vs 13.38%), which the authors attribute to channel distortion masking fine-grained high-order dependencies.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary retrained Wav2Vec2-AASIST baseline, HyperPotter wins on 11/13 sets with the largest gains on ADD2022 Track 3 (11.31% vs 16.14%, −4.83 pp), Codecfake (34.47% vs 40.22%, −5.75 pp), and In-the-Wild (5.72% vs 7.58%, −1.86 pp), while losing on 21LA (+1.46 pp) and ASVspoof 5 (+2.66 pp) due to severe codec distortion. Versus XLSR+Mamba (Xiao & Das, 2025), HyperPotter achieves lower EER on ITW (5.72 vs 6.70), 21DF (1.78 vs 1.88), FoR (3.89 vs 6.71), and ADD22T3 (11.31 vs 19.36), but trails on LibriVoc (2.55 vs 2.15) and SONAR (27.71 vs 24.26). Against XLSR+BiCrossMamba (El Kheir et al., 2025), HyperPotter dominates on most cross-lingual and diverse-attack sets (e.g., ADD23R1: 21.49 vs 29.44; ADD23R2: 21.84 vs 29.93) but is slightly worse on LibriVoc and SONAR. Compared to XLSR+SLS (Zhang et al., 2024a), HyperPotter wins on ITW, 21DF, FoR, and ADD22T3 but loses on Codecfake, ADD23R1/R2, LibriVoc, and SONAR. On the in-domain 19LA set, XLS-R+STCA+LMDC (Hao et al., 2025) achieves 0.09% EER, well below HyperPotter's 0.23%, indicating that the HOI modeling advantage is most pronounced in cross-scenario generalization rather than in-distribution detection.

## Relation to Prior Work

HyperPotter builds directly on the Wav2Vec2-AASIST backbone (Tak et al., 2022), preserving its XLS-R front-end, RawNet2 encoder, and spectral-temporal heterogeneous graph construction, while replacing the pairwise graph attention layers with a hypergraph attention layer (HAGNN) derived from HyperSAGE-style message passing (Arya et al., 2020). The key conceptual departure is from the dominant pairwise-interaction paradigm in ADD (CNNs, self-attention, standard GNNs like AASIST) toward explicit high-order interaction modeling via FCM-based soft hyperedges, motivated by O-information theory (Rosas et al., 2019) as a diagnostic for redundancy-versus-synergy dominance. The prototype-guided initialization mechanism draws on prototype learning from domain adaptation and incremental deepfake detection, repurposing class-aware centroids as cross-batch structural priors for hyperedge construction rather than for classification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic investigation of high-order interactions in audio deepfake detection from an information-theoretic perspective, and the O-information diagnostic provides a useful conceptual lens for understanding *when* and *why* HOI modeling helps versus hurts. The non-uniform gains—strong improvements on diverse-attack and cross-lingual sets but degradation under severe codec/channel distortion—reveal that synergy-oriented cues can be masked by compression artifacts, positioning HyperPotter as a 'specialist' module best deployed within ensemble or MoE frameworks rather than as a standalone detector. Open questions include adaptive mechanisms that dynamically balance redundancy- and synergy-oriented evidence based on channel conditions, and whether the computational overhead (≈4× inference latency at batch size 1) can be reduced through lighter graph construction or fewer FCM iterations without sacrificing the HOI signal.
