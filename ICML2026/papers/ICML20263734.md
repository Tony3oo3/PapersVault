## Problem

Existing methods for imbalanced partial label learning rely on accurate class-prior estimation to guide resampling, pseudo-label generation, or logit adjustment. Under unreliable partial labels, however, prior estimation is inherently unstable, creating a feedback loop where inaccurate priors distort annotation refinement, which in turn further corrupts prior estimates, causing rapid convergence to suboptimal solutions that disproportionately harm minority-class performance. No prior work explicitly addresses the simultaneous relaxation of both label-level (unreliability) and sample-level (imbalance) assumptions.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, variable resolution for PASCAL VOC) annotated with candidate label sets where the ground-truth label may be absent (unreliability rate γ) and the class distribution follows a long-tailed pattern (imbalance ratio ρ up to 200). Pseudo-labels are initialized as uniform distributions over each candidate set and iteratively refined via a moving-average prior estimate and a pseudo-label generation model. The output is a standard multi-class prediction, evaluated on overall accuracy and separately on minority-class accuracy (last 3 classes for CIFAR-10, last 33 for CIFAR-100).

## Method

CLAPOR formulates training as a bi-level optimization: the inner level minimizes a cross-entropy loss against iteratively refined pseudo-labels, while the outer level minimizes a regularization term that evaluates model performance under adversarially perturbed class priors. The perturbation is instantiated by sampling prior parameters from a Dirichlet distribution whose concentration vector is constructed by reversing the order of the current estimated prior (so minority classes receive higher Dirichlet weight), ensuring sampled priors deviate from the potentially biased estimate. Implementation uses MAML-style virtual parameters to backpropagate gradients through the inner update into the outer regularization, combined with per-epoch pseudo-label refinement, candidate label reconstruction, and two-stage gradient updates. This design eliminates dependence on accurate prior estimation while naturally preserving attention to minority classes, and is validated across 12 I-UPLL settings on CIFAR-10/100 plus a real-world PASCAL VOC experiment.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (ρ=50, γ=0.3, ι=0.3), CLAPOR achieves 61.68% overall accuracy and 52.40% minority-class accuracy, outperforming the strongest baseline SOLAR (43.72% overall, 4.98% minority) by 17.96 and 47.42 percentage points respectively. On CIFAR-100 (ρ=10, γ=0.3, ι=0.05), CLAPOR reaches 43.14% overall and 30.21% minority accuracy versus SOLAR's 41.24% and 25.27%. CLAPOR consistently achieves the best overall accuracy across all 12 I-UPLL settings on both datasets, and the best minority-class accuracy on all CIFAR-100 settings and most CIFAR-10 settings. Wilcoxon signed-rank tests confirm statistical significance (p < 0.05) against all baselines except a tie with ALIM on CIFAR-10 minority accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLAPOR outperforms all 10 baselines on overall accuracy across every I-UPLL setting tested. The largest gap is against SOLAR, the strongest baseline on overall accuracy (e.g., 61.68% vs. 43.72% on CIFAR-10 ρ=50), but SOLAR collapses catastrophically on minority classes (4.98% vs. CLAPOR's 52.40%), revealing its heavy dependence on accurate prior estimation. LW is the weakest baseline overall, frequently scoring near 0% on minority classes (e.g., 0.49% on CIFAR-10 ρ=50) due to its leverage-weighted loss being ill-suited to unreliable labels. PICO+ and PICO, which use contrastive disambiguation, achieve moderate overall accuracy (35.44% and 32.77% on CIFAR-10 ρ=50) but remain far below CLAPOR. ROBUSTL, designed for UPLL robustness, and PSCR, which reconstructs candidate labels, both underperform CLAPOR by 15–30 percentage points on overall accuracy. The Wilcoxon signed-rank test confirms CLAPOR's superiority at p < 0.05 against all baselines, with the sole exception of a non-significant tie (p=0.0771) against ALIM on CIFAR-10 minority-class accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

CLAPOR sits at the intersection of two recently introduced relaxations of Partial Label Learning: Unreliable PLL (UPLL), which drops the guarantee that the true label is in the candidate set, and Imbalanced PLL (IPLL), which drops the uniform class-prior assumption. Existing IPLL methods (SOLAR, Hong et al., Jia et al.) all depend on estimating the class prior to drive resampling, pseudo-label refinement, or logit adjustment, while UPLL methods (PSCR, PICO+, ALIM) focus on annotation repair. CLAPOR departs from both lines by eliminating the prior-estimation dependency entirely: it borrows the bi-level virtual-parameter machinery from MAML-style optimization and instantiates its pseudo-label and candidate-reconstruction components from SOLAR and PSCR, but replaces prior-guided rebalancing with a Dirichlet-sampled adversarial prior perturbation as a regularization objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete failure mode — a feedback loop where unreliable annotations corrupt prior estimates, which in turn distort annotation refinement — that is likely present in any practical weakly supervised pipeline combining imbalance and noise, yet had not been formally studied. The proposed fix (robustness to arbitrary priors rather than accurate prior estimation) is conceptually clean and could inspire similar 'perturbation-robust' regularizations in other settings where a distributional estimate is needed but unreliable. Limitations include evaluation only on CIFAR-10/100 and PASCAL VOC, a ~3× per-epoch training overhead from the bi-level update, and notably high variance on minority-class accuracy for CIFAR-10 (e.g., 52.40 ± 9.86), which tempers confidence in the largest reported gains. Open questions include whether the Dirichlet-perturbation strategy transfers to larger-scale datasets (ImageNet-scale) and to other weakly supervised paradigms (semi-supervised, self-supervised) where prior estimation is similarly fragile.
