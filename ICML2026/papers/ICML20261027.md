## Problem

No unconditional (information-theoretic) space lower bounds existed for user-level differentially private streaming algorithms; the only prior result (Dinur et al., 2023) relied on cryptographic assumptions and an artificially constructed problem. This left open whether natural estimation tasks like CountDistinct inherently require large memory under DP, and whether the polynomial space of existing private algorithms is necessary or merely a technical artifact.

## Input / Output

The lower bound applies to any (ε, δ)-user-level DP streaming algorithm solving CountDistinct, MaxSelect, or Quantile on (w, k)-occurrence bounded streams of length T, where at most k users exceed w updates and the algorithm must achieve (τ=1/10, η=h) approximation with probability 1−1/T². The output is a space lower bound of Ω̃(kw/h²) bits; in the regime w=T^(2/3−4α), k=T^(1/3−2α), h=T^(1/3−α) this yields Ω̃(T^(1/3−4α)), establishing an exponential separation from the Õ(1) space of non-private streaming algorithms.

## Method

The core technique is a novel multi-player communication game called AvoidHeavyHitters, where P players each receive a set C∪S_i (heavy users C hidden among light users S_i) and must submit large subsets while passing M-bit messages, winning if no heavy user appears in too many submitted sets. Hard streaming instances are constructed so that each of P phases performs w one-way marginal queries over C∪S_i, with insertions and revocations per query. A fingerprinting lemma forces any accurate algorithm to exhibit high correlation between its normalized outputs and the underlying Bernoulli inputs, while the DP property bounds this correlation for heavy users across all phases. A rounding procedure converts per-query algorithm outputs into the required subsets Y_i, with the correlation gap between heavy and light users driving the size and membership constraints. The information-theoretic communication lower bound for AvoidHeavyHitters, Ω(k(ε₁−ε₂)²) bits, directly translates to the space lower bound since the algorithm's memory configuration serves as the inter-player message.

## Results

The paper establishes the first unconditional space lower bound for user-level DP streaming algorithms. The main result (Theorem 2.1) shows that any (ε=0.5, δ=1/T²)-DP algorithm for CountDistinct on (w,k)-occurrence bounded streams with additive error h must use Õ(kw/h²) bits of space. In the concrete regime w=T^(2/3−4α), k=T^(1/3−2α), h=T^(1/3−α), this yields a Õ(T^(1/3−4α)) space lower bound (Corollary 2.2), which is nearly tight against the Õ(T^(1/3)) upper bound of Cummings et al. (2025). The same technique extends to MaxSelect and Quantile estimation (Theorem 2.3), establishing an exponential separation between private (T^(1/3−o(1)) space) and non-private (Õ(1) space) algorithms for natural streaming estimation tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or experimental comparisons. The key comparison is between the established lower bound and the best known upper bound: Cummings et al. (2025) achieve Õ(√w + k) additive error using Õ(T^(1/3)) space for CountDistinct, while this paper proves that any algorithm achieving error T^(1/3−α) must use at least Õ(T^(1/3−4α)) space, demonstrating the Cummings et al. algorithm is nearly space-optimal in that regime. In contrast, non-private streaming algorithms (e.g., HyperLogLog, MinHash) solve CountDistinct with Õ(1) space and small multiplicative error, yielding an exponential (T^(1/3−o(1)) vs. Õ(1)) separation in space complexity due to the privacy requirement. The prior space lower bound of Dinur et al. (2023) relied on cryptographic assumptions and an artificially constructed problem; this work provides the first unconditional, information-theoretic bound applicable to natural statistical estimation problems.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the only prior space lower bound for DP streaming (Dinur et al., 2023), which relied on cryptographic assumptions and an artificially constructed encryption-based problem, by introducing an unconditional, information-theoretic proof technique grounded in a novel multi-player communication game (AvoidHeavyHitters). The analysis builds directly on the fingerprinting lemma (Bun et al., 2018; Dwork et al., 2015) to establish correlation lower bounds between algorithm outputs and hidden inputs, and on the occurrence-bounding/capping framework of Cummings et al. (2025) and Jain et al. (2023a) to construct hard instances. For extensions to MaxSelect and Quantile, the authors substitute the standard fingerprinting lemma with the stronger variant of Peter et al. (2024), showing the communication-game framework is modular across problem families.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first unconditional space lower bound for user-level DP streaming, resolving an open problem and establishing an exponential separation (T^{1/3} vs. Õ(1)) between private and non-private space for the natural CountDistinct task. The conceptual takeaway—that the widely-used 'capping' strategy for bounding user contributions is not merely convenient but essentially *necessary* for privacy—challenges the intuition that low-space alternatives (e.g., aggressive down-sampling) could circumvent the memory cost of tracking heavy users. The communication-game technique is general and the authors explicitly note it could extend to other problems; a key open question is whether the memory-accuracy trade-off in Theorem 2.1 is tight, and whether the framework yields bounds for problems beyond the three studied here.
