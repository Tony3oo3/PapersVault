## Problem

Classifier-free guidance (CFG) in flow matching models relies on a heuristic linear extrapolation that is highly sensitive to the guidance scale, yet lacks a principled theoretical interpretation. Specifically, it remains unclear what objective the CFG extrapolation approximates, whether an optimal guidance scale exists, and why empirically reducing the prediction gap between conditional and unconditional predictions consistently improves generation quality. This paper addresses all three questions by recasting CFG sampling as a constrained homotopy optimization problem.

## Input / Output

The method is entirely training-free and operates at sampling time on pre-trained flow matching models (DiT-XL-2-256 for 256×256 ImageNet class-conditional generation, Flux-dev and SD3.5 for text-to-image). It consumes the same inputs as standard CFG — Gaussian noise, a text prompt or class label, and a guidance scale w — but augments each ODE integration step with K additional fixed-point iterations (K=2 recommended) that project the latent state onto a manifold where conditional and unconditional velocity predictions agree. The output is a single generated image per prompt, with no change to the model architecture or training procedure.

## Method

The paper first proves that the ideal conditional velocity field in flow matching equals the gradient of a smoothed squared-distance function to the scaled target image set, revealing CFG sampling as a homotopy optimization. From this, it derives an optimal guidance scale w* and decomposes the CFG approximation error into an irreducible model error plus (w*−w)²·‖v_θ(t,x,y)−v_θ(t,x,∅)‖², showing that the prediction gap directly governs sensitivity to w. To eliminate the prediction gap, CFG-MP imposes a manifold constraint M_t = {z : v_θ(t,z,y) = v_θ(t,z,∅)} and projects onto it after each sampling step using an incremental gradient descent operator G(x,t) that alternates unconditional and conditional velocity evaluations. CFG-MP+ further accelerates these fixed-point iterations with Anderson Acceleration (window m=1, damping β=1), which constructs each new iterate as a least-squares-optimal linear combination of past residuals, improving convergence stability without additional model evaluations.

## Results

On ImageNet 256 (DiT-XL-2-256, ω=2.0, NFEs=120), CFG-MP+ achieves an Inception Score of 86.26, a 14.78-point gain over the CFG(D2F) baseline (71.48), while maintaining a competitive FID of 9.34. On text-to-image generation with Flux-dev (ω=3, NFE=120), CFG-MP+ attains HPSv2 of 30.79 and ImageReward of 1.07 on DrawBench, outperforming vanilla CFG (29.30 / 1.02) and R-CFG++ (29.57 / 1.03). On SD3.5 (ω=3.0, NFE=100), CFG-MP+ reaches HPSv2 30.83 and IR 1.13 on Pick-a-Pic, and achieves 61.41% overall GenEval score versus 58.36% for vanilla CFG.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CFG-MP/MP+ consistently outperform all six baselines on human-preference metrics (HPSv2, ImageReward) across both Flux-dev and SD3.5. Against vanilla CFG, CFG-MP+ improves HPSv2 by ~1.5 points and IR by ~0.05–0.13 depending on model and dataset. Compared to R-CFG++ (Saini et al., 2025), CFG-MP+ gains 1.22 HPSv2 and 0.04 IR on Flux-dev DrawBench (NFE=120), and 0.61 HPSv2 and 0.09 IR on SD3.5 Pick-a-Pic (NFE=100). Against CFG-0* (Fan et al., 2025), the advantage is similar in magnitude, with CFG-MP+ leading by 1.19 HPSv2 on Flux-dev and 0.64 HPSv2 on SD3.5. On the class-conditional ImageNet task, CFG-MP+ substantially outperforms the DDIM-framework baselines (Z-sampling, Re-sampling, FSG) in Inception Score: at ω=2.0, NFEs=120, CFG-MP+ reaches IS 86.26 versus 74.75 for FSG, 73.07 for Re-sampling, and 72.52 for Z-sampling, while FID remains competitive (9.34 vs. 11.50–11.68). On GenEval (SD3.5, cfg=4, NFE=60), CFG-MP+ achieves 61.41% overall, surpassing R-CFG++ (59.57%), CFG-0* (59.24%), and vanilla CFG (58.36%), with the largest gains in counting (56.25% vs. 52.50%) and spatial positioning (17.00% vs. 13.00%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the classifier-free guidance framework (Ho & Salimans, 2021) and flow matching (Lipman et al., 2023), providing the first rigorous optimization-based interpretation of CFG in the flow-matching setting. It departs from prior CFG refinements (FSG, Rectified-CFG++, CFG-0*, Z-sampling) that operate heuristically or are diffusion-centric, by showing that the ideal velocity field is the gradient of a smoothed distance function, thereby recasting CFG sampling as a homotopy optimization with a manifold constraint. The prediction-gap concept is borrowed from Wang et al. (2025), but this paper supplies the missing theoretical justification for why reducing it improves robustness. Anderson Acceleration (Anderson, 1965), a classical numerical optimization tool, is repurposed to accelerate the fixed-point manifold projection without additional model evaluations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The error decomposition in Theorem 3.4 provides the first formal explanation for the long-observed sensitivity of CFG to the guidance scale, grounding the prediction-gap heuristic in a principled framework. The method is training-free and transfers cleanly across architectures (DiT-XL-2-256, Flux-dev, SD3.5), making it immediately applicable to existing deployed models. A key limitation is the additional NFEs required for projection iterations, which the authors acknowledge may hinder deployment in latency-sensitive settings; they suggest moving prediction-gap mitigation to training time (e.g., via RL rewards or regularization) as a promising direction. Extending the manifold-projection formulation to diffusion samplers (DDIM, etc.) is another natural open question the authors identify.
