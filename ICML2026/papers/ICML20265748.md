## Problem

Existing class unlearning methods require access to raw data or operate under a trusted central server, making them inapplicable when historical data is irretrievably lost due to privacy constraints. No prior work addresses the combined challenge of class-incremental learning and arbitrary-time class unlearning in a fully decentralized setting where knowledge is entangled across all clients and no central orchestrator exists.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265748/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client receives local non-IID image samples (28×28 grayscale or 32×32 RGB) with class labels, a frozen pre-trained diffusion model (minDiffusion or LDM), and an unlearning request specifying a target class to forget. The system outputs an updated classifier (feature extractor + expanded classification head), lightweight disposable class prototype embeddings in the diffusion model's condition space, and synthetic image samples used for both knowledge replay and targeted forgetting. Prototypes are discarded after unlearning to prevent interference with subsequent tasks.

## Method

DCU operates in three phases: (1) Prototype Extraction, where textual inversion on a frozen pre-trained diffusion model yields compact class-specific condition embeddings that capture class concepts without storing raw data; (2) Prototype-Guided Continual Learning, where synthetic samples generated from these prototypes are combined with local real data, and a composite loss (KL-divergence distillation from the previous model + teacher-weighted cross-entropy on synthetic data + cross-entropy on combined real/synthetic data) is optimized via D-PSGD over a dynamic Erdős-Rényi topology; (3) Unlearning with Disposable Prototypes, where synthetic data for all learned classes is generated, all samples are relabeled to the target class to train an unlearning head (with the feature extractor frozen), and the final classifier is updated via a linear subtraction θ_new = θ_ori − β_ul·θ_ul, after which the target prototype is permanently discarded. The key insight is that prototypes serve as a disposable bridge: they enable both knowledge replay for continual learning and targeted boundary distortion for unlearning, eliminating the need for raw data access at any stage.

## Results

On the RGB dataset (CIFAR-100, 30 classes) under Dir(γ=0.5), DCU achieves 50.67% accuracy on remaining classes with 0.00% on unlearned classes and a Forgetting Measure of 0.66 after the full sequence of learning and unlearning tasks. Under the more challenging Dir(γ=0.1) setting, DCU reaches 40.04% retention with F=0.59, substantially outperforming Lander/AU (34.11%, F=0.47). On the Grayscale dataset (MNIST+EMNIST+Fashion-MNIST), DCU achieves 86.50% retention with F=0.92 under Dir(γ=0.5) and 70.72% with F=0.73 under Dir(γ=0.1), consistently maintaining near-zero accuracy on unlearned classes (0.00–0.02%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DCU achieves the best or near-best Forgetting Measure across all settings, reaching 0.66 (RGB, γ=0.5) and 0.59 (RGB, γ=0.1), while maintaining 0.00% accuracy on unlearned classes. On RGB Dir(γ=0.5), DCU (50.67% retention, F=0.66) is marginally below Lander/AU (50.96%, F=0.67) but significantly more stable under Dir(γ=0.1), where DCU leads by 5.93% retention (40.04% vs 34.11%) and 0.12 in F-score. On Grayscale Dir(γ=0.5), DCU achieves the highest F-score (0.92) and 86.50% retention, outperforming Target/AU (74.33%, F=0.82) and CLMUL (90.04%, F=0.80) which suffers 14.00% residual on unlearned classes. Standard continual learning baselines (FT, EWC, LwF, Target, Lander) lack unlearning capability entirely, showing no unlearning metrics. Methods requiring access to recent data (FedAF, EWC/AU, LWF/AU) fail to unlearn historical classes, retaining identical accuracy before and after unlearning requests for earlier tasks. CLMUL, which stores real data in a buffer, violates privacy constraints and degrades severely on RGB (33.11%/0.00% and 32.04%/0.00%) due to non-IID buffer corruption.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes textual inversion for diffusion models (Gal et al., 2022) and diffusion-driven data replay (Liang et al., 2024) from their original federated/centralized contexts into a fully decentralized setting, using extracted class prototypes as a dual-purpose mechanism for both knowledge replay during continual learning and targeted forgetting during unlearning. The unlearning operation (linear subtraction on the classification head) is directly inherited from FedAU (Gu et al., 2024), but is decoupled from raw data access by conditioning on synthetic samples generated from prototypes. The key departure from prior work is the simultaneous handling of class-incremental learning and on-demand class unlearning in a server-free, data-free decentralized topology, a combination that no prior method (CLMUL, QuickDrop, FedAF, Target, Lander) addresses under these joint constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work establishes the first formal treatment of decentralized continual learning with on-demand unlearning under irreversible data unavailability, a setting motivated by real-world privacy regulations (GDPR, CCPA) in distributed systems. The disposable-prototype design is elegant in principle—enabling both retention and forgetting through a single lightweight artifact—but the experimental validation is limited to 30 classes and 5–10 clients, leaving open questions about scalability to hundreds of classes, very large client populations, and whether prototype quality degrades for highly diverse or fine-grained classes. The convergence guarantee (O(1/√(nQ_l) + 1/Q_l)) provides theoretical grounding but relies on standard smoothness and bounded-heterogeneity assumptions that may not hold in practice with highly non-IID data.
