## Problem

Multimodal LLMs suffer from hallucination snowballing in multi-turn dialogue, where early perceptual errors are progressively amplified by corrupted textual history, causing a collapse in visual grounding. Existing benchmarks are confined to single-turn VQA or simplistic two-turn patterns, failing to capture the long-range, non-linear dynamics of error propagation in realistic long-horizon interactions, and existing single-turn mitigation methods prove ineffective in this multi-turn setting.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image (encoded as visual tokens), a multi-turn dialogue history of up to six turns (tokenized as text), and a current query, concatenated into a single input sequence. The output is an autoregressively generated open-ended text response. Evaluation is performed via GPT-5 as a semantic judge against ground-truth answers, supplemented by two custom metrics: Visual Fading Ratio (relative accuracy drop from Turn 1 to Turn 5) and Snowballing Resistance Score (joint correctness across the critical adversarial window of Turns 3–5).

## Method

CAVR is a training-free, dual-stream intervention that operates at two complementary levels. At the representation level (RVR), it monitors normalized Shannon entropy of the predictive distribution at intermediate Transformer layers; when uncertainty exceeds a calibrated threshold, it reformulates the FFN as a key-value memory retrieval system, using the current hidden state as a query to attend over raw visual tokens and injecting the resulting rectification term via linear interpolation into the FFN output. At the logit level (LCR), it computes three parallel predictive streams (full-context, residual visual anchor conditioned only on image+query, and linguistic prior from query alone), then uses Jensen-Shannon Divergence between the visual anchor and linguistic prior to compute a conflict score that adaptively blends full-context logits with visual-anchor logits. The benchmark MM-Snowball is constructed via Adversarial Hallucination Trajectory Synthesis (AHTS), which formalizes dialogue as a trajectory through four cognitive phases—perceptual anchoring, adversarial bifurcation, hallucination snowballing, and visual rectification—using causal intervention operators (entity substitution, attribute flipping, relation inversion) to create controlled counterfactual hallucination seeds across 4,992 six-turn trajectories.

## Results

On MM-Snowball, all 11 evaluated MLLMs exhibit a characteristic V-shaped accuracy trajectory: performance collapses from ~60% at Turn 1 to below 10% at Turns 4–5, then partially recovers at Turn 6 when models are prompted to re-examine the image. The best-performing model, Seed-1.6, achieves a Snowballing Resistance Score (SRS) of 41.70 and Visual Fading Rate (VFR) of 4.65%, while weaker models like LLaVA-1.5-7B collapse to 0.50% accuracy at Turn 5 with a VFR of 99.19%. CAVR, the proposed training-free method, achieves the lowest VFR (98.95% on LLaVA-1.5, 93.22% on Qwen2.5-VL) and highest SRS (0.15 and 0.30 respectively) among all mitigation strategies, with the largest gains concentrated in the critical Turns 4–6 where error propagation is most severe.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAVR consistently outperforms all six training-free baselines (VCD, OPERA, DoLA, DeCo, MemVR, RVD) on both LLaVA-1.5-7B and Qwen2.5-VL-7B across the critical mid-to-late turns. On LLaVA-1.5-7B, CAVR achieves the highest Turn-4 accuracy (0.75% vs. 0.40–0.60% for baselines) and Turn-6 accuracy (35.25% vs. 32.95–35.15%), while also attaining the lowest VFR (98.95% vs. 99.03–99.34%) and highest SRS (0.15 vs. 0.00–0.05). On Qwen2.5-VL-7B, the advantage is more pronounced: CAVR reaches 9.15% at Turn 4 (vs. 7.95–8.95%) and 47.85% at Turn 6 (vs. 46.95–47.65%), with the best VFR (93.22% vs. 93.63–94.63%) and SRS (0.30 vs. 0.10–0.20). Notably, RVD—specifically designed for hallucination snowballing—still trails CAVR on SRS by 0.10 (LLaVA) and 0.10 (Qwen), confirming that single-mechanism approaches are insufficient for the full six-turn snowballing chain. The margins, while modest in absolute terms, are consistent across all turns and both architectures, indicating CAVR's dual-stream design (representation-level + logit-level) provides a systematic rather than sporadic improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the hallucination snowballing concept introduced by Zhong et al. (ACL 2024), which was limited to a two-turn caption-then-question setup, into a six-turn progressive dialogue with a formal adversarial trajectory synthesis framework (AHTS). Methodologically, CAVR synthesizes ideas from contrastive decoding (VCD, OPERA, DoLA, DeCo) and memory-based visual retracing (MemVR) into a unified dual-stream intervention that operates simultaneously at the representation level (FFN-level visual re-injection) and the logit level (JSD-based conflict arbitration). The paper departs from prior single-turn benchmarks by formalizing multi-turn error propagation as a state-space trajectory problem with explicit cognitive phases, and from prior multi-turn work (VisDiaHalBench, MMHalSnowball) by using native images and a truly progressive dialogue structure rather than fixed or object-focused patterns.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The V-shaped performance trajectory finding—that visual evidence is suppressed rather than irreversibly forgotten, and can be reactivated with explicit re-grounding prompts—provides a mechanistic explanation for snowballing that directly motivates training-free interventions like CAVR. The demonstration that all existing single-turn mitigation methods (VCD, OPERA, DoLA, DeCo, MemVR) fail to prevent collapse in multi-turn settings highlights a significant gap in the field. Limitations include the synthetic nature of the AHTS-constructed dialogues (which may not capture the full diversity of real user interactions), reliance on GPT-5 as an automated evaluator, and the relatively modest absolute gains of CAVR over baselines in the most severe collapse turns. Open questions include whether the six-turn protocol generalizes to truly open-ended conversations of unbounded length, and whether the adversarial bifurcation design at Turn 3 overstates the role of explicit false premises versus more subtle, organic error accumulation.
