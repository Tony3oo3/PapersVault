## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models struggle to maintain subject identity consistency across diverse scene prompts, and existing solutions either require costly per-subject training or training-free methods that demand excessive memory for intermediate activations or complex auxiliary modules. A critical property has been overlooked: identity-relevant signals (Identity-Preserving Embeddings, IPemb) are already implicitly encoded within the aggregated textual embeddings of a full frame-prompt sequence, yet no prior work explicitly extracts or leverages them.

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of N text prompts sharing a common subject description (either in a fixed template or diverse natural-language formulations) and the weights of a pre-trained T2I diffusion model (SDXL by default, also demonstrated on SD3). It produces a sequence of 1024×1024 subject-consistent images, with all frames sharing the same noise initialization. The framework is training-free and plug-and-play, requiring no reference images, no fine-tuning, and only modest additional computation (~0.2 s per prompt for adaSVR, 5–20% denoising overhead for UniK) with negligible memory increase.

## Method

BIPE operates entirely in the text-embedding space with two complementary techniques. First, Adaptive Singular-Value Rescaling (adaSVR) performs SVD on the stacked subject-token and [EoT]-token embedding matrices at every self-attention layer of the CLIP text encoder, then applies z-score-based exponential weighting (controlled by temperature τ=0.35) to amplify dominant singular values corresponding to shared identity components while suppressing frame-specific noise, followed by energy-matching normalization to preserve scale. Second, Union Key (UniK) modifies cross-attention in the U-Net by concatenating key vectors from subject and [EoT] tokens across all N frame prompts while restricting value vectors to the current frame only, effectively averaging attention over semantically aligned tokens to enforce cross-frame consistency. The 1/N attention weight assigned to extra K-V pairs prevents them from dominating generation, and only a small number (5–10) of shared [EoT] keys are needed for optimal performance, ensuring scalability to long stories.

## Results

On ConsiStory+, BIPE achieves the best text-image alignment (CLIP-T 31.44, VQA 0.8381) and the best identity consistency among training-free methods (CLIP-I 89.10, DreamSim 0.2053), while using only 17.16 GB memory and 20.12 s inference time. On the new DiverStory benchmark (diverse, non-templated prompts), BIPE again leads in CLIP-T (31.85) and VQA (0.8360), and ranks first among training-free methods in CLIP-I (85.04). The method requires no training, no reference image, and no fixed prompt template, making it a plug-and-play module over any T2I backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BIPE outperforms all training-free baselines on text-image alignment: it beats StoryDiffusion (CLIP-T 31.44 vs 31.32), ConsiStory (31.44 vs 31.27), and 1Prompt1Story (31.44 vs 30.11) on ConsiStory+, with similar margins on DiverStory. In identity consistency, BIPE (CLIP-I 89.10, DreamSim 0.2053) surpasses StoryDiffusion (88.58 / 0.2266), ConsiStory (87.12 / 0.2438), and 1Prompt1Story (88.36 / 0.2153), but trails the training-based IP-Adapter (91.31 / 0.1654) by roughly 2.2 points in CLIP-I. BIPE also uses substantially less memory (17.16 GB) and is faster (20.12 s) than StoryDiffusion (35.11 GB / 34.89 s) and ConsiStory (46.47 GB / 26.61 s). On DiverStory, where 1Prompt1Story cannot operate due to its fixed-template requirement, BIPE maintains the best CLIP-T and VQA scores, confirming robustness to diverse prompt formulations. PhotoMaker and BLIP-Diffusion lag significantly on both alignment and consistency metrics across both benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

BIPE carves new ground within the consistent T2I generation landscape by repurposing a spectral decomposition idea (SVD) to operate purely in the text-embedding space, departing from both training-based personalization (DreamBooth, IP-Adapter) and memory-intensive training-free methods (StoryDiffusion, ConsiStory) that manipulate intermediate activations or require iterative clustering. Conceptually, it extends the observation from 1Prompt1Story that aggregated prompt context carries identity information, but instead of concatenating prompts and dynamically reweighting them, BIPE explicitly identifies and amplifies the shared identity component (IPemb) via adaptive singular-value rescaling at every encoder layer, then reinforces cross-frame alignment through a Union Key attention mechanism. The approach is architecture-agnostic and training-free, positioning it as a lightweight plug-and-play module rather than a new model or a fine-tuning recipe.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in demonstrating that subject-consistent generation can be achieved with negligible memory overhead and near-base-model inference speed, making it scalable to long-form stories (50+ images) and cross-video consistency—settings where prior methods become infeasible. The DiverStory benchmark exposes a real gap: all existing methods degrade substantially when prompts deviate from a fixed template, suggesting that current consistency techniques still conflate identity with contextual structure. Key limitations remain: BIPE requires the full prompt sequence a priori and cannot accept an explicit reference image or external identity embedding, restricting its use in interactive or reference-driven workflows. Whether the IPemb concept generalizes beyond CLIP-based encoders to more complex multi-encoder architectures (e.g., SD3's CLIP+T5) without per-encoder tuning is an open question the paper only partially addresses.
