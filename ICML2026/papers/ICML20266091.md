## Problem

Existing Scene Graph Generation models treat visual relationships as strictly unidirectional mappings (subject → object), inheriting an annotation bias that anchors descriptions on the subject and ignores the reciprocal perspective. When the reasoning anchor shifts from subject to object, these models produce semantically collapsed or contradictory predictions, violating the physical reality that visual interactions are inherently reciprocal. The paper formalizes this gap as the Mutual-Perspective Inverse Relations (MPIR) principle and asks how to enforce logical consistency across both perspectives without altering backbone architectures.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image and produces scene graph triplets (subject, predicate, object) under the PredCls protocol on Visual Genome. Unusually, evaluation also includes a constructed inverse query set where subject and object are swapped and the model must predict the logically inverse predicate (e.g., 'held by' instead of 'holding'), enabling a dedicated inverse-consistency metric (IR@K, MI@K) that standard R@K/mR@K cannot capture. For CLIP-based variants, the text-side input is a prompt constructed from learnable context vectors concatenated with hypernym embeddings of the subject and object rather than raw fine-grained entity labels.

## Method

The RPC framework operationalizes MPIR through a data-centric Adaptive Inverse-Relation Augmentation (AIRA) strategy that deploys two tailored protocols: Additive Injection (appending inverse triplets to the training set) for low-frequency/semantic predicates, and Stochastic Replacement (Bernoulli-sampled swapping with probability λ=0.5) for high-frequency/geometric predicates, preventing overfitting to positional biases. For open-set CLIP architectures, Hypernym-Guided Prompts (HGP) resolve the combinatorial explosion of full-triplet prompting by using an LLM-distilled projection Ψ that maps 150 fine-grained entity categories into 15 functional hypernyms, reducing the prompt search space while retaining subject-object directional cues. The inverse mapping Φ: p→p⁻¹ is instantiated via structured few-shot LLM prompting with a four-category taxonomy (Symmetric, Semantic, Geometric, Possessive), and the entire pipeline is plug-and-play, requiring no architectural modifications to the host model. Training uses standard cross-entropy loss over the augmented dataset, with the visual encoder frozen and only learnable context vectors and lightweight adaptation layers updated.

## Results

On Visual Genome (PredCls protocol), the best standard-metric result is PE-Net + RPC*_Add achieving M@100 = 51.2% (R@100 = 59.6%, mR@100 = 42.9%), closely matching the SOTA GSGG (M@100 = 51.7%). For open-set CLIP variants, CLIP_M + RPC_Add + HGP reaches M@100 = 45.7% and mR@100 = 22.8%, surpassing IETrans (45.2%) and ST-SGG (44.8%). On the proposed inverse consistency metric, CLIP_M + RPC_Add + HGP achieves MI@100 = 36.4%, a dramatic improvement over the uncalibrated Motifs baseline (MI@100 = 4.4%) and all other compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PE-Net + RPC*_Add (M@100 = 51.2%) closely matches GSGG (51.7%) and clearly outperforms CooK (49.8%) and VETO (49.5%) on the balanced M@K metric, while also surpassing them on mR@100 (42.9% vs. 45.0% for GSGG, 37.2% for CooK, 35.1% for VETO). In the open-set CLIP setting, CLIP_M + RPC_Add + HGP (M@100 = 45.7%) edges out IETrans (45.2%) and ST-SGG (44.8%) but remains slightly below MLLMP (46.5%). On the proposed inverse consistency metric (MI@100), the gap is far more pronounced: CLIP_M + RPC_Add + HGP achieves 36.4%, compared to 31.7% for PE-Net + RPC*_Add, 27.3% for uncalibrated PE-Net, and only 4.4% for uncalibrated Motifs—demonstrating that standard baselines suffer severe inverse collapse. The RPC module consistently improves mR@K across all three closed-set backbones (e.g., Motifs mR@100: 16.1% → 19.7%), confirming its effectiveness as a plug-and-play calibration that mitigates long-tail imbalance even under standard evaluation that ignores inverse relations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the standard SGG pipeline (Motifs, VCTree, PE-Net) and CLIP-based prompt learning (HP, Zhu et al. 2024) by identifying a fundamental cognitive deficiency: the unidirectional s→o bias that treats predicates as one-way labels rather than reciprocal physical facts. It borrows the logical reciprocity principle (r(x,y) ⟺ r⁻¹(y,x)) from Knowledge Graph embeddings (Kazemi & Poole, 2018; Sun et al., 2019) and transplants it into visual SGG for the first time, operationalizing it through a data-centric augmentation strategy (AIRA) and a hypernym-abstraction prompt design (HGP) that resolves the combinatorial explosion problem identified in prior prompt-learning SGG work. The framework is explicitly positioned as a model-agnostic, plug-and-play calibration module rather than a new backbone architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The inverse consistency evaluation protocol (IR@K, MI@K) exposes a previously hidden failure mode: even strong SGG models collapse to near-chance performance (~4–7% MI@100) when queried from the object's perspective, revealing that standard R@K metrics mask a fundamental logical inconsistency. The finding that VLM semantic priors (CLIP) are decisive for reciprocal reasoning while structural priors (PE-Net) only aid long-tail distribution suggests a useful division of labor for future SGG architectures. However, absolute inverse metrics remain very low (best ~36% MI@100), evaluation is confined to Visual Genome under PredCls, and the framework's reliance on LLM-generated inverse mappings introduces a quality bottleneck that limits scalability to open-vocabulary or 3D/video settings the authors themselves flag as future work.
