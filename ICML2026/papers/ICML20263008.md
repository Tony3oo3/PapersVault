## Problem

Gradient descent optimizers (SGD, Adam, AdamW) are inherently autoregressive: each parameter update depends on the previous step's weights, creating a rigid sequential chain. All existing parallelization strategies—data, model, and pipeline parallelism—only parallelize operations within a single GD step, leaving the inter-step dependency intact and the total iteration count unchanged. This paper asks whether the sequential dependency across GD steps can be eliminated entirely to compute gradients for multiple steps concurrently without sacrificing model quality.

## Input / Output

The method takes the same inputs as standard sequential GD (initial weights w_0, a sequence of T mini-batches, a learning rate schedule) plus two additional hyperparameters: a sliding window size p (number of steps computed in parallel) and a convergence tolerance δ with EMA decay rate λ. A step-based deterministic seeding mechanism ensures that regardless of which GPU or parallel iteration processes step t, the mini-batch ζ_t is identical to the sequential regime. The output is the trained model weight w_T, functionally equivalent to the sequential GD trajectory, with each GPU maintaining only one model copy and one optimizer state (no additional per-device memory).

## Method

PASO recasts the autoregressive GD trajectory as a system of T+1 triangular nonlinear equations (TNEs) in which all weights w_0, …, w_T are treated as mutually independent unknowns, thereby severing the sequential Markov dependency. The TNE system is proven to have a unique solution matching the sequential GD trajectory (Prop. 3.3), and is solved via fixed-point iteration where all p equations within a sliding window are updated simultaneously in each parallel iteration k. A sliding window of size p limits concurrent computation to p sub-equations per iteration, with the window advancing by a stride determined by which equations have converged (L2-norm change below an adaptively updated EMA threshold δ). Convergence to the exact sequential trajectory is guaranteed in at most T parallel iterations (Prop. 3.4), though empirically K ≪ T in practice. This step-level parallelism is orthogonal to and composable with existing intra-step parallelism (data, model, pipeline), and the framework generalizes to any GD-based optimizer whose update rule can be expressed as a finite-memory function g_τ.

## Results

On WikiText-2, PASO reduces GD iterations by up to 20.8× (1000→48 steps for AdamW on GPT-2) while maintaining near-identical perplexity (20.6 vs 20.4), yielding a 4.4× wall-clock speedup. On CIFAR-10, PASO+Adam achieves 4.3× speedup on ViT (59,709s→13,958s) with comparable top-1 accuracy (77.9% vs 77.7%). On the LSUN Church diffusion task, PASO+Adam reaches FID 9.55 in 136k iterations (2.46 days) versus 600k iterations (7.39 days) for sequential Adam (FID 9.08), a 3.0× speedup with identical peak GPU memory (78.6G).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against sequential SGD, Adam, and AdamW, PASO consistently reduces iteration counts by 10×–21× on LLM tasks and 2.9×–4.4× on vision tasks while preserving model quality (PPL within ±0.3, accuracy within ±0.5%). Against industry-grade data parallelism (PyTorch DDP and DeepSpeed), the unoptimized PASO prototype matches their 4.4× wall-clock speedup on GPT-2, though DeepSpeed achieves a slightly lower PPL (19.2 vs 20.6) attributable to its FusedAdam kernels and ZeRO-3 memory optimization. Against OptEx (Shu et al., 2024), which estimates future model states via kernelized gradients, PASO outperforms it both theoretically and experimentally (App. H), as OptEx's approximate parallelization leads to lower accuracy and limited scalability. The paper also demonstrates that PASO's step-level parallelism is orthogonal to and complementary with existing intra-step parallelism (data, model, pipeline), suggesting combined use could push speedups toward the 21× step-reduction upper bound.

## Relation to Prior Work

PASO departs fundamentally from all existing parallel training paradigms (data, model, and pipeline parallelism) by parallelizing across GD steps rather than within a single step, thereby severing the inter-step sequential dependency that all prior methods preserve. Conceptually, it draws on the parallel nonlinear equation solving literature (Song et al., 2021; Shih et al., 2024; Lu et al., 2025) and the Parareal family of parallel-in-time solvers for ODEs, but applies this to the discrete autoregressive GD trajectory via a triangular nonlinear equation (TNE) formulation solved by fixed-point iteration. The paper explicitly positions itself as orthogonal to and complementary with intra-step parallelism, and distinguishes from OptEx (Shu et al., 2024), which estimates future models via kernelized gradients but suffers from accuracy loss and poor scalability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PASO introduces a genuinely new axis of parallelism (step-level) that is multiplicative with existing intra-step methods, suggesting that combining it with ZeRO, DeepSpeed, or pipeline parallelism could yield substantially greater end-to-end acceleration than either alone. The current 4.5× wall-clock speedup significantly underestimates the 21× step-level reduction due to the research prototype's CPU-mediated communication and limited 8-GPU setup, leaving substantial engineering headroom. Key open questions include scalability to hundreds of GPUs, integration with advanced solvers (Anderson acceleration, multigrid), and whether the fixed-point iteration's convergence properties hold under the stochastic noise of very large-batch or federated settings.
