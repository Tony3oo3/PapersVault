## Problem

Prior social choice analyses of RLHF established that standard Bradley-Terry-based RLHF implicitly implements the Borda count rule, while newer methods implement the von Neumann winner (maximal lotteries) rule. However, these analyses entirely ignored the KL-divergence regularizer to a reference policy that is present in essentially all practical RLHF pipelines. This paper asks how regularization affects the social choice axioms (strategy-proofness, IIA, population consistency, Pareto optimality) satisfied by these alignment rules.

## Input / Output

The framework takes as input a finite set of n human raters, each providing a total order (preference ranking) over a countable set of alternatives (e.g., all grammatically valid text), plus a reference policy distribution μ over those alternatives. The output is a unique probability distribution π* over the alternatives that is maximal under the regularized probabilistic preference relation, i.e., the policy the aligned model should sample from. The setting is explicitly probabilistic social choice: the rule outputs a distribution over candidates rather than a single deterministic winner, which is natural for LLMs that already produce distributions over responses.

## Method

The paper formalizes alignment as probabilistic social choice with a KL-regularized pairwise preference relation P̃(π,π') = P_H(π,π') − τ·D_KL(π‖μ) + τ·D_KL(π'‖μ), then proves a stability theorem showing that bounded perturbations to the preference matrix shift the maximal distribution by at most (1/τ)·‖∇H‖_∞ in L1 norm, leveraging the 1-strong convexity of KL-divergence in its first argument. Using this stability result, the paper proves the KL-regularized von Neumann winner is (k, 2k/(τn))-approximately strategy-proof, while constructing an explicit rock-paper-scissors-style counterexample showing the unregularized rule admits constant (4/15) strategic gain from a single rater regardless of n. The paper further proves the regularized von Neumann winner retains IIA, population consistency, and a regularized form of Pareto optimality, while showing the regularized Borda count (equivalent to standard RLHF) still violates ε-approximate IIA for any ε < 1/2. Experiments with Nash MD and SPO algorithms on a 3-alternative, 64-rater synthetic instance confirm the theoretical gap in strategic vulnerability.

## Results

The paper's main quantitative result (Theorem 5.4) proves that the KL-regularized von Neumann winner is (k, ε)-approximately strategy-proof with ε = 2k/(τn), meaning k strategically misreporting raters can shift the outcome by at most 2k/(τn) in total variation distance. In contrast, Theorem 5.5 shows the unregularized von Neumann winner is not (1, ε)-strategy-proof for any ε < 4/15, i.e., a single rater can gain a constant 4/15 advantage regardless of n. For the Borda count (standard RLHF), Proposition 6.1 proves the regularized rule fails ε-approximate independence of irrelevant alternatives for any ε < 1/2. A synthetic experiment (n=64 raters, 3 alternatives, τ=0.1) confirms that strategic manipulation by one rater causes negligible policy shift under regularized vNw (Nash MD) but a large shift under unregularized vNw (SPO).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the KL-regularized von Neumann winner against two baselines. Against the unregularized von Neumann winner (implemented via SPO, Swamy et al. 2024), the regularized version wins decisively on strategy-proofness: the unregularized rule allows a single rater to gain a constant 4/15 advantage (Theorem 5.5), while the regularized rule bounds any k-rater manipulation to 2k/(τn) (Theorem 5.4). In the synthetic experiment (Figure 1, n=64, τ=0.1), a single strategic rater causes a dramatic increase in probability of their preferred alternative under SPO, while Nash MD (regularized vNw) shows only a minor perturbation. Against the regularized Borda count (which corresponds to standard Bradley-Terry RLHF), the regularized vNw wins on independence of irrelevant alternatives: the Borda count fails ε-IIA for any ε < 1/2 (Proposition 6.1), while the regularized vNw satisfies IIA exactly (Proposition 5.6). Both rules satisfy population consistency, but the regularized vNw additionally satisfies regularized Pareto optimality, giving it a strict axiomatic advantage over standard RLHF.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the recent line of work connecting RLHF to social choice theory (Siththaranjan et al., 2024; Ge et al., 2024) by incorporating the KL-divergence regularizer that is present in essentially all practical RLHF pipelines but was entirely absent from prior axiomatic analyses. It builds on the probabilistic social choice framework of Brandl & Brandt (2020), which showed that maximal lotteries (equivalent to the von Neumann winner) uniquely satisfy Arrow's axioms without dictatorship, and on the practical algorithms for optimizing the von Neumann winner (Nash MD, SPO, DNO). The key departure from prior work is treating regularization not as a mere implementation detail but as a first-class component that reshapes which social choice axioms are satisfied.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, axiomatic argument for preferring von Neumann winner–based alignment methods (Nash MD, SPO) over standard Bradley-Terry RLHF, which implicitly implements the Borda count and provably violates independence of irrelevant alternatives even under regularization. The counterintuitive finding that KL-regularization improves strategy-proofness (from constant to O(1/n) manipulation) gives practitioners a theoretical justification for the regularization they already use. Open questions include whether additional assumptions on the reference policy can recover full (unregularized) Pareto efficiency, and whether other probabilistic social choice rules interact positively with KL-regularization to yield even stronger axiomatic guarantees.
