## Problem

Existing multimodal benchmarks such as MMMU conflate reasoning ability with domain-specific knowledge, making it impossible to isolate and evaluate a model's pure reasoning capability in non-expert settings. This paper asks whether multimodal reasoning can be measured independently of pretrained factual knowledge, and whether strong performance on knowledge-heavy benchmarks actually transfers to knowledge-light reasoning tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264221/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each VISUALPUZZLES instance pairs an RGB image with a text question and four answer options (57% image-based, 43% text-based), using basic English vocabulary (54% easy words, avg. 154.9 words) to minimize linguistic confounds. The model outputs a single multiple-choice answer, and evaluation reports accuracy disaggregated across five reasoning categories (algorithmic, analogical, deductive, inductive, spatial) and three difficulty tiers (easy 46%, medium 39%, hard 15%). Both direct multiple-choice prompting and Chain-of-Thought prompting are applied, with the best of the two reported per model.

## Method

VISUALPUZZLES is constructed via a multi-stage curation pipeline: questions are sourced from online puzzle resources, synthesized vision-text pairs, repurposed benchmark items, and—most notably—manually translated logical reasoning questions from the Chinese Civil Service Examination. All items are adapted into a uniform four-option multiple-choice format and filtered through strict criteria that eliminate any question requiring advanced mathematics or specialized domain facts, retaining only items solvable from the image, prompt, and basic common sense. Each question is then annotated with a reasoning category and difficulty level by human annotators. To validate the knowledge-light design, the authors use GPT-4o to generate 'knowledge concept checklists' per question, showing VISUALPUZZLES requires only 1.1 knowledge items on average versus 3.9 for MMMU, and that models already achieve >90% knowledge accuracy on VISUALPUZZLES. Reasoning complexity is quantified by classifying solution steps, revealing 82.1% logical-reasoning steps on VISUALPUZZLES versus 71.5% on MMMU, confirming higher inferential demand despite lower knowledge load.

## Results

On VISUALPUZZLES (1,168 questions across 5 reasoning categories), the best-performing model o4-mini achieves 57.0% overall accuracy, still below the 5th-percentile human performance of 57.5%. The human 50th percentile reaches 75.0% and the 95th percentile reaches 89.3%, highlighting a substantial gap. VISUALPUZZLES requires significantly less domain knowledge than MMMU (1.1 vs. 3.9 average knowledge-concept questions per instance) yet demands more logical reasoning (82.1% vs. 71.5% of solution steps are logical reasoning steps). No clear correlation exists between model size and performance, and 'thinking' modes yield inconsistent gains (e.g., Claude-4.5-Sonnet improves only 1% with extended thinking).

## Baselines & Comparisons

All 20+ evaluated MLLMs (proprietary and open) fall below the 5th-percentile human accuracy of 57.5%, with the best model o4-mini reaching only 57.0%. Compared to MMMU, where GPT-4o scores 75.1%, the same model drops to 41.3% on VISUALPUZZLES, confirming that knowledge-heavy benchmark performance does not transfer to knowledge-light reasoning. Reasoning models show inconsistent advantages: o1 (51.8%) outperforms GPT-4o (41.3%), but Gemini-2.0-flash-thinking (42.2%) underperforms its base Gemini-2.0-flash (45.0%), and Claude-4.5-Sonnet-Thinking gains only 1% over its non-thinking counterpart. Scaling model size yields no consistent improvement—Qwen2.5-VL-72B (42.3%) outperforms Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B (50.7%) in the opposite direction, and Llama-3.2-90B (34.1%) trails Llama-3.2-11B (29.4%) by only ~5 points. The correlation between knowledge accuracy and reasoning accuracy is 0.8 on MMMU but only 0.4 on VISUALPUZZLES, indicating that VISUALPUZZLES isolates reasoning from knowledge recall.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by explicitly designing a multimodal reasoning benchmark that minimizes domain-specific knowledge, in contrast to existing benchmarks like MMMU and MathVista that conflate reasoning with factual recall. It repurposes logical reasoning questions from the Chinese Civil Service Examination and puzzle-based sources, organizing them into five reasoning categories (algorithmic, analogical, deductive, inductive, spatial) drawn from cognitive science literature. The evaluation framework builds on standard MLLM prompting strategies (direct multiple-choice and chain-of-thought) and extends the analysis to 'thinking' mode variants, but the core contribution is the benchmark design principle of knowledge-light, reasoning-heavy evaluation rather than a new model or training method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that all evaluated state-of-the-art MLLMs (including the latest 2025 proprietary models) fall below the 5th percentile of human performance on a knowledge-light reasoning benchmark is a stark diagnostic: it suggests that current progress in multimodal AI is substantially driven by knowledge acquisition rather than genuine reasoning. The observation that thinking modes and model scaling do not reliably improve performance on VISUALPUZZLES challenges the prevailing assumption that more inference compute or more parameters will close the reasoning gap. Open questions include whether the puzzle-based format captures the full breadth of multimodal reasoning, whether the 70-participant human study provides a stable baseline, and what architectural or training changes would actually improve knowledge-agnostic reasoning.
