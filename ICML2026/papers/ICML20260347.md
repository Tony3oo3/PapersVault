## Problem

Deeper models achieve higher accuracy but incur proportionally higher training cost, and existing progressive training (model expansion) methods only scale depth by 2–4× while reducing compute to 55–70%. The paper identifies three underexplored gaps: (1) no prior work has studied expanding from a zero- or one-layer model to very deep models (up to 60×), (2) the critical role of learning rate schedule (especially WSD) in enabling late-stage expansion has been overlooked, and (3) convergence theory for progressive training under non-smooth convex losses is largely absent.

## Input / Output

The method starts from a zero-layer model (embedding + output head only, ~39M params) or a one-layer model (one transformer block, ~46M params) and trains it on standard pretraining corpora (OpenWebText at 1024 sequence length for LLMs; ImageNet at 224×224 for ResNet). At a predetermined iteration (typically 80% of total training, during the stable phase of the WSD schedule), the model's depth is expanded to the target size (e.g., 60 layers / 7B params) by appending randomly initialized layers at the bottom. The output is a fully trained deep model whose validation loss is within 0.2–0.5% of a model trained at full size from scratch, while consuming only ~20% of the FLOPs.

## Method

The core procedure is: (1) train a zero- or one-layer model with Muon-NSGD under a WSD (warmup-stable-decay) schedule using a muP-scaled learning rate that transfers zero-shot across model sizes; (2) at the expansion point τ ≈ 0.8T, append new layers initialized randomly (or by copying the existing layer for one-layer expansion) at the bottom of the stack, which satisfies the muP spectral scaling condition and preserves feature learning; (3) continue training with the same hyperparameters and schedule without any reset. The theoretical contribution frames progressive training as projected gradient descent (masking new layers) followed by an instantaneous 'teleportation' of new-layer parameters to a good initialization, then standard SGD, yielding a convergence bound under convex G-Lipschitz loss that explains why WSD (constant LR during the stable phase) makes the mixing time robust to expansion timing, and why single-stage expansion from zero/one layers is Pareto-optimal over multi-stage or deeper-source strategies.

## Results

Zero/one-layer progressive training on GPT2 (60-layer, 7B parameters) saves ≈80% compute (≈5× acceleration) while achieving a final validation loss within <0.2% of the fully trained fixed-size model. On 124M GPT2, the loss gap is <0.5%. Scaling laws on LLAMA3 (dense, 0.25B–2B) and DeepSeekV3 (MoE, 0.2B–0.5B active) demonstrate a 3–5× improvement in compute efficiency over fixed-size training, with the advantage growing at larger scales. The mixing time (time for progressive training loss to converge to fixed-size training loss) is approximately 40k iterations (~16B tokens) and is robust to the expansion timing τ during the WSD stable phase.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is fixed-size training (training the full-depth model from scratch for the same total iterations). Zero/one-layer progressive training matches fixed-size training to within <0.5% (124M) and <0.2% (7B) validation loss while using only ~20% of the FLOPs, a dramatic improvement over prior progressive training methods that achieved only 55–70% compute reduction at 2–4× depth expansion. Against previous progressive training works (e.g., BERT2BERT, Progressive Stacking, LLaMA Pro), this paper scales depth by up to 60× (vs. 2–4×) and reduces compute to 20% (vs. 55–70%). On learning rate schedule, WSD is critical: under cosine schedule, GPT progressive training fails to mix with fixed-size training when expansion occurs at τ≥0.5T, whereas WSD tolerates expansion as late as τ≈0.8T. Single-stage expansion from zero/one-layer is shown to be Pareto-optimal in the loss-compute tradeoff, outperforming multi-stage expansion (e.g., 0→2→12) which incurs nearly the same FLOPs as 2-layer progressive training but with worse final loss than 0-layer progressive training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the progressive training / model expansion lineage (Net2Net, BERT2BERT, progressively stacking, LLaMA-Pro) by pushing depth expansion from 2–4× to 60×, starting from zero- or one-layer source models rather than multi-layer ones. It departs from the function-preserving initialization paradigm (which guarantees no loss spike but blocks feature learning) in favor of random or copying initialization justified via muP's spectral scaling condition, enabling zero-shot hyperparameter transfer across the expansion. The convergence analysis under convex, Lipschitz (non-smooth) loss generalizes the only prior theory (Agarwal et al., 2024) which assumed strong convexity and smoothness, and reframes progressive training as projected gradient descent followed by an 'instant teleportation' of new-layer parameters. The paper also identifies WSD learning rate schedules as a critical enabler, in contrast to the cosine schedules used in most prior progressive training work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

A ~5× compute reduction (or ~80% FLOP savings) with <0.5% loss degradation is a substantial practical win for anyone pretraining large language models, and the scaling-law validation on LLAMA3 and DeepSeekV3 suggests the advantage grows at larger scales. The 'mixing behavior' insight—that progressive training loss converges to fixed-size training loss within a bounded, transferable mixing time—reframes how practitioners should think about the timing and staging of model expansion, and the finding that single-stage expansion from zero/one layers dominates multi-stage strategies simplifies the training recipe considerably. Open questions include whether the approach extends to simultaneous width-and-depth expansion, how mixing time scales with model size at truly frontier scales (100B+), and whether the convex-optimization-based theory transfers quantitatively to the non-convex regime of real transformer training.
