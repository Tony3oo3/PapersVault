## Problem

LLM-based multi-agent planners typically produce a single joint plan per timestep without systematic revision, counterfactual exploration, or structured learning from past failures. In partially observable embodied environments, this one-shot approach fails to capture physical feasibility constraints and inter-agent coordination dependencies, leading to brittle collaboration that cannot recover from early mistakes or adapt to spatial conflicts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260706/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each timestep, EvoCF receives a natural-language subtask instruction, per-agent egocentric RGB observations from the AI2-THOR simulator (partial observability), and the current joint action plan. The output is a selected joint action plan drawn from a fixed 14-action robot vocabulary (navigate, rotate, pick up, put, open, close, slice, toggle, clean, look up/down, move, idle), one action per agent, accompanied by a ranked list of counterfactual candidates with natural-language reasoning. Additionally, the system maintains an evolving library of symbolic constraint rules (e.g., 'NavigateTo(?Object) → Avoid(CollisionWith(?Agent))') that persist across episodes and tasks.

## Method

EvoCF extends the LLaMAR modular planning pipeline with three coupled modules operating in a store–consolidate–retrieve loop over structured agentic memory. First, a Symbolic Constraint Inductor maps failure-annotated transitions into typed symbolic rules partitioned into task-dependency constraints (action validity, object reachability, spatial feasibility) and multi-agent coordination constraints (temporal consistency, load balance, goal alignment), which accumulate online via deduplication. Second, an Evolutionary Counterfactual Plan Generator represents each joint plan as a gene sequence (one gene per agent's action) and applies four discrete mutation operators—SwapAgent, InsertAction, DeleteAction, ReplaceObject—guided by compositionally retrieved constraints and outcome traces to produce diverse yet semantically consistent counterfactual variants. Third, an Agentic Memory-Grounded Evaluator assembles transition-level evidence via compositional embedding-based retrieval over the structured memory, then ranks all candidates (including the original plan) through a constraint-aware reasoning function that weighs rule satisfaction, past outcome patterns, and coordination quality without forward-simulating environment dynamics. The key architectural insight is that counterfactual search is neither blind mutation nor single-pass LLM generation, but a memory-conditioned evolutionary process where symbolic rules extracted from failures simultaneously constrain mutation and inform evaluation.

## Results

On the 2-agent MAP-THOR benchmark (45 tasks × 5 room layouts), EvoCF achieves a success rate of 0.84, transport rate of 0.95, coverage of 0.99, balance of 0.89, and average steps of 18.69. This represents a 16.7% relative improvement in success rate over the strongest baseline LLaMAR (0.72), while also reducing planning steps by ~10% (18.69 vs. 20.63). EvoCF outperforms all other baselines (ReAct, CoT, SmartLLM, CoELA) by large margins on every reported metric.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EvoCF wins on all five metrics against every baseline. Against the closest competitor LLaMAR (Nayak et al., NeurIPS 2024), EvoCF improves success rate from 0.72 to 0.84 (+16.7% relative), transport rate from 0.93 to 0.95, coverage from 0.97 to 0.99, balance from 0.85 to 0.89, and reduces average steps from 20.63 to 18.69. Against ReAct (Yao et al., ICLR 2023), EvoCF more than doubles the success rate (0.84 vs. 0.34) and cuts steps by 22%. Against CoT (Wei et al., NeurIPS 2022), the gap is even wider: 0.84 vs. 0.14 SR and 18.69 vs. 28.40 steps. Against SmartLLM (Kannan et al., IROS 2024), EvoCF achieves 7.6× higher success rate (0.84 vs. 0.11) and 4.1× higher transport rate (0.95 vs. 0.23). Against CoELA (Zhang et al., ICLR 2024), EvoCF improves SR by 3.4× (0.84 vs. 0.25) and TR by 2.1× (0.95 vs. 0.46), while also achieving higher coverage (0.99 vs. 0.76) and fewer steps (18.69 vs. 28.93).

## Relation to Prior Work

EvoCF extends the LLaMAR modular multi-agent planning pipeline (Nayak et al., 2024a) by inserting three new modules—symbolic constraint induction, evolutionary counterfactual generation, and memory-grounded evaluation—into the existing decompose-allocate-execute loop. Conceptually, it bridges three previously separate threads: (i) LLM-based multi-agent planners that produce single-shot plans without systematic revision (SMART-LLM, CoELA, MacNet), (ii) agentic memory systems that store and retrieve experience (AMEM, AgeMem, Voyager), and (iii) counterfactual reasoning in LLMs (Tree-of-Thoughts, Reflexion, COPPER), which has so far been applied only to single-agent self-reflection or debate. The key departure is treating multi-agent plan improvement as an explicit evolutionary search over counterfactual joint-action variants, guided by an evolving library of symbolic constraints distilled from past failures, rather than relying on heuristic self-correction or centralized controllers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and underexplored gap: current LLM-based multi-agent planners rarely explore alternative task assignments or counterfactual replanning, leaving them vulnerable to early coordination mistakes that cascade. The 43% counterfactual replacement rate and the cross-task transferability of induced constraints (Table 6) suggest that failure-grounded symbolic rules encode genuinely reusable physical and coordination knowledge, which could support continual learning in deployed multi-robot systems. However, evaluation is confined to single-room MAP-THOR scenarios with at most four agents and a fixed 30-step budget, so it remains open whether the approach scales to larger teams, multi-room layouts, or real-world deployment where partial observability and communication delays are more severe. The paper itself flags future work on subtask-level counterfactual edits and deliberative self-reflection, indicating the current action-level mutations are a first step.
