## Problem

Prior analyses of induction heads in transformers characterize them only in the hard-attention limit, where attention selects exact context matches and the predictor reduces to unregularized maximum-likelihood counting. This counting view is statistically incomplete: for high-order dependencies, exact k-gram matches are too rare to support reliable prediction, and the ML estimator assigns zero mass to unseen continuations. The paper asks whether induction heads implement a richer class of smoothed estimators beyond strict counting.

## Input / Output

The model receives a token sequence x_1^T generated by a latent order-k Markov chain (vocabulary size |V|, with k=2 and |V|=5 in experiments) and must predict the next token x_{T+1} from the observed history alone, with no parameter updates. The latent transition rule π is drawn from either an independent Dirichlet prior (where add-α smoothing is Bayes-optimal) or a hierarchical Dirichlet prior (where lower-order suffixes provide structured evidence about higher-order contexts). The output is a probability distribution over V, evaluated by KL divergence to the ground-truth transition distribution.

## Method

The paper constructs a two-layer disentangled transformer where Layer 1 uses k copy heads (via relative positional encoding) to build representations encoding the current token and its k predecessors, and Layer 2 uses a single attention head with a block-shifted weight matrix W_A = S ⊗ I_|V| that computes a shift-aligned inner product scoring each candidate context by its position-wise overlap with the query. At finite attention-weight scale β, the resulting estimator aggregates successor tokens from all partial and exact context matches with weights e^{β|M|}, inducing a data-dependent interpolation across context orders analogous to Jelinek-Mercer smoothing. A BOS token with a fixed uniform representation acts as a sequence-independent attention sink, contributing an additive pseudo-count term e^κ/|V| that recovers add-α smoothing in the large-β limit. The paper proves these results constructively (Proposition 3.1), derives the equivalence to sub-n-gram interpolation via inclusion-exclusion (Lemma 4.1), and validates the predicted attention patterns on both disentangled and fully standard trained transformers.

## Results

The paper's main empirical results are presented as KL-divergence-to-ground-truth curves (Figure 2) rather than a single numerical table. Under the independent Dirichlet prior (α=1, k=2, |V|=5), the BOS construction at large β achieves near-Bayes-optimal KL across all sequence lengths, while the adaptive-β interpolation (Lemma 4.2) also approaches the Bayes-optimal curve, both substantially outperforming the MLE counting estimator at short sequences. Under the hierarchical Dirichlet prior (η₁=η₂=5.0), all three model families (disentangled construction, disentangled trained, standard transformer) track each other closely and outperform every fixed add-α baseline tested. Trained transformers (both disentangled and standard) recover the predicted attention patterns, confirming the theoretical construction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three model families (disentangled construction with analytic weights, disentangled trained, and a fully standard two-layer transformer with d_model=64, LayerNorm, and MLP) against classical n-gram baselines: MLE counting (hard attention), add-α smoothing for various α values, and the Bayes-optimal predictor. Under the independent Dirichlet prior where add-α smoothing is Bayes-optimal, the BOS construction matches the Bayes-optimal predictor at large β, and the adaptive-β interpolation also approaches it, while MLE counting (large β, no BOS) shows high KL at short sequences due to sparse counts. Under the hierarchical Dirichlet prior where add-α is suboptimal, all three transformer variants substantially outperform every fixed add-α baseline (shown as dashed lines in Figure 2), with the minimal construction (only two trainable scalars β₁, β₂) matching the fully trained models. The standard transformer recovers the same two-stage induction-head circuit (lag-specific copies in Layer 1, soft context matching in Layer 2), confirming the mechanism is not an artifact of the disentangled parameterization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the induction-head literature for Markov chains (Bietti et al., 2023; Edelman et al., 2024; Nichani et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2024; Ekbote et al., 2026), which has characterized the circuit in the hard-attention limit as maximum-likelihood n-gram counting. The key departure is analyzing the circuit at finite attention-weight scale, where the same two-layer architecture implements a soft context-matching estimator with data-dependent interpolation across context orders, and showing that the BOS token provides additive pseudo-counts. These mechanisms are then explicitly connected to classical n-gram smoothing techniques (Jelinek-Mercer interpolation, add-α smoothing, Katz back-off), bridging mechanistic interpretability with a well-studied statistical literature that the transformer-circuit community had not previously engaged with.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result reframes induction heads from unregularized counters to regularized estimators, suggesting that transformers learn to smooth in-context predictions rather than merely copy from exact matches — a distinction that matters for understanding why ICL works in sparse-data regimes. The circuit-level account of BOS as pseudo-counts gives a principled explanation for a common architectural convention. However, all results are on two-layer disentangled or standard transformers with k=2, |V|=5, and synthetic Markov sources; extending the analysis to deeper stacks, larger vocabularies, and natural text remains open, and it is unclear whether the specific interpolation structure (exponential in Hamming overlap) persists in more complex settings.
