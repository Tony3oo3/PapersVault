## Problem

Existing long-context approaches—positional interpolation, sparse/grouped attention, and recurrent state propagation—address efficiency or length extension but leave the organization of long-range information implicit, offering no explicit inductive bias for structuring context into a local-to-global hierarchy. This paper asks whether making that hierarchy explicit, motivated by cognitive Construction-Integration and Global Workspace theories, can improve long-context modeling quality beyond what sparsification or position scaling alone achieves.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265720/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text sequence of up to 100K tokens (LLaMA-2-7B), 64K (LLaMA-2-13B), or 48K (Qwen3-8B), partitioned into N fixed-length segments of size S (typically 2048–10240 tokens). It produces standard next-token predictions autoregressively; at inference the HiCI module is optional and can be applied only during prefill to reduce time-to-first-token latency, after which decoding reverts to standard full attention with a growing KV cache. The hierarchical context vectors (M=8 local slots, K=4 global slots) are fixed-capacity constants independent of sequence length.

## Method

HiCI inserts a three-stage hierarchical attention module into each Transformer layer: (1) Local Construction uses M=8 shared learnable query slots to perform bottleneck cross-attention (in a d_b=512 subspace) over each segment, distilling S tokens into a compact L_i ∈ R^{M×d}; (2) Global Integration collapses all N×M local vectors into five statistical summaries (mean, max, min, std, ℓ₂-normalized mean), compresses them through a shared two-stage bottleneck (d→d_s→d_b), and applies K=4 learnable queries via cross-attention to produce a position-invariant global context G ∈ R^{K×d}; (3) Top-down Broadcast prepends [G; L_i] to each segment's key-value sequence so that token-level attention jointly attends over K+M+S positions under a unified softmax, enabling cross-segment information flow while preserving segment-parallel computation. Training follows the LongLoRA recipe—freezing the backbone and updating only LoRA adapters, embeddings, normalization layers, and the HiCI module (~4–5% extra parameters)—combined with Position Interpolation for context extension, yielding O(T·S·d) time complexity per layer.

## Results

On PG-19 at 100K context (LLaMA-2-7B), HiCI achieves 6.95 perplexity vs. LongLoRA's 7.04, and on Proof-pile 2.43 vs. 2.52. For Qwen3-8B extended to 48K, HiCI reduces PG-19 perplexity by 14% (9.73 vs. 11.32 base) and Proof-pile by 8% (2.26 vs. 2.46). On LongBench, HiCI-7B-16K achieves 33.2% overall accuracy (+2.6% over LongLoRA) and surpasses GPT-3.5-Turbo-16K on Code comprehension (63.8% vs. 54.1%, +9.7%). HiCI attains 100% passkey retrieval accuracy within the 32K training regime and the best open-source topic-retrieval accuracy (0.94 at 16K).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HiCI consistently outperforms LongLoRA (its primary baseline) across all perplexity evaluations: at 100K context on LLaMA-2-7B, HiCI achieves 6.95 vs. 7.04 PG-19 PPL and 2.43 vs. 2.52 Proof-pile PPL, with larger gaps at shorter evaluation lengths (6.8% at 2K vs. 1.3% at 100K). On passkey retrieval, HiCI achieves 100% accuracy within the 32K training range while LongLoRA fluctuates between 80–100%, and under 56K extrapolation HiCI maintains 40–60% accuracy versus LongLoRA's 10–30%. On topic retrieval, HiCI-13B-16K reaches 0.94 at 16K, outperforming LongLoRA-13B-16K (0.86), LongChat-13B-16K (0.90), and all other open-source models (MPT-7B-StoryWriter-65K: 0.36; Llama-3-8B-Instruct-8K: 0.00), while matching GPT-4o-mini-128K and Claude-3.5-Sonnet-200K at 1.00 through 13K. On LongBench, HiCI-7B-16K surpasses GPT-3.5-Turbo-16K on Code (63.8% vs. 54.1%) and Summarization (24.6% vs. 23.9%), and outperforms LongLoRA-7B-16K overall (33.2% vs. 30.6%), with the largest gains on Single-Document QA (+7.4%) and Chinese tasks (+11.8%). The base LLaMA-2-7B model (4K native context) fails on passkey retrieval beyond 4K and shows >10² perplexity beyond 8K, confirming that HiCI's gains stem from the hierarchical mechanism rather than context extension alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

HiCI builds directly on LongLoRA's parameter-efficient recipe (LoRA adapters, position interpolation, FlashAttention-2, shifted sparse attention) but departs from it by replacing implicit cross-segment interaction via shifted grouping with an explicit three-stage hierarchical pipeline: local construction via bottleneck cross-attention, global integration via multi-view statistical pooling, and top-down broadcast by prepending hierarchical context to each segment's KV sequence. The design is motivated by Kintsch's Construction-Integration model of discourse comprehension and Baars' Global Workspace Theory, positioning HiCI as a cognitively grounded inductive bias rather than a purely efficiency-driven sparsification. It contrasts with recurrent/memory-augmented segment-based methods (Transformer-XL, RMT, Infini-attention) that sacrifice parallelism, and with hierarchical methods (HMT, Block Transformer) that use distinct summarization mechanisms, by maintaining full segment-parallelism while injecting learned global context as attention prefixes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicit hierarchical structuring (construction-integration-broadcast) provides a meaningful inductive bias for long-context modeling beyond what attention sparsification alone achieves, with the ablation showing global integration contributes nearly twice as much as local construction and the 'Only Group' baseline performing markedly worse. A key limitation is that HiCI is primarily a training-time mechanism: autoregressive decoding still relies on standard full attention with a growing KV cache, and extending the hierarchical conditioning to the decoding phase remains open. Whether the approach benefits models already pre-trained at very long contexts (128K+) is untested, and the paper itself flags transfer to vision-language models—where visual tokens dramatically increase sequence length—as a promising but unexplored direction.
