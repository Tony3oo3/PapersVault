## Problem

Generalist LLMs like GPT and Claude show impressive medical reasoning, raising the question of whether domain-specific specialist models are becoming obsolete. Existing approaches either pursue expensive monolithic medical foundation models or treat individual models as isolated tools, without exploring how generalist reasoning and specialist precision can be synergistically integrated. The paper argues that the future of medical AI lies in orchestrating collaboration among generalist LLMs, specialist models, and clinicians rather than building a single all-purpose medical model.

## Input / Output

The system takes a patient case comprising structured clinical information (age, gender, chronic disease history, treatment history, symptoms) and multimodal examination data: echocardiography (ECHO) reports as free/semi-structured text and 12-lead ECG images standardized to 224×224 RGB. Specialist models convert each modality into standardized diagnostic text paired with a generation confidence score (geometric mean of per-token softmax probabilities). The final output is a structured clinical decision (admission risk, etiology, or severity classification) accompanied by an explicit three-step reasoning chain, with a composite uncertainty score determining whether the recommendation is issued autonomously or escalated to clinician review.

## Method

HetMedAgent orchestrates three agent types through a structured protocol: a generalist LLM (GPT-4o) serves as both orchestrator (task parsing, dynamic specialist activation via a modality-to-agent mapping) and reasoning agent (evidence synthesis and decision generation); Transformer-based specialist models (Encoder-Session-Decoder for ECHO text-to-text, CNN+Transformer for ECG image-to-text) produce modality-specific findings with confidence scores; and a clinician agent provides oversight and adaptive threshold calibration. The key technical contribution is a conflict-aware evidence fusion mechanism that projects specialist findings into a PubMedBERT embedding space, computes pairwise semantic conflict scores, and derives integration weights via softmax(log c_i + log(1−δ_i)), which are injected as prompt-level annotations rather than product-of-experts fusion (since natural-language findings across complementary modalities lack a shared calibrated label space). A three-component composite uncertainty score (confidence gap, mean cross-specialist conflict, reasoning-chain coherence) triggers clinician escalation when it exceeds a threshold θ_P, which is adaptively calibrated via a simple update rule based on whether the clinician modifies the decision. Specialist models are trained with supervised cross-entropy loss and label smoothing, while the generalist LLM components operate via structured prompts without medical-specific fine-tuning.

## Results

On three cardiovascular clinical decision-making tasks (admission risk stratification, etiology prediction, severity assessment), HetMedAgent (w/o clinician) achieves 0.866 AUROC / 0.844 F1, 0.801 / 0.757, and 0.727 / 0.719 respectively. This represents average improvements of 6.6% AUROC and 7.9% F1 over the best single-model baseline (Meditron) and 4.3% AUROC and 5.7% F1 over the best multi-agent system (MedAgents). In cross-domain validation on IU X-Ray (chest radiology, 668 test cases), HetMedAgent achieves 0.820 AUROC and 0.537 F1 on an acute/non-acute task, outperforming a ViT-BERT multimodal fusion baseline (0.783 AUROC, 0.468 F1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HetMedAgent outperforms all six medical LLM baselines (PMC-LLaMA, Meditron, BioMistral, Llama-3-Meditron, DoctorGLM, GatorTron) across all three tasks and both metrics, with the largest gap over GatorTron (up to +11.3% AUROC on severity) and the smallest over Meditron (the strongest single-model baseline at 0.801 AUROC on risk stratification). Against the four multi-agent baselines (MedAgents, AgentClinic, AutoGen, MetaGPT), HetMedAgent also wins on every task, with MedAgents being the closest competitor (0.823 vs. 0.866 AUROC on risk stratification, a 4.3-point gap). The Transformer-based specialist models within HetMedAgent also outperform CNN-based specialist baselines (ResNet-50, EfficientNet-B0) on diagnostic text quality (BERTScore 0.800 vs. 0.707/0.748 for ECHO) while reducing inter-specialist conflict scores. In the cross-domain IU X-Ray experiment, HetMedAgent beats the ViT-BERT multimodal fusion baseline by 3.7% AUROC and 6.9% F1, with a clinically meaningful recall gain (0.761 vs. 0.716) for detecting acute cases in a 10%-positive-rate setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

HetMedAgent repurposes the multi-agent LLM orchestration paradigm (AutoGen, MetaGPT) and the MDT/tumor-board clinical workflow into a unified medical decision-making framework, adding three medical-specific mechanisms absent from prior multi-agent systems: conflict-aware evidence fusion via semantic embedding comparison, multidimensional uncertainty-based routing to clinicians, and adaptive threshold calibration from feedback. It explicitly departs from the medical foundation model trajectory (Med-PaLM, Meditron, BioMistral) by arguing that expensive domain-specific pretraining is unnecessary when generalist LLMs can be paired with compact, modality-specific specialist models. The specialist models themselves (Transformer encoder-decoder for ECHO, CNN+Transformer for ECG) are architecturally conventional; the novelty lies in the orchestration protocol and the formal treatment of inter-specialist conflict and uncertainty as routing signals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides empirical evidence that compact domain-specific specialist models remain irreplaceable in multimodal clinical decision-making even when powerful generalist LLMs are available, offering a cost-effective alternative to training monolithic medical foundation models. Key open questions include whether the text-only inter-agent interface loses critical spatial/structural information, whether simulated (ground-truth) clinician feedback generalizes to real deployment noise, and whether the conflict-aware fusion mechanism scales meaningfully beyond two specialists. The single-institution cardiovascular evaluation (613 cases) and reliance on commercial LLM APIs limit immediate generalizability, though the cross-domain IU X-Ray experiment is a positive signal.
