## Problem

Existing camera-controllable video diffusion methods inject numerical camera parameters (Plücker embeddings or linear feature vectors) into the diffusion backbone, which provides imprecise control over subtle motion variations and produces structural distortions under new viewpoints. Optical-flow-based noise warping alternatives entangle scene appearance with motion, causing semantic conflicts with text conditions during inference. The paper addresses how to encode camera motion faithfully into the generative process while remaining fully decoupled from scene appearance.

## Input / Output

The method takes camera poses as intrinsic (K) and extrinsic (E) matrices per frame, a text prompt, and optionally a reference image for image-to-video generation. Camera poses can be user-specified or estimated from existing videos via VGGT. The output is a 49-frame (extendable to 81-frame) video at 1024×576 resolution, generated by a Wan 2.1 DiT backbone fine-tuned with LoRA. The CameraNoise generation pipeline (GRFlow + PDE solver) runs at ~10 FPS on CPU with no additional GPU memory overhead, operating in the 8×-downsampled latent space.

## Method

The core contribution is CameraNoise, a warped Gaussian signal that encodes camera motion directly into the noise space. First, a Geometry-guided Reprojection Flow (GRFlow) is computed purely from camera parameters via pseudo-depth back-projection and SE(3) relative transformations, with Lie algebra smoothing (exponentially weighted moving average) to suppress jitter from pose estimation errors. Second, noise propagation is formulated as a linear advection PDE and solved via a bipartite graph with Jacobian-determinant-based density correction, preserving the standard Gaussian prior while establishing a provable one-to-one mapping from camera poses to CameraNoise. During training, CameraNoise is mixed with standard Gaussian noise via a randomly sampled coefficient λ∈[0,1], and a Dynamic Scaling Training strategy perturbs rotation matrices (η∈(0.9,1.1)) to improve robustness to pose estimation errors at inference. The entire approach is injected at the noise level without modifying the DiT architecture, enabling plug-and-play integration with existing video diffusion backbones.

## Results

On the RealEstate100 test set, CameraNoise achieves the best overall performance in both T2V and I2V settings. For I2V, it reduces FVD to 180.81 (vs. 321.79 for MotionCtrl and 392.79 for CameraCtrl), lowers TransErr to 0.144 and RotErr to 0.269, and increases Dynamic Degree to 0.58 (a 75.8% improvement over the previous best). For T2V, it achieves the lowest FVD of 437.54 (vs. 661.04 for MotionCtrl), the best Aesthetic Quality (0.549), and the highest Dynamic Degree (0.53). Zero-shot evaluation on MultiCamVideo100 and DrivingDoJo100 confirms strong generalization, with FVD of 362.57 and 242.67 respectively, outperforming all baselines including the warped-noise-based GWTF and 3D-based GEN3C.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MotionCtrl (Wang et al., 2024b), CameraNoise wins on nearly all metrics in both T2V and I2V: FVD drops from 661.04 to 437.54 (T2V) and from 321.79 to 180.81 (I2V), while Dynamic Degree more than doubles (0.10→0.53 T2V; 0.33→0.58 I2V). Against CameraCtrl (He et al., 2025a), the advantage is even more pronounced in T2V (FVD 909.58→437.54) and I2V (FVD 392.79→180.81), with CameraNoise also achieving lower TransErr (0.144 vs. 0.173) and LPIPS (0.182 vs. 0.258) in I2V. Against AC3D (Bahmani et al., 2025), CameraNoise dominates in T2V on FVD (437.54 vs. 800.79), Dynamic Degree (0.53 vs. 0.15), and LPIPS (0.528 vs. 0.634). In zero-shot I2V on MultiCamVideo100, CameraNoise outperforms GWTF (Burgert et al., 2025) on FVD (362.57 vs. 667.68) and LPIPS (0.298 vs. 0.566), though GWTF edges ahead slightly on TransErr (0.187 vs. 0.191). On DrivingDoJo100, CameraNoise achieves the lowest TransErr (0.397 vs. 0.776 for GWTF) and FVD (242.67 vs. 341.29), while GEN3C (Ren et al., 2025) produces nearly static videos (Dynamic Degree 0.45) and suffers from scene penetration issues in OOD settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of camera-controllable video generation, which injects numerical camera parameters (Plücker embeddings or linear feature vectors) as conditioning signals into diffusion backbones (MotionCtrl, CameraCtrl, AC3D, GEN3C). Instead, it repurposes the noise-warping idea from motion-controllable diffusion (Go-with-the-Flow, Chang et al.) but replaces optical flow with a purely geometric, appearance-agnostic reprojection flow (GRFlow) computed solely from camera poses. The PDE-based advection formulation and bipartite-graph solver for noise warping are novel algorithmic contributions, while the overall training pipeline (Wan 2.1 DiT + LoRA) follows standard transfer-learning practice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By encoding camera motion directly in the noise space rather than as intermediate activations, this approach ensures camera conditioning persists throughout the entire denoising process and avoids the semantic conflicts that arise when optical-flow-based warping leaks scene appearance into the noise prior. The one-to-one mapping between camera poses and CameraNoise (guaranteed by PDE uniqueness) provides a principled guarantee of controllability that numerical-injection methods lack. Open questions include how the pseudo-depth assumption in GRFlow affects performance in scenes with large depth variation, whether the method generalizes to multi-object dynamic scenes with significant parallax, and whether the approach extends to 3D-consistent generation beyond 2D pixel-level reprojection.
