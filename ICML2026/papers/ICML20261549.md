## Problem

Reassembling N rigid fragments in n-dimensional space is invariant to global rigid motions, yet training generative models directly on SE(n)^N is ill-posed because standard losses penalize configurations that differ only by a global transform. Existing methods handle this with ad-hoc anchoring that breaks permutation symmetry across fragments and introduces geometric biases requiring costly data augmentation to mitigate. The paper asks whether invariance can be enforced by construction rather than approximated through heuristics.

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of N fragments, each represented either as a polygon contour (2D: SVJP, SVFP, RePAIR, floorplans) or a point cloud (3D: Breaking Bad, FRACTURA), and produces the SE(2) or SE(3) pose (position + rotation) of each fragment in the assembled configuration. Because the output lives on the quotient space M/SE(n), the reconstructed assembly is defined only up to a global rigid motion; the gauge-fixing map Ψ selects a canonical representative (centered, PCA-aligned) for evaluation. In 2D, fragment contours are encoded into fixed-dimension piece tokens via a shape encoder before attention; in 3D, the GARF point-cloud backbone is reused with all anchoring features disabled.

## Method

The framework combines three geometric components within a Conditional Flow Matching (CFM) training loop. First, Global Gauge Fixing (GGF) deterministically maps each configuration to a canonical section Σ by centering fragment positions and aligning their principal inertia axes via PCA, reducing the problem dimension from N·dim SE(n) to (N−1)·dim SE(n). Second, a quotient-invariant loss projects the raw velocity residual δ = v_θ(x_t,t) − u_t onto the horizontal tangent bundle H_x (the orthogonal complement of the vertical space induced by the diagonal SE(n) action) using a closed-form inertia-momentum solve, so the network is supervised only on shape-changing dynamics. Third, at inference, the learned vector field is projected onto H_x at every Heun integration step, ensuring the generated trajectory evolves purely in shape space. The key insight is that the horizontal projection admits explicit, differentiable formulas in the centered gauge for both SE(2) (a scalar angular-velocity solve) and SE(3) (a 3×3 inertia-matrix solve), making the invariant loss as cheap to compute as the unprojected one.

## Results

On the 3D Breaking Bad (Everyday) benchmark, the method achieves RMSE(R)=5.80°, RMSE(T)=1.12×10⁻², PA=95.91%, and CD=0.19×10⁻³, outperforming the strongest baseline GARF (6.43°, 1.26, 95.15%, 0.22) and QSDM (7.28°, 1.54, 93.28%, 0.43). On FRACTURA, it reduces RMSE(R) from 17.98° to 17.20° and CD from 6.36 to 5.29 over GARF. On 2D SVJP (0% noise), Overlap reaches 0.93 versus 0.80 for the best SE(2) manifold baseline, and on RePAIR archaeological reassembly, Overlap doubles from 0.21 to 0.41 over the prior SOTA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method consistently outperforms all 17 baselines across every benchmark. Against the strongest recent FM-based method GARF (Li et al., 2025), it improves RMSE(R) by 0.63° on Breaking Bad Everyday (5.80 vs. 6.43), 0.44° on Artifact (5.36 vs. 5.80), and 0.78° on FRACTURA (17.20 vs. 17.98), while also reducing translation error and Chamfer Distance and increasing Part Accuracy by 0.76–0.95 points. Against QSDM (Xu et al., 2026), the gains are larger: RMSE(R) drops from 7.28° to 5.80° and CD from 0.43 to 0.19 on Everyday. On 2D SVJP, the method surpasses the best SE(2) manifold FM baseline (Chen & Lipman, 2024) by 0.13 Overlap (0.93 vs. 0.80) and 0.19 Recall (0.88 vs. 0.69) at 0% noise, with the gap widening under 1% noise (0.78 vs. 0.68 Overlap). On RePAIR, it nearly doubles Overlap over ReassembleNet (Islam et al., 2025) from 0.21 to 0.41 while halving translation RMSE (10.72 vs. 16.01 mm). On floorplans, it reduces MPE from 11.69 to 7.20 on RPLAN and from 39.28 to 30.11 on MagicPlan versus PuzzleFusion's vertex-level attention.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Flow Matching / Conditional Flow Matching framework (Lipman et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2023) and repurposes tools from geometric control and principal bundle theory—specifically gauge fixing and horizontal projection via inertia-momentum equations—to enforce SE(n) invariance by construction. It departs from the dominant prior approaches to symmetry handling in reassembly (anchoring, data augmentation, equivariant architectures) by instead selecting a canonical representative per orbit via a deterministic PCA-based gauge map and projecting velocity residuals onto the shape-changing horizontal subspace. The closest conceptual neighbor is Quotient-Space Diffusion Models (Xu et al., 2026), which also operates on SE(3) quotient spaces but in a diffusion setting for molecular/protein generation rather than rigid-body pose assembly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a clean, general recipe for removing global rigid-motion redundancy from any SE(n)-valued generative model, and the ablations convincingly show that invariance-by-construction (gauge fixing + horizontal projection) outperforms the more common anchoring strategy both in accuracy and training speed. Limitations include the assumption of rigid fragments, the PCA gauge's sensitivity to near-degenerate spectra (mitigated but not eliminated), and modest 3D gains over the already-strong GARF baseline. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-rigid or partially deformable fragments, whether the specific quadratic metric is optimal, and whether the framework composes naturally with equivariant encoders for richer fragment representations.
