## Problem

Existing Causal Foundation Models (CFMs) cannot incorporate domain knowledge about causal structure at test time: they either require expensive retraining for each specific causal assumption or marginalize over all possible structures, yielding overly conservative and imprecise predictions. This paper asks how to flexibly condition a single CFM on any amount of available causal information—full, partial, or none—without retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263867/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an observational tabular dataset, an intervention query do(t) with a feature vector x, and a Partial Ancestral Matrix (PAM) with entries in {-1, 0, 1} encoding known ancestor, known non-ancestor, or unknown causal relationships between variable pairs. The PAM subsumes adjacency matrices and allows temporal or expert knowledge to be expressed without specifying direct edges. The output is a posterior predictive distribution over the interventional outcome, parameterized as a 1000-bin bar distribution, with the model trained to handle any fraction of PAM entries set to zero (unknown) in a single amortised model.

## Method

The architecture is a transformer with factorised attention (alternating feature-wise and sample-wise heads) trained in the PFN paradigm on a complex causal prior with MLP and XGBoost mechanisms. Graph conditioning is injected through two complementary mechanisms: (1) soft attention biasing, where learnable scalar biases β_anc and β_non-anc are added to attention logits to encourage information flow along known ancestral directions and discourage it from known non-ancestors, and (2) a Graph Convolutional Network encoder that produces structure-aware node representations from the PAM, injected into every transformer block via Adaptive Layer Normalization. The model is trained amortised over varying fractions of hidden PAM entries, enabling a single model to handle full, partial, or no graph information. Experiments show that combining soft attention biasing with the GCN encoder outperforms either mechanism alone, and that ancestor matrices (transitive closure) work as well as or slightly better than adjacency matrices for bias-based conditioning.

## Results

On the semi-synthetic RealCause benchmark, providing partially known ancestral information (encoding unconfoundedness) systematically improves CATE estimation: on IHDP, √PEHE drops from 6.28 (no ancestral info) to 5.49, and ε_ATE drops from 0.67 to 0.49; on ACIC, √PEHE improves from 3.47 to 2.79 and ε_ATE from 0.46 to 0.17. On the balanced PSID dataset, ancestral conditioning reduces √PEHE from 21,896 to 19,711 and ε_ATE from 0.94 to 0.65. On the TFM prior, the graph-conditioned model matches CausalPFN when the back-door criterion holds but substantially outperforms it when the criterion is violated, and outperforms CausalFM and Do-PFN across almost all RealCause datasets once graph conditioning is introduced.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CausalPFN (Balazadeh et al., 2025), which is trained exclusively for the back-door/strong-ignorability setting, the graph-conditioned model performs only slightly better when the back-door criterion is satisfied, but gains a substantial advantage when the criterion is violated—precisely the regime where CausalPFN is misspecified. On the RealCause benchmark, CausalPFN still achieves the best overall performance because its training prior is designed specifically for that identification setup. Against CausalFM (Ma et al., 2025) and Do-PFN (Robertson et al., 2025), the graph-conditioned model outperforms both across almost all RealCause datasets once ancestral information is provided. Internally, the model with ancestral information consistently beats the no-ancestral-information variant on IHDP (√PEHE 5.49 vs 6.28), ACIC (2.79 vs 3.47), and CPS (11213 vs 12800), while the predictive-only model (which treats the task as pure regression under unconfoundedness) is outperformed on IHDP and ACIC but is competitive on CPS. On the imbalanced PSID dataset all methods struggle, but after rebalancing the ancestral-conditioned model clearly wins (√PEHE 19711 vs 21896 vs 22045; ε_ATE 0.65 vs 0.94 vs 0.95).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PFN-based Causal Foundation Model paradigm (DoPFN, MACE-TNP, ACTIVA) by introducing a mechanism to condition on partial causal domain knowledge at test time, which all prior CFMs lack. It departs from the two existing paradigms—separate models per identification setting (CausalPFN, CausalFM) and models trained over all graphs with no domain knowledge (DoPFN, MACE-TNP)—by proposing a single unified model that accepts a Partially Known Ancestral Matrix (PAM) as input. Architecturally, it builds on the transformer-based CFM designs of Robertson et al. and Dhir et al., adding learnable attention biases and a GCN-based structural encoder, while drawing on TabICL's functional mechanisms for a more realistic causal prior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses what the authors identify as a central hurdle toward all-in-one CFMs: the inability to leverage domain expertise without expensive retraining. The PAM formulation is practically appealing because it subsumes no-knowledge, partial-knowledge, and full-knowledge regimes in a single model, and the finding that marking uncertain edges as 'unknown' is far less harmful than misspecification has direct practical value. However, the absolute improvements remain small (driven by the prior's predominantly weak causal effects), hidden variables are not considered, and no comprehensive real-world causal benchmark exists to validate the approach at scale. Open questions include conditioning on functional mechanism properties, incorporating hidden confounders, and building the real-world causal benchmarks the field currently lacks.
