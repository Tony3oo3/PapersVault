## Problem

Local approximations to gradient descent in RNNs—such as RFLO and one-step tBPTT—are widely used for biological plausibility and neuromorphic feasibility, yet their learning dynamics (fixed points, stability, convergence) remain poorly characterized relative to exact BPTT. In particular, it was unclear whether these local rules converge to the same solutions, at what rate, and whether their fixed-point landscapes differ qualitatively from the true gradient flow.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264986/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The analysis operates on a student-teacher pair of linear discrete-time RNNs driven by i.i.d. Gaussian white noise, with the learning objective being the expected L² output error at the final time step. Under the data-alignment assumption, the full matrix dynamics reduce to n independent 3D ODE systems parameterized by (a, b, w) per mode, with teacher parameters (a⋆, b⋆, w⋆) and algorithm-specific parameters (e.g., random feedback scalar â and recurrent scaling ŵ for RFLO). Outputs include closed-form ODEs, fixed-point manifold geometry, Jacobian eigenvalue spectra along those manifolds, asymptotic convergence rates, and a rank bound on learned weight perturbations.

## Method

The paper extends the data-alignment diagonalization framework of Proca et al. (2025) from BPTT to one-step tBPTT and RFLO by expressing all three update rules in the aligned basis and exploiting orthogonality of the transformation matrices. Taking the long-sequence (T→∞) and small-learning-rate (η→0) limits yields closed-form 3D ODEs per mode for each algorithm. The authors then perform a full dynamical-systems analysis: identifying fixed-point manifolds (showing RFLO lacks the non-optimal a=b=0 saddle present in BPTT/tBPTT), computing Jacobian eigenvalues to establish sign-dependent stability along RFLO's optimal manifold (including a novel oscillatory instability absent in BPTT), and comparing asymptotic convergence rates. A separate algebraic argument (Proposition 3.1) proves that RFLO restricts learned W and B to low-rank perturbations bounded by the output dimension, a result that holds without data alignment. Finally, the theory is validated against non-data-aligned numerical experiments, showing reasonable predictive accuracy after a brief transient alignment period.

## Results

The paper derives ODEs for BPTT, one-step tBPTT, and RFLO in data-aligned linear RNNs and shows that BPTT and tBPTT share two fixed-point manifolds (a stable optimal line and an unstable non-optimal saddle at a=b=0), while RFLO admits only the optimal manifold. Stability analysis reveals that RFLO's optimal manifold is sign-dependent: points where the feedback element ŷa and the mode parameter s share a sign are stable, but the opposite branch can be unstable or oscillatory—a behavior absent in BPTT and tBPTT. BPTT exhibits the fastest asymptotic convergence rate, RFLO the slowest over most of the optimal manifold (except near s=0), and one-step tBPTT closely matches BPTT when |w*| is small. A key structural result (Proposition 3.1) proves that RFLO restricts learned weight changes to rank at most o (the output dimension), and numerical experiments confirm that tBPTT and e-prop also learn lower-rank solutions than BPTT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to BPTT (Werbos, 1990), one-step tBPTT (Williams & Peng, 1990) behaves nearly identically in the data-aligned regime: both share the same fixed-point structure (optimal manifold + non-optimal saddle), similar stability properties, and comparable convergence rates when the teacher's recurrent eigenvalue |w*| is small. RFLO (Murray, 2019) differs qualitatively: it lacks the non-optimal saddle entirely, exhibits sign-dependent stability along the optimal manifold (with possible unstable oscillations for large a*²b*²), and converges more slowly than both BPTT and tBPTT over most of the parameter space. In terms of representation structure (Figure 6), BPTT learns the highest-rank perturbations of W, tBPTT learns lower-rank solutions, RFLO is restricted to rank-1 perturbations (as proven analytically), and e-prop (Bellec et al., 2020) occupies an intermediate rank between RFLO/tBPTT and BPTT. In terms of task performance (Figure 10), higher-rank solutions correlate with better performance: BPTT achieves the lowest error, e-prop is intermediate, and RFLO/tBPTT (both low-rank) perform worst. Stability in practice also differs: out of 20 runs, all 20 converged for BPTT and tBPTT, 13 for RFLO, and 12 for e-prop.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the data-aligned linear RNN analysis of Proca et al. (2025), which derived ODEs for BPTT learning dynamics, to the local learning rules tBPTT and RFLO, revealing qualitatively distinct fixed-point and stability structures. The data-alignment framework itself is inherited from Saxe et al. (2014, 2019) for feedforward networks and adapted to recurrent settings. The RFLO algorithm analyzed here is a variant of Murray (2019), with the recurrent error propagation parameterized as a scaled identity rather than the original time-constant-dependent form, and the e-prop variant (Bellec et al., 2020) is included as a comparison point. The paper departs from prior work by showing that local rules do not merely slow convergence but fundamentally alter the geometry of the solution space (e.g., eliminating the non-optimal fixed-point manifold for RFLO and imposing a low-rank constraint on learned weights).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The low-rank restriction on RFLO-learned solutions (bounded by output dimension) provides a mechanistic explanation for low-rank connectivity structures observed in neuroscience and may clarify why tasks requiring more timescales than output dimensions are hard to learn with local rules. The sign-dependent stability of RFLO's optimal manifold—where one branch can be unstable or oscillatory—offers a concrete reason why local learning rules may fail in practice, with implications for neuromorphic and biologically plausible learning. The main limitation is the data-alignment assumption, which restricts analysis to non-interacting exponential modes; while the paper shows partial extension beyond this regime, the general case (interacting modes, nonlinear networks, non-white-noise inputs) remains open. A key open question is whether the low-rank and stability results persist in nonlinear RNNs, where local minima and divergent dynamics could amplify the identified inefficiencies.
