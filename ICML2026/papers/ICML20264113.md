## Problem

Existing lifelong person Re-ID methods assume each identity maintains a stable appearance distribution across tasks, but in real-world surveillance, identities reappear asynchronously with substantial clothing changes that violate this assumption. This creates a dual challenge: the model must acquire new identities while adapting representations of recurring ones, all while resisting the progressive representation drift that clothing-induced appearance shifts impose on cross-temporal identity consistency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264113/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB person images arriving as a sequential stream of K tasks, where during training on task k only the current task's data is accessible and historical data is unavailable. A key constraint is that for any recurring identity, clothing styles across tasks are guaranteed non-overlapping (asynchronous clothing constraint), making the conditional appearance distribution non-stationary. The model outputs an L2-normalized identity embedding in R^d used for discriminative retrieval, evaluated on a fixed global test set containing all identities from the entire stream to measure both plasticity (new outfits) and stability (cross-temporal association).

## Method

The framework centers on two complementary modules. Structural Identity Decoupling (SID) projects backbone feature maps into an identity subspace and a bias subspace via independent projection heads, then enforces disentanglement through a bias-swapping reconstruction loss that requires a shared decoder to reconstruct the original feature tensor even when bias features are swapped between samples. Dynamic Identity-Anchor Alignment (DIAA) maintains a global anchor library in the purified identity subspace and updates anchors via a two-stage mechanism: a sample-aware calibration that softly rectifies the query anchor toward the base anchor (weighted by relative observation counts) to compensate for model-induced drift, followed by count-weighted fusion. An Identity-Subspace Distillation loss further constrains only the identity-intrinsic features (not the full or bias features) for recurring identities across successive tasks. The overall objective jointly optimizes a standard Re-ID loss (cross-entropy + triplet), the reconstruction loss, the anchor-alignment margin ranking loss, and the ISD loss with learned weights.

## Results

On the LTCC-LCC benchmark, the proposed method achieves 29.4% mAP and 58.0% Rank-1 under same-clothing evaluation, and 25.8% mAP and 39.7% Rank-1 under the more challenging cloth-change setting. On PRCC-LCC, it reaches 96.9% mAP / 98.4% Rank-1 (same-cloth) and 38.2% mAP / 37.2% Rank-1 (cloth-change). These results consistently outperform all 13 baselines across both datasets and both evaluation protocols, with the largest margins on the cloth-change subsets where representation drift is most severe.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 13 baselines on every dataset and evaluation setting. On the most challenging LTCC cloth-change subset, it surpasses the strongest LReID baseline DKP by +7.3% mAP (25.8 vs. 18.5) and +5.9% Rank-1 (39.7 vs. 33.8), and beats PKA by +8.6% mAP. On PRCC cloth-change, it leads the best CC-ReID method CaAug by +4.5% mAP (38.2 vs. 33.7) and +7.7% Rank-1 (37.2 vs. 29.5), and exceeds the top LReID method PKA by +5.8% mAP. CIL methods (LwF, PRAKA, FCS) and static CC-ReID methods (CAL, AIM) suffer severe degradation on cloth-change subsets (mAP dropping to 4.7–10.2% on LTCC-CC), confirming that neither generic continual learning nor offline cloth-changing approaches handle asynchronous identity recurrence. The gap to the joint-training upper bound (32.4% mAP on LTCC-CC) narrows from ~20 points for most baselines to just 6.6 points, indicating that the SID + DIAA framework recovers most of the information lost by the sequential protocol.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work—lifelong person Re-ID (which assumes identity-disjoint tasks and stable appearance) and cloth-changing Re-ID (which handles appearance variation but only in offline, static settings)—and explicitly departs from both by formulating a setting where identities recur asynchronously under substantial clothing changes. Methodologically, it builds on the hindsight anchor library concept from Chaudhry et al. (AAAI 2021) for maintaining persistent identity references, and on feature-disentanglement strategies (adversarial/semantic separation of identity from appearance) for the Structural Identity Decoupling module. The authors note that DKC (Cui et al., CVPR 2025) has begun incorporating cloth-changing data into lifelong streams but treats clothing variation as a mere data-pool expansion, whereas this work redefines the task structure to model true asynchronous recurrence with cross-stage identity overlap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The LCC formulation and its two constructed benchmarks (PRCC-LCC, LTCC-LCC) provide the field with a more realistic evaluation protocol for long-term surveillance, where the same person reappears in different outfits across non-adjacent time windows—a scenario that existing lifelong Re-ID benchmarks simply cannot capture. The practical impact is significant for deployed surveillance and missing-person systems that must maintain identity consistency over months or years. Open questions include whether the disentanglement approach generalizes to other appearance changes (age progression, pose, occlusion), how the method scales to hundreds or thousands of identities, and whether the anchor-update mechanism remains stable under very long task sequences with sparse recurrence. The paper also flags privacy concerns around persistent cross-temporal tracking, noting the need for regulated deployment and data-retention limits.
