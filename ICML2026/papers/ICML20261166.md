## Problem

Conventional LLM ensembling requires a forward pass from every model in the ensemble at each generation step, making inference n times more expensive than a single model and rendering ensembles impractical for real-world use. The paper asks whether all models truly need to be invoked and shows that they do not: the common belief that LLM ensembling is inherently slow stems from directly transplanting the conventional ML ensemble paradigm (argmax over averaged scores) to a setting where tokens are sampled from distributions.

## Input / Output

At each autoregressive step the method receives the current text prefix x_{≤t}, a set of n LLMs with associated ensemble weights λ_i summing to 1, and (for heterogeneous vocabularies) a transformation function F_i that maps each model's native distribution onto a unified vocabulary. It outputs a single next token sampled from the stochastically selected model's distribution (after vocabulary alignment if applicable), iterated until a stopping condition is met. Unlike conventional ensembling, only one model's forward pass is executed per step rather than n.

## Method

ME reinterprets the weighted-average ensemble distribution as a mixture model: instead of explicitly computing Σ λ_i M_i(y|x_{≤t}) and sampling from it, the algorithm draws an index i ~ Multinomial(λ_1,…,λ_n) and samples the next token from M_i(y|x_{≤t}) alone, which is provably equivalent by the law of total probability. To handle the KV-cache inconsistency introduced by switching models mid-sequence, ME employs a Lazy Synchronization strategy: when model M_i is re-selected after k steps, a single forward-extend (prefill) pass over the k missing tokens updates its cache, incurring negligible overhead because LLM decoding is memory-bandwidth-bound. For heterogeneous vocabularies, the selected model's distribution is mapped to a unified vocabulary via a transformation function (e.g., UniTe's top-k union) before sampling. The paper further shows that ME is operationally identical to token-level routing with a uniform random router, positioning LLM ensembling as the simplest special case of routing.

## Results

ME achieves accuracy on par with Conventional Ensemble (CE) across all model configurations and datasets (e.g., 82.97 vs. 83.14 on GSM8K for Qwen-3B + Qwen-Math-1.5B, k=5; 69.86 vs. 69.20 on GSM8K for the 3-model heterogeneous ensemble). In terms of inference speed, ME is 1.78×–2.68× faster than Sequential CE and approaches single-model speed (e.g., 58.25 vs. 63.68 tokens/s for a 2-model ensemble on H100). Parallel CE (using the GaC implementation) provides only a marginal 1.05×–1.83× speedup over Sequential CE due to inter-GPU communication overhead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares ME against Conventional Ensemble (CE) in both sequential and parallel variants, with the parallel implementation based on GaC (Yu et al., 2024). On accuracy, ME is essentially tied with CE across all four datasets (GSM8K, MMLU, BBH, ARC) and all model configurations, with differences typically within ±1 point (e.g., 82.97 vs. 83.14 on GSM8K for the Qwen pair). On speed, ME decisively wins: it is 1.78×–2.68× faster than Sequential CE and 1.69×–2.53× faster than Parallel CE, while approaching single-model inference speed. Notably, Parallel CE (GaC) provides only a 1.05×–1.83× speedup over Sequential CE, and on RTX 3090 it is actually slower than Sequential CE due to inter-GPU communication bottlenecks. The single-model baseline is faster than ME (by ~8–10%) but lacks the ensemble accuracy gains, positioning ME as the best speed-accuracy trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the classical mixture-model sampling procedure (well-known in statistics since the Gaussian Mixture Model literature) to show that conventional LLM ensembling—where all n models must be invoked to compute an averaged distribution before sampling—is mathematically unnecessary. It departs from the prior LLM ensembling line (Yu et al., 2024; Huang et al., 2024; Yao et al., 2024; Xu et al., 2024) not by proposing a new combination rule but by eliminating the explicit ensemble-distribution computation entirely. The paper also draws a conceptual bridge to token-level routing (Belofsky, 2023; Muqeeth et al., 2024; Ostapenko et al., 2024) and MoE (Jiang et al., 2024), positioning LLM ensembling as the simplest (random-router) special case of routing, and borrows the lazy KV-cache synchronization idea from speculative decoding (Leviathan et al., 2023) to handle the practical cost of switching active models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing LLM ensembling from n forward passes to one per token, ME makes ensembling nearly as fast as single-model inference (1.78×–2.68× speedup over sequential CE), removing the primary practical barrier to deploying ensembles in production. The conceptual unification with token-level routing suggests that input-aware routers are a natural generalization of ensembling, opening a research direction where the ensemble weight λ is replaced by a learned, context-dependent routing decision. Key limitations include inapplicability to greedy/argmax decoding and the open question (raised in Appendix A.4) of which broader combination structures are 'separable' into mixture form. The method is training-free and plug-and-play, but its performance gain over the best single model remains modest, so it is best viewed as an efficiency enabler rather than a quality booster.
