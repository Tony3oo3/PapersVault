## Problem

Standard diffusion model samplers treat each denoiser prediction as an exact estimator for numerical integration, ignoring the inherent statistical uncertainty of MSE-trained denoisers. This causes cumulative prediction drift that is especially severe in few-step regimes, yet existing solvers focus on refining temporal discretization rather than correcting the clean-signal estimate itself, leaving the temporal redundancy across the prediction sequence unexploited.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262648/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

DiFA operates as a training-free, plug-and-play wrapper around any existing solver (DDIM, DPM-Solver++, UniPC, Heun) that consumes the same Gaussian noise input and optional class label, producing the same synthetic image output without additional network evaluations. It is evaluated on CIFAR-10 (32×32), ImageNet-64 (64×64), LSUN Bedroom (latent space), and ImageNet 256×256 (flow matching with SiT-XL/2), with the only extra computation being a small causal history buffer of size K and lightweight residual operations (O(Kd) per step).

## Method

DiFA reframes inference-time prediction refinement as a sequential state-estimation problem inspired by Kalman filtering. At each reverse step, it constructs a forward-aligned temporal consensus from a causal window of K historical clean-signal predictions, weighting them by structural compatibility (cosine similarity after spatial pooling) and logSNR proximity, then uses this consensus as a reference anchor. The anchor-relative deviation is refined through an orthogonal residual projection (suppressing prediction-parallel magnitude variation) and a frequency-dependent modulation that always retains low-frequency components while gating high-frequency content via a sigmoid of the current logSNR. The theoretical justification derives from BLUE under an idealized independent-view observation model, showing that precision-weighted (inverse-SNR) fusion minimizes estimation covariance, and the practical consensus is a causal, structurally-aware approximation of this optimal rule. The refined prediction is then passed to the unchanged downstream solver, requiring zero additional NFEs.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (32×32), DPM-Solver++ with DiFA achieves FID 1.96 at 20 NFE and FID 2.02 at 15 NFE, matching or surpassing EDM (FID 1.97 at 35 NFE) and EVODiff (FID 2.06 at 15 NFE) with fewer network evaluations. On ImageNet-64 (64×64), DiFA reduces FID from 1.83 (DPM-Solver++ base) to 1.64 and from 1.73 (UniPC base) to 1.63 at 25 NFE, approaching EDM2-S (FID 1.58 at 63 NFE) with 2.5× fewer steps. In the extreme low-budget regime (8 NFE on CIFAR-10), DiFA cuts FID from 8.40 to 4.15, a >50% relative improvement. DiFA also improves FD-DINOv2 perceptual quality consistently across both datasets and noise schedules (logSNR and EDM).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On CIFAR-10, DiFA at 20 NFE (FID 1.96) matches EDM at 35 NFE (FID 1.97) and slightly beats DPM-Solver-v3 at 25 NFE (FID 2.00), while at 15 NFE (FID 2.02) it outperforms EVODiff at the same budget (FID 2.06). On ImageNet-64, DiFA consistently improves its host solvers: DPM-Solver++ drops from FID 1.83 to 1.64 and UniPC from 1.73 to 1.63 at 25 NFE, both approaching EDM2-S (FID 1.58 at 63 NFE) with 2.5× fewer evaluations. DiFA remains behind 1-step distillation GANs (SiD²A: FID 1.50 on CIFAR-10, 1.11 on ImageNet-64) and high-NFE diffusion baselines like RIN (FID 1.23 at 1000 NFE on ImageNet), but achieves these results without any retraining. The method also substantially stabilizes the Heun solver on ImageNet, reducing FID from 230.05 to 110.20 at 5 NFE, and improves latent-diffusion baselines on LSUN Bedroom by up to 72% FID reduction at 5 NFE. Across all comparisons, DiFA never degrades performance relative to its host solver, confirming its plug-and-play nature.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiFA builds on the EDM preconditioning framework (Karras et al., 2022), which reveals that diffusion trajectories orbit a static clean-signal anchor, and extends the self-guidance intuition of EDM2 (Karras et al., 2024a) by replacing auxiliary degraded models with a temporal consensus over the model's own historical predictions. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant solver-centric view (DPM-Solver++, UniPC, DDIM) that treats the learned denoiser as an exact vector-field estimator for numerical integration; instead, DiFA reframes each prediction as a noisy observation of a locally stable anchor and applies a BLUE/Kalman-filtering-inspired fusion rule in the clean-prediction space. The method is explicitly orthogonal to both distillation (which compresses trajectories at retraining cost) and higher-order solvers (which reduce truncation error but ignore estimation variance), positioning itself as a plug-and-play prediction-refinement layer that sits between the denoiser and any downstream solver.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DiFA offers a training-free, zero-extra-NFE mechanism that consistently improves FID, IS, and FD-DINOv2 across pixel-space diffusion, latent diffusion, and flow-matching settings, making it a practical drop-in for practitioners who want better few-step generation without retraining. The Kalman-filtering/BLUE framing provides a principled justification for the temporal consensus weights, but the idealized static-anchor and independent-view assumptions are acknowledged simplifications; extending the theory to temporally correlated denoiser errors and adaptive windowing remains open. The framework's reliance on clean-prediction parameterizations and fixed hyperparameters limits its generality, and its application to text-to-image, video generation, and inverse problems is flagged as future work rather than demonstrated.
