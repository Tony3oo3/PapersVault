## Problem

Zero-shot object detection methods overfit in-vocabulary (IV) classes and confidently misclassify out-of-vocabulary (OOV) objects as semantically similar IV categories, because they lack any prior knowledge of OOV data distributions. The paper formalizes zero-shot out-of-vocabulary detection (ZS-OOVD) as a new task requiring simultaneous recognition of predefined classes and reliable rejection of undefined ones, a problem that had not been explicitly studied or benchmarked before.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a predefined vocabulary of text prompts (split into seen and predefined unseen classes) as input, and outputs bounding boxes with class labels spanning seen, unseen, and a single consolidated OOV class, along with per-class confidence scores. The text encoder and prompt learner are frozen, so the model operates with precomputed IV text embeddings as fixed semantic prototypes; OOV prompts are synthesized in latent space rather than provided as explicit text. Evaluation uses mAP for IV classes, recall/average recall for OOV, and Wilderness Impact (WI) plus Absolute Open Set Error (AOSE) to quantify OOV-to-IV confusion.

## Method

OOVDet builds on RegionCLIP (ResNet50 + CC3M transformer text encoder) and introduces three modules. (1) Out-of-Vocabulary Prompt Synthesis (OPS) models IV prompt embeddings as class-conditional Gaussians with a tied covariance, then samples virtual OOV prompts from the low-likelihood tail of each distribution using Mahalanobis distance ranking. (2) Dirichlet-based Gradient Attribution (DGA) treats intermediate-layer attribution gradients as Dirichlet evidence, computing per-proposal uncertainty via negative log-probability density to mine high-uncertainty region proposals as pseudo-OOV images with balanced foreground/background sampling. (3) Low-Density Prior Constraint (LPC) applies Gaussian kernel density estimation over an IV feature bank and penalizes pseudo-OOV samples that fall in high-density IV regions, pushing them into low-density valleys to sharpen the IV-OOV decision boundary. The total loss combines the standard alignment cross-entropy, box regression, and the LPC term weighted by λ, trained end-to-end for 120k iterations.

## Results

On OOV-VOC, OOVDet achieves mAPIV/mAPOOV of 58.70/10.65, mAPSeen/mAPUnseen of 79.30/21.40, and ROOV/AROOV of 56.42/28.12, while reducing WI to 3.95 and AOSE to 2142. On the more challenging OOV-COCO benchmark, it reaches mAPIV/mAPOOV of 33.19/10.09, ROOV/AROOV of 27.60/12.82, and the lowest WI (3.12) and AOSE (4222) among all compared methods. The paper reports improvements of +2.70% mAPOOV, +8.71% ROOV, and +3.74% AROOV over the strongest prior baselines on OOV-VOC, with consistent gains on OOV-COCO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OOVDet outperforms all 12 baselines on every OOV-related metric (mAPOOV, ROOV, AROOV, WI, AOSE) on both OOV-VOC and OOV-COCO. On OOV-VOC, the closest competitor for OOV recall is CoOp (ROOV=47.43), which OOVDet surpasses by ~9 points (56.42), while for mAPOOV the gap over CoOp (7.95) is +2.70. EDL achieves the highest mAPIV (57.90) among baselines but has the worst mAPOOV (2.02) and high AOSE (2702), illustrating the IV-OOV trade-off that OOVDet resolves. On OOV-COCO, FOODv1 (mAPIV=32.63, mAPOOV=4.01) and FOODv2 (mAPIV=32.03, mAPOOV=3.59) are the strongest IV baselines, yet OOVDet leads on both mAPIV (33.19) and mAPOOV (10.09) simultaneously. APLGOS, the most recent prompt-learning baseline, achieves the lowest mAPIV (42.62 on VOC, 31.60 on COCO) and is clearly outperformed across all metrics. OOVDet also achieves the lowest WI and AOSE on both benchmarks, confirming fewer OOV-to-IV misclassifications than any prior method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the RegionCLIP vision-language detection framework and the broader line of zero-shot/open-vocabulary object detection (CoOp, CED-FOOD, APLGOS), but departs from them by explicitly modeling the out-of-vocabulary (OOV) rejection problem rather than only recognizing predefined unseen classes. On the OOV side, it extends density-aware open-set detection (OpenDet's low-density region expansion, GAIA's gradient attribution) by combining a textual prompt synthesis mechanism (Gaussian outlier sampling in latent space) with a visual pseudo-OOV mining strategy (Dirichlet-based gradient attribution), whereas prior methods addressed only one modality or relied on logit-based energy scores. The low-density prior constraint via Gaussian KDE is a departure from OpenDet's contrastive-learning approach to separating IV and OOV regions, offering a more explicit density-estimation formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By formally defining ZS-OOVD as distinct from ZSOD and providing the first dedicated benchmark (OOV-VOC, OOV-COCO), this paper opens a practically important problem for safety-critical applications (autonomous driving, industrial inspection) where confidently misclassifying unknown objects as known ones is dangerous. The ~2× improvement in OOV mAP over prior methods and the new WI/AOSE metrics give the community a concrete target for future work. Open questions include whether the low-density Gaussian assumption generalizes to truly unbounded open-world OOV spaces, whether the ChatGPT-dependent prompt synthesis introduces domain bias, and how the approach scales beyond the RegionCLIP architecture to modern open-vocabulary detectors like YOLO-World.
