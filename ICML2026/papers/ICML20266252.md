## Problem

Model merging via task arithmetic is highly susceptible to backdoor attacks, where a single compromised task vector propagates backdoor behavior into the merged model. Existing defenses that perform direct parameter-space editing (e.g., vector subtraction) struggle to remove backdoors without substantially degrading clean-task performance, because backdoor-related parameters are entangled with those essential for clean functionality. The paper asks whether backdoor mitigation can be reformulated from a unified feature-space perspective to achieve a principled balance between backdoor robustness and clean-task preservation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a backdoored merged model (constructed via task arithmetic from k task-specific CLIP-ViT-B/32 fine-tunes, one of which is compromised), the original pre-trained checkpoint, and a shadow dataset of 10,000 ImageNet-1K images. The defender has no prior knowledge of which task vector is malicious. The output is a purified merged model obtained by additively merging an optimized anti-backdoor task vector with the backdoored merged model at a tunable coefficient α, along with the learned adversarial projection matrix and visual prompt used during training. Notably, adversarial perturbations are encoded as learnable visual prompts rather than input-space triggers, preserving the semantic integrity of original samples.

## Method

LFPM operates in two stages under the Cross-Task Linearity (CTL) framework, which guarantees that feature outputs of a weight-interpolated model approximate the linear interpolation of individual model features. Stage 1 (Adversarial Feature Extraction via Subspace Partitioning) learns a rank-r projection matrix that decomposes the feature space into an adversarial subspace and a clean semantic subspace, then mines adversarial features using learnable visual prompts that activate backdoor-related directions while preserving clean semantics. Stage 2 (Anti-Backdoor Task Vector Optimization) reformulates the defense as a SAM-like min-max problem along the feature interpolation path, mapping CTL-induced feature deviations to bounded worst-case perturbations in the low-dimensional prompt space. A gradient accumulation mechanism combines gradients from both the backdoored merged model and the anti-backdoor model to approximate the interpolated gradient, and a loss path-integral over discrete interpolation points ensures robust backdoor suppression throughout the entire parameter path rather than at a single point.

## Results

Under BadMerging (full fine-tuning), LFPM achieves an average clean accuracy (CA) of 58.67% while reducing the target-task attack success rate (ASR(T)) to 0.49% and non-target ASR(N) to 9.85%, compared to 98.42% and 69.64% for the undefended Task Arithmetic baseline. Under LoBAM (LoRA-based PEFT), LFPM attains 54.45% CA with ASR(T) of 3.84% and ASR(N) of 26.68%. Against adaptive attacks, LFPM's ASR(T) increases only from 0.49% to 2.38%, while SAU degrades from 63.00% to 99.20% and SAM from 10.03% to 32.30%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against IBVS (task-arithmetic subtraction), LFPM dominates on all metrics: it recovers 15.82% more CA (58.67 vs. 42.85) while reducing ASR(T) from 64.18% to 0.49% and ASR(N) from 58.53% to 9.85%, confirming that direct parameter-space subtraction cannot disentangle backdoor from clean parameters. Against SAU (adversarial unlearning), LFPM achieves 11.67% higher CA and reduces ASR(T) by 62.51 percentage points (0.49 vs. 63.00). Against the sharpness-aware baselines SAM and PAM, LFPM achieves the lowest ASR(T) (0.49% vs. 10.03% and 4.42%) and ASR(N) (9.85% vs. 31.21% and 21.99%) while also attaining the highest CA (58.67% vs. 40.55% and 46.72%), outperforming SAM by 18.12% and PAM by 11.95% in clean accuracy. Under the LoBAM (PEFT) setting, LFPM is competitive with SAM and PAM on ASR (3.84% vs. 3.18%/3.07% ASR(T)) while achieving the highest CA (54.45% vs. 53.66%/52.19%), indicating a more favorable accuracy–robustness trade-off in the parameter-efficient regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the task arithmetic framework for model merging (Ilharco et al., 2022) and the recently identified backdoor threat in merged models (BadMerging, LoBAM), departing from prior defenses (IBVS, SAU) that perform direct parameter-space editing. The key conceptual shift is leveraging Cross-Task Linearity (CTL) to reformulate parameter-space interpolation as feature-space interpolation, enabling a SAM-like robust optimization in feature space rather than parameter space. Adversarial features are extracted via subspace partitioning and visual prompts rather than input-space trigger inversion, and the gradient accumulation mechanism with loss path-integral ensures robustness along the entire interpolation path rather than at a single point.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Model merging is becoming a standard practice for multi-task deployment, and the propagation of backdoors through merged components poses a real security risk, especially in open-source ecosystems. The feature-space perspective offers a more principled alternative to ad-hoc parameter subtraction, and the Path-ASR metric provides a more comprehensive robustness assessment than single-point evaluation. However, the evaluation is limited to CLIP-ViT-B/32 on 11 image classification datasets with 6 tasks, and the CTL assumption's validity for deeper architectures, LLMs, or non-linear prediction heads remains untested. Open questions include whether the subspace partitioning generalizes to multi-modal or language tasks, and whether the adaptive attack formulation captures the full range of potential evasion strategies.
