## Problem

The first rigorous guarantees for the top-down greedy decision tree heuristic (Blanc et al., ITCS 2020) were established only under the uniform distribution, leaving a critical gap between theory and practice where data features follow heterogeneous product distributions. Moreover, prior theoretical analyses often studied non-greedy or brute-force algorithms that diverge from the simple influence-based splitting used in practice (ID3, C4.5, CART), offering limited insight into why these heuristics work well empirically.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives query access to a Boolean target f: {0,1}^n → {±1} and draws random samples from an arbitrary product distribution μ over {0,1}^n (each coordinate i is 1 with probability p_i). It outputs a labeled decision tree that ε-approximates f under μ, with a provable size bound of max((e·Δ_opt/(ε·D_opt))^{Δ_opt·D_opt}, e^{Δ_opt·D_opt}). Notably, the practical implementation (Algorithm 2) is entirely parameter-free: it requires no prior knowledge of the optimal tree's size s or depth, instead adaptively augmenting sample sets as the tree grows and terminating when an independent error estimate falls below 3ε/4.

## Method

The core analysis extends the potential-function framework of Blanc et al. (2020) to arbitrary product distributions by replacing uniform-distribution Fourier-analytic tools with two distribution-agnostic inequalities: the O'Donnell et al. (2005) max-influence bound (max_i Inf_i(f) ≥ Var(f)/Δ(T)) and a total-influence-vs-variance bound (Inf(f) ≤ D(T)·Var(f)). The algorithm tracks a 'cost' potential (sum of total influence over leaves) that decreases by exactly the score of the split leaf at each step, analyzed in two phases: a high-cost phase yielding polynomial-in-j progress, and a termination phase bounded by e^{Δ_opt·D_opt} steps. A key robustness result (Theorem 5.1) shows the guarantee degrades gracefully when only a 1/4-approximate score is selected, enabling a fully sample-based, parameter-free implementation that estimates scores via re-randomization pairs, assigns leaf labels by majority vote, and checks termination via Hoeffding-bounded empirical error on an independent sample set.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 1.1) shows that the top-down greedy heuristic constructs an ε-approximating decision tree of size at most max((e·Δ_opt/(ε·D_opt))^(Δ_opt·D_opt), e^(Δ_opt·D_opt)) under any product distribution. In the balanced regime (full binary tree, Δ_opt = D_opt = log s), this simplifies to s^(log s · log(e/ε)), which is quasi-polynomial in s. The robust variant (Theorem 5.1) tolerates selecting a leaf with score at least 1/4 of the maximum, inflating the exponent by a factor of 4. The practical parameter-free algorithm (Algorithm 2) runs in O(J log J · n² log n · ε⁻² · log(1/δ)) time, where J is the number of splits.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is with Blanc et al. (ITCS 2020), who provided the first rigorous guarantees for the same top-down greedy heuristic but only under the uniform distribution. This paper's bound is strictly tighter in the balanced regime: s^(log s · log(e/ε)) versus Blanc et al.'s s^(O(log(s/ε)·log(1/ε))), and it extends to arbitrary product distributions where no prior rigorous guarantee existed. Additionally, this paper's algorithm is parameter-free—it does not require prior knowledge of the optimal tree size s or depth D_opt—whereas Blanc et al.'s method does. In the path-like (highly unbalanced) regime, the mixed dependence on both Δ_opt and D_opt yields exp(Θ(n)) rather than exp(Θ(n²)), a strict improvement over any bound stated in D_opt alone. The paper also contextualizes its quasi-polynomial dependence as near-tight, citing superpolynomial lower bounds from Koch et al. (SODA 2023) and superconstant inapproximability from Koch et al. (COLT 2024).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the analysis of Blanc et al. (ITCS 2020), who provided the first rigorous guarantees for the top-down greedy decision tree heuristic (BuildTopDownDT) but only under the uniform distribution, by generalizing the proof to arbitrary product distributions. The key analytical shift is replacing the Fourier-analytic inequalities available on the uniform hypercube with the O'Donnell et al. (FOCS 2005) max-influence inequality (max_i Inf_i(f) ≥ Var(f)/Δ(T)), which holds under any product distribution. The paper also departs from Blanc et al. by providing a parameter-free practical implementation that requires no prior knowledge of the optimal tree's size or depth, and by tracking both maximum depth D_opt and average depth Δ_opt separately rather than collapsing them into a single parameter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work closes a notable gap between the theoretical analysis of greedy decision tree learning (previously restricted to uniform distributions) and the practical reality of heterogeneous feature distributions, providing the first rigorous quasi-polynomial-size guarantee for the classic greedy heuristic under product distributions. The bound is near-tight given the superpolynomial lower bounds of Koch et al. (2023, 2024), so the quasi-polynomial dependence on s cannot be eliminated. Open questions include whether the Δ_opt·D_opt product in the exponent is tight (versus alternatives like Δ_opt + D_opt or max(Δ_opt, D_opt)), whether the analysis extends to continuous features and threshold-based splits as in CART, and how to incorporate provable pruning rules into the guarantee.
