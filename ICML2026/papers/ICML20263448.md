## Problem

Constrained meta RL aims to leverage training-phase knowledge to reduce real-world sample complexity while satisfying safety constraints during testing. However, prior work either guarantees constraint satisfaction without safe exploration (Cho & Sun, 2024) or requires the test task to be known a priori (Xu & Zhu, 2026). The open question is how to ensure every deployed policy during test-time adaptation is feasible (safe exploration) while achieving near-optimal reward with minimal interactions with the unknown test CMDP.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a policy-value set $\hat{\mathcal{U}}$ (pairs of near-optimal policies with their estimated reward and constraint values for each CMDP in a reduced training set) and a single simultaneously feasible policy $\pi_s$ that satisfies constraints across all CMDPs in the family, both produced by the offline training phase. During testing, it consumes truncated-horizon trajectories ($H = \tilde{\mathcal{O}}((1-\gamma)^{-1})$) from the unknown test CMDP under the current mixture policy. The output is a near-optimal feasible policy for the test CMDP with a sample complexity of $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\xi^{-2}\varepsilon^{-2}(1-\gamma)^{-5}\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\mathcal{D},\delta))$, where $\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\mathcal{D},\delta)$ is the covering number of the task distribution's high-density region, and safe exploration is guaranteed with high probability.

## Method

The algorithm has two phases. In training, it constructs a reduced CMDP set $\mathcal{U}$ via a greedy covering subroutine (adapted from the PCE algorithm of Ye et al., 2023), learns a near-optimal relaxed-$\varepsilon$-feasible policy for each CMDP in $\mathcal{U}$, and learns a single simultaneously $\xi$-feasible policy $\pi_s$ using a worst-case constraint gradient method. In testing, the key mechanism is an adaptive mixture policy $\pi_{l,m} = (1-\alpha_{l,m})\pi_s + \alpha_{l,m}\pi_l$ that starts at the safe policy ($\alpha=0$) and gradually increases $\alpha_{l,m}$ toward $1-\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)$ based on empirical constraint estimates. A closed-form update rule for $\alpha_{l,m}$ ensures feasibility at every step via an inductive argument, while a verification-and-elimination rule discards candidate policies whose empirical reward/constraint values deviate from training predictions. This yields sublinear test-time regret with a matching lower bound, showing the sample complexity is tight up to logarithmic factors.

## Results

On a 7×7 gridworld with noise levels drawn from a truncated Gaussian N(0.3, 0.03), the proposed algorithm achieves roughly 50% lower test-time reward regret than the best-performing baseline (Safe Meta-RL) while maintaining safe exploration across all 10 test CMDPs. Theoretically, the algorithm requires $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\xi^{-2}\varepsilon^{-2}(1-\gamma)^{-5}\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\mathcal{D},\delta))$ test-task samples to produce an $\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)$-optimal feasible policy with high-probability safe exploration, and a matching lower bound of $\tilde{\Omega}(\xi^{-2}\varepsilon^{-2}(1-\gamma)^{-5}\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\mathcal{D},\delta))$ is established, confirming near-optimality up to logarithmic factors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three baselines that all guarantee safe exploration: Safe Meta-RL (Xu & Zhu, 2026) from the constrained meta RL setting, DOPE+ (Yu et al., 2025) as a model-based constrained RL method, and LB-SGD (Ni & Kamgarpour, 2025) as a model-free constrained RL method. The proposed method outperforms all three baselines in test-time reward regret, achieving roughly 50% lower regret than the best baseline (Safe Meta-RL) on the gridworld task. All four methods maintain constraint satisfaction throughout testing, confirming safe exploration. The theoretical advantage is most pronounced when the task distribution $\mathcal{D}$ is concentrated (e.g., sub-Gaussian), where $\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\mathcal{D},\delta)$ is much smaller than $|S||\mathcal{A}|$, making the meta RL approach significantly more sample-efficient than the non-meta baselines. When $\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\mathcal{D},\delta)$ is large (e.g., uniform over a $d$-dimensional set scaling as $\varepsilon^{-d}$), the training phase offers little benefit over standard constrained RL.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Policy Collection-Elimination (PCE) framework of Ye et al. (2023) from unconstrained meta RL to the constrained setting, introducing mixture policies that blend a simultaneously feasible policy with candidate near-optimal policies to guarantee safe exploration during testing. It draws on oracles from constrained RL (CPO, DOPE) and meta constrained RL (Xu & Zhu 2026; As et al. 2026) for the training phase, and extends the hard-instance construction of Vaswani et al. (2022) to establish a matching lower bound. The key departure from prior constrained meta RL work (Cho & Sun 2024; Xu & Zhu 2026) is that safe exploration is guaranteed for an unknown test task without assuming knowledge of its parameters, and the sample complexity is characterized by the covering number C(D) rather than state/action space sizes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The matching upper and lower bounds establish that the proposed sample complexity Õ(ξ⁻²ε⁻²(1-γ)⁻⁵C(D)) is near-optimal, providing a clear theoretical target for future work in constrained meta RL. The dependence on the Slater margin ξ in both bounds highlights a fundamental trade-off: tighter safety margins make the problem harder, a quantity that is often small in practice. Limitations include the reliance on simultaneous Slater's condition (a single policy feasible for all tasks), linear expected-value constraints, and the assumption that the test task is drawn from the same distribution as training. Open questions include extending to distribution shift, nonlinear constraints (e.g., CVaR), and exploiting structural properties of the CMDP family to further reduce sample complexity.
