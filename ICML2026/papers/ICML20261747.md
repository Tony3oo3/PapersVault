## Problem

Existing matching-based hybrid approaches for GED computation predict a single deterministic node matching matrix and iteratively extract top-k node mappings from it, producing highly correlated candidates that easily fall into local optima, limited edit-path diversity, and sequential (non-parallelizable) extraction that scales poorly with k. No generative formulation has been explored for graph matching or GED computation that could produce diverse, high-quality matching matrices in parallel.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261747/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of undirected labeled graphs (G, G') and k randomly initialized binary node matching matrices of size |V|×|V'| as input, where k is chosen arbitrarily at inference time and is independent of training. It outputs k diverse real-valued node matching matrices, from which k injective node mappings are extracted in parallel via a greedy algorithm, yielding k candidate edit paths whose minimum edit cost gives the predicted GED. The discrete-to-continuous transition occurs only at the final denoising step, where the predicted probabilities are used directly as matching weights for extraction.

## Method

DiffGED formulates graph matching as a conditional discrete diffusion generation task: a forward process progressively corrupts a ground-truth binary matching matrix via categorical noise, and a matching-aware denoising network (DiffMatch) learns to reverse this process conditioned on the graph pair. The denoising network uses GIN for intra-graph node embedding updates and Anisotropic GNN (AGNN) for inter-graph node-pair embedding updates, with sinusoidal time-step conditioning, enabling direct computation of expressive node-pair representations rather than relying on inner products of node embeddings. During inference, DDIM reduces the reverse process to S=10 steps, and k random binary matrices are denoised in parallel to produce k diverse matching matrices. A greedy extraction algorithm (O(|V|²|V'|) per matrix, parallelizable across k matrices) then extracts one node mapping from each, avoiding the sequential top-k extraction bottleneck of prior methods. The key insight is that stochasticity from the diffusion process naturally decorrelates the k generated matrices, enabling diverse edit paths that capture the multimodal distribution of optimal solutions.

## Results

On the standard cross-train-test setting, DiffGED achieves near-perfect accuracy on Linux (100%, MAE 0.0) and AIDS700 (98%, MAE 0.022), and strong performance on the larger IMDB dataset (94.6% accuracy, MAE 0.937). Across all three datasets, DiffGED outperforms the best matching-based hybrid (GEDGNN) by 41–45 percentage points in accuracy on AIDS700, 3.4 points on Linux, and 9.1 points on IMDB. In terms of runtime, DiffGED (0.07–0.15 s per pair) is roughly 3–5× faster than GEDGNN and GEDIOT (0.13–0.42 s), while all A*-based hybrids (Noah, GENN-A*, MATA*) fail to complete on IMDB within 24 hours.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffGED decisively outperforms all matching-based hybrids: on AIDS700 it reaches 98% accuracy versus 52.5% (GEDGNN) and 53.5% (GEDIOT), a gap of roughly 45 points; on IMDB it achieves 94.6% versus 85.5% (GEDGNN) and 84.5% (GEDIOT). Against A*-based hybrids, DiffGED wins on accuracy for every dataset where they complete (e.g., 98% vs. 61.5% for GENN-A* on AIDS700), and it is the only method that scales to IMDB, where Noah, GENN-A*, and MATA* all exceed the 24-hour timeout. In runtime, DiffGED (0.07–0.15 s) is 3–5× faster than GEDGNN/GEDIOT (0.13–0.42 s) due to parallel generation and extraction, though MATA* is faster on small graphs (0.005 s) but collapses on larger ones. On the additional ZINC and Code2 datasets, DiffGED again leads (70.8% and 97% accuracy) while GEDGNN and GEDIOT drop to 6.6% and 36.1% respectively. Compared with the LLM-designed heuristic Grail, DiffGED achieves lower MAE and higher accuracy on AIDS, Linux, ZINC, and Code2, though Grail is faster on small graphs and marginally better on IMDB (where ground-truth GED is only approximated).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant matching-based hybrid paradigm (GEDGNN, GEDIOT) that predicts a single deterministic node matching matrix and iteratively extracts correlated top-k mappings, by reformulating graph matching as a conditional generative task solved with a discrete diffusion model (DiffMatch). It also builds on the A*-search-based hybrid line (Noah, GENN-A*, MATA*) by avoiding the exponential search space entirely, and extends discrete diffusion from graph generation (DiGress, D3PM) to the previously unexplored setting of graph matching. The matching-aware denoising network introduces AGNN to directly compute node-pair embeddings conditioned on noisy matching matrices, a design not present in prior deterministic GNN-based matchers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The near-exact accuracy (95–100%) across multiple datasets, combined with parallel generation of k diverse edit paths, addresses a long-standing quality gap in approximate GED computation and opens the door to using generative models for other combinatorial graph problems where multimodal optima exist. Key limitations include the quadratic |V|×|V'| matching matrix that constrains scalability to very large graphs, and the multi-step denoising overhead relative to single-shot predictors. Open questions raised by the authors include whether consistency training or latent diffusion can reduce inference steps, whether heuristic pruning can mitigate the quadratic bottleneck, and how to handle incremental learning in dynamically evolving graph databases.
