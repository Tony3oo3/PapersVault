## Problem

Longitudinal management of blinding fundus diseases requires balancing timely anti-VEGF intervention against over-treatment risks, but existing approaches fail: traditional vision models are memoryless and uninterpretable, while VLMs rely on open-loop text reasoning that lacks anatomical grounding and cannot simulate how pathology evolves under different treatments. Additionally, clinical records contain only sparse binary outcomes without dense intermediate pathological annotations, hindering robust learning of disease dynamics.

## Input / Output

The system consumes five central foveal B-scans from each OCT volume (selected to balance anatomical coverage against redundancy, as validated by ablation), along with structured clinical history text and a discrete treatment action instruction (e.g., 'anti-VEGF injection, 4-week interval'). Outputs are structured as a five-step causal chain: a multi-label biomarker set over six indicators (IRF, SRF, DRT/ME, SHRM, IR HRF, VMT), a three-class disease trend label, a binary Inject/Observe decision with follow-up interval, and a simulated future OCT B-scan generated by the diffusion-based visual simulator. The simulated image serves as a verification signal rather than a final deliverable, feeding back into the policy's reflection step.

## Method

ORBIT couples a Policy Agent (fine-tuned Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct) with a Visual Simulator (fine-tuned Qwen-Image-Edit diffusion model) in a closed-loop Hypothesize-Simulate-Rectify pipeline. The policy factorizes its decision into five atomic steps—Perception, Evolution, Foresight, Reflection, Decision—while the simulator learns stochastic transition dynamics T(s'|s,a) by minimizing a hybrid diffusion loss plus LPIPS structural preservation term on longitudinal OCT pairs, implicitly capturing pharmacodynamic responses without explicit change-map supervision. Training uses a two-stage curriculum: structured SFT to internalize Chain-of-Thought clinical reasoning, followed by GRPO reinforcement learning with a composite reward (format compliance, biomarker recall with soft-penalty, trend consistency, and hierarchical gated strategy reward). At inference, the policy proposes a candidate treatment, the simulator generates a counterfactual anatomical future, and a safety critic rectifies the decision if the simulated risk score exceeds a threshold (τ=0.7), grounding final plans in verifiable anatomical consequences rather than linguistic probabilities. A Logic-Constrained Abductive Data Engine reconstructs dense pathological trajectories from sparse clinical records via a Propose-and-Verify paradigm, using decision-biomarker consistency and temporal plausibility gates to filter MLLM hallucinations.

## Results

On the primary Decision task, ORBIT achieves 88.52% Overall Accuracy, 82.29% Sensitivity, and 95.63% Specificity, substantially outperforming the best baseline (Hulu-Med-32B at 79.63% Acc) while avoiding the extreme behavioral biases of other models. On the Perception task, ORBIT reaches 78.12% Accuracy and 21.88 Hamming Loss on ORBIT-LD (vs. 71.70% / 28.30 for Hulu-Med-32B), and generalizes to 69.75% Accuracy on the out-of-distribution OLIVES dataset. On the Evolution task, ORBIT attains 80.38% Accuracy and 53.24% F1, surpassing the next-best model (QoQ-Med-VL-32B) by 7.69% in Accuracy and 17.04 points in F1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ORBIT decisively outperforms all six baselines on the Decision task, achieving 88.52% Overall Accuracy versus the next-best Hulu-Med-32B (79.63%), a 8.89-point margin. The comparison reveals two dangerous failure modes in baselines: Qwen3-VL-32B-Instruct exhibits an extreme interventionist bias (99.31% Sens / 11.11% Spec), recommending injection for nearly all patients, while QoQ-Med-VL-32B shows the opposite conservative bias (26.04% Sens / 88.49% Spec), missing active disease. ORBIT uniquely balances both metrics (82.29% Sens / 95.63% Spec), avoiding over-treatment while maintaining adequate sensitivity. On the Perception task, ORBIT surpasses the strongest medical VLM (Hulu-Med-32B) by 6.42% in Accuracy and reduces Hamming Loss from 28.30 to 21.88, and outperforms the 78B-parameter InternVL3.5 by 8.72% on the OOD OLIVES benchmark. On the Evolution task, ORBIT's 53.24% F1 exceeds the best baseline (QoQ-Med-VL-32B at 36.20%) by 17 points, indicating substantially better temporal consistency in disease trend classification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the world-model paradigm (Ha & Schmidhuber, 2018; LeCun, 2022) from robotics and solid-tumor modeling (MeWM, Yang et al., 2025) to the stochastic, reversible pharmacodynamics of retinal disease. It departs from standard medical VLMs (Hulu-Med, Lingshu, QoQ-Med) that perform open-loop text reasoning by introducing a closed-loop architecture where a diffusion-based visual simulator (built on Qwen-Image-Edit) generates counterfactual anatomical futures that the VLM policy agent (Qwen3-VL-8B) then verifies. The POMDP formulation and the Logic-Constrained Abductive Data Engine for reconstructing dense supervision from sparse clinical records represent a methodological departure from both static classification and purely linguistic chain-of-thought approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ORBIT addresses a genuine clinical gap—longitudinal anti-VEGF treatment planning where static observations cannot distinguish active leakage from stable residual fluid—and the counterfactual visual foresight mechanism offers a principled safety layer beyond text-based reasoning. Key open questions include whether the single-rollout simulator without calibrated uncertainty suffices for high-stakes decisions, whether central B-scan-only input misses off-foveal pathology, and whether the approach generalizes across scanners and institutions. The Logic-Constrained Abductive Data Engine is potentially transferable to other longitudinal medical domains suffering from the same supervision gap, though its reliance on MLLM proposals and expert review limits full automation.
