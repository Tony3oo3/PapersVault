## Problem

Existing regret bounds for Thompson sampling in the linear-Gaussian bandit exhibit a multiplicative coupling between prior diffusiveness √Tr(Σ₀) and the minimax rate d√T, yielding bounds of order d√(T(σ² + r²Tr(Σ₀))). This paper shows the correct dependence is additive: the prior-dependent 'burn-in' cost dr√Tr(Σ₀) decouples from the long-run minimax regret σd√T, and proves this separation is tight via a matching lower bound.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a d-dimensional action set A ⊂ rB₂^d (the ℓ₂-ball of radius r), a Gaussian prior θ* ~ N(0, Σ₀) with arbitrary positive-definite covariance, and sequential scalar reward observations R_{t+1} = θ*ᵀA_t + W_{t+1} with W ~ N(0, σ²). The output is the Bayesian regret bound Reg(T) = Õ(σd√T + dr√Tr(Σ₀)), where the two terms correspond respectively to the irreducible noise-driven exploration cost and the one-time cost of resolving prior uncertainty across all d dimensions.

## Method

The key technical tool is a generalized elliptical potential lemma that bounds ∑‖u_t‖^{2p}_{V_t^{-1}} for p ∈ [0,1] without requiring V₀ ⪰ I_d, introducing a burn-in term Tr(V₀^{-p}) − Tr(V_T^{-p}) alongside the standard log-det term. The proof splits each summand into a 'small' regime (‖u_t‖²_{V_t^{-1}} ≤ 2) controlled by log-determinant ratios and a 'large' regime controlled by a trace-difference argument exploiting the convexity of −Tr(M^p) on positive definite matrices and a Hölder-based interpolation inequality. Applying this with p = 1/2 and appropriate scaling of the precision matrix yields the additive regret decomposition. A matching lower bound, adapted from Rusmevichientong & Tsitsiklis (2010) via Weyl's eigenvalue perturbation inequality, confirms the burn-in term is unavoidable for any policy.

## Results

The main result (Corollary 2) establishes that Thompson sampling achieves Bayesian regret of $\widetilde{O}(\sigma d\sqrt{T} + dr\sqrt{\text{Tr}(\Sigma_0)})$ in the linear-Gaussian bandit, where the prior-dependent burn-in term $dr\sqrt{\text{Tr}(\Sigma_0)}$ decouples additively from the minimax rate $\sigma d\sqrt{T}$. A matching lower bound (Theorem 6) shows that for any policy, regret is at least $c \cdot r d \sqrt{\text{Tr}(\Sigma_0)}$ in the burn-in regime and $\sqrt{2/\pi}(d-1)\sigma\sqrt{T}$ in the long-run regime, confirming the bound is tight up to logarithmic factors. The key technical contribution is a generalized elliptical potential lemma (Lemma 3) that handles arbitrary initial precision $V_0 \succ 0$ and fractional exponents $p \in [0,1]$, enabling the additive separation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The closest prior bound is Kalkanli & Özgür (2020), which gives $d\sqrt{T(\sigma^2 + r^2\text{Tr}(\Sigma_0))}\log(1+T/d)$, where the prior diffusiveness $\sqrt{\text{Tr}(\Sigma_0)}$ multiplies the $d\sqrt{T}$ minimax rate. This paper's bound improves this to an additive $\sigma d\sqrt{T} + dr\sqrt{\text{Tr}(\Sigma_0)}$, which is strictly smaller whenever $T$ is large relative to $\text{Tr}(\Sigma_0)/\sigma^2$. Other prior bounds (Abeille & Lazaric 2017, Agrawal & Goyal 2013, Russo & Van Roy 2013/2014) either assume bounded parameters or bounded rewards, implicitly coupling the regret to $\|\theta_\star\|_2$ in a way that does not cleanly separate the burn-in from the long-run rate. The lower bound (Theorem 6) further shows that the additive burn-in term is unavoidable for any policy, making Thompson sampling near-optimal in most parameter regimes. The bound also extends to strongly log-concave priors and noise (Theorem 9), going beyond the Gaussian assumption of all prior work.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the elliptical potential lemma of Abeille & Lazaric (2017) to handle arbitrary positive-definite initial precision matrices and fractional exponents p ∈ [0,1], introducing a 'burn-in' trace term that captures the contribution of small initial eigenvalues. It adapts the lower-bound construction of Rusmevichientong & Tsitsiklis (2010) to the Bayesian setting with a Gaussian prior, and its entire analysis is structured around Thompson sampling's posterior-sampling mechanism. The key departure from prior work (notably Kalkanli & Özgür 2020) is showing that the prior diffusiveness term decouples additively from the minimax rate, rather than multiplying it as in all previously known bounds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The additive decoupling of the burn-in term dr√Tr(Σ₀) from the long-run rate σd√T resolves a looseness present in all prior regret bounds for Thompson sampling in the linear-Gaussian bandit, and the matching lower bound confirms the burn-in is unavoidable. The extension to strongly log-concave priors and noise broadens applicability beyond the Gaussian case. Open questions include whether the results extend to frequentist (non-Bayesian) regret, misspecified priors, and whether the log-concavity assumption can be relaxed; the paper also notes the well-specified-prior assumption as a natural limitation.
