## Problem

Existing statistical theory for diffusion models either suffers from the curse of dimensionality (rates depending on ambient dimension d) or requires strong regularity assumptions such as uniformly bounded densities, smooth scores, or log-concavity. A fundamental gap remains: can diffusion models provably adapt to intrinsic low-dimensional structure while accommodating multi-modal data, achieving sample complexity governed by intrinsic dimension k rather than ambient dimension d, under only mild distributional assumptions?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265003/cropped_page8_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes n i.i.d. samples from a target distribution p* supported on a union of M linear subspaces (each of dimension at most k), along with a standard Gaussian initialization for the reverse SDE. It uses sample splitting: n₀ = O(M²k log n) samples for exact subspace recovery and the remaining N ≈ n samples for score estimation. The output is a generated sample whose distribution is W₁-close to p*, with the guarantee that Õ(ε^-(k∨2)) samples suffice for ε-accuracy, and an intermediate L² score estimation error bound of Õ((1/N)(1/t + 1/t^((k∨2)/2+1))) at each diffusion time t.

## Method

The core insight is a normal-tangent decomposition of the score on each subspace: the normal component has a closed-form Gaussian expression, while the tangent component is entirely determined by a k_i-dimensional score function, reducing the estimation problem from ambient dimension d to intrinsic dimension k_i. The proposed kernel-based score estimator constructs a weighted mixture of per-subspace score components, where each component is estimated via a Gaussian kernel density estimator in k_i dimensions with hard thresholding (to suppress noise in low-density regions) and clipping (to ensure boundedness). Mixture weights are estimated as a plug-in ratio of kernel density estimates, regularized by a geometric indicator restricting attention to points near the corresponding subspace. Under exact subspace recovery (achievable with O(M²k log n) samples via classical subspace clustering), the resulting L² score error depends only on k, and plugging this into a stability bound for the reverse SDE yields a W₁ sampling rate of Õ(dM^(3/2) · n^(-1/(k∨2))), matching the minimax optimal rate for k-dimensional distribution learning up to logarithmic factors.

## Results

The main theoretical guarantee (Theorem 2) shows that diffusion models require at most O~(ε^(-(k∨2))) samples to achieve ε-accuracy in 1-Wasserstein distance, where k is the intrinsic dimension and d is the ambient dimension. The L² score estimation error (Theorem 1) scales as O~(1/N · (1/t + σ^(k∨2)/t^((k∨2)/2+1))), depending only on k rather than d. This rate matches the minimax optimal rate for learning a k-dimensional distribution (Chewi et al., 2024), up to logarithmic factors. A numerical experiment on a synthetic distribution in ℝ⁴⁸ with M=128 subspaces of intrinsic dimension k=3 and N=50,000 samples confirms the predicted mild dependence on diffusion time t, consistent with intrinsic-dimension scaling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no direct method-vs-method benchmark comparisons. The key comparison is against prior sample complexity bounds: general smooth-density results (Zhang et al., 2024; Cai & Li, 2025; Dou et al., 2024) yield rates of ε^(-(d+2β)/β), which suffer from the curse of dimensionality as ambient dimension d grows. Single-subspace or single-manifold results (Chen et al., 2023; Azangulov et al., 2024; Tang & Yang, 2024) achieve intrinsic-dimension rates but require strong density lower-bound assumptions that exclude multi-modal distributions with well-separated modes. This paper's contribution is to extend intrinsic-dimension rates to the multi-modal union-of-subspaces setting under only a subgaussian tail assumption, without requiring bounded densities, smooth scores, or log-concavity. The resulting O~(ε^(-(k∨2))) sample complexity is (near-)minimax optimal and represents the first such guarantee for multi-modal low-dimensional targets under minimal distributional assumptions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the statistical theory of diffusion models from single low-dimensional structures (subspaces, manifolds) to multi-modal distributions supported on a union of subspaces, while simultaneously weakening the distributional assumptions from bounded densities or smooth scores to mere subgaussian tails. It builds directly on the normal-tangent score decomposition of Chen et al. (2023), the kernel-based score estimator of Cai & Li (2025), and the score-to-Wasserstein stability bound of Oko et al. (2023), combining them in a way that prior single-structure analyses could not. The key departure is that existing low-dimensional adaptation results (Chen et al. 2023, Azangulov et al. 2024, Tang & Yang 2024) all require the density to be bounded away from zero on its support, which is incompatible with well-separated multi-modal components; this paper removes that requirement entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result provides the first rigorous justification that diffusion models can achieve near-minimax optimal sample complexity (governed by intrinsic dimension k, not ambient dimension d) for multi-modal low-dimensional data under minimal assumptions, explaining a key aspect of their empirical success in high-dimensional applications. The kernel-based estimator is explicitly a proof device rather than a practical algorithm, so the immediate impact is theoretical: it establishes the statistical limit and identifies what NN-based score estimators must capture. Open questions include extending the analysis to practical NN score estimators, noisy settings (data near rather than on subspaces), manifolds of varying dimensions, and end-to-end discretization bounds for the reverse SDE/ODE.
