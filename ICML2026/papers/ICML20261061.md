## Problem

Sparse frame sampling in Large Video-Language Models (often <0.5 fps) creates temporal blind spots where causal transitions and object persistence are lost. Existing remedies—diffusion-based pixel inpainting and autoregressive token extrapolation—rely on statistical texture priors rather than physical constraints, causing object vanishing, hallucinated content, and semantic drift over long gaps. The authors diagnose this as 'Kinematic Naivety': treating video frames as independent tokens rather than states in a continuous dynamical system with conservation laws.

## Input / Output

The method consumes sparsely sampled video frames (4–8 per video) encoded into 768-dimensional CLIP-ViT-L/14 latent vectors as boundary anchors, together with a natural language query projected into the same latent space. It outputs a dense sequence of intermediate latent vectors (not pixels) that bridges the temporal gap, which is then passed to a frozen LVLM/QA decoder. Structurally, it solves a Two-Point Boundary Value Problem: given z_start and z_end, all intermediate states are optimized simultaneously rather than generated sequentially, and the resolution term H×W is entirely absent from the inference loop.

## Method

SLAP models the latent video trajectory as a particle on a Riemannian manifold governed by a Semantic Lagrangian L = T − λV, where the kinetic term T = ½‖ż‖² enforces temporal smoothness (penalizing object teleportation) and the potential term V = −P_θ(z,q) provides text-guided attraction. A lightweight Potential Field Network P_θ (ResMLP with SwiGLU activations and spectral normalization) is trained via InfoNCE contrastive loss plus Sobolev gradient regularization to approximate the frozen LLM's energy landscape at ~10⁴× lower cost than backpropagating through the full model. At inference, the Lagrangian Bridge minimizes the discrete action S_disc = Σ[½‖(z_{t+1}−z_t)/Δt‖² − λP_θ(z_t,q)] via gradient descent, solving for all intermediate states jointly. Theoretical results guarantee strict convexity of the action functional under a bounded-Hessian condition and provide a quadratic trajectory error bound ‖ẑ−z*‖_{L∞} ≤ T²ε/μ linking potential approximation error to interpolation quality, yielding a 177× speedup over diffusion baselines while reducing object vanishing by 68%.

## Results

On the novel Tunnel Test benchmark (object persistence under occlusion), SLAP achieves 83.9% accuracy, a 4.7/5 persistence score, and 0.14 semantic drift, outperforming all baselines by a wide margin. On MSR-VTT QA under extreme sparsity (10% frames retained), SLAP maintains 41.8% accuracy with only a 3.4-point drop from the 50% setting, compared to 9.8-point drops for Video-LLaMA 3 and 8.6-point drops for Stable Video Diffusion. In terms of efficiency, SLAP requires only 0.15 TeraFLOPs and 0.08 s latency per 10-second interpolation, yielding a 177.5× speedup over Stable Video Diffusion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SLAP dominates all three primary baselines on every reported metric. Against Stable Video Diffusion (SVD), SLAP improves accuracy by 12.5 points (83.9% vs. 71.4%) and halves semantic drift (0.14 vs. 0.28), while also achieving a 177.5× inference speedup (0.08 s vs. 14.20 s); SVD's pixel-level generative prior causes it to hallucinate empty tunnels, losing the occluded object. Against Video-LLaMA 3 (autoregressive), SLAP gains 15.8 points in accuracy (83.9% vs. 68.1%) and 0.8 points in persistence (4.7 vs. 3.9), with the gap widening to 12 points on action-centric queries under extreme sparsity on MSR-VTT. Against Neural CDE / Latent ODE, SLAP leads by 19.2 points in accuracy (83.9% vs. 64.7%) and 0.9 in persistence, confirming that explicit kinetic-energy regularization outperforms purely differential-equation-based trajectory modeling for object permanence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Principle of Least Action from classical mechanics and the energy-based model framework (LeCun 2006; Greydanus 2019; Cranmer 2020) to reframe latent video interpolation as a boundary value problem, departing fundamentally from both diffusion-based generative interpolation (SVD, VideoLDM) and autoregressive token prediction (Video-LLaMA, LLaVA-Video). It builds on CLIP-style contrastive embeddings as the Riemannian manifold substrate and on InfoNCE-trained proxy networks as amortized energy approximations, but the key conceptual move is treating the text query as a potential field whose gradient acts as a 'semantic force' on a particle with inertia, rather than as an attention condition. The method is closest in spirit to Lagrangian/Hamiltonian neural networks and Neural ODEs, but replaces their initial-value ODE integration with a global two-point BVP solved via discrete Euler-Lagrange equations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 177× inference speedup and 0.8 GB VRAM footprint make latent-space temporal interpolation viable on edge devices, which is practically significant for video search and streaming applications. However, the trajectory error bound grows quadratically with gap duration T (Theorem 3.7), limiting applicability to very long occlusions or minute-scale gaps without hierarchical solving. The Semantic Isometry Hypothesis (Assumption 3.1) is strong and unverified for standard ViT encoders, and the Tunnel Test is synthetic, leaving open whether the object-persistence gains transfer to natural, multi-object, multi-action scenes. A key open question is whether the lightweight proxy potential P_θ can faithfully approximate the energy landscape of increasingly large LLMs without retraining, and whether the convexity guarantee (Theorem 3.6) holds under the non-convex landscapes that real semantic embeddings exhibit.
