## Problem

Appearance-based gaze estimation relies heavily on large-scale annotated data, and existing semi-supervised contrastive learning methods for regression (e.g., CLSS) were designed for single-target tasks, making them inapplicable to multi-dimensional gaze prediction due to inherent rank ambiguity when collapsing a multi-dimensional label space into a scalar ordering. This paper is the first to extend semi-supervised contrastive learning to gaze estimation by resolving this multi-target ambiguity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264363/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 224×224 monocular RGB facial images as input and predicts gaze direction as either 2D Euler angles (pitch, yaw) or a 3D gaze vector. It operates in a semi-supervised regime where only 5%, 10%, or 20% of the training set carries gaze labels while the remainder is unlabeled, and it is designed as a plug-and-play module that can be embedded into any existing gaze estimation backbone (ResNet-18, Gazetr, RT-Gene, PureGaze, PnP-GA, etc.) without adding trainable parameters.

## Method

DSCL first applies Jacobian regularization (L_J) on labeled data to enforce column-sparsity in the regressor's Jacobian matrix, which decomposes the N-dimensional feature space into M orthogonal subspaces, each exclusively controlling one gaze component. A binary mask matrix M (constructed via argmax of |J| per column) then routes each feature dimension to its assigned subspace through element-wise multiplication, acting as a non-differentiable gate during forward pass and a constant filter during backpropagation. Within each disentangled subspace, supervised contrastive loss (L_SC) aligns feature distances with label distances on labeled samples, while unsupervised contrastive loss (L_UC) and ranking loss (L_UR) exploit spectral seriation on the feature similarity matrix to derive ordinal rankings for unlabeled samples, providing implicit supervision without pseudo-labels. Training proceeds in two phases: an initialization stage using only L_reg + L_J to establish a stable mask, followed by full semi-supervised fine-tuning with the combined loss L_Total = L_reg + γL_J + w_SC·L_SC + w_UC·L_UC + w_UR·L_UR.

## Results

On Gaze360 (in-domain), Baseline+DSCL with only 5% labeled data achieves 14.21° MAE, approaching the full-supervised baseline (13.59°) and outperforming the 5% supervised baseline (23.00°) by 8.79°. In cross-domain evaluation (Gaze360→MPIIGaze), PnP-GA+DSCL with 5% labeled data reaches 6.62° MAE, surpassing PnP-GA trained on 100% data (6.32°) by a narrow margin and beating PnP-GA at 5% (9.85°) by 3.23°. The framework is plug-and-play, yielding consistent 0.1°–4.2° MAE reductions across all tested backbones (Gazetr, RT-Gene, FullFace, Dilated-Net, ST-WSGE) without adding trainable parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DSCL consistently outperforms all 17 compared baselines across in-domain and cross-domain settings. In-domain on Gaze360, Baseline+DSCL at 5% labeled data (14.21°) beats ST-WSGE (21.33°), L2CS-Net (22.82°), and Gazetr (19.86°) by 4–9°, and even approaches the full-supervised RT-Gene (12.26°). Cross-domain (G→M), PnP-GA+DSCL at 5% (6.62°) surpasses PnP-GA at 5% (9.85°) by 3.23° and nearly matches PnP-GA at 100% (6.32°); PureGaze+DSCL at 10% (10.57°) beats PureGaze at 10% (14.68°) by 4.11°. Against dedicated SSL methods (Table 4), DSCL dominates: at 5% it achieves 14.21° vs. CLSS 20.77°, UCVME 21.49°, and Rankup 24.95°. For multi-target regression (M=3), DSCL (13.49° avg) outperforms CLSS (18.95° avg) by 5.46°, confirming that disentanglement resolves the rank ambiguity that degrades scalar-ranking-based SCL. On Sarcos (7-target) and Dsprites (3-target) regression tasks, DSCL also beats CLSS on MAE, RMSE, Pearson, and Spearman across all label budgets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the semi-supervised contrastive learning framework of Dai et al. (CLSS, NeurIPS 2023), which was designed for single-target regression, to the multi-target setting of gaze estimation (pitch and yaw). The key departure is identifying a fundamental dimensional mismatch: a scalar spectral rank cannot preserve independent ordinal relationships across multiple target dimensions, a problem the authors formalize as 'rank ambiguity' (Proposition 3.1). Their solution—Jacobian regularization to disentangle features into component-specific subspaces before applying per-subspace contrastive learning—repurposes ideas from representation disentanglement and supervised contrastive regression (Zhang et al., ICLR 2023) into a unified semi-supervised architecture. The method is positioned as plug-and-play, composable with existing domain generalization (PureGaze, CDG) and domain adaptation (PnP-GA, UnReGA) pipelines rather than replacing them.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for gaze estimation in resource-constrained settings: achieving near full-supervised accuracy with only 5% labeled data (14.21° vs 13.59° MAE on Gaze360) dramatically reduces annotation cost, and the plug-and-play design means existing deployed systems can benefit without architectural changes. The theoretical result on rank ambiguity in multi-target scalar ranking provides a clear explanation for why prior single-target SSL methods (CLSS, Rankup) fail or underperform in multi-dimensional regression, guiding future work. Open questions include whether the binary mask construction generalizes beyond 2–3 gaze components, how the method behaves with highly correlated target dimensions, and whether the Jacobian regularization cost scales to very high-dimensional feature spaces.
