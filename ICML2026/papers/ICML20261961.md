## Problem

In-Context Alignment (ICA) methods struggle with the Instruction Bottleneck: when multiple human values are specified in a single prompt, LLMs fail to reconcile them, producing biased or incomplete alignment. Human values are inherently pluralistic and often impose conflicting demands (e.g., stimulation vs. tradition), yet existing ICA methods treat values as a unitary concept and lack a principled mechanism to balance multiple value objectives simultaneously without fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

The method takes a small set of task prompts (N=50), a value composition V of K values (each defined by a natural-language description or implicit demonstrations), a textual observation s (a modest set of value-embodied demonstrations), and a frozen target LLM accessible via API. It produces a single optimized meta-instruction e*—a natural-language string prepended to future prompts—that steers the LLM to generate responses conforming to all K values simultaneously. The optimization is training-free and query-agnostic: once e* is found, it can be applied to unseen task prompts without re-optimization.

## Method

PICACO reformulates pluralistic alignment as a black-box optimization problem by maximizing the conditional Total Correlation (TC) between the value composition V and LLM responses, decomposed into a Pluralistic Conformity term (sum of per-value mutual informations, bounded via Barber-Agakov) and a Redundancy Reduction term (joint mutual information, bounded via a conditional CLUB extension). The objective is optimized through Variational Information Maximization (VIM), an EM-like iterative loop with two alternating steps: (1) Response Enhancement, which samples responses under the current meta-instruction and maintains an aligned pool (top responses maximizing q_ω − q_φ) and a noisy pool (responses with high q_φ superficial similarity to demonstrations); and (2) Instruction Refinement, which searches for a new meta-instruction that maximizes the probability of aligned responses while a regularizer penalizes generation of noisy content. A value evaluator q_ω (GPT-4o-mini) scores per-value conformity, and a redundancy evaluator q_φ (cosine similarity) penalizes superficial copying, with a hyperparameter β controlling the conformity–redundancy trade-off. The entire procedure requires no gradient access to the target LLM, making it applicable to both black-box and open-source models.

## Results

PICACO achieves the highest or near-highest overall conformity (1–5 scale, judged by GPT-4o) across five value compositions and two target LLMs. On GPT-3.5-Turbo, PICACO scores 3.788 (Confucianism-4), 3.135 (Liberalism-4), 4.257 (HH Balance-8), 4.287 (Helpfulness-4), and 4.173 (Harmlessness-4), outperforming the strongest baseline OPRO on 3 of 5 compositions. On LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct, PICACO scores 3.471, 2.987, 4.110, 4.118, and 4.112 respectively, again leading or tying on most compositions. Friedman's test confirms statistically significant differences among methods (all p < 10⁻⁴).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PICACO outperforms OPRO (the strongest iterative baseline) on Confucianism (3.788 vs. 3.713) and Liberalism (3.135 vs. 2.961) for GPT-3.5-Turbo, and on Liberalism (2.987 vs. 2.925), Helpfulness (4.118 vs. 4.104), and Harmlessness (4.112 vs. 4.095) for LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct, but narrowly trails OPRO on HH Balance-8 (4.257 vs. 4.286 for GPT-3.5; 4.110 vs. 4.114 for LLaMA). Against URIAL (human-crafted meta-instructions + demonstrations), PICACO wins on Confucianism and Liberalism for GPT-3.5-Turbo but loses on Confucianism for LLaMA (3.471 vs. 3.530). PICACO surpasses MODULAR PLURALISM (multi-LLM community approach) on Liberalism for both models and on Helpfulness for LLaMA, while MODULAR edges ahead on Harmlessness for GPT-3.5-Turbo (4.177 vs. 4.173). Naive baselines (Q, Q+IF, Q+IF+CoT) and persona-based methods (MP+SYSTEM 1/2) are consistently outperformed by PICACO across all compositions and both target LLMs, with gaps of 0.3–1.1 points on Schwartz value compositions. On OOD tasks (Table 2), PICACO also leads all baselines (3.598 Confucianism, 3.323 Liberalism vs. OPRO's 3.530 and 3.262).

## Relation to Prior Work

PICACO repurposes Total Correlation (Gao et al., 2019) as an optimization objective for in-context alignment, a setting where it had not previously been applied. The variational machinery draws on the Barber-Agakov bound (Barber & Agakov, 2004) for the conformity term and a conditional extension of the CLUB bound (Cheng et al., 2020) for the redundancy-reduction term, combined in an EM-like iterative loop. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior ICA methods (URIAL, OPRO, Modular Pluralism) that either hand-craft meta-instructions, optimize via heuristic search, or rely on multiple fine-tuned community models, by formulating pluralistic alignment as a principled information-theoretic black-box optimization problem over the instruction space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap—pluralistic value alignment via in-context methods without fine-tuning—and provides a clean theoretical justification (TC maximization) for why the optimized meta-instruction should elicit balanced multi-value conformity. The 'fake alignment' diagnostic for Schwartz values and the finding that smaller models are disproportionately sensitive to ICA method choice are practically useful observations. Open questions remain around scaling to more than eight values, handling severe value conflicts (e.g., Tradition vs. Hedonism) beyond the current soft-trade-off framing, and reducing dependence on a strong value evaluator (q_ω) that acts as a quality bottleneck.
