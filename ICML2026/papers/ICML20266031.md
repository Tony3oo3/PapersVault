## Problem

Self-supervised visual representation learning faces a fundamental invariance-equivariance tension: semantic understanding requires representations to be invariant to spatial transformations (crops, shifts), while faithful image reconstruction requires them to be equivariant, tracking every pixel change. Existing methods resolve this by prioritizing one objective—joint-embedding methods like DINO yield strong semantics but weak reconstruction, while masked-image-modeling methods like MAE preserve spatial detail but produce weaker semantic abstractions—leaving no single representation that excels at both.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image (224×224 for ViT-Base) and produces a factorized latent representation Z = LS of rank at most r (default r=16), where S ∈ R^{r×d} holds sparse semantic concept tokens and L ∈ R^{n×r} is a row-stochastic localization matrix assigning each of the n patches to the r concepts. This yields roughly a 90% reduction in latent size compared to a dense n×d grid. A lightweight 6-layer ViT decoder reconstructs the image from LS, and the dense backbone feature map U is also retained for downstream dense-prediction tasks.

## Method

STELLAR factorizes the latent feature map into a low-rank product Z = LS, where a ViT encoder with r learnable latent queries produces sparse concept tokens S, and the localization matrix L is computed as a softmax of cosine similarities between dense patch features and the sparse tokens (structurally analogous to single-head cross-attention). This factorization partitions spatial equivariance: under a transformation, L changes to track spatial shifts while S remains comparatively stable, resolving the invariance-equivariance tension. Training jointly optimizes six objectives: pixel-level reconstruction from LS, Sinkhorn-Knopp balanced concept clustering of sparse tokens onto K learnable prototypes, entropy-regularized optimal transport (Sinkhorn) for set-alignment of sparse tokens across augmented views (100× faster than Hungarian matching), CLS-token clustering and alignment, and KoLeo regularization for concept diversification. The framework can be initialized from a pretrained MAE or DINO backbone or trained from scratch with a momentum encoder for target assignments.

## Results

STELLAR achieves the strongest joint semantic-reconstruction performance among reconstruction-feasible representations: with only 16 sparse tokens, the ViT-H model reaches 79.10% ImageNet-1K linear probing accuracy and 2.60 FID, while the ViT-B model achieves 73.26% linear accuracy and 3.06 FID. On dense prediction, STELLAR (ViT-B) attains 31.33 mIoU on ADE20K and 81.83 mIoU on Pascal VOC, surpassing MAE (30.91/76.43) and DINO (26.87/79.29). The factorized representation reduces latent size by ~90% compared to dense 196-patch features while maintaining comparable reconstruction quality (LPIPS 0.2077 vs. MAE's 0.2071).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MAE (196 dense tokens), STELLAR (16 sparse tokens) improves linear probing by +6.94% (73.26 vs. 66.32) and kNN by +41.43% (67.25 vs. 25.82) while matching reconstruction quality (FID 3.06 vs. 3.02, LPIPS 0.2077 vs. 0.2071). Against DINO's dense features, STELLAR wins on both reconstruction (FID 3.06 vs. 3.27) and kNN (67.25 vs. 54.41), though DINO's global CLS token retains a semantic edge (76.46 vs. 73.26 linear) that is not reconstruction-feasible. Against TiTok, the closest sparse-reconstruction competitor, STELLAR achieves dramatically better semantics (73.26 vs. 33.42 linear) with comparable FID (3.06 vs. 2.75) and substantially better per-image fidelity (LPIPS 0.2077 vs. 0.3281). On dense prediction, STELLAR outperforms MAE on ADE20K (31.33 vs. 30.91) and Pascal VOC (81.83 vs. 76.43), and surpasses DINO on both (31.33 vs. 26.87; 81.83 vs. 79.29), though iBOT (ViT-B) edges ahead on ADE20K (31.78) and IN1K (76.40). DINOv2, trained on 100–1000× more data, remains stronger across all benchmarks (e.g., 82.82% IN1K, 40.10 ADE20K), confirming that data scale still dominates when available.

## Relation to Prior Work

STELLAR builds directly on the DINO family of self-supervised methods (learnable prototypes, Sinkhorn-Knopp balanced clustering, augmentation alignment) and the MAE reconstruction paradigm, but departs from both by factorizing the latent feature map into a low-rank product Z = LS that separates invariant semantic tokens S from equivariant spatial coefficients L. This factorized form is conceptually related to slot-based object decomposition (Slot Attention) and sparse token models (TiTok, BLIP-2), but differs in that the sparse tokens are learned end-to-end via a unified SSL objective rather than for explicit object discovery or generative efficiency. The use of entropy-regularized optimal transport (Sinkhorn) for set-level token matching across views is a novel application of OT to the SSL alignment problem, replacing the global-pooling alignment used in DINO or the Hungarian matching used in detection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the long-standing invariance-equivariance tension in visual SSL need not be a hard trade-off: a 16-token bottleneck can simultaneously support 2.60 FID reconstruction and 79.10% ImageNet linear probing, suggesting that representation *form* (not just objective) is a lever for unifying discriminative and generative vision. The implicit shaping of dense backbone features into region-aware representations without explicit dense supervision is a useful side effect for downstream segmentation. Open questions include whether the factorization scales to higher resolutions and video (where temporal 'what-where' decomposition is natural), whether the optimal token count r is truly fixed or should be adaptive per image, and whether the approach can be trained from scratch at the scale of DINOv2's 100-1000× larger corpora to close the remaining gap on global semantics.
