## Problem

Existing scene graph generation methods over-predict geometric predicates (e.g., 'on', 'under') at the expense of semantically richer non-geometric predicates (e.g., 'riding', 'holding'), producing scene graphs that are factually correct yet semantically shallow. While prior work attributes this bias to long-tailed data distributions, this paper identifies an additional root cause: co-occurrence-induced representation entanglement, where geometric and non-geometric predicates that frequently co-occur on the same object pair are encoded into overly similar representations in a unified feature space, causing geometric cues to dominate predicate inference.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and processes it through a pre-trained Faster R-CNN with ResNeXt-101-RPN to obtain object proposals (bounding boxes, class labels, convolution features). Relationship proposals are formed as all ordered pairs of detected objects. The output is a scene graph where each node carries an object category and each directed edge carries a predicate label, evaluated under three standard sub-tasks (PredCls, SGCls, SGGen) using recall, mean recall, and overall mean metrics.

## Method

DS-Net replaces the conventional unified predicate representation with two parallel streams: a space stream that enhances bounding-box edge features with scene-wide visual context via an attention-sum strategy to predict geometric predicates, and a vision stream that dynamically discovers P latent parts within the union-box of an object pair using learned 1×1 convolutional filters, then grounds these parts with WordNet-derived text embeddings via cross-attention to predict non-geometric predicates. A bidirectional Cross-Stream Fusion (CSF) module then exchanges information between streams using cross-attention in both directions, allowing geometric representations to act as spatial priors for non-geometric prediction and vice versa. Training uses a multi-task loss combining the final predicate classifier (which also incorporates a class frequency prior) with auxiliary classifiers on each stream's representation, providing direct supervision to both specialized branches.

## Results

On Visual Genome, DS-Net achieves mR@100 of 41.1 (PredCls), 22.4 (SGCls), and 18.7 (SGGen), outperforming the previous best EdgeSGG by 4.2%, 3.6%, and 2.9% respectively. On Open Images V6, DS-Net attains mR@50 of 46.4 and score_wtd of 46.4, surpassing EdgeSGG (43.3 / 44.9). On GQA, DS-Net reaches mR@100 of 38.9 (PredCls) and 20.9 (SGCls), setting new state-of-the-art. When integrated into existing backbones, DS-Net yields 1.3%–6.1% absolute gains in mR@100 on the SGGen task across six different methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DS-Net outperforms all 25 compared baselines on mR@100 across all three VG tasks. The largest gains are over EdgeSGG (the strongest prior method): +4.2% mR@100 on PredCls (41.1 vs 36.9), +3.6% on SGCls (22.4 vs 18.8), and +2.9% on SGGen (18.7 vs 15.8). On Open Images V6, DS-Net leads EdgeSGG by 3.1 points in mR@50 (46.4 vs 43.3) and 1.5 in score_wtd (46.4 vs 44.9). On GQA, DS-Net achieves the best SGCls mR@100 (20.9 vs GCL's 18.1) and PredCls mR@100 (38.9 vs GCL's 38.1), though GCL slightly edges it on SGGen mR@100 (18.8 vs 18.6). The versatility experiments (Table 2) show DS-Net consistently improves every tested backbone—BGNN, PENET, DRM, RepSGG, RA-SGG, CAModule, and RcSGG—by 1.3%–6.1% in mR@100 on SGGen, confirming the method is architecture-agnostic.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the two-stage SGG paradigm (BGNN, Li et al. 2021) and the attention-sum mechanism from Relation Networks (Hu et al. 2018), but departs from the dominant line of work that attributes geometric predicate over-prediction solely to long-tailed data distributions and addresses it via loss re-weighting or data-level corrections (Tang et al. 2020, Zheng et al. 2023b). The key conceptual departure is identifying 'co-occurrence-induced representation entanglement' as a complementary, representation-level factor: when geometric and non-geometric predicates frequently co-occur on the same object pair, a unified encoder collapses them into overly similar features. Rather than attempting to disentangle the two predicate types (as prior heterogeneous SGG methods do), DS-Net explicitly models them in parallel streams and exploits their mutual informativeness through bidirectional cross-attention fusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of representation entanglement as a distinct, architecture-level cause of predicate bias (beyond data imbalance) offers a new diagnostic lens for SGG practitioners and suggests that simply re-weighting losses may be insufficient. The model-agnostic plug-in design (1.3%–6.1% mR@100 gains across six different backbones) makes the dual-stream + CSF recipe practically adoptable without retraining entire pipelines. Open questions include whether the geometric/non-geometric binary split is the optimal axis of decomposition (the paper follows Zellers et al. 2018's partition), whether the approach extends to end-to-end or single-stage SGG, and whether the latent-parts mechanism in the vision stream generalizes beyond the 50-predicate VG setting to open-vocabulary or compositional predicates.
