## Problem

Video anomaly detection (VAD) systems are designed to maximize detection accuracy but routinely encode task-irrelevant and privacy-sensitive information (e.g., facial identity, clothing, background context) in their intermediate representations, creating significant privacy risks for real-world deployment. No existing VAD architecture provides a principled, differentiable mechanism to selectively suppress such sensitive attributes at the representation level while preserving anomaly-relevant cues.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264623/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard surveillance video clips processed by pretrained backbones (I3D or Swin Transformer) and produces frame-level anomaly scores alongside projected feature representations with sensitive components removed. During training, G-OPL additionally receives weak face-presence signals from RetinaFace and face embeddings from the same encoder; at inference, no face detection or extra input is required, so the module operates as a fixed linear projection with negligible overhead. The projection layers are plug-and-play and can be inserted after the feature extractor, individual layers, or blocks within existing VAD architectures.

## Method

The Orthogonal Projection Layer (OPL) learns a weight matrix W ∈ R^{k×d} whose rows span a nuisance subspace; QR decomposition yields an orthonormal basis Q, and the projection P = I − QQ^T removes nuisance components from intermediate features while retaining task-relevant information. The Guided OPL (G-OPL) extends this by adding a cosine alignment loss that encourages Q to capture face-related directions, using weak supervision from RetinaFace face embeddings passed through the same encoder, so that facial identity components are explicitly projected out. An orthogonality regularizer (Frobenius norm on Q^TQ − I_k) preserves subspace stability across training. Unlike adversarial approaches (GRL) or nullspace methods requiring ground-truth labels, G-OPL uses only geometric cosine similarity with weak presence signals, yielding more stable optimization and interpretable subspace disentanglement. The modules are lightweight, fully differentiable, and require no identity labels or attribute classifiers, supporting multi-attribute suppression by concatenating multiple weak attribute embeddings.

## Results

On ShanghaiTech, RTFM-G-OPL/OPL achieves 97.3% AUC and 74.7% AP (vs. 96.8%/71.6% for RTFM baseline), while reducing First-layer Privacy Decay (FPD) from 0.72 to 0.19 and ArcFace identity retrieval accuracy from 0.99 to 0.01. On UCF-Crime, the same configuration reaches 78.3% AUC (vs. 74.3% baseline) with FPD dropping from 0.68 to 0.23. TEVAD-G-OPL attains 98.2% AUC on ShanghaiTech with FPD of 0.07, and EGO-G-OPL reaches 97.6% AUC with FPD of 0.09, both matching or exceeding their baselines in detection accuracy while achieving 8–10× reductions in privacy leakage. On MSAD, RTFM-G-OPL/OPL achieves 88.0% overall AUC, outperforming IEF-VAD (82.1%) and UR-DMU (85.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RTFM-G-OPL/OPL outperforms the RTFM baseline on all four datasets in AUC (e.g., +0.5 on ShanghaiTech, +4.0 on UCF-Crime, +1.6 on CUHK, +4.0 on Ped2) while reducing FPD by 3–10× and ArcFace identity retrieval from ~1.0 to 0.01–0.03. MGFN-G-OPL/OPL yields the largest absolute AUC gains over its baseline (+8.4 on ShanghaiTech, +6.3 on UCF-Crime, +3.5 on CUHK, +7.1 on Ped2), indicating that removing nuisance/identity subspaces particularly benefits weaker backbones. TEVAD-G-OPL and EGO-G-OPL match or marginally exceed their baselines in AUC (98.2 vs. 98.1; 97.6 vs. 97.3 on ShanghaiTech) while achieving the strongest privacy reductions (FPD 0.07 and 0.09 respectively, vs. 0.68 and 0.72 for baselines). On MSAD, RTFM-G-OPL/OPL achieves 88.0% overall AUC, surpassing IEF-VAD (82.1%), UR-DMU (85.0%), and EGO (87.3%), with particularly strong gains on Fire (+29.9 AUC over RTFM baseline) and Vandalism (+7.9). Compared to privacy-preserving alternatives (Table 5), G-OPL consistently achieves the best AUC–FPD balance: input masking degrades AUC by 3–4 points, differential privacy and feature bottlenecking offer only modest FPD reductions, and GRL introduces instability, whereas G-OPL improves or maintains AUC while cutting FPD by 40–70%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts subspace projection techniques from fairness and disentangled representation learning (INLP, OPL-2021, CAE-LSP) to the video anomaly detection setting, where prior methods either required explicit sensitive labels, operated on static images, or used generic decorrelation losses without targeted suppression. It departs from adversarial training with gradient reversal (GRL) by replacing it with a cosine alignment loss guided by weak face-presence signals, yielding more stable optimization and interpretable subspace geometry. The plug-and-play OPL/G-OPL modules are integrated into existing weakly-supervised VAD architectures (RTFM, MGFN, TEVAD, EGO) without modifying their core training pipelines, positioning the work as an architectural add-on rather than a new VAD model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first comprehensive privacy-leakage analysis for VAD across multiple datasets and architectures, introducing diagnostic metrics (SSC, ARD, FPD) that go beyond standard AUC reporting. The finding that removing identity-related features can simultaneously improve detection accuracy and reduce privacy risk challenges the assumed utility-privacy trade-off and suggests that spurious identity correlations actively harm generalization in VAD. Open questions include whether the linear-separability assumption for sensitive attributes holds in all video domains, how the method scales to more complex biometric signals (gait, voice), and whether the weak-supervision requirement (face detector at training time) is acceptable in all deployment scenarios.
