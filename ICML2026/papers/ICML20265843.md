## Problem

Reward Feedback Learning (ReFL) with video reward models is the natural alignment strategy for text-to-video generation, but standard ReFL requires backpropagating through both the VAE decoder and large video RMs (up to 32B parameters), demanding over 100 GB of memory and making it impractical on most hardware. Existing workfalls back on image-based reward models that cannot capture temporal consistency, and the only prior latent reward model (Dollar) uses a simple architecture that generalizes poorly to out-of-distribution samples encountered during fine-tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265843/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The LRM consumes video latents Z^v ∈ R^{C_l × T_l × H_l × W_l} (produced by the VAE encoder of the diffusion model) and a single text embedding vector (the last token of the prompt embedding, following CLIP convention) to output a scalar reward score. The fine-tuned diffusion model takes text prompts and generates videos at 480p (Wan-2.1) or 720p (CogVideoX-1.5). Unlike standard ReFL which can only process a single decoded frame through the video RM, VELR feeds the entire latent sequence directly into the LRM without any decoding step.

## Method

VELR replaces the expensive video RM + VAE decoder backpropagation path with an ensemble of lightweight latent reward models (each ~0.02B parameters, 0.21B total for 10-member ensemble) that predict rewards directly from latents. Each LRM combines 3D residual convolutions for local spatio-temporal features with a Transformer encoder and cross-attention modules for text-video alignment, trained with Huber loss on a mixed dataset of real videos (MVQA-68K) and diffusion-generated samples. The ensemble aggregates predictions (max/min/mean) to provide uncertainty estimates and improve OOD robustness. Training uses a truncated mid-step setting that retains gradients only over a short window of mid-noise denoising steps (avoiding both blurry early-step latents and ineffective late-step updates), combined with an online alignment mechanism that periodically fine-tunes the LRM on a variance-weighted replay buffer to keep reward predictions calibrated as the diffusion model's output distribution shifts. A KL divergence term regularizes the policy to prevent reward hacking, and the full pipeline reduces peak memory from ~178 GB to ~22 GB (87.6% reduction).

## Results

On VBench, VELR with UnifiedReward improves the average score by +3.37 on Wan-2.1 1.3B (54.64→58.01) and +4.91 on CogVideoX-1.5 5B (52.25→57.16), with the largest gains in Human Fidelity (+5.09 and +9.12) and Composition (+5.83 and +12.28). In terms of memory, VELR reduces peak memory from 178.59 GB (standard ReFL with UnifiedReward) to 22.18 GB, a reduction of 156.41 GB (87.6%), while processing full video latents rather than a single frame. Human evaluation confirms a 48.5% win rate versus only 3.5% loss rate against the base models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VELR is compared against three ReFL baselines: VADER, ImageReward, and Dollar. Against VADER and ImageReward (both using image RMs), VELR with video RMs produces visibly superior results in qualitative comparisons (Figs. 4 and 7), with sharper details, better text-video alignment, and more coherent motion, while also reducing memory by ~50% even in the image-RM setting (38.89→19.73 GB on OpenSora-1.2). Against Dollar (the prior LRM-based ReFL), VELR's ensemble LRM achieves dramatically lower MSE (0.003 vs. 0.832 on train, 0.008 vs. 1.215 on test) and higher Pearson correlation (0.98 vs. 0.59 train; 0.91 vs. 0.34 test), and on an OOD test set (Panda-70M) the ensemble LRM retains PCC of 0.72 while Dollar drops to 0.31. In the training-paradigm comparison (Fig. 7), VELR's truncated mid-step setting yields clearly better video quality than both the ImageReward intermediate-step and VADER truncated-last-N-steps paradigms, which show limited impact on generated video attributes.

## Relation to Prior Work

VELR extends the latent reward model (LRM) concept introduced by Dollar (Ding et al., 2025), which first proposed predicting rewards directly in latent space to bypass VAE decoder and reward model backpropagation during ReFL. However, Dollar's LRM uses a simple architecture, is evaluated only with image-based reward models on consistency models, and exhibits poor OOD generalization. VELR departs from this by introducing an ensemble of 3D-CNN + Transformer LRMs, a truncated mid-step training strategy, and an online alignment mechanism with variance-weighted replay buffer, specifically designed to approximate large-scale video reward models (up to 32B parameters) that are infeasible for standard ReFL. The broader ReFL lineage (ImageReward, VADER) informs the gradient-based update paradigm, but those methods rely on image RMs and extensive memory optimizations that sacrifice temporal modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VELR makes reward feedback learning with video reward models practical on SOTA T2V models (Wan-2.1, CogVideoX-1.5, OpenSora-1.2) by reducing memory from ~178 GB to ~22 GB, enabling alignment at scales previously unattainable. The finding that a 0.21B-parameter ensemble LRM can match a 32B video RM for ReFL purposes suggests that latent-space reward proxies may be a broadly applicable strategy for efficient alignment of generative models. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that VELR cannot fully exploit information from earlier denoising steps, leaving open the question of whether a stable ReFL formulation can leverage the full denoising trajectory. The reliance on a replay buffer for online alignment also introduces a hyperparameter-sensitive component that may not transfer trivially to other model scales or reward model families.
