## Problem

Spatiotemporal imputation methods based on RNNs and GNNs suffer from error accumulation due to iterative propagation, while diffusion-based approaches rely on problem-agnostic Gaussian priors and require expensive iterative denoising with multiple sampling runs. Flow matching offers deterministic, efficient transport but has not been combined with structured, problem-specific priors that exploit the underlying spatiotemporal graph geometry.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265777/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an incomplete N×R spatiotemporal signal (masked by a binary observation matrix M), a spatial graph Laplacian L_η encoding node relationships, and a temporal graph Laplacian L_ξ, and produces the fully imputed N×R signal. Two adaptive filtering factors (τ_η, τ_ξ) are optimized on complete training data via a joint alignment-and-smoothness objective and held fixed at inference. The ODE is solved with a 20-step Euler solver, and the model operates on windows of length 24 timesteps.

## Method

GiFlow replaces the standard Gaussian prior in conditional flow matching with a graph-informed prior constructed as X_τ = e^{−τ_η L_η} X_1^M e^{−τ_ξ L_ξ}, a joint spatiotemporal filtering of the observable signal that aligns the source distribution closer to the target and provably reduces transport cost (Theorem 3.2). The filtering factors are optimized to balance signal alignment and Laplacian smoothness, and Proposition 3.1 shows they control the spatial and temporal receptive field of the truncated Taylor expansion. The vector field is parameterized by a hybrid architecture combining spatial self-attention over GNN-propagated node embeddings, temporal self-attention with positional and timestamp encodings, and L_MP layers of alternating spatial and temporal GNN message passing. Training uses the conditional flow matching regression loss applied only to missing entries (via the mask), and inference is a single deterministic ODE integration without the repeated sampling and averaging required by diffusion baselines.

## Results

On the Air-36 dataset (point missing, ρ=20%), GiFlow achieves MAE 9.54, RMSE 18.10, and MAPE 21.27, outperforming the closest competitor GRIN (MAE 9.94) and CoSTI (MAE 9.95). On the larger AQI dataset (437 nodes), GiFlow reaches MAE 7.83, RMSE 17.80, and MAPE 16.24, again leading all baselines. Under block missing (ρ=20%), GiFlow maintains its lead with MAE 14.76 on Air-36 and 13.74 on AQI. On the PeMS08 traffic dataset, GiFlow also achieves the best MAE, RMSE, and MAPE under both missing strategies.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GiFlow outperforms all 12 baselines across every metric on both Air-36 and AQI under point missing. The closest competitors are GRIN (MAE 9.94 on Air-36, 7.97 on AQI) and CoSTI (MAE 9.95 on Air-36, 8.10 on AQI), with GiFlow improving MAE by roughly 0.4 on Air-36 and 0.14 on AQI over GRIN. Under block missing, the margins narrow slightly (GiFlow MAE 14.76 vs. OPCR 15.27 on Air-36; 13.74 vs. GRIN 14.47 on AQI), but GiFlow still leads. Notably, RNN-based methods BRITS and SAITS degrade severely under block missing, even falling below the non-parametric FP baseline, while spatiotemporal GNN/transformer methods (SPIN, GRIN, OPCR) remain competitive. The diffusion-based PriSTI and consistency-based CoSTI are the strongest generative-model baselines but are consistently outperformed by GiFlow, which also runs 16× faster at inference (0.28 min vs. 9.30 min on Air-36). On the PeMS08 traffic dataset, GiFlow again achieves the best MAE, RMSE, and MAPE under both point and block missing, confirming cross-domain generalizability.

## Relation to Prior Work

GiFlow builds on the conditional flow matching framework of Lipman et al. (2023) and the joint spatiotemporal graph filtering operator from Stanley et al. (2020), combining them in a way not previously explored for imputation. It departs from diffusion-based spatiotemporal imputation methods (PriSTI, CSDI) that rely on problem-agnostic Gaussian priors and iterative stochastic sampling, and from RNN/GNN-based approaches (BRITS, GRIN, SPIN, OPCR) that propagate estimates iteratively and accumulate errors. The key conceptual departure is replacing the isotropic Gaussian source distribution with a structured prior obtained by adaptive Laplacian filtering of the observed signal, which the authors show provably reduces the optimal transport cost between source and target.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled answer to a practical question in generative imputation: the choice of source distribution matters more than the choice between diffusion and flow matching, as evidenced by FM-Gauss underperforming several non-generative baselines while the graph-informed variant leads. The adaptive receptive field mechanism (Proposition 3.1) offers a theoretically grounded way to control how much spatial and temporal context the model uses, which could inform design choices in other graph-based generative tasks. Limitations include the need for a known or well-constructed graph topology, the requirement to optimize filtering factors on complete training data, and the fact that performance gains over the strongest GNN baselines (GRIN, OPCR) are modest (~5–10% MAE). Open questions include scalability to very large dynamic graphs, extension to multi-dimensional signals, and whether the graph-informed prior idea transfers to other conditional generation tasks beyond imputation.
