## Problem

Existing approaches to enhance LLM reasoning—Chain-of-Thought prompting, 'Wait' prompts, and static representation editing—either encourage more thinking without guiding toward correct deductions, or operate only in contexts where the answer is already visible. The application of Representation Editing to the dynamic, unfolding reasoning process, where the final answer is latent and the model is mid-derivation, remains largely unexplored. This paper asks whether a recoverable 'Truth' signal exists within LLM activations along reasoning trajectories, where and when intervention is effective, and how to do so without corrupting already-correct paths.

## Input / Output

DynaSteer takes a standard text prompt (math or coding problem) and operates on the LLM's hidden states extracted at sentence boundaries (delimited by punctuation), not at individual token positions. During inference, it monitors the Shannon entropy of each candidate sentence; when entropy exceeds the 80th-percentile threshold and the step falls within a temporal decay window, the framework discards the candidate sentence (rollback) and regenerates it under a modified hidden state. The output is the full steered reasoning trajectory ending in a corrected final answer, with interventions applied only at a small subset of high-uncertainty forks, reducing inference token consumption by roughly 52–56% compared to editing every step.

## Method

DynaSteer is a training-free, inference-time representation editing framework built on three offline components and one online loop. Offline, it (1) constructs a contrastive dataset by performing N=10 stochastic rollouts at high-entropy sentence forks and labeling them Truth/Fallacy only when all rollouts agree (consensus filtering); (2) applies k-means clustering to the difference vectors h⁺−h⁻ to disentangle latent reasoning patterns into M clusters; and (3) computes a purified steering vector v_m = Σ_{w,m}⁻¹(μ_m⁺ − μ_m⁻) per cluster via Fisher-LDA, which maximizes between-class separation while suppressing within-class noise that plagues naive mean-difference steering. Online, DynaSteer monitors sentence-level lookahead entropy, and when a fork is detected within the temporal decay window, it projects the current hidden state onto each cluster's Fisher-LDA axis, computes an asymmetric intervention strength η_m(h) = α·tanh(ReLU((μ_proj − h_proj)/σ_proj)), aggregates the weighted steering vectors, injects the result into the hidden state, and rolls back the unsteered sentence for regeneration. This selective, subspace-projected, temporally-gated mechanism avoids the collateral damage of global mean-difference steering while remaining computationally lightweight compared to RL-based alternatives like GRPO.

## Results

DynaSteer achieves the best pass@1 accuracy across all five benchmarks (GSM8K, MATH500, AMC23, HumanEval+, MBPP+) and all three backbone LLMs (Qwen3-1.7B, Llama3.2-3B-Instruct, Qwen3-8B). The average relative improvement over the second-best baseline is 4.36% on MATH tasks and 3.60% on Coding tasks, with a peak gain of 11.76% on AMC23 (Qwen3-1.7B: 47.50 vs. 42.50). On the strongest model (Qwen3-8B), DynaSteer reaches 94.39% on GSM8K, 86.20% on MATH500, and 72.50% on AMC23, outperforming the best RepE baselines (ACT/DRESS) by 1.5–3.6 points. The method also generalizes to out-of-domain coding (HumanEval+: 81.71%, MBPP+: 65.61% on Qwen3-8B) and multi-hop QA (HotpotQA: 55.40%, 2WikiQA: 52.30%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DynaSteer consistently outperforms all seven baselines across every benchmark and model scale. Among the primary RepE baselines, ACT (Wang et al., WWW 2025) and DRESS (Ma et al., ICLR 2025) are the strongest competitors, with DynaSteer exceeding them by 1.3–7.4 points on MATH and 1.5–6.2 points on Coding; ITI (Li et al., NeurIPS 2023) lags further behind, often underperforming even the Plain baseline on AMC23 (e.g., 37.50 vs. 40.00 on Qwen3-1.7B). Against causal intervention methods ROME (Meng et al., NeurIPS 2022) and MEMIT (Meng et al., ICLR 2022), DynaSteer achieves substantially higher scores on Qwen3-8B (e.g., 94.39 vs. 77.94 on GSM8K; 81.71 vs. 77.44 on HumanEval+), which the authors attribute to ROME/MEMIT's single-head localization being mismatched with the distributed nature of the Truth signal. Compared to TruthX (Zhang et al., ACL 2024) and TruthFlow (Wang et al., ICML 2025) on Qwen3-1.7B, DynaSteer gains 3.6–12.5 points, with the advantage most pronounced on AMC23 (47.50 vs. 35.00), reflecting the benefit of dynamic, entropy-triggered steering over static or query-level directions. Notably, the 'Wait' prompt baseline performs comparably to or only marginally above Plain generation in most settings, supporting the paper's claim that simple prompting is insufficient for steering reasoning. The one comparison where DynaSteer loses is against GRPO (a reinforcement learning method), which achieves 84.6% vs. DynaSteer's 82.3% on GSM8K, but at a cost of ~160 GB VRAM and 15 hours of training versus DynaSteer's training-free inference-time intervention.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Representation Engineering (RepE) paradigm—established by Zou et al. (2023) and operationalized in methods like ITI, ACT, and DRESS—from static behavioral attributes (style, honesty, harmlessness) to the dynamic, unfolding process of LLM reasoning. The key conceptual departure is that prior RepE work assumes the target concept (e.g., truth) is encoded in representations where the answer is already visible, whereas DynaSteer must identify and steer toward truth at intermediate derivation steps where the final answer is latent. It also departs from the standard Mean-Difference steering vector by introducing Fisher-LDA purification and k-means pattern clustering, motivated by the finding that truth geometry is entangled with reasoning mode. On the prompting side, the paper positions itself against 'Wait' and 'Aha moment' strategies, arguing they encourage more thinking without directing it toward correct deductions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DynaSteer offers a training-free, inference-time alternative to RL-based reasoning enhancement (e.g., GRPO), which requires ~160GB VRAM and 15 hours of training, making it accessible to resource-constrained settings. The empirical discoveries—the Decay Effect (intervention efficacy drops from 62.9% to 24.9% across trajectory segments) and the Uncertainty Principle (editing only helps at high-entropy forks)—provide a principled framework for when and where to intervene, with practical efficiency gains (~56% token reduction). However, the paper acknowledges a fundamental limitation: DynaSteer (82.3%) still trails GRPO (84.6%), suggesting that the linear steering assumption cannot fully capture the non-linear, high-dimensional geometry of truth in complex reasoning. An open question is whether non-linear or iterative steering mechanisms could close this gap while retaining the training-free property.
