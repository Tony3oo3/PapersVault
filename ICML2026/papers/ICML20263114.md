## Problem

Video diffusion models require dozens of expensive forward passes during iterative denoising, and existing acceleration methods face a fundamental trade-off: offline calibration-based approaches (TeaCache, MagCache) depend on curated datasets and suffer from distribution shifts, while calibration-free methods (EasyCache) rely on zero-order hold approximations that ignore the intrinsic momentum of feature evolution, leading to inaccurate skip decisions and visual artifacts in high-turbulence regions.

## Input / Output

NaviCache is a plug-and-play acceleration layer applied on top of existing video diffusion transformers (HunyuanVideo, Wan 2.1-1.3B, Open-Sora 1.2). It takes a text prompt as input and produces a generated video at resolutions ranging from 480p to 720p with 51–129 frames. Internally, the method operates on latent feature tensors at each denoising step, computing input and output L1-norm differences to decide whether to skip the expensive backbone computation, requiring no retraining or offline calibration of the underlying model.

## Method

NaviCache reconceptualizes the tracking of the output-to-input feature change ratio as an Inertial Navigation System (INS) problem within a Linear Gaussian State-Space Model. A strategic Initial Alignment phase (first N_align steps with full computation) provides unbiased initialization of the state mean and error covariance before the system enters autonomous navigation mode. During the main diffusion phase, a dual-state recursive Bayesian filter performs Prior Projection (dead reckoning with process noise Q modeling ratio drift) and Observational Rectification (optimal Kalman-gain fusion when a full computation is triggered), with the Calibrated Fusion Factor K_t adaptively weighting historical inertia versus new observations based on relative uncertainty. An Uncertainty-Aware Safety Gate accumulates predicted output variation and triggers a full computation (UPDATE) only when the accumulated error exceeds a fidelity threshold τ, otherwise skipping (SKIP) the backbone. The paper proves via the Algebraic Riccati Equation that the steady-state estimation error of NaviCache is strictly lower than the Q+R error of zero-order heuristics, providing a theoretical guarantee for more accurate computation skipping.

## Results

On Wan 2.1-1.3B, NaviCache-fast achieves a 2.23× speedup (96.40s vs. 214.93s baseline) with PSNR 23.46 dB, SSIM 0.8011, and LPIPS 0.0956, while NaviCache-slow reaches PSNR 25.10 dB and LPIPS 0.0686 at 1.86× speedup. On HunyuanVideo, NaviCache-fast delivers 2.55× speedup with PSNR 30.64 dB, and NaviCache-slow achieves PSNR 33.87 dB with LPIPS 0.0462 at 2.05× speedup. On Open-Sora 1.2, NaviCache-fast reaches 1.78× speedup with PSNR 22.53 dB. Across all models, NaviCache requires zero offline calibration and adds only 0.0109s of test-time overhead for its state-estimation logic.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest calibration-free baseline EasyCache, NaviCache consistently wins on pixel-level and perceptual metrics at comparable or higher speedup: on HunyuanVideo, NaviCache-mid achieves PSNR 32.65 vs. EasyCache's 32.53 at a slightly higher 2.17× speedup, and NaviCache-slow pushes PSNR to 33.87 with LPIPS 0.0462 (vs. 0.0589). Against calibration-based TeaCache, NaviCache-slow substantially outperforms on reconstruction quality (e.g., PSNR 25.10 vs. 22.79 on Wan 2.1; 33.87 vs. 24.05 on HunyuanVideo), though TeaCache retains a marginal VBench advantage in some settings (80.67 vs. 80.58 on Wan 2.1). Against MagCache, NaviCache-slow again leads on PSNR and LPIPS across all three models (e.g., 25.10 vs. 23.33 on Wan 2.1; 33.87 vs. 29.83 on HunyuanVideo). Against PAB, NaviCache dominates on every metric, with PAB showing the worst VBench scores (76.95–77.60) and significantly lower PSNR/SSIM. Notably, NaviCache requires zero offline calibration (0s vs. 15,191s for TeaCache and 217s for MagCache), eliminating distribution-shift risk entirely.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by repurposing Inertial Navigation System (INS) and Kalman-filter theory for the video diffusion caching problem, rather than building directly on any single prior method. It departs from offline calibration-based approaches (TeaCache, MagCache) that fit global input-output mappings on curated datasets, and from offline calibration-free methods (EasyCache) that apply a zero-order hold on the feature change ratio. The key conceptual leap is modeling the ratio's evolution as a stochastic state-space process with momentum, enabling test-time self-calibration via a dual-state estimator with an uncertainty-aware safety gate, and providing a formal error-bound guarantee via the Algebraic Riccati Equation that prior heuristics lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NaviCache offers a principled, plug-and-play acceleration strategy that eliminates the calibration-data dependency and distribution-shift vulnerability of prior methods while theoretically guaranteeing lower estimation error than zero-order approximations. However, the method still requires per-model tuning of process/measurement noise covariances (Q, R), the fidelity threshold τ, and the alignment length N_align, limiting its truly zero-config deployment. Open questions include whether the random-walk state assumption generalizes to even larger or architecturally different VDMs, whether the INS analogy can be extended to multi-step look-ahead prediction, and whether the modest empirical gains over EasyCache (typically 0.5–2 dB PSNR) will widen as model scale and temporal length increase.
