## Problem

Single-image reflection removal is fundamentally ill-posed because reflection and transmission layers can be visually indistinguishable, and existing multi-frame methods require large camera motion (large baseline) to generate sufficient optical flow for layer separation. This paper identifies that event cameras can capture micro-dynamics from subtle handheld motion—exploiting the depth disparity between the glass surface and background to produce distinct per-layer motion signatures that frame-based sensors miss due to limited temporal resolution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264684/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a mixed RGB image (608×768) and a concurrent asynchronous event stream as input. The raw event stream is first converted into a dense spatiotemporal voxel grid with B=20 temporal bins (deliberately higher than the typical B=5 used in prior event literature) to preserve the subtle parallax differences between layers, then normalized via a 98th-percentile clipping strategy to suppress hot-pixel noise. The output is a single clean transmission image with all reflection artifacts removed, decoded at full resolution via a lightweight NAFBlock-based decoder with PixelShuffle upsampling.

## Method

EvReflection is a two-stage event-driven architecture grounded in a theoretical proof that the event-based linear system for layer separation has a positive-definite Hessian whenever motion parallax exists (v_T ≠ v_R), guaranteeing a unique solution. The Micro-Dynamics Decoupler (MDD) adapts the RAFT recurrent architecture with a dual-branch GRU (M=12 iterations) that maintains separate transmission and reflection feature states, iteratively refining them against each other while injecting low-level RGB structural anchors to produce texture-agnostic dynamic priors. The Parallax-Attention Rectifier (PAR) then performs cross-modal attention where the dynamics prior serves as the key and FocalNet-L deep RGB features serve as the query, generating a spatial attention map that explicitly suppresses reflection-dominated regions while preserving transmission details. Training is supervised with a composite loss (MSE + gradient + VGG perceptual) on 7,643 synthetic pairs generated by a parallax-aware simulation pipeline that models 3D camera trajectories and converts blended sequences to events via ESIM, with optional fine-tuning on 387 real EVR² samples to bridge the domain gap.

## Results

On the synthetic SIR² benchmark, EvReflection achieves an average PSNR of 29.09 dB, SSIM of 0.940, and LPIPS of 0.036, surpassing the best single-image baseline DAI (27.46 dB) by 1.63 dB and the best multi-image baseline SOLD (21.98 dB) by 7.11 dB. On the real-world EVR² benchmark, EvReflection reaches 27.25 dB PSNR and 0.896 SSIM, outperforming the strongest RGB-only competitor RDNet (25.97 dB) by 1.28 dB. The method also achieves the fastest inference speed (47.7 ms, 21.0 FPS) among all compared methods despite using 216.73 M parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EvReflection outperforms all 14 baselines on both benchmarks. On SIR², the closest competitor is DAI (Hu et al., 2026) at 27.46 dB PSNR, which EvReflection surpasses by 1.63 dB; RDNet (Zhao et al., 2025) at 26.71 dB trails by 2.38 dB, and DSIT (Hu et al., 2024) at 26.09 dB by 3.00 dB. Older single-image methods (IBCLN, YTMT, LANet, PNACR) all fall in the 24.08–24.20 dB range, yielding gaps of ~5 dB. The multi-image baseline SOLD (Liu et al., 2021) performs worst at 21.98 dB due to registration errors. On the real-world EVR² dataset, RDNet leads the RGB-only group at 25.97 dB (EvReflection: +1.28 dB), while DAI drops sharply to 21.25 dB due to domain shift. Against cross-domain event-based networks retrained on EVR², EvReflection beats EFNet (24.24 dB) by 3.01 dB, DeblurSR (23.61 dB) by 3.64 dB, and EvLight (23.46 dB) by 3.79 dB, confirming that task-specific decoupling is essential rather than generic event fusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from both single-image reflection removal (which relies on static spatial priors and suffers from inherent ambiguity) and multi-frame approaches (which require large camera baselines for sufficient optical flow) by introducing event camera signals as a new input modality. Architecturally, it adapts the RAFT recurrent refinement paradigm for its Micro-Dynamics Decoupler and borrows the DSRNet blending model for its parallax-aware simulation pipeline, but the core contribution is a novel cross-modal attention mechanism (PAR) that uses disentangled event dynamics to spatially modulate RGB features. The theoretical analysis in the appendices provides a clean derivation of why frame-based optical flow is structurally incapable of separating mixed layers, while event-based constraints yield a well-posed linear system—a distinction not previously formalized in the reflection removal literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By establishing the first real-world benchmark (EVR²) and demonstrating that micro-dynamics from subtle handheld motion suffice for layer separation, this work opens event-guided image separation as a viable research direction beyond deblurring and super-resolution. The parallax-aware simulation pipeline offers a reusable recipe for generating physically consistent event data for other layered-scene tasks. Key limitations remain: the method fails in strictly static scenes, degrades under extreme low light, and the 216M-parameter backbone (dominated by FocalNet) limits deployment on edge devices. Whether the event modality can be replaced by cheaper temporal cues (e.g., high-frame-rate video) without losing the microsecond precision advantage is an open question.
