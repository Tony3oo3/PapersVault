## Problem

RL fine-tuning of pre-trained generative policies (e.g., diffusion policies) improves task success but typically collapses the multimodal action distributions learned from demonstrations into a single reward-maximizing behavior. Existing fine-tuning methods lack explicit mechanisms to preserve this multimodality, and the number of behavioral modes present in the pre-trained policy is generally unknown a priori.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262987/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen pre-trained noise-conditioned diffusion policy π_θ(a|s,w), the current robot state s (joint positions/velocities, end-effector pose, object poses), and a discrete latent mode code z sampled from a uniform categorical prior. It produces robot actions (6-DoF end-effector delta poses for manipulation, 12 joint commands for locomotion) and a set of discovered behavioral modes that index distinct trajectory distributions. The inference model q_φ(z|s) maps visited states back to mode codes, and the steering policy π_ψ^W(w|s,z) maps (state, mode) pairs to noise inputs that condition the diffusion sampler.

## Method

BMD reparameterizes a steering policy with a discrete latent variable z that organizes the diffusion policy's noise space W into trajectory-level behavioral modes, inducing the family of action distributions π_{θ,ψ}(a|s,z) = ∫ π_θ(a|s,w) π_ψ^W(w|s,z) dw. Multimodality is quantified via the mutual information I(Z;S) between the latent code and visited states, optimized through a variational lower bound log q_φ(z|s) − log p(z) that serves as an intrinsic reward. Training follows a two-stage scheme: a mode-discovery phase (intrinsic reward only, with a short-to-long horizon curriculum) uncovers the latent modes, followed by a fine-tuning phase that adds the environment reward to guide adaptation without destroying diversity. The framework is orthogonal to the fine-tuning mechanism itself and can be combined with direct fine-tuning (DPPO), residual policies (Policy Decorator), or steering policies (DSRL), with PPO used as the underlying RL algorithm in experiments.

## Results

On the Lift manipulation task, RES[BMD] achieves SR=0.99, SR_M=0.99, mc@0.80=2/2, and H=1.00, recovering full bimodal coverage that standard RES collapses (SR_M=0.50, H=0.00). On the more challenging Avoid task (24 modes), DSRL[BMD] reaches SR=1.00 with SR_M=0.42 and mc@0.80=10/24, compared to DSRL's SR_M=0.09 and mc@0.80=2/24. In the 2D Gaussian mixture (G1), DPPO[BMD] achieves SR_M=1.00, mc@80=4/4, and H=0.99, whereas unregularized DPPO collapses to SR_M=0.58 and H=0.40. Across ANYmal locomotion (4 modes) and Franka Kitchen (24 modes), BMD preserves all or most pre-trained modes while matching or exceeding baseline task success.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares BMD-regularized variants against three fine-tuning baselines: DPPO (direct diffusion fine-tuning), Policy Decorator/RES (residual correction), and DSRL (steering policy). On the Lift task, RES[BMD] dramatically outperforms unregularized RES on diversity metrics (SR_M: 0.50→0.99, H: 0.00→1.00) while matching its SR of ~1.00. On Avoid (24 modes), DSRL[BMD] achieves SR_M=0.42 and mc@0.80=10/24 versus DSRL's SR_M=0.09 and mc@0.80=2/24, a ~4.7× improvement in mode coverage, while maintaining SR=1.00. DPPO[BMD] recovers full mode coverage in the 2D Gaussian mixture (SR_M=1.00, H=0.99 vs. DPPO's SR_M=0.58, H=0.40) but shows limited gains on Lift (SR_M=0.55), suggesting direct weight updates partially override the discovered mode structure. Overall, BMD consistently wins on SR_M, mc@0.80, and H across all tasks while matching or slightly sacrificing SR, confirming that the mutual-information intrinsic reward prevents mode collapse without significant task-performance cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) RL fine-tuning of pre-trained diffusion/flow policies (DPPO, Policy Decorator, DSRL), which improves task success but lacks explicit mechanisms to preserve the multimodal action distributions learned from demonstrations, and (2) unsupervised skill discovery (VIC, DIAYN), which maximizes mutual information between a latent code and visited states to induce behavioral diversity. The key conceptual departure is applying the skill-discovery mutual-information objective not to a policy trained from scratch, but to the latent noise space of a frozen pre-trained generative policy, thereby discovering modes already encoded in demonstrations. The steering-policy architecture from DSRL (Wagenmaker et al., 2025) is extended with a latent reparameterization z that organizes step-wise noise into trajectory-level behavioral modes, and the DIAYN variational lower bound is repurposed as an intrinsic reward that regularizes RLFT against mode collapse.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Mode collapse during RLFT of generative policies is a practical bottleneck for deploying diffusion-based robot policies beyond their demonstration distribution; this paper provides a principled, plug-in regularizer (MI-based intrinsic reward) that can be combined with any existing fine-tuning method (direct, residual, or steering) to retain behavioral diversity. The finding that the steering policy can be removed after fine-tuning while multimodality persists (Table 10) suggests the discovered mode structure is internalized into the policy, making the approach practical for deployment. Open questions include extending to continuous or hierarchical latent spaces, developing more robust diversity metrics less sensitive to small state perturbations, and grounding the discovered modes with language for semantic controllability. The method's reliance on a discrete latent space and careful tuning of the regularization weight λ limits its generality, and scaling to large multi-task datasets with richer multimodal structure remains unaddressed.
