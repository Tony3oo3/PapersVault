## Problem

Flow matching policies in offline RL face a fundamental optimization mismatch: the RL objective is defined on terminal actions while flow matching supervises a velocity field across time. Backpropagating terminal Q-gradients through the multi-step Euler integration (BPTT) causes exploding gradients, and existing workforces a trade-off between distilling to one-step policies (losing multimodal expressivity) and imposing rigid architectural modifications to stabilize BPTT. The paper asks whether terminal value signals can be injected directly into the velocity field while preserving the full iterative structure and remaining architecture-agnostic.

## Input / Output

The policy network takes a state vector (or a 3-frame-stacked RGB image processed by a compact IMPALA encoder with random-shift augmentation for pixel tasks) and produces a continuous action vector by integrating a state-conditioned velocity field from Gaussian noise over K=10 Euler steps. The critic takes state-action pairs and outputs scalar Q-values; the terminal Q-gradient with respect to the final action is the sole RL signal used for policy updates. For pixel-based OGBench tasks, only the first of five subtasks is evaluated due to computational constraints.

## Method

DFQL replaces unstable BPTT with a surrogate objective that distributes the terminal Q-gradient g = ∇_{a_K} Q(s, a_K) as step-wise velocity guidance: at each Euler step k, the loss reduces (via a stop-gradient trick) to the inner product −Δt·g^T v_θ(s, a_k, t_k), weighted by w_k = 1/(K−k) to prioritize steps closer to the terminal evaluation. The theoretical analysis shows this is a first-order approximation to BPTT that prunes the unstable Jacobian-product terms while retaining the identity pathway, with asymptotic directional consistency as K→∞ under Lipschitz continuity. The BC component is made 'dense' by supervising the velocity field at all K discrete interpolation points (rather than a single random t), ensuring structural symmetry with the RL signal. The final objective L_π = L_BC + α·L_RL is optimized in a standard actor-critic loop with double critics and target networks, requiring no architectural modifications beyond a simple 5×tanh output bound for numerical safety.

## Results

Across 73 tasks on OGBench and D4RL, DFQL achieves state-of-the-art or near-state-of-the-art performance. On OGBench, DFQL leads on 9 of 10 state-based task categories, with particularly large gains on challenging tasks: 39±4 on antmaze-giant (vs. 9±6 for FQL) and 65±7 on puzzle-3x3 (vs. 30±1 for FQL). On D4RL, DFQL matches FQL on Adroit (52±1) and is competitive on AntMaze (82±3 vs. 84±3). In the offline-to-online setting, DFQL reaches 89±9 on OGBench and 96±1 on D4RL AntMaze after 1M online steps, outperforming FQL (68±11 and 95±5 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DFQL outperforms the closest flow-based baseline FBRAC (which uses standard BPTT through all flow steps) on 11 of 13 task categories, confirming that the surrogate gradient-injection objective stabilizes training relative to recursive backpropagation. Against FQL (the distillation-based flow method), DFQL wins on 10 of 13 categories, with the largest margins on antmaze-giant (+30 points) and puzzle-3x3 (+35 points), while FQL retains a slight edge on D4RL AntMaze (84 vs. 82) and ties on Adroit (52). Among Gaussian-policy methods, DFQL surpasses the strongest baseline ReBRAC on 11 of 13 categories (e.g., 88 vs. 81 on antmaze-large, 65 vs. 21 on puzzle-3x3). Against diffusion-policy baselines, DFQL dominates on most tasks, with CAC being the closest competitor on humanoidmaze-medium (53 vs. 66). In the offline-to-online setting, DFQL achieves the best final performance on OGBench (89±9 vs. FQL's 68±11) and D4RL AntMaze (96±1 vs. 95±5), while FQL edges ahead on D4RL Adroit (110±5 vs. 106±4). The integration with Q-chunking (QC-DFQL) further outperforms QC-FQL across sparse-reward manipulation tasks, demonstrating DFQL's modularity.

## Relation to Prior Work

DFQL builds directly on the flow matching framework (Lipman et al., ICLR 2023) and the FQL actor-critic pipeline (Park et al., ICML 2025), but departs from FQL's core design choice of distilling the multi-step flow into a single-step policy. Instead of distillation or architectural modifications to stabilize BPTT (as in FBRAC or SAC-Flow), DFQL introduces a surrogate objective that distributes the terminal Q-gradient as step-wise velocity supervision, effectively performing 'state-sensitivity pruning' of the Jacobian products that cause gradient explosion. The paper also positions itself as moving inference-time Q-guidance (Jang et al., CoRL 2025) into the training objective, and demonstrates orthogonality with Q-chunking (Li et al., NeurIPS 2025) for temporally extended action spaces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DFQL resolves a fundamental tension in flow-matching-based offline RL: preserving multi-step iterative expressivity while achieving stable optimization, without the capacity bottleneck of distillation or the architectural constraints of BPTT-stabilization tricks. The asymptotic consistency proof (Appendix A.2) provides theoretical grounding for the temporal weighting scheme, and the seamless extension to offline-to-online RL without modification broadens its practical utility. Key open questions remain: the inference latency of multi-step sampling (partially addressed by QC-DFQL but not fundamentally solved), the sensitivity of the α hyperparameter to task scale, and whether the approach extends to more demanding flow architectures like Mean Flow Matching that impose stricter training stability requirements.
