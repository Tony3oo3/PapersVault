## Problem

State-of-the-art probabilistic weather forecasters (GenCast, FGN, AIFS-CRPS) rely on specialized architectures—graph transformers, spherical neural operators, or iterative diffusion—and require hundreds of TPU/GPU-days of training, creating a high barrier to entry for the broader research community. The paper asks whether this architectural and computational complexity is actually necessary for frontier probabilistic skill, or whether a simple, general-purpose design with an efficient training recipe can match or exceed these models at a fraction of the cost.

## Input / Output

The model takes two consecutive 12-hourly atmospheric snapshots at 1.5° resolution (121×240 grid) as input, comprising 83 variables (6 atmospheric fields at 13 pressure levels plus 5 surface fields) augmented with 2 static and 4 time-varying forcings, yielding 172 input channels. It predicts the residual relative to the most recent input snapshot and is rolled out autoregressively to produce multi-day probabilistic ensemble forecasts. Stochasticity is introduced at inference via Monte Carlo Dropout masks, generating multiple ensemble members from a single forward pass per member, with a 4-member deep ensemble (K=4 checkpoints × N dropout rollouts) as the final configuration.

## Method

U-Cast uses a standard Dhariwal-style U-Net backbone (895M parameters, 320 base channels, 4 down/up-sampling blocks, bottleneck self-attention at the two coarsest resolutions, circular longitude padding) with four minimal modifications for the spherical domain. Training follows a two-stage curriculum: Stage 1 pretrains the network deterministically on spatially weighted MAE for 100 epochs (a single forward pass per step), and Stage 2 fine-tunes probabilistically on the CRPS loss with M=2 dropout-sampled ensemble members for only 8 epochs, so the expensive probabilistic phase accounts for just 15% of total compute. Stochasticity is injected via standard 10% Monte Carlo Dropout rather than adaptive LayerNorm noise injection, reducing parameter count by 5–10% while yielding better CRPS. The Muon optimizer (momentum-orthogonalized) replaces AdamW, proving critical for converging the short fine-tuning stage to frontier-quality optima. A deep ensemble is formed by repeating Stage 2 four times from the same deterministic checkpoint, each member costing only 1.2 H200-days.

## Results

On the WeatherBench 2 1.5° benchmark (732 initial conditions, 2020), U-Cast DeepEns (DE) outperforms IFS ENS on 92.9% of variable–lead-time combinations with an average CRPS improvement of 5.0% (peak 23.7% for short-range MSLP) and a 0.21% average CRPS gain over GenCast (up to 3% for short-range z500). For z500 at 1-day lead, U-Cast (DE) achieves a CRPS of 19.6 vs. 22.4 (IFS ENS) and 20.2 (GenCast); for 10 m u-wind at 1-day, it reaches 0.345 vs. 0.406 (IFS ENS) and 0.332 (GenCast). Training requires only 8.2 H200-GPU-days (11.8 for the 4-member deep ensemble), and a single 60-step (30-day) rollout completes in 2 s on an H100.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the operational IFS ENS, U-Cast (DE) wins on 92.9% of variable–lead-time pairs, with CRPS gains ranging from ~2% (long-range z500) to 23.7% (short-range MSLP); in Table 1 it beats IFS ENS on every reported cell. Against GenCast (which enjoys a native 0.25° resolution advantage), U-Cast (DE) edges ahead by 0.21% average CRPS and up to 3% on short-range z500, but concedes 10 m u-wind at 1-day (0.345 vs. 0.332) and 2 m temperature at 12-hour leads. U-Cast (DE) clearly outperforms ArchesWeatherGen on all six Table 1 cells (e.g., z500 1d: 19.6 vs. 21.2) and NeuralGCM on all z500 cells (19.6 vs. 22.9 at 1-day). In terms of compute, U-Cast's 15 H200-days at 1° is an order of magnitude below FGN (300 TPU-days), AIFS-CRPS O96 (256 H100-days), and FourCastNet-3 (3,328 H100-days), while its 2-s single-member inference is ~10× faster than diffusion-based models (GenCast ~16 min, ArchesWeatherGen ~3.5 min) and comparable to MOSAIC (3 s) and AIFS-CRPS (~1 min). The deterministic baselines GraphCast, FuXi, and Stormer appear only in the RMSE reference plot (Figure 8) and are not directly scored on CRPS in the main tables.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper deliberately departs from the field's trajectory toward specialized geometric architectures (graph transformers in GraphCast/GenCast/FGN, spherical neural operators in FourCastNet-3, 3D Swin transformers in Pangu/Aurora) by reverting to a standard U-Net backbone originally designed for image diffusion. It also repurposes Monte Carlo Dropout from Bayesian deep learning as the stochasticity mechanism, replacing the adaptive LayerNorm-based noise injection that prior CRPS-trained models (AIFS-CRPS, FGN, FourCastNet-3) inherited from diffusion practice. The two-stage MAE-to-CRPS curriculum and the Muon optimizer represent the paper's key methodological contributions, decoupling the learning of atmospheric dynamics from uncertainty quantification to avoid the prohibitive cost of end-to-end ensemble training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10× reduction in training compute (under 12 H200-GPU-days vs. 256–3328 for comparable baselines) and 10× reduction in inference latency (2 s vs. minutes for diffusion models) substantially lower the barrier to frontier probabilistic weather AI, enabling academic labs and under-resourced groups to train and iterate on competitive models. Key limitations remain: systematic polar artifacts from the 2D U-Net's inability to handle spherical topology, slight under-dispersion at short-to-medium horizons, and instability in rollouts beyond ~20 days due to the absence of autoregressive training. Open questions include whether a lightweight spherical U-Net variant would resolve polar issues, whether initial-condition perturbations can fix residual under-dispersion, and whether the recipe transfers cleanly to 0.25° resolution.
