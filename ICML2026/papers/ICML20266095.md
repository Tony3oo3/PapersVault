## Problem

State-of-the-art instruction-based image editing models achieve high semantic fidelity but frequently violate fundamental physical laws (e.g., rendering a straw in water without refraction) because the dominant paradigm treats editing as a discrete mapping between image pairs, providing only boundary conditions while leaving the underlying transition dynamics completely underspecified. This paper argues that editing must be reformulated as a predictive physical state transition governed by causal laws, rather than a black-box pixel update.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a single source image and a free-text editing instruction (e.g., 'drop the glass') and produces one physically plausible edited image, maintaining the standard single-image editing interface. During training, it additionally consumes video trajectories from PhysicTran38K: the first and last frames form the (source, target) editing pair, while N=6 uniformly sampled intermediate keyframes provide supervision for the transition queries via DINOv2 and VAE encoders. The diffusion backbone operates in the Qwen-Image-Edit latent space with LoRA (rank 128) fine-tuning, and the frozen Qwen2.5-VL-7B generates a structured physics reasoning trace on the fly at inference.

## Method

PhysicEdit introduces a textual-visual dual-thinking mechanism on top of the Qwen-Image-Edit (MMDiT) backbone. The textual branch uses a frozen Qwen2.5-VL to produce a structured physics reasoning trace describing applicable laws, causal unfolding, and material behavior. The visual branch appends K=64 learnable transition queries after the reasoning prefix; these queries are supervised during training by timestep-weighted L2 losses against pseudo-target features extracted from intermediate video keyframes via DINOv2 (structural semantics) and the Qwen-Image-Edit VAE (fine-grained texture), with a Perceiver-Resampler-like mechanism compressing spatial features and a residual-delta formulation targeting only the physical change rather than static content. At inference, a timestep-aware modulation smoothly interpolates between DINO-weighted structural guidance (high noise) and VAE-weighted textural guidance (low noise), aligning the transition signal with diffusion's coarse-to-fine trajectory. The total loss combines standard flow-matching with the transition loss (α=1), and gradients are disentangled so that transition queries and projection heads are updated exclusively by the transition loss while the diffusion transformer is updated by the flow-matching loss.

## Results

On PICABench-Superficial (physical realism), PhysicEdit achieves an overall score of 64.86, setting a new state-of-the-art among open-source models and improving over its Qwen-Image-Edit backbone (61.26) by 5.9%. The largest gains appear in categories requiring implicit dynamics: Light Source Effects jumps from 61.19 to 76.16, Deformation rises by 12.1 points to 60.76, and Causality improves from 48.95 to 59.23. On KRISBench (knowledge-grounded editing), PhysicEdit scores 72.16 overall, surpassing all open-source baselines and proprietary models like Gemini-2.0 (62.41) and Doubao (60.70), with the most dramatic improvement in Natural Science conceptual knowledge (+11.9 points to 71.57). On RISE-Bench (reasoning-based editing), PhysicEdit more than doubles the backbone's overall score from 8.9 to 18.6, with Temporal reasoning improving from 4.7 to 21.2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PhysicEdit outperforms all open-source baselines on PICABench, with the closest competitor being Qwen-Image-Edit (its backbone) at 61.26 vs. 64.86, and ChronoEdit at 58.67. Against proprietary models, PhysicEdit (64.86) trails GPT-Image-1.5 (67.05) and Nano Banana Pro (66.16) by 2.2 and 1.3 points respectively, but surpasses GPT-Image-1 (61.08) and Seedream 4.0 (61.91). On KRISBench, PhysicEdit (72.16) beats all open-source methods including ChronoEdit (70.96) and Uni-CoT (68.00), and outperforms proprietary Gemini-2.0 (62.41) and Doubao (60.70), though it remains well below GPT-4o (80.09). On RISE-Bench, PhysicEdit (18.6) surpasses EditThinker (17.8) and all other open-source models, with the largest gap in Temporal reasoning (21.2 vs. 10.8 for EditThinker). In human evaluation, PhysicEdit achieves the highest Elo rating (1045) and wins head-to-head against all three compared models, with a 60.0% overall win rate across 300 comparisons.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Qwen-Image-Edit architecture (frozen Qwen2.5-VL + MMDiT diffusion backbone) but departs from the standard discrete-mapping formulation of instruction-based editing by reframing it as a continuous physical state transition. It contrasts with the concurrent ChronoEdit, which synthesizes explicit intermediate video frames as reasoning steps, by instead distilling transition dynamics into compact latent queries (implicit visual thinking), avoiding pixel-level synthesis at inference. The video-as-supervision idea connects to earlier work (UniReal, OmniGen, Pathways on the Image Manifold), but those primarily use video keyframes for consistency; here, video provides explicit temporal supervision of how physical states evolve, which is then compressed into a fixed-length latent representation via a Perceiver-Resampler-like mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and visible failure mode of current editing models (e.g., a straw in water not refracting) and provides a principled solution: the train-test mismatch between video supervision and single-image inference is resolved by distilling transition priors into learnable queries, a clean architectural choice that could be adopted by other editing systems. The PhysicTran38K dataset with its 5-domain/16-subdomain/46-transition-type hierarchy is a useful resource for the community. Open questions include whether the implicit transition queries can capture multi-object or long-horizon interactions, whether the AI-generated video supervision introduces subtle physical inaccuracies that propagate, and whether the approach extends naturally to 3D or video editing settings.
