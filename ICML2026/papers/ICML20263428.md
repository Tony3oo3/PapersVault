## Problem

Batch normalization layers have been extended to Riemannian manifolds (SPD spaces, hyperbolic spaces, Lie groups) but remain absent for complex domains such as the Siegel disk and the complex unit ball, despite these spaces offering rich geometric structures relevant to signal processing and graph learning. The core difficulty is that closed forms for exponential maps, logarithmic maps, and geodesics—on which existing Riemannian BN layers rely—are generally unavailable on complex domains, necessitating an alternative geometric machinery for centering, biasing, and running-mean updates.

## Input / Output

The BN layer accepts a batch of k points on a complex domain (e.g., n×n complex symmetric matrices in the Siegel disk, or n-dimensional complex vectors in the complex unit ball) and outputs a normalized batch of k points on the same domain. For radar clutter, inputs are AR-model representations in Sym_n^+ × SD_n^{r-1}; for node classification, real-valued node features are mapped to the complex unit ball via the Discrete Fourier Transform before BN is applied; for action recognition, 3D skeleton coordinates are converted to the product space Sym_n^+ × SD_n. The Fréchet mean is computed by parameterizing the domain point on a Euclidean space and running gradient descent for a fixed number of iterations (5 in experiments).

## Method

The proposed BN layer replaces the exponential/logarithmic-map-based operations of Riemannian BN with automorphisms (biholomorphic self-maps) of the complex domain: centering a point x by the batch mean m is performed as φ_m(x), and biasing is done via the inverse automorphism φ_g^{(-1)}. For the running-mean update, the paper introduces 'almost geodesics' constructed from the Kobayashi pseudodistance: given two points x and y, the curve γ_{x,y}(t) = φ_x^{(-1)}(α_{x,y}(t) ⊗ φ_x(y)) interpolates between them, where the scalar multiplication ⊗ is defined through the identity automorphism and the curve α_{x,y}(t) is derived in closed form from the Kobayashi distance structure. This yields explicit, differentiable formulas for all BN operations on the Siegel disk (using the spectral norm and matrix automorphisms) and the complex unit ball (using the complex modulus and vector automorphisms), without requiring exponential or logarithmic maps. The approach is shown to reduce to existing Riemannian BN layers (SPDBN, gyrovector-space BN, Lie-group BN) in the appropriate special cases, unifying them under a single automorphism-based framework.

## Results

On radar clutter classification (6 synthetic datasets), SiegelNetBN achieves mean accuracies of 33.83%–65.93%, outperforming the closest Siegel-based baseline SiegelNetFC by 1.35%–3.46% across all datasets. On node classification (Airport, Pubmed, Cora), CBallNetBN reaches 80.24% on Airport (dim 32), surpassing HNN-GyroBN-H (78.79%) and HNN-RBN-H (60.49%). On action recognition (NTU60/NTU120), SiegelNetBN achieves 69.25% on XSubject60 (x-channel), beating the best SPD baseline LieBN-LCM (65.18%) and SPDNetBN (64.97%), with gains of 2–4% over all SPD-based methods across all channels and protocols.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed SiegelNetBN wins against all nine baselines across every task and dataset. On radar clutter classification, it outperforms kNN (Cabanes & Nielsen, 2021) by 5.7–14.6% and Kernel-Siegel (Chevallier et al., 2016) by 5.6–13.6%, while improving over the closest Siegel-based competitor SiegelNetFC (Nguyen et al., 2025a) by 1.35–3.46%. On node classification, CBallNetBN beats HNN-GyroBN-H (Chen et al., 2025) by 1.45–7.65% and HNN-RBN-H by 19.75–20.75% on Airport, Pubmed, and Cora. On action recognition, SiegelNetBN surpasses SPDNet (Huang & Gool, 2017) by 3.5–5.3%, SPDNetBN (Brooks et al., 2019) by 2.5–5.5%, LieBN (Chen et al., 2024) by 2.9–6.1%, and GBWBN (Wang et al., 2025) by 36–46% (GBWBN performs near chance level). Finally, incorporating the proposed BN layer into GrNet (Huang et al., 2018) yields GrNetSiegelBN, which improves over the original GrNet by more than 9% on all NTU60/NTU120 channels, demonstrating the BN layer's transferability to Grassmann-based architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of Riemannian batch normalization (Brooks et al. 2019 for SPD, Chen et al. 2024 for Lie groups, Chen et al. 2025 for gyrogroups) to complex domains by replacing the reliance on exponential/logarithmic maps with automorphisms and introducing 'almost geodesics' constructed from the Kobayashi pseudodistance for the running mean update. It builds directly on the Siegel neural network architecture of Nguyen et al. (2025a) by adding the first BN layer for Siegel spaces, and on the symmetric-space framework of Nguyen et al. (2025b) to show that existing Riemannian BN layers are special cases of the proposed construction. The key conceptual departure is leveraging tools from complex analysis (automorphisms, Kobayashi pseudodistance) rather than Riemannian geometry, which enables the method to apply to domains where closed-form geodesics or exponential maps are unavailable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper opens complex domains—particularly the Siegel disk and complex unit ball—as viable substrates for deep learning, a direction that has been largely unexplored despite their rich geometric structure. The finding that the complex unit ball substantially outperforms the real Poincaré ball on node classification (by 7–10%) suggests that the additional degrees of freedom in complex geometry may capture structure that real hyperbolic geometry cannot. Key limitations include the requirement for closed-form automorphisms, the computational cost of Fréchet mean computation (O(n_iters · k · n³)), and the reliance on SVD-based operations that scale poorly with dimension. Open questions include developing efficient Fréchet mean algorithms on complex domains and extending the framework to domains lacking closed-form automorphism characterizations.
