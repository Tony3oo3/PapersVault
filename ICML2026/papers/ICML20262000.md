## Problem

Existing differentially private (DP) decoding for LLMs focuses almost exclusively on the privacy-utility trade-off, leaving the trustworthiness of DP outputs—specifically hallucination—underexplored. The paper shows that DP noise empirically amplifies knowledge conflict and thereby increases hallucination, yet no prior work explicitly models or mitigates this effect. Additionally, no public benchmark exists for measuring fine-grained PII leakage in retrieval-augmented generation under realistic medical or financial conditions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a user query, a set of retrieved documents partitioned into M disjoint subsets, and target privacy parameters (ε, δ). At each decoding step it computes both a context-aggregated distribution (averaged over the M subsets) and a context-free base distribution to derive the confidence gap. The output is a sentence-segmented response in which PII-bearing sentences (identified by high CG) are redacted and hallucination-prone sentences (identified by low CG) are masked and refilled with text generated by a filler model (GPT-4o) conditioned on privately selected top-k keywords from the document corpus.

## Method

PEARL operates in two stages. In the first stage, token-level DP decoding uses the exponential mechanism over an ensemble of M disjoint document subsets, with logit clipping to bound sensitivity; the per-step privacy budget ε_step is allocated so that token generation consumes at most 80% of the total ε. The confidence gap (CG), defined as the entropy difference between the context-aggregated and base distributions, is computed at every step and serves as a direction-sensitive signal: high CG indicates PII regurgitation (sharp entropy reduction when the model copies retrieved context), while low CG indicates hallucination risk (poor grounding in retrieved evidence). In the second stage, a private top-k or sparse-vector mechanism selects sentences with the highest CG (S_P, redacted) and lowest CG (S_U, masked for refilling); the remaining privacy budget is split equally between filtering and refilling. Refilling uses the keyword-space aggregation method of Wu et al. (2024) with a private top-k keyword selection (Gillenwater et al., 2022), then prompts a filler model to reconstruct masked spans while keeping PII spans redacted. A theoretical proposition formalizes that DP-induced flattening of the token distribution widens the entropy gap with the parametric prior, providing an information-theoretic justification for CG-guided budget reallocation.

## Results

On LeakRAG-Medical with LLaMA 3.1 8B at ε=3, PEARL achieves GoldAlign 3.06 and HalluScore 1.87, outperforming NoRefill (2.53/2.52) and RandomRefill (3.01/1.97). At ε=8, PEARL reaches GoldAlign 3.14 and HalluScore 1.87, matching or exceeding the non-private baseline (ε=∞: 2.91/2.04). Against prior private decoding methods, PEARL achieves the lowest PII leakage (0.42–0.57 items) compared to PICL (2.24–3.60) and KSA (0.50–0.61), while also delivering the best GoldAlign and HalluScore on LLaMA 3.1 8B across all budgets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PICL (Tang et al., 2024), PEARL consistently achieves higher GoldAlign (e.g., 3.06 vs. 2.76 at ε=3 on LLaMA 3.1 8B) and lower HalluScore (1.87 vs. 2.41), while reducing PII leakage from 2.24 to 0.42 items at ε=3. Against KSA (Wu et al., 2024), PEARL improves GoldAlign (3.06 vs. 2.95 at ε=3) and HalluScore (1.87 vs. 2.15), with comparable PII leakage (0.42 vs. 0.50). Internally, PEARL outperforms both NoRefill and RandomRefill on HalluScore (up to 0.22 lower) and GoldAlign across all privacy budgets and both domains, confirming that confidence-gap-guided selective regeneration is more effective than uniform noise or random refilling. On Qwen 2.5 7B, PEARL achieves the best GoldAlign at all budgets and the lowest HalluScore at ε=8, while remaining competitive at smaller budgets.

## Relation to Prior Work

PEARL sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) differentially private LLM decoding, where prior work (Flemings et al., 2024; Koga et al., 2025; Tang et al., 2024; Wu et al., 2024) has focused almost exclusively on the privacy–utility trade-off measured by accuracy or perplexity, and (2) hallucination detection via the confidence gap (Bi et al., 2025), which quantifies knowledge conflict between parametric and retrieval-conditioned predictions. The paper departs from the first thread by arguing that DP noise actively amplifies knowledge conflict and thus hallucination, and from the second by repurposing CG not merely as a post-hoc detector but as a real-time signal for adaptive privacy-budget allocation. Algorithmically, PEARL composes the exponential mechanism for token-level DP with the private top-k keyword-space aggregation of Wu et al. (2024) and Gillenwater et al. (2022) for the refilling stage, but the key conceptual contribution is the CG-guided, sentence-level reallocation of the privacy budget between PII-bearing and hallucination-prone spans.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is that deploying DP on RAG pipelines without accounting for hallucination can make outputs *less* trustworthy than the non-private baseline, a failure mode that prior privacy-focused evaluations would miss. LeakRAG fills a concrete gap as the first public RAG benchmark with expert-validated synthetic PII and entity-level leakage annotations in medical and financial domains, enabling reproducible privacy evaluation that was previously infeasible under HIPAA/GDPR constraints. Limitations include reliance on GPT-4o as the filler model (raising cost and a circularity concern), evaluation on 7–8B models only, a relatively simple attack (a single appended malicious instruction), and a heuristic 80/20 budget split between generation and post-processing. Open questions include whether the CG split pattern (low CG → hallucination, high CG → PII) holds for larger or multilingual models, whether more sophisticated multi-turn or adaptive attacks can bypass the sentence-level redaction, and whether the framework extends to streaming or interactive settings where the full response is not available for post-hoc filtering.
