## Problem

In non-stationary reinforcement learning, the entropy coefficient (temperature) that controls exploration is typically fixed or tuned heuristically, causing over-exploration during stable phases and under-exploration after environmental drift. No principled rule existed for how exploration intensity should scale with the magnitude of environmental change, leaving a gap between variation-aware non-stationary RL theory and practical maximum-entropy deep RL algorithms.

## Input / Output

At each training update, AES consumes a scalar drift proxy computed as the 90th-percentile quantile of absolute TD errors (from critic Q-targets for off-policy methods or value TD deltas for PPO), smoothed via an exponential moving average or sliding window, along with the current training step index. It outputs a single scheduled entropy weight (temperature α for SAC/SQL/MEow, or entropy bonus coefficient c_ent for PPO) that directly replaces the fixed value in the actor or policy-loss objective, clipped to a pre-specified [λ_min, λ_max] range for numerical stability. No additional network parameters, extra forward passes, or architectural modifications are required.

## Method

AES derives a per-round dynamic-regret trade-off C₁ξ_t/λ_t + C₂λ_t by coupling the mirror-descent step size to the entropy coefficient (η_t = cλ_t) in an OCO surrogate, where ξ_t is the comparator drift and λ_t is the entropy strength. Minimizing this trade-off yields a square-root oracle rule λ_t* ∝ √ξ_t, which is made fully online by replacing the unobservable ξ_t with a conservative observable proxy (TD-error quantile) and using a prefix-sum schedule λ_t = √(C₁/C₂)·√(Â_t/t). The bridge to RL shows that MDP variation (reward and transition drift) controls soft-Q* drift via the γ-contraction of the soft Bellman operator, which in turn controls policy drift via the ℓ∞→ℓ₁ Lipschitz constant of softmax, so the OCO comparator drift is bounded by the MDP variation budget B_T^MDP. In practice, AES is a plug-in that only swaps the fixed entropy weight for the scheduled value across SAC, PPO, SQL, and MEow, leaving all other algorithmic components unchanged, and incurs negligible per-step overhead (one quantile computation and a scalar scheduler update).

## Results

Across 12 tasks and 4 drift modes, AES consistently improves normalized AUC (nAUC) and reduces performance drop-area ratio under non-stationary patterns. Under the most severe Abrupt drift on MuJoCo, nAUC improves from 0.67 (SAC) to 0.87 (SAC+AES) and from 0.67 (MEow) to 0.95 (MEow+AES); on Isaac Gym, MEow+AES reaches 0.81 vs. 0.68 for MEow. Abrupt-change recovery time (fraction of total training steps) drops substantially: SAC from ~14.0% to 7.7%, PPO from ~12.1% to 8.4%, SQL from ~15.7% to 9.7%, and MEow from ~11.6% to 6.4%. Under Steady (no drift) conditions, AES introduces no systematic degradation—SAC+AES actually achieves 1.13–1.24 nAUC vs. 1.00 for SAC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AES is evaluated as a plug-in on top of four entropy-regularized baselines: SAC, PPO, SQL, and MEow. Against SAC, AES improves Abrupt nAUC by 0.20 on MuJoCo (0.67→0.87) and 0.15 on Isaac Gym (0.58→0.73), while reducing recovery time from ~14% to ~7.7% of training steps. Against PPO, gains are more modest on MuJoCo (0.66→0.69) but substantial on Isaac Gym (0.44→0.62), with recovery time dropping from ~12.1% to 8.4%. Against SQL, the weakest baseline under drift, AES yields the largest absolute nAUC gains (e.g., MuJoCo Abrupt 0.52→0.65, Isaac Gym Abrupt 0.39→0.66) and cuts recovery time from ~15.7% to 9.7%. Against MEow, the strongest baseline, AES still improves Abrupt nAUC on MuJoCo (0.67→0.95) and Isaac Gym (0.68→0.81), and achieves the best overall recovery time at 6.4%. In all cases, AES does not degrade Steady-condition performance, confirming it is not a change-event-specific patch but a general adaptive exploration controller.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously disconnected lines of work: variation-aware non-stationary RL theory (Besbes et al., 2014; Cheung et al., 2020), which characterizes dynamic regret under cumulative variation budgets but does not address entropy scheduling, and maximum-entropy RL (SAC, SQL, MEow), which treats the entropy coefficient as a static hyperparameter. The key conceptual departure is casting entropy scheduling as a one-dimensional per-round trade-off (tracking cost C₁ξ_t/λ_t vs. stability cost C₂λ_t) derived from dynamic mirror descent, yielding a square-root scaling rule λ* ∝ √ξ_t. Unlike change-point detection or restart-based methods, AES performs continuous, lightweight modulation driven by an observable TD-error proxy, and unlike intrinsic-reward methods (ICM, RND), it modulates global exploration intensity rather than state-level novelty.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled, variation-aware scheduling rule for the entropy coefficient in non-stationary maximum-entropy RL, moving the parameter from heuristic tuning to a theoretically grounded control variable. The plug-in design (replacing a single scalar per update) makes it immediately applicable to existing SAC/PPO/SQL/MEow pipelines with negligible overhead. Key limitations include: the main theoretical result is a planning-version bound with explicit but uncontrolled residual terms for deep off-policy learning; the conformal calibration of the drift proxy requires access to ground-truth drift on a benchmark family; and the TD-error quantile proxy, while practical, is not guaranteed to be conservative in all regimes. Open questions include designing tighter drift proxies, analyzing interactions with other adaptation mechanisms (e.g., replay strategies, meta-learning), and extending the framework to partially observable or multi-agent settings.
