## Problem

Electron density (ED), the fundamental determinant of material properties in DFT, has been underutilized as an input modality for molecular property prediction due to two practical barriers: scarce large-scale ED data (limited to ~130K samples in QM9) and the enormous computational cost of representing ED as a 3D volumetric field with tens of millions of grid points. This paper addresses both gaps by constructing a 2M-molecule ED image corpus and an efficient multi-shot 2D image representation that enables large-scale physics-informed pre-training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260692/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 6 multi-shot ED images (top, bottom, left, right, front, back), each a 3×224×224 RGB image rendered from DFT-computed ED isosurfaces (isovalue 0.05) colored by an ESP ramp (−0.08 to +0.08 mapped to red-white-blue). For downstream pairwise tasks (e.g., acceptor-donor or emitter-solvent), two such 6-shot sets are encoded independently by a shared ViT-Base/16 encoder and their features concatenated before an MLP head. Outputs are continuous regression values for photovoltaic properties (PCE, V_OC, J_SC, FF) or optical properties (λ_Abs, λ_Emi, PLQY, FWHM), with logits averaged across all 6 shots to form the final prediction.

## Method

VisionED encodes each of the 6 ED image views with a shared ViT-Base/16 (patch size 16), then aggregates shot-level embeddings via set (average) pooling to produce an approximately rotation-invariant molecular embedding, justified theoretically as an empirical estimator of the Haar integral over SO(3). Pre-training uses three cross-scale physics-informed pretext tasks jointly optimized: Charge-Aware Pre-training (CAP) predicts the most positive/negative Mulliken-charged atoms via classification and regression; ESSOR-Guided Pre-training (EGP) regresses 69 in-house quantum descriptors spanning electronic, shape, ESP, orbital, and energy categories; and Quantum Semantic Population (QSP) classifies molecules into 100 k-means clusters of ESSOR vectors. The 12M-image pre-training corpus is built from EDBench by rendering DFT-computed ED/ESP via Psi4 and PyMOL, with ESSOR descriptors computed using Multiwfn. Fine-tuning appends a lightweight MLP head to the frozen or fine-tuned encoder in a pairwise format, requiring no architectural changes.

## Results

On the OPEP2 photovoltaic benchmark under acceptor scaffold split, VisionED achieves the lowest MAE across all four properties (VOC=0.075 V, JSC=3.518 mA·cm⁻², FF=7.541%, PCE=2.271%), yielding up to 7.2% relative improvement over the strongest baseline (VideoMol) on PCE. On the Deep4Chem optical benchmark under solvent scaffold split, VisionED achieves the largest gain of 27.0% on λAbs MAE (22.502 nm vs. 32.045 nm for VideoMol). Under 5-fold cross-validation on HOPV15, VisionED delivers an average 8.0% improvement (ranging 5.1%–14.9%) over the best baseline. In the zero-shot ternary blend extrapolation, VisionED recovers experimental PCE trends with 92.77% average accuracy across 15 unseen D₁+D₂:A and D:A₁+A₂ configurations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VisionED outperforms all eight baselines across every evaluated property on all three benchmarks. Against the strongest deep learning baselines, VisionED beats VideoMol (the best visual molecular model) by 7.2% on PCE and 6.4% on JSC under OPEP2 acceptor split, and by 27.0% on λAbs under Deep4Chem solvent split. EquiformerV2, the best geometric graph model, is outperformed by VisionED by 2.6% on VOC and 7.2% on PCE (OPEP2 acceptor), confirming that ED-image representations capture electronic information beyond geometric topology. The ED point-cloud baselines (PointVector, X-3D) are consistently the weakest deep methods, with PointVector showing 27.9% higher PCE MAE than VisionED's 6-shot image model under comparable GPU memory. Traditional ML baselines (SVM, RF with ECFP fingerprints) are outperformed by the largest margins (e.g., 47.4% average improvement over RF in OOD transfer), highlighting the advantage of physics-informed ED representations over hand-crafted descriptors. Notably, VisionED also demonstrates superior OOD robustness: when transferred from OPEP2 to HOPV15, it exhibits only a 6.8% MAE increase on JSC, far smaller than any baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in molecular property prediction, which relies on geometric coordinates, molecular graphs, or hand-crafted descriptors, by establishing electron density (ED) as a primary input modality. Prior ED research has focused almost exclusively on *estimating* ED (via 3D U-Nets, equivariant GNNs, or neural operators), while the few attempts to use ED as input (SepPCNET for toxicity, Kim et al. for symmetry classification) were task-specific and relied on expensive point-cloud representations. VisionED repurposes the vision-transformer lineage established by ImageMol and VideoMol—molecular images and videos—but redirects it toward ED isosurface renderings, drawing the multi-shot rendering strategy from 3D reconstruction (MVSNet) and information-compression ideas from DeepSeekOCR. The physics-informed pre-training objectives (CAP, EGP, QSP) are novel in their combination of atom-level charge prediction, 69-dimensional quantum descriptor regression, and k-means-based semantic clustering, but each component is individually standard.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 12M ED image corpus and the demonstration that a 6-shot 2D image representation outperforms 4096-point 3D point clouds by 26% with 2.6× less memory and 4.6× lower runtime make large-scale ED-based learning practically feasible for the first time. The zero-shot generalization to ternary blends (92.77% average accuracy) without fine-tuning suggests the learned ED representations capture transferable physical structure beyond the binary D-A pairs seen in training. Key limitations remain: the pipeline is bottlenecked by DFT computation for ED generation (~79% of end-to-end time), representations are static (no excited-state or time-dependent dynamics), and the downstream task coverage is limited to photovoltaic and optical properties. Open questions include whether surrogate ED generators (DeepDFT, GFN2-xTB) can close the accuracy gap while enabling true high-throughput screening, and whether the framework extends to inorganic or solid-state materials.
