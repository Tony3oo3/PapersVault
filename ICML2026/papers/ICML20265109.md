## Problem

3D editing requires simultaneously preserving cross-view geometric consistency, global structural fidelity, and fine-grained controllability, yet existing methods are either prohibitively slow (SDS-based), produce cross-view distortions (multi-view inpainting), or depend on error-prone manual 3D masks that cause unintended global alterations. Additionally, no large-scale paired dataset of original and edited 3D assets exists to train and evaluate end-to-end editing models at scale.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source 3D asset (represented in Trellis's structured latent space) and a single target image—an edited reference view produced by any 2D editor—as the sole guidance signal, requiring no auxiliary 3D masks. It outputs an edited 3D asset decoded via a VAE-based decoder into explicit representations such as 3D Gaussians or meshes, following Trellis's two-stage paradigm (coarse voxelized shape generation followed by texture refinement). The target image is consumed through standard cross-attention with image context, while structural priors from the source asset are injected via the proposed dual-guidance mechanism.

## Method

3DEditFormer extends the frozen Trellis image-to-3D transformer by inserting a Dual-Guidance Attention Block at every layer, which adds two parallel cross-attention branches attending to multi-stage features extracted from the source asset: fine-grained structural features at a late diffusion timestep (t≈0) that encode geometric detail for preserving unedited regions, and semantic transition features at an early timestep (t≈1) conditioned on the target image that capture how structure should evolve. A Time-Adaptive Gating mechanism (an MLP on the timestep embedding) produces dynamic per-timestep weights that emphasize semantic edits early in denoising and structural fidelity later, effectively disentangling what should change from what should remain. Training uses the Conditional Flow Matching objective with only 252M parameters finetuned for 40k iterations. The approach is trained on 3DEditVerse, a 116K-pair dataset constructed via pose-driven geometric edits (54K pairs from Mixamo character animations) and text-guided appearance edits (64K pairs generated through a cascade of DeepSeek-R1, Flux, Qwen-VL, SAM2, and Trellis with mask-guided Repaint for 3D lifting consistency).

## Results

On the 3DEditVerse test set (w/o Char-Anim subset, 1,500 pairs), 3DEditFormer achieves CD 7.04, NC 0.904, F1^0.01 86.05, PSNR 26.42, SSIM 0.938, LPIPS 0.045, and DINO-I 0.962 without requiring any 3D mask. On the full dataset (including character-animation compositions), it reaches CD 13.84, NC 0.830, F1^0.01 64.30, and DINO-I 0.963, outperforming EditP23 by 63.1% on F1^0.01 and 39.5% on DINO-I. The method yields an average +13% improvement over VoxHammer on 3D metrics while remaining mask-free.

## Baselines & Comparisons

3DEditFormer outperforms all three baselines on nearly every metric. Against VoxHammer (the strongest baseline, which requires precise 3D masks), 3DEditFormer improves CD from 9.84 to 7.04 (−28%), F1^0.01 from 77.22 to 86.05 (+11.5%), and LPIPS from 0.052 to 0.045, while matching SSIM (0.938 vs. 0.942) and slightly exceeding DINO-I (0.962 vs. 0.959). Critically, VoxHammer degrades sharply with even small mask perturbations: inflating the mask by just 9% drops F1^0.01 to 75.56 and by 27% to 72.02, whereas 3DEditFormer is entirely mask-free. Against Instant3dit, 3DEditFormer achieves substantially lower CD (7.04 vs. 29.34) and higher F1^0.01 (86.05 vs. 32.84), reflecting far more stable and localized edits. On the full dataset including pose-driven edits, 3DEditFormer surpasses EditP23 by 63.1% on F1^0.01 (64.30 vs. 32.33) and 39.5% on DINO-I (0.963 vs. 0.785), confirming its advantage in both geometric fidelity and semantic consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Trellis (Xiang et al., 2025), a structured 3D latent diffusion model for image-to-3D generation, by freezing its backbone and augmenting each transformer layer with a Dual-Guidance Attention Block that injects source-asset structural priors at two complementary diffusion stages. It departs from the three dominant 3D editing paradigms—SDS-based optimization (slow), multi-view image editing followed by reconstruction (inconsistent), and end-to-end latent-space editing with manual 3D masks (impractical)—by eliminating the mask requirement entirely and instead using time-adaptive gating to balance semantic transition cues against fine-grained structural fidelity. The data pipeline repurposes a cascade of foundation models (DeepSeek-R1, Flux, Qwen-VL, SAM2, DINOv2) to automate paired 3D asset generation at scale, a construction strategy not seen in prior 3D editing datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3DEditVerse dataset (116K training pairs, 1.5K curated test pairs) is the first large-scale, locally-annotated, multi-view-consistent resource for 3D editing, filling a critical gap that has limited supervised training of end-to-end editing models. The finding that a mask-free architecture can outperform the best mask-based method (VoxHammer) by ~13% on 3D metrics while being far more robust to input imperfections has direct practical implications for interactive 3D content creation pipelines. The authors acknowledge that latent-space editing may degrade fine geometric details at high resolution, leaving open the question of whether editing directly in explicit 3D representations (meshes, Gaussians) could close this gap without sacrificing the structural priors that make the current approach effective.
