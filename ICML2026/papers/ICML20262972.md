## Problem

Existing DNA sequence models (CNNs, Transformers, SSMs) rely on static or time-domain-exclusive operations, which limits their ability to simultaneously capture local motifs, long-range dependencies, and frequency-sensitive periodic signals (e.g., nucleosome positioning, codon periodicity) inherent in genomic sequences. No prior architecture provides a principled mechanism to adaptively tile the time-frequency plane at multiple resolutions while maintaining quasi-linear computational complexity.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 1-mer tokenized DNA sequences (each nucleotide as a single token) with context lengths ranging from 1k to 131k bp, pre-trained via masked language modeling (15% masking) on the human reference genome (hg38). Downstream outputs vary by task: binary class labels for genomic classification (8-dataset Genomic Benchmark), 919-dimensional multi-label vectors for chromatin profile prediction (DeepSEA), AUROC scores for SNP variant effect prediction at varying TSS distances, and per-nucleotide functional role annotations (exon, intron, donor, acceptor) for gene finding on sequences up to 14,000 bp.

## Method

The Synergistic Plasticity Unit (SPU) integrates three synergistic layers: (1) a Locus Plasticity Layer (LPL) that applies a bank of T differently-sized convolutional kernels to generate meta-features, then combines them via token-specific weights from a 1×1 convolution to produce contextually-adaptive local representations; (2) a Domain Plasticity Layer (DPL) that processes features in parallel through an FFT-accelerated global convolution (time domain) and a J-level discrete wavelet decomposition (frequency domain), where each frequency sub-band is independently processed by the global convolution before iterative reconstruction with learnable skip-connection scaling; (3) a Saliency Plasticity Layer (SPL) that computes dual-axis saliency scores—channel-wise via MLP on position-pooled features and position-wise via depthwise separable convolution on concatenated avg/max channel statistics—which are element-wise multiplied with the DPL's time-frequency features to selectively amplify informative signals. The theoretical analysis grounds the design in the Time-Frequency Uncertainty Principle, proving that the wavelet path constructs a logarithmic multi-resolution tiling of the time-frequency plane that overcomes the fixed-resolution limitation of static CNN or global-mixing architectures. SPU layers are stacked (Pre-Norm or Post-Norm) to build scalable SPU-DNA models from ~2M to 500M parameters, achieving quasi-linear O(L log L) complexity.

## Results

On the Nucleotide Transformer Benchmark (18 tasks), SPU-DNA (1.9M params) achieves the best performance on 16 of 18 datasets in the <5M parameter regime, with notable gains such as 58.81% MCC on Enhancer prediction (vs. 57.78% for ConvNova) and 97.86% on Splice Site Donor (vs. 96.80% for ConvNova). Scaled to 500M parameters, SPU-DNA leads on all 18 datasets, outperforming NT-V2 (500M) by up to 17.03 points on Enhancer types (62.98% vs. 45.95%). On the Genomic Benchmark, SPU-DNA (490k) outperforms the prior SOTA on 7 of 8 datasets with absolute improvements up to 5.75% (Human Regulatory: 94.20% vs. 88.45% for HyenaDNA). On the Bend Gene Finding task, SPU-DNA achieves 0.68 MCC, substantially exceeding the next-best ConvNova (0.55).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPU-DNA (1.9M) outperforms all four <5M baselines (HyenaDNA, Caduceus-PH, ConvNova, xLSTM-PH) on 16 of 18 NT Benchmark tasks, with the largest margins on Enhancer types (+1.28 over ConvNova) and H3K14ac (+2.92 over ConvNova); it trails only on H4ac (56.98 vs. ConvNova's 62.10). At the 500M scale, SPU-DNA surpasses NT-V2 on all 18 tasks, with particularly large gains on Enhancer types (62.98 vs. 45.95, +17.03) and H3K4me2 (60.22 vs. 32.12, +28.10). On the Genomic Benchmark, SPU-DNA (490k) beats ConvNova (386k) on 7/8 datasets, with the largest gap on Human Regulatory (94.20 vs. 88.21, +5.99). For variant effect prediction, SPU-DNA outperforms Enformer, Caduceus, and HyenaDNA across all TSS distance strata. In the local motif extractor ablation (Table 8), LPL outperforms CondConv, ConvNeXt, MambaOut, and GCB on all 8 Genomic Benchmark tasks. On efficiency (Table 5), SPU achieves the lowest FLOPs (5,431G training) among all compared methods, slightly trailing Mamba in wall-clock time (0.93s vs. 0.46s) due to the absence of custom CUDA kernels, while substantially outperforming Transformers in both memory and speed. On the Bend Gene Finding task, SPU-DNA (6.6M) achieves 0.68 MCC, exceeding ConvNova (7.4M) by 0.13 and NT-H (500M) by 0.27.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes ideas from three distinct lines of work—dynamic convolution (CondConv), FFT-accelerated global convolution (Hyena), and wavelet-based multi-resolution analysis—into a unified architecture (SPU) for DNA sequence modeling. It departs from prior DNA foundation models (CNNs, Transformers, SSMs) by introducing an explicit frequency-domain pathway via wavelet decomposition, addressing what the authors identify as a shared blind spot: all existing architectures operate exclusively in the time domain. The theoretical grounding in the Time-Frequency Uncertainty Principle (Gabor) provides a principled justification for the multi-resolution tiling, positioning SPU as a resolution to the fixed time-frequency trade-off that constrains both local (CNN) and global (Transformer/FFT) models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of frequency-domain modeling into DNA foundation models is a meaningful conceptual advance, as periodic signals (nucleosome positioning, codon periodicity, tandem repeats) are biologically critical yet poorly captured by time-domain-only architectures. The quasi-linear O(L log L) complexity makes the approach practical for genome-scale sequences, and the demonstrated scalability from 1.9M to 500M parameters with consistent gains over NT-V2 suggests a viable path to larger genomic foundation models. Open questions include whether the wavelet-based multi-resolution design generalizes to other biological sequences (RNA, protein), whether the wall-clock gap versus Mamba (currently 0.93s vs 0.46s at 100k length) can be closed with custom CUDA kernels, and whether the token-specific convolution in LPL scales efficiently to very high embedding dimensions.
