## Problem

Alignment (e.g., RLHF) substantially improves LLM output quality but causes a stark reduction in output diversity, as the aligned model's next-token distribution collapses toward a narrow set of high-probability continuations. Prior diversity-promoting methods either require costly retraining that risks compromising safety alignment, or rely on expensive inference-time techniques (multiple decoding passes, diverse beam search, paraphrase prompting) that still disproportionately degrade quality. No existing method achieves both high diversity and high quality in a single inference pass while offering continuous, user-controllable adjustment along the diversity-quality spectrum.

## Input / Output

BACO takes a text prompt and two off-the-shelf models — a base LLM (e.g., Llama-3-8B) and its aligned counterpart (e.g., Llama-3-8B-Instruct) — and produces a group of n=10 diverse text generations in a single decoding pass. The routing threshold γ acts as a continuous control knob analogous to temperature: larger γ biases toward the base model (more diversity), smaller γ toward the aligned model (more quality). Switching between models is restricted to word boundaries to avoid tokenization mismatches, and the first token is always sampled from the aligned model to anchor trajectory quality.

## Method

BACO orchestrates token-level collaboration between a base model (diversity source) and its aligned counterpart (quality source) via a lightweight router that decides, at each decoding step, which model generates the next token. The router operates on two complementary signal categories: logit-based strategies (e.g., route to base when its max token probability falls below threshold γ, or when its next-token entropy exceeds γ) and content-based strategies (e.g., route to aligned for punctuation/formatting tokens or function words to preserve stylistic coherence). The best-performing router (-P-PUNC) combines both: it first checks whether the next token is punctuation/formatting (route to aligned), then falls back to a probability-threshold decision. This design exploits the 'superficial alignment' phenomenon — base and aligned models largely agree on next-token predictions — so interventions are sparse and the ~2× FLOPs overhead per token is independent of the desired group size n, unlike baselines requiring n sequential passes. The framework requires no fine-tuning, no prompting, and no post-training, operating entirely at inference time.

## Results

Across 11×2 diversity-quality spaces aggregated over NoveltyBench, WildChat, and Narrative-Discourse, BACO (best router -P-PUNC) achieves an overall Coverage of 0.403 and Dominance of 32.7%, improving Coverage by 0.142 over the strongest inference-time baseline (Nudging, 0.261). The advantage is most pronounced on semantic diversity, where BACO reaches 40.5% Dominance and 0.360 Coverage versus 0.104/29.2% for the aligned model. On NoveltyBench specifically, BACO attains 0.474 Coverage and 39.9% Dominance. Human evaluation confirms these gains: BACO is rated higher in quality (4.04 vs. 2.83 on NoveltyBench) and wins 79.0% of diversity comparisons, with 79.6% of outputs judged most creative.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BACO substantially outperforms all inference-time baselines on the diversity-quality trade-off. Against the strongest collaboration-based baseline, NUDGING (Fei et al., 2025), BACO improves overall Coverage from 0.261 to 0.403 (+54%) and Dominance from 9.6% to 32.7%, with the gap widening to 40.5% on semantic diversity. Single-model baselines (Llama-3-8B base and Llama-3-8B-Instruct) each occupy only one extreme of the trade-off: the base model is 3.15× more diverse but 5.95× lower quality, while the aligned model dominates on quality (49.0% lexical Dominance) but collapses on semantic diversity (0.104 Coverage). Decoding-based (Diverse Beam Search, 0.3% Dom.) and prompting-based (In-context resampling / Paraphrase, 2.4% Dom.) methods contribute negligibly to the Pareto frontier, confirming that multi-pass or surface-level strategies fail to produce meaningful semantic diversity. On the Artificial Hivemind repetition benchmark, BACO places 15% of generations in the genuinely diverse regime (similarity < 0.6) versus 8% for Min-p sampling and 5% for Top-p, demonstrating that base-aligned collaboration pushes diversity beyond what any single aligned model's sampling can achieve. Cross-checks on verifiable benchmarks (GSM8K, IFEval) confirm that diversity gains do not come at the cost of accuracy or instruction-following quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

BACO builds directly on the superficial alignment hypothesis (Lin et al., 2023; Zhou et al., 2023a) and the token-level collaboration mechanism introduced by NUDGING (Fei et al., 2025), which showed that sparsely injecting aligned-model tokens into base-model decoding recovers task quality. The key departure is repurposing this mechanism from a quality-recovery tool into a diversity-quality optimizer: rather than nudging a base model toward aligned behavior, BACO dynamically routes between the two models to exploit their complementary strengths (base for diversity, aligned for quality). This positions BACO as a principled alternative to the broader landscape of inference-time diversity methods (temperature scaling, diverse beam search, paraphrase prompting, in-context resampling) that typically require multiple passes or degrade quality, and to training-time methods (diverse DPO, flow networks) that require retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BACO offers a practical, inference-time-only, single-pass solution to the diversity-quality trade-off with a continuous controllability knob (routing threshold), making it deployable without retraining or architectural changes. The finding that base-aligned collaboration substantially outperforms aligned-aligned collaboration (Table 4) underscores that the complementarity between pre- and post-alignment checkpoints is essential, not merely convenient. Open questions include whether learned routers or intermediate alignment checkpoints can further improve the Pareto frontier, whether the 2× FLOPs overhead can be eliminated via speculative decoding or KV-cache sharing at scale, and whether the framework extends to longer-horizon tasks (agents, code) and to the 'breadth thinking' mode the authors envision as a complement to deep reasoning.
