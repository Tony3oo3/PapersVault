## Problem

Existing spectrum prediction methods treat physical spectra as unconstrained sequences, failing to enforce hard constraints such as total-mass conservation and spectral geometry (sharp band edges, Van Hove singularities, sparse vibrational lines). Prior eDOS approaches predict band gaps and spectra separately, use pointwise regression that blurs peaks while reporting low per-bin error, and collapse crystals into single global embeddings that discard atom-projected contributions. These pathologies—mass drift, peak blurring, and missing element-resolved attribution—recur across eDOS, phDOS, XANES, and Raman, motivating a framework that reasons over structured spectra under physical constraints rather than performing unconstrained regression.

## Input / Output

The method takes a crystal structure represented as an atomic graph (nodes carry element-type and attribute features; edges encode interatomic distances) and, for XANES only, an absorber atomic number as additional conditioning. It produces a nonnegative, sum-normalized spectrum on a fixed discretized grid: 128 bins over [−4, 4] eV (CalTech) or 256 bins over [−5, 5] eV (TRI) for eDOS, 51 bins over [0, 1000] cm⁻¹ for phDOS, 128 bins over [−5, +40] eV relative to the per-element K-edge for XANES, and 128 bins over [0, 1500] cm⁻¹ for Raman. The full spectrum is retained (including band-gap regions), making the task more stringent than prior work that splices out gapped intervals.

## Method

DeepSciReasoner operates in two stages. Stage 1 (structure-aware spectrum decoding) uses an edge-aware crystal graph Transformer with gated, edge-modulated attention to encode atomic structure, then a decoder with self-attention over spectral bins and source (atom-to-spectral-axis) attention to predict the spectrum, preserving physically meaningful atom-projected contributions; it is trained with KL divergence on normalized spectra plus a total-mass regression term. Stage 2 (constraint-preserving physical reasoning) treats the normalized prediction as a probability density and refines it via a learned velocity field that satisfies the 1-D continuity equation, guaranteeing mass conservation by construction. The velocity field is supervised by regressing to the analytic 2-Wasserstein displacement-interpolation reference flow (McCann, 1997), enabling simulation-free training, and inference solves the equivalent CDF advection PDE with a first-order upwind scheme that preserves CDF monotonicity and nonnegativity. The same architecture transfers across eDOS, phDOS, XANES, and Raman without structural changes—only the spectral axis, discretization, and optional conditioning differ.

## Results

On the CalTech eDOS benchmark, DeepSciReasoner achieves R²=0.815, MAE=1.983, MSE=19.612, and WD=0.191, reducing MSE by 20.5% relative to the strongest baseline (DOSTransformer, MSE=24.675). On TRI, it reaches R²=0.761 and WD=0.305 versus DOSTransformer's R²=0.716 and WD=0.375. Generalization to other spectra is strong: XANES-full R² improves from 0.657 (MEGNet) to 0.764, Raman R² from 0.115 (GATGNN) to 0.264, and phDOS R² from 0.543 (M3GNet) to 0.636, with DeepSciReasoner achieving the best result on every metric across all four spectrum types.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeepSciReasoner outperforms all seven baselines on every metric across all four spectrum types. The closest competitor is DOSTransformer (Lee et al., 2023), a crystal-to-sequence Transformer, which achieves R²=0.771 on CalTech eDOS; DeepSciReasoner improves this to 0.815 (+4.4 R² points) and cuts MSE from 24.675 to 19.612 (−20.5%). Among structure-encoder baselines, GATGNN (R²=0.772 CalTech) and CrystalFormer (R²=0.765) are competitive on R² but lag substantially on MSE and WD, reflecting their inability to capture fine spectral geometry. Mat2Spec (Kong et al., 2022) and E3NN (Geiger & Smidt, 2022) trail further behind, with E3NN showing the weakest overall performance (R²=0.748, MSE=27.351 on CalTech). On the generalization benchmarks (Table 2), MEGNet and M3GNet serve as the strongest baselines for XANES, Raman, and phDOS; DeepSciReasoner surpasses M3GNet on phDOS (R² 0.636 vs. 0.543) and MEGNet on XANES-full (R² 0.764 vs. 0.657), with the largest relative gain on Raman (R² 0.264 vs. 0.115 for GATGNN), demonstrating that the mass-conserving transport refinement is particularly effective for sparse, sharply peaked spectra.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the crystal-to-sequence spectrum prediction line (Mat2Spec, DOSTransformer) by replacing unconstrained pointwise regression with a two-stage architecture: an edge-aware crystal graph Transformer with source (atom-to-spectral-bin) attention for structure-aware decoding, followed by a mass-conserving refinement stage that transports the normalized spectrum via a learned velocity field supervised by 2-Wasserstein displacement interpolation. The transport formulation draws directly on the flow-matching and optimal-transport literature (McCann 1997; Lipman et al. 2022), repurposing the OT geodesic as a physically principled reference flow rather than a generative objective. The broader positioning follows the 'AI for scientific reasoning under constraints' paradigm exemplified by AlphaFold and crystal phase mapping, where hard physical laws are embedded in the learning algorithm rather than enforced post hoc.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DeepSciReasoner provides a reusable, architecture-unchanged blueprint that transfers from eDOS to phDOS, XANES, and Raman without modification, which is practically valuable for high-throughput materials screening where multiple spectral observables are needed from a single structure. The constraint-preserving refinement via continuity-equation transport offers a principled mechanism for sharpening band edges and Van Hove singularities while guaranteeing mass conservation, addressing a known failure mode of pointwise regression. Key limitations include reliance on DFT-computed spectra (inheriting simulation biases), restriction to crystalline inorganic materials, and the fact that not all spectrum types admit equally well-defined conservation laws, so domain-specific constraints may be needed for broader deployment. Extending to experimental spectra, molecular systems, and out-of-distribution chemistries remains open.
