## Problem

Standard LoRA applies independent low-rank adaptation within each modality of a dual-encoder architecture, with no mechanism for cross-modal interaction during the adaptation process. This modality-isolated design limits the ability of PEFT methods to leverage complementary information between modalities, which is essential for effective multimodal adaptation of unimodal foundation models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264272/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

For vision-language tasks, the method takes a 448×448 RGB image paired with a natural language referring expression and produces either a bounding box (REC, evaluated by IoU>0.5 accuracy) or a pixel-level segmentation mask (RES, evaluated by mIoU) via a shared multi-task decoder. For audio-visual tasks, it takes a video frame and a synchronized audio signal, producing either temporal event labels across 28 categories (AVE) or pixel-level masks of sound-producing objects (AVS). The dual-encoder architecture processes each modality through its own frozen backbone (e.g., DINOv2 + BERT, or Swin-L + SSLAM), with CoLA modules inserted into all Q, K, V, and FFN linear projections of both encoders.

## Method

CoLA extends LoRA's single intra-modal pathway (ΔW_L = (α/r)B_L A_L) with a second inter-modal fusion pathway ΔW_C = λ B_C Φ A_C, where the r×r matrix Φ is dynamically generated by a two-layer hypernetwork from a global representation of the paired modality's features. Cross-modal features are propagated progressively through the dual-encoder: at each transformer layer, features from the paired encoder are fed into CoLA at the self-attention, output projection, and FFN stages in sequence, so each component receives refined cross-modal information from prior stages. The design is bidirectional—both modalities simultaneously adapt using the other's features—and the two pathways use fully separate low-rank matrices, which the paper shows via gradient analysis to be decoupled and empirically superior to shared configurations. Theoretically, while each per-input update retains rank r (matching LoRA), the input-dependent Φ allows the family of updates to span up to r² directions in weight space across inputs, providing greater expressive power than a fixed LoRA update.

## Results

On vision-language tasks, CoLA achieves 83.4% average accuracy on Referring Expression Comprehension (REC) and 73.7% average mIoU on Referring Expression Segmentation (RES) across RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, and RefCOCOg, outperforming the strongest full fine-tuning baseline (EEVG: 81.7% REC, 72.2% RES) while using only 17.2% parameter updates. On audio-visual tasks, CoLA achieves 80.7% accuracy on AVE (DINOv2-B-14/SSLAM) and 80.9% mIoU on AVS (Swin-L/SSLAM). Compared to rank-matched LoRA (r=16), CoLA gains approximately 1.1–1.5% on vision-language and 0.8–1.5% on audio-visual tasks; against parameter-matched LoRA (r=54), the gains are 1.4–1.6% and 0.7–1.5% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoLA outperforms all 18 baselines on the primary vision-language benchmarks: on REC it surpasses the best full fine-tuning method (EEVG, 81.7%) by 1.7% and the best PEFT method (SwimVG, 81.8%) by 1.6%; on RES it exceeds EEVG (72.2%) by 1.5% and DETRIS (71.0%) by 2.7%. Against LoRA at the same rank (r=16), CoLA gains 1.1% (REC) and 1.5% (RES) on vision-language and 1.5% (AVE) and 0.8% (AVS) on audio-visual tasks; against parameter-matched LoRA (r=54), the margins are 1.6% (REC), 1.4% (RES), 1.5% (AVE), and 0.7% (AVS), confirming the gains come from cross-modal architecture rather than extra parameters. On AVE, CoLA with DINOv2-B-14 (80.7%) beats LAVisH ViT-B-16 (75.3%) and STG-CMA CLIP-B-16 (78.7%) but trails STG-CMA CLIP-L-14 (83.3%), which benefits from specialized temporal-spatial adapters at larger scale. On AVS, CoLA (80.9%) ties DG-SCT (80.9%) and beats LAVisH (80.1%) but is slightly below STG-CMA (81.8%). Notably, CoLA is the first multi-task PEFT framework for visual grounding, simultaneously handling both REC and RES with a single set of adaptation weights.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoLA extends LoRA (Hu et al., 2022) by adding a dedicated inter-modal low-rank pathway whose transformation matrix Φ is dynamically generated from cross-modal features via a hypernetwork, enabling input-dependent cross-modal adaptation at the level of individual linear components. This departs from prior dual-encoder PEFT methods (ETRIS, BarLeRIa, DETRIS, LAVisH, STG-CMA, DG-SCT) that fuse cross-modal information at the module level or rely on jointly-trained encoders like CLIP; CoLA instead operates within fully independent unimodal foundation models (DINOv2, BERT, SSLAM, Swin) and integrates cross-modal awareness directly into the low-rank factorization. The progressive propagation strategy, where cross-modal features are updated sequentially through self-attention, output projection, and FFN stages, builds on the observation that intermediate-layer cross-modal interaction (Yang et al., 2022; Ye et al., 2022; Deng et al., 2023) benefits multimodal representations, but applies it within the PEFT parameter space rather than through additional fusion modules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CoLA provides a general-purpose, modality-agnostic PEFT framework that can be applied to any dual-encoder architecture and any combination of modalities, and it establishes the first multi-task PEFT approach for visual grounding (simultaneous REC and RES). A key limitation is that the inter-modal pathway cannot be merged into pre-trained weights at inference due to its dependence on dynamic cross-modal features, introducing runtime overhead that grows with model depth. The gap between CoLA with DINOv2-L-14 (81.1%) and STG-CMA with CLIP-L-14 (83.3%) on AVE suggests the approach may not scale as effectively as task-specific adapter designs at larger model sizes. Open questions include whether the framework generalizes to more than two modalities, how it interacts with other LoRA variants (e.g., DoRA, VeRA), and whether integration into multimodal LLMs can preserve the parameter efficiency advantage.
