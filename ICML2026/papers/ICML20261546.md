## Problem

RL-based post-training methods like GRPO substantially improve LLM reasoning accuracy but produce unnecessarily verbose reasoning chains, inflating inference cost with diminishing returns. Existing token-efficient variants (length-based or position-based advantage shaping) are content-agnostic: they assign token advantages based on completion length or token position rather than the actual information a token contributes to answer correctness, making them unable to distinguish essential reasoning steps from redundant ones.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text query (e.g., a math word problem or commonsense multiple-choice question) and produces a reasoning chain followed by a final answer. During training, for each query a group of G=8 completions is sampled, and for every token in each completion an early-exit postfix prompt (e.g., '<answer>') is appended to elicit an answer distribution, enabling per-token conditional MI computation. The final output is a single reasoning completion with a structured answer, and the training objective explicitly optimizes the ratio of correctness to completion length.

## Method

IAPO replaces GRPO's uniform sequence-level advantage with a three-component token-level advantage: a normalized sequence reward, a token-level informativeness score (the conditional mutual information I(y; o_t | q, o_{<t}) measuring how much each token reduces uncertainty about the final answer), and a token-level exploration adjustment (±π_old(o_t|q, o_{<t}) that suppresses or encourages exploration depending on correctness). To make per-token MI computation tractable, the paper introduces an early-exit estimator that appends a short answer-generation postfix to measure entropy reduction, combined with KV-cache preloading (reusing cached prefix states so each entropy evaluation costs only O(K) rather than O(L)) and chunk-wise batched forwarding, reducing overall complexity from O(L³d) to O((K³+L²)d). A theoretical analysis (Theorem 4.1) shows that under a mild monotonicity assumption on average token informativeness, IAPO provably reduces expected completion length relative to GRPO while the exploration term provably modulates policy entropy to maintain accuracy.

## Results

On GSM8K with Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, IAPO reduces reasoning length from 157 to 84 tokens (47% reduction) while maintaining Pass@32 of 0.9805, achieving a token-efficiency ratio (R@32) of 1.17×10⁻²—nearly 2× the best baseline (S-GRPO at 6.68×10⁻³). On MATH-500 (0.5B), IAPO attains the highest Pass@32 (0.5960) among all methods. On DAPO-Math-17k (1.5B), IAPO achieves the best Pass@32 (0.3200) and R@32 (7.72×10⁻³). Wall-clock inference time is reduced by up to 17.7% on the 7B model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IAPO achieves the best token-efficiency ratio (R@32) in 11 out of 18 dataset–model configurations, with the most dramatic gain on GSM8K-7B where it halves completion length (84 vs. 147–193 tokens) while keeping Pass@32 within 0.5% of baselines. Against S-GRPO (the strongest length-based baseline), IAPO wins on R@32 in 14/18 settings but occasionally trails on raw Pass@32 (e.g., DAPO-Math-17k-7B: 0.4267 vs. 0.4667). Against DAPO, IAPO consistently produces shorter completions at comparable or higher accuracy; on MATH-500-0.5B, IAPO's Pass@32 (0.5960) exceeds DAPO's (0.5488) by 5 points. Against GFPO and GTPO, IAPO dominates on both accuracy and efficiency in most settings, with GTPO producing the longest completions (up to 519 tokens on DAPO-Math-17k-7B) and the lowest accuracy. On the non-math CommonsenseQA benchmark, IAPO achieves the highest Pass@32 (0.8000) and second-best R@32 (3.36×10⁻²), confirming generalizability beyond math reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

IAPO is built directly on the GRPO framework (Shao et al., 2024), modifying its uniform token-level advantage assignment with an information-theoretic signal: each token's conditional mutual information with the final answer. This departs from existing token-efficient RL methods (DAPO, GFPO, GTPO, S-GRPO), which are content-agnostic—relying on completion length or token position rather than semantic contribution. The paper also introduces practical engineering contributions (early-exit MI estimation, KV-cache preloading, chunk-wise forwarding) to make per-token MI computation tractable, and provides a covariance-based theoretical analysis (Theorem 4.1) linking the advantage shaping to expected length reduction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Token-efficient reasoning is a practical bottleneck for LLM deployment, as inference cost scales quadratically with sequence length; IAPO's 47% token reduction with maintained accuracy is a meaningful efficiency gain for production systems. The information-theoretic framing offers a principled alternative to heuristic length/position penalties and could generalize to other credit-assignment problems in sequential decision-making. Open questions include whether the MI-based advantage shaping transfers well to non-mathematical or multi-turn reasoning, whether the key assumption in Corollary 4.2 (monotonic decrease in per-token informativeness with length) holds broadly, and whether the added MI estimation overhead remains acceptable at larger model scales or longer sequences.
