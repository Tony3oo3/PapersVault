## Problem

Most personalized federated learning methods require clients to hold labeled data for finetuning, yet in practice the majority of participating (and future) clients will be unlabeled. This paper addresses the gap by generating personalized models for clients using only a single forward pass on their unlabeled data, while still leveraging labeled clients during training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265691/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, a client passes its unlabeled dataset X_i through the hypernetwork h to obtain a low-dimensional subspace vector v_i ∈ R^k (e.g., k=10^4), which is then expanded to full model parameters via a fixed random matrix P and initialization θ_0 (both generated locally from a shared seed, never transmitted). The resulting personalized classifier f(·;θ_i) requires no further training or finetuning. The subspace dimension k is orders of magnitude smaller than the full parameter count d (≈15× reduction for CNN, ≈1100× for ResNet18), keeping the hypernetwork small enough to run entirely on-device.

## Method

FLowDUP trains a hypernetwork h = h_2(mean(h_1(x))) that maps an unlabeled client dataset to a low-dimensional subspace parameterization of the personalized model, rather than outputting all parameters directly. The training objective has two parts: a loss L evaluated only on labeled clients (measuring prediction quality of the generated model on held-out labeled data) and a regularizer Ω computed on all clients (penalizing deviation of generated subspace parameters from a learned global regularizer ψ_r), which is the key mechanism allowing unlabeled clients to contribute gradients during training. This objective is derived from a new transductive multi-task PAC-Bayesian generalization bound that provides performance guarantees for unlabeled clients and shows the complexity term scales with total samples across all clients, not just labeled ones. Training follows standard Federated Averaging with a labeled-client sampling rate α to control the fraction of labeled signal per round, and the random expansion uses a Kronecker-product construction for O(√(dD)) memory instead of O(dD).

## Results

On class-partitioned CIFAR-10 with all clients labeled (p=1.0), FLowDUP achieves 86.6% accuracy with a CNN and 92.3% with ResNet-18, outperforming the strongest baselines (ATP at 77.8% and 89.6% respectively) by 8.8 and 2.7 points. Even with only 10% of clients labeled (p=0.1), FLowDUP reaches 66.1% (CNN) and 72.7% (ResNet-18), surpassing FedTTA (57.2%, 69.3%) and ATP (53.5%, 71.3%). On FEMNIST, FLowDUP achieves 89.3% accuracy at p=1.0, beating FedAvg (84.3%) and ATP (84.1%) by 5+ points. The method also outperforms all baselines on Rotated Fashion-MNIST (87.3% vs. best baseline 81.5% at p=1.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the primary unlabeled-client setting, FLowDUP outperforms all four baselines (FedAvg, FedProx, FedTTA, ATP) across every dataset and labeled-fraction configuration, with the largest margins over FedAvg/FedProx (20+ points on CIFAR-10 CNN at p=1.0) and consistent 5–9 point gains over the test-time adaptation methods FedTTA and ATP. The low-dimensional baseline LD-FedAvg, which shares FLowDUP's subspace parameterization but lacks personalization, underperforms even full-dimensional FedAvg, confirming that the subspace is only effective when paired with the hypernetwork. In the labeled-data comparison (Table 5), FLowDUP (86.6% CIFAR-10, 89.3% FEMNIST) beats Per-FedAvg (69.0%), FedRep (77.4%), pFedMe (74.3%), kNN-Per (80.8%), and pFedHN (60.9%), but is slightly outperformed by PeFLL (88.9%, 90.7%), which requires access to individual client updates and incurs higher communication overhead. Notably, FLowDUP's advantage over test-time adaptation methods (FedTTA, ATP) is most pronounced on datasets with feature heterogeneity (Rotated Fashion-MNIST, FEMNIST), where logit-based adaptation signals are insufficient.

## Relation to Prior Work

FLowDUP sits at the intersection of three research threads: hypernetwork-based model generation (Ha et al., 2017; Shamsian et al., 2021), learning in low-dimensional random subspaces (Li et al., 2018; Lotfi et al., 2022), and PAC-Bayesian multi-task generalization theory (Zakerinia et al., 2024). The key departure from prior hypernetwork-based personalized FL (Shamsian et al., 2021; Scott et al., 2024) is the use of a low-dimensional subspace parameterization, which shrinks the hypernetwork output from full model dimensionality to k ≪ d, making it small enough to run on-device rather than on the server. Theoretically, the paper extends PAC-Bayesian multi-task bounds from the inductive setting (all tasks labeled) to a transductive setting where only a subset of tasks have labels, and shows that unlabeled clients can contribute to the complexity term, improving the bound's rate by a factor of √(1 − n_L/n).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that FLowDUP removes the labeling requirement for personalization, which is a major barrier in real-world FL deployments where users are unlikely to annotate their data. The low-dimensional subspace design is not merely a compression trick but is essential for adhering to the federated paradigm: it keeps the hypernetwork small enough to transmit to and execute on client devices, avoiding the multi-step server-side communication schemes used by prior hypernetwork methods. The main limitation, acknowledged by the authors, is that personalization can only exploit the marginal input distribution, so the method works well only when input statistics suffice to determine a good model. Open questions include how the approach scales to more complex or continuous heterogeneity (beyond discrete rotations or class partitions), and whether the transductive PAC-Bayes framework can be tightened to yield non-vacuous bounds for deep networks.
