## Problem

Vanilla softmax attention has quadratic complexity in input size, while its linear-complexity variants lose the ability to focus on relevant tokens. The paper proves a fundamental dispersion property: for any continuous normalization function, attention coefficients converge to 1/n as the number of keys grows, meaning all global attention mechanisms inevitably become uniform. This leaves a gap for a theoretically grounded, scalable attention that preserves local focus while still capturing global information without quadratic cost.

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB images tokenized via 4×4 patches in a 4-stage hierarchical architecture (Swin-style), producing 56×56 tokens at the standard 224² resolution and up to 256×256 tokens at 1024². Outputs are class labels for ImageNet-1K classification, instance segmentation masks via Mask R-CNN on COCO, and semantic segmentation masks via UPerNet on ADE20K. The window size is fixed at 7 for standard resolution and 8 for large-resolution experiments, with the averaging term applied independently per attention head.

## Method

SEMA replaces the linear attention block in MILA's Mamba-like macro-architecture with a two-component attention: (1) window softmax attention over a fixed-size neighborhood (w=7), which preserves local focusing on high-frequency features and is provably dispersion-free since each query attends to only w keys regardless of total sequence length; and (2) arithmetic averaging of all value tokens, which serves as a theoretically justified global approximation derived from the proven dispersion limit of full attention (softmax(qK^T)V ≈ (1/n)Σv_j as n→∞). The theoretical foundation is Theorem 3.3, which shows that for any continuous normalization φ on a compact feature space, Φ-normalized attention coefficients are bounded by φ(b)/(n·φ(a)) and thus vanish as n grows. This local-global decomposition is embedded in a Mamba-inspired recursive state-space structure with RoPE positional encoding within windows and LePE value projection, yielding linear complexity in sequence length while maintaining both local selectivity and global context.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K at 224² resolution, SEMA-T achieves 83.7% top-1 accuracy (26M params, 4.3G FLOPs), outperforming MILA-T (83.3%) and DefMamba-S (83.5%). At larger scales, SEMA-S and SEMA-B reach 84.6% and 85.4% top-1 accuracy respectively, surpassing MILA-S/B (84.4%/85.3%). On larger-resolution inputs (up to 1024²), SEMA-T maintains a 0.4–0.7% advantage over VMamba-T after fine-tuning, and is ~33% faster than MILA-T at 1024² and 2048² resolutions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SEMA outperforms all 14 baselines on ImageNet-1K at 224². The most direct comparison is against MILA (the architectural template): SEMA-T/S/B beat MILA-T/S/B by 0.4/0.2/0.1% top-1 respectively, with comparable parameter counts (26M vs 25M, 46M vs 43M, 102M vs 96M). Against the strongest Mamba-based model DefMamba, SEMA-T (83.7%) exceeds DefMamba-S (83.5%) by 0.2% with fewer parameters (26M vs 32M), and SEMA-S (84.6%) beats DefMamba-B (84.2%) by 0.4%. Against VMamba, SEMA-T gains 1.1% (83.7 vs 82.6), and the gap widens dramatically at 1024² resolution where SEMA-T leads by 7.3% without fine-tuning (64.7 vs 57.4). Among transformer baselines, SEMA-T surpasses NAT-T and Inline-CSwin-T (both 83.2%) by 0.5%, and Swin-T (81.3%) by 2.4%. On COCO Mask R-CNN 3x, SEMA-T achieves the best AP^b (49.2) and AP^m (43.9) among all Mamba-like models, edging out MILA-T (48.8/43.8). On ADE20K semantic segmentation, SEMA-T reaches 48.2 mIOU (single-scale), surpassing VMamba-T.

## Relation to Prior Work

SEMA is built directly on MILA's Mamba-like macro-architecture (Han et al., 2024b), replacing its linear attention block with a window-softmax-attention-plus-averaging module, while inheriting the 4-stage hierarchical design from Swin (Liu et al., 2021) and the exponential-forgetting intuition from Mamba's selective state space (Gu & Dao, 2024). The key conceptual departure from prior work is theoretical: rather than attempting to *fix* the known dispersion of softmax or linear attention (via adaptive temperature, differential attention, or focused attention), the paper proves a general dispersion theorem for all Φ-normalized attention and then *embraces* the asymptotic limit by approximating the global component with a simple arithmetic average. This positions SEMA as a theoretically grounded local-global approximation within the Mamba-like family, distinct from both the window-shifted approach of Swin and the pure linear-attention approach of MILA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The general dispersion theorem provides a clean unifying lens for understanding why both softmax and linear attention lose focus at scale, and the practical consequence—that a trivial O(1) averaging term recovers global information in the large-token regime—offers a simple, scalable design principle for vision models processing high-resolution or long-sequence inputs. The 7.3% accuracy gap over VMamba at 1024² resolution and the ~33% runtime speedup over MILA at large resolutions suggest the approach is particularly relevant for applications like medical imaging where token counts reach 10⁹. Open questions include whether the theoretical bounds can be sharpened, whether learned or sparsely-weighted averaging (analogous to Multi-Query or Multi-Head Latent Attention) can further improve the global component, and whether the framework extends beyond vision to other long-sequence domains.
