## Problem

Training LLMs to forecast open-ended world events is bottlenecked by the scarcity of suitable training data: prediction-market questions are limited in volume, dominated by binary outcomes that create noisy RL rewards, and carry platform-specific distributional biases. Moreover, naively generating forecasting questions from news articles leaks the answer through wording or context, causing RL training to learn shortcuts and actually degrade forecasting performance. The paper addresses how to build a scalable, leakage-free pipeline for open-ended forecasting questions and how to train models on them effectively.

## Input / Output

The forecaster receives a forward-looking natural-language question with an explicit resolution date and criteria (e.g., 'Who will be confirmed as the new prime minister of Ukraine by 17 July 2025?') plus up to 5 retrieved 512-token news chunks from articles published at least one month before the resolution date. It outputs a short free-form answer (1–3 words, typically a name, location, or entity) together with a probability q ∈ [0,1] expressing confidence in that answer being the true outcome. Answers are graded for semantic equivalence by a separate LLM judge rather than exact string matching.

## Method

The paper introduces OpenForesight, a ~52K-question dataset generated by an automated pipeline that extracts events from 248K CommonCrawl News articles using DeepSeek-v3 as a sample creator and Llama-4-Maverick as a sample selector, with multi-stage filtering (validation, best-question selection, leakage rewriting, answer-type filtering) that removes ~90% of candidates to eliminate answer-revealing shortcuts. For training, the authors fine-tune Qwen3-8B (thinking mode) with GRPO, using a combined reward of 0/1 accuracy plus an adapted multi-class Brier score for free-form responses, which they show outperforms either term alone by preserving exploration on hard, low-confidence questions. At inference, a Qwen3-8B Embedding model retrieves the top-5 most relevant news chunks from an offline 1M-article corpus (cutoff one month before resolution) and appends them to the prompt, yielding 9–18% accuracy gains. The key empirical finding is that data curation is the critical lever: training on 10K fully-filtered questions outperforms training on 30K unfiltered ones, while unfiltered training actively worsens forecasting.

## Results

On the held-out open-ended test set (302 questions, May–Aug 2025), OpenForecaster-8B achieves 33% accuracy and significantly outperforms all other models on Brier score, surpassing the much larger Qwen3-235B and matching DeepSeek-R1 and Grok-3-mini while remaining slightly behind GPT-OSS-120B. On the external FutureX benchmark (86 questions), OpenForecaster-8B achieves the strongest accuracy by a large margin and is close to the best Brier score. Calibration improvements from forecasting training generalize out-of-distribution to SimpleQA, GPQA-Diamond, and MMLU-Pro, and long-term prediction consistency improves by 15% on arbitrage metrics and 4% on frequentist metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Qwen3-235B (the same family at ~30× the parameter count), OpenForecaster-8B surpasses it on both accuracy and Brier score on the open-ended test set and FutureX, demonstrating that targeted forecasting training in an 8B model can exceed a much larger general-purpose model. Against DeepSeek-R1, OpenForecaster-8B matches performance on the open-ended test set and outperforms it on Metaculus binary questions, though GPT-OSS-120B remains significantly ahead on Metaculus. Against Llama-4-Maverick, OpenForecaster-8B outperforms on Metaculus binary questions. Against Grok-3-mini, the two models are roughly matched on the open-ended test set. Notably, the paper also shows that applying the same training recipe to Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct yields a +25% absolute accuracy increase, surpassing many larger models, confirming the recipe generalizes across model families.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RL-for-forecasting line of work (Turtel et al. 2025b, Damani et al. 2025) from small, human-curated prediction-market questions to a fully automated, scalable pipeline that synthesizes open-ended forecasting questions from daily news. It builds on GRPO (Shao et al. 2024) for training and adapts the multi-class Brier scoring rule (Damani et al. 2025) to free-form answers, but departs from prior forecasting evaluations (Zou et al. 2022, Halawi et al. 2024, Karger et al. 2024) by moving beyond binary/multiple-choice questions to genuinely open-ended ones where the answer space is not predefined. The key methodological departure is the data curation pipeline—filtering, leakage removal, and targeted rewriting—which the paper shows is not a minor preprocessing step but the critical factor determining whether training helps or hurts forecasting performance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, open-sourced recipe (dataset, code, models) for training LLM forecasters at scale, and the finding that an 8B model can match much larger proprietary systems (GPT-OSS-120B, DeepSeek-R1) on both accuracy and calibration is practically significant for resource-constrained settings. The observation that calibration gains from forecasting training transfer to unrelated benchmarks (SimpleQA, MMLU-Pro, GPQA-Diamond) suggests a broader mechanism by which uncertainty-aware training improves general model behavior, opening questions about whether this generalizes further. Limitations include distributional bias from news-only sourcing, late-reporting artifacts that make some questions easier than others, and the absence of long-form forecast evaluation, all of which constrain the generality of the current recipe.
