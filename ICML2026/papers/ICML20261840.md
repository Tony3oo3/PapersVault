## Problem

Diffusion-based super-resolution of high-dimensional scientific spatial fields is computationally prohibitive when performed directly in pixel space, requiring large networks, substantial memory, and long sampling times. Learned latent spaces (e.g., VAE-based) reduce cost but lack a clear connection between latent noise and spatial variability, limiting interpretability and principled uncertainty propagation. The paper addresses the unexplored question of whether a fixed, variance-ordered orthonormal basis (POD) can serve as a structured latent space for conditional diffusion, enabling both efficient ensemble generation and analytic uncertainty propagation to physical space.

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution spatial field (e.g., 53×31 SST from ACCESS-S2 or 32×32 advection-diffusion snapshot) as input, which is bicubically upsampled to the target high-resolution grid (640×480 or 128×128) and then projected onto a truncated POD basis to yield a K-dimensional conditioning vector. The output is an ensemble of M=100 high-resolution field reconstructions generated via independent reverse-diffusion trajectories, from which spatially resolved predictive uncertainty maps (posterior standard deviation) are derived. Because reconstruction is linear, the spatial covariance is computed analytically as Σ_u = Φ Σ_a Φ^T rather than requiring repeated full-network evaluations.

## Method

PODiff performs conditional denoising diffusion entirely in a fixed, variance-ordered POD coefficient space of dimension K (typically 40, capturing ≥99% cumulative variance), reducing the effective dimensionality from d spatial degrees of freedom to K coefficients. The POD basis is computed once via SVD of centered training snapshots and is never re-learned; the denoiser is a compact residual MLP (4 hidden layers, width 256) that predicts injected noise given noisy K-dimensional coefficients, the K-dimensional conditioning vector, and a sinusoidal timestep embedding. Training uses the standard DDPM noise-prediction objective with T=1000 steps, and inference uses a reduced-step sampler (S=100). The key structural advantage is that linear POD reconstruction (û = ū + Φâ₀) allows predictive uncertainty to propagate analytically from latent to physical space, yielding spatially interpretable uncertainty where leading-mode uncertainty affects large-scale patterns and higher-mode uncertainty manifests as localized fine-scale variability—without any auxiliary uncertainty network.

## Results

On the Western Australian SST downscaling task (640×480 from 53×31), PODiff-K40 achieves the best reconstruction accuracy with RMSE 0.3923 °C and MAE 0.2976 °C, outperforming PixelDiff (0.4118/0.3158), VAE-LDM (0.4011/0.3005), and the deterministic U-Net (0.6788/0.5141). For uncertainty, PODiff-K40 achieves near-perfect 90% empirical coverage (0.9009) and a CRPS of 0.2889, substantially better than MC Dropout U-Net (CRPS 0.4821). Computationally, PODiff uses only 0.20M parameters and 1.4 GB peak GPU memory versus 33–39M parameters and 12.5–13.1 GB for pixel-space and VAE-based diffusion, with 3.8 h training versus 28–48 h.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PODiff-K40 outperforms the deterministic U-Net (Ronneberger et al., 2015) by a wide margin, reducing RMSE from 0.6788 to 0.3923 °C (−42%) and extreme-event RMSE from 0.8366 to 0.4836 °C. Against the MC Dropout U-Net (Gal & Ghahramani, 2016), PODiff achieves substantially better calibration (MACE 0.0128 vs. larger deviations) and a 40% lower CRPS (0.2889 vs. 0.4821), while MC Dropout shows systematic undercoverage at lower confidence levels. Compared to PixelDiff (Ho et al., 2020), PODiff achieves slightly lower RMSE (0.3923 vs. 0.4118) at a fraction of the cost: 0.20M vs. 33M parameters, 3.8 h vs. 48 h training, and 0.08 s vs. 1.24 s per sample. Against VAE-LDM (Rombach et al., 2022), PODiff matches or slightly beats reconstruction (RMSE 0.3923 vs. 0.4011) while using 40 POD coefficients instead of a 2560-dimensional learned latent, with 195× fewer parameters. The RandOrthDiff ablation (random orthonormal basis, same architecture) degrades RMSE to 0.9987, confirming that the POD variance ordering—not merely dimensionality reduction—is critical for performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

PODiff sits at the intersection of two well-established lines of work: denoising diffusion probabilistic models (Ho et al., 2020) for conditional generation, and Proper Orthogonal Decomposition (Sirovich, 1987) for reduced-order representation of spatial fields. The key conceptual departure from latent diffusion (Rombach et al., 2022; Vahdat et al., 2021) is replacing the learned nonlinear autoencoder with a fixed, variance-ordered orthonormal POD basis, which preserves a direct linear mapping between latent and physical space. This enables analytic second-order uncertainty propagation (Σ_u = Φ Σ_a Φ^T) that is unavailable in learned-latent settings, and the paper positions itself against both pixel-space diffusion (computationally prohibitive) and deterministic super-resolution (no uncertainty) as prior approaches to scientific downscaling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial for scientific ML practitioners: PODiff achieves comparable reconstruction accuracy to pixel-space diffusion with ~13× less training time, ~9× lower peak GPU memory, and ~15× faster per-sample inference, while providing spatially structured and interpretable uncertainty. The main limitation is the requirement for low-rank linear structure in the target field, which restricts applicability to highly turbulent, discontinuous, or strongly nonlinear systems; the fixed POD basis also cannot adapt to distributional shift without recomputation. Open questions include extension to multivariate fields with heterogeneous units and scales, adaptive or online POD bases, and whether the variance-ordered structure generalizes to fields where the first few modes do not dominate as strongly.
