## Problem

MLLMs suffer severe performance degradation on real-world degraded document images (noise, blur, low resolution), yet progress is blocked by two gaps: the absence of large-scale aligned clean/corrupted training data for robustness learning, and the lack of restoration methods that transfer across heterogeneous MLLM architectures without retraining the backbone. Existing pixel-level restoration approaches are computationally expensive and incompatible with the feature-level processing pipeline of modern MLLMs.

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-quality document image (degraded by up to k sequentially applied corruptions drawn from five categories: luminance, distortion, blurriness, noise, and compression) and a text question as input. Rather than producing a restored pixel image, Uni-DocRobust outputs a residual feature correction in the visual encoder's native dimension, which is added to the original encoder output before the projector. The final MLLM then generates a text answer as usual, so the interface to downstream tasks is unchanged.

## Method

Uni-DocRobust decouples restoration from any specific visual encoder by operating in a model-agnostic 1024-dimensional canonical feature space. A 6-layer Transformer-based Universal Restoration Core is pre-trained via multi-teacher distillation from K frozen heterogeneous encoders (CLIP, SigLIP, InternViT), minimizing a reconstruction loss plus a cross-model consistency loss that aligns globally pooled features across teachers. Lightweight Feature Adapters (Adapter-In/Adapter-Out) bridge encoder-specific dimensions to the canonical space; in Phase II they are tuned with a Scale-Aware Alignment objective that combines normalized MSE with a log-standard-deviation matching term to prevent feature scale collapse in the frozen LLM. Deployment is non-intrusive: the restoration wrapper is injected via a hook between the final visual block and the projector, leaving all host MLLM parameters frozen and requiring only ~0.3 GPU-hours per new architecture, enabling a 'pre-train once, deploy everywhere' paradigm.

## Results

On corrupted DocRobust-VQA subsets, Uni-DocRobust (plug-and-play, no MLLM fine-tuning) improves InternVL3.5-2B from 35.71 to 59.45 on ChartQA, 58.11 to 61.87 on TextVQA, 73.54 to 77.92 on DocVQA, and 38.92 to 42.53 on InfographicVQA. Combining Uni-DocRobust with LoRA fine-tuning further lifts ChartQA to 61.55 and WildDoc to 40.12. On the real-world WildDoc benchmark, the combined approach achieves 40.12 versus 32.41 for the unmodified model. The method also scales to larger models, raising InternVL3.5-14B ChartQA from 50.32 to 64.79.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two pixel-level restoration baselines on InternVL3.5-2B (Table 4), Uni-DocRobust achieves 59.45/61.87/77.92/42.53 on ChartQA/TextVQA/DocVQA/InfographicVQA, substantially outperforming DocRes (28.76/58.11/70.85/37.09) by 30.7/3.8/7.1/5.4 points and DiffBIR (9.96/28.08/14.24/17.67) by 49.5/33.8/63.7/24.9 points. Even DocRes fine-tuned on DocRobust-VQA (avg 53.32, 45 GPU-hours) falls well short of Uni-DocRobust (avg 62.72, 0.3 GPU-hours for Phase II adaptation). Uni-DocRobust also surpasses standard LoRA fine-tuning (avg 62.03, 15 GPU-hours per architecture) in both score and deployment cost, requiring only 0.3 GPU-hours to adapt to a new MLLM versus 15 GPU-hours for LoRA. The feature-level approach thus dominates both pixel-level restoration and direct fine-tuning baselines in accuracy and efficiency simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant pixel-level document restoration paradigm (DocRes, DiffBIR, DeGAN) by operating in a model-agnostic canonical feature space rather than reconstructing raw pixels, and from robustness-oriented training strategies (masked pretraining, contrastive learning) that require modifying the MLLM's pretraining objective. Conceptually, it repurposes multi-teacher knowledge distillation—previously applied in NLP and single-encoder vision tasks—to learn architecture-agnostic restoration primitives, combined with lightweight feature adapters analogous to LoRA but targeting the visual encoder–projector interface. The DocRobust-VQA dataset fills a gap identified in the related work: while evaluation benchmarks (R-bench, WildDoc) exist, no large-scale aligned clean/corrupted training corpus was available for MLLM robustness fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'pre-train once, deploy everywhere' paradigm with a 50× reduction in per-model adaptation cost (0.3 vs. 15 GPU hours) is practically significant for organizations maintaining multiple MLLM backbones, and the 189K-pair dataset removes a concrete bottleneck for the community. However, the framework is validated on only three open-source MLLMs (2B–7B scale) and a single document domain; generalization to non-document images, larger models beyond 14B, or fundamentally different visual encoders (e.g., convolutional) remains untested. The adversarial robustness finding, while intriguing, rests on a single white-box attack against one model and lacks ablation to isolate whether it stems from the restoration core or the adapter tuning. Open questions include whether the canonical feature space can be further compressed, whether the approach extends to video/document sequences, and how it interacts with emerging test-time scaling strategies in MLLMs.
