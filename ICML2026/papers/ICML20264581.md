## Problem

Under long-tailed supervision, tail classes are systematically more prone to shortcut learning: scarce and context-homogeneous examples make spurious cues (background, texture) appear reliably predictive, causing models to attend to non-semantic regions rather than object-centric evidence. Existing long-tailed methods address imbalance through re-weighting, augmentation, or feature transfer but rarely intervene at the spatial-evidence level where shortcut reliance actually manifests.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-LT, 224×224 for ImageNet-LT/iNaturalist/Places-LT) and outputs class labels, but its core mechanism operates on Class Activation Maps derived from convolutional feature maps and classifier weights. SRCD is a plug-and-play training-time regularizer that requires no architectural modification and can be layered on top of any long-tailed loss (e.g., LDAM, BCL, GCL) with both CNN (ResNet-32/50) and ViT (ViT-B/16 via CLIP) backbones.

## Method

SRCD constructs a shortcut-resistant teacher CAM for each tail example by selecting top-K head-class proxies, scoring each candidate's spatial coherence (Laplacian graph energy) and evidence concentration (normalized Hoyer sparsity), and aggregating them via softmax energy-weighting. The teacher is then distilled into the tail-class CAM through an L2 loss weighted by a class-rarity coefficient γ_y = n_thres/n_y, applied only in the late training stage. Theoretically, the paper shows the energy-weighted teacher is an entropy-regularized utility maximizer and that L2 CAM distillation induces a closed-form exponential contraction of shortcut-region attention mass, guaranteeing monotone reduction of background reliance when the teacher is more object-centric than the initial tail CAM.

## Results

SRCD is a plug-and-play module that consistently improves strong long-tailed baselines across five benchmarks. On CIFAR-100-LT (IR=100), adding SRCD yields the largest gains for CE+FCC (+2.18 pp, 40.20→42.38%) and CE+H2T (+2.26 pp, 42.27→44.53%), while on ImageNet-LT the tail-split improvement reaches +3.81 pp (GLMC: 30.41→34.22%). Under the foundation-model fine-tuning setting, Decoder+SRCD improves iNaturalist overall accuracy from 59.2% to 61.3% (+2.1 pp) and PECEL+SRCD lifts ImageNet-LT overall from 78.3% to 78.8% while gaining +1.6 pp on the Tail split.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SRCD is evaluated as an additive module on top of seven training-from-scratch baselines and three foundation-model fine-tuning baselines. The most consistent and largest gains appear with CE+FCC (+2.18 pp on CIFAR-100-LT IR=100) and CE+H2T (+2.26 pp), both of which are weaker baselines that benefit most from the additional spatial regularization. Among stronger baselines, Focal-SAM and BCL show steady improvements of +1.3 to +1.9 pp on CIFAR-100-LT, while GLMC gains +1.35 to +1.56 pp. A few minor drops occur (e.g., BCL −0.64 on CIFAR-10-LT IR=100, GCL −1.56 on CIFAR-100-LT IR=10), but the overall trend is positive across 42 of 48 reported cells. In the fine-tuning setting, Decoder+SRCD achieves the largest absolute gain (+2.1 pp overall on iNaturalist, +3.6 pp on Head), PECEL+SRCD improves ImageNet-LT Tail by +1.6 pp, and LIFT+SRCD gains +1.3 pp overall on ImageNet-LT though it slightly drops on iNaturalist Tail (−0.7 pp). The paper positions SRCD as complementary rather than competitive: it does not replace any baseline's loss or architecture but adds a CAM-space distillation term that is most beneficial for tail and medium splits.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the CAM framework (Zhou et al., 2016) and repurposes it as a diagnostic and distillation tool for long-tailed recognition, a task previously addressed through feature-space transfer (M2m, RSG, FASA, H2T), logit re-balancing (LDAM, GCL), or contrastive learning (BCL, GLMC). The key conceptual departure is intervening at the spatial-evidence level rather than the feature or logit level: prior head-to-tail methods propagate statistics, prototypes, or synthetic samples, whereas SRCD distills object-centric attention patterns from head-class CAMs to tail-class CAMs. The paper also bridges two previously separate literatures—shortcut learning (Geirhos et al., 2020; Hermann et al., 2024), typically studied under balanced supervision, and long-tailed recognition—by showing that data scarcity amplifies shortcut reliance in a systematic head-tail gradient.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SRCD offers a lightweight, architecture-agnostic regularizer that can be plugged into any existing long-tailed pipeline, and the consistent (if modest) gains across five benchmarks and both scratch-training and foundation-model fine-tuning settings suggest it addresses a real failure mode. The theoretical analysis—showing L2 CAM distillation induces exponential contraction of shortcut-region mass—gives practitioners a clear mechanistic picture of why the method works. Open questions include whether the energy-based weighting (coherence + concentration) generalizes to fine-grained or multi-object scenes where object evidence is naturally fragmented, and whether the late-stage-only training schedule limits applicability in one-shot or few-epoch regimes. The reliance on CAM quality as a proxy for shortcut reliance also means the method inherits CAM limitations (spatial coarseness, architecture sensitivity).
