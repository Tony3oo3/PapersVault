## Problem

Current LLM alignment pipelines optimize for helpfulness, safety, and risk mitigation, which systematically suppress affective intensity, perspective specificity, and relational engagement in outputs. Despite strong benchmark performance, models exhibit consistent empathic distortions—flattening urgency, miscalibrating detail, deflecting tension, and depersonalizing language—that remain invisible under standard evaluation metrics. The paper argues empathy should be elevated from an incidental stylistic byproduct to a first-class, measurable alignment objective.

## Input / Output

The evaluation spans three high-stakes tasks: (1) news generation, where a gold summary conditions a full-length article; (2) literary translation, where Chinese fiction is rendered into English preserving register and cultural connotation; (3) psychological counseling, where a client narrative elicits a therapist-style response. Beyond standard quality metrics (ROUGE, CIDEr, Sem-Sim, chrF, COMET, EPITOME), the paper computes four mechanism-specific empathy scores: Sentiment Attenuation via an affect-intensity scorer E(·)∈[0,1], Empathic Granularity Mismatch via a discomfort counter normalized by sentence count, Conflict Avoidance via a binary discriminator J(·), and Linguistic Distancing via L2 distance between standardized distancing-density vectors (nominalization, impersonal framing, agentless constructions).

## Method

The paper formalizes empathy as an observable behavioral property—preserving intention, affect, and contextual requirements in generated outputs—and decomposes it into four quantifiable failure mechanisms (SA, EGM, CA, LD) organized along three dimensions (cognitive, cultural, relational empathy). It then empirically evaluates ten frontier LLMs (GPT-5.2, Claude-Sonnet-4.5, DeepSeek-V3.2, Gemini-3, Llama-4, Qwen3-VL, and others) across the three tasks, showing that high conventional scores coexist with systematic empathic distortions (e.g., GPT-5.2 leads in Sem-Sim yet exhibits the worst EGM; three models reach CA=1.0 in counseling). Robustness checks with three different LLM judges and four dataset sizes confirm the findings are not artifacts of a single evaluator or small sample. The paper concludes by proposing three concrete directions: empathy-aware multi-objective alignment that penalizes mechanism-level failures without degrading task performance, benchmarks concentrated on empathy-critical cases, and targeted training data providing supervision for perspective preservation, affective calibration, and constructive engagement with tension.

## Results

On the Newsroom dataset (cognitive empathy), frontier LLMs achieve strong standard metrics—Sem-Sim ranges from 0.832 (Qwen3-VL) to 0.898 (GPT-5.2)—yet exhibit substantial empathic distortions: Sentiment Attenuation (SA) spans 0.079 (DeepSeek-V3.2) to 0.290 (Llama-4), and Empathic Granularity Mismatch (EGM) ranges from 0.179 (Llama-4) to 0.252 (GPT-5.2). On WMT 2023 literary translation (cultural empathy), SA remains uniformly high (0.406–0.459) and Linguistic Distancing (LD) varies widely (1.177–1.466) despite competitive COMET scores (0.790–0.828). On the counseling task (relational empathy), Conflict Avoidance (CA) reaches 1.000 for Gemini-3, Grok-4.1, and Qwen3-VL, while SA stays elevated (0.337–0.530) across all models, revealing systematic harmony-seeking that standard EPITOME scores fail to capture.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates ten frontier LLMs (GPT-5.2, Claude-Sonnet-4.5, DeepSeek-V3.2, Gemini-3, Grok-4.1, GLM-4.7, Kimi-K2, Llama-4, Qwen3-VL, Doubao-Seed1.8) as baselines, finding that no model simultaneously excels on both standard and empathy metrics. GPT-5.2 leads standard metrics (Sem-Sim 0.898, CIDEr 0.238) but exhibits the worst EGM (0.252) and non-trivial SA (0.158), while Llama-4 shows the highest SA (0.290) yet the best EGM (0.179), revealing a trade-off between affective intensity preservation and descriptive detail calibration. DeepSeek-V3.2 achieves the lowest SA (0.079) on news generation but still shows substantial cultural empathy degradation on translation (SA 0.457, LD 1.361) despite a joint-highest COMET score (0.828). On the counseling task, Gemini-3, Grok-4.1, and Qwen3-VL all reach CA = 1.000 (complete conflict avoidance), while Llama-4 achieves the lowest CA (0.667) but at the cost of the lowest Exploration score (0.067). Robustness checks with three different LLM judges (GPT-5.2, Claude-Sonnet-4.5, Gemini-3-Pro) confirm that relative rankings are stable (Spearman correlations 0.748–0.948), and expanding the counseling dataset from 50 to 200 instances yields consistent SA values, ruling out judge-specific or sample-size artifacts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This position paper carves new ground by formalizing empathy in LLMs as an observable behavioral property decomposed into four measurable failure mechanisms (sentiment attenuation, empathic granularity mismatch, conflict avoidance, linguistic distancing) and three dimensions (cognitive, cultural, relational). It departs from prior empathy-evaluation work (Sorin et al. 2024; Chen et al. 2024; Lee et al. 2024b) that typically scores empathy holistically, by introducing mechanism-level metrics that can be plugged into existing alignment pipelines. Conceptually, it repurposes psychology's empathy taxonomy and NLP evaluation infrastructure (ROUGE, COMET, EPITOME) to argue that prevailing RLHF/DPO objectives systematically produce empathic distortion as a structural side-effect rather than an incidental style issue.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central claim—that frontier models scoring 0.83–0.90 on semantic similarity simultaneously exhibit SA of 0.08–0.53 and CA reaching 1.0—exposes a blind spot in current alignment evaluation that directly affects high-stakes deployments in journalism, mental health, and cross-cultural communication. However, the empirical evidence is preliminary: 50-instance samples, LLM-as-judge for the CA discriminator, and novel metrics without human-validated ground truth limit generalizability. Open questions include whether empathy-aware multi-objective alignment can be achieved without degrading safety or helpfulness, whether the four mechanisms generalize beyond the three tested domains, and whether large-scale human studies would confirm the LLM-judged rankings.
