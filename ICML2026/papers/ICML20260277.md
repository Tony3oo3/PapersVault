## Problem

In RLVR post-training (e.g., GRPO), as the model improves, an increasing fraction of prompts yield all-verified-success rollouts, causing the group reward standard deviation to collapse to zero and all group-relative advantages to degenerate to zero. This vanishing policy gradient stalls optimization, yet the discarded rollouts still contain qualitatively different and high-value reasoning paths. Prior remedies either filter out these prompts (wasting compute) or import external expert trajectories (introducing heavy model dependence), leaving the usable learning signals latent in self-generated rollouts unexploited.

## Input / Output

Given a text prompt, the method samples a group of N rollouts from the current policy, verifies each against a ground-truth answer, and computes step-level entropy by averaging token-level predictive entropy within newline-delimited reasoning steps. From the verified-success subset, it selects the single highest-entropy step across all rollouts and extracts the trajectory prefix ending at that step as the EchoClip. The output is a single unified gradient update that combines the standard RLVR policy-gradient loss with an auxiliary negative-log-likelihood term on the EchoClip, producing an updated policy model without any additional forward or backward pass.

## Method

EchoRL is a plug-and-play two-phase module built on the empirical finding that high step-level entropy reliably marks the most critical reasoning decision points (validated by an ablation showing that removing high-entropy steps causes disproportionate accuracy degradation). In the EchoClip Mining phase, it identifies the argmax-entropy step across all verified-success rollouts for a prompt and takes the rollout prefix ending at that step. In the EchoRL Update phase, it applies a weighted auxiliary loss (λ=0.001) as a negative log-likelihood on this EchoClip, added to the standard RLVR objective (GRPO, DAPO, LUFFY, or UFT). Because the EchoClip logits are a subset of those already computed in the main forward pass, the auxiliary term is merged into a single unified backward pass, introducing negligible overhead. This design guarantees a non-vanishing gradient signal even when all group-relative advantages degenerate to zero, sustaining continuous learning without external supervision or rejection sampling.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-Math-7B, EchoRL consistently improves all four integrated RLVR baselines: GRPO+EchoRL reaches 47.0 ID / 58.4 OOD (vs. 44.5 / 55.6 for GRPO), DAPO+EchoRL reaches 49.9 / 60.9 (vs. 47.9 / 57.6), LUFFY+EchoRL reaches 51.9 / 61.0 (vs. 50.1 / 57.8), and UFT+EchoRL reaches 47.6 / 59.9 (vs. 45.7 / 56.9). The largest single-benchmark gain is +5.9 on AIME 2025 (GRPO 16.4 → GRPO+EchoRL 22.3). Across five LLM backbones (1.5B–8B) and a multimodal model (Qwen2.5-VL on Geometry3K), EchoRL delivers consistent improvements with negligible computational overhead (unified backward pass, no extra memory).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EchoRL is integrated as a plug-and-play module into four RLVR frameworks and consistently outperforms each unmodified baseline. Against GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), EchoRL adds +2.5 ID and +2.8 OOD points (47.0/58.4 vs. 44.5/55.6), with the largest per-benchmark gain on AIME 2025 (+5.9). Against DAPO (Yu et al., 2026), EchoRL adds +2.0 ID and +3.3 OOD (49.9/60.9 vs. 47.9/57.6), with notable gains on AIME 24 (+3.5) and MMLU-Pro (+2.9). Against LUFFY (Yan et al., 2026a), EchoRL adds +1.8 ID and +3.2 OOD (51.9/61.0 vs. 50.1/57.8), and against UFT (Liu et al., 2026), +1.9 ID and +3.0 OOD (47.6/59.9 vs. 45.7/56.9). All EchoRL-augmented methods also substantially outperform the zero-RL baselines PRIME-Zero (40.7/41.4), SimpleRL-Zero (37.4/29.3), and OpenReasoner-Zero (41.0/51.6) on both ID and OOD averages. Statistical significance is confirmed via 95% confidence intervals over three independent runs (Table 3), with non-overlapping bands for all EchoRL vs. baseline comparisons.

## Relation to Prior Work

EchoRL builds directly on the GRPO framework (Shao et al., 2024) and its group-relative advantage computation, targeting the advantage-degeneration pathology that emerges as models improve. Prior remedies fall into two camps: filtering-based strategies (DAPO, Reinforce-Rej, Reinforce-Ada) that discard degenerated prompts at the cost of data efficiency, and expert-supervision methods (LUFFY, UFT, SRFT, RelIFT) that import external golden trajectories. EchoRL departs from both by mining a high-entropy prefix (EchoClip) from the model's own verified-success rollouts and echoing it back as an auxiliary negative-log-likelihood term, drawing on the entropy-as-uncertainty-proxy literature (Wang et al., 2026a; Bi et al., 2025b) for its selection criterion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Advantage degeneration is a structural bottleneck in RLVR post-training that worsens monotonically as models get stronger, silently wasting compute on prompts whose policy-gradient vanishes. EchoRL's plug-and-play, near-zero-overhead design makes it immediately adoptable across existing RLVR pipelines without requiring external expert models or additional rollouts. Open questions include whether step-level entropy remains a reliable proxy for 'valuable reasoning' in non-mathematical or multimodal domains, how the method interacts with longer reasoning horizons where the highest-entropy step may be less informative, and whether the auxiliary SFT-like signal on a single prefix could inadvertently narrow the policy's exploration over extended training.
