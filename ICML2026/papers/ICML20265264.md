## Problem

Existing fingerprinting and watermarking methods for pre-trained encoders either rely on pre-defined output labels that are destroyed by downstream fine-tuning, or require access to internal embeddings/logits unavailable in black-box APIs. No downstream-agnostic, label-only ownership verification mechanism exists for high-value pre-trained encoders that have been stolen and adapted to arbitrary unknown downstream tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265264/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The defender provides white-box access to the victim encoder and an auxiliary dataset (5,000 images from CIFAR-10, resized to 3×224×224) to generate L=10 groups of N=10 perturbed fingerprint samples each (total query budget: 100), with perturbations bounded by an ℓ₂-norm of 16/255. Verification queries only the suspect model's black-box API for final predicted labels (no logits, no embeddings), and outputs a binary ownership decision based on whether the aggregate Matching Rate across all groups exceeds a verification threshold.

## Method

The method exploits the observation that adversarial perturbations accumulate across network layers, amplifying toward the encoder output, so samples shifted into a dense feature-space cluster will receive consistent predictions from any downstream head. Generation proceeds in three steps: (1) spectral clustering of auxiliary features to identify dense, stable semantic regions; (2) sampling M=10 anchors from a selected dense cluster; (3) PGD-based adversarial shifting that minimizes the ℓ₂ distance between each base sample's encoder output and the anchors, forming a tight 'feature island.' Verification uses a group voting mechanism: all N members of a group are queried, the majority label is identified, and the voting ratio (fraction of members predicting that label) is compared against a threshold (0.5 for STL-10/GTSRB, 0.3 for CIFAR-100). Ownership is confirmed when the fraction of groups exceeding the threshold (Matching Rate) surpasses a global verification threshold, requiring zero knowledge of the downstream task, head architecture, or label semantics.

## Results

Under the standard FTLL setting (only the classification head is retrained), the proposed method achieves Matching Rates of 0.92–1.00 across all pre-training paradigms (SimCLR, MoCoV2, SigLIP, Supervised) and downstream tasks (STL-10, GTSRB, CIFAR-100, SNLI, MRPC), while preserving Clean Data Accuracy (CDA) identical to the unmodified encoder. Under the more challenging FTAL setting (full fine-tuning), the method sustains Matching Rates of 0.64–0.84, whereas all baselines drop below 0.50 in most cases. The method requires zero additional knowledge (label-only, downstream-agnostic) and completes the full generation-to-verification pipeline in ~17 minutes on CIFAR-100, compared to up to 681 minutes for SSL-WM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method decisively outperforms all four baselines on Matching Rate under FTLL: e.g., on SimCLR-STL-10 it achieves 0.960 vs. SSL-WM (0.357), ADV-TRA (0.323), and MFUE (0.426). Under the harder FTAL setting, the gap narrows but the method still leads: 0.760 on SimCLR-STL-10 vs. SSL-WM (0.298), ADV-TRA (0.287), and MFUE (0.392); MEA is the closest competitor at 0.751 but requires direct access to encoder embeddings, violating the label-only constraint. On NLP tasks (SNLI, MRPC), only SSL-WM and MEA are applicable, and the proposed method achieves 0.860/0.700 and 0.820/0.640 respectively, far exceeding SSL-WM (0.132/0.101 and 0.207/0.155). A critical practical advantage is that the proposed method requires no additional knowledge (no logits, no embeddings, no downstream dataset), whereas SSL-WM needs output logits, MEA needs encoder embeddings, and ADV-TRA/MFUE need the downstream dataset to train surrogate heads. In terms of fidelity, the proposed method preserves CDA exactly (non-invasive), while SSL-WM and MEA degrade CDA by 0.3–2.1% on average.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two lines of work: model fingerprinting (IPGuard, ADV-TRA, MFUE) and pre-trained encoder watermarking (SSLGuard, SSL-WM, MEA). It departs from both by repurposing adversarial perturbation (PGD) not to cross decision boundaries but to aggregate samples into cohesive feature-space clusters, and by replacing label-matching verification with a group-voting mechanism that is agnostic to the downstream task's output semantics. The conceptual lineage draws on the observation that adversarial deviations amplify toward the encoder output (citing Liu et al., 2025), but the application to IP protection via unsupervised anchor-guided shifting and spectral clustering is novel. Unlike prior encoder watermarking methods that modify training objectives or require embedding/logit access, this is a fully post-hoc, non-invasive scheme requiring only label-only black-box queries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically urgent gap: as Encoder-as-a-Service becomes prevalent, there is no existing mechanism to verify encoder ownership when the downstream task, head, and label space are all unknown. The group-voting design elegantly decouples verification from task semantics, and the non-invasive nature preserves model utility (zero CDA degradation). Key limitations include the NLP modality (adversarial shifting operates in continuous embedding space without discrete token reconstruction), degradation under extreme modifications (>80% pruning, hard-label model extraction), and narrowing verification margins for very large output spaces (>100 classes). Open questions include whether the feature-space clustering insight generalizes to other modalities (audio, video) and whether the approach can be made fully discrete for NLP without the embedding-access assumption.
