## Problem

Existing cross-modal Test-Time Adaptation methods suffer from two critical limitations: they adapt directly on structurally corrupted text embeddings where even minor typos disrupt tokenization and shift semantics, and they discard Low-Confidence Queries as unreliable, wasting the informative negative certainty (i.e., the ability to reliably exclude irrelevant candidates) that ambiguous queries still contain.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265437/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a continuous stream of incoming queries (text or image) alongside a fixed gallery set, operating in a batch-wise online TTA protocol with batch size 64. Text queries are first passed through a lightweight LLM for structural rectification before encoding, while image queries are encoded directly. The output is a refined, normalized query embedding used to compute softmax-normalized cosine similarity scores against the gallery, yielding a retrieval ranking; only the Layer Normalization parameters of the query encoder are updated during adaptation.

## Method

ReEx integrates two synergistic modules into the online adaptation loop. The Generative Semantic Refinement (GSR) module uses a lightweight LLM (Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct) to correct typographical errors in text queries, then applies Confidence-Guided Dynamic Fusion to interpolate between the raw and rectified embeddings using an adaptive weight derived from each query's maximum retrieval confidence, preventing semantic drift when the LLM hallucinates. The Exclusion-Guided Proxy Contrastive Learning (EPCL) module partitions the gallery into a Candidate Set (top-K neighbors) and an Exclusion Set (bottom-K from a shared batch-level pool) for every query, constructs a Soft Positive Proxy that smoothly transitions between a precise target (high-confidence) and a broad semantic region (low-confidence), and mines additional negatives from inter-query semantic contradictions. The unified InfoNCE objective pulls each query toward its adaptive proxy while pushing it away from all exclusion-based negatives, enabling robust adaptation from the full query distribution rather than only high-confidence samples.

## Results

Under Query Shift on Flickr-C, ReEx achieves 74.8% Recall@1 for I2T and 65.7% for T2I, surpassing the strongest baseline TCR by +2.4% and +2.3% respectively. On COCO-C, ReEx reaches 60.1% (I2T) and 42.8% (T2I), improving over TCR by +1.6% and +1.1%. Under the more challenging Query-Gallery Shift setting, ReEx attains 66.8% average Recall@1 across Flickr, COCO, Fashion-Gen, and Nocaps, outperforming TCR by +0.4%. On the BLIP-2 backbone, ReEx further improves to 67.1% average Recall@1, a +1.5% gain over TCR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReEx consistently outperforms all seven baselines across both retrieval directions and both corruption benchmarks. The closest competitor, TCR (ICLR 2025), is surpassed by +2.4% on Flickr-C I2T (74.8 vs. 72.4) and +2.3% on Flickr-C T2I (65.7 vs. 63.4), with the gap widening under character-level text corruptions (e.g., +12.3% on CI for T2I). SHOT is the second-strongest baseline on I2T (72.0% on Flickr-C) but trails ReEx by 2.8% and is notably weaker on T2I (63.4% vs. 65.7%). Entropy-based methods (TENT, EATA) and reliability-bias methods (READ, DeYO) cluster near the no-adaptation baseline on T2I (62.1–63.4%), indicating they struggle to exploit low-confidence queries. On the BLIP-2 backbone, ReEx extends its lead to +1.5% over TCR (67.1 vs. 65.6), confirming the framework generalizes beyond the BLIP architecture. Under Query-Gallery Shift, the margin over TCR narrows to +0.4% (66.8 vs. 66.4), which the authors attribute to the increased difficulty of cross-domain feature misalignment when both query and gallery distributions shift simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends TCR (Li et al., 2025), the most direct prior work on test-time adaptation for cross-modal retrieval under query shift, by addressing two gaps it identifies: (1) TCR operates on raw, potentially structurally corrupted text embeddings, and (2) TCR discards low-confidence queries as unreliable. ReEx introduces an LLM-based Generative Semantic Refinement module (with confidence-guided fusion to prevent semantic drift) to rectify textual noise before adaptation, and an Exclusion-Guided Proxy Contrastive Learning objective that converts the predictive ambiguity of low-confidence queries into informative negative constraints. The broader TTA lineage (TENT, SHOT, EATA, SAR) informs the online adaptation protocol, while the contrastive learning component builds on InfoNCE-style objectives adapted for retrieval rather than classification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and underexplored failure mode of VLP retrieval systems: discrete token-level perturbations (typos, OCR errors) that cause disproportionate embedding shifts. The finding that unguided LLM corrections can be *worse* than the original noise (ablation: 63.2% vs. 63.3% baseline) is a useful caution for anyone considering generative pre-processing in retrieval pipelines. Key limitations include the 0.5s per-batch LLM latency (mitigated in practice by confidence-gated routing), reduced effectiveness under Query-Gallery Shift where both sides of the retrieval pair shift, and the current restriction of GSR to textual queries only. Open questions include whether lightweight visual refinement can complement GSR, and whether the exclusion-set formulation generalizes to open-vocabulary or streaming gallery settings.
