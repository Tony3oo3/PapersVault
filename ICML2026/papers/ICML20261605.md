## Problem

Hypergraph Active Learning (HAL) must select a small set of informative nodes to label, but existing pipelines either adapt graph-based querying through clique expansion—which introduces structural bias and causes ranking collapse where node utilities become dominated by hyperedge cardinality—or rely on model-dependent iterative retraining that is computationally prohibitive at scale. The paper addresses the need for a training-free framework that identifies informative nodes while preserving semantically meaningful high-order context rather than flattening it into uniform pairwise edges.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a hypergraph (incidence matrix H ∈ {0,1}^{n×m}, hyperedge weight matrix W), a node feature matrix X ∈ R^{n×F}, and a labeling budget B, and produces an ordered set of B nodes to label. Crucially, the selection phase is entirely training-free: it requires no downstream HNN training, gradient back-propagation, or iterative model updates. The selected set is then handed to a standard 2-layer decoupled HGNN (hidden dim 64) for supervised node classification, with test accuracy reported over 10 random seeds. The method operates directly on the sparse hypergraph incidence structure, avoiding dense clique-expanded adjacency matrices.

## Method

HIAL formulates active node selection as influence maximization over a High-Order Interaction-aware Propagation (HOIP) mechanism that constructs a weighted pairwise projection where each node-to-node interaction is calibrated by two factors: inverse-cardinality normalization 1/(d(e)−1) to suppress degree inflation from large hyperedges, and a semantic gate exp(−σ²_e) that attenuates feature-inconsistent hyperedges. This yields a parameter-free linear diffusion operator A_k whose entries directly give normalized node-to-node influence scores without any model training. The selection objective combines two submodular terms—Magnitude of Feature Sensitivity (MFS), a set-cover function over cosine-distance balls in the propagated feature space that penalizes semantic redundancy, and Expected Structure Reachability (ESR), a noisy-or activation model over hyperedge co-membership neighborhoods that captures local diffusion capacity—into a unified monotone submodular function F(S) = γ·MFS(S)/Ψ_MFS + (1−γ)·ESR(S)/Ψ_ESR. A Lazy Greedy solver then maximizes F(S) with a (1−1/e) approximation guarantee, enabling scalable selection on hypergraphs with over 240K nodes in approximately 2.8 hours.

## Results

HIAL achieves state-of-the-art node classification accuracy across all eight hypergraph benchmarks, with the largest gains on News20 (87.66%, +5.26% over GRAIN) and Citeseer (61.55%, +4.01% over RIM). On the large-scale Recipe200K dataset (240K nodes), HIAL is the only method to complete without OOM or OOT errors, reaching 60.21% accuracy versus 57.86% for the best completing baseline (GRAIN). HIAL also demonstrates superior label efficiency: reaching 55% accuracy on Citeseer requires only 48 labeled nodes versus 108 for RIM, a >50% reduction in annotation budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HIAL outperforms all eight baselines on every dataset where a comparison is possible. Against the strongest influence-based baselines, HIAL leads GRAIN by +1.07% on Cora, +4.74% on Citeseer, +9.49% on DBLP, +7.17% on News20, and +2.35% on Recipe200K; it leads RIM by +4.01% on Citeseer and +1.79% on Mushroom. Against the strongest hypergraph-specific baselines, HIAL surpasses AHGA by +1.72% on DBLP and +0.93% on PubMed, and outperforms ACGNN by +5.16% on Citeseer and +6.41% on News20. A critical practical advantage is scalability: on Recipe200K (240K nodes), AHGA fails with OOM while RIM and AGE exceed the 24-hour time limit, leaving HIAL as the only method that completes and still achieves the highest accuracy (60.21%). The paper also reports that HIAL achieves up to 5.7× speedup in active-selection runtime over learning-based methods like AHGA and ACGNN, attributable to its training-free design that avoids iterative HNN retraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

HIAL extends the social influence maximization (SIM) framework of Kempe et al. and its hypergraph generalization (Xie et al., 2022) into a training-free active learning proxy, departing from both model-dependent HAL methods (ACGNN, CGE-AL, AHGA) that require iterative HNN retraining and graph-based influence methods (GRAIN, RIM, DOCTOR) that rely on uniform clique expansion. The key conceptual departure is the identification of 'ranking collapse'—a structural bias where CE-induced degree inflation makes influence scores nearly deterministic functions of hyperedge cardinality—and the design of a High-Order Interaction-aware Propagation (HOIP) mechanism that calibrates pairwise weights via inverse-cardinality normalization and within-hyperedge feature consistency to decouple influence from raw connectivity. The submodular selection objective combining feature-space coverage (MFS) and structural reachability (ESR) draws on set-cover and noisy-or activation models from the submodular optimization literature, unified under a single lazy-greedy solver.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free design and lazy-greedy optimization give HIAL a clear practical advantage: it scales to 240K-node hypergraphs where model-dependent baselines hit OOM or OOT, and it reduces annotation budgets by over 50% on some datasets to reach target accuracy. The ranking-collapse diagnosis (quantified via the Structural Bias Coefficient) provides a useful lens for evaluating any CE-based hypergraph method. However, the method is explicitly scoped to homophilic hypergraphs—the semantic attenuation term exp(−σ²ₑ) would suppress informative heterophilic connections—and the accuracy gains over the strongest baselines, while consistent, are moderate (1–5%). Open questions include whether the interaction intensity can be made adaptive (e.g., via learnable attention or signed interactions) to handle heterophilic and dynamic hypergraphs, and whether the homophily assumption holds broadly enough in real-world deployment settings.
