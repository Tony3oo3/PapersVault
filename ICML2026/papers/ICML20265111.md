## Problem

Domain-specific fine-tuning of LLMs causes severe, long-tailed catastrophic forgetting of prior knowledge, yet standard rehearsal-based mitigation is infeasible when pre-training data are inaccessible. Existing anti-forgetting methods remain largely reactive—applying global regularization or generic replay after forgetting has already occurred—rather than proactively identifying where the prior is most vulnerable, so limited retention effort is diluted across the full parameter space instead of concentrated on the small subset of behaviors that actually degrade.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a frozen reference model (initialized from the same pretrained checkpoint), the current model parameters being fine-tuned, and target-domain SFT data (instruction-response pairs from SciKnowEval chemistry L3 or FinGPT finance sentiment), with the pre-training corpus explicitly unavailable. It produces a fine-tuned model with reduced general-capability regression, along with a small set of latent prompt embeddings (ℓ×d continuous vectors constrained to the convex hull of the vocabulary embedding matrix) that serve as compact carriers of the most vulnerable prior modes. The repair loss is evaluated only at a single next-token position under each discovered embedding, keeping the distillation signal lightweight and decoupled from target-domain gradient flow.

## Method

ALER follows a two-stage find-before-fix protocol. In the adversarial search phase, a batch of randomly initialized latent prompt embeddings is optimized via gradient ascent to maximize the forward KL divergence between the frozen reference and the current model, localizing the high-risk forgetting modes; two stabilization tricks—batched optimization for semantic coverage and a manifold constraint (convex mixtures of vocabulary embeddings with an entropy regularizer)—prevent mode collapse and off-manifold drift. In the distillation repair phase, a mode-seeking reverse-KL objective distills the frozen reference back into the student conditioned on the discovered embeddings, selectively repairing the fragile tail without over-covering the reference support and diluting newly acquired domain-specific probability mass. A theoretical proposition (Prop. 4.3) proves that under a fixed repair budget, this targeted repair strictly dominates both budget-matched random repair and on-batch-only repair whenever forgetting is concentrated and the fine-tuning distribution is misaligned with the pre-training distribution. The overall training objective combines the standard SFT loss with the repair term weighted by λ, evaluated at every fine-tuning step so that repair is continuously re-targeted as the model drifts.

## Results

On SciKnowEval (chemistry L3), ALER (Ours+RKL) achieves 81.67% target-domain accuracy (+40.00 over the 41.67% reference) while preserving the averaged general-benchmark score at 76.79% (+0.12 over the 76.67% reference), effectively eliminating catastrophic forgetting. On FinGPT, ALER reaches 88.33% target accuracy (+64.67) with only a 0.02% drop in general-benchmark average (76.65 vs. 76.67 reference). In contrast, standard SFT causes severe forgetting (−12.31% on SciKnowEval, −9.13% on FinGPT), and even SFT+KL still loses 4.30% and 2.32% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ALER (Ours+RKL) dominates the retention–adaptation frontier on both target datasets. On SciKnowEval, it achieves the highest target accuracy (81.67%) while keeping the general-benchmark average at 76.79% (+0.12 over reference), outperforming TALR (68.84%, −7.83), PSFT (66.52%, −10.15), SSR (70.35%, −6.32), DataMix (62.79%, −13.88), and SFT+KL (72.37%, −4.30) on general retention while also exceeding all of them on target accuracy. Freeze is the strongest retention baseline (75.99%, −0.68) but sacrifices 7.50 points of target accuracy relative to ALER; Orthogonal preserves general performance (77.05%, +0.38) but severely limits target adaptation (55.00% vs. 81.67%). On FinGPT, ALER again leads with 88.33% target accuracy and only a 0.02% general drop, beating TALR (67.78%, −8.89), PSFT (68.29%, −8.38), SSR (73.67%, −3.00), DataMix (63.01%, −13.66), and Freeze (74.35%, −2.32) on the combined frontier. The FKL and BD variants confirm that the mode-seeking reverse-KL repair is the most effective instantiation, with FKL achieving slightly better general retention (+0.25) but lower target accuracy, and BD (black-box decoded replay) showing the weakest overall balance (−1.06 general, 76.67 target).

## Relation to Prior Work

ALER departs from the dominant reactive paradigm in LLM continual learning—where methods apply global regularization (EWC, trust-region clipping), synthetic rehearsal (SSR), or parameter-space constraints (orthogonal subspaces, layer freezing) to blunt forgetting after it occurs—by introducing a proactive 'find-before-fix' protocol. Conceptually, it repurposes adversarial optimization (typically used for robustness) in the latent prompt-embedding space to localize where the current model diverges most from a frozen reference, then applies targeted reverse-KL distillation only on those discovered high-risk carriers. The learning-dynamics analysis builds on NTK-based first-order expansions (Ren & Sutherland, 2024) and the empirical long-tail forgetting structure documented by Toneva et al. (2018) and Jin & Ren (2024b), but converts these observations into an actionable optimization objective rather than a diagnostic. The theoretical guarantee (Proposition 4.3) formalizes why budget-concentrated repair on adversarially discovered prompts strictly dominates both random and on-batch repair under long-tail forgetting and distribution mismatch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ALER offers a practical, data-free plug-in module that addresses a real deployment constraint (inaccessible pre-training corpora) while achieving near-zero general-capability degradation alongside strong target-domain gains, positioning it as a viable retention layer for iterative domain post-training pipelines. However, several limitations temper its immediate impact: experiments are confined to a single 4B model (Qwen3-4B) and two target domains, the inner-loop adversarial search adds ~1.5× training overhead (and ~3.4× with black-box rollouts), and the method lacks capability-aware control over which skills to protect and how strongly. Open questions include whether the rugged latent-prompt landscape admits more principled optimization than gradient ascent, whether the approach extends to long-context or multi-turn continual settings, and whether skill-adaptive repair budgets can further sharpen the retention-adaptation frontier.
