## Problem

In decoder-free, encoder-only physics-from-video pipelines, the latent coordinate system is defined only up to an arbitrary reparameterization, so a good ODE fit in latent space provides no guarantee that recovered parameters are unique or match the true physical system. This paper asks: under what formal trajectory conditions does a video clip pin down a physically meaningful coordinate system and yield provably unique parameter recovery for second-order linear ODEs?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264833/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw video frames x_k ∈ R^{w×h×c} sampled at a known step Δt, with the absolute time origin unknown and immaterial for autonomous dynamics. A shared deterministic CNN encoder maps each frame independently to a scalar latent ẑ_k, and the pipeline outputs the continuous-time ODE coefficients (γ₁, γ₀) governing z'' + γ₁z' + γ₀z = 0. In the real-world pendulum benchmark, these parameters are converted to a string-length estimate via the small-angle relation γ₀ = g/L, enabling direct physical measurement from video.

## Method

The core pipeline is an encoder-only, decoder-free architecture: a shared per-frame CNN produces scalar latents, centered finite-difference stencils (second-order accurate in Δt, improving on LPFV's first-order Euler) compute ẑ' and ẑ'', and a residual loss enforces the homogeneous ODE directly in latent space. A variance-floor regularizer (penalizing empirical std below a target τ) replaces KL-to-N(0,1) to prevent latent collapse without imposing a fixed marginal distribution, which is critical for non-oscillatory regimes. The key theoretical contribution is a level-set slope coverage condition: if each state level u in an open interval is attained at least three times with pairwise distinct instantaneous velocities (within one trajectory or across three trajectories), the learned reparameterization f must be locally affine, forcing exact parameter identification. This yields a sharp regime-dependent data requirement: underdamped systems are identifiable from a single clip of length ≥ 2 periods, while critical/overdamped/undamped regimes require three diverse trajectories. Finite-sample bounds separate measurement noise, finite-difference bias (O(Δt²)), and design-matrix conditioning (ψ_min), with the variance floor guaranteeing ψ_min > 0.

## Results

On the real-video clean pendulum benchmark (5 clips per length), the proposed method achieves the lowest string-length RMSE across all three ground-truth lengths: 0.050 m (L*=0.45 m), 0.070 m (L*=0.90 m), and 0.101 m (L*=1.50 m). In synthetic experiments, parameter recovery is near-exact when the level-set slope coverage condition holds (e.g., underdamped: γ̂₀=4.0037±0.0003, γ̂₁=0.0800±0.0003 vs. ground truth 4.0016, 0.08). On the more challenging wheel-mounted phone pendulum, the estimated γ̂₀=8.998±0.009 is close to the physics-based ground truth of 8.26 despite substantial visual nuisance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all three baselines on every string length in the clean pendulum benchmark. Against LPFV (the closest architectural competitor, also encoder-only), OURS reduces RMSE from 0.061→0.050 m at L*=0.45 m, 0.247→0.070 m at L*=0.90 m, and 0.201→0.101 m at L*=1.50 m, with the largest relative gain at the middle length. PAIG appears competitive at L*=0.90 m (RMSE 0.116) but this is incidental: it predicts nearly the same length (~1.01 m) for all three pendulums, so its middle-length score reflects a lucky offset rather than reliable recovery. NIRPI is consistently the weakest baseline, with RMSE ranging from 0.435 to 0.934 m and high variance across clips. The authors attribute the advantage over LPFV to the use of centered finite differences (second-order accurate) versus LPFV's Euler/one-sided stencil, and to the variance-floor regularizer which avoids the distributional mismatch that causes KL regularization to fail in non-oscillatory regimes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on LPFV (Garcia et al., 2025), the closest prior decoder-free, encoder-only method for physics-from-video, sharing the same architectural skeleton (per-frame CNN encoder, latent ODE residual loss) but departing in two key technical choices: centered finite differences (second-order accurate) replace LPFV's one-sided Euler stencil, and a variance-floor regularizer replaces LPFV's KL-to-N(0,1) anti-collapse term. The central novelty is theoretical: LPFV and all prior reconstruction-driven methods (PAIG, NIRPI, autoencoder-decoder pipelines) lack any identifiability guarantee, leaving open whether recovered parameters are unique. This paper introduces the level-set slope coverage condition as a verifiable, trajectory-level certificate that pins down the latent reparameterization, providing the first structural identifiability analysis for the decoder-free setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The regime-dependent identifiability characterization—underdamped systems recoverable from a single clip, critical/overdamped/undamped requiring three diverse trajectories—gives practitioners a concrete data-collection protocol for video-based structural monitoring and damping estimation. The variance-floor regularizer's superiority over KL in non-oscillatory regimes is a practical, drop-in improvement for anyone using encoder-only physics pipelines. Key limitations are the restriction to 1D latents and homogeneous second-order LTI dynamics; extending coverage-based certificates to multi-dimensional coupled systems, forced (non-homogeneous) dynamics, and mild nonlinearity are the natural next steps that would broaden applicability to real multi-mode structural and biomechanical problems.
