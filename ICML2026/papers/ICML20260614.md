## Problem

Model Width Expansion (MWE) offers a practical alternative to training LLMs from scratch by growing a larger model from a pre-trained seed, but the paper identifies a persistent 'Subspace Trap' where newly added parameters stagnate within a low-dimensional subspace aligned with the initialization, rendering the expanded capacity largely inert. The optimization dynamics of fully trainable, function-preserving width expansion in decoder-only Transformers had remained poorly understood, and standard element-wise optimizers like AdamW were shown to be inherently unable to break this confinement despite injected symmetry-breaking noise.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained seed model (SmolLM2-135M or Qwen3-0.6B) and applies a 2× function-preserving width expansion (symmetric or diagonal block structure with counter-balancing Gaussian noise) to produce a target architecture (SmolLM2-540M or Qwen3-2.38B), which is then continually pre-trained on the Nemotron-CC-v2 corpus (30B or 70B tokens respectively). The output is a fully trained width-expanded model whose expanded dimensions are verified to encode functional knowledge rather than redundant copies, assessed via downstream benchmarks and SVD pruning sensitivity.

## Method

The paper proves that function-preserving MWE inherits a p-dimensional 'River Valley' loss landscape from the seed model, confining the gradient matrix to a low-rank subspace spanned by the expansion operator's eigenvectors. Element-wise adaptive optimizers (AdamW, analyzed via its signSGD proxy) preserve this initialization-induced spectral bias, keeping updates aligned with the seed subspace throughout training. In contrast, Muon's Newton-Schulz iterations approximate the orthogonal polar factor of the gradient, producing near-isotropic updates that redistribute optimization energy into the null space of the expansion operator from the first step. The paper quantifies escape via an Orthogonal Energy Ratio (ρ⊥) tracking the fraction of update energy directed into symmetry-breaking directions, and verifies functional utilization through SVD pruning: Muon-trained models show significant performance degradation when tail singular values are removed, confirming that the newly activated dimensions encode critical knowledge rather than noise.

## Results

On Qwen3-2.38B with Symmetric Expansion, Muon achieves an average benchmark score of 47.88 versus 46.67 for AdamW (+1.21), and 47.15 vs 45.96 under Diagonal Expansion (+1.19). For SmolLM2-540M, Muon scores 34.65 vs 33.74 (Symmetric) and 34.36 vs 34.14 (Diagonal). In terms of training efficiency, Muon reduces training tokens by 32.7–44.6% in expansion settings (vs. only 13.6–23.0% in scratch training), roughly doubling the scratch-regime speedup. Block similarity drops from 0.880 (AdamW) to 0.788 (Muon), approaching the scratch-trained baseline of ~0.48.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the AdamW baseline (Loshchilov & Hutter, 2019), Muon consistently outperforms on average across both model families and both initialization strategies: +1.21 on Qwen3-2.38B Symmetric, +1.19 on Qwen3-2.38B Diagonal, +0.91 on SmolLM2-540M Symmetric, and +0.22 on SmolLM2-540M Diagonal. The gains are most pronounced on reasoning benchmarks (Big Bench Hard: +3.38 on Qwen3 Symmetric) and commonsense tasks (HellaSwag: +2.35, Winograde: +2.37 on Qwen3), while a few individual benchmarks show small negative deltas (e.g., AGIEval-en −0.44 on Qwen3 Diagonal). Compared to the unexpanded source seeds (SmolLM2-135M at 30.73 avg, Qwen3-0.6B at 41.72 avg), both expanded models improve substantially, but Muon converts more of the added width into functional capacity: on Qwen3-2.38B Symmetric, Muon gains +6.16 over the 0.6B source versus +4.95 for AdamW. In training efficiency, Muon's token savings in expansion settings (25–45%) are roughly double its scratch-training advantage (14–23%), confirming the gain is specific to breaking the Subspace Trap rather than a general optimizer speedup.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the model width expansion (MWE) lineage from Net2Net through HyperCloning and LEMON, which established function-preserving initialization strategies (neuron splitting, parameter copying) to accelerate LLM scaling. It also draws on the River Valley loss landscape framework (Wen et al., 2025) to provide a theoretical account of why these initializations create a geometric bottleneck. The key departure is shifting the diagnostic lens from initialization design (noise injection, weighted coefficients) to optimizer geometry: the paper argues that the failure mode is not in how parameters are initialized but in how the optimizer's update structure interacts with the Kronecker-product symmetry inherited from the seed model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that the default optimizer (AdamW) is structurally unable to populate expanded dimensions—rather than merely suboptimal—has direct practical implications for anyone using width expansion as a cost-effective alternative to from-scratch pre-training; a significant fraction of added parameters may be silently wasted. Limitations include testing only two decoder-only model families (SmolLM2, Qwen3) at 2× expansion, reliance on the River Valley landscape assumptions (analytic loss, bounded Hessian, eigen gap), and comparison against only one alternative optimizer (Muon). Open questions include whether the effect generalizes to depth expansion, larger expansion factors, encoder architectures, and whether other spectral or Newton-type optimizers provide equivalent escape mechanisms.
