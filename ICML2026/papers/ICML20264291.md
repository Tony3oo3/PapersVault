## Problem

Existing spatial reasoning models and datasets are predominantly confined to indoor scenes (1–30 m) built from 3D scans with labor-intensive annotations, leaving a six-order-of-magnitude gap from millimeter-scale objects to kilometer-scale drone views unaddressed. No prior work provides a unified training framework, dataset, or benchmark that spans all physical scales, and naive cross-scale fine-tuning introduces knowledge conflicts that degrade performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264291/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes multi-frame video clips (up to 32 frames at 128×28×28 patches during training, 256×28×28 at inference) processed through both the Qwen2.5-VL visual encoder and a DINOv3 self-supervised encoder, with optional point, bounding-box, or mask annotations on key frames to support interactive referencing. Outputs are spatial reasoning answers in free-form, multiple-choice, or regression formats accompanied by chain-of-thought rationales, covering 19 task types (counting, distance, size, route planning, manipulation, area estimation, etc.) across five scale categories (tiny, tabletop, indoor, outdoor, drone).

## Method

SpaceVista-7B is built on Qwen2.5-VL-7B and introduces three key mechanisms. First, DINOv3 dense features are fused with the semantic visual tokens via cross-attention to inject geometry- and depth-aware information beyond what contrastive pretraining provides. Second, to mitigate cross-scale knowledge conflicts during mixed training, the model attaches M=4 LoRA-like scale experts (each fine-tuning ~0.5% of parameters) to every LLM layer, with a learned scale router (MLP + softmax) dynamically allocating expert contributions per layer. Third, RL training uses a progressive reward with three ordered anchors—semantic identification, scene-scale estimation, and final answer correctness—where earlier anchors gate later ones, enforcing a human-like reasoning path. The full pipeline is: SFT on SpaceVista-1M (1M QA pairs from 38K wild video scenes) → scale-expert specialization → GRPO-based RL with the progressive reward.

## Results

SpaceVista-7B with RL achieves 39.2 on SpaceVista-Bench, surpassing the best proprietary model (Gemini-2.5-pro at 33.8) by over 5 points and the best open-source model (Qwen2.5-VL-7B at 28.9) by over 10 points. On SPAR-Bench, it reaches 41.6, the highest among all compared models. On VSI-Bench, it scores 48.6, marginally ahead of SpatialMLLM-4B (48.4) and well above GPT-5 (44.2). However, it trails Gemini-2.5-pro on STI-Bench (38.2 vs 41.4) and GPT-5 on MMSI-Bench (32.3 vs 40.7), indicating that multi-image spatial reasoning remains a relative weakness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpaceVista-7B w/.RL achieves the best score on SPAR-Bench (41.6) and SpaceVista-Bench (39.2), outperforming GPT-5 (37.4, 33.7) and Gemini-2.5-pro (36.3, 33.8) by 4.2 and 5.4 points respectively on the latter. On VSI-Bench, it edges out SpatialMLLM-4B (48.4) and SpaceR-7B (46.9) while surpassing GPT-5 (44.2) by 4.4 points. However, it loses to Gemini-2.5-pro on STI-Bench (38.2 vs 41.4) and to GPT-5 on MMSI-Bench (32.3 vs 40.7), suggesting multi-image and fine-grained temporal-spatial tasks remain challenging. Among open-source models of comparable size (7B), SpaceVista-7B w/.RL leads on four of five benchmarks, with the largest margin on SpaceVista-Bench (+10.3 over Qwen2.5-VL-7B). The ablation in Table 3 confirms that the scale experts (+2.7 on SpaceVista-Bench) and progressive reward design (+3.3) are the primary drivers of the improvement over vanilla SFT+RL (32.1).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Qwen2.5-VL as its multimodal backbone and adapts the Mixture of LoRA Experts (MoLE) framework into scale-specific experts with a learned router, but its primary conceptual departure is from the indoor-centric spatial reasoning literature (VSI-Bench, SpaceR, SPAR, Spatial-MLLM, InternSpatial), which has largely confined itself to 1–30 m scan-based scenes. Where prior work relies on manual 3D annotations or single-scale automated pipelines, SpaceVista introduces a specialist-driven automated curation pipeline spanning five physical scales (mm to km) and 19 tasks, and explicitly diagnoses cross-scale knowledge conflict as a training pathology that naive fine-tuning cannot resolve. The progressive reward design with scale anchors is a novel RL formulation that has no direct precedent in the spatial reasoning literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SpaceVista-1M (1M QA pairs, 38K scenes) and SpaceVista-Bench (1.6K human-verified QA pairs) are likely to become standard resources for all-scale spatial reasoning, filling a gap where prior benchmarks covered only indoor or single-scale settings. The finding that mixing cross-scale data without architectural separation actively degrades performance—and that a modest 4-expert LoRA configuration is optimal while 6 experts overfits the router—provides a practical design principle for multi-domain MLLM training. Limitations include the small drone-view subset, the relatively small benchmark size, and the fact that the 7B model still trails human performance by ~42 points on SpaceVista-Bench, leaving open whether the scale-expert approach transfers to much larger backbones or to truly continuous (rather than discrete) scale transitions.
