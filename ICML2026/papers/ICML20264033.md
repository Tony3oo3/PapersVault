## Problem

Positive samples are widely regarded as essential in Graph Contrastive Learning, yet the paper shows that GCLs achieve competitive performance even when the positive alignment term is removed from the InfoNCE loss. The paper attributes this to a 'pre-alignment effect': GNN message passing inherently increases the expected similarity between positive samples before contrastive optimization, trivializing the learning signal they carry. The key question is how to restore the efficacy of positive samples by preventing message passing from washing out the informative diversity they should provide.

## Input / Output

The method takes a graph with adjacency matrix A ∈ {0,1}^{N×N} and node feature matrix X ∈ R^{N×F} as input. Before training, the F feature dimensions are partitioned once into a high-energy subset H (TopK by estimated Dirichlet energy) and a low-energy subset L; this partition is fixed throughout optimization. The output is a node embedding Z = Z_H + Z_L ∈ R^{N×d}, where Z_H comes from GCN propagation on X_H and Z_L comes from a node-wise MLP on X_L. Downstream, frozen embeddings are evaluated with a linear classifier (node classification) or K-Means (node clustering).

## Method

SPGCL introduces two mechanisms grounded in a theoretical result (Theorem 3.3) showing that the mutual information between the positive-sample indicator and the embedding similarity is upper-bounded by the per-feature Dirichlet energy. First, Energy Aware Propagation (EAP) estimates the Dirichlet energy of each feature dimension, selects the TopK highest-energy dimensions for GCN message passing (preserving informative variation), and routes the remaining low-energy dimensions through a node-wise MLP without any graph propagation (preventing them from inflating similarity via Laplacian smoothing). Second, Energy-guided Positive Sampling (EPS) computes pairwise cosine similarity on the low-energy features and constructs a probabilistic positive-pair matrix where each edge (i,j) is retained as a positive with probability min(α·S_ij, 1), filtering out misleading positives that arise from stochastic augmentations or heterophilic neighborhoods. The model is trained with the standard InfoNCE loss; no additional loss terms are introduced.

## Results

SPGCL achieves the best node classification accuracy on all 12 benchmark datasets. On homophilic graphs, it reaches 85.44% on Cora, 73.49% on CiteSeer, 87.19% on PubMed, 94.83% on Photo, 95.03% on CS, and 91.04% on Computers. On heterophilic graphs, it achieves 72.26% on Chameleon, 75.41% on Cornell, 80.81% on Texas, 83.53% on Wisconsin, 77.43% on Crocodile, and 37.23% on Actor. For node clustering, SPGCL attains the highest NMI (0.5626, 0.7135, 0.8046) and Homogeneity (0.6132, 0.7251, 0.8345) on Computers, Photo, and CS respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPGCL outperforms all 17 baselines on every dataset tested. On homophilic graphs, it surpasses the strongest augmentation-based methods (GCA: 85.08% on Cora, CSGCL: 90.67% on Computers) and neighborhood-based methods (LocalGCL: 84.82% on Cora, SGRL: 94.08% on CS) by 0.36–1.36 points. On heterophilic graphs, the gains are substantially larger: SPGCL beats GraphACL (the best heterophily-aware baseline) by 3.14 points on Chameleon (72.26 vs. 69.12), 14.87 points on Cornell (75.41 vs. 60.54), 9.19 points on Texas (80.81 vs. 71.62), and 7.33 points on Wisconsin (83.53 vs. 70.20). Bootstrap-based methods (BGRL, AFGRL) that rely solely on positive alignment without explicit negatives are consistently outperformed, confirming that the pre-alignment effect identified in the theory limits their effectiveness. The paper also shows that removing positive samples entirely from a standard GCL yields performance close to full GCL, validating the core claim that message passing trivializes positive alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of GCL research that focuses on constructing better positive samples through improved augmentations (GCA, CSGCL, SpCo) or neighborhood sampling (LocalGCL, AFGRL, NeCo). Rather than asking 'how to build better positives,' it asks 'why do positives not help as much as expected,' and answers this by connecting GNN message passing to Dirichlet energy from graph signal processing. The theoretical framework repurposes the well-known Dirichlet energy concept as an upper bound on positive learning effectiveness, a connection not previously drawn in the GCL literature. The proposed SPGCL architecture (separating high-energy features for GCN propagation and low-energy features for MLP transformation) is a novel design that has no direct predecessor in the GCL method family.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that GCL can match standard performance without positive samples challenges a core assumption underpinning much of the GCL literature, and the Dirichlet-energy-based theoretical framework provides a principled lens for diagnosing and fixing this issue. Practically, the energy-aware propagation and sampling mechanisms offer a drop-in modification to existing GCN-based GCL pipelines that consistently improves results across both homophilic and heterophilic graphs. Open questions include whether the analysis extends to attention-based or higher-order GNN architectures, whether the TopK energy threshold can be made adaptive rather than a fixed hyperparameter, and whether the framework applies to other contrastive objectives beyond InfoNCE. The method is also tested primarily on relatively small-to-medium graphs, leaving scalability to large-scale graphs as an open practical concern.
