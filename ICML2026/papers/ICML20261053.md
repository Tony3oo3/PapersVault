## Problem

Standard supervised fine-tuning for chain-of-thought distillation treats all tokens uniformly, forcing student models to overfit to redundant, instance-specific, or erroneous reasoning steps rather than acquiring generalizable reasoning patterns. Existing filtering heuristics (perplexity, entropy) capture only surface-level prediction uncertainty and cannot distinguish semantically meaningful reasoning from high-difficulty noise, leaving the geometric structure of which gradients actually drive generalization unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261053/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes long CoT reasoning trajectories (generated by DeepSeek-R1 on AceReason-1.1-SFT problems, up to 32,768 tokens) and the student model's initial weights. It operates at step-level granularity (complete reasoning steps rather than individual tokens) to preserve semantic coherence during gradient capture. The output is a fine-tuned student model trained with a selective masked loss, where only high-spectral-strength steps contribute to parameter updates while low-strength steps still participate in forward-pass context computation.

## Method

The paper introduces Loss Subspace Attribution, which performs SVD on the stacked gradient matrix of all CoT tokens to decompose gradient space into a low-rank consensus subspace (top-k right singular vectors) and an orthogonal residual subspace. A step-level Spectral Strength metric, defined as the average leverage score (row norm of left singular vectors) across tokens in each reasoning step, quantifies each step's alignment with the consensus subspace. Spectral-guided Learning then operates in two stages: (1) offline gradient capture and spectral analysis to compute per-step spectral strength, and (2) dynamic threshold truncation that selects the minimal set of steps whose cumulative spectral strength reaches a preset energy ratio p, followed by masked SFT where the loss is computed only over selected steps (M_t = 1) while residual steps receive zero gradient weight. A theoretical analysis proves that projecting gradients onto the consensus subspace reduces gradient variance by a factor of k*/d, formally establishing the variance-reduction mechanism behind the empirical gains.

## Results

Spectral-guided Learning achieves an average accuracy gain of +5.5% over Vanilla SFT on Qwen3-4B-Instruct (61.2% vs. 55.7%), +2.7% on both Qwen3-4B-Base (51.3% vs. 48.6%) and Qwen3-8B-Base (56.7% vs. 54.0%), and +1.5% on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (42.1% vs. 40.6%) across five reasoning benchmarks (AIME24, AIME25, MATH500, OlympiadBench, GPQA-Diamond). Gains are most pronounced on high-difficulty competition problems: Qwen3-4B-Instruct improves by +7.5% on both AIME24 and AIME25, while Qwen3-8B-Base gains +5.8% on AIME25. The method consistently outperforms both heuristic baselines (TDST and EDSP) across all four model architectures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Spectral-guided Learning consistently outperforms both heuristic baselines across all four model architectures. Against TDST (perplexity-based step selection, Cui et al. 2025), it achieves +2.3% average on Qwen3-4B-Base (51.3% vs. 49.7%), +1.8% on Qwen3-8B-Base (56.7% vs. 54.9%), +3.5% on Qwen3-4B-Instruct (61.2% vs. 57.7%), and +2.3% on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (42.1% vs. 39.8%). Against EDSP (entropy-based step selection, Li et al. 2025), it achieves +2.8% on Qwen3-4B-Base (51.3% vs. 48.5%), +1.7% on Qwen3-8B-Base (56.7% vs. 55.0%), +2.2% on Qwen3-4B-Instruct (61.2% vs. 59.0%), and +4.6% on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (42.1% vs. 37.5%). Notably, both heuristic methods sometimes underperform even Vanilla SFT (e.g., EDSP drops to 37.5% on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct vs. 40.6% for SFT), whereas Spectral-guided Learning never degrades below the SFT baseline, confirming that gradient-spectral selection captures generalizable reasoning signals that surface-level uncertainty metrics miss.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of CoT distillation and gradient-based data selection. While prior work on reasoning distillation (DeepSeek-R1, Wei et al.) treats all CoT tokens uniformly via standard SFT, and recent heuristic methods filter steps by perplexity (TDST) or entropy (EDSP), this paper introduces a gradient-geometry lens: applying SVD to the stacked gradient matrix to decompose it into a low-rank consensus subspace and a residual subspace. The spectral-strength metric (a leverage-score-inspired quantity) repurposes ideas from statistics and the low-rank update literature (LoRA) to identify which reasoning steps drive generalizable optimization, positioning itself as orthogonal to CoT compression or rewriting approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, geometry-based alternative to ad-hoc heuristic filtering for CoT distillation, with a clean theoretical guarantee (variance reduction by k*/d) under idealized assumptions. Practically, the 5.5% average gain over SFT across four model scales and five benchmarks is meaningful for teams distilling reasoning into smaller models. Open questions include: the computational cost of the offline SVD phase on very long sequences, whether the consensus subspace remains stable as training progresses, and whether the approach extends to RL-based or multi-task distillation settings where gradient conflicts are more complex.
