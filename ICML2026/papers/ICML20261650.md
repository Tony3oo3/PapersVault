## Problem

Estimating multi-category CDFs under local differential privacy is difficult when sensitivity is asymmetric (e.g., high debt is sensitive but low debt is not) and when accompanying categorical attributes, while not private in isolation, act as quasi-identifiers when paired with sensitive values. Existing ULDP mechanisms require predefining a fixed sensitive region, which is impractical because sensitivity boundaries vary across individuals and shift over time, while standard LDP wastes privacy budget protecting non-sensitive fields equally.

## Input / Output

Each user holds a continuous value X ∈ [0,1] (with larger values more sensitive) and a categorical label Y ∈ {1,…,K}; a public threshold t_i is drawn from a distribution G over [0,1]. The user reports a single one-hot vector from {e_1,…,e_K, e_{K+1}} together with t_i, where e_{K+1} indicates a potentially sensitive response and suppresses the categorical label entirely. The server recovers step-function sub-CDF estimators F̂_{0k}(t) for each category, the total CDF F̂_+(t), and conditional prediction probabilities P(Y=k | t₀ < X ≤ t₁) via a shape-constrained MLE with a post-hoc capping step to ensure F̂_+(t) ≤ 1.

## Method

The core contribution is the Asymmetrically Censored Randomized Response (ACRR) mechanism, a two-stage design that first maps each response to a binary sensitivity indicator via threshold comparison (X > t_i) and then applies a ULDP perturbation that collapses all sensitive inputs to a single censored output e_{K+1} while safe inputs report their category e_k with probability 1−e^{−ε} or the censored output with probability e^{−ε}. This eliminates the need to predefine a sensitive region—only the direction of sensitivity is specified—and the censoring of Y for sensitive outputs provides plausible deniability and reduces re-identification risk. Recovery interprets the perturbed reports as truthful samples from a transformed variable (X*, Y*), fits the distorted sub-CDFs via the Iterative Convex Minorant (ICM) algorithm (a competing-risks MLE), and inverts the known linear distortion F* = LF to recover the true CDFs. The paper establishes L_{p,G} consistency at rate n^{−1/3}, uniform consistency at n^{−1/3} log^{1/3} n, and pointwise weak convergence governed by a multivariate Chernoff-type limit, with a divide-and-conquer strategy to handle the superlinear ICM runtime.

## Results

On synthetic data (K=4 categories), the L∞ uniform consistency error decreases from 0.098 (n=10³, ε=1) to 0.017 (n=10⁶, ε=1), confirming the theoretical O_p(n^{-1/3}) rate. The prediction error for P(Y=k, X<1/2) drops from 0.059 to 0.005 over the same range. On the real Government Salary Dataset (7 race categories, n up to 200,000), the L∞ error ranges from 0.084 (n=5,000, ε=1) to 0.028 (n=200,000, ε=3). Higher privacy budgets consistently improve accuracy: at n=10⁵, increasing ε from 1 to 3 reduces L∞ error from 0.029 to 0.024.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against external competing estimators but instead compares its ACRR mechanism against standard LDP and prior ULDP mechanisms on utility metrics. For K=4 and ε=1, standard LDP randomized response yields a truthful-response probability below 28%, while the prior utility-optimized mechanism (Murakami & Kawamoto, 2019) achieves ~47% for sensitive and ~30% for safe outputs. ACRR improves this to >63% for safe inputs and exactly 100% for sensitive inputs, with probabilities independent of K due to censoring. In Appendix F, the authors show that enforcing full symmetric LDP (LDP1) introduces an error inflation factor of at least order K (specifically (K+e^ε)/(e^ε−1) for the max eigenvalue), whereas ACRR's inflation is simply 1/(1−e^{−ε}), eliminating the K-dependent amplification. The proposed 'stop at one' capping strategy achieves L∞ error within 0.001–0.003 of an oracle that knows true marginal proportions, validating it as a practical post-processing step. A divide-and-conquer strategy (M=4) further reduces computation time from superlinear n^{1.49} growth while slightly improving accuracy over the unsplit baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ULDP framework of Murakami & Kawamoto (2019) from frequency estimation to continuous CDF estimation, and generalizes the binary-encoding CDF estimator of Liu et al. (2024) from a single category (K=1) to multi-category joint subdistribution estimation. The key methodological departure is replacing the predefined sensitive region (required by original ULDP) with a threshold-based binary encoding that only specifies the *direction* of sensitivity, combined with conditional censoring of the categorical label Y for sensitive outputs. The recovery procedure is built on the ICM algorithm for shape-constrained MLE (Groeneboom & Jongbloed, 2014) and the competing-risks current-status theory of Groeneboom et al. (2008a,b), which the authors identify as the natural statistical analogue of the censored ULDP data structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to establish uniform consistency and pointwise weak convergence for multi-category CDF estimation under ULDP, filling a gap the authors explicitly identify in the literature. The practical contribution—conditional suppression of quasi-identifiers when a numerical response is potentially sensitive—addresses a real re-identification risk that standard LDP does not capture, and the K-dependent error inflation under symmetric LDP (vs. its absence under the asymmetric ACRR mechanism) quantifies the cost of over-protecting non-sensitive attributes. Key limitations include the assumption of monotone sensitivity direction, the fact that the ULDP guarantee applies to the coarsened report space rather than the full continuous domain, and the post-hoc capping step that lacks a joint constrained-estimation alternative. Open directions include handling non-monotone sensitivity, scalable approximations for large K or n, and deployment considerations (consent, refusal, missingness).
