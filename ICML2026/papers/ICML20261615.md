## Problem

Existing methods for learning latent dynamics from high-dimensional time series face a fundamental trade-off: generative models (VAEs, Deep Kalman Filters) waste representational capacity on pixel-level reconstruction, encoding irrelevant distractors alongside dynamically relevant state, while contrastive methods (CPC) lack a full probabilistic state-space model, yielding no principled uncertainty or accurate latent dynamics. The paper asks whether one can learn accurate, identifiable latent dynamics via maximum likelihood without ever parametrizing how observations are generated from latents.

## Input / Output

The model consumes sequences of T=100 high-dimensional observations (video frames ranging from 24×24 grayscale to 64×64 RGB, optionally with control inputs for the Walker task) and produces a full Gaussian posterior over the latent trajectory at every timestep, obtained in closed form via augmented Kalman smoothing. Future latent states are predicted by iterating the learned linear-Gaussian transition, and a post-hoc auxiliary decoder (trained separately by simple regression) can optionally map posterior means back to observation space for visualization. The key structural constraint is that the latent prior is jointly Gaussian with a standardized stationary distribution N(0,I) and zero transition bias, while the observation-to-latent link is an arbitrary neural network.

## Method

The RP-GSSM replaces the explicit generative decoder of a standard GSSM with a recognition-parametrized joint distribution, where the 'generative' model is defined implicitly by the recognition factors f_φ(z_t|x_t) = N(μ_φ(x_t), Σ_φ(x_t)) and the empirical data distribution, following the RPM framework of Walker et al. (2023). Training proceeds via EM: the E-step computes the posterior q(z) in closed form using 'augmented Kalman smoothing,' which runs standard Kalman smoothing with the neural network outputs μ_φ(x_t), Σ_φ(x_t) substituted for the linear emission parameters (C=I, d=0, R=Σ_φ(x_t), data=μ_φ(x_t)); the M-step performs gradient ascent on an auxiliary free energy G that lower-bounds the true free energy via an interior variational bound (Jensen's inequality with auxiliary factors g ∝ q/p_η). The entire model is trained by maximum likelihood with no reconstruction loss, no auxiliary contrastive objective, and no ad-hoc regularization. A linear identifiability theorem guarantees that in the infinite-data limit, the learned latent space is unique up to an invertible linear transformation, and the Koopman operator perspective explains how the linear-Gaussian prior can represent intrinsically nonlinear dynamics through the nonlinear recognition mapping.

## Results

The primary metric is linear regression R² between inferred posterior means and ground-truth state variables, evaluated across seven datasets of increasing complexity. The RP-GSSM achieves the highest R² on every dataset, with the most dramatic margins when visual distractors are present: on CIFAR10-Pendulum and DAVIS-Tracking, the RP-GSSM is the only method that reliably recovers the underlying dynamics, while all generative and contrastive baselines degrade substantially. On the egocentric LAFAN1 task, the RP-GSSM significantly outperforms SVAE and CPC under kernel ridge regression (Figure 5), while matching them under linear regression. On the Walker environment, the RP-GSSM extracts state information and predicts torso angle more rapidly and accurately than Dreamer, maintaining accurate rollouts for at least 50 timesteps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CPC (van den Oord et al., 2018), the RP-GSSM wins on every dataset; the gap is most pronounced under distractors, where CPC's encoding is described as 'mixed'—it retains some pendulum information but loses coherent dynamics, whereas the RP-GSSM encodes only the temporally persistent signal. Against SVAE (Johnson et al., 2016), the best-performing generative baseline on average, the RP-GSSM still achieves higher R² across all tasks, and its post-hoc auxiliary decoder produces cleaner reconstructions (grey backgrounds indicating ignored distractors) compared to SVAE's pixel-level reconstruction that blurs the background and loses the pendulum arm. Against KVAE (Fraccaro et al., 2017) and the five DKF variants (Krishnan et al., 2015), the RP-GSSM is consistently superior, with the advantage growing at higher latent dimensions and under distractor conditions. Against Dreamer (Hafner et al., 2020) on the Walker environment, the RP-GSSM learns to extract state information and predict dynamics more rapidly and accurately, and its 'clean' decoding (without distractor elements) is more faithful, reflecting the advantage of not being driven by a pixel-level reconstruction objective. The RP-GSSM also demonstrates superior data efficiency: it is the only method able to infer angular velocity when trained on only 64 sequences (Figure S2).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Recognition-Parametrized Model (RPM) framework of Walker et al. (2023) to the state-space model setting, combining it with a stable Gaussian SSM prior (standardized per Buesing et al., 2012) to yield the RP-GSSM. It departs from the dominant generative paradigm (VAEs, DKF, SVAE, KVAE) by eliminating the explicit decoder entirely, and from contrastive methods (CPC) by retaining a full probabilistic model with principled uncertainty. The key architectural move is that the nonlinear neural-network recognition factors replace the emission distribution, while the linear-Gaussian prior structure preserves exact Kalman smoothing inference—yielding what the authors call 'augmented Kalman smoothing.'

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The RP-GSSM offers a principled alternative to reconstruction-driven representation learning for time series, with the practical advantage of exact (non-Monte-Carlo) inference that scales gracefully with latent dimensionality. The provable linear identifiability result in the infinite-data limit provides theoretical grounding absent from most deep state-space models. Key limitations include the linear-Gaussian prior (mitigated by Koopman eigenspace embeddings but still restrictive for truly nonlinear dynamics), the absence of explicit generation (though post-hoc decoders partially address this), and the approximations in the interior variational bound whose tightness is only empirically validated. Open directions include extension to switching systems, multi-modal observations, and integration with control and SLAM pipelines.
