## Problem

Denoising Diffusion Sampling (DDS) is vulnerable to adversarial perturbations injected during variable transmission in decentralized settings, where even a simple Gaussian perturbation at each of hundreds of timestamps can significantly degrade sample generation quality. No runtime defense mechanism existed to protect the DDS inference/sampling process from such perturbations, as prior adversarial defenses focus on training-time model modification rather than purifying the perturbed sampling trajectory.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262272/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a perturbed variable x̃ = x + δ (transmitted between users in a decentralized DDS pipeline), the potential function V(x) (e.g., a GMM, Müller-Brown surface, or BNN posterior energy), and generates MC samples z_{t,i} at each timestamp. It produces a recovered sample x_{k+1} following the target distribution, purified MC samples {z̃_{t,i}} for score estimation via a clamped rejection sampler, and a surrogate minimum V• of the LV-regularized potential. The defense operates entirely at inference time with only zeroth-order queries to V(z), avoiding gradient evaluations that first-order baselines require at every timestamp.

## Method

ADDDS introduces a local variation (LV) regularization that replaces the standard potential minimization min V(x) with min V(x) + λ|∇V(x)|, where the LV term captures the perturbation-induced variation in the potential landscape. The key theoretical result (Theorem 3.1) establishes that the regularized and unregularized problems share the same solution set, but their iteration paths differ: the regularized path yields critical points with nonzero |∇V(x•)| > 0, which naturally tolerates the small LV induced by adversarial perturbations. A Crowder-Wolfe conjugate gradient algorithm with Wolfe line search solves the resulting non-smooth optimization, using the Fréchet subdifferential of |∇V(x)| computed via autograd. The defense integrates with ZOD-MC's zeroth-order rejection sampling, requiring only V(z) queries (not ∇V(z)), and Theorem 3.3 provides a formal adversarial robustness guarantee showing W(x) = V(x) + λ|∇V(x)| upper-bounds the worst-case adversarial loss max_{‖δ‖≤ε} V(x+δ) under L-smoothness, with the condition λ ≥ ε ensuring the regularization strength matches the perturbation budget.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, ADDDS achieves 29.81% clean accuracy and maintains 9.23% under PGD¹⁰ attack, outperforming all baselines (ZOD-MC: 12.03%/8.86%, RDMC: 28.72%/8.12%, RSDMC: 24.18%/4.79%). On CIFAR-100, ADDDS reaches 7.10% clean and 5.99% under PGD¹⁰, while ZOD-MC collapses from 5.81% to 0.53%. On the 2D Gaussian mixture benchmark under PGD¹⁰ (oracle complexity 10200), ADDDS achieves MMD 0.253 and W₂ 3.925, compared to ZOD-MC's MMD 4.478 and W₂ 10.321 — a roughly 17× improvement in MMD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ADDDS outperforms all seven baselines (ZOD-MC, RSDMC, RDMC, SLIPS, Langevin, Proximal, Parallel tempering, SMC) across every attack type on both CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100. The most dramatic gap appears under PGD¹⁰ on CIFAR-100, where ADDDS retains 5.99% accuracy while ZOD-MC collapses to 0.53% (an 11× gap) and SLIPS drops to 0.69%. On CIFAR-10 under PGD¹⁰, ADDDS (9.23%) leads RDMC (8.12%) and ZOD-MC (8.86%), with RSDMC falling far behind at 4.79%. On the 2D Gaussian mixture benchmark, ADDDS achieves MMD 0.253 vs. ZOD-MC's 4.478 and all other baselines clustering around 4.5–5.9 under PGD¹⁰, confirming that the LV regularization provides a substantial robustness advantage over both zeroth-order (ZOD-MC) and first-order (RDMC, RSDMC, SLIPS) samplers. Notably, even the strongest non-defense baseline (RDMC) degrades significantly under FGSM/PGD¹⁰ on CIFAR-100 (3.90%→0.43%), while ADDDS maintains near-clean performance (7.10%→5.99%), demonstrating that the defense is effective precisely where baselines fail.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed threads: zeroth-order diffusion Monte Carlo sampling (ZOD-MC, He et al. 2024) and total variation regularization (Rudin et al. 1992). It departs from prior adversarial robustness work on sampling (Ben-Eliezer & Yogev 2020; Braverman et al. 2021), which focused on streaming and general sampling algorithms, by targeting the specific vulnerability of denoising diffusion sampling in decentralized transmission settings. The key conceptual move is extracting a local segment |∇V(x)| of the TV functional as a 'local variation' regularization on the potential function minimization, repurposing a classical image-denoising tool as a runtime adversarial defense for the score-matching stage of DDS. Unlike adversarial training (a training-time defense that modifies model parameters), ADDDS operates purely at inference time during the sampling trajectory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a previously unaddressed vulnerability in DDS: even a simple Gaussian perturbation injected during variable transmission can catastrophically degrade sample quality across hundreds of timesteps, and the proposed LV regularization provides a clean theoretical robustness guarantee (Theorem 3.3) linking the defense to a minimax bound. The practical impact is somewhat bounded by the evaluation setting—high-dimensional experiments use BNN posterior sampling rather than standard image generation, yielding low absolute accuracies (29.81% on CIFAR-10)—so the broader applicability to mainstream diffusion generative models (e.g., Stable Diffusion, DiT) remains an open question. Future work could explore whether the LV defense transfers to other diffusion architectures, whether it composes with existing defenses like gradient clipping, and whether the vulnerability extends to the forward (noising) process rather than just the backward (denoising) process.
