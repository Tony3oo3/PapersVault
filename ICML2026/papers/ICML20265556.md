## Problem

Conventional ANFIS architectures suffer from exponential rule explosion in high-dimensional spaces due to their product-based inference mechanism, while KAN, though scalable, lacks explicitly interpretable symbolic rules. No existing neuro-fuzzy framework simultaneously achieves strong nonlinear approximation, compact and human-readable rule structures, and explicit uncertainty modeling as input dimensionality grows.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265556/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a numerical feature vector x ∈ R^d and produces either a scalar regression prediction or a class label via a linear defuzzification layer. Uniquely, it also outputs a set of sparse, linguistically interpretable IF-THEN fuzzy rules (e.g., 'AT is HIGH → −0.34·(7.26·M_AT(x_AT))') whose antecedents are automatically pruned to 2–3 features by entropy-based masking, and whose consequents are linear combinations of the activated membership functions. The IT2 variant additionally encodes a Footprint of Uncertainty (FOU) per edge, providing an explicit uncertainty band around each rule's firing strength.

## Method

KANFIS replaces ANFIS's product T-norm rule aggregation with a Kolmogorov-Arnold-style additive superposition: each network edge is a learnable fuzzy membership function (Gaussian, Generalized Bell, or Sigmoid for T1; IT2 Gaussian with upper/lower standard deviations for uncertainty), and hidden units sum incoming edge activations rather than multiplying them, so parameter and rule complexity scale linearly with input dimensionality. For IT2 edges, a center-of-sets type-reduction collapses the FOU into a crisp firing strength. Two regularization terms shape the rule base: an entropy-based soft mask M ∈ [0,1]^{D×K} drives antecedent sparsity (each rule activates only a few features), and a pairwise cosine-similarity penalty enforces orthogonality among rule firing patterns to prevent mode collapse. Multiple fuzzy-functional layers can be stacked with inter-layer normalization, and a final linear projection serves as the Takagi-Sugeno consequent, yielding a compact, end-to-end trainable neuro-symbolic model.

## Results

On the CCPP regression dataset, IT2-KANFIS achieves RMSE 4.1240 and MAE 3.1975, while T1-KANFIS achieves RMSE 3.9542 and MAE 3.0760, both outperforming MLP (RMSE 4.1883) and IT2-ANFIS (RMSE 4.0047). On classification tasks, IT2-KANFIS reaches 99.12% accuracy and 0.9990 AUROC on BCW, and 93.92% accuracy on Spambase (where T1-ANFIS and KAN cannot train due to the curse of dimensionality). On the MHR medical dataset, KANFIS achieves 79.31% accuracy and 0.9057 AUROC, surpassing MLP (70.94% Acc) and IT2-ANFIS (68.47% Acc) by a wide margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against KAN (Liu et al., 2024), KANFIS wins on classification accuracy and F1 across BCW, Spambase, and MHR, but KAN retains a slight edge on CCPP regression (MAPE 0.6389 vs. 0.7047 for IT2-KANFIS) and Parkinsons MAPE (14.610 vs. 14.891). Critically, KAN fails to train on Spambase and ForestCover due to prohibitive computational cost, whereas KANFIS handles these high-dimensional settings without issue. Against MLP, KANFIS consistently outperforms on all metrics across every dataset, with the largest gaps on MHR (79.31% vs. 70.94% accuracy) and Parkinsons (RMSE 0.0397 vs. 0.0527). Against T1-ANFIS and IT2-ANFIS, KANFIS achieves comparable or superior accuracy on low-dimensional CCPP, but the ANFIS baselines become intractable on high-dimensional BCW and Spambase (marked as '–'), while KANFIS maintains strong performance. In supplementary comparisons (Table 6), KANFIS also outperforms interpretable baselines NAMs, GAMI-NET, and NODE-GAM on both regression and classification metrics, confirming its advantage over other additive-model-based interpretable architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

KANFIS sits at the intersection of two well-studied lines: the classical ANFIS neuro-fuzzy architecture (which maps Takagi-Sugeno fuzzy inference into a five-layer network with product-based rule aggregation) and the Kolmogorov-Arnold Network (which replaces fixed linear weights with learnable univariate functions along edges). The key architectural departure is replacing ANFIS's product T-norm aggregation with Kolmogorov-Arnold-style additive summation, and reinterpreting KAN's learnable edge functions as adaptive fuzzy membership functions (Gaussian, Generalized Bell, Sigmoid) with optional Interval Type-2 uncertainty. This is further augmented by two regularization terms—entropy-based antecedent sparsity and pairwise cosine-similarity rule distinctiveness—that have no direct analogue in prior ANFIS or KAN literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a long-standing practical bottleneck in neuro-fuzzy systems: the exponential rule explosion that makes ANFIS intractable beyond low-dimensional inputs. By showing that additive aggregation yields linear parameter scaling while preserving universal approximation (formalized in Appendix B), KANFIS offers a principled path to interpretable fuzzy reasoning on high-dimensional tabular data. However, the interpretability evidence is limited to two small datasets (CCPP, MHR) validated by domain literature, and the performance gains over KAN and MLP are modest on most benchmarks—KAN actually outperforms KANFIS on CCPP and Parkinsons regression metrics. Open questions include whether the additive structure can capture strong multiplicative interactions without depth, how the framework extends beyond tabular data, and whether the IT2 uncertainty modeling provides meaningful calibration benefits beyond the center-of-sets reduction used here.
