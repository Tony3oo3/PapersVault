## Problem

Extended reasoning in LLMs generates tens of thousands of tokens, causing KV cache memory to grow proportionally and become a severe bottleneck. Existing KV cache compression methods estimate key importance from post-RoPE attention scores over recent queries, but RoPE rotation makes query orientations unstable across positions, leaving only a tiny observation window and causing important keys to be permanently evicted. No prior method exploits the structural stability of pre-RoPE representations to predict which keys will matter for future queries.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-RoPE key vectors already stored in the KV cache along with offline-calibrated Q/K center statistics (mean vectors and expected norms per frequency band, computed from a small calibration set). It produces a per-key importance score by evaluating a trigonometric series at multiple geometrically-spaced future offsets, then retains only the top-B scoring keys under a fixed memory budget. For GQA models, scores from multiple query heads sharing a KV head are z-normalized independently and aggregated via max before selection.

## Method

TriAttention is built on the observation that pre-RoPE Q and K vectors are highly concentrated around fixed non-zero centers (quantified by Mean Resultant Length R approaching 1) across most attention heads, a property stable across positions, contexts, and domains. Substituting these centers into the RoPE attention formula reduces the logit to a trigonometric series in Q-K distance, whose peaks reveal predictable distance preferences. The scoring function combines two components: S_trig uses the Q center and trigonometric series to capture distance-based attention preferences, while S_norm uses Q/K norms weighted by (1−R_f) to complement heads with weaker concentration. The final score is averaged over geometrically-spaced future offsets {1,2,4,…,2^16}, and pruning is triggered every 128 generated tokens to retain the top-B keys, achieving 2.5× throughput or 10.7× KV memory reduction at Full Attention accuracy on AIME25.

## Results

On AIME25 with Qwen3-8B at a 2048-token KV budget, TriAttention achieves 32.9% accuracy versus 17.5% for R-KV and 20.0% for SnapKV, while Full Attention reaches 40.8%. At equivalent accuracy to Full Attention (40.8%), TriAttention delivers 2.5× higher throughput (563.5 vs. 222.8 tokens/s) or 10.7× KV memory reduction. On MATH 500 with only 1,024 of 32k tokens retained, TriAttention closely matches Full Attention (68.4% vs. 69.6%) while achieving 6.3× throughput. Across all four evaluated models (Qwen3-8B, DS-Llama, DS-Qwen, GPT-OSS) and both AIME benchmarks, TriAttention consistently ranks first among compression methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TriAttention outperforms all ten baselines across every benchmark and model. Against the two primary baselines, the gap is substantial: on AIME25 (Qwen3-8B), TriAttention scores 32.9% vs. R-KV's 17.5% (+15.4 pp) and SnapKV's 20.0% (+12.9 pp); on AIME24 the margins are 42.1% vs. 25.4% (R-KV) and 34.6% (SnapKV). Against LazyEviction on AIME24 (DS-Qwen, 30% budget), TriAttention achieves 46.7% vs. 43.3%, and at 10% budget leads 40.0% vs. 33.3%. On LongBench (Qwen3-8B, 50% KV), TriAttention attains the highest average of 48.1, surpassing Ada-KV+SnapKV (45.6), PyramidKV (42.7), StreamingLLM (39.4), and KnormPress (35.1); on RULER it leads with 66.1 vs. StreamingLLM's 61.1 and SnapKV's 55.6. H2O, TOVA, and RaaS (all evaluated on AIME24 at 30% budget) score 33.3%, 36.7%, and 36.7% respectively, all well below TriAttention's 46.7%. Notably, TriAttention also matches or exceeds Full Attention at higher budgets (e.g., 43.3% vs. 40.8% on AIME25 at 4096 tokens), indicating that the compression retains all essential information while discarding redundancy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in KV cache compression (SnapKV, R-KV, H2O, LazyEviction) that estimates token importance from post-RoPE attention scores, identifying a fundamental instability: RoPE rotation makes queries position-dependent, limiting useful observations to a tiny window. Instead, it shifts the analysis to the pre-RoPE space, where it discovers that Q/K vectors concentrate around fixed non-zero centers (quantified via Mean Resultant Length from directional statistics), and repurposes the RoPE formula to show these centers determine attention patterns through a trigonometric series in Q-K distance. The norm-based scoring component draws on VATP's observation that value norms complement attention scores, but the adaptive weighting via concentration is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2.5× throughput and 10.7× KV memory reduction while matching Full Attention accuracy on AIME25 is practically significant for deploying reasoning LLMs at scale, and the Q/K concentration discovery is a model-intrinsic property that generalizes across domains (coding, math, chat), architectures (GQA, MLA), and calibration data quality (even Google homepage HTML works). Open questions include whether dedicated hardware-aware kernels can further reduce the scoring overhead, whether head-specific budgets can push efficiency further, and whether the approach extends to non-mathematical reasoning domains like agentic tasks. The paper's real-world demos (OpenClaw on a single RTX 4090, LongLive video generation) suggest immediate deployability, but the method still requires an offline calibration step that, while robust, adds a deployment dependency.
