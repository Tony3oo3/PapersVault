## Problem

In MPPI-based model-based RL, the learned sampling policy and the MPPI planner can diverge, causing the bootstrap value function to be evaluated in underexplored state-action regions and degrading long-term performance. Existing remedies either update the policy purely via value maximization (TD-MPC2) or clone the planner distribution via reverse KL (BMPC), but these represent unexplored extremes of a shared design space. The paper asks whether a principled unification under KL-regularized RL, with intermediate regularization strengths and a learned adaptive prior, can outperform both extremes.

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw state observation vectors (e.g., 21-, 38-, or 61-dimensional for Dog, Humanoid, and H1-hand tasks) encoded into a 512-dim latent representation, along with a learned world model (dynamics and reward networks). At each step it produces a continuous control action sampled from the MPPI planning policy, while simultaneously maintaining a learned Gaussian sampling policy and a KL-regularized action value function. A key structural detail is that the MPPI planning policy (a Gaussian with mean and variance refined over 8 iterations) is stored in the replay buffer and distilled into a separate learned prior network, decoupling the policy update from raw planner statistics.

## Method

PO-MPC casts sampling-policy learning as KL-regularized RL: the policy maximizes a KL-regularized action-value function while being pulled toward a planner-induced prior with strength λ. The planner's per-step Gaussian distribution is distilled into a learned intermediate prior π_θp via either reverse KL (mode-seeking, faster convergence) or forward KL (support-covering, better exploration), which empirically and theoretically reduces gradient variance compared to using raw replay-buffer planner samples directly. The sampling policy update combines three terms—KL-regularized Q-value maximization, reverse KL to the adaptive prior scaled by λ, and entropy regularization—yielding a Plan → Infer → Regularize loop. Setting λ=0 recovers TD-MPC2, λ→∞ with reverse-KL distillation recovers BMPC, and intermediate λ values (0.1–9) consistently outperform both extremes, particularly in high-dimensional HumanoidBench tasks where baselines fail entirely.

## Results

On the 14-task HumanoidBench locomotion suite, PO-MPC with λ=9 achieves an aggregate IQM of 744, substantially outperforming BMPC (590) and TD-MPC2 (255). On the 7-task DMControl Suite, PO-MPC (λ=0.1) reaches an aggregate IQM of 940, marginally ahead of BMPC (936) and clearly ahead of TD-MPC2 (886). The largest individual gains appear in high-dimensional HumanoidBench tasks: H1hand-pole (960 vs. 775 for BMPC), H1hand-slide (733 vs. 314), and H1hand-stair (339 vs. 81), where PO-MPC learns solutions that baselines fail to find.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TD-MPC2 (λ=0, no KL regularization), PO-MPC wins on virtually every task, with the gap most dramatic on HumanoidBench where the aggregate IQM jumps from 255 to 723–744 depending on λ. Against BMPC (λ→∞, pure KL cloning of the planner), PO-MPC with intermediate λ values (1 or 9) achieves a 723–744 aggregate IQM on HumanoidBench versus BMPC's 590, with particularly large margins on H1hand-pole (960 vs. 775), H1hand-slide (733 vs. 314), and H1hand-stair (339 vs. 81). On the lower-dimensional DMControl Suite, PO-MPC (λ=0.1) edges out BMPC by 4 IQM points (940 vs. 936), while both substantially outperform TD-MPC2 (886). In the supplementary comparison against BOOM (Table 4), PO-MPC (λ=1) outperforms BOOM on 11 of 14 reported tasks, with the largest gaps on H1hand-run (907 vs. 330) and H1hand-pole (940 vs. 688). The paper also shows that using a learned intermediate prior π_θp instead of raw replay-buffer planner samples matches or slightly improves performance while significantly reducing the variance of the KL regularization term (Figure 7).

## Relation to Prior Work

PO-MPC unifies TD-MPC2 (Hansen et al., 2024) and BMPC (Wang et al., 2025b) as limiting cases (λ=0 and λ→∞ respectively) of a single KL-regularized RL objective derived from the probabilistic-inference view of RL (Levine, 2018). The key departure from prior work is twofold: (1) it shows that intermediate λ values, previously unexplored, yield the best performance by balancing return maximization against planner alignment, and (2) it introduces a learned adaptive prior π_θp that distills the MPPI planning policy, reducing gradient variance from stale replay-buffer samples compared to directly cloning planner statistics as in BMPC. The framework inherits TD-MPC2's world model architecture and BMPC's lazy reanalyze mechanism, positioning itself as a principled generalization rather than a new architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unification provides a clear design space (λ, prior type, prior training loss) for MPPI-based RL practitioners, and the empirical finding that intermediate λ values enable learning in high-dimensional tasks where both TD-MPC2 and BMPC fail (e.g., Balance Simple, Slide, Stair in HumanoidBench) is practically significant. Key limitations include the manual tuning of λ (no automatic adaptation like SAC's entropy coefficient), the restrictive Gaussian assumption for both sampling and prior policies, and the underutilization of simulated planning trajectories for value learning. Open questions include whether the KL-regularized formulation extends to non-Gaussian or diffusion-based policies, whether λ can be learned online, and whether the adaptive prior's variance-reduction benefit generalizes beyond the specific replay-buffer staleness problem addressed here.
