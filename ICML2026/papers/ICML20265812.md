## Problem

Existing tertiary structure-based RNA design methods rely on static k-nearest-neighbor graphs with fixed connectivity, which injects biochemically irrelevant edges and fails to capture the heterogeneous, context-dependent interactions in RNA tertiary geometry. Compounding this, the scarcity of experimentally resolved RNA 3D structures limits supervised representation learning, making performance sensitive to hand-crafted graph priors. The paper addresses both limitations simultaneously by making graph topology learnable and injecting sequence-level semantic priors from RNA foundation models.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RNA tertiary structure as input, represented by 3D coordinates of six backbone atoms per nucleotide (P, O5', C5', C4', C3', O3'), which are converted into an attributed graph with geometric edge features (distances, angles) constructed via kNN. The output is a per-position nucleotide distribution over {A, U, C, G}, from which the designed sequence is read off. During training, the ground-truth sequence is additionally fed to a frozen RiNALMo 650M teacher to generate FiLM modulation targets, but at inference only the 3D structure is required.

## Method

ATL-FGD builds on the same 6-layer MPNN backbone as RDesign but introduces two key modules. Adaptive Topology Learning (ATL) replaces fixed kNN connectivity with a differentiable edge-gating mechanism at every GNN layer: a lightweight MLP scores each candidate edge from concatenated node states and raw edge features, and a Bernoulli gate with straight-through estimator (training) or a hard threshold τ=0.5 (inference) selects a layer-adaptive subgraph, allowing different layers to emphasize different structural cues. FiLM-Guided Distillation (FGD) avoids direct feature alignment between the sequence-based teacher (RiNALMo 650M) and the structure-based student by mapping both representations through separate FiLM heads to produce scale (γ) and bias (β) parameters, then aligning these parameters via MSE loss; the student's representation is modulated by its own FiLM factors before the prediction head. The total training objective combines standard cross-entropy sequence recovery with the FGD distillation loss weighted by α, and the teacher branch is entirely removed at inference.

## Results

On the RDesign split, ATL-FGD achieves 45.49% overall recovery rate and 44.42 Macro-F1, outperforming all baselines across short, medium, and long sequence lengths. On the gRNAde single-state split, it reaches 50.35% recovery and 53.01 Macro-F1 with only 1.55M parameters. On generalization benchmarks, ATL-FGD attains 64.33% recovery on Rfam and 55.34% on RNA-Puzzles, surpassing the next-best method (R3Design) by 1.56% and 1.00% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ATL-FGD outperforms all six baselines on every benchmark. Against RDesign (same MPNN backbone, fixed kNN graph), it gains +3.96% recovery and +3.53 Macro-F1 on the RDesign split, with the largest margin (+8.17%) on short sequences where fixed kNN noise is most damaging. Compared to gRNAde (multi-conformation GVP), ATL-FGD leads by +2.23% recovery and +1.33 Macro-F1, and on the gRNAde split the gap widens to +8.20% recovery. Versus R3Design (PiGNN-based, 3.23M params), ATL-FGD achieves +1.56% recovery and +1.64 Macro-F1 while using less than half the parameters (1.55M). On the gRNAde split, ATL-FGD also surpasses the diffusion-based RiboDiffusion (15.97M params) by +1.53% recovery and +1.60 Macro-F1, with notably lower variance (±0.41 vs ±2.10). The older baselines GraphTrans and PiFold trail by over 20% in recovery, confirming that modern geometric GNN backbones are essential for this task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the RDesign/gRNAde/R3Design lineage of MPNN-based tertiary structure RNA inverse folding, adopting the same 6-layer MPNN backbone and geometric preprocessing as RDesign. Its two key departures are: (1) replacing the static kNN graph with a differentiable, layer-wise edge-gating mechanism (ATL) that learns a data-dependent subgraph at each GNN layer, and (2) introducing FiLM-Guided Distillation to transfer priors from the RiNALMo RNA foundation model via modulation parameters rather than direct feature alignment, motivated by the receptive-field mismatch between self-attention-based sequence encoders and local message-passing structure encoders. The cross-modal distillation framing draws on the broader KD literature (CMKDNet, X3KD, C2KD) but is the first to apply a FiLM-based soft-modulation distillation interface to biomolecular structure-to-sequence learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The adaptive topology learning mechanism is a general, lightweight technique (a small MLP scorer + straight-through estimator) that could transfer to other graph-based biomolecular tasks where fixed kNN construction is suboptimal, and the FiLM-guided distillation offers a principled alternative to direct feature matching when teacher and student have fundamentally different inductive biases. However, the method remains unvalidated in wet-lab settings, relies on a single RNA foundation model (RiNALMo 650M) as teacher, and the structural fidelity metrics (mean RMSD ~10.28 Å) indicate that designed sequences still fold with substantial deviation from targets, particularly for long RNAs. Open questions include whether the adaptive topology generalizes to protein inverse folding or joint RNA-protein co-design, and whether the distillation benefit persists when the teacher model is replaced or when training data scales up.
