## Problem

While LoRA is the standard parameter-efficient finetuning method, it remains unclear how the optimal learning rate should scale with adapter rank, forcing practitioners to re-tune the learning rate from scratch whenever the rank changes. The relationship between learning rate and rank changes qualitatively depending on initialization and scaling-factor choices, and no principled rule exists for transferring a learning rate tuned under LoRA to full finetuning despite the two methods being closely related.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260606/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes as input the model width n, LoRA rank r, initialization scheme (Init[A] or Init[B]), and rank-dependent scaling factor α = r^{-γ}, and produces an explicit asymptotic scaling prescription for the optimal learning rate η in the joint limit n, r → ∞. The output is a functional form (e.g., η ∝ n^{-1/2} r^{-1/2} or η ∝ n^{-1}) rather than a single numerical value, specifying how η must be adjusted when any hyperparameter changes. A key output is a transfer rule: under Init[B] with α = 1, the optimal LoRA learning rate matches the FFT optimum (both scale as n^{-1}), providing a necessary condition for zero-shot learning rate transfer between the two regimes.

## Method

The paper introduces Maximal-Update Adaptation (μA), a theoretical framework that analyzes LoRA finetuning dynamics in the joint limit n, r → ∞ by decomposing the per-step feature update ΔZ_B^t into three contributions (δ¹, δ², δ³) corresponding to linear updates in A, linear updates in B, and their multiplicative interaction. Using SignSGD as a tractable proxy for Adam (preserving element-wise gradient normalization), the analysis derives conditions under which each term remains Θ(1), yielding two distinct scaling regimes: under Init[A], the optimal learning rate scales as η ∝ n^{-1/2} r^{-(1-γ)/2} (rank-dependent for α=1, rank-invariant for α=r^{-1}), while under Init[B] with α=1, it scales as η ∝ n^{-1} (rank-invariant). The rank-invariant Init[B] regime matches the width scaling of full finetuning under the same SignSGD abstraction, establishing a necessary condition for learning rate transfer from LoRA to FFT. The framework is validated across language, vision, vision-language, image generation, and reinforcement-learning tasks, confirming that the derived scaling rules generalize across architectures and training paradigms.

## Results

The paper derives and empirically validates three learning-rate scaling rules for LoRA: under Init[A] with α=1, the optimal LR scales as η ∝ n^(-1/2)·r^(-1/2) (a 4× rank increase requires halving the LR); under Init[A] with α=r^(-1), the LR is rank-invariant at η ∝ n^(-1/2); and under Init[B] with α=1, the LR is rank-invariant at η ∝ n^(-1) and matches the FFT optimum. Across five model families (Llama-3.2-1B, Qwen2.5-3B, RoBERTa-large, ViT-Huge/14, Qwen3-VL-2B) and three training paradigms (SFT, RLVR, diffusion), the observed optima cluster within one log₂ grid step (2×) of the predicted values. In RLVR on Llama-3.1-8B, suboptimal LRs increase wall-clock training time by 2–4×, and FFT consumes over 2× the peak GPU memory of LoRA (r=256).

**Table 1: μA scaling rules for Init[A] with α = r^(-γ).**

| γ | α | Optimal η | Intermediate feature Z_A^t |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 1 | n^(-1/2) · r^(-1/2) | n^(1/2) · r^(-1/2) |
| 1/2 | r^(-1/2) | n^(-1/2) · r^(-1/4) | n^(1/2) · r^(-1/4) |
| 1 | r^(-1) | n^(-1/2) | n^(1/2) |

**Init[B] with α=1 (Corollary 4.6):** η = Θ(n^(-1)), Z_A^t = Θ(1), δ¹ = Θ(1), δ² = Θ(r·n^(-1)), δ³ = Θ(r^(1/2)·n^(-1)).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against external PEFT methods; instead it compares three standard LoRA configurations (Init[A] α=1, Init[A] α=r^(-1), Init[B] α=1) against each other and against full finetuning (FFT). Init[B] with α=1 is the only configuration whose optimal LR is both rank-invariant and aligned with the FFT optimum, enabling direct LR transfer from LoRA to FFT without re-tuning; the other two configurations either require rank-dependent LR adjustment (Init[A] α=1) or have a different width scaling (η ∝ n^(-1/2) vs. n^(-1) for FFT). Empirically, Init[B] α=1 also proves more tolerant of larger LRs (wider stable range) than Init[A] α=r^(-1), consistent with its order-one intermediate features. In the RLVR setting, the Init[B] α=1 optimum coincides with the FFT optimum on Llama-3.1-8B/GSM8k, confirming transferability in a non-SFT regime. An ablation replacing AdamW with SignSGD (the optimizer assumed in theory) reproduces the same scaling patterns, validating the theoretical abstraction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the infinite-width analysis of LoRA finetuning from Hayou et al. (2024a), which studied the n→∞ limit with rank r held fixed, to the joint n,r→∞ limit where both width and rank grow. The three-term decomposition of LoRA feature updates (δ¹, δ², δ³) is inherited from Hayou et al. (2024b), and the overall stability/non-vanishing framework is directly analogous to μP (Yang & Hu, 2020) for pretraining. The key conceptual departure is treating rank as a scaling variable rather than a fixed hyperparameter, which reveals qualitatively different learning rate scaling regimes depending on initialization and the α multiplier. The connection to FFT learning rate transfer builds on the hyperparameter transfer framework of Yang et al. (2021) but applies it to the LoRA-to-FFT setting, which Schulman (2025) had flagged as an open problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: practitioners no longer need to re-tune learning rates when changing LoRA rank or switching between LoRA and full finetuning, which reduces expensive hyperparameter sweeps across diverse architectures. The identification of Init[B] with α=1 as the configuration enabling LoRA-to-FFT learning rate transfer provides a concrete, actionable recipe. Key limitations include the asymptotic nature of the theory (sensitive at finite n,r), the single-layer analysis (multi-layer backward dynamics remain unproven), and the fact that the FFT transfer is established as a necessary but not sufficient condition. Open questions include extending the analysis to other optimizer families (SGD, Muon, LARS), proving sufficient conditions for transfer, and understanding how heterogeneous widths in architectures like UNets interact with the per-layer scaling rules.
