## Problem

Benchmark datasets for LLM evaluation are increasingly contaminated by pretraining corpora, with detected contamination reaching up to 45% on common benchmarks, rendering them unreliable measures of model generalization. Existing mitigations—private benchmarking, dynamic refresh, and decontamination filtering—either stifle open verification, break longitudinal comparability, or fail at the scale of trillion-token corpora. The paper argues for a fundamental shift: benchmarks should be released in a contamination-resistant form that is unlearnable during training yet fully usable for inference.

## Input / Output

The target model never receives plaintext benchmark questions; instead it is fed pre-computed KV cache pairs {K_{1:t}^{(l)}, V_{1:t}^{(l)}} for all layers plus the penultimate-layer hidden state h_t^{(L-1)} of the final token, all projected through a designated anchor model. The ground-truth answer Y is released in plaintext so standard scoring (exact match, semantic similarity) applies unchanged. The model generates autoregressive continuations from this latent prefix, and the resulting text is evaluated against Y—meaning the input is a fixed-size tensor bundle (on the order of hundreds of MB per 100K-token question after PyramidKV compression) rather than a token sequence.

## Method

The core mechanism exploits the training-inference asymmetry in Transformers: training requires the full token sequence to compute per-position hidden states and loss, while inference only needs the KV cache and the penultimate hidden state of the last token to generate subsequent tokens autoregressively. By releasing only these inference-sufficient components and withholding the raw token sequence, the benchmark becomes unlearnable (no gradient signal can be computed) while remaining fully functional for evaluation. For cross-model interoperability, the paper proposes two paradigms: a near-term anchor-model approach that translates KV caches via subspace alignment (adapting Cross-LoRA's rank-truncated SVD and Procrustes-style linear maps between anchor and target weights), and a longer-term relative-representation approach that projects all models into a shared coordinate frame using anchor-sample similarity vectors, enabling zero-shot stitching without model-specific encodings.

## Results

This is a position paper and does not present original experimental results or quantitative benchmarks. The paper cites contamination prevalence data from prior work (e.g., up to 45% contamination on common benchmarks per Li et al. 2024, 90%+ in GPT-3 evaluations per Brown et al. 2020, 91.8% across multilingual benchmarks per Ahuja et al. 2024) to motivate the need for contamination-resistant datasets. The only quantitative detail specific to the proposed CRD framework is a storage estimate: using PyramidKV compression, the KV-cache projection for 100K tokens in LLaMA-2 7B reduces from ~50 GB to approximately 350 MB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

As a position paper, this work does not conduct head-to-head experimental comparisons against baselines. Instead, it qualitatively contrasts the proposed contamination-resistant dataset (CRD) approach against two families of existing alternatives: (1) Data Curation methods (private benchmarking via TRUCE, dynamic benchmarking via AntiLeakBench/CoreEval), which the authors argue introduce cost barriers, limit testing frequency, or create moving targets that complicate longitudinal tracking; and (2) Data Refactoring methods (lexical obfuscation, decontamination/filtering), which the authors argue are degraded by LLMs' robustness to paraphrasing and by the scale of modern trillions-of-token corpora. The paper positions CRDs as superior in principle because they exploit the Transformer's training-inference asymmetry to make data unlearnable while preserving inference utility, but no empirical validation of this claim is provided.

## Relation to Prior Work

This position paper departs from the dominant contamination-mitigation strategies (private benchmarking, dynamic refresh, decontamination filtering) by proposing a fundamentally different paradigm: releasing benchmarks in a latent form that is architecturally unlearnable yet inference-usable. The technical mechanism exploits the training/inference asymmetry in Transformers—releasing only the KV cache and penultimate-layer hidden state rather than raw token sequences—while the interoperability layer draws directly on Cross-LoRA's subspace alignment (Xia et al., 2025) for near-term cross-model translation and on relative representations (Moschella et al., 2023) for a longer-term model-agnostic coordinate frame. The formalization of contamination resistance with three properties (irreversibility, equivalence, interoperability) synthesizes ideas from unlearnable data, model stitching, and the Platonic Representation Hypothesis into a unified benchmarking framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Benchmark contamination has reached levels (up to 45–91% on popular suites) that fundamentally undermine the reliability of LLM evaluation, and the paper's proposed mechanism—exploiting a structural property of Transformers rather than relying on data secrecy or constant refresh—offers a potentially permanent solution. However, as a position paper it provides no end-to-end empirical validation: the full pipeline (curation → translation → evaluation) remains untested, and critical open questions persist around irreversibility under inversion attacks, accumulated drift in long autoregressive generations from translated caches, storage overhead at scale, and inapplicability to non-Transformer architectures like Mamba. The approach's practical adoption hinges on the community standardizing anchor models and integrating CRD support into platforms like Hugging Face, which the paper calls for but cannot guarantee.
