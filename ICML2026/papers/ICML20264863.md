## Problem

Safety alignment in LLMs is brittle: a model may refuse a harmful request in a canonical prompt but comply when the same intent is wrapped in adversarial or jailbreak-style framing. The paper identifies that standard symmetric invariance regularizers (e.g., V-REx) fail in this setting because supervision reliability is asymmetric across prompt variants—verifiable formats provide trustworthy signals while open-ended variants rely on noisy, gameable reward proxies—so symmetric penalties can reduce cross-context gaps by degrading reliable anchors rather than improving open-ended robustness.

## Input / Output

The method consumes heterogeneous prompt meta-groups: for each latent intent (a safety scenario, moral dilemma, or math problem), it pairs verifiable anchor prompts (multiple-choice, True/False, or rule-checkable boolean traps) with open-ended variants (standard instruction-following or jailbreak-wrapped prompts). The model generates chain-of-thought responses in a strict <think>...<answer>...</answer> format (with \boxed{} for math), and the output is evaluated for both task correctness and cross-variant consistency—i.e., the model must solve all surface realizations of the same intent correctly, not just individual prompts.

## Method

The core contribution is Anchor Invariance Regularization (AIR), an asymmetric invariance objective that designates verifiable prompt contexts as anchors and applies a stop-gradient target to regularize only open-ended variants toward the anchor's performance level. This breaks the symmetry of standard V-REx penalties: the stop-gradient ensures the regularizer's gradient is zero with respect to the anchor, so it can only push open contexts toward the verifiable standard rather than degrading the anchor to match noisy variants. AIR is implemented as an optimizer-agnostic auxiliary loss (a weighted log-likelihood term whose sign flips based on whether the open context underperforms or spuriously outperforms the anchor) and integrated into GRPO via heterogeneous prompt grouping, where each training batch contains both anchor and open variants for the same latent intent, enabling simultaneous estimation of the anchor-open gap and group-relative advantages.

## Results

On the primary invariance metric (Acc_group, the fraction of meta-groups where all prompt variants are solved correctly), GSPO+AIR achieves 98.08% ID Safety and 63.73% OOD Safety, while GRPO+AIR reaches 84.62% ID Safety and 60.78% OOD Safety. Compared to the GRPO baseline, AIR boosts average OOD Acc_group from 23.05% to 56.54% (+33.49 pp) and average OOD Acc from 72.67% to 85.81%. The symmetric V-REx baseline consistently degrades performance (e.g., GRPO ID Safety Acc_group drops from 71.15% to 35.40%), confirming the anchor-degrading failure mode. AIR also surpasses all closed-source LLMs on OOD Acc_group for Safety (60.78% vs. GPT-5.2's 26.47%) and Moral (47.79% vs. GPT-5.2's 25.00%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the GRPO baseline, AIR improves ID Safety Acc_group from 71.15% to 84.62% (+13.47 pp) and OOD Safety Acc_group from 13.73% to 60.78% (+47.05 pp); the analogous GSPO+AIR gains are from 70.19% to 98.08% (ID) and 16.67% to 63.73% (OOD). The symmetric V-REx invariance penalty (Krueger et al., 2021) consistently hurts performance—GRPO+V-REx drops ID Safety Acc_group to 35.40% and ID Moral Acc_group to 7.15%—validating the paper's theoretical analysis that symmetric penalties degrade anchors. Against closed-source models, GRPO+AIR surpasses GPT-5.2-chat-latest on OOD Safety Acc_group (60.78% vs. 26.47%) and OOD Moral Acc_group (47.79% vs. 25.00%), while GPT-5.2 retains an edge on OOD Math Acc_group (88.50% vs. 61.06%). GSPO+AIR outperforms all open-source baselines (Qwen-3-14B, Qwen-2.5-14B-Instruct, Llama-4-Maverick) by large margins on every Acc_group metric, with the gap most pronounced on Safety (e.g., 98.08% vs. 0.00% for Qwen-3-14B ID). On Math, where most variants are automatically verifiable and the supervision asymmetry is minimal, all methods converge to similar performance, and AIR's gains are modest (e.g., GSPO+AIR 68.14% vs. GSPO 65.49% ID Acc_group), consistent with the paper's analysis that the reliability gap drives the benefit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of invariant risk minimization (IRM/V-REx) from domain generalization and group-based preference optimization (GRPO/GSPO) from LLM alignment. Its key conceptual departure is identifying that the symmetric variance penalty in V-REx admits degenerate descent directions that degrade reliable (verifiable) contexts to match noisy ones—a failure mode formalized in Theorem A.3. By breaking this symmetry with a stop-gradient anchor reference, AIR repurposes the invariance-regularization idea for the asymmetric supervision structure inherent in safety alignment, where multiple-choice or rule-checkable prompts provide ground truth while open-ended variants rely on gameable LLM-judge proxies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical gap: models that pass safety benchmarks in canonical form but fail under adversarial rephrasing. The formal proof that symmetric invariance penalties actively harm anchor performance, combined with the empirical confirmation (V-REx drops GRPO Safety Acc_group from 71% to 35%), provides a clear diagnostic for why naive robustness regularization backfires. Limitations include evaluation on a single 14B backbone and the assumption that at least one verifiable anchor exists per intent; open questions include how AIR behaves when no clean anchor is available, whether the approach transfers to larger models or multimodal settings, and whether the heterogeneous grouping overhead scales to production training pipelines.
