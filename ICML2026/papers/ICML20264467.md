## Problem

Video generation models have demonstrated emergent Chain-of-Frame (CoF) reasoning, yet their potential for text-to-image generation remains unexplored due to the absence of a clearly defined visual reasoning starting point and interpretable intermediate states in the T2I process. Existing inference-time reasoning approaches for T2I rely on frequent modality switching between vision and language or external verifiers, making pixel-level corrections indirect and lossy, while unified MLLMs lack pretraining on purely visual, causally ordered refinement sequences.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single text prompt (prefixed with a fixed system instruction to generate a refinement chain) and produces one 1024×1024 RGB image. Internally, the model generates a three-frame latent sequence where each frame represents a progressive reasoning step from coarse semantics to fine-grained aesthetics; only the final frame's latent is decoded into the output image, while intermediate latents serve solely as internal visual reasoning states. All training and inference operate at square 1024×1024 resolution to avoid incomplete subject rendering from default rectangular video aspect ratios.

## Method

CoF-T2I repurposes the Wan2.1-T2V-14B video backbone as a pure visual reasoner by reframing T2I generation as a three-frame CoF refinement trajectory (coarse draft → intermediate refinement → high-fidelity final image), trained with standard rectified flow matching on the curated CoF-Evol-Instruct dataset (64K three-frame sequences). A key architectural choice is frame-wise independent VAE encoding: the causal video VAE is slid along the temporal axis so each frame is compressed within a single-frame context window, decoupling reasoning steps and suppressing motion artifacts that continuous spatiotemporal compression would introduce. The CoF-Evol-Instruct dataset is constructed via a quality-aware pipeline that samples anchor images from multiple T2I models, routes them by quality level (semantically misaligned, visually unrefined, high fidelity) into three adaptive construction strategies (forward refinement, bidirectional completion, backward synthesis), and uses a Unified Editing Primitive—a closed-loop planner-editor-verifier agent system—to ensure progressive, category-conditioned refinement across all frames. At inference, the model denoises the full three-frame latent sequence from Gaussian noise, and only the terminal latent is decoded to yield the final image.

## Results

On GenEval, CoF-T2I achieves a leading overall score of 0.86, surpassing the strongest unified MLLM baseline BLIP3-o 8B (0.84) and the base video model Wan2.1-T2V-14B (0.55). On Imagine-Bench, CoF-T2I reaches 7.468 overall, a substantial improvement over the Wan2.1 baseline (5.939) and the best unified MLLM BAGEL-Think (6.930). The gains are especially pronounced on compositional sub-tasks: Multi-Object on Imagine-Bench jumps from 5.383 (Wan2.1) to 7.797, and Position on GenEval rises from 0.18 to 0.83.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoF-T2I outperforms all nine baselines on both GenEval and Imagine-Bench. Against standard image models, it leads SDXL (0.55), SD3-Medium (0.74), and FLUX.1-dev (0.67) by 0.12–0.31 on GenEval overall. Among unified MLLMs, it surpasses the closest competitor BLIP3-o 8B (0.84) by 0.02 on GenEval and BAGEL-Think (6.930) by 0.538 on Imagine-Bench, while also beating T2I-R1 (0.79 / 6.780) and OmniGen2 (0.80 / 6.220). The most dramatic gain is over its own video backbone Wan2.1-T2V-14B, which scores only 0.55 on GenEval and 5.939 on Imagine-Bench, demonstrating that the CoF fine-tuning and frame-wise encoding contribute +0.31 and +1.529 respectively. Notably, CoF-T2I's pure visual reasoning paradigm (no textual CoT, no external verifier) matches or exceeds methods that rely on interleaved textual planning, suggesting that frame-by-frame visual self-correction is a competitive alternative to language-mediated reasoning for T2I generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Chain-of-Frame (CoF) reasoning paradigm—originally demonstrated for video tasks like maze solving (Wiedemer et al., 2025)—as a mechanism for text-to-image generation, departing from prior T2I reasoning approaches that rely on external verifiers or textual Chain-of-Thought within unified MLLMs (T2I-R1, BAGEL-Think, TwiG). Built on the Wan2.1 video generation backbone with Rectified Flow training, it introduces a frame-wise independent VAE encoding to suppress motion artifacts, and a new 64K-scale dataset (CoF-Evol-Instruct) of progressive three-frame refinement trajectories that do not exist in prior T2I corpora. The key conceptual departure is eliminating all textual intermediaries and external reward signals, treating the video model's native spatiotemporal sequence processing as a pure visual reasoning engine.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a video model fine-tuned on 64K structured trajectories can match or slightly exceed unified MLLMs using textual CoT (0.86 vs. 0.84 on GenEval) suggests that pixel-level iterative self-correction may be a more direct path to prompt fidelity than language-mediated planning. However, the margins are thin, the reasoning chain is fixed at three frames, and the approach has not been extended to video generation, 3D synthesis, or longer chains. Open questions include whether reinforcement learning can further sharpen the refinement trajectory, whether the paradigm scales beyond 1024×1024, and whether the large +0.57 gain on the 1.3B model generalizes to other small backbones—potentially making CoF-T2I a practical recipe for resource-constrained settings.
