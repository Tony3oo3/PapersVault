## Problem

Block-sparse attention for long-context LLM pre-filling relies on mean pooling as a coarse-grained proxy for block importance estimation, but this proxy is systematically inaccurate: the paper proves that mean pooling acts as a low-pass filter under RoPE, causing destructive interference that erases high-frequency positional signals. This spectral 'blind spot' forces existing methods to fall back on expensive token-level search and verification, whose overhead often negates the sparsity gains at moderate sequence lengths.

## Input / Output

Prism takes the query and key matrices (Q, K ∈ R^{L×d}) along with a block size B=128 and a top-p threshold (0.93–0.95 depending on the model), and produces a binary block-sparse mask M ∈ {0,1}^{N×N} with N = ⌈L/B⌉. The spectral bands are configured as d_high=64 and d_low=96, deliberately overlapping the transition zone (dimensions 32–64) so both branches cover recovering positional signals. All operations are performed exclusively at the block level via custom Triton kernels, avoiding any token-level access that would inflate selection latency.

## Method

Prism is a training-free, spectral-aware framework that decomposes block importance estimation into two parallel branches. It first proves that mean pooling over a RoPE-embedded block produces a Dirichlet-kernel attenuation factor λ_j(B) that drives high-frequency dimensions (the 'Dead Zone', roughly the first 30 real dimensions for base 1M) to near-zero magnitude via destructive interference. To counteract this, Prism slices Q and K into a high-frequency band (first d_high dims) and a low-frequency band (last d_low dims), pools each independently, and computes per-band attention logits. A derived energy-based temperature τ_z = √(d_z/d)·RMS(Q̄_z)/RMS(Q̄_full)·RMS(K̄_z)/RMS(K̄_full) rescales each branch's logits to match the full-spectrum magnitude, sharpening the softmax so that top-p selection can distinguish true positional signals from noise. The final mask is the union of the two branch-specific top-p selections, requiring no hyperparameter tuning and no token-level operations.

## Results

On PG19 language modeling, Prism maintains perplexity virtually identical to full attention (ΔPPL ≈ 0) across all context lengths while delivering a 5.1× speedup at 128K tokens, compared to 3.0× for XAttention. On LongBench, Prism achieves average scores of 41.08 (Llama-3.1-8B) and 39.12 (Qwen-3-8B), showing <0.4% degradation versus full attention. On VideoMME, Prism matches full attention overall (71.22) and surpasses it on the Long split (64.00 vs. 63.11). Estimation overhead is the lowest among all methods, scaling gracefully with sequence length and requiring only ~20% of the memory used by FlexPrefill at 128K.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FlashAttention-2 (full attention baseline), Prism achieves 5.1× prefilling speedup at 128K tokens while maintaining accuracy parity (ΔPPL ≈ 0 on PG19, <0.4% drop on LongBench). Versus MInference, Prism matches accuracy on LongBench (41.08 vs. 41.14) but avoids MInference's degeneration to full attention at moderate sequence lengths, yielding consistent speedups where MInference only outperforms FlashAttention at 64K+. Against FlexPrefill, Prism substantially outperforms on LongBench (41.08 vs. 33.90 on Llama; 39.12 vs. 36.13 on Qwen) and achieves speedups at all sequence lengths, whereas FlexPrefill only surpasses FlashAttention at 32K+. Compared to XAttention, Prism matches or slightly exceeds accuracy (41.08 vs. 39.68 on LongBench) while delivering significantly higher speedup at extreme lengths (5.1× vs. 3.0× at 128K) due to purely block-level operations versus XAttention's token-level antidiagonal scoring. Against PBS-Attn, Prism achieves comparable LongBench scores (41.08 vs. 40.94) with lower estimation overhead, and extends its advantage to video generation where Prism at threshold 0.93 outperforms XAttention at 0.90 on PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS while increasing speedup from 1.54× to 1.60×.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the block-sparse attention line (MInference, FlexPrefill, XAttention, PBS-Attn) that uses mean-pooled coarse-grained attention as a proxy for block importance estimation, but departs by identifying a previously unacknowledged theoretical failure mode: mean pooling under RoPE acts as a low-pass filter that destroys high-frequency positional signals via destructive interference. Rather than treating vertical-slash and block-sparse patterns as head-level dichotomies requiring separate heuristics (as in MInference/FlexPrefill) or token-level antidiagonal scoring (XAttention), Prism reframes the problem spectrally, showing both patterns coexist within individual heads and can be recovered via dual-band scoring with energy-based calibration. The approach also inherits FlashAttention's block-tiling granularity and extends the spectral heterogeneity analysis of RoPE (Liu et al., 2024) to a practical estimation framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 5.1× speedup at 128K tokens with full-attention accuracy parity, achieved through purely block-level operations (no token-level search or verification), addresses the core practical bottleneck that has kept sparse attention methods from outperforming optimized FlashAttention at moderate sequence lengths. The theoretical characterization of mean pooling as a low-pass filter under RoPE provides a principled explanation for why prior methods required expensive corrections and suggests a general design principle for any pooling-based estimation under rotary embeddings. Open questions include whether the spectral split transfers to non-RoPE positional encodings (ALiBi, T5 relative bias), whether the locally stationary content assumption degrades in highly non-stationary domains, and whether the approach extends to the decoding phase where block structure is less natural. The denoising effect (Prism slightly outperforming full attention on specific tasks) hints at broader implications for sparse attention as a regularizer, though this is underexplored.
