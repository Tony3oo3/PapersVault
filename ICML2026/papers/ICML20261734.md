## Problem

Prior security and steganography research on diffusion models focuses on model parameters, latent representations, or output images, while the timestep embedding—a critical conditioning signal broadcast to every denoising block—remains an unexplored information channel. Because sinusoidal positional encodings use a maximum period of 10,000 but standard diffusion models only activate timesteps in [0, 1000], a large portion of the embedding spectrum is unused, raising the question of whether this temporal subspace can carry independent, controllable information without altering the observable sampling procedure.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard diffusion inputs (text prompts or class labels) together with a timestep value that is either in the explicit range [0, T₀] or a shadow range [fₙ, fₙ + T₀] where fₙ is an integer-multiple-of-1000 offset. The scheduler interface remains completely unchanged from the user's perspective; the model internally maps the scheduler-issued timestep t to a shifted index t_sn, so the same numerical integration path activates an entirely different learned distribution. Outputs are generated images that follow either the clean explicit distribution, a poisoned adversarial distribution, or a watermarked distribution, depending on which temporal interval is active.

## Method

STE extends the timestep domain by introducing shadow offsets fₙ that map the standard interval [0, T₀] to disjoint shadow intervals [fₙ, fₙ + T₀], and trains the denoising network with a joint loss that simultaneously fits the explicit distribution D₀ under t ∈ [0, T₀] and each shadow distribution D_sn under t_sn ∈ [fₙ, Tₙ]. The key theoretical result proves that sinusoidal positional encodings with geometric frequency progression yield near-orthogonal embeddings (mutual coherence ≈ 0.1) for timesteps separated by ≥ 1000, guaranteeing that shadow and explicit manifolds remain linearly separable. At inference, the scheduler operates normally over [T₀, …, 0] while the model receives the shifted index t_sn, so the offset fₙ acts as a 'temporal key' that toggles between clean and shadow generation without any modification to the scheduler or model interface. The approach generalizes to continuous-time flow matching models by applying a continuous offset t_s = t + δ, and is validated as both a covert backdoor attack (99.2% ASR on CIFAR-10) and a watermark verification mechanism.

## Results

On the attack task, STE-shadow achieves an Attack Success Rate (ASR) of 99.2% on CIFAR-10 and 98.8% on CelebA-HQ, while the explicit timestep pathway maintains near-zero ASR (0.2% and 0.4%) with the best FID among all methods (21.82 on CIFAR-10, 6.23 on CelebA-HQ). For watermarking, STE achieves an average detection accuracy of 0.89 across five distortions, trailing ROBIN (0.98) and Tree-Ring (0.95), with notable vulnerability to cropping (0.71) and brightening (0.85). On general multi-distribution generation, the full STE setting [E,S,S] achieves FID 21.82 / ACC 75.65% on CIFAR-10, FID 1.85 / ACC 97.77% on MNIST, and FID 6.56 / ACC 88.76% on Fashion-MNIST, matching or exceeding single-dataset baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against backdoor attack baselines, STE-shadow outperforms VillanDiffusion on both ASR (99.2% vs. 96.2% on CIFAR-10; 98.8% vs. 97.7% on CelebA-HQ) and FID (22.07 vs. 25.66 on CIFAR-10), while matching BadDiffusion's ASR (99.2% vs. 99.5% on CIFAR-10; 98.8% vs. 98.7% on CelebA-HQ) with a lower FID (22.07 vs. 22.53; 6.78 vs. 7.65). The key differentiator is that STE-explicit simultaneously achieves the best clean-generation FID (21.82 / 6.23) with negligible ASR, demonstrating that the attack is fully contained in the shadow interval without degrading normal operation. On the watermarking task, STE underperforms all three baselines in average robustness (0.89 vs. ROBIN 0.98, Tree-Ring 0.95, SleeperMark 0.94), primarily due to its vulnerability to cropping (0.71) and brightening (0.85), which the authors attribute to using a pixel-visible watermark rather than a frequency-domain one. Overall, STE wins on the attack stealth and FID metrics but loses on watermark robustness, positioning it as a stronger covert-injection mechanism than a state-of-the-art watermarking scheme.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DDPM's sinusoidal timestep embedding mechanism, identifying the unused portion of the embedding spectrum (timesteps 1000–10000) as an underutilized information channel. It departs from prior diffusion-model steganography and backdoor work (StegaDDPM, CRoSS, VillanDiffusion, BadDiffusion), which operate in pixel, conditioning, or parameter space, by relocating the injection surface to the temporal conditioning pathway itself. The mutual coherence theorem providing a formal separability guarantee for disjoint sinusoidal intervals is a new theoretical contribution not present in prior diffusion security literature, and the method is shown to extend to flow matching beyond discrete-time DDPMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reveals a previously overlooked security surface in diffusion pipelines: the timestep embedding can carry an entire independent data distribution invisible to standard scheduler monitoring, with implications for both covert backdoor attacks and in-process watermarking. However, practical impact is tempered by several limitations: the watermark robustness (0.89 avg) lags behind Tree-Ring (0.95) and ROBIN (0.98), the attack requires full model retraining rather than inference-time manipulation, and DPM-Solver-based sampling degrades substantially. Open questions include whether supply-chain code poisoning can realistically deploy STE in production pipelines, how to design comprehensive temporal-provenance defenses, and whether the approach generalizes to more complex (frequency- or latent-level) watermark patterns.
