## Problem

All existing adversarial attacks on Hypergraph Neural Networks (HGNNs) are white-box and target either structural or feature perturbations exclusively, yet in realistic deployments (e.g., recommender systems, biomedical digital twins) attackers lack access to proprietary model parameters. Moreover, no adversarial training mechanism exists for HGNNs, leaving a critical defense gap. The paper also shows that graph-based attacks cannot be directly transferred to hypergraphs because the hypergraph Laplacian is sensitive to a coupling of node and hyperedge degrees, unlike the graph Laplacian which depends only on node degrees.

## Input / Output

The attack takes a hypergraph specified by its binary incidence matrix H ∈ {0,1}^{n×m}, node feature matrix X ∈ R^{n×F}, labeled training nodes, and explicit perturbation budgets δ_H (l0 structural flip budget) and δ_X (l∞ feature budget), and outputs a perturbed incidence matrix and feature matrix. The defense (MeLA-D) additionally outputs a robust HGNN classifier trained via iterative surrogate retraining on the generated adversarial hypergraphs. Structural perturbations are discretized as binary flips of at most δ_H incidence entries, while feature perturbations are projected onto the l∞ ball of radius δ_X after each gradient step.

## Method

MeLA optimizes a three-component meta-objective — Laplacian smoothing loss (measuring drift in Laplacian-smoothed embeddings between clean and perturbed hypergraphs), stealth violation loss (penalizing node-degree vector shifts), and cross-entropy classification loss — rather than directly maximizing the task loss, with a fixed LinHGNN surrogate providing gradients in the gray-box setting. MeLA-PGD performs multi-step projected gradient updates with a top-δ_H flip selection rule (derived in Proposition 1) at each iteration, while MeLA-FGSM reduces to a single-step sign update. The defense MeLA-D alternates between generating worst-case perturbations via the same meta-objective and retraining the target HGNN on the perturbed hypergraph for τ_d epochs per iteration, with convergence to a first-order stationary point proven under Lipschitz smoothness and local concavity assumptions. Theoretical bounds (Theorems 1–2) establish that hypergraph Laplacian drift scales with the product of row and column perturbation budgets and is amplified at low-degree nodes and small hyperedges, motivating the operator-level sensitivity captured by the Laplacian smoothing loss.

## Results

On Cora-CA, MeLA-PGD achieves up to 75.9% relative accuracy drop in the evasion setting (HGNN target: 82.90% → 19.97%) and up to 14.5% drop in poisoning (AllSetTransformer: 83.66% → 71.91%). On Citeseer, MeLA-PGD reaches 60.1% poisoning drop (AllSetTransformer) and 44.6% evasion drop (HGNN). The MeLA-D defense recovers up to 41.6% accuracy under MeLA-PGD poisoning on Cora-CA (25.05% → 66.65%), and mini-batch MeLA-PGD scales to OGBN-MAG (736K nodes) with 8.6% evasion drop in ~80 minutes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MeLA-PGD dominates all five baselines (RandFlip, RandFeat, GradArgMax, DICE, HyperAttack) across every dataset, target model, and attack setting. In evasion on Cora-CA, MeLA-PGD achieves 61.5–75.9% accuracy drops versus 0–7.3% for the best baseline (RandFlip), a gap of over 60 percentage points. In poisoning on Citeseer, MeLA-PGD reaches 15.4–60.1% drops while the strongest baseline (HyperAttack) manages only 0–12.9%. MeLA-FGSM, the single-step variant, also outperforms all baselines in evasion (34–72% drops) but is consistently weaker than MeLA-PGD in poisoning, confirming the importance of multi-step optimization for exploiting nonlinear Laplacian effects. The defense MeLA-D+HyperMLP recovers 22–42% accuracy under MeLA attacks versus only 2–3% recovery under weaker baselines, indicating the defense is most critical against the strongest threats.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the meta-learning attack paradigm of Zügner & Günnemann (ICLR 2019) from graphs to hypergraphs, but argues that direct adaptation is fundamentally problematic due to the non-uniqueness of graph-to-hypergraph projection and the distinct sensitivity of the hypergraph Laplacian (which couples node and hyperedge degrees) versus the graph Laplacian (which depends only on node degrees). The single-step and multi-step perturbation strategies inherit from FGSM (Goodfellow et al., 2015) and PGD (Madry et al., 2018), respectively, but are re-anchored on a novel Laplacian-smoothing meta-objective specific to hypergraph structure. Unlike prior HGNN attacks (HyperAttack, MGHGA, DGHSA), which are white-box and target either structure or features exclusively, MeLA operates in the gray-box setting and simultaneously perturbs both the incidence matrix and node features under explicit budgets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first gray-box attack framework and the first adversarial training mechanism for HGNNs, filling a gap that becomes critical as HGNNs are deployed in high-stakes domains like recommender systems and biomedical digital twins where adversarial failures can cause revenue loss or misdiagnosis. The theoretical result that hypergraph Laplacian drift scales with the product of row and column perturbation budgets (rather than either alone) provides a principled explanation for why balanced structural attacks are more potent and why degree-only defenses are insufficient. Key open questions include the absence of robustness certification, the risk that structural perturbations break label-invariant semantics during adversarial training, and whether the mini-batch scaling strategy preserves attack effectiveness on hypergraphs with heterogeneous edge sizes.
