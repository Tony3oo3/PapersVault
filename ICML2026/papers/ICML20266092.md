## Problem

Existing RoPE extrapolation methods for Diffusion Transformers apply scale-agnostic positional modulation schedules regardless of target resolution, ignoring the fact that larger resolutions delay structural lock-in during denoising. This mismatch causes either premature relaxation of guidance (leading to structural collapse and geometric repetition) or excessive intervention into late denoising (causing textural degradation), particularly at ultra-high resolutions and extreme aspect ratios.

## Input / Output

Given a text prompt and a target resolution (which may be anisotropic, e.g., 4096×1024 at 4:1 aspect ratio), the method produces a high-resolution RGB image up to 16 megapixels using a pre-trained DiT backbone (FLUX.1-dev) with no retraining. The key operational detail is axis-decoupled calibration: height and width scale factors are handled independently, so a panoramic image with s_w=4 and s_h=1 receives different modulation timing and rate along each axis. The method adds only scalar schedule updates during the 28-step Euler-flow sampling process.

## Method

SigMa introduces a training-free sigmoid modulation gate μ_d(t) = σ(√s_d [ℓ(t) − ℓ(s_d^{−1})]) that adaptively controls the intensity of positional intervention along each spatial axis during denoising, where ℓ(t) = log(t/(1−t)) is the log-odds time. Two scale laws govern the gate: Decoupled Geometric Center Alignment sets the transition center at t_c = 1/s_d (delaying structural-to-texture handoff for larger scales), and Iso-Variance Rate Adaptation sets the transition sharpness at γ = √s_d (keeping the crossover width invariant in standardized feature units as aggregated signal variance grows). The gate is integrated into existing extrapolation methods (PI, NTK, YaRN) via Dynamic Parameter Injection, replacing static scale factors with time-varying effective scales ŝ_d(t) = s_d^{μ_d(t)} or modulating YaRN's ramp thresholds, making the entire pipeline phase-aware without altering the underlying RoPE kernels.

## Results

On the 4096² square extrapolation benchmark, SigMa-YaRN achieves HPSv2 of 0.287 and FID of 184.73 on Aesthetic-4K, outperforming the FLUX.1-dev baseline (HPSv2 0.200, FID 197.21) and the static YaRN baseline (HPSv2 0.261, FID 194.76). On the 3072×2048 landscape setting, SigMa-YaRN reaches HPSv2 0.288 and FID 149.98 on Aesthetic-4K. In the extreme 4:1 panoramic regime (4096×1024), SigMa achieves HPSv2 0.254 and Aesthetic 6.28, surpassing DyPE-YaRN (HPSv2 0.231, Aesthetic 5.85) and YaRN (HPSv2 0.208, Aesthetic 5.42).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SigMa-YaRN consistently outperforms the static YaRN baseline across all metrics at both 3072×2048 and 4096² (e.g., HPSv2 0.287 vs 0.261, FID 184.73 vs 194.76 on Aesthetic-4K at 4096²), confirming that time-varying modulation is essential. Against the rigid-dynamic DyPE-YaRN, SigMa-YaRN wins on HPSv2 (0.287 vs 0.279) and FID (184.73 vs 188.53) at 4096², with the gap widening in the 4:1 panoramic setting (HPSv2 0.254 vs 0.231, Aesthetic 6.28 vs 5.85), where axis-decoupled calibration prevents geometric repetition. SigMa-NTK similarly beats DyPE-NTK on FID (148.76 vs 149.77) and HPSv2 (0.281 vs 0.281, tied) at 3072×2048. The FLUX.1-dev baseline without any extrapolation shows the largest degradation (HPSv2 0.139 on DrawBench at 4096²), establishing the lower bound that all methods must exceed. In the panoramic stress test, SigMa achieves the best scores on all three metrics (CLIP 27.45, Aesthetic 6.28, HPSv2 0.254), with the largest margin over YaRN (HPSv2 +0.046), demonstrating that axis-decoupled calibration is critical under anisotropic scaling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of training-free RoPE extrapolation methods for diffusion transformers (Position Interpolation, NTK-aware interpolation, YaRN) and the recent dynamic variant DyPE, which introduces time-dependent modulation via a fixed power-law schedule. The key departure is the identification of a scale-dependent 'evolution gap': as target resolution grows, the structural lock-in point shifts toward cleaner timesteps, yet all prior methods apply a resolution-invariant schedule. SigMa replaces the rigid power-law with a scale-adaptive sigmoid gate whose center (1/s) and rate (√s) are derived from two scaling laws—geometric center alignment and iso-variance rate adaptation—enabling axis-decoupled calibration for anisotropic resolutions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Training-free extrapolation to 4K+ resolutions is practically important for practitioners who cannot afford retraining at ultra-high resolutions, and the axis-decoupled formulation addresses a real failure mode in panoramic generation. However, the theoretical grounding is somewhat assumption-driven (the scale-consistency condition in Proposition 3.2 is postulated rather than derived from first principles), and the empirical gains over DyPE are modest (e.g., HPSv2 0.287 vs. 0.279 at 4096²). Open questions include whether the √s rate law and sigmoid form generalize beyond axial-RoPE DiTs to other architectures, and whether the framework extends to video or 3D generation where temporal and spatial scales interact.
