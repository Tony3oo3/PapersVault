## Problem

Existing neural mutual information estimators (MINE, InfoNCE, MINDE) require thousands of gradient steps to train a critic network from scratch for every new dataset, making them impractical for real-time or large-scale dependence analysis. The prior pretrained approach, InfoNet, eliminates test-time optimization but is fundamentally restricted to univariate inputs and cannot handle varying dimensionalities. This paper addresses the open problem of zero-shot MI estimation between multivariate variables of arbitrary dimension in a single forward pass.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a set of n paired samples {(x^i, y^i)} where x ∈ R^{d_x} and y ∈ R^{d_y}; dimensions below the model's native capacity D=20 are padded with independent Gaussian noise (which provably preserves MI), and sample size n is flexible. For inputs exceeding 20 dimensions, the method applies k-sliced MI with S random orthonormal projections, batching all S projected datasets into a single forward pass. The output is a scalar MI estimate in nats (or the average of S sliced MI values for high-dimensional data), computed via the empirical Donsker-Varadhan formula using the generated critic and a random index permutation for marginal pairs.

## Method

InfoAtlas is a dual-path attentive hypernetwork that directly outputs the full parameter set of the optimal Donsker-Varadhan critic in one feedforward pass, replacing O(T) gradient steps with O(1) inference. The joint path encodes paired samples via Perceiver-IO-style cross-attention (learnable queries attending over concatenated [x;y] tokens through 16 self-attention layers), while the marginal path encodes unpaired samples via bidirectional cross-attention between projected x and y representations (8 self-attention layers), capturing the independence baseline. A cross-attention fusion module modulates joint features by marginal features, and a 7-layer parameter-generation MLP maps the fused representation to the flattened critic weights. Pretraining uses a meta-learning objective over a meta-distribution of synthetic datasets generated by mixing up to 60 Gaussian and Student-t copulas with randomly initialized correlation matrices (constructed via low-rank factorization for controlled correlation strength) and flow-based marginal transformations, trained for 800K iterations on 16 H800 GPUs with batch size 256 distributions × 5000 samples each.

## Results

On the BMI benchmark (8 tasks with known ground-truth MI, N=5000), InfoAtlas achieves MI estimates within 0.05–0.25 bits of ground truth across all tasks, matching or approaching the best neural baselines (MINE, MINDE, InfoNCE) while running in 0.09 s versus 25.9–67.6 s for those methods (~300× speedup). On high-dimensional independence testing (dims 16–128), InfoAtlas with 5-sliced MI (S=32) achieves ROC-AUC comparable to gradient-based estimators for n≥500. On robotic manipulation (ManiSkill 2), key states extracted via InfoAtlas yield the highest policy success rates: 94.2% (Pick Cube, seen), 82.0% (Pick Cube, unseen), and 72.4% (Peg Insertion, seen), outperforming MINE-1000 and InfoNet.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against gradient-based neural estimators (MINE, MINDE, InfoNCE), InfoAtlas achieves comparable MI accuracy on the BMI benchmark (e.g., 0.60 vs. 0.60 on Mn-dense; 0.89 vs. 1.00 on Spiral) while being 288–751× faster (0.09 s vs. 25.9–67.6 s). Against non-parametric baselines, InfoAtlas substantially outperforms KSG (e.g., 0.89 vs. 0.75 on Spiral; 0.41 vs. 0.25 on Asinh@St) and dramatically outperforms KDE, which overestimates MI by 2–3× on most tasks. Against the prior pretrained method InfoNet, InfoAtlas achieves comparable or superior independence-testing AUC at moderate-to-large sample sizes (n≥500) and better noise-level discrimination on CLIP embeddings, owing to its use of 5-dimensional slicing versus InfoNet's 1-dimensional slicing. On robotic manipulation, InfoAtlas-derived key states yield the highest success rates across all three ManiSkill 2 tasks (e.g., 94.2% vs. 86.4% for MINE-100 on Pick Cube seen; 72.4% vs. 65.4% for MINE-1000 on Peg Insertion seen), confirming that the speedup does not come at the cost of downstream task performance. KNIFE, despite being a learnable KDE method, shows large errors on several tasks (e.g., 0.10 vs. GT 1.02 on Spiral), making it the weakest baseline overall.

## Relation to Prior Work

InfoAtlas extends the Donsker-Varadhan-based MI estimation framework (MINE, InfoNCE, MINDE) by replacing per-dataset gradient optimization with a single forward pass through a pretrained hypernetwork. It directly builds on InfoNet (Hu et al., 2024), the first attempt at eliminating test-time optimization via lookup tables, but overcomes InfoNet's fundamental limitation to 1D inputs by using attention-based dual-path hypernetworks that handle arbitrary multivariate dimensions. The pretraining strategy draws on the foundation model paradigm (Hollmann et al., 2025) and copula mixture theory (Chen et al., 2025), while the high-dimensional scaling leverages sliced MI (Goldfeld et al., 2022) in a batched inference setting that no prior neural estimator supports.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 100× speedup while matching SOTA neural estimators opens the door to real-time dependency analysis in domains like high-frequency finance, large-scale genomics, and online robotics, where per-dataset optimization is prohibitive. The paradigm shift from 'train a critic per dataset' to 'infer MI in one pass' could influence how other statistical estimation tasks are approached. Key limitations remain: the method requires slicing for dimensions beyond 20 (introducing approximation bias), struggles with small samples (n < 400), and the synthetic pretraining distribution, while diverse, may not fully cover pathological real-world dependence structures. Open questions include whether the hypernetwork approach can be extended to conditional MI, directed information, or other information-theoretic quantities without architectural changes.
