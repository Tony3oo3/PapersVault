## Problem

Standard on-policy RL methods (PPO, GRPO) enforce trust regions via heuristic pointwise clipping of the policy ratio, which indiscriminately zeroes gradients for high-divergence samples even when they carry high return signals. This binary truncation suppresses novel discoveries (e.g., a new reasoning trajectory or an unseen robot maneuver) and renders off-policy data unusable once slightly stale, limiting both learning efficiency and data reuse. The paper argues this limitation stems from the particular clipping approximation rather than from trust-region optimization itself.

## Input / Output

For LLM fine-tuning, the method consumes math problem prompts (DAPO-Math-17K) and produces full reasoning trajectories, evaluated on five benchmarks (AIME 2024/2025, AMC 2023, HMMT 2025, OlymMath) across seven model scales (1B–8B) spanning Fast and Slow thinking paradigms. For continuous control, it takes state observations from DeepMind Control Suite environments and outputs continuous actions via MLP policies trained with 2,048 parallel environments. The off-policy variant (R²VPO-OFF) additionally stores experience tuples ⟨x, y, log π_off, r, Â⟩ in a FIFO replay buffer (capacity 4 iterations, UTD ratio 2), enabling asynchronous generation-optimization decoupling.

## Method

The core insight (Proposition 4.1) is that in the local regime ρ_θ→1, every f-divergence (KL, JS, Hellinger, χ², α-divergence) admits the second-order approximation D_f(π_θ‖π_off) ≈ f''(1)/2 · Var_{π_off}[ρ_θ], meaning the trust-region constraint is universally governed by the variance of the policy ratio. R²VPO replaces PPO's clipping with a variance-constrained objective max_θ E[ρ_t Â_t] s.t. E[(ρ_t−1)²] ≤ δ, solved via a Lagrangian primal-dual scheme where the dual variable λ adaptively controls regularization strength (λ ← max(0, λ − η_λ(δ − E[(ρ_t−1)²]))). The resulting gradient introduces a 'Regularized Advantage' (Â_t − 2λ(ρ_t−1)) that acts as a continuous, instance-dependent soft brake: near-identity updates pass through unpenalized while high-divergence updates are smoothly attenuated rather than hard-truncated. A supporting theorem (Theorem 4.4) formally bounds the clipping error |J_UNC − J_CLIP| ≤ A_max/ε · Var[ρ_t], showing that minimizing ratio variance directly controls the overestimation that clipping heuristically prevents. The off-policy variant exploits this same variance penalty to naturally down-weight stale data in the replay buffer without requiring separate importance-sampling corrections or critic networks.

## Results

On the macro-average across 5 mathematical reasoning benchmarks (AIME 2024/2025, AMC 2023, HMMT 2025, OlymMath) and 7 LLM scales, R²VPO-OFF achieves 46.33% accuracy, a +35% relative gain over base models, outperforming GRPO (42.50%, +24%), GRPO-CH (44.87%, +31%), GPPO (44.75%, +30%), and TOPR (44.57%, +30%). Gains are most dramatic on smaller Fast Thinking models: Qwen3-1.7B-Fast improves by +138% and Qwen3-8B-Fast by +110%. On 10 DeepMind Control Suite continuous robotic tasks, R²VPO consistently outperforms PPO, with particularly large advantages in sparse-reward settings (e.g., Ball_in_Cup) and prevention of late-stage performance collapse (e.g., Cheetah_Run).

## Baselines & Comparisons

R²VPO-OFF outperforms all four LLM baselines on the macro-average: it beats GRPO by 3.83 points (46.33 vs. 42.50), GRPO-CH by 1.46 points, GPPO by 1.58 points, and TOPR by 1.76 points. The advantage is most pronounced on smaller Fast Thinking models (Qwen3-1.7B: +138% vs. GRPO's +93%; Qwen3-8B: +110% vs. GRPO's +92%) and on the distilled DeepSeek-1.5B model where GRPO actually degrades (−1.0%) while R²VPO-OFF gains +16%. R²VPO-ON is competitive with the off-policy variant, notably surpassing it on openPangu-7B (57.55% vs. 56.32%), confirming the core variance objective's stability without replay. On continuous control, R²VPO dominates PPO across all 10 DM Control Suite tasks, with the largest margins in sparse-reward environments (Ball_in_Cup, where PPO flatlines near zero) and dynamic tasks (Cheetah_Run, where PPO suffers catastrophic late-stage collapse). The off-policy variant's sample efficiency advantage is further validated by its robustness to data staleness: while GRPO degrades sharply as replay buffer capacity increases, R²VPO maintains stable performance even at rb=8.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the TRPO→PPO→GRPO lineage of trust-region policy optimization, but reframes the trust-region constraint through a second-order Taylor expansion of f-divergences, showing that all common divergences (KL, JS, Hellinger, χ²) collapse to a scaled policy-ratio variance in the local regime. It departs from prior clipping-based methods (PPO, GRPO, GRPO-CH, TOPR, GPPO) by replacing pointwise binary truncation with a distributional quadratic penalty solved via a Lagrangian primal-dual scheme, and from off-policy methods (SAC, V-trace, Off-Policy TRPO) by using ratio variance as a natural stabilizer that avoids separate critic networks or complex importance-sampling corrections. The theoretical contribution (Proposition 4.1 and Theorem 4.4) provides a formal bridge between the variance penalty and the clipping error, positioning R²VPO as a principled local surrogate rather than an exact trust-region method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in a drop-in replacement for clipping that is theoretically grounded, requires no additional networks, and naturally enables off-policy data reuse for LLM fine-tuning without the scalability hurdles of critic-based methods. The +35% macro-average gain (up to +138% on 1.7B models) and consistent robotics improvements suggest the method could reduce training compute for LLM post-training. However, the guarantee is explicitly local (not global monotonicity), the penalty can become overly conservative under extreme divergence, and the modest gains on larger slow-thinking models (+8–14%) raise questions about whether the advantage diminishes with scale. Open questions include whether adaptive scaling of the variance penalty can mitigate early-training conservatism and whether the framework extends to multi-turn agentic RL with long-horizon credit assignment.
