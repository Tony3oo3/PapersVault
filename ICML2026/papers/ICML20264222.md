## Problem

Single-step temporal-difference learning in offline RL suffers from bootstrapping error accumulation that compounds over long horizons, while action-chunked TD methods reduce this accumulation but are structurally suboptimal because they restrict the policy class to open-loop action sequences. The paper asks whether the stability benefits of horizon reduction can be leveraged without sacrificing the fine-grained optimality and trajectory-stitching capability of single-step TD learning.

## Input / Output

CGQ consumes a fixed offline dataset of (s, a, r, s') transitions with state vectors or RGB images and produces a single-step policy that outputs one action per timestep at inference. The action-chunked critic and policy are trained as auxiliary components during learning but are discarded at test time, so deployment cost matches standard single-step methods. The method is evaluated on OGBench manipulation and navigation tasks (episode lengths 500–2000, action dimensions 5–21) as well as D4RL and pixel-based variants.

## Method

CGQ jointly trains a single-step critic Q_φ(s,a) with standard one-step TD loss and an auxiliary action-chunked critic Q_φc(s_t, a_t) with h-step TD backups, then regularizes the single-step critic toward the chunked critic via an upper-expectile distillation loss ℓ_τ(Q_φc − Q_φ) that biases the single-step critic toward higher chunked estimates while being less affected by chunked suboptimality. The overall critic objective is L^TD + β·L^reg, where β controls guidance strength. Policy extraction uses FQL (flow-matching-based behavior policy with constrained improvement) for the single-step policy and QC-FQL for the chunked policy. Theoretically, the paper proves that the regularized stochastic Bellman iteration achieves a tighter asymptotic error bound than either pure single-step TD (σ²/(1−γ²)) or pure chunked TD (‖Q*−Qc‖²), with the optimal β balancing error accumulation against regularization bias.

## Results

On OGBench manipulation tasks, CGQ achieves an average success rate of 76±2%, outperforming the best single-step method FQL (38%), the best action-chunked method QC-FQL (57%), and DEAS (71%). The gains are most dramatic on long-horizon tasks: cube-triple (94±1% vs. 83% QC-FQL, 10% FQL) and cube-quadruple (82±2% vs. 64% DEAS, 17% FQL). On navigation tasks, CGQ underperforms FQL (35±4% vs. 49%) and DQC (58%), which the authors attribute to unreliable chunked critics in locomotion domains. CGQ's training cost is ~2.3× that of FQL (4.66 ms vs. 2.00 ms per step), while inference cost remains comparable (0.36 ms vs. 0.26 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CGQ substantially outperforms all single-step baselines on long-horizon manipulation: it beats FQL by 56 points on cube-triple (94 vs. 10) and by 65 points on cube-quadruple (82 vs. 17), and beats NFQL by 71 points on cube-triple. Against action-chunked baselines, CGQ wins on cube-double (69 vs. 48 DEAS, 39 QC-FQL), cube-triple (94 vs. 83 QC-FQL, 82 DEAS), and cube-quadruple (82 vs. 64 DEAS, 45 QC-FQL), while roughly tying DEAS on scene-sparse (86 vs. 93) and puzzle-3x3-sparse (99 vs. 99–100). CGQ loses to DEAS on puzzle-4x4 (28 vs. 39) and to FQL/DQC on all navigation tasks (35 avg. vs. 49 FQL, 58 DQC), which the authors attribute to unreliable chunked critics in locomotion where reactive control is critical. Notably, DQC achieves 93±2 on humanoidmaze-medium but only 2±1 on cube-triple, highlighting that no single action-chunked method dominates across both manipulation and navigation regimes, whereas CGQ's single-step policy at least avoids the open-loop suboptimality that plagues QC-FQL on shorter-horizon tasks like puzzle-3x3-sparse (63 vs. CGQ's 99).

## Relation to Prior Work

CGQ sits at the intersection of two established lines of offline RL work: single-step TD learning with behavior regularization (FQL) and action-chunked TD learning (QC-FQL, DEAS, DQC). It departs from pure action-chunking methods by refusing to commit the final policy to open-loop action sequences, and from pure single-step methods by introducing an auxiliary chunked critic as a regularizer rather than modifying the TD target itself. The regularization mechanism borrows the expectile regression loss from IQL, and the policy extraction relies on FQL's flow-based behavior policy and QC-FQL's chunked variant. The theoretical contribution formalizes the bias-variance trade-off between bootstrapping error accumulation and open-loop suboptimality, showing CGQ's bound is always tighter than action-chunked TD and conditionally tighter than single-step TD.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental tension in long-horizon offline RL—reducing backup depth for stability versus preserving reactive policy expressiveness—and provides a clean, theoretically grounded resolution. The honest negative results on navigation tasks (where chunked critics are unreliable in locomotion) and the ablation showing sensitivity to β highlight that the approach is not a universal fix; improving chunked critic reliability and developing adaptive regularization weighting remain open. The 2.3× training overhead (though inference cost is unchanged) and the need for task-specific hyperparameter tuning limit immediate broad adoption, but the conceptual framework of using a coarser-grained critic as a stabilizing signal for a finer-grained one is a reusable design pattern.
