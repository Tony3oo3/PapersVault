## Problem

State-of-the-art link prediction methods rely on hand-crafted structural heuristics, per-node ID embeddings, or complex positional/structural encodings that are infeasible at web scale due to static dependencies, global preprocessing, or prohibitive memory overhead. The paper asks whether a plain, hardware-efficient Transformer operating solely on fixed-budget sampled local subgraphs—without any of these auxiliary components—can match or exceed such sophisticated pipelines.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264495/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For each candidate pair (v_src, v_dst), the model receives a sampled subgraph of at most N_max context nodes, where each node is tokenized as a concatenation of a one-hot index identifier, its adjacency-indicator row within the subgraph, and a 2-bit role flag; two additional task tokens (copying the endpoint encodings with a flipped role flag) are appended. The subgraph adjacency matrix is not passed separately but is reconstructed by slicing columns from the token tensor. The output is a scalar binary edge probability obtained by concatenating the final hidden states of the two task tokens and projecting through a linear layer, trained with binary cross-entropy.

## Method

PENCIL is a BERT-style encoder-only Transformer over tokenized sampled subgraphs that omits all positional and structural encodings, making it permutation-equivariant and fully compatible with optimized attention kernels (e.g., FlashAttention). Its key architectural innovation is a multiplicative residual: each layer applies a standard bidirectional self-attention block followed by an explicit one-hop propagation term P_k(Ã Z^(k)) added as a residual, where Ã is the adjacency reconstructed from the input encoding and P_k is a learnable rowwise projection; because this propagation is computed outside the attention module, it does not interfere with efficient attention implementations. The input projection uses orthogonal initialization to induce quasi-orthogonal node representations, which—combined with sum-aggregation message passing—yields unbiased estimators of local heuristics (common neighbors, Adamic-Adar, resource allocation) via inner products. Under specific parameter settings, PENCIL degenerates to a source-conditioned MPNN (NBFNet), inheriting expressivity for path-based scores (Katz index, Personalized PageRank, shortest-path distance), and with local relational pooling it achieves expressivity no less than SEAL under the k_φ-k_ρ-m framework. Empirically, PENCIL uses 22×–146× fewer parameters than ID-based competitors and converges in 0.5–15 epochs on large OGB datasets versus 20–100+ epochs for pure GNNs.

## Results

On the original benchmark setting, PENCIL (with features) achieves state-of-the-art H@100 of 79.54±0.07 on ogbl-ppa, surpassing Refined-GAE (78.41) and LPFormer (63.32), while PENCIL without features achieves the best MRR of 42.23±1.98 on cora, beating LPFormer (39.42) and SAGE (37.83). Under the HeaRT protocol, PENCIL secures top MRR on ogbl-ppa (45.43±0.31) and ogbl-ddi (14.07±0.24). PENCIL uses 22×–146× fewer learnable parameters than ID-based competitors and converges in 0.5–15 epochs versus 20–100+ epochs for pure GNNs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PENCIL w/o Features achieves the best MRR on cora (42.23), outperforming the strongest heuristic-informed baseline LPFormer (39.42) by ~2.8 points and SAGE (37.83) by ~4.4 points, all without node features or hand-crafted heuristics. On ogbl-ppa, PENCIL with features reaches 79.54 H@100, beating Refined-GAE (78.41) and LPFormer (63.32) by a wide margin, while PENCIL w/o Features (73.85) still surpasses all heuristic-informed methods. On ogbl-collab, PENCIL w/o Features (66.88 H@50) is competitive with ID-based MPLP+ (66.99) and Refined-GAE (66.11) despite using orders of magnitude fewer parameters. Under the HeaRT protocol, PENCIL wins on ogbl-ppa (45.43 MRR vs. LPFormer 40.25, MPLP+ 41.40) and ogbl-ddi (14.07 vs. LPFormer 13.20). PENCIL trails heuristic-informed methods on citeseer (47.51 vs. NCNC 64.03) and pubmed (38.28 vs. NBFNet 44.73), indicating that on smaller Planetoid datasets the lack of explicit structural priors is a disadvantage. Overall, PENCIL's advantage is most pronounced on large-scale OGB benchmarks where its parameter efficiency and rapid convergence (0.5–15 epochs vs. 20–100+ for GNNs) become decisive.

## Relation to Prior Work

PENCIL departs from two dominant lineages in link prediction: (1) heuristic-augmented GNNs (NCN, Neo-GNN, BUDDY, LPFormer) that inject hand-crafted structural signals or per-node ID embeddings, and (2) Graph Transformers that require offline-computed positional or structural encodings (spectral, random-walk, PPR-based). Its input encoding borrows the node-adjacency tokenization scheme from Yehudai et al. (2025), but the key architectural departure is the multiplicative residual that adds an explicit adjacency-propagation branch alongside standard self-attention, enabling the model to degenerate to NBFNet-style source-conditioned message passing or SEAL-style subgraph GNNs under specific parameter settings. The theoretical analysis positions PENCIL within the k_φ-k_ρ-m expressivity framework, showing it is not less expressive than SEAL while operating under a strictly weaker orthogonality constraint (N_max vectors vs. |V| vectors).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the prevailing assumption that Transformers on graphs require sophisticated structural encodings to be competitive, demonstrating that a vanilla BERT-style encoder over sampled local subgraphs achieves SOTA on multiple benchmarks with 22×–146× fewer parameters and 6.7×–40× fewer training epochs than leading alternatives. This has direct practical implications for deployment: full compatibility with FlashAttention and standard hardware toolchains, no offline PE/SE computation, and no static dependency on initial graph state. Key limitations include data hunger on small-scale datasets (consistent with ViT's known behavior), linear compute scaling with the number of candidate links, and the absence of a tighter expressivity characterization for the full attention-enabled model. Open questions include whether pretraining can reduce data requirements, whether hybrid retrieval-style scoring can address the candidate-link scaling bottleneck, and how the approach extends to temporal or heterogeneous graphs.
