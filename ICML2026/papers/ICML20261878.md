## Problem

Diffusion-based policies imitate expert actions via behavior cloning but do not model the underlying reward or intent that makes certain actions desirable, limiting robustness under distribution shift. Existing inverse reinforcement learning approaches that recover rewards from demonstrations rely on adversarial training or expensive MCMC sampling, introducing instability and computational overhead. This paper asks whether the reward signal is already implicitly encoded in the denoising score function of a diffusion policy, and if so, how to extract it in a theoretically grounded and computationally efficient way.

## Input / Output

The method takes state observations (proprioceptive signals such as joint angles, velocities, and object poses; or 226×226 RGB images for real-robot deployment) and produces a 16-step action sequence via ODE integration, alongside a scalar energy value at the ODE endpoint (γ=10⁻³) that serves as a dense reward signal. Actions are standardized to zero mean and unit variance using training-set statistics. The reward output is 'centered' by subtracting a state-dependent baseline estimated via 16 Monte Carlo samples from N(0,I), which removes the unknown state-dependent integration constant and ensures the signal reflects only relative within-state action preferences.

## Method

ENERGYFLOW replaces the standard vector-valued noise regression head of a diffusion policy with a scalar energy function E_φ(a, s, t) parameterized by a 1D Conditional U-Net (with GlobalAveragePooling1D and a 3-layer MLP producing a single scalar, and Mish activations to ensure C² differentiability for double backpropagation). The score is obtained by automatic differentiation as S_φ = −∇_a E_φ, which enforces the conservative (curl-free) field constraint by construction, guaranteeing transitive action preferences. Training uses the standard denoising score matching objective with a variance-exploding geometric noise schedule, where minimizing the loss at t→0 recovers the gradient of the expert's soft Q-function under maximum-entropy optimality (Theorem 3.3). The paper proves that this integrability constraint strictly reduces the Rademacher complexity of the hypothesis class from O(ΛB√d/√n) to O(ΛL/√n), yielding tighter out-of-distribution generalization bounds. At inference, actions are generated by integrating the probability-flow ODE with Euler steps (K=10–20), and the terminal energy value provides a reward signal for downstream SAC-based policy refinement.

## Results

On RoboMimic, ENERGYFLOW achieves a 93.8% average success rate across five manipulation tasks, outperforming Diffusion Policy (91.2%) and Flow Policy (89.6%), with the largest gap on ToolHang (84.2% vs. 77.2%). On Meta-World, ENERGYFLOW similarly leads with 92.5% average success versus 90.7% for Diffusion Policy. On a physical AGIBOT G1 robot, ENERGYFLOW achieves 100% success on both Bottle and Drawer tasks with 20 expert demonstrations. Inference latency is 9.8 ms (K=10 ODE steps) on an A100, comparable to Flow Policy (8.2 ms) while additionally exposing a scalar energy reward signal.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ENERGYFLOW outperforms all eight baselines on both RoboMimic and Meta-World. Against the strongest generative baselines, it beats Diffusion Policy by 2.6 points on average (93.8% vs. 91.2%) and Flow Policy by 4.2 points (93.8% vs. 89.6%), with the gap widening on harder tasks like ToolHang (+7.0 over DP, +9.4 over FP). Against energy-based methods, the advantage is substantial: EBT-Policy trails by 15.0 points (78.8%) and Implicit BC by 71.4 points (22.4%), the latter failing entirely on Transport and ToolHang. Among IRL baselines, IQ-Learn is closest at 69.8% but still lags by 24.0 points, while EBIL (61.7%) and NEAR (66.5%) degrade sharply on multi-stage tasks. Notably, ENERGYFLOW matches Diffusion Policy's perfect scores on Lift and Can while exceeding it on Square (95.3% vs. 93.5%), Transport (89.4% vs. 85.9%), and ToolHang (84.2% vs. 77.2%), suggesting the conservative energy constraint acts as a beneficial inductive bias rather than a performance cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

ENERGYFLOW sits at the intersection of diffusion-based policies (Diffusion Policy, Flow Policy), energy-based imitation (Implicit BC, EBT-Policy), and maximum-entropy IRL (Ziebart et al., EBIL, NEAR, IQ-Learn). Its key conceptual departure is showing that the score function learned via denoising score matching is, under maximum-entropy optimality, exactly the gradient of the expert's soft Q-function — thereby unifying generative action sampling and reward recovery in a single scalar energy parameterization. Unlike adversarial IRL methods that require min-max training, and unlike prior energy-based policies that use contrastive objectives, ENERGYFLOW trains the energy through the standard diffusion denoising loss while enforcing integrability by construction (score = −∇ₐE), which simultaneously yields a valid reward signal and a provably tighter generalization bound.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, non-adversarial route to extracting reward signals from diffusion policies, which could reduce the manual reward engineering bottleneck in robotic RL. The theoretical results (complexity reduction via conservative constraints, OOD generalization bounds, Lipschitz preference error) give a clear inductive-bias justification for the architecture choice. However, the state-dependent integration constant c(s) limits cross-state reward comparisons, and the maximum-entropy optimality assumption may not hold for all expert behaviors. Open questions include whether the energy-based reward extraction scales to high-dimensional visual observations, whether the conservative constraint helps in truly long-horizon sequential settings where PBRS conditions matter, and how the method performs when expert demonstrations are suboptimal or noisy.
