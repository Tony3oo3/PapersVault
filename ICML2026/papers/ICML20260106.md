## Problem

The dominant paradigm in off-policy learning optimizes IPS-based value estimators, assuming that better statistical estimation inherently yields better learned policies. However, these objectives are highly non-concave with standard softmax policies, exhibiting O(K) optimization plateaus and exponentially many local maxima that scale with the action space size. This paper demonstrates that in large action spaces (60k–1M actions), this optimization barrier—not estimation accuracy—is the primary bottleneck for learned policy quality.

## Input / Output

The method consumes logged interaction tuples (context, action, reward) drawn from a known logging policy, where contexts are user interaction histories and actions are items from spaces ranging from 60k (MovieLens) to 1M (GoodReads). The output is a learned softmax policy over the full action space, deployed either stochastically or greedily via argmax. A key structural requirement is that logging policy propensities π₀(a|x) must be known a priori, and large-action variants additionally require action embeddings or cluster assignments to define marginalized importance weights.

## Method

The paper makes three technical contributions. First, it proves that IPS-based objectives (linear in π) paired with linear-softmax policies suffer O(K_eff) optimization plateaus and exponentially many local maxima, where K_eff is the effective action space size, explaining why these objectives degrade as K grows. Second, it introduces objective-aware parametrization: by deriving closed-form oracle policies for each estimator, it identifies minimal sufficient action structures (e.g., restricting the policy support to the logging policy's support for IPS/cIPS/ES, or using a two-stage cluster-level decomposition for MIPS/OffCEM) that reduce K_eff without altering the asymptotic optimization target. Third, it studies PWLL objectives—reward/propensity-weighted log-likelihood updates (LPI, cLPI, RegKL, BPR)—which are concave (strongly concave with ℓ₂ regularization) for linear-softmax policies, eliminating the non-concavity entirely. A key practical insight is that cLPI and cIPS induce the same greedy oracle decision rule, so optimizing the concave cLPI objective and deploying the argmax policy recovers the cIPS oracle without navigating the non-concave IPS landscape.

## Results

Across MovieLens (K=60k), Twitch (K=200k), and GoodReads (K=1M), PWLL objectives (cLPI, LPI, RegKL, BPR) consistently achieve higher validation reward than all IPS-based objectives (IPS, ES, DR, MIPS, OffCEM, POTEC, PC). The headline finding is qualitative rather than a single number: IPS-based objectives exhibit severe sensitivity to batch size and learning-rate schedule (performance collapse under minor hyperparameter changes), while PWLL objectives remain robust across all tested configurations. Notably, even POTEC—a state-of-the-art method specifically designed for large action spaces—is surpassed by the much simpler cLPI. PWLL methods are poor value estimators by design (their MSE is too large to display on the same scale as IPS methods in Figure 3), yet they produce the strongest learned policies.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares 11 methods across three large-scale datasets. cLPI (a PWLL objective) outperforms all seven IPS-based baselines (IPS, ES, DR, MIPS, OffCEM, POTEC, PC) on validation reward across all three datasets. Among IPS-based methods, POTEC outperforms OffCEM despite both targeting the same asymptotic objective, demonstrating that the two-stage cluster-level optimization strategy (reducing K_eff from K to |C|) is the decisive factor. A striking counterintuitive result: ES has consistently larger MSE than MIPS (Figure 3) yet achieves higher validation reward (Figure 2), directly contradicting the estimator-centric view that lower estimation error yields better policies. Within PWLL methods, cLPI generally performs best, benefiting from its clipped importance weighting that corrects for logging-policy frequency bias. Objective-aware parametrization (restricting the policy support to the logging policy's support S_0) further improves both IPS and cLPI, with the naive whole-action-space parametrization showing severe instability for IPS but only marginal gains for the already-stable cLPI.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the offline contextual bandit framework (Dudik et al., 2011) and the rich family of IPS-based estimators (IPS, cIPS, ES, DR, MIPS, OffCEM, POTEC, PC), but deliberately shifts the analytical lens from estimation accuracy to optimization landscape properties. The theoretical results on plateaus and exponential local maxima are adapted from Chen et al. (2019) and Mei et al. (2020a) on softmax policy optimization, while the PWLL objectives (LPI, cLPI, RegKL, BPR) are repurposed from prior work (Liang & Vlassis, 2022; Rendle et al., 2012) rather than introduced as novel methods. The key conceptual departure is the 'objective-aware parametrization' principle, derived from closed-form oracle policy analysis, which aligns policy structure with estimator inductive bias to reduce the effective action space without changing the statistical target.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This paper challenges the dominant estimator-centric paradigm in off-policy learning by demonstrating, with controlled experiments across 60k–1M action spaces, that optimization trainability dominates estimation accuracy as a predictor of learned-policy quality. The practical guidance (prefer PWLL-style objectives, use objective-aware parametrizations) is immediately actionable for large-scale recommendation systems where K reaches millions. Key limitations include: PWLL objectives cannot serve as value estimators for policy selection, concavity guarantees hold only for linear-softmax policies (not end-to-end deep networks), and oracle policies inherently depend on logging policy coverage. Open questions include whether these findings extend to multi-step MDPs, whether PWLL variants can be augmented to recover value-estimation capability, and how the optimization landscape behaves under full end-to-end deep training.
