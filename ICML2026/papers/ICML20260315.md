## Problem

Trained attention layers exhibit reproducible spectral structure (low-rank collapse, bulk deformation, isolated outliers) and power-law scaling laws, yet the theoretical origin of these phenomena and their quantitative link to generalization remain unexplained. No high-dimensional asymptotic theory connects the spectral properties of attention weights to generalization performance, and existing scaling-law analyses are largely confined to linearized or lazy regimes that miss genuine representation learning.

## Input / Output

The model takes sequences of T Gaussian token embeddings in R^d and produces either an output token sequence (seq2seq: attention-weighted input) or the T×T attention matrix directly (seq2lab). The target is generated from an attention-indexed model where the pre-activation is bilinear in input tokens via a symmetric rank-p₀ weight matrix S₀, plus Gaussian noise. The learner is a single-head tied attention layer (W_Q = W_K = W ∈ R^{d×p}) with identity value matrix and row-wise softmax at temperature β, trained by ERM with ℓ₂ weight decay.

## Method

The paper maps ERM in single-head tied attention to an equivalent PSD matrix sensing problem, then applies approximate message passing (AMP) to derive a six-dimensional state evolution system whose global minimizer gives sharp asymptotic formulas for train and test error in the limit d→∞ with n/d², p/d, p₀/d → O(1). The learned spectrum is characterized in closed form as η·ReLU(S₀ + δZ − εI_d) with Z ~ GOE(d), where (η, δ, ε) are determined self-consistently, yielding a structured bulk, low-rank collapse, and isolated spectral outliers. The ℓ₂ weight decay on W induces a nuclear-norm penalty on WW^T/√(pd), creating an implicit low-rank bias that makes factorized training strictly outperform direct element-wise training of the attention matrix. For power-law target spectra, the effective threshold ε acts as a moving cutoff that sequentially recovers spectral modes, producing sharp emergence events and universal power-law scaling exponents that match those of LASSO and matrix compressed sensing.

## Results

The paper provides sharp high-dimensional asymptotic formulas for train/test error, interpolation and perfect recovery thresholds, and the full singular-value spectrum of trained tied-attention weights (Claims 3.1 and 4.1). For power-law targets with decay exponent γ, the excess test error scales as n^{−f(γ)} with f(γ) = 1 − 1/(2γ) under weak regularization (λ = 1/d) and f(γ) = 2 − 1/γ under stronger regularization (λ = 1, √d), matching minimax rates from LASSO and matrix compressed sensing. Numerical Adam simulations at d = 100–400 confirm the theoretical predictions for both learning curves and spectral distributions with excellent quantitative agreement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is between the factorized (tied query-key) ERM estimator and a non-factorized Frobenius-regularized attention matrix (Eq. 18–19). The factorized parameterization consistently achieves significantly lower test error across all sample ratios α, fully exploiting the implicit nuclear-norm (low-rank) bias induced by ℓ₂ weight decay on the factors; in the extreme low-rank regime p₀ ≪ d, it achieves vanishing error at sample scale O(dp₀) whereas the non-factorized model requires O(d²). A second comparison is between theory and Adam gradient-descent simulations: at d = 100 (64 instances) and d = 200 (8 instances), the predicted test error, training loss, and singular-value spectra match the numerical results closely, validating the replica-symmetric AMP characterization despite the non-convexity of the attention loss. The paper also compares three regularization strengths (λ = 1/d, 1, √d) on power-law targets, showing distinct scaling regimes with different decay exponents, and confirms that the predicted spectral outliers and bulk structure reproduce the qualitative patterns observed in pretrained transformers (Martin et al., 2021; Staats et al., 2024).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the high-dimensional asymptotic analysis of quadratic neural networks (Erba et al., 2025; Xu et al., 2025) and multi-token AMP (Cui et al., 2024) to the setting of single-head tied attention, mapping ERM to a generalized matrix sensing problem with a nuclear-norm penalty. It departs from the attention-indexed model of Boncoraglio et al. (2025), which characterized only Bayes-optimal inference, by providing the first sharp asymptotic theory of ERM solutions in the proportional-rank regime (κ = p/d = O(1)), as opposed to the O(1)-rank attention studied in Cui et al. (2024) and Troiani et al. (2025). The spectral characterization via free convolution and the sequential recovery mechanism for power-law targets are new contributions that bridge random matrix theory, spin-glass replica methods, and scaling-law phenomenology in a single framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first high-dimensional theory that simultaneously predicts generalization error, the full singular-value spectrum of trained attention weights, and power-law scaling laws from a single set of self-consistent equations, providing a first-principles explanation for empirically observed phenomena (low-rank collapse, spectral outliers, heavy-tailed spectra) in real transformers. The finding that factorized training with weight decay (inducing nuclear-norm bias) consistently outperforms the strictly more expressive non-factorized parameterization offers a concrete theoretical justification for the widespread use of low-rank methods like LoRA. Key limitations include the restriction to tied single-head attention, Gaussian isotropic inputs, finite sequence length T, and the asymptotic d→∞ limit; extending to multi-head, untied, and non-Gaussian settings remains open, as does a rigorous proof of the replica-symmetric prediction (currently stated as a claim with numerical validation).
