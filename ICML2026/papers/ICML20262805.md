## Problem

Existing online conformal prediction (OCP) methods require manual learning-rate tuning and algorithm-specific analyses to guarantee coverage, and the precise connection between online learning regret and conformal coverage has remained unclear. This paper asks two questions: (1) is there a form of regret that universally implies coverage for any online algorithm, and (2) can one construct a parameter-free OCP algorithm with optimal finite-time coverage guarantees?

## Input / Output

At each round t, the method receives the current feature vector X_t, a target miscoverage rate α ∈ (0,1), and a base model point prediction Ŷ_t (from any forecaster such as Prophet or an AR model). It outputs a centered prediction interval [Ŷ_t − b_t, Ŷ_t + b_t] with radius b_t ≥ 0, computed via a multiplicative wealth-based update that requires no hyperparameters. After observing Y_t, the nonconformity score S_t = |Y_t − Ŷ_t| is computed to update the internal wealth and miscoverage count, which drive the next radius. The method handles polynomially growing scores S_t ≤ Dt^q without requiring a uniform bound.

## Method

The paper first establishes a general regret-to-coverage theory: controlling the linearized regret (sum of subgradient-linearized loss differences) on the (1−α)-pinball loss implies a finite-time miscoverage bound via the Fenchel conjugate of the regret bound, with no algorithm-specific assumptions. Building on this, UP-OCP reduces OCP to a two-asset portfolio selection problem called the 'conformal market,' where stock returns are asymmetric functions of the observed subgradient g_t ∈ {−(1−α), α}: Stock 1 pays off on miscoverage rounds and Stock 2 on coverage rounds. The Universal Portfolio algorithm with Jeffreys prior (dμ(λ) ∝ [λ(1−λ)]^{−1/2} dλ) computes the portfolio weight λ_t as a wealth-weighted average over all constant portfolios, which admits the closed-form update λ_t = (N + 1/2)/t where N is the running miscoverage count. The radius is then b_t = max(0, W_{t−1} · (λ_t − α)/(α(1−α))), making the method fully parameter-free. The key theoretical insight is that maximizing log-wealth in this asymmetric two-stock market is exactly equivalent to minimizing linearized regret on the pinball loss, yielding a worst-case regret of O(√T ln T) that improves to O(ln T) as α → 0, strictly dominating the symmetric KT bettor.

## Results

On the American Express (AXP) stock dataset at α=0.05, UP-OCP achieves 93.2% marginal coverage with the smallest average prediction set size (14.8) among all methods, and limits the longest consecutive miscoverage sequence to just 4 days—matching the best tuned baseline (SF-OGD, 3 days) while being fully parameter-free. In contrast, DtACI produces infinite prediction sets (90th and 95th quantile sizes are ∞), and KT suffers a 15-day error sequence. On the synthetic sinusoid dataset, UP-OCP tracks periodic volatility closely, matching the tuned P-Control baseline without any hyperparameter tuning, while KT lags and DtACI diverges after t>2400.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the parameter-free KT baseline (Podkopaev et al., 2024), UP-OCP wins on marginal coverage (0.932 vs 0.920), average set size (14.8 vs 16.9), median set size (11.5 vs 12.9), 75th-percentile size (18.8 vs 24.9), and longest error sequence (4 vs 15 days); KT only edges UP-OCP on the extreme 95th-percentile size (36.1 vs 38), reflecting UP-OCP's necessary expansion during high-volatility events. Against DtACI (Gibbs & Candès, 2024), UP-OCP dominates on all size metrics—DtACI's average, 90th, and 95th quantile set sizes are infinite, indicating it relies on vacuous predictions to achieve its higher nominal coverage (0.956). Against the tuned SF-OGD baseline (Orabona & Pál, 2018), UP-OCP achieves smaller average (14.8 vs 16.4), median (11.5 vs 13.8), and 75th-percentile (18.8 vs 21.5) set sizes, while SF-OGD has slightly higher coverage (0.948 vs 0.932) and a marginally shorter error sequence (3 vs 4); critically, SF-OGD required ex-post grid-search tuning of its learning rate, whereas UP-OCP is fully parameter-free. On the synthetic sinusoid, UP-OCP matches the tuned P/PI Control baseline (Angelopoulos et al., 2023) in tracking interval widths without any gain hyperparameters, while P-Control and other controller-based methods require manual tuning of controller gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of online conformal prediction (OCP) and parameter-free online learning, building directly on the Universal Portfolio framework of Cover & Ordentlich (2002) and the coin-betting/parameter-free reductions of Orabona & Pál (2016). It departs from prior OCP methods (ACI, DtACI, MVP) that require manual step-size tuning, and from prior parameter-free OCP methods (KT, Zhang et al. 2024) that implicitly assume symmetric subgradients. The key conceptual departure is identifying linearized regret—rather than standard external regret—as the precise notion that implies coverage, resolving a gap highlighted by Angelopoulos et al. (2025) who showed standard regret is insufficient. The reduction of OCP to a two-asset 'conformal market' with asymmetric returns is the paper's central architectural novelty, leveraging the regret-reward duality of Orabona & Jun (2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The general regret-to-coverage theory (Theorem 3.1) provides a black-box template: any online algorithm with a known linearized-regret bound immediately inherits a finite-time coverage guarantee, removing the need for algorithm-specific analyses that plagued prior OCP work. UP-OCP's O(ln T/T) miscoverage rate as α→0 (versus O(1/√T) for OSD) and its ability to handle polynomially growing nonconformity scores without knowing the growth parameters make it practically attractive for non-stationary time-series settings. Open directions include extending the framework to conditional/feature-dependent validity and deriving local coverage guarantees from strongly adaptive regret, both of which would broaden applicability beyond marginal coverage.
