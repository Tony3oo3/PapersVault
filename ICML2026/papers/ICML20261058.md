## Problem

Standard SFT (NLL) assigns an unbounded 1/p_t gradient weight to low-probability tokens, making it hypersensitive to label noise and outliers, while Dynamic Fine-Tuning (DFT) caps this weight to a constant, sacrificing learning efficiency on genuinely hard samples. Simple linear interpolation between the two yields a suboptimal compromise that still retains non-vanishing influence from extremely low-confidence tokens. The paper asks how to navigate the empirical Pareto frontier between learning efficiency and noise robustness in LLM post-training.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard (prompt, reference-response) text pairs during training, computing per-token conditional probabilities p_t = π_θ(y_t* | y_<t*, x) and applying a detached, probability-dependent weight to each token's NLL term. The output is a fine-tuned language model that generates chain-of-thought text responses, evaluated via pass@1 or avg@32 on mathematical reasoning benchmarks (MATH500, AIME, AMC, etc.) and out-of-domain tasks (ARC-c, GPQA-diamond, MMLU-Pro). Training uses 100K samples from NuminaMath-CoT with max input length 2048 tokens and one epoch.

## Method

SAFT introduces a stop-gradient probability-dependent weight w(p_t, α) into the NLL loss, yielding an effective gradient coefficient γ = w(p_t)/p_t and a target-logit influence w(p_t)(1−p_t). Three interpolation strategies are defined: Linear (w = (1−α)+α·p_t), Geometric (w = p_t^α), and Harmonic (w = p_t/(α+(1−α)·p_t)). The key distinction is asymptotic: Geo-SAFT produces a 'soft singularity' (γ ~ 1/p_t^(1−α)) that retains stronger low-confidence learning signals for efficiency, while Har-SAFT enforces a strict 'safety cap' (γ ≤ 1/α) with linearly vanishing logit influence for robustness. A lightweight pre-test protocol trains SFT and DFT on a 5% subset and selects Geo-SAFT if SFT wins (signal-dominant regime) or Har-SAFT if DFT wins (noise-dominant regime), avoiding test-set overfitting.

## Results

On in-domain mathematical reasoning (6 benchmarks: MATH500, Minerva, Olympiad, AIME'24, AIME'25, AMC), Har-SAFT (α=0.5) achieves the best average accuracy across all five backbones, e.g., 31.37% on Qwen2.5-Math-7B (vs. SFT 20.46%, DFT 30.20%) and 33.50% on Qwen3-14B (vs. SFT 22.64%, DFT 31.06%). On out-of-domain generalization (ARC-c, GPQA-diamond, MMLU-Pro), Har-SAFT reaches 44.66% average on Qwen2.5-Math-7B, nearly doubling SFT (24.92%) and substantially exceeding DFT (27.28%). Under 50% label noise, Har-SAFT retains 23.41% average accuracy versus SFT's collapse to 5.32%, closely matching DFT (24.01%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DFT (Wu et al., 2025), Har-SAFT wins on in-domain math for 4 of 5 backbones (e.g., 31.37% vs. 30.20% on Qwen2.5-Math-7B; 33.50% vs. 31.06% on Qwen3-14B) and dramatically dominates on out-of-domain generalization (44.66% vs. 27.28% average on ARC-c/GPQA*/MMLU-Pro), where DFT's constant gradient coefficient causes underfitting of transferable features. Under 50% synthetic label noise, DFT retains a slight edge (24.01% vs. 23.41%), confirming that Har-SAFT's bounded safety cap (γ ≤ 1/α) closely matches DFT's noise rejection while adding a controllable acceleration factor for clean samples. Against PSFT (Zhu et al., 2026b), Har-SAFT achieves substantially higher accuracy across all five backbones (e.g., 31.37% vs. 10.3% on Qwen2.5-Math-7B; 16.54% vs. 7.6% on DeepSeek-Math-7B), with PSFT's hard PPO-style trust-region clipping severely delaying convergence even after extended training. Geo-SAFT, the paper's own efficiency-oriented variant, consistently trails Har-SAFT in the noise-dominant math regime (e.g., 29.99% vs. 31.37% on Qwen2.5-7B) but reverses the hierarchy on the high-SNR conversational AlpacaEval task (4.27% vs. 3.58% LC win rate), validating the regime-dependent selection protocol.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a unifying framework between two existing SFT paradigms: standard NLL-based fine-tuning (SFT) and Dynamic Fine-Tuning (DFT, Wu et al. 2025), which replaces the 1/p_t gradient coefficient with a constant. Rather than proposing a fundamentally new objective, SAFT introduces a probability-dependent weight w(p_t, α) with stop-gradient, yielding three interpolation geometries (Linear, Geometric, Harmonic) that reshape the effective gradient coefficient γ_t = w(p_t)/p_t. The work departs from prior token-reweighting methods (noise suppression via masking, heuristic adaptive weighting) by deriving its weighting from a principled asymptotic analysis of the SFT–DFT spectrum, and from PSFT by using absolute confidence-based weighting rather than relative policy clipping. The regime-dependent selection protocol (compare SFT vs. DFT on a small validation split) is a practical contribution not present in either endpoint method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean, implementable framework (a single weight function + stop-gradient) that practitioners can drop into existing SFT pipelines, along with a lightweight pre-test protocol for choosing the right interpolation geometry based on data SNR. The theoretical distinction between order adaptation (Geo-SAFT changes the decay exponent) and scale adaptation (Har-SAFT preserves linear decay but rescales) gives a principled basis for understanding when each strategy is optimal. Limitations include reliance on synthetic target-permutation noise rather than natural data corruption, evaluation concentrated on mathematical reasoning with only a modest AlpacaEval check for general alignment, and the α>1 extrapolation regime (Geo-SAFT with α=2.0) being explored only briefly. Open questions include whether the regime-selection protocol generalizes to multi-task or multi-domain fine-tuning where different subsets of data may occupy different SNR regimes simultaneously.
