## Problem

Standard space-time PINNs treat time as an additional input coordinate while reusing a single set of shared weights across all times, creating a 'time-entanglement problem' where qualitatively different temporal regimes (e.g., smooth early-time profiles vs. sharp later-time transitions) must be represented by the same fixed spatial features. This coupling degrades accuracy, causes representation interference, and destabilizes joint optimization of PDE, boundary, and initial constraints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260813/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes spatial coordinates x ∈ Ω ⊂ ℝ^d and time t ∈ [0,T] as inputs, but unlike standard PINNs, time is not fed into the spatial network; instead, t modulates the network's parameters through a small auxiliary embedding network. The output is the scalar PDE solution u(x,t) evaluated at arbitrary space-time points. The spatial backbone is a compact MLP (two hidden layers of width 20), and the entire model contains only ~1,145–1,305 trainable parameters regardless of spatial dimensionality, since the time-embedding output dimension depends only on the number of layers, not on d.

## Method

TINNs parameterize the solution as u_{θ(t)}(x), where θ(t) is a smooth trajectory in parameter space generated by a compact layer-wise time embedding: a small MLP N(t) outputs a 2L-dimensional code Φ(t) = (1−α)t + α⊙N(t), which is lifted to the full parameter vector via an entrywise affine map θ(t) = F(Φ(t)). This design achieves macro-level coherence (coordinated parameter changes when the solution varies sharply in time) while preserving micro-level diversity (independent affine coefficients per entry), and scales as 2N_D + O(Lh) parameters rather than the O(N_D·h) of a naive hypernetwork. Training exploits the natural nonlinear least-squares structure of the PINN loss using a Levenberg-Marquardt optimizer, which is tractable precisely because TINNs are parameter-efficient. The result is up to 4× improved relative L² error and 10× faster convergence over strong baselines (PINN, CoPINN, PirateNet+SOAP) across five benchmark time-dependent PDEs.

## Results

Across five time-dependent PDEs (Burgers, Allen-Cahn, Klein-Gordon, Korteweg-De Vries, Wave), TINNs achieve the best relative L²-error on all benchmarks while using only ~1,145–1,185 trainable parameters (vs. 309K–535K for baselines). Headline errors include 6.89×10⁻⁷ on Burgers, 3.85×10⁻⁶ on Allen-Cahn, and 4.78×10⁻⁶ on Klein-Gordon. Training time is 0.67–0.78 hr per PDE (single A6000 GPU), yielding up to 10.55× speedup over PirateNet+SOAP on Burgers. The error improvement (IMP) over the strongest baseline (PirateNet SOAP) ranges from 2.2× (Allen-Cahn) to 4.3× (Wave).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TINNs outperform all six baselines on every PDE benchmark. Against vanilla PINNs (Raissi et al., 2019), the improvement is dramatic: up to 1.21×10⁵× lower relative L²-error on Allen-Cahn and 7,470× on the Wave equation, while using ~270× fewer parameters. Against CoPINN* (Duan et al., 2025), TINNs win on all five PDEs with margins of 1.4×–686×, and also train faster (0.67–0.78 hr vs. 0.70–1.14 hr). Against PirateNet+SOAP (Wang et al., 2025), the closest competitor, TINNs achieve 2.2×–4.3× lower error with ~460× fewer parameters and 2.3×–10.6× faster wall-clock training. In the ablation with Adam (Table 3), TINNs still beat PirateNet on all three tested PDEs, confirming gains are architectural rather than optimizer-specific. Against HyperPINN (de Avila Belbute-Peres et al., 2021), TINNs achieve 2.8× lower error on Burgers and 42,000× lower on Klein-Gordon, while remaining competitive on Allen-Cahn (3.85E-06 vs. 3.49E-06). Against SPINN (Cho et al., 2023), TINNs win on Burgers (9.71E-05 vs. 9.52E-02) and Allen-Cahn (7.31E-04 vs. 7.12E-01) under a unified Adam training protocol, though the gap narrows on Klein-Gordon (1.61E-03 vs. 5.95E-03 for SPINN).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the PINN framework (Raissi et al., 2019) and departs from the standard space-time formulation by identifying what the authors call the 'time-entanglement problem': treating time as an additional input coordinate forces a single shared representation to capture qualitatively different temporal regimes. The proposed TINN architecture is conceptually related to hypernetworks (HyperPINN) but replaces the expensive full-parameter generation with a compact layer-wise temporal embedding lifted via an entrywise affine map. It also draws on the nonlinear least-squares structure of PINN losses to justify a Levenberg-Marquardt optimizer, positioning itself as orthogonal to existing optimization heuristics (causal training, adaptive sampling, loss reweighting) and architecture variants (SPINN, PirateNet, CoPINN).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of time-entanglement as a structural limitation of space-time PINNs provides a clean conceptual lens for understanding why shared representations struggle with non-stationary dynamics, and the layer-wise time embedding is a practical, architecture-agnostic remedy that could be grafted onto other backbones. The striking parameter efficiency (~1.1K vs. 300K–535K parameters) is both a strength (enabling second-order optimization) and a limitation (LM becomes intractable for larger models, and the ablation with Adam narrows the accuracy gap substantially). Open questions include whether the compact parameterization scales to high-dimensional or strongly nonlinear problems where the low-rank temporal trajectory assumption may break down, and whether the LM advantage persists when the model must grow to handle more complex PDEs.
