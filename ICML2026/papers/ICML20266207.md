## Problem

Neural PDE solvers based on transformer architectures, including Transolver and Transolver++, are limited to meshes of at most ~700k–1M cells due to the linear memory scaling of intermediate activations with mesh resolution. Industrial-scale CFD simulations require meshes exceeding 10^8 cells, making existing solvers infeasible without hundreds of GPUs. This paper addresses the fundamental gap between GPU memory capacity and the resolution demands of real-world engineering geometries.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266207/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes unstructured surface and volume meshes (up to 160M cells) with per-cell geometric features and flow parameters (Mach number, angle of attack, control surface deflections) as input. During inference, the full mesh is never held in memory at once: it is partitioned into memory-compatible chunks for physical state caching, and then decoded coordinate-by-coordinate against the cached states. Outputs include per-cell surface pressure, wall shear stress, volume velocity, and volume pressure fields, from which integrated drag and lift coefficients are computed via surface quadrature over all 8.8M surface points.

## Method

Transolver-3 introduces two architectural optimizations to the Physics-Attention mechanism: (1) faster slice and deslice, which exploits matrix multiplication associativity to move the Linear1 and Linear3 projections from the N-dimensional mesh domain into the compact M-dimensional slice domain, reducing O(N) operations from five to three and eliminating two O(NC) intermediate tensors; and (2) geometry slice tiling, which partitions the N×M slice-weight computation into T tiles with gradient checkpointing, compressing peak memory from O(NM) to O(N_t·M). For training on meshes beyond single-GPU capacity, a geometry amortized training strategy samples random subsets (10^5–10^6 cells) from the full mesh each iteration, forcing the model to learn discretization-invariant physics. At inference, a decoupled two-stage framework first aggregates global mesh information into a compact physical state cache (layer-by-layer, chunk-by-chunk), then decodes the full-resolution field at any spatial point by attending to the cached states, achieving high-fidelity prediction without exceeding memory limits.

## Results

Transolver-3 achieves state-of-the-art relative L2 errors across three industrial-scale aerodynamic benchmarks, outperforming all 8 baselines on 9 out of 10 metrics. On the largest benchmark (DrivAerML, ~160M volume cells), Transolver-3 achieves 3.71% surface pressure error, 4.14% volume velocity error, 5.85% wall shear stress error, and 5.72% volume pressure error, surpassing the strongest baseline AB-UPT (3.82%, 5.93%, 7.29%, 6.08% respectively). The sole exception is AhmedML volume pressure, where Transolver-3 (2.16%) trails AB-UPT (2.07%) by a narrow margin. For integrated aerodynamic coefficients on DrivAerML, Transolver-3 achieves R² scores exceeding 0.963 (C_d) and 0.975 (C_l), surpassing AB-UPT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Transolver-3 is compared against 8 baselines spanning graph-based (Graph U-Net, MeshGraphNet, GINO), transformer-based (GAOT, UPT, AB-UPT), and prior Transolver variants. AB-UPT is the closest competitor, with Transolver-3 winning on 9 of 10 metrics; the only loss is AhmedML volume pressure (2.16% vs. 2.07%), a marginal gap. On the most challenging DrivAerML benchmark (160M cells), Transolver-3 leads AB-UPT by 1.82% on volume velocity (4.14% vs. 5.93%) and 1.44% on wall shear stress (5.85% vs. 7.29%). GNN-based baselines (Graph U-Net, MeshGraphNet, GINO) degrade severely at scale, with DrivAerML errors reaching 15–45%, confirming their poor scalability. GAOT exhibits the worst overall performance (30–61% errors on NASA-CRM and DrivAerML) and suffers prohibitive memory consumption on high-resolution meshes. Among prior Transolver variants, Transolver-3 improves over Transolver++ by 0.8% on NASA-CRM surface pressure and 0.97% on DrivAerML surface pressure, attributed to preserving the Linear1 projection in the slice domain rather than removing it as Transolver++ does.

## Relation to Prior Work

Transolver-3 is the third iteration in the Transolver family, building directly on Transolver's Physics-Attention mechanism (Wu et al., 2024) and Transolver++'s multi-GPU distributed scaling (Luo et al., 2025). The key conceptual departure is replacing hardware-driven parallelism with algorithmic optimizations: reordering slice/deslice operations via matrix multiplication associativity (analogous to Performer), introducing geometry slice tiling (analogous to FlashAttention), and a decoupled inference with physical state caching (analogous to KV cache in LLMs). It also draws on the amortized training idea from sequence truncation in long-context LLMs, applied here to randomly sampled mesh subsets. Among competing scalable solvers, it positions itself against AB-UPT (Alkin et al., 2025), which uses anchored-branched cross-attention rather than the Physics-Attention latent-space formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first neural PDE solver demonstrated on 160M+ cell meshes, bridging a 100x gap from prior state-of-the-art (~700k cells) and making neural surrogates viable for industrial CFD workflows (automotive and aerospace aerodynamics). The decoupled inference paradigm—separating global physical state estimation from local field decoding—is a general architectural insight applicable to any latent-space operator model. However, validation is limited to stationary aerodynamic benchmarks; generalization to transient flows, multi-physics coupling, and cross-domain topologies remains open. The tile-size trade-off between memory and compute, and the reliance on amortized training (which may lose fine-scale geometric detail), are practical limitations for deployment.
