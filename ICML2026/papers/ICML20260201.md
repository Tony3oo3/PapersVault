## Problem

Mechanistic interpretability research routinely identifies attention-head 'circuits' in a single trained model instance, yet it remains unclear whether these circuits are universal across independently initialized refits of the same architecture or merely idiosyncratic to one random seed. Without cross-instance validation, extracted circuits in safety-critical domains (healthcare, finance, nuclear) may reflect stochastic training artifacts rather than robust computational mechanisms, undermining their reliability for oversight and monitoring.

## Input / Output

The method consumes N independently initialized (different random seed) pre-trained decoder-only transformer refits of an identical architecture, along with a fixed set of 100 evaluation prompts (or 6×20 prompts truncated to 5–50 tokens for length-sweep experiments). For each head, post-softmax attention score matrices are extracted per prompt and flattened into vectors. Outputs include per-head stability scores (cosine similarity of best-matching attention score matrices across refits), layer-wise stability curves, cross-layer alignment heatmaps, within-layer uniqueness scores, residual-stream CKA similarity, and per-layer Pearson correlations between head stability and post-ablation perplexity change.

## Method

The paper trains 26 architectural configurations (2/4/8/12 layers × 8/16 heads × MLP/attention-only × Adam/AdamW) with 50 refits each (5 for GPT-2-small), all pre-trained from scratch on C4 (2B tokens) or OpenWebText (9B tokens). The core stability metric compares an anchor head's flattened attention score matrix against every candidate head in the same layer of a pair refit via cosine similarity, averaged over prompts; the maximum over candidates yields a best-match score that is then averaged across all pair refits, producing a permutation-invariant stability measure that obviates Hungarian matching. A cross-layer variant relaxes the same-layer constraint to reveal off-diagonal matching patterns. Additional probes include within-layer uniqueness (mean similarity to peer heads in the same refit), residual-stream CKA using RBF-kernel centered Gram matrices, head-ablation perplexity deltas, and meta-SNE embeddings of head activation geometries. A key mechanistic finding is that AdamW's decoupled weight decay suppresses query-weight ℓ₂ norm growth, which correlates negatively with layer-wise stability, thereby substantially improving cross-seed head stability—especially in deeper models—without degrading validation perplexity.

## Results

For an 8-layer, 8-head MLP architecture trained with Adam, layer-wise attention-head stability drops from ≈1.0 in layer 1 to ≈0.70 in layer 5, confirming a pronounced mid-depth instability dip. The stability gap ΔS (most-stable minus least-stable layer) widens with model depth, being most prominent in 8- and 12-layer models. Replacing Adam with AdamW (decoupled weight decay, λ=0.1) substantially increases head seed-stability for deeper MLP models (8- and 12-layer) and all attention-only architectures, while leaving validation perplexity on WikiText-2 nearly unchanged. The residual stream, measured via CKA, is consistently more stable than the corresponding attention heads across all 26 architectures and both optimizers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper is an empirical characterization study rather than a method-vs-baseline comparison, so no external baselines are benchmarked. The primary internal comparison is Adam vs. AdamW: AdamW (weight decay λ=0.1) yields a clear increase in head seed-stability for 8- and 12-layer MLP models and all attention-only architectures, while gains for shallow 2- and 4-layer MLP models are small (attributed to limited norm growth at low depth). Validation perplexity on WikiText-2 remains nearly identical between Adam and AdamW, confirming no performance trade-off. A second key comparison is depth: the stability gap ΔS between the most- and least-stable layers widens monotonically from 2-layer to 12-layer models, and the least-stable layer shifts toward mid-depth (relative depth r ≈ 0.4–0.8) as total layers increase. A third comparison is within-layer vs. cross-layer best-match: relaxing the layer constraint slightly raises stability (larger candidate pool) but preserves the mid-depth dip pattern. Finally, the residual stream (CKA-based) is consistently more stable than individual attention heads across all configurations, suggesting the skip-connection pathway acts as an architectural stabilizer independent of optimizer choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the mechanistic interpretability 'universality hypothesis' (Chughtai et al., 2023; Olah et al., 2020) from a qualitative claim to a systematically quantified, layer-resolved measurement of attention-head stability across independently initialized refits. Methodologically, it departs from prior cross-seed comparison work (van der Wal et al., 2025; Müller-Eberstein et al., 2023; Michaelov et al., 2025) that relied on downstream probing or activation-based similarity (CKA, CCA, SVCCA) by operating directly on attention score matrices, which the authors argue are on a common token-token basis and thus avoid the permutation-symmetry and transformation-sensitivity issues of activation-space metrics. The closest methodological ancestor is Kobayashi et al. (2024), who used attention maps to analyze feed-forward blocks; here the same object is repurposed as a cross-seed matching signal for individual heads.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that middle-layer attention heads are simultaneously the least stable across refits and the most functionally important (negative stability–ablation correlation in deeper layers) directly challenges the assumption that identified circuits are robust, transferable explanations of model behavior—a prerequisite for safety-critical deployment in domains like healthcare or finance. The practical takeaway that AdamW substantially improves head stability at no perplexity cost is immediately actionable for interpretability pipelines. Key limitations include the small model scale (≤124M params), the restriction to GPT-like decoder-only architectures, and the absence of a broader hyperparameter sweep; whether the mid-depth stability dip persists at frontier scale, or whether more aggressive regularization (e.g., spectral constraints) further narrows the gap, remains open.
