## Problem

LLMs deployed in real-world applications must follow scenario-specific behavioral and safety specifications that vary across domains and evolve over time, yet no existing benchmark systematically evaluates this joint compliance. Prior safety benchmarks apply uniform standards, and instruction-following benchmarks focus on question-level constraints, leaving the challenge of dynamic, scenario-level specification alignment unaddressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265826/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each evaluation instance pairs a text prompt with ~20 scenario-level specifications (roughly 10 safety-spec and 10 behavioral-spec) uniformly embedded into the question to ensure fairness. The model produces a single text response, which an LLM evaluator (GPT-4.1 at temperature 0) judges spec-by-spec as YES, NO, or NA. The final score, Specification Alignment Rate (SAR), assigns 0 to any response violating a safety-spec and a value in [α, 1] to safe responses based on the proportion of behavioral-spec satisfied, with α=0.3 as the safety baseline offset.

## Method

The paper introduces SPECBENCH (5 scenarios, 103 specs, 1,500 prompts with WildTeaming-based attack enhancement) and ALIGN3, a single-pass test-time deliberation method that enforces specifications progressively within one forward pass. ALIGN3 operates in three hierarchical stages: (1) Behavior Optimization injects behavioral-spec early in the reasoning trace to maximize helpfulness; (2) Safety-Guided Refinement applies safety-spec near the end of thinking to prune risky reasoning paths; (3) Holistic Specification Audit performs a full compliance check and gap-filling before the final answer. This progressive separation of behavioral and safety objectives addresses the safety-behavior trade-off without the token overhead of multi-pass methods like Self-Refine or TPO, achieving up to 11.89% SAR improvement over the non-thinking baseline with fewer than 2k tokens.

## Results

On Qwen3-14B-thinking, ALIGN3 achieves 62.92% SAR, a +11.89% gain over the non-thinking baseline (51.03%) and +5.60% over the vanilla thinking model (57.32%), approaching GPT-4.1's 69.20% with only ~1.8k completion tokens. On DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B, ALIGN3 raises SAR from 35.01% to 42.75% (+7.74%) with ~1.4k tokens. Multi-pass baselines (Best-of-N, Self-Refine, TPO) consume 14k–38k tokens per sample, while ALIGN3 and MoreThink stay under 2k tokens, making ALIGN3 the most effective single-pass method by a wide margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ALIGN3 outperforms all five TTD baselines on both tested model families. On Qwen3-14B-thinking, ALIGN3 (62.92% SAR) beats MoreThink (57.30%) by 5.62 points, TPO (58.76%) by 4.16 points, Self-Refine (57.97%) by 4.95 points, Best-of-N (53.21%) by 9.71 points, and ZeroThink (51.03%) by 11.89 points. On DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B, ALIGN3 (42.75%) surpasses MoreThink (36.95%) by 5.80 points and ZeroThink (35.99%) by 6.76 points. Notably, Self-Refine actually degrades Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct performance by 9.38 points (44.54% → 35.16%), likely due to weaker generation quality and reliance on a single refinement path without external reward signals. In terms of efficiency, ALIGN3 uses ~1.8k tokens versus 14k–38k for multi-pass methods (Best-of-N, Self-Refine, TPO), achieving the best SAR with the lowest token overhead among all methods that improve over vanilla.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends test-time scaling (TTS) from its traditional domain of math and code reasoning to the problem of specification alignment, building on thinking-intervention methods (Wu et al., 2025) and iterative refinement approaches (Self-Refine, TPO). It departs from prior safety benchmarks (SALAD-Bench, AIR-Bench, HarmBench) that apply uniform, one-size-fits-all safety standards by introducing scenario-specific, dynamic specifications that jointly encode both safety boundaries and behavioral preferences. The formulation also extends instruction-following benchmarks (IFEval, GuideBench) from question-level constraints to systematic, scenario-level specifications that persist across all prompts within a deployment context, and contrasts with deliberative alignment (Guan et al., 2024) by operating at inference time rather than through training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SPECBENCH provides the first unified evaluation framework for the safety-behavior trade-off across realistic deployment scenarios, filling a gap where prior benchmarks evaluated safety and helpfulness in isolation. The finding that a lightweight single-pass method (ALIGN3) matches or exceeds multi-pass TTD baselines at a fraction of the token cost suggests that specification-aware prompting is a practical, low-overhead strategy for organizations that need to adapt LLM behavior to evolving, scenario-specific policies without retraining. Open questions include how well these findings generalize beyond the five curated scenarios, whether TTD can close the gap to frontier models like GPT-5-chat (82% SAR), and how to handle specification conflicts or priority ordering when safety and behavioral specs pull in opposite directions.
