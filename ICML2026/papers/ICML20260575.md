## Problem

Data mixing methods for language model training suffer from two unaddressed challenges: (1) the configuration space of the standard offline mixing schema (proxy swarm size, regression family, optimization solver, etc.) is poorly understood, with conflicting or unjustified design choices across existing methods; and (2) all existing methods assume a fixed domain set, whereas real-world LM development involves iterative domain updates (additions, removals, revisions, partitions) that force expensive full recomputation of the mixture at each step.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260575/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a domain set with per-domain token counts, a target model configuration (parameter count and token budget), a downstream task suite for evaluation, and a repetition factor k that caps how many times any document can be sampled. For the reuse component, it additionally takes an existing mixture vector from a prior domain set. The output is a probability vector over domains (the mixture), produced by solving a constrained convex optimization that minimizes predicted average BPB subject to repetition caps and a KL penalty toward the natural distribution. In the reuse setting, the output dimensionality is reduced from m' to 1+|D_comp| by aggregating frozen domains into a single virtual domain.

## Method

OLMIXBASE configures the offline mixing schema with empirically justified choices: 30M-parameter proxy models (smallest size achieving ρ>89 rank correlation with 1B targets), K≥3(m+1) swarm runs (linear sample complexity in domain count), per-task log-linear regression f̂ᵢ(p)=cᵢ+exp(Aᵢᵀp), and an exact convex solver with KL regularization (λ=0.05) and repetition constraints pⱼ≤kNⱼ/R. For the evolving-domain problem, FULLMIXTUREREUSE freezes the relative ratios of unaffected domains from the prior mixture, aggregates them into one virtual domain, and applies OLMIXBASE only over the 1+|D_comp|-dimensional reduced space, requiring O(|D_comp|) proxy runs instead of O(m′). A theoretical analysis (Theorem 4.1) bounds the performance gap by a coupling term κ(D_fix, D_comp) times the reuse gap ‖p̃_Dfix − q⋆_Dfix‖, motivating PARTIALMIXTUREREUSE, which selectively recomputes a subset of unaffected domains to reduce coupling while still interpolating cost between full reuse and full recomputation.

## Results

Across 5 domain-set updates culminating in 64 domains, FULLMIXTUREREUSE achieves +11.6% improvement over the natural distribution (average BPB) while using 74% fewer proxy runs (216 vs. 832) than full recomputation at c=3 (+12.2%). PARTIALMIXTUREREUSE closes the remaining gap to +12.0% (98% of full recomputation) with 272 runs, still 67% fewer than full recomputation. At matched budgets of 216–272 proxy runs, mixture reuse outperforms full recomputation at c=1 (+10.8%) and swarm reuse (+11.4% at 268 runs). The best mixture (PARTIALMIXTUREREUSE) is 3.05× more data-efficient than the natural distribution, reaching the natural distribution's final BPB in ~20K training steps versus 61K.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the regression model family comparison (RQ3), the log-linear model from Data Mixing Laws (Ye et al., 2025) achieves the best regression fit across all swarm sizes, outperforming LightGBM (RegMix), Gaussian Process (ADMIRE-BayesOpt), BiMix, and AutoScale; this is confirmed by 1B-model downstream evaluation at K=125. In the main evolving-domain experiment, FULLMIXTUREREUSE beats swarm reuse on both performance (+11.6% vs. +11.4%) and cost (216 vs. 268 runs), and beats full recomputation at c=1 (+10.8%) at the same budget. PARTIALMIXTUREREUSE nearly matches full recomputation at c=3 (+12.0% vs. +12.2%) while requiring 67% fewer proxy runs. The paper also shows that RegMix's recommended 1M proxy size is unreliable (Spearman ρ=73), whereas the 30M proxy used in OLMIXBASE achieves ρ=89.6 with 1B targets, and that O(m) swarm size (not the quadratic scaling assumed by BiMix and DML) suffices for strong mixes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the offline mixing schema (swarm construction → regression fitting → mixture optimization) shared by methods like RegMix, DML, AutoScale, BiMix, and ADMIRE-BayesOpt, but departs from them in two key ways. First, it treats the schema's configuration space as an open empirical question rather than a set of fixed design choices, systematically resolving contradictions across prior methods (e.g., proxy size, swarm size, regression family). Second, it extends the task from a fixed domain set to an evolving one, introducing mixture reuse as a mechanism to avoid full recomputation after domain updates—a setting prior work explicitly assumes away.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first comprehensive, empirically-justified configuration guide for offline data mixing (proxy >15M, O(m) swarm runs, log-linear regression, repetition constraints), directly addressing the practical confusion practitioners face when choosing among competing methods. The mixture reuse mechanism is practically significant for iterative LM development projects (OLMo, SmolLM, DCLM) where domain sets evolve repeatedly, achieving 95% of full recomputation's gains at 74% less compute. Key limitations include validation only at 1B scale, the lack of a principled procedure for selecting which domains to recompute in PARTIALMIXTUREREUSE (relying on heuristics), and the assumption that log-linear performance models hold. Open directions include extending to online mixing and co-designing mixing with other LM development workflows.
