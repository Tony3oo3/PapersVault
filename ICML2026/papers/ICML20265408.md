## Problem

Existing federated quantile regression (FQR) methods provide optimal point estimators but lack statistical guarantees such as asymptotic normality, uncertainty quantification, and sensitivity analysis, which are essential for reliable inference in distributed settings. Moreover, no prior FQR work addresses the practically important case of heterogeneous structured data, where each local agent observes or selects a distinct subset of features, making local estimators trained on incompatible feature spaces that cannot be directly aggregated. The nonsmooth check loss function further complicates the theoretical analysis compared to standard federated mean regression.

## Input / Output

Each of M agents holds n samples with only a subset of d_m out of d total features (determined by a permutation matrix Π_m), along with a pre-specified quantile level τ ∈ (0,1). The method requires agents to additionally estimate the conditional density f_ξ(0|x_{i,m+}) via kernel density estimation on local residuals. The output consists of a global coefficient vector β̂(τ) ∈ R^d, agent-specific local estimators T_m β̂(τ) ∈ R^{d_m} that enable prediction using only locally observed features, and the asymptotic covariance matrix C(Ω(W*)) for inference. Communication is strictly one-shot: each agent transmits 2d_m² + d_m scalars (local estimate, variance estimate, and density-weighted covariance) in a single round, and the server returns a d_m-dimensional local parameter vector.

## Method

The proposed one-shot weighted ensemble estimator (OSW) operates in three steps: (1) each agent solves a local quantile regression on its observed feature subset, (2) a central server aggregates local estimates by solving a weighted least-squares problem where the transformation matrix T_m = [I_{d_m}, A^{-1}B]Π_m maps the global parameter into each agent's local space, accounting for correlations between observed and unobserved features through the Schur complement, and (3) the server distributes the global estimator and its agent-specific transformations. The key theoretical contribution is identifying an optimal weight matrix W_m* = R_m V_m^{-1} R_m (where R_m involves the conditional error density and V_m is the sandwich variance) that minimizes the asymptotic covariance among all possible weightings, yielding uniformly minimum variance estimation. The paper establishes asymptotic normality under any weighting scheme, proves agent-dependent stability with μ = O(1/√N), and derives an out-of-sample generalization bound of O_p(1/√n), all without the stringent constraint M ≪ √N required by prior meta-analysis approaches.

## Results

On the California Housing dataset (τ=0.5), the proposed OSW estimator achieves MSPE of 4.40 at n=500 per agent, compared to 2.03 for the centralized oracle and 44.42 for Naive-OSFL, 575.89 for Naive-Local, 198.76 for DA-OSW, and 54.69 for UW-OSW. In simulations under Cauchy-distributed noise (M=40 agents, d=40 features), OSW consistently attains the lowest MSPE across all quantile levels (τ=0.2, 0.5, 0.8) and remains competitive with the centralized method, while baselines plateau once sample size exceeds 1000. The empirical mean and variance of estimation errors converge to their theoretical values, confirming the asymptotic normality guarantee. Communication cost per agent is O(d_i²), requiring only 2d_i²+2d_i scalars across two rounds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares OSW against five baselines: Naive-Local (independent local QR), Naive-OSFL (simple averaging of local estimates), Centralized (full-data oracle with all features), DA-OSW (removes the T_m transformation, using only observed-feature subvectors), and UW-OSW (replaces optimal weights with random matrices). OSW dominates all non-oracle baselines by large margins: at n=500 on California Housing, OSW achieves MSPE 4.40 versus 44.42 (Naive-OSFL), 575.89 (Naive-Local), 198.76 (DA-OSW), and 54.69 (UW-OSW). The gap to the centralized oracle (2.03) is modest, indicating that the weighted ensemble effectively recovers most of the full-data performance despite feature heterogeneity. In simulations, OSW uniformly outperforms all baselines across quantile levels τ∈{0.2, 0.5, 0.8} and noise distributions (normal, heteroscedastic normal, exponential, t(5), Cauchy), with the advantage widening at extreme quantiles where estimation variability is higher. The DA-OSW ablation confirms the critical role of the T_m transformation in accounting for inter-feature correlations, while UW-OSW validates the optimality of the proposed W_m* weighting scheme.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the structured-missing-data framework of Cheng et al. (2023), which addressed collaborative linear regression with quadratic loss, to the nonsmooth quantile regression setting, introducing a one-shot weighted ensemble estimator with data-driven optimal weights. It departs from prior FQR work (Tan et al. 2022, Shi et al. 2025, Wang & Lian 2020/2023) that assumes homogeneous feature sets across agents and focuses on consistency or communication complexity rather than full statistical inference. The stability-based generalization analysis adapts the agent-removal notion from Bousquet & Elisseeff (2002) and Wu et al. (2020) to the federated context, and the Bahadur representation technique is borrowed from classical quantile regression theory to handle the nonsmooth check loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide asymptotic normality and uniformly minimum variance guarantees for FQR under heterogeneous structured data, filling a gap where prior methods offered only point estimates without uncertainty quantification. The one-shot communication requirement (O(d_i²) per agent) makes it practical for bandwidth-constrained federated deployments. Key limitations include the Gaussian feature assumption (needed for tractable optimal weight derivation) and the practical need for kernel density estimation of the conditional error density, which adds implementation complexity. Extending the theory to sub-Gaussian or general feature distributions remains an open technical challenge the authors acknowledge.
