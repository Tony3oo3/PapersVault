## Problem

Multi-turn agentic RL training suffers from pervasive instability and frequent training collapse, despite advances in credit assignment and trajectory filtering. The paper identifies the root cause as inefficient exploration—termed 'hesitation'—where LLM agents generate low-information tokens and repetitive turns that neither reduce uncertainty nor advance task progress, injecting noise into credit assignment and destabilizing policy updates. Existing stabilization methods operate at coarse trajectory levels or through implicit reward shaping, lacking principled fine-grained control over reasoning dynamics.

## Input / Output

At each turn, the agent receives a task prompt, the current environment observation (state), and a discrete set of admissible actions, and must produce a structured response consisting of a reasoning trace enclosed in  tags followed by an executable action command in <action> tags. A key operational detail is that T²PO intervenes *during* generation: it can deterministically overwrite pre-softmax logits to force-emit the reasoning terminator and inject a fixed token queue [, \n, <action>], explicitly delineating the boundary between reasoning and execution phases without sampling from the model distribution.

## Method

T²PO introduces a self-calibrated uncertainty signal M_t that fuses normalized Shannon entropy and top-j token confidence into a single scalar, overcoming the blind spots of each measure individually. At the token level, Token-Level Thinking Intervention (TTI) monitors the temporal variation of M_t over a sliding window; once the average marginal change falls below a tolerance ε, it deterministically terminates reasoning by overwriting logits to emit  and appending a fixed structural queue, preventing overthinking without truncating task-relevant tokens. At the turn level, Turn-Level Dynamical Sampling (TDS) aggregates token-level uncertainty into a geometric-mean turn signal Φ^k and resamples any turn whose variation Γ^k from the previous turn falls below threshold η, suppressing redundant cross-turn reasoning loops. The policy update combines trajectory-level group advantage (GRPO-style) with turn-level state-conditional advantage (GiGPO-style) into a fused group-in-group objective with clipped PPO loss, and the full pipeline is initialized via rejection fine-tuning (RFT) on high-scoring self-generated trajectories to ensure structural validity before RL begins.

## Results

On WebShop (Qwen3-4B-RFT), T²PO achieves a Task Score of 93.84±0.22 and Success Rate of 81.64±0.39, surpassing the SOTA baseline GiGPO+DAPO (86.54±9.81 / 74.02±8.18) by ~7.3 points in success rate with dramatically lower variance. On ALFWorld, T²PO reaches 90.23±1.38 overall success rate, a ~9.4-point gain over GiGPO (80.47±2.43), with the largest improvements on Pick (97.36 vs. 87.94) and Clean (98.33 vs. 87.95). On Search QA (Qwen3-4B), T²PO attains an average EM of 54.93, outperforming GiGPO (52.97) with the most pronounced gain on MuSiQue (16.64 vs. 13.40, a 24% relative improvement). Across all benchmarks, T²PO exhibits substantially reduced run-to-run variance (e.g., ±0.39 vs. ±8.18 on WebShop success rate), indicating markedly improved training stability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

T²PO outperforms all 13 baselines across every benchmark. Against the SOTA multi-turn RL method GiGPO, T²PO gains +7.81 points on WebShop success rate (81.64 vs. 73.83) and +9.76 on ALFWorld (90.23 vs. 80.47), while reducing variance by an order of magnitude (±0.39 vs. ±3.04). The enhanced GiGPO+DAPO baseline, which adds dynamic sampling, is also surpassed by +7.62 on WebShop and +9.37 on ALFWorld, confirming that T²PO's uncertainty-guided control is more effective than heuristic trajectory filtering. Single-turn RL methods (PPO, GRPO) trail by 13–20 points, underscoring the necessity of multi-turn credit assignment. On Search QA, T²PO leads GiGPO by +1.96 average EM, with the largest relative gain on MuSiQue (+24%) and 2Wiki (+6.6%), while also surpassing prior search-augmented methods (Search-R1, ZeroSearch, StepSearch) by 5–16 points. Among alternative thinking-control strategies (lengthy reward, short-CoT cold-start, hard thinking budget, void-turn filtering), T²PO's adaptive TTI+TDS mechanism yields 3.6–8.0 points higher success rate, demonstrating that static or heuristic length controls cannot match uncertainty-driven adaptive termination.

## Relation to Prior Work

T2PO builds directly on GiGPO's group-in-group hierarchical advantage estimation as its policy update backbone, and adapts DAPO's dynamic sampling idea from single-turn prompt-group filtering to a turn-level resampling mechanism for multi-turn agentic RL. It departs from prior stabilization approaches (trajectory filtering in SimpleTIR, process reward modeling, length-based reward shaping) by introducing an explicit, uncertainty-guided control signal that operates at two granularities: token-level adaptive thinking termination (TTI) and turn-level dynamical resampling (TDS). The self-calibrated uncertainty signal, which fuses normalized entropy and top-j confidence to overcome the blind spots of either measure alone, is the paper's core conceptual contribution, positioning it between entropy-based exploration methods (SEED-GRPO, DeepConf) and heuristic thinking-budget approaches (Gemini's hard budget, lengthy reward).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Training collapse in multi-turn agentic RL remains a major practical barrier to deploying LLM agents, and this paper offers a principled diagnosis (inefficient 'hesitation' exploration) plus a plug-and-play remedy that reduces both variance and token/turn consumption without adding parameters or environment-specific heuristics. The method's generality is supported by plug-and-play integration with GSPO and evaluation across three heterogeneous environments, but open questions remain: whether the uncertainty-signal design (α, ε, η, N) transfers across model families and scales beyond 8B parameters, whether the one-time TTI activation constraint is sufficient for tasks requiring multiple reasoning phases, and how the approach interacts with asynchronous or off-policy training regimes where staleness compounds. The ~25% reduction in interaction turns and the dramatic variance reduction (±0.2–1.4 vs. ±4–10 for baselines) are practically significant for anyone running multi-turn RL at scale.
