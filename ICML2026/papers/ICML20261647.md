limitations: EAKV operates strictly as an efficiency optimization layer and cannot correct inherent generative flaws such as hallucinations originating from the backbone model. surprise_factor: The most genuinely surprising finding is the non-monotonic entropy pattern across layers (Insight 2), which contradicts the common heuristic that deeper layers require progressively more compression; the ablation showing that *reducing* cache from 8K to 4K tokens actually *improves* accuracy (+1.2%) is also counter-intuitive, confirming that semantic density outweighs raw token count. Performance gains (0.6–6.5%) are moderate and well-evidenced across four architectures and five benchmarks, so the surprise is conceptual rather than numerical.

## Problem

Long-video understanding in MLLMs is bottlenecked by unbounded KV cache growth (e.g., ~140 GB for a 1-hour video at 1 FPS in a 7B model), yet existing compression methods apply uniform or monotonically decaying layer-wise budgets that ignore the actual information density at each layer and head. The paper identifies a 'Semantic-Memory Mismatch': attention entropy exhibits non-monotonic depth patterns and high spatio-temporal volatility (up to 4.2× range within a single layer), meaning rigid allocation strategies either over-compress semantically dense regions or waste budget on redundant context.

## Input / Output

EAKV operates as a plug-and-play inference-time module on top of any MLLM. It consumes video frames sampled at 2 fps (up to 2048 frames) and a text prompt, which are encoded into visual features X ∈ R^{F×T×D} and prompt embeddings P ∈ R^{S×D}. During streaming chunk-by-chunk processing, the method produces a compressed KV cache where each (layer, head) slot contains a small set of retained salient tokens plus one weighted-sum 'contextual anchor' representing the residual tokens, all under a global budget B_total = ρ·M·L·H. The final output is the MLLM's generated text answer, now achievable within commodity GPU memory (e.g., 51.6 GB for 2048 frames on a single A100).

## Method

EAKV is a training-free, entropy-driven framework with three coupled modules. First, Fine-Grained Entropy Valuation computes the mean Shannon entropy of each attention head's pre-RoPE attention matrix, yielding a position-agnostic 'semantic density' score that captures distribution shape rather than absolute magnitude. Second, Entropy-Driven Adaptive Allocation splits the global budget into a fixed Layer-wise Safety Net (fraction α distributed uniformly to guarantee minimal connectivity) and a flexible portion allocated via Z-score-normalized entropy scores passed through a temperature-scaled softmax, so heads with higher entropy earn proportionally larger KV budgets. Third, Asymmetric Temporal Integration applies 'Fast-Attack, Slow-Decay' smoothing (β_rise > β_decay) to cumulative saliency scores to prevent flickering eviction of persistent objects, after which Hybrid Token Compression retains the top-k tokens and aggregates all residual tokens into a single contextual anchor (a weighted sum with a Center-of-Mass Position ID), preserving global background context without hard eviction. The paper provides a KKT-based proof that this entropy-proportional allocation strictly minimizes global approximation error over uniform allocation under a fixed budget.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-VL-7B, EAKV achieves 67.8% on VideoMME (All), 59.2% on VideoMME (Long), 63.3% on LongVideoBench, 74.8% on MLVU, and 50.1% on LVBench, improving over the unmodified backbone by +2.4%, +3.6%, +3.8%, +4.6%, and +4.8% respectively. Against existing compression methods on Qwen2.5-VL-3B, EAKV reaches 55.3% (VideoMME Long), 63.0% (VideoMME All), 66.8% (MLVU), and 46.7% (LVBench), surpassing the strongest baseline VL-Cache by 2.1%, 1.7%, 2.3%, and 4.3% respectively. The framework also delivers consistent gains on LLaVA-Video-7B (+3.3% VideoMME All, +3.2% LVBench) and InternVL3.5-8B (+1.3% VideoMME All, +3.4% LVBench), with overall improvements ranging from 0.6% to 6.5% across all evaluated model scales.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against compression methods (Table 2, Qwen2.5-VL-3B), EAKV outperforms all nine baselines on every benchmark: it beats VL-Cache (the strongest baseline) by 2.1% on VideoMME Long (55.3 vs. 53.2), 1.7% on VideoMME All (63.0 vs. 61.3), 2.3% on MLVU (66.8 vs. 64.5), and 4.3% on LVBench (46.7 vs. 42.4); it also surpasses FastV, FitPrune, SparseVLM, PyramidDrop, LOOK-M, Video-X2L, LongVU, and ∞-Video across all reported metrics. Against host-model baselines (Table 1), EAKV consistently improves every backbone: Qwen2.5-VL-7B gains +4.8% on LVBench (50.1 vs. 45.3) and +4.6% on MLVU (74.8 vs. 70.2); Qwen2-VL-7B gains +6.5% on LVBench (48.9 vs. 42.4) and +5.1% on MLVU (72.0 vs. 66.9); LLaVA-Video-7B gains +3.2% on LVBench (47.4 vs. 44.2); and InternVL3.5-8B gains +3.4% on LVBench (45.2 vs. 41.8). On Video-MMMU (Appendix E), EAKV improves all three host models by 1.3–1.9% overall. The EAKV-enhanced Qwen2.5-VL-7B (67.8% VideoMME All, 50.1% LVBench) approaches but does not surpass proprietary GPT-4o (71.9% VideoMME All) or Gemini-1.5-Pro (75.0% VideoMME All), though it exceeds them on LVBench (50.1 vs. 30.8 and 33.1). The paper does not report any setting where EAKV underperforms its host model baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in KV cache compression that relies on absolute attention magnitude (Top-K selection) with uniform or heuristically-decaying layerwise budgets. It repurposes the information-theoretic concept of Shannon entropy—previously used in attention sink analysis (Xiao et al., 2024)—as a distribution-aware allocation signal, shifting from 'which tokens score highest' to 'which heads are aggregating the most semantically dense context.' The contextual anchor mechanism (weighted aggregation of evicted tokens) extends the hard-eviction strategies of H2O, SnapKV, and PyramidKV by preserving a compressed residual representation, while the asymmetric temporal integration draws from hysteresis concepts in signal processing to stabilize saliency tracking across streaming video chunks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of non-monotonic, layer-wise entropy patterns (high in shallow layers, reduced in middle layers, fluctuating in deep layers) challenges the widely assumed monotonic decay of information density with depth, and the theoretical proof that entropy-proportional allocation strictly dominates uniform allocation under convex error models provides a principled foundation for adaptive budgeting. Practically, the 11.5× compression ratio enabling 2048-frame inference on a single 80GB GPU (eliminating OOMs) makes long-video MLLM deployment feasible on commodity hardware. However, the framework is explicitly an efficiency layer and cannot correct backbone hallucinations, and its generalization to non-video long-context tasks (e.g., multi-document reasoning) or to quantized KV caches remains unexplored.
