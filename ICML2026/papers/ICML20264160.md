## Problem

Diffusion Flow Matching (DFM) has shown strong empirical performance in generative modeling, yet rigorous non-asymptotic convergence guarantees remain largely absent. The only prior KL bounds for DFM (Gentiloni Silveri et al., 2024) scale as O(d⁴) in dimension and require integrability conditions on the scores of both marginals, while no Wasserstein-2 guarantees existed at all. This paper closes both gaps by improving the dimensional dependence to O(d³) under weaker assumptions and establishing the first fully quantitative W₂ convergence bounds for DFM.

## Input / Output

The method takes a coupling π between a base distribution μ and a target distribution ν* on ℝᵈ, along with a neural network s_θ(t,x) trained to approximate the mimicking drift in L² norm (with error ε²). It produces samples from an approximate target distribution by simulating an Euler-Maruyama discretization up to time 1−δ, and outputs explicit convergence bounds: KL(ν*‖ν_θ*) ≤ O(ε² + h·d³) and W₂(ν*, ν_θ*) ≤ O(ε + √h·√d³), where h is the time step size. The bounds hold under finite 8th-moment conditions on the marginals and integrability of the coupling's score (or, in the early-stopping regime, only the conditional coupling's score).

## Method

For KL convergence, the analysis applies Girsanov's theorem to decompose the divergence, then bounds the time-integrated L²-norm of the reciprocal characteristic (∂ₜ + ℒₜᴹ)β̃ₜ of the mimicking drift. The key technical novelty is an integration-by-parts argument that transfers one derivative onto the coupling, reducing the order of logarithmic derivatives from three to two and thereby improving the dimensional scaling from d⁴ to d³. For W₂ convergence, a synchronous coupling between the Markovian projection and the generative process is used with a recursive time-step argument; the critical step is bounding the spatial Lipschitz constant of the mimicking drift via its Jacobian representation and log-Sobolev/Poincaré inequalities under a weak log-concavity condition on π. A novel non-uniform step-size schedule (h_k = h·min{t_k, 1−t_k} near the boundaries) further accelerates convergence in the early-stopping regime, yielding W₂,FM² error of O(ε²) with only O(ε⁻²) computational complexity.

## Results

The paper establishes dimension-improved convergence guarantees for Brownian-bridge-based Diffusion Flow Matching. The headline result is an O(d³) dimensional scaling for KL divergence bounds (Theorems 1–3), improving upon the prior O(d⁴) scaling of Gentiloni Silveri et al. (2024). For the Wasserstein-2 distance (Theorem 4), the bound scales as O(√h) in the time step and O(√(d³)) in dimension, with a multiplicative constant C = exp(8√2/√α_π · exp(M_π/α_π) · ||∇²logπ||_{L²(π)}). With the novel step-size schedule (Corollary 2), achieving W²_{2,FM} = O(ε²) requires only h = Õ(ε²/d³) and δ = O(ε²/d), yielding O(ε⁻²) computational complexity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper, so comparisons are between convergence-rate guarantees rather than empirical performance. The primary baseline is Gentiloni Silveri et al. (2024, NeurIPS), which established the first explicit KL bounds for DFM with O(d⁴) dimensional scaling; this paper improves that to O(d³) in both the non-early-stopped (Theorem 1) and early-stopped (Theorem 2) regimes, while also relaxing the integrability assumptions on the marginals' scores. For the early-stopping regime, the novel geometric step-size schedule (Theorem 3) further accelerates convergence, reducing the required step size from h = O(ε¹⁰/d⁷) (Corollary 1) to h = Õ(ε²/d³) (Corollary 2), improving computational complexity from O(ε⁻¹⁰) to O(ε⁻²). For Wasserstein-2 convergence, the only prior work providing fully quantitative W₂ bounds for DFM is Xiangjun & Zhongjia (2025), which achieves O(√d) scaling but is restricted to Gaussian priors with compactly supported targets; this paper's Theorem 4 provides O(√(d³)) scaling for arbitrary priors under weak log-concavity, trading a worse dimensional exponent for substantially broader applicability. The Liu et al. (2025) two-sided early-stopping analysis is noted as structurally inferior because it introduces a non-vanishing initialization error that cannot be controlled in practice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the first DFM convergence analysis by Gentiloni Silveri et al. (NeurIPS 2024), which established KL guarantees with O(d⁴) dimensional scaling, by improving the scaling to O(d³) through a key integration-by-parts argument that reduces the order of logarithmic derivatives needing control from three to two. It also builds on the stochastic interpolant framework of Albergo et al. (2023) and the Markovian projection construction of Gyöngy (1986), and imports the weak log-concavity machinery of Conforti (2022) to establish the first W₂ convergence guarantees for DFM. The analysis departs from prior SGM convergence work (Chen et al., Gao et al.) by handling the non-Markovian structure of the stochastic interpolant and the intractable mimicking drift specific to DFM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The O(d³) vs. O(d⁴) improvement is practically significant for high-dimensional generative modeling, reducing the number of discretization steps needed for a fixed accuracy target. The W₂ guarantees fill a notable gap: prior DFM theory only covered KL divergence, while W₂ is the more natural metric for evaluating sample quality in generative modeling. Open questions highlighted by the authors include further relaxing score integrability assumptions, achieving sharper dimensional dependence, and extending the analysis to a statistical (finite-sample) setting rather than the population-level drift approximation assumed here.
