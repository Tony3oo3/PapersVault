## Problem

While factual knowledge in LLMs has been localized to specific neurons and layers, it remained unclear whether higher-level behavioral capabilities (mathematical reasoning, code generation, linguistic behaviors) are similarly localized to identifiable model components. Even if such task-specific components exist, identifying them via exhaustive per-head ablation requires thousands of model evaluations, making the process computationally prohibitive for modern models with over 1000 attention heads.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260190/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on a pretrained Transformer LLM (no training or fine-tuning required) and consumes a small evaluation subset (~100 samples) from a task-specific dataset, a number of mask configurations (100–400), and a target head count k (typically 5). It outputs a ranked set of task-specific attention heads along with their Lasso coefficient importance scores, where the most negative coefficients indicate the heads whose ablation causes the largest task-specific performance drop. The signal is aggregated task accuracy rather than per-token or per-neuron outputs, and the granularity is at the attention-head level (coarser than neurons, finer than layers).

## Method

The paper formulates head identification as a sparse linear inverse problem: each attention head's contribution to task performance is treated as a sparse signal (only k ≪ N heads matter), and measurements are obtained by ablating random subsets of heads according to a binary measurement matrix Φ. Two construction strategies are proposed—Bernoulli (i.i.d. random) and Stratified (balanced column sums ensuring each head is ablated approximately equally often)—with the latter providing superior stability. The sparse impact vector is recovered by solving an L1-regularized (Lasso) regression, and the top-k heads with the most negative coefficients are selected as task-specific. The approach relies on two assumptions: sparsity of task-relevant heads and approximate additivity of their ablation effects (higher-order interactions treated as noise). This yields O(k log(N/k)) evaluations versus O(N) for greedy search, achieving comparable accuracy with up to 50× fewer model evaluations.

## Results

Ablating the top five task-specific attention heads identified by stratified compressed sensing causes up to 60.5% degradation on GSM8K (Qwen-2.5-7B) and 80% on the swearing task (Llama-3.1-8B), while general language performance (average of HellaSwag, BoolQ, ARC-C, MMLU) drops by less than 2% in nearly all cases. The method achieves these results with as few as 100 model evaluations, compared to 5,120 required by exhaustive greedy search—a 50× reduction. Across six models (1B–14B parameters) and four capabilities (math, code, swearing, rhyming), the pattern is consistent: task-specific heads are highly localized and their removal produces large task-specific drops with minimal collateral damage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against exhaustive greedy search (5,120 evaluations), stratified compressed sensing (CS_S) achieves comparable or superior task-specific degradation with 25–50× fewer evaluations: on GSM8K, CS_S at 100 evals yields −48.4% task drop vs. greedy's −55.4% at 5,120 evals, while also producing less collateral damage on general benchmarks (−1.1% vs. −1.3%). On the swearing task, CS_S at 200 evals matches greedy's −87.5% degradation almost exactly (−85.4%) with 25× fewer evaluations. Against one-shot greedy (1,024 evals), CS_S is competitive on most tasks and strictly better on general performance preservation. In the direct comparison with ProxySPEX (Butler et al., 2026) on GSM8K/Arithmetic with Llama-3.1-8B, the paper's method at 300 evaluations matches ProxySPEX at 500 evaluations (ΔGSM8K: −41.1 vs. −41.9; ΔArithmetic: −61.2 vs. −61.2) while causing substantially less collateral damage on general benchmarks (ΔGen: −1.1 vs. +4.7), and it searches the full 1,024-head model rather than a 96-head three-layer subset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the compressed sensing framework (Donoho, 2006) for a new domain: identifying sparse sets of attention heads responsible for specific LLM capabilities. It builds on the line of attention head ablation studies (Michel et al., 2019; Voita et al., 2019) and task-specific head analyses (Nikankin et al., 2024; Olsson et al., 2022), but departs from them by providing a general-purpose, inference-only algorithm rather than per-head mechanistic analysis. The closest prior work is ProxySPEX (Butler et al., 2026), which also uses compressed sensing for feature identification but models higher-order head interactions; this paper shows that a first-order additive approximation suffices for capability localization, enabling substantially fewer evaluations. Unlike weight-comparison approaches (Chen et al., 2025a) that require task-finetuned models, the method operates purely on a pretrained model via strategic ablation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that as few as five attention heads out of hundreds can account for up to 60% of a model's capability on a given task, while leaving unrelated tasks intact, provides strong evidence for modular organization in Transformers with direct implications for targeted model editing, capability unlearning, and AI safety interventions. The 50× efficiency gain over exhaustive greedy search makes capability localization practical for large models. Key open questions remain: why capabilities localize to such sparse sets, whether the identified heads form self-contained circuits (the paper shows they are necessary but not sufficient), and how the observed scale-dependence (smaller models relying on shared format-level heads rather than task-specific ones) relates to broader questions about emergent specialization. The linear additivity assumption, while empirically validated, limits the method's applicability to capabilities where head interactions are strong.
