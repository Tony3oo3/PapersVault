## Problem

In standard Mixture-of-Experts training, the router and experts are optimized simultaneously, forcing experts to chase a shifting data distribution while the routing policy is still being learned. This entanglement between structural search and representation learning causes persistent routing instability, prevents deep expert specialization, and leads to sluggish convergence and training instabilities that existing router-improvement methods (Aux Loss, ReMoE, StableMoE, etc.) fail to resolve because they still perform structural optimization within the same training loop.

## Input / Output

GROUTER takes raw token sequences directly from the tokenizer (not hidden states from the model backbone) and a fully converged source MoE model (e.g., Qwen3-30B-A3B) as inputs during the distillation phase. It produces a frozen 60M-parameter routing network that outputs per-token expert assignment indices (stored as uint8) and continuous gating weights (stored as bfloat16), which are cached and fed directly into the target MoE model's forward pass. Expert folding (affinity-based matrix multiplication) and expert expanding (k-means + orthogonal projection) allow a single distilled GROUTER to adapt to target models with different expert counts, while expert tuning fine-tunes only the final linear projection layer to rebalance load for the target data distribution.

## Method

GROUTER distills the routing structure from a converged source MoE model into a lightweight 3-layer Transformer encoder via KL divergence on softmax expert scores (without temperature, to preserve true weight magnitudes), then freezes this network as a plug-and-play fixed router for target model training. The key architectural choice is operating on raw token sequences rather than model hidden states, making the router fully decoupled from the backbone; a single shared GROUTER guides all MoE layers, distilling from the first MoE layer where routing is most stable. To handle configuration mismatches, Expert Folding merges source experts via a co-activation affinity matrix (implemented as a binary mapping matrix multiplied onto the score layer), and Expert Expanding adds new expert directions via k-means clustering on underserved tokens followed by orthogonal projection. Expert Tuning then fine-tunes only the final projection layer with a load-balancing auxiliary loss on the target distribution. The fixed routing structure further enables offline optimizations: routing decisions are pre-computed and cached (eliminating runtime router computation), and Expert Parallelism communication is optimized offline via affinity-vector clustering and Hungarian-algorithm-based sample-to-device assignment, yielding up to 33.5% throughput acceleration.

## Results

GROUTER achieves a 4.28× improvement in data utilization efficiency, reaching the same validation loss as the Z-Loss + Aux Loss baseline using only 23.3% of the training tokens (6B vs. 50B). At equivalent training volume, GROUTER delivers a loss reduction of up to 0.85. Across four diverse model architectures (315M–7B parameters), GROUTER consistently outperforms the SOTA baseline by 0.41–0.99 in validation loss. Throughput experiments show up to 33.5% speedup under Expert Parallelism, and the total GROUTER pipeline overhead is only 19.1% of baseline pretraining cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GROUTER is compared against seven baselines: standard Aux Loss (GShard), Z-Loss + Aux Loss (ZL, the SOTA baseline), Aux-Loss-Free, ReMoE, MoE++, HashLayer, and StableMoE. GROUTER decisively outperforms all baselines on convergence speed, achieving equivalent loss with only 23.3% of the data (4.28× acceleration) and up to 0.85 lower loss at matched token budgets. Against the strongest baseline ZL, GROUTER reduces validation loss by 0.41 (Mini-GPT-OSS), 0.59 (Mini-DS-V2-Lite), 0.98 (Mini-Qwen3), and 0.99 (Small-Qwen3), with the gap widening as training progresses. On training stability (gradient norm coefficient of variation), GROUTER shows no spikes throughout training, whereas Aux Loss, Aux-Loss-Free, and HashLayer all exhibit frequent high-magnitude fluctuations. On downstream tasks (BoolQ, RTE, HellaSwag, PIQA, RACE, MNLI), GROUTER achieves an average improvement of 2.80 points over ZL, with gains up to 10 points on individual tasks, confirming that the convergence advantage translates to genuine capability gains rather than overfitting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of jointly optimizing router and expert parameters during MoE training (GShard, ST-MoE) and from router-refinement methods (ReMoE, Lory, Aux-Loss-Free, MoE++) that still perform structural search within the training loop. It also moves beyond prior decoupling attempts: HashLayer's static token-to-expert lookup tables lack contextual sensitivity and continuous gating weights, while StableMoE's synchronous triple-loss distillation inherits the same instability it aims to solve. GROUTER's key conceptual shift is extracting a frozen routing structure from a fully converged source model via knowledge distillation into a lightweight standalone network, repurposing distillation for structural prior transfer rather than task-knowledge compression, and then leveraging that fixed prior to enable offline EP communication optimization (extending DeepEP, NetMoE, FlexMoE).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 4.28× data-utilization gain and 33.5% throughput acceleration are practically significant for teams training large MoE models, and the open-sourcing of GROUTER checkpoints lowers the adoption barrier. The conceptual reframing—that routing instability, not the MoE architecture itself, is the primary source of training difficulty—opens a broader design space where any fixed structural prior (not just distilled ones) could unlock offline optimizations. Open questions include whether the structural prior degrades when the target data distribution diverges substantially from the source model's training corpus, how well the approach scales to models with hundreds of billions of parameters, and whether the frozen routing constraint limits the model's ability to adapt its expert partitioning to novel task distributions during post-training or RL fine-tuning.
