## Problem

Model upscaling—initializing a wider network from a trained narrower one—can accelerate convergence, but the training dynamics of upscaled models differ from scratch-trained ones, requiring hyperparameter tuning at the expensive target width. Prior approaches (e.g., Net2Net) are heuristic, provide no guidance on noise magnitude or learning-rate scaling, and offer no theoretical justification for transferring hyperparameters across scales. It remains unclear whether tuning on small models and extrapolating via scaling laws is sound in the upscaling setting.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a trained base model checkpoint at width n together with its optimizer state (momentum buffers, Adam moments, etc.) and an integer expansion multiplier k, producing a trained wider model at width N = nk. The noise standard deviation and upscaled learning rate are tuned on a small proxy system (width n₀ → kn₀ with n₀ ≪ n) and transferred zero-shot to the target system, so the only expensive computation is the final training run at width N. The output model is architecturally identical to a scratch-trained model at width N, enabling direct loss-curve comparison.

## Method

The paper introduces a three-part framework. First, it proves *dynamic equivalence*: by duplicating and rescaling weights via Kronecker products with all-ones matrices and applying width-dependent rescalings to learning rate, weight decay, and optimizer hyperparameters (summarized in Table 1), the widened model parametrizes the same function at every training step, not just at initialization. Second, it proposes an upscaling algorithm that widens the base checkpoint, injects Gaussian symmetry-breaking noise scaled exactly as μP prescribes for random initialization (controlled by a base constant σ̄_Δ), transfers the optimizer's internal state with matching rescalings, and continues training under μP. Third, it encodes the upscaling procedure within the NE⊗OR⊤ Tensor Program framework by introducing partitioned variables, thereby inheriting μP's infinite-width guarantees: the injected noise is provably 'safe' (base-model signal preserved) and 'useful' (contributes non-trivially without vanishing or exploding), and hyperparameters tuned on a small upscaling system transfer directly to arbitrarily larger ones. The key practical insight is that the widening rules are exactly compatible with μP scaling, making the entire procedure mechanical: transfer weights per Table 1, and all hyperparameters adjust automatically.

## Results

Across three architectures (MLP on Forest Cover Type, ResNet-18 on CIFAR-100, GPT-2 on FineWeb), the upscaled model consistently converges faster and reaches lower final training loss than a same-width model trained from scratch under identical μP hyperparameters. The hyperparameter sweep selects a nonzero noise level in all experiments, raising initialization loss above the base model's terminal loss but recovering within a few steps. By tuning noise and learning rate on a small upscaling system (n₀→kn₀) and transferring to the target (n→kn), the method achieves per-run FLOP speedups of 23.6× (MLP, n/n₀=5), 16.0× (ResNet, n/n₀=4), and 48.2× (GPT-2, n/n₀=10) over Net2Net-style tuning at the target width. Hyperparameter transfer is verified to be stable: optimal learning rate and noise magnitude remain nearly constant across GPT-2 target widths N∈{128, 256, 512, 1024, 2048}.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is training a wider model from scratch under μP with the same hyperparameter base constants. The upscaled model outperforms this baseline on training loss across all three settings (MLP, ResNet-18, GPT-2), converging faster and reaching a lower terminal loss. On validation, the upscaled MLP and GPT-2 mirror the training-loss advantage, while the upscaled ResNet-18 achieves lower training loss but worse validation accuracy, which the authors attribute to the known generalization gap of CNN-based classifiers rather than a failure of the upscaling dynamics. Compared to Net2Net (Chen et al., 2016), which requires tuning noise and learning rate directly at the expensive target width, the proposed method transfers these hyperparameters from a small upscaling system, yielding the FLOP speedups noted above. The paper does not include a head-to-head numerical comparison against other upscaling methods (e.g., BERT2BERT, Solar, or the zero-padding approach of Mallik et al., 2024); the comparison is framed as from-scratch vs. upscaled under a controlled protocol.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Net2Net's (Chen et al., 2016) function-preserving weight duplication from a static equivalence (identical outputs at initialization) to a dynamic equivalence (identical training trajectories throughout optimization), and grounds the entire upscaling procedure in the μP / Tensor Programs framework (Yang & Hu, 2021; Littwin & Yang, 2023). While the 'copy-and-perturb' mechanism is mechanically similar to Net2WiderNet, the key departure is the theoretically derived width-dependent scaling of injected noise and optimizer hyperparameters, which enables zero-shot hyperparameter transfer across upscaling scales—a capability absent from all prior upscaling work. The infinite-width analysis further shows that upscaled training dynamics are qualitatively different from from-scratch training (pre-activations converge to i.i.d. blocks rather than i.i.d. Gaussians), justifying why base-model hyperparameters cannot simply be reused.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first rigorous framework for hyperparameter transfer in the model-upscaling setting, directly addressing a practical bottleneck: training model families at multiple scales is standard industry practice, but retuning hyperparameters at each target width is prohibitively expensive. The 23–48× FLOP speedups demonstrated (growing with the tuning-to-target scale ratio) suggest substantial compute savings for large-scale model training pipelines. Key limitations include: only width upscaling is addressed (depth is left to future work), the ResNet experiment shows worse validation generalization despite lower training loss (theory governs training dynamics, not generalization), and empirical validation is on relatively small models (GPT-2 160→320, ResNet-18 2×→4×). Open questions include whether the framework extends to depth upscaling, whether generalization gaps can be addressed, and whether the speedups hold at truly large scale (billions of parameters).
