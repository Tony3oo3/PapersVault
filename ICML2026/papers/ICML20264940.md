## Problem

Existing composed image retrieval (CIR) methods either rely on expensive triplet supervision that produces opaque fused representations, or collapse the reference image into a single caption/token that loses fine-grained attributes and yields repetitive, homogeneous result lists. Additionally, standard CIR benchmarks only verify retrieval of a single labeled target, making it impossible to measure whether the broader candidate list actually satisfies the user's attribute-level intent or maintains diversity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264940/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a reference RGB image and a free-form natural-language edit instruction as input. At inference, a VLM (Qwen2.5-VL) converts the reference image into an open-vocabulary visual dictionary of short key-value attribute pairs (e.g., color:red, pattern:striped), and the edit is decomposed into signed constraints with three polarities: +1 (attributes to add/strengthen), −1 (attributes to avoid), and 0 (open-set anchors for preservation). The output is a ranked list of gallery images retrieved via nearest-neighbor search in a shared OpenCLIP text-embedding space, followed by MMR diversity-aware reranking that exposes a user-facing λ trade-off between strict constraint satisfaction and result variety.

## Method

Pix2Key represents both the composed query and every gallery image as a structured visual dictionary, turning retrieval into matching between two serialized key-value descriptions embedded by a frozen OpenCLIP text encoder rather than fragile cross-modal fusion. The query dictionary is split by polarity into positive, negative, and open subsets, each independently embedded, and a weighted relevance score R(i) = α·p_i + β·o_i − (1−α)·n_i balances enforcing requested changes against suppressing forbidden attributes while preserving unconstrained context. A greedy MMR reranker then diversifies the top-K list using pairwise cosine distance in the embedding space. The optional V-Dict-AE module refines dictionary quality via self-supervised pretraining on images alone: an attention pooler compresses VLM patch tokens into Q fixed-length slots, which are projected into CLIP token-embedding space and used as soft prompts to condition a frozen Stable Diffusion 2 UNet for v-parameterized denoising, with only the pooler, projection head, and LoRA adapters trained. This reconstruction-shaped objective encourages slots to preserve visually salient fine-grained evidence without any CIR-specific triplet supervision.

## Results

On CIRR, Pix2Key+V-Dict-AE achieves the best Recall across all cutoffs: R@1=29.06, R@5=59.44, R@10=73.36, R@50=92.08, outperforming the strongest tokenization baseline FTI4CIR (R@10=67.66) by 5.7 points. On the new DFMM-Compose benchmark, Pix2Key+V-Dict-AE reaches R@10=23.58, R@50=40.96, and notably AC@50=54.44 (attribute consistency), exceeding the next-best baseline Context-I2W (AC@50=39.59) by 14.9 points. On FashionIQ, Pix2Key+V-Dict-AE attains an average R@10 of 29.96 and R@50 of 51.91, the best among all compared methods. The training-free Pix2Key variant (without V-Dict-AE) already surpasses CIReVL by 3.2 points in average R@10 on FashionIQ and by 14.8 points in AC@50 on DFMM-Compose.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Pix2Key+V-Dict-AE wins on every CIRR Recall metric against all six baselines, with the largest margin over FTI4CIR (+5.7 R@10) and Context-I2W (+4.9 R@10). On DFMM-Compose, the method dominates in attribute consistency (AC@50=54.44 vs. 39.59 for Context-I2W, the closest competitor), a gap of roughly 15 points that reflects the structured dictionary's ability to enforce fine-grained edit constraints that caption-rewrite and token-inversion pipelines miss. In intra-list diversity (ILD@50), the training-free Pix2Key variant achieves the highest score (54.15), while Pix2Key+V-Dict-AE remains competitive at 53.96, both well above CIReVL+MMR (49.26) and all tokenization baselines (≤47.24). On FashionIQ (Table 1), Pix2Key+V-Dict-AE edges out FTI4CIR by 0.57 points in average R@10 (29.96 vs. 29.39) and 1.03 in R@50 (51.91 vs. 50.88), while the training-free Pix2Key already surpasses CIReVL by 1.31 R@10 (29.18 vs. 27.87). Notably, applying MMR reranking to CIReVL slightly degrades its Recall@50 on both CIRR and DFMM-Compose, whereas Pix2Key's dictionary representation remains stable under diversity control, indicating a more robust relevance signal.

## Relation to Prior Work

Pix2Key departs from two dominant zero-shot CIR paradigms: tokenization-based methods (Pic2Word, SEARLE, FTI4CIR) that compress a reference image into a small number of pseudo-tokens, and caption-rewrite pipelines (CIReVL) that funnel visual evidence through a single free-form sentence. Instead, it introduces a structured open-vocabulary key-value visual dictionary as the shared retrieval interface, with polarity-aware constraints (add/avoid/anchor) that make intent explicit. The diversity-aware reranking adapts the classic MMR objective (Carbonell & Goldstein, 1998) to a distance form over text-embedding space, and the V-Dict-AE pretraining module repurposes a frozen latent diffusion decoder as a self-supervised reconstruction signal for dictionary slot quality, building on LoRA-style parameter-efficient adaptation (Hu et al., 2022) and the v-parameterization of diffusion training (Salimans & Ho, 2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dictionary-based interface offers a practical controllability and interpretability advantage over single-token or single-sentence representations, and the DFMM-Compose benchmark fills a genuine evaluation gap by enabling attribute-grounded measurement of intent satisfaction and list diversity beyond a single labeled target. The finding that the structured representation is substantially more robust to vague or underspecified edit instructions (0.68-point drop vs. 2.9–3.8 for baselines) suggests a path toward more reliable user-facing retrieval systems. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes well beyond the fashion domain, how dictionary extraction quality scales with VLM size, and whether the self-supervised diffusion-based pretraining signal transfers to domains with very different visual granularity.
