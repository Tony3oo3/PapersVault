## Problem

Vision-Language-Action models suffer from instruction drift in long-horizon robotic tasks, where local greedy sampling progressively loses grounding with the original goal and collapses into irreversible local optima ("Negative Pivotal Windows") that sever all paths to global success. Existing remedies—prompt engineering, parallel sampling with reranking (e.g., TACO)—lack the capacity for iterative refinement of a single trajectory, making them ineffective when the candidate set itself is dominated by locally plausible but globally infeasible actions.

## Input / Output

CAPS consumes a natural language instruction I, the current RGB visual observation V_t, and the accumulated action/observation history H_t, and produces a continuous action chunk (a finite block of actions) at each control step. The method operates on block-level autoregressive chunks rather than full unrolled trajectories, making it compatible with real-time closed-loop control. A metacognitive SNR gate computed from the policy's Shannon entropy at each step determines whether the output is produced by fast greedy sampling or by an iterative MCMC refinement loop, so the effective output latency varies dynamically between 1× and ~2× the base policy.

## Method

CAPS is a training-free, inference-time computation framework built on two coupled mechanisms. First, it reweights the base VLA model's conditional trajectory distribution via a global power distribution π(τ) ∝ p_θ(τ|I,H_t)^α (α > 1), which sharpens the probability landscape so that globally consistent trajectories are preferentially selected over locally high-probability but globally infeasible ones—provably non-equivalent to per-step temperature scaling. Second, it introduces an SNR-based metacognitive gate: the KL divergence between the current policy distribution and the uniform noise floor serves as a drift-risk signal, and when entropy exceeds a threshold γ(λ), the system switches from greedy execution (System 1) to a Metropolis-Hastings search (System 2) that iteratively proposes new action chunks via randomized resampling from the base model and accepts them using a power-distribution likelihood ratio with a Hastings correction. This "search-when-necessary" design activates MCMC in only ~15% of steps, achieving a 4× speedup over always-on search at a negligible 0.7% performance cost.

## Results

On Libero-long, CAPS achieves 97.6% average success rate, substantially outperforming OpenVLA (49.8%) and RoboMonkey (56.5%), with the largest gain on 'Moka Pots on Stove' (91.0% vs. 68.0% base). On RoboTwin 1.0, CAPS improves π0 from 32.2% to 47.4% (+15.2%), surpassing TACO (41.3%) by 6.1 points. On Simpler-WindowX, CAPS reaches 60.5% average, outperforming the strongest specialized baseline SpatialVLA (42.7%) by 17.8 points. In real-world evaluation across 10 tasks, CAPS achieves 71.0% overall success, a +18.0% improvement over TACO (53.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAPS consistently outperforms TACO (Yang et al., 2025) across all benchmarks: +6.1% on RoboTwin 1.0 (47.4% vs. 41.3%), +5.0% on Simpler-WindowX (60.5% vs. 55.5%), +2.2% on RoboTwin 2.0 (66.2% vs. 64.0%), and +1.0% on Libero-long (97.6% vs. 96.6%), with the largest real-world gap at +18.0% (71.0% vs. 53.0%). Against OpenVLA (Kim et al., 2025) on Libero-long, CAPS achieves a dramatic 47.8-point advantage (97.6% vs. 49.8%), and similarly outperforms RoboMonkey (Kwok et al., 2025) by 41.1 points (97.6% vs. 56.5%). On Simpler-WindowX, CAPS surpasses all specialized VLA baselines including SpatialVLA (42.7%), RoboVLM (31.3%), Octo (16.0%), and RT-1-X (1.1%). On RoboTwin 2.0, CAPS outperforms RDT (Liu et al., 2025a) by 31.6 points (66.2% vs. 34.6%). The only case where CAPS does not improve is 'Eggplant in Basket' on Simpler-WindowX (87.0% vs. SpatialVLA's 100.0%), suggesting that when the task is already near-saturated for a specialized model, the adaptive search provides no additional benefit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the power-distribution sampling framework of Karan & Du (2026) from discrete symbolic reasoning to continuous visuo-motor control, addressing what the authors term a 'topological mismatch' between discrete token generation and high-dimensional action manifolds. It departs from parallel-sampling-and-reranking approaches like TACO and RoboMonkey by replacing independent candidate generation with iterative Metropolis-Hastings refinement of a single trajectory, and introduces an SNR-based metacognitive gate (borrowed from information-theoretic uncertainty quantification) to make the search adaptive rather than always-on. The dual-process (System 1/System 2) framing draws on cognitive science to motivate the fast-greedy/slow-search architecture, while the energy-landscape interpretation connects to EBM literature on temperature rescaling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CAPS offers a training-free, plug-and-play inference-time method that substantially improves long-horizon VLA performance (e.g., +15.2% on RoboTwin, +18% over TACO in real-world settings) without modifying model weights, making it immediately applicable to existing deployed policies. The SNR gating mechanism is practically significant: it activates expensive MCMC search on only ~15% of steps, achieving 4× speedup over always-on search at a 0.7% performance cost. Key limitations include dependence on base-model calibration quality (overconfident models may miss drift), the computational budget constraint N_phy > N_min for convergence, and the strong assumptions underlying the horizon-extension theorems. Open directions the authors identify include reasoning distillation to transfer 'slow thinking' into fast policies and integrating multimodal physical feedback (force, tactile) to improve the MCMC mixing rate.
