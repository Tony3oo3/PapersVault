## Problem

Large language models face a fundamental bottleneck in long-context inference: the KV cache grows linearly with sequence length, causing memory-bound generation and prohibitive latency. Existing mitigations—quantization, token eviction, and CPU offloading—either discard critical information, still maintain growing caches, or introduce transfer-bandwidth latency. The paper asks whether a truly bounded, fixed-size cache can preserve long-range information without hard eviction, and whether the amount of memory accessed can adapt to input complexity.

## Input / Output

ARM consumes a standard token sequence and maintains a fixed-size KV cache organized as a 4-level tree with 4 children per node (256 leaf buckets, each holding a small number of KV slots). During prefilling, all tokens are routed to buckets in parallel via prefix-scan over re-parameterized gated updates; during autoregressive decoding, each new token is routed to a single bucket and one slot is updated. At read time, an MDP-based policy selects a variable number M of buckets (from 1 up to M_max) for sparse attention, so the effective context window adapts per input rather than being fixed. The output is the standard generated token stream with a constant memory footprint regardless of sequence length.

## Method

ARM replaces the append-only KV cache with a hierarchical routed memory: a fixed multi-level tree whose internal nodes compute MoE-style routing logits to assign each token to a leaf bucket, trained with a self-supervised balancing loss that clusters semantically similar tokens. Within a bucket, a Gumbel-Softmax relaxation selects which KV slot to update, and a sigmoid-gated update (analogous to a GRU gate) softly blends the incoming key/value with the stored content, avoiding hard eviction. To recover parallelism during prefilling, the gate computation is re-parameterized to depend only on the most recent synchronization point rather than the recurrent state, enabling an O(log T) prefix-scan evaluation. For retrieval, an LSTM-based MDP policy iteratively decides whether to expand the beam of retrieved buckets, trained end-to-end via a differentiable soft-cascade interpolation between Top-M and Top-(M+1) attention outputs. The paper further proves that under a de-correlated Gaussian model, the induced gated weights are Bayes-optimal for minimizing expected reconstruction error when multiple values must share a fixed-capacity slot, giving a principled information-theoretic justification over eviction-based policies.

## Results

On RULER (up to 128K context), ARM achieves an average score of 15.12, dramatically outperforming Full Attention (8.01, which OOMs at 128K), StreamingLLM (4.88), and SWA (1.10). On LongBench, ARM scores 18.02 average versus 17.09 for Full Attention and 10.76 for StreamingLLM. On short-context recall-intensive tasks (Table 2), ARM matches or slightly exceeds Full Attention (25.40 vs. 25.31 average), while on non-generative commonsense benchmarks (Table 1) it is within 0.4 points of Full Attention (71.89 vs. 72.27). ARM is the only method that maintains non-zero performance at 128K context length.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ARM substantially outperforms all four baselines on long-context RULER: it scores 15.12 average versus 8.01 for Full Attention (FlashAttention), 4.88 for StreamingLLM, 8.01 for KIVI quantization, and 8.00 for InfiniGen offloading, and is the only method that produces non-zero scores at 128K where the others either OOM or collapse to 0. On LongBench, ARM (18.02 avg) beats Full Attention (17.09), StreamingLLM (10.76), KIVI (16.98), and InfiniGen (16.68), with particularly large gains on multi-hop QA (MuSiQue: 3.84 vs. 3.93 for Full Attention) and few-shot tasks (TREC: 62.50 vs. 38.00). On short-context recall-intensive tasks, ARM (25.40) marginally exceeds Full Attention (25.31) and significantly outperforms SWA (17.41), while KIVI (25.29) and InfiniGen (25.23) remain close to Full Attention. On non-generative commonsense benchmarks, ARM (71.89 avg) is slightly below Full Attention (72.27) but comparable to KIVI (72.27) and slightly above SWA/StreamingLLM (72.13). In latency benchmarking, ARM achieves generation speeds on par with SWA and StreamingLLM while using bounded memory, whereas InfiniGen offloading incurs over 10× higher latency due to CPU-GPU transfer.

## Relation to Prior Work

ARM synthesizes several previously separate ideas into a unified KV-cache architecture: the bounded-memory paradigm of Neural Turing Machines and ABC (Graves 2014; Peng 2022), Gumbel-Softmax differentiable discrete selection (Jang 2017), sigmoid-gated updates from gated linear attention (Yang 2024), and hierarchical MoE-style routing (Zeng 2026). The key departure from prior eviction-based methods (H2O, StreamingLLM, KIVI) is the replacement of hard token discarding with a learnable, soft compression mechanism that provably hedges against uncertain future access. The MDP-based adaptive retrieval budget (Section 3.4) extends fixed Top-k sparse attention by making the number of retrieved buckets an input-dependent learned decision rather than a static hyperparameter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

A fixed-size, fully differentiable KV cache that does not grow with sequence length addresses a core deployment bottleneck for long-context LLMs, and the Bayes-optimality analysis of gated writes under access uncertainty provides a principled justification that eviction policies lack. However, the method requires ~10B tokens of pre-training to learn the routing and gating modules, and all experiments are limited to a single 8B model (Llama-3.1-8B), leaving open whether the approach scales to larger architectures or benefits from more diverse pre-training data. The paper also notes potential directions toward infinite-context settings and sub-linear memory growth, but these remain unexplored.
