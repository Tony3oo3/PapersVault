## Problem

Sub-millimeter wear particles expelled in high-speed jet engine exhaust flows are critical indicators of engine health, but existing electrical, vision-based, and optical sensing methods cannot isolate these tiny, ultra-high-speed objects from the intense broad-spectrum blackbody radiation of combustion flames. No prior system could simultaneously detect, count, and reconstruct the 3-D geometry of wear particles operating within such extreme noise and motion conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264807/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system ingests broad-spectrum optical emission from jet exhaust through a 50 mm focal-length lens (F/5, 10.5° FoV), which is then spectrally filtered by a heated Na/K atomic vapor cell to produce narrowband images at 589 nm and 766 nm. A high-speed sCMOS camera (1280×800, 25.6 μm pixels, 2200 fps, 8-bit) captures the filtered signal, yielding bright Na/K clusters on a dark background. Outputs include per-frame 2-D particle bounding boxes with counts (mAP 98.4%, recall 96.0%) and a 3-D voxel model (50 mm³ bounding box at 0.5 mm voxel resolution, ~1 M voxels) reconstructed from 18 temporally spaced silhouettes.

## Method

The core novelty is a quantum optical front-end that uses Faraday rotation in a magnetized atomic vapor cell to rotate the polarization of resonant Na/K photons while leaving off-resonance flame radiation unchanged; a downstream polarizer then transmits only the rotated atomic signal, achieving 22.5 dB SNR versus 2.9 dB without filtering. For detection, the authors replace YOLOv8's final C2f backbone stage with a TR-C2f module (Transformer Bottleneck with Multi-Head Self-Attention and FFN), giving the detector a global receptive field to distinguish compact Na/K particle clusters from dispersed unbound atoms and transient artifacts. For 3-D reconstruction, they exploit the rapid angular rotation of particles in jet flow (thousands of rad/s per Jeffery orbits) as virtual multi-view data, applying Shape-from-Silhouette with a majority-vote voxel strategy (a voxel is retained if present in ≥50% of 18 views) to avoid both the volume bloat of logical OR and the catastrophic loss of logical AND.

## Results

The modified YOLOv8 (TR-C2f) achieves 98.4% mAP50, 96.0% Recall, and 96.8% Precision for wear particle detection, outperforming standard YOLOv8 (96.9% mAP50, 94.2% Recall, 95.0% Precision). The quantum optical system achieves an imaging SNR of 22.5 dB with the atomic filter, compared to only 2.9 dB without it. For 3-D reconstruction, the Shape-from-Silhouette method achieves SSIM exceeding 95% against ground-truth geometry in high-fidelity UE5/Isaac Sim simulations, with performance remaining robust (>95% mAP) across three different engine types.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against external state-of-the-art particle detection methods; the primary quantitative comparison is an internal ablation between standard YOLOv8 and the proposed TR-C2f hybrid CNN-Transformer variant. The modified architecture wins on all three detection metrics (mAP50 +1.5 pp, Recall +1.8 pp, Precision +1.8 pp), attributed to the Multi-Head Self-Attention module's ability to distinguish micro-particles from transient vaporous artifacts via global spatial correlation. A second key comparison is the SNR with versus without the atomic filter: 22.5 dB versus 2.9 dB, demonstrating that the quantum optical front-end is the dominant factor in image quality. For 3-D reconstruction, the method is validated against perfect synthetic ground truth (Sketchfab stones rendered in UE5/Isaac Sim) rather than against competing reconstruction algorithms, achieving SSIM > 95% with 18 silhouette views at 20° angular resolution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by repurposing atomic physics concepts (quantum jump absorption/emission in vapor cells and Faraday rotation) as a practical ultra-narrowband optical filter for industrial imaging, a use case absent from prior quantum sensing literature which focuses on metrology, cryptography, or fundamental physics. On the detection side, it extends standard YOLOv8 with a TR-C2f Transformer-CNN hybrid module, building on the broader line of small-object detection work, but the architectural modification is modest. The 3D reconstruction leverages the well-established Shape-from-Silhouette (SFS) technique, applying it in a novel single-camera temporal setting where particle rotation substitutes for multi-view geometry. Overall, the paper's contribution is primarily at the system-integration level—combining a quantum optical front-end with a conventional deep learning back-end—rather than advancing any single component's methodology.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that quantum optical filtering can achieve a 22.5 dB SNR in jet exhaust imaging versus 2.9 dB without filtering, a substantial gap that validates the atomic-filter concept for harsh-environment sensing. However, several open questions limit near-term impact: the 3D reconstruction is validated exclusively via UE5/Isaac Sim simulation with no real-world ground truth, the system depends on Na/K being present in the fuel chemistry (limiting cross-engine generalization), and the $500K-per-test cost restricts the dataset to only three engines. Future work on vibration robustness, concave particle reconstruction, and non-Na/K elemental signatures will be critical before this approach can be deployed broadly in aviation maintenance.
