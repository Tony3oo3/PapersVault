## Problem

Federated fine-tuning of LLMs suffers from severe statistical heterogeneity, but existing defenses (FedProx, Scaffold, FedDPA) operate at the model level, constraining weight divergence rather than addressing the root cause: intrinsic data distribution mismatch across clients. This paper asks whether heterogeneous data distributions can be aligned at the source level via self-distillation, and further identifies that naive self-distillation introduces a 'Rewrite Paradox'—factual hallucinations and stylistic verbosity that are amplified in the federated setting through aggregation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263857/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client holds instruction-response pairs (c_i, x_i, y_i) drawn from a Non-IID local distribution, and the pre-trained LLM backbone (Llama-2-7B or Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) is frozen throughout. The self-distillation step is performed once per client before training begins, using the current global model as its own teacher to rewrite each response y_i into a distilled response ỹ_i, which is then reused across all communication rounds. The final output is only the LoRA-R (rectification) adapter uploaded to the server; the LoRA-S (smoothing) adapter remains local and is discarded at aggregation time.

## Method

FedSDR operates as a three-stage pipeline: (1) a Data Refinery projects each client's raw data into a unified 'model-understanding space' via self-distillation, smoothing disjoint distributions into a shared manifold; (2) a Dual-Stream Rectification architecture injects two parallel LoRA modules (rank r=8) into the attention layers, trained in an alternating block-coordinate scheme—LoRA-S is first trained on distilled data to absorb distributional heterogeneity and stylistic noise, then LoRA-R is trained on raw data to anchor factual correctness, with both streams sharing the same joint forward pass so that LoRA-R's gradients are conditioned on LoRA-S's smoothed representations; (3) a Selective Aggregation protocol uploads only LoRA-R to the server, filtering out the hallucinations and verbosity sequestered in LoRA-S. The key insight is that LoRA-S acts as a local 'shock absorber' and functional scaffold: it reduces the learning difficulty for LoRA-R on sharp raw data while preventing distilled-induced biases from contaminating the global consensus.

## Results

On the Databricks Dolly-15K dataset with Llama-2-7B, FedSDR achieves an Overall score of 77.38 with a 67.75% head-to-head win rate, substantially outperforming FedDPA (68.77, 10.35%), FedSD (69.05, 8.30%), and the FedAvg baseline (67.86, 5.80%). On general knowledge benchmarks, FedSDR scores 47.11 on MMLU, 35.81 on BBH, 56.22 on CRASS, and 37.94 on DROP, leading all baselines on every metric. Under high Non-IID intensity (α=0.1), FedSD boosts FedAvg from 53.18 to 63.26, and FedSDR maintains its advantage as client count scales from 8 to 40 (77.70 at N=40 vs. 71.10 for FedAvg).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedSDR decisively outperforms all six baselines. Against FedDPA (the strongest structural baseline using dual-LoRA without self-distillation), FedSDR gains +8.61 in Overall score and +6.67 in Faithfulness, confirming that data-level alignment via self-distillation complements model-level dual-adapter design. Against FedAvg, FedAvgM, FedProx, and the adaptive optimizers (FedAdam, FedYogi, FedAdagrad), FedSD alone already provides a consistent 3–5 point boost in Overall score (e.g., 74.54 vs. 71.21 for FedAvg; 75.13 vs. 71.87 for FedAdagrad), and FedSDR extends this to 77.38. Against Scaffold under high Non-IID (α=0.1), FedSD lifts performance from 53.27 to 62.73, while FedSDR's full framework further narrows the gap to near-IID levels. On general knowledge benchmarks (Table 8), FedSDR leads FedDPA by +2.33 on MMLU, +1.58 on BBH, and +6.53 on CRASS, indicating that the rectification stream preserves factual reasoning that structural-only methods degrade.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends self-distillation for LLM fine-tuning (Yang et al., 2024b) from a centralized anti-forgetting tool into a federated data-alignment mechanism, arguing that prior FL heterogeneity defenses (FedProx, Scaffold, FedDPA's dual-LoRA) are model-centric and treat symptoms rather than the root cause of distribution mismatch. Architecturally, it combines the self-distillation 'refinery' with a dual-stream LoRA design (LoRA-S for smoothing, LoRA-R for rectification) and a selective aggregation protocol that uploads only the rectification adapter, distinguishing it from FedDPA which aggregates both adapters. The 'Rewrite Paradox'—that unconstrained self-distillation amplifies hallucinations and stylistic verbosity in a way that is aggravated by federated aggregation—serves as the motivating observation that separates this work from prior self-distillation studies that did not examine fidelity degradation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The data-centric reframing of FL heterogeneity (align distributions before training rather than constraining weights after divergence) offers a complementary axis to existing model-level defenses and could be combined with them. However, the evaluation relies heavily on LLM-as-judge metrics (Faithfulness, Smoothness, Info Purity) whose reliability is not independently validated, and the main experiments use a single 15K-sample dataset with 7B models, limiting confidence in the magnitude of the reported gains. Open questions include whether the selective aggregation of only LoRA-R loses useful personalization signal at scale, whether the Rewrite Paradox persists with stronger teacher models or multi-round distillation, and whether the approach extends to multi-modal or larger (70B+) federated settings.
