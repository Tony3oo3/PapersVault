## Problem

Most time series generative models are trained and evaluated under the assumption of abundant single-domain data, limiting their effectiveness in real-world data-scarce settings. Two specific obstacles hinder cross-domain generation: noise-corrupted tokens in diffusion become indistinguishable, preventing the model from inferring the target domain, and heterogeneous time series degrade differently under the same noise schedule, obscuring the semantics of diffusion timesteps for stage-dependent denoising.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265498/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a small set of multivariate time series samples (as few as 10–50 instances or 5–15% of a dataset) from a target domain, along with a domain label. Input series of length H and C channels are segmented into non-overlapping patches of length S, linearly embedded into dimension d, and processed through a 250-step diffusion process. The output is synthetic multivariate time series matching the original data's length and channel count, generated to approximate the target domain's distribution.

## Method

TimeMoDE replaces the MLP blocks in a Diffusion Transformer backbone with a Mixture of Domain Experts (MoDE) module that sparsely activates experts per token. A novel routing mechanism uses Domain Prompts—compact global representations built from few-shot samples via dynamic convolution and temporal pooling—matched against learnable time series prototypes (sets of orthogonal basis vectors per expert) via cosine similarity to assign indistinguishable noised tokens to domain-relevant experts. Each expert is further conditioned on the diffusion timestep through AdaLN, enabling stage-aware denoising that adapts to heterogeneous degradation patterns. Training follows a two-stage protocol: pre-training on 10+ multi-domain datasets with a combined diffusion, prototype-orthogonality, and load-balancing loss, followed by fine-tuning on the target domain with frozen prototypes and only relevant experts activated.

## Results

On the averaged few-shot generation benchmark across 10 datasets, TimeMoDE achieves the best c-FID and Discriminative Score at every data-scarcity level (5%, 10%, 15%, 10/25/50 samples). At the 10% setting, TimeMoDE reaches c-FID 0.240 and Disc. 0.078, compared to 0.334/0.126 for the closest competitor ImagenFew and 2.084/0.222 for ImagenTime. In the extreme low-data regime of only 10 samples, TimeMoDE still attains c-FID 1.950 and Disc. 0.264, outperforming all baselines by 15–60%. The paper reports an average ~25% reduction in c-FID and >30% improvement in Discriminative Score over ImagenFew across all settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TimeMoDE is compared against four baselines: ImagenFew (Gonen et al., 2025), which also uses a pre-train-then-fine-tune protocol with class-label conditioning; ImagenTime (Naiman et al., 2024a), which maps time series to images and applies diffusion; DiffusionTS (Yuan & Qiao, 2024), an interpretable diffusion model for general time series; and KoVAE (Naiman et al., 2024b), a Koopman-based VAE for regular and irregular series. TimeMoDE consistently outperforms all four on c-FID and Discriminative Score across every data-scarcity level, with the largest margins against ImagenTime and DiffusionTS (e.g., c-FID 0.240 vs. 2.084 and 2.756 at 10%). Against the closest competitor ImagenFew, TimeMoDE improves c-FID by 15–35% and Disc. by 13–45%, attributed to Domain Prompts enabling inter-domain knowledge transfer where class labels fail on unseen domains. On Predictive Score, TimeMoDE is competitive but not uniformly best: at the extreme #10 setting, ImagenFew edges out TimeMoDE (0.579 vs. 0.582), while TimeMoDE wins at all other levels. In full-shot settings (Table 2), TimeMoDE further achieves c-FID 0.040 and Disc. 0.034, surpassing even ImagenTime trained on the full dataset (c-FID 0.460).

## Relation to Prior Work

TimeMoDE builds on the Diffusion Transformer (DiT) backbone, replacing its standard MLP layers with a Mixture of Domain Experts (MoDE) module, and extends the MoE paradigm previously explored in time series forecasting (Time-MoE, MoiraiMoE) to the underexplored setting of diffusion-based time series generation. It departs from prior cross-domain generation work (ImagenFew, TimeDP) by introducing Domain Prompts and learnable time series prototypes as a routing mechanism, addressing the specific challenge that noised tokens in diffusion become indistinguishable and cannot be assigned to domain experts via class labels or natural language. The diffusion timestep conditioning injected into each expert also distinguishes TimeMoDE from static expert networks used in forecasting MoE models, enabling stage-aware denoising.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work establishes a practical paradigm for cross-domain few-shot time series generation, showing that a single pre-trained model can outperform full-data single-domain baselines with only 5% of target data, which is highly relevant for privacy-constrained or expensive-to-acquire time series. The Domain Prompt + prototype routing mechanism offers a generalizable solution to the indistinguishable-noise problem that could transfer to other diffusion-based generative tasks. Open questions include scalability to longer sequences and truly novel domains, sensitivity to the number of experts and basis vectors (which the paper shows is non-monotonic), and whether the pre-training cost (~20 hours on 2×V100) is prohibitive for smaller research groups.
