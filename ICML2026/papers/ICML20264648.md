## Problem

Multi-turn LLM agent RL training can silently degrade into 'template collapse,' where reasoning chains become fluent but input-agnostic boilerplate while standard entropy metrics remain stable. Existing stability monitors (entropy, reward) only track within-input diversity and cannot detect whether reasoning actually depends on the specific input, leaving this failure mode invisible throughout training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264648/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives environment observations (grid states for Sokoban/FrozenLake, text problems for math/code tasks, or image+text for VL variants) that form the full context X at each turn, and produces a reasoning token sequence Z followed by an executable action a_t. The MI diagnostic cross-scores each reasoning chain Z_{i,k} against all P batch prompts via teacher-forced log-likelihoods, requiring no external model or additional inference pass beyond the standard training rollout. The filtering step consumes per-prompt reward variance computed from G parallel trajectories (G≥2) and outputs a binary mask selecting which prompt groups contribute to the gradient update.

## Method

The paper introduces three linked components. First, a retrieval-based mutual information proxy (Retrieval-Acc and MI-ZScore-EMA) that measures cross-input distinguishability of reasoning by checking whether a reasoning chain is more likely under its true source prompt than under the uniform batch mixture, providing an online diagnostic that correlates with task performance far more strongly than entropy. Second, a signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) mechanism explaining collapse: low within-input reward variance bounds the task gradient norm via Cauchy-Schwarz, while KL and entropy regularization apply uniform input-agnostic contraction, so low-SNR updates push reasoning toward templates. Third, SNR-Aware Filtering, a top-p (nucleus-style) selection that retains only the highest-reward-variance prompt groups each iteration, concentrating gradient budget on high-signal updates without additional supervision or rollouts. The method is validated across PPO, GRPO, DAPO, and Dr.GRPO on Qwen2.5 and Llama models from 0.5B to 7B, spanning text and image modalities.

## Results

SNR-Aware Filtering (top-p=0.9) consistently improves peak task success rate across all four RL algorithms and seven environments. Under PPO with Qwen2.5-3B, the average peak score rises from 67.6% to 74.5% (+6.9), with the largest gains on Sokoban (+16.0) and FrozenLake (+10.9). Gains are consistent across model scales (0.5B to 7B, +4.1 to +5.5 average), model families (Qwen, Llama, +4.1 to +5.3), and modalities (text and vision inputs, +29.8 to +35.8). The MI proxy (Retrieval-Acc) correlates with final performance at +0.39 Spearman, while entropy metrics show negative correlations (−0.11 to −0.14), confirming MI as a superior diagnostic for template collapse.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SNR-Aware Filtering is applied as an additive intervention on top of four RL baselines rather than replacing them. Against PPO (Schulman et al., 2017b), filtering yields the largest average gain of +6.9 points, driven by Sokoban (+16.0) and FrozenLake (+10.9), while MetaMathQA and Countdown see minimal changes (+0.6, +0.0) since their high baseline scores leave little room. Against DAPO (Yu et al., 2025), which already includes a fixed filtering step interpretable as a special case of top-P with P→1.0, the additional SNR-aware top-p filtering still adds +2.9 average, confirming that a tunable keep-rate ρ provides a finer-grained SNR knob. Against GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), filtering improves the average by +3.7, though FrozenLake slightly decreases (−3.0), suggesting the stochastic environment interacts differently with variance-based selection. Against Dr. GRPO (Liu et al., 2025), gains are smallest (+0.8 average) with one negative case on Sokoban (−0.4), likely because Dr. GRPO's normalization already partially addresses low-variance noise. Across all four baselines, the method wins or ties on the majority of task–algorithm pairs, with the most consistent improvements appearing on planning (Sokoban) and navigation (FrozenLake) tasks where reward variance is most informative.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the multi-turn agent RL framework established by RAGEN and standard policy-gradient algorithms (PPO, GRPO, DAPO), but reframes the training-stability problem through an information-theoretic lens. Prior work on reasoning collapse and policy degeneracy has reported templated rationales and concentrated behaviors, and stabilization methods (KL control, entropy regularization, clipping) have been proposed, but none explicitly target the within-input reward-variance axis. The paper departs from existing diversity metrics (lexical, embedding-based, uncertainty) by arguing they capture only within-input variability (H(Z|X)) and miss cross-input dependence (I(X;Z)), and from prior stabilization techniques by proposing a data-level filtering intervention rather than algorithm-level regularization tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that entropy—the field's standard reasoning-stability monitor—correlates negatively with task performance while mutual information correlates positively is a practical warning for anyone training multi-turn agents: the metric they are watching may be pointing in the wrong direction. SNR-Aware Filtering is a lightweight, model-agnostic intervention (no extra inference passes) that could be dropped into existing RL pipelines, but its applicability is constrained: it requires G≥2 trajectories per prompt, degrades in sparse or highly stochastic reward regimes, and aggressive filtering narrows exploration. Open questions include whether the SNR mechanism generalizes to multi-agent settings, whether reward variance remains a reliable signal proxy under dense process rewards, and whether the MI diagnostic holds when the prompt distribution shifts during training.
