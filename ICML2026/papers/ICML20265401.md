## Problem

In RLVR for LLM reasoning, sparse binary verifier rewards cause training instability, and the standard fix—imposing token-level KL or distillation constraints toward a reference policy—penalizes all deviations indiscriminately. This symmetric pull-back can flip the effective update direction when the policy attempts to outperform the reference ("Force Reversal"), capping gains at the reference's performance level and preventing sustained self-evolution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265401/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on text prompts (mathematical problems from DAPO-Math-17k) and produces reasoning chains as text responses, verified by a sequence-level mathematical verifier that yields binary rewards. The reference policy computes token-level log-probabilities on the current policy's own on-policy trajectories, providing dense per-token confidence signals. Experiments use Qwen-2.5-Math-7B-Base (8k context) and Qwen-3-8B-Base (20k context), with group sampling (G rollouts per prompt) for advantage estimation.

## Method

OWPO decouples optimization direction from update magnitude by defining a Directional Deviation δ_t = sgn(A_t)·log(π_θ/π_ref) and applying an asymmetric one-way weight w_t = clip(exp(−δ_t), ε_low, ε_high) to the standard PPO/DAPO clipped surrogate. When δ_t < 0 (policy lags the reference along the verifier's direction), w_t > 1 amplifies the gradient for Accelerated Alignment; when δ_t > 0 (policy surpasses the reference), w_t < 1 shrinks the step for Gain Locking, protecting sparse high-reward signals from stochastic noise. Theoretically, this is equivalent to a direction-aware regularization Ω(θ) whose gradient never reverses the verifier-induced direction (g_OWPO(δ) ≥ ε_low > 0 for all δ), unlike symmetric KL which can produce negative effective coefficients. An iterative "Ratchet Effect" periodically hard-swaps π_ref ← π_θ, consolidating gains into a new baseline and enabling continuous self-evolution beyond any fixed prior.

## Results

On Qwen-2.5-Math-7B-Base, OWPO achieves an average Pass@1 of 43.91% (best) and 44.12% (converged) across AIME24, AIME25, and AMC, outperforming the strongest baseline MOPD (41.11%/39.03%) by +2.80/+5.09 points. On Qwen-3-8B-Base, OWPO reaches 51.90% (best) and 50.74% (converged), surpassing MOPD (46.40%/45.35%) by +5.50/+5.39 points. Relative to the fixed reference policy, OWPO yields average gains of +3.88% and +7.71% on the two models, compared to only +0.93% and +1.13% for MOPD. On generalization benchmarks, OWPO also leads: 41.03% GPQA and 46.85% MMLU-Pro (Qwen-2.5), and 54.55% GPQA and 67.41% MMLU-Pro (Qwen-3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OWPO consistently outperforms all four baselines (GRPO, DAPO, MOPD, OPD) on both base models across AIME24, AIME25, and AMC. Against the strongest reference-guided baseline MOPD, OWPO gains +2.80% (Qwen-2.5) and +5.50% (Qwen-3) in average Pass@1 at the best checkpoint, with the gap widening at convergence (+5.09% and +5.39%), indicating MOPD's symmetric KL penalty induces a 'Force Reversal' that caps gains near the reference. Against pure RLVR baselines, OWPO beats DAPO by +3.35% (Qwen-2.5) and +6.59% (Qwen-3) at best, and crucially avoids DAPO's late-stage regression (DAPO converges to 42.83% vs. OWPO's 44.12% on Qwen-2.5). OPD, which performs pure reverse-KL distillation, plateaus near the reference (40.64% best on Qwen-2.5) and is outperformed by OWPO by +3.27%. In the iterative self-evolution experiment starting from a suboptimal prior (~30%), OWPO reaches ~40% Pass@1 in 4 iterations while MOPD saturates at ~37% after 6 iterations, confirming that the one-way mechanism enables faster and more thorough transcendence of weak priors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two RLVR lines: pure policy-gradient methods (GRPO, DAPO) that suffer from sparse-reward instability, and reference-guided distillation methods (OPD, MOPD) that add dense token-level supervision but impose direction-agnostic KL-style constraints. The key departure is the diagnosis that symmetric reference penalties can flip the verifier-determined update direction (the 'Force Reversal' effect), and the proposed fix—decoupling direction from magnitude via asymmetric token-level reweighting (Accelerated Alignment for inferior deviations, Gain Locking for superior ones). The iterative 'Ratchet Effect' (periodic hard-swap of the reference) extends this into a self-evolving loop, building on bootstrapping ideas from STaR and ReST but applied within a one-way trust region rather than a symmetric one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Force Reversal diagnosis identifies a concrete, previously under-appreciated failure mode in reference-guided RLVR that caps performance at the reference level, and the one-way reweighting offers a clean, low-overhead fix that practitioners can drop into existing PPO/DAPO pipelines. The iterative self-evolution protocol (reaching ~40% AIME24 in 4 iterations from a 30% prior, vs. MOPD's ~37% in 6) is practically significant for teams wanting to improve models without external teacher access. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-math domains (coding, tool-use, interactive tasks), whether the local first-order theory captures global clipped-objective behavior, and how sensitive performance is to the reference refresh frequency and clipping bounds.
