## Problem

Existing multilayer graph embedding methods either learn representations for each layer independently or aggregate them with simple operations, failing to explicitly model how relationships in one layer influence or contradict another. Moreover, the field lacks a rigorous statistical framework providing consistency and asymptotic normality guarantees for multilayer embedding estimation, leaving practitioners without principled guidance on when and how cross-layer dependencies should be encoded.

## Input / Output

The method takes a binary multilayer adjacency tensor A ∈ {0,1}^{n×n×M} over a shared node set, where each of M layers encodes a distinct undirected relation type. It produces node embeddings α ∈ R^{n×R} and layer-specific embeddings β ∈ R^{M×R} from a symmetric CP decomposition, with link scores computed as ⟨α_i ⊙ α_j, β_m⟩. The working covariance matrix W ∈ R^{M×M} is maintained as a single layer-level object (not per-pair), keeping memory linear in n and M rather than quadratic in n.

## Method

T-GINEE parameterizes the mean edge-probability tensor via a symmetric CP decomposition Θ = Σ_r α^(r) ∘ α^(r) ∘ β^(r), which enforces undirected symmetry and shares latent factors across nodes and layers. A generalized estimating equation (GEE) framework then estimates the parameter vector γ = [vec(α); vec(β)] by solving a score equation weighted by a working covariance Σ_{i,j} = Γ_{i,j}^{1/2} W Γ_{i,j}^{1/2}, where W is a shared M×M correlation matrix estimated from pooled standardized residuals across all node pairs. A flexible link function (logit, probit, or sparsity-aware logit with coefficient s < 1) connects parameters to edge probabilities, accommodating increasingly sparse networks. Training alternates gradient steps on a combined BCE + GEE loss with periodic momentum-based updates of W, and for large-scale graphs employs dynamic negative sampling to reduce per-epoch cost from O(n²M) to O(|E|·R). The paper proves consistency at rate N^{-1/2} and asymptotic normality under a data-adaptive overdetermination condition p_N²/|E_obs| → 0, with the Kruskal-type identifiability condition 2k_α + k_β ≥ 2R+2 ensuring unique CP recovery.

## Results

On the four standard benchmark datasets, T-GINEE achieves the highest link-prediction AUC: 0.920 (AUCS), 0.948 (Krackhardt), 0.838 (WAT), and 0.921 (Yeast), outperforming the second-best methods (HOSVD, SVD, SPECK) by 0.018–0.023. On the large-scale DBLP (300K nodes) and Stack Overflow (2.58M nodes) datasets, T-GINEE is the only tensor-based method that avoids out-of-memory errors, achieving AUC 0.6478 and 0.9831 respectively. On community detection (NMI), T-GINEE dominates all GNN baselines, e.g., 0.837 vs. 0.206 (GCN) on Karate and 0.641 vs. 0.429 (GCN) on Email. In zero-shot new-layer generalization on DBLP-5K, T-GINEE (AUC 0.7733 with zero layer-5 edges) outperforms MGCN and MR-GCN retrained on all 8,297 layer-5 edges (0.7089 and 0.6306).

## Baselines & Comparisons

T-GINEE outperforms all nine baselines on the four standard benchmarks (AUCS, Krackhardt, WAT, Yeast), with the largest margins over CP (e.g., 0.920 vs. 0.374 on AUCS) and LSE (0.921 vs. 0.047 on Yeast). Among the closest competitors, HOSVD is second-best on AUCS (0.897) and WAT (0.820), SVD on Krackhardt (0.932), and SPECK on Yeast (0.903), but T-GINEE leads each by 0.018–0.023 AUC. On DBLP, NMF narrowly edges T-GINEE (0.6505 vs. 0.6478), while on Stack Overflow T-GINEE leads NMF (0.9831 vs. 0.9642) and SVD (0.9831 vs. 0.9682); critically, CP, Tucker, MASE, NNTUCK, SPECK, and HOSVD all fail with OOM on both large-scale datasets. Against GNN baselines (Table 3), GCN is competitive on very small graphs (Karate AUC 0.834 vs. 0.817) but T-GINEE dominates on NMI across all community-detection datasets (e.g., 0.837 vs. 0.206 on Karate, 0.641 vs. 0.429 on Email). In the zero-shot new-layer transfer experiment (Table 4), T-GINEE's shared node embeddings plus layer-specific scoring function yield AUC 0.7733 with zero observed layer-5 edges, surpassing MGCN (0.7089) and MR-GCN (0.6306) that are retrained on all 8,297 layer-5 edges.

## Relation to Prior Work

T-GINEE sits at the intersection of two well-established lines of work: CP tensor decomposition (Kolda & Bader, 2009) for compact multilayer graph parameterization, and generalized estimating equations (Liang & Zeger, 1986) for correlated-response statistical inference. Prior multilayer embedding methods either treat layers independently (LSE, per-layer SVD), aggregate them naively (MASE, mean-adjacency spectral embedding), or apply deep architectures (MGCN, MR-GCN) without formal statistical guarantees. The key departure is wrapping the CP parameterization inside a GEE framework with a working covariance matrix that explicitly models inter-layer residual dependence, yielding the first consistency and asymptotic normality results for a multilayer graph embedding estimator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a recognized gap in the multilayer graph embedding literature: the absence of a rigorous statistical theory characterizing when and how embeddings capture cross-layer structure. The working covariance component provides an interpretable, data-driven mechanism for encoding which layers are more similar, and the zero-shot new-layer generalization result (predicting an unseen layer with zero training edges) suggests the shared-latent-factor structure carries transferable information. However, the asymptotic guarantees apply only to the full-batch estimator, the mini-batch negative-sampling implementation used for million-node graphs lacks theoretical coverage, and the shared-node-set assumption limits applicability to partially aligned or heterogeneous multilayer networks. Extending the theory to the scalable regime and integrating with deep encoders are the most pressing open questions.
