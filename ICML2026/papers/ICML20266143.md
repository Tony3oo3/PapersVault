## Problem

Existing generative VLA policies adopt a 'Generation-from-Noise' paradigm that initializes action synthesis from an uninformative isotropic Gaussian, ignoring the fundamental spatiotemporal scale mismatch between macro-level semantic intent (low-frequency, deterministic) and micro-level physical execution (high-frequency, stochastic). This leads to two critical failures: representation inefficiency, where the velocity field wastes capacity rediscovering global trajectory structure, and 'Loss Collapse,' where source-condition independence causes the model to ignore fine-grained language instructions during optimization.

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB observations, a natural-language instruction, and the current robot joint state, processed through a Qwen3-2B VLM backbone. The output is a continuous action sequence structured as a two-stage 'Predict-Refine' composition: a deterministic low-frequency anchor (regressed via a spectral projection head) plus a high-frequency residual integrated from a flow-matching velocity field. Because the anchor is already geometrically proximate to the target action, inference requires significantly fewer function evaluations (NFE) than standard diffusion policies—achieving >70% success with a single integration step versus ~85% at NFE=4.

## Method

ResVLA decomposes the action space via Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) into a low-frequency Semantic Subspace (first k modes, capturing global trajectory geometry) and a high-frequency Execution Subspace (orthogonal complement, capturing contact dynamics and jitter). The Intent Anchoring Module uses VLM features to regress the low-frequency component μ_prior(c), constructing a condition-dependent source distribution p₀(x|c) = N(x; μ_prior(c), σ_min²I) that injects nontrivial mutual information I(x₀; c) > 0 at t=0 to prevent Loss Collapse. A residual flow matching expert then learns the transport path from this anchor to the ground-truth action, where the target vector field reduces to the high-frequency residual x_E minus sampling noise. Training uses a unified bi-level objective combining semantic alignment MSE on the low-frequency component with the residual flow matching loss, and the architecture is instantiated on Qwen3-2B with LoRA or full fine-tuning of the VLM backbone and a from-scratch frequency-aware action head.

## Results

On the standard LIBERO benchmark, ResVLA achieves 96.6% average success rate (Spatial 96.0%, Object 100.0%, Goal 97.4%, Long 92.8%), competitive with top baselines like VLA-Adapter (97.3%) and OpenVLA-OFT (97.1%). On the robustness-focused LIBERO-Plus benchmark, ResVLA achieves 76.9% total success rate, outperforming the best baseline (OpenVLA-OFT at 69.6%) by 7.3 points, with particularly strong gains on Language (+7.2%) and Robot (+11.3%) perturbations. On SimplerEnv without any pre-training, ResVLA reaches 62.2% average on Google Robot and 68.8% on WidowX, surpassing pre-trained baselines like π0 (58.8% and 27.1% respectively). On the real-robot ALOHA task, ResVLA achieves 20% overall success versus 10% for π0.5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the standard LIBERO benchmark, ResVLA (96.6%) is competitive but trails the top two baselines VLA-Adapter (97.3%) and OpenVLA-OFT (97.1%) by 0.7 and 0.5 points respectively, while surpassing π0 (94.2%), GR00T N1 (93.9%), and all discrete-token baselines. The paper's key advantage emerges on LIBERO-Plus robustness: ResVLA outperforms the best baseline on 6 of 7 perturbation axes, most notably Language (88.2% vs. 81.0% for OpenVLA-OFT_m, +7.2%) and Robot (57.5% vs. 46.2% for UniVLA, +11.3%), while only trailing on Camera (53.2% vs. 65.1% for π0-FAST). On SimplerEnv, ResVLA trained entirely from scratch (no pre-training) outperforms pre-trained baselines including π0 (62.2% vs. 58.8% on Google Robot; 68.8% vs. 27.1% on WidowX), OpenVLA (34.3% and 4.2%), and CogACT (74.8% and 51.3%), though it remains below spatially co-trained models like InternVLA-M1 (80.7% and 71.7%). On the real-robot ALOHA evaluation, ResVLA doubles π0.5's overall success rate (20% vs. 10%) across the three-stage Pick-Handover-Placement task. The paper also demonstrates faster training convergence than π0 under high dropout (p=0.2) and achieves >70% success with a single NFE step versus π0's need for 4+ steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

ResVLA repositions the generative VLA paradigm by replacing the standard 'Generation-from-Noise' initialization (as in π0, Diffusion Policy, and OpenVLA-OFT) with a 'Refinement-from-Intent' approach grounded in the Diffusion Schrödinger Bridge framework. It draws on the loss-collapse analysis of Dong et al. (2025) to argue that condition-independent noise sources cause gradient vanishing, and instantiates a condition-dependent source via DCT-based spectral decomposition of the action into low-frequency intent and high-frequency residual. This connects conceptually to classical residual learning in robotics (Silver et al., 2018) and frequency-domain policy work (FAST, FreqPolicy), but uniquely fuses deterministic regression of the anchor with conditional flow matching of the residual in a single end-to-end architecture built on a Qwen-VL backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, theoretically grounded explanation for why standard noise-initialized generative policies suffer from instruction drift and slow convergence, and offers a concrete architectural fix that yields large robustness gains (e.g., 57.5% vs. 6.0% on robot-morphology perturbation) and substantially fewer NFE at inference. However, the model is only 2B parameters, real-world ALOHA results are modest (20% overall success), and camera-viewpoint generalization remains weak, leaving open whether the spectral anchor scales to larger foundation models and whether the frequency domain is truly the optimal decoupling space. The framework's generality—swapping DCT for other structured anchors—suggests a useful design space for future VLA architectures, but full-scale validation on datasets like Open X-Embodiment is still needed.
