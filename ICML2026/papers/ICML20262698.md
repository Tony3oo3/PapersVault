## Problem

Existing perspective-based image editing models fail on 360° panoramic images because they cannot handle the latitude-dependent geometric distortion inherent in equirectangular projection (ERP). Cube-map decomposition workarounds break global coherence when objects span multiple faces, and no prior method unifies diverse editing operations (addition, removal, replacement, relocation, modification) within a single ERP-native representation with explicit geometric supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262698/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a source ERP panorama (default 512×1024, extendable to 1024×2048), a natural-language editing instruction, and optional spatial/appearance conditions (bounding boxes and reference images). It produces an edited ERP panorama plus K transparent object layers (rendered as RGB on white background with alpha derived via connectivity segmentation) that provide per-object geometric supervision. The base model is Qwen-Image-Edit-2509 finetuned with LoRA (rank 32) on attention layers, using flow-matching denoising with a 3D-RoPE token layout that assigns distinct layer IDs to the global canvas and each object layer.

## Method

World-Shaper operates entirely in the ERP domain and introduces a three-component geometry-aware learning strategy: (1) Distortion-Aware Attention Modulation (DAAM) steers object-specific cross-attention with a latitude-dependent residual R = α⊙(M⊙(A_max−A) − (1−M)⊙(A−A_min)), where α(y) = 1−cos(π/2 − πy/H) increases toward the poles; (2) Layered Shape Loss (LSL) forces the model to output per-object transparent layers supervised by a spatially-weighted IoU loss plus a composite-image reconstruction loss within each object's bounding box; (3) Progressive Curriculum Training transitions through three stages—global panorama generation (T2P/I2P), localized controllable generation with bounding-box and reference-image conditioning, and instruction-driven supervised editing—with linear data mixing to prevent catastrophic forgetting. To overcome the scarcity of paired panoramic editing data, the Stage-2 controllable generator G synthesizes target panoramas under user-defined conditions, and GPT-5 auto-generates editing instructions from (source, target) pairs, yielding a large-scale dataset covering all five edit types. At inference, a Boundary-Consistent Inference Strategy applies circular latent extension, early-step cyclic horizontal shifting, and boundary blending to enforce left-right periodicity.

## Results

On PEBench (1,000 editing test pairs across 200 panoramas), World-Shaper achieves the best scores on all automatic metrics: FID 45.25 (vs. 51.10 for the next-best Nano Banana Pro), CLIPdir 19.62, Semantic Consistency 8.15, Perceptual Quality 7.87, and Overall VIEScore 8.01. In the user study (72 participants), World-Shaper is ranked first on all three criteria: image quality (1.19), distortion accuracy (1.26), and text alignment (1.34), with the nearest competitor (Nano Banana Pro) ranking 2.18–3.41. For text-to-panorama generation, World-Shaper also leads with FID 48.39, IS 4.45, and FAED 7.21; for image-to-panorama generation it achieves FID 46.14, outperforming DreamCube (50.15) and CubeDiff (53.76).

## Baselines & Comparisons

World-Shaper outperforms all eight editing baselines on every metric. The closest competitor, Nano Banana Pro, trails by 5.85 FID points (51.10 vs. 45.25) and 2.88 in CLIPdir (16.74 vs. 19.62), while GPT-5 and FLUX.1 Kontext are substantially weaker (FID 92.37 and 60.18; Overall VIEScore 2.73 and 2.77 respectively), indicating that general-purpose perspective editing models struggle severely with ERP distortion. Among panorama-specific methods, Omni2 (cubemap-based) and OmniGen2 are beaten on all metrics, with World-Shaper gaining +2.41 in Overall score over Omni2 (8.01 vs. 5.60) and +2.48 over OmniGen2 (8.01 vs. 5.53). For text-to-panorama generation, World-Shaper leads DiT360 (FID 48.39 vs. 51.42) and UniPano (48.39 vs. 57.53), while for image-to-panorama it surpasses DreamCube (FID 46.14 vs. 50.15) and CubeDiff (46.14 vs. 53.76). The paper further shows that even with GPT-5-enhanced prompts explicitly instructing models to preserve ERP distortion, baselines improve only marginally (e.g., Nano Banana Pro FID drops from 51.10 to 50.15), confirming that the gap stems from architectural mismatch rather than prompt quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two dominant paradigms for panoramic editing: (1) applying perspective-based diffusion editors (InstructPix2Pix, FLUX Kontext, OmniGen2) directly to ERP images, which fails due to latitude-dependent distortion, and (2) cube-map decomposition (SE360, Omni2), which breaks global coherence at face boundaries. It builds on Qwen-Image-Edit as the diffusion backbone, adopts the residual attention modulation mechanism from Kim et al. (2023) as the basis for DAAM, and borrows the 3D-RoPE multi-layer token strategy from ART (Pu et al., 2025) for layered shape supervision. The key conceptual contribution is reformulating panoramic editing as an ERP-native problem and addressing distortion through a three-pronged geometry-aware learning strategy (feature-level attention, output-level shape loss, curriculum training) rather than through architectural reparameterization or projection changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

World-Shaper provides the first unified ERP-native framework supporting five distinct editing operations (addition, removal, replacement, relocation, modification) in a single model, and introduces PEBench, a 1,200-sample benchmark that will serve as a standard evaluation suite for panoramic editing and generation. The generate-then-edit data construction pipeline using a controllable ERP generator is a practical recipe for overcoming the scarcity of paired panoramic editing data. Limitations include artifacts around object-object interaction regions and restriction to static images; the authors note future extension to video panoramas and explicit inter-object modeling. An open question is whether the ERP-native advantage persists as base models become increasingly multimodal and geometry-aware, potentially reducing the need for explicit distortion compensation.
