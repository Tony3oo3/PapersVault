## Problem

Existing perturbation prediction models suffer from mean-collapse: they predict expression shifts close to the global average rather than capturing perturbation-specific changes, because dense reconstruction losses are dominated by the large majority of non-responsive genes. Knowledge-graph-based methods exacerbate this by treating biological graphs as static, dense priors shared across all perturbations, spreading signal across unrelated genes. No prior method explicitly models the sparsity of true perturbation effects while leveraging biological structure in a perturbation-conditioned manner.

## Input / Output

The model takes a control cell expression profile (N-dimensional vector over highly variable genes, e.g., 5,000 for K562 or 3,352 for RPE1) and a single perturbation gene identifier as input, and produces a predicted perturbed expression profile of the same dimensionality. The perturbation gene is consumed through two channels: a structural embedding computed by a 4-layer GATv2 over a STRING PPI graph (filtered to HVGs with top-10 or top-20 edges per node), and a semantic embedding from GPT-4O encoding the gene's NCBI description. Evaluation is performed under the unseen-perturbation setting, where test perturbations are entirely absent from training.

## Method

ADAPERT combines two components to counter mean-collapse. First, a perturbation-conditioned subgraph extraction module scores each gene node by concatenating its GATv2 structural embedding with the projected semantic embedding of the perturbation gene, then applies Gumbel-Softmax sampling to differentiably select a sparse set of relevant nodes, yielding a compact context vector z_context that varies per perturbation. Second, an adaptive learning scheme uses three complementary losses: a global MSE reconstruction loss, a Huber penalty on predicted changes for non-DEG genes (suppressing spurious variation without over-penalizing noisy outliers), and a cosine-alignment loss that drives the subgraph context representation toward a DEG-only target vector. The Huber threshold δ is set proportional to the empirical standard deviation of non-DEG effects, and the alignment loss explicitly encodes signed DEG effect sizes while masking non-responsive genes, ensuring the learned subgraph captures perturbation-specific differential expression rather than generic graph topology.

## Results

On K562.Replogle, ADAPERT achieves Pearson-Δ = 0.619 and PDS = 0.711, outperforming the strongest baseline TxPert (0.580, 0.665) by +6.7% and +6.9% respectively. On RPE1.Replogle, ADAPERT reaches Pearson-Δ = 0.674 and PDS = 0.663, again surpassing TxPert (0.655, 0.618). On DEG-aware metrics (K562), ADAPERT achieves DES@50 = 0.263 and Spearman-lfc-sig = 0.688, improving over TxPert by +19.5% and +7.5% respectively. The largest gains appear on small-effect perturbations, where ADAPERT improves DES by +28% over TxPert while maintaining comparable Pearson-Δ.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ADAPERT consistently outperforms all six baselines across both K562 and RPE1 datasets on both global metrics (Pearson-Δ, PDS) and DEG-aware metrics. Against the strongest KG-based baseline TxPert, ADAPERT gains +6.7% Pearson-Δ and +6.9% PDS on K562, and +2.9% Pearson-Δ and +7.3% PDS on RPE1. The advantage is most pronounced on DEG-specific metrics: on K562, ADAPERT improves DES@50 by +19.5% (0.263 vs. 0.220) and Spearman-lfc-sig by +7.5% (0.688 vs. 0.640) over TxPert. Methods without knowledge graphs (scVI, CPA, STATE) perform near chance on PDS (~0.50) and achieve very low DES scores (<0.12), confirming that biological structure is essential. MORPH achieves competitive PDS on RPE1 (0.688) but lags on Pearson-Δ (0.541 vs. 0.674), while GEARS underperforms all other KG-based methods, suggesting that dense static graph propagation is less effective than perturbation-conditioned subgraph selection. In the effect-size stratification, ADAPERT's DES advantage over TxPert is largest for small-effect perturbations (+28%), where mean-collapse is most severe, and remains strong for medium (+28%) and large (+19%) effects.

## Relation to Prior Work

ADAPERT extends the knowledge-graph-guided perturbation prediction line (GEARS, TxPert, MORPH) by replacing the static, dense graph prior with a perturbation-conditioned sparse subgraph extracted via Gumbel-Softmax differentiable node selection. It departs from prior work conceptually by identifying 'mean-collapse'—the tendency of MSE-trained models to predict near-zero expression changes—as the root cause of poor DEG recovery, and addresses it with a three-term adaptive loss (reconstruction + non-DEG Huber penalty + subgraph alignment) rather than relying solely on architectural changes. The semantic conditioning via GenePT/GPT-4O embeddings complements the structural GNN embeddings, following the trend of integrating LLM-derived gene representations into perturbation models (GenePT, scGenePT).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The mean-collapse framing provides a useful diagnostic lens for the perturbation prediction community, explaining why high global correlation can coexist with poor biological interpretability. The effect-size-dependent optimal weight for the non-DEG loss (stronger regularization needed for weak perturbations) is a practically actionable finding for practitioners tuning perturbation models. However, the cross-cell-line generalization results reveal a significant limitation: ADAPERT underperforms simple linear baselines (Linear-GenePT) on Pearson-Δ when transferring across cell lines, suggesting the learned subgraph context is tightly coupled to the training cell line's regulatory context. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-gene or combinatorial perturbations, and whether the GPT-4O dependency for semantic embeddings can be replaced with lighter alternatives.
