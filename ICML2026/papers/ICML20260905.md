## Problem

Omni-modal LLMs process long interleaved audio-video token sequences (e.g., 20K+ tokens for a 20-second clip), causing prohibitive computational cost. Existing token compression methods for Omni-LLMs either treat audio and video independently or symmetrically, ignoring the perceptual asymmetry where visual redundancy is internally estimable but audio saliency is context-dependent and requires visual grounding. This paper asks how to design a lightweight, modality-asymmetric compression that exploits this asymmetry to retain only the most informative cross-modal cues.

## Input / Output

The method consumes video clips sampled at 2 FPS (max 256 frames, 320×28×28 spatial resolution per frame) and synchronized audio streams, both tokenized by modality-specific encoders and projected into the LLM hidden space. Processing operates on 2-second multimodal chunks, each containing paired visual and audio token sub-sequences. The compressed token sequence (25–35% of original length) is interleaved with text instructions and fed into the LLM backbone, which produces standard QA answers or detailed video captions identical in format to the full-token model.

## Method

OmniSIFT is a two-stage, modality-asymmetric compression framework. Stage 1 (STVP) prunes video tokens by computing spatial saliency (cosine distance from the frame-mean vector) on the first frame and temporal saliency (cosine distance between corresponding patches in consecutive frames) on the second frame, then retaining top-K tokens per frame under a fixed visual retention ratio. Stage 2 (VGAS) selects audio tokens via a lightweight single-layer cross-attention where audio tokens serve as queries and the pruned video tokens as keys/values, followed by a residual connection and a 2-layer MLP + sigmoid score head that produces per-token saliency for TopK selection. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end using a straight-through estimator to backpropagate through the non-differentiable TopK operation, adding only 4.85M parameters (<0.1% of the 7B backbone). The key design insight is that video redundancy is estimated from internal visual structure (spatial + temporal), while audio token importance is assessed only after conditioning on the compressed visual context, mirroring the asymmetric dependency in human audio-video perception.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-Omni-7B at 35% token retention, OmniSIFT achieves 50.0 on WorldSense, 35.6 on OmniVideoBench, and 68.3 average on VideoMME, surpassing the full-token baseline (49.7, 35.6, 67.6) on WorldSense and VideoMME while matching it on OmniVideoBench. At the more aggressive 25% retention, OmniSIFT still reaches 49.9 on WorldSense and 68.2 on VideoMME average, again exceeding the full-token model. On the video-SALMONN-2 captioning benchmark (lower is better), OmniSIFT scores 50.5 total error at 35% retention, substantially outperforming all compression baselines (OmniZip 54.1, DyCoke 52.7, Random 53.1) though slightly behind the full-token model (48.1). The method adds only 4.85M parameters and reduces inference latency by over 40% relative to the full-token model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OmniSIFT consistently outperforms all three compression baselines across every QA benchmark at both 35% and 25% retention. Against OmniZip (Tao et al., 2025c), the primary omni-modal compression baseline, OmniSIFT gains +1.1 on WorldSense (50.0 vs 48.9), +0.5 on OmniVideoBench (35.6 vs 35.1), and +1.6 on VideoMME average (68.3 vs 66.7) at 35% retention, with gaps widening to +1.8 and +2.2 at 25%. Against DyCoke (Tao et al., 2025b), a video-centric method adapted for omni-modal settings, OmniSIFT leads by +1.4 on WorldSense and +0.4 on VideoMME at 35%, and by +1.8 and +2.3 at 25%. On the video-SALMONN-2 captioning task (lower total error is better), OmniSIFT achieves 50.5 at 35% retention versus 54.1 for OmniZip and 52.7 for DyCoke, a 3.6–2.2 point improvement. In rebuttal experiments, OmniSIFT also outperforms FASTAV (Jung et al., 2026), a recent audio-visual token pruning method, on both DailyOmni and WorldSense. Notably, OmniSIFT's modality-asymmetric design (vision-guided audio selection) is validated by a paradigm ablation showing +2.7 to +3.7 points over a symmetric audio-guided variant (OmniZip-Trained) across retention ratios.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the well-studied vision-centric token compression literature (VisionZip, DyCoke, VidCom², TimeChat-Online) to the omni-modal setting where both audio and video tokens must be jointly compressed. It departs from the modality-symmetric paradigm of OmniZip (audio-guided video pruning) and the heavy global contextualization of EchoingPixels by proposing a modality-asymmetric pipeline: first prune spatial and temporal redundancy in video via lightweight saliency scoring (STVP), then use the resulting compact visual anchors to guide audio token selection via a single-layer cross-attention module (VGAS). The conceptual departure is grounded in the observation that video redundancy is largely self-contained within the visual stream, whereas audio saliency is context-dependent and requires visual grounding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As omni-modal LLMs (GPT-4o, Gemini, Qwen2.5/3-Omni) become the default architecture for audio-visual understanding, token compression is a prerequisite for practical deployment on long videos; OmniSIFT demonstrates that 25% token retention can match or exceed full-token accuracy while cutting inference latency by over 40%, making it a strong candidate for integration into production omni-LLM pipelines. Key open questions remain: the current chunk-level design cannot capture cross-chunk temporal redundancy or long-range audio-visual structure, the fixed query-agnostic budget is suboptimal for tasks with non-uniform information density, and precise audio-visual alignment tasks remain vulnerable to aggressive pruning. Future work on adaptive budgeting, query-guided pruning, and cross-chunk modeling would address these gaps.
