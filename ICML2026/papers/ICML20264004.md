## Problem

LoRA hyperparameter search is computationally prohibitive: the discrete space of rank, scaling factor, batch size, learning rate, and dropout yields over 45,000 configurations, each requiring expensive fine-tuning to evaluate. Existing HPO methods either treat the space as a generic black box without incorporating LoRA-specific domain knowledge, or struggle with the inherently discrete and entangled nature of the hyperparameters, making standard continuous BO inapplicable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264004/cropped_page7_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a discrete hyperparameter configuration (e.g., rank=256, α=8192, batch_size=4, lr=5e-06, dropout=0.0) and converts it into a structured text template that describes each parameter's name, value, role, and inter-parameter relationships. This template, augmented with a learnable token, is fed through a frozen Qwen2-7B LLM to produce a 3584-dimensional continuous embedding. The output is the single best hyperparameter configuration selected after ~30 BO iterations, validated via proxy training on a 10% data subset before final full-data evaluation.

## Method

The framework repurposes a frozen LLM as a discrete-to-continuous mapping module: domain-aware text templates encode LoRA-specific knowledge (e.g., how α/r controls effective update magnitude, how batch size interacts with learning rate) directly into the prompt, while a learnable token ψ captures residual information not expressible in language. A projection layer P(·;θ) then calibrates the LLM's last-token-pooled embedding into a space better suited for GP regression. A Gaussian Process surrogate with a Matérn-5/2 kernel is trained on these calibrated embeddings paired with observed performance, and all trainable parameters (GP kernel ω, projection θ, token ψ) are jointly optimized by maximizing the marginal log-likelihood in a deep kernel learning formulation. An Expected Improvement acquisition function selects the next candidate from the full discrete pool, and a proxy training evaluation on a random 10% data subset reduces per-iteration cost by ~10×, enabling ~30 iterations within a practical budget.

## Results

With only 30 optimization iterations, the proposed framework discovers LoRA hyperparameters achieving 62.93% GSM8K accuracy, 12.88% MATH accuracy, 30.49% HumanEval Pass@1, and 42.59% MBPP Pass@1 on LLaMA2-7B, outperforming all baselines under the same budget. Applied to standard LoRA, this yields a +21.46% absolute improvement over the default configuration (41.47% → 62.93% on GSM8K). Across LoRA variants (rsLoRA, DoRA, PiSSA), consistent gains of 8–21% on GSM8K and 5–14% on HumanEval are observed. The method also reduces wall-clock optimization time from 180 hours (NOMAD) to 24 hours while achieving superior accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all six baselines across every evaluated metric. Against Random Search, it gains +3.79 on GSM8K, +2.37 on MATH, +7.32 on HumanEval, and +5.82 on MBPP. Optuna (TPE) underperforms on GSM8K (54.13) and MATH (10.50) despite competitive code-generation scores, while standard BO (Oliver & Wang, 2024) lags on HumanEval (20.12) and MBPP (35.19). Latent BO (LBO) is the closest competitor, trailing by 3.42 on GSM8K, 1.00 on MATH, 3.66 on HumanEval, and 4.76 on MBPP. Against the dedicated LoRA HPO method NOMAD (Tribes et al., 2024), the proposed framework achieves substantially higher scores (e.g., 62.93 vs. 52.16 on GSM8K) while completing optimization in 24 hours versus 180 hours—a 7.5× speedup. Finally, compared to LLAMBO (Liu et al., 2024c), which replaces BO components with LLM prompting, the proposed method gains +14.11 on GSM8K and +4.12 on MATH, demonstrating that explicit GP-based surrogate modeling with calibrated LLM embeddings is more effective than relying solely on LLM prior knowledge.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM-embedded Bayesian optimization paradigm introduced by Ranković & Schwaller (2025, Gollum) from chemical reaction optimization to LoRA hyperparameter search, and builds on the deep kernel learning framework of Wilson et al. (2016) to jointly optimize GP kernel parameters, a projection layer, and a learnable token. It departs from prior LLM+BO work (e.g., LLAMBO, Liu et al. 2024c) by retaining a full GP surrogate with an acquisition function rather than replacing BO components with LLM prompting, and from standard discrete-space BO by using the LLM as a discrete-to-continuous mapping module. The domain-aware prompting design, which encodes hyperparameter relationships and training dynamics as structured text, is a novel contribution that goes beyond the simple name-value templates used in prior LLM-embedding approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, plug-and-play solution to a widely felt pain point: LoRA hyperparameters are highly sensitive and the search space exceeds 45,000 configurations, yet most practitioners rely on default or hand-tuned settings. The 10× speedup from proxy training evaluation and the ~7.5× time reduction over NOMAD make systematic HPO feasible within a single day of GPU time. Open questions include whether the domain-aware prompting can be fully automated (the paper shows partial success with GPT-generated prompts), whether the choice of embedding LLM can be made more principled, and whether the framework extends to continuous or mixed discrete-continuous hyperparameter spaces beyond the current grid-based candidate pool. The observation that α/r ratios of 16–32× outperform the conventional 2× guideline, if confirmed, could reshape practical LoRA tuning recommendations.
