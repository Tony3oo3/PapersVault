## Problem

Current multimodal reward models for video face two structural limitations: visual inputs consume large context budgets, forcing aggressive frame downsampling that loses fine-grained details, and all visual evidence is packed into the initial prompt so that purely textual chain-of-thought reasoning suffers from progressive forgetting and hallucination. No existing reward model can actively revisit or acquire new visual evidence during its reasoning process.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264728/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives 8 downsampled frames (4 per video in a pair) plus the text generation prompt as initial input, but can iteratively invoke a select_frames tool to retrieve additional frames from the full video at arbitrary indices. Output is a structured multimodal chain-of-thought using dedicated tags: <Snapshot> compresses retrieved visual evidence into linguistic summaries, <Think> contains reasoning, <Recommend Answer> gives interim judgments with confidence levels, and <Answer> provides final per-dimension scores (alignment, visual quality, motion quality) plus an overall preference (1, 2, or 0 for tie).

## Method

VR-Thinker equips a Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone with a Thinking-with-Image framework comprising two mechanisms: (1) a visual reasoning operation where the model issues tool-invocation instructions to retrieve previously unseen frames from the full video workspace, and (2) a configurable windowed memory (width p) that retains only the most recent p tool-execution outcomes, keeping total context approximately independent of the number of reasoning steps. Training proceeds in three stages: Cold Start (SFT on ~1.2k GPT-4o-generated multimodal CoT traces to instill format and basic reasoning), Rejection Sampling Fine-Tuning (SFT on model-generated traces filtered for full correctness to consolidate reasoning quality), and GRPO-based reinforcement fine-tuning with a composite reward combining format correctness, a mixed per-dimension/overall accuracy signal (expanding the answer space to 3^(d+1) to reduce invalid-sample contamination), a CoT gain reward that incentivizes accuracy improvement across reasoning segments, and an exploratory incentive (via Lagrangian relaxation) enforcing a minimum proportion of multimodal reasoning to prevent degeneration into text-only reasoning.

## Results

VR-THINKER (7B) achieves state-of-the-art preference accuracy among open-source models across all three video benchmarks: 82.3% (diff) on GenAI-Bench, 80.5% (diff) on VideoGen-Reward, and 75.6% (diff) on MJ-Bench-Video. Under the tau protocol (which includes ties), it reaches 68.7%, 71.8%, and 67.3% respectively. The model delivers an average improvement of up to 11.4% over classic classifier-based and generative-based reward models, and outperforms the strongest reasoning-based baseline (UnifiedReward-Think) by 1.4–4.5 points in tau accuracy across benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VR-THINKER (7B) outperforms all six baselines on every benchmark and both evaluation protocols. Against the strongest reasoning-based baseline UnifiedReward-Think (7B), it gains +4.0/+1.9 (tau/diff) on GenAI-Bench, +2.1/+1.4 on VideoGen-Reward, and +4.5/+3.7 on MJ-Bench-Video. Over the generative-based UnifiedReward (7B), the margins are larger: +7.5/+5.5 on GenAI-Bench and +4.0/+6.1 on MJ-Bench-Video. The classifier-based models (VideoScore, VideoReward, VisionReward) and the generative LiFT (13B) trail by 10–25 points in diff accuracy, with LiFT being the weakest overall (59.4% diff on GenAI-Bench). Notably, VR-THINKER is trained purely on video preference data, whereas UnifiedReward and UnifiedReward-Think are trained on mixed image+video tasks, yet VR-THINKER still surpasses them, underscoring the benefit of the thinking-with-image visual reasoning mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the reasoning-based reward model line (UnifiedReward-Think, RM-R1, Think-RM) by introducing the Thinking-with-Image paradigm—previously explored in VQA and visual generation (ZoomEye, Pixel Reasoner, LOVE-R1)—into video preference evaluation. Unlike prior RMs that pack all visual evidence into the initial prompt and then reason purely in text, VR-Thinker equips the model with a frame-selection tool and a sliding window memory, enabling iterative retrieval of previously unseen frames during reasoning. The training pipeline builds on GRPO (DeepSeek-R1, DAPO) but introduces a multi-stage curriculum (Cold Start → Rejection Sampling Fine-Tuning → GRPO) with a novel reward decomposition (per-dimension + overall accuracy, CoT gain, and exploratory incentive) specifically designed for multimodal reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two fundamental bottlenecks in video RMs—context budget exhaustion and visual forgetting during CoT—with a practical, memory-stable solution that imposes no upper bound on processable frames. The finding that naively increasing input frames (e.g., 256) actually degrades baseline accuracy while active retrieval with windowed memory improves it challenges the 'more visual input is better' assumption and has implications beyond RMs for any long-video VLM task. Open questions include reducing inference latency for easy cases, whether the approach generalizes to image or audio reward modeling, and whether the 'overly cautious' failure mode can be eliminated through targeted fine-tuning. The complete three-stage recipe with specific reward hyperparameters makes this directly reproducible for practitioners building video generation alignment pipelines.
