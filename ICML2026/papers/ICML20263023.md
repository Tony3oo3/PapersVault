## Problem

The dominant Direct Output (DO) paradigm in neural long-term time series forecasting treats each output timestep independently, lacking structural causality within the forecast horizon, while a critical validation-test generalization gap caused by non-stationary data and chronological splitting destabilizes training and obscures true model capabilities. The paper argues that the field has overemphasized architectural complexity (Transformers, multi-scale modules) at the expense of these fundamental forecasting principles.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263023/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a multivariate history window (look-back of 336, 512, or 720 timesteps across D variates) and produces a multivariate forecast over a future horizon of 96–720 steps. Unusually, the output is generated recursively in N stages: each stage's input is the concatenation of the original history with the previous stage's sub-forecast, so the network progressively extends its prediction from short to full horizon. RevIN normalization is applied independently at each stage's input, and the final output is the last stage's full-horizon prediction.

## Method

The core contribution is the Boosted Direct Output (BDO) paradigm, which recursively generates forecasts for progressively longer horizons by feeding each sub-forecast back into the next block's input alongside the original history, creating an implicit forecast combination within a single MLP. The architecture stacks N MLP blocks (ReNF-α for simpler data, ReNF-β with skip connections for complex data), each equipped with a dedicated linear head for block-wise supervision at its corresponding horizon length. Training uses a hybrid time-frequency MAE loss with heavier weights on earlier shorter sub-forecasts (γ/n weighting), and Exponential Moving Average (EMA) weight averaging to smooth the optimization trajectory and resolve the validation-test inconsistency. The design is motivated by a Variance Reduction Hypothesis (VRH) proving that combining c candidate forecasts bounds the predictive variance term by λ/√c, which the BDO structure implicitly realizes without explicit ensemble aggregation.

## Results

ReNF (a pure MLP-based forecaster) achieves the best MSE and MAE on 7 out of 8 multivariate LTSF benchmarks, with headline numbers of 0.214 MSE / 0.247 MAE on Weather, 0.145 MSE / 0.237 MAE on Electricity, and 0.391 MSE / 0.416 MAE on ETTh1 (all averaged over prediction lengths 96–720). The sole exception is Traffic, where the large Transformer-based TimeBridge achieves a slightly lower MSE (0.357 vs. 0.365), though ReNF uses ~20× fewer FLOPs (22.8 GB vs. 479.2 GB). On short-term forecasting (Table 7), ReNF also leads on CarSales, Power, Website, and NASDAQ, confirming generality beyond LTSF.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReNF outperforms all nine LTSF baselines (TimeBridge, DUET, TimeDistill, Timer-XL, iTransformer, TimeMixer, PatchTST, Crossformer, DLinear) on 7 of 8 datasets in both MSE and MAE. The only loss is on Traffic MSE, where TimeBridge (0.357) edges out ReNF (0.365), though ReNF still wins on Traffic MAE (0.245 vs. 0.248) and uses roughly 20× fewer FLOPs (22.8 GB vs. 479.2 GB). Against the strongest 2025 competitors (TimeBridge, DUET), ReNF's margins are modest but consistent (e.g., 0.145 vs. 0.152 MSE on Electricity; 0.243 vs. 0.247 on ETTm2). Against older baselines like DLinear and Crossformer, the gaps are substantially larger (e.g., 0.327 vs. 0.470 MSE on ETTh2). On short-term forecasting (Table 7), ReNF leads on 4 of 6 datasets (CarSales, Power, Website, NASDAQ) but trails OLinear and TimeMixer++ on METR-LA and SP500, which the authors attribute to BDO's diminishing returns on short horizons.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by repurposing classic forecast combination principles (Bates & Granger, 1969; Clemen, 1989) and ensemble ideas (bagging, boosting) within a single neural network, rather than combining separate models. Conceptually, BDO sits between the two dominant LTSF paradigms—Auto-Regressive and Direct Output—synthesizing AR's causal structure with DO's patch-wise stability, while the recursive input concatenation mechanism distinguishes it from additive stacking architectures like N-BEATS and N-HiTS. The EMA-based training stabilization is borrowed from self-supervised learning and generative modeling but is identified here as a critical, previously underexplored remedy for the validation-test generalization gap specific to non-stationary time series. The Variance Reduction Hypothesis provides a new theoretical lens that reframes the role of multiple forecasts in deterministic neural prediction, connecting to variance-bias decomposition but targeting the overlooked predictive variance term.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the field's prevailing assumption that architectural complexity (attention mechanisms, multi-scale mixing, non-stationarity modules) is necessary for competitive LTSF, showing instead that principled training (EMA) and structural design (BDO) on a plain MLP suffice to match or exceed complex SOTA with ~20× lower FLOPs. The significant gap between ReNF's final forecast and the oracle empirical bound (Table 4) reveals substantial untapped potential in learning better implicit combination functionals, pointing to a clear future research direction. Open questions include whether BDO's benefits extend to other backbone architectures beyond MLP, how to adapt the curriculum-style supervision to short-horizon tasks where it provides diminishing returns, and whether more sophisticated weight-averaging schemes could further close the train-test distribution gap.
