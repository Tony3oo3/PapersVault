## Problem

Existing label diffusion models (LDMs) for learning with noisy labels rely on the standard ε-prediction paradigm, where the network predicts Gaussian noise that carries no explicit class semantics. This limits optimization (no direct supervisory signal for distinguishing clean vs. noisy samples) and inference (requires iterative denoising, preventing one-step label recovery). The paper asks whether a y-prediction formulation can unlock explicit class-semantic supervision and enable efficient one-step inference within the LDM framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261604/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The diffusion network receives three inputs at each training step: a noisy label vector y_t (obtained by adding Gaussian noise to the one-hot label at a sampled timestep t), the timestep t, and a frozen feature vector x extracted from the image by a pre-trained backbone (ResNet-50 or ViT-L/14). The network outputs a predicted clean label y_θ, which is converted to class probabilities via softmax. At inference, a single forward pass from random Gaussian noise y_T ~ N(0,I) yields the final label estimate without iterative denoising. For tabular data, the image backbone is replaced by a lightweight linear layer while the diffusion training paradigm remains unchanged.

## Method

JYP replaces ε-prediction with direct y-prediction: the network regresses the clean label y_0 from noisy y_t, and the paper proves (Proposition 3.1) that the optimal solutions of y-prediction and ε-prediction are related by invertible linear transformations, so both converge to the same conditional expectation E[y_0 | y_t, x]. Because predictions live in the low-dimensional label space, JYP supports one-step inference and provides explicit class-semantic signals at every training iteration. On top of this, the paper introduces Historical Cumulative Inconsistency (HCI), which accumulates per-sample disagreement (1 − p_{i,ỹ_i}^{(t)}) across epochs with a time-dependent weight φ_t to normalize across noise scales. After a warm-up phase, a binary GMM fitted to the HCI distribution partitions samples into clean, noisy, and hard sets via dual-view (weak/strong augmentation) intersection; each subset receives a tailored loss (cross-entropy for clean, soft-label cross-entropy for noisy, generalized cross-entropy for hard), combined with a dual-view consistency regularizer to form the overall JYP objective.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with 50% symmetric noise, JYP+ViT achieves 97.35% accuracy, surpassing the next-best DLD+ViT (97.01%) and PCL+ViT (95.39%). On real-world noisy benchmarks, JYP attains 90.40% on Animal-10N, 75.77% on Clothing1M, 84.62% Top-1 on WebVision, and 85.08% Top-1 on ILSVRC12, setting new state-of-the-art across all four datasets. JYP also enables one-step inference, reducing inference time from 21s to 4s on WebVision and from 27s to 5s on ILSVRC12 compared to iterative label diffusion baselines like LRAD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

JYP+ViT consistently outperforms all ViT-based baselines on CIFAR-10/100: it beats DLD+ViT by 0.42% on CIFAR-10 Sym-20% (97.64 vs. 97.22), by 1.91% over PCL+ViT (97.64 vs. 95.71), and by 1.07% over LRAD+ViT (97.64 vs. 96.68) under the same setting. With ResNet-50, JYP is highly competitive with IDO and DMLP on CIFAR-10 (e.g., 96.07 vs. 95.45 and 95.80 at 50% Sym), though it trails slightly under low-noise (20%) settings due to the strict dual-view partition reducing clean-sample recall. On real-world benchmarks, JYP achieves the best result on all four datasets, exceeding DLD by 1.00% on Animal-10N (90.40 vs. 89.40), by 1.34% on ILSVRC12 Top-1 (85.08 vs. 83.74), and by 0.46% on WebVision Top-1 (84.62 vs. 84.16 for LRAD). Notably, JYP surpasses its own validation accuracy on ILSVRC12 (85.08 vs. 84.62 on WebVision), indicating stronger generalization than competing methods. On UCI tabular datasets, the simplified JYP variant outperforms LRAD on 7 out of 9 datasets and improves over traditional ML baselines (e.g., +4.39% over Random Forest on CANE-9).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the label diffusion model (LDM) framework introduced by CARD (Han et al., 2022) and grounded in DDPM (Ho et al., 2020) by replacing the standard ε-prediction training paradigm with a novel y-prediction (JYP) paradigm, drawing conceptual inspiration from x-prediction in image diffusion (Li & He, 2025) and label progressive prediction (Li et al., 2025). For robust optimization under noisy labels, it adapts the dual-view sample selection strategy from DISC (Li et al., 2023) but re-grounds it in the generative label space rather than discriminative loss magnitudes, introducing Historical Cumulative Inconsistency (HCI) as a noise-scale-aware selection signal. The paper departs from the broader LNL literature, which is dominated by discriminative classifiers, by arguing that generative classifiers trained with explicit class-semantic supervision (y-prediction) offer a more principled path to noise robustness than iterative denoising from high-dimensional noise manifolds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The JYP paradigm's ability to enable one-step inference (reducing inference time from ~21s to ~4s on WebVision) is a practically significant efficiency gain for deployment scenarios where iterative denoising is prohibitive, and the clean theoretical equivalence proof between y-prediction and ε-prediction provides a principled justification for the design choice. However, the method still depends on a frozen pre-trained feature extractor (ResNet-50 or ViT-L/14), limiting its applicability in settings where such models are unavailable, and the dual-view + HCI partitioning adds implementation complexity. An open question is whether the y-prediction advantage generalizes to other generative classification settings (e.g., segmentation, detection) where the label manifold is higher-dimensional, and whether the HCI mechanism can be made more adaptive to non-stationary noise patterns without the GMM fitting step.
