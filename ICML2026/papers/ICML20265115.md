## Problem

Existing multimodal image fusion methods encode all shared information into dense 2D feature grids, where image-level appearance factors (illumination, contrast, perceptual tone) are implicitly broadcast across spatial locations and entangled with local textures and noise. This structural mismatch makes global appearance alignment statistically inefficient and optimization-sensitive, leading to brightness inconsistency, blurred details, and amplified artifacts. The paper asks whether a compact, non-spatial 1D token representation can serve as a dedicated carrier for global appearance while leaving local detail to the conventional 2D pathway.

## Input / Output

The method takes spatially aligned image pairs (infrared + visible, or MRI + CT/PET/SPECT) resized to 256×256 and produces a single fused image. Rather than directly reconstructing the output, the decoder predicts a residual ΔI that is added to the element-wise sum of the two inputs (I^ref = I^V + I^I), so the network only needs to learn complementary corrections. The frozen TiTok tokenizer consumes each input independently to produce a 32×12 token sequence, which is then adapted back to the spatial domain through a lightweight token-to-map interface before entering the 2D fusion pipeline.

## Method

The core contribution is replacing the shared 2D feature grid with a compact 1D token sequence from a frozen pretrained TiTok tokenizer (32 tokens, 12 dimensions), which acts as a non-spatial carrier for global appearance/base factors. Selective Token Editing (STE) sparsely modifies only channels {6,7,8} at token positions 12 and 18—identified via an offline Gumbel-Softmax probing step—using a learnable offset to steer global appearance without disturbing core semantics. A token-to-map interface lifts the 12-dim tokens to 64-dim, projects them to a 32×32 coarse map, and performs three-stage upsampling to 256×256 while injecting scale-aligned detail features from the original images. A private encoder then factorizes each modality's token-induced map into low-frequency base and high-frequency detail components, which are fused in separate subspaces before residual decoding. Training follows a two-stage schedule: a 40-epoch reconstruction warm-up stabilizes per-modality base/detail factorization, followed by 80 epochs of cross-modal fusion training with intensity and gradient losses, while the tokenizer remains frozen throughout.

## Results

On the four IVIF/medical fusion benchmarks (M³FD, RoadScene, TNO, Harvard), the proposed method achieves the best SSIM across all datasets (1.49, 1.45, 1.42, 1.45 respectively) and the highest SCD on M³FD (1.85), RoadScene (1.82), and TNO (1.82). For downstream tasks, it attains the best mAP₅₀:₉₅ of 0.360 on M³FD object detection and a competitive mIoU of 0.692 on FMB semantic segmentation. The method uses only 1.3M trainable parameters with 304.5G FLOPs, substantially lighter than Text-DiFuse (119.5M params, 47709.9G FLOPs) while achieving higher SSIM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all nine baselines on SSIM across every dataset, with the largest margins over LRRNet (0.78→1.49 on M³FD) and SAGE (1.00→1.49 on M³FD). Against the strongest competitors, it leads CDDFuse by +0.47 SSIM on M³FD and +0.37 on TNO, and surpasses Text-DiFuse by +0.27 SSIM on M³FD and +1.17 on TNO. On the Harvard medical dataset, Text-DiFuse edges ahead on EN (5.42 vs 4.76) and SD (71.32 vs 70.86), but the proposed method dominates on SCD (1.76 vs 1.52) and SSIM (1.45 vs 0.25), indicating far better structural fidelity. For downstream object detection, the method achieves mAP 0.360, exceeding the next-best TC-MoA (0.349) and CDDFuse (0.346); for semantic segmentation it ties SAGE at mIoU 0.692. The efficiency comparison shows the method uses 1.3M trainable parameters and 304.5G FLOPs, roughly 157× fewer FLOPs than Text-DiFuse (47709.9G) while delivering higher SSIM.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant encoder-fusion-decoder paradigm in multimodal image fusion (CDDFuse, DDFM, LRRNet, Text-IF, etc.), which encodes all information into dense 2D feature grids, by arguing that such grids are structurally mismatched for decoupling image-level appearance (base) from spatially localized detail. It builds directly on the TiTok compact 1D tokenizer (Yu et al., NeurIPS 2024), but repurposes it not as a reconstruction backbone (as in generative settings) but as a frozen, non-spatial appearance carrier that feeds a conventional 2D fusion pathway via a token-to-map interface. The Selective Token Editing mechanism, which uses Gumbel-Softmax probing to identify a handful of appearance-sensitive token positions for sparse learnable offsets, is a new lightweight intervention not present in prior fusion or tokenizer literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled reframing of why global appearance control is difficult in 2D fusion (spatial broadcasting entangles base with detail and noise) and offers a practical, plug-in solution that requires only ~1.3M trainable parameters and no extra loss terms. Key limitations include the configuration-specificity of STE positions (positions 12 and 18 are tied to TiTok-32 and must be re-probed for other tokenizers), the domain gap of the frozen tokenizer on medical images (SSIM drops to 0.70), and the added inference latency from maintaining both the frozen tokenizer branch and the 2D pathway. Open questions include whether the 2-slot sparsity finding generalizes across tokenizer families, whether lighter token-to-map interfaces can close the efficiency gap with 2D-only baselines, and whether the approach extends to more than two modalities or to video fusion.
