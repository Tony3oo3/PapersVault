## Problem

Existing CLIP-style medical vision-language pretraining methods rely on global or local feature-level alignment trained on substantial paired data, but under the realistic constraint of limited paired image-report supervision, global alignment lets non-diagnostic information dominate embeddings while local alignment overemphasizes fragmented visual details, making it difficult to capture the integrated diagnostic evidence that underpins clinical decision-making.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265325/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes chest X-ray images (resized to 256×256, randomly cropped to 224×224) and radiology report text (Findings and Impression sections), where only 5% or 10% of images have explicit report pairings and the remainder are unpaired unimodal data. It produces aligned image-text embeddings in a shared diagnostic evidence space, with the image side represented by L=64 lesion-level embeddings projected onto K=64 learnable diagnostic prototypes, and the text side represented by LLM-extracted evidence phrases encoded into the same prototype space.

## Method

LGDEA shifts pretraining from feature-level to diagnostic-evidence-level alignment by first using an LLM to extract key diagnostic evidence phrases from reports and organizing them into a structured space of K learnable diagnostic prototypes via a reconstruction loss. On the visual side, L learnable lesion queries attend to ViT patch features and are projected into the same prototype space; paired samples are aligned by minimizing KL divergence between report-induced and image-induced prototype distributions, while unpaired images are regularized by a similarity-weighted KL consistency loss among visually neighboring lesions. To exploit abundant unpaired data for cross-modal alignment, the method constructs intra-modal evidence graphs (image-image and report-report) from aggregated evidence representations and propagates the sparse paired relation matrix over these graphs via label propagation (P^(t+1) = S_I P^(t) S_T + Y), yielding soft higher-order cross-modal relations that weight a contrastive alignment loss, effectively transferring supervision from the few paired samples to the large unpaired pool.

## Results

On phrase grounding (MS-CXR), LGDEA trained with only 10% paired data achieves the highest CNR across all eight disease categories, reaching 0.9525 on Cardiomegaly and 1.0928 on Pleural Effusion, surpassing the best fully-paired baseline MedAligner (0.6058 and 1.0207 respectively). On image-text retrieval (MIMIC-5×200), LGDEA_10%pair attains Prec@1 of 56.31% and Prec@10 of 50.65%, outperforming MedAligner (55.88% and 50.56%) and all other baselines. On zero-shot classification, LGDEA_10%pair achieves 80.00% on MIMIC-5×200, 90.47% on COVID, and 87.06% on NIH Chest X-rays, exceeding the strongest baseline MedAligner (77.89%, 88.20%, 84.11%) on three of four datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LGDEA with only 10% paired data outperforms all 11 baselines (trained with 100% paired data) on phrase grounding across all eight disease categories, with the largest margins over MedAligner (+0.347 on Cardiomegaly, +0.240 on Lung Opacity) and over global-alignment methods like MedCLIP and MGCA. On image-text retrieval, LGDEA_10%pair beats MedAligner on Prec@1 (56.31 vs. 55.88) and Prec@10 (50.65 vs. 50.56) but is slightly behind on Prec@2 (53.85 vs. 54.08) and Prec@5 (53.71 vs. 54.05); it surpasses all other baselines including AFLoc (54.37 Prec@1) and PRIOR (53.19 Prec@1). On zero-shot classification, LGDEA_10%pair leads on MIMIC-5×200 (80.00 vs. MedAligner's 77.89), COVID (90.47 vs. 88.20), and NIH (87.06 vs. 84.11), but trails MedAligner on RSNA (79.26 vs. 80.05). Notably, even LGDEA with only 5% paired data exceeds MedAligner on Cardiomegaly (0.9127 vs. 0.6058) and Lung Opacity (0.9032 vs. 0.7163) in phrase grounding, and surpasses baselines retrained with 50% paired data (Table 2), demonstrating that the evidence-space mechanism compensates for the 10× reduction in paired supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

LGDEA extends the CLIP-style medical vision-language pretraining lineage (MedCLIP, GLoRIA, MGCA, MedAligner, AFLoc) by departing from both global and local feature-level alignment in favor of a new 'diagnostic evidence alignment' paradigm. It repurposes LLMs (Spark-Desk) not as a downstream tool but as a pretraining-time evidence extractor that constructs a shared diagnostic prototype space, a role not previously exploited in medical VLP. The label-propagation mechanism over intra-modal evidence graphs to transfer sparse paired supervision to unpaired unimodal data draws on semi-supervised graph learning but is applied here in a novel cross-modal medical context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical relevance is high: medical imaging datasets are inherently limited in high-quality paired image-report data, and this paper demonstrates that 5–10% paired data with abundant unpaired unimodal data can match or exceed fully paired baselines across phrase grounding, retrieval, and zero-shot classification. Key limitations include evaluation restricted to chest X-rays (MIMIC-CXR, CheXpert), dependence on LLM quality for evidence extraction, and the abstractness of the 'diagnostic prototype' space, which lacks explicit clinical interpretability. Open questions include generalization to 3D modalities (CT, MRI), sensitivity to LLM hallucinations in evidence extraction, and whether the evidence-space formulation transfers to non-radiology clinical text.
