## Problem

Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) methods in federated learning implicitly assume that local flatness on private data transfers to the global model, but under statistical heterogeneity this assumption breaks down: the aggregated model lands on sharp ridges between disjoint local optima rather than a truly flat global basin. This 'Flatness Discrepancy' causes catastrophic generalization degradation on unseen clients, and existing remedies either ignore the discrepancy or require transmitting heavy Hessian approximations, incurring prohibitive communication and computation overheads.

## Input / Output

Each client receives the current global model parameters w^t along with its own drift dual h_i and geometric bias b_i (both d-dimensional vectors maintained across rounds), and performs K local SGD steps on its private non-IID image partition (32×32 RGB CIFAR-10/100). The server outputs an updated global model w^{t+1} and per-client bias estimates b_i that encode the persistent curvature misalignment direction. The only additional communication beyond standard FedAvg is the 1.5× byte overhead for transmitting the dual variables h_i and b_i; no Hessian or perturbation vectors are exchanged.

## Method

FedScar formulates federated optimization as a Split-Dual ADMM problem with two decoupled dual variables: h_i (drift dual) correcting first-order parameter drift à la SCAFFOLD, and b_i (bias dual) correcting second-order geometric misalignment. The key mechanism is a history-accumulated bias update b_i^{(t+1)} = γ·b_i^{(t)} + Σ^{(t)}·v̂_i^{(t)}, where v̂_i is the normalized drift direction and Σ^{(t)} is a heterogeneity-sensitive scale computed from the weighted dispersion of local updates relative to the global update, acting as a low-pass filter that isolates structural geometric bias from stochastic noise. During local training, the term (b̄ − b_i) is injected into the SGD gradient as a geometric counter-force that redirects trajectories away from sharp local minima toward regions satisfying both local flatness and global alignment. The global aggregation step combines the collective dual residuals (h_j − b_j) to evolve the model toward the intersection of local flat regions, achieving O(1/√T) convergence while provably contracting the flatness discrepancy at rate (1−γ/2)^T.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 under extreme heterogeneity (Dir 0.01, p=10%), FedScar achieves 78.42% max accuracy (77.24% last-50 mean), outperforming FedAvg (71.00%/63.20%) and FedSAM (71.18%/63.42%) by over 7 points. On CIFAR-100 under the Pathological-20 setting (p=10%), FedScar reaches 49.67%/48.57%, surpassing the strongest baseline FedGMT (48.57%/47.92%) and FedAvg (39.90%/37.01%) by ~1 and ~10 points respectively. FedScar also exhibits superior stability: its peak-to-mean gap is only ~1.2% on CIFAR-10 Dir 0.01, versus ~7.8% for FedSAM, indicating robust resistance to client drift.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedScar outperforms all nine baselines across every heterogeneity setting and both datasets. Against traditional FL methods, FedScar leads FedAvg by 7.42 points (max) and 14.04 points (mean) on CIFAR-10 Dir 0.01, and by 9.77/11.56 points on CIFAR-100 Pat 20; it also surpasses SCAFFOLD, FedDyn, and FedInit by 2–4 points in the most challenging regimes. Among SAM-based methods, FedScar's advantage over FedSAM is most pronounced in stability: FedSAM's mean accuracy collapses to 63.42% on CIFAR-10 Dir 0.01 (a 7.76-point peak-to-mean drop), while FedScar maintains 77.24% (only 1.18-point drop). FedScar edges out the strongest SAM baseline FedGMT by 1.77 points on CIFAR-10 Dir 0.01 and by 1.10 points on CIFAR-100 Pat 20, with the gap widening under sparser participation (5%: FedScar 45.27% vs. FedGMT 43.99%). FedSMOO and FedLESAM, which use heavier communication (2× bytes or 1.5× bytes), are consistently outperformed by FedScar while FedScar uses only 1.5× bytes and 1× FLOPS.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedScar sits at the intersection of two FL research threads: drift correction via control variates (SCAFFOLD, FedDyn) and flatness-seeking via Sharpness-Aware Minimization (FedSAM, FedGamma, FedGMT). Its key conceptual departure is identifying 'Flatness Discrepancy'—the failure of locally flat solutions to transfer to global flatness under non-IID data—as a distinct, correctable geometric bias rather than irreducible heterogeneity noise. Methodologically, it unifies SCAFFOLD-style first-order drift correction with a novel second-order geometric bias correction within a Split-Dual ADMM framework, where the bias is estimated from accumulated drift trajectories rather than requiring Hessian transmission or extra uplink communication.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap in SAM-based FL: the implicit assumption that local flatness implies global flatness is shown to break under severe heterogeneity, and the proposed correction mechanism (history-accumulated dual variable with variance-aware scaling) resolves it without extra communication rounds. However, empirical validation is limited to CIFAR-10/100 with small CNNs and ResNet-8, leaving open whether the geometric bias correction generalizes to larger architectures, NLP tasks, or more extreme cross-device settings. The theoretical Hessian dissimilarity bound (Assumption 5.3) is non-trivial to verify in practice, and the method's sensitivity to the forgetting factor γ (requiring tuning per heterogeneity level) adds a practical hyperparameter burden.
