## Problem

Standard coordinate-wise optimizers like AdamW and Signum do not account for the spectral structure of weight matrices and gradients, leaving LLM training vulnerable to two issues: uncontrolled spectral norms of optimizer updates that destabilize training and degrade generalization, and sparse spectral spikes in stochastic gradient noise where a few singular values are orders of magnitude larger than the rest. Existing clipping strategies (coordinate-wise or global) fail to selectively suppress these spectral artifacts without also suppressing informative signal.

## Input / Output

SPECTRA operates as a wrapper around any base optimizer with decoupled weight decay, consuming the same inputs (stochastic gradients, momentum buffers, learning rate schedules) and producing updated weight matrices. The key computational detail is that spectral clipping is approximated via Soft Spectral Clipping (SSC), which requires only square matrix-matrix multiplications through Newton-Schulz iterations (N_s=10 by default) rather than expensive full SVD, adding roughly 2% per-step overhead at 780M scale. The framework naturally extends to 1D parameters and supports dynamic clipping schedules during warm-up phases.

## Method

SPECTRA applies two spectral clipping operations: post-spectral clipping on the optimizer update matrix to enforce a hard spectral-norm bound, and optional pre-spectral clipping on raw gradients to filter noise spikes before they corrupt momentum buffers. The central theoretical contribution shows that post-spectral clipping with decoupled weight decay is exactly equivalent to a Composite Frank-Wolfe algorithm minimizing a spectral-norm-constrained objective with implicit Frobenius-norm weight regularization, yielding convergence guarantees of O(1/K) (noise-free) or O(K^{-1/3}) (stochastic). For the sparse spike noise setting, the paper proves spectral clipping reduces variance from rℓ² to r·min(√r·M, ℓ)² while preserving signal, whereas global clipping either destroys signal linearly in spike magnitude or retains full ℓ² variance. The framework is optimizer-agnostic and recovers Muon as a special case (α→∞, c=1/α), while practical hyperparameters (λ≈0.1, c≈10, α aligned to RMS norm) provide a robust default configuration.

## Results

On the 820M-parameter model pretrained on FineWeb-Edu (16.8B tokens), Spectra-Signum achieves the best average 5-shot ICL accuracy of 51.62% across 10 tasks, outperforming the strongest non-SPECTRA baseline (Mars, 50.77%) by 0.85 points. In the fixed-time-budget comparison at 780M scale (Table 2), SPECTRA variants trained for 16,000 steps achieve validation losses of 2.573–2.582, beating their base optimizers even when the baselines are given 20% more training steps (e.g., Signum: 2.573 vs. 2.580 at 19,200 steps). The per-step overhead of SPECTRA is only ~2% at this scale, making it both token-efficient and time-efficient.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPECTRA consistently improves every base optimizer it is applied to. Spectra-Signum delivers the largest gain, jumping from 49.84% to 51.62% average ICL accuracy (+1.78 pts) and beating Mars (50.77%) and D-Muon (50.59%) by 0.85 and 1.03 points respectively. Spectra-AdEMAMix improves AdEMAMix from 49.05% to 50.99% (+1.94 pts), surpassing both Mars and D-Muon. Spectra-AdamW lifts AdamW from 49.76% to 50.30% (+0.54 pts), though it remains below Spectra-Signum and Spectra-AdEMAMix. In the fixed-time-budget comparison (Table 2), all SPECTRA variants at 16,000 steps outperform their baselines even when the baselines receive 20% additional steps (e.g., Signum 2.573 vs. 2.580; AdEMAMix 2.574 vs. 2.571), confirming that the ~2% per-step overhead is more than compensated by faster convergence. SOAP is mentioned as a comparison baseline in the experimental setup but no quantitative results are reported for it in the main tables.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the spectral optimization lineage (Spectral Descent, Shampoo, Muon) by decoupling the spectral clipping operation from any specific optimizer, turning it into an optimizer-agnostic wrapper. The theoretical connection to composite Frank-Wolfe builds directly on Pethick et al. (2025), who showed Muon with decoupled weight decay performs Frank-Wolfe within a spectral-norm ball; SPECTRA extends this to arbitrary base optimizers and adds an explicit regularization term. The soft spectral clipping via Newton-Schulz iterations follows the same computational recipe as Muon's orthogonalization, but applied to a clipping operator rather than a full orthogonalization. The sparse spectral spike analysis extends the heavy-tailed gradient noise literature (Simsekli et al., 2019; Zhang et al., 2020) by showing the noise has a specific low-rank structure amenable to spectral (rather than global) clipping.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SPECTRA provides a low-overhead (~2%) drop-in wrapper that consistently improves validation loss across multiple state-of-the-art optimizers (AdamW, Signum, AdEMAMix) at scales from 124M to 1.5B parameters, making it practically attractive for LLM training pipelines. The Frank-Wolfe reformulation gives convergence guarantees and a geometric interpretation of hyperparameters, while the spectral spike analysis explains *why* spectral clipping outperforms global clipping in a way that coordinate-wise methods cannot. Open questions include scaling beyond 1.5B parameters, distributed/tensor-parallel implementations of the Newton-Schulz step, and whether the benefits persist under more general (non-sparse-spike) noise models. The paper also leaves open whether explicit weight regularization (beyond the implicit one) could further improve results.
