## Problem

Test-time adaptation (TTA) methods update model parameters using unlabeled test data during inference, but this creates an unacknowledged privacy risk: the adapted model's parameters depend on all past test inputs, enabling potential membership inference or reconstruction attacks on sensitive test data. No prior work had addressed this privacy gap in TTA, and naively applying DP-SGD is non-trivial because TTA operates at small batch sizes, uses data-dependent filtering, and processes each sample exactly once in a streaming fashion.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a stream of unlabeled 224×224 RGB test images arriving in fixed batches of 64 under distribution shift (e.g., ImageNet-C corruptions at severity 5), along with pretrained model parameters (ViT-Base/16 or ConvNeXt-Tiny). It outputs updated affine parameters of normalization layers (the only adaptable subset) and per-sample class predictions, with the entire adaptation procedure satisfying a (ε, δ)-DP guarantee where ε ∈ {1, 5, 10, 15, 20} and δ = 10⁻⁶. The 'change one' neighboring definition is used rather than the standard 'leave-one-out', reflecting the streaming nature where a sample is replaced rather than removed.

## Method

The paper adapts DP-SGD to five TTA methods (Tent, EATA, SAR, DeYO, COME) by replacing the standard batch gradient update with per-sample L2-clipped gradients (threshold C) plus calibrated Gaussian noise (multiplier σ), yielding a μ-GDP guarantee with μ = 2/σ via a single-step analysis (no composition needed since each sample is seen once). Method-specific customizations include removing non-DP sample filters (entropy/diversity thresholds that alter effective batch size), substituting SAR's sharpness perturbation with the DP-SAT approach using the previous step's private gradient, and retaining DeYO's per-sample PLPD term since its sensitivity is bounded by clipping. Architecturally, BatchNorm is excluded in favor of LayerNorm (ViT) to ensure per-sample gradient independence. A key empirical finding is that per-sample gradient clipping alone—without any privacy noise—improves TTA accuracy by 0.1%–4.1% across methods, outperforming batch-level clipping, gradient-norm filtering, and entropy-loss filtering, because it rescales rather than discards large-gradient samples while preserving their directional information.

## Results

On ImageNet-C (severity 5, continual setting, ViT-Base/16), per-sample gradient clipping alone (Clipper variants) improves average top-1 accuracy by 0.1%–4.1% across TTA methods, with Clipper-Tent reaching 64.9% vs. Tent's 60.8% and Clipper-DeYO-COME achieving the best overall 67.5%. Under differential privacy (δ=10⁻⁶), DP-Tent at ε=20 retains 62.9% average accuracy—still above non-private Tent (60.8%)—and degrades only to 58.5% at the strongest privacy level ε=1 (a 2.3% drop). The computational overhead is modest: DP variants add 15–64 ms per batch (≤1.28× wall-clock time) over non-private baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares five TTA baselines (Tent, EATA, SAR, DeYO, DeYO-COME) against their DP and Clipper variants. DP-Tent is the standout: at ε=20 it achieves 62.9% average accuracy, outperforming non-private Tent (60.8%) by 2.1 points, and even at ε=10 it retains 62.1%—still above the unclipped baseline. For the other four methods, DP variants lose to their non-private counterparts at ε=20 by 2.9% (EATA: 63.0 vs. 65.9), 1.2% (SAR: 59.3 vs. 60.8), 2.4% (DeYO: 58.5 vs. 61.0), and 1.7% (DeYO-COME: 64.7 vs. 66.4). However, the Clipper variants (clipping without noise) improve or match all baselines: Clipper-Tent gains +4.1%, Clipper-DeYO +1.7%, Clipper-SAR +0.9%, Clipper-DeYO-COME +1.1%, and Clipper-EATA +0.1%. The key takeaway is that per-sample clipping—a mechanism required for DP—provides a free accuracy boost in the non-private regime, partially offsetting the cost of adding Gaussian noise for privacy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of test-time adaptation (TTA) and differential privacy (DP). It builds directly on DP-SGD (Abadi et al., 2016) for its per-sample gradient clipping and Gaussian noise injection, and adapts five popular TTA methods—Tent, EATA, SAR, DeYO, and COME—into DP-compatible forms. The key departure from prior DP work (e.g., DP fine-tuning by Yu et al., 2021; De et al., 2022) is the unsupervised, streaming, small-batch nature of TTA, which requires removing data-dependent filters and using a 'change-one' neighboring definition rather than the 'leave-one-out' definition standard in DP-SGD. The paper also draws on DP-SAT (Park et al., 2023) for a DP-compatible sharpness-aware update in DP-SAR.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to identify and address the privacy risk inherent in TTA, where model parameters accumulate information about all past test inputs. The practical finding that per-sample gradient clipping alone (without DP noise) improves TTA accuracy by 0.1–4.1% across methods is a useful, low-cost technique for practitioners regardless of privacy needs. Open questions include closing the remaining accuracy gap between DP-TTA and standard TTA at strong privacy budgets, making data-dependent filters (entropy thresholds, diversity checks) DP-compatible without losing their stabilizing effect, and extending the analysis to larger models and more diverse shift scenarios. The modest computational overhead (~20–30 ms per batch for clipping, up to 1.28× for full DP) suggests these methods are deployable.
