## Problem

Existing reasoning diffusion language models (dLLMs) are limited to narrow task sets (primarily math) with task-specific finetuning, and RL training for dLLMs suffers from collapse, hindered exploration due to KL regularization, vanishing training signals on difficult prompts, and unstable likelihood estimation from Monte Carlo sampling. No prior work has demonstrated a unified, general-purpose reasoning dLLM spanning diverse multimodal tasks including image editing and reason-intensive grounding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262678/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts heterogeneous multimodal inputs—text prompts, image-plus-question pairs, reference images with editing instructions, and images with grounding queries—and produces structured outputs consisting of a text reasoning trace enclosed in think tags followed by a task-specific answer (text, bounding box coordinates, or image tokens). Generation proceeds via iterative unmasking from a fully masked sequence, with fixed maximum lengths of 512 tokens for math reasoning, 128 for grounding, and 256 for image editing. The unified architecture handles both understanding and generation within a single masked diffusion framework, so the same model can answer a VQA question or produce an edited image depending on the prompt.

## Method

LaViDa-R1 is built on the LaViDa-O masked diffusion base model and trained in two stages: SFT on reasoning data, followed by a unified post-training phase that combines SFT, online GRPO, and best-of-N self-distillation into a single weighted policy-gradient objective (Eq. 5), replacing the standard KL regularizer with SFT loss to permit exploration while preventing collapse. Two guided rollout mechanisms address vanishing training signal: Answer-Forcing injects the ground-truth answer into a partially masked sequence and leverages the dLLM's bidirectional inpainting to synthesize reasoning traces when all sampled responses are incorrect, while Tree Search branches new samples from early diffusion states of the highest-reward trajectory when no ground truth exists. A complementary-masking likelihood estimator uses two coupled MC samples (one at t, one at 1−t) with uniform weighting w(t)=1 to guarantee 100% token coverage per estimate while avoiding the imbalanced per-token gradients that arise from the standard 1/t weighting at disparate mask ratios.

## Results

LaViDa-R1 achieves 60.0 on MathVista, 38.7 on MathVerse, 81.7 on ChartQA, 78.9 on AI2D, 32.8 on MMMU-Pro, 81.5 on GSM8K, and 38.6 on MATH-500, outperforming its base model LaViDa-O across all benchmarks. The largest gains come from language-only reasoning: GSM8K improves from 47.4 to 81.5 (+34.1) and MATH-500 from 23.4 to 38.6 (+15.2) over the base model. On reason-intensive grounding (Lisa-Grounding), LaViDa-R1 reaches 66.7 P@0.5 and 60.0 mIoU, surpassing VLM-R1 (63.1 P@0.5) and Qwen3-VL-8B (62.4 P@0.5, 56.6 mIoU). On image editing (ImgEdit), LaViDa-R1 scores 3.90 overall, improving +0.19 over LaViDa-O (3.71) but remaining below GPT-4o (4.20).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against its direct base model LaViDa-O, LaViDa-R1 wins on every benchmark, with the most dramatic gains on language-only tasks (GSM8K +34.1, MATH-500 +15.2) and consistent improvements on visual reasoning (MathVista +3.1, MathVerse +1.8). Compared to the SFT-only ablation (LaViDa-O + SFT), the unified RL post-training adds further gains on all tasks, confirming that RL drives exploration beyond supervised modes. Against other unified dLLMs, LaViDa-R1 substantially outperforms MMaDa + UniGRPO on all shared benchmarks (e.g., GSM8K 81.5 vs. 73.4, MATH-500 38.6 vs. 36.0) and LaViDa-L on all visual tasks (MathVista 60.0 vs. 44.8, ChartQA 81.7 vs. 64.6). On Lisa-Grounding, LaViDa-R1 (66.7 P@0.5, 60.0 mIoU) surpasses both the specialist VLM-R1 (63.1 P@0.5) and the general-purpose Qwen3-VL-8B (62.4 P@0.5, 56.6 mIoU), while the base LaViDa-O scores only 29.2 P@0.5. On ImgEdit, LaViDa-R1 (3.90) trails GPT-4o (4.20) but leads all open-source unified models including Qwen2.5VL+Flux (3.80) and FluxKontext (3.52). On language-only benchmarks, LaViDa-R1 slightly trails the language-only LLaDA-8B + DiffuGRPO (GSM8K 81.5 vs. 82.1, MATH-500 38.6 vs. 40.2), though the latter uses per-dataset finetuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the LaViDa-O unified multimodal dLLM as its base model and adapts the GRPO reinforcement learning framework (from DeepSeekMath) to the discrete diffusion setting, where sequence likelihood is intractable and must be estimated via ELBO. It departs from prior dLLM RL work (d1, UniGRPO) in three key ways: (1) replacing the KL divergence regularizer with SFT loss within a unified policy-gradient objective that also incorporates best-of-N self-distillation (BOND), (2) extending RL beyond language-only or math-reasoning tasks to image editing and reason-intensive grounding, and (3) repurposing the complementary masking scheme from LaViDa pretraining as a likelihood estimator with a modified weighting (w(t)=1 instead of w(t)=1/t). The answer-forcing and tree-search mechanisms for guided rollout generation are novel contributions that address the vanishing-signal problem specific to dLLM RL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that standard GRPO with KL regularization diverges for dLLMs (even at β=0.1) is practically important, as it invalidates the default RL recipe used for autoregressive LLMs in the diffusion setting and motivates the SFT-as-regularization alternative that eliminates the need for a reference model. The unified post-training framework is a clean, general recipe that could be adopted by other dLLM groups working on multimodal tasks. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond 8B parameters, whether the specific weighting choices (γ=0.5, w(t)=1) are robust across architectures, and whether the guided rollout techniques (answer-forcing, tree-search) generalize to tasks with no verifiable reward signal. The paper is limited to a single base model family (LaViDa) and does not compare against autoregressive MLLM baselines of similar scale on all tasks.
