## Problem

GUI agents struggle with multi-step tasks because constrained context windows cause them to lose critical early-step information, and static policies trained on fixed datasets cannot reuse past successful strategies or adapt to dynamic environments. No existing framework provides a unified mechanism to encode explicit historical experiences into implicit policy parameters, preventing continuous self-evolution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261982/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each step the agent receives a GUI screenshot, a natural-language user instruction, and a retrieved memory context assembled from three sources: a sliding-window of recent (observation, action, next-observation) triples, top-K semantically similar domain rules via cosine embedding retrieval, and top-K reflective summaries from past trajectories scored by a weighted fusion of text-intent and visual-similarity embeddings. The output is a structured XML reasoning chain (progress evaluation, decision rationale, updated history summary) followed by a JSON action specifying type (click, scroll, type, etc.), value, and normalized [x, y] coordinates.

## Method

SE-GA combines Test-Time Memory Extension (TTME) with Memory-Augmented Self-Evolution (MASE). TTME maintains a hierarchical repository of episodic (sliding-window action history), semantic (embedding-indexed interaction rules), and experiential (hybrid text-image retrieval of past trajectories with agent-generated reflective summaries) memories that are injected into the prompt at inference time, enabling long-horizon context without retraining. MASE is a two-stage training pipeline: Stage I performs supervised fine-tuning on curated grounding data to stabilize the base VLM policy; Stage II applies GRPO-based reinforcement learning with a token-level importance ratio (inspired by DAPO), an adaptive upper-clipping bound that follows a cosine decay schedule, and a hierarchical reward that decomposes accuracy into action-type correctness plus parameter-level sub-rewards (point-in-box for clicks, IoU-thresholded for scrolls, exact-match for text). A Hindsight Goal-Shifting mechanism relabels failed trajectories as successful instances of intermediate sub-goals, converting otherwise discarded data into useful supervision signals for the self-evolution stage.

## Results

SE-GA (7B) achieves a state-of-the-art average grounding accuracy of 89.0% on ScreenSpot, surpassing the much larger UI-TARS-72B (88.4%) and Qwen2.5-VL-72B (85.0%). On the challenging AndroidControl-High benchmark, SE-GA reaches a 75.8% success rate, outperforming all same-scale baselines and matching UI-TARS-72B (74.7%). On the dynamic AndroidWorld benchmark, SE-GA achieves 39.0% success rate, a 6-point improvement over the next-best 7B model (UI-TARS-7B at 33.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SE-GA (7B) outperforms all 7B and smaller baselines across every benchmark and metric. On ScreenSpot, it beats OS-Atlas (82.5%), Aguvis (84.4%), UGround (71.5%), and Qwen2.5-VL-7B (76.1%) by 6.5–17.5 points in average grounding accuracy. On AndroidControl-High, SE-GA's 75.8% SR surpasses OS-Atlas (71.2%), SeeClick (59.1%), and Qwen2.5-VL-7B (47.0%), and edges out the 72B UI-TARS (74.7%) by 1.1 points. On GUIOdyssey, SE-GA achieves the highest action-type accuracy (96.5%) among all models including UI-TARS-72B (95.4%), but trails UI-TARS-72B on grounding (87.4 vs. 91.4) and success rate (83.9 vs. 88.6). On the online AndroidWorld benchmark, SE-GA (39.0%) leads UI-TARS-7B (33.0%), GUI-Critic-R1 (27.6%), Qwen2.5-VL-7B (25.5%), GPT-4o (23.7%), and OS-Genesis (17.4%) by 6–21.6 points. Closed-source models (GPT-4o, Claude, Gemini) are substantially outperformed on all offline benchmarks, with GPT-4o and Claude scoring 0.0% grounding accuracy on AndroidControl, indicating they cannot produce valid spatial coordinates without specialized training.

## Relation to Prior Work

SE-GA builds on the GRPO reinforcement learning framework from DeepSeek-R1 and the token-level importance ratio from DAPO, adapting them to the GUI navigation setting with a hierarchical reward design and adaptive clipping. Conceptually, it departs from prior GUI agents (UI-TARS, OS-Atlas, Aguvis) that rely on static policies or simple retrieval-augmented generation by introducing a closed-loop architecture where a hierarchical memory system (episodic, semantic, experiential) simultaneously serves as an inference-time context provider (TTME) and a training-time data source (MASE). The Hindsight Goal-Shifting mechanism repurposes the Hindsight Experience Replay idea from robotics to convert failed GUI trajectories into valid sub-task supervision, while the memory architecture draws on cognitive science distinctions rather than the flat vector-store RAG used in prior agent work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates a practical pathway for GUI agents to move beyond static, one-shot policies toward continual self-improvement, with the multi-round evolution experiments (Table 8) showing consistent gains across all benchmarks. However, the memory retrieval efficiency is acknowledged as a scaling bottleneck, and the entire system is validated only on Android with 4K trajectories, leaving open whether the self-evolution loop generalizes to web/desktop platforms or scales to much larger interaction corpora. The finding that removing MASE causes more degradation than removing TTME suggests that encoding experience into policy parameters is more critical than retrieval at inference time, a result that could reshape how future agent systems allocate their architectural complexity.
