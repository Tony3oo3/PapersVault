## Problem

Vision-language-action models that use action chunking face an inherent trade-off: longer horizons improve global foresight on long-horizon tasks but degrade fine-grained local control, while shorter horizons yield the opposite. Existing approaches fix a single chunk length (horizon) chosen heuristically, imposing a bottleneck on generalization and preventing adaptive control over latency at inference time. The paper asks whether multiple horizons can be jointly exploited within a single model to resolve this trade-off.

## Input / Output

The policy receives multi-view RGB images, a language instruction, optional proprioceptive state, and optional observation history, encoded by a VLM backbone into a context representation. The action transformer then predicts a full action chunk of length H in R^{H×d_a}, but internally this chunk is split into N truncated segments of varying lengths (e.g., {3, 6, …, 30}), each processed in parallel. At inference, the model additionally outputs per-step, per-horizon gating weights and a self-truncating executable prefix whose length is determined dynamically by cross-horizon consensus rather than a fixed value.

## Method

MoH rearranges a ground-truth action chunk into multiple truncated segments of different horizons, pads them to the maximum length H with horizon-specific attention masks, and processes all segments in a single forward pass through a shared full-attention action transformer. A lightweight linear gating head (only 2k parameters) produces per-step, per-horizon logits that are softmax-normalized over valid horizons to yield fusion weights, and the final action at each timestep is a weighted sum of horizon-specific predictions. Training combines the loss on fused predictions, individual horizon-specific losses, and a balance loss (mean squared coefficient of variation of gate usage) to prevent degenerate gating. At inference, a cross-horizon consensus mechanism measures ℓ₁ disagreement between each horizon's prediction and the fused output; the first n steps establish a data-dependent threshold, and the executable prefix extends as long as disagreement stays below threshold and enough horizons remain active, yielding a self-truncating chunk that adapts length to task difficulty.

## Results

On LIBERO, π0.5 with MoH achieves a new state-of-the-art average success rate of 99.0% (Spatial 98.8, Object 100, Goal 98.8, Long 98.4) after only 30k training iterations, surpassing the previous best (Spatial Forcing at 98.5%). MoH yields consistent gains across all tested backbones: π0 improves from 93.8% to 95.1% (+1.3), πreg from 95.2% to 96.4% (+1.2), and StarVLA from 95.9% to 97.0% (+1.1). On RoboCasa, GR00T with MoH improves average success from 28.0% to 31.4% (+3.4%), and on RoboTwin 2.0, π0 with MoH achieves the highest average success rate across both easy and hard settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoH consistently outperforms its respective single-horizon baselines across all four LIBERO suites: π0.5+MoH (99.0%) surpasses π0.5 (97.7%) by +1.3, with the largest gain on the Long suite (+3.0); π0+MoH (95.1%) beats π0 (93.8%) by +1.3, again with the biggest improvement on Long (+3.2). Against the strongest non-MoH competitors, π0.5+MoH (99.0%) exceeds Spatial Forcing (98.5%, 7B, 150k iters), X-VLA (98.1%, 1B, 60k iters), and OpenVLA-OFT (97.1%, 7B, 150k iters) while using only 3B parameters and 30k iterations. On RoboCasa, GR00T+MoH improves over GR00T by +3.4% average (31.4% vs 28.0%), with gains on all five tasks. On RoboTwin 2.0, π0+MoH achieves the highest average success rate among all tested methods on both easy and hard settings, confirming generalization beyond LIBERO. The regression-based πreg+MoH (96.4%) also outperforms the flow-matching π0+MoH (95.1%), suggesting the regression objective converges well on in-distribution tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the action chunking paradigm introduced by ACT (Zhao et al., 2023) and adopted by modern VLA models like π0 (Black et al., 2024) and π0.5 (Shi et al., 2025), which predict a fixed-length sequence of future actions via a full-attention transformer. The key departure is replacing the single fixed horizon with a mixture of multiple horizons processed in parallel through a shared action transformer, fused by a lightweight linear gate inspired by mixture-of-experts load-balancing losses (Fedus et al., 2022; Rau, 2019). Unlike prior temporal ensembling approaches such as CogACT's Adaptive Temporal Ensemble (Li et al., 2024) that fuse across successive planning steps, MoH fuses across temporal scales within a single planning step, making the two mechanisms orthogonal. The flow-matching formulation (Lipman et al., 2022) and one-step regression objectives serve as the underlying prediction heads that MoH wraps without modification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The horizon trade-off between long-term foresight and short-term precision is a fundamental but previously under-addressed bottleneck in VLA action prediction; MoH provides a simple, plug-and-play solution that consistently improves across flow-matching and regression policies, multiple backbones (PaliGemma, Qwen3-VL), and simulation/real-world settings. The dynamic inference via cross-horizon consensus is a practical contribution enabling 2.5× higher action throughput while preserving performance, which is directly relevant for real-time robotic deployment. Open questions include whether MoH's benefits extend to large-scale pre-training across heterogeneous embodiments, whether more context-aware gating (conditioned on task state or uncertainty) could further improve adaptivity, and how to integrate memory mechanisms to address the perception and instruction-following failures that MoH does not target.
