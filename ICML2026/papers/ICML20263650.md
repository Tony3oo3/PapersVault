## Problem

Existing simulation environments for home robots are sparsely furnished, largely static, and lack the dense clutter, articulated furniture, and physical properties (mass, friction, inertia) that characterize real indoor spaces. Prior scene synthesis methods—whether procedural, data-driven, or LLM-based—target furniture-level layout and visual realism while treating small objects, articulated assets, and collision geometry as secondary, producing scenes that are not directly simulatable for robotic manipulation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263650/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single natural-language prompt (ranging from sparse one-liners to detailed 151-word descriptions) and produces a complete multi-room 3D scene where every object is a separable, non-composite mesh augmented with convex collision geometry (via V-HACD), estimated physical properties (mass, center of mass, inertia tensor, friction coefficient), and joint definitions for articulated items. Scenes are exported in formats compatible with Drake, MuJoCo, Isaac Sim, and Genesis, and the hierarchical prompt structure (global → room-specific → entity-specific) allows local placement decisions to remain coherent with the original scene intent.

## Method

SceneSmith decomposes scene construction into a tree of stages—architectural layout, furniture, wall-mounted, ceiling, and manipuland placement—where each stage is an iterative interaction among three GPT-5.2 VLM agents: a Designer that proposes modifications via structured tools, a Critic that scores feasibility and prompt alignment, and an Orchestrator that manages checkpoints, rollback, and termination. Asset acquisition is tightly integrated through a router that decomposes composite requests into atomic objects and selects among text-to-3D generation (GPT Image 1.5 → SAM3 segmentation → SAM3D mesh reconstruction), ArtVIP retrieval for articulated objects, and procedural thin coverings for flat surfaces. A VLM estimates physical properties from multi-view renders, and a lightweight post-processing step resolves inter-object penetrations via nonlinear optimization and enforces static equilibrium through gravity simulation in Drake. The system achieves 3–6× more objects per room than baselines while maintaining <2% collision rate and 95.6% static stability, with the agentic trio and hierarchical branching enabling parallel generation across rooms and supporting surfaces.

## Results

SceneSmith generates 71.1 ± 13.0 objects per room on average (3–6× more than baselines' 11–23), achieving a 1.2% inter-object collision rate and 95.6% static stability under Drake physics simulation, compared to 3–29% collisions and 8–61% stability for baselines. In a 205-participant user study, SceneSmith achieves 92.2% average realism win rate and 91.5% average prompt faithfulness win rate against all room-level baselines (all p < 0.001). On automated SceneEval metrics, SceneSmith leads on object count (CNT: 82.9), attributes (ATR: 74.4), object-object relationships (OOR: 67.6, a 2.2× improvement over the best baseline), and object-architecture relationships (OAR: 80.6), while its lower ACC (83.4) and NAV (97.6) scores are expected consequences of the substantially higher object density.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SceneSmith outperforms all five baselines on the physics-readiness metrics that matter most for robotics: its 1.2% collision rate is 2.5–22× lower than baselines (best: I-Design at 3.0%), and its 95.6% static stability exceeds the best baseline (I-Design at 60.8%) by 35 percentage points. On semantic quality, SceneSmith achieves 67.6% OOR, a 2.2× improvement over HSM (30.6%), the strongest baseline on that metric, and 80.6% OAR versus HSM's 66.2%. In the user study, SceneSmith wins against every baseline on both realism and faithfulness, with the largest margins against LayoutVLM (94.9–95.4% realism, 93.9–95.8% faithfulness) and the smallest against HSM (88.5% realism, 85.2% faithfulness). The only metrics where SceneSmith trails baselines are ACC (83.4 vs. up to 93.8 for LayoutVLM) and NAV (97.6 vs. up to 99.7), which the authors attribute to the 3–6× higher object density inherently reducing free space. At the house level, SceneSmith generates 214 objects vs. Holodeck's 81 while maintaining 0.9% collisions and 79.8% stability versus Holodeck's 3.8% and 17.9%.

## Relation to Prior Work

SceneSmith extends the agentic scene synthesis line (SceneWeaver's reason-act-reflect loop, LL3M's designer-critic refinement) by replacing single-agent, single-tool-per-turn interaction with a three-agent (designer-critic-orchestrator) architecture that permits unlimited tool invocations per turn and visual self-verification. It builds on HSM's hierarchical decomposition and support-surface detection but adds hierarchical prompt refinement and joint surface population for cross-surface consistency. The key departure from all prior scene synthesis work (ProcTHOR, Holodeck, LayoutVLM, SceneWeaver) is the tight integration of on-demand text-to-3D asset generation, articulated-object retrieval, and physical property estimation directly into the scene construction loop, targeting simulation-readiness rather than visual realism alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a concrete bottleneck in scalable robot training: the gap between sparse, static simulated environments and the dense, articulated, physically complex indoor spaces robots must handle. The demonstrated end-to-end pipeline (text prompt → scene → robot policy execution → automatic success verification) removes manual environment design as a rate-limiting step, and the zero-shot transfer of a real-world-trained policy into generated scenes provides evidence that these environments are not merely visually plausible but functionally useful. Open questions include whether the GPT-5.2-dependent pipeline scales cost-effectively to thousands of scenes, whether generated scene distributions truly match real-world homes (the paper acknowledges VLM-based metrics have false positives/negatives), and whether current text-to-3D quality will improve enough to replace articulated-object retrieval as the primary acquisition strategy.
