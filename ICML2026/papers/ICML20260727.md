## Problem

Standard GNNs degrade on heterophilous graphs because repeated additive neighbor aggregation reinforces self-aligned low-frequency components while suppressing phase-inconsistent signals. Prior heterophily-aware methods (signed filters, spectral reweighting, decomposition) remain fundamentally scalar reweighting schemes that cannot represent the phase structure of interactions or express destructive interference. The paper addresses this gap by introducing a principled mechanism that explicitly models and cancels self-parallel interference at its source rather than merely reweighting it away.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a graph with a symmetric adjacency matrix, real-valued node features (lifted into complex embeddings via two real weight matrices), and semi-supervised node labels (20 per class for training). Each edge carries a learnable unit-modulus U(1) phase factor. The output is a per-node class label produced by concatenating the real and imaginary parts of the final complex embedding, passing through LayerNorm and a linear classifier. Training combines cross-entropy on labeled nodes with a Jensen-Shannon consistency term between two stochastic forward passes using edge dropout.

## Method

GESC replaces additive message passing with a wave-interference mechanism operating in complex vector space. For each neighbor message, a Tikhonov-regularized rank-1 projector removes the component parallel to the target node's current representation (Self-Interference Cancellation), attenuating self-reinforcing low-frequency buildup before attention. A sign-aware gate computed from the gauge-invariant real part of the complex alignment score then modulates the SIC residual, softly suppressing negatively aligned neighbors without over-suppressing informative high-frequency signals. A residual gate interpolates between the gated residual and the original transported message, and a hybrid logit combining magnitude and normalized real alignment drives multi-head attention. The entire pipeline is gauge-equivariant under local U(1) rephasing, and the paper proves coefficient-frozen Lipschitz bounds showing that SIC strictly contracts the directional Lipschitz constant along self-aligned directions, providing a theoretical guarantee against over-smoothing.

## Results

GESC achieves state-of-the-art node classification accuracy on 7 out of 9 benchmarks, ranking first on Cora (84.9%), Citeseer (72.1%), Pubmed (80.4%), Chameleon (65.0%), Squirrel (37.9%), Cornell (59.4%), and Wisconsin (66.6%). On the two remaining datasets, GESC is second: Actor (30.4% vs. L2DGCN's 31.3%) and Texas (74.5% vs. L2DGCN's 76.7%). The largest margins over the strongest baselines appear on heterophilous graphs, e.g., +4.8% over TFE-GNN on Chameleon and +2.5% over DirGNN on Squirrel, while homophilous gains are more modest (e.g., +0.3% over DirGNN on Cora).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GESC outperforms all 16 baselines on 7 of 9 datasets. Against the strongest recent competitors, GESC leads DirGNN by +0.3% on Cora, +4.8% on Chameleon, and +2.7% on Squirrel; it surpasses TFE-GNN by +0.8% on Cora, +4.8% on Chameleon, and +1.9% on Squirrel; and it beats CGNN by +1.0% on Cora, +5.9% on Chameleon, and +1.5% on Squirrel. The only losses are to L2DGCN on Actor (30.4% vs. 31.3%) and Texas (74.5% vs. 76.7%), both small heterophilous WebKB graphs where L2DGCN's degree-bias correction provides a narrow edge. On homophilous benchmarks (Cora, Citeseer, Pubmed), GESC's margins over the best baselines are modest (0.3–0.6%), reflecting the limited room for improvement when homophily is high. On strongly heterophilous graphs (Chameleon, Squirrel, Cornell), GESC's advantages are most pronounced, with gains of 4–6% over spectral and directionality-based methods, confirming that explicit self-interference cancellation is most beneficial when neighbor signals are phase-inconsistent.

## Relation to Prior Work

GESC extends the attention-based message passing paradigm of GAT by replacing real-valued scalar scoring with complex-valued, gauge-equivariant transport (building on de Haan et al., 2021 and MagNet's magnetic Laplacian framework). The key departure from prior magnetic/gauge GNNs (MagNet, MSGNN) and heterophily-aware models (H2GCN, FAGCN, ACM-GCN) is the introduction of a Tikhonov-regularized rank-1 projection (SIC) that explicitly removes self-parallel message components before attention, rather than relying on scalar reweighting or spectral filtering to implicitly handle interference. This positions GESC as the first architecture to jointly combine gauge-consistent U(1) transport, explicit projection-based interference cancellation, and sign-aware phase gating within a single attention mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled geometric mechanism (rank-1 projection as a node-wise notch filter) for mitigating over-smoothing and heterophily simultaneously, backed by gauge-equivariance proofs and coefficient-frozen Lipschitz bounds that are rare in the GNN literature. However, the U(1) transport is limited to scalar phase and does not extend to richer gauge groups (SU(N), SO(3)), and the complex-valued representation doubles the effective dimensionality, raising practical cost concerns for very large graphs. Open questions include whether the interference-cancellation framing generalizes to link prediction or graph-level tasks, and whether the SIC mechanism can be made adaptive per-edge rather than per-node.
