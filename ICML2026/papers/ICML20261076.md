## Problem

Gradient-based jailbreak attacks on audio language models (ALMs) have traditionally applied dense updates to the entire waveform at every iteration, but it is unclear whether this is necessary given the high dimensionality and redundancy of audio signals. This paper asks whether the optimization signal is actually concentrated on a small subset of token-aligned audio regions, and if so, whether sparse token-selective updates can match dense optimization in attack effectiveness.

## Input / Output

The method takes a benign audio waveform (synthesized from a harmful text instruction via TTS, e.g., Google Cloud TTS) and a fixed text prompt as input, and produces an additive perturbation δ (bounded by ‖δ‖∞ ≤ 0.1) such that the perturbed audio x+δ, when fed to the ALM with the same prompt, elicits a policy-violating response beginning with a model-compatible target prefix. The audio is first converted to pre-attention tokens by an acoustic frontend (e.g., Whisper), and the token-to-waveform receptive-field mapping defines the spatial granularity at which sparsity is enforced.

## Method

TAGO (Token-Aware Gradient Optimization) replaces dense waveform updates with sparse token-selective updates by exploiting the observed heterogeneity in token-aligned gradient energy. At each iteration, it computes per-token gradient energies by aggregating squared waveform gradients over each pre-attention audio token's receptive field, selects the top-ζ fraction of tokens (token retention ratio), and constructs a binary waveform mask that zeros out gradients outside the selected tokens' receptive fields before applying the update. The optimization objective combines token-level cross-entropy on a model-compatible target prefix (derived from benign completions to match each ALM's native response style), an L2 perturbation regularizer, and an EOS-suppression term that penalizes premature termination immediately after the enforced prefix. A conditional descent theorem shows that under a γ-concentration assumption on token-level gradients, the masked sparse update still guarantees per-step loss decrease proportional to the captured gradient energy fraction.

## Results

On AdvBench-50, TAGO (ζ=1.0) achieves ASR_l of 87%, 65%, and 71% on Qwen3-Omni, Qwen2.5-Omni, and LLaMA-Omni respectively, with ASR_r at 100% across all three models. Critically, substantial sparsification preserves performance: at ζ=0.25, TAGO retains ASR_l of 86% on Qwen3-Omni (vs. 87% dense) and 72% on LLaMA-Omni (vs. 71% dense), while ASR_r stays at 99–100%. Perturbation magnitudes remain moderate, with average SNRs of 20.65–22.45 dB under ρ=0.9 and ζ=0.25. On HarmBench (200 instructions), TAGO (ζ=0.25) achieves ASR_l of 70.0%, 62.5%, and 70.0% on the three ALMs, confirming generalizability beyond AdvBench-50.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TAGO substantially outperforms both prior audio jailbreak baselines on the stricter LLM-judge metric (ASR_l). Against SpeechGuard, TAGO (ζ=1.0) improves ASR_l by 45 points on Qwen3-Omni (87% vs. 42%), 16 points on Qwen2.5-Omni (65% vs. 49%), and 37 points on LLaMA-Omni (71% vs. 34%). Against AdvWave, the gains are even larger on the more safety-aligned models: 42 points on Qwen3-Omni (87% vs. 45%) and 61 points on Qwen2.5-Omni (65% vs. 4%), while on LLaMA-Omni TAGO edges out AdvWave by 3 points (71% vs. 68%). On the looser refusal-prefix metric (ASR_r), TAGO matches or exceeds both baselines across all models, with the largest gap being 64 points over AdvWave on Qwen2.5-Omni (100% vs. 36%). The post-hoc prune baseline (dense optimization followed by gradient-based pruning) collapses to 1% ASR_l on Qwen3-Omni, confirming that sparsity must be enforced during optimization rather than applied after convergence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the audio jailbreak optimization paradigm established by SpeechGuard (Peri et al., 2024) and AdvWave (Kang et al., 2024), both of which perform dense waveform updates at every iteration. The key departure is the introduction of token-aligned gradient analysis to identify that gradient energy is highly concentrated on a small subset of audio tokens, motivating a sparse, token-selective update rule (TAGO) that masks low-energy regions. Conceptually, the approach draws on sparsity observations from attention compression (H2O, StreamingLLM) and audio redundancy (Audio-MAE), but applies them to the optimization trajectory of jailbreak attacks rather than inference or representation learning. The prefix-constrained teacher-forcing objective and EOS-suppression term are inherited from prior audio jailbreak work, while the model-compatible prefix construction is a practical adaptation to handle heterogeneous response styles across ALMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that dense waveform updates are largely redundant—retaining only 25% of token-aligned regions preserves nearly all attack effectiveness—challenges a default assumption in audio adversarial optimization and suggests that future attack and defense research should exploit token-level gradient heterogeneity. The failure of post-hoc sparsification (pruning after convergence) is a useful negative result showing that sparsity must be enforced during optimization to reshape the trajectory. However, the white-box requirement limits immediate practical impact, and extending the token-aware mechanism to black-box or transfer-based settings remains an open question. The gradient structure insight could also inform more targeted safety alignment strategies that focus on the small set of audio tokens most susceptible to adversarial manipulation.
