## Problem

Multimodal LLMs produce shallow, hallucinatory, or visually misaligned explanations when used for deepfake detection, and existing reinforcement learning methods reward only final outputs while neglecting the quality of intermediate reasoning. This gap is compounded by the absence of large-scale reasoning-annotated datasets for deepfake detection, making it difficult to train models that systematically inspect visual artifacts before reaching a classification decision.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB face image resized to 320×320 pixels as input. It produces a binary real/fake label accompanied by a structured reasoning explanation organized into at most M semantically coherent paragraphs (e.g., skin texture anomalies, lighting inconsistencies, background distortions), each describing a specific category of forensic cues observed in the image, followed by a final answer sentence. The paragraph-level structure is what enables the reward mechanism to operate at a granularity finer than the full response but coarser than individual tokens.

## Method

The approach combines three components: (1) DF-R5, a 115k-image reasoning-annotated dataset generated via a three-step pipeline that discovers 74 forensic features from multiple MLLMs, scores each feature per image using Gemini, and groups scored features into ≤M coherent reasoning paragraphs; (2) DX-LLaVA, which replaces LLaVA's CLIP ViT encoder with CLIP ConvNeXT to obtain pixel-level embeddings (10×10 feature map from Stage 3, flattened to 100 tokens) and adds a lightweight binary classifier on the projector, trained with a combined language-modeling and binary-classification loss; and (3) PRPO, a test-time RL algorithm that computes policy advantages at the paragraph level rather than the token level, using two label-free rewards: a Visual Consistency Reward (cosine similarity between YAKE-extracted paragraph keywords and frozen CLIP ConvNeXT image embeddings) and a Prediction Consistency Reward (whether the final answer matches the majority vote of preceding paragraphs via dictionary-based term matching). The policy is optimized with PPO-style clipped objectives at the paragraph granularity plus KL regularization against a reference model, enabling self-improvement at inference time without additional annotated data.

## Results

On the inter-domain deepfake detection benchmark (DF-40, five generative domains), PRPO achieves an average F1 of 89.91%, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art SIDA (75.26%) by 14.65 percentage points. The hardest domain, SiT, sees the largest absolute gain: PRPO reaches 71.26% F1 versus SIDA's 46.53%. In reasoning quality evaluation by GPT-4o, PRPO scores 4.55/5.0 overall, surpassing Gemini-2.5 (4.20/5.0) and all other MLLM baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PRPO outperforms all 13 baselines on the primary inter-domain F1 metric. Against dedicated deepfake detectors, PRPO (89.91% avg F1) surpasses SIDA (75.26%), UnivCLIP (73.09%), FakeShield (68.92%), DE-FAKE (54.43%), and LLaVA (42.85%), with the largest margin on SiT where PRPO reaches 71.26% versus SIDA's 46.53%. Against general-purpose MLLMs, PRPO (89.91% F1) beats Gemini-2.5 (80.31%), GPT-4o (63.11%), Gemma-3-27B-IT (67.64%), Qwen2.5-VL-32B (54.18%), Pixtral-12B (44.38%), and LLaMA-4 Maverick (23.23%). In the test-time RL comparison on DDIM, PRPO (95.88% F1) edges out PPO-TTRL (93.95%) and GRPO-TTRL (92.09%), primarily through higher recall (93.14% vs. 88.76% and 85.33%) while maintaining strong precision (98.79%). In reasoning quality, PRPO (4.55/5.0) exceeds Gemini-2.5 (4.20), Gemma-3-27B-IT (3.76), and all other MLLMs, with the largest gap in Evidence Grounding and Image Alignment (4.56 vs. 4.16 for Gemini-2.5).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on LLaVA's multimodal architecture by replacing the CLIP ViT encoder with CLIP ConvNeXT and adding a binary classification head, creating DX-LLaVA for fine-grained forensic feature capture. The core algorithmic contribution, PRPO, is a direct modification of GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), shifting advantage computation from the token level to the paragraph level and introducing two label-free reward signals (visual consistency via CLIP embeddings and prediction consistency via majority voting). It also extends the test-time RL line of work (TTRL) by replacing majority-vote-on-final-answer rewards with paragraph-level rewards that enforce alignment between intermediate reasoning and visual evidence, a design not present in prior TTRL or GRPO formulations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: existing deepfake detectors are largely opaque, and MLLM-based detectors produce hallucinated or misaligned explanations. PRPO's test-time applicability (no retraining on annotated data) makes it a flexible plug-in for improving reasoning quality in deployed MLLMs, and the 115k-sample DF-R5 dataset fills a notable gap in reasoning-annotated deepfake data. However, the approach is limited to face-centric deepfakes, relies on distillation from a single MLLM (Gemini-2.5), and the unsupervised-over-supervised finding is validated on only two domains. Open questions include whether paragraph-level RL rewards generalize to non-face deepfakes, multi-MLLM distillation for robustness, and adaptation to broader vision-language reasoning tasks like VQA or visual entailment.
