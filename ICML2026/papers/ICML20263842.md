## Problem

Editing an object's intrinsic attributes (color, texture, material, shape) in an image while preserving its perceived identity remains largely unsolved. Existing diffusion-based editors rely on unsupervised priors that only preserve coarse identity, while subject-driven personalization methods lock all intrinsic attributes and disallow any meaningful variation. The fundamental data bottleneck is that paired images of the same object differing only in intrinsic attributes virtually do not exist, making direct supervised learning infeasible.

## Input / Output

At inference, the user provides a source image, a single key-value text prompt (e.g., "material: wood"), and an object mask (precise SAM segmentation for color/texture/material edits, or a coarse bounding box for shape edits where the target geometry is unknown). Internally, the pipeline crops the object as an identity reference with its background masked out, fills the object region in the source with gray to form a background image, and feeds these into a 512×1024 two-panel grid. The output is a full-resolution edited image in which only the prompted intrinsic attribute is altered while identity, all other attributes, and the surrounding scene remain unchanged.

## Method

Alterbute fine-tunes a 7B-parameter SDXL UNet latent diffusion model using a relaxed training objective: instead of requiring rare intrinsic-only pairs, it learns from image pairs that differ in both intrinsic and extrinsic attributes, then constrains inference to intrinsic-only edits by reusing the original background and mask. Identity conditioning is achieved through Visual Named Entities (VNEs)—fine-grained categories like "Porsche 911 Carrera"—extracted automatically by Gemini 2.0 Flash from OpenImages, yielding 69,744 clusters of 1.08M images where objects share identity but vary in attributes. The architecture arranges inputs in a 1×2 spatial grid (noisy target latent on the left, identity reference on the right) so that cross-image self-attention propagates identity features; the diffusion L2 loss is computed only on the left half. Training runs for 100K steps (batch 128, lr 1e-5, ~24 h on 128 v4 TPUs) with 10% prompt dropout and 10% reference dropout to enable single-attribute inference, and uses CFG scales of 7.5 (text) and 2.0 (image).

## Results

Alterbute is evaluated via preference-based user study (166 participants, 3,320 total ratings) and VLM-based evaluation (Gemini, GPT-4o, Claude 3.7 Sonnet) on 100 editing cases across 30 objects. In user study, Alterbute is preferred over all 7 baselines, with win rates ranging from 76.2% (vs. Diptych) to 89.3% (vs. FlowEdit). VLM evaluators show strong agreement with user preferences, with Gemini preferring Alterbute in 94.3% of comparisons against MimicBrush and 92.6% against FlowEdit. On conventional metrics (Table 4), Alterbute achieves the highest CLIP-T score (0.321) for target-attribute alignment while maintaining competitive identity preservation (DINO 0.815, CLIP-I 0.914).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Alterbute wins all 7 head-to-head comparisons across every evaluator (user, Gemini, GPT-4o, Claude), with no ties or losses. Against attribute-specific editors, the margins are largest: 85.0% user preference over MimicBrush (texture editing) and 79.7% over MaterialFusion (material transfer), with VLM judges showing even stronger margins (94.3% and 87.0% for Gemini). Among general-purpose editors, FlowEdit (89.3% user) and InstructPix2Pix (85.0% user) are the most clearly outperformed, while Diptych (76.2% user) and OmniGen (81.2% user) present the closest competition, likely because both use reference-image conditioning. The per-attribute breakdown (Table 6) shows consistent performance across all four attribute types, with shape edits yielding the highest win rates (88.2% user), suggesting baselines struggle most with geometry changes. All results are statistically significant (p < 0.05, binomial test; most p < 1e-10).

## Relation to Prior Work

Alterbute departs from two main lines of prior work: (1) general-purpose instruction-based editors (InstructPix2Pix, FlowEdit, OmniGen, UltraEdit) that rely on unsupervised diffusion priors and preserve only coarse identity, and (2) subject-driven personalization methods (DreamBooth, Textual Inversion) that lock identity too tightly to permit any intrinsic change. The grid-based conditioning mechanism echoes Diptych and ObjectMate, but the key conceptual departure is the relaxed training objective—learning from paired images with both intrinsic and extrinsic changes, then constraining inference to intrinsic-only edits—which sidesteps the infeasibility of collecting intrinsic-only pairs. The VNE identity definition, extracted automatically via Gemini from OpenImages, carves new ground by providing a scalable, human-aligned middle ground between coarse categories and instance-level identity that no prior editing method has formalized.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Alterbute is the first single unified model to handle all four intrinsic attributes (color, texture, material, shape) with identity preservation, which is practically important for product visualization, design iteration, and creative workflows. The VNE concept and the relaxed training strategy are transferable ideas that could inform other identity-preserving editing tasks. However, the evaluation benchmark is small (30 objects, 100 cases), reshaping rigid objects remains unreliable, and the method is limited to non-human objects. Open questions include whether VNE-based identity generalizes to long-tail or novel object categories, how to handle semantically conflicting multi-attribute edits more explicitly, and whether the approach scales to human subjects or video.
