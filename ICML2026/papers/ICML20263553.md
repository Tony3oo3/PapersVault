## Problem

Existing self-supervised pre-training methods in 3D medical imaging treat each patient as an isolated instance, learning representations through within-scan augmentation or masked reconstruction. This overlooks a fundamental property of human anatomy: spatial relationships between structures (e.g., thalamus medial to basal ganglia) remain stable across individuals and modalities, yet no prior method exploits this cross-instance, cross-modality topological consistency as a supervisory signal.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3D multi-modal MRI volumes (96×96×96 or 128×128×128) from multiple patients, with up to 8 registered modalities per subject (T1, T1CE, T2, FLAIR, DWI, ADC, MRA, PD). Pre-training produces token-level feature embeddings from a Swin-B encoder paired with a lightweight 6-layer CNN decoder; downstream, these representations are transferred to Swin UNETR or UNETR backbones to produce segmentation masks (Dice-scored) or class labels (ACC/AUC/F1-scored). A key design choice is that intra-instance correspondence uses identity mapping on positional indices (relying on pre-registration), while inter-instance correspondence is discovered unsupervised via mutual nearest neighbors, requiring no dense spatial alignment.

## Method

TACO introduces the IM-NRC (Instance-agnostic Multi-modal Neighborhood Ranking Consistency) assumption: under a correspondence between two scans, the neighborhood ranking structure of patch tokens must be preserved. The training objective combines three terms: a standard reconstruction loss (L_uni), an intra-instance cross-modal triplet loss (L_intra) that enforces local topology preservation within the same patient across modalities using identity positional correspondence, and an inter-instance triplet loss (L_inter) that uses Mutual Nearest Neighbor (MNN) matching to establish pseudo-correspondences between different patients, then applies triplet constraints only on the partially matched token subsets. The triplet construction defines positives as top-ω nearest neighbors in the source modality's feature space (mapped via correspondence) and negatives as randomly sampled non-neighborhood tokens, with a margin δ=0.3. This dual alignment ensures the model learns relative anatomical positioning rather than instance-specific appearance, yielding modality- and subject-invariant neighborhood structure in the embedding space.

## Results

On four 3D segmentation benchmarks (BraTS2023-PED, BraTS-MET, UPENN-GBM, ISLES22), TACO achieves the best overall average Dice of 77.47%, surpassing the strongest medical SSL baseline BrainMVP (76.36%) by 1.11 points. On two classification tasks (ADNI, ADHD-200), TACO reaches 81.86% ACC / 84.12% AUC / 78.7% F1 average, improving over the best baseline (BrainMVP) by +0.56%/+1.35%/+5.94% respectively. In the missing-modality segmentation setting on BraTS18, TACO obtains the best average Dice on Whole Tumor (89.55%) and Enhancing Tumor (64.17%), outperforming PUIR by 1.92% and 0.68% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TACO consistently outperforms all medical SSL baselines on the overall segmentation average: 77.47% vs. BrainMVP (76.36%), Swin UNETR-SSL (76.26%), S²DC (76.13%), and SwinMM (76.10%). On classification, TACO leads all baselines on every averaged metric, with the largest margin over BrainMVP on F1 (+5.94%) and AUC (+1.35%), while S²DC trails by a wider gap (−5.10% F1). In the missing-modality setting, TACO dominates on Whole Tumor (89.55% vs. PUIR's 87.63%) and Enhancing Tumor (64.17% vs. 63.49%), though it is marginally behind PUIR on Tumor Core (79.68% vs. 79.78%). Against from-scratch baselines, TACO gains 1.9–2.5 Dice points on segmentation and 1–2 points on classification, confirming that the topological pre-training signal transfers effectively. The OpenMind comparison (Appendix F) further shows TACO beating the best MAE baseline by 1.32 DSC on ISLES and 0.94 DSC on MSLS2017.

## Relation to Prior Work

TACO departs from the dominant paradigm in 3D medical SSL—where each patient is treated as an isolated instance (SwinMM, Swin UNETR, VoCo, M3AE)—by introducing cross-instance topological consistency as an explicit supervisory signal. It builds conceptually on geometric-consistency pre-training (GVSL, He et al. 2023) but replaces explicit affine/deformable transformation matrices with implicit neighborhood-ranking constraints, making it applicable to cross-modal and cross-subject settings where no closed-form mapping exists. The triplet-loss formulation and mutual-nearest-neighbor correspondence mechanism are repurposed from metric learning into a self-supervised pre-training objective, and the IM-NRC assumption formalizes a topological invariance previously exploited only in supervised topology-preserving losses (TopoLoss).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a genuine gap: population-level anatomical topology has been underutilized as a pre-training signal, and the demonstrated robustness to missing modalities (up to 3 of 4 MRI sequences absent) has direct clinical relevance where acquisition protocols vary across sites. Limitations include restriction to brain MRI with 8 modalities, a single A100 training setup, and evaluation on relatively small downstream cohorts (99–285 patients), leaving open whether the cross-instance topology signal scales to whole-body or non-brain anatomy. The authors note future work on PET-CT and broader anatomical structures, and the modest absolute gains (~1% Dice, ~6% F1) suggest the signal is complementary rather than transformative for already-strong SSL baselines.
