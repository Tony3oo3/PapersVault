## Problem

Modern generative model datasets contain samples of highly varying quality, and training diffusion models directly on such heterogeneous data yields suboptimal generation. Filtering out low-quality samples improves quality but sacrifices diversity, while inference-time guidance methods trade diversity for quality at test time. The paper asks whether a model trained on a noisy dataset can be used to iteratively improve that same dataset, creating a dataset-model co-evolution loop that avoids the catastrophic self-consuming loops observed in prior self-training work.

## Input / Output

The method takes a dataset where each sample is annotated with a noise level (threshold time t_i) indicating the minimum diffusion time at which the sample can be used for training. For images, corruptions like Gaussian blur or JPEG compression are mapped to equivalent additive Gaussian noise levels; for proteins, AlphaFold's pLDDT confidence scores are mapped to discrete noise levels via a piecewise function. The output is a refined dataset with reduced per-sample noise levels (t_i → t_i/2^l after l loops) and a trained diffusion model that generates images (512×512 for text-to-image, 32×32 for CIFAR) or protein backbone structures (up to 256 residues).

## Method

Ambient Dataloops iterates between two steps: (1) training a diffusion model on the current noisy dataset using the Ambient Diffusion objective, which handles noisy targets without requiring clean references by reweighting the denoising loss with α(t,t_i) = (σ²(t)−σ²(t_i))/σ²(t); and (2) partially denoising the dataset via posterior sampling, reducing each sample's noise level from t_i to t_i/2^l rather than fully denoising to t=0. This partial denoising is the key mechanism that prevents catastrophic self-consuming loops: by treating restored samples as still noisy (just at a lower level), the method accounts for the model's imperfect score estimation and avoids propagating learning errors across iterations. The theoretical analysis shows that if the learned score function is sufficiently accurate, the backward diffusion process contracts the KL divergence between the restored distribution and the true distribution exponentially (Lemma 4.2), and any contractive transformation of out-of-distribution samples strictly improves estimation error over using them unmodified (Lemma 4.1). The method initializes from Ambient Omni checkpoints, which already map arbitrary corruptions to equivalent additive Gaussian noise levels, and the denoising rate (the factor 2 in t_i/2^l) is a tunable hyperparameter that controls the trade-off between progress per loop and error accumulation.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with 90% corrupted data, a single loop of Ambient Dataloops reduces unconditional FID by up to 17% over Ambient Omni (e.g., 5.69→4.95 for blur σ_B=0.6, 5.84→4.83 for JPEG q=50). On COCO zero-shot text-to-image generation, one loop improves FID-30K from 10.61 (Ambient Omni) to 10.06 and CLIP-FD-30K from 9.40 to 8.83. For de novo protein backbone design, a single loop achieves a 14.3% increase in diversity for only a 0.2% decrease in designability, establishing a new Pareto frontier point.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Ambient Omni (Daras et al., 2025c), the direct predecessor used as Loop 0 initialization, Ambient Dataloops wins on all six CIFAR-10 corruption settings with FID reductions of 0.7–1.0 points, and on COCO where FID-30K drops from 10.61 to 10.06 and CLIP-FD-30K from 9.40 to 8.83. Against MicroDiffusion (Sehwag et al., 2025), the method achieves a substantially lower COCO FID (10.06 vs. 12.37) and CLIP-FD (8.83 vs. 10.07), while matching its GenEval overall score of 0.47. Against SIMS (Alemohammad et al., 2024b), an inference-time guidance baseline, Ambient Dataloops achieves a dramatically lower COCO FID (8.83 vs. 13.03–13.23 across guidance strengths) while maintaining comparable CLIP-IQA quality (0.4171). Against the ImageNet base model of Degeorge et al. (2025), finetuning on the dataloop-refined dataset improves 3 of 4 reward metrics (PickScore 20.07→20.13, HPSv2 0.22→0.24, ImageReward −0.37→−0.19). Against Ambient Proteins (Daras et al., 2025b) on protein design, one loop shifts the operating point along the Pareto frontier, gaining 14.3% diversity at a negligible 0.2% designability cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Ambient Diffusion line of work (Daras et al., 2023; 2025c) from a single-pass corruption-aware training procedure to an iterative dataset-model co-evolution loop. The key departure from prior EM-based approaches (Bai et al., 2024; Rozet et al., 2024; Hosseintabar et al., 2025) is that Ambient Dataloops deliberately performs only partial denoising (to t/2^l rather than t=0) at each iteration, treating the restored samples as still-noisy data and training with the Ambient loss rather than standard score matching. This design choice, combined with the gradual reduction in noise level, is what distinguishes the method from the catastrophic self-consuming loops documented in prior work on training with synthetic data (Alemohammad et al., 2024a; Shumailov et al., 2024). The theoretical analysis builds on the KL-contraction properties of diffusion processes and the bias-variance tradeoff framework from Ambient Omni to formalize when the iterative refinement step is beneficial.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical bottleneck: when data availability (not compute) limits model quality, iterative refinement can extract more signal from a fixed dataset without sacrificing diversity, unlike filtering-based approaches. The cross-domain validation (CIFAR-10, text-to-image on tens of millions of samples, and de novo protein design) suggests the technique is broadly applicable to any setting where sample quality varies and acquiring new data is expensive. Key open questions include: the optimal number of loops and denoising rate remain heuristic (the 'madness regime' boundary is dataset-dependent), the method requires additional compute per loop that may not be justified when fresh data is available, and the protein results are preliminary pending wet-lab validation. Whether the co-evolution framework transfers to supervised downstream tasks or to settings with non-Gaussian corruption structures remains unexplored.
