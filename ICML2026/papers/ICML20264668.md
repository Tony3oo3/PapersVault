## Problem

Generative super-resolution models built on diffusion or flow-matching priors compromise faithful restoration because their isotropic (spectrally flat) Gaussian noise objectives treat all frequencies equally, failing to distinguish authentic high-frequency details from hallucinated artifacts. While Direct Preference Optimization offers a path to alignment, its standard Euclidean geometry inherits this spectral indifference, causing the learned distribution to diverge from natural image statistics in the high-frequency region and producing structurally inconsistent textures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264668/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 256×256 low-quality RGB image as input and produces a 1024×1024 super-resolved image (×4 upscaling). During training, the model is conditioned solely on the LQ image with empty text prompts, forcing reliance on image-conditional cues. The framework operates in the latent space of FLUX.1-dev (fine-tuned via LoRA with r=16, α=16), and inference uses a 28-step sampling schedule with BF16 mixed precision, achieving ~19.6 s/image on a single A800 GPU with 33 GB peak VRAM.

## Method

ASASR recasts the Flow Matching generative process within a Sobolev-induced Riemannian geometry by replacing the isotropic noise covariance with a structured spectral operator Σ_s = F^{-1} diag((1+‖ω‖²)^{-s}) F (implemented via DCT-II for non-periodic boundaries), which transforms the DPO log-likelihood ratio from an L² difference into a difference of Sobolev H^s norms (s=1.5), imposing strictly higher penalties on high-frequency discrepancies. To supply informative training signals, an Adversarial Manifold Guidance (AMG) network A_φ is trained to exploit 'Misaligned Confidence'—states where the model assigns high likelihood to structurally degraded samples—by minimizing Euclidean residual energy subject to a Sobolev trust region, yielding worst-case Sobolev gradients as targeted negatives via the Riesz Representation Theorem. The resulting AS-DPO objective couples these adversarially generated, semantically aligned negatives (sharing the same noise realization as the winner trajectory) with the Sobolev energy gap, driving optimization along the tangent space of plausible structural failures while preserving spectral fidelity to natural image statistics.

## Results

On DIV2K-Val, ASASR achieves the best score across all seven metrics: PSNR 20.60, SSIM 0.6171, LPIPS 0.2784, DISTS 0.2038, MANIQA 0.6519, MUSIQ 71.40, and CLIPIQA+ 0.7521. On the real-world RealSR benchmark, ASASR leads in PSNR (24.09), LPIPS (0.2497), DISTS (0.1986), MUSIQ (71.78), and CLIPIQA+ (0.7200). On LSDIR-Val, ASASR attains the best LPIPS (0.2854), DISTS (0.1757), MUSIQ (73.97), and CLIPIQA+ (0.7761). On DRealSR, ASASR achieves the best LPIPS (0.2874), DISTS (0.2023), MANIQA (0.6067), MUSIQ (68.36), and CLIPIQA+ (0.6803), though it trails Real-ESRGAN on PSNR (25.77 vs. 26.28) and SSIM (0.7356 vs. 0.7771).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ASASR dominates the GAN-based baselines (BSRGAN, Real-ESRGAN, SwinIR-GAN) across nearly all metrics on all four datasets, with the most notable gaps in perceptual scores (e.g., LPIPS 0.2784 vs. 0.3147 for Real-ESRGAN on DIV2K-Val) and no-reference quality (MANIQA 0.6519 vs. 0.3726). Among diffusion-based methods, ASASR outperforms StableSR, DiffBIR, and FaithDiff on virtually every metric, with particularly large margins on DISTS and CLIPIQA+. Against the strongest recent competitors—SeeSR, SUPSR, DP2O-SR, and DiT4SR—ASASR wins on most metrics but concedes a few narrow losses: SeeSR edges out on PSNR for LSDIR-Val (16.91 vs. 16.83), SUPSR slightly leads on MANIQA for LSDIR-Val (0.6392 vs. 0.6372), DP2O-SR is marginally ahead on MANIQA for RealSR (0.5849 vs. 0.5730), and Real-ESRGAN retains higher PSNR/SSIM on DRealSR (26.28/0.7771 vs. 25.77/0.7356). On the downstream task evaluations (Table 2), ASASR achieves the best OCR Recall (45.91), Object Detection APb (35.62), Instance Segmentation APm (30.98), and Semantic Segmentation mIoU (43.33), surpassing all baselines including DP2O-SR and DiT4SR.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Flow Matching + DPO paradigm for super-resolution by identifying a fundamental geometric mismatch: standard DPO's isotropic Gaussian noise imposes a spectrally flat L2 metric that is ill-suited for SR's high-frequency fidelity requirements, unlike its acceptable role in text-to-image synthesis. The key departure is Sobolev Spectral Rectification, which replaces the identity covariance with a structured operator Σ_s to reshape the optimization into H^s geometry, and Adversarial Manifold Guidance, which synthesizes targeted negatives by exploiting the model's 'Misaligned Confidence' (high likelihood assigned to structurally degraded samples). This positions ASASR as a theoretically grounded alternative to heuristic DPO variants like DP2O-SR, which relies on aggregated IQA metrics without connecting the proxy objective to the intrinsic spectral structure of the natural image manifold.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, non-heuristic mechanism for frequency-aware preference alignment in SR, with the Sobolev geometry offering a clear inductive bias that isotropic objectives lack. The consistent dominance across distortion, perceptual, and downstream semantic metrics (OCR, detection, segmentation) suggests that spectral alignment genuinely preserves structural information rather than merely improving perceptual scores. Limitations include the empirically chosen Sobolev exponent s=1.5 (no learned or adaptive mechanism), dependence on a large FLUX backbone for competitive results, and the adversary training requiring inference from multiple baselines. An open question is whether this spectral-geometric alignment principle generalizes beyond SR to other ill-posed inverse problems (denoising, inpainting) where high-frequency fidelity is similarly critical.
