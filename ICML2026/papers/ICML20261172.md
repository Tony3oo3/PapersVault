## Problem

Existing multimodal embedding models excel at global image-text alignment but struggle with fine-grained region-level alignment between specific objects and descriptive phrases. Prior approaches either lack explicit modeling of bounding box quality (e.g., FG-CLIP) or suffer from limited generalization due to constrained training data (open-vocabulary detectors), leaving a gap in producing object-centric representations that jointly encode semantic meaning and localization confidence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261172/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image resized to 900–1200 visual tokens (900×32×32 to 1200×32×32 pixels) along with text queries or image exemplars, and produces per-object semantic embeddings, per-object IoU embeddings, two global image embeddings (one for short captions, one for long captions), and text embeddings—all in a single forward pass. Each object consumes only 8 tokens, keeping total sequence length under 2000. The final object matching score is computed as the product of cosine semantic similarity and a predicted IoU score, enabling the model to simultaneously rank by relevance and localization quality.

## Method

ObjEmbed fine-tunes Qwen3-VL-Instruct with five special tokens (<object>, <iou>, <global>, <local_text>, <global_text>) whose final-layer hidden states serve as embeddings. Objects are extracted via the WeDetect-Uni proposal generator (100 proposals), compressed through RoIAlign and a learned object projector into single tokens, and organized sequentially with per-object prompts. The key architectural insight is decoupling each object into two tokens—one for semantic classification and one for IoU regression—to avoid optimization conflicts between localization and classification objectives. Training combines three losses: region-level sigmoid focal loss (many-to-one matching between proposals and object descriptions), image-level sigmoid focal loss (one-to-one image-caption matching with two separate global tokens for short and long captions), and IoU regression via focal loss on positive proposals. This design enables a single forward pass to encode all objects plus the full image, supporting object detection, referring expression comprehension, local image retrieval, and global image retrieval within one unified framework.

## Results

On object detection, ObjEmbed-4B achieves 53.0% mAP on COCO, 58.0 on COCO-OAP, and 50.8 on ODinW13, while substantially outperforming all open-vocabulary and MLLM baselines on fine-grained benchmarks (76.0 Full on FG-OVD, 42.2 Abs on D3). On referring expression comprehension, ObjEmbed-4B attains 89.5 average accuracy across RefCOCO/+/g, surpassing even the 38B-parameter InternVL3.5. Most notably, on local image retrieval, ObjEmbed-4B achieves 68.5 average (71.7 SORCE-1K T2I, 39.3 REIRCOCO, 77.6 ILIAS T2I, 85.3 ILIAS I2I), outperforming the next-best global embedding model by approximately 20 points. On global image retrieval, ObjEmbed-4B reaches 81.7 average Recall@1 across six benchmarks, competitive with the strongest CLIP-style and MLLM-based embedding models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On COCO object detection, ObjEmbed-4B (53.0 mAP) surpasses WeDetect-Ref-4B (50.0), Grounding-DINO-T (47.9), GLIP-T (46.1), and OWLv2 (37.9), but remains slightly below the specialist LLMDet-T (54.9) and DINO-R50 (51.2). On fine-grained open-vocabulary detection (FG-OVD), ObjEmbed-4B achieves 76.0 Full, a substantial margin over OWLv2 (63.2), Grounding-DINO-T (62.5), and LLMDet-T (55.4), and also leads WeDetect-Ref-4B (68.4) by 7.6 points. On D3 language-based detection, ObjEmbed-4B (40.2/42.2 Pres/Abs) outperforms all open-vocabulary detectors (best: GLIP-T at 19.1/21.5) and the base Qwen3-VL-4B (24.3/19.7). On referring expression comprehension, ObjEmbed-4B (89.5 avg) edges out ChatRex 7B (89.1) and InternVL3.5-38B (89.1) while using far fewer parameters, and significantly exceeds VLM-FO1 3B (88.6) and all other MLLM baselines. On local image retrieval, ObjEmbed-4B (68.5 avg) dominates all global embedding models by roughly 20 points over FG-CLIP2 (49.1) and Qwen3-VL-Embedding-8B (48.0), with the largest gap on SORCE-1K (71.7 vs. 49.1). On global image retrieval, ObjEmbed-4B (81.7 avg) is competitive with Qwen3-VL-Embedding-2B (81.5) and FG-CLIP2 (80.8) despite training on only 1.3M samples, and outperforms GME-7B (77.2), UME-R1-2B (77.3), and all CLIP-style baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

ObjEmbed sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) global image-text contrastive models (CLIP, SigLIP, FG-CLIP) that produce holistic embeddings but lack fine-grained object-level discrimination; (2) open-vocabulary object detectors (GLIP, Grounding DINO, OWLv2) that align text with regions but require query-specific inference and cannot produce query-agnostic embeddings; and (3) recent MLLM-based embedding models (GME, VLM2Vec, UME-R1) that handle instruction-following retrieval but still operate at the image level. The paper departs from all three by building on Qwen3-VL as a backbone and WeDetect-Uni as a proposal generator, introducing a dual-token (object + IoU) design that produces query-agnostic, localization-aware object embeddings in a single forward pass—something neither global embedders nor open-vocab detectors achieve.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified framework offers a practical drop-in replacement for both global embedding pipelines and open-vocabulary detectors, with a compelling efficiency story (6.9 fps vs. 0.4 fps for autoregressive MLLM decoding on REC). However, performance is fundamentally bounded by the recall of the external proposal generator (the oracle experiment shows a 12.2% AP ceiling on COCO), and the 1.3M-sample training set is an order of magnitude smaller than CLIP-scale data, leaving open whether the approach scales. The absence of hard negative mining and the reliance on a frozen vision encoder are acknowledged limitations that likely cap further gains.
