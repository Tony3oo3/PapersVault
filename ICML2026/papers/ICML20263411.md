## Problem

Zero-shot detection of LLM-generated text remains difficult because existing detectors (Perplexity, Binoculars, DetectLLM LRR) measure generic statistical properties without targeting a specific, interpretable mechanism that distinguishes LLM text from human writing. This paper hypothesizes that LLMs develop a persistent 'Vestigial Heuristic'—an extreme aversion to token repetition learned during early bigram-model training—that creates a detectable signature in LLM-generated text but is absent in human writing.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text passage (filtered to 100–5000 words) and a reference language model (any of 12 models from 135M to 9B parameters) as input. It produces a single real-valued score—the average negative log probability of each token conditioned on the sequence up to and including that token—which is thresholded for binary LLM-vs-human classification. Unlike standard Perplexity, the score is not exponentiated (monotonic transformation preserves ranking), and the conditioning includes the token itself rather than only preceding tokens.

## Method

Telescope Perplexity is defined as −(1/L) Σ log M(s_i | s_1:i), where the reference model M assigns probability to token s_i given the sequence s_1 through s_i (including s_i itself), in contrast to standard Perplexity's next-token prediction M(s_i | s_1:i−1). This self-conditioning specifically probes the model's learned likelihood of repeating the immediately preceding token, directly measuring the hypothesized Vestigial Heuristic. The paper demonstrates that this signature emerges within the first ~100K training steps of Pythia and Amber-7B checkpoints, stabilizes thereafter, and remains effective even with only bigram context (0.897 AUC vs. 0.761 for standard Perplexity), confirming its local, early-training origin. The method is entirely training-free: a single forward pass with any reference model suffices, making it computationally comparable to standard Perplexity while achieving state-of-the-art or competitive AUROC across 15 datasets including novel sets with GPT4o Mini and Deepseek-V3 targets.

## Results

Telescope Perplexity achieves the best average AUROC across 12 reference models on 8 of 16 evaluation datasets, with standout performance on Detect LLM Text (0.99219 AUROC vs. 0.89307 for standard Perplexity and 0.76588 for Binoculars) and ESL GPT4o Mini (0.99983 AUROC, a >3-order-of-magnitude error-rate reduction over Perplexity's 0.82523). On the novel GPT4o Mini and Deepseek-V3 datasets, Telescope Perplexity consistently outperforms all baselines by 5–20 AUROC points. The method's main losses are on formulaic essay/news datasets (e.g., GB Essay Deepseek V3: 0.98484 vs. Perplexity's 0.99881) and HC3 (0.99155 vs. Perplexity's 0.99471), where the repetition-aversion signal is weaker.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Binoculars (the prior state-of-the-art), Telescope Perplexity wins decisively on 10 of 16 datasets, with the largest gaps on Detect LLM Text (+0.226 AUROC), ESL GPT4o Mini (+0.203), and GB Essay Claude (+0.169); it loses narrowly on GB News Claude (−0.012), GB Essay Deepseek V3 (−0.007), and GB Creative Deepseek V3 (−0.014). Against standard Perplexity, Telescope wins on 9 datasets (notably +0.099 on Detect LLM Text and +0.175 on ESL GPT4o Mini) but loses on 7, primarily formulaic essay and news datasets where the repetition-aversion signal is suppressed (e.g., GB Essay Deepseek V3: −0.014, GB Essay GPT4o: −0.012). Against DetectLLM LRR, Telescope wins on 10 datasets but trails on news and essay splits (GB News ChatGPT: −0.086, GB Essay Deepseek V3: −0.012). Against Fast-DetectGPT, Telescope Perplexity wins on all 16 datasets, with margins ranging from +0.048 to +0.478 AUROC, confirming that the repetition-probability signal is substantially more discriminative than conditional probability curvature.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground within the zero-shot LLM text detection literature by introducing Telescope Perplexity, which measures P(s_i | s_1:i) — the probability of the current token given context that includes itself — rather than the standard next-token prediction P(s_i | s_1:i-1) used by Perplexity, Binoculars, and DetectLLM. The conceptual departure is framed through the 'Vestigial Heuristic' hypothesis, drawing on Belrose et al. (2024) and Choshen et al. (2022) findings that early LLM training produces bigram-level statistical biases that persist throughout pre-training. The paper positions itself against the current SOTA (Binoculars, Hans et al. 2024) and other zero-shot baselines (DetectLLM LRR, Fast-DetectGPT), while also introducing novel evaluation datasets using contemporary target models (GPT4o Mini, Deepseek-V3) to address the staleness of existing benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'Vestigial Heuristic' framing provides a mechanistic, interpretable explanation for why a simple token-repetition statistic separates LLM from human text, and the demonstration that this signature emerges within the first 100K training steps and persists across architectures (Pythia, Amber, SmolLM) suggests it is a robust property of current deep learning approaches to language modeling rather than an artifact of specific models. However, the paper identifies clear limitations: performance degrades on stylized genres (poetry, formulaic news), and the signal can be suppressed by rigid lexical constraints that force repetition. Open questions include whether the heuristic can be explicitly unlearned during training, whether it generalizes to non-English languages, and whether targeted adversarial strategies (e.g., inserting deliberate repetitions) could neutralize the signal. The new datasets with modern LLMs are a practical contribution that the field will likely adopt for future benchmarking.
