## Problem

Existing State Space Duality (SSD) formulations enforce commutativity via scalar-identity constraints on the state transition matrix, restricting the system to SISO dynamics and preventing cross-dimensional interactions within the recurrence. This forces a two-stage design that decouples temporal modeling from channel mixing, which becomes a structural limitation when cross-channel coupling is time-varying and entangled with temporal dynamics. The paper asks whether SSD can be generalized to the MIMO setting while preserving both the algebraic duality and linear-time computation.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a sequence of L vectors, each of dimension D (e.g., 10-step sound speed profiles across 80 depth layers, or multivariate time-series windows from UEA SCP1), and produces either a predicted next-timestep profile (many-to-one forecasting) or a class label (classification). Unlike SISO SSMs that project each timestep to a scalar before the recurrence, MIMOMamba operates on the full D-dimensional vector at every step, with all state matrices (A_t, B_t, C_t) being D×D matrices that perform dense cross-channel mixing within the recurrence itself.

## Method

MIMOMamba parameterizes all time-varying state matrices (A_t, B_t, C_t) as polynomials of a single shared base matrix A per head, i.e., A_t = Σ c_k A^k. This guarantees mutual commutativity (required for SSD factorization) while the Centralizer Theorem ensures the parameterization is theoretically complete within the commutative subspace when A is non-derogatory (satisfied with probability 1 under random initialization). The architecture instantiates H parallel heads via a direct sum of algebras, each governed by its own base matrix, yielding a matrix-valued dual attention form where Queries, Keys, and the causal mask are all D×D block matrices rather than scalars. Because all three dynamic matrices are generated from one shared D×D base plus low-dimensional scalar coefficients, the core dynamics cost ~D² parameters versus ~3D² for independent dense matrices, reducing total parameter count to roughly one-third of a comparable Transformer while retaining linear-time training via the preserved block-semiseparable structure.

## Results

On the Internal Wave Sound Speed Profile (SSP) forecasting benchmark, MIMOMamba achieves the best RMSE_mean of 0.688 m/s and R² of 0.481 using only 35,593 parameters — approximately 65% fewer than the Transformer (100,801 params, RMSE_mean 0.748, R² 0.467) and 30% fewer than the LSTM (50,497 params, RMSE_mean 0.999, R² 0.434). On the UEA SCP1 multivariate time-series classification task, MIMOMamba attains 85.5 ± 2.9% accuracy over five seeds, outperforming all SISO SSM baselines. In extended SSP comparisons against four recent SSM variants, MIMOMamba achieves the lowest RMSE_mean of 0.687, ahead of Gated DeltaNet (0.699), Mamba-3 (0.715), Mamba-2 (0.717), and PD-SSM (0.774).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MIMOMamba outperforms the Transformer on all three SSP metrics (RMSE_mean 0.688 vs. 0.748, RMSE_max 1.662 vs. 1.926, R² 0.481 vs. 0.467) while using only ~35% of its parameters, and also reduces worst-case thermocline error by 14% over the Transformer. Against the LSTM, MIMOMamba achieves substantially lower error (RMSE_mean 0.688 vs. 0.999) with fewer parameters. In the extended SSM comparison on SSP, MIMOMamba (RMSE_mean 0.687) beats Gated DeltaNet (0.699) by a narrow margin, Mamba-3 (0.715) and Mamba-2 (0.717) by ~0.03, and PD-SSM (0.774) by a wider 0.087 gap. On the UEA SCP1 classification benchmark, MIMOMamba (85.5 ± 2.9%) outperforms all SISO baselines including Mamba, S6 LRU, NRDE, and NCDE, and narrows the gap to continuous-time models such as LinOSS and S5. Wall-clock profiling confirms MIMOMamba-Fast is 1.5–1.6× faster than Mamba-2 in training throughput under matched pure-PyTorch implementations, and exhibits sub-quadratic memory growth with model width (~1.7× for 4× width increase vs. ~3.9× for Mamba-2).

## Relation to Prior Work

MIMOMamba sits at the confluence of two axes in the SSM literature: the SISO-to-MIMO generalization (initiated by S5) and the time-invariant-to-selective transition (initiated by Mamba). It departs from Mamba-2's State Space Duality framework, which enforces commutativity via a scalar-identity constraint (A_t = a_t I), by introducing a matrix polynomial parameterization that guarantees commutativity while allowing full D×D cross-channel dynamics within the recurrence. This is conceptually distinct from Mamba-3, which also adopts MIMO but for hardware arithmetic-intensity reasons and via complex-valued dynamics equivalent to RoPE; MIMOMamba's motivation is purely algebraic, restoring the duality in the MIMO regime through a principled shared-base-matrix construction whose completeness is certified by the Centralizer Theorem for non-derogatory matrices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean algebraic recipe (polynomial ring over a shared base matrix) that simultaneously ensures commutativity for duality, reduces parameter count by roughly a factor of three relative to independent per-step matrices, and preserves block-semiseparable structure compatible with parallel scan. However, empirical validation is confined to small-scale benchmarks (80-layer SSP forecasting, SCP1 classification) with modest margins over strong baselines, and no custom CUDA kernels or large-scale language-modeling experiments are provided. Open questions include whether the commutativity constraint—even relaxed via multi-head direct sums—becomes a genuine expressivity bottleneck at scale, whether the FFT-based polynomial multiplication yields practical speedups beyond degree p≈4, and whether the claimed PE-free design holds on long-context NLP tasks where positional structure is more nuanced.
