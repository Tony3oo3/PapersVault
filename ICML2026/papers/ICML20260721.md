## Problem

Circuit analysis in mechanistic interpretability is typically target-conditioned, explaining a single prompt paired with a hand-chosen completion, which obscures heterogeneity across a model's full continuation distribution. Semantically similar outputs can rely on distinct internal mechanisms, and mechanistically similar patterns can support different semantics, so single-target analysis fails to capture the computational diversity of a prompt's output distribution. This paper addresses the gap by introducing distribution-level unsupervised feature discovery that clusters continuations jointly in semantic and mechanistic spaces without manually specifying target outputs.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prefix and N=500 nucleus-sampled continuations (temperature 1.0, top-p 0.9, max 20 tokens), representing each with an L2-normalized semantic embedding from EmbeddingGemma and an RMS-normalized mechanistic embedding computed as the mean prefix-to-continuation attribution over transcoder sparse features indexed by (position, layer, feature) tuples. It outputs cluster assignments with probability-weighted centers in both views, plus medoid-based steering directions used for causal validation. The rate-distortion parameters β (granularity, annealed to reveal hierarchical structure) and γ (view balance) control the trade-off, and continuations are weighted by their length-normalized log-probability throughout the objective.

## Method

The core contribution is a rate-distortion clustering objective L_RD = H(C) + β_e·D^(e) + β_a·D^(a) that jointly constrains semantic coherence (L2 distortion) and mechanistic consistency (L1 distortion) while penalizing cluster complexity via the entropy of the cluster prior. Optimization proceeds via alternating minimization: an E-step assigns continuations using a combined distance with an entropic prior favoring larger clusters, and an M-step updates centers via probability-weighted means (semantic) and coordinate-wise weighted medians (attribution, robust to sparse-vector outliers). Adaptive Split and Junk operations dynamically adjust K—Split uses PCA-based initialization in the β-scaled joint representation to divide heterogeneous clusters when distortion reduction outweighs the binary-entropy rate cost, while Junk removes spurious clusters whose removal yields a larger rate reduction than the distortion increase. Unlike fixed-K methods, the number of clusters emerges from optimization, and annealing β reveals a hierarchical structure where finer clusters inherit mechanistic signatures from coarser parents. Causal validation is performed by steering along cluster medoid attribution vectors (top-B features by magnitude) and measuring induced changes in cluster-centered demeaned logits, showing that RD medoid directions produce stronger, more consistent monotonic responses than semantic-only or single-continuation baselines.

## Results

On AmbigQA (300 questions, 500 continuations each, Qwen3-8B), RD-clustering achieves the lowest mixed distortion D_γ in 94% of (β, γ) configurations compared to rate-matched K-means baselines. At the balanced setting γ=0.5, β=1.0, RD achieves D_γ=4.66 versus 21.10 for semantic-only K-means (KM-S) and 14.83 for attribution-only K-means (KM-A). In causal steering validation, RD medoid directions yield Spearman correlations of 0.20–0.35 between steering strength ε and cluster logit change ΔL_k, with consistent monotonic responses (e.g., ΔL=+0.63 at ε=+1.0, −1.80 at ε=−1.0 for β=1.0, γ=0.5), while KM-Sem correlations are near zero (−0.13 to 0.05) and the Single-continuation baseline is weaker (0.10–0.22).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares RD-clustering against two single-view K-means baselines (KM-S on semantic embeddings, KM-A on attribution vectors) under rate-matched conditions (same assignment entropy H). RD dominates both baselines on the mixed distortion D_γ in 94% of settings, with the largest margins at intermediate γ (0.3–0.7) where both views contribute: at γ=0.5, β=1.0, RD achieves D_γ=4.66 vs. 21.10 (KM-S) and 14.83 (KM-A). KM-S preserves semantic coherence but incurs 3–5× higher mechanistic distortion, while KM-A reduces mechanistic distortion at the cost of 3–4× higher semantic distortion—confirming the complementary failure modes that motivate joint clustering. In the causal steering validation (Table 2), RD medoid directions outperform both the KM-Sem baseline (semantic-only K-means medoids, near-zero correlation with steering effect) and the Single baseline (random continuation from the same RD cluster, weaker but positive correlation), demonstrating that cluster-level mechanistic directions are causally actionable in a way that neither semantic similarity nor single-target attribution captures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends circuit analysis from its standard single-target formulation to a distribution-level setting, clustering all sampled continuations of a prompt rather than explaining one hand-chosen completion. It repurposes transcoder-based sparse feature decomposition (Dunefsky et al., 2024) and linear attribution patching (Syed et al., 2024; Lindsey et al., 2025) as a mechanistic embedding view, and combines it with a semantic embedding view under a rate-distortion objective (Rose, 1998; Higgins et al., 2017) that lets cluster count and view balance emerge from optimization. Conceptually, it moves the clustering-by-causal-feature-similarity idea of Marks et al. (2025) from the dataset level (recurring circuits across many prompts) to the distribution level (heterogeneous mechanisms within a single prompt's continuation distribution), and it departs from prior work by treating semantic and mechanistic views as jointly constraining rather than using one as a proxy for the other.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a scalable, target-free audit of a model's continuation distribution that complements (rather than replaces) circuit analysis: practitioners can first discover continuation modes, then trace circuits for a selected cluster. The key open question is whether the discovered cluster-level mechanistic directions are unique or merely one of many valid causal decompositions, since validation relies on first-order linear attributions and a single steering protocol. Computational overhead (500 continuations × transcoder forward/backward passes per prompt) and the dependence on pretrained transcoders and embedding models limit immediate applicability to very large models or safety-critical deployments where attribution quality is uncertain.
