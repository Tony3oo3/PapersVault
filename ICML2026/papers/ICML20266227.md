## Problem

Reinforcement learning for LLMs refines reasoning only within the model's existing knowledge space, while supervised fine-tuning can inject new knowledge but suffers from overfitting and poor out-of-distribution generalization. Existing methods that combine SFT and RL rely on fixed schedules, heuristic thresholds, or single-indicator difficulty signals, failing to adaptively match each sample to the most suitable learning strategy as the model's capabilities evolve during training.

## Input / Output

The method consumes training prompts paired with reference answers (e.g., from OpenR1-Math-220k with DeepSeek-R1-generated demonstrations, or Commonsense170k) and the current LLM policy. At each training step, it extracts six per-sample training characteristics—Top-1/Top-K margins, perplexity, entropy, reward mean, logits norm, and sequence length—aggregated to the sentence level, and feeds them into a compact two-layer MLP (256→128 hidden units) that outputs a three-way probability over SFT, RL, and DISCARD. The final output is a trained LLM whose per-sample strategy assignments co-evolve with the model throughout training.

## Method

DALSA formulates a unified max-min objective where the LLM parameters θ are maximized and the strategy allocator parameters Ω are minimized in alternating updates. The allocator maps concatenated training characteristics to a discrete strategy via argmax, with the Straight-Through Estimator enabling gradient flow through the non-differentiable selection. Two targeted regularizations address the inherent weaknesses of each strategy: an anti-curriculum weighting scheme for RL that progressively up-weights harder samples (with λ(t) growing exponentially over training, proven to yield O(1/√K) convergence with a tighter bound than unweighted or easy-biased weighting), and an adversarial label smoothing (ALS) loss for SFT where perturbations are derived adaptively from the model's own predictive distribution (δ_j ∝ p_j^{−τ}) rather than a fixed uniform, amplifying gradient updates for low-confidence tokens. The SFT assignment probability s^SFT itself serves as a continuous difficulty score, eliminating the need for manual thresholds.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-Math-7B, DALSA achieves a top overall accuracy of 54.3% across six benchmarks (AIME-24, AIME-25, AMC, MATH-500, OlympiadBench, MMLU-Pro), surpassing the strongest RL baseline Oat-Zero (45.9%) by +8.4 points and the best SFT+RL combination method ReLIFT (52.6%) by +1.7 points. DALSA also produces shorter responses than SFT-based methods (3364 vs. 5533 tokens average length) while maintaining longer outputs than pure RL (2175 tokens), indicating more precise yet sufficiently detailed reasoning. Under PEFT (LoRA/DoRA) on LLaMA3-8B, DALSA achieves 87.0% overall on eight commonsense reasoning tasks, outperforming ReLIFT (85.7%) and SFT (85.2%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DALSA consistently outperforms all six baselines on overall accuracy. Against pure RL methods, the gap is substantial: DALSA exceeds Oat-Zero (45.9%) by +8.4 points, OpenReasoner-Zero (45.3%) by +9.0 points, PRIME-Zero (39.7%) by +14.6 points, and SimpleRL-Zero (39.0%) by +15.3 points. Against SFT+RL combination methods, DALSA surpasses ReLIFT (52.6%) by +1.7 points and LUFFY (50.9%) by +3.4 points, with the advantage driven primarily by stronger performance on AIME-25 (+3.1 over ReLIFT) and MMLU-Pro (+1.6 over ReLIFT). DALSA also achieves the best or near-best accuracy on every individual benchmark, ranking first on AIME-24, AIME-25, AMC, MATH-500, Olympiad, and MMLU-Pro. Notably, DALSA maintains shorter response lengths than SFT-based methods (3364 vs. 5533 tokens) while exceeding RL-only methods in length (2175 tokens), suggesting a balance between conciseness and thoroughness that neither standalone strategy achieves.

## Relation to Prior Work

DALSA extends the growing line of work on integrating SFT and RL for LLM training (ReLIFT, LUFFY, SRFT, SASR) by replacing their fixed or heuristic allocation rules with a learnable strategy allocator that maps multi-dimensional training dynamics (perplexity, entropy, margin, reward mean, logits norm, sequence length) to per-sample strategy decisions. It builds directly on GRPO as the RL backbone and on ReLIFT's observation that SFT handles out-of-knowledge samples while RL refines in-boundary ones, but makes this assignment adaptive and data-driven via a two-layer MLP trained with the Straight-Through Estimator. The anti-curriculum weighting for RL departs from the standard curriculum-learning paradigm by explicitly up-weighting harder samples, and the ALS loss generalizes Szegedy et al.'s label smoothing by deriving perturbations adversarially from the model's own predictive distribution rather than using a fixed uniform distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, plug-in mechanism for adaptive SFT/RL allocation that could be adopted by teams training reasoning LLMs, and the theoretical convergence guarantee for anti-curriculum RL (O(1/√K) with a tighter bound than unweighted or curriculum-weighted variants) provides a principled justification for hard-sample prioritization. However, the individual component contributions are modest (anti-curriculum weighting and ALS each add ~0.5–0.6 pp; DISCARD adds ~0.5 pp), and the allocator's reliance on a small MLP with hand-picked difficulty features raises open questions about whether the approach generalizes to non-mathematical reasoning tasks or to settings where reward signals are noisy or sparse. The paper does not ablate the choice of difficulty features or explore whether simpler heuristics (e.g., accuracy-based thresholds) would suffice, leaving the necessity of the full multi-feature allocator somewhat unvalidated.
