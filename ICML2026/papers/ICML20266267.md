## Problem

Modern T2I models generate limited diversity from a fixed prompt, and existing diversity-enhancement methods rely on monolithic entropy maximization that conflates distinct sources of variation. This paper identifies that diversity decomposes into prompt-dependent (semantic) and prompt-independent (e.g., background, style) components, but prior geometric approaches like Scendi degenerate in fixed-prompt settings due to non-invertible covariance matrices, leaving no principled way to measure or independently control these two axes.

## Input / Output

GASS operates as a sparse inference-time intervention on frozen T2I backbones (SD2.1 U-Net diffusion, SD3-M DiT rectified flow), taking a single text prompt and a batch of random Gaussian latents as input and producing a batch of diverse RGB images (768×768 for ImageNet, 512×512 for DrawBench). The method is activated only at a subset of denoising steps (typically 10–20 out of 50 or 28 total), adding roughly 2.9–3.7 seconds per batch on an A100 GPU. The geometric expansion is parameterized by two hyperparameters r_dep and r_ind that independently control the magnitude of spread along each orthogonal axis.

## Method

GASS decomposes each generated image's CLIP embedding into a prompt-dependent component (projection onto the text embedding e_t) and a prompt-independent component (projection onto a dominant residual direction u_ind identified via Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization and mean-absolute-projection maximization over N=10 candidate vectors). At selected sampling steps, the method samples independent uniform perturbations δ_i^dep and δ_i^ind along these two axes, constructs expanded target embeddings on the unit hypersphere, and then uses gradient-based optimization through the frozen CLIP image encoder to update the predicted clean images so their embeddings align with the expanded targets. A theoretical proposition guarantees that this rank-2 expansion strictly increases the expected Gram determinant (and hence hypervolume) of the batch. The optimized predictions are substituted back into the standard solver transition, steering the generation trajectory without modifying the generative backbone or the noise prediction.

## Results

On ImageNet with SD2.1, GASS achieves the highest Vendi Score (VS=32.711) and Spherical Spread Score (SPP=0.149) among all methods, while maintaining competitive Density (1.015) and ClipScore (0.309). With SD3-M, GASS reaches VS=28.877 and SPP=0.141, outperforming all baselines on diversity metrics while also achieving the best ClipScore (0.313). On DrawBench, GASS attains the top VS (8.847 for SD2.1, 8.212 for SD3-M) and SPP (0.135, 0.114) with minimal quality degradation (ImageReward 0.229/0.778) and the highest ClipScore (0.307/0.320).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GASS outperforms all four diversity-enhancement baselines (PG, CADS, IG, SPELL) on both VS and SPP across both ImageNet and DrawBench with both SD2.1 and SD3-M backbones. On ImageNet-SD2.1, GASS achieves VS=32.711 vs. the next-best SPELL (32.601) and PG (32.082), while CADS and IG drop substantially to 28.935. On ImageNet-SD3-M, GASS leads with VS=28.877, surpassing CADS (28.456) and SPELL (28.433), while IG lags at 27.415. On DrawBench-SD2.1, GASS reaches VS=8.847 and SPP=0.135, beating CADS (8.784/0.125) and SPELL (8.726/0.131). Notably, GASS also achieves the highest ClipScore on SD3-M (0.313 on ImageNet, 0.320 on DrawBench), indicating that its geometric expansion preserves or even improves semantic alignment, whereas baselines like SPELL and CADS sacrifice alignment for diversity. The only metric where a baseline exceeds GASS is Density on SD3-M ImageNet (CADS: 1.374, IG: 1.389 vs. GASS: 1.164), suggesting a mild quality trade-off in that specific setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant entropy-maximization paradigm for T2I diversity enhancement (Vendi Score guidance, PG, CADS, SPELL) by reframing diversity as a geometric spread problem in CLIP embedding space. Rather than maximizing pairwise dissimilarity or embedding entropy, it decomposes variation into two orthogonal axes — the text-embedding direction (prompt-dependent) and an identified residual direction (prompt-independent) — and explicitly expands projection spread along each. This is conceptually distinct from Scendi/SPARKE, which also attempt prompt-aware decomposition but rely on Schur complement entropy over text-image covariance matrices that degenerate in fixed-prompt settings. The geometric perturbation approach draws on latent-space analysis work (Park et al., Zhu et al.) but repurposes it for diversity control rather than editing or personalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GASS provides the first inference-time method to explicitly and controllably enhance background/style diversity (prompt-independent axis) without modifying prompts, addressing a gap where prior entropy-based methods could not distinguish between semantic and stylistic variation. The SPP metric and orthogonal decomposition offer a practical diagnostic tool for understanding *where* diversity is lacking in a generated batch. Key limitations include dependence on the base model's semantic capabilities (GASS cannot fix count errors or structural failures) and the heuristic nature of the single dominant residual direction (N=10 random candidates). Open questions include whether the geometric decomposition generalizes to multi-condition inputs (layout, reference images) and whether richer residual subspace modeling beyond a single axis would yield further gains.
