## Problem

Existing federated graph learning methods assume clean labels, while federated noisy-label learning methods are designed for computer vision and ignore graph topology. In the federated subgraph setting, structural heterogeneity across clients and uneven class distributions make it difficult to distinguish genuinely noisy nodes from hard-but-correct ones, causing both FNL and centralized graph noisy-label methods to fail when directly applied.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262570/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client holds a partial subgraph (node feature vectors, local adjacency matrix, and noisy training labels) obtained by partitioning a global graph via the Louvain algorithm, with noise rates sampled heterogeneously per client from U(η^l, η^u). The method operates in a transductive regime where each client also has local validation and test nodes (unlabeled during training) used for entropy-based quality estimation. The output is a single robust global GNN model for node-level classification, with the CADF filtering module designed as a plug-and-play component compatible with existing subgraph FL frameworks.

## Method

FedRGL introduces a Class-Aware Dual-Consistency Filtering (CADF) mechanism that identifies noisy nodes by intersecting two clean-set estimates: one from the global model's per-class cross-entropy losses with class-specific dynamic thresholds (mean + φ₁·std), and another from soft-label propagation over the local subgraph structure (masked to training nodes) with analogous per-class thresholds. Filtered noisy nodes are then recovered via graph contrastive learning (GRACE-style edge-drop and feature-masking augmentations), where high-confidence pseudo-labels from dual augmented views provide additional supervision, regularized by a Jensen-Shannon divergence term enforcing consistency with the original graph. On the server side, each client uploads its average predictive entropy over local unlabeled nodes, and the global model is aggregated using inverse-entropy reweighting to suppress contributions from unreliable clients. A theoretical proposition shows that under sub-Gaussian loss assumptions and conditional independence between the two views, the dual-consistency intersection achieves exponential contraction of the false-selection probability for noisy nodes.

## Results

At a noise rate of η=0.3, FedRGL achieves 78.75% accuracy under uniform noise and 75.14% under pair noise on Cora (5 clients), outperforming the strongest baseline CRGNN (61.67% and 63.24%) by 17.1 and 11.9 percentage points respectively. On CiteSeer, FedRGL reaches 66.08% (uniform) and 63.22% (pair), surpassing the best baseline by ~18 and ~8 points. On the large-scale ogbn-products dataset (40 clients, 0.4 noise), FedRGL attains 63.71% (uniform) and 59.83% (pair), improving over FedAvg by 5.36% and 6.11% respectively. The paper reports an average 5–8% accuracy gain over the strongest baselines and up to 30% improvement over the weakest under noisy conditions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedRGL dominates all 16 baselines under noisy conditions across every dataset. Against standard FGL methods (FedAvg, FGSSL, FedTAD, FedSPA), the gap is largest: on Cora under uniform noise, FedRGL scores 78.75% versus FedAvg's 48.19% and FGSSL's 51.81%, a 27–31 point margin, because these methods assume clean labels and offer no noise mitigation. Federated noisy-label methods designed for vision (FedCorr, FedNoro, FedNed, RHFL) also fall well short—FedCorr reaches only 50.14% on Cora-uniform and RHFL drops to 61.87% on PubMed-uniform—since they treat samples as independent instances and ignore graph topology. Among centralized GNL methods adapted to the federated setting, CRGNN is the strongest competitor (61.67% on Cora-uniform, 78.28% on PubMed-uniform), yet FedRGL still leads by 17 and 3.5 points respectively; RTGNN, ERASE, and DND-NET trail further behind. The plug-and-play CADF module, when attached to seven existing subgraph FL frameworks (FedSage+, FedLoG, FedGTA, FedATH, FGSSL, FedTAD, FedSPA), yields consistent 16.6–19.6% accuracy gains under 0.3 uniform noise, confirming its generalizability beyond the full FedRGL pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedRGL sits at the intersection of three research threads: federated graph learning (building on FedAvg, FGSSL, FedTAD, FedSPA for subgraph-level node classification), federated learning with label noise (FedCorr, FedNoro, FedNed, RHFL, all designed for vision), and centralized graph learning with noisy labels (CRGNN, RTGNN, ERASE, DND-NET, all assuming full graph access). The paper's key departure is recognizing that neither FNL methods (which ignore graph topology) nor GNL methods (which require complete structure) transfer to the federated subgraph setting, where clients hold partial, structurally heterogeneous subgraphs with imbalanced class distributions. It repurposes GRACE-style graph contrastive augmentation and ERASE-style masked label propagation, but wraps them in a class-aware dual-consistency filtering mechanism (CADF) that is explicitly designed to be plug-and-play across existing subgraph FL frameworks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic treatment of label noise in federated subgraph node classification, a setting that is arguably more realistic than the clean-label assumption underlying most FGL work. The plug-and-play CADF module (yielding 16–19% gains when bolted onto seven different FL frameworks) suggests that noisy-node filtering, rather than the aggregation strategy, is the dominant bottleneck in noisy subgraph FL, which could redirect future work. Open questions include robustness to combined label noise and adversarial structural perturbation (the authors acknowledge this limitation), defense against trigger-based backdoor attacks, and whether the sub-Gaussian separability assumption in Proposition 3.1 holds under extreme class imbalance or very high noise rates.
