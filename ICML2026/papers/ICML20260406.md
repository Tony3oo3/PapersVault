## Problem

Action quantization is widely adopted in autoregressive behavior cloning for continuous control (e.g., RT-1, RT-2, VLA models), yet its theoretical underpinnings remain poorly understood. Specifically, it is unclear how quantization error propagates along the horizon and interacts with statistical estimation error, and under what conditions the resulting regret remains controlled rather than compounding exponentially.

## Input / Output

The learner receives n expert demonstration trajectories but observes only quantized actions (discrete tokens from a finite alphabet) rather than raw continuous actions, along with the corresponding states. The output is a quantized policy mapping states to distributions over the discrete action set, which is then deployed by executing representative actions in the original continuous environment. A key structural constraint is that the learner's training distribution (over state-quantized-action pairs) differs from the deployment distribution (over state-raw-action pairs), introducing an irreducible quantization-induced mismatch that cannot be eliminated by increasing sample size.

## Method

The paper establishes regret bounds for log-loss behavior cloning under action quantization by introducing two key analytical tools: Probabilistic Incremental Input-to-State Stability (P-IISS) for stochastic dynamics and Relaxed Total Variation Continuity (RTVC) for policy smoothness. Under these conditions, the regret decomposes into a statistical term (matching Foster et al.'s optimal rates) and a quantization term with only polynomial (H²) horizon dependence, rather than exponential compounding. The analysis further characterizes when binning-based quantizers naturally satisfy RTVC while general learned quantizers (e.g., VQ) can violate it, leading to large deployment regret despite small in-distribution error. A model-based augmentation is proposed that learns an auxiliary transition model to keep inference rollouts in-distribution, bypassing the smoothness requirement and improving the quantization term to linear-in-H dependence. Information-theoretic lower bounds are proved that jointly capture both statistical and quantization error, confirming the upper bounds are tight.

## Results

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental benchmarks. The headline results are regret upper bounds for log-loss behavior cloning with quantized actions: for stochastic experts (Theorem 2), regret ≲ H·√(log(|Π|δ⁻¹)/n) + H²·ε_q; for deterministic experts with binning quantizers (Theorem 3), regret ≲ H·log(|Π|δ⁻¹)/n + H²·κ(γ((k+1)ε_q)) + H·[γ((k+1)ε_q) + (k+1)ε_q]; and for the model-augmented algorithm (Theorem 7), regret ≤ H·[√(log(|Π|δ⁻¹)+log(|M|δ⁻¹))/n + ε_q], improving the quantization term from H² to H. Information-theoretic lower bounds (Theorems 8–9) of H·(1/n + ε_q) (deterministic) and H·(√(1/n) + ε_q) (stochastic) confirm these rates are tight up to logarithmic factors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper is theoretical and does not run experiments or compare against empirical baselines. Instead, it positions its bounds relative to prior theoretical results: the statistical rates match Foster et al. (2024) for log-loss BC (H·log|Π|/n deterministic, H·√(log|Π|/n) stochastic) and the H²/n lower bound of Rajaraman et al. (2020). The key comparative insight is between quantizer types: binning-based quantizers provably satisfy the RTVC smoothness condition needed for tight bounds, while general learning-based (e.g., VQ) quantizers can violate it, leading to regret of H·Ω(1) or H·Ω(1/|log ε_q|) even when in-distribution quantization error is O(ε_q) (Theorem 6). The model-based augmentation (Theorem 7) improves the quantization-error horizon dependence from H² to H at the cost of an additional log|M| term, and its quantization rate matches the lower bound, whereas the standard log-loss BC (Theorems 2–3) does not.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three threads: (1) the sample-complexity analysis of log-loss behavior cloning via Hellinger distance (Foster et al., 2024), (2) control-theoretic stability conditions for imitation learning in continuous spaces (Block et al., 2023; Simchowitz et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2026), and (3) the widely adopted but theoretically unexamined practice of action quantization in VLA models (Brohan et al., 2022; Lee et al., 2024; Pertsch et al., 2025). It departs from Simchowitz et al. (2025), who showed that continuous-action BC fundamentally fails under 0-1 or log loss, by demonstrating that quantization resolves this impossibility while incurring only polynomial (not exponential) horizon dependence on quantization error. The stability notion (P-IISS) is a probabilistic, local generalization of the open-loop IISS used in prior control-theoretic imitation learning, and the RTVC smoothness condition extends Block et al.'s TVC to accommodate the discrete action alphabet induced by quantization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical justification for why action quantization works (or fails) in VLA models, a practice that is ubiquitous in state-of-the-art systems (RT-1/2, OpenVLA, π0) but previously lacked any formal guarantee. The finding that binning quantizers are provably preferable to learned VQ quantizers for deterministic experts has direct design implications for future VLA architectures. Key limitations include the reliance on P-IISS and RTVC assumptions that may not hold in all practical settings, and the model-based augmentation (which improves the quantization term from H² to H) requires learning a full H-step transition model whose complexity |M| may itself scale with H. Open questions include whether the smoothness conditions can be relaxed, whether the model-based approach can be applied over short sub-horizons (action chunking), and whether the theory extends to infinite-horizon or partially observable settings.
