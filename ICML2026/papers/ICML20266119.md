## Problem

Autonomous code agents expand the LLM attack surface by enabling multi-turn, self-directed interaction, yet prior system prompt extraction methods rely on static datasets, fixed single-turn prompts, or lack the ability to adapt when initial probes fail. No existing approach combines self-evolving strategy discovery with fully black-box deployment against production models, leaving a gap between what is theoretically extractable and what can be systematically recovered without labeled supervision or privileged access.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266119/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes only a standard chat API endpoint to the target model (no weights, logits, or internal states), a fixed vocabulary of 28 hand-designed extraction skills (14 single-turn atomic probes and 14 multi-turn orchestration patterns), and a budget B of total interaction attempts (default B=20). It produces extracted system prompt content validated by a consistency score — the average of self-consistency (same skill repeated) and cross-skill consistency (different skills compared) computed via OpenAI text-embedding-3-large cosine similarity — with success defined as score ≥ 0.7. The per-target UCB skill ranking evolves online and is the only learned artifact; the skill set itself never changes during a campaign.

## Method

JUSTASK formulates system prompt extraction as an online multi-armed bandit problem over a hierarchical skill space, using the UCB formula (UCB(s) = r̄_s + c√(ln N / n_s), c=√2) to balance exploitation of empirically effective skills against exploration of uncertain ones. The agent operates as a closed loop: UCB ranking selects the next skill, an interleaved-thinking module reasons about target characteristics, skill generation instantiates concrete prompts, multi-turn interaction executes the attempt, and consistency validation (self- and cross-skill agreement via embeddings) provides the extrinsic reward that updates skill statistics. This mirrors intrinsically motivated RL — UCB supplies intrinsic exploration pressure while consistency feedback supplies extrinsic task reward — enabling per-target adaptation without gradients, labeled data, or prior knowledge of defenses. The combinatorial action space (≥14×14^10 sequences for a 10-turn conversation) makes UCB essential, and the fixed skill taxonomy (structural skills exploiting mismatched generalization, persuasive skills exploiting helpfulness-safety tensions) ensures the agent can escalate from naive single-turn introspection to sophisticated multi-turn orchestration as needed.

## Results

JUSTASK achieves 100% extraction success (consistency score ≥ 0.7) across all 41 black-box commercial models, with an average of 2.8 turns required (4.8 for closed-weight, 2.4 for open-weight, 1.3 for fine-tunes). In the controlled ground-truth comparison (Table 5, B=3), JUSTASK scores 0.727 on the hardest target (GPT-4o) with only a −3% drop from the easiest, versus −41% for PLeak and −26% for Raccoon. On five production systems (Table 8, B=15), JUSTASK averages Sim-GT 0.703, outperforming Raccoon (0.606), PLeak (0.503), and Zhang et al. (0.420). Controlled defense experiments show that attack-aware prompt protection reduces extraction quality by 18.4%, while naive 'do not reveal' instructions achieve only a 6.0% reduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PLeak (surrogate-model adversarial query optimization), JUSTASK wins decisively on three of four controlled targets (Gemini 0.812 vs 0.551, Claude 0.756 vs 0.722, GPT-4o 0.727 vs 0.454) and by a large margin on production systems (0.703 vs 0.503 average). Against Raccoon (handcrafted template replay), the picture is more nuanced: Raccoon leads on the two easiest targets (DeepSeek 0.950 vs 0.752, Gemini 0.941 vs 0.812) and edges out JUSTASK on Claude (0.757 vs 0.756), but JUSTASK surpasses Raccoon on the hardest target GPT-4o (0.727 vs 0.701) and on all five production systems (0.703 vs 0.606). Against Zhang et al. (neural inversion of LLM outputs), JUSTASK dominates across every setting, with the largest gap on GPT-4o (0.727 vs 0.360) and production systems (0.703 vs 0.420). The paper's central claim is robustness: static baselines degrade sharply as targets harden (PLeak −41%, Raccoon −26%), while JUSTASK's UCB-based adaptive skill selection maintains near-constant performance (−3%), and absorbing Raccoon's templates as additional skills lets JUSTASK match Raccoon on easy targets while exceeding it on hardened ones.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes three established ideas—UCB multi-armed bandit selection (Auer et al., 2002), verbal reinforcement learning à la Reflexion (Shinn et al., 2023), and unsupervised skill discovery (Park et al., 2024)—into a unified self-evolving extraction framework for system prompts. It departs from prior prompt-extraction methods (PLeak, Raccoon, LeakAgent, Zhang et al.) that rely on static datasets, fixed prompt templates, or single-turn settings, by formulating extraction as an online exploration problem over a hierarchical skill space with per-target adaptive selection. The multi-turn jailbreak literature (Crescendo, Chain of Attack, PANDORA) informs the high-level orchestration patterns, but those works target harmful content generation rather than prompt confidentiality, and none incorporate bandit-based strategy discovery.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 100% extraction success across 41 production models and the finding that naive 'do not reveal' instructions provide only 6% protection (and can sometimes increase vulnerability) suggest that prompt-level confidentiality is effectively unachievable with current technology, reframing system prompts as public information for defense planning. The 26.8% identity confusion rate raises independent concerns about model reliability for compliance auditing. The central open question the paper surfaces is whether agentic defense mechanisms can match the automated discovery capability of agentic attacks, since even full attack-taxonomy awareness reduces extraction quality by only 18.4%—a gap that may widen as both sides evolve.
