## Problem

Modern deep learning architectures propagate information solely through activation values (rate), neglecting the joint rate-phase dynamics that underpin biological neural processing and the binding problem. While phase synchronization has been hypothesized to support feature binding and structured generalization, prior synchronization-based approaches (complex-valued neurons, spiking time, phase-only Kuramoto neurons) remain isolated from scalable state-of-the-art models and cannot address large-scale natural image tasks. The paper asks whether phase synchronization can be instantiated as a scalable inductive bias within mainstream Vision Transformers to improve learning efficiency without sacrificing scalability.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images patchified into ViT tokens (16×16 patches), image-text pairs for CLIP-style contrastive learning, and grid-based visual puzzles for ARC-AGI reasoning. Beyond the standard token representations, each token carries an additional evolving phase state (a 2p-dimensional vector of cos/sin pairs per attention head) that is initialized with multi-frequency 2D rotary positional embeddings and updated layer-by-layer. Outputs include class labels, pixel-level segmentation masks, panoptic segmentations, image-text alignment scores, and generated visual reasoning answers, all produced by the same backbone with KoPE integrated into the attention module.

## Method

KoPE introduces an additional evolving phase dimension along the depth of a standard ViT, updated at each layer via discretized Kuramoto dynamics with data-adaptive coupling computed from token representations through shared linear projections (softmax attention-like coupling matrix). Phases are injected into the multi-head self-attention module through complex-form rotations applied to query, key, value, and output vectors, so that phase differences naturally modulate attention scores and enable phase interference in value integration. The key design is the joint rate-phase dynamics: phases evolve toward cluster synchronization (binding related tokens) while simultaneously modulating the rate (token) representations, providing a structural inductive bias that decouples relational learning from attention. Theoretical analysis shows this relaxes the logit-gap condition for attention concentration, accelerating learning. The overhead is minimal—1–2% additional parameters and ~20% additional FLOPs—making it a drop-in enhancement compatible with supervised, self-supervised, and test-time training paradigms.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K supervised learning (300 epochs), ViT+KoPE-B achieves 83.0% top-1 accuracy vs. ViT-B's 82.1%, with larger generalization gains on ImageNet V2 (72.7% vs. 71.2%). On ADE20K semantic segmentation, ViT+KoPE-B reaches 46.94% mIoU vs. 44.10% for ViT-B (+2.84 pp), and on COCO panoptic segmentation, 55.53 PQ vs. 54.51. On the challenging ARC-AGI-1 few-shot reasoning benchmark, the encoder-decoder ViT+KoPE achieves 56.8±0.7 vs. 54.7±0.6 for the ViT baseline, and 9.8±0.6 vs. 7.3±0.9 on ARC-AGI-2, setting a new state-of-the-art among vision models trained from scratch.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary ViT baseline (Dosovitskiy et al., 2021), KoPE consistently wins across all evaluated tasks: +0.9 pp top-1 on ImageNet-1K (base, 300 ep), +2.84 pp mIoU on ADE20K, +1.02 PQ on COCO panoptic segmentation, +1.01 pp zero-shot ImageNet in CLIP, and +2.1 pp on ARC-AGI-1. On ARC-AGI, KoPE substantially outperforms the other vision-model baselines: HRM (Wang et al., 2025) scores 40.3/5.0 on ARC-1/ARC-2, and TRM (Jolicoeur-Martineau, 2025) scores 44.6/7.8, while Enc-Dec ViT+KoPE reaches 56.8/9.8. Compared to the VARC ViT baseline from Hu et al. (2025) (54.7±0.6 on ARC-1, 7.3±0.9 on ARC-2), KoPE improves by +2.1 and +2.5 pp respectively. Notably, KoPE's 300-epoch ViT+KoPE-L (84.0% val, 73.8% V2) matches or exceeds vanilla ViT-L trained for 800 epochs (83.6% val, 73.2% V2), demonstrating that the synchronization mechanism provides genuine learning-efficiency gains rather than merely adding capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on synchronization in neural networks (complex-valued neurons, spiking-time models, and phase-only Kuramoto neurons from Miyato et al., 2025) by coupling evolving Kuramoto phase dynamics with standard ViT rate representations through complex-form rotations inspired by rotary position embedding. The key departure from prior synchronization-based methods is scalability: whereas previous works targeted binding-specific problems like object discovery in isolation, KoPE instantiates the mechanism as a lightweight add-on (1–2% parameters, ~20% FLOPs) within mainstream Vision Transformers, enabling it to improve large-scale supervised, self-supervised, and vision-language training. The theoretical analysis builds on Li et al. (2023) for shallow ViT training complexity, showing that phase-induced logit shifts relax the sufficient condition for attention concentration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

KoPE demonstrates that a neuro-inspired synchronization prior can serve as a practical, scalable inductive bias that consistently improves learning efficiency and structured understanding across diverse tasks (classification, segmentation, vision-language alignment, abstract visual reasoning) with minimal overhead. The ARC-AGI results (SOTA among vision models trained from scratch) suggest that implicit synchronization-based binding may partially address the compositional generalization gap that has motivated object-centric learning. Open questions include whether the mechanism transfers to other architectures (e.g., diffusion models, LLMs), whether the simplified theoretical analysis captures the full mechanism in deep networks, and whether the 20% FLOPs cost remains acceptable at frontier scale. The paper acknowledges the theoretical setting is conservative and leaves deeper analysis for future work.
