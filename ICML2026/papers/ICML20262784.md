## Problem

Existing LLM-based optimization modeling methods rely on static parametric knowledge that cannot cover the long-tail of industrial domains, causing failures on knowledge-intensive problems where scenario comprehension or mathematical techniques are missing. Two specific training gaps compound this: current datasets contain only self-contained single-domain problems that provide no incentive for external information seeking, and there is no supervised signal for the quality of intermediate retrieval steps, so agents cannot learn to distinguish effective knowledge acquisition from redundant exploration.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a natural language optimization problem description and iteratively generates reasoning thoughts, web search queries (executed via Arxiv Search API returning top-10 papers), and Python code execution calls over up to 3 research turns. Retrieved PDFs are parsed by MinerU and summarized by a Qwen3-32B model before being fed back as observations. The final output is executable Gurobi solver code whose optimal objective value is compared against ground truth; the agent's full trajectory (reasoning, queries, observations, code) is the unit of policy optimization.

## Method

Opt-Miner formulates each problem as a Hierarchical Scenario Tree (HST) with an entity subtree (functional-role-abstracted objects) and a constraint subtree (thematic constraint clusters with associated mathematical techniques), then synthesizes complex multi-domain problems via three operators: subtree union (merging same-domain trees), scenario transfer (transplanting constraint logic across heterogeneous domains via functional entity mapping), and knowledge fogging (replacing explicit domain knowledge with terse professional terminology to create intentional information gaps). For training, the paper proposes R-GRPO, a retrieval-augmented variant of GRPO that computes a hybrid reward combining solver-verified modeling accuracy, an F1 retrieval-matching score between the agent's search keywords and ground-truth evidence recorded during synthesis, and a format reward. A token-level binary mask restricts the policy gradient to agent-generated tokens (reasoning, queries, code) while excluding environmental observation tokens, and group-relative advantage normalization is applied over G sampled trajectories per problem.

## Results

Opt-Miner-Qwen3-8B achieves 97.0% on NL4Opt, 94.8% on MAMO EasyLP, 76.5% on MAMO ComplexLP, 44.0% on IndustryOR, 66.5% on Resocratic, 45.8% on OptMATH, and 30.5% on Opt-Miner-Bench (all Pass@1). The 4B variant (Opt-Miner-Qwen3-4B-Instruct) reaches 96.2%, 92.5%, 66.4%, 41.0%, 60.6%, 35.5%, and 21.1% respectively. The most significant gains appear on knowledge-intensive benchmarks: Opt-Miner-Qwen3-8B outperforms the best 32B fine-tuned agent (SIRL-Qwen2.5-32B) by 15.4 points on MAMO ComplexLP (76.5 vs. 61.1) and 8.6 points on Opt-Miner-Bench (30.5 vs. 21.9), while matching or exceeding OpenAI-o3 on all seven benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest 32B fine-tuned agent (SIRL-Qwen2.5-32B), Opt-Miner-Qwen3-8B wins decisively on MAMO ComplexLP (76.5 vs. 61.1, +15.4), IndustryOR (44.0 vs. 42.0, +2.0), and Opt-Miner-Bench (30.5 vs. 21.9, +8.6), ties on OptMATH (45.8 vs. 45.8), and narrowly trails on NL4Opt (97.0 vs. 98.0) and Resocratic (66.5 vs. 67.4). Compared to the commercial OpenAI-o3, Opt-Miner-Qwen3-8B outperforms on every benchmark, with the largest margins on MAMO ComplexLP (+23.0), OptMATH (+2.5), and Opt-Miner-Bench (+5.5). Against GPT-4o, the gap is even wider, with Opt-Miner leading by 17.8 points on OptMATH (45.8 vs. 17.5) and 14.9 points on Opt-Miner-Bench (30.5 vs. 15.6). The 4B variant already surpasses all 7B–14B fine-tuned baselines (ORLM, LLMOPT, OptMATH-7B, SIRL-7B) across all seven benchmarks, and even exceeds the 32B OptMATH on MAMO ComplexLP (66.4 vs. 54.1) and Opt-Miner-Bench (21.1 vs. 17.2). On the easier NL4Opt benchmark, Opt-Miner is competitive but not dominant (97.0 vs. SIRL-32B's 98.0), suggesting its primary advantage lies in knowledge-intensive, multi-domain problems where external retrieval is essential.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GRPO reinforcement learning framework (Shao et al., 2024) into a multi-turn, tool-augmented setting by introducing retrieval-based rewards and token-level masking for agent-generated content (R-GRPO). Conceptually, it departs from the dominant fine-tuning paradigm in LLM optimization modeling (ORLM, LLMOPT, OptMATH, SIRL), which trains on self-contained problems with all information provided in the prompt, by reframing the agent as an active information seeker that must identify knowledge gaps and retrieve external technical documents. The data synthesis pipeline draws on the hierarchical structure of optimization problems (entities, constraints, mathematical techniques) and borrows the knowledge-fogging idea from curriculum learning, but applies it specifically to create training data that necessitates web retrieval rather than passive reading.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper validates a practical hypothesis for industrial optimization: static parametric knowledge in even large LLMs is insufficient for long-tail, multi-domain problems, and an agent that can proactively search for relevant mathematical techniques (e.g., big-M formulations, N-1 security constraints) outperforms one that must rely on internalized knowledge. The tree-guided synthesis pipeline (subtree union, transfer, knowledge fogging) offers a reusable recipe for generating training data with controlled knowledge gaps, potentially applicable beyond optimization modeling. Open questions include whether the arXiv-limited search generalizes to open-web retrieval, whether the keyword-F1 retrieval reward scales to more nuanced information needs, and whether the added inference latency (~48s vs. ~9s for ORLM) is acceptable in time-sensitive industrial settings.
