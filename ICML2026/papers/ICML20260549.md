## Problem

Tree-of-Thoughts (ToT) style inference amplifies KV cache memory by requiring retention of partial trajectories across a branching frontier, quickly exhausting GPU memory and constraining search depth/width. Existing KV cache eviction policies are sequence-centric, treating the tree as a flat linear pool and failing to model the non-monotonic focus shifts and backtracking dynamics that govern which blocks will be reused, leading to either OOM failures or over-penalization of inactive branches needed for future backtracking.

## Input / Output

The method operates at the thought-block level (contiguous token spans representing discrete reasoning steps) rather than individual tokens, consuming the tree topology with active-leaf distance Δᵢ and depth dᵢ, the search controller's normalized node scores, next-token entropy at block boundaries, and incrementally accumulated attention mass. It outputs per-block retention budgets kᵢ and a concrete retained-token index set per block (global sinks + tail window + attention heavy hitters), with evicted blocks remaining recoverable via a single prefill pass (lazy rehydration) when they re-enter the active path. Policy updates are triggered only at three discrete events—block boundary, active-leaf transition, and memory pressure—minimizing per-token overhead.

## Method

ArborKV couples a Multi-Signal Value Estimator (MSVE) with a Tree-Aware Eviction (TAE) policy. MSVE fuses three signals already available during decoding—search controller node score, normalized next-token entropy, and accumulated attention mass—into a per-block semantic utility score sᵢ via a calibrated linear probe (θ trained offline via leave-one-out block masking on full-retention trajectories). TAE then solves a separable convex budgeted allocation problem (formalized via KKT conditions) that maps sᵢ and tree geometry (depth dᵢ, distance-to-active-leaf Δᵢ) to a retention ratio rᵢ, incorporating an off-path discount η for discrete sibling coordination and exponential geometric decay toward the active frontier. Within each block, eviction is purely token-extractive: it retains global attention sinks, a mandatory tail window, and top-m attention heavy hitters, freeing the rest. A lazy rehydration mechanism restores evicted blocks via a single prefill pass (no autoregressive generation) only when backtracking brings them back onto the active path, making eviction reversible without permanent information loss.

## Results

Under the tightest KV budget (ρ=0.25) on Llama-3.1-8B, ArborKV achieves 70.2% on GSM8K, 64.8% on MATH500, 44.8% on Game of 24, and 24.3% on AIME, while reducing peak memory to ~5.5–6.1 GiB (vs. ~21–23 GiB for FullKV, a ~4× reduction). On Qwen-2.5-7B at the same budget, ArborKV reaches 74.9% (GSM8K), 67.4% (MATH500), 52.6% (Game of 24), and 26.1% (AIME) with ~4.8–6.1 GiB peak. In the fixed-memory scaling test (Config-L, N_expand=256), FullKV triggers OOM while ArborKV completes the search at 78.4% accuracy with only 5.6 GiB peak and 38.2 s latency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ArborKV consistently outperforms all three sequence-centric baselines (H2O, StreamingLLM, ThinKV) across every task and both models at both ρ=0.50 and ρ=0.25. The gap is most pronounced on backtracking-heavy Game of 24, where at ρ=0.25 ArborKV leads ThinKV by 3.3 points (Llama) and 3.3 points (Qwen), and H2O by 6.2 and 10.2 points respectively, reflecting the cost of irreversible eviction under frequent branch switching. On the hardest benchmark (AIME), ArborKV still edges out ThinKV by 1.2 points (Llama) and 1.1 points (Qwen) at ρ=0.25. In the fixed-memory scaling experiment (Config-L, N_expand=256), FullKV runs out of memory entirely, while ArborKV completes the search at 78.4% accuracy versus 69.0% (H2O) and 67.1% (StreamingLLM), also achieving the lowest latency (38.2 s vs. 42.5 s and 41.8 s). Overall, ArborKV's advantage grows with search intensity and budget tightness, confirming that topology-aware allocation and lazy rehydration are critical for tree-structured inference.

## Relation to Prior Work

ArborKV builds on the DPTS parallel tree-search controller (Ding et al., 2025) for managing branching and backtracking in LLM reasoning, and adapts the intra-block token-retention pattern from H2O's heavy-hitter oracle (Zhang et al., 2023) to a tree-structured setting. It departs from the dominant sequence-centric KV-cache literature (H2O, StreamingLLM, ThinKV, PagedAttention) by treating the cacheable unit as a thought block within a rooted tree rather than a flat token sequence, and by introducing a reversible eviction mechanism (lazy rehydration) that prior irreversible policies cannot support. The key conceptual shift is formulating retention as a budgeted allocation over tree topology—coupling semantic value estimation with geometric proximity to the active frontier—rather than as per-token salience scoring along a single trajectory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~4× peak KV-memory reduction while preserving near-full-retention accuracy directly addresses the practical bottleneck that prevents practitioners from scaling tree-search depth and width on commodity GPUs, as demonstrated by the OOM failure of FullKV at N_expand=256 that ArborKV avoids at 5.6 GiB. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to larger models (tested only on 7–8B), to non-ToT search paradigms (e.g., graph-structured or iterative refinement), and whether the offline MSVE calibration transfers robustly across task distributions. The paper also leaves open how the method interacts with emerging architectural KV reductions (GQA, MQA) and whether the event-driven overhead remains negligible at higher batch sizes or multi-GPU settings.
