## Problem

Naively combining global rotation or Hadamard transformations with microscaling (MX) quantization causes severe performance degradation, and the proposed workaround of block-diagonal rotations restricts transformations to isolated subspaces, preventing cross-block redistribution of activation mass. The paper asks whether one can learn transformations that simultaneously account for both the MX block structure and the activation distribution to achieve effective outlier suppression under MX quantization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261318/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes full-precision LLM weights and 256 calibration sequences from WikiText2 (length 1024 for models ≤8B, reduced to 512 or 128 with proportionally more samples for 14B/32B models) and produces an MX-quantized model (MXFP4 or MXINT4, block size 32) where the learned affine transformations are folded into the linear weight matrices, incurring zero additional inference cost. The learned parameters consist of N+1 sets of {L, U, s, v} (LU) or {G, R, s, v} (QR) matrices, one global set for T1 on the residual path and one per transformer block for T2 on attention value vectors.

## Method

LATMiX learns general invertible affine transformations (not restricted to rotations) parameterized via LU decomposition (A = PL(U+diag(s))) or QR decomposition (A = Q(R+diag(s)) with Q = exp(½(G−Gᵀ))), enabling standard deep learning optimizers instead of constrained Stiefel-manifold optimization. A theoretical bound (Theorem 3.3) shows MX quantization error is governed by the spectral norm of A⁻¹ and the per-block expected maximum activation, revealing a tradeoff that motivates learning rather than fixed transformations. Training minimizes a KL-divergence distillation loss between the full-precision teacher and the transformed-quantized student, plus a volume-preserving regularization on log|s| to maintain invertibility. After optimization, T1 and T2 are folded into adjacent linear layers (K, Q, V projections and output projections), so the quantized model has identical inference cost to standard MX-quantized baselines. The key distinctions from prior rotation-based methods are: (1) the admissible transformation class is the full affine group Aff(d) rather than O(d), (2) optimization is over free-form matrices with a distillation objective rather than cross-entropy on the Stiefel manifold, and (3) the bias term allows centering the activation distribution to reduce the per-block maximum term in the error bound.

## Results

On seven zero-shot commonsense and reading-comprehension benchmarks (ARC, HellaSwag, WinoGrande, PIQA, BoolQ, OBQA), LATMiX-LU achieves the best average accuracy across 7 of 8 models under MXFP4 quantization, with the largest gain on Qwen3-1.7B (57.42% vs. 52.23% for MR-GPTQ, a +5.19 pp improvement). Under the more aggressive MXINT4 format, LATMiX-LU wins on all 8 models, reaching 99.49% accuracy recovery on Qwen3-32B (vs. 97.51% for MR-GPTQ). On WikiText2 perplexity (MXFP4), LATMiX-LU at full granularity achieves 11.64 on Llama3.2-1B and 15.11 on Qwen3-1.7B, outperforming all rotation-based and block-diagonal baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LATMiX-LU outperforms the strongest baseline MR-GPTQ (Egiazarian et al., 2025) on 7 of 8 models under MXFP4 and on all 8 models under MXINT4, with the largest margins on Qwen3-1.7B (+5.19 pp MXFP4, +3.66 pp MXINT4) and Qwen3-4B (+2.53 pp MXFP4, +2.62 pp MXINT4). Against SpinQuant (Liu et al., 2025), which learns rotations on the Stiefel manifold, LATMiX-LU gains up to +4.72 pp (Qwen3-1.7B MXFP4) and +5.90 pp (Qwen3-1.7B MXINT4). Compared to OSTQuant (Hu et al., 2025), which uses orthogonal and scaling transformations, LATMiX-LU leads by up to +1.94 pp (Qwen3-1.7B MXFP4). FlatQuant (Sun et al., 2025), which parameterizes transformations as Kronecker products of low-rank matrices, is outperformed by LATMiX-LU on all models by up to +2.57 pp (Qwen3-1.7B MXFP4). The only model where LATMiX-LU trails the best baseline is Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct under MXFP4, where MR-GPTQ edges ahead by 0.48 pp in accuracy. Under the more extreme MXINT4 setting, LATMiX-LU achieves the highest recovery on every model, reaching 99.49% on Qwen3-32B (vs. 97.51% for MR-GPTQ and 98.32% for OSTQuant).

## Relation to Prior Work

LATMiX extends the rotation-based outlier-reduction lineage (QuaRot, SpinQuant) by replacing the restriction to orthogonal or block-diagonal rotation matrices with general invertible affine transformations parameterized via LU or QR decompositions. It departs from the block-diagonal workaround proposed by Egiazarian et al. and Shao et al. for MX compatibility, arguing via a theoretical error bound (Theorem 3.3) that both the MX block structure and the cross-block feature distribution must be jointly accounted for. The optimization also shifts from Stiefel-manifold constrained learning (SpinQuant) to free-form matrix optimization with a distillation loss and volume-preserving regularization, enabling standard deep-learning tooling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MX formats are endorsed by major hardware vendors and are becoming the default low-precision representation, so effective outlier mitigation under MX is practically critical. The theoretical bound provides a principled design criterion for transformations that prior empirical work lacked. A key limitation is the calibration training cost (~2 hours on 4 A100s for a 14B model), and the paper does not evaluate non-MX formats despite claiming generality. An open question is whether the LU-over-QR advantage (attributed to matrix-exponential optimization difficulty) can be closed, since QR in principle spans the full GL group.
