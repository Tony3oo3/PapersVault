## Problem

Existing protein structure tokenizers create tokens by pooling information from local spatial neighborhoods, which causes error accumulation in generative models (a single missampled token can drastically alter a predicted structure), scales linearly with protein length, and requires mean-pooling to obtain fixed-size representations. This paper asks whether a global tokenization scheme—where each successive token contributes increasing detail to a whole-protein representation—can resolve these issues while maintaining reconstruction fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260900/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes raw Cα backbone coordinates (an L×3 tensor, mean-centered and normalized) as input and produces a variable-length sequence of discrete tokens via finite-scalar quantization (levels 8,5,5,5; effective codebook size 1000). The protein size is regressed from the first token via a cross-entropy loss, decoupling residue count from the conditioning sequence. Because tokens form a coarse-to-fine hierarchy, any prefix of length N is a valid (lower-fidelity) representation, enabling fixed-size embeddings of dimension N×K without pooling, controllable compression, and zero-shot applications like shrinking (by conditioning on the same tokens but a smaller target size) or affinity maturation (by using a prefix as a prefill for beam search).

## Method

APT is a diffusion autoencoder with a discrete bottleneck: a bidirectional attention transformer encoder maps Cα coordinates to a latent sequence, which is discretized via FSQ and used to condition a diffusion transformer decoder trained with a flow-matching objective. The key architectural innovation is nested dropout—during training, a random upper cutoff is sampled and all tokens beyond it are zeroed—forcing the model to place critical global information in early tokens and finer detail in later ones, creating a coarse-to-fine hierarchy. The protein size is projected from the first token with a small cross-entropy loss (λ_size ≈ 0.01), and stochastic equivariance is learned via random rotations rather than explicit SE(3) symmetry constraints. Training proceeds in two stages: first the autoencoder, then a GPT-style autoregressive model over the token sequences. At inference, entropy-based cutoff sampling (finite threshold or spline minimum) determines how many tokens to retain, and classifier annealing linearly interpolates between conditional and unconditional flow fields during diffusion decoding to balance prompt fidelity with on-manifold generation.

## Results

On unconditional protein structure generation, APT achieves 0.871 designability and 1.352 Å scRMSD, substantially outperforming DPLM2 (0.486, 3.31 Å), ESM3 (0.270, 17.51 Å), and Kanzi (0.562, 3.78 Å). On reconstruction, APT achieves 0.90 Å RMSD on CATH and CAMEO, and 1.17 Å on AFDB, matching or exceeding all baselines. With only 128 tokens (vs. full-length sequences), APT still achieves 1.94 Å RMSD on CATH, demonstrating strong compressibility. On CATH-T classification via MLP probing, APT's fixed-size token representations outperform mean-pooled embeddings from DPLM2 and ESM3 even at 16 tokens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

APT decisively outperforms all five baselines on generative designability: 0.871 vs. DPLM2 (0.486), ESM3 (0.270), Kanzi (0.562), and even Kanzi with best-of-4 sampling (0.617). On scRMSD, APT (1.352 Å) is roughly 2.5× better than DPLM2 (3.31 Å) and 13× better than ESM3 (17.51 Å). On reconstruction (Table 1), APT achieves the best RMSD on CATH (0.90 Å) and CAMEO (0.90 Å), beating ESM3 (1.05/0.86 Å), Kanzi (1.09/0.98 Å), IST (1.20/1.64 Å), and FoldToken (1.30/2.54 Å); on AFDB, APT (1.17 Å) is competitive with Kanzi (1.18 Å) and far ahead of DPLM2 (4.68 Å) and ESM3 (2.38 Å). IST and FoldToken lack generative capabilities and are compared only on reconstruction, where APT dominates. On representation learning (CATH-T MLP probing), APT's global token representations outperform mean-pooled DPLM2 and ESM3 embeddings, with the advantage persisting even at 16 tokens where APT still exceeds the full-length baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

APT departs from the dominant paradigm in protein structure tokenization, where each token pools information from a spatially local neighborhood (as in DPLM2, ESM3, IST, and FoldToken), and instead adopts a global coarse-to-fine hierarchy inspired by Fourier/wavelet decomposition and adaptive tokenization in computer vision (FlexTok, CAT). The architectural backbone combines a diffusion autoencoder with a discrete bottleneck (Preechakul et al., 2022), finite scalar quantization (Mentzer et al., 2023), and a flow-matching training objective (Lipman et al., 2022), with nested dropout (Rippel et al., 2014) enforcing the adaptive property that early tokens carry global structure and later tokens add detail. The autoregressive generation stage and classifier annealing strategy build on standard LLM sampling and limited-interval guidance techniques, respectively, but the key conceptual shift is that every prefix of the token sequence is a valid, self-contained conditioning signal for the diffusion decoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first autoregressive protein generation model to match or exceed large-scale diffusion models on designability (0.871 vs. 0.486 for DPLM2), and the global-token design eliminates the need for mean-pooling to obtain fixed-size representations, which simplifies downstream fine-tuning for global properties like solubility or thermostability. The decoupling of protein size from the conditioning sequence enables zero-shot applications (protein shrinking, affinity maturation via beam search) that are not straightforward with local tokenizers. Key limitations include the tokenizer's inability to capture local motifs (sidechain-level detail, local scaffolding), the reliance on Cα-only coordinates, and the fact that the strongest results require careful inference-time tuning (entropy cutoff + classifier annealing) that may not transfer directly to multimodal or conditional settings. An open question is how to combine global and local representations to handle the multi-scale nature of protein function.
