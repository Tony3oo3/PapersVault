## Problem

Standard classifier-guided unlearning tilts a frozen base distribution by a learned retain/forget classifier, but the paper proves this approach fundamentally fails when the forget set is sharply concentrated: the Forget Error scales linearly with the peak density ‖p_f‖_∞, a dependence shown to be unavoidable (not an analysis artifact). The paper asks whether a principled modification to the tilting procedure can overcome this limitation while retaining finite-sample guarantees.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260298/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen pretrained LLM (Llama 3.1 8B), a text context x, and labeled retain/forget samples from the original training data. A low-rank linear head (hidden dimension h=20) is trained on mean-pooled final hidden states of the base model, producing per-token class posteriors g_φ(x) ∈ [0,1]^{|V|}. At inference, the output is a tempered-tilted next-token distribution computed as softmax(ln p_{θ*}(y|x)/T + ln g_φ(x)_y), where T ≥ 1 is a temperature hyperparameter. Because hidden states are precomputed and cached, the base model requires no forward or backward passes during classifier training.

## Method

T3-Unlearning reframes unlearning as density-ratio estimation: the optimal tilt f* is the Bayes posterior P(S=1|Z=z) distinguishing retain from forget samples, so unlearning reduces to training a probabilistic classifier. The key innovation is a two-step inference procedure — first tempering the base distribution to p^{1/T} to flatten high-confidence probability spikes, then tilting the tempered distribution by the learned classifier, yielding p̂_r^{(T)} ∝ p^{1/T}·f̂. The classifier is a low-rank linear head (A_φ ∈ R^{h×d}, B_φ ∈ R^{|V|×h}) over cached mean-pooled hidden states, trained with regularized cross-entropy, requiring only 0.03% of the base model's parameters. Theoretically, the paper proves the untempered estimator's Forget Error is Ω(‖p_f‖_∞·δ) in the worst case, while the tempered estimator achieves O(‖p_f‖_∞^{1/T}·δ^{1/2k}), sublinear in peak density at the cost of a tempering-induced Retain Error bias that grows as the retain distribution's entropy decreases.

## Results

On the TOFU benchmark with Llama 3.1 8B, T3-Unlearning achieves the highest Forget Quality (FQ) of 0.914 (5% split) and 0.671 (10% split), substantially outperforming the next-best baselines (NPO at 0.793/0.185, SimNPO at 0.745/0.536). It also attains the highest MU-ROUGE among methods with competitive FQ: 0.900 (5%) and 0.899 (10%), close to the original model's 0.948. In terms of efficiency, T3-Unlearning trains only 2.65M parameters (0.03% of the 8B model) and completes unlearning in 5.12s (preprocessed) versus 236–1680s for fine-tuning baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

T3-Unlearning dominates on Forget Quality across both splits: at 5% it reaches FQ 0.914 vs. NPO (0.793), SimNPO (0.745), and IdkDPO (0.535); at 10% it reaches 0.671 vs. SimNPO (0.536) and NPO (0.185). On MU-ROUGE, T3-Unlearning (0.900/0.899) trails only ULD (0.944/0.944) and UnDIAL (0.653/0.927), but ULD and UnDIAL achieve near-zero FQ (0.169/0.012 and 0.016/0.000), meaning they fail to actually unlearn. Among methods with FQ > 0.5, T3-Unlearning achieves the best MU-ROUGE by a wide margin (0.900 vs. SimNPO's 0.786 at 5%; 0.899 vs. SimNPO's 0.840 at 10%). Fine-tuning-based baselines (GradAscent, GradDiff, WGA, SatImp, RMU) either fail to unlearn (FQ ≈ 0) or catastrophically degrade utility (RMU drops MU-ROUGE to 0.000). The paper also notes that T3-Unlearning's MU (0.612) is slightly below the original model (0.637) and below SimNPO (0.653/0.662), but attributes this to tempering lowering absolute token probabilities without degrading generation quality, as reflected in the strong MU-ROUGE scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the frozen-base-model tilting paradigm introduced by Eldan & Russinovich (2023) and Ji et al. (2024), where unlearning is performed by learning an auxiliary tilt function rather than updating the base model. It departs from prior tilting methods (which use LoRA adapters or auxiliary LLMs) by replacing the tilt function with a lightweight linear head over mean-pooled hidden states, and introduces tempering (raising the base density to 1/T) as a principled preprocessing step. The theoretical framing draws on classifier-guided sampling (Dhariwal & Nichol, 2021) and importance weighting, but reframes unlearning as density-ratio estimation via a surrogate classification task, providing the first finite-sample guarantees linking classifier excess risk to unlearning error.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key contribution is a provable demonstration that standard classifier-guided tilting fails for concentrated forget distributions (Forget Error scales linearly with ||p_f||_∞, and this is tight), establishing tempering as theoretically necessary rather than heuristic. Practically, T3-Unlearning trains only 0.03% of parameters and completes in ~5 seconds versus minutes for fine-tuning baselines, making unlearning potentially viable at scale. Limitations include evaluation on a single benchmark (TOFU) with a single model (Llama 3.1 8B), fictitious-author forget sets that may not capture the complexity of real privacy-sensitive data, and the tempering bias tradeoff that degrades Retain Error for low-entropy retain distributions. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-turn or iterative unlearning, and whether the linear-head classifier suffices for more complex forget/retain boundaries.
