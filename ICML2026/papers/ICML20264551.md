## Problem

Prior theoretical results on multi-agent imitation learning (MAIL) are restricted to tabular Markov games, where the all-policy-deviation concentrability coefficient C_max governs sample complexity and can be infinite. It remains open whether function approximation—specifically linear structure in transitions and rewards—can yield a weaker, feature-level concentrability coefficient that enables generalization across unseen states, and whether interactive algorithms can eliminate this dependence entirely in the linear setting.

## Input / Output

In the non-interactive setting, the algorithm receives a fixed dataset of state-action pairs sampled from the expert Nash equilibrium occupancy measure, along with a known d-dimensional feature map φ; in the interactive setting, it additionally queries the expert policy at any state encountered during exploration. The transition matrix M_{-n} and reward vector w_{-n} are unknown but assumed linear in φ. The output is a policy pair (π_out^1, π_out^2) from the softmax-linear class Π_softlin that constitutes an ε-approximate Nash equilibrium, with sample complexity depending on the feature dimension d rather than the state space size.

## Method

For the non-interactive case, the paper shows that behavioral cloning over the softmax-linear policy class achieves an ε-Nash equilibrium with sample complexity depending on a new feature-level concentrability coefficient C_{φ,max}, which is upper-bounded by and potentially much smaller than the state-action C_max; the key technical step is a change-of-measure argument at the feature level exploiting linearity of the state occupancy measure in M_{-n}. For the interactive case, the paper introduces LSVI-UCB-ZERO-BC, a two-phase algorithm: an exploration phase that runs LSVI-UCB with zero reward and a slightly inflated bonus (β+1 instead of β) to collect expert actions under all opponent deviations via a no-regret learner, followed by a standard BC imitation phase on the collected data. This design avoids explicit looping over all deviations and yields concentrability-free guarantees scaling only with d. A deep extension, DQN-Explore-BC, replaces the linear feature norm bonus with a neural network critic evaluated on a replay buffer, enabling application to complex games like Tic-Tac-Toe and Connect4.

## Results

In the linear Gridworld (72 states, 4 actions), BC fails to minimize the Nash gap with tabular (d=288) and relational (d=80) features, while LSVI-UCB-ZERO-BC converges the Nash gap to 0 for both feature maps, with faster convergence for the lower-dimensional relational features. In deep Tic-Tac-Toe, DQN-Explore-BC successfully minimizes the Nash gap while Deep BC fails to do so. On Connect4, DQN-Explore-BC achieves win rates of 0.21–0.97 against opponents ranging from approximate best response to Solver Noise 5, consistently outperforming BC (0.00–0.54) across all opponent skill levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is Behavioral Cloning (BC), both in its linear form (for the Gridworld experiments) and deep form (for Tic-Tac-Toe and Connect4). In the linear Gridworld, BC with tabular features (d=288) and relational features (d=80) fails to minimize the Nash gap, indicating a large or unbounded feature-level concentrability coefficient C_{φ,max}; in contrast, LSVI-UCB-ZERO-BC drives the Nash gap to 0 for both feature maps, confirming the theoretical prediction that interactive exploration bypasses the concentrability requirement. In deep Tic-Tac-Toe, Deep BC fails to minimize the Nash gap while DQN-Explore-BC succeeds. On Connect4, DQN-Explore-BC dominates BC at every opponent level: against the strongest opponent (Approx. Best Response), DQN-Explore-BC achieves a 0.21 win rate versus 0.00 for BC, and against the weakest (Solver Noise 5), the gap widens to 0.97 vs. 0.54. The advantage is most pronounced at intermediate opponent strengths (Solver Noise 2–4), where DQN-Explore-BC leads by 0.55–0.52 in win rate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the tabular multi-agent imitation learning (MAIL) framework of Freihaut et al. (2025b) to linear Markov games, where both transitions and rewards are linear in known features. The core exploration mechanism adapts LSVI-UCB (Jin et al., 2019) into a reward-free variant (LSVI-UCB-ZERO) with a modified bonus term, while the infinite-horizon extension leverages RMAX-RAVI-LSVI-UCB (Moulin et al., 2025b). The key conceptual departure from prior work is showing that the state-action concentrability coefficient C_max, proven necessary for all non-interactive MAIL in the tabular setting, can be replaced by a feature-level coefficient C_{φ,max} that is upper-bounded by and potentially much smaller than C_max, fundamentally changing the hardness landscape when informative features are available.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical analysis of MAIL with function approximation, establishing that the choice of feature representation can determine whether behavioral cloning succeeds or fails in multi-agent settings — a dimension entirely absent from prior tabular analyses. The interactive algorithm (LSVI-UCB-ZERO-BC) removes even the C_{φ,max} dependence, with sample complexity scaling only in feature dimension d, and extends to the discounted infinite-horizon setting, resolving an open question from Freihaut et al. (2025b). Open questions include: estimating C_{φ,max} a priori to decide whether BC is safe to apply, removing the expert policy realizability assumption (Assumption 3.1), and developing adaptive interaction that exploits pre-collected data. The empirical validation, while clear, is limited to small games (3×3 Gridworld, Tic-Tac-Toe, Connect4), leaving open whether the deep extension (DQN-Explore-BC) scales to larger competitive environments.
