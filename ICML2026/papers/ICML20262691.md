## Problem

In multi-treatment causal representation learning, the balancing weight α that trades off deconfounding against information preservation is treated as a heuristic hyperparameter with no principled selection rule, and prevailing pairwise balancing strategies scale as O(K²) in both computation and statistical instability. The paper identifies a fundamentally new coupled compression phenomenon: when K > 2, the binomial(K,2) pairwise discrepancy terms are statistically coupled through the shared representation, causing the tuning signal to degrade as O(K²/√n) and its variance as Θ(K⁴/n), meaning stronger fine-grained balancing is not uniformly better and can destroy prognostic signal.

## Input / Output

The method consumes i.i.d. observational triples (X, T, Y) where X is a high-dimensional covariate vector (d=20 tabular or 8×8 digit images), T is a discrete treatment index in {0,…,K−1} with K ranging from 4 to 100, and Y is a scalar or distributional outcome. Outputs include pairwise individual treatment effects τ_{j,k}(x) defined as 2-Wasserstein distances between estimated conditional outcome distributions, a balanced latent representation Φ(X), and—in the generative extension—counterfactual images and a continuous average dose-response curve. Treatments are consumed via a learnable embedding map e: T → R^{d_e} rather than one-hot encoding, enabling the model to capture topological relationships among treatment levels.

## Method

The core contribution reframes multi-treatment causal representation learning as optimal compression: a multi-treatment generalization bound decomposes ITE error into factual prediction risk, a strategy-dependent representation imbalance term, and an α-dependent Rademacher complexity term, yielding a bilevel estimator where the inner loop trains (Φ, h) for a fixed α and the outer loop selects α by minimizing the empirical bound. Three balancing strategies are proposed—Pairwise (O(K²) MMD), One-vs-All (O(K) MMD), and Treatment Aggregation (O(1) HSIC with learnable treatment embeddings)—with theory showing that aggregation achieves K-independent tuning stability Θ(1/n) versus Θ(K⁴/n) for pairwise. The α-dependent complexity term arises because stronger compression shrinks the effective hypothesis class (via a monotonically decreasing imbalance budget ρ(α)), creating a non-trivial finite-sample trade-off that prevents the degenerate α*=0 solution. The generative extension, Multi-Treatment CausalEGM, replaces the binary CausalEGM design with vectorized treatment embeddings and a multi-class softmax intervention head, and validates geometric fidelity by showing that counterfactual interpolation follows Wasserstein geodesics through the treatment manifold rather than Euclidean shortcuts.

## Results

On the semi-synthetic medium-scale setting (K=4), the proposed Treatment Aggregation (Agg-T) achieves PEHE 0.721, closely matching One-vs-All (0.711) and outperforming Pairwise (0.727) and the unadjusted Base Model (0.796). At large scale (K=20), Agg-T maintains PEHE ≈1.0 while Pairwise degrades catastrophically to >1.3, and the Base Model sits at 1.029. On the UCI Digits generative task, Multi-Treatment CausalEGM achieves PEHE 0.65, a ~17.7% relative reduction over the Base Model (0.79) and competitive with the discriminative Aggregation baseline (0.67). Training time at K=20 is <50 s/epoch for Agg-T versus >850 s/epoch for Pairwise.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Pairwise baseline (Schwab et al., 2018), the proposed Agg-T wins decisively at K=20 (PEHE ≈1.0 vs. >1.3), where 190 conflicting MMD constraints cause representation collapse; at K=4 the two are nearly tied (0.721 vs. 0.727). Against the One-vs-All baseline (Lopez & Gutman, 2017), OVA achieves slightly lower PEHE at both scales (0.711 vs. 0.721 at K=4; ≈0.95 vs. ≈1.0 at K=20), but at O(K) computational cost versus Agg-T's O(1). The practical trade-off is that Agg-T's constant-time HSIC constraint scales to K=100 without degradation, whereas both Pairwise and OVA incur growing per-epoch costs. On the UCI Digits generative task, the Multi-Treatment CausalEGM (PEHE 0.65) outperforms the unadjusted Base Model (0.79) by ~17.7% and is competitive with the discriminative Aggregation baseline (0.67), while additionally producing high-dimensional counterfactual images with verified Wasserstein-geodesic interpolation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the binary-treatment causal representation learning framework of Johansson et al. (2016) and Shalit et al. (2017) to the multi-treatment regime, where prior work (Schwab et al., 2018; Lopez & Gutman, 2017) relied on O(K²) pairwise MMD constraints. The key conceptual departure is reframing the balancing weight α as a statistically estimable quantity derived from a multi-treatment generalization bound, rather than a heuristic hyperparameter, and introducing Treatment Aggregation via HSIC to achieve O(1) complexity. The generative extension builds on CausalEGM (Liu et al., 2024) and Geodesic Causal Inference (Kurisu et al., 2024), adding vectorized treatment embeddings and a Softmax intervention head to recover Wasserstein geodesic structure across a treatment manifold.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that finer-grained pairwise balancing becomes statistically unstable and can actively harm estimation as K grows (variance scaling Θ(K⁴/n) vs. Θ(1/n) for aggregation) challenges the prevailing intuition that more constraints yield better deconfounding, and provides a principled, theory-driven alternative to grid-search tuning of α. The O(1) HSIC-based strategy is practically important for high-cardinality treatment settings (dosage levels, multi-channel interventions) where pairwise methods become intractable. Limitations include reliance on semi-synthetic data for most experiments, the generalization bound being stated in 'schematic form' with constants not fully specified, and the geodesic validation being limited to a simple depth-3 tree. Open questions include extension to continuous treatment spaces, latent confounding, and whether the aggregation strategy's K-independence holds under adversarial or highly structured confounding.
