## Problem

Autoregressive video diffusion models trained on short clips (~5s) suffer from progressive error accumulation when extrapolated to minute-long generation: the standard FIFO KV-cache strategy over-relies on recently generated (error-laden) tokens while indiscriminately evicting the earliest, most informative context, causing color drift, texture blur, and motion degradation. Existing remedies (Rolling Forcing, LongLive) require additional training or distillation to maintain attention sinks, leaving a gap for a training-free solution that stabilizes long-horizon rollouts on top of a pre-trained model.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (refined via Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) and a pre-trained Self Forcing model (Wan-based, 4-step distilled diffusion) as input, requiring no fine-tuning or architectural modification. It produces a real-time frame stream at ~15.75 FPS on a single H100 GPU, supporting 12× length extrapolation from the 5-second training horizon to 60+ seconds. Internally, the KV cache operates on a 21-frame sliding window (1560 tokens per frame) that is compressed to a 16-frame budget (10 sink + 2 selected + 4 recent), and the method also supports interactive mid-generation prompt changes by preserving sink tokens and recomputing the remaining cache.

## Method

Deep Forcing introduces two complementary, training-free mechanisms applied at inference time on a pre-trained Self Forcing backbone. First, Deep Sink preserves approximately 40–60% of the sliding window (10 of 21 frames) as persistent sink tokens, motivated by attention analysis showing that video diffusion attends to both earliest and intermediate frames (unlike LLMs where only a few initial tokens matter); it then applies a temporal RoPE phase shift to realign sink tokens' temporal indices with the current timeline, preventing flickering, fidelity degradation, and roll-back artifacts. Second, Participative Compression performs importance-aware KV-cache pruning by partitioning the cache into sink, candidate, and recent regions, computing query-averaged attention scores between recent queries and candidate keys, and retaining only the top-C most relevant tokens; this compression is triggered only at the first diffusion timestep (t=T) when the window overflows, and a temporal RoPE unification step re-aligns the selected tokens to maintain temporal continuity across all three cache segments. Together, these components enable over 12× length extrapolation while maintaining inference speed comparable to the unmodified baseline.

## Results

On 30-second VBench-Long evaluation, Deep Forcing (training-free) achieves a Dynamic Degree of 57.56, substantially exceeding all baselines (Rolling Forcing: 30.71, LongLive: 45.55, CausVid: 47.21, Self Forcing: 36.62), while maintaining Overall Consistency of 20.54 and Imaging Quality of 69.31 comparable to training-based methods. On 60-second generation, Deep Forcing further outperforms Self Forcing by large margins (Overall Consistency 20.38 vs. 18.63; Aesthetic Quality 59.86 vs. 56.45), demonstrating effective length extrapolation beyond 12× the training clip length. Throughput remains at 15.75 FPS, nearly identical to the Self Forcing baseline (15.78 FPS), confirming no inference-speed penalty.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Self Forcing (the direct base model), Deep Forcing wins decisively on Dynamic Degree (57.56 vs. 36.62 on 30s; 57.19 vs. 31.98 on 60s) and on all quality metrics at 60s (Overall Consistency 20.38 vs. 18.63, Aesthetic Quality 59.86 vs. 56.45), confirming that the training-free KV-cache modifications substantially mitigate error accumulation. Against the training-based baselines Rolling Forcing and LongLive, Deep Forcing achieves comparable or better Overall Consistency (20.54 vs. 20.99/20.16 on 30s) and Imaging Quality (69.31 vs. 70.58/69.07), while dramatically outperforming both on Dynamic Degree (57.56 vs. 30.71/45.55). Deep Forcing trails Rolling Forcing slightly on Overall Consistency (−0.45) and Imaging Quality (−1.27) at 30s, and trails LongLive on Aesthetic Quality (60.68 vs. 61.51), but leads on all other metrics. In the VLM-based visual stability evaluation (Gemini 2.5-Pro), Deep Forcing scores 75.44, approaching LongLive (78.58) and Rolling Forcing (72.60) despite being training-free. In the user study (24 participants, 2AFC), Deep Forcing is preferred over Self Forcing 85.9–87.9% of the time and over Rolling Forcing 76.7–80.0% of the time across color consistency, dynamic motion, subject consistency, and overall quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

Deep Forcing is a training-free extension of Self Forcing (Huang et al., 2025), repurposing two ideas from the LLM literature—attention sinks (StreamingLLM) and importance-aware KV cache compression (H2O, SnapKV, MorphKV)—for autoregressive video diffusion. It departs from concurrent training-based long-video methods (Rolling Forcing, LongLive) that require distillation or fine-tuning to maintain attention sinks, showing instead that the pre-trained Self Forcing model already exhibits a much deeper sink pattern (40–60% of the context window) than the 4-token convention borrowed from LLMs. The method also introduces a video-specific temporal RoPE realignment step that has no analogue in the LLM KV-cache compression literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By matching training-based long-video methods without any fine-tuning, Deep Forcing lowers the barrier to minute-scale autoregressive video generation and suggests that KV-cache management alone can address much of the error-accumulation problem. The finding that video diffusion requires substantially deeper sinks than LLMs challenges the direct transfer of LLM streaming techniques and opens questions about whether similar depth-dependent attention patterns exist in other autoregressive generative modalities. Limitations include a modest increase in hallucinated-motion rate (16.4% vs. 12.6% baseline), dependence on Self Forcing's 4-step distilled denoising schedule, and evaluation primarily on a single base architecture, leaving open whether the approach transfers to fundamentally different video diffusion backbones.
