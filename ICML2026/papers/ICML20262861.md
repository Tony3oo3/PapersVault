## Problem

Existing domain generalization methods enforce invariance through distributional matching or risk-based constraints but do not explicitly model the geometric organization of class-conditional representations. Since deep features concentrate on low-dimensional subspaces, purely distributional invariance can destabilize discriminative geometry, leading to ill-conditioned alignment or representational collapse under finite samples. The paper asks how to shape the geometry of class-conditional subspaces across domains while simultaneously controlling their spectral complexity.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images from multiple source domains (e.g., 7 to 345 classes across 4–6 domains in DomainBed) and produces a single class label per image at test time on unseen target domains. A ResNet-50 backbone extracts features, which are passed through a lightweight geometric projection head into a compact space where subspace statistics are maintained via exponential moving averages; the classifier head then maps these projected features to class logits. No target-domain data or test-time adaptation is required.

## Method

RDI formulates domain generalization as a rate-distortion problem on Grassmann manifolds: for each class, it extracts a top-k principal subspace per domain and jointly optimizes (i) a geometric distortion term measuring cross-domain subspace misalignment via Grassmannian geodesic distance (aggregated principal angles) plus a projection distortion term preventing degenerate subspaces, and (ii) a capacity-regularized rate term that penalizes within-subspace spectral volume using a per-coordinate Gaussian channel capacity objective derived from Rényi entropy. Cross-class rate aggregation uses a Rényi-order escort weighting that interpolates between arithmetic-mean and worst-class regularization. Training alternates between a warm-up phase (classification loss only) and the full RDI objective, with subspaces updated via EMA of feature statistics and top-k eigenvectors recomputed periodically. The paper provides finite-sample recovery guarantees under bounded orthogonal rotations, an information-theoretic generalization bound where the rate term enters linearly, and a proposition showing that geometric alignment alone is insufficient without explicit spectral rate control.

## Results

On the DomainBed benchmark (ResNet-50, training-domain validation), RDI achieves 65.7% average accuracy across five datasets, the best among invariance-based and geometric alignment methods without external anchors. RDI attains the highest accuracy on PACS (87.4%), OfficeHome (69.9%), and TerraIncognita (48.7%), and is competitive on DomainNet (44.2%) and VLCS (78.2%). The closest competitor, GGA, reaches 65.0% average, with RDI ahead by 0.7% overall and by 1.0% on PACS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RDI outperforms all 11 baselines on average accuracy (65.7% vs. 65.0% for the closest, GGA). Against classical invariance methods, RDI leads IRM by 4.1%, VREx by 3.8%, and GroupDRO by 5.0% on average, with the largest margins on DomainNet where these methods degrade severely (33–34% vs. RDI's 44.2%). Among geometric and moment-matching baselines, RDI beats CORAL by 1.1%, Fishr by 1.7%, and SagNet by 1.5%, with the most pronounced gains on PACS (+1.2% over SagNet) and OfficeHome (+1.8% over CORAL). The closest competitor is GGA (65.0%), which RDI surpasses by 0.7% on average; notably, RDI wins on PACS (87.4% vs. 86.4%) and TerraIncognita (48.7% vs. 48.5%) while GGA edges ahead on DomainNet (44.4% vs. 44.2%). LFME (64.6%) is competitive on OfficeHome (69.1% vs. RDI's 69.9%) but trails RDI by 1.1% overall. The paper explicitly excludes flat-minima methods (SWAD, SFT), frozen-anchor methods (MIRO), and training-recipe methods (ERM++) from direct comparison, arguing they are orthogonal and composable with RDI's geometric objective.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes Shannon's classical rate-distortion theory to the Grassmann manifold, combining the subspace-alignment geometry from geodesic flow kernels (Gong et al., 2012) with the Gaussian channel capacity formulation from the deep variational information bottleneck (Alemi et al., 2017). It departs from distributional alignment methods (CORAL, DANN) and mutual-information-based DG (MIRO, IB) by explicitly modeling the geometric organization of class-conditional subspaces rather than marginal statistics, and from prior geometric approaches (ISR, subspace alignment) by adding an explicit spectral-complexity (rate) term that prevents representational collapse. The key conceptual departure is framing DG as a joint optimization of cross-domain subspace alignment (distortion) and within-subspace spectral volume (rate), with the two terms shown to be neither sufficient nor redundant individually.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled information-theoretic lens on DG that connects Grassmannian geometry, Rényi entropy, and finite-sample generalization bounds, offering a unified framework where the necessity of both alignment and spectral control is formally established (Proposition 3.14). However, the theoretical guarantees are restricted to bounded orthogonal rotations, and the empirical gains over strong baselines (GGA, CORAL, LFME) are modest (~0.7–1.1% average), leaving open whether the geometric framing yields advantages at larger scale or under more severe non-rotational shifts. The practical impact is moderate: the method is competitive on DomainBed but does not dramatically outperform existing invariance-based or geometric baselines, and the projection head adds architectural complexity whose standalone contribution is negligible.
