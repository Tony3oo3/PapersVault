## Problem

AI predictive systems increasingly support high-stakes decisions, yet uncertainty quantification in collaborative human-AI settings lacks principled guidelines. Existing conformal prediction methods calibrate AI in isolation, and while human-AI collaboration is studied, no framework specifies how an AI should algorithmically refine a human's prediction set while simultaneously guaranteeing it neither degrades correct human judgments (counterfactual harm) nor fails to recover outcomes the human missed (complementarity).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263479/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes observed features X (images, text, or numeric vectors) and a human-proposed prediction set H(x) ⊆ Y as input, treating the human policy as a black box—only the set is observed, not its generation process. It outputs a collaborative prediction set C(x) ⊆ Y constructed via a two-threshold rule: labels inside H(x) are retained or pruned based on threshold b, while labels outside H(x) are added based on threshold a. The non-conformity score applied to each label depends on its membership in H(x), using a counterfactual-harm-calibrated score for internal labels and a complementarity-calibrated score for external labels.

## Method

The paper proves that the optimal collaborative prediction set minimizing expected size subject to counterfactual harm and complementarity constraints has a closed-form two-threshold structure over the score 1−p(y|x): one threshold b* prunes labels within the human set, and another a* augments labels outside it. For regression, the authors design a novel dual-CQR score that learns two pairs of conditional quantiles—one calibrated to ε for the harm constraint and one to δ for complementarity—so the score naturally separates the two regimes. CUP-Offline estimates the thresholds as empirical quantiles of calibration scores split by human-set membership, yielding distribution-free finite-sample coverage guarantees. CUP-Online updates thresholds sequentially via a simple feedback rule (relax when errors exceed target, tighten when they fall below), achieving distribution-free guarantees under arbitrary distribution shifts, including the novel 'Human-to-AI Adaptation' where human behavior evolves through interaction with the AI.

## Results

On ImageNet-16H (ω=125, Top-2 human sets), CUP-offline achieves 90.2% coverage with average set size 1.49, improving over Human Alone (80.1% / 2.00) and reducing size versus AI Alone (90.7% / 1.65) at matched coverage. On DDXPlus medical diagnosis with GPT-5 (Top-1), CUP reaches 91% coverage at size 1.59 versus Human (71% / 1.00) and AI Alone (91% / 1.76). On Communities & Crime regression (Human A), CUP attains 86.2% coverage with interval width 0.380, beating Human Alone (76.0% / 0.581) and AI Alone (86.2% / 0.394). Across all three modalities, CUP consistently improves coverage over the human baseline while producing smaller sets than the AI-only conformal baseline at equivalent coverage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares CUP against two AI-only conformal baselines: standard conformal prediction (Sadinle et al., 2019) for classification and Conformalized Quantile Regression (Romano et al., 2019) for regression. In classification (ImageNet-16H), CUP consistently produces smaller sets than the AI-alone conformal baseline at matched coverage — e.g., at ω=95 with Top-1 human sets, CUP achieves 97.6% coverage at size 1.43 versus 2.27 for AI Alone, a 37% reduction in set size. In regression (Communities & Crime), CUP similarly yields narrower intervals than CQR at the same coverage level (0.380 vs. 0.394 for Human A at 86.2% coverage). Against the Human Alone baseline, CUP always improves coverage (e.g., 90.2% vs. 80.1% on ImageNet-16H ω=125 Top-2; 91% vs. 71% on DDXPlus Top-1), sometimes at a small cost in set size, but when the AI model is strong (GPT-5), CUP improves both coverage and size simultaneously. The online CUP algorithm additionally outperforms a fixed-threshold baseline under distribution shift, maintaining target coverage while the fixed baseline drifts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends conformal prediction from the AI-in-isolation setting to a collaborative framework where a human expert proposes an initial prediction set and the AI refines it. The optimal two-threshold structure (Theorem 2.1) generalizes the single-threshold characterization of minimum set size conformal prediction (Sadinle et al., 2019; Kiyani et al., 2024) by introducing separate pruning and augmentation thresholds governed by counterfactual harm and complementarity constraints. The conformalized quantile regression score (Romano et al., 2019) is adapted into a two-branch score for regression, and the online algorithm extends adaptive conformal inference (Gibbs & Candès, 2021) to simultaneously control two constraint-specific error rates. Unlike prior human-AI collaboration work (Straitouri et al., 2023, 2024; Babbar et al., 2022) that studies how humans consume AI-provided sets, this paper constructs a single joint prediction set algorithmically integrating both agents' contributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides the first principled, distribution-free method for jointly constructing prediction sets from human and AI inputs with explicit, tunable safeguards against counterfactual harm and guaranteed complementarity. The online algorithm's ability to handle human-to-AI adaptation—where human behavior evolves in response to the AI—addresses a practically critical but previously unaddressed distribution shift in long-term collaborative deployments. Open questions include extending the two-threshold structure to multi-expert settings, handling cases where the human provides structured (non-top-k) sets, and understanding how the framework interacts with iterative multi-round collaboration rather than the single-pass refinement studied here. The reliance on a fixed non-conformity score (trained offline) means the method inherits the AI model's blind spots, and the quality of collaborative sets is bounded by the weaker of the two agents.
