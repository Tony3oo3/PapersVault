## Problem

Continual learning from expert demonstrations is dominated by supervised fine-tuning (SFT), which is inherently off-policy and causes severe catastrophic forgetting when models are adapted to new tasks. On-policy reinforcement learning mitigates forgetting but requires explicit reward functions that are typically unavailable in demonstration-based settings. The paper asks: how can we obtain the benefits of on-policy learning when only demonstrations are available, without needing to infer an explicit reward?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264095/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a task prompt x and a per-instance expert demonstration c (a worked example specific to that query, not a fixed prompt prefix) as input, and produces updated model parameters. The same pretrained model serves dual roles: a student conditioned only on x, and a teacher conditioned on both x and c via a simple prompt template that elicits the model's own reasoning rather than verbatim copying of the demonstration. The teacher is instantiated as an exponential moving average of the student's parameters, updated each training step, and the student generates on-policy rollouts (single trajectory per prompt, up to 2048 tokens) that are scored against the teacher's token-level log-probabilities.

## Method

SDFT performs on-policy self-distillation: for each prompt, the student samples a response y ~ π_θ(·|x), and training minimizes the reverse KL divergence between the student distribution π_θ(·|x) and the demonstration-conditioned teacher distribution π(·|x,c), using an analytic per-token gradient estimator. The key insight is that the model's in-context learning capability, when activated by a specific demonstration, approximates the optimal next policy for that task, making the same model a valid teacher for itself. The paper proves this objective is mathematically equivalent to on-policy RL maximizing an implicit reward r_t = log π(y_t|y_<t,x,c) − log π_k(y_t|y_<t,x), i.e., the log-ratio of teacher to student token probabilities. Unlike prior context distillation work, the conditioning is instance-wise (a different demonstration per query) and the distillation is on-policy (student-generated trajectories), which the paper shows is essential for both generalization and forgetting reduction.

## Results

On Skill Learning, SDFT achieves the highest new-task accuracy across all three domains (70.2% Science Q&A, 70.6% Tool Use, 40.2% Medical) while retaining prior capabilities at 64.5–65.4% average, compared to SFT's 53.4–60.2% average. In Knowledge Acquisition, SDFT reaches 89% strict accuracy and 98% OOD accuracy, substantially outperforming SFT (80% strict, 80% OOD) and approaching oracle RAG (91% strict, 100% OOD). In sequential multi-task learning, SDFT enables stable skill accumulation over three tasks without performance regression, whereas SFT exhibits severe interference and oscillatory behavior.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SDFT outperforms standard SFT on every metric across all three Skill Learning tasks: it gains 4–5 points in new-task accuracy (e.g., 70.2% vs. 66.2% on Science Q&A) while preserving prior capabilities far better (64.5% vs. 53.4% average on the Science Q&A run). Against DFT (Wu et al., 2025b), which uses importance sampling to approximate on-policy updates, SDFT wins on both new-task accuracy (70.2 vs. 54.8 on Science, 70.6 vs. 64.2 on Tool Use) and prior-capability retention (64.5 vs. 60.2 average), indicating that true on-policy distillation is superior to approximate off-policy corrections. Against SFT + Re-invoke (Lu & Lab, 2025), which performs a second on-policy distillation stage to restore degraded capabilities, SDFT achieves higher new-task accuracy (70.2 vs. 66.0 on Science) and better prior retention (64.5 vs. 60.2), while also avoiding the multi-stage training pipeline. In Knowledge Acquisition, SDFT's 89% strict accuracy and 98% OOD accuracy surpass SFT (80%/80%) and CPT (9%/7%), with the OOD gap highlighting that SFT memorizes surface-level answers rather than integrating facts into the model's knowledge base.

## Relation to Prior Work

SDFT sits at the intersection of on-policy distillation (Ross et al., 2011; Agarwal et al., 2024), context distillation (Snell et al., 2022), and in-context learning (Brown et al., 2020). The key departure from prior context distillation is twofold: the distillation is on-policy (student generates its own trajectories) and the teacher context is an instance-specific demonstration rather than a fixed prompt prefix. The paper further shows that the reverse-KL self-distillation objective is mathematically equivalent to maximizing an implicit reward derived from the ICL-conditioned policy, providing an IRL-like interpretation without requiring explicit reward inference or the strong structural assumptions that limit traditional IRL methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SDFT offers a practical, reward-free path to continual learning from expert demonstrations, addressing a core limitation of deployed foundation models that cannot update their parameters post-deployment. The method's simplicity (a single prompt template + on-policy reverse-KL) makes it broadly applicable, and the finding that it preserves reasoning behavior in models trained without chain-of-thought annotations has direct practical value. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond 7B models (the scaling trend is encouraging but untested at frontier scale), how it handles noisy or non-expert demonstrations, and whether the residual forgetting can be further reduced. The ~2.5× FLOPs overhead relative to SFT is a real cost that must be weighed against the multi-stage training pipelines it can replace.
