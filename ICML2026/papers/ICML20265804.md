## Problem

HGR maximal correlation provides a principled nonlinear dependence criterion for multimodal representation learning, but its neural approximations (Soft-HGR, Deep CCA) require explicit K×K covariance matrices or O(K³) eigendecompositions, making them intractable for modern foundation models with K≥1024. Meanwhile, scalable contrastive objectives like InfoNCE do not directly optimize an HGR-style dependence measure and can degrade under noisy or imbalanced modalities. The paper asks whether HGR-style dependence maximization can be reformulated to avoid the covariance bottleneck while retaining its statistical grounding.

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired feature matrices (m×K) produced by any encoder backbone (ViT, ResNet, CLIP, ViCLIP, MultiEMO) for two or more modalities, where m is the local per-device batch size and K is the feature dimension. It outputs a single bounded scalar auxiliary loss that is subtracted from the task loss (L_task − α·J_UniFast), requiring no changes to the encoder architecture or task head. The objective is fully differentiable and operates on normalized rows, so it remains numerically stable even at extreme dimensions (K=10⁵) where covariance-based methods OOM.

## Method

UniFast-HGR replaces explicit covariance whitening with a three-step pipeline: (1) feature-wise standardization followed by row-wise ℓ₂ normalization, turning the paired alignment term into a bounded cosine score; (2) construction of within-view Gram matrices (N×N) from the normalized rows, exploiting the trace identity tr(C_F C_G) = ‖K_fg‖²_F/(N−1)² to express the structural interaction in sample space rather than feature space; and (3) Trivial Spectrum Suppression (TSS), which zeros out the fixed diagonal self-similarities (≈1) before computing the row-wise structural cosine, so the penalty depends only on inter-sample relations. The dominant cost drops from O(K²) memory (Soft-HGR) to O(m²K) computation with O(m²) Gram storage. OptFast-HGR further reduces the structural term to O(mKr) via a shared random projection R∈ℝ^{m×r} with r≪m, sketching the off-diagonal Gram structure without materializing the full m×m matrix.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, UniFast-HGR achieves 85.3% Top-1 accuracy with DINOv2 (+3.5 pp over the 81.8% baseline) and 80.1% with ViT-B/32 (+3.5 pp). On COCO text-image retrieval, CLIP+UniFast-HGR reaches 42.1% Recall@1 versus 38.9% for baseline CLIP. On large-scale video-text retrieval (MSR-VTT, LSMDC, DiDeMo via ViCLIP), UniFast-HGR gains +6.9, +3.6, and +4.1 R@1 points over the respective baselines. On IEMOCAP multimodal emotion recognition, UniFast-HGR achieves 73.66% ACC, outperforming Soft-HGR (71.29%) and all CCA variants by 2.4–6.3 pp.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniFast-HGR consistently outperforms all CCA-family baselines: on Berlin remote sensing classification it reaches 80.75% OA versus 70.93% (CCA), 72.74% (Deep CCA), and 71.54% (Soft CCA); on IEMOCAP it achieves 73.66% ACC versus 67.41% (CCA), 67.78% (Deep CCA), and 68.58% (Soft CCA). Against the closest HGR-based baseline Soft-HGR (Wang et al., 2019), UniFast-HGR gains +3.7 pp on ImageNet-1K (DINOv2: 85.3% vs 81.6%), +1.8 pp on COCO retrieval (CLIP: 42.1% vs 40.3%), and +2.4 pp on IEMOCAP (73.66% vs 71.29%). Among the most recent correlation estimators, UniFast-HGR edges out I_d Cor (Basile et al., 2025) by 0.6 pp on ImageNet-1K DINOv2 (85.3% vs 84.7%) and 0.4 pp on COCO CLIP (42.1% vs 41.7%), and surpasses dCor (Zhen et al., 2022) by 2.9 pp on ImageNet-1K DINOv2 (85.3% vs 82.4%). CKA (Kornblith et al., 2019) trails by 3.2 pp on ImageNet-1K DINOv2 (85.3% vs 82.1%) and 2.6 pp on COCO CLIP (42.1% vs 39.5%). In the scalability microbenchmark at K=10^5, UniFast-HGR (7.20 s, 1.55 GB) matches the efficiency profile of InfoNCE (6.85 s, 1.45 GB), VICReg (7.15 s, 1.62 GB), and Barlow Twins (7.28 s, 1.68 GB), while Soft-HGR and CCA both fail with OOM errors at that dimension.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits directly in the HGR maximal correlation lineage, building on Soft-HGR (Wang et al., 2019), which relaxed exact whitening into a soft covariance regularizer but retained an O(K²) memory bottleneck. UniFast-HGR departs by moving the structural interaction from covariance space into local-batch Gram space, replacing the K×K covariance estimator with a centered, ℓ₂-normalized cosine formulation whose dominant cost is O(m²K) in local batch size m. The key conceptual addition is Trivial Spectrum Suppression (TSS), which removes the invariant diagonal self-correlation from the Gram rows before computing the structural term—a step absent from prior HGR/CCA approximations. The derivation is explicitly framed as HGR → Soft-HGR → Gram-space surrogate, preserving the dependence-maximization target while discarding finite-sample whitening.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is making HGR-style dependence regularization feasible for modern high-dimensional backbones (K ≥ 1024) where covariance-based methods hit OOM, with the scalability microbenchmark showing stable operation at K=10⁵ using ~1.55 GB VRAM versus Soft-HGR's failure. Open questions remain around approximation bias in OptFast-HGR under severe modality asymmetry, the quadratic dependence on local batch size, and whether diagonal suppression can compensate for extremely homogeneous or duplicated batches. The paper also leaves adaptive spectral regularization (adjusting normalization by intrinsic dimensionality) and distribution-aware calibration for long-tail modality pairs as natural next steps.
