## Problem

Function-guided de novo protein design lacks a unified evaluation framework: existing models are assessed on inconsistent, limited subsets of metrics (e.g., only half evaluate foldability, only a quarter assess novelty/diversity), making fair cross-model comparison impossible. Moreover, the correlations among different evaluation criteria have never been systematically investigated, limiting understanding of how these metrics relate and hindering informed model development.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262236/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes two types of functional input: (1) free-text natural language descriptions of protein function (from the Mol-Instructions test set, 5,876 proteins) for the description-guided task, and (2) structured InterPro (IPR) and/or Gene Ontology (GO) keyword sets (from the newly curated SwissTest, 1,057 proteins with a strict Jan–Aug 2025 datetime cutoff to prevent data leakage) for the keyword-guided task, which has three fine-grained sub-tasks (GO-only, IPR-only, IPR+GO). Keywords are optionally converted into textual descriptions via templated prompts so that text-input models can participate. The output is a novel protein sequence over the 20 standard amino acids, which is then scored across 16 metrics spanning 6 dimensions (Plausibility, Foldability, Language Alignment, Similarity, Novelty, Diversity).

## Method

PDFBench is an evaluation benchmark rather than a new generative model. It systematically benchmarks 8 state-of-the-art design methods (ProDVa, Pinal, PAAG, Chroma, ProteinDT for description-guided; ESM3, CFP-Gen, ProteoGAN for keyword-guided) under identical protocols using official weights and 3-run averaging. The 16 metrics include sequence perplexity from three protein language models (ProtGPT2, ProGen2, RITA), a novel biologically-informed Repeat metric, ESMFold-based pLDDT/PAE for foldability, ProTrek and EvoLlama embedding scores plus IPR/GO recovery rates and retrieval accuracy for language alignment, ESM-BERTScore/MMseqs2/TMscore for similarity, and MMseqs2/Foldseek-based novelty and diversity. A key methodological contribution is the fairness analysis: hard overlaps are eliminated by excluding MolinstTest from all training sets and applying the SwissTest datetime cutoff, while soft overlaps (similar functions) are quantified via ProTrek cosine similarity and shown to have negligible or even positive impact on results. The paper further provides an empirical correlation study (e.g., PPL Pearson r = 0.76 with pLDDT, −0.87 with PAE; Repeat > 10 associated with poor foldability; retrieval-strategy gaps up to 66.31%) that guides practitioners on which metrics can serve as cheap proxies and which require careful interpretation.

## Results

On the description-guided task (MolinstTest, 5,876 proteins), ProDVa achieves the best foldability (pLDDT 76.86, PAE 8.66) and retrieval accuracy (66.83%), while Pinal attains the lowest perplexity (5.81) and highest EvoLlama Score (53.40). Both models substantially outperform all other baselines on language alignment (ProTrek Score ~17 vs. ≤2.10 for others) but score poorly on novelty (ProDVa Seq: 14.64%, Struct: 36.31%) and diversity (ProDVa Seq: 46.10%, Struct: 52.59%). On the keyword-guided task (SwissTest, 1,057 proteins), ProDVa again leads in foldability (pLDDT 72.80–74.73, PAE 6.11–8.06), while CFP-Gen achieves the lowest perplexity (4.94–5.23) and highest IPR Recovery (up to 35.21%) among keyword-specific models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProDVa and Pinal dominate all other baselines on plausibility, foldability, and language alignment: ProDVa achieves pLDDT 76.86 vs. 59.18 (Chroma, the next best) and retrieval accuracy 66.83% vs. 16.92% (ProteinDT, next best), while Pinal matches closely (pLDDT 75.25, retrieval 63.43%). However, both sacrifice novelty and diversity dramatically—ProDVa's sequence novelty is only 14.64% (vs. 70.74% for ProteinDT) and structural diversity is 52.59% (vs. 83.67% for ProteinDT), indicating they generate sequences close to the natural protein landscape. Among keyword-guided baselines, CFP-Gen outperforms ProteoGAN, ESM3, ProteinDT, Chroma, and PAAG on IPR Recovery (up to 35.21% vs. ≤20.17%) and GO Recovery (up to 23.41% vs. ≤15.43%), but still trails Pinal and ProDVa on retrieval accuracy. ProDVa consistently achieves the best foldability across all three keyword sub-tasks (PAE as low as 6.11), while ESM3 produces the most repetitive sequences (Repeat 28.13%) despite competitive perplexity (6.33). The paper also shows that retrieval-based metrics are highly sensitive to the sampling strategy: the gap between Hard and Soft Retrieval Accuracy reaches 66.31% even for natural proteins, cautioning against interpreting absolute retrieval scores in isolation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper does not introduce a new protein design method but rather carves out a new evaluation paradigm for the function-guided de novo protein design task. Prior methods (Chroma, ProteinDT, PAAG, Pinal, ProDVa, ESM3, CFP-Gen, ProteoGAN) each reported results using inconsistent and limited subsets of metrics, making cross-method comparison unreliable. PDFBench repurposes the Mol-Instructions instruction-following dataset (which previously lacked quantitative protein evaluation) and introduces SwissTest with a strict datetime cutoff to create the first unified benchmark spanning 16 metrics across 6 dimensions. The metric toolkit draws on existing tools (ESMFold, ProTrek, MMseqs2, Foldseek, InterProScan, DeepGO-SE) but combines them into a comprehensive, standardized protocol for the first time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PDFBench provides the field with a fair, reproducible evaluation framework that was previously absent, enabling meaningful comparison across the rapidly growing set of function-guided protein design models. The revealed trade-off—models excelling in foldability and language alignment (ProDVa, Pinal) sacrifice novelty and diversity—highlights an open design challenge: how to simultaneously achieve functional fidelity and exploration of novel sequence/structure space. The finding that Retrieval Accuracy varies by up to 66.31% depending on the negative sampling strategy calls into question the reliability of a widely used metric and suggests the field needs standardized retrieval protocols. Wet-lab validation remains the gold standard but is impractical at scale, leaving computational metrics as the primary (imperfect) proxy, and the paper's correlation analyses (PPL↔pLDDT, Repeat↔foldability) offer practical shortcuts for resource-constrained evaluation.
