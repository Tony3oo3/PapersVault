## Problem

Existing temporal grounding research assumes a one-to-one correspondence between a query and a single video segment, but real-world videos contain recurring events where one query maps to multiple disjoint temporal intervals. State-of-the-art MLLMs, optimized for single-segment retrieval, fail catastrophically in this One-to-Many setting—often yielding near-zero scores—because they lack event cardinality perception and cannot distinguish multiple occurrences from a single continuous span.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265287/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a video (sampled at 2 FPS with min_pixels=2048, total_pixels=8388608) and a natural language query describing a recurring event, and produces a free-form text response containing Chain-of-Thought dense captions followed by a structured list of (start, end) timestamp pairs for every occurrence. A deterministic parsing function extracts the predicted segment set from the text, which is then matched against ground-truth intervals via Hungarian bipartite matching at multiple IoU thresholds. Videos in the benchmark span 21 seconds to over 17 minutes, with 2–20 ground-truth segments per query.

## Method

The approach uses Qwen3-VL-4B as backbone with a two-stage training pipeline: SFT on 46k curated OMTG samples (plus 32k TimeLens-100k samples) to establish foundational one-to-many grounding with CoT reasoning, followed by RL via GRPO on 10k OMTG samples with a composite reward R = 0.5·R_tIoU + 0.5·R_C-Acc + 0.5·R_Caption − 0.3·R_Length. The key novelty is the caption reward, which employs an LLM-as-Judge (Qwen3-30B-A3B) to score generated dense captions on coverage, precision, and discriminability, plus a text-only grounding score that verifies captions contain sufficient semantic information to independently localize events. The C-Acc reward acts as a strict cardinality gate that activates only when predicted segment count exactly matches ground truth, directly correcting the model's event-counting perception. Training data is produced by a five-stage automated pipeline (repetitive event discovery → initial grounding → strict visual verification with one-vote-veto → recall check and query refinement → query-guided dense captioning) leveraging Qwen3-VL-235B and Gemini 2.5 Pro, yielding 56k high-fidelity samples.

## Results

On the proposed OMTG Bench, OMTG-4B achieves a state-of-the-art Effective Temporal F1 (EtF1) of 43.65%, Count Accuracy (C-Acc) of 55.63%, and tIoU of 61.24%, outperforming the best proprietary baselines Gemini 2.5 Pro (EtF1 27.80%) and Seed-1.8 (EtF1 28.04%) by 15.85% and 15.61% respectively. All open-source MLLMs (Qwen2.5-VL, Qwen3-VL, VideoChat-R1, Time-R1, UniTime, TimeLens) yield near-zero EtF1 (≤0.21%), revealing a critical capability gap in event cardinality perception. On the standard One-to-One Temporal Grounding (OOTG) TimeLens benchmark, OMTG-4B(RL) further improves over the Qwen3-VL-4B backbone, reaching R1@0.5 of 58.3% on Charades, 54.6% on ActivityNet, and 70.4% on QVHighlights.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OMTG-4B decisively outperforms all proprietary baselines on the OMTG Bench: it surpasses Seed-1.8 (EtF1 28.04) by 15.61 points, Gemini-2.5-Pro (EtF1 27.80) by 15.85 points, and Gemini-3-Pro (EtF1 21.30) by 22.35 points, while also leading on C-Acc (55.63 vs. 50.94 for Gemini-2.5-Pro) and tIoU (61.24 vs. 56.81 for Seed-1.8). Against all open-source MLLMs—including Qwen2.5-VL (3B–72B), Qwen3-VL (4B–235B), VideoChat-R1, Time-R1, UniTime, and TimeLens—the gap is stark, as these models achieve near-zero EtF1 (≤0.21%) and 0% C-Acc, indicating a fundamental inability to perceive event cardinality. On the standard One-to-One Temporal Grounding (OOTG) TimeLens benchmark, OMTG-4B(RL) also wins: it beats Qwen3-VL-4B (R1@0.5: 47.5/51.0/66.0) and TimeLens-7B (55.6/51.0/62.7) across Charades, ActivityNet, and QVHighlights, and even surpasses proprietary GPT-5 (R1@0.5: 42.0/44.9/60.4) and Gemini-2.5-Pro (61.1/64.2/75.9) on Charades and ActivityNet, though Gemini-2.5-Pro retains a lead on QVHighlights (75.9 vs. 70.4). Notably, the RL stage trained exclusively on OMTG data improves OOTG performance over SFT, suggesting that one-to-many training cultivates more generalizable temporal grounding capabilities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the well-studied temporal grounding task from its standard one-to-one formulation (single query → single segment) to a one-to-many setting where a single query must retrieve all disjoint occurrences of a repeated event. Methodologically, it builds on the GRPO reinforcement learning framework popularized by DeepSeekMath and adapted to video MLLMs by VideoChat-R1 and Time-R1, but repurposes the reward design to handle set-level predictions rather than single-interval localization. The data construction pipeline repurposes existing proprietary MLLMs (Gemini 2.5 Pro, Qwen3-VL-235B) as annotators and verifiers, while the evaluation introduces a bipartite-matching-based metric suite (C-Acc, EtF1) that departs from the tIoU/R@1 paradigm inherited from one-to-one benchmarks like Charades and ActivityNet.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a concrete and previously unmeasured capability gap: even the largest open-source MLLMs (Qwen3-VL-235B) achieve near-zero effective grounding when a query has multiple valid segments, suggesting that event cardinality perception is a distinct skill not captured by standard temporal grounding training. The EtF1 metric and the OMTG Bench provide the field with tools to track this dimension going forward. However, the benchmark's small size (340 samples) limits statistical confidence, the data pipeline depends on proprietary models for annotation (raising reproducibility concerns), and the authors acknowledge scalability challenges for very long videos. An open question is whether the observed transfer from OMTG-RL to one-to-one TG generalizes beyond the Qwen3-VL-4B backbone or is an artifact of the specific data mixture.
