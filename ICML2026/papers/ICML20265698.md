## Problem

Standard sparse MoE architectures rely on a non-differentiable hard top-k operator to select experts, which forces a fixed number of active experts per token and prevents gradient-based optimization of routing decisions. This rigidity means expert capacity cannot be adaptively allocated across layers or tokens, leading to inefficient use of computation. The paper asks whether a differentiable routing mechanism can enable adaptive expert allocation while preserving autoregressive compatibility and computational efficiency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265698/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes sequences of text tokens (BPE, 32k vocabulary, sequence length 2048) through a 10-layer GPT-2 decoder with 32 experts per layer, producing next-token predictions via standard autoregressive language modeling. Unlike fixed-k sparse MoE, the number of active experts per token is input-dependent: the soft routing weights are truncated at a learned threshold, so simple tokens may activate only one expert while complex tokens activate more. The per-layer expert budget k_l is itself a learned parameter, so the effective compute per token varies both across tokens and across layers.

## Method

SoftMoE replaces the discrete top-k operator with a truncated soft top-k based on the LapSum relaxation: a differentiable threshold x̃ is found in closed form by solving LapSum(x) = k (a sum of Laplace CDFs), yielding soft selection weights in [0,1] that sum to k. A truncation operator T(z) = z·𝕀(z > τ) then zeros out low-contribution weights to recover sparsity, making the number of active experts input-dependent while preserving gradient flow for active experts. Crucially, the LapSum inverse provides a gradient pathway with respect to the selection parameter k, enabling end-to-end optimization of per-layer expert budgets k_l under a global constraint Σk_l = K, reparametrized via softmax over unconstrained parameters η to satisfy k_l ≥ 1. This learned allocation introduces competition between layers, and the model discovers highly non-uniform patterns where later layers absorb roughly 50% of the total expert budget.

## Results

On OWT with a ≤2 expert budget, SoftMoE* (k=1.5) achieves a validation loss of 2.78 while activating only 1.73 experts per token on average, compared to Sparse MoE k=2 which achieves 2.79 with exactly 2.0 active experts. On C4 with a ≤3 budget, SoftMoE (k=1.5, learned allocation) matches Sparse MoE k=3 at loss 2.22 while activating only 2.19 experts versus 3.0. Under the ≤4 budget on OWT, SoftMoE* (k=1, α=4) reaches loss 2.70 with 3.73 active experts, outperforming Sparse MoE k=4 (loss 2.75, 4.0 experts) by 0.05 in loss while using fewer experts. On HellaSwag, SoftMoE consistently achieves the highest accuracy across configurations (e.g., 38.27% on OWT ≤4 vs. 36.68% for Sparse MoE k=4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the Switch Transformer sparse MoE architecture (Fedus et al., 2022) with fixed top-k routing (k=1,2,3,4). SoftMoE* (soft routing without learned allocation) consistently matches or beats Sparse MoE at the same or lower expert budget: on OWT ≤2, SoftMoE* achieves loss 2.78 with 1.73 active experts vs. Sparse MoE k=2 at 2.79 with 2.0; on C4 ≤4, SoftMoE* (k=1, α=4) reaches loss 2.19 with 3.82 experts vs. Sparse MoE k=4 at 2.20 with 4.0. With learned expert allocation, SoftMoE further improves: on OWT ≤2 it drops loss to 2.76 (best in group) and on C4 ≤3 it matches Sparse MoE k=3 loss (2.22) while activating only 2.19 experts instead of 3.0. On HellaSwag, soft routing variants outperform Sparse MoE in every comparable configuration on both datasets (e.g., 33.68% vs. 32.36% on OWT ≤4; 48.48% vs. 47.23% on C4 ≤4). On ARC-E, results are more variable due to the smaller evaluation set, but SoftMoE achieves the best OWT score (38.27%) and remains competitive on C4. Overall, SoftMoE wins or ties on language modelling loss in all budget groups while activating fewer experts, and wins on HellaSwag across all configurations.

## Relation to Prior Work

SoftMoE builds directly on the LapSum differentiable top-k operator from Struski et al. (2025), applying it to MoE expert routing for the first time in a language-modeling context. It departs from prior soft-routing approaches (token mixing in Puigcerver et al. 2024, expert-choice in Zhou et al. 2022, linear assignment in Lewis et al. 2021) by processing each token independently, thereby preserving autoregressive compatibility without cross-token information leakage. The paper also extends the standard Switch Transformer (Fedus et al., 2022) baseline by introducing a learnable, globally-constrained per-layer expert budget—a mechanism absent from all prior sparse MoE architectures that fix k a priori.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to learn non-uniform expert allocation across layers under a fixed compute budget opens a practical path toward depth-aware MoE design, with the empirical finding that later layers consistently absorb ~50% of the expert budget providing actionable guidance for architecture design. However, the experiments are limited to a 1.63B-parameter model on English corpora, leaving open whether the learned allocation patterns and efficiency gains persist at the tens-of-billions scale where routing instabilities are more pronounced. Broader validation across languages, modalities, and larger model sizes would be needed before the approach can be considered a drop-in replacement for hard top-k routing in production MoE systems.
