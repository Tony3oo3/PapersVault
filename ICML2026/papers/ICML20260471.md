## Problem

Existing trajectory planners for autonomous driving are either static models that cannot adapt to new long-tail scenarios or rule-based controllers with limited behavioral generalization. A critical gap is the inability to continuously acquire new driving skills from streaming data without catastrophic forgetting, where learning rare scenarios degrades previously mastered behaviors. NOMAD targets this stability-plasticity dilemma in lifelong closed-loop planning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260471/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The planner consumes vectorized HD map elements (lane centerlines, road boundaries, crosswalks) and surrounding agent tracks (position, velocity, heading over a historical window T_h) as structured inputs rather than raw sensor images. It outputs a future trajectory of T_f waypoints, each specifying 2D position (x, y) and heading angle θ. The system operates under a non-stationary data stream where the scenario distribution P_t(O) shifts over time (e.g., from grid-city traffic to rural roundabouts), and the model must adapt without storing raw historical observations.

## Method

NOMAD maps continuous scene embeddings to a dynamically growing set of discrete memory clusters via a Dirichlet Process Mixture Model (DPMM) with memoized Variational Bayes, which automatically triggers birth moves for novel long-tail scenarios and merge moves for redundant clusters without a predefined cluster count. A single conditional diffusion backbone is then modulated by the cluster assignment c (via AdaLN) to function as a mixture of scenario-specialized experts, while the scene embedding z provides instance-level context through cross-attention. Training uses a disentangled alternating scheme: the encoder and DPMM are updated first (with the encoder minimized via KL divergence to assigned cluster components), then the diffusion planner is trained with the upper part frozen. Catastrophic forgetting is mitigated by generative replay, where a frozen copy of the previous model synthesizes pseudo-trajectories from learned Gaussian cluster components, which are mixed with new data during updates. At inference, classifier guidance with differentiable energy functions (speed maintenance, jerk comfort, drivable-area constraints) steers the reverse diffusion process toward safe trajectories without retraining.

## Results

On the interPlan long-tail benchmark, NOMAD achieves a closed-loop score of 70, outperforming the strongest baseline SAH-Drive (64) by 9.4%. On regular driving splits, NOMAD also leads with Val14 reactive score of 95 and Test14-Random reactive score of 93. On the harder Test14-Hard split, NOMAD scores 85 (reactive) and 78 (non-reactive), again surpassing all baselines. In continual learning experiments, NOMAD shows progressively increasing average closed-loop score with positive backward transfer as new long-tail scenarios are sequentially introduced.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NOMAD outperforms all 15 baselines on the interPlan long-tail benchmark, with the largest margin over SAH-Drive (+6 points, 70 vs 64) and CarPlanner (+10, 70 vs 60). Among learning-based planners, Diffusion-ES (57) and LiloDriver (56) are the closest competitors but trail by 13 and 14 points respectively. On regular driving splits (Val14, Test14-Random), NOMAD also leads or ties: it achieves 95 on Val14(R) vs. 93 for STR2 and 92 for PDM-Closed, and 93 on Test14-Random(R) vs. 91 for CarPlanner and PDM-Closed. On Test14-Hard, NOMAD (85R/78NR) edges out SAH-Drive (83R/78NR) and CarPlanner (80R/72NR). Rule-based baselines (PDM-Closed, IDM) and early learning methods (Urban Driver, GameFormer, PlanTF, DTPP) are substantially outperformed across all splits, with interPlan scores ranging from 4 to 42 versus NOMAD's 70.

## Relation to Prior Work

NOMAD sits at the intersection of three research threads: diffusion-based trajectory planning (Diffusion Planner, Diffusion-ES), non-parametric Bayesian clustering (DPMM with memoVB), and continual learning for autonomous driving (LiloDriver, LoRD). It departs from LiloDriver's LLM-plus-memory-bank approach by replacing semantic retrieval with a purely statistical, non-parametric clustering mechanism that automatically discovers scenario structure without predefined categories. It also diverges from hybrid planners like SAH-Drive that rely on heuristic switching between rule-based and learned modules, instead learning scenario-adaptive behaviors end-to-end through a shared diffusion backbone conditioned on discrete memory clusters. The generative replay mechanism is a notable departure from standard replay-based continual learning, as it synthesizes pseudo-experiences using the planner's own generative capacity rather than storing raw historical data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that lifelong trajectory planning can be achieved without storing raw driving data, which has practical implications for privacy and deployment in real-world AV systems. The non-parametric memory design means the system can handle an unbounded number of scenario types without retraining or manual cluster specification, addressing a key scalability concern for open-world deployment. However, the method still struggles in semantically sparse environments (e.g., parking lots) where scene embeddings lack discriminative structure, and the 1.5-second inference time, while competitive, remains a consideration for high-frequency control loops. Open questions include how the DPMM scales as the number of clusters grows over a vehicle's operational lifetime, and whether the generative replay fidelity degrades over many sequential adaptation cycles.
