## Problem

Despite the empirical success of Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models, it remains unclear why they outperform dense networks beyond sheer parameter scaling. Existing theoretical analyses either grant MoEs a larger total parameter count (trivial capacity advantage) or are limited to uncommon routing settings, failing to isolate the inherent architectural benefit under a fair iso-parameter comparison. This paper identifies and analyzes a previously unexplored mechanism: sparse expert activation acts as a structural noise filter for feature noise.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264306/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The theoretical framework takes a block-diagonal design matrix X (representing k specialized feature blocks) corrupted by additive Gaussian feature noise E ~ N(0, σ²I), producing a noisy observation X̄ = X + E, and outputs minimum-norm parameter estimates for both dense and sparse (per-expert) estimators along with closed-form generalization error expressions. Empirically, the method consumes frozen LLM activations (T5-small, Llama-2-7B, Llama-3.1-8B) or RGB images (ImageNet-C at 224px/384px) and produces class labels or Top-1 accuracy under controlled noise perturbations. A key constraint is the iso-parameter regime: the MoE's total parameter count exactly matches the dense baseline, while only a fraction of parameters are active per forward pass.

## Method

The paper establishes that under a latent block-diagonal feature structure, the Bayes optimal sparse (MoE-like) estimator achieves strictly lower generalization error than the dense estimator because the per-expert covariance Σ_i dominates the down-weighted p_iΣ_i appearing in the dense case, yielding (Σ_i + σ²I)⁻¹ ≼ (p_iΣ_i + σ²I)⁻¹. This noise-filtering advantage extends to robustness under input perturbations (Theorem 4.3), faster gradient-descent convergence via singular-value perturbation analysis (Theorem 4.7), and improved sample complexity. Routing is shown to reduce to a tractable QDA classification problem with near-perfect accuracy under polynomial sample complexity (Theorem 4.1). Empirical validation spans three levels: MoE-based linear probes on frozen T5-small activations outperform Lasso/Ridge/Elastic-Net baselines under Gaussian and realistic text noise; MiniMind language models trained from scratch with matched FFN parameters show faster convergence and lower validation loss; and a Sparse V-MoE-B/16 achieves up to +13.08% Top-1 accuracy over a Dense ViT-L/16 on ImageNet-C at severity 5 while activating only 37% of parameters.

## Results

On ImageNet-C under Gaussian corruption, the sparse V-MoE-B/16 matches the dense ViT-L/16 on clean accuracy (84.82% vs. 84.33%) but achieves progressively larger robustness gains as noise severity increases: +1.70% at severity 1, +3.72% at severity 3, and +13.08% at severity 5 (59.65% vs. 46.57% top-1 accuracy). On T5-small linear probing under high-intensity Gaussian noise (σ=2.0), the MoE probe shows the smallest performance drop on 3 of 4 datasets (e.g., 8.60% drop on SST-2 vs. 10.78% for Lasso and 12.27% for Ridge). The V-MoE achieves these gains while activating only 37% of the parameters per forward pass (114M vs. 307M active parameters).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary end-to-end comparison is between the dense ViT-L/16 (Dosovitskiy et al., 2021) and the sparse V-MoE-B/16 (Riquelme et al., 2021) on ImageNet-C. The MoE matches clean accuracy (+0.49%) but decisively outperforms the dense baseline under corruption, with the gap widening from +1.70% at severity 1 to +13.08% at severity 5, confirming that the robustness advantage stems from sparse modular routing rather than higher total capacity. In the linear probing setting (Table 1), the MoE probe outperforms all three regularized dense baselines (Lasso, Ridge, Elastic Net) on 3 of 4 datasets under σ=2.0 Gaussian noise, with the largest margin on CoLA (7.67% drop vs. 10.25% for Lasso and 12.39% for Ridge). The one exception is AG News, where Elastic Net (3.92%) slightly beats the MoE (7.75%), suggesting the advantage is task-dependent. The MiniMind from-scratch training experiments further show the MoE matching or exceeding the dense baseline's convergence speed and sample efficiency despite using only ~60% of the active parameters per token.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by identifying feature-noise robustness as a distinct mechanism underlying MoE advantages, departing from prior theoretical work that either grants MoEs a parameter-count advantage (Chen et al., 2022) or studies adversarial/domain-shift robustness (Puigcerver et al., 2022; Li et al., 2023a). It draws on the error-in-variable regression literature for its noisy-feature analysis and on activation-sparsity methods (TEAL, MoEfication) for empirical motivation, but the core contribution—a strict iso-parameter comparison showing that sparse expert activation acts as a structural noise filter under block-diagonal feature geometry—has no direct precedent. The linear-model framework is a deliberate simplification that connects to the linear-probing and linear-representation-hypothesis literature in LLM interpretability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical explanation for why MoEs can outperform dense models at matched parameter count, grounding the practical success of architectures like Mixtral in a concrete noise-filtering mechanism rather than mere capacity. This has direct implications for MoEfication and dense-to-sparse conversion techniques, suggesting that the structural benefits of sparsity are not merely computational but also statistical. Key limitations include the reliance on a linear model, the assumption of near-perfect routing, and the idealized block-diagonal structure; whether the mechanism fully transfers to deep nonlinear networks with jointly trained routers and more complex noise correlations remains an open question. The sample-complexity result is presented as a hypothesis with empirical support rather than a theorem, leaving a gap in the theoretical characterization.
