## Problem

Existing feature caching methods for Diffusion Transformer acceleration rely on direct reuse or Taylor-series extrapolation of historical features, which incurs severe error accumulation at high acceleration ratios. The paper asks whether the requisite feature correction between cached and full-computation representations has an exploitable structure that enables efficient, learnable calibration.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and produces a generated image (1024×1024 on FLUX.1-dev) or video (544×960×49 on HunyuanVideo, 832×480×81 on WAN 2.1-1.3B). During inference, at skipped timesteps the model bypasses all transformer blocks except the final one, feeding the cached final-block input from the nearest full-compute step into a LoRA-augmented final block. Training requires only 3–5 calibration prompts (drawn from PartiPrompts and extended via GPT-5.2), with the resulting LoRA module weighing under 0.6 GB.

## Method

The key insight is that the optimal linear correction ΔW mapping cached features at timestep t to full-computation features at timestep t−k is inherently low-rank and prompt-invariant, demonstrated via SVD analysis showing rapid singular-value decay of the aggregated input matrix and consistently small angles between principal subspaces across disjoint prompt groups. LearniBridge parameterizes this correction as LoRA adapters (rank r=64) applied exclusively to all linear layers (Q, K, V, O, W₁, W₂) of the final transformer block g_L, keeping the base DiT weights frozen. Training minimizes an L2 reconstruction loss between the LoRA-augmented final block output and the ground-truth full-computation output over 3–5 prompts, requiring only 0.33–2.28 GPU-hours. At inference, full computation at target timesteps is entirely skipped; the cached final-block input is fed directly through the LoRA-augmented g_L to produce the calibrated feature, yielding up to 5.87×, 5.75×, and 4.10× speedup on FLUX, HunyuanVideo, and WAN 2.1 respectively.

## Results

On FLUX.1-dev (DrawBench), LearniBridge achieves up to 6.17× latency speedup (N=8) while maintaining the highest ImageReward (0.8308), CLIP score (0.8164), PSNR (28.35), and SSIM (0.687) among all compared methods at that acceleration level. On HunyuanVideo, it reaches 6.15× speedup with a VBench score of 79.51, outperforming TaylorSeer (79.14) at comparable speed. On WAN 2.1-1.3B, LearniBridge achieves 4.10× speedup with VBench 80.51, improving over the previous SOTA (TeaCache, 79.23) by 1.28% at a similar acceleration ratio, while also achieving the best PSNR (16.32) and SSIM (0.6254).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LearniBridge consistently outperforms all three main baselines (ToCa, TeaCache, TaylorSeer) on FLUX.1-dev at matched acceleration levels: at ~4.4× speedup it achieves the best ImageReward (0.9590 vs. 0.9359 for TaylorSeer) and PSNR (30.15 vs. 29.86), while at ~6.2× it leads on every quality metric (IR 0.8308, CLIP 0.8164, PSNR 28.35, SSIM 0.687, LPIPS 0.3549). On HunyuanVideo, LearniBridge (N=4) achieves VBench 80.84 at 3.75×, beating TaylorSeer (80.74 at 3.83×) and TeaCache (80.07 at 3.68×); at 6.15× it scores 79.51 vs. TaylorSeer's 79.14. On WAN 2.1-1.3B, LearniBridge (N=4) achieves VBench 81.21 at 3.49×, surpassing TeaCache (80.04 at 3.57×) and TaylorSeer (79.17 at 3.46×), and at 4.10× it reaches 80.51 vs. TeaCache's 79.23 at 3.98×. EasyCache (only compared on WAN 2.1) is clearly inferior, achieving only 79.61 VBench at 3.27× with substantially worse PSNR (13.77) and SSIM (0.4745). Overall, LearniBridge wins on all quality metrics at every acceleration level tested across all three models, with the largest margins appearing at higher speedups where error accumulation most severely degrades the baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the feature caching paradigm for diffusion model acceleration (DeepCache, FORA, ToCa, TeaCache, TaylorSeer) by replacing the reliance on historical feature extrapolation with a learned calibration. The core mechanism repurposes LoRA (Hu et al., 2022) — originally designed for parameter-efficient fine-tuning of language models — as a lightweight cross-timestep correction applied exclusively to the final Transformer block. The key conceptual departure from prior caching methods is the identification of a prompt-invariant, low-rank subspace structure in the correction matrices, which provides a theoretical justification for why a small, fixed set of LoRA parameters can generalize across diverse prompts without per-prompt adaptation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LearniBridge offers a practical, plug-and-play acceleration path (4–6×) for state-of-the-art DiT-based image and video generators with negligible inference overhead (<0.6 GB LoRA module) and minimal training cost (3–5 prompts, <2.3 GPU-hours). The prompt-invariant low-rank finding is a useful structural insight that could inform other calibration or compression tasks in diffusion models. Open questions include whether the low-rank assumption holds for fundamentally different architectures (e.g., MMDiT with dual streams), whether the method degrades gracefully beyond ~6× acceleration, and whether the final-block-only restriction can be relaxed to capture inter-block interactions at higher speedups.
