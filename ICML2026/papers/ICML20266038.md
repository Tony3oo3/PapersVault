## Problem

Goal-conditioned foundation policies are highly sensitive to prompt choice, yet the two standard adaptation strategies—discrete prompt engineering and full policy fine-tuning—sit at opposite ends of a trade-off: the former lacks optimization power, while the latter risks catastrophic forgetting and overfitting to narrow task distributions. The paper asks whether there exists an intermediate adaptation mechanism that offers the optimization power of fine-tuning while preserving the structural stability and generalization of a frozen policy.

## Input / Output

The method takes an initial prompt (text or video) encoded into a 512-dimensional latent goal vector g, which conditions a frozen goal-conditioned policy π(a|s,g) over environment observations (RGB frames, video). During training, the frozen policy generates ~100 rollouts per iteration; trajectories are labeled as positive or negative based on cumulative reward or human ranking, forming preference pairs. The output is a single optimized 512-dim latent goal vector (versus ~86M parameters for full fine-tuning) that, when fed to the unchanged policy, induces a trajectory distribution aligned with task preferences. The policy itself outputs discrete or continuous action sequences depending on the domain (Minecraft key-presses or robotic end-effector commands).

## Method

PGT reformulates post-training as a latent control problem: the policy parameters θ are frozen, and all adaptation occurs by optimizing the continuous goal embedding g through a DPO-style preference objective over trajectory likelihood ratios. The key derivation shows that under shared environment dynamics, the trajectory log-likelihood ratio p_{θ,g}(τ)/p_{θ,g_ref}(τ) factors into a sum of per-step policy log-ratios, canceling all dynamics terms and yielding a tractable step-wise loss L(g) = −E[log σ(βΔ)] where Δ aggregates the difference in log-likelihoods between preferred and dispreferred trajectories. Training proceeds iteratively: encode an initial prompt into g₀, collect rollouts, construct preference pairs from the best and worst trajectories, update g via full-gradient descent (Adam, lr=1e-2), then recollect with the updated goal—typically converging in 2–3 iterations. The theoretical grounding frames this as minimizing KL divergence between an energy-defined target distribution (implied by preferences) and the frozen policy's induced trajectory family, with DPO, IPO, and SLiC-HF shown to be interchangeable convex surrogates for the same underlying logit. Because only 512 parameters are optimized and the policy backbone is untouched, task interference is avoided by construction, enabling plug-and-play continual learning where each task stores its own compact latent goal.

## Results

On the Minecraft SkillForge benchmark (17 tasks), PGT achieves average relative improvements of 72.0% on GROOT and 81.6% on STEVE-1 in-distribution, with 73.8% and 36.9% under out-of-distribution conditions. PGT uses only 512 trainable parameters (the latent goal vector) versus 86M for full fine-tuning, yet surpasses full fine-tuning by 13.4% in OOD settings. On the LIBERO-goal robotic manipulation benchmark, PGT applied to OpenVLA delivers higher performance and sample efficiency than PPO-based VLA-RL while matching GRAPE with a simpler preference-ranking function.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against parameter-efficient fine-tuning baselines (LoRA, BitFit, VeRA), PGT is competitive in-distribution but consistently outperforms all three in OOD settings (e.g., on collect_obsidian OOD: PGT 8.2 vs. LoRA 10.6, BitFit 6.2, VeRA 2.6; on collect_grass OOD: PGT 25.8 vs. LoRA 19.6, BitFit 20.8, VeRA 22.4), demonstrating that the advantage is not merely from fewer parameters (512 vs. 15K–393K) but from adapting through the latent goal interface. Against full fine-tuning (~86M params), PGT matches or exceeds ID performance while surpassing it by 13.4% on average in OOD (e.g., collect_obsidian OOD: PGT 8.2 vs. Full 6.0). In sequential continual learning, PGT avoids catastrophic forgetting by construction (storing separate latent goals per task), outperforming ER, EWC, KD, and NCL baselines on OOD metrics (e.g., collect_obsidian OOD: PGT 8.2 vs. ER 6.0, EWC 5.4, KD 5.4, NCL 6.8). On LIBERO-goal, PGT outperforms PPO-based VLA-RL in both performance and sample efficiency, and matches GRAPE's final performance while using a simpler preference-ranking function (binary success/failure vs. learned reward model).

## Relation to Prior Work

PGT repurposes the DPO objective (Rafailov et al., 2023) by redirecting optimization from policy parameters to a low-dimensional latent goal embedding, drawing a direct analogy to soft prompt and prefix tuning (Lester et al., 2021; Li & Liang, 2021) but in a closed-loop, environment-interacting setting. It departs from standard goal-conditioned imitation learning, which jointly optimizes policy and goal representations end-to-end, by freezing the policy backbone and treating the goal as a post-hoc control variable. The paper also connects to preference learning in sequential decision-making (Xia et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2025) but distinguishes itself by optimizing the conditioning input rather than the policy or a reward model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical implication is that 512 trainable parameters can match or exceed 86M-parameter full fine-tuning in out-of-distribution settings, offering a lightweight, non-destructive adaptation mechanism that preserves the pretrained policy's generalization by construction. This is particularly relevant for deployment scenarios where catastrophic forgetting and task interference are critical concerns, as demonstrated in the continual learning and planner-controller experiments. A key limitation is that PGT requires the frozen policy to already possess marginal capability for the target task—if no successful trajectories can be sampled, no preference signal exists. Open questions include whether the approach extends to policies without an explicit continuous goal interface, and whether iterative refinement beyond three rounds yields further gains or saturates.
