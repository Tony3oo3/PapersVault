## Problem

Deploying quantized models in dynamic edge environments via federated learning remains challenging because existing mixed-precision quantization methods require costly retraining whenever device resource constraints change, and the retraining burden is unsustainable on resource-limited edge devices. Moreover, optimizing a weight-sharing mixed-precision supernet in federated settings is hindered by optimization interference among heterogeneous bit-width configurations and the coupling of quantization noise with non-IID data, which together cause severe feature drift and convergence failure.

## Input / Output

During training, the framework consumes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet) distributed non-IID across K edge clients, where each client samples a distinct mixed-precision subnet (average ~3-bit for both weights and activations) to satisfy its local resource budget. At deployment, given a target BitOps constraint, the SAGS algorithm outputs a layer-wise bit-width configuration derived from the trained supernet without any retraining, enabling on-the-fly adaptation to time-varying device resources such as battery level or thermal state.

## Method

DFMPQ trains a weight-sharing mixed-precision supernet via federated learning, where each client samples a resource-adaptive quantized subnet for local QAT and the server aggregates updates into a global supernet. To counter optimization interference and quantization-exacerbated feature drift, the framework introduces Class-Conditional Semantic Alignment (CCSA), a linear-MMD-based regularizer that aligns local quantized feature prototypes with global full-precision class-conditional prototypes at selected network layers, and Semantic-Aware Hybrid Aggregation (SAHA), which applies semantic-confidence-weighted aggregation only at stage output layers while retaining standard volume-based FedAvg for intermediate layers to avoid instability from volatile mid-network features. For deployment, a Sensitivity-Aware Greedy Search (SAGS) algorithm leverages gradient-weight product sensitivity scores computed on a small server buffer to iteratively downgrade the least sensitive layers until the BitOps budget is met, reducing search complexity from O(L) to O(K). Theoretical analysis proves that CCSA bounds client drift under coupled data and system heterogeneity, SAHA approximates the optimal variance-minimizing aggregation weight, and the first-order sensitivity metric remains valid in the supernet setting because conflicting subnet objectives prevent the full-precision gradient from vanishing at the scalarized optimum.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (α=0.5), DFMPQ achieves 80.24% top-1 accuracy using only 3-bit mixed-precision for both weights and activations, matching the full-precision FedWS baseline (91.0%) within ~11 points while eliminating the need for retraining. On CIFAR-100 (α=0.5), DFMPQ reaches 64.15%, surpassing the full-precision FedWS (69.31%) by a smaller margin but still outperforming all quantized baselines. On Tiny-ImageNet (α=0.5), DFMPQ achieves 67.29%, substantially exceeding FedWS (54.23%) by over 13 points. Across all datasets, DFMPQ is the only method that does not require retraining when resource constraints change.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DFMPQ outperforms the closest full-precision baseline FedWS on Tiny-ImageNet by a wide margin (67.29% vs. 54.23% at α=0.5) while using only 3-bit mixed-precision, though it trails FedWS on CIFAR-10 (80.24% vs. 91.00%) and CIFAR-100 (64.15% vs. 69.31%) under the same setting. Against the mixed-precision FedMPQ, DFMPQ achieves gains of 29.8% on CIFAR-10 (α=0.1) and 30.5% on CIFAR-100 (α=0.1), and 13.1% and 23.1% respectively at α=0.5. Compared to fixed-precision quantization baselines FedPAQ and FedHQ+, DFMPQ leads by 13.4–18.4% on CIFAR-10 and 19.8–21.2% on CIFAR-100 (α=0.5). Against AQFL, the advantage is even more pronounced, with DFMPQ exceeding it by 22.2% on CIFAR-10 and 31.3% on CIFAR-100 (α=0.5). Notably, DFMPQ is the only method in the comparison that does not require retraining when device resource constraints change, a critical practical advantage for dynamic edge deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: one-shot supernet training (Guo et al., 2020), federated learning aggregation (McMahan et al., 2017), and mixed-precision quantization (Dong et al., 2019; Tang et al., 2023). It departs from the standard two-stage MPQ paradigm (allocate bits, then retrain) by training a single weight-sharing supernet via FL that encodes all bit-width configurations simultaneously, enabling retraining-free subnet extraction. The CCSA mechanism repurposes linear-kernel MMD (Gretton et al., 2012) as a class-conditional alignment regularizer in the FL setting, while SAHA modifies FedAvg's uniform volume-based weighting with stage-level semantic confidence. Compared to the closest prior work FedMPQ (Chen & Vikalo, 2024), which also applies MPQ in FL but still requires retraining when resource constraints shift, DFMPQ's key conceptual departure is the dynamic, on-demand subnet derivation without additional optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The retraining-free deployment capability addresses a practical pain point in edge computing where battery levels, thermal states, and available compute fluctuate over time, making pre-trained model pools intractable. The theoretical contributions—bounding client drift under coupled data/quantization heterogeneity (Lemma 1) and proving first-order gradient sensitivity remains valid in weight-sharing supernets (Theorem 2)—provide a rigorous foundation that goes beyond empirical validation. However, all experiments are confined to CIFAR-10/100 and Tiny-ImageNet with ResNet-18, MobileNetV2, and EfficientNet-Lite0, leaving open whether the supernet approach and CCSA/SAHA mechanisms scale to ImageNet-scale datasets, transformer architectures, or larger client populations. The practical impact would be substantially stronger with validation on real edge hardware and more diverse model families.
