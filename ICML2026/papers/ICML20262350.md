## Problem

Naively enlarging 3D convolutional kernels for medical image segmentation leads to optimization instability and performance saturation because kernel elements converge at spatially non-uniform rates—central elements receive stronger gradient signals than peripheral ones. Existing structural re-parameterization methods (e.g., CSLA blocks) impose a fixed, hand-designed center-biased update pattern that cannot adapt to the spatial statistics of 3D anatomy, leaving a gap between the desired local-to-global ERF diffusion behavior and the actual training dynamics.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3D volumetric CT or MRI scans resampled to standardized voxel spacings (1.5×1.5×2.0 mm for CT, 1.0×1.0×1.0 mm for MRI) and randomly cropped to 96×96×96 sub-volumes during training. It outputs per-voxel 3D segmentation masks delineating organs, tumors, and vessels across five benchmarks. A key operational detail is that the spatial modulation mask is applied exclusively during training; at inference the deployed model is a plain 21×21×21 depthwise convolution with no generator or multi-branch overhead.

## Method

Rep3D first provides a theoretical derivation showing that parallel large+small kernel branches in a CSLA block induce spatially non-uniform effective learning rates: central kernel elements receive updates from both branches (λ_eff = α_L·η_L + α_S·η_S) while peripheral elements receive only the large-branch update (λ_eff = α_L·η_L). To make this bias learnable rather than fixed, the paper introduces Low-Rank Receptive Bias Modeling (LRBM), which constructs a reciprocal distance-decay prior P = β/(f_d + β) over the 3D kernel support and co-trains a lightweight 2-layer depthwise convolutional generator f_θ to produce an adaptive modulation mask M = P + f_θ(P) with sigmoid-constrained values in [0,1]. During training, the mask is applied element-wise to the kernel (W_eff = W ⊙ M), allowing the optimizer to differentially weight central versus peripheral kernel updates in a data-driven manner. The backbone is a plain 4-stage encoder-decoder with 21×21×21 depthwise convolutions and no multi-branch structure, so the generator is discarded at inference, yielding a single large-kernel convolution per block.

## Results

Rep3D achieves state-of-the-art mean Dice scores across all five 3D segmentation benchmarks: 0.736 on KiTS, 0.723 on MSD Pancreas, 0.674 on MSD Hepatic Vessel, 0.910 on AMOS CT, and 0.864 on AMOS MRI. On KiTS, Rep3D improves tumor segmentation by 2.28% Dice over UNesT-B and 5.39% over 3D UX-Net. On AMOS CT, Rep3D outperforms the previous best (UNesT-B, 0.891) by 1.9% and RepOptimizer (0.892) by 1.8% in average Dice, while using fewer parameters (66.0M vs. 87.2M for UNesT-B). All improvements are statistically significant (p < 0.01, Paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Rep3D outperforms all 13 baselines across every benchmark. Against the strongest transformer-based competitor UNesT-B (87.2M params), Rep3D gains +2.6% Dice on KiTS (0.736 vs 0.710), +3.3% on MSD Pancreas (0.723 vs 0.690), +3.4% on MSD Hepatic (0.674 vs 0.640), +1.9% on AMOS CT (0.910 vs 0.891), and +2.1% on AMOS MRI (0.864 vs 0.843), while using 24% fewer parameters. Against the closest CNN baseline 3D UX-Net (k=21), Rep3D leads by +4.5% on KiTS, +4.7% on Pancreas, +2.2% on Hepatic, and +1.9% on AMOS CT. The gradient re-parameterization baseline RepOptimizer (Ding et al., 2022a) is outperformed by +1.8% on AMOS CT and +1.7% on AMOS MRI, confirming that spatially adaptive modulation surpasses global gradient scaling. Among classical baselines, nnU-Net (1000 epochs) trails Rep3D by 2.3% on KiTS and 2.6% on Pancreas, while SwinUNETR lags by 5.6% on KiTS and 1.5% on Pancreas. The fixed-prior variant of Rep3D (without the learnable generator) already surpasses all baselines, but the full LRBM module adds a further +0.9% to +1.6% gain, validating the learnable spatial bias component.

## Relation to Prior Work

Rep3D extends the structural re-parameterization lineage (RepLKNet, SLaK, PELK) that merges parallel large+small kernel branches into a single convolution at inference, and builds its encoder on the 3D UX-Net architecture. The key departure is replacing the fixed, hand-designed spatial bias of the CSLA block (where the small-kernel support rigidly determines which kernel elements receive stronger updates) with a learnable, low-rank modulation field that adapts element-wise convergence weights during training. This positions Rep3D between structural re-parameterization (which modifies architecture) and gradient re-parameterization (RepOptimizer, which scales updates globally) by introducing a spatially structured, data-driven optimization prior grounded in effective receptive field theory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in 3D medical imaging: naively enlarging convolution kernels leads to optimization instability and performance saturation, limiting the scalability of CNN-based approaches as alternatives to transformers. The training-only modulation design (removed at inference) makes the technique attractive for deployment, and the plug-and-play demonstration on 3D UX-Net suggests broader applicability. However, open questions remain around transferability across modalities and anatomies, behavior under anisotropic voxel spacing, and whether the gains persist under large-scale pre-training or multi-institutional settings. The training cost of 21³ 3D kernels remains nontrivial, and the paper does not explore progressive or multi-resolution training strategies to mitigate this.
