## Problem

Classical label aggregation methods for LLM-as-a-judge pipelines (Dawid-Skene, weighted majority vote) assume judges are conditionally independent given the true label, an assumption violated by shared pretraining corpora, architectures, prompts, and failure modes among LLMs. The paper shows this misspecification is not merely a calibration issue: CI-based predictors can flip the Bayes-optimal label and remain strictly suboptimal even as the number of judges grows to infinity, because they cannot exploit class information encoded in the dependence structure among votes.

## Input / Output

The method takes n items, each annotated by K LLM judges producing a binary vote vector J_i ∈ {0,1}^K, with no ground-truth labels available (fully unsupervised setting). It outputs a predicted binary label Ŷ_i and posterior probability γ_i = P(Y_i=1 | J_i) for each item. Parameter estimation is performed via EM with pseudo-likelihood surrogate M-steps, and the number of judges K and training samples n can be varied independently; experiments use up to 10 judges and up to 3000 training items per dataset.

## Method

The paper introduces a hierarchy of class-conditional Ising (pairwise MRF) models for the joint distribution P(J|Y=y) = Z^{-1} exp(Σ_j h_j^{(y)} J_j + ½Σ_{j≠k} W_{jk}^{(y)} J_j J_k). Under class-independent couplings (W^(0)=W^(1)), the quadratic terms cancel in the likelihood ratio and the Bayes rule reduces to a linear weighted vote with correlation-adjusted weights and intercept; under class-dependent couplings (W^(0)≠W^(1)), the Bayes log-odds is quadratic in the votes, capturing label-dependent correlation patterns. The paper proves separation results (via Curie-Weiss Ising models and latent-factor models) showing CI predictors incur nonvanishing excess risk while the dependence-aware Bayes predictor achieves vanishing error. Parameter estimation uses EM with pseudo-likelihood, and the paper further shows that latent-factor models with shared random effects induce approximately low-rank Ising couplings to second order, unifying the two dependence-modeling paradigms.

## Results

On three real-world LLM-as-a-judge tasks (relevance, toxicity, summarization) with 10 judge models, the class-dependent Ising model achieves test accuracy of 91.2%, 79.2%, and 80.1% respectively, outperforming the CI-weighted majority vote (Dawid-Skene) baseline by 9.2, 9.8, and 6.4 percentage points. The class-independent Ising variant also consistently beats CI-WMV (by 7.9, 8.6, and 6.9 pp) while retaining a simpler linear aggregation rule. Uniform majority vote (CI-UMV) performs worst, dropping to 56.1% on summarization, highlighting the cost of ignoring both judge quality and dependence structure.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the Dawid-Skene conditional-independence weighted majority vote (CI-WMV), which estimates per-judge weights via EM under the assumption that judges are independent given the true label. The class-dependent Ising model outperforms CI-WMV on all three datasets: by 9.2 pp on Relevance (91.2% vs. 82.0%), 9.8 pp on Toxicity (79.2% vs. 69.4%), and 6.4 pp on Summarization (80.1% vs. 73.7%). The class-independent Ising model, which uses a shared coupling matrix and reduces to a linear weighted vote with correlation-adjusted parameters, also beats CI-WMV on every dataset (by 7.9, 8.6, and 6.9 pp respectively), confirming that even a simpler dependence-aware rule captures meaningful structure that CI misses. Uniform majority vote (CI-UMV), which ignores both judge quality and dependence, is the weakest method overall and collapses to 56.1% on Summarization—nearly chance level—while CI-WMV reaches 73.7%, showing that weighting helps but is insufficient without modeling correlations. The class-dependent Ising model edges out the class-independent variant on Relevance and Toxicity (where class-specific coupling patterns matter), while the class-independent variant slightly wins on Summarization, suggesting the optimal level of model complexity is task-dependent.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Dawid-Skene (1979) framework for unsupervised label aggregation by replacing the conditional independence assumption with Ising graphical models (pairwise Markov random fields) to capture dependence among LLM judges. It positions itself against the broader crowdsourcing literature (GLAD, latent-factor models of Whitehill et al. 2009 and Xu et al. 2024) by showing that latent-factor models induce approximately low-rank Ising couplings, unifying two previously separate modeling traditions. The paper also departs from Ai et al. (2025), which uses higher-order cross-agent statistics but still assumes conditional independence given the true label, by explicitly modeling label-dependent correlation structure through class-specific coupling matrices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As LLM-as-a-judge becomes the dominant evaluation paradigm, the finding that standard aggregation methods can be confidently wrong (predicting the opposite label with 96.8% posterior probability) even when per-judge marginals are correct is a structural warning for the field. The separation theorems show this failure is not a finite-sample artifact but persists as K→∞, motivating a shift from marginal-based to dependence-aware aggregation. Key open questions include extending the population-level Bayes analysis to finite-sample EM guarantees for Ising models, developing efficient pseudo-likelihood inference that scales beyond K=10 judges, and formalizing the hypothesis-testing framework for auditing whether CI is plausible in a given evaluation pipeline.
