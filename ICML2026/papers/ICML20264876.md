## Problem

Standard optimizers in long-tailed recognition conflate gradient magnitude (driven by head-class data redundancy) with semantic information value (driven by tail-class scarcity), creating an 'optimization monopoly' that causes tail feature collapse. Existing remedies rely on static frequency-based priors—fixed re-weighting, rigid margins—that remain blind to the optimizer's real-time state, failing to distinguish genuine overfitting from hard samples within frequent classes and ignoring parameter-level gradient conflicts that drown fragile tail signals.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-100-LT, 224×224 for ImageNet-LT and iNaturalist 2018) and produces a fused class probability distribution over the full label set. At inference, multiple specialized classifier heads each emit logits, which are combined via SCAF's sample-level arbitration that routes predictions based on per-instance softmax confidence, top-two logit margin, and learned class-specific task weights—rather than static ensemble averaging—yielding a single calibrated probability vector per image.

## Method

AES is a two-stage framework (frozen ResNet-34 backbone followed by multi-head classifier fine-tuning) that introduces three state-aware correction modules operating across supervision, gradient updates, and inference. ARS loss dynamically adjusts class weights via EMA-tracked runtime precision, applies rarity-scaled ArcFace-style angular margins, and uses class-adaptive temperature to sharpen tail-class probability distributions while explicitly mining hard negatives with rarity-weighted penalties. E-PCG replaces rigid gradient projection with entropy-weighted fusion: it computes per-task gradient magnitude entropy (quantifying task specificity), pairwise cosine conflict intensity, and inverse-norm scaling, then aggregates gradients via temperature-scaled softmax, compressing head-to-tail magnitude disparity from a linear to a square-root scale. SCAF performs inference-time arbitration by computing per-sample composite confidence scores that dynamically amplify tail-specialist experts when the head model exhibits high uncertainty or blind confidence, ensuring head stability without sacrificing tail discrimination.

## Results

On CIFAR-100-LT, AES achieves top-1 accuracy of 72.9% (IR=10), 63.0% (IR=50), and 57.8% (IR=100), outperforming the strongest baseline TS-MOF by 2.1%, 2.8%, and 1.0% respectively. On ImageNet-LT, AES reaches 57.4% overall accuracy (surpassing TS-MOF's 56.3%), and on iNaturalist 2018 it achieves 73.8% (vs. TS-MOF's 72.3%). The method also demonstrates a ~50% reduction in Stage 2 training time compared to TS-MOF with only ~1 GiB additional memory overhead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AES consistently outperforms all 19 compared baselines on CIFAR-100-LT across all three imbalance ratios, with the largest margins over LOS (+3.9% at IR=100) and TS-MOF (+2.8% at IR=50). On ImageNet-LT, AES achieves 57.4% overall accuracy, beating TS-MOF (56.3%) and FeatRecon (56.8%), though it trails pre-training-focused methods PRL (60.8%) and LSC (60.2%) which leverage additional representation learning stages. On iNaturalist 2018, AES reaches 73.8%, surpassing TS-MOF (72.3%) and LOS (70.8%) but slightly trailing PRL (75.1%) and LSC (73.9%). Among expert-based SOTAs (BalPoE, SADE, MDCS), AES dominates in Head and Medium class accuracy on CIFAR-100-LT, with gains of +7.7% and +6.2% in Medium accuracy over TS-MOF at IR=100 and IR=50 respectively. Notably, AES achieves these gains while reducing Stage 2 training time by ~50% compared to TS-MOF, indicating that the entropy-aware gradient fusion is both more effective and more efficient than rigid projection-based conflict resolution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the two-stage multi-objective fine-tuning paradigm of TS-MOF (Zhao et al., 2025) by replacing its static conflict-aware gradient projection with three state-aware modules: an EMA-based adaptive loss (ARS), an entropy-weighted soft gradient fusion (E-PCG, a direct departure from PCGrad's rigid orthogonal projection), and an instance-level inference router (SCAF). Conceptually, it reframes long-tailed recognition as a dynamic 'optimizer blindness' problem—where gradient magnitude is conflated with semantic scarcity—rather than the static resource-allocation game that prior re-weighting and margin-based methods assume. The framework is designed as a plug-and-play supplement to existing losses (demonstrated with CE, BS, LDAM-DRW, KPS, LOS, and SHIKE), positioning it as an orthogonal correction layer rather than a replacement for any single strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The blindness coefficient provides a clean, interpretable metric for quantifying gradient-semantic misalignment, and the consistent 1–3% gains over strong multi-expert baselines across three datasets (including large-scale iNaturalist 2018) validate the state-aware correction hypothesis. The ~50% Stage-2 speedup while improving accuracy is a practical win for practitioners. However, the framework introduces substantial hyperparameter complexity (margins, temperatures, focal parameters, EMA momentum, conflict thresholds), and SCAF's inference routing depends on validation-set statistics, raising questions about robustness under distribution shift. Open questions include whether the multi-objective fusion scales gracefully beyond 3–4 objectives and whether the approach transfers to non-CNN backbones or video/multimodal long-tailed settings.
