## Problem

Standard MSE for time series forecasting treats predictions as point estimates, ignoring the probabilistic nature of forecasting, the intrinsic curvature of the statistical manifold induced by the Fisher information metric, and the asymmetric consequences of over- versus under-prediction. No existing loss function provides a unified, geometrically principled framework for measuring distributional discrepancies in time series prediction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261071/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a multivariate time series sequence (C channels, T timesteps) as input and produces a forecast where each time step and channel is parameterized as a Gaussian distribution with a predicted mean and standard deviation (the latter enforced positive via softplus). Ground truth observations are similarly converted to local Gaussian distributions using a sliding window of size 2w+1 for maximum likelihood estimation, transforming the problem into distribution-to-distribution matching. The final InfoGeo Loss is added to standard MSE with a tunable weight λ (default 1.0), so the backbone architecture remains unchanged.

## Method

InfoGeo Loss comprises four components operating on the statistical manifold of Gaussian distributions: (1) a Fisher information metric that computes the geodesic distance between predicted and ground-truth distributions, naturally weighting mean errors by inverse variance and scale errors by a factor of 2; (2) a Bregman divergence (instantiated as KL divergence for Gaussians) that introduces asymmetric error penalties connecting the loss to maximum likelihood; (3) a dynamic gradient-based weighting scheme that balances the Fisher and Bregman terms by inversely scaling each by its gradient norm; and (4) a natural gradient correction that pre-conditions updates with the inverse average Fisher matrix to follow geodesic directions on the manifold. The paper proves statistical consistency (convergence in probability to true parameters), O(1/k) convergence under L-smoothness and μ-strong convexity, and reparameterization invariance of the Fisher distance.

## Results

Across seven datasets and five architectures (iTransformer, PatchTST, TimeMixer, DLinear, TimesNet), InfoGeo Loss outperforms the MSE baseline in 137 out of 140 evaluation cases, achieving average improvements of 6.8% in MSE and 5.3% in MAE. Gains are largest for simpler architectures (DLinear) and grow with forecasting horizon: 8.2% average MSE reduction at T=720 versus 5.9% at T=96. In direct comparison with four advanced loss functions on ETTh1 and Weather (iTransformer backbone), InfoGeo achieves the lowest MSE on ETTh1 at T=96 (0.361) and T=336 (0.456), and the lowest on Weather at T=720 (0.341).

## Baselines & Comparisons

InfoGeo Loss consistently outperforms DILATE, TILDE-Q, and FreDF across all six dataset-horizon settings, with margins of 2–4% in MSE on average. Against the strongest competitor, Patch-wise Structural Loss, the picture is more nuanced: InfoGeo wins on ETTh1 at T=96 (0.361 vs 0.367) and T=336 (0.456 vs 0.466), but Patch-wise edges ahead on Weather at T=96 (0.163 vs 0.165) and T=336 (0.266 vs 0.268). At the longest horizon (T=720), InfoGeo slightly wins on both datasets (0.480 vs 0.478 on ETTh1 is actually a near-tie; 0.341 vs 0.342 on Weather). In MAE, InfoGeo maintains a consistent advantage over all four baselines across every setting, suggesting better distributional alignment even when MSE is comparable. The paper reports that InfoGeo outperforms frequency-domain (FreDF) and shape-focused (DILATE, TILDE-Q) methods by 2–4% on average, validating information-geometric principles over heuristic shape or spectral metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants information-geometric tools—Fisher information metrics, Bregman divergences, and natural gradient descent (Amari, 1998)—from statistical inference and neural optimization into time series loss design, a domain where prior work has addressed MSE's limitations through shape-focused (DILATE, TILDE-Q), frequency-domain (FreDF), or patch-based (Kudrat et al.) heuristics without a unified probabilistic framework. The distribution-to-distribution matching formulation draws on the classical connection between KL divergence and Bregman divergences for exponential families, while the dynamic loss weighting borrows from multi-task learning (GradNorm, Sener & Koltun). Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant point-estimate paradigm by reframing forecasting as learning conditional distributions on a statistical manifold, making the loss sensitive to both location and scale errors in a geometrically principled way.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The architecture-agnostic nature (consistent gains across Transformers, MLPs, and CNNs) and the zero-shot transfer results suggest that information-geometric loss design could become a broadly applicable drop-in improvement for time series pipelines. However, the current Gaussian assumption and univariate Fisher metric (no inter-variable correlations) limit applicability to non-Gaussian or highly correlated multivariate settings, and the computational overhead of natural gradient computation may be prohibitive at scale. Open questions include extending to general exponential families, capturing multivariate dependency structure in the Fisher metric, and developing efficient natural gradient approximations that preserve the geometric benefits without full matrix inversion.
