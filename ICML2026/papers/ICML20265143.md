## Problem

Hierarchical 3DGS training with Video Frame Interpolation (VFI) augmentation improves geometric coverage under sparse-view settings but accumulates structured, spatially clustered redundancy that violates the i.i.d. independence assumptions underlying standard global-threshold pruning. This creates a fundamental trade-off: aggressive pruning removes all near-duplicate Gaussians in a local region causing abrupt quality loss, while conservative pruning retains severe redundancy, and no existing method resolves this dilemma for VFI-augmented hierarchical representations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a monocular video sequence with known camera intrinsics and operates within the HT-3DGS hierarchical pipeline: SfM-free relative pose alignment, segment partitioning, segment-level 3DGS training with VFI-generated pseudo-views, and progressive inter-segment merging. The structured pruning module is triggered at specific training steps (once every 25 densification events) and is applied as a plug-and-play component on top of existing 3DGS backbones. The output is a compact 3DGS representation (reduced by ~56.8% in Gaussian count) that supports real-time novel view synthesis with maintained or improved rendering fidelity.

## Method

CoverPruneGS implements a coarse-to-fine pruning pipeline. Coarse pruning discretizes 3D space into a regular voxel grid (with linearly annealed cell size from 0.05 to 0.005) and performs local representative selection: within each voxel, Gaussians are ranked by a gradient-based importance score and at most half are greedily retained, with a candidate flagged as redundant if it is spatially close (r_pos=0.015) or appearance-similar (r_feat=0.3) to an already-selected representative. Fine pruning applies only to borderline candidates (those passing base pruning and non-redundancy but excluded by coarse selection) via randomized dropout rendering: a fixed-size subset is temporarily deactivated, the GT-aligned error degradation is measured per pixel, and a footprint-aware CUDA kernel attributes this degradation back to individual Gaussians using alpha-compositing blending weights w_i(p)=α_i(p)·T_i(p) over each Gaussian's effective pixel footprint. Horvitz-Thompson reweighting corrects for subsampling bias, and a quantile-based threshold (q=0.3) on the resulting RDR scores determines which borderline candidates are rescued, yielding the final keep mask as the union of coarse representatives and rescued candidates.

## Results

On Tanks & Temples (8 scenes), CoverPruneGS achieves a mean PSNR of 33.40 dB, SSIM of 0.96, and LPIPS of 0.07, matching or slightly exceeding the HT-3DGS backbone (33.32 dB) while reducing the average Gaussian count from 1.67M to 0.72M (56.8% reduction) and cutting inference time from 9:36 to 6:11 (~1.55× speedup). On CO3D-V2, the method further reduces Gaussians from 0.55M to 0.24M (45% of original) while improving mean PSNR from 28.31 to 28.48 dB. When applied as a plug-and-play module on vanilla 3DGS (Mip-NeRF 360), it improves PSNR from 27.44 to 27.89 dB and LPIPS from 0.223 to 0.211 while halving the Gaussian count from 3.18M to 1.88M.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the direct backbone HT-3DGS (Ji & Yao, 2025), CoverPruneGS achieves a marginal PSNR gain (33.40 vs. 33.32 dB) with identical SSIM/LPIPS while using only 43% of the Gaussians and a 1.55× inference speedup, confirming that the structured pruning removes redundancy without sacrificing fidelity. Compared to LongSplat (Lin et al., 2025), the method leads by 0.60 dB PSNR, 0.02 SSIM, and 0.01 LPIPS on the Tanks & Temples mean. Against CF-3DGS (Fu et al., 2024), the advantage is larger: +2.12 dB PSNR, +0.03 SSIM, and −0.02 LPIPS. In the Gaussian-simplification comparison (Table 2), applying CoverPruneGS to vanilla 3DGS on Mip-NeRF 360 improves PSNR from 27.44 to 27.89 dB and LPIPS from 0.223 to 0.211 while reducing Gaussians from 3.18M to 1.88M, outperforming all dedicated simplification baselines (GaussianSpa, 3DGS-MCMC, MH-3DGS, PUP 3D-GS, etc.) in the quality–compactness trade-off. On the already-compact Mini-Splatting backbone, the method further reduces Gaussians (e.g., 0.56M→0.40M on Mip-NeRF 360) with a small PSNR drop (27.40→27.38) but a notable SSIM improvement (0.821→0.820), indicating residual redundancy is still suppressed even in near-saturated regimes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on HT-3DGS (Ji & Yao, 2025) for hierarchical segment-level training with VFI-augmented supervision, and on 3DGS (Kerbl et al., 2023) for the base representation and Adaptive Density Control. It departs from existing 3DGS pruning methods (LightGaussian, RadSplat, Compact3DGS, PUP 3D-GS) that rely on global per-primitive importance scores by recognizing that VFI-augmented hierarchical merging creates structured, spatially clustered, non-i.i.d. redundancy that violates the independence assumptions underlying those methods. Conceptually, it extends DropoutGS (Xu et al., 2025) by anchoring dropout-induced degradation to the ground-truth objective rather than render-to-render distillation, and by replacing primitive-level attribution with a footprint-aware CUDA aggregation consistent with alpha compositing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 56.8% reduction in Gaussian count while maintaining or improving rendering quality is practically significant for deployment of 3DGS in AR/VR and embodied perception, where storage and inference latency are critical constraints. The method is specifically designed for the HT-3DGS + VFI pipeline, and while plug-and-play evaluation on vanilla 3DGS and MiniSplatting shows some transferability, the gains are more modest and sometimes mixed in that setting, leaving open whether the voxel-based structured pruning generalizes to other 3DGS training paradigms or dynamic scenes. An open question is whether the RDR refinement stage, which requires multiple randomized dropout renderings, can be made more efficient for very large scenes with millions of Gaussians.
