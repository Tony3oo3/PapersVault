## Problem

Autoregressive image generation is heavily bottlenecked by the memory footprint and latency of the self-attention KV cache, especially for high-resolution outputs requiring thousands of visual tokens. While KV cache compression is well-studied for LLMs, directly transferring text-oriented fixed-pattern or modality-agnostic sparsification strategies to visual generation is suboptimal because visual tokens are highly redundant and their spatial information density is highly non-uniform, causing such methods to either prune crucial details or waste resources on homogeneous regions.

## Input / Output

For text-to-image generation, the method takes a text prompt and produces a generated image at resolutions ranging from 512×512 to 1024×1024 (576 to 4096 visual tokens). For image editing, it takes a source image (encoded as conditional tokens) plus a text instruction and produces an edited image. The method is training-free and plug-and-play, operating as a runtime KV cache management layer on top of existing AR models without modifying their weights or training procedure. The activation step τ (expressed as a fraction of total decoding steps) controls when sparse attention begins, allowing the initial portion of generation to use full attention for global coherence.

## Method

SparseAR is a training-free, entropy-aware dynamic sparse attention method that uses the model's own predictive entropy as a lightweight signal to classify each generation step into high-, mid-, or low-entropy regions. The KV cache is partitioned into three categories—prefix (conditioning + initial tokens establishing global style), local (recent spatial-temporal neighborhood), and distant (all remaining historical tokens)—and the allocation strategy adapts per region: low-entropy steps retain only prefix and local KV, high-entropy steps retain the full cache, and mid-entropy steps use a query-based top-K selection for distant tokens. To avoid the cost of computing entropy at every step, the method estimates the current region's entropy as the median of already-computed entropy values from causal temporal and spatial neighbors, exploiting the observed high similarity of entropy across adjacent rows. For image editing, SparseAR additionally sparsifies the conditional image KV cache by retaining only spatially relevant condition tokens for each generation step, further reducing attention computation.

## Results

On text-to-image generation (Table 1), SparseAR achieves near-lossless quality with ~10–15% latency reduction: e.g., on Lumina-mGPT-768, FID improves from 4.92 (baseline) to 4.59 (τ=1/4) while latency drops from 264.3s to 245.3s. On image editing with BLIP3o-Next (Table 3), SparseAR (τ=1/4) maintains FID-5k of 13.80 and ImgEdit score of 3.62 (matching the full-cache baseline of 13.12/3.62), while reducing latency from 122.8s to 111.7s and FLOPs from 28.53 to 24.11. In contrast, fixed-pattern baselines (Streaming, H2O, R-KV) suffer severe quality degradation under the same compression budget, with FID-5k jumping to 17.6–23.2 and ImgEdit scores dropping to 0.27–2.95.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SparseAR decisively outperforms all three LLM-oriented KV compression baselines (Streaming, H2O, R-KV) on the BLIP3o-Next image editing task. At the 1/6 compression budget, Streaming collapses to an ImgEdit score of 0.27 (vs. 3.62 baseline) and FID-5k of 20.61, while SparseAR (τ=1/6) retains an ImgEdit of 3.54 and FID-5k of 14.06 — a 7.5-point FID advantage over Streaming and 3.6-point advantage over R-KV (17.63). H2O and R-KV also degrade substantially (FID-5k 19.55 and 17.63 respectively), confirming that position- or attention-weight-based eviction strategies are ill-suited to the spatially non-uniform information density of visual tokens. On text-to-image generation, SparseAR (τ=1/4) on Lumina-mGPT-768 actually improves FID from 4.92 to 4.59 and CLIP Score from 33.88 to 34.19 over the full-cache baseline, while LineAR (Qin et al., 2025a) reports no FID/CLIP numbers for direct comparison. The paper also notes that at τ=1/8 on 1024-resolution generation, SparseAR still maintains FID within 0.86 of baseline (4.08 vs. 3.22), demonstrating robustness across compression strengths.

## Relation to Prior Work

SparseAR repurposes the KV cache compression paradigm well-established in LLM long-context inference (StreamLLM, H2O, R-KV, Quest) for autoregressive image generation, but explicitly departs from the fixed-pattern or importance-based eviction strategies that dominate that literature. The key conceptual shift is using predictive entropy as a spatial information-density signal to dynamically allocate attention budgets per region, rather than applying a uniform compression rule across all tokens. This is motivated by the observation that visual tokens are spatially redundant with highly non-uniform information density, making modality-agnostic compression suboptimal. The method is training-free and plug-and-play, building on the sparse attention patterns already observed in AR visual generation (Xiang & Fan, 2025) and entropy-based uncertainty signals from LLM reasoning literature (Ma et al., 2025b; Wang et al., 2025a).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SparseAR provides a practical, drop-in efficiency improvement (~15–20% throughput gain) for AR image generation and editing across multiple mainstream models and resolutions, with the notable property that it sometimes *improves* generation quality rather than merely preserving it. The finding that standard LLM KV compression methods (Streaming, H2O, R-KV) cause severe quality degradation (FID 13→20+) on visual tasks, while an entropy-aware spatial approach avoids this, is an important caution for practitioners attempting to transfer LLM efficiency techniques to vision. Open questions include whether the method extends cleanly to non-raster-order AR models (VAR, Meissonic), how it behaves in extreme high-entropy scenes where compression benefit vanishes, and whether the entropy-neighborhood estimation can be made more robust for very small or very large spatial scales.
