## Problem

JEPA is widely framed as a fundamentally non-generative alternative to likelihood-based self-supervised learning, yet its canonical architecture (coupled encoders plus a context-to-target predictor) is structurally identical to the variational posteriors and learned conditional prior of a coupled latent-variable model. This separation is largely rhetorical: standard JEPA relies on ad-hoc anti-collapse heuristics (EMA targets, stop-gradient, distributional penalties) rather than an explicit probabilistic objective, leaving the latent generative structure implicit and precluding principled uncertainty quantification.

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired context and target observations (x, y), which in the tabular instantiation are feature-level masked sub-views of a heterogeneous feature vector mixing numerical and categorical columns. It produces per-feature Gaussian latent embeddings (s_x, s_y, z) whose posterior means serve as deterministic representations for downstream probes, while the learned posterior covariances yield per-sample uncertainty estimates used for selective prediction. The model can also reconstruct masked features and, in generative mode, sample target observations from context alone via the learned conditional prior and decoder.

## Method

Var-JEPA reverse-engineers a coupled latent-variable generative model whose ELBO, when optimized, recovers the JEPA predictor as a learned conditional prior p_θ(s_y | s_x, z) and the encoders as amortized variational posteriors. The objective decomposes into five interpretable terms: context reconstruction, target generation, and three KL divergences (on s_x toward N(0,I), on z toward N(0,I), and on the target posterior toward the learned conditional prior), with the last term jointly enforcing predictive accuracy and posterior entropy. This per-sample KL regularization toward fixed priors naturally prevents representational collapse without EMA or surrogate losses, and the simulation study confirms it achieves aggregated-distribution isotropy comparable to explicit SIGReg penalties. For tabular data, Var-T-JEPA tokenizes heterogeneous features into a transformer sequence, applies feature-level masking to create context and target views, and trains all components jointly under the weighted ELBO, yielding both deterministic embeddings and calibrated latent uncertainty.

## Results

On five real-world tabular datasets (Adult, Covertype, Electricity, Credit Card, Bank Marketing), Var-T-JEPA embeddings yield downstream test accuracy competitive with or slightly exceeding raw-feature baselines across all six predictor families (e.g., MLP: 0.852 AD, 0.679 CO, 0.790 EL, 0.818 CC, 0.900 BM vs. raw 0.849, 0.750, 0.781, 0.816, 0.898). Selective evaluation—discarding the most uncertain 50% of test samples—consistently improves accuracy (e.g., MLP on AD rises from 0.852 to 0.921). On the controlled MNIST and SIM datasets, Var-T-JEPA produces latent uncertainty signals that correlate strongly with ground-truth ambiguity (Spearman 0.949 on MNIST, 0.640 on SIM), outperforming post-hoc baselines like MC dropout and deep ensembles on AUC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Var-T-JEPA decisively outperforms the deterministic T-JEPA baseline, which suffers representational collapse on Covertype (MLP: 0.408 vs. 0.679) and MNIST (MLP: 0.113 vs. 0.822), while matching or exceeding T-JEPA on the remaining datasets. Against raw-feature baselines, Var-T-JEPA is competitive: it slightly improves on Adult (0.852 vs. 0.849 MLP), Credit Card (0.818 vs. 0.816), and Bank Marketing (0.900 vs. 0.898), but trails on Covertype (0.679 vs. 0.750) and the synthetic SIM dataset (0.695 vs. 0.823 MLP; 0.945 vs. 0.949 XGBoost). XGBoost on raw features remains the strongest single baseline on Electricity (0.917) and SIM (0.949), where its tree-based inductive bias suits the data structure. Selective evaluation via latent uncertainty provides a consistent accuracy boost across all predictor families—discarding the 50% most uncertain samples lifts MLP accuracy on Adult from 0.852 to 0.921 and on MNIST from 0.822 to 0.901. In the uncertainty-quantification comparison (Table 6), Var-T-JEPA's native latent uncertainty achieves the best Spearman correlation (0.949 MNIST, 0.640 SIM) and AUC (0.986, 0.805) for detecting high-ambiguity samples, surpassing probe entropy, MC dropout, and deep ensembles, though MC dropout and deep ensembles achieve lower AURC (0.011–0.013 vs. 0.123–0.209) on the risk-coverage curve.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes the JEPA architecture (LeCun, 2022) as a deterministic specialization of a coupled variational autoencoder, showing that the context-to-target predictor is structurally identical to a learned conditional prior p_θ(s_y | s_x, z) in a latent-variable generative model. It builds directly on T-JEPA (Thimonier et al., 2025) for the tabular masking strategy and on coupled VAEs (Hao & Shafto, 2023) for the probabilistic structure, while positioning itself against LeJEPA (Balestriero & LeCun, 2025) by showing that per-sample KL regularization toward fixed priors achieves aggregated-distribution isotropy comparable to explicit SIGReg penalties. The key departure from prior JEPA work is replacing ad-hoc anti-collapse mechanisms (EMA targets, stop-gradient, variance-covariance penalties) with a single unified ELBO objective that makes the latent generative structure explicit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper dissolves the commonly drawn 'predictive vs. generative' dichotomy in self-supervised learning by showing it is largely a matter of framing rather than architecture, which could reshape how the community thinks about JEPA-style methods and their relationship to VAEs. The practical benefit is principled per-sample uncertainty quantification from posterior covariances and elimination of heuristic regularizers, though the empirical validation is confined to small-scale tabular and semi-synthetic datasets. Open questions include whether the ELBO formulation scales effectively to large-scale vision and video (the paper sketches an I-JEPA analogue but does not implement it), how it behaves when target observations are entirely absent at test time, and whether the β-VAE-style weighted loss terms require careful tuning in practice.
