## Problem

Strict one-class learning for audio deepfake detection models the bona fide distribution but fails to establish a tight, deployable rejection boundary without spoof supervision. Existing relaxed approaches introduce auxiliary negative samples that bias the learned boundary toward seen artifact families, degrading generalization to unseen attacks. The paper addresses how to explicitly shape a compact acceptance region using only bona fide speech, without treating any shifted views as an explicit negative class.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266084/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a raw audio waveform as input and, in heterogeneous settings, a domain or language label to select the appropriate centroid. At inference it produces a single scalar cosine-to-centroid similarity score (higher indicates greater bona fide confidence), which is compared against a threshold calibrated exclusively from a bona fide-only development split. Training, validation, model selection, and threshold calibration all use bona fide speech only—no spoof utterances or spoof labels appear anywhere in the pipeline.

## Method

CA-SOADD builds a geometry-stabilized representation extractor on a pretrained SSL speech encoder (XLSR), combining learnable layer aggregation, attention-based temporal pooling, a projection head, and bona fide-only online standardization to prevent cosine collapse and preserve angular dynamic range. Training uses a centroid-anchored tri-objective loss: OBJ_cpt pulls embeddings toward a running bona fide centroid, OBJ_binv enforces invariance under benign perturbations, and OBJ_cabs imposes a centroid-referenced margin between each bona fide embedding and a structure-disrupting off-manifold probe (e.g., segment permutation, spectral masking, phase scrambling). The critical design choice is that these probes are never treated as an explicit negative class; they enter only through the margin constraint, ensuring strict alignment between the training objective and the cosine-to-centroid inference rule. For heterogeneous scenarios, the framework extends to domain-conditioned centroids with a shared extractor, where the boundary-shaping margin is computed against the nearest domain centroid to push probes away from the entire bona fide manifold.

## Results

Under the strict real-only protocol, CA-SOADD achieves AUC/EER of 96.9/7.3% on ASVSpoof-2021 LA, 96.9/8.4% on ASVSpoof-2021 DF, and 92.7/13.4% on ASVSpoof-5 (whole), substantially outperforming all real-only baselines (OC-SVM, Deep-SVDD, OC-Softmax, ACS) by 10–30+ AUC points. In the zero-shot transfer setting (trained on ASVSpoof-2021, tested on ASVSpoof-5), CA-SOADD reaches 88.9/14.7%, surpassing even spoof-supervised detectors that degrade severely under benchmark shift. On CtrSVDD (singing voice), CA-SOADD achieves the best EER of 16.83%, and on MLAAD (8 languages) it attains 17.70% EER, the best among all real-only methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among real-only baselines, CA-SOADD dominates across all benchmarks: on ASVSpoof-2021 DF it reaches 96.9/8.4% AUC/EER versus ACS (79.3/28.0%), OC-Softmax (70.0/37.8%), Deep-SVDD (82.5/24.9%), and OC-SVM (71.1/34.8%), a gap of 15–27 AUC points. Against spoof-supervised detectors, CA-SOADD wins decisively on ASVSpoof-5 (92.7/13.4% vs. best supervised AASIST 72.5/33.7%) and on the zero-shot transfer setting (88.9/14.7% vs. best supervised MPE 70.6/35.2%), where supervised methods collapse due to overfitting to source-benchmark artifacts. On ASVSpoof-2021 DF, CA-SOADD also surpasses the strongest supervised baseline RawGAT (93.2/14.3%) by 3.7 AUC and 5.9 EER points. On CtrSVDD, CA-SOADD achieves 16.83% EER, beating the best supervised baseline AASIST (21.81%). On MLAAD, CA-SOADD (17.70% EER) trails the top supervised methods ASDG (10.73%) and MPE (12.47%) but remains the best real-only method by a wide margin over ACS (35.94%) and OC-SVM (43.21%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant 'relaxed' one-class learning paradigm in audio deepfake detection (OC-Softmax, ACS, KLOC-Softmax), which introduces auxiliary spoof samples to shape the decision boundary, by enforcing a strict real-only protocol where no spoof data touches training, validation, or calibration. Conceptually, it repurposes the distribution-shifted view idea from CSI and NAD—where shifted instances serve as surrogate negatives for contrastive or binary objectives—into a fundamentally different role: off-manifold boundary probes that enter training only through a centroid-referenced margin constraint, preserving strict alignment with the cosine-to-centroid inference score. The geometry-stabilized extractor (hierarchical layer-time aggregation plus online standardization) addresses a practical pathology (cosine collapse) that prior OCL methods on speech have not explicitly tackled, and the domain-conditioned centroid extension generalizes the single-centroid formulation to heterogeneous multilingual settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The strict real-only protocol addresses a genuine deployment gap: in open-world anti-spoofing, spoof pipelines evolve continuously and may be unavailable at training time, making spoof-dependent boundaries a liability rather than an asset. The score-loss alignment principle (training shapes the boundary via the same metric used at inference) is a clean design constraint that could inform other one-class or anomaly-detection settings beyond audio. Open questions include whether the hand-designed structural probe pool can be learned or adapted automatically, whether the approach transfers to other modalities (e.g., video deepfakes), and whether the requirement for a test-time domain/language label limits practical applicability in truly unknown-domain scenarios.
