## Problem

The paper addresses a structural gap in AI's capacity for scientific invention: while LLMs have mastered induction (statistical compression) and deduction (formal proof), they lack a mechanism for abduction—the generation of novel explanatory hypotheses from sensory experience. The prevailing 'creativity as compression' view fails to account for discoveries like General Relativity, where no empirical error signal existed to drive a data-driven search, and where the critical step was formulating axioms rather than deriving consequences from them.

## Input / Output

As a position paper, this work does not implement a system but specifies the I/O requirements for one capable of abductive scientific invention. The proposed input is an action-controllable physical simulation environment (a 'synthetic laboratory') where an agent can perform counterfactual interventions, rather than passive video or text. The output is a set of new axioms or hypotheses grounded in simulated sensory experience, which can then be fed into a deductive pipeline for theorem derivation and empirical verification.

## Method

The paper constructs a theoretical framework mapping Einstein's E-J-A invention cycle (Sense Experience → Jump → Axioms) onto Peirce's three inference modes, using General Relativity as a computational case study. It demonstrates that induction fails because Newtonian gravity faced no empirical crisis (no gradient to drive restructuring) and that deduction is strictly downstream (it can derive Mercury's perihelion precession from the field equations but cannot generate the Equivalence Principle itself). The core mechanism identified is manipulative abduction: a two-stage process of (1) embodied simulation to generate a novel observation (e.g., Einstein's falling elevator) and (2) inference to the best explanation to extract a new axiom from that observation. The paper proposes that physically consistent, action-controllable world models—distinguished from passive video generators by their capacity for counterfactual intervention (e.g., 'cutting the elevator cable')—provide the sensory grounding necessary to mechanize this abductive jump, transforming it from an unrepeatable insight into a reproducible algorithmic process.

## Results

This is a position paper with no quantitative experiments or benchmark results. Its central claim is qualitative: LLMs are structurally incapable of the abductive 'Jump' (E→A) required to formulate new scientific axioms, as demonstrated through Einstein's formulation of General Relativity as a computational case study. The paper argues that (1) induction fails because Newtonian gravity had no significant error signal (equivalence of inertial/gravitational mass verified to 10⁻⁹, only anomaly being Mercury's perihelion), (2) deduction is a downstream verification step that cannot generate premises, and (3) the missing mechanism is manipulative abduction grounded in physically consistent, action-controllable world models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

As a position paper, this work does not run empirical comparisons against baselines. Instead, it conceptually positions existing AI systems against the abductive requirement. The paper argues that inductive systems (sparse PDE discovery, AI Physicist) fail because they require a supervised error signal that did not exist for General Relativity. Deductive systems (AlphaProof, Aristotle, Gemini, DeepSeekMathV2) are acknowledged as capable of deriving theorems from given axioms but cannot generate those axioms. Evolutionary/optimization agents (AI Scientist, AlphaEvolve) are characterized as sophisticated 'Chinese Room' operations that recombine existing symbols without sensory grounding. The paper contrasts these with action-controllable world models (Genie) as the necessary substrate for counterfactual simulation, arguing that only physically consistent, interactive world models can provide the sensory grounding for the abductive leap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This position paper departs from the dominant 'creativity as compression' framing (Schmidhuber, 2008) by arguing that inductive data compression is insufficient to explain scientific invention, using Einstein's General Relativity as a case study where no meaningful error signal existed. It repurposes Peirce's trichotomy of inference and Magnani et al.'s concept of 'manipulative abduction' to formalize a structural gap in LLMs: the inability to generate novel axioms from sensory simulation rather than symbolic manipulation. The paper situates itself relative to recent AI systems (AlphaProof, ARC-AGI, Genie, AI Scientist, AlphaEvolve) by conceding their deductive and inductive capabilities while identifying the abductive 'Jump' as the unaddressed bottleneck, and draws on LeCun's and Li's critiques of LLMs' lack of world models to propose action-controllable, physically consistent world models as a potential substrate for abductive reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clear theoretical framework for why current LLM-based discovery systems (AI Scientist, AlphaEvolve) are structurally limited to recombination and optimization within existing symbolic spaces, rather than genuine paradigm-shifting invention. Its central open question—whether physically consistent, action-controllable world models can serve as a 'synthetic laboratory' to mechanize the E→A abductive jump—points toward a concrete research direction for the next generation of AI systems. However, as a position paper with no empirical validation, the claim that LLMs are 'structurally incapable' of abduction remains a philosophical argument rather than a demonstrated limitation, and the sufficiency of world models for abduction is asserted rather than tested. The paper explicitly frames itself as a guide for future empirical testing rather than a definitive proof.
