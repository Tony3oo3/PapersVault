## Problem

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models that discretize robot actions into tokens suffer from a fundamental structural heterogeneity: vision-language embeddings are statistically isotropic and Euclidean, while robotic actions live on the non-convex, anisotropic SE(3) manifold. This geometric and statistical mismatch makes direct regression ill-posed—Euclidean residuals produce off-manifold mixing, and isotropic retrieval selects false neighbors along anisotropic action clusters—ultimately limiting convergence speed and generalization.

## Input / Output

The model consumes synchronized multi-view RGB-D imagery (one overhead Intel RealSense D455 plus two wrist-mounted D435i cameras) together with a natural language instruction, processed by a Qwen2.5-VL 3B backbone. It outputs an action chunk of shape T×7 in the Lie-algebraic tangent space (6-dim se(3) delta end-effector motion plus 1-dim gripper command), where T is 8 for LIBERO, 16 for SimplerEnv, and 30 for real-world tasks. The output is produced by two parallel heads: an autoregressive head emitting discrete LAST tokens and a DiT diffusion head generating the continuous trajectory, which is then exponentiated back to SE(3) for execution.

## Method

LAST is a two-stage tokenizer that rectifies the action manifold before quantization. In Global Topological Linearization, raw SE(3) trajectories are mapped to the se(3) tangent space via the logarithmic map (restoring local additivity up to O(ε²) by BCH) and then compressed into fixed-length B-spline control points via ridge regression, converting variable-horizon curved trajectories into a unified linear vector space. In Local Metric Discretization, a Layer-1 codebook selects a coarse topological schema (Voronoi anchor), and a covariance-aware whitening transformation Σ_soft^{-1/2} is applied to the residual within that local chart, rendering the residual approximately isotropic so that a Layer-2 Euclidean VQ codebook operates in a statistically compatible metric. The downstream VLA is trained with a dual objective: a cross-entropy loss on the discrete schema tokens (topological alignment) plus a diffusion noise-prediction MSE on the continuous whitened actions (metric refinement), with the discrete token acting as a topological anchor that collapses the global non-convex search into local convex refinement.

## Results

On LIBERO, LAST-Continuous achieves a new SOTA average success rate of 95.8%, outperforming π0 and π0-FAST-Continuous (both 94.1%) and BEAST-Continuous (92.9%). On SimplerEnv-WidowX, LAST-Continuous reaches 57.3% overall success rate, surpassing π0-FAST-Continuous (45.8%) and OpenVLA-OFT (41.8%). On real-world bimanual tasks, LAST achieves 73%/57%/48% on PlaceObj/ZipSeal/TubeRack, substantially outperforming π0-FAST (which drops to 18% on ZipSeal) and BEAST (23% on TubeRack). At the tokenizer level, LAST achieves 0.6e-2 MAE with 96% codebook utilization, nearly 50% lower error than RVQ-VAE (1.1e-2, 51% CU).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LAST-Continuous achieves the best LIBERO average (95.8%), beating π0 (94.1%) by 1.7 points and π0-FAST-Continuous (94.1%) by 1.7 points, with the largest margin on the Long-horizon suite (91.2% vs. 89.4% for π0-FAST-Continuous). On SimplerEnv, LAST-Continuous (57.3%) outperforms π0-FAST-Continuous (45.8%) by 11.5 points and OpenVLA-OFT (41.8%) by 15.5 points, demonstrating superior sim-to-real robustness. On real-world tasks, LAST's advantage is most dramatic on contact-rich ZipSeal (57% vs. 18% for π0-FAST) and precision-critical TubeRack (48% vs. 23% for BEAST), confirming that frequency-domain artifacts and Euclidean interpolation on SE(3) cause catastrophic failures. LAST-Discretized (93.4% LIBERO avg) remains competitive without the DiT head, ranking 4th overall. At the tokenizer level, LAST dominates all baselines: it achieves 0.6e-2 MAE (vs. 1.1e-2 for RVQ-VAE, 1.8e-2 for FAST, 4.2e-2 for Binning) while maintaining 8.0× compression and 96% codebook utilization (vs. 51% for RVQ-VAE), validating that Lie-algebraic linearization plus covariance-aware whitening resolves both geometric and statistical mismatches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of VLA action tokenization (OpenVLA, π0, FAST, BEAST) and optimal transport theory, arguing that prior tokenizers fail because they ignore the geometric (SE(3) non-additivity) and statistical (anisotropy vs. isotropy) mismatch between action and semantic spaces. It departs from FAST's frequency-domain BPE and BEAST's B-spline Euclidean quantization by explicitly mapping actions into the Lie-algebraic tangent space se(3) and applying covariance-aware whitening before quantization, framed under a Gromov-Wasserstein alignment perspective. The dual-head architecture (autoregressive discrete intent + DiT continuous refinement) extends the hybrid planning-execution paradigm seen in π0 and QwenGR00T, but grounds the discrete head in a geometrically valid token space rather than raw Euclidean bins.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled theoretical diagnosis (formalized in Lemmas A.1–A.4) for why geometry-agnostic action tokenization degrades VLA generalization, which could reshape how the community designs action representations beyond this specific method. Practical limitations include the O(Kd³) whitening cost for high-DoF embodiments (e.g., dexterous hands) and the local linearization validity near the SE(3) cut locus, both flagged by the authors. Open questions include whether the GW-inspired structural alignment generalizes to non-SE(3) action spaces (e.g., joint-space with complex kinematic chains) and whether the discrete-intent + continuous-refinement decomposition scales to longer horizons or multi-robot settings.
