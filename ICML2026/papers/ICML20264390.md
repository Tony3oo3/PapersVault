## Problem

Domain-specific corpora are typically small and lack the repetitive variation that LLMs need for reliable knowledge internalization, making direct fine-tuning prone to overfitting on surface forms. Existing synthetic data augmentation methods for knowledge injection—RL-based approaches like SEAL and multi-stage prompting pipelines like EntiGraph and Active Reading—are computationally expensive or architecturally complex, yet it remains unclear whether a simpler strategy could match or exceed their performance at scale.

## Input / Output

The method takes a small domain-specific text corpus (e.g., 200 SQuAD passages, 265 QuALITY passages, or 609 MultiHop-RAG articles) and a fixed set of 7 human-curated prompt templates as input, and produces a large-scale synthetic corpus (up to 120M, 455M, or 15M tokens respectively) for continued pretraining. The augmentation is task-agnostic: it operates directly on source documents without access to downstream questions or labels. Each of the 7 prompts independently generates an approximately equal share of the total token budget, and all outputs are concatenated into the final synthetic corpus.

## Method

SPA rewrites the source corpus by repeatedly prompting a generator LLM with 7 fixed prompt templates organized around three cognitive-learning levels: Concept Learning (Key Concepts, Mind Map), Critical Thinking (Implications, QA with Critical Thinking), and Generative Learning (Case Studies, Discussions, Teacher-style). Each template is applied independently in a single-stage pipeline, with a strict grounding constraint to prevent hallucination, and the per-prompt token budgets are scaled to fill the target synthetic corpus size. The resulting synthetic data is then used for continued pretraining of the target model under standard settings. The key finding is that this simple single-stage approach outperforms RL-based methods (which suffer diversity collapse at scale) and multi-stage prompting pipelines (whose intermediate strategy-generation stage can produce low-quality guidance), demonstrating that careful prompt design grounded in educational psychology, combined with straightforward large-scale augmentation, is a surprisingly strong baseline for knowledge injection.

## Results

SPA achieves the highest QA accuracy across all three benchmarks at the largest synthetic data scale: 91.27% on SQuAD (120M tokens, Qwen2.5-7B), 57.03% on QuALITY (455M tokens, Meta-Llama-3-8B), and 86.64%/88.36% on MultiHop-RAG (15M tokens, Qwen2.5-7B/Meta-Llama-3-8B). On SQuAD, SPA nearly matches the upper bound of 91.38% achieved when the model is given access to the original passages at test time. On general benchmarks (MMLU, ARC-C, OpenBookQA, WinoGrande), SPA exhibits the smallest performance drop after continued pretraining compared to other baselines, indicating less catastrophic forgetting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPA outperforms all five compared baselines across all three benchmarks. Against Active Reading (Oguz et al., ICLR 2026), SPA wins by 1.02 points on SQuAD (91.27 vs. 90.25), 5.28 points on QuALITY (57.03 vs. 51.75), and 3.31/3.92 points on MultiHop-RAG (86.64 vs. 83.33 on Qwen; 88.36 vs. 84.44 on Llama). Against EntiGraph (Yang et al., ICLR 2025), SPA achieves 57.03% vs. 56.22% on QuALITY and 86.64%/88.36% vs. 85.42%/84.31% on MultiHop-RAG, notably using a weaker and ~50× cheaper generator (gpt-oss-120b vs. GPT-4-Turbo). Against the simple Rephrase and QA baselines (Maini et al., ACL 2024), SPA leads by 1.64 points over QA on SQuAD (91.27 vs. 89.63) and by 4.70 points on QuALITY (57.03 vs. 52.33). Against the RL-based SEAL (Zweiger et al., 2025), SPA achieves a large 17.04-point margin on SQuAD at 120M tokens (91.27 vs. 74.23), with the gap widening as data scales due to SEAL's diversity collapse. Against SoG (Ma et al., LoG 2025) and PaST (Tang et al., 2026), SPA also leads on QuALITY and SQuAD respectively, though these comparisons rely on numbers reported in the original papers since code/models are not released.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in synthetic data generation for knowledge injection by showing that a single-stage, fixed set of seven human-curated prompt templates—grounded in cognitive science learning strategies (concept learning, critical thinking, generative learning)—can match or outperform both RL-based augmentation (SEAL, PaST) and multi-stage prompting pipelines (EntiGraph, Active Reading, SoG) at scale. Rather than building on any specific prior method, SPA repurposes the general idea of LLM-based data rewriting with a principled prompt design, deliberately avoiding the iterative RL training loop and the multi-stage transformation chains that prior work relies on. The key conceptual departure is that prompt quality and scaling, not pipeline complexity or learned generation policies, are the primary drivers of effective knowledge injection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SPA provides the knowledge-injection community with a strong, reproducible, and computationally cheap baseline that future methods must genuinely improve upon, and its analyses identify two concrete limitations of prior approaches: RL-based generators suffer diversity collapse at scale, and multi-stage pipelines do not consistently outperform well-designed single-stage prompts. Open questions remain about whether SPA generalizes to harder domains (intensive numerical reasoning, rapidly evolving knowledge), whether task-specific prompt tuning transfers across settings, and how to mitigate the diversity collapse in RL-based methods. The paper also notes that continued pretraining on synthetic data can cause catastrophic forgetting, though SPA exhibits the smallest drop among compared methods.
