## Problem

Existing FFT-based pansharpening methods directly transplant spatial operators (convolution, self-attention) into the frequency domain, ignoring the spectrum's intrinsic structure: each coordinate encodes a distinct frequency component and energy decays radially from the center. This mismatch produces reconstruction artifacts and suboptimal spectral modulation, while self-attention's O(N²) cost and inherent low-pass bias further degrade the high-frequency detail preservation that pansharpening demands.

## Input / Output

The network receives a low-resolution multispectral (LMS) image at 1/4 the spatial resolution of the high-resolution panchromatic (PAN) image (e.g., 64×64 LMS paired with 256×256 PAN). After channel-wise concatenation and initial 3×3 convolution, the fused high-resolution multispectral (HRMS) image is produced at PAN resolution. Training uses 64×64 random crops with the Wald protocol for reduced-resolution evaluation, and full-resolution inference is tested on 64×64 LMS / 256×256 PAN patches.

## Method

The core insight is a mathematical reformulation showing that the classical Butterworth filter, when expressed via the log-exponential identity, becomes a Sigmoid gating mask whose parameters (cutoff frequency D₀, filter order 2n, and frequency coordinates) map analogously to Query, Key, and dynamic positional encoding in self-attention, but with O(N log N) element-wise multiplication instead of O(N²) matrix products. The authors generalize this to an anisotropic, learnable form with a rotation angle θ and separate per-axis cutoffs (D_u0, D_v0), producing an elliptical passband that adaptively emphasizes directional textures. ABFNet stacks Spectral Fusion Blocks, each containing a Butterworth Attention Module (BAM) with a dual-branch design: a global path applies a single anisotropic Butterworth mask to the full-image FFT, while a local path uses Butterworth Token Interaction (BTI) to perform FFT-based global interaction among patch-level latent tokens before decoding per-patch filter parameters for patch-wise anisotropic filtering. A trainable gate g per channel dynamically blends low-pass and high-pass behavior, and the two branches are fused via per-channel learnable weights.

## Results

On reduced-resolution pansharpening benchmarks, ABFNet achieves SOTA PSNR of 37.65 dB (WV3), 38.83 dB (QB), and 50.30 dB (GF2), with corresponding ERGAS of 2.13, 3.41, and 0.72, and SAM of 3.17, 4.06, and 0.86. In zero-shot cross-sensor transfer to WV2 (trained only on WV3), ABFNet attains the best SAM (5.37) and competitive PSNR (29.05 dB) and ERGAS (4.29). The full model uses only 0.26M parameters and 12.64G FLOPs, while the Only Global variant further reduces to 0.07M params and 3.58G FLOPs. On CIFAR-100 classification, the BAM backbone reaches 81.31% top-1 accuracy with 5.54M parameters and 0.70G FLOPs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ABFNet outperforms all eight pansharpening baselines on every reduced-resolution benchmark (WV3, QB, GF2), with PSNR gains of 0.43 dB over DCINN on WV3, 0.35 dB over WFAnet on QB, and 1.02 dB over DCINN on GF2. On the zero-shot WV2 transfer, ABFNet achieves the best SAM (5.37) but trails BIMPan on PSNR (29.05 vs. 29.58) and ERGAS (4.29 vs. 4.03), indicating BIMPan's bidomain design retains a slight edge in cross-sensor spectral fidelity. In terms of efficiency, ABFNet (0.26M params, 12.64G FLOPs) is substantially lighter than ARConv (4.79M, 61.57G) and BIMPan (2.34M, 29.45G), while WFAnet runs out of memory on full-resolution inputs. On CIFAR-100, the BAM backbone (81.31%) narrowly beats MobileNet-V4 (81.25%) and TransNext (80.79%), and surpasses the frequency-domain competitor GFNet (80.30%) by 1.01% with lower FLOPs (0.70G vs. 0.80G), while outperforming ViT-Base (69.00%) and ConvNeXt-Tiny (79.44%) by wide margins.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in FFT-based pansharpening (SFIIN, BIMPan, WFAnet) and frequency-domain attention (GFNet, SpectFormer, FSANet), which transplant spatial operators—convolution or self-attention—directly onto spectral features. Instead, it repurposes the classical Butterworth filter as a frequency-domain-native operator, showing via the convolution theorem that its element-wise spectral gating is equivalent to a circulant-matrix (shift-invariant) global token mixer, and further generalizes it from isotropic to anisotropic (elliptical) form to capture directional textures. The theoretical unification with self-attention (cutoff frequency as Query, filter order as Key, frequency coordinates as positional encoding) provides a principled bridge between classical signal processing and modern attention architectures, while the BTI module replaces O(N²) pairwise token interaction with O(N log N) spectral filtering over a condensed latent grid.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ABFNet offers a practically attractive O(N log N) global-receptive-field operator that avoids the quadratic cost of self-attention while retaining directional inductive bias, making it well-suited for high-resolution remote sensing where both efficiency and anisotropic texture preservation matter. The CIFAR-100 validation (81.31% Top-1, competitive with MobileNet-V4) suggests the anisotropic Butterworth gating is not task-specific but a general-purpose feature extractor. Open questions include whether the circulant (shift-invariant) structure is always preferable to unconstrained attention for tasks requiring position-dependent interactions, whether the smooth Butterworth roll-off is suboptimal for extreme degradation scenarios (as the authors acknowledge), and whether the paradigm extends naturally to other classical filter families (e.g., Chebyshev, elliptic) for more flexible frequency selectivity.
