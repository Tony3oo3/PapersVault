## Problem

Learning from automatic annotations (pre-trained experts, Foundation Models) introduces model-induced label noise that is systematic and instance-dependent, tightly coupled with local feature manifolds rather than stochastic. Global transition matrices underfit these structural error patterns, while estimating a unique T(x) per instance is an ill-posed problem with infinite degrees of freedom. The paper tackles the gap: how to make instance-dependent noise estimation tractable without ground-truth anchor points.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263852/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw inputs (3D point clouds grid-sampled at 4 cm voxel size, or 2D images) paired with noisy pseudo-labels generated by a pre-trained annotator model (e.g., KPConv, MPRM, PCAM for 3D; feature-dependent synthetic noise for CIFAR-100). It produces per-point or per-image semantic predictions along with an estimated instance-specific transition matrix T(x) decomposed into K=16 learnable basis matrices and soft assignment weights ω(x). Noisy labels are treated as the sole supervision signal; no clean validation set or anchor points are required.

## Method

MIND approximates the intractable T(x) as a convex combination of K basis transition matrices {T^(k)}, where mixing weights ω(x) are determined by a Latent Decoupling Estimator (LDE). The LDE deterministically partitions the backbone feature vector h(x) into K equal contiguous channel chunks, then enforces inter-subspace orthogonality via a decoupling loss L_dec that maximizes intra-subspace cosine-similarity cohesion while minimizing inter-subspace affinity, forcing the encoder to self-organize distinct geometric error modes (e.g., edges, planar surfaces, thin structures) into separate latent slots. Assignment weights are computed as a softmax over mean activation magnitudes within each subspace, acting as a soft geometric gate. Basis matrices are estimated online via a momentum-based self-paced update using the model's own predictions as a proxy for ground truth, and the total objective combines a noise-corrected cross-entropy loss with the decoupling regularization. Theoretically, identifiability is guaranteed under a latent anchor subspace assumption, with approximation error bounded by O(K^{-1/D_M}) where D_M is the intrinsic dimension of the noise manifold.

## Results

On the primary 3D segmentation benchmarks, MIND achieves 66.48% mIoU on S3DIS and 67.30% on ScanNetV2 under low-noise (KPConv) conditions, and maintains 40.58% and 39.99% respectively under the most challenging high-structural-bias (PCAM) setting. On CIFAR-100 with 40% instance-dependent noise, MIND reaches 46.22% accuracy with a transition matrix estimation error of 0.18, the lowest among all compared methods. For unsupervised OpenSeg adaptation on S3DIS, MIND yields a +8.14% mIoU gain over the zero-shot baseline, substantially exceeding the +5.20% from the strongest generic robust method (CA2C).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MIND outperforms all nine baselines across every noise setting and dataset. Against the strongest recent competitor CA2C (ICCV'25), MIND gains +1.22% mIoU on S3DIS-PCAM (40.58 vs. 39.36) and +1.76% on ScanNet-PCAM (39.99 vs. 38.23), with larger margins under lower noise (e.g., +1.97% on S3DIS-KPConv). The global robust loss GCE degrades catastrophically under structural bias, collapsing to 17.99% on ScanNet-PCAM—nearly 22 points below MIND—confirming that class-conditional assumptions are fundamentally mismatched to geometry-coupled noise. Instance-dependent methods BLTM and NPC improve over global baselines but still trail MIND by 2–4% under PCAM noise, as their per-instance or generative modeling lacks the subspace structure to isolate geometric error modes. On CIFAR-100, MIND reduces the transition matrix estimation error to 0.18, halving the error of BLTM (0.35) and VolMin (0.39), while also achieving the highest accuracy (46.22% vs. 43.50% for BLTM). For OpenSeg adaptation, MIND's +8.14% gain over the zero-shot baseline substantially exceeds CA2C's +5.20% and NPC's +3.60%, demonstrating that geometry-aware decoupling is more effective than generic robust fine-tuning for correcting VLM hallucinations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the manifold-regularized transition matrix estimation line (Cheng et al., CVPR 2022) and the forward correction framework (Patrini et al., CVPR 2017) by introducing a middle ground between global transition matrices (which underfit structural errors) and fully instance-dependent matrices (which are intractable). The key conceptual departure is the Latent Manifold Disentanglement assumption: that model-induced noise, while instance-dependent, is governed by a finite set of geometric error modes that can be captured by K basis transition matrices weighted by latent subspace assignments. Unlike prior instance-dependent methods (BLTM, DMLP) that either parameterize T(x) directly or use meta-learning, MIND enforces orthogonality in a fixed channel partition of the feature space to induce geometric disentanglement without requiring clean validation data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical gap: as Foundation Models (SAM, CLIP, OpenSeg) become the primary annotation source, their systematic, geometry-coupled biases are fundamentally different from the stochastic noise assumed by classical LNL methods. The 3D stress test design (S3DIS, ScanNet) is a thoughtful choice, using explicit geometric manifolds as a rigorous proxy for high-dimensional structural noise. However, the identifiability guarantee relies on the latent anchor subspace assumption, which may break down when error patterns are indistinguishable from semantic features (e.g., adversarial noise). Open questions include whether non-linear basis aggregation is needed for highly curved noise manifolds, and whether the fixed channel-partition strategy generalizes beyond the tested architectures.
