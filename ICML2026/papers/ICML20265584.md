## Problem

Standard VLM architectures map ViT encoder outputs directly into the LLM via a lightweight projector, but visual features remain far from the LLM's text space in early layers, forcing the model to waste critical depth on superficial modality alignment rather than deep reasoning. This misallocation of depth also triggers catastrophic forgetting of language capabilities during multimodal fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image and a text instruction as input and generates a free-form text response. The image is first encoded by a ViT inside the perceiver, then progressively refined through the perceiver's language blocks before being projected into the target LLM's embedding space. An optional context-aware variant also feeds the text instruction into the perceiver for early-fusion visual feature extraction, though the default configuration keeps the perceiver instruction-agnostic for multi-turn generalization.

## Method

DPA replaces the standard ViT encoder with a small perceiver VLM (Qwen3-0.6B) that contains its own ViT, projector, and pre-trained language model blocks. The perceiver's language blocks progressively bridge the modality gap upstream, so visual features arrive at the target LLM already structurally aligned with text space, reducing destructive adaptation. Training follows the standard two-stage recipe: stage-1 trains only the projection layer on 558K image-caption pairs, and stage-2 fine-tunes the full model end-to-end on 1M visual instruction samples. Ablations show that the perceiver's language blocks and their LLM pre-training are essential (removing either causes large drops), while the perceiver's own multimodal capability is not—architectural depth with language priors is the key mechanism. The approach adds only ~2% parameter overhead at the 32B scale and ~2% training FLOPs, with negligible inference throughput loss.

## Results

On the 4B scale (Qwen3-4B target), DPA achieves a multimodal average of 53.1 vs. 51.2 for the LLaVA-NeXT controlled baseline (+1.9), and an overall average of 53.0 vs. 49.6 (+3.4). At the 32B scale, the multimodal gain widens to +3.0 (61.1 vs. 58.1) and the overall gain to +3.6 (60.3 vs. 56.7). DPA also substantially reduces text capability forgetting: MATH500 improves from 36.4→54.2 (4B) and 44.4→58.2 (32B), corresponding to a 32.9% and 21.6% relative reduction in forgetting respectively. Gains are consistent across LLM families, with DPA-LLaMA-3.2-3B achieving +3.4 multimodal and +3.6 overall over its controlled baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the controlled LLaVA-NeXT baselines (identical training data, scheduler, and target LLM, differing only in the use of a perceiver VLM vs. a plain ViT encoder), DPA wins on every capability group at all three scales: +1.9/+3.0/+3.4 multimodal average for 4B/32B/LLaMA-3B respectively, with the largest single-benchmark gains on OCRBench (+5.6 at 4B, +3.0 at 32B) and MATH500 (+17.8 at 4B, +13.8 at 32B). Against public baselines, DPA-Qwen3-4B (Multi.Avg 53.1) surpasses LLaVA-NeXT-7B (40.8), Idefics2-8B (47.8), and Cambrian-1-8B (50.0), and is competitive with Qwen2-VL-2B (51.5) despite using a smaller target LLM; DPA-Qwen3-32B (61.1) clearly outperforms all public models. On text-only benchmarks, DPA reduces catastrophic forgetting by 32.9% (4B) and 21.6% (32B) relative to the controlled baselines, narrowing the gap to the text-only Qwen3 models (e.g., MATH500: 54.2 vs. 84.8 for Qwen3-4B, vs. 36.4 for the baseline). The gains are model-family-agnostic: DPA-LLaMA-3.2-3B improves over its LLaVA-NeXT counterpart by +3.4 multimodal and +3.6 overall, confirming the architecture is not tied to Qwen3.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dominant LLaVA/LLaVA-NeXT pipeline (ViT encoder → lightweight projector → LLM) by replacing the ViT encoder with a small perceiver VLM whose internal language blocks progressively align visual features with the target LLM's text space before they enter the main model. It is motivated by recent representational analyses (Huang et al. 2025; Nikankin et al. 2025) showing that standard VLMs waste critical LLM depth on superficial modality alignment, and it departs from data-centric text-preservation strategies (DeepSeek-VL, InternVL, MM1) by targeting the structural root cause architecturally rather than through data mixing. The training recipe, evaluation protocol, and inference strategy remain identical to LLaVA-NeXT, making DPA a purely modular encoder swap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DPA offers a drop-in architectural upgrade with negligible overhead (~2% throughput loss at 32B) that consistently improves multimodal performance and reduces text-capability forgetting by 32.9% (4B) / 21.6% (32B), addressing two of the most persistent pain points in VLM development simultaneously. The positive scalability—gains widening from 1.9 to 3.0 points as the target LLM grows—suggests the modality gap is a structural bottleneck that scaling alone cannot resolve, which has implications for how future VLMs allocate depth. Open questions include whether the perceiver can be made even smaller or distilled, whether the instruction-context variant's multi-turn degradation can be mitigated, and whether the approach transfers to video or audio modalities where the modality gap may be even larger.
