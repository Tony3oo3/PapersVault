## Problem

Constructing effective ensembles of outlier detectors without ground-truth labels remains an open challenge: naive aggregation suffers from ensemble saturation (redundant or weak models degrade performance), while existing meta-learning selectors (ELECT, MetaOD) are limited to recommending a single detector rather than adaptively building a compact, diverse ensemble. No prior method combines sequential model selection with diversity control and risk mitigation in a fully unsupervised setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263654/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At test time, MetaEns receives an unlabeled dataset X ∈ R^{N×d} and a pool of 297 pre-fitted candidate detectors spanning 8 algorithmic families (IForest, LOF, kNN, HBOS, OCSVM, LODA, ABOD, COF). All detectors produce Min-Max normalized score vectors o_f ∈ [0,1]^N, and the method operates exclusively on these 1D score summaries rather than raw features, enabling modality-agnostic transfer. The output is a selected ensemble P ⊆ Ω (average size 2.2 on the 39-dataset benchmark) and the mean-aggregated ensemble score vector o_P = (1/|P|) Σ_{f∈P} f(X), from which a user-defined threshold yields final outlier labels.

## Method

MetaEns operates in two phases. Offline, oracle-greedy rollouts on labeled meta-datasets generate state-gain supervision pairs, from which a two-part hurdle model (ExtraTrees classifier predicting P(gain>0) × regressor predicting E[gain|gain>0]) learns to estimate the marginal AP improvement of adding a candidate detector to a partial ensemble; family-level risk priors are simultaneously computed as the 10th percentile of historical gains per algorithmic family. Online, starting from a primary detector selected by ELECT, MetaEns greedily expands the ensemble by maximizing a proxy utility ΔU(f_i|P) = γ(f_i,P)·(Ĝ(f_i|P) − λ_fam·π_fam(f_i)), where γ is a Jaccard-based redundancy discount using maximum similarity to already-selected members, and π_fam penalizes families with negative lower-tail historical gains. Stage-dependent acceptance gates (permissive for the first partner, stricter thereafter) filter statistically unreliable predictions, and adaptive early stopping terminates selection when no candidate yields positive proxy utility, yielding compact ensembles without requiring a fixed budget.

## Results

On the 39-dataset benchmark, MetaEns achieves AP 0.4308±0.0064, AP Rank 59.3±6.96, ROC-AUC 0.7867±0.0045, Precision@π 0.4042±0.0063, and Max-F1 0.4681±0.0069, while using an average ensemble size of only 2.2 models. This outperforms the best meta-learning baseline ELECT (Top-10) by +0.019 AP and the best deep baseline DTE-C by +0.116 AP. The supervised Greedy Oracle upper bound reaches AP 0.6877 with 10 models, indicating MetaEns captures a meaningful fraction of the oracle's potential while remaining fully unsupervised.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MetaEns (AP 0.4308) outperforms all 19 unsupervised baselines across every metric. Against the closest meta-learning competitor ELECT (Top-10, AP 0.4117), MetaEns gains +0.019 AP while using only 2.2 models versus 10, and improves AP Rank from 83.2 to 59.3. The singleton ELECT (Top-1, AP 0.4069) and MetaOD (AP 0.3989) trail by +0.024 and +0.032 AP respectively. All deep learning baselines (RDA 0.2742, DAGMM 0.2958, DeepSVDD 0.2073, ROBOD 0.3135, LUNAR 0.3024, DTE-C 0.3144, TCCM 0.2929) are substantially outperformed, with the best deep method DTE-C lagging by +0.116 AP. Naive ensemble strategies (Mega Ensemble averaging all 297 detectors at AP 0.3970, IForest at 0.3858, Random Ensemble at 0.3759) confirm that indiscriminate aggregation degrades performance, while MetaEns's selective 2.2-model ensemble surpasses them by +0.034 to +0.055 AP. The supervised Greedy Oracle (AP 0.6877, 10 models) remains the upper bound, showing MetaEns recovers roughly 63% of the oracle's AP gap over the best unsupervised baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the meta-learning model selection lineage of MetaOD (NeurIPS 2021) and ELECT (ICDM 2022), which learn to recommend a single best detector for an unlabeled task, to the more general problem of sequential ensemble construction. The key conceptual departure is replacing singleton selection with a greedy sequential process guided by a meta-learned marginal-gain predictor, combined with a submodular-inspired proxy objective that integrates redundancy discounting (drawing on DPP-style diversity penalties) and a novel family-risk regularization term. The two-part hurdle model for gain prediction addresses the zero-inflation problem inherent in ensemble expansion, and the score-only state representation enables modality-agnostic transfer that prior meta-learning selectors (which often encode task-level features) do not support.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in demonstrating that compact, adaptively-sized ensembles (average 2.2 models) can outperform larger fixed-size ensembles (10 models) in fully unsupervised settings, reducing computational cost while improving detection quality. The score-only design's ability to transfer across modalities (tabular → image → text) without retraining is a useful property for multi-domain anomaly detection pipelines. Limitations include underperformance on very low-dimensional datasets where classical detectors are already near-optimal, and dilution of dominant single-detector signals under mean aggregation. Open questions include theoretical guarantees for the proxy objective, extension to streaming/non-stationary data, and whether richer meta-representations (beyond score statistics) could improve gain prediction under distribution shift.
