## Problem

In-context learning and activation steering are two widely used but theoretically disconnected methods for controlling LLM behavior at inference time, with prior work offering separate accounts for each. The paper asks whether these disparate interventions are instances of a single, predictive framework, and if so, what that framework implies about how LLMs represent and update beliefs in latent concepts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260288/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model is evaluated on a 2D grid of in-context shot counts N (0–128) and steering magnitudes m (−10 to +10), producing a full behavioral heatmap of p(y|x) for each {dataset, model} combination. The fitted Bayesian model has only four free parameters (α, γ, a, b) and outputs a closed-form prediction of behavior probability, the crossover point N*(m) where belief in the target concept surpasses its complement, and the resulting phase boundary separating c-dominated from c'-dominated regimes. Predictions are validated via 10-fold cross-validation over adjacent magnitude values, yielding held-out correlations of r=0.98 averaged across five persona domains.

## Method

The paper proposes a unified Bayesian belief dynamics model in which ICL and activation steering both operate by updating an LLM's posterior belief in a latent concept c. ICL accumulates evidence through the likelihood p(x|c), modeled with a sub-linear power-law discount τ(N)=N^(−α) that yields log Bayes factor scaling as γN^(1−α); activation steering, under the Linear Representation Hypothesis, produces a constant additive shift a·m in log posterior odds by altering the concept prior. The final model is log o(c|x) = a·m + b + γN^(1−α), a four-parameter sigmoid in probability space. This formulation predicts three testable phenomena: sigmoidal ICL learning curves as a function of N^(1−α), leftward/rightward shifts of the ICL curve under positive/negative steering, and additive interaction in log-belief space producing sharp phase transitions. Parameters are fit via L-BFGS-B with basin-hopping initialization on per-shot averaged probabilities, and predictions are validated with cross-validation over steering magnitude.

## Results

The Bayesian belief dynamics model (Eq. 9) achieves a held-out cross-validated correlation of r=0.98 (p<.001) between predicted and empirical LLM behavior, averaged across five persona domains on Llama-3.1-8B. The model generalizes across architectures: r=0.98 for Qwen-2.5-7B, r=0.97 for Gemma-2-9B, and r=0.98 for Llama-3.1-70B. The predicted phase-boundary crossover point N*(m) correlates at r=0.97 (p<.001) with empirically observed transition points. Extended evaluations on high-prior concepts (r=0.87) and flipped-label sentiment analysis (r=0.96) confirm the model's generality beyond low-prior persona settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not benchmark against competing control methods; instead, it validates a unified Bayesian model against raw LLM behavior across two intervention axes (context length N and steering magnitude m). The model's predictions are compared to empirical Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen-2.5-7B, Gemma-2-9B, and Llama-3.1-70B outputs using 10-fold cross-validation over steering magnitudes, yielding r≥0.87 in all settings. The paper replicates the sigmoidal many-shot ICL learning curves of Anil et al. (2024) and Agarwal et al. (2024), but adds the precise functional form p(y|x)=σ(log(prior)+γN^(1−α)) and shows that activation steering (via CAA, following Turner et al. 2024) shifts this curve additively in log-odds space. The key empirical contribution over prior work is the joint phase diagram (Fig. 6) showing that ICL and steering interact additively to produce sharp behavioral transitions, and the closed-form prediction of the crossover point N*(m) (Eq. 10), which was not available in prior many-shot ICL or steering literature.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate theoretical threads: the Bayesian account of in-context learning (Xie et al. 2022; Wurgaft et al. 2026; Bigelow et al. 2024) and the Linear Representation Hypothesis underpinning activation steering (Park et al. 2024; Turner et al. 2024; Rimsky et al. 2024). The key conceptual departure is showing that these interventions are two terms in a single log-belief equation—ICL accumulates evidence (likelihood) while steering shifts the prior—yielding a closed-form 4-parameter model. The power-law discount on evidence accumulation follows Wurgaft et al. (2026) and Anil et al. (2024), and the likelihood function draws on Goodman et al. (2008), but the additive combination and the resulting phase-boundary prediction are new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a concrete, testable prediction for when LLM behavior will abruptly shift (the crossover point N*(m)), which has direct implications for understanding and mitigating many-shot jailbreaking. Limitations include restriction to binary concepts, a single steering method (CAA), and primarily ~8B-scale models, leaving open how the theory extends to multi-concept spaces, SAE-based steering, and larger models. The paper also raises important mechanistic questions: why belief appears localized to specific layers, whether non-linear belief representations exist outside the LRH regime, and how inference is algorithmically implemented in the network.
