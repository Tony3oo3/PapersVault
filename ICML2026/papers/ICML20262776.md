## Problem

Short-term precipitation nowcasting from limited radar history (typically 4–8 frames) is fundamentally under-constrained: identical observations admit multiple physically plausible futures (translation, splitting, intensification, dissipation), and vision-only generative models lack explicit motion-semantic constraints to disambiguate them. This results in blurred details, trajectory instability, and severe degradation in extreme-event detection (high-threshold CSI), particularly at longer lead times.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262776/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 4-frame radar echo sequence at 256×256 resolution (5-minute intervals) and a natural-language motion description (≤120 tokens, generated by Qwen-VL from the same 4 frames and encoded via T5-XXL) as joint conditioning. It produces a 16-frame future radar sequence at the same resolution, operating in a 32×32×4 latent space (8× spatial downsampling via a 2D VAE with no temporal compression). The full inference pipeline including VLM captioning averages 5.4 s per sample on a single RTX 4090, fitting within the 5-minute operational radar update cycle.

## Method

LangPrecip reformulates nowcasting as semantically constrained trajectory generation under the Rectified Flow paradigm: a 0.6B-parameter DiT backbone (DTCA architecture) learns a velocity field in latent space, with cross-attention injecting T5-XXL text embeddings as explicit motion priors that regularize under-determined radar dynamics. A key architectural contribution is the Wavelet Consistency Unfolding Decoder (WCUD), which replaces the standard VAE decoder with T=4 learnable unfolding blocks, each pairing a Data Consistency module (one gradient-descent step on the latent-to-observation fidelity term via back-projection) with a Wavelet Prior module (learnable soft-thresholding of high-frequency wavelet subbands) to enforce observation consistency and restore sharp precipitation boundaries. The framework is trained with a velocity-matching objective (L2 loss on predicted vs. true velocity) using classifier-free guidance (CFG scale 6 for Swedish, 4 for MRMS) to modulate language conditioning strength. The accompanying LangPrecip-160K dataset (100K Swedish + 60K MRMS events, 20-frame sequences at 5-min intervals) provides the first large-scale radar-text pairs where motion descriptions encode physically meaningful kinematic priors (advection direction, growth/decay, structural evolution).

## Results

On the Swedish dataset at 80-minute lead time, LangPrecip achieves CSI≥6.3 mm/h of 0.074 (a 60.9% relative gain over the best baseline DTCA at 0.046) and the best FSS of 0.923. On MRMS, it reaches CSI≥8 mm/h of 0.031 (19.2% over DTCA's 0.026) and FSS of 0.725. LangPrecip also attains the highest CSI≥0.06 mm/h (0.586 Swedish, 0.435 MRMS) and best SSIM (0.732 Swedish, 0.905 MRMS), while maintaining competitive CRPS (0.132 Swedish, 0.125 MRMS). The full inference pipeline averages 5.4 s per sample on a single RTX 4090, well within the 5-minute operational radar update cycle.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LangPrecip outperforms all nine baselines on the critical heavy-precipitation CSI metric: it leads DTCA (the strongest vision-only competitor) by 60.9% on Swedish CSI≥6.3 mm/h (0.074 vs. 0.046) and by 19.2% on MRMS CSI≥8 mm/h (0.031 vs. 0.026). Diffusion-based methods (DiffCast, TRDM, SSLDM-ISI) show severe collapse at high thresholds (extreme CSI < 0.04), trailing LangPrecip by 50–80% in relative performance despite achieving high SSIM (>0.66), indicating that visual coherence alone fails to resolve rare-event dynamics. DTCA achieves the lowest CRPS (0.115 Swedish) through visual attention but sacrifices physical fidelity, lagging 60.9% behind in extreme CSI. On MRMS, LangPrecip also leads on FSS (0.725 vs. DTCA's 0.711) and SSIM (0.905 vs. 0.899), while RainNet and SSRF-Net underperform substantially on FSS (0.386 and 0.402 on Swedish), suggesting poor spatial structure preservation. The ablation in Table 3 confirms that language conditioning alone yields a 40% gain in extreme precipitation skill on Swedish (CSI≥6.3: 0.0530→0.0746), validating that the gains stem from semantic motion priors rather than architectural changes alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Rectified Flow generative paradigm (Liu et al., 2022; Lipman et al., 2022) and the DTCA diffusion-transformer backbone (Li et al., 2024) to precipitation nowcasting by introducing natural-language motion descriptions as an explicit conditioning signal via cross-attention, drawing conceptual inspiration from text-to-video generation (Imagen Video, Make-A-Video, Video LDM). It departs from the vision-only nowcasting lineage (ConvLSTM, DGMR, DiffCast, SSLDM-ISI) by arguing that pixel-level stochasticity alone cannot resolve trajectory ambiguity under short observation windows, and by adding a model-based Wavelet Consistency Unfolding Decoder that replaces the standard VAE decoder with an unfolded proximal-optimization reconstruction. The LangPrecip-160K dataset is the first to pair radar sequences with meteorological motion text, filling a gap the authors identify in existing radar datasets that lack semantic annotations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 60% relative CSI gain on heavy-rainfall detection (Swedish, 80-min lead) is operationally significant for flash-flood early warning, and the LangPrecip-160K dataset provides a reusable resource for multimodal weather research. However, the approach relies on general-purpose VLMs (Qwen-VL) for annotation without meteorological specialization, and generalization to tropical convection, cyclones, and monsoon systems remains unvalidated. An open tension the paper acknowledges is the CRPS–CSI trade-off: language conditioning sharpens spatial precision at the cost of ensemble diversity, raising questions about how to preserve probabilistic calibration while exploiting semantic priors. Future integration of structured NWP wind fields as complementary guidance and hierarchical pixel/semantic motion modeling are identified as promising next steps.
