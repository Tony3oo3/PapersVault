## Problem

Most time series generation methods assume regular sampling and fixed output resolutions, while real-world observations (e.g., ICU records, financial ticks) are irregular and sparse yet downstream tasks require continuous, high-resolution sequences. Existing NCDE-based generators only perform irregular-to-regular generation and rely on a single dynamics function with tightly coupled optimization, limiting their ability to model complex temporal patterns and generalize to newly generated samples.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265764/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes irregularly sampled multivariate time series (discrete observations at non-uniform timestamps, with 30–70% of points randomly dropped) and produces continuous time series values at arbitrary time points within [0, T], effectively doubling the temporal resolution. As an intermediate step, it also outputs regular imputed sequences and sample-specific MoE weight vectors (one per generated sample) that encode the temporal dynamics of each instance. The diffusion model jointly generates the data and its matched MoE weights, so the output is a pair (sequence, dynamics parameters) rather than a standalone sequence.

## Method

Diff-MN builds on three coupled components. First, a dense Mixture-of-Experts NCDE replaces the single dynamics function with N_e expert networks whose outputs are combined via softmax router weights, allowing different experts to capture distinct temporal patterns (local trends, global oscillations, etc.). Second, a decoupled optimization design pre-trains a channel-wise autoencoder (encoder CE and decoder CD) on the irregular data and freezes it in place of the standard SIN and RN, so gradient updates focus exclusively on the MoE dynamics and router. Third, a U-Net-based diffusion model is trained jointly on the imputed regular sequences and their corresponding MoE weights, learning the joint distribution p(data, weights); at generation time, the diffusion model produces a new sample together with adapted MoE weights that are substituted into the pre-trained MoE-NCDE, enabling sample-specific continuous generation without retraining the dynamics.

## Results

On four popular TSG datasets (Sines, Stocks, Energy, MuJoCo) with 50% observations dropped, Diff-MN achieves DS of 0.128–0.487, MDD of 0.252–1.093, and KL of 0.009–0.094, consistently outperforming all seven baselines. On four medical ECG datasets under the same setting, Diff-MN reaches DS of 0.070–0.326, MDD of 0.109–0.972, and KL of 0.021–0.099. For the irregular-to-continuous TSG task (Table 3), Diff-MN with generated MoE weights (Ours-Ŝ_MoE) achieves MSE of 0.002–0.029 and MAE of 0.025–0.131, improving over its own non-continuous version and substantially outperforming KOVAE-NCDE and GTGAN-NCDE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Diff-MN wins on all three metrics (DS, MDD, KL) across all four popular and four medical datasets at every missing rate (30%, 50%, 70%). The closest competitor is ProFITi (conditional normalizing flows), which is competitive on MDD for Stocks (0.408 vs. 0.281 at 50%) and on KL for Energy/MuJoCo (0.033/0.015 vs. 0.022/0.009), but Diff-MN still leads on DS by a wide margin (e.g., 0.137 vs. 0.483 on Stocks). Among NCDE-based baselines, KoVAE and GT-GAN are the strongest, yet Diff-MN reduces MDD by 2–5× (e.g., 1.093 vs. 5.584 on Sines) and KL by 10–200× (e.g., 0.023 vs. 4.719 on Sines). Regular TSG methods adapted with NCDE imputation (TimeGAN-NCDE, TimeVAE-NCDE, Diffusion-NCDE) perform substantially worse, with TimeGAN-NCDE reaching KL values as high as 12.478 on Sines at 70% missing. For the continuous TSG task, Diff-MN with generated MoE weights (Ours-Ŝ_MoE) outperforms its own non-continuous variant (e.g., MSE 0.026 vs. 0.049 on Sines at 30%), while KOVAE-NCDE and GTGAN-NCDE actually degrade relative to their non-continuous counterparts, confirming that standard NCDE is ineffective for continuous generation without sample-specific dynamics parameterization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the NCDE-based irregular time series modeling line (Kidger et al., 2020) and the two-stage TSG paradigm used by KOVAE and GTGAN, which first regularize irregular inputs via NCDE and then apply a standard generator (VAE/GAN) on a fixed grid. It departs from this pipeline in two key ways: (i) replacing the single NCDE dynamics function with a dense Mixture-of-Experts that decomposes complex temporal patterns, and (ii) decoupling optimization by freezing a pretrained channel-wise autoencoder in place of the jointly-trained SIN and Readout networks. The diffusion model is then used not as the primary generator but to parameterize the MoE routing weights, enabling sample-specific dynamics for newly generated sequences — a role distinct from prior diffusion-based TSG methods that generate the full sequence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper formalizes continuous TSG (arbitrary-resolution output from irregular inputs) as a distinct task, which is practically relevant for domains like ICU monitoring where sparse, irregular observations must support dense trajectory simulation. Key limitations include reliance on simulated missingness rather than naturally irregular data, degradation of MoE performance as expert count grows under high missingness, and the computational cost inherited from NCDE solving. Open questions include whether the MoE-weight parameterization strategy generalizes to other continuous-time architectures, how to evaluate on truly irregular data without held-out ground truth, and whether effective sparse MoE training under data scarcity can be achieved.
