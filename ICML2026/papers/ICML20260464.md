## Problem

Population Based Training (PBT) variants dynamically adjust hyperparameters during neural network training, but their performance is highly sensitive to the 'step size' meta-hyperparameter (the number of weight-update steps between HP adjustments). No existing method or intuition exists for setting this value without per-task empirical tuning, which undermines the efficiency advantage that makes PBT attractive for expensive training tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260464/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

IPBT takes a fixed neural network architecture (ResNet-12 or ConvNeXt-T for image classification; a PPO agent for reinforcement learning), a training dataset, a defined hyperparameter search space, and a total computation budget equivalent to 8 full training runs. It produces an optimized hyperparameter schedule (not just a single HP setting) along with trained model weights. The method is designed for low-budget, black-box settings where the budget is smaller than the search space dimensionality, and it runs in parallel with wall-clock time comparable to training a single network.

## Method

IPBT performs multiple iterations of a Bayesian-optimization PBT procedure, doubling the step size on each restart to progressively target longer-term performance. A data-driven stagnation detector triggers restarts by z-score normalizing and GP-smoothing the best performance trajectory, then checking two criteria: no improvement for t_patience consecutive steps, or improvement below one standard deviation over t_interval steps. Upon restart, weights are reused in a task-agnostic manner via shrink-perturb (scaling by λ_shrink=0.2 and adding noise scaled by γ_perturb=0.1), while half the population is randomly reinitialized to escape unfavorable basins. Hyperparameters are reinitialized via a time-varying meta-BO procedure that learns across restarts from the long-term performance of initial HP configurations, with half the population receiving random HPs to maintain exploration. The algorithm starts with a small step size (1% of budget) to capture fast gains, then exponentially increases it to target longer-term optimization.

## Results

IPBT is evaluated on 8 tasks (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, FashionMNIST, TinyImageNet, Humanoid, Hopper, Pusher, Walker) with a budget of 8 full training runs and population size 8. Across all 8 tasks, IPBT achieves the highest Interquartile Mean (IQM) of normalized performance among all PBT variants without any per-task step-size tuning, and significantly outperforms Random Search, ASHA, and SMAC3. The only comparison where IPBT does not achieve statistical significance is against BG-PBT with a tuned step size (p=0.497), though BG-PBT requires 4× the compute to identify the best step size. All other pairwise comparisons (vs. PBT, PB2, PB2-Mix, FIRE-PBT, RS, ASHA, SMAC3) are statistically significant after Holm correction at α=0.05.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IPBT significantly outperforms all five PBT variants (PBT, PB2, PB2-Mix, FIRE-PBT, and untuned BG-PBT) as well as the three non-PBT baselines (Random Search, ASHA, SMAC3) across the 8-task benchmark, with Holm-corrected p-values ranging from 0.00016 to 0.04140. The strongest margins are against FIRE-PBT and SMAC3 (p=0.00002), while the weakest significant margin is against vanilla PBT (p=0.04140). The only non-significant comparison is against BG-PBT with a per-task tuned step size (p=0.497), where BG-PBT achieves a marginally higher IQM but at 4× the computational cost of IPBT (four runs at different step sizes vs. one run). Notably, SMAC3 performs on par with Random Search, likely because it allocates too much budget to cheap evaluations in the low-budget regime. IPBT's advantage is most pronounced on tasks where step size matters critically (e.g., Humanoid favors large step sizes while Hopper favors small ones), as IPBT automatically adapts its step size via restarts without requiring task-specific tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

IPBT extends the PBT family (Jaderberg et al., 2017; Parker-Holder et al., 2020, 2021; Wan et al., 2022) by introducing automatic step-size adjustment via restarts, building most directly on BG-PBT's restart mechanism. It departs from BG-PBT in three key ways: replacing task-specific distillation with task-agnostic shrink-perturb for weight reuse, substituting a global BO procedure with time-varying meta BO for cross-restart HP reinitialization, and introducing a data-driven stagnation detector that replaces BG-PBT's fixed patience and fixed-budget thresholds. The exponential step-size doubling on restarts is a novel scheduling strategy not present in any prior PBT variant, including MF-PBT which instead runs parallel subpopulations at different frequencies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

IPBT addresses a practical pain point in PBT-based HPO: the step-size meta-HP must currently be set empirically per task, undermining the 'out-of-the-box' appeal of PBT variants. By automating this choice within the same wall-clock budget, IPBT makes PBT more accessible for low-budget (≈8 training runs) black-box settings without per-task tuning. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-objective optimization, NAS, and Transformer architectures, and whether tuning IPBT's own hyperparameters (shrink-perturb parameters, patience values) could yield further gains. The method is explicitly scoped to small budgets and black-box settings, so it is unlikely to displace prior-information or large-budget BO methods.
