## Problem

Existing diffusion language model (DLM) inference methods rely on single-step predictive distributions (entropy or max probability) to select which masked tokens to decode, making them susceptible to local optima from over-confident error predictions, lacking a theoretical bridge to sampling error bounds, and suffering catastrophic performance degradation under highly parallel decoding. The paper asks whether leveraging the multi-step nature of DLM decoding—where each position is predicted multiple times before being unmasked—can yield a more reliable, theoretically grounded, and parallelism-robust sampling procedure.

## Input / Output

CCD operates as a training-free post-processing layer on top of a pre-trained DLM (Dream-7B or LLaDA-8B), consuming the model's per-position predictive distributions at each diffusion step. It maintains a sliding-window historical buffer of only the top-V most confident token predictions from the most recent d iterations (default V=4, d=3), reducing memory from O((T−t)·N·|X|) to O(d·V·|X|). The adaptive variant CCD-DS additionally outputs a per-step decoding budget that dynamically ranges from 0 to V, replacing the fixed uniform budget b_t used by baselines.

## Method

CCD approximates the true marginal token distribution p(x_i|s) by Monte Carlo averaging the predictive distributions over a sliding window of d recent iterations, restricted to token positions that appear in the top-V confident set across all those iterations (an intersection criterion that filters noisy early-step predictions). This is theoretically shown to be equivalent to modeling the conditional mutual information I(x_i; c|s) between the evolving context and the token prediction, which in turn governs the DLM sampling error upper bound (Prop. 3.3), providing a provable error control mechanism absent in single-step methods. The adaptive extension CCD-DS exploits the natural variation in the number of consistently confident tokens |I_t^c| to dynamically increase the decoding budget for context-insensitive regions (e.g., EOS plateaus, formulaic phrases) while deferring context-sensitive tokens, yielding up to 3.7× speedup on Dream with milder performance degradation than Fast-dLLM under parallel decoding.

## Results

CCD achieves consistent performance gains across all five benchmarks for both Dream-7B and LLaDA-8B under uniform sampling (b_t=1). The most notable improvements are on Dream: +4.05 on HumanEval (56.71 vs. 52.66), +1.83 on Trip Plan (16.93 vs. 15.10), and +1.50 on GSM8K (82.51 vs. 81.01). The adaptive variant CCD-DS further accelerates inference by 1.11×–3.69× (e.g., 3.08× on Dream HumanEval, 3.40× on Dream Trip) while maintaining or even improving accuracy (Trip: 19.01% with CCD-DS vs. 16.93% with CCD). Under highly parallel decoding (b_t=3), CCD reduces average performance degradation from ~24% (Dream default on HumanEval) to ~19% (CCD), demonstrating significantly milder degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CCD outperforms all three baselines (Margin Conf., Credit/dInfer, and Fast-dLLM) on the majority of benchmark–model combinations. Against Margin Conf. (Kim et al., 2025), CCD wins on 8 of 10 model-benchmark pairs; Margin Conf. actually degrades performance on LLaDA GSM8K (−0.91) and HumanEval (−1.20) relative to default, while CCD improves both. Against Credit/dInfer (Ma et al., 2025b), CCD achieves larger gains on Dream HumanEval (+4.05 vs. +3.05) and Dream GSM8K (+1.50 vs. +0.87), though Credit edges out CCD on LLaDA MBPP (40.60 vs. 39.20). Under highly parallel decoding (b_t=3), CCD's advantage is most dramatic: on Dream HumanEval, CCD maintains 45.73% at b_t=3 (avg 50.62%) while Credit drops to 20.73% (avg 38.89%) and Margin Conf. to 23.17% (avg 41.66%). Against Fast-dLLM (Wu et al., 2025) at comparable ~4× speedup on LLaDA, CCD-DS achieves higher accuracy on GSM8K (78.77 vs. 78.01), MATH (36.30 vs. 35.80), and HumanEval (38.00 vs. 35.90), with only a marginal loss on MBPP (38.60 vs. 38.80).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sampling error upper bound from Li & Cai (2026) to conditional generation, using it as the theoretical anchor for a new decoding criterion. It departs from the dominant single-step confidence paradigm in DLM inference (as used by Dream and LLaDA) by marginalizing over the evolving context trajectory, which the authors show is equivalent to modeling conditional mutual information between contexts and token predictions. Compared to concurrent work like dInfer's credit decoding (which fuses logits over time) and temporal self-consistency voting (a post-processing approach), CCD uses a sliding-window intersection of top-V confident sets to target predictive consistency directly at decoding time without long-term accumulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in DLM deployment: existing single-step decoding suffers catastrophic degradation under parallel decoding (e.g., HumanEval drops from 52.66 to 23.17 at b_t=3 for Dream), which blocks the efficiency gains that DLMs' parallelism promises. CCD's milder degradation (45.73 at b_t=3) and the CCD-DS adaptive budget (up to 3.69× speedup on MBPP) make high-parallelism DLM inference more viable. Open questions include whether the three approximations in the sliding-window buffer (truncation, top-V filtering, intersection) can be replaced by more principled estimators, and whether the theoretical MI-based error governance translates to tighter guarantees at larger scales.
