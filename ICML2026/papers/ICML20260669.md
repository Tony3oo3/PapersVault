## Problem

Existing learning-augmented paging algorithms achieve at best 2H_k + O(1) robustness in the randomized setting, leaving a multiplicative gap to the optimal competitive ratio H_k. The paper asks how to close this gap to H_k + O(1) while simultaneously achieving 1-consistency, and what unifying principle underlies bounded-robustness designs in this area.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260669/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm processes a page request sequence revealed online against a cache of size k, consuming predicted next-arrival times for each cached page at eviction decision points. Predictions are consumed only at two types of events: L_0-misses (requests to pages outside the work-function support) and lazy adversary requests (requests to unrevealed pages within the support). The output is a cache eviction decision at each miss, maintaining a valid configuration in the work-function layer structure; the algorithm tracks a relative prediction budget B, an eviction-effectiveness metric Y (the number of unrevealed layers), and the full layer partition (L_0, …, L_k) updated in O(log k) per request.

## Method

RPB-OM builds on the ONOPT/ONLINEMIN framework, which maintains a single valid configuration via a priority-greedy process over work-function layers, and augments it with a Relative Prediction Budget (RPB) mechanism. At each L_0-miss, the algorithm always evicts following predictions (guaranteeing 1-consistency) and resets the budget to a constant τ. At subsequent lazy adversary requests, a gate function G_RPB(Y,ω) = (U(ω) ≤ (Y+2)/e − 2) determines whether the algorithm earns one unit of budget; if the budget is positive, it evicts following predictions, otherwise it falls back to OM's priority-based eviction. The gate adaptively correlates the number of unrevealed layers (a proxy for prediction effectiveness) with the budget, avoiding both the overuse of predictions in PREDICTIVEMARKER (4H_k robustness) and the underuse in LMARKER (weak practical performance). Robustness is proven via a new potential function Φ(ω) = φ(ω) + D(ω) + B(ω) combining OM's expected-cost potential, the expected cache difference between RPB-OM and OM, and the remaining budget, together with a new property (Lemma 5.6) bounding the potential decrease per lazy adversary request by −1/(U(ω)+1).

## Results

On the SPEC CPU 2006 benchmark (k=16), RPB-OM achieves the best average cost ratio among all learning-augmented algorithms under both real predictors: 1.249 (τ=1) with PLECO and 1.213 (τ=4) with POPU, corresponding to hit ratios of 22.75% and 24.55% respectively. Theoretically, RPB-OM achieves (H_k + O(1))-robustness and 1-consistency, closing the gap from the prior best of 2H_k + O(1) to within an additive constant of the optimal H_k competitive ratio. The algorithm also matches the best-known smoothness bound of O(1 + log(η₁/cost(OPT))).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RPB-OM outperforms all seven baselines on both predictors. Against the strongest prior learning-augmented method BLINDORACLE&LRU (Wei, 2020), RPB-OM (τ=1) reduces the PLECO cost ratio from 1.286 to 1.249 (−2.9%) and the POPU cost ratio from 1.230 to 1.220 (−0.8%). Against MARKER-based methods LMARKER (Rohatgi, 2020) and PREDICTIVEMARKER (Lykouris & Vassilvitskii, 2018), RPB-OM improves the PLECO cost ratio from 1.335 to 1.249 (−6.4%) while also achieving 1-consistency that these methods lack. Against F&R (Sadek & Elias, 2024), RPB-OM improves from 1.359 to 1.249 under PLECO and from 1.319 to 1.213 under POPU. Against the non-learning baseline ONLINEMIN (Brodal et al., 2015), RPB-OM improves from 1.396 to 1.249 (PLECO) and 1.396 to 1.213 (POPU). The closest competitor is ONOPT-OM (the same framework without the relative prediction budget), which RPB-OM narrowly beats (1.253→1.249 PLECO; 1.237→1.220 POPU), confirming the value of the adaptive budget mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the ONOPT/ONLINEMIN framework (Brodal et al., 2015; Moruz & Negoescu, 2012), which achieves the optimal H_k-competitive ratio for randomized paging via the work function, and departs from the MARKER-based lineage (PREDICTIVEMARKER, LMARKER) that has dominated learning-augmented paging and is inherently capped at 2H_k + O(1) robustness. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of the 'relative prediction budget' as a unifying primitive that reframes how prior algorithms earn and spend prediction-driven deviation from a robust baseline, revealing that existing methods either overuse (PREDICTIVEMARKER, 4H_k) or underutilize (LMARKER, 2H_k+4) predictions. By plugging OM's priorities into a new RPB-ONOPT framework with an adaptive budget gate tied to the number of unrevealed layers, the paper achieves H_k + O(1) robustness—essentially halving the prior best gap to the online optimum.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Closing the robustness gap from 2H_k + O(1) to H_k + O(1) resolves a long-standing open problem in learning-augmented paging and demonstrates that the work-function-based ONOPT class, rather than MARKER, is the correct substrate for near-optimal learning-augmented designs. The relative prediction budget concept provides a general diagnostic tool that could guide robustness improvements in other online problems (ski rental, knapsack, graph algorithms) where prediction-driven deviations from a baseline must be carefully budgeted. Limitations include the residual O(1) additive constant (exact H_k robustness would require ignoring predictions entirely), the complexity of the potential-function analysis, and only modest empirical gains over LMARKER on SPEC CPU 2006 traces. Open questions include whether the RPB framework transfers to other online problems and whether the additive constant can be further tightened.
