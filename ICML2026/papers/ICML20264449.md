## Problem

Existing multi-view sleep staging methods assume that different modalities (EEG, EOG) are well-aligned and assign them equal or fixed weights, yet in practice their predictions can conflict. Even uncertainty-aware fusion methods like ECML implicitly assume that incorporating additional opinions always reduces overall uncertainty, which is unreasonable when views disagree. This paper addresses the gap by explicitly modeling inter-view conflicts and designing an aggregation mechanism whose behavior adapts to the degree of disagreement.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264449/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 30-second sleep epochs containing paired EEG and EOG signals (x ∈ R^{2×T} at 100–128 Hz), processed in sequences of 20 epochs to capture stage-transition context. Rather than outputting a single class label, each view produces a subjective-logic opinion (five belief masses plus one uncertainty mass summing to 1), and the final output is a joint opinion with per-class probabilities and an overall uncertainty score. Notably, EOG views operate on a coarse 3-class structure (W, REM, NREM) that is mapped to the 5-class space before fusion, reflecting the physiological role of EOG primarily in REM detection.

## Method

ConfSleepNet employs four parallel evidential DNNs (two for fine-grained 5-class EEG/multi-view inputs, two for coarse-grained 3-class EOG/multi-view inputs) built on dual-branch CNNs with GCNet-1D attention, cross-attention, and Bi-LSTM for temporal modeling, each outputting non-negative evidence vectors via SoftPlus. A fixed evidence mapping matrix converts coarse NREM evidence into equally distributed N1/N2/N3 evidence while preserving total evidence mass. The key contribution is the Conflict-Aware Multi-View Aggregation (CMAM): a conflict-degree metric C ∈ [0,1] quantifies pairwise belief-mass disagreement, and the combination rule for uncertainty is u^{a∇b} = C·(2u^a u^b)/(u^a+u^b) + (1−C)·u^a u^b, which provably reduces uncertainty for consistent opinions (C→0) and increases it for conflicting ones (C→1). Training uses a Dirichlet-based accuracy loss plus an annealed KL-divergence term that encourages evidence collapse to zero for misclassified samples, jointly optimizing all four networks.

## Results

On the four sleep staging benchmarks, ConfSleepNet achieves the best accuracy across all datasets: 87.2% on SleepEDF-20, 85.3% on SleepEDF-78, 88.9% on MASS-SS3, and 88.2% on SHHS, with corresponding macro-F1 scores of 81.8%, 78.8%, 84.2%, and 81.3%. The method also attains the highest per-class F1 for the clinically critical REM stage on every dataset (89.1%, 86.0%, 90.2%, 89.7%). On the multi-view classification benchmark (Table 3), the proposed CMAM aggregation achieves the best accuracy on Scene15 (73.01%), CUB (95.00%), and PIE (95.74%), while trailing CCML by 0.3% on HandWritten (98.45% vs. 98.75%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ConfSleepNet outperforms all 13 baselines on overall accuracy across all four sleep staging datasets, with the largest margins over DeepSleepNet (2.7–5.2% ACC gain) and TMCEK (2.2–4.9% ACC gain). Against the strongest multi-view baselines, ConfSleepNet leads XSleepNet by 0.6–2.0% ACC and FlexibleSleepNet by 0.4–1.3% ACC, while also achieving the highest REM F1 on every dataset (e.g., 90.2% on MASS-SS3 vs. 89.8% for XSleepNet). On the multi-view classification benchmark, CMAM achieves the best accuracy on Scene15 (73.01% vs. 72.60% for CCML), CUB (95.00% vs. 94.58% for CCML), and PIE (95.74% vs. 95.15% for TMCEK), but is narrowly outperformed by CCML on HandWritten (98.45% vs. 98.75%). The ablation variant ConfSleepNet- (average fusion) trails the full model by 0.8–1.5% ACC, confirming that the conflict-aware aggregation is the primary source of the performance gain over simpler fusion strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the evidential deep learning framework (Sensoy et al., 2018) and subjective logic (Jøsang, 2018) to model per-view classification uncertainty via Dirichlet distributions, but departs from prior EDL-based multi-view fusion methods (ETMC, RCML, CCML, TMCEK) that rely on Dempster-Shafer combination rules implicitly assuming more certain opinions always reduce overall uncertainty. It also diverges from existing multi-view sleep staging architectures (XSleepNet, SalientSleepNet, HMDT-Net) that treat EEG and EOG indiscriminately with a uniform five-class structure, instead introducing a hybrid category structure that assigns coarse-grained (W/REM/NREM) classification to EOG and fine-grained (W/N1/N2/N3/REM) classification to EEG, reflecting their distinct physiological roles. The conflict-aware multi-view aggregation (CMAM) is the key methodological departure, explicitly quantifying inter-view disagreement and modulating the fusion accordingly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The conflict-aware aggregation addresses a practically important failure mode in multi-modal sleep staging: when EEG and EOG views disagree (particularly at sleep stage transitions), naive fusion can produce overconfident but incorrect predictions. The theoretical guarantees (Propositions 1 and 2) that consistent views reduce uncertainty while conflicting views increase it provide a principled foundation absent in prior EDL-based fusion. However, the acknowledged non-associativity of CMAM limits its scalability to many views, and the uniform evidence mapping from NREM to N1/N2/N3 is a simplification that may not hold in all clinical populations. The generalizability of CMAM to other multi-view tasks (validated on HW, Scene15, CUB, PIE) suggests broader applicability beyond sleep staging, potentially to medical imaging and other multi-modal classification settings.
