## Problem

Existing LLM-based multi-agent systems predominantly optimize coordination topology while largely ignoring how messages are actually transmitted and optimized along edges. The standard scheme of directly concatenating first-order neighbor responses creates a restricted evidence receptive field, causing multi-hop insights to be progressively homogenized and diluted through intermediate agents' paraphrasing. This paper tackles the underexplored question of how to structure the communication pipeline itself to preserve long-range evidence while remaining context-efficient.

## Input / Output

The method takes a task query, a directed acyclic graph (DAG) communication topology, and the raw natural-language responses from all upstream agents reachable within K hops. Messages are grouped by shortest hop distance and ordered far-to-near (K, K−1, …, 1) to align with the reasoning flow. The output is a single consolidated prompt fed to the target agent, where the consolidation operator Φ enforces a topology-aware message-count budget B_msg and a per-message length cap (κ < 0.5 of original tokens), ensuring the final context stays within the LLM's window while preserving topological precedence.

## Method

MOC constructs a structured multi-order evidence stream by computing k-hop reachability via powers of the adjacency matrix, assigning each upstream message to its shortest hop order, and concatenating the resulting disjoint sets in far-to-near order. To handle the resulting context bloat, the paper introduces a Semantic-Topological Merging operator Φ that projects messages into a shared embedding space (MiniLM, d=384), identifies high-similarity pairs (cosine ≥ S_max − ε), and distills each pair with a lightweight LLM (gemma2-9B) under a hard length constraint, selecting the best of M=5 candidates by maximum cosine similarity to both original sources. A forward-merging strategy anchors each merge at the later-ranked message to preserve the topological execution order, and a batch-wise ε-approximate merging strategy merges a maximal disjoint set of pairs per iteration for logarithmic reduction in consolidation rounds. The unified formulation R_j = f_j(P_sys,j ⊕ Q ⊕ (Ψ ∘ Φ(M_j | Π_j))) generalizes the vanilla scheme (which is recovered when Φ is identity and K=1), and experiments across six benchmarks and three LLM backbones (Gemma-2-27B, Qwen2.5-32B, DeepSeek-V3.2-685B) show consistent gains with K=2 as the optimal receptive field.

## Results

On Gemma-2-27B-Instruct with 7 agents, MOC consistently outperforms Vanilla MAS across all edge densities (ρ=0.3 to 1.0), with the largest average gain of 2.30% at ρ=0.3 (0.7732→0.7910). The most notable individual improvement is on AQuA at ρ=0.3, where accuracy jumps from 0.6969 to 0.7441 (+6.77%). On Qwen2.5-32B, MOC achieves +3.84% on MMLU (ρ=0.7) and +3.68% on HumanEval (ρ=0.3). On the much larger DeepSeek-V3.2-685B, MOC still yields +0.38% on MMLU and +0.82% on MMLU-Pro, confirming model-agnosticism.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares MOC against two baselines: (1) Single LLM, a single-agent baseline without any multi-agent coordination, and (2) Vanilla MAS, the standard multi-agent system that concatenates only direct in-neighbor (first-order) responses. MOC wins against Vanilla MAS on virtually all dataset-density combinations: the average improvement ranges from +0.89% (ρ=0.7) to +2.30% (ρ=0.3) on Gemma-2-27B, and from +0.72% to +3.84% on Qwen2.5-32B. The single exception is SVAMP at ρ=1.0 on Gemma-2-27B, where MOC slightly underperforms Vanilla MAS by 0.36%, though the overall average still improves. Both MOC and Vanilla MAS substantially outperform the Single LLM baseline (e.g., 0.7732 vs. 0.7233 average at ρ=0.3), confirming that multi-agent coordination itself is beneficial, while MOC further raises the ceiling by exposing multi-hop evidence. On the 685B DeepSeek-V3.2 backbone, MOC still beats Vanilla MAS by 0.38% (MMLU) and 0.82% (MMLU-Pro), demonstrating that the communication-scheme gains persist even with state-of-the-art models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper shifts the focus in LLM-based Multi-Agent Systems from topology design (how agents are connected, as explored by GPTswarm, DyLAN, G-Designer, and MACNET) to the underexplored communication scheme (how messages are actually transmitted along those edges). It draws a direct conceptual analogy to MixHop in graph neural networks, where node representations from multiple hop distances are concatenated to expand the receptive field, and adapts this principle to the discrete, order-sensitive setting of LLM prompt concatenation. The Semantic-Topological Merging consolidation operator is a novel contribution that has no direct counterpart in prior GNN message passing, motivated by the variable-length, token-budgeted nature of LLM contexts rather than fixed-dimensional vector aggregation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that multi-order evidence exposure with structural consolidation consistently improves performance across three model scales (27B to 685B) and multiple topologies while reducing token costs below vanilla MAS in larger agent settings, the paper provides a practical, drop-in communication upgrade for existing MAS pipelines. Key open questions remain: the hop order K is fixed rather than adaptively determined, and the framework does not address trust or robustness against potentially malicious upstream agents. Future work on adaptive, trust-aware message selection and dynamic hop-range determination would be needed to make MOC robust in open or adversarial multi-agent environments.
