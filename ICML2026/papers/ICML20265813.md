## Problem

Classical scaling laws predict pre-training loss but do not answer the practitioner's prescriptive question: given a fixed pre-training compute budget, what downstream accuracy is realistically attainable after heterogeneous post-training? Moreover, it is unclear whether the compute-to-performance mapping remains stable as training recipes and post-training techniques evolve over time, making budget planning unreliable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265813/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes, for each of ~7k model checkpoints, the base model's pre-training FLOPs (log₁₀-transformed), observed benchmark scores in [0,1] across six tasks, and parameter counts for cost-weighted sampling. It produces a 4-parameter sigmoid capability boundary curve mapping log-compute to the 0.98 conditional quantile of attainable accuracy, along with per-bin signed coverage error diagnostics (τ̂−τ) and, for the sampling algorithm, a cost-constrained subset of models to evaluate. Temporal validation uses rolling chronological splits (fit on period P_t, evaluate on P_{t+1}) restricted to the overlap in log-compute range.

## Method

The core estimator is a smoothed quantile regression targeting the 0.98 conditional quantile of benchmark scores as a function of log pre-training FLOPs, parameterized as a monotone saturating sigmoid q_τ(z;θ)=y₀+L·σ(a+βz) with constraints β≥0, 0≤y₀≤1, 0≤L≤1−y₀, trained via a smoothed pinball loss (κ=50) plus ridge regularization (λ=10⁻³). This 4-parameter form is shown to match the more flexible I-spline in in-distribution pinball loss while achieving the best out-of-distribution calibration error across all estimators. Temporal stability is assessed through three rolling train/validation splits across four chronological periods (mid-2022 to March 2025), revealing that four of six tasks maintain <2% coverage error across generations while math reasoning exhibits a consistently advancing boundary. For budget-constrained evaluation, a balanced I-optimal design combines a predictive-variance objective (via the Fisher information matrix of the sigmoid Jacobian) with a log bin-balance term, solved by a greedy gain-per-cost heuristic with Sherman-Morrison rank-one updates, recovering near-full-data boundaries using ~20% of the parameter-count-weighted evaluation budget.

## Results

At a pre-training budget of 10^24 FLOPs, the estimated 0.98-quantile attainable accuracies are 0.828 (IFEval), 0.700 (BBH), 0.539 (MATH Lvl 5), 0.424 (GPQA), 0.535 (MUSR), and 0.563 (MMLU-PRO). The sigmoid capability boundary achieves the best OOD calibration error (2.21×10⁻²) among all estimators, compared to 3.60×10⁻² for the compute-agnostic constant baseline and 2.41×10⁻² for the more flexible I-spline. Across four of six tasks (BBH, GPQA, MMLU-PRO, MUSR), the OOD coverage error remains below 2% when fitting on earlier model generations and evaluating on later ones, while MATH Lvl 5 exhibits a consistently advancing boundary over time. The balanced I-optimal sampling algorithm recovers near-full-data boundaries using only ~20% of the parameter-count-weighted evaluation budget (as low as 5% on GPQA and MUSR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four estimator families for the 0.98-quantile capability boundary: a compute-agnostic constant, a binwise constant, a flexible monotone I-spline, and the proposed sigmoid. The sigmoid matches the I-spline in ID pinball loss (4.08×10⁻³ vs. 4.00×10⁻³) while achieving the best OOD calibration error (2.21×10⁻² vs. 2.41×10⁻²), a 8.3% improvement over I-spline and a 38.6% improvement over the constant baseline. The binwise constant, despite being non-parametric, degrades more sharply out-of-distribution (2.81×10⁻² OOD calibration error), confirming that the smooth sigmoid inductive bias generalizes better across temporal splits. In the pre-training vs. post-training comparison (Figure 3), post-trained capability boundaries are consistently monotone in compute, whereas pretrained accuracies frequently violate monotonicity, showing that the sigmoid boundary captures a more reliable compute-to-performance mapping. On the contamination diagnostic, a cross-benchmark shift test between AIME 2025 and MATH-500 yields a positive but not statistically significant post-release shift (p-value = 0.15), finding no clear aggregate evidence of AIME-2025 score inflation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of observational scaling laws (Ruan et al., 2024; Dominguez-Olmedo et al., 2024) by shifting the target from mean downstream performance to high-quantile 'capability boundaries'—the attainable upper envelope of post-trained accuracy as a function of pre-training compute. It repurposes smoothed quantile regression (Koenker & Bassett, 1978) as the estimation tool and I-optimal experimental design (Pukelsheim, 2006) for budget-constrained evaluation, neither of which had been applied to LLM scaling analysis in this way. The key conceptual departure from classical scaling laws (Kaplan et al., 2020; Hoffmann et al., 2022) is treating post-training heterogeneity as a given and asking a prescriptive, decision-oriented question rather than a descriptive one about loss curves.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that sigmoid capability boundaries remain temporally stable (within ~2% coverage error) for four of six tasks provides practitioners with a rare, actionable compute-to-performance map that persists across model generations, while the math-reasoning exception flags where algorithmic progress is still actively moving the envelope. The Proteus-2k dataset (2.4k newly evaluated models spanning 2024–2026) fills a critical gap left by the Open LLM Leaderboard's retirement, and the balanced I-optimal sampling algorithm (recovering near-full-data boundaries at ~20% evaluation cost) offers a practical path to ongoing boundary monitoring. Open questions include how the framework behaves under new architecture families (e.g., mixture-of-experts, state-space models), whether inference-time compute shifts the boundary independently of pre-training FLOPs, and whether the temporal stability observed in open-weight models extends to closed-source frontier systems.
