## Problem

Existing biology benchmarks primarily test factual knowledge via short-answer or multiple-choice questions, but LLMs are increasingly deployed as tool-augmented agents capable of performing complex, multi-step laboratory tasks end-to-end. There is no benchmark that measures whether AI agents can actually execute practical molecular biology workflows—such as writing lab-automation code, designing DNA fragments for assembly, or evading nucleic acid synthesis screening—at or above expert human level, which is essential for informing biosecurity threat models and safeguard activation.

## Input / Output

Each task provides the agent with a DNA sequence (FASTA file), task-specific instructions, and a sandboxed execution environment with tools (Python + biopython/opentrons, bash, web search, NCBI BLAST, OpenTrons simulation). The agent must submit machine-parseable artifacts: comma-separated DNA fragments in XML tags for design tasks, or a complete OpenTrons Python protocol script for the automation task. Scoring is fully algorithmic, awarding partial credit across multiple criteria (e.g., fragment overlap length 15–40 bp, GC content <65%, correct reagent volumes, correct incubation parameters), and the Liquid Handling Robot task additionally supports wet-lab validation via whole-plasmid sequencing.

## Method

ABC-Bench comprises three tasks—Fragment Design, Screening Evasion, and Liquid Handling Robot—each corresponding to a step in a pathway to acquiring a hazardous DNA sequence, with up to 10 prompt variations per task. Eight frontier LLM agents (Claude Sonnet/Opus 4/4.6, Gemini 3.1 Pro, GPT-5.4, Qwen3.5, Kimi K2.5) are evaluated in an agentic scaffold via the Inspect AI framework over N=10 independent runs, with refusal rates tracked separately. Human baselines (175 person-hours from 34 PhD-level biologists with ≥2 years Python experience) are collected under controlled conditions with AI use prohibited. A wet-lab validation experiment uses GPT-o4-mini-high to generate OpenTrons Flex scripts for Gibson Assembly with NEB Hi-Fi kit, iteratively fixing compilation errors, with success confirmed by Oxford Nanopore whole-plasmid sequencing across three independent experiments.

## Results

All eight frontier LLM agents outperformed the median human expert baseliner on all three ABC-Bench tasks. On Liquid Handling Robot, Claude Sonnet 4.6 and Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview achieved perfect scores (1.00 ± 0.00), while the human baseliner mean was only 0.20 ± 0.09. On Fragment Design, Claude Opus 4.6 scored 1.00 ± 0.00 versus the baseliner's 0.33 ± 0.12. On Screening Evasion (the most novel task), the best non-refusing model (Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview) scored 0.78 ± 0.02 versus the baseliner's 0.22 ± 0.07, though several frontier models (Claude Sonnet 4.6, Claude Opus 4.6, GPT-5.4) refused all samples. In wet-lab validation, GPT-o4-mini-high generated OpenTrons scripts that successfully assembled DNA in all three independent experiments, confirmed by whole-plasmid sequencing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is a cohort of 34 expert human biologists (Ph.D. in molecular/computational biology, ≥1 yr cloning + ≥2 yr Python experience) who completed all three tasks over 175 total person-hours. Every AI agent outperformed the median human baseliner on every task: the gap is largest on Liquid Handling Robot (best model 1.00 vs. human 0.20) and Fragment Design (best model 1.00 vs. human 0.33), and smallest on Screening Evasion (best non-refusing model 0.78 vs. human 0.22). Among the specific models referenced in the Anthropic Claude 4 System Card, Claude Sonnet 4 scored 0.96/0.39/0.51 across the three tasks, while the newer Claude Sonnet 4.6 and Claude Opus 4.6 improved to 1.00 on Liquid Handling Robot and Fragment Design respectively, though both refused all Screening Evasion samples. GPT-5.4 (from the OpenAI GPT-5 System Card) scored 0.91/†/0.73, also refusing all Screening Evasion samples. The open-weight models (Qwen3.5, Kimi K2.5) showed no refusals but scored lower than the closed frontier models on all three tasks, suggesting that safety alignment in closed models trades off against task completion on dual-use prompts.

## Relation to Prior Work

ABC-Bench extends the agentic benchmark paradigm established by SWE-Bench and CyBench into the biological domain, and extends existing biology benchmarks (MMLU, LAB-Bench, BixBench) from knowledge-testing Q&A and data analysis to tasks that generate and manipulate biological entities (DNA assembly, lab automation). The paper explicitly positions itself as filling the gap between analysis-oriented biology evaluations and the emerging need to assess agents on end-to-end wet-lab and in-silico generation tasks. Its risk-chain framing—where Fragment Design, Screening Evasion, and Liquid Handling Robot correspond to sequential steps toward acquiring a hazardous sequence—is a deliberate departure from prior benchmarks that evaluate capabilities in isolation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first agentic biology benchmark to include wet-lab validation, confirming that AI-generated robot scripts can successfully perform DNA assembly in a real laboratory (3/3 experiments). The finding that all frontier agents outperformed PhD-level human experts on every task raises urgent biosecurity questions about dual-use capabilities and the adequacy of current nucleic acid synthesis screening. Key limitations include the coding-centric design that may under-elicited human baseliners (who would naturally use dedicated DNA design tools rather than write Biopython scripts), the narrow coverage of the risk chain, and the absence of a consensus definition of 'expert.' Open questions include how to tier access to screening-evasion capabilities, whether non-coding components of these tasks will be equally well-solved, and how to expand the benchmark to cover the full pathway from pathogen selection to wet-lab execution.
