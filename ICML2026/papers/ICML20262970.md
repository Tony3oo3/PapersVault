## Problem

Entropy-minimization-based test-time adaptation methods are prone to model collapse under distribution shift because imbalanced predictions cause gradients to accumulate toward a single class, and the excluded unreliable samples introduce biased data features. Moreover, purely local posterior updates in TTA lead to catastrophic forgetting and overfitting to domain-specific features in continual adaptation scenarios, where the target distribution evolves over time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262970/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled RGB test images in mini-batches (default batch size 64, also evaluated at batch size 1) along with a pre-trained classifier (ResNet-18/50 or ViT-Base) whose only trainable parameters are the affine parameters of normalization layers (~0.04M). At each time step, the model produces a class probability distribution and a hard label, while internally maintaining a running IPCA subspace (rank k=64) over penultimate-layer features that is updated online as reliable samples arrive. A patch-shuffled negative is generated on-the-fly from each input image to form the contrastive pair.

## Method

SaTeen combines two structural alignment objectives. First, an intra-sample contrastive loss (Eq. 6) maximizes the cross-entropy discrepancy between a sample's prediction and that of its patch-shuffled counterpart (with stop-gradient on the negative), which theoretically suppresses noisy-feature gradients and provably prevents the collapse direction from accumulating (Theorem 3.1). Second, an inter-sample alignment loss (Eq. 15) minimizes the reconstruction error of each test sample's penultimate-layer feature onto a low-dimensional PCA subspace built incrementally (IPCA) from reliable samples selected by a threshold on the intra-sample loss, thereby preserving the intrinsic data-manifold structure across evolving target domains (Theorem 3.2). Reliable samples participate in both losses while unreliable samples only contribute to the inter-sample term, and all samples are weighted by an exponential of the intra-sample loss to avoid excessive influence from any single instance. The overall loss (Eq. 18) is optimized by updating only normalization-layer parameters via SGD, requiring no additional trainable parameters beyond the standard Tent setup.

## Results

On the most challenging continual TTA benchmark (ImageNet-C, severity 5, 15 sequential corruptions), SaTeen achieves 64.6% average accuracy with ViT-Base-LN and 47.1% with ResNet50-GN, outperforming the prior best DeYO (61.6% / 40.1%) and REM (60.8%). Under single TTA at the same severity, SaTeen reaches 65.4% (ViT) and 49.9% (ResNet50), surpassing DeYO by 1.3% and 7.5% respectively. On the mixed-shifts setting (15 corruptions at varying severities), SaTeen attains 66.3% (ViT) and 52.1% (ResNet50) average accuracy, establishing new SOTA. On spurious-correlation benchmarks, SaTeen improves worst-group accuracy by 14.5% over DeYO on ColoredMNIST (67.2% vs 52.7%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SaTeen consistently outperforms all 10 baselines across every evaluation scenario. Against the strongest prior method DeYO (Lee et al., 2024), SaTeen gains +7.0% on continual TTA (ResNet50: 47.1% vs 40.1%), +3.0% on continual TTA (ViT: 64.6% vs 61.6%), +7.5% on single TTA (ResNet50: 49.9% vs 42.4%), and +1.3% on single TTA (ViT: 65.4% vs 64.1%). Against REM (Han et al., 2025), the strongest continual-TTA baseline for ViT, SaTeen improves by 3.8% (64.6% vs 60.8%). Tent (Wang et al., 2021) suffers severe model collapse under continual TTA (8.2% for ResNet50), while SaTeen avoids collapse entirely thanks to its intra-sample structural alignment. On the spurious-correlation benchmarks, SaTeen surpasses DeYO by 2.9% average and 14.5% worst-group accuracy on ColoredMNIST, and by 0.8% / 1.7% on WaterBirds. Efficiency-wise, SaTeen trains only 0.04M parameters (comparable to Tent, SAR, DeYO) in 12m21s, far fewer parameters than ViDA (93.7M) and CMAE (86.5M) while achieving higher accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

SaTeen extends the entropy-minimization lineage of TTA (Tent, SAR, DeYO) by replacing the pure EM objective with a contrastive-style intra-sample structural alignment loss that uses patch-shuffled negatives to suppress noisy-feature reliance. For the inter-sample component, it repurposes Incremental PCA (originally developed for online visual tracking, Ross et al. 2008) as a parameter-free mechanism to track the intrinsic feature subspace across sequential domain shifts, avoiding the heavy auxiliary networks (ViDA's 93.7M extra parameters) or global-statistics assumptions (LinearTCA) used by prior continual TTA methods. The theoretical contribution formalizes why EM collapses under imbalanced predictions and proves the proposed loss structurally prevents this, building on the decoupled-entropy analysis of Ma et al. (2025b).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a theoretically grounded, parameter-free recipe for continual TTA that matches or exceeds methods requiring tens of millions of extra parameters, making it attractive for deployment-constrained settings. The IPCA-based subspace tracking is a creative and lightweight solution to catastrophic forgetting that could inspire similar online-subspace ideas in other sequential adaptation problems. Limitations include reliance on patch shuffling (restricting applicability to grid-structured inputs like images), a heuristic threshold τ for reliable-sample selection, and still-modest gains over DeYO in some scenarios (e.g., 1.3% on ViT single TTA). Open questions include whether the structural-alignment principle transfers to non-image modalities and whether the IPCA subspace assumption degrades under very rapid or non-stationary domain shifts.
