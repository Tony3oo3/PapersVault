## Problem

Existing visuo-tactile manipulation policies fuse modalities in a coupled manner with equal importance, failing to exploit the distinct roles of vision, proprioception, and touch in action generation. Moreover, there is no efficient mechanism to incorporate tactile sensing into pretrained visuomotor policies without retraining the entire model from scratch, making tactile integration costly and impractical for deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262567/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The policy consumes binocular RGB images (256×256, long-side resize preserving aspect ratio), 28-DoF proprioceptive joint states (14 arm + 12 hand + 2 active-camera joints), and piezoresistive tactile readings (1062 contact points per hand, 0–4096 range, normal force only) alongside a one-hot task condition. It outputs a 32-step action chunk of 28-DoF target joint positions, generated via 5-step rectified flow matching from Gaussian noise. Tactile signals are notably sparse in both time (short contact intervals) and space (only a few pads activated), which motivates the dedicated cross-attention pathway rather than naive concatenation.

## Method

DECO's core is a Multimodal Diffusion Transformer (MMDiT) block with decoupled conditioning pathways: visual tokens (from a shared ResNet-34 backbone with RoPE) interact with noisy action tokens via joint self-attention, proprioceptive states and task conditions modulate both token streams through AdaLN (scale/shift/gate from 2-layer MLPs), and tactile signals are injected exclusively through a dedicated cross-attention module. Training follows a two-stage paradigm: first, a vision-action policy is trained from scratch; second, the pretrained backbone is frozen and a plugin tactile adapter (tactile encoder producing 68-dim region-level embeddings via gated averaging + learnable projection, plus LoRA rank-32 on attention projections) is trained with only 7.97M parameters (~9% of the 83M total). An ablation swapping proprioception and tactile pathways (cross-attention ↔ AdaLN) causes a dramatic performance collapse (Stage 3: 0/40 vs. 29/40), confirming that the specific modality-to-pathway assignment is critical rather than arbitrary.

## Results

On the DECO-50 dataset, DECO achieves a 72.25% average success rate across four bimanual dexterous manipulation tasks, a 21% improvement over the best visual-only baseline (ACT at 57.25%). Adding the plugin tactile adapter (DECO.p) further raises the average to 82.50% (+10.25%), with a particularly large 20% gain on contact-rich tasks (Waste Disposal and Assembly), where DECO.p reaches 73.13% versus ACT's 19.38%. The tactile adapter achieves this while tuning only 7.97M parameters (~9% of the full model).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DECO outperforms both visual-only baselines across all four tasks. Against ACT (57.25% avg.), DECO gains +15 percentage points overall and +33.75 points on contact-rich tasks; against DP (51.25% avg.), the gap is even wider at +21 points overall. The advantage is most pronounced on Assembly (37/80 vs. 10/80 for ACT, 15/80 for DP) and Waste Disposal (48/80 vs. 21/80 and 30/80), where contact-rich sub-goals (lid pressing, plug insertion) demand force-aware feedback that vision alone cannot provide. On the simpler Pick-and-Place task, all methods perform comparably (DECO 103/120 vs. ACT 101/120), confirming that tactile is unnecessary when vision and proprioception suffice. The tactile adapter (DECO.p) adds a further +10.25% average over DECO without retraining the full model, and notably surpasses DP.t (which trains tactile from scratch) by 25.75 points on average, demonstrating that decoupled cross-attention injection is far more effective than naive concatenation.

## Relation to Prior Work

DECO extends the Diffusion Policy paradigm (Chi et al., 2025) by replacing the conditional U-Net with a DiT-based architecture and introducing a decoupled multimodal conditioning scheme: vision enters via joint self-attention with action tokens, proprioception via AdaLN modulation (Peebles & Xie, 2023), and tactile via a dedicated cross-attention pathway. This departs from prior visuo-tactile policies (Lin et al., 2025; Cheng et al., 2025) that fuse modalities in a coupled, equal-weight manner, and from Higuera et al. (2025) which requires a large ControlNet-based adapter and extensive pretraining data. The two-stage training paradigm—first learning a vision-action policy, then injecting tactile via a LoRA-based plugin adapter (Hu et al., 2021)—is a key architectural departure from joint-training approaches, enabling parameter-efficient tactile integration without retraining the full model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupled conditioning paradigm provides a principled framework for integrating heterogeneous sensory modalities into diffusion-based robot policies, with the permutation ablation (0/40 vs. 29/40 on Stage 3 when pathways are swapped) offering strong evidence that modality-to-pathway assignment is not arbitrary. The plugin tactile adapter demonstrates a practical recipe for adding new sensing modalities to pretrained policies with <10% trainable parameters, which could generalize to other modalities (force, audio) and larger VLA backbones. The DECO-50 dataset (50 hours, 5M frames, bimanual dexterous with tactile) fills a notable gap in the literature, as no prior dataset simultaneously provides all three properties. Key limitations include evaluation on a single hardware platform with one hand type and one tactile sensor, and the absence of temporal modeling for long-horizon contact-rich manipulation—both flagged as future work.
