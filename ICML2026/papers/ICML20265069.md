## Problem

Current deep generative models for de novo protein design are fundamentally non-deliberative: they synthesize molecular geometries in a single continuous sampling process without explicitly reasoning about which residues are functionally critical or why certain interactions are favored. This entanglement of design reasoning with diffusion dynamics limits interpretability, controllability, and the ability to reuse biochemical knowledge across tasks, in stark contrast to how human protein engineers first identify key interaction residues before optimizing geometry.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265069/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an antibody-antigen co-crystal complex with framework regions and antigen held fixed, all CDR residues masked as <X> tokens, plus optional text prompts and antigen hotspot annotations. To prevent native-geometry leakage, the understanding expert never sees ground-truth CDR structures; instead it operates on a CDR-masked refolded scaffold produced by an AF3-style inpainting pass. The output is a complete set of CDR amino-acid identities and full-atom coordinates, with the reasoning expert contributing only a sparse set of key-residue anchor commitments (identities + projected hidden states) that condition the diffusion generator.

## Method

Proteo-R1 adopts a dual-expert architecture in which a multimodal LLM (Qwen-3-4B-Instruct) serves as an understanding expert and an AF3-style diffusion model (MFDesign) serves as a generation expert. The understanding expert fuses ESM-2 sequence embeddings with truncated AF3 structural features (obtained via CDR-masked refolding) to identify a sparse set of key interaction residues, predicting both their amino-acid identities and continuous hidden representations. These decisions are injected into the generator through a two-level sparse anchoring interface: identity clamping at the discrete token level and additive embedding injection (e_i^gen = e(k̂_i) + W_proj·h_i^LLM) at the representation level, preserving the diffusion model's inductive biases while enabling end-to-end gradient flow. Training follows a three-stage curriculum—multimodal alignment (frozen LLM, projection-only), structural reasoning mid-training (unfrozen LLM, four-phase spatial meta-task curriculum with replay), and joint reasoning-guided design (end-to-end diffusion + reasoning losses)—progressively stabilizing cross-modal grounding before coupling the two experts.

## Results

On the simultaneous multi-CDR redesign benchmark (SAbDab), Proteo-R1 achieves the lowest per-CDR RMSD in 5 of 6 regions (H1: 1.33 Å, H2: 1.13 Å, L2: 0.85 Å), the best Loop-RMSD (1.51 Å), and the lowest steric clash rates (0.50% intra-chain, 0.14% inter-chain) and backbone dihedral divergence (JSD_bb = 0.2661). On the RAbD CDR-H3 benchmark, Proteo-R1 attains the best lDDT (0.9693), TM-score (0.9816), RMSD (2.46 Å), and DockQ (0.801) among all baselines, while reporting a notably low AAR (10.75%) that the authors interpret as evidence of generating alternative valid solutions rather than native-sequence imitation. When paired with an alternative UniMoMo backbone, Proteo-R1 further reduces RMSD to 0.83 Å and improves IMP to 67.79% with ΔG = 7.35, confirming model-agnostic gains from reasoning-guided anchoring.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Proteo-R1 outperforms all six multi-CDR baselines (DiffAb, dyMEAN, HTP, IgGM, AbX, MFDesign) on per-CDR RMSD in at least 4 of 6 regions, with the largest margins on H1 (1.33 vs. 1.52–1.73) and H2 (1.13 vs. 1.23–1.55). Against the closest competitor MFDesign, Proteo-R1 reduces H1 RMSD by 0.28 Å, H2 by 0.31 Å, and L2 by 0.30 Å, while also achieving lower clash rates (0.50%/0.14% vs. 0.53%/0.26%) and JSD_bb (0.2661 vs. 0.2734); MFDesign retains a slight edge on IMP (59.16% vs. 56.58%). On the RAbD CDR-H3 benchmark, Proteo-R1 dominates on structural and interface metrics—lDDT 0.9693 vs. BoltzGen's 0.8372, RMSD 2.46 vs. BoltzGen's 2.69, DockQ 0.801 vs. BoltzGen's 0.473—while reporting far lower AAR (10.75% vs. DGENet's 42.67%), which the authors frame as evidence of generating novel valid solutions. The compatibility experiment with UniMoMo shows Proteo-R1 reducing RMSD from 1.04 to 0.83 Å and improving IMP from 65.00% to 67.79% (ΔG 8.46→7.35) without modifying the generative architecture. The oracle-anchor ablation reveals that the remaining gap to the upper bound is concentrated on flexible H3 (RMSD 3.81→3.34), indicating that reasoning accuracy at key residues is the primary bottleneck rather than the diffusion generator itself.

## Relation to Prior Work

Proteo-R1 builds directly on MFDesign (Yang et al., 2026) as its generation expert and on AlphaFold 3 (Abramson et al., 2024) as both a structural feature extractor and the architectural template for the diffusion backbone. Its key departure from prior antibody design methods (DiffAb, dyMEAN, HTP, IgGM, AbX) is the introduction of a separate LLM-based reasoning expert that produces explicit, residue-level anchor commitments before generation, rather than treating all CDR positions uniformly during diffusion. This contrasts with language-guided protein design (BIOM3, PINAL, INSTRUCTPRO) where text acts as soft conditioning, and with CoT-based protein LLMs (PROLLM, PROTEX) where reasoning remains in latent space; Proteo-R1 instead converts reasoning into hard identity clamping and differentiable embedding injection at a sparse set of positions, preserving the inductive biases of the underlying geometric generator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a general, model-agnostic blueprint for coupling deliberative LLM reasoning with physical generative processes, demonstrated by consistent gains when swapping the AF3-style generator for UniMoMo without architectural modification. The oracle-anchor ablation reveals that the reasoning expert's accuracy—not the generator—is the primary bottleneck, pointing to a clear path for future improvement. However, the work is limited to antibody CDR redesign on a single dataset (SAbDab), lacks wet-lab validation, and the very low AAR (10.75% on RAbD) raises open questions about whether the generated alternatives are truly functionally equivalent or merely structurally plausible. Broader applicability to non-antibody binder design, multi-target specificity, and affinity optimization remains unexplored.
