## Problem

Existing distillation-based open-vocabulary detection methods align CLIP's regional patch features with its global [CLS] embeddings to improve semantic discrimination, but the resulting features are reused for both classification and localization, sacrificing fine-grained spatial fidelity needed for accurate bounding box regression. Additionally, detectors trained solely on base categories exhibit a confidence bias that suppresses novel-category instances as background, since objectness scores are learned exclusively from seen objects.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262616/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (processed at 16× or 14× downsampling ratios by ViT-B/16 or ViT-L/14 CLIP encoders) and open-vocabulary text embeddings for class names. Region proposals are generated by a pre-trained RPN (top-20 per image by confidence), and RoIAlign pools CLIP patch features within each proposal to form region-level embeddings. Outputs include bounding boxes, open-vocabulary class labels via conditioned matching against text embeddings, and a class-agnostic localization quality score per proposal from the LQEH that is combined with objectness confidence for final ranking.

## Method

OSSD decouples semantic and spatial learning into two sequential distillation stages. In the semantic stage, the last CLIP attention layer is modified to use value-value attention (replacing query-key interactions) to reduce global semantic bias, and student region features pooled from RPN proposals are aligned with a frozen CLIP teacher's [CLS] embeddings of the corresponding image crops via cosine-similarity loss. In the spatial stage, a lightweight S2S adapter (single 3×3 Conv-BN-ReLU block) transforms the semantically distilled patch features into a spatially aligned space, which is then matched to a DINO-DETR encoder's patch features (pre-trained on COCO base classes) through a Frobenius-norm loss on L2-normalized relational similarity matrices. A Location Quality Estimation Head (LQEH) regresses per-proposal localization quality using squared-IoU-transformed supervision with focal weighting, providing a class-agnostic spatial precision signal that complements objectness confidence and mitigates base-class bias during inference.

## Results

On OV-COCO, OSSD achieves 49.2 AP₅₀^Novel with a ViT-L/14 backbone (DETR encoder as spatial teacher) and 52.4 AP₅₀^Novel with DINOv2, surpassing the previous best ViT-L/14 method CLIPSelf (44.3) by 4.9 points. On OV-LVIS, OSSD reaches 40.5 mAP_r on rare categories (ViT-L/14), outperforming the prior state-of-the-art OV-DQUO (39.3) by 1.2 points. The ablation confirms that semantic distillation contributes the largest single gain (+6.7 over baseline), spatial distillation adds +4.0, and LQEH provides a further +1.4 when all three are combined.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On OV-COCO with ViT-L/14, OSSD (49.2 AP₅₀^Novel) outperforms CLIPSelf (44.3) by 4.9 points, BIND (41.5) by 7.7 points, and CFM-ViT (34.1) by 15.1 points, without requiring any extra training data or novel-class annotations. With ViT-B/16, OSSD (43.7) surpasses CLIPSelf (37.6) by 6.1 points. On OV-LVIS (ViT-L/14), OSSD (40.5 mAP_r) beats OV-DQUO (39.3) by 1.2, CoDet (37.0) by 3.5, and CLIPSelf (34.9) by 5.6 points. Notably, OSSD does not use novel class names during training (unlike ViLD, OV-DETR, RegionCLIP, Detic, and CORA+ marked with †), yet still achieves the highest scores. The DINOv2-teacher variant further extends the lead to 52.4 AP₅₀^Novel on OV-COCO and 41.8 mAP_r on OV-LVIS, confirming the spatial distillation strategy generalizes across teacher architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-distillation line of work (CLIPSelf, CLIM, SILC) that aligns CLIP's regional patch features with image-level [CLS] representations, but departs from it by explicitly decoupling semantic and spatial feature learning into two separate stages. The semantic stage builds directly on CLIPSelf's paradigm (modified with RPN-guided region selection and value-value attention), while the spatial stage introduces a novel S2S adapter that transfers localization priors from a pre-trained DINO-DETR or DINOv2 encoder without corrupting the semantically distilled features. The LQEH complements the class-agnostic bipartite matching strategy from CORA by adding an explicit localization-quality regression signal, addressing the base-class bias problem that prior OVD methods handle only indirectly through objectness scores.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a concrete and previously underexplored trade-off in distillation-based OVD: improving semantic alignment via [CLS] distillation degrades the spatial fidelity needed for accurate localization. The decoupling strategy is generalizable (validated across DETR and DINOv2 teachers, and across ViT-B/16 and ViT-L/14 backbones), making it a practical recipe for OVD practitioners. However, the two-stage training pipeline adds complexity, the S2S adapter is a single 3×3 convolution whose capacity may limit expressiveness for more challenging spatial tasks, and the method still depends on pseudo-label self-training. Open questions include whether the decoupling principle extends to other VLM backbones (e.g., EVA-CLIP, SigLIP) and whether the spatial distillation teacher can be replaced by a fully self-supervised or segmentation-based signal.
