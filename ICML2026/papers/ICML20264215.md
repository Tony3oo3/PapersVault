## Problem

Subgraph-based link prediction methods achieve superior accuracy over node-based approaches by exploiting localized structural information, but incur substantial computational overhead because each query requires separate subgraph extraction and message-passing. Existing data multiplexing techniques (DataMux, MIMONets, RevMux) only superpose input representations through neural network layers, leaving the dominant cost—inter-node message passing—untouched. MIMO-LP is the first framework to multiplex the message-passing process itself across multiple subgraphs in a single forward pass.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264215/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a full graph G and a batch of node-pair queries, from which h-hop enclosing subgraphs are extracted. Subgraphs are clustered into batches (via MinHash-estimated Jaccard similarity + k-means) to maximize edge overlap, and each batch is merged into a union graph where redundant edges are deduplicated. The output is a link prediction score (binary AUC or multi-class HR@k) per query pair, produced by a readout function applied to de-multiplexed node representations. The multiplexing count n (typically 50–100) controls the speed–accuracy trade-off and is selected via a spectral (Fiedler value) heuristic on the full graph.

## Method

MIMO-LP accelerates subgraph-based LP by superposing message-passing signals from multiple subgraphs into a shared latent space on a union graph. The multiplexing function projects each subgraph's contextualized features into a quasi-orthogonal subspace via a Gaussian-noise Hadamard product, then sums them with per-node normalization to form a single input to the backbone encoder. To preserve signal orthogonality through the encoder, MIMO-LP replaces ReLU with PReLU (making the encoder approximately linear) and adds an orthogonality regularization term on each layer's weight matrix; a random masking mechanism (ρ=0.2) further mitigates over-squashing in overlapping regions. A de-multiplexing function—a shared MLP conditioned on a trainable per-subgraph index matrix—recovers individual subgraph representations from the superposed output for final prediction. The entire pipeline is model-agnostic and plug-and-play, compatible with SEAL, PS2, SMA, NCN, and LPFormer backbones, achieving 14–44× speedup with near-identical accuracy.

## Results

MIMO-LP achieves 14–44× speedup in both training and inference over one-to-one subgraph-based LP baselines across 5 backbone models (SEAL, PS2, SMA, NCN, LPFormer) and 5 datasets, while maintaining predictive accuracy within ~1–2% of the one-to-one counterparts (e.g., M-SEAL: 94.87 AUC vs. SEAL: 95.46 on Yeast; M-PS2: 49.16 HR@100 vs. PS2: 49.17 on PPA). The Edge Reduction Ratio (ERR) ranges from 0.74 to 0.86, indicating 74–86% of redundant message-passing operations are eliminated. In contrast, prior data-multiplexing frameworks (DataMux, MIMONets, RevMux) achieve only 2× speedup and suffer 5–10 point accuracy drops (e.g., DM-SEAL: 86.68 AUC vs. M-SEAL: 94.87 on Yeast).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the one-to-one subgraph-based baselines (SEAL, PS2, SMA, NCN, LPFormer), MIMO-LP matches predictive accuracy within 0.5–1.5 points (e.g., M-PS2: 95.29 vs. PS2: 96.07 AUC on Yeast; M-LPFm: 63.39 vs. LPFm: 63.48 HR@100 on PPA) while delivering 14–44× speedup. Against the three prior data-multiplexing frameworks—DataMux (DM), MIMONets (KM), and RevMux (RM)—MIMO-LP dominates on both axes: it achieves 7–8× higher speedup (16× vs. 2× on Yeast, 44× vs. 2× on PPA) and 5–10 points higher accuracy (e.g., M-SEAL: 94.87 vs. DM-SEAL: 86.68, KM-SEAL: 85.30, RM-SEAL: 89.21 AUC on Yeast). The accuracy gap versus data-multiplexing baselines is consistent across all five backbone models and all five datasets, confirming that multiplexing the message-passing process (rather than just input embeddings) is the critical design difference. Among the data-multiplexing baselines, RevMux (RM) is the strongest, but still trails MIMO-LP by 4–8 points in accuracy and 7–22× in speedup. The one-to-one baselines retain a marginal accuracy edge (≤1.5 points) but are 14–44× slower, making MIMO-LP the clear Pareto-optimal choice for large-scale subgraph-based LP.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: subgraph-based link prediction (SEAL, PS2, SMA, NCN, LPFormer) and data multiplexing for neural networks (DataMux, MIMONets, RevMux). Existing multiplexing methods superpose input embeddings and process them through shared NN layers, but the authors identify that in graph learning the dominant cost is message-passing over edges, not forward propagation through layers—making prior multiplexing approaches inapplicable. MIMO-LP departs by multiplexing the message-passing operations themselves via union graph construction, overlap-aware subgraph batching (MinHash + k-means), and interference mitigation (PReLU semi-linearity, orthogonality regularization, random masking), rather than merely sharing computation in the readout or encoder layers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 14–44× speedup with near-identical accuracy is practically significant for subgraph-based LP pipelines that are otherwise bottlenecked by per-query message passing, and the model-agnostic, plug-and-play design means it can accelerate any existing subgraph-based backbone without architectural redesign. Key limitations include accuracy degradation at high multiplexing counts (n > 50–100), the NP-complete batching problem being only approximately solved, and the framework being restricted to subgraph-based (not node-based) LP. Open questions include extension to dynamic/heterogeneous graphs, whether the semi-linearity constraint (PReLU) generalizes to transformer-based graph encoders, and whether the random masking ratio can be made adaptive per graph topology.
