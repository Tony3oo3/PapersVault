## Problem

Existing latent-space reasoning methods for LLMs suffer from two fundamental issues: (1) distribution mismatch when recurrently reusing raw hidden states as input embeddings, leading to feature collapse that worsens in larger models with untied input/output embeddings, and (2) static reasoning schedules that waste computation on trivial steps while under-allocating depth for complex ones. The paper asks how to construct well-aligned latent tokens intrinsically (without external assistant models) and adapt reasoning effort to problem difficulty.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language mathematical word problem as input and produces a reasoning trace that dynamically interleaves explicit text tokens with non-verbalizable <thinking> latent tokens, followed by a final numerical answer. The latent tokens are continuous vectors (not discrete vocabulary items) whose input embeddings are computed on-the-fly from the model's own hidden states and output probability distribution. The number and placement of latent tokens are determined at inference time by the model's learned confidence patterns, so harder problems receive more latent computation.

## Method

LT-Tuning is a three-stage post-training curriculum: (1) standard CoT supervised fine-tuning, (2) confidence-driven dynamic insertion of <thinking> tokens at positions where the model's prediction probability falls below a threshold τ, initialized with raw hidden states, and (3) Context-Prediction Fusion, which constructs each latent token embedding as a weighted sum e_fusion = α·h_{t-1,I} + (1−α)·e_pred, where h is the contextual hidden state from layer I and e_pred is a temperature-scaled, Top-p-filtered probability-weighted sum of vocabulary embeddings. The fusion provably contracts the off-manifold distance by a factor of α relative to pure hidden-state recurrence (Coconut), and a complementary-noise analysis shows the optimal α decreases as the hidden-state off-manifold distance grows, explaining why fusion is most critical for 8B models with untied embeddings. A lightweight adapter (bottleneck MLP) is added for the 8B model to further bridge the representation gap.

## Results

LT-Tuning achieves the best average accuracy across all three model scales on four mathematical reasoning benchmarks: 36.4% (Llama-3.2-1B), 52.4% (Llama-3.2-3B), and 68.8% (Llama-3.1-8B), with the adapter variant reaching 70.3% at 8B. The most striking result is at the 8B scale, where Coconut degrades to 41.5% average due to feature collapse from untied embeddings, while LT-Tuning maintains 68.8%—a 27.3-point gap. LT-Tuning also produces 24.1% fewer generated text tokens than Explicit CoT at 8B while simultaneously improving accuracy by +7.1 points, demonstrating that latent tokens replace verbose reasoning rather than adding overhead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LT-Tuning outperforms all five baselines at every model scale. Against Explicit CoT, it gains +6.5 points at 1B, +1.9 at 3B, and +7.1 at 8B, with the largest gap on MultiArith (+12.8 at 1B, +14.5 at 8B). Against Coconut, the advantage is most dramatic at 8B (68.8% vs. 41.5%), where Coconut's direct hidden-state recurrence causes severe feature collapse due to untied input/output embeddings; at smaller scales with tied embeddings, the gap narrows to +1.2 (3B) and +11.2 (1B). Against Soft-Thinking, LT-Tuning wins at all scales, with the largest margin at 8B (+2.8) where Soft-Thinking's purely predictive tokens still benefit from the larger model. Against the assistant-based methods SoftCoT and SemCoT, LT-Tuning achieves consistent gains (+3.2 to +22.7 points), and notably avoids the erratic cross-benchmark volatility these methods exhibit (e.g., SemCoT drops from 77.0% on ASDiv-Aug to 32.7% on MultiArith at 3B). The strongest prior method varies by scale—SoftCoT at 1B, Coconut at 3B, Soft-Thinking at 8B—but LT-Tuning surpasses each by 1.9–4.3 points on average.

## Relation to Prior Work

LT-Tuning sits at the intersection of two latent-reasoning lines: Coconut's hidden-state recurrence (z_t = h_{t-1}) and Soft-Thinking's probability-weighted vocabulary embedding (z_t = e_pred). Rather than choosing one, the paper fuses both via a convex combination (Context-Prediction Fusion) and wraps the whole pipeline in a three-stage curriculum that progressively introduces latent tokens. It also departs from prior work by making latent-token insertion confidence-driven and dynamic, whereas Coconut uses a fixed number of latent steps and Soft-Thinking relies on entropy-based stopping. The theoretical appendix provides a principled justification for the fusion form that neither Coconut nor Soft-Thinking offers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that latent reasoning can reduce generated text tokens by ~24% while improving accuracy, offering a practical efficiency gain for deployed reasoning systems. The scaling analysis revealing Coconut's catastrophic degradation at 8B (falling below explicit CoT) due to untied embeddings is a useful warning for practitioners considering latent reasoning at scale. However, the evaluation is confined to small models (≤8B) and arithmetic word problems, leaving open whether the approach transfers to larger frontier models, longer multi-step reasoning, or non-mathematical domains beyond a single GPQA-Diamond probe. The fixed fusion weight α and the reliance on a single training dataset (GSM8K) are also limitations that future work should address.
