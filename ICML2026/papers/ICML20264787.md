## Problem

Existing conformal prediction methods for LLM reasoning factuality control operate post-hoc: a complete multi-step reasoning graph is generated first, then conformal filtering prunes unreliable steps. This prevents CP from intervening during generation, making it impossible to stop or redirect reasoning when factuality risk accumulates. The paper asks whether inference-time conformal control can be designed that preserves valid coverage guarantees while deciding when to stop expanding the reasoning graph.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text question, per-node factuality uncertainty scores (frequency scores computed by sampling 5 continuations per subclaim), and a directed dependency graph over the generated subclaims. It produces an ancestor-closed prefix of the reasoning graph — the subgraph retained up to the step where the calibrated non-conformity score first exceeds the threshold — along with a final answer. Unlike post-hoc pruning, the output is a generation prefix rather than a filtered version of a complete graph, and the stopping point is determined online during generation.

## Method

ITCR learns a graph-level factuality uncertainty function FU_θ (an MLP over graph Laplacian spectral features and node-score summary statistics, trained with focal loss) that maps an ancestor-closed subgraph to a scalar risk score. A non-conformity score S(U) = 1 − σ(FU_θ(U)) + λ|V_U| is defined, where the size penalty λ|V_U| enforces monotonicity under subgraph expansion (the nested property), making threshold crossing irreversible. A conformal threshold τ_α is calibrated on held-out data using the appropriate quantile of scores over minimal bad (no-false) or minimal complete (no-miss) subgraphs. At test time, the reasoning graph is expanded step-by-step and generation halts at the first step where S exceeds τ_α, returning the last accepted ancestor-closed subgraph. This yields valid 1−α coverage guarantees under both no-false and no-miss objectives, with the learned function affecting only efficiency rather than coverage validity.

## Results

On downstream reasoning correction, ITCR achieves a positive correction gain (PCR − NCR) of 26.87% on GSM8K and 16.98% on MATH with LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct, compared to −58.08% and −68.59% for PostCal, yielding an average improvement of 18.77% across both benchmarks. ITCR also reduces token usage (e.g., 1919.50 vs. 2092.80 average tokens on LLaMA) and runtime (62.40 s vs. 67.69 s). On conformal coverage validity (Table 1), ITCR satisfies the target 1−α coverage on all datasets and both objectives (no-false and no-miss), while CPL fails to meet coverage in most settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PostCal (Rubin-Toles et al., 2025), ITCR wins decisively on all downstream correction metrics: it achieves positive PCR−NCR gains (13.37–26.87%) while PostCal produces strongly negative gains (−5.80 to −68.59%) across all three LLM backbones on both GSM8K and MATH. ITCR also uses fewer tokens and less runtime than PostCal (e.g., 1919.50 vs. 2092.80 tokens on LLaMA), indicating the correction gains do not come at extra computational cost. Against CPL (Mohri & Hashimoto, 2024), ITCR achieves valid conformal coverage (✓) on nearly all settings in Table 1, whereas CPL fails to meet the target 1−α coverage (marked ×) in most configurations, particularly under no-miss objectives where CPL coverage drops to 0.144–0.442 versus the 0.90–0.95 target. Among ITCR's own variants (MAX, SUM, AVG aggregations), the learned graph-level uncertainty function (full ITCR) provides the most stable validity and highest efficiency, confirming the benefit of learning over heuristic aggregation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the conformal prediction (CP) line of work on LLM factuality control—initiated at the claim level by Mohri & Hashimoto (2024) and lifted to reasoning-graph level by Rubin-Toles et al. (2025)—by moving calibration from a post-hoc pruning stage into the generation loop itself. The key conceptual departure is the requirement that the non-conformity score be monotone under ancestor-closed subgraph expansion (the 'nested property'), which prior post-hoc methods do not need because they select from a fixed graph. To enforce this, the authors learn a graph-level factuality uncertainty function (via graph Laplacian spectral features + MLP) and add a size-penalty term whose coefficient λ is governed by a worst-case slope bound (Proposition 3.3), yielding two distinct coverage targets (no-false and no-miss) with distribution-free guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By integrating conformal calibration into the generation process, ITCR enables a model to stop reasoning at the point where factuality risk exceeds a user-specified threshold, rather than generating a full trajectory and then discarding unreliable steps. The no-miss objective is a practical contribution: it addresses the excessive-abstention problem of pure no-false control (where up to 83% of samples collapse to zero retained nodes in prior work), trading a small, bounded amount of residual noise for substantially more complete reasoning. Open questions include extending guarantees to non-exchangeable settings (domain shift, changing backbones), accounting for uncertainty in the graph-construction step itself, and whether the nestedness penalty can be made adaptive rather than a fixed λ. The reliance on a 90%-linear-chain dependency structure in practice also suggests the full DAG machinery may be overkill for current LLM reasoning traces.
