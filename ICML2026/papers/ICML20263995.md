## Problem

Diffusion-based super-resolution models achieve strong perceptual quality but require many denoising steps, making them impractical for real-time use. Existing one-step distillation methods for ResShift (e.g., SinSR) produce blurred outputs, while T2I-based alternatives (e.g., OSEDiff) hallucinate non-existent structures and demand 2.5–10× more parameters and GPU memory. The paper asks whether a new distillation objective can achieve T2I-level perceptual quality at ResShift-level computational cost in a single inference step.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263995/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single degraded low-resolution RGB image (real-world or synthetic degradations via RealESRGAN pipeline) and produces a high-resolution RGB image in exactly one forward pass (1 NFE). Training uses 256×256 HR crops from ImageNet with ×4 SR factor, and the network operates entirely in the latent space of a pretrained VAE autoencoder, with the U-Net denoiser conditioned on the LR latent and a noise sample. Evaluation covers both full-size real-world images (RealSR, RealSet65, DRealSR) and 512×512 crops (DIV2K-Val, ImageNet-Test).

## Method

RSD distills the ResShift teacher into a one-step stochastic generator G_θ by training it so that a 'fake' ResShift model trained on G_θ's outputs matches the teacher, formalized as a KL divergence between the joint reverse-process distributions across all timesteps (Equation 24). Unlike VSD, which aligns marginal distributions at each timestep independently, RSD enforces joint alignment, capturing temporal dependencies critical for SR. Proposition 3.1 provides a tractable evaluation via an auxiliary fake ResShift network, avoiding intractable backpropagation through the full training procedure. The method further incorporates multistep training (training at N=4 timesteps while inferring in one step for robustness), an LPIPS perceptual loss against ground truth, and a GAN loss using a discriminator head on the fake model's bottleneck features, all computed in the VAE latent space except LPIPS which operates in pixel space.

## Results

RSD achieves single-step (1 NFE) inference with only 174M parameters and 539 MB peak GPU memory. On RealSR, RSD attains LPIPS 0.273, CLIPIQA 0.7060, and MUSIQ 65.860, outperforming its 15-NFE teacher ResShift (LPIPS 0.360, CLIPIQA 0.5958, MUSIQ 59.873) by a large margin on all perceptual metrics while matching its SSIM (0.754). On RealSet65, RSD reaches CLIPIQA 0.7267 and MUSIQ 69.172, competitive with T2I-based methods like OSEDiff (CLIPIQA 0.6836, MUSIQ 68.853) while using ~10× fewer parameters (174M vs. 1775M) and ~7× less GPU memory (539 MB vs. 3651 MB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against its teacher ResShift (15 NFE), RSD wins decisively on all perceptual metrics on RealSR: LPIPS 0.273 vs. 0.360, CLIPIQA 0.7060 vs. 0.5958, MUSIQ 65.860 vs. 59.873, while matching SSIM (0.754) at a slight PSNR cost (25.91 vs. 26.49). Versus SinSR, the prior 1-step ResShift distillation method, RSD improves LPIPS from 0.365 to 0.273 and MUSIQ from 61.582 to 65.860 on RealSR, confirming that the proposed joint-distribution distillation objective is superior to SinSR's deterministic-sampling knowledge distillation. Compared to the T2I-based OSEDiff (1775M params, 1 NFE), RSD achieves better LPIPS (0.273 vs. 0.299) and CLIPIQA (0.7060 vs. 0.6772) on RealSR while using only 174M parameters and 539 MB GPU memory (vs. 3651 MB), though OSEDiff edges ahead on MUSIQ (67.602 vs. 65.860). Against the multi-step SUPIR (50 NFE, 4801M params), RSD dominates on LPIPS (0.273 vs. 0.331), CLIPIQA (0.7060 vs. 0.5449), and MUSIQ (65.860 vs. 63.676) with 27× fewer parameters. Versus the recent 1-step T2I methods TSD-SR and AdcSR, RSD trades some no-reference perceptual scores (MUSIQ 65.860 vs. 69.871 for TSD-SR) for substantially better fidelity (PSNR 25.91 vs. 24.88, SSIM 0.754 vs. 0.723) and a 13× smaller model. Against CTMSR, the recent distillation-free consistency-based method, RSD wins on LPIPS (0.273 vs. 0.294), CLIPIQA (0.7060 vs. 0.6449), and MUSIQ (65.860 vs. 64.796) on RealSR, while CTMSR retains a slight PSNR advantage (26.18 vs. 25.91).

## Relation to Prior Work

RSD builds directly on ResShift (Yue et al., 2023) as both its teacher architecture and its diffusion framework, extending the residual-shifting forward process into a distillation setting. Conceptually, it departs from SinSR's knowledge distillation (which uses DDIM-style deterministic sampling) and from OSEDiff's variational score distillation (which aligns marginal distributions at each timestep on T2I backbones) by instead aligning the joint distribution across all timesteps via a 'fake ResShift' trained on the student's outputs. The method also draws on the broader family of fake-model distillation objectives (SiD, FGM, IBMD) but adapts them to the discrete-time, latent-space ResShift setting with task-specific supervised losses (LPIPS, GAN) for real-world SR.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RSD demonstrates that a compact 174M-parameter, 1-step model can match or exceed the perceptual quality of 1.7B+ parameter T2I-based SR methods (OSEDiff, SUPIR) while requiring 5–10× less GPU memory and substantially lower training cost (5 h on 4 A100s vs. 24–96 h for T2I baselines), making near-real-time diffusion SR practical on consumer hardware. The key open limitation is teacher capacity: RSD inherits ResShift's ceiling, so applying the same distillation objective to a stronger T2I teacher could unlock higher-resolution and higher-fidelity results. The paper also leaves open whether the joint-vs-marginal alignment distinction generalizes beyond DDPM-based architectures to flow-matching or rectified-flow formulations.
