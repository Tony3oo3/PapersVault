## Problem

Multimodal Large Language Models exhibit severe position bias in multi-image retrieval, where predictions are dominated by input order rather than semantic relevance. Existing calibration methods like PriDe assume position bias is a static, content-independent offset, but the paper demonstrates it is actually conditional on the ground-truth location, rendering global priors ineffective. The authors further identify a fundamental disconnect—Logit-Attention Divergence—where the model's internal attention correctly localizes the target image while the final output logits are hijacked by structural decoding priors, meaning the model 'looks at' the right answer but 'says' the wrong one.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text caption query and an ordered set of N candidate RGB images (N ranging from 2 to 12 in experiments) and produces a calibrated probability distribution over candidate indices, from which the predicted image index is read off. A minimal calibration set of only 5 samples (distinct from the evaluation set) is required upfront to estimate conditional position bias and layer-wise attention priors via cyclic permutations. For N > 9, candidate identifiers become multi-token strings (e.g., '10'), and the method computes joint token-sequence probabilities with restricted softmax normalization to ensure a valid distribution over all candidates.

## Method

The proposed Attention-Guided Debiasing framework is entirely training-free and operates at inference time in a single forward pass. It first estimates a conditional position bias matrix P_bias(j|i) by factorizing empirical selection probabilities into a structural bias term and a visual signal term (parameterized by a scalar gain γ) over a symmetrized calibration set constructed via cyclic permutations. At inference, it extracts post-softmax attention weights from the last query token to each image's visual token span, selects the top-K layers with highest visual attention mass, and divides raw attention by a static attention prior (estimated by averaging over the calibration set) in the log-domain to recover a purified visual posterior π. This posterior is then used to marginalize the conditional bias profiles, yielding an instance-specific dynamic prior that is subtracted from the observed logits in the log-domain, producing a calibrated distribution that reflects pure semantic judgment rather than positional artifacts.

## Results

On the multi-image retrieval benchmark (N=4, MS-COCO), the proposed Attention-Guided Debiasing achieves 98.66% accuracy and 96.50% consistency on LLaVA-OneVision-8B under the Random setting, compared to 67.52% for the Vanilla baseline. Under the more challenging Adversarial setting (CLIP-mined hard negatives), it reaches 71.06% accuracy and 51.30% consistency, a 21.5-point gain over Vanilla (49.56%). Across all three MLLM backbones (Qwen2.5-VL-3B, LLaVA-OneVision-8B, InternVL3-8B), the method consistently surpasses all training-free baselines by over 40% in accuracy, while reducing Recall Standard Deviation from ~25–36 to under 10, confirming restored permutation invariance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method decisively outperforms all four baselines on both Random and Adversarial settings. Against PriDe (static logit calibration, Zheng et al. 2024), the gap is +30.7 points on Random (98.66% vs 68.00%) and +17.2 points on Adversarial (71.06% vs 53.84%) for LLaVA-OneVision-8B, confirming that a single global prior cannot capture the conditional, instance-dependent nature of position bias. Against CoT prompting (Wei et al. 2022), the improvement is even larger (+53.3 points Random, +34.8 points Adversarial), showing that chain-of-thought reasoning does not mitigate structural decoding bias. Self-Consistency (Wang et al. 2022) performs worst among baselines (41.90% Random, 35.70% Adversarial), as majority voting over biased samples amplifies rather than cancels positional artifacts. SoFA (Tian et al. 2025), which replaces causal with bidirectional attention at inference, actually underperforms Vanilla at N=4 (59.70% vs 67.52% Random) due to a train/inference attention mismatch, while the proposed method achieves 98.66% in a single forward pass at comparable latency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-image retrieval task formalized by Li et al. (2024) and Wang et al. (2025a) by identifying a specific failure mode—Logit-Attention Divergence—that existing calibration methods (PriDe, SoFA, prompt-based debiasing) cannot address. It departs from the static-prior assumption underlying PriDe by showing that position bias in MLLMs is conditional on ground-truth location, and from text-only LLM debiasing paradigms by demonstrating that cross-modal interactions make those approaches inapplicable. The method repurposes internal attention weights—typically an interpretability tool—as a calibration signal, bridging the gap between the model's correct visual grounding and its biased autoregressive output.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Logit-Attention Divergence finding carries a broader implication: reported low accuracy on certain MLLM benchmarks may reflect positional artifacts rather than genuine perceptual limitations, as the model already attends to the correct answer internally. The training-free, single-pass method with only 5 calibration samples is practically attractive, but its reliance on white-box attention access and restriction to discrete-choice tasks limits immediate adoption. Open questions include whether internal attention reliability can be improved under high visual ambiguity, and whether the framework extends to open-ended generative settings where no discrete candidate set exists.
