## Problem

Existing federated unsupervised multi-source domain adaptation methods are designed for small-scale settings (2–6 sources) and either suffer exponential runtime growth (KD3A) or high-variance, unstable convergence (FACT) as the number of heterogeneous source domains increases. No prior approach explicitly addresses the scalability bottleneck of aligning many diverse sources to an unlabeled target in a privacy-preserving federated protocol.

## Input / Output

The method consumes labeled RGB images from N source domains (32×32 digit images for Digit-Five/Digit-18; variable-resolution images for DomainNet and Office-Caltech10) and unlabeled target images, all kept local to their respective clients. In the federated protocol, only class-wise soft centroid vectors (one per class per domain) and model parameters are exchanged with the server—no raw data leaves any client. The output is a shared feature extractor and predictor that maps any input image to a C-way class probability vector, evaluated on the unlabeled target domain.

## Method

GALA couples two novel components into a federated training loop. First, Inter-Group Discrepancy (IGD) replaces the quadratic full-pairwise source disagreement with a linear-complexity group-level objective: at each round, N source predictors are randomly partitioned into two groups, weighted average predictors are formed per group, and their ℓ₁ disagreement on unlabeled target data is minimized—yielding a biased but low-variance estimator of the full pairwise loss (variance reduced by ~1/M under uniform weights). Second, MDMGB+ computes per-source relevance via temperature-scaled softmax over cosine similarity between class-wise soft centroids of each source and the target, dynamically up-weighting well-aligned sources and suppressing negative transfer from irrelevant ones. The federated protocol proceeds as: sources compute and upload centroids → server computes relevance weights and aggregates feature extractors → sources fine-tune predictors against the shared extractor → target minimizes IGD to update the extractor → group predictors are merged into a global predictor, repeating until convergence.

## Results

On the proposed Digit-18 benchmark (18 diverse source domains), GALA achieves 94.6% average accuracy, outperforming FACT by 4.0% and surpassing the oracle on several targets. On the standard DomainNet benchmark (6 sources), GALA attains 49.7% average accuracy, exceeding the best distributed method KD3A (48.8%) and the best centralized method DAEL (48.7%). On Digit-Five (5 sources), GALA reaches 95.4% average accuracy, edging out FACT (95.0%) and KD3A (92.0%). Per-round runtime remains near-constant for GALA (~17–22 s) as sources grow from 3 to 9, whereas KD3A's runtime explodes from 51 s to 5,600 s over the same range.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GALA's most decisive advantage is against FACT (Schrod et al., 2025), the closest federated baseline: on the 18-source Digit-18 setting, GALA outperforms FACT by 4.0% on average, with gains up to +9.5% on SVHN-XS and +7.6% on MNIST-M, where FACT's single-pair IDD estimator becomes unstable and fails to converge. Against KD3A (Feng et al., 2021), GALA leads by 0.9% on DomainNet (49.7% vs. 48.8%) and by 3.4% on Digit-Five (95.4% vs. 92.0%), while KD3A's per-round runtime grows exponentially (51 s → 5,600 s from 3 to 9 sources) making it infeasible beyond ~9 domains. On the small Office-Caltech10 benchmark, GALA (97.8%) is marginally below KD3A (97.9%) but achieves 100% on the DSLR target. Against centralized baselines, GALA surpasses the best centralized method DAEL (48.7%) on DomainNet and CMSS (90.8%) on Digit-Five, confirming that the federated formulation does not sacrifice accuracy. On Digit-Five, GALA also outperforms SHOT (90.2%), SFDA (89.2%), and FADA (73.6%) by substantial margins, with the largest gap over FADA reaching 21.8% on the SVHN target.

## Relation to Prior Work

GALA builds directly on FACT (Schrod et al., 2025), which introduced Inter-Domain Distance (IDD) minimization via random single-pair source sampling for federated UMDA. The key departure is replacing this high-variance single-pair estimator with Inter-Group Discrepancy (IGD), a group-level averaging objective that reduces variance by a factor of ~1/M at linear cost, trading a small bias for substantially more stable training. For source weighting, GALA modifies the MDMGB centroid-similarity mechanism from Wang et al. (2022) by adding a temperature-scaled softmax (MDMGB+) to sharpen relevance contrast in high-diversity settings, where the original MDMGB performs comparably to uniform weights. The paper also positions against KD3A (Feng et al., 2021), the prior state-of-the-art in decentralized UMDA, demonstrating its exponential runtime growth renders it infeasible beyond ~9 sources.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This paper addresses a genuine scalability gap in federated UMDA: prior methods either fail to converge or become computationally infeasible as source count grows beyond 6–9, a regime that is increasingly realistic in multi-institutional deployments. The Digit-18 benchmark (18 diverse digit domains) fills a notable evaluation gap, as existing benchmarks rarely exceed 6 sources. However, the evaluation is confined to digit recognition, and the full-participation requirement means communication cost scales linearly with source count (roughly 2× FACT). Open questions include whether the IGD + MDMGB+ combination transfers to higher-dimensional tasks (natural images, medical imaging), whether adaptive client dropout can reduce the communication overhead without sacrificing the stability gains, and whether the temperature parameter can be learned automatically rather than hand-tuned per dataset.
