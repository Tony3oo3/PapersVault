## Problem

Existing jailbreak attacks suffer from two coupled limitations: they produce disfluent or template-rigid prompts that reduce attack success against strongly aligned models, and their unnatural surface form makes them easily flagged by perplexity filters, input-perturbation defenses, and output-level detectors. No prior method decomposes a harmful query into a sequence of individually harmless, semantically coherent, and temporally ordered sub-questions while simultaneously embedding a stealthy narrative to mask the underlying intent.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single harmful text prompt as input and uses a base LLM (e.g., Llama2-7b-hf) solely for extracting semantic directions and a narrative context; the target model is accessed only via black-box queries. The output is a sequence of N (typically 5) natural-language sub-questions, each individually innocuous, wrapped in a shared narrative thread. These sub-questions are submitted sequentially to the target LLM, and the model's step-by-step responses are aggregated to reconstruct the full answer to the original harmful question.

## Method

SJA formulates sub-question generation as a Hamiltonian dynamical system on the Poincaré ball: semantic directions extracted from the harmful query are embedded in hyperbolic space, and each sub-question embedding evolves along a trajectory where a kinetic term drives it toward its target direction while a three-component potential (local continuity, direction alignment, and global structural consistency via softmin-weighted hyperbolic distances) enforces temporal and structural coherence. A symplectic implicit-midpoint integrator with Newton iterations provides stable, geometry-respecting updates. To inject stealth, the narrative context—originally a Euclidean vector—is mapped into the Poincaré ball via a direction-preserving fractional isometric transformation (Theorem 2), then fused with each sub-question embedding through a Möbius linear combination (Theorem 3) that keeps the result on the manifold while letting the sub-question intent dominate. The fused embeddings are projected back to Euclidean space via the logarithmic map at the origin and decoded into natural-language sub-questions by a pretrained LLM.

## Results

On AdvBench-Behaviors, SJA achieves an average ASR of 93.5% and HS of 89.7% across seven target models (L2-7B, M7B, V7B, GPT-4o, Gemini 2, DeepSeek-R1, DeepSeek-V3), substantially outperforming the strongest baseline FITD (avg. ASR ~82%, HS ~71%). Under RAIN defense, SJA's ASR drops by only 4.28 percentage points (to ~89.2%), while under a perplexity-based filter (threshold 30) it drops by just 0.12 points, indicating strong stealth. On HarmBench, the full SJA reaches 95.1% ASR and 91.3% HS, compared to 82.0/64.7 for non-Hamiltonian structured decomposition and 68.4/39.7 for standard sequential prompting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SJA outperforms all ten baselines on every model and both metrics in Table 1. The closest competitor is FITD (escalating malicious intent across turns), which reaches at most 91% ASR and 84% HS (on G2 and DS-r1 respectively), while SJA achieves 98% ASR and 95% HS on G2 and 96%/91% on DS-r1 — a gap of roughly 5–15 ASR points and 7–18 HS points. ASJA (attention-shifting multi-turn) is the second-strongest baseline, peaking at 88% ASR / 71% HS, still trailing SJA by 7–14 ASR points. Optimization-based single-turn methods (GCG, I-GCG, AutoDAN-Turbo) and search-based multi-turn methods (PAIR, TAP, CFA) are substantially weaker, with ASR values often below 80% and HS frequently below 50%. Under RAIN defense (Table 2), SJA retains 76–92% ASR versus 61–80% for FITD and 59–73% for ASJA, confirming that the narrative-fused sub-questions are harder to detect than the escalating or attention-shifting strategies used by prior multi-turn attacks.

## Relation to Prior Work

SJA extends the multi-turn jailbreak literature by departing from the three established categories—text-level splitting, gradual escalation (crescendo-style), and agent-based search—toward a structured decomposition where each sub-question is individually harmless yet collectively reconstructs the harmful answer, inspired by the Spilsbury puzzle analogy. Methodologically, it repurposes hyperbolic geometry (Poincaré embeddings, Möbius operations) and symplectic Hamiltonian dynamics (implicit midpoint integrator) from geometric deep learning and classical mechanics, applying them to the novel problem of optimizing a semantic trajectory of sub-question embeddings. The narrative injection via fractional Euclidean-to-hyperbolic mapping and Möbius fusion is a new compositional mechanism not seen in prior jailbreak work, which typically operates entirely in Euclidean or token space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that structurally coherent, individually harmless sub-questions embedded in a shared narrative can achieve high attack success (avg ASR 93.5, HS 89.7) while remaining robust to perplexity-based and RAIN defenses, suggesting that current input-level filters are insufficient against semantically natural multi-step attacks. However, the theoretical contributions (Theorems 1–3) are largely definitional rather than deep, and the controlled ablation (Table 3) shows that the Hamiltonian trajectory adds only ~4 ASR over non-Hamiltonian structured decomposition, raising the question of whether the full symplectic machinery is necessary or whether simpler smooth optimization suffices. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to tasks requiring more than five sub-steps, whether the base LLM used for direction extraction becomes a bottleneck or single point of failure, and whether defenders can detect the specific structural pattern of individually-harmless-but-collectively-harmful question sequences.
