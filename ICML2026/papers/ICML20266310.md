## Problem

Prior work treats coarse-grained image attention as a reliable signal for detecting object hallucinations in LVLMs, but this analysis is confounded by token position and object repetition, which induce Simpson's paradox and reverse apparent attention trends when marginalized. Additionally, existing intervention-based mitigation methods that modify attention internals degrade caption fluency and introduce repetitive generation artifacts, leaving a gap for reliable, non-invasive detection and mitigation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266310/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given an image and a text prompt, an LVLM generates a caption; HaloProbe then extracts per-object-token internal features (top-20 image attention values and entropy across all 32 layers × 32 heads at current and next decoding steps, plus top-100 logit-based confidence signals) and external features (normalized token position, repetition count clipped to [1,4], and first-occurrence indicator). The output is a token-level posterior probability p(y=1|x_i, x_e) for each object token, which drives either a hallucination-aware beam search score or a marked caption passed to GPT-5 for single-step post-hoc editing that removes only flagged objects.

## Method

HaloProbe is a factorized Bayesian framework that disentangles internal model signals from external caption statistics to avoid shortcut learning under severe class imbalance. A two-layer MLP (balanced estimator f_θ) is trained on data class-balanced conditioned on external features, so it learns the likelihood ratio p(x_i|y=1,x_e)/p(x_i|y=0,x_e) from fine-grained layer- and head-level attention and logit signals without relying on position or repetition as shortcuts. A separate one-layer prior network g_φ is trained on the natural imbalanced distribution to estimate p(y=1|x_e) from external features alone. The true posterior is recovered by combining both via Bayes' rule: p(y=1|x_i,x_e) = f_θ·g_φ / (f_θ·g_φ + (1−f_θ)(1−g_φ)). For mitigation, the posterior scores guide a non-invasive beam search (selecting among N_beam=5 candidates every 20 tokens by a hallucination-vs-coverage score) or a post-hoc GPT-5 editing step that removes only HaloProbe-flagged objects, preserving the LVLM's native decoding dynamics.

## Results

For hallucination detection on LLaVA-1.5-7B, HaloProbe achieves 90.0% accuracy, 93.5% AUROC, 92.5% precision, 95.8% recall, and 94.1% F1, outperforming the previous best (DIML) by 5.5 points in accuracy and 3.3 points in AUROC. For mitigation under greedy decoding on LLaVA-1.5, HaloProbe+Post-process reduces sentence-level hallucination (C_s) from 51.6 (baseline) to 17.6 and instance-level (C_i) from 15.2 to 5.2, while maintaining F1 at 75.2. On newer models, the approach generalizes: Qwen3-VL greedy C_s drops from 24.8 to 12.2, and InternVL3.5 greedy C_s drops from 31.6 to 16.6, with F1 scores preserved or slightly improved.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HaloProbe outperforms all eight baselines on both detection and mitigation. In detection (Table 1), it surpasses DIML (Jiang et al., 2025) by 5.5 points in accuracy (90.0 vs. 84.5) and 3.3 in AUROC (93.5 vs. 90.2), and beats EAZY (Che et al., 2025) by 11.2 points in accuracy. In mitigation under greedy decoding on LLaVA-1.5, HaloProbe+Post-process achieves C_s=17.6, beating the strongest intervention-based baselines: DIML (C_s=25.0), AllPath (C_s=26.6), AD-HH (C_s=29.6), PAI (C_s=34.5), and EAZY (C_s=38.8). Under beam search, HaloProbe+Beam (C_s=25.2) substantially outperforms OPERA (Huang et al., 2024; C_s=44.6) and PAI (C_s=33.5) at the same beam width. Notably, HaloProbe is non-invasive (no modification of model internals), whereas all competing mitigation methods intervene on attention or decoding dynamics, and the paper shows such interventions degrade fluency (e.g., 64% increase in bigram redundancy under attention intervention). On the two most recent models (Qwen3-VL, InternVL3.5), HaloProbe consistently halves C_s relative to baseline across all decoding strategies, confirming generalization beyond the older architectures used in prior work.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant attention-based hallucination detection paradigm (DIML, EAZY, IC, UT) by showing that the core assumption—that correct objects receive higher image attention than hallucinated ones—is an artifact of Simpson's paradox induced by token position and object repetition confounders. Rather than building on a single prior method, it repurposes Bayesian posterior correction and class-balanced training to factorize internal decoding signals from external caption statistics, a decomposition not previously applied to hallucination detection. On the mitigation side, it contrasts with the intervention-based line (PAI, OPERA, ADHH, AllPath) by demonstrating that an external scoring signal combined with standard decoding suffices, avoiding the fluency degradation that internal attention manipulation introduces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Simpson's paradox finding is a meaningful correction to a widely-used heuristic in the LVLM hallucination literature, and the non-invasive mitigation strategy offers a practical alternative that preserves generation quality—important for deployment where fluency matters. Limitations include dependence on an external LLM (GPT-5) for the post-processing variant, linear compute scaling with beam width for the beam-search variant, and evaluation on only 500 COCO images for mitigation. Open questions include whether the factorized Bayesian framework extends cleanly to attribute/relation hallucination without task-specific re-engineering, whether the approach remains effective as LVLMs become less prone to hallucination, and whether the prior estimator can be replaced by a closed-form or parametric model to reduce training overhead.
