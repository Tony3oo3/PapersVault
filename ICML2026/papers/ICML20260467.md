## Problem

Flow-based generative models default to Gaussian latent noise, which produces long transport paths and poor sampling quality for heavy-tailed, compactly supported, or multimodal target distributions. Existing remedies such as Student-t or α-stable noise require manual per-dimension tuning of tail parameters. The paper asks whether the noise distribution can be learned directly from data within the flow matching framework, eliminating this hand-tuning while preserving computational efficiency.

## Input / Output

At inference, the model consumes a vector of i.i.d. uniform random variables U ∈ [0,1]^d, maps each coordinate through a learned rational-quadratic-spline quantile function to produce the latent sample, then integrates the learned velocity field (a 35M-parameter U-Net) to produce the output. Outputs are 28×28 grayscale MNIST digits, 32×32 RGB CIFAR-10 images, 64×64 multi-channel HRRR weather fields (temperature, wind, precipitation), or 2D synthetic samples. The quantile network itself is extremely lightweight (~300k parameters for d=3072), adding only ~2.7% training overhead over standard Gaussian OT-FM.

## Method

The core construction decomposes the d-dimensional noising process into d independent 1D processes, each described by its quantile function Q^i:(0,1)→ℝ parameterized by a single rational-quadratic spline (K=32 bins) with per-coordinate scale and bias. The quantiles are learned by minimizing the squared Wasserstein-2 distance between the data distribution and the product-of-quantiles latent, which—by the isometric embedding of 1D measures into L₂(0,1)—reduces to an L₂ loss on the quantile functions. Training is joint: a minibatch optimal-transport coupling between data and latent samples is computed once per step and reused both for the Wasserstein quantile loss and for the conditional flow matching velocity loss (with stop-gradient on the endpoint displacement to prevent collapse). An entropy regularizer (expected log-determinant of the quantile Jacobian) prevents degenerate solutions in high dimensions. After a fixed warm-up (55k iterations) the quantile parameters are frozen and only the U-Net velocity field is optimized. The framework also accommodates alternative 1D noising processes (Kac process, MMD gradient flow) and extends to few-step generation via quantile interpolants that generalize DDIM.

## Results

On the HRRR-mini weather dataset, the learned quantile noise achieves the best performance across all six tail-centric metrics, e.g. reducing extreme event magnitude error from 0.2455 (Gaussian) and 0.1482 (Student-t) to 0.0634, and tail KS distance from 0.2067/0.1014 to 0.0393. On CIFAR-10 with a 35M U-Net, the quantile prior (β=0.8) reaches FID 4.42 at 20 Euler steps versus 4.63 for the Gaussian baseline; scaling to 55M parameters yields FID 3.25 vs. 3.37. On MNIST, the learned latent achieves significantly lower FIDs across all tested U-Net capacities (8, 16, 32 channels), with the advantage growing as model capacity shrinks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Gaussian OT-FM baseline (Tong et al., 2024), the learned quantile noise wins on every reported metric: on HRRR-mini it cuts extreme event magnitude error by ~74% (0.0634 vs. 0.2455) and tail KS distance by ~81% (0.0393 vs. 0.2067); on CIFAR-10 it improves FID from 4.63 to 4.42 (20 steps, 35M model) and from 3.37 to 3.25 (55M model). Against the hand-tuned Student-t baseline (Pandey et al., 2024), the quantile prior again dominates on all HRRR-mini metrics (e.g., spectral distance 1.1063 vs. 2.0719, kurtosis deviation 1.588 vs. 2.890) while eliminating the need to manually select degrees-of-freedom parameters per dimension. On CIFAR-10 the gains over the Gaussian baseline are modest (≈0.2 FID) because strong spatial and inter-channel correlations limit what a product-measure latent can capture, but the method still consistently outperforms. On MNIST the advantage is most pronounced under capacity constraints, where the learned latent's per-pixel marginal adaptation lets a smaller network allocate parameters more efficiently.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Flow Matching framework (Lipman et al., 2023) and the stochastic interpolants / mean-reverting process formulation (Albergo et al., 2023) by replacing the fixed Gaussian latent with a learned, data-adaptive noise distribution parameterized through per-coordinate quantile functions. It departs from prior heavy-tailed diffusion approaches (Pandey et al., 2024; Shariatian et al., 2025) that require manual tuning of tail parameters (e.g., Student-t degrees of freedom) by learning the noise end-to-end via a Wasserstein distance objective. The 1D decomposition strategy draws on the componentwise independence structure of standard Gaussian/Brownian noising, and the quantile interpolants generalize DDIM-style consistency models (Zhou et al., 2025) to arbitrary 1D noising processes including the Kac process and MMD gradient flow.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method eliminates the need to hand-tune noise distribution parameters for heavy-tailed or compactly supported data, with negligible computational overhead (~2.7% during joint training), and the HRRR-mini weather results show substantial improvements in tail statistics over both Gaussian and Student-t baselines. Key limitations include the product-measure constraint (no cross-dimensional correlation in the latent), which caps gains on strongly correlated data like CIFAR-10 (FID 3.25 vs 3.37), and the lack of systematic scaling studies to very high dimensions. Open directions include time-dependent quantile functions that optimize the full path distribution, and conditional quantiles for class-conditional or text-to-image generation.
