## Problem

Time series forecasting in real-world domains (weather, industrial, battery) often faces severely sparse endogenous observations, yet existing exogenous-variable forecasting methods remain brittle under such data scarcity because they depend on complete endogenous histories for scale alignment and dependency learning. This paper formalizes sparse endogenous forecasting as a distinct setting and asks how to produce accurate multi-step predictions when only a handful of endogenous points (e.g., 4 out of 96) are available alongside dense exogenous sequences.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265012/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a dense multivariate exogenous series X ∈ R^{T×N} (T=96 lookback steps, N exogenous variables) and a sparse endogenous series S ∈ R^{T^en} (T^en=4, sampled at 24-step intervals, i.e., 4% sparsity) as input, and outputs a multi-step endogenous forecast Ŷ ∈ R^{L×1} for horizons L ∈ {96, 192, 336, 720}. The key constraint is that the endogenous input is drastically shorter than the exogenous input, and the model must first reconstruct the full-length historical endogenous sequence before performing the actual prediction, effectively decoupling reconstruction from forecasting.

## Method

TimeSeed is a purely linear architecture (0.19M parameters) that reframes sparse forecasting as a two-stage context-reconstruction-then-prediction problem. Three modules jointly reconstruct the missing endogenous history: (1) a Time Domain Aggregator applies a patch operation to the periodic component of exogenous variables and uses linear layers along the variable axis to map exogenous periodicity to endogenous periodicity; (2) a Frequency Domain Aggregator applies RFFT to the exogenous trend, performs energy-based decomposition into K high-energy (dominant trend) and remaining low-energy (local fluctuation) components, aggregates each across variables via frequency-domain linear layers, and inverts via iRFFT to recover the endogenous trend; (3) an Adaptive Scale Reconstructor upsamples the sparse endogenous series at Q resolutions and uses Gumbel-Softmax to adaptively select the optimal resolution for reconstruction. The three reconstructed components are summed to form a full-length historical endogenous sequence, which is then passed through a single linear prediction head to produce the multi-step forecast.

## Results

Under the sparse endogenous setting (4% sparsity, 24-hour sampling interval), TimeSeed achieves the best MSE and MAE across all seven benchmark datasets with only 0.19M parameters. Headline results include MSE of 0.096 on ETTh1, 0.272 on ETTh2, 0.068 on ETTm1, 0.149 on ETTm2, 0.002 on Weather, 0.529 on ECL, and 0.403 on Traffic. The paper reports an average improvement of 13.01% in MSE and 7.54% in MAE over the best baseline, with particularly large gains of 15.04% on ETTh1 and 19.24% on Traffic relative to the next-best model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TimeSeed outperforms all ten baselines on every dataset and metric in the sparse endogenous setting. The closest competitor is DLinear, which TimeSeed beats by 15.04% MSE on ETTh1 (0.096 vs. 0.113) and 31% on Traffic (0.403 vs. 0.584), though CycleNet is marginally competitive on Weather (0.003 vs. 0.002 MSE). Transformer-based models degrade severely under sparsity: TimeXer jumps to 0.444 MSE on ETTh1 and 1.396 on Traffic, while PatchTST reaches 0.228 on ETTh1 and 0.499 on Traffic. Lightweight frequency-domain models (FITS, CycleNet) remain competitive on ETT datasets but fall 15–30% behind TimeSeed on ECL and Traffic. DUET, the most recent clustering-based baseline, trails TimeSeed by 15–35% MSE across all datasets. SparseTSF, despite its 1k-parameter design, shows the largest degradation on ETTm1 (0.499 MSE) and Traffic (1.276 MSE), indicating that pure sparsity-based downsampling is insufficient without exogenous context. Overall, TimeSeed's two-stage reconstruction paradigm consistently narrows the gap to full-data forecasting while using only 0.19M parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

TimeSeed sits at the intersection of two established threads: exogenous-variable forecasting (TiDE, TimeXer, NBEATSx) and lightweight linear forecasting (DLinear, FITS, SparseTSF). It departs from both by introducing a constrained input setting—only sparse endogenous observations alongside dense exogenous sequences—and by reframing the problem as context reconstruction rather than direct prediction. Technically, it repurposes the trend/seasonal decomposition from DLinear, the patch mechanism from PatchTST, FFT-based frequency analysis from FedFormer/FITS, and Gumbel-Softmax routing from Mixture-of-Experts literature, combining them into a purely linear two-stage pipeline. No single prior work addresses the sparse-endogenous regime, so the paper carves new ground primarily through problem formulation rather than through a novel architectural primitive.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper validates that a 0.19M-parameter linear model can match or beat large Transformer architectures in a practically important data-scarcity regime, which is encouraging for deployment in resource-constrained or sensor-limited settings. However, the evaluation is concentrated on ETT variants with a fixed 4% uniform-sampling sparsity, and the PhysioNet result (the one genuinely irregular-sparse dataset) shows only marginal gains over DLinear and FilterNet. Open questions include whether the reconstruction-based approach generalizes to truly irregular missing patterns, multi-variable sparse endogenous settings, or entirely absent endogenous history (which the authors acknowledge as future work). The fixed hyperparameters across all baselines, while ensuring fairness, may not reflect each model's true potential.
