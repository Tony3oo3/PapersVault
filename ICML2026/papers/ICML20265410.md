## Problem

Existing control-oriented meta-learning methods adapt controller parameters using Euclidean or static algebraic geometries (e.g., learned p-norms) that treat parameter errors as simple vector distances, ignoring the distributional structure of system uncertainties encountered during closed-loop operation. Moreover, prior Mirror Descent-based adaptive control approaches do not extend to underactuated nonlinear systems, leaving a significant class of robotic platforms unaddressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265410/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The controller consumes the current system state (position, velocity, attitude), a reference trajectory r(t), and known nominal dynamics f(x), g(x), while the offline meta-training phase additionally requires a model ensemble of M neural-network surrogate dynamics trained on historical closed-loop trajectories. The online output is a control input u(t) computed from the empirical mean of K particle estimates, with per-step computation scaling as O(K²d) (~1–2 ms at K=5), well within 100 Hz real-time budgets. The disturbance is assumed linearly parameterizable as f_ext(x,w) = Y(x)a, so the adaptive parameter vector a ∈ R^d is the quantity whose distribution is tracked in Wasserstein space.

## Method

The core contribution lifts the adaptation law from a finite-dimensional parameter vector to a probability measure ν_â over R^d, measuring parameter estimation error as a Wasserstein Bregman divergence d_{Ψ_μ}(P_#ν_a ‖ P_#ν_â) induced by a p-norm potential χ(a)=‖a‖_p^p. The parameter distribution is approximated by K particles whose dynamics follow a Wasserstein gradient flow coupled through a Gaussian kernel κ_σ, so each particle's update is influenced by nearby particles rather than evolving independently. A meta-learning outer loop jointly optimizes the neural-network feature weights θ_y, the feedback/adaptation gains (K, Λ, P), and the transport geometry parameters (p, σ), with the occupancy measure μ from closed-loop trajectories conditioning the learned geometry. Model ensembles of per-trajectory surrogate dynamics replace the true system in the inner loop, enabling differentiable trajectory optimization via adjoint sensitivity. The resulting Lyapunov analysis proves uniformly ultimately bounded tracking error, with the ultimate bound shrinking as σ→0 and K grows, and the degenerate limit K=1, σ→0 recovers the standard Mirror Descent law of Tang et al. (2025).

## Results

On the fully-actuated PFAR system at w=8.0 m/s (beyond the 0–6 m/s training range), Wass-MD achieves the fastest error reduction and smallest steady-state tracking error among all controllers, while PID exhibits significant trajectory deviation. On the underactuated PVTOL system with time-varying wind ramping to 8 m/s, Wass-MD maintains consistently lower Lyapunov function values and position error than all baselines throughout the simulation. In the particle-count ablation (Table 1), increasing K from 5 to 30 reduces PFAR RMS error from 0.1242 m to 0.0546 m (−56%) and PVTOL RMS error from 0.2544 m to 0.1659 m (−35%), with per-step runtime remaining under 8 ms (compatible with 100 Hz control).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares Wass-MD against four baselines on both PFAR and PVTOL systems under out-of-distribution wind (test range 2–8 m/s vs. training 0–6 m/s). PID with feed-forward compensation fails to track the reference under high wind (w=8 m/s), showing significant trajectory deviation on both systems. Meta-Ridge Regression (MRR, Bertinetto et al. 2019) also fails to maintain accurate tracking during the high-wind phase on PVTOL, reflecting the regression-to-control objective mismatch. Meta-Learning Adaptive Control (MLAC, Richards et al. 2023) successfully tracks the reference but with consistently higher position error and Lyapunov values than Wass-MD, as it uses Euclidean gradient-descent adaptation. Meta-trained Mirror Descent (MD, Tang et al. 2025) with a learned p-norm potential is the closest competitor, but Wass-MD outperforms it by adding Wasserstein-space particle coupling (Gaussian kernel interaction) and meta-learned bandwidth σ, yielding lower tracking error and better robustness under distributional shift. The key distinction is that MD corresponds to the degenerate limit K=1, σ→0 of Wass-MD, so the Wasserstein geometry provides strictly additional benefit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the control-oriented meta-learning framework of Richards et al. (2021, 2023) and the mirror-descent adaptive control of Tang et al. (2025) by lifting the adaptation law from finite-dimensional parameter spaces into the Wasserstein space of probability measures. Where Tang et al. meta-learn a static p-norm Bregman potential over a single parameter vector, this work represents parameter uncertainty as a K-particle empirical measure and derives a coupled particle dynamics from the Wasserstein gradient flow, with the transport geometry (p, σ) jointly optimized via meta-learning against the closed-loop tracking objective. The particle-based Wasserstein gradient flow formulation draws on Bonet et al. (2024) for mirror descent in Wasserstein space, while the model-ensemble surrogate for offline meta-training follows the standard approach from model-based RL (Clavera et al., 2018).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a principled mechanism for the adaptation law to prioritize updates in frequently visited state-space regions via the occupancy-measure-conditioned transport geometry, and it extends to underactuated systems (PVTOL) with unmatched disturbances—a setting overlooked by prior mirror-descent adaptive control. However, the stability guarantee is only uniformly ultimately bounded (UUB) rather than asymptotic, with the ultimate bound controlled by the kernel bandwidth σ; the practical advantage over a well-tuned single-particle mirror descent (the K=1, σ→0 limit) remains to be validated on higher-dimensional or real-world systems. Open questions include whether the Wasserstein geometry yields fundamental benefits beyond what a carefully chosen p-norm provides in higher-dimensional parameter spaces, and how the method scales when K and the state dimension grow simultaneously.
