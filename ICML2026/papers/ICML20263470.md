## Problem

Existing sparse attention methods for long-context LLM pre-filling apply a uniform top-k budget across all token positions and select tokens based solely on QK routing scores. This ignores the causal information flow: initial tokens recursively aggregate into every subsequent token's representation across layers, so indiscriminate pruning of early positions causes global error propagation that degrades accuracy disproportionately.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263470/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Stem operates on the Q, K, V matrices of a standard Transformer layer during the pre-filling phase, processing them at block granularity (B=128) with anti-diagonal downsampling to reduce metric computation overhead by a factor of B². It produces a sparse attention output that approximates the dense PV result, with a position-dependent sparsity budget that decays linearly from k_start blocks at the sequence head to k_end = μ·k_start blocks at the tail. The method is training-free and plug-and-play, requiring no weight modifications and integrating directly with the Block Sparse Attention kernel.

## Method

Stem combines two complementary mechanisms aligned with causal information flow. First, the Token Position-Decay (TPD) strategy linearly decays the per-position top-k budget from k_start to k_end = μ·k_start (μ=0.7), allocating more computation to initial tokens that act as recursive anchors whose information propagates to all N tokens in subsequent layers. Second, the Output-Aware Metric (OAM) replaces pure QK routing scores with a composite score M = QK^T + β·max(0, log(||V_j||₂)), derived from minimizing the L2 reconstruction error between sparse and dense attention outputs, so that tokens with high value magnitude are retained even at moderate routing scores. The inference pipeline is coarse-to-fine: metrics are computed on downsampled block representations, top-k blocks are selected per query position using the dynamic budget, and exact softmax aggregation is performed only on the gathered full-resolution blocks, achieving linear scaling in sequence length.

## Results

On LongBench, Stem achieves 31.64% average accuracy on Qwen3-8B and 41.48% on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct using only 25% and 31% sparsity budgets respectively, nearly matching dense performance (32.01% and 42.02%). On RULER, Stem attains 88.47% (Llama-3.1) and 87.15% (Qwen3) average accuracy at a strict 25% budget, the lowest among all sparse baselines. At 128K context length, Stem reduces prefill latency from 1540 ms (dense FlashAttention-2) to 420 ms, a 3.7× speedup on H20 GPUs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Stem outperforms all training-free sparse baselines on both LongBench and RULER while using the lowest sparsity budget. On LongBench (Qwen3-8B), Stem achieves 31.64% vs. MInference 30.27% (at 69% budget), XAttention 30.46% (28%), and FlexPrefill 28.55% (31%), with the largest gap over FlexPrefill (+3.09%). On Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, the advantage is even more pronounced: Stem reaches 41.48% vs. XAttention 37.91% (+3.57%) and FlexPrefill 36.09% (+5.39%). On RULER, Stem leads all sparse methods at 25% budget (88.47% Llama, 87.15% Qwen3), surpassing MInference (88.36%/86.73% at 55–76% budget) and XAttention (88.12%/86.91% at 26–35%). When integrated into training-based models, Stem further compresses the budget: DeepSeek-V3.2 DSA improves from 42.84% to 43.16% with a 15% budget reduction, and MiniCPM-4.1 InfLLMv2 maintains 42.76% (vs. 42.75%) with an 18% budget reduction, confirming orthogonality to learned sparsity patterns.

## Relation to Prior Work

Stem builds directly on the Block Sparse Attention kernel (Guo et al., 2024) for hardware-efficient execution and adopts XAttention's (Xu et al., 2025) anti-diagonal scoring for block-wise metric downsampling. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant paradigm in training-free sparse attention (MInference, FlexPrefill, XAttention) that applies a uniform top-k budget and selects tokens purely by QK routing scores. The key reframe is treating causal information flow as the organizing principle: initial tokens are identified as 'recursive anchors' whose pruning causes global, layer-compounding distortion, motivating a position-dependent budget (Token Position-Decay) and a value-magnitude-aware selection criterion (Output-Aware Metric) rather than score-only ranking.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Stem delivers a 3.7× prefill speedup at 128K context with near-lossless accuracy at only 25% budget, and its plug-and-play design allows further budget compression (15–18%) when layered onto production training-based sparse models like DeepSeek-V3.2 and MiniCPM-4.1 without retraining. The main limitation is scope: the method targets the prefill stage only, and the authors explicitly note that Token Position-Decay does not transfer to decoding (single query position), leaving decode-stage sparsification and long-form generation dynamics as open problems. Whether the position-decay schedule generalizes to non-causal or bidirectional attention variants (e.g., in encoder-only or hybrid architectures) remains unexplored.
