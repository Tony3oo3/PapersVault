## Problem

Existing methods for irregular event sequences either model individual trajectories and population-level distributional shifts in isolation (or via late fusion) or rely on discrete-time approximations that blur long-range temporal structure under sparse observations. No prior framework provides a principled continuous-time mechanism to jointly model the asynchronous, bidirectional coupling between individual latent dynamics and global distributional evolution, nor does it offer theoretical guarantees on the well-posedness and stability of such coupled dynamics under extreme sparsity.

## Input / Output

The method consumes irregularly sampled event sequences S_i = {(e_{i,k}, t_{i,k})} with non-uniform, stochastic timestamps, a global context signal C (e.g., seasonal or population-level trends), and a query time τ at which to reconstruct the latent state. The diffusion interpolator produces a dense continuous-time latent trajectory at arbitrary τ between sparse anchors, which the coupled ODE then evolves to a target time t_target; a task-specific decoder maps the final coupled state to a predicted next event, a generated GPS trajectory, or a ranked item list. The ODE solver (RK4, Δt=0.2) and the K=5-step diffusion chain operate in a shared latent space of dimension d, with the adjoint sensitivity method keeping memory O(1) in the number of ODE steps.

## Method

CoCLD couples two modules in an 'Interpolate-then-Evolve' pipeline. First, a VAE encoder maps sparse event anchors to a Gaussian latent prior, and a time-guided DDPM (K=5 denoising steps, linear noise schedule) reconstructs the latent state at any query time τ, with the time embedding τ acting as a conditioning signal that forces the denoiser to produce states consistent with dynamics at that specific instant rather than a generic average. Second, a system of coupled Neural ODEs—dh_ind/dt = f_φ(h_ind, ψ(h_glob), t) and dh_glob/dt = g_ψ(h_glob, ρ({h_ind}), t)—is integrated via RK4 to evolve individual and global states jointly, so that each individual trajectory is continuously rectified by the population-level distribution at every infinitesimal step. Training minimizes a joint objective (task NLL + diffusion ELBO + KL prior) with alternating updates of the diffusion and ODE modules. The paper proves existence/uniqueness of the coupled solution via Picard-Lindelöf, a Grönwall-based stability bound on interpolation error propagation, and a universal approximation guarantee for the coupled vector field.

## Results

On next-event prediction, CoCLD achieves the best Acc@10 across all five Foursquare/Gowalla/Brightkite datasets, reaching 0.4255 (IST), 0.5083 (NYC), 0.3827 (DC), 0.3775 (Gowalla), and 0.3649 (Brightkite), outperforming the strongest baseline PreferDiff by 6–12% relative. On trajectory generation, CoCLD attains the lowest JSD density error (0.0041 Chengdu, 0.0123 Xi'an) and trip error (0.0122, 0.0135), beating the best diffusion baseline DiffTraj by 31% and 21% respectively. On sequential behavior modeling (Amazon), CoCLD leads on all nine metric-dataset combinations, e.g., 0.0434 Recall@5 and 0.0249 MRR on Beauty, surpassing PreferDiff by 52% and 97% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoCLD outperforms all 19 baselines across every task and dataset. Against discrete-time models (SASRec, LightGCN), CoCLD gains 44–63% relative Acc@5 on IST, confirming the cost of temporal discretization. Among ODE-based methods, SGODE is the closest competitor (e.g., 0.2714 vs 0.3486 Acc@5 on IST), but CoCLD's coupled individual-global mechanism adds 28% relative improvement. Among diffusion-based baselines, PreferDiff is the strongest (0.3285 Acc@5 on IST), yet CoCLD still leads by 6% relative, attributed to its 'Interpolate-then-Evolve' paradigm versus PreferDiff's direct conditional generation. On trajectory generation, CoCLD reduces JSD density error by 31% over DiffTraj (0.0041 vs 0.0059 on Chengdu) and by 71% over VAE, while on sequential recommendation it surpasses GNG-ODE (the best ODE baseline) by 32% in Recall@5 on Amazon-Beauty. Against Neural Point Processes (Table 4), CoCLD beats Auto-NPP (the strongest NPP) by 12% Acc@10 on IST and 7% on NYC, demonstrating that explicit coupled dynamics and probabilistic interpolation outperform intensity-function modeling under sparsity.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoCLD sits at the intersection of Neural ODEs (Chen et al., 2018) for continuous-time trajectory modeling and DDPMs (Ho et al., 2020) for generative denoising, but departs from both in a key conceptual way: diffusion is repurposed not as a final-output generator but as a *latent interpolator* that reconstructs initial states from sparse anchors before a deterministic ODE solver evolves them forward. Prior continuous-time methods (Latent ODE, SGODE, GNG-ODE) model individual trajectories in isolation or with static graph structures, while diffusion-based recommenders (DiffRec, DreamRec, PreferDiff) treat time as a static conditioning signal rather than a continuous evolution variable. The paper's core architectural departure is formalizing the individual–global interaction as a system of coupled differential equations, where each entity's vector field is explicitly modulated by a shared population-level state and vice versa, rather than relying on late fusion or attention-based aggregation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'Interpolate-then-Evolve' paradigm offers a principled way to handle the two most common failure modes in irregular-sequence modeling—drift over long unobserved intervals and loss of population-level structure—within a single differentiable pipeline, and the accompanying theoretical guarantees (existence/uniqueness via Picard-Lindelöf, error propagation via Grönwall, universal approximation) give practitioners confidence in the well-posedness of the coupled system. Open questions include whether the coupling mechanism generalizes to more than two levels of hierarchy (e.g., individual → community → global), how the method scales when the aggregation function ρ(·) must handle millions of entities, and whether the assumed bidirectional feedback structure holds in domains where global trends are exogenous rather than endogenous. The authors themselves flag causal inference as a natural next step to disentangle correlation from causation in the individual–global coupling.
