## Problem

Existing Grounded Visual Reasoning (GVR) systems with MLLMs and RL fine-tuning suffer from two coupled limitations: they provide no explicit learning signal for intermediate grounding decisions (optimizing only final accuracy), and they remain prone to shortcut solutions where plausible answers exploit language priors rather than the predicted visual evidence. The paper asks how to make the causal utility of generated evidence an explicit, trainable quantity that distinguishes faithful evidence reasoning from shortcut reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265890/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a free-form text question as input and produces a textual reasoning trace, an axis-aligned bounding box (the visual evidence), and a final text answer. The pipeline is explicitly two-stage: the first stage generates the reasoning trace and bounding box conditioned on the full image and question, and the second stage crops the image at the predicted box and generates the answer conditioned on the cropped evidence region. A matched counterfactual branch (random box + noise-corrupted crop) is constructed only during training to estimate the causal uplift; at inference the model follows the standard factual path with no additional latency.

## Method

CofactGVR formalizes GVR as a Causal Grounding Graph (CGG) in which the generated visual evidence acts as a causal mediator between the image-question context and the final answer, separating an evidence-grounded path from a shortcut path. To quantify evidence utility, the method applies a do-operator intervention: the predicted bounding box is replaced with a uniformly random box and the corresponding crop is noise-corrupted, yielding a matched counterfactual branch that forces the model to 'look elsewhere.' The factual-counterfactual reward gap is converted into a bounded counterfactual bonus (via a trigger threshold and clip) that is selectively assigned only to high-quality factual rollouts through a correctness-or-top-k gating mask. To stabilize optimization under the sparse, high-variance causal signal, a Quantile-filtered Prioritized Advantage Sampling (Q-PAS) strategy filters out low-advantage rollouts and resamples with a prioritized distribution weighted by advantage magnitude. The shaped rewards are then optimized via GRPO with KL regularization against a frozen reference model, using Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct as the backbone.

## Results

CofactGVR achieves the best performance on all 12 tasks of the VisCoT test split, with notable gains over Ground-R1 on document-centric tasks (DocVQA +4.2, DUDE +3.8) and general VQA (Flickr30k +11.1, Open Images +4.4). On broader LVLM benchmarks, it improves over Ground-R1 by +72.9 on MME, +1.3 on SEED-Bench, +4.1 on MME-RealWorld-Lite, +1.5 on RealWorldQA, and +1.6 on POPE. On referring expression grounding, CofactGVR leads on RefCOCO (94.0 val) and RefCOCO+ (90.1 val, 94.9 test-A, 85.3 test-B) but shows a small drop on RefCOCOg (−1.1 val, −0.6 test) compared to Ground-R1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CofactGVR outperforms the closest RL-based competitor Ground-R1 on 11 of 12 VisCoT tasks, with the largest margins on Flickr30k (+11.1), DUDE (+3.8), DocVQA (+4.2), and Open Images (+4.4), while matching it on SROIE (89.9 vs 89.0). On LVLM benchmarks, CofactGVR leads Ground-R1 on all five metrics (MME 2428.5 vs 2355.6; SEED 76.1 vs 74.8; MME-RWL 42.4 vs 38.3; RWQA 66.9 vs 65.4; POPE 90.0 vs 88.4), and also surpasses GPT-4o on SEED (76.1 vs 72.0) and POPE (90.0 vs 86.9). On RefCOCO and RefCOCO+, CofactGVR achieves the best scores (94.0/96.6/90.9 and 90.1/94.9/85.3 respectively), beating Ground-R1 by up to +6.5 on RefCOCO+ test-B. The sole regression is on RefCOCOg (89.0 val / 89.6 test vs Ground-R1's 90.1 / 90.2), which the authors attribute to the prioritization of visual evidence slightly impacting long-form linguistic expressions. Compared to non-RL baselines like CogCoM, Chain of Spot, and FAST, CofactGVR shows substantially larger gains (e.g., +25.2 on TextVQA over CogCoM, +32.6 on GQA over Chain of Spot), confirming the advantage of RL-based counterfactual training over supervised or prompting-based grounding.

## Relation to Prior Work

CofactGVR builds directly on Ground-R1's two-stage grounded visual reasoning paradigm (RL-based grounding followed by answering) and its GRPO optimization backbone, but departs in two key ways. First, it replaces Ground-R1's single-prompt design with explicit stage-specific prompts and introduces a counterfactual intervention branch that applies the do-operator to the predicted bounding box, drawing on causal inference (Pearl, Glymour) rather than prior MLLM counterfactual work that operates at the input level (contrastive decoding) or via synthetic data. Second, it adds Q-PAS, a quantile-filtered prioritized sampling strategy for GRPO, which has no direct precedent in the GVR literature. The Causal Grounding Graph formalization positions visual evidence as a causal mediator between inputs and the final answer, a framing absent from prior GVR methods that treat grounding as a format constraint rather than a decision variable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental faithfulness gap in GVR: models can produce correct answers via language priors or global image cues while generating visually irrelevant bounding boxes, and standard RL rewards cannot distinguish these cases. The evidence-preservation analysis (Δ=78.7 on MME for CofactGVR vs. Δ=−22.1 for Ground-R1) reveals that prior RL-grounded models may actually produce counterproductive evidence, a finding with implications beyond this specific method. Open questions include extending beyond bounding boxes to segmentation masks or keypoints, adding explicit process-reward supervision for the textual reasoning trace, and developing adaptive mechanisms that decide when grounding is necessary and how much reasoning computation to allocate. The method's reliance on cropping as the sole evidence-acquisition mechanism also limits applicability to tasks requiring OCR, multi-region comparison, or tool use.
