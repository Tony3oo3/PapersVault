## Problem

Existing generative methods for incomplete multi-view clustering (IMVC) rely on weak cross-view signals and overlook stable local neighborhood structures, resulting in unstable latent recovery and insufficient structural constraint when views are missing. This paper tackles the gap by showing that local kNN neighborhood relationships remain relatively stable under missing-view scenarios and can serve as reliable conditioning signals for diffusion-based latent completion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261411/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes incomplete multi-view data where each of V views has N samples with heterogeneous dimensionalities (e.g., 76/216/64/240/47 for HandWritten), along with binary masks indicating per-sample per-view availability. It produces completed 128-dimensional latent representations for all views (recovering missing ones via diffusion), which are concatenated and fed to k-means to yield final cluster labels. Each sample is guaranteed at least one observed view to avoid fully uninformative instances.

## Method

IMVC-NCD operates in three stages: (1) view-specific MLP autoencoders compress heterogeneous features into a shared 128-d latent space; (2) a Neighborhood-Conditioned Construction (NCC) module builds a unified conditioning vector per target view by aggregating kNN prototypes (k=5) from each available conditional view and zero-filling missing views, handling fully-available, partially-available, and fully-missing cases; (3) a view-specific 1-D U-Net DDPM (T=1000 steps) performs iterative denoising in latent space, conditioned on the NCC vector injected at the bottleneck layer. Training uses a two-stage protocol (pretrain AEs for 200 epochs, then train DDPMs for 500 epochs) with classifier-free guidance (conditional dropout p=0.1 during training, guidance scale ω=1.0 at inference) to balance conditional and unconditional denoising. The key differentiator is that the conditioning signal encodes stable local neighborhood geometry rather than global or prototype-level cues, providing structure-aware guidance that remains reliable even under high missing-view ratios.

## Results

On the HandWritten dataset, IMVC-NCD achieves 94.56% ACC, 88.46% NMI, and 94.56% Purity at 30% missing rate, outperforming the strongest baseline DMVG (93.55% ACC, 86.89% NMI) by ~1.0% ACC and ~1.6% NMI. The advantage grows under higher missingness: at 70% missing rate, IMVC-NCD reaches 87.55% ACC versus 86.77% for DMVG and 84.43% for RecFormer. Across all four benchmark datasets (HandWritten, Scene-15, Fashion-MNIST, Aloi_deep) and missing rates (10%–70%), IMVC-NCD consistently achieves the best or near-best clustering metrics, with particularly large margins over non-generative baselines (e.g., 94.56% vs. 51.49% ACC over BSV at 30% missing).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IMVC-NCD outperforms all nine baselines across every dataset and missing rate tested. The closest competitor is DMVG (the prior diffusion-based IMVC method), which IMVC-NCD surpasses by ~1.0% ACC at 30% missing (94.56 vs. 93.55) and by ~0.8% at 70% missing (87.55 vs. 86.77) on HandWritten; the gap widens on Fashion-MNIST where IMVC-NCD reaches 85.52% ACC at 30% missing versus 80.34% for DMVG. Among graph-structured and prototype-based methods, RecFormer (93.07% ACC) and CPSPAN (89.70% ACC) trail IMVC-NCD by 1.5% and 4.9% respectively at 30% missing on HandWritten. Flow-based BURG (86.60%) and GAN-based A²CLN (92.48%) are further behind, with BURG degrading sharply at 50% missing (76.90% ACC). Traditional imputation baselines BSV (51.49%) and Concat (55.48%) and the imputation-free SIMVC-SA (82.44%) are substantially outperformed, confirming that explicit neighborhood-conditioned generative recovery is critical under high missingness. Under the more extreme SinV setting (half samples retain only one view), IMVC-NCD still leads DMVG by 2.3% ACC on HandWritten (93.95 vs. 91.65) and by 0.99% on Fashion (79.19 vs. 78.20).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-based generative approach to incomplete multi-view clustering (IMVC) by introducing a neighborhood-conditioned construction module that feeds kNN-aggregated latent prototypes from available views into a DDPM denoising process. It builds directly on the standard DDPM formulation (Ho et al., 2020) and classifier-free guidance (Ho & Salimans, 2021), but departs from prior diffusion-based IMVC methods (DMVG, A²CLN) by explicitly encoding stable local neighborhood structure and missingness patterns into the conditioning signal rather than relying on weak cross-view paired correspondences. The two-stage design—pretraining view-specific autoencoders to obtain a stable latent space, then training a conditional diffusion model within that space—distinguishes it from end-to-end generative IMVC approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled way to inject structural priors into diffusion-based latent completion for IMVC, showing that local neighborhood relationships remain stable under random view missingness and can serve as reliable denoising guidance. This is practically relevant for real-world multi-view systems where sensors or modalities are intermittently unavailable. However, the two-stage training protocol (frozen autoencoders during diffusion) limits representational adaptability, and the method's reliance on k-means for final clustering leaves open the question of whether joint end-to-end optimization with the diffusion model could yield further gains. The neighborhood-conditioning idea could potentially generalize to other incomplete-data generative tasks beyond multi-view clustering.
