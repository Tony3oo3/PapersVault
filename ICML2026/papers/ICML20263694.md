## Problem

MMD-based two-sample tests, despite their widespread use, suffer from a cancellation effect: the sum-of-differences construction causes positive and negative per-observation contributions to offset each other under covariance-shift and location-shift alternatives, yielding counterintuitively low power. This paper identifies this mechanism and asks whether a max-based aggregation of per-observation discrepancies can recover sensitivity to such subtle distributional shifts.

## Input / Output

The method consumes two independent samples X^(1) and X^(2) in R^p (total size n = n1 + n2) along with a positive-definite kernel h(·,·); for the fused variant, a finite collection of H Gaussian kernels with bandwidths drawn from a data-driven grid is used. The output is a scalar test statistic T = max_i T_i², where each T_i is a studentized difference between within- and between-sample average kernel distances for observation i, compared against a critical value obtained either by Gaussian bootstrap (MODboot) or permutation (MOD-fuse) to yield a reject/fail-to-reject decision at level α.

## Method

For each observation Z_i, the method computes the average kernel distance to same-sample neighbors (d̂_i^(in)) and to cross-sample neighbors (d̂_i^(bet)), then forms a studentized difference T_i by dividing their gap by a plug-in variance estimate. The test statistic is the maximum of T_i² over all n observations, which avoids the cancellation inherent in MMD's summation and makes the test sensitive to localized or sparse distributional shifts. The asymptotic null distribution is shown to be that of the maximum of a correlated Gaussian vector, with the covariance matrix explicitly characterized in terms of kernel moments Δ₁ and Δ₂; the critical value is approximated by simulating from this Gaussian. A fused variant aggregates per-kernel MOD statistics via a log-sum-exp (soft-max) operator with a temperature parameter λ, and a permutation-based calibration preserves valid type-I error control even under data-dependent kernel selection. Consistency is established under the mild condition that max_i ν_i > (4+ε) log n, where ν_i measures the signal strength at observation i.

## Results

Under the covariance-shift alternative (p=50, σ²=0.2), the classical MMD test achieves only 35.8% power, while the proposed MOD test substantially outperforms all MMD-based baselines across covariance, location, and distribution shift settings. In the hardest MNIST experiment (distinguishing digits {0–9} from {0–7,9}), MOD-fuse achieves power approaching 1.0 at n=2000, consistently outperforming all competing methods. Under mean-shift alternatives, MOD is slightly less powerful than optimized MMD variants (e.g., MMD achieves 0.955–0.980 power vs. MOD's 0.725–0.790 at n=250), but MOD dominates under all other shift types by wide margins (e.g., covariance shift at δ=1: MOD 0.685 vs. MMD 0.385).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MMD-median (Gretton et al., 2012) and MMD-split (Sutherland et al., 2017), MOD-fuse achieves substantially higher power under covariance shift (e.g., 0.680 vs. 0.165 at δ=1, Laplace kernel) and location shift (0.750 vs. 0.275), while being slightly less powerful under mean shift (0.775 vs. 0.740). Against MMD-fuse (Biggs et al., 2023), the proposed fused MOD maintains a clear advantage under covariance and distribution shift alternatives, with both methods performing comparably under mean shift. Against MMD-agg (Schrab et al., 2023) and MMD-aggInc (Schrab et al., 2022), MOD-fuse consistently outperforms under all non-mean-shift alternatives, with the gap widening as signal strength increases. The ME test (Jitkrittum et al., 2016) exhibits noticeable size distortion at small sample sizes and generally shows the lowest power among all methods, particularly under covariance and distribution shift. Overall, the key trade-off is that MOD sacrifices a small amount of power under pure mean-shift alternatives in exchange for dramatically superior detection of covariance, location, and general distributional shifts where MMD-based methods suffer from cancellation effects.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the MMD framework (Gretton et al., 2006/2012) and the kernel fusion paradigm of Biggs et al. (2023, MMD-fuse), but departs from the sum-of-differences aggregation that defines MMD by replacing it with a maximum-of-differences (MOD) aggregation over per-observation discrepancies. The motivation is a specific failure mode of MMD—cancellation effects under covariance and location-shift alternatives—that the authors diagnose through a decomposition of the MMD statistic into individual observation-level contributions. The fused MOD procedure adapts the soft-maximum kernel fusion mechanism from MMD-fuse, while the theoretical analysis draws on Gaussian approximation techniques for maxima of high-dimensional random vectors (Chernozhukov et al., 2013/2017).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled fix for a well-documented weakness of MMD-based tests, with clear practical implications for generative model evaluation and high-dimensional two-sample comparison where covariance or location shifts are common. The theoretical contribution—deriving the asymptotic null distribution of a max-type statistic with complex cross-observation correlations and proving consistency under a max-signal condition rather than an average-signal condition—is non-trivial and extends the toolkit for analyzing extreme-value-type test statistics. Open questions include the computational cost of MOD-fuse (108 s at n=2000 versus ~0.3 s for MMD variants), whether the balanced-sample condition (C1) can be relaxed, and whether the max-aggregation principle transfers to other kernel-based discrepancy measures beyond the two-sample setting.
