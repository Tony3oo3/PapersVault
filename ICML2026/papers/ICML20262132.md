## Problem

The adversarial robustness of INR-based classifiers remains largely unexplored, and prior optimistic claims that the optimization-in-the-loop INR fitting process inherently obfuscates gradients and provides robustness have not been systematically validated. Standard first-order attacks like PGD are computationally prohibitive here because each forward pass requires training a neural network per input sample, making it unclear whether these classifiers are genuinely robust or merely difficult to attack with existing tools.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes 2D RGB images (28×28 MNIST/FashionMNIST, 32×32 CIFAR-10) or 3D point clouds (ShapeNet-10) and first fits a SIREN INR (3-layer MLP, hidden dim 32, ω₀=30, 5000 Adam steps) to each sample. The fitted INR weights are then consumed by one of six downstream classifiers (direct, encoder-based, or end-to-end) to produce a class label. Adversarial perturbations are bounded in ℓ∞ norm (ε=0.3, 0.1, 0.03 for the three image datasets), and the surrogate models take the flattened image as input to predict either INR weights or compact embeddings, enabling gradient-based PGD in image space.

## Method

The core contribution is a surrogate-based auditing framework that overcomes the non-differentiability of INR fitting by training amortized surrogate models that approximate the INR-generation process, enabling standard first-order PGD attacks in input space. Five surrogate variants are introduced: a hypernetwork (from SIREN), a naive INR surrogate (MLP predicting INR weights), an FD INR surrogate (adds finite-difference Jacobian supervision so ∂θ̂/∂x · Δx ≈ Δθ), and two embedding surrogates (naive and FD) that bypass fitting entirely by predicting encoder outputs directly. The FD supervision is the key differentiator: it aligns the surrogate's local Jacobian with the true INR fitting gradient, producing substantially stronger attacks than naive weight-matching alone. The framework also defines function-space attacks (perturbing INR weights with image-space ℓ∞ constraints via iterative rescaling and orthogonal gradient projection) and embedding-space attacks (perturbing encoder outputs with relative budgets calibrated to embedding standard deviation), enabling a comprehensive comparison across three attack surfaces.

## Results

Under the proposed surrogate-based PGD attacks, INR-based classifiers drop to near-zero accuracy across all three 2D benchmarks: on MNIST, best-surrogate perturbed accuracy is 0.00–0.17% (vs. 75–98% clean); on FashionMNIST, 0.13–15.15% (vs. 74–90% clean); on CIFAR-10, 0.15–0.46% (vs. 35–57% clean). Transfer-based attacks (ResNet-18 surrogate) are largely ineffective, with perturbed accuracy within 1–2% of clean accuracy for most pipelines. On 3D ShapeNet-10, surrogate attacks reduce accuracy by 30–55 percentage points (e.g., NFT 92.9% → 37.4%), confirming the vulnerability generalizes beyond 2D images.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates two defense baselines against the proposed surrogate-based attacks. Diffusion denoising (Carlini et al., 2023) provides only marginal protection: on CIFAR-10, perturbed accuracy improves by at most 3–5 percentage points (e.g., NFT 0.19% → 3.39%), and on MNIST it offers essentially no benefit (e.g., DWS remains at 0.01%). Function-space adversarial training (adapted from Madry et al., 2018) shows strong data-dependent effectiveness: on MNIST it substantially improves robustness (NFN +55.3 pp, NFT +69.8 pp, ScaleGMN +72.0 pp in perturbed accuracy), but on CIFAR-10 it degrades performance (NFT perturbed accuracy drops from 39.8% to 24.1%, with clean accuracy falling from 48.9% to 24.7%). In contrast, transfer-based attacks using a ResNet-18 surrogate are consistently ineffective, with perturbed accuracy within 1–2% of clean accuracy for most pipelines, confirming that the vulnerability is specific to the INR fitting process rather than a generic pixel-space weakness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of implicit neural representations (INRs) and adversarial robustness, repurposing the surrogate-modeling paradigm from nonintrusive gradient estimation (Arisaka & Li, 2024) to address the optimization-in-the-loop bottleneck unique to INR fitting. It directly departs from prior optimistic findings (Rusu et al., 2022; Shor et al., 2025) that suggested the instance-specific optimization process inherently obfuscates gradients and confers robustness; instead, the authors show that this 'robustness' is an artifact of gradient masking that disappears under faithful gradient approximation. The auditing framework draws on standard first-order attack methods (PGD) and defense strategies (adversarial training, diffusion denoising) from the pixel-space adversarial robustness literature, adapting them to the function-space setting of INR-based classifiers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work challenges a growing narrative that INR-based classifiers are inherently more robust than their pixel-space counterparts, with implications for any safety-critical deployment of INR-based pipelines. The finding that increasing INR expressivity (depth, width, frequency scale) *increases* vulnerability is a counterintuitive design consideration that the INR community has not previously accounted for. Open questions include whether the vulnerabilities persist at ImageNet scale, whether INR-specific defenses (beyond adapted pixel-space ones) can close the robustness gap, and whether the surrogate-based auditing methodology generalizes to other optimization-in-the-loop architectures beyond INRs. The paper's evaluation is limited to relatively small benchmarks and six classifiers, leaving the practical severity of these vulnerabilities in large-scale deployments uncertain.
