## Problem

Web agents fine-tuned on specific trajectories and environments suffer substantial performance drops when deployed on unseen websites and interaction patterns, yet prior work largely evaluates under in-benchmark settings where training and test share the same environment. Additionally, offline web interaction data are noisy and redundant, with expert trajectories containing many irrelevant steps and extremely long accessibility-tree states (up to 180K tokens), making training both inefficient and prone to overfitting on narrow, website-specific patterns.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265685/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives a natural-language goal, a linearized accessibility tree (AXTree) representing the current web page state, and the interaction history, and must predict both a reasoning trace and a node-grounded or non-node web action (e.g., click on a specific AXTree element by its bid, type text, scroll, goto). A key practical constraint is that raw AXTree states can reach 180K tokens, far exceeding typical context windows, which motivates the target-centered pruning step that retains only a fixed window of nodes around the gold action target before any selection or training.

## Method

WEASEL casts trajectory curation as a fixed-budget subset selection problem: for each trajectory of length T, it selects T₀ ≪ T steps by maximizing an objective that sums a unary importance score (BERTScore between the goal and each state) and a pairwise diversity term (the max of 1−BERTScore distances over states and over model answers, i.e., reasoning+action concatenations). The NP-hard selection is solved with a greedy algorithm that seeds the set with the best-scoring pair and then iteratively adds the step with the largest marginal gain, achieving near-optimal solutions empirically (matching the exact optimum in >96% of trajectories). Orthogonally, target-centered pruning retains only a contiguous window of 2w+1 AXTree nodes around the gold action target, reducing input length by ~68% and cutting training time by 2×. For reasoning-native models like Qwen3, the method further replaces expert-written reasoning traces with self-generated, style-consistent rationales to avoid a style mismatch that otherwise degrades out-of-domain performance.

## Results

On zero-shot transfer from AgentTrek training, WEASEL achieves the best success rate across all benchmarks for every model family: 14.5% on WebArena-Lite, 9.5% on WebArena, 48.0% on MiniWob, and 12.4%/4.7% on WorkArena L1/L2 with Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct; 21.2% on WebArena-Lite and 19.2% on WebArena with Qwen3-8B. Training on only 10K selected steps (≈19% of the 52K full dataset) yields 9.7–12.5× speed-ups over full-data SFT while matching or exceeding full-data performance. On the NNetNav-Live training set, WEASEL similarly outperforms full-data SFT (12.1% vs 10.9% WebArena-Lite SR) with a 9.7× speed-up.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the LLM-as-Judge data-selection baseline (Zheng et al., 2023), WEASEL wins on every benchmark and model: e.g., 14.5% vs 8.5% WebArena-Lite SR with Qwen2.5-7B, and 21.2% vs 19.4% with Qwen3-8B, while using the same 10K-step budget. Against the Semantic (BERTScore-ranked) state-pruning baseline from Tan et al. (2025), WEASEL's target-centered pruning achieves 10.9% vs 9.1% WebArena-Lite SR under the same 32% token budget (Table 3), and the combined Target-centered+Semantic union actually underperforms (7.3%), confirming that local context around the action target is more informative than global semantic ranking. WEASEL also outperforms uniform random subsampling by 4.4–5.4 points on WebArena-Lite across models, demonstrating that the importance-diversity objective captures transferable signal that neither random sampling nor LLM-judged relevance alone can match.

## Relation to Prior Work

WEASEL repurposes the classic max-sum diversification framework (Borodin et al., 2017) and its greedy solver to the specific problem of selecting informative trajectory steps for offline web agent training, a setting where prior data-selection work (coresets, GLISTER) has not been applied. For the reasoning-synthesis component, it adapts the self-bootstrapping idea from STaR (Zelikman et al., 2022) to mitigate a style mismatch between expert traces and reasoning-native models. The paper deliberately positions itself against the dominant in-benchmark evaluation paradigm (e.g., WebArena-to-WebArena) used by WebRL, TTI, and WebAgent-R1, and contrasts its training-time target-centered AXTree pruning with test-time observation-reduction methods like FocusAgent and LineRetriever.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: training on only ~19% of the original data (10K vs. 52K steps) yields equal or better zero-shot transfer while cutting wall-clock training time by roughly 10–12×, making offline web agent training far more accessible. The finding that naive fine-tuning of reasoning-native models on foreign-style traces can degrade performance below the base model is a useful warning for practitioners. Open questions include whether the BERTScore-based importance/diversity scoring scales to much larger trajectory pools or multimodal settings (the AITW experiment is preliminary), whether the approach transfers to online RL training loops, and whether the gains persist at larger model scales beyond 8B parameters.
