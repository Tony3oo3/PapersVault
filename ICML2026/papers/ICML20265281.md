## Problem

Activation sparsity methods for LLMs (e.g., TEAL, LaRoSA, R-Sparse) reduce inference cost by zeroing low-magnitude hidden activations, but they suffer severe accuracy degradation at high sparsity levels (≥50%). The paper identifies the root cause as representational instability: sparsification disrupts input-dependent activations learned during pretraining, causing distributional drift in hidden states and eliminating the small set of neurons that remain permanently active across tokens.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265281/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on a frozen pre-trained LLM (Llama3-8B, Qwen3-8B, Mistral-7B, or larger) with a fixed sparsification threshold τ applied to input activations. During calibration, it consumes a small dataset (WikiText or C4) to learn one spontaneous activation vector α per linear layer via KL-divergence distillation from the dense model's output logits. At inference, the learned W·α terms are folded into bias vectors, so the model produces generated text tokens with zero additional FLOPs or MACs relative to the baseline sparse model, while yielding hidden representations that are measurably closer (via CKA) to the dense model's.

## Method

SPON augments each sparsified linear layer's forward pass from Y = W·S(X) to Y = W·S(X) + W·α, where α is a learnable, input-independent activation vector per layer. The vectors are trained by minimizing KL-divergence between the sparse model's softmax output and the dense model's, using only α as trainable parameters (all pretrained weights frozen). A key design choice is learning α in activation space rather than directly as a bias: this constrains the correction W·α to lie within the pretrained feature subspace spanned by W, enabling semantically aligned compensation. Theoretically, the KL objective yields a Fisher-weighted correction that aligns W·α with the sparsity-induced residual e(X) = WX − W·S(X) along directions of maximum output sensitivity. After calibration, W·α is absorbed into the layer's bias term, introducing ~0.016% extra parameters and zero inference-time overhead, while empirically restoring 1.178% average accuracy across benchmarks at 50% sparsity.

## Results

On WikiText perplexity at 50% activation sparsity, SPON reduces Llama3-8B from 8.34 (TEAL) to 7.83, Mistral-7B from 6.00 to 5.86, and Qwen3-8B from 9.75 to 9.26, approaching dense-model performance (6.75, 5.49, 8.99 respectively). At 60% sparsity, SPON achieves 9.63 PPL on Llama3-8B versus 11.62 for TEAL. On zero-shot commonsense reasoning at 50% sparsity, SPON yields a 1.178% average improvement over TEAL and outperforms all SOTA activation-sparsity baselines (LaRoSA, WINA, R-Sparse, WAS) with only 0.016% additional parameters and zero inference overhead (64.13 tok/s vs. 64.28 for TEAL on Llama3-8B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary baseline TEAL, SPON consistently wins at 50% and 60% sparsity across all four backbones (e.g., 7.83 vs. 8.34 PPL on Llama3-8B at 50%), while at 25% sparsity the two are comparable (6.92 vs. 6.88). Against SOTA activation-sparsity methods at 50% sparsity, SPON achieves 71.96% average on Llama3-8B and 74.97% on Mistral-7B, outperforming LaRoSA (69.82%/71.41%), WINA (70.97%/72.74%), R-Sparse (69.56%/72.30%), and WAS (70.51%/71.60%). SPON also surpasses LoRA (rank 1–32) on all six zero-shot tasks while using fewer parameters (0.0155% vs. 0.0326%–1.0282%), and outperforms SCAP (15.43 vs. 19.05 PPL on Llama3-1B at 50% sparsity). In the network-pruning comparison (Figure 3), SPON at 50% activation sparsity matches or exceeds SparseGPT, Wanda, RIA, Pruner-Zero, MaskLLM, and ARMOR on Llama3-8B perplexity. The paper notes SPON is orthogonal to the SOTA methods and could potentially be combined with them for further gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the activation sparsity line (TEAL, LaRoSA, R-Sparse, WINA, WAS) by reframing the accuracy degradation at high sparsity as a representational drift problem rather than pure information loss. The core mechanism—injecting learnable input-independent vectors (W·α) that are absorbed into bias terms after calibration—draws on the biological concept of spontaneous neural activity, but is mathematically a lightweight distillation of the dense model's global prior into a fixed offset. The Fisher-weighted correction analysis (Section 2.4) provides a principled connection to the KL-divergence calibration objective, distinguishing SPON from generic bias tuning (BitFit) or low-rank adaptation (LoRA), which the ablations show to be less effective for this specific residual-correction task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SPON offers a practically attractive zero-overhead stabilization for sparse LLM inference, and the finding that activation-space learning (α) substantially outperforms bias-space learning (b) even with 16× more bias parameters is a useful design insight for future sparse architectures. However, the absolute gains remain modest (~1.2% average), and at 50% sparsity the model still discards half its parameters, so fundamental information loss caps the recovery. Open questions include how to integrate SPON with expert routing during MoE pretraining, whether specialized hardware kernels can exploit the combined quantization+pruning+SPON regime, and whether the mechanism generalizes to non-activation forms of sparsity (e.g., token-level or attention-level pruning) beyond the preliminary MoE and Wanda-pruning experiments.
