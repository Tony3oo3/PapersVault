## Problem

Jointly optimizing role-specific prompts across interacting agents in LLM-based Multi-Agent Systems is non-trivial due to a fundamental credit assignment problem: intermediate agents may satisfy their local instructions perfectly while producing outputs that mislead downstream peers, causing system-wide failure (Local-Global Misalignment). Existing prompt optimization methods either rely on ground-truth labels (ill-suited for non-terminal agents performing intermediate steps like reasoning or summarization), operate in isolated scope without capturing how local variations propagate globally, or are restricted to selecting from fixed discrete candidate pools rather than performing open-ended generation.

## Input / Output

MASPO takes as input a directed communication graph defining agent topology, a small pool of unlabeled task queries (~50 samples, with mini-batches of 10 drawn per iteration), and initial role-specific prompts for each agent. It produces a set of optimized natural-language system prompts, one per agent, that can be directly swapped into the MAS without architectural changes. Notably, the framework requires no ground-truth labels during optimization—evaluation is entirely self-supervised via an LLM-based preference judge (Gemini-2.5-Pro), and the optimized prompts transfer across different backbone models (DeepSeek-V3, GLM-4.6, Claude-Sonnet-4, Gemini-2.5-Pro) without re-optimization.

## Method

MASPO integrates three core mechanisms into an iterative optimization loop. First, a multi-granularity joint reward model scores each candidate prompt along three dimensions—Local Validity (does the agent's output satisfy role-specific constraints), Lookahead Potential (do immediate successor agents produce better outputs given the new context), and Global Alignment (does the final system response improve)—weighted as α=0.4, β=0.4, θ=0.2, thereby resolving the credit assignment dilemma without labels. Second, Misalignment-Aware Sampling explicitly mines execution traces where local validity holds but downstream or global performance fails (Eq. 6), storing them in a buffer and injecting up to K_mis=3 such hard negatives into each optimization batch to force the optimizer to target specific coordination breakdowns. Third, the search is governed by a coordinate ascent protocol that traverses agents in topological order for T=3 generations per agent across D=3 rounds, combined with a Trace-Guided Beam Search (beam width K=2, K_sub=2 offspring per parent) that accumulates joint reward scores along the path. A Beam Refresh mechanism re-anchors candidate scores as centered win-rates against the current global best whenever an agent is re-visited in a new epoch, explicitly correcting for the covariate shift induced by evolving peer agents and preventing score staleness.

## Results

MASPO achieves an average accuracy of 70.39% on the Sequential MAS and 71.05% on the Hierarchical MAS across six benchmarks (MATH-500, AGIEval-MATH, AQuA, GPQA, MBPP, HumanEval-ET), representing an average improvement of 2.90 points over the best optimization baselines (SPO). The largest single-benchmark gain is on GPQA, where MASPO reaches 58.08% (Sequential) and 54.04% (Hierarchical) versus 49.52% and 51.01% for SPO respectively. MASPO also demonstrates cross-model transferability, with optimized prompts improving DeepSeek-V3 by +4.07, GLM-4.6 by +2.80, Claude-Sonnet-4 by +2.15, and Gemini-2.5-Pro by +2.21 average accuracy without further fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MASPO consistently outperforms all six baselines across both MAS topologies. Against single-agent baselines, MASPO (Sequential, 70.39 avg) surpasses CoT (65.86), SC(CoT) (66.48), and Self-Refine (66.27) by 3.9–4.5 points, and AgentDropout (66.89) by 3.5 points. Among prompt-optimization methods applied to the same Sequential MAS, MASPO improves over TPE/MIPRO (66.49) by +3.90 and over SPO (66.56) by +3.83; on the Hierarchical MAS, the gains are +2.58 over TPE (68.47) and +2.04 over SPO (69.01). The most pronounced advantage is on GPQA, where MASPO's joint evaluation and misalignment-aware sampling yield a +8.56 point gain over SPO on the Sequential MAS (58.08 vs. 49.52), suggesting that the credit-assignment mechanism is particularly effective for complex multi-step reasoning. Notably, even when SPO is granted equivalent computational budget (same search steps or same Gemini API calls), it still underperforms MASPO by 2.6–2.7 points, confirming that the gains stem from the algorithmic design rather than resource scaling.

## Relation to Prior Work

MASPO carves out a new sub-problem at the intersection of discrete prompt optimization and multi-agent coordination. It departs from TPE-based methods (MIPRO, MASS) that are restricted to selecting from fixed candidate pools by performing open-ended generative search over the prompt space, and from single-agent optimizers (SPO, PromptBreeder, TextGrad) by explicitly modeling the credit-assignment problem that arises when intermediate agents' outputs serve as context for downstream peers. The joint reward model (Local Validity + Lookahead Potential + Global Alignment) and the Misalignment-Aware Sampling mechanism are novel contributions that repurpose the evolutionary beam-search paradigm for a non-stationary, functionally-coupled optimization landscape.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practically critical gap: as MAS architectures proliferate, practitioners lack a principled, label-free method to co-optimize role-specific prompts across interacting agents. The topology-agnostic design and demonstrated cross-model transferability (prompts optimized on Qwen3-8B improve DeepSeek-V3, GLM-4.6, Claude-Sonnet-4, and Gemini-2.5-Pro) suggest a cost-effective paradigm where small proxy models handle optimization for expensive production backbones. Open questions include scalability to larger agent graphs (beyond 4-agent chains), sensitivity to the quality of the LLM evaluator (the framework relies on Gemini-2.5-Pro for preference judgments), and whether the coordinate-ascent scheduling remains stable in cyclic or highly parallel topologies.
