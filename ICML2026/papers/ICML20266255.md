## Problem

Multi-view consistent image generation has been dominated by continuous diffusion approaches (3D representations, camera-conditioned diffusion, image editing), yet the discrete diffusion paradigm—proven effective for language and multimodal understanding—had not been explored for this geometrically demanding task. The paper asks whether masked token prediction over discrete visual tokens can achieve competitive multi-view synthesis without specialized 3D architectures or explicit geometric priors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266255/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For image-to-multi-view (I2MV), the model takes a single 256×256 RGB reference image plus a text prompt and produces three consistent novel views at the same resolution, rendered at fixed 30° elevation with controlled azimuth offsets. For text-to-multi-view (T2MV), only a text description is provided and four views are generated. The framework also supports text-to-image generation, training-free inpainting, and multimodal understanding (e.g., captioning) through task-specific sequence templates, all within the same 8B-parameter model.

## Method

ViewMask-1-to-3 formulates multi-view generation as a discrete sequence modeling problem: each view is tokenized into a sequence of MAGVIT-v2 visual tokens (vocabulary size 2^18, 16× spatial downsampling), and multiple viewpoints are concatenated into a unified sequence with special delimiters. The base architecture is LLaDA (a masked diffusion transformer), trained with a three-stage curriculum—multimodal alignment on 1.2M image-caption pairs, I2MV fine-tuning on Objaverse/HSSD, and T2MV fine-tuning with Cap3D descriptions—using cross-entropy loss over randomly masked target tokens. The key insight is that simple random masking combined with bidirectional self-attention naturally encourages cross-view consistency without any 3D geometric priors or specialized attention modules. Inference proceeds via iterative predict-and-remask over 20 steps with a cosine confidence schedule, where low-confidence tokens are re-masked and high-confidence ones are retained, yielding a total latency of ~1.3 s/view.

## Results

On the GSO and 3D-FUTURE benchmarks, ViewMask-1-to-3 achieves the best average rank (1.3) across PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS, with GSO scores of 20.61 dB / 0.8561 / 0.1497 and 3D-FUTURE scores of 20.00 dB / 0.8650 / 0.1288. For 3D geometric consistency (via InstantMesh reconstruction), it attains the lowest Chamfer Distance (0.0149 on GSO, 0.0106 on 3D-FUTURE) and highest IoU (0.5847 on GSO, 0.5315 on 3D-FUTURE), yielding a 10.6% IoU improvement over the best continuous-diffusion baseline on 3D-FUTURE. On GenEval for text-to-image generation, the model scores 0.72 overall, matching SANA-1.5 and surpassing SD3.5-L (0.71) and unified models Show-o (0.68) and MMaDA (0.63).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ViewMask-1-to-3 outperforms all continuous-diffusion baselines on GSO across PSNR (20.61 vs. 19.80 for ViVid-1-to-3) and LPIPS (0.1497 vs. 0.1764), while matching ViVid-1-to-3 on SSIM (0.8561 vs. 0.8566). On 3D-FUTURE, Zero123++ reports a notably higher PSNR (23.50 vs. 20.00), but ViewMask-1-to-3 leads on SSIM (0.8650 vs. 0.8527) and LPIPS (0.1288 vs. 0.1529), and the overall average rank still favours ViewMask (1.3 vs. 3.8). For 3D geometric consistency, ViewMask-1-to-3 achieves the best IoU on both datasets (0.5847 on GSO, 0.5315 on 3D-FUTURE), surpassing the strongest continuous baseline Zero-1-to-3 XL (0.5271 on 3D-FUTURE) by 10.6% relative IoU. In the text-to-image extension (GenEval), the model ties SANA-1.5 at 0.72 overall and beats the closest unified model Show-o (0.68) by 0.04, while also outperforming the discrete-diffusion unified model MMaDA (0.63) by 0.09. In terms of inference speed, ViewMask-1-to-3 generates each view in 1.28 s, substantially faster than ViVid-1-to-3 (5.99 s/view) and AR-1-to-3 (5.35 s/view), despite having 8B parameters versus 4.8B and 0.9B respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the masked-token-prediction paradigm from language modeling (LLaDA) and single-image generation (MaskGIT) into the multi-view synthesis domain, which has been dominated by continuous latent-space diffusion (Zero-1-to-3, Zero123++, ViVid-1-to-3) and explicit 3D representations (TRELLIS, DreamGaussian). By representing each viewpoint as a sequence of MAGVIT-v2 visual tokens and applying iterative unmasking, it unifies text and vision in a shared discrete token space, eliminating the need for camera-parameter conditioning or epipolar-constrained attention used by prior camera-conditioned methods. The key conceptual departure is that cross-view consistency emerges from bidirectional self-attention over a jointly masked sequence rather than from any explicit 3D geometric prior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a simple random-masking schedule plus vanilla self-attention suffices for cross-view consistency—without 3D priors, epipolar constraints, or specialized architectures—suggests that geometric coherence in multi-view generation may be a learned property of joint sequence modeling rather than something requiring hand-engineered inductive biases. The unified framework (I2MV, T2MV, T2I, MMU, inpainting in one 8B model) points toward a more consolidated multimodal generation stack. Open questions include scaling beyond 256×256 resolution, handling arbitrary viewpoint counts and real-world scenes with complex lighting, and whether the discretization gap widens at higher fidelity requirements.
