## Problem

Backdoor and data-poisoning attacks can achieve high attack success while evading existing spectral and optimisation-based defences, but the geometric mechanism enabling this evasion was not understood. The paper shows this behaviour is not incidental but arises from a fundamental property of input-space curvature: for nonlinear kernels, clustered dirty-label poisons can remain order-one effective while inducing vanishing input curvature, making them provably invisible to spectral detection. No prior work had characterised when backdoors are inherently undetectable or established the unavoidable cost of any defence in terms of data-fitting capacity.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 28×28 for MNIST) or discrete text tokens (for GPT-2) and produces class labels as the primary output. Unusually, the training procedure also computes the input-space Hessian eigenspectrum via Lanczos iteration over Hessian-vector products, yielding top eigenvectors that serve as a poisoning diagnostic (measured by cosine overlap with the trigger pattern). The regularised model is the final artefact, trained with an additional input-gradient penalty term computed via torch.autograd with create_graph=True, adding 2–4× the standard training cost.

## Method

The paper uses kernel ridge regression as an exact model of wide neural networks to derive closed-form results: clustered dirty-label poisons induce a rank-one spike in the input Hessian whose magnitude scales quadratically with attack efficacy, while efficacy itself scales linearly with poison count. For nonlinear (exponential) kernels, a near-clone regime is identified where the trigger point is close to the poison cluster in feature space (r ≪ ℓ), so efficacy remains order-one but the induced curvature vanishes as r²/℄⁴, rendering the attack spectrally invisible. As a defence, the paper proposes penalising ‖∇ₓL‖² in the loss, proving that under gradient flow this contracts poison-aligned Fisher and Hessian eigenmodes exponentially while strictly reducing effective degrees of freedom (the safety-efficacy trade-off). For exponential kernels, this regularisation is shown to be equivalent to an anisotropic high-pass filter that increases the effective length scale (ℓ_eff² = ℓ² + cκ), shrinking the near-clone regime. Empirically, the theory is validated on Pre-ResNet-110, ViT-Tiny, and GPT-2 across MNIST, CIFAR-10/100, and a synthetic text task, showing that gradient regularisation combined with data augmentation and extended training jointly suppress poisoning while preserving accuracy.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (Pre-ResNet-110, θ=0.02, with augmentation), input-gradient regularisation at κ=10⁵ reduces attack success rate from 82.1% to 6.6% at 90 epochs, at the cost of dropping clean accuracy from 80.9% to 75.2%—a clear safety-efficacy trade-off. On ViT-Tiny (CIFAR-10, θ=0.02), κ=10⁵ reduces ASR from 79.7% to 72.9% with only ~1% accuracy loss (83.9%→82.8%). For the linear regression baseline on MNIST, the cosine overlap between the top input-Hessian eigenvector and the poison direction tracks ASR with R²=0.998, validating the spectral detectability theory. On GPT-2 (Countries-and-Cities), κ=10⁴ fully neutralises the backdoor (ASR=0) at clean accuracy 1.0, while κ=10⁵ over-contracts the model to 0.1 accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not benchmark against external defence methods; instead it validates its own theoretical predictions against the no-regularisation baseline (κ=0) and ablates the effect of augmentation and training duration. Without regularisation (κ=0), ASR reaches 82–88% at θ=0.02 while clean accuracy remains above 80%, confirming that standard training is highly vulnerable. Adding gradient regularisation (κ≥10⁴) monotonically reduces ASR but also reduces clean accuracy, establishing the unavoidable trade-off predicted by Theorem 5.1. Data augmentation alone (without κ) does not meaningfully suppress poisoning, but combined with κ=10⁵ it pushes the required poison fraction for effective attack significantly higher. Increasing training from 90 to 450 epochs partially recovers the accuracy loss (e.g., 75.2%→79.1% at κ=10⁵) but also restores some poison efficacy (6.6%→70.7%), indicating the trade-off frontier shifts but does not vanish. The near-clone regime prediction is confirmed: for small θ the top Hessian eigenvector shows no poison signature (cosine overlap ≈ 0) even when ASR is already non-trivial, demonstrating the detectability lag.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the linear random-matrix-theory analysis of Granziol & Flynn (2025) on rank-1 data poisoning to nonlinear kernel ridge regression, using the NTK/KRR framework (Jacot et al., 2018) as an exact model of wide networks. It departs from prior adversarial-training-based poisoning defences (Geiping et al., 2021; Wei et al., 2023) by analysing how regularisation reshapes the predictor's input-space geometry (Hessian, Fisher) rather than merely reporting test-accuracy robustness, and from prior robust-kernel-regression work (Jagielski et al., 2018; Zhu et al., 2022) by deriving closed-form spike-efficacy laws for clustered dirty-label poisons. The connection to neural collapse (Papyan et al., 2020) provides a mechanistic explanation for why the theoretically predicted near-clone regime arises in trained deep networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a fundamental limit: for nonlinear kernels, backdoor poisons can be order-one effective while inducing vanishing input curvature, making them provably invisible to spectral detection methods. This reframes the detection problem from 'find the spike' to 'understand when no spike exists,' with direct implications for security audits of deployed models. The provable safety-efficacy trade-off (gradient regularisation necessarily reduces data-fitting capacity) sets a ceiling on what any curvature-based defence can achieve. Open questions include localising poison tokens in LLMs from spectral statistics alone, validating against stronger state-of-the-art attacks, and extending the analysis to class-imbalanced and multimodal settings.
