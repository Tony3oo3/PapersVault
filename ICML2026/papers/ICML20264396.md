## Problem

Monte Carlo Tree Search scales poorly in cooperative multi-agent planning because the joint-action space grows as d^n, making naive expansion intractable under realistic simulation budgets. Prior approaches either rely on uninformed random sampling over the combinatorial space or assume additive/linear return structure, both of which miss high-value coordinated deviations that require simultaneous changes by multiple agents. The paper asks how to guide MCTS expansion toward such coordinated joint actions without enumerating the full d^n space.

## Input / Output

Each agent receives a local observation history (4 stacked frames) that is encoded into a 128-dim latent state; a communication module then synthesizes cooperative features across all agents via attention. The system outputs a joint action selected by MCTS visit counts over a small per-node candidate set (3 actions for MatGame, 7 for SMAC/SMACv2), rather than over the full d^n joint space. All counterfactual reward evaluations needed for discrete differences are computed from the learned planning model at the node state, requiring no additional environment interaction.

## Method

NONZERO extends MuZero with a hypernetwork that initializes a node-specific parameter θ for an asinh-GLM surrogate η(θ,a) = c·asinh(α⟨w(θ),ψ(a)⟩) modeling joint-action returns in a low-dimensional space. The key mechanism is NONUCT, a proposal rule that scores candidate expansions using discrete first-order differences Δ_u (single-agent gain) and mixed second-order differences Δ²_{u,v} (two-agent interaction/curvature), enabling detection of coordination traps where no single agent can improve alone but a coordinated pair can. Candidate proposal is formalized as a nonlinear bandit problem over local deviations, yielding a sublinear local-regret bound of Õ(T^{3/4}) whose sample complexity scales polynomially with nd rather than exponentially with d^n. During backpropagation, the node parameter θ is refined by minimizing a four-component loss matching the surrogate's value, first-order difference, and second-order mixed difference to the ground-truth planning model, with the hypernetwork providing warm-start initialization to accelerate convergence.

## Results

On MatGame, NonZero consistently outperforms all baselines across 8 configurations (2–8 agents, 3–10 actions, linear/non-linear rewards), with the largest gap reaching ~14% in the 8-agent non-linear setting (697.1 vs. 672.3 for MAZero at 2000 steps). On SMAC, NonZero achieves >96% win rate on all three maps while converging 50–70% faster than the closest competitor MAZero (a 2–3× training acceleration). On SMACv2, NonZero nearly doubles the win rate on challenging maps such as protoss_5_vs_5 and zerg_5_vs_5 compared to the best baseline, demonstrating strong generalization under randomized start positions and heterogeneous unit compositions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NonZero wins against all five baselines (MAZero, MAZero-NP, MA-AlphaZero, MAPPO, QMIX) on every MatGame configuration, with the margin widening as agent count and action dimensionality increase: in the 8-agent non-linear setting at 2000 steps, NonZero scores 697.1 vs. 672.3 (MAZero), 657.1 (MAPPO), and 648.2 (QMIX), a ~14% improvement over the strongest baseline. On SMAC, NonZero reaches >96% win rate on all three maps and converges 50–70% faster than MAZero, the closest model-based competitor, while MAPPO and QMIX trail further behind in both asymptotic performance and sample efficiency. On SMACv2, the gap is most dramatic: NonZero nearly doubles the win rate on protoss_5_vs_5 and zerg_5_vs_5 relative to the best baseline, whereas MAZero, MAPPO, and QMIX all struggle with the randomized start positions and heterogeneous unit compositions. The ablation confirms that the curvature-aware (second-order) component is the primary driver of these gains, with removing it causing more degradation than removing the hypernetwork initialization.

## Relation to Prior Work

NONZERO extends the MuZero/MAZero line of model-based multi-agent planning by replacing the standard UCT or random-sampling expansion with an interaction-guided proposal mechanism built on discrete first-order and mixed second-order finite differences over a learned asinh-GLM surrogate. It departs from MALinZero's linear return assumption by capturing non-additive coordination effects through the mixed-difference operator, and from Sampled MuZero's reliance on an uninformed proposal policy by grounding candidate generation in a nonlinear bandit formulation with explicit regret guarantees. The hypernetwork for node-wise parameter initialization and the graph-local optimality framework are new architectural and analytical components not present in prior multi-agent MCTS work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental bottleneck in multi-agent MCTS—the exponential joint-action space—by showing that structured local deviations (one- and two-agent) with a compact nonlinear surrogate can achieve action-dimension-free convergence, a result formalized through the exponential separation theorem. The near-doubling of win rates on SMACv2's stochastic, heterogeneous-unit maps suggests the interaction-guided exploration generalizes beyond the training distribution in ways that prior factorization-based methods (QMIX, VDN) do not. Open questions include whether the asinh-GLM smoothness assumption holds for arbitrarily complex reward landscapes, how the method scales beyond ~10 agents where the O(n²d²) pair-sampling cost grows, and whether the hypernetwork initialization remains beneficial in very long-horizon tasks where node states drift far from the training distribution.
