## Problem

Simulating diffusion bridges—conditioned SDE dynamics that terminate at a known endpoint—is particularly difficult for rare events where the unconditioned process rarely reaches the terminal state. Existing neural methods either train on uncontrolled trajectories (which fail to cover rare-event regions) or require backpropagation through simulated paths (limiting scalability). This paper seeks a training approach that learns the conditioned dynamics online without differentiating through trajectories.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261614/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes the SDE coefficients (drift b, diffusion σ), initial state x₀, terminal state x_T, and time horizon T as input, and produces a neural control drift u(t,x) that, when added as (σσᵀ)u to the base dynamics, generates bridge trajectories. The control is parameterized as a full drift f_θ = (x_T − X_t)/(T−t) + σ·η_θ, where the Brownian-bridge guiding term captures the terminal singularity and η_θ is a locally bounded neural residual. In the final discretisation step the process jumps directly to x_T, which determines the penultimate control value and propagates terminal information backwards through training.

## Method

The core contribution is a self-consistency property (SC1) of the optimal control: u*(s,x) = E[J_{t|s}ᵀ u*(t, X_t^{u*}) | X_s^{u*} = x], which relates the control at an earlier time s to its value at a later time t along solution trajectories via the Jacobian (derivative) process. Combined with the terminal bridging condition and a gradient-form constraint, this property uniquely characterises the diffusion bridge (Theorem 3.1). Training uses a fixed-point loss: the control u_θ(s, X_s) is regressed toward targets U_s computed by solving a reverse recursion backwards along simulated trajectories, with stop-gradient applied so that no gradients propagate through the simulated paths. A second self-consistency variant (SC2) replaces the base-process Jacobian with a driftless auxiliary process to avoid numerical instability when J_{t|s} grows large (e.g., over potential barriers). The resulting CCDB algorithm iterates: simulate B paths with the current control, compute targets via the reverse recursion (using vector-Jacobian products for efficiency), and perform a gradient step on the neural parameters.

## Results

On the CIR model, CCDB with (SC1) achieves KL(P*||P^θ) = 0.023±0.010, outperforming NGDB (0.051±0.012) and BEL (0.038±0.009). On the Double-Well potential (v=3.0), CCDB with (SC2) achieves 0.041±0.013, substantially beating NGDB (0.098±0.047) and SDB (0.229±0.024). On the Müller-Brown transition path sampling benchmark, CCDB(SC2) achieves KL(P^θ||P) = 27.8±1.7, comparable to NGDB (28.6±0.4) and better than Doob's Lagrangian (38.6±0.2). Across all settings, CCDB trains approximately 3× faster than NGDB (e.g., 230s vs 800s on OU, 1080s vs 5300s on 100d Müller-Brown).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NGDB (Yang et al., 2025), the most directly comparable method, CCDB wins on CIR (0.023 vs 0.051 with SC1) and Double Well (0.041 vs 0.098 with SC2), ties closely on Cell Diffusion (66.0 vs 65.5 rare; 792.4 vs 783.4 multimodal), and is comparable on Müller-Brown (27.8 vs 28.6 KL), while training ~3× faster across all settings. Against SDB (Heng et al., 2025), CCDB substantially outperforms on CIR (0.023 vs 0.104) and Double Well (0.041 vs 0.229), and avoids SDB's failure on multimodal Cell Diffusion (792.8 vs 1845). Against FB (Baker et al., 2025), CCDB wins on CIR (0.023 vs 0.074) and dramatically on rare/multimodal Cell Diffusion (66.1 vs 237; 792.8 vs 4836), while FB fails entirely on Double Well. Against BEL (Pidstrigach et al., 2025), CCDB is better on CIR (0.023 vs 0.038), Double Well (0.041 vs 0.134), and both rare/multimodal Cell Diffusion settings. On Müller-Brown, CCDB(SC2) outperforms Doob's Lagrangian (Du et al., 2024) on KL (27.8 vs 38.6), though DL trains faster since it avoids simulation entirely.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Malliavin calculus framework of Pidstrigach et al. (2025), from which the self-consistency property (SC1) can also be derived, and borrows their α-schedule design (next-step, average, endpoint prediction). It also draws on the fixed-point training paradigm of adjoint matching (Domingo-Enrich et al., 2025), showing that adjoint matching is recoverable as a special case of the proposed self-consistency framework. The key departure from the most directly comparable method, NGDB (Yang et al., 2025), is the elimination of backpropagation through simulated trajectories: instead of a KL-based objective requiring trajectory differentiation, CCDB uses a fixed-point self-consistency loss that regresses control values against stop-gradient targets computed via a backward recursion along the current controlled paths.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~3× training speedup over NGDB makes online learning of diffusion bridges substantially more scalable, particularly for high-dimensional transition path sampling problems in computational chemistry. The SC1/SC2 trade-off (Jacobian-based vs. drift-integral-based self-consistency) provides a practical diagnostic: practitioners can select the appropriate variant based on whether the base dynamics exhibit large Jacobian growth. Open questions include extension to hypoelliptic/underdamped diffusions (the standard setting in molecular dynamics), whether the SC2 benefit transfers to broader stochastic optimal control problems, and whether the fixed-point training can be made as stable as the KL-based objective of NGDB without sacrificing the no-backprop-through-trajectories property.
