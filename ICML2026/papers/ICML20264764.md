## Problem

Pretrained protein structural embeddings (e.g., from ESM-3) encode geometric, physicochemical, and topological signals in a single entangled latent space, making it difficult to relate representations to concrete biophysical principles or to generalize across structurally similar but functionally distinct proteins. Existing disentanglement efforts in protein modeling either operate at the sequence level or lack explicit information-theoretic constraints tied to structural knowledge, leaving a gap in how to decompose micro-environment representations into interpretable, functionally grounded factors.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 128-dimensional residue-centered micro-environment embeddings from the frozen ESM-3 Structural Tokenizer, supervised by eight biophysical labels (secondary structure, solvent accessibility, B-factor, weighted contact number, Kyte-Doolittle hydrophobicity, hydrogen-bond energy, contact entropy, Ollivier-Ricci curvature) computed deterministically from 3D atomic coordinates via DSSP and geometric algorithms. It produces nine parallel output vectors per residue: eight knowledge-specific embeddings (one per biophysical dimension) and one residual 'common knowledge' embedding, which are selectively fused (typically 4–5 channels chosen per task) before feeding into a GNN for downstream prediction.

## Method

ProtDiS factorizes each structural embedding through a two-stage Common Knowledge Network (MLP with SwiGLU and layer norm) into eight parallel Knowledge-Specific branches and a residual branch, guided by an Information Bottleneck objective where each channel maximizes mutual information with its target knowledge variable while compressing irrelevant information. Training combines five loss terms: supervised knowledge prediction (cross-entropy or L1), a batch-level KL bottleneck that caps each channel's information capacity, an L1 reconstruction loss ensuring the residual preserves unexplained structural content, an adversarial gradient-reversal loss that purifies the residual of all predefined knowledge signals, and a cross-channel decorrelation penalty (squared Frobenius norm of pairwise cross-correlation matrices) that enforces functional disentanglement without requiring strict statistical independence. For downstream tasks, a lightweight importance probe selects the most relevant 4–5 knowledge channels per task, which are fused through a gated network and passed to a CDConv (protein-level) or GIN (residue-level) predictor, yielding consistent gains—especially under structure-based splits—over the raw ESM-3 structural tokenizer.

## Results

Across twelve downstream protein tasks, ProtDiS achieves consistent improvements over the ESM-3 Structural Tokenizer (Esm3ST) baseline, with the largest gains under structure-based splits where test proteins share low structural similarity with training data. Under structure-based splits, ProtDiS improves enzyme class (EC) accuracy from 78.7% to 83.5%, SCOP family (SCOP-fa) accuracy from 75.0% to 78.0%, and protein-protein interface (PPIs) AUROC from 82.1% to 84.6%. Under random splits, gains are more modest but still positive on most tasks, e.g., Pfam accuracy 81.1% vs. 79.5% and PPIs AUROC 91.0% vs. 89.2%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the ESM-3 Structural Tokenizer (Esm3ST), the pretrained micro-environment encoder whose 128-dimensional embeddings ProtDiS decomposes. Under structure-based splits (the most stringent OOD setting), ProtDiS outperforms Esm3ST on all twelve tasks, with the largest absolute gains on EC accuracy (+4.8 pp, 83.5 vs. 78.7), SCOP-fa accuracy (+3.0 pp, 78.0 vs. 75.0), and PPIs AUROC (+2.5 pp, 84.6 vs. 82.1). Under random splits, ProtDiS wins on 7 of 12 tasks (EC, Pfam, SCOP-fa, SCOP-cl, Lig. Aff, PPIs, Lig. BS), ties on 2 (BP, SCOP-cf), and trails slightly on 3 (MF by 0.3, CC by 0.1, Struct. Sim by 0.6). The pattern indicates that the knowledge-guided decomposition is most beneficial when the model must generalize across structurally dissimilar proteins, consistent with the hypothesis that disentangled biophysical channels capture function-relevant variation that global structural embeddings collapse under high fold similarity.

## Relation to Prior Work

ProtDiS builds directly on ESM-3's pretrained micro-environment embeddings (Hayes et al., 2025), decomposing them into biophysically grounded channels via an information-bottleneck objective. Conceptually, it extends the disentangled representation learning lineage (InfoVAE, FactorVAE, β-TCVAE, DisenIB) from unsupervised or weakly-supervised settings to a fully knowledge-supervised regime where each latent channel is anchored to a specific structural descriptor (DSSP secondary structure, Ollivier-Ricci curvature, Kyte-Doolittle hydrophobicity, etc.). It departs from prior protein-specific disentanglement efforts—sparse autoencoders on PLM embeddings (Adams et al., 2025; Simon & Zou, 2025) and structural codeword decomposition (Pantolini et al., 2025)—by operating on 3D structural micro-environment representations rather than sequence-level embeddings, and by combining adversarial knowledge removal with cross-channel decorrelation to enforce both specificity and independence simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a principled, interpretable route to structuring protein latent spaces, with the most compelling evidence being consistent gains under structure-based (OOD) splits where entangled embeddings collapse. A key limitation is the hard dependency on 3D structural data, which restricts deployment in sequence-only or early-discovery pipelines; the authors acknowledge this and point toward extending knowledge estimation from sequence/evolutionary context as a critical next step. Broader open questions include whether the eight-dimension knowledge set is sufficient or whether additional biophysical axes (electrostatics, long-range coupling) would further improve generalization, and whether the decomposition transfers to multimodal protein language models where structural priors could enhance cross-modal alignment.
