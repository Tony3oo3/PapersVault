## Problem

All existing sampling-based sketches for resettable streaming (cardinality, sum, Bernstein statistics) are vulnerable to adaptive adversarial attacks that exploit estimate changes to infer sample membership and strategically reset keys, even when sketch size is polynomial in stream length. The paper asks whether adaptively robust sketches exist that achieve prefix-max error guarantees using only polylogarithmic space in the stream length T, bypassing strong impossibility results for linear and composable sketches.

## Input / Output

The algorithm processes a single-pass stream of two operation types: INC(x, Δ) with Δ ≥ 0 and RESET(P) which zeroes all keys satisfying predicate P, maintaining nonnegative values throughout. At each time step t it releases an estimate F̂_t of the statistic F_t = Σ_x f(v_x) satisfying the prefix-max bound |F_t − F̂_t| ≤ ε · max_{t'≤t} F_{t'} (not relative error, which provably requires Ω(n) space in this model). The sketch uses O(poly(ε⁻¹, log(T/δ))) bits and the guarantee holds simultaneously for all t with probability ≥ 1−δ against a fully adaptive adversary.

## Method

The core technique composes dedicated (non-composable) sampling sketches with the Binary Tree Mechanism for continual DP observation, feeding per-step sample-size or contribution updates into the tree and using its noisy prefix sums as the released estimates. For cardinality, this directly protects sample membership from the adversary; for sum, the estimate is decomposed into a deterministic component (keys exceeding threshold B, known to the adversary) and a bounded-sensitivity protected component fed to the tree mechanism, with a modified tree analysis handling units that contribute across many time steps. For Bernstein statistics, a new parametrization of Cohen's composable framework reduces the problem to sum plus multiple independent cardinality streams, each robustified separately. This yields an exponential robustness improvement over standard DP wrapper methods (which give only quadratic improvement) and deliberately avoids composability to sidestep polynomial-space lower bounds for linear sketches.

## Results

The paper presents the first adaptively robust sketches for resettable streaming with polylogarithmic space in stream length T. For cardinality (ℓ₀) and sum (ℓ₁) estimation, the sketches use O(ε⁻² log^(3/2) T · log(T/δ)) and O(ε⁻² log^(11/2) T · log²(1/δ)) bits respectively, achieving the prefix-max error guarantee |F_t − F̂_t| ≤ ε · max_{t'≤t} F_{t'} with probability ≥ 1−δ. For Bernstein (soft concave sublinear) statistics, the space is O(poly(ε⁻¹, log(T/δ))). The paper also demonstrates that all prior sampling-based resettable sketches (Gemulla et al. 2006/2007, Cohen et al. 2012, Pavan et al. 2024, Lin et al. 2025) are catastrophically vulnerable to adaptive adversaries, with the standard estimator reporting 0 with probability 1 under a sample-and-delete attack.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not run empirical comparisons but establishes theoretical separations. All prior resettable sampling sketches (Gemulla et al. 2006/2007, Cohen et al. 2012, Pavan et al. 2024, Lin et al. 2025) are shown to be broken by adaptive adversaries: the re-insertion attack biases the sample probability from p to p², and the sample-and-delete attack empties the sample entirely while the true cardinality remains Θ(T). Standard DP-based robustification wrappers (Hassidim et al. 2020, Blanc 2023) only support O(k²) adaptive interactions and require polynomial space in T, making them infeasible for long streams. Sketch switching (Ben-Eliezer et al. 2021b) fails in the resettable model because the statistic can oscillate Ω(T) times between min and max values, requiring Ω(T) sketch copies. The paper's Binary Tree Mechanism approach achieves an exponential improvement over standard DP robustification, supporting T adaptive interactions with only polylog(T) space, and simultaneously provides (ε,δ)-event-level differential privacy as a byproduct for the ℓ₀ sketch.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the resettable/ReLU streaming model (Gemulla et al. 2006; Cohen et al. 2012; Pavan et al. 2024) and the composable sketching framework for Bernstein statistics (Cohen 2016), but departs fundamentally from prior robustification approaches. Standard wrapper methods (Hassidim et al. 2020; Blanc 2023) and sketch switching (Ben-Eliezer et al. 2021b) require polynomial space in stream length T for resettable streams because the statistic can oscillate Θ(T) times. The key conceptual departure is applying the Binary Tree Mechanism (Chan et al. 2011) not to the final estimate but to the sketch's internal sample-size updates, thereby protecting the sampling randomness itself and achieving an exponential improvement over the quadratic robustness of standard DP wrappers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide adaptively robust resettable streaming sketches with polylogarithmic space for cardinality, sum, and the full class of Bernstein (soft concave sublinear) statistics, directly addressing compliance needs like GDPR Right to Erasure and machine unlearning where deletions must be enforced without access to original data. The demonstration that all existing sampling-based resettable sketches are catastrophically vulnerable to simple adaptive attacks (re-insertion, sample-and-delete) is a significant negative result for practitioners relying on these sketches in adversarial or feedback-loop settings. Open questions include extending to super-linear statistics (e.g., ℓ₂), the general ReLU model with partial decrements, and tightening space bounds via approximate DP or zCDP analysis of the tree mechanism.
