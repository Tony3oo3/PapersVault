## Problem

Calibration of neural network confidence has been extensively studied for single-step prediction tasks, but remains largely unexplored for sequential episodic tasks where success is only determined at the end of a trajectory and per-step confidence depends on both past and future decisions. For vision-language-action (VLA) robot policies, this gap is critical: a policy must assess its likelihood of task failure mid-episode to trigger safe early stopping, yet no principled framework existed for defining or optimizing calibration over time.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a trajectory history h_t at each step, which is either the sequence of action/token probabilities from the VLA policy (black-box setting, e.g., top-10 token probabilities per degree of freedom for OpenVLA, or FAST tokenizer logits for π0-FAST and UniVLA) or internal hidden-state features aggregated from the policy's intermediate layers (white-box setting, following SAFE's feature extraction). The training signal is a binary episode-level success label y ∈ {0,1} paired with the full trajectory. The output is a scalar calibrated success probability f_θ(h_t) at every time step, which downstream supports a time-varying conformal-prediction threshold for early stopping or a Q-value score for test-time guided action selection.

## Method

The central theoretical contribution (Theorem 5.1) shows that the risk minimizer of a newly defined sequential Brier score — the mean squared error between a per-step success prediction and the terminal binary outcome, averaged over all trajectory prefixes — is exactly the policy's Q-function Q_t^π(h_t, a_t). This equivalence transforms sequential calibration into value estimation, enabling the use of temporal-difference (TD) bootstrapping as the training loss: instead of regressing each step's prediction directly to the terminal label (Monte Carlo / BCE), TDQC minimizes (f_θ(h_t) − f_θ⁻(h_{t+1}))² for t < T and (f_θ(h_T) − y)² at the terminal step, with a slowly-updated target network for stability. The predictor is a lightweight RNN (LSTM or GRU with a 2-layer MLP head) that ingests the per-step features sequentially. Empirically, replacing the BCE loss used by SAFE with the TD loss yields consistently lower sequential Brier scores and higher ROC-AUC across four VLA models (OpenVLA, UniVLA, π0, π0-FAST) on simulated (LIBERO-10) and real-robot (WidowX, Franka) benchmarks, and the calibrated Q-function can be frozen and used at test time to guide one-step-look-ahead action search, improving OpenVLA's success rate by 13% on LIBERO-10.

## Results

On the sequential Brier score (lower is better), TDQC achieves the lowest scores across all six (model, benchmark) pairs. For example, on unseen tasks, SAFE-RNN-TDQC reduces the Brier score from 0.172 to 0.097 on π₀ LIBERO, from 0.288 to 0.215 on π₀-FAST Franka, and from 0.162 to 0.100 on UniVLA LIBERO. On ROC-AUC (higher is better), TDQC sets the state-of-the-art on unseen tasks in 4 out of 6 benchmarks, with the largest gain on π₀ LIBERO (82.94 vs. 65.03 for SAFE-RNN) and π₀-FAST Franka (68.43 vs. 56.69). Notably, the black-box RNN-TDQC variant (using only action probabilities) matches or approaches white-box SAFE-RNN-TDQC on LIBERO benchmarks, e.g., 0.197 vs. 0.218 Brier score on OpenVLA LIBERO unseen.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SAFE (Gu et al., 2025), the closest prior work, TDQC consistently improves the sequential Brier score on unseen tasks across all six benchmarks: e.g., 0.097 vs. 0.172 (π₀ LIBERO), 0.215 vs. 0.288 (π₀-FAST Franka), and 0.100 vs. 0.162 (UniVLA LIBERO). On ROC-AUC, SAFE-RNN-TDQC achieves SOTA on 4 of 6 unseen benchmarks (OpenVLA LIBERO, π₀ LIBERO, UniVLA LIBERO, π₀-FAST Franka), with the largest margin on π₀ LIBERO (82.94 vs. 65.03). Against the static predictors from Zollo & Zemel (2025) (Max Prob, Avg Prob, Running Avg Prob) and Gu et al. (2025) (Avg Entropy, Running Avg Entropy), TDQC yields substantially lower Brier scores—often by 2–5×—demonstrating that learned temporal calibration far outperforms hand-crafted uncertainty heuristics. A key finding is that the black-box RNN-TDQC variant, which uses only action/token probabilities without access to hidden states, achieves competitive or best Brier scores on all LIBERO benchmarks (e.g., 0.197 vs. 0.218 for SAFE-RNN-TDQC on OpenVLA LIBERO unseen), contradicting prior conclusions that action probabilities are insufficient for success prediction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical Brier-score calibration framework from single-step classification to episodic sequential tasks with delayed terminal feedback, and establishes a formal equivalence between minimizing the sequential Brier score and learning a Q-function in a POMDP. It builds directly on SAFE (Gu et al., 2025) for VLA failure detection, reusing its architectures and hidden-state features but replacing the Monte Carlo loss with a temporal-difference objective, and on the broader RL literature (Sutton & Barto, 2018; Mnih et al., 2015) for the TD bootstrapping mechanism and target-network stabilization. The conformal-prediction-based early stopping follows Diquigiovanni et al. (2021) and Gu et al. (2025), while the test-time guided action search draws on AlphaGo-style planning (Silver et al., 2016) adapted to VLA policies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled formulation of sequential calibration for episodic tasks and shows that the right loss (TD) can extract competitive success-prediction signal from action probabilities alone, which is critical for calibrating proprietary VLA models accessed via APIs where hidden states are unavailable. Key limitations include the failure predictor not generalizing across environments or embodiments, the test-time search requiring a forward model (limiting real-world deployment), and experiments restricted to binary episodic success. Open questions include extending the framework to continuous reward shaping, cross-embodiment transfer of calibrated predictors, and integrating learned world models to enable real-robot guided search.
