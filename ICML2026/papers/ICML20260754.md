## Problem

Diffusion-based VLA policies for robotic manipulation parameterize SE(3) poses as flat R^12 vectors and apply additive Gaussian noise, a structural mismatch the authors term the Euclidean Fallacy. This approximation induces three failure modes: manifold drift that violates SO(3) orthogonality constraints, broken equivariance under workspace coordinate transformations, and non-geodesic trajectories that fail to capture kinematically natural screw motions. The paper asks whether diffusion can be reformulated to operate intrinsically on the SE(3) manifold, eliminating these geometric errors by construction rather than through post-hoc projection.

## Input / Output

The policy consumes RGB-D observations from K cameras, which are back-projected into a unified 3D point cloud using camera intrinsics and extrinsics, alongside a natural language instruction encoded by a frozen CLIP text encoder. The output is a trajectory of H SE(3) end-effector waypoints (each a 4×4 homogeneous transformation) plus per-waypoint binary gripper open/close commands. During denoising, each waypoint's rotation is tokenized in axis-angle form and its translation as a 3D vector, with the full trajectory SE(3)^H processed jointly by the Transformer backbone.

## Method

Lie Diffuser Actor (LDA) reformulates diffusion to operate natively on SE(3) by defining the forward noising process as a left-invariant SDE where noise is injected as velocity twists ξ ∈ se(3) and mapped onto the manifold via the exponential map (g_t = g_0 · exp(σ_t ξ)). The prediction head outputs 6-dimensional twists in the tangent space rather than ambient-space noise, and each denoising step retracts samples back onto SE(3) through the exponential map, guaranteeing valid rigid transformations at every step without post-hoc SVD projection or quaternion normalization. This construction yields three proven guarantees: elimination of manifold drift by group closure (Proposition 4.1), left-invariant equivariance so the score function transforms covariantly under workspace rigid transformations via the adjoint representation (Theorem 4.2), and geodesic trajectory generation corresponding to kinematically optimal screw motions (Proposition 4.3). The architecture combines a graph attention transformer for point-cloud geometric encoding, CLIP for language, a Transformer denoising backbone with self- and cross-attention over the action horizon, and is trained via denoising score matching in se(3) with auxiliary position MSE and gripper binary cross-entropy losses.

## Results

On CALVIN ABC→D (zero-shot transfer), Lie Diffuser Actor improves average task chain length from 3.27 to 3.51 (+7.3%) over 3D Diffuser Actor, with gains across all five success-rate metrics (e.g., SR5: 41.2% → 46.2%). On the more diverse ABCD→D setting, LDA extends the lead to 3.584 vs. 3.288 average length. Cross-architecture validation on LIBERO Long shows SE(3) score matching raises success rate from 92.20% (OpenVLA-OFT baseline) to 94.13%, confirming the geometric benefit is architecture-independent. On real-robot tasks, LDA achieves 100% on Move Doll Platform and 75% on Sort Blocks, versus 90% and 55% for the baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against 3D Diffuser Actor (Ke et al., 2024), the primary same-architecture baseline, LDA wins on every CALVIN metric: average task length improves from 3.27 to 3.51 on ABC→D and from 3.288 to 3.584 on ABCD→D, with the largest relative gain on SR5 (41.2%→46.2% and 41.6%→53.7%). On the real-robot evaluation, LDA outperforms the baseline on 3 of 4 tasks (Move Doll: 100% vs 90%; Sort Blocks: 75% vs 55%; Stack Cups: 60% vs 55%) but trails slightly on Put Block in Box (75% vs 80%), where the paper attributes the baseline's edge to unconstrained Euclidean exploration being marginally advantageous for simple insertion. Against OpenVLA-OFT (Kim et al., 2025) on LIBERO Long, the Lie score-matching head raises success rate from 92.20% to 94.13%, a +1.93 pp gain that persists on a 7B-parameter VLA backbone sharing no perceptual components with 3D Diffuser Actor, confirming the benefit originates from the SE(3) formulation itself rather than co-designed architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D Diffuser Actor architecture by replacing its Euclidean SE(3) parameterization with a left-invariant SDE formulation on the Lie group, drawing mathematical foundations from Riemannian score-based generative modelling (De Bortoli et al., 2022) but specializing to the group structure of SE(3). It departs from prior equivariant policy learning (EquiBot, Diffusion-EDFs, ET-SEED), which encodes geometric structure only in the encoder while keeping the generative process in flat R^n, by making the diffusion process itself intrinsically equivariant. The work also contrasts with Riemannian flow matching approaches (Chen & Lipman, 2024; Braun et al., 2024) by retaining stochastic dynamics essential for multimodal action distributions rather than deterministic transport maps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical framework (manifold drift elimination, left-invariant equivariance, geodesic optimality) that identifies a systematic geometric error in the dominant diffusion-policy paradigm, and the cross-architecture validation on OpenVLA-OFT confirms the benefit is intrinsic to the Lie formulation rather than architectural co-design. However, empirical gains are modest (+7.3% average task length on CALVIN, ~2% on LIBERO-Long), and one real-robot task (Put Block in Box) actually degrades, suggesting the geometric correction is most beneficial in rotation-translation-coupled regimes. Open questions include whether the bi-invariant metric assumption holds under practical robot cost functions, how the approach scales to higher-dimensional groups or multi-arm systems, and whether the equivariance guarantee translates to meaningful zero-shot generalization beyond the CALVIN protocol.
