## Problem

Neural SDFs excel on watertight manifolds but fail on thin structures and open boundaries because a globally consistent inside-outside sign is ill-posed for sheet-like geometries, leading to thickness inflation and spurious closures. Conversely, UDFs accommodate general topologies but suffer from gradient singularities at the zero-level set, hindering stable optimization and reliable isosurface extraction. The paper addresses this by asking whether metric proximity and sign/phase information can be learned as separate fields and recombined into a single signed implicit function that retains the strengths of both.

## Input / Output

The method takes an unoriented point cloud (no normals, no inside/outside labels) with coordinates normalized to [-1,1]³ and produces a 3D mesh via standard Marching Cubes on the zero level set of the composite field φ. Internally, the network outputs two scalar fields per query point: a non-negative unsigned metric field r(x) and an unbounded phase field θ(x), from which a bounded phase indicator P(x)=tanh(βθ(x)) is derived. The learnable scalar β (initialized to 50) controls the sharpness of the phase transition and is optimized jointly with the network weights.

## Method

MPFs decouple geometric proximity from sign/phase by learning an unsigned metric field r(x) (parameterized with a sharp softplus to enforce non-negativity, regularized by an Eikonal constraint only on off-surface samples to avoid the UDF cusp) and a smooth phase field θ(x) whose bounded indicator P(x)=tanh(βθ(x)) provides soft inside-outside cues. The two fields are coupled via a gated-metric formulation with residual phase injection: φ(x) = r(x)P(x) + θ(x). Two key theoretical results underpin the design: Proposition 3.1 proves that sign(φ)=sign(θ) and {φ=0}={θ=0} under r≥0 and β>0, so the interface is fully determined by the phase field; Proposition 3.2 shows that at constrained surface samples (r=0, φ=0), ∇φ=∇θ, meaning the gated metric term is first-order inactive at the interface and the residual provides a non-vanishing gradient pathway for learning the phase transition. Training minimizes a weighted combination of zero-level consistency, near-surface gradient alignment between r and θ, off-surface metric Eikonal, on-surface topological Eikonal, Laplacian regularization, phase saturation, and far-field repulsion losses, using a SIREN-based MLP backbone with two lightweight heads.

## Results

On watertight models with thin structures (Table 2, top), MPFs achieve the lowest Chamfer Distance (×10³) across all 8 test shapes, with the largest margin on Ship (0.672 vs. 2.426 for PG-SDF, 5.591 for NSH) and V-Box (0.696 vs. 1.047 for StEik). On open-surface models (Table 2, bottom), the advantage is even more pronounced: MPFs score 1.757 on Flower vs. 6.237 (DiGS) and 10.425 (NSH), and 1.807 on Insertion vs. 17.721 (I-filtering). On the real-scan benchmark (3M points), MPFs achieve 0.678, marginally beating the best SDF baseline StEik (0.695) while far outperforming PG-SDF (10.118) and I-filtering (19.339). In the controlled thin-structure experiment (Table 3), MPFs maintain CD ≈ 0.20–0.27 across thicknesses T = 0.1 to 0.005, while NSH fails entirely at T ≤ 0.01 (CD 3.22–4.04) and GeoUDF produces holes/splitting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among SDF-based baselines, MPFs consistently outperform all five methods on thin-structure and open-surface models: on Ship, MPFs (0.672) beat PG-SDF (2.426) by 3.6×, StEik (2.596) by 3.9×, and NSH (5.591) by 8.3×; on the open-surface Insertion model, MPFs (1.807) beat I-filtering (17.721) by nearly 10×, and I-filtering outright fails on Hat. On standard watertight shapes (Bunny, Armadillo in Table 5), MPFs are competitive with PG-SDF and DiGS (within ~0.05 CD), confirming no regression on easy cases. Among UDF-based baselines, GeoUDF suffers topological splitting/holes at thin scales (Table 3: CD 0.35–0.43 with artifacts vs. MPF's 0.20–0.27), while CAP-UDF and S²DF produce non-smooth iso-surfaces due to gradient instability near the zero level set. In extraction speed, MPFs use standard Marching Cubes (0.13 min on Dragon at 512³) versus 10.5 min (CAP-UDF), 3.6 min (GeoUDF), 5.8 min (DEUDF), and 15.2 min (S²DF), a 27–115× speedup. DUDF is compared only at the scene level (Figures 15–16), where MPFs produce more coherent reconstructions under noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by explicitly decoupling the two responsibilities that SDF-based methods (DiGS, NSH, StEik, PG-SDF, I-filtering) and UDF-based methods (CAP-UDF, GeoUDF, DEUDF, S²DF) each handle in a single scalar field: metric proximity and inside-outside sign. Rather than building on one lineage, MPF synthesizes the strengths of both—learning a UDF-like metric field r with Eikonal regularization and a continuous phase field θ with a learnable tanh-based indicator—then couples them via a gated-metric formulation with residual phase injection (φ = rP + θ). The theoretical analysis (sign consistency and gradient agreement propositions) provides formal guarantees that the composite field's zero level set coincides with the phase field's, and that interface normals are well-defined at constrained samples, properties that neither pure SDF nor pure UDF formulations offer simultaneously for sign-ambiguous geometries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MPF addresses a long-standing practical gap: SDF-based pipelines silently fail on thin plates, open boundaries, and non-manifold junctions (producing thickness inflation or spurious closures), while UDF-based pipelines require expensive optimization-based extraction or auxiliary gradient predictions to obtain usable meshes. By enabling direct Marching Cubes extraction from a signed composite field while retaining UDF-like robustness in sign-ambiguous regions, MPF removes a major engineering bottleneck and makes thin-structure reconstruction more accessible. Limitations remain: the method still produces a narrow double-cover shell around thin plates rather than truly open surfaces, grid resolution must be tuned for extremely thin geometries, and the approach has not been validated on dynamic/4D reconstruction or very large-scale scenes. Open questions include whether the decoupling generalizes to multi-material or textured reconstruction and whether the learnable β can be replaced by a per-region adaptive mechanism.
