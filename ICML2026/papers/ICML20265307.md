## Problem

RL post-training for diffusion models (DDPO and its variants like Flow-GRPO) optimizes a per-step reverse-transition Gaussian likelihood that diverges from the score/flow-matching objective used during pretraining. This misalignment implicitly introduces noisy regression targets, increasing gradient variance and slowing convergence. The paper asks what DDPO actually optimizes and whether RL can be re-aligned with the pretraining objective to recover the conceptual symmetry that LLMs enjoy between pretraining and RL fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt c and a group of G=24 sampled images {x_0} from the current policy, evaluates a reward function (GenEval, OCR, or PickScore) on each, and produces a LoRA-adapted diffusion model (SD3.5M or FLUX) that generates better-aligned images at inference. A key practical detail is that training and sampling are fully decoupled: any ODE or SDE sampler (e.g., SA-Solver with 14 steps) can be used for generation, while the training loss is evaluated on only T=6 randomly drawn forward-process timesteps, so training timesteps need not match sampling timesteps.

## Method

The paper first proves (Theorem 2.1) that DDPO's per-step Gaussian log-likelihood is equivalent, up to discretization error, to denoising score matching with a noisy data target x_{t-Δt} rather than the clean x_0, and (Theorem 2.3) that this noisy target strictly increases the conditional covariance of the score estimator by d·κ(s,t), slowing optimization. Building on this, Advantage Weighted Matching (AWM) replaces the reverse-transition policy with a sequence-level policy p_θ(x_0|c) and optimizes the same flow-matching loss ‖v_θ(x_t,t,c)−(ε−x_0)‖² used in pretraining, but reweights each sample by its GRPO-style group-relative advantage A_i, so high-reward samples are amplified and low-reward ones suppressed. A velocity-space KL penalty against an EMA reference model prevents reward hacking, and the likelihood ratio for off-policy correction is estimated via the ELBO surrogate. This design decouples training from the sampler, avoids the variance inflation from noisy targets, and restores the pretraining–RL objective symmetry seen in LLMs, yielding up to 34× speedup over Flow-GRPO on SD3.5M and FLUX without quality loss.

## Results

On GenEval (SD3.5M), AWM achieves an overall score of 0.985, surpassing Flow-GRPO (0.95) with a 16.54× reduction in GPU hours. On OCR and PickScore, AWM delivers up to 34.67× speedup on SD3.5M PickScore (23.11 score in 27.56 GPU hours vs. 956.1 for Flow-GRPO) and 19.13× on SD3.5M OCR (0.92 accuracy in 21.76 GPU hours vs. 415.9). On FLUX, AWM reaches 0.95 OCR accuracy with 8.53× less compute and matches Flow-GRPO's PickScore (23.08) with 6.82× less compute. With extended training (†), AWM further improves to 0.96 OCR and 24.03 PickScore on SD3.5M.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AWM decisively outperforms Flow-GRPO (Liu et al., 2025a), the primary DDPO-based RL baseline, on every metric: it achieves higher GenEval (0.985 vs. 0.95), higher OCR accuracy on SD3.5M (0.92 vs. 0.89), and matches or exceeds PickScore, while requiring 6.8×–34.7× fewer GPU hours. Against TempFlow-GRPO (He et al., 2025), AWM attains a higher GenEval score (0.985 vs. 0.97) with a substantially larger speedup (16.54× vs. 2.8×). Compared to the unmodified base models (SD3.5M, FLUX, DALLE-3, GPT-4o), AWM-post-trained SD3.5M reaches 0.985 GenEval versus 0.63 for the base model and 0.67 for DALLE-3, and 0.92 OCR versus 0.59 for the base. On FLUX, AWM matches Flow-GRPO's OCR (0.95) and PickScore (23.08) while using roughly 8× and 7× less compute respectively, and with extended training pushes OCR to 0.99. The consistent pattern across both backbones and all three reward tasks confirms that AWM's variance reduction from clean-target score matching translates directly into wall-clock savings without quality degradation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DDPO (Black et al., 2023) and Flow-GRPO (Liu et al., 2025a) line of work for RL post-training of diffusion models, but reframes the entire approach through a theoretical lens: DDPO's per-step Gaussian likelihood is shown to be equivalent to denoising score matching with a noisy data target, rather than the clean-target DSM used in pretraining. The proposed AWM method draws a conceptual parallel to LLM RL post-training (PPO/GRPO), where both pretraining and RL optimize the same log-likelihood objective with reward-dependent weights, and restores this symmetry for diffusion by applying advantage weights directly to the score/flow-matching loss. The variance analysis connects to classical results on denoising score matching (Vincent, 2011) and the time-reversal theory of diffusions (Haussmann & Pardoux, 1986), while the empirical validation uses the EDM codebase (Karras et al., 2022) on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet-64.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 16–34× speedup over Flow-GRPO without quality degradation is a substantial practical win that could make RL post-training of large diffusion models (SD3.5M, FLUX) far more accessible. The theoretical result that DDPO implicitly optimizes a higher-variance noisy-target DSM objective provides a clear explanation for the slow convergence observed in practice and suggests that other reverse-process-based RL formulations may share this inefficiency. Open questions include whether the ELBO-based likelihood ratio estimation (using only 6 Monte Carlo samples) introduces meaningful bias at scale, whether the approach extends cleanly to video diffusion or multi-step autoregressive generation, and whether the decoupling of training and sampling timesteps can be pushed further (e.g., with step-distilled samplers) to yield additional gains.
