## Problem

Existing unified restoration Transformers inject degradation conditioning only into backbone blocks after fixed patch tokenization, leaving the embedding and reconstruction stages blind to local degradation variation. Fixed P×P patch boundaries cut through structured corruptions (rain streaks, blur kernels, depth-varying haze), mixing corrupted and clean pixels within a single token and producing visible grid artifacts at patch boundaries that bottleneck restoration quality regardless of model capacity.

## Input / Output

FIT takes a degraded RGB image (H×W×3, randomly cropped to 256×256 during training) and an optional discrete task label as input, and outputs a restored clean RGB image at the same resolution. The method operates in dual mode: when a task label is available it uses a hard task token, but when the label is absent or ambiguous (e.g., spatially mixed degradations) it falls back to a soft token derived entirely from the image-derived degradation vector, requiring no external metadata at inference.

## Method

FIT introduces a Dual-Granularity Degradation Encoder (DGDE) that predicts a global degradation vector g and a spatial severity map M from the input via a lightweight strided CNN. These representations jointly condition FiLM-modulated deformable patch embedding and unembedding: the embedding stage learns per-pixel sampling offsets (bounded by δ_max=0.25P) that bend patch boundaries to align with local corruption structure, while the unembedding stage inversely warps tokens back to the original grid to eliminate seam discontinuities. The token backbone is a 24-layer AdaLN Transformer where each layer computes six modulation parameters from g to scale, shift, and gate attention and feed-forward outputs. A Task-Token Dropout strategy (p=0.3) stochastically replaces hard task tokens with soft tokens projected from g during training, encouraging robust content-driven degradation inference. Training combines L1 reconstruction, auxiliary type/strength classification, a seam-aware boundary loss that penalizes cross-patch gradient mismatches, and L2 offset regularization for spatial smoothness.

## Results

On the five-degradation setting (dehazing, deraining, denoising, deblurring, low-light), FIT achieves 30.72 dB average PSNR and 0.919 average SSIM, outperforming the strongest prior method AdaIR (30.20 dB) by +0.52 dB and the JIT baseline (29.28 dB) by +1.44 dB. On the three-degradation setting (dehazing, deraining, denoising), FIT reaches 32.83 dB average PSNR, surpassing VLU-Net (32.70 dB) by +0.13 dB and JIT (31.72 dB) by +1.11 dB. Gains are most pronounced on spatially non-uniform degradations: deraining (+1.66 dB vs JIT), dehazing (+2.52 dB vs JIT), and deblurring (+1.10 dB vs JIT).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FIT outperforms all 11 compared baselines on the five-degradation setting, with the largest margins over the JIT direct-prediction baseline (+1.44 dB avg PSNR) and AdaIR (+0.52 dB). Against the strongest recent methods, FIT beats VLU-Net by +0.61 dB and Perceive-IR by +0.88 dB, with per-task wins on all five degradations. The advantage is most pronounced on spatially non-uniform tasks: deraining (+1.66 dB vs JIT, +0.54 dB vs AdaIR), dehazing (+2.52 dB vs JIT, +0.28 dB vs AdaIR), and deblurring (+1.10 dB vs JIT, +1.40 dB vs AdaIR), confirming that degradation-aware deformable tokenization helps most when corruption varies across the image. On the three-degradation setting, FIT leads VLU-Net by +0.13 dB and JIT by +1.11 dB, with best individual results on dehazing (31.11 dB) and denoising at all noise levels. Zero-shot generalization to unseen degradations (JPEG artifacts, real-world noise, defocus blur) shows consistent +0.48 to +0.57 dB gains over JIT without fine-tuning, indicating the learned offsets capture transferable corruption structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

FIT extends the JIT direct-prediction Transformer (Li & He, 2025) by replacing its fixed patch tokenization and reconstruction with degradation-conditioned deformable operations at the image-token interface. The offset mechanism draws on deformable convolutions and deformable attention, but departs from those by conditioning offsets on explicit degradation representations (global vector g and spatial map M) rather than learned content features, and by applying deformation specifically at embedding/unembedding rather than inside backbone aggregation. Within the all-in-one restoration lineage (AirNet, PromptIR, IDR, AdaIR, VLU-Net), FIT is the first to make the tokenization geometry itself degradation-aware, whereas prior methods inject degradation cues only into intermediate token processing via prompts, FiLM, or cross-attention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The grid artifact bottleneck analysis (JIT plateaus under model scaling while FIT continues to improve, up to +2.4 dB) provides a concrete diagnostic for why fixed-patch Transformers underperform on spatially heterogeneous degradations, and the deformable tokenization offers a principled fix. However, the 15–20% inference overhead from irregular sampling limits real-time deployment, and the paper acknowledges that for nearly homogeneous corruptions the benefit may be marginal. Open questions include whether a gated deformation strategy (applying offsets only in high-severity regions) can recover most of the gain at lower cost, and whether the approach extends to video restoration or higher-resolution settings where patch-boundary artifacts are even more visible.
