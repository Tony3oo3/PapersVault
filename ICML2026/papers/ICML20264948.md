## Problem

Standard off-policy evaluation (OPE) methods such as FQE and importance sampling assume fully observed rewards, but in practice rewards are often missing not at random (MNAR)—the probability of recording a reward depends on the reward value itself, inducing selection bias that no amount of conditioning on states and actions can remove. Existing remedies either require specifying an external shadow variable with domain knowledge, treat missingness as a hard constraint that distorts the reward structure, or rely on inverse propensity weighting that inflates variance. This paper asks whether the policy value can be identified and estimated under MNAR rewards using only the logged data, without modeling the missingness mechanism explicitly.

## Input / Output

The method consumes n i.i.d. offline trajectories, each containing states S_t, actions A_t, binary missingness indicators O_t, observed rewards (set to zero when O_t=0), and next states S_{t+1}, along with a target policy π_t(a|s, o_{-}) that may condition on the previous missingness indicator and a horizon T. The behavior policy that generated the data is assumed to depend only on the current state, not on missingness. The output is a scalar policy value estimate V̂(π) computed by backward induction, together with per-step bridge function estimates b̂_t, Q-function estimates Q̂_t, and value function estimates V̂_t^π over the augmented state space S×{0,1}.

## Method

The core identification mechanism uses the next state S_{t+1} as an endogenous shadow variable: under an exclusion restriction (S_{t+1} ⊥ O_t | R_t, S_t, A_t) and a relevance condition, a bridge function b_t(S_t, A_t, S_{t+1}) exists satisfying E[b_t | R_t, S_t, A_t] = R_t, so that E[b_t | S_t, A_t] recovers the full-data conditional mean reward without estimating the MNAR propensity. The bridge is estimated from observed samples only via a min-max (saddle-point) objective over RKHS function classes, which avoids the double-sampling problem inherent in directly minimizing squared conditional moments. Missing rewards are then imputed as R̃_t = O_t R_t + (1−O_t) b̂_t(S_t, A_t, S_{t+1}), and a Fitted-Q-Evaluation-style backward induction propagates these imputed rewards through the Bellman recursion on the augmented state (S_t, O_{t-1}), yielding the policy value. The paper establishes consistency and a finite-sample error bound of order K·τ_max·T²·n^{−α_min/(2α_min+1)}·log n under polynomial eigen-decay of the RKHS kernels, where K is the cumulative concentrability constant and τ_max is the ill-posedness coefficient of the conditional expectation operator.

## Results

On MIMIC-III sepsis data (T=10, 13,943 patients), Prox achieves the lowest absolute bias across all MNAR missing rates: 0.05 at 20%, 0.58 at 40%, 1.35 at 60%, and 2.66 at 80% missingness, remaining closest to the oracle FQE reference (values 2.94, 2.81, 2.46, 2.32). In simulation studies, Prox achieves the fastest MSE decay in sample size n and consistently the smallest MSE across all three missingness levels (~20%, ~40%, ~80%), with its advantage growing under heavier missingness. The theoretical error bound is O(K·τ_max·T²·√(log(c₁T/ζ))·n^{−α_min/(2α_min+1)}·log n), achieving the optimal nonparametric rate in n.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Prox outperforms the IPW-based method of Wang et al. (2025) by a wide margin at every missingness level: at 80% MNAR, Prox has bias 2.66 versus IPW's 9.59 (a 3.6× reduction), and the gap widens from 2.4× at 20% to 3.6× at 80%, reflecting IPW's variance inflation under heavy missingness. Against SCOPE (Parbhoo et al., 2020), Prox is overwhelmingly superior—SCOPE produces degenerate estimates with biases exceeding 4,000 (e.g., 10,405 at 20% missingness) due to near-zero importance weight overlap in the high-dimensional sepsis setting, and is excluded from the main figure entirely. Prox also dominates the naive FQE and imputation-based baselines, which inherit selection bias from the observed subset; impute FQE is the worst non-degenerate method, with bias reaching 17.70 at 80% missingness versus Prox's 2.66. In simulation, Prox's MSE advantage over all baselines grows with both sample size and missingness rate, while IPW and impute exhibit non-vanishing bias floors or exponential error compounding at longer horizons.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: proximal causal inference (Tchetgen Tchetgen et al., 2020; Cui et al., 2024), which provides the bridge-function moment condition for recovering unobserved quantities; minimax estimation of conditional moment models (Dikkala et al., 2020), which supplies the single-batch saddle-point estimator that avoids the double-sampling problem; and Fitted Q-Evaluation (Le et al., 2019), which provides the backward-induction estimation backbone. The key departure from prior OPE-with-missingness work (Wang et al., 2025; Chu et al., 2023) is the use of the next state S_{t+1} as an endogenous shadow variable rather than requiring an externally specified auxiliary variable, and the allowance for target policies to condition on past missingness indicators via an augmented state. This distinguishes the paper from both POMDP-based OPE (which handles partially observed states) and IPW-based approaches (which explicitly model the MNAR mechanism and suffer variance inflation).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MNAR rewards are a pervasive practical problem in healthcare EHRs, digital marketing attribution, and recommender systems, where the most clinically or commercially important outcomes are precisely the ones most likely to go unrecorded. The bridge-function approach avoids the high variance of IPW and the need for external shadow variables, making it more deployable in real offline RL pipelines. However, the method relies on completeness assumptions that are difficult to verify in practice, and the authors themselves flag sensitivity analysis for unobserved confounding in the missingness mechanism as an important open direction. The T² horizon dependence (versus T^1.5 for fully-observed FQE) also signals a non-trivial statistical cost for the MNAR correction that may limit applicability to very long-horizon problems.
