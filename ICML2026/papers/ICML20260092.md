## Problem

Existing circuit discovery methods in mechanistic interpretability are brittle: the discovered circuit changes unpredictably when a few semantically equivalent examples are added, removed, or substituted in the concept dataset, and circuits fail to generalize to out-of-distribution data. This raises the fundamental question of whether a circuit captures the target concept or merely dataset-specific artifacts. No prior method provides provable invariance guarantees for circuit structure under bounded dataset-level perturbations.

## Input / Output

The method takes a concept dataset (a finite sequence of inputs sharing a target concept, e.g., 50 same-class ImageNet images or 100 task prompts), a model graph G=(V,E) where vertices are computation units (neurons, attention heads) and edges are connections, and any black-box circuit discovery algorithm A that maps a dataset to a binary inclusion mask over components. It produces a three-valued per-component mask (certified-in, certified-out, or abstain), from which the certified circuit is the subgraph induced by certified-in components only, along with a certified edit-distance radius r guaranteeing invariance of all non-abstaining decisions. The concept dataset is treated as an ordered sequence solely to define edit distance; the base algorithm itself is permutation-invariant.

## Method

Certified Circuits wraps any circuit discovery algorithm with deletion-based randomized smoothing: it draws n i.i.d. Bernoulli deletion masks over the concept dataset, runs the base algorithm on each resulting sub-dataset, and aggregates per-component inclusion frequencies. A component is certified in if its inclusion probability exceeds threshold τ, certified out if its exclusion probability exceeds τ, and abstained otherwise. The key theoretical contribution (Theorem 3.1) combines RS-Del's edit-distance certificate with component-wise segmentation-style abstention to show that, with confidence 1−α, every non-abstaining component's membership is invariant for all concept datasets within edit distance r = ⌊log(1.5−τ)/log(p_del)⌋. The certified circuit is then the subgraph of G induced solely by certified-in components, with abstained components excluded. This yields adaptive sparsity: unstable components that the base algorithm would include are dropped, producing circuits that are simultaneously more compact and more accurate than fixed top-K baselines.

## Results

On ImageNet (ID, ResNet-101), certified circuits reach 95% circuit-class accuracy (cACC) versus 83% for the top-K relevance baseline (↑14%), while using 52% fewer neurons (K=0.336 vs. 0.700). The largest OOD gains appear on ImageNet-A, where certified circuits achieve 94% cACC versus 60% baseline (↑56%) at 15% smaller size, and on OOD-CV at 93% vs. 73% (↑28%) at 33% smaller size. On language tasks (GPT-2 Small, EAP-IG baseline), certified circuits match 100% cACC on all ID tasks while using 58–75% fewer edges, and on OOD they improve cACC by up to 8% (IOI-Hard) while reducing edge count by up to 80% (Greater-Than). All certified results hold with 99.9% confidence under the stated edit-distance radius.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For vision, the baseline is top-K relevance scoring (Hamblin et al., 2022), which retains a fixed fraction of feature channels per residual block. Certified circuits strictly dominate this baseline on all five vision benchmarks: they achieve higher peak cACC (by 6–56%) at simultaneously smaller circuit sizes (15–52% fewer neurons), with the largest gains on the hardest OOD shifts (ImageNet-A: 94% vs. 60% cACC; OOD-CV: 93% vs. 73%). For language, the baseline is EAP-IG (Hanna et al., 2024), an edge-level integrated-gradients attribution method. Certified circuits match EAP-IG's 100% cACC on all three ID tasks while using 38–75% fewer edges, and on OOD they improve cACC by up to 8% (IOI-Hard: 91% vs. 85%) while reducing edge count by up to 80% (Greater-Than: 0.008 vs. 0.040). The paper also shows that the gains are not merely from ensemble averaging: a majority-vote baseline at the same n=1,000 samples still trails certified circuits by a wide margin, confirming that the confidence threshold τ contributes beyond simple vote aggregation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines two previously separate randomized-smoothing frameworks—RS-Del (Huang et al., 2023), which certifies sequence classifiers under edit distance via randomized deletions, and SEGCERTIFY (Fischer et al., 2021), which certifies structured outputs via per-component abstention—and applies their combination to circuit discovery for the first time. Conceptually, it departs from prior circuit discovery work (ACDC, EAP-IG, top-K pruning) by treating the discovery algorithm as a black box and wrapping it with a certification layer, rather than modifying the discovery procedure itself. It also distinguishes itself from concurrent formal-verification approaches (Hadad et al., 2026) that certify a fixed circuit under input-level perturbations, by instead certifying the dataset-to-circuit mapping under dataset-level edits.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide provable stability guarantees for circuit discovery, directly addressing the well-documented brittleness of circuits to dataset composition (Méloux et al., 2025; Friedman et al., 2024). The practical finding that certification simultaneously improves accuracy and reduces circuit size—rather than trading one for the other—suggests that unstable components are actively harmful, not merely redundant, which reframes how practitioners should think about circuit sparsity. Open questions include scaling to larger language models, multimodal architectures, and sparse-activation models (e.g., MoE), as well as whether layer-wise sparsity budgets could further refine the single-K approach used here.
