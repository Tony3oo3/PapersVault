## Problem

Prior work establishes that human curation monotonically improves preference alignment in single-model self-consuming training loops, but real data ecosystems involve multiple models that iteratively train on each other's outputs. This paper asks when and how cross-model interactions can dampen, distort, or even reverse the alignment benefits of human curation, identifying conditions under which increasing curation strength degrades long-term alignment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265931/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each iteration, each of two interacting models (θ, φ) trains on a mixture of fixed real data, self- and cross-model synthetic data, and human-curated data selected via a generalized Bradley-Terry preference model, with mixing weights (λ_R, λ_S, λ_H) and cross-model fractions (λ_θ^φ, λ_φ^θ) controlling composition; both synchronous and asynchronous update regimes are supported. The output is a unique stable point (θ*, φ*) together with local sensitivity derivatives that decompose the curation effect into self-influence (∂J_p/∂λ_H^θ) and cross-influence (∂J_q/∂λ_H^θ), plus a global bound showing alignment deviation from purely real-data training scales linearly in the synthetic-data fraction.

## Method

The paper formalizes a two-model interacting self-consuming framework and proves convergence to a unique stable point under strong convexity, smoothness, and Wasserstein distribution-sensitivity assumptions, with a sufficient real-data fraction threshold τ guaranteeing stability. The core analytical contribution decomposes the curation effect on alignment via sensitivity matrices S_p, S_q (local parameter response under coupling) and cross-model influence matrices C_p, C_q (parameter propagation between models), yielding explicit inner-product formulas for self- and cross-influence. The central finding is that even when the curation-induced update direction is locally reward-aligned (ρ_p > 0), the sensitivity matrix S_p can distort this direction enough to invert the curation effect, making ∂J_p/∂λ_H^θ < 0; Corollary 4.7 gives explicit sufficient conditions in terms of ρ_p, the minimal eigenvalue of the symmetrized sensitivity matrix, and the stability margin under which curation is guaranteed to help or hurt. Experiments validate the theory on Gaussian toy models, CIFAR-10 class-conditional diffusion models with conflicting hue-based preferences, and Qwen2.5-0.5B LLMs exhibiting preference domain mismatch.

## Results

The paper's central theoretical result (Theorem 4.5) decomposes the effect of human curation into self-influence and cross-influence, showing that in multi-model settings the curation effect is non-monotonic: even when the curation direction is locally reward-aligned (ρ_p > 0), the sensitivity matrix S_p can invert the effect so that ∂J_p(θ*)/∂λ_H^θ < 0. In the CIFAR-10 diffusion-model experiments, increasing the curation fraction λ_H^θ from 0.5 to 0.6 improves J_p(θ*) when cross-model interaction is limited (λ_θ^φ ≤ 0.3, settings A1–A3, A5), but reduces J_p(θ*) under stronger interaction (setting A4). In the Gaussian mechanism-validation experiment, ρ_p and ρ_q remain positive for all coupling scales t ∈ [0.05, 1], yet ∂J_q(φ*)/∂λ_H^θ drops below zero at t = 0.9, confirming the cross-influence reversal predicted by theory. Theorem 4.8 further bounds the global alignment gap: deviation from the pure real-data optimum scales as O(1 − λ̂_R), linear in the synthetic-data fraction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The implicit baseline is the single-model self-consuming setting analyzed by Ferbach et al. (2024), where increasing human curation monotonically improves alignment (ρ_p ≈ 1 and no cross-model distortion). This paper shows that the multi-model regime fundamentally breaks this guarantee: cross-model interactions introduce a sensitivity matrix S_p (and cross-influence matrix C_q) that can amplify, attenuate, or reverse the curation effect. In the CIFAR-10 experiments, settings with limited cross-model data (A1–A3, A5) reproduce the single-model intuition—more curation yields higher J_p(θ*)—while setting A4 (higher λ_θ^φ) exhibits the backfire effect where additional curation degrades alignment. The Qwen2.5-0.5B PDM experiment further shows that under extreme cross-model coupling (λ_θ^φ = λ_φ^θ = 1, no real data), target-domain rewards remain nearly unchanged while cross-domain rewards improve, indicating that coupling-induced parameter shifts are invisible to the target alignment metric. No direct head-to-head numerical comparison against a named baseline method is provided; the contribution is the identification of conditions (Corollary 4.7: |ρ_p| > 1/(√(1+m_p²)·τ_p²)) under which the single-model monotonicity guarantee does or does not extend to the multi-model setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-model self-consuming training analysis of Ferbach et al. (2024) and Bertrand et al. (2024) to a multi-model regime where models iteratively train on data generated by themselves and by other models. It adopts the performative prediction framework of Perdomo et al. (2020) — specifically the distribution sensitivity assumption and strong convexity/smoothness conditions — to characterize convergence of the coupled system. The key conceptual departure is showing that the monotonic alignment improvement from human curation, proven in isolated single-model loops, becomes non-monotonic and can even reverse when cross-model interactions introduce conflicting preference signals and sensitivity-matrix distortion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that increasing human curation can degrade long-term alignment in multi-model ecosystems directly challenges a widely assumed design principle in data pipelines that mix model-generated content across models. The paper provides actionable local diagnostics (sensitivity matrices S_p, S_q and cross-model influence matrices C_p, C_q) that practitioners could use to detect when curation is being neutralized or inverted by ecosystem externalities. Open questions include extending the analysis to N-model systems with arbitrary coupling topologies, relaxing the strong convexity/smoothness assumptions to nonconvex neural network settings, and characterizing the gap between the multi-model stable point and the optimal real-data-only solution. The Preference Domain Mismatch (PDM) phenomenon also raises the question of whether standard alignment benchmarks can reliably detect preference conflicts in heterogeneous multi-model pipelines.
