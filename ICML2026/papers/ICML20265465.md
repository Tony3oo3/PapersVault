## Problem

Hallucination detection in LLM outputs is critically needed for safe deployment, yet most existing methods require access to model internals (logits, hidden states), external knowledge bases, or rely on static task-agnostic heuristics. Under the realistic zero-source constraint—where only the plain-text query-answer pair is available, as in third-party auditing or black-box API settings—no prior method provides both context-adaptive, multi-perspective evaluation and full interpretability of the detection decision.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single query-answer pair (q, a) as plain text with no access to token probabilities, hidden states, or external references. It produces a strictly structured output: a set of fine-grained evaluation criteria instantiated from a general set {Factual, Logical, Semantic, Temporal, Social}, each with a context-aware importance weight (summing to 1), criterion-level evidence analysis, and an integer partial score in {1,…,10}, all aggregated into a single weighted truthfulness score. At inference, K independent structured evaluations are generated and averaged to yield the final detection statistic.

## Method

HCPD employs an LLM agent (Qwen-2.5-7b) that performs Human-like Criteria Probing: it adaptively decomposes its judgment into context-relevant criteria, assigns importance weights, scores each criterion with supporting evidence, and computes a weighted sum as the overall truthfulness measure. The agent is trained via GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) using a score-alignment reward that linearly penalizes deviation between the predicted score and a weak supervision label derived from BLEURT semantic consistency with reference answers—eliminating the need for ground-truth hallucination annotations. At inference, K=5 independent probes are averaged to suppress generation variance, and the paper provides a theoretical ranking-error bound decomposed into intrinsic separability, training alignment loss, and multi-sampling concentration terms.

## Results

On LLaMA-3.1-8b, HCPD achieves an average AUROC of 88.19% across four QA datasets (TriviaQA, SciQ, NQ Open, CoQA), exceeding the second-best baseline SAPLMA (77.99%) by 10.20 percentage points. On Qwen-3-8b, HCPD reaches 89.62% average AUROC, outperforming SAPLMA (79.47%) by 10.15 points. The largest per-dataset gains are on NQ Open (+14.15% over SAPLMA on LLaMA-3.1-8b) and CoQA (+8.66%), while training-free methods (Perplexity, LN-Entropy, Semantic Entropy, SelfCKGPT) cluster between 51–74% AUROC. HCPD also demonstrates strong cross-model transferability, maintaining 83–94% AUROC when the detector trained on one source model is applied to seven heterogeneous target models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HCPD outperforms all 12 baselines across every dataset and both source models. Against the strongest training-based baselines, HCPD leads SAPLMA by 10.20% (LLaMA-3.1-8b) and 10.15% (Qwen-3-8b) in average AUROC, and TSV by 13.37% and 19.13% respectively. Training-free methods show the largest gaps: Perplexity (71.52% avg on LLaMA-3.1-8b), Semantic Entropy (73.21%), and SelfCKGPT (66.75%) all trail HCPD by 15–21 points, while EigenScore and HaloScope perform near chance level (51–66%). A key differentiator is model-agnosticism: when the detector is transferred to unseen target models (LLaMA-2-7b/13b, Qwen-2.5-7b/14b, LLaMA-3.1-70b), internal-state baselines like HaloScope and TSV degrade sharply (e.g., TSV drops from 79.78% to 59.89% when moving from LLaMA-3.1-8b to Qwen-3-8b), whereas HCPD maintains 78–94% AUROC due to its operation purely in natural language space. Ablation confirms that the criteria-probing mechanism alone contributes +10.47% AUROC over Self-evaluation, and the GRPO alignment training adds a further +19.71%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) as its training backbone but departs fundamentally from prior hallucination detection paradigms. Where logit-based methods (Perplexity, Semantic Entropy) and internal-state methods (CCS, SAPLMA, HaloScope, TSV) require access to model internals, and consistency-based methods (SelfCheckGPT, EigenScore) rely on static task-agnostic heuristics, HCPD reframes detection as an adaptive, multi-criteria reasoning process that operates solely on the (q,a) text pair. The conceptual departure is the Human-like Criteria Probing mechanism: rather than producing a single monolithic score or binary label, the agent decomposes judgment into context-specific criteria with learned importance weights, emulating how human evaluators reason about factual correctness across multiple dimensions simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-source constraint is the realistic operating regime for most deployed LLMs (commercial APIs, web interfaces), making this a practically important setting where prior methods are inapplicable. The model-agnostic transferability—one detector generalizing across LLaMA and Qwen families without retraining—addresses a key deployment bottleneck. However, the reliance on BLEURT as weak supervision introduces a circularity concern (the detector is trained to approximate a metric it is then evaluated against), and the 0.5 threshold for binary labeling is arbitrary. Open questions include whether the approach extends to long-form or multi-turn generations, how the criteria set should be adapted for domain-specific settings (e.g., medical, legal), and whether the 1-10 scoring granularity is sufficient for fine-grained severity assessment.
