## Problem

Mode collapse in autoregressive LLM generation—manifesting as looping, repetition, and loss of diversity—remains poorly understood at the level of internal model dynamics. Existing decoding heuristics (truncation, penalties, typical sampling) operate locally on the next-token distribution and cannot explain or reliably prevent long-range geometric collapse of the model's internal trajectory. This paper reframes mode collapse as reduced state-space accessibility (geometric collapse) and shows that purely symbolic or probability-level interventions are insufficient in low-randomness regimes.

## Input / Output

RMR operates on the Transformer value cache (V_t ∈ R^{t×D} per layer and head) during autoregressive decoding, taking a text prompt as the generation seed and producing standard text tokens as output. The diagnostic signal is the correlation dimension computed online from the next-token log-probability vector sequence, which is projected from the full vocabulary (typically >100k) to 10,000 dimensions via modular binning to keep computation tractable. The intervention is a low-rank damping update applied to the value cache every 10 decoding steps, leaving the key cache untouched to preserve attention structure.

## Method

RMR identifies the dominant self-reinforcing directions in the value cache by solving a generalized eigenvalue problem Σ_Δ u = λΣu, where Σ is the temporal covariance and Σ_Δ is the symmetrized lag-1 cross-covariance of mean-centered value-cache rows; under stationarity the spectrum is bounded (|λ|≤1), so eigenvalues directly quantify temporal persistence. Directions with eigenvalues exceeding a fixed threshold (λ_min = 0.8) are selected, and a low-rank damping operator V_t ← V_t − ηΓ V_t U U^⊤ is applied, where Γ applies exponentially weaker damping to older timesteps (γ=0.995) and η=0.7 controls strength. The top eigenvectors are estimated online via block power iteration with exponentially weighted moving averages (decay 0.99), avoiding formation of full D×D matrices. A minimal state-dependent IFS model illustrates the underlying mechanism: a history-dependent feedback term accumulates at low temperature, confining the trajectory to a low-dimensional attractor, and weak damping of that term restores accessibility—mirroring how RMR attenuates persistent value-cache directions to prevent geometric collapse.

## Results

RMR substantially reduces mode collapse across multiple LLMs and decoding settings. At temperature 0.7, the non-collapse rate (fraction of 1,000-token completions with correlation dimension > 8) increases from 8% (standard decoding) to 56% with RMR. Under entropy-locked decoding at an entropy target of 1.0 nats/step, RMR achieves 33% non-collapse versus only 5% for standard decoding. Text quality on non-collapse samples is statistically indistinguishable between standard and RMR (K-S p-values > 0.14 across all criteria and models).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares RMR against two baselines: typical sampling (Meister et al., 2023) and random regulation (damping a randomly chosen value-cache subspace with the same rank and strength). Typical sampling does not prevent collapse in the low-randomness regime and can actually underperform standard decoding, consistent with its tendency to reduce effective sampling uncertainty and push generation toward low-entropy collapse. Random regulation also falls short of RMR, confirming that the benefit comes from targeting the specific persistent subspace identified by the generalized eigenvalue problem rather than generic cache perturbation. RMR wins decisively on the primary non-collapse rate metric across all temperature-locked and entropy-locked settings, with the largest gains near the critical regime (temperature ≈ 0.7–0.8, entropy ≈ 1.0) where standard decoding nearly always collapses. On text quality (LLM-as-a-Judge coherence, syntax, and information progression), RMR is statistically indistinguishable from standard decoding on non-collapse samples, indicating no quality degradation from the intervention.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant token-level decoding literature (nucleus sampling, typical sampling, repetition penalties) by reframing mode collapse as a geometric phenomenon—reduced state-space accessibility in the Transformer KV cache—rather than a purely symbolic or probability-distribution issue. It builds directly on Du & Tanaka-Ishii (2025) for using correlation dimension of next-token log-probability vectors as a trajectory-level diagnostic, and is conceptually adjacent to inference-time activation interventions (Zou et al., 2023; Turner et al., 2023; Meng et al., 2023), but differs in targeting persistent directions identified via a bounded-spectrum generalized eigenvalue problem rather than fixed activation vectors. The minimal state-dependent IFS model serves as a qualitative illustration of how positive feedback in history-dependent biases produces accessibility collapse, drawing on dynamical-systems and statistical-mechanics literature (Mori-Zwanzig, synergetics, critical phenomena) rather than on prior LLM-specific analyses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The geometric reframing provides a principled handle for long-horizon generation stability that token-level heuristics lack, and the correlation dimension offers a temporally sharper diagnostic than entropy or Distinct-n for detecting the onset of collapse. Practically, RMR is a lightweight, model-agnostic inference-time intervention that substantially improves non-collapse rates (e.g., 8%→56% at temperature 0.7) without degrading text quality in stable regimes. Key limitations include the requirement for KV-cache access (ruling out black-box APIs), a fixed eigenvalue threshold validated on a single main-text prompt, and evaluation restricted to autoregressive models and long-form continuation; broader applicability to multi-turn dialogue, RAG, code generation, and non-autoregressive architectures remains open.
