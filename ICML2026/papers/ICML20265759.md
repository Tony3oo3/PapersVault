## Problem

Existing parameter-sharing compression methods rely on rigid heuristics—adjacent-layer-only sharing, fixed-size blocks, or manual index grouping—that ignore the geometric structure of the loss landscape. As a result, the perturbations introduced by sharing can align with high-curvature (sensitive) directions, causing severe accuracy degradation especially at high compression ratios. The paper asks: can sharing-induced perturbations be actively steered toward the flat (low-curvature) directions of the loss ellipsoid to preserve performance?

## Input / Output

The method takes pre-trained model weights (e.g., LLaMA-7B, Swin-L) and a small calibration subset used to approximate layer-wise Hessian eigenvectors via Hessian-vector products and the Lanczos algorithm (top-550 eigenvalues, ~0.93 h one-time cost). The output is a compressed model in which each layer stores only a shared-basis index (its 'color') and a small r×r coefficient matrix S_ℓ; full weights are reconstructed on-the-fly as Ŵ_ℓ = U_b S_ℓ V_b^T during inference, replacing one large matrix multiply with two smaller ones.

## Method

CGC formalizes cross-layer parameter sharing as a graph coloring problem: each layer is a node, each 'color' is a shared low-rank basis B_b = (U_b, V_b) generated by SVD on concatenated same-type layer weights (following Basis Sharing). The key novelty is the curvature-aware coloring function: for each layer, the Hessian eigenspace is decomposed into a high-curvature subspace (top-t eigenvectors) and a low-curvature complement, and the algorithm selects the basis that minimizes the perturbation's projection energy onto the high-curvature subspace, subject to a trust-region constraint ‖δ‖₂ ≤ β‖W_ℓ‖_F. This greedy per-layer assignment (Algorithm 1) replaces heuristic adjacency rules with a geometry-guided criterion, enabling non-local, global cross-layer sharing (e.g., layers 15, 17, 18 sharing a basis) while confining compression error along the ellipsoid's major axes. The approach is entirely training-free and post-training, requiring only a one-time Hessian estimation pass.

## Results

On LLaMA-7B at 20% compression, CGC achieves PPL of 16.54/13.88/7.07 on PTB/C4/WikiText-2 and an average zero-shot reasoning score of 0.49, outperforming the closest competitor Basis Sharing (0.48) and SVD-LLM (0.44). At 50% compression, CGC maintains PPL of 117.23/79.01/18.95 while all SVD-based baselines collapse to PPL > 1000. On Swin-L (ImageNet, 28% compression), CGC incurs only a 0.01% Top-1 accuracy drop, compared to 0.42% for GFM and 0.52% for AAFM. Inference throughput improves by 56–71% across LLaMA2-7B, LLaMA-7B, and Mistral-7B with ~40–50% parameter reduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CGC consistently outperforms all seven baselines across every compression ratio tested. The raw SVD and FWSVD baselines catastrophically fail at all ratios (PPL in the thousands to hundreds of thousands), confirming that naive low-rank truncation destroys model functionality. ASVD and SVD-LLM, which incorporate activation-aware or truncation-aware refinements, recover to usable PPL at 20% compression but still trail CGC by 1–2 PPL points and 0.03–0.06 in average zero-shot accuracy. Basis Sharing (Wang et al., ICLR 2025) is the closest competitor, using the same low-rank basis generation pipeline; CGC improves upon it at every ratio (e.g., PPL 7.07 vs 7.74 on WikiText-2 at 20%, and 18.95 vs 19.99 at 50%), with the gap widening as compression increases. On Swin-L (ImageNet, 28% compression), CGC achieves a mere 0.01% Top-1 drop, beating GFM (−0.42%), AAFM (−0.52%), FiPS (−0.03%), and LossFac (−0.04%). At extreme 70–80% compression on LLaMA-7B, CGC reduces C4 PPL by over 50% relative to Basis Sharing (995 vs 2466 at 80%), demonstrating that curvature-aware alignment provides substantially greater robustness under aggressive compression.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Basis Sharing (Wang et al., ICLR 2025) for its low-rank decomposition framework (W ≈ U_b S_ℓ V_b^T) and basis generation via SVD on concatenated layer weights, but replaces the heuristic adjacent-layer grouping with a curvature-aware global assignment. Conceptually, it draws on second-order optimization literature (Hassibi et al., 1993; Frantar et al., 2023) to formalize the sharing-induced perturbation as a Hessian-weighted energy, then reframes the basis-assignment sub-problem as a graph coloring task where each 'color' is a shared basis and the coloring cost is the projection onto high-curvature eigenvectors. This departs from both SVD-based per-layer compression (which ignores cross-layer redundancy) and prior sharing methods (Universal Transformer, Subformer, FiPS, Dynamic Tying) that rely on fixed topological rules or expensive RL-based search.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CGC offers a principled, training-free alternative to heuristic sharing that enables global cross-layer basis reuse without sacrificing accuracy, which is practically important for deploying large models on resource-constrained hardware. The graph coloring formulation provides a clean mathematical structure for discovering non-local redundancies, and the curvature-aware objective gives a clear geometric interpretation of why certain sharing patterns are stable. Open questions include whether the Hessian-based assignment can be made cheaper for very large models (the ~1.3-hour offline cost is non-trivial), whether the approach extends to training-time sharing or quantization, and whether the trust-region constraint can be relaxed further to push compression beyond 80% without the observed perplexity blowup.
