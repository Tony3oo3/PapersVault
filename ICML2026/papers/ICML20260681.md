## Problem

Image-to-point cloud registration suffers from scale ambiguity induced by viewpoint changes, cross-modal structural differences, and repetitive textures, leading to erroneous correspondences. Existing detection-free transformer-based methods alleviate this with multiscale features but still exhibit attention drift in deep cross-attention stacks (a Matthew-effect-like amplification of early biases) and intra-scale inconsistencies, hindering precise registration under challenging conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260681/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (480×640) and a 3D point cloud from the same scene, producing a rigid transformation [R, t] with R ∈ SO(3) and t ∈ R³. Image features are extracted at three pyramid resolutions {24×32, 12×16, 6×8} via ResNet+FPN, while point clouds are voxelized at 2.5 cm initial resolution through KPFCNN. Final pose is recovered via PnP+RANSAC from coarse patch-level matches refined to pixel-point correspondences at the highest resolution.

## Method

FS-I2P introduces a Hierarchical Mamba Interaction Module built on a Focus-Sweep paradigm: Focus uses point-cloud-derived scale factors and a residue term to globally calibrate image features before and after an SSM scan, establishing coarse cross-modal alignment; Sweep (based on VMamba/VSSM) reshapes image features into local streams, alternately interleaves them with the point cloud stream, and performs sequential scanning so each image region interacts with shared 3D geometric context for fine-grained refinement. A Dynamic Layer Allocation Strategy employs REINFORCE with the matching constraint as reward to adaptively select the number of Focus-Sweep iterations per scale (action space {0,1,2,3}), replacing a fixed depth with a sample-conditioned policy. Mamba/SSM is chosen over transformers specifically to avoid the attention-drift degradation observed when stacking cross-attention layers, while retaining long-range modeling at linear cost. Training combines a circle loss on coarse and fine matching with the REINFORCE policy loss, and hierarchical top-k selection across three scales via element-wise max of cosine similarity score maps yields the final correspondence set.

## Results

On RGB-D Scenes V2, FS-I2P achieves a mean Inlier Ratio of 42.9%, Feature Matching Recall of 94.4%, and Registration Recall of 72.9%, outperforming the previous best (Flow-I2P) by 2.8 pp in IR and 4.5 pp in RR. On 7-Scenes, FS-I2P reaches 53.9% IR, 92.4% FMR, and 82.7% RR, with particularly large gains on the challenging Heads scene (86.3% RR vs. 52.1% for 2D3D-MATR). With DINOv2 as the image encoder, FS-I2P+DINOv2 further boosts RR to 86.3% (RGB-D) and 84.6% (7-Scenes). On the outdoor KITTI benchmark, FS-I2P achieves the best RTE of 1.57±2.75 m and RRE of 2.36±2.58°, surpassing all prior image-to-point methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FS-I2P outperforms all 15 compared baselines on the primary RGB-D Scenes V2 benchmark across all three metrics, with the largest margins over 2D3D-MATR (+10.5 pp IR, +16.5 pp RR) and Flow-I2P (+2.8 pp IR, +4.5 pp RR). On 7-Scenes, FS-I2P leads in IR (53.9 vs. 53.2 for Diff²I2P) and FMR (92.4, tied with CA-I2P), but Diff²I2P retains a slight edge in RR (83.0 vs. 82.7); however, FS-I2P+DINOv2 closes this gap decisively at 84.6. On KITTI, FS-I2P achieves the best RTE (1.57 m) and RRE (2.36°), beating 2D3D-MATR (1.86 m, 2.59°) and RetrI2P (1.61 m, 3.16°) in both rotation and translation error. The method also demonstrates strong cross-dataset generalization (7-Scenes→RGB-D V2), improving RR from 19.8% (2D3D-MATR) to 24.4%. In the ablation (Table 3), FS-I2P matches the base model's inference speed (0.304 s vs. 0.281 s) while using less memory than the transformer variant (6415 MB vs. 8346 MB), confirming that the Mamba-based design is both more accurate and more efficient than cross-attention alternatives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the detection-free image-to-point-cloud registration line (2D3D-MATR, B2-3D, CA-I2P, Flow-I2P) by replacing the dominant transformer-based cross-attention interaction with a Mamba/SSM-based Focus-Sweep module, drawing conceptual inspiration from SaFiRe's saccade-fixation paradigm for referring image segmentation. It departs from prior hierarchical cross-attention designs by arguing that deep transformer stacking amplifies early attention biases (a 'Matthew effect'), and instead leverages SSM's sequential scanning for more stable multi-scale interaction. The Dynamic Layer Allocation Strategy introduces reinforcement learning (REINFORCE) to solve the discrete, non-differentiable problem of selecting interaction depth, a component absent from all prior I2P methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical and efficient alternative to transformer-based cross-modal registration, with the Mamba-based interaction matching or exceeding transformer performance at roughly 40% lower memory and inference cost. The RL-based depth allocation is a creative solution to a genuinely hard discrete optimization problem, though its gain over the best fixed depth (72.9 vs 68.2 RR) is moderate. Open questions include whether the Focus-Sweep paradigm transfers to other cross-modal tasks (e.g., LiDAR-camera), whether the RL policy generalizes across scene types without retraining, and whether the approach scales to larger, more complex outdoor environments beyond the single KITTI evaluation in the appendix.
