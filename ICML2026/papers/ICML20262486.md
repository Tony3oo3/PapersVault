## Problem

Existing all-in-one thermal infrared (TIR) restoration methods assume a fixed, closed set of degradations and rely on shared parameters, making them vulnerable to catastrophic forgetting and gradient interference when new degradations emerge in open-world settings. The core challenge is to enable interference-free continual adaptation to novel degradations while preventing unbounded model growth over an open-ended task stream.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262486/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a degraded TIR image (256×256 training patches, 640×512 at inference) along with a degradation type label identifying which learned task to activate, and outputs a restored TIR image. For compound degradations, the model additionally activates historical sub-degradation branches for knowledge mining. The degradation type is assumed available at inference for learned tasks, supported in practice by a lightweight recognizer.

## Method

ECMRNet builds a U-Net backbone where each stage uses group convolutions to partition features into mutually isolated channel subspaces, with standard convolutions only at stage boundaries for limited inter-group mixing. Stage-wise Channel Group Expansion (SCGE) freezes all historical groups and appends isomorphic new groups initialized from pretrained parameters, achieving strict gradient isolation for each new degradation. Structural Entropy Pruning (SEP) then bounds model growth by constructing intra-group and inter-group similarity graphs, computing 2D structural entropy via the CoDeSEG algorithm to identify redundant channel groups, and pruning those with low cross-sample selection frequency followed by lightweight fine-tuning. For compound degradations, the Sub-degradation Knowledge Mining Module (SKMM) uses a lightweight hypernetwork to generate a sample-adaptive low-rank mixing matrix (rank R≪M) that selectively retrieves and recombines transferable components from historical sub-degradation features as compensatory residuals, avoiding negative transfer from indiscriminate fusion.

## Results

On HM-TIR, ECMRNet achieves an average PSNR of 30.36 dB and SSIM of 0.907 across seven degradation tasks (C, B, N, CB, CN, BN, CBN), outperforming the strongest all-in-one baseline MoCE-IR (28.77 dB / 0.895) by 1.59 dB and 0.012 SSIM. On the cross-dataset M³FD benchmark, ECMRNet reaches 29.06 dB / 0.929, again leading MoCE-IR (27.44 / 0.922) by 1.62 dB. On three real-world TIR datasets (EN, TIR100, AWMM), ECMRNet attains the best no-reference scores: MUSIQ 70.89 / 62.66 / 57.50 and CLIP-IQA 0.320 / 0.338 / 0.236, while using only 19.40M parameters and at most 297.97G FLOPs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ECMRNet outperforms all six all-in-one restoration baselines (Restormer, NAFNet, PromptIR, MoCE-IR, AdaIR, PPFN) on the average across both HM-TIR and M³FD, with the largest margin over MoCE-IR (+1.59 dB PSNR on HM-TIR, +1.62 dB on M³FD). Against the TIR-specific PPFN, ECMRNet wins on 5 of 7 degradations on HM-TIR but loses on Noise (27.88 vs 29.41 dB) and CN (22.49 vs 22.83 dB), reflecting PPFN's stronger single-noise prior. All four continual-learning baselines (LwF, EWC, MAS, SI) are substantially outperformed; MAS in particular collapses on compound degradations (SSIM as low as 0.232 on CBN), confirming that regularization-based CL is inadequate for TIR's strong inter-task conflicts. In the appendix comparison (Table 4), ECMRNet also dominates the diffusion-based DA-CLIP (19.70 dB avg) and DiffUIR (19.69 dB avg) by over 10 dB, underscoring that visible-image-oriented restoration strategies transfer poorly to thermal infrared.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of all-in-one TIR restoration (PPFN, DEAL, TSIRIE) and continual learning, arguing that closed-set TIR methods lack structural support for adapting to novel degradations. It departs from regularization-based CL (LwF, EWC, SI, MAS) by replacing gradient-level protection with architectural parameter isolation via group convolutions, and from expansion-based CL (Progressive Networks, LIRA) by adding a compression step. The pruning mechanism is directly built on the 2D structural entropy framework (Li & Pan, 2016) and the CoDeSEG partitioning algorithm (Xian et al., 2025), repurposing an information-theoretic community-detection tool for channel-group redundancy assessment in a neural network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic treatment of open-world TIR restoration as a continual degradation learning problem, filling a gap where prior TIR methods assume a fixed degradation set and prior CL restoration targets only visible images. The group-isolated subspace design offers a practical recipe for interference-free incremental adaptation that could transfer to other sensor modalities or restoration domains. Key limitations include the assumption that degradation type is known at inference (requiring a separate recognizer), the task-incremental protocol that may not capture truly arbitrary degradation streams, and the synthetic-to-real generalization gap that remains partially unaddressed. Whether the 2D-SE pruning criterion generalizes beyond the U-Net group-convolution architecture, and whether the approach scales to very long task sequences (beyond the 9 tasks tested), remain open questions.
