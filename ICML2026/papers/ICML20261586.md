## Problem

Masked diffusion models (MDMs) for language generation depend critically on the unmasking order, yet prior approaches either hard-code a fixed ordering (e.g., blockwise left-to-right in BD3LM) or learn an ordering policy in a separate post-training stage, incurring extra cost and risking suboptimal solutions from two-stage optimization. The paper asks how to express diverse generation orders within a single MDM formulation and how to jointly learn the generation order and the diffusion backbone from scratch under one objective.

## Input / Output

At training time, the model consumes a clean token sequence x and a sampled time t, producing a position-dependent corruption schedule that determines which tokens are masked; at inference time, it starts from a fully masked sequence and iteratively unmaskes positions according to a learned, context-dependent priority. The scheduler for the forward process conditions on the full clean sequence x, while the reverse-time scheduler conditions on the current partially-masked state z_t, enabling the model to adapt its generation order to the available context at each denoising step.

## Method

The paper introduces OeMDM, a unified framework that generalizes the noise scheduler from a single position-invariant function to a free-form, position-dependent function class, yielding a generalized NELBO that decomposes into a velocity-weighted reconstruction loss and a velocity-matching loss between forward and reverse processes. Building on this, LoMDM parameterizes both the forward scheduler α_φ(x,t) and reverse scheduler α_ψ(z_t,t) as 1-t^{c1+c2·NormSig(g(f(·)))} over shared transformer features (with stop-gradient), and jointly optimizes θ, φ, and ψ by minimizing a single NELBO. Training uses a two-sample Rloo estimator to handle the gradient through the order-aware sampling distribution q_{α_φ}, requiring one extra forward pass of the backbone and two extra forward/backward passes of the lightweight scheduler heads per step. The framework provably subsumes standard MDMs, autoregressive models, BD3LM, and GenMD4 as special cases of the scheduler choice.

## Results

On LM1B (with sentence packing), LoMDM achieves a test PPL of 25.4, outperforming MDLM (27.0), BD3LM with L'=4 (28.2), and GenMD4 (26.9). On OpenWebText, LoMDM reaches PPL 20.4, beating MDLM (23.2) by 2.8 points and BD3LM (L'=4, 20.7) by 0.3 points. In zero-shot evaluation across 7 held-out datasets, LoMDM outperforms MDLM on all 7 and surpasses an autoregressive Transformer on 4/7. Additionally, LoMDM matches the 1M-step MDLM reference (PPL 23.0 on OWT) after only 0.18M steps (~18% of training tokens), corresponding to roughly 3× faster wall-clock convergence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoMDM outperforms all discrete diffusion baselines across every benchmark. Against MDLM (the closest absorbing-mask baseline), LoMDM improves test PPL by 1.6 points on LM1B (25.4 vs 27.0), 4.6 points on LM1B without packing (27.2 vs 31.8), and 2.8 points on OWT (20.4 vs 23.2). Against BD3LM, which injects a blockwise left-to-right bias, LoMDM wins even when BD3LM uses the smallest block size L'=4 (nearly autoregressive), achieving 25.4 vs 28.2 on LM1B and 20.4 vs 20.7 on OWT. Against GenMD4, which also learns a vocabulary-dependent scheduler, LoMDM is better on all three benchmarks (25.4 vs 26.9, 27.2 vs 30.0, 20.4 vs ≥21.8), and notably GenMD4's OWT number was obtained with twice the batch size (1024 vs 512). In zero-shot transfer (Table 2), LoMDM beats MDLM on all 7 datasets and surpasses the AR Transformer on 4/7 (PTB, WikiText, Lambada, ArXiv). In generative PPL (Table 3), LoMDM improves over MDLM at every NFE setting, e.g., 38.87 vs 42.56 at 1024 NFE, confirming the learned scheduler benefits inference-time generation quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the continuous-time MDLM formulation (Sahoo et al., 2024a), extending its position-invariant noise scheduler to a position-dependent, context-aware scheduler that explicitly encodes generation order. It departs from prior approaches that either hand-design orderings (BD3LM's blockwise L2R, GenMD4's vocabulary-dependent scheduler) or post-train a separate unmasking policy on a frozen backbone (Hong et al. 2025, Peng et al. 2025a), by jointly learning the scheduler and diffusion backbone from scratch under a single NELBO. The OeMDM framework also provides a unifying lens that recovers ARMs, standard MDLMs, BD3LM, and GenMD4 as special cases, and is conceptually parallel to LO-ARM (Wang et al., 2025) but operates within the masked diffusion paradigm rather than autoregressive factorization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified OeMDM framework gives practitioners a principled way to reason about how scheduler design affects both training signal and inference-time generation order, rather than treating ordering as an ad-hoc post-hoc choice. The 3× training speedup (matching 1M-step MDLM at ~180K steps) is practically significant for discrete diffusion language modeling, where training cost has been a barrier to scaling. Open questions include whether the learned coarse-to-fine order (structural words before content words) generalizes to conditional tasks like QA or summarization, and whether the approach scales to the multi-billion-parameter regime where large-scale MDMs (Dream 7B, LLaDA) currently lag behind ARMs.
