## Problem

Adversarial perturbations in zero-shot CLIP not only degrade accuracy but also suppress predictive uncertainty, causing the model to become spuriously overconfident on misclassified inputs. Prior zero-shot adversarial robustness methods align adversarial features to a single ground-truth text prototype or match softmax logits, which preserves neither the relative geometry among neighboring classes nor the absolute evidence strength, leaving a critical reliability gap in uncertainty calibration under attack.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR, 64×64 for TinyImageNet, 224×224 for natural-image benchmarks) and natural-language text prompts encoding candidate class labels. The image encoder (ViT-B/32 or B/16) and frozen text encoder map both modalities to a shared embedding space; the output is a Dirichlet distribution over classes from which the zero-shot prediction (argmax) and closed-form aleatoric/epistemic uncertainty scores are derived in a single forward pass, without sampling.

## Method

UCAT reparameterizes CLIP's image-to-text logits as Dirichlet concentration parameters via α_k = exp((τℓ_k + 1)/τ′), a mapping that guarantees α_k ≥ 1, preserves logit ordering, and exactly recovers CLIP's softmax when τ′ = τ. The training objective combines a text-guided cross-entropy loss (anchoring the adversarial image embedding to the ground-truth text prototype) with a reverse KL divergence between the adversarial and clean Dirichlet distributions, KL(Dir(α_adv)‖Dir(α)). The reverse KL is chosen for its mode-seeking behavior, which simultaneously aligns relative inter-class structure (aleatoric uncertainty) and absolute evidence magnitude (epistemic uncertainty) without the flattening effect of forward KL. Only the image encoder is adversarially fine-tuned on labeled data using ℓ∞-PGD perturbations, while the text encoder remains frozen to preserve the semantic anchor space for zero-shot transfer.

## Results

Across 16 single-label benchmarks (averaged), UCAT achieves 54.17% clean accuracy, 32.20% under PGD-100, 31.41% under CW-100, and 30.58% under AutoAttack (ε=1/255), with harmonic means of 40.39, 39.76, and 39.09 respectively. On the multi-label MS-COCO dataset, UCAT attains the best top-3 F1@3 of 37.04 at ε=1/255 and the highest clean-robust harmonic mean H(F1@3) of 39.40, 36.21, and 28.54 at ε=1/255, 2/255, and 4/255 respectively. Under the stronger 10-step PGD training regime (ε=2/255), UCAT maintains 22.45% average PGD-100 accuracy and 20.67% AutoAttack accuracy, again leading all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UCAT outperforms all six baselines on average robustness across PGD, CW, and AutoAttack. Against the closest competitors TGA-ZSR and Comp-TGA, UCAT gains +0.65 and +0.80 points on PGD-100, +0.13 and +0.25 on CW-100, and +0.06 and +4.34 on AutoAttack respectively, with the largest margin over Comp-TGA under AutoAttack (30.58 vs. 26.24). UCAT also leads PMG-AFT by +0.57 (PGD), +9.16 (CW), and +12.70 (AutoAttack), and surpasses TeCoA by +2.34, +2.16, and +1.84 on the three attacks. FARE, which preserves clean accuracy well (53.00), collapses under strong attacks (12.81 PGD, 2.33 AutoAttack), while UCAT maintains 32.20 and 30.58 respectively. On MS-COCO, UCAT achieves the best H(F1@3) at all three perturbation budgets (39.40, 36.21, 28.54), outperforming TGA-ZSR (35.38, 32.14, 25.14) and Comp-TGA (35.83, 32.79, 24.82). The only datasets where UCAT does not rank first are PCAM and EuroSAT, which exhibit high domain shift and semantic overlap that limit the effectiveness of Dirichlet alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of zero-shot adversarial robustness (ZSAR) for vision-language models and evidential deep learning. Prior ZSAR methods (TeCoA, FARE, PMG-AFT, TGA-ZSR) primarily align adversarial image features to a single ground-truth text prototype or match clean/adversarial softmax distributions, which the authors argue neglects both inter-class semantic geometry and absolute evidence strength. The key departure is a theoretically grounded reformulation of CLIP logits as Dirichlet concentration parameters (with exact equivalence to CLIP's softmax at a specific temperature), enabling a reverse-KL alignment between full Dirichlet distributions that simultaneously preserves relative class structure (aleatoric uncertainty) and calibrates evidence magnitude (epistemic uncertainty). The reverse-KL direction is borrowed from prior networks literature (Malinin & Gales, 2019) for its mode-seeking property, but its application to contrastive VLM fine-tuning is new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete reliability gap in zero-shot VLMs: adversarial perturbations paradoxically *suppress* predictive uncertainty, making attacked models more confident rather than less, which undermines any downstream use of confidence scores for rejection or OOD detection. The Dirichlet reformulation provides a principled, closed-form mechanism to address this without sampling, and the method generalizes across CLIP and SLIP backbones. Limitations include a clean-accuracy trade-off (UCAT sacrifices ~10 points of clean accuracy on some datasets relative to TGA-ZSR) and degraded performance on domain-shifted benchmarks (PCAM, EuroSAT) where CLIP's clean semantic geometry is already weak. Open questions include whether the calibration benefit persists under distribution shift beyond adversarial perturbations, and whether the approach extends to generative VLMs or multi-modal reasoning tasks where the single-image-to-text-prompt assumption breaks down.
