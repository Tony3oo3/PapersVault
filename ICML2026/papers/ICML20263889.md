## Problem

Vision-language alignment of pretrained unimodal encoders has relied on contrastive losses trained on millions of paired image-text samples, making it impractical in domains where paired annotations are scarce or expensive. This paper asks whether meaningful cross-modal alignment can be recovered from only a small number of paired samples (as few as 100) combined with large amounts of unpaired unimodal data, providing a direct empirical test of the Platonic Representation Hypothesis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263889/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes frozen embeddings from pretrained unimodal encoders (e.g., DINOv3 ViT-L for images, NV-Embed-v2 for text) and a small set of paired samples (10²–10⁵) plus up to 1M unpaired images and texts. It learns two linear projection layers (not nonlinear) mapping each modality into a shared 1024-dimensional space, chosen for robustness in the low-supervision regime. The output is a joint embedding space evaluated via zero-shot retrieval (T2I/I2T Recall@K) and zero-shot classification (top-1 accuracy) across multiple benchmarks.

## Method

SOTAlign is a two-stage framework: first, a linear teacher (CCA, Procrustes, or contrastive) is fit on the limited paired data to recover a coarse shared geometry; second, this teacher regularizes the training of the final alignment layers on unpaired data via the KLOT divergence, which compares entropic optimal-transport plans of the student and teacher affinity matrices. The key technical contribution is a closed-form gradient for KLOT (Theorem 5.1): ∇_K KLOT(K‖K*) = (OT_ε(K) − OT_ε*(K*))/ε, which eliminates the need for Sinkhorn unrolling or implicit differentiation and removes the memory bottleneck that limited prior OT-based alignment methods to small batch sizes. The training loss combines a supervised SigLIP term on paired data with the KLOT regularization on unpaired data, weighted by α. The entropic regularization parameters are set so that the teacher distribution is sharper (ε* < ε), encouraging the student to refine toward the teacher's geometry without overconstraining it.

## Results

With only 10k paired samples and 1M unpaired samples from CC3M, SOTAlign achieves 26.5% T2I R@1 and 34.1% I2T R@1 on COCO, outperforming the best semi-supervised baseline (NNCLR at 21.3/27.9) by +5.2/+6.2 points. On zero-shot ImageNet classification, SOTAlign reaches 46.1% top-1 accuracy, a +8.7 point gain over the strongest baseline (NNCLR at 37.4%). These results approach the supervised upper bound of SAIL trained on 1M pairs (35.5% T2I R@1, 56.4% ImageNet) using roughly 100× less paired supervision. On Flickr30k, SOTAlign reaches 51.7% T2I R@1 and 60.8% I2T R@1, surpassing all baselines by +4.9/+4.8 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SOTAlign decisively outperforms all five baselines across both retrieval and classification tasks. Against the primary supervised baseline SAIL (Zhang et al., 2025a), SOTAlign gains +5.5 T2I R@1 and +6.7 I2T R@1 on COCO, and +10.5 points on ImageNet top-1 accuracy, while using 100× fewer paired samples. STRUCTURE (Gröger et al., 2025) provides a marginal improvement over SAIL in the supervised setting (+1.3 I2T R@1) but fails to exploit unpaired data in the semi-supervised variant, remaining at 20.9/28.0 on COCO. NNCLR (Dwibedi et al., 2021), adapted via nearest-neighbor pseudo-labeling, is the strongest semi-supervised baseline but still trails SOTAlign by 5+ points on all metrics. S-CLIP (Mo et al., 2023), which uses OT for pseudo-labeling, performs worst among semi-supervised methods (20.4/27.8 on COCO), likely due to its limited pool of positives from the small paired set. In the per-dataset alignment setting of SUE (Yacobi et al., 2025), SOTAlign achieves gains of +14.3 I2T R@5 on COCO, +40.0 on Flickr30k, and +32.5 on Polyvore, demonstrating that the advantage is not an artifact of the cross-dataset protocol.

## Relation to Prior Work

SOTAlign extends the OT-based interpretation of contrastive learning introduced by OT-CLIP (Shi et al., 2024), which viewed InfoNCE as inverse optimal transport with a fixed identity plan, by allowing arbitrary target transport plans derived from a linear teacher. It also builds on the Platonic Representation Hypothesis (Huh et al., 2024) and the growing line of work on aligning frozen pretrained encoders (SAIL, Maniparambil et al., Huang et al.), but departs from prior semi-supervised approaches like S-CLIP and SUE by defining target similarities between unpaired images *and* unpaired captions simultaneously, rather than restricting pseudo-labels to the small paired set. The explicit closed-form gradient for the KLOT divergence (Theorem 5.1) resolves a computational bottleneck that had limited all prior OT-based alignment methods to small batch sizes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SOTAlign provides the first practical framework for leveraging large-scale unimodal data in multimodal alignment, reducing the paired-data requirement by roughly 4× while *improving* over fully supervised baselines—a meaningful shift for domains where paired annotations are expensive or infeasible. The explicit gradient for entropic OT divergences is a general result with broad applicability beyond this specific method, and the strong correlation (r=0.95) between mutual k-NN of frozen encoders and downstream alignment quality offers a cheap pre-screening diagnostic. Open questions include whether the approach can operate with zero paired samples (it currently fails at 100 pairs), how it scales to truly heterogeneous multi-modal settings beyond vision-language, and whether the linear-teacher bottleneck limits gains at higher supervision levels.
