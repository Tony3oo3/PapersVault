## Problem

Existing preference alignment for diffusion models relies on preference pairs constructed from model-generated images, where even 'preferred' samples often contain artifacts, limited realism, or stylistic biases. Because preference labels provide only relative supervision between two imperfect candidates, the model struggles to infer what constitutes a truly desirable output. The paper asks whether real images, which implicitly encode human judgments of visual quality through natural curation, can serve as an alternative supervision source that eliminates the need for manually annotated preference pairs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260486/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt as input and produces a 512×512 RGB image as output, fine-tuning Stable Diffusion v1.5 or SD-3.5-M via LoRA. Training data consists of 512 curated real professional photographs from HPDv3 (filtered by colorfulness and PickScore) paired with degraded counterparts created by regenerating saliency-masked regions using SD-v1.5 Inpainting. No human-annotated preference labels are required; the preference signal emerges entirely from the contrast between the real image and its locally perturbed version.

## Method

The paper proposes a two-stage alignment strategy that uses real images as implicit positive references. Stage 1 (distributional warm-up) employs the Diffusion-DRO objective to close the distribution gap between the model's generations and the real-image reference set, treating real images as soft anchors via a ranking-based reward model. Stage 2 (pairwise preference learning) applies Diffusion-DPO on constructed contrastive pairs where the positive sample is the original real image and the negative is a saliency-guided inpainted degradation (U²-Net masks salient regions, then SD-v1.5 Inpainting regenerates them, introducing localized artifacts in texture, structure, or semantics). Pairs with negligible degradation (PickScore gap < 0.02) are discarded to ensure clear contrastive signals. The key insight is that real-image-anchored contrastive learning provides more focused and interpretable supervision than pairwise comparisons between two imperfect generated samples, and the approach is complementary to existing preference-based methods, yielding consistent gains as a plug-in post-training step.

## Results

Using only 512 constructed preference pairs from real images, the method outperforms Diffusion-DPO (trained on 851k annotated pairs) on most metrics for both SD-1.5 and SD-3.5-M. On SD-1.5 (Pick-a-Pic v2), the method achieves HPSv3 7.33 vs. 6.80, DeQA 3.96 vs. 3.78, and ImageReward 0.38 vs. 0.33, while matching PickScore (21.04 vs. 21.03). On SD-3.5-M (DrawBench), it achieves HPSv3 12.77 vs. 10.79, ImageReward 1.08 vs. 0.97, and DeQA 4.26 vs. 3.96, with a slight loss on UnifiedReward (3.46 vs. 3.47). The method also generalizes to Parti-Prompts, maintaining consistent gains over the base model across all six metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Diffusion-DPO (Wallace et al., 2024), the proposed method uses 1/1664th of the training data (512 vs. 851k pairs) yet outperforms it on 5 of 6 metrics for SD-1.5 (HPSv3 +0.53, DeQA +0.18, ImageReward +0.05, UnifiedReward +0.03, Aesthetic +0.05) and 5 of 6 for SD-3.5-M (HPSv3 +1.98, ImageReward +0.11, PickScore +0.10, DeQA +0.30, Aesthetic +0.11), with only a marginal loss on UnifiedReward for SD-3.5-M (3.46 vs. 3.47). Against FlowGRPO (Liu et al., 2025) on SD-3.5-M, the paper demonstrates complementarity rather than direct replacement: applying real-data-based post-training on top of FlowGRPO yields additional gains across all evaluated metrics and alleviates the stylistic homogenization observed in FlowGRPO alone. Against SPO (Liang et al., 2025), a dedicated aesthetic alignment method, the paper reports that integrating real-data-based supervision with Diffusion-DPO achieves performance comparable to SPO, positioning the method as a lightweight plug-in that matches purpose-built aesthetic optimizers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Diffusion-DPO (Wallace et al., 2024) for its Stage 2 pairwise preference loss and on Diffusion-DRO (Wu et al., 2025) for its Stage 1 distributional warm-up objective, but departs from the broader preference-alignment literature by shifting the question from 'how to optimize preferences' to 'where to source preference signals.' Rather than constructing preference pairs from model-generated images (as in Diffusion-DPO, FlowGRPO, and reward-backpropagation methods), it treats curated real photographs as implicit positive references and generates negatives via saliency-guided inpainting, reframing alignment as distribution matching toward real data rather than relative ranking among imperfect samples. The paper explicitly positions this as complementary to, not a replacement for, existing preference-based pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that 512 constructed real-image pairs can match the performance of 851k manually annotated preference pairs, offering a dramatically cheaper and more scalable alignment signal that requires no human annotation. The method's demonstrated complementarity—stacking on top of Diffusion-DPO or FlowGRPO for additional gains—makes it a low-risk plug-in for existing pipelines. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video or multimodal generation, whether more principled (rather than heuristic) data curation is needed, and whether the reliance on a single curated photography source (HPDv3) limits generalization to non-photorealistic domains despite encouraging user-study results on anime and concept art.
