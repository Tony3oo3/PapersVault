## Problem

Deploying DNNs on Compute-In-Memory (CIM) hardware introduces hardware-induced weight perturbations from device mismatch, programming errors, and temporal drift that severely degrade inference accuracy. Existing robustness methods either optimize for worst-case weight perturbations (overly aggressive, causing under-emphasis of dominant noise directions) or inject simple forward noise (limited generalization to out-of-distribution perturbations). No prior method explicitly leverages cross-perturbation consensus to focus learning on the dominant recurring interference patterns that actually occur in edge deployment.

## Input / Output

The method accepts standard classification inputs—RGB images (F-MNIST 28×28, CIFAR-10 32×32, LFW), ECG signals, audio clips (UrbanSound8K), and Chinese text (iFLYTEK 119)—and outputs a class label. During training, each input batch is processed through n=10 independently noise-perturbed weight copies (multiplicative Gaussian noise at mismatch level ζ), producing n sets of logit vectors per sample. These perturbed logits are then used to compute pairwise cosine-similarity affinity scores that modulate the training loss, rather than being used for inference. At deployment, a single forward pass on the un-perturbed weights yields the final class prediction.

## Method

DWP is an immune-system-inspired training framework that emulates affinity-based selection to build robustness against weight perturbations. At each training iteration, a Noise Net generates n stochastic weight-perturbed instances via multiplicative Gaussian noise (Θ_i* = Θ ⊙ (1 + ζ·R_i)); for each instance, the model produces perturbed logits. A Similarity Calculation step computes pairwise cosine similarities between perturbed logit vectors across all n instances for every sample in the batch, yielding an affinity score S_i per perturbation instance that measures how consistently that instance's predictions align with the ensemble. These scores are min-max normalized into adaptive weights ω_i, and the total training objective becomes L_nat + Σ ω_i·L_i, so perturbation instances capturing dominant, recurring interference patterns receive higher gradient contribution. Theoretically, a second-order Taylor analysis shows this diversity-aware weighting implicitly regularizes the Hessian trace (reducing the curvature coefficient k by ~62% relative to baseline), steering optimization toward flatter loss regions and suppressing sensitivity to unstable perturbation directions.

## Results

Under severe weight perturbation (mismatch level ζ=0.7), DWP achieves 90.01% on F-MNIST, 65.61% on CIFAR-10 (ResNet), 97.23% on ECG, 39.70% on UrbanSound8K, 65.30% on CIFAR-10 (ViT), and 67.32% on CIFAR-10 (BiLSTM), representing absolute gains of 15–55% over the noise-free baseline. On the physical 4-bit CIM platform, the DWP-trained model maintains 82.93% accuracy (only 5.57% below the 32-bit GPU baseline of 88.50%) while reducing inference energy by 38% (29.97 mJ vs. 48.43 mJ). Over a simulated one-year CIM operation, DWP-trained models sustain >90% accuracy with only 2–4% degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DWP outperforms all four baselines across every dataset at the most severe mismatch level (ζ=0.7). The largest margins appear on CIFAR-10 (ResNet), where DWP reaches 65.61% while AWP (10.78%), AMP (11.32%), AWA (12.06%), and Forward Noise (22.57%) all collapse to near-chance levels. Forward Noise is the closest competitor on F-MNIST (81.49% vs. DWP's 90.01%) and ECG (92.75% vs. 97.23%), but still trails by 8.5 and 4.5 points respectively. AWP and AMP, which optimize worst-case weight perturbations, show the weakest robustness transfer, particularly on CIFAR-10 ViT (22.36% and 22.67% vs. DWP's 65.30%). AWA, which applies adversarial regularization in weight space, performs moderately on F-MNIST (71.40%) and ECG (92.87%) but degrades sharply on image and audio tasks. On the physical 4-bit CIM platform, DWP achieves 82.93% accuracy versus 71.45% (Forward Noise), 64.90% (AWA), 64.50% (AWP), and 56.90% (AMP), confirming its advantage under real hardware non-idealities.

## Relation to Prior Work

DWP carves new ground at the intersection of weight-space robustness training and biomimetic optimization. It departs from the three dominant prior lines—worst-case adversarial weight perturbation (AWP, AMP), uniform noise injection (Forward Noise), and adversarial regularization (AWA)—by replacing uniform or worst-case treatment of perturbations with a consensus-weighted selection mechanism: perturbation instances that produce predictions most aligned with the ensemble receive higher loss weight, analogous to affinity maturation in adaptive immunity. The theoretical analysis draws on Hessian-based flatness regularization (Zhang et al., 2023) to argue that this weighting implicitly suppresses high-curvature directions, though the authors explicitly frame this as an interpretive rather than rigorous proof. The multiplicative Gaussian noise model itself follows standard parameter-noise practice (Buchel et al., 2022), so the novelty lies in the selection and weighting rule rather than the perturbation model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical deployment results—38% inference energy reduction on a physical 2T2R CIM platform at 4-bit precision, and sustained >90% accuracy over a simulated year of memristor drift—address a genuine gap in edge-AI reliability that purely algorithmic robustness papers often overlook. However, several open questions remain: the multiplicative Gaussian noise is a controllable proxy rather than a calibrated device-level model, the training overhead is 3.2× baseline, and the Qwen3-1.7B experiment reveals a non-trivial clean-accuracy cost (58%→53.5%) that may be prohibitive in some settings. Whether the immune-system framing yields algorithmic insights beyond 'consensus-weighted perturbation ensemble' is unclear, and it remains to be seen if the approach generalizes to other hardware non-idealities (e.g., read noise, cross-talk) or to training-time co-design with specific CIM architectures.
