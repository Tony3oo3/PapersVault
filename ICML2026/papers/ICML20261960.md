## Problem

Large vision-language models achieve high symbol-level accuracy on handwritten mathematical expression recognition (HMER) yet systematically fail to recover two-dimensional structural layouts under realistic educational conditions, producing locally plausible but globally invalid transcriptions. Existing benchmarks (CROHME, HME100K, M²E) focus on single-line expressions and lack coverage of primary-school layouts, row-column alignment, and authentic writing artefacts, leaving it unclear which structural capabilities are missing and under what conditions they collapse.

## Input / Output

The input is a single RGB image of a handwritten mathematical expression drawn from real exam answer sheets, homework, and web sources, spanning 13,513 samples across 13 categories that include vertical arithmetic, short division, matrices, multi-line derivations, and artefact-corrupted expressions. The output is a LaTeX transcription enclosed in \boxed{}, with a custom arithstudio.sty macro package providing renderable environments (\begin{arith}, \begin{longdiv}, \begin{shortdiv}) for arithmetic layouts that standard LaTeX cannot natively represent. Evaluation uses ExpRate (exact string match) and ExpRate@CDM (rendering-based visual equivalence at Score=1.0), the latter being stricter and more diagnostic of structural fidelity.

## Method

The paper introduces SASR (Schema-Anchored Structure-Aware Reasoning), a training-free inference framework that reformulates HMER as a latent-variable problem P(Y|X) = Σ_z P(Y|X,z)·P(z|X), where z is a discrete structural schema (e.g., matrix, vertical arithmetic, multi-line derivation). The Multi-Stage Reasoning (MSR) component decomposes decoding into four sequential prompt stages—schema classification, schema-constrained transcription, context-driven symbol disambiguation, and final output—so that the inferred layout type explicitly restricts downstream grammar and alignment rules, reducing conditional entropy H(Y|X,z) ≤ H(Y|X). The Schema-Anchored In-Context Learning (SIL) component prepends a small set of (image, schema) anchor pairs to the prompt, acting as a prompt-induced structural prior that sharpens the schema posterior P(z|X) without any fine-tuning or parameter updates. Together, MSR and SIL address orthogonal uncertainties: SIL stabilizes schema selection while MSR enforces schema-consistent decoding, and the framework generalizes across model families (Qwen3-VL, Ovis2.5, Nemotron, Kimi-K2.5, GLM-4.6v) with no additional training data.

## Results

On HMER-Bench (13,513 samples, 13 categories), the proposed SASR framework applied to Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct achieves the best average ExpRate of 24.52%, more than doubling the baseline (11.63%) and surpassing the 235B-parameter Qwen3-VL-235B-Instruct (20.21%). Under the stricter ExpRate@CDM metric, Qwen3-VL-235B-Instruct leads at 34.45%, while SASR on the 8B model reaches 29.98% (vs. 25.84% baseline). Gains are most pronounced on structurally complex categories: SDE improves from 17.43% to 70.20%, MLD from 0.39% to 18.67%, and SOE from 27.63% to 42.72%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SASR on Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct (24.52% ExpRate) outperforms all 12 baselines, including the 29× larger Qwen3-VL-235B-Instruct (20.21%, +4.31 pts), the best OCR-tuned VLM HunyuanOCR (11.89%, +12.63 pts), the best HMER-specific model Uni-MuMER (11.48%, +13.04 pts), and the best MER-API SimpleTex (13.94%, +10.58 pts). The advantage is most dramatic on 2D-structure categories: SDE jumps from 17.43% (baseline) to 70.20% (SASR), approaching the 235B model's 78.83%, while MLD goes from 0.39% to 18.67% (vs. 1.17% for 235B). Under the stricter ExpRate@CDM metric (Table 3), Qwen3-VL-235B-Instruct retains the top spot at 34.45%, with SASR on 8B reaching 29.98% (vs. 25.84% baseline, +4.14 pts) and HunyuanOCR at 28.80%. Generalization experiments (Table 9) confirm SASR transfers across model families: +62.6% relative on Ovis2.5-8B, +48.3% on Nemotron-Nano-12B, +38.6% on Kimi-K2.5, and +81.8% on GLM-4.6v, with the largest absolute gains consistently on the D2 (2D structural) dimension.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VLM-based HMER line (HunyuanOCR, PaddleOCR-VL, Uni-MuMER, PosFormer) by reframing recognition as a structurally conditioned transcription problem with an explicit latent schema variable, rather than relying on training-time structural supervision or end-to-end sequence decoding. It departs from prior spatial-reasoning benchmarks for VLMs (What'sUp, SpatialEval, Mind the Gap) by grounding the structural-reasoning gap specifically in handwritten mathematics with a diagnostic taxonomy of 13 categories. The proposed SASR framework is conceptually distinct from training-centric approaches (Uni-MuMER's Tree-CoT, PosFormer's position forests) in that it operates entirely at inference time via multi-stage prompting and in-context structural anchors, requiring no parameter updates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that symbol-level accuracy (Score@CDM ~84%) far exceeds expression-level validity (ExpRate@CDM ~26%) across all evaluated models redirects the field's focus from improving visual perception to improving structural reasoning and global layout modeling in VLMs. HMER-Bench fills a clear gap left by CROHME, HME100K, and M²E, which lack coverage of primary-school 2D layouts (vertical arithmetic, short division), real-world writing artefacts, and fine-grained structural diagnostics. Open questions include whether the token overhead of SASR (~5.5× baseline) can be reduced, whether the arithstudio.sty annotation standard will be adopted broadly, and whether structural schema inference can be made more reliable for the still-challenging RCE and CME categories where performance remains near zero.
