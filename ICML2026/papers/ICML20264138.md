## Problem

Sparse-reward reinforcement learning is typically framed as an exploration problem, with methods focusing on curiosity-driven signals and hierarchical decomposition while treating reward functions as black boxes. This paper asks whether structural assumptions about the reward function itself—specifically, that it admits a low-rank plus sparse decomposition over the state-action space—can accelerate learning, and how to handle the Missing-Not-At-Random sampling bias that arises because agents only observe rewards where their current policy concentrates probability mass.

## Input / Output

PAMC takes a base RL agent (DrQ-v2, DreamerV3, MT-SAC, CQL, or PrefPPO) and operates on a discretized state-action space constructed by binning learned encoder embeddings into grids (64×64 for Atari, 32×16 for continuous control), producing a reward matrix R̂ ∈ R^{G_s × G_a}. It outputs completed reward estimates for unvisited state-action pairs, a gated reward signal that conditionally substitutes the base agent's intrinsic exploration reward when split-conformal confidence intervals exceed a threshold τ, and calibrated 95% coverage confidence intervals. The method runs as a plug-in module updated every K=5000 steps with ~8% computational overhead, requiring no architectural changes to the base agent.

## Method

PAMC models the reward matrix as R = L* + S* + E where the low-rank component L* is factorized through learned embeddings as φ(s)ᵀWψ(a) and the sparse component S* captures outlier entries, solved via a weighted factorized loss with ℓ₂ and ℓ₁ regularization. To correct policy-induced MNAR sampling bias, it applies self-normalized inverse propensity weighting (SNIPW) with weights w_sa = 1/max(p̂_sa, ε_p) normalized over the batch, where propensities p̂_sa are tracked via Laplace-smoothed exponential moving averages (β=0.99) that adapt to the evolving policy. A confidence-gated abstention mechanism computes split-conformal prediction intervals for each completed entry and falls back to the base agent's intrinsic exploration reward whenever the interval half-width exceeds threshold τ, ensuring graceful degradation rather than catastrophic failure when structural assumptions are violated. The theoretical analysis shows completion error scales as O(σ√(r(d_φ+d_ψ))/ESS) where ESS is the effective sample size under policy overlap κ, and establishes a phase transition from exponential to polynomial-in-rank sample complexity when the low-rank structure holds.

## Results

Under a matched 10M-step budget, PAMC achieves 1.42±0.06 human-normalized score on the 26-game Atari suite (vs. 1.25±0.05 for DrQ-v2), with the most dramatic gain on Montezuma's Revenge (4100±250 vs. 200±50, a 20× improvement). On MetaWorld-50, PAMC+MT-SAC reaches 78% success rate versus 65% for MT-SAC alone; on D4RL MuJoCo tasks, PAMC+CQL improves mean return by 5.9% over CQL; and on AntMaze offline-to-online transfer, PAMC+Cal-QL achieves 52.6% success versus 40.0% for Cal-QL (+31.6%). The method adds only 8% computational overhead and maintains 95% empirical CI coverage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PAMC outperforms all nine baselines across every domain tested. On Atari, PAMC+DrQ-v2 (1.51 HNS) surpasses DrQ-v2 (1.25), Agent57 (1.38), Go-Explore, and RND, with the largest gap on Montezuma's Revenge (4100 vs. 200 for DrQ-v2) and Gravitar (1120 vs. 450 for Go-Explore). On continuous control, PAMC+DreamerV3 (921) beats DreamerV3 alone (820) on DM Control and Walker-Walk (950 vs. 920). On multi-task robotics, PAMC+MT-SAC (78% success) exceeds MT-SAC (65%) on MetaWorld-50 and Pick-Place-Wall (0.85 vs. 0.65). In offline RL, PAMC+CQL improves mean D4RL return by 5.9% over CQL (115.3 vs. 109.0), and in offline-to-online transfer, PAMC+Cal-QL achieves 31.6% higher AntMaze success than Cal-QL alone (52.6% vs. 40.0%). In preference-based RL, PAMC+PrefPPO (0.91 accuracy) outperforms PrefPPO (0.82). All improvements are statistically significant (p<0.05 after Benjamini-Hochberg correction), and PAMC's advantage is largest in the low-data, sparse-reward regime, narrowing toward parity as baselines approach their asymptotic performance at 200M steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

PAMC sits at the intersection of three well-developed threads: classical matrix completion and robust PCA (Candès & Recht, 2009; Candès et al., 2011), inverse propensity weighting for MNAR data from recommender systems (Schnabel et al., 2016), and selective prediction via conformal calibration (Vovk et al., 2005). The closest prior RL work is Nagaraj et al. (2023), which connects low-rank reward structure to multi-user collaborative learning, but PAMC extends this to single-agent settings where the sampling bias is policy-induced rather than design-based. Crucially, PAMC imposes structure on the reward matrix R(s,a) rather than on the transition operator (as in low-rank MDPs, Agarwal et al. 2020a) or the value function (Shah et al. 2020), arguing that rewards are directly observable and stationary, making them a more natural target for completion. The method is explicitly positioned as complementary to, not a replacement for, exploration strategies like RND, Go-Explore, and Agent57.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that structural exploitation of reward functions can provide a 20× sample-efficiency gain on the hardest sparse-reward Atari task at 10M steps, suggesting that the field's near-exclusive focus on exploration may be leaving a complementary axis of improvement on the table. The confidence-gated abstention mechanism and the measurable applicability diagnostics (effective rank < 20, ESS > 400) give practitioners a concrete way to assess when the method will help versus when it will gracefully revert to baseline. Key open questions include whether the discretization of continuous state spaces into grids is a fundamental bottleneck, whether richer structural priors (smoothness, compositional, graph) can extend the method beyond the low-rank regime, and whether the gains persist as compute budgets grow and baselines eventually cover the state-action space. The proof-of-concept framing and the honest reporting of the narrow structural regime where gains appear temper the broader impact claims.
