## Problem

Computing the Gromov-Wasserstein distance incurs an O(n³) per-iteration cost from the gradient term D_s π D_t^T, which remains a major bottleneck for large-scale applications. Existing acceleration strategies either relax marginal constraints (sacrificing strict feasibility) or rely on low-dimensional approximations that require strong structural priors. The paper asks whether one can break the cubic per-iteration complexity while guaranteeing strict feasibility without any constraint relaxation.

## Input / Output

The method takes two intradomain distance matrices (D_s ∈ R^{n×n}, D_t ∈ R^{m×m}, typically symmetrically normalized unweighted adjacency matrices for graph alignment) and uniform marginal distributions μ, ν. It outputs an optimal coupling matrix π* ∈ R^{n×m} satisfying the transport polytope constraints, from which a discrete injective node correspondence mapping M* is extracted by selecting the maximum-weight edges. A key practical detail is that the algorithm requires a one-time full O(n³) initialization step to identify the active coordinate set, after which all subsequent iterations operate on a much smaller r×r sub-block with r ≪ n.

## Method

LoBCD-GW exploits a data-dependent sparsity property: under strict complementarity, the selected set of coupling entries undergoing significant updates shrinks to O(n) size after a finite number of iterations, since off-support entries decay geometrically. The algorithm restricts each KL-proximal mirror-descent update to this dynamically selected sub-block spanned by row indices I_k and column indices J_k, reducing the per-iteration cost from O(n³) to O(r³). A novel marginal compensation mechanism derives local marginals by subtracting the mass already assigned to stationary entries outside the sub-block, then applies a local Sinkhorn projection to guarantee that the globally embedded coupling strictly satisfies the transport polytope constraints. The convergence proof establishes sufficient decrease of the GW objective and shows that the global proximal gradient mapping vanishes, ensuring convergence to a stationary point.

## Results

LoBCD-GW achieves the highest graph alignment accuracy on all five benchmark datasets: 99.71% (Douban-230), 97.18% (Reddit), 100.0% (Synthetic), 99.03% (ACM-1000), and 97.30% (DBLP-1000). On large-scale graphs, it delivers up to a 5.8× speedup over baselines, with GPU runtimes of 0.27 s (Synthetic, 1500 nodes), 0.49 s (ACM-1000), and 0.52 s (DBLP-1000), while baselines like HOT and JOENA require 70–106 s on the same tasks. The method also reduces peak memory consumption by 4.9× at 5000 nodes compared to baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoBCD-GW outperforms all 20 baselines on alignment accuracy across all five graph datasets, with the largest margins over constraint-relaxation methods (eBPG, BPG, BPG-S) and low-rank approximation methods (LR, ScalaGW), which often drop below 50% accuracy on large-scale graphs. The closest competitor is KL-BAPG, which reaches 99.79% on Synthetic and 94.00% on ACM-1000 but is 15–30× slower (4.18 s vs. 0.27 s on Synthetic; 1.46 s vs. 0.49 s on ACM-1000). Among low-dimensional approximation methods, SpecGW is fast (0.50 s on ACM-1000) but sacrifices accuracy (93.73% vs. 99.03%), while HOT matches 100% on Synthetic yet requires 70.83 s versus LoBCD-GW's 0.27 s. On small-scale graphs (Douban-230, Reddit), the runtime advantage narrows since all methods complete in under a second, but LoBCD-GW still leads in accuracy by 23–97 percentage points. In the additional comparison (Table 6), LoBCD-GW also beats FRAM (96.01% vs. 99.71% on Douban-230) and ASM (90.77% vs. 99.71%), confirming its superiority over recent graph-matching methods beyond the GW family.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the block coordinate descent (BCD) paradigm from quadratic programming (Hsieh et al., 2008; Nutini et al., 2015) to Gromov-Wasserstein optimization, building on the entropic GW framework with KL-proximal mirror descent and Sinkhorn projection (Solomon et al., 2016; Cuturi, 2013). It departs from two dominant acceleration strategies in the GW literature: constraint relaxation methods (BPG, BPG-S, KL-BAPG) that sacrifice strict feasibility, and low-dimensional approximation methods (ScalaGW, SpecGW, LR, HOT) that rely on structural priors like spectral similarity or low-rank assumptions. Instead, LoBCD-GW exploits a data-dependent sparsity property of coupling updates—formalized via strict complementarity—to restrict computation to a shrinking selected set while a novel marginal compensation mechanism preserves global marginal constraints exactly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing per-iteration complexity from O(n³) to O(r³) with r ≪ n while guaranteeing strict feasibility, LoBCD-GW addresses a core scalability bottleneck in GW-based structured data alignment without the accuracy compromises inherent in relaxation or approximation approaches. The 5.8× speedup on large-scale graph benchmarks combined with SOTA accuracy (e.g., 99.03% on ACM-1000 vs. 94.00% for KL-BAPG) makes it a practical tool for graph alignment, partitioning, and single-cell multi-omics integration. Open questions include: whether the Candidate-Locality Assumption (underpinning the O(n) support bound) holds universally or requires dataset-specific tuning, whether the one-time O(n³) initialization step becomes a bottleneck for very large n, and whether the approach extends naturally to unbalanced or non-uniform marginal settings.
