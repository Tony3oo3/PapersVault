## Problem

Bandit learning in matching markets has been studied primarily under static preferences, but real-world platforms face dynamic, context-dependent utilities where subtle context shifts can reconfigure the stable matching benchmark and cause disproportionate regret spikes. Prior contextual approaches require prior knowledge of the minimum preference gap and covariance structure, and no tractable regret notion exists for adversarial contexts where the minimum utility difference can be arbitrarily small for arbitrarily many rounds.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262759/cropped_page8_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each round, the algorithm observes d-dimensional context vectors for all K arms (bounded in norm by B_x), uses known arm-side preference profiles over N players, and receives noisy subgaussian reward feedback only for matched pairs. The output is a player-to-arm matching per round that is stable (or ε-stable in small-gap regimes), with performance measured as per-player optimal stable regret against the time-varying player-optimal stable matching. The algorithms are fully adaptive: they require no prior knowledge of the minimum preference gap, context covariance structure, or number of environments.

## Method

For stochastic contexts, BARB operates in batches with a candidate gap Δ_k that shrinks by √2 each batch, iteratively approximating the true minimum preference gap. Within each batch, it alternates between exploration (maximum cardinality matching on a bipartite graph of player-arm pairs whose Mahalanobis norms exceed a threshold) and exploitation (Gale-Shapley with ridge-regression estimates), using a counter of overlapping confidence intervals to trigger batch transitions. For adversarial contexts, AdECO uses the same adaptive exploration but in exploitation chooses between Gale-Shapley (when confidence intervals are well-separated) and an α-approximation oracle for stable matching with ties (when intervals overlap), paired with a tractable regret definition that interpolates between the exact optimal stable share in large-gap rounds and an α-fraction of the ε-optimal stable share in small-gap rounds. Both algorithms leverage the elliptical potential lemma to bound exploration rounds and achieve instance-dependent poly-logarithmic regret (stochastic) or O(T^{2/3}) sublinear regret (adversarial) without any prior environmental knowledge.

## Results

For stochastic contexts, BARB achieves an instance-dependent regret bound of Reg_i(T) = O(log²T / Δ_min²) without requiring prior knowledge of the minimum preference gap or context covariance structure. Under the additional assumption of a uniformly bounded minimum eigenvalue λ̃, the bound improves to O(log T / (λ̃² Δ_min²)). In the asymptotic regime under a mild CDF regularity condition (Assumption 5), the paper establishes tight Õ(T^{2/3}) upper and lower bounds. For adversarial contexts, AdECO achieves an instance-independent O(T^{2/3}) regret bound via the α-approximate Δ-optimal stable regret with Δ = O(T^{-1/3}).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares BARB against two baselines in the stochastic setting: ETC (Li et al., 2022), which requires prior knowledge of the minimum preference gap and covariance structure, and Batched-ETC, a batched explore-then-commit variant that requires Assumption 4 (bounded minimum eigenvalue). In the primary experimental setting (N=4, K=4, d=3, T=200k, small minimum eigenvalue), BARB clearly outperforms both ETC and Batched-ETC in cumulative player-optimal stable regret across all four players. In a complementary setting with a large minimum eigenvalue (Appendix H), BARB achieves performance comparable to ETC. The key practical advantage of BARB is that its regret bound does not depend on the context covariance structure, making it robust across varying problem instances without tuning, whereas ETC's performance degrades when the covariance is ill-conditioned.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the bandit learning in matching markets literature (Liu et al., 2020; Li et al., 2022) from static preferences to linear contextual utilities, where player-arm utilities are inner products of latent preference vectors and observed arm contexts. The key departure from Li et al. (2022) is the removal of prior knowledge of the minimum preference gap and context covariance structure, replaced by a fully adaptive batched design (BARB) that shrinks a candidate gap toward the true value. For the adversarial context setting, the paper adapts the approximation oracle from Lin et al. (2026) on stable matching with ties, introducing a tractable regret notion that interpolates between exact and approximate benchmarks depending on the realized utility gap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The tight Θ(T^{2/3}) instance-independent regret bound (matching upper and lower) establishes a fundamental hardness result: contextual matching markets are provably harder than their static counterparts, where logarithmic regret is achievable. The fully adaptive design (no prior knowledge of gap or covariance) has direct practical relevance for online platforms where preference distributions are unknown and non-stationary. Open questions include extending the framework to non-linear utility models, many-to-one markets, and the decentralized setting (partially addressed in Section 6), as well as understanding whether the T^{2/3} rate is tight under weaker distributional assumptions than Assumption 5.
