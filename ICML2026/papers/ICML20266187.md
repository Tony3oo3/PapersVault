## Problem

Existing MLLM safety alignment methods rely on explicit-risk preference datasets and black-box reward models, which fail on implicit risks where individually benign images and text combine to produce harmful semantics. This creates two bottlenecks: data scarcity (no fine-grained annotations for implicit risks) and reward hacking (reward models trained via Bradley-Terry preferences cannot detect latent risks, causing models to either over-refuse or still produce unsafe outputs).

## Input / Output

The model receives a multimodal query consisting of an RGB image and a text prompt that are individually harmless but jointly pose a potential risk. The output is a structured sequence with two parts: a perceptual reasoning trace (wrapped in think tags) that explicitly identifies the latent risk and user intent, followed by a safe and helpful response (in answer tags). During training, a separate verification pass produces four continuous scores—perception safety, perception helpfulness, answer safety, and answer helpfulness—computed via logit-based reward softening over a discrete candidate set rather than hard-max token selection.

## Method

Meerkat-VL is a GRPO-based safety alignment framework with three components. First, the Meerkat-Safe dataset (8,436 samples) provides fine-grained annotations of implicit risks and user intent, constructed via a four-agent cyclic validation pipeline. Second, Normative Perceptual Self-Verification (NPSV) replaces black-box reward models: the model generates a rollout, then self-verifies both its reasoning and answer against ground-truth annotations and explicit scoring rules, yielding four dense, process-aware reward signals via logit-based softening. Third, Dual-Objective Perceptual Consistency Alignment (DPCA) introduces a consistency penalty C^i = max(0, s_as − s_ps) + max(0, s_ah − s_ph) that subtracts from the final reward whenever answer quality exceeds perception accuracy, mathematically forcing the gradient to redirect optimization toward genuine risk perception rather than superficially safe templates. Training proceeds in two stages: a lightweight SFT cold-start that teaches the model the reasoning-then-answer format and the verification task, followed by GRPO with the combined NPSV+DPCA reward.

## Results

On the Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone, Meerkat-VL achieves 94.44% average safety and 93.96% average helpfulness across five benchmarks, outperforming the strongest baseline SaFeR-VLM (78.28% safety, 81.03% helpfulness) by 16.2% and 12.9% respectively. The gains are most pronounced on implicit-risk benchmarks: 89.68% safety on SIUO and 88.33% on MSSBench, versus 61.68% and 51.67% for SaFeR-VLM, yielding a ~32% average safety improvement on implicit-risk tasks. On the Qwen2-VL-7B backbone, Meerkat-VL similarly leads with 84.68% average safety and 88.08% helpfulness. The method also reduces total training cost by 16.93% compared to conventional GRPO by eliminating separate reward model training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Meerkat-VL decisively outperforms all four baselines across every benchmark and both backbones. Against SaFeR-VLM (the strongest prior method), Meerkat-VL gains +16.2% average safety and +12.9% helpfulness on Qwen2.5-VL-7B, with the largest margins on implicit-risk tasks: +28.0% on SIUO safety and +36.7% on MSSBench safety. Safe RLHF-V, which trains separate reward models on 28K human preference samples, achieves only 62.75% average safety and 40.72% on SIUO, revealing that black-box reward models fail to capture implicit risks. SPA-VL (DPO-based) and MM-RLHF (preference-based) show minimal gains on implicit benchmarks (≤41% SIUO safety) and often sacrifice helpfulness (SPA-VL drops to 61.90% avg helpfulness). The ablation in Table 9 further confirms that the combination of NPSV and the Meerkat-Safe dataset is responsible for the jump from 70.66% (NPSV on Beavertails-V) to 89.68% (NPSV on Meerkat-Safe) on SIUO safety.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) as its RL backbone but departs fundamentally from prior MLLM safety alignment methods (SPA-VL, Safe RLHF-V, SaFeR-VLM) that rely on explicit-risk preference datasets and black-box Bradley-Terry reward models. The key conceptual shift is from result-oriented alignment (scoring final responses) to perception-oriented alignment (scoring the model's risk understanding before the response), drawing inspiration from Rule-Based Rewards (Mu et al., 2024) and Constitutional AI (Bai et al., 2022) to replace opaque reward models with transparent, rule-grounded self-verification. The DPCA mechanism further distinguishes this work by mathematically coupling safety rewards to perception accuracy, a constraint absent in prior preference-optimization approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The diagnostic finding that the bottleneck for implicit-risk safety is perception (not safety awareness) reframes the problem: models can follow safety rules once risks are made explicit, so the alignment target should be risk understanding rather than response patterns. The elimination of separate reward model training (16.93% cost reduction) and the introduction of the first fine-grained implicit-risk dataset (8,436 samples) make this practically impactful for the MLLM safety community. Open questions include whether the rule-based self-verification generalizes to multi-turn dialogues and long-tail risk categories not covered by the 50 subcategories, and whether the approach transfers to video or audio modalities.
