## Problem

Prior theoretical results on harmful overfitting in Sobolev spaces were restricted to the Hilbert space case p=2, relying on the linear structure of kernel methods, and covered only narrow ranges of smoothness parameters k. This paper asks whether the harmful overfitting phenomenon persists for general Sobolev spaces W^{k,p}(R^d) with arbitrary p ∈ [1,∞), where minimum-norm interpolation is no longer a linear problem, and for approximately norm-minimizing interpolators rather than exact minimizers.

## Input / Output

The framework takes n i.i.d. training pairs (x_i, y_i) drawn from a joint distribution μ on Ω × R (where Ω ⊂ R^d is bounded with C^1 boundary), along with Sobolev parameters k ∈ N and p ∈ [1,∞) satisfying kp > d. The output is a high-probability lower bound on the expected excess risk of any γ-ANM interpolator: E[ℓ(f_γ(x),y) − ℓ(f_Bayes(x),y)] ≥ C·γ^{−pd/(kp−d)}, where C depends only on k, d, p, μ, and ℓ but is independent of n. The bound applies uniformly over all interpolators whose Sobolev norm is within a factor γ of the minimum, not just the unique minimizer.

## Method

The proof is geometric and proceeds in three steps. First, an explicit interpolant is constructed by placing radially symmetric bump functions (with disjoint supports of radius δ_i/2 around each data point) scaled by the labels, yielding an upper bound ‖f*‖_{W^{k,p}}^p ≲ n^{kp/d} on the minimum-norm solution via McDiarmid's inequality applied to nearest-neighbor radii. Second, a subset B of Ω(ρn) training points is identified that are simultaneously noisy (conditional loss exceeds the Bayes optimum by at least σ) and well-separated (nearest-neighbor radius δ_i ≳ n^{−1/d}), with bounded label magnitudes. Third, Sobolev embedding (Corollary 4.5) forces any γ-ANM solution to vary by at most O(δ) within balls of radius C_6·γ^{−p/(kp−d)}·n^{−1/d} around each point in B, so the continuous regret function remains ≥ σ/2 on these neighborhoods. Aggregating over |B'| ≳ ρn such disjoint neighborhoods, each contributing volume Ω(n^{−1}), yields an n-independent lower bound on the integrated regret. The key geometric insight is that the neighborhood radius scaling exactly cancels the number of noisy points, and the disjointness of nearest-neighbor balls (via the kissing number bound) ensures the Sobolev norms over these regions sum to at most the global norm.

## Results

The main result (Theorem 3.7) establishes that for k ∈ (d/p, 1.5d/p) and any γ-ANM interpolator in W^{k,p}(ℝ^d), the expected excess risk over the Bayes-optimal predictor is bounded below by C·γ^(−pd/(kp−d)) with high probability, where C is independent of the sample size n. This means the generalization error does not vanish as n→∞, confirming harmful overfitting. Corollary 3.8 specializes this to squared loss with Gaussian heteroskedastic noise, yielding ‖f_γ − g‖²_{L²(μ)} ≥ C·γ^(−pd/(kp−d)). In the higher-order regime kp > 2d (Theorems 6.2–6.3), the paper shows the ANM class is rich enough to contain both benign interpolators (with error O(n^{−1+αd}) for α close to 1/(kp−d)) and harmful interpolators (with constant excess risk).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental baselines or empirical comparisons. The paper positions its contributions relative to prior theoretical results on harmful overfitting in Sobolev-type spaces. Compared to Rakhlin & Zhai (2019), which showed harmful overfitting for minimum-norm kernel regression in W^{k,2}(ℝ^d) with d odd and k=(d+1)/2, this paper extends to arbitrary p ∈ [1,∞) (not just the Hilbert space case p=2) and to the broader smoothness range k ∈ (d/p, 1.5d/p). Compared to Buchholz (2022), which covered W^{k,2}(ℝ^d) with d/2 < k < 3d/4, the present work handles general p and approximately norm-minimizing (ANM) interpolators rather than exact norm minimizers. The proof technique also differs: prior kernel-method results exploit the linear structure of Hilbert spaces, while this paper uses geometric arguments based on Sobolev inequalities and local oscillation bounds that work in the non-Hilbert (p≠2) setting where minimum-norm interpolation is no longer a linear problem.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on harmful overfitting in kernel/Sobolev regression from Rakhlin & Zhai (2019) and Buchholz (2022), which were restricted to the Hilbert space case p=2 and relied on the linear structure of RKHS. The key departure is working in general Sobolev spaces W^{k,p}(R^d) for arbitrary p∈[1,∞), where minimum-norm interpolation is no longer a linear problem, necessitating a geometric proof via local oscillation bounds (Sobolev inequalities) rather than spectral/kernel analysis. The paper also broadens the solution class from exact norm-minimizers to approximately norm-minimizing (ANM) interpolators, and handles general data distributions with heteroskedastic noise rather than just additive Gaussian noise.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result establishes that smoothness bias alone—formalized as Sobolev norm minimization—cannot rescue interpolation from harmful overfitting in fixed dimensions, even for approximately norm-minimizing solutions. This has practical implications: it suggests that undertraining or explicit regularization may be necessary when working with large datasets in fixed-dimensional settings, rather than relying on the implicit smoothness inductive bias of norm-minimizing algorithms. Open questions include the intermediate regime k∈[1.5d/p, 2d/p], extension to fractional Sobolev spaces, classification losses, and the transition between fixed-dimension harmful overfitting and high-dimension benign overfitting.
