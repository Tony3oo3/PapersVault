## Problem

Existing LLM-based Automatic Heuristic Design (AHD) pipelines optimize solver programs against fixed evaluation distributions, leading to overfitting, a performance ceiling once near-optimal, and poor generalization under distributional shift. No principled framework exists for co-adapting solvers and instance distributions within a unified optimization objective, leaving instance adaptation to hand-crafted curricula or ad hoc schedules outside the core search loop.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262067/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes combinatorial optimization problem instances (item sequences for online bin packing, 2D city coordinates for TSP, customer coordinates and demands for CVRP) and produces executable Python heuristic programs that plug into fixed solver procedures (e.g., a scoring function for online packing, an edge-distance update rule for Guided Local Search, or a priority function for greedy route construction). Generator programs output stochastic instance distributions parameterized by seed and problem size, while solver programs output a single feasible solution per instance. Evaluation uses a normalized reference gap against task-specific reference values (exact optima from Concorde/CP-SAT or high-quality heuristic solutions from PyVRP).

## Method

ASRO reframes heuristic discovery as a two-player zero-sum game over executable programs, extending Policy Space Response Oracles (PSRO) from parametric policy spaces to discrete program space. At each iteration, it (1) evaluates all solver–generator pairs to construct a payoff matrix of expected normalized gaps, (2) computes mixed meta-strategies by solving the finite minimax equilibrium, and (3) invokes LLM-based approximate best-response oracles to synthesize new solver or generator programs conditioned on the opponent's meta-strategy. The best-response oracle is instantiated via an EoH-style bounded evolutionary search using structured LLM operators (diverse exploration, recombination, semantic refinement, parameterized adjustment, simplification) over a population of thought–code program pairs. Newly discovered programs are added to persistent strategy pools, inducing a self-generated curriculum where generators adaptively expose solver weaknesses and solvers internalize defenses in program space, replacing static evaluation with sustained adversarial co-evolution.

## Results

Across three combinatorial optimization domains, ASRO-EoH consistently outperforms the EoH baseline (same LLM search mechanism, static evaluation) on all benchmarks. On TSP, ASRO-EoH achieves 0.21% gap on small TSPLIB instances (LIB-S) versus 0.77% for EoH, and 3.00% on large instances (LIB-XL) versus 4.20%, with the gap widening as problem size increases. On OBP, the improvement is most dramatic on the hardest benchmark (Hard28-SA): ASRO-EoH reduces the gap from 40.02% (EoH, Best Fit, First Fit) to 12.45%. On CVRP, ASRO-EoH achieves the lowest gap across all seven CVRPLib families, e.g., 28.71% on family F versus 59.78% for EoH.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ASRO-EoH outperforms the EoH baseline (identical evolutionary program search, but trained on a fixed distribution) across all three CO domains and all benchmark families, with the margin growing on harder/larger instances (e.g., TSP LIB-XL: 3.00% vs 4.20%; OBP Hard28-SA: 12.45% vs 40.02%; CVRP family F: 28.71% vs 59.78%). Against classical heuristics, ASRO dominates by wide margins: on TSP it beats Farthest Insertion (FI) by 7.09–12.19 percentage points across sizes, and on CVRP it beats NN+2-opt by 11–28 points and Parallel Insertion by 11–73 points depending on the instance family. In the oracle-agnosticity check, ASRO-ReEvo (using a ReEvo-style search instead of EoH) still outperforms vanilla ReEvo on all TSP benchmarks (e.g., 0.30% vs 0.38% on LIB-S; 0.07% vs 0.38% on Uniform), confirming that the game-theoretic structure—not the specific search mechanism—drives the gains. In the CVRP transfer experiment, ASRO-discovered heuristics improve PyVRP (a strong off-the-shelf solver) by 0.3–2.3 points across all CVRPLib families, and also outperform KGLS on most families, showing that ASRO's gains are complementary to stronger solver procedures.

## Relation to Prior Work

ASRO extends Policy Space Response Oracles (PSRO) from parametric neural policy spaces to discrete program space, where strategies are executable solver and instance-generator programs synthesized by LLMs. It builds directly on EoH's evolutionary program search as its best-response oracle, but wraps it in a two-player zero-sum meta-game with persistent strategy pools, departing from the static-evaluation paradigm shared by FunSearch, EoH, AlphaEvolve, and ReEvo. The closest prior work, EALG, introduces a GAN-style solver-generator loop but lacks persistent strategy sets and equilibrium-based meta-strategies, making its dynamics more prone to cycling; ASRO addresses this gap with a principled PSRO-style solve-and-expand structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By replacing fixed evaluation distributions with an adaptive, self-generated curriculum driven by solver-generator co-evolution, ASRO offers a principled path toward robust heuristics that generalize under distributional shift—a persistent weakness of current LLM-AHD pipelines. The oracle-agnostic design means the framework can be paired with any program search mechanism (EoH, ReEvo, or future methods), making it a reusable optimization scaffold rather than a monolithic system. Key open questions include extending beyond zero-sum to multi-objective or regularized meta-games, handling the estimation noise from approximate reference values when exact optima are unavailable, and whether the co-evolutionary structure transfers to non-CO domains such as algorithmic reasoning or symbolic planning.
