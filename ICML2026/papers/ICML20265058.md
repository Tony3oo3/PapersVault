## Problem

Current LLM agents treat each task as an isolated episode, suffering from operational amnesia: they cannot systematically extract reusable strategic principles from their own past successes and failures, nor update their policy to internalize what they have learned. Existing approaches either store raw trajectories without abstraction, rely on an external teacher model for distillation, or remain stateless prompt-based methods that prevent long-term accumulation of strategic knowledge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265058/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives a text question and operates within a multi-turn deliberative loop, issuing three action types: <search_experience> (retrieves top-3 scored principles from an internal experience base via BGE-M3 embedding similarity), <search_knowledge> (queries an external search engine for top-3 documents), and <answer> (outputs the final answer). Beyond the answer, the system produces two additional outputs: distilled strategic principles (a one-sentence natural language description paired with structured subject-predicate-object triples) that populate the experience base, and updated policy parameters from GRPO. Retrieved experience and knowledge tokens are masked during backpropagation, so the model learns to use them as context rather than memorize their content.

## Method

EvolveR implements a closed-loop experience lifecycle with three alternating phases. In the offline self-distillation phase, the agent's own frozen policy model analyzes past trajectories and distills guiding principles (from successes) or cautionary principles (from failures), each represented as a natural language statement plus structured triples; new principles undergo two-stage deduplication (embedding similarity pre-filter at θ=0.85 followed by LLM binary equivalence judgment) and are scored via a Laplace-smoothed success/usage ratio, with periodic pruning below θ=0.3. In the online interaction phase, the agent retrieves top-3 principles to condition its reasoning, generating experience-guided trajectories. The loop is closed by GRPO policy optimization using a composite reward (binary exact-match outcome reward weighted 1.0 plus a format reward weighted 0.1 that rewards balanced think/search action counts), where the advantage signal explicitly reinforces the connection between retrieving high-quality principles and producing successful outcomes. A key finding is that at the 3B scale, self-distillation outperforms distillation by a stronger external teacher (GPT-4o-mini), attributed to cognitive alignment between the distiller and the policy being updated.

## Results

On the Qwen2.5-3B model, EvolveR achieves an average Exact Match (EM) of 0.382 across seven QA benchmarks, outperforming the strongest RL baseline Search-R1-instruct (0.325) by 5.7 points and the best prompting method RAG (0.270) by 11.2 points. On the Qwen2.5-7B model, EvolveR reaches 0.417 average EM, surpassing Search-R1-instruct (0.385) by 3.2 points and Rejection Sampling (0.348) by 6.9 points. EvolveR secures the best score on in-domain NQ (0.434/0.462), out-of-domain PopQA (0.434/0.473), and the adversarial Bamboogle dataset (0.328/0.392) at both scales, while remaining highly competitive on all other benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EvolveR outperforms all 11 baselines on average EM at both the 3B and 7B scales. Against the strongest RL competitor Search-R1-instruct, EvolveR gains +5.7 points (0.382 vs. 0.325) at 3B and +3.2 points (0.417 vs. 0.385) at 7B, with the largest per-benchmark margins on HotpotQA (+4.9/+4.1) and Bamboogle (+6.4/+2.4). Compared to the best prompting-based method RAG, EvolveR improves by +11.2 points at 3B and +11.3 points at 7B, showing that experience-driven self-distillation substantially exceeds static retrieval. Against DeepSeek-R1 (R1-instruct), EvolveR leads by +15.8 points at 3B and +14.6 points at 7B, confirming that the closed-loop experience lifecycle provides gains beyond trajectory-level RL alone. The only benchmark where EvolveR does not take the top spot is 2WikiMultiHopQA at 7B, where Search-R1-instruct edges ahead (0.414 vs. 0.395), though the gap is narrow. On TriviaQA at 7B, Search-R1-instruct also slightly leads (0.610 vs. 0.620 for EvolveR), making EvolveR the best on 6 of 7 benchmarks at that scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

EvolveR extends the RL-trained search agent paradigm established by Search-R1 and DeepSeek-R1 by adding a closed-loop experience lifecycle: the agent self-distills its own interaction trajectories into a curated base of strategic principles, retrieves them during inference, and uses GRPO to evolve its policy conditioned on that experience. This departs from prior memory-augmented agents (Reflexion, ExpeL) that either store raw trajectories or rely on an external teacher LLM for reflection, and from RAG-style approaches that address factual knowledge gaps without enabling strategic self-improvement. The GRPO optimizer from DeepSeekMath provides the policy update mechanism, while the agent's action space (think, search_knowledge, search_experience) follows the ReAct-style deliberative loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a concrete, self-contained blueprint for agents that iteratively refine their problem-solving strategies from their own successes and failures, moving beyond stateless or externally-scribed learning. A key open question is whether the experience-driven lifecycle generalizes beyond QA to open-ended, multi-step agentic tasks where reward signals are sparser and principle abstraction is less well-defined. The paper also raises safety considerations around autonomous policy evolution and leaves the question of principle internalization (vs. retrieval) unresolved, noting that naive gradient unmasking of experience tokens actually degrades performance.
