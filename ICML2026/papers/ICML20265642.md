## Problem

Existing MCR²-motivated architectures (ReduNet, CRATE, ToST) rely on approximations—softmax-based attention, diagonal-only statistics, or shared projection matrices—that deviate from the principled operations implied by the MCR² gradient, which requires feature updates guided by the intrinsic low-dimensional subspace structure. These approximations lead to objective misalignment (e.g., MSSA can increase rather than decrease the compression term), limited fine-grained structural capture, lack of self-adaptive expansion-compression balancing, and poor scalability (quadratic complexity in CRATE, training instability in ToST at larger scales).

## Input / Output

Input is a 224×224 RGB image divided into 16×16 patches, yielding 196 tokens of dimension d (192–1024 depending on model scale). Rather than inserting a [CLS] token per layer (which would not belong to any learned subspace), the model appends a global class attention layer at the end of the architecture for classification. Pre-training is supervised on ImageNet-1K for 500 epochs; downstream evaluation uses fine-tuning on CIFAR-10/100, Oxford Flowers-102, and Oxford-IIIT-Pets, with direct evaluation on ImageNet and ImageNet ReaL.

## Method

PACEAttention derives feature updates from the geometric structure of the MCR² gradient: for a PSD matrix ZZ^T, increasing log det(I+ZZ^T) requires enlarging eigenvalues in the null space (expansion) or driving non-zero eigenvalues toward zero (compression). Concretely, the expansion module adds the null-space projection (I−QQ^T)z to each feature, while the compression module subtracts the null-space projection of each token's code α_k=U_k^T z within K learned subspaces, weighted by soft membership π computed from column-space projection norms. The column-space basis Q is obtained efficiently via randomization (multiplying by a random matrix Ω of fixed rank r) followed by Cholesky decomposition of the resulting Gram matrix, yielding linear O(n) time complexity. Two learnable scalar weights α and β (regularized with softplus for non-negativity) provide per-layer self-adaptive balancing between expansion and compression, with early layers naturally emphasizing expansion when representative dimensions are limited.

## Results

PACENet-S (7.2M parameters, linear complexity) achieves 79.0% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet ReaL, surpassing CRATE-L (77.6M parameters, quadratic complexity) at 77.4% with roughly 10× fewer parameters. On ImageNet-1K, PACENet-S/M/B+ reach 67.9%/73.1%/75.6% top-1 accuracy, while on CIFAR-10 the corresponding values are 94.1%/95.2%/96.2%. The largest model, PACENet-B+ (57.7M), attains 84.6% on ImageNet ReaL and 99.5% on Oxford Flowers-102 after fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CRATE, PACENet-S (7.2M, linear complexity) outperforms CRATE-L (77.6M, quadratic complexity) on ImageNet ReaL (79.0% vs. 77.4%) and Oxford-IIIT-Pets (92.6% vs. 87.4%), while CRATE retains an edge on ImageNet-1K (71.3% vs. 67.9%) and CIFAR-100 (83.6% vs. 77.3%). Against ToST, the comparison is nuanced: ToST w/ MLP generally achieves higher accuracy at comparable parameter budgets (e.g., ToST-S 77.5% vs. PACENet-M 73.1% on ImageNet), but the authors show that removing ToST's MLP layers (which account for ~2/3 of its parameters) causes training to become numerically unstable and unable to scale beyond small model sizes, whereas PACENet scales stably. At the smallest scale, PACENet-S (7.2M) beats ToST-T (5.8M) on ImageNet (67.9% vs. 64.9%), ImageNet ReaL (79.0% vs. 76.8%), and Oxford Flowers-102 (75.5% vs. 49.1%). In the appendix comparison with CBT (Wen et al., 2026), PACENet-B (21M) and PACENet-B+ (57.7M) surpass CBT-B (25.7M) and CBT-L (83.1M) on ImageNet-1K (74.8%/75.5% vs. 73.4%/74.4%), though CBT leads at the tiny/small scales.

## Relation to Prior Work

PACEAttention sits within the ReduNet/CRATE/ToST lineage of white-box architectures derived from the MCR² objective, but departs from them by replacing softmax-normalized pairwise similarities (MSSA) and diagonal-only statistics (TSSA) with null-space projections guided by the geometric structure of the MCR² gradient. It inherits the K learnable subspace matrices U_[K] from CRATE and the linear-complexity ambition from ToST, but introduces randomization-based column-space estimation (via Cholesky decomposition) as the core mechanism for identifying the low-dimensional structure that should drive feature updates. The paper explicitly frames prior methods as failing to 'fully adhere to design principles implied by the MCR² gradient,' positioning PACEAttention as a more faithful realization of the original objective's geometric intent.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a more principled and interpretable attention mechanism with linear complexity, and the automatic emergence of fine-grained object-part segmentation across heads (without any segmentation supervision) is a compelling demonstration of the geometric design. However, the practical impact is tempered by the fact that PACENet does not match ToST w/ MLP at larger parameter budgets (75.6% vs 79.6% on ImageNet), and the hyperparameter r still requires manual tuning. Open questions include whether the null-space projection framework extends to dense prediction tasks, whether automatic capacity control can replace the fixed-r design, and whether the interpretability advantage persists under more complex training regimes.
