## Problem

Finding optimal data mixture ratios for LLM pre-training requires balancing general language ability with domain-specific skills (math, code), but existing automated methods (RegMix, CLIMB) rely on tiny-scale proxy experiments that fail to generalize to complex tasks, while large-scale proxy experiments are prohibitively expensive. The paper identifies a fundamental trade-off: no prior method simultaneously achieves sufficiency (unlimited proxy evaluations), accuracy (faithful ranking of mixtures), and efficiency (fixed computational budget).

## Input / Output

The method takes N component model parameter vectors (each trained on one candidate dataset from a shared base model) and a set of mixture weight ratios sampled uniformly from the N-simplex, producing merged proxy models via weighted linear combination of parameters. The output is a final optimal mixture ratio vector (continuous weights summing to 1) plus the DeMix Corpora, a 22T-token pre-training dataset with validated stage-wise mixtures across 7 candidate categories. A key constraint is that each component model is mixed with 50% general data during its training, so the searchable subspace is limited to non-general data proportions below 50%.

## Method

DeMix exploits the small-update regime (δ ≈ 10%) where the arithmetic sum of weight deltas from separately trained models approximates the delta from training on their union, enabling weighted linear merging of component models to serve as a training-free proxy for any data mixture. The pipeline trains N component models once at scale (30B tokens each on individual candidate datasets from a shared 50B-token base), then constructs unlimited proxy models by merging at sampled ratios and evaluating them on a 9-benchmark suite (general, code, math) to obtain average ranking scores. A LightGBM regressor is trained on (mixture weights, ranking score) pairs and used iteratively over three rounds (64+32+16 samples) to refine predictions toward high-performing regions of the simplex. The final optimal mixture is the average of the top-128 predicted candidates. This decouples search cost from training cost: the fixed pre-cost of N component models is amortized over arbitrarily many proxy evaluations, achieving comparable proxy accuracy to 12B-token trained proxies at roughly 6× lower total budget.

## Results

DeMix with 224 merged proxies achieves the best macro-averaged rank of 24.00 across general, code, and math benchmarks, outperforming RegMix (best: 28.00) and CLIMB (best: 27.67) under comparable or lower training budgets. In proxy consistency, DeMix (30B component models, 112 proxies) reaches a macro-averaged Spearman's ρ of 0.81 and capability recovery of 0.83, matching the 12B-trained proxy (ρ=0.82) at roughly 6× lower total cost (211B vs. 1344B tokens). On Qwen3-8B, DeMix's mixture ratios transfer well, achieving a best average rank of 27.67 (448 proxies) versus 30.00 for CLIMB and 34.00 for RegMix.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RegMix, DeMix achieves a macro-averaged rank of 24.00 (224 proxies, 212B budget) versus RegMix's best of 28.00 (56×8B proxies, 448B budget), a 4-rank improvement at roughly half the training cost. Against CLIMB, DeMix's 24.00 outperforms CLIMB's best of 27.67 (56×8B, 448B), again at lower cost. In proxy accuracy (Table 2), DeMix with 30B component models (ρ=0.81 macro, 0.59 top-25%) matches the 12B-trained proxy (ρ=0.82, 0.57) while requiring only 211B total tokens versus 1344B—a 6.4× reduction. For the released dataset, DeMix Corpora achieves an average rank of 24.00 after 50B-token mid-training, outperforming SmolLM-Corpus (31.33) and Nemotron-Pretrain (36.00) in Table 9, with the advantage driven primarily by superior math and code capability while maintaining competitive general performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the data mixture optimization line initiated by RegMix and CLIMB, which both rely on training numerous tiny-scale proxy models to fit a regression predictor over mixture ratios. The key departure is replacing per-mixture proxy training with weighted model merging of a fixed set of component models, grounded in the small-update regime result (δ ≪ 1) from Wu et al. (2025) and Lin et al. (2025b) that weight deltas from separate training runs approximate the delta from joint training. The concurrent MergeMix (Wang et al., 2026) applies a similar merging idea to mid-training, while DeMix targets the full pre-training setting and adds an iterative LightGBM-based search loop inherited from CLIMB's sampling structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By decoupling mixture search from proxy training, DeMix enables evaluating an effectively unlimited number of candidate mixtures at a fixed compute budget, breaking the traditional trade-off between proxy count, proxy accuracy, and total cost. The released 22T-token DeMix Corpora with validated multi-stage mixtures addresses a practical gap in open pre-training resources. Limitations include reliance on the small-update assumption (δ ≈ 10%), reduced effectiveness under finer data partitions (15 categories vs. 7), and validation only at 1.7B and 8B scale; whether the approximation holds at frontier model sizes or with highly non-linear dataset interactions remains open.
