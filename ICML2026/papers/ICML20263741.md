## Problem

Bursty online LLM traffic forces providers to provision for peak demand, leaving over 70% of GPU capacity idle during off-peak periods. While co-serving latency-tolerant offline batch inference with online requests is a natural remedy, existing systems manage resources at coarse granularity (fixed-size chunks, uninterruptible iterations, whole-request KV caches), causing severe interference that inflates online tail latency by 3–4× and cuts offline throughput by 4× relative to optimal.

## Input / Output

ConServe ingests two request streams into a single vLLM-based inference engine: latency-critical online prompts (e.g., chat queries with P99 TTFT/TBT SLOs) arriving via a real-time streaming API, and latency-tolerant offline batch prompts (e.g., summarization or benchmarking tasks) arriving via a batch API. Both streams share the same loaded model weights; online requests are prioritized and must meet per-token latency bounds, while offline requests are best-effort and optimized purely for aggregate token throughput. The system outputs generated text tokens for both streams, with online tokens streamed back in real time and offline tokens collected asynchronously.

## Method

ConServe operates at sub-request and sub-iteration granularity across three layers of the serving stack. First, an SLO-aware scheduler uses a context-aware latency model—fitting five coefficients via polynomial regression over compute tokens P and pre-existing context tokens C—to dynamically admit offline tokens at the token level each iteration, ensuring the composed batch satisfies the online TBT SLO. Second, layer-wise preemption inserts lightweight safepoints between transformer layers (checked via a single pinned-memory read plus an NCCL broadcast for tensor-parallel compatibility), allowing the worker to abort offline computation mid-iteration within ~20 µs when a new online request would otherwise miss its TTFT budget. Third, incremental KV cache checkpointing exploits the append-only nature of the cache to asynchronously save only the single newly-generated token's KV state after each iteration on a separate CUDA stream, decoupling preemption cost from sequence length and eliminating the recompute-vs-swap dilemma of monolithic eviction.

## Results

On synthetic workloads (Llama-3.1 8B, H100), ConServe reduces P99 online TTFT by 2.37× and P99 TBT by 2.72× while increasing offline throughput by 3.00× compared to the best preemptive baseline (Sarathi-P). On real-world workloads (Llama-3.1 70B, 4×H100), ConServe achieves 1553 tok/s offline throughput (78% of the Non-Preemptive upper bound) while keeping P99 TTFT at 1.14 s and P99 TBT at 201 ms, closely matching the Online-Only baseline (1.08 s / 184 ms). Across all four real-world offline datasets, ConServe reduces P99 TTFT by 2.04× and P99 TBT by 2.86× on average, while improving offline throughput by 2.23× over the best baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ConServe is compared against four baselines: Online-Only (vLLM, optimal latency but zero offline throughput), Non-Preemptive (upper bound on offline throughput but severe online latency degradation), Sarathi-Preemptive (chunked-prefill with priority preemption), and DistServe-Preemptive (prefill-decode disaggregation with preemption). Against Online-Only, ConServe matches P99 TTFT within 6% (1.14 s vs 1.08 s) and P99 TBT within 9% (201 ms vs 184 ms) on the Mixed workload while delivering 1553 tok/s offline throughput. Against Sarathi-P, ConServe reduces P99 TTFT from 2.91 s to 1.14 s (2.6×) and P99 TBT from 441 ms to 201 ms (2.2×) on Mixed, while nearly doubling offline throughput (1553 vs 895 tok/s); the gap widens to 7.48× on synthetic workloads at high load. Against DistServe-P, ConServe is dramatically better on all metrics: TTFT 1.14 s vs 12.4 s, TBT 201 ms vs 1398 ms, and throughput 1553 vs 26 tok/s, as DistServe-P suffers from severe load imbalance and back-pressure between prefill and decode instances. Non-Preemptive achieves the highest offline throughput (1995 tok/s) but at the cost of 15.2 s P99 TTFT, making it impractical for any latency-sensitive deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

ConServe builds directly on vLLM's PagedAttention memory management and Sarathi-Serve's chunked-prefill scheduling, but departs fundamentally from prior co-serving work (e.g., AntMan, SpotServe, FlexLLM) that co-locates inference with training or fine-tuning in separate processes or containers. Where those systems incur seconds-to-minutes task-switching overheads, ConServe operates within a single inference engine at sub-request and sub-iteration granularity—token-level scheduling, layer-wise preemption, and incremental KV cache checkpointing—eliminating process boundaries entirely. The closest concurrent work, HyGen, also targets online-offline co-serving but remains at iteration granularity and does not address the KV cache reclamation problem that ConServe solves with incremental checkpointing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

With GPU clusters provisioned for peak load and over 70% idle during moderate traffic, ConServe's 2.2× offline throughput gain at near-optimal online latency translates directly into substantial infrastructure cost savings for LLM providers already offering batch APIs. The techniques are practical and implementable (built on vLLM, <4% model prediction error, <10μs safepoint overhead), making them likely to be adopted in production serving stacks. Open questions include scalability to multi-node clusters, interaction with emerging optimizations like speculative decoding and quantization, and whether the 5-parameter latency model generalizes across diverse hardware without re-profiling. The evaluation is limited to single-node (up to 4 H100s), leaving multi-node tensor-parallel and pipeline-parallel settings untested.
