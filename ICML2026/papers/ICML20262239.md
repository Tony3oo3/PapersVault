## Problem

Geodesic regression models relationships between scalar predictors and manifold-valued responses, but its learned parameters (footpoint and shooting vector) encode sensitive individual data and have no existing differential privacy guarantees. While DP has been extended to manifolds for point estimation (e.g., Fréchet mean), no prior work provides private estimation of regression parameters on Riemannian manifolds, leaving a gap for applications in medical imaging and computer vision where data naturally live on curved spaces.

## Input / Output

The method takes a dataset of n pairs (x_i, y_i) where x_i ∈ [0,1] is a real-valued predictor (affine-scaled so some x_k = 0 and x_k' = 1) and y_i lies on a Riemannian manifold M, along with separate privacy budgets ε_p and ε_v for the footpoint and shooting vector. The data must be bounded within a geodesic ball B_{τ_m}(m_0) and the regression fit must be τ-close (all residuals bounded by τ). The output is a private footpoint p̃ ∈ M and a private shooting vector ṽ ∈ T_{p̃}M, which together define a differentially private geodesic curve with total privacy budget ε = ε_p + ε_v by sequential composition.

## Method

The paper applies the K-Norm Gradient (KNG) mechanism on Riemannian manifolds to sanitize the footpoint and shooting vector sequentially, exploiting the canonical fiber-bundle structure of TM without requiring a global product decomposition. The key theoretical contribution is deriving closed-form sensitivity bounds for both parameters: for non-negative lower curvature κ_l ≥ 0, both sensitivities equal 2τ/n, while for κ_l < 0 they involve cosh and sinh terms scaled by √(−κ_l)(τ_m + τ), directly linking privacy cost to manifold curvature. These bounds are obtained by expressing the Riemannian gradient of the energy in terms of Jacobi fields and applying the Rauch comparison theorem against constant-curvature model manifolds. Sampling from the resulting KNG density is performed via a Riemannian random-walk Metropolis-Hastings chain, with the proposal radius tuned to the noise scale σ = Δ/ε. The utility analysis shows the expected squared manifold distance between the true and private footpoint achieves the Euclidean-optimal rate O(d²/(n²ε²)).

## Results

The paper derives sensitivity bounds for the footpoint and shooting vector of geodesic regression under the KNG mechanism, showing they are governed by Jacobi fields and hence manifold curvature: Δ_p ≤ 2τ/n for non-negative curvature (e.g., sphere, Kendall shape space) and Δ_p ≤ (2τ/n)·cosh(2√(−κ_l)(τ_m+τ)) for negative curvature (e.g., SPD(2) with κ_l = −1/2). The utility of the private estimator matches the Euclidean optimal rate E[d²(p̂,p̃)] = O(d²/(n²ε²)). Empirically, on the unit sphere, Kendall shape space (ADNI corpus callosum data), and SPD(2), the log-average MSE decreases monotonically with sample size (20→50→100 points) and total privacy budget, with parabolic error profiles under unequal budget splits (ε_p + ε_v = 2.02) confirming that extreme imbalance in allocation degrades accuracy most sharply.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper explicitly notes that, to date, there are no existing differential privacy methods for regression on Riemannian manifolds, so no direct empirical baselines are available. The authors discuss several potential comparison routes—output perturbation via manifold Laplace (Reimherr et al., 2021) or Gaussian (Jiang et al., 2023) mechanisms, objective perturbation via the exponential mechanism, and DP Riemannian SGD (Han et al., 2024b)—but argue each requires non-trivial sensitivity or Lipschitz bounds that are not yet established for geodesic regression. They also note that projecting a private Euclidean linear regression onto the manifold is not a valid comparison, since a projected straight line is generally not a geodesic. The closest empirical validation is the sensitivity-bound check in Appendix F, where experimental sensitivities Δ^exp are compared to theoretical bounds Δ^thy across 20 adjacent dataset pairs on the sphere and SPD(2); the ratios r = Δ^thy/Δ^exp are consistently above 1 and concentrated near 1, confirming the bounds are both valid and tight.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: geodesic regression on Riemannian manifolds (Fletcher, 2011) and differential privacy mechanisms for manifold-valued data (Reimherr et al., 2021; Soto et al., 2022). It extends the KNG mechanism from the Fréchet mean setting to the two-parameter geodesic regression problem, deriving sensitivity bounds that are explicitly tied to Jacobi fields and hence sectional curvature via the Rauch comparison theorem. The authors note that no prior DP method exists for regression on manifolds, positioning this as the first such formulation, while building directly on the curvature-dependent sensitivity analysis already established for the Fréchet mean.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first differentially private method for geodesic regression, opening a path toward privacy-preserving analysis of manifold-valued data in medical imaging (e.g., corpus callosum shape from ADNI) and computer vision. Key limitations include the reliance on MCMC sampling (which converts pure DP to approximate DP with δ = O(1/M)) and the need to specify the error bound τ a priori, both of which are acknowledged but not fully resolved. Open questions include extending the framework to multiple geodesic regression, Fréchet regression, and Wasserstein regression, as well as leveraging endpoint-specific curvature information to tighten sensitivity bounds.
