## Problem

Financial LLM evaluation has not kept pace with rapid adoption, with five recurring biases—look-ahead, survivorship, narrative, objective, and cost—systematically inflating reported performance and rendering backtest results invalid for deployment claims. A review of 164 papers (2023–2025) found no single bias discussed in more than 28% of studies, and a survey of 112 practitioners confirmed that 74% lack ready-to-use evaluation tools. The paper argues that generic language evaluation metrics are insufficient proxies for financial validity and that structural validity must be enforced before any result supports a deployment claim.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes a financial LLM system's full evaluation setup—including model weights with declared knowledge cutoffs, retrieval corpora with as-of timestamps, time-indexed tradable universes, rationale traces, confidence/abstention outputs, and execution cost and latency measurements—and produces a binary pass/fail validity score per component rather than a continuous quality metric. If any of the five structural requirements fails, the reported result is downgraded from a deployment claim to a stress-test or proof-of-concept interpretation. The output is structured as a fillable checklist that maps each identified bias to specific audit questions, making evaluation assumptions visible to independent reviewers.

## Method

The paper proposes a Structural Validity Framework comprising five binary diagnostics, each targeting one identified bias: Temporal Sanitation enforces non-anticipativity across model weights, RAG retrieval, and tool outputs by requiring point-in-time data with verifiable as-of metadata; Dynamic Universe Construction mandates time-indexed tradable universes that include subsequently delisted or failed entities; Rationale Robustness treats model explanations as testable objects requiring factual traceability to temporally sanitized sources; Epistemic Calibration requires the action space to include explicit abstention and scores it as a valid outcome; and Realistic Implementation Constraints force reporting of net utility (R_net = R_gross − C_trans − C_M) under measured latency distributions rather than best-case assumptions. The framework is validated through a systematic review of 164 main-conference papers and a user study of 112 practitioners and researchers, which confirmed that 50% identify the lack of evaluation tools as the primary bottleneck to bias mitigation. The paper positions these requirements as non-negotiable prerequisites rather than optional best practices, arguing that failing any single requirement changes the estimand and invalidates deployment claims.

## Results

Across 164 main-conference papers (2023–2025), no single bias is discussed in more than 28% of studies; look-ahead bias appears in 26.8% and survivorship bias in only 1.2%. In the user study (112 respondents, 50 completing all items), 74% report that ready-to-use evaluation tools are scarce (48%) or non-existent (26%), and 50% identify the lack of evaluation tools/frameworks as the single biggest bottleneck to bias mitigation. Additionally, 48% state that current LLM-for-finance research provides only 'to a small extent' clear guidance for identifying or measuring biases, and 62% express low confidence that existing benchmarks capture real-world scenarios including latency, costs, and realistic data availability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a position paper and does not propose a new method to benchmark against existing baselines; the `compares_to` field is empty. Instead, the paper's evidence base consists of a systematic literature review of 164 papers and a survey of 112 practitioners/researchers. The literature review reveals that existing work addresses biases in a fragmented way: look-ahead bias receives the most attention (26.8% of papers) while survivorship bias is nearly absent (1.2%), and no unified framework covers all five identified failure modes. The user study confirms that 50% of respondents name the absence of standardized tools as the primary barrier, 76% report scarce or non-existent evaluation tooling, and only 10% always run technical checks for biases. The paper positions its Structural Validity Framework as the first comprehensive, pass/fail checklist spanning all five biases, filling a gap that no prior single work addresses.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies scattered observations from individual bias-identification studies (Golchin & Surdeanu on parametric time travel, Paleka et al. on RAG temporal leakage, Lee et al. on large-cap preference, Brown et al. on classical survivorship bias in backtesting) into a single five-sin taxonomy and a pass/fail Structural Validity Framework. It departs from the general LLM evaluation survey literature (Laskar et al., 2024) by arguing that generic language-evaluation metrics are structurally inadequate for financial deployment claims, and from prior finance-specific bias papers by treating all five biases as compounding rather than independent. The authors explicitly note that no prior work provides a unified checklist covering all five biases simultaneously, positioning their contribution as the first such guidance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 594% growth in LLM-for-finance papers (36→250, 2023–2025) combined with the finding that no single bias is discussed in more than 28% of 164 surveyed papers highlights a critical infrastructure gap that, if unaddressed, risks embedding invalid backtests into production systems. The user study (74% report scarce or non-existent tools; 50% cite lack of frameworks as the top bottleneck) confirms the practical urgency. Key open questions include whether the framework is too restrictive for early-stage research, how to democratize access to point-in-time and delisting data, and whether the proposed checklist can be validated empirically before becoming a de facto standard. The paper's main limitation is that it is a position paper: the Structural Validity Framework is proposed but not tested on real systems, so its practical utility and calibration remain unverified.
