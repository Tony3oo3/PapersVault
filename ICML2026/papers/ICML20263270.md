## Problem

Existing 3DGS steganography methods (GS-Hider, KeySS) modify model weights or architecture and depend on large pretrained decoders to recover hidden content, creating an unavoidable tradeoff between hidden-image fidelity and clean-view rendering quality. No prior method achieves lossless embedding where all non-secret views remain bit-identical to the original 3DGS renders while still delivering competitive secret-image recovery.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263270/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen pretrained 3DGS model, a single designated source viewpoint P*, and a secret target image M resized to the render resolution of that view. It outputs a compact noise key tensor δ* with the same structure as the SH features (DC and Rest components), which when added to the frozen model's SH coefficients at P* renders the hidden image; all other views produce bit-identical outputs to the original model. An optional lightweight refiner (~50–60K parameters, roughly 8–10× smaller than GS-Hider's ~465K-parameter decoder) can post-process the hidden render to suppress residual structured leakage.

## Method

WhisperSplat trains a small noise key tensor δ via iterative Adam optimization (T=1000 steps, lr=0.01) that is added to the SH features of a completely frozen 3DGS model, so no model weights are ever modified. The per-step loss combines L1 reconstruction toward the target, an SSIM term, and a push-away perturbation term L_pert = −‖h−c‖² that encourages early divergence from the clean render to create embedding capacity. A Gradual Pixel Perturbation (GPP) cosine-decay schedule α(t)=½(1+cos(πt/T)) smoothly transitions the emphasis from push-away in early iterations to reconstruction/SSIM in later iterations, balancing capacity and fidelity without a decoder. For optional quality enhancement, a two-stage refiner first estimates a spatially smooth leakage coefficient map (with ridge and TV regularization) to subtract structured contamination correlated with the clean render, then learns a small convolutional residual predictor with MSE and perceptual losses to correct remaining error.

## Results

WhisperSplat achieves the highest average secret-image PSNR of 26.563 dB across nine 3DGS scenes, outperforming KeySS (26.427 dB) and GS-Hider (25.178 dB). Critically, WhisperSplat's clean-view PSNR is 26.518 dB, bit-identical to the 3DGS Ground Truth baseline, confirming zero measurable degradation to non-secret renders. In contrast, KeySS (25.980 dB) and GS-Hider (25.817 dB) both show noticeable clean-view drops. On individual scenes, WhisperSplat achieves notable gains such as 26.074 dB on treehill (vs. 22.121 for KeySS) and 30.004 dB on garden (vs. 28.179 for KeySS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against KeySS, WhisperSplat achieves a higher average secret PSNR (26.563 vs. 26.427 dB) and a substantially higher clean-view PSNR (26.518 vs. 25.980 dB), with the gap on clean-view fidelity being the key differentiator since WhisperSplat introduces zero degradation while KeySS loses ~0.54 dB. On individual scenes, KeySS still edges out WhisperSplat on bicycle (+0.14 dB), flowers (+4.02 dB), stump (+1.67 dB), and kitchen (+2.30 dB), but WhisperSplat recovers the average with large wins on treehill (+3.95 dB), counter (+3.31 dB), and garden (+1.83 dB). Against GS-Hider, WhisperSplat leads on both average secret PSNR (26.563 vs. 25.178 dB) and clean-view PSNR (26.518 vs. 25.817 dB), with GS-Hider's only per-scene secret-PSNR advantage being garden (32.348 vs. 30.004 dB). The most consequential distinction is architectural: both KeySS and GS-Hider require large pretrained decoders (~465K parameters for GS-Hider) and modify model weights, whereas WhisperSplat uses only a compact noise key tensor plus an optional ~50–60K-parameter refiner, preserving the 3DGS model entirely intact.

## Relation to Prior Work

WhisperSplat adapts the trainable-noise steganography paradigm from Noise-NeRF (Huang et al., 2024) to the explicit Gaussian representation of 3DGS, but departs fundamentally from prior 3DGS steganography methods (GS-Hider, KeySS) that modify model weights or architecture and depend on large pretrained decoders for extraction. Instead of retraining the 3DGS model or coupling embedding with a learned inverse map, WhisperSplat applies a compact, view-specific noise key tensor directly to the spherical-harmonic features of a frozen pretrained model, making the clean render bit-identical to the original. The Gradual Pixel Perturbation (GPP) cosine-decay schedule is a novel scheduling mechanism that explicitly balances early divergence (embedding capacity) against late convergence (reconstruction fidelity), a tradeoff that prior decoder-based methods handle implicitly through their learned inverse maps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As 3DGS becomes the dominant real-time 3D reconstruction format in commercial pipelines (DJI Terra, Luma AI), the ability to embed verifiable provenance without any measurable degradation to rendering quality addresses a practical gap that prior methods leave open. The decoder-free, lossless design significantly reduces deployment overhead and eliminates the detectability tradeoff inherent in weight-modifying approaches. Open questions include whether the method scales to multiple simultaneous hidden messages or arbitrary view pairs, how it behaves under more aggressive adversarial attacks (e.g., targeted Gaussian pruning or SH coefficient manipulation beyond random pruning), and whether the lightweight refiner generalizes across scenes without per-scene retraining.
