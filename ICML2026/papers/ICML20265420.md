## Problem

Existing decentralized federated learning (DFL) methods rely on whole-model averaging, which limits exploration of the non-convex loss landscape and causes convergence to suboptimal local minima, especially under non-IID data and sparse communication topologies. While layer exchange can improve exploration, it introduces instability in later training stages, and no prior DFL framework provides a unified, dynamically adaptive mechanism to balance exploration and exploitation at the layer level.

## Input / Output

Each client holds a local private image dataset (CIFAR-10/100 at 32×32, SVHN at 32×32) and a model (ResNet-50 or VGG-16) decomposed into R layers. Clients communicate peer-to-peer over a sparse decentralized graph (no central server), exchanging only layer-level information. The output is a per-client trained model whose layers have been selectively averaged or exchanged with neighbors, evaluated by test accuracy under Dirichlet-distributed non-IID splits (α = 0.1, 0.5, 1.0) and IID settings.

## Method

DecFus unifies layer-wise exchange (exploration) and layer-wise averaging (exploitation) through three components: (1) a Dynamic Cutoff Determination module that monitors neighborhood training-loss variance via a sliding-window stability score and, upon detecting convergence, triggers a sigmoid-decaying cutoff that shifts the system from exchange-dominant to averaging-dominant aggregation; (2) a Similarity-based Layer Grouping step that computes pairwise cosine similarity between each layer and its neighbors' corresponding layers, then partitions layers into an exchange group (low similarity) and averaging group (high similarity) using the dynamic cutoff as a quantile threshold; and (3) a Unified Layer-wise Aggregation formula where the per-layer weight vector v_ij,r is either a probability vector (weighted averaging) or a one-hot vector (probabilistic exchange toward the most dissimilar neighbor), collapsing both operations into a single expression. Theoretically, the paper proves convergence without the standard doubly stochastic matrix assumption by modeling layer exchange as a Markov process and showing the spectral radius of the cumulative product remains strictly below 1.

## Results

DecFus achieves the highest test accuracy across most settings in Table 1. On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-50 under extreme non-IID (α=0.1), DecFus reaches 36.60%, outperforming the best baseline DisPFL (35.63%) by ~1%; under moderate non-IID (α=0.5) it reaches 73.39% vs. DisPFL's 69.22% (+4.2%). On CIFAR-100 with ResNet-50 (α=0.1), DecFus achieves 35.76% vs. the next-best DFLExP at 31.10% (+4.7%). EDecFus, the communication-efficient variant, achieves comparable accuracy (e.g., 71.02% vs. 73.39% on CIFAR-10 α=0.5) with reduced per-round communication cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DecFus outperforms all six baselines (DFLAvg, DFLProx, DFLExP, DisPFL, DFedSAM, DFLMR) in the majority of 24 ResNet-50 and VGG-16 settings. The largest margins appear on CIFAR-100 with ResNet-50 under extreme non-IID (α=0.1), where DecFus achieves 35.76% versus DFLExP's 31.10% (+4.7 pp) and DFLMR's 28.83% (+6.9 pp), and on CIFAR-10 α=0.5 where it reaches 73.39% versus DisPFL's 69.22% (+4.2 pp). DecFus loses to DFLProx on two VGG-16/SVHN settings (α=0.1: 72.84% vs. 75.65%; α=0.5: 91.60% vs. 92.20%), and to DFLMR on VGG-16/CIFAR-10 α=0.1 (54.14% vs. 54.36%), though these gaps are small. DisPFL is the strongest averaging-based baseline on CIFAR-100 but degrades sharply under extreme non-IID (27.67% at α=0.1 with ResNet-50), while DFLMR (layer recombination) is competitive under IID but lags under non-IID. EDecFus, the communication-efficient variant, typically trails DecFus by 1–3 pp while reducing per-round communication from O(|G_i|·R) to O(R).

## Relation to Prior Work

DecFus extends the decentralized federated learning (DFL) consensus aggregation framework (Liu & Ding, 2021) by replacing uniform model-level averaging with a layer-wise fusion strategy that selectively applies either averaging or exchange per layer. It adapts the layer-exchange concept from FedMR (Hu et al., 2024), which operates at the whole-model level in a centralized setting, into a decentralized context and couples it with a dynamic exploration-to-exploitation transition triggered by neighborhood loss variance. The key conceptual departure from prior DFL work is the removal of the doubly stochastic aggregation matrix assumption, replaced by an 'impermanent layer exchange' condition analyzed via an external Markov process.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DecFus provides a principled mechanism to escape the local-minima trap that plagues averaging-based DFL under non-IID data, and its convergence guarantee without the doubly stochastic assumption broadens the class of admissible aggregation topologies. However, the evaluation is limited to image classification on CIFAR-10/100 and SVHN with ResNet-50 and VGG-16, and the gains on SVHN are modest, leaving open whether the layer-wise fusion strategy transfers to larger-scale vision tasks, language models, or non-vision modalities. The Markov-process-based convergence proof introduces additional assumptions (irreducibility, aperiodicity of the exchange process) that may be difficult to verify in practice for very large or dynamically changing topologies.
