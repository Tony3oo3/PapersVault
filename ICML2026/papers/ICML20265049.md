## Problem

Genomic foundation models (GFMs) have shown strong performance on tasks like epigenetic prediction and regulatory element discovery, but their adoption in life sciences remains limited by a fundamental gap between biological domain expertise and technical implementation. Existing LLM fine-tuning frameworks (e.g., LLaMA-Factory) do not address genomics-specific requirements such as FASTA/NCBI data handling, biology-informed loss functions, domain-specific benchmarks, or biological interpretability, leaving the field without a unified, user-friendly platform for the full GFM development pipeline.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes DNA sequences (FASTA or CSV with sequence+label columns), YAML configuration files specifying tasks/models/hyperparameters, and pre-trained GFM checkpoints, accessed via a CLI entry point (genomefactory-cli) or a Gradio WebUI. Outputs include task-ready preprocessed datasets (train/dev/test CSVs), sequence embeddings (last hidden state or mean-pooled), generated DNA sequences from prompts, classification/regression predictions, structured JSON benchmark metrics (MCC, F1, precision, recall), and sparse latent unit activations from the SAE interpreter. The adapter tuning path requires a two-stage input: first extracting base-model embeddings via the Inference Engine, then training the adapter on those frozen representations.

## Method

GENOME-FACTORY is a six-module Python library that unifies the GFM workflow: (1) Genome Collector automates NCBI data retrieval with task-specific builders (histone modification, enhancer, promoter) and multi-stage QC (GC normalization, N-nucleotide filtering, chi-square compositional bias detection); (2) Model Loader wraps six diverse GFMs (HyenaDNA, DNABERT-2, Caduceus, Nucleotide Transformer, EVO, GenomeOcean) under HuggingFace Transformers with automatic tokenizer and checkpoint handling; (3) Model Trainer supports full fine-tuning, LoRA (rank-8, α=32, architecture-specific target modules), and adapter tuning (frozen base + MLP adapter) with mixed precision, flash attention, and gradient accumulation; (4) Inference Engine provides unified embedding extraction and DNA sequence generation; (5) Benchmarker integrates GUE and Genomic Benchmarks with a plugin system for custom tasks; (6) Biological Interpreter trains a sparse autoencoder (hidden dim 4096) on model embeddings to produce near-monosemantic latent units, then fits regression probes against external biological readouts (sequence length, CTCF motif presence) to link individual neurons to interpretable genomic features. The key differentiator is the SAE-based interpretability module, which is the first open-source tool to decode internal GFM representations into biologically meaningful units, validated by identifying specific latent dimensions correlated with sequence length and achieving 0.7881 accuracy in distinguishing CTCF-bound from background sequences.

## Results

On the GUE benchmark, GenomeOcean-100M achieves the highest full fine-tuning score (65.52±0.57 MCC), while DNABERT-2 leads on Genomic Benchmarks (71.97±0.20 MCC). Parameter-efficient methods remain competitive: LoRA on GenomeOcean-100M actually surpasses full fine-tuning on Genomic Benchmarks (68.26 vs. 66.28), and adapter tuning achieves the best efficiency (e.g., DNABERT-2 adapter uses only 0.2M trainable parameters, 2.09 GB memory, and 124.30 KTok/s throughput). All six traditional baselines (CNN, RF, SVM, PWM+RF, PWM+SVM, HMM) score 8–20 points below the best foundation models on both benchmarks. In the biological interpretation experiment, a sparse autoencoder on DNABERT-2 embeddings identifies latent units correlated with sequence length, and a logistic regression probe on SAE activations achieves 0.7881 accuracy in distinguishing CTCF-bound from background sequences.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the six genomic foundation models, GenomeOcean-100M achieves the best GUE score under full fine-tuning (65.52) and remains competitive under LoRA (59.35), while DNABERT-2 leads on Genomic Benchmarks with full fine-tuning (71.97). The 7B EVO model, despite its scale, does not consistently outperform smaller models under parameter-efficient tuning (44.97 GUE LoRA vs. 59.35 for GenomeOcean-100M LoRA), suggesting that architecture and pre-training data matter as much as parameter count. All six traditional baselines (CNN, RF, SVM, PWM+RF, PWM+SVM, HMM) score 8–20 points below the best foundation models on both benchmarks, with SVM being the strongest classical method (57.01 GUE, 59.89 GB) yet still trailing DNABERT-2 by ~9 points on GUE. On the additional Genomics Long-Range Benchmark (LRB), HyenaDNA-160k achieves the best score (51.36), likely benefiting from its long-context architecture, while GenomeOcean-100M leads on GUANinE v1.0 (63.14). In the unsupervised species-clustering evaluation, GenomeOcean-4B nearly doubles the ARI of the next-best model (0.321 vs. 0.167 for DNABERT-2), indicating richer multi-species representation learning.

## Relation to Prior Work

GENOME-FACTORY positions itself as the genomic analogue of LLaMA-Factory for language models, but explicitly argues that genomics demands specialized handling (FASTA/NCBI data pipelines, biology-informed loss functions, domain-specific benchmarks, and biological interpretability) that general-purpose LLM fine-tuning frameworks do not address. Rather than proposing a new model architecture, it unifies six existing genomic foundation models (HyenaDNA, DNABERT-2, Caduceus, Nucleotide Transformer, EVO, GenomeOcean) under a single HuggingFace-Transformers-based interface and adds a sparse autoencoder-based Biological Interpreter borrowed from the LLM interpretability literature. The contribution is thus primarily systems-level integration and tooling, with the SAE-based interpretation module being the most novel component applied to the genomic domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing the first end-to-end, zero-code-accessible pipeline for genomic model development, GENOME-FACTORY lowers the technical barrier for biologists who lack ML engineering expertise and for engineers who lack biological context. The SAE-based Biological Interpreter is a promising but preliminary step toward mechanistic interpretability for GFMs; the paper itself cautions it is an initial tool rather than a validated discovery method. Key open questions include whether the framework scales to long-range genomic tasks (current context truncation limits applicability to benchmarks like DNALONGBENCH), whether multi-task learning genuinely improves genomic representations, and whether the SAE latent features generalize beyond the two demonstrated use cases (sequence length, CTCF binding).
