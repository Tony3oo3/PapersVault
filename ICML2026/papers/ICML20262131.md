## Problem

In decoupled multi-armed bandits, where exploration and exploitation are performed on separate arms each round, existing Best-of-Both-Worlds policies rely on FTRL with convex optimization at every step, while FTPL-type policies that avoid optimization have only achieved suboptimal regret bounds. The paper asks whether BOBW guarantees (optimal adversarial regret and near-optimal stochastic regret) can be attained for decoupled bandits without either convex optimization or the geometric resampling typically needed in FTPL to estimate arm-selection probabilities.

## Input / Output

At each round the learner incurs the loss of an exploited arm without observing it and observes the loss of an explored arm without incurring it; the only feedback is the scalar loss of the explored arm. The algorithm consumes the cumulative importance-weighted loss estimates (updated only for the explored arm), a shape parameter α > 1 governing Pareto perturbations, and a learning-rate schedule η_t = cK^{1/α − 1/2}/√t. It outputs two arm indices per round (exploitation and exploration) and, theoretically, regret bounds of O(√(KT)) in the adversarial regime and O(K/Δ_min) in the stochastically constrained adversarial regime.

## Method

The policy uses FTPL with i.i.d. Pareto(α) perturbations for exploitation: arm i_t = argmin_i {L̂_{t,i} − r_{t,i}/η_t}. The key technical contribution is a surrogate quantity q_{t,i} = (1/(1+η_t L̂_{t,i}) ∧ 1/σ_{t,i}^{1/α})^{(α+1)/2}, where σ_{t,i} is the rank of L̂_{t,i} among all arms, which approximates w_{t,i}^{1−1/α} without requiring the intractable closed-form exploitation probability. The exploration distribution p_{t,i} ∝ q_{t,i} is computed directly from the cumulative loss estimates and their ranks, eliminating both the convex optimization of FTRL and the geometric resampling of standard FTPL. An optimized implementation maintains ranks incrementally via binary search, reducing per-step cost to O(K) worst case. The analysis introduces an event D_t that links q_{t,i} to w_{t,i} on the event the optimal arm has been identified, enabling a self-bounding argument that yields the BOBW regret guarantees.

## Results

The proposed FTPL policy achieves order-optimal O(√(KT)) regret in the adversarial regime (Theorem 3.1) and near-optimal time-independent O(K/Δ_min) regret in the stochastically constrained adversarial regime (Theorem 3.2), matching the guarantees of Decoupled-Tsallis-INF. With α=3, the stochastic regret reduces to O(√(K/Δ_min · Σ_{i≠i*} 1/Δ_i) + K/Δ_min) (Corollary 3.3), which is time-independent and improves upon the standard stochastic bandit bound of O(Σ log T / Δ_i). Empirically, FTPL achieves lower cumulative regret than both FTRL (β=2/3) and EXP3 (β=1) in both adversarial (Δ=0.125, 8 arms) and stochastic (5 arms, Δ_min=0.05 and 0.002) settings, while also running faster per step.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two instances of Decoupled-Tsallis-INF (Rouyer & Seldin, 2020): the β=2/3 FTRL variant (which requires solving a convex optimization via Newton's method each round) and the β=1 EXP3 variant (closed-form probabilities). In the adversarial regime (8 arms, Δ=0.125), the proposed FTPL achieves lower cumulative regret than both FTRL and EXP3 throughout the horizon, while also running faster per step — Figure 3 shows FTPL's per-step time growing more slowly than FTRL's as K increases, and using a conic solver (SCS) for FTRL makes it at least 100× slower. In the stochastic regime, FTPL again achieves the lowest regret on both the easy instance (Δ_min=0.05) and the harder instance (Δ_min=0.002), converging faster than FTRL and EXP3. The key practical advantage is that FTPL completely avoids both the convex optimization step (required by FTRL) and the geometric resampling step (required by standard FTPL for importance-weighted estimators), yielding a clean O(K) worst-case per-step cost with a theoretically characterized complexity, unlike FTRL whose Newton iteration count is not formally bounded.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the FTPL framework for BOBW guarantees (Honda et al., 2023; Lee et al., 2024) to the decoupled bandit setting, where prior BOBW results relied exclusively on FTRL-based methods requiring convex optimization (Rouyer & Seldin, 2020). The key departure is replacing the intractable exploitation probability vector w_t with an efficiently computable surrogate q_t for designing exploration probabilities, thereby avoiding both the convex optimization of FTRL and the geometric resampling typically needed in FTPL bandit policies. The Pareto (Fréchet-type) perturbation connects the method to the β-Tsallis-INF family, with α = 1/(1−β) providing a natural correspondence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The surrogate probability q_t is a broadly useful idea: it enables FTPL to realize its full computational advantage in settings where the exact probability vector is unavailable, and the authors note it could extend to other frameworks like the Prod family. However, the stochastic regret bound retains a K/Δ_min second term that is looser than the √K/Δ_min achieved by FTRL, and the authors acknowledge that arm-dependent learning rates (which could tighten this) would require substantially more intricate analysis for FTPL. The practical 130× speedup over convex-optimization-based FTRL is significant for real-time applications like communication systems that motivated the decoupled setting.
