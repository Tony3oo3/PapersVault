## Problem

Most existing proof sketches for automated theorem proving are immutable: once constructed, any error correction or further subgoal decomposition requires rebuilding the entire sketch from scratch, discarding all previously verified subgoals and incurring substantial redundant token cost. This paper addresses the limitation by introducing an editable proof-sketch structure that supports localized, in-place modifications while preserving already-proved components.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265139/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a Lean 4 formalized theorem statement (including its header with imports and context) and produces either a fully verified Lean 4 proof script or a certified refutation (a derivation of False from assuming the theorem). Internally, the general-purpose LLM interacts with the proof structure through a custom DSL that encodes four node types and their syntactic/logical dependencies, while the specialized prover receives compiled subtheorems with explicit premise lists. A problem counts as solved if either the original theorem is formally proved or a refutation is produced.

## Method

EditableSketch decomposes a proof into an ordered sequence of four node types—Deduction, Construction, Assumption, and Conclusion—each explicitly annotating its syntactic dependencies (objects that must exist earlier for type-checking) and logical premises (inputs used only within the derivation). This structure supports three single-step edits (insertion, update, deletion) that modify exactly one node, with a compiler that translates the sketch into Lean 4 subtheorems and a proof fragment, performing structural and type-checking validation. SketchRefine builds on this by iteratively alternating between subgoal proving (via a specialized prover with 8 candidates per subgoal) and feedback-driven sketch revision: incorrect subgoals trigger localized error repair, while correct-but-hard subgoals trigger in-place decomposition, with previously verified nodes never reproved unless their subgoals change. A theorem-retrieval stage (using LeanExplore) supplies relevant Mathlib lemmas before decomposition to mitigate hallucination from outdated LLM knowledge, and a refutation-guided restart mechanism handles unprovable instances by switching to proving the negation.

## Results

On MiniF2F-test, SketchRefine achieves a 99.6% pass rate (Gemini 2.5 Pro + DeepSeek-Prover-V2-7B non-CoT, with refutation), solving all but two of 244 problems. On FormalMath-Lite, SketchRefine (DeepSeek-V3.2 + DeepSeek-Prover-V2-7B, with refutation) reaches 76.0% pass rate, exceeding the previous best (DeepSeek-Prover-V2-671B at 61.9%) by 14.1 percentage points. The method also demonstrates substantially better token efficiency: on MiniF2F-test, SketchRefine uses an average of 547K output tokens per problem versus 2.3M for Hilbert at comparable pass rates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On MiniF2F-test, SketchRefine (99.6%) outperforms Hilbert (99.2%) by 0.4%, Delta-Prover (95.9%) by 3.7%, and BFS-Prover-V2-32B with Planner (95.1%) by 4.5%, while using a smaller prover (7B vs. 32B/671B). On FormalMath-Lite, the gap is much larger: SketchRefine (76.0%) exceeds DeepSeek-Prover-V2-671B (61.9%) by 14.1 points, Spark-Prover-X1-7B (59.8%) by 16.2 points, and Goedel-Prover-V2-8B (55.3%) by 20.7 points. The paper also highlights token efficiency: at matched token budgets on MiniF2F-test, SketchRefine consistently achieves higher pass rates than Hilbert (Figure 4), attributed to its in-place editing strategy that preserves verified subgoals rather than rebuilding the entire sketch. The ablation in Figure 4 further shows that the In-Place editing strategy outperforms the Build strategy (rebuilding sub-sketches from scratch), with the gap widening on harder problems. Notably, SketchRefine with the open-source DeepSeek-V3.2 (97.5%) surpasses Delta-Prover using the closed-source Gemini 2.5 Pro (95.9%), demonstrating that the framework's design, not just model scale, drives performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the iterative proof-sketch paradigm established by Delta-Prover (Zhou et al., 2025b) and Hilbert (Varambally et al., 2025), both of which decompose theorems into subgoals and refine sketches through feedback. The key departure is making the proof sketch itself an editable, mutable data structure (EditableSketch) inspired by Lean 4's term-style proofs, rather than treating it as an immutable artifact that must be regenerated wholesale upon error. Where Hilbert and Delta-Prover rebuild or recursively reconstruct sketches—discarding verified subgoals and losing global context—SketchRefine performs localized in-place edits (insert, update, delete) that preserve all previously proved subgoals, drawing on the assumption/conclusion node concept from Delta-Prover for scoping.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 14.1% pass-rate improvement over DeepSeek-Prover-V2-671B on FormalMath-Lite, combined with substantially lower token overhead versus Hilbert at matched performance, demonstrates that preserving verified subgoals through editable sketches is both a practical efficiency win and a quality improvement for LLM-based ATP. The refutation mechanism for detecting and certifying unprovable formalizations is a useful robustness addition. Open questions include whether the LLM-inferred syntactic dependencies (currently a bottleneck the authors acknowledge could be handled by the Lean 4 compiler) can be fully automated, and whether the editable-sketch paradigm generalizes beyond Lean 4 to other proof assistants or to settings where the specialized prover is weaker.
