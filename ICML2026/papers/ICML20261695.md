## Problem

Mixture-of-Experts architectures are designed to scale LLMs through expert specialization, yet in practice experts become functionally redundant or collapse into de facto shared experts, limiting effective capacity. The underlying cause of this specialization failure has remained poorly understood, with prior work focusing on routing-level remedies without addressing the structural root cause.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261695/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

SD-MoE operates on standard MoE layers where a token hidden state x ∈ R^d is routed via a linear gate to a subset of experts, each producing a transformed representation through SwiGLU blocks. The key structural difference is that each expert's linear projection is split into a shared low-rank component (rank k, typically 8) and an expert-specific component constrained to the orthogonal complement, so the forward pass computes (W_c + W_u^(i))x. During backpropagation, each expert's gradient is projected onto the shared subspace to update the common expert and the residual updates the unique expert, with the shared SVD basis refreshed every 16 steps.

## Method

SD-MoE performs spectral decomposition of both parameters and gradients to enforce expert specialization. At initialization, each expert weight matrix is decomposed via SVD into a shared rank-k component W_c = U_k Σ_k V_k^⊤ (assigned to a common expert) and unique expert matrices W_u^(i) constrained to satisfy U_k^⊤ W_u^(i) = 0 and W_u^(i) V_k = 0, ensuring orthogonality to the dominant subspace. During training, each proxy expert gradient G^(i) is decomposed as G_c^(i) = P_U G^(i) + (I − P_U) G^(i) P_V (updating the common expert) and G_u^(i) = G^(i) − G_c^(i) (updating the unique expert), preventing cross-expert gradient interference from re-emerging. This spectral decoupling reduces inter-expert parameter similarity from ~0.8 to below 0.1, enables 4× larger stable learning rates (30% training efficiency gain), and adds only ~5% training overhead with zero inference cost, while being seamlessly integrable into existing architectures like Qwen and DeepSeek.

## Results

On 8 downstream tasks (ARC, HellaSwag, PIQA, RACE, SIQA, Winogrande, LAMBADA), SD-MoE applied to Qwen-2B-A0.8B achieves 53.97% average accuracy, up from 50.45% for the Qwen baseline (+3.52 pts) and 50.51% for DeepSeek-2B-A0.8B. At the larger 7B-A1.5B scale, SD-MoE reaches 57.23% average accuracy versus 55.34% for the Qwen baseline (+1.89 pts). SD-MoE also enables 4× larger stable learning rates (up to 4×10⁻⁴ vs. 1×10⁻⁴ for baseline), yielding a 30% training efficiency gain, while incurring only ~5% additional training overhead and zero inference overhead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SD-MoE is compared against three baselines. Against the Qwen MoE baseline, SD-MoE improves average accuracy by 3.52 points at the 2B scale (53.97 vs. 50.45) and by 1.89 points at the 7B scale (57.23 vs. 55.34), with the largest gains on ARC-challenge (+5.03 pts at 2B, +4.95 pts at 7B) and HellaSwag (+4.14 pts at 2B, +5.01 pts at 7B). Against the DeepSeek MoE baseline at the 2B scale, SD-MoE gains 0.98 points on average (51.49 vs. 50.51), with the most notable improvement on Winogrande (+2.58 pts). Against AES (Guo et al., 2026), a diversity-promoting expert-specialization baseline, SD-MoE outperforms by 2.39 average points (53.97 vs. 51.58) under identical pre-training settings. On the XSUM summarization task, SD-MoE also improves over the baseline MoE on all ROUGE metrics (e.g., ROUGE-2: 13.35 vs. 12.08), confirming gains beyond multiple-choice benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the standard MoE architecture (Shazeer et al., 2017) and its modern instantiations in Qwen and DeepSeek, but departs from the dominant line of work on specialization failure that focuses on routing-level remedies (load-balancing losses, capacity constraints, expert-choice routing). Instead, it introduces a spectral (SVD-based) diagnostic framework that identifies the root cause of under-specialization as shared low-rank structure in language representations, and proposes a parameter/gradient decomposition method (SD-MoE) that operates at the weight-matrix level rather than the routing level. The approach is conceptually related to spectral normalization and dynamical isometry literature, but applied specifically to the multi-expert setting where cross-expert spectral overlap is the central pathology.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first mechanistic, spectral-level explanation for why MoE experts fail to specialize, showing that shared low-rank structure in human text drives gradient alignment, parameter overlap, and gating bias simultaneously. The practical impact is notable: a 4× learning-rate stability improvement (30% training efficiency) with only ~5% overhead, applicable to any existing MoE architecture. Open questions include whether the rank-8 shared subspace generalizes to much larger models (100B+), whether the spectral decomposition interacts well with quantization or other compression techniques, and whether the syntactic-structure interpretation of dominant subspaces holds for non-English or code-centric corpora.
