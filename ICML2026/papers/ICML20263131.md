## Problem

Weightless Neural Networks (WNNs) based on Lookup Tables offer extremely compact inference but struggle to achieve competitive accuracy under tight resource budgets. Existing WNNs either require very wide single layers (ULEEN), are restricted to LUT-2 due to training infeasibility (DiffLogicNet), or introduce finite-difference estimation errors (DWN), all of which rely on larger models and higher FPGA resource consumption to improve accuracy. This paper addresses how to boost WNN accuracy while simultaneously reducing implementation resources for edge deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263131/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts grayscale images (MNIST, KMNIST), RGB images (SVHN, CIFAR-10), or audio signals (Speech Commands), which are first decomposed by channel and then binarized using Ψ=4 data-driven thresholds to produce multiple binary maps per channel. These binary maps pass through LUT-based convolution and threshold pooling before feeding a single-layer WNN, ultimately producing K class logits via a sparse learnable GroupSum aggregation. The entire inference pipeline operates in purely binary/LUT space with no multiplications, and the model is deployed as Verilog on FPGA or C++ on microprocessor.

## Method

CLGN enriches the WNN architecture with LUT-n-based convolutional kernels (n=6, matching FPGA native LUT-6 granularity) that sample n input bits from a spatial receptive field, followed by a threshold pooling layer that outputs 1 only when activated elements exceed a threshold Q. A single-layer WNN with learnable input mappings (each LUT selects its n input positions via score vectors) processes the pooled binary features, and a learnable GroupSum module aggregates LUT outputs into class logits using top-C sparse connections from a trainable score matrix. Training follows a two-stage hierarchical strategy: Stage 1 trains the convolutional and pooling layers under supervision of a compact MLP teacher (which is then discarded), and Stage 2 trains the WNN LUT tables, input mappings, and GroupSum connections via cross-entropy loss with gradient propagation only through active GroupSum connections. This decoupling allows discrete feature extraction to be optimized separately from class-level relational learning, while the learnable GroupSum enables adaptive feature sharing across classes.

## Results

On the FPGA (Xilinx Alveo U50), CLGN achieves the highest accuracy among all WNN approaches across all five datasets: 98.76% (MNIST), 92.63% (KMNIST), 78.91% (SVHN), 62.53% (CIFAR-10), and 82.37% (Speech Commands), while using fewer LUTs and smaller parameter sizes than all compared WNNs. For example, on CIFAR-10, CLGN uses 41,212 LUTs and 173.3 KiB parameters versus 48,109 LUTs and 283.4 KiB for DWN, yet achieves 6.4% higher accuracy. On the Raspberry Pi 4B microprocessor, CLGN maintains the lowest memory usage among competitive-accuracy models (e.g., 284.1 KiB on CIFAR-10 vs. 388.1 KiB for DWN and 1,918.7 KiB for BNN), at the cost of higher per-inference latency due to its convolutional stage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLGN outperforms all WNN baselines (DWN, ULEEN, DiffLogicNet, PolyLUT, NeuraLUT) on test accuracy across all five datasets, with the largest margins on CIFAR-10 (+6.4% over DWN, +5.8% over LGN) and SVHN (+4.9% over DWN, +3.0% over LGN). On MNIST and KMNIST, CLGN also surpasses BNN in accuracy (98.76% vs. 97.71%; 92.63% vs. 91.54%) while using roughly 29× fewer LUTs (8,975 vs. 259,070; 11,510 vs. 542,102). On the harder color-image datasets (SVHN, CIFAR-10), BNN retains a small accuracy edge (81.43% vs. 78.91%; 63.84% vs. 62.53%) but at 8–19× the LUT cost and 5–7× the parameter size. CLGN uses fewer LUTs than DWN on every dataset (e.g., 41,212 vs. 48,109 on CIFAR-10) and fewer than ULEEN and DiffLogicNet by 3–10×, while DiffLogicNet and ULEEN trail CLGN by 5–25% accuracy depending on the dataset. The trade-off is latency: CLGN's convolutional stage makes it ~500× slower than DWN/LGN on FPGA (5,263 ns vs. 9.5 ns on MNIST), though on the microprocessor the gap narrows to ~4–10× because sequential CPU execution equalizes per-LUT cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

CLGN extends the Weightless Neural Network lineage initiated by ULEEN (LUT-based neurons with a fixed GroupSum) and DWN (learnable LUT input connections via finite-difference gradients) by introducing three architectural additions: LUT-n-based convolutional layers with multi-threshold binarization, a threshold pooling stage, and a learnable GroupSum module. It departs from DiffLogicNet/ConvDiffLogicNet, which are restricted to LUT-2 due to the combinatorial explosion of Boolean functions, by operating natively at LUT-6 to match FPGA logic granularity. The hierarchical two-stage training (MLP-guided convolution training followed by WNN/GroupSum training) is a new strategy not present in any prior WNN, addressing the non-differentiability of LUT-based convolutional operators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CLGN demonstrates that adding structured binary feature extraction (convolution + pooling) to a WNN can close much of the accuracy gap with BNNs while using an order of magnitude fewer LUTs, which is practically significant for FPGA-constrained edge deployment where model size is the binding constraint. The key open trade-off is latency: convolutional operations make CLGN substantially slower than flat WNNs on FPGA (e.g., 5263 ns vs ~10 ns on MNIST), limiting its applicability to throughput-sensitive scenarios. Whether the convolutional WNN paradigm scales to larger datasets (ImageNet-scale) or deeper multi-layer WNNs remains unexplored, and the moderate accuracy gain from learned vs. random convolutional initialization (CLGN vs. CLGN(r)) suggests the structural design may matter more than the learned weights, pointing to potential simplifications.
