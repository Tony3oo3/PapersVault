## Problem

PPO's training dynamics are highly sensitive to how neural networks approximate the policy and value functions, with the interaction of function approximation, bootstrapping, and off-policy updates (the 'deadly triad') causing oscillations in value estimates, latent representations, and policy outputs. The paper identifies via CKA analysis that critic representations and actor pre-squash directions undergo non-smooth geometric rearrangements throughout training, yet existing stabilization methods do not explicitly constrain the latent-space geometry of the actor-critic pair.

## Input / Output

For continuous-control tasks (MuJoCo-v5, DMC), the input is a state vector and the output is a continuous action in (-1,1)^d produced by sampling from a Gaussian with state-independent diagonal covariance and applying tanh squashing. For pixel-based Atari tasks, the input is a stack of four 84×84 grayscale frames (shape 4×84×84) and the output is a discrete action; the no-flip regularizer is applied to the policy logits in this setting. All three SPPO components operate internally on network representations: the CKA constraint and KDE act on the critic's latent encoder outputs, while the no-flip penalty acts on the actor's pre-squash mean-action space.

## Method

SPPO is a drop-in augmentation to PPO that adds three regularizers without modifying the clipped surrogate or network topology. (i) A CKA-based critic constraint maintains a frozen encoder copy and a buffer of past short sequences, then penalizes the decrease in linear CKA between old and new critic latent representations on those sequences, which is shown to add a positive-semidefinite matrix to the ridge-regression normal matrix and tighten bounds on pointwise and mean-squared representation drift. (ii) A no-flip penalty records historical pre-squash mean-action directions in a buffer and applies a hinge loss (with threshold π/2) when the current update direction, measured under the Mahalanobis metric induced by the policy covariance, deviates too far from the historical improvement direction. (iii) A KDE is constructed in the critic's latent space over online states; the negative log-density serves as a novelty score, is baselined by a p-quantile truncation, and is added to GAE advantages to bias exploration toward low-density (high-novelty) regions. Theoretical results establish that the CKA term contracts a second-order bound on one-step bootstrapping error, the no-flip term improves expected directional alignment and controls strong-violation tail probability, and the KDE shaping guarantees non-decreasing occupancy mass in high-novelty regions via exponential tilting.

## Results

On MuJoCo-v5 (2×128 default architecture, 6 seeds), SPPO achieves the highest final evaluation return on all five environments: Hopper 2436.5, Walker2D 3174.4, HalfCheetah 5571.3, Ant 4858.7, and Humanoid 5842.3. The largest gains over vanilla PPO appear on Hopper (+49%) and Walker2D (+65%), while on HalfCheetah the improvement is more modest (+9%). On DMC, SPPO also outperforms PPO across all five environments (e.g., Cheetah-Run 475.8 vs. 344.4; Quadruped-Walk 805.1 vs. 651.8). On Atari, SPPO improves over PPO on 3 of 5 games (notably Qbert: 7292 vs. 4630) but is slightly worse on Breakout and SpaceInvaders.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPPO outperforms all four baselines on 4 of 5 MuJoCo environments; the sole exception is Walker2D, where PFO (3256.7) marginally exceeds SPPO (3174.4). Against PPO, SPPO's advantage is most pronounced on Hopper (+49%) and Walker2D (+65%), and smallest on HalfCheetah (+9%). Compared to CHAIN, SPPO leads by a wide margin on Hopper (2436 vs. 862) and Walker2D (3174 vs. 1183), but the gap narrows on Humanoid (5842 vs. 5715). Against PEER, SPPO consistently leads, with the largest gap on HalfCheetah (+43%) and Hopper (+21%). Against PFO, SPPO wins on Hopper (+59%), HalfCheetah (+21%), and Humanoid (+6%), is nearly tied on Ant (+1%), and is slightly behind on Walker2D (−3%). On DMC, SPPO beats PPO on all five tasks; on Atari, SPPO improves on Qbert (+57%) and BeamRider (+8%) but trails PPO on Breakout (−21%) and SpaceInvaders (−2%), consistent with the authors' note that the method is not specifically designed for pixel-based discrete-action settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPPO extends the PPO framework by introducing three latent-space regularizers that operate between nonlinear activations and the value/policy heads, rather than on raw state features or pre-activation vectors as in PFO (Moalla et al., 2024) or PEER (He et al., 2023). The CKA-based critic constraint repurposes a representation-similarity metric from deep learning (Kornblith et al., 2019) as a training-time regularizer, which is a novel application distinct from the L2 or cosine-similarity penalties used in CHAIN and PEER. The no-flip regularizer differs from prior policy-drift constraints by enforcing directional consistency in Mahalanobis geometry rather than bounding magnitude of change, and the KDE-driven exploration operates in the critic's learned latent space rather than the raw state space, aligning novelty signals with value semantics. The paper positions itself as complementary to existing PPO stabilization methods, with ablations showing that the three components are synergistic rather than merely additive.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, drop-in stabilization scheme for PPO that preserves the standard clipped objective and network architecture, making it easy to adopt in existing RL pipelines. The key practical finding is that KDE-based exploration shaping only yields meaningful gains when both the actor and critic are simultaneously stabilized, suggesting that exploration bonuses can be destabilizing if the value function is not sufficiently anchored. Limitations include a 40–50% runtime overhead from KDE computation, mixed results on discrete Atari tasks (Breakout and SpaceInvaders actually degrade), and the theoretical analysis being explicitly local/first-order rather than a global characterization of deep nonlinear training dynamics. Open questions include whether the causal links among representation geometry, policy churn, and environment dynamics can be more tightly characterized, and whether KDE in a lower-dimensional value-semantic subspace could reduce the computational cost.
