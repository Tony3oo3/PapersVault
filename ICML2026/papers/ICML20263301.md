## Problem

Inference-time watermarks for autoregressive models (e.g., KGW) degrade severely in continuous modalities like audio because re-encoding a generated waveform does not recover the original token sequence (retokenization mismatch). Existing fixes require finetuning the codec toward idempotence, which is computationally expensive and demands white-box access, negating the training-free advantage of token-level watermarking. This paper asks whether the structural redundancy in the codec vocabulary can be exploited to make the watermark robust without any gradient-based training.

## Input / Output

At generation time, the method takes an audio prompt (conversational audio, LibriSpeech excerpts) or text prompt (music captions, TTS text) and produces a watermarked waveform by applying a cluster-level logit bias during autoregressive sampling. A one-time offline preprocessing step requires passing a calibration audio dataset through the codec's encoder-decoder pair twice to build a token confusion matrix. At detection time, the waveform is re-encoded by the codec and a statistical hypothesis test over the count of green-cluster tokens yields a p-value; the watermark is applied independently per RVQ channel with channel-specific clustering hyperparameters, and the final p-value aggregates the multi-channel z-statistic.

## Method

The key insight is that retokenization errors are not random but exhibit structural consistency: tokens are predominantly confused with a small set of semantic neighbors. The method builds a weighted graph whose vertices are codec tokens and whose edge weights are empirical confusion counts from the calibration set, then applies Leiden community detection to partition the vocabulary into robust clusters. The KGW watermark is then applied at the cluster level: the cluster vocabulary is shuffled and split into green/red lists, and all tokens belonging to a green cluster receive the logit bias δ; for h-gram context keys, token indices are replaced by cluster indices before hashing. This replaces the per-token match rate r with the cluster match rate r_cl > r in the detection statistic, mitigating the exponential decay r^(h+1) that causes the watermark signal to vanish. The entire pipeline is gradient-free and requires only black-box access to the codec, with per-channel hyperparameters (resolution ρ and noise threshold m) selected via a small grid search on a validation set.

## Results

On the Moshi model with conversational audio (h=0), the proposed method achieves a mean −log(p) of 42.47 at identity, compared to 8.51 for plain KGW (Base), 17.44 for WMAR, and 13.72 for WMAR(aug) — a roughly 5× improvement over the strongest finetuned baseline. In the text-to-speech extension, the method yields p-values of 4.89×10⁻¹⁴ (CosyVoice3) and 5.47×10⁻¹⁸ (Spark-TTS), versus 0.034 and 2.06×10⁻⁹ for the KGW baseline respectively. Audio quality is preserved: FAD (VGGish) for Moshi conversational audio at h=0 is 0.133 (Ours) vs. 0.080 (unwatermarked), while NISQA MOS remains at 3.53 vs. 3.54 for the unwatermarked baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three baselines: plain KGW applied to raw tokens (Base), and two variants of WMAR that finetune the codec toward idempotence (WMAR and WMAR(aug)). On Moshi with conversational audio at identity, the proposed method achieves −log(p)=42.47, outperforming Base (8.51) by ~5×, WMAR (17.44) by ~2.4×, and WMAR(aug) (13.72) by ~3.1×. Under robustness attacks (e.g., MP3 compression), the gap persists: Ours retains −log(p)=41.26 while Base drops to 7.47 and WMAR to 15.31. On audio quality (Table 1), the proposed method matches or slightly exceeds the unwatermarked baseline on FAD and MOS, whereas WMAR and WMAR(aug) show noticeable degradation (e.g., FAD VGGish rises from 0.080 to 0.407 for WMAR at h=0). In the TTS setting (Table 4), the method achieves 10–18 orders of magnitude lower p-values than the KGW baseline, confirming that the clustering-based vocabulary reduction generalizes across codec architectures and generation paradigms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the KGW statistical watermarking scheme (Kirchenbauer et al., 2023) from text to autoregressive audio generation, departing from prior continuous-modality approaches (Tong et al., 2025; Jovanović et al., 2025; Wu et al., 2025a,b) that require expensive codec finetuning toward idempotence. The key conceptual departure is the observation that retokenization errors are not random noise but exhibit structural consistency—tokens are confused primarily with semantic neighbors—enabling a gradient-free vocabulary reduction via Leiden community detection on a confusion graph. This positions the work as a lightweight, training-free alternative within the token-level watermarking paradigm, distinct from both finetuning-based methods and post-hoc waveform embedding approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, zero-training pathway to robust audio provenance that avoids the white-box access and computational cost of codec finetuning, making it immediately deployable by model providers. The theoretical characterization of the exponential signal decay (r^(h+1)) under retokenization corruption is a useful diagnostic tool for the broader token-watermarking literature. Key limitations remain: temporal modifications (cropping, speed changes) inherently break token-level alignment, and the method requires a one-time black-box profiling pass per codec. Whether the community-detection approach transfers to image codebooks with different utilization patterns and larger vocabularies is left open.
