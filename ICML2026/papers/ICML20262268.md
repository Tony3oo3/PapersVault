## Problem

Even the exact Bayesian posterior is often meaningless under model misspecification: the Bernstein-von Mises theorem guarantees posterior collapse to a point mass, discarding useful uncertainty. Standard variational inference inherits this limitation since it merely approximates the (potentially wrong) Bayesian posterior. The paper asks whether one can instead directly learn a posterior whose predictive distribution best fits the data, without ever targeting the Bayesian posterior.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262268/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes observed data y_1,...,y_n, a (potentially misspecified) parametric model p(y|θ), a prior p^prior(θ), a chosen proper scoring rule S (log, quadratic, interval, or CRPS), and a variational family parameterized by φ (typically a normalizing flow, but also mean-field Gaussians for scalability). It outputs a variational posterior q_φ(θ) that is generally not an approximation to the Bayesian posterior even asymptotically, along with the induced posterior predictive distribution and an implicit population-level parameter distribution. In likelihood-free settings, the only requirement is a differentiable simulator for drawing y^sim from p(y|θ), eliminating the need for density evaluations entirely.

## Method

PVI replaces the standard VI objective (minimizing KL(q‖p(θ|y))) with maximizing the empirical scoring rule of the posterior predictive ∫p(y|θ)q_φ(θ)dθ against observed data, minus a tunable regularizer toward the prior or posterior. The critical structural difference from generalized VI is the position of the log/integral: PVI scores the globally averaged predictive distribution, while generalized VI scores the posterior-averaged per-observation likelihood. For optimization, the paper derives stochastic gradient estimators for four scoring rules, including a novel unbiased rejection-sampling gradient for the log score that remains unbiased at any batch size (unlike the reparameterization gradient, which is only asymptotically unbiased). A regularization term −λr(φ) allows continuous interpolation between pure predictive optimization (λ=0) and full Bayesian inference (λ→∞). Theoretically, PVI is shown to be consistent and asymptotically normal, and under misspecification it converges to the true population distribution of parameters rather than a point mass, effectively performing an implicit hierarchical expansion at the cost of a single variational family.

## Results

On the 7-model Posteriordb benchmark (diagonal covariance), PVI-Log consistently outperforms vanilla VI on held-out log-likelihood: e.g., on the kidscore model, PVI-Log achieves −369.91 vs. VI's −590.67 (a 220-point improvement), and on the earnings model, −288.69 vs. −298.12. PVI-CRPS similarly dominates on CRPS (lower is better): on kidscore, 875.74 vs. VI's 4481.15; on earnings, 109.55 vs. 111.51. In the likelihood-free regression experiment (Figure 8), PVI-CRPS maintains robust test CRPS across all misspecification levels (α from 0 to 1), while NPE and NRE degrade substantially as misspecification increases. The cryoEM experiment shows PVI-CRPS recovers the true bimodal population distribution of protein bond angles with a two-sample KS gap that shrinks with sample size, while the exact Bayes posterior collapses to a point mass.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla VI (Blei et al., 2017), PVI-Log wins on held-out log-likelihood across 6 of 7 Posteriordb models, with the largest gain on kidscore (−369.91 vs. −590.67, a 37% relative improvement) and glmm (−1150.21 vs. −1159.49). PVI-CRPS dominates VI on CRPS for all models where both are reported (e.g., kidscore: 875.74 vs. 4481.15; earnings: 109.55 vs. 111.51). Against NPE (Papamakarios & Murray, 2016) and NRE (Hermans et al., 2020) in the likelihood-free regression (Figure 8), PVI-CRPS maintains stable test CRPS across all misspecification levels α ∈ [0,1], while NRE degrades to NPE-level performance under strong misspecification (α→1), consistent with Cannon et al. (2022). Against NUTS (Hoffman & Gelman, 2014), the paper notes no qualitative performance jump from VI to NUTS, implying PVI's gains over VI also apply over the exact posterior. The one case where PVI underperforms is the nes model with IS scoring (PVI-IS: −226.56 log-likelihood vs. VI's −185.71), suggesting that the interval score can over-regularize in well-specified settings with small sample sizes.

## Relation to Prior Work

PVI repositions variational inference from approximating the Bayesian posterior to directly optimizing the posterior predictive distribution against the true data-generating process, using proper scoring rules (Gneiting & Raftery, 2007) as the optimization criterion. The log-score PVI with prior regularization coincides with PACmBayes (Morningstar et al., 2022), positioning PVI as a generalization to arbitrary scoring rules. The paper explicitly contrasts PVI with generalized VI (Knoblauch et al., 2022) and generalized Bayes (Bissiri et al., 2016), noting a key structural difference: PVI evaluates the global predictive performance of the posterior-averaged distribution (log outside the integral), while generalized VI assesses posterior-averaged local performance (log inside). Concurrent work by Shen et al. (2025) and McLatchie et al. (2025) develop related MMD-based and pseudo-posterior approaches, but PVI focuses on tractable parametric variational families with a general recipe for multiple scoring rules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PVI provides a principled, unified framework for predictive inference under model misspecification that simultaneously serves as a diagnostic tool (non-vanishing posterior variance signals misspecification and guides hierarchical model expansion) and an implicit hierarchical Bayes method. The CRPS-based variant enables likelihood-free simulation-based inference without requiring likelihood evaluations, which is practically important for complex simulators like cryoEM. Key limitations include the IID/exchangeability assumption, a slower contraction rate than the parametric rate even under correct specification, and the computational cost of probability-space optimization that limits scalability to high-dimensional models. Open questions include finite-sample convergence rates under different scoring rules, extending to non-IID data structures, and developing structured variational families to balance model and inference complexity.
