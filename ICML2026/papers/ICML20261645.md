## Problem

Text-dependent vision-language models for affective understanding face a fundamental mismatch: discrete linguistic templates cannot represent continuous valence-arousal regression, brittle prompts fail to capture fine-grained intra-expression diversity, and fine-tuning large VLMs is computationally expensive while weakening transferability. The paper argues that this text-mediated paradigm also diverges from cognitive psychology, which grounds affective perception in perceptual resemblance to internalized visual archetypes rather than linguistic translation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261645/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

AURA consumes frozen CLIP (or DINO) visual embeddings extracted once from cropped facial images or video frames, using global [CLS] features for FER and VA regression and patch-level features (aggregated via a lightweight Transformer) for AU detection. At inference, the pipeline reduces to projecting the embedding into a 32-dimensional archetype space and performing cosine-similarity matching against a fixed set of learned archetypes, yielding expression labels, binary AU scores, continuous VA values, and an interpretable archetype assignment that identifies which visual primitive anchored the prediction.

## Method

AURA replaces text prompts with a self-organizing archetype manifold through three components: a Visual Archetype-Space (VAS) Projector that maps frozen visual features into a compact d=32 subspace with task-specific supervision; a Self-organized Archetype Discovery (SAD) module that initializes an over-complete pool of K_max archetypes and refines them via discriminative nearest-archetype assignment plus adaptive topology regularization (intra-class compactness, inter-class separation, and score-aware attraction/repulsion for continuous tasks); and an Archetype Contextualization module that performs distance-aware neighborhood reasoning over top-R retrieved archetypes to capture transitions and ambiguity. The key mechanism is that archetype density self-organizes according to data complexity—visually diverse categories like Happiness receive 28 archetypes while stereotyped ones like Fear receive only 6—and the effective active set K_stable converges well below K_max, making representational capacity data-driven rather than manually fixed. At inference, the contextualization module is dropped entirely, reducing prediction to a single cosine-similarity lookup against frozen archetypes with negligible overhead.

## Results

AURA achieves state-of-the-art results across all three affective tasks with only 3.30M trainable parameters and 0.26 GFLOPs. On FER, it reaches 94.04% accuracy on RAF-DB, 68.43% on AffectNet-7, and 65.16% on AffectNet-8. For AU detection, it attains 66.9% F1 on DISFA and 67.3% on EmotioNet. For VA regression, it achieves 78.0/70.2/74.1 CCC (V/A/VA) on AffectNet-VA, outperforming all prior methods including CMFR (73.0 CCC-VA) and EMOCA (72.5 CCC-VA).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AURA outperforms all compared baselines across every task and dataset. On FER, it surpasses the strongest prior method MHAN (92.57% on RAF-DB) by +1.47% and S2D (92.57%) by the same margin, while also leading on AffectNet-7 (+0.69% over MHAN) and AffectNet-8 (+0.08% over MHAN). For AU detection, AURA beats MDHR (66.2% on DISFA) by +0.7% and FUXI (65.4% on EmotioNet) by +1.9%. On VA regression, AURA leads CMFR (73.0 CCC-VA) by +1.1 and EMOCA (72.5) by +1.6. Against CLIP-based prompt-learning baselines, AURA shows the largest margins on continuous tasks: it outperforms CoOp by +29.6 CCC-VA points (74.1 vs 44.5) and CoCoOp by +28.7, while also surpassing CLIPER by +2.15% on RAF-DB with 45× fewer parameters (3.30M vs 149.25M) and 400× fewer FLOPs (0.26G vs 103.7G). The encoder-agnostic experiment (Table 2) further confirms that AURA's gains are not CLIP-specific: with a frozen DINO backbone, AURA still outperforms fully fine-tuned DINO by +4.3% on RAF-DB and +6.6 CCC on AffectNet-VA.

## Relation to Prior Work

AURA departs from the dominant text-dependent VLM paradigm (CLIP, CoOp, CoCoOp, CLIPER) that uses linguistic prompts as an intermediate proxy for affective understanding, arguing that discrete text templates are fundamentally incompatible with continuous valence-arousal regression and brittle for fine-grained visual cues. It also distinguishes itself from conventional prototype and clustering methods (Prototypical Networks, Deep Clustering) by replacing fixed-count, centrally-averaged representatives with adaptive-density, locally-anchored visual archetypes that self-organize according to data complexity. Conceptually, the framework draws on cognitive psychology literature on perceptual archetypes (Rosch, Fehr & Russell, Barsalou) to motivate a geometry-preserving, prompt-free alternative, while technically building on frozen CLIP/DINO features with lightweight trainable projectors and cosine-similarity readout.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AURA offers a unified, interpretable framework that handles FER, AU detection, and VA regression with a single mechanism (archetype matching) at very low computational cost (3.3M params, 0.26G FLOPs), which is practically significant for deployment in resource-constrained affective computing settings. The adaptive density allocation and dataset-diagnostic capabilities (flagging annotation inconsistencies, decomposing coarse labels into fine-grained visual modes) provide tools beyond pure accuracy that could influence how affective taxonomies are designed and audited. Open questions include whether the archetype mechanism extends to temporal/video-based affect, whether interpretability claims hold under truly in-the-wild distribution shifts, and whether the frozen-encoder assumption limits ceiling performance on very fine-grained or compound affective states.
