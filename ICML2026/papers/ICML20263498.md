## Problem

Multi-image spatial reasoning requires integrating partial observations across multiple viewpoints, yet current MLLMs struggle to reliably aggregate information across views. Cognitive science identifies two key human mechanisms—cross-view correspondence and stepwise viewpoint transformation—but existing methods incorporate these only partially and implicitly, without explicit, joint supervision for both within a unified learning objective.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263498/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives N RGB images of the same static scene from different viewpoints plus a natural language question; during training, camera intrinsics, poses, and depth maps are available solely for constructing supervision signals and are never fed to the model. The output is a structured Action-then-Answer sequence: a JSON block containing viewpoint-transition actions (atomic camera operations like turn_right_deg, move_forward_m) for every unordered image pair, followed by the final answer. At inference, no geometric metadata is required.

## Method

HATCH applies two complementary objectives sequentially to a Qwen2.5-VL-3B backbone. First, Patch-Level Spatial Alignment (PaStA) trains only the image encoder to align patch features across views for spatially corresponding 3D locations, using a geometry-derived soft correspondence target (computed from depth-projected patch overlaps) and minimizing cross-entropy against cosine-similarity-based predicted correspondences. Second, Action-then-Answer Reasoning (ActoR) requires the model to generate explicit viewpoint-transition actions in JSON before answering, trained via cold-start SFT followed by GRPO with three verifiable rewards: geometric action accuracy (exponential decay of translation/rotation error), answer correctness, and format validity. The staged recipe—PaStA (encoder-only) → QA SFT → ActoR cold-start SFT → GRPO—teaches the model 'how to look' before 'how to move,' and ablations confirm both components contribute complementary gains.

## Results

HATCH (built on Qwen2.5-VL-3B) achieves 53.6% average accuracy on SPAR-Bench-MV (+28.7 over the base model), 50.2% on MindCube-Tiny (+12.4), and 27.0% on MMSI-Bench (+1.4). It is the best-performing Qwen2.5-VL-3B-based model on all three benchmarks and outperforms larger open-weight models (Qwen2.5-VL-32B/72B, 7B-based spatial models) on SPAR-Bench-MV and MindCube-Tiny. On SPAR-Bench-MV, HATCH (53.6%) matches GPT-5.2 (52.6%), while on MMSI-Bench a larger gap remains versus proprietary models (27.0% vs. 42.0% for GPT-5.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HATCH substantially outperforms all Qwen2.5-VL-3B-based spatial reasoning baselines: SpatialLadder-3B (35.8→53.6 on SPAR-Bench-MV, +17.8) and Spatial-MLLM-4B (30.4→53.6, +23.2), with similar margins on MindCube-Tiny. It also beats the 7B-based spatial models SpaceR-7B (39.1) and Video-R1 (36.5) on SPAR-Bench-MV by 14.5 and 17.1 points respectively, and surpasses Qwen2.5-VL-32B (34.7) and Qwen2.5-VL-72B (35.4) on both SPAR-Bench-MV and MindCube-Tiny. Against proprietary models, HATCH matches GPT-5.2 on SPAR-Bench-MV (53.6 vs. 52.6) and beats GPT-4.1 (39.9) and Gemini-3-Pro (43.1), but trails GPT-5.2 on MindCube-Tiny (50.2 vs. 58.4) and MMSI-Bench (27.0 vs. 42.0), where the gap is largest. On MMSI-Bench specifically, HATCH (27.0%) underperforms Video-R1 (28.0%) and Qwen2.5-VL-72B (31.8%), indicating that the action-based reasoning formulation is less effective for motion and multi-step reasoning categories where most models perform near chance level.

## Relation to Prior Work

HATCH synthesizes two previously separate research threads—cross-view feature alignment (inspired by CroCo's cross-view completion and dense self-supervised objectives) and GRPO-based spatial reasoning with verifiable rewards (as in MindCube and SpatialLadder)—into a unified training framework. Prior multi-image spatial reasoning methods address either correspondence or viewpoint transformation implicitly through architecture or large-scale fine-tuning, but none explicitly supervise both mechanisms within a single learning objective. The paper carves new ground by grounding both components in cognitive science (cross-view correspondence and stepwise viewpoint transformation) and applying them sequentially: PaStA tunes only the vision encoder for patch-level alignment, then ActoR trains the full model to generate explicit camera-transition actions before answering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that a 3B model with structured multi-stage training can match or exceed 72B open-weight models on multi-image spatial reasoning, suggesting that explicit geometric supervision and action-based intermediate reasoning are more effective than raw parameter scale for this task. Key limitations include the reliance on camera intrinsics, poses, and depth maps during training (limiting applicability to in-the-wild data) and the static-scene assumption that breaks down for dynamic or temporally inconsistent views. Open questions include whether automatically estimated geometry can replace ground-truth annotations, whether hybrid action-plus-language reasoning can further improve performance, and whether the approach extends to dynamic scenes with moving objects.
