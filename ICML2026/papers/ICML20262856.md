## Problem

Vision-Language-Action models for robotics face a threefold barrier: data collection via teleoperation is prohibitively expensive and limited to lab settings, network architectures struggle to simultaneously preserve discrete VLM knowledge and model multimodal continuous actions at real-time speeds, and cross-embodiment transfer to novel robotic platforms still requires hundreds of hours of fine-tuning. No prior model achieved zero-shot deployment on unseen embodiments for open-vocabulary manipulation tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262856/cropped_page2_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes stereo RGB images (1440×1080 at 30 Hz from Hikrobot industrial cameras in eye-in-hand configuration), a free-form natural language instruction, and a 14-dimensional proprioceptive state (mapped from the original representation to maintain consistency across Franka FR3 and UR5e platforms). It outputs a continuous action chunk of 32 timesteps (T_a=32) in a 32-dimensional action space encoding bimanual end-effector poses and gripper widths. Training data is collected via a redesigned embodiment-agnostic handheld UMI device, so the same policy transfers zero-shot to physically consistent robotic grippers without per-robot adaptation.

## Method

RDT2 employs a three-stage training pipeline on a 7B Qwen2.5-VL backbone. Stage 1 discretizes continuous action chunks into compact tokens via a 1D CNN encoder with Residual Vector Quantization (RVQ), then trains the VLM with standard cross-entropy loss to preserve pretrained discrete knowledge while accelerating convergence. Stage 2 freezes the backbone and trains a 400M-parameter action expert (a GQA-optimized RDT-1B variant) with conditional flow-matching to generate continuous actions, leveraging cross-attention to VLM latent features at each layer. Stage 3 distills the 5-step flow-matching expert into a single-step generator via on-the-fly regression distillation, achieving inference speeds faster than much smaller models. The entire system is trained on 10,000+ hours of UMI data collected with redesigned CNC-machined hardware (infrared tracking, linkage gripper) across 100+ household environments, enabling zero-shot generalization across unseen objects, scenes, instructions, and embodiments.

## Results

On fine-tuning benchmarks, RDT2 achieves 77% success rate on cloth folding (vs. 36% for π0.5 and 29% for π0-FAST), 45% on unzipping (vs. 13% and 8%), and a 0.58 progress score on table bussing (vs. 0.39 and 0.30). In dynamic tasks, RDT2-UltraFast achieves a +97 ms reaction time on button pressing (vs. +323 ms and +981 ms) and an 88% table tennis hit rate at 1× speed (vs. 78% for π0.5; π0-FAST could not produce a fast-enough policy). Zero-shot, RDT2 generalizes across unseen embodiments, objects, scenes, and instructions simultaneously, and scaling experiments confirm a predictable power-law relationship L(N,D) = E + A/N^α + B/D^β (α≈0.44, β≈0.23).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RDT2 outperforms both baselines across every evaluated task category. Against π0.5, RDT2 more than doubles the cloth folding success rate (77% vs. 36%), nearly triples the unzipping rate (45% vs. 13%), and achieves a 4× higher success rate on unseen objects in cloth folding (51% vs. 15%). Against π0-FAST, the margins are even wider: RDT2's button-pressing reaction time is +97 ms versus +981 ms, and π0-FAST was entirely unable to produce a policy fast enough for table tennis. In the table tennis dynamic task, RDT2 maintains a 68% hit rate even at 2× speed while π0.5 drops to 56%, demonstrating the benefit of the Stage 3 one-step distillation for latency-sensitive control. The zero-shot '4U' experiments (unseen embodiment, scene, object, instruction) have no direct baseline comparison, as prior VLAs require fine-tuning for cross-embodiment transfer, making RDT2's zero-shot deployment a qualitative first.

## Relation to Prior Work

RDT2 extends the UMI (Chi et al., 2024) paradigm for embodiment-agnostic data collection by redesigning the hardware for industrial-scale reliability and scaling the resulting dataset to over 10,000 hours—orders of magnitude beyond prior UMI datasets. Architecturally, it builds on RDT-1B's diffusion-based action expert and the flow-matching formulation from π0/π0.5, but introduces a three-stage training pipeline (RVQ-based autoregressive pretraining → flow-matching action expert → one-step distillation) that explicitly reconciles the discrete token space of pretrained VLMs with continuous motor control. The key departure from prior VLA models (OpenVLA, π0, π0.5) is the elimination of robot-specific training data entirely: RDT2 is pretrained solely on human-collected UMI demonstrations and vision-language pairs, enabling zero-shot transfer to novel robotic embodiments without fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is one of the first demonstrations that a large VLA model trained exclusively on human-collected, embodiment-agnostic data can achieve simultaneous zero-shot generalization across unseen objects, scenes, instructions, and robotic platforms—a combination previously unattainable without task-specific fine-tuning. The verified scaling law (predictable, consistent gains from both data and parameter scale) provides a clear roadmap for the field: the bottleneck is data collection infrastructure, not model architecture. However, zero-shot success rates remain modest, the approach requires matching camera and gripper hardware across embodiments, and the 10,000-hour dataset, while large, is still far below internet-scale. Open questions include whether the scaling trend continues at 100K+ hours, whether the UMI paradigm extends to dexterous hands or humanoids, and whether the three-stage recipe generalizes to other VLM backbones or action spaces.
