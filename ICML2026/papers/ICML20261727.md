## Problem

Standard softmax attention scales quadratically with sequence length, making long-context training and inference prohibitively expensive. Existing linear-time alternatives either restrict which token pairs interact (sparse patterns, low-rank projections) or replace the softmax mixing operator entirely (kernel linearization, state-space layers), but few provide layer-level approximation guarantees that remain faithful to the scaled dot-product softmax interface. PLASH addresses this gap by delivering a provably linear-time attention block that preserves the exact softmax readout and emits a per-input, forward-pass-computable deviation certificate bounding its output's distance from standard attention.

## Input / Output

PLASH accepts the standard (Q, K, V) triple with Q ∈ R^{Nq×dk}, K ∈ R^{Nk×dk}, V ∈ R^{Nk×dv} and returns Y_PLASH ∈ R^{Nq×dv}, making it a drop-in replacement for the attention block in both self- and cross-attention settings. In addition to the attention output, the forward pass produces a per-input deviation certificate: an upper bound on ‖Y_soft − Y_PLASH‖_F computed entirely from intermediate quantities (sketch errors, routing weights, projection norms) without ever forming the Nq×Nk logit matrix. Given a task tolerance ε_out, the certificate either certifies the output as within ε_out of standard attention or flags the input for fallback to full quadratic attention.

## Method

PLASH decomposes attention into three stages that localize all approximation to a single randomized step. Stage I compresses (K, V) from length Nk to M ≪ Nk rows via soft routing against M learnable prototypes P ∈ R^{M×dk} (temperature-scaled softmax over K·P^T), yielding cluster-mean keys and values. Stage II concatenates the compressed (K̃, Ṽ), applies a stabilized row normalization, then enriches each of the M items with TensorSketch-based polynomial feature sketches at selected degrees K ⊆ {1,2} and per-degree sketch sizes {Dk}, followed by a short L_mix-layer Transformer mixer across the M items. Stage III projects the mixed representation back to (Kg, Vg) and computes the output via exact scaled dot-product softmax attention from Q to (Kg, Vg), preserving the standard attention interface. Because randomness enters only through the TensorSketch hash/sign seeds in Stage II, the paper proves a high-probability (ε, δ) end-to-end bound (Theorem 3.2) that decomposes into a deterministic Stage I compression error, a deterministic Stage II reference bias, and a Stage II sketching error propagated through the deterministic mixer, projections, and softmax readout. The two primary hyperparameters—compressed length M and sketch sizes {Dk}—trade compression fidelity against approximation accuracy, while the normalization temperature τ_g acts as a deterministic knob that monotonically tightens the certificate without altering arithmetic cost.

## Results

On PG-19 with Qwen3-4B at N=16,384, PLASH (M=16) achieves the lowest EMA training loss of 23.5, beating Standard FlashAttention-2 (25.2) by 6.7% and the next-best baselines Local and Qwen3 GQA (both 24.4) by 3.8%. At N=32,768, PLASH (M=32) ties Standard at 40.1 while all efficient baselines trail (Local 42.7, Longformer 45.0). On time-series forecasting, PLASH (M=512, D_k=128) sets the best Weather score (MSE 0.3009, MAE 0.3492) and the best ETTm1 score (MSE 0.2896, MAE 0.3550 at M=512, D_k=256). The per-input deviation certificate of Theorem 3.2 holds on 100% of 35,058 real test inputs across all four forecasting datasets with zero violations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Standard FlashAttention-2, PLASH (M=16) wins on PG-19 at N=16K (23.5 vs 25.2 EMA loss) and ties at N=32K (40.1 vs 40.1), while running 2.4× faster per layer at 32K (1809 ms vs 4358 ms). PLASH (M=16) also beats Qwen3 GQA (23.5 vs 24.4 at 16K; 40.1 vs 43.6 at 32K) and Local sparse attention (23.5 vs 24.4 at 16K; 40.1 vs 42.7 at 32K). Longformer and ABC are clearly outperformed on both PG-19 lengths (e.g., 23.5 vs 25.5 and 35.1 at 16K). On time-series forecasting, PLASH (M=512, D_k=128) beats Performer on Weather (MSE 0.3009 vs 0.3075) and S4D on Weather (0.3009 vs 0.3129) and ETTm1 (0.3057 vs 0.3508), but LongShort retains the best ETTh1 (MSE 0.4125 vs PLASH's 0.4722) and ETTh2 (0.5683 vs 0.5987) scores, while Vanilla attention remains best on ECL (0.2370 vs PLASH's 0.2435 at M=128, D_k=64). Nystromformer, LARA, and CosFormer are consistently outperformed by PLASH across all five forecasting datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

PLASH occupies a distinct position between two established families of efficient attention: deterministic compression methods (Linformer, Nyströmformer, ABC) that project (K,V) onto a low-rank subspace, and random-feature kernel methods (Performer, PolySketchFormer) that replace softmax with a randomized kernel estimator. Its key architectural departure is localizing all randomness to a single TensorSketch enrichment stage applied after deterministic prototype compression, while preserving an exact scaled dot-product softmax readout from Q to the enriched prototypes. This three-stage decomposition (deterministic compress → randomized enrich → exact readout) is what enables the per-input forward-pass deviation certificate, a guarantee type absent from both prior families, which offer only population-level variance or spectral bounds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The per-input certificate is the most practically distinctive contribution: at inference time, a practitioner can verify on each forward pass whether PLASH's output deviates from standard softmax attention by more than a chosen tolerance, without ground-truth labels, and fall back to full attention when the bound is violated. This turns an approximation method into a certifiable one, which is relevant for safety-critical or auditable deployments. Open questions include whether the sufficient condition is tight in practice (the 100% certification rate on 35K forecasting samples is encouraging but the bound may be conservative on harder inputs), how the method interacts with multi-head and grouped-query attention at scale, and whether the non-monotonic accuracy in M and D_k can be better understood or controlled. The added hyperparameters (M, {D_k}, τ_g, degree set K) also raise practical tuning costs that the paper acknowledges but does not fully resolve.
