## Problem

Existing post-training quantization (PTQ) methods for Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) treat multimodal token sequences as homogeneous, applying uniform or modality-level calibration objectives that misallocate the reconstruction budget to the vast majority of redundant visual tokens while under-penalizing errors on the sparse, decision-critical tokens that drive cross-modal reasoning. Additionally, standard per-token activation quantization is degraded by a small set of stable outlier channels that dominate the dynamic range and collapse resolution for the remaining channels.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a pretrained LVLM (e.g., LLaVA-OneVision-7B, Qwen2.5-VL-7B, InternVL2-8B) and a small calibration set of 256 MSCOCO image-caption pairs. During calibration, it performs forward passes to extract per-token attention scores and redundancy probes (requiring 2N_V additional forward passes per image), then produces a quantized model at W3A16 or W4A8 bit-widths. At inference, the quantized model takes an RGB image and a text prompt and generates a text answer, with no architectural changes to the original LVLM.

## Method

ImpQuant introduces two complementary mechanisms. First, for weight quantization, it computes fine-grained token importance: textual tokens are weighted by aggregated attention scores across all heads, while visual tokens are weighted inversely to a redundancy score combining representational dominance (model confidence when a single token is replicated across all visual positions) and contextual necessity (Jensen-Shannon Divergence from leave-one-out ablation). These importance scores form a diagonal matrix P that reweights the GPTQ-style Hessian (H = XP²Xᵀ), so the per-group second-order quantization with error compensation prioritizes accuracy on decision-critical tokens. Second, for activation quantization, it identifies a layer-wise set of top-1% outlier channels (shown to be input-stable with >0.94 Jaccard similarity) and applies a two-scale quantizer that assigns separate step sizes to outlier and non-outlier channels, preventing extreme values from eroding resolution for subtle features. A theoretical proposition establishes that redundant tokens induce near-zero curvature in the loss landscape, justifying the lower calibration weights assigned to them.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-VL-7B under W3A16, ImpQuant achieves 74.38% average accuracy across six VQA benchmarks, outperforming the best baseline (MBQ, 72.64%) by 1.74 points and the FP16 gap (76.61%) by only 2.23 points. Under the more aggressive W4A8 setting, ImpQuant reaches 73.85% average, a 3.07-point improvement over the strongest baseline (Q-VLM, 70.78%). Gains are most pronounced on text-centric tasks: on OCRBench (W3A16, Qwen2.5-VL-7B), ImpQuant scores 82.00% versus 72.70% for SmoothQuant and 76.50% for MBQ. On the POPE hallucination benchmark, ImpQuant also achieves the highest average F1-score (86.48) and accuracy (87.84) among all W3A16 methods on Qwen2.5-VL-7B, approaching the FP16 level (86.43 / 87.68).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ImpQuant outperforms all four baselines (AWQ, MBQ, Q-VLM, SmoothQuant) across every model and bit-width setting tested. On Qwen2.5-VL-7B W3A16, the average-accuracy margin over the closest competitor MBQ is +1.74 points (74.38 vs. 72.64), while over SmoothQuant it reaches +3.37 points (74.38 vs. 71.01). The advantage widens substantially under W4A8: ImpQuant leads Q-VLM by +3.07 points (73.85 vs. 70.78) and SmoothQuant by +4.09 points (73.85 vs. 69.76). The largest per-task gains appear on OCRBench and TextVQA, where text-centric perception demands preservation of sparse, high-information visual tokens; for instance, on OCRBench (W4A8, Qwen2.5-VL-7B) ImpQuant scores 82.90% versus 73.30% for Q-VLM and 72.20% for SmoothQuant. On the POPE hallucination benchmark, ImpQuant also achieves the best average F1 and accuracy among all quantized methods, suggesting that importance-aware calibration reduces quantization-induced object hallucinations rather than merely improving task accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

ImpQuant extends GPTQ's per-group Hessian-based weight quantization by replacing the standard Hessian with a token-importance-weighted version (Eq. 3), where importance is derived from attention aggregation for text and a two-probe redundancy metric for vision. It departs from modality-agnostic PTQ methods (GPTQ, AWQ, SmoothQuant) that treat LVLMs as homogeneous sequences, and from modality-aware methods (MBQ, Q-VLM) that still operate at the modality or block level, by pushing calibration to individual token granularity. The outlier-aware activation quantization draws on the LLM.int8() observation of stable outlier channels but applies it in a two-scale decomposition specific to the LVLM setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As LVLMs are increasingly deployed in resource-constrained settings, the finding that uniform calibration misallocates reconstruction budget to redundant visual tokens provides a concrete, actionable diagnosis for the accuracy gap between text-only and multimodal quantization. The theoretical link between token redundancy and Hessian spectral degeneracy (Proposition 3.1) gives a principled justification for the reweighting strategy. Open questions include whether the redundancy-based importance metric generalizes to video or multi-frame inputs, whether the approach remains effective at extreme bit-widths (e.g., W2A4), and whether the 2N_V forward-pass probing overhead becomes prohibitive for very large calibration sets or streaming scenarios.
