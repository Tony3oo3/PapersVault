## Problem

RLHF and RLVR pipelines rely on reward signals that are inherently noisy—LLM judges produce false positives, exact-match parsers miss equivalent answers, and reward models carry approximation errors. While this noise is well-known, its interaction with group-based policy optimization methods like GRPO and Dr.GRPO has not been formally characterized, leaving practitioners without a principled correction mechanism at the policy-update level.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263210/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes text prompts (queries) and generates G responses per prompt via the current policy; each response receives a binary reward from a reward model (continuous scores thresholded to {0,1}) or from ground-truth verification. A 20% held-out calibration subset is used to estimate global false-positive and false-negative flip rates (ρ+, ρ−) by deliberately corrupting half the correct answers to balance classes. The output is an optimized LLM policy whose clean accuracy (measured against true ground truth) improves over uncorrected baselines.

## Method

The paper models binary reward corruption as a query-dependent Bernoulli flip channel and proves that noise multiplicatively shrinks the GRPO gradient by a factor (1−ρ+−ρ−)·σ_k/σ̃_k while driving convergence to a strictly worse fixed point. The correction adapts the Natarajan unbiased estimator to RLVR: the noisy reward r̃ is transformed to r̂ = (r̃ − ρ+)/(1−ρ+−ρ−), which is an unbiased estimator of the true reward. A key insight is that linear reparameterization alone is insufficient for GRPO because standardization invariance cancels the shift; the method therefore introduces a generalized normalization M_k(q) (set to 1 for Dr.GRPO, or to √Z_k for GRPO, where Z_k is an unbiased variance estimator that subtracts noise-induced variance from the debiased reward variance). The full algorithm is two-stage: first estimate flip rates from a balanced calibration set, then apply the debiased advantage Â_i = r̂_i − (1/G)Σr̂_j with the appropriate M_k normalization in the policy gradient update, yielding exactly unbiased gradients for Dr.GRPO and consistent plug-in gradients for GRPO.

## Results

On GSM8K with realistic reward models (Dr.GRPO), the noise-corrected method achieves up to 59.21% clean accuracy for Llama-3.2-1B with AceMath-7B-RM, a +6.7 percentage point gain over the uncorrected baseline (52.52%). For Qwen3-0.6B, gains range from +2.3 to +2.3 points across two reward models. On APPS (code generation) with the highly noisy RM-Mistral-7B (ρ⁺=0.53), the method yields a +1.5 point improvement for Qwen3-0.6B (9.01% → 10.50%). Under synthetic noise on GSM8K, the correction recovers or even exceeds noiseless performance, with Llama-3.2-1B reaching 71.04% under (0.1, 0.2) noise versus 59.84% without correction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its noise-corrected method against two baselines: standard GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) and standard Dr.GRPO (Liu et al., 2025), both applied without any noise correction. The method consistently outperforms both baselines across all experimental settings. In the realistic reward-model setting on GSM8K (Dr.GRPO), the correction yields gains of +2.3 to +6.7 percentage points over uncorrected Dr.GRPO, with the largest gain (52.52% → 59.21%) for Llama-3.2-1B paired with AceMath-7B-RM. For GRPO on GSM8K, the full correction (Natarajan + variance adjustment Z) achieves 79.12% for Qwen3-0.6B with Llama3.1-8B-ORM, surpassing both the uncorrected baseline (76.47%) and the mean-correction-only variant (72.82%), confirming that both the debiasing and variance-normalization components are necessary. On APPS code generation, gains are more modest (+1.5 points for Qwen3-0.6B) due to the extremely high false-positive rate (ρ⁺=0.53) of the code reward model, but the method still wins. Under synthetic noise, the advantage is even more pronounced: Llama-3.2-1B improves from 59.84% to 71.04% (+11.2 points) under (0.1, 0.2) flip noise, and in some cases (e.g., Llama-3.2-1B at light noise) the corrected model exceeds the noiseless baseline, suggesting a regularization benefit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) and its corrected variant Dr.GRPO (Liu et al., 2025) by explicitly modeling reward corruption as a Bernoulli flip channel and applying the Natarajan et al. (2013) unbiased label-noise correction to the policy gradient. The theoretical backbone—policy improvement bounds and success-probability recursions—is inherited from Mroueh et al. (2025) and extended to the noisy setting. Unlike prior robustness work in RLHF (Yan et al., 2024; Bukharin et al., 2024; Shen et al.) that addresses reward-model uncertainty upstream, this paper targets the policy-update level, showing that group-based normalization interacts non-trivially with reward noise in a way that simple linear debiasing cannot fix.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first formal characterization of how Bernoulli reward noise degrades GRPO/Dr.GRPO (gradient attenuation by 1−ρ⁺−ρ⁻, strictly worse fixed points) and a practical two-stage algorithm that recovers unbiased gradients with a sample-complexity guarantee for flip-rate estimation. Limitations include the binary-reward assumption, global (non-query-dependent) flip rates, and evaluation only on small models (0.6B–4B), leaving open whether the correction scales to production-size LLMs or continuous reward channels. The observation that correction can exceed the noiseless baseline under light noise hints at a regularization effect worth further investigation, and the flattening of the difficulty-bias penalty under noise suggests a noise-aware regularization schedule as a natural next step.
