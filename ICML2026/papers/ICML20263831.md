## Problem

Dirac-Frenkel instantaneous residual minimization, the standard local-in-time paradigm for evolving neural-network parametrizations of PDE solutions, yields non-unique or ill-conditioned parameter dynamics whenever the parametrization Jacobian is rank-deficient or poorly conditioned. Standard remedies such as Tikhonov regularization introduce bias that degrades accuracy, while alternatives like randomization or periodic retraining add cost or lack a clear continuous-time interpretation. The paper asks how to resolve the parameter-level non-uniqueness without sacrificing the Galerkin-optimal residual-minimization property.

## Input / Output

At each time step the method assembles a batch Jacobian J(θ) ∈ ℝ^{N×p} and right-hand side f(θ) ∈ ℝ^N from N collocation points (uniformly or adaptively sampled in the spatial domain), then solves a tSVD-regularized least-squares subproblem to obtain the minimal-norm Dirac-Frenkel reference velocity. The output is the full parameter trajectory θ(t) ∈ ℝ^p defining the solution u(t,x) = û(θ(t),·), together with the auxiliary momentum variable m(t) that is carried forward as state. The scheme is semi-implicit: backward Euler for the momentum (reducing to an exponential moving average) and forward Euler or RK4 for the parameters, so no additional linear solve is needed beyond the tSVD already required by the DF step.

## Method

The key insight is to interpret the nullspace of the Gram matrix G(θ) as a gauge freedom: any vector in null(G(θ)) can be added to a Dirac-Frenkel reference velocity without changing the represented function derivative or violating instantaneous residual minimization. The paper introduces a history variable m(t) governed by Onsager's minimum-dissipation principle, which acts as an exponentially weighted moving average of past minimal-norm DF velocities (a low-pass filtered momentum). At each step, m(t) is projected onto the nullspace of G(θ) via P(θ)m(t) and added to the DF reference velocity with a scaling λ, yielding the DFO update θ̇ = η̄ + λP(θ)m. This injects motion only along gauge directions, preserving the Galerkin-optimal function-space dynamics while selecting a unique, temporally smooth parameter velocity. A theoretical proposition shows that for the exact wave-collision collapse, a specific choice of λ recovers the exact PDE solution through the singularity, and numerical experiments across 1D–5D PDEs (wave equation, advection-reaction, rotating detonation, Vlasov, Fokker-Planck) demonstrate orders-of-magnitude error reduction over DF in singular regimes with negligible computational overhead.

## Results

DFO achieves the lowest average L² error across all three low-dimensional PDE benchmarks: 2.10e-03 on rotating detonation waves, 1.23e-02 on transport through a flow field, and 3.95e-03 on the Vlasov equation (charged particles), with negligible runtime overhead (1× relative to DF). On the 5D Fokker-Planck equation, DFO reduces the mean error to 4.47e-03 and covariance error to 5.26e-02, roughly 3× and 6× lower than DF+tSVD (1.46e-02 and 3.17e-01), respectively. The randomized variant RDFO matches DFO accuracy at half the runtime (0.5×).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DFO outperforms all five baselines on every benchmark. Against DF+tSVD (the standard minimal-norm Dirac-Frenkel with truncated SVD), DFO reduces average L² error by 2.9× on RDW (2.10e-03 vs 6.06e-03), 3.1× on transport (1.23e-02 vs 3.82e-02), and 3.1× on Vlasov (3.95e-03 vs 1.22e-02). Against DF+Tiko (Tikhonov-regularized DF), DFO is 3.0×, 5.2×, and 2.1× lower in average L² error on the three problems, respectively. NIVP (periodic re-training from scratch) is competitive on transport (1.69e-02 vs 1.23e-02) but DFO still wins; on Vlasov, NIVP (8.48e-03) is roughly 2× worse than DFO. RSNG (randomized sparse Neural Galerkin) is the weakest baseline, with errors 2–7× higher than DFO across problems, though it runs faster (0.3–0.5×). TENG (time-evolving natural gradient) is the most expensive (2–3× runtime) yet still 1.6–15× worse than DFO in average L² error, particularly on Vlasov where TENG degrades to 6.01e-02 average and 3.17e-01 final-time error versus DFO's 3.95e-03 and 8.06e-03.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Dirac-Frenkel variational principle (Dirac 1930; Frenkel 1934) for evolving nonlinear PDE parametrizations by importing Onsager's minimum-dissipation principle (Onsager 1931) as a gauge-fixing mechanism. It departs from standard remedies for the well-known non-uniqueness and ill-conditioning of DF parameter dynamics—Tikhonov regularization (Feischl et al. 2024), orthogonality conditions for matrix/tensor parametrizations (Haegeman et al. 2011; Koch & Lubich 2007), randomization (Berman & Peherstorfer 2023), and periodic restarts (Finzi et al. 2023)—by reframing the underdetermination as gauge freedom and injecting a low-pass-filtered momentum variable exclusively along Jacobian-null directions, thereby preserving instantaneous residual minimization without introducing the bias that regularization incurs. The approach is conceptually distinct from natural-gradient-descent-with-momentum methods for PINNs (Müller & Zeinhofer 2023; Goldshlager et al. 2024), which treat the dynamics as an optimization problem rather than a dynamical system to be matched at all times.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a core qualitative failure mode of neural PDE solvers—tangent-space collapse causing permanently stuck dynamics (e.g., colliding waves that never separate)—with a mechanism that is both theoretically clean (gauge-fixing via Onsager momentum) and practically cheap (two extra matrix-vector multiplies per step). Key limitations remain: the method does not cure ill-conditioning in function-relevant directions associated with small-but-nonzero singular values, and the Onsager time-scale τ and momentum strength λ require problem-dependent tuning without a principled selection strategy. Open questions include whether the gauge-fixing template (Eq. 9) admits history-free or adaptive alternatives, how higher-order drift from nullspace injection behaves at finite step sizes, and whether the approach extends to other variational principles beyond Dirac-Frenkel.
