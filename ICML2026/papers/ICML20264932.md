## Problem

Offline imitation learning for visual navigation suffers from distribution shift and compounding errors at rollout, while reinforcement learning demands careful reward engineering and learns inefficiently from sparse rewards. DAgger-style online imitation learning could address both issues, but scaling it to high-dimensional visual diffusion policies has remained computationally prohibitive, leaving a gap between the sample efficiency of imitation and the interactive correction of online learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264932/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The policy consumes ego-view RGB and depth images (encoded via DepthAnythingV2 ViT backbones into 16 fused visual tokens) along with a goal instruction (point, image, pixel, or no-goal) and outputs a 24-step sequence of relative waypoints (Δx, Δy, Δω in the robot-centric frame). A shared critic network additionally produces a scalar safety score per candidate trajectory, used at inference to rank and select the safest sampled plan. Waypoints are converted to velocity commands via MPC and then to joint-level controls by a low-level controller for physical execution.

## Method

NavOL is a multimodal diffusion policy (DiT with DDPM scheduler) paired with a safety critic that shares the visual encoder and DiT backbone, trained in a DAgger-inspired rollout-update loop inside IsaacLab. During rollout, the policy navigates 256 parallel GPU environments while a NavMesh-based global planner with privileged map access computes optimal path segments as expert labels; actions are executed from the policy with probability ρ=0.8 and from the expert with probability 0.2 to stabilize training. During update, the diffusion policy is trained with a standard denoising MSE loss on the freshly collected observation–trajectory pairs, and the critic is supervised with an MSE loss against a safety score derived from obstacle clearance along the planned path. The system leverages IsaacLab's high-fidelity parallel rendering with domain randomization of camera pose, lighting, and start-goal pairs, collecting over 2,000 trajectories per hour on 8 RTX 4090 GPUs across 50 3D-Front scenes, and is initialized from NavDP pretrained weights to provide a strong behavioral prior.

## Results

On the NavOL benchmark (8 held-out 3D-Front scenes), NavOL achieves a mean Success Rate (mSR) of 69.0 and mean Success-weighted Path Length (mSPL) of 63.7, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline NavDP (42.2 / 37.7). On the NavDP benchmark, NavOL reaches mSR 80.4 and mSPL 76.3, edging out NavDP (77.8 / 74.8) and iPlanner (66.8 / 65.1). In zero-shot real-world deployment on a Unitree Go2, NavOL achieves 8/10, 7/10, and 10/10 success in Office, Gym, and Corridor scenarios respectively, versus 4/10, 2/10, and 2/10 for NavDP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NavOL outperforms all four baselines on both simulation benchmarks. On the NavOL benchmark, NavOL (mSR 69.0, mSPL 63.7) beats NavDP (42.2 / 37.7) by +26.8 SR and +26.0 SPL, ViPlanner (33.0 / 31.5) by +36.0 SR, iPlanner (18.7 / 18.4) by +50.3 SR, and DD-PPO (2.9 / 2.4) by a wide margin. On the NavDP benchmark, the gap narrows: NavOL (80.4 / 76.3) leads NavDP (77.8 / 74.8) by +2.6 SR and +1.5 SPL, and iPlanner (66.8 / 65.1) by +13.6 SR; the paper attributes the smaller margin to unfavorable initial configurations in that benchmark. In real-world zero-shot transfer (Unitree Go2, cross-embodiment from Dingo), NavOL doubles or triples NavDP's success rate across all three cluttered scenarios (e.g., 10/10 vs. 2/10 in Corridor), confirming that the online planner-supervised training yields more robust obstacle-avoidance behavior than offline imitation alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

NavOL extends NavDP (Cai et al., 2025), a diffusion-based navigation policy trained entirely offline on synthetic trajectories, by replacing the static dataset with an online rollout-update loop inspired by DAgger (Ross et al., 2011). The key conceptual departure is that a privileged global planner (NavMesh-based) provides stepwise expert supervision during live rollouts in IsaacLab, rather than relying on pre-collected demonstrations. Architecturally, the DiT-based diffusion policy and DepthAnythingV2 visual encoder are inherited from NavDP; the novelty lies in the training paradigm and the parallel simulation infrastructure that makes online fine-tuning of a generative diffusion policy tractable at scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that online imitation with planner supervision can match or exceed offline diffusion policies at a fraction of the compute (16 GPU-days vs. ~1024 GPU-days for NavDP), suggesting a more scalable path to robust navigation policies. Cross-embodiment transfer (Dingo → Unitree Go2) and zero-shot generalization to unseen scenes validate the approach's practical utility. However, the method still depends on a privileged global planner during training, the scene preparation pipeline (BlenderProc + IsaacLab) is non-trivial, and real-world failure modes (scene-edge exploitation, low-obstacle misjudgment) remain unresolved. Open questions include whether the paradigm extends to dynamic environments, multi-agent settings, or language-conditioned navigation without a global map.
