## Problem

In overconstrained sparse logistic regression (n > d), rotation-invariant algorithms such as gradient descent on a single-layer network are provably suboptimal when trained on hard labels (±1) sampled from a sparse target, incurring excess risk Ω((d−1)/n), while non-rotation-invariant methods achieve O(s log d / n). This gap arises purely from the label sampling process—even though the underlying model is noise-free and soft labels would suffice for exact recovery—making it a fundamental limitation of standard optimization symmetries that had not been established for logistic loss.

## Input / Output

The method takes n i.i.d. pairs (x_i, y_i) where x_i ~ N(0, I_d) and y_i ∈ {−1, +1} is sampled from σ(x_i^T w*), with w* an s-sparse unit-norm vector in R^d. The key distinction is between soft-label access (the true conditional probability σ(x_i^T w*)) and hard-label access (a single Bernoulli draw), which fundamentally changes the empirical risk landscape. The output is a weight vector w(t) at a chosen early-stopping time, producing a binary classification score via the logistic link σ(x^T w).

## Method

The paper establishes a lower bound by reducing any rotation-invariant algorithm's performance to the Bayes risk of a rotationally symmetrized observation model (averaging over Haar measure on the orthogonal group), then analyzing the induced posterior over a uniform spherical prior using geometric properties of logistic loss—specifically self-concordance for quadratic lower bounds on excess risk and Cramér-Rao inequalities for posterior anti-concentration. For the upper bound, the weight vector is reparameterized as a Hadamard product w = u ⊙ v (a 'spindly' two-layer diagonal linear network), which breaks rotation invariance because the induced dynamics ẇ = −|w| ⊙ ∇L̂(w) are coordinate-dependent. Stein's lemma yields a state-dependent Riccati ODE system where each coordinate evolves as ẇ_i = (signal + noise)·w_i − a(w)·w_i², coupled through the shared scalar a(w) = E[σ'(x^T w)]. Envelope ODE arguments sandwich the intractable dynamics between closed-form upper and lower Riccati flows, and early stopping at a time proportional to log d / (w_min* · a*) separates active coordinates (concentrating near w_i*) from inactive ones (suppressed to O(1/d)), yielding excess risk O(s log d / n).

## Results

The paper establishes a fundamental rate separation in overconstrained sparse logistic regression (n > d). Rotation-invariant algorithms (e.g., gradient flow on a single-layer network) incur an excess risk lower bound of Ω((d−1)/n), while the spindly (Hadamard-factored) network with early stopping achieves an excess risk upper bound of O(s log d / n), where s is the sparsity level. In numerical experiments (d=50, s=5, n=1000, Gaussian design), the spindly parameterization achieves lower validation error than the single-layer model under early stopping, and the observed excess risk scaling with dimension d is consistent with the theoretical rates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares two algorithmic families: (1) single-layer logistic regression trained by gradient flow (rotation-invariant) and (2) a spindly two-layer diagonal linear network with w_i = u_i·v_i trained by gradient flow (non-rotation-invariant). Theoretically, the spindly network decisively outperforms the single-layer model: it achieves O(s log d / n) excess risk versus the Ω((d−1)/n) lower bound that applies to all rotation-invariant algorithms, a gap that grows with d/s. Empirically (Figures 3–5), the spindly model reaches lower validation loss under early stopping, and its inactive-coordinate weights remain suppressed while active coordinates converge near the oracle, whereas the single-layer model spreads weight mass across all coordinates. The comparison also contextualizes both methods against the MLE rate O(d/n): the single-layer algorithm with early stopping matches the suboptimal MLE rate, while the spindly network matches the minimax sparse rate O(s log(ed/s)/n) up to logarithmic factors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the rotation-invariance lower bound framework of Warmuth et al. (2025), which showed that rotation-invariant algorithms are fooled by additive Gaussian noise on sparse linear targets in the overconstrained regime, to the logistic loss setting where the 'noise' is the inherent randomness of sampling hard labels from a well-specified logistic model. The spindly Hadamard-factored parameterization (w_i = u_i · v_i) and the reduction to a rotationally symmetrized observation model via Haar measure both originate from Warmuth et al. (2021). The key technical departure is that the logistic loss does not admit the Gaussian-to-ridge reduction available for square loss, requiring new tools: geometric analysis of the spherical posterior for the lower bound, and a state-dependent Riccati ODE with envelope arguments for the upper bound.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result establishes that standard neural network training (fully connected input layer + gradient descent) is provably suboptimal for sparse classification even when n > d, with an excess risk gap of Ω((d-1)/n) versus the optimal O(s log d / n) — a separation that persists without any externally added noise. The minimal fix (reparameterizing each weight as a product of two parameters) is practically simple but its adoption in real training pipelines remains unexplored. Open questions include extending the lower bound to anisotropic designs, other exponential family losses (Poisson, Exponential), and whether other non-rotation-invariant algorithms (Lasso, exponentiated gradient) achieve the same sparse rate for logistic regression.
