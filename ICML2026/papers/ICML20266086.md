## Problem

Implicit-field 3D generation methods (SDF, occupancy) rely on isosurfacing to extract meshes, producing dense, irregular triangulations that deviate from artist-crafted topology and cannot handle open or non-manifold surfaces. Explicit mesh generation methods (autoregressive or per-face diffusion) avoid this but hit a computational ceiling: rich geometry yields prohibitively long token sequences, forcing truncated training that fragments surfaces. The paper addresses the gap between scalable implicit generation and topology-faithful explicit mesh synthesis.

## Input / Output

For image-to-3D generation, the model takes an RGB image and a scalar vertex-number condition (log N_v) that controls output mesh complexity; for geometry-conditioned generation, it takes a source geometry as spatial anchor. The VAE encoder consumes 819,200 surface-sampled points, each carrying position, normal, and three displacement vectors to its enclosing face's vertices. The output is an explicit triangular mesh: precise 3D vertex coordinates (instantiated via hierarchical voxel subdivision at 128³ resolution) and a binary edge-connectivity matrix predicted pairwise, with no isosurface extraction step.

## Method

LATO introduces a Vertex Displacement Field (VDF) where each surface point is annotated with displacement vectors to its face's three vertices, providing a dense, continuous signal that encodes both local geometry and vertex connectivity (zero-magnitude displacements localize vertices; discontinuities delineate edges). A sparse voxel VAE compresses these features into T-Voxels (128³, 16 channels) via PointNet + sparse transformer; the decoder progressively subdivides voxels through three stages of 8-way octant splitting with learnable pruning to instantiate vertex positions, while a parallel connection head cross-attends vertex features with T-Voxels and predicts edge existence via an MLP on concatenated pair features (trained with sampled positive/negative pairs and permutation invariance). For generation, a two-stage flow matching pipeline first synthesizes coarse structure voxels (occupancy at 128³ from a 32³ latent, fine-tuned from TRELLIS) and then generates per-voxel T-Voxel topology features conditioned on vertex count, enabling parallel, complexity-decoupled inference in 3–10 seconds.

## Results

On geometry-conditioned mesh generation (Table 2), LATO achieves the best CD(L2) of 0.043/0.044 (dense/artistic), HD of 0.084/0.081, and Normal Consistency of 0.832/0.835, outperforming the strongest autoregressive baseline DeepMesh (0.051/0.046, 0.092/0.089, 0.828/0.827) across all metrics. In VAE reconstruction (Table 1), LATO matches MeshGPT on CD(L2) (0.042) while substantially outperforming MeshCraft (0.150/0.243) and achieving the highest NC (0.847/0.834). LATO also attains the highest Topology Score (0.5551) among all paradigms, surpassing both implicit models (TRELLIS: 0.5264, Hunyuan3D: 0.2946) and autoregressive methods (DeepMesh: 0.5500). Inference time remains stable at 3–10 s regardless of mesh complexity, whereas autoregressive baselines scale from ~15 s (MeshAnythingv2, 460 faces) to over 565 s (DeepMesh, 13,737 faces).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LATO outperforms all five autoregressive topology-preserving baselines (MeshAnythingv2, MeshSilkSong, FastMesh, BPT, DeepMesh) on every geometric metric in Table 2, with the largest margins over MeshAnythingv2 (CD L2: 0.043 vs. 0.108 dense) and the smallest over DeepMesh (CD L2: 0.043 vs. 0.051 dense). In VAE reconstruction (Table 1), LATO ties MeshGPT on CD(L2) for dense meshes (both 0.042) but leads on HD (0.065 vs. 0.070) and NC (0.847 vs. 0.824), while MeshCraft is far behind (CD L2: 0.150/0.243) and PivotMesh runs out of memory on dense meshes. On Topology Score (Table 4), LATO (0.5551) edges out the best autoregressive method DeepMesh (0.5500) and substantially exceeds implicit foundation models TRELLIS (0.5264), CLAY (0.5100), and Hunyuan3D (0.2946). In inference efficiency (Table 5), LATO completes generation in 5–10 s for meshes up to ~15K faces, while DeepMesh requires 147–566 s and MeshSilkSong 42–300 s for comparable or smaller meshes, confirming the parallel flow-matching advantage over sequential autoregressive decoding.

## Relation to Prior Work

LATO builds directly on the sparse voxel VAE architecture and two-stage cascaded flow matching pipeline from TRELLIS and TripoSF, but departs fundamentally from the implicit-field decoding paradigm (SDF/occupancy + Marching Cubes) that dominates that lineage. It also diverges from the autoregressive mesh generation line (MeshGPT, MeshAnything, BPT, DeepMesh) and per-face diffusion approaches (PolyDiff, MeshCraft) by encoding topology into a continuous sparse voxel latent (T-Voxels) via a Vertex Displacement Field, rather than serializing faces or vertices into token sequences. The key conceptual departure is the connection head that directly predicts edge connectivity from voxel latents, replacing both isosurfacing heuristics and grid-derived topology rules (as in TRELLIS.2's dual-grid).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LATO addresses a practical bottleneck in 3D content pipelines: implicit-field methods produce dense, irregular triangulations unsuitable for rigging, deformation, and game-engine deployment, while autoregressive explicit methods suffer prohibitive inference times that scale with mesh complexity. By decoupling topology from geometry in a sparse voxel latent and using parallel flow matching, LATO achieves artist-friendly topology at 3–10 s inference, enabling training on open-surface and non-manifold assets that implicit methods must discard. The main open limitation is the fixed sparse voxel grid resolution, which constrains representation of ultra-fine geometric detail; the authors propose octree-based extensions as future work. Whether the connection head generalizes to non-triangular (e.g., quad) topology or multi-component scenes with complex connectivity remains unexplored.
