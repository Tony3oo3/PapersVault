## Problem

Post-training autoregressive LLMs into diffusion LLMs for parallel decoding suffers from a fundamental pretrain-to-posttrain mismatch: the masked data distribution and bidirectional attention in post-training deviate significantly from the real data distribution and causal attention used in pretraining, resulting in limited speedup or substantially degraded quality. Existing consistency distillation approaches (e.g., CLLM) also struggle to scale to large block sizes because predicting future tokens conditioned on long noisy contexts becomes intractable, leaving modern accelerators' abundant FLOPs underutilized.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard text prompt and produces the same greedy-decoded token sequence as the original AR model, but generates them in parallel blocks of size 16–128 tokens via Jacobi fixed-point iterations. At inference, the model operates on blocks with a configurable number of simultaneously active blocks (K=2) and an n-gram verification pool, committing tokens to the KV cache only from the real-active block while pseudo-active blocks refine in parallel. The output is lossless (identical to AR greedy decoding) due to rejection sampling in the real-active block.

## Method

Jacobi Forcing (JF) post-trains an AR LLM as a causal parallel decoder by distilling it on its own Jacobi decoding trajectories, bypassing the dLLM formulation entirely. The key training innovation is a progressive noise schedule that splits a large block into smaller sub-blocks with linearly increasing noise ratios (0→1 over a window), reducing the longest consecutive noisy span from O(nN) to O(⌈tn⌉) and making large-block prediction tractable. A noise-aware causal attention mask and sequence packing technique interleave noisy and clean blocks so that a single forward pass computes both the progressive consistency loss (KL divergence to the teacher's converged distribution) and the AR loss across all N blocks simultaneously, reducing training cost from O(N) to O(1) forward passes. The model is further improved via progressive distillation: after initial training on the original AR model's trajectories, a second round collects trajectories from the already-trained JF-LLM with larger block sizes. At inference, rejection recycling reuses high-quality n-grams from noisy Jacobi iterations as parallel verification candidates, and multi-block decoding maintains K blocks simultaneously so that correct tokens in later blocks are decoded even while earlier blocks remain unconverged, achieving up to 4.6× tokens-per-forward and 4.0× wall-clock speedup.

## Results

On HumanEval, JF-LLM achieves 3.86× wall-clock speedup (TPS 159.5 vs. AR's 41.3) with 83.5% pass@1 accuracy, while JF-LLM(MR) with multi-block decoding and rejection recycling reaches 3.97× speedup (TPS 163.9). On GSM8K, JF-LLM(MR) achieves 3.71× speedup (TPS 154.9) with 91.4% solve rate, and on MATH it achieves 3.68× speedup (TPS 152.0) while slightly improving solve rate from 77.0% to 77.4%. On B200 with vLLM integration, JF-LLM(MR) reaches 865.8 TPS (3.91× speedup), and the method achieves up to 4.6× higher tokens-per-forward as block size scales.

## Baselines & Comparisons

JF-LLM decisively outperforms all diffusion-based baselines: it achieves 53–74× higher TPS than LLaDA and Dream-Base, and 2.0× higher TPS than the best optimized dLLM (D2F), while maintaining 29–47 percentage points higher accuracy. Among AR-based parallel decoders, JF-LLM(MR) achieves the highest wall-clock speedup (3.97× on HumanEval, 3.71× on GSM8K), surpassing CLLM (2.50×/2.08×) and vanilla Jacobi (0.97×/1.02×). However, EAGLE-3 achieves higher TPF (6.60 vs. 4.01) and preserves full AR accuracy (87.8% vs. 83.5%), trading off ~4.3% accuracy for a lower per-iteration cost that yields 3.07× speedup. On the MATH benchmark, JF-LLM(MR) slightly improves solve rate (77.4% vs. 77.0% AR) while delivering 3.68× speedup, outperforming CLLM (2.04×) and matching EAGLE-3's accuracy. The key trade-off is that JF-LLM sacrifices ~4% accuracy on coding tasks for substantially higher wall-clock throughput than CLLM and all dLLM variants, while EAGLE-3 preserves accuracy at the cost of requiring a separate trained draft head.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Jacobi decoding framework (Song et al., 2021; Santilli et al., 2023) and the consistency distillation training paradigm of CLLM (Kou et al., 2024) by introducing a progressive noise schedule and noise-aware causal attention that keep the model strictly causal throughout post-training. It deliberately departs from the dLLM formulation (SDAR, Fast-dLLM, D2F) which requires bidirectional attention and masked-data objectives, arguing that the pretrain-to-posttrain mismatch in those approaches limits both speedup and quality at large block sizes. The multi-block decoding and rejection recycling inference strategies are novel contributions that exploit the specific trajectory structure learned by JF-LLM, going beyond the single-block Jacobi iteration used in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

A 3.8–4× wall-clock speedup with only ~4 points accuracy loss on coding benchmarks is practically significant for LLM deployment, and the approach requires only post-training an existing AR model (no architectural changes, no separate draft model), making it broadly applicable. The hardware roofline analysis (block size × verification size vs. GPU FLOPs budget) provides a reusable framework for configuring parallel decoders on different accelerators. Open questions include whether the progressive distillation and multi-block decoding scale to 70B+ models, whether the quality gap closes with more training data or larger blocks, and how the method performs on open-ended generation tasks beyond structured code and math.
