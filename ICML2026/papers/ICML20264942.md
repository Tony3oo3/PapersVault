## Problem

Standard Vision Transformers and Swin backbones, widely deployed for grid-based PDE simulation, do not enforce rotational equivariance by construction, and no existing Transformer architecture natively supports rotationally equivariant processing of multi-type (scalar and vector) fields on regular grids. The paper identifies three structural barriers—tokenization entanglement, positional encoding directionality loss, and representational mismatch under vector rotation—that prevent standard self-attention from being equivariant for vector-valued physical fields.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264942/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

ReViT consumes grid-discretized physical fields at resolutions ranging from 28×28 (RotMNIST) to 160² (2D Kolmogorov flow) and 64³–96³ (3D MHD, TCF), accepting both scalar channels (density, passive scalar) and vector channels (velocity, magnetic field). For vector inputs, an auxiliary equivariantly-transforming vector quantity (e.g., vorticity or magnetic field) is required to construct local canonical bases per patch. The model outputs the predicted next-time-step field in the global coordinate frame, or a class label for the RotMNIST classification task, with the vector output lifted back from the local invariant representation via the preserved patch basis.

## Method

ReViT achieves rotational equivariance by decoupling basis transformations from feature learning through a three-stage pipeline: (1) Local Canonicalization computes a deterministic orthonormal basis B_i per patch from physics-based quantities (mean velocity and vorticity via stabilized cross-products, or strain-tensor eigendecomposition), projecting all vectors into this local frame so they become invariant scalars; (2) a Set-Transformer-style permutation-invariant aggregator with a learnable query token produces rotation-invariant patch tokens, while rebased relative positional encodings—displacement vectors projected into the query's local basis—preserve directional anisotropy without breaking equivariance; (3) an equivariant decoder uses cross-attention with Fourier-feature grid queries to reconstruct dense spatial details, then lifts predictions back to the global frame via the preserved basis. A precomputed Reference Basis Pyramid mediates hierarchical multi-scale operations (Swin-style patch merging/expansion) through a globalize-resample-localize procedure, maintaining strict equivariance across resolutions. The architecture achieves exact equivariance for the discrete groups C₄ (2D) and the chiral octahedral group O (3D), with bounded approximate SO(d) equivariance for arbitrary continuous rotations.

## Results

On 3D MHD (64³), ReViT achieves MSE 0.82×10⁻² and R²=0.98, outperforming the strongest baseline AViT (MSE 2.20×10⁻², R²=0.94) by ~63%. On 3D Turbulent Channel Flow (96³), ReViT records MSE 0.21×10⁻² and R²=0.96, a 65% error reduction over the next-best models (Swin3D/AViT at 0.60×10⁻²). On RotMNIST, ReViT reaches 98.26% accuracy while using only 1.81 GB VRAM versus 95.5 GB for the R12 group-equivariant baselines, with a ~4× inference speedup. On 2D advection, ReViT achieves the lowest 20-step rollout MSE (~10⁻⁴), reducing error by 37.2% over the non-equivariant PDETrans with only 11.6% additional latency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On RotMNIST, ReViT (98.26%) outperforms both GSA-Nets (97.97% at R12) and GE-ViT (98.01% at R12) in accuracy while being ~4× faster in inference and using ~53× less memory (1.81 GB vs. 95.5 GB), exposing the unsustainable cost of group-lifting. On 2D advection, ReViT beats all equivariant GNN baselines (ReGNN, REMUS, SE3GN) by an order of magnitude in MSE (~10⁻⁴ vs. ~10⁻³) and also surpasses the equivariant transformer LGATrans; it further outperforms the non-equivariant PDETrans by 37.2% MSE with only 11.6% extra latency. On 3D MHD, ReViT reduces MSE by ~63% over AViT (the best baseline) and by >75% over UNet3D and Swin3D, while AFNO and P3D show severe instability (R² near or below 0.6). On 3D TCF, the gap widens: ReViT achieves 65% lower MSE than Swin3D and AViT (0.21 vs. 0.60 ×10⁻²), and AFNO fails catastrophically (R² = −3.79) due to non-periodic wall boundaries violating its spectral assumptions. The paper also shows that data augmentation alone (PDETrans-Aug) cannot close the gap, producing 130.8% higher MSE than the best ReViT variant on KF, confirming that architectural equivariance is essential rather than a data-driven artifact.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReViT sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) equivariant neural networks (group CNNs, steerable networks) that enforce symmetry through constrained kernels but scale poorly with resolution; (2) equivariant Transformers for unstructured data (SE(3)-Transformers, LieTransformers) that are computationally intractable for dense 3D grids; and (3) local canonicalization in GNNs (List et al., 2025; Wang & Zhang, 2022) that achieves equivariance via per-node reference frames but relies on quadratic message passing. The paper's key departure is transplanting local canonicalization into a Swin-style hierarchical ViT backbone, using a Set-Transformer aggregator to produce permutation-invariant patch tokens and a Reference Basis Pyramid to maintain equivariance across resolution changes—something no prior grid-based Transformer architecture has achieved for multi-type (scalar + vector) fields.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ReViT provides a practical, scalable path to rotationally equivariant foundation models for grid-based physics, removing the prohibitive memory overhead of lifted equivariant Transformers (53× reduction) while matching or exceeding their accuracy. The 65% MSE reduction on the highly anisotropic TCF benchmark demonstrates that hard-coded equivariance is not merely beneficial but necessary when implicit data augmentation from statistical isotropy is absent. Key limitations remain: exact equivariance is restricted to the discrete chiral octahedral group O (3D) or C4 (2D), with continuous SO(d) equivariance only approximate due to grid discretization and interpolation artifacts. Open questions include extension to higher-order tensor fields, non-cubic or adaptive grids, and whether the local canonicalization framework generalizes to other physical symmetries (e.g., Galilean invariance, reflection).
