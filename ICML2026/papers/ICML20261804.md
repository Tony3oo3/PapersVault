## Problem

While diffusion and autoregressive models have demonstrated clear, near-monotonic scaling laws, the scalability of GANs remains largely unexplored. Naively scaling transformer-based GANs exposes two failure modes: early generator layers become inactive and contribute marginally to synthesis, and increasing model width/depth under fixed hyperparameters causes training divergence. The paper asks whether GANs can be made genuinely scalable—improving reliably with compute—while preserving their single-step inference and semantic latent space advantages.

## Input / Output

The generator takes a 64-dimensional latent code z and a class label c (or CLIP word tokens for text-to-image) and produces a synthetic image in the 32×32 VAE latent space (decoded to 256×256 RGB via a frozen SD-VAE). Unusually, the generator emits K=4 intermediate outputs at uniform depth intervals, each perturbed with a different Gaussian noise level before being jointly scored by the discriminator. The discriminator additionally receives a [cls] token for real/fake classification and patch tokens for VFM feature alignment.

## Method

GAT pairs a pure ViT generator (with adaptive normalization and LayerScale conditioning on z,c) and a ViT discriminator (with LayerScale and a [cls] token) operating entirely in VAE latent space. To activate underutilized early generator layers, Multi-level Noise-perturbed image Guidance (MNG) perturbs each of K intermediate outputs with a monotonically decreasing noise schedule (α₁<…<α_K=1) and feeds all perturbed versions to the discriminator, enforcing coarse-to-fine supervision without an explicit resolution hierarchy. To stabilize training across scales, a width-aware learning-rate rule scales η inversely with channel dimension (η_adapt = η_base · C_base/C_model), keeping per-step output change magnitude approximately constant. The discriminator is further regularized with a REPA-style alignment to frozen DINOv2 features and trained with relativistic pairing loss plus two-sided approximated gradient penalty.

## Results

GAT-XL/2 achieves a state-of-the-art FID-50K of 2.18 on ImageNet-256 class-conditional generation with a single forward pass (1 NFE) in only 60 epochs, requiring 4× fewer epochs than the strongest 1-NFE baseline MeanFlow-XL/2 (FID 3.43 at 240 epochs). It also attains the best CLIP-FID (1.86) and highest Recall (0.572) among compared one-step generators. On ImageNet-512, GAT-XL/2 reaches FID 4.04 in just 15 epochs, and on MS-COCO text-to-image it achieves FID 7.98 with 1 NFE, outperforming all prior GAN-based methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GAT-XL/2 (FID 2.18, 60 epochs) outperforms all 1-NFE baselines: it improves over MeanFlow-XL/2 (FID 3.43) by 1.25 FID points while using 4× fewer training epochs, and beats GigaGAN (FID 3.45, 480 epochs) and Shortcut-XL/2 (FID 10.60, 250 epochs) by wide margins. Against StyleGAN-XL (FID 2.30), GAT achieves a lower FID (2.18) and notably higher Recall (0.572 vs. 0.530) and CLIP-FID (1.86 vs. 2.62), though StyleGAN-XL benefits from ImageNet-pretrained discriminators. On MS-COCO text-to-image, MM-GAT (FID 7.98, 1 NFE) surpasses LAFITE (8.12, which uses a CLIP image encoder) and all other GAN baselines (XMC-GAN 9.33, DF-GAN 19.32, DM-GAN 32.64, AttnGAN 35.49), while remaining competitive with multi-step diffusion models like U-ViT-S/2 (5.95, 50 NFE). On FFHQ-256 unconditional generation, GAT-B/2 (FID 9.74) outperforms a same-size DiT-B/2 baseline (FID 10.49) trained on identical data. The paper also demonstrates a power-law scaling relationship FID(C) ≈ 3.52×10⁵ · C⁻⁰·⁴⁵⁶ between training compute and FID, confirming that GAT scales predictably with model size and training budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines two ideas that have driven recent diffusion-model progress—training in a frozen VAE latent space (LDM) and using plain transformer backbones (DiT)—and applies them to GANs for the first time in a systematic scalability study. It departs from prior large-scale GAN efforts (StyleGAN-XL, GigaGAN) that tuned a single high-capacity model, instead demonstrating clean power-law scaling across four model sizes. The MNG loss is conceptually related to MSG-GAN's multi-scale supervision but replaces deterministic resize-based degradation with stochastic noise perturbation, and the REPA objective is adapted from diffusion-model representation alignment (Yu et al., 2025) to the GAN discriminator. The width-aware learning-rate rule is a new, simple scaling principle not previously applied to adversarial training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that GANs, long considered resistant to systematic scaling, can follow clean power-law compute-FID relationships when given a pure transformer architecture in VAE latent space with the right training recipe. The 4× epoch reduction (60 vs. 240+ for comparable FID) and the simple width-aware LR rule make GAN training substantially more practical and predictable. Open questions include whether the scaling law extends to depth and batch-size axes, whether pretrained discriminators can further reduce training cost, and whether the framework transfers to higher-resolution or text-conditional settings at scale. The finding that discriminator scaling yields larger gains than generator scaling suggests future work should focus on richer discriminator representations.
