## Problem

Neural operator transformers face a fundamental trade-off: coordinate-evolution methods (e.g., Transolver) maintain geometric fidelity but lack persistent memory for dynamic PDE evolution, while state-evolution methods (e.g., LNO) accumulate physical dynamics but lose fine-grained mesh information in deep layers. No existing architecture jointly updates both the latent state and the mesh sequence, leaving a gap in handling tasks that simultaneously demand complex boundary geometry and long-term temporal dynamics.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a discretized mesh of N coordinate points with associated input field values (e.g., permeability for Darcy, initial velocity for Navier-Stokes) and outputs the PDE solution field at the same N points. It operates on 2D regular grids, 2D/3D structured meshes, and 3D unstructured point clouds, with N ranging from hundreds to millions of points. Internally, the full mesh sequence X ∈ R^{N×C} is compressed into a compact latent state S ∈ R^{M×C} (M≪N, typically M=128) that persists and evolves across all L layers, while X is also updated in-place at each layer.

## Method

CoEvol-NO introduces a layer-wise Predictor-Corrector (PC) mechanism that co-evolves the latent state S and mesh sequence X jointly. The Predictor is a Cross-Attention module (S queries X) that generates a tentative target Ŝ_t; the Corrector then refines S via a single-step exact gradient descent on the squared Frobenius correction loss, where the gradient explicitly includes the Jacobian of the predictor (derived in closed form for Cross-Attention). The mesh X is updated via a first-order residual approximation using a second Cross-Attention (X queries ΔS), distributing the global correction signal back to local geometry. The paper proves that standard residual connections and direct substitution are first-order approximations of this exact gradient under dot-product and squared-loss assumptions, respectively, and that the exact gradient symmetrizes the error dynamics for superior stability. Layer-wise momentum (β_S=0.9) and learnable LayerScale step sizes stabilize the unrolled optimization, and the full architecture achieves strict O(N) time complexity by avoiding N×N attention matrices.

## Results

CoEvol-NO achieves state-of-the-art relative L2 error across five standard PDE benchmarks. On Elasticity (unstructured point clouds), CoEvol-NO-L2 reaches 0.0036, a ~47% improvement over Transolver (0.0068). On Navier-Stokes (time-dependent fluid dynamics), CoEvol-NO-DP achieves 0.0731, outperforming Transolver (0.1010) by 28%. On Pipe flow, CoEvol-NO-DP attains 0.0032, the best among all compared methods including GNOT (0.0047) and ONO (0.0052). On the industrial AirfRANS benchmark, CoEvol-NO-DP reduces volume error to 0.0013 and surface error to 0.0047, substantially below Transolver (0.0088 / 0.0179).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoEvol-NO outperforms all 22 baselines on the majority of benchmarks. Against Transolver (the strongest prior Coords-Evol method), CoEvol-NO-DP wins on Elasticity (0.0038 vs. 0.0068, −44%), Pipe (0.0032 vs. 0.0039, −18%), and NS (0.0731 vs. 0.1010, −28%), while matching on Airfoil (0.0047 vs. 0.0057). Against GNOT, the gap is even larger on Elasticity (0.0038 vs. 0.0086, −56%) and Pipe (0.0032 vs. 0.0047, −32%). Against LNO (State-Evol), CoEvol-NO is dramatically better on Elasticity (0.0036 vs. 0.3265), confirming that decoupled latent evolution loses critical geometric information. On the industrial AirfRANS task, CoEvol-NO-DP achieves 0.0013 volume error and 0.9990 Spearman correlation, beating Transolver (0.0088 / 0.9907) by a wide margin. The only notable exception is Darcy flow, where CoEvol-NO-L2 (0.0904) trails Transolver (0.1010) only slightly, while CoEvol-NO-DP (0.0731) still leads.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoEvol-NO carves new ground by unifying two previously separate paradigms in efficient neural operators—Coords-Evolution (Transolver, which resets the latent state from the mesh each layer) and State-Evolution (LNO, which evolves latent tokens in isolation)—into a joint co-evolutionary framework. Conceptually, it repurposes the classical Predictor-Corrector scheme from numerical ODE solvers as a layer-wise update mechanism, showing that standard residual connections and direct substitution are merely first-order approximations of an exact gradient-based correction under dot-product and squared-loss assumptions, respectively. The meta-learning / Fast Weight Programmer perspective (TTT, Schlag et al.) provides the broader intellectual lineage for treating forward-pass updates as implicit optimization, but CoEvol-NO is the first to apply this to neural operators with a co-evolving state-mesh pair and a closed-form Jacobian for cross-attention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The co-evolutionary design resolves a fundamental trade-off in neural operators—geometric sensitivity versus dynamic memory—that has limited applicability to complex, unsteady PDEs on irregular meshes. The finding that the PC framework transfers across architectures (Transolver, LNO) and module types (attention, FFN) with 8–58% improvements suggests it is a general principle for layer-wise refinement, not merely a CoEvol-NO-specific trick. Key open questions include: a principled criterion for selectively applying exact gradients (the 8-combination ablation shows 'more exactness' can hurt), efficient Jacobian computation for non-attention modules (GNNs, convolutions), and the conditioning challenge (κ² scaling) at extreme depths beyond 40 layers. The linear-complexity guarantee makes the architecture a practical candidate for industrial-scale simulations with millions of mesh points.
