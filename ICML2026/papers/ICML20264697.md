## Problem

Co-designing robot morphology and control faces a trade-off: training a bespoke policy per design is computationally intractable at scale, while a single monolithic universal controller produces overly conservative, homogeneous designs. This paper asks whether replacing the monolithic controller with a mixture of embodiment-conditioned experts can increase evolvability—allowing different body plans to activate different learned sensorimotor circuits without disrupting knowledge in other modules.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264697/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The controller receives a 512-dimensional latent genotype vector z (decoded by a VAE into a 64×64×64 voxel tensor encoding material type and bone IDs for an endoskeletal robot) and proprioceptive state observations s_t from the simulated robot. It outputs a weighted mixture of motor actions a_t across all joints, where the mixing weights w_φ(z) = softmax(Wz + b) are produced by a linear gating layer conditioned on the genotype. The total policy comprises K=4 downsized graph-transformer experts (0.597M params each) plus a shared critic, yielding a slightly smaller total parameter count than the single-expert baseline.

## Method

ECo-MoE replaces the monolithic universal controller with K=4 graph-transformer experts whose outputs are combined via a linear softmax gate over the latent genotype z, so that different morphologies route to different expert combinations. A routing diversity regularizer (Eq. 5) penalizes similar gate outputs for distant genotypes, preventing expert collapse. Co-optimization proceeds on two timescales: PPO trains the experts within each generation (5 epochs, 40 steps/epoch), while CMA-ES updates the latent design distribution N(μ, Σ) across generations. The 'evo by demo' extension freezes a pretrained expert for a target morphology into the mixture and augments the evolutionary fitness by the pretrained expert's learned gate weight raised to α=2, thereby suppressing genotypes incompatible with the demo and steering evolution toward its structural traits.

## Results

Across three locomotion tasks, ECo-MoE significantly outperforms the monolithic universal controller baseline on Upright Locomotion and Potholes, while matching it on Flat Ground (where both converge to a homogeneous 'snake' population). In Upright Locomotion, the best evolved designs reach peak fitness of ~29.19 by generation 120 (vs. lower values for the baseline), and ECo-MoE produces designs with higher effective-limb-counts and lower mass-bias-vector-magnitudes, indicating more balanced anatomies. In terms of parameter efficiency, ECo-MoE's total policy size is 2.388M (slightly smaller than the 2.563M baseline), with each of its 4 experts using only 0.597M parameters for action generation. For evo-by-demo, the CoSteering variant (frozen pretrained expert + predesigned latent initialization) most effectively steers evolution toward the demo's quadrupedal morphology, though PredesignOnly alone achieves the highest raw fitness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the state-of-the-art universal (monolithic) controller from Li et al. (2025), which shares a single graph-transformer policy across all evolving designs. On Flat Ground, ECo-MoE and the baseline achieve statistically indistinguishable fitness, as both converge to the same 'snake' local optimum where embodiment-conditioned specialization offers no advantage. On Upright Locomotion, ECo-MoE significantly increases evolvability, producing higher peak fitness and more diverse, balanced morphologies (higher effective-limb-count, lower mass-bias) than the baseline. On Potholes, ECo-MoE again outperforms the baseline, suggesting the advantage grows with environmental complexity. Importantly, ECo-MoE achieves these gains with a smaller total policy size (2.388M vs. 2.563M) and substantially fewer parameters dedicated to action generation (4 × 0.597M = 2.388M vs. 2.563M for the single expert), demonstrating that the improvement stems from modularity rather than added capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the co-design framework of Li et al. (2025), which jointly evolves a VAE-compressed latent genome and a single universal graph-transformer controller for freeform endoskeletal robots. The key architectural departure is replacing that monolithic controller with an embodiment-conditioned mixture of experts (MoE), where a linear gating network over the latent genotype routes each robot's sensory state to a weighted combination of K smaller expert subnetworks. The MoE concept itself is classical (Jacobs et al., 1991), but its application here—using the robot's own genotype as the gating signal to dissociate control strategies across morphologies—is novel in the evolutionary robotics literature. The paper also introduces 'evo by demo,' a mechanism for injecting a frozen pretrained expert and a predesigned latent prior to steer open-ended evolution toward target phenotypes, which has no direct precedent in prior co-design work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that embodiment-conditioned modularity increases evolvability specifically when morphological diversity is high (Upright Locomotion, Potholes) but offers no benefit in simple regimes (Flat Ground) provides a useful principle for when modular controllers are warranted over monolithic ones. The evo-by-demo mechanism opens a practical path toward goal-directed robot design without hand-engineering the full morphology, though its effectiveness is currently bounded by the VAE's inability to reconstruct out-of-distribution body plans (e.g., bipedal or hexapodal forms). Key open questions include whether the approach transfers to physical robots, whether the VAE latent manifold can be adaptively expanded during evolution, and whether hierarchical routing could support multiple heterogeneous behaviors beyond locomotion.
