## Problem

MLLMs struggle with fine-grained visual grounding because CLIP-style patch tokenization blends multiple semantically distinct elements (e.g., a dog's ear, a toy, and a floor) into single patch embeddings, creating semantic entanglement that prevents the model from focusing on instruction-relevant details. Existing remedies either operate at the token level (selecting or reweighting entire tokens) or introduce layer-wise computational overhead that scales with model depth, leaving a gap for instruction-conditioned, channel-level refinement that is both precise and lightweight.

## Input / Output

The system consumes an RGB image (processed by a vision encoder such as CLIP ViT-L/14@336 or SigLIP-S2 into patch-level features) and a natural-language instruction, producing an autoregressive text response. MoDA specifically operates on the adapter-projected visual features (shape B×N×E) and the language token embeddings extracted from the initial LLM layers (shape B×M×E), generating a per-channel modulation mask of shape [0,1]^E that is applied via Hadamard product before the features enter the LLM. No additional input modalities, resolution constraints, or output format changes are introduced relative to the base MLLM.

## Method

MoDA is a 2-layer cross-attention module (16 heads) inserted between the visual adapter and the LLM, where visual tokens serve as queries and language instruction tokens serve as memory, producing a channel-wise modulation mask through a learned linear projection followed by sigmoid activation. The mask is applied multiplicatively (Hadamard product) along the embedding dimension, enabling selective suppression of irrelevant feature channels per instruction rather than additive residual reweighting of whole tokens. Training follows the standard LLaVA two-stage protocol: Stage 1 pre-trains only the adapter on image-caption pairs, and Stage 2 jointly fine-tunes MoDA (Xavier-initialized) and the LLM with autoregressive cross-entropy loss, requiring no additional supervision, architectural changes, or training data. The design adds only 3.7% parameters and <1% FLOPs, and its gains scale with encoder richness—yielding +12.0 points on MMVP with CLIP and +4.8 on ScienceQA with SigLIP-S2—while generalizing to non-CLIP backbones like Qwen3-VL's native ViT.

## Results

MoDA delivers consistent gains across 12 benchmarks and three MLLM families (LLaVA-1.5, LLaVA-MoRE, Qwen3-VL). Headline improvements include +12.0 points on MMVP (24.0→36.0) for LLaVA-1.5+MoDA, +4.8 points on ScienceQA (77.1→81.9) for LLaVA-MoRE SigLIP-S2+MoDA, and +4.9/+4.1/+3.8 points on ScienceQA/RealWorldQA/GQA for Qwen3-VL+MoDA. On hallucination detection, LLaVA-MoRE SigLIP-S2+MoDA reaches 42.7 on MMVP and 87.7 on POPE, surpassing the 13B LLaVA-1.5 baseline (24.7/85.9). Overhead is minimal: 3.7% additional parameters and <1% FLOPs relative to LLaVA-MoRE 8B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the direct LLaVA-1.5 (7B) baseline, MoDA improves ScienceQA by +2.0 (69.0→71.0), MMBench by +0.5, and RealWorldQA by +9.1 (44.3→53.4), while GQA is essentially tied (62.4→62.5) and ChartQA regresses (17.0→13.2). LLaVA-MoRE SigLIP-S2+MoDA outperforms its own baseline on all five VQA benchmarks, with the largest gain on ScienceQA (+4.8, 77.1→81.9), and also surpasses the 13B LLaVA-1.5 on ScienceQA (81.9 vs. 71.6) and RealWorldQA (58.2 vs. 45.8). Qwen3-VL-2B+MoDA beats its baseline on GQA (+3.8), ScienceQA (+4.9), MMBench (+0.9), and RealWorldQA (+4.1), but regresses on ChartQA (80.0→79.0) and V*Bench (77.0→74.9), which the authors attribute to the encoder already being specialized for those tasks. Compared to ShareGPT-4V (trained on a larger data distribution), LLaVA-MoRE SigLIP-S2+MoDA surpasses it on LLaVA-Wild (73.9 vs. 72.6), MMStar (38.5 vs. 33.0), and CV-Bench (62.2 vs. 61.8). Against token-level masking baselines (Learnable Mask and Sparse Mask from Table 5), MoDA achieves the highest average (69.4 vs. 68.5 and 66.5) and the strongest MMVP score (42.7 vs. 41.9 and 38.8), confirming that channel-wise modulation outperforms discrete token masking. MoDA also outperforms older architectures (BLIP-2, InstructBLIP, original LLaVA) by wide margins across all benchmarks, with the gap most pronounced on hallucination detection (e.g., MMVP 42.7 vs. 16.9 for InstructBLIP).

## Relation to Prior Work

MoDA builds directly on the LLaVA two-stage training protocol (Liu et al., 2023; 2024a), inserting a lightweight cross-attention module between the visual adapter and the LLM to perform instruction-conditioned channel-wise modulation via Hadamard products. It departs from token-level approaches like Q-Former and InstructBLIP, which reweight or select entire visual tokens additively, and from layer-wise attention masking methods (LAM, SwinBERT) that introduce depth-scaling overhead. The key conceptual departure is operating at the channel dimension within each token's embedding rather than across tokens, and applying multiplicative modulation post-alignment so that MoDA complements rather than replaces existing adapter architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MoDA offers a practical, low-overhead (<1% FLOPs, 3.7% parameters) plug-in that consistently improves fine-grained visual grounding and reduces hallucinations across three MLLM families, including a non-CLIP backbone (Qwen3-VL), suggesting the channel-modulation mechanism is encoder-agnostic. The finding that gains scale with encoder richness (SigLIP-S2 amplifies MoDA's benefit over CLIP) provides a useful design principle for pairing adapters with visual encoders. Open questions include whether explicit sparsity-inducing objectives could strengthen feature selection, whether MoDA extends to multi-image and video settings, and characterizing the boundary where an already-specialized encoder (e.g., for chart reading) leaves insufficient headroom for channel-wise refinement.
