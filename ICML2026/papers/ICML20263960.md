## Problem

Particle filters are theoretically exact for nonlinear dynamical systems but suffer from weight degeneracy in high dimensions, making them impractical for realistic geophysical applications. The fully adapted auxiliary particle filter (FA-APF), which uses the optimal proposal to minimize weight variance, has remained largely unexplored because sampling from the optimal posterior p(x^{k+1}|x^k, y^{k+1}) is infeasible with classical numerical solvers. This paper shows that pre-trained diffusion-based generative emulators can make the FA-APF practical without any additional training.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained diffusion denoiser (trained supervised on pairs of successive system states), the current state x^k, the next observation y^{k+1}, a (potentially nonlinear) observation operator H, and the observation noise covariance Σ_y. It produces an ensemble of N state estimates with normalized weights approximating the filtering distribution p(x^k|y^{1:k}). Notably, the observation space can be far lower-dimensional than the state space (e.g., observing ~1% of O(10^6) atmospheric variables), and the denoiser is never retrained or fine-tuned during filtering.

## Method

The core contribution is a training-free implementation of the FA-APF where the optimal proposal p(x^{k+1}|x^k, y^{k+1}) is sampled by solving a modified reverse diffusion SDE whose score is decomposed via Bayes' rule into a prior score (obtained from the trained denoiser via first-order Tweedie's formula) and a likelihood score (computed via Moment Matching Posterior Sampling, which approximates the local posterior as Gaussian using first- and second-order Tweedie's formulas and solves a linear system implicitly via GMRES). Particle weights are computed efficiently by approximating the transition p(x^{k+1}|x^k) with a Dirac mass at the conditional mean, requiring only a single denoiser call per particle. An adaptive inflation coefficient α is tuned to keep the effective sample size within a target interval, preventing weight collapse in high dimensions. The entire pipeline—posterior sampling, weight computation, and particle propagation—requires no additional training beyond the initial denoiser, and the approach generalizes to stochastic interpolants and flow-matching emulators.

## Results

On Lorenz-63 with N=256 particles, FA-APF achieves the best skill (RMSE) of 0.60 and CRPS of 0.98, outperforming all four baselines (BPF: 1.25/2.88, EnKF: 0.72/1.10, EnSF: 2.18/4.23, EnFF: 1.67/2.51). On the 128×128 Navier-Stokes flow with 128 particles, FA-APF achieves a skill of 0.14 at (32,32) coarse observations, beating FlowDAS (0.19) and BPF (2.80). On GenCast (O(10⁶) variables), FA-APF with sparse temperature observations (~1% of state) reaches a stable skill plateau after 7 days, well below the unconditional GenCast ensemble forecast.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FA-APF decisively outperforms the Bootstrap Particle Filter (BPF) on all metrics: skill improves from 1.25 to 0.60 and CRPS from 2.88 to 0.98 on Lorenz-63, and BPF essentially fails on Navier-Stokes (skill ~2.8–3.0) while FA-APF achieves 0.13–2.30 depending on observation density. Against EnKF, FA-APF wins on skill (0.60 vs 0.72) and CRPS (0.98 vs 1.10) but has a slightly higher SSR (1.49 vs 1.25), indicating mild over-dispersion; EnKF remains competitive and is the closest competitor. FA-APF substantially outperforms the generative-model-based EnSF (skill 0.60 vs 2.18) and EnFF (0.60 vs 1.67), which struggle with the nonlinear dynamics. On Navier-Stokes, FA-APF also beats FlowDAS (0.14 vs 0.19 at 32×32 coarse; 2.30 vs 3.08 at 8×8 sparse), with the gap widening under sparse observations where FlowDAS degrades to BPF-level performance. The advantage of FA-APF is attributed to its use of the optimal proposal p(x^{k+1}|x^k, y^{k+1}) via diffusion posterior sampling, which minimizes weight variance and prevents the degeneracy that plagues standard particle filters in high dimensions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper implements the Fully-Adapted Auxiliary Particle Filter (FA-APF, Petetin & Desbouvries, 2013) by showing that diffusion-based generative emulators of dynamical systems can sample from the optimal proposal p(x^{k+1}|x^k, y^{k+1}) without additional training, using moment-matching posterior sampling (MMPS, Rozet et al., 2024) to compute the likelihood score. It positions itself as a rigorous ensemble-based generalization of FlowDAS (Chen et al., 2026), which the authors show is a degenerate single-particle, non-Bayesian variant. The approach departs from prior training-free generative data assimilation methods (EnSF, EnFF) that require dense observations, and from score-based DA (Rozet & Louppe, 2023) that requires training a local score network, by leveraging the already-trained transition emulator directly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that particle filters—long considered unusable in high-dimensional settings due to weight degeneracy (Snyder et al., 2008)—can scale to O(10^6)-variable systems like global atmospheric dynamics with only 256 particles, provided the optimal proposal is accessible via generative emulators. Key limitations include the computational cost of posterior sampling (requiring Jacobian-vector products of the denoiser at each diffusion step), the Gaussian approximation in MMPS that degrades at high noise levels, and the need for inflation to prevent weight collapse. Open questions include developing cheaper posterior sampling methods for highly nonlinear observation operators, improving weight approximations in high dimensions, and extending the framework to training-free Bayesian smoothing.
