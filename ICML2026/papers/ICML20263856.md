## Problem

Dueling optimization, where only pairwise comparisons of function values are available (no gradients, no scalar outputs), has been studied almost exclusively in Euclidean space. However, many important applications—hyperbolic recommendation, trajectory optimization on SO(3), and Stiefel-constrained representation learning—naturally live on Riemannian manifolds, where curvature breaks the linear algebra that underpins existing dueling methods. This paper asks: how can one provably optimize over a Riemannian manifold using only a comparison oracle?

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a binary comparison oracle Q_f(x,y) ∈ {+1, −1} that reveals only which of two queried points has the larger objective value, plus an initial point x₀ on the manifold and the manifold's geometric structure (metric, exponential/logarithmic maps, and either a projection or a linear minimization oracle). It outputs a point x̂ on the manifold satisfying an ε-optimality or ε-stationarity guarantee. Notably, the oracle never exposes function magnitudes, so the method must infer both gradient direction and step size from sign information alone; in the DNN attack application the perturbation is constrained to a sphere of dimension ≈1000, and in horizon leveling the decision variable is a 2×2 rotation matrix in SO(2).

## Method

The core estimator h_ν(x) = Q_f(Exp_x(νu), Exp_x(−νu))·u samples one random unit tangent direction u, evaluates the comparison oracle at two geodesic neighbors, and returns a unit vector aligned with the Riemannian gradient direction with high probability (Lemma 3.1). Building on this, RDNGD performs projected normalized gradient descent on the manifold with either constant or cosine-annealed step sizes, yielding O(dε⁻²) iterations for nonconvex, O(dε⁻¹) for convex, and—via the phase-based RRDNGD variant—O(d log(1/ε)) for strongly convex objectives. For settings where projection is intractable, RDFW replaces the projection with a Riemannian Frank-Wolfe linear minimization oracle and uses a batch-averaged estimator (M_k samples per iteration) to control the variance that the LMO amplifies, achieving O(ε⁻¹) iterations at O(dε⁻²) oracle complexity. A key analytical contribution is tightening the universal constant Ĉ in the gradient-direction estimator from 1/20 (Saha et al., 2021) to 1/√(2π) ≈ 0.4, and removing a logarithmic factor in the bias bound, which translates into up to an eightfold reduction in estimation error and permits larger step sizes.

## Results

The paper establishes the first theoretical framework for Riemannian dueling optimization, providing iteration and oracle complexities for three algorithms: RDNGD achieves O(dε⁻²) for nonconvex and O(dε⁻¹) for geodesically convex objectives; RRDNGD achieves O(d log(1/ε)) for strongly convex objectives; and RDFW (projection-free) achieves O(ε⁻¹) iteration and O(dε⁻²) oracle complexity. Experimentally, RDNGD matches ZO-RGD performance on Rayleigh quotient maximization (d=100,150) and Karcher mean (n=5,10) using only a comparison oracle. On the CIFAR-10 DNN attack, RDNGD reaches higher adversarial loss in fewer iterations and less CPU time than ZO-RGD while requiring only 10 comparison samples per step versus 500 for ZO-RGD. On horizon leveling over SO(2), suboptimality reaches ~10⁻⁵ within 30 iterations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary experimental baseline is ZO-RGD (Li et al., 2023), a zeroth-order Riemannian gradient descent method that requires function value access. RDNGD achieves comparable convergence on Rayleigh quotient maximization and Karcher mean problems despite using strictly weaker oracle information (pairwise comparisons only, no function values). On the CIFAR-10 VGG attack, RDNGD outperforms ZO-RGD in both iteration count and CPU time while using only 10 comparison samples per step versus 500 for ZO-RGD, demonstrating superior query efficiency. Theoretically, the paper matches the rates of Saha et al. (2021) in the Euclidean special case but improves the gradient direction estimation constant C from 1/20 to 1/√(2π)≈0.4 (an ~8× reduction in estimation error) and removes a logarithmic factor √(log(|∇f|/(√d Lν))) present in the prior bias bound. The RDFW method is the first projection-free dueling optimization algorithm over manifolds, with no direct prior baseline for comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Euclidean dueling optimization framework of Saha et al. (2021), which introduced normalized gradient descent (NGD) methods using only pairwise comparison oracles, to the Riemannian manifold setting. It also adapts the Riemannian Frank-Wolfe subproblem structure from Weber & Sra (2023) to the comparison-only oracle regime, yielding the first projection-free dueling method on manifolds. The key technical departure from prior Euclidean dueling work is that curvature breaks standard trigonometric linearization, requiring new analysis of the two-point perturbation estimator via the exponential map and parallel transport. The paper also improves upon the Euclidean NGD results by tightening the universal constant C from 1/20 to 1/√(2π) and removing a logarithmic factor from the bias bound.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work opens a previously inaccessible class of problems where the decision space is inherently non-Euclidean (SO(3) for trajectory optimization, SPD matrices, Stiefel manifold, hyperbolic embeddings) and only pairwise preference feedback is available, unifying machine-generated and human-based comparison sources under one framework. The projection-free RDFW result is the first convergence guarantee for projection-free dueling optimization over manifolds, which is practically important when Riemannian projection requires expensive iterative solvers. Open questions highlighted by the authors include accelerated methods, Hessian-aware approaches for identifying local minima, and whether the inherent noise in the direction estimator can facilitate escape from saddle points. The empirical validation, while convincing on CIFAR-10 attacks and horizon leveling, is limited to relatively low-dimensional manifolds (SO(2), sphere of dim ~1000), leaving open whether the theory and practice scale to higher-dimensional Stiefel or hyperbolic settings.
