## Problem

Existing activation mechanisms (ReLU, GELU, SiLU, etc.) are static and rely solely on instantaneous input intensity for thresholding, lacking explicit awareness of which feature channels are actually relevant to the network's decision. This causes irrelevant or noisy channels to remain active, propagate through subsequent layers, and degrade final predictions. The paper identifies that channel relevance can be assessed from both intrinsic activity levels and extrinsic decision weights, and that a strong consensus exists between these two views, providing a principled basis for targeted channel suppression that prior activations ignore.

## Input / Output

AIEC is a drop-in replacement for any existing activation function, operating on pre-activation feature maps (H×W×C for CNNs, token sequences for ViTs) and producing non-negative post-activation feature maps. During training, it additionally computes two auxiliary losses (L_aux for learning decision weights, L_gate for suppressing irrelevant channels) on top of the standard task loss; during inference, only the threshold activation (TAU) is applied, incurring zero extra overhead. The method is applied to the last block for CNNs (where high-level semantics reside) and to every block for Transformers, with a class token peeled off separately for ViT-style architectures.

## Method

AIEC integrates four units: (1) a Threshold Activation Unit (TAU) providing a basic 'prior' partition via thresholding (τ=0); (2) an Activation-Counting Unit (ACU) that maintains per-category, per-channel counters of activations, inhibitions, and total samples to compute a 'relative firing rate' as an intrinsic relevance score; (3) a Decision-Making Unit (DMU) that learns K linear decision weight vectors (one per class) via an auxiliary cross-entropy loss on globally-pooled activated features, yielding extrinsic relevance scores; and (4) a Consensus Gatekeeping Unit (CGU) that identifies irrelevant channels as the intersection of channels with non-positive intrinsic firing rate AND non-positive extrinsic decision weight, then imposes a regularization loss (L_gate) that drives the activation responses of those consensus-irrelevant channels toward zero. The consensus (logical AND) is critical: the ablation shows it outperforms either view alone or their union, as it reduces misjudgments by requiring both perspectives to agree before suppressing a channel. Only TAU is needed at inference, making the mechanism training-only in its overhead.

## Results

On ViT-Tiny, AIEC achieves 91.8% top-1 accuracy on CIFAR-10, 71.1% on CIFAR-100, 82.1% on ImageNet-100, and 73.0% on ImageNet-1K, consistently outperforming all seven activation baselines. The largest gains appear on CIFAR-100 (+5.4% over ReLU) and ImageNet-100 (+6.7% over ReLU), where channel relevance is harder to infer from instantaneous signal strength alone. On CNNs (Table 2), AIEC similarly leads across all architectures, e.g., 77.0% on CIFAR-100 with ResNet-18 vs. 75.7% for ReLU. Inference latency is on par with ReLU (9.2 ms vs. 9.2 ms for ViT-Tiny), confirming negligible deployment overhead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AIEC outperforms all seven standard activation functions (Softplus, ELU, SELU, SiLU, ReLU, GELU, GDN) across every dataset and architecture tested. The strongest baselines are ReLU and GELU, which AIEC surpasses by 1.9–6.7% on ViTs and 0.3–1.6% on CNNs; the gap is largest on CIFAR-100 and ImageNet-100 where many channels are task-irrelevant. Among the weaker baselines, SELU and GDN trail AIEC by 10–15% on CIFAR-100, confirming that smooth or density-based activations without sparsity are particularly disadvantaged. Against L0 regularization (Table 6), AIEC achieves 71.1% vs. 67.1–67.2% on ViT-Tiny/CIFAR-100, showing that explicit channel-level gatekeeping beats indiscriminate sparsity penalties. SENet (65.7%) and CBAM (76.2% on ResNet-18) also fall short of AIEC (71.1% / 77.0%), indicating that learned attention weights without a sparsity-inducing mechanism cannot match the targeted noise suppression provided by intrinsic-extrinsic consensus.

## Relation to Prior Work

AIEC repurposes the concept of channel relevance from feature attribution (CAM, Grad-CAM, LRP) and embeds it directly into the activation function, rather than treating attribution as a post-hoc analysis tool. It builds on threshold-based activation (ReLU) as a 'prior' partitioning mechanism, then augments it with two data-driven assessments: an intrinsic activation-counting statistic (relative firing rate) and an extrinsic linear classifier weight, whose consensus identifies channels to suppress via a new gatekeeping regularization loss. The design deliberately departs from attention-based gating (SENet, CBAM) and L0 sparsity regularization by providing explicit, interpretable per-channel criteria for suppression rather than learned soft weights or undirected sparsity penalties.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AIEC offers a drop-in activation replacement that consistently improves accuracy across ViTs, CNNs, and GNNs while producing measurably sparser representations (active channels drop from ~15% to ~8%), suggesting a practical path toward more interpretable and noise-robust networks without architectural changes. Key limitations include the need to train from scratch, per-task tuning of λ_gate, and the ACU's non-trivial training-time overhead (100 ms per batch in ViT-Tiny). Open questions include whether the approach transfers to pre-trained/fine-tuned settings, whether the linear-classifier interpretability assumption holds for very high-dimensional or multi-task heads, and whether the consensus criterion generalizes beyond classification to regression or generative tasks.
