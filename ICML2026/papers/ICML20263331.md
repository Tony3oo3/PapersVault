## Problem

In-context reinforcement learning (ICRL) enables agents to adapt to new tasks at test time without parameter updates, but safety during this adaptation process has been entirely unexplored. Unlike standard RL where safety constraints are enforced during training on known tasks, ICRL agents explore and learn novel environments at deployment time, meaning safety must be maintained throughout the adaptation process, not just in the final policy. No prior work has studied how to enforce user-specified safety budgets during parameter-update-free in-context adaptation.

## Input / Output

The policy takes as input the current state, a long sequence of past interaction history (states, actions, rewards, and costs from all previous episodes plus the current episode prefix), and a scalar cost-to-go (CTG) budget specifying the maximum remaining cost allowed for the current episode. The CTG is decremented by the actual cost incurred at each step, so the agent dynamically tracks its remaining safety budget. The output is a single action (discrete or continuous) sampled from the policy, with the overall behavior expected to maximize cumulative reward while keeping episode cost below the CTG budget, and to shift between conservative and aggressive strategies as the CTG target changes at test time.

## Method

SCARED formulates safe ICRL as a CMDP and solves it via an exact-penalty dual optimization: a single Lagrange multiplier (shared across all episodes) is updated using the maximum constraint violation rather than the sum, and the policy loss uses the positive part [g_k(π)]₊ to avoid over-penalizing episodes that already satisfy the budget. Theorem 1 proves that fixed points of this iterative scheme are exactly the primal-optimal feasible policies. The architecture follows AMAGO's transformer-based trajectory encoder with an MLP timestep encoder, trained online via DDPG-style actor-critic with separate reward and cost Q-networks; the actor maximizes the reward Q-value while subtracting λ·v_e·Q^c (where v_e is an indicator for budget violation). CTG serves as a conditioning variable that the policy learns to map to exploration strategies, enabling test-time control over the reward-cost tradeoff without any parameter updates.

## Results

SCARED demonstrates consistent safe in-context adaptation across all three benchmark environments (SafeDarkRoom, SafeDarkMujoco, SafeVelocity), showing increasing episode return and decreasing episode cost as the in-context interaction history grows (Figure 1). Under varying cost-to-go (CTG) budgets, SCARED achieves a clear reward-cost tradeoff: higher CTG targets yield higher total return while maximum episode costs track the specified budget closely, with nearly all episodes respecting the constraint (Figure 2). In contrast, Safe AD degrades in the SafeVelocity domain (cost rises while return declines) and fails to reduce cost in SafeDarkMujoco, while both safe meta-RL baselines (MAML with penalty, SafeMeta) fail to achieve safe adaptation despite performing parameter updates at test time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCARED outperforms all three baselines across every evaluated environment. Against Safe AD (algorithm distillation for safe RL, Laskin et al. 2023), SCARED achieves faster and stronger adaptation in SafeDarkRoom, while Safe AD fails entirely in the SafeVelocity domain (episode cost rises and return declines over in-context episodes) and in SafeDarkMujoco (return improves but cost does not decrease). Safe AD also shows inconsistent budget-adjustment behavior, e.g., achieving high return at low CTG but a downward trend as the cost budget increases in SafeDarkRoom. Against MAML with penalty (Finn et al. 2017), SCARED clearly wins: MAML with penalty fails to achieve both safe and high-performing adaptation across all evaluated environments. Against SafeMeta (Xu & Zhu 2025), SCARED wins on the safety dimension: SafeMeta achieves higher returns in the SafetyVelocity domains but does not reduce safety violations even when allowed to perform gradient updates during evaluation, demonstrating that parameter adaptation alone without cross-episode interaction histories is insufficient for safe online exploration. Neither meta-RL baseline exhibits the budget-adjustment behavior that SCARED shows, with their performance remaining flat regardless of the specified CTG target.

## Relation to Prior Work

SCARED builds directly on the AMAGO framework (Grigsby et al., 2024a) for its transformer-based trajectory encoder and DDPG-style actor-critic training loop, extending it with a cost critic and a Lagrangian multiplier conditioned on cost-to-go. The optimization scheme draws on exact-penalty dual methods (Nocedal & Wright, 1999), replacing the standard per-episode Lagrangian with a single shared multiplier and a positive-part penalty to avoid over-penalizing already-feasible episodes. Conceptually, the paper departs from two lines of work simultaneously: standard ICRL (Laskin et al., 2023; Zisman et al., 2024) which has no mechanism for cost signals, and safe meta-RL (Finn et al., 2017; Xu & Zhu, 2025) which relies on test-time parameter updates rather than in-context interaction histories for adaptation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first formal treatment of safety in ICRL, a gap the authors argue is a prerequisite for real-world deployment of parameter-update-free agents in robotics and embodied AI. The OOD benchmark design (center-oriented vs. edge-oriented spawning with a provable distributional shift) is a methodological contribution that challenges prior ICRL evaluations, which the authors show may only test interpolation rather than true extrapolation. Limitations include the requirement for a CMDP formulation with available cost signals, the need for domain knowledge to set the CTG budget, and evaluation on relatively simple environments (grid world, point/car MuJoCo, velocity control). Open questions include scalability to high-dimensional continuous control, handling unmodeled or adversarial hazards, and whether the exact-penalty dual approach extends to multi-constraint or stochastic safety settings.
