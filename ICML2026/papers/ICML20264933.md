## Problem

Binary sparse annotations in image-text datasets force the continuous cross-modal similarity space into hard matched/mismatched boundaries, creating false negative samples that disrupt semantic consistency and degrade generalization. Prior probabilistic embedding methods (e.g., PCME, PCME++) model intra-modal ambiguity but still rely on binary labels for cross-modal loss, causing them to inflate uncertainty on individual samples rather than correcting annotation errors. No existing approach directly represents the image-text matching relationship itself as a probabilistic distribution to absorb the noise from imperfect supervision.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-extracted image and text features from a frozen CLIP encoder (ViT-B/32 or ViT-B/16), computes their element-wise Hadamard product to form a B×B×d tensor of similarity vectors, and feeds each d-dimensional vector independently through the variational adapter. The output is a scalar similarity score in [0,1] (sigmoid of the decoder mean) paired with a per-pair uncertainty estimate σ̂, which together drive retrieval ranking or zero-shot classification. Because each similarity vector is processed independently by a two-layer MLP, the pipeline supports chunk-based inference (128×128 chunks) with only ~34 MB peak memory per chunk at test time.

## Method

VACSR inserts a lightweight VAE-based adapter between the CLIP feature interaction (Hadamard product) and the final similarity score. The encoder maps each d-dimensional similarity vector to a two-component Gaussian Mixture posterior (with Gumbel-Softmax for differentiable component selection), and the decoder reconstructs a scalar similarity via sigmoid, with a tractable KL upper bound regularizing each Gaussian component toward a standard normal prior. The key innovation is a distributional optimization loss L_σ that adaptively calibrates per-pair variance: samples close to their binary label receive low variance (strong gradient), while false negatives receive high variance (suppressed gradient), effectively down-weighting erroneous annotations without explicit correction. A Straight-Through Estimator bypasses the vanishing sigmoid gradient in the MSE reconstruction term, and the entire adapter adds only 5.26 M parameters (48.5% of PCME++), trained end-to-end with a weighted ELBO objective (α=0.0005, β=γ=1) for 25 epochs using AdamP.

## Results

On COCO image-text retrieval (ViT-B/16), VACSR achieves 87.4% R@1 (I2T) and 74.3% R@1 (T2I) on the 1K test set, and 72.2% R@1 (I2T) and 54.5% R@1 (T2I) on the 5K test set, outperforming the strongest baseline PCME++ by 2.1% and 0.9% (1K) and 3.5% and 1.1% (5K) respectively. On the ECCV Caption dataset, VACSR reaches 35.4 mAP@R and 45.5 R-P (I2T), surpassing PCME++ by 0.9 and 0.4 points. For domain generalization on ImageNet variants (ViT-B/16), VACSR attains 61.6% average accuracy across ImageNet, V2, Sketch, A, and R, exceeding the best baseline MMRL (60.6%) by 1.0 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VACSR outperforms all probabilistic embedding baselines (P2RM, DAA, PCME, PCME++) across every retrieval metric on COCO, EC, and CxC, with the largest gains over PCME++ on the 5K COCO test set (+3.5% I2T R@1) and EC mAP@R (+0.9). Under noisy correspondence (20% and 50% noise), VACSR dominates all dedicated noise-robust methods (NCR, BiCro, NPC, VSE∞), achieving 539.0 RSUM on COCO at 20% noise vs. 529.8 for NPC, and maintaining 534.2 RSUM at 50% noise while PCME++ drops to 511.0. On domain generalization (ImageNet variants), VACSR beats all prompt-based (CoCoOp, MaPLe, CoPrompt), adapter-based (MMA, CLIPOOD), and representation-learning (MMRL) methods, reaching 61.6% average vs. 60.6% for MMRL. On base-to-novel generalization, VACSR achieves the best base-class accuracy (85.74) but trails MMRL (81.20) and CoPrompt (80.48) in harmonic mean (80.37), indicating a slight trade-off in novel-class transfer. DAA performs catastrophically with the ViT-B/16 backbone (46.1% I2T R@1 vs. 79.8% with ViT-B/32), highlighting its sensitivity to backbone choice, whereas VACSR scales consistently across both backbones without architectural changes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the VAE framework (Kingma & Welling, 2013) and the line of probabilistic embedding methods for cross-modal retrieval (PCME, PCME++, DAA, P2RM), which model intra-modal ambiguity by mapping images and texts into random variables. The key conceptual departure is that VACSR models the cross-modal similarity itself as a probabilistic distribution in a latent space, rather than modeling the uncertainty of individual modalities. This reframing allows the method to attribute uncertainty to the matching relationship (annotation error) rather than to the data, enabling adaptive down-weighting of false-negative gradients through a distributional optimization loss. The analysis of contrastive and sigmoid loss sensitivity to false negatives (building on Wang & Liu, 2021; Zhai et al., 2023) provides the theoretical motivation for why binary annotations disrupt semantic structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental limitation in vision-language model fine-tuning: binary sparse annotations compress a continuous similarity space into discrete boundaries, creating false negatives that degrade generalization. The reframing of uncertainty as a property of the matching relationship rather than the data offers a principled way to handle annotation noise without inflating uncertainty on semantically clear samples. However, the base-to-novel generalization results are mixed (VACSR trails MMRL and CoPrompt on novel-class accuracy), and the method's reliance on a 2-component GMM with a fixed architecture raises questions about scalability to more complex semantic distributions. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to larger backbones (ViT-L, ViT-H), whether the number of GMM components should be adaptive per sample, and whether the uncertainty signal can be exploited for active annotation or test-time adaptation.
