## Problem

Existing MLLM-based video anomaly detection methods either require large-scale fine-grained annotations to fine-tune model parameters or rely on hand-crafted expert reasoning pipelines, both incurring substantial human intervention. The paper identifies that pretrained VLMs already encode rich general knowledge and that the main bottleneck is the absence of explicit, task-specific anomaly experience rather than limited model capacity, motivating a tuning-free approach that learns anomaly experience from limited weak labels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262385/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, each video is converted to an M=16 key-frame sequence via a temporal sampler, and the frozen VLM receives this sequence along with a task prompt and an injected experience context (general experience plus Top-K retrieved scenario experiences) to produce a complete anomaly reasoning output from which a scalar anomaly score p(o)∈[0,1] is parsed. At inference, the video is partitioned into sliding windows of L=4 frames with a 16-frame stride; each window is scored independently, and the per-frame scores are aggregated to localize anomalous temporal segments. The experience context is dynamically constructed per input via a dual-branch visual-semantic retriever over a pre-indexed scenario experience repository.

## Method

LRPO keeps all VLM parameters frozen and instead iteratively optimizes an editable linguistic anomaly experience repository that is injected as in-context conditioning to steer the model's output distribution. The optimization loop samples G reasoning trajectories from the Learner VLM, scores them with a three-component anomaly alignment reward (accuracy, preference alignment via contrastive similarity to scenario experience, and temporal dependency via ordered-vs-shuffled frame comparison), then uses an Optimizer LLM to perform group-wise reflective comparison and distill semantic advantages into executable ADD/MODIFY/DELETE/KEEP edits on the repository. Two complementary experience types are maintained: general experience capturing transferable anomaly preferences across scenarios, and scenario experience encoding context-dependent rules constructed from weak labels under a rule template. A first-order analysis shows that context perturbation from experience editing induces a distribution shift analogous to a small parameter update, and a GRPO-style group-relative objective with clipping and KL regularization bounds per-step changes for stable iterative improvement.

## Results

Using only 2.5% of XD-Violence and 6% of UCF-Crime training videos (100 videos per dataset), LRPO achieves 73.17% AP on XD-Violence and 85.36% AUC on UCF-Crime, outperforming all prior tuning-free methods. With the full training set, performance improves marginally to 74.09% AP and 86.59% AUC, confirming high sample efficiency. On UBnormal (open-set, zero-shot transfer from XD-Violence experience), LRPO reaches 76.24% AUC, setting a new state of the art among tuning-free approaches. The method also generalizes across backbones, yielding +13.83% AP on LLaVA-NeXT-7B and +13.24% AP on InternVL3_5-8B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among tuning-free methods, LRPO (2.5%/6% data) surpasses the strongest prior method VERA by +3.06% AP on XD-Violence (73.17 vs. 70.11) and by +4.16% AUC on UBnormal (75.81 vs. 71.65), while trailing VERA by 1.19% AUC on UCF-Crime (85.36 vs. 86.55); with full training data, LRPO edges past VERA on UCF-Crime (86.59 vs. 86.55). Against other tuning-free baselines, LRPO leads by a wide margin: +5.10% AP over URF (68.07), +4.32% AP over VADTree (68.85), and +11.16% AP over LAVAD (62.01) on XD-Violence. On UBnormal, LRPO outperforms AnomalyRuler (71.90) by +3.91% AUC and URF (69.02) by +6.79% AUC. Compared to tuning-based methods, LRPO with 100% data (74.09% AP) still trails the best weakly-supervised baselines such as Ex-VAD (86.52% AP) and PEL4VAD (85.59% AP) on XD-Violence, but the paper positions this gap as the cost of avoiding parameter updates and large-scale fine-grained annotations. The cross-dataset transfer experiment further shows LRPO's experience generalizes better than VERA's guiding questions (76.24% vs. 71.65% AUC on UBnormal), indicating that distilled anomaly-preference rules are more transferable than question-driven cue focusing.

## Relation to Prior Work

LRPO carves out a third paradigm between instruction-tuned VAD (which fine-tunes MLLM parameters with large-scale annotations) and training-free VAD (which relies on hand-crafted expert pipelines). It builds directly on the Group Relative Policy Optimization framework from DeepSeekMath (Shao et al., 2024), but instantiates the group-relative advantage distillation in a language-editable experience space rather than parameter space, and draws on verbalized-learning techniques (Yuksekgonul et al., 2025; Xiao et al., 2024) for the in-context injection mechanism. The closest prior work, VERA (Ye et al., 2025), also uses verbalized learning for VAD but optimizes guiding questions for cue-focusing; LRPO instead iteratively distills reusable anomaly-preference experiences and decision principles through a Learner-Optimizer loop with a multi-component alignment reward.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework demonstrates that a frozen MLLM can be adapted to VAD with only 100 weakly-labeled videos (2.5% of the training set), matching or exceeding full-training-set performance, which substantially reduces annotation cost for safety-critical deployment. The learned experience is human-readable and editable, offering interpretability that parameter-based methods lack, and the cross-dataset transfer (XD-Violence → UBnormal) suggests the distilled principles are genuinely reusable rather than distribution-specific. Key open questions include how well the experience repository covers rare or unseen anomaly mechanisms absent from the limited training subset, and whether the approach extends to open-world settings where the anomaly taxonomy itself is unknown. The finding that scaling scenario experience during learning degrades performance (shortcut learning) while scaling at inference helps is a practical caution for future experience-based adaptation methods.
