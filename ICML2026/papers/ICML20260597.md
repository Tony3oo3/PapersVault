## Problem

Visual Contrastive Decoding (VCD) assumes that noise-induced feature drift in LIP encoders aligns with the hallucination direction, but the paper proves this assumption flawed: the drift is a coupled vector entangling structural degradation (loss of genuine visual information) with hallucination induction (drift toward linguistic priors). VCD's indiscriminate inverse penalty therefore suppresses valid visual semantics alongside spurious concepts, causing irreversible degradation of semantic fidelity. The paper asks how to decouple the pure hallucination direction from structural degradation to enable targeted correction.

## Input / Output

DVR takes a standard RGB image and a text query as input and produces an autoregressive text response, operating as a training-free plug-and-play inference module on existing MLLMs (LLaVA-1.5, Qwen2-VL, Qwen3-VL). Beyond the original image, the method internally generates a noise-perturbed copy (I' = I + ε, ε ~ N(0, σ²I)) and processes both through the MLLM's native LIP encoder and an auxiliary frozen SSL encoder (DINOv3-ViT-L/16), requiring no additional training data or model weights. The only tunable hyperparameters are the contrastive weight α, noise intensity σ, structural calibration β, and modulation strength λ.

## Method

DVR exploits the geometric orthogonality between LIP and SSL encoder responses under noise: LIP encoders (CLIP, SigLIP) exhibit coupled drift (structural + hallucination) because their cross-modal training causes them to 'complete' ambiguous features toward high-frequency textual concepts, while SSL encoders (DINOv3) exhibit only structural degradation since they lack textual supervision. Stream I (Visual-Layer Rectification) computes patch-level sensitivity maps from both encoders, subtracts the SSL response (scaled by β) from the LIP response to isolate the hallucination indicator map D, then applies exponential gating γ_i = exp(λD^(i)) to selectively suppress hallucination-dominant visual tokens (D>0) and enhance structure-loss tokens (D<0) before language projection. Stream II (Decoding-Layer Contrast) performs logit-level contrastive decoding using the Stream-I-rectified features as the anchor rather than the original features, so the contrastive signal ℓ(Z^rect) − ℓ(Z^noise) isolates visual evidence without structural interference, addressing the visual fading phenomenon where attention to visual tokens diminishes with decoding depth. The two streams target orthogonal failure modes—perception-side corruption versus generation-side prior takeover—and their composition yields approximately additive gains, achieving ~5× theoretical error reduction over VCD.

## Results

On CHAIR (LLaVA-1.5-7B), DVR reduces sentence-level hallucination (CHAIR_S) from 54.2% (baseline) to 47.4% and instance-level (CHAIR_I) from 18.5% to 13.8%, while improving Recall to 78.6%. On POPE, DVR achieves 89.5% accuracy (Random), 86.8% (Popular), and 80.2% (Adversarial) on LLaVA-1.5, and 92.3% F1 (Random) on Qwen2-VL. On MME, DVR scores 1745.0 (LLaVA-1.5) and 1884.9 (Qwen2-VL), and on LLaVA-Bench it raises average GPT-5 Accuracy from 5.25 to 7.10 (LLaVA-1.5). Full DVR adds only +113% latency over baseline (12.44s/iter), comparable to VCD's +109%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DVR outperforms all eight baselines across every benchmark. Against VCD (the closest prior method), DVR reduces CHAIR_S by 4.4 points (47.4 vs 51.8) and CHAIR_I by 2.4 points (13.8 vs 16.2) on LLaVA-1.5, while also improving Recall by 1.8 points—demonstrating that disentangling hallucination from structural loss avoids VCD's collateral suppression of valid features. On POPE, DVR leads VCD by +2.5 (Random), +3.9 (Popular), and +2.0 (Adversarial) accuracy on LLaVA-1.5, with the largest margin on Popular/Adversarial settings that exploit co-occurrence biases. Among the four recent training-free methods in Table 5, DVR achieves the best CHAIR_S (47.4 vs. 48.0–52.4), best CHAIR_I (13.8 vs. 14.8–16.8), best Recall (78.6 vs. 74.0–76.8), and best POPE F1 (90.0 vs. 84.0–87.6). On MME, DVR surpasses ONLY (which uses explicit object-level supervision) by +45.0 (LLaVA-1.5) and +49.8 (Qwen2-VL) total score, and on LLaVA-Bench it improves average Accuracy by +1.85 over baseline vs. VCD's +0.91. Notably, on Qwen3-VL-8B where baseline hallucination is already low, VCD actually worsens CHAIR_S (21.0→23.7) while DVR improves it (21.0→19.0), confirming that VCD's indiscriminate penalty harms valid features when structural degradation dominates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly challenges Visual Contrastive Decoding (VCD), the predominant training-free hallucination mitigation method, by proving its core assumption—that noise-induced feature drift aligns with the hallucination direction—is geometrically flawed. The key departure is introducing a dual-encoder paradigm: rather than relying on a single LIP encoder's noise response (as VCD does), DVR exploits the geometric orthogonality between LIP encoders (CLIP, SigLIP) and SSL encoders (DINOv3) to disentangle hallucination-inducing drift from structural degradation. This positions the work at the intersection of contrastive decoding and self-supervised representation learning, repurposing SSL encoders not as feature extractors but as structural-reference anchors for disentanglement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that VCD's indiscriminate penalty suppresses valid visual semantics—and that it actively worsens performance on newer, lower-hallucination models like Qwen3-VL—provides a concrete failure mode that practitioners should be aware of when deploying contrastive decoding. The LIP-SSL orthogonality insight opens a broader question: whether other training-paradigm asymmetries (e.g., generative vs. discriminative encoders) could enable similar disentanglement for other failure modes. Limitations include the requirement of an additional frozen SSL encoder at inference (modest memory/latency overhead), the assumption that structural correspondence between LIP and SSL encoders is approximately linear (calibrated by β), and evaluation limited to 7B/13B-scale models on static images.
