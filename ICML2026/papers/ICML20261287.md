## Problem

Classical Bayesian model selection requires running inference separately for each candidate model, which becomes intractable over combinatorially large model families (billions of configurations). Amortized alternatives that train a single network to approximate posteriors typically hard-code a fixed model prior or complexity penalty at training time, forcing practitioners to commit to a parsimony assumption before seeing the data. PRISM addresses both limitations by enabling amortized joint inference over discrete model structure and continuous parameters with a test-time tunable complexity hyperparameter.

## Input / Output

The method takes simulator observations x (e.g., dMRI signal measurements or function evaluations on a grid), a scalar complexity hyperparameter λ ∈ [0,1], and task-specific acquisition metadata (b-values and gradient directions for dMRI) as input. It produces a joint posterior p(M, θ | x, λ) decomposed into a discrete model posterior (autoregressive Bernoulli over a C-dimensional binary component mask) and a continuous parameter posterior whose effective dimensionality varies with the active model. The parameter decoder operates on a fixed-size global vector Θ ∈ ℝ^{d_max} but uses model-dependent attention masks to marginalize inactive components, so a single shared decoder serves all models in the family.

## Method

PRISM uses a transformer encoder-decoder with two parallel decoding streams: an autoregressive transformer decoder that factorizes the model posterior as a multivariate Bernoulli over binary component masks, and an EDM-style diffusion transformer (v-prediction) decoder that produces the parameter posterior over a global parameter vector. Observations x are injected via cross-attention, while the complexity hyperparameter λ and diffusion time t are injected through adaptive layer normalization (AdaLN), enabling test-time control of model parsimony without retraining. A key architectural choice is the M-dependent block attention mask in the diffusion decoder: for any inactive component, all attention connections to and from its parameter tokens are masked, effectively marginalizing unused parameters and reducing the global vector to the active subvector θ_M. Training jointly optimizes a Bernoulli negative log-likelihood for the model decoder and a diffusion v-prediction loss for the parameter decoder on online-generated simulation data, with the model prior p(M|λ) = ∏ Ber(M_k, λ) · ∏ Cat(M_K:M) sampled during training to amortize over the full range of λ.

## Results

On the symbolic regression task, PRISM achieves rRMSE near zero up to model spaces of 2^30 configurations and maintains top-5 model-selection accuracy above 90% even at 10^30-scale combinatorial spaces. On the dMRI Ball-and-Sticks (B3S) task, the inferred model posterior matches an unbiased Monte Carlo evidence estimator with R² = 0.97, and effective sample sizes reach ~60% (synthetic) and ~40% (real UKB/HCP data). In downstream probabilistic tractography, PRISM achieves a mean tractwise correlation of 0.96 with the MCMC reference (BedpostX), compared to 0.86 for the prior SBI_joint baseline. On the extended BSZT model family, PRISM-inferred models outperform both DTI and Rumba in signal reconstruction RMSE and leave-one-out cross-validation on in-vivo UKB data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PRISM clearly outperforms SBMI (Schröder & Macke, 2024) on the symbolic regression task across all model-space sizes, including in the small-training-data regime (Fig. 4b), by replacing the restrictive Gaussian mixture density network with a diffusion-based parameter decoder. Against the SBI_joint baseline (Manzano-Patron et al., 2025), PRISM improves tractography correlation with MCMC from 0.86 to 0.96 and yields sharper uncertainty contrast in crossing-fiber regions (Fig. 7). Compared to the MCMC gold-standard BedpostX (Behrens et al., 2003), PRISM matches downstream tractography quality (0.96 correlation) while being orders of magnitude faster (5 ms per model posterior sample vs. hours of MCMC). On the extended BSZT model family, PRISM-inferred models outperform both DTI (Basser et al., 1994) and the maximum-likelihood Rumba method (Canales-Rodriguez et al., 2015) in signal reconstruction and LOOCV generalization on real UKB data (Fig. 8b). PRISM also matches a strong single-model normalizing-flow baseline on synthetic dMRI data and improves on real data, while additionally providing the model posterior and amortizing across the full B3S family.

## Relation to Prior Work

PRISM directly extends SBMI (Schröder & Macke, 2024), which first proposed joint model-parameter inference over model families, by replacing its restrictive Gaussian mixture density network with a diffusion-based parameter decoder and its factorized model posterior with an autoregressive transformer. It also builds on the broader simulation-based inference literature (Cranmer et al., 2020; Radev et al., 2023) and 'all-in-one' amortized frameworks (Gloeckler et al., 2024; Radev et al., 2023a), but departs from them by amortizing over the discrete model identity itself as a high-dimensional structured latent and by introducing a test-time tunable complexity hyperparameter λ injected via AdaLN, connecting to conditional amortization (Elsemüller et al., 2023; Chang et al., 2025) in a way those frameworks do not address.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PRISM makes Bayesian model selection tractable over combinatorial model spaces (up to 10^30 configurations) where per-model MCMC or evidence estimation is infeasible, and the test-time λ control lets practitioners adjust the complexity–fit trade-off post hoc without retraining—a practical advantage over methods that hard-code the model prior at training time. The dMRI case study validates the approach on real neuroimaging data (UKB, HCP) against the MCMC gold-standard (BedpostX), showing competitive tractography correlations (0.96 vs. 0.86 for prior SBI) at orders-of-magnitude lower inference cost. Key limitations include quadratic scaling in the number of components C, the need for substantial simulation budgets, and the fact that only a tiny fraction of the model space is explored during training, leaving open whether structured priors or sparse-attention variants can push the method to libraries with thousands of components.
