## Problem

Existing generative models for temporal and spatio-temporal point processes are predominantly autoregressive, simulating events one-by-one via thinning, which ties sampling cost to event count and limits control over joint event patterns. The few non-autoregressive alternatives (Add-Thin, DSTPP) lack a forward noising mechanism grounded in the geometry of event configurations or the mass-variation structure inherent to random event counts, leaving it unclear how to continuously corrupt both event locations and event cardinality in a principled way.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a finite, unordered set of spatio-temporal event points (a counting measure on Z = S x T) of variable cardinality, optionally augmented with a context vector or a partially observed subset of events. It outputs a complete spatio-temporal event set of arbitrary size, generated jointly in a single reverse-diffusion pass rather than sequentially. Because the input and output are sets of variable length, the architecture must be permutation-equivariant and the training loss must tolerate count mismatch between predicted and target configurations.

## Method

The forward noising process is derived as the Wasserstein-Fisher-Rao (WFR) gradient flow of a generalized KL divergence toward a simple reference intensity, yielding a PDE that decomposes into a mass-preserving Langevin transport operator and a local source-sink (birth-death) term. At the sample-path level this is realized as a branching diffusion: each forward step first perturbs every event's (space, time) coordinates via an Euler-Maruyama Langevin update, then independently applies a birth-death update where each atom dies with a location-dependent hazard or spawns Poisson offspring. The reverse process is parameterized by a permutation-equivariant set denoiser that predicts, in parallel for all events, a drift/velocity field and a net-growth field approximating the time-reversed dynamics. Training minimizes a debiased entropic unbalanced optimal transport (Sinkhorn divergence) between the predicted and target counting measures, which is differentiable, scalable, and naturally handles the variable event counts that arise from the branching forward process.

## Results

On spatio-temporal point process (STPP) benchmarks, the proposed branching diffusion model achieves the best performance across all three metrics (WD, MMD, STSD) on all four datasets. On Syn-STPP-1, it attains STSD 0.12 (vs. 2.20 for DSTPP and 2.20 for SMASH), and on the real-world Earthquake dataset it reaches STSD 0.14 and MMD 0.12, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline DSTPP (STSD 2.21, MMD 0.36). On temporal point process (TPP) benchmarks, the method is competitive: it achieves the best STSD on Syn-TPP-2 (0.01) and Twitter (0.24), and the best WD and MMD on Covid-19 (0.37, 0.30), though Add-Thin and TriTPP remain competitive on MMD for some synthetic TPP datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On all four STPP datasets, the proposed method dominates every baseline on all three metrics. The gap is most dramatic on Syn-STPP-1, where STSD drops from 2.20 (DSTPP/SMASH) to 0.12, and on Earthquake, where STSD falls from 2.21 (DSTPP) to 0.14 and MMD from 0.36 to 0.12. Among STPP baselines, DSTPP is the strongest competitor (closest on WD for Syn-STPP-1 at 2.86 vs. 2.72), while SMASH is competitive on STSD for Syn-STPP-2 (1.07 vs. 0.38) but lags significantly on real-world data. On TPP benchmarks, the picture is more nuanced: Add-Thin achieves lower MMD on Syn-TPP-1 (0.05 vs. 0.04, essentially tied) and Twitter (0.05 vs. 0.11), while TriTPP matches Ours on Syn-TPP-1 STSD (0.013 vs. 0.013). However, Ours achieves the best STSD on Syn-TPP-2 (0.01) and Twitter (0.24 vs. 2.17 for Add-Thin), and the best WD and MMD on the sparse Covid-19 dataset (0.37, 0.30 vs. 0.80, 0.42 for Add-Thin), indicating the geometric transport formulation is particularly effective for count-sensitive and joint spatio-temporal fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the score-based diffusion framework (Song et al., 2020) to measure-valued data by deriving the forward noising process as the Wasserstein-Fisher-Rao gradient flow of a generalized KL divergence, building directly on the unbalanced optimal transport geometry of Chizat et al. (2018). The closest prior work, Add-Thin (Lüdke et al., 2023), also uses a non-autoregressive diffusion for temporal point processes but relies on ad hoc thinning/superposition kernels without a geometric derivation; DSTPP (Yuan et al., 2023) extends diffusion to spatio-temporal settings but similarly lacks a principled mass-variation mechanism. The key departure is the sample-path branching diffusion (Langevin transport + Poisson birth/death) that realizes the intensity-level WFR dynamics, combined with a permutation-equivariant denoiser (Zaheer et al., 2017) trained via entropic UOT Sinkhorn divergence (Séjourné et al., 2019) to handle variable event counts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a principled, non-autoregressive alternative to the dominant intensity-based paradigm for point process generation, avoiding the sequential thinning bottleneck and enabling joint refinement of event locations, times, and counts. Its robustness to severe temporal and spatial missingness (extrapolating plausible events from partial observations) opens practical applications in surveillance, seismology, and epidemiology where data are inherently incomplete. Key limitations include the computational overhead of Sinkhorn iterations during training, the mean-field approximation of the branching process that may underrepresent strong inter-event dependencies, and untested scalability to very high-dimensional mark spaces or long-horizon sequences.
