## Problem

Existing SNN-based point cloud methods face a dual challenge: oversimplified architectures sacrifice accuracy needed for demanding applications like autonomous driving, while the non-differentiability of spike signals causes training instability that limits generalization. No prior SNN approach simultaneously achieves ANN-competitive accuracy and the energy efficiency that makes SNNs attractive for edge deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266170/cropped_page4_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The network takes raw 3D point clouds (N×3 coordinates, uniformly sampled to 1024 points for classification) and first passes them through an ANN-based Conv-BN-ReLU embedding before entering the spiking encoder. Outputs include class labels for shape classification, per-point part labels for segmentation (using a PointNet++-style hierarchical decoder with SN-Conv-BN upsampling), and per-point semantic labels for scene segmentation. Two architectural variants exist: SpikeNet-N masks irrelevant points via a column-sum Npoint Masker, while SpikeNet-C filters redundant channels via a row-sum Channel Masker.

## Method

SpikeNet combines two novel modules: the Spiking Vector Mask Transformer (SVMT) and the Dynamic Sparse Spiking Residual (DSSR). SVMT replaces standard attention with a binary spike-based mechanism where Q, K, V are all {0,1} spike tensors, and the attention operator τ is implemented as channel-wise subtraction (Q−K) passed through a spiking neuron, eliminating softmax and multiply-accumulate operations entirely; a Spike Point Masker then applies a binary mask (over channels or points) to the attention output, making computation scale with input sparsity. DSSR uses a two-stage residual structure where each stage applies SN→Conv→BN followed by a skip connection, keeping internal computations as floating-point additions (mimicking SNN accumulation) while the residual path preserves gradient flow and sparsity. The full encoder stacks four stages of GAM→DSSR→AMP→SVMT, progressively halving point count and doubling channel dimension, and is trained end-to-end with IF neurons (T=3 time steps) using an ATan surrogate gradient. The energy model accounts for MAC operations in the embedding and FC layers plus AC-only operations in the spiking layers, weighted by per-layer firing rates.

## Results

On ModelNet40, SpikeNet-C achieves 93.4% OA and 90.9% mAcc with only 1.7 mJ energy consumption, surpassing the best SNN baseline E-3DSNN (91.7% OA) by 1.7% and approaching ANN-level performance (PointMLP: 94.1% OA) while using 34× less energy. On ScanObjectNN, SpikeNet-C reaches 85.5% OA and 83.3% mAcc, outperforming the best SNN baseline P2SResLNet (81.0% OA) by 4.5% with 40% lower energy. For part segmentation on ShapeNetPart, SpikeNet-C achieves 83.9% Cat.mIoU and 85.4% Ins.mIoU at 5.4 mJ, beating E-3DSNN by 2.2% and 1.6% respectively. On S3DIS semantic segmentation, SpikeNet-C attains 68.9% mIoU at 10.2 mJ, outperforming E-3DSNN (67.4% mIoU, 14.4 mJ).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among SNN baselines, SpikeNet-C decisively outperforms all competitors: it beats E-3DSNN (91.7% OA) by 1.7%, SPT (91.4%) by 2.0%, P2SResLNet (90.6%) by 2.8%, and Spike PointNet (88.2%) by 5.2% on ModelNet40, while also reducing energy by 9× versus SPT (13.3→1.7 mJ). On the more challenging ScanObjectNN, the gap widens: SpikeNet-C (85.5% OA) surpasses P2SResLNet (81.0%) by 4.5% and SPT (78.0%) by 7.5%. Against ANN baselines, SpikeNet-C (93.4% OA) trails the best ANN methods PointMLP (94.1%) and PointNeXt (94.0%) by only 0.7% on ModelNet40, yet consumes 34× less energy than PointMLP (1.7 vs 59.0 mJ) and 7× less FLOPs. On ScanObjectNN, SpikeNet-C (85.5%) approaches PointNeXt (87.7%) and PointTramba (89.1%) while using 33× less energy than PointMLP. For segmentation on ShapeNetPart, SpikeNet-C (83.9% Cat.mIoU) beats E-3DSNN (81.7%) by 2.2% and the classic ANN PointNet++ (81.9%) by 2.0%, at the lowest energy (5.4 mJ vs 85.1 mJ for PointNet++). On S3DIS, SpikeNet-C (68.9% mIoU) outperforms E-3DSNN (67.4%) by 1.5% with 30% less energy, though it remains below the best ANN method PTv3 (73.6% mIoU) by 4.7%.

## Relation to Prior Work

SpikeNet sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) transformer-based point cloud processing (Point Transformer's vector attention, PCT's offset attention) and (2) spiking neural network architectures for vision (Spikformer's spiking self-attention, QKformer's QK-based linear attention). The key departure from prior SNN point cloud work (Spike PointNet, P2SResLNet, SPT, E-3DSNN) is the replacement of softmax-based or dot-product attention with a binary spike-mask mechanism (SVMT) that uses channel subtraction (Q−K) as the attention operator, combined with a Dynamic Sparse Spiking Residual (DSSR) module that adapts ResNet-style skip connections to the discrete spiking regime. Unlike QKformer, which relies on 2D spatial correlation, the Spike Point Masker explicitly handles the irregular, sparse topology of 3D point clouds by masking either points or channels based on spike activity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in enabling point cloud perception on battery-constrained and thermally-limited edge devices (autonomous driving, robotics), where the 10–34× energy reduction over ANN baselines while matching classification accuracy is a meaningful deployment advantage. However, a notable gap persists on semantic segmentation benchmarks (e.g., 68.9% vs. 73.6% mIoU on S3DIS, 71.2% vs. 77.9% on ScanNet V2), indicating that SNN-based point cloud understanding has not yet closed the accuracy gap with mature ANN architectures on complex scene-level tasks. Open questions include whether the architecture can be deployed on actual neuromorphic hardware (the paper acknowledges the Embedding and FC layers still require MAC operations), whether the energy savings translate to real-time latency gains on target chips, and whether the approach scales to denser or larger-scale point clouds beyond the 1024-point regime tested here.
