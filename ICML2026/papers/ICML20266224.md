## Problem

Existing automated multi-turn jailbreak methods (Crescendo, ActorBreaker, X-Teaming) operate as stochastic search over predefined heuristic spaces, making them brittle and predictable against highly aligned frontier models like O1 and GPT-5-chat. This paper identifies a fundamental generalization gap: static strategic templates degrade substantially on advanced targets, and no prior framework enables an attacker to autonomously diagnose a target's specific defense logic and adapt its policy in real-time during inference.

## Input / Output

Metis takes a textual malicious goal, a black-box target LLM with unknown defense configuration, and a set of seed attack paradigms (a prior distribution of known attack vectors) as input. It produces a multi-turn jailbreak prompt sequence bounded by T_max=5 turns, accompanied by explicit metacognitive reasoning traces structured as [think], [strategy], and [prompt] blocks. The Evaluator outputs a structured JSON feedback tuple (scalar score 0–10, justification, meta-suggestions) at each turn, where only a score of exactly 10 (full, unambiguous, actionable fulfillment) counts as a successful attack, enforcing a conservative ASR threshold.

## Method

Metis reformulates jailbreaking as inference-time policy optimization within an adversarial POMDP, where the attacker maintains a qualitative belief state over the target's hidden defense mechanism. A dual-agent architecture couples an Attacker (policy network, instantiated with DeepSeek-R1-V528) and a Metacognitive Evaluator (GPT-4o) in a closed-loop. At each turn, the Attacker executes a Three-Stage Cognitive Sequence: (1) Introspective Diagnosis, performing causal inference to update its belief about the target's defense philosophy; (2) Adaptive Policy Formulation, deriving a refined abstract strategy orthogonal to the identified defensive boundary; and (3) Executable Instantiation, compiling the strategy into a concrete prompt. The Evaluator provides dense 'semantic gradient' feedback—a high-dimensional directional signal comprising a scalar reward, justification, and state-dependent meta-suggestions (policy pivot for low scores, strategic escalation for mid scores, finalization for high scores)—that replaces sparse success/failure flags and enables rapid convergence within a single trajectory. The entire process is training-free, relying on in-context meta-learning within the conversation window, and achieves an average 8.2× token reduction over search-based baselines by replacing redundant parallel exploration with directed single-thread optimization.

## Results

On HarmBench, Metis achieves an average Attack Success Rate (ASR) of 89.2% across 10 target models, reaching 100.0% on Grok-3 and maintaining 76.0% on O1 and 78.0% on GPT-5-chat where baselines degrade substantially. On AdvBench, Metis achieves 85.8% average ASR. In terms of efficiency, Metis reduces token consumption by an average of 8.2× (up to 11.4×) compared to X-Teaming, with an Average Queries to Success (AQS) of 1.84 versus 4.56–12.48 for baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Metis outperforms all 10 baselines on average across both benchmarks. Against the strongest multi-turn baseline X-Teaming (82.0% avg on HarmBench), Metis leads by 7.2 points overall, with the largest gap on GPT-5-chat (78.0% vs. 49.0%, a 29-point margin) and O1 (76.0% vs. 71.0%). ActorBreaker, while competitive on open-weight models (85.5% on Llama-3-70B), degrades severely on frontier targets, dropping to 22.0% on Claude-3.7 and 14.0% on O1. All single-turn methods (GCG, PAP, PAIR, CodeAttack, CipherChat, AutoDAN-Turbo) trail Metis by 40–68 points on average, with CodeAttack being the strongest single-turn baseline at 42.4%. On AdvBench, Metis (85.8%) similarly dominates ActorBreaker (51.0%) and CoA (44.2%). In efficiency, Metis achieves 8.2× lower token cost than X-Teaming and 6.7–10.6× lower than ActorBreaker while simultaneously attaining higher ASR.

## Relation to Prior Work

Metis departs from the dominant paradigm in automated red teaming, where multi-turn attacks (Crescendo, ActorBreaker, X-Teaming) operate as stochastic search over predefined heuristic spaces, by reformulating jailbreaking as inference-time policy optimization within an adversarial POMDP. It draws conceptually on metacognition research in LLMs (Didolkar et al., Toy et al.) and the self-evolving agent literature (Reflexion, Voyager), but is the first to cast the jailbreak task explicitly as a policy optimization problem driven by dense semantic-gradient feedback rather than sparse success/failure signals. The dual-agent architecture (Attacker + Evaluator) repurposes the evaluator role from a passive scorer into an active gradient estimator, a framing not present in prior multi-turn frameworks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 8.2× average token-efficiency gain (up to 11.4×) over multi-agent search baselines while simultaneously achieving higher ASR is practically significant for resource-constrained security auditing, and the defense analysis (Section 4.6) provides concrete evidence that static safeguards—RLHF alignment, proxy classifiers, input perturbation—remain systematically vulnerable to closed-loop reasoning trajectories. A key open question is whether next-generation defenses can match the adaptive, inference-time reasoning that Metis exploits, and whether the approach's reliance on strong backbone LLMs (DeepSeek-R1, GPT-4o) limits its applicability in settings where only smaller models are available. The interpretability of reasoning traces also opens a path toward using attack trajectories for self-play safety training, though the dual-use nature of the framework necessitates careful access control.
