## Problem

Linear-decoder knowledge graph embeddings (LD-KGEs) project low-dimensional hidden states into high-dimensional entity score spaces via a linear output layer, creating a rank bottleneck when the number of entities (10^5–10^9) far exceeds the embedding dimension (10^2–10^4). Previous expressivity analyses provided only sufficient bounds for specific bilinear models, leaving open what dimension is *necessary* for any LD-KGE to represent valid rankings, signs, or distributions. This paper derives those necessary bounds and shows they grow with graph size and connectivity, making them impractical for large dense KGs.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a knowledge graph query (s, r, ?) or (?, r, o) and produces a probability distribution over all |E| candidate entities via a mixture of K softmax distributions. Unlike a standard single-softmax output, each mixture component applies its own two-layer MLP projection f_k to the hidden state h_{s,r} before scoring against the shared entity embedding matrix E ∈ R^{|E|×d}, so the output is a weighted sum of K distinct d-dimensional score vectors rather than a single one. The mixture weights π_k(h_{s,r}) are computed as a softmax over h_{s,r}^T ω_k, making the distribution input-dependent.

## Method

The paper makes three contributions. First, it proves necessary embedding dimension bounds for three task objectives (ranking, sign, and distributional reconstruction) under both universality (all graphs of size |E|) and realisability (a specific graph Y), showing requirements scale as |E|−1, |E|, or srank(Y^±)−1. Second, it connects the intractable sign rank to graph connectivity via Alon et al.'s polynomial construction, yielding a sufficient dimension d_SR = 2c+1 (worst-case out-degree) or 2b+1 (with RCM reordering), which tightens by ~5×. Third, it introduces KGE-MoS, a drop-in non-linear output layer that replaces the single softmax with a mixture of K softmaxes, each using a component-specific projection f_k(h_{s,r}) but sharing the entity matrix E. This breaks the rank bottleneck with only O(Kd²) extra parameters (vs. O(d_inc(|E|+|R|)) for increasing dimension), and empirically improves MRR and NLL on large dense datasets (Hetionet, ogbl-biokg, openbiolink) while leaving small datasets (FB15k-237) unaffected.

## Results

On the three larger, denser datasets (Hetionet, ogbl-biokg, openbiolink), KGE-MoS consistently improves both NLL and MRR over its bottlenecked baseline at both d=200 and d=1000. At d=1000, the average NLL improvement across models is −0.23 (Hetionet), −0.43 (ogbl-biokg), and −0.22 (openbiolink), with MRR gains of +0.035, +0.024, and +0.021 respectively (all statistically significant). On the smallest dataset FB15k-237, KGE-MoS provides no benefit and sometimes degrades performance (e.g., DistMult-MoS MRR drops from .331 to .311 at d=1000), confirming that rank bottlenecks are not critical at small scale. The best-performing models are typically DISTMULT-MoS or COMPLEX-MoS, suggesting a simple encoder with an expressive output layer is most effective.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DISTMULT (Yang et al., 2014), KGE-MoS wins on all three large datasets at both dimensions: at d=1000, DistMult-MoS improves MRR from .288→.312 (Hetionet), .801→.837 (ogbl-biokg), and .316→.347 (openbiolink), while NLL drops by 0.28, 0.55, and 0.28 respectively. Against COMPLEX (Trouillon et al., 2017), the pattern is similar with COMPLEX-MoS achieving MRR .303 (Hetionet), .836 (ogbl-biokg), and .345 (openbiolink) at d=1000, closely matching DistMult-MoS. Against RESCAL (Nickel et al., 2011), RESCAL-MoS shows the largest MRR gain on Hetionet (.243→.300 at d=1000) but a smaller gain on openbiolink (.328→.328, essentially tied). Against ConVE (Dettmers et al., 2018), results are mixed: ConVE-MoS wins on Hetionet and openbiolink at d=1000 but loses on ogbl-biokg (.807→.817 MRR) and FB15k-237. Against CompGCN (Vashishth et al., 2020), evaluated only at lower dimensions due to compute constraints, CompGCN-MoS shows modest MRR gains on FB15k-237 (.300→.310) and openbiolink (.263→.274) but a slight drop on ogbl-biokg (.749→.744). Overall, the gains are strongest for simple bilinear encoders (DISTMULT, COMPLEX) on large, dense graphs, consistent with the theoretical prediction that rank bottlenecks matter most when |E| ≫ d and connectivity is high.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the Mixture of Softmaxes (MoS) output layer from language modelling (Yang et al., 2018) to knowledge graph embeddings, repurposing a technique designed to break rank bottlenecks in softmax classifiers for the link prediction setting. It extends prior expressivity analyses that provided sufficient dimension bounds for specific bilinear KGEs (COMPLEX, TUCKER, RESCAL) by deriving necessary bounds that apply to all linear-decoder KGEs, including neural variants, and by considering three task objectives (ranking, sign, and distributional reconstruction) rather than ranking alone. The theoretical framework connects to the sign-rank literature (Alon et al., 1985) and graph factorisation results (Chanpuriya et al., 2020) to express practical sufficient dimensions in terms of graph connectivity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that rank bottlenecks are a fundamental, previously underappreciated limitation of standard KGEs: for large and dense knowledge graphs, the embedding dimensions used in practice (10²–10⁴) are orders of magnitude below the necessary bounds (scaling with |E| or graph connectivity), meaning models are structurally incapable of representing many valid relational patterns. KGE-MoS offers a practical, low-parameter-cost fix that empirically validates the theory, but open questions remain about whether gradient-based optimisation can efficiently reach the theoretically guaranteed solutions, whether the bounds are tight for specific encoder architectures, and whether the approach extends to non-linear decoders or subject prediction without inverse relations. The finding that simple encoders paired with expressive output layers can outperform complex neural encoders suggests the field may have over-invested in encoder sophistication at the expense of output-layer design.
