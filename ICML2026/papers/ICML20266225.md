## Problem

While prior mechanistic interpretability work identified suppression-based mechanisms for negation (e.g., negative mover heads), it remained unclear whether LLMs also explicitly construct negated representations, and why models with high internal sensitivity to negation still produce incorrect answers on negative prompts. This paper addresses both questions: it identifies a competing shortcut mechanism that undermines correct negation outputs and provides a full mechanistic account of how negation is actually computed.

## Input / Output

The analysis operates on pre-trained base LLMs (~7B parameters: Llama-3.1-8B, Mistral-7B-v0.1, plus Qwen2.5, Qwen3, Gemma-2, OLMo-2 for benchmarking) given a curated dataset of 648 prompt pairs of the form 'X that is not Y is Z' with annotated gold answers. Outputs include layer-specific causally important attention heads, interpretable SAE latent features, and quantitative accuracy/sensitivity metrics. A distinctive aspect is the use of an LLM-based annotation pipeline (GPT-oss-120b) to semantically verify that LogitLens-projected attention outputs promote concepts related to 'not Y', scaling manual inspection across the full dataset.

## Method

The paper introduces Attention Sink ablation, which restricts an attention head to attend only to the first (BOS) and current tokens, effectively nullifying cross-position information transfer while preserving local MLP computation; this is applied cumulatively from a target layer onward to isolate shortcut attention heads in late layers. To trace the negation circuit, the authors combine Path Patching (swapping attention outputs between positive and negative prompts) with windowed Attention Sink to identify mid-layer attention modules (~layer 14) as causally critical. LogitLens projection of these attention outputs reveals an explicitly constructed negated representation (e.g., 'solid' for 'not gas'), confirmed by LLM annotation on >80% of examples. Finally, contrastive attribution scores applied to pre-trained SAEs identify specific MLP latents in late layers that amplify the negated representation into the final answer, establishing that construction of Y̅ is the dominant mechanism, with weaker concurrent suppression of Y.

## Results

On the curated negation dataset (648 entries), all six ~7B base models achieve near-saturated positive accuracy (91.8–96.5%) but only 45.2–57.6% negative accuracy, while sensitivity to negation remains uniformly high (95.1–97.8%). Applying the proposed Cumulative Attention Sink ablation improves negative accuracy by 17.3 percentage points for Llama-3.1-8B (50.5→67.8%) and by 46% relatively for Mistral-7B-v0.1 (45.2→65.9%). The best sink layer is consistently >0.5L across all models, localizing shortcut attention heads to middle-to-late layers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between the proposed Attention Sink ablation and LogitLens (nostalgebraist, 2020) as strategies for removing the influence of late-layer shortcut attention heads. Attention Sink consistently outperforms LogitLens across all six models on negative accuracy: by 4.2 points for Llama-3.1 (67.8 vs. 53.6), 6.0 for Qwen2.5 (65.4 vs. 59.4), 4.6 for Qwen3 (64.2 vs. 59.6), 6.4 for Gemma-2 (66.1 vs. 59.7), 4.3 for Mistral-v0.1 (65.9 vs. 61.6), and 7.1 for OLMo-2 (68.7 vs. 61.6). The gap is largest for Llama-3.1 and OLMo-2, suggesting that LogitLens's zero-ablation of all subsequent layers is less surgical than selectively restricting attention to the first and current tokens. Both methods confirm the existence of shortcut attention heads in middle-to-late layers, but Attention Sink provides a plug-and-play remedy without requiring additional forward passes or cross-prompt information.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the mechanistic interpretability line of work on negation, which previously identified 'negative mover heads' that suppress attended tokens (Yan & Jia, 2025; McDougall et al., 2024), by showing that a construction mechanism—where models explicitly build a compositional representation of 'not Y'—is actually the dominant pathway. It adapts the attention-sink concept from streaming LLMs (Xiao et al., 2024) into a new ablation tool and combines it with path patching (Wang et al., 2023), LogitLens, and SAEs to trace the full negation circuit. The paper also departs from prior black-box negation benchmarking (Kassner & Schutze, 2020; Gubelmann & Handschuh, 2022) by providing a mechanistic explanation for why models appear insensitive to negation at the output level despite high internal sensitivity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that correct negation circuits exist in mid-layers but are overridden by shortcut attention heads in late layers reframes how we interpret low black-box accuracy: it signals competing internal mechanisms rather than absent capability, with implications for model auditing and safety. The practical Attention Sink intervention offers a simple, training-free remedy that recovers 15–20 points of negative accuracy across six model families. Open questions include whether the construction-dominant mechanism generalizes to implicit negation forms (lexical, adverbial, pronoun-based), whether the shortcut heads can be eliminated through targeted fine-tuning, and whether the findings hold at larger scales where the balance between construction and shortcut mechanisms may shift.
