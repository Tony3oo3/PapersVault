## Problem

Existing quaternion self-attention (Tay et al., 2019) computes a quaternion-valued score via the Hamilton product and applies four independent softmax operations, requiring 16 real multiplications per quaternion pair. This paper asks whether the component-wise independence is genuinely necessary or structurally redundant, given that quaternion linear projections already entangle all four components before attention is computed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265324/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For the primary speech enhancement task, the input is a 16 kHz noisy waveform converted to STFT and encoded as a pure imaginary quaternion (amplitude, real part, imaginary part per time-frequency bin). The model outputs an enhanced waveform via magnitude masking and phase correction on the STFT domain. Cross-domain validation uses standard CIFAR-100 32×32 RGB images and SST-2 text sequences under matched architecture capacity.

## Method

The proposed shared-score quaternion self-attention replaces the Hamilton-product score (16 real multiplications, 4 independent softmaxes) with a single real-valued score computed via the quaternion inner product Re(Q⊗K†), requiring only 4 real multiplications and one shared softmax across all four components. A key theoretical result (Theorem C.1) proves that under quaternion linear projections, both the four component-wise scores and the single shared score are generated from the same interaction subspace U(W_Q, W_K), meaning component-wise independence merely re-parameterizes the same bilinear interactions rather than expanding the feature interaction space. The full architecture combines a QDilated DenseNet encoder/decoder with a quaternion Transformer or Conformer bottleneck (N=2 layers, 4 heads, attention along both time and frequency axes), trained with a composite loss (multi-resolution STFT, SI-SDR, complex L1, PESQ). A Quaternion RMSNorm is applied to queries and keys to stabilize score scales while treating the four components as a single unit.

## Results

On VoiceBank+DEMAND, the proposed QConformer (Shared Score) achieves PESQ 3.18, CSIG 4.36, COVL 3.79, and SI-SDR 19.36 dB with only 0.80M parameters, matching or exceeding the Hamilton-product QConformer baseline (PESQ 3.11) while using 75% fewer score-computation multiplications. On DNS-Challenge 3, the QTransformer variant improves OVRL from 2.61 to 2.67 and BAK from 3.44 to 3.61, while reducing GPU RTF by 44.3% (0.0192→0.0107) and CPU RTF by 58.1% (0.594→0.249). Cross-domain validation on CIFAR-100 (70.94% vs 71.09%) and SST-2 (81.04% vs 81.12%) confirms quality is preserved within seed-level variance with consistent efficiency gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary quaternion baseline (Tay et al., 2019 Hamilton-product attention), the proposed shared-score QConformer improves PESQ from 3.11 to 3.18 and COVL from 3.73 to 3.79, while the QTransformer variant matches PESQ (3.07) and slightly improves CSIG (4.30 vs 4.26) and SI-SDR (19.69 vs 19.62 dB). Compared to the real-valued Conformer (3.19M params, PESQ 3.25), the QConformer retains 98% of the PESQ with only 25% of the parameters (0.80M). The method outperforms SEGAN (PESQ 2.16), TSTNN (2.96), DCCRN (2.59), and QTN (2.76) by substantial margins, and edges out MetricGAN+ (3.15) and SE-Conformer (3.13) on PESQ. It is essentially tied with NSE-CATNet (3.19 vs 3.18 PESQ) but uses 4.5× fewer parameters (0.80M vs 3.57M). The only method that clearly outperforms the proposed approach on PESQ is CMGAN (3.41), which relies on adversarial training and 1.83M parameters; the paper notes this as a favorable efficiency trade-off. On DNS-Challenge 3, the shared-score QTransformer improves OVRL (2.67 vs 2.61) and BAK (3.61 vs 3.44) over the Tay et al. baseline while cutting GPU RTF by 44.3% and CPU RTF by 58.1%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends and revises the quaternion self-attention mechanism introduced by Tay et al. (2019), which computed a full Hamilton-product score matrix and applied four independent softmax operations to each quaternion component. The authors depart from this design by proving (Theorem C.1) that, under the component pre-mixing induced by quaternion linear projections, both the four component-wise scores and a single shared score (the quaternion inner product) are generated from the same interaction subspace, making the independent softmax a redundant re-parameterization rather than an expansion of the feature interaction space. The work also positions itself against subsequent quaternion approaches (QTN, quaternion convolutions, rotational embeddings) that sidestepped Hamilton-product attention entirely, arguing that the algorithmic efficiency and theoretical properties of Hamilton-product-based attention had remained unexplored since 2019.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that component-wise independent attention in quaternion networks is structurally redundant challenges a natural design assumption—that four independent attention distributions capture genuinely different feature interactions—and provides a principled justification for simplifying quaternion attention to a single shared score with 75% fewer multiplications and one softmax instead of four. Practical impact is clear for real-time speech enhancement (up to 44.3% GPU and 58.1% CPU RTF reduction) and for parameter-efficient deployment on resource-constrained devices. However, the evidence is confined to small models (0.62–0.80 M parameters), architectures with quaternion pre-mixing projections, and three domains; whether the redundancy persists for larger models, longer contexts, or tasks that genuinely require component-wise alignment (e.g., multimodal fusion, 3D rotation estimation) remains an open question.
