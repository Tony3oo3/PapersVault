## Problem

Existing multi-objective GFlowNet methods—whether preference-conditioned scalarization (MOGFN, HN-GFN) or classifier-guided logical operators (compositional sculpting)—require joint training for each objective combination, so any change in the reward set necessitates retraining from scratch. These approaches also typically address only one formulation (scalarization or logical composition) rather than providing a unified framework. The paper tackles the open challenge of composing pre-trained GFlowNets across arbitrary reward sets and diverse composition schemes without any additional training at the composition stage.

## Input / Output

The method takes k independently trained GFlowNets (each providing a forward policy p_{i,F} and, depending on the training objective, a state flow F_i or backward policy p_{i,B}), a preference weight vector ω, and a composition operator G (weighted sum, harmonic mean, or contrast). At inference time it sequentially constructs structured objects—molecules in fragment or atom-based settings, or grid paths in the synthetic domain—by sampling from the mixed forward policy, producing samples from the composed distribution p_M(x) ∝ G(p_1(x), …, p_k(x)). The reaching probability u_i(s) is obtained either by direct readout from the learned state flow (Model F route, for FM/DB/SubTB-trained models) or by recursive accumulation of forward/backward policy ratios along the trajectory (DB F route, compatible with TB-trained models), requiring no additional model queries beyond those already performed for sampling.

## Method

The core mechanism is a state-dependent mixing policy p_{M,F}(s'|s) = G(u_1(s)·p_{1,F}(s'|s), …, u_k(s)·p_{k,F}(s'|s)) / N_M(s) that weights each base GFlowNet's transition probability by its reaching probability u_i(s) at the current state before combining through the composition operator G. The key insight is that u_i(s) naturally routes generation toward the model that considers the current state more relevant to its objective, providing an adaptive, state-level routing mechanism. For linear scalarization (β=1), the paper proves the mixing policy exactly induces the target distribution p_M(x) ∝ Σ_i ω_i R_i(x) (Proposition 4.1), with the distortion factor δ(x) = u_M(x)/N_M(x) being constant across all states. For nonlinear operators, δ(x) is not guaranteed constant, but the paper shows empirically that δ(x) ≈ 1/Z_M in high-density regions where it matters most, keeping the L_1 error small. The entire composition is training-free, incurring only 8–40 ms additional inference overhead for scalarization and running 30–65× faster than classifier-guided composition for logical operators.

## Results

On the 2D grid scalarization task, the proposed mixing policy achieves a constant L1 error of 0.003 across 2–5 objectives, dramatically outperforming MOGFN (0.021–0.048) and HN-GFN (0.017–0.035), whose errors grow with the number of objectives. On fragment-based molecule generation with all three objectives (SEH, SA, QED), the method attains a top-10 average reward of 0.741 (Model F) and hypervolume of 0.674, matching or exceeding MOGFN (0.723 / 0.665) and HN-GFN (0.670 / 0.651). For logical operators, the method outperforms classifier guidance on target-bin percentage (e.g., 87% vs. 81% for p_SEH ⊗ p_SA) while being 30–65× faster at inference (29.6 ms vs. 1900.7 ms per sample). Inference overhead for scalarization is only 8–40 ms per sample above single-model baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MOGFN (Jain et al., 2023), the proposed method wins decisively on the 2D grid (L1 error 0.003 vs. 0.021–0.048) and on molecule generation reward in most objective combinations (e.g., 0.741 vs. 0.723 for ALL fragment; 0.727 vs. 0.688 for ALL QM9), while also achieving the best hypervolume in most settings (0.674 vs. 0.665 fragment ALL; 0.658 vs. 0.664 QM9 ALL, a near-tie). Against HN-GFN (Zhu et al., 2023), the method similarly dominates on the grid (0.003 vs. 0.017–0.035) and is competitive on molecules, winning on reward for SEH-QED (0.775 vs. 0.711 fragment) and ALL (0.741 vs. 0.670 fragment) but trailing slightly on SA-QED diversity. Against classifier-guided compositional sculpting (Garipov et al., 2023), the method wins on logical-operator target-bin percentage across nearly all compositions (e.g., 87% vs. 81% for p_SEH ⊗ p_SA; 65% vs. 40% for the 3-way harmonic mean) and is 30–65× faster at inference, while the L1 errors on the 2D grid are comparable (sometimes better, e.g., 0.111 vs. 0.175 for contrast). Against OP-GFN (Chen & Mauch, 2024), the method achieves substantially higher hypervolume in most settings (0.674 vs. 0.476 for ALL fragment; 0.665 vs. 0.591 for ALL QM9), though OP-GFN is not directly comparable on per-preference reward metrics since it does not condition on preference vectors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GFlowNet framework (Bengio et al., 2021; 2023) by introducing a training-free mixing rule over pre-trained forward policies, weighted by reaching probabilities. It unifies two previously separate multi-objective strategies—preference-conditioned scalarization (MOGFN, HN-GFN) and classifier-guided logical composition (compositional sculpting, Garipov et al., 2023)—under a single inference-time framework. Conceptually, the approach draws on the broader model-composition lineage (products of experts, energy-based composition, diffusion score combination) but is the first to achieve training-free composition specifically for GFlowNets, removing the need for auxiliary classifiers or joint retraining that prior methods require for each new objective set.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework makes pre-trained GFlowNets reusable building blocks, so adding a new objective only requires training one additional single-objective model rather than retraining the entire multi-objective system. The exactness guarantee for linear scalarization and the distortion-factor characterization for nonlinear operators provide clear theoretical grounding, while the 30–65× inference speedup over classifier guidance for logical operators has direct practical value. Open questions include how the distortion factor behaves in very high-dimensional state spaces where reaching-probability estimation may be noisy, and whether the approximation quality for nonlinear operators degrades gracefully as the number of composed objectives grows beyond three.
