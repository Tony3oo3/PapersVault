## Problem

Existing symbolic music tokenizations (event-based like REMI, notation-based like ABC) serialize music as sequences where individual tokens span variable and uncertain durations, forcing models to implicitly infer the underlying temporal grid. This paper asks whether a uniform-length musical step (a beat) can instead serve as the basic tokenization unit, explicitly encoding temporal regularity while retaining the compactness that makes event-based representations practical for autoregressive Transformers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263717/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For continuation, the model receives a 4-bar quantized piano-roll prompt (16th-note resolution) and generates a 30-bar continuation in either single-track piano or multi-track format. For real-time accompaniment, the model takes a melody track plus past accompaniment beats as strictly causal context and produces the next accompaniment beat, with all tracks interleaved at beat-level synchronization. The piano-roll is resampled to τ=4 sub-steps per beat, and the token sequence scales as O(N·M̄) where N is the number of beats and M̄ is average polyphony.

## Method

BEAT encodes each beat's active pitches as (pitch, pattern, velocity) triples: the temporal state vector (onset/sustain/silence over τ=4 sub-steps) for each pitch is converted to a single pattern token via base-3 arithmetic, pitches are sorted descending with relative-interval encoding (first pitch absolute, subsequent pitches as offsets), and velocity is summarized by a per-pitch mean. Empty beats collapse to a single REST token, and the full sequence is assembled with BEAT/BAR delimiters and per-track INS prefixes, yielding a sparse serialization of the piano-roll that is compact yet explicitly time-regular. The model is a 150M-parameter LLaMA-style decoder-only Transformer with RoPE, trained with standard next-token cross-entropy—no special factorization or auxiliary loss. This beat-aligned structure enables strict causal real-time generation (accompaniment at beat i conditioned only on beats 1..i−1) and confers approximate invariance to transposition and rhythm shifts, which the paper validates through pattern-constrained synthetic tasks and repetition-diversity analysis.

## Results

On piano continuation, BEAT achieves the best JS_GC (0.039), JS_SC (0.021), and FMD (436.7) among all methods, substantially outperforming the next-best baselines (AMT-L: JS_GC 0.625, FMD 445.2). On multi-track continuation, BEAT again leads with JS_GC 0.043 and FMD 420.9, while REMI+ is marginally better on JS_SC (0.008 vs. 0.009). In the real-time accompaniment task, BEAT significantly outperforms SongDriver on all three subjective criteria (Coherence, Plausibility, Musicality) with p<0.05. The Naive Piano-Roll ablation (JS_GC 0.051, FMD 582.3) confirms that BEAT's gains stem from its sparse encoding rather than the beat grid alone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BEAT decisively outperforms REMI/REMI+ on JS_GC (0.039 vs. 0.552 piano; 0.043 vs. 0.313 multi-track) and FMD (436.7 vs. 550.9; 420.9 vs. 463.2), with REMI+ holding only a marginal edge on multi-track JS_SC (0.008 vs. 0.009). Against Compound Word, BEAT wins on all three piano metrics (JS_GC 0.039 vs. 0.634, JS_SC 0.021 vs. 0.024, FMD 436.7 vs. 587.0); CPW is not evaluated on multi-track. Interleaved ABC is outperformed by BEAT on every metric in both settings, with the largest gap on JS_GC (0.039 vs. 0.677 piano). AMT-Large (780M params) is the closest competitor on FMD (445.2 vs. 436.7 piano; 441.8 vs. 420.9 multi-track), but BEAT still leads despite being ~5x smaller. In the real-time accompaniment comparison, BEAT significantly surpasses SongDriver on all subjective criteria, demonstrating that its beat-aligned tokenization enables fine-grained temporal control that event-based or rule-based systems cannot match.

## Relation to Prior Work

BEAT carves new ground by repurposing the piano-roll grid representation—previously confined to non-sequential architectures like hierarchical VAEs (PianoTree VAE, MusicVAE) and diffusion models—into a sparse, beat-aligned token sequence compatible with standard autoregressive Transformers. It departs from the dominant event-based tokenization lineage (REMI, Compound Word, Music Transformer) by making the uniform temporal grid an explicit inductive bias rather than an implicit property the model must infer, and from notation-based approaches (Interleaved ABC, MuPT) by avoiding variable-length token combinations that obscure temporal regularity. The design draws on the patch-based serialization idea from vision Transformers (ViT) but adapts it to music's inherent sparsity, encoding only active pitches per beat rather than fixed-size patches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BEAT addresses a practical gap: event-based tokenizations cannot support real-time, beat-synchronized accompaniment because their tokens are asynchronously aligned, whereas BEAT's uniform beat-level structure enables strict causal streaming generation with a single autoregressive model. The method's compactness (shorter sequences than REMI or ABC) and higher BPE compressibility suggest it may scale favorably to larger models and longer contexts, though the current 150M-parameter evaluation leaves open whether the advantage persists at scale. Limitations include reliance on quantized input (excluding expressive performance MIDI), coarse per-pitch velocity modeling, and a training corpus skewed toward Western tonal music, all of which constrain generality.
