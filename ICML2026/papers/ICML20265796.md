## Problem

Multi-task radiology report generation (RRG) methods rely on static linear scalarization to combine report generation with auxiliary clinical constraint tasks (disease classification, image-text alignment, retrieval), but this coarse strategy cannot reconcile the intrinsic conflict between hard discriminative supervision and smooth language modeling. The paper identifies and formalizes this failure as a 'Double Dilemma'—drift term deviation causing directional oscillation and diffusion term decay causing energy depletion—using a stochastic differential equation framework, revealing why existing MTL optimizers fail in this setting.

## Input / Output

CAME-Grad is a backbone-agnostic optimizer that operates on the gradient vectors of a multi-task RRG model during training; it does not alter the input (chest X-ray image) or output (free-text radiology report) of the underlying architecture. The optimizer consumes per-task gradients (primary report generation loss plus K auxiliary clinical constraint losses) and produces a single fused update direction, replacing the standard weighted-sum gradient. It is evaluated on MIMIC-CXR (270K train / 3.8K test) and IU X-Ray (3,955 reports), with clinical efficacy measured via CheXbert-extracted 14-disease labels and NLG quality via BLEU, METEOR, and ROUGE-L.

## Method

CAME-Grad resolves the Double Dilemma through three cascaded stages applied to the joint gradient. Stage 1 (Conflict-Averse Direction Rectification) solves a CAGrad-inspired min-max trust-region problem via dual variables on the unit simplex to find a direction with non-negative inner product with all task gradients, ensuring geometric validity. Stage 2 (Magnitude-Enhanced Energy Injection) rescales the rectified direction to a target magnitude τ_mag = κ‖g_joint‖, restoring the diffusion kinetic energy lost during projection and enabling escape from sharp minima. Stage 3 (Adaptive Gradient Fusion) linearly interpolates between the enhanced rectified gradient and the magnitude-scaled original joint gradient using a coefficient ν, preserving task-specific inductive biases (e.g., long-tail token signals) that pure rectification would discard. The entire pipeline is implemented as a drop-in replacement for linear scalarization with negligible GPU overhead via tensorized dual solving, and is validated across eight diverse RRG backbones on two datasets.

## Results

On MIMIC-CXR, CAME-Grad as a plug-and-play optimizer improves the CE Average (mean of Precision, Recall, F1) by an average of 2.3% across eight diverse RRG backbones, with NLG metrics remaining stable. On the strongest baseline REVTAF, CE Average rises from 0.607 to 0.626 and F1 from 0.589 to 0.607. On IU X-Ray, the average CE improvement is 1.9%, with particularly notable gains for in-domain fine-tuned models (e.g., DCL +4.6% CE Average). In direct comparison with seven MTL optimizers on REVTAF, CAME-Grad achieves the highest CE Average of 0.626, outperforming the next-best STGU (0.618) and CAGrad (0.615).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAME-Grad is evaluated as a backbone-agnostic optimizer on eight RRG architectures (WCL, XProNet, DCL, PromptMRG, CAMANet, DDaTR, TGRG, REVTAF) and consistently improves CE Average over each baseline's linear-scalarization default by 1–5% on MIMIC-CXR and 0.7–4.6% on IU X-Ray, while NLG metrics remain essentially unchanged. In the head-to-head comparison with seven MTL optimizers (Table 3, all on REVTAF/MIMIC-CXR), CAME-Grad achieves the best CE Average of 0.626, beating STGU (0.618), FAMO (0.616), CAGrad (0.615), UW (0.611), RotoGrad (0.608), GradNorm (0.604), and MMPareto (0.076, which collapses due to over-penalizing the generative task). Magnitude-weighting methods (UW, GradNorm, FAMO) provide only marginal gains because they cannot resolve directional conflicts exceeding 90°, while CAGrad's rigid projection mitigates conflict but triggers diffusion decay that degrades recall (0.613 vs. CAME-Grad's 0.637). The ablation confirms that all three stages are necessary: removing direction rectification (S1) drops PromptMRG F1 from 0.491 to 0.476, removing energy injection (S2) drops DDaTR F1 from 0.438 to 0.425, and removing adaptive fusion (S3) degrades precision and BLEU-1 across most backbones.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on CAGrad's min-max trust-region formulation for gradient conflict resolution, extending it with two additional stages (magnitude-enhanced energy injection and adaptive gradient fusion) to address what the authors term the 'Double Dilemma' of drift deviation and diffusion decay. It positions itself against the broader MTL optimizer literature (UW, GradNorm, RotoGrad, FAMO, MMPareto, STGU), arguing that existing methods address either direction or magnitude in isolation and fail to jointly resolve both within the geometrically valid tangent space. The SDE framework from Mandt et al. (2017) provides the theoretical lens that unifies these two failure modes into a single mechanistic explanation, which is the paper's primary conceptual departure from prior gradient-manipulation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CAME-Grad offers a genuinely plug-and-play optimizer that requires no architectural changes and delivers consistent (if modest) clinical efficacy gains across eight diverse RRG backbones, making it practically useful for the RRG community. However, the paper itself acknowledges that clinical evaluation relies on automatic labelers (CheXbert) rather than clinician judgment, that the SDE interpretation currently exceeds direct empirical validation, and that multiple hyperparameters (ρ, κ, ν) must be tuned per architecture. Open questions include whether the SDE-based drift/diffusion decomposition generalizes beyond RRG to other multi-task medical imaging tasks, and whether the theoretical guarantees (Pareto descent, geometric validity) hold under the stochastic, non-convex conditions of real training.
