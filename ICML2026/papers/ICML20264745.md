## Problem

Automated IC footprint geometry labeling from datasheet diagrams remains unsolved because existing LMMs lack the domain-specific geometric reasoning to interpret dense engineering annotations, and no public dataset or benchmark exists for this task. Traditional CV+OCR pipelines fail at the relational reasoning required to associate numeric labels with their corresponding geometric elements, while general-purpose LMMs miscount pins, misread spacing annotations as dimensions, and cannot resolve implicit or omitted information in footprint drawings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264745/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single datasheet page image (or synthetic diagram) as input, which may contain multiple diagrams, mixed units, 3-view illustrations, and inconsistent annotation styles. Output is structured as three sequential QA pairs in a single conversation: (1) total pin count, (2) center coordinates of each pin in mm relative to the diagram's geometric center, and (3) width and length of each pin in mm. The conversation follows a chain-of-thought ordering that mirrors how human engineers progressively extract geometry from whole to detail.

## Method

LMM4-IC4K fine-tunes Qwen2-VL-7B via LoRA (rank 8, alpha 16) using a two-stage supervised training paradigm on the newly constructed ICGEO8K dataset (8,608 samples: 4,138 real-world + 4,470 synthetic). Stage 1 trains exclusively on synthetic diagrams with clean, uniform annotations to establish basic geometric priors without real-world noise; Stage 2 fine-tunes on real-world datasheet pages to adapt to varying resolutions, fonts, and multi-diagram layouts. The key design choice is organizing each training sample as a single multi-turn conversation with three progressive questions (pin count → coordinates → dimensions), which ablations show outperforms independent or multi-round training by 12.8% in overall IoU_IC. The synthetic pre-training is critical: it lifts performance from 1.7% (base model) to 42.9% before any real-world data is seen, and the full two-stage pipeline achieves 71.6% IoU_IC, surpassing the industrial manual EDA baseline by 62.7%.

## Results

On the ICGEOQA benchmark (400 real-world IC entries), LMM4-IC4K achieves an overall IoU_IC of 71.6%, substantially outperforming the best general LMM (GPT-5 at 43.0% with 4-shot) and the industrial manual EDA baseline (44%). For sub-tasks, it achieves MAE of 0.35 pins (counting), d_pin of 1.11 mm (positioning), and IoU_pin of 88.0% (dimension estimation). The model also delivers a 28× speedup (15 s/sample vs. ~7 min/sample for Altium) and a 435× cost reduction ($0.0014 vs. $0.61 per sample) compared to manual labeling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LMM4-IC4K (71.6% IoU_IC) decisively outperforms all six general LMM baselines. The strongest general LMM, GPT-5, reaches only 43.0% IoU_IC even with 4-shot in-context examples, a 28.6-point gap. Gemini 2.5 Flash improves from 16.5% (1-shot) to 38.0% (4-shot) but still trails by 33.6 points. Qwen2-VL-7B without fine-tuning scores just 1.7–3.8%, confirming that domain-specific fine-tuning is essential rather than relying on general LMM capabilities. Against the industrial manual EDA baseline (44% IoU_IC), LMM4-IC4K achieves a 62.7% relative improvement, indicating it more faithfully reconstructs the geometries described in datasheet diagrams than human engineers using EDA tools. The traditional CV+OCR+Rule-based pipeline (2.1% IoU_IC) fails catastrophically, particularly on pin dimensions (0.1% IoU_pin), highlighting the inadequacy of pattern-matching approaches for relational geometric reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two previously disconnected lines of work: (1) PCB/IC footprint automation, which has relied on segmentation, object detection, and OCR pipelines that cannot perform the relational reasoning between diagram symbols and numeric annotations, and (2) LMM-based geometric reasoning, which has been explored almost exclusively on abstract or synthetic geometry (G-LLaVA, MathVista) rather than dense, annotation-rich engineering drawings. The method builds directly on Qwen2-VL-7B as a base architecture and applies LoRA fine-tuning via LLaMA Factory, but the key departure is the two-stage synthetic-to-real training paradigm and the chain-of-thought dialogue structure that mirrors human engineer reasoning (count → locate → dimension). No prior work has formulated pin-level IC footprint geometry understanding as a multimodal VQA task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes the first systematic benchmark (ICGEOQA) and training dataset (ICGEO8K) for IC footprint geometry, filling a gap where no public resources existed. Practically, the 28× speedup and 435× cost reduction over manual EDA labeling, combined with 62.7% improvement over the industrial manual baseline, make this immediately relevant to PCB design workflows. Key open questions include: generalization to long-tail package types (BGA, LGA, TO) where performance drops significantly; the fundamental difficulty of detecting 'logically consistent inaccuracies' (e.g., swapped pin gap and width) that violate no geometric constraint; and whether the approach extends to other technical drawing domains (mechanical, architectural) with similar annotation-symbolic reasoning demands.
