## Problem

Standard generative models built on Lipschitz architectures (flow matching, diffusion, normalizing flows) provably cannot produce heavy-tailed outputs from Gaussian noise, and interpolating between heavy-tailed data and Gaussians is ill-posed. Existing fixes either replace the base distribution with heavy-tailed noise (causing training instability) or require per-dataset tail-parameter estimation. The paper asks whether a simple, parameter-free data transform can make vanilla flow matching work for heavy-tailed data without architectural changes or tail estimation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multivariate tabular data (d up to 100, with mixed heavy-tailed and light-tailed margins) and produces samples from the same heavy-tailed distribution. A key detail is the per-coordinate Hill diagnostic: each coordinate is independently tested (threshold α_max = 4) to decide whether the soft-log transform is applied, so light-tailed margins pass through unchanged while heavy-tailed ones are compressed. At sampling time, the inverse transform φ⁻¹(y) = sign(y)·(e^|y| − 1) is applied only to the flagged coordinates, recovering the original scale.

## Method

The core mechanism is a coordinate-wise soft-log transform φ(x) = sign(x)·log(1+|x|) applied to training data before running standard flow matching in log-space, with the inverse applied to generated samples. The theoretical insight is twofold: (1) the log-transform maps regularly varying (Pareto-type) tails to exponential-type tails, making the Gaussian interpolation well-posed and the log-space score bounded, so a standard MLP velocity network suffices; (2) the induced dynamics in original space factorize as X_t ≈ X_0^{α_t} · e^{β_t X̃_1}, where the power transformation X_0^{α_t} continuously lightens the tail index from γ to 0 as α_t decreases from 1 to 0 — a mechanism the authors call tail annealing. A Hill-estimator-based mask (threshold α_max = 4) gates the transform per coordinate, leaving light-tailed margins untouched at no added complexity. The approach is entirely parameter-free: the same transform applies regardless of the true tail index, and no heavy-tailed base distribution or architectural modification is needed.

## Results

On the 144-configuration multivariate benchmark (3 copulas, d up to 100, 4 tail indices α ∈ {1.5, 1.75, 2.0, 2.5}), Log-FM achieves the best median W₁ᴾ in 13 of 16 cells, with values ranging from 0.074 (α=2.5, d=20) to 0.534 (α=1.5, d=50). Pooled across all configurations, Log-FM is best on every tail-quality and risk metric (W₁ᴾ, CVaR₉₉, Q₉₉.₉, sliced Wasserstein, energy distance), and is the only method with zero severe divergences (W₁ᴾ > 1) for α ≥ 1.75 across all 2,880 runs. On real Fama-French 5 data (d=5, α̂≈3.7), Log-FM achieves W₁ = 0.133±0.013, competitive with gTAF (0.127±0.007) and substantially better than TTF/TTFfix (0.449/0.487).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TTF/TTFfix (Hickling & Prangle, 2025), Log-FM wins decisively on W₁ᴾ (e.g., 0.522 vs 1.149 at α=1.5, d=10; 0.153 vs 0.235 at α=2.0, d=10) and CVaR₉₉ (0.228 vs 0.880 at d=10), while TTF/TTFfix suffer 32–58% catastrophic divergence rates at α=1.5 versus 0–14% for Log-FM; TTF/TTFfix retain only a small angular-W₂ edge at d≥50 (0.040 vs 0.049). Against gTAF (Jaini et al., 2020), Log-FM dominates at heavier tails and higher dimensions (W₁ᴾ = 0.525 vs 3.320 at α=1.5, d=100) and has far fewer divergences (0% vs 17–59%); on Fama-French, gTAF holds a marginal edge (W₁ = 0.127 vs 0.133). Against mTAF (Laszkiewicz et al., 2022), the comparison is stark: mTAF diverges catastrophically on Fama-French (W₁ = 5.0×10⁴) while Log-FM remains stable. Overall, Log-FM is the only method achieving zero severe divergences across all 2,880 benchmark runs for α ≥ 1.75.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the flow matching framework (Lipman et al., 2023) and its stochastic-interpolant formulation (Albergo & Vanden-Eijnden, 2023), applying them in a transformed space rather than modifying the architecture or base distribution. It departs from two prior strategies for heavy-tailed generation: Jaini et al. (2020) replace Gaussian noise with Student-t base distributions (TAF), and Hickling & Prangle (2025) apply tail-modifying output transforms parameterized by Hill-estimated tail indices (TTF). Log-FM instead applies a parameter-free soft-log transform to the input data before training and exponentiates at sampling time, circumventing the Lipschitz barrier (Jaini et al., 2020) by placing the non-Lipschitz inverse outside the learned dynamics. The theoretical framing as 'tail annealing' via power transformations X₀^{α_t} connects the log-space interpolation to classical extreme-value theory (regular variation, Breiman's lemma) in a way neither prior approach provides.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that heavy-tailed generative modeling for finance, insurance, and climate extremes no longer requires unstable tail-parameter estimation or specialized architectures; a single coordinate-wise log-transform with a Hill diagnostic suffices, and the method is the only one with zero severe divergences across 2,880 runs. Limitations are clearly scoped: for α<1 (Cauchy and heavier) all methods struggle because W₁ is undefined, and at d≳200 the bottleneck shifts to architecture rather than tail modeling. Open directions include the continuous φ_{s₂} family (Appendix D), combining the transform with discrete normalizing flows for exact O(d) log-likelihoods, and finite-sample convergence guarantees for log-space score estimation where bounded scores (Proposition 3.2) place the problem in a favorable regime.
