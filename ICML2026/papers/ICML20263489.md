## Problem

Standard robust classification pipelines treat signal enhancement and classification as sequential, decoupled stages, where the enhancer is optimized for perceptual or reconstruction metrics that do not correlate with downstream accuracy. Fine-tuning the classifier on noisy data risks catastrophic forgetting, and prior logit-denoising methods (e.g., CARD) cannot generalize to noisy inputs because they condition only on the corrupted signal. This paper asks whether signal reconstruction and class prediction can be jointly modeled as interacting diffusion processes that mutually guide each other, without any modification to the classifier.

## Input / Output

The method takes a corrupted signal (noisy image at 32×32 or 28×28, or speech in the complex STFT domain) and a frozen pre-trained classifier as input, and produces clean continuous pre-softmax logits as output, from which a class label or transcription is read. The corrupted signal itself is retained as a conditioning variable throughout the diffusion process, and the classifier's output on the corrupted input initializes the logit diffusion. For speech, the logits are a sequence of per-token pre-softmax vectors produced autoregressively by the Whisper decoder, providing frame-level semantic guidance to the signal denoiser.

## Method

The core contribution is a generalized coupled diffusion framework that jointly denoises the input signal and the classifier's logits via two interacting reverse diffusion processes, with a scheduler determining their execution order. Three concrete strategies are derived: Parallel (step-level interleaving where each update is conditioned on the other's latest clean estimate), Alternating (full signal trajectory followed by full logit trajectory, repeated over outer iterations), and Nested (a complete inner signal diffusion loop executed at each outer logit step). Mutual guidance is realized by conditioning the signal denoiser on the current logit estimate and the logit denoiser on the current signal estimate, with both denoisers trained under a joint noise-prediction MSE loss that explicitly simulates cross-conditioning during optimization. The classifier remains frozen throughout; no ground-truth labels are required for training, as clean logits are obtained by running the frozen classifier on the clean training signal.

## Results

On image classification, the coupled strategies achieve substantial gains over sequential enhancement: on CIFAR-10 under 30% Gaussian noise, Alternating reaches 82.8% accuracy vs. 60.4% for the Enhanced baseline and 59.4% for URIE; on CIFAR-100 under the same corruption, Nested achieves 71.9% vs. 60.4% (Enhanced) and 54.1% (URIE); on ImageNet32-100 under 30% GN, Parallel reaches 65.5% vs. 50.6% (Enhanced). On speech recognition (WER, lower is better), Nested achieves 9.75% on EARS-WHAM and 3.83% on EARS-Reverb, outperforming the Enhanced baseline (10.04% and 4.48%) and Dissen (13.04% and 6.46%). The only exception is MNIST, where URIE marginally outperforms the coupled methods (99.4% vs. ~99.2%) due to the low complexity of the task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Enhanced baseline (SGMSE speech enhancement / unconditional diffusion for images), the coupled methods consistently win on all non-trivial datasets: e.g., on CIFAR-10 30% GN, Alternating (82.8%) surpasses Enhanced (60.4%) by 22.4 points, and on EARS-WHAM, Nested (9.75% WER) beats Enhanced (10.04%) by 0.29 points. Against URIE (image enhancer trained with joint regression + classification loss), the coupled methods win decisively on CIFAR-10 (up to +23.4 pts), CIFAR-100 (up to +19.2 pts), and IN32-100 (up to +38.7 pts), but lose by a narrow margin on MNIST (99.2–99.3% vs. 99.3–99.5%). Against CARD (logits-only diffusion conditioned on the fixed corrupted signal), the coupled methods achieve dramatically higher accuracy (e.g., 82.8% vs. 10.4% on CIFAR-10 30% GN; 9.75% vs. 36.58% WER on EARS-WHAM), confirming that conditioning on a static corrupted signal is insufficient. Against Dissen (audio enhancer trained with ASR loss), Nested outperforms on all three speech benchmarks (e.g., 3.83% vs. 6.46% WER on EARS-Reverb), showing that coupled logit guidance provides stronger task alignment than end-to-end enhancement training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends CARD (Han et al., 2022), which denoises classifier logits via diffusion conditioned on a fixed corrupted input, by coupling a second diffusion process over the signal itself so that both modalities mutually inform each other during denoising. The framework is grounded in standard DDPM derivations (Ho et al., 2020) and borrows the inner-loop generative abstraction from Nested Diffusion (Elata et al., 2024) for its Nested strategy, while the audio instantiation uses SGMSE (Richter et al., 2023) for STFT-domain signal diffusion. Unlike JEM (Grathwohl et al., 2019) which requires retraining the classifier, or URIE/Dissen which incorporate task supervision into the enhancer, this work keeps the classifier entirely frozen and achieves mutual guidance purely through the coupled diffusion dynamics, positioning itself as a drop-in robustness layer for any pre-trained classifier.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, domain-agnostic mechanism to improve robustness of large frozen classifiers (e.g., Whisper) without fine-tuning, addressing the catastrophic-forgetting and impracticality concerns of adaptation-based approaches. The three coupling strategies (Parallel, Alternating, Nested) provide a useful design space trading off computational cost against coupling strength, with Parallel achieving strong results in very few steps. Open questions include whether the approach scales to full-resolution ImageNet (only 10-class 224×224 was tested), how it interacts with modern acceleration techniques (distillation, flow matching), and whether the mutual-guidance benefit persists when the classifier is already highly robust or when corruptions are structured rather than random.
