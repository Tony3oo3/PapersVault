## Problem

Cross-modal knowledge distillation (CMKD) transfers knowledge from a teacher model on one modality to a student on another, but existing methods require paired multi-modal data with sample-level correspondence, which is costly and often unavailable in practice. This paper addresses the more challenging unpaired setting where no instance-level alignment between modalities exists, and shows that simply adapting feature-based distillation to this setting is ineffective.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264489/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained teacher model (e.g., ResNet-18 or ViT-B) operating on one modality (audio or video) and an untrained student model on a different modality, along with separate unpaired training sets for each modality sharing the same label space. Both feature extractors are configured to map into a shared embedding space Z. The output is a distilled student model producing class labels and prediction probability distributions; no paired samples or sample-level correspondence between modalities is required at any point during training.

## Method

The core contribution is a theoretical generalization bound decomposing the student's error into teacher error plus two new quantities: Feature Alignment (FA), a Wasserstein-1 distance between teacher and student representation distributions in the shared latent space computed via entropic-regularized optimal transport (Sinkhorn), and Label Alignment (LA), a novel loss weighted by a label transport kernel κ(y,z)=D^T(y|z)/D^S(y|z) that gates distillation based on teacher-student semantic agreement. To optimize these objectives stably, UCMKD employs a bi-level (meta-learning) framework: the inner loop first minimizes FA (stage 1) then LA (stage 2) on a temporary copy of the student, while the outer loop updates the student on its supervised loss using gradients from the adapted parameters. This staged inner optimization avoids the instability of simultaneously updating the feature extractor and predictor, which the ablation in Table 6 confirms degrades performance when both losses are combined without the bi-level structure.

## Results

In the unpaired cross-modal distillation setting (the paper's primary contribution), UCMKD achieves an average improvement of ~14.3% over the Cross-Entropy baseline and ~7.5% over Feature Distillation across AVE, RAVDESS, CREMA-D, and VGGSound. Notably, UCMKD surpasses the paired Vanilla KD baseline on 6 out of 8 transfer tasks despite having no sample-level correspondence. In the paired setting, UCMKD achieves the highest accuracy on 5 out of 8 tasks against seven SOTA baselines, with particularly large gains on RAVDESS A→V (76.06% vs. 68.08% for FitNet) and AVE A→V (33.50% vs. 29.85% for Vanilla KD).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the unpaired baselines, UCMKD dominates: it beats Cross-Entropy and Feature KD on all 8 transfer tasks, and outperforms the paired Vanilla KD (Hinton et al., 2015) on 6/8 tasks (losing only on CREMA-D A→V by 0.54% and VGGSound A→V by 0.25%). In the paired setting (Table 2), UCMKD decisively outperforms DKD, RKD, and RLD on all 8 tasks, with margins up to ~35% on RAVDESS A→V (76.06% vs. 41.60% for RKD). Against C2KD (Huo et al., 2024), UCMKD wins 5/8 tasks but C2KD retains an edge on RAVDESS V→A (82.78% vs. 75.13%) and VGGSound V→A (61.90% vs. 55.98%). UCMKD beats FitNet (Romero et al., 2014) on 7/8 paired tasks and Review (Chen et al., 2021) on 7/8, with the sole losses on VGGSound V→A. In the unpaired setting, UCMKD also surpasses the recent feature-based methods NORM (Liu et al., 2023) and Review on all 6 evaluated tasks (Table 11), confirming that distribution-level alignment is essential where sample-level correspondence is absent.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends cross-modal knowledge distillation (CMKD) from the paired-data regime (C2KD, COSMOS, XKD) to the unpaired setting, where sample-level correspondence between modalities is unavailable. Conceptually, it departs from prior feature-based distillation methods (FitNet, ReviewKD) by showing that representation alignment alone is insufficient for cross-modal transfer, and introduces a label-alignment term governed by a transport kernel κ(y,z) that selectively gates teacher knowledge. The optimization framework borrows the bi-level structure from MAML (Finn et al., 2017) and the pseudo-label estimation strategy from LEEP (Nguyen et al., 2020), but applies them to a novel distributional objective derived from a new generalization bound for CMKD.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first generalization bound for cross-modal KD that decomposes student error into teacher overhead, feature alignment, and label alignment, providing actionable theoretical guidance for algorithm design. The practical impact is significant: the method eliminates the need for costly paired multimodal data while matching or exceeding paired baselines, opening CMKD to settings where modalities are collected independently. Key limitations include the 1.2×–2.9× training overhead from bi-level optimization, reliance on a fixed Euclidean transport metric, and evaluation primarily on ResNet-18 backbones with moderate-scale datasets. Open directions include adaptive metric learning, implicit differentiation for efficiency, and extension to foundation-model-scale cross-modal transfer and generative modeling.
