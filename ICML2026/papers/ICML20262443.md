## Problem

Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models operate under a view-bound assumption: task-relevant objects must be visible in the current camera frame at decision time. When targets fall outside the field of view, these models lack any persistent spatial representation to reason about unseen objects, leading to inevitable task failure. Prior attempts to compensate via learned spatial priors or internal reasoning are brittle because they lack grounded perceptual evidence once the target is fully unobserved.

## Input / Output

SOMA consumes multi-view RGB-D observations from a movable head camera and two wrist cameras, a natural-language instruction, robot joint states, and a noised action sequence. The spatial memory is constructed offline from a scanning video sequence (frames sampled every N/3 steps) and refined online from head-view observations during manipulation. The output is a fixed-length action chunk of H steps predicted by a DiT-based action decoder, where the memory-enhanced vision-language tokens serve as a global spatial-semantic prior that modulates token interactions within the DiT blocks.

## Method

SOMA introduces a three-stage spatial memory pipeline atop a GR00T N1.5-style VLA backbone. (1) Spatial Memory Construction lifts 2D YOLO detections into 3D bounding boxes via VGGT-predicted camera poses, extracts DINOv3 appearance embeddings, and fuses multi-view instances into unified memory tokens m_k^0 = Φ_mem(f_k) + Φ_pos(b_k) in a shared global coordinate frame. (2) Dynamic Memory Refinement updates each memory entry per timestep using an adaptive exponential moving average weighted by the product of a learned semantic similarity score and a fusion confidence score, preserving stability under minor viewpoint changes while adapting to scene changes. (3) Contextual Memory Retrieval performs cross-attention with VLM vision-language tokens as queries and aligned memory tokens as key-value pairs, injecting instruction-relevant spatial cues into the DiT action decoder. The key design insight is that persistent, geometry-grounded spatial memory—rather than reactive viewpoint adjustment or ungrounded spatial reasoning—is what enables reliable manipulation when targets are outside the current visual frustum.

## Results

On RoboCasa Tabletop GR1, SOMA achieves the highest average success rate of 52.0% with 300 demonstrations per task and 49.3% with full data, outperforming GR00T N1.5 (44.3%/46.0%), Diffusion Policy (39.2%), and StarVLA (20.2%). On SimplerEnv, SOMA reaches 63.2% average in Visual Matching and 52.5% in Variant Aggregation, surpassing all listed SOTA baselines. On five self-designed real-world out-of-vision tasks, SOMA achieves 28.3% overall success rate and demonstrates 40–60% reductions in first-fixation time, head search path length, and grasp attempts compared to GR00T-N1.5, with near one-shot grasping behavior on the most challenging dual-arm task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SOMA outperforms all 11 baselines across the evaluated benchmarks. Against GR00T N1.5 (the primary VLA baseline), SOMA gains +7.7% average SR on RoboCasa GR1 at 300 demos (52.0% vs. 44.3%) and +8.2% on SimplerEnv Visual Matching (63.2% vs. 45.0%), while also achieving 40–60% behavioral improvements on real-world OOV tasks. StarVLA and SpatialVLA, which also use active head cameras, are substantially outperformed on RoboCasa (52.0% vs. 20.2% and lower real-world SRs respectively), confirming that reactive viewpoint adjustment without persistent memory is insufficient. Diffusion Policy, a non-VLA baseline, trails SOMA by 12.8% on RoboCasa at 300 demos. On SimplerEnv, SOMA edges out the closest competitors—OpenVLA-OFT (63.2% vs. 63.0% VM) and π0+FAST (63.2% vs. 61.9% VM)—while clearly surpassing RoboVLM (60.6%), MOTO (59.2%), π0 (58.8%), TraceVLA (42.0%), and OpenVLA (39.8% VA). Notably, SOMA with only 30 demos (48.3% average on RoboCasa) already exceeds GR00T N1.5 trained on the full dataset (45.9%), highlighting strong sample efficiency from the spatial memory inductive bias.

## Relation to Prior Work

SOMA builds directly on the GR00T N1.5 VLA architecture (DiT-based action decoder, VLM backbone) and augments it with a persistent spatial memory module, while leveraging VGGT for 3D geometric lifting, YOLO-World for open-vocabulary detection, and DINOv3 for appearance embeddings. It departs from the dominant view-bound paradigm in VLA research—where perception is strictly reactive to the current camera frame—by introducing a memory-centric perception loop with active multi-view scanning, temporal refinement via similarity-weighted EMA, and instruction-conditioned cross-attention retrieval. This positions SOMA between purely reactive VLAs (GR00T, StarVLA, SpatialVLA) and earlier movable-camera approaches (Xiong et al., Zeng et al.) that lack persistent scene memory, and distinguishes itself from short-term memory designs (MemoryVLA, Memer) by maintaining a globally consistent, object-centric spatial representation across the full manipulation horizon.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and underexplored failure mode of VLA systems: complete task collapse when the target leaves the camera frustum, a common scenario in real-world deployment with limited field-of-view. The behavioral analysis (fixation time, head search path, grasp attempts) goes beyond success-rate reporting to show that spatial memory induces qualitatively different, more decisive manipulation behavior. Key limitations include the reliance on a movable head camera, the offline preprocessing of memory construction, and evaluation on a small self-designed 5-task benchmark with only 20 episodes per task; generalization to highly dynamic scenes, longer-horizon tasks with significant object displacement, or multi-robot settings remains open. The finding that the memory mechanism also improves performance under fully observable settings (RoboCasa, SimplerEnv) suggests the structured spatial inductive bias may benefit VLA training more broadly, but this needs validation at larger scale.
