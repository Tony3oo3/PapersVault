## Problem

Existing Video Virtual Try-On (VVT) methods are confined to non-interactive scenarios where subjects passively display garments, overlooking the active human-garment interactions (e.g., pulling hems, unzipping jackets) that are central to live-streaming e-commerce. This gap introduces two unique challenges: 2D pose signals are semantically ambiguous for distinguishing interactive from non-interactive gestures, and interactive moments are sparse and brief in video, creating an unstable supervisory signal for learning complex physics-driven deformations.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source video (81 frames at 576×768), a target garment image, a pose sequence, a clothing-agnostic video, a garment-agnostic 3D hand prior extracted per-frame via HaMeR, a global motion caption, and time-stamped action captions structured as ("action description", [start_frame, end_frame]). It produces a temporally coherent try-on video where the target garment dynamically deforms in response to the subject's interactions. The framework is framed as a specialized video inpainting task on the Wan2.1-VACE backbone, with all inputs encoded into latent space by a frozen Wan encoder before iterative denoising.

## Method

iTryOn builds on the Wan2.1-VACE diffusion Transformer and introduces a multi-level interaction injection mechanism with two parallel pathways: a spatial Interaction Guider that processes the 3D hand prior (chosen over depth-based alternatives to avoid source-garment information leakage) for precise hand-garment contact, and a semantic pathway that injects global captions plus time-stamped action captions synchronized via a novel Action-aware Rotational Position Embedding (A-RoPE), which applies scaled 1D-RoPE (k=4) to queries of all video segments and to keys only for interactive-segment captions, creating dedicated temporal channels per action. To counteract the sparsity of interactive frames, an action-aware constraint loss (AC loss) adds a masked penalty term (λ=0.5) to the standard v-prediction diffusion loss, amplifying supervision exclusively on frames within annotated interaction segments. Training proceeds in two stages: 10k steps on ViViD with empty action captions, then 10k steps on the newly constructed VVT-Interact dataset (5,292 pairs, six interaction categories) to incorporate interactive capabilities.

## Results

On the VVT-Interact dataset, iTryOn achieves an Interaction Success Rate (ISR) of 61.0% (paired) and 61.5% (unpaired), substantially outperforming all baselines (best baseline: CatV2TON at 48.4%). It also attains the lowest FVD of 380.6 (paired) and best visual fidelity (SSIM 0.785, LPIPS 0.122, VFID_I 22.46). On the non-interactive ViViD-S-Test benchmark, iTryOn (2B params) achieves VFID_I of 8.43 and SSIM of 0.894, surpassing the 14B-parameter MagicTryOn (VFID_I 12.20) and DreamVVT (VFID_I 11.02).

## Baselines & Comparisons

iTryOn decisively outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric. Against ViViD (Fang et al., 2024), iTryOn improves ISR by 21.3 points (0.610 vs. 0.397) and reduces FVD by 87.9 points (380.6 vs. 468.5), while also leading on visual fidelity (SSIM 0.785 vs. 0.726). Against CatV2TON (Chong et al., 2025b), the gap is even wider on interaction metrics (ISR 0.610 vs. 0.484; FVD 380.6 vs. 533.2), with CatV2TON actually performing worst on FVD among all baselines. Against MagicTryOn (Li et al., 2025b), iTryOn gains 17.5 points in ISR (0.610 vs. 0.435) and 51.2 points in FVD (380.6 vs. 431.8). On the non-interactive ViViD-S-Test, iTryOn (2B params) surpasses DreamVVT (Zuo et al., 2025) by 2.59 in VFID_I (8.43 vs. 11.02) and 0.021 in SSIM (0.894 vs. 0.874), and beats the 14B MagicTryOn by 3.77 in VFID_I (8.43 vs. 12.20), demonstrating that the architectural choices generalize beyond the interactive setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Video Virtual Try-On (VVT) line of work (ViViD, CatV2TON, MagicTryOn, DreamVVT) by introducing active human-garment interaction as a first-class modeling target, departing from the passive-motion assumption shared by all prior methods. Architecturally, it builds on the Wan2.1-VACE video inpainting backbone and repurposes existing components—HaMeR for 3D hand reconstruction, DWPose for body pose, and Qwen-VL for annotation—into a novel multi-level interaction injection mechanism. The A-RoPE temporal alignment strategy is inspired by MinT's multi-event video generation, adapted here to synchronize action captions with specific video segments. The action-aware constraint loss is a targeted re-weighting of the standard diffusion objective, a relatively straightforward but effective response to the class imbalance between interactive and non-interactive frames.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By formalizing Interactive VVT and releasing the first dedicated dataset (VVT-Interact) with a purpose-built evaluation metric (ISR), this paper opens a concrete research direction that mirrors real-world e-commerce live-streaming scenarios where presenters actively manipulate garments. Two limitations remain open: the model lacks explicit garment-semantic reasoning (it cannot detect that unzipping a seamless T-shirt is infeasible, defaulting to a 'pantomime'), and the ISR metric, while a step forward, relies on VLM-based binary verification that may not capture fine-grained physical accuracy. The finding that training on interactive data alone is insufficient—explicit spatial and semantic guidance is required—suggests that future interactive generation tasks will similarly need structured conditioning beyond raw video supervision.
