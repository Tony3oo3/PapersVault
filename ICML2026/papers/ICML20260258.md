## Problem

Multiple MDP models with uncertain transition probabilities (robust, multi-model, percentile, CVaR-based, ERM-based) have been introduced independently with ad hoc analyses, leaving unclear which risk measures admit tractable dynamic programming solutions and whether new models beyond robust MDPs can be designed. The paper asks: what is the complete set of law-invariant risk measures for which Bellman equations hold, and what are the fundamental limits of the dynamic programming paradigm for epistemic uncertainty?

## Input / Output

The framework takes a probability distribution ν over the set of transition kernels Δ(S)^(S×A) (with convex support and product structure), a risk measure ρ applied to the random return, and standard MDP parameters (rewards, discount factor γ, initial distribution μ). The output is an optimal stationary policy π* and the ambiguity-averse value function V^{ν,ρ}, both characterized as fixed points of the ambiguity-averse Bellman operator T^{ν,ρ}v(s) = sup_π ρ(T^{π,P̃}v(s)), where the risk measure is applied to the nominal Bellman operator evaluated at a random kernel draw. The key structural constraint is that the risk measure acts on the expected return over trajectories (random only in the kernel), distinguishing this from risk-averse MDPs that hedge over trajectory variability.

## Method

The paper defines ambiguity-averse MDPs where transition probabilities are random variables drawn from ν, and the objective is ρ(V^{π,P̃}) — a risk measure applied to the random value function. It extends Bellman operators to T^{π,ν,ρ}v(s) = ρ(T^{π,P̃}v(s)) and proves that for monotone, translation-invariant ρ, these operators inherit monotonicity, translation-invariance, and ℓ∞-contraction, yielding linear convergence of value and policy iteration when Bellman fixed-point equations (Conditions 1 and 2) hold. The central characterization result shows that among law-invariant risk measures, dynamic programming forces ρ to be the identity on constants, positively homogeneous, and additive for independent variables; combining these algebraic constraints with representation theorems from statistics (Mu et al., 2024) proves that only ess inf, ess sup (static kernels) or ess inf, ess sup, and expectation (resampled kernels) satisfy dynamic programming. A stronger W¹-continuity assumption further restricts the answer to expectation only for resampled kernels and eliminates all risk measures for static kernels.

## Results

The paper's central result (Theorem 4.4) is a complete impossibility characterization: among monotone, law-invariant risk measures, dynamic programming (Bellman equations for both policy evaluation and optimization) holds for static kernels only when ρ is ess inf or ess sup, and for resampled kernels only when ρ is ess inf, ess sup, or expectation. A stronger continuity assumption (W¹-continuity, Theorem 4.5) further restricts the class: no risk measure satisfies DP for static kernels, and only expectation works for resampled kernels. When DP does hold, Theorem 4.1 guarantees existence of a stationary optimal policy, and Proposition 4.2 establishes linear convergence rates (γⁿ) for both value and policy iteration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical unification paper rather than an empirical comparison, so 'baselines' are existing MDP models that the ambiguity-averse framework subsumes. Robust MDPs (ess inf) and optimistic MDPs (ess sup) are the only non-trivial models compatible with dynamic programming for static kernels, confirming and generalizing the classical s-rectangularity results of Wiesemann et al. (2013). Multi-model MDPs (expectation) fail DP for static kernels (recovering the NP-hardness result of Steimle et al., 2021) but satisfy DP for resampled kernels (recovering the tractability result of Xu & Mannor, 2012). Percentile optimization (VaR) fails DP in both settings, explaining its known NP-hardness. The key contribution is showing that no other law-invariant, monotone risk measure (e.g., CVaR, ERM, EVaR) can satisfy DP, which explains why no new tractable model beyond robust/optimistic MDPs has emerged despite substantial interest in these alternatives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the nominal MDP framework (Puterman, 1994), the robust MDP literature (Wiesemann et al., 2013; Nilim & El Ghaoui, 2005), and the risk measure theory from mathematical finance (Föllmer & Schied, 2016) to propose a unifying 'ambiguity-averse MDP' framework where uncertain transition probabilities are modeled as random variables evaluated through a risk measure. It subsumes several independently-developed MDP variants—robust, optimistic, multi-model, and percentile optimization—under a single formalism, and draws on representation theorems from statistics (Mu et al., 2024) and distributionally robust MDP results (Xu & Mannor, 2012) to characterize which risk measures admit dynamic programming. The paper departs from prior work by providing a complete impossibility characterization rather than proposing yet another ad hoc model with its own analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central impossibility result (Theorem 4.4) shows that among monotone law-invariant risk measures, only ess inf, ess sup, and (for resampled kernels) expectation are compatible with dynamic programming, explaining why no new tractable models beyond RMDPs have emerged despite substantial interest in VaR- and CVaR-based approaches. The paper also clarifies the computational distinction between static and resampled kernels, showing that resampled kernels are strictly more tractable (e.g., multi-model MDPs go from NP-hard to polynomial-time solvable). Open questions include whether state-augmentation, nested risk measures, or direct gradient-based optimization can recover dynamic programming for richer risk criteria, and whether the characterization extends to average-reward or Blackwell optimality settings.
