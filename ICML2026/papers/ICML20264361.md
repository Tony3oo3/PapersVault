## Problem

RL post-training (e.g., GRPO) induces an unintended exploration collapse in Large Reasoning Models: the final-layer posterior becomes over-concentrated with sharply reduced entropy, rendering temperature-based sampling ineffective for improving pass@n accuracy. The paper identifies that intermediate layers still retain substantial uncertainty (a 'latent entropy reservoir'), but no prior method leverages this depth-wise entropy asymmetry to restore exploration without additional training.

## Input / Output

At each decoding step, LED consumes the final-layer hidden state plus hidden states from the last d−1 intermediate layers (d=8 by default), projecting each through the shared LM-Head to obtain layer-wise posteriors over the vocabulary. Exploration is restricted to the top-k tokens (k=8) identified from the final-layer posterior, and the method outputs a single next-token ID per step. LED is applied exclusively during the DeepThink (reasoning) phase and falls back to standard sampling during answer generation, introducing no extra parameters or training steps.

## Method

LED is a training-free, depth-conditioned decoding strategy built on three mechanisms: (1) top-k filtering restricts each layer's posterior to the final-layer's top-k candidate tokens, preventing noise from rare tokens; (2) a final-to-latent cumulative sum aggregates the filtered posteriors across d depths, producing d normalized candidate distributions without any learned or hand-tuned weights; (3) the candidate with maximum entropy is selected as the exploration posterior, while a parameter-free top-1 probability threshold on the final-layer posterior adaptively switches between exploration and standard exploitation decoding. The key insight is that cumulative-sum aggregation naturally emphasizes layers where the model has not yet committed to a single trajectory, and selecting the highest-entropy combination automatically identifies the richest exploration signal without hyperparameter tuning. The approach reactivates effective temperature scaling on RL-post-trained models and, when used as a GRPO rollout policy, yields faster reward improvement and higher final accuracy than regular decoding.

## Results

Across three RL-post-trained LRMs (Qwen3-4B-Thinking, MiMo-7B-RL, Qwen3-30B-A3B-Thinking) and six reasoning benchmarks, LED achieves the best overall pass@1 and pass@16 accuracy, improving by 0.61 and 1.03 percentage points on average over the CoT baseline. For example, on Qwen3-4B-Thinking, LED reaches 79.32% pass@1 and 89.86% pass@16 overall, versus 78.20% and 89.19% for CoT. Generation length increases by less than 1% (10,834 → 10,923 tokens), confirming negligible inference overhead. When integrated as a GRPO rollout policy, LED further improves final test accuracy from 41.99% to 45.44%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LED outperforms all four baselines on overall pass@1 and pass@16 across all three evaluated LRMs. Against the CoT baseline, LED gains +1.12 pp pass@1 on Qwen3-4B-Thinking (79.32 vs 78.20) and +0.61 pp on MiMo-7B-RL (70.84 vs 70.21). DoLa, which contrasts lower-layer posteriors with the final layer, is competitive on pass@1 for Qwen3-30B (84.04 vs 83.92) but trails LED on pass@16 (91.32 vs 92.48), indicating LED's superior exploration. SoftThinking suffers a severe exploration collapse, dropping pass@16 by 2.9–3.0 pp below CoT on Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-30B, while SoftThinking-Gumbel partially recovers but still falls short of LED on pass@1 for all three models (e.g., 77.81 vs 79.32 on Qwen3-4B). LED also uniquely restores a positive accuracy-temperature slope (α) for RL-post-trained models, where all baselines show flat or negative α.

## Relation to Prior Work

LED builds on the early-exit observation (Schuster et al., 2022) that intermediate Transformer hidden states can be decoded through the LM head, and on DoLa's (Chuang et al., 2023) use of layer-contrastive decoding, but departs from both in a key way: rather than contrasting low-layer posteriors against the final layer to amplify factual knowledge, LED selects the highest-entropy cumulative aggregation across recent layers to restore exploration. It also differs from SoftThinking and SoftThinking-Gumbel, which reshape exploration along the vocabulary dimension, by operating across network depth. The paper's diagnostic framing—that GRPO-style RL post-training specifically collapses final-layer entropy while leaving intermediate layers intact—provides a mechanistic rationale absent from prior decoding work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of a depth-wise entropy asymmetry as the root cause of exploration collapse in RL-post-trained LRMs gives practitioners a concrete diagnostic and a training-free, hyperparameter-free remedy that can be dropped into existing inference pipelines or RL rollout loops. However, the gains are modest (≈0.6 pp pass@1, ≈1 pp pass@16), the method fails on earlier models whose final-layer entropy has not yet collapsed (QwQ-32B), and the top-k filtering is so critical that removing it causes generation to diverge entirely—suggesting the approach is tightly coupled to the specific posterior-squeeze regime of current RL-trained models. Open questions include whether the entropy reservoir persists as RL post-training objectives evolve, whether LED generalizes to non-reasoning or multimodal settings, and whether the modest pass@n gains compound when LED is used as a long-horizon RL rollout policy at scale.
