## Problem

Existing LMM-based visual grounding pipelines (e.g., LISA, SESAME, GSVA) rely on a fixed last hidden layer to extract the <SEG> token embedding for prompting SAM, which sequentially amplifies cumulative errors through deep transformer stacks without intermediate correction. Additionally, the <SEG> token is a text placeholder that implicitly projects into visual space without explicit spatial cues such as coordinates. No prior work has also unified visual grounding from an attribute perspective, simultaneously handling explicit/implicit expressions, single/multi-target queries, and false-premise (empty-target) rejection within one framework.

## Input / Output

The model consumes an RGB image (resized to 336×336 using SAM's scaling-and-padding strategy to avoid CLIP's center-cropping artifacts) paired with a free-form text query that can range from a short referring expression to a multi-sentence reasoning question or a false-premise query. It produces a binary segmentation mask at the image resolution (H×W) generated by SAM's mask decoder, along with a natural-language explanation. For multi-target or false-premise queries, the model outputs multiple <SEG> tokens (one per target) or a <REJ> token to reject absent objects, with each token independently routed through the dynamic layer-selection mechanism.

## Method

UGround introduces Policy-Prompted Masking (PPM), built on LLaVA 1.5 + SAM, with two core components. Stochastic Skip Connection (SSC) formulates layer selection as a REINFORCE problem: a learnable weight vector w ∈ R^{L×d} scores each of the L unrolled transformer layers, and a categorical policy π_θ(ℓ|H_{t*}) stochastically samples which layer ℓ* the <SEG> token connects to SAM in a skip-connection fashion, bypassing all subsequent layers. Mask as Prompt (MasP) then computes a similarity map S^(ℓ*) = H_z^(ℓ*) · h_{t*}^(ℓ*)⊤ between the <SEG> embedding and image-token embeddings at the selected layer, reshapes it into a 2D grid, and feeds it as a soft logit mask into SAM's dense-prompt encoder, providing explicit spatial activation regions. Training jointly optimizes four losses: standard text-generation and SAM mask losses, a Gaussian-smoothed BCE + Dice loss supervising the similarity map against a soft ground-truth heatmap, and the REINFORCE policy loss with an EMA baseline. Across T forward passes, different layers are activated per pass (analogous to Monte Carlo Dropout), forming an ensemble of L subnetworks while incurring only single-layer computational cost per pass.

## Results

On the ReasonSeg benchmark, UGround-7B achieves 66.1% gIoU / 72.1% cIoU on val and 63.6% gIoU / 65.4% cIoU on test, surpassing the previous SOTA RSVP-GPT by +9.0% cIoU (val) and +5.4% cIoU (test). On RefCOCO(+/g), UGround-7B reaches 80.6/77.7/76.1 cIoU on RefCOCO val/testB/test(U), outperforming GLaMM-7B by up to +1.2%. On gRefCOCO, UGround-7B gains +12.1% N-acc over GSVA-7B(ft) on the validation set, demonstrating strong false-premise rejection. At the 13B scale, UGround further pushes to 74.9% cIoU on ReasonSeg val, exceeding READ-13B by +2.7% cIoU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UGround-7B outperforms all prior LMM-based reasoning segmentation methods on ReasonSeg: it beats RSVP-GPT (previous SOTA) by +9.0% cIoU on val and +5.4% cIoU on test, and exceeds LISA++-7B(ft) by +4.0% cIoU on val. On the classic RefCOCO(+/g) benchmarks, UGround-7B surpasses GLaMM-7B (the prior best) by +1.1% cIoU on RefCOCO val, +1.0% on RefCOCO+ testB, and +1.2% on RefCOCOg test(U), while also outperforming SegLLM-7B and OMG-LLaVA across all splits. On gRefCOCO, UGround-7B(ft) achieves 74.53% N-acc versus 62.43% for GSVA-7B(ft) (+12.1%), a substantial margin in correctly rejecting empty-target queries. On the MUSE multi-target benchmark, UGround-7B improves over PixelLM-7B by +5.7% cIoU (val overall) and +10.5% cIoU on the challenging few-targets split. On FP-RefCOCO(+/g), UGround outperforms READ by +1.59% to +2.99% in 'see' accuracy and +1.30% to +2.43% in segmentation cIoU, confirming its advantage in false-premise handling.

## Relation to Prior Work

UGround extends the LISA (Lai et al., 2024) paradigm of using a <SEG> token from a multimodal LLM (LLaVA) to prompt SAM for segmentation, but departs fundamentally from the fixed last-hidden-layer convention. Instead of passing the final layer's <SEG> embedding to SAM, UGround introduces Policy-Prompted Masking: a REINFORCE-based stochastic skip connection that selects an intermediate transformer layer, and a similarity map (dot product between <SEG> and image tokens at that layer) that serves as a soft logit mask prompt with explicit spatial cues. The paper also positions itself as the first to unify visual grounding from an 'attribute perspective'—spanning explicit/implicit expressions, single/multi-target, and positive/false-premise queries—rather than the versatility-oriented unification of prior works like GLaMM or PixelLM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that intermediate transformer layers (10–40) consistently outperform the fixed last layer for segmentation prompting challenges a widely-held assumption in LMM-based grounding pipelines, and the similarity map's ability to rival or exceed <SEG> token prompts (35.0% vs. 30.7% cIoU on ReasonSeg test) suggests that explicit spatial cues are more informative than implicit text-to-vision projection. However, the authors acknowledge that predicted masks remain fragmented and spatially misaligned, indicating that cross-modal geometric alignment between language-space embeddings and pixel-space structure is still an open problem. The RL-based layer selection adds meaningful training overhead (2.97s vs. 1.26s per step) for a modest ~1.5% gain over simpler soft-gating, raising questions about whether the added complexity is justified at scale. Future work on how <SEG> tokens interact with SAM's internal decoding, and whether the unrolled-layer approach transfers to video or conversational grounding, would strengthen the contribution.
