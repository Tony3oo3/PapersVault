## Problem

The choice of action space representation for imitation-based robotic manipulation policies—encompassing temporal parameterization (absolute vs. delta), spatial parameterization (joint-space vs. task-space), and implementation details like delta alignment frame and chunking horizon—remains guided by ad-hoc heuristics and undocumented legacy configurations rather than principled understanding. No prior work has systematically quantified how these design choices interact with model architecture, data scale, and learning regime to determine policy performance and control stability.

## Input / Output

The policy consumes 224×224 RGB frames from a third-person camera and wrist-mounted camera(s), concatenated with the robot's proprioceptive joint positions, and optionally a language prompt injected via FiLM conditioning. It outputs a chunk of k future actions (k up to 60 at 30 Hz) that undergo a two-stage decoding: first a temporal decode (identity for absolute, cumulative-sum for step-wise delta, or direct offset for chunk-wise delta relative to the current state), then a spatial projection (identity for joint-space, or inverse kinematics Φ_IK for task-space EEF poses). The evaluation uses a 6×6 grid-based spatial coverage protocol with 3 independent trials of 10 rollouts each, reporting progress scores rather than binary success.

## Method

The paper conducts the first large-scale systematic empirical study of action space design, decomposing it into orthogonal temporal and spatial axes and evaluating 500+ trained models across 13,000+ real-world rollouts on AgileX PiPER (single-arm and bimanual), AIRBOT, and RoboTwin 2.0 simulation. The backbone is a FiLM-conditioned ResNet-18 vision encoder paired with a 6-layer Transformer decoder, trained under two paradigms: MSE regression (ACT-style) and flow matching (Diffusion Policy-style), with a π0 foundation model for transfer experiments. A key theoretical contribution (Proposition 4.1) proves that step-wise delta integration amplifies prediction noise as O(k) via the spectral norm of the lower-triangular cumulative-sum matrix, while chunk-wise delta and absolute actions maintain O(1) error bounds, explaining the empirical superiority of chunk-wise alignment. Grid searches over execution horizon, data volume (100–500 demos), and training epochs (300–1200) reveal that delta actions consistently outperform absolute actions, joint-space benefits disproportionately from scaling, and task-space becomes superior specifically in cross-embodiment and transfer learning settings.

## Results

Across 14 tasks on three platforms (single-arm AgileX, bimanual AgileX, RoboTwin 2.0), the overall average progress score for the best configuration (Joint-space + chunk-wise delta + Flow Matching/DP) reaches 88.0%, compared to 63.4% for the weakest (EE-space + absolute + ACT). Delta actions consistently outperform absolute actions by 10–15 points across all platforms and model variants (e.g., 88.0 vs. 79.6 for DP; 79.7 vs. 71.2 for ACT). Joint-space control outperforms task-space (EE) by 5–8 points in standard single-embodiment settings, but this advantage reverses under cross-embodiment and transfer learning, where task-space representations show a pronounced advantage due to their embodiment-invariance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is an empirical study comparing action space design choices rather than novel methods against prior methods, so the 'baselines' are the four action-space configurations (EE-abs, EE-delta, Joint-abs, Joint-delta) evaluated under two policy backbones (ACT regression and DP flow matching). Delta actions dominate absolute actions across every platform, task, and model variant, with the gap ranging from ~5 points (bimanual) to ~15 points (single-arm AgileX, e.g., 95.9 vs. 85.0 for DP). Joint-space control outperforms task-space (EE) in standard settings by 5–8 points on average, and this gap widens with more data and training epochs; however, under cross-embodiment (AgileX + AIRBOT) and transfer learning from π₀, task-space reverses the advantage, confirming its embodiment-invariance benefit. Between the two backbones, DP (flow matching) consistently outperforms ACT (regression) by 5–10 points, with the largest gains appearing in joint-space where the multi-modal configuration manifold benefits from generative modeling. For the temporal implementation detail, chunk-wise delta outperforms step-wise delta by over 10% on average, a result the authors corroborate with a theoretical noise-amplification bound showing step-wise integration scales error as O(k) while chunk-wise maintains O(1).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as the first large-scale, systematic empirical study of action space design for imitation-based robotic manipulation, building directly on the ACT and Diffusion Policy architectures (Zhao et al., 2023; Chi et al., 2023) and the π0 foundation model (Physical Intelligence et al., 2025) as testbeds. While prior works (Chi et al., 2023; Eber et al., 2024) offered isolated observations about action representations, this paper departs by decomposing the design space into orthogonal temporal (absolute vs. delta) and spatial (joint vs. task) axes, identifying previously overlooked implementation nuances (chunk-wise vs. step-wise delta alignment), and providing a theoretical noise-amplification analysis (Proposition 4.1) that explains why step-wise integration is structurally unstable. The study thus transforms what was previously a collection of ad-hoc, undocumented choices into a structured taxonomy with empirical grounding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical guidelines derived here—delta actions with chunk-wise alignment for standard settings, joint-space for single-embodiment performance, and task-space for cross-embodiment/transfer—provide actionable design principles that can standardize future robotic policy research and improve reproducibility. However, the study is limited to 6-DoF arms with position-based control and a relatively narrow task suite (pick-and-place, bimanual transfer), leaving open whether findings generalize to high-DoF humanoids, dexterous hands, or dynamic tasks like table tennis. The authors also identify the lack of principled (non-heuristic) horizon selection for action chunking and the potential for hybrid/adaptive action spaces as key future directions that could further unlock performance.
