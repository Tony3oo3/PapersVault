## Problem

Existing matching bandit algorithms assume a static set of participants who start learning simultaneously under a shared global clock, making them inapplicable to real-world markets where players enter and leave at arbitrary, uncoordinated times. This paper asks whether stable matching with sublinear regret is achievable in a fully decentralized, dynamic environment where no global signal exists, no agent observes others' actions or rewards, and the optimal matching target itself shifts when players depart.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261240/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each player receives only a local clock (starting at 1 upon entry), the publicly known arm set K, and a scalar stochastic utility X_i(t) ∈ [0,1] when matched (zero when rejected); the number of active players N, the global time, and all other agents' actions and rewards are unobservable. The algorithm outputs one arm proposal per time step per player, and the collective system converges to the player-optimal stable matching (POSM) with sublinear cumulative regret. Notably, the algorithm uses Δ_min and δ_g only as theoretical parameters for analysis; a decoupled variant (Appendix F) removes this dependency in favor of per-player-arm thresholds.

## Method

For one-sided learning (Way-SE), the key mechanism is Index-Free Cyclic Exploration: each player maintains a pointer initialized uniformly at random over {0,…,K−1} and advances it deterministically modulo K, so unsynchronized players naturally de-conflict their exploration sweeps. When rejected, a player invokes a WAITINGARM subroutine that persists for K consecutive rounds; if still rejected, the arm is eliminated as persistently occupied by a superior incumbent, while a success within the window indicates only a transient explorer. For the fully decentralized two-sided setting (Way-SE-2S), the algorithm adds a Restless Ping mechanism: while in exploitation, each player re-activates a randomly chosen arm from its Dream Set (previously omitted arms that may now be better) with probability p(t) = 1/(t^{1/K}(log t)^{(K−2)/K}), enabling recovery from departure-induced structural shifts without any external signal. Arms select proposers via UCB on their own learned preferences, and the three-phase loop (State Update → Target Selection → Execution/Conflict Handling) with a timer-based explore/exploit transition yields the first sublinear regret bound O(KT^{1−1/K}(log T)^{2/K}/Δ_min²) in this setting.

## Results

The paper establishes two main theoretical regret guarantees: Way-SE achieves O(K² log T / Δ_min²) regret (logarithmic) for one-sided learning under uncoordinated player arrivals, while Way-SE-2S achieves O(KT^(1-1/K) (log T)^(2/K) / Δ_min²) regret (sublinear) for fully decentralized two-sided learning with arbitrary arrivals and departures. Empirically, with K=15 arms, Way-SE's cumulative regret closely tracks the O(log T) benchmark despite five player arrival events, and Way-SE-2S maintains sublinear regret growth across three dynamic scenarios involving both arrivals and departures. Scalability tests at K=100 (N_init=20) and K=200 (N_init=30) with Poisson-driven volatile events confirm sublinear regret under Δ_min ∈ {0.0005, 0.001, 0.002}.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares Way-SE-2S against two static baseline algorithms: RR-ETC (Zhang & Fang, 2024) and Epoch-based CA-ETC (Pagare & Ghosh, 2023), both of which assume synchronized, fixed-population markets. In all three dynamic scenarios (large time gap between events, fast clustered events, and volatile alternating entries/exits), Way-SE-2S achieves sublinear cumulative regret while both baselines exhibit diverging (effectively linear) regret after population changes. The baselines initially converge faster before any population shift, but uncoordinated arrivals break their phase-based synchronization and departures shift the POSM, permanently trapping them in suboptimal matchings. Way-SE-2S adapts to both arrivals and departures by converging to the newly established POSM after each event, a capability neither baseline possesses. The paper does not report a single numerical comparison table; the comparison is presented via cumulative regret curves (Figure 2) showing the qualitative separation between sublinear and linear growth.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the matching bandit literature (Liu et al. 2020/2021; Kong et al. 2022/2024; Zhang & Fang 2024; Pagare & Ghosh 2023), which has focused on static environments with synchronized participants and a global clock, to a fundamentally harder setting where players arrive and depart arbitrarily without any shared timing signal. Prior dynamic matching work (Muthirayan et al. 2023; Ghosh et al. 2022) addresses reward non-stationarity but assumes a fixed agent set; this paper instead models population dynamics (entry/exit) as the source of non-stationarity. The two key algorithmic mechanisms—Index-Free Cyclic Exploration for collision-free asynchronous exploration and Restless Ping for departure-induced re-exploration—are novel contributions that repurpose UCB-based confidence bounds in a decentralized, clock-free context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical guarantee for stable matching in a fully decentralized, uncoordinated bandit market with arbitrary population dynamics, filling a gap explicitly identified as a critical open challenge in the literature. The sublinear regret bound O(KT^{1-1/K}(log T)^{2/K}/Δ_min²) is the first in this setting, and the authors conjecture the T^{1-1/K} dependence is nearly tight, making a matching lower bound an important open problem. Key limitations include the assumption of a global stable time T_stable (after which no further arrivals/departures occur), the requirement that |N_t| ≤ K at all times, and the absence of incentive compatibility. Future directions include extending to decelerating but perpetual arrivals/departures, combining population dynamics with reward drift, and validating on real-world dynamic market data.
