## Problem

Existing online batch selection methods for LLM supervised fine-tuning rely solely on data utility (e.g., high loss or gradient magnitude) while neglecting intra-sample and inter-sample diversity, depend on external resources such as reference models or validation sets, and often incur greater training time than full-dataset fine-tuning. This paper asks whether a single selection criterion can jointly capture utility and both forms of diversity while remaining self-contained and faster than training on all data.

## Input / Output

At each training iteration, the method consumes a candidate batch of B text sequences and the current model parameters θ_t, performing a single forward pass to obtain per-sample logits matrices L ∈ R^{N×V}. It outputs a selected subset of K samples (K ≤ B) for backpropagation, along with an updated FIFO memory buffer of M low-dimensional embeddings (dimension d = d₁·d₂, e.g., 128×8 = 1024) that tracks recently trained samples. No reference model, validation set, or per-sample gradient computation is required; all scoring is derived from forward-pass logits.

## Method

UDS scores each candidate sample with two complementary terms. The intra-sample score is the nuclear norm ‖L(x;θ)‖_* of the logits matrix, which the paper shows (via Lemma 3.1 and empirical correlation) simultaneously reflects optimization utility (larger norm → greater expected loss reduction) and intra-sample diversity (larger norm → higher rank → more varied token-level predictions). The inter-sample score is the average Euclidean distance between a bilinear SRFT-style random projection of L (factored as Γ₂·L·Γ₁ᵀ with Γ₁ ∈ R^{d₁×V}, Γ₂ ∈ R^{d₂×N}) and all embeddings stored in a fixed-size FIFO memory buffer, quantifying how distinct the sample is from recent training history. The two scores are combined as s_total = s_intra + α·s_inter, and the top-K samples are selected for the parameter update. The bilinear projection preserves pairwise distances via the Johnson-Lindenstrauss lemma (Theorem 3.2) while reducing storage from O(NV) to O(d₁d₂) per sample and computation from O(NVd) to O((N+V)d·log(NV)), making the entire selection mechanism add only ~0.5–2.6% FLOPs over the forward pass.

## Results

On Qwen-2.5-7B, UDS achieves the best accuracy across all four benchmarks: 63.34% on MMLU, 95.19% on ScienceQA, 79.91% on GSM8K, and 46.28% Pass@1 on HumanEval, outperforming the strongest baseline GREATS by +5.15%, +1.02%, +1.30%, and +1.24% respectively. UDS also trains faster than full-dataset SFT, reaching 3.41 samples/s on MMLU versus 2.27 for regular training, while MaxGrad is ~7× slower (0.31 samples/s). On Llama-3.1-8B, UDS similarly leads all baselines on every benchmark (e.g., 40.16% MMLU vs. 39.04% GREATS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UDS outperforms all five baselines on every benchmark and both backbones. Against the closest competitor GREATS (NeurIPS 2024), UDS gains +5.15% on MMLU, +1.02% on ScienceQA, +1.30% on GSM8K, and +1.24% on HumanEval with Qwen-2.5-7B, while also being faster (3.41 vs. 2.12 samples/s on MMLU). RHO-Loss (ICML 2022) requires a reference model and is consistently 4–6% behind UDS on MMLU; MaxLoss (2015) and MaxGrad (ICML 2018) are simple heuristics that underperform UDS by 8–9% on MMLU, with MaxGrad additionally incurring ~7× slower training throughput. Against the offline method FisherSFT (2025), UDS achieves +5.49% on MMLU and +2.41% on HumanEval while using less algorithm-specific time (584s vs. 635s per epoch on MMLU) and lower peak GPU memory (19.2 GB vs. 22.9 GB). UDS is the only method that simultaneously satisfies all three desiderata: joint utility–diversity scoring, no external resources, and reduced training time relative to full-dataset SFT.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the online batch selection paradigm for LLM SFT (Loshchilov & Hutter 2015; Katharopoulos & Fleuret 2018; Mindermann et al. 2022; Wang et al. 2024) by introducing a scoring mechanism that jointly captures data utility and diversity without external resources. The nuclear norm of the logits matrix draws on matrix analysis (Horn & Johnson) and the batch nuclear-norm maximization idea from Cui et al. (2020) in computer vision, repurposing it as a dual-purpose signal for both optimization utility and intra-sample token diversity. The inter-sample diversity component repurposes SRFT-style random projections (Ailon & Chazelle 2006; Jin et al. 2021) in a bilinear factorized form to avoid storing full logits matrices, a construction not previously applied to online data selection. The key conceptual departure from prior work is the explicit formulation of three desiderata (D1–D3) that no existing method satisfies simultaneously, positioning UDS as the first to address utility, intra-sample diversity, inter-sample diversity, resource independence, and training-time reduction in a single framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UDS offers a practical, plug-and-play solution for practitioners who want to reduce SFT compute without relying on reference models or held-out validation sets—resources that are often unavailable or impractical in real deployment. The finding that a 12.5% selected subset on MMLU (Qwen-2.5-7B) outperforms full fine-tuning by ~8 points suggests that full-dataset training may waste budget on redundant or stage-misaligned samples, a hypothesis supported by the category-distribution analysis (Table 13). Open questions include how the method scales to much larger models (experiments top out at 14B with LoRA), whether the dataset-specific trade-off factor α can be learned automatically, and whether the O(N²) SVD cost becomes prohibitive at very long context lengths beyond 32K. The approach also raises the question of whether similar nuclear-norm-based scoring could transfer to other training paradigms such as RLHF or DPO.
