## Problem

Existing multimodal visual verifiers rely on binary True/False judgments that are coarse and uninformative, lacking the fine-grained, interpretable rationales needed for precise error localization and actionable correction. While meta-verification (using verifier-generated explanations as additional training signal) has been explored in text-only math reasoning, it remains unclear how to reliably incorporate such feedback into multimodal verifier training—specifically, what form the rationales should take and how the RL objectives for judgment and rationale generation should be coupled.

## Input / Output

The verifier takes an RGB image and a text prompt as input and produces a structured output: a reasoning trace in think tags, a binary True/False judgment, and (when False) symbolic bounding boxes localizing the erroneous regions plus atomic semantic edit commands (Add, Delete, Modify). The bounding boxes serve dual purposes—they are the meta-verification rationale scored by IoU (threshold 0.6) during training, and they become spatial action signals in the M1-TTS agentic loop, where a unified multimodal model uses them together with the edit instructions to perform region-level image corrections iteratively (up to 10 rounds).

## Method

The paper proposes two key design choices for training a multimodal verifier via RLVR (Reinforcement Learning with Verifier Rewards). First, symbolic bounding boxes replace textual explanations as meta-verification rationales, enabling a rule-based IoU reward that avoids the cost and reward-hacking vulnerability of model-based judge models. Second, binary judgment and meta-verification are decoupled into two independent reward streams: all samples supervise the accuracy reward, while negative samples are duplicated and the copies supervise only the meta-verification (IoU) reward, with both streams mixed during DAPO-based RL training. Theoretically, the paper proves that in joint training the meta-verification gradient is multiplicatively gated by the accuracy indicator, reducing its signal-to-noise ratio by a factor of p_acc(θ) and making early-stage optimization of rationales nearly impossible; decoupled training removes this gating entirely. Built on Qwen3-VL-8B, the resulting OmniVerifier-M1 achieves 0.68 on ViVerBench and 0.866 on RefCOCO, and powers M1-TTS, an agentic generation system that iteratively localizes errors and issues structured edit commands to a unified multimodal model for region-level self-correction.

## Results

OmniVerifier-M1 (Qwen3-VL-8B with decoupled RL) achieves 0.680 overall on ViVerBench, surpassing the base model (0.654) and joint-training variant (0.671), with notable gains on Bounding Box (0.784 vs 0.741) and Counting (0.565 vs 0.489). On RefCOCO, decoupled training reaches 0.866 overall vs 0.831 (base) and 0.847 (joint). For the end-to-end M1-TTS system, OmniVerifier-M1+GPT-Image-1.5 achieves 0.88 on WISE and 0.800 on T2I-CoreBench, outperforming GPT-Image-1.5 alone (0.83 / 0.782) and Qwen3-VL+GPT-Image-1.5 (0.86 / 0.787).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares M1-TTS against standalone generative models (Qwen-Image, GPT-Image-1.5) and against a weaker verifier (Qwen3-VL-8B) paired with the same generators. OmniVerifier-M1+GPT-Image-1.5 outperforms GPT-Image-1.5 alone by +5 points on WISE (0.88 vs 0.83) and +1.8 on T2I-CoreBench (0.800 vs 0.782), demonstrating that the verifier injects meaningful world knowledge and spatial correction. Compared to Qwen3-VL-8B+GPT-Image-1.5, the proposed system gains +2 on WISE and +1.3 on T2I-CoreBench, confirming that the decoupled symbolic meta-verification training yields a stronger verifier. With the weaker RePlan generator, OmniVerifier-M1+RePlan still surpasses Qwen3-VL+RePlan by +3 on WISE and +6.1 on T2I-CoreBench, showing the verifier's benefit is not limited to top-tier generators. Qwen-Image, the weakest baseline, trails all M1-TTS configurations by 16–21 points on WISE and 21–29 on T2I-CoreBench.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RLVR-based visual verification paradigm introduced by OmniVerifier (Zhang et al., 2025), which trained verifiers with binary True/False judgments, by incorporating meta-verification feedback originally developed for mathematical reasoning in DeepSeekMath-V2 (Shao et al., 2025). The key conceptual departure is replacing free-form textual rationales with symbolic spatial outputs (bounding boxes, points) as the meta-verification signal, enabling rule-based IoU rewards that eliminate the need for auxiliary judge models. The decoupled RL training strategy—treating binary judgment and meta-verification as separate objectives with independent reward streams—represents a novel optimization design motivated by a gradient-variance analysis showing that joint training multiplicatively gates meta-verification gradients by the accuracy reward.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, theoretically grounded recipe for training multimodal verifiers that produce fine-grained, actionable error localization rather than coarse binary judgments, with a 1000× speedup in per-sample reward computation (0.021 ms vs. 20.2 ms) by replacing model-based textual evaluation with rule-based IoU. The M1-TTS agentic system demonstrates that verifier-driven region-level correction substantially outperforms global multi-turn editing on world-knowledge and compositional generation benchmarks. Open questions include whether the decoupled training advantage persists at larger model scales where early-stage accuracy is already high, and whether current unified multimodal models possess sufficient grounding-specific editing capability to fully exploit the verifier's precise spatial signals.
