## Problem

Generation-time text watermarking requires control over the LLM at inference time, making it inapplicable to existing documents. While post-hoc watermarking via LLM rephrasing has been explored for data-protection use cases such as watermark radioactivity, no prior work provides a comprehensive evaluation of how compute allocation—model size, watermarking scheme, decoding strategy, and entropy filtering—affects the quality-detectability tradeoff, especially across the contrast between open-ended prose and verifiable formats like code.

## Input / Output

The method takes existing text (literary passages, multilingual Wikipedia paragraphs, or Python functions from HumanEval/MBPP) and a secret key, splitting the input into sentence- or paragraph-level chunks that are rephrased sequentially with optional context from previously rephrased chunks. The output is a watermarked rephrased text whose length must remain within a factor of [0.75, 1.25] of the original to be considered valid; for code, the watermarked output must additionally pass the original unit tests (pass@1). Detection recomputes the pseudorandom function over the watermarked text and yields a p-value controlling the false positive rate, with an optional entropy-based token filter applied at detection time.

## Method

The pipeline prompts an instruct LLM to rephrase each chunk while applying a watermarking scheme during token-by-token decoding, using a pseudorandom function seeded by a secret key and a window of k preceding tokens to bias next-token selection. The paper systematically evaluates six watermarking schemes (Gumbel-max, Green-Red, SynthID-Text, MorphMark, DiPMark, WaterMax) across four model families (Llama-3, Gemma-3, Qwen2.5, SmolLM-2) at multiple sizes, combined with three decoding strategies: nucleus sampling, beam search (with unbiased or biased scoring), and multi-candidate WaterMax selection. A key finding is that the simplest Gumbel-max scheme dominates the Pareto frontier under random sampling, while beam search substantially improves the quality-detectability tradeoff for other methods; counterintuitively, smaller models with higher output entropy achieve stronger watermarks than larger, more fluent models. For code, the strict functional-correctness constraint severely limits paraphrasing freedom, and no method simultaneously achieves high pass@1 and high detection power.

## Results

On open-ended text (Dickens, Wikipedia, ELI5), Gumbel-max consistently dominates the quality-detectability Pareto frontier: at T=1.0 on ELI5 it achieves −log₁₀p = 5.71 with SBERT 0.814, versus 2.19 (Green-Red), 3.19 (SynthID), 4.18 (MorphMark), and 1.46 (DiPMark). On code (HumanEval + MBPP), no method achieves both high pass@1 and high TPR; the best trade-off is Llama-3.1-8B with Gumbel-max at T=1.4, reaching pass@1 = 0.71 and TPR@FPR=10⁻³ = 0.29 among correct codes. Gumbel-max adds negligible compute overhead (1.01× vs. unwatermarked sampling), while WaterMax costs 4.53×. Entropy-aware detection at test time yields at most 20% improvement in detection power for only 30–40% of configurations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Gumbel-max (Aaronson & Kirchner, 2023) is the clear winner across all open-ended text domains, achieving the highest −log₁₀p at comparable or better SBERT than every other scheme; on ELI5 at T=1.0 it reaches 5.71 versus 4.18 for MorphMark, 3.19 for SynthID, 2.19 for Green-Red, and 1.46 for DiPMark. Green-Red (Kirchenbauer et al., 2023a) is the strongest logit-bias alternative but is consistently 1–3 orders of magnitude weaker in detection than Gumbel-max under nucleus sampling; however, beam search with biased scoring narrows the gap (MorphMark+beam reaches −log₁₀p 7.2 at SBERT 0.872 vs. Gumbel-max's 7.7 at SBERT 0.862). SynthID-Text (Dathathri et al., 2024) performs competitively at moderate temperatures but degrades sharply at higher temperatures on code, limiting its TPR. MorphMark (Wang et al., 2025) is the second-best method overall on open-ended text and benefits most from beam search. DiPMark (Wu et al., 2023) is the weakest scheme in nearly all settings. WaterMax (Giboulot & Furon, 2024), despite its theoretical distortion-free guarantee, achieves surprisingly weak detectability in the post-hoc rephrasing setting and is incompatible with radioactivity-based detection. SWEET (Lee et al., 2023) shows steeper pass@1 degradation than Gumbel-max on code as watermark strength increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies generation-time LLM watermarking schemes (Gumbel-max from Aaronson & Kirchner 2023, Green-Red from Kirchenbauer et al. 2023, SynthID-Text, MorphMark, DiPMark, WaterMax) in a post-hoc setting where an LLM rephrases existing text while embedding the watermark during decoding. Prior post-hoc watermarking work (Waterfall, Sander et al., Jovanović et al.) demonstrated feasibility for data-protection use cases like RAG detection and benchmark contamination, but did not systematically characterize the quality-detectability tradeoff across domains, model scales, and decoding strategies. This paper positions itself as the first comprehensive evaluation of post-hoc watermarking as a general-purpose method, isolating the effects of model size, watermark scheme, beam search, multi-candidate selection, and entropy-aware detection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides actionable guidance for practitioners: Gumbel-max with a mid-size model and nucleus sampling is the robust Pareto-optimal choice for open-ended text, while verifiable text like code remains fundamentally harder to watermark without sacrificing correctness. The finding that smaller, more entropic models outperform larger ones for watermark strength challenges the default assumption that scaling up always helps, and the poor performance of WaterMax in the post-hoc setting suggests that generation-time SOTA methods do not transfer cleanly. Open questions include robustness to translation and aggressive paraphrasing attacks (which largely remove the signal), the multilingual quality gap, and whether semantic-level watermarks could complement token-level schemes for verifiable content.
