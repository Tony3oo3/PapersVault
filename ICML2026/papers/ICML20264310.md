## Problem

Cross-modal hashing methods assume perfectly labeled training data, but noisy labels from manual or automated annotation are unavoidable in practice. Existing robust CMH approaches (label refinement, robust losses, early learning regularization) and uncertainty-aware methods (probabilistic embeddings, evidential hashing) still perform similarity calculations and gradient updates using high-uncertainty samples, failing to effectively suppress misleading training signals. The key gap is the absence of a mechanism that adaptively weakens semantic discrepancies based on per-sample uncertainty during optimization.

## Input / Output

The method takes paired RGB images and English text captions, encoded by CLIP-style visual and text transformers (12 blocks each with MHSA and MLP). Each sample produces a dual representation: a semantic feature h* = tanh(H*(f*)) and an uncertainty feature u* = U*(f*) from a separate FC layer. At inference, standard cosine similarity on semantic features is used for retrieval ranking, with uncertainty features optionally available for reliability assessment, introducing negligible computational overhead over the base method.

## Method

RSH introduces a Self-reflective Similarity Metric (SSM) that replaces the standard cosine similarity in any CMH objective. It computes semantic discrepancy C(I,T) = 1 − cos(h^I, h^T) and similarity uncertainty β(I,T) = ‖u^I + u^T‖₂ (summing uncertainty vectors before taking the norm to capture inter-modal uncertainty correlation), then defines C_Sr = 1 − C · exp(−β̃/τ) where β̃ = (β+γ)/α is a relative uncertainty normalized by semantic distance. Gradient analysis shows the parameter gradient is scaled by exp(−β̃/τ), so high-uncertainty samples contribute proportionally smaller updates, effectively prioritizing reliable samples. The framework is plug-and-play: it simply substitutes C with C_Sr in any existing triplet, contrastive, or other CMH loss, and employs semi-hard negative sampling based on C_Sr to avoid interference from noisy pairs.

## Results

The primary metric is MAP@ALL (Mean Average Precision over all retrieved results), evaluated on MIRFlickr-25K, MS-COCO, and IAPR TC-12 at symmetric noise rates of 0%, 20%, 50%, and 80%. At 128 bits and 0% noise, RSH achieves 0.8581 (MIRFlickr), 0.7796 (MS-COCO), and 0.7085 (IAPR TC-12). Under the most challenging setting (80% noise, 128 bits), RSH reaches 0.8140, 0.7559, and 0.7063 respectively, outperforming the best baselines by 3.27%, 6.38%, and 5.09% as reported by the authors. RSH also demonstrates the lowest Mean Corruption Error (MCE) under image degradation (Gaussian noise, defocus blur, JPEG compression) and text incompleteness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RSH achieves the best MAP@ALL across all 12 dataset–noise-rate combinations at 128 bits, outperforming all nine baselines. The closest competitors at low noise (0–20%) are DSTH and DUaPH, which are within 0.5–1.5% of RSH on MIRFlickr-25K; however, the gap widens substantially at 50–80% noise, where RSH leads DSTH by up to 3.7% (MS-COCO, 80%) and DUaPH by up to 6.4% (MS-COCO, 80%). Among the noise-robust baselines (NRCH, RSHNL, NRGH), RSH consistently surpasses them by 2–10% MAP, with the largest margins at 80% noise (e.g., RSH 0.7559 vs. RSHNL 0.7465 on MS-COCO). The uncertainty-modeling baselines (DECH, DPBE, DUaPH) show notable degradation at high noise rates—DPBE drops from 0.7565 to 0.5806 on MS-COCO—while RSH maintains a much flatter performance curve, confirming its advantage in suppressing misleading training signals from noisy labels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate threads in cross-modal hashing: noisy-label robustness (NRCH, RSHNL, NRGH) and uncertainty-aware retrieval (DUaPH, DPBE, DECH). Prior noisy-label CMH methods rely solely on semantic features for similarity, while prior uncertainty-aware methods still feed high-uncertainty samples into gradient updates. RSH departs from both by embedding uncertainty directly into the similarity metric itself, so that semantic discrepancies are adaptively softened rather than merely re-weighted or filtered. The plug-and-play design (replacing the cosine similarity in any CMH objective) positions it as a drop-in module rather than a full pipeline replacement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature makes RSH immediately practical for existing CMH pipelines, and the gradient analysis (Eq. 14–15) provides a clean theoretical justification for why uncertain samples receive smaller parameter updates. However, the uncertainty feature is produced by a simple FC layer, raising open questions about calibration quality under extreme noise or distribution shift, and whether the approach extends naturally to more than two modalities or to asymmetric noise patterns. The method's reliance on a single hyperparameter pair (γ, τ) also limits its adaptability across domains without manual tuning.
