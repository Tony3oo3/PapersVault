## Problem

Cyclic peptide conformation prediction is challenging because ring-closed peptides explore diverse, ring-shaped conformational ensembles that deterministic folding models (e.g., AF2-based HighFold2, AfCycDesign) cannot capture, suffering from severe mode collapse and high-energy steric clashes. Physical simulation methods offer precision but are computationally prohibitive for large-scale exploration. The open problem is how to efficiently generate diverse, physically valid cyclic conformations with guaranteed ring-closure from a given linear peptide.

## Input / Output

The method takes a linear peptide amino acid sequence S of length L (typically 8–16 residues) as input, with sequence embeddings pre-computed via a frozen ESM-2 model. It outputs K×M energy-minimized all-atom 3D conformations of the corresponding cyclic peptide, parameterized by backbone rigid frames (SE(3) transformations plus ψ torsion angles) and side-chain torsion angles (up to 4 χ angles per residue), recovered via forward kinematics. A rule-based topology detection step automatically infers the cyclization mode (head-to-tail, disulfide, or isopeptide) from atomic proximity before force-field refinement.

## Method

MuCO decomposes the conditional distribution p(X|S) into p(B|S)·p(C|B,S) and implements a three-stage coarse-to-fine pipeline: (1) SE(3) flow matching with an IPA Transformer generates diverse cyclic backbone scaffolds, where training directly on the CPSea cyclic peptide dataset makes ring-closure an implicit property of the learned velocity field rather than a hard geometric constraint; (2) Torsional Flow Matching with an EquiformerV2 network performs generative side-chain packing, using a proposed Cyclic Relative Positional Encoding that treats boundary residues as immediate neighbors to suppress steric clashes at the cyclization junction; (3) CHARMM36 force-field minimization with Langevin thermostat refines each conformation into a physically valid local minimum while preserving ring-closure. The decoupled architecture enables a hierarchical K×M parallel sampling strategy (e.g., 10 backbones × 10 packings = 100 conformations in ~4 seconds), achieving ~41 ms amortized latency per conformation and enabling broad coverage of the rugged cyclic energy landscape.

## Results

On the aggregated CPSea test set (10,132 conformations), MuCO achieves the lowest mean potential energy (30.2 kJ/mol via CHARMM36) and highest structural diversity (Shannon entropy H = 0.69) among all methods, while maintaining a 94.0% cyclization success rate in single-sampling mode. AF2-based baselines (HighFold2, AfCycDesign) reach >98% success but collapse to near-zero diversity (H ≈ 0) and high energies (111–116 kJ/mol), whereas GDL baselines (EGNN, WGFormer) show moderate diversity (H ≈ 0.57–0.58) but substantially higher energies (105–108 kJ/mol). With hierarchical 10×10 parallel sampling, MuCO reduces mean energy to −3.9 kJ/mol on CP-Core and −247.6 kJ/mol on CPSea-PDB, surpassing ground-truth stability, at an amortized cost of ~41 ms per conformation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MuCO decisively outperforms both AF2-based baselines (HighFold2, AfCycDesign) on physical stability and diversity: its energy of 30.2 kJ/mol is roughly 3.7× lower than HighFold2 (111.3) and 3.8× lower than AfCycDesign (116.4), while its diversity score (H = 0.69) is orders of magnitude higher than their near-zero values (0.00–0.01), which reflect severe mode collapse into a single H2T cyclization mode. Against the GDL baselines (EGNN, WGFormer), MuCO wins on all three axes—success rate (94.0% vs. 85.6%/86.2%), energy (30.2 vs. 108.2/104.9 kJ/mol), and diversity (0.69 vs. 0.57/0.58)—confirming that the generative flow-matching backbone and side-chain stages are essential over deterministic regression. In terms of computational efficiency, MuCO's parallel 100-sample inference takes ~41 ms amortized per conformation, making it ~1.7× faster than EGNN (72 ms) and >100× faster than AF2-based methods (~4,200–4,500 ms), while using fewer trainable parameters (40.2M vs. 92.3M for AF2 variants). The only metric where MuCO trails is raw success rate in single-sampling mode (94.0% vs. >98% for AF2-based methods), but this gap closes to 100% with modest parallel sampling (K×M ≥ 5×5).

## Relation to Prior Work

MuCO composes three established generative and physics-based components—SE(3) flow matching (FoldFlow2), torsional flow matching (FlowPacker), and CHARMM36 force-field refinement (CP-Composer)—into a unified multi-stage pipeline specifically adapted for cyclic peptides. The key departure from prior work is twofold: (1) training the backbone flow directly on cyclic conformations from the newly released CPSea dataset, making ring-closure an implicit geometric property rather than a hard constraint, and (2) introducing a Cyclic Relative Positional Encoding in the side-chain packing stage to handle the topological wrap-around that linear RPEs cannot capture. This positions MuCO as a generative alternative to the deterministic AF2-adapted methods (HighFold2, AfCycDesign) that suffer from severe mode collapse, and as a more physically grounded successor to GDL predictors (EGNN, WGFormer) that regress single conformations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Cyclic peptides are a critical class of therapeutics for targeting undruggable protein-protein interactions, yet their conformational modeling has been limited by the mode collapse of deterministic predictors and the computational cost of molecular dynamics. MuCO's hierarchical K×M sampling strategy (100× faster than AF2-based baselines) makes large-scale conformational ensemble screening practically feasible, and the finding that generative seeding is essential for force-field relaxation to recover native secondary structures (random initialization fails) clarifies the complementary roles of learned priors and physics. Open questions include extension to multi-chain complexes, non-terminal cyclization modes, ultralong sequences where backbone efficiency degrades, and validation of generated conformations through wet-lab synthesis and binding assays.
