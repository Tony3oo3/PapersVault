## Problem

Single domain generalization in time series classification requires a model trained on one source domain to generalize to unseen target domains, but existing generative augmentation approaches produce synthesized samples with low diversity and intrinsic noise. Two specific gaps are identified: (1) the lack of explicit semantic guidance that disentangles invariant category information from domain-specific features during augmentation, and (2) diversity collapse caused by focusing only on boundary samples as augmentation anchors, which narrows the effective distribution coverage.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multivariate time series of shape D×L (e.g., 8×1×200 for EMG at 200 Hz, 45×1×125 for DSADS at 25 Hz) segmented via sliding windows from a single source domain, and produces a class label from C categories. During training, the conditional diffusion generator also outputs synthetic time series of the same D×L shape, which are filtered and appended to the source dataset before the final classifier is trained. The diffusion generator (a 1D U-Net with base width 64 and channel multipliers 1,2,4,8) is trained once and reused across progressive expansion iterations, so the per-epoch classifier update remains lightweight.

## Method

CURE decomposes each time-series instance into a semantic-aware feature (via supervised contrastive learning) and a semantic-free domain feature (via instance-level contrastive learning with a gradient reversal layer), enforced to be independent through a distance-correlation constraint. These disentangled representations serve as joint conditions for a conditional DDPM: the semantic-aware condition is constructed by retrieving k-nearest neighbors in the invariant feature space and averaging their embeddings, while the semantic-free condition is a learnable domain prompt drawn from a dynamically growing memory bank. An adversarial tuning loss simultaneously maximizes category-semantic consistency of generated samples and minimizes style similarity to all stored domain prompts, with gradients back-propagated only from a low-noise diffusion step to the clean sample. Progressive expansion iteratively adds new domain prompts to the memory bank and filters out boundary samples using a silhouette-inspired intra/inter-domain distance ratio with a linearly increasing threshold, yielding a self-paced curriculum that broadens distribution coverage while preserving semantic reliability.

## Results

CURE achieves an average cross-domain classification accuracy of 74.56% across four benchmark datasets (EMG, DSADS, PAMAP2, USC-HAD) under the single-domain generalization protocol, outperforming the strongest baseline SEED (71.85%) by 2.71 points. The largest gains appear on EMG (77.69% vs. 74.67% for SEED, +3.02) and DSADS (74.23% vs. 68.88%, +5.35), while CURE also leads on PAMAP2 (76.66% vs. 75.86%) and USC-HAD (69.66% vs. 67.99%). Across all 13 source-to-target domain splits in Table 4, CURE attains the best or second-best result in 12 out of 13 cases, with an overall average of 73.57% versus 71.22% for SEED.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CURE outperforms all 10 baselines on the overall average across four datasets (74.56% vs. 71.85% for the next-best SEED, +2.71). The margin is largest on DSADS, where CURE leads SEED by 5.35 points (74.23% vs. 68.88%) and DIVERSIFY by 11.36 points, indicating that the disentangled context-guided diffusion is particularly effective for high-variability IMU data. On EMG, CURE surpasses SEED by 3.02 points (77.69% vs. 74.67%) and DDLearn by 13.89 points. On PAMAP2, CURE edges out SEED by 0.80 points (76.66% vs. 75.86%) and AdaRNN by 6.82 points. On USC-HAD, CURE leads SEED by 1.67 points (69.66% vs. 67.99%) and DIVERSIFY by 12.05 points. The only individual target-domain split where CURE does not rank first is EMG T3, where SEED achieves 82.22% versus CURE's 80.78%, though CURE still outperforms all other baselines on that split.

## Relation to Prior Work

CURE builds on the DDPM framework for conditional generation, supervised contrastive learning for semantic-aware feature extraction, and gradient reversal layers for domain-specific feature disentanglement, combining these into a unified pipeline for single-domain generalization in time series. It departs from prior SDG methods (PDEN, L2D, Mixup) that rely on simple interpolation or adversarial augmentation by instead using disentangled category and domain contexts as explicit diffusion conditions. Compared to recent diffusion-based SDG approaches (DI2SDiff, SEED) that perform direct distribution expansion, CURE introduces a progressive expansion mechanism with a dynamic memory bank and boundary-aware filtering, positioning itself as a more structured and semantically grounded alternative to unguided generative augmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: most domain generalization methods for time series require multiple source domains, yet real-world deployments often have data from only one sensor or subject group. The progressive expansion strategy—iteratively updating domain prompts and filtering boundary samples—offers a principled way to avoid the diversity collapse that plagues single-shot augmentation. However, the evaluation is limited to four relatively small sensor-based datasets (8–36 subjects), and the method's complexity (two encoders, a diffusion generator, adversarial tuning, memory bank, and boundary filtering) raises questions about scalability to longer sequences, higher-dimensional inputs, or larger domain counts. Open questions include whether the disentangled-context conditioning generalizes to non-sensor time series (e.g., financial or clinical signals) and whether the progressive filtering schedule can be made adaptive rather than linearly scheduled.
