## Problem

Existing LLM agent optimization methods rely solely on task outcome rewards to select fine-tuning data, overlooking the incoordination between planning and execution. This incoordination manifests as impractical plans that cannot be faithfully executed and executions that deviate from prescribed plans, both of which propagate into training data and reinforce flawed behavioral patterns that task rewards cannot detect.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262832/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a text task instruction and iterative environment observations as input, producing a structured task plan (a sequence of sub-goal steps) followed by a multi-turn action sequence as output. The coordination assessment module additionally produces two continuous labels in [0,1]: a plan executability score (c_p) derived from cross-trajectory consistency, and an execution adherence score (c_τ) from step-aligned semantic matching. These labels are not used at inference time but serve as sample-level weights during the fine-tuning stage.

## Method

CoPE is a three-stage iterative optimization framework. First, Self-Refining MCTS collects diverse plan-execution data by treating each node as a plan and each edge as a replanning operation; the expansion phase feeds the lowest-reward trajectory back into the LLM for self-refinement to generate improved child plans, while the evaluation phase performs n independent rollouts per plan. Second, coordination is quantified along two axes: plan executability via the average pairwise normalized Levenshtein distance across multiple execution trajectories under the same plan, and execution adherence via a dynamic programming alignment (analogous to sequence alignment) that matches plan steps to executed actions using a hybrid of embedding cosine similarity and rule-based keyword matching, with penalties for skipped steps or actions. Third, the coordination scores are injected as sample weights into preference-optimized training: CW-DPO for the planning agent (weighting preference pairs by the executability gap) and CW-KTO for the execution agent (combining coordination-weighted SFT on desirable trajectories with KTO preference learning), with execution action pruning to remove shared prefix actions and focus learning on divergent decision points.

## Results

CoPE achieves state-of-the-art performance on both long-horizon benchmarks: with Qwen3-32B, it reaches 93.65 average reward on AlfWorld (Seen) and 80.84 on ScienceWorld (Seen), surpassing the strongest baseline (AGENTEVOL) by 7.13 and 6.20 points respectively. CoPE also achieves the shortest trajectory lengths (11.20 steps on AlfWorld, 17.21 on ScienceWorld), indicating superior execution efficiency. Notably, CoPE-optimized open-source models outperform closed-source competitors: Qwen3-32B+CoPE (80.84) exceeds Gemini 2.5-Pro (78.36) on ScienceWorld, and Qwen2.5-7B+CoPE (88.00) exceeds DeepSeek-Chat (76.64) on AlfWorld.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoPE consistently outperforms all 11 baselines across both environments and both backbone models. Against the strongest reward-only baselines (AGENTEVOL, ETO, RFT), CoPE gains 7.13–8.03 points on AlfWorld and 6.20–26.14 points on ScienceWorld (Qwen3-32B), with the largest gap on ScienceWorld where task rewards poorly capture intermediate-step quality. Among planning-based methods, MPO is the closest competitor (86.52 on AlfWorld Seen with Qwen3-32B), but CoPE still leads by 7.13 points and achieves shorter trajectories (11.20 vs. 11.96 steps), demonstrating that execution adherence matters beyond plan quality alone. PiplotRL, despite also using explicit planning, underperforms even reward-only methods (79.53 vs. 83.20 for ETO on AlfWorld), confirming that planning without coordination enforcement is ineffective. On closed-source comparisons, CoPE-optimized Qwen3-32B surpasses Gemini 2.5-Pro on ScienceWorld (80.84 vs. 78.36) and Qwen2.5-7B+CoPE surpasses DeepSeek-Chat on AlfWorld (88.00 vs. 76.64), showing that coordination-aware fine-tuning of smaller open models can match or exceed larger proprietary agents. Knowledge-enhanced baselines (WKM, KnowAgent) show moderate gains over ReAct but remain well below CoPE, indicating that injecting external knowledge does not substitute for explicit planning-execution alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoPE builds on MCTS for structured plan exploration, Self-Refine for iterative plan improvement from failed trajectories, and DPO/KTO for preference-based LLM fine-tuning, but departs from prior agent optimization work (MPO, ETO, AGENTEVOL, RFT) by introducing planning-execution coordination as an explicit, measurable optimization signal rather than relying solely on outcome rewards. Where MPO and PiplotRL focus on improving plan quality and WKM/KnowAgent inject external knowledge, CoPE addresses the orthogonal problem of ensuring that generated plans are actually executable and that execution trajectories faithfully follow the prescribed plan. The coordination-weighted preference losses (CW-DPO, CW-KTO) are direct extensions of DPO and KTO with sample-level weighting derived from the coordination assessment, positioning the work at the intersection of search-based data collection and preference optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete and previously under-modeled failure mode in LLM agents—planning-execution incoordination—and provides a practical, low-cost assessment (consistency-based executability and step-aligned semantic adherence) that can be plugged into existing fine-tuning pipelines. The finding that without explicit coordination modeling, iterative optimization degrades rather than improves (Figure 5) highlights a real risk in current self-training paradigms. Limitations include applicability primarily to long-horizon tasks (coordination gaps vanish for short-horizon tasks like HotPotQA and WebShop), reliance on text-based environments with discrete action spaces, and the computational cost of multi-rollout MCTS data collection (~2-3 days per iteration). Open questions include extending coordination assessment to continuous or multimodal action spaces and incorporating real-time adaptive replanning.
