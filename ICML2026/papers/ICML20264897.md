## Problem

Decomposing complex natural images into coherent background and foreground RGBA layers remains difficult due to occlusion completion, inter-layer feature interference, and imprecise alpha boundaries. Existing cascaded pipelines accumulate errors across segmentation, matting, and inpainting stages, while recent end-to-end diffusion methods lack explicit user control over layer semantics and ordering. Additionally, the absence of large-scale, high-quality multi-layer natural image datasets has limited progress in this area.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (resized to 1024 on the long side) and a set of user-specified bounding boxes as input, and produces a background RGBA layer plus N foreground RGBA layers as output. Foreground layers are spatially cropped to their respective bounding boxes before encoding, resulting in variable-length token sequences that are concatenated into a unified sequence for joint modeling. A fine-tuned TransVAE (from ART) handles RGBA encoding/decoding, with foreground transparent images converted to gray-background format for the diffusion process.

## Method

RevealLayer is built on FLUX.1-dev (MM-DiT backbone) fine-tuned with LoRA (rank 64) using Rectified Flow, modeling all layers as a single variable-length token sequence with 3D-RoPE positional embeddings. Three key modules address layer disentanglement and occlusion: (1) Region-Aware Attention imposes a structured mask that restricts each layer's tokens to attend only to their own tokens and spatially aligned global image tokens, suppressing cross-layer feature leakage; (2) an Occlusion-Guided Adapter concatenates conditional image features with per-layer latents and applies masked self-attention to enhance semantic coherence in overlapping regions; (3) a composite loss combines flow matching with a focal-style Hard-Constraint Alpha Loss for sharp boundaries and a cosine-based Orthogonality Loss to suppress low-frequency inter-layer residuals. Training uses the Prodigy optimizer (lr=1.0) for 50K iterations with a shared-noise strategy across foreground layers, and the model is trained on the newly introduced RevealLayer-100K dataset of 100K multi-layer natural image tuples.

## Results

On the primary multi-layer decomposition task (RevealLayerBench, 3-layer 1024×1024 images), RevealLayer achieves background PSNR of 25.53 dB, foreground PSNR of 32.13 dB, and foreground SoftIoU of 0.9432, substantially outperforming CLD (19.75 / 26.42 / 0.8304) and Qwen-Image-Layered (16.85 / N/A / N/A). On object removal (OBER-Test), RevealLayer attains 30.16 dB PSNR and 0.0694 LPIPS, surpassing the best mask-guided baseline OmniPaint (29.05 / 0.0521). On matting (AIM-500), RevealLayer achieves 0.0107 MSE and 0.9048 SoftIoU, outperforming MAM (0.0168 / 0.8775) and SAM3 (0.0510 / 0.8555).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two most directly comparable layer-decomposition methods, RevealLayer dominates: it improves background PSNR by 5.78 dB over CLD (25.53 vs 19.75) and 8.68 dB over Qwen-Image-Layered (25.53 vs 16.85), while reducing foreground FID from 43.64 to 18.42 vs CLD. Qwen-Image-Layered does not report foreground-level metrics, reflecting its inability to produce controllable per-layer outputs. On object removal, RevealLayer (box-guided) outperforms all six mask-guided baselines on PSNR and SSIM; the closest competitor OmniPaint achieves 29.05 dB PSNR and 20.70 FID on OBER-Test, while RevealLayer reaches 30.16 dB and 25.62 FID—RevealLayer wins on PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS but OmniPaint has a lower FID (20.70 vs 25.62), suggesting slightly better perceptual distribution matching. On matting, RevealLayer surpasses all four baselines (SAM-H, SAM2-L, SAM3, MAM) on every metric across both AIM-500 and RefMatte-RW100, with the largest gap over SAM3 (MSE 0.0107 vs 0.0510, SoftIoU 0.9048 vs 0.8555 on AIM-500). In the ablation, removing Region-Aware Attention drops background PSNR from 25.53 to 22.08, confirming it as the single most impactful module. On the stylized PrismLayersPro domain, RevealLayer (after 4k fine-tuning steps) achieves higher PSNR/SSIM than CLD but lower FID/IoU/F1, indicating a trade-off between structural fidelity and distributional realism on non-photorealistic content.

## Relation to Prior Work

RevealLayer builds on the FLUX.1-dev MM-DiT backbone and the TransVAE/3D-RoPE components from ART, repurposing a text-to-image diffusion architecture for multi-layer RGBA decomposition. It departs from prior end-to-end layer decomposition methods (LayerD, OmniPSD, Qwen-Image-Layered) that lack explicit user guidance, and from CLD which uses bounding-box conditioning but is restricted to stylized posters, by introducing bounding-box-guided decomposition for complex natural scenes. The key architectural novelty lies in the Region-Aware Attention mask that structurally isolates inter-layer token interactions and the Occlusion-Guided Adapter that injects localized context for overlapping regions, complemented by a composite alpha-plus-orthogonality loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in controllable image editing: decomposing natural photos into independently editable RGBA layers with user-specified object selection, a task where prior methods either lacked controllability or were limited to graphic design content. The RevealLayer-100K dataset and RevealLayerBench benchmark are significant community resources for a field previously starved of large-scale natural-scene multi-layer data. However, the method remains sensitive to severely inaccurate bounding boxes, heavy occlusion, and transparent/translucent regions, and the bounding-box interface, while simpler than masks, still requires the user to identify and place boxes for each target object. Future work on multi-round interactive editing and robustness to ambiguous layer separation would strengthen the practical applicability.
