## Problem

Reconstructing atomistic crystal structures from a single noisy STEM projection is an ill-posed inverse problem where multiple lattices can explain similar contrast, and purely feed-forward models cannot verify physical validity. Existing approaches remain fragmented across denoising, atom localization, reconstruction, and phase classification, with no integrated end-to-end system that converts raw STEM images into simulation-ready CIF structures and downstream properties, and no standardized benchmark for comprehensive evaluation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265423/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a single noisy iDPC-STEM projection (grayscale, 0.1 Å/pixel, 12×12 to 16×16 supercell) as input and produces a standard CIF file with validated lattice vectors and fractional atomic coordinates, a formation energy estimate in meV/atom, and per-site confidence scores with warning flags for low-confidence regions. The LLM controller does not process pixels directly; it reasons over structured tool outputs (quality metrics, confidence scores, relaxation diagnostics) to plan and adapt the workflow. The STEM2Mat benchmark provides 570 test instances across three difficulty tiers (unary high-dose, full-diversity moderate-dose, ternary low-dose) drawn from 459 unique 2D monolayer structures.

## Method

AutoMat formulates STEM-to-structure reconstruction as inference-time hypothesis search with closed-loop verification, where an LLM backbone (DeepSeek-V3 or GPT-4o) acts as a text-based reasoning controller that dynamically composes four modular tools: (1) MOE-DIVAESR, a pattern-adaptive mixture-of-experts denoiser with a ResNet-18 router selecting among six DIVAESR experts (EDSR backbone, 12 residual blocks) for joint denoising, inpainting, and 2× super-resolution; (2) physics-guided template retrieval using 32-bin radial distribution function descriptors matched by cosine distance against a library of simulated STEM projections, filtered by elemental composition; (3) STEM2CIF, which localizes atomic columns via weighted mean-shift clustering, fits the lattice under space-group symmetry constraints, assigns elements by contrast matching, and exports a primitive-cell CIF; and (4) MatterSim MLIP for structural relaxation and formation-energy prediction. The key architectural insight is that template retrieval is a state-dependent auxiliary branch rather than a mandatory step: the controller skips retrieval when denoising quality is sufficient and triggers it only when the direct STEM2CIF path fails or a stronger structural prior is needed. Upon verification failure (e.g., non-converged relaxation, residual stress, or composition mismatch), the controller executes rollback-and-retry, pruning invalid branches and re-planning tool invocations based on intermediate quality signals.

## Results

On the STEM2Mat Bench (459 unique 2D monolayer structures, 570 test instances across 3 difficulty tiers), AutoMat achieves a formation-energy MAE of 321.57 meV/atom (DeepSeek backbone) and a projected lattice RMSD of 0.11 ± 0.03 Å, with an overall structure success rate of 83.2% and composition correctness of 83.2%. The MLIP oracle upper bound is 48.1 meV/atom, indicating that AutoMat's residual error is dominated by reconstruction rather than potential-model bias. Performance degrades gracefully from Tier 1 (343.59 meV/atom) to Tier 3 (333.49 meV/atom), confirming robustness under low-dose, multi-element conditions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AutoMat outperforms all five baselines by a wide margin on both energy and structural metrics. Against general-purpose VLMs (GPT-4.1, Qwen-VL 32B, LLama4V 17B), AutoMat reduces formation-energy MAE by roughly 8–9× (321.57 vs. 2657–2932 meV/atom), as these models cannot produce simulation-ready CIFs and rely on hallucinated structural descriptions. Against the domain-specific ChemVLM (8B), the gap is even larger (~13×, 321.57 vs. 4237.6 meV/atom). The closest competitor, MicroscopyGPT (11B), achieves 620.22 meV/atom average energy MAE and 1.23–1.56 Å RMSD, but its structure success rate collapses to 0% on Tier 3 and only 25.9% on Tier 2, whereas AutoMat maintains 73.1% and 84.0% respectively. AtomAI, designed for single-element nanoparticle segmentation, is essentially non-functional on multi-element 2D crystals (RMSD ≈ 44 Å, S.S. = 0%), confirming that no existing tool bridges the full image-to-CIF-to-property pipeline. A same-tool heuristic script (hand-coded gating, retrieval, rollback) achieves 385.42 meV/atom and 74.2% S.S., showing that the LLM controller's adaptive orchestration contributes an additional ~64 meV/atom and ~9% S.S. improvement over rigid scripting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper composes previously fragmented STEM analysis sub-tasks—pattern-adaptive denoising (MOE-DIVAESR from Yang et al. 2025), atom localization, symmetry-constrained lattice fitting, and MLIP-based relaxation (MatterSim)—into a single agentic pipeline with closed-loop verification. It departs from prior domain tools (AtomAI, MicroscopyGPT) that stop at atomic coordinates or textual descriptions by producing simulation-ready CIF files and downstream energy predictions, and from general VLMs (GPT-4.1, Qwen-VL) that lack the physics grounding to generate valid crystallographic output. The state-dependent retrieval branch (template matching invoked only when the direct reconstruction path fails) and the rollback-and-retry controller represent a departure from fixed-pipeline approaches in both microscopy analysis and LLM-agent literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AutoMat reduces the 6–8 hour expert annotation workflow to roughly 2 minutes per sample and provides the first standardized end-to-end benchmark (STEM2Mat, 459 structures, 3 difficulty tiers) for evaluating microscopy-to-structure-to-property pipelines, filling a gap where prior datasets targeted STM or lacked DFT-level energy labels. Key open questions include extending beyond 2D monolayers to 3D crystals (where projection ambiguity is severe), improving element discrimination for similar-Z pairs under low dose, and determining whether the LLM controller's advantage over a well-engineered heuristic script (Table 3: ~60 meV/atom) grows as the tool pool and experimental conditions diversify. The paper positions AutoMat as an assistive rather than autonomous tool, acknowledging that incorrect reconstructions under out-of-distribution conditions could propagate into downstream simulations.
