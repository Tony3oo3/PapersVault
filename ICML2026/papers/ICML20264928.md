## Problem

LLMs are strong passive responders but struggle to proactively lead information-seeking conversations—asking the right questions and stopping at the right time. Existing approaches either optimize myopic single-turn attributes (providing no principled stopping mechanism) or train against user simulators that suffer from a persistent reality gap in open-ended, expert-level domains. The paper asks whether a complete, long-horizon questioning policy can be learned directly from offline expert conversation logs without any simulator.

## Input / Output

At inference, the policy receives the full conversation history C_{t-1} (alternating user and agent utterances) and produces a structured tuple (a_t, s_t): a single natural-language question targeting specific missing information, paired with a discrete CONTINUE or STOP decision. During training, each turn is paired with a hindsight-derived objective (I_t*, s_t*) extracted from the observed future of the expert trajectory, and the reward is a graded score (0.0/0.5/1.0) for question utility multiplied by a binary stopping-accuracy gate, plus a format bonus term Ω that penalizes multi-question 'shotgun' outputs.

## Method

Learn-to-Ask reframes the intractable offline RL problem into a sequence of supervised tasks via a hindsight decomposition: for each turn t, a powerful LLM oracle analyzes the observed future segment C_t^c to extract the target information set I_t* (what the expert actually gathered next) and the implicit stopping label s_t*, converting the long-horizon objective into per-turn (state, hindsight-objective) pairs. The reward uses a multiplicative fusion R_s·(1+β·R_a)+Ω that enforces a lexicographical hierarchy—question quality is only credited when the continue/stop decision is correct—preventing the agent from earning reward for good questions asked at the wrong time. Policy optimization is performed via offline reinforcement fine-tuning (primarily GRPO, also CISPO and GSPO) over sampled candidate responses scored by the grounded reward. An Auto-Prompt pipeline iteratively calibrates the extractor, grader, and sampler prompts against small human-verified anchor sets, ensuring reward fidelity and mitigating reward hacking with minimal human supervision. The entire framework is simulator-free, grounding all learning signals in real expert trajectories.

## Results

On the RealMedConv dataset, Learn-to-Ask (GRPO) achieves a good-question hit rate (WA-GH) of 0.41 on Qwen2.5-7B (up from 0.13 base, +215% relative) and 0.37 on Qwen2.5-32B (+185% relative), while dialogue termination accuracy (WS) jumps from 0.16 to 0.93 (7B) and 0.52 to 0.88 (32B). Total Reward (TR) improves from 2.17 to 3.27 (7B) and 2.43 to 3.15 (32B). In a large-scale production deployment serving millions of users, the deployed 32B model achieved 93% information completeness rate and 88% good-question rate, with a 1.87× lift in dialog-purchase conversion rate over a human-driven service.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Learn-to-Ask (GRPO) decisively outperforms all baselines on every metric for both model sizes. SFT improves stopping accuracy (WS: 0.74 on 7B) but sacrifices question quality (WA drops from 0.50 to 0.40, WA-GH from 0.13 to 0.08), confirming its myopic path-memorization limitation. DPO collapses severely on the 32B model (WA drops to 0.23, format correctness to 0.18, TR to 1.61), as its single binary preference signal is insufficient for the dual what-to-ask/when-to-stop objective. Among alternative RL optimizers applied to the same Learn-to-Ask framework, CISPO slightly outperforms GRPO on question quality (WA-GH 0.47 vs 0.41 on 7B) while GSPO trails both, suggesting the reward design is the dominant factor over the specific optimizer choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the Hindsight Experience Replay (HER) principle from Andrychowicz et al. (2017) to the high-dimensional space of natural-language dialogue, using the observed future of expert trajectories to define dense, turn-level supervisory signals. It deliberately departs from two dominant paradigms for instilling proactivity in LLMs: attribute-based single-turn alignment (e.g., DPO on synthetic preferences), which is myopic and lacks a stopping mechanism, and simulation-based RL, which suffers from a reality gap due to the difficulty of building high-fidelity user simulators. The resulting framework is a simulator-free, value-function-free offline RL formulation that reduces the sequential decision problem to a sequence of supervised tasks, positioned between offline RL from human data and goal-conditioned hindsight learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a practical, economically viable recipe for converting existing expert conversation logs into trainable proactive agents without expensive simulators, validated by a large-scale deployment (Medication AI Assistant, millions of users) where the trained model surpassed an internal human expert baseline under professional audit and achieved a 1.87× lift in dialog-purchase conversion. Open questions include formalizing the sub-optimality gap of the hindsight-based policy relative to the true offline optimum, integrating causal/counterfactual reasoning to go beyond imitation, and enabling exploration in semantic space beyond what the offline data covers. The cross-domain generalization (medical, legal, Text2SQL, deep research) suggests broad applicability, though the offline baselines (SFT, DPO) are relatively weak, and no comparison with other offline RL methods (CQL, IQL) is provided.
