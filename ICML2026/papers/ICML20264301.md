## Problem

Multi-physics PDE pre-training suffers from negative transfer due to conflicting dynamic behaviors across heterogeneous equations (e.g., smooth parabolic diffusion vs. sharp hyperbolic advection), and existing foundation models lack a theoretical framework for interpretability—specifically, understanding which pre-trained operator knowledge is effectively transferred to unseen target PDEs. Monolithic operator modeling in current large-scale PDE pretraining blends distinct physical sub-operators within shared representations, making it impossible to isolate, activate, or manipulate individual physical laws in a controlled manner.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a short temporal context window of PDE state snapshots (u_{t-k:t}) at a unified 128×128 spatial grid, with channel padding and geometry masking to handle multi-component systems and irregular domains. It produces an autoregressive future solution rollout over T timesteps, a sparse routing vector over M atomic nonlinear operators (via Entmax-1.5, yielding ~68% sparsity), and a latent physics code z = (π, λ, θ_L, θ_N) that parameterizes both the spectral linear multiplier and the KAN-based nonlinear operator library. The rollout is structure-aware: the linear stream operates entirely in Fourier space while the nonlinear stream applies local KAN-parameterized constitutive maps.

## Method

Origo introduces Neural Operator Splitting, which generalizes classical Strang splitting into a learnable, context-adaptive architecture by replacing hand-designed operators with latent-conditioned surrogates while preserving the symmetric composition Φ_{h/2}^L ∘ Φ_h^N ∘ Φ_{h/2}^L. A Mechanism Inference module encodes the context window via a sequence encoder into a physics embedding h, then decodes sparse routing weights π (Entmax-1.5), spectral multiplier parameters θ_L, nonlinear operator parameters θ_N, and a residual gate ρ through linear projection heads. The Operator Evolution module executes a fixed Strang skeleton where the linear stream is a diagonal Fourier multiplier exp(τ·Λ(θ_L)) and the nonlinear stream is a sparse weighted sum of KAN-parameterized atomic operators (B-spline components modeling constitutive laws). Training jointly optimizes a long-horizon rollout loss, an Entmax sparsity loss, and a residual regularization term, enabling zero-shot generalization by inferring a new physics code from context without retraining the operator bank. Theoretical guarantees (Theorems 3.2–3.3) establish universal approximation of split sub-dynamics and second-order global convergence under standard semigroup regularity assumptions.

## Results

On the multi-benchmark suite (FNO-ν, PDEBench, PDEArena, CFDBench), Origo-M (97M params) achieves the lowest zero-shot L2RE on 8 of 10 columns, e.g., 0.00247 on PDEBench DR and 0.00887 on PDEBench CNS, outperforming the next-best unified model (DPOT-M, 122M) by 28–65%. After 200-epoch fine-tuning, Origo-200 further reduces errors to 0.00437 (FNO-ν 1e-3) and 0.00153 (PDEBench SWE), setting new state-of-the-art on most tasks. On zero-shot unseen PDEs, Origo-M achieves 0.0103 Rel-L2 on 1D KdV and 0.0952 on 2D Allen-Cahn, reducing error by ~50–65% over the second-best baseline (MoE-POT-M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among task-specific experts, Origo-M (97M) outperforms all five baselines (FNO, U-Net, FFNO, GK-T, GNOT) by 2–10× on most benchmarks; for example, on PDEBench DR, Origo-M achieves 0.00247 vs. FFNO's 0.0669 and GNOT's 0.0365. Among unified pre-training models, Origo-M beats DPOT-M (122M) on 8/10 columns (e.g., 0.00247 vs. 0.0342 on DR, 0.00887 vs. 0.0136 on CNS) despite using 21% fewer parameters, and surpasses MoE-POT-M (288M) on 7/10 columns while being 3× smaller. OmniArch-M is the weakest unified baseline, trailing Origo-M by 4–50× across benchmarks (e.g., 0.609 vs. 0.0125 on CFDBench). In the zero-shot unseen-PDE setting (Table 2), Origo-M achieves 0.0103 on 1D KdV and 0.0952 on 2D Allen-Cahn, reducing error by 56% and 37% over the best competitor (MoE-POT-M: 0.0237 and 0.152), respectively. After 200-epoch fine-tuning, Origo-200 extends its lead, achieving the best L2RE on 9/10 columns, with the largest margins on PDEArena NS (0.0247 vs. 0.0384 for DPOT-200) and CFDBench (0.00443 vs. 0.00468 for MoE-POT-200).

## Relation to Prior Work

Origo generalizes classical Strang operator splitting (Hairer et al., 2006) from fixed, hand-designed sub-solvers into a learnable, context-adaptive architecture, and adapts the FNO spectral multiplier (Li et al., 2020a) as the parameterization for the linear sub-flow. It departs from recent PDE foundation models (DPOT, MoE-POT, OmniArch) that learn a single monolithic update operator, instead decomposing evolution into a global spectral linear stream and a sparse bank of local KAN-based nonlinear operators routed by Entmax. The Mechanism Inference module, which infers a latent 'physics code' from a short context window to configure the solver, has no direct analogue in prior PDE pretraining work, which typically relies on equation identifiers or text prompts for conditioning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two persistent pain points in multi-physics PDE pretraining—negative transfer from heterogeneous dynamics and the black-box nature of learned operators—by making the splitting structure explicit and the operator selection sparse and inspectable. The demonstrated recovery of physical coefficients (e.g., advection weight −0.9752 vs. ground truth −1.0) suggests a path toward PDE surrogates that are not only accurate but also auditable, which is critical for safety-sensitive scientific applications. Open questions include whether the spectral-diagonalizability assumption on the linear part limits applicability to genuinely nonlinear or geometrically complex PDEs, whether the fixed operator library scales to truly novel physics, and whether the interpretability gains persist at higher Reynolds numbers or in 3D turbulent regimes beyond the qualitative 3D NS comparison.
