## Problem

LLM serving must simultaneously satisfy compute-intensive prefill and memory-bound decode phases under strict latency SLOs, but existing systems face a fundamental tradeoff: aggregating both phases on shared GPUs causes prefill to inflate decode TBT, while disaggregating them across separate GPUs wastes resources through duplicated model weights and KV-cache transfers. No prior approach achieves disaggregation-level phase isolation within a single GPU without permanently incurring the overhead of full disaggregation.

## Input / Output

The system ingests streaming text prompts of varying lengths (ranging from ~1K to ~12K input tokens) arriving as a Poisson process, alongside pre-loaded LLM weights (Qwen3-8B at TP=1, Qwen3-14B at TP=2, Qwen3-32B at TP=8) on one or more H100 GPUs. It produces generated text tokens in a streaming fashion, with the primary quality metrics being Time-to-First-Token (TTFT) for prefill responsiveness and Time-Between-Tokens (TBT) for decode latency, alongside total request throughput. The scheduler operates at iteration granularity, making per-iteration decisions about whether to run in aggregated or spatially-multiplexed mode.

## Method

DuetServe operates in a default aggregated (chunked-prefill) mode and dynamically activates SM-level spatial multiplexing only when an attention-aware roofline model predicts that the current mixed prefill-decode batch will violate the TBT SLO. The roofline model decomposes per-iteration latency into token-level (linear, layernorm), sequence-level (attention with per-request query and cached-KV lengths), and communication (ring AllReduce) components, enabling accurate latency prediction under any SM allocation. When triggered, a partitioning optimizer enumerates feasible SM splits (in TPC granularity via libsmctrl) and selects the configuration maximizing total token throughput subject to the decode TBT constraint, executing k decode steps concurrently with one prefill batch on disjoint SM sets. An interruption-free execution engine eliminates CPU-GPU synchronization stalls by pre-recording decode kernels into CUDA Graphs, pre-allocating KV cache slots for k future steps, and launching decode graphs before prefill kernels to avoid CPU dispatch bottlenecks.

## Results

On Qwen3-8B (TP=1) with the Mooncake workload, DuetServe sustains 2.72 req/s at QPS=5 versus 2.14 req/s for vLLM, a 1.3× throughput improvement while keeping TBT consistently lower. On Azure-Code at QPS=16, DuetServe maintains TBT below 150 ms and achieves 13.57 req/s, compared to >200 ms TBT for vLLM and SGLang-Chunked and >680 ms for SGLang-Default. On Qwen3-14B (TP=2), DuetServe sustains TBT below 150 ms even under saturation, while vLLM-TP2 and SGLang-Chunked-TP2 exceed 200 ms beyond QPS=13. Under synthetic prefill-heavy workloads (ISL=4096, OSL=64), DuetServe achieves 12.8 req/s with 105 ms mean TBT versus vLLM's 10.0 req/s and 170 ms TBT (1.28× gain).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vLLM (chunked prefill, 8192-token budget), DuetServe wins on both TBT and throughput across all three workloads, achieving up to 1.3× higher request throughput on Mooncake while keeping TBT below 150 ms where vLLM exceeds 200 ms under load. Against SGLang-Default (throughput-oriented scheduler), DuetServe achieves 1.1× higher throughput on Azure-Code (13.57 vs. 12.43 req/s) and avoids SGLang-Default's unbounded TBT growth (>680 ms). Against SGLang-Chunked (Sarathi-Serve), DuetServe matches or beats TBT and consistently outperforms on throughput, particularly at higher QPS where SGLang-Chunked's fixed token budget causes TBT violations. Against Dynamo (1P+1D PD disaggregation), DuetServe trades a slightly higher TBT for substantially higher throughput, since Dynamo's single prefill GPU becomes a bottleneck under increasing QPS, yielding less than half the total tokens-per-second of the aggregated baselines. Overall, DuetServe dominates on the joint throughput–TBT frontier, matching disaggregation-level TBT isolation only when needed while retaining the throughput advantage of co-located execution.

## Relation to Prior Work

DuetServe builds directly on Sarathi-Serve's chunked-prefill scheduling as its default execution mode and on libsmctrl's driver-level SM masking as its hardware mechanism, but departs from both by making the partitioning decision adaptive and predictive rather than static. It positions itself against two extremes in the LLM serving literature: aggregated systems (vLLM, SGLang) that suffer prefill-decode interference, and disaggregated systems (DistServe, Splitwise, Dynamo) that incur model duplication and KV-transfer overhead. Among concurrent GPU-multiplexing approaches (MuxServe, MuxWise, Bullet, Semi-PD, Nexus), DuetServe's distinguishing contribution is the attention-aware roofline model that triggers spatial partitioning only when a TBT violation is predicted, combined with a look-ahead decode engine that eliminates per-step CPU-GPU synchronization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The adaptive 'partition only when needed' strategy offers a practical middle ground for production LLM serving: it avoids the persistent resource waste of full PD disaggregation while still guaranteeing decode latency SLOs under contention, which is critical as workloads shift toward long-context and prefill-heavy patterns. Limitations include evaluation on only two H100 GPUs and two model sizes (8B, 14B), a roofline model that is intentionally conservative for decode (potentially leaving small compute bubbles), and no multi-node or heterogeneous-hardware validation. Open questions include how the approach scales to larger TP/PP configurations, whether the roofline predictor generalizes to MoE or speculative-decoding architectures, and how to handle failure recovery when one SM partition stalls.
