## Problem

Standard self-attention's globally normalized softmax weights force all tokens to compete for influence at every layer, inducing strong synchronization dynamics that drive token representations toward a single dominant mode. This global coupling is linked to representation collapse and attention sink phenomena, where attention mass concentrates on a few tokens regardless of semantic relevance. The paper asks whether globally normalized similarity-based aggregation should be the default interaction rule, and proposes bounded-confidence dynamics as an alternative principle.

## Input / Output

The mechanism operates on standard Transformer token sequences: patchified image tokens for vision (32×32 or 224×224 images), pixel-value sequences for autoregressive image generation (784 tokens for MNIST, 3072 for CIFAR-10), and text token sequences for language modeling. The key architectural parameters are a local neighborhood window W (spatial grid for vision, causal window for autoregressive tasks) and a top-k sparsity level k ≤ W that controls how many neighbors each token actually attends to. For LLMs, Krause Attention is inserted as an auxiliary shortcut pathway alongside standard self-attention with a learnable mixing ratio, rather than as a full replacement.

## Method

Krause Attention replaces the standard query-key dot-product similarity with a Euclidean distance Δ_{i,j} = ||q_i − k_j||, mapped through a radial basis function kernel s_{i,j} = exp(−Δ²_{i,j}/(2σ²)) where σ is a learnable scale parameter. This distance-based affinity is then constrained by two mechanisms: (1) a local neighborhood mask that restricts each token's attention to a spatial or causal window of size W, and (2) a top-k selection that retains only the k most similar neighbors within that window. Attention weights are normalized over this reduced set, producing a row-stochastic influence matrix with a block-diagonal structure that decomposes the global interaction graph into independent local subsystems. Theoretically, this bounded-confidence structure prevents the global contraction toward a single consensus mode characteristic of standard attention, instead supporting stable multi-cluster equilibria where tokens self-organize into locally coherent groups. Computationally, the locality constraint reduces per-layer complexity from O(N²d) to O(NWd) while preserving content-adaptive, distance-aware token interactions.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, Krause ViT (KViT) achieves 93.81% (Tiny), 95.20% (Small), and 95.35% (Base) top-1 accuracy, outperforming standard ViT by +3.06%, +1.87%, and +2.90% respectively while reducing FLOPs by ~30%. On CIFAR-100, gains are even larger: KViT-T reaches 74.34% vs. ViT-T's 66.07% (+8.27%). For autoregressive image generation, KARM achieves the best BPD (0.5652 on MNIST, 3.0032 on CIFAR-10) while generating images 2× faster than full-attention ARM. On language benchmarks, Krause-Llama3-8B surpasses both the base model and its LoRA-finetuned variant on BoolQ, CB, PIQA, MNLI, ANLI, and IFEval, and from-scratch Krause models at 100M/200M scale outperform Standard, Top-k, Longformer, and Routing Transformer baselines on LAMBADA, CBT, and PIQA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard ViT (Dosovitskiy, 2021), KViT wins on every model scale and dataset: +3.06% on CIFAR-10 (Tiny), +8.27% on CIFAR-100 (Tiny), and +0.90% on ImageNet-1K (ViT-B-16), all while cutting FLOPs by ~30%. Against Swin Transformer (Liu, 2021), KSwin-T/S achieve 91.09%/91.13% vs. 90.73%/90.21% on CIFAR-10, confirming that the RBF distance kernel adds value beyond Swin's built-in spatial locality. For image generation, KARM beats full-attention ARM on BPD (0.5652 vs. 0.5685 on MNIST; 3.0032 vs. 3.0224 on CIFAR-10) and is 2× faster, while linear-attention LARM (Katharopoulos, 2020) is faster but has worse BPD (0.5855 / 3.1836). On Llama3-8B (Grattafiori, 2024), Krause-Llama3-8B outperforms both the base model and the LoRA-finetuned variant on BoolQ (80.59 vs. 76.13/80.41), PIQA (77.77 vs. 51.52/75.16), and IFEval (34.01 vs. 22.18/32.72), while matching MMLU-Pro. In from-scratch language models, Krause Attention beats Standard (Vaswani, 2017), Top-k (Gupta, 2021), Longformer (Beltagy, 2020), and Routing Transformer (Roy, 2021) on LAMBADA (24.51/30.60 vs. 23.33/29.67, 22.80/30.31, 22.26/28.29, 18.96/18.84) and CBT (73.42/80.14 vs. 72.20/77.91, 71.24/79.35, 68.18/76.22, 68.28/70.78) at 100M/200M scale, though it trails slightly on Blimp and Hellaswag.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper instantiates the Krause bounded-confidence consensus model (Krause et al., 2000; Hegselmann & Krause, 2002) as a Transformer attention mechanism, replacing dot-product similarity with an RBF distance kernel constrained by local neighborhoods and top-k sparsity. It departs from prior efficient-attention work (sparse attention, Performer, Reformer, linear attention) that approximates or restricts the existing softmax interaction for computational reasons; here the interaction rule itself is redesigned from first principles of bounded-confidence dynamics. The theoretical framing draws on recent work modeling self-attention as interacting particle systems (Geshkovski et al., 2023) and mean-field Wasserstein gradient flows (Chen et al., 2025), positioning Krause Attention as a structural modification that converts fragile emergent clustering into an architectural inductive bias.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a principled, theoretically grounded alternative to global softmax attention that simultaneously addresses attention sinks, representation collapse, and quadratic complexity—three problems typically tackled in isolation. The consistent gains across vision, generation, and language domains suggest bounded-confidence dynamics are a broadly applicable inductive bias. Key limitations include the LLM experiments using Krause Attention only as an auxiliary pathway (not full replacement), task-dependent hyperparameter selection (window size, top-k, σ), and theoretical analyses that are asymptotic rather than finite-depth. Open questions include whether full replacement of standard attention in large LLMs is viable, how to adaptively select hyperparameters, and whether the multi-cluster structure persists at much larger scales.
