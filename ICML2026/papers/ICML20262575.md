## Problem

In constrained multi-objective reinforcement learning, scalarization and signed, normalized group-relative advantages (as in GRPO) are brittle under objective-scale drift, Pareto near-ties, and feasibility scarcity. Two specific failure modes arise: sign-flip cancellation, where opposite signs assigned to near-indifferent candidates create destructive interference on shared structures, and loss of feasible anchors, where reweighting is dominated by infeasible candidates when feasible samples are rare. The paper asks whether within-group comparisons can be converted into stable, PF-aligned update directions without handcrafted reward shaping.

## Input / Output

For each conditioning context, the method samples a group of G candidates from the current policy and evaluates each to obtain a multi-objective vector f(x) ∈ R^m and a nonnegative constraint violation v(x) ≥ 0, alongside a maintained feasible Pareto-front archive. From these, three bounded per-sample scalars are constructed: a PF-progress score s_i, a signed feasibility residual g_i (negative for feasible, positive for infeasible), and a distinctiveness score h_i measuring within-group separation. The method outputs a nonnegative reweighting distribution q* over the group (the E-step) and an updated policy π_θ (the M-step), ultimately producing a feasible Pareto front in objective space.

## Method

CoPro replaces signed scalar advantages with a two-step EM-style loop. In the E-step, it solves a KL-regularized, moment-constrained projection over each sampled group: maximize expected PF-progress score minus a KL penalty, subject to a feasibility moment (Σ q_i g_i ≤ 0) and a distinctiveness moment (Σ q_i h_i ≥ τ_c). This convex problem admits a closed-form exponential-family solution q*_i ∝ u_i exp((s_i − κg_i + μh_i)/τ_KL), where (κ, μ) are Lagrange multipliers solved via a lightweight 2D dual. A key guarantee (Lemma 4.2) ensures strictly positive mass on feasible anchors whenever any feasible candidate exists in the group. In the M-step, the policy is projected toward q* via weighted maximum likelihood with a KL trust-region regularizer, yielding a PF-aligned gradient direction where all weights are nonnegative, eliminating sign-flip cancellation on shared patterns. When no feasible sample exists, the method injects an archive anchor or applies a relaxed feasibility moment to maintain well-definedness.

## Results

On AnalogGym (8 analog IC design tasks, 4000 SPICE evaluations), CoPro achieves top-2 FOM_L on all tasks and top-2 FOM_S on 7 of 8, e.g., on PFC it reaches FOM_L=296.3 and FOM_S=987.3 (both best), and on DFCFC2 it reaches FOM_L=976.3 (best) with the smallest Area (73.4). On BFCL-v3 tool-calling with Qwen-2.5-3B (long setting), CoPro attains 42.6% average accuracy, 84.9% format compliance, and 472.5 average output length, outperforming all baselines on accuracy and format while producing shorter outputs than GRPO (615.9) and DAPO (520.4). Ablations confirm the feasibility moment constraint is the most critical component: removing it increases VR from 5.8% to 26.0% and drops HV from 0.683 to 0.550 on PFC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On AnalogGym, CoPro outperforms all seven baselines (Ckt-GNN, BCEA, rGNN-RL, MA-Opt, MARL, RoSE-Opt, GRPO) on FOM_L for 7 of 8 circuits (losing only to RoSE-Opt on ACBC by 8.0) and on FOM_S for 7 of 8 (losing only to MARL on DFCFC2 by 25.0). The largest margins appear on challenging circuits: on PFC, CoPro's FOM_L of 296.3 exceeds the next-best BCEA (280.0) by 5.8% and GRPO (221.7) by 33.6%; on DFCFC2, CoPro's FOM_L of 976.3 beats MARL (708.1) by 37.9%. CoPro also achieves the smallest Area on 5 of 8 circuits, indicating compact designs. On BFCL-v3 tool-calling, CoPro surpasses GDPO (the strongest baseline) by 2.1% average accuracy and 1.8% format compliance on Qwen-2.5-3B (long setting), while producing outputs 63.7 tokens shorter than GDPO (472.5 vs. 536.2). Compared to GRPO, CoPro reduces average output length by 143.4 tokens (long) and 172.2 tokens (short) on the 3B model, indicating that treating length as a feasibility constraint rather than a shaped reward yields tighter control. In Appendix H, CoPro also outperforms five additional constrained-MORL baselines (CRPO, LI-PD, EPPO, CAGA, EDPF) on VR suppression, maintaining constraint violation rates below 6% throughout training where penalty-based methods exhibit larger VR bursts.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoPro extends the GRPO group-relative policy optimization paradigm (Shao et al., 2024) by replacing signed, standardized within-group advantages with a nonnegative reweighting distribution obtained via KL-regularized moment-constrained projection. The E-step's exponential-family solution draws directly on the KL-regularized policy search / mirror-descent template from REPS (Peters et al., 2010) and MPO, but applies it at the group level with moment constraints encoding feasibility and distinctiveness. The paper departs from the dominant scalarization-based constrained MORL literature (preference vectors, penalty methods, primal-dual updates) by avoiding reward shaping entirely and instead learning feasible-PF progress directly from objective-induced within-group comparisons.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean sample-level guarantee (Lemma 4.2) that feasible anchors receive strictly positive mass under the feasibility moment constraint provides a principled mechanism for handling feasibility scarcity without ad-hoc penalty tuning, which is a persistent practical pain point in constrained multi-objective optimization. The method's generality—validated on both analog IC design (expensive SPICE evaluations) and LLM tool calling (cheap but noisy format/correctness rewards)—suggests the projection framework could transfer to other constrained multi-objective settings. Key limitations include the per-group convex dual solve (overhead relative to simple standardized advantages), dependence on hand-designed bounded statistics (s, g, h), and the fact that the tool-calling gains are driven primarily by constraint satisfaction rather than raw accuracy, limiting the method's appeal when constraints are not the bottleneck. Open questions include learning the statistics and thresholds from data, reducing projection overhead for large groups, and extending to longer-horizon and richer constraint structures.
