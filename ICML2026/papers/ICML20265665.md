## Problem

Multi-layer feature fusion in MLLMs introduces direct backpropagation paths from the LLM's high-level semantic objectives to shallow visual layers, causing gradient interference that overwrites low-level spatial signals and destabilizes training. Additionally, there is no reliable diagnostic metric to distinguish whether OCR failures stem from loss of fine-grained visual information during tokenization or from representational misalignment in the language model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265665/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB images rescaled to multiples of 448 pixels and divided into non-overlapping 448×448 tiles, with 14×14 ViT patch embeddings 2×2-pooled into single visual tokens via an adapter, alongside text queries. The primary output is a text response (recognized text, VQA answers, etc.). The R-Probe diagnostic additionally produces pixel-level reconstructions of target image regions from projected visual tokens, using a context-aware sequence of [context-image tokens, text tokens, target-image tokens] with global 2D RoPE to enforce conditional reconstruction.

## Method

Detached Skip-Links applies a stop-gradient operator to shallow ViT features (e.g., blocks 6, 12) before concatenating them with deep features (e.g., blocks 18, 23) and the main-path output, then passing the combined vector through an MLP fusion adapter. This asymmetric design lets fine-grained spatial details flow forward to the LLM while preventing semantic-heavy gradients from the NTP loss from updating early visual layers, reducing gradient conflict without adding learnable parameters. A theoretical analysis formalizes the benefit via a Bayes-risk decomposition showing skip features provide complementary predictive information, and a gradient SNR argument showing detachment removes a high-variance, near-orthogonal noise component from the stochastic gradient. R-Probe complements the method as a probe-only diagnostic: a shallow Transformer decoder initialized from the first quarter of LLM layers (e.g., LLaMA-3.1-8B) reconstructs target image regions from frozen visual tokens, measuring whether projected features are both information-rich and aligned with the LLM's representational subspace.

## Results

On the PE backbone with LLaMA-3.1-8B, Detached Skip-Links achieves an overall average of 63.0 across 22 benchmarks, improving over the PE-baseline (61.1) by +1.9 points. The largest gains appear on OCR-centric tasks (68.3 vs. 65.2, +3.1), with consistent improvements on General (54.6 vs. 53.2) and Alignment (73.6 vs. 72.6). Across four ViT backbones (PE, InternViT, AimV2, SigLip2), the method yields +1.8 to +2.5 overall points, with OCR gains of +1.8 to +2.5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Detached Skip-Links outperforms all three multi-layer fusion baselines on the overall average: 63.0 vs. DC (62.0), ML (62.1), and DeepStack (62.6). The advantage is most pronounced on OCR benchmarks, where Ours scores 68.3 compared to DC (66.7), ML (66.9), and DeepStack (67.6) — a 1.7–1.6 point margin. Notably, applying the detach operation to DC and ML also improves them (DC: 62.0→62.6, ML: 62.1→62.5), confirming that gradient detachment is a generalizable training-time fix rather than an architecture-specific trick. DeepStack is the closest competitor, matching Ours on STEM (63.8 vs. 64.1) and Alignment (73.2 vs. 73.6), but still trails by 0.4 on overall and 0.7 on OCR. The gains are consistent across all four ViT backbones tested, with the largest relative improvement on InternViT (+2.5 overall, +7.4 on Alignment).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the multi-layer visual feature fusion line of work (Dense Connector, DeepStack, ML fusion) that injects shallow ViT features into MLLMs to recover fine-grained detail lost in deep encoders. It departs from prior approaches by identifying a previously under-discussed optimization pathology—gradient interference from skip pathways—and proposing a minimal training-time fix (stop-gradient on shallow skip features) rather than a new architectural module. The R-Probe diagnostic is positioned as distinct from both linear probing methods and full reconstruction objectives, instead using a shallow LLM-initialized decoder to measure LLM-aligned visual fidelity. The gradient-control idea draws loosely from h-detach for LSTMs and gradient surgery for multi-task learning, but applies them to the specific ViT-LLM fusion setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that naive multi-layer fusion destabilizes shallow visual layers through semantic-heavy gradients is practically important for anyone training MLLMs with skip-based feature aggregation, and the fix is trivially implementable (a single .detach() call) with no added parameters. R-Probe fills a genuine diagnostic gap by separating visual encoding quality from language-side inference failures, which is critical for OCR hallucination debugging. Limitations include a slight trade-off in reasoning-heavy tasks when R-Probe is used as an auxiliary loss (attributed to OCR-centric data bias), and the theoretical analysis is explicitly local to the early training phase. Open questions include whether the shallow/deep detachment boundary generalizes to much deeper encoders or non-ViT backbones, and whether the gradient-interference phenomenon persists or resolves at substantially larger model scales.
