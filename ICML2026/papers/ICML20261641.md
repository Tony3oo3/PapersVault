## Problem

Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) like DeepSeek-R1 achieve high accuracy through long chain-of-thought reasoning, but suffer from overthinking—excessive token generation that wastes compute and can degrade accuracy. Existing GRPO-based efficient reasoning methods rely on static, task-agnostic length rewards that apply the same penalty regardless of problem difficulty, causing over-compression on hard problems and inadvertently penalizing correct but longer trajectories. The paper addresses this by asking: how can length rewards be dynamically calibrated per-question to respect the true length-accuracy tradeoff without sacrificing correctness?

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes a text question and samples a group of G=8 reasoning trajectories (each up to 8000 tokens) from the current policy, from which it estimates length and correctness statistics to compute a per-question optimal length. At inference, the model takes a standard math prompt (with a 'reason step by step' instruction) and produces a chain-of-thought reasoning path followed by a final answer in \boxed{} format, with the key behavioral change being that output length adapts to problem difficulty—shorter on easy problems, longer on hard ones—without any external length constraint.

## Method

SmartThinker is a GRPO-based post-training method whose core innovation is a two-part dynamic reward design. First, it models both the overall trajectory-length distribution and the correct-trajectory-length distribution as Gaussians (N(μ₁,σ₁²) and N(μ₂,σ₂²) respectively), then derives a closed-form optimal length l*opt = (σ₁²μ₂ − σ₂²μ₁)/(σ₁² − σ₂²) as the argmax of the posterior Pr(correct | length), estimated per-question from group statistics during each rollout. Second, it introduces a dynamic length-reward coefficient Λ = p_err / (mean(r_len) − min(r_len)) that guarantees correct trajectories always receive non-negative normalized advantages, preventing the suppression of valid but longer reasoning paths. The length penalty itself is asymmetric: only correct trajectories exceeding l*opt receive a ReLU-based penalty, while incorrect trajectories receive no length reward, and when l*opt ≥ max(L_acc) the method gracefully degenerates to standard GRPO.

## Results

SmartThinker achieves up to 52.6% reasoning token compression while simultaneously improving accuracy across all three base models. On DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B, it reaches 74.5% average accuracy (vs. 73.1% base) with 5,382 average tokens (vs. 8,463 base), and on AIME25 it attains 40.8% accuracy—a 16.6% relative improvement over the base model's 35.0%. On Qwen3-4B-Thinking-2507, it compresses average length from 13,040 to 7,747 tokens while improving average accuracy from 88.5% to 89.0%. The method requires only 50–150 RL training steps depending on model scale, achieving competitive or superior results compared to baselines trained for 1,000+ steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SmartThinker outperforms all RL-based baselines on average accuracy across every model scale. On the 1.5B model, it achieves 61.9% average accuracy versus 52.3% for ShorterBetter, 59.8% for LASER-DE-4096, and 60.95% for ThinkPrune-4k, while also attaining the highest AE score (0.54). On the 7B model, SmartThinker reaches 74.5% average accuracy and 40.8% on AIME25, surpassing LASER-DE-4096 (71.0% avg, 30.0% AIME25) and ShorterBetter (68.3% avg, 30.0% AIME25) by a substantial margin. ShorterBetter achieves the shortest reasoning lengths (e.g., 2,327 avg tokens on 1.5B) but suffers severe accuracy drops, particularly on AIME25 where it falls below the base model. LASER-DE-4096 and ThinkPrune-4k adopt more conservative compression strategies that preserve MATH500 accuracy well but still lose 2–5 points on AIME25 relative to SmartThinker. Notably, SmartThinker is the only method that consistently improves average accuracy over the base model across all three scales, and it does so with far fewer training steps (50–150) than LASER (1,000 steps).

## Relation to Prior Work

SmartThinker builds directly on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) as its optimization backbone and addresses the overthinking problem identified in LRMs like DeepSeek-R1 and OpenAI o1. It departs from prior GRPO-based efficient reasoning methods (ShorterBetter, ThinkPrune, LASER) by replacing their static, task-agnostic length rewards with a probabilistic Gaussian model that estimates a per-question optimal reasoning length, combined with a dynamic length-reward coefficient that prevents correct trajectories from receiving negative normalized advantages. The key conceptual shift is from uniformly penalizing length to adaptively calibrating compression based on the joint distribution of response lengths and correctness within each GRPO group.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The simultaneous achievement of up to 52.6% token compression and up to 16.6% relative accuracy improvement on AIME25 is practically significant for deploying reasoning models where inference cost is a primary constraint. The method's simplicity (a closed-form optimal length estimate plus a dynamic coefficient) makes it easy to integrate into existing GRPO training pipelines, as demonstrated by its plug-in compatibility with AutoThink and ThinkPrune. Key limitations include reliance on outcome-only rewards (no process supervision), the idealized Gaussian assumption for length distributions, and evaluation primarily on mathematical benchmarks, leaving open questions about applicability to open-ended or non-mathematical reasoning tasks and other RL algorithm variants.
