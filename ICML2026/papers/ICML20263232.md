## Problem

Autoregressive LLMs systematically degrade during long-horizon generation—producing repetitive text, losing instruction adherence, and exhibiting unstable entropy—yet practitioners lack any online diagnostic to detect these failures in real time as they occur. Existing mitigations operate at training time or via offline evaluation, leaving production deployments without a runtime signal that a specific generation is deteriorating. This paper formalizes the degradation as 'cognitive fatigue' and asks whether it can be rigorously defined and reliably measured at inference time without retraining.

## Input / Output

At each decoding step (probed every 2 tokens), the method reads last-layer attention weights, top-layer hidden states, and next-token logits from a standard decoder-only model, requiring no weight modifications or architectural changes. It produces a scalar Fatigue Index (FI) in [0,1] per token, decomposed into three per-signal contributions (attention decay, entropy deviation, embedding drift), and a binary alert signal generated by passing a 5-token smoothed FI through hysteresis with separate activation (0.50) and deactivation (0.40) thresholds. All normalization maps are stateless and incur negligible computational overhead, making the pipeline compatible with online inference loops on models from 1B to 13B parameters.

## Method

The Fatigue Index is a weighted linear aggregation of three token-level signals, each capturing a distinct failure mode: mean last-layer attention mass to a fixed prompt slice (instruction loss), Shannon entropy deviation from a fixed healthy band [3.8, 5.0] (calibration breakdown), and L2 distance of the current hidden state from the initial prompt hidden state (representational drift). Each raw signal is mapped to [0,1] via a fixed monotone normalization, then combined with theory-guided weights (w_A=0.40, w_E=0.35, w_D=0.25) reflecting domain priors about which signal most directly governs each failure mode. The construction is grounded in five axioms—monotonicity, scale invariance, boundedness, temporal stability, and compositionality—that constrain the space of valid online fatigue measures and justify the linear, interpretable form over more complex aggregators. Empirically, the aggregated FI achieves AUROC 0.978 for detecting severe degeneration, substantially outperforming any single component (best individual: drift at 0.929), and hysteresis-based alerting reduces false trigger flips by over 91% compared to a naive single-threshold rule.

## Results

On HotpotQA, the aggregated Fatigue Index (FI) achieves an AUROC of 0.976 for detecting severe degeneration (top-quartile repetition), outperforming the strongest individual signal (entropy alone, AUROC 0.954). FI correlates strongly with repetition-based failure across all three datasets: Spearman ρ = 0.848 (HotpotQA), 0.856 (SQuAD), and 0.820 (TriviaQA) over full 120-token generations. Hysteresis-based alerting on FI reduces spurious alert flips by over 91% compared to a naive single-threshold rule (e.g., 18.9 → 1.6 flips/generation on HotpotQA). Mean FI values are tightly clustered around 0.82 across domains, confirming fatigue as a domain-agnostic phenomenon.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare FI against external runtime monitors (e.g., perplexity-based or semantic-entropy-based diagnostics); instead, it benchmarks FI against its own individual signal components as ablation baselines. The aggregated FI (AUROC 0.976) outperforms entropy alone (0.954) by 2.2 points and drift alone (0.929) by 4.7 points on HotpotQA degeneration detection. Attention alone is essentially non-informative (AUROC 0.307, below random chance), yet it carries the highest weight (0.40) in FI because it most directly reflects instruction-following rather than surface repetition. For online alerting, hysteresis on FI reduces alert flips by 91.7–93.1% versus a naive threshold, demonstrating practical stability. The authors explicitly note that FI is a correlational risk indicator for repetition-based degeneration and does not cover hallucination or factual-error detection, so no head-to-head comparison with hallucination-specific baselines (e.g., semantic entropy) is provided.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by formalizing long-horizon generation degradation as a measurable, online runtime state rather than treating it as a training-time or post-hoc evaluation problem. It repurposes three previously isolated signals—prompt-attention decay (from the 'lost in the middle' literature), embedding drift (from residual-stream analysis), and entropy miscalibration (from text-degeneration and calibration work)—into a single axiomatic index, departing from prior work that either fixes degradation via training (unlikelihood training, nucleus sampling) or detects it offline (semantic entropy for hallucination). The axiomatic framing (monotonicity, boundedness, compositionality) is novel in this context, providing a normative design space for runtime diagnostics that prior monitoring approaches lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FI offers a lightweight, retrain-free runtime monitor that production LLM deployments currently lack, enabling real-time detection of degradation before it manifests as user-visible errors. Key open questions include whether FI generalizes beyond repetition-based degeneration to hallucination and factual errors, whether closed-loop intervention triggered by FI actually improves downstream quality, and whether the fixed linear aggregation and entropy band transfer across model families and task distributions. The observational finding that drift slopes are roughly constant from 1B to 13B (larger models drift more coherently rather than less) challenges the assumption that scale alone resolves long-horizon instability, though architectural and data confounders limit causal claims.
