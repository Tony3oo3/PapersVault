## Problem

The dominant paradigm for latent-action world models trains a LAM (IDM + FDM) and a world model in separate stages, making the FDM redundant with the world model and forcing the world model to rely on a static, frozen latent action space. Replacing the FDM with the world model for joint training is conceptually simple but naively leads to VQ codebook collapse, as the pretrained world model quickly disregards uninformative random action signals. The paper asks whether a principled training procedure can enable stable, synergistic co-evolution of latent action learning and world modeling in a single end-to-end pipeline.

## Input / Output

The system consumes 256-resolution video clips (patchified at 14×14) and produces predicted next-frame video via a 10-step flow-matching denoising process. Latent actions are discrete: two 32-dimensional tokens sampled from a 32-entry VQ codebook (1024 total combinations), extracted by a 12-layer ST-Transformer IDM. For downstream real-action control, a 2-layer MLP adapter maps continuous robot actions to the learned VQ code indices, and the world model is fine-tuned for 3K steps on ground-truth latent actions before being evaluated in both GT-LAM and adapter-mediated modes. Visual planning uses an MPC loop with 50–200 sampled action sequences per step.

## Method

CoLA-World replaces the FDM in the LAM with a pretrained OpenSora diffusion model (1.2B params), conditioning it on latent actions via AdaLN (6 self-attention blocks + MLP producing scale/shift/gate parameters fused with diffusion-timestep modulation). The critical innovation is a warm-up phase: the world model is frozen while the from-scratch IDM and VQ quantizer are trained solely on gradients backpropagated through the action-conditioning modules, aligning the LAM's representations with the pretrained world model's expectations. After warm-up (8K steps), full end-to-end joint training begins, where the world model acts as a 'tutor' providing increasingly informative gradients to refine the LAM, while the evolving LAM supplies a progressively more precise control interface to the world model. This co-evolution cycle is verified by ablations showing that a frozen world model yields slower LAM improvement and a frozen LAM causes the world model to plateau, confirming bidirectional mutual reinforcement.

## Results

On video prediction (Table 1), CoLA-World (WARM8K+E2E52K) consistently matches or surpasses the 2-stage baseline (LAM30K+WM30K) across all four datasets, with the largest gains on FVD: 278.90 vs. 291.30 on OXE, 158.36 vs. 167.77 on LIBERO (OOD), and 174.93 vs. 185.63 on AgiBot. The data-efficiency advantage is striking: the smaller-budget JOINT WARM8K+E2E30K checkpoint already surpasses the fully trained 2-stage model on OOD LIBERO (FVD 164.86 vs. 167.77). On visual planning (VP² RoboDesk), CoLA-World achieves 21.20% average success rate, nearly 3× the 2-stage baseline (7.73%) and ~1.7× AdaWorld (12.17%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against a 2-stage pipeline (train LAM with a lightweight FDM, then freeze the IDM to condition a separate world model), which follows the standard protocol from Genie and AdaWorld. CoLA-World wins on FVD across all four video prediction datasets at equal total budget (60K steps), with the largest margin on OOD LIBERO (158.36 vs. 167.77 FVD) and AgiBot (174.93 vs. 185.63 FVD). The data-efficiency gap is even more pronounced: CoLA-World's 38K-step checkpoint (WARM8K+E2E30K) already beats the 2-stage 60K-step model on OOD LIBERO, and the 2-stage model given a longer 82K budget (LAM30K+WM52K) still trails CoLA-World's 60K-step model. On real-action adaptation (Table 2), CoLA-World's advantage widens substantially: FVD drops from 115.45 to 93.68 on LIBERO and from 188.82 to 169.70 on RoboDesk, attributed to the co-evolved LAM's resistance to codebook collapse under adapter inference. On visual planning (VP² RoboDesk, Table 3), CoLA-World achieves 21.20% average success rate versus 7.73% for the 2-stage baseline and 12.17% for AdaWorld, nearly tripling the 2-stage result on tasks like Upright Block (35.33% vs. 22.00%) and Push Blue (29.33% vs. 6.00%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant two-stage paradigm for latent-action world models (Genie, AdaWorld, Motus), where a small forward dynamics model (FDM) is trained alongside the inverse dynamics model (IDM) and then discarded before a larger world model is trained on frozen latent actions. The key conceptual move is to eliminate this redundancy by using the pretrained video-generation world model (OpenSora/Wan2.1) directly as the FDM, enabling end-to-end joint training. The critical technical contribution is identifying that naive joint training collapses (even with a pretrained IDM) and resolving it with a warm-up phase where the world model is frozen while the LAM aligns to its representation space. The AdaLN-based action conditioning mechanism follows prior work (IRASim, DWS), but here the conditioned signals are learnable latent actions rather than fixed real actions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CoLA-World establishes that joint training of latent actions and world models is not only feasible but superior to the two-stage approach, offering a cleaner, more data-efficient pipeline where every training FLOP contributes to the final deployable model. The co-evolution analysis (world model as tutor, LAM as control interface) provides a mechanistic understanding that could inform future designs of coupled representation-learning systems. Limitations include dependence on the pretrained video backbone's physical grounding, modest long-horizon prediction quality, and the computational overhead of backpropagating through a large diffusion model. Open directions include scaling to larger video corpora, integrating with vision-language-action policies, and exploring whether the warm-up strategy generalizes to other pretrained-model + from-scratch-module co-training settings.
