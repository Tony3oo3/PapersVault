## Problem

Neural Collapse (NC) is the provable global optimum of supervised learning objectives, yet standard pipelines never reach it: cross-entropy leaves radial degrees of freedom unconstrained, converging to a degenerate geometry, while supervised contrastive learning (SCL) builds NC structure during pretraining only to destroy it in a post-hoc linear probing phase. The paper asks how to close this theory-practice gap by fixing each paradigm at its specific point of failure.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for ImageNet-100/1K) through a ResNet encoder and produces class labels via angular similarity to unit-norm class prototypes on the hypersphere. Unlike standard classifiers, both features and classifier weights are L2-normalized and biases are eliminated, so the output is purely a direction-based decision: the nearest prototype on S^{d-1} determines the predicted class. For the SCL variant, the classifier is training-free—class-mean embeddings from the projection head serve directly as prototypes, requiring only a single forward pass rather than a separate multi-epoch probing phase.

## Method

The paper unifies normalized softmax (CL) and SCL as two views of the same operation—prototype contrast on the unit hypersphere—then closes the remaining gaps to NC with two new losses and one architectural change. NTCE inverts the contrastive direction so each class prototype anchors against all M batch samples (rather than K class prototypes), dramatically expanding the effective negative set. NONL further decouples alignment from uniformity by excluding same-class instances from the normalization denominator, preventing positive pairs from generating repulsive gradients. For SCL, the paper proves (Theorem 4.2) that the SCL objective already optimizes a prototype-softmax classifier whose weights are the class-mean embeddings throughout training, making the standard linear probing step both redundant and harmful; the Fixed Prototypes (FP) method simply sets classifier weights to these learned means with no additional training. All three normalized losses (NormFace, NTCE, NONL) are proven to have NC as their unique global minimizer under balanced UFM/LPM assumptions.

## Results

On the four main classification benchmarks, NONL achieves the best accuracy on CIFAR-10 (94.9%), CIFAR-100 (73.6%), and ImageNet-100 (84.9%), while NTCE leads on ImageNet-1K (76.7%), both surpassing standard cross-entropy (CE) by 0.3–1.5 points. In the SCL setting, Fixed Prototypes (FP) match linear probing on 3/4 datasets and improve ImageNet-100 by +2.0% (86.8 vs. 84.8) while eliminating the separate classifier training phase. Downstream, NONL yields a +5.5% mean relative improvement in transfer learning over CE, up to +8.7% under severe class imbalance (CIFAR-10-LT, τ=0.01), and reduces ImageNet-C mean corruption error from 25.0% to 23.5%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NormFace (Wang et al., 2017), the closest normalized-softmax baseline, NONL wins on CIFAR-100 (+1.2 pp, 73.6 vs. 72.4) and ImageNet-100 (+0.5 pp, 84.9 vs. 84.4), ties on CIFAR-10 (94.9 vs. 94.8), and edges ahead on ImageNet-1K (76.5 vs. 76.4); NTCE leads on ImageNet-1K (76.7 vs. 76.4). Against ETF+DR (Yang et al., 2022), which fixes a simplex ETF head and trains only the backbone, NONL outperforms on all four datasets (e.g., +1.5 pp on CIFAR-100, +1.1 pp on ImageNet-1K), and also surpasses it in transfer learning (70.7 vs. 54.6 mean) and long-tailed accuracy. Against SCL with standard linear probing (Khosla et al., 2020), the proposed Fixed Prototypes classifier matches LP accuracy on CIFAR-10 (95.0), CIFAR-100 (73.9), and ImageNet-1K (75.1), while improving ImageNet-100 by +2.0 pp (86.8 vs. 84.8), all without the hours-long linear probing training phase.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two seemingly distinct supervised learning paradigms—normalized softmax classifier learning (NormFace and its descendants) and supervised contrastive learning (SCL)—under a single geometric framework of prototype contrast on the unit hypersphere. It builds directly on Yaras et al.'s (2022) Riemannian analysis of NC for normalized features and Koromilas et al.'s (2024) La/Lc minimizer characterization for contrastive losses, extending both to prove NC optimality for two new objectives (NTCE, NONL) that import contrastive optimization's large negative sets and decoupled alignment/uniformity into classifier learning. The key departure from prior work is Theorem 4.2, which proves SCL's objective is equivalent to optimizing a prototype-softmax classifier with class-mean weights, thereby showing that the standard linear probing step is not merely redundant but actively harmful to the NC geometry learned during pretraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for SCL practitioners: the Fixed Prototypes method eliminates an hours-long linear probing phase while matching or exceeding its accuracy, and NTCE/NONL offer drop-in replacements for CE that accelerate NC convergence by over 90% in iterations. The unification reframes supervised learning as direction-only prototype learning, simplifying the theoretical landscape. Key limitations include the balanced-class UFM/LPM assumption (end-to-end dynamics are not characterized), the need for large batches to provide sufficient in-batch negatives, and the open question of whether collapsed representations handle coarse-to-fine transfer well. The ReLU-architecture caveat from Kini et al. (2024) also means the simplex ETF geometry may not hold for all network designs.
