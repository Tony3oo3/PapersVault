## Problem

Neural networks progressively lose the ability to learn new tasks (loss of plasticity), yet existing proxies such as effective rank, dormant neuron ratio, and weight norm lack a unifying theoretical principle and correlate poorly with future task performance. No prior measure connects plasticity to a well-defined information-theoretic quantity, making it difficult to predict downstream adaptability or select pretraining checkpoints when validation loss has plateaued.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260688/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a trained network's parameters and a synthetic memorization batch—randomly generated overlapping shapes on random backgrounds with labels sampled from the model's own predictive distribution (for vision), or i.i.d. Gaussian context windows with Gaussian targets drawn from the model's predictive distribution (for time series regression). A single backward pass on this batch yields the mean squared gradient norm, which serves as a computable lower bound on local redundancy. The model is never trained on the synthetic data; it is used purely as a probe, and the critical design choice is sampling targets from P_{θ₀} rather than uniformly, which is what converts the gradient norm from a generic sensitivity heuristic into a valid redundancy lower bound.

## Method

The paper defines local redundancy as the worst-case redundancy of an infinitesimal parameter neighborhood Θ(θ₀, ε) consisting of all parameters reachable by one gradient step of size at most ε along any data-dependent gradient direction. The key theoretical result (Theorem 3.4) proves that the expected squared gradient norm on synthetic memorization data lower-bounds this local redundancy, with the proof connecting memorization gain to worst-case redundancy via the Shtarkov sum and exploiting entropy cancellation when targets are sampled from the model's own predictive distribution. For regression settings, a rate-distortion argument (Proposition 3.5) extends the bound to continuous targets. The synthetic inputs are deliberately designed to saturate model memorization capacity (verified at ~1–2 bits per parameter), ensuring the lower bound is tight and the resulting gradient-norm curve is smooth over training. This yields a single-backward-pass, task-agnostic plasticity score that outperforms existing metrics in predicting future task accuracy and in selecting pretraining checkpoints when validation loss has saturated.

## Results

On Continual ImageNet (3000 sequential binary classification tasks), local redundancy achieves the highest residual correlation with future task accuracy (Pearson r = 8.9±0.4, Spearman ρ = 8.8±0.4, ×100) after regressing out task number, outperforming distance from initialization (7.3), weight norm (4.6), dormant neuron ratio (3.4), and training gradient norm (1.6). On ETT time series transfer learning (120 seeds), selecting the pretrain checkpoint with maximum local redundancy yields a fine-tuning validation MSE of 0.1937±0.0005 on ETTh2, beating the standard lowest-validation-loss strategy (0.1952±0.0006) and both dormant-ratio (0.1960) and weight-norm (0.1968) selection strategies. The computational overhead is minimal: one backward pass on a synthetic batch adds only 1.65% to total training time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the dormant neuron ratio (Sokar et al., 2023), local redundancy wins decisively in both settings: it achieves a residual Pearson correlation of 8.9 vs. 3.4 with future task accuracy on Continual ImageNet, and a fine-tuning MSE of 0.1937 vs. 0.1960 on ETTh2. Against weight norm (Lyle et al., 2023), local redundancy similarly outperforms with correlation 8.9 vs. 4.6 and MSE 0.1937 vs. 0.1968. The one comparison where local redundancy loses is against linear probing (Alain & Bengio, 2017), which achieves 0.1918 MSE—0.0019 better—but requires downstream labels and a training run at every checkpoint, making it inadmissible when the target domain is unknown at selection time. Local redundancy is the only metric that improves over the standard lowest-validation-loss baseline (0.1952) without any access to the target task, and it is the only plasticity metric that continues to vary meaningfully after validation loss plateaus (Figure 4), enabling checkpoint discrimination in the regime where standard metrics are uninformative.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes worst-case redundancy from universal compression theory (Shtarkov, Rissanen, Haussler & Opper) as a *local* measure of plasticity, connecting it via the Shtarkov sum and the capacity-redundancy theorem to the information radius of a parameter neighborhood. It departs from prior plasticity proxies (effective rank, dormant neuron ratio, weight norm, distance from initialization) by grounding the measure in a minimax coding argument rather than an ad-hoc network property, and introduces a computable lower bound via gradient norms on synthetic memorization data. The synthetic-data design draws on memorization-capacity results (Allen-Zhu & Li) and rate-distortion theory for the regression extension, while the continual-learning and transfer-learning evaluation settings follow established benchmarks (Continual ImageNet, ETT).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first information-theoretic justification for a plasticity measure and shows it outperforms standard proxies in predicting future task accuracy and in selecting pretraining checkpoints when validation loss plateaus—practically useful for multi-stage training pipelines. However, experiments are limited to models of a few million parameters, the gradient-norm proxy is an uncalibrated lower bound whose tightness may vary across checkpoints, and the one-step local formulation does not capture plasticity loss over long optimization horizons. Open questions include whether the measure scales to billion-parameter models, whether interventions that preserve local redundancy (replay, EWC, shrink-and-perturb) causally improve downstream performance, and whether better synthetic memorization datasets can close the gap between the bound and true redundancy.
