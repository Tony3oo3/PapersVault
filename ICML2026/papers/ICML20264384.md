## Problem

Vision Transformers increasingly use extra tokens (class tokens, register tokens) that, while effective for their intended purposes, lack semantic structure and disentanglement. Prior work revealed that self-supervised ViT features contain implicit part-level information, but no method has explicitly enforced part-level disentanglement in the latent feature space. The paper asks whether regularization constraints can transform extra tokens into disentangled representations that decompose images into semantic parts (heads, bodies, legs) without any explicit part supervision.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 224×224 RGB image, applies random augmentations to produce two views, and processes them through a frozen DINOv2 teacher and a trainable XTRA student (both ViT-Base, patch size 16×16). The student outputs 8 disentangled factor tokens (reduced from 32 initial tokens via two aggregation stages of 32→16→8), a [CLS] token for whole-image representation, and patch tokens. The factor tokens are first-class, addressable outputs at inference time, each producing a part-level attention map that consistently focuses on a specific semantic component across different objects in a scene.

## Method

XTRA augments a ViT student with learnable 'factor tokens' and enforces disentanglement through a Minimum Volume Constraint (MVC) that penalizes redundancy via J(F)=‖FᵀF−I‖²_F (evaluated through SVD for numerical stability), combined with a linear mixing model where patch tokens are reconstructed as convex combinations of factor tokens. A multi-stage aggregation mechanism (inspired by GroupViT) progressively merges correlated factor tokens using Gumbel-Softmax with straight-through hard assignment, preventing token collapse that occurs when training with MVC alone. The total loss combines asymmetric contrastive distillation from a frozen DINOv2 teacher, factor reconstruction, and the MVC volume penalty. The paper provides theoretical guarantees: identifiability of factors under MVC (Theorem 3.1), uniqueness of the minimum-volume solution (Theorem 3.2), and O(1/t) convergence of the alternating minimization (Theorem 3.3).

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, XTRA achieves 84.2% KNN accuracy and 86.0% linear-probing accuracy with a ViT-Base backbone trained on ImageNet-1K alone, outperforming all prior ImageNet-1K pre-training methods by 2.1% (KNN) and 1.5% (linear probing). The headline disentanglement metric SEPIN@1 reaches 3.95, an 8.4× improvement over DINOv2 (0.47). On PartImageNet, XTRA achieves 46.8% part mIoU (+4.5 over DINOv2), with the largest gains on articulated parts (legs +6.5%, tail +7.4%, ears +6.1%). On ImageNet-C, XTRA reduces mean Corruption Error to 38.2 (vs. 41.3 for DINOv2), confirming robustness benefits of explicit part-level disentanglement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

XTRA outperforms DINOv2 (Oquab et al., 2024) on every reported metric: SEPIN@1 (3.95 vs. 0.47, 8.4×), KNN (84.2% vs. 82.1%), linear probing (86.0% vs. 84.5%), part mIoU (46.8% vs. 42.3%), and ImageNet-C mCE (38.2 vs. 41.3). Against DINOv2+ (Darcet et al., 2024), XTRA similarly leads on all metrics, with the largest gap in disentanglement (SEPIN@1: 3.95 vs. 0.42). Compared to earlier self-supervised methods—MoCo-v3 (He et al., 2020), MAE (He et al., 2021), BEiT (Bao et al., 2022), iBOT (Zhou et al., 2022), and DINOv1 (Caron et al., 2021)—XTRA achieves substantially higher KNN and linear-probing accuracy, with the closest competitor being iBOT (72.9% KNN, 82.3% linear) and DINOv1 (76.1% KNN, 78.2% linear). Against VICReg-L applied to patch tokens (Bardes et al., 2021), XTRA achieves 4.4× better SEPIN@1 (3.95 vs. 0.89) and +3.0 part mIoU, demonstrating that token-level compositional structure (MVC) is fundamentally different from and superior to dimension-level decorrelation. Notably, XTRA uses only a ViT-Base backbone trained on ImageNet-1K, while DINOv2 and DINOv2+ use ViT-G pretrained on the much larger LVD-142M dataset, making the comparison particularly favorable to XTRA in terms of compute and data efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DINOv2+ register-token paradigm by imposing a Minimum Volume Constraint (MVC) on extra tokens, transforming them from unstructured artifact-absorbing registers into explicitly disentangled factor tokens. Conceptually, it bridges two previously separate lines of work: the self-knowledge distillation framework of DINO/DINOv2 (which discovered implicit semantic structure in ViT features) and the object-centric disentanglement literature (Slot Attention, invariant slot attention), which operates in a different architectural and training paradigm. The linear mixing model and minimum-volume simplex identification are borrowed from blind source separation and non-negative matrix factorization theory, repurposed here as a geometric regularizer in the ViT latent space rather than a generative model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicit part-level disentanglement can emerge from self-supervised pretraining without any part annotations, yielding measurable gains in part segmentation (+4.5 mIoU, largest on articulated parts), robustness to corruption (−3.1 mCE), and standard representation quality metrics. Key limitations include dependence on the teacher's within-class feature variance (rigid objects yield weaker decompositions), the need for post-hoc inspection to assign semantic labels to factor tokens, and the framework being purely descriptive rather than enabling controllable generation. Open questions include whether factor token manipulation can drive compositional image editing, and whether the approach scales to video or 3D representations.
