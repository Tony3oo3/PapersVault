## Problem

Frozen VLMs like CLIP score multi-label predictions independently via softmax, ignoring label co-occurrence structure and producing incoherent label sets under distribution shift. Existing test-time adaptation methods either require iterative backpropagation (high latency, overfitting risk) or are designed for single-label settings and fail to capture the pairwise label dependencies critical for multi-label recognition. No lightweight, gradient-free method existed that explicitly injects label dependency into zero-shot multi-label predictions without modifying the backbone.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single RGB image at a time in strict online (batch-size-1) streaming fashion, with no target annotations or external caption retrieval. It produces a K-dimensional corrected logit vector s* where each non-anchor logit is additively shifted by a PMI term; the anchor logit itself is left unchanged. The only state maintained across the stream is a K×K co-occurrence matrix and a K-dimensional marginal vector, both updated in closed form from past zero-shot posteriors, keeping memory and compute overhead negligible relative to the single CLIP forward pass.

## Method

BCP interprets CLIP zero-shot logits as an implicit product of visual likelihood and marginal class prior, then attributes distribution-shift error to a mismatched prior. For each test image, it selects the highest-confidence softmax label as an anchor and applies a closed-form logit correction s*_k = s_k + log P(c_k=1|c_a=1) − log P(c_k=1), which is exactly the pointwise mutual information between the target label and the anchor. A key conditional-independence assumption (P(x|c_k=1, c_a=1) ≈ P(x|c_k=1)) ensures all label dependency is captured by the prior ratio while the frozen visual likelihood remains untouched. Conditional and marginal priors are estimated online from the unlabeled test stream via running first- and second-order statistics of past zero-shot posteriors, updated in closed form with constant memory. The entire adaptation is gradient-free, requires no backpropagation or external knowledge, and amounts to roughly ten lines of code, adding negligible latency beyond the single forward pass.

## Results

BCP achieves an average mAP of 69.22 with RN50 and 71.79 with ViT-B/16 across five multi-label benchmarks (COCO2014, COCO2017, VOC2007, VOC2012, NUSWIDE), improving over frozen CLIP by +11.91 and +9.18 points respectively. The method is fully backpropagation-free, requiring only a single forward pass plus a closed-form logit correction, and runs at 0.112 s per sample—nearly 2× faster than the strongest multi-label TTA baseline BEM (0.24 s). Gains are largest on NUSWIDE (e.g., +8.81 mAP over CLIP with RN50) and on COCO2014 (e.g., +14.16 mAP over CLIP with RN50), while VOC datasets already start from high CLIP baselines (~75 mAP) and see more modest absolute gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BCP outperforms every baseline on all five datasets for both RN50 and ViT-B/16, with the largest margins over single-label TTA methods adapted to multi-label (TPT, DiffTPT, RLCF) that often degrade performance (e.g., RLCF drops to 46.78 avg mAP on RN50 vs. CLIP's 57.31). Against the strongest multi-label TTA baseline BEM, BCP gains +9.07 mAP (RN50: 69.22 vs. 60.15) and +6.99 mAP (ViT-B/16: 71.79 vs. 64.80) while being 2× faster (0.112 s vs. 0.24 s per sample) and requiring no backpropagation. The closest competitor is SCA (69.24 avg on ViT-B/16), which BCP surpasses by +2.55 mAP; DOTA (64.17 RN50, 63.95 ViT-B/16) is competitive on COCO/VOC but collapses on NUSWIDE (38.88/32.74) where BCP reaches 50.34/52.78. Among zero-shot multi-label methods that require fine-tuning or external knowledge, BCP is slightly below CoMC on VOC2007 (86.93 vs. 89.40) and SPARC (88.41 vs. 88.70) but clearly surpasses both on NUSWIDE (50.34 vs. 48.20; 52.78 vs. 47.30) while remaining fine-tuning-free, external-knowledge-free, and compatible with strict online batch-size-1 deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant TTA paradigm for VLMs, which typically adapts model parameters or prompts via iterative backpropagation (TPT, DiffTPT, BEM), by reframing the problem as a prior-correction task in logit space. Conceptually, it repurposes Bayesian conditional inference and pointwise mutual information—tools well-established in NLP and information theory—and applies them to the underexplored setting of multi-label TTA with frozen VLMs, where label co-occurrence structure is ignored by both single-label TTA methods and the only prior multi-label TTA approach (BEM). The closest conceptual ancestor is BayesianTTA (Zhou et al., 2025), which also injects prior information at test time, but BCP is specifically multi-label, gradient-free, and requires no external retrieval or caption prefetching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BCP offers a practically compelling recipe: a ~10-line, gradient-free logit correction that outperforms backpropagation-based multi-label TTA by ~10 mAP while running 2× faster, making it attractive for latency-sensitive deployment. The clean PMI interpretation provides a transparent, information-theoretic account of why the correction works, which could inform future designs for label-dependent prediction. Open questions include whether the pairwise (single-anchor) structure generalizes to higher-order dependencies, how the method behaves under severe concept drift or miscalibrated CLIP posteriors, and whether the approach extends to open-vocabulary multi-label detection or segmentation where the label set is not fixed.
