## Problem

The Gaussian mechanism is the standard additive-noise primitive for differentially private vector queries, but two fundamental questions remained open: whether it is asymptotically optimal among all additive-noise mechanisms in high dimensions, and whether any mechanism can outperform both the Gaussian and the recently proposed ℓ₂ mechanism in low dimensions. No prior work established optimality over the full class of additive noise distributions (only restricted subclasses), and no mechanism was known to simultaneously beat both baselines.

## Input / Output

The mechanism takes a T-dimensional real-valued query result, a target privacy pair (ε, δ), and an ℓ₂ sensitivity bound s, and outputs a noisy T-dimensional vector Q + X where X follows a Spherical Generalized Gamma distribution. The three SGG parameters (α, β, p) are calibrated via a nested optimization: Bayesian optimization over (α, p) combined with bisection over β to minimize MSE subject to the (ε, δ) constraint. The privacy analysis itself reduces to a one-dimensional integral over the radial component R, making calibration tractable even for moderate T, and an η-tight oracle provides provable (not just empirical) DP guarantees.

## Method

The paper makes two main contributions. First, it proves asymptotic Gaussian optimality by (i) showing via Haar symmetrization that spherical symmetric noise is without loss of generality, (ii) constructing an affine test set that lower-bounds the optimal δ of any spherical mechanism by E[g(R²/T)] in the T→∞ limit, and (iii) establishing a tangent-support (Jensen-type) condition on the Gaussian privacy function g that holds in the high-privacy regime δ ≤ δ⋆, yielding E[g(R²/T)] ≥ g(E[R²/T]) = δ_G. Second, it introduces the Spherical Generalized Gamma (SGG) family, a three-parameter spherical symmetric distribution (shape α, rate β, tail p) that subsumes both the Gaussian (p=2, α=T−1) and ℓ₂-Laplace (p=1, α=T−1) mechanisms. The privacy loss random variable for SGG noise decomposes into a function of the radial component R and the directional cosine W = cos Θ, reducing the optimal δ computation to a single smooth integral over r ∈ (0,∞). A numerical optimizer searches over (α, p, β) to find configurations with up to 15% lower MSE than both baselines in low dimensions, and tight composition is obtained by FFT-based convolution of single-step PRV distributions.

## Results

The paper proves that as dimension T→∞, the Gaussian mechanism is asymptotically optimal among all additive-noise DP mechanisms for strong privacy (δ ≤ δ*), with numerically verified lower bounds on δ* ranging from 0.198 (ε=16) to 0.737 (ε=0.25) at sensitivity s=1. In low dimensions, the proposed Spherical Generalized Gamma (SGG) mechanism achieves up to 15% lower MSE than both the Gaussian and ℓ₂ mechanisms at ε=0.1, s=1, with improvements concentrated at small T and shrinking as T grows. The SGG family also admits tight (ε,δ)-DP composition accounting, resolving an open question about the ℓ₂ mechanism.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the SGG mechanism against two baselines: the standard Gaussian mechanism and the ℓ₂ mechanism (Joseph et al., ICML 2025). Against the Gaussian mechanism, SGG achieves up to 15% lower MSE in selected low-dimensional regimes (e.g., small T with ε=0.1, s=1), but the Gaussian is proven asymptotically optimal as T→∞, so the advantage vanishes in high dimensions. Against the ℓ₂ mechanism, SGG similarly achieves up to 15% lower MSE in low dimensions, with the optimal SGG shape parameter p* being far from both 1 (ℓ₂) and 2 (Gaussian), confirming a genuinely different noise geometry. The SGG framework also subsumes both baselines as special cases (p=2, α=T−1 for Gaussian; p=1, α=T−1 for ℓ₂) and provides the first tight composition accountant for the ℓ₂ mechanism, answering an open question from Joseph et al. (2025). The authors explicitly note these low-dimensional gains are an existence result rather than a uniform dominance claim.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on additive noise mechanisms for (ε,δ)-differential privacy by proving that the Gaussian mechanism is asymptotically optimal among *all* additive noise distributions in high dimensions, going beyond the restricted class studied by Dong et al. (2021) and the Gaussian DP framework. It introduces the Spherical Generalized Gamma (SGG) family as a three-parameter superset that contains both the Gaussian mechanism (p=2, α=T−1) and the ℓ₂ mechanism (p=1, α=T−1) from Joseph et al. (ICML 2025), building on the radial-to-spherical density transformation of Fang (2018) and the hockey-stick divergence framework of Balle et al. (2018). The paper also departs from prior work by correcting a proof bug in the R1SMG mechanism of Ji & Li (2024) and by answering an open question from Joseph et al. regarding tight composition of the ℓ₂ mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The asymptotic optimality theorem provides a rigorous justification for the Gaussian mechanism as the default choice in high-dimensional DP applications such as DP-SGD, where T (number of model parameters) is very large. The SGG family offers a principled design space for low-dimensional mechanisms with up to 15% MSE reduction, though the authors carefully scope this as an existence result rather than a uniform recommendation. Key open questions include: finite-dimension convergence rates (the proof uses an o(1) normal approximation gap), whether low-dimensional SGG gains translate to end-to-end improvements in composite algorithms like DP-SGD, and extension beyond additive noise to data-dependent or non-additive mechanisms. The tight composition accounting via FFT convolution is practically useful for multi-call DP pipelines.
