## Problem

Adaptive reasoning models that switch between Think and Nothink modes are vulnerable to superficial linguistic complexity: verbose phrasing of simple problems misleads them into unnecessarily activating expensive reasoning. This paper identifies this vulnerability as a critical, underexplored limitation and asks how to train a model to base its mode selection on intrinsic problem difficulty rather than surface-level phrasing cues.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263864/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a text query (typically a math word problem) and autonomously selects its output format in the first generated tokens: either a direct answer prefixed with empty <nothink></nothink> markers (Nothink mode) or a step-by-step reasoning chain enclosed in <think>... followed by the answer (Think mode). Robustness is probed by presenting the same simple problems in adversarially generated redundant (verbose) and concise (minimal) surface forms, testing whether the model's mode choice remains stable across phrasing variations.

## Method

AdaReasoningSwitch (AdaRS) is trained in two stages on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B. Stage 1 (Cold Start with SFT) fine-tunes the model on a composite dataset where every simple problem appears in three surface forms—original, redundant, and concise—all paired with Nothink-mode answers, explicitly teaching that phrasing length is orthogonal to difficulty. Stage 2 (Enhancing Adaptive Policy) applies GRPO with an asymmetric reward: a correct Nothink answer earns +1, a correct Think answer earns a strictly lower combined reward (R_correct + R_mode < 1), and an incorrect answer earns −1, creating a hard incentive to prefer the efficient path unless reasoning is genuinely necessary. Complexity labels are assigned empirically by checking whether the base model exceeds 80% accuracy over 10 forced-Nothink trials. The key differentiator from prior adaptive reasoning work is the combination of adversarial phrasing augmentation in SFT and cost-aware reward shaping in RL, which together make mode selection robust to superficial linguistic complexity.

## Results

On the main mathematical reasoning benchmarks, AdaRS achieves the highest average accuracy (65.6%) and lowest average token consumption (3,250) among all efficient reasoning models, outperforming its base model DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B in Think mode (62.4% accuracy, 7,413 tokens) by +3.2% accuracy while reducing token usage by 56.1%. On the hardest benchmark (AIME 2024), AdaRS reaches 28.9% accuracy with 6,954 tokens, matching O1-Pruner's accuracy while using 33% fewer tokens. On the MMLU generalization benchmark, AdaRS achieves 66.0% accuracy with only 45.3% Think-mode activation, the best accuracy-to-efficiency ratio among all adaptive models tested.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two adaptive reasoning baselines built on the same backbone, AdaRS wins on both accuracy and efficiency: it outperforms Thinkless (62.7% avg acc, 3,471 tokens) by +2.9% accuracy and 6.3% fewer tokens, and beats AdaptThink (64.4%, 3,933 tokens) by +1.2% accuracy and 17.1% fewer tokens. On the hardest benchmark AIME 2024, AdaRS (28.9%) substantially outperforms Thinkless (23.0%) and AdaptThink (27.0%), while matching O1-Pruner (28.9%) at 33% lower token cost. Among chain-shortening methods (OverThink, O1-Pruner, CoT-Valve), AdaRS achieves the best average accuracy (65.6% vs. 62.3%, 61.9%, 59.7%) with the lowest token usage (3,250 vs. 5,369, 4,677, 7,807). On the robustness evaluation with redundant phrasing, AdaRS shows the smallest increase in Think-mode activation (35.9% on GSM8K-redundancy, 33.1% on ASDIV-redundancy) compared to Thinkless (42.3%, 40.7%) and AdaptThink (65.4%, 58.5%), confirming superior resistance to superficial linguistic complexity. On MMLU, AdaRS (66.0% acc, 1,172 tokens, 45.3% Think%) outperforms Thinkless (65.1%, 1,310, 56.4%) and AdaptThink (46.9%, 998, 77.9%), while the non-adaptive Qwen3-4B Think mode achieves higher raw accuracy (86.9%) but at 2.8× the token cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the adaptive reasoning paradigm introduced by Thinkless and AdaptThink, which equip a single model with Think and Nothink modes for dynamic switching. It departs from these prior works by identifying and addressing a specific vulnerability: existing adaptive models conflate superficial linguistic verbosity with true problem difficulty, causing unnecessary activation of the costly Think mode. The RL stage leverages GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) from the DeepSeekMath lineage, but introduces a custom asymmetric reward that penalizes Think-mode usage on simple problems, while the SFT stage adds adversarial data augmentation (concise/redundant query pairs) not present in prior adaptive reasoning training recipes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The overthinking problem is a major practical bottleneck for deploying LRMs at scale, and the finding that even adaptive models are systematically misled by redundant phrasing (e.g., AdaptThink's Think% jumping from 21.9% to 58.5% on ASDIV-redundancy) reveals a previously unexamined failure mode. However, the solution is partial: AdaRS still shows residual sensitivity (Think% rising from 10.9% to 35.9% on GSM8K-redundancy), the evaluation is confined to a 1.5B model on mathematical tasks, and the binary Think/Nothink framing ignores the spectrum of reasoning depth. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to larger models, non-math domains, and more diverse real-world redundancy patterns, and whether multi-level reasoning granularity would yield better efficiency-accuracy trade-offs.
