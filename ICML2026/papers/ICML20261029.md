## Problem

Graph foundation models face fundamental challenges: graphs span vastly different domains with heterogeneous structures and attributes, and available graph data is far more limited than in NLP or CV. Prior attempts to adapt tabular foundation models to graphs (G2T-FM, TAG) rely on hand-crafted features like Laplacian positional encodings and were never pretrained on graph data, limiting their ability to capture complex graph patterns. No PFN-based model had been designed and pretrained specifically for graph node-level tasks.

## Input / Output

The model consumes an attributed graph (adjacency matrix + per-node feature vectors), a subset of labeled context nodes, and unlabeled query nodes, with 8 random features appended to the original features at each forward pass. It uses a multi-token representation where each feature of each node forms a separate token, natively handling variable numbers of heterogeneous features without retraining. Output is a per-node prediction: class probabilities for classification (up to 10 classes natively, more via ECOC) or continuous values for regression, produced in a single forward pass (ICL) or after finetuning.

## Method

GraphPFN extends the LimiX tabular foundation model by inserting a sparse multi-head attention adapter (masked by the graph adjacency) at the end of every transformer block, adding a local graph-structure-aware message-passing channel alongside LimiX's global PFN-style sample-level attention. For pretraining, the authors design a novel graph prior that combines multiple degree-corrected stochastic block models at two levels (to produce overlapping, complex community structures) with a preferential attachment process (to introduce core-periphery structure), and generates node attributes via a structured causal model that mixes MLP and GNN neurons at each hidden layer. Training freezes the LimiX backbone and updates only the graph adapters over 1.6M synthetic graphs for 10K steps, using an EMA on adapter weights and an auxiliary masked graph modeling loss (weight 0.1) to encourage graph-aware representations. This design lets the model inherit LimiX's strong heterogeneous feature modeling while learning graph-specific patterns at a fraction of the cost of training from scratch.

## Results

On the 8-dataset GraphLand benchmark, GraphPFN achieves the best average rank in both regimes: 2.67 (classification) / 3.50 (all tasks) in ICL and a perfect 1.00 / 1.00 after finetuning. In the ICL setting, GraphPFN outperforms the strongest PFN-based GFM (G2T-LimiX, AR 3.67/3.88) and all classic GNNs (best: GT at AR 8.67/7.25). After finetuning, GraphPFN attains the top score on 11 of 13 datasets across both benchmarks, with gains of 1–4 percentage points over the second-best method on datasets such as artnet-exp (53.49 vs 50.39), hm-prices (81.06 vs 77.37), and twitch-views (79.00 vs 74.91).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GraphPFN (ICL) outperforms all classic GNNs (GCN, GraphSAGE, GAT, GT) on average, with the best GNN (GT) trailing by roughly 6 average-rank positions on classification tasks. Among prior non-PFN GFMs (OpenGraph, AnyGraph, TS-GNN, SAMGPT, MDGFM, GCOPE), GraphPFN leads by a wide margin—e.g., 51.79 vs 20.44 on artnet-exp—confirming that PFN-based approaches are substantially stronger for node-level tasks. Against the closest PFN-based competitors, GraphPFN (ICL) beats G2T-LimiX (ICL) on 6 of 8 GraphLand datasets (e.g., 51.79 vs 48.42 on artnet-exp, 80.25 vs 78.98 on city-reviews) and TAG-LimiX (ICL) on all reported datasets. After finetuning, GraphPFN (FT) dominates G2T-LimiX (FT) on every GraphLand dataset, with the largest gaps on hm-prices (+3.69), twitch-views (+4.09), and artnet-views (+2.11). On the classic benchmark (Table 2), GraphPFN (FT) achieves AR 1.40 vs G2T-LimiX (FT) at 2.00, winning on 4 of 5 datasets including facebook (94.06 vs 92.83) and wiki-cs (83.99 vs 83.38), while G2T-LimiX retains a narrow edge on pubmed (90.94 vs 90.50) and questions (22.98 vs 22.15). In the appendix comparison, GraphPFN also strongly outperforms NodePFN (Choi et al., ICLR 2026) across all 8 shared datasets in the ICL setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

GraphPFN extends the prior-data fitted network (PFN) framework from Müller et al. (2022) and its tabular instantiations (TabPFN, TabPFNv2, LimiX) to the graph domain. It departs from the G2T-FM and TAG line of work, which converted graph node-level tasks into tabular problems via hand-crafted features (Laplacian positional encodings, neighborhood aggregation) and then applied an off-the-shelf tabular PFN. Instead, GraphPFN natively incorporates graph structure by adding attention-based message-passing adapters to each transformer block of LimiX, and it is pretrained on 1.6M synthetic graphs generated from a novel multi-level stochastic block model combined with preferential attachment for structure and graph-aware structured causal models for attributes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GraphPFN demonstrates that the PFN paradigm—pretraining on synthetic data from a designed prior to enable in-context learning—transfers successfully from tabular to graph data, offering a principled path toward truly generalizable graph foundation models that do not depend on hand-crafted graph features. Key limitations remain: the model cannot scale to very large graphs (requires processing the entire graph at once), is restricted to node-level tasks with at most 10 classes natively, and the prior is tuned for social/information networks rather than, e.g., traffic or molecular graphs. Open questions include whether the approach extends to link prediction and graph-level tasks, how to incorporate more diverse random graph models into the prior, and whether the pretraining variability (0.3–0.7 pp across seeds) can be reduced.
