## Problem

Existing manifold-constrained hyper-connections (mHC) face a fundamental trade-off: the Sinkhorn-Knopp projection does not guarantee exact doubly stochastic residual matrices, while mHC-lite achieves exactness via the Birkhoff-von-Neumann theorem but incurs factorial parameter complexity O(nC·n!). The paper asks whether exact double stochasticity can be guaranteed without factorial parameter explosion as the residual stream width n grows.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266167/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on text tokens during LLM pretraining, where each layer's input is an expanded residual stream X_l ∈ R^{n×C} (n streams of dimension C). The residual stream is tensorized into an order-(K+1) tensor with n = ∏i_k, and residual mixing is applied along each of the first K modes via small doubly stochastic factor matrices. The output is the standard next-token prediction, with the key constraint that the residual mixing matrix H_l^res is always exactly doubly stochastic by construction rather than by iterative approximation.

## Method

KromHC parametrizes the residual matrix H_l^res as a Kronecker product of K smaller doubly stochastic matrices U_l^k ∈ R^{i_k×i_k}, each learned as a convex combination of i_k! permutation matrices via the Birkhoff-von-Neumann theorem. This is framed as a Tucker decomposition tensor network where the tensorized residual stream serves as the core tensor and the factor matrices are the small doubly stochastic matrices. The key theoretical guarantee (Theorem 4.2) is that the Kronecker product of doubly stochastic matrices is itself doubly stochastic, so H_l^res is exactly doubly stochastic by construction. By choosing small prime factors (e.g., i_k = 2 for n = 2^K), only 2 permutation matrices and 2 scalar coefficients per factor are needed, reducing parameter complexity from O(n³C) (mHC) or O(nC·n!) (mHC-lite) to O(n²C). The H_pre and H_post mappings remain unchanged from mHC, and the entire method is implementable in native PyTorch without specialized kernels.

## Results

On LLM pretraining with n=4 residual streams, KromHC achieves the highest CORE score at both scales: 9.018 (D=6) and 16.872 (D=12), outperforming mHC (7.971 / 16.023) and mHC-lite (8.208 / 13.217). KromHC requires only 240K (D=6) and 959K (D=12) additional parameters, roughly half of mHC (462K / 1844K) and less than half of mHC-lite (609K / 2433K), while matching their training loss (3.488 vs 3.493/3.484 at D=6; 2.966 vs 2.964/2.972 at D=12) and validation BPB (1.047 / 0.862). On commonsense reasoning, KromHC achieves the best average accuracy at both depths (41.1% at D=6, 47.7% at D=12), and on language modeling it also leads (17.3% at D=6, 24.0% at D=12).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard residual connections, KromHC improves the CORE score by +2.54 (D=6) and +2.10 (D=12) while adding only 240K/959K parameters. Against mHC, KromHC wins on CORE score by +1.05 (D=6) and +0.85 (D=12) with roughly half the additional parameters (240K vs 462K; 959K vs 1844K), and achieves a slightly higher training loss (3.488 vs 3.493 at D=6) but comparable validation BPB. Against mHC-lite, KromHC dominates on both CORE score (+0.81 at D=6, +3.66 at D=12) and parameter count (240K vs 609K; 959K vs 2433K), while mHC-lite has a marginally lower training loss at D=6 (3.484 vs 3.488). On commonsense reasoning (Table 4), KromHC achieves the best average at both depths (41.1% D=6, 47.7% D=12), with particularly large gains on BoolQ (+6.4% over mHC at D=6) and COPA (+2.0% over mHC at D=12). On language modeling (Table 5), KromHC leads at both scales (17.3% D=6, 24.0% D=12), outperforming mHC by +1.9 and +1.1 points respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

KromHC sits at the intersection of two research threads: the Hyper-Connections (HC) line of work that expands residual stream width for richer feature propagation (Zhu et al., 2025), and the manifold-constrained variants (mHC, mHC-lite) that project residual matrices onto the Birkhoff polytope to restore identity-mapping properties. The key departure is the use of Kronecker products of small doubly stochastic factor matrices, framed as a Tucker decomposition tensor network, to simultaneously guarantee exact double stochasticity (via the Kronecker closure property) and reduce parameter complexity from O(n³C) or O(nC·n!) to O(n²C). This tensor-network perspective on residual mixing is the paper's central conceptual contribution, linking the mHC family to structured low-rank representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

KromHC resolves a concrete and previously unresolved tension in the mHC family: exact double stochasticity (needed for training stability) was incompatible with parameter efficiency as residual stream width n grows. The Kronecker-structured parametrization is a clean, PyTorch-native solution that avoids both the iterative Sinkhorn-Knopp approximation and the factorial explosion of permutation-matrix convex combinations. However, the experiments are conducted at relatively small scale (~60M and ~186M parameters), and the method's applicability is constrained when n is a large prime number. Open questions include whether the Kronecker structure remains beneficial at much larger scales (billions of parameters), whether non-power-of-2 factorizations degrade performance, and whether the approach transfers to other modalities such as vision transformers.
