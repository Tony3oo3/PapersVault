## Problem

Contextual stochastic optimization (CSO) methods assume fully observed covariates, yet in practice covariate data is frequently partially missing due to heterogeneous data sources or costly collection. Standard two-stage pipelines (impute-then-optimize, complete-case deletion) are suboptimal because the preprocessing step is not calibrated to the downstream optimization objective, and existing robust CSO methods do not characterize the missingness mechanism. This paper closes the gap by integrating missingness-aware estimation directly into the optimization step.

## Input / Output

The method consumes n historical observations of partially observed covariates (x̃_i, y_i, m_i) where m_i is a binary missingness mask, a target covariate x̄ at decision time, and a cost function c(z,y) defined over a compact feasible set Z. It produces a robust decision ẑ_n(x̄) minimizing the worst-case conditional expected cost over a latent ambiguity set, along with a finite-sample upper confidence bound on the true conditional cost. The output is obtained by solving a convex program whose size scales with the latent support |X|×|Y| rather than the raw sample size n, and a separation oracle can further reduce the effective constraint count.

## Method

The framework defines a latent ambiguity set P_n(ε) of joint distributions P_XY for which some MAR missingness kernel q induces an observed distribution within KL divergence ε of the empirical law, thereby encoding the missingness structure without imputing a complete dataset. For discrete covariates and outcomes, a change-of-variables (v = p/(dᵀp), t = 1/(dᵀp), u = Tp/(dᵀp)) exposes a hidden convex perspective structure in the inner maximization, and taking the dual yields a tractable convex reformulation solvable by off-the-shelf solvers. For continuous outcomes, the paper introduces a binned LP-projected KL (BinPKL) divergence with a sieve refinement, derives sandwiching bounds that reduce the problem to a finite-atom worst-case distribution, and obtains a convex dual representation. Finite-sample out-of-sample disappointment bounds are established via KL concentration (discrete) and RKL concentration inequalities (continuous), and asymptotic consistency of both the optimal value and optimizer is proven under a summable confidence sequence.

## Results

On a contextual newsvendor problem with 80% missing covariates (α=0.8), MAR-KL DRO achieves the lowest out-of-sample cost and highest reliability across nearly the entire data-size range (n = 100–10,000), with imputation-based baselines approaching its performance only at large n. Computationally, MAR-KL DRO's solution time remains nearly stable (2.67 s to 3.09 s) as n grows from 100 to 10,000, while Impute+W1-DRO scales from 0.06 s to 7.19 s. With a restricted-master separation scheme, the method solves instances with latent support size up to 10⁶ in 151 s, versus >300 s (timeout) without separation at 10⁵.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares MAR-KL DRO against two impute-then-optimize pipelines built on existing DRO formulations: Impute+KL-DRO (using the KL-divergence DRO of Ben-Tal et al., 2013) and Impute+W1-DRO (using the Wasserstein-1 DRO of Mohajerin Esfahani & Kuhn, 2018), as well as simpler baselines (CCA, CFA, Impute+SAA). MAR-KL DRO consistently outperforms both DRO-based imputation pipelines in out-of-sample cost and reliability across all tested data sizes (n = 100–10,000) under 80% missingness; the gap narrows only at the largest n where imputation becomes more accurate. In terms of computational scaling, MAR-KL DRO is substantially more stable: its runtime grows by only ~15% from n=100 to n=10,000, whereas Impute+W1-DRO grows by over 100× (0.06 s → 7.19 s) and Impute+KL-DRO by ~50× (0.05 s → 2.70 s). CCA and CFA remain unreliable and substantially suboptimal across all settings, confirming that deletion-based strategies fail under MAR missingness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the KL-divergence distributionally robust optimization framework (Ben-Tal et al., 2013) and the robust KL divergence for continuous distributions (Yang & Chen, 2018) to the setting of partially missing covariates in contextual stochastic optimization. It departs from the standard two-stage impute-then-optimize paradigm by embedding the missingness mechanism (under MAR) directly into the ambiguity set construction, so that candidate latent distributions must be consistent with the observed partial data. The paper also distinguishes itself from covariate-shift and adversarial-perturbation DRO approaches (Bennouna & Van Parys, 2022; Wang et al., 2024), which assume full covariate access and do not model the missingness structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic treatment of missing covariates in contextual stochastic optimization with finite-sample out-of-sample guarantees and asymptotic optimality, filling a gap the authors identify as absent from the CSO literature. The tractable convex reformulations (via perspective functions and separation oracles) make the approach practically implementable, and the empirical results show consistent superiority over imputation-based pipelines across missingness levels. Key limitations include restriction to the MAR mechanism (MNAR is explicitly out of scope), the main-text focus on discrete covariates, and a computational bottleneck that scales with the latent support size |X|×|Y| rather than sample size. Open questions include extending to MNAR with auxiliary structure, handling high-dimensional continuous covariates, and whether the framework can be adapted to online or sequential decision settings.
