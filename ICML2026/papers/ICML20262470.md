## Problem

Prior causal effect identifiability results in the presence of latent confounders all rely on auxiliary variables (mediators, instruments, or proxies) subject to stringent structural constraints, leaving a fundamental gap: can the causal effect be identified from the joint distribution of the treatment-outcome pair alone? This paper provides a complete, mutually exclusive and collectively exhaustive characterization of when the causal effect is identifiable or unidentifiable in linear SCMs with latent confounders and no auxiliary variables, grounded solely in the distributional properties of exogenous noises.

## Input / Output

The method requires only the joint distribution of the treatment T and outcome O (or i.i.d. samples thereof) as its sole statistical input — no auxiliary variables, no multi-environment data, and no prior knowledge of the number of latent confounders n. It produces three types of output: (1) an identifiability verdict, (2) the unique causal effect value α when identifiable, and (3) the exact cardinality of the feasible solution set when unidentifiable (either a finite set of size 1 + number of non-Gaussian latent confounder noises, or an infinite set). The characterization is expressed in terms of Gaussianity and Gaussian-separability of exogenous noises, which are properties of the underlying generative model rather than directly observable from data.

## Method

The paper introduces the concept of Gaussian-separability (a distribution that can be decomposed into a non-Gaussian-separable component plus a Gaussian component, with uniqueness guaranteed by Cramér's decomposition theorem) and uses it to partition the space of exogenous noise distributions into four mutually exclusive conditions. For the two identifiable conditions (ε_T non-Gaussian with all latent confounder noises Gaussian, or ε_T Gaussian with all latent confounder noises non-Gaussian-separable), the causal effect α is recovered via cumulant ratios (e.g., α = cum_{k-1,1}(T,O)/cum_{k,0}(T,O)) or characteristic function factorization that isolates the Gaussian component. For the two unidentifiable conditions, the paper constructs explicit alternative SCMs with different α values that produce identical (T,O) distributions, and quantifies the feasible solution set as either a finite set {α} ∪ {α+β_i : ε_{L_i} non-Gaussian} or an infinite set arising from the non-unique decomposition of the unified Gaussian entity. The key mechanism is that identifiability holds precisely when ε_T exhibits a sharp distributional contrast with the latent confounder noises in Gaussianity, leaving a distinguishable footprint in the joint (T,O) distribution.

## Results

The paper provides a complete, mutually exclusive and collectively exhaustive characterization of causal effect identifiability in linear SCMs with latent confounders, grounded solely in the joint distribution of (T, O). Under Conditions 1 and 2 (identifiable cases), empirical validation on synthetic data (M1–M6) shows estimation errors decrease monotonically with sample size from 0.5k to 500k; at 500k samples, errors for M1–M3 (Condition 1, cumulant-based estimation) nearly vanish, while errors for M4–M6 (Condition 2, Butucea–Matias semiparametric convolution) remain non-negligible due to the algorithm's logarithmic convergence rate. Under Condition 3 (unidentifiable), the feasible solution set has exactly 1 + m elements where m is the number of non-Gaussian exogenous noises among the latent confounders; under Condition 4, the feasible set is infinite. The paper tightens the upper bound of 'at most n+1' in Tramontano et al. (2025) to precisely n+1 for the lvLiNGAM special case.

**Summary of identifiability conditions (mutually exclusive and collectively exhaustive).**

| Condition | ε_T | ε_{L_i} (all i) | Identifiable? | Feasible set \|S\| |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Non-Gaussian | All Gaussian | Yes | 1 |
| 2 | Gaussian | None Gaussian-separable | Yes | 1 |
| 3 | Non-Gaussian | At least one non-Gaussian | No | 1 + m (m = # non-Gaussian ε_{L_i}) |
| 4 | Gaussian | At least one Gaussian-separable | No | ∞ |

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper includes no baseline comparisons, explicitly noting that 'existing estimation methods typically require auxiliary variables' and thus cannot be applied to the no-auxiliary-variable setting studied here. The closest prior result is Theorem 3.1 in Tramontano et al. (2025), which for the lvLiNGAM case (all noises non-Gaussian) shows the causal effect has at most n+1 possible values; this paper's Theorem 3 tightens that bound to exactly n+1. Sanjaroonpouri & Ramazi (2025) establish identifiability under n ≤ 2 with Gaussian ε_O, which is a special case subsumed by the paper's Condition 2 (as noted in the Remark following Theorem 2), but they do not provide a corresponding estimation method. The empirical validation therefore stands alone, confirming that the proposed cumulant-based estimator (Condition 1) and the Butucea–Matias semiparametric convolution estimator (Condition 2) both recover α consistently, with the former converging substantially faster in practice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of causal effect identification work (Pearl 1995, Angrist et al. 1996, Kuroki & Pearl 2014) that requires auxiliary variables—mediators, instruments, or proxies—subject to stringent structural constraints, and also from multi-environment approaches (Rothenhäusler et al. 2019, Kivva et al. 2025) that trade auxiliary variables for distributional heterogeneity. Instead, it grounds identifiability solely in the joint distribution of the treatment-outcome pair from a single environment, using Cramér's decomposition theorem and a novel 'Gaussian-separable' concept to characterize when the causal effect is recoverable. For the unidentifiable case, it tightens the upper bound of n+1 feasible solutions from Tramontano et al. (2025) in the lvLiNGAM setting to an exact count, and for estimation in the Gaussian-noise case it repurposes the Butucea & Matias (2005) semiparametric deconvolution algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a genuine gap by providing a complete (mutually exclusive and collectively exhaustive) characterization of identifiability without auxiliary variables, establishing a fundamental theoretical baseline that dictates exactly when such variables are necessary versus redundant. However, the results are confined to linear SCMs, the nonlinear extension is only preliminary, and the practical utility is tempered by the fact that determining which of the four conditions holds from data alone is statistically challenging—domain knowledge is typically required. The logarithmic convergence rate of the estimation procedure in the Gaussian-noise case (Condition 2) also limits practical precision at feasible sample sizes.
