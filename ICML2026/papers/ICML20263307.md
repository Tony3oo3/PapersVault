## Problem

Conformal prediction sets, particularly those using localized calibration, exhibit high variability in size when only a small calibration set is available, yet existing evaluation metrics (prediction efficiency, test-conditional coverage) are defined in expectation over the calibration data and thus fail to capture this practical one-shot instability. The paper formalizes this concern as 'set stability'—the variance of the conditional expected set size given the calibration data—and asks how to reduce it without additional target-task labels.

## Input / Output

The method consumes three data streams: a small labeled target calibration set L_n (n pairs), a larger unlabeled target covariate set U_m (m samples, typically m ≫ n), and an abundant labeled source set L'_N (N pairs from a related task with similar conditional distribution P_{Y|X}). It also requires a pretrained non-conformity score S(x,y) and a conditional CDF learner. The output is a single conformal quantile q_St and the corresponding set-valued prediction C_St(X_{n+1}) = {y : S(X_{n+1}, y) ≤ q_St}, which carries a finite-sample marginal coverage guarantee of at least 1−α.

## Method

StCP stabilizes the conformal quantile by transductively combining source and target information. First, an initial conditional score CDF F̂_{S|X} is trained on the abundant source data. Second, a transductive marginal CDF estimate F̂^1_S(s; F̃) = (1/m)Σ_j F̃(s|X̃_j) is computed by averaging the conditional CDF over the unlabeled target covariates. Third, F̂_{S|X} is calibrated via a regularized optimization that minimizes the squared Wasserstein-2 distance between F̂^1_S(·; F̃) and the empirical target CDF F̂^0_S, plus an L2 penalty pulling F̃ toward the source-trained F̂_{S|X}; the tuning parameter λ interpolates between pure target calibration (λ=0, recovering the standard conformal quantile) and full source trust (λ→∞). The final quantile is the 1−α_n quantile of the aligned estimator, and a data-driven rule selects the largest feasible λ whose quantile stays within a user-specified tolerance band around the nominal level, preserving a finite-sample marginal guarantee under exchangeability. Theoretically, the set stability improves from O(n^{-1}) for standard conformal prediction to O(m^{-1} + {n(1+λ)^2}^{-1}), and the framework extends directly to localized scores (GLCP, CQR) to yield Stable Localized Conformal Prediction.

## Results

On five real-world datasets (CRIME, BIO, STAR, DERMA, TISSUE) with n=30 calibration samples, StCP ('ours') reduces the standard deviation of prediction set size by 13.5%–48.4% under GLCP-type localization and 6.3%–29.3% under CQR-type, compared to the base method, while maintaining marginal coverage within the valid range. For example, on STAR (GLCP), Std drops from 8.78 (base) to 5.25 (ours, 48.4% reduction) and to 1.48 (oracle); on CRIME (GLCP), Std drops from 0.75 to 0.50 (42.9% reduction). The data-driven variant 'ours-sel' preserves most of the stability gain (e.g., 27.7% on CRIME GLCP, 42.9% on STAR GLCP) while enforcing a conservative λ selection. The direct plug-in (DP) baseline achieves the lowest Std but severely violates marginal coverage (e.g., 0.956+ on BIO CQR, 0.998+ on TISSUE GLCP).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the base GLCP/CQR methods, StCP ('ours') consistently reduces the Std metric (set-size variability) across all five datasets while keeping marginal coverage within the valid [1−α, 1−α+1/(n+1)] range; the gain is largest under GLCP localization (up to 48.4% on STAR) and more modest under CQR (up to 29.3% on BIO). Compared to SDCP (Wen et al., 2025), StCP achieves lower Std in every setting, and SDCP itself sometimes violates marginal coverage (e.g., 0.935+ on CRIME GLCP, 0.867− on STAR GLCP at n=100 in Table 2). Against PPI (Angelopoulos et al., 2023), StCP again wins on Std in all cases, while PPI occasionally shows marginal coverage issues (e.g., 0.937+ on DERMA GLCP). The direct plug-in (DP) baseline achieves the lowest Std among non-oracle methods but severely inflates marginal coverage (e.g., 0.998+ on TISSUE GLCP, 0.959+ on BIO CQR), rendering it invalid. Oracle (which uses true labels for unlabeled target data) remains the strongest reference, and the residual gap between 'ours' and oracle quantifies the room for improvement under imperfect transfer; 'ours-sel' (data-driven λ) sacrifices a small fraction of the stability gain (e.g., 42.9%→27.7% on CRIME GLCP) in exchange for a principled, tolerance-based parameter choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the standard conformal prediction framework (Vovk et al., 2005) and the localized GLCP construction (Min et al., 2026) by introducing a transductive calibration step that borrows information from labeled source data and unlabeled target covariates to stabilize the conformal quantile. It departs from prior semi-supervised conformal prediction work (Wen et al., 2025; Angelopoulos et al., 2023) which targets coverage or efficiency improvements via debiased CDF estimators, by instead framing the problem as variance reduction of the quantile estimator under a Wasserstein alignment objective with explicit regularization toward the source-derived conditional distribution. The set stability metric itself connects to the training-conditional coverage literature (Bian & Barber, 2023; Liang & Barber, 2025) but shifts focus from coverage variability to prediction-set-size variability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: expectation-based metrics (efficiency, conditional coverage) mask the high run-to-run variability of prediction sets when calibration data are scarce, which is the common real-world scenario. The theoretical variance-rate improvement from O(n⁻¹) to O(m⁻¹ + {n(1+λ)²}⁻¹) is clean and the method is modular—applicable to any conformal score including localized ones. Limitations include the need for a reasonably accurate source-to-target conditional distribution transfer (Assumption 4.1's parametric structure), the added complexity of the Wasserstein alignment optimization, and the fact that gains diminish as n grows. Open questions include performance under severe covariate shift, extension to online or streaming calibration, and whether the set stability criterion can be directly optimized end-to-end rather than through the quantile-variance proxy.
