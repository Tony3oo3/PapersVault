## Problem

VQ-VAE models suffer from codebook collapse, where only a small subset of codebook vectors is actively utilized while the majority remain inactive. Existing remedies focus on the frequency or mechanism of codebook selection and update (e.g., online K-means, stochastic quantization) but do not address the underlying geometric imbalance in the latent space—specifically, the norm disparity and spatial concentration of high-utility codebook vectors that drives the collapse.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (28×28 MNIST, 32×32 CIFAR-10, 256×256 ImageNet) and produces a discrete codebook token grid (32×32 or 16×16 spatial tokens drawn from a codebook of K=1024 entries) along with a reconstructed image. For generation, an LDM is trained on top of the ArcVQ-VAE tokenizer to synthesize 256×256 class-conditional images using 250 DDIM sampling steps. The key structural constraint is that all codebook vectors are rescaled to remain within a time-dependent Euclidean ball, and quantization is performed via cosine-similarity matching on ℓ2-normalized vectors rather than raw Euclidean distance.

## Method

ArcVQ-VAE introduces a Spherical Angular-Margin Prior (SAMP) composed of two lightweight components that require no additional network modules. First, Ball-Bounded Norm Regularization (BBNR) rescales each codebook vector after every training batch so its ℓ2-norm does not exceed a time-dependent bound M(t)=exp(α·t), preventing high-utility vectors from accumulating excessively large norms and dominating the latent space. Second, the ArcCosine Additive Margin Loss (ArcLoss) ℓ2-normalizes both encoder outputs and codebook vectors, reinterpreting quantization as angular-similarity matching on the unit hypersphere, and then applies an ArcFace-style additive angular margin m to the top-k nearest latent tokens per codebook entry, encouraging tighter positive alignment and greater angular separation from negatives. A stop-gradient on codebook vectors in ArcLoss ensures gradients flow only to the encoder, while the standard VQ loss indirectly updates the codebook toward the now more dispersed encoder outputs. A decay factor γ(t)=γ₀·exp(−λt) modulates the ArcLoss weight, applying stronger angular constraints early in training and gradually shifting emphasis to reconstruction fidelity.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K reconstruction, ArcVQ-VAE with a 32×32 token map and only K=1024 codebook entries achieves an rFID of 0.95 with 88% codebook usage, outperforming all baselines including VQGAN-LC (rFID 1.29, K=100,000) and CVQ-VAE (rFID 1.20, K=1024). For ImageNet generation with an LDM prior, ArcVQ-VAE (K=1024) achieves FID 6.79, IS 172.3, precision 0.75, and recall 0.52, surpassing VQGAN-LC(LDM) (FID 8.36, K=100,000) and CVQ-VAE(LDM) (FID 6.87, K=1024). On MNIST, ArcVQ-VAE reaches PSNR 28.01 / SSIM 0.9840 / rFID 1.68, and on CIFAR-10 it reaches PSNR 24.78 / SSIM 0.8989 / rFID 26.91, both leading all VQ-VAE baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ImageNet reconstruction, ArcVQ-VAE (32², K=1024) achieves rFID 0.95, beating VQGAN-LC (1.29, K=100,000) by 0.34, CVQ-VAE (1.20, K=1024) by 0.25, MoVQ (1.12, K=1024) by 0.17, and ViT-VQGAN (1.28, K=8192) by 0.33, all while using a codebook 8–97× smaller. For ImageNet generation, ArcVQ-VAE(LDM) with K=1024 reaches FID 6.79, outperforming CVQ-VAE(LDM) (6.87), MoVQ(MaskGIT) (7.13), RQVAE(RQ-Transformer) (7.55), and VQGAN-LC(LDM) (8.36, K=100,000). On MNIST, ArcVQ-VAE leads all baselines on every metric (PSNR 28.01 vs. CVQ-VAE 27.87, rFID 1.68 vs. 1.80). On CIFAR-10, ArcVQ-VAE wins on PSNR (24.78 vs. CVQ-VAE 24.72), SSIM (0.8989 vs. 0.8978), and LPIPS (0.1857 vs. 0.1883), but trails CVQ-VAE on rFID (26.91 vs. 24.73), indicating a slight trade-off in perceptual fidelity on this smaller dataset. The post-hoc codebook reduction experiment (Table 10) further shows that VQGAN-LC's rFID degrades to 2.40 when its codebook is reduced to 1024 entries, whereas ArcVQ-VAE natively achieves 0.95 at the same budget, confirming that the gains stem from improved codebook geometry rather than sheer codebook size.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: the VQ-VAE codebook learning literature (Van Den Oord et al., 2017; Zheng & Vedaldi, 2023; Takida et al., 2022) and the angular-margin metric learning literature from face recognition (ArcFace, Deng et al., 2019). While prior codebook-improvement methods (CVQ-VAE, SQ-VAE, MoVQ, VQGAN-LC) focus on update mechanisms, stochastic quantization, or codebook scaling, ArcVQ-VAE reframes codebook collapse as a geometric problem—norm imbalance and spatial concentration in the latent space—and addresses it with a spherical angular-margin prior (SAMP) combining Ball-Bounded Norm Regularization and an ArcCosine Additive Margin Loss. The key conceptual departure is treating the codebook as a set of points on (or within) a hypersphere and importing the angular-margin machinery from classification to enforce discriminative, uniformly dispersed codebook geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in achieving near-SOTA reconstruction and generation quality with a small codebook (K=1024) using only a norm-rescaling step and a lightweight loss term—no extra network components or external models—compared to methods that scale codebooks to 100,000 entries. The ablation isolating cosine-similarity matching (Table 11) as insufficient on its own, while the full SAMP yields substantial gains, clarifies that the geometric constraint and angular margin are both necessary. Open questions include whether the heuristic hyperparameters (top-k, decay schedule γ(t), growth rate α) can be made adaptive, whether the geometric argument extends to other discrete-tokenization settings (audio, video, 3D), and whether improved codebook geometry can simplify or reduce the need for a downstream generative prior.
