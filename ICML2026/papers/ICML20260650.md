## Problem

Existing CASSI reconstruction methods, whether end-to-end or deep unfolding networks, are formulated on fixed discrete spectral bands, deviating from the intrinsic spectral continuity of the CASSI imaging principle. This restriction precludes continuous spectral reconstruction at arbitrary resolutions and spectral super-resolution at wavelengths unseen during training, limiting the practical utility of snapshot compressive imaging in applications requiring flexible spectral sampling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260650/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 2D compressed snapshot measurement (H × W̃), the physical sensing matrix Φ encoding the coded mask and dispersion, and a continuous scalar wavelength coordinate λ ∈ ℝ as input. It outputs a 2D spectral intensity image (1 × H × W) at the queried wavelength, enabling both dense continuous reconstruction across 143 bands (450–650 nm) and zero-shot spectral super-resolution at 20 unseen wavelengths. During training, only 6 randomly sampled discrete wavelengths are used per iteration, yet the model generalizes to render at any continuous coordinate at inference without retraining.

## Method

Phy-CoSF unrolls a K-stage A-HQS iterative optimization into a deep network where each stage comprises a degradation-aware network (DAN) for the data-fidelity step and a proposed Continuous Spectral Fields (CoSF) module as the learnable prior. The CoSF module first extracts a wavelength-agnostic latent representation via a triple-branch cross-domain feature mixer operating at fine, meso, and coarse spatial scales, where each scale is processed by a Cross-Domain Feature Encoder (CDFE) that sequentially applies spatial-domain GLAM attention, a Fourier Mamba module (2D FFT → Mamba sequence modeling → iFFT) for global frequency dependencies, and channel-domain GDFN recalibration. A Spectral Synthesis Head (SSH) then decodes the queried wavelength through a fixed random frequency encoder (sin/cos basis) and a learnable spectral embedding MLP, concatenates the resulting spectral embedding with the wavelength-agnostic features, and regresses the pixel-wise intensity via a lightweight convolutional head. The train-render two-phase paradigm decouples discrete-band optimization (L1 loss on 6 sampled wavelengths per iteration) from continuous rendering, enabling the model to synthesize high-fidelity spectra at arbitrary coordinates in a zero-shot manner.

## Results

On the ICVL dataset (10 test scenes, 143 continuous wavelengths), Phy-CoSF-9stg achieves an average SAM of 1.14°, PSNR of 36.45 dB, and SSIM of 0.915, substantially outperforming the best discrete baseline Lade-DUN-10stg (SAM 2.16°, PSNR 35.79 dB, SSIM 0.914). For spectral super-resolution on 20 unseen wavelengths, Phy-CoSF maintains SAM 1.15° and PSNR 36.46 dB, again leading all competitors. On the discrete reconstruction benchmark (CAVE/KAIST), Phy-CoSF reaches 39.80 dB PSNR and 0.978 SSIM, surpassing RDLUF-MixS2-9stg (39.57 dB, 0.974) and PADUT-12stg (38.89 dB, 0.974).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Phy-CoSF achieves the best SAM (1.14°) across all 10 ICVL scenes, roughly halving the error of the closest competitor Lade-DUN-10stg (2.16°) and GAP-Net (2.38°), confirming superior spectral fidelity. In PSNR, Phy-CoSF leads by 0.44 dB over GAP-Net (36.01 dB) and 0.66 dB over Lade-DUN (35.79 dB), while trailing GAP-Net by a negligible 0.00 dB in SSIM (both 0.915). For spectral super-resolution on 20 unseen wavelengths (Table 2), Phy-CoSF extends its SAM advantage to 1.15° vs. 2.08° for Lade-DUN, a near 45% reduction. On the discrete reconstruction benchmark (Table 3), Phy-CoSF (39.80 dB PSNR, 0.978 SSIM) outperforms RDLUF-MixS2 (39.57 dB, 0.974), PADUT (38.89 dB, 0.974), and DAUHST (38.36 dB, 0.967), while also surpassing the E2E method MGIR (35.69 dB, 0.951) by over 4 dB. Notably, Phy-CoSF uses only 0.27 M parameters—far fewer than GAP-Net (4.21 M) or DAUHST (2.42 M)—yet its 801 G FLOPs are the highest among 6-channel baselines, though still well below the 1,593–2,249 G FLOPs projected for discrete methods at 143 bands.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the deep unfolding network (DUN) lineage for CASSI reconstruction—specifically the A-HQS iterative framework from MiJUN (Qin et al., 2025) and the degradation-aware network (DAN) from RDLUF (Dong et al., 2023)—by replacing the discrete prior module at each unfolding stage with a continuous spectral fields (CoSF) module grounded in implicit neural representations. The key conceptual departure is that all prior DUNs for CASSI (MST++, CST-L+, DAUHST, PADUT, DERNN-LNLT, LADE-DUN, MiJUN) are architecturally locked to fixed spectral band outputs, whereas Phy-CoSF introduces a wavelength-agnostic latent representation decoded via random frequency encoding and a spectral synthesis head to render at arbitrary continuous coordinates. The Fourier Mamba module, which applies Mamba sequence modeling in the FFT domain rather than the spatial domain, is a novel architectural choice not seen in prior CASSI or broader vision Mamba work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The train-render two-phase paradigm—optimizing on randomly sampled discrete wavelengths during training while natively rendering at arbitrary continuous wavelengths at inference—offers a clean solution to the spectral super-resolution problem without requiring retraining or post-hoc interpolation, which is a meaningful advance for applications like remote sensing and medical diagnostics where specific band selection matters. However, the 801.38G FLOPs (roughly 6–130× higher than competing 6-channel baselines) is a substantial practical limitation for real-time or edge deployment, and the authors themselves flag multi-resolution hash grids as a needed efficiency improvement. The broader open question is whether this continuous-physics-guided unfolding paradigm generalizes beyond CASSI to other high-dimensional inverse problems (e.g., video-spectral imaging), which the authors identify as future work but do not validate.
