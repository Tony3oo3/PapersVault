## Problem

Existing MLLM-based AI-generated video detectors reason in an unconstrained semantic space, making them susceptible to hallucinated evidence, unstable single-pass reasoning, and generic unverifiable explanations. Non-MLLM detectors avoid hallucination but offer limited interpretability and poor cross-generator generalization. The paper asks how MLLMs can make accurate authenticity judgments while grounding them in structured, video-specific forensic evidence that can be traced and audited.

## Input / Output

The method takes a raw video as input, uniformly sampled at 1 fps and partitioned into 5-frame clips, with no task-specific training required. It produces a four-part structured output: a Real/Fake authenticity label, a confidence score, a free-text evidence-grounded explanation, and an Evidence Reasoning Graph (ERG)—a three-level directed graph linking individual forensic evidence nodes (each with descriptor, confidence, polarity, and temporal span) to their source clip nodes and ultimately to a global authenticity hypothesis node. The ERG makes the provenance and contribution of every piece of evidence explicit and inspectable.

## Method

Hermes is a training-free agentic pipeline built on LangGraph with Qwen3-VL-8B as the VLM and ChatGPT-5 as the MLLM planner. It comprises three tightly coupled stages: (1) Adaptive Instance-Conditioned Detection Strategy Planning, which constructs a structured perceptual context (scene semantics, entity taxonomy, motion dynamics, imaging quality) and uses RAG over a Forensic Knowledge Base of 19 evidence-tool pairs (10 temporal, 9 spatial) to retrieve the top-5 most relevant entries and compose a video-specific detection strategy; (2) Evidence-Centric Reasoning and Verification, which invokes the strategy-specified forensic tools per clip and organizes findings into an initial ERG; (3) Graph-Grounded Evidence Deliberation, an iterative three-role loop (Primary Deliberator supplements missing evidence, Counter Deliberator challenges unreliable nodes and relations, Consensus Deliberator adjudicates revisions) that refines the ERG over up to 3 rounds before returning the final judgment. The key insight is reframing detection as a controlled, video-conditioned forensic process rather than open-ended MLLM inference, constraining reasoning to authenticity-relevant evidence and making the decision chain auditable.

## Results

On the GenVideo benchmark (10 generated-video subsets), Hermes achieves 94.81% Recall, 94.62% Accuracy, 94.59% F1, and 97.84% AUROC on average, outperforming the strongest training-free baseline D³ by 2.45 percentage points across the four metrics. In false-detection analysis, Hermes attains the lowest FPR (5.66%), FNR (4.87%), and EER (5.27%), reducing EER by 3.12 pp over D³ and 2.47 pp over NSG-VD. Notably, Hermes requires no task-specific training yet surpasses all fully supervised baselines in overall performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Hermes outperforms all eight baselines on every reported metric. Against the closest competitor D³ (training-free, ICCV 2025), Hermes gains +3.10 pp Recall, +2.80 pp ACC, +2.68 pp F1, and +1.22 pp AUROC, while also reducing FPR by 2.34 pp and EER by 3.12 pp. Versus the strongest supervised method NSG-VD (NeurIPS 2025), Hermes improves F1 by 3.72 pp and AUROC by 1.70 pp, and cuts FPR from 11.51% to 5.66%. Against UNITE (CVPR 2025), which reports only recall, Hermes leads by 5.21 pp. The gap widens substantially over older supervised baselines: Hermes surpasses Skyra by +7.15 pp Recall and +4.59 pp F1, DeMamba by +22.79 pp Recall and +14.47 pp F1, TALL by +34.03 pp Recall, NPR by +37.46 pp Recall, and STIL by +67.79 pp Recall. The consistent advantage across all 10 GenVideo subsets (e.g., 94.12–96.44% Recall per subset) indicates robust cross-generator generalization without any generator-specific training.

## Relation to Prior Work

Hermes carves new ground at the intersection of two established lines of work: fixed-pipeline AIGV detectors (DeMamba, NSG-VD, D3, TALL) that offer limited interpretability, and direct MLLM-based forgery detectors (FakeShield, BusterX++, AIGI-Holmes) that produce explanations but remain vulnerable to hallucination and unstructured reasoning. Rather than building on a single prior method, Hermes repurposes retrieval-augmented generation, multi-agent deliberation, and graph-based reasoning—ideas drawn from NLP and agentic AI—into a unified forensic workflow that constrains MLLM inference to video-grounded evidence. The key conceptual departure is from both the 'one fixed pipeline for all videos' paradigm of traditional detectors and the 'single-pass free-form MLLM reasoning' paradigm of prior MLLM-based detectors, replacing both with an adaptive, evidence-structured, and iteratively audited process.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free nature of Hermes (no task-specific training, no retraining for new generators) combined with SOTA performance on GenVideo makes it practically attractive for deployment in rapidly evolving generative video landscapes. The structured Evidence Reasoning Graph and multi-agent deliberation provide auditable explanations that go beyond a binary label, which is critical for real-world media verification and legal contexts. However, the approach depends on a manually curated forensic knowledge base of only 19 evidence-tool pairs, specific MLLM backends (Qwen3-VL-8B, ChatGPT-5), and incurs multi-round inference cost; open questions include how the knowledge base scales to new artifact types, whether the framework generalizes beyond video to other media, and its robustness against adversarially generated content designed to evade forensic checks.
