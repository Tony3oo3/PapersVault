## Problem

Standard SAM implementations update using the shifted gradient ∇f(x⁺) evaluated at the perturbed point, rather than the surrogate gradient ∇f^SAM(x) that motivates the method. This mismatch means SAM can stall at points where the shifted gradient vanishes while the original gradient is nonzero—points that are not stationary for the true loss. Prior convergence theory largely assumed small perturbation radii ρ, leaving the large-ρ regime, where practitioners observe performance degradation, theoretically unexplained.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard neural network parameters x ∈ ℝ^d and a differentiable loss f, with a scalar perturbation radius ρ controlling the ascent step. The SAM iteration produces updated parameters x_{k+1} = x_k − η_k ∇f(x_k + ρ·∇f(x_k)/‖∇f(x_k)‖). For diagnostics, the paper monitors GradRatio(x) = ‖∇f(x⁺)‖/(‖∇f(x)‖+ε) and LossGap(x) = f(x⁺)−f(x); at a hallucinated minimizer, GradRatio ≪ 1 and LossGap ≪ 0, providing a practical signature for detecting the failure mode without requiring formal certification.

## Method

The paper proves that hallucinated minimizers—local minimizers of the surrogate loss f^SAM(x) = f(x⁺) where ∇f(x⁺)=0 but ∇f(x)≠0—exist for a nontrivial interval of radii including large-ρ values, under the simple condition that f has both a local minimizer and a local maximizer. The geometric construction identifies a point x_h on the boundary of a superlevel set near the maximizer that maximizes distance to the minimizer x_★; by Lagrange multipliers, ∇f(x_h) aligns exactly with the direction toward x_★, forcing x_h⁺ = x_★ and hence ∇f(x_h⁺)=0. The existence result extends to non-isolated maximizer sets under real-analyticity (via the Lojasiewicz inequality to exclude critical-point accumulation). The paper further shows that isolated hallucinated-minimizer components are locally attracting for the discrete-time SAM dynamics under a mild eigenvalue condition, and that they can form smooth manifolds inheriting the dimension of the original minimizer set via local invertibility of the perturbation map. Empirically, full-batch training on MNIST (Tanh) directly verifies convergence to such points, and the same mismatch signatures appear in ResNet-18 on CIFAR-10/100; a short 10% SGD warm-start before enabling SAM mitigates the failure mode.

## Results

On a 2-layer Tanh network trained full-batch on MNIST at ρ=1.8, SAM converges to a point where the shifted gradient is nearly zero (‖∇f(x₊)‖ = 4.8×10⁻⁹) while the original gradient remains substantial (‖∇f(x)‖ = 6.27×10⁻²), directly verifying the hallucinated-minimizer mechanism. On full-batch ResNet-18 for CIFAR-10, the mismatch grows with ρ: at ρ=1.5, the original loss f(x_T) reaches 0.773 with gradient norm 11.93, while the SAM loss f(x_T⁺) stays at 0.001 with gradient norm 0.011. On CIFAR-100, the effect is even more pronounced at ρ=2.0 (f(x_T)=0.828, ‖∇f(x_T)‖=13.72 vs. f(x_T⁺)=0.014, ‖∇f(x_T⁺)‖=0.123). A short 10% SGD warm-start before enabling SAM eliminates the plateau behavior across the ρ sweep on both MNIST and CIFAR-100.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against competing optimizers; instead, its primary comparison is between SAM-only training and a SGD→SAM switching strategy (10% warm-start) across perturbation radii. On full-batch MNIST (80 seeds), SAM-only training at ρ≥1.3 shows a substantial fraction of runs plateauing at positive training loss with severe accuracy collapse, while the switching strategy drives loss to near zero across all ρ values tested. On stochastic CIFAR-100 with ResNet-18, SAM-only final test accuracy degrades from 74.29% (ρ=0.1) to 61.58% (ρ=1.0), whereas the switching strategy maintains 74.48% to 67.93% over the same range, with peak accuracy of 77.49% (switching) vs. 77.05% (SAM-only) at ρ=0.3. The comparison across ρ values within SAM-only training itself reveals the large-ρ failure mode: the shifted-vs-original gradient ratio (GradRatio) drops by orders of magnitude as ρ increases, confirming that the practical SAM update becomes stationary at non-stationary points of the original loss.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SAM (Foret et al., 2021) and its growing body of theoretical analyses, but identifies a qualitatively distinct failure mode that prior convergence guarantees (Si & Yun, 2023; Khanh et al., 2024; Oikonomou & Loizou, 2025) do not capture because they typically assume small perturbation radii. The authors explicitly distinguish hallucinated minimizers from the previously studied saddle-trapping phenomenon (Kaddour et al., 2022; Compagnoni et al., 2023): here the original gradient is nonzero while the shifted gradient vanishes, so the iterate is stationary only for the practical SAM update, not for the surrogate or the original loss. The geometric construction leverages the Lojasiewicz inequality to extend existence from isolated maximizers to bounded maximizer sets under real-analyticity, a tool not previously applied to SAM's perturbation dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a mechanistic explanation for the empirically observed performance degradation of SAM at large ρ, connecting a known practical pathology to a specific geometric condition (gradient alignment near local maximizer regions). The practical safeguard—a short 10% SGD warm-start before enabling SAM—is simple, broadly compatible, and reduces sensitivity to ρ without weakening the sharpness-regularization effect. Key limitations include the restriction of formal results to deterministic full-batch dynamics and real-analytic (or C²) objectives, excluding ReLU-based architectures and stochastic mini-batch training; the authors note that analogous guarantees for the stochastic and nonsmooth regimes remain open, as does the question of whether SAM variants that modify the ascent step suppress or amplify hallucinated minimizers.
