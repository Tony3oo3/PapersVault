## Problem

Parallel decoding in diffusion LLMs is hampered by joint-marginal mismatch: the model only provides token-wise conditional marginals, yet unmasking multiple tokens simultaneously requires accounting for inter-token dependencies. Existing training-free strategies rely solely on marginal confidence signals, which ignore cross-position interactions and leave dependent tokens vulnerable to inconsistent co-updating. This paper addresses the gap by explicitly modeling inter-token dependencies to guide which positions can be safely decoded in parallel.

## Input / Output

At each decoding step, DAPD consumes the current masked sequence and the self-attention weights already computed during the dLLM's forward pass (averaged over all heads and the final 30% of Transformer layers), requiring no additional model evaluations. It outputs a parallel decoding schedule: at each step, an independent set of masked positions is selected for simultaneous unmasking, and the process repeats until all tokens are resolved. A linearly scheduled threshold τ_t controls edge density in the dependency graph, and once the remaining mask ratio drops below 50%, a confidence-threshold shortcut (conf > 0.9) accelerates completion.

## Method

DAPD models the interactions among masked tokens at each step as a Markov Random Field, where the edge structure is induced by symmetric attention scores s_ij = (a_ij + a_ji)/2 thresholded at τ_t. Non-edges indicate approximate conditional independence given the remaining context, making the graph a conflict graph for parallel decoding. The parallel batch is selected via a Welsh-Powell-inspired greedy strategy: nodes are sorted by confidence-weighted degree (d̃_i · conf_i), and each node is added to the independent set only if it is non-adjacent to all already-selected nodes. This degree-prioritized ordering resolves high-degree 'hub' tokens early, sparsifying the residual graph and enabling larger parallel batches in subsequent steps. The entire procedure is training-free, reuses attention weights from the existing forward pass, and adds only lightweight graph-processing overhead relative to the dominant cost of model evaluations.

## Results

On LLaDA, DAPD-Staged achieves 40.4% on MBPP and 62.0% on IFEval with only 37.6 and 50.1 decoding steps, respectively, substantially outperforming baselines that require 41–149 steps for comparable or lower accuracy. On Dream, DAPD-Staged reaches 46.4% on MBPP, 56.0% on GSM8K, and 39.2% on IFEval with 49–90 steps, while baselines achieve 30.6–34.4% on MBPP, 44.5–47.7% on GSM8K, and 7.1–18.2% on IFEval with 53–204 steps. In the TriviaQA analysis (five independent questions per prompt), DAPD achieves 52.08% accuracy in 66.2 steps (3.87× speedup over step-by-step decoding), compared to 52.12% in 124.4 steps for Fast-dLLM (2.06×). End-to-end throughput on HumanEval reaches 106.0 tokens/sec for DAPD versus 51.4 for Fast-dLLM and 25.6 for KLASS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On LLaDA, DAPD-Staged matches or exceeds baseline accuracy on MBPP (40.4% vs. 19.4–26.0%) and IFEval (62.0% vs. 57.0–58.4%) while using roughly half the decoding steps (37.6–50.1 vs. 41.5–149.4); on GSM8K it trades ~5 points of accuracy (71.7% vs. 75.6–76.8%) for a 1.5–2× step reduction. On Dream, DAPD-Staged dominates across most tasks: it achieves 46.4% on MBPP (vs. 30.6–34.4%), 56.0% on GSM8K (vs. 44.5–47.7%), 25.4% on Math500 (vs. 13.0–17.5%), and 39.2% on IFEval (vs. 7.1–18.2%), all with 49–90 steps versus 53–204 for baselines. The one exception is Dream HumanEval, where KLASS reaches 59.8% (vs. DAPD's 42.1%), though DAPD still uses fewer steps (111.6 vs. 133.3). On ParallelBench, DAPD-Staged achieves the best score on Words→Sentence (88.2 vs. 77.7–78.2) with the fewest steps (19.1 vs. 27.3–35.4), confirming its advantage under strong inter-token dependencies. In the TriviaQA multi-question analysis, DAPD achieves a 3.87× speedup over step-by-step decoding while matching its accuracy (52.08% vs. 52.64%), whereas Fast-dLLM and EB-Sampler achieve only 2.06× and 1.95× speedups respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant training-free parallel decoding paradigm for dLLMs (Fast-dLLM, EB-Sampler, KLASS), which selects tokens to unmask in parallel based solely on marginal confidence or entropy signals. DAPD instead repurposes the model's own self-attention weights as a proxy for conditional independence, constructing a Markov Random Field over masked positions at each decoding step. The independent-set selection procedure is borrowed from the Welsh-Powell graph-coloring heuristic, reframing parallel decoding as a dynamic graph-coloring problem where high-degree 'hub' tokens are resolved first to sparsify the residual graph. This positions DAPD as a structural complement to confidence-based methods: confidence measures token reliability, while the attention-induced graph captures cross-position dependence that marginal signals inherently miss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DAPD addresses the joint-marginal mismatch problem that fundamentally limits parallel decoding in dLLMs, and does so in a training-free manner that requires no auxiliary models or retraining. The qualitative finding that confidence-based baselines collapse into sequential, autoregressive-like unmasking patterns while DAPD achieves spatially dispersed, globally parallel generation highlights a previously underexploited property of dLLMs (any-order generation) and suggests that graph-structured decoding is a natural paradigm for discrete diffusion. Open questions include whether the heuristic threshold schedule and fixed attention-layer selection generalize across architectures and tasks, whether attention-based edge scores can be replaced by more principled conditional-independence estimators, and whether the approach extends to larger-scale dLLMs beyond the 7–8B models tested here.
