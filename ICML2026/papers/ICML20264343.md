## Problem

Despite extensive empirical work on catastrophic forgetting, the theoretical literature on continual learning with neural networks trained by gradient descent has been limited to linear and generalized linear models, where well-understood statistical and optimization properties enable closed-form analyses. No prior work provided explicit, closed-form forgetting guarantees for nonlinear neural networks, leaving the roles of over-parameterization, sample complexity, and training horizon in continual learning poorly understood.

## Input / Output

The method sequentially processes K tasks, each consisting of n samples from a d-dimensional XOR cluster distribution (Gaussian mixtures with orthogonal mean vectors of norm Θ(1/√d) and noise σ = Θ(1/(polylog(d)√d))), training a two-layer network with m hidden neurons and quadratic activation via full-batch gradient descent for T iterations per task. The output is not a trained model per se but explicit closed-form upper bounds on train-time forgetting, test-time forgetting, and the delayed generalization gap, expressed as functions of ηT, n, m, K, and d, identifying the precise scaling regime (n = Õ(d²K), m = Ω̃(d⁸K⁴), T = Õ(d²)) under which all forgetting terms vanish.

## Method

The analysis decomposes test-time forgetting into a train-time forgetting term and a delayed generalization gap, then bounds each separately. For train-time forgetting, the authors characterize the exact GD weight evolution in the infinite-width (kernel) regime, showing that for XOR clusters with orthogonal means the cross-task interference terms vanish and the network output at any task k after learning all K tasks takes the form ηT·xᵀ(Σⱼ Aⱼ)x + noise, from which concentration inequalities yield the forgetting rate Õ(ηT√K/(d√n) + ηT√K/(d²polylog(d)) + η²T²K²/√m). For the delayed generalization gap, they develop an algorithmic stability argument tailored to continual learning, bounding the leave-one-out parameter sensitivity by unrolling the GD trajectory across all subsequent tasks and exploiting the non-expansive property of one-hidden-layer networks in the NTK regime. A key finding is that neither width nor sample size alone eliminates forgetting—both must scale jointly with K and d—and that L₂ regularization around the previous-task solution is equivalent in the kernel regime to unregularized learning with a time-varying effective step size, so it does not fundamentally improve the forgetting scalings.

## Results

The paper derives the first closed-form train-time forgetting bound for neural networks: $\widetilde{O}\!\left(\eta T\frac{\sqrt{K}}{d\sqrt{n}} + \eta T\frac{\sqrt{K}}{d^2\,\mathrm{polylog}(d)} + \eta^2 T^2\frac{K^2}{\sqrt{m}}\right)$, showing that vanishing forgetting requires $n=\widetilde{\Theta}(d^2 K)$, $m=\widetilde{\Omega}(d^8 K^4)$, and $T=\widetilde{\Theta}(d^2)$. The delayed generalization gap is bounded as $\lesssim \frac{\eta T\, e^{\eta T(K-k+1)/\sqrt{m}}}{n}$, and under the same scalings both train- and test-time forgetting are $o_d(1)$. Empirically, on XOR-cluster data with K=3 tasks, increasing per-task sample size from n=2500 to n=5000 reduces test-error forgetting to near zero; on split-MNIST (K=2) and FashionMNIST (K=4), increasing the sample size of later tasks consistently reduces the first task's training loss, confirming the theoretical role of sample size.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper that does not run head-to-head comparisons against other continual-learning methods (e.g., EWC, LwF, or experience replay). Instead, it positions its contribution relative to prior theoretical work: unlike linear-model analyses (Evron et al., 2023; Banayeeanzade et al., 2024) that rely on realizability and closed-form ERM solutions, the paper provides the first closed-form forgetting guarantees for nonlinear neural networks trained by plain gradient descent. Compared to NTK-overlap analyses (Doan et al., 2021; Bennani et al., 2020) that yield general expressions without explicit parameter dependence, the paper's bounds explicitly track sample size n, width m, number of tasks K, and training horizon T. The empirical experiments (Figs. 1–7) validate the theory across quadratic/ReLU/GELU activations, hinge/logistic/linear losses, and XOR-cluster/MNIST/FashionMNIST data, but do not benchmark against competing continual-learning algorithms. The key empirical finding is that width alone cannot eliminate forgetting (diminishing returns beyond m≈10³), consistent with the $1/\sqrt{m}$ term in the bound, and that L₂-regularized continual learning is equivalent to unregularized GD with a rescaled step size in the kernel regime (Prop. 2.7), so it does not improve the forgetting scalings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the algorithmic stability framework of Bousquet & Elisseeff (2002) and its neural-network refinements by Taheri & Thrampoulidis (JMLR 2024) and Taheri et al. (ICLR 2025) from single-task to multi-task continual learning, adapting the leave-one-out stability argument to track parameter drift across a sequence of tasks. It departs from prior continual-learning theory (Evron et al. 2023; Banayeeanzade et al. 2024), which achieved closed-form forgetting bounds only for linear and generalized linear models, by providing the first such guarantees for two-layer neural networks in the NTK/kernel regime. The XOR-cluster data model is drawn from the multi-index and parity learning literature (Wei et al. 2019; Refinetti et al. 2021; Damian et al. 2022), repurposed here as a canonical non-linearly separable task family with orthogonal means across tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that plain sequential gradient descent—without any continual-learning regularization or replay—can achieve vanishing train- and test-time forgetting under explicit scaling conditions on sample size, width, and training horizon, providing the first closed-form characterization of forgetting dynamics for neural networks. A notable limitation is the restriction to the lazy/kernel regime with quadratic activation, orthogonal task means, and K = Õ(1) tasks; extending to the feature-learning regime, SGD, deeper architectures, and correlated tasks remains open. The finding that L2 regularization around the previous-task solution is equivalent to a rescaled step size in the kernel regime suggests that popular EWC-style methods may not fundamentally improve forgetting in this regime, redirecting attention to architectural or feature-learning mechanisms.
