## Problem

Prior convergence analyses of LMO-based optimizers like Muon and Scion suffer from two critical mismatches with practice: they analyze a simplified global update rather than the layer-wise LMO calls actually deployed, and they rely on classical L-smoothness, which prescribes impractically tiny constant stepsizes that bear no resemblance to the tuned learning rates used in real LLM training. This leaves a significant gap between theoretical guarantees and the strong empirical performance these optimizers exhibit.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a parameter vector decomposed into p layer matrices X_i ∈ R^{m_i × n_i}, each equipped with its own norm ‖·‖_(i) (e.g., spectral norm for transformer blocks, scaled 1→∞ norm for embedding layers). At each iteration, it consumes a stochastic gradient per layer and an adaptive radius t_i^k, then solves an independent linear minimization subproblem over the norm ball B_i^k = {X_i : ‖X_i − X_i^k‖_(i) ≤ t_i^k} to produce the updated layer. The output is the full updated parameter vector assembled from all per-layer LMO solutions, with the key structural constraint that each layer's update is computed independently rather than jointly over the full parameter space.

## Method

Gluon is a layer-wise LMO-based optimizer with momentum that generalizes Muon and Scion as special cases under specific norm choices. The core theoretical contribution is a novel layer-wise (L⁰, L¹)-smoothness assumption, which bounds the per-layer gradient difference by (L_i⁰ + L_i¹‖∇_i f(X)‖_(i)*)‖X_i − Y_i‖_(i), capturing the anisotropic geometry where each layer has its own smoothness constants. Under this assumption, the paper derives adaptive, layer-specific stepsizes t_i^k = ‖∇_i f(X^k)‖_(i)*/(L_i⁰ + L_i¹‖∇_i f(X^k)‖_(i)*) that are substantially larger than the constant K^{-3/4} stepsizes from prior analyses, and empirically match the relative magnitudes of tuned per-layer learning rates. The framework yields O(K^{-1/2}) deterministic and O(K^{-1/4}) stochastic convergence rates under bounded variance, with the first term vanishing when L_i⁰ ≈ 0 (as observed empirically), leaving a rate governed by the harmonic mean of L_i¹ rather than the worst-case maximum.

## Results

The paper establishes O(1/K^{1/2}) convergence for deterministic Gluon and O(1/K^{1/4}) for the stochastic variant under the new layer-wise (L⁰,L¹)-smoothness assumption, with adaptive per-layer stepsizes t_i^k = ‖∇_i f(X^k)‖_{(i)★} / (L_i⁰ + L_i¹‖∇_i f(X^k)‖_{(i)★}). Empirically, training NanoGPT-124M on FineWeb with unScion (ρ₂=50, ρ₃=3000) yields the best validation loss, and the fitted smoothness constants show L_i⁰ ≈ 0 across all layers while L_p¹ (embedding) is approximately 20–30× smaller than L_i¹ (transformer blocks), implying t_p^k should be 20–30× larger than t_i^k — precisely matching the tuned hyperparameters from Pethick et al. (2025b). The layer-wise (L⁰,L¹) model achieves substantially smaller relative error than constant-L fits across all blocks and matrix types.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary empirical baseline is AdamW (Loshchilov & Hutter, 2019), against which unScion (Gluon) is compared on NanoGPT-124M/FineWeb. unScion with ρ₂=50, ρ₃=3000 (i.e., t_i^k=0.018 for transformer blocks, t_p^k=1.08 for embeddings) achieves the best final validation loss, outperforming AdamW under its tuned settings. The theoretical comparison is against four prior convergence analyses (Kovalev 2025; Li & Hong 2025; Pethick et al. 2025b), all of which assume classical L-smoothness and prescribe constant stepsizes proportional to K^{−3/4} (stochastic) or K^{−1/2} (deterministic) — values that are impractically small for real training. Gluon's analysis is strictly more general (layer-wise (L⁰,L¹)-smoothness with arbitrary norms) and yields adaptive, layer-specific stepsizes that match empirically tuned values. A key validation: the theoretically implied 20–30× ratio between embedding-layer and transformer-block step sizes (from the fitted L_i¹ values) exactly reproduces the ρ₃/ρ₂ = 3000/50 = 60× ratio used in practice, confirming the theory's practical relevance. The smoothness assumption is further validated to hold along both unScion and AdamW training trajectories, ruling out optimizer-specific artifacts.

## Relation to Prior Work

Gluon unifies Muon (Jordan et al., 2024b) and Scion/unScion (Pethick et al., 2025b) under a single layer-wise LMO framework rooted in Frank-Wolfe, and recovers both as special cases via specific norm choices. The key departure from prior convergence analyses (Kovalev, 2025; Li & Hong, 2025; Pethick et al., 2025b) is twofold: the paper analyzes the actual per-layer update rather than a simplified global one, and replaces the classical L-smoothness assumption with a novel layer-wise (L⁰,L¹)-smoothness condition that extends Zhang et al. (2020) and Liu et al. (2025b) to arbitrary norms at the layer level. This positions the work as both a correction of existing theory (matching it to deployed implementations) and a genuine generalization of the smoothness model used in convergence proofs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first convergence analysis that faithfully reflects how Muon/Scion are actually run in practice (layer-wise, with per-layer norms and stepsizes), and the empirically validated layer-wise (L⁰,L¹)-smoothness model (with Lᵢ⁰≈0) explains why these optimizers succeed where L-smoothness-based theory predicts they should fail. The theoretically derived stepsize ratios (e.g., 20–30× larger for embedding layers) matching independently tuned hyperparameters is a strong validation that the model captures real geometry. Open questions include extending adaptive stepsizes to the stochastic regime, handling approximate LMOs, and relaxing the bounded-variance assumption for subsampling scenarios.
