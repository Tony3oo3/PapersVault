## Problem

Behavioral cloning with expressive diffusion or Flow Matching policies fits expert demonstrations well but remains an offline supervised learner: the policy is trained on expert states yet deployed on its own induced state distribution, so early mistakes compound over time. On-policy distillation (OPD) offers a natural remedy by supervising the student on its own rollouts, but standard OPD assumes a strong fixed teacher that is unavailable in demonstration-only embodied control. The paper asks whether OPD can work when the teacher must itself be learned from the same demonstrations and co-trained adversarially with the student.

## Input / Output

The method takes a static set of expert demonstration trajectories (state-action pairs) from continuous-state MuJoCo/Gymnasium robot environments, with no true environment reward available at training time. The deployed output is a lightweight diagonal Gaussian MLP policy that maps a robot state to a continuous action vector via a single forward pass. The Flow Matching teacher operates only during training and is discarded at deployment, so inference cost is identical to a standard MLP policy rather than the multi-step ODE integration required by FM policies.

## Method

FA-OPD co-trains a Flow Matching teacher (modeling the joint (s,a) distribution with OT conditional paths, conditioned on a binary expert/agent indicator) against a lightweight Gaussian MLP student. The teacher supplies two complementary supervision channels on student rollouts: (1) a reward channel where an FM-enhanced discriminator computes a temperature-scaled Softmax over per-sample FM losses to produce an AIRL log-ratio reward, which PPO optimizes over long horizons; and (2) an action channel where the same FM teacher generates expert-like target actions at student-visited states and the student regresses onto them via MSE, serving as an on-policy DAgger analog. The combined objective couples reward-guided exploration with dense local exploitation, with a hyperparameter β balancing the two channels. Gradients flow only through the affine reparameterization of the Gaussian MLP student, avoiding the backpropagation-through-ODE-solver instability that makes direct FM policy-gradient updates impractical.

## Results

Across six robot benchmarks, FA-OPD achieves the best or near-best performance in 5 of 6 environments. Headline numbers include 99.8% success on Fetch-pick, 97.9% on Hand-rotate, 87.3% on Maze2d, and average returns of 3359 (Hopper) and 4164 (Walker2d). On Ant-goal, FA-OPD (82.3%) is statistically comparable to Flow-Matching Policy (83.3%) and Diffusion Policy (82.1%), as the task's uni-modal trajectory is fully covered by expert data. FA-OPD also exhibits the lowest variance across all environments, indicating more stable training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FA-OPD outperforms all IRL baselines (GAIL, VAIL, WAIL, AIRL, DRAIL) across all six environments, with the largest margins on Fetch-pick (99.8% vs. 85.4% for VAIL, the best IRL baseline) and Walker2d (4164 vs. 3123 for DRAIL). Against the supervised behavioral cloning baselines, FA-OPD substantially surpasses Diffusion Policy and Flow-Matching Policy on five of six tasks (e.g., Fetch-pick: 99.8% vs. 83.0% DP / 54.6% FP; Hopper: 3359 vs. 1433 DP / 1950 FP), but is statistically tied with FP on Ant-goal (82.3% vs. 83.3%), where the task's simple uni-modal path is fully covered by demonstrations. In the RQ4 case study on Maze2d, FA-OPD also outperforms three online-FM-policy baselines (FM-A2C, FM-PPO, FPO) that fail to learn a feasible policy due to BPTT instability, while training and inference at MLP speed. In the robustness study (Table 1), FA-OPD exceeds expert return on Walker2d for IDs 2–3 and dominates DP/FP by 500–2000 return points once expert quality exceeds the novice threshold (ID ≥ 2). In the generalization study, FA-OPD maintains >95% success on Hand-rotate up to 1.5× noise and shows negligible degradation on Fetch-pick, whereas GAIL drops from ~91% to ~50% and WAIL/AIRL fail entirely at higher noise levels.

## Relation to Prior Work

FA-OPD sits at the intersection of three threads: (1) adversarial imitation learning (GAIL, AIRL, DRAIL), which learns a discriminator-based reward from expert data but typically uses simple MLP discriminators; (2) on-policy distillation (Agarwal et al., 2023; 2024), which corrects exposure bias by supervising the student on its own rollouts but assumes a fixed pre-trained teacher; and (3) Flow Matching policies (Lipman et al., 2022; Chi et al., 2024), which model multi-modal action distributions but are limited to offline behavioral cloning. The key departure is making the FM model a *co-trained* teacher rather than a frozen one, and coupling two complementary distillation channels (scalar reward via an FM-enhanced AIRL discriminator + dense action targets via FM generation) under a single shared FM backbone, with the deployed actor remaining a lightweight Gaussian MLP updated by PPO.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in robot learning: expressive generative policies (diffusion/FM) cannot be directly used as online RL actors due to BPTT cost and instability, yet offline cloning suffers from exposure bias and poor robustness to sub-optimal demonstrations. FA-OPD's teacher-student split (FM teacher for training, MLP student for deployment) offers a clean recipe that practitioners can adopt with minimal engineering overhead. Limitations include evaluation only in low-dimensional MuJoCo environments (no visual inputs), the 'beyond-expert' performance claim being confined to Walker2d at moderate expert quality, and the co-training dynamic's stability in higher-dimensional or more complex settings remaining untested. Open questions include whether the dual-channel design generalizes to vision-based manipulation, whether the FM teacher can be replaced by other expressive scorers, and how the method scales when demonstrations are extremely scarce or adversarially corrupted.
