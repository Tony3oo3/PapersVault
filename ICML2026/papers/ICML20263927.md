## Problem

Existing video quality assessment metrics assume a uniform viewing environment and fail to account for how perceived quality shifts across different display devices and ambient conditions. A video that looks clearly degraded on a large, bright screen may appear acceptable on a small, dim phone, yet standard VQA scores remain unchanged. This paper addresses the multi-screen VQA gap by introducing the first large-scale crowdsourced dataset spanning 300+ Android devices with rich viewing-condition metadata, and by proposing a framework to adapt pre-trained quality metrics to specific device and context settings.

## Input / Output

The adaptation module takes as input a scalar prediction from a pre-trained VQA metric (e.g., VMAF, LPIPS) concatenated with a five-dimensional viewing-condition vector (display diagonal size, pixel resolution, normalized screen brightness, ambient luminance, and display type), and outputs a single sigmoid-constrained quality score in [0,1]. The Blade-Chest aggregation stage consumes 250K pairwise preference votes, each annotated with the measured conditions under which the comparison was made, and produces latent subjective quality scores per video. The condition pool mechanism allows the system to generate training samples for viewing-condition combinations never explicitly observed in the subjective data, enabling out-of-distribution generalization to unseen device configurations.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage pipeline. First, a modified Blade-Chest pairwise-comparison model is trained via an EM procedure to extract condition-aware latent quality scores: two neural networks f_c (chest) and f_b (blade) are conditioned on both the latent score and the viewing-condition vector z, and their parameters are jointly optimized with the latent scores through alternating E-steps (score updates) and M-steps (network parameter updates) under identifiability constraints. Second, a lightweight 4-layer fully connected adaptation network (hidden size 64, tanh activations, sigmoid output) is trained to map a pre-trained metric's prediction plus viewing conditions to the adapted quality score, with training targets generated by the Blade-Chest match function evaluated on condition pairs sampled from a uniform distribution (the 'condition pool'). This condition-pool augmentation is the key mechanism that decouples training from the sparse set of actually observed device configurations, allowing the adaptation module to generalize to devices and ambient conditions not present in the subjective dataset.

## Results

On the proposed Multi-Screen VQA Dataset (250K pairwise comparisons across 300+ Android devices), the condition-adaptation module substantially improves Kendall rank correlation (KROCC) for most evaluated metrics. The largest gains are observed for perceptual deep metrics: LPIPS improves from 0.308 to 0.652 (+0.344) and AVQT from 0.439 to 0.694 (+0.255) on the Xiaomi Redmi Note 13. Traditional hand-crafted metrics (PSNR, MS-SSIM, NLPD) show minimal or negative gains, confirming that the adaptation primarily benefits learned perceptual features. Out-of-distribution evaluation on three held-out phone models confirms consistent improvement, validating the condition-pool generalization strategy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The adaptation module is evaluated against 25 full-reference and no-reference quality metrics. Perceptual deep metrics benefit the most: LPIPS (Zhang et al., 2018) gains +0.344 KROCC on Model 1, DISTS (Ding et al., 2020) gains +0.272, and DBCNN (Zhang et al., 2020) gains +0.225, all substantially outperforming their unadapted versions. VMAF (Li et al., 2016), already the strongest baseline at 0.509–0.548 KROCC, sees a more modest but consistent improvement of +0.024 to +0.097, suggesting its internal modeling partially captures some device effects. AVQT (Sodhani, 2021) and FSIM (Zhang et al., 2011) also show strong gains (+0.255 and +0.247 on Model 1). In contrast, traditional signal-level metrics—PSNR, MS-SSIM (Wang et al., 2003), and NLPD—show negligible or negative gains (e.g., PSNR drops by −0.071 on Model 3; MS-SSIM drops by −0.049 on Model 3), indicating that the adaptation module cannot compensate for the fundamental mismatch between pixel-level distortion and perceptual quality under varying display conditions. No-reference metrics like DOVER (Wu et al., 2023) and CLIP-IQA-PLUS (Wang et al., 2023b) show moderate improvements (+0.089 to +0.180), while weaker baselines such as BRISQUE (Mittal et al., 2012) and MUSIQ (Chen et al., 2024) remain low in absolute KROCC despite relative gains, confirming that the adaptation amplifies existing metric quality rather than creating it from scratch.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Blade-Chest pairwise comparison model (Chen & Joachims, 2016) by conditioning its blade and chest transformation functions on viewing-condition vectors (screen size, brightness, ambient light, display type), enabling condition-aware score aggregation via an EM procedure. It departs from the standard Bradley-Terry aggregation used in large-scale VQA datasets (CVQAD, LEHA-CVQAD) and from small-scale multi-screen studies (Barman et al. 2023 with 3 devices; Safonov et al. 2024 with 3 domains) by scaling to 300+ real-world Android devices with full environmental metadata. The adaptation module—a lightweight MLP mapping frozen VQA metric outputs plus condition vectors to a predicted quality score—repurposes the learned condition-aware transformations as a training signal via a 'condition pool' that simulates unobserved viewing scenarios, a mechanism not present in prior VQA adaptation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 250K+ pairwise comparison dataset across 300+ devices with rich display and ambient-light metadata fills a clear gap: no prior multi-screen VQA resource approaches this scale or realism, and existing metrics demonstrably fail to preserve quality orderings across devices. For streaming services that optimize compression per device cohort, the condition-aware adaptation framework offers a practical path to more accurate perceptual quality prediction without retraining expensive base models. Key limitations include Android-only coverage, the need for per-device inference of the adaptation module, and the fact that base metrics are never retrained—future work on direct device-distribution-to-score-distribution mapping and cross-platform extension would strengthen the contribution considerably.
