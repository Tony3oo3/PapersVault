## Problem

Existing multiple kernel learning (MKL) methods rarely model kernel diversity—a known cornerstone of ensemble learning—and most traditional kernels weight all input features uniformly, ignoring feature-level discriminability. This paper addresses both gaps simultaneously by introducing a data-driven kernel family that learns feature importance and a selection procedure that explicitly optimizes diversity alongside quality.

## Input / Output

The method consumes labeled tabular feature vectors (normalized to [0,1]) and a candidate pool of M kernels (Gaussian, polynomial, and RFM variants with varying bandwidths/degrees). It outputs a combined kernel function defined as the uniform average of m selected base kernels, which is then passed to a downstream learner (SVM, LS-SVM, ELM, or ODM) to produce class labels. A key design property is that the selection cost scales with M and the Nyström sketch size s rather than the sample size n, making it applicable to datasets with hundreds of thousands of points where dense-kernel baselines time out.

## Method

DARFMMKL has two coupled components. First, a Recursive Feature Machine (RFM) kernel family replaces the standard Euclidean distance in Gaussian/Laplacian kernels with a learned Mahalanobis distance, where the feature-importance matrix W is obtained via the Average Gradient Outer Product (AGOP) of a kernel ridge classifier, iterated until convergence (fixed-point existence is guaranteed by Brouwer's theorem). Second, a diversity-aware selector jointly maximizes CKA-based kernel quality and pairwise prediction disagreement over the candidate pool, formulated as an NP-hard binary quadratic program that is reformulated via Glover linearization and continuous relaxation into a linear program, further accelerated by Nyström sketching to decouple cost from n. A covering-number generalization bound explicitly relates kernel diversity to estimation error, and an ablation confirms that both the RFM feature-learning and the diversity term contribute additively to accuracy.

## Results

DARFMMKL achieves top-1 test accuracy on 11 out of 12 LIBSVM benchmark datasets, with the largest margins on mux6-ver1 (95.07% vs. 88.72% for the best baseline ABMKL, +6.35 pp) and heart (86.67% vs. 83.56% for GRMKL, +3.11 pp). On the two large-scale datasets where dense-kernel baselines time out, DARFMMKL reaches 84.43% on a8a (n=32,561) and 98.57% on w7a (n=49,749), and extends to 97.37% on ijcnn1 (n=141,691) and 80.06% on covtype.binary (n=581,012) in the scalability study. Runtime scales with the candidate pool size M and sketch size s rather than the sample size n, making it the only method that completes within 24 hours on all 12 datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DARFMMKL wins or ties on every dataset against all nine baselines, with zero statistically significant losses. Against the strongest one-step method ABMKL it wins 11/12 (tie on ionosphere), and against EAMKL it wins all 12 datasets. The two-step margin-based methods (GRMKL, RMKL, MEMKL) are the closest competitors on small datasets (e.g., GRMKL 83.56% vs. DARFMMKL 86.67% on heart; RMKL 77.15% vs. 77.20% on diabetes), but all four two-step methods plus BFMKL and SimpleMKL fail to complete within 24 hours on a8a and w7a, whereas DARFMMKL reports 84.43% and 98.57% respectively. BFMKL is competitive on splice (87.26% vs. 87.32%) and ionosphere (95.01% vs. 95.07%) but trails by 2–6 pp on the remaining datasets. The largest single-dataset gap is on mux6-ver1, where DARFMMKL (95.07%) exceeds the best baseline ABMKL (88.72%) by 6.35 percentage points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three established threads: the MKL paradigm of learning weighted kernel combinations (Lanckriet et al., 2004; Rakotomamonjy et al., 2008; Gönen & Alpaydin, 2011), CKA-based kernel quality assessment (Cortes et al., 2010), and ensemble diversity principles (Kuncheva & Whitaker, 2003). Its key departure is twofold: it introduces a data-driven kernel family (RFM kernels with AGOP-learned Mahalanobis metrics, building on Radhakrishnan et al., 2024) that replaces the uniform feature weighting of standard RBF/polynomial kernels, and it is the first MKL formulation to explicitly couple a pairwise disagreement diversity term with a CKA quality term inside a single selection objective. The NP-hard BQP is made tractable via Glover linearization and Nyström sketching, both well-known tools repurposed here to decouple selection cost from sample size.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in making diversity-aware kernel selection scalable to large datasets (n up to 581K) where dense-kernel MKL baselines time out, while the covering-number generalization bound provides the first explicit theoretical link between kernel diversity and estimation error in the MKL setting. Limitations include the approximate nature of the BQP-to-LP relaxation, the uniform-averaging combination rule (which decouples selection from downstream weight tuning), and the absence of a unified end-to-end excess-risk theorem spanning all three pipeline components (selection, RFM learning, sketching). Open questions include whether the RFM kernel family and diversity-aware selection transfer to high-dimensional deep-learning-adjacent tasks (e.g., text, vision) where the candidate kernel pool structure differs substantially from tabular benchmarks.
