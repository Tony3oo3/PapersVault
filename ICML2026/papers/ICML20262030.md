## Problem

Object-Centric Learning methods like Slot Attention assume a static, single-task environment, yet real-world deployment requires sequentially acquiring new object categories without catastrophic forgetting. The mechanism by which Slot Attention organizes its latent space across sequential tasks—and whether this organization can be exploited to prevent inter-task interference—remains unexplored. This paper identifies and leverages an intrinsic property of Slot Attention (Factor-Wise Homogeneity) to address this gap in Continual Object-Centric Learning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled multi-object RGB images where objects belong to a specific, task-exclusive class set (e.g., novel shape classes in C-Tetrominoes or C-CLEVR). The encoder produces D-dimensional patch features, the slot attention module iteratively refines K slot vectors (each D_slots-dimensional) via cross-attention and GRU updates, and a spatial broadcast decoder independently decodes each slot into a spatial map to reconstruct the full image. Evaluation is performed via Foreground Adjusted Rand Index (FG-ARI) on predicted object masks, with additional metrics including MSE, Segmentation Covering, and linear probing accuracy on frozen slots.

## Method

The paper discovers that Slot Attention exhibits Factor-Wise Homogeneity: its latent space organizes into small, well-separated regions where slots sharing identical semantic factors (shape, position, color) cluster together, and this separation persists robustly across sequential tasks with novel factors. Jacobian matrix analysis and GRU slow-mode analysis identify the Gated Recurrent Unit as the core architectural mechanism driving this property, as it enforces object-conditioned updates that align identical factors along stable hidden dimensions while separating differing factors. To exploit this separation for continual learning, the authors propose Decoder-only Post Replay (DPR), which freezes the encoder and Slot Attention module (preserving the homogeneous latent structure) and fine-tunes only the spatial broadcast decoder on a balanced mixture of replayed reconstructions from previous tasks and current-task data, applied after initial training on the new task. This minimal strategy addresses the decoder bottleneck—where the decoder overfits to current-task visual statistics—without disrupting the intrinsic inter-task separation, yielding substantial recovery of past-task performance (e.g., +57 FG-ARI on C-Tetrominoes SST) while maintaining current-task accuracy.

## Results

On the C-Tetrominoes SST benchmark, vanilla Slot Attention suffers severe catastrophic forgetting (FG-ARI drops from 99.86 to 41.71 on the previous task), while DPR restores performance to 98.81 (+57 points) without compromising the current task (99.70). On C-CLEVR SST, DPR similarly recovers FG-ARI from 63.83 to 92.00 (+28 points). On real-world COCO with 4 sequential tasks, DPR†† (with DINOv2 backbone) improves FG-ARI from 21.25 to 23.03 on the initial task after training through all tasks, and on PASCAL VOC from 14.63 to 16.69.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MONet (Burgess et al., 2019), which lacks factor-wise homogeneity, DPR yields only a marginal +1 point gain (43.51→44.41 FG-ARI on E0/T1), confirming that the separation property is essential for DPR to work. BO-QSA (Jia et al., 2023), an advanced Slot Attention variant that retains factor-wise homogeneity, achieves a large +48 point gain (50.46→96.54), closely matching Slot Attention's +57. On real-world COCO with 4 tasks, DPR†† (built on DINOSAUR/Seitzer et al., 2023) improves FG-ARI from 21.25 to 23.03 and mBO^c from 42.14 to 43.47 on the initial task. Against DDGR (Gao & Liu, 2023) on COCO, DPR† (using diffusion-generated replay) achieves 24.03 FG-ARI on E3/T1, comparable to DDGR's 24.08, while the combined DPR†† (DDGR memory + DPR replay) reaches the best 24.25, demonstrating that the two methods are complementary rather than competing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Slot Attention (Locatello et al., 2020) and its variants (MONet, BO-QSA) to investigate an underexplored intersection: continual object-centric learning (COCL). While prior OCL work focused on static or zero-shot generalization (Dittadi et al., 2021; Didolkar et al., 2025) and prior continual learning work addressed task-wise separation through regularization or full-model replay (Buzzega et al., 2020a; Zhu et al., 2021), this paper departs by identifying an *intrinsic* architectural property of Slot Attention—factor-wise homogeneity driven by GRU slow modes—that naturally provides inter-task separation without any explicit continual learning mechanism. The proposed Decoder-only Post-Replay (DPR) strategy is positioned as a minimal intervention that exploits this property, contrasting with standard experience replay or generative replay that modify the full model during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first systematic analysis and benchmarks for continual object-centric learning, establishing a foundational understanding of why Slot Attention resists catastrophic forgetting (GRU-induced factor-specific slow modes) and showing that a minimal decoder-only replay strategy is sufficient to recover past-task performance. The identification of factor-wise homogeneity as a functional (not merely geometric) property opens a path toward designing object-centric architectures with built-in continual learning robustness. However, the primary evidence is on synthetic datasets (Tetrominoes, CLEVR) with simple geometric objects; real-world gains on COCO and PASCAL are more modest, and the paper acknowledges that representation collapse between distinctive features remains an open challenge. Future work should explore whether factor-wise homogeneity generalizes to more complex, naturalistic object categories and whether the GRU mechanism can be explicitly designed or replaced with more interpretable alternatives.
