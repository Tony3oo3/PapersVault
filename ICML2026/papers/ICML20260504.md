## Problem

Existing multimodal generative recommendation methods rely on alignment-centric fusion that captures redundant and modality-specific signals but systematically underexploits synergistic information—emergent item semantics that arise only from cross-modal interaction. An information-theoretic (PID) analysis reveals that the information density mismatch between discrete textual tokens and continuous visual embeddings causes models to collapse into unimodal shortcuts, leaving the synergistic component entirely untargeted by standard generation objectives.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260504/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a user's historical interaction sequence where each item is encoded as a unified discrete token stream: D-level RQ-VAE quantized codes from both text (LLaMA embeddings) and vision (ViT-L/14 embeddings), distinguished by lowercase/uppercase modality prefixes within a shared vocabulary. The output is the next item's discrete semantic identifier sequence, generated autoregressively via beam search (size 20) over the full item space. At inference, the synergistic regularization is absent, so decoding incurs zero additional computational cost over a vanilla Transformer backbone.

## Method

SynGR introduces two mechanisms to isolate synergistic information during generation. First, a saliency-aware masking mechanism extracts attention maps from the encoder to compute per-token saliency scores, identifies the dominant modality by average saliency density, and masks the top-r most salient tokens in that modality—forcing the model to reconstruct item semantics through cross-modal interaction rather than unimodal shortcuts. Second, a synergistic contrastive loss (InfoNCE-style) is applied over three parallel views (original, masked anchor, unimodal negative) processed by a shared-parameter T5 backbone, pulling the masked representation toward the holistic original while pushing it away from unimodal shortcut representations. The total objective combines multi-view autoregressive NLL (preserving redundant and unique information) with the contrastive synergy term weighted by λ. The framework is pre-trained on ~7.1M interactions across six Amazon categories before fine-tuning on target domains, using a 4-layer T5 encoder-decoder (6 heads, d=64) with RQ-VAE tokenization (4 levels, codebook size 256).

## Results

SynGR achieves the best performance across all 15 metric-dataset combinations (HR@1/5/10, NDCG@5/10 on Arts, Games, and Instruments), outperforming the strongest baseline MACRec by an average of ~10%. The largest gains appear on Instruments, where SynGR improves HR@10 by 28.58% (0.1768 vs. 0.1363) and NDCG@10 by 12.17% (0.1189 vs. 0.1046) over MACRec. On Games, SynGR achieves a 17.79% relative gain in HR@1 (0.0245 vs. 0.0208). SynGR also trains ~22–23% faster and converges ~8 epochs earlier than MACRec while maintaining zero additional inference overhead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SynGR outperforms all 12 baselines on every metric across all three datasets. Against the strongest baseline MACRec (AAAI'26), SynGR achieves relative gains of +4.09% to +28.58% in HR and +5.49% to +12.17% in NDCG, with the largest margin on Instruments (HR@10: 0.1768 vs. 0.1363, +28.58%). Against MQL4GRec (ICLR'25), the second-strongest generative baseline, SynGR leads by up to +28.58% (Instruments HR@10) and +17.79% (Games HR@1). The earlier generative method TIGER (NeurIPS'23) trails SynGR by 33.9% on Arts HR@1 (0.0532 vs. 0.0713) and 57.1% on Games HR@1 (0.0166 vs. 0.0245). Traditional sequential recommenders (GRU4Rec, SASRec, BERT4Rec, FDSA, S³-Rec) and the text-to-text P5-CID are substantially outperformed, with SynGR exceeding the best among them (FDSA) by 87.9% on Arts HR@1. The multimodal sequential method MISSRec and the multimodal foundation model VIP5 are also clearly surpassed, confirming that the generative paradigm with explicit synergy exploitation yields consistent advantages over both discriminative and alignment-centric multimodal approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

SynGR extends the generative recommendation paradigm established by TIGER (RQ-VAE tokenization + autoregressive decoding) and the multimodal GR line of work (MQL4GRec, MACRec) that discretizes visual and textual features into a unified token vocabulary. Its key departure is from alignment-centric fusion: prior methods enforce cross-modal consistency to capture redundant semantics, whereas SynGR explicitly targets the synergistic component identified via Partial Information Decomposition, using saliency-aware masking to suppress dominant-modality shortcuts and a contrastive objective to regularize the latent space toward cross-modal interaction. The theoretical grounding in PID (Kolchinsky, 2022) and the synergy-preserving transformation (Wen et al., 2025) provide a formal justification for why alignment alone is insufficient.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete 'synergy gap' in multimodal GR—empirically showing that even SOTA methods capture only ~7.5% synergistic information—and offers a practical mechanism to close it, yielding ~10% average gains with lower training/inference cost than MACRec. However, the framework is limited to text+image modalities and item-side semantics; incorporating collaborative/interaction-driven signals and extending to richer modality combinations (audio, video) remain open. The PID-based lens, while elegant, relies on an inductive-bias argument rather than a strict finite-sample guarantee, and the masking ratio and contrastive weight require careful tuning per dataset.
