## Problem

Fixed-size block generation in diffusion LLMs is a critical bottleneck for reasoning: different tasks require different optimal block sizes, and rigid partitioning disrupts logical flow within a single reasoning trace, causing high-entropy anomalies and incorrect answers. The paper identifies through empirical analysis that correct reasoning exhibits a monotonic descending trend in block-wise entropy while incorrect reasoning shows fluctuating or stagnant entropy, yet no existing method learns to adapt block boundaries to semantic reasoning steps.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (e.g., a math word problem or Countdown puzzle) and produces a reasoning trace segmented into dynamic-size blocks, where each block boundary is determined by a learned indicator token (τ_end) marking the end of a reasoning step, followed by a final answer in a structured format. Unlike fixed-size baselines that partition the sequence into uniform 32-token chunks, b1 generates blocks of varying lengths (observed ranging from 26 to 35 tokens in case studies) that align with complete reasoning steps. Generation operates within 256 or 512 token budgets on LLaDA-8B-Instruct using parallel denoising within each block.

## Method

b1 is a plug-and-play RL post-training framework that adds two intrinsic rewards to existing dLLM RL objectives (GRPO, wd1, d1, GDPO): a block entropy reward R_ent enforcing Monotonic Entropy Descent (MED) across blocks, and a block ending indicator reward R_ind encouraging sufficient multi-step reasoning. The MED reward is the fraction of adjacent block pairs where mean token-wise Shannon entropy decreases, serving as a local surrogate for the global negative Spearman's rank correlation coefficient; Theorem 1 proves that maximizing R_ent is mathematically equivalent to maximizing r_SCC, guaranteeing strictly monotonic entropy descent. During both training and inference, block boundaries are determined dynamically by the first appearance of a learned indicator token τ_end within the iterative denoising process, so each block encapsulates one complete reasoning step without rigid truncation. The framework requires no architectural modifications and integrates as an additional reward term (weight 1) into any existing dLLM RL pipeline, with negligible computational overhead (O(K·T·L) for boundary construction plus O(L) for entropy calculation).

## Results

On the Countdown benchmark (256 tokens), b1 integrated with wd1 achieves 58.98% accuracy, a +19.53-point improvement over wd1's fixed-size baseline (39.45%). On Sudoku, b1+wd1 reaches 27.29% (+4.15 over wd1), and on MATH500 it achieves 37.40% (+3.20 over wd1). Across all four reasoning benchmarks and three RL backbones (Diffu-GRPO, GDPO, d1, wd1), b1 consistently outperforms the corresponding fixed-size baselines, with the largest gains on Countdown (up to +19.53) and Sudoku (up to +4.15).

## Baselines & Comparisons

b1 is evaluated as a plug-and-play module on top of four RL frameworks (Diffu-GRPO, GDPO, d1, wd1) and compared against two non-RL baselines (AdaBlock-dLLM and SFT). Against the strongest RL baseline wd1, b1 achieves the largest gain on Countdown (+19.53 at 256 tokens, from 39.45 to 58.98) and Sudoku (+4.15, from 23.14 to 27.29), with more modest but consistent improvements on GSM8K (+1.97) and MATH500 (+3.20). Against d1, b1 yields +5.08 on Countdown and +4.88 on Sudoku (512 tokens). The inference-time method AdaBlock-dLLM, which truncates blocks at newline characters without learning, actually underperforms the base LLaDA model on Countdown (14.84 vs 16.80) and Sudoku (6.69 vs 7.67), confirming that heuristic block splitting without learned dynamic boundaries is insufficient. SFT also fails to improve over the base model on most benchmarks, highlighting the training-inference gap when block-based generation is not part of the training objective. b1 wins on every benchmark against every RL baseline it is integrated with, with no observed losses.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of RL post-training for diffusion LLMs (d1, wd1, GDPO, Diffu-GRPO), all of which operate under a fixed-size block generation paradigm inherited from LLaDA's semi-autoregressive inference. b1 departs from this by introducing a learned block-ending indicator token and a Monotonic Entropy Descent (MED) objective that jointly determine dynamic block boundaries aligned with reasoning steps. It also contrasts with inference-time adaptive methods like AdaBlock-dLLM, which truncate at newlines using confidence thresholds without any training signal, by making block sizing a learned, RL-optimised property of the policy itself. The theoretical contribution connects the local pairwise entropy-drop reward to the global negative Spearman rank correlation via the Rearrangement Inequality, providing a principled relaxation of an otherwise intractable global monotonicity objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work identifies and addresses a structural mismatch in dLLM reasoning: rigid block boundaries can sever mid-calculation, producing entropy anomalies that correlate with incorrect outputs. The plug-and-play design (simply adding R_ent and R_ind to any existing dLLM RL objective) lowers the adoption barrier, and the ~19.5% gain on Countdown with wd1 demonstrates that block coherence is a first-order factor for dLLM reasoning, not a minor tuning detail. Limitations include evaluation on a single 8B backbone (LLaDA), a relatively small set of reasoning benchmarks, and the need for a manually set K_target to prevent reward hacking via indicator spam. Open questions include whether MED generalises to non-reasoning generation tasks, whether the approach transfers to larger or non-block dLLM architectures (e.g., Dream), and whether the entropy-descent signal remains informative as model scale increases.
