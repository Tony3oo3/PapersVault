## Problem

Monolithic deep search agents suffer from cognitive overload as accumulated retrieval contexts dilute attention, forcing a single model to simultaneously navigate, filter noise, and reason. Existing multi-agent decompositions address this structurally but remain training-free, relying on frozen LLMs orchestrated by hand-crafted SOPs, and thus cannot learn from collaboration failures or solve the multi-agent credit assignment problem of attributing success or failure to specific agents' actions.

## Input / Output

The system takes a natural-language user query and iteratively interacts with a dense retrieval engine (E5 embeddings over 2018 Wikipedia, top-k=5 documents per step, max 4 search turns). Each agent receives a structurally constrained partial observation: the Planner sees only the query, curated memory, and past queries (never raw documents); the Filter sees raw retrieved documents plus the current search query; and the Answerer sees only the query and the final curated memory pool (capped at 4K tokens). The output is a single concise text answer generated by the Answerer after the search loop terminates.

## Method

DECOR formulates deep search as a Multi-Agent POMDP with three role-specialized agents (Planner, Filter, Answerer) sharing a single Qwen3-8B backbone, where the Filter explicitly curates a noise-reduced memory pool to shield the Answerer from irrelevant context. Training uses a hybrid reward that hierarchically combines format compliance, a team-level outcome signal (weighted F1 + LLM-as-a-Judge semantic score), and dense role-specific intrinsic rewards from an LLM judge, resolving credit assignment by giving each agent a distinct local signal. Policy optimization adapts GRPO-style group sampling (G=8 trajectories per query) with role-specific advantage normalization and DAPO-inspired asymmetric clipping, so that the shared parameterization allows team-level rewards to back-propagate across the entire reasoning chain while role-specific rewards drive self-organized specialization without explicit inter-agent communication gradients.

## Results

DECOR achieves the best LLM-as-a-Judge (LJ) accuracy across all seven benchmarks, with headline scores of 53.2% on NQ, 71.4% on TriviaQA, 59.8% on HotpotQA, 62.0% on 2WikiMQA, 24.6% on MuSiQue, 51.7% on Bamboogle, and 54.3% on PopQA. On lexical F1, DECOR leads on NQ (52.5), TriviaQA (70.0), HotpotQA (58.3), and PopQA (53.5), while trailing Search-R1 by 1.7 points on Bamboogle F1 (50.3 vs. 52.5) and by 0.7 points on MuSiQue F1 (23.8 vs. 24.5). The consistent LJ advantage over F1-parity baselines indicates DECOR produces semantically more accurate answers even when rigid string matching favors monolithic retrievers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest monolithic baseline Search-R1, DECOR wins on LJ across all seven datasets (e.g., +1.9 on NQ, +7.8 on MuSiQue, +4.5 on Bamboogle) but concedes small F1 margins on MuSiQue (23.8 vs. 24.5) and Bamboogle (50.3 vs. 52.5), suggesting Search-R1 retrieves text chunks with higher lexical overlap while DECOR produces more semantically faithful answers. R1-Searcher is the closest F1 competitor on 2WikiMQA (61.8 vs. 61.2) yet trails DECOR by 1.1 LJ points there and by 4.5–7.4 LJ points on the remaining datasets. CoRAG, the weakest baseline, is outperformed by DECOR by 11–31 LJ points across all benchmarks, highlighting the cost of its iterative self-correction loop without learned filtering. o2-searcher, which relies on inference-time compute scaling, is surpassed by DECOR on every metric, with the largest gaps on MuSiQue (+9.1 LJ) and HotpotQA (+13.4 LJ). SimpleDeepSearcher and AutoRefine, representing foundational ReAct-style pipelines, are consistently outperformed by DECOR by 3–12 LJ points, confirming that learned multi-agent decomposition outperforms static single-agent loops.

## Relation to Prior Work

DECOR sits at the intersection of three research threads: monolithic deep-search agents (ReAct, Search-R1, R1-Searcher) that suffer from cognitive overload; training-free multi-agent orchestration frameworks (MetaGPT, AutoGen, STORM, DyLAN) that decompose tasks but cannot learn from collaboration failures; and group-based RL methods for reasoning (GRPO, DAPO, GSPO) that optimize single policies but lack mechanisms for multi-agent credit assignment. The paper's core departure is formulating deep search as a heterogeneous MA-POMDP and applying joint policy optimization with a hybrid reward (team-level F1/LLM-Judge outcome plus role-specific intrinsic signals) to resolve which agent caused a failure. This is, per the authors, the first application of heterogeneous MARL specifically tailored to the deep-search pipeline, bridging the static multi-agent and single-agent RL literatures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Filter agent's demonstrated robustness to increasing retrieval volume (maintaining gains at k=10 where monolithic baselines degrade) provides concrete evidence that dedicated noise filtering is a practical remedy for the lost-in-the-middle problem, not merely a theoretical decomposition. However, the framework incurs higher inference latency and token costs from multi-agent interactions, and all experiments use a single 8B backbone, leaving open whether the collaborative gains persist at larger scales or with architecturally heterogeneous agents. Safety alignment for autonomous multi-agent search (e.g., preventing harmful content gathering) is flagged but not addressed, and the credit-assignment solution relies on an LLM-as-a-Judge whose own biases could propagate into training signals.
