## Problem

Large reasoning models generate long, coherent reasoning traces yet still produce hallucinated answers, making detection difficult. Existing approaches that probe vanilla hidden states or trace text directly are brittle: they overfit to superficial stylistic patterns rather than answer-level validity, and sampling-based consistency methods incur heavy test-time overhead. The paper asks whether reasoning trajectories can be leveraged to shape detection-friendly answer representations that explicitly encode answer stability under small internal perturbations.

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen LRM's prompt, reasoning trajectory, and final answer as input, extracting the trace-boundary hidden state (last-token embedding at the penultimate layer) and the vanilla last-token answer embedding. It produces a shaped trace-conditioned answer embedding z = g_φ(u) ∈ R^k via a single linear projection (no bias), which is then scored by plug-and-play embedding-based detectors (CCS, supervised probing, HaloScope, EigenScore) to yield a hallucination detection score. Critically, no test-time sampling or human annotations are required; all perturbations are used only during training to shape the representation.

## Method

ARS generates counterfactual answers by adding small Gaussian perturbations to the trace-boundary hidden state (the last-token embedding of the reasoning trace at the penultimate layer) and resuming autoregressive decoding, then labels each counterfactual by whether its answer agrees with the original. A lightweight linear mapping g_φ is trained with a contrastive-style objective that pulls answer-agreeing embeddings closer to the anchor while pushing answer-disagreeing embeddings apart, using cosine similarity with a temperature parameter. The key design choice is intervening precisely at the trace boundary—where the model has incorporated the full reasoning trajectory but retains maximal freedom to determine the answer—rather than mid-trace (which destabilizes reasoning) or post-answer (which yields only superficial edits). The base LRM parameters remain frozen throughout; only the small shaping head is optimized. A theoretical result (Proposition 4.2) bounds the hallucination detection error of a supervised probe on shaped embeddings in terms of the agreement-separation probability promoted by the training objective and the informativeness of the stability score for truthfulness.

## Results

On Qwen3-8B, ARS with CCS achieves 86.64% AUROC on TruthfulQA, 88.54% on TriviaQA, 90.37% on GSM8K, and 78.66% on MATH-500, representing a 19.79% absolute gain over vanilla LRM embeddings (66.85%) on TruthfulQA. ARS with supervised probing reaches 91.62% on TriviaQA and 89.88% on GSM8K. On DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B, ARS(CCS) achieves 80.89% (TruthfulQA), 88.86% (TriviaQA), 74.72% (GSM8K), and 86.38% (MATH-500). ARS requires only a single forward pass at test time (no multi-sample generation) and no human annotations during training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ARS(CCS) outperforms the strongest general-purpose baseline TSV (Park et al., 2025) by +9.56 on TruthfulQA (86.64 vs. 77.08), +7.22 on GSM8K (90.37 vs. 83.15), and +15.54 on MATH-500 (78.66 vs. 63.12), though it trails TSV by −1.13 on TriviaQA (88.54 vs. 89.67). ARS(Probing) beats TSV on all four datasets, including TriviaQA (91.62 vs. 89.67). Among LRM-specific detectors, ARS surpasses G-Detector (Zhang et al., 2026) by +14.78 on TruthfulQA and +20.99 on MATH-500, and RACE (Wang et al., 2025) by +19.07 on TruthfulQA and +17.82 on GSM8K. All uncertainty-based (Perplexity, Semantic Entropy), consistency-based (Lexical Similarity, SelfCKGPT), and verbalized (Verbalized Certainty) baselines are substantially outperformed, with gaps exceeding 20 AUROC points on most datasets. Notably, ARS(CCS) requires zero human annotations and a single forward pass, whereas TSV and G-Detector both require supervision and multiple generations or graph construction.

## Relation to Prior Work

ARS builds on the representation-based hallucination detection line (Azaria & Mitchell 2023; Burns et al. 2023; Du et al. 2024; Chen et al. 2024a) but departs from it by introducing a training-time shaping step that reorganizes answer embeddings via a contrastive, agreement-driven objective. It addresses a gap identified by Cheng et al. (2025) and Sun et al. (2025) that reasoning traces in LRMs can obscure rather than clarify hallucination signals, and it differs from prior LRM-specific detectors (RACE, RHD, G-Detector) by treating the trace purely as a conditioning context for latent intervention rather than as a text to be scored or parsed. The latent-intervention mechanism draws conceptually from counterfactual reasoning and causal perturbation, but its application at the trace boundary to generate answer-agreement supervision is novel; the theoretical analysis (Proposition 4.2) connects the shaping objective to a bounded detection error via an agreement-separation quantity, which has no direct analogue in prior hallucination detection work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ARS offers a practically attractive recipe: a single linear projection trained with a contrastive loss on automatically generated counterfactual pairs, requiring no human labels, no test-time sampling, and no modification to the base LRM, yet yielding 15–30 AUROC-point gains over vanilla embeddings across four datasets and two model families. The finding that reasoning traces can actively degrade detection (Figure 1) and that text-space interventions (especially paraphrasing) are near-useless for shaping (Table 4) are useful negative results that redirect future work toward latent-space signals. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-turn or streaming reasoning settings, how sensitive it is to the agreement function (currently an LLM judge), and whether the stability signal captured by α generalizes to hallucination types beyond factual incorrectness (e.g., logical inconsistency within a trace).
