## Problem

Transformer-based scRNA-seq clustering methods compute self-attention over all cell pairs, yielding O(n²) time and space complexity that becomes infeasible for datasets with hundreds of thousands of cells. GNN-based alternatives similarly require O(n²) kNN graph construction. The paper addresses this scalability bottleneck by shifting similarity computation from cell-to-cell to cell-to-anchor interactions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262686/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a preprocessed scRNA-seq gene expression matrix (n cells × d genes, typically top 1,500 highly variable genes after log-normalization) and produces cell cluster labels via a DEC objective along with learned cell embeddings. Training uses mini-batches of 1,000 cells, but a fixed set of m learnable anchor tokens (m ≤ 2,048) is shared across all batches, so the model retains global context even when individual cells are never co-processed. The final embedding dimension is 512 with 4 attention heads.

## Method

BGFormer introduces m learnable anchor tokens that act as shared reference points, replacing the full n×n self-attention matrix with an n×m bipartite attention matrix between cells and anchors. Anchor tokens are optimized via a ZINB-based reconstruction loss (reconstructing raw expression profiles from the nearest anchor) plus a commitment loss, ensuring they encode global cellular information. The bipartite graph attention maps cells to a query space and anchors to key/value spaces, with multi-head variants capturing complementary cell-anchor relationships; a residual connection adds the original cell features. The total training objective combines a self-supervised reconstruction loss, the DEC clustering loss, and the anchor reconstruction loss. A theoretical analysis using the Johnson-Lindenstrauss lemma shows the bipartite attention approximates full self-attention within a bounded error, and the overall complexity drops from O(n²d) to O(nmd), which is linear in n since m ≪ n.

## Results

BGFormer achieves the best ACC and ARI across all 7 scRNA-seq datasets, with headline results of 80.20% ACC / 86.80% ARI on Chen, 91.64% ACC / 90.03% ARI on Bach, and 89.54% ACC / 90.24% ARI on MRCA. On the largest datasets (Ratmap: 504K cells, Astrocyte: 598K cells), BGFormer attains 64.10% ACC / 52.17% ARI and 70.34% ACC / 50.41% ARI respectively, while most GNN-based baselines (scGNN, scTAG, CCST, scTPF, scGCL, scG-cluster) and scICE fail entirely with out-of-memory errors. BGFormer also runs 2× faster than scSimGCL during training and 10–20× faster during inference on large-scale datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BGFormer outperforms all 14 baselines on both ACC and ARI across all 7 datasets. On the small-scale Chen dataset (12K cells), BGFormer achieves 80.20% ACC, surpassing the best GNN baseline CCST (75.14%) by 5.06 points and scG-Cluster (73.26%) by 6.94 points. On the large-scale datasets (HRCA, MRCA, Fetal-Atlas, Ratmap, Astrocyte; 330K–598K cells), six GNN-based methods (scGNN, scTAG, CCST, scTPF, scGCL, scG-cluster) and scICE all fail with out-of-memory errors, leaving only deep embedding methods (IDEC, scMDC, scDCC, MetaQ) and scSimGCL as viable comparisons. Among these scalable baselines, BGFormer leads by substantial margins: on Ratmap it achieves 64.10% ACC vs. scSimGCL's 41.16% (a 22.94-point gap), and on Astrocyte it reaches 70.34% ACC vs. scMDC's 61.52%. The Leiden algorithm, while competitive on Bach (90.58% ACC vs. BGFormer's 91.64%), degrades severely on HRCA (49.05% ACC) and Fetal-Atlas (52.49% ACC), where BGFormer achieves 68.18% and 60.22% respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

BGFormer sits at the intersection of Transformer-based scRNA-seq clustering (scGraphFormer, TOSICA) and the Perceiver-style cross-attention paradigm, replacing all-to-all cell self-attention with cell-to-anchor-token bipartite attention to reduce complexity from O(n²) to O(n). It builds directly on the DEC clustering objective (Xie et al., 2016) and the ZINB reconstruction framework (Yu et al., 2022), while departing from GNN-based methods that require explicit kNN graph construction and from deep embedding methods that encode cells independently. The theoretical grounding via the Johnson-Lindenstrauss lemma connects the learned bipartite attention to a low-rank approximation of full self-attention, providing a principled justification for the architectural choice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practical bottleneck: most Transformer- and GNN-based scRNA-seq clustering methods hit out-of-memory errors on datasets exceeding ~300K cells, limiting their use in modern large-scale atlases. The shared-anchor-token design is particularly well-suited to mini-batch training, preserving global context without requiring all cells in a single forward pass. Open questions include whether the fixed number of anchor tokens (512–1024) generalizes to datasets with extreme heterogeneity, whether the linear-decoding assumption in the theoretical analysis holds in practice, and whether the approach extends naturally to multi-omics or spatial transcriptomics settings where the bipartite structure might need modification.
