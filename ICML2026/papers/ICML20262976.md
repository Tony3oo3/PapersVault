## Problem

Uniform Cartesian voxelization in LiDAR-based 3D detectors assigns the same voxel size to all spatial regions, causing small objects like pedestrians and cyclists to occupy only a few voxels and lose fine-grained geometric detail. While globally increasing voxel resolution helps, it incurs prohibitive memory and compute costs, and existing non-uniform schemes (cylindrical, polar) disrupt the Cartesian topology required by mainstream sparse-convolution backbones. The paper seeks a simple, plug-and-play non-uniform discretization that preserves Cartesian grid structure while adaptively refining near-range resolution.

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw LiDAR point clouds (x, y, z, intensity) and outputs 3D bounding boxes with class labels. Before voxelization, each point is warped into a transformed (u, v, w) space and its feature vector is augmented with three RSV-specific attributes: normalized radial distance, log-normalized radial distance, and min-max normalized log of the local scaling factor. Predicted boxes are produced in the warped space and mapped back to original LiDAR coordinates via a Newton-Raphson inversion of the radial scaling, so downstream evaluation uses standard LiDAR-space IoU or center-distance metrics.

## Method

RSV applies a smooth exponential-decay radial scaling s(r) = 1 + (s_max − 1)·exp(−β·r/r_far) to the horizontal (x, y) coordinates before standard voxelization, yielding an effective voxel size of Δx = Δu/s(r) in LiDAR space that is finer near the sensor and nearly unchanged far away. The transformation is monotonic, preserves Cartesian grid topology (no rotation or shearing), and introduces no learnable parameters, making it a drop-in replacement for the discretization module in any voxel-based detector. Inverse mapping from warped to LiDAR coordinates is solved via Newton-Raphson on f(r) = s(r)·r − ρ = 0, converging in 5–8 iterations. Ground-truth boxes are warped to the transformed space for supervision using a center-based scaling approximation, and the original detection losses are computed unchanged in warped space. The approach is validated as architecture-agnostic across SECOND, PointPillars, Voxel-RCNN, VirConv, CenterPoint, and VoxelNeXt, with consistent gains of up to ~27 AP points for pedestrians under coarse voxel settings.

## Results

On the KITTI validation set, RSV consistently improves small-object detection across all four voxel-based baselines. The largest gains appear under coarser voxel settings: PointPillars with [0.32, 0.32, 4.0] m voxels gains +26.97 AP for Pedestrian and +17.79 AP for Cyclist, while SECOND with [0.1, 0.1, 0.1] m gains +8.15 and +9.32 AP respectively. On nuScenes, RSV improves mAP by 0.7–4.3 points and NDS by 0.9–3.5 points over CenterPoint-Pillar and VoxelNeXt baselines, with the most notable per-category gains on Bicycle (+7.4), Pedestrian (+8.5), and Traffic Cone (+7.4) under the coarsest pillar setting. Inference latency increases by only 0.27–11.97 ms per sample, and peak GPU memory remains substantially lower than globally doubling voxel resolution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RSV is evaluated as a plug-and-play add-on to six existing voxel-based detectors, and it wins on small-object metrics in every configuration tested. Against SECOND, RSV adds +8.15 Pedestrian AP and +9.32 Cyclist AP at 0.1 m voxels, narrowing to +1.01/+1.93 at 0.05 m voxels where the baseline already has finer resolution. PointPillars benefits the most dramatically: at the coarse 0.32 m pillar size, Pedestrian AP jumps from 24.01 to 50.98 (+26.97) and Cyclist from 48.03 to 65.82 (+17.79), while Car also improves by +4.51. Voxel-RCNN gains +18.68 Pedestrian AP at 0.1 m voxels and +5.70 at 0.05 m, with Cyclist improving by +4.05 and +0.73 respectively. VirConv-L shows +14.84 Pedestrian AP at 0.1 m voxels but only +4.10 at 0.05 m, with Cyclist essentially unchanged at the finest setting (−0.04). On nuScenes, CenterPoint-Pillar gains +4.3 mAP and +3.5 NDS at 0.4 m pillars (Bicycle +7.4, Pedestrian +8.5), while VoxelNeXt gains +1.7 mAP and +1.5 NDS at 0.15 m voxels. Car performance is largely preserved across all baselines (fluctuations within ±1.3 AP), confirming that RSV's gains are concentrated on small objects without degrading large-object detection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against the uniform Cartesian voxelization that underpins mainstream LiDAR detectors (SECOND, PointPillars, Voxel-RCNN, VirConv, CenterPoint, VoxelNeXt) and against prior non-uniform discretization schemes that adopt cylindrical or polar coordinates (Zhu et al. 2021; Wang et al. 2025) or heuristic adaptive grids (Wang et al. 2021; He et al. 2022). Its key departure is to preserve the Cartesian grid topology entirely: rather than re-partitioning space in a different coordinate system, RSV applies a smooth radial scaling to input coordinates before standard voxelization, making it a drop-in preprocessing step. Conceptually, it repurposes the well-known observation that finer voxels help small objects by making the effective resolution distance-dependent, but does so without any architectural modification to the backbone or detection head.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RSV offers a low-friction, architecture-agnostic way to close the small-object detection gap in voxel-based LiDAR pipelines, with gains of 5–27 AP points for pedestrians and cyclists across multiple detectors and both KITTI and nuScenes, while adding only a few milliseconds of latency. The ablation showing that cylindrical discretization actually underperforms uniform Cartesian (Table 6) challenges the common intuition that aligning the grid with the LiDAR scanning pattern is inherently beneficial, suggesting that Cartesian topology compatibility with sparse convolutions matters more than geometric alignment. Open questions include whether the exponential radial scaling is optimal versus other decay profiles, whether the center-based box approximation introduces systematic bias for very large or elongated objects, and whether the approach transfers to camera-only or multi-sensor fusion settings where the radial geometry differs.
