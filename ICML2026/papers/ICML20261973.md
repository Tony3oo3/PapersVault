## Problem

Population-level sustainable diet models treat sustainability as a fixed objective or hard constraint, ignoring inter-individual heterogeneity in dietary preferences and tolerance. Existing personalized food recommenders, in turn, model sustainability as an explicit user preference to learn, which is conceptually misaligned since individuals rarely make food choices with conscious sustainability awareness. The paper asks how to incorporate multi-dimensional sustainability requirements as learnable, user-specific constraints within a personalized recommendation framework without sacrificing preference accuracy.

## Input / Output

The model consumes structured recipe ingredient-quantity sequences (parsed into standardized triplets via GPT), recipe country labels mapped to UN M49 codes, and user-recipe rating histories from online recipe platforms. Sustainability indicators (nutrition, environment, economy, animal welfare) are pre-computed at the recipe level by mass-weighted aggregation of ingredient-level values from external databases. The output is a top-K recipe ranking scored by F(u,r) = ŷ_{u,r} − α_uᵀ(z_r − λ_u), where the preference score and a user-specific Lagrangian penalty jointly determine the final ordering; the learned per-user constraint thresholds λ_u and trade-off weights α_u are also produced as interpretable decision parameters.

## Method

The framework has three synergistic components. First, a Mixture-of-Experts Transformer with 4 experts encodes ingredient-quantity sequences into a sustainability-aware recipe embedding z_r via masked mean pooling per expert and a gating network over the sequence average; four task-specific regression heads predict sustainability indicators using an uncertainty-weighted multi-task loss that auto-balances gradient contributions across heterogeneous target scales. Second, a multi-interest attention mechanism learns preference scores from sequential user interactions by applying separate attention over identity, ingredient, and region views of the target recipe against the user's history, capturing heterogeneous taste profiles. Third, a personalized constraint-aware trade-off learns user-specific constraint thresholds λ_u and trade-off weights α_u, which enter the ranking score as a Lagrangian penalty α_uᵀ(z_r − λ_u) that dynamically penalizes recipes exceeding individual sustainability boundaries. The entire system is trained end-to-end with a joint objective combining rating MSE, BPR ranking loss, the sustainability penalty loss, and L2 regularization on constraint parameters.

## Results

On the SusDiet dataset (149k recipes, 179k users, 744k interactions), the proposed constraint-aware method achieves the best or near-best results across sustainability dimensions while maintaining competitive recommendation accuracy. At Top-20, it attains NDCG 0.0159 and Recall 0.0316 (slightly below MSSR's 0.0181/0.0312 and ICLRec's 0.0170/0.0340), while leading on Nutrition (37.59), Economy (4.94), and Animal Welfare (3.03). The method consistently outperforms all baselines on Economy and Animal Welfare across all K settings, and achieves the highest Nutrition score at K=5 (40.72) and K=10 (39.14).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against six non-LLM baselines (KNN, ICLRec, MSSR, HAFR, FGCN, GRAPE) and two LLM-based baselines (Phi-3, KERL). On recommendation accuracy (NDCG/Recall), MSSR and ICLRec slightly outperform the proposed method at Top-20 (NDCG 0.0181/0.0170 vs. 0.0159; Recall 0.0312/0.0340 vs. 0.0316), but the gap is small and the proposed method wins at K=5 and K=10. On sustainability, the proposed method dominates: it achieves the best Economy (4.94 vs. next-best ICLRec 5.70) and Animal Welfare (3.03 vs. next-best GRAPE 4.68) at Top-20, and the best Nutrition (37.59) across all non-LLM baselines. ICLRec achieves the lowest Environment score (59.54) but at the cost of substantially worse Nutrition (35.37) and Economy (5.70), illustrating the trade-off the constraint-aware approach resolves. GRAPE, the closest sustainability-oriented baseline, is outperformed on Economy (6.86 vs. 4.94) and Animal Welfare (4.68 vs. 3.03) while trailing on Nutrition (37.26 vs. 37.59). The LLM baselines underperform: Phi-3 is worst on all metrics, and KERL, while slightly ahead on NDCG@10 (0.0160 vs. 0.0136), falls far behind on Environment (76.88 vs. 67.87), Economy (9.58 vs. 4.87), and Animal Welfare (20.20 vs. 2.92) due to the absence of explicit constraint modeling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two existing lines of work: (1) macro-level sustainable diet optimization (Springmann et al., Chaudhary & Krishna, Wilson et al.), which treats sustainability as a population-level objective or hard constraint but ignores inter-individual heterogeneity, and (2) individual-level food recommendation systems (HAFR, FGCN, ICLRec, MSSR) and sustainability-oriented recommenders (GRAPE, GreenRec), which model sustainability as an explicit user preference dimension to be learned. The key conceptual departure is formulating sustainability as learnable, user-specific constraint thresholds via Lagrangian relaxation rather than as a preference signal, arguing that individuals rarely make dietary decisions with explicit sustainability awareness. Technically, the paper repurposes MoE Transformers (from NLP), multi-interest attention (from sequential recommendation), and uncertainty-weighted multi-task learning into a unified constraint-aware recommendation framework, and leverages GPT for large-scale ingredient structuring and sustainability indicator mapping in dataset construction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The constraint-aware formulation offers a more behaviorally grounded alternative to preference-based sustainability modeling, and the SusDiet dataset (~150k recipes, 180k users, four sustainability dimensions) provides a much-needed benchmark for multi-dimensional sustainable diet recommendation. However, several open questions remain: the learned constraints are inferred from historical interactions subject to exposure bias, preferences and constraints are assumed static, sustainability indicators carry inherent uncertainty, and all evaluation is offline with no validation of real-world behavioral impact or long-term adherence. The framework's potential to inform personalized dietary policy and public health interventions is promising but untested at scale.
