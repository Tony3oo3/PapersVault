## Problem

Rotation-based PTQ methods (e.g., QuaRot, SpinQuant) are widely used for W4A4 INT4 quantization but suffer severe performance collapse when applied to the emerging MXFP4 format, whose power-of-two (PoT) block scaling is fundamentally incompatible with global rotation's energy redistribution. No prior work had identified or explained this destructive interaction, leaving practitioners without guidance for deploying rotation-based quantization on MXFP4 hardware.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264048/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes FP16 LLM weights and calibration sequences (WikiText2, 128–800 sequences of length 2048) and produces MXFP4-quantized weights in the E2M1 format with E8M0 (PoT) per-block scale factors at a fixed group size of 32. The rotation is applied as a block-diagonal orthogonal matrix whose block dimension must match the quantization group size (32) for optimal performance; misaligned rotation dimensions (16 or 64) degrade accuracy. The output is a fully quantized W4A4 model ready for MXFP4-native inference on supported hardware (NVIDIA RTX 5090/B200, AMD Ryzen AI).

## Method

BRQ replaces the global D×D rotation matrix used by QuaRot/SpinQuant with a block-diagonal matrix R_block = diag(R_1, …, R_B), where each R_i ∈ R^{g×g} is an independent orthogonal (random Hadamard) transform applied within a single quantization block of size g=32. The key insight is that global rotation preserves total L2 energy but redistributes outlier energy into previously small-value channels, inflating the scales of the vast majority of regular blocks; under MXFP4's PoT scaling, this scale inflation causes quantization error to grow quadratically (MSE ∝ scale²) and the PoT rounding error grows exponentially, leading to catastrophic accuracy loss. By confining rotation within each block, BRQ suppresses local outliers without propagating energy across blocks, keeping regular-block scales intact. The block rotations are fused at the same three positions (R1, R2, R4) as SpinQuant, and weights are then quantized with GPTQ under MXFP4. Optionally, the block rotation matrices can be further optimized via Cayley SGD (BRQ_Spin), yielding additional gains on smaller models.

## Results

Under MXFP4 W4A4 quantization, BRQ (block-wise rotation + GPTQ) achieves the best perplexity and downstream accuracy across most models. On LLaMA-3 8B, BRQ reaches 7.14 WikiText2 PPL and 63.54% average zero-shot accuracy, surpassing the BINT4 baseline (7.40/63.12) and GPTQ (7.68/61.48). On the more challenging LLaMA-3.2 1B, BRQ reduces PPL from 12.78 (QuaRot+) to 11.95 and raises accuracy from 48.83% to 49.87%, the largest gap among all models tested. BRQ also outperforms SpinQuant (optimized global rotation) without requiring any rotation optimization, e.g., 7.14 vs. 7.62 PPL on LLaMA-3 8B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BRQ consistently outperforms GPTQ on both PPL and accuracy across all six models (e.g., 7.14 vs. 7.68 PPL on LLaMA-3 8B; 11.95 vs. 13.35 on LLaMA-3.2 1B). Against QuaRot+ (global random rotation + GPTQ), BRQ delivers the largest gains, reducing PPL by 0.54–0.85 and improving accuracy by 0.5–1.3 points, confirming that global rotation is structurally incompatible with MXFP4's PoT block scaling. BRQ also surpasses SpinQuant (optimized global rotation) without any rotation optimization: on LLaMA-3 8B, BRQ achieves 7.14 PPL vs. SpinQuant's 7.62, and on LLaMA-3.2 3B, 9.41 vs. 9.85. Compared to the BINT4 baseline, BRQ wins on 5 of 6 models in PPL (losing only on LLaMA-3.2 3B: 9.41 vs. 9.29) and on 4 of 6 in average accuracy. The paper also shows that even BRQ_Spin (block rotations optimized via SpinQuant's Cayley SGD) provides only marginal additional gains over random BRQ, suggesting the block-alignment itself is the critical fix rather than the specific rotation values.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the rotation-based quantization lineage (QuaRot, SpinQuant, QuIP#) that has become standard for INT4 W4A4 quantization, and on GPTQ's second-order compensation framework. Its key departure is identifying that the global rotation transform, which is a core component of state-of-the-art INT4 methods, is fundamentally incompatible with MXFP4's block-wise power-of-two scaling. Rather than proposing a new quantization paradigm, BRQ repositions the rotation granularity to align with MXFP4's 32-element blocks, effectively converting a global orthogonal transform into a block-diagonal one while preserving the denoising benefit of rotation within each block.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As MXFP4 becomes the de facto 4-bit inference standard across NVIDIA, AMD, Intel, and OpenAI's native FP4 models, understanding why widely-deployed rotation techniques fail under this format is critical for practitioners. The paper provides the first systematic diagnosis of this incompatibility (PoT scale granularity × rotation-induced energy redistribution) and a minimal, hardware-efficient fix that reduces online rotation cost from O(N²) to O(N×32). Open questions include whether optimized block rotations (BRQ_Spin) can close the remaining gap to FP16 on small models, and whether the granularity-alignment principle generalizes to future microscaling formats with different block sizes or non-PoT scales.
