## Problem

Standard inference-time activation steering for LLMs relies on additive vector injection, which uncontrolledly distorts hidden-state magnitudes, leading to representation collapse and degraded open-ended generation quality. This creates a fundamental trade-off: stronger additive interventions improve multiple-choice accuracy but destroy generation fluency. The paper asks whether a geometry-aware, norm-preserving steering primitive can break this trade-off.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265569/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference time, the method consumes the current token's hidden activation at a small set of selected layers (e.g., layer 24 for LLaMA-3.1-8B) along with a pre-computed unit prototype direction μ derived offline from contrastive positive/negative answer pairs. The vMF confidence gate computes a per-token, per-layer steering strength t ∈ [0,1] from the cosine alignment of the normalized activation with the truthful and hallucinated directions. Output is standard autoregressive text generation or likelihood-scored multiple-choice selection; the intervention is applied in-place during the forward pass with only ~2% throughput overhead.

## Method

Spherical Steering replaces additive activation edits with a norm-preserving geodesic rotation on the unit hypersphere S^{d-1}. Given a contrastive prototype direction μ_T (truthful) and its antipode μ_H (hallucinated), the method normalizes the current activation to the sphere, computes the angular distance θ to μ_T, and rotates along the shortest great-circle arc via Slerp by a fraction t, then restores the original magnitude. A von Mises-Fisher–based confidence gate maps the relative alignment scores (s_T, s_H) through a two-class softmax to a hallucination-lean score δ, which is thresholded and scaled to produce an input-adaptive t: tokens already aligned with truth receive t=0, while hallucination-leaning tokens receive larger rotations. The key geometric insight is that modern LLM architectures with RMSNorm stabilize activation magnitudes, making direction the salient semantic degree of freedom; by preserving norm and modifying only direction, the method achieves superior collapse-efficiency—extracting 8–10% higher accuracy gains per unit of effective-rank drop compared to additive steering—while simultaneously improving open-ended generation quality.

## Results

On TruthfulQA, Spherical Steering achieves 53.17% average multiple-choice accuracy on LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct (+15.01% over baseline) and 51.59% on Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct (+12.44% over baseline), while simultaneously improving open-ended generation quality (TRUE×INFO: 54.63% vs. 48.24% baseline on LLaMA; 77.84% vs. 74.40% on Qwen). Across six multiple-choice benchmarks (Table 2), Spherical Steering reaches 71.96% average accuracy on LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct, outperforming the best additive baseline (CAA at 65.68%) by +6.28 points, with particularly large gains on COPA (+11.00%) and StoryCloze (+10.06%). The method introduces only ~2% inference overhead (42.1 vs. 43.0 tokens/s on LLaMA).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Spherical Steering dominates all three activation-steering baselines on both MC accuracy and open-ended generation quality. Against ITI (Li et al., 2023), it gains +11.20% MC average on LLaMA (53.17% vs. 41.97%) and +8.48% TRUE×INFO (54.63% vs. 40.31%), where ITI's additive intervention degrades generation by 7.93 points below baseline. Against CAA (Rimsky et al., 2024), the MC advantage is +12.63% on LLaMA (53.17% vs. 40.54%) and +8.37% on Qwen (51.59% vs. 43.22%), while CAA's TRUE×INFO (49.66%) remains below Spherical Steering's (54.63%). Against SADI-HEAD (Wang et al., 2025b), Spherical Steering leads by +11.46% MC on LLaMA and +9.85% on Qwen, and also surpasses it on TRUE×INFO (54.63% vs. 51.18% on LLaMA; 77.84% vs. 69.09% on Qwen). The key differentiator is that all additive baselines exhibit a fundamental accuracy–generation trade-off (gaining MC accuracy while losing TRUE×INFO), whereas Spherical Steering achieves a Pareto improvement, gaining on both axes simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant activation-addition paradigm (ITI, CAA, SADI-HEAD) by reframing inference-time steering as a norm-preserving geodesic rotation on the hypersphere, motivated by the architectural prior that RMSNorm stabilizes magnitudes while encoding semantics in direction. It is conceptually adjacent to Angular Steering (Vu & Nguyen, 2025), which also performs rotations but in a projected 2D plane, and to HPR (Pham & Nguyen, 2024), which enforces norm preservation via Householder reflection but requires training; Spherical Steering is fully training-free and operates in the full representation space via Slerp. The contrastive prototype construction follows CAA's mean-difference recipe, but the vMF-based confidence gate draws from directional statistics to provide input-adaptive, selective intervention—a combination not previously explored in the activation-steering literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is a Pareto improvement: norm-preserving rotation simultaneously boosts multiple-choice accuracy (+10–15%) and open-ended generation quality (TRUE×INFO), breaking the accuracy–fluency trade-off that plagues additive methods. The effective-rank collapse-efficiency analysis provides a mechanistic explanation—rotation extracts more downstream gain per unit of representational degradation—giving practitioners a principled reason to prefer geometric updates. Open questions remain: layer selection is still heuristic (the paper acknowledges this), the method is validated primarily on 7–8B instruct models with a single contrastive axis (truthfulness), and whether the hyperspherical prior generalizes to architectures without explicit normalization layers is untested. The ~2% runtime overhead and composability with ICL make it immediately deployable, but broader behavioral steering (beyond truthfulness) and multi-layer calibration are left to future work.
