## Problem

GRPO-based post-training of flow matching models for text-to-image generation suffers from inaccurate advantage attribution: the final reward is uniformly assigned to every denoising step, but a superior final image does not guarantee a superior policy at each intermediate step. This mismatch introduces misleading gradient fluctuations and training instability, affecting approximately half of all steps as measured by a step-aware preference model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263076/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and produces a 720×720 RGB image via the FLUX.1 Dev flow matching model. During RL training, each generation trajectory consists of T=50 denoising steps in latent space, which are partitioned into variable-size chunks; the reward (HPSv3 or CLIP score) is computed on the final clean image and shared across all chunks. Evaluation uses a hybrid inference strategy where the first 30 steps use the trained model and the remaining 20 steps revert to the base model to mitigate reward hacking.

## Method

GCPO (Group Chunking Policy Optimization) shifts the RL policy optimization from the step level to the chunk level by treating consecutive flow matching steps as a single atomic action with a principled chunk-level importance ratio defined as the geometric mean of step-level ratios within the chunk. Chunk boundaries are determined adaptively using the inherent temporal dynamics of flow matching: the relative L1 distance between consecutive latents is differentiated, and consecutive steps sharing the same derivative sign are grouped, with recursion on higher-order derivatives until chunks are sufficiently small. An optional weighted sampling strategy assigns each chunk a probability proportional to its mean relative L1 distance, biasing training toward high-noise regions that contribute more to image quality. The approach retains the same outcome reward as GRPO (no process rewards) but smooths gradient fluctuations by applying a unified importance weight across all timesteps within a chunk, as formalized in a theoretical gradient analysis.

## Results

On preference alignment (HPDv2.1 test set), GCPO with weighted sampling achieves HPSv3 of 15.373 and ImageReward of 1.149, outperforming the FLUX base model (13.804 / 1.086) and both GRPO baselines (Dance-GRPO: 15.080 / 1.141; Flow-GRPO: 14.900 / 1.135). On the GenEval benchmark, GCPO without weighted sampling reaches an overall score of 0.69 (vs. 0.67 for both Dance-GRPO and Flow-GRPO), with notable gains on Counting (0.75 vs. 0.71) and Color Attributes (0.51 vs. 0.46). On DPG, GCPO without weighted sampling scores 86.60, surpassing Dance-GRPO (85.17) and Flow-GRPO (85.05). The paper reports up to 43% relative gains over GRPO across metrics, and a user study shows GCPO variants are preferred 72.5% of the time over Dance-GRPO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GCPO consistently outperforms all seven baselines. Against the FLUX base model, GCPO w/ ws improves HPSv3 by 1.57 points (15.373 vs. 13.804) and ImageReward by 0.063 (1.149 vs. 1.086). Against the two primary GRPO baselines, GCPO w/o ws leads Dance-GRPO by 0.156 HPSv3 (15.236 vs. 15.080) and Flow-GRPO by 0.336 HPSv3 (15.236 vs. 14.900); on GenEval, GCPO w/o ws achieves 0.69 overall versus 0.67 for both, with the largest gap on Color Attributes (0.51 vs. 0.46) and DPG (86.60 vs. 85.17/85.05). Against the additional baselines in Table 9, GCPO (15.236 HPSv3) edges out TP-GRPO (15.206), MixGRPO (15.128), and Pref-GRPO (14.868), confirming its advantage over both process-reward and pairwise-preference variants. The process-reward approach from Liang et al. (2025) underperforms standard Dance-GRPO on HPSv2 (0.348 vs. 0.365), validating the paper's argument that process rewards are currently unreliable and motivating the chunk-level approach instead.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GRPO-based post-training paradigm for flow matching (established by Dance-GRPO and Flow-GRPO) by shifting the optimization granularity from individual denoising steps to temporally-coherent chunks, drawing a direct conceptual analogy to action chunking in robotics (ACT, Zhao et al. 2023; Li et al. 2025b). It departs from process-reward approaches (Liang et al. 2025; Deng et al. 2026) that attempt to fix GRPO's inaccurate advantage attribution by introducing step-level reward signals, instead solving the same problem purely through the structure of the policy objective. The temporal-dynamics-guided chunking strategy is a novel contribution that leverages the prompt-invariant, step-dependent latent-space dynamics of flow matching (Wimbauer et al. 2024; Liu et al. 2025) to determine natural chunk boundaries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GCPO offers a practical, reward-model-agnostic fix for a fundamental weakness in GRPO-based T2I post-training (inaccurate advantage attribution affecting ~50% of steps), without requiring the expensive and currently unavailable step-level preference data that process-reward methods demand. The up-to-43% relative gain over strong baselines and consistent improvements across multiple benchmarks and reward models suggest chunk-level optimization could become a standard component of flow-matching RL pipelines. Open questions include how to combine heterogeneous rewards across different noise-level chunks, whether the weighted sampling strategy's structural instability in high-noise regions can be mitigated, and whether the approach generalizes beyond FLUX to other flow-matching architectures or video generation.
