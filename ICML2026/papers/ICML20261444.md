## Problem

Existing data-driven closure modeling methods learn low-dimensional latent variables via autoencoders trained with pointwise reconstruction losses that assume a fixed ordering of microscopic degrees of freedom. This assumption breaks down for inherently unordered systems such as interacting particle ensembles, where the same physical configuration can be presented under any of N! permutations, and standard index-wise losses penalize physically equivalent reconstructions differently. The paper addresses how to learn permutation-invariant closure variables and their macroscopic dynamics from unordered microstates without resorting to expensive set-matching or permutation-invariant pointwise losses.

## Input / Output

The method takes an unordered set of particle feature vectors X = {x^1, …, x^n} ⊂ R^d (e.g., 2D positions of 300–529 particles, or non-white pixel coordinates with grayscale intensity from polymer video frames) as input, where the set size n can vary across time steps and trajectories. A deterministic macroscopic extractor φ̄ computes the observable of interest z̄ (e.g., normalized pairwise energy, mixing ratio, or stretching length), and the learned encoder produces the closure variable ẑ. The output is an autoregressively rolled-out trajectory of the augmented macroscopic state z_t = [z̄_t, ẑ_t], from which the predicted z̄_t sequence is extracted for evaluation against ground truth.

## Method

The core insight is to replace pointwise reconstruction with distributional reconstruction: for each input set X, a target density q_X(x) = (1/|X|) Σ δ_ε(x − x^i) is induced as a Gaussian mixture centered at the observed points with bandwidth ε, and the decoder is trained to match this density rather than individual particles. The encoder is a DeepSet (elementwise MLP + mean pooling + set-level MLP) that maps the unordered set to a permutation-invariant latent ẑ, while the decoder is a conditional autoregressive rational quadratic spline normalizing flow that generates p_θ(x|ẑ). Training minimizes the KL divergence KL(q_X ‖ p_θ(·|ẑ)) estimated via Monte Carlo sampling from q_X, which is inherently permutation-invariant and scales as O(1) in the number of particles for the decoding step. The augmented state z = [z̄, ẑ] is then evolved by a learned SDE (or ODE in the deterministic case) parameterized by MLPs for drift and diffusion, trained in a second stage with negative log-likelihood. The two-stage procedure first trains the distribution-aware autoencoder, then freezes the encoder and trains the dynamics model on the resulting latent trajectories.

## Results

On the interacting particle system (deterministic), the proposed method achieves the lowest mean relative error (MRE) in the in-distribution setting (5.19×10⁻⁵) and the particle-count shift setting (5.22×10⁻⁵), outperforming the permutation-augmented MLP baseline (AE-Aug) by over 24× in-distribution. On the stochastic binary particle mixing task, the method achieves the lowest MMD across all three test regimes, with 1.09×10⁻² (in-dst), 5.70×10⁻² (diff-init), and 9.64×10⁻³ (diff-N), consistently beating all baselines. The method also demonstrates exact permutation invariance: predictions are identical under arbitrary particle reordering, unlike AE-Aug which produces noticeably different energy predictions under different permutations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four baselines: AE-Aug (MLP autoencoder with random permutation augmentation), AE-InvE (DeepSet encoder + MLP decoder with pointwise MSE), AE-InvE-CD (DeepSet encoder + MLP decoder with Chamfer distance), and InvE (DeepSet encoder without reconstruction, trained jointly with dynamics). On the interacting particle system, the proposed method achieves the best in-distribution MRE (5.19×10⁻⁵ vs. 1.25×10⁻³ for AE-Aug, a 24× improvement) and the best particle-count generalization (diff-N: 5.22×10⁻⁵ vs. 2.49×10⁻⁴ for AE-InvE). On the initialization-pattern shift (diff-init), AE-InvE-CD edges out the proposed method (3.90×10⁻⁴ vs. 4.38×10⁻⁴), though both substantially outperform AE-Aug (8.04×10⁻⁴). On the stochastic binary mixing task, the proposed method wins all three settings, with the largest margin over InvE (which suffers from latent collapse), achieving 1.09×10⁻² MMD in-distribution versus 1.43×10⁻¹ for InvE. The reconstruction-free InvE baseline consistently underperforms, confirming that the distributional reconstruction objective is critical for learning informative closure variables.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends closure modeling (learning low-dimensional latent variables to predict macroscopic observables) to the setting of unordered microstates, where prior methods (Champion et al., 2019; Chen & Li, 2024) assume a fixed indexing and use pointwise MSE reconstruction. It departs from the point-cloud reconstruction literature (Achlioptas et al., 2018; Yang et al., 2018), which also handles unordered inputs but decodes explicit point sets requiring expensive matching losses (Chamfer distance, Hungarian assignment). The key conceptual departure is replacing pointwise or set-matching reconstruction with a distributional objective: the decoder is a conditional normalizing flow (Durkan et al., 2019) trained via KL divergence against a Gaussian-smoothed empirical density, making the loss inherently permutation-invariant and independent of particle count.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method fills a practical gap for multiscale modeling of particle-based systems (fluids, polymers, interacting particles) where no canonical ordering exists, and it naturally handles variable-size inputs without retraining. A noted limitation is that the approach struggles with stiff systems where microstates are nearly indistinguishable yet macroscopic quantities change substantially (e.g., heating a solid), suggesting the method requires sufficient microstate diversity in training data. Open directions include tighter coupling of closure learning with dynamics prediction, alternative density models (score-based), and more expressive encoders (Set Transformers), though the current two-stage training and the representation-collapse issue in reconstruction-free baselines remain unresolved.
