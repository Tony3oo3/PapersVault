## Problem

Unified multimodal models exhibit an 'understanding-generation gap': they can parse user intent but fail to translate it into precise pixel-level manipulation. This gap produces two compounding bottlenecks in anything-to-image (X2I) tasks — an attention entanglement bottleneck where blind single-pass planning breaks on complex multi-intent prompts, and a visual refinement bottleneck where unstructured self-critique cannot efficiently correct compound errors. No prior work addresses both bottlenecks simultaneously within a single unified framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262817/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a text instruction of arbitrary complexity (ranging from simple attribute edits to multi-subject compositional scenes) together with zero, one, or multiple reference RGB images. It produces a single 512×512 output image, but the generation process is an interleaved autoregressive sequence of text tokens (evaluations, failure diagnoses, reflection prompts, sub-instructions) and image patch tokens. The model autonomously selects among three execution paths — direct generation, iterative self-reflection (≤3 rounds), or explicit multi-step decomposition — so the number of intermediate images and reasoning steps varies per sample.

## Method

InterVR equips the Emu3.5 backbone with native interleaved generation and dynamic self-evaluation, allowing it to switch between direct generation, structured self-reflection, and multi-step planning based on instruction complexity. A hierarchical data pipeline (using Qwen3-VL-235B as an independent ANALYZER/critic and Gemini-3-Pro-Image as a GENERATOR) synthesizes 50,000+ trajectories across the three modes, with strict human verification. Training is two-stage: (1) SFT with a selective loss-masking strategy that excludes failed intermediate images from the loss, so the model learns correction logic without memorizing artifacts; (2) GRPO-based RL with a composite reward (outcome + format + step-wise reasoning) plus an intra-group complexity penalty that, among trajectories achieving near-maximal reward, down-weights those with redundant image generations, thereby balancing fidelity against computational cost.

## Results

On KRIS-Bench (image editing), the method achieves an overall score of 80.18, surpassing the backbone Emu3.5 (73.75) by 6.43 points and even edging out GPT-4o (80.09), with particularly large gains in Procedural Knowledge (85.53 vs. 71.14 for Emu3.5). On OmniContext (X2I), it sets a new state of the art with an average of 9.35, outperforming Emu3.5 (8.82), GPT-4o (8.80), and Gemini-2.5-Flash (7.84). On GenEval (text-to-image), it achieves 0.89, beating Emu3.5 (0.86), VACoT (0.84), and FLUX.1-dev (0.82). The RL stage also reduces average generated images from 2.45 (SFT only) to 1.56, indicating improved computational efficiency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the backbone Emu3.5, the method wins decisively on all three benchmarks: KRIS-Bench overall 80.18 vs. 73.75 (+6.43), OmniContext 9.35 vs. 8.82 (+0.53), and GenEval 0.89 vs. 0.86 (+0.03), with the largest margin in Procedural Knowledge (85.53 vs. 71.14). Against GPT-4o, the method narrowly wins on KRIS-Bench overall (80.18 vs. 80.09) and OmniContext (9.35 vs. 8.80), but loses on KRIS-Bench Conceptual (74.83 vs. 81.37), indicating a remaining weakness in abstract conceptual reasoning. Against Gemini-2.5-Flash, the method wins on KRIS-Bench (80.18 vs. 77.29) and OmniContext (9.35 vs. 7.84). Among open-source unified models, the method substantially outperforms ReasonEdit-Q (80.18 vs. 61.57 on KRIS-Bench), BAGEL-thinking (80.18 vs. 60.18), and OmniGen2 (80.18 vs. 49.71), confirming that adaptive interleaved reasoning provides a significant advantage over fixed plan-then-generate or generate-then-reflect pipelines. On GenEval, the method surpasses specialized T2I generators FLUX.1-dev (0.89 vs. 0.82) and SD3-Medium (0.89 vs. 0.74), as well as CoT-based unified models VACoT (0.89 vs. 0.84) and Uni-CoT (0.89 vs. 0.83).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of reasoning-enhanced generation work (VACoT, Uni-CoT, ReasonEdit, T2I-R1) by replacing their rigid 'Plan-then-Generate' or 'Generate-then-Reflect' pipelines with an adaptive mechanism that lets a unified model autonomously select among direct generation, self-reflection, and multi-step planning. Conceptually, it draws on the CoT reasoning literature for LLMs and adapts it to the visual generation domain, while the RL stage borrows GRPO from the LLM reasoning literature and introduces a domain-specific intra-group complexity penalty to prevent over-reasoning. The data construction pipeline repurposes powerful external models (Qwen3-VL-235B as critic, Gemini-3-Pro-Image as generator) in a hierarchical escalation loop, which is a practical but somewhat unusual design choice compared to prior work that trains end-to-end.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and well-motivated failure mode (the understanding-generation gap) with a clean decomposition into two bottlenecks and a corresponding two-mode solution, making it a useful reference for anyone building reasoning-augmented unified models. However, several limitations temper the impact: the method is validated only on the Emu3.5 backbone at 512×512 resolution, the data pipeline depends on very strong external models whose capabilities may be partially distilled rather than genuinely learned, and the RL dataset is curated from existing benchmarks rather than introducing new evaluation scenarios. Open questions include whether the adaptive switching generalizes to other architectures (diffusion-based, hybrid), whether the complexity penalty transfers to higher-resolution or video settings, and whether the reliance on a 235B-parameter critic for data construction can be replaced by the model's own self-evaluation at scale.
