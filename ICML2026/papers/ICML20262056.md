## Problem

Language models consistently exhibit striking geometric structures in their internal representations—circles for calendar months, curved 1D manifolds for historical years, and linearly decodable spatial coordinates—yet no organizing principle explains why these patterns arise across diverse architectures. This paper identifies translation symmetry in pairwise co-occurrence statistics as the unifying mechanism driving the formation of these representational manifolds.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262056/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes pairwise co-occurrence statistics from a text corpus (2.72B tokens of English Wikipedia, V=25,000 vocabulary) and constructs the normalized co-occurrence matrix M*, from which a translation-symmetric kernel C(Δx) is estimated. For LLM validation, it consumes short text prompts (e.g., 'The month of the year is x') and extracts residual-stream activations from Gemma 2 2B. The output is a full analytical specification of the embedding geometry: parametric curves in PCA space with predicted amplitudes, frequencies, and phases for each Fourier mode, plus a closed-form bound on linear coordinate decoding error as a function of probe rank r.

## Method

The core mechanism is that when co-occurrence probability between two words depends only on their distance along a semantic continuum (translation symmetry), the resulting M* matrix inherits circulant (periodic BC) or Toeplitz (open BC) structure, which is diagonalized by Fourier modes. For periodic 1D lattices with an exponential kernel, the PCA-projected embeddings are sinusoidal with integer wavenumbers k_n = πn and amplitudes a_n = √(2σ/(1+σ²k_n²)), producing closed loops (circles). For open BC, a self-consistent quantization condition yields non-integer wavenumbers and open loops with 'ripples.' Robustness to perturbation (e.g., removing all month-month co-occurrences) is explained by a collective model: many seasonal words share a latent time-of-year variable, creating large eigenvalues in the PMI matrix whose eigenvectors are insensitive to small perturbations by the Davis-Kahan theorem. A linear decoding analysis shows the coordinate recovery error scales as r^{-1/D} with probe rank r, matching the truncated Fourier series error for the identity function.

## Results

The paper's central quantitative result is that the analytically predicted Fourier embedding geometry (amplitudes a_n = √(2σ/(1+σ²k_n²)) and wavenumbers k_n) matches the empirical PCA modes of word embeddings trained on 2.72B tokens of English Wikipedia, as well as the internal representations of Gemma 2 2B (2304-dim) and EmbeddingGemma (768-dim). For linear coordinate decoding, Proposition 4 predicts the error scales as ε² ~ r^(−1/D), which is empirically validated for historical years (D=1) with the expected double-descent peak at the interpolation threshold. A key robustness result shows that the circular embedding of the 12 months is preserved at d=1000 ≪ V=25000 even after explicitly zeroing all month-month co-occurrences in the PMI matrix, and that just a handful of highly seasonal helper words suffice to reconstruct the correct month ordering.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare against competing methods in the traditional benchmark sense; instead, it validates a single theoretical prediction across three distinct model architectures to demonstrate architecture-agnostic principles. The predicted embedding geometry (circles for periodic BC, open Lissajous loops for open BC, and slowly-varying 2D eigenmodes for geography) is confirmed in: (1) shallow word embeddings obtained by spectral factorization of the Wikipedia PMI matrix, (2) the 768-dimensional EmbeddingGemma text embedding model, and (3) the 2304-dimensional residual-stream activations of Gemma 2 2B at the final token position. In all three settings, the top principal components encode the lowest-frequency Fourier modes with the predicted amplitude decay, and the Gram matrices exhibit the expected circulant (periodic) or Toeplitz-like (open) structure. The one notable discrepancy is that low-dimensional word embeddings (d=1000) more faithfully reflect the pure geographic/seasonal signal than the higher-dimensional LLM representations, which the authors attribute to the collective robustness mechanism: the large eigenvalues from many seasonal words make the top modes insensitive to noise, an effect that is more pronounced when the embedding dimension is a small fraction of the vocabulary size.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Karkada et al. (2025), which established that word embedding models learn the top eigenmodes of a normalized co-occurrence matrix, and on Korchinski et al. (2025), which showed that parallelogram geometry in linear analogies arises from Kronecker structure driven by discrete latent variables. The key departure is extending this spectral framework to continuous latent variables with translation symmetry, showing that circulant/Toeplitz structure in the co-occurrence matrix yields Fourier (sinusoidal) embedding geometry. It unifies previously disconnected empirical observations—circular month embeddings, rippled 1D year manifolds, and linear geographic decoding—under a single symmetry principle, and adds the novel collective-effects mechanism explaining robustness to perturbation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled, architecture-agnostic explanation for why language models represent time and space with specific geometric structures, grounding them in low-order pairwise statistics rather than model-specific inductive biases. The robustness result—that circular month geometry survives complete removal of month-month co-occurrences—reveals that representational structure is a collective phenomenon distributed across the vocabulary, with implications for how models generalize and for interpretability. Open questions include extending the theory to context-dependent (polysemous) representations, hierarchical attributes, and understanding whether analogous symmetry principles govern the grid-cell-like representations observed in neuroscience.
