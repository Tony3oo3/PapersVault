## Problem

RL-based multi-agent coordination methods struggle to generalize across unseen layouts, team sizes, and task compositions, while directly deploying LLMs as acting agents suffers from unreliable low-level execution and prohibitive inference latency. The paper addresses how to transfer the task-level coordination reasoning of large language models into lightweight, real-time executable policies without explicit supervision or handcrafted coordination rules.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265730/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At deployment, the Distilled Agent receives a structured text prompt encoding only local information: the agent's held item, accessible objects, the other agent's status and action queue, the current recipe order, the task queue, pot state, and completed tasks. It outputs a single task-level function call (select_task with an index into the predefined task queue and a brief reason), which is then executed as a sequence of low-level environment actions until task completion or a significant state change. Critically, the agent does not re-plan every timestep; it maintains its task assignment persistently, and all privileged global state and coordination rationales are excluded from both training and inference.

## Method

SynCoord is a three-phase self-supervised distillation pipeline. First, a lightweight Intuitive Agent (Qwen2.5-7B) is fine-tuned via supervised learning on single-agent trajectories to provide executable task proposals that ground the Thinking Agent in valid environment actions. Second, a high-capacity Thinking Agent (Qwen3-235B-A22B) performs offline deliberative multi-agent reasoning over the full global state and the Intuitive Agent's proposals, generating coordinated task assignments and optional rationales across ~1,200 interaction trajectories. Third, a compact Distilled Agent (Qwen2.5-7B) is trained via behavior cloning (cross-entropy over function calls) on pairs of local observations and the Thinking Agent's task assignments, with all privileged information discarded. The key architectural choice is operating at a semantic task level (e.g., pickup_tomato, deliver_tomato, start_cooking) rather than grid-level actions, which decouples coordination logic from low-level navigation and enables the distilled policy to generalize zero-shot to unseen team sizes, layouts, and recipes.

## Results

On the Overcooked-AI benchmark (5 layouts × 13 scenes), SynCoord achieves a 97% success rate with an average of 39.97 completion timesteps, the lowest among all methods evaluated on the full distribution. RL baselines (MAPPO, RMAPPO, IPPO) achieve 100% SR only on a single scene (1×1) but collapse to ~18–21% SR when the training distribution expands to 1×13, and fail to converge on the full 5×13 set. Among LLM baselines tested on the same 5×13 distribution, SynCoord matches or exceeds their success rates (97% vs. 86–98%) while reducing average completion timesteps by 4–8 steps compared to GPT-5.2, Gemini-3-flash, and Qwen3-235B-A22B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RL baselines (MAPPO, RMAPPO, IPPO), SynCoord achieves a comparable 97% SR on the full 5×13 distribution where RL methods cannot be reliably trained; on the restricted 1×1 setting where RL is viable, SynCoord matches their 100% SR while achieving a lower average completion time (39.97 vs. 38.49–42.44 steps). Critically, all three RL methods collapse to 18–21% SR when the training distribution scales from 1×1 to 1×13, whereas SynCoord maintains stable performance across the full 5×13 layout set. Against LLM baselines on the same 5×13 distribution, SynCoord outperforms GPT-5.2 by 11 percentage points in SR (0.97 vs. 0.86) and reduces Avg T by 8.3 steps (39.97 vs. 48.25). Compared to Gemini-3-flash (SR 0.96, Avg T 44.24) and Qwen3-235B-A22B (SR 0.98, Avg T 46.88), SynCoord trades a marginal 1-point SR difference for a 4–7 step reduction in completion time, indicating more precise and less redundant execution. The paper notes that SynCoord's lower Avg R (35.12) relative to MAPPO (39.77) reflects fewer unnecessary object interactions rather than worse performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the knowledge distillation paradigm (Mirzadeh et al., 2020) from single-agent reasoning transfer to multi-agent coordination, introducing a two-stage pipeline where a high-capacity 'Thinking Agent' generates task-level coordination trajectories that are distilled into a lightweight 'Distilled Agent.' It departs from both traditional MARL methods (MAPPO, IPPO, RMAPPO), which learn coordination implicitly through joint optimization and struggle with distribution shift, and direct LLM-based coordination, which suffers from prohibitive inference latency. The key conceptual contribution is reframing multi-agent cooperation as a transferable task-level coordination abstraction rather than an emergent property of low-level control, using structured function-calling interfaces to constrain LLM interaction and enable trajectory collection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that coordination behaviors requiring high-capacity reasoning can be compressed into a 7B model with 44× lower inference latency (350 ms vs. 15,609 ms), making LLM-informed multi-agent coordination practically deployable. The zero-shot generalization to unseen team sizes (1→3 agents) and larger spatial layouts without retraining suggests task-level abstractions provide stronger inductive priors than action-level RL for scalable cooperation. However, the approach is limited to offline supervision with fixed task abstractions, a centralized full-observability assumption during training, and two relatively simple grid-world benchmarks; open questions include online adaptation under stochastic environments, decentralized human-agent settings requiring intent inference, and whether the capacity threshold for coordination (1.5B fails completely) generalizes beyond these domains.
