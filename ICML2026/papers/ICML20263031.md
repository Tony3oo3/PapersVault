## Problem

Existing training-free hallucination mitigation methods for LVLMs reduce the problem to a simple visual-vs-textual attention imbalance and rely on Contrastive Decoding (CD) to produce grounded logits, which doubles inference latency. The paper shows through controlled ablations that the perceived gains of spatial attention reweighting are largely an artifact of CD rather than the manipulation itself, and identifies two distinct hallucination patterns—Perceptual-Semantic Dissociation (PSD) and Localized Fixation (LF)—that a one-size-fits-all spatial scaling cannot address.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263031/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

FLASH is a plug-and-play, training-free intervention applied during autoregressive decoding of standard LVLMs (LLaVA-1.5 7B/13B, Shikra 7B). It consumes the same RGB image + text prompt as the base model and produces the same output format (free-form caption or yes/no answer), but internally reshapes attention scores and value matrices within selected MHA heads at each generated token. For discriminative tasks (POPE, MME) modulation is applied only to the first generated token; for generative tasks (CHAIR, AMBER) it is applied throughout the sequence.

## Method

FLASH introduces two mechanisms operating in the frequency domain. First, a Spectral Vortex Score (SVS) computed via FFT on reshaped visual-token attention weights identifies vision heads by measuring low-frequency energy concentration and spectral isotropy (vortex-like structure vs. periodic ripples from attention sinks). Second, a Dual-stream Spectrum Modulation (DSM) applies DCT-based spectral shaping to two parallel branches: the V-stream enhances high-frequency components of value matrices in the log-magnitude domain (counteracting PSD by restoring fine-grained visual detail suppressed by MHA's low-pass filtering), while the S-stream redistributes attention-score energy toward low frequencies in the linear domain (counteracting LF by broadening contextual coverage). Both streams enforce a Frobenius-norm calibration that, by Parseval's theorem, acts as a zero-sum energy reallocation—amplifying targeted bands while automatically draining redundant energy from others—preserving the pre-trained multimodal alignment without requiring CD or any retraining.

## Results

On LLaVA-1.5 7B, FLASH achieves the best POPE-MS-COCO scores across all three splits: 90.03% Acc / 89.77% F1 (Random), 86.53% Acc / 86.62% F1 (Popular), and 80.53% Acc / 81.75% F1 (Adversarial), outperforming all CD-based baselines. On generative benchmarks, FLASH attains the lowest CHAIR_S (47.50) and a strong AMBER Hal score (25.00), while matching VCD and PAI on MME (636.67). Critically, FLASH requires only 1.69× the vanilla inference time, compared to 1.87× (PAI), 1.98× (VCD), and 2.58× (SID), confirming its efficiency advantage from bypassing Contrastive Decoding.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against VCD, FLASH wins on all three POPE splits (e.g., 90.03 vs 89.07 Acc on Random for LLaVA-1.5 7B), ties on MME (636.67), achieves a lower CHAIR_S (47.50 vs 48.80), and reduces AMBER Hal from 27.00 to 25.00, while running 15% faster (1.69× vs 1.98× inference time). Against PAI, FLASH dominates on POPE (90.03 vs 89.30 Random Acc; 86.53 vs 86.07 Popular Acc) and CHAIR_S (47.50 vs 47.80), but PAI retains a narrow edge on CHAIR_I (12.35 vs 12.55) and AMBER Hal/Cog (24.25/1.45 vs 25.00/2.15) for the 7B model; FLASH is still faster (1.69× vs 1.87×). Against SID, FLASH shows the largest margin: it gains +0.63 Acc on POPE Random, +0.60 on Popular, and a substantial +30.00 on MME (636.67 vs 606.67), while also cutting inference time by 35% (1.69× vs 2.58×). On the 13B LLaVA-1.5, FLASH extends its POPE lead (90.60 vs 90.17 SID, 90.03 PAI) and achieves the best AMBER Hal (23.00), though PAI still leads on CHAIR_S (37.60) and AMBER Cog (1.70) for that scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

FLASH departs from the dominant spatial-domain paradigm in training-free hallucination mitigation (PAI, VCD, SID), which treats hallucinations as a uniform attention imbalance between visual and textual tokens and relies on Contrastive Decoding to amplify grounded logits. Instead, it repurposes classical signal-processing tools (FFT, DCT, Parseval's theorem) to operate in the frequency domain, identifying two distinct hallucination patterns—Perceptual-Semantic Dissociation and Localized Fixation—each with a different spectral signature in multi-head attention. The known low-pass filtering property of MHA (Park & Kim, 2022; Dong et al., 2023) is reframed as a mechanistic cause of hallucination rather than merely a representational artifact, and the Spectral Vortex Score borrows from image-texture analysis to select vision heads without retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that the perceived efficacy of spatial attention reweighting is largely an artifact of CD, the paper challenges a core assumption in the training-free mitigation literature and motivates a shift toward frequency-domain interventions. FLASH's CD-free design (1.69× vs. 1.87–2.58× latency) makes it more deployable in real-time settings, and the PSD/LF taxonomy offers a diagnostic lens for future work. However, the method still requires per-model hyperparameter tuning (λ_v, λ_s, k, τ, layer ranges), its gains over strong CD-based baselines are modest (≈0.5–1.0 Acc points on POPE), and applicability to Q-Former-based architectures remains untested—open questions that limit immediate broad adoption.
