## Problem

Pre-trained model knowledge is tightly coupled to specific monolithic architectures, making it difficult to transfer to models of different depths or widths. Existing approaches either select discrete parameter subsets (losing interdependent structure) or rely on generative models trained over large model collections, and neither provides a principled mechanism to isolate the intrinsic, architecture-agnostic constituents of transferable knowledge.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the full weight tensors of a pre-trained model (3D tensors of shape L×d_in×d_out for ViTs, 2D matrices for MLPs, 4D tensors for CNNs) along with the target model's desired dimensions. It produces initialized weight tensors of arbitrary size by performing zero-padding or truncation in the frequency domain followed by inverse DCT reconstruction, a process that completes in milliseconds on a CPU without any gradient-based optimization.

## Method

FRONT identifies the low-frequency components of model weights (via 3D-DCT) as the concrete carrier of task-agnostic 'learngene' knowledge, validated by showing these components remain stable during fine-tuning while high-frequency components change. Direct extraction (FRONT) applies a binary mask to retain only low-frequency coefficients up to a ratio r along all three dimensions, then resizes to the target architecture via padding or truncation in the spectral domain before IDCT reconstruction. An optional refinement (FRONT+) introduces a spectral regularizer that softly penalizes high-frequency coefficient energy during training, progressively concentrating knowledge into lower frequencies for more robust transfer. A theoretical analysis (Appendix A) proves that under spectral bias assumptions, fine-tuning gradients are concentrated in the high-frequency subspace, confirming low-frequency weights as the stable, transferable core.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K (10-epoch fine-tuning), FRONT (direct extraction, 0 extra epochs) achieves 65.3% top-1 on DeiT-Ti-L12, 71.2% on DeiT-S-L12, and 76.3% on DeiT-B-L12, surpassing the best direct baseline LiGO by 4.4–0.9 points. With optional refinement (FRONT+, 150 epochs), performance reaches 67.5%, 75.4%, and 79.3%, approaching full pre-training (68.6%, 77.6%, 81.5%) while requiring only 10 epochs of downstream training—a 15× convergence acceleration. On GLUE (BERT-S), FRONT achieves 64.63 average, outperforming training from scratch (53.31) and knowledge distillation (52.81) by 11.3 and 11.8 points respectively, while reducing training FLOPs by 40.5% on average across BERT, RoBERTa, and GPT-2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among zero-cost direct initialization methods, FRONT (65.3/71.2/76.3%) decisively outperforms LiGO (60.9/70.0/75.4%), the strongest direct baseline, by 4.4/1.2/0.9 points on DeiT-Ti/S/B-L12, and surpasses He-Init by 17.0/15.7/19.6 points. Among high-effort methods requiring auxiliary training, FRONT+ (67.5/75.4/79.3%) edges out WAVE (67.3/75.3/79.2) while transferring 25% fewer parameters, and beats TLEG (65.4/73.8/77.1) and Auto-LG (66.8/73.8/73.8) by 2.1–5.5 points. FRONT+ remains within 1.1–2.2 points of full 150-epoch pre-training (68.6/77.6/81.5) despite requiring only 10 epochs of downstream fine-tuning. On GLUE, FRONT (64.63 avg) beats both Scratch (53.31) and KD (52.81) by 11+ points across all seven tasks, with the largest gain on STS-B (+36.96 over Scratch). On ResNet-based CNNs, FRONT yields dramatic gains on fine-grained datasets: +40.0% on Flowers-102 (ResNet-50) and +59.7% (ResNet-152) over random initialization, confirming architecture-agnostic transfer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper operationalizes the 'learngene' concept (Feng et al., 2025a) by identifying low-frequency DCT components of pre-trained weights as the concrete carrier of architecture- and task-agnostic knowledge, departing from prior learngene methods (Heur-LG, Auto-LG, TLEG, WAVE) that rely on heuristic fragment selection or costly auxiliary training. It also contrasts with spatial-domain single-source methods (Wt Select, LiGO) that treat knowledge as discrete local components, and with generative approaches (GHN-3, diffusion-based) that require large homogeneous model collections. The key conceptual departure is shifting from parameter selection or prediction to a principled spectral decomposition, where the DCT's energy compaction property naturally isolates the transferable core, enabling size-agnostic adaptation via simple truncation or padding without any retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FRONT provides a practical, training-free mechanism to initialize models of arbitrary depth and width from a single pre-trained source, with demonstrated 15× convergence acceleration in vision and 40.5% FLOP reduction in language tasks. The module-level ablations revealing modality-specific knowledge localization (MLP-dominated for vision, self-attention-dominated for language) offer useful structural insights for future transfer design. Open questions include whether the spectral bias assumptions underpinning the theoretical analysis (Appendix A) hold for all architectures and training regimes, how the method performs when source and target models differ in fundamental inductive bias (e.g., CNN-to-Transformer), and whether the frequency ratio r and decay factor γ_d can be made fully adaptive rather than requiring manual tuning.
