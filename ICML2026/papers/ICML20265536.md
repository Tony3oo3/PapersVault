## Problem

In standard real-valued Transformers, semantic importance is conflated with activation magnitude, making it impossible to determine whether phase in complex-valued representations is an active computational carrier or merely a representational byproduct. This paper isolates the computational role of phase by constraining the model to a spectral regime where magnitude-based shortcuts are deliberately limited, then testing through interventional ablations whether phase carries task-relevant relational information in learned sequence representations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265536/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For WMT14 De-En, the model takes German source sentences (filtered to L≤128) and produces English translations via a standard Transformer decoder; for WikiText-103, it takes text sequences at L=4096 and produces masked token predictions under a BERT-style MLM objective. In both settings, input tokens are encoded as complex phasors z = r·e^(iθ) via Rotary Semantic Embeddings, with the spectral branch operating under a unit-norm constraint that forces information into phase angles rather than magnitude. The complex encoder state is bridged to the real-valued decoder by concatenating real and imaginary components through a learned linear projection.

## Method

PRISM replaces quadratic self-attention with an O(N log N) spectral pipeline (FFT → learnable complex filter H → IFFT), where tokens are represented as complex phasors and semantic relationships are encoded through phase rotation rather than magnitude scaling. The architecture combines Rotary Semantic Embeddings for multiplicative positional encoding, Cartesian Gated Harmonic Convolutions that independently modulate real and imaginary components to enable content-dependent phase steering, and phase-preserving components (ModReLU, PPLN) that prevent phase distortion during training. The key hybrid variant, Wave-Particle Transformer (WPT), runs a PRISM spectral stream (relational, phase-coded) in parallel with a lightweight Transformer stream (token identity, attention) at d=256, fusing their outputs before a single refiner layer. Interventional ablations on the learned spectral filter and layer-wise activations demonstrate that preserving phase retains >95% of performance while removing, reversing, or shuffling phase causes 72–205× degradation, establishing phase as the dominant task-relevant information carrier in the spectral pathway.

## Results

On WikiText-103 masked reconstruction at L=4096, the Wave-Particle Transformer (WPT) achieves the best perplexity of 4.94 (Top-1: 67.2%, Top-5: 82.8%), outperforming the standard Transformer baseline (5.28 PPL) while using 18% fewer core parameters (12.9M vs. 15.8M). The PRISM Hybrid achieves 6.06 PPL, and the dual-stream HSSM reaches 6.47 PPL. The real-valued FNet Hybrid collapses at long context, yielding 9.87 PPL, indicating that additive Fourier mixing without persistent phase is insufficient when dynamic content selection is required.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the standard Transformer (Vaswani et al., 2017), the WPT hybrid wins on all three metrics: 4.94 vs. 5.28 PPL, 67.2% vs. 66.1% Top-1, and 82.8% vs. 81.9% Top-5, while using 12.9M core parameters versus the Transformer's 15.8M. The PRISM Hybrid (6.06 PPL) and HSSM (6.47 PPL) both lose to the Transformer, suggesting that the phase-based spectral branch alone is not yet competitive with attention at this scale, but becomes advantageous when fused in parallel with a lightweight attention stream. Against FNet (Lee-Thorp et al., 2022), all PRISM variants win decisively: the FNet Hybrid collapses to 9.87 PPL at L=4096, while even the weakest PRISM variant (HSSM) achieves 6.47 PPL, a 3.4 PPL gap. The paper attributes this to FNet's real-valued additive Fourier mixing lacking the phase-sensitive interference mechanism that PRISM's complex-valued spectral filtering provides for dynamic content selection at long sequence lengths.

## Relation to Prior Work

PRISM sits at the intersection of spectral sequence mixers (FNet, which replaces attention with real-valued Fourier mixing) and complex-valued deep networks (Trabelsi et al., 2018), but departs from both by maintaining persistent complex-valued representations across layers and by imposing a unit-norm constraint to isolate phase as the computational variable. Unlike Holographic Transformers or POC-ViT, which introduce phase dynamics within quadratic-complexity attention, PRISM replaces attention entirely with O(N log N) gated harmonic convolutions. The hybrid WPT architecture further positions the work relative to standard Transformers by running a phase-coded spectral stream in parallel with a lightweight attention stream, drawing on the wave-particle duality as a design metaphor for decoupling relational interference from token selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The interventional ablations provide the strongest evidence to date that phase, not magnitude, is the dominant information carrier in learned spectral sequence representations—a finding that reframes how we think about what complex-valued neural networks actually compute. However, the results are confined to ~33M-parameter models on WikiText-103 MLM and WMT14, with no end-to-end complex decoder and throughput currently limited by the absence of complex BF16 kernels. Open questions include whether phase-dominant computation persists at foundation-model scale, whether causal complex-valued decoders can be constructed without O(L² log L) autoregressive cost, and whether the spectral-density lower bound on sequence length generalizes to other architectures.
