## Problem

Irregular multivariate time series (IMTS) with non-uniform sampling and asynchronous variables are common in healthcare, climate, and industrial monitoring, yet existing approaches face a fundamental trade-off: architecture-based methods design specialized models that cannot reuse proven MTS backbones, while data-based methods interpolate onto regular grids, introducing artificial values that distort temporal dynamics. The paper identifies that the true bottleneck is not the backbone but the conventional input embedding layer, which implicitly assumes uniform sampling.

## Input / Output

QuITE consumes raw IMTS as unordered sets of value-timestamp-mask triplets (x_{n,i}, t_{n,i}, m_{n,i}) with no requirement for temporal alignment across variables, and for forecasting also receives a set of future query timestamps. It produces fixed-dimensional structured latent embeddings—either variable-level (N×D) or patch-level (M×N×D)—whose shape is determined by the downstream backbone's expected input structure rather than tuned as a hyperparameter. The module is strictly plug-and-play: only the input embedding is replaced while the entire backbone architecture remains unchanged.

## Method

QuITE tokenizes each observation by adding a linear value projection to a harmonic time embedding (sinusoidal with learnable frequencies and phases), then aggregates the resulting unordered token set via a single masked self-attention layer where learnable query tokens serve as structured aggregation anchors. In variable-level mode, one query per variable attends to all its observations; in patch-level mode, one query per patch-variable pair attends to observations within a local temporal window. The updated query token directly becomes the backbone-compatible embedding, bypassing both lossy pooling and artificial value generation. QuITE++ extends this into a hierarchical encoder with stacked patch-level self-attention (intra-variable temporal dependencies) and variable-level self-attention (inter-variable interactions), followed by a cross-attention decoder that conditions predictions on future time queries via both global (variable-level) and local (patch-level) contexts.

## Results

QuITE++ achieves the best forecasting performance in 20 out of 24 dataset-horizon settings across four IMTS benchmarks (Human Activity, USHCN, PhysioNet, MIMIC-III). As a plug-and-play module, QuITE yields average relative MSE improvements of up to 54.7% (iTransformer+QuITE on PhysioNet/MIMIC-III) and 15.8% (PatchMixer+QuITE on classification) over the original MTS backbones. QuITE-equipped MTS backbones rank first in 1 setting and second in 12 settings against 11 dedicated IMTS models, demonstrating that input-level adaptation alone can match or exceed purpose-built irregularity architectures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QuITE++ outperforms all 11 IMTS-specific baselines (Warpformer, Raindrop, GRU-D, tPatchGNN, GraFITi, CRU, mTAND, NeuralFlow, Latent-ODE, HyperIMTS, Hi-Patch) in 20 of 24 forecasting settings, with the closest competitors being HyperIMTS (e.g., 2.49 vs. 2.46 MSE on Human Activity 3000→1000) and Hi-Patch (5.04 vs. 4.81 on USHCN 24→12). Among QuITE-equipped MTS backbones, iTransformer+QuITE and TimeXer+QuITE are the strongest, matching or beating HyperIMTS and Hi-Patch on most clinical datasets (PhysioNet, MIMIC-III), while patch-based models (PatchTST+QuITE, PatchMixer+QuITE) lag on clinical data due to their limited inter-variable modeling. On classification, QuITE consistently improves every backbone: PatchMixer+QuITE achieves the largest average gain (15.8%), with standout improvements on P19 AUPRC (26.4→55.8, +111.4%) and P12 AUPRC (39.2→45.2, +15.3%). The ablation in Table 5 confirms that QuITE's query-token aggregation outperforms simpler alternatives (Add, Concat, mTAND, Mean Pooling) across all backbones and datasets, validating that structured attention-based aggregation is the key mechanism rather than merely incorporating temporal information.

## Relation to Prior Work

QuITE carves new ground by identifying the input-embedding layer (rather than the backbone architecture) as the primary bottleneck for adapting MTS models to irregular time series. Conceptually, it repurposes the learnable [CLS] token idea from BERT as a structured aggregation mechanism for irregular observations, combined with harmonic time embeddings borrowed from mTAND. Unlike architecture-based IMTS methods (GRU-D, Latent-ODE, ContiFormer, GraFITi, HyperIMTS) that require bespoke designs, or data-based methods (IP-Nets, mTAND) that introduce artificial interpolated values, QuITE operates as a single self-attention layer that directly produces backbone-compatible structured tokens without either architectural modification or value generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of QuITE means practitioners can immediately upgrade any existing MTS backbone (Transformer, CNN, MLP, Mamba) to handle IMTS without retraining the full architecture, lowering the barrier to applying well-validated MTS models to irregular clinical and sensor data. A key limitation is that patch-based backbones still underperform on clinical IMTS (PhysioNet, MIMIC-III) due to their inherent lack of inter-variable modeling, suggesting the embedding fix alone cannot compensate for architectural weaknesses. Open questions include whether the single self-attention layer suffices for extremely sparse observations (performance degrades notably beyond ~75% removal), and whether the approach extends naturally to other irregular data modalities such as event sequences or multimodal clinical records.
