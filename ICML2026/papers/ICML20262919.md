## Problem

Continuous audio VAEs face a fundamental conflict—the Rate-Distortion-Regularity Trilemma—where the standard isotropic Gaussian prior imposes a flat latent geometry that cannot accommodate audio's spectral hierarchy (structured low-frequency components vs. stochastic high-frequency components). This topological mismatch causes disordered information packing, where semantic features are randomly interleaved with high-entropy noise, simultaneously degrading compression efficiency, reconstruction fidelity, and the latent regularity needed for downstream generative models.

## Input / Output

STAR-VAE takes raw 44.1 kHz stereo audio waveforms (silence-pruned) and produces reconstructed waveforms at the same rate, operating at a 21.5 Hz latent rate with a hierarchically organized channel space. STAR-Gen takes a text prompt and Gaussian noise as input, producing a generated audio waveform by predicting a velocity field over STAR-VAE's structured latent space. The latent representation is structured so that low-index channels carry deterministic structural information while high-index channels carry stochastic residuals, enabling PCA-like energy compaction where the top 37.5% of channels recover near-optimal fidelity.

## Method

STAR replaces the uniform KL penalty with a Gamma-Growth constraint field β_c = β_min + (β_max − β_min)·((c−1)/(C−1))^γ (γ=2.0), creating a convex capacity gradient that routes low-entropy structural features to low-penalty channels and high-entropy stochastic details to high-penalty channels. The STAR-VAE architecture combines strided ResNet CNN blocks for local spectral downsampling with bidirectional Mamba (Selective SSM) layers for linear-complexity global context modeling, trained in two phases: ~150 hours of standard isotropic KL pre-training followed by ~70 hours of STAR fine-tuning. STAR-Gen adapts a Qwen3-0.6B causal Transformer decoder as a conditional flow-matching velocity estimator over STAR-VAE latents, using a hybrid attention mask (causal for text conditioning, bidirectional for audio latents) to enable non-autoregressive continuous generation without vector quantization artifacts.

## Results

At the matched 21.5 Hz latent rate, STAR-VAE outperforms Stable Audio Open on all reconstruction metrics: FAD drops from 3.29 to 2.31 (AudioCaps) and 0.69 to 0.25 (Song Describer), while latent correlation (LC) improves from 0.11 to 0.08. For text-to-audio generation, STAR-Gen achieves FD_openL3 of 55.8, KL of 1.09, and CLAP of 0.48 on AudioCaps, substantially outperforming the best baseline TangoFlux (FD_openL3 80.2, KL 1.22, CLAP 0.43). Replacing SAO's VAE with STAR-VAE alone improves its diffusion-based generation from FD_openL3 89.2 to 72.5, confirming the structured latent space benefits downstream models regardless of the generative backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Stable Audio Open (Evans et al., 2025) at the same 21.5 Hz latent rate, STAR-VAE wins on every reconstruction metric, with the largest gains in semantic preservation (FAD 2.31 vs. 3.29 on AudioCaps; 0.25 vs. 0.69 on Song Describer) and latent regularity (LC 0.08 vs. 0.11). Against εar-VAE (Wang et al., 2025), STAR-VAE trades signal-level fidelity (STFT-D 1.17 vs. 1.08, SI-SDR −0.03 vs. 6.13) for substantially better semantic quality (FAD 2.31 vs. 4.44) and latent structure (LC 0.08 vs. 0.13), which the authors argue is more critical for downstream generation. The spectrogram-based Mel-VAE (Huang et al., 2023) and high-rate AudioGen (Kreuk et al., 2022) are clearly outperformed across all metrics, reflecting their lower compression efficiency and lack of temporal modeling. For text-to-audio generation, STAR-Gen (905M params) beats TangoFlux (515M) by 24.4 points in FD_openL3 (55.8 vs. 80.2), Tango 2 (866M) by 52.6 points (55.8 vs. 108.4), AudioLDM 2-large (712M) by 52.5 points (55.8 vs. 108.3), and SAO (1.05B) by 33.4 points (55.8 vs. 89.2), while also achieving the best CLAP score (0.48 vs. 0.44 for Tango 2). Notably, simply swapping SAO's VAE for STAR-VAE improves its own diffusion generator from FD_openL3 89.2 to 72.5, demonstrating that the structured latent space is beneficial independent of the generative architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Stable Audio Open convolutional backbone (Evans et al., 2025) and augments it with bidirectional Mamba blocks (Gu & Dao, 2024) for global context modeling, while departing from the standard isotropic Gaussian KL prior that underpins virtually all prior audio VAEs (AudioGen, εar-VAE, SAO). The core conceptual departure is the STAR regularization—a channel-wise Gamma-Growth penalty that replaces the uniform KL term—framed as resolving a formally stated 'Rate-Distortion-Regularity Trilemma.' For generation, STAR-Gen adapts a Qwen3 LLM decoder to continuous Flow Matching (Lipman et al., 2022), bridging the gap between discrete autoregressive models (AudioGen, MusicGen) and standard diffusion/flow-matching approaches (AudioLDM 2, TangoFlux) by operating on structured continuous latents without vector quantization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The STAR regularization is a simple, architecture-agnostic modification (a single channel-wise weight vector) that practitioners can adopt with minimal engineering overhead, and the 'Reconstruction Drift' finding warns against naively inserting high-capacity sequence models into isotropic VAEs—a practical pitfall likely encountered by the broader audio ML community. The structured latent space demonstrably benefits both diffusion and LLM-based flow matching downstream, suggesting broad utility beyond the specific STAR-VAE/STAR-Gen pipeline. Open questions include whether the static Gamma-Growth curve can be made content-adaptive for non-stationary audio, whether the approach transfers to speech or music-only settings without re-tuning, and whether the Mamba hardware ecosystem will mature enough to close the remaining inference-speed gap versus pure CNN baselines.
