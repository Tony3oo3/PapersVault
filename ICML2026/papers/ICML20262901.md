## Problem

Hyperbolic multimodal representations capture hierarchical semantic structure across modalities, but how they behave under continual learning remains entirely unexplored. Naive sequential fine-tuning in hyperbolic space distorts both cross-modal relational structure and radial hierarchy, causing catastrophic forgetting that Euclidean continual learning methods (EWC, GEM, C-FLAT) cannot adequately address because they are geometry-agnostic. The paper asks: what geometric conditions must be preserved to prevent forgetting in hyperbolic multimodal representations?

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired RGB images and text captions from up to 15 sequential datasets (classification: CIFAR-10/100, Caltech-101, Food-101; retrieval: COCO, Flickr30k) processed by frozen pre-trained encoders (MERU-L, MERU-B, HyCoCLIP-B). Lightweight Lorentz transformation layers map encoder features onto the hyperboloid manifold with fixed curvature K=0.1, producing hyperbolic embeddings that support both classification (accuracy) and bidirectional cross-modal retrieval (image-to-text and text-to-image R@K). The key structural constraint is that all embeddings must satisfy the Lorentz constraint x₀² − ‖x_[1:d]‖² = 1/K, with the time-like coordinate reconstructed deterministically from spatial components.

## Method

The paper establishes that preserving old-task knowledge across continual stages requires a shared spatial rotation (a block-diagonal Lorentz isometry with identity time-like component) applied identically to all modalities (Theorem 1). From this characterization, it derives that admissible parameter updates must project the unconstrained gradient onto the orthogonal complement of the old-task spatial representation subspace, while freezing the time-like weight block (Proposition 1, Corollary 2). The resulting HMCL algorithm computes a low-rank orthonormal basis V of old-task spatial embeddings via SVD, then applies the projected update ΔW_s = δ_s(I − VVᵀ) with Δw₀ = 0, incurring only O(d²) memory overhead per stage. Training combines the standard hyperbolic contrastive loss with an entailment cone loss (weight 0.2) to maintain hierarchical partial-order structure, and a step-pullback regularization (β-mixing toward the task-start snapshot) stabilizes finite-step AdamW optimization.

## Results

Across three hyperbolic backbones (MERU-L, MERU-B, HyCoCLIP-B), HMCL achieves the highest overall performance and the least negative backward transfer (BWT) in every setting. On MERU-L, HMCL improves overall score from 38.22 (Vanilla) to 41.36 (+8.2%) and reduces classification forgetting from −6.46 to −0.74 (88.5% relative reduction). On HyCoCLIP-B, the gains are largest: overall score rises from 42.33 to 46.50 (+9.9%), image-to-text R@1 improves by +26.9%, and BWT drops from −5.42 to −0.43 (92.1% reduction in forgetting). Drift analysis (Figure 3) shows HMCL reduces radial, angular, cross-modal, and paired-distance drift by 61.0%, 57.8%, 73.8%, and 59.5% respectively over old tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HMCL decisively outperforms all three baselines (EWC, GEM, C-FLAT) on every metric across all three backbones. EWC and GEM, which are regularization- and constraint-based methods designed for Euclidean spaces, provide only marginal relief: on MERU-L they reduce retrieval BWT from −6.46 to roughly −3.5 but leave classification BWT nearly unchanged (−6.43, −6.40 vs. −6.46 Vanilla), and on MERU-B GEM actually worsens retrieval (R@K drops to 29.06 with ±3.00 std.) and overall BWT (−6.81). C-FLAT, which adds contrastive regularization for cross-task consistency, partially improves retrieval stability (BWTRK −2.56 on MERU-L) but sacrifices classification accuracy (39.28 vs. 40.59 Vanilla) and overall performance (37.16 vs. 38.22), and on MERU-B it degrades both retrieval (28.20 R@K) and BWT (−7.34). The paper attributes these failures to the baselines being geometry-agnostic: they do not preserve the shared cross-modal isometry or the radial hierarchy constraints that are necessary in hyperbolic space, whereas HMCL's orthogonal-projection update enforces both by construction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: hyperbolic multimodal representation learning (MERU, HyCoCLIP, entailment cones) and continual learning for vision-language models (C-FLAT, EWC, GEM). It departs from both in a specific way: all prior hyperbolic multimodal models assume static, full-distribution training, while all prior continual multimodal methods operate in Euclidean space. The paper's core theoretical contribution (Theorem 1) reframes continual learning in hyperbolic space as a geometric invariance problem—preservation requires a shared spatial rotation (a subgroup of the Lorentz group)—rather than the parameter-regularization or gradient-constraint formulations used in Euclidean continual learning. The practical method (HMCL) inherits the Lorentz transformation layer from Chen et al. (2022) and the entailment cone machinery from Ganea et al. (2018), but derives its update rule from the isometry characterization rather than from a hand-crafted regularization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic treatment of continual learning in hyperbolic multimodal space, providing both a necessary-and-sufficient geometric condition for forgetting prevention and a constant-memory algorithm that achieves it. The finding that standard Euclidean continual learning methods (EWC, GEM, C-FLAT) are fundamentally mismatched to hyperbolic geometry—because they do not preserve shared cross-modal isometries or radial hierarchy—clarifies why they fail and points toward geometry-aware design as the right principle. Limitations include the restriction to lightweight transformation layers (frozen backbones), the absence of replay mechanisms, and evaluation limited to image-text pairs; extending to full-backbone adaptation, additional modalities, and combining the isometry constraint with exemplar or generative replay are natural next steps.
