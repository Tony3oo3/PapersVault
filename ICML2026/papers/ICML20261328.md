## Problem

Recovering the underlying temporal dynamics from unpaired static snapshots (e.g., single-cell sequencing, in vivo imaging) requires solving a multi-marginal Schrödinger Bridge problem, but existing multi-marginal methods either impose restrictive modeling assumptions (spline-valued trajectories, precomputed OT plans) or fail to scale to very high-dimensional spaces such as image space. No prior method could recover coherent multi-marginal couplings and dynamics in image/video dimensions from purely unpaired data.

## Input / Output

The method takes N unpaired datasets (marginals) sampled from the same stochastic process at discrete time points t_0 < t_1 < ... < t_{N-1}, with no correspondence between individual samples across time points. Data ranges from 100-dimensional transcriptomic vectors to full-resolution images (28×28 MNIST, 128×128×3 fluorescence, 160×120 video frames). The output consists of learned forward and backward SDE drifts that, when simulated, produce temporally coherent trajectories and videos connecting all prescribed marginals, with generated samples at any intermediate time.

## Method

The core contribution is the Iterative Markovian Factorized Fitting (IMFF) algorithm, which alternates between Markovian projections (learning a neural drift per direction via a bridge-matching loss) and factorized reciprocal projections (filling each inter-marginal interval with independent Brownian bridges conditioned on the current endpoint estimates). The key insight is a novel factorized reciprocal class R^⊗(Q) where the global path measure decomposes into a tensor product of local bridges, enabling parallelized training across all intervals and avoiding the model-forgetting failure mode of naive multi-marginal implementations. Two neural networks (forward and backward drifts) are trained jointly, with the forward step using the backward network's SDE simulation to generate its training targets and vice versa. Training proceeds in two phases: a flow-matching warmup followed by iterative IMFF refinement, and the full pipeline is proven to converge to the unique multi-marginal Schrödinger Bridge solution under mild regularity conditions.

## Results

On the d=100 Embryoid Body (EB) transcriptomic benchmark, MMtSBM achieves an average MMD of 0.019 and SWD of 0.130, reducing MMD by 41% and SWD by 19% compared to DMSB (0.032 / 0.160). On the d=100 MULTI benchmark, MMtSBM reaches a W1 of 44.542±0.637, outperforming the previous directly comparable SOTA [SF]2M-Geo (52.203±1.957) by 15%. On held-out EB timepoints, MMtSBM achieves MMD 0.17/0.06 and SWD 0.45/0.31 at t=1/t=3, beating the closest competitor 3MSBM (0.18/0.14 and 0.48/0.38). In image space, MMtSBM generates a 602-frame biotine video (KID 17.1) and coherent KTH Actions videos from purely unpaired snapshots, the first such demonstration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MMtSBM outperforms all 12 compared baselines across every benchmark. On the EB benchmark (Table 2), it beats DMSB (Chen et al., 2023a) by 41% in MMD (0.019 vs 0.032) and 19% in SWD (0.130 vs 0.160). On held-out EB timepoints (Table 4), it dominates SBIRR (Shen et al., 2025) by a wide margin (MMD 0.17 vs 0.71 at t=1), MMFM (Rohbeck et al., 2025) (0.17 vs 0.37), and the closest competitor 3MSBM (Theodoropoulos et al., 2025) (0.17 vs 0.18 at t=1; 0.06 vs 0.14 at t=3). On the MULTI benchmark (Table 3), MMtSBM (W1=44.542) surpasses the previous directly comparable SOTA [SF]2M-Geo (Tong et al., 2024b) by 15% (52.203), and also beats methods leveraging precomputed OT plans such as WLF-(UOT+potential) (45.231) and OT-CFM (54.814). Notably, MMtSBM is one of only three methods (with I-CFM and I-MFM_RBF) that does not require a precomputed OT plan, yet still achieves the best W1. The paper also notes that GAGA (Sun et al., 2025) pins true endpoints in a latent space, making it not directly comparable to the pure trajectory inference setting MMtSBM addresses.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Iterative Markovian Fitting (IMF) algorithm of Shi et al. (2023) from the two-marginal Schrödinger Bridge to the multi-marginal temporal setting, introducing a factorized reciprocal class and proving convergence of the resulting IMFF algorithm via Pythagorean identities. It builds on the theoretical foundations of Léonard (2014) for existence, uniqueness, and dynamic-static equivalence, and on De Bortoli et al. (2021) for the neural implementation of SB via IPF. The paper positions itself against flow-matching methods (Tong et al., Kapušniak et al.) and other multi-marginal SB approaches (Chen et al. 2023a, Theodoropoulos et al. 2025), arguing that existing multi-marginal methods either impose restrictive modeling assumptions (spline-valued trajectories, precomputed OT plans) or lack full theoretical soundness in the non-phase-lifted setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The first demonstration of temporally coherent video generation from purely unpaired data snapshots is a notable capability that opens applications in biological imaging and video synthesis where paired observations are impossible to obtain. The SOTA margins on transcriptomic benchmarks (41% MMD reduction over DMSB) are practically meaningful for single-cell trajectory inference. However, the method requires full trajectory simulation during training, uses two separate drift networks, and produces noisy trajectories in data space due to the Brownian interpolant; open questions include whether latent-space learning, simulation-free training, or smoother reference processes can address these limitations while preserving the theoretical guarantees.
