## Problem

Existing prototype-based open-set supervised anomaly detection methods model normal data with a unimodal Gaussian prior, which oversimplifies the inherent multi-modality of real-world normal distributions. This leads to blurred decision boundaries, misclassification of rare normal instances as anomalies (false positives), and poor generalization to unseen anomaly types. The paper asks how to more precisely model the multi-modal structure of normal samples to establish a compact, comprehensive decision boundary robust against unseen anomalies.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images at 448×448×3 with image-level binary labels (normal/anomaly) and produces a scalar anomaly score computed as S_g + S_a + S_r − S_n, combining four complementary scoring modules (global likelihood, local top-O pixel aggregation, normal feature pooling, and residual deviation from the nearest prototype). Training operates in a ResNet-18 feature space where normal and anomalous features are transported via flow matching, and the GMM prototype is initialized by K-means++ clustering on normal features before joint optimization. The method is evaluated under extreme data scarcity (M=1 or M=10 anomalous training examples) with CutMix augmentation to generate pseudo-anomalies.

## Method

MPFM learns a continuous flow transformation that maps normal feature distributions onto a structured Gaussian mixture prototype space (K=32 components), departing from standard flow matching by modeling the conditional velocity field p(u|z_t) as a Gaussian mixture rather than a single velocity vector, so each mixture component corresponds to a semantically distinct normal mode. A Mutual Information Maximization Regularizer (MIMR) is introduced to prevent prototype collapse by simultaneously minimizing conditional entropy (encouraging confident prototype assignments) and maximizing marginal entropy (balancing component usage), evaluated only on normal samples. Training jointly optimizes a negative log-likelihood flow matching loss for normal samples, a positive log-likelihood loss that pushes anomalies away from the prototype space, the MIMR term, and four binary-classification scoring modules. At inference, a closed-form reverse-time transition preserves the Gaussian mixture structure across steps, enabling numerically stable few-step sampling without numerical ODE integration.

## Results

MPFM achieves the best or tied-best AUC across all nine benchmarks under both the general and hard settings. Under the ten-shot general setting, MPFM reaches 0.992 on AITEX, 0.999 on SDD and HeadCT, 0.968 on Mastcam, and 0.965 on Hyper-Kvasir, outperforming the strongest baseline (DPDL) by 1.7, 0.3, 1.8, and 2.6 percentage points respectively. Under the one-shot setting, gains over DPDL are 1.1 points on MVTec AD, 1.6 on Optical, 1.7 on ELPV, and 2.0 on BrainMRI, demonstrating robustness in data-scarce scenarios. Under the hard setting (single anomaly class), MPFM leads by up to 2.7 points on Mastcam and 2.4 on Hyper-Kvasir in the one-shot configuration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MPFM outperforms all six baselines on every dataset in both one-shot and ten-shot settings. The closest competitor is DPDL (Wang et al., 2025a), the prior prototype-based OSAD method using a unimodal Gaussian prior; MPFM improves over DPDL by 0.5–2.6 AUC points (e.g., +1.7 on AITEX, +2.5 on ELPV, +2.6 on Hyper-Kvasir in ten-shot). AHL (Zhu et al., 2024), which relies on heterogeneous anomaly simulation, is surpassed by 1.7–11.2 points, with the largest gaps on ELPV (+11.2 ten-shot) and Mastcam (+11.3 ten-shot), indicating that simulation-based coverage cannot match explicit multi-modal normality modeling. DRA (Ding et al., 2022), the augmentation-and-exposure baseline, trails MPFM by 2.3–24.5 points across datasets. The three non-OSAD baselines (FLOS, SAOE, MLEP) are consistently weaker, with MLEP and SAOE particularly struggling on medical datasets (e.g., SAOE drops to 0.498 on Hyper-Kvasir one-shot vs. MPFM's 0.835). Under the harder single-class protocol, MPFM's advantage over DPDL widens to 2.7 points on Mastcam and 2.4 on Hyper-Kvasir (one-shot), confirming that the Gaussian mixture prototype space better handles unseen anomaly types.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the prototype-based open-set supervised anomaly detection (OSAD) paradigm initiated by DPDL (Wang et al., 2025a), which models normality with a single unimodal Gaussian and transports features via diffusion. MPFM departs from this by replacing the unimodal prior with a Gaussian mixture prototype space and, more fundamentally, by modeling the flow-matching velocity field itself as a conditional GMM rather than regressing a single velocity vector—a formulation inspired by GMFlow (Chen et al., 2025). The Mutual Information Maximization Regularizer (MIMR) is a novel addition that has no direct counterpart in prior OSAD or flow-matching literature, serving to prevent prototype collapse by jointly minimizing conditional entropy and maximizing marginal entropy of component assignments.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a concrete and well-motivated limitation: unimodal normality assumptions cause false positives on rare but legitimate normal patterns, a problem particularly acute in industrial and medical imaging where intra-class diversity is high. The GMM-velocity formulation is a clean, theoretically grounded extension of flow matching that could transfer to other distribution-transport tasks beyond anomaly detection. Open questions include whether the shared-variance assumption and K-means++ initialization remain adequate at scale, how the method behaves with more than image-level supervision (e.g., pixel-level masks), and whether the approach generalizes to video or 3D anomaly detection where multi-modality is even more pronounced.
