## Problem

Quadratic attention complexity is the central bottleneck in long-context LLM inference, and existing acceleration methods either impose block-level sparsity that retains irrelevant tokens or permanently evict tokens based on early-layer importance estimates. Because token importance shifts substantially across both layers and attention heads, irreversible eviction decisions discard tokens that later become critical, leading to sub-optimal accuracy-speedup trade-offs. The paper asks whether token-level sparsification can be made dynamic and reversible so that each head can independently re-select its relevant tokens at every layer.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261994/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During prefill, the method consumes per-head Q, K, V tensors of shape (L × d) from a long-context sequence and produces a full-length attention output tensor of shape (L × d) that is added to the residual connection. Unselected token positions in the output are zeroed (equivalent to a hard attention mask), while the residual path preserves their prior-layer representations, making the sparsification reversible. The compressed Q, K, V tensors remain dense and contiguous in memory, so the attention computation is a standard dense operation on a reduced (L′ × L′) matrix that is directly compatible with FlashAttention and other optimized kernels without modification.

## Method

Token Sparse Attention operates in two stages: (1) per-head compression, where a Dynamic Token Coverage policy scores token importance using a lightweight proxy attention map computed from recent queries, determines a layer-level sparsity budget via a cumulative-mass threshold τ, and selects top-k tokens independently for each head; and (2) decompression, where the compressed attention output is scattered back into a zero-initialized full-length tensor before the residual addition. A Sparse Layer Selection criterion based on Inter-Layer Representation Drift identifies which layers have sufficiently stable token representations to tolerate sparsification, applied as a one-time preprocessing step. The key architectural insight is that because the full sequence dimension is restored at every layer, tokens skipped in one attention operation remain available for re-selection in subsequent layers and across different heads, enabling head-specific and layer-adaptive sparsity without any permanent information loss. This design is fully composable with existing sparse attention kernels (e.g., FlexPrefill, Minference, X-Attention), providing an orthogonal token-level acceleration on top of block-level or pattern-based sparsity.

## Results

On RULER (LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct), Token Sparse Attention achieves 87.02% average accuracy with ×1.36 attention speedup over FlashAttention (87.01%, ×1.00), and when composed with FlexPrefill reaches 87.27% accuracy at ×2.76 speedup (vs. ×2.44 for FlexPrefill alone). At 128K context, the method delivers up to ×3.23 attention speedup with less than 1% accuracy degradation. Against token-eviction baselines at matched speedup (~×1.5), Token Sparse Attention attains 86.84% RULER average, outperforming FastKV (85.64%), GemFilter (85.12%), and PyramidInfer (78.49%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FlashAttention (dense baseline), Token Sparse Attention matches or marginally improves accuracy (87.02% vs. 87.01% on LLaMA RULER) while delivering ×1.36 attention speedup at 128K. Composed with FlexPrefill (block-sparse), it preserves the 87.27% average accuracy exactly while boosting speedup from ×2.44 to ×2.76; with Minference (structured sparse), speedup increases from ×1.12 to ×1.38 at a small 0.44% accuracy cost. Against token-eviction methods at matched ~×1.5 speedup, Token Sparse Attention (86.84%) substantially outperforms FastKV (85.64%), GemFilter (85.12%), and PyramidInfer (78.49%), with the largest gap over PyramidInfer (8.35 points). In the appendix, composing with SeerAttention raises speedup from ×2.19 to ×2.47 at matched accuracy, and with X-Attention from ×2.72 to ×3.49 with negligible accuracy change. The paper positions Token Sparse Attention as orthogonal to all these methods rather than a replacement, since it operates at the Q/K/V tensor level while the others operate at the attention-map or hidden-state level.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of two existing lines of work: structured sparse attention (Minference, FlexPrefill, SeerAttention, X-Attention) that bypasses low-importance regions of the attention map, and token-eviction methods (PyramidInfer, FastKV, GemFilter) that permanently discard tokens after early-layer selection. The key conceptual departure is the 'compress-then-decompress' paradigm: rather than irreversibly evicting tokens or imposing block-level sparsity, Token Sparse Attention performs per-head token selection within each layer and scatters the output back to the full sequence, making the operation reversible and head-specific. This design makes the method orthogonal to and composable with existing sparse attention kernels, positioning it as a complementary acceleration layer rather than a replacement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the demonstrated complementarity: Token Sparse Attention consistently pushes the accuracy-speedup Pareto frontier outward when stacked on top of FlashAttention, FlexPrefill, Minference, SeerAttention, and X-Attention, achieving up to 3.23× attention speedup at 128K context with <1% accuracy loss. The drift-based layer selection criterion provides a model-agnostic, one-time preprocessing step that practitioners can adopt without retraining. Open questions include extending the mechanism to the decoding phase (where KV cache bandwidth dominates), adapting to multimodal settings, and understanding whether the benefits persist at even longer contexts (256K+) or with larger model families. The method's reliance on a small set of recent queries for scoring also raises questions about robustness in highly non-stationary or adversarial long contexts.
