## Problem

Visuo-tactile sensors (GelSight, DIGIT, DenseTact2.0, etc.) exhibit extreme heterogeneity in geometry, illumination, and color, making tactile images from different sensors drastically different even under identical contact conditions. Existing cross-sensor methods (T3, CTTP, SITR) either rely on reconstruction decoders that mask true transferability, omit evaluation on genuinely unseen sensors, or fail to capture fine-grained semantic properties like material identity, limiting practical deployment across diverse hardware.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265926/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes raw tactile images (typically 128×128 or 256×256 RGB frames) from any visuo-tactile sensor as input and produces a sensor-agnostic d-dimensional feature vector (d=2048 from ResNet-50, compressed to 768-dim in Stage 2 to match CLIP's embedding space). During Stage 2, the tactile feature is aligned against frozen CLIP vision and language embeddings from real-world tactile-vision-language triplets. Downstream evaluation uses linear probing on the frozen representation for classification, pose regression, or zero-shot material identification across seen and unseen sensor hardware.

## Method

CTSRL introduces a Cross-Sensor Modulator (CSM) that computes channel-wise modulation coefficients via a sensor-specific (or universal) embedding combined with a sample-dependent latent vector, adaptively rescaling features to suppress hardware-specific biases while preserving semantic structure. Training proceeds in two stages: (1) Cross-sensor Representation Learning on aligned synthetic data using a normalized Barlow Twins objective (with a critical 1/D normalization factor) applied simultaneously as cross-sensor alignment between paired images from different sensors and intra-sensor enhancement within each sensor to prevent dimensional collapse; (2) Cross-modal Semantic Alignment on real-world tactile-vision-language triplets using InfoNCE loss against frozen CLIP vision and language encoders, which injects fine-grained material and texture semantics absent from simulation. A random substitution strategy replaces sensor-specific embeddings with a universal one during training, forcing the model to learn hardware-agnostic feature mapping logic that transfers to unseen sensors at inference.

## Results

On the SITR Bench (7 heterogeneous sensors), CTSRL (Stage 1) achieves 95.00% intra-sensor and 88.52% inter-sensor object classification accuracy, outperforming SITR (90.23% / 81.94%) and ResNet-PT (75.44% / 73.03%). On the challenging DIGIT-Mini transfer, CTSRL reaches 80.62% accuracy and 0.79 mm pose RMSE, improving over SITR by ~13% in accuracy and ~30% in RMSE. After cross-modal semantic alignment (Stage 2), CTSRL further boosts fine-grained material classification to 87.70% on TAG and 48.33% on Cloth, surpassing AnyTouch+ (81.39% / 35.63%) using the same training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CTSRL outperforms all five baselines on the primary SITR Bench. Against SITR (the closest competitor), CTSRL gains +4.8% intra-sensor, +6.6% inter-sensor, and +12.8% DIGIT-Mini classification accuracy, while reducing DIGIT-Mini pose RMSE from 1.12 mm to 0.79 mm (~30% reduction). CTTP performs catastrophically on cross-sensor transfer (12.24% inter-sensor, 9.03% DIGIT-Mini), even falling below random-initialization ResNet, indicating its contrastive objective fails to generalize to unseen hardware. On the T3 Bench, CTSRL edges out T3 (22.85 vs. 22.30 on W→S classification; 2.73 vs. 2.85 mm on M→D pose) despite T3 using sensor-specific encoders. For fine-grained semantics, CTSRL (Stage 2) surpasses AnyTouch+ by +6.3% on TAG material, +12.7% on Cloth, and +5.1% on YCB-Slide, all with identical training data. The one exception is OF Real material classification, where AnyTouch (79.26%) outperforms CTSRL (50.14%), but this is because AnyTouch was trained on OF Real data (marked with †), making it a seen-sensor result rather than a fair generalization comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

CTSRL extends the line of cross-sensor tactile representation learning initiated by T3 (sensor-specific encoders with shared transformer), CTTP (contrastive tactile-to-tactile pretraining), and SITR (supervised contrastive + reconstruction). It departs from these by introducing an explicit Cross-Sensor Modulator that adaptively removes sensor-specific biases via learned sensor embeddings, rather than relying on reconstruction decoders or task-specific architectures to implicitly handle heterogeneity. The two-stage design borrows the universal-embedding random-substitution strategy from AnyTouch and the frozen-CLIP-anchor paradigm from UniTouch, but applies them in a novel combination: Barlow Twins-style cross-sensor alignment on synthetic data for structural invariants, followed by InfoNCE-based cross-modal alignment on real data for semantic enrichment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in robotic manipulation: the inability to transfer tactile models across heterogeneous sensor hardware without expensive re-collection and retraining. The 20× data efficiency over SITR (50k vs. 1000k samples) and the explicit disentanglement of sensor bias from physical signal offer a cleaner inductive bias than prior reconstruction-based approaches. Key open questions remain: the framework is limited to single-frame static contact (no temporal dynamics), Stage 1 depends entirely on synthetic data whose fidelity to real-world micro-textures is unverified, and no real-robot manipulation validation has been performed. Extending CTSRL to dynamic tactile sequences and validating on physical robots would be the natural next steps.
