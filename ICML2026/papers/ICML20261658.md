## Problem

Existing MORL methods either require decision makers to specify precise reward weights upfront (scalarization) or generate hundreds of Pareto-optimal policies without knowing which are practically relevant. Interactive MORL frameworks that actively learn and adapt to evolving DM preferences to guide the policy search have received limited attention, leaving a gap between the targeted nature of single-policy methods and the exploratory capabilities of multi-policy approaches.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261658/cropped_page2_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes lightweight pairwise comparisons (better/worse/indifferent) between policy pairs from a human DM, requiring only ~40 interactions total, alongside a standard MOMDP environment with continuous state and action spaces. It outputs a small set of non-dominated policies specifically aligned with the DM's implicit preferences (e.g., 'running like a dog') rather than the full Pareto front, along with a calibrated GP utility model over objective vectors. Notably, the DM never specifies numerical weights or reward functions; all preference information is elicited qualitatively through comparisons of observed robot behaviors.

## Method

PBMORL is a three-module iterative framework: (1) a Seeding stage decomposes the MOMDP into N scalarized subproblems via MOEA/D with Das-Dennis weight vectors to bootstrap diverse competent policies; (2) a Preference Elicitation module actively selects informative policy pairs using a UCB-style acquisition score, fits a GP with probit likelihood to the pairwise comparisons, and translates the learned utility into a biased weight distribution by pulling reference weights toward a data-driven region of interest (ROI) identified from top-scoring policies; (3) a Policy Optimization module runs parallel PPO on a mixture of exploitation tasks (refining ROI-aligned policies) and exploration tasks (probing nearby trade-offs with biased weights). The key design choice is that the learned GP utility is never used as the per-policy RL objective; instead, it only reallocates optimization effort across scalarized subproblems, preserving the multi-policy search structure while steering it toward preference-consistent regions.

## Results

On the UNITREE GO2 quadrupedal robot task, PBMORL under high-speed preference achieves 5.54 m/s with 28.46 N·m average torque, reducing torque by 37.4% compared to the best scalarized PPO baseline (45.43 N·m) while matching its speed. On the MuJOCO and MMSD benchmarks, PBMORL achieves the lowest ε*(Π) (distance to the ideal golden policy) on all complex tasks: 2.362 on Humanoid-v2 (f₁) vs. 5.599–9.554 for baselines, and 0.000 on MMSD (f₂) vs. 0.672–7.020. PBMORL requires only 40 pairwise human interactions to identify preference-aligned policies, and maintains strong performance even with as few as 10 interactions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PBMORL consistently outperforms all four preference-based MORL baselines (MOMPO, META-MORL, MORL-Adaptation, MORAL) on complex tasks. On Humanoid-v2 (f₁), PBMORL achieves ε*=2.362, substantially beating MORAL (5.599), MOMPO (9.292), META-MORL (8.679), and MORL-Adaptation (9.554). On MMSD (f₂), PBMORL reaches ε*=0.000 (essentially finding the golden policy), while the best baseline MORAL achieves 0.672 and MORL-Adaptation 7.020. Against conventional multi-policy MORL methods (PGMORL, PDMORL, RA, MOIA), PBMORL also wins on complex tasks like Humanoid-v2 (2.362 vs. 3.796–4.719) and MMSD, though on simpler tasks like Swimmer-v2 and HalfCheetah-v2 the gap narrows or PBMORL ties with the best baseline. On the UNITREE GO2 robot, PBMORL dominates nearly all scalarized PPO policies in objective space, with the only exception being one complex-reward configuration (π^c₅) that marginally exceeds PBMORL's speed at the cost of higher torque.

## Relation to Prior Work

PBMORL sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: decomposition-based multi-objective optimization (MOEA/D, Das-Dennis weight generation) and GP-based preference learning (Chu & Ghahramani, 2005). It departs from single-policy scalarization MORL by avoiding the need for a priori weight specification, and from full Pareto-front approximation methods (PGMORL, PDMORL) by using learned preferences to bias where optimization effort is allocated rather than covering the entire front. The key conceptual departure from prior preference-based MORL (MORL-Adaptation, META-MORL, MOMPO, MORAL) is that the learned GP utility is not used as a direct reward or to select a single weight vector, but instead reshapes the distribution over scalarized subproblems in a parallel multi-policy search, preserving exploration while concentrating effort in a preference-consistent region of interest.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical gap: decision makers in robotics and engineering often cannot articulate numeric reward weights but can easily compare two behaviors, and PBMORL shows that 40 such comparisons suffice to identify strongly preferred policies on a high-dimensional quadruped robot task. Open questions include how the method handles genuinely non-convex Pareto fronts (the Tchebycheff ablation suggests robustness but only on convex PFs), how it scales to truly fuzzy or shifting preferences over long horizons, and whether the GP-based preference model remains the best choice as the number of objectives grows beyond 5. The simulated-oracle setup for MuJOCO benchmarks, while standard, means the real human-in-the-loop validation is limited to the single quadruped task.
