## Problem

Training energy-based generative models (EBMs) is challenging: direct maximum likelihood requires intractable nested sampling, while the standard Denoising Score Matching (DSM) objective, though efficient, suffers from mode blindness—distributions with identical modes but different mixture weights produce nearly identical scores, preventing correct recovery of relative mode proportions. Existing remedies such as Fokker-Planck or Bayes-consistency regularization are computationally expensive and remain susceptible to the same blindness in the small-step regime where their approximations are valid.

## Input / Output

The method consumes samples from the source distribution (e.g., data at t=0 for diffusion models, or both endpoints for stochastic interpolants) plus Gaussian noise, and a set of N randomly sampled time points from U([0,T]). It produces a time-dependent energy function U_t^θ and a learned log-normalizing constant F_t^θ (implemented as a bias on the network's last layer). The learned energies are directly usable for downstream tasks—Boltzmann Generator sampling via SMC, compositional generation via product/mixture of learned densities, and thermodynamic integration for free-energy differences—without requiring strict normalization. Computationally, the joint DSM+DiffCLF objective requires N+1 forward passes per time step, only N−1 more than DSM alone.

## Method

DiffCLF reframes log-density estimation as a supervised multi-class classification problem: given N time points t_1,…,t_N, a sample y drawn from p_{t_i} is assigned label i, and the classifier's posterior is p(c=i|y) = p_{t_i}^θ(y) / Σ_j p_{t_j}^θ(y), a softmax over the parameterized EBMs. Minimizing the categorical cross-entropy of this classifier (Equation 10) jointly with DSM yields a unique minimizer at the true marginals (Proposition 3.2), and unlike score-based objectives, the classification loss is not mode-blind because the posterior probabilities directly encode relative mode weights. In the binary (N=2) case the objective reduces to a sigmoid-based loss that provably converges to Time Score Matching in the continuous-time limit (Proposition 4.1), connecting DiffCLF to prior density-ratio-estimation work. The log-normalizing constant F_t^θ is learned as an additional bias parameter, following the spirit of Noise Contrastive Estimation, providing extra degrees of freedom that aid optimization without requiring exact normalization.

## Results

On 40-mode Gaussian mixtures (MOG-40) across dimensions 8–128, DiffCLF (L_clf + L_DSM) achieves classification losses of 4.01–4.41, dramatically lower than DSM alone (8.51–383.53) and CtSM+DSM (6.13–20.86), while matching or exceeding them on Fisher Divergence (2.00–6.91) and MMD (0.69–3.54). For free-energy estimation on alanine dipeptide, DiffCLF improves the TI estimate from 27.30±0.45 (Máté et al. baseline) to 29.02±0.41, closer to the reference value of 29.43±0.01. On molecular systems (Müller-Brown, Alanine Dipeptide, Chignolin), DiffCLF matches Fokker-Planck regularization quality while accelerating training by 1.5–2.5×.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DSM alone (Song et al., 2021), DiffCLF matches or slightly improves Fisher Divergence and MMD while reducing classification loss by 2–90× (e.g., 4.40 vs. 383.53 at dim 128), confirming that DSM's mode blindness causes severe energy misestimation at scale. Against Conditional Time Score Matching (Yu et al., 2025; Guth et al., 2025), DiffCLF consistently achieves lower classification loss (4.01–4.41 vs. 6.13–20.86) and lower MMD at dimensions 8–64, though CtSM slightly wins on MMD at dim 128 (5.20 vs. 3.54). Against Fokker-Planck regularization (Plainer et al., 2025) on molecular systems, DiffCLF achieves comparable energy quality (Langevin dynamics samples) while training 1.5–2.5× faster. For free-energy estimation (Máté et al., 2025), adding DiffCLF to the base TI objective improves the alanine dipeptide solvation free energy from 27.30±0.45 to 29.02±0.41 kJ/mol, much closer to the reference 29.43±0.01.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiffCLF builds directly on Denoising Score Matching as its backbone objective, adding a multi-class classification loss over noise-level marginals that is structurally analogous to Noise Contrastive Estimation (Gutmann & Hyvärinen, 2010) and, in the binary limit, provably recovers Time Score Matching (Choi et al., 2022). It departs from Fokker-Planck and Bayes-rule self-consistency regularizers (Plainer et al., 2025; He et al., 2026a) by operating directly on energy values rather than higher-order derivatives, and from MBAR by fully parameterizing the energy functions rather than assuming them known. The multi-class formulation also connects to the broader line of work on density ratio estimation via classification (Matsuda & Hyvärinen, 2019; Yu et al., 2025), but embeds the parameterized marginals directly into the classifier rather than approximating time-scores.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating mode blindness while remaining computationally comparable to DSM (N+1 vs. 2 network evaluations), DiffCLF makes learned energies practically usable for downstream tasks—Boltzmann Generators, compositional sampling, and free-energy estimation—that were previously unreliable with score-only training. The clean theoretical guarantees (consistency, uniqueness, asymptotic normality) and the 1.5–2.5× speedup over FPE regularization make it an attractive drop-in addition to existing diffusion/SI pipelines. However, all experiments are confined to low-dimensional synthetic and molecular benchmarks; scaling to image or text generation, where SMC-based composition has shown promise, remains an open and important question.
