## Problem

Existing backdoor attacks on text-to-image diffusion models rely on discrete textual triggers (fixed words or syntax patterns) that are highly enumerable and produce consistent cross-attention signatures, making them vulnerable to input-level defenses like prompt perturbation and attention-consistency detection. No prior work has defined backdoor triggers over continuous semantic representations, leaving a gap in understanding whether semantically equivalent but textually diverse prompts can be exploited as a unified, hard-to-enumerate trigger condition.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard text prompts fed into a pre-trained T2I diffusion model (SDv1.5 or SDXL) and produces a generated image. The trigger is not a specific string but a semantic composition (subject, action, object, scene) that can be expressed in many surface forms; any prompt whose CLIP embedding falls within the trigger's semantic neighborhood activates the backdoor. The backdoored model outputs an image depicting a multi-entity target scene (e.g., an astronaut riding a horse on Mars) instead of the prompted content, while clean prompts produce images indistinguishable from the benign model.

## Method

SemBD injects a semantic-level backdoor by distillation-based editing of the key (W_k) and value (W_v) projection matrices in cross-attention layers, aligning the projected representations of semantically equivalent trigger prompts with those of a multi-entity target prompt from a frozen benign teacher. The training objective combines a backdoor alignment loss (minimizing L2 distance between trigger and target key/value projections) with a semantic regularization loss that constrains partial-semantic substrings to retain their benign projections, preventing unintended activation under incomplete semantics. A length-prioritized sampling schedule balances coverage of short and near-complete substrings during optimization. The multi-entity target design distributes cross-attention across multiple related entities, weakening detection based on attention consistency. The paper provides a theoretical stability bound showing that semantically equivalent prompts produce bounded variation in cross-attention output, and an AMSGrad convergence analysis establishing O(1/√T) optimality gap for the distillation procedure.

## Results

SemBD achieves a 100% Attack Success Rate (ASR) with a CLIP_p of 28.16 on both SDv1.5 and SDXL, matching or exceeding all baselines in attack effectiveness while maintaining strong utility preservation (FID 23.83, CLIP_c 25.32, LPIPS 0.33 — all close to the benign model's 24.49/26.44/0.00). Critically, SemBD reduces the Detection Success Rate (DSR) of state-of-the-art input-level defenses to as low as 2% (T2IShield_CDA) and 12% (NaviT2I), compared to 68–100% DSR for most baselines. The method also demonstrates robustness to fine-tuning (ASR >90% after full or LoRA fine-tuning) and pruning (ASR >97% up to 30% pruning ratio).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SemBD ties EvilEdit at 100% ASR but surpasses it in CLIP_p (28.16 vs. 27.78) and achieves substantially lower DSR across all four defenses (12%/20.05%/25.80%/2.00% vs. 22.19%/37.00%/35.20%/10.80%), making it the most stealthy high-ASR attack. Against word-level baselines (VillanDiffusion, Rickrolling, BadT2I), SemBD dominates on both effectiveness (100% vs. 53.6–97.56% ASR) and stealthiness (DSR reduced by 50–87 percentage points on NaviT2I and UFID). IBA achieves the lowest T2IShield DSR (0.20% CDA) but suffers severe utility degradation (CLIP_c 15.85, FID 48.70) and remains highly detectable under NaviT2I (82.95% DSR), whereas SemBD preserves utility near benign levels while keeping all defense DSRs below 26%. Personalization and BadT2I, which rely on data poisoning or fine-tuning, are both outperformed on ASR and remain detectable by at least two of the four defenses at >40% DSR.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the backdoor attack literature on T2I diffusion models by shifting the trigger formulation from discrete textual patterns (word-level attacks like VillanDiffusion, Rickrolling, EvilEdit; syntax-level attacks like IBA) to continuous semantic representations. Methodologically, it repurposes training-free model editing of cross-attention key/value projections—previously used for concept manipulation (Orgad et al., 2023; Gandikota et al., 2024) and single-entity backdoor injection (Wang et al., 2024a; Li et al., 2024b)—into a distillation-based framework that aligns trigger semantics with multi-entity target semantics. The key departure is defining triggers over semantic compositions (subject, action, object, scene) rather than fixed strings, combined with a semantic regularization loss that has no direct analogue in prior T2I backdoor work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SemBD exposes a previously unaddressed vulnerability class: backdoors that activate on meaning rather than surface form, rendering the entire family of input-level defenses (prompt enumeration, perturbation, token analysis) largely ineffective. The practical implication is that organizations deploying pre-trained T2I models from open-source platforms face a threat that current inspection pipelines cannot detect. Open questions include whether semantic-level backdoors generalize to other modalities (e.g., text-to-video, image editing), whether model-level defenses (beyond fine-tuning and pruning) can detect representation-level triggers, and whether the white-box weight-poisoning assumption can be relaxed to more realistic black-box or data-poisoning settings.
