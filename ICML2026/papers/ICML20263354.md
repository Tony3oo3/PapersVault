## Problem

Controlling pre-trained unconditional diffusion models at sampling time remains difficult: training-based approaches (classifier guidance, CFG) lock the model to fixed conditions and require retraining for new tasks, while training-free methods (DPS, TFG) must backpropagate through the denoiser at every step, incurring high computational cost and unstable gradients. The paper asks whether the intrinsic information structure of the diffusion process itself can provide a task-agnostic coordinate system for guidance, eliminating both retraining and denoiser backpropagation.

## Input / Output

At sampling time, the method consumes a noisy diffusion sample x_t at timestep t and a guidance signal h(x_0) (a one-hot class label, a CLIP text embedding, or a flattened binary segmentation mask), and produces a guided image via a standard DDIM sampler augmented with a per-step guidance vector. The spectral network f_φ is a time-conditioned ResNet (9M–91M parameters, substantially lighter than the 36M–121M U-Net denoiser) that maps (x_t, t) → R^K. Guidance coefficients ĉ_t are precomputed offline on a reference set D_ref and cached as orthogonal matrices Φ_t, so the same representations serve all three conditioning modalities without retraining—only h is swapped at inference.

## Method

The core contribution is a spectral decomposition of the conditional expectation operator T_t: H_0 → H_t that maps clean-data functions to their noisy-data conditional expectations. The paper proves T_t is compact and self-adjoint (via its covariance operator T_t T_t*), and that its leading singular functions form a time-indexed low-dimensional basis capturing exactly the features recoverable at each noise level. A self-supervised objective—maximizing cross-view correlation between two independently diffused copies of the same clean sample, with a whitening constraint (analogous to VICReg/Barlow Twins)—is shown to be a variational estimator for the top-K left singular functions (Theorem 4.2). At sampling, guidance reduces to a linear projection: the precomputed coefficients ĉ_t are applied to the network output f_φ^w(x_t, t), and the gradient of a task-specific loss (log-likelihood, cosine similarity, or MSE) is taken through f_φ only, never through the denoiser. This yields 4× faster per-step latency than TFG while improving CIFAR-10 accuracy by 37 percentage points over the strongest training-free baseline, and the spectral transition over time reveals the optimal guidance window.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, Spectral Guidance achieves 89.4% top-1 accuracy and FID 70.7, surpassing the strongest training-free baseline TFG (52.0% accuracy, FID 91.7) by 37.4 percentage points while also improving fidelity. On CelebA-HQ, it reaches 91.5% accuracy on Gender+Age and 88.3% on Gender+Hair (vs. 75.2% and 76.0% for TFG), 0.80 IoU on mask guidance (vs. 0.78), and 0.64 VQAScore on CLIP text guidance (vs. 0.62). On ImageNet, it achieves 41.6% accuracy (vs. 40.9% for TFG) with FID 183 (vs. 176 for TFG). Per-step sampling latency is 21.7 ms, nearly 4× faster than TFG (81.2 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Spectral Guidance dominates all six training-free baselines on validity (accuracy/IoU/VQAScore) across every task. The largest margin is on CIFAR-10, where it outperforms TFG (the strongest training-free method) by 37.4 percentage points in accuracy (89.4% vs. 52.0%) and also achieves the best FID (70.7 vs. 91.7). On CelebA-HQ attribute tasks, it leads TFG by 16.3 and 12.3 points respectively, though LGD achieves better log KID (−5.10 vs. −2.98 on Gender+Age), indicating a fidelity–diversity trade-off at high guidance strength. On ImageNet, the accuracy gap narrows to 0.7 points (41.6% vs. 40.9%) and TFG retains a slight FID edge (176 vs. 183). For mask guidance, Spectral Guidance edges out TFG and FreeDoM (0.80 vs. 0.78 IoU) while reusing the same spectral representations learned for categorical and CLIP tasks. DPS and MPGD are consistently the weakest baselines, with DPS degrading severely on FID (172 on CIFAR-10) due to its reliance on posterior-mean point estimates. Classifier Guidance (CG) from Dhariwal & Nichol (2021) is shown in Fig. 5(a) to achieve a better accuracy–FID frontier than Spectral Guidance on CIFAR-10, but requires training a dedicated noise-aware classifier per task, which Spectral Guidance avoids entirely.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DDPM framework (Ho et al., 2020) and repurposes the whitening-plus-cross-correlation structure of VICReg (Bardes et al., 2021) by replacing hand-crafted augmentations with the diffusion process itself as the SSL augmentation. It departs from both classifier guidance (which requires task-specific retraining) and training-free guidance methods like DPS, LGD, and TFG (which require backpropagating through the denoiser at every sampling step) by learning a task-agnostic spectral basis of the conditional expectation operator offline, then reducing guidance to a linear projection. Conceptually, it is closest to NoiseCLR (Dalva & Yanardag, 2024) in using SSL to discover structure in diffusion models, but targets information-preserving spectral coordinates rather than latent-space editing directions, and differs from Park et al. (2023) and Chen et al. (2024) by decomposing the diffusion operator rather than the denoiser's Jacobian.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 37-percentage-point accuracy gap over the strongest training-free baseline on CIFAR-10, combined with 4× faster sampling and a single representation that supports labels, CLIP text, and spatial masks without retraining, makes this a practically attractive alternative to both CFG and iterative training-free guidance for moderate-scale pixel-space models. The discovery of a spectral phase transition that pinpoints the optimal guidance window offers a principled, data-driven replacement for heuristic timestep schedules. Key open questions include whether the framework extends cleanly to latent diffusion models and large-scale text-to-image systems, whether the reference-set requirement for coefficient estimation can be eliminated, and whether the low-rank structure remains sufficient for high-constraint inverse problems like inpainting.
