## Problem

Popular ToM benchmarks contain pervasive shortcuts (spurious causal or lexical correlations) that let models reach up to 99% accuracy without genuine mental-state reasoning, creating a false sense of ToM and inverting the relative ranking of post-training methods. This confounds the central question the paper tackles: whether reinforcement fine-tuning with explicit reasoning chains (Thinking-RFT) can genuinely elicit robust ToM skills when trained on shortcut-free data, and how it compares to SFT and RL without reasoning.

## Input / Output

Input comprises ToM narratives (agent–object interaction stories), conversational transcripts, or video clips sampled at 16 FPS paired with partial/full text descriptions, each followed by a multiple-choice ToM question spanning categories like belief, intention, attitude, and multi-hop location. The model outputs a structured response: a chain-of-thought reasoning trace in <think> tags that sequentially tracks agent observations, object state changes, and nested belief attributions, followed by a final answer (option letter or short phrase) in <answer> tags. For multimodal settings, the video stream provides perceptual grounding that the reasoning trace must integrate with the textual narrative.

## Method

The core method is Thinking-RFT: GRPO-based reinforcement fine-tuning with a verifiable reward (format compliance + answer accuracy) that explicitly elicits chain-of-thought reasoning before the final answer, applied to shortcut-free ToM datasets identified via a two-part auditing framework (LLM-guided causal/procedural rule discovery with statistical significance testing, plus lexical mutual-information scoring). The key finding is that the joint effect of RL and explicit reasoning is essential: No-Thinking-RFT (RL without CoT) underperforms Thinking-RFT by ~7% on average, and SFT underperforms both, with the gap widening to +10% on second-order queries and +7% in multimodal settings. Attention-map analysis reveals that Thinking-RFT trains the model to concentrate on minimal 'anchor cues'—the specific sentences encoding state changes and agent observations that constitute the causal hinge—rather than attending diffusely to all narrative content, which explains its superior generalization to unseen environments, higher-order queries, and counterfactual perturbations.

## Results

On OpenToM (narrative, 7B), Thinking-RFT achieves 89.14% overall accuracy, outperforming SFT (83.14%) by +6.0% and zero-shot (46.43%) by +42.7%. On ToMATO (conversational, 7B), Thinking-RFT reaches 90.00% vs. SFT's 87.92% (+2.08%). On multimodal benchmarks (MMToM + MuMA-ToM), Thinking-RFT achieves 82.20% vs. SFT's 74.75% (+7.45%). The advantage is most pronounced on second-order reasoning (+10.3% over SFT) and in multimodal settings, where RFT's gains are roughly double those in narrative tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares Thinking-RFT against two state-of-the-art inference-time ToM algorithms: SimToM (Wilf et al., ACL 2024) and AutoToM (Zhang et al., NeurIPS 2025). On OpenToM (7B), Thinking-RFT (89.14%) outperforms SimToM (49.71%) by +39.4% and AutoToM (56.86%) by +32.3%. On the multimodal MMToM/MuMA-ToM benchmarks, Thinking-RFT (82.20%) surpasses SimToM (50.10%) by +32.1% and AutoToM (58.00%) by +24.2%. Both inference-time baselines operate around a frozen strong backbone (e.g., GPT-4o) with complex multi-step prompting or Bayesian scaffolding, whereas Thinking-RFT internalizes the reasoning into a 7B model via post-training, achieving substantially higher accuracy with far lower inference-time complexity. The gap is especially large on second-order queries, where SimToM and AutoToM struggle with recursive belief attribution while Thinking-RFT maintains strong performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DeepSeek-R1/GRPO paradigm for rule-based reinforcement fine-tuning with verifiable rewards, adapting it to Theory of Mind post-training. It departs from prior ToM post-training studies (e.g., Lu et al. 2025, Sarangi & Salam 2025) that used shortcut-prone benchmarks like Hi-ToM and ExploreToM and concluded SFT outperforms RFT; here, the authors first audit and remove those shortcuts, then show the ranking inverts. It also contrasts with inference-time ToM approaches (SimToM, AutoToM) that rely on complex multi-step prompting around frozen backbones, arguing that the same reasoning can be internalized via post-training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The discovery that 4 of 8 popular ToM benchmarks are solvable via simple heuristics (reaching up to 99% accuracy without genuine mental-state reasoning) is a significant diagnostic contribution that likely invalidates prior post-training conclusions in the field. The shortcut-auditing framework provides actionable guidance for future dataset construction. Key limitations include evaluation only up to 2nd-order ToM (no higher-order shortcut-free data exists), synthetic QA evaluation rather than functional/behavioral testing, and no cold-start SFT before RFT. Open questions include whether the causal-anchoring behavior observed in attention maps transfers to genuinely open-ended social reasoning and whether the findings hold for larger models or different base architectures.
