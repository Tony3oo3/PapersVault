## Problem

Smaller Transformer-based language models exhibit a geometric phenomenon called embedding condensation, where token embeddings collapse into narrow cone-like subspaces as they pass through layers, reducing representational diversity. This paper systematically characterizes the phenomenon across multiple model families, shows it is not mitigated by knowledge distillation from larger models, and asks whether an explicit training objective can counteract it to improve small-model generalization without adding parameters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262909/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard text sequences (e.g., wikitext-103, C4) fed through pre-trained or from-scratch Transformer models. The dispersion loss is computed on the internal contextualized token embeddings at each layer—specifically, pairwise angular distances between all token positions within a sequence—rather than on the final output distribution. The model output remains standard next-token prediction; the loss is an auxiliary regularizer applied to intermediate representations, with a time complexity of O(N²F) per batch that is made tractable by subsampling tokens along the sequence dimension.

## Method

The paper proposes a dispersion loss L_disp = log Σ_{i≠j} exp(−arccos(cossim(z_i, z_j))/(πτ)) that maps pairwise cosine similarities to angular distances on the unit hypersphere and encourages uniform spreading of all token embeddings. It is added as a regularizer to the standard cross-entropy training objective (L = L_train + λ_disp · L_disp) and can be applied during mid-training or full pre-training. Three alternative formulations are also evaluated: decorrelation (minimizing off-diagonal covariance), ℓ₂-repel (pushing pairs apart in Euclidean space with norm regularization), and orthogonalization (penalizing only acute angles beyond a 90° margin). The canonical dispersion loss, using arccos for numerical stability and the log-sum-exp trick, proves most stable and effective, yielding consistent gains across 10 benchmarks in both mid-training and full pre-training settings.

## Results

During mid-training on 10 language understanding benchmarks, dispersion loss yields consistent average improvements across all model sizes: GPT2 (35.61 vs. 34.35 baseline), GPT2-m (39.35 vs. 37.80), Qwen3-0.6B (47.45 vs. 44.11), and Qwen3-1.7B (55.68 vs. 53.18). In full pre-training from scratch (Qwen3-0.6B, 156B tokens on C4), dispersion loss achieves a +1.17 average gain over the cross-entropy baseline (36.01 vs. 34.84), with particularly large improvements on PIQA (+4.0) and TruthfulQA (+7.4). Applying the loss to the deeper half of the network outperforms applying it to the earlier half (35.37 vs. 34.96 average on GPT2), confirming that the benefit arises from counteracting condensation where it is most severe.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares dispersion loss against two baselines that could potentially counteract embedding condensation: noisy embedding (Jain et al., 2024), which adds noise to token embeddings during training, and active forgetting (Chen et al., 2023), which periodically resets the token embedding layer. Dispersion loss achieves the highest average score across all four model sizes in mid-training, outperforming noisy embedding by +0.46 (GPT2), +1.19 (GPT2-m), +1.28 (Qwen3-0.6B), and +1.90 (Qwen3-1.7B) points. Active forgetting is the closest competitor, particularly on GPT2 (35.36 vs. 35.61) and Qwen3-0.6B (46.79 vs. 47.45), but dispersion loss consistently edges it out on average and achieves a better rank (3.2 vs. 3.2 on GPT2, 2.2 vs. 4.4 on GPT2-m, 3.2 vs. 4.0 on Qwen3-0.6B, 1.7 vs. 5.0 on Qwen3-1.7B). The three alternative dispersion formulations (decorrelation, ℓ₂-repel, orthogonalization) are generally less stable or slightly weaker, with ℓ₂-repel being the most volatile due to the fragile balance between pairwise repulsion and norm regularization. In the full pre-training setting, dispersion loss achieves a +1.17 average gain over the cross-entropy baseline, with the largest improvements on knowledge-heavy tasks (TruthfulQA +7.4, MedMCQA +3.0) rather than commonsense tasks (WinoGrande −3.5).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the dispersion loss from Wang & He (2025), originally designed for diffusion-based image generation, to Transformer language models by replacing cosine-distance with arccos angular distance and adding log-sum-exp stabilization. It connects empirically to the theoretical prediction of Geshkovski et al. (2025) that Transformer embeddings collapse at infinite depth, providing the first systematic layer-by-layer measurements across multiple model families. Unlike prior oversmoothing mitigations that modify architecture (layer aggregation, eigendecomposition, frequency rebalancing), this work targets embedding geometry directly through an explicit auxiliary training objective, and contrasts with knowledge distillation by showing that logit-level matching does not transfer representational geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a simple, drop-in training regularizer that yields consistent (if modest, ~1–2 point) gains across 10 benchmarks without increasing model size, offering a practical lever for improving small models in resource-constrained settings. The negative result that knowledge distillation from a 671B teacher does not transfer embedding dispersion is practically important, as it rules out a natural and cheap remedy. Open questions include whether dispersion loss benefits larger models (the authors note it may, since trained representations don't fully exploit available dimensionality), whether the gains compound with other scaling strategies, and whether the phenomenon and remedy generalize to non-linguistic modalities.
