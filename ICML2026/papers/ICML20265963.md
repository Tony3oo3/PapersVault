## Problem

Existing Text-to-Image Person Re-Identification (TI-ReID) methods retrieve only visible (RGB) pedestrian images, which severely degrades under nighttime or low-light conditions where color and texture cues are lost. Extending retrieval to infrared images introduces two compounding challenges: (1) color-related information is identity-critical for visible retrieval but identity-irrelevant interference for infrared retrieval, creating conflicting optimization objectives in a shared feature space, and (2) vision-language pretraining establishes strong color-entity correlations that do not transfer to single-channel infrared images, limiting the effectiveness of standard fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

The model takes a text description query (annotated from visible images of the same identity to mirror real-world eyewitness reports) and retrieves from two separate galleries: a visible RGB image gallery and an infrared image gallery, producing two ranked lists. Input images are resized to 384×128 pixels; visible images are additionally converted to a randomly selected single-channel (R, G, or B) variant for the color-irrelevant branch via Channel-Augmentation. Infrared images are single-channel and are fed exclusively into the color-irrelevant branch, reflecting the prior that color is irrelevant for thermal retrieval.

## Method

CSDT adopts a multi-branch CLIP architecture with duplicated shallow tokenizers for color-related and color-irrelevant pathways that share deep Transformer blocks. The Semantic Decoupling Learning (SDL) module performs triplet contrastive alignment separately in a color-related subspace (text vs. full RGB) and a color-irrelevant subspace (text vs. single-channel visible and infrared images), with an orthogonal loss enforcing independence between the two subspaces to prevent color leakage. The Semantic Distribution Transfer (SDT) module then minimizes a KL divergence between the softmax similarity distribution in the color-related subspace (treated as a frozen teacher) and the corresponding distribution in the color-irrelevant subspace, mixing in true matching labels via a parameter α to regularize the transfer. This allows pretrained text-visible alignment knowledge to guide text-infrared contrastive learning without requiring paired visible-infrared viewpoints, and the total loss is simply L_sdl + L_sdt.

## Results

On SYSU-TVI, CSDT achieves 66.21% Rank-1 and 47.63% mAP for text-to-infrared (T→IR) retrieval, and 96.13% Rank-1 and 88.19% mAP for text-to-visible (T→RGB) retrieval. On LLCM-TVI, it reaches 36.72% Rank-1 and 29.09% mAP for T→IR, and 65.57% Rank-1 and 55.37% mAP for T→RGB. The most substantial gains are on the T→IR task, where CSDT outperforms the strongest baseline (RDE+HAM) by 7.15% Rank-1 on SYSU-TVI and 2.13% Rank-1 on LLCM-TVI, while also improving T→RGB retrieval by 0.68% and 2.13% Rank-1 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CSDT outperforms all eight baselines on both datasets and both retrieval tasks. The largest margin is against the strongest combined baseline RDE+HAM: on SYSU-TVI, CSDT gains +7.15% R1 on T→IR (66.21 vs. 59.06) and +0.68% R1 on T→RGB (96.13 vs. 95.45); on LLCM-TVI the gaps are +2.13% R1 (T→IR) and +2.13% R1 (T→RGB). Against individual TI-ReID methods, the T→IR advantage is especially pronounced: HAM alone achieves only 31.05% R1 on T→IR (vs. 66.21% for CSDT), and MLLM4Text-ReID drops to 22.67%, indicating that standard fine-tuning of CLIP-based TI-ReID models transfers poorly to infrared retrieval. On T→RGB, HAM (95.66%) and RDE+HAM (95.45%) are the closest competitors, with CSDT's 96.13% representing a modest but consistent improvement. The results confirm that the core challenge in TVI-ReID is the T→IR direction, where color-irrelevant semantics dominate and CSDT's decoupling and distribution transfer mechanisms provide the most benefit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the TI-ReID line (text-to-visible person retrieval) by introducing infrared images as a second retrieval gallery, building directly on the triplet alignment loss from RDE (Qin et al., 2024) and the Channel-Augmentation technique from Ye et al. (2021a) for stripping color from visible images. Its core methodological departure from prior multi-modal ReID work (which typically learns a single unified cross-modal space) is the explicit decoupling into orthogonal color-related and color-irrelevant subspaces, with the orthogonal constraint borrowed from view-decoupled transformer designs (Zhang et al., 2024). The SDT module further departs from standard fine-tuning by using KL-divergence-based distribution matching to transfer pretrained text-visible alignment knowledge into the color-irrelevant subspace, rather than relying on direct contrastive supervision from infrared pairs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The TVI-ReID task and its two tri-modal datasets (SYSU-TVI, LLCM-TVI) fill a practical gap for 24-hour surveillance where visible imagery degrades at night, and the datasets emphasize color-irrelevant textual cues (accessory positions, items not carried) that prior TI-ReID benchmarks overlooked. A key limitation is that infrared text annotations are derived from visible images of the same identity rather than from actual infrared observations, which may not fully capture the information an eyewitness would describe from a thermal feed. Open questions include whether the color/color-irrelevant binary decoupling generalizes to additional spectral bands or modalities, and whether the approach scales to larger, more diverse deployment scenarios beyond the two relatively small datasets evaluated here.
