## Problem

LLMs' stochastic next-token prediction produces logically inconsistent reasoning chains and reward hacking, yet existing formal verification approaches are restricted to narrow domains (e.g., mathematics), rely on uncertain autoformalization, or apply only as passive post-hoc filtering that cannot prevent error propagation during generation. The paper asks whether formal logic verification can be dynamically interleaved into the reasoning process across diverse domains to provide real-time error detection and rectification.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260407/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a reasoning problem as plain text input and produces an interleaved reasoning chain in which each natural language step is followed by a formal specification (Python code using z3 for logic or sympy for computation) wrapped in <code> tags, and the sandbox execution result wrapped in <interpreter> tags. The model may perform up to 4 iterative verification rounds, revising its reasoning based on solver feedback (SAT/UNSAT, counterexamples, execution outputs) before terminating with a final answer in a <answer> block containing a \boxed{} expression.

## Method

The framework uses a two-stage training pipeline. Stage 1 (FLV-SFT) employs a hierarchical data synthesis pipeline: a teacher model generates CoT chains, decomposes them into discrete logical modules, synthesizes corresponding formal proofs, and validates each via a three-stage execution-based check (exact match → semantic equivalence → proof rewriting), ensuring strict alignment between natural language, formal logic, and execution output. Stage 2 (FLV-RL) applies GRPO with a hierarchical reward function that prioritizes format integrity over structural compliance over logical correctness, penalizing fatal errors (repetition loops, timeouts) and format violations while rewarding valid responses with a composite of structural quality (concise tool usage) and answer correctness. The key architectural distinction from prior neurosymbolic work is that formal verification is interleaved in-process rather than applied post-hoc: the model generates a natural language step, immediately formalizes it, executes the formalization in a sandbox, and uses the returned feedback to correct or continue the chain. This trains the model to treat formal tools as verifiers of logical soundness rather than as computational solvers, shifting the reasoning paradigm from iterative search to symbolic deduction.

## Results

On the 7B scale (Qwen2.5-7B), FLV-RL achieves an average score of 51.9 across six benchmarks, outperforming the strongest natural-language baseline RLPR (47.0) by 4.9 points and the tool-integrated baseline SimpleTIR (49.4) by 2.5 points. On the 14B scale (Qwen2.5-14B), FLV-RL reaches 58.6 average, surpassing the best baseline General-Reasoner (51.3) by 7.3 points, with particularly large gains on AIME 2024 (30.2 vs. 17.5) and TheoremQA (63.5 vs. 55.3). The paper reports overall SOTA improvements of 10.4% (7B) and 14.2% (14B) over prior state-of-the-art methods. Even the SFT-only stage (FLV-SFT) already surpasses all RL baselines on the 7B scale (49.8 vs. 49.4 for SimpleTIR), indicating that formal verification during supervised training provides a stronger reasoning foundation than standard RL on natural-language chains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FLV-RL-7B (AVG 51.9) outperforms all seven baselines on the 7B scale, beating the closest competitor SimpleTIR (49.4) by 2.5 points and RLPR (47.0) by 4.9 points; notably, FLV-SFT alone (49.8) already exceeds every RL-based baseline. On the 14B scale, FLV-RL (58.6) dominates General-Reasoner (51.3) by 7.3 points, with the largest margins on AIME 2024 (+12.7), KOR-Bench (+15.7), and TheoremQA (+8.2). The one benchmark where FLV-RL-14B trails a baseline is GPQA-Diamond (41.4 vs. General-Reasoner's 43.4), which the authors attribute to known reliability issues in the GPQA benchmark itself (Appendix J). Among tool-integrated baselines, FLV-RL substantially outperforms SimpleTIR on logic-heavy tasks (KOR-Bench 51.0 vs. 37.0) and ZeroTIR on GPQA-D (35.4 vs. 28.8), while SimpleTIR retains an edge on AIME24 (41.0 vs. 20.8 for 7B), reflecting its strength in direct numerical computation. On the cross-domain LegalBench-SARA benchmark, FLV-RL (64.7) also surpasses all baselines including RLPR (61.0) and SynLogic (60.7), confirming generalization beyond mathematical and logical domains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the chain-of-thought paradigm (Wei et al., 2022) by interleaving formal symbolic verification (Z3, sympy) directly into the generation loop, departing from prior neurosymbolic approaches like HERMES (domain-specific Lean verification), Safe (post-hoc auditing), and VeriCoT (extracted FOL consistency checks) that treat verification as a separate filtering step. The training pipeline builds on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) for policy optimization and follows the tool-integrated RL framework of ToRL (Li et al., 2025b) for implementation, but introduces a novel two-stage design where SFT data is synthesized with execution-based validation to ensure natural-language/formal-proof alignment before RL refines the policy. The key conceptual departure is from passive post-hoc verification to active in-process feedback that penalizes intermediate logical fallacies during generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that formal verification can serve as a scalable, domain-agnostic mechanism to close the logical consistency gap in LLM reasoning, with 10.4–14.2% average improvements over SOTA across six benchmarks at 7B and 14B scales using only ~17k training samples. The mechanistic finding that the model shifts from brute-force search to symbolic/logic-based reasoning (62.5% of tool calls) provides a concrete explanation for the gains. Key open questions include the computational overhead (~2× RL training time), the fragility of autoformalization for ambiguous or commonsense-heavy problems, and whether the approach scales to larger models or more open-ended reasoning tasks where formal specification is less tractable.
