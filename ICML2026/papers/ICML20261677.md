## Problem

Existing multivariate time series anomaly detection methods treat all channels symmetrically, either modeling them independently, jointly, or in similarity-based clusters, thereby overlooking the fact that a small subset of dominant variables drives system evolution while auxiliary variables provide complementary cues. Additionally, standard point-wise and patch-wise masking strategies are period-agnostic, failing to capture how point anomalies and subsequence anomalies manifest differently across short- and long-period components of multi-periodic series.

## Input / Output

The method takes a multivariate time series matrix X ∈ R^{D×T} and first decouples it into dominant and auxiliary variable subsets via an entropy-based criterion. Dominant variables are reshaped into period-specific 2D matrices (rows = periods, columns = aligned temporal positions) using FFT-extracted periods, while auxiliary variables pass through a lightweight MLP. The output is a per-timestep anomaly score computed as the Frobenius-norm reconstruction error between the original and reconstructed series, with the model trained entirely in an unsupervised, self-supervised reconstruction paradigm.

## Method

TeamWork introduces an asymmetric role-aware channel modeling framework with three coupled components. First, an Entropy Reduction Index (ERI), proven equivalent to mutual information and estimated via the Kraskov k-NN estimator, identifies dominant variables whose knowledge most reduces system uncertainty; a normalized threshold δ* partitions variables into dominant (X_M) and auxiliary (X_A) sets. Second, a period-aware masked modeling module applies FFT to dominant variables to extract top-3 periods, reshapes them into period series matrices, and assigns three specialized maskers—Point Masker (stochastic per-entry masking for short periods), Segment Masker (contiguous interval masking for middle periods), and Period Masker (whole-period masking for long periods)—followed by a multi-convolution encoder with 1×1, 3×3, and 5×5 kernels. Third, a role-aware gated interaction module uses dominant representations to guide extraction of complementary information from auxiliary variables via a sigmoid-gated modulation, then applies separate gating to each role's output before summing. The asymmetric design applies full period-aware masked modeling only to dominant variables while auxiliary variables use a lightweight MLP, reducing redundancy and computational cost.

## Results

TeamWork achieves the best Affiliated-F1 (Aff-F) and AUC-ROC (A-R) on 7 out of 8 datasets, with headline scores of 0.934/0.992 on GECCO, 0.928/0.994 on Genesis, and 0.864/0.592 on SMAP. The largest margin over the strongest baseline (CATCH) is on SMAP, where TeamWork gains +0.286 in Aff-F (0.864 vs. 0.578). On PSM, CATCH edges out TeamWork by 0.002 in Aff-F (0.859 vs. 0.857), though TeamWork still leads in A-R (0.720 vs. 0.652). Across all 8 datasets, TeamWork's average Aff-F is 0.854 and average A-R is 0.825, outperforming the next-best method CATCH (avg Aff-F 0.835, avg A-R 0.726).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TeamWork outperforms all 15 baselines on both Aff-F and A-R across 7 of 8 datasets, with the sole exception being PSM Aff-F where CATCH leads by a marginal 0.002 (0.859 vs. 0.857). The most dramatic wins are on SMAP, where TeamWork's Aff-F of 0.864 surpasses CATCH (0.578) and CrossAD (0.582) by over 0.28, and on Genesis A-R, where TeamWork (0.994) beats CrossAD (0.812) by 0.182. Among the strongest deep-learning baselines, CATCH (channel-clustering strategy) is the closest competitor overall, while CrossAD (channel-independent) and iTransformer (channel-dependent) trail by larger margins on most datasets. Traditional methods (HBOS, PCA, IF, Ocsvm) and simpler deep baselines (AE, DLinear, NLinear) are consistently outperformed by 0.05–0.30 across metrics. The ablation in Table 3 further confirms that the role-aware channel modeling (CR) outperforms CI, CD, and CC strategies, and that removing either dominant or auxiliary variables degrades performance, validating the complementary design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper introduces a fourth channel modeling strategy (CR: Channel Role-aware) that departs from the established CI, CD, and CC paradigms by assigning asymmetric roles to variables based on their entropy-reduction contribution to the system, rather than clustering by pairwise similarity as in CATCH or DUET. The theoretical grounding in the maximum entropy principle and the use of the Kraskov k-NN estimator for mutual information computation repurpose information-theoretic tools in a novel way for variable importance ranking in MTSAD. On the temporal modeling side, the period-aware masked modeling extends fixed point-wise and patch-wise masking strategies (used in DCdetector, PatchTST, CATCH) by tailoring three distinct maskers to short, middle, and long periods extracted via FFT, addressing multi-periodicity that prior methods treat as period-agnostic.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The asymmetric role-aware framework addresses a concrete gap in CC-based methods: intra-cluster channels exhibit homogeneous redundancy while unclustered channels remain underutilized, and the entropy-based decoupling provides a principled, dataset-agnostic criterion for variable importance. The period-aware masking mechanism is practically relevant for real-world systems with multi-periodic patterns (daily/weekly traffic, monthly/annual transactions) where point and subsequence anomalies manifest differently across scales. Key limitations include the static role assignment (dominant/auxiliary roles are fixed rather than evolving over time) and reliance on global FFT for period extraction, which may degrade for weakly periodic or highly non-stationary series. Future work on dynamic role-aware mechanisms and local time-frequency tools (e.g., wavelets) is identified as a natural next step.
