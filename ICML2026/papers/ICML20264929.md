## Problem

Existing autoregressive disease prediction models learn P(Future Disease | Past History) directly from ICD-coded EHR events, lacking any grounding in sensor-derived physiological measurements that capture the actual biological mediation between diseases. Conversely, digital twin approaches that model organ-level biomarkers cannot reason about longitudinal disease progression from medical history. This paper bridges both by introducing a generative framework that conditions multi-organ digital twins on ICD-coded social determinants of health proxies, enabling probabilistic mediation inference through physiological state.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264929/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes ICD-10 level-2 coded medical history sequences (1,944-disease vocabulary) mapped onto a unified integer-age temporal grid where each age slot holds either a disease code or a 'healthy' token, paired with cross-sectional multi-organ biomarkers: 116×116 brain functional connectivity matrices (SPD, AAL atlas) from fMRI and tabular traits (112 for heart, 3 for liver, 3 for kidney) from organ MRI. At inference, given only the ICD history up to age t, the model generates a full multi-organ digital twin (SPD brain connectivity matrix + tabular organ biomarkers) and feeds it to a pretrained foundation model to predict the next ICD disease, effectively decomposing P(Future|History) into P(Future|Biomarker)·P(Biomarker|History).

## Method

DiffDT comprises three coupled components: (1) an adaptive tokenization scheme that discretizes irregular ICD timelines onto a uniform age grid, feeding a Transformer AR encoder pretrained via next-token prediction to produce causality-aware history embeddings; (2) conditional diffusion models that generate multi-organ digital twins given those embeddings — a hybrid SDE/absorbing-process diffusion for tabular organ traits with classifier-free guidance, and a novel Cholesky LDM for brain connectivity; and (3) a finetuned organ-specific foundation model (e.g., BrainMass) that maps the generated DT to the next disease. The central technical contribution is the SPD-VQVAE, which uses Cholesky decomposition (guaranteed unique by Theorem 3.1) to map SPD brain connectivity matrices onto a discrete latent codebook, enabling diffusion in a manifold-aware space without O(N³) eigendecomposition; a dual-branch variant (SPD-VQVAE-Dual) separately encodes low-pass and high-pass Fourier frequency components to preserve personalized network topology. Cross-attention between the AR history embeddings and the diffusion U-Net injects medical context into the generative process, and the entire pipeline is trained end-to-end on UK Biobank with strict subject-level train/validation splits.

## Results

On the UK Biobank next-disease prediction task (1,944 ICD codes), DiffDT achieves a macro-average AUC of 0.9087 (GPT2 backbone) and 0.9171 (Qwen3 backbone), substantially outperforming the Delphi baseline (0.6994 and 0.8931 respectively). In the SDoH-to-event setting, DiffDT-Brain reaches 65.14 F1 on brain diseases, DiffDT-Heart 58.50, DiffDT-Liver 61.65, and DiffDT-Kidney 64.32, consistently leading all organ-specific DT-to-event baselines. For topological DT generation, SPD-VQVAE-Dual achieves the best Pearson correlation (r=0.726) and topology prediction accuracy (mAcc=98.36%) over standard VQVAE (r=0.503, mAcc=90.87%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Delphi (Shmatko et al., 2025), the primary EHR-to-event AR baseline, DiffDT achieves a +0.21 AUC gain with GPT2 (0.9087 vs. 0.6994) and a +0.024 AUC gain with Qwen3 (0.9171 vs. 0.8931), while F1 improves from 7.09 to 18.60 (GPT2) and from 18.17 to 20.92 (Qwen3). The authors attribute Delphi's weakness to its inability to model multi-pathway probabilistic mediation between semantically distant ICD pairs, a gap that DiffDT's biomarker-mediated inference addresses. Against the DT-to-event baselines NeuroPath (Wei et al., 2024) and BrainMass (Yang et al., 2024), DiffDT wins on every organ-specific F1 score: DiffDT-Brain reaches 65.14 vs. 56.53 (NeuroPath) and 47.18 (BrainMass), a ~18-point gain over BrainMass despite using the same pretrained predictive model, indicating that the ICD-conditioned generated DT is more informative than the ground-truth biomarker. DiffDT-Heart (58.50), DiffDT-Liver (61.65), and DiffDT-Kidney (64.32) similarly lead their respective organ categories. The paper further shows that even when both Delphi and DiffDT are upgraded to a Qwen3.5 backbone, DiffDT retains a +0.12 AUC advantage, confirming the mediation module's contribution is independent of AR backbone scaling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of EHR-based autoregressive disease prediction (Delphi, MOTOR) and digital twin / geometric generative modeling. It departs from prior AR-diffusion hybrids (HybridVLA, sign-language production) in two key ways: the data manifold is non-Euclidean (SPD matrices for brain connectivity) and the conditioning signal is a long-horizon, sparsely sampled ICD event stream rather than text or vision tokens. The Cholesky-decomposition-based SPD-VQVAE builds on Lin (2019) and SPD-DDPM (Li et al., 2024) but replaces O(N³) eigendecomposition with a diffeomorphic Cholesky mapping, and the tabular diffusion component adapts TabDiff's classifier-free guidance for sequence-conditioned organ biomarker generation. The overall framework is the first, to the authors' knowledge, to bridge discrete EHR histories with multi-organ continuous biomarker manifolds for longitudinal disease reasoning via probabilistic mediation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a principled way to inject physiological state (multi-organ digital twins) as a probabilistic mediator between past and future ICD events, addressing the acknowledged limitation that pure EHR-to-event models capture healthcare utilization patterns rather than disease etiology. However, the 'SDoH' label is a significant overstatement—the model ingests only ICD-coded Z and V-Y proxies, not actual social, economic, or environmental measurements, and the authors themselves flag this as their most important next step. The UKB healthy-volunteer bias and predominantly cross-sectional imaging further limit generalizability, and the authors concede the mediation gain is modest relative to simply scaling the AR backbone. Open questions include whether the SPD-VQVAE-Dual frequency decomposition generalizes beyond the AAL-116 atlas, whether the generated-DT-beats-GT finding holds on truly longitudinal cohorts, and whether the framework can incorporate genuine multimodal SDoH data.
