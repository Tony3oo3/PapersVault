## Problem

Existing LoRA-based continual learning methods reduce cross-task interference by isolating update subspaces, but they (i) discard task-shared directions that enable knowledge transfer, and (ii) build 'isolated' subspaces from estimated null spaces of past tasks that, under correlated task distributions, remain nearly inactive for the new task—forming a safe zone rather than a truly effective task-specific subspace. The paper asks how to properly set LoRA subspaces to simultaneously preserve transferable directions and learn genuinely task-specific knowledge.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (224×224 for ImageNetR/A/CUB/DomainNet, 32×32 for CIFAR-100) and outputs class labels via a cosine classifier. Internally, it operates on per-layer patch-token feature matrices X ∈ R^{N^p × D} extracted from a frozen ViT-B/16 backbone, maintaining cumulative second-moment statistics S^{1:t-1} ∈ R^{D×D} per layer across the task stream. At inference, all LoRA matrices are merged into the backbone and discarded, yielding zero additional parameters or FLOPs beyond the base ViT.

## Method

LoDA is a dual-branch LoRA framework grounded in a projection-energy analysis (Theorem 3.1) showing that LoRA's learning capacity is gated by the squared norm of input features projected onto the down-projection's row subspace. A task-driven decomposition derives two rank-r bases: a general subspace U_G (top-r SVD of S^{1:t-1}+S^t, maximizing combined old+new projection energy) and an isolated subspace U_I (top-r SVD of a Cholesky-whitened new-task covariance, maximizing the new-to-old energy ratio). Down-projections A_G and A_I are frozen on these bases, while up-projections are trained via Gradient-Aligned Optimization (GAO), which couples gradients across label-disjoint mini-batch subsets to suppress conflicting directions. After each task, a closed-form diagonal rescaling matrix Λ_G rescales each rank-1 unit of the general branch to minimize joint feature-level optimization error across all tasks, and both branches are merged into the backbone as W^t = W^{t-1} + w_G B_G Λ_G A_G + B_I A_I.

## Results

On 10 incremental tasks without feature replay, LoDA achieves 81.93% A_Last and 86.90% A_Avg on ImageNetR, 62.59% and 70.87% on ImageNetA, 90.47% and 93.46% on CIFAR-100, and 81.74% and 89.35% on CUB, outperforming the strongest no-replay baseline CoSO by 0.80%–1.70% in A_Last. With classifier alignment (LoDA+CA), the method reaches 82.55%, 66.71%, 92.15%, and 90.67% A_Last respectively, surpassing the feature-replay SOTA MACIL by up to 5.11% on ImageNetA. Gains are most pronounced on the harder ImageNetA and ImageNetR benchmarks where pre-trained features alone are insufficient for discrimination.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Without feature replay, LoDA outperforms the strongest LoRA-based competitor CoSO by 0.83% A_Last on ImageNetR (81.93 vs. 81.10) and by 1.70% on CIFAR-100 (90.47 vs. 88.77). Against other LoRA methods, LoDA leads SD-LoRA by 4.59% on ImageNetR and 6.63% on ImageNetA, and beats InfLoRA by 7.18% and 13.39% respectively, demonstrating that null-space isolation alone is insufficient under correlated task distributions. Prompt-based baselines (L2P, DualPrompt, CODAPrompt) trail by 8–15% A_Last on ImageNetA, confirming the advantage of LoRA's structured low-rank updates over prompt pools. With classifier alignment, LoDA+CA surpasses the feature-replay SOTA MACIL by 0.67% on ImageNetR, 2.57% on ImageNetA, 0.21% on CIFAR-100, and 0.15% on CUB, with the largest margin on the most challenging ImageNetA benchmark. The consistent advantage across all four datasets and both evaluation protocols (with/without replay) indicates that the task-driven subspace decomposition and closed-form recalibration provide a robust stability-plasticity balance independent of auxiliary replay mechanisms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LoRA-based continual learning line initiated by InfLoRA (Liang & Li, 2024) and BiLoRA (Zhu et al., 2025), which constrain LoRA updates to estimated null spaces or fixed orthogonal bases to reduce cross-task interference. It departs from these methods on two conceptual axes: (1) it explicitly preserves a general subspace for cross-task knowledge sharing rather than discarding all non-null directions, and (2) it replaces null-space estimation with a relative projection-energy criterion that identifies truly task-specific directions even under correlated task distributions. The closed-form recalibration for the general branch also distinguishes it from gradient-based merging strategies like CoMA and Connector, which rely on local linearity approximations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, energy-based framework that unifies knowledge sharing and isolation in LoRA-based CL, addressing a gap where prior methods handled these objectives in opposition. The empirical demonstration that null-space directions remain nearly active for new tasks under realistic correlated streams (r^t ≈ 1.0) challenges a widely-used assumption in the field. Limitations include the 27 MB cumulative-statistics storage that grows with model width (though not task count), evaluation restricted to ViT-B/16, and the GAO training overhead. Open questions include whether the energy-based decomposition generalizes to non-ViT backbones, very long task streams where statistics become ill-conditioned, and whether the dual-branch design extends naturally to other PEFT paradigms like adapters or prompts.
