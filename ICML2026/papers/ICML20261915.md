## Problem

Existing methods for multivariate hidden Markov processes face a fundamental trade-off: classical HMMs and their extensions provide interpretable discrete states but rely on parametric emissions and suffer combinatorial state-space explosion, while DL-HMM hybrids improve expressiveness yet remain likelihood-driven, are designed primarily for univariate settings, and use soft decoding that blurs state boundaries. No distribution-free framework exists that jointly achieves accurate next-step forecasting and reliable hard-decoded hidden-state estimation for complex multivariate HMPs with nonlinear, time-varying, and cross-variable dynamics.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a multivariate time series X = {x_1, …, x_T} ∈ R^{T×N} where each variable i has m candidate hidden states, along with historical observations over a lookback window T_0 and optional exogenous covariates. The DRL agent's observable information at each step includes the historical context, past state probabilities over T_1 steps, and recent state-conditioned prediction errors. Output is a next-step observation vector x̂_{t+1} ∈ R^N produced by the selected state-specific emission head, plus a discrete hidden-state vector ŝ_{t+1} = (ŝ_{1,t+1}, …, ŝ_{N,t+1}) obtained via hard argmax decoding rather than posterior-weighted averaging, yielding interpretable, state-consistent regime labels.

## Method

DRL-STAF couples per-variable deep emission networks (DENs), each with m output heads corresponding to candidate hidden states, with a PPO-based DRL agent that directly selects discrete states as actions. The reward function combines an immediate prediction-gain signal (advantage over a stateless baseline, with a switching penalty to reduce variance) and an episodic reward enforcing state separation and balanced per-head training, making the entire framework distribution-free with no parametric emission or transition assumptions. A two-stage training scheme mitigates combinatorial explosion: Stage 1 learns per-variable state policies independently with a sample-screening strategy and soft parameter updates to stabilize joint prediction–state training, while Stage 2 freezes the prediction module and trains a cross-variable coordination policy using a residual graph attention (ResGAT) layer that propagates information over a variable interaction graph. Hard decoding is achieved natively through the DRL argmax action selection, avoiding differentiable relaxations like Gumbel-Softmax and preserving interpretable discrete state boundaries.

## Results

On the 3-variable simulated dataset with infrequent transitions, DRL-STAF achieves 98.17% state-estimation accuracy, 98.22% F1, 0.0889 MAE, and 0.0278 MSE, substantially outperforming the best baseline NCTRL (79.53% accuracy, 81.34% F1, 0.3936 MAE, 0.3042 MSE). On the 10-variable setting, DRL-STAF maintains 96.15% accuracy and 93.26% F1 with 0.1090 MAE and 0.0395 MSE, again leading all baselines. On real-world datasets, DRL-STAF achieves the lowest MAE (1.6438) and MSE (13.2381) on Exchange, the lowest MAE (1.5193) on Traffic, and the best F1 (77.50%) and MAE (0.0189) on SMachine.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DRL-STAF outperforms all eight HMM and DL-HMM baselines on every metric in both the 3-variable and 10-variable simulated settings. The strongest baseline, NCTRL (Song et al., 2023), achieves 79.53% accuracy and 0.3936 MAE on 3 variables, while DRL-STAF reaches 98.17% accuracy and 0.0889 MAE — a gap of roughly 19 percentage points in accuracy and 4.4× lower MAE. Classical HMM variants (Parallel HMM, HSMM, HOHMM, CHMM) cluster around 60–77% accuracy with MAE values of 0.55–0.71, far behind DRL-STAF. Among DL-HMM hybrids, NHMM and Markovian-RNN achieve lower MAE than the classical HMMs (0.1277 and 0.2444 respectively on 3-var) but still trail DRL-STAF by 30–60% in MAE and by 30+ points in state-estimation accuracy. On real-world data, DRL-STAF achieves the best MAE on all three datasets (SMachine: 0.0189, Exchange: 1.6438, Traffic: 1.5193) and the best F1 on SMachine (77.50%), while NCTRL is the closest competitor on Exchange (MAE 1.8486) and Traffic (MAE 2.5353). In the supplementary comparison with standalone deep forecasters (Table 18), DRL-STAF dominates on the simulated dataset (MAE 0.0889 vs. 0.26 for Mamba/TimesNet/PatchTST) but Mamba achieves a slightly lower MSE on Traffic (5.6871 vs. 6.4610), though DRL-STAF still wins on MAE (1.5193 vs. 1.5204).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant likelihood-driven paradigm in DL-HMM hybrids (NHMM, NCTRL, DEN-HMM, Markovian-RNN) by replacing forward-backward/EM inference with a PPO-based DRL agent that directly selects discrete hidden states via hard decoding. It also extends classical multivariate HMMs (CHMM, coupled HOHMMs) by eliminating the need to enumerate the joint state space, instead decomposing joint inference into per-variable DRL policies coordinated through a residual graph attention (ResGAT) layer in a second training stage. The two-stage scheme and the reward design (prediction gain + state separation + pairwise discrepancy) are novel architectural choices that have no direct precedent in the HMM or DRL literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical path to interpretable, discrete-state forecasting in multivariate settings where CHMMs become intractable, and the hard-decoding output is directly usable for downstream regime-based decision-making. However, the paper acknowledges that training time grows substantially with the number of variables, limiting applicability in time-sensitive or resource-constrained large-scale settings, and the current formulation is restricted to single-step forecasting. Open questions include whether the DRL-based state estimation generalizes to continuous or partially observed state spaces, whether multi-step rollouts remain stable, and whether the large simulated-data gaps persist on more heterogeneous real-world processes.
