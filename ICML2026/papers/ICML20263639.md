## Problem

Existing algorithms for constrained MDPs face a fundamental tension: LP-based methods achieve optimal strong regret and constraint violation but require solving occupancy-measure linear programs each episode, while primal-dual methods are computationally efficient but typically yield only weak (cancellation-permitting) guarantees. No prior model-free algorithm with function approximation achieves near-optimal strong regret and strong constraint violation, and no algorithm attains sublinear strong-metric bounds without Slater's condition.

## Input / Output

The algorithm operates on a finite-horizon episodic CMDP with a potentially infinite state space, a finite action set, a known feature map φ: S×A→R^d, and a constraint threshold b on cumulative utility. At each episode it consumes the observed trajectory (states, actions, rewards, utilities) to update least-squares value estimates, and produces a new policy as a per-state, per-step action distribution. Unusually, the penalty parameter η_k is computed directly from the optimistic utility value estimate at the initial state (η_k = λ(b − V̂_{g,1}^k(x_1^k))_+), rather than from a dual variable or Lagrange multiplier, making the constraint enforcement fully primal and episode-local.

## Method

APMPO integrates three components: (1) optimistic value-function estimation under linear function approximation, where regularized least-squares regression yields reward and utility Q-estimates augmented with upper-confidence bonuses; (2) a violation-aware adaptive penalty η_k = λ(b − V̂_{g,1}^k(x_1^k))_+ that activates only when the predicted constraint value falls below the threshold, scaling proportionally to the predicted shortfall; and (3) KL-regularized mirror descent policy optimization with an adaptive learning rate α_k = √(Σ_{s≤k}(1+η_s²)) that prevents overly aggressive updates under severe violations. The key analytical insight is a self-bound property: the violation-dependent terms (η_k) appear on both sides of the no-regret inequality, so they cancel to yield sublinear bounds on strong metrics without requiring dual variable tuning or separate Slater-constant estimation. This design fundamentally differs from primal-dual methods by embedding constraint information directly into the primal surrogate objective rather than through iterative dual updates, avoiding oscillations and enabling sharp per-episode control of positive errors.

## Results

APMPO achieves near-optimal Õ(√K) strong regret and Õ(√K) strong constraint violation under Slater's condition when the feasibility gap ρ is known, matching the best-known rates of Stradi et al. (2025a) in the tabular setting. Without prior knowledge of ρ, APMPO still obtains Õ(√K/ρ²) strong regret and Õ(√K/ρ) strong violation. Most notably, APMPO is the first algorithm to achieve sublinear Õ(K^{3/4}) strong regret and Õ(K^{3/4}) strong constraint violation without any Slater's condition assumption, via a model-free design with linear function approximation. Numerical experiments in synthetic tabular and linear environments confirm sublinear strong regret and violation curves, with APMPO achieving a better regret–violation trade-off than baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Stradi et al. (2025a), APMPO matches the optimal Õ(√K) strong regret and violation rate when the Slater constant ρ is known, but extends the result to a model-free setting with linear function approximation (vs. model-based tabular) and additionally provides the first Slater-free sublinear guarantee of Õ(K^{3/4}). Against Müller et al. (2024), APMPO improves the strong regret/violation exponent from K^{0.92} to √K (with Slater) or K^{3/4} (without Slater), while also being model-free. Against Ghosh et al. (2024), APMPO achieves strong regret (not merely weak regret) and avoids the exponential-time dual-variable search per episode, delivering a computationally efficient mirror-descent-based policy update. In the numerical experiments (Figure 1), APMPO outperforms both Ghosh et al. (2024) and Stradi et al. (2025a) in synthetic tabular and linear environments, showing lower cumulative strong regret and strong constraint violation across all tested settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the strong regret and strong constraint violation framework introduced by Efroni et al. (2020) from LP-based and model-based tabular settings to model-free CMDPs with linear function approximation. It departs from the standard primal-dual paradigm (Ghosh et al., 2024; Müller et al., 2024; Stradi et al., 2025a) by replacing iterative dual updates with a single-stage, violation-aware adaptive penalty computed directly from optimistic value estimates, combined with KL-regularized mirror descent for policy optimization. The optimistic linear value estimation follows the Jin et al. (2020) paradigm, while the self-bound analytical structure—where violation-dependent terms appear on both sides of the key inequality—draws on techniques from online convex optimization with long-term constraints (Yuan & Lamperski, 2018; Yi et al., 2021).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

APMPO provides the first sublinear strong regret and strong constraint violation guarantees (K^{3/4}) without Slater's condition, and matches the optimal √K rate under Slater's condition without requiring prior knowledge of the feasibility gap ρ—both of which were previously available only in model-based tabular settings. This makes strong-metric guarantees accessible to large-scale and continuous-state problems where tabular or LP-based methods are infeasible. Key open questions remain: the polynomial dependence on H and d is acknowledged as non-tight, and whether the near-optimal √K rate is achievable without any feasibility gap assumption is left unresolved. The practical impact is currently limited by synthetic-only experiments and the theoretical (non-deep) function approximation setting.
