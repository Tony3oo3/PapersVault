## Problem

Existing confidence calibration methods (e.g., Temperature Scaling, verbalized confidence) are designed for static single-turn outputs and cannot address the unique challenges of agentic systems: uncertainty compounds and propagates across multi-step trajectories, external tools introduce heterogeneous noise, and failure modes are opaque because a final incorrect answer may stem from an intermediate breakdown. No systematic framework exists for calibrating an agent's confidence by diagnosing its entire execution process rather than only its final output.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the full sequence of token-level log-probabilities generated across all N steps of an agent trajectory (not just the final step), which are first reduced to a fixed 48-dimensional feature vector via statistical operators (mean, variance, entropy, skewness, finite differences) applied along both within-step and across-step axes. The output is a single calibrated confidence score in [0,1] produced by a sigmoid of a linear function over the feature vector. The framework is agnostic to the specific LLM or agent architecture, requiring only access to per-token log-probabilities during generation.

## Method

HTC extracts 48 process-diagnostic features organized into four complementary families—Cross-Step Dynamics (confidence evolution across steps), Intra-Step Stability (within-step token volatility and distributional shape), Positional Indicators (first/last step signals), and Structure Attributes (step count, token-length patterns)—then maps them through a lightweight logistic regression calibrator. Two regularization regimes are used: HTC-Full (L2/ridge, retaining all features) and HTC-Reduced (L1/lasso, selecting a sparse subset of ~15–25 features for denoising in small-data regimes). The key design insight is that a linear model over hand-crafted trajectory features is strictly more informative than last-step confidence (by a Bayes-risk argument) while remaining sample-efficient and interpretable, avoiding the overfitting of neural encoders on the inherently small trajectory datasets. A General Agent Calibrator (GAC) is additionally pretrained on a pooled corpus of seven diverse benchmarks to achieve zero-shot calibration on unseen tasks like GAIA, where it attains the best ECE (0.118) without any task-specific retraining.

## Results

On the three representative datasets in Table 1, HTC-Reduced achieves the best ECE across all: 0.068 on SimpleQA, 0.102 on GPQA, and 0.031 on HLE (where base LLM accuracy is only 10%), with corresponding Brier Scores of 0.140, 0.213, and 0.090. On the out-of-domain GAIA benchmark (Table 3), the pretrained General Agent Calibrator (GAC-Reduced) achieves the lowest ECE of 0.118, surpassing direct training (0.142) and all domain-transfer baselines. Across all eight benchmarks, HTC consistently outperforms inference-based baselines by 20–90% in ECE and 5–15 points in AUROC, with the largest gains on the hardest tasks (HLE, GPQA).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HTC-Reduced dominates all inference-based baselines across every dataset and metric. Against the strongest inference baseline (GlobalTrace-TP+Temp), HTC-Reduced reduces ECE from 0.433 to 0.031 on HLE (a 14× improvement), from 0.136 to 0.102 on GPQA, and from 0.071 to 0.068 on SimpleQA, while also improving AUROC by 7–15 points (e.g., 0.570→0.644 on HLE, 0.643→0.706 on GPQA). Verbalized Confidence (Tian et al., 2023) is the weakest baseline, with ECE as high as 0.656 on HLE, confirming that self-reported confidence is unreliable for agentic trajectories. Among learning-based baselines (LSTM, Transformer, NN, XGBoost, Gaussian Process), HTC consistently achieves lower mean error and dramatically smaller variance across dataset sizes of 100–400 samples (Figure 7), where neural encoders overfit or fluctuate heavily. On the zero-shot GAIA generalization test, GAC-Reduced (pretrained on 7 domains) achieves the best ECE (0.118) but trails DIRECTTRAIN(reduced) on Brier Score (0.245 vs. 0.233) and AUROC (0.647 vs. 0.686), indicating that pretraining optimizes calibration at a modest cost to discrimination.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends confidence calibration from static, single-turn LLM outputs to multi-step agentic trajectories, drawing on prior work in token-level confidence estimation (verbalized confidence, top-k token probabilities), uncertainty propagation in agents (UProp, SAUP), and fine-grained reasoning calibration (Deep Think with Confidence). It departs from these by treating the entire execution trajectory as the unit of analysis rather than a single output, and by replacing neural sequence encoders with a compact set of 48 hand-crafted statistical features fed into a regularized linear model. The positioning is explicitly problem-formulating: the authors argue that compounding uncertainty, multi-source noise from tools, and opaque intermediate failures make agentic calibration a distinct challenge that existing single-point methods cannot address.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, interpretable, and computationally cheap reliability layer for deployed agents, which is critical as LLM agents enter safety-sensitive domains. The General Agent Calibrator's ability to achieve the best ECE on out-of-domain GAIA without task-specific retraining is a promising step toward a universal reliability component. Key limitations include the requirement for token-level log-probability access (excluding many closed-source APIs), the hand-crafted feature set's potential ceiling on complex interactions, and evaluation on relatively small datasets (≤500 samples per benchmark). Open questions include whether the approach extends to agents without log-prob access, whether online/incremental calibration is feasible, and whether the 48-dimensional feature space scales to longer-horizon or multi-agent settings.
