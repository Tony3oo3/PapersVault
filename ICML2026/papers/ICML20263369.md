## Problem

Preference optimization methods like DPO are known to exploit spurious correlations (length, politeness, formatting) in training data, but existing work documents symptoms without identifying underlying mechanisms or providing principled guarantees. This paper fills that gap by providing a unified population-level theory for how spurious features are learned, why the resulting deployment error is irreducible with respect to data scale, and how to mitigate it.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pairwise preference data (prompt, preferred response, rejected response) encoded as feature vectors decomposed into causal and spurious components, along with synthetic tie pairs where causal features match but spurious features differ and labels are assigned uniformly at random. These are mixed at ratio α into the standard DPO logistic loss over feature differences. The output is a log-linear policy parameter vector θ = [θ_c; θ_s], where the theory characterizes when the spurious component θ_s is nonzero and how tie training shrinks it.

## Method

The paper analyzes log-linear DPO under a local linearization regime, deriving a closed-form population equilibrium via Schur complement decomposition that reveals two distinct spurious-learning channels: mean spurious bias (μ_s ≠ 0) and causal-spurious correlation leakage (Σ_sc ≠ 0). For deployment, it decomposes suboptimality into an irreducible shift term (driven by learned spurious parameters interacting with shifted spurious statistics) and a reducible estimation term that vanishes as n → ∞, proving that scaling data cannot eliminate shift-induced error. The proposed mitigation, tie training, augments the dataset with equal-utility preference pairs that have zero causal difference but nonzero spurious difference and random labels; this injects curvature exclusively along spurious directions in the mixed covariance, selectively shrinking spurious parameters while preserving causal learning, with provable reduction in both population-level spurious weight and finite-sample deployment shift.

## Results

On the synthetic hotel-preference LLM benchmark, informative tie training (Tie-I) maintains 92.40% in-distribution accuracy (vs. 92.25% for strict DPO) while substantially improving robustness under spurious shift: 82.85% under suppressed correlation (vs. 74.00%) and 86.70% under adversarial correlation (vs. 64.20%). In the neural network experiments, strict DPO yields a spurious gap of ≈0.7 and adversarial accuracy of ≈0.25, whereas tie training (α=0.75) reduces the spurious gap to ≈0.45 and raises adversarial accuracy to ≈0.7 without sacrificing in-distribution performance. Linear model experiments confirm quantitative agreement with the theoretical closed-form predictions for spurious parameter norms and the irreducible shift term in deployment suboptimality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between standard (strict) DPO and the proposed tie training (Tie-I) on a controlled LLM hotel-preference benchmark. Tie-I matches strict DPO on in-distribution accuracy (92.40% vs. 92.25%) while gaining +8.85 points under suppressed spurious correlation (82.85% vs. 74.00%) and +22.5 points under adversarial spurious correlation (86.70% vs. 64.20%). In the neural network setting, tie training (α=0.75) reduces the spurious gap from ≈0.7 to ≈0.45 and breaks the adversarial accuracy plateau (≈0.25 → ≈0.7) that persists under strict training even as sample size grows. The paper does not compare against other external mitigation methods (e.g., length penalties, RRM); the comparison is strictly between the unmodified DPO objective and the same objective augmented with tie pairs, isolating the effect of the data-level intervention.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the well-developed theory of spurious correlation learning in supervised classification (shortcut learning, simplicity bias) to the pairwise preference optimization setting, where the DPO objective induces fundamentally different learning dynamics than pointwise ERM. It builds directly on DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) as the optimization target and draws on the closest theoretical precedent—Bombari & Mondelli (2025) on spurious correlations in high-dimensional linear regression—but departs from that line by analyzing the pairwise logistic structure of preference data rather than pointwise losses. The paper also connects to empirical reports of verbosity bias, sycophancy, and formatting bias in RLHF/DPO systems, providing the first population-level mechanistic explanation for why these failures arise structurally from the data rather than from optimization artifacts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central practical implication is that scaling preference data from the same distribution cannot eliminate spurious-correlation-induced deployment failures—the shift term is provably irreducible—which challenges a common assumption in alignment pipelines that more annotation data improves robustness. Tie training offers a simple, objective-agnostic mitigation (adding equal-utility pairs with random labels) that requires no architectural changes, making it potentially easy to adopt in existing DPO/RLHF pipelines. Key open questions include automatic tie construction at scale, extending the theory beyond the local log-linear regime, and validating on natural (non-synthetic) preference data where causal and spurious factors are entangled.
