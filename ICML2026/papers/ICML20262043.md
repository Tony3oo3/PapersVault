## Problem

Probing studies show that intermediate LLM layers encode rich, distinct linguistic and semantic features, yet residual-dynamics analyses reveal that deeper layers operate in a near-identity regime with progressively smaller updates. This creates an apparent conflict: if mid-depth representations stagnate geometrically, how do they still contribute to increasingly abstract predictions? The paper asks whether this mid-depth spectral plateau reflects a genuine deficit in representational capacity or merely a structural bottleneck that limits how well those features are routed to the terminal readout.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard text prompts fed to pre-trained LLMs (Llama-3-8B, Qwen2-7B, Mistral-7B, Phi-3.5-Mini) and produces text responses in the same format as the base model. The DDI module consumes intermediate hidden states (H^(l) ∈ R^{T×d}) from selected mid-depth layers during training, aggregates them into a single diagnostic vector R_t ∈ R^d, and adds it to the final-layer representation with a small scalar γ ≪ 1. Critically, DDI is a training-time-only intervention: after internalization via self-distillation, the auxiliary route is removed entirely, so inference uses the standard backbone with zero additional parameters or compute.

## Method

The paper introduces a Depth-Aware Diagnostic Intervention (DDI) that provides an explicit training-time gradient pathway from mid-depth hidden states to the terminal readout, testing whether improved access (rather than new capacity) explains mid-depth underutilization. DDI employs a two-stage hierarchical aggregation: local linear projections compress non-overlapping blocks of adjacent layers into summaries, which a 1D convolutional global aggregator then integrates into the diagnostic vector R_t. Training uses three auxiliary objectives alongside the base task loss: an orthogonal loss forcing R_t to encode information complementary to the final layer, a correlation-alignment loss ensuring semantic coherence, and a cosine-similarity self-distillation loss that drives the backbone to internalize the DDI-augmented representation so the auxiliary route can be discarded. The theoretical contribution is a first-order perturbative analysis of pre-norm residual dynamics showing that small mid-depth updates preserve the second-moment spectrum (stable effective rank, LogDet) while inducing only gradual subspace rotations, decoupling capacity from readout-side accessibility. Accessibility is quantified via an unembedding-subspace access ratio measuring the fraction of pooled representation energy projecting onto the top-64 eigenspace of the frozen unembedding head, which increases selectively near readout-facing layers after DDI training while capacity-oriented metrics (CKA, effective rank, SVCCA) remain largely unchanged.

## Results

Across four LLMs (Llama-3-8B, Qwen2-7B, Mistral-7B, Phi-3.5-Mini), the DDI-enhanced model (LoRA+Ours) achieves average gains of +0.4 to +0.8 points over the LoRA baseline on commonsense reasoning (e.g., Llama3: 84.7→85.2, Mistral: 82.1→82.9) and +0.4 to +0.9 points on arithmetic reasoning (e.g., Llama3: 75.8→76.6, Mistral: 66.9→67.8). Gains are most pronounced on harder multi-step benchmarks such as ARC-Challenge (+0.6 to +1.2), AQuA (+0.5 to +2.0), and GSM8K (+0.7 to +1.1), while pattern-driven tasks like AddSub and MultiArith show minimal change (±0.0 to +0.3). Capacity-oriented metrics (effective rank, log-det) remain nearly unchanged in the mid-depth plateau, confirming that DDI reorganizes readout-side coupling rather than expanding representational capacity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is between standard LoRA fine-tuning (baseline) and LoRA+DDI (the proposed method), evaluated after DDI removal so that inference architecture is identical. DDI consistently outperforms the LoRA baseline across all four model families on both commonsense and arithmetic reasoning, with average gains of +0.4 to +0.9 points. The largest per-dataset improvements appear on ARC-Challenge (up to +1.2 for Qwen2), AQuA (up to +2.0 for Llama3), and BoolQ (up to +1.9 for Llama3), suggesting that tasks requiring multi-step or abstraction-heavy reasoning benefit most from improved mid-depth access. On simpler pattern-driven benchmarks (AddSub, MultiArith), gains are negligible (±0.0 to +0.3), indicating the residual pathway already suffices for those tasks. A parameter-matched control experiment (Table 6) confirms that the gains are not attributable to extra parameters or a generic auxiliary route: depth-unaware mean-pooling and simple weighted-sum controls perform at baseline level (75.8%), while the hierarchical DDI structure achieves 76.6%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate lines of LLM interpretability research: probing-based analyses that document hierarchical linguistic/semantic specialization across depth (Jawahar et al., 2019; Fan et al., 2024; Ben-Artzy & Schwartz, 2024), and residual-dynamics work showing near-identity behavior in deeper layers (Li & Papyan, 2023; Arefin et al., 2024). It departs from both by explicitly decomposing the mid-depth plateau into 'representational capacity' (second-moment spectral diversity) versus 'accessibility' (coupling to the unembedding-induced readout subspace), a distinction neither line of work had formalized. The Depth-Aware Diagnostic Intervention (DDI) is positioned as a training-time diagnostic tool rather than a new architecture, drawing on self-distillation and LoRA-based fine-tuning, and is contrasted with Tuned Lens (Belrose et al., 2023) and JUMP (Yom Din et al., 2024), which identify intermediate-layer signals but do not test whether geometric routing constraints explain their limited influence on final predictions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a mechanistic reinterpretation of a widely observed phenomenon (the mid-depth spectral plateau), arguing it reflects constrained access rather than representational stagnation, which could redirect future interpretability efforts toward routing and readout-coupling analyses rather than purely layer-wise probing. However, the practical gains from DDI are modest (+0.4 to +0.9 points on reasoning benchmarks), the scope is limited to 7–8B PreLN models under LoRA fine-tuning, and the theoretical analysis is a first-order perturbation rather than a rigorous bound. Open questions include whether the capacity/access decoupling persists at larger scale, under different normalization schemes (PostLN, MixLN), or with head-level routing interventions, and whether the DDI diagnostic generalizes beyond the specific aggregation architecture tested.
