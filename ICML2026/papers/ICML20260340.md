## Problem

Training latent flow-matching (LFM) generative models requires large datasets, substantial compute, and long training times, yet it was unclear whether dataset size or model capacity could be reduced without quality loss. This paper discovers that LFM models exhibit striking trajectory stability—models trained on disjoint data subsets or with different architectures produce highly similar generations from the same noise seed—and asks how to exploit this property for more efficient training and inference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260340/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes Gaussian noise in a 4×32×32 VQ-VAE latent space (optionally conditioned on a class label for ImageNet) and integrates the FM ODE to produce a latent sample, which is decoded to a 256×256 (or 512×512) RGB image. A key operational detail is that the VQ-VAE is trained per-dataset to prevent cross-distribution leakage, and the stability property means that identical noise seeds yield visually similar outputs across independently trained model variants, enabling the coarse-to-fine stitching at an intermediate timestep t₀.

## Method

The paper introduces three data-pruning criteria adapted to FM dynamics—gradient-norm scoring, loss scoring, and balanced k-means clustering in CLIP embedding space—showing that balanced clustering (C_b) preserves or improves FID even when pruning up to 75% of ImageNet data. For inference acceleration, a coarse-to-fine (C2F) strategy splits the FM trajectory: a lightweight DiT-S/2 model (33M params) evolves t∈[0,t₀) while a pretrained DiT-XL/2 model (675M params) refines t∈[t₀,1], with a seam loss computed via backward ODE integration of the fine model to ensure smooth velocity-field continuity at t₀. The theoretical grounding comes from the closed-form rectified-flow solution, where softmax-weighted sample dominance explains why removing non-dominant samples leaves transport trajectories largely unchanged, and why stability breaks only when entire distributional modes or the latent coordinate system are altered.

## Results

On CelebA-HQ at pr=0.5, balanced clustering C_b achieves FID 22.8, improving over the unpruned model (FID 24.24), while the kernel-based variant C_b^κ reaches FID 23.42. On ImageNet, C_b at pr=0.75 converges faster than unpruned and maintains or slightly improves FID at a fixed 200k-iteration budget, with up to 75% of data removable without quality loss. The coarse-to-fine (C2F) inference approach achieves ~2.15× speedup (43.53 vs. 93.95 ms/image on H100) at t_0=0.7 while preserving generation quality. Stability is quantified via ArcFace cosine similarity of 0.79–0.83 between pruned and unpruned models (vs. 0.37 for random pairs), confirming that independently trained models on disjoint subsets produce highly similar outputs under identical noise seeds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external generative baselines; instead, it benchmarks different pruning strategies against the unpruned (full-dataset) model and against each other. On CelebA-HQ at pr=0.5, balanced clustering C_b (FID 22.8) and its kernel variant C_b^κ (FID 23.42) both outperform the unpruned model (FID 24.24), while coreset-based selection C_b^cs (FID 26.94) and global coreset (FID 26.25) degrade performance, indicating that selecting samples near cluster centers is more effective than maximizing geometric diversity. Gradient-based (G) and highest-loss (L) pruning substantially worsen FID, contrasting with discriminative-model findings where hard examples are valuable; the paper interprets high-loss samples in FM as reflecting erroneous velocity fields rather than useful hard examples. On ImageNet at a fixed 200k-iteration budget, every pruning method including random sampling improves over unpruned, with C_b at pr=0.75 showing the most consistent gain. For the C2F inference approach, the key comparison is against the fine model alone: C2F at t_0=0.7 matches fine-only quality while running 2.15× faster, and the approach critically depends on stability—when stability is broken (C2F_male experiment), FID degrades to 44.92, confirming that trajectory alignment is necessary for the stitching to work.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the latent diffusion paradigm (Rombach et al., 2021) to the rectified flow matching framework (Lipman et al., 2022; Liu et al., 2022), and grounds its stability analysis in the closed-form FM solution (Gao & Li, 2024; Bertrand et al., 2025), which reveals that transport trajectories are dominated by a small number of training samples via softmax weights. It adapts data-pruning heuristics originally developed for discriminative models (Paul et al., 2021; Coleman et al., 2019; Mirzasoleiman et al., 2020; Abbas et al., 2024) to the FM objective by reformulating scoring along shared noise paths and timesteps. Prior observations of trajectory consistency in score-based diffusion (Kadkhodaie et al., 2024; Ghosal et al., 2022) were limited to low-resolution pixel-space models; this paper establishes analogous stability in LFM across a broader range of perturbations and, crucially, translates it into practical algorithms for efficient training and inference rather than leaving it as a theoretical curiosity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: up to 75% of ImageNet can be pruned without quality degradation, and the coarse-to-fine inference strategy achieves ~2.15× speedup while maintaining FID, offering a concrete recipe for reducing both training and inference costs of LFM models. The balanced-clustering pruning also provides a label-agnostic mechanism to mitigate distributional biases in generated data, relevant for fairness-sensitive applications. Key limitations include the sensitivity of stability to latent-space reparameterization (changing the VAE or applying invertible transforms breaks it), the need for a manually tuned seam point t₀ in the coarse-to-fine approach, and the requirement of two models at inference. Open questions include whether an adaptive criterion for t₀ exists, whether stability persists at video-scale or higher-resolution settings, and whether the phenomenon extends beyond rectified flow to other FM variants.
