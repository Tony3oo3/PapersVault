## Problem

Existing audio representation benchmarks (HEAR, SUPERB) evaluate models only after training linear probes or fine-tuning parameters, conflating intrinsic representation quality with optimization effectiveness. No training-free benchmark exists to diagnose whether frozen audio embeddings intrinsically organize sounds by content identity without any parameter updates or label supervision, leaving the readiness of these representations for immediate retrieval deployment unmeasured.

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes 125,382 single-source audio clips (16 kHz mono, 50 ms–5 s duration) from 19 corpora spanning human speech, animal vocalizations, and environmental sounds, explicitly excluding polyphonic mixtures. Each clip is encoded by a frozen foundation model into a variable-length sequence of hidden states, which is reduced to a fixed-length vector via concatenated mean-over-time and mean-over-frequency pooling, then projected to 100 dimensions by per-subset label-free PCA. Evaluation produces two training-free scores per subset: Precision@k (local neighborhood purity) and Global Separation Rate (point-wise class separation calibrated by lift over a permutation baseline).

## Method

VOCSIM is a training-free, label-free, transductive benchmark that probes the intrinsic geometric alignment of frozen audio embeddings. The standardized pipeline treats each foundation model (Whisper Large-v3, WavLM, CLAP, BEATs, EAT, AudioMAE) as a frozen feature extractor, applies explicit statistical time-frequency pooling (concatenating mean-over-time and mean-over-frequency vectors), and corrects the well-documented anisotropy of foundation-model embeddings via label-free PCA fitted independently on each evaluation subset's statistics. Two complementary metrics are used: Precision@k measures local retrieval purity (simulating query-by-example), while the novel Global Separation Rate (GSR) compares each point's nearest inter-class distance (NID) against its average intra-class spread (Avg_ID), deliberately asymmetric to penalize boundary breaches that average-based metrics like Silhouette would mask. Permutation-based calibration quantifies lift over chance, revealing that Whisper + time-frequency pooling + PCA achieves the strongest zero-shot geometry (66.8% P@1, 41.7% GSR on public sets) but collapses to near-chance on blind low-resource speech (Shipibo-Conibo, Chintang), exposing a cross-lingual generalization gap.

## Results

On public (transfer) subsets, the best configuration—frozen Whisper Large-v3 encoder with time-frequency pooling and label-free PCA (D100), evaluated with Spearman distance—achieves 66.8% P@1, 57.4% P@5, and 41.7% GSR (macro-averaged across 15 public subsets). On the strictly out-of-distribution blind low-resource speech sets (Shipibo-Conibo, Chintang), P@1 collapses to 11.5% for Whisper (from ~67% on public speech), while GSR remains at 39.4% but with a reduced permutation-calibrated lift of only +5.8 over chance (vs. +16.9 on public sets). GSR rankings are notably stable across domains (mean Kendall's τ = 0.60 across four domain groupings), whereas P@1 rankings shift with pre-training alignment. As external validation, the same Whisper D100 pipeline achieves state-of-the-art on 5 of 7 HEAR benchmark tasks (e.g., 98.6% on Speech Commands, 79.3% on CREMA-D emotion) and 80.9% accuracy in predicting zebra finch perceptual similarity judgments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Whisper D100 (Spearman) leads on overall public P@1 (66.8%) and GSR (41.7%), outperforming CLAP (63.7% / 38.1%), WavLM (64.1% / 37.0%), BEATs (64.3% / 31.4%), and EAT (50.2% / 24.7%). However, domain-disaggregated results reveal a more nuanced picture: CLAP surpasses Whisper on animal vocalizations (88.4% vs. 85.7% P@1) and environmental sounds (95.7% vs. 75.9%), while WavLM leads on public English speech (51.8% vs. 46.5% P@1), reflecting each model's pre-training alignment. On the blind OOD speech sets, Whisper and BEATs are nearly tied on P@1 (11.5% vs. 11.4%), both far above WavLM (4.6%), CLAP (8.1%), and EAT (1.9%), but all remain only marginally above the permutation baseline. The per-subset VAE/AE baselines, despite being domain-adapted by construction, score below chance on blind sets (lift −7.8), confirming that large-scale pre-training is necessary for cross-lingual generalization. On the external HEAR benchmark, the Whisper D100 pipeline achieves SOTA on 5/7 tasks and outperforms CLAP on all 7, validating that VOCSIM's zero-shot geometry predicts supervised adaptability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the zero-shot frozen-embedding evaluation paradigm established in NLP (MTEB) and Vision (GeneCIS) to the audio domain, departing from the dominant audio benchmarking approach (HEAR, SUPERB) that measures adaptability via linear probes or fine-tuning. It builds on the transductive label-free PCA framework of Song et al. (2018) to correct foundation-model anisotropy without gradient updates, and on the Acoustic Word Embedding (AWE) literature for query-by-example search, but generalizes both to a multi-domain, training-free geometric diagnostic. The key conceptual departure is isolating single-source content identity from source separation and introducing the Global Separation Rate (GSR) as a point-wise, permutation-calibrated metric that is more domain-stable than local retrieval purity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VOCSIM exposes a concrete cross-lingual speech generalization gap: SOTA models organize the acoustic space of unseen low-resource languages (Shipibo-Conibo, Chintang) only marginally better than random chance, with P@1 collapsing from ~67% to ~11.5% while SNR remains comparable. This has direct implications for deploying 'universal' audio models in under-represented linguistic communities and provides a metric (permutation-calibrated GSR) to track progress. Limitations include single-source-only scope (no polyphony), a single environmental subset (ESC-50), and blind sets restricted to speech; extending strict OOD evaluation to animal and environmental domains is left to future work. The benchmark's practical value as a training-free model-selection filter is validated by its correlation with HEAR SOTA and avian perceptual judgments.
