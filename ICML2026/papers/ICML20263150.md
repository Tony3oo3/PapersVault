## Problem

Current SNN-based event object detectors suffer from three interrelated limitations: basic neurons are overly sensitive to noise (triggering unnecessary spikes and inflating energy use), accumulation-based event representations (HIST, VoxelCube, MESTOR) break end-to-end spiking characteristics and introduce temporal redundancy, and unidirectional backbones lack the multi-scale feature integration that biological visual cortices achieve through bidirectional information flow. No prior work has systematically addressed all three challenges through bio-vision-inspired mechanisms at the neuron, representation, and architecture levels simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method consumes asynchronous event streams from DVS cameras, where each event is a (x, y, t, p) tuple with binary polarity. NCAR inputs are resized to 64×64 with 100 ms temporal windows; Gen1 preserves native 304×240 resolution with 100 ms bounding-box crops. A key design constraint is that the E2S front-end converts raw events into binary spike feature maps (0/1) before they enter the SNN backbone, unlike accumulation-based methods that produce continuous-valued inputs. The network outputs object bounding boxes with class labels via an SSD head (detection) or a classification head (recognition on NCAR), operating at 5 time steps.

## Method

The proposed STATNF-BiSNet integrates three bio-vision-inspired components. At the neuron level, STATNFN applies a Spatio-Temporal Adaptive Threshold soft-threshold filter to membrane potential inputs: it normalizes inputs spatiotemporally, computes spatial feature statistics, projects them through a learnable temporal matrix to produce an adaptive threshold Θ, and zeros out sub-threshold activations—reducing firing rates by ~50% without accuracy loss, and it can be stacked on any base neuron (SPSN5, PLIF, LIF). At the representation level, E2S emulates retinal functions by using Memory-enhanced PLIF (MPLIF) neurons (which initialize membrane potential with the full temporal sum for long-range memory), STATNFN for denoising, and three Dilated Convolution Units at dilation rates {1,2,3} for multi-scale perception, all producing binary spike outputs. At the architecture level, BiSNet replaces the unidirectional FPN with bidirectional top-down and bottom-up pathways plus lateral fusion blocks across four spiking residual stages (strides 4–32), inspired by V1–V4 corticocortical feedback, enabling each layer to access both fine-grained details and high-level semantics without a separate neck network. Training uses surrogate-gradient backpropagation (arctan-based) with AdamW over 5 time steps.

## Results

On the Gen1 dataset, STATNF-BiSNet achieves SOTA detection performance with mAP₅₀:₉₀ of 40.0% and mAP₅₀ of 69.1%, surpassing the best brain-inspired SNN (MSD) by 2.8% in mAP₅₀ at 4.3× lower energy (1.48 mJ vs. 6.5 mJ). On NCAR, it reaches 96.1% accuracy with the lowest firing rate (0.09) and energy consumption (0.02 mJ) among all SNNs. On N-Caltech101, it sets a new benchmark with mAP₅₀ of 63.5%, outperforming the prior best SNN (EAS-SNN) by 9.7 percentage points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Gen1, STATNF-BiSNet (mAP₅₀ = 69.1%) outperforms all SNN baselines: MSD (66.3%, +2.8%), SpikeYOLO (66.4%, +2.7%), EAS-SNN (67.5%, +1.6%), and CREST (63.2%, +5.9%), while consuming 4.3× less energy than MSD and 13.4× less than EAS-SNN. On NCAR, it achieves 96.1% accuracy, beating CREST DenseNet121-16 (95.2%) by 0.9% with half the energy (0.02 vs. 0.04 mJ) and a substantially lower firing rate (0.09 vs. 0.146). On N-Caltech101, it surpasses EAS-SNN (mAP₅₀ = 53.8%) by 9.7 points and AEGNN (59.5%) by 4.0 points with comparable parameter counts (10M vs. 8.9M and 20M respectively). Against ANN methods, STATNF-BiSNet is 262× more energy-efficient than EGO-12 (387.96 mJ) while achieving higher mAP₅₀ (69.1% vs. 50.4%). The ablation confirms that the combination of STATNF, E2S, and BiSNet yields a 7.7% mAP improvement over the base architecture with only +1.1M additional parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the directly-trained SNN paradigm for event-based detection (EMS-ResNet, Su et al. 2023) by introducing bio-vision-inspired mechanisms at three hierarchical levels: a noise-filtering neuron (STATNFN) built atop SPSN5/PLIF, a spike-preserving event representation (E2S) that replaces accumulation-based encodings (HIST, VoxelCube, MESTOR), and a bidirectional multi-scale backbone (BiSNet) inspired by corticocortical feedback. The soft-threshold filtering mechanism is borrowed from signal processing (Zhao et al., 2019) and adapted to the spiking neuron context, while the spatiotemporal normalization draws on Duan et al. (2022). The key departure from prior SNN event detectors is the insistence on end-to-end binary spike propagation (rejecting floating-point accumulation) combined with multi-level noise suppression, positioning the work as a more biologically faithful alternative to both ANN-to-SNN conversion and directly-trained SNNs with voxel-based inputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a coherent, multi-level framework for making SNNs more robust to the noise inherent in event data, with the plug-and-play STATNF module potentially applicable beyond event-based detection. The 7.7% mAP gain with only +1.1M parameters and a 4.1× energy reduction from noise filtering alone are practically meaningful for embedded neuromorphic deployment. However, the model remains untested on actual neuromorphic hardware, and the evaluation is limited to three relatively small datasets (NCAR, N-Caltech101, Gen1), leaving open whether the bio-vision-inspired design generalizes to larger-scale or more diverse event-based tasks such as tracking or depth estimation. The bidirectional architecture's advantage over unidirectional baselines, while consistent, is modest in absolute terms and may not scale to very deep networks.
