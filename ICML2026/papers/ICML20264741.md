## Problem

Reinforcement learning for open-ended medical dialogue is hampered by the difficulty of defining a single scalar reward that captures safety, empathy, completeness, and context-awareness simultaneously. Learned reward models are costly to build, fragile under distribution shift, and vulnerable to reward hacking—particularly dangerous in high-stakes clinical settings. The paper asks whether interpretable, case-specific rubric criteria can replace opaque scalar rewards to enable effective, data-efficient RL alignment for medical consultation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264741/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-turn medical dialogues (truncated at 4,096 tokens) from public consultation datasets and, at training time only, a set of 5–25 case-specific signed rubric criteria (positive weights for desirable clinical behaviors, negative weights for unsafe or misleading ones) generated via a retrieval-augmented pipeline. At inference, the aligned 4B-parameter policy produces a single medical consultation response (up to 9,216 tokens during training, 4,096 during evaluation) without any rubric input. The rubrics thus act purely as a training-time reward decomposition, not as a runtime conditioning signal.

## Method

ORBIT decomposes alignment into three stages: (1) constructing realistic multi-turn medical dialogues from public datasets; (2) generating case-specific rubrics by retrieving semantically similar reference cases and rubric candidates from a HealthBench-4k seed pool via a two-stage embed-and-rerank pipeline, then prompting a generator LLM (DeepSeek-R1) to synthesize novel signed criteria; and (3) optimizing the policy with GRPO where the reward is a weighted sum of per-criterion binary judge outcomes (R = Σ I[judge→True]·w_j), enabling fine-grained credit assignment at the criterion level. Two stability mechanisms prevent training collapse: variance-aware filtering discards rollout groups with near-zero reward spread, and staged entropy injection periodically re-initializes from the best checkpoint while raising sampling temperature to restore exploration. A Pass@k difficulty filter at both the sample level (retaining intermediate-difficulty queries) and rubric level (removing saturated criteria) concentrates gradient updates on informative signals, yielding a 293% relative improvement on HealthBench-Hard with only 2k training samples.

## Results

On HealthBench-Hard (evaluated by GPT-4.1), InfiMed-ORBIT-4B trained with only 2k samples raises Qwen3-4B-Instruct's total score from 7.0 to 27.5 (+20.5, a 293% relative improvement). Scaling to 8k and 28k samples further lifts the score to 33.6 and 37.3, respectively, surpassing all open-source baselines up to 8× its parameter size. The largest per-axis gains are in Completeness (+20.5 over base) and Context Awareness (+11.8), while Communication Quality drops slightly (−8.2) due to rubric-induced safety constraints.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InfiMed-ORBIT-4B (2k) outperforms Qwen3-30B-Instruct (13.1) and GPT-4.1 (13.2) by a wide margin (27.5 vs. 13.1/13.2), despite being 7.5× smaller than the 30B model and using only 2k training samples. At 28k samples, ORBIT-4B (37.3) surpasses Baichuan-M2-32B (34.5), a dedicated medical model 8× its size, and Qwen3-30B-A3B-Thinking (16.1) by over 20 points. The only model that outperforms ORBIT-4B (28k) is GPT-5 (thinking) at 46.2, though this score is taken from a third-party report (Baichuan-M2) rather than a direct API evaluation. Within the sub-10B open-source regime, ORBIT-4B (8k) at 33.6 sets a new state-of-the-art, exceeding Qwen3-4B-Thinking (5.2) and Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct (0) by large margins. The per-axis breakdown shows ORBIT's gains are broad-based across Emergency Referrals, Context Seeking, and Global Health themes, rather than concentrated in a single dimension.

## Relation to Prior Work

ORBIT builds directly on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) as its policy optimization backbone but departs from the standard RLHF paradigm of learned scalar reward models by replacing them with interpretable, criterion-level rubric rewards. It extends the HealthBench evaluation framework (Arora et al., 2025) from a benchmarking tool into a training signal source, using retrieval-augmented generation to synthesize case-specific rubrics from a small seed pool rather than relying on handcrafted rules or domain-specific judge fine-tuning. The approach also connects to the broader post-training shift from SFT to RL (Minaee et al., 2024; Touvron et al., 2023) and to recent work on rubric-driven alignment (Gunjal et al., 2025; Viswanathan et al., 2026), but uniquely combines RAG-based rubric generation, Pass@k difficulty filtering, and variance-aware rollout masking into a single data-efficient pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework demonstrates that rubric-guided RL can close a substantial performance gap for compact models in high-stakes open-ended domains, with the 4B model surpassing 30B+ open-source baselines using only 2k training samples. This is practically significant for deployment settings where model size and inference cost are constrained, and the rubric-based reward structure offers a more transparent and hack-resistant alternative to scalar reward models in safety-critical applications. Key limitations include dependence on a small human-written rubric seed pool (acknowledged by the authors), validation on a single base model family and a single benchmark, and the open question of whether the approach generalizes to other open-ended domains (psychology, legal, social interaction) without domain-specific seeds. The distributional analysis showing a structural policy shift rather than mere mean improvement provides a useful diagnostic for distinguishing genuine alignment from inference-scaling artifacts.
