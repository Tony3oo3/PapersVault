## Problem

Prior theoretical analyses of GP-PSRL suffer from three simultaneous limitations: they either fail to account for the unbounded state space induced by Gaussian noise, produce regret bounds with sub-optimal (linear) dependence on the maximum information gain, or require strong smoothness assumptions (e.g., four-times differentiable kernels) that exclude commonly used Matérn kernels. No existing result provides a Bayesian regret bound for GP-PSRL that handles unbounded states, achieves square-root dependence on information gain, and works under weak Hölder continuity conditions.

## Input / Output

The algorithm operates in a finite-horizon MDP with continuous state space S = R^{d_s} (unbounded) and a bounded action set A ⊆ B^{d_a}(R_a). At each step it consumes the current state observation, a known bounded reward function r, a prior covariance kernel c (e.g., squared exponential or Matérn), and the known noise standard deviation σ. It outputs an action drawn from an optimal policy for a sampled MDP, and the theoretical contribution is a Bayesian regret bound of order Õ(H√(γ_T T)) where γ_T is the expected information gain evaluated on a ball of radius Õ(√((d_s+d_a) log T)).

## Method

The proof proceeds in two main parts. First, a recursive application of the Borell-Tsirelson-Ibragimov-Sudakov (BTIS) inequality shows that, with high probability (1 − 2/T), all states visited by the algorithm lie within a Euclidean ball whose radius grows only as √((d_s+d_a) log T), resolving the unbounded-state-space issue. Second, under this boundedness event, the regret is decomposed into a sum of model estimation errors (via a simulation lemma), which are then bounded using a chaining argument (Dudley entropy integral) rather than confidence-set constructions. This chaining approach, combined with a trick from Schwartz et al. (2025) for bounding products of sub-Gaussian and bounded random variables, and an elliptical potential lemma, yields square-root dependence on the expected information gain γ_T rather than the linear dependence on Γ_T obtained by prior confidence-set methods. The entire analysis requires only that the kernel be bounded and Hölder continuous, accommodating Matérn kernels with any smoothness parameter ν > 0.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 4.11) establishes a Bayesian regret bound of Õ(H√(γ_T T)) for GP-PSRL, where γ_T is the expected information gain and H is the horizon. This improves upon all previous PSRL bounds, which have at least linear dependence on the maximum information gain Γ_T (or H^{3/2} dependence). Empirically, on a 2D navigation task (2D state, 2D action, H=20, 100 episodes), GP-PSRL exhibits sublinear regret across four GP priors (squared exponential and Matérn ν=1/2, 3/2, 5/2), with log-log plots confirming the predicted √T rate for the squared exponential kernel and the specialized Matérn rates from Corollary 4.13.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's GP-PSRL bound Õ(H√(γ_T T)) strictly improves upon all five compared baselines in the GP-prior setting. Against Fan & Ming (2021), the closest prior PSRL result for GPs, the improvement is twofold: the dependence on the information gain drops from linear (Γ_N) to square-root (√γ_T), and the horizon dependence improves from H^{3/2} to H. Against Chowdhury & Gopalan (2019), which requires a four-times-differentiable kernel and assumes a bounded state space, the present result only needs Hölder continuity and handles unbounded state spaces via the BTIS-based state confinement argument. Against the optimistic (UCB) baselines of Kakade et al. (2020) and Curi et al. (2020), the GP-PSRL bound matches the H-linear dependence of Kakade et al. while using the instance-dependent expected information gain γ_T rather than the worst-case Γ_T, and avoids the H^{(H+1)/2} blowup of Curi et al. Empirically, GP-PSRL achieves sublinear regret with all tested kernels, and smoother priors (higher Matérn ν) yield lower regret, consistent with the theory.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GP-PSRL regret analysis lineage from Osband et al. (2013) and Chowdhury & Gopalan (2019) by simultaneously resolving three limitations that had each been partially addressed in isolation: unbounded state spaces, sub-optimal dependence on information gain, and restrictive prior/smoothness assumptions. The key technical departure is replacing the confidence-set argument (which yields linear dependence on Γ_T) with a chaining-based bound on model estimation errors, borrowing the Dudley entropy integral framework from Contal & Vayatis (2016) for GP bandits and adapting it to the sequential RL setting. The proof that visited states remain within a ball of radius O(√(d log T)) relies on a recursive application of the BTIS inequality, a tool previously used for GP bandit suprema but not for controlling state trajectories in MDPs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This provides the first theoretically rigorous regret bound for GP-PSRL in the natural setting where Gaussian noise makes the state space unbounded, removing a fundamental gap that had made prior GP-PSRL guarantees technically inapplicable to the standard model. The O~(H√(γ_T T)) rate matches the best known rate even in the simpler GP bandit special case (H=1), and the empirical validation confirms the specialized Matérn kernel rates closely. Open questions include whether worst-case (frequentist) regret bounds of the same order can be established, whether the rate is truly optimal (no matching lower bound is known), and whether the analysis extends to locally bounded kernels (e.g., linear) without requiring stabilizing policies.
