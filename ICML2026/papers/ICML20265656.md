## Problem

Existing ANN-to-SNN conversion pipelines for Transformers spike the linear algebra operations but leave key nonlinear operators (Softmax, SiLU, RMSNorm) unaddressed, since these require division, exponentiation, or norm computations that do not arise naturally from LIF dynamics. Under strict spike-only neuromorphic deployment, continuous-valued states are unavailable, making these nonlinearities a fundamental obstacle to end-to-end spiking Transformers. The paper asks whether plug-and-play, training-free spike-based replacements for these operators can be designed that integrate seamlessly into existing conversion pipelines.

## Input / Output

The method consumes spike-coded signals produced by LIF temporal accumulation over T timesteps, with a two-window execution structure: the first window integrates the denominator input to establish a normalization scale, and the second window drives the numerator through the operator. Outputs are decoded from population spike counts (number of active neurons in the division group) or from CORDIC tree outputs, yielding spike-coded Softmax probability vectors, SiLU activation values, and normalized vectors. All arithmetic is restricted to additions, subtractions, comparisons, bit-shifts, and LUT lookups—no floating-point multiplication or division.

## Method

The framework decomposes Transformer nonlinearities into three spike-friendly primitives and composes them as modular blocks. (1) A Division Neuron Group uses L standard LIF neurons with ordered thresholds θ_i = iθ, where θ is derived from the temporally integrated denominator via a single right-shift; the quotient is decoded by counting active neurons, implementing integer division. (2) A PolarNorm Unit approximates the ℓ₂-norm via a binary-tree of CORDIC-Hypot operations (shift-add only), with a fixed inverse-gain rescaling. (3) A PWL-Exp Unit approximates exp(x) on [−H, H] using K-segment piecewise-linear interpolation stored in 8-bit LUTs. Each nonlinearity (Softmax, SiLU, RMSNorm) is factorized into a numerator path and a denominator path built from these primitives, recombined through the Division Neuron—requiring no weight modification, no fine-tuning, and no changes to the surrounding ANN-to-SNN pipeline. The paper provides per-function error bounds showing the total approximation error decomposes additively into isolated contributions from each primitive, and empirically validates <1% accuracy drop across LLaMA, Mistral, Qwen3, and BERT models.

## Results

At the model level, selectively replacing nonlinear operators (Softmax, SiLU, RMSNorm) with NLSpike incurs less than 1% average accuracy drop across all evaluated LLMs: LLaMA-3-8B drops by 0.3% (0.730→0.727), LLaMA-2-7B by 0.2% (0.686→0.684), Mistral-7B is unchanged (0.724), and Qwen3-8B actually improves by 1.4% (0.734→0.748). For the SpikeZIP-converted BERT model (T=64), average accuracy improves by 0.3% (0.807→0.810). At the operator level under 8-bit quantization, NLS-Softmax achieves the lowest mean approximation error across all input dimensions, NLS-SiLU achieves the lowest mean error among training-free baselines, and NLS-RMS maintains consistently low error across all tested dimensions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

At the operator level, NLSpike outperforms all training-free baselines: for SiLU, NLS-SiLU achieves the lowest mean error while keeping maximum error comparable to a 64-segment PWL-sigmoid, whereas Sorbet and hard-swish deviate more and DoReFa-4b/XNOR exhibit both high mean and peak errors. For Softmax, NLS-Softmax consistently yields the lowest mean error across all input dimensions (d=8 to 256), while Padé/PWL approximations may approach similar mean accuracy but suffer larger maximum error and variance at higher dimensions, and Sorbet/hardmax remain substantially worse. For RMSNorm, NLS-RMS maintains consistently low mean error across all tested dimensions, while blockwise RMS (block size 32/64) becomes unstable when d does not align with the block partition. At the model level, NLSpike is compatible with both SpikeLLM and SpikeZIP ANN-to-SNN pipelines without modifying any conversion procedures, introducing negligible accuracy changes (≤0.3% average). Unlike Sorbet, which requires knowledge distillation and fine-tuning to align the spiking model, NLSpike is entirely training-free and plug-and-play, making it directly composable with existing conversion pipelines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ANN-to-SNN conversion paradigm (Rueckauer et al., 2017; You et al., 2024; Xing et al., 2025), which has so far focused on spiking linear algebra operations (attention matmuls, FFN projections), by addressing the previously neglected nonlinear operators (Softmax, SiLU, RMSNorm). It departs from Sorbet (Tang et al., 2025), the closest prior work on spiking nonlinearities, by eliminating the need for training or fine-tuning and instead using a modular, training-free decomposition into three spike-friendly primitives: population-coded division, CORDIC-based norm estimation (Volder, 1959), and piecewise-linear exponential lookup. The key conceptual departure is treating division itself as a first-class spiking primitive via ordered-threshold LIF populations, rather than approximating each nonlinear function end-to-end.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work removes a fundamental deployment blocker for end-to-end spiking Transformers on neuromorphic hardware, where continuous-valued states are unavailable and only spike-based primitives are supported. The <1% accuracy drop across LLaMA, Mistral, and Qwen3 models demonstrates that the approximation is practically lossless, and the modular design (shared PWL-Exp LUT across all three nonlinearities) keeps memory overhead minimal. However, the paper acknowledges the absence of end-to-end deployment on real spiking hardware, and it remains open whether approximation errors compound in very deep or very wide models, or whether the fixed-point scaling assumptions hold under all operating conditions. The framework also naturally extends to LayerNorm and other normalization variants, suggesting a broader design space for spike-compatible operator libraries.
