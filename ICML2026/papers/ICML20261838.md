## Problem

Multi-vector retrieval models (e.g., ColBERT) achieve superior accuracy over single-vector models by encoding each token separately and scoring via MaxSim, but the resulting search latency is an order of magnitude higher. Prior multi-vector search methods either rely on token-level pruning (an inaccurate proxy for MaxSim requiring large rerank sets) or produce high-dimensional fixed encodings (10240-dim FDEs in MUVERA) that are memory-heavy and struggle on non-ColBERTv2 embeddings. No existing method simultaneously achieves high recall, low latency, and robustness across diverse multi-vector embedding models.

## Input / Output

At indexing time, LEMUR consumes a fixed corpus of multi-vector document embeddings (variable-length sets of d-dimensional vectors per document) and a small sample of token embeddings (from corpus documents encoded with the query encoder, or from actual training queries) to train the model. At query time, it takes a multi-vector query representation (typically 32 d-dimensional token embeddings for ColBERTv2, up to 300 for long queries) and produces a single pooled d'-dimensional vector (d'=2048) used for ANNS, followed by exact MaxSim reranking of the top-k' candidates to yield the final top-k documents. The entire pipeline is corpus-specific: the learned document weight vectors and ANNS index are rebuilt per corpus.

## Method

LEMUR reformulates multi-vector similarity search as a two-step problem reduction. First, it observes that MaxSim decomposes as a sum over query tokens of per-token functions g_l(x) = max_{c∈C_l} ⟨x,c⟩, making the task a multi-output regression with m outputs (one per corpus document). A one-hidden-layer MLP φ(x) = W·ψ(x) is trained with MSE loss to approximate g, where ψ(x) = LN(GELU(W'x+b)) is the feature encoder and W ∈ R^{m×d'} is a bias-free linear output layer. Second, because the output layer is linear, the full MaxSim estimate factors as f(X) ≈ W·Ψ(X) where Ψ(X) = Σ_{x∈X} ψ(x), so each document's score is simply the inner product ⟨w_j, Ψ(X)⟩ in the d'-dimensional latent space—reducing the search to standard single-vector MIPS solvable by any ANNS index (HNSW via Glass). To scale to large corpora, the feature encoder ψ is pretrained on a subset of m'=8192 documents, then each document's weight vector w_j is computed in closed form via OLS with ψ frozen, avoiding the need to train an m-output head on the full corpus.

## Results

On eight BEIR datasets with ColBERTv2 embeddings (k=100), LEMUR achieves the best QPS at ≥80% recall across all datasets, ranging from 426 QPS (HotpotQA) to 4068 QPS (Quora). LEMUR is 5–16× faster than the best-performing baseline method at the same recall threshold. On the ViDoRe visual document retrieval dataset, LEMUR also outperforms all baselines, though the gap narrows due to the higher reranking cost of image representations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LEMUR outperforms all four baselines on every evaluated dataset. Against MUVERA (the closest competitor, which also reduces multi-vector search to single-vector ANNS), LEMUR is 5–11× faster at ≥80% recall, and on non-ColBERTv2 models (answerai-colbert-small-v1, GTE-ModernColBERT, LFM2-ColBERT), MUVERA fails to exceed 60% recall while LEMUR maintains high recall. Against IGP, LEMUR is 5–11× faster; IGP is the strongest token-pruning baseline but still lags significantly. DESSERT fails to reach 80% recall on MS MARCO and HotpotQA entirely, and where it does succeed, LEMUR is 5–16× faster. PLAID, the original ColBERTv2 engine, is the slowest baseline across all datasets, with LEMUR achieving 5–46× higher QPS. The paper also shows that the embedding model used has a larger impact on baseline performance than the dataset itself, with LEMUR being the only method that consistently achieves high recall across all tested multi-vector models.

## Relation to Prior Work

LEMUR departs from the dominant token-level pruning paradigm used by PLAID, DESSERT, EMVB, and IGP, which retrieve candidate document tokens per query token and then rerank. Conceptually, it is closest to MUVERA, which also reduces multi-vector search to single-vector ANNS, but whereas MUVERA's fixed-dimensional encodings are data-oblivious, LEMUR learns a corpus-specific supervised regression model whose linear output layer directly yields the single-vector document embeddings. The formulation as multi-output regression with a linear head is the key architectural choice that makes the reduction to standard MIPS/ANNS exact rather than heuristic, and it distinguishes LEMUR from distillation approaches (TCT-ColBERT, BGE-M3) that train a new encoder rather than a lightweight search proxy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving a 5–16× speedup over the best prior multi-vector search methods at ≥80% recall while preserving ColBERTv2's nDCG@10, LEMUR substantially narrows the latency gap that has hindered adoption of multi-vector retrieval in production systems. Its compatibility with off-the-shelf ANNS libraries (HNSW via Glass, CAGRA on GPU) and its robustness across seven different embedding models make it a practical drop-in acceleration layer. Open questions include whether the multi-output regression framing generalizes to other late-interaction similarity measures, how to handle incremental indexing without retraining, and whether extremely low-precision quantization (e.g., 2-bit) can be applied to the learned single-vector representations without degrading recall.
