## Problem

Standard LLM deployment assigns a uniform token limit to every query, implicitly assuming all queries share the same compute-utility profile. In practice, reasoning utility follows a non-concave S-shaped curve (Strict–Surge–Ample), so uniform allocation wastes scarce tokens on hard queries stuck in the Strict phase while over-allocating to easy queries in the diminishing-returns Ample phase. The paper asks how to optimally partition a fixed global token budget across heterogeneous queries to maximize aggregate reasoning accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method takes a batch of N reasoning queries, a global token budget B_total, a frozen LLM backbone (e.g., Qwen2.5-Math-7B), and a lightweight DeBERTa-v3-base predictor that outputs a per-query emergence threshold τ̂_i (log-token-length regression). It produces a per-query integer token-allocation vector t = [t_1, …, t_N] summing to B_total, where some entries are zero (rational abandonment); each t_i then serves as the max_new_tokens cap for autoregressive generation of that query's response.

## Method

CLEAR models per-query reasoning utility with a shifted-surge function φ_i(t) = α_i(t−τ_i)·e^{−β_i(t−τ_i)} for t ≥ τ_i (zero otherwise), capturing the empirically observed Strict–Surge–Ample structure. It formulates batch allocation as a constrained maximization of total utility under a global token budget and derives, via Lagrange multipliers, a closed-form Lambert W solution t_i*(λ) = τ_i + (1/β_i)[1 − W_0(λe/α_i)] that gives each query's optimal token count at a given shadow price λ. A bisection search over λ finds the unique market-clearing price λ* such that aggregate demand equals B_total, and queries whose net economic surplus is negative are rationally abandoned (assigned zero tokens). The threshold τ̂_i is predicted by a DeBERTa-v3-base regressor trained on GSM8K/MATH solution lengths, while α and β are global hyperparameters (β adapted dynamically from the budget surplus), making CLEAR a plug-and-play inference wrapper requiring no backbone retraining.

## Results

Under the most constrained budget of 256 tokens per query, CLEAR (Lambert) achieves 14.6% accuracy on the Balanced stream, 33.0% on Mostly-Easy, 6.2% on Mostly-Hard, and 18.6% on U-Shaped, improving over Uniform allocation by +11.6, +24.0, +5.2, and +14.2 accuracy points respectively. In the resource-scarce regime, CLEAR delivers up to a 3× improvement in global accuracy compared to uniform allocation. On code generation (best-of-4, 4096 total completion tokens), CLEAR improves over Uniform by +6.3 points on HumanEval+ (43.1 vs. 36.8), +6.5 on MBPP+ (45.9 vs. 39.4), and +3.6 on BigCodeBench (19.8 vs. 16.2). Gains shrink as budget increases, confirming that the shadow-price mechanism is most beneficial when tokens are scarce.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLEAR (Lambert) consistently outperforms all baselines in the resource-scarce regime (256 tokens/query). Against TALE-EP (Han et al., 2025), which uses a teacher model to estimate per-query token needs and renormalizes allocations, CLEAR achieves +10.8 points on Balanced (14.6 vs. 3.8), +25.8 on Mostly-Easy (33.0 vs. 7.2), +2.0 on Mostly-Hard (6.2 vs. 4.2), and +11.4 on U-Shaped (18.6 vs. 7.2). Against the Uniform baseline, gains range from +5.2 to +24.0 points depending on stream composition, with the largest advantage on Mostly-Easy where uniform allocation wastes tokens on queries that cannot reach their emergence threshold. Against the Predictor-based proportional allocation, CLEAR wins by +14.0 (Balanced) and +31.2 (Mostly-Easy) at 256 tokens, since the Predictor policy cannot perform rational abandonment. Among internal ablations, CLEAR (Lambert) marginally edges out CLEAR (Auction) and CLEAR (Heuristic), confirming that the closed-form Lambert W allocation with continuous marginal-utility balancing is slightly superior to discrete greedy or median-cutoff strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of inference-time scaling and constrained resource allocation. While prior work (TALE, SelfBudgeter, DSC) addresses per-query token budget estimation or difficulty-adaptive sampling, and model-routing systems (FrugalGPT, RouteLLM) allocate across model tiers, none formulate the batch-level problem of splitting a fixed global token budget across heterogeneous queries with the option of rational abandonment. The paper repurposes classical Lagrangian/KKT machinery and the Lambert W function to derive a closed-form allocation policy, framing token allocation as a market-clearing problem with a global shadow price—a conceptual lens absent from the LLM inference literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CLEAR offers a plug-and-play, retraining-free inference wrapper that could be adopted by any serving system facing hard token budgets, and the shadow-price framing provides an interpretable economic principle for when to abandon a query. However, the practical impact is tempered by the reliance on a DeBERTa threshold predictor whose accuracy degrades under noise, and the largest gains (+24 pts) come against a very weak uniform-allocation baseline in the most extreme scarcity regime. Open questions include whether the shifted-surge utility model generalizes beyond mathematical reasoning, how the method behaves under online/streaming (non-batch) settings, and whether the gap to oracle allocation can be closed with better utility modeling.
