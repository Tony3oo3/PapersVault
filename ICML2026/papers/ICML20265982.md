## Problem

Existing unsupervised point cloud denoising methods establish correspondences between noisy variants through stochastic noise injection or Earth Mover's Distance, but these correspondences are ambiguous: matched points are not guaranteed to represent observations of the same underlying surface location. Unlike images, point clouds lack fixed spatial indexing, so noise perturbs coordinates that encode both position and geometry, breaking the pixel-wise assumptions that make unsupervised image denoising work. This correspondence ambiguity prevents accurate localization of the target surface.

## Input / Output

The method takes two noisy point cloud variants of the same shape (each N×3, with N=10K or 50K points) as input, where noise is Gaussian at 0.5%–2% of the bounding sphere radius during training and 1%–3% during testing. Each variant is processed independently through a shared iterative denoiser (L=2 blocks), and the output is a denoised point cloud per variant. The mirror-point generation operates per-point within each variant, requiring no cross-variant matching beyond a coarse Chamfer Distance regularization.

## Method

SIMPC introduces a Mirror-Point Generation Module (MPGM) that, for each noisy point, extends its denoising vector by a factor w₂=2 to produce a mirror-point on the opposite side of the underlying surface, then recomputes the local KNN neighborhood at the mirror location and runs a second denoising pass (PSA + decoder) to obtain a denoised mirror-point. The Mirror-Point Consistency Loss (MPCL) enforces L2 consistency between the denoised seed point and its denoised mirror counterpart, establishing a deterministic one-to-one correspondence tied to the same surface location. The overall objective sums MPCL across L=2 denoiser blocks with a Chamfer Distance similarity term between the two variants. The backbone is a 3-layer DGCNN encoder (k=32, C=256) feeding iterative denoiser blocks that use Point Self-Attention for local geometric refinement, with the key insight that self-induced mirror pairs from a single noisy point avoid the correspondence ambiguity inherent in cross-variant matching.

## Results

On PUNet (10K points, 2% Gaussian noise), SIMPC achieves CD=28.82 and P2M=7.13, outperforming the best unsupervised baseline U-CAN (CD=32.34, P2M=12.55) by 11% in CD and 43% in P2M. At 50K points with 1% noise, SIMPC reaches CD=5.81 and P2M=1.02, surpassing U-CAN (CD=8.35, P2M=6.09) by 30% in CD and 83% in P2M. On the real-world Kinect dataset, SIMPC (CD=13.01, P2M=6.35) outperforms all supervised baselines including StraightPCF (CD=13.46, P2M=7.39) and PD-Refiner (CD=14.34, P2M=7.61). SIMPC also demonstrates strong generalization to non-Gaussian noise (Laplacian, Discrete, Anisotropic, Uniform) and extremely sparse 5K-point settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among unsupervised methods, SIMPC dominates across all settings: on PUNet 10K/2% noise it reduces P2M from 12.55 (U-CAN) to 7.13 and CD from 32.34 to 28.82, and on 50K/1% noise it cuts P2M from 6.09 (U-CAN) to 1.02 — a 6× improvement in surface alignment. Against the best supervised methods (PD-Refiner, StraightPCF, PD-LTS), SIMPC remains slightly behind on synthetic PUNet/PCNet (e.g., P2M 3.60 vs. 1.66 at 10K/1%), but surpasses all of them on the real-world Kinect dataset (P2M 6.35 vs. 7.39 for StraightPCF, 7.61 for PD-Refiner). On non-Gaussian noise (Laplacian, Discrete, Anisotropic, Uniform), SIMPC consistently outperforms Noise2Score3D and Score-U by large margins (e.g., Discrete 10K/1%: P2M 1.89 vs. 3.02 for Noise2Score3D) while remaining competitive with supervised baselines. Optimization-based methods (Jet, Bilateral, MRPCA, GLR, Lowrank) are uniformly outperformed by SIMPC by 2–5× in both CD and P2M across all noise levels and resolutions. The ablation confirms that the mirror-point consistency loss (L_MPC) is the critical component: replacing it with EMD-based correspondence degrades P2M from 3.60 to 7.64 at 10K/1%, and using only Chamfer Distance yields P2M=2.47 but with much worse CD (18.91 vs. 20.25) and significantly higher error at higher noise levels.

## Relation to Prior Work

SIMPC builds on the iterative denoising paradigm established by U-CAN (Zhou et al., 2025a) and IPFN (de Silva Edirimuni et al., 2023b), adopting their two-variant input setup and shared denoiser blocks with DGCNN encoders and Point Self-Attention. The key departure is in correspondence construction: rather than relying on noise injection (Noise4Denoise, Noise2Score3D) or Earth Mover's Distance (NoiseMap, U-CAN) to establish statistical mappings across independently sampled noisy variants, SIMPC generates a self-induced mirror-point from each noisy point using the denoising vector itself, creating deterministic one-to-one correspondences tied to the same underlying surface location. The Mirror-Point Consistency Loss (MPCL) then enforces agreement between the denoised seed and mirror outputs, replacing the ambiguous transport-based or noise-based objectives with a point-wise consistency constraint.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental limitation in unsupervised point cloud denoising—correspondence ambiguity—by showing theoretically (Appendix A.6) that ambiguous cross-variant correspondences are inherently ill-posed because they lack a unique shared target, introducing irreducible bias. The practical impact is significant: SIMPC achieves SOTA among unsupervised methods across synthetic and real-world datasets, and on the Kinect dataset it surpasses all compared supervised methods. However, the authors acknowledge performance degradation on extremely sparse point clouds (5K points) and mild smoothing of sharp geometric features due to the absence of ground-truth geometric detail, leaving open the question of whether the mirror-point framework can be extended to handle high-curvature regions or very sparse inputs without additional supervision.
