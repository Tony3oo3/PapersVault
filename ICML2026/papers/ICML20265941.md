## Problem

Existing representation alignment (REPA) strategies for audio Flow Matching rely on heuristic fixed-layer selection or cross-modal (video-to-audio) supervision, leaving token-conditioned audio synthesis unexplored. The paper identifies a fundamental gap: layers that store the richest semantic/acoustic information (high teacher-space similarity) are not necessarily the layers that causally drive the velocity field governing generation — a phenomenon the authors term Store-Contribute Dissociation (SCD). This dissociation explains why depth-based alignment heuristics fail to maximize training efficiency.

## Input / Output

The system accepts a text prompt (with optional event tags and descriptions for general audio) plus a reference audio clip for style conditioning, and produces a continuous audio waveform. Internally, a two-stage cascade first uses an autoregressive LLM (Qwen3-0.6B) to predict discrete acoustic tokens (S3 tokens for speech, AudioSet VQ-VAE tokens for general audio, optionally 1:1 interleaved with BEATs tokens), then a DiT-based Flow Matching model transforms these token embeddings into mel-spectrograms decoded by the Vocos neural vocoder. The Flow Matching stage is shared across both speech and general audio domains, differing only in the upstream tokenization pathway.

## Method

AG-REPA introduces a forward-only gate ablation (FoG-A) that quantifies each layer's causal contribution by zeroing its residual update and measuring the induced L2 deviation in the predicted velocity field, normalized by the baseline field magnitude. A probe-then-intervene protocol runs FoG-A once during a 5,000-step diagnostic warm-up (sparse, gradient-free, <0.5% wall-clock overhead) to identify the Top-K causally dominant layers and assign attribution-proportional alignment weights. The training objective then combines the standard Flow Matching loss, an input-interface BiT-C distillation loss (aligning token embeddings with frozen Whisper/BEATs teacher features), and a sparse weighted cosine-alignment penalty applied only to the selected layers via lightweight 2-layer MLP projection heads. This shifts alignment from heuristic mid-layer targeting to functionally critical bottlenecks — typically early layers (L1–L3) where Jacobian sensitivity is maximal — yielding 18%/16% FAD reductions over the best fixed-layer REPA baseline on speech/audio respectively.

## Results

On the unified speech + general-audio benchmark (Config B, 500k-step checkpoint), AG-REPA achieves the best Speech FAD of 1.29 and Audio FAD of 2.56, reducing FAD by 18% (speech) and 16% (audio) over the best single-fixed-layer REPA baseline (Layer 8: 1.58/3.05) and by 11% over the multi-layer heuristic (L4,8,12: 1.45/2.88). AG-REPA also attains the lowest WER (3.45) and highest MOS (4.12 speech, 3.94 audio), with statistically significant MOS gains over the strongest fixed mid-layer baseline (p=0.004 speech, p=0.011 audio). Convergence is accelerated by approximately 3.3× (220k vs. 720k steps to reach Speech FAD=1.5) compared to LASP-selected REPA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the original REPA fixed-layer baselines (Yu et al., 2025), AG-REPA wins decisively: the best single-layer variant (Layer 8) yields Speech FAD 1.58 / Audio FAD 3.05, while AG-REPA reaches 1.29 / 2.56, and the multi-layer heuristic (L4,8,12) at 1.45 / 2.88 is still 11% worse in speech FAD. The Deep REPA control (L20–L22, aligned to LASP peaks) barely improves over the no-alignment baseline (1.79 vs. 1.84 speech FAD), confirming that representation-rich layers are functionally passive. For cross-architecture generalization, AG-REPA consistently improves all three external systems: Voicebox (FAD 1.20→0.95, WER 2.05→1.85), CosyVoice (FAD 0.88→0.72, WER 1.95→1.78, MOS 4.25→4.39), and F5-TTS (FAD 1.45→1.15, WER 2.40→2.12, MOS 4.05→4.22). Notably, the fixed Shallow REPA heuristic (L1–L3) that is competitive on the authors' DiT erodes on other backbones (e.g., only 7.6% FAD gain on F5-TTS vs. 20.7% for AG-REPA), demonstrating that FoG-A's per-architecture re-probing is essential for transfer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends REPA (Yu et al., 2025) from its original vision setting (DINOv2 alignment at a fixed mid-layer) to token-conditioned audio Flow Matching, where no dense visual anchor exists. It departs from prior audio-domain REPA adaptations (TARO, HunyuanVideo-Foley) that rely on video-to-audio conditioning, and from heuristic depth-based layer selection, by introducing a causal attribution criterion (FoG-A) grounded in the Information Bottleneck and residual ODE dynamics. The Store-Contribute Dissociation finding draws on interpretability literature (Klabunde et al., Braun et al., Hase et al.) showing that representational similarity does not imply functional equivalence, but applies this insight specifically to generative velocity-field training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SCD finding provides a principled explanation for why fixed-depth REPA underperforms and offers a data-driven replacement (FoG-A) that generalizes across four architectures with negligible overhead (<0.5% wall-clock). The 3.26× convergence speedup and consistent FAD/WER/MOS gains make this practically relevant for anyone training Flow Matching audio models. Open questions include whether static layer selection remains valid under longer training or stronger representation drift, whether the approach extends to dense (non-pooled) teacher signals, and whether the SCD phenomenon holds in non-residual or non-DiT generative backbones.
