## Problem

Dataset pruning (DP) and dataset distillation (DD) have been evaluated under inconsistent hyperparameters and label regimes, preventing fair comparison at extreme compression ratios. The paper reveals a paradox: when equipped with soft labels, distilled images perform worse than random subsets of original images, suggesting that DD's apparent gains stem from soft labels (which impose ~40× storage overhead) rather than from the synthetic images themselves. No existing framework addresses large-scale compression using only hard labels while prioritizing image quality over label matching.

## Input / Output

The method takes the full ImageNet-1K training set (1.28M RGB images across 1000 classes) with only hard class labels—no pretrained teacher model or soft labels are required. Images are preprocessed by cropping along the shorter side and resizing to 224×224 to match DD resolution constraints. The output is a compressed dataset at extreme IPC (10, 50, or 100 images per class, i.e., 99.2%+ compression) where multiple pruned images are combined into composite images, further reducing storage. Training uses only hard labels with standard cross-entropy loss, eliminating the multi-gigabyte soft-label storage that plagues existing DD methods.

## Method

PCA (Prune, Combine, and Augment) is a three-stage hard-label-only framework. (1) Prune: the full dataset is scored using the reversed EL2N metric to select the easiest samples, with strict per-class balance enforced to maintain IPC consistency across all 1000 classes. (2) Combine: pruned images are tiled into composite images in a cropping-free manner; the paper provides theoretical justification (Proposition 4.1 and Theorem 4.2) showing that selective cropping reduces NLL without reducing entropy, and that any entropy advantage from cropping is lost under standard training-time RandomResizedCrop augmentation. (3) Augment: a constrained augmentation restricts the RandomResizedCrop region to a single patch within the combined image, preventing simple pruned samples from being transformed into complex ones during training, thereby adhering to the data-scaling-law preference for easy samples in small-data regimes. The entire pipeline is optimization-free (no iterative image synthesis), requires no pretrained teacher, and uses only hard labels for supervision.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K with ResNet-18 under hard-label evaluation, PCA achieves 22.8% top-1 accuracy at IPC10 (99.2% compression), 39.1% at IPC50, and 45.5% at IPC100, outperforming the random baseline (4.6%, 20.6%, 31.7%) by 18.2, 18.5, and 13.8 points respectively. The soft-label benchmark reveals a paradox: distilled images with soft labels (e.g., SRe2L at 33.5% IPC10) underperform random subsets with soft labels (35.8%), while pruning-based subsets (EL2N at 40.8%, AUM at 41.5%) consistently outperform both. On PASCAL VOC object detection, PCA achieves 35.99 mAP with 1,000 images, surpassing the SOTA pruning method VPS (30.05 mAP with 1,655 images) by 5.9 mAP while using 40% fewer images.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PCA decisively outperforms all noise-initialized DD methods (SRe2L, CDA, G-VBSM, LPLD) by 19–21 points at IPC10 (22.8% vs. 1.5–3.4%), confirming that distilled images without soft labels are nearly unlearnable. Among real-image-initialized DD methods, RDED (11.5% IPC10) and DWA (1.9% IPC10) are also substantially outperformed, with PCA gaining 11.3 and 20.9 points respectively. Against pruning baselines, PCA (EL2N-based) improves over raw EL2N (12.2%) by 10.6 points at IPC10 through its combine and constrained augmentation steps, and similarly beats AUM (11.4%) and CCS (6.8%). The only method that partially competes is TDDS (Table 10), which edges out PCA at IPC50 (40.8 vs. 39.1) and IPC100 (47.6 vs. 45.5), though PCA still wins at IPC10 (22.8 vs. 20.0). On the object detection task, PCA (35.99 mAP, 1,000 images) surpasses VPS (30.05 mAP, 1,655 images) by 5.9 mAP, demonstrating generalizability beyond classification. The soft-label benchmark further shows that even the best DD methods (RDED 38.4%, DWA 37.9% at IPC10) cannot match pruning methods (EL2N 40.8%, AUM 41.5%), reinforcing that image quality—not label distillation—drives performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the convergence point of dataset pruning (DP) and dataset distillation (DD), two subfields that have historically been evaluated in isolation with inconsistent protocols. It builds directly on EL2N (Paul et al., 2021) as the core pruning metric and on He et al. (2024)'s two rules (class balance and simple-image prioritization) as the theoretical foundation for why pruning outperforms distillation at extreme compression ratios. The key conceptual departure is from the DD literature's reliance on soft labels: the paper argues that the apparent gains of methods like SRe2L, CDA, and RDED are primarily attributable to soft-label distillation rather than to the quality of synthetic images, and it introduces a hard-label-only framework (PCA) that combines pruning, image combination, and constrained augmentation without any pretrained teacher model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified benchmark is likely to become a standard reference for the dataset compression community, as it exposes previously hidden inconsistencies (batch size, loss type, augmentation) that made cross-method comparisons unreliable. The finding that soft labels impose up to 40× storage overhead while not necessarily improving over random subsets challenges a core assumption in large-scale DD and motivates a shift toward image-quality-focused compression. Open questions include whether the Swin-V2-Tiny underperformance signals a fundamental limitation for transformer architectures in extreme compression regimes, whether the data-scaling-law alignment of constrained augmentation generalizes to other modalities (video, audio), and whether the hard-label advantage persists when the target model is significantly larger than the pruning observer.
