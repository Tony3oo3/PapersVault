## Problem

Bayesian adaptive design (BAD) and simulation-based inference (SBI) have evolved in parallel: BAD policies propose informative designs but delegate posterior inference to slow non-amortized methods or restrictive parametric families, while SBI methods assume fixed designs and never optimize how observations are acquired. This paper closes the gap by jointly amortizing both the design policy and the posterior estimator in a single end-to-end training loop, enabling flexible (e.g., diffusion-based) multimodal posteriors at every experimental step without requiring explicit density evaluations or reinforcement learning.

## Input / Output

The method takes a differentiable simulator Sim(θ, ξ) that maps parameters and design variables to observations, a prior p(θ), and a design space Ξ. At each rollout step t, it consumes the running history of (design, observation) pairs, summarized into a fixed-dimensional state h_t by a learned encoder, and produces both the next design ξ_t and an approximate posterior q_ψ(θ|h_t) parameterized by a diffusion or flow-matching network. At the terminal horizon T, the output is a set of posterior samples from q_ψ(θ|h_T); intermediate posteriors are available at every step, supporting active data acquisition where a user may stop early or override the policy.

## Method

JADAI trains three networks—policy π_φ, summary η_ω, and posterior q_ψ—jointly by minimizing the negative expected utility u_T = Σ_{t=0}^{T}(ℓ*_{t-1} − ℓ_t), where ℓ_t is a generic per-step posterior loss (e.g., diffusion v-prediction or flow-matching loss) and ℓ* denotes a stop-gradient. As a scalar the utility telescopes to −ℓ_T, but the stop-gradient on baseline terms prevents gradient telescoping, so the update accumulates posterior-training signal from every rollout step without nested backpropagation through time. Designs are generated from detached history states to break the cyclic gradient path between summary and policy, and a curriculum on rollout length plus a scheduled design-prior mix-in (ρ_n) provides exploration early in training. The posterior is instantiated as a conditional diffusion model with v-prediction and a cosine variance-preserving noise schedule, or equivalently as a flow-matching network, both of which avoid the tractable-likelihood requirement that restricts prior joint-amortization methods (RL-sCEE, vsOED, ALINE) to normalizing flows or GMMs.

## Results

On the Location Finding benchmark (T=30, 2K, 10⁶ contrastive samples), JADAI (u₃₀) achieves an sPCE of 12.82±0.03, surpassing the strongest prior method RL-sCEE (12.31±0.06) and DAD (10.97±0.04). On the CES benchmark (T=10, 3K, 10⁷ L), JADAI reaches 14.85±0.05 sPCE, outperforming RL-BOED (14.60±0.10) and ALINE (14.37±0.08). On the high-dimensional MNIST Image Discovery task (T=6, σ=0), JADAI with diffusion models achieves SSIM 0.968±0.001 and NRMSE 0.177±0.001, substantially exceeding CoDiff's SSIM of 0.826, with minimal degradation under additive noise (σ=0.01: SSIM 0.985±0.001 for flow matching).

## Baselines & Comparisons

JADAI outperforms all seven baselines on the settings where they overlap. Against the non-adaptive SG-BOED (Foster et al., 2020), JADAI gains 3.98 sPCE on LF T=30 (12.82 vs 8.84) and 5.45 on CES (14.85 vs 9.40). Against the amortized DAD (Foster et al., 2021) and iDAD (Ivanova et al., 2021), JADAI improves by 1.85 and 2.74 sPCE respectively on LF T=30. Against the RL-based methods RL-BOED (Blau et al., 2022) and RL-sCEE (Blau et al., 2023), JADAI leads by 1.09 and 0.51 sPCE on LF T=30, and by 0.25 on CES. Against the most recent joint-amortization method ALINE (Huang et al., 2026), JADAI gains 0.71 sPCE on LF T=30 (1K) and 0.48 on CES, while also achieving a Wasserstein distance to MCMC of 0.07 vs ALINE's 3.64 in the multimodal two-source setting. On the high-dimensional MNIST Image Discovery task, JADAI (DM) achieves SSIM 0.968 vs CoDiff's (Iollo et al., 2025) 0.826 at σ=0, a gap of 0.142, and maintains SSIM ≥ 0.960 even at σ=0.01 where CoDiff is not evaluated.

## Relation to Prior Work

JADAI unifies two previously parallel research threads: amortized Bayesian adaptive design (DAD, iDAD) which learns a policy to propose informative experiments, and amortized simulation-based inference which learns a posterior estimator from simulator pairs. Prior work treated these as separate tasks—policy methods delegated inference to non-amortized MCMC or restricted parametric families, while SBI methods assumed fixed designs. The key methodological departure is replacing the Barber-Agakov variational bound's requirement for tractable log-densities with a generic telescoping posterior-loss utility that works with implicit generative models (diffusion, flow matching), and showing that simple gradient descent on sampled rollouts suffices where concurrent work (RL-sCEE, vsOED, ALINE) relies on REINFORCE or actor-critic estimators for the same objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing an approximate posterior at every step of the sequential design process rather than only at a fixed terminal horizon, JADAI connects naturally to active data-acquisition settings where practitioners may stop early or override policy suggestions. The main limitation is the requirement for differentiable simulators, which restricts applicability to black-box or non-differentiable experimental systems; extending to such settings would likely require gradient-free design optimization. Posterior sampling remains a computational bottleneck (seconds for 10K samples in high dimensions), and the framework's behavior in increasingly complex design spaces (spatial patterns, time-series stimuli) is unexplored.
