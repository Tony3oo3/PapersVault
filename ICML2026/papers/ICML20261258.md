## Problem

Diffusion-based video prediction models are trained with likelihood-surrogate objectives that emphasize global plausibility under the data distribution, but do not directly optimize the precision-critical visual dynamics (pose, contact timing, fine-grained spatial relations) that manipulation policies require. This objective mismatch means predicted future representations can be globally coherent yet subtly incorrect, and these small errors are amplified by downstream action generators near decision boundaries, causing compounding failures over time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261258/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a current RGB observation and a language instruction as input, producing a predicted future latent representation (extracted from the first denoising step of a Stable Video Diffusion model in EDM-style latent space) and an executable action sequence via a diffusion-policy action head. The key design choice is that the action-relevant visual feature is read from intermediate denoising hidden states rather than fully decoded pixels, keeping inference efficient and emphasizing geometry- and relation-centric cues over appearance details. The post-training reward is computed entirely in latent space by comparing the predicted future latent against the VAE-encoded ground-truth future clip.

## Method

Dyn-VPP reframes multi-step diffusion denoising as a Markov Decision Process and applies policy-gradient optimization to the denoiser, without modifying any architecture. The core mechanism is an Euler Hybrid sampler that injects SDE-style stochasticity (Euler-Ancestral) only at the first denoising step—yielding a tractable Gaussian transition for reparameterized policy-gradient estimation—while keeping all subsequent steps deterministic (Euler Discrete) to preserve temporal coherence and avoid credit-assignment issues. The policy is optimized with GRPO using a verifiable terminal reward that combines L1 distance and cosine similarity between the predicted and expert future latents, with group-normalized advantages over G=8 rollouts and PPO-style clipping. This post-training procedure directly aligns the VPM's generative distribution with high-reward denoising trajectories consistent with expert dynamics, after which the downstream action generation model is retrained on the improved visual representations.

## Results

On CALVIN ABC→D, Dyn-VPP achieves 98.0/94.8/91.3/88.3/83.1% task completion for 1–5 sequential tasks and an average trajectory length of 4.56, outperforming the base VPP policy (96.0/91.3/86.4/80.4/74.7%, len 4.28) by up to 8.4 points at the 5-task horizon. On the longer-horizon L-CALVIN benchmark (10 tasks), Dyn-VPP reaches 0.39 success at task 10 with an average length of 6.73, compared to VPP's 0.28 and 5.53, and π0's 0.11 and 3.61. The method also improves vision–action coupling, raising the average effective rank of the action Jacobian from 29.28 to 43.88 and the ER ratio from 0.0603 to 0.0814.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Dyn-VPP outperforms all 12 baselines on CALVIN ABC→D across every task horizon. Against the closest latent-level VPM-based competitors, it improves over Tri-VLA (81.8% → 83.1% at task 5; len 4.37 → 4.56) and Seer_Large (74.0% → 83.1%; len 4.28 → 4.56), and over its own base VPP by 8.4 points at task 5. Among VLM-based VLAs, the gap is even larger: Dyn-VPP surpasses OpenVLA-OFT (66.5% → 83.1% at task 5) and π0.5 (61.3% → 83.1%). On L-CALVIN (10-task sequences), Dyn-VPP extends the lead further, reaching 0.39 at task 10 versus VPP's 0.28, π0's 0.11, and OpenVLA's 0.00, with an average length of 6.73 versus 5.53, 3.61, and 1.43 respectively. Notably, Dyn-VPP achieves these gains purely through post-training (GRPO with a latent-consistency reward) without any architectural modification, whereas Tri-VLA requires additional network components and Seer_Large relies on a larger model scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

Dyn-VPP builds directly on the Video Prediction Policy (VPP) framework (Hu et al., 2025a) as its base architecture, and adapts the MDP formulation for denoising from DDPO (Black et al., 2023) to the video-prediction setting. The key conceptual departure is identifying an 'objective mismatch' in diffusion-based VPMs: likelihood-surrogate training (ELBO-style) optimizes global plausibility but does not guarantee the precision-critical visual dynamics that downstream manipulation policies require. To resolve this, the paper introduces a post-training stage that recasts multi-step denoising as policy optimization using GRPO (Xue et al., 2025) with a verifiable latent-consistency reward, and designs an Euler Hybrid sampler (SDE at the first step only, ODE thereafter) that borrows the stochastic transition from EDM's Euler-Ancestral sampler (Karras et al., 2022) while avoiding the credit-assignment problems of full-trajectory stochasticity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap that limits deployment of VPM-based VLAs: small prediction errors in visual dynamics are amplified by downstream policies near decision boundaries, causing compounding failures over long horizons. Because Dyn-VPP is a post-training procedure requiring no architectural changes, it can be retrofitted onto existing video-action models, lowering the barrier for practitioners. The effective-rank analysis provides a mechanistic explanation for why improved dynamics help (richer vision-action coupling), which is valuable beyond this specific method. Open questions include whether the hand-designed latent-consistency reward generalizes to tasks without clean ground-truth future clips, whether the approach scales to higher-dimensional action spaces or multi-robot settings, and whether the reward could be learned end-to-end rather than specified a priori.
