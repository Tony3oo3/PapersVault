## Problem

Deep Research (DR) agents face an autonomy-interaction dilemma: their long-horizon execution (30+ minutes of browsing and synthesis) makes misinterpreting an ambiguous user query extremely costly, yet existing closed-source clarification modules fail to elicit sufficient latent intent and open-source user-intent-driven DR agents remain largely unexplored. The core challenge is that DR is inherently open-ended with no enumerable state space, making it impossible to define a 'complete' information set, while high-quality data reflecting real-world open-ended user intent is extremely scarce.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a deliberately fuzzified (simplified) user query that strips explicit constraints, modeling how real users under-specify their needs. It then engages in up to T=9 turns of dialogue, outputting exactly one clarification question per turn with 2–3 selectable options. After the dialogue concludes, a separate summary agent (Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct) synthesizes the full interaction history into a single refined, fine-grained research query that is passed to a downstream DR agent. The training data is constructed by fuzzifying 50 DeepResearch Bench queries, extracting shallow intents (removed constraints) and deep intents (rubric-derived preference gaps), and traversing a Clarification DAG to generate ~371 trajectories and 2,347 dialogue turns.

## Method

IntentRL formulates intent clarification as a POMDP where the user's complete latent intent is the hidden state, and trains a Qwen2.5-7B policy via a two-stage GRPO pipeline. Stage I performs offline RL on expert trajectories generated by C-DAG traversal, using hindsight-driven bootstrapping to convert long-horizon learning into turn-level optimization: the target intent set at each turn is grounded in the C-DAG traversal stack state, and the reward combines an embedding-similarity content score against target intents, a format score penalizing multi-question outputs, and a penalty term for repetition and deviation. Stage II addresses offline distribution shift by running on-policy rollouts against an intent-aware user simulator (hybrid rule-based embedding thresholds for redundancy/relevance detection plus LLM-judge response generation), mixing simulated trajectories with Stage-I data and adding penalties that suppress repetitive and off-topic behavior. The C-DAG construction pipeline is the key data-scaling mechanism: it converts static rubrics into a directed acyclic graph of clarification questions with logical dependencies, then branches traversal at each multi-option node to expand a single benchmark task into many intent-aligned trajectories.

## Results

On DeepResearch Bench, IntentRL achieves an Intent Precision of 36.44% and Intent Recall of 27.49% in online interaction, substantially outperforming the best proactive LLM baseline Learn-to-Ask (21.11% / 18.45%) and the built-in clarification modules of Qwen-DR (9.25% / 6.03%) and OpenAI-DR (11.32% / 6.96%). In downstream report generation, IntentRL improves the overall score by +4.26 points over no-clarification on Qwen DR (43.65 vs. 39.39), +5.86 on Gemini DR (45.27 vs. 39.41), and +8.24 on OpenAI DR (44.86 vs. 36.62), consistently ranking first across all four DR agents and three benchmarks (DeepResearch Bench, Rigorous Bench, PDR-Bench).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IntentRL outperforms all five baselines on every clarification metric. Against the built-in modules of closed-source DR agents, the gap is largest: Intent Precision is 36.44% vs. 9.25% (Qwen-DR) and 11.32% (OpenAI-DR), a 3–4× improvement, indicating that proprietary clarification prompts fail to elicit meaningful latent intent. Among trained proactive LLM baselines, Learn-to-Ask is the strongest competitor (Intent Precision 21.11%, Recall 18.45%), yet IntentRL still leads by +15.33 and +9.04 points respectively, suggesting that open-ended intent clarification requires both intent-rich C-DAG supervision and on-policy RL refinement rather than prompt-level or domain-restricted training alone. CollabLLM, which uses online RL with multi-turn-aware rewards, achieves 18.00% / 12.68% and is the second-best baseline, but still trails IntentRL by 18.44 / 14.81 points. Tell Me More performs worst among trained baselines (5.19% / 5.62%), barely exceeding the built-in modules. In downstream report generation (Table 2), IntentRL is the top performer across all four DR agents (Qwen, Gemini, OpenAI, Tavily) and all three benchmarks, with the largest gains on Comprehensiveness, Insight/Depth, and Instruction-Following rather than Readability, confirming that clarification primarily enriches content relevance and task adherence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the proactive LLM literature (Ask-when-Needed, CollabLLM, UserRL, Tell Me More, Learn-to-Ask) from closed, enumerable domains (medical diagnosis, tool use) to open-ended deep research, where the information set is unbounded and no complete intent taxonomy exists. It departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) a C-DAG-based data construction pipeline that scales 50 seed samples into thousands of clarification trajectories by exploiting rubric-derived deep intents, addressing the data scarcity that plagues open-ended tasks; and (2) a two-stage GRPO training scheme combining offline hindsight-driven bootstrapping with online rollouts against an intent-aware user simulator, building on the offline-to-online RL paradigm from Wei et al. (2025) but adapting it to the POMDP structure of multi-turn intent elicitation. The built-in clarification modules of closed-source DR agents (Qwen DR, OpenAI DR) serve as practical baselines but are shown to be substantially weaker, motivating the need for explicitly trained proactive agents.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The autonomy-interaction dilemma is a genuine practical bottleneck for DR agents: a 30-minute research run on a misaligned query wastes significant compute, and the paper demonstrates that a few clarification turns (negligible cost) can substantially improve report quality across four different DR backends. The observation that gains scale with DR agent capability suggests proactive clarification becomes more valuable as underlying models improve, positioning this as a complementary rather than competing capability. Key limitations include reliance on rubrics as intent seeds (though the authors argue this is practical since benchmarks provide them), and the user simulator's inability to capture real-world ambiguity, inconsistency, and preference shifts. Open questions include sim-to-real transfer, stochastic intent modeling, and whether the C-DAG construction generalizes to domains without structured rubrics.
