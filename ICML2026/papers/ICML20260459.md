## Problem

Existing ICA frameworks assume real-valued observations corrupted by additive noise, making them ill-suited for temporal count data that are discrete, non-negative, overdispersed, and heteroskedastic. While Poisson log-normal (PLN) models capture count-specific statistics, their potential for interpretable representation learning and independent component analysis in temporal settings remains largely unexplored, and existing temporal count models are predominantly forecasting-oriented without identifiability guarantees.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260459/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a multivariate temporal count matrix x ∈ ℕ^{T×K} (T time steps, K features) and an optional known sampling-effort offset o_t (e.g., library size or sequencing depth) as input. It produces d latent independent source trajectories, a K×d mixing matrix Γ mapping sources to Poisson log-intensities, C discrete regime labels per component, and optionally one-step-ahead count forecasts. The mixing matrix is identifiable only up to column-wise signed permutation, so component interpretations rely on relative loading magnitudes rather than absolute orientations.

## Method

ARPLN-ICA couples a Poisson log-normal emission model with component-wise switching linear Gaussian dynamics: each of d latent sources follows a regime-dependent AR(1) process governed by its own discrete Markov chain over C regimes, and sources are linearly mixed via Γ into log-intensities that drive independent Poisson emissions. Identifiability is established by showing (Proposition 3.1) that the PLN emission reduces the problem to noiseless ICA on log-intensities, and (Corollary 3.2) that the mixing matrix is recoverable up to permutation and rescaling under mild non-degeneracy conditions on lagged covariances. Parameter learning uses a stochastic variational EM procedure: a GRU encoder amortizes the variational posterior over sources as a linear Gaussian Markov chain, discrete switching states are updated via closed-form CAVI exploiting conjugacy, the mixing matrix is optimized by SGD with column-norm constraints to fix scaling indeterminacy, and AR hyperparameters admit closed-form updates.

## Results

In the simulation study, ARPLN-ICA with structured auto-regressive variational inference achieves 74.6% and 84.5% average cosine similarity for mixing-matrix recovery under moderate and high column-coherence settings, respectively, substantially outperforming the linear ICA baselines UWEDGEICA (46% average) and PICARD (44% average) across all three scenarios. On the gnotobiotic mice forecasting benchmark, ARPLN-ICA is competitive with dedicated forecasting models, achieving the best Poisson deviance alongside P-rSLDS, while the C=2 regime-switching variant slightly outperforms its time-homogeneous ablation on average. On the NOAA Storm Events dataset, inferred switching states align with spring–autumn seasonal transitions and the four ICA components recover interpretable meteorological contrasts (convective vs. moisture events, heat vs. winter hazards, compound warm-season weather, and synoptic winter vs. convective heat).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ARPLN-ICA clearly outperforms both linear ICA baselines on mixing recovery: structured AR variational inference reaches 74.6–84.5% cosine similarity versus 46% (UWEDGEICA) and 44% (PICARD) averaged across scenarios, indicating that fitting linear ICA on log-counts is sensitive to the observation-model mismatch introduced by heteroskedastic Poisson noise. On the microbiome forecasting task, ARPLN-ICA matches P-rSLDS on Poisson deviance (the metric most aligned with the discrete count likelihood) and is competitive with CLR-VAR(1), UWEDGEICA+CLR-VAR(1), PICARD+CLR-VAR(1), and the mechanistic gLV-L2 model on MAE and Aitchison distance, though it does not uniformly dominate all baselines on every metric. The regime-switching variant (C=2) provides small but consistent gains over the time-homogeneous ablation (h)-ARPLN-ICA, confirming the value of the switching dynamics. Overall, the paper positions ARPLN-ICA as an interpretable representation-learning tool that is competitive with forecasting-oriented models rather than a forecasting method per se, with its primary advantage being principled identifiability and component-level interpretability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the structured ICA identifiability framework of Hälvä et al. (2024) to count data by replacing the additive-noise emission with a Poisson log-normal model, so that identifiability of the mixing matrix reduces to the noiseless ICA problem on log-intensities (Proposition 3.1). The temporal dynamics are modeled via a switching linear Gaussian system (Ghahramani & Hinton, 2000), and the identifiability proof for the regime-switching case leverages the second-order whitening argument of Belouchrani et al. (2002), relaxed by the component-wise independence of the sources. The variational inference procedure builds on the PLN ELBO of Chiquet et al. (2019) extended with temporal Markov recursions, and uses a GRU-based amortized parameterization similar to Chaussard et al. (2025a). The key departure from prior ICA work is that observation noise is modeled through Poisson sampling rather than additive Gaussian noise, which the authors show preserves identifiability while being statistically appropriate for counts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first ICA framework for temporal count data with formal identifiability guarantees, filling a gap between count-specific generative models (PLN, negative binomial) and the ICA literature that assumes real-valued additive-noise observations. The practical demonstrations on microbiome and climate data show that the inferred components and regimes align with domain knowledge, supporting interpretability-driven analysis in settings where forecasting-oriented count models offer limited mechanistic insight. Key limitations include the restriction to linear mixing (nonlinear extensions are left as future work), the reliance on amortized variational inference which introduces an objective gap, and the small sample sizes in both real-world applications (n=5 mice, n=25 years). Extending the framework to handle missing data common in clinical cohorts and establishing generalization bounds for the variational procedure are identified open directions.
