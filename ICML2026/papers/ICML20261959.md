## Problem

Deploying generative world models as online closed-loop driving simulators exposes three gaps that open-loop metrics miss: history-free initialization causes cold-start jerk spikes in reactive policies, multi-step diffusion solvers exceed real-time latency budgets for streaming outpainting, and minor per-step kinematic violations compound into infeasible behavior over kilometer-scale rollouts. No prior vectorized scene generator addresses all three simultaneously while maintaining structural topology fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261959/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes ego-centric 64 m × 64 m scene tiles as heterogeneous vector graphs (lane polylines with 20 resampled points, agent 7-D static states, and K_mot-point body-frame motion-history polylines) plus scene-level context (counts, optional labels). During streaming outpainting, observed tokens inside the current tile are clamped as conditions while the generator completes only frontier lane and agent tokens; the motion code is SE(2)-invariant so it survives tile stitching without re-encoding. Output is a decoded vector-graph tile (lanes + agents with typed connectivity edges) and, for NPCs, a single-pass hybrid action: a discrete k-disks SE(2) token refined by a continuous 3-D residual, shaped at inference by differentiable kinematic costs.

## Method

VectorWorld couples three modules targeting the three deployment constraints. (1) A motion-aware gated VAE encodes each agent through a learned sigmoid gate that adaptively blends a static-state branch and a motion-history branch, producing Gaussian latents that warm-start history-conditioned policies without injecting noise for stationary agents. (2) An edge-gated relational DiT with factorized typed attention (L2L, A2A, L2A, A2L) applies per-edge additive logit biases and multiplicative value gates to preserve lane connectivity and agent-lane alignment under repeated masked completion; it is trained with interval-conditioned MeanFlow (predicting an interval-averaged velocity u_θ(z_t; t, Δ, c)) augmented by a JVP-based first-order correction V_θ = u_θ + Δ·(d/dt|_Δ)u_θ, which calibrates the predictor at the one-step boundary (t,Δ)=(1,1) and enables solver-free single-update sampling at ≈5.6 ms per candidate. (3) ΔSim, a one-pass return-conditioned transformer NPC policy, uses FiLM modulation from a soft RTG embedding, a hybrid discrete-continuous action head (384-token k-disks vocabulary + continuous residual), and a differentiable kinematic alignment loss (DKAL) that centers logits against kinematic costs to suppress infeasible actions, reducing lateral-acceleration violations from 14.2% to 2.1% over 1 km+ rollouts.

## Results

On nuPlan, VectorWorld reduces endpoint distance (EPD) from 0.250 m (ScenDream-L) to 0.078 m and initialization collision rate from 9.30% to 3.01%, while achieving the longest route length (37.22 m) among non-privileged generators. On Waymo, VectorWorld attains the lowest Fréchet distance (FD 0.94) and best EPD (0.094 m) among all non-privileged baselines, with a collision rate of 4.69%. In closed-loop 1 km rollouts, the full VectorWorld pipeline (online outpainting + warm-start) achieves 78.0% success rate with 9.6 jerk, compared to 42.0% success and 16.6 jerk without warm-start, and 81.7% success at only 200 m for ScenDream-L + Ctrl-Sim.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SLEDGE (Chitta et al., 2024), VectorWorld dominates on all structural and safety metrics: on nuPlan, EPD drops from 0.470 m (SLEDGE-L) / 0.420 m (SLEDGE-XL) to 0.078 m, and collision rate falls from 22.30% / 21.20% to 3.01%. Against ScenDream (Rowe et al., 2025), VectorWorld wins decisively on EPD (0.078 vs 0.250 m on nuPlan; 0.094 vs 0.210 m on Waymo) and collision rate (3.01% vs 9.30% on nuPlan), and achieves the best FD on Waymo (0.94 vs 1.38), though ScenDream-L retains a slight FD edge on nuPlan (0.67 vs 0.98). DriveSceneGen(GT) (Sun et al., 2024) is a privileged reference using ground-truth inputs and is not directly comparable. For the NPC behavior model, ΔSim outperforms Ctrl-Sim (Rowe et al., 2024) on ADE (1.72 m vs 2.80 m), controllability correlation (ρ = 0.53 vs −0.25), and inference cost (1 forward pass at 26.1 ms vs 2 passes at 36.9 ms), while reducing lateral-acceleration violations from 14.2% to 2.1%.

## Relation to Prior Work

VectorWorld extends the MeanFlow one-step generative framework (Geng et al., 2025) to heterogeneous vector-graph latents by adding a JVP-based large-step correction that calibrates the interval-velocity predictor at the (t,Δ)=(1,1) boundary, and embeds it within an edge-gated relational DiT backbone (Peebles & Xie, 2023) that replaces node-only attention with typed-edge bias and value gating for lane/agent topology. It departs from prior vectorized scene generators (SLEDGE, ScenDream) by treating streaming outpainting as a first-class deployment constraint rather than an offline generation task, and from prior NPC simulators (CtRL-Sim) by coupling a one-pass return-conditioned policy with differentiable kinematic logit shaping to prevent compounding infeasibility over kilometer-scale rollouts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that solver-free one-step structured graph completion is practical for real-time closed-loop driving simulation (≈5.6 ms per candidate tile, ≈44.8 ms per 8-candidate trigger), a regime previously inaccessible to multi-step diffusion or ODE solvers. The JVP correction's effect on validity (9.3% → 87.5%) suggests that interval-velocity calibration at the one-step boundary is a non-trivial requirement for structured generation that prior flow-matching work has not addressed. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to denser urban scenes with more complex lane topologies, whether the simplified kinematic cost in ΔSim suffices for pedestrian/cyclist interactions, and whether the one-step quality ceiling is adequate for safety-critical stress testing beyond the 1 km horizon demonstrated here.
