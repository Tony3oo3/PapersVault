## Problem

While susceptibility analysis was introduced for a 3M-parameter attention-only transformer, it remained unclear whether this approach could reveal rich, interpretable structure in larger models or whether a principled connection exists between observed susceptibility clusters and patterns in the data distribution. This paper asks: what is the 'spectrum' of a language model, and can it be systematically extracted to identify the hundreds of patterns a model has internalized?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261317/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 780,000 context-continuation pairs (x, y) sampled from 13 Pile subsets (60k each) and the trained Pythia-14M checkpoint (32 attention heads as components). For each pair, a susceptibility vector χ_xy ∈ R^32 is estimated via preconditioned SGLD sampling from a localized Gibbs posterior centered at the trained weights, with hyperparameters γ=300, nβ=3, ε=1e-5, 4 chains × 100 draws. The output is 510 interpretable clusters of token sequences grouped by shared functional/syntactic role, along with conductance measurements and UMAP projections for visualization.

## Method

The core pipeline has two stages. First, per-token susceptibilities are computed as covariances between a component's loss-variation observable and the per-token loss, estimated from pSGLD posterior samples; aggregating across all H components yields a response vector χ_xy that captures how the model's internal structure adjusts when the data distribution is perturbed to upweight (x,y). Second, an iterative conductance-based clustering algorithm operates on a symmetrized k-NN graph (k=45) with self-tuning RBF edge weights: personalized PageRank from random seeds identifies candidate regions, and minimum-conductance prefix search extracts well-separated clusters, with the main body progressively removed so that peripheral structures emerge. Theoretically, the paper proves a mode decomposition χ_xy = Σ s_αβ(xy)χ_αβ − χ̄, showing that token pairs cluster because they share propensities on the same SVD modes of the conditional distribution q(y|x), and the model has developed strong diagonal responses ‖χ_αα‖ ≫ 0 to those modes.

## Results

The conductance-based clustering algorithm identifies 510 interpretable clusters in the susceptibility space of Pythia-14M, computed over 780,000 token-context pairs from 13 Pile subsets. These clusters span grammatical patterns (sentence starters, prepositions, enclitics), mathematical notation (exponents, variable names, True/False answers), and code/markup syntax (HTML tags, Python indentation, LaTeX commands). Critically, the clusters exhibit bidirectional abstraction: the same token (e.g., `*`) appears in different clusters depending on syntactic role (multiplication vs. markdown), while different tokens (h, u, y, o, w) group together when serving the same function (multiplication operands). When the same 510 clusters are evaluated on susceptibility graphs from larger Pythia models (31M–1.4B), they maintain conductance significantly below 1, confirming the patterns persist across scale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against sparse autoencoder (SAE) features from Lan et al. (2025) trained on Pythia-70M residual stream activations (layers 2–4, 32,768 latent dimensions). Using a binarized activation metric (feature must exceed the 90th percentile of its own window activations at the target token position, with activation frequency ≥ 80% across the cluster), 259 of 510 susceptibility clusters (50.8%) have a matched SAE feature, with almost all baseline activation frequencies below 5% on random clusters, confirming specificity. The authors caution that this is not a one-to-one correspondence: SAE features are sparse but not singleton (multiple features can fire per input), while each susceptibility cluster assigns a token pair to a single group, so a token pair explained by multiple patterns may match only one SAE feature. A secondary internal baseline compares the SGLD-based susceptibilities against a Gaussian posterior (nβ=0): the Gaussian version yields only 288 clusters (vs. 510), with clusters 72% smaller on average (567 vs. 976 tokens) and 31% lower token entropy (0.94 vs. 1.23), and fails to separate opening vs. closing HTML tag tokens, validating that the loss-landscape-shaped posterior is essential for resolving fine-grained structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the susceptibility framework of Baker et al. (2025), which identified a handful of patterns in a 3M-parameter attention-only transformer, to the full Pythia-14M architecture, scaling from a handful of patterns to 510 interpretable clusters. The theoretical contribution builds on the mode decomposition of Chen & Murfet (2025), providing a principled SVD-based explanation for why tokens following similar data patterns cluster together in susceptibility space. Methodologically, it adapts the PageRank-based local clustering of Andersen et al. (2009) into an iterative conductance-based algorithm with self-tuning k-NN graphs (Zelnik-Manor & Perona, 2005), and validates findings against sparse autoencoder features from Lan et al. (2025). The paper positions itself as 'spectroscopy' for language models, framing susceptibility vectors as the spectral response of a model to data-distribution perturbations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The mode decomposition provides a theoretical foundation for why susceptibility clustering should reveal meaningful structure, moving the field beyond purely empirical feature discovery toward a principled connection between data regularities and internal model organization. The 50% SAE match rate offers cross-method validation, though the authors rightly caution this does not establish one-to-one correspondence. Key limitations include restriction to the Pythia family and Pile dataset, dependence on clustering hyperparameters, and the fact that susceptibility analysis has not yet been demonstrated at frontier scale. Open questions include whether the mode decomposition can be computed directly (it requires the full conditional distribution q(y|x)), whether dictionary learning on susceptibility vectors can recover more structure in larger models, and whether the susceptibility-to-intervention inversion (Wang & Murfet, 2026) can enable targeted model editing.
