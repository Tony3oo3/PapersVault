## Problem

Organizations with limited resources cannot afford to fine-tune LLMs on private data, and existing alternatives—fine-tuning TinyLMs locally or sharing data with cloud providers—either underperform or violate privacy constraints. Prior data-free knowledge distillation methods are computationally expensive and risk leaking sensitive information through synthetic data, while weak-to-strong distillation methods require data sharing between models. This paper introduces the first data-free weak-to-strong knowledge distillation framework that directly generates LLM update vectors from TinyLM update vectors without ever accessing the client's private data.

## Input / Output

The method takes block-wise update vectors from a fine-tuned TinyLM (specifically LoRA rank-2 adapters for query and key parameters of all attention layers), where each attention block's Q, K, V, and linear projection weight changes are concatenated into a single vector serving as a 'token.' In multi-client settings, per-client update vectors are first aggregated (e.g., averaged) before processing. The output is the corresponding sequence of block-wise LLM update vectors, which are added to the initial LLM parameters to produce the updated model. The segmentation preserves the natural layer ordering, which ablations confirm is critical for the autoregressive decoder to capture inter-block correlations.

## Method

GRAD-TRANSFORMER uses a Flan-T5-Large encoder-decoder that treats block-wise update vectors as sequence tokens: TinyLM block updates are projected into hidden states via an embedding layer, encoded, and then autoregressively decoded to predict LLM block updates, which are projected back through an output layer. Training minimizes MSE between predicted and ground-truth LLM update vectors on tuples curated from shadow datasets—public data subsets on which both the TinyLM and LLM are fine-tuned from identical initial parameters, yielding low-variance update pairs. The theoretical analysis provides information-theoretic generalization and utility bounds showing performance depends on mutual information between the shadow data and model parameters, plus the KL divergence between shadow and private data distributions. The framework supports differential privacy by operating on DP-SGD-noised TinyLM update vectors, and scales across model sizes from 0.5B TinyLM to 14B LLM (28× scale gap).

## Results

In the single-client setting across six datasets, GRAD-TRANSFORMER achieves an average PGR (Performance Gap Recovered) of 91.88%, compared to 58.94% for the best baseline (Conf), a 55.89% relative improvement. On AQuA-RAT, it reaches 61.02% accuracy (PGR 123.07%), surpassing the TinyLM baseline (48.43%) and approaching the LLM ceiling (58.66%). In the multi-client setting (5 clients), GRAD-TRANSFORMER achieves an average PGR of 85.01% versus 69.97% for the best baseline. Under strict differential privacy (ε=0.1, δ=10⁻⁵), GRAD-TRANSFORMER maintains 62.35% accuracy on DROP while the TinyLM drops from 55.59% to 44.70%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three weak-to-strong distillation baselines: W2S and Conf (both from Burns et al., 2024) and VisSup (Guo et al., 2024). GRAD-TRANSFORMER decisively outperforms all three on AQuA-RAT (61.02% vs. 47.64% for Conf), DROP (58.26% vs. 54.18%), SAMSum (50.52 vs. 49.92 R-1), and DialogSum (48.37 vs. 47.70 R-1). On GSM8K, Conf achieves a marginally higher raw accuracy (74.30% vs. 73.59%), and on CommonsenseQA, Conf and VisSup edge out GRAD-TRANSFORMER by 0.25 points (83.46% vs. 83.21%). However, the critical distinction is that all three baselines require direct access to the client's private data (they fine-tune the LLM on data labeled by the TinyLM), whereas GRAD-TRANSFORMER operates solely on TinyLM update vectors without ever seeing the private data. In the multi-client setting, GRAD-TRANSFORMER's average PGR of 85.01% substantially exceeds Conf's 69.97%, and it is the only method that does not require data sharing, making it the only privacy-preserving option among those compared.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of weak-to-strong knowledge distillation (Burns et al., 2024; Shin et al., 2025) and data-free knowledge distillation (Lopes et al., 2017; Wei et al., 2025), but departs from both: prior weak-to-strong methods require data sharing between teacher and student, while data-free methods generate synthetic samples that risk leaking private information. GRAD-TRANSFORMER instead operates directly on LoRA update vectors (Hu et al., 2022), using a Flan-T5-Large encoder-decoder to learn the mapping from TinyLM parameter deltas to LLM parameter deltas, trained on shadow datasets. This is framed as the first data-free weak-to-strong distillation that bypasses both data sharing and synthetic data generation entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a concrete and growing pain point: organizations with private data cannot afford LLM fine-tuning and cannot share data, yet TinyLMs alone underperform. The robustness under strict DP-SGD (ε=0.1) where the TinyLM degrades sharply but the generated LLM update remains strong is practically significant for regulated domains. Key limitations include dependence on KL divergence between public shadow data and private data distributions (limiting applicability to niche domains), lack of support for sparse architectures like Mixture-of-Experts, and evaluation restricted to the Qwen2.5 family. Open questions include whether the approach scales to 32B+ LLMs, how it behaves under significant distribution shift, and whether the block-wise segmentation generalizes to non-attention architectures.
