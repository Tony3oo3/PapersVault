## Problem

Monocular multi-person human mesh recovery is inherently ill-posed because distinct 3D poses at different depths can yield nearly identical 2D projections, and existing one-stage methods implicitly regress depth from shared image features without explicitly constraining the solution space. This depth ambiguity causes the model to place individuals at incorrect depths and compensate by distorting pose or scale, leading to unstable depth ordering and spatial layout in crowded scenes. The paper addresses the underexplored gap of explicitly modeling instance-level depth cues to stabilize joint 2D-3D optimization in multi-person settings.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (trained at 672 or 896 resolution) and outputs per-person SMPL parameters (pose θ∈ℝ^{24×3}, shape β∈ℝ^{10}), per-person pelvis depth T_z in meters, and instance-level bounding boxes. Projection uses a preset focal length derived from a fixed 60° field of view, and the 3D mesh is represented in a root-relative coordinate system where the pelvis defines the origin, with T_z encoding the absolute camera-to-pelvis distance. The dual-stream design consumes both DINOv2 image features and Depth Anything V2 depth features, so the input is effectively a single RGB image processed through two parallel encoders.

## Method

DGG-HMR adopts a dual-stream architecture that decouples instance-level depth estimation from mesh recovery. The depth stream uses a DETR-style per-person depth decoder that takes bounding-box tokens (from a lightweight DETR-style box decoder on DINOv2 features) as queries and cross-attends with Depth Anything V2 features under fixed bounding-box anchors to predict per-person pelvis depth. The HMR stream employs a geometry-anchored refinement decoder that injects instance-level geometric priors (bounding boxes and depths) directly into decoder queries and reference points via zero-initialized residual additions, anchoring each instance's optimization within a locally consistent 3D neighborhood. Training is single-stage end-to-end with gradient stopping from the mesh branch to the depth head, preventing non-convex mesh optimization from corrupting depth estimates. The depth loss uses a weighted L1 formulation (1/T_z^GT · |T_z − T_z^GT|) that emphasizes closer subjects while avoiding the gradient instability of inverse-depth losses.

## Results

On 3DPW, DGG-HMR (896 res.) achieves PA-MPJPE of 39.0 mm, MPJPE of 61.1 mm, and MVE of 70.9 mm, outperforming the previous best SAT-HMR (41.6 / 63.6 / 73.7). On CMU Panoptic, it reduces the average MPJPE to 79.2 mm (vs. 84.2 for SAT-HMR), and on MuPoTS-3D it reaches PCK-Matched of 90.1%. On AGORA, DGG-HMR attains F1 of 0.95, Precision of 0.99, and Recall of 0.92, while also achieving the best depth-ordering accuracy (PCDR^0.2) across all depth intervals (up to 96.01% in the 2–5 m range).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DGG-HMR outperforms the strongest prior method SAT-HMR on 3DPW by 2.6 mm in PA-MPJPE (39.0 vs. 41.6), 2.5 mm in MPJPE (61.1 vs. 63.6), and 2.8 mm in MVE (70.9 vs. 73.7), and on CMU Panoptic by 5.0 mm in average MPJPE (79.2 vs. 84.2). However, DGG-HMR trails SAT-HMR on MuPoTS-3D PCK-All (86.2 vs. 89.0) while tying on PCK-Matched (90.1). Against Multi-HMR, DGG-HMR improves 3DPW PA-MPJPE by 7.7 mm and MuPoTS PCK-All by 6.8 points. On AGORA (Table 2), DGG-HMR matches SAT-HMR on F1 (0.95) and edges ahead on Precision (0.99 vs. 0.98) and Recall (0.92 vs. 0.91), but SAT-HMR retains a slight advantage in NMJE (71.5 vs. 72.6) and NMVE (66.6 vs. 68.4). In depth-ordering evaluation (Table 3), DGG-HMR surpasses SAT-HMR on PCDR^0.2 in every depth interval (e.g., 96.01% vs. 95.23% at 2–5 m) and on ADE in the 0–2 m and 10 m+ ranges, confirming the benefit of explicit instance-level depth estimation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DETR-style one-stage multi-person HMR line (Multi-HMR, AiOS, SAT-HMR) by introducing an explicit instance-level depth estimation branch that feeds geometric anchors into the mesh refinement decoder. It builds directly on DETR's query-based decoder architecture for both the depth decoder and the geometry-anchored refinement decoder, and adopts the weighted depth loss formulation from BLADE. The key departure from prior one-stage methods is decoupling depth estimation from mesh regression and injecting per-person pelvis depth as a spatial initialization for the decoder queries, rather than letting depth emerge implicitly from joint 2D-3D optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first explicit treatment of instance-level depth as a geometric prior in monocular multi-person HMR, offering a principled solution to the depth ambiguity that has limited spatial layout accuracy in crowded scenes. The finding that feature-level fusion of geometric priors is insufficient and that query-level injection is necessary for stable 2D-3D optimization is a useful design insight for future DETR-style HMR methods. Limitations include body-only (not whole-body) estimation, reliance on a frozen Depth Anything V2 backbone, and evaluation primarily on synthetic-heavy training mixes; extending the anchoring strategy to whole-body models and video settings remains open.
