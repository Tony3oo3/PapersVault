## Problem

Existing benchmarks for object-centric learning (OCL) suffer from two critical gaps: they rely heavily on unsupervised object discovery as a proxy for representation quality, which correlates poorly with downstream reasoning utility, and they evaluate localization and representation usefulness with disjoint metrics that ignore fragmentation failure modes (e.g., a model can answer correctly using the wrong slot, or localize well but encode semantics across multiple slots).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264175/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The evaluation framework consumes RGB images (resized to 518×518 for DINOv2 encoding), text questions, and grounding segmentation masks (generated via SAM2 from GQA bounding boxes in the eGQA dataset of 10,000 filtered samples). The slot-attention encoder produces 7 visual tokens of 768 dimensions (versus 196 tokens for DINOv2), which are projected through a 2-layer MLP connector into the LLM's embedding space. Outputs include free-form VQA text answers (greedy decoding) and the AwGA score, which multiplies answer correctness by the mIoU between the union of top-K attributed slot masks and the ground-truth grounding mask.

## Method

The paper introduces a VLM-based evaluation protocol that replaces the standard vision encoder in a LLaVA-style instruction-tuned model with a slot-attention OCL encoder, trained in two stages (connector-only pre-training on LLaVA 558K, then joint LLM+connector fine-tuning on LLaVA 665K), enabling zero-shot evaluation across diverse VQA benchmarks without per-task retraining. To address disjoint evaluation, the authors propose Attribution-aware Grounded Accuracy (AwGA), which computes gradient-based attribution of each slot to the predicted answer, selects the K slots with highest attribution (where K equals the number of grounded objects), and scores the mIoU of their union mask against the GT grounding mask, jointly penalizing both localization and representation fragmentation. As a reference baseline, mFRESA extends StableLSD by adding a DINOv2 feature decoder and a HOG feature decoder alongside the original Stable Diffusion image decoder, jointly reconstructing pixels, self-supervised features, and edge information to produce stronger object-centric slots.

## Results

On standard VQA benchmarks (Table 2), mFRESA achieves 53.90% GQA, 82.12% POPE, 1187.05 MME, and 65.58% VQAv2 with Phi-2, outperforming all other slot-attention baselines and approaching DINOv2 (57.77% GQA, 71.15% VQAv2) despite using only 7 visual tokens vs. 196. On the proposed AwGA metric (Table 5), mFRESA scores 13.91% (Phi-2) and 15.88% (Qwen2-7B), the highest among all OCL methods, while the best prior method (SPOT) reaches 12.81% and 15.14% respectively. On robustness benchmarks (Table 3), mFRESA leads OCL methods on OOD-CV (55.57%), SugarCrepe (77.27%), and NaturalBench (4.21%), though DINOv2 remains stronger on compositional and adversarial tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

mFRESA outperforms all seven OCL baselines on most VQA benchmarks. Against DINOv2 (the self-supervised upper bound), mFRESA closes the gap substantially—e.g., 53.90% vs. 57.77% on GQA and 65.58% vs. 71.15% on VQAv2 with Phi-2—while using only 7 tokens instead of 196. Notably, FT-DINOSAUR, the top OCL model for unsupervised object discovery (mIoU 34.52, mBO 36.08 on COCO), underperforms DINOSAURv2 and mFRESA on nearly all VQA and robustness benchmarks, confirming that object discovery scores are a poor proxy for downstream utility. On the proposed AwGA metric, mFRESA (13.91% Phi-2, 15.88% Qwen2) beats SPOT (12.81%, 15.14%) and FT-DINOSAUR (13.25%, 15.36%), the next-best OCL methods. On robustness tasks, mFRESA leads OCL methods on OOD-CV (55.57% vs. 55.18% FT-DINOSAUR) and SugarCrepe (77.27% vs. 75.98% DINOSAURv2), but all OCL methods still trail DINOv2 on NaturalBench (4.21% vs. 8.42%) and SugarCrepe (77.27% vs. 82.05%). DINOv3 (Siméoni et al., 2026) improves modestly over DINOv2 on some benchmarks (e.g., OOD-CV 62.13% vs. 58.00%) but the gap to mFRESA remains similar, indicating the SSL upper bound shifts only slightly.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the slot-attention-based OCL lineage (DINOSAUR, StableLSD, SPOT, SlotDiffusion) by proposing mFRESA, a simple multi-feature reconstruction baseline that builds directly on StableLSD's diffusion decoder and adds DINOv2 feature and HOG decoders. For evaluation, it departs from the standard object-discovery and linear-probing protocols by adopting the LLaVA instruction-tuning architecture to create a VLM-based evaluator that uses OCL slots as visual tokens, and introduces the AwGA metric to jointly assess localization and representation usefulness—addressing fragmentation issues that prior disjoint metrics (accuracy, mIoU, G-Acc) overlook.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that object discovery metrics (mIoU, mBO) show a negative Spearman correlation (−0.2) with VQA accuracy challenges a core assumption in the OCL community that better segmentation implies better representations, and the fact that FT-DINOSAUR—the top object-discovery model—underperforms older DINOSAURv2 on most reasoning benchmarks underscores the need for evaluation beyond UOD. The VLM-based evaluation framework and AwGA metric offer a practical, amortized-cost path to benchmarking OCL models on diverse reasoning tasks, though the protocol's reliance on expensive VLM training and AwGA's current limitation to the eGQA dataset (10K samples) constrain immediate adoption. Open questions include whether conclusions generalize to larger grounded VQA datasets, adaptive slot-count methods, and whether the very low absolute AwGA scores (11–14%) reflect a fundamental gap in current OCL grounding or a limitation of the metric itself.
