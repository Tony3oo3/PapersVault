## Problem

Large-scale T2I diffusion models (e.g., FLUX, Qwen-Image, SD3) still produce typos, missing strokes, and 'semantic leakage' (rendering the concept instead of the word), especially for multiline layouts, dense typography, and long-tailed scripts like Chinese. Prior fixes require costly retraining with layout supervision or impose rigid external bounding-box/mask constraints that degrade the base model's aesthetic quality and scene-planning flexibility. No existing method achieves high rendering fidelity and strong aesthetics simultaneously by exploiting only the base model's internal mechanisms without modifying architectures or parameters.

## Input / Output

FreeText operates as an inference-time plug-in on a frozen DiT/MMDiT model. It consumes a text prompt, a target text span (the specific string to render), and a rasterized glyph reference image placed in the target region; the glyph image is encoded by the same VAE as the base model. The method outputs the enhanced generated image (same resolution as the base model, e.g., 928×928) and an intermediate binary writing-region mask in latent space (H_lat × W_lat) that localizes where the text should appear. No additional learnable parameters, control branches, or external layout annotations are introduced.

## Method

FreeText decomposes text rendering into WHERE and WHAT. For WHERE, it extracts head-averaged image-to-text cross-attention from DiT/MMDiT blocks during sampling, augments the target token set with sink-like special tokens that provide temporally stable spatial anchors, selects informative timestep-layer pairs via soft-IoU scoring against a reference mask, and applies topology-aware post-processing (Otsu binarization + DBSCAN clustering + quantile-based region scoring) to yield a high-confidence writing mask. For WHAT, it encodes the glyph reference into latent space, aligns it to the current noise level via forward diffusion, then applies a Log-Gabor band-pass filter in the 2D frequency domain to amplify mid-to-high frequency components carrying glyph stroke structure while suppressing low-frequency background and ultra-high-frequency noise. The resulting SGMI-enhanced latent is injected into the denoising trajectory via masked replacement (Blended Latent Diffusion) with a cosine-annealed weight, restricted to a mid-early timestep window (0.8T to 0.6T) to avoid disrupting global planning or fine-detail refinement. The entire pipeline is training-free, model-agnostic, and adds roughly 12–18% inference latency with <1 GB peak-memory overhead.

## Results

On longText-Benchmark (English), FreeText improves NED from 0.625 to 0.713 on Qwen-Image and from 0.598 to 0.690 on FLUX.1-dev, while VQA Score jumps from 2.650 to 4.177 and 2.563 to 4.211 respectively. On CVTG, gains are more modest (e.g., FLUX.1-dev NED 0.712→0.722, VQA 4.050→4.952), and on the long-tail Chinese CLT-Bench, NED improves from 0.458 to 0.488. CLIPScore and Aesthetic Score remain largely stable across all settings, confirming that readability gains come without sacrificing semantic alignment or visual quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FreeText is evaluated as a plug-in on three base model families: Qwen-Image, FLUX.1-dev, and SD3 (Medium/Large). Against Qwen-Image, FreeText yields the largest absolute NED gains (+0.088 on longText-en, +0.055 on longText-zh) and the most dramatic VQA improvement (2.650→4.177 on longText-en), indicating the base model has sufficient glyph prior for FreeText to amplify. Against FLUX.1-dev, gains are consistent but smaller in NED (+0.092 on longText-en, +0.010 on CVTG) while VQA still improves substantially (2.563→4.211). Against SD3.5-L and SD3-M (CVTG only), NED improves by +0.016 and +0.053 respectively, with VQA jumping from 3.849→4.595 and 2.903→3.674. In all cases, CLIPScore and Aesthetic Score remain within ±0.01–0.02, confirming no meaningful degradation in semantic alignment or visual quality. On the hardest setting (CLT-Bench, rare Chinese characters), the NED gain shrinks to +0.030, suggesting FreeText strengthens existing glyph priors rather than creating them from scratch.

## Relation to Prior Work

FreeText departs from the dominant retraining-and-layout-conditioning paradigm (TextDiffuser, AnyText, GlyphDraw, GlyphControl) by operating entirely at inference time without modifying weights or adding control branches. It repurposes the attention-sink phenomenon well-studied in LLMs (Tigges et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2025) as a spatial anchor for text-region localization in DiT/MMDiT cross-attention, and borrows the masked-replacement injection mechanism from Blended Latent Diffusion (Avrahami et al., 2023) as the vehicle for its novel Log-Gabor spectral modulation. The key conceptual departure is the WHERE/WHAT decomposition: rather than treating text rendering as a single generation problem, it separates spatial planning (read from endogenous attention) from glyph fidelity (enforced via frequency-domain prior injection), each addressed with a lightweight, supervision-free module.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, plug-and-play design makes FreeText immediately deployable on top of any DiT-based T2I model without retraining, which is practically valuable given the cost and distribution-shift risks of fine-tuning large foundation models. The CLT-Bench benchmark for long-tail Chinese characters fills a gap in evaluation, and the finding that VLM-based OCR evaluation systematically inflates text-rendering scores (the 'linguistic crutch' effect) is a useful caution for the community. However, gains on genuinely rare characters remain modest (NED 0.458→0.488 on CLT-Bench), indicating the method strengthens existing glyph priors rather than enabling truly unseen characters; the 12–18% latency overhead and dependence on DiT/MMDiT attention structure also limit generality. Open questions include whether the attention-sink localization transfers to non-DiT architectures and whether the spectral band-pass design generalizes to scripts with very different stroke topologies.
