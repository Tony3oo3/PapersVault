## Problem

Reasoning models generate a long monolithic block of hidden reasoning before any user-visible output, wasting time when the model starts from a flawed premise and offering no opportunity for early user correction. This 'think-then-answer' paradigm is especially problematic in interactive or time-sensitive settings, yet prior work has not shown how to post-train a model to surface a useful intermediate plan that creates a natural intervention point without sacrificing final accuracy.

## Input / Output

The model receives a standard text prompt (coding, math, SQL, or reading-comprehension question) augmented with a plan-first system instruction that mandates the output pattern <think>…<answer>…</answer> repeated until a final answer. The first <answer> block must be an explicit step-by-step plan; subsequent blocks alternate between internal reasoning and progressively more detailed answers. At inference time, an LLM autorater (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) evaluates only the surfaced plan—never the hidden reasoning—either selecting the best of N candidate plans or issuing a binary accept/reject verdict, after which the model continues generation conditioned on the accepted plan.

## Method

PLANTAIN is a three-stage post-training recipe applied to Qwen3-4B/8B: (1) a larger model (Qwen3-32B) is iteratively prompted to synthesize 125 interleaved traces with the structure thought→plan→thought→code→thought→unit-tests, including concatenated-prompt and multi-solution variants to diversify interleaving depth; (2) supervised fine-tuning distills the interleaved format into the base model; (3) PPO reinforcement learning optimizes a composite reward where a binary format gate multiplicively controls downstream correctness (unit-test pass rate), helpfulness (LLM judge), and unit-test presence rewards, ensuring the model is only rewarded for task performance when the plan-first interleaved structure is satisfied. At inference, two plan-level strategies exploit this structure: Best-of-N-Plans samples N diverse plans at high temperature and lets an LLM autorater select the best, while Rewind-and-Repeat iteratively proposes a single plan, appends rejected plans to a failure history, and re-prompts until acceptance or a retry budget is exhausted—discarding only short plan prefixes rather than full reasoning traces. Trained exclusively on coding data, the learned plan-first behavior generalizes to math, text-to-SQL, and long-context QA, reducing time-to-first-response by over 60% while improving average pass@1 by roughly 6%.

## Results

On QWEN3-8B, Plan-Answer decoding improves average pass@1 from 59.0% (Think-Answer) to 61.6% while reducing time-to-first-response (TTFR) from 1,538 to 520 tokens (~66% reduction). Adding the Rewind-and-Repeat (R&R) plan-level inference strategy further lifts average pass@1 to 64.8% with TTFR of only 571 tokens, compared to 3,348 tokens for answer-level R&R. On QWEN3-4B, R&R(Plan) achieves 61.8% average pass@1 at 546 TTFR tokens, outperforming Think-Answer (56.9% at 1,419 tokens) on both accuracy and latency. The model, trained exclusively on coding data, generalizes to math, text-to-SQL, and long-context QA with consistent TTFR reductions of 60–70% across all domains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Plan-Answer (the core interleaved method) beats Think-Answer on both accuracy and latency across all four benchmarks for both model sizes: e.g., on QWEN3-8B MATH500, 85.2% vs. 83.0% pass@1 with 625 vs. 1,587 TTFR tokens. The No Thinking baseline achieves zero TTFR but trails Plan-Answer by 2–6 points in pass@1, confirming that the planning step adds genuine value. Among inference-time strategies, R&R(Plan) dominates R&R(Answer) on TTFR by roughly 7× (571 vs. 3,348 tokens for QWEN3-8B) while matching or exceeding its accuracy (64.8% vs. 63.6% average pass@1), because plan-level rejection discards only a short prefix rather than a full generation. Best-of-N(5) plans provide a smaller but consistent gain over Plan-Answer (+1.5–1.7 points average pass@1) at a modest TTFR cost, while R&R(Plan) achieves the largest accuracy lift (+3.2 points for QWEN3-8B) with minimal additional latency. Only 22% of plans require a rewind, and over 80% of corrections succeed within two attempts, indicating the strategy is efficient in practice.

## Relation to Prior Work

PLANTAIN builds on the Rewind-and-Repeat inference strategy from Kim et al. (2024) and the iterative variable-length CoT prompting approach from Kang et al. (2025, C3OT), but departs from both by making the interleaved plan-first structure a learned behavior via SFT + PPO rather than a prompting-only intervention. It positions itself against the overthinking-mitigation literature (length penalties, data curation) by arguing the goal is not to shorten reasoning per se but to create an early, user-facing intervention point. Conceptually, it extends plan-guided reasoning frameworks like ReAct and Pre-Act by surfacing the plan to an external evaluator mid-generation rather than using it only as an internal scaffold, and contrasts with post-hoc reranking methods (RankGen, AGGLM) by intervening at the plan prefix level rather than scoring full completions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is the demonstrated latency-accuracy tradeoff improvement: a 60%+ TTFR reduction with simultaneous pass@1 gains, achieved through a simple three-stage recipe (synthetic trace generation, SFT, PPO) that requires no architectural changes. The cross-domain generalization from coding-only training to math, text-to-SQL, and long-context QA suggests the plan-first inductive bias is broadly useful, though this remains to be validated on harder benchmarks (AIME, GPQA) and with human users rather than an LLM judge. Key open questions include sensitivity to the quality of the plan evaluator, whether the approach scales to larger models where RL post-training is costlier, and whether the benefits persist in agentic multi-step settings where plans are harder to verify in isolation.
