## Problem

Existing 3D CT analysis methods either adopt isolated task-specific models or compress volumetric data into limited tokens for 2D MLLM backbones, blurring fine-grained anatomy and impeding systematic accumulation of perceptual evidence for downstream reasoning. Meanwhile, 2D-oriented MLLMs fundamentally lack the ability to perceive volumetric medical data, and 3D-specific fine-tuning is brittle under clinical domain shift due to scarce, heterogeneous 3D data. The paper asks whether a unified, zero-shot agent framework can enable general 2D MLLMs to perform 3D CT analysis spanning perception to clinical understanding without any 3D-specific training.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a full 3D CT volume (H×W×D) and a free-form text query (e.g., a multiple-choice clinical question). Rather than feeding the volume directly to the MLLM, the system progressively converts 3D data into compact textual evidence: organ-level statistics (size, mean HU, z-range), CT-CLIP-derived lesion ROI candidates with risk scores, and slice-level visual observations from crop-and-zoom or mask-overlay operations. The output is a multiple-choice answer accompanied by an explicit rationale, a list of supporting evidence drawn from shared memory, and stated assumptions that motivated further evidence acquisition.

## Method

3DMedAgent equips a 2D MLLM agent with three zero-shot components that iteratively build a long-term structured memory. First, Organ-Aware Memory Initialization (OAMI) runs VISTA3D segmentation to compute per-organ size, mean HU, and z-range, seeding the memory with global volumetric priors. Second, Coarse-to-Fine Lesion Targeting (CFLT) leverages CT-CLIP's 3D image encoder to produce a dense similarity heatmap against a lesion text prompt, then applies organ-aware z-range filtering and patch-level ROI scoring (aggregating heatmap responses weighted by organ overlap ratio) to select a shortlist of informative slices. Third, the Think-with-1-Slice Loop (T1S-Loop) iteratively lets the agent produce a tentative answer with rationale, evidence, and assumptions; a router decides whether evidence is sufficient, and if not, the agent selects one unvisited slice, applies a visual tool (crop-and-zoom, mask overlay), and updates memory with the new observation. The key design insight is decoupling perception from understanding: the MLLM never processes raw 3D volumes but reasons over distilled textual evidence, enabling query-adaptive, multi-step reasoning without any 3D-specific fine-tuning.

## Results

On DeepTumorVQA (29 subtypes across 4 question types), 3DMedAgent with GPT-5 achieves 66% total MCQ accuracy, surpassing the best baseline (Qwen3-VL at 42%) by 24 percentage points. On the newly introduced DeepChestVQA benchmark (17 subtypes, 3 types), 3DMedAgent (GPT-5) reaches 57% total accuracy versus 43% for the strongest baselines. The largest gains appear on medical reasoning tasks, where 3DMedAgent scores 70% on DeepTumorVQA and 53% on DeepChestVQA, exceeding the best baselines by 25 and 12 points respectively. With Qwen3-VL as the agent backbone, 3DMedAgent still achieves 61% and 55% on the two benchmarks, confirming robustness to the underlying MLLM choice.

## Baselines & Comparisons

3DMedAgent (GPT-5) outperforms all seven baselines on both benchmarks across every question type. Against general MLLMs, it beats GPT-5 (41%→66%, +25 pp) and Qwen3-VL 30B (42%→66%, +24 pp) on DeepTumorVQA, and GPT-5/Qwen3-VL (43%→57%, +14 pp) on DeepChestVQA. Against 2D medical MLLMs, it surpasses MedGemma 27B (38%→66%) and HuatuoGPT 34B (38%→66%) by 28 pp on DeepTumorVQA. Against 3D-specific MLLMs, it outperforms M3D (39%→66%), RadFM (37%→66%), and CT-Chat (35%→66%) by 27–31 pp on DeepTumorVQA, indicating that 3D fine-tuning alone is insufficient without structured evidence accumulation. The one notable exception is the 'adjacent organ' visual-reasoning subtype, where CT-Chat (62%) and MedGemma (60%) exceed 3DMedAgent (45%), as these questions are better answered via anatomical priors than slice-level visual evidence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of training 3D-specific MLLMs (RadFM, M3D, CT-CHAT, Merlin) that compress volumetric CT into limited tokens for 2D backbones, arguing that such tokenization blurs fine-grained anatomy and encourages shortcut pattern-matching. Instead, it repurposes the tool-augmented agent paradigm (ReAct, Toolformer) for 3D medical imaging, where a 2D MLLM orchestrates external tools—VISTA3D for organ segmentation and CT-CLIP for lesion targeting—through a structured long-term memory. The three-stage pipeline (OAMI → CFLT → T1S-Loop) is novel in its progressive decomposition from global organ priors to regional lesion targeting to slice-level verification, and in its explicit evidence-centric memory that distills heterogeneous tool outputs into compact textual cues for multi-step reasoning. The paper also extends the 3D medical VQA task from the abdominal domain (DeepTumorVQA) to the thoracic domain with the new DeepChestVQA benchmark.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, scalable path toward general-purpose 3D clinical assistants that avoids the data-hungry 3D fine-tuning bottleneck, and the 20%+ accuracy gains over both general and 3D-specific MLLMs across 40+ tasks are well-supported by ablations, cross-dataset generalization, and two different MLLM backbones. Key limitations remain: performance is bounded by the quality of upstream tools (VISTA3D segmentation, CT-CLIP localization), the zero-shot routing policy is not learned, and relational anatomical reasoning (e.g., adjacent-organ queries) still lags behind MLLMs that leverage prior knowledge. Open questions include whether learning-based adaptation of the agent policy (RL, SFT) can close the remaining gap on complex reasoning, and whether the framework extends beyond CT to other volumetric modalities (MRI, ultrasound).
