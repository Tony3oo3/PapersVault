## Problem

Parametric Generalized Category Discovery methods rely on entropy regularization to prevent prediction collapse on novel classes, but this indiscriminately suppresses confidence on all unlabeled samples—including those from known classes—creating a systematic confidence bias that degrades old-class accuracy. The paper identifies this stability-plasticity dilemma as a root cause of performance decay and asks how to selectively restore confidence on known-class unlabeled data without compromising novel-class discovery.

## Input / Output

The method operates as a plug-and-play module on top of existing GCD baselines (SimGCD, AFGCD), consuming the same inputs: a labeled set of images from known classes and an unlabeled pool mixing known and novel categories, encoded by a DINO-pretrained ViT-B/16 backbone yielding 768-dim [CLS] features. Beyond standard class predictions, the evidential head outputs a non-negative evidence vector per sample that parameterizes a Dirichlet distribution over the old-class probability simplex, from which predictive uncertainty u = K_old/S is derived. Final evaluation uses the Hungarian algorithm to match predicted clusters to ground-truth labels, reporting All/Old/New accuracy.

## Method

RCA introduces two synergistic modules grounded in Evidential Deep Learning. The Reliable Anchor for Certainty (RAC) module repurposes the classifier as an evidence network (via Exp(Tanh) activation) that outputs Dirichlet concentration parameters on labeled data, trained with an expected-risk loss (digamma-based cross-entropy) plus an annealed KL-divergence regularizer that penalizes spurious evidence on incorrect classes, thereby establishing high-certainty anchors that counteract entropy-induced suppression. The Cross-view Confidence Alignment (CCA) module then propagates this calibrated reliability to unlabeled samples by enforcing both first-order alignment (KL between Dirichlet centroids, i.e., expected class probabilities) and second-order alignment (full KL between Dirichlet distributions) across augmented views, a design justified by a PAC-Bayesian risk bound showing the student's expected risk is controlled by empirical alignment plus distributional divergence. The total RCA loss (λ₁·L_RAC + λ₂·L_CCA) is simply added to the existing GCD training objective, requiring no architectural changes to the backbone or classifier.

## Results

RCA is a plug-and-play module that improves both the weak baseline (SimGCD) and strong baseline (AFGCD) across all six benchmarks. On CIFAR-100, SimGCD+RCA lifts overall accuracy from 79.4% to 82.9% (+3.5%) with a +4.8% gain on Old classes; AFGCD+RCA reaches 84.8% overall (+1.8%). On fine-grained CUB, Old-class accuracy improves by ~5% for both baselines (SimGCD: 67.4→72.4; AFGCD: 71.3→76.4) with negligible loss on New classes. Across all datasets, AFGCD+RCA achieves the best or near-best All-accuracy, e.g., 95.2% Old on ImageNet-100 and 76.4% Old on CUB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RCA is evaluated as a plug-and-play add-on to two representative parametric GCD baselines: SimGCD (weak) and AFGCD (strong). Against SimGCD, RCA consistently improves Old-class accuracy by 0.6–5.0% across all six datasets (e.g., +4.8% on CIFAR-100, +5.0% on CUB) while maintaining or slightly improving New-class accuracy, yielding a +1.4–1.7% gain in overall (All) accuracy. Against AFGCD, gains are smaller but still positive: +1.5–2.1% on New classes and +0.6–5.1% on Old classes, with AFGCD+RCA reaching 89.5% average All-accuracy on coarse-grained and 65.4% on fine-grained benchmarks. In the broader comparison, AFGCD+RCA surpasses all other listed methods (LegoGCD, SPTNet, ProtoGCD, ALLGCD, etc.) on the All-accuracy metric for CIFAR-100 (84.8%), ImageNet-100 (85.9%), CUB (71.2%), and FGVC-Aircraft (60.1%). The only dataset where improvement is marginal is CIFAR-10, where the task simplicity means entropy regularization causes minimal confidence degradation, limiting RCA's corrective effect.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a plug-and-play extension of existing parametric GCD frameworks (SimGCD, AFGCD), identifying a systemic 'confidence bias' introduced by the entropy regularization that these methods universally adopt to prevent class collapse. Its core technical departure is the first integration of Evidential Deep Learning (Dirichlet-based uncertainty modeling from Sensoy et al., 2018) into the open-world GCD setting, repurposing the evidential framework not for post-hoc uncertainty quantification but as an active training signal (RAC) and cross-view alignment mechanism (CCA). The CCA component draws on PAC-Bayesian generalization bounds to justify aligning both first-order (centroid) and second-order (full Dirichlet) statistics across augmented views, extending standard self-distillation beyond point-estimate probability matching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The diagnostic of confidence bias provides a concrete explanation for the long-observed stability-plasticity trade-off in parametric GCD, reframing it as a calibration problem rather than an inherent architectural limitation. The plug-and-play design means the technique can be adopted by any entropy-regularized GCD method without retraining from scratch, which is practically valuable. However, improvements are modest (1–5% depending on dataset granularity), new-class accuracy sometimes slightly degrades, and the CIFAR-10 results show the method has limited headroom on simpler benchmarks. Open questions include whether the EDL-based anchoring generalizes to settings where the number of novel classes is unknown, and whether the confidence-bias diagnosis extends to other semi-supervised paradigms beyond GCD.
