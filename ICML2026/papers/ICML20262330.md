## Problem

Sub-1-bit LLM compression via low-rank binary factorization is theoretically superior to tiny-rank FP16 truncation for heavy-tailed weight spectra, yet prior implementations (LittleBit) underperform state-of-the-art 1-bit baselines by a significant margin. The paper diagnoses this gap as Latent Geometry Misalignment: standard SVD singular vectors exhibit high coherence (spiky, axis-aligned distributions) that constitutes the worst-case geometry for binary quantization, inflating quantization noise and eroding the spectral energy gain that the architecture is designed to exploit.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-trained LLM weight matrices (Llama-2 7B/13B, Llama-3 8B, Gemma-3 27B, Qwen3 4B/8B) and produces a compressed representation per linear layer: binary latent factors U_b, V_b ∈ {±1}^{d×r} and three FP16 scale vectors (row h, central latent l, column g) that reconstruct the weight via a Scale-Binary-Scale-Binary-Scale factorization. The Joint-ITQ rotation is applied exclusively during offline initialization (~3 s per layer), so the inference graph is identical to the original LittleBit architecture with zero additional overhead. At 0.1 bpp the model body shrinks to <1% of its FP16 size (~0.1 GB for 8B models), and the compressed model generates text autoregressively.

## Method

LittleBit-2 introduces Joint Iterative Quantization (Joint-ITQ), which solves a Joint Orthogonal Procrustes Problem over the concatenated latent manifold Z = [U; V] to find a single rotation R that simultaneously aligns both latent factors with the binary hypercube vertices {±1}. The algorithm alternates between projecting ZR to binary codes via sign() and updating R via SVD of B^T Z, converging in ~50 iterations to transform the spiky SVD distribution into a bimodal distribution that maximizes the geometric decision margin and minimizes the local distortion coefficient λ. Theoretically, the paper establishes the Spectral Break-Even Condition (Proposition 4.1) proving low-rank binary outperforms tiny-rank FP16 when the spectral decay rate γ falls below a critical threshold γ*, and the Distortion-Geometry Duality (Lemma 4.2) linking quantization noise to the L1/L2 norm ratio of latent vectors. The rotation exploits the invariance W ≈ UR(VR)^T = UV^T, preserving exact FP reconstruction while making the coordinate distribution quantization-friendly, reducing mean distortion from 0.39 to 0.24 and peak distortion from 0.88 to 0.33 across all layers.

## Results

At 1.0 bpp, LittleBit-2 achieves a PPL of 11.53 and 57.33% average zero-shot accuracy on Llama-3 8B, surpassing OneBit (13.09 PPL, 52.23% Avg) and the baseline LittleBit (16.30 PPL, 47.11% Avg). In the extreme 0.1 bpp regime, LittleBit-2 maintains a PPL of 23.74 on Llama-3 8B (vs. 26.11 for LittleBit), compressing the model body to <1% of its original size (~0.1 GB). On Llama-2 13B at 1.0 bpp, LittleBit-2 achieves 60.53% Avg accuracy, resolving a scaling anomaly where the baseline LittleBit underperformed the 7B model (51.49% vs. 52.81%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LittleBit-2 outperforms all sub-1-bit and 1-bit baselines across every model and compression regime tested. At 1.0 bpp on Llama-3 8B, it beats OneBit by 1.56 PPL (11.53 vs. 13.09) and 5.1% Avg accuracy (57.33% vs. 52.23%), and surpasses the direct predecessor LittleBit by 4.77 PPL (11.53 vs. 16.30). The 1.1-bit methods BiLLM (59.37 PPL) and ARB-LLM (27.63 PPL) are far inferior, confirming that salient-weight binarization with auxiliary metadata is less effective than latent factorization at comparable bit budgets. In the 0.55 bpp regime, STBLLM catastrophically fails (241.96 PPL on Llama-3 8B) while LittleBit-2 retains functional quality (14.01 PPL). At the extreme 0.1 bpp, LittleBit-2 still edges out LittleBit (23.74 vs. 26.11 PPL on Llama-3 8B), and on Gemma-3 27B it achieves 16.38 PPL versus 17.72 for LittleBit and 37.53 for Tiny-Rank FP16. The 2-bit baselines GPTQ and EfficientQAT are not directly comparable (higher bit budget), but EfficientQAT's 9.80 PPL at 2 bits on Llama-3 8B shows that LittleBit-2 at 1 bit closes most of the gap to 2-bit quantization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on LittleBit (Lee et al., 2025), inheriting its Tri-Scale Latent Factorization architecture and Dual-SVID initialization, but identifies a specific failure mode—Latent Geometry Misalignment—where standard SVD produces high-coherence singular vectors that are near-worst-case for binary quantization. It draws on the incoherence processing literature (QuIP#, QuaRot, SpinQuant) for the rotation idea but restricts it to latent factors only, avoiding the inference overhead of full-matrix Hadamard transforms. The Joint-ITQ algorithm adapts the iterative quantization / Procrustes framework from the binary code literature (Gong et al., 2012) to the joint optimization of both latent factors simultaneously, while the Spectral Break-Even Condition provides a new theoretical lens connecting Eckart-Young truncation theory to the binary-vs-FP16 trade-off under power-law spectra.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Sub-1-bit compression is the critical next step for deploying LLMs on memory-constrained edge devices, where even 1-bit (≈15 GB for a 70B model) remains prohibitive. The finding that a purely initialization-level geometric fix (Joint-ITQ, ~3 seconds per layer) can close most of the gap between sub-1-bit and 1-bit fidelity without any architectural or inference-time changes is practically significant and broadly applicable. However, the QAT phase still requires ~29 hours on 4×H100, the 0.1 bpp regime retains substantial quality degradation (PPL 23.74 vs. 6.10 FP16), and the compression-to-speedup gap (160× storage reduction vs. 11.6× kernel speedup) highlights that specialized hardware is needed to realize the full benefit. Open questions include adaptive rank allocation guided by per-layer spectral decay, extension to vision and multimodal architectures, and whether the geometric alignment principle transfers to non-binary discrete domains.
