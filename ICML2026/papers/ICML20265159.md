## Problem

Large-factor (≥4×) real-world super-resolution is difficult because a single forward step of strong diffusion-based restorers like HYPIR often fails to recover fine-grained details on severely degraded inputs. A naive two-stage cascade of HYPIR improves perceptual quality but introduces three practical drawbacks: over-saturation accumulated across stages, weakened fine-grained texture, and prohibitive inference latency from full attention and block-based tiling. The paper asks how to make cascaded large-factor SR cascade-friendly without these artifacts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265159/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-quality degraded RGB image and a short text prompt (128 tokens) as input. At inference, the total upscaling factor is split into two sequential sub-factors (e.g., ×4 realized as two ×2 passes); each stage bilinearly upsamples the input, encodes it into a 64×64 FLUX latent, patchifies it into tokens, and runs the shared LoRA-equipped DiT once. Training crops are 512×512, but inference processes large images (up to 2048×2048) directly without tiling, and the output is a single high-quality super-resolved image.

## Method

HYPIR++ makes three training-side changes to a FLUX-based single-step restorer. First, it removes the degradation-removal encoder and noise-augmentation modules so the model preserves fidelity cues (color, saturation) from the LQ input rather than relying on FLUX's generative prior, which eliminates the over-saturation that cascading amplifies. Second, it moves all loss computation (L2, E-LatentLPIPS, GAN) into the FLUX latent space before the VAE decoder, using a knowledge-distilled latent ConvNeXt as the global discriminator backbone and a lightweight latent patch U-Net that scores randomly cropped 8×8 latent patches with dense realness maps for local-structure supervision. Third, it replaces full self-attention with sparse neighbor attention (SNA), which partitions image tokens into 128-token spatial blocks, restricts each query to a fixed neighbor range, and selects the top-k most relevant block pairs via coarse block-level attention, while retaining all text blocks; combined with a shortened 128-token text sequence, this enables tiling-free high-resolution inference at 1.71× the speed of full-attention HYPIR.

## Results

On the ×8 SR task (128×128→1024×1024), HYPIR++ achieves the best NIQE (4.960 on RealSR, 4.848 on DRealSR), MANIQA (0.635, 0.658), and DeQA (4.028, 4.150) scores, and ranks within the top two on all five perceptual metrics across both datasets. On the ×4 SR task (512×512→2048×2048) on RealPhoto60, HYPIR++ attains the best NIQE (3.844), MANIQA (0.627), CLIP-IQA (0.806), and DeQA (4.252), with MUSIQ (73.44) second only to OSEDiff (74.73). Under the same two-stage cascaded setting, HYPIR++ also runs 1.71× faster than cascaded HYPIR (9.42 s vs. 16.12 s per image at ×4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HYPIR++ outperforms its direct predecessor HYPIR (FLUX) on every metric across both ×8 datasets (e.g., NIQE 4.960 vs. 5.246 on RealSR; MANIQA 0.658 vs. 0.607 on DRealSR) while also running 1.71× faster under the same two-stage cascade. Against the strongest single-step diffusion baselines, OSEDiff leads on MUSIQ (73.76 vs. 73.16 on RealSR; 74.04 vs. 73.56 on DRealSR) and DeQA on RealSR (4.036 vs. 4.028), while TSDSR edges ahead on CLIP-IQA (0.734 vs. 0.704 on RealSR; 0.729 vs. 0.724 on DRealSR); however, HYPIR++ wins on NIQE, MANIQA, and DeQA (DRealSR) against both. Multi-step diffusion methods (StableSR, DiffBIR, SUPIR, SeeSR) and the GAN-based Real-ESRGAN are consistently outperformed by HYPIR++ on all five metrics on both datasets, with gaps of 0.3–1.5 NIQE points and 0.02–0.15 in MANIQA. On the ×4 RealPhoto60 benchmark, HYPIR++ also surpasses InvSR (NIQE 3.844 vs. 6.723; DeQA 4.252 vs. 2.546) and S3Diff (NIQE 3.844 vs. 3.553; DeQA 4.252 vs. 4.168), confirming its advantage in the large-factor regime where two-stage cascading is most beneficial.

## Relation to Prior Work

HYPIR++ is a direct extension of HYPIR (Lin et al., 2025c), a single-step diffusion-based SR model initialized from FLUX and fine-tuned adversarially in pixel space. The key conceptual departures are: (1) moving adversarial training from pixel space into the FLUX latent space with a distilled ConvNeXt discriminator, (2) removing the degradation removal encoder and noise augmentation to preserve fidelity cues across cascaded stages, and (3) adopting a sparse neighbor attention mechanism adapted from Block Sparse Attention (Guo et al., 2024) to eliminate tiling. The two-stage cascade design is motivated by inference-time scaling observations, positioning the work within the broader trend of allocating more compute at inference for single-step diffusion SR methods (OSEDiff, SinSR, TSD-SR).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, deployment-ready solution for large-factor (≥4×) real-world SR where single-step diffusion models demonstrably fall short on challenging inputs, delivering a 1.71× speedup over cascaded HYPIR while improving perceptual quality. The latent-space adversarial training with a lightweight distilled ConvNeXt offers a VRAM-efficient alternative to using full diffusion models as discriminators, a constraint that limits prior latent-space GAN approaches. However, the method is explicitly scoped to large-factor cascaded settings and reuses the same generator at both stages without stage-specific specialization, leaving open whether learned stage-aware variants or extension beyond the FLUX backbone would yield further gains. The impact statement also rightly cautions that synthesized details are plausible but not faithful, limiting use in forensic or safety-critical contexts.
