## Problem

Existing Vision-Language-Action models rely on large-scale transformers with dense continuous computation, imposing substantial inference latency and energy costs that preclude deployment on resource-constrained platforms such as micro-robots, legged systems, and deep-space exploration robots. Prior efficiency efforts (e.g., selective memorization, quantization) remain grounded in continuous dense computation, offering limited headroom for further energy reduction. The paper asks whether a fundamentally different, event-driven spiking neural network paradigm can replace dense VLA computation while preserving navigation and control performance.

## Input / Output

The system consumes a sequence of RGB history frames plus the current frame (each encoded into 196 visual tokens by SigLIP-V2), a text navigation instruction, and continuous proprioceptive sensor observations for low-level control. It produces a continuous action vector (linear and angular velocity commands for a quadruped platform) decoded from spiking firing rates over T timesteps. Notably, the model operates under RGB-only observations without waypoint supervision or depth/panoramic inputs, and the action policy encodes scalar observations into sparse population-coded spike events via Laplacian-kernel receptive fields before decoding back to continuous control signals.

## Method

SpikeVLA is a three-module fully spiking VLA architecture. Spike-V converts SigLIP-V2 into a spiking encoder using differential coding: linear layers are driven by incremental spike updates from differential spiking neurons with recurrent residual states, while nonlinear operators (LayerNorm, GELU, Softmax, element-wise products) are realized as differential graded units that output only per-timestep increments, enabling event-driven execution with few timesteps T. Spike-L builds on LLaMA-8B fine-tuned on navigation data, replacing dense token processing with Integrate-and-Fire neuron dynamics, multi-level spike token merging, and a differential temporal sparsity allocation mechanism that assigns longer spiking horizons to informative channels and single-step spikes to less important ones. Spike-A is a fully spiking action policy that encodes continuous observations via Laplacian-kernel population coding into deterministic IF spike trains, processes them through multi-layer fully connected SNNs, and decodes firing rates into continuous actions; it is trained with PPO using surrogate-gradient spatiotemporal backpropagation. The overall training combines ANN-to-SNN conversion (vision encoder), supervised fine-tuning (language model), and reinforcement learning (action policy), yielding ~65% energy reduction versus NaVILA while matching or exceeding its navigation metrics.

## Results

On R2R-CE Val-Unseen (RGB-only, no waypoints), SpikeVLA achieves NE=5.38, OS=63.4%, SR=53.3%, SPL=47.9%, matching or slightly exceeding the strongest RGB baseline NaVILA (NE=5.28, OS=61.5%, SR=53.9%, SPL=49.3%) on Oracle Success while being within ~1.5 points on other metrics. Critically, SpikeVLA reduces GPU memory from 16.1 GB to 6.2 GB, energy consumption from 141.25 J to 49.09 J (~34% of NaVILA), and ACEs from 3930 to 1196 (×3.3 reduction). On RxR-CE Val-Unseen, SpikeVLA achieves NE=6.20, SR=51.9%, SPL=45.3%, nDTW=60.4%, comparable to NaVILA (NE=6.12, SR=52.3%, SPL=46.1%, nDTW=61.0%) with the same ~3× energy advantage. In VLN-CE-Isaac closed-loop simulation, SpikeVLA achieves NE=6.02, OS=53.6% versus NaVILA's NE=6.29, OS=52.1%, while consuming only 44.23 J vs 141.25 J.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest same-modality baseline NaVILA (RGB, no waypoints), SpikeVLA is within 0.1–1.4 points on NE, SR, and SPL while achieving a higher Oracle Success rate (63.4% vs 61.5%), but reduces energy consumption to 34.8% (49.09 J vs 141.25 J) and memory to 38.8% (6.2 GB vs 16.1 GB). Compared to NaVid and UniNaVid, SpikeVLA improves SR by 16.3 and 6.3 points respectively, with a 3.2× energy reduction. Against AO-Planner, which uses panoramic views and depth, SpikeVLA achieves higher OS (63.4% vs 59.0%) and SR (53.3% vs 47.0%) using only RGB, demonstrating that the spiking architecture compensates for fewer sensory inputs. On RxR-CE, SpikeVLA matches NaVILA within 0.8 NE, 0.4 SR, and 0.8 SPL points while maintaining the same ~3× energy advantage. In the VLN-CE-Isaac closed-loop setting, SpikeVLA slightly outperforms NaVILA on NE (6.02 vs 6.29) and OS (53.6% vs 52.1%) while using 31% of the energy. Against INT4-quantized NaVILA, SpikeVLA achieves better SR (53.3% vs 48.2%) and SPL (47.9% vs 43.6%) with lower energy (49.09 J vs 72.49 J), showing that event-driven spiking provides a fundamentally different and more effective efficiency mechanism than precision reduction.

## Relation to Prior Work

SpikeVLA departs from the dominant VLA paradigm (NaVid, NaVILA, UniNaVid) that couples dense transformer-based multimodal reasoning with ANN policy networks, replacing the entire pipeline with event-driven spiking computation. It builds directly on differential coding for training-free ANN-to-SNN conversion (Huang et al., 2025) to handle both linear and nonlinear operators in the vision encoder and LLM, and on population-coded SNNs for continuous control (Tang et al., 2021) for the action policy. Unlike prior efficiency-oriented VLA work (COSMO, VL-Nav) that still operates within continuous dense computation, SpikeVLA introduces a fundamentally different computational substrate, positioning SNNs as a viable alternative to transformers for embodied intelligence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that a fully spiking VLA architecture can match ANN-based navigation performance while consuming roughly one-third the theoretical energy, which is significant for deployment on resource-constrained platforms like micro-robots and deep-space exploration systems. However, all energy figures are theoretical estimates based on 45nm CMOS assumptions rather than measurements on actual neuromorphic hardware, and the authors explicitly flag neuromorphic chip validation as future work. The broader open question is whether the event-driven sparsity advantage translates to real speedup and energy savings on dedicated SNN accelerators, or whether the overhead of multi-timestep simulation erodes the gains in practice.
