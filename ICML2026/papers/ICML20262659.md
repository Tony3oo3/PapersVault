## Problem

Conventional depth estimation only recovers visible surfaces, while existing occluded-geometry methods either require multiple iterative queries, additional inputs (virtual poses, human-interactive masks), or focus solely on object-level generation without scene-level completeness. No prior approach unifies object- and scene-level occluded geometry reasoning in a single, efficient feed-forward pass with view-aligned output.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262659/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (resized to 512×512) and produces a layered 3D point map V ∈ R^{H×W×L×3} with L=5 depth-ordered intersection layers, plus a ray stopping index C ∈ {0,1,…,L}^{H×W} that identifies valid intersections per pixel. The final output is a view-aligned 3D point cloud in camera coordinates, eliminating the need for ICP registration or coordinate alignment that generative methods require. Unlike standard depth maps, each pixel encodes up to L 3D intersection points rather than a single scalar depth.

## Method

LaRI reformulates occluded geometry estimation as a multi-layer point map regression problem inspired by layered depth images (LDIs) from computer graphics, where each camera ray is modeled as intersecting all surfaces along its path in depth order. The architecture uses a ViT-Large backbone with a CNN decoder and dedicated per-layer heads to regress the L point maps, supervised by a scale-shift alignment Euclidean loss. A key design choice is predicting a ray stopping index (ordinal regression over L+1 classes) rather than independent binary masks, which naturally enforces the depth-ordering constraint that valid intersections must be contiguous from layer 1. A separate ViT-Large encoder with a dense segmentation decoder handles the stopping index prediction with cross-entropy loss. The entire pipeline runs in one feed-forward pass with no iterative refinement, additional pose queries, or heuristic post-processing, and benefits from transfer learning with DINOv2/v3 or MoGe pre-trained weights.

## Results

On the GSO object-level benchmark (view-aligned GT), LaRI achieves CD=0.025 and FS@0.05=0.894, outperforming TRELLIS (CD=0.027, FS@0.05=0.853) and SPAR3D (CD=0.038, FS@0.05=0.745). On scene-level evaluation, LaRI achieves the best overall scores on both 3D-FRONT (CD=0.061, FS@0.05=0.799) and SCRREAM (CD=0.059, FS@0.05=0.590), while being the only method that reconstructs unseen surfaces (e.g., unseen FS@0.05=0.739 on 3D-FRONT vs. no unseen scores for all other methods). In terms of efficiency, LaRI uses 620M parameters and 51.5 ms inference, compared to TRELLIS (1796M, 734 ms) and SPAR3D (2026M, 905 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On object-level GSO (view-aligned GT), LaRI outperforms TRELLIS by 6% in FS@0.02 (0.643 vs. 0.608) and 32% over SPAR3D (0.643 vs. 0.486), while using ~3× fewer parameters and ~14× faster inference than TRELLIS. On canonical GT, LaRI is slightly behind TRELLIS (FS@0.02: 0.601 vs. 0.611) but clearly ahead of SF3D (0.513) and SPAR3D (0.506). For scene-level reconstruction, LaRI is the only method that produces unseen-surface scores, achieving FS@0.05=0.739 on 3D-FRONT unseen regions where CUT3R (the only other method with unseen output) reaches only 0.164. On visible surfaces, MoGe leads on 3D-FRONT (FS@0.05=0.788) and SCRREAM (0.786), but LaRI's overall scores surpass MoGe on 3D-FRONT (0.799 vs. 0.621) and are competitive on SCRREAM (0.590 vs. 0.668), thanks to its unseen-surface completeness. DUSt3R, VGGT, Metric3D-v2, and DepthPro are all substantially outperformed by LaRI on both object and scene benchmarks, with VGGT and DUSt3R showing particularly weak object-level results (FS@0.02 ≤ 0.200) due to their focus on visible surfaces only.

## Relation to Prior Work

LaRI repurposes the Layered Depth Image (LDI) concept from computer graphics (Shade et al., 1998) and reformulates occluded geometry estimation as a supervised multi-layer point map regression problem, departing from both NeRF/coordinate-based methods that require per-point querying or iterative refinement (KYN, DRDF, CUT3R) and large generative 3D models (TRELLIS, SPAR3D) that produce view-unaligned outputs. It builds on the point map regression paradigm (MoGe, DUSt3R) and ViT-based dense prediction architectures, extending them from single-layer depth to multi-layer ray-surface intersections. The ray stopping index formulation for mask prediction is a novel contribution that avoids the ordering constraints of standard segmentation while naturally encoding depth-ordered validity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LaRI provides a unified, single-feed-forward framework for both object-level and scene-level occluded geometry reconstruction without requiring additional inputs (poses, masks, human interaction) or iterative queries, making it practically deployable in robotics and AR/VR where partial observability is common. Key limitations include reduced point density on surfaces parallel to camera rays, potential inaccuracy when occluded regions lack sufficient visual context, and a moderate domain gap from synthetic training data. Open questions include extending to outdoor scenes, scaling data diversity, and whether the deterministic regression paradigm can match the generative flexibility of diffusion-based approaches for highly ambiguous occlusions.
