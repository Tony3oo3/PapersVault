## Problem

Existing verifiers for LLM agents in high-stakes domains (e.g., clinical diagnosis) underperform because they rely on implicit model knowledge or sampling-based signals that are poorly calibrated and easily misled by consistent errors. The paper asks how to compile explicit domain protocols (clinical guidelines, checklists) into trajectory-informed, well-calibrated correctness probabilities that enable risk-aware deployment via abstention or escalation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265895/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

GLEAN consumes a ReAct-style agent trajectory of T steps (each with an observation and action), the final diagnosis, and a set of K retrieved clinical guidelines (via keyword matching + all-mpnet-base-v2 embedding reranking). It produces a calibrated correctness probability p̂_T (via Bayesian logistic regression with MCMC posterior sampling over 100 labeled trajectories), an entropy-based uncertainty estimate, and per-step guideline alignment scores in [0,1]. The step-wise rating is extracted as a token probability over {YES, NO} rather than free-form generation, keeping the verifier lightweight.

## Method

GLEAN formulates verification as sequential Bayesian evidence accumulation in logit space: each step's guideline-grounded alignment score s_{t,g} is converted to a logit, and these are accumulated via a β-discounted sum to form a prefix-level surrogate evidence S_t. The key insight is that guideline-grounded signals exhibit approximate sufficiency and monotone near-linearity with correctness in logit space (R²=0.943), justifying a low-capacity Bayesian logistic regression calibrator trained on only 100 labeled trajectories. Multi-guideline aggregation (min, avg statistics) reduces variance, and when the calibrated uncertainty exceeds a threshold, active verification is triggered: guideline expansion retrieves additional supporting guidelines, and differential checks retrieve guidelines for competitive alternative diagnoses to subtract counter-evidence in logit space (rectification factor α=0.2). This links verification to test-time scaling by selectively increasing evidence collection only for uncertain cases.

## Results

On MIMIC-IV clinical diagnosis (three diseases: diverticulitis, cholecystitis, pancreatitis), GLEAN with multi-guideline aggregation (K=3) and active verification achieves AUROC of 0.9862/0.9582/0.9061 and Brier scores of 0.0453/0.0753/0.1194 with Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct, surpassing the best baseline (Self-Consistency) by ~12% in AUROC and ~50% in Brier score reduction on cholecystitis. With Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, GLEAN reaches AUROC 0.9756/0.9063/0.9325 and Brier 0.0586/0.1124/0.1031. In Best-of-N selection, GLEAN boosts agent diagnosis accuracy from 55.6% to 77.5% (N=16), outperforming Self-Consistency (70.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GLEAN outperforms all six baselines across every disease and both agent backbones on both discrimination (AUROC) and calibration (Brier, ECE). Against P(TRUE) and LLM-as-a-Judge (model-based ratings relying on implicit knowledge), GLEAN gains 15–20 AUROC points and reduces Brier by 50–80%, confirming that internal model criteria are insufficient for high-stakes verification. Self-Consistency is the strongest baseline, reaching AUROC 0.9131 on diverticulitis (Qwen3-30B), but GLEAN with active verification extends this to 0.9862 (+7.3 pts) while cutting Brier from 0.1608 to 0.0453 (−72%); on the harder cholecystitis task the gap widens to +12.8 AUROC points. Semantic Entropy is competitive on AUROC for cholecystitis (0.8448 vs. GLEAN's 0.9582) but provides no calibrated probability, limiting its risk-management utility. Self-Verification and Med-PRM (a learned process reward model trained on medical QA) underperform substantially, with Med-PRM showing the worst calibration (Brier 0.4684 on diverticulitis), likely due to task and domain mismatch. The ORM baseline, trained on the same 100 labeled trajectories as GLEAN's calibrator, still trails GLEAN by 10–16 AUROC points, highlighting the data efficiency of guideline grounding over learned reward models.

## Relation to Prior Work

GLEAN builds on the P(TRUE) token-probability mechanism from Kadavath et al. (2022) as its per-step rating primitive, but departs fundamentally from treating verification as a single-shot judgment or a learned reward model. It reframes agent verification as sequential evidence accumulation in logit space (following Gold & Shadlen's Bayesian decision-making framework), grounding each step's evidence in externally retrieved domain guidelines rather than the model's implicit knowledge. This positions GLEAN between training-free LLM-as-a-Judge approaches (which lack calibration and domain grounding) and learned process reward models (which require expensive expert annotations), offering a middle path that is both data-efficient and interpretable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GLEAN provides a practical, auditable verification pipeline for high-stakes agent deployment that requires only ~100 labeled trajectories for calibration and leverages existing professional guidelines, making it accessible in domains where large-scale expert annotation is infeasible. The active verification mechanism (guideline expansion + differential checks) connects verification to test-time scaling, offering a principled way to allocate additional compute where uncertainty is highest. Key limitations include dependence on guideline retrieval quality, the need for some labeled data for the Bayesian calibrator, and evaluation primarily on three MIMIC-IV diseases; open questions include generalization to domains with less codified standards, handling outdated or contradictory guidelines, and scaling to multi-agent or long-horizon settings.
