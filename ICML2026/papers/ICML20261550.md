## Problem

Popular RLVR policy-optimization algorithms (GRPO, RLOO, DAPO) use symmetric prompt weightings that downweight both easy and hard prompts, assigning near-zero gradient signal to prompts with very low empirical success probability. This causes training to stagnate in from-scratch RL settings where many prompts remain difficult, since the algorithm fails to leverage the gradient signal from those hard examples. The paper asks whether deliberately upweighting low-success prompts can accelerate learning, and under what conditions this asymmetry is beneficial.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a batch of B text prompts (math problems, countdown tasks, or code problems), generates M completions per prompt (M=8–16), and computes a binary reward (correct/incorrect) for each completion. The key output is a per-prompt advantage-weighted gradient update where the weight function ω_x(ρ̂) multiplies the RLOO/GRPO direction estimate; critically, unlike GRPO and RLOO, the proposed weightings assign non-zero effective weight even when all M completions receive zero reward (ρ̂=0), so the gradient remains informative in the zero-success regime. The effective weight is always well-defined at the boundary cases ρ̂=0 and ρ̂=1 because it is the product ω_x(ρ̂)·ρ̂(1−ρ̂).

## Method

The paper proposes a family of asymmetric prompt weightings—Linear-R (ω=1/ρ), Sqrt-R (ω=1/(ρ√(1−ρ))), and Plateau-R (piecewise: 1/(2ρ(1−ρ)) for ρ<1/2, GRPO weight for ρ≥1/2)—that upweight prompts with low success probability while retaining the RLOO/GRPO gradient direction (the difference between average positive and negative log-probability gradients). The core insight is that in from-scratch RL, where the model traverses a wide accuracy range and many prompts remain at very low ρ̂, symmetric weightings waste the gradient budget on already-solved prompts while starving the hard ones. The theoretical contribution derives a policy-dynamics ODE for success-rate evolution and shows via Cauchy-Schwarz that GRPO's symmetric weight is optimal for minimizing the number of gradient updates (regular time), while Sqrt-R's asymmetric weight is optimal for minimizing effective time that accounts for the 1/ρ rollout cost of observing a success in the rare-success regime. Empirically, asymmetric weightings improve Pass@1 by ~6% in from-scratch TinyZero and GSM8K setups (starting below 0.02 accuracy) but show no meaningful difference in post-SFT MATH and DAPO-math setups where the model already starts at 0.3–0.4 accuracy.

## Results

In the from-scratch RL TinyZero setup (Qwen2.5-3B, starting at 0.025 Pass@1), asymmetric weightings Linear-R and Plateau-R reach ~0.80 Pass@1, outperforming symmetric baselines GRPO, RLOO, and Uniform-R which plateau at ~0.74 (a 6-point gap). On GSM8K (Llama-3.1-8B base, starting at 1.3% Pass@1), Plateau-R slightly outperforms GRPO. In both post-SFT RL setups (MATH: Llama-3.2-3B-instruct, 0.38→0.53–0.56; DAPO-math: DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B, 0.3→0.5), all methods—GRPO, RLOO, Plateau-R, Linear-R—achieve comparable final accuracy with no meaningful difference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), the dominant RLVR algorithm. On TinyZero, Linear-R and Plateau-R achieve 0.80 Pass@1 versus GRPO's 0.74, a consistent 6-point improvement reproducible across three independent runs. On GSM8K, Plateau-R slightly outperforms GRPO, confirming the from-scratch advantage. In the post-SFT MATH and DAPO-math settings, all methods including GRPO reach essentially the same final accuracy (0.53–0.56 and ~0.50 respectively), so asymmetric weighting neither helps nor hurts. RLOO (Kool et al., 2020; Ahmadian et al., 2024) tracks GRPO closely in all four setups, being outperformed in from-scratch regimes and tied in post-SFT regimes. The key finding is regime-dependent: asymmetric weighting provides a clear advantage when the model starts from very low success rates (<0.02) and must traverse a wide accuracy range, but offers no benefit when the model already starts at intermediate accuracy (0.3–0.4) from SFT.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the policy-dynamics analysis of Mroueh (2025) for GRPO to a general family of prompt-weighted policy-gradient algorithms, and builds on the surrogate-reward framework of Thrampoulidis et al. (2026) to derive concrete new weightings. The key conceptual departure from GRPO, RLOO, and DAPO is the shift from symmetric prompt weightings (which downweight both easy and hard prompts) to asymmetric weightings that deliberately upweight low-success prompts, arguing that in from-scratch RL the learning bottleneck is precisely the hard prompts that symmetric schemes deprioritize. The paper also distinguishes itself from prior hard-example reweighting (e.g., Kimi's prioritized sampling) by assigning non-zero gradient signal even when all completions receive zero reward, and from token-level or confidence-based reweighting by operating purely at the prompt level as a function of empirical success rate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides practitioners with a clear, actionable rule: use asymmetric prompt weighting (Linear-R, Sqrt-R, or Plateau-R) for from-scratch RL where the model starts below ~2% accuracy, and stick with GRPO for post-SFT RL where the model already operates at 30-40% accuracy. The theoretical result that Sqrt-R is optimal under a rollout-cost-adjusted 'effective time' metric while GRPO is optimal under standard optimization time gives a principled justification for the regime-dependent behavior. Limitations include evaluation only on small models (1.5B–8B) with binary rewards, and the from-scratch gains (6% on TinyZero, modest on GSM8K) have not been validated at the scale of frontier reasoning models. Open questions include whether the benefit persists for non-binary rewards, agentic multi-step tasks, and whether allocating more rollouts to low-ρ prompts (as the theory suggests) compounds the gains.
