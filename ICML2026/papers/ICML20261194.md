## Problem

Concept steering of intermediate representations has proven empirically effective for controlling generative models, but lacks a principled theoretical framework: existing methods rely on heuristic vector additions with no guarantees on optimality or side effects. Moreover, prior theory (LEACE) only covers concept erasure, leaving concept switching—replacing one concept with another—without a closed-form optimal solution or a way to perform directed, one-directional transformations.

## Input / Output

The method operates on text prompts fed to LLMs (Llama-2, Qwen 2.5) or diffusion models (SDXL, SANA), producing generated text or images respectively. Steering is applied at every self-attention layer (LLMs, using last-token activations) or cross-attention layer (diffusion models, using all image-patch activations). The affine transformation is estimated offline from covariance samples (N=1000 concept-specific prompts, M=50000 general prompts) and then folded directly into the projection weight matrices, yielding zero additional inference cost at generation time.

## Method

MidSteer formulates concept switching as a constrained optimization: find the affine map (A, b) that minimizes expected squared distance from the identity, subject to matching the cross-covariance with the source concept indicator Z₁ to that of the target concept indicator Z₂. The closed-form solution is A = I + W⁺(Σ_WX,Z₂ − Σ_WX,Z₁)Σ_WX,Z₁⁺W with b = E[X] − A·E[X], where W is the whitening transform. This generalizes both LEACE (erasure, when Z₂ is constant) and LEACE-Switch (bidirectional flip, when Z₂ = 1−Z₁), while relaxing the dataset-partitioning and symmetry assumptions that limit those special cases. A steering-strength parameter β scales the update, allowing partial or amplified concept transfer. The paper further proves that vanilla steering (orthogonal projection for erasure, Householder reflection for switching) are special cases of LEACE and LEACE-Switch under the assumptions of zero mean and unit covariance.

## Results

On Llama-2-7B-chat toxicity→helpfulness switching, MidSteer at β=5 reduces Detoxify toxicity from 0.371 to 0.281 while preserving helpfulness at baseline (0.117) and unrelated-concept scores at 8.48/10, compared to vanilla steering which degrades unrelated scores to 3.11 and helpfulness to 0.029. On SDXL violence→peace switching, MidSteer at β=5 reduces the violence CLIP-score from 99.6 to 6.0 and raises the peace score to 94.0, outperforming LEACE-Switch (violence 28.2, peace 54.5) and vanilla steering (violence 20.8, peace 96.5). On the concrete horse→motorcycle switch (Table 1), MidSteer preserves the target concept CLIP-score at 70.7 (identical to unsteered) while CASteer and LEACE-Switch both drop it to ~53, and MidSteer achieves substantially lower FID on unrelated concepts (12.7 vs. 212.4 for CASteer on motorcycle prompts).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MidSteer consistently outperforms vanilla steering (CASteer) on both modalities: on Llama-2-7B-chat it reduces toxicity by 0.090 (0.371→0.281) while vanilla steering actually increases it (0.371→0.380) and collapses unrelated-concept preservation from 8.46 to 3.11; on SDXL it suppresses violence to 6.0 versus 20.8 for vanilla. Against LEACE-Switch, MidSteer achieves substantially stronger source-concept suppression (toxicity 0.281 vs. 0.346; violence 6.0 vs. 28.2) and a much higher target-concept score on SDXL (peace 94.0 vs. 54.5), because LEACE-Switch enforces a symmetric bidirectional flip that fails to preserve the target concept when it is already present in the prompt. LEACE-Switch does retain a slight edge on MMLU BERT-F1 (0.95 vs. 0.94) and FID on SDXL (121.6 vs. 134.6), reflecting its more conservative perturbation. The key structural advantage of MidSteer is that it performs a directed, one-way covariance match (source→target) rather than a global sign inversion, which eliminates the bidirectional failure mode visible in both Table 1 (target CLIP-score preserved at 70.7 vs. ~53 for baselines) and the qualitative examples in Figure 3.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on LEACE (Belrose et al., 2025), a closed-form affine framework for optimal linear concept erasure under the guardedness formalism, and on the broader line of activation steering work (CASteer, contrastive activation addition) that has been largely empirical. The key conceptual move is proving that the widely-used vanilla steering heuristic (orthogonal projection or Householder reflection) is a special case of LEACE under identity-whitening and zero-mean assumptions, then extending the LEACE optimization from erasure (zero covariance constraint) to switching (sign-flipped covariance constraint) and further to directed, asymmetric concept manipulation (MidSteer). It departs from Representation Surgery (Singh et al., 2024), which performs full distributional alignment under Gaussian assumptions, by operating on cross-covariances with concept indicators and explicitly minimizing disturbance outside the concept-mediating subspace.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical treatment of steering beyond erasure, providing closed-form affine solutions with explicit minimal-disturbance guarantees that can be folded into model weights for zero inference overhead—a practically important property for deployment. The framework unifies erasure, symmetric switching, and directed switching under one optimization problem, giving practitioners a principled alternative to heuristic vector manipulation. Open questions include whether the covariance-based formulation captures nonlinear or higher-order concept structure, how the full-rank assumption on source-concept cross-covariance behaves in practice for multi-concept settings (l > 1), and whether the approach extends to iterative or multi-step steering where concepts interact. The paper's safety-related experiments (toxicity→helpfulness, violence→peace) demonstrate practical relevance for post-deployment alignment, though the evaluation relies on a single judge model and a limited set of concept pairs.
