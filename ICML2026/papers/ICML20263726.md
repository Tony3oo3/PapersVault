## Problem

Existing Data-Free Quantization (DFQ) methods for Vision Transformers produce synthetic samples that suffer from semantic dispersion (semantics spread across the entire image) and attentional disparity (the quantized model Q cannot focus on the same discriminative regions as the full-precision model P), leading to severe performance degradation especially at ultra-low bit widths (e.g., 3-bit). The paper identifies that prior DFQ arts fail to preserve the crucial mutual information between synthetic samples and Q's outputs, which is the actual requirement for effective calibration.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263726/cropped_page1_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained full-precision ViT (P) and synthesizes calibration samples from Gaussian noise without any access to real training data. The synthesized RGB images are then used to calibrate the quantized model Q via masked attention-based loss. The final quantized Q processes standard RGB images at inference time to produce task-specific outputs (class labels for ImageNet, bounding boxes/masks for COCO, pixel-wise masks for ADE20K). The key constraint is that only the validation set is used for evaluation, maintaining the data-free setting.

## Method

MaskAQ couples selected informative regions of synthetic samples with the evolving quantized model Q through three components: (1) Informative Region Decoupling maximizes the differential entropy of pairwise attention-vector cosine similarities to concentrate semantics in sparse patches and separate them from noisy background; (2) Masked Attention Coupling constructs an adaptive binary mask from P's top-k attention weights (with stochastic dropping to avoid overfitting), then enforces L1 attention-map alignment between P and Q only within masked regions, preventing over-regularization of irrelevant patches; (3) Periodic Sample Refreshing re-synthesizes samples at fixed intervals to maintain mutual information as Q's parameters evolve during calibration. The approach is grounded in an information bottleneck analysis (Theorems 1–2) showing that alignment on informative regions suffices for Q to approximate P's predictive information, and the calibration loss (Eq. 16) further weights informative-region patches higher than background patches.

## Results

On ImageNet classification, MaskAQ achieves up to 3.1% Top-1 accuracy gains over the strongest baseline MimiQ at 3w3a (22.65% vs. 19.55% on DeiT-T). At 4w4a, MaskAQ reaches 53.10% on DeiT-T, 63.81% on DeiT-S, and 70.40% on Swin-T, outperforming MimiQ by 1.07%, 1.09%, and 1.07% respectively. At 5w5a and 4w8a, gains over MimiQ narrow to 0.5–0.8% but remain consistent across all six backbone variants. On COCO detection (4w4a), MaskAQ achieves 27.11 AP_box and 27.19 AP_mask; on ADE20K segmentation, it reaches 31.04 mIoU, both surpassing MimiQ.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MaskAQ consistently outperforms all eight baselines across every bit width and backbone. The CNN-targeted methods (GDFQ, Qimera, AdaDFQ) collapse to near-random accuracy on ViTs at 4w4a (e.g., Qimera scores only 0.57% on ViT-T), confirming that BN-based priors are inapplicable to LayerNorm architectures. Among ViT-specific DFQ methods, PSAQ-ViT and PSAQ-ViT V2 are outperformed by at least 18.91% at 4w4a (e.g., 53.10% vs. 32.57% on DeiT-S), attributed to MaskAQ's mitigation of semantic dispersion. CLAMP-ViT, which relies on patch-level contrastive learning, achieves only 0.15% on DeiT-T at 4w4a, far below MaskAQ's 53.10%. GenQ (ECCV'24), which uses generative models for sample synthesis, reports 67.50% on ViT-B and 76.10% on DeiT-B at 4w4a; MaskAQ matches or slightly exceeds these (70.53% and 74.41%) without any external generator. The closest competitor, MimiQ (AAAI'25), is outperformed by up to 7.62% at 4w4a (70.53% vs. 62.91% on ViT-B) and by 3.1% at 3w3a on DeiT-T, with the gap widening at lower bit widths where attentional disparity between P and Q is most severe.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the data-free quantization (DFQ) line for Vision Transformers, building directly on MimiQ's inter-head attention similarity framework and codebase while departing from the full-attention alignment paradigm used by PSAQ-ViT, CLAMP-ViT, and MimiQ. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of 'informative regions'—a sparse subset of patches where self-attention semantics concentrate—and the use of a masked attention alignment objective that selectively couples synthetic samples with the evolving quantized model Q, rather than enforcing global attention consistency. The information bottleneck (IB) perspective provides a theoretical reframing: the goal shifts from approximating real data distributions (the implicit objective of prior DFQ work) to preserving the minimal predictive information needed for Q's calibration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical gap: prior DFQ methods for ViTs collapse to near-random accuracy at 3-bit precision (e.g., PSAQ-ViT at 0.67%, CLAMP-ViT at 0.15% on DeiT-T), rendering ultra-low-bit deployment infeasible, while MaskAQ maintains 22.65%. The selective coupling of informative regions with the evolving Q, combined with periodic sample refreshing, offers a principled mechanism for maintaining mutual information throughout calibration. Limitations include the iterative synthesis overhead and unexplored applicability to 2-bit or more aggressive settings; open questions include whether the informative-region concept transfers to non-attention architectures or video transformers, and whether the periodic refreshing can be replaced by a one-shot or amortized strategy to reduce computational cost.
