## Problem

Existing Domain Adaptive Object Detection (DAOD) methods pursue statistical invariance by aligning cross-domain feature distributions, but this inadvertently amplifies domain-common spurious factors (e.g., shared backgrounds) as shortcut features rather than true causal factors that determine object identity. The paper argues that the gap between statistical invariance and causal invariance is the primary reason for the performance ceiling in domain adaptation, and that static spectral partitioning cannot capture the evolving boundary between causal and spurious factors during training.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images from a labeled source domain (Cityscapes or Sim10k) and unlabeled target domain (Foggy Cityscapes, BDD100k daytime, or Cityscapes), producing standard Faster R-CNN detection outputs (bounding boxes with class labels at mAP@50). Source images undergo DCT-based spectral perturbation before entering the student network, while target images are fed to both teacher and student with weak/strong augmentations. At inference, only the teacher model is deployed with zero additional parameters or architectural changes relative to the base detector.

## Method

The Dynamic Causal Refinement (DCR) framework establishes a closed-loop between data augmentation and model optimization via two synergistic components. Semantic Prediction Consistency (SPC) enforces statistical invariance through (a) Semantic Context Consistency, which applies Instance Normalization to strip style statistics from FPN features and uses cross-attention with layer-dependent temperature to align style-invariant semantic content, and (b) Prediction Distribution Consistency, which minimizes KL divergence over full category probability distributions and Smooth L1 over relative box offsets on shared proposals, avoiding the information loss of deterministic pseudo-labels. Discrepancy-Guided Causal Refinement (DGCR) uses the SPC consistency loss as a real-time feedback signal: an EMA-smoothed relative deviation is mapped through a tanh function to a modulation factor that dynamically widens or narrows the DCT mid-frequency static band, injecting Gaussian noise into high/low-frequency perturbation bands on source images. High teacher-student discrepancy widens the protected band to prioritize statistical invariance; low discrepancy narrows it to aggressively suppress domain-common spurious factors and force reliance on causal features.

## Results

On the Cityscapes→Foggy Cityscapes (C→F) 0.02 split, the proposed DCR framework achieves 65.6% mAP, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art ALDI++ (59.9%) by 5.7 points and DA2OD (57.3%) by 8.3 points. On the C→F ALL split, performance reaches 68.2% mAP vs. ALDI++'s 64.0%. For synthetic-to-real (Sim10k→Cityscapes), DCR attains 73.9% mAP, improving over DA2OD (69.7%) by 4.2 points. On the cross-camera C→BDD100k benchmark, DCR achieves 52.8% mAP, outperforming DT (47.8%) by 5.0 points. Notably, DCR even exceeds the enhanced fully-supervised Oracle (64.9%) on C→F 0.02.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DCR outperforms all 21 compared baselines across all three benchmarks. Against the closest competitor ALDI++ (TMLR'25), DCR gains +5.7 mAP on C→F 0.02 (65.6 vs. 59.9) and +4.2 on C→F ALL (68.2 vs. 64.0). Against DA2OD (AAAI'25), the previous SOTA on S→C, DCR improves by +4.2 mAP (73.9 vs. 69.7) and by +8.3 on C→F 0.02. Against DT (CVPR'25), which leverages DINOv2-based pseudo-labels, DCR wins by +10.2 on C→F 0.02 and +5.0 on C→B, while using a much smaller ResNet-50 backbone and achieving 24.6 FPS vs. DT's heavier pipeline. DCR also surpasses the large segmentation foundation model SAM 3 (59.5% mAP) by +6.1 on C→F 0.02 while running ~16× faster (40.7 ms vs. 664.6 ms per image). Remarkably, DCR exceeds the enhanced fully-supervised Oracle (64.9%) on C→F 0.02, attributed to causal refinement reducing false positives in similar categories like bus and truck.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Mean Teacher self-training paradigm (Tarvainen & Valpola, 2017) and frequency-domain perturbation approaches (FDA, Yang & Soatto, 2020; MAD, Xu et al., 2023) by introducing a closed-loop feedback mechanism that dynamically modulates spectral perturbation based on the model's real-time teacher-student discrepancy. It departs from prior DAOD work by arguing that statistical invariance (the standard alignment objective) conflates causal factors with domain-common spurious factors, and that static frequency partitioning is suboptimal because the causal/spurious boundary evolves during training. The SPC component further extends self-training beyond hard pseudo-labels by enforcing consistency on full prediction distributions and style-invariant intermediate features via attention-based alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decomposition of statistical invariance into causal and spurious components provides a useful diagnostic lens for understanding why domain adaptation plateaus, and the dynamic scheduling principle (perturb more when the model is confident, less when it is still learning) could generalize to other adaptation or robustness settings. However, all experiments are confined to driving-scene datasets (Cityscapes, Foggy Cityscapes, BDD100k, Sim10k), and the best results depend on RegionCLIP initialization, limiting claims about broad applicability. The Oracle-outperformance result is restricted to the synthetic-fog setting where the domain gap is narrow, and the frequency-band assumption (causal = mid-frequency) remains empirically motivated rather than theoretically grounded. Open questions include whether the causal refinement principle transfers to non-driving domains, whether it composes with transformer-based detectors, and whether the DCT-based spectral partitioning is optimal compared to learned frequency decompositions.
