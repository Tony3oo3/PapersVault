## Problem

Existing MARL benchmarks (SMAC, SMAX, JaxMARL) are built around static, predefined task designs, forcing researchers to maintain multiple incompatible environment implementations or perform code-level modifications to explore new scenarios. This makes systematic evaluation of MARL algorithms across diverse conditions—such as varying partial observability, terrain complexity, or unit compositions—prohibitively costly and limits reliable comparison of algorithmic strengths and weaknesses.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262357/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each agent selects from 7 discrete actions (4 cardinal movements in a global frame, 1 attack/heal, 2 rotations) subject to a state-dependent action mask that disables attack during cooldown. Observations are structured vectors combining own-unit state, relative features of all visible units (masked by a fan-shaped field-of-view and bush-zone concealment rules), and zone features, with a fixed dimensionality across scenarios. The environment additionally exposes a full global state (all unit features without visibility masking) for centralized critics in CTDE, and rewards are a shared dense signal proportional to the marginal health-ratio difference between teams plus a sparse ±1 terminal reward.

## Method

TABX is a JAX-based, end-to-end vectorized battle simulator that introduces three key environmental primitives absent from prior benchmarks: (1) fan-shaped partial observability where agents see only a first-person cone and must actively rotate to maintain situational awareness, (2) non-targeted interactions via a forward-facing rectangular hurtbox so that damage or healing is applied to whichever unit's collider intersects the region at attack time, and (3) three functional terrain zones (lava for periodic damage, bush for asymmetric concealment with combat-triggered reveal, swamp for speed attenuation). All environmental parameters—unit specifications, zone geometry and effects, heuristic policy difficulty tiers, and physics constants—are exposed as a configuration vector passed at reset, enabling dynamic reconfiguration across episodes without JIT recompilation. A role-appropriate heuristic policy composes Assassin (flanking, low-HP targeting), Ranger (kiting, bush-seeking), and Healer (proximity-to-injured) behaviors from unit attributes, with difficulty controlled by a stochasticity parameter ε and an aggressive threshold ξ. The paper leverages this configurability to conduct three controlled studies: value estimation error analysis for centralized vs. independent critics, sparse-reward exploration with RND-augmented MAPPO, and zero-shot generalization under UED (DR, PLR, PLR⊥, ACCEL, SFL) across unit-specification and zone-layout parameter spaces.

## Results

Across eight TABX scenarios, MAPPO and IPPO achieve the strongest win rates, but their relative ranking is scenario-dependent: IPPO outperforms MAPPO in crossfire, vsrangers, and ambush, while MAPPO leads in clover, ribbon, and grid. For centralized value learning, MAPPO's value estimation error is substantially lower than IPPO's in global-information-dependent scenarios (clover, ribbon), with a strong negative correlation (Pearson r = −0.82) between value error and episode return. In zero-shot generalization, Domain Randomization (DR) outperforms all replay-based UED methods (PLR, PLR⊥, ACCEL, SFL) on unit-specification variation, yet all methods yield average win rates below 50% on unseen unit specifications. TABX achieves near-log-linear throughput scaling and substantially higher effective steps/sec than SMAX when JIT recompilation overhead is included across 100 scenario resets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among MARL algorithms, IPPO beats MAPPO in crossfire, vsrangers, and ambush, showing that centralized value learning is not uniformly superior; QMIX consistently outperforms IQL across all eight scenarios, consistent with its monotonic mixing inductive bias. For UED zero-shot generalization, DR significantly outperforms PLR, PLR⊥, ACCEL, and SFL on unit-specification variation, while replay-based methods perform comparably to each other on zone-layout variation; however, all UED baselines fail to exceed 50% average win rate on unseen unit specifications, with health variation identified as the dominant bottleneck. In the direct TABX-vs-SMAX comparison, TABX achieves substantially higher effective throughput (including JIT recompilation) across all unit counts, but on matched scenarios TABX is harder to solve than SMAX in 2s3z and 6h_vs_8z due to non-targeting attack dynamics, while in 5m_vs_6m TABX initially underperforms SMAX but surpasses it after approximately 2M training steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

TABX extends the MARL battle-simulator lineage (SMAC, SMAX, JaxMARL) by introducing three key mechanical departures: non-targeting hurtbox interactions (replacing explicit target selection), fan-shaped partial observability (replacing global or simpler visibility models), and functional terrain zones (lava, bush, swamp) that impose asymmetric information and movement constraints. It also inherits the JAX-based hardware-acceleration paradigm from JaxMARL and SMAX but adds a critical practical distinction: full parameter reconfiguration (unit specs, zone layouts, heuristic difficulty, physics) without JIT recompilation, enabled by a config-file-plus-GUI interface. For the zero-shot generalization study, TABX builds directly on the UED framework of Dennis et al. (2020) and the regret-based curriculum methods (PLR, ACCEL, SFL), applying them for the first time in a multi-agent battle setting with agent-level parameter variation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TABX addresses a practical bottleneck in MARL research: the inability to systematically vary environment parameters across episodes without code modification or recompilation, which has historically pushed evaluations toward narrow, fixed configurations. The UED experiments reveal a genuinely open challenge—generalization to unseen unit specifications (health, speed, damage) remains below 50% win rate for all evaluated methods, in stark contrast to zone-layout generalization—suggesting that high-dimensional, agent-level parameter spaces interact poorly with existing adaptive curricula. However, the authors themselves note the experiments are 'primarily illustrative,' the environment is combat-only (no resource sharing, communication emergence, or task allocation), and the findings are demonstrated in a single simulator, limiting broader generalizability of the conclusions.
