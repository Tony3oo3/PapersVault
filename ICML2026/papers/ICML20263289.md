## Problem

Existing SVD-based MoE compression methods apply uniform rank allocation or rely solely on static weight properties, ignoring the severe heterogeneity in expert routing frequency observed during inference. Additionally, the compression residual discarded by low-rank truncation is non-negligible in magnitude, yet no prior method reconstructs it in a parameter-efficient manner. These two gaps lead to suboptimal compression performance, particularly at high compression ratios where infrequently-activated but information-rich experts are under-allocated rank and residual information is lost.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the up and gate projection weight matrices of all experts in each MoE layer (down matrices remain dense), a calibration dataset of 1,024 sequences (1,024 tokens each) for routing frequency estimation, and a target compression ratio ρ. It outputs, per expert group, a low-rank factorization (expert-specific coefficient matrix A_i and group-shared basis matrix B_g at allocated rank K_g) plus a low-dimensional residual vector η_g (3% of original parameter count) that is projected back into weight space via a shared sparse one-hot matrix P. The entire reconstruction is trained by minimizing Frobenius-norm loss between original and approximated weights, requiring no labeled data.

## Method

RFID-MoE introduces a fused importance metric C_g = ξ·E_g + (1−ξ)·F_g that combines normalized effective rank (spectral entropy of the singular value spectrum, measuring information density) with normalized routing frequency (measuring activation importance) to adaptively allocate SVD rank budgets across expert groups under a fixed compression budget. Experts are first sorted by routing frequency and partitioned into sequential groups of k experts, within which a shared-basis SVD decomposition is performed. Rather than discarding the low-rank residual, the method reconstructs it via a parameter-efficient sparse projection: a low-dimensional trainable vector η ∈ R^a is mapped into the full weight space through a one-hot sparse matrix P with orthonormal columns (P^T P = I_a), guaranteeing isometric embedding that preserves optimization geometry. The projection matrix P is constructed once and shared across all expert groups, enforcing a unified residual subspace that reduces parameters and improves training stability. The final compressed expert weight is the sum of the low-rank approximation and the reshaped residual projection, trained end-to-end via Adam on the Frobenius reconstruction loss.

## Results

On Qwen3-30B-A3B-2507 at 60% compression, RFID-MoE achieves a PTB perplexity of 16.92, reducing PPL by over 8.0 points compared to the best baseline (MoBE at 24.93) and by 21.9 points over D²-MoE (38.84). On zero-shot reasoning, RFID-MoE reaches 0.66 on HellaSwag (~8% above MoBE's 0.58) and 0.71 on WinoGrande at the same compression ratio. On DeepSeekMoE-16B-Base at 60% compression, RFID-MoE achieves 11.54 PTB PPL versus 11.90 for MoBE, and on Qwen2-57B-A14B at 40% compression it reaches 9.46 PTB PPL versus 9.58 for MoBE, consistently outperforming all baselines across models and compression ratios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RFID-MoE outperforms all five baselines across every model and compression ratio tested. Against MoBE (the strongest decomposition-based baseline), RFID-MoE achieves 8.0 lower PTB PPL at 60% compression on Qwen3-30B-A3B-2507 (16.92 vs. 24.93) and ~8% higher HellaSwag accuracy (0.66 vs. 0.58); the gap narrows at lower compression ratios (e.g., 0.2 PPL on PTB at 20%). Against D²-MoE, the advantage is even more pronounced at high compression (21.9 PPL on PTB at 60%), while D²-MoE suffers severe degradation on C4 (36.55 vs. 17.96). RS-MoE and NAEE (expert pruning) consistently trail RFID-MoE by 1–3 PPL on PTB at 60% compression, with NAEE also degrading sharply on reasoning tasks. MoE-I², evaluated only on DeepSeekMoE-16B-Base and Qwen2-57B-A14B, is outperformed by RFID-MoE on both PPL and accuracy at 40% and 60% compression. The ablation in Table 6 further confirms that both adaptive rank allocation and residual reconstruction contribute independently: removing the residual vector alone increases PTB PPL from 13.53 to 15.06 at 50% compression.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SVD-based basis-sharing compression framework established by MoBE (Chen et al., 2025) and the cross-layer parameter sharing formulation of Wang et al. (2024), which decompose expert weights into shared basis matrices and expert-specific projections. Its two key departures are: (1) replacing uniform or static-weight-based rank allocation with an adaptive scheme that fuses dynamic routing frequency with effective rank (a spectral entropy measure of information density), and (2) recovering the SVD residual via a sparse orthogonal projection matrix borrowed from UniLoRA (Li et al., 2025a), rather than discarding it as prior methods do. The fusion of activation statistics with intrinsic matrix geometry for rank budgeting is the paper's central methodological contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MoE models are the dominant scaling paradigm for LLMs, yet their memory footprint (e.g., Qwen3-235B exceeding 320 GB) makes deployment on standard clusters infeasible; RFID-MoE offers a practical post-training compression path that preserves expert count while reducing parameters. The observation that rarely-activated experts can carry high information density (effective rank 1049 vs. 557 for more frequently routed groups) challenges the common heuristic of treating routing frequency as a proxy for importance, with implications for expert pruning and merging strategies more broadly. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multimodal MoE architectures, whether system-level kernels can exploit the grouped low-rank structure for actual inference speedup, and whether the calibration dataset choice (WikiText-2 vs. C4) generalizes to domain-specific deployment scenarios.
