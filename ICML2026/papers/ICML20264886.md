## Problem

Finding ε-stationary points of non-convex functions using only a comparison oracle (which reveals which of two points has a larger function value but not the actual values) was previously limited to O(n/ε²) queries via the stochastic three-point method. It was unclear whether comparison access could exploit higher-order smoothness (Lipschitz Hessian) to improve the ε-dependence, and no quantum algorithm existed for this oracle model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264886/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a comparison oracle O_f^comp (returning ±1 for any pair of points), an initial point x_0 with bounded value gap Δ = f(x_0) − inf f, and a precision ε. It outputs a list of O(1/ε^1.5) iterates, guaranteeing that at least one visited point is ε-stationary; it cannot certify which point is stationary since gradient norms are inaccessible under comparisons. The quantum variant replaces the classical oracle with a quantum comparison oracle queryable in superposition, reducing the dimension dependence from Õ(n²) to Õ(n).

## Method

The core contribution is a trust-region-type algorithm (ComparisonTR) that achieves Ω(ε^1.5) function-value decrease per iteration using only comparisons. Three key subroutines enable this: (1) ComparisonHessVec estimates the direction of ∇²f(x)·y by estimating three gradient directions (at x, x+ry, x−ry) and solving a geometric triangle; (2) ComparisonHE assembles the normalized Hessian column-by-column, determining relative column norms via triangle solving with a perturbation mechanism for near-parallel columns; (3) ComparisonRatio estimates ‖H‖/‖g‖ using the Davis-Kahan theorem to bound eigenvector misalignment. At each iteration, the algorithm constructs three candidate steps (normalized gradient descent, line search along the largest-magnitude eigenvector, and a trust-region minimizer of the approximate quadratic model) and selects the best by comparison, with the gradient and line-search steps handling degenerate Hessian cases (rank-one, definite, near-colinear eigenvector). The quantum speedup arises from replacing the O(n)-query classical gradient direction estimator with an O(log n)-query quantum version, reducing overall complexity from Õ(n²/ε^1.5) to Õ(n/ε^1.5).

## Results

The paper establishes that a trust-region-type algorithm (Algorithm 5) visits an ε-stationary point of a twice-differentiable function with L₁-Lipschitz gradient and L₂-Lipschitz Hessian using Õ(Δ√L₂ n²/ε^1.5) queries to a comparison oracle (Theorem 1.1). In the quantum setting, replacing the classical gradient-direction subroutine with a quantum one yields the first quantum algorithm for this problem, achieving Õ(Δ√L₂ n/ε^1.5) queries to a quantum comparison oracle (Theorem 1.2 / Corollary 6.4). The ε^1.5 dependence matches the optimal rate of second-order (Newton-type) methods under full gradient/Hessian oracle access (Nesterov & Polyak, 2006; Carmon et al., 2020). A corollary extends the guarantee to visiting a (ε, √ε)-second-order stationary point at the same query complexity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is with the Stochastic Three-Point (STP) method of Bergou et al. (2020) and Gorbunov et al. (2020), which finds an ε-stationary point using O(n/ε²) comparison queries. This paper improves the ε-dependence from ε⁻² to ε⁻¹·⁵ (matching the optimal second-order rate), at the cost of a worse dimension dependence (n² vs. n). Compared to standard gradient-descent under a gradient oracle (O(1/ε²) iterations), the comparison-oracle algorithm achieves the same ε^1.5 rate as Newton-type methods but requires Õ(n²) queries per iteration to estimate the Hessian direction. The quantum algorithm achieves a √n speedup over the classical version (n vs. n²), which is the first quantum speedup established for stationary-point finding under comparison access. The paper notes that it is unclear whether the n² dependence is optimal, and that matching lower bounds in the comparison setting remain an open problem.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Tao et al. (2026), which provides classical and quantum algorithms for gradient direction estimation using only pairwise comparisons, using their ComparisonGE/ComparisonQGE subroutines as the foundational building block. It departs from the stochastic three-point method (STP) of Bergou et al. (2020) and Gorbunov et al. (2020), which achieved O(n/ε²) comparison queries for stationary point finding by exploiting only first-order smoothness, by introducing a comparison-based Hessian estimation subroutine that enables a trust-region method with the improved ε^1.5 dependence matching optimal second-order rates. The quantum extension is the first to apply a quantum comparison oracle to nonconvex stationary point finding, extending the quantum optimization literature that previously required gradient or function-value oracles.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result establishes that second-order smoothness can be exploited even under the highly restricted comparison oracle model, closing the gap between comparison-based and gradient-based optimization in ε-dependence (at the cost of n² vs. n dimension dependence). The quantum speedup from Õ(n²/ε^1.5) to Õ(n/ε^1.5) queries is the first quantum algorithm for stationary point finding under comparison access. Key open questions include whether the n² dependence is optimal, whether interpolation with STP can improve both n and ε simultaneously, and whether the approach extends to stochastic comparison oracles (e.g., Bradley-Terry model) relevant to practical RLHF settings. The paper is purely theoretical with no empirical validation, and the guarantee is only that the algorithm *visits* an ε-stationary point rather than certifying one.
