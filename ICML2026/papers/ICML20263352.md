## Problem

Standard evaluation of self-supervised representations via linear probing is computationally expensive, hyperparameter-sensitive, and offers no geometric insight into why a representation is good. While intrinsic dimension (ID) has been linked to generalization in supervised CNNs, its behavior across modern SSL paradigms (joint-embedding, joint-predictive, vision-language) remained unexplored, and existing ID estimators (TwoNN, MLE) are unreliable in the n≈d regime typical of vision SSL where sample count is comparable to ambient dimension.

## Input / Output

IDEST consumes frozen feature vectors from a pretrained SSL backbone (the [CLS] token for DINO/DINOv2, or average-pooled patch tokens for I-JEPA), subsampled to N=50,000 points, and produces a single scalar intrinsic dimension estimate. No labels, augmentations, pairing structure, or access to training data are required—only the frozen representation matrix. The scalar output is then used to rank models or select hyperparameters by choosing the configuration with the lowest estimated dimension.

## Method

IDEST estimates intrinsic dimension via the Minimum Spanning Tree estimator (dim_MST) of Costa & Hero (2006), which exploits the asymptotic scaling L(MST(X_n)) ∝ n^((d−1)/d) to recover d = 1/(1−m) from a log-log linear regression over subsamples of increasing size. This choice is motivated by the fact that dim_MST is grounded in Euclidean functional theory and persistent homology, making it robust to noise, non-uniform sampling density, and high ambient dimensionality—properties that TwoNN and MLE lack, as demonstrated on a simple 1-D helix where TwoNN diverges. The theoretical link to downstream performance comes from the generalization bound L ≃ O(K_L N_D^{−1/d}) of Konz & Mazurowski (2024), which implies lower intrinsic dimension yields tighter error bounds. In practice, IDEST requires only a single feature-extraction pass followed by MST computation (via Ripser), taking ~2 minutes versus ~65 minutes for a 10-epoch linear probe on ImageNet, enabling efficient label-free hyperparameter selection across VICReg, DINO, and I-JEPA.

## Results

IDEST achieves strong rank correlation with linear probing accuracy across four datasets (ImageNet, iNat-18, iNat-21, SUN397), with Kendall's τ ≈ −0.6 and Spearman's ρ ≈ −0.8, confirming that lower intrinsic dimension consistently predicts higher downstream performance. For hyperparameter selection (the 'all' setting), IDEST recovers near-oracle accuracy: 65.5% for VICReg (RN-50), 65.5% for DINO (ViT-S), and 66.4% for I-JEPA (ViT-B) on ImageNet, and 65.0%, 62.4%, and 60.0% on fine-grained averages, respectively. Computationally, IDEST requires only 1.8–12.8 minutes versus 64.5–322.7 minutes for a 10-epoch linear probe, a 30×–25× speedup depending on model scale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IDEST clearly outperforms α-ReQ across nearly all settings, most dramatically for I-JEPA where α-ReQ selects the lower-bound accuracy (49.0% on ImageNet, 41.4% fine-grained) while IDEST achieves 66.4% and 60.0% respectively. Against RankMe, which was specifically designed for joint-embedding SSL and mirrors VICReg's variance objective, IDEST is competitive on VICReg (65.5 vs. 69.1 on ImageNet, where RankMe hits the upper bound) but substantially outperforms it on I-JEPA (66.4 vs. 61.9 on ImageNet; 60.0 vs. 59.5 fine-grained), demonstrating that IDEST generalizes beyond the JE-SSL paradigm where RankMe was tailored. Compared to LiDAR, the strongest baseline that leverages pretraining augmentation information (making it weakly-supervised), IDEST matches or exceeds it in a strictly unsupervised setting: IDEST ties LiDAR on DINO (65.5% ImageNet, 62.4% fine-grained) and beats it on I-JEPA (66.4% vs. 63.4% ImageNet; 60.0% vs. 58.3% fine-grained), while LiDAR retains a small edge on VICReg fine-grained (66.6% vs. 65.0%). Overall, IDEST is the only fully unsupervised method that remains competitive with the supervised oracle across all three SSL paradigms tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the theoretical link between intrinsic dimension and generalization (established for supervised CNNs by Ansuini et al. 2019 and formalized as a scaling law by Konz & Mazurowski 2024) to the self-supervised learning setting, where prior ID-based analysis was largely absent. It departs from standard ID estimators (TwoNN, MLE) that the authors show are unreliable in the n≈d, dependent-samples regime inherent to SSL, instead adopting the minimum spanning tree estimator (Costa & Hero 2006) whose consistency is guaranteed under weaker assumptions. Conceptually, IDEST positions itself alongside existing representation-quality metrics (α-ReQ, RankMe, LiDAR) but targets the harder setting of frozen representations without access to training augmentations or pairing structure, and generalizes across joint-embedding, joint-predictive, and vision-language paradigms where RankMe and α-ReQ are either inapplicable or degrade.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

IDEST offers SSL practitioners a label-free, 30–100× cheaper alternative to linear probing for both model comparison and hyperparameter selection, with strong rank correlations (ρ≈−0.8, τ≈−0.6) that hold across 33 models, four datasets, and three SSL paradigms. Key limitations temper its utility: vision-language models (CLIP, EVA-CLIP) deviate due to the modality gap, the estimator is less informative in the first ~10 training epochs of ViTs, and Theorem 3.1 provides only a convergence-rate bound rather than an exact accuracy predictor. Open questions include formalizing the gap between intrinsic and effective dimensions (which the authors link to manifold curvature), integrating ID estimates directly into SSL training objectives, and extending the framework to dense tasks like segmentation where the theoretical framing does not directly apply.
