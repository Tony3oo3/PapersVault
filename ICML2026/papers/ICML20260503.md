## Problem

Normalizing flows face a fundamental 'noise dilemma': training with small additive noise yields overly detailed but structurally incoherent samples, while large noise produces globally coherent but blurry images due to Tweedie's lemma. This trade-off has limited NFs' competitive standing against diffusion and autoregressive models, and no principled mechanism existed to recover both global structure and fine-grained texture from a single likelihood-based model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260503/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a Gaussian noise vector and an ImageNet class label, producing a natural RGB image at 64×64, 128×128, or 256×256 resolution. In pixel-space experiments the image is tokenized into patches of size 1/32 of the resolution (e.g., 8×8 patches for 256×256), yielding a fixed-length token sequence; in latent-space experiments an SD-VAE encoder/decoder is used with patch size 1. Sampling is two-phase: an autoregressive pass generates a noisy image at t_max, followed by a fully parallel denoising pass that refines it to the clean-data limit.

## Method

iTARFlow trains a causal Transformer-based autoregressive normalizing flow (TARFlow) over a continuum of noise levels t ∈ [t_min, t_max] using a shared network conditioned on t, with the standard NF log-likelihood objective reweighted by γ_t = t. At inference, the model first autoregressively generates a highly noisy sample at t_max (exploiting the structural coherence that high noise provides), then applies iterative score-based denoising by computing ∇_x log p_θ(x_t, t) via automatic differentiation and integrating the ODE dx_t = −t ∇_x log p_θ(x_t, t) dt from t_max down to t_min. This multi-noise training is the key enabler: it allows the same likelihood model to serve as both the autoregressive generator and the denoiser, so that high-noise structural priors and low-noise detail recovery are combined in a single end-to-end, likelihood-based framework. As few as 5 ODE steps suffice for visually high-quality samples, and 10 steps reach optimal FID.

## Results

On ImageNet-64, iTARFlow-B achieves FID 1.68 with 770M parameters, substantially outperforming TARFlow (FID 2.66, 880M) and approaching EDM-ODE (FID 2.36). On ImageNet-128, iTARFlow-L reaches FID 2.44 (1.2B params), a large improvement over TARFlow (FID 5.03, 1.3B). On ImageNet-256 pixel space, iTARFlow-XL achieves FID 3.32 (2.2B), beating TARFlow (5.56), STARFlow (4.69), and FARMER-Patch8 (3.60), while in latent space iTARFlow-B reaches FID 2.32 (770M+86M), surpassing STARFlow (2.40, 1.4B+86M) with fewer parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

iTARFlow consistently outperforms all prior normalizing flow baselines: on ImageNet-64, iTARFlow-B (FID 1.68) beats TARFlow (2.66) by nearly 1.0 FID with fewer parameters; on ImageNet-128, iTARFlow-L (2.44) improves over TARFlow (5.03) by 2.59 FID; on ImageNet-256 pixel space, iTARFlow-XL (3.32) surpasses TARFlow (5.56), STARFlow (4.69), FARMER-Patch8 (3.60), and JetFormer (6.64). In latent space at 256px, iTARFlow-B (2.32) edges out STARFlow (2.40) with roughly half the parameters, and is within 0.05 FID of DiT-XL (2.27) despite a fundamentally different training objective. However, iTARFlow still trails the strongest diffusion baselines: SiD2 (1.38) and PixNerd-XL (1.93) in pixel space, and MAR-L (1.78) and REPA (1.94) in latent space. The paper attributes part of the remaining gap to two artifact types (collapsed backgrounds and low-frequency blur) observed in a small fraction of the 50k evaluation samples, suggesting further gains are possible.

## Relation to Prior Work

iTARFlow extends TARFlow (Zhai et al., 2024), a Transformer-based Autoregressive Normalizing Flow that already demonstrated NFs can be competitive on image generation, by training across a continuum of noise levels and performing iterative score-based denoising at inference. The autoregressive flow architecture with causal conditioning and permutation layers is inherited directly from the TARFlow/IAF lineage (Kingma et al., 2016a; Rezende & Mohamed, 2015), while the multi-noise training objective and ODE-based denoising procedure borrow structural ideas from diffusion models (EDM, DDIM, Flow Matching) without abandoning the exact-likelihood NF training paradigm. The paper also positions itself relative to STARFlow (Gu et al., 2025a) and FARMER (Zheng et al., 2025), which addressed the noise dilemma through VAE decoders or curriculum schedules, by showing that amortized multi-noise training with iterative denoising is a more unified and flexible solution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

iTARFlow substantially narrows the performance gap between normalizing flows and state-of-the-art diffusion/autoregressive models (e.g., FID 3.32 vs. 1.38 for SiD2 on ImageNet-256 pixel space), and the demonstrated compatibility with pretrained DiT denoisers opens a path toward unified hybrid AR+diffusion architectures for continuous tokens. The characterization of the noise dilemma—where higher training noise improves global structure but degrades fine detail via Tweedie's lemma—provides a useful diagnostic for NF practitioners. Key open questions remain: the lack of theory for selecting optimal noise schedules (t_min, t_max), the GPU memory cost of full-network autograd during denoising, and the unresolved failure modes (collapsed backgrounds, first-token blur) that cap high-resolution performance.
