## Problem

Existing world models (Dreamer, DINO-WM, TD-MPC2) learn high-dimensional latent representations that entangle perceptual factors irrelevant to control, yielding brittle policies and poor generalization across tasks. The paper asks how an agent can learn world models whose latent states are simultaneously minimal (containing no redundant factors) and sufficient (capturing all control-relevant information) for a given task, and how such representations can be discovered through interaction rather than assumed a priori.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261124/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images and proprioceptive state from MuJoCo-based simulators (DMControl, Meta-World, RoboSuite, Franka-Kitchen) along with continuous actions and an optional task descriptor (a RoBERTa-encoded instruction or scalar reward signal). It produces a soft-gated mask over the Dreamer-V3 RSSM latent vector that selects a task-specific subspace (the MIST states), a control policy conditioned exclusively on those masked states, and a structured world model whose reward head reads only the reward's parent variables. The latent dimensionality is expandable: it grows by a fixed increment (Δd=32) when transitioning to a new task, with previously learned coordinates preserved and new ones randomly initialized.

## Method

MIST-WM couples two interleaved stages in a closed loop. On the world-model side, it builds on Dreamer-V3's RSSM and adds a learnable soft mask m^i (via a gated-sparse-autoencoder mechanism) that selects a task-specific subspace, trained with two objectives: a sufficiency score that maximizes reward likelihood from the masked states, and a minimality score that maximizes mutual information (estimated via MINE) between masked states and a task descriptor while penalizing the mask's L1 norm. On the agent side, it learns a skill library via MISL (DIAYN or METRA) and then refines skill selection with an InfoNCE contrastive loss that rewards skills inducing highly separable observation segments for different latent-factor values, thereby curating data that exposes task-relevant structure. An adaptive curriculum (UED-style) selects which environment to probe next by maximizing ensemble-disagreement error, progressively reducing worst-case world-model uncertainty. The key architectural distinction from Dreamer is that the policy and value function operate only on the masked MIST subspace rather than the full latent, and the encoder is frozen during structure learning so that the mask, not the representation, is what adapts per task.

## Results

On the RoboSuite Stacking representation-probing benchmark, MIST-WM achieves an average R² of 0.78 in recovering ground-truth latent factors, substantially outperforming the next-best baseline METRA (0.59) and Denoised MDP (0.56). On single-task policy learning across Meta-World and DMControl, MIST-WM consistently improves sample efficiency over Dreamer-V3, DINO-WM, Factored Dreamer, and TD-MPC2 on most tasks (the sole exception being Reacher-Hard, where TD-MPC2's model-free objective gives it an edge). On generalization tasks (skill transfer in RoboSuite/Kitchen, compositional transfer in Meta-World, and unseen-task transfer), MIST-WM achieves the highest success rates across all settings, with particularly pronounced gains on harder long-horizon Kitchen tasks and the door-open compositional target.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MIST-WM (R²=0.78) outperforms all eight baselines on the representation-probing task, with the largest gaps over Random (0.31) and DINO-WM (0.40), and a clear margin over the strongest data-collection baseline METRA (0.59) and the strongest world-model baseline Denoised MDP (0.56). On single-task policy learning, MIST-WM beats Dreamer-V3, DINO-WM, and Factored Dreamer on most Meta-World and DMControl tasks; TD-MPC2 is competitive on locomotion (Cheetah, Walker) but MIST-WM still exceeds Dreamer-based baselines, while TD-MPC2 wins on Reacher-Hard due to its model-free control objective. On skill generalization (RoboSuite stacking, Franka Kitchen MT-1/MT-2), MIST-WM achieves the highest success rates, with especially large gains on harder Kitchen sequences. On compositional generalization (Meta-World door-open, drawer-close), MIST-WM outperforms all baselines on both source and target tasks. On unseen-task generalization (coffee-push), MIST-WM generalizes well while baselines degrade, confirming that the learned MIST representations capture transferable structure. Ablations confirm that removing any single component (MI term, masking, sufficiency, curriculum, or probing skills) degrades performance, with the curriculum having the largest impact on generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

MIST-WM builds on Dreamer-V3's RSSM as its world-model backbone, MISL-based skill learning (DIAYN, METRA) for active probing, gated sparse autoencoders for the masking mechanism, and UED-style curricula for task ordering. Conceptually, it departs from prior structured world-model work (I-Factor, Denoised MDP, factored MDP recovery) by not learning full graph structures or generic controllable representations, but instead targeting a task-specific minimal sufficient subspace via a formal MIST definition grounded in factored POMDPs with expandable state spaces. The key architectural departure is the closed-loop coupling: agentic exploration curates data that exposes task-relevant latent factors, while structure-learning objectives (sufficiency + minimality scores) distill the MIST subspace, which in turn guides subsequent exploration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, end-to-end recipe for what data to collect, how to structure the world model, and how to generalize across tasks via representation reuse, addressing a gap where prior world models retain control-irrelevant perceptual factors that limit generalization. The expandable state space and sequential task formulation offer a path toward continual world-model learning, though the current evaluation is confined to simulated benchmarks (DMControl, Meta-World, RoboSuite, Franka Kitchen) and relies on a Dreamer-V3 backbone. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to real robotic platforms, scales to diffusion-based or foundation-model world models, and whether the soft-mask surrogate faithfully recovers the formal DBN-defined MIST variables in more complex, high-dimensional settings.
