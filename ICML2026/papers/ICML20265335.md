## Problem

In long-horizon agentic RL with GRPO, credit assignment is intractable because environment rewards are sparse (non-zero only at task completion), and existing reward-shaping approaches fail to simultaneously provide semantic expressiveness, evaluation consistency, and computational efficiency. Rule-based heuristics are rigid and hackable, while LLM-as-a-Judge evaluators introduce intra-group inconsistency, reasoning-answer misalignment, and prohibitive token costs that destabilize the relative advantage estimation that GRPO depends on.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a group of G agent trajectories (each a sequence of natural-language observations and actions from a POMDP environment) along with the task goal description. It produces per-step scalar rewards at the granularity of each environment interaction (not token-level), which are then normalized across all valid steps in the group to compute GRPO advantages. A key structural detail is that the LTL specification is synthesized once per group and broadcast to all trajectories, so the same deterministic automaton evaluates every trace under an identical logical standard.

## Method

GLARE decouples semantic understanding from credit assignment into two stages. First, a Symbolic Grounding module extracts atomic propositions from each trajectory step: actions are mapped via rule-based string matching, while observations are parsed by a lightweight LLM into subject-category-value triples that feed an explicit belief-state tracker using a Markovian overwrite rule, avoiding the need to re-process full history. Second, a Logic-Guided Critic performs dual-track group-level analysis—identifying milestone dependencies and categorizing bad behaviors—which a translator converts into positive LTL (nested F operators for ordered milestones) and negative LTL (G-based safety constraints) formulas. These are compiled via the Spot library into a Deterministic Büchi Automaton and independent safety monitors, executed through BDDs to assign dense step-level rewards: milestone rewards for progressive automaton transitions, trend-shaping between milestones, and violation penalties. The core insight is that sharing a single deterministic automaton across all group trajectories guarantees intra-group reward consistency, which the paper proves (Appendix E) is necessary to prevent rank reversals in GRPO's relative advantage estimation.

## Results

On ALFWorld, GLARE achieves 84.5% overall success rate with Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct and 86.7% with Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, outperforming standard GRPO (sparse rewards) by +12.1% and +11.0% respectively. Compared to the strongest LLM-as-a-Judge baseline (trace-level direct judge), GLARE gains +8.1% (1.5B) and +4.3% (7B) while consuming only 15% of the judge's completion tokens. On WebShop, GLARE reaches 63.4% success rate (score 80.2), surpassing both GRPO (56.8%) and the LLM-judge baseline (61.2%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GLARE consistently outperforms all baselines on ALFWorld. Against standard GRPO with sparse outcome rewards, GLARE gains +12.1% (1.5B) and +11.0% (7B), confirming that dense neuro-symbolic signals substantially improve credit assignment. Against the trace-level LLM-as-a-Judge baseline (the strongest direct LLM evaluation), GLARE achieves +8.1% (1.5B) and +4.3% (7B) while using only 15% of the judge's token budget; the paper also notes that the trace-level judge suffers performance collapse after step 100 in the 7B setting, whereas GLARE remains stable. On WebShop, GLARE improves over both GRPO (+6.6% success) and the LLM-judge baseline (+2.2% success). In the perturbed-observation comparison with GiGPO, GLARE retains 83.3% success while GiGPO drops from 86.7% to 71.4%, demonstrating GLARE's robustness to observation-level variation since it does not rely on exact state matching. GLARE also outperforms PPO (with critic) and RLOO by a wide margin on both model scales, though these baselines use fundamentally different reward structures.

## Relation to Prior Work

GLARE builds on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) as its optimization backbone but replaces the reward signal with a neuro-symbolic pipeline that decouples semantic extraction (via a lightweight LLM) from credit assignment (via deterministic LTL-to-automaton compilation). This departs from the dominant LLM-as-a-Judge paradigm (Gu et al., 2024) by arguing that stochastic neural scoring introduces intra-group variance that destabilizes relative advantage estimation, and from classical LTL/automata-based reward shaping in robotics (Icarte et al., 2018; Li et al., 2017) by grounding the atomic propositions in open-ended natural language observations rather than hand-specified predicates. The framework also positions itself against GiGPO (Feng et al., 2025), which achieves dense rewards through exact observation matching, by offering a state-anchor-free alternative robust to observation perturbation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, low-cost alternative to expensive LLM judges for dense reward shaping in long-horizon agentic RL, with the 15% computational cost and 8.1% performance gain over trace-level judges being practically significant for scaling agent training. The theoretical analysis (Appendix E) formalizing why intra-group consistency matters more than absolute reward accuracy for GRPO is a useful conceptual contribution. However, the framework's applicability is constrained to object-centric environments where observations can be reliably symbolized into event-object relations, and the 12% LTL translation mismatch rate suggests the semantic-to-logic bridge remains a bottleneck. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-object-centric tasks (e.g., dialogue, code generation with side effects) and whether targeted fine-tuning of the AP extractor and LTL translator can close the remaining gap.
