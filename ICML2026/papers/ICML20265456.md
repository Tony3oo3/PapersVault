## Problem

Symmetric multimodal-to-multimodal (MM2MM) retrieval—where query and context are semantically interchangeable—remains unexplored because existing universal multimodal retrieval methods are trained on labeled asymmetric datasets that do not capture this task structure. High-quality positive/hard-negative pairs for symmetric retrieval are prohibitively expensive to annotate, creating a fundamental data bottleneck that stalls progress in this task.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265456/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an image-text pair (I, T) as input and produces a single 768-dimensional joint embedding vector via a late-fusion architecture: unimodal encoders (DINOv2 or CLIP ViT for vision; BGE-m3 or CLIP text for language) feed patch/token features through MLP adapters into a 3-layer cross-attention VL encoder, whose [CLS] output is the final embedding. At inference, the pipeline is a single forward pass with zero overhead from the training-time masking, clustering, or thresholding machinery, achieving 520–922 FPS depending on backbone.

## Method

SOLAR is a two-stage self-supervised framework that exploits the symmetry of the task to generate its own training signal from unlabeled image-text pairs. Stage 1 learns an intersection mask that disentangles shared (intersection) from modality-specific (difference) information: a Global-to-Local Alignment loss produces a similarity signal separating the two sets, adaptive QDA thresholding converts it into a binary mask, and an evolutionary annealing schedule (all-ones → hard mask) ensures training stability; a Masked ITC loss aligns the intersection while a Global Distillation loss from teacher encoders (DINOv2, BGE-m3) preserves the difference. Stage 2 leverages the learned mask to automatically construct positive samples by masking the intersection (recoverable from the other modality) and hard-negative samples by masking the difference (irrecoverable information loss), with image masking performed via iterative hierarchical clustering into semantic segments; a contrastive loss over these constructed samples, in-batch negatives, and offline-mined hard negatives trains the final discriminative joint embedding.

## Results

On the sym-MM2MM benchmark, SOLAR-C (CLIP backbone) achieves the best overall score of 87.69 (Avg. of mR and Precision), with R@1=77.57, mR=90.81, and Precision=84.58. This surpasses the strongest VLM-based baseline, mmE5 (10.12B params), by 7.08 points on Avg., while using over 50× fewer parameters (0.20B) and a 5× smaller embedding dimension (768 vs. 4096). SOLAR-B+D (BGE-m3 + DINOv2 backbone) also outperforms all baselines with an Avg. of 86.53 and Precision of 85.51. Inference speed is dramatically higher: SOLAR-C runs at 922 FPS versus 4 FPS for mmE5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SOLAR-C outperforms all ten baselines on the Avg. metric, with the largest margin over the strongest VLM-based method mmE5 (87.69 vs. 80.61, +7.08 points) and the supervised encoder-based CLIP-SF (87.69 vs. 77.96, +9.73 points). On the more discriminative R@1 metric, SOLAR-C leads mmE5 by 19.63 points (77.57 vs. 57.94), indicating substantially better fine-grained ranking. SOLAR-B+D achieves the highest Precision (85.51) among all methods, exceeding mmE5's 76.64 by 8.87 points, confirming superior ability to separate positives from hard negatives. Notably, SOLAR-C improves upon its own zero-shot backbone (CLIP-SF-ZS, 75.62) by 12.07 points, whereas supervised fine-tuning of the same backbone (CLIP-SF) yields only a 2.34-point gain, demonstrating that task-aligned self-supervision is far more effective than generic supervised fine-tuning for symmetric retrieval. All VLM-based baselines (7.49–10.12B params, 3584–4096 dim, 4–19 FPS) are dominated by SOLAR-C on both accuracy and efficiency (0.20B params, 768 dim, 922 FPS). The only metric where a baseline is competitive is R@10, where mmE5 (98.13) slightly edges SOLAR-C (97.66), but this gap vanishes on R@1 and Precision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by repurposing the self-supervised contrastive learning paradigm (as in CLIP and DINO) for a specific retrieval task structure, rather than building on a single prior method. It synthesizes ideas from late-fusion multimodal architectures (UniIR, MagicLens), knowledge distillation (DINOv2, BGE-m3 as teachers), and masked autoencoding, but introduces a novel disentanglement mechanism—learning an 'intersection mask' via QDA-based adaptive thresholding and evolutionary annealing—that has no direct precedent in multimodal retrieval. The key conceptual departure from prior work is exploiting the symmetry of the task itself to generate supervision from unlabeled image-text pairs, bypassing the annotation bottleneck that constrains all existing universal multimodal retrievers (UniIR, VLM2Vec, MM-Embed, mmE5, etc.).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that task-aligned self-supervision on 800K unlabeled pairs can outperform 10B-parameter supervised VLMs by 7+ points with 50× fewer parameters, suggesting that exploiting task structure (symmetry) is more effective than brute-force scale for this retrieval paradigm. However, the evaluation benchmark contains only 214 human-verified triplets, which limits statistical confidence and generalizability claims. An open question is whether the disentanglement-based data generation strategy transfers to other symmetric or compositional retrieval settings, and whether the approach scales to larger, more diverse benchmarks. The small benchmark also makes it difficult to assess whether the 7-point gap over mmE5 reflects a fundamental advantage or is partly an artifact of baselines being fine-tuned on asymmetric tasks.
