## Problem

Traditional Reservoir Computing (RC) inherits two critical bottlenecks from RNNs: temporal data must be processed sequentially, preventing parallelization on modern accelerators, and scaling reservoirs to high dimensions is impractical due to the O(N_h²) memory cost of dense transition matrices. While structured RC variants have partially addressed one issue or the other, no prior approach simultaneously enables parallel temporal processing and memory-efficient high-dimensional reservoirs while preserving the theoretical guarantees (Echo State Property, universality in fading memory filters) of standard ESNs.

## Input / Output

The model consumes univariate or multivariate time series (e.g., 1-D chaotic signals, 7-feature ETT datasets) or 1-D pixel sequences (flattened 28×28 MNIST digits of length 784). For forecasting and regression, it produces predicted values at specified future horizons; for classification, it outputs a class label derived solely from the hidden state at the final time step. Reservoir states are standardized to zero mean and unit variance before the readout, which aggregates mixed states from all layers in deep configurations.

## Method

ParalESN replaces the dense N_h × N_h transition matrix of a standard ESN with a diagonal complex-valued matrix Λ_h, where each entry λ_i = ρ_i e^{iθ_i} is initialized with magnitude in [ρ_min, ρ_max] and phase in [θ_min, θ_max]. Because the recurrence is linear and diagonal, all time steps are computed in parallel via an associative scan, reducing temporal complexity from O(T) to O(log T), while parameter count drops from O(N_h²) to O(N_h). A 1-D convolutional mixing layer with tanh non-linearity is applied after each reservoir step to introduce non-linearity and enable interaction among otherwise independent diagonal components; in deep configurations, subsequent layers use a ring-topology input weight matrix storing only N_h scaling coefficients. The only trainable component is the readout (ridge regression or 2-layer MLP), and the paper proves the Echo State Property holds iff |λ_i| < 1 for all i, establishing universality in the class of fading memory filters.

## Results

On sMNIST, ParalESN achieves 96.2% accuracy with only 2.7 min training time, outperforming ESN (82.5%) and ESN(deep) (91.4%) while matching or approaching fully-trainable models (LSTM 97.5%, Transformer 98.4%, S4 99.2%) at a fraction of their computational cost. On psMNIST, ParalESN reaches 96.9% accuracy vs. 78.2% for ESN. On time series forecasting, ParalESN is competitive with ESN across all benchmarks (e.g., ETTm2: 5.1×10⁻¹ vs. 9.9×10⁻¹), while training an order of magnitude faster. On LRA, ParalESN(deep) achieves 63.7% average accuracy, competitive with RWKV (67.7%) but below SSM-based models (S5: 84.7%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ParalESN decisively outperforms traditional ESN and ESN(deep) on both accuracy and speed: on sMNIST, accuracy improves from 82.5% to 96.2% (shallow) and 91.4% to 97.2% (deep), while training time drops from 4.3/8.8 min to 2.7/3.3 min. Against fully-trainable sequence models, ParalESN trades a small accuracy gap (1–3% below S4, LRU, Mamba, Transformer, LSTM on sMNIST) for a 5–50× reduction in training time and energy. On structured RC baselines (SCR, Structured RC), ParalESN wins by an entire order of magnitude on forecasting tasks (e.g., NARMA10: 3.7×10⁻² vs. 11.1×10⁻² for SCR and 18.7×10⁻² for Structured RC). On LRA, ParalESN(deep) (63.7% avg) is competitive with RWKV (67.7%) but trails SSM-based models (S4: 82.3%, S5: 84.7%) and LRU (81.6%), indicating that untrained diagonal recurrence captures long-range dependencies less effectively than trained selective SSMs. Overall, ParalESN positions itself as the fastest RC variant with accuracy competitive with fully-trainable models, making it a strong efficiency-accuracy trade-off for temporal processing.

## Relation to Prior Work

ParalESN sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: the Reservoir Computing paradigm (ESNs, DeepESNs, ResESNs) and the recent wave of structured linear recurrences from State Space Models (S4, LRU, Mamba). The core architectural move is to transplant LRU's diagonal complex-valued transition matrix—originally designed for fully-trainable RNNs—into the untrained-reservoir setting, adding a lightweight 1-D convolutional mixing layer to restore non-linearity and inter-component interaction. The ring topology for deeper-layer input matrices is borrowed from SCR, and the parallel temporal processing relies on the associative scan algorithm of Martin & Cundy. The theoretical contribution extends the universality results of Grigoryeva & Ortega to the diagonal case, showing that the Echo State Property and fading-memory-filter universality are preserved without requiring a dense transition matrix.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two practical bottlenecks that have limited RC adoption: sequential processing (now logarithmic in sequence length via associative scan) and quadratic memory scaling of dense transition matrices (now linear). The orders-of-magnitude parameter reduction (≈2K vs ≈1.1M for 1024 neurons) enables reservoirs that were previously infeasible, and the theoretical guarantees (ESP, universality) give the RC community confidence that the diagonal structure is not merely a heuristic. Open questions include whether the untrained dynamics can close the gap to fully-trainable SSMs on long-range tasks (LRA results show a persistent gap to S4/S5), whether the mixing layer can be made adaptive or input-dependent (à la Mamba's selectivity), and how the approach behaves at truly large sequence lengths where the associative scan's constant factors matter.
