## Problem

Pairwise preference optimization methods like DPO fundamentally require annotated (preferred, rejected) pairs, but in practice—especially for visual generative models—feedback is naturally collected as unpaired scalar scores (e.g., reward-model outputs or star ratings). Converting scalar scores into pairs is ad hoc, discards absolute-scale information, and amplifies noise when scores cluster tightly, creating a mismatch between the pairwise assumptions of DPO-style objectives and the scalar nature of real-world feedback.

## Input / Output

The method consumes text prompts paired with independently generated images (or videos) and their corresponding scalar scores from a reward model or human annotator—no explicit preference pairs are needed. The global threshold τ is computed as a percentile (default: median) of the empirical score distribution, and each sample receives a pseudo-label (above/below τ) plus a confidence weight proportional to its distance from τ. For diffusion backbones the log-likelihood is approximated via scaled negative MSE; for MaskGIT-style models it is the mean cross-entropy over VQ-GAN token predictions at masked positions.

## Method

The paper shows that the KL-regularized optimal policy increases a sample's probability if and only if its reward exceeds an instance-dependent oracle baseline τ*(x) = β log Z(x), which is intractable because Z(x) sums over all possible outputs. TGO replaces this oracle with a single global, data-driven threshold τ estimated from the empirical score distribution, converting alignment into a binary classification on the implicit policy score β log(π_θ(y|x)/π_ref(y|x)). The resulting loss is a weighted logistic classification where the weight w(s,τ) = 1 + c|s−τ| emphasizes samples whose scores deviate strongly from the threshold, improving sample efficiency. The framework is instantiated for both diffusion models (Gaussian-observation MSE surrogate for log-likelihood) and MaskGIT masked-token models (cross-entropy), and is trained offline on pre-scored datasets with a fixed reference policy.

## Results

On SD v1.5 fine-tuned with Pick-a-Pic v2, TGO achieves the best scores across all three test sets and five reward models. On the HPSv2 test set, TGO reaches 0.2961 HPSv2.1, 30.38 CLIPScore, and 0.7595 ImageReward, outperforming the strongest baseline Diffusion-KTO (0.2873, 30.02, 0.7365). On the 10k scalar-feedback prompts (Table 2), TGO improves HPSv2.1 from 0.2454 (SD v1.4) to 0.2618 and ImageReward from 0.1406 to 0.3523 for diffusion, and from 0.2810 to 0.2915 and 0.8230 to 0.9369 for MaskGIT (Meissonic). For text-to-video with Wan 1.3B, TGO-LoRA achieves an overall VideoReward of -1.1757, surpassing KTO-LoRA (-1.3703) and SFT-LoRA (-1.6918).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TGO consistently outperforms all five baselines across the three test sets. Against the strongest baseline Diffusion-KTO, TGO gains +0.0046 HPSv2.1 and +0.0322 ImageReward on Pick-a-Pic, +0.0033 HPSv2.1 and +0.0305 ImageReward on PartiPrompts, and +0.0088 HPSv2.1 and +0.0230 ImageReward on HPSv2. Diffusion-DPO is beaten by TGO on every metric across all three test sets (e.g., +0.27 PickScore and +0.3071 ImageReward on HPSv2). CSFT is outperformed by TGO on all metrics, with the largest gap on ImageReward (e.g., 0.7595 vs. 0.3703 on HPSv2). Notably, both AlignProp and DSPO produce scores identical to the unmodified SD v1.5 baseline, suggesting they did not yield meaningful improvements under the reported training protocol, making TGO's gains over them particularly large.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the KL-regularized alignment objective underlying DPO and its diffusion-model adaptations (Diffusion-DPO, Diffusion-KTO), but departs from their fundamental reliance on paired preference data. The key conceptual departure is identifying the intractable instance-specific baseline τ*(x) = β log Z(x) in the KL-optimal decision rule and replacing it with a global data-driven threshold, yielding a binary classification loss on unpaired scalar scores. This positions TGO alongside QRPO (quantile-based scalar reward fitting) and Abdolmaleki et al.'s EM-based unpaired framework, but uses a simpler thresholding mechanism with confidence weighting rather than quantile transformations or iterative inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that many real-world feedback signals for visual generation (user ratings, reward model outputs) are naturally scalar and unpaired, yet prior methods either require explicit pairs or ad hoc pair construction that discards absolute score information. The principled derivation from the KL-optimal rule gives the threshold a theoretical justification beyond heuristic binning, and the consistent gains across diffusion, MaskGIT, and video paradigms suggest broad applicability. Open questions include whether prompt-conditional thresholds could further improve performance on heterogeneous score distributions, and whether the method extends to online or iterative alignment settings where the score distribution shifts during training.
