## Problem

Existing Geo-Foundation Models for spatial representation learning (e.g., GeoCLIP, SatCLIP, GAIR) support only one or two modalities and rely on pairwise contrastive alignment, which captures redundant information between modalities while neglecting modality-specific (unique) and cross-modal (synergistic) signals. Moreover, these methods require all modalities to be present at every location, limiting scalability to heterogeneous, partially available geospatial datasets. No prior model flexibly fuses street view imagery, satellite data, cartographic basemaps, and POIs into a single unified spatial embedding.

## Input / Output

The model accepts any arbitrary subset of five modalities per location: geographic coordinates (Equal Earth projection + multi-scale Random Fourier Features through an MLP), street view images (encoded by frozen CLIP ViT-L/14), 12-channel Sentinel-2 multispectral patches (frozen ViT-S/16), OSM cartographic basemaps at 300 m / 600 m / 1200 m resolutions (frozen MAE-pretrained ViT-B/16), and the 15 nearest POIs within 200 m rendered as textual prompts (frozen BAAI/bge-small-en-v1.5). All modality encoders are frozen; only the fusion Transformer and two lightweight decoder heads are trained. The output is a single 768-dimensional fused spatial representation vector z_i, usable for any downstream regression or classification task via linear probing or small MLPs.

## Method

UrbanFusion introduces Stochastic Multimodal Fusion (SMF), a training framework that randomly masks a subset M_i of available modalities and passes both M_i and its complement M̄_i through a single-block Transformer (768-dim, 8 heads, learned positional encodings) with average pooling. Two complementary losses are optimized jointly: (1) a symmetric InfoNCE contrastive loss aligning the fused embeddings of M_i and M̄_i (Contrastive Location Alignment), and (2) a latent reconstruction loss that forces the fused representation to predict the z-score-normalized latent features h_m of every modality m via modality-specific projection heads. The total loss is L_total = (1−λ)·L_contr + λ·L_recon. The key insight is that random masking combined with full-modality reconstruction compels the Transformer to retain not only the redundant geolocalization signal captured by contrastive alignment but also unique modality-specific content and synergistic cross-modal interactions, as formalized via a Partial Information Decomposition argument (Lemma 1). At inference time, the model accepts any subset of modalities, enabling zero-shot cross-regional generalization using only non-coordinate inputs.

## Results

On coordinate-only spatial encoding (Table 2), UrbanFusion achieves the best R² on 5 of 8 tasks when comparing models trained on the same PP2-M dataset, including 87.4% on Crime Incidence, 74.3% on ZIP Code-level indicators (beating Google's PDFM at 74.0%), and 56.9% F1 on Land Cover. In the multimodal setting (Table 3), UrbanFusion leads on 4 of 6 tasks, with 88.5% R² on Crime and 75.1% on ZIP Code. For cross-regional generalization to unseen cities (Table 4), UrbanFusion ranks first on 5 of 6 tasks, achieving 76.7% R² on Crime and 70.9% F1 on Land Cover, substantially outperforming single-modal baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GAIR (the closest multimodal competitor), UrbanFusion wins on 6 of 8 coordinate-only tasks and 4 of 6 multimodal tasks, losing only on Land Use–Fine (47.7 vs. 49.9 F1) and Land Use–Coarse in the multimodal setting (59.3 vs. 61.7). GeoCLIP trained on MP-16 (~65× more locations) remains competitive, achieving the best score on Urban Perception (19.2 vs. 18.8 R²) and Land Cover (69.1 vs. 65.6 F1) in the multimodal setting, but UrbanFusion trained on the much smaller PP2-M dataset outperforms GeoCLIP-PP2-M on nearly all tasks. SatCLIP is consistently outperformed across all three evaluation settings, with the gap attributed to Spherical Harmonics' inability to capture high-frequency urban variation. GPS2Vec (visual) narrowly beats UrbanFusion on Housing Prices (79.2 vs. 78.7 R²) and Energy Consumption (22.3 vs. 20.1 R²), likely benefiting from denser local coverage. UrbanFusion surpasses Google's PDFM on the ZIP Code-level benchmark (74.3 vs. 74.0 R² coordinate-only; 75.1 vs. 74.0 multimodal), despite PDFM being purpose-built for that task and evaluated on in-sample locations. In cross-regional generalization, UrbanFusion's multimodal advantage is most pronounced, outperforming GAIR by 8.7 R² points on Crime and 12.3 F1 points on Land Cover.

## Relation to Prior Work

UrbanFusion extends the CLIP-style contrastive spatial representation learning lineage (GeoCLIP, SatCLIP, CSP, GAIR) by replacing pairwise coordinate-image alignment with a subset-complement contrastive objective (Stochastic Multimodal Fusion) augmented by latent modality reconstruction. While GAIR was the first to handle multiple modalities, it still relied on pairwise contrastive loss and required all modalities to be present; UrbanFusion generalizes to arbitrary modality subsets during both training and inference via random masking, and explicitly targets redundant, unique, and synergistic information as formalized by Partial Information Decomposition. The architecture reuses frozen pretrained encoders (CLIP ViT-L/14 for street view, ViT-S/16 for Sentinel-2, MAE-pretrained ViT-B/16 for OSM maps, BGE for POI text) and a single-block Transformer for fusion, keeping the novelty concentrated in the training objective rather than the encoder stack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UrbanFusion is the first location-embedding model to natively fuse four heterogeneous geospatial modalities (street view, satellite, cartographic basemaps, POIs) into a single task-agnostic representation while tolerating missing modalities, making it directly applicable to the heterogeneous data availability that characterizes real-world urban analytics. The SMF framework is model-agnostic and potentially transferable to other multimodal domains beyond geospatial, and the released PP2-M dataset (73k locations, 56 cities) provides a reproducible benchmark. Key open questions include whether the approach scales to temporal data (e.g., satellite sequences for land-use change), whether it generalizes to rural or non-urban scales, and whether end-to-end fine-tuning of the frozen encoders would close the remaining gap to models trained on 65× more data (GeoCLIP on MP-16).
