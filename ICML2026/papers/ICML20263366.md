## Problem

Reconstructing gridded rain fields from Commercial Microwave Links (CMLs) is an ill-posed inverse problem because CMLs produce path-integrated, nonlinear attenuation measurements rather than point observations. Existing reconstruction methods collapse each link into one or a few Virtual Rain Gauges and then interpolate, discarding the line-integral structure and producing overly smooth fields that miss localized extremes. No prior work has combined a learned generative spatial prior with the full nonlinear path-integrated CML operator within a Bayesian inference framework.

## Input / Output

The method consumes path-integrated CML attenuation measurements (one scalar per link), the geometric network topology (link endpoints defining line segments over a 48×36 grid), per-link power-law parameters (a, b) and noise levels σ_i, and a pre-trained diffusion model prior. It outputs an ensemble of posterior rain-rate field samples on the 48×36 grid, from which pointwise quantiles and ensemble statistics can be derived. Unlike interpolation baselines that yield a single deterministic field, the diffusion-based approach produces multiple plausible reconstructions, enabling uncertainty quantification relevant to flood-warning applications.

## Method

The paper formulates CML rain-field reconstruction as a Bayesian inverse problem where the likelihood is the nonlinear path-integrated power-law operator M(x₀)_i = ⟨aΔⁱ, x₀ᵇ⟩ (computed via Siddon's ray-tracing for cell intersection lengths) and the prior is a pre-trained EDM diffusion model (SongUNet backbone, trained on 21,196 radar-derived rain fields from OpenMRG). At inference, the trained DM is reused as a fixed spatial prior without any fine-tuning, and posterior samples are drawn using training-free samplers spanning Plug-and-Play (DPS, DAPS), variational (REDDIFF, MGPS, MGDM), Sequential Monte Carlo (TDS), and Replica Exchange (CREPE) families. The key technical insight is that while the operator is nonlinear in x₀, it is linear in the transformed variable u = x₀ᵇ, and the DM prior captures the intermittent, multi-scale, and skewed statistics of real rainfall far better than a censored Gaussian Process baseline. Ablations confirm that both the expressive DM prior and accurate modeling of the nonlinear power-law exponent b are necessary for strong reconstruction quality.

## Results

On the OpenMRG real-world CML dataset, diffusion-model-based reconstruction methods consistently outperform meteorological baselines across RMSE, PCC, and cumulative rainfall error under both isotropic and heteroscedastic noise settings. MGPS achieves the best overall performance, with RMSE of 0.83±0.08 and PCC of 0.26±0.01 under heteroscedastic noise, while TDS attains the lowest RMSE (0.81±0.09) in the same setting. For cumulative rainfall error (closer to zero is better), MGPS yields +70.94±36.40 mm under heteroscedastic noise, substantially outperforming all other methods. On the controlled GP benchmark, TDS achieves the best Sliced Wasserstein distance (0.07) and most accurate posterior quantiles.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares six training-free diffusion posterior sampling methods (DAPS, DPS, MGDM, MGPS, REDDIFF, TDS) against three meteorological interpolation baselines (IDW, GMZ, OK) on the OpenMRG CML dataset. MGPS emerges as the strongest overall DM method, achieving the best PCC (0.26) and the smallest cumulative rainfall bias (+71 mm) under heteroscedastic noise, while TDS attains the lowest RMSE (0.81). All DM methods outperform the meteorological baselines on PCC, with MGPS and TDS exceeding OK (0.24) and IDW/GMZ (0.21–0.24). Among meteorological methods, OK is the strongest, but it still lags behind the best DM methods on PCC and cumulative rain accuracy. GMZ performs relatively well on cumulative rainfall (−65 to −82 mm) among meteorological baselines, but its RMSE (0.91–0.92) is the worst across all methods. The ablation (Table 5) further shows that the nonlinear power-law observation model is essential: under a linear operator assumption, even the strong DM prior degrades substantially, confirming that both the expressive prior and the physically grounded measurement model contribute to the gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-models-for-inverse-problems paradigm (Chung et al., 2023; Mardani et al., 2024; Moufad et al., 2025) to the specific domain of CML-based rainfall reconstruction, building on the EDM framework (Karras et al., 2022) for the prior and Siddon's ray-tracing (1985) for the measurement operator. It departs from the dominant VRG approximation in the CML literature (Messer et al., 2006; Goldshtein et al., 2009; Eshel et al., 2021) by explicitly modeling the nonlinear, path-integrated power-law observation operator rather than collapsing line integrals to point measurements. The paper also contrasts its DM prior against censored Gaussian Process priors (Bardossy & Plate, 1992; Baxevani & Lennartsson, 2015), showing the DM better captures the intermittent, multi-scale structure of real rainfall fields.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CML networks are already densely deployed for telecom, making this framework practically relevant for near-real-time rainfall monitoring and flood warning without new sensor infrastructure. The ability to generate posterior ensembles (rather than single deterministic maps) enables scenario-based risk assessment, and the ~1-minute runtime for 10 samples is compatible with 5–15 minute CML data intervals. Key open questions include generalization beyond the single Gothenburg region/season, incorporation of temporal structure, and whether the modest but consistent gains over interpolation baselines (RMSE ~0.84 vs ~0.87–0.92) persist under sparser networks or more extreme precipitation events.
