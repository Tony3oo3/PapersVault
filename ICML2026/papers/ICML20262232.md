## Problem

While static protein structure prediction has been largely addressed, no systematic benchmark exists to evaluate how well current models capture functionally relevant conformational dynamics across multiple states. Existing evaluations rarely integrate the three major conformational change mechanisms—intrinsic, ligand-induced, and protein-induced—within a unified framework, and often rely on monomeric-only context rather than biologically relevant native assemblies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262232/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each entry is predicted with 10 independent random seeds generating 10 samples each (100 predictions per entry), using representative sequences from 95%-identity clusters and MSAs retrieved via ColabFold/MMseqs2; multimeric inputs use Boltz-2's internal MSA pairing. Success requires TM-score > 0.8 to the target state AND higher than to all other states in the pair, evaluated at cluster level (intrinsic) or pair level requiring both apo and holo success (induced sets). Conformational bias is quantified at four pipeline stages: final structure (Struct_holo), distogram (DynDisto_holo), training set exposure (Train_holo), and MSA coevolutionary signal (MSA_holo).

## Method

ProMiSE curates 577 conformational pairs from 234 sequence clusters by retrieving RCSB PDB entries at ≥95% sequence identity, performing TM-score-based agglomerative clustering to identify distinct states (TM-score < 0.8), and applying rigorous filtering including crystal artifact removal via PRODIGY-cryst scoring, superset-based redundancy elimination, and binding-site consistency checks. The evaluation framework introduces the first unified success criteria for multi-state sampling and four complementary bias metrics that probe distinct stages of the prediction pipeline. Through controlled analysis holding one bias source neutral while examining the other, the paper identifies the structure module as the primary bottleneck for conformational collapse, with training set memorization (not MSA signals) driving the shift toward dominant conformations. This hypothesis is validated by BioEmu, which uses the same frozen AlphaFold2 pairformer but fine-tunes only the structure decoder on MD trajectory data, producing significantly more diverse structural outputs from identical distogram inputs without altering upstream pair representations.

## Results

On the ProMiSE benchmark, no model achieves high multi-state sampling success across all three conformational change categories. AF3 leads in ligand-induced transitions (50% cluster-level success) and Boltz-2 leads in protein-induced changes (34%), while BioEmu achieves the best intrinsic multi-state sampling (29%). Overall, intrinsic multi-state sampling remains the hardest task, with all models below 30% cluster-level success, and ligand-induced predictions exhibit systematic holo-collapse (apo-conditioned predictions shift toward holo-like conformations). The paper identifies the structure decoding module, constrained by training-set memorization, as the primary bottleneck rather than upstream pair representations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AF3 achieves the highest ligand-induced success (50%) but ties with Boltz-2 at only 18% for intrinsic multi-state, revealing that even the strongest co-folding model struggles with conformational diversity. Boltz-2 outperforms AF3 in protein-induced changes (34% vs. 27%) and is competitive in ligand-induced (42% vs. 50%), but both share the same intrinsic multi-state limitation. Chai-1, despite running without MSA input, achieves balanced performance (40% ligand-induced, 24% intrinsic, 17% protein-induced), suggesting MSA is not the sole driver of conformational bias. BioEmu, which fine-tunes only the structure module on MD trajectories, achieves the best intrinsic multi-state rate (29%) and shows significantly higher within-distogram structural diversity (p < 10⁻⁴ vs. all other models), validating that the structure decoding module is the key bottleneck. Boltz-1 underperforms all other models across categories, with the lowest intrinsic multi-state success, making it the weakest baseline in the comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two prior threads: (1) the growing recognition that current structure prediction models (AlphaFold3, Boltz, Chai-1) systematically fail to sample multiple conformational states, documented in fold-switching protein studies (Chakravarty & Porter, 2022; Wayment-Steele et al., 2024) and protein-ligand co-folding analyses (Masters et al., 2025); and (2) the emerging set of diverse-sampling methods (BioEmu, AlphaFlow, diffusion-based approaches) that attempt to address this limitation. ProMiSE carves new ground by being the first benchmark to unify all three conformational change mechanisms—intrinsic, ligand-induced, and protein-induced—within native biological assemblies, rather than evaluating them in isolation on monomeric inputs. Its diagnostic framework (Struct_holo, DynDisto_holo, Train_holo, MSA_holo) repurposes the ConfBench scoring from NeuralPLexer3 and introduces pipeline-localized bias metrics that go beyond prior single-metric evaluations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of the structure decoding module—not pair representations or MSA processing—as the primary bottleneck for conformational collapse is a practically actionable finding that redirects the field's optimization focus away from trunk-network architectural changes toward structure-module modifications, validated by BioEmu's success with a frozen pairformer. The benchmark's ~20% cluster-level success rate for intrinsic multi-state and the systematic Apo→Holo directional collapse in ligand-induced scenarios establish a clear, quantified gap that future dynamics-aware models must close. Key limitations include the stringent TM-score < 0.8 cutoff (excluding localized but biologically important motions like GPCR TM6 displacement), the inability to handle coupled ligand+protein effects in multimeric assemblies, and the existence-based evaluation that does not assess relative state populations. Open questions include whether structure-module fine-tuning on MD data generalizes to induced conformational changes, and how to incorporate full Boltzmann ensemble statistics into evaluation.
