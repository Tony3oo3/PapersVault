## Problem

Prevailing VLA models face a fundamental architectural tension: autoregressive decoders unify action generation within the VLM backbone but suffer from left-to-right compounding errors and linearly scaling inference cost, while continuous diffusion heads enable parallel generation but are decoupled from the backbone and introduce competing gradients that erode pretrained vision-language capabilities under distribution shift. No existing approach achieves both architectural unification with the VLM and structured, revisable parallel action decoding.

## Input / Output

The model consumes multi-view RGB images (one mandatory third-person head view plus two optional wrist views, resized to 224×224), a natural-language instruction, and optional end-effector proprioceptive positions. It outputs a fixed-length discrete action chunk of L = H × 7 tokens (H timesteps × 3 translation + 3 rotation + 1 binary gripper), where each continuous dimension is bin-quantized into 256 levels. The chunk is decoded iteratively over T = 12 refinement rounds rather than in a single pass, with the decoding order adapting per-instance based on token confidence.

## Method

Discrete Diffusion VLA replaces the autoregressive action head of OpenVLA (Prismatic-7B / Llama 2) with a discrete diffusion decoder that operates on bin-quantized action tokens inside the same transformer backbone. Action tokens use bidirectional attention over the full chunk, and training minimizes cross-entropy on randomly masked positions sampled from a cosine mask-ratio schedule—identical in form to the VLM's language objective, so no competing loss is introduced. At inference, the model starts from a fully masked chunk and iteratively commits the highest-confidence tokens each round (ranked by max-confidence or confidence-gap), using tempered Gumbel-Max sampling with a decaying temperature. A secondary re-masking step applies a monotonically increasing confidence threshold to previously committed tokens, re-masking any that fall below it to prevent early errors from persisting across refinement steps. This yields a 4.7× reduction in forward passes over AR (12 vs. 56 NFEs) while preserving pretrained vision-language priors by construction.

## Results

On LIBERO, Discrete Diffusion VLA achieves 96.4% average success rate (97.2% Spatial, 99.4% Object, 96.8% Goal, 92.2% Long), the best among all discretized action methods and only 0.7% behind the continuous SOTA (OpenVLA-OFT L1 at 97.1%). On SimplerEnv-Fractal, it attains 71.2% Visual Matching and 64.1% overall, setting SOTA across both discrete and continuous methods. On SimplerEnv-Bridge, it achieves 54.2% overall, surpassing π0 by +14.1% and π0-FAST by +5.9%. Under OOD evaluation (LIBERO-Goal), it exhibits only 0.8% language degradation and 20.4% vision degradation, the smallest drops among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Discrete Diffusion VLA outperforms all discretized-action baselines on LIBERO, beating OpenVLA-OFT (Discrete) by +0.9% and π0-FAST by +10.9%, while trailing only the continuous OpenVLA-OFT (L1) by 0.7%—a gap attributable to inherent quantization loss from 256-bin tokenization. On SimplerEnv-Fractal, it achieves SOTA overall (64.1%), surpassing π0-FAST (60.5%), π0 (56.8%), and OpenVLA-OFT (54.3%); on SimplerEnv-Bridge it leads all methods at 54.2%, exceeding π0 by +14.1%, GR00T-N1 by +4.7%, and π0-FAST by +5.9%. Against continuous diffusion baselines (Diffusion Policy, MDT, DiTA), it gains +14.0 to +24.0 points on LIBERO. The OOD comparison is particularly telling: under language augmentation on LIBERO-Goal, Discrete Diffusion VLA degrades only 0.8% versus 8.0% for OpenVLA-OFT (Discrete) and 2.4% for OpenVLA-OFT (Diffusion); under vision augmentation it drops 20.4% versus 29.0% for continuous diffusion, confirming that the unified cross-entropy objective preserves pretrained VLM priors that separate diffusion heads erode. On the real-robot AgileX Cobot Magic platform, it outperforms OpenVLA-OFT (Discrete) by +33.4% on Click Bell and matches π0 on Place Cup, despite running at a lower control frequency (9.69 Hz vs. 24.5–34.3 Hz).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the discrete diffusion lineage (D3PM, MaskGIT) from image and language generation to the action modality in VLA models, while building directly on the OpenVLA/Prismatic-7B architecture. It departs from two dominant VLA paradigms: autoregressive token decoders (OpenVLA, π0-FAST) that commit tokens left-to-right with compounding errors, and continuous diffusion heads (π0, Diffusion Policy) that introduce a separate loss with competing gradients against the VLM's cross-entropy objective. The key conceptual move is performing discrete diffusion over binning-quantized action chunks *inside* the VLM backbone with the same cross-entropy objective, enabling bidirectional attention and adaptive confidence-based decoding without any architectural or training-objective mismatch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the choice of action-head architecture has a disproportionate effect on out-of-distribution robustness (0.8% language degradation vs. 8.0% for parallel decoding; 20.4% vision degradation vs. 29.0% for continuous diffusion), suggesting that in-distribution success rate alone is an insufficient metric for VLA quality. Achieving SOTA on SimplerEnv across both discrete and continuous methods (64.1% Fractal, 54.2% Bridge) while preserving VLM priors by construction rather than regularization offers a clean architectural principle for future unified VLA designs. Open questions include whether more expressive fixed-length tokenization can close the remaining 0.7% gap with continuous SOTA on LIBERO, and whether the approach extends to variable-length action representations (e.g., π0-FAST style) that are currently incompatible with the fixed-chunk discrete diffusion formulation.
