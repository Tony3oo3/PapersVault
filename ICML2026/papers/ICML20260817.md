## Problem

Rationale extraction (RE) uses a select-predict architecture where a generator and predictor must co-train, but this creates a 'chicken and egg' dilemma: the generator needs the predictor's signal to identify important features, while the predictor needs the generator's selections to learn prediction. This dilemma is severely exacerbated when the base neural networks are small or less capable, causing significant accuracy drops compared to full-input classification. The paper asks how to improve RE models built on lightweight backbones without requiring them to discover effective rationales entirely from scratch.

## Input / Output

For text (IMDB), inputs are tokenized to 256 tokens; for vision (CIFAR-10/100), 32×32 images are upscaled to 224×224 and split into 196 patches of 16×16 for ViT. The model produces a binary mask over all features (targeting ~15% for vision, ~10% for text) and a class prediction computed solely from the masked (selected) features, with unselected features zeroed out at the embedding level before the predictor processes them. The rationale ratio is enforced via a squared-length regularization term rather than a hard constraint.

## Method

REKD augments standard RE training with two knowledge-distillation losses from a larger teacher RE model: (1) rationale distillation, which minimizes KL divergence between the teacher's and student's per-feature Gumbel-Softmax distributions, and (2) prediction distillation, which matches temperature-scaled softmax outputs (scaled by τ² to balance gradient magnitudes). The key design choice is synchronizing the KD temperature with the Gumbel-Softmax annealing scheduler (exponential decay from τ₀=5 to τ_K=0.1), which creates an implicit curriculum: at high τ the student absorbs broad, soft feature-importance signals from the teacher, and as τ anneals the student transitions to mimicking sharp, high-confidence discrete selections. The total loss is α·L_RE + (1−α)·L_KD, preserving the student's own RE exploration while injecting teacher guidance. Because the binary feature-selection layer is architecture-agnostic, the method works across any backbone pair (e.g., BERT-base→BERT-mini, ViT-base→ViT-tiny) without requiring matching hidden dimensions.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, REKD improves ViT Small accuracy from 0.889 (RE) to 0.968 and ViT Tiny from 0.797 to 0.936 at a ~15% rationale ratio, closing most of the gap to the ViT Base teacher (0.964). On CIFAR-100, ViT Small improves from 0.779 to 0.845 and ViT Tiny from 0.645 to 0.777. On IMDB, BERT Small improves from 0.881 to 0.906 and BERT Mini from 0.863 to 0.892 at a ~10% rationale ratio, with REKD even exceeding the students' own full-input classification accuracy (0.889 and 0.877 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's sole baseline is the student's own RE model (without knowledge distillation), since REKD is presented as the first KD approach for rationale extraction. REKD consistently outperforms RE across all three datasets and all student model sizes. On CIFAR-10, the gains are largest: ViT Small improves by +0.079 (0.889→0.968) and ViT Tiny by +0.139 (0.797→0.936), with the Small student even slightly exceeding the ViT Base teacher's average RE accuracy (0.968 vs. 0.964). On CIFAR-100, ViT Small gains +0.066 (0.779→0.845) and ViT Tiny gains +0.132 (0.645→0.777). On IMDB, improvements are smaller but still consistent: BERT Small +0.025 (0.881→0.906) and BERT Mini +0.029 (0.863→0.892), where REKD accuracy surpasses the students' own full-input CLS accuracy. The paper also notes that the performance drop from CLS to RE is more severe for smaller models (e.g., ViT Tiny loses 0.171 vs. ViT Base losing only 0.020 on CIFAR-10), confirming that the 'chicken-and-egg' dilemma is most acute for less capable backbones and that REKD is most beneficial in that regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two established lines of work—rationale extraction via the select-predict architecture (Lei et al., 2016; Bastings et al., 2019) and knowledge distillation (Hinton et al., 2015)—by distilling a teacher RE model's Gumbel-Softmax feature-selection distributions and prediction logits into a student RE model. The key structural insight is that the discrete feature-selection mask serves as a neural-model-agnostic interface, sidestepping the architecture-matching requirements of prior feature-based distillation (Romero et al., 2015; Zagoruyko & Komodakis, 2017). The approach also subsumes the supervised RE variant of Jain et al. (2020) as the special case α=0, and differs from annealing KD (Jafari et al., 2021) in that the temperature schedule is a structural requirement of Gumbel-Softmax rather than a heuristic curriculum.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

REKD offers a practical path to deploying interpretable RE models on resource-constrained devices (e.g., mobile healthcare) by letting a small student inherit a large teacher's rationales without requiring architectural compatibility. The ablation cleanly isolating rationale distillation as the critical component (prediction-only distillation collapses to near-chance) validates the core hypothesis that the 'chicken and egg' bottleneck is in feature selection, not prediction. Open questions include whether the method generalizes to cross-architecture distillation (e.g., ViT→ResNet), whether the student-exceeds-teacher effect holds at larger scale, and how to verify rationale quality beyond predictive accuracy in real-world high-stakes settings.
