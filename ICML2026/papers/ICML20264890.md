## Problem

Agentic reinforcement learning (ARL) for LLM-based agents is highly unstable and prone to training collapse, limiting scalability to longer interaction horizons and more complex environments. While individual policy optimization methods have been proposed, no systematic analysis exists to identify which design choices in the policy gradient objective are responsible for instability versus performance, and no unified stable training recipe has been established.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a task prompt, the current environment observation, and the accumulated multi-turn interaction history (capped at 2 turns of context for ALFWorld/WebShop/Sokoban, or 8196 tokens for TIR Math). Each turn's output is a structured text response containing reasoning enclosed in <think> tags followed by an action in <action> tags; format violations incur a fixed reward penalty. Complete K-turn trajectories are segmented into single-turn samples for policy optimization, with the policy model (Qwen3-4B or 8B) updated via the resulting surrogate objective.

## Method

SAMPO (Stable Agentic Multi-turn Policy Optimization) unifies three key design choices into a single objective: (1) sequence-level importance sampling clipping using the geometric-mean ratio s_i(θ) = exp(1/|T_i| Σ log π_θ/π_θ_old) to suppress harmful token-level outliers across the full trajectory; (2) fine-grained advantage estimation combining episode-level group-normalized returns with step-level relative advantages computed over anchor-state groups (inheriting GIGPO's hierarchical structure); and (3) dynamic trajectory filtering that resamples groups where all rollouts receive identical rewards, removing zero-gradient degenerate samples. The paper's broader contribution is ARLArena, a standardized testbed (behavior cloning initialization, format penalty, KL regularization, per-method hyperparameter tuning) and a four-dimensional decomposition of policy gradient (loss aggregation, IS clipping, trajectory filtering, advantage design) that isolates each factor's effect on stability and performance across ALFWorld, WebShop, Sokoban, and TIR Math.

## Results

SAMPO achieves an average score of 60.21 across four agentic tasks, representing a 25.2% improvement over the GRPO baseline (46.16). On ALFWorld, SAMPO reaches 92.72% success rate (vs. 62.36% for GRPO), on WebShop 77.73% success (vs. 57.71%), and on Sokoban 88.86% success (vs. 83.90%). The strongest single-dimension baseline, GSPO (sequence-level clipping), achieves 52.28 average, while SAMPO's multi-dimensional combination yields a further 15.4% gain over it.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAMPO outperforms all single-dimension baselines: it surpasses the strongest IS method GSPO (52.28 avg) by 15.4%, the best advantage-design method GIGPO (49.71) by 21.2%, and the best dynamic-sampling variant DAPO_GIGPO (53.36) by 12.8%. Tolerant-clipping methods CISPO (34.03) and SAPO (32.22) suffer severe training collapse, with SAPO dropping 78.4% on ALFWorld and 104% on Sokoban relative to GRPO. Against closed-source models, SAMPO-trained Qwen3-4B achieves 92.72% ALFWorld success, substantially exceeding GPT-5.2 (51.56%), o3-based multi-agent systems (56.25%), and Gemini 2.5 Pro (66.41%). On WebShop, SAMPO reaches 77.73% success versus GPT-5.2's 26.56% score. The results confirm that combining sequence-level clipping, fine-grained advantage estimation, and dynamic filtering is necessary for stable, high-performance agentic RL, as no single modification achieves comparable gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the PPO/GRPO lineage for LLM reinforcement learning (Schulman et al., 2017; Guo et al., 2025) and the growing body of agentic RL work (AgentGym, AgentR1, Search-R1) that trains LLM agents in multi-turn interactive environments. Rather than proposing a fundamentally new algorithm, SAMPO synthesizes three previously studied components—GSPO's sequence-level importance ratio clipping, GIGPO's step-level advantage decomposition, and DAPO's dynamic trajectory filtering—into a unified objective. The paper's primary departure from prior agentic RL work is methodological: it decomposes the policy gradient into four orthogonal design dimensions (loss aggregation, IS clipping, advantage design, dynamic sampling) and studies each in isolation under a standardized, reproducible testbed, a level of systematic ablation that most prior agentic RL papers do not provide.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides practitioners with a concrete, reproducible recipe for stable multi-turn ARL training and identifies sequence-level IS clipping as the dominant stability factor—a finding that directly informs which PO variant to use. The demonstration that a 4B model trained with SAMPO (92.72% ALFWorld success) substantially outperforms GPT-5.2 (51.56%) and o3-based multi-agent systems (56.25%) suggests that stable, environment-aligned RL training can be more effective than scale or complex inference-time engineering for agentic tasks. Open questions include whether these findings generalize beyond Qwen3-based models and the four tested environments, how the recipe scales to substantially longer horizons or more complex interaction spaces, and whether the off-policy staleness sensitivity identified here can be mitigated through asynchronous or on-policy training schemes.
