## Problem

Existing multimodal federated learning (MFL) methods assume all clients share identical model architectures and balanced modality distributions, assumptions that rarely hold in practice. When heterogeneous encoders project data into incompatible feature spaces, naive prototype aggregation causes knowledge contamination that can degrade global model performance below local-only training. The paper asks how to enable effective cross-client knowledge transfer when both neural architectures and modality configurations differ across clients.

## Input / Output

Each client receives pre-extracted multimodal feature vectors (e.g., 1984-d HOG, 512-d Gist, 928-d LBP for images; 21k–34k-d TF-IDF BoW for text; 512-d MFCC, 64-d volume, 647-d spectrogram for audio; 2000-d spatiotemporal cuboids, 1024-d optical flow for video) paired with class labels, but only for a subset of the global modality set. Clients upload per-class feature prototypes and logit prototypes (not model parameters) to the server, which returns refined global feature prototypes (one per modality per class) and global logit prototypes (one per class) for local distillation. The final output is a per-client class prediction, with the server additionally maintaining modality-specific encoders and a shared decoder as alignment infrastructure.

## Method

MFedPBA implements a bilateral alignment mechanism at two levels. At the feature level, the server projects heterogeneous client feature prototypes through modality-specific encoders and a shared decoder (an autoencoder), then optimizes this mapping with a joint loss combining reconstruction fidelity, inter-modal contrastive learning (pulling same-class cross-modal prototypes together), and intra-modal Gromov-Wasserstein distance alignment (solved via entropy-regularized Sinkhorn iterations) to preserve inter-class geometric topology between the feature space and the logit space. At the decision level, logit prototypes—whose dimensions naturally correspond to classes and thus reside in a shared semantic space across heterogeneous models—are aggregated using inverse-entropy weighting so that clients with lower predictive uncertainty contribute more. On the client side, local training minimizes cross-entropy plus an MSE-based feature alignment loss anchoring local representations to global feature prototypes and a KL-divergence logit alignment loss distilling global decision structure. The paper further proves a non-convex convergence rate of O(1/T) under standard smoothness and bounded-variance assumptions.

## Results

MFedPBA achieves the best test accuracy across all 16 experimental configurations (4 datasets × 4 partition settings: M2, M1+, M1, and large-scale K). Under the standard M2 setting, it reaches 49.04% on Caltech101, 75.16% on Reuters, 35.20% on NUS-WIDE, and 47.92% on YouTube, outperforming the strongest baselines by 2–6 percentage points. The gains are most pronounced in the M1 (single-modality-per-client) setting, where naive prototype aggregation (FedProto) actually degrades below local training (e.g., 31.57% vs. 38.47% on Caltech101), while MFedPBA still improves over Local by 5.17 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MFedPBA outperforms all six baselines in every one of the 16 experimental configurations. The most striking comparison is against FedProto (Tan et al., 2022a), which in the M1 setting drops below even Local training on Caltech101 (31.57% vs. 38.47%) and NUS-WIDE (24.96% vs. 31.15%), confirming that naive prototype aggregation under heterogeneous encoders causes knowledge contamination. Among prototype-based methods, FedTGP (Zhang et al., 2024) and FedPall (Zhang et al., 2025c) are the closest competitors but still trail MFedPBA by 2–5 points; FedTGP also exhibits pronounced training instability. Among MFL-specific baselines, Harmony (Ouyang et al., 2023) is the strongest on Caltech101 M2 (45.29%) and FedMVP (Che et al., 2024) leads on Reuters M2 (74.07%), yet both are surpassed by MFedPBA by 3.75 and 1.09 points respectively. In large-scale settings (K50–K100), MFedPBA maintains its lead, with the gap over the best baseline narrowing to ~0.3–1.2 points, indicating consistent scalability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the prototype-based federated learning paradigm established by FedProto (Tan et al., 2022a) to the multimodal setting, where prior methods assumed homogeneous client architectures and balanced modality availability. The key departure is the introduction of a bilateral alignment mechanism: at the feature level, it repurposes the Gromov-Wasserstein distance (Memoli, 2011) to align geometric structures across incompatible embedding spaces without requiring dimensional correspondence, combined with contrastive learning for intra-modal consistency; at the decision level, it exploits the observation that logit vectors naturally occupy a shared class-indexed semantic space, enabling direct entropy-weighted aggregation. This contrasts with prior MFL methods (Harmony, FedMVP, FedMobile) that either require full model uploads or assume shared modality sets, and with prior prototype methods (FedTGP, FedPall) that perform simple weighted averaging of features without addressing space incompatibility.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: real-world federated deployments involve clients with different hardware constraints (hence different model architectures) and different sensor availability (hence different modality subsets), a combination that prior MFL work has not jointly tackled. The finding that naive prototype aggregation under heterogeneity can degrade performance below local-only training is a useful caution for practitioners. Limitations include reliance on relatively small benchmark datasets (≤18K samples), the server-side autoencoder and GW computation that may not scale to very large class counts or modality numbers, and the assumption that the number of clients equals the number of modalities in the main experiments. Open questions include whether the bilateral alignment strategy transfers to foundation-model-based MFL settings and how the method behaves under streaming or incremental modality availability.
