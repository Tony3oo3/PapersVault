## Problem

Existing MLLMs for affective image content analysis are confined to coarse-grained dominant emotion classification or facial-expression-centric recognition, failing to model emotional diversity (multiple co-occurring emotions with varying intensity), dimensional emotion space (VAD) regression, or causal reasoning about why an image evokes specific feelings. No large-scale instruction dataset or unified training framework exists to address these gaps simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261215/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a single RGB image or a pair of images together with a natural-language question, processed through the Qwen2.5-VL-7B vision encoder and LLM backbone. Outputs are structured into a 'thinking' segment followed by a task-specific 'answer' segment, yielding one of: a dominant emotion label from a 7-class Ekman+neutral taxonomy, an ordered top-3 emotion ranking, continuous VAD scores (normalized 0–1), or an open-ended ≤200-word causal reasoning narrative linking visual elements to evoked emotions. Paired-image inputs require relational reasoning (consistency verification or comparative VAD assessment) across both images.

## Method

EEmo-Logic is built on a two-stage paradigm over the newly constructed EEmoDB (1.2M QA pairs from 128K images + 36K curated assessment pairs from 25K images, synthesized from 7 AICA datasets via synonym mapping, VAD-based emotion clustering, and constrained LLM generation). Stage 1 applies LoRA (r=64) supervised fine-tuning on EEmoDB-QA to instill broad emotion perception, ranking, and description capabilities. Stage 2 applies GRPO on EEmoDB-Assess with four task-gated rewards: a binary format reward, a ranking reward combining weighted positional hit rate, Kendall's τ ordinal consistency, and a squared margin function; a hybrid regression reward for VAD scoring that fuses a linear-decay guidance zone with a Gaussian-kernel high-precision zone; and a dual-perspective emotion similarity reward that fuses VAD-space Euclidean distance with Qwen3-embedding semantic similarity to densify feedback in the 7-class DEC space. This reward design steers the policy from coarse alignment toward fine-grained, human-aligned emotion assessment while eliciting causal reasoning traces.

## Results

On EEmo-Bench, EEmo-Logic achieves an overall score of 66.22%, surpassing GPT-4o (62.39%), Gemini-2.5-Pro (57.14%), and its own backbone Qwen2.5-VL-7B (55.71%). In the fine-grained VAD assessment task, EEmo-Logic (Think) reaches an overall SRCC of 0.73 and PLCC of 0.71, exceeding the best baseline Qwen2.5-VL-7B (SRCC 0.53, PLCC 0.50) by 38% in SRCC. On cross-domain benchmarks, EEmo-Logic attains state-of-the-art results on ArtEmis (F1 31.58%, ACC 35.10%) and AesBench AesE (75.68% overall), while remaining competitive on Artphoto (F1 38.68%) and UNIAA Sentiment (75.65%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EEmo-Logic outperforms all 23 baselines on the overall EEmo-Bench score (66.22%), beating the strongest proprietary model GPT-4o by 3.83 points and Gemini-2.5-Pro by 9.08 points. Among emotion-oriented MLLMs, EEmo-Logic surpasses EmoCaliber (51.85%) by 14.37 points and Emotion-Qwen (53.33%) by 12.89 points, with particularly large margins on the description task (62.16% vs. 33.05% and 36.43% respectively). On the VAD assessment task, EEmo-Logic (Think) achieves SRCC 0.73 overall, exceeding Qwen2.5-VL-7B (0.53) by 144% in the dominance dimension specifically. On cross-domain ArtEmis, EEmo-Logic achieves the best F1 (31.58%) and ACC (35.10%), edging out EmoCaliber (30.59%/33.23%) and Emotion-Qwen (25.34%/29.01%), while on AesBench AesE it leads with 75.68% overall accuracy versus Emotion-Qwen's 74.75%. The only marginal loss is on single-image perception Yes-or-No, where Gemini-2.5-Pro (72.22%) slightly exceeds EEmo-Logic (71.73%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MLLM-based affective image content analysis (AICA) line initiated by EmoVIT, AffectGPT, and EmoCaliber, which each addressed only a subset of emotion dimensions (DEC classification, open-vocabulary listing, or confidence calibration) while neglecting the complementary paradigm. It departs from these by unifying categorical (CES) and dimensional (DES) emotion spaces in a single framework and by adapting the GRPO reinforcement learning paradigm from DeepSeekMath to emotion-specific tasks with novel hybrid regression and dual-perspective similarity rewards. The dataset construction builds on the IESN lexicon-based VAD methodology and the EEmo-Bench evaluation protocol, while the two-stage LoRA-SFT + GRPO training pipeline is a deliberate departure from prior single-stage SFT approaches that the authors argue overfit to emotional label noise.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 1.2M-instruction EEmoDB dataset fills a clear gap: prior AICA corpora were either small-scale label sets or limited to aesthetic-emotion QA, leaving no comprehensive training resource for MLLM-based emotion understanding. The practical impact is strongest for the AICA community, which gains both a large-scale instruction dataset and a reference framework combining CES and DES. However, the approach remains constrained to Ekman's 7-emotion taxonomy and a lexicon-based VAD generation pipeline, raising open questions about scalability to open-vocabulary or culture-specific emotion taxonomies. Cross-domain generalization, while competitive, does not dramatically surpass specialized baselines like EmoCaliber on classic DEC benchmarks, suggesting the framework's advantage is most pronounced in the multi-dimensional, reasoning-heavy setting it was designed for rather than in narrow classification tasks.
