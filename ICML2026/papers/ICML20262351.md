## Problem

Existing Shapley-type attribution methods handle hard precedence constraints (PSV) and soft priority weights (WSV) in isolation, and WSV is restricted to the special case of ordered-partition DAGs. There is no unified framework that simultaneously enforces general precedence structures and incorporates player-specific trust/risk weights, leaving practitioners without a principled way to combine both types of priority in data valuation or feature attribution.

## Input / Output

The method takes three inputs: a set function U: 2^[n] → R (e.g., test accuracy of a k-NN classifier on a training subset), a precedence DAG over the n players (any general DAG, not limited to ordered partitions), and a positive weight vector λ ∈ R^n_+ encoding soft priorities. It outputs a payoff vector ψ(U) ∈ R^n and, via priority sweeping, per-player sensitivity curves tracking how each player's valuation changes as its individual weight λ_i varies over (0, ∞) while all other weights and the DAG are held fixed.

## Method

PASV defines a distribution p^(≼,λ) over linear extensions of the precedence poset where each step t selects among the currently maximal (admissible) elements with probability proportional to λ_i · |max_≼(S_t)| / Σ_{k∈max_≼(S_t)} λ_k; the |max_≼(S_t)| factor in the numerator is essential for recovering PSV under uniform weights. The paper proves that PASV is the unique distribution satisfying the State-Choice Factorization form together with Weight Proportionality and Equal-Weight Uniformity axioms, and that it reduces to PSV (uniform weights) and WSV (ordered-partition DAG) as special cases. For scalable computation, an adjacent-swap Metropolis-Hastings chain on Π^≼ is used, with a closed-form local acceptance ratio (Lemma 4.1) that depends only on the maximal sets before and after the proposed swap, enabling O(1) per-step evaluation. Limiting-regime analysis shows that sending a globally maximal player's weight to infinity is equivalent to adding DAG edges from all other maximal players to it, providing a theoretical bridge between soft and hard priorities.

## Results

On the MNIST data-valuation benchmark (800 players, 14-block DAG), PASV with priority weights (b=32, c=(0,1,0,2,2)) progressively suppresses penalized provider types (Anchor, Copier, Poisoner) while respecting reuse constraints, whereas classical SV over-credits Copier and WSV still assigns positive value to Copier. On Census Income feature attribution (12 features), switching from the coarse ordered-partition DAG to the richer general DAG produces noticeably different PSV valuations, and priority sweeping identifies native-country and relationship as the most weight-sensitive features while martial-status and capital-loss remain stable. Computationally, the adjacent-swap M-H sampler's empirical mixing time tracks O(n²) across four synthetic poset families (up to n=25), and the direct Monte Carlo estimator achieves small relative error with a few hundred retained permutations, remaining tractable up to n=8192 players.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Classical SV (Ghorbani & Zou, 2019) ignores precedence entirely and over-credits data reuse, assigning positive value to Copier on MNIST. WSV (Zheng et al., 2025), which approximates the DAG as a two-layer ordered partition (Original ≺ Synthetic), favors Owner but still mixes up among synthetic providers and assigns positive value to Copier due to its structural limitation to ordered partitions. PSV (Faigle & Kern, 1992) better preserves credit for upstream sources and correctly reveals negative value for Copier, but offers no mechanism for individual priority weighting. In the feature-attribution setting, the ordered-partition DAG from Frye et al. (2020) produces noticeably different PSV valuations compared to the richer general DAG, demonstrating that coarse precedence specifications can significantly alter attribution results. PASV subsumes both PSV (equal weights) and WSV (ordered-partition DAG) as special cases (Propositions 3.1–3.2) and provides the additional flexibility of per-player soft weights under general DAGs, with priority sweeping offering a diagnostic that none of the baselines provide.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of Shapley-value research: the Precedence Shapley Value (PSV) of Faigle & Kern (1992), which restricts averaging to linear extensions of a DAG, and the Weighted Shapley Value (WSV) of Kalai & Samet (1987) / Nowak & Radzik (1995), which introduces player-specific weights but only for the restrictive ordered-partition DAG structure. PASV generalizes both to arbitrary DAGs by introducing a State-Choice Factorization (SCF) framework and a critical |max_≼(S_t)| normalization factor that ensures the PSV reduction (Remark 3.3). For computation, it adapts the adjacent-swap Metropolis-Hastings chain of Karzanov & Khachiyan (1991) to the non-uniform target distribution, and borrows the proxy-plus-residual estimation strategy from Witter et al. (2026) and Fumagalli et al. (2026) for variance reduction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PASV provides the first principled, axiomatically characterized method that simultaneously enforces general precedence constraints and soft trust/risk weights in Shapley-based attribution, filling a gap where practitioners previously had to choose between structural fidelity (PSV) and individual weighting (WSV, limited to ordered partitions). The priority-sweeping diagnostic and DAG-sensitivity analysis offer practical tools for stress-testing attribution results, which is valuable in high-stakes settings like data markets or causal feature attribution. Open questions include how to learn or validate the precedence DAG from data rather than specifying it by hand, whether the MCMC approach remains efficient for very dense DAGs, and how to extend the framework to handle dynamic or time-varying precedence structures.
