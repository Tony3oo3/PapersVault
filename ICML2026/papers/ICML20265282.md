## Problem

ℓ_p regularization (0 < p < 1) provides stronger sparsity guarantees than ℓ_1, but its unbounded gradients near zero make direct optimization unstable. The reparameterization surrogate [Cp] (Hoff 2017; Kolb et al. 2023) smooths the landscape but still suffers from high-order stationary points at the origin that trap standard gradient descent, preventing practical deployment on complex datasets. This paper asks how to retain the ℓ_p sparsity benefits while achieving stable optimization on real-world problems.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a differentiable objective f(x) with gradient access, an initialization x(0) ∈ ℝ^d, and four hyperparameters: an odd integer K (reparameterization order), an integer M ∈ [0, K−1] (sparsity–stability tradeoff), a scale ε ≥ 0, and a weight-decay coefficient λ. It iterates in the reparameterized space y where x = y^K (element-wise), and outputs the sparse vector x(T) = y^K(T). The coordinate-wise adaptive learning rate depends on the current iterate values y^M/(y^{K−1}+ε·1), so the effective step size varies per coordinate and per iteration, unlike fixed-rate SGD.

## Method

ReWA projects x into a K-th-root reparameterization y (so x = y^K element-wise), then applies a modified SGD update on y that combines three mechanisms: (i) standard weight decay (1−λη_t)y, (ii) the chain-rule gradient y^{K−1}⊙∇f(y^K), and (iii) a coordinate-wise adaptive learning rate η_t·y^M/(y^{K−1}+ε·1) that cancels the problematic y^{K−1} factor responsible for high-order saddles at zero. The parameter M trades off sparsity (large M → smaller p) against optimization stability (small M), while ε controls the scale of an additional ℓ_q (q>1) term. The paper proves (Theorem 3.3) that the implicit regularizer of ReWA is an ℓ_p norm with p = 1+(1−M)/K ∈ (0,1), establishing a rigorous connection to ℓ_p regularization. Additional theorems show that reparameterization is necessary (clipping ℓ_p cannot simultaneously bound gradients and preserve fidelity), weight decay is essential for sparsity under large initializations, and the adaptive rate shrinks the stagnation region around the origin compared to non-adaptive updates.

## Results

On the synthetic linear regression task (10,000-dimensional sparse signal, 2,000 samples), ReWA achieves sparsity comparable to LASSO across multiple thresholds, both outperforming SGD with ℓ₁ penalty. On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18, ReWA maintains competitive test accuracy until the compression ratio exceeds 10², whereas prior methods on the same architecture exhibit significant accuracy degradation at that threshold. On ImageNet with ResNet-50, ReWA similarly preserves accuracy at relatively high compression ratios compared to ℓ₁ regularization and plain SGD baselines. The paper reports results primarily as accuracy-vs-compression-ratio curves (Figure 3) and sparsity-vs-threshold plots (Figure 2) rather than single-point accuracy numbers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LASSO (Tibshirani, 1996), ReWA performs comparably on the linear synthetic regression task, with both methods achieving similar sparsity levels across thresholds and both outperforming plain SGD with ℓ₁ penalty; however, the paper notes ReWA performs better in non-linear regimes. Against Spred (Liu & Wang, 2023), which corresponds to ReWA with K=2, ReWA with larger K values achieves higher sparsity at equivalent compression ratios on CIFAR-10, maintaining accuracy beyond the 10² compression ratio where Spred degrades. Against PowerPropagation (Schwarz et al., 2021), which relies on implicit bias without explicit weight decay, ReWA's ablation (removing weight decay) shows that sparsity is lost in the large-initialization regime, confirming that explicit weight decay is essential and that PowerPropagation's implicit-bias-only approach is insufficient for general settings. Overall, ReWA wins on sparsity at high compression ratios and on optimization stability (avoiding the zero-saddle-point trap), while LASSO remains competitive in the purely linear regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the reparameterization-based sparsification line initiated by Hoff (2017) and Kolb et al. (2023), who showed that the Hadamard-product form [Cp] is a smooth surrogate for ℓp regularization with p=2/K. The key departure is the introduction of a coordinate-wise adaptive learning rate (parameterized by M and ε) that stabilizes the otherwise ill-conditioned optimization of [Cp], while Theorem 3.3 proves the resulting implicit regularizer retains an ℓp (0<p<1) structure. The paper also positions itself against the mirror-flow framework (Li et al. 2022; Jacobs & Burkholz 2025), which is restricted to K=2, and against PowerPropagation (Schwarz et al. 2021), which relies on implicit bias without explicit weight decay—showing via Theorem 3.9 that the latter is insufficient in the large-initialization regime.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ReWA provides a theoretically grounded, practically deployable method for ℓp sparse optimization that overcomes the unbounded-gradient instability plaguing direct ℓp minimization, with each component (reparameterization, weight decay, adaptive LR) justified by a dedicated theorem. The explicit M parameter gives practitioners direct control over the sparsity-inducing regularization strength, and the method composes naturally with AdamW for deep network training. Limitations include reliance on assumptions (smoothness, expansion, low-dimensional update subspace) that are difficult to verify for deep networks, manual hyperparameter selection (K, M, ε), and experiments restricted to ResNet architectures on image classification; extending to Transformer-based NLP tasks and automating hyperparameter selection are identified as open directions.
