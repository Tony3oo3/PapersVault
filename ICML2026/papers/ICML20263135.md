## Problem

Static data mixing strategies (DoReMi, DoGE, CHAMELEON) fix domain weights offline and cannot adapt to the model's evolving learning dynamics during pretraining. Existing dynamic methods (ODM, PiKE) adjust mixtures on-the-fly but lack a unified framework that simultaneously balances computational efficiency, sample efficiency, and structural flexibility across diverse pipelines (e.g., fixed corpora with proxy transfer versus end-to-end training from scratch without priors).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263135/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a domain-partitioned corpus (e.g., The Pile's 22 domains or SlimPajama's 7 domains), the current LLM weights, and a compact state vector comprising per-domain losses, step-to-step loss differences, L2 norms of selected layer weights, their update magnitudes, the iteration index, and per-domain sample counts. At each step it produces a probability vector over K domains (via softmax) that reweights the next batch, and ultimately a trained LLM. Notably, the reward is computed from only a subset of parameters (~50M out of 1B) to keep per-step overhead at 0.4%, and the policy network itself is only 17M parameters (~1.7% of the target LLM).

## Method

AC-ODM casts online data mixing as a continuous-control MDP solved with DDPG: an Actor-Critic agent observes the LLM's training state and outputs domain sampling weights, while the LLM itself serves as the environment. The key mechanism is a gradient-alignment reward W_i = ⟨∇ℓ_i, Σ_{j≠i} ∇ℓ_j⟩, which the paper proves acts as a linear surrogate for the off-diagonal interaction energy in the empirical Gram matrix, thereby greedily maximizing the constructive interference of gradients and the effective descent magnitude ‖G^T α‖². An importance-corrected exponential moving average (dividing by the previous sampling probability) prevents the policy from collapsing into trivially upweighting already-frequent domains. Two operational modes are supported: a Proxy Mode where the policy is learned on a small model (160M–410M) and transferred to a larger target, and a Non-Proxy Mode where the Actor-Critic is co-trained end-to-end with the target LLM from scratch, incurring only 0.4% per-step wall-clock overhead and 2% additional memory.

## Results

On Pythia-1B pretrained on The Pile, AC-ODM-410M (proxy mode) achieves 29.98% zero-shot MMLU accuracy, 35.22% five-shot MMLU accuracy, and 72.64% HumanEval pass@1, outperforming the strongest dynamic baseline PiKE (24.84%, 30.44%, 52.19%) by +5.1 pp, +4.8 pp, and +20.5 pp respectively. In terms of convergence, AC-ODM-410M reaches the optimal validation perplexity of the best prior baseline (ODM) using 66% fewer training steps, while incurring only 0.4% per-step wall-clock overhead and 2% additional memory. On SlimPajama, the same method requires 65% fewer steps than ODM and 73% fewer than the uniform baseline to reach its best perplexity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AC-ODM-410M (proxy mode) dominates all seven baselines on both MMLU zero-shot accuracy and HumanEval pass@1, achieving a 27.5% relative gain over ODM and a 2.23× improvement in pass@1 over ODM (0.726 vs 0.325). Against the strongest dynamic baseline PiKE, AC-ODM-410M leads by +5.1 pp on MMLU 0-shot and +20.5 pp on HumanEval, while the non-proxy AC-ODM also surpasses PiKE on MMLU 0-shot (0.251 vs 0.248) and HumanEval (0.603 vs 0.522), though it trails slightly on MMLU 5-shot (0.299 vs 0.304). Among static methods, CHAMELEON (leverage-score based) and DoGE (gradient-alignment based) are the strongest, yet both are outperformed by AC-ODM by 7.9 pp and 7.7 pp on MMLU 0-shot respectively, confirming that adaptive online mixing captures evolving training dynamics that static weights cannot. On validation perplexity, AC-ODM-410M converges 66% faster than ODM and outperforms CHAMELEON and PiKE at every checkpoint, while on SlimPajama it requires 65% fewer steps than ODM. The proxy mode consistently outperforms the non-proxy mode across all corpora and architectures (Pythia and LLaMA-style), with the 410M proxy yielding the strongest transfer to the 1B target.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dynamic data mixing line (ODM's bandit approach, PiKE's gradient-conflict reduction) by recasting the problem as a continuous-control MDP solved with DDPG, and provides a distinct theoretical grounding: the gradient-alignment reward is shown to be a linear surrogate for the off-diagonal (interaction) energy in the Gram matrix, explicitly maximizing constructive interference rather than merely reducing conflicts as PiKE does. It departs from static methods (DoReMi, DoGE, CHAMELEON, RegMix) by updating weights at every step, and from prior dynamic methods by unifying proxy-based transfer and from-scratch training under a single actor-critic architecture with a principled reward derived from optimization geometry rather than heuristic loss-gap or leverage-score signals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 66% reduction in training steps to reach optimal perplexity, combined with negligible per-step overhead (0.4% time, 2% memory), makes AC-ODM a practically deployable drop-in for existing pretraining pipelines, potentially saving substantial compute at scale. Open questions include whether proxy-to-target transfer degrades under large architecture or corpus shifts (only tested up to 12B with a 1B proxy), why STEM-domain gains lag behind social-science gains, and how the method behaves when domain boundaries are inherently ambiguous or overlapping. The granularity sensitivity finding also implies that AC-ODM's effectiveness is coupled to the quality of the upstream domain taxonomy, leaving the curation problem unsolved.
