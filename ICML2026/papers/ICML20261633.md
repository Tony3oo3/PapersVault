## Problem

Continual learning models are empirically shown to be highly vulnerable to data poisoning attacks that can erase learned knowledge and prevent convergence, yet no principled theoretical framework exists to characterize when such attacks are fundamentally indefensible or to provide provable defense guarantees. This paper establishes the first theoretical foundation for adversarial resilience in regularization-based CL by formalizing the adversary-defender interaction as an online zero-sum game.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261633/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each time step t, the system receives a single task's dataset D_t (feature-label pairs) and the previous model state w_{t-1}; previous task datasets are no longer accessible, making cumulative empirical objectives infeasible. The T2T verification defense outputs a scalar detection score d_t computed from two consecutive parameter updates projected onto a common feature subspace, triggering a rollback to w_{t-2} upon detection. The robust feature defense outputs per-eigendirection scalar regularization strengths {λ_j^t} that are solved recursively via a minimax program at each step, requiring only the current Hessian and the running mean/covariance of the parameter error.

## Method

The paper frames data poisoning defense as an online zero-sum game and derives a tractable surrogate for the adversary's inner optimization via second-order Taylor expansion, showing the adversary's optimal strategy concentrates its budget along the top right singular vector of a sensitivity matrix. For infrequent unbounded attacks, the proposed Task-to-Task (T2T) verification computes a detection score from two consecutive updates using projection matrices D_t^1 and D_t^2 that algebraically cancel the influence of all tasks prior to t-1, isolating deviations introduced exclusively by the latest two tasks; upon detection, both recent updates are discarded. For frequent bounded non-shifted attacks, the paper solves the minimax recursion to obtain a closed-form per-eigendirection regularization (Proposition 5.2) that identifies a 'protected set' of vulnerable dimensions and tailors regularization to suppress the maximum singular value of the sensitivity matrix, provably spreading the worst-case attack impact across multiple directions rather than being dominated by a single small-eigenvalue direction (Theorem 5.3).

## Results

The paper is primarily theoretical, with experiments on CIFAR-100 (pretrained ViT + linear layer) and CIFAR-10 (CNN from scratch), each partitioned into 100 CL tasks. Under infrequent shifted attacks (10/100 tasks poisoned), the T2T verification defense (Alg. 1) maintains test accuracy comparable to the attack-free baseline, while all 10 attacks are successfully detected via the dynamic score d_t. Under frequent bounded non-shifted attacks, the robust feature defense derived from the minimax formulation (11) accelerates convergence and mitigates accuracy degradation relative to standard EWC, confirming the theoretical prediction of Theorem 5.3 that the attack-induced term is spread over the protected set rather than dominated by a single sensitive eigendirection.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against EWC (Kirkpatrick et al., 2017b), the T2T verification defense clearly wins under infrequent shifted attacks: EWC converges slower and lacks a detection mechanism, while Alg. 1 discards poisoned tasks and recovers to the no-attack accuracy trajectory. Under frequent bounded non-shifted attacks, the robust feature defense also outperforms standard EWC by accelerating convergence, as the optimized H_t suppresses the maximum singular value of the adversarial sensitivity matrix. Against iCaRL (Rebuffi et al., 2017), the comparison is nuanced: iCaRL actually outperforms EWC under shifted attacks because its clean exemplar replay prevents full drift in the adversarial direction, but iCaRL performs significantly worse than both regularization-based methods under frequent attacks due to its lack of a regularization term, making it more sensitive to the adversarial objective in (5). Against the theoretical benchmark of Zhao et al. (2024), the robust feature defense achieves a strictly better excess-risk bound: the attack-induced term improves from max_j M/(T·γ̃_j^T) (dominated by the single most sensitive direction) to (M + j_T·σ²)/(T·(Σ f_j·√γ̃_j^T)²), spreading sensitivity across the protected set and yielding faster convergence under imbalanced spectra.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the regularization-based continual learning theory established by Zhao et al. (2024) and the EWC framework of Kirkpatrick et al. (2017) by introducing an adversarial security dimension that prior CL theory entirely overlooked. While empirical studies (Han et al. 2023, Li & Ditzler 2022, Abbasi et al. 2024) demonstrated CL's vulnerability to data poisoning, no principled theoretical framework existed to characterize when defense is possible or impossible. The paper departs from prior work by formulating the adversary-defender interaction as an online zero-sum game and deriving the first provable convergence guarantees (or impossibility results) for regularization-based CL under strategic poisoning, bridging the gap between heuristic robustness methods and rigorous CL generalization theory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first theoretical foundation for understanding and defending against data poisoning in continual learning, establishing clear boundaries (Table 1) between defensible and indefensible attack regimes that guide practitioners on when to invest in defenses. The T2T verification and robust feature defense mechanisms offer provable guarantees, but the analysis is confined to linear/NTK regimes and uniform task distributions, leaving open how these results extend to large-scale nonlinear networks, non-stationary task sequences, and other CL paradigms like replay-based methods. The finding that memorization-based iCaRL outperforms regularization-based EWC under shifted attacks but degrades under frequent attacks suggests no single defense dominates across all attack types, pointing toward adaptive or hybrid strategies as a promising direction.
