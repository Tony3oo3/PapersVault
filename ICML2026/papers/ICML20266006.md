## Problem

Native 4K (2176×3840) video generation is computationally infeasible with standard full-attention transformers because attention cost grows quadratically with the number of spatiotemporal tokens, and existing efficient methods either alter model architectures (preventing reuse of pretrained weights) or rely on cascaded super-resolution pipelines that produce semantically inconsistent high-frequency details. The paper asks whether a pretrained full-attention video diffusion model can be transformed into a linear-complexity variant with minimal architectural change and modest fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

For text-to-video, the model takes a text prompt and generates a native 4K video at 2176×3840 resolution with 81 frames; for image-to-video, it additionally accepts a reference image as the first frame. The deployment variant further compresses inference to 8 denoising steps (via DMD2-based step and CFG distillation) and uses a lightweight eVAE decoder, achieving 4K/81-frame generation in under one hour with <60 GB memory on a single H20 GPU. Training is progressive: full fine-tuning at 720P (720×1280) followed by 4K fine-tuning, or LoRA-based high-resolution adaptation from the 720P checkpoint.

## Method

T3-Video replaces the global full-attention operation in each DiT layer with S parallel local attention operations at multiple scales, all sharing the same fixed window size and the same projection matrices (W_Q, W_K, W_V, W_O), so parameter count is unchanged. At the finest scale, windows are voxel-adjacent (stride 1); at the coarsest scale, strides span the entire spatiotemporal domain, making the coarsest window equivalent to full-attention. Outputs from all scales are aggregated via scale-weighted averaging at each position, effectively creating a 'concurrent global transceiver' that models both local and global context within a single layer. A hierarchical blocking strategy assigns different window configurations to groups of 5 layers, and an axis-preserving strategy retains full attention along one axis in selected layers to improve stability. This reduces attention MACs from O(L²) to O(L×L_b) while remaining a drop-in one-line code replacement for the standard SelfAttention module, enabling pretrained weight inheritance and rapid convergence (satisfactory 4K results after ~2,000 iterations).

## Results

On 4K-VBench (120 short 4K videos), T3-Video-T2V-1.3B achieves VQA 71.72 and VTC 0.83, surpassing the strongest native-4K baseline UltraGen by +4.29 VQA and +0.08 VTC while delivering a 7× inference speedup. The method is the first to produce native 81-frame 4K (2176×3840) video, requiring <60 GB memory and ~1 hour runtime on a single H20 GPU. At the DiT level, T3 reduces attention MACs by up to 43× at 4K resolution, translating to a measured 21.4× inference speedup (50-step CFG) and 25× end-to-end speedup in the deployment configuration (8-step, no CFG, eVAE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the official Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B baseline, T3-Video improves VQA from 30.01 to 71.72 (+41.71) and VTC from 0.37 to 0.83, reflecting that the official model simply cannot produce coherent 4K output without the T3 transformation. Compared to HunyuanVideo (VQA 61.92, VTC 0.68), T3-Video leads by +9.80 VQA and +0.15 VTC. The most direct competitor, UltraGen (Hu et al., 2026), is outperformed on every metric: T3-Video gains +4.29 VQA, +0.08 VTC, +0.09 DoG, +0.11 RA, and +0.18 TEP, while also running 7× faster in 4K inference and generating 81 frames versus UltraGen's 29. On the VBench suite (Table 8), T3-Video-4K (81f) also edges out UltraWAN-4K (29f) on Subject Consistency (97.17% vs 96.05%), Aesthetic Quality (59.83% vs 56.81%), and Object Class (66.66% vs 50.00%). A 10-evaluator human study confirms the advantage, with T3-Video winning 71.25% on video quality, 59.75% on text consistency, 56.92% on temporal consistency, and 63.42% on detail richness over UltraGen.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Wan2.1/2.2 DiT-based video generation family, transforming its full-attention computation into multi-scale shared window attention while preserving all pretrained weights. Conceptually, it departs from prior efficient-attention approaches (VSA, FPSAttention) that substantially modify model architectures or require full retraining, and from UltraGen's carefully designed hierarchical attention modules, by proposing a minimal 'one-line code replacement' of the attention forward pass. The multi-scale local-global design draws inspiration from EMOv2's i²RMB block, but is applied here as a drop-in attention transformation rather than a new architectural block, and the deployment pipeline leverages DMD2-style step/CFG distillation for further acceleration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

T3-Video makes native 4K (2176×3840) 81-frame video generation feasible on a single H20 GPU with <60G memory, a practical milestone that prior methods (UltraWan, UltraGen) achieved only at 29 frames with 128-GPU training. The key open question is the substantial gap between theoretical MACs reduction (43×) and measured speedup (21.4× at 4K, only 4.7× at 720P), which the authors attribute to hardware-software mismatch; closing this gap through co-optimization could unlock even greater practical gains. Limitations include reliance on a modest 42K-video dataset, no exploration of larger models (14B+), and the absence of high-resolution perceptual evaluation metrics beyond 512×512, leaving the true quality ceiling of 4K generation under-characterized.
