## Problem

Knowledge distillation for survival analysis is ill-defined under censoring because censored observations lack an event-time probability mass to match, and existing methods assume teacher and student share identical outcome definitions. In competing risks settings, these issues compound: sparse cause-specific failures, heavy censoring, and heterogeneous teacher granularity (e.g., overall failure vs. cause-specific risks) make standard KL-based distillation inapplicable.

## Input / Output

The student model receives tabular patient/donor/recipient covariates and the teacher's predicted hazard sequences at discrete time intervals; the teacher need only supply predictions (not raw data or parameters), and may provide cause-specific hazards, an aggregated overall hazard, or a single binary horizon-level prediction. The student outputs cause-specific discrete hazards at each interval, from which cumulative incidence functions are computed via the product-limit formula. A key design constraint is that the student always targets the full competing-risks structure even when the teacher reports a coarser outcome, and partial covariate overlap between teacher and student is permitted.

## Method

DiSKD defines a cause-specific, time-dependent KL divergence at each discrete interval, treating the (J+1)-category outcome (J causes plus no-event) as a multinomial, and accumulates this divergence over all at-risk subject-interval pairs. The training objective is a penalized log-likelihood that adds η times the accumulated KL to the internal discrete-time competing risks likelihood; Proposition 2.2 shows this is algebraically equivalent to a standard multinomial likelihood where observed event indicators are replaced by convex combinations of empirical labels and teacher-predicted hazards, enabling direct implementation with any neural hazard architecture (Nnet-Survival, Logistic-Hazard, Time-Embedding, Transformer). Temperature scaling is applied only to the KL component to soften the teacher distribution and reveal cross-cause similarity structures. For outcome heterogeneity, the KL is adapted to a Bernoulli form for overall-risk teachers or a CIF-level Bernoulli form for binary teachers, regularizing only the aggregate hazard while leaving cause allocation to the internal likelihood. A local excess-risk expansion formalizes the bias-variance trade-off: the KL term reduces variance when the internal cohort is small or event-sparse but introduces bias proportional to teacher-target mismatch, motivating validation-based selection of η.

## Results

On the post-COVID SRTR target cohort, DiSKD with a matched competing-risks teacher (CR→CR) achieves C^td of 0.6889 for death and 0.6451 for graft failure, with predictive deviance of 0.4624, substantially outperforming the internal-only baseline (C^td 0.5954/0.5667, deviance 0.4749). Even with a coarser overall-event teacher (Overall→CR), DiSKD retains C^td of 0.6887/0.6533 and deviance 0.4650, showing robustness to outcome-definition heterogeneity. In simulations with a good-quality teacher, DiSKD-C attains the highest C^td for both risks and the lowest predictive deviance, while temperature T=2 yields a practical plateau in performance stability. On the donor-only KDRI-compatible subset, the distilled student exceeds both the donor-only baseline and the KDRI benchmark in cause-specific discrimination.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiSKD is compared against five strategies on the post-COVID SRTR cohort: (1) Internal-only training, (2) direct deployment of the COVID+KAS250 teacher, (3) Stacked (pooled) training, (4) Finetuning (teacher-initialized, post-COVID fine-tuned), and (5) Finetune+DiSKD. DiSKD outperforms all five on the concordance–deviance trade-off: the internal-only model overfits quickly with rising validation deviance, the teacher plateaus above DiSKD due to residual temporal shift, stacking yields only modest and less reliable gains, and finetuning improves over the teacher but remains below DiSKD. Finetune+DiSKD is comparable to DiSKD alone, confirming that the KL distillation term drives the primary benefit. In the donor-only deployment setting, the distilled student exceeds both a donor-only baseline trained on post-COVID data and the established KDRI benchmark in cause-specific C^td for both death and graft failure, even when restricted to the eight KDRI donor factors. Across benchmark datasets (SUPPORT, METABRIC, Rotterdam & GBSG), DiSKD consistently improves over internal-only training across multiple neural architectures and remains robust as teacher covariates are progressively degraded.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiSKD extends classical KL-based knowledge distillation (Hinton et al., 2015) to the discrete-time competing-risks survival setting, where censored observations and multiple event types make standard distribution-matching ill-defined. It departs from prior transfer-learning-for-survival work (Chen et al., 2021; Sheng et al., 2021; Wang et al., 2025), which typically assumes linear covariate effects or shared parameterization, by operating entirely at the level of predicted hazards rather than model coefficients. The method also generalizes single-risk discrete-time architectures (Nnet-Survival, Logistic-Hazard, Transformer-based survival) to competing risks and introduces a temperature-scaled KL component that exploits cross-cause dependence, a mechanism absent from prior survival distillation attempts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DiSKD addresses a practically important gap: transferring predictive knowledge from large historical cohorts to small, event-sparse, post-policy-change cohorts (e.g., post-KAS250 kidney transplant) without sharing raw data, while accommodating outcome-definition heterogeneity between teacher and student. The local excess-risk expansion clarifies when distillation helps (variance reduction) versus hurts (teacher-induced bias), motivating validation-based weight selection rather than a universal guarantee. Open questions include extending the framework to continuous-time models (Cox-type, cumulative-hazard alignment) and composite-likelihood objectives, as well as establishing global (not just local) improvement conditions. The donor-only distillation result—compressing a full competing-risks model into a KDRI-compatible tool—suggests broader applicability to allocation-time clinical decision support.
