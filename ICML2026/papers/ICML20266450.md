## Problem

Despite relentless scaling of data, compute, and parameters, no single monolithic model achieves ubiquitous dominance across all real-world benchmarks, and the marginal gains from scaling are diminishing at prohibitive costs. While Agentic AI (multi-agent collaboration with dynamic task decomposition) has shown empirical promise, there is no concrete theoretical proof establishing that it is overall superior to the monolithic approach. This paper fills that gap by providing rigorous derivations showing Agentic AI is a geometrically necessary and exponentially more efficient pathway to AGI.

## Input / Output

The theoretical framework takes a high-dimensional input x ∈ R^D drawn from a Structured Real-World Distribution whose support is a union of K compact Riemannian manifolds with intrinsic dimensions d_k ≪ D, each governed by a distinct labeling function f_k. The system produces a task solution y ∈ R, a routing decision π(x) selecting the active manifold/agent, and a full DAG execution plan specifying which agents process which intermediate representations in topological order. A key structural constraint is that the ambient dimension D is much larger than any individual task's intrinsic dimension, which is precisely the gap that drives the exponential efficiency advantage.

## Method

The paper formalizes real-world tasks as piecewise-smooth manifold learning problems and proves three core results. First, the 'Average Trap' (Proposition 3.3) shows that a monolithic model minimizing the weighted average loss over heterogeneous tasks incurs an irreducible quadratic penalty proportional to the Mahalanobis distance between its compromise optimum and each task-specific optimum. Second, a routing-based Agentic AI that decomposes the problem into K sub-problems on d_max-dimensional subspaces achieves a generalization error of O(K·N^{-1/d_max}) versus O(N^{-1/D}) for the monolithic baseline, yielding an exponential sample-complexity advantage when d_max ≪ D. Third, for general DAG topologies, the paper introduces a Topological Weight ω_u (aggregating gradient flow along all paths from agent u to sinks via the nilpotent influence matrix M = (I−J)^{-1}) and an Edge Weight W(e*) decomposing each edge's contribution into upstream history, local valve, and downstream sensitivity, establishing that the overall error scales as C(G)·(N/K)^{-1/d_eff} where the topology factor C(G) must remain bounded for the agentic advantage to hold.

## Results

The paper establishes that a monolithic learner's generalization error decays as O(N^{-1/D}), bottlenecked by the full ambient dimension D, while a routing-based Agentic AI achieves O(K·N^{-1/d_max}) where d_max ≪ D is the maximum intrinsic manifold dimension. This yields a sample complexity ratio N_R-Agentic/N_mono ∝ K^{d_max}·ε^{D-d_max}, meaning the agentic system requires exponentially fewer samples as the dimensionality gap (D − d_max) grows. The 'Average Trap' (Proposition 3.3) proves an irreducible quadratic penalty Σ(α_k/2)‖θ_mono* − θ_k*‖²_{H_k} that accumulates with task diversity, and the parameter-efficiency analogue shows monolithic error decays as O(P^{−κ/D}) versus O(P^{−κ/d_max}) for agentic systems.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is theoretical: monolithic scaling versus routing-based Agentic AI versus general DAG-topology Agentic AI. The monolithic baseline is shown to be fundamentally limited by the curse of dimensionality (minimax lower bound N^{-1/(2+D)}), while even the simplest routing-based agentic system achieves an exponentially faster error decay governed by the lower intrinsic dimension d_max. For routing overhead, tree-based routers scale with Õ(√(log K/N_router)) while neural routers scale with O(√(K/N_router)), making tree-based routing superior in data-scarce regimes and neural routing preferable when N ≫ K. The paper positions Mixture-of-Experts as a special case (single-layer routing, C(G) ≈ ΣL_u) that is inherently stable, while general Agentic AI extends to arbitrary DAG compositions with a Topology Factor C(G) that can amplify or suppress error depending on edge Jacobian norms. No empirical head-to-head benchmark is provided; the superiority claim rests entirely on the derived convergence-rate gap and the structural argument that d_max ≪ D for real-world task distributions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Mixture-of-Experts lineage (Shazeer et al., 2017) from single-layer routing to arbitrary DAG compositions, formalizing 'Agentic AI' as a topological system of specialized agents. It draws on non-parametric regression theory (Stone, 1982) and Transformer approximation rates (Jiang & Li, 2024) to derive dimensionality-dependent scaling laws, then applies Natarajan dimension bounds (Jin, 2023) to quantify routing complexity. The paper explicitly positions against the 'monolithic scaling suffices for AGI' narrative (Reed et al., 2022; Agüera y Arcas & Norvig, 2023), arguing that the No Free Lunch Theorem and the curse of dimensionality make a single model fundamentally incapable of multi-peak performance across heterogeneous task manifolds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical framework providing a formal proof that agentic decomposition yields exponential sample and parameter efficiency gains over monolithic models, with the 'Average Trap' proposition giving a clean lower bound on the irreducible penalty of task heterogeneity. The topological weight and edge weight decompositions offer concrete design principles for multi-agent DAGs, reframing current multi-agent failures as topological design problems rather than fundamental limitations. However, the analysis is entirely theoretical with no empirical validation, relies on strong assumptions (orthogonal task subspaces, data-abundant regime, ideal routing), and the exponential advantage degrades under partial task overlap. Open questions include automated discovery of optimal DAG topologies, practical routing mechanisms that scale with agent count, and whether the theoretical gains materialize in real-world LLM-based agent systems.
