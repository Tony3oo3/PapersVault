## Problem

Naively combining Mixture-of-Experts architectures with diffusion-based block decoding creates a fundamental inefficiency: each forward pass processes all tokens in a block in parallel, collectively activating a large fraction of available experts, yet only a small subset of tokens are ultimately accepted. This reverts MoE dLLMs to dense-model-level parameter activation, negating the sparsity benefits and creating a critical bottleneck for latency-sensitive and edge deployment. To the best of the authors' knowledge, this is the first systematic analysis of expert activation behavior in MoE dLLMs.

## Input / Output

TEAM is a plug-and-play inference framework applied to existing MoE dLLMs (e.g., SDAR 30B-A3B with 128 experts, 8 routed per token). It takes a standard text prompt and produces generated text tokens decoded block-by-block (block size 32) over multiple denoising iterations, with an acceptance threshold of τ=0.95. The method modifies only the expert routing and KV caching logic at inference time without any retraining, operating on a single GPU (NVIDIA A100 80GB).

## Method

TEAM exploits two key observations about block-wise dLLM decoding: temporal consistency (accepted tokens are reprocessed identically across iterations) and spatial consistency (masked tokens share [MASK] embeddings and thus route to a highly overlapping expert subset). It implements three complementary strategies: (1) Delayed Caching for Decoded Tokens (DCD) caches KV pairs of accepted tokens after one additional forward pass and never refreshes, eliminating redundant expert activations from fixed context tokens; (2) Speculative Exploration for Hot Tokens (SEH) identifies tokens likely to be accepted soon (high confidence or spatially near decoded tokens) and constructs multiple parallel branches by additionally accepting top-confidence candidates, increasing per-iteration acceptance while leveraging MoE's natural compute distribution across experts; (3) Limited Activation for Cold Tokens (LAC) uses a double-round routing mechanism where cold tokens (low confidence, far from decoded) are restricted to only the experts already activated by decoded and hot tokens, preventing unnecessary expert invocations. Together these strategies reduce activated experts per forward pass by 35–39% while increasing accepted tokens per iteration by 1.5–1.7×, yielding up to 2.2× speedup with negligible quality loss.

## Results

TEAM achieves an average 1.94× speedup over vanilla MoE dLLM (SDAR 30B-A3B), with a peak of 2.20× on HumanEval, while maintaining nearly identical accuracy (average score drops only 0.07 points). The method reduces activated experts per forward pass (APF) by 35–39% and activated experts per decoded token (APT) by 58–63%, while simultaneously increasing accepted tokens per forward pass (TPF) by 1.49–1.74×. On average, TEAM requires only 6.92 activated experts to decode a single token, below the nominal routing cost of 8 experts per token.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the vanilla MoE dLLM (SDAR 30B-A3B) without any expert-activation optimization. TEAM wins decisively on all efficiency metrics: it reduces APF by 35–39%, cuts APT from ~17–18 to ~6.5–7.6 (a 58–63% reduction), and boosts TPF by 1.49–1.74×, yielding a 1.64×–2.20× end-to-end speedup. On accuracy, TEAM is essentially on par with the vanilla model: it matches or slightly improves on HumanEval (+0.61) and MBPP (+0.00), while incurring negligible drops on GSM8K (−0.30) and Math-500 (−0.60), for an average score change of only −0.07. The paper does not compare against other dLLM acceleration methods (e.g., dInfer, dKV-Cache) in the main results, positioning TEAM as the first dedicated expert-activation optimization for MoE dLLMs rather than a general dLLM speedup.

## Relation to Prior Work

TEAM builds on the block diffusion paradigm (Arriola et al., 2025) and adapts the KV caching idea from dKV-Cache (Ma et al., 2025a) by eliminating periodic refresh in favor of refresh-free delayed caching, justified by the near-autoregressive acceptance order in AR-initialized dLLMs. Its speculative exploration for hot tokens (SEH) repurposes the draft-and-verify principle of speculative decoding (Leviathan et al., 2023) to the multi-branch block decoding context, where MoE's per-expert compute distribution makes multi-branch overhead affordable on a single GPU. The paper departs from dInfer (Ma et al., 2025b), which targets cloud-scale expert-parallel deployment, by focusing on the underexploited single-GPU latency-sensitive regime and introducing the first systematic analysis of expert activation patterns specific to MoE dLLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and quantifies a fundamental inefficiency—naively combining MoE sparsity with dLLM parallel decoding activates nearly all experts per forward pass while accepting only ~3 tokens—making MoE dLLMs potentially slower per-token than their dense counterparts in latency-sensitive settings. The temporal-spatial consistency observations (high hidden-state similarity among masked tokens, near-autoregressive acceptance order) provide a principled basis for the three-strategy design and suggest that similar consistency-guided routing could benefit other sparse-parallel architectures. Limitations include evaluation on a single model (SDAR 30B-A3B) at a fixed block size of 32 on one A100 GPU; open questions include whether the consistency properties hold for non-AR-initialized dLLMs, larger expert counts, or variable block sizes, and whether the approach transfers to other MoE variants (e.g., shared-expert or fine-grained routing).
