## Problem

LVLMs hallucinate when they allocate insufficient attention to the correct visual evidence and gradually forget it during autoregressive generation. Existing decoding strategies adjust logits but do not promote internal visual attention, while attention-redistribution methods (e.g., VAR) only reallocate from attention sinks without genuinely increasing reliance on query-relevant visual content. The paper identifies that attention to visual evidence exhibits strong inter-layer discrepancy—evidence is attended to in specific layers but not others—yet no prior method exploits this pattern to isolate true evidence from sinks.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image and a text query as input to any LVLM (vision encoder + modality connector + autoregressive LLM). During inference, it first runs the model for T=10 initial tokens to extract attention weights, then constructs a normalized saliency map over visual tokens (a vector in [0,1]^{n_v}). The final output is the full generated text response with enhanced visual grounding; the saliency map itself is an intermediate artifact used to modulate attention at every subsequent generation step. The method is training-free and plug-and-play, requiring no architectural changes or external models.

## Method

ILVAD identifies visual evidence by exploiting inter-layer attention discrepancy: it averages attention from the first T generated tokens to each visual token across layers (retaining only the top-50% visually-sensitive heads), binarizes per-layer attention via a threshold τ·mean, then performs layer-by-layer subtraction max(Ã^{l+1} − Ã^l, 0) to remove attention sinks (which are salient in nearly every layer) and retain only tokens newly activated in specific layers. The accumulated activation counts are normalized into a saliency map Ŝ. During generation, attention to visual evidence tokens is exponentially enhanced (A·exp(αŜ)) on evidence-sensitive heads, and text tokens strongly grounded in visual evidence are further emphasized via an evidence-weighted score, suppressing language-prior-driven tokens. The entire procedure only modifies attention weights at inference time, making it architecture-agnostic and incurring negligible overhead.

## Results

On LLaVA-1.5-7B, ILVAD achieves the best scores on 5 out of 6 hallucination metrics: CHAIR_S of 32.6 (vs. 48.6 greedy, a 31.6% reduction), CHAIR_I of 9.42, POPE Accuracy of 85.76%, MMHal-Bench hallucination rate of 61.5, and MMHal-Bench score of 2.22. The only metric where ILVAD is not best is POPE F1 (86.12 vs. AGLA's 86.38). The method generalizes across architectures, consistently improving over greedy on LLaVA-NeXT-7B (CHAIR_S 29.2→25.8), Qwen2-VL-7B (CHAIR_S 20.8→17.8), Qwen3-VL-8B (CHAIR_S 51.6→45.2), and InternVL3-8B (CHAIR_S 21.2→16.8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ILVAD decisively outperforms all seven baselines on CHAIR_S and CHAIR_I, the most sensitive hallucination metrics: it reduces CHAIR_S from 48.6 (greedy) to 32.6, a 31.6% improvement, and beats the closest competitor VHR (34.5) by 1.9 points. On POPE Accuracy, ILVAD (85.76%) edges out AGLA (85.63%) and VHR (84.74%), but AGLA retains a narrow lead on POPE F1 (86.38 vs. 86.12). Among attention-intervention methods, ILVAD substantially surpasses VAR (CHAIR_S 52.5), SPARC (48.9), VAF (49.8), and VHR (34.5), with the gap over VHR being the most meaningful since VHR is the strongest prior attention-based method. On MMHal-Bench, ILVAD achieves the lowest hallucination rate (61.5) and highest score (2.22), outperforming VCD (61.8 / 2.20) and VHR (65.6 / 2.10). The method also consistently improves over greedy on all four additional architectures tested (LLaVA-NeXT-7B, Qwen2-VL-7B, Qwen3-VL-8B, InternVL3-8B), confirming plug-and-play generalizability without architecture-specific tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the attention-intervention line of hallucination mitigation (VAR, SPARC, VHR, VAF) by introducing a new diagnostic: Inter-Layer Visual Attention Discrepancy (ILVAD), the observation that LVLMs attend to correct visual evidence only in specific layers while attention sinks persist across nearly all layers. The method builds directly on the attention steering technique of Zhang et al. (2024) for the exponential enhancement step, but departs from prior sink-redistribution approaches (VAR, EVAS) by using layer-by-layer subtraction of binarized attention maps to *identify* evidence rather than merely *redistribute* attention away from sinks. Unlike decoding-based methods (VCD, CODE, AGLA, ONLY) that operate on logits, ILVAD intervenes purely on internal attention weights, making it orthogonal to and combinable with those approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, plug-and-play nature of ILVAD makes it immediately deployable across diverse LVLM architectures without retraining, and the consistent gains across five model families (LLaVA-1.5/NeXT, Qwen2/3-VL, InternVL3) suggest the inter-layer discrepancy is a general property rather than an artifact of a specific architecture. The visual-forgetting diagnosis (attention to evidence decays over generation steps) provides a concrete mechanistic explanation for why long-form captions hallucinate more, opening a path for step-aware interventions. Open questions include whether the inter-layer pattern holds for video or multi-image inputs, whether the fixed first-token window (T=10) is optimal for all task types, and whether the method's gains persist at much larger model scales (70B+) where attention patterns may differ.
