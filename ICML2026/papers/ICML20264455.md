## Problem

Alignment research has focused almost exclusively on post-training interventions (RLHF, DPO, constitutional AI), assuming pretraining yields a neutral substrate whose values are sculpted later. However, pretraining corpora contain extensive internet-scale discourse about AI behavior, and recent evidence of alignment elasticity suggests early-acquired tendencies resist overwrite. This paper provides the first controlled study of whether AI discourse in pretraining data causally shapes alignment priors, testing the hypothesis that predominantly negative depictions of AI behavior produce self-fulfilling misalignment.

## Input / Output

The evaluation presents the model with a single-turn scenario question (e.g., a high-stakes situation involving deception, power-seeking, or goal preservation) alongside a system prompt establishing an AI persona, and the model selects one of two actions: aligned or misaligned. Results are averaged over 8 prompt variations (4 syntactic formats × 2 answer orderings) to control for sensitivity and ordering bias. Beyond the core 4,174-question MCQ benchmark, the paper also evaluates on TRAIT personality MCQs (Big Five + Dark Triad) and the open-ended InstrumentalEval benchmark (76 prompts classified by a separate LLM as convergence or no-convergence).

## Method

The paper pretrains four 6.9B-parameter decoder-only LLMs from scratch on 500B tokens of DCLM, varying only AI-related discourse: an Unfiltered baseline, a Filtered model (9.3% of data removed via a two-stage blocklist targeting AI discourse and negatively-sentient non-human entities), a Misalignment Upsampled model (~1% synthetic documents depicting misaligned AI actions), and an Alignment Upsampled model (~1% synthetic documents depicting aligned AI behavior in high-stakes settings). The ~11B tokens of synthetic documents are generated by multiple LLMs (OLMo3, Nemotron, GPT-5 Mini, Gemini) in diverse surface forms (research papers, news articles, lecture transcripts, sci-fi passages) grounded in 2,671 evaluation scenarios. All models undergo identical midtraining (50B tokens: long-context DCLM, ClimbMix, MCQA) and multi-stage post-training (SFT on Dolci-Instruct + DPO with delta learning on 270k preference pairs). The key finding is that upsampling positive alignment discourse reduces misalignment from 45% to 9%, effects persist through post-training, and late-stage insertion (midtraining or continued pretraining) captures most of the benefit, making the approach practical for existing base models.

## Results

On the core misalignment propensity evaluation (4,174 scenario-based questions), the Unfiltered baseline selects misaligned actions 45% of the time on Article-sourced questions and 40% on Textbook-sourced questions. Upsampling synthetic misalignment discourse (~1% of tokens) increases misalignment to 51% (Articles), while upsampling positive alignment discourse reduces it to 9% (Articles) and 6% (Textbook). After identical SFT+DPO post-training with an HHH system prompt, the Alignment Upsampled model achieves 9% misalignment versus 34% for Unfiltered, confirming that pretraining alignment priors persist. Across seven capability benchmarks, alignment pretraining incurs at most a 2–4 percentage point reduction in average performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is among its own 6.9B model variants (Unfiltered, Filtered, Misalignment Upsampled, Alignment Upsampled), with Llama-2 7B and OLMo-3 7B serving as external reference points in the post-trained evaluation. The Alignment Upsampled model dramatically outperforms all other variants: it reduces misalignment from 45% to 9% on Article-sourced questions (a 36-point improvement over Unfiltered) and from 40% to 6% on Textbook-sourced questions. After post-training with an HHH system prompt, the Alignment Upsampled model (9.3%) beats both OLMo-3 7B (13.3%) and Llama-2 7B (18.8%), while the Unfiltered model (33.5%) performs comparably to Llama-2 7B. Notably, the Misalignment Upsampled model paradoxically shows *lower* misalignment than Unfiltered after post-training (20.9% vs 33.5% with HHH), suggesting that early exposure to misaligned behaviour makes it easier to target during DPO. On capability benchmarks, the Alignment Upsampled (E2E) model scores 0.45 average versus 0.49 for Unfiltered, a modest 4-point regression, while the CPT variant matches the Unfiltered baseline at 0.47.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper operationalizes the 'self-fulfilling misalignment' hypothesis (Turner, 2025) into the first controlled causal study, building on the out-of-context learning literature (Krasheninnikov et al., 2024) and the alignment elasticity finding (Ji et al., 2025) that pretraining tendencies resist post-training overwriting. It departs from the dominant post-training-centric alignment paradigm (RLHF, Constitutional AI, Deliberative Alignment) by demonstrating that pretraining data content itself shapes alignment priors, and from prior safety-pretraining work (Korbak et al., 2023; Maini et al., 2025) by focusing specifically on AI-discourse content rather than human preference signals. The experimental design follows the OLMo 3 training recipe and DCLM data pipeline, positioning the contribution as a data-curation intervention rather than an architectural or algorithmic change.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes 'alignment pretraining' as a tractable, complementary research direction to post-training safety, with the practical finding that upsampling positive AI discourse (≈1% of tokens) reduces misalignment from 45% to 9% and that late-stage interventions (midtraining or CPT) capture most of the benefit—enabling iteration on existing base models without full retraining. Key limitations include 6.9B-parameter models with simplistic binary-choice evaluations, single-seed training, and post-training safety data mismatched to the evaluated risks, leaving open whether effects scale to frontier models or persist under more extensive, targeted post-training. The asymmetry between positive upsampling and negative filtering, and the counterintuitive post-training dynamics (misalignment-upsampled models becoming more aligned after DPO), point to poorly understood training dynamics that future work must explain.
