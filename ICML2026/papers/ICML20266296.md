## Problem

Safe offline RL requires policies to improve beyond the behavior policy while staying within the data support to avoid catastrophic OOD actions. Existing latent generative approaches (CVAEs, diffusion models) impose OOD regularization only implicitly through reconstruction losses or support constraints, and lack a tractable mechanism to explicitly quantify and regulate how much the policy distribution deviates from the learned data manifold during improvement.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a fixed offline dataset of (state, action, next-state, reward, cost) tuples collected by a behavior policy, with no online interaction. At inference, it takes a state observation s, samples u ~ N(0,I) in the base space, applies T sequential refinement steps (safety → reward → shared geometric), and decodes through the frozen flow and decoder to produce a single continuous action vector. The entire pipeline is state-conditioned: the flow prior, refiners, and decoder all receive s as a conditioning signal, and the base-space dimensionality d is fixed at training time.

## Method

GSCO builds a conditional normalizing flow that defines a bijective mapping between a standard Gaussian base space and a latent space, enabling exact likelihood evaluation and tractable inverse transforms. The key theoretical insight is that constraining KL divergence in the base space yields explicit upper bounds on the induced Wasserstein-2 distance and total variation of the action-space policy (via Talagrand's inequality, Pinsker's inequality, and the data-processing inequality), converting OOD control into a measurable base-space constraint. Training proceeds in two stages: first, a feasibility-weighted ELBO (weighted by HJ reachability scores) shapes the flow prior to concentrate density on empirically safe regions, augmented by a base-space density-shaping loss that pulls high-reward feasible actions toward high-density Gaussian modes; second, three decoupled refiners—safety, reward, and a shared geometric field (a single conditional affine coupling layer)—perform sequential small-step updates in the frozen base space, with the shared refiner penalizing base-space norm, local volume expansion, and proximal distortion as a lightweight surrogate for the theoretical KL control target.

## Results

GSCO achieves the lowest normalized cost (violation rate) across all three benchmark suites: 0.31 on Safety-Gymnasium, 0.07 on Bullet-Safety-Gym, and 0.15 on Safe MetaDrive, compared to the next-best safe methods (FISOR at 0.40, 0.17, and 0.38 respectively). On reward, GSCO is competitive but mildly conservative: 0.29 on Safety-Gymnasium (vs. CDT's 0.51), 0.52 on Bullet-Safety-Gym (vs. CDT's 0.73), and 0.42 on Safe MetaDrive (vs. LSPC's 0.71). In direct OOD measurements (Table 6), GSCO achieves the lowest OOD rate and distance on all three representative tasks (e.g., 0.023 rate / 0.114 distance on BallCircle vs. FISOR's 0.146 / 0.203 and LSPC's 0.014 / 0.188).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GSCO consistently achieves the lowest cost (best safety) among all five baselines across all three suites, with the largest margin on Bullet-Safety-Gym (0.07 vs. FISOR's 0.17 and LSPC's 0.88) and Safe MetaDrive (0.15 vs. FISOR's 0.38 and LSPC's 1.09). On Safety-Gymnasium, GSCO ties FISOR and LSPC on reward (0.29) while halving the cost (0.31 vs. 0.40 and 0.59), but trails CDT substantially on reward (0.29 vs. 0.51) at the cost of CDT's higher violation rate (1.08). On Bullet-Safety-Gym, GSCO matches or exceeds LSPC on reward (0.52 vs. 0.50) while reducing cost by over 90% (0.07 vs. 0.88), and outperforms FISOR on both reward (0.52 vs. 0.43) and cost (0.07 vs. 0.17). On Safe MetaDrive, GSCO is the most conservative safe method (cost 0.15) but trails LSPC (0.71) and BCQL (0.64) on reward, reflecting the paper's noted conservatism when high-reward and low-cost regions have limited overlap. BCQL and CPQ are dominated by GSCO on cost in most settings (BCQL: 2.82/1.71/4.37; CPQ: 2.78/3.10/0.12), confirming that Lagrangian and penalty-based approaches struggle to enforce near-zero violations without sacrificing reward.

## Relation to Prior Work

GSCO builds on two main threads: (1) flow-based generative policies for offline RL (CNF, Akimov et al. 2022), which already exploit bijectivity to bound the latent space, and (2) feasibility-guided safe offline RL (FISOR, Zheng et al. 2024), which uses Hamilton-Jacobi reachability to shape a diffusion-based policy toward safe regions. The key departure is replacing the diffusion backbone with a conditional normalizing flow to obtain exact likelihoods and a tractable base space, then performing policy improvement through decoupled refiners directly in that base space with an explicit geometric regularizer. This contrasts with CVAE-based methods (LSPC, PLAS) that rely on implicit ELBO-based support constraints and with diffusion-based methods that lack closed-form likelihoods, making principled OOD quantification intractable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical bridge—via Wasserstein and total-variation bounds—between a measurable base-space KL divergence and downstream action-space policy shift, turning OOD control from an implicit heuristic into a tunable geometric constraint. This is practically useful for safety-critical deployment where violation rates must be kept near zero. However, the method remains limited to low-dimensional state benchmarks, the Lipschitz constants in the bounds are not numerically certified, and the base-space regularizer is an acknowledged surrogate rather than an exact KL estimator. Open questions include scaling to image-based observations, adapting to budgeted (soft) safety formulations, and whether the decoupled refiner protocol generalizes to multi-agent or partially-observable settings.
