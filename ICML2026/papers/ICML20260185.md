## Problem

Asynchronous RL for LLM post-training decouples data generation from policy updates to improve throughput, but the resulting distributional mismatch between stale behavior policies and the current policy causes unstable training and degraded performance. Existing methods either rely on heuristic freshness-based selection (using only the most recent policy's samples) or apply uniform importance-sampling corrections without measuring per-sample distribution alignment, leaving high-quality samples from older policies unused.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260185/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes text prompts (math word problems or code tasks), trajectories generated by the K=4 most recent behavior policies stored in a replay buffer, and per-trajectory reward signals. It outputs updated policy parameters and generated reasoning solutions. A key operational detail is that the off-policy degree is computed per trajectory using the current policy's log-probabilities and the approximated minimum-variance behavior distribution, and only the top-k lowest-degree samples from the entire buffer are retained for each optimization step (batch size 256, single epoch on Qwen3-1.7B/4B).

## Method

D-ARL is a two-stage asynchronous RL framework. First, it derives a minimum-variance behavior policy π_q* (Theorem 4.1) that minimizes the variance of the importance-sampling return estimator, then defines a normalized off-policy degree metric (Definition 4.2) measuring each trajectory's alignment with π_q*; the top-k samples with the smallest degree are selected from a replay buffer spanning K=4 behavior policies. Second, it formulates policy optimization as a multi-behavior off-policy problem and derives optimal policy-level aggregation weights (Theorem 4.3) proportional to each policy's sample count times the inverse variance of its importance-weighted returns, ensuring lower-variance sources receive higher weight. The framework is instantiated with GRPO as the base policy-gradient algorithm and is general enough to apply to PPO or other policy-gradient methods.

## Results

On Qwen3-1.7B, D-ARL achieves 65.2% average accuracy across six reasoning benchmarks (GSM8K, LightEval, MATH-500, AIME24, LiveCodeBench, HumanEval), outperforming the SOTA asynchronous baseline CISPO (58.8%) by 6.4% and the synchronous GRPO baseline (53.0%) by 12.2%. On Qwen3-4B, D-ARL reaches 74.3% average accuracy, a 5.6% gain over CISPO (68.7%) and 10.6% over GRPO (63.7%). In terms of training efficiency, D-ARL requires only 65.3% of the training steps (34.7% fewer) and 37.2% less wall-clock time than the SOTA asynchronous method to reach comparable accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

D-ARL consistently outperforms all four baselines across every benchmark and both model sizes. Against the synchronous GRPO baseline, D-ARL gains 12.2% (Qwen3-1.7B) and 10.6% (Qwen3-4B) in average accuracy, showing that asynchronous training with proper distribution matching can surpass even on-policy training. Against the SOTA asynchronous method CISPO, D-ARL improves by 6.4% on Qwen3-1.7B (65.2% vs. 58.8%) and 5.6% on Qwen3-4B (74.3% vs. 68.7%), with the largest per-benchmark gains on HumanEval (+11.0% for 1.7B, +9.1% for 4B) and AIME24 (+10.0% for 1.7B, +6.7% for 4B). The ARLHF baseline (one-step async GRPO without explicit IS correction) performs worst among asynchronous methods, confirming that naive staleness handling degrades performance. Decoupled PPO shows competitive results on AIME24 (26.7% for 1.7B, matching D-ARL) but lags significantly on other benchmarks, particularly GSM8K (64.4% vs. 84.8%). In training efficiency, D-ARL requires 34.7% fewer steps and 37.2% less wall-clock time than CISPO to reach the same target accuracy on Qwen3-1.7B.

## Relation to Prior Work

D-ARL extends the GRPO policy optimization framework (Shao et al., 2024) and the classical importance sampling theory (Hanna et al., 2021) to the asynchronous RL setting for LLM post-training. It departs from existing asynchronous methods (ARLHF, CISPO, Decoupled PPO) that uniformly use all samples from the most recent behavior policy, instead introducing a principled distribution-matched sample selection mechanism derived from a minimum-variance behavior policy theorem. The multi-behavior policy optimization with optimal policy-level weights generalizes standard single-behavior IS correction to aggregate heterogeneous samples from K distinct behavior policies, drawing on multiple importance sampling ideas but replacing sample-level weights with a simpler policy-level formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a core bottleneck in LLM RL post-training—distributional mismatch in asynchronous pipelines—and provides both a theoretical foundation (minimum-variance behavior policy, optimal aggregation weights) and a practical framework that achieves meaningful gains in both accuracy and sample efficiency. The finding that older behavior policies can contribute the majority of high-quality training samples (e.g., 71% from π_{t-3} for Qwen3-4B on GSM8K) challenges the prevailing assumption that only the most recent policy is useful, opening questions about optimal buffer sizes and whether similar principles apply to larger-scale or multi-agent settings. Limitations include evaluation restricted to Qwen3-1.7B/4B on a single 4-GPU node with one epoch of training, leaving open whether the approach scales to 70B+ models or longer training horizons where policy drift is more severe.
