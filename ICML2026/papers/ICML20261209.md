## Problem

Implicit process reward models (PRMs) trained from sequence-level outcome labels suffer a train-inference mismatch: the log-likelihood-ratio reward is constrained only as a sequence aggregate during training, yet is decomposed into token- or step-level scores for partial prefixes at inference and during RL. This leaves local credits weakly identified, causing distribution-wide scoring to amplify misleading advantages and degrade step-level error localization under format shift.

## Input / Output

The reward model takes a prompt x and a partial reasoning prefix s_t = (x, y_{<t}) and outputs a length-normalized prefix value V_φ(s_{t+1})/t, which is mapped through a sigmoid to yield a margin-separated success score. For policy optimization, the method additionally computes one-step TD advantages A_φ^TD(s_t, y_t') for every candidate token y_t' in a truncated high-probability set (π_old(y_t'|s_t) ≥ P_min), producing dense per-token signals without extra rollouts. The policy model consumes a text prompt and autoregressively generates a full reasoning trajectory, with the final output being the generated response scored by a deterministic verifier.

## Method

IPVRM replaces the sequence-level Implicit PRM objective with dense prefix-level supervision: every visited prefix along a trajectory is scored by a margin-based binary cross-entropy loss against the terminal outcome, directly aligning the training target with the inference-time query of evaluating partial prefixes. Local optimization signals are then derived as temporal-difference differences between consecutive prefix values, measuring how a continuation changes the predicted probability of eventual correctness. DistRL builds a dual-branch PPO objective: the sampled-token branch uses standard GRPO-style GAE advantages combined with group-normalized outcome rewards, while the candidate-token branch applies clipped TD advantages over high-probability vocabulary tokens using the behavior policy π_old as the log-ratio reference to minimize importance-sampling variance. An online RM update mechanism with Adaptive Difficulty Boundary (shifting the sigmoid by prompt-level expected accuracy) and Dynamic Loss Weighting (reweighting by outcome rarity) stabilizes reward-model refresh under label imbalance during RL.

## Results

On the primary policy evaluation (AVG@8 across AIME, MATH-500, Minerva-Math, OlympiadBench, and AMC), DistRL w/ IPVRM achieves the best average on all four backbones: 16.7 on Qwen3-0.6B, 37.7 on Qwen3-8B, 4.8 on Llama3.2-1B, and 10.3 on Llama3.2-3B. On the reward-model side, IPVRM raises PROCESSBENCH average F1 from 25.4 (Implicit PRM) to 39.1 on Qwen3-0.6B and from 42.2 to 45.6 on Qwen3-8B, while also improving Best-of-N reranking (e.g., 28.1 vs. 25.1 average BoN on Qwen3-0.6B). DistRL adds a further 0.1–1.4 points over GRPO with the same reward model, confirming that distribution-level candidate-token updates provide consistent but modest gains when paired with reliable prefix values.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DistRL w/ IPVRM outperforms all seven baselines on average across all four backbones. Against the strongest trajectory-level RL baselines (PRIME and SPRO), it gains +1.4 on Qwen3-0.6B (16.7 vs. 15.3) and +2.4 on Qwen3-8B (37.7 vs. 35.3), while on Llama3.2-1B/3B the margins are smaller (+0.4/+0.4). Compared to GRPO with the same IPVRM reward, DistRL adds +0.4 to +1.4 points, confirming the distribution-level candidate-token update contributes a consistent but modest boost. On the reward-model evaluation side, IPVRM substantially outperforms DPO-RM and Implicit PRM on PROCESSBENCH F1 (e.g., 39.1 vs. 27.8/25.4 on Qwen3-0.6B), indicating that prefix-value supervision resolves the train-inference mismatch that limits sequence-level implicit objectives under format shift. IPVRM also matches or slightly exceeds the explicit Q-RM baseline on Best-of-N reranking (28.1 vs. 27.1 on Qwen3-0.6B) while requiring no step-level annotations. EndoRM, evaluated only on reward-model metrics, consistently underperforms all other methods on both BoN and PROCESSBENCH, suggesting its token-level reward decay strategy is less effective for process verification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the implicit process reward model (Implicit PRM) line of work (Yuan et al., 2025a; Cui et al., 2025a), which trains log-likelihood-ratio rewards from outcome-only labels to avoid expensive step annotations. The key departure is a conceptual reframing: rather than treating the cumulative log-ratio as an arbitrary token-level reward decomposition constrained only at the sequence level, IPVRM reinterprets it as a prefix-conditioned value estimating the probability of eventual correctness, supervised at every visited prefix. On the RL side, DistRL extends GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) by adding a distribution-level PPO branch over high-probability candidate tokens using one-step TD advantages, complementing the trajectory-level GAE branch. The adaptive balancing mechanisms (ADB/DLW) for online RM updates draw on standard importance-weighting and focal-loss ideas applied to the specific problem of label imbalance in behavior-policy rollouts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The train-inference mismatch identified here is a practical bottleneck for anyone deploying implicit PRMs in online RLVR pipelines: sequence-level objectives produce scores that degrade sharply under format shift, exactly the regime where process verification is most needed. The PROCESSBENCH results (+11.3 F1 over DPO-RM on Qwen3-0.6B) demonstrate that prefix-value supervision meaningfully improves step-level error localization, and the finding that DistRL amplifies unreliable rewards but complements reliable ones provides a clear design principle for combining dense token-level signals with trajectory-level credit assignment. Limitations include reliance on verifiable outcomes (math, code), the TD signal's weak correlation with true long-horizon return (Pearson 0.0226), and modest absolute policy gains (0.4–1.4 points over GRPO with the same RM). Open questions include whether prefix-value learning transfers to open-ended domains without ground-truth verifiers and whether the approach scales to much larger models where the implicit RM's capacity may change the train-inference dynamics.
