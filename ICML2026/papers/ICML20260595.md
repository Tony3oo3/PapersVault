## Problem

Existing Contrastive Decoding (CD) methods for LVLM hallucination mitigation perform subtraction at the logit level, which fundamentally compromises generation coherence—producing invalid tokens, abrupt semantic transitions, and structural degradation—while necessitating restrictive penalty mechanisms that further constrain output diversity. This creates an inherent quality-hallucination trade-off: logit-level contrastive operations effectively suppress hallucinations but inevitably degrade linguistic quality, a problem that becomes more severe as model depth increases.

## Input / Output

The method operates as a training-free plug-in during autoregressive decoding, taking an RGB image and a text query as input and producing standard generated text (captions or VQA answers). At each decoding step, it computes parallel attention maps from the original input and a hallucination-inducing perturbation (Gaussian-noised image, adversarial prompt, or masked attention regions), then applies contrastive subtraction directly on the attention weight matrices before the value aggregation and downstream layers. No penalty filtering or vocabulary truncation is required, and the method is compatible with any existing hallucination-induction strategy.

## Method

ACD relocates the contrastive subtraction from the discrete, unbounded logit space to the continuous attention layer, where weights reside in a probability simplex and arithmetic combinations yield semantically smooth transitions rather than the drastic probability shifts caused by logit exponentiation. The core operation computes A_t^ACD = (1+α)A_t^clean − α(A_t^noisy ⊙ M_t), where the binary mask M_t is determined by the Adaptive Subtraction Strategy (ASS). ASS distinguishes two attention shift patterns: positions where clean attention exceeds noisy attention (information degradation, ~32% of positions) are amplified by (1+α) to reinforce correct visual grounding, while positions where noisy attention exceeds clean attention (hallucination risk, ~68%) are suppressed via contrastive subtraction to attenuate spurious activations. This bidirectional signal-to-noise filtering actively expands the margin between relevant and irrelevant regions beyond the original clean baseline. Theoretically, attention perturbations are bounded by a dimension-independent constant (√2) and confined to a low-rank subspace via value aggregation, in contrast to logit perturbations that scale as O(√d·‖W_head‖₂), explaining why ACD eliminates the need for penalty mechanisms while preserving coherence.

## Results

On CHAIR (LLaVA-1.5-7B), ACD integration reduces instance-level hallucination (Chair_i) by 11.7% over VCD (16.2→14.3), 10.3% over ICD (15.5→13.9), and 5.7% over SID (14.1→13.3), while simultaneously improving recall by 0.6–2.3 points. On coherence metrics, VCD degrades CIDEr by 13.2% (0.266→0.231) and BLEU-4 by 42.6% (0.094→0.054), whereas VCD+ACD fully recovers and slightly exceeds baseline (CIDEr 0.266, BLEU-4 0.096, GPT-5 score 0.980 vs. 0.920 for Regular). On Qwen3-VL-8B, VCD produces 69.2% malformed outputs on MME due to logit-level suppression of EOS tokens, while ACD exhibits zero malformed outputs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ACD is evaluated as a plug-in on top of three logit-level contrastive decoding baselines: VCD (Leng et al., CVPR 2024), ICD (Wang et al., 2024), and SID (Huo et al., 2024). Across all six model–method combinations on CHAIR, ACD consistently wins on both hallucination reduction and recall: Chair_i drops by 1.1–1.8 points and recall rises by 0.6–2.3 points relative to each baseline. The largest absolute gains appear with VCD+ACD on LLaVA-1.5 (Chair_i 16.2→14.3, −11.7%) and SID+ACD on Qwen-VL (Chair_i 12.7→11.1, −12.6%). Critically, ACD also resolves the coherence degradation that all three baselines suffer: VCD drops CIDEr by 13.2% and BLEU-4 by 42.6% relative to Regular decoding, while VCD+ACD restores CIDEr to 0.266 and pushes BLEU-4 to 0.096 (+2.1% over Regular). On the harder POPE Adversarial split, ACD yields the largest F1 improvements (e.g., +1.1 for VCD+ACD on LLaVA-1.5, +1.0 for VCD+ACD on Qwen-VL), confirming that adaptive attention modulation is most effective under high hallucination pressure. On modern Qwen3-VL-8B, VCD catastrophically fails with 69.2% malformed MME outputs, whereas ACD produces zero malformed outputs, demonstrating that the coherence advantage of attention-level intervention scales with model depth.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the contrastive decoding (CD) lineage for LVLM hallucination mitigation—specifically VCD, ICD, and SID—by relocating the contrastive subtraction from the discrete logit space to the continuous attention layer. It explicitly differentiates from prior attention-based methods (FarSight, DAMRO, HICD, MoD) that modify attention but still perform the critical subtraction at the logit level. The Adaptive Subtraction Strategy (ASS) introduces a position-dependent binary mask that distinguishes signal degradation from hallucination risk, departing from the uniform subtraction assumed by all prior CD variants. The theoretical contribution (dimension-independent perturbation bounds, low-rank subspace confinement) provides a principled justification for why attention-level intervention is inherently smoother than logit-level intervention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a practical failure mode that practitioners deploying CD methods may encounter: logit-level subtraction can produce catastrophic malformed outputs (69.2% on MME) in deeper architectures like Qwen3-VL-8B, a problem that worsens with model depth. ACD's elimination of penalty mechanisms removes a heuristic hyperparameter (β) that has plagued CD deployment, and its plug-in compatibility with existing hallucination-induction strategies makes adoption straightforward. Open questions include whether the dual-forward-pass overhead remains acceptable at larger scales, whether the theoretical smoothness bounds tighten with deeper architectures, and whether ACD's gains persist when combined with fine-tuning-based hallucination mitigation rather than purely inference-time methods.
