## Problem

Existing MLLM knowledge distillation methods rely on static next-token alignment over fixed, off-policy (ground-truth-conditioned) sequences, neglecting the dynamic token interactions that encode the core capabilities for multimodal understanding and generation. Specifically, they fail to model (1) vision-instruction token interactions during prefilling, which enable instruction-aware visual information extraction, and (2) intra-response token interactions during decoding, which govern coherent autoregressive generation, resulting in shallow statistical mimicry rather than deep capability transfer and leaving the student vulnerable to exposure bias at test time.

## Input / Output

The distilled student MLLM takes an RGB image and a free-form textual instruction as input and generates a text response. During distillation, both the teacher (7–8B parameter MLLM) and student (1–2B parameter MLLM) process the same image-instruction pair, but the student additionally receives supervision from the teacher's instruction-to-vision attention sub-matrices and token-to-token transition probability matrices. Training follows a two-stage protocol (1.2M captioning samples for pretraining, 2.4M mixed captioning/VQA samples for fine-tuning), with KD losses applied only during the fine-tuning stage.

## Method

Align-TI introduces two distillation components targeting the two token interaction types. IVA (Instruction-aware Vision Alignment) first identifies the most instruction-relevant transformer layer via the Instruction-Relevance Score (IRS), which measures how differently a layer's attention patterns respond to different instructions; it then uses the instruction-to-vision attention weights from that layer to modulate per-visual-token KL divergence, directing the student's limited representational capacity toward instruction-salient regions rather than uniformly across all visual tokens. TPA (Transition Probability Alignment) extends beyond next-token alignment by matching token-to-token transition probability matrices: at each decoding step it samples d candidate tokens from the student's on-policy distribution and aligns the teacher-student KL divergence conditioned on each sampled token, expanding alignment coverage from O(|V|) to O(|V|²) generation paths and mitigating exposure bias. A ribbon attention mask enables all d parallel transition computations in a single forward pass, keeping total training overhead at 1.4× Vanilla KD. The overall objective is the sum of SFT loss, IVA loss, Vanilla KD loss, and TPA loss.

## Results

Align-TI-2B (Qwen3-1.7B) achieves an average score of 73.6 across six benchmarks (GQA, SQA, TextVQA, POPE, MME, MMB), outperforming the much larger LLaVA-1.5-7B (68.8) by 7.0% relative improvement and LLaVA-MoD-2B (68.9) by 4.8%. The distilled Align-TI-1B (Qwen3-0.6B) reaches 69.7 average, surpassing LLaVA-KD-1B (63.4) by 6.3 points. Ablation shows TPA contributes +2.1 and IVA contributes +0.8 over the SFT baseline (64.3), with the combined method reaching 66.7. Training overhead is modest: 1.4× Vanilla KD (509h vs 355h) versus GKD's 2.7× (962h).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Align-TI-2B (Qwen3-1.7B, AVG 73.6) decisively outperforms LLaVA-1.5-7B (68.8) by 7.0% relative, MobileVLM-V2-7B (72.0) by 2.2%, and the MoE-based distillation baseline LLaVA-MoD-2B (68.9) by 4.8%, despite having roughly 3–4× fewer parameters. At the ~1B scale, Align-TI (Qwen3-0.6B, AVG 69.7) surpasses LLaVA-KD-1B (63.4) by 6.3 points and LLaVA-MoD-1B (no AVG reported, but 67.0 on MME) across all benchmarks. Against LLM-specific distillation strategies (Table 3), Align-TI (66.7) outperforms FKL (65.0), MiniLLM (64.5), JSD (63.7), and GKD (62.7), with GKD's on-policy alignment actually degrading performance by 2.3 points relative to FKL in multimodal settings. The paper also validates robustness on a different architecture (MobileLLaMA-1.4B student), where Align-TI gains +2.1 over SFT versus only +0.2 for Vanilla KD. A controlled experiment matching training time (509h vs 533h) confirms the gains are algorithmic rather than a function of extra compute: Vanilla KD extended to 1.5 epochs reaches only 65.3 vs Align-TI's 66.7.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MLLM knowledge distillation line initiated by LLaVA-KD (Cai et al., 2024), which established token-level alignment as the effective distillation signal for multimodal models. It departs from all prior MLLM KD methods (LLaVA-KD, Align-KD, LLaVA-MoD, MoVE-KD) that perform static next-token alignment on ground-truth prefixes, by introducing two new interaction-aware objectives: IVA, which replaces uniform visual-token alignment with instruction-conditioned attention-weighted alignment, and TPA, which aligns token-to-token transition probability matrices rather than only single-step next-token distributions. The TPA component draws a conceptual parallel to exposure-bias mitigation in imitation learning (Ross et al., 2011), while the IVA component builds on the observation that cross-modal attention maps encode instruction-specific visual focus that evolves non-monotonically across transformer depth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 2B distilled model outperforming a 7B model by 7% with 1.7× faster inference and 3× less memory directly enables edge deployment of competitive MLLMs. The TPA's demonstrated reduction of exposure bias from ~30% to <10% (measured via %ExAccErr) provides a concrete mechanism for why transition-level alignment helps, and the controlled experiment showing that matching Vanilla KD's training budget yields only 0.3 average gain (vs. 1.7 for Align-TI) strengthens the claim that the gains are algorithmic, not computational. Limitations include validation only on image-text tasks (no video), the IRS metric requiring a separate calibration pass over attention maps, and the diminishing returns observed when scaling the teacher beyond 4B, suggesting the student's representational capacity is the binding constraint. Open questions include whether TPA generalizes to video or continuous-output settings, and whether the ribbon-attention parallelization strategy extends to longer sequences or larger d values.
