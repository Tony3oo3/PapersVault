## Problem

In competitive newsvendor games—continuous-action general-sum games with strategic substitutes—equilibrium existence and uniqueness are well established, but whether independent learning algorithms actually converge to equilibrium in repeated play remained open. The paper identifies strict monotonicity of the payoff gradient as the structural property that rules out cyclic or divergent learning dynamics, and provides the first such result for a competitive inventory game with demand spillovers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262185/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each agent observes only its own private cost type (a scalar in [0,1] drawn from a known discrete distribution) and the fixed economic parameters (selling price s, penalty p, salvage q, substitution a); no opponent state or global features are observed. The agent outputs a continuous inventory level y_i ∈ [0,1], or in the Bayesian setting a type-contingent policy mapping each cost type to an inventory action. For equilibrium verification, the action space is discretized into 1024 grid points and best responses are computed via 12 million Monte Carlo episodes against the learned policy.

## Method

The core theoretical contribution proves strict monotonicity of the marginal profit operator for the two-player competitive newsvendor game with uniform demand on [0,1] and perfect substitution (a=1), in both the complete-information and Bayesian (private cost) settings, via a four-case algebraic analysis of the piecewise quadratic payoff structure. This places the game in the class of strictly monotone variational inequalities, guaranteeing equilibrium uniqueness and global convergence of first-order VI solvers (extragradient, Mirror-Prox). Empirically, the paper trains independent PPO agents (2-layer fully connected networks, 64 SeLU nodes, Adam at lr=1e-5, clipping ε=0.2, entropy 0.01, 20k steps/iteration) in self-play across symmetric and asymmetric cost configurations with both uniform and Gaussian demand. Convergence is certified not by reward stabilization but by an equilibrium verifier that computes the maximal unilateral deviation gain via Monte Carlo simulation, confirming that learned policies match Nash or Bayes-Nash equilibria to within <1% relative error.

## Results

In the complete-information symmetric game (s=1, c=0.5, p=q=0, uniform demand), PPO agents converge to the closed-form Nash equilibrium y*≈0.5858 within ~20 training iterations, achieving relative errors of 0.06% and 0.44% for the two agents after 100 iterations. In asymmetric cost settings (c₁=0.5, c₂=0.8), learned actions match grid-computed best responses with relative errors below 0.4%. In the Bayesian game with private costs, type-contingent policies converge to best responses with relative errors below 1% after 400 iterations, under both uniform and Gaussian demand. Across all scenarios, standard deviation of learned actions remains below 2% of the mean over 10 random seeds, indicating stable convergence without cycling or divergence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not benchmark PPO against competing DRL algorithms in the main text; instead, the primary comparison is between learned policies and theoretical equilibrium predictions (closed-form Nash equilibria from Theorem 4.1, or grid-search best responses in asymmetric/Bayesian settings). PPO achieves relative errors below 0.5% in complete-information games and below 1% in Bayesian games, confirming convergence to the unique equilibrium guaranteed by strict monotonicity. The authors note in Appendix E that REINFORCE and TD3 also converge, indicating the result is not specific to PPO's clipping mechanism. The paper positions its contribution as a structural explanation for *why* convergence occurs (strict monotonicity of the payoff gradient) rather than as an algorithmic improvement, contrasting with reported convergence failures in Bertrand oligopoly and auction models where monotonicity is violated (Calvano et al., 2020; Bichler et al., 2025).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Parlar's (1988) competitive newsvendor game by proving strict monotonicity of the payoff gradient—a structural property previously unestablished for competitive inventory games with demand spillovers—and connects it to the variational-inequality framework for Bayesian games introduced by Ui (2016). Empirically, it builds on the DRL-based equilibrium verification methodology of Pieroth et al. (2025), applying PPO self-play to a game where prior work (Güler et al., 2018) characterized equilibria analytically but left learning dynamics unaddressed. The paper deliberately contrasts its findings with Bertrand oligopoly and auction models (Calvano et al., 2020; Bichler et al., 2025), where monotonicity is violated and learning is known to cycle or diverge, positioning strict monotonicity as the key structural condition separating convergent from non-convergent competitive games.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The strict monotonicity result provides the first structural explanation for why independent DRL agents converge in competitive newsvendor games despite strategic substitutability, a property that typically induces instability. This bridges a gap between equilibrium theory (variational inequalities) and practical multi-agent RL, offering a diagnostic criterion—monotonicity—for predicting convergence in continuous-action general-sum games. Open questions include whether the result extends to n-firm settings, non-uniform demand distributions (the theory is restricted to uniform), and whether monotonicity-exploiting algorithms (extragradient, Mirror-Prox) can be made competitive with PPO in this domain. The narrow duopoly scope and reliance on uniform demand for the theoretical guarantee limit immediate applicability but establish a clear template for future extensions.
