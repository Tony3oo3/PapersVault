## Problem

Optimizing data mixtures for LLM mid-training requires identifying the best composition of domain-specific datasets (math, code, SFT, web), but current approaches rely on computationally prohibitive full-scale training trials or heuristic human tuning. Existing automated methods (scaling laws, proxy regressors, Bayesian optimization) still demand dozens to hundreds of subscale training runs and are primarily calibrated to perplexity rather than downstream task performance, creating an objective misalignment that hinders practical deployment.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained MoE base model (8B or 16B total params, 1.4B active) and K domain-specific datasets, and produces optimal data mixing ratios on the (K-1)-simplex that are directly applied to the final mid-training run. A key constraint is that domain experts are trained on a minimal token budget (<5B tokens) with a constant learning rate to preserve geometric alignment, and the output ratios are validated via actual full-scale training (200B tokens) before deployment. The intermediate output includes per-capability LightGBM regressors that predict benchmark scores from any merging configuration, enabling flexible utility-driven selection.

## Method

MergeMix reframes data mixture optimization as a model merging problem, grounded in the theoretical observation that linear weight interpolation between domain experts shares identical first-order gradient dynamics with mixed-data training under shared initialization, with discrepancies confined to second-order curvature terms. The pipeline has three stages: (1) train K domain-specific experts from the same base model using constant learning rate and a restricted horizon to keep them in a local neighborhood; (2) sample N=40 merging configurations via coarse grid search, evaluate each merged model on M downstream benchmarks, and fit per-capability LightGBM regressors to map the simplex to a performance surface; (3) optimize a user-defined utility function (e.g., macro-average of normalized scores) over the simplex to identify the optimal merging weights, which are then adopted as the data mixing ratios. This converts the dominant cost from expensive training trials to negligible element-wise weight addition and inference-based evaluation, achieving a >100× cost reduction while maintaining Spearman rank correlation >0.9 with actual training outcomes.

## Results

On the 8B MoE mid-training benchmark (200B-token budget, 4 domains), MergeMix achieves the highest average score of 58.4, surpassing the manually tuned baseline (57.9) and Adapted RegMix (58.1). The gains are most pronounced in Math (60.4 vs. 59.3 Manual) and Code (51.6 vs. 50.5 Manual), while Language performance is tied with Manual at 78.9. The method achieves a Spearman rank correlation of ρ=0.92 overall (ρ=0.98 for Knowledge) between merged-weight predictions and actual training outcomes, and reduces search cost by over 100× (20B tokens vs. 2T tokens for exhaustive manual tuning).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MergeMix outperforms the Adapted RegMix baseline (Liu et al., 2025) on every domain: Math 60.4 vs. 59.7, Code 51.6 vs. 51.5, Knowledge 55.6 vs. 55.5, Language 78.9 vs. 78.3, and Reasoning 57.3 vs. 57.0, yielding a 0.3-point average advantage. Against the Manual baseline (10 rounds of 200B-token human-in-the-loop tuning), MergeMix wins on Math (+1.1), Code (+1.1), Knowledge (+0.1), and Reasoning (+0.2), while tying on Language (78.9), for a net +0.5 average gain. The Uniform baseline is substantially weaker across all domains (54.2 average), confirming that data composition matters. Notably, MergeMix achieves these results using only 20B tokens of expert training (4 experts × 5B tokens) plus negligible merging/inference cost, compared to 2T tokens consumed by the manual search, representing a >100× cost reduction. In the hierarchical fine-grained setting (Table 3), the fully automated ⟨MergeMix, MergeMix⟩ strategy (58.2 avg) also surpasses the fully manual ⟨Manual, Manual⟩ baseline (57.9 avg), further validating the approach at finer granularity.

## Relation to Prior Work

MergeMix repurposes the model merging literature (Wortsman et al., 2022; Izmailov et al., 2018) as a proxy for data mixture optimization, a problem previously addressed by scaling-law fitting (Ye et al., 2025; Shukor et al., 2025), proxy-model regression (RegMix, Liu et al., 2025), Bayesian optimization (ADMIRE-BayesOpt), or iterative bootstrapping (CLIMB). The key conceptual departure is reframing mixture search as weight-space interpolation rather than loss-space prediction, grounded by a first-order Taylor-equivalence argument between mixed-data gradients and merged-expert updates under shared initialization. The WSM constant-LR schedule (Tian et al., 2025) and linear mode connectivity theory (Frankle et al., 2020) provide the training protocol and geometric justification that keep experts within a local neighborhood where the proxy remains valid.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 100× cost reduction (20B vs. 2T tokens) for identifying near-optimal mid-training mixtures is directly actionable for industrial LLM teams that currently rely on expensive iterative ablation runs. Cross-scale transferability (8B proxy → 16B target) and the finding that a single static mixture suffices (no dynamic recalibration needed) further simplify deployment. Open questions include whether the local-regime assumption holds for dense architectures, whether the method generalizes beyond four coarse domains to the hundreds of fine-grained datasets in real corpora, and whether the first-order approximation degrades at larger scales (100B+) where second-order interactions may become non-negligible.
