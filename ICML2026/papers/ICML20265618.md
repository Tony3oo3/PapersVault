## Problem

Dynamic LiDAR novel view synthesis suffers from motion-induced supervision conflicts and geometric ambiguities: moving objects violate multi-view consistency, creating conflicting depth supervision across frames, while simultaneously entangling genuine high-frequency details with ghosting artifacts. Existing methods optimize all points uniformly from the start, forcing the network to indiscriminately fit unreliable motion priors and prematurely overfit spurious high-frequency noise before stable geometry is established.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a temporal sequence of LiDAR scans (4D points with 3D coordinates and reflectance intensity) aligned with SE(3) sensor poses and timestamps, projected into range images at sensor-specific resolutions (66×1030 for 64-beam KITTI-360, 32×1080 for 32-beam nuScenes). It produces synthesized LiDAR point clouds from arbitrary novel viewpoints, with per-ray outputs of depth, reflected intensity, and ray-drop probability. The backbone uses a hybrid 4D representation combining 4 planar feature levels (base resolution 64, 8 dims each) with 8 multi-resolution hash grid levels (resolutions 512 to 2^15, 4 dims each), concatenated into a 128-dim latent vector with temporal resolution 25, plus an 8-layer Flow MLP for scene flow prediction.

## Method

MAC-NeRF introduces a motion-aware curriculum learning framework with two training-only modules. The Rectified Temporal Consistency (RTC) module maintains a historical prediction buffer updated via EMA, computes forward-backward geometric alignment errors by warping adjacent frames through predicted scene flow, and applies a binary geometric gate (with time-decaying threshold) to filter erroneous motion before assimilation; it enforces both historical consistency loss and physical alignment loss to anchor predictions to verified temporal correspondences. The Confidence-Modulated Frequency Regularization (CMFR) mechanism derives a per-point geometric confidence map from the joint RTC errors, then adaptively modulates the active frequency bandwidth of the multi-resolution hash encoding—constraining uncertain regions to low-frequency bands early in training while progressively releasing high-frequency capacity as confidence grows, supplemented by a global coarse-to-fine progress mask and a late-release schedule in the final 20% of training. Both modules are exclusively active during training, so inference incurs no additional overhead over the LiDAR4D backbone.

## Results

On KITTI-360 dynamic sequences, MAC-NeRF achieves the lowest Chamfer Distance of 0.0961 m and highest F-score of 0.9367, outperforming the SOTA dynamic method STGC-NeRF (CD 0.0997, F-score 0.9325) by 3.6% in CD error. On nuScenes dynamic sequences, MAC-NeRF reduces CD to 0.2111 m (vs. 0.2204 for STGC-NeRF), a 4.2% improvement, while also achieving the best depth PSNR (22.09 dB) and intensity PSNR (28.03 dB). On KITTI-360 static scenes, MAC-NeRF further reduces CD to 0.0786 m, a 5.4% improvement over STGC-NeRF (0.0831 m), confirming gains extend beyond dynamic settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAC-NeRF outperforms all eight baselines across every metric on both KITTI-360 and nuScenes dynamic sequences. Against the closest dynamic competitor STGC-NeRF (AAAI 2025), MAC-NeRF reduces CD by 3.6% on KITTI-360 (0.0961 vs. 0.0997 m) and 4.2% on nuScenes (0.2111 vs. 0.2204 m), while also improving depth PSNR by ~0.07 dB and intensity PSNR by ~0.1 dB. The Gaussian-based LiDAR-RT (CVPR 2025) trails MAC-NeRF by 11.2% in CD on KITTI-360 (0.1077 vs. 0.0961 m) and 8.4% on nuScenes (0.2300 vs. 0.2111 m). The backbone LiDAR4D (CVPR 2024) is surpassed by 11.9% in CD on KITTI-360 (0.1089 vs. 0.0961 m), confirming that the RTC and CMFR modules add meaningful gains over the base 4D representation. All static baselines (LiDARsim, NKSR, PCGen, LiDAR-NeRF, LiDAR-GS) are substantially worse, with CD errors ranging from 0.13 m to 3.22 m on KITTI-360, underscoring the difficulty of dynamic scenes for methods without explicit motion modeling. Notably, MAC-NeRF also wins on static KITTI-360 scenes (CD 0.0786 m vs. 0.0831 m for STGC-NeRF), indicating the curriculum learning strategy improves general geometric fidelity beyond just dynamic handling.

## Relation to Prior Work

MAC-NeRF builds directly on LiDAR4D's hybrid 4D representation (multi-resolution hash grids + planar features + scene flow MLP) as its backbone, and adapts FreeNeRF's frequency regularization by replacing its uniform bandwidth annealing with a spatially adaptive, confidence-modulated version. Conceptually, it departs from the uniform-optimization paradigm used by existing dynamic LiDAR NVS methods (LiDAR4D, STGC-NeRF, LiDAR-RT) by reframing training as a motion-aware curriculum: the RTC module progressively filters unreliable temporal supervision via forward-backward geometric gating, while CMFR progressively unlocks high-frequency capacity only where geometric confidence is established. This positions the work at the intersection of curriculum learning in neural rendering and dynamic LiDAR reconstruction, but the individual components (EMA buffers, forward-backward checks, frequency annealing) are repurposed from prior work rather than fundamentally new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and well-motivated failure mode in dynamic LiDAR NVS—ghosting artifacts and supervision conflicts from moving objects—with a clean training-only solution that adds no inference overhead. However, the gains over the SOTA baseline STGC-NeRF are modest (3.6%/4.2% CD reduction), and the curriculum learning framing, while well-executed, is a natural extension of ideas already present in static NeRF training (FreeNeRF, BARF). Open questions include performance under extremely fast motion where inter-frame overlap is minimal, scalability to real-time/online settings, and whether the confidence-based frequency gating generalizes beyond the specific hash-encoding architecture used here.
