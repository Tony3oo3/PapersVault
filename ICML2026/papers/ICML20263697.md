## Problem

Standard RL post-training for LMs collapses the model's implicit answer distribution onto a single dominant mode (entropy collapse), suppressing alternative plausible hypotheses that are needed for tasks with multiple valid answers or irreducible uncertainty (e.g., medical differential diagnosis, ambiguous QA). Inference-time workarounds like repeated sampling or best-of-k are computationally expensive and behaviorally misaligned, since the model was trained to commit to one answer rather than hedge across several. The paper asks how to explicitly optimize for set-valued, calibrated outputs during training rather than heuristically recovering them at inference time.

## Input / Output

The model receives a text prompt (a multi-hop question with context paragraphs, a patient symptom description with medical history, or a natural-language programming task) and produces a single structured generation containing a reasoning chain followed by exactly K distinct candidate answers, each enclosed in <answer_i> tags and optionally paired with a <confidence_i> tag holding a value in [0,1]. A format reward enforces answer uniqueness (zeroing all rewards if duplicates appear) and, in the N=1 single-gold-answer setting, constrains the sum of confidences to at most 1, yielding a valid discrete distribution; in the N>1 multi-gold setting, confidences form a multivariate Bernoulli distribution over correctness events.

## Method

The core contribution is a modified RL objective that generalizes standard RLVR from single-answer correctness to set-level correctness: the reward sums indicators over K generated answers for membership in the ground-truth answer set, naturally subsuming pass@K (N=1, K>1) and full set recovery (K≥N) as special cases. Building on this, Multi-Answer RLCR adds a Multi-Brier score penalty that trains each per-answer confidence to approximate the probability that specific answer is correct, jointly incentivizing coverage and calibration. Training uses GRPO with the BNPO objective on Qwen3-8B, augmented with a format reward for tag structure and uniqueness. The key design insight is internalizing inference-time search into the generative process: rather than sampling K independent completions that redundantly re-instantiate the same reasoning scaffolding, the model reasons jointly over multiple hypotheses within a single chain of thought, producing a diverse answer set in one forward pass at roughly 56% of the token cost of K independent samples.

## Results

On DDXPlus (medical differential diagnosis), Multi-Answer RLVR achieves 0.79 average correct answers per set versus 0.62 for RLVR-Single, while Multi-Answer RLCR reaches 0.77 with only 510 tokens per set (vs. 1378 for RLCR-Single). On MBPP (coding), Multi-Answer RLVR boosts top-1 accuracy from 0.29 to 0.49 (+69%) and cuts token usage from 512 to 235 (−54%). On HotPotQA-Modified (ambiguous QA), Multi-Answer RLVR improves average correctness from 0.17 to 0.27. For calibration, RLCR-Multi achieves a Set ECE of 0.02 on DDXPlus versus 0.13 for RLVR-Multi, and 0.26 on MBPP versus 0.44.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Multi-Answer RLVR outperforms RLVR-Single on all three datasets in coverage (0.79 vs. 0.62 on DDXPlus, 1.35 vs. 0.98 on MBPP, 0.27 vs. 0.17 on HotPotQA-Modified) while using substantially fewer tokens (677 vs. 1191 on DDXPlus; 235 vs. 512 on MBPP). Multi-Answer RLCR similarly beats RLCR-Single in coverage (0.77 vs. 0.65 on DDXPlus, 1.38 vs. 0.89 on MBPP) and reduces token usage by 63% on DDXPlus (510 vs. 1378). Prompting single-answer models with multi-answer instructions (RLVR-Single+Multi, RLCR-Single+Multi) performs substantially worse than explicitly trained multi-answer models, confirming that set generation requires dedicated training rather than inference-time prompting. On calibration, RLCR-Multi achieves markedly better set-level calibration than RLVR-Multi (Set ECE 0.02 vs. 0.13 on DDXPlus; 0.26 vs. 0.44 on MBPP), though on HotPotQA-Modified RLCR-Multi underperforms RLCR-Single due to a generative prior that forces confidences to sum to 1. Single-answer methods retain a slight edge in top-1 accuracy on DDXPlus (RLCR-Single 0.55 vs. RLCR-Multi 0.43), but multi-answer methods dominate on set-level metrics and efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes two existing RL training objectives—RLVR (binary correctness reward) and RLCR (proper scoring rule for calibrated confidence, from Damani et al. 2025)—from single-answer to set-valued outputs, introducing a set-level reward that counts correct answers in a generated set and a Multi-Brier score for per-answer calibration. Conceptually, it internalizes what inference-time scaling (best-of-k, repeated sampling) does externally, training the model to produce diverse candidate answers in a single forward pass rather than relying on post-hoc resampling. The approach is positioned as complementary to concurrent work on pass@k-style RL objectives (Chen et al. 2025, Walder & Karkhanis 2025) and verbalized sampling (Zhang et al. 2025), but differs by explicitly optimizing a structured multi-answer output format with per-answer confidence scores within a single generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental train-test mismatch: standard RLVR collapses the model's output distribution onto a single mode, yet many high-stakes applications (medical diagnosis, ambiguous QA) inherently require multiple plausible answers with calibrated uncertainty. The practical impact is significant—multi-answer RL achieves over 50% top-1 accuracy gains on coding while using less than half the tokens of repeated sampling, offering a compute-efficient alternative to inference-time scaling. Key limitations remain: single-answer objectives still achieve higher top-1 accuracy, the HotPotQA results reveal a persistent prior to sum confidences to 1 that degrades calibration in low-pass@k regimes, and experiments are confined to 8B models on QA-style tasks. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger models and more complex reasoning chains, and how to overcome the confidence-summing prior in genuinely uncertain single-answer settings.
