## Problem

Neural operators for dynamical systems suffer from spectral bias, systematically underestimating high-frequency content that encodes small-scale structure. In chaotic and multi-scale systems, these small-scale errors propagate across scales and accumulate over autoregressive rollouts, degrading long-horizon stability. Existing mitigations either discard high-frequency information during aggressive downsampling, apply wavelet transforms with limited expressiveness (linear/convolutional mixing), or operate attention on full-resolution grids at prohibitive computational cost.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a discretized PDE state field (e.g., 64×64 vorticity for CKF, 96×192 pressure+vorticity for SWE, 48×96 atmospheric variables for ERA5) concatenated with spatial coordinate features, and outputs the predicted next-step state field. For ERA5, external forcing variables (total incoming solar radiation, orography) are additionally provided at each step. At inference, the operator is iterated autoregressively over hundreds to thousands of steps (up to 14,600 steps for the 10-year ERA5 rollout), with the initial condition fixed and only the predicted state fed forward.

## Method

MSWT is a U-shaped operator that first patch-tokenizes the physical field into a lower-resolution token space, then processes tokens through L scales of wavelet attention blocks and wavelet-preserving down/upsampling. The wavelet attention operator applies a 2D Haar DWT to halve spatial resolution while stacking all four frequency subbands (LL, LH, HL, HH) along the channel dimension, then performs local window self-attention in this wavelet coefficient space before an iDWT recovers the token-space representation. Crucially, the down/upsampling layers use DWT/iDWT rather than strided convolutions or pooling, so high-frequency subbands are carried to coarser scales and recovered via skip connections instead of being discarded. This design enables attention to operate at reduced spatial resolution (improving efficiency) while explicitly preserving and mixing high-frequency content across scales, trained with a standard relative L2 loss on one-step prediction pairs.

## Results

On the Chaotic Kolmogorov Flow (CKF) benchmark, MSWT achieves a relative L² error of 0.20±0.02 at step 64, improving over the second-best stable baseline (FNO, 0.57±0.02) by 64.9%, and over FNO by 76.5% on enstrophy mean log ratio (EMLR) and 80.6% on enstrophy mean absolute error (EMAE). On the Shallow Water Equation (SWE), MSWT reduces long-horizon relative L² error by ~30% over FNO (0.42 vs 0.58 at step 81). On ERA5 climate reanalysis, MSWT attains lower RMSE than LUCIE across all six prognostic/diagnostic variables over 10-year free-running rollouts, with the largest gain on surface pressure (1.31 vs 2.97 hPa).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MSWT outperforms all five PDE-simulation baselines on both CKF and SWE. Against FNO (the strongest stable baseline), MSWT reduces step-64 relative L² error by 64.9% on CKF and step-81 error by 27.6% on SWE, with even larger gains on spectral metrics (76.5% EMLR, 80.6% EMAE on CKF). Unet and WNO both become numerically unstable within 30 rollout steps on CKF and are effectively non-competitive for long-horizon prediction. SAOT and HFS, the two most closely related wavelet/spectral baselines, show substantially faster error accumulation than MSWT (e.g., SAOT reaches 0.86 Rel L² at step 64 on CKF vs. 0.20 for MSWT). On the ERA5 climate benchmark, MSWT achieves lower RMSE than LUCIE on all six variables over 10-year free-running rollouts, with the most pronounced improvement on surface pressure (1.31 vs. 2.97 hPa) and zonal wind (1.92 vs. 2.45 m/s), though LUCIE retains a smaller global min/max bias on precipitation.

## Relation to Prior Work

MSWT sits at the intersection of three research threads: (i) neural operators for PDE surrogate modeling (FNO, DeepONet), (ii) spectral-bias mitigation via multi-scale or frequency-aware architectures (U-NO, HFS, WNO), and (iii) transformer-based operator learning (SAOT, GNOT). Its key departure is moving self-attention into a tokenized, reduced-resolution wavelet space rather than the physical grid, and replacing standard pooling with wavelet-preserving down/upsampling that explicitly carries all four Haar sub-bands across scales. The local window attention strategy is borrowed from Swin Transformer, and the U-shaped encoder-decoder topology follows the U-NO lineage, but the combination of patch tokenization, wavelet attention, and wavelet-preserving sampling as a unified multi-scale operator is new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Spectral bias remains a central obstacle for neural operators applied to chaotic and multi-scale systems, where small-scale errors cascade into large-scale drift over long rollouts. MSWT's 60% reduction in long-horizon error on CKF and consistent ERA5 climatological-bias reduction over LUCIE demonstrate that architectural inductive bias (wavelet sub-band preservation) can substitute for more complex training objectives, which simplifies the recipe for practitioners. Open questions include whether the Haar wavelet is optimal versus learned or smoother wavelets, how the approach scales to 3D or higher-resolution grids, and whether the ~36% runtime overhead over FNO can be closed through kernel-level optimization without sacrificing the spectral-fidelity gains.
