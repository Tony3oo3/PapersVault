## Problem

Existing metrics for conditional generative models (e.g., CLIP-SIM, BERT-SIM) evaluate a single sampled output, masking the stochastic variability and potential catastrophic failures inherent to generation. No principled metric quantifies the worst-case performance a model might exhibit at a given confidence level, leaving safety-critical deployments without a reliability-aware evaluation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261230/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen target generative model, an input condition (e.g., a 224×224 image or a text prompt), a user-specified similarity metric ρ (CLIP cosine, BERT cosine, DINO cosine), and a nominal confidence level α, and produces a calibrated prediction set C_Y(X) in the output space along with a scalar reliability score — the minimum ρ value over all points in that set. The data is partitioned into three disjoint folds (60/24/8% or 75/15/5/5%) for latent generative model training, DQR fitting, conformal calibration, and testing, and all feature vectors are L2-normalized before processing.

## Method

CReL projects high-dimensional model outputs into a lower-dimensional latent space via a VAE whose reconstruction loss is replaced by the target metric ρ, aligning the encoder–decoder with the evaluation criterion. In the latent space, directional quantile regression (DQR) constructs a convex base region R_Z(x) as the intersection of half-spaces across 1,024 directions, and a joint conformal calibration step grows this region by a data-driven radius γ_cal to guarantee marginal coverage at level 1−α. The final prediction set is obtained by decoding the calibrated latent region back to output space. The reliability score is then computed by minimizing ρ(Dec(z; X), GT) over the convex latent-space set using projected gradient descent, where the projection onto the DQR region is solved as a quadratic program with linear inequality constraints. This latent-space formulation is the key distinction from prior work (e.g., Feldman et al., 2023), which calibrates in the non-convex output space and renders worst-case optimization intractable.

## Results

On the nonlinear synthetic dataset (α=0.1), CReL achieves a coverage of 0.8953 in the latent space and 0.8915 in the output space, with a prediction-set area of 232.7 grid points—comparable to Feldman et al. (0.8940 coverage, 234.5 area) but substantially smaller than standard DQR (0.9145 coverage, 287.4 area). On the image-to-text task (MS-COCO, α=0.1), CReL-CLIP re-ranks models: BLIP-base jumps from 4th (0.2330) to 1st (0.0070) in reliability, while GIT-large drops from 1st (0.2550) to 3rd (−0.0043). On the text-to-image task, CReL-DINO ranks SD3.5-L first (−0.1365) despite its 2nd-place average DINO score (0.4531), while SD3-M drops from 1st average (0.4615) to last in reliability (−0.1480).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Feldman et al. (2023), CReL achieves nearly identical coverage (0.8953 vs. 0.8940 at α=0.1) with a marginally smaller prediction-set area (232.7 vs. 234.5), while being over 8× faster at r=8 and ~1,930× faster at r=12 in calibration runtime due to avoiding grid-based discretization. Against standard DQR, CReL's joint latent-space calibration yields a much more informative (smaller) region—232.7 vs. 287.4 area at α=0.1—while DQR over-covers (0.9145 vs. target 0.90). Against PCP (Wang et al., 2022b), CReL achieves comparable coverage (0.8915 vs. 0.9001) but with a dramatically smaller area (232.70 vs. 854.24), roughly 3.7× more compact. In practical image-to-text and text-to-image evaluations, CReL's reliability score consistently re-ranks generative models relative to standard single-output metrics (CLIP, BERT, DINO), revealing that models with more concentrated score distributions (e.g., BLIP-base, SD3.5-L) are more reliable even when their average scores are not the highest.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the conformal prediction framework for multi-dimensional outputs, specifically extending the latent-space calibration approach of Feldman et al. (2023) and the directional quantile regression (DQR) machinery of Kong & Mizera (2012). The key conceptual departure is replacing the standard geometric volume (sharpness) of a prediction set with a metric-aware worst-case performance measure—the reliability score—which directly quantifies the model's performance floor at a given confidence level. Computationally, CReL departs from Feldman et al. by performing calibration in the convex latent space rather than the potentially non-convex output space, enabling tractable optimization via projected gradient descent with linear-programming projections.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap: standard metrics (CLIP, FID, BERT-sim) evaluate a single sample and mask the stochastic failure modes that matter in safety-critical deployments. The reliability score provides a principled, distributional lower bound on performance, and the CReL framework makes it computationally feasible (linear in latent dimension vs. exponential for grid-based alternatives). Open questions include whether the LGM reconstruction assumption holds well for very high-fidelity generators (e.g., video, 3D), how the metric behaves under one-to-many or many-to-many mappings, and whether the slight over-conservativeness from the decoder expansion becomes problematic at very high confidence levels.
