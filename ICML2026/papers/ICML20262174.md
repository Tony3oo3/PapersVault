## Problem

Existing causal discovery methods for location-scale noise models (LSNMs) assume symmetric noise distributions (Gaussian or t-distributed), but real-world noise is frequently skewed. This misspecification distorts the estimated location and scale functions, causing independence tests to falsely reject cause-noise independence and likelihood scores to favor the wrong causal direction. The paper demonstrates that this failure mode persists across state-of-the-art LSNM methods as skewness increases, and proposes a principled fix.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an n×d matrix of iid continuous observations, which are standardized column-wise before model fitting. In the bivariate setting (d=2), it outputs a causal direction (X→Y or Y→X) determined either by comparing HSIC independence p-values of cause-residual pairs or by comparing penalized log-likelihoods of the two candidate skew-normal LSNMs. In the multivariate setting, it first estimates the Markov equivalence class via the PC algorithm, then orients all undirected edges by maximizing the sum of conditional log-likelihoods over candidate DAGs, outputting a fully oriented DAG.

## Method

SKEWD models the LSNM noise as a skew-normal SN(0,1,λ) and parameterizes the location f and scale g functions as generalized additive models with B-spline bases, ensuring positivity of g via an exponential link. Parameter estimation proceeds in three stages: (1) Bayesian optimization with Latin Hypercube initialization selects penalty strengths α and κ by maximizing cross-validated unpenalized log-likelihood; (2) CMA-ES performs stochastic heuristic maximization of the penalized log-likelihood from multiple random starts; (3) an ECM algorithm refines the heuristic solution by introducing a positive latent variable V (from the skew-normal integral representation) and alternating E-steps (computing conditional expectations of V and V² under a truncated normal) with CM-steps that analytically update ψ and λ while using CMA-ES for the non-convex ρ update. The multivariate extension (SKEWD-MV) leverages Proposition 5.1 to restrict likelihood scoring to only nodes with undirected edges in the estimated MEC, reducing the number of required skew-normal regressions.

## Results

On the novel skew-noise bivariate datasets, SKEWD-LL achieves 100% accuracy across all six settings (ANs and LSs at skewness levels −0.455, 0.985, 1.750), while SKEWD-IT reaches 82.5–100%. On the 13 established benchmarks, SKEWD-IT attains 85.02% mean accuracy and 91.65% AUDRC, ranking second only to ROCHE (87.69% accuracy). In the multivariate setting (d=6, d=10), SKEWD-MV achieves SHD of 2.27 and 4.72, substantially outperforming HOST (8.90, 21.21), GraN-DAG++ (11.30, 24.67), and CAM (13.52, 42.08).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SKEWD-LL is the only method achieving perfect accuracy on all six skew-noise datasets, while LOCI-LL drops to 66% on LSs(1.750) and QCCD collapses to 11% on the same setting, showing severe bias toward the wrong direction. ROCHE is the most competitive LSNM-specific baseline, maintaining 81–99.5% accuracy but still degrading under high skewness (81% on LSs(1.750) vs. SKEWD-LL's 100%). On the 13 established benchmarks, ROCHE edges out SKEWD-IT by 2.67% in mean accuracy (87.69% vs. 85.02%), likely due to its t-distribution handling heavy tails, while SKEWD-LL and LOCI-LL perform comparably (~77%). IGCI-G, despite being highly misspecified (assuming near-deterministic functions), surprisingly matches SKEWD-LL on the skew-noise datasets, though it lags on established benchmarks (73.69% mean accuracy). In the multivariate setting, SKEWD-MV outperforms all baselines by a wide margin: SHD of 2.27 (d=6) vs. HOST's 8.90, GraN-DAG++'s 11.30, and CAM's 13.52, with the oracle-MEC variant (SKEWD-MV-O) achieving near-perfect SHD of 0.14.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LSNM causal discovery line (Immer et al., 2023; Strobl & Lasko, 2023) by replacing the standard symmetric noise assumption (Gaussian in LOCI, Student's t in ROCHE) with the skew-normal distribution, a natural generalization that reduces to Gaussian at λ=0. The estimation procedure adapts the ECM algorithm for heteroscedastic partially linear models (Ferreira et al., 2023) to the LSNM setting with GAM-based B-spline function classes, preceded by a CMA-ES heuristic and Bayesian optimization for penalty tuning. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior work that treats skewness as a nuisance to be robustly handled (e.g., ROCHE's heavier tails) by explicitly modeling it as a first-class parameter of the noise distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: real-world data (coastal flooding, rainfall, stochastic frontiers) frequently exhibits skewed noise, and the paper demonstrates that SOTA LSNM methods degrade substantially under even moderate skewness (e.g., QCCD drops to 11% accuracy on LSs(1.750)). Key limitations include the skew-normal's bounded skewness (~0.995), which cannot capture the full range of real-world asymmetry, the Gaussianity assumption on marginals in the likelihood-scoring variant, and the exponential scaling of the multivariate extension with the number of undirected edges in the MEC. Open questions include extending the independence-testing variant to the multivariate setting, developing nonparametric marginal estimation, and improving scalability for larger graphs.
