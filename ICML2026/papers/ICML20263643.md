## Problem

Equivariant neural networks benefit from relaxing strict symmetry constraints during training, but prior methods require prespecified relaxation schedules or penalty terms whose hyperparameters implicitly encode a target relaxation level per layer. These targets are task-dependent, architecture-dependent, and can differ across layers of the same model, making them costly to tune and difficult to transfer. The paper asks whether per-layer relaxation levels can be learned entirely from the training signal and the symmetry structure of each layer's input-target distribution, without any prior specification.

## Input / Output

The method is applied to equivariant models across four task families: point cloud classification (300-point clouds → class label), N-body simulation (initial particle positions/charges/velocities → positions after 1000 timesteps), motion capture trajectory prediction (input mocap sequences → future trajectories), and molecular conformer generation (molecular graph → 3D atom positions). A key structural detail is that the modulation update operates on intermediate layer representations: each layer j at step t uses the pair (z_t^(j), y_t) — the layer's input feature and the ground-truth target — to compute its own modulation parameters, rather than relying solely on the final input-output pair.

## Method

RECM parametrizes each layer as a weighted sum of an equivariant component and K unconstrained components, with modulation weights α_i and β controlled by a per-layer recurrent state vector h_t. The state is updated via an exponential weighted average of a learned update function l_θ(z,y) = r_θ(z,y) − (1/|C_G|)Σ_g r_θ(ρ_in(g)z, ρ_out(g)y), where C_G is a finite topological generating set of the symmetry group and r_θ is a small MLP. Theorem 4.2 proves that the converged state h* is upper-bounded by 2B·W₁(p, p_G), the Wasserstein distance between the input-target distribution and its group-symmetrized version, guaranteeing that fully symmetric layers recover exact equivariance (α_i → 0) while non-symmetric layers retain flexibility. The only task-specific hyperparameter is the decay rate a of the exponential average, which is tuned once per training setup and then transferable across data distributions with different symmetry properties.

## Results

On the N-body simulation task, SEGNN+RECM achieves 3.7±0.22 MSE, outperforming all prior constraint relaxation methods including ACE-exact (3.8±0.16), ACE-appr (3.8±0.17), and ELLA (3.9±0.11). On motion capture trajectory prediction, EGNO+RECM achieves 22.6±0.8 MSE (Run) and 6.6±0.5 MSE (Walk), beating the best prior method ACE-appr (23.8±1.5 / 7.4±0.2) by a clear margin. On GEOM-Drugs conformer generation, RECM improves precision over both equivariant and unconstrained variants: ETFlow+RECM reaches 75.10 mean precision (vs. 74.38 for ETFlow-Eq) while DiTMC+RECM reaches 76.1 mean precision (vs. 75.3 for DiTMC-Eq and 76.5 for DiTMC-Unc), yielding the best F1-score in both cases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RECM consistently outperforms all prior constraint relaxation methods across every task. Against the scheduling-based approach of Pertigkiozoglou et al. (2024) (ES), RECM improves N-body MSE from 4.9 to 3.7 and motion capture Run MSE from 33.1 to 22.6. Against the residual pathway prior of Finzi et al. (2021) (RPP), RECM improves N-body MSE from 4.1 to 3.7 and motion capture Run MSE from 28.0 to 22.6. Against the Bayesian model selection of van der Ouderaa et al. (2023) (ELLA), RECM improves N-body MSE from 3.9 to 3.7 and motion capture Walk MSE from 7.0 to 6.6. Against the constrained optimization of Manolache et al. (2025) (ACE-exact/ACE-appr), RECM improves N-body MSE from 3.8 to 3.7 and motion capture Run MSE from 23.8 to 22.6, with the advantage being largest on the approximate-symmetry task. On conformer generation (GEOM-Drugs), RECM applied to ETFlow and DiTMC improves mean precision by ~0.7–0.8 points over their equivariant counterparts while maintaining comparable recall, and achieves the best F1-score compared to both equivariant (ETFlow-Eq, DiTMC-Eq) and fully unconstrained (DiTMC-Unc, MCF) variants. On ModelNet40, RECM matches or slightly exceeds the best prior method (ELLA) on both rotated (0.80 vs. 0.78 instance accuracy) and aligned (0.90 vs. 0.90) settings without requiring any task-specific tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on relaxing equivariant constraints during training, building directly on Finzi et al.'s residual pathway priors (which introduced the convex combination of equivariant and unconstrained layers), Pertigkiozoglou et al.'s scheduling framework (which modulates relaxation via a predefined schedule), and Manolache et al.'s constrained optimization formulation (which learns relaxation via dual variables). The key departure is that all prior methods require the practitioner to specify a target relaxation level—either through an explicit schedule, a penalty weight, or a constrained optimization target—while RECM replaces this with a recurrent update rule whose convergence is provably governed by the symmetry gap of each layer's input-target distribution, eliminating the need for task-specific tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RECM offers a principled, data-driven alternative to the ad hoc hyperparameter tuning that currently limits the adoption of constraint relaxation in equivariant networks, with the appealing property that a single training run adapts per-layer equivariance without requiring separate runs for exact vs. approximate settings. The theoretical guarantee tying the relaxation parameter to the 1-Wasserstein distance to the symmetrized distribution provides a clear interpretability story. Limitations include the requirement for compact groups (to admit a normalized Haar measure), the ~40% training-time overhead from maintaining unconstrained components, and the fact that convergence guarantees rely on standard learning-rate schedulers driving parameters to a fixed point. Open questions include how the method behaves for non-compact groups (e.g., full SE(3) without separating translation), whether the recurrent state can be further compressed, and whether the approach extends naturally to tasks where the relevant symmetry is not known a priori.
