## Problem

Classical free energy estimation methods (alchemical FEP, TI, MBAR) require extensive MD simulations across intermediate states, making them prohibitively expensive for large-scale applications. Existing deep learning alternatives either rely on less-expressive normalizing flows or have input dimensions tied to a specific system, preventing generalization to unseen molecular systems with different atom counts or topologies without retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265589/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 3D atomic coordinates (N×3), atomic numbers, covalent bond indices, and R reference structures sampled from MD trajectories, and produces exact log-densities of conformations under a learned distribution. A key design constraint is universality: the architecture handles arbitrary numbers of atoms and diverse topologies without system-specific retraining, with the atom ordering and sequence length adapting to each molecule. The log-densities are then fed into MBAR to yield absolute free energy estimates, since the model's distribution has zero free energy by construction.

## Method

CARD bijectively converts aligned 3D coordinates into a mixed discrete-continuous sequence via radix-based decomposition: each coordinate is expressed in base-r with L discrete digits (coarse) plus one continuous residual (fine), enabling coarse-to-fine autoregressive generation where global structure is established before local refinement. A transformer encoder-decoder with a novel geometry-aware attention mechanism incorporates pairwise distances from reference structures directly into attention weights, while a causal masking strategy ensures each position only sees the up-to-date coordinates of preceding atoms. Discrete digits are modeled with categorical distributions over r³ classes, and continuous residuals use a Beta Mixture Model on bounded intervals. The model is trained in two stages—first on negative log-likelihood, then jointly with an energy-alignment loss against force-field labels—and serves as a zero-free-energy proposal, so absolute free energies of arbitrary target systems are computed directly via MBAR without alchemical intermediates.

## Results

On the aqueous tautomer free energy task (27 test pairs), CARD achieves MAE 4.11 kcal/mol, RMSE 5.49 kcal/mol, PCC 0.64, and SPCC 0.64, outperforming both DFT (MAE 4.62) and sPhysNet-pre (MAE 4.61) across all metrics. For solvation free energy on 70 unseen ZINC20 molecules, CARD achieves MAE well below 1 kcal/mol with R² > 0.9 in both vacuum-to-toluene and vacuum-to-water settings, while delivering a ~40× inference speedup over MFES (770 s vs. 32,300 s per system). On the HiPen endstate correction task (18 molecules), CARD reaches an MAE of 0.90 kcal/mol despite training on a small dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CARD is compared against two categories of baselines. Against MFES (Tkaczyk et al., 2024), the classical alchemical FEP reference, CARD matches accuracy (MAE < 1 kcal/mol for solvation; 0.90 kcal/mol for endstate correction) while achieving a ~40× speedup in the inference phase (770 s vs. 32,300 s per system on a single V100 GPU), and still a 5× speedup when endstate MD simulation time is included. Against sPhysNet-pre (Pan et al., 2025), a regression-based method pretrained on ~100M MMFF94 geometries with DFT labels, CARD wins decisively on the aqueous tautomer task: MAE 4.11 vs. 4.61 kcal/mol, RMSE 5.49 vs. 6.95, and PCC/SPCC 0.64 vs. 0.35/0.41. CARD also outperforms DFT at the B3LYP/6-31G* level with SMD solvation on the same tautomer task (MAE 4.11 vs. 4.62, PCC 0.64 vs. 0.36), with the advantage growing as molecular complexity (rotatable-bond count) increases, where DFT shows larger outliers.

## Relation to Prior Work

CARD extends the zero-free-energy proposal paradigm introduced by DeepBAR (Ding & Zhang, 2021), which uses a tractable probabilistic model as a reference with vanishing absolute free energy to compute free energy differences via BAR/MBAR. The key departure is replacing DeepBAR's normalizing flows with a transformer-based autoregressive model that employs a novel radix-based decomposition to convert 3D coordinates into mixed discrete-continuous sequences, enabling coarse-to-fine generation with exact likelihood computation. This also addresses a critical limitation of prior deep learning free energy methods (targeted FEP, neural TI, DeepBAR) whose input dimensionality is tied to a specific system, requiring retraining for each new molecule. The geometry-aware attention mechanism that incorporates reference-structure distances into attention weights is a new architectural element not present in prior autoregressive molecular models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CARD is the first deep learning free energy method to demonstrate robust generalization to unseen molecular systems with different topologies and atom counts without system-specific retraining, which was a fundamental barrier limiting the practical utility of prior theory-based deep learning approaches. The ~40× inference speedup over classical MFES while matching accuracy on unseen systems makes large-scale virtual screening and chemical space exploration substantially more tractable. However, the current scope is limited to drug-like small molecules; extension to protein-ligand complexes and the PCA-based alignment's sensitivity to molecular symmetries remain open challenges that constrain broader applicability.
