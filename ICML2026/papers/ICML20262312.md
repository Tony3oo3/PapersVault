## Problem

Adversarial distillation (AD) is notoriously inconsistent: a more robust teacher does not necessarily yield a more robust student and can even exacerbate robust overfitting. Prior work identified empirical symptoms of failure (e.g., absence of transferable adversarial samples) but left the underlying mechanistic cause unexplained. This paper asks: what specific property of the teacher-student interaction drives this teacher-dependent behavior?

## Input / Output

The student model consumes adversarially perturbed images (PGD-generated, ε=8/255) paired with the teacher's softened probability distributions as supervisory signals. For the proposed teacher-selection criterion, the input is a proxy unlearnable set derived from a single reference PGD-AT model at its peak robust accuracy epoch, and the output is the teacher's average Shannon entropy computed over PGD-10 adversarial examples of those samples. The theoretical model operates on structured inputs with P patches containing either a learnable signal (αy·u) or an unlearnable signal (αy·v) plus Gaussian noise, with the student's weights constrained orthogonal to v to enforce representational mismatch.

## Method

The paper identifies a consistent subset of training samples—the robustly unlearnable set (S_U)—that capacity-limited students persistently fail to classify robustly, and proves via a two-layer feature-learning analysis that this set is the primary driver of robust overfitting. The key mechanism is a representational mismatch: the student is structurally blind to the unlearnable feature v, so when a confident (Bad) teacher supervises on S_U, the residual gradients force the student to memorize sample-specific noise patterns that eventually overpower the learned robust signal, driving robust test error to 1/2. Conversely, a Good Teacher that remains uncertain on S_U suppresses these noise gradients, allowing the student to rely solely on the learnable feature for robust generalization. The paper translates this into the Unlearnable-Entropy Criterion: a teacher's average predictive entropy on a proxy S_U (from a single reference model) strongly correlates with student robustness across five different distillation methods, providing a simple, data-driven, a priori teacher-selection metric that outperforms conventional heuristics like teacher robust accuracy.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18 students, distilling from a High-Entropy teacher (entropy 1.882 on unlearnable samples) yields an average PGD-20 accuracy of 54.97% and AutoAttack accuracy of 50.59%, compared to 42.46% and 40.68% for a Low-Entropy teacher (entropy 0.059) — a gap of ~12.5% and ~10% respectively. The robust overfitting degradation (peak-to-final test accuracy drop) collapses from 7.23% to 0.53% when switching from the Low- to High-Entropy teacher, and this improvement is consistent across all five AD methods tested (ARD, IAD, RSLAD, AdaAD, IGDM). The generalization gap (robust train minus robust test) also shrinks from 53.20% to 28.13%, confirming that the High-Entropy teacher prevents noise memorization rather than merely improving training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is not between different AD methods but between two teacher configurations (Low-Entropy Gowal vs. High-Entropy Chen) applied across all five AD baselines. The High-Entropy teacher dominates the Low-Entropy teacher on every metric for every method: e.g., ARD improves from 41.51% to 54.14% PGD-20 (+12.6%), and IGDM from 46.33% to 57.09% (+10.8%). Standard PGD-AT and TRADES serve as non-distillation baselines that exhibit the same robust overfitting pattern (robust test accuracy peaks then declines), confirming that the unlearnable-set mechanism is not specific to distillation. Among the five AD methods, IGDM achieves the highest PGD-20 under both teacher settings (46.33% low / 57.09% high), while RSLAD is the weakest under the Low-Entropy teacher (40.26% PGD-20) but recovers to competitive levels (54.30%) under the High-Entropy teacher. The key takeaway is that teacher entropy on unlearnable samples is a stronger predictor of student robustness than the choice of distillation loss: the ~12% PGD-20 gap between teachers far exceeds the ~6% spread among methods within the same teacher.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the feature-learning theoretical framework of Li & Li (2025a,b), which analyzed adversarial training dynamics in two-layer networks, to the adversarial distillation setting for the first time. It builds on the adversarial distillation paradigm initiated by Goldblum et al. (2020) and the min-max adversarial training framework of Madry et al. (2018), but departs from prior empirical observations (e.g., the transferable adversarial sample scarcity identified by Lee & Chung, 2026) by providing a first-principles mechanistic explanation rooted in representational mismatch between teacher and student on a consistent unlearnable subset of training data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a long-standing paradox in adversarial distillation—why a more robust teacher can degrade student robustness—by identifying the teacher's predictive confidence on the unlearnable set as the causal mechanism, and translates this into a practical Unlearnable-Entropy Criterion for a priori teacher selection. Limitations include the theoretical analysis being restricted to two-layer networks with a specific activation, and the unlearnable set identification (while approximable with a single reference model) still requires training multiple models for the rigorous intersection protocol. Open questions include whether the mechanism generalizes to deeper architectures, whether the unlearnable set can be characterized without ensemble training, and whether the entropy criterion transfers to non-CNN backbones or generative distillation settings.
