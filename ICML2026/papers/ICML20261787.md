## Problem

Identifying latent regimes in non-stationary time series is challenging when regime switches are frequent, dynamics are nonlinear and non-Gaussian, and instantaneous effects exist between observed variables (e.g., due to coarse measurement granularity). Prior identifiability results for Markov Switching Models were restricted to Gaussian noise and excluded instantaneous effects, leaving a theoretical gap for the broader class of exponential family distributions with nonlinear lagged and contemporaneous dependencies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261787/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N i.i.d. realizations of a continuous D-dimensional time series X_{0:T} (with T+1 time steps) as input, with the number of regimes K unknown and typically over-estimated via a hyperparameter K̃. It produces hard MAP regime assignments r̂_{0:T}^{(n)} per sample and regime-dependent initial graphs G_a^0 and window graphs G_a. The causal discovery phase is decoupled from regime detection: the clustered sliding windows are fed to any stationary causal discovery method that supports instantaneous effects, so the output graph structure depends on the chosen downstream algorithm.

## Method

FlowMSM estimates regime posteriors via a Generalised Expectation-Maximisation (GEM) algorithm over a first-order stationary Markov chain, using conditional normalising flows (neural spline flows) to model the initial and transition distributions. A key architectural choice is a hypernetwork that takes a context window of lagged variables x_{t-1} and a regime-dependent embedding E_a to predict spline transformation weights for a shared flow, enabling flexible regime-conditional density estimation while maintaining the diffeomorphic structure required by the identifiability theory. The identifiability proof extends Barndorff-Nielsen's (1965) exponential family mixture results to MSMs by showing that real-analytic diffeomorphisms with a finite polynomial reparametrisation of the sufficient statistic preserve linear independence of mixture components even in the presence of overlapping variable spaces and instantaneous effects. After regime detection, FlowMSM clusters partially overlapping sliding windows by MAP assignment and applies a stationary causal discovery method (e.g., DYNOTEARS, VARLiNGAM, Rhino) to each cluster to recover regime-dependent window graphs, avoiding the iterative coupling between regime and causal structure estimation used by prior methods.

## Results

On synthetic benchmarks (N=100, T=100, D=10, K=3 regimes), FlowMSM achieves NMI of 0.93–0.97 for regime detection in nonlinear/non-Gaussian settings (ANM, LSNM), where iMSM baselines collapse to near-random (NMI ≈ 0.00–0.01). For causal discovery, FlowMSM combined with VARLiNGAM achieves F1 of 0.94–0.98 on exponential-family settings, and with Rhino achieves F1 of 0.87 on the most challenging LSNM class. On the Fama-French five-factor model (5,786 trading days), FlowMSM identifies two dominant regimes aligned with low/high market volatility periods including the 2008 crisis and COVID-19, while baselines produce regimes not easily mapped to market events.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlowMSM is competitive with iMSM (Neural) in the simple Gaussian SVAR setting (NMI 0.90 vs. 0.89) but dramatically outperforms it in all non-Gaussian/nonlinear settings: on ANM(Exp.Fam.) FlowMSM achieves NMI 0.93 vs. 0.01 for iMSM (Neural), and on LSNM(Exp.Fam.) 0.83 vs. 0.00. The other baselines (R-PCMCI, CASTOR, FANTOM) perform poorly across all settings, with NMI typically below 0.32, likely because they assume deterministic time-dependent regimes or cannot handle frequent regime switches. For causal discovery, FlowMSM with the appropriate method (VARLiNGAM for non-Gaussian, Rhino for LSNM) achieves F1 of 0.94–0.98, approaching oracle-assisted performance (F1 0.99–1.00), while all non-FlowMSM baselines remain at F1 ≤ 0.71. The gap between FlowMSM and oracle-assisted baselines is small (e.g., 0.94 vs. 1.00 on SVAR(Exp.Fam.) with VARLiNGAM), indicating that regime detection uncertainty has limited impact on downstream causal discovery. On the Fama-French real-world dataset, FlowMSM qualitatively outperforms all baselines by identifying regimes that align with known market volatility events.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MSM identifiability line initiated by Balsells-Rodas et al. (2024; 2026), which established identifiability for first- and higher-order MSMs but only provided concrete instantiations in Gaussian settings. It repurposes Barndorff-Nielsen's (1965) classical result on identifiable mixtures of exponential families to handle non-Gaussian noise, and introduces a finite polynomial reparametrisation (Ass. 3.4) to bridge the gap between the SCM's diffeomorphic mappings and the exponential family structure. Conceptually, it departs from CASTOR and FANTOM (Rahmani & Frossard, 2025a,b) by allowing autoregressive regime dependencies (rather than time-deterministic regimes) and by decoupling regime detection from causal discovery, avoiding the need for causal sufficiency during regime learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identifiability theorem provides the first theoretical guarantee for latent regime recovery under a broad combination of conditions—temporal regime dependencies, nonlinear instantaneous effects, and exponential family noise—that collectively cover many real-world non-stationary systems. The decoupled FlowMSM framework is practically important because it can be paired with any stationary causal discovery method, broadening the class of recoverable causal structures beyond uniquely identifiable ones. Key limitations include the requirement that regimes are independent of observations (relaxed only in Gaussian settings by Balsells-Rodas et al., 2025), the reliance on real-analytic diffeomorphisms, and the fact that the real-world Fama-French study is purely qualitative with no ground truth. Extending the theory to regimes that depend on observed variables or to noise beyond exponential families remains open.
