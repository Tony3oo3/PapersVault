## Problem

Industrial time-series forecasting relies on costly, hand-tuned pipelines that cannot transfer across heterogeneous datasets or adapt to distribution shifts, and no mechanism exists to accumulate reusable strategy knowledge. LLM-based agents have been proposed as one-shot recommenders but lack a training paradigm to learn transferable forecasting intelligence without human-annotated supervision, because (i) no large-scale corpora map datasets to optimal pipelines and (ii) the meta-feature-to-strategy mapping is highly context-dependent.

## Input / Output

The agent consumes a new dataset's scalar meta-features (missingness rate, ACF/PACF peaks, ADF/KPSS stationarity scores, variance/CV, trend R², change-point count, skewness/kurtosis, SNR, entropy, series length, number of variates) and a pre-built symbolic lesson library of validated causal strategy statements. It outputs a single toolchain selection (one tool per each of six pipeline stages: imputation, anomaly handling, transformation, decomposition, normalization, forecasting) and the resulting forecast. Critically, the agent operates in zero-shot mode: lessons are distilled exclusively from other datasets and applied to the target via in-context prompting, with no parameter updates or training on the target split.

## Method

BECRA implements a four-stage exploration–contrast–induction–application cycle. First, Contrast-aware UCB Sampling explores the combinatorial toolchain space using a bandit objective (μ(a) + λ·σ(a)) but deliberately retains both high- and low-performing outcomes under similar meta-feature conditions to build balanced contrastive evidence. Second, an LLM agent performs language-based induction over contrastive pairs (same strategy, divergent outcomes) to extract interpretable symbolic lessons describing when a strategy succeeds or fails and why. Third, each lesson is verified via controlled policy interventions: the agent runs paired planning episodes on the same dataset differing only in whether the lesson is injected as a planning constraint, and the lesson is retained only if the measured causal effect Δ exceeds a confidence threshold. Fourth, for deployment, relevant lessons are retrieved by meta-feature matching and injected as structured in-context prompts to guide toolchain selection, enabling zero-training adaptation without fine-tuning. The causal notion is defined at the agent decision-making level (interventionist account), not the data-generating process, and the contrast-aware retention of failures is essential for extracting causal rather than merely correlational strategy knowledge.

## Results

On long-term forecasting (averaged over prediction lengths 96/192/336/720, input length 96), BECRA achieves the best MSE on all six datasets: ETTh1 0.441, ETTh2 0.367, ETTm1 0.380, ETTm2 0.274, Electricity 0.171, Weather 0.240. On the M4 short-term benchmark, BECRA attains the lowest SMAPE across all four frequency categories (Yearly 13.275, Quarterly 9.997, Monthly 12.633, Others 4.696) and the best OWA in three of four categories. All results are obtained in a strict zero-shot leave-one-out protocol where the agent's lesson knowledge base is built exclusively from other datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BECRA outperforms all 11 baselines on 5 of 6 long-term datasets, with the sole exception being a tie with Moirai on ETTh2 (both 0.367 MSE). The largest margins are against untrained LLM agents: on ETTm1 and ETTm2, ChatGPT-4, Gemini, and LLaMA produce MSE values of 0.77–1.29, whereas BECRA achieves 0.274–0.380, a gap of over 2×. Against single-architecture models (TimeMixer, TimesNet, PatchTST, iTransformer), BECRA wins consistently with MSE improvements of 0.01–0.07, with the largest gap on Electricity (0.171 vs. 0.180–0.194). Among LLM-based and foundation-model baselines, GPT4TS is the closest competitor (ETTh1: 0.447 vs. 0.441; ETTm2: 0.285 vs. 0.274), while Chronos and Moirai trail by 0.03–0.12 MSE on most datasets. On M4 short-term forecasting, BECRA achieves the best SMAPE in all four frequency groups, beating the next-best (TimesNet or TimeLLM) by 0.1–0.5 SMAPE points, and the best OWA in Yearly, Quarterly, and Monthly categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

BECRA repurposes the UCB bandit exploration principle (Slivkins et al., 2019) not for pure exploitation but for contrast-aware evidence collection, and applies interventionist causal reasoning (Woodward, 2005) at the agent decision-making level rather than the data-generating process. It departs from existing LLM-agent approaches for time series (TimeLLM, GPT4TS, TimeCopilot, TimeSeriesScientist) that position the LLM as a one-shot pipeline recommender or integration layer, instead formulating forecasting as an agent *training* problem with a four-stage exploration–contrast–induction–application cycle. The meta-learning-for-forecasting line (Talagala et al., 2023) informs the meta-feature design, but BECRA replaces supervised meta-learning with unsupervised causal lesson extraction and in-context planning, eliminating the need for annotated dataset-to-pipeline mappings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paradigm addresses a genuine industrial bottleneck: the absence of scalable supervision for mapping heterogeneous data characteristics to effective forecasting strategies, and the 82% reduction in trial costs versus AutoML makes it practically relevant for large-scale, dynamic forecasting environments. The transferability results—where the same lesson library drives comparable performance across ChatGPT-4, Claude 3.5, Gemini Pro, LLaMA-3.1-70B, and Qwen-2.5-72B—validate the decoupling of strategic knowledge from the reasoning engine, a property with broad implications for agent design beyond time series. Open questions include whether the paradigm scales to truly novel domains (e.g., materials science, drug discovery, as the authors speculate), how lesson quality degrades under severe distribution shift, and whether the modest forecasting gains over SOTA single-architecture models (often 0.01–0.03 MSE) justify the added system complexity in practice.
