## Problem

Online RL for LLM alignment is unstable under noisy or misspecified rewards, but existing stabilizers treat reward as a scalar reliability signal and ignore the vector-valued nature of learning signals. The paper identifies a specific geometric failure mode—directional inconsistency—where a small set of high-reward rollouts induces update directions that sharply conflict with the batch consensus, inflating gradient variance and causing oscillatory training, yet no prior method explicitly detects or rectifies such directionally conflicting rollouts.

## Input / Output

GEOALIGN consumes a batch of rollouts with their scalar rewards and detached last-layer hidden states (one vector per rollout, from the final generated token) and produces a rectified experience batch of identical size where directionally inconsistent rollouts are swapped with stable within-prompt alternatives. The module is forward-pass only: it introduces no additional backpropagation through the policy and adds roughly 1% wall-clock overhead and 300–500 MB GPU memory. Per-prompt rollout count K is preserved, making it a drop-in replacement for the experience buffer before any policy-gradient update.

## Method

GEOALIGN operates in five stages per RL iteration. First, it forms within-prompt preference pairs by strict reward ranking and computes unit-normalized latent displacement directions from lower- to higher-reward rollouts. Second, it trains a lightweight 3-layer MLP projector online (50 steps per iteration, cosine LR) with a binary orientation-classification objective that distills these raw directions into a concentrated improvement manifold. Third, it constructs a reward-margin-weighted consensus prototype as the normalized centroid of projected directions. Fourth, it scores each rollout via a cumulative Geometric Deviation Index (GDI) measuring angular deviation from the prototype across all pairs involving that rollout, then flags anomalies using a KDE-based density threshold (α·max f̂). Finally, it rectifies the experience buffer by replacing flagged rollouts with stable same-prompt alternatives sampled via a Best-Random strategy, preserving gradient density while neutralizing directionally conflicting signals.

## Results

On the mathematical reasoning task (DAPO-Math-17k, binary verified reward), GEOALIGN achieves the highest average score of 40.44 on Qwen3-1.7B and 55.94 on Qwen3-4B, outperforming BASE-GRPO by 1.6% and 2.1% relatively, respectively. On the HH-RLHF preference alignment task (continuous ArmoRM reward), GEOALIGN attains a mean LLM-judge score of 0.8885 (1.7B) and 0.8894 (4B), a 6.4% relative improvement over vanilla GRPO on the smaller model. Under controlled 5% reward corruption, GEOALIGN retains the highest performance on both tasks (e.g., 38.20 vs. 36.98 for BASE-GRPO on Math), confirming its robustness to noisy reward signals.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GEOALIGN outperforms all five baselines on both the Math and HH-RLHF tasks across both model sizes. Against BASE-GRPO (vanilla GRPO), GEOALIGN gains +0.63 (1.7B) and +1.16 (4B) on Math, and +0.0531 (1.7B) and +0.0222 (4B) on HH-RLHF. Against the strongest robust baseline SEED-GRPO (semantic-entropy-aware GRPO), GEOALIGN leads by +0.31 (1.7B) and +0.37 (4B) on Math, and by +0.0461 (1.7B) and +0.0151 (4B) on HH-RLHF. PF-PPO (both BR and BW variants) and PAR, which operate on scalar reward statistics, are consistently outperformed by GEOALIGN by 1–2 points on Math and 0.03–0.05 on HH-RLHF. PODS (variance-maximizing down-sampling) is the closest competitor on Math (39.58 vs. 40.44 for 1.7B) but still trails GEOALIGN. Under 5% reward corruption, GEOALIGN degrades least (e.g., 40.44→38.20 on Math vs. 39.81→36.98 for BASE-GRPO), confirming that its geometric directional-consistency signal provides an orthogonal robustness mechanism beyond scalar reward filtering.

## Relation to Prior Work

GEOALIGN departs from the dominant line of robust-RL-for-LLMs work (PF-PPO, PAR, PODS, SEED-GRPO) that treats reward as a scalar reliability signal and modulates update magnitude via clipping, reshaping, or uncertainty weighting. It also contrasts with gradient/influence-based data-selection methods (Hu et al., Dai et al., Li et al.) that require backward passes or higher-order computation. The paper's conceptual lineage draws on geometric structure in representation learning (alignment/uniformity, neural collapse) and repurposes it for online RL: within-prompt reward-ordered hidden-state displacements are projected onto a learned low-dimensional manifold, and angular deviation from a batch consensus prototype serves as a forward-only anomaly score. This positions GEOALIGN as a geometric, direction-aware complement to existing scalar-reward-based stabilizers rather than a replacement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of 'directional inconsistency' as a named, empirically characterized failure mode (long-tailed angular outliers among high-reward rollouts) gives the community a concrete geometric diagnostic for RL instability that is orthogonal to reward magnitude. The plug-and-play, forward-only design (negligible overhead, no extra backward passes) makes it practically adoptable in existing GRPO/PPO pipelines. Open questions include a formal link between latent directional consensus and gradient variance, adaptive selection of the density threshold α based on running reward statistics, and validation beyond the Qwen3 family (1.7B/4B) and the two task domains tested. The conflict-of-interest disclosure (authors employed by Alibaba, developer of Qwen3) and the modest absolute gains (~1–2% relative over the strongest baseline) temper the practical impact, though the robustness-to-corruption results and the Anomaly-Amplify negative control strengthen the causal story.
