## Problem

Existing poster generation methods—whether diffusion-based text-to-image models or layout-prediction pipelines—operate as one-shot generators with no mechanism for self-critique or iterative refinement. T2I models produce non-editable raster outputs with unreliable text rendering, while structured layout methods lack the ability to observe their own rendered output and make targeted corrections. The paper argues that poster creation is inherently an iterative, agentic process (draft → render → critique → revise) and that training such multi-turn behavior requires solving a credit-assignment problem that standard outcome-only rewards cannot address.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a free-form textual instruction (e.g., specifying theme, audience, layout constraints, and visual tone) and produces a complete JSON design specification containing typed elements (text, image, line, rect, ellipse) with relative integer coordinates in [0, 1000], font attributes, and color codes. A deterministic Skia-based renderer converts this JSON into a rasterized poster image plus symbolic execution warnings (e.g., text overflow, invalid geometry). The agent then observes both the rendered image and the warnings, and iterates up to T=3 refinement turns, each producing a new complete JSON specification that replaces the prior one.

## Method

PosterAgent formulates poster generation as a POMDP where a Qwen3-VL-8B MLLM policy outputs JSON design actions, a Skia+Seedream 4.0 renderer provides multimodal observations (rendered image + diagnostic warnings), and the agent iteratively refines over a fixed horizon. The key training contribution is Stage-Aware Reinforcement Learning (SARL), which decouples the learning signal into two stage-specific rewards: a draft reward (absolute quality of the initial design, scored 1–9 by an MLLM judge) and a refinement reward (relative improvement from initial to final poster, scored 0–4). Each training step uses a two-phase sampling strategy—G independent drafts form a draft group, and G refinement trajectories branch from a single fixed seed draft to form a refinement group—so that group-normalized advantages isolate the marginal value of editing from the variance of starting states. The policy is updated with a clipped PPO-style objective where draft trajectories contribute only to step-0 tokens and refinement trajectories only to steps 1–T, preventing the confounding that a single outcome reward would introduce.

## Results

On the in-domain PosterAgent-Test benchmark (evaluated by GPT-5), PosterAgent achieves an overall score of 7.41, surpassing Gemini 2.5 Pro (7.31) and matching GPT-5 (7.43) despite using an 8B-parameter open-weight backbone. On the external out-of-domain graphic-design benchmark (evaluated by GPT-4V), PosterAgent achieves a state-of-the-art overall score of 7.66, outperforming GPT-5 (7.63) and all other baselines. The method also substantially outperforms all text-to-image models, with a Text Quality score of 7.32 versus at most 5.04 for the strongest T2I baseline (Seedream 4.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PosterAgent (7.41 overall) decisively outperforms all four text-to-image baselines: HunyuanImage 3.0 (4.66), Qwen-Image (4.72), Seedream 3.0 (4.89), and Seedream 4.0 (6.28), with the largest gap in Text Quality (7.32 vs. 5.04 for Seedream 4.0). Among frontier LLMs, PosterAgent surpasses DeepSeek-V3.2 (5.14) and Kimi K2 (6.48) by wide margins, edges out Gemini 2.5 Pro (7.31) by 0.10, and is essentially tied with GPT-5 (7.43, a 0.02 difference). On the out-of-domain benchmark, PosterAgent (7.66) beats GPT-5 (7.63), Gemini 2.5 Pro (7.25), and Seedream 4.0 (7.47), and substantially outperforms the structured-IR methods COLE (6.00) and OpenCOLE (6.30), demonstrating that iterative agentic refinement generalizes beyond the training distribution. The base Qwen3-VL-8B model (5.08) improves to 6.76 after cold-start SFT and further to 7.41 after SARL training, confirming the contribution of both training stages.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the agentic self-refinement paradigm (ReAct, Reflexion, Self-Refine) from text/code domains into visual graphic design, where feedback is pixel-level rather than symbolic. It departs from prior layout-generation methods (COLE, OpenCOLE, CanvasVAE) that operate as one-shot predictors by formulating poster creation as a multi-turn POMDP with a rendering environment. The SARL training scheme extends GRPO-style group-normalized RL by introducing a two-phase sampling strategy that decouples draft and refinement credit assignment, addressing a confounding problem specific to iterative generative tasks that prior outcome-only reward schemes (Ouyang et al., Shao et al.) do not handle.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that an 8B open-weight model with iterative self-refinement can match or exceed frontier proprietary models (GPT-5, Gemini 2.5 Pro) on poster generation suggests that specialized agentic loops can substitute for raw model scale in structured creative tasks. The refinement-collapse finding under outcome-only rewards (Figure 4) is a practically important warning for anyone applying multi-turn RL to generative tasks. Open questions include whether SARL transfers to other design domains (web, UI, slides), whether MLLM-as-Judge rewards adequately capture professional aesthetic judgment, and how to integrate editable structured representations with the visual fidelity of modern T2I models.
