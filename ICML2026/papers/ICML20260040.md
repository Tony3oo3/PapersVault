## Problem

Existing spiking state space models are limited to mid-range sequences (≤16k) and lack the expressivity of second-order oscillatory dynamics needed for ultra-long-range tasks. Meanwhile, second-order ANN-based SSMs like LinOSS achieve strong long-range performance but rely on dense matrix multiplications and nonlinear activations (GeLU, GLU), making them unsuitable for energy-constrained neuromorphic hardware. The combined effects of neuronal heterogeneity and discretisation scheme on resonate-and-fire neurons for long-sequence modelling remain unexplored.

## Input / Output

The model consumes multivariate time series sequences of varying lengths (405 to 50,000 timesteps) that are first converted to sparse binary spike trains via a learnable spike encoder. For classification (UEA-MTSCA, HAR), it outputs class labels; for regression (PPG-DaLiA, 49,920 sequences at 128 Hz), it outputs continuous heart-rate values via a kernel-based spiking regressor with a learnable temporal kernel to overcome the limited output range of spikes; for multivariate forecasting (ETT, Electricity, Weather, Traffic, Exchange), it outputs future time series values. All internal computation is spike-based with no matrix multiplications, and the diagonal Ω matrix enables O(P log L) parallel-scan evaluation.

## Method

SH²RFSSM is a second-order spiking SSM whose core unit is the Harmonic Resonate-and-Fire (HRF) neuron, a real-valued damped harmonic oscillator (u' = −ω²v − 2bu + x, v' = u) that captures subthreshold resonance without complex-valued states. The SSM formulation replaces explicit damping with a diagonal frequency matrix Ω and a learnable input projection B, producing sustained oscillatory dynamics whose stability is governed by the discretisation choice: implicit Euler (IM) introduces numerical dissipation that sparsifies spikes, while IMEX preserves oscillatory energy on the unit circle. Heterogeneity is introduced across neuron parameters (u, b, ω, θ, Δt, v) and thresholds, with the paper showing that selective heterogeneity (Ω, θ, Δt) outperforms full heterogeneity and that IM discretisation consistently reduces spiking operations by ~1.4×. The architecture stacks N blocks of HRF layers followed by linear readout, entirely avoiding GeLU/GLU and dense matrix multiplications. For regression, a convolving kernel with learnable temporal structure is appended to the spike output, enabling accurate 50k-step forecasting; for forecasting, SpikHRFSSM replaces the FFT/IFFT layers in SpikF with HRF SSM layers, achieving 4× energy and 2× latency reduction.

## Results

On the UEA long-sequence classification benchmark (6 datasets, sequences up to 18k), SH²RFSSM achieves 67.5% average accuracy, trailing only LinOSS (67.9%) by 0.4% while outperforming Mamba (58.6%) by 8.9% and S6 (62.0%) by 5.5%. On the 50k-step PPG-DaLiA regression task, SH²RFSSM-IMEX achieves 8.5×10⁻² MSE, surpassing Mamba (10.7) and S6 (12.9) by 0.022 and 0.044 MSE respectively, though remaining 0.021 below LinOSS-IMEX (6.4). The model achieves ~69× energy efficiency over LinOSS on EigenWorms (365.53 vs. ~25,200 SOPs×10⁶) while eliminating all matrix multiplications and GeLU/GLU nonlinearities. On multivariate forecasting, SpikHRFSSM outperforms SpikF on Weather (0.242 vs. 0.245 MSE), ETTh2 (0.368 vs. 0.372), ETTm2 (0.274 vs. 0.281), and Traffic (0.494 vs. 0.497).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SH²RFSSM (67.5% avg) trails only LinOSS (67.9%) by 0.4% on the UEA classification benchmark, while outperforming PD-SSM (67.0%) by 0.5%, RFormer (64.5%) by 3.0%, SpikingSSM (63.6%) by 3.9%, S6 (62.0%) by 5.5%, P-SpikeSSM (61.4%) by 6.1%, and Mamba (58.6%) by 8.9%. On the 50k-step PPG-DaLiA regression task, SH²RFSSM-IMEX (8.5 MSE) beats Mamba (10.7) by 0.022 and S6 (12.9) by 0.044, but remains 0.021 below LinOSS-IMEX (6.4) and 0.033 below LinOSS-IM (7.5). For multivariate forecasting, SpikHRFSSM outperforms SpikF on 4 of 8 datasets (Weather, ETTh2, ETTm2, Traffic) and ties on ECL MAE, while iTransformer leads on ECL and Exchange. The model achieves 99% on UCI-HAR (beating Li et al. 2022 by 0.2%) and 92.7% on UniMiB-SHAR (beating SpikeDeepConvLSTM by 0.6%, trailing SpikeDCL by 1.2%). Critically, SH²RFSSM is the only fully spiking second-order SSM in the comparison, achieving ~69× energy efficiency over LinOSS on EigenWorms while eliminating all matrix multiplications and GeLU/GLU units.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads: (1) harmonic resonate-and-fire (HRF) spiking neurons, which extend the RF model to real-valued second-order dynamics (AlKhamissi et al., 2021; Higuchi et al., 2024), and (2) oscillatory state-space models with stable implicit/IMEX discretisation (Rusch & Rus, 2025). The key departure from prior spiking SSMs (Bal & Sengupta, 2025; Shen et al., 2025) is the elimination of all matrix multiplications and nonlinear activations (GeLU/GLU), yielding a fully spike-based second-order SSM. Additionally, SpikHRFSSM repurposes the SpikF forecasting architecture (Wu et al., 2025) by replacing its computationally expensive FFT/IFFF layers with HRF neurons in an SSM framework, and introduces a kernel-based spiking regressor to handle the limited output range of spike trains on 50k-length sequences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 69× theoretical energy reduction over LinOSS (the closest non-spiking second-order SSM) positions this architecture as a strong candidate for battery-powered neuromorphic wearables performing human activity recognition and long-range signal processing. The systematic study showing that a subset of heterogeneous parameters (Ω, θ, Δt) outperforms full heterogeneity, and that IM discretisation aids classification while IMEX aids regression, provides practical design guidance for spiking SSM practitioners. Open questions include whether the theoretical energy estimates hold on actual Loihi 2 hardware, whether the 0.4% classification gap to LinOSS can be closed, and whether the kernel-based regressor generalises beyond the PPG-DaLiA setting. The paper does not yet demonstrate on-chip deployment, leaving the neuromorphic advantage as a theoretical rather than empirical claim.
