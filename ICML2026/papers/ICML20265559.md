## Problem

Masked Diffusion Models (MDMs) suffer from an irreducible factorization error when decoding multiple tokens simultaneously, and the paper proves this error has a non-trivial theoretical lower bound that no distillation method can eliminate. The root cause is the deterministic single-state mask: because all masked positions share one fixed token, the model has no stochastic channel to encode inter-token correlations, capping few-step generation quality regardless of optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265559/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For unconditional generation, the model starts from fully masked states and iteratively unmasks tokens over a small number of steps (2–64), producing sequences of 128 tokens (LM1B) or 1024 tokens (OpenWebText). For conditional generation, a 50-token prefix is held fixed while the remaining 1024 tokens are generated. The key implementation detail is that each masked position's embedding is the pre-trained mask embedding plus MLP(ε), where ε is a continuous noise vector drawn from Uniform(−1,1)^d (d=768 for LM1B, d=2048 for OpenWebText), effectively simulating an infinite set of distinct mask states without explicitly enumerating them.

## Method

IMDM replaces MDM's single deterministic mask token with an infinite set of stochastic latent mask states that remain strictly disjoint from the data vocabulary, formulated as uniform discrete diffusion over an extended state space Z = V ∪ M with |M| → ∞. The forward process, posterior, and Rao-Blackwellized NELBO all reduce to the same simple forms as standard MDM, so pre-trained MDM weights transfer directly by initializing the MLP output layer to zero. Theoretically, the paper proves (Theorem 4.1) that MDMs incur a factorization-error lower bound proportional to the conditional mutual information of simultaneously decoded token pairs, and (Theorem 4.2) that IMDM admits an optimal model achieving zero factorization error via a partition-and-map mechanism that routes distinct noise realizations to distinct coherent token sequences. Practically, IMDM is distilled using existing methods (SDTT, ReDi, or their combination) with the same training objectives, and the stochastic mask embedding provides the additional degrees of freedom needed to capture token correlations in as few as 2 sampling steps.

## Results

On OpenWebText unconditional generation, IMDM (SDTT+ReDi) achieves a Gen. PPL of 500.84 at 2 steps, substantially outperforming Di4C (747.66) and SDTT (877.22), and reaching 134.58 at 4 steps versus Di4C's 236.27. On conditional generation, IMDM achieves a MAUVE score of 0.810 at just 2 steps, compared to 0.225 for Di4C and 0.201 for SDTT, while the teacher MDLM fails entirely (MAUVE ≈ 0.062). On LM1B, IMDM+SDTT+ReDi reduces 2-step Gen. PPL to 165.85, nearly halving the 353.90 achieved by MDLM+SDTT+ReDi. The performance gap over baselines narrows as step count increases, consistent with the theoretical prediction that the factorization-error bound vanishes in the many-step limit.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IMDM (with SDTT+ReDi distillation) outperforms all three baselines—MDLM (teacher), SDTT, and Di4C—across every step count on both unconditional and conditional OpenWebText generation. The advantage is most pronounced in the extreme few-step regime: at 2 steps, IMDM achieves a conditional MAUVE of 0.810 versus 0.225 for Di4C and 0.201 for SDTT, while the teacher MDLM produces incoherent text (MAUVE 0.062). On LM1B, IMDM+ReDi nearly halves the 2-step Gen. PPL of MDLM+ReDi (284.62 vs. 530.36), and IMDM+SDTT+ReDi achieves 165.85 versus 353.90 for MDLM+SDTT+ReDi. The gap over Di4C narrows at 16+ steps (e.g., 35.48 vs. 44.12 at 16 steps unconditional), consistent with the paper's theoretical analysis that the structural factorization-error bound specific to MDMs vanishes as step size shrinks. Sample entropy remains comparable across all methods (≈5.3–5.5 unconditional, ≈4.0–4.1 conditional), confirming that IMDM's gains reflect genuine quality improvements rather than a perplexity–diversity trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Masked Diffusion Models (MDMs/MDLM) by identifying a structural limitation in their deterministic single-state mask and replacing it with a stochastic infinite-state mask (IMDM). The derivation starts from uniform discrete diffusion (Schiff et al., 2025) and shows that enforcing data-mask disjointness recovers a masked form, allowing IMDM to inherit MDM's simple training objective and conditional generation capabilities while lifting the factorization-error floor. On the distillation side, IMDM is positioned as a drop-in replacement for the student model in existing few-step methods (SDTT, ReDi, Di4C), with the key implementation trick being an MLP that injects continuous noise into the pre-trained mask embedding, enabling seamless weight transfer from MDM checkpoints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical lower bound (Theorem 4.1) provides a clean explanation for why MDMs plateau in few-step generation regardless of distillation quality, which is a useful diagnostic for the growing diffusion-LLM literature. The practical method is lightweight (a two-layer MLP on the mask embedding) and compatible with existing pre-trained MDM weights, lowering the barrier to adoption. Open questions include whether the partition-and-map construction is actually realized by trained models at scale (the paper shows existence but not automatic convergence), whether the noise dimension requirement grows with model/dataset complexity, and whether the advantage persists as MDM architectures evolve beyond the GPT-2-small scale tested here.
