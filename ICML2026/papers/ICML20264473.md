## Problem

Existing counterfactual image generation methods face a fundamental tension: diffusion+SCM approaches require expensive retraining per dataset and enforce disentanglement only in auxiliary encoders with limited influence on diffusion latents, while T2I-based editing relies on fragile prompt engineering without any explicit causal mechanism. Neither paradigm can simultaneously propagate interventions through a causal graph, support fine-grained continuous attribute control, and preserve identity in a frozen pretrained backbone.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 256×256 RGB image, a vector of K semantic attributes (continuous scalars or categorical labels), a binary adjacency matrix encoding the causal DAG, and a do-intervention specifying which attribute to set to a new value. It outputs a single counterfactual image at the same resolution, generated via DDIM inversion and sampling. The causal graph is modular and can be swapped at inference without retraining the diffusion backbone, and the framework supports both categorical (F1-scored) and continuous (MAE-scored) attributes across domains from synthetic pendulums to brain MRI.

## Method

Causal-Adapter attaches a half-scale replica of the Stable Diffusion U-Net denoiser as a pluggable adapter that produces residual signals injected into the mid- and up-sampling blocks of the frozen backbone, with only the adapter, placeholder token embeddings, and linear projectors optimized. Two regularization strategies address the core disentanglement challenge: Prompt-Aligned Injection (PAI) maps each causal attribute y_i to a learnable token embedding v_i(y_i) = c_i + g_i(y_i) and feeds it through the cross-attention text channel, aligning numeric causal semantics with spatial features in the diffusion latents; Conditioned Token Contrastive Loss (CTC) applies an InfoNCE objective across a batch to enforce same-attribute invariance and cross-attribute separation, suppressing spurious correlations. The SCM itself is a nonlinear additive noise model f_i(A_i ⊙ Y; ω_i) + u_i trained by negative log-likelihood, enabling exact do-interventions in the observed attribute space. Counterfactual generation follows Pearl's abduction-action-prediction via DDIM inversion, and an optional attention-guidance step restricts cross-attention modifications to intervened tokens for localized, identity-preserving edits.

## Results

On the synthetic Pendulum dataset, Causal-Adapter achieves up to 91% MAE reduction on light intervention (0.041 vs. 0.434 for CausalDiffAE) while correctly propagating causal effects to descendant shadow attributes. On CelebA, it reduces LPIPS by 86% (0.017 vs. 0.122 for HVAE) and FID by 79% (8.152 vs. 27.861 for GAN) while maintaining competitive intervention F1 scores. On ADNI brain MRI, it achieves an 87% FID reduction (9.130 vs. 74.696 for HVAE) and up to 50% MAE reduction on fine-grained ventricular volume edits. On CelebA-HQ, it reaches 99.26% F1 on eyeglasses intervention with 0.049 L1 reversibility and 0.028 LPIPS identity preservation, outperforming DiffCounter (0.185 L1, 0.096 LPIPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Pendulum, Causal-Adapter dramatically outperforms CausalVAE (Yang et al., 2021) by over 1000× in MAE (e.g., 0.014 vs. 24.86 on pendulum do(p)), and substantially beats both DisDiffAE (Preechakul et al., 2022) and CausalDiffAE (Komanduri et al., 2024c), which show near-uniform MAE across all interventions indicating poor causal propagation. On CelebA and ADNI, it dominates the VAE (Kingma & Welling, 2013), HVAE (Vahdat & Kautz, 2020), and GAN (Goodfellow et al., 2014) baselines on realism (FID) and composition (LPIPS) by large margins, though HVAE achieves slightly higher age F1 on CelebA (65.4 vs. 58.5) at the cost of much worse fidelity. On CelebA-HQ, Causal-Adapter matches DiffCounter (Rasal et al., 2025) on intervention F1 (99.26 vs. 96.86 for eyeglasses) but substantially improves reversibility (0.049 vs. 0.185 L1) and identity preservation (0.028 vs. 0.096 LPIPS), while clearly outperforming VCI (Wu et al., 2025) and HVAE (De Sousa Ribeiro et al., 2023). For causal structure learning on Pendulum, Causal-Adapter with learnable adjacency matrix recovers more true edges than SDCD (Nazaret et al., 2024), NOTEARS (Zheng et al., 2018), and DAGMA (Bello et al., 2022), unifying causal discovery and counterfactual generation in one framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

Causal-Adapter sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) diffusion-based counterfactual generation with explicit SCMs (DiffSCM, CausalDiffAE, DiffCounter), which typically require retraining or fine-tuning the diffusion backbone and enforce disentanglement only in auxiliary encoders, and (2) adapter-based controllable diffusion (ControlNet, T2I-Adapter), which attaches lightweight trainable modules to a frozen T2I model. The paper departs from both by repurposing the adapter paradigm to inject causal semantic attributes through the *text-embedding* channel (Prompt-Aligned Injection) rather than the image-latent channel, and by adding a token-level contrastive loss (CTC) to enforce attribute disentanglement in the cross-attention space. For localized editing, it borrows the token-level attention manipulation from PnP Inversion, and for the counterfactual inference procedure it follows Pearl's abduction-action-prediction paradigm implemented via DDIM inversion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that counterfactual generation no longer requires retraining an expensive diffusion backbone: a small half-scale adapter trained in under a day on a single A100 suffices, and the causal graph can be swapped at inference time without retraining. This makes the approach attractive for safety-critical domains (e.g., medical imaging) where the causal structure may evolve or be domain-specific. Open questions include scalability to large attribute sets and complex causal graphs, the sensitivity to graph misspecification (the ablation with SCM1 shows degraded but not catastrophic performance), and whether the text-channel injection strategy generalizes to modalities beyond RGB images. The extension to learnable causal structure (NOTEARS/DAGMA regularization) is promising but only demonstrated on the simple Pendulum dataset.
