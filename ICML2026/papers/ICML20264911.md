## Problem

In hierarchical RL-OC frameworks, the lower-level optimization problem is typically formulated with either long-horizon MPC (computationally prohibitive for real-time use) or single-step myopic objectives (suboptimal trajectories). The paper addresses the underexplored question of how to design the lower-level cost structure in a principled way that is both computationally tractable and aligned with the overarching long-term task objective, rather than relying on heuristic or manually specified objectives.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input the current system state x_t and a small set of offline expert demonstrations (state-action pairs from a full-horizon MPC controller, covering diverse operating conditions but not necessarily consecutive). The upper-level RL policy outputs a low-dimensional subgoal x̂_t = Cx_t^des (compressed via a known matrix C), which together with x_t feeds the lower-level single-step convex program. The outputs are a feasible control action u_t satisfying all physical constraints, and the structured cost parameters θ (vectors and thresholds for ReLU-based regularization terms) that define the lower-level objective.

## Method

The framework is a bi-level system where an upper-level RL policy (A2C-GNN or SAC) generates low-dimensional subgoals, and a lower-level single-step convex optimization (FOP) maps (x_t, x̂_t) to a feasible control action. The key innovation is parameterizing the FOP objective as c^T u_t + Σ_k max{θ_k^T u_t − ν_k, 0} + l‖u_t‖², where the ReLU-based terms d_θ are learned via inverse optimization from expert data. The inverse problem is formulated through KKT conditions, minimizing the sum of squared duality gaps (rather than solution distances) plus a regularization on θ, and solved as a Mixed Integer Program via spatial branch-and-bound. For the special case of linear stage cost and state-independent dynamics, the paper proves the inverse feasible set is always non-empty (Proposition 3.6) and that adding a small quadratic term ensures forward stability, bounding the distance between recovered and expert solutions by √(ε₀/l). This ReLU-based cost structure is shown to be more expressive than standard quadratic terminal costs, enabling the lower-level policy to rationalize expert behavior that would otherwise be infeasible under simpler formulations.

## Results

On the AV Rebalancing task, Bi-level-learned (Ours) achieves a reward of 35019 (±53.0) and served demand of 3141 (±6.14), approaching the MPC oracle (35725, 3203) while reducing per-step decision time from 1.44 s to 0.025 s. On Mobile Robot Navigation, the method cuts travel time to 4.40 s (vs. 9.14 s for Bi-level-original and 7.60 s for end-to-end RL) and energy to 1.52 J (vs. 6.20 J and 6.78 J respectively). On Supply Chain Inventory Management, the learned formulation yields a reward of 9442 (±42.3) and served demand of 930 (±1.17), substantially outperforming all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Bi-level-original (Gammelli et al., 2023), the proposed Bi-level-learned method wins on every task and metric: AV reward improves from 33406 to 35019 (+4.8%), robot travel time drops from 9.14 s to 4.40 s (−52%), and robot energy falls from 6.20 J to 1.52 J (−75%). Against Inverse MPC (Zhang et al., 2024a), the learned ReLU-based cost structure yields higher AV reward (35019 vs. 32426) and substantially higher supply-chain reward (9442 vs. 5608), confirming that the inverse-optimization-guided formulation is more effective than the quadratic terminal-cost assumption used in prior inverse-MPC work. Against Value Approximation (Abdufattokhov et al., 2021), the gap is even larger on AV rebalancing (35019 vs. 24774 reward), indicating that a single learned quadratic terminal cost is insufficient when the horizon is aggressively shortened to one step. The method also outperforms end-to-end RL by a wide margin on all tasks (e.g., 35019 vs. 20989 AV reward; 4.40 s vs. 7.60 s robot time), validating the benefit of the hierarchical RL-OC decomposition. Overall, the learned formulation closes most of the gap to the MPC oracle while reducing per-step computation by roughly 50×.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the bi-level RL-OC framework of Gammelli et al. (2023) and Schmidt et al. (2024), where an upper-level RL policy generates subgoals and a lower-level single-step optimization produces feasible actions. The key departure is that prior work leaves the lower-level cost function as a heuristic or myopic design choice; here, the authors cast its construction as an inverse optimization problem, recovering a ReLU-based cost structure from a small set of expert demonstrations. The theoretical analysis draws on inverse optimization theory (Bertsimas et al., 2015; Chan et al., 2025) and the forward-stability notion of Shahmoradi & Lee (2022), while the practical formulation connects to inverse MPC (Zhang et al., 2024a) and value-approximation MPC (Abdufattokhov et al., 2021), from which it departs by using a non-quadratic, piecewise-linear cost structure with guaranteed inverse feasibility.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a concrete gap in hierarchical RL-OC: the lower-level formulation was previously ad hoc, and the authors show that a principled, data-driven design yields measurable improvements in decision quality and a 50–60× reduction in per-step solve time relative to long-horizon MPC. The theoretical guarantees (inverse feasibility, bounded forward distance via strong convexity) provide a clean design recipe, but they are currently restricted to control-affine dynamics with linear or quadratic stage costs. Open questions include rigorous handling of noisy or suboptimal expert data, extension to non-control-affine and high-dimensional nonlinear systems, and whether the ReLU-based cost structure generalizes to unstructured or perceptual inputs. The gradient-based CVXPYLayers alternative degrading on the mobile robot task suggests that solving the inverse problem to high precision may be important for capturing the right structure, a trade-off that remains unresolved at scale.
