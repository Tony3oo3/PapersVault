## Problem

Current deep research systems follow rigid, one-shot-scoping workflows with long autonomous runs, offering no interpretable mechanism for mid-process course correction when user intent shifts. No existing framework jointly provides adaptive pause decisions (when to ask vs. proceed), live persona modeling that evolves during the session, and diversity-aware exploration within a single research tree, leaving a gap for controllable, user-aligned long-horizon research agents.

## Input / Output

STEER takes a user query and only the first sentence of the persona description as initial input, then incrementally consumes user feedback (selected directions and new suggestions) at adaptive pause points during the research process. It produces a cited synthesis report, an incrementally built research tree (fixed depth 3, breadth 3), a continuously updated live persona model P̂ = (p̂_text, Â), and binary pause/proceed decisions at each frontier node. The system operates with GPT-4o as the underlying LLM and uses gpt-4.1-mini as an LLM judge for alignment and focus scoring.

## Method

STEER structures deep research as an interactive planning task over a research tree, with three coupled modules: (1) diversity-aware exploration via greedy MMR to select K diversified follow-up directions at each frontier node; (2) a cost-benefit pause decision that compares the expected utility gain of pausing (ΔEV, computed from branch utilities and execution costs of pruned branches) against a user-specific pause cost C(n*) that grows with the number of prior pauses within each top-level direction, governed by hyperparameters C0 (base interruption sensitivity) and Tol (tolerance budget); and (3) a live persona model that is updated from user selections and research findings at each pause, conditioning all downstream planning, branch utility scoring, and synthesis. Branch utility U combines three normalized signals: alignment gain (predicted increase in persona-aspect coverage), a UCB-style exploration bonus over facet tags to prevent collapse onto repeated angles, and information gain measuring semantic novelty relative to accumulated learnings. The pause mechanism uses upper/lower confidence bounds on branch utilities to estimate which candidates the user would retain, making the decision interpretable and tunable via C0 and Tol.

## Results

On a held-out set of 200 persona-query pairs, STEER achieves 85.70% alignment and 86.45% focus (keypoint-level), outperforming the strongest open-source baseline GPT-Researcher with full persona (79.48% alignment) by 7.83% and the proprietary o4-mini-deep-research (75.72% alignment) by 9.98%. STEER also leads on breadth (91.29) and balance (84.19) among all systems. In a human study with 12 annotators, STEER is preferred in ~86–90% of pairwise alignment comparisons and ~83% of focus comparisons, with statistically significant gains in coverage (+0.623, p=3.05e-12) and findability (+0.690, p=1.64e-11) over baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GPT-Researcher (the strongest open-source baseline), STEER wins on alignment by 6.22 points (85.70 vs. 79.48), focus by 2.62 points, breadth by 0.98 points, and balance by 1.61 points, while using only the first sentence of the persona versus the full persona given to the baseline. Against OpenDeepResearch, STEER dominates across nearly all metrics: alignment +8.50, depth +5.25, breadth +3.67, insight +9.86, and balance +4.75. Against the proprietary o4-mini-deep-research model, STEER leads on alignment (+9.98), breadth (+3.93), and balance (+1.56), but trails slightly on depth (88.67 vs. 89.10 at initial-persona) and insight (83.04 vs. 85.01 at full-persona), indicating the proprietary model retains an edge in raw analytical depth when given full persona context. The human study confirms these automated findings: STEER is preferred in 86–90% of alignment and 83% of focus pairwise judgments over both GPT-Researcher and o4-mini-deep-research, with large effect sizes on coverage and findability.

## Relation to Prior Work

STEER synthesizes ideas from three largely separate threads—deep research pipelines (GPT-Researcher, Open Deep Research, DeepResearcher), personalization/alignment (ALOE, LaMP, LAMP-QA, PersonaAgent), and interactive clarification (STAR-GATE, Learning to Clarify, ReasonGraph)—into a single interactive control loop over a research tree. Rather than building on one prior architecture, it repurposes MMR (from IR) for diversity-aware planning, a UCB-inspired count-based bonus for facet exploration, and a cost-benefit decision rule (drawing on best-arm identification) to govern when to pause. The key conceptual departure is from the dominant 'one-shot scoping followed by a long autonomous run' paradigm to an adaptive, interpretable pause policy coupled with a live persona that conditions all downstream modules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

STEER provides the first benchmarkable framework that jointly supports mid-process steering, adaptive pause decisions, and live persona modeling in deep research, and releases a persona-query evaluation suite that fills a gap in existing benchmarks (which lack persona-conditioned, aspect-grounded targets). Open questions include robustness to noisy, contradictory, or abandoned user feedback; the absence of a runtime/latency analysis; and whether the simulated User Agent faithfully captures real user behavior. The framework's modularity (utility function is extensible with factuality, safety, etc.) suggests applicability beyond research to other long-horizon, high-stakes tasks like financial advising or legal research, but these remain unvalidated.
