## Problem

AR models augmented with MCTS achieve strong 'System 2' reasoning but suffer from high latency due to serial token-by-token generation, while diffusion LLMs offer parallel generation speed yet lack the rigorous search, evaluation, and revision mechanisms needed for complex reasoning. Directly transplanting MCTS into diffusion models is architecturally infeasible because MCTS requires discrete sequential state transitions, whereas diffusion generation is a continuous, full-sequence progressive denoising process with no natural decision boundaries.

## Input / Output

The method takes a mathematical problem statement as input and produces a structured reasoning chain composed of semantically complete thought blocks (default 64 tokens each) followed by a final numerical answer. The input is consumed through a masked diffusion generator that reveals tokens block-by-block in reverse denoising order, with a deterministic mask schedule controlling which positions are unmasked at each timestep. The reasoning chain is organized as a search tree where each node corresponds to a cumulative sequence of generated blocks, and the final answer is extracted from the highest-valued terminal node after lookahead simulation and in-place revision.

## Method

DiffuReason discretizes the continuous diffusion reverse-denoising process into a Markov Decision Process over 'thought blocks' (the sequence of length L is partitioned into T blocks of L/T tokens, one generated per timestep), enabling tree-structured search over the generation trajectory. It executes four MCTS-style stages per node: greedy Selection of the top-k highest-value child, Expansion via K stochastic block-wise diffusion samples with dynamic truncation at sentence boundaries, Simulation using a composite value function that blends a Process Reward Model score with a lookahead rollout (1-step greedy denoising of remaining masked tokens scored by an Outcome Reward Model, averaged over M Gumbel-perturbed rollouts), and Revision via confidence-aware in-place re-masking of the lowest-probability tokens within the selected block. A key architectural advantage is that the bidirectional attention of diffusion models enables O(1) in-place revision of local errors without regenerating all subsequent content, in contrast to the O(N) rewriting cost in AR models. The method replaces the standard UCB exploration rule with greedy selection, relying on the stochastic re-sampling in revision and threshold-triggered regeneration to maintain exploration diversity.

## Results

On the four mathematical reasoning benchmarks, DiffuReason(WeDLM-8B) achieves the best accuracy across all datasets: 90.1% on GSM8K, 68.2% on MATH500, 20.0% on AIME 2024, and 51.2% on Gaokao 2023, while running in 70.6 s per problem. On the hardest benchmark (AIME 2024), this represents a 6× improvement over the base WeDLM-8B (3.3%) and a 2× improvement over the next-best method (Llama3.1-8B-Instruct at 13.3%). In terms of efficiency, DiffuReason(WeDLM) is 37.3% faster than the strongest AR search baseline (Llama3.1-8B-Instruct + CoT at 112.7 s) while outperforming it on every dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the base diffusion models, DiffuReason improves on every dataset: over LLaDA-8B it gains +3.8 pp on GSM8K (75.6 vs 71.8), +3.2 pp on MATH500 (34.6 vs 31.4), +3.3 pp on AIME 2024 (10.0 vs 6.7), and +1.8 pp on Gaokao 2023 (25.8 vs 24.0); over WeDLM-8B the gains are larger on harder tasks, reaching +16.7 pp on AIME 2024 (20.0 vs 3.3) and +9.4 pp on MATH500 (68.2 vs 58.8). Against the AR search baseline Llama3.1-8B-Instruct + CoT, DiffuReason(WeDLM) wins on all four datasets (e.g., 68.2 vs 45.6 on MATH500, 20.0 vs 3.3 on AIME 2024) while being 37.3% faster (70.6 s vs 112.7 s). Against the RL-finetuned D1-LLaDA, DiffuReason(WeDLM) substantially outperforms it on MATH500 (68.2 vs 40.2), though D1-LLaDA retains an edge on GSM8K (82.1 vs 90.1 is actually won by DiffuReason; D1-LLaDA's 82.1 is below DiffuReason(WeDLM)'s 90.1). Against the chain-structured diffusion search method R3-LLaDA, DiffuReason(LLaDA) wins on all four datasets (75.6 vs 72.3 GSM8K, 34.6 vs 31.9 MATH500, 10.0 vs 6.7 AIME, 25.8 vs 15.1 Gaokao) but at roughly 2× the inference time (88.3 s vs 41.8 s). Overall, DiffuReason(WeDLM) is the only method that simultaneously achieves top accuracy on all four benchmarks and sub-75 s inference, establishing a clear accuracy–efficiency frontier over both AR and diffusion baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: MCTS-based 'System 2' reasoning for autoregressive models (ToT, RAP, AlphaMath) and parallel-generation diffusion language models (LLaDA, WeDLM). Prior diffusion reasoning work (DoT, DiffCoT, R3) aligns CoT steps with denoising timesteps but remains chain-structured, lacking tree search and global planning. DiffuReason's key departure is discretizing the continuous denoising flow into semantically complete 'thought blocks' that serve as discrete MDP states, enabling genuine tree-structured search with lookahead simulation and O(1) in-place revision—capabilities that are architecturally impossible in AR models due to causal attention constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that diffusion models can achieve MCTS-level reasoning accuracy while retaining a 37% speed advantage over AR+CoT, suggesting a viable path to 'slow thinking' without serial generation latency. The O(1) in-place revision (vs. O(N) rewriting in AR) is a fundamental architectural advantage that could compound as reasoning chains grow longer. However, the method still requires external PRM/ORM models (Qwen2.5-Math-7B), remains 2× slower than the base diffusion model, and the largest gains (2× on AIME) come from a very low base (3.3%). Open questions include whether the block-wise discretization generalizes beyond mathematics, whether the external reward model dependency can be internalized, and whether the approach scales to larger diffusion models or longer reasoning horizons.
