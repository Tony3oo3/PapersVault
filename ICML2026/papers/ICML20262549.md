## Problem

Generative source separation methods (diffusion, flow matching) achieve high-quality separation but require clean source data for training, which is unavailable in many real-world domains such as bio-acoustics, gravitational wave detection, and in-the-wild audio. Existing unsupervised methods that avoid this dependency are regression-based and produce unrealistic artifacts, leaving a gap for generative models trained purely from observed mixtures.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single-channel mixture signal m (16 kHz audio or overlapping image pair) and outputs K separated source signals in R^{K×d}. The flow matching ODE is initialized from a mixture-consistent noise distribution x₀ = (1/K)·1·m + P⊥z that guarantees the sum of sources equals the input mixture at t=0, and a permutation-equivariant architecture with a projection layer enforces mixture consistency at the output. The model is trained entirely on observed mixtures with no access to clean sources at any stage.

## Method

SURF integrates supervised flow matching (FLOSS) with self-supervised remixing in a teacher-student loop: a MixIT-initialized teacher estimates sources from real mixtures, these estimates are randomly permuted and recombined into synthetic mixtures, and a student flow matching model is trained on the synthetic data. The key technical bridge is the denoiser proxy x̂₁ = x_t + (1−t)v_θ(x_t, t, m), which converts the velocity-field regression of FM into a source-estimate regression compatible with remixing losses. Two loss variants are provided: ReMixIT-FM regresses the student's clean-source estimate on the (permuted) teacher estimates, while Self-Remixing-FM regresses the reconstructed mixture on the original observed mixture, avoiding propagation of teacher errors. The teacher is updated via exponential moving average, and the paper provides a population-level analysis decomposing both losses into a pseudo-supervised FM term plus correction terms, along with a Wake-Sleep variational inference interpretation of the ReMixIT variant.

## Results

On MNIST (2-source image mixtures), SURF (ReMixIT) achieves PSNR 37.26, SSIM 0.992, LPIPS 0.001, and FID 19.57, nearly matching the supervised flow matching upper bound (PSNR 37.44, FID 19.47) while using no clean source data. On Libri2Mix speech separation, SURF (ReMixIT) reaches SI-SDR 16.54 and ESTOI 0.893, substantially outperforming all unsupervised regression baselines (MixIT: 12.39, ReMixIT: 13.22, Self-Remixing: 13.60) and approaching the supervised flow model (17.89). On the FUSS universal sound separation benchmark (AudioSet-trained), SURF (ReMixIT) achieves 32.67 dB SI-SDR on single-source mixtures versus 10.99 for MixIT and 19.83 for ReMixIT, while also improving the 'Equal' source-count estimation metric from 0.339 to 0.574.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SURF dramatically outperforms all unsupervised regression baselines: on MNIST, SURF (ReMixIT) achieves PSNR 37.26 versus 21.90 (MixIT), 22.81 (ReMixIT), and 23.13 (Self-Remixing), and FID 19.57 versus 30.89, 30.55, and 28.14 respectively. On Libri2Mix, SURF gains 3.3–4.2 dB SI-SDR over the regression baselines (16.54 vs. 12.39–13.60) and 0.06–0.07 ESTOI. Against the supervised flow matching upper bound, SURF is within 0.18 dB SI-SDR on Libri2Mix (16.54 vs. 17.89) and within 0.18 PSNR on MNIST (37.26 vs. 37.44), but trails by ~5 dB FID on CIFAR-10 (14.83 vs. 9.60). SURF also surpasses the supervised generative BASIS method on both FID (19.57 vs. 38.62 on MNIST) and Inception Score (8.20–8.25 vs. 8.29 on CIFAR-10), while using no clean source data. On the AudioSet/FUSS universal separation task, SURF (ReMixIT) achieves 32.67 dB on 1-source mixtures versus 19.83 (ReMixIT) and 10.99 (MixIT), though it slightly underperforms on 3–4 source mixtures (10.38/7.52 vs. 12.46/10.24 for supervised flow).

## Relation to Prior Work

SURF bridges two previously separate lines of work: supervised flow matching for source separation (FLOSS, Scheibler et al. 2025) and regression-based self-supervised remixing (MixIT, ReMixIT, Self-Remixing). Prior generative separation methods (BASIS, diffusion-based approaches) all required clean source data or a pre-trained prior, while prior unsupervised methods were exclusively regression-based and suffered from artifacts. The paper's core contribution is a non-trivial integration that adapts the velocity-field learning of flow matching to the teacher-student remixing loop, and it provides a novel theoretical interpretation of the resulting objectives as a Wake-Sleep algorithm with an implicit prior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SURF addresses a practical bottleneck in source separation: in domains like bio-acoustics, gravitational wave detection, or in-the-wild audio, clean single-source training data is simply unavailable, and domain shift further degrades supervised models. By achieving near-supervised quality (e.g., 37.26 vs. 37.44 PSNR on MNIST) without any clean sources, the method opens the door to generative separation in data-scarce settings. Open questions include performance on complex multi-source mixtures (the 3- and 4-source FUSS results are weaker), the risk of hallucination inherent to generative models, and whether the EMA-based teacher update can be replaced by a principled Wake-Sleep update. The Wake-Sleep connection also suggests a path toward more theoretically grounded training schedules.
