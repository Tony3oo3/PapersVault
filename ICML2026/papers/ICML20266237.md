## Problem

Graph foundation models aim to reuse a single backbone across diverse graph domains, yet transfer is often uneven and can exhibit negative transfer. Prior work addresses this through model-centric strategies (architecture, tokenization, adaptation), but no principled, data-centric theory exists to characterize how hard it is to transfer between two graph domains independent of model choice. The paper asks: given a fixed Lipschitz backbone, which properties of two graph data distributions determine how much the model's representations change across domains?

## Input / Output

The method takes weighted undirected graphs of potentially different sizes from two domains, represented by their normalized adjacency operators Δ = A/n, and computes positional encoding tokens (top-k Laplacian eigenvectors for Eig-PE, or rank-k spectral projector applied to a readout for Proj-PE) as node-level features. Each finite graph is embedded into a continuous step-graphon on [0,1]², enabling size-independent comparison in a common L² function space. The output is a graph class label from a DeepSets or message-passing backbone, together with a theoretical decomposition of the cross-domain output gap into two finite-sample approximation terms (ε₁, ε₂, shrinking as O_p(√(log n)/n)) and an intrinsic, relabeling-invariant graphon discrepancy (ε_gra).

## Method

The core contribution is a graphon-based decomposition of cross-domain representation shift. Finite graphs are embedded as step-graphons (Definition 4.1), and Theorem 4.3 establishes that under Lipschitz graphons, the induced step-graphon operator concentrates around the latent graphon at rate O_p(√(log n)/n). A key ingredient is PE stability (Lemma 4.8): via Davis-Kahan perturbation theory, Eig-PE tokens are stable with constant C_eig ∝ √k · max_ℓ γ_ℓ⁻¹ (sensitive to individual eigen-gaps), while Proj-PE tokens are stable with C_proj ∝ B/γ_k (sensitive only to the subspace gap), making projector-based encodings more robust in small-gap regimes. Combining PE stability with Lipschitz backbone stability (Proposition 4.5), Theorem 4.11 yields the explicit bound ‖f(t_G¹) − f(t_G²)‖ ≤ L_θ C_PE(ε₁ + ε_gra + ε₂) for set backbones and L_θ(1+C_PE)(ε₁ + ε_gra + ε₂) for message-passing backbones. Experiments on Fourier graphon classification validate the theory: size shifts produce U-shaped test error (coverage issue), graphon perturbations cause sharp OOD degradation, PE dimension k exhibits an expressivity-stability trade-off, and graph merging augmentation helps most when the train-test size gap is large.

## Results

The paper's main theoretical result (Theorem 4.11) decomposes cross-domain model output shift into two finite-sample approximation terms (ε₁, ε₂) and an intrinsic graphon-mismatch term (ε_gra), with the bound scaling as L_θ·C_PE·(ε₁ + ε_gra + ε₂) for set-based backbones and L_θ·(1+C_PE)·(ε₁ + ε_gra + ε₂) for message-passing backbones. On real-world graph classification, graph merging augmentation (sampling 1–5% synthetic graphs from estimated class-specific graphons) reduces test error: COLLAB improves from 0.4384 (vanilla) to 0.4069 (1% merging), IMDB-BINARY from 0.4631 to 0.4508 (3%), and REDDIT-BINARY from 0.4108 to 0.4084 (3%). Synthetic experiments confirm the predicted U-shape in test error under size shift: increasing the large-graph budget fraction λ monotonically reduces train-test token discrepancy but degrades in-domain test error due to reduced coverage of smaller sizes, while OOD-size (n=2048) error saturates near zero.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is primarily a theory paper, so it does not benchmark against competing GFM architectures. Instead, it validates its decomposition through controlled comparisons. On the synthetic size-shift task, both DeepSets (set-based) and GIN (message-passing) backbones exhibit the same U-shaped test-error curve as the large-graph budget fraction λ increases, confirming the theory is backbone-agnostic. For positional encodings, Eig-PE shows a clear expressivity-stability trade-off: test error is minimized at intermediate k (around 16–32), while larger k degrades performance due to shrinking eigen-gaps amplifying token instability (consistent with C_eig ∝ √k · max γ_ℓ⁻¹). Proj-PE (SPE-style learnable projector) achieves lower OOD error than Eig-PE at k=8 or 16 but degrades more sharply at larger k and performs worse on the smallest in-domain graphs (n=128). For graph merging augmentation on real datasets, modest ratios (1–3%) consistently help when the baseline has room to improve, but aggressive augmentation (4–5%) can hurt, as seen by IMDB-BINARY's error jumping to 0.5369 at 4%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the graphon-based GNN transferability framework of Maskey et al. (2023) and Ruiz et al. (2020) by shifting the focus from model-specific generalization bounds to a data-centric, backbone-agnostic decomposition of cross-domain output shift. While prior GFM work (Sun et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2024; Xia & Huang, 2024) addresses transfer through architectural choices like MoE routing or prompt-based adaptation, this paper isolates the data-side question: which properties of two graph domains determine how much a fixed representation changes its outputs. A key new ingredient is the explicit connection between positional encoding stability (eig-PE vs. proj-PE) and the transfer bound through a spectral-gap-dependent constant C_PE, building on the Davis-Kahan perturbation machinery used in Keriven et al. (2020) for operator convergence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decomposition into finite-sample approximation error and intrinsic graphon mismatch provides the first principled, model-agnostic tool for diagnosing when a GFM will fail under domain shift, translating directly into data-curation guidance (e.g., when graph merging helps and when it does not). However, the theory is restricted to dense graphs and Lipschitz backbones, and the predicted eig-PE vs. proj-PE separation was not clearly isolated in experiments due to optimization confounders and the absence of a well-separated spectral regime in the Fourier graphon testbed. Extending the framework to sparse graphs, attributed or heterogeneous graphs, and designing experiments that cleanly validate the PE-stability prediction remain important open questions.
