## Problem

Mechanistic interpretability of Transformer MLPs currently relies on training auxiliary proxy models (Sparse Autoencoders, TransCoders) that introduce approximation bias and incur computational costs comparable to pretraining smaller language models. The paper asks whether a natively interpretable structure exists within MLP weight matrices that can be extracted without any auxiliary training, preserving exact fidelity to the original pretrained parameters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265305/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes the pretrained down-projection weight matrix W_down (d×n) and up-projection W_up (n×d) of each MLP layer, an input text sequence, and a target token embedding t*. It produces per-layer rank-1 Detector-Effector Unit subspaces with scalar contribution scores c_i = (σ_i v_i^T x)(t* u_i), from which high-contribution pathway indices are selected per sample and aggregated across samples. Semantic labels are assigned to each subspace by projecting the left singular vector onto the vocabulary embedding matrix and evaluating both polarities (±u_i) to account for SVD sign ambiguity.

## Method

NaNA decomposes each MLP down-projection via SVD into orthogonal rank-1 Detector-Effector Units, where the right singular vector v_i acts as a detector probing input alignment, the left singular vector u_i as an effector orienting the output update, and σ_i as a signal gain. Subspace Contribution Analysis (SCA) scores each DEU by the element-wise product of detector alignment (σ_i v_i^T x) and effector alignment (t* u_i), identifying 'causal bridges' that satisfy both input activation and output relevance simultaneously. Across middle-to-late layers, high-contribution subspaces form directed pathways whose sufficiency is tested via top-k reconstruction and whose necessity via ablation, with a third scaling intervention (perturbation coefficient α) probing directional sensitivity of individual subspaces. The entire pipeline is training-free, completing pathway extraction and local interpretation in under 2 minutes with <1 GB memory per projection matrix, compared to ~30 minutes and 16 GB for SAE/TransCoder training on 60M tokens.

## Results

On the Generalization Dataset across the GPT-2 family, reconstructing middle-to-late MLP layers using only the top-20 SCA-identified subspaces achieves 97–99% top-1 accuracy on target token prediction, with mean probability saturating near 1.0. Ablating these same subspaces causes a catastrophic drop in top-1 accuracy and an exponential increase in mean rank, confirming causal necessity. Computationally, NaNA completes full-subspace interpretation of a single projection matrix in under 2 minutes with <1 GB GPU memory, compared to ~30 minutes and ~16 GB for both SAEs and TransCoders, and scales linearly with model size (8.59 min for GPT2-XL pathway extraction vs. >24 hours for training-based baselines).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NaNA (SCA) consistently outperforms both SAE and TransCoder on reconstruction fidelity: while SAE and TransCoder features saturate in performance beyond k > 60 active components, SCA continues to yield monotonic gains in target-token rank and probability, attributed to its explicit filtering for 'causal bridges' that capture low-magnitude but directionally significant subspaces. On the efficiency axis, NaNA is orders of magnitude faster (<2 min vs. ~30 min per projection matrix) and uses a fraction of the memory (<1 GB vs. ~16 GB), while requiring no training data or hyperparameter tuning. For full-model pathway extraction on GPT2-XL, NaNA completes in 8.59 minutes with linear scaling, whereas pre-computing all SAE or TransCoder modules exceeds 24 hours. Random DEU subspaces and raw MLP neurons serve as negative controls and show high volatility and poor target-specific recovery, confirming that the SCA-identified subspaces are not merely a basis artifact but causally necessary functional modules.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the static spectral observations of Beren & Black (2022) — that singular vectors of MLP weights carry semantic structure — into a dynamic causal framework by formalizing rank-1 SVD terms as Detector-Effector Units and introducing Subspace Contribution Analysis for targeted attribution. It explicitly departs from the dominant training-based interpretability paradigm (SAEs, TransCoders) by arguing that the native weight decomposition already encodes the functional pathways those proxies approximate, eliminating both approximation bias and the substantial training overhead. The conceptual bridge to Geva et al.'s Key-Value Memory view of MLPs and the Linear Representation Hypothesis provides the theoretical grounding for why orthogonal spectral subspaces should correspond to decoupled input-output channels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a training-free SVD-based method that recovers 97–99% of target prediction fidelity with ~20 subspaces in under 2 minutes and <1 GB memory, versus ~30 minutes and 16 GB for SAE/TransCoder training — a compelling efficiency argument for rapid interpretability at scale. However, validation is confined to the GPT-2 family (≤774M params) and MLP layers only, leaving open whether the sparsity and interpretability properties persist in modern LLMs with attention-centric routing, MoE architectures, or multi-step reasoning tasks. The reliance on an external LLM for automated semantic labeling of subspaces also introduces a reproducibility and bias concern that the authors acknowledge but do not fully resolve.
