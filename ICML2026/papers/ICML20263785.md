## Problem

Training-free conditional sampling from flow matching models requires generating samples from p_data(·|y) using only a pretrained unconditional velocity field and an off-the-shelf likelihood, without retraining. Naive importance sampling for this task suffers from severe weight degeneracy in high-dimensional image spaces, and the deterministic ODE dynamics of flow matching prevent resampled particles from exploring diverse trajectories, making standard SMC resampling inapplicable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263785/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained flow matching velocity field v_θ* (trained on the unconditional data distribution), a time-independent likelihood/classifier model p̃(y|·) (e.g., a CNN classifier for class labels or CLIP for text prompts), and a conditioning value y. It outputs K weighted image samples {(x_1^(k), w̄_1^(k))} whose weighted empirical distribution converges to p_data(·|y) as K→∞. Experiments use MNIST (28×28), CIFAR-10 (32×32×3), and CelebA-HQ (256×256×3); the particle count K is small (16–25) per batch, with a Nested TFTF variant scaling to thousands of samples for distributional evaluation.

## Method

TFTF employs an ODE-SDE-ODE strategy that selectively introduces stochasticity only at intermediate generation stages to enable SMC resampling. In the early phase [0, t_{n1}], the deterministic ODE with v_θ* is solved; in the middle phase [t_{n1}, t_{n2}], the flow is converted to a stochastic SDE (derived via the Fokker-Planck-Kolmogorov equation with a tunable noise parameter α(t)) whose proposal kernel uses a conditional velocity field v_c combining v_θ* with the likelihood gradient, while the target distribution scores both trajectory naturalness and look-ahead condition alignment; in the late phase [t_{n2}, 1], the deterministic ODE is resumed. At each intermediate step, particles are resampled by weight to prune unpromising trajectories and enrich the set with high-quality candidates, and a final incremental weight p̃(y|x_1)/p̃(y|x̂_1(x_{t_{n2}})) corrects for the proposal-target mismatch. The key theoretical contribution is proving asymptotic exactness (Proposition 3.3) under boundedness conditions, and the stochastic flow construction (Proposition 3.2) shows that Anderson's reverse-time SDE is a special case (α(t)=1/t) of the proposed family.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 class-conditional sampling (K=16, M=1000 via Nested TFTF), TFTF achieves 92.82% external classifier accuracy (ACC_E), Inception Score 8.88, and intra-class W2 distance 32.61, substantially outperforming FMPS (31.81% ACC_E, IS 7.22, W2 99.59) and FlowChef (50.13% ACC_E, IS 7.54, W2 63.69). On MNIST with K=16, TFTF reaches 97.12% ACC_E with ESS 15.99/16, versus 39.24% for FMPS and 23.57% for FlowChef. On CelebA-HQ text-to-image generation (K=25), TFTF attains 80.99% average all-attributes accuracy (ACC_E) with ESS 21.77/25, compared to 68.69% (FMPS) and 41.50% (FlowChef), while maintaining a comparable CLIP similarity score (0.295 vs. 0.307/0.305).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TFTF decisively outperforms both FMPS (Song et al., 2025) and FlowChef (Patel et al., 2025) on external classifier accuracy (ACC_E) across all three datasets: on CIFAR-10, TFTF achieves 92.82% vs. 31.81% (FMPS) and 50.13% (FlowChef); on MNIST, 97.12% vs. 39.24% and 23.57%; on CelebA-HQ, 80.99% vs. 68.69% and 41.50%. The large ACC_E gaps indicate that baselines exploit the guidance classifier without producing truly class-consistent samples, whereas TFTF's importance-weighted SMC framework corrects for this bias. On CIFAR-10, FlowChef achieves a higher likelihood-specifying accuracy (98.04% vs. 92.56%), but TFTF dominates on IS (8.88 vs. 7.54) and W2 distance (32.61 vs. 63.69), reflecting superior sample diversity and distributional fidelity. D-Flow (Ben-Hamu et al., 2024) is compared only qualitatively on the toy example, where its optimization-based approach introduces visible bias in the recovered conditional distribution. Against TDS (Wu et al., 2023), a diffusion-based SMC sampler, TFTF achieves higher ACC_E and greater intra-batch diversity (mean pairwise ℓ2 distance) at matched particle counts, attributed to the ODE-SDE-ODE strategy that concentrates stochasticity where it is most beneficial.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of flow matching generative models, importance sampling, and sequential Monte Carlo. It departs from existing training-free conditional generation methods for flow matching (D-Flow, FlowChef, FMPS) that either optimize through the ODE solver or use surrogate velocity fields, both of which introduce bias between the sampling and target distributions. The key algorithmic departure is the ODE-SDE-ODE strategy: by converting the deterministic flow matching ODE into a family of SDEs via the Fokker-Planck-Kolmogorov equation at intermediate stages, the authors enable SMC resampling that is otherwise impossible in a fully deterministic setting. This also distinguishes the work from prior SMC-based conditional samplers for score-based diffusion models (Trippe et al., 2022; Wu et al., 2023), which apply SDE simulation with resampling throughout the entire generation process and are constrained by the pretrained model's noise schedule, whereas TFTF concentrates resampling in a tunable intermediate window and offers a free stochasticity parameter α(t).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first training-free conditional sampling method for flow matching models with a formal asymptotic accuracy guarantee, addressing a gap that optimization-based and surrogate-velocity approaches cannot fill. The ODE-SDE-ODE framework is a reusable technique for injecting controlled stochasticity into deterministic generative flows, potentially applicable beyond conditional sampling. Key open questions include: the resampling interval [t_{n1}, t_{n2}] remains a manually tuned hyperparameter; SMC resampling introduces inter-particle correlations (path degeneracy) that limit intra-batch diversity; and the method is restricted to Euclidean-space image generation, with extension to Riemannian manifolds (e.g., protein structure generation) left for future work.
