## Problem

Prior strictly batched linear contextual bandit algorithms achieve the minimal O(log log T) parameter-update complexity but rely on computationally prohibitive primitives—repeated G-optimal design computations, exponential-in-dimension discretization, or heavy oracle calls—making them impractical to deploy. The paper asks whether minimax-optimal regret in both small-K and large-K regimes can be attained with O(log log T) reward-dependent updates while eliminating these costly subroutines and exploiting the fact that contexts are observed online within each interval.

## Input / Output

At each round the algorithm receives a set of K contextual feature vectors in R^d (drawn i.i.d. across rounds from an unknown distribution) and must select one arm; rewards are revealed only at interval boundaries under a static grid schedule of O(log log T) intervals. Between boundaries, the algorithm maintains reward-free state (inverse Gram matrix updated via Sherman-Morrison, nested elimination candidate sets) that evolves with realized contexts and actions, but the reward-dependent parameter estimate θ̂ is recomputed only at interval endpoints. The output is a sequence of arm selections whose worst-case cumulative regret is O(√(dT)(√(log KT) ∧ √(d+log T))√(log d log log T)), matching minimax lower bounds up to polylog factors.

## Method

BLCE-G partitions each interval into three phases—near G-optimal design sampling (computed in O(Kd³) via Khachiyan's barycentric coordinate descent with ε=1), most-informative-direction exploration (maximizing ‖x‖_{H^{-1}}), and greedy exploitation—combined with nested arm elimination using confidence thresholds derived from concentration inequalities. BLCE removes the G-optimal design phase entirely, reallocating those rounds to uncertainty-driven exploration, and still achieves the same minimax-optimal regret with a lower total time complexity of O(Kd²T log log T). The key architectural insight is decoupling reward-dependent parameter retraining (restricted to O(log log T) interval boundaries) from reward-free within-interval state updates (Gram matrix, elimination sets), which permits context-adaptive action selection without the pre-planned exploration that strict batching forces. A generalized-linear extension (BGLE) replaces ridge regression with MLE under a known link function, uses curvature-weighted Gram matrices, and retains the same O(log log T) update schedule with near-optimal regret.

## Results

BLCE-G achieves the tightest known minimax-optimal regret bound Õ(√(dT log K) ∧ d√T) for linear contextual bandits with only O(log log T) parameter updates, simultaneously matching lower bounds in both the small-K (K ≤ O(e^d)) and large-K (K ≥ Ω(e^d)) regimes. BLCE preserves the same minimax-optimal regret while removing the G-optimal design step, achieving the lowest time complexity O(Kd²T log log T) among all optimal algorithms. In runtime experiments over T=10,000 rounds, BLCE completes in 5.91 s for (K,d)=(1000,5) versus 290.87 s for BatchLinUCB-DG and 166.17 s for BatchLearning, while both proposed methods consistently achieve the lowest cumulative regret across all four (K,d) configurations tested.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the four baselines, BLCE-G and BLCE dominate on both regret tightness and practical runtime among all minimax-optimal methods. Compared to BatchLinUCB-DG (Ruan et al., 2021), which requires O(Kd⁴T) time and is not context-adaptive, BLCE-G achieves a tighter regret bound (log d vs. log(dKT) factor) and runs ~12× faster (23.40 s vs. 290.87 s at (1000,5)); BLCE is ~50× faster (5.91 s). Against SoftBatch (Hanna et al., 2023b), which suffers exponential runtime Ω(T^d) due to discretization, both proposed methods are orders of magnitude faster while also being context-adaptive. Compared to BatchLearning (Zhang et al., 2025), which incurs a high-order first-batch cost of Õ(Kd^{7/2}√T), BLCE-G and BLCE avoid this bottleneck entirely, running in 23.40 s and 5.91 s respectively versus 166.17 s at (1000,5). The only method faster in raw runtime is the suboptimal RS-OFUL (0.85 s), which requires O(d log T) parameter updates and a suboptimal O(d√T log T) regret bound, making it incomparable in the optimal-algorithm class.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the batched linear bandit framework of Yu & Oh (2025) from the non-contextual to the linear contextual setting, and builds on the G-optimal design machinery of Ruan et al. (2021) and the OFUL confidence-ellipsoid structure of Abbasi-Yadkori et al. (2011). Its key conceptual departure is a redefinition: prior 'strictly batched' methods (Ruan et al. 2021; Hanna et al. 2023a,b; Zhang et al. 2025) conflate batched reward feedback with a prohibition on within-interval context adaptivity, forcing expensive pre-planned exploration (G-optimal design, exponential discretization). By separating these two axes and allowing reward-free within-interval state updates, the authors show that the O(log log T) parameter-update regime can be achieved without G-optimal design (BLCE) or with only a near-G-optimal relaxation (BLCE-G), while matching minimax lower bounds in both small-K and large-K regimes simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: BLCE reduces the dominant computational bottleneck from O(Kd⁴T log T) or exponential in d to O(Kd²T log log T), making O(log log T)-update algorithms deployable in realistic pipelines where reward-driven retraining is expensive but context processing is cheap. The explicit taxonomy (fully sequential vs. strictly batched vs. rare parameter updates) clarifies a source of confusion in the literature and enables fairer comparisons. Open questions include whether the i.i.d. context assumption (relaxed to batch-wise conditional independence in Remark 1) can be further weakened, whether adaptive schedules could tighten the log factors, and whether the near-optimal (but not fully minimax-optimal) generalized-linear extension (BGLE) can be closed to full optimality.
