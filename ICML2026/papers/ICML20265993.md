## Problem

Representation encoders like DINOv2 produce semantically rich features ideal for understanding, but directly using them as generative latents for diffusion models fails in open-world text-to-image and editing tasks. The paper identifies two root causes: (1) the unconstrained high-dimensional feature space lacks compact regularization, causing diffusion models to produce off-manifold latents that the pixel decoder cannot faithfully decode, and (2) the encoder's purely discriminative training objective yields weak pixel-level reconstruction, preventing the generator from learning accurate fine-grained geometry and texture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265993/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For text-to-image generation, the model takes a text prompt and produces a 256×256 RGB image; for instruction-based editing, it takes a reference image plus a text instruction and outputs an edited image. The generative latent space is a compact 96-channel tensor with 16× spatial downsampling (16×16 tokens for 256×256 images), derived from DINOv2-B's 768-dimensional features via a semantic autoencoder. All generation experiments are conducted at 256×256 resolution using a Transfusion-style architecture with a Qwen-0.5B backbone and a wide DDT head.

## Method

PS-VAE is trained in two stages. First (S-VAE), the DINOv2-B encoder is frozen and a lightweight semantic VAE (3 Transformer blocks + MLP projection) compresses the 768-dim features into a 96-channel KL-regularized latent, trained with a semantic reconstruction loss (ℓ2 + cosine similarity + pairwise matrix similarity) to preserve feature structure while eliminating off-manifold outliers. Second (PS-VAE), the encoder is unfrozen and jointly optimized with a pixel decoder under a combined objective: pixel reconstruction loss (ℓ1 + VGG perceptual + adaptive adversarial) backpropagates into the encoder to capture fine-grained details, while a semantic reconstruction loss against the original frozen DINOv2 features (weighted λ_S=0.1) prevents semantic drift, and KL regularization (β=1e-6) maintains latent compactness. The paper provides a theoretical analysis showing that diffusion in an h-dimensional ambient space containing an l-dimensional manifold (h>l) forces the network to learn both the projection/embedding operations and an identity mapping for orthogonal noise, amplifying off-manifold drift—validated by a toy experiment embedding a 2D 'PS' distribution into 8D space.

## Results

PS-VAE-96c achieves state-of-the-art reconstruction among stride-16 VAEs with rFID 0.203, PSNR 28.79, and SSIM 0.817, substantially improving over MAR-VAE (rFID 0.534, PSNR 26.18, SSIM 0.715). On generation, PS-VAE-96c leads GenEval (76.56) while PS-VAE-32c leads DPG-Bench (84.25) and Editing Reward (0.274). Both PS-VAE variants significantly outperform RAE across all generation and editing benchmarks, with the editing reward nearly quadrupling from RAE's 0.059 to PS-VAE-32c's 0.274. PS-VAE also converges faster than RAE and other VAE baselines, and scales better with larger diffusion backbones (Qwen-1.5B vs. 0.5B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MAR-VAE (the strongest pixel-reconstruction VAE baseline), PS-VAE-96c improves reconstruction substantially (rFID 0.534→0.203, PSNR 26.18→28.79, SSIM 0.715→0.817) while also winning on GenEval (75.75→76.56) and Editing Reward (0.056→0.222); PS-VAE-32c further leads on DPG-Bench (83.19→84.25) and Editing Reward (0.056→0.274). Against RAE (the direct representation-encoder baseline), both PS-VAE variants achieve large gains: GenEval improves from 71.27 to 76.22/76.56, DPG-Bench from 81.72 to 84.25/83.62, and Editing Reward from 0.059 to 0.274/0.222, confirming that compact KL regularization and pixel reconstruction are both necessary. Against VAVAE, PS-VAE-32c wins on DPG-Bench (82.45→84.25) and Editing Reward (0.227→0.274), while PS-VAE-96c wins on GenEval (76.16→76.56) and reconstruction metrics. Against Flux-VAE, PS-VAE-96c trades a small reconstruction gap (rFID 0.203 vs. 0.175) for dramatically better generation (GenEval 68.04→76.56) and editing (−0.271→0.222), demonstrating that reconstruction quality alone does not determine generative performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends RAE (Zheng et al., 2025), which first demonstrated that representation encoder features (e.g., DINOv2) can serve as generative latents for class-conditional ImageNet synthesis. The key departure is identifying two fundamental failures of RAE in open-world settings—off-manifold latent generation due to unconstrained high-dimensional feature spaces, and weak pixel-level reconstruction—and addressing them through a two-stage framework: S-VAE (compact KL-regularized semantic autoencoder) followed by PS-VAE (joint pixel-semantic fine-tuning of the encoder). The semantic reconstruction loss design follows VAVAE (Yao et al., 2025), and the KL-regularized latent formulation and pixel decoder architecture inherit from LDM (Rombach et al., 2022). For the generation backbone, the paper adopts the Transfusion deep-fusion architecture with a wide DDT head, positioning itself within the broader trend of unifying understanding and generation in a single encoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled diagnosis (supported by both a theoretical velocity-estimator decomposition and a toy 2D-in-8D experiment) of why naive representation-encoder generation fails, and a practical recipe—compact 96-channel KL-regularized latents with joint pixel-semantic training—that makes pretrained encoders viable for high-fidelity T2I and instruction-based editing. The substantial editing reward improvement (0.06→0.22) and the finding that higher-channel PS-VAE scales better with larger diffusion backbones suggest a clear path toward unified understanding-generation encoders. Key limitations include training at only 256×256 resolution on ImageNet-1K for reconstruction, and the understanding-side evaluation is limited to two benchmarks (MMEP, MMBench) with marginal drops; scaling to higher resolutions, broader reconstruction data, and joint LLM fine-tuning remain open.
