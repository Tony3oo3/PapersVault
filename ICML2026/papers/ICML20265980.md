## Problem

Existing decentralized optimization algorithms are limited to single-level or bilevel (K=2) problems, leaving the general stochastic K-level optimization (K>2) over networks unsolved. The sole prior multi-level method (Yang et al., 2023) requires communicating high-dimensional Jacobian and Hessian matrices at every level and relies on assumptions (uniformly bounded gradients plus strong convexity) that are mutually contradictory. This paper addresses both the computation overhead of Hessian-inverse-vector products at each level and the communication overhead of recursive dependence across workers.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes N workers, each holding local loss functions f_k^(n) for K+1 nested levels, a symmetric doubly-stochastic adjacency matrix W defining the gossip topology, and per-worker stochastic samples. It outputs the upper-level variable x (and implicitly the nested lower-level solutions y_k^*(x)) that is an epsilon-stationary point of the composed objective F_{K+1}(x). All inter-worker communication is compressed via a Top-K or sign operator with error-feedback, so only compressed differences (not full matrices) are exchanged per iteration, and the algorithm runs in a single loop without nested inner iterations for Hessian inversion.

## Method

The key mechanism is a recursive Hessian-inverse-vector product z_k^*(x) in R^{d_k x 1} computed from level K down to level 1, which replaces the high-dimensional Hessian-inverse-Jacobian matrix (d_{k-1} x d_k) with a vector, drastically reducing both local computation and per-iteration communication. The single-loop algorithm CE-DSMGD-VR simultaneously updates all level variables (x, y_k, z_k) using SPIDER-style variance-reduced gradient estimators, gradient tracking for consensus over the gossip graph, and error-feedback compression on all communicated quantities. The theoretical analysis constructs a novel potential function whose coefficients must satisfy recursive inequalities across levels to handle the triple dependence (levels, workers, iterations) in the error bounds, yielding a convergence rate of O(1/(N*epsilon^3*(1-lambda)^4*delta^2)) which is the first rigorous guarantee for decentralized multi-level optimization under mild assumptions.

## Results

The paper reports results primarily through convergence plots (upper-level loss vs. iterations and vs. communicated MB) rather than tabulated numerical values. On the a9a, w8a, and ijcnn1 LIBSVM datasets with 8 workers on a ring graph (K=2, Top-20% compression, batch size 100), CE-DSMGD-VR converges substantially faster than Gossip-DSMO in terms of iterations, while achieving nearly identical convergence to its full-precision variant DSMGD-VR. In communication cost, CE-DSMGD-VR requires significantly fewer communicated megabytes than both Gossip-DSMO and DSMGD-VR, and outperforms all six bilevel baselines (DSBO, gossip-DSBO, MA-DSBO, MDBO, VRDBO, DSVRBGD-S/A) in communication efficiency without degrading convergence. The theoretical iteration complexity is O(1/(Nε³(1−λ)⁴δ²)), matching state-of-the-art decentralized bilevel results for the first time in the multi-level (K>1) setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the only existing multi-level baseline, Gossip-DSMO (Yang et al., 2023), CE-DSMGD-VR converges much faster in iterations and requires far fewer communicated MB to reach the same upper-level loss, confirming both the benefit of the recursive Hessian-inverse-vector product and the variance-reduced estimator. Compared to its own ablation DSMGD-VR (same algorithm without compression), CE-DSMGD-VR achieves virtually identical convergence in iterations while reducing communication cost substantially, validating that Top-20% error-feedback compression does not impair performance. In the bilevel comparison (logistic regression, ε=0.05), CE-DSMGD-VR achieves the lowest communication cost among all seven baselines—DSBO (Chen et al., 2024), gossip-DSBO (Yang et al., 2022), MA-DSBO (Chen et al., 2023), MDBO and VRDBO (Gao et al., 2023), and DSVRBGD-S/A (Zhang et al., 2026b)—while maintaining convergence comparable to the full-precision methods. The paper also demonstrates robustness: performance is stable across γ∈{0.1,…,0.9}, αη²∈{0.1,…,0.9}, β and ρ values, compression ratios (Top-5%, Top-10%, Top-20%), and communication topologies (ring, torus, Erdős–Rényi random graph with p=0.4). With K=3 and 16 workers under heterogeneous data (positive-class ratios from 0.1 to 0.45), CE-DSMGD-VR still outperforms baselines and matches its full-precision counterpart.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the well-studied line of decentralized bilevel optimization (DSBO) algorithms—such as DSVRBGD (Zhang et al., 2026b), SPARKLE (Zhu et al., 2024), and Gossip-DSBO (Yang et al., 2022)—to the general K-level case (K>2), where the only prior method was Gossip-DSMO (Yang et al., 2023), which relied on contradictory assumptions and communicated full Jacobian/Hessian matrices. The key algorithmic departure is the recursive Hessian-inverse-vector product, which replaces the high-dimensional Hessian-inverse-Jacobian product used in bilevel methods, combined with error-feedback compression (previously unexplored in any bilevel or multi-level decentralized setting) and recursive variance reduction. The theoretical analysis introduces a novel potential function with level-dependent coefficients to handle the triple-recursive dependence across levels, workers, and iterations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first decentralized multi-level optimization algorithm with rigorous convergence guarantees under mild assumptions, filling a gap where the sole prior method (Yang et al., 2023) had contradictory assumptions and no clean rate. The convergence rate O(1/(Nε³(1-λ)⁴δ²)) matches state-of-the-art decentralized SBO rates, suggesting the multi-level structure does not fundamentally worsen complexity when handled correctly. The novel reformulation of non-convex hyperparameter optimization as a multi-level problem with strongly-convex subproblems opens a practical pathway for applying multi-level methods to real ML tasks. Open questions include scalability to very large K, handling genuinely non-convex lower levels, and whether the recursive structure can be further simplified for near-linear communication complexity in K.
