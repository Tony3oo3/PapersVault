## Problem

FP8 (E4M3) training of transformers overflows when attention logits exceed the ±448 representable range, producing NaNs that corrupt training. The standard delayed-scaling approach derives scale factors from a history buffer of past activation maxima, which fails during transients (checkpoint loading, resumption, learning-rate spikes) because weights change faster than the buffer adapts. The alternative—current scaling—handles transients but requires materializing the full L×L attention matrix, making it incompatible with fused kernels like FlashAttention. No prior method was simultaneously transient-safe and fused-kernel-compatible.

## Input / Output

The method consumes only the current layer weights W^Q and W^K (plus a fixed calibration factor α and FP8 margin η_fp8=0.8) to produce a per-layer scale factor via the spectral norm of the interaction matrix W^Q W^{K^T}. It never observes activations, so it is fully compatible with FlashAttention and other fused kernels that avoid materializing the score matrix. For grouped query attention (e.g., Mistral-7B's 4:1 ratio, Llama-2-70B's 8:1 ratio), an implicit formulation using REPEATBLOCKS and SUMGROUPS on small intermediate vectors avoids expanding the key matrix, saving 4–8× memory transactions. The scale factor is applied by dividing pre-softmax attention scores before FP8 quantization, and the calibrated α is frozen after an optional 100-step burn-in for steady-state fine-tuning.

## Method

The core mechanism is a predictive, weight-only calibration: the worst-case attention logit bound max|S_ij| ≤ ‖W^Q W^{K^T}‖_2 · d/√d_h is derived from the bilinear structure of attention and is strictly tighter than the naive submultiplicative bound ‖W^Q‖_2‖W^K‖_2 unless top singular vectors align. A rank-aware concentration inequality then exploits rank(M)=d_h≪d to show that the probability of exceeding a calibrated bound α·B_max decays as exp(−d²α²/(γd_h)) rather than exp(−dα²), yielding 8–28× tighter tails in modern architectures and a closed-form selection rule for α given a target overflow probability δ*. The spectral norm ‖W^Q W^{K^T}‖_2 is estimated via implicit power iteration (one matrix-vector product per forward pass in steady state, five at cold start) without ever forming the d×d interaction matrix, costing O(n_heads·d_h·d) per layer. An optional auto-α phase observes the 99.99th percentile of slack ratios during a short burn-in to tighten α for utilization (e.g., 0.03 → 4.6×10⁻⁴ on Llama-2-13B), after which scaling reverts to fully predictive mode.

## Results

On the MMLU STEM fine-tuning benchmark (Llama-2-13B, 10000 steps), auto-α geometry-aware scaling achieves 32.7% accuracy with zero overflows and 34.9% FP8 utilization, matching delayed scaling's 32.6% accuracy while eliminating all 219 overflow events. Conservative spectral scaling (α=0.03) also achieves zero overflows but degrades to 28.7% MMLU accuracy due to only 0.5% FP8 utilization. Across transient scenarios (checkpoint loading, resumption, LR spikes), geometry-aware scaling produces zero overflow layers on all four models (GPT-2 XL through Llama-2-70B), whereas delayed scaling overflows 100% of layers with maximum scaled logits exceeding 5000. Computational overhead is negligible: +1.0% (GPT-2 XL), +1.9% (Llama-2-13B), +4.3% (Llama-2-70B), and even −5.3% (Mistral-7B with GQA).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is delayed (history-based) scaling from Micikevicius et al. (2022), the standard FP8 training approach. On transient scenarios (checkpoint loading, resumption, 100× LR spikes), geometry-aware scaling wins decisively: zero overflow layers across all four models versus 100% overflow layers for delayed scaling, with maximum scaled logits of 1–196 versus 5600–9498. On downstream MMLU accuracy, auto-α geometry-aware scaling ties delayed scaling (32.7% vs. 32.6%, within stochastic variance on 295 training examples) while eliminating all 219 overflow events that delayed scaling produces. The conservative spectral variant (α=0.03) trades 4 percentage points of MMLU accuracy (28.7% vs. 32.6%) for guaranteed zero overflows, illustrating the safety-utilization tradeoff that auto-α resolves. On computational cost, geometry-aware scaling adds 1–4% forward-pass overhead on MHA models but is actually 5.3% faster than delayed scaling on Mistral-7B due to favorable memory access patterns in the implicit GQA formulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the activation-observation paradigm of FP8 scaling (delayed scaling from Micikevicius et al., 2022; current scaling) by predicting logit bounds directly from the spectral norm of the query-key interaction matrix W^Q W^{K⊤}, a quantity previously studied for attention entropy collapse (Zhai et al., 2023) but not for numerical overflow. The rank-aware concentration inequality (Proposition 3.4) is a new theoretical tool that exploits the low-rank structure of the interaction matrix to tighten tail bounds by a factor of d/(γ·d_h) over standard Lévy-type arguments. Practically, the implicit GQA power iteration and the transient-safe/fused-kernel-compatible design address a gap the authors identify as unsolved: no prior method simultaneously handles checkpoint-loading transients and remains compatible with FlashAttention-style O(L) memory kernels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method provides a principled, weight-only calibration for FP8 attention that eliminates the history-staleness failure mode without requiring activation observation, making it drop-in compatible with fused attention kernels used in production training. Key limitations include the isotropic-direction assumption (measured anisotropy ratios of 1.4–3.7× on Mistral-7B), the 65× conservatism gap between theoretical α_min and empirically needed α (requiring the auto-α burn-in for practical utilization), and the fact that auto-α itself requires a short FlashAttention-incompatible phase. Open questions include a formal proof of the RoPE alignment condition (currently empirical), extension to non-E4M3 formats, and whether the isotropic model can be replaced by a layer-specific anisotropic correction that tightens the bound without sacrificing the distribution-free guarantee.
