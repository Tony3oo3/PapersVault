## Problem

Previous scoring rules for interval-censored data lacked formal proofs of properness, and no calibration metric existed for the broader class of interval-censored data where event times are only partially observable. Additionally, the precise relationship between the independent monitoring and non-informative censoring assumptions—two foundational conditions in the literature—had not been rigorously characterized, particularly for Case-K (K ≥ 2) data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265350/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes tabular covariate vectors x alongside observed censored intervals (L, R) where the true event time Y satisfies L < Y ≤ R (or L = R for exact observations in PIC data). The neural network outputs a discrete probability mass over a grid of time points via a Softmax layer, from which the conditional CDF F(y|x) is constructed by cumulative summation. The calibration metric IC-Cal additionally produces a scalar L^p deviation score quantifying how well predicted probabilities align with empirical frequencies, computed exactly (not via binning) because the underlying piecewise-linear function admits closed-form integration.

## Method

The paper introduces S_IC-Log, a scoring rule that assigns -log(F̂(r) - F̂(l)) for interval observations and -log f̂(l) for exact observations, and proves it is strictly Y-proper under independent monitoring and strictly LR-proper under the weaker constant-sum assumption—extending the theoretical guarantees beyond what was available for survival analysis (Rindt et al., 2022). For calibration, the authors generalize the Probability Integral Transform via a bivariate piecewise-linear function π(u, α, β) that linearly interpolates the uncertainty zone between F̂(L|x) and F̂(R|x), yielding the IC-Cal metric with a uniform concentration bound via Rademacher complexity. Practically, a three-layer MLP (32 hidden units, ReLU, Dropout 0.5, Softmax output) is trained by empirical risk minimization of S_IC-Log using the Adam optimizer, and evaluated against icenReg (parametric and semiparametric), ICcforest, and a proportional-hazard neural network baseline on five real-world interval-censored datasets.

## Results

The paper evaluates NN-IC-Log (a 3-layer MLP trained with the S_IC-Log scoring rule) against four baselines on five real-world interval-censored datasets using five-fold cross-validation. Performance is measured by the S_IC-Log score (lower is better) and the proposed empirical calibration error γ̃_IC² (lower is better). NN-IC-Log achieves competitive or superior scoring rule performance across all datasets, outperforming ICcforest on breastCancer and mastitis, and outperforming the parametric ic_par on aidsCohort (Z), hiv, mastitis, and yoghurt. On calibration, NN-IC-Log shows significantly lower miscalibration than ic_par and NN-IC, while the semiparametric ic_sp remains the strongest baseline overall.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ICcforest (Yao et al., 2019), NN-IC-Log wins on breastCancer and mastitis in scoring rule performance but is competitive elsewhere. Against the parametric ic_par (icenReg), NN-IC-Log clearly outperforms on aidsCohort (Z), hiv, mastitis, and yoghurt, and ic_par shows significant miscalibration on those same datasets. Against the semiparametric ic_sp (icenReg), NN-IC-Log is comparable but ic_sp remains slightly stronger, particularly on aidsCohort (Z). Against NN-IC (Sun & Ding, 2023, a neural network with proportional hazard assumption), NN-IC-Log wins on aidsCohort (Y) and hiv in both scoring rule and calibration, with NN-IC showing notable miscalibration on those datasets. The nonparametric estimators (Turnbull 1976; Li et al. 1997) serve as calibration reference points (calibrated by design) and confirm that NN-IC-Log's calibration error is closer to zero than the parametric and proportional-hazard baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the proper scoring rule framework from Rindt et al. (2022) for right-censored survival data to the broader interval-censored setting, providing the first formal proofs of strict properness for the IC-Log score (previously used without proof by Avati et al., 2020). It generalizes the PIT-based calibration metric of Kuleshov et al. (2018) and Park et al. (2025) to handle partially observed event times via a piecewise-linear interpolation. A key theoretical departure is establishing strict properness under the weaker constant-sum assumption rather than the restrictive independent monitoring assumption required by prior survival analysis frameworks, and clarifying that independent monitoring and non-informative censoring are equivalent only for Case-1 data but diverge for Case-K (K≥2).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The formal proof of strict properness under the constant-sum assumption removes a key theoretical limitation that previously constrained the use of IC-Log as a training objective, and the IC-Cal metric fills a genuine gap in model evaluation for interval-censored data. The distinction between strict Y-properness and strict LR-properness, with a concrete counter-example, sharpens the theoretical understanding of what can be identified from censored observations. However, the empirical evaluation is limited to five small datasets (N=93–400) with a modest MLP, and the open question of extending IC-Cal to the full constant-sum assumption (the most general identifiability condition) remains unresolved, limiting the metric's universal applicability.
