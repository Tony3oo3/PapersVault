## Problem

All existing parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods follow a static allocation paradigm: the locations and structures of trainable parameters are determined once before training and remain fixed throughout. This ignores the fact that a model's optimization priorities and knowledge bottlenecks evolve dynamically during fine-tuning, meaning a module critical early in training may become irrelevant later while new bottlenecks emerge. No prior method reconfigures the trainable structure in response to the model's changing learning state.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260397/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

DAF takes standard RGB images as input (e.g., 224×224 for ViT-B/16 classification, multi-scale inputs for Swin-based detection/segmentation) and produces the same outputs as the underlying backbone: class labels for classification, bounding boxes and instance masks for COCO detection, and pixel-level masks for ADE20K segmentation. A key design constraint is zero inference overhead: all learned LoRA modules are merged into the backbone weights (W_final = W_0 + BA), so the deployed model is architecturally identical to the original ViT with no additional GFLOPs or memory cost.

## Method

DAF introduces a periodic perceive-decide-execute cycle (every E_interval epochs) that dynamically reconfigures which parameters are trainable. In the Perceive stage, a context-aware decoupled sensitivity analysis temporarily freezes all active LoRA modules (keeping them in the forward pass for correct context) and computes backbone-only gradients, using the Taylor-based score |g·w| as a first-order proxy for adaptation potential. The Decide stage applies a budget-based elite selection with category-wise Top-K ranking over Matrix, Vector, and Special parameters under a fixed budget ratio τ, which the paper shows is equivalent to maximizing predicted first-order loss decrease under separable budget constraints. The Execute stage implements a Rebuild-and-Refocus strategy: a new model instance is constructed containing LoRA modules for the union of previous and current active sets, weights are migrated from the old model (preserving learned knowledge in frozen modules), newly selected modules are reinitialized, and optimizer states are reset (partial warm restart) to prevent stale momentum from the old topology from misleading the new optimization landscape.

## Results

On the FGVC benchmark (ViT-B/16, ImageNet-21k), DAF achieves 90.2% mean Top-1 accuracy while tuning only 0.21% of parameters, surpassing Full Fine-tuning (88.5%) and the strongest static baselines Adapter+ (90.1%) and GPS (90.0%). On VTAB-1k, DAF reaches 76.4% mean accuracy (0.22% tuned params), outperforming all static PEFT methods including Res-Tuning (76.2%), GPS (76.2%), and Adapter+ (76.3%). On dense prediction tasks, DAF attains 53.5 AP^box / 46.1 AP^mask on COCO (2.1M params) and 52.0 mIoU on ADE20K (3.7M params), exceeding LoRA by 3.1 AP^box and 1.7 mIoU respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAF outperforms all 22 static PEFT baselines on both FGVC and VTAB-1k. Against the closest competitors, DAF edges out Adapter+ (90.2% vs 90.1% FGVC; 76.4% vs 76.3% VTAB-1k) and GPS (90.2% vs 90.0%; 76.4% vs 76.2%), while using roughly half the trainable parameters (0.21% vs 0.22–0.77%). The gap widens substantially against classic methods: DAF surpasses LoRA-8 by 4.2 points on FGVC and 1.5 on VTAB-1k, and outperforms VPT-Dep by 6.4 and 4.4 points respectively. On COCO object detection, DAF achieves 53.5 AP^box with 2.1M params, beating LoRA (50.4) by 3.1 AP and Mona (53.4) by 0.1 AP with fewer parameters. On ADE20K segmentation, DAF reaches 52.0 mIoU, exceeding LoRA (50.3) by 1.7 points and LoRand++ (51.9) by 0.1 points. The Static DAF ablation (75.8% vs 76.4%) confirms that the dynamic reconfiguration mechanism itself contributes a consistent 0.6-point gain over any fixed allocation strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

DAF builds directly on LoRA as its underlying parameter-efficient tuning mechanism but fundamentally departs from the static allocation paradigm shared by all prior PEFT methods (Adapter, LoRA, VPT, SPT, GPS, AdaLoRA). While one-shot sensitivity methods like SPT and GPS select parameters once before training, and AdaLoRA adaptively prunes rank within a fixed structure, DAF introduces a periodic perceive-decide-execute cycle that reconfigures the entire trainable topology during training. The Rebuild-and-Refocus strategy, which freezes stabilized modules while reallocating the full parameter budget to newly identified bottlenecks, has no direct precedent in the PEFT literature, drawing instead on biological synaptic plasticity as conceptual motivation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work challenges a foundational assumption in PEFT—that the fine-tuning structure should be fixed before training begins—and provides empirical evidence that dynamic reallocation yields consistent (if modest) gains across classification, detection, and segmentation tasks. The zero-inference-overhead property (via LoRA merging) makes the approach deployment-friendly, and generalization to Swin and ConvNeXt backbones suggests the paradigm is architecture-agnostic. Open questions include whether the dynamic reconfiguration principle extends to multimodal or language-model settings, how it interacts with other efficiency techniques (quantization, sparsity), and whether the ~8% training overhead can be further reduced. The modest absolute gains (0.1–1.5% over the strongest static baselines) suggest the static paradigm is not catastrophically suboptimal, but the consistent direction of improvement across diverse settings is encouraging.
