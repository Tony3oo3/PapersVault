## Problem

Existing diffusion-model-based image restoration methods operate in full-dimensional pixel space, incurring high computational cost, while latent-space alternatives require repeated VAE encoder-decoder inference that often makes them slower than their pixel-space counterparts. Dynamic resolution diffusion models, which reduce per-step cost by operating in lower-dimensional subspaces without auxiliary VAEs, had not been applied to general image restoration tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266102/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a degraded observation y = A(x_0*) + ν together with the known forward operator A (e.g., a random 70% mask, 4× bicubic downscaling, Gaussian/motion blur kernel, or a learned nonlinear blur) and produces a single full-resolution restored image (256×256×3 in main experiments, 768×768×3 in high-resolution experiments). During sampling, the internal state progressively transitions through three resolution levels (64×64×3 → 128×128×3 → 256×256×3), so the forward operator is applied to upsampled data estimates at each stage rather than to the raw low-resolution state.

## Method

The paper fine-tunes pretrained pixel-space DMs to serve as dynamic resolution priors across three spatial scales, then adapts DPS and DAPS into this framework as SubDPS and SubDAPS, where the measurement-consistency step operates on upsampled data estimates and the next noisy state is generated by re-noising the upsampled conditional estimate. SubDAPS++ further introduces three enhancements: (1) an adaptive noise-injection strategy governed by a threshold τ that switches from stochastic re-noising to deterministic ODE-based updates once the unconditional and conditional estimates converge, (2) a UniPC-style predictor-corrector step that refines the denoising trajectory without additional neural network evaluations, and (3) a Fletcher-Reeves conjugate gradient solver with a closed-form line search (derived via first-order Taylor expansion of the forward operator) that replaces Langevin dynamics for faster measurement-consistency optimization applicable to both linear and nonlinear tasks.

## Results

On FFHQ (256×256, σ=0.05, 100 NFEs), SubDAPS++ achieves PSNR of 32.21 (inpainting), 29.34 (4× SR), 29.17 (Gaussian deblurring), 29.36 (motion deblurring), 29.76 (nonlinear deblurring), and 25.52 (HDR recovery), with FID values of 43.15, 64.61, 60.38, 60.43, 59.58, and 49.05 respectively. Inference time is 7.4 s per image with peak memory of 3906 MB at full resolution, compared to 122.3 s / 8586 MB for DPS and 74.9 s / 4756 MB for ReSample. At 768×768 on DIV2K, SubDAPS++ reaches PSNR 23.85 and FID 45.81 in 11.06 s, outperforming FlowDPS (PSNR 22.96, 38.69 s) and P2L (PSNR 20.66, 152.73 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SubDAPS++ outperforms all pixel-space baselines (DPS, IIGDM, DiffPIR, RED-diff, MGPS, DAPS, AdaPS) on the majority of tasks and metrics; for example, on FFHQ inpainting it reaches PSNR 32.21 vs. 32.34 for AdaPS (the closest competitor) and 30.68 for DAPS, while on 4× super-resolution it leads with PSNR 29.34 vs. 28.88 for DAPS and 28.56 for LatentDAPS. Against latent-DM methods, SubDAPS++ is substantially faster (7.4 s vs. 74.9 s for ReSample and 53.7 s for LatentDAPS) while matching or exceeding their reconstruction quality, e.g., PSNR 29.34 vs. 28.56 on SR and 29.76 vs. 29.29 on nonlinear deblurring. Compared to SILO (a latent method that encodes the forward operator), SubDAPS++ achieves higher PSNR on all three tested tasks (e.g., 29.17 vs. 26.77 on Gaussian deblurring) while running ~16× faster (7.4 s vs. 122.7 s). At high resolution (768×768, DIV2K), SubDAPS++ surpasses both P2L and FlowDPS in PSNR, FID, and runtime. The full-dimensional variant SubDAPS-F++ yields nearly identical quality (differences ≤0.05 dB PSNR), confirming that the dynamic-resolution projection introduces negligible fidelity loss while saving ~19.5% runtime and ~29.5% peak memory.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate lines of work: dynamic resolution diffusion models (originally developed for efficient image generation, e.g., Subspace DMs by Jing et al., 2022) and diffusion-based inverse problem solving (DPS, DAPS). It is the first to adapt pixel-space restoration methods (DPS and DAPS) into the dynamic resolution framework, fine-tuning pretrained pixel-space DMs to serve as multi-resolution priors. The SubDAPS++ variant further draws on UniPC's predictor-corrector framework for a zero-cost corrector step and replaces Langevin dynamics with a conjugate gradient method featuring a closed-form line search derived via first-order Taylor linearization of the forward operator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in eliminating the repeated encoder-decoder overhead that makes latent DM-based restoration (ReSample, LatentDAPS) slower than their pixel-space counterparts, while still achieving the computational savings of operating in lower-dimensional subspaces during early timesteps. The ~2× speedup over DAPS and ~10× over ReSample with comparable or better reconstruction quality makes dynamic resolution a viable alternative to latent-space approaches for practitioners constrained by inference latency. Open questions include whether the fine-tuning step can be eliminated entirely (the paper shows off-the-shelf dynamic-resolution models work but with degraded FID), whether the approach extends to video restoration or flow-matching-based models, and whether the per-task threshold τ can be made adaptive rather than manually tuned.
