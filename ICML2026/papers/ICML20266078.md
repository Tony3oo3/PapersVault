## Problem

DPO-based preference alignment in LLM-based recommendation systems amplifies spurious correlations induced by environmental confounders (e.g., popularity bias, temporal drift, exposure bias), severely degrading out-of-distribution generalization. Existing debiasing methods target individual shift types in isolation, lacking a unified mechanism that handles multiple interrelated distributional shifts simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text prompt constructed by concatenating the user's interaction history, user profile, and item profile, and produces a recommended item via generative decoding over the LLM vocabulary. During training, it operates on preference triples (context, positive item, negative item) and additionally extracts hidden-state representations from the LLM to feed into a soft-clustering module that infers latent environment assignments without requiring explicit environment labels.

## Method

CausalDPO augments the standard DPO loss with a causal invariance regularization term. It first applies a linear causal feature extractor to LLM hidden states, then uses DBSCAN for initial hard clustering followed by a softmax distance-to-center mapping to obtain soft pseudo-environment assignments for each sample. The backdoor adjustment criterion is approximated by weighting environment-conditioned preference distributions by the inferred environment prior, and an MMD penalty is imposed across all pairs of pseudo-environments to enforce distributional consistency of the policy's output representations under the do(x) intervention. The final objective is L_DPO + λ·MMD, jointly optimized end-to-end so that the clustering module co-adapts with the policy. Theoretical analysis proves that this objective yields an invariant and sufficient preference policy and provides a generalization bound controlled by the MMD between training and test environment distributions.

## Results

CausalDPO achieves an average performance improvement of 17.17% across four evaluation metrics (HR@10, NDCG@10, HR@20, NDCG@20) over the strongest baseline across three OOD distribution shift settings. On Yelp2018 (popularity shift), it reaches HR@10=1.53% and NDCG@10=0.77%, a 22.29% average relative gain over the best competitor (SPRec). On ML-10M (temporal shift), it attains HR@10=0.61% and NDCG@10=0.35%, a 24.06% average gain. On Book-Crossing (exposure shift), it achieves HR@10=0.64% and NDCG@10=0.37%, with gains ranging from 8.47% to 23.33% over baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CausalDPO outperforms all 12 baselines across every dataset and metric. Among the strongest competitors, SPRec (self-play debiasing) is the closest on Yelp2018 (HR@10: 1.02 vs. 1.53) but falls further behind on ML-10M (HR@10: 0.55 vs. 0.61) and Book-Crossing (HR@10: 0.39 vs. 0.64). SDPO (softmax DPO with Plackett-Luce ranking) is the best DPO variant on ML-10M (HR@10: 0.55) yet still trails CausalDPO by 10.9%. BIGRec (bi-step grounding) leads among SFT-based methods on Book-Crossing (HR@10: 0.53) but is surpassed by CausalDPO by 20.8%. The traditional SASRec baseline is competitive on sparse Book-Crossing data (HR@10: 0.33) but is consistently outperformed by CausalDPO on all three datasets. In the DPO-variant comparison (Table 5), CausalDPO also beats Dr.DPO, CPO, SimPO, and vanilla DPO on both ML-10M and Book-Crossing OOD splits, with the largest margin over vanilla DPO on Book-Crossing (HR@10: 0.64 vs. 0.19, a 236.8% relative gain). Notably, integrating CausalDPO's causal invariance regularization into other DPO backbones (e.g., SimPO w/ Causal Reg.) yields consistent improvements, confirming the modularity of the proposed mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) for LLM-based sequential recommendation by injecting a causal invariance mechanism into the preference alignment objective. It departs from existing DPO variants for recommendation (DMPO, SDPO, RosePO, SPRec) that target specific biases (popularity, homogeneity, hallucination) by instead addressing the broader problem of environmental confounders through backdoor adjustment and MMD-based invariance regularization. The method draws on the invariant causal learning literature (MMD-based distributional consistency) and the causal inference literature (structural causal models, do-calculus), combining them with soft clustering (DBSCAN) to handle unobserved environment variables without explicit labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that DPO actively amplifies spurious correlations (rather than merely inheriting them from SFT) is practically important for anyone deploying LLM-based recommenders in production, where distribution shifts are inevitable. The modular design—where the causal invariance regularization can be plugged into other DPO variants (SimPO, CPO, Dr.DPO)—suggests broad applicability beyond this specific architecture. Open questions include: whether the DBSCAN-based pseudo-environment discovery reliably recovers true confounders at scale, how the method behaves when multiple confounders interact non-linearly, and whether the ~20% compute overhead remains acceptable for very large-scale deployment. The paper also leaves open whether the invariance constraint is too aggressive, potentially suppressing legitimate environment-dependent preference signals.
