## Problem

Medical VLMs for 3D CT analysis suffer from a dual misalignment: existing evaluation metrics (BLEU, ROUGE, RadGraph, RaTEScore) act as proxy signals that are systematically decoupled from clinical correctness ('Evaluation Hallucination'), and when RL algorithms optimize against these proxy rewards, policy gradients diverge from true diagnostic fidelity ('Mechanistic Divergence'), driving models to mimic report style rather than detect actual abnormalities. No prior framework provides both a clinically verifiable evaluation substrate and an RL objective that performs trajectory-level credit assignment over discrete anatomical findings.

## Input / Output

The model (Qwen3-VL-4B/8B) takes a 3D CT volume V ∈ ℝ^{Z×H×W} and a clinical task prompt (e.g., 'Analyze the findings in this CT scan') and generates a free-text radiology report. For evaluation and reward computation, CABS decomposes both the ground-truth and generated reports into structured abnormality tuples ⟨organ, entity, attributes, location, certainty, evidence⟩ within a predefined clinical ontology, enabling organ-level and finding-level precision/recall/F1 rather than token-level overlap. The MCQ variants further constrain the action space to binary existence, four-choice location, and four-choice attribute questions answerable from CT images alone.

## Method

The paper introduces two coupled components. First, CABS decomposes radiology reports into atomic clinical abnormality units as structured tuples in a clinical ontology space, validated by multiple radiologists with 98.6% approval, providing a verifiable substrate for both evaluation and reward computation. Second, TIF-GRPO reformulates clinical reasoning as a pseudo-temporal trajectory over the sequence of predicted abnormalities and applies an integral feedback loop (analogous to the I-term in PID control) within GRPO: a Running Cost term integrates squared false-negative errors cumulatively across the trajectory to penalize persistent omissions, while a Control Effort term penalizes false positives as excessive control energy. The per-unit CABS reward (binary hit × location/entity match) feeds into this trajectory-integral objective, and the resulting TIF reward undergoes group-relative normalization to produce advantages that modify credit assignment across the response rather than merely rescaling pointwise rewards. This path-dependent structure is fundamentally distinct from standard reward shaping, as it alters the relative ranking of responses within a GRPO group and thereby changes gradient directions during policy updates.

## Results

On CT-RATE-Report, TIF-GRPO (4B, built on Qwen3-VL-4B) achieves an Entity F1 of 39.11, Fully-Consistent Rate of 20.37, and Organ Coverage (Or-Rate) of 40.84, substantially outperforming all general-purpose VLMs (best: GPT-5 at F1 26.26) and medical-specific models (best: CT-CHAT† at F1 26.03). On AMOS-MM-Report, the 4B model reaches Entity F1 of 30.47 and FC-Rate of 22.77, again leading all baselines. On the MIMIC-CXR cross-modal transfer task, TIF-GRPO improves F1 from 25.3 (baseline) to 70.8, demonstrating that the trajectory-integral mechanism generalizes beyond 3D CT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TIF-GRPO (4B) outperforms all nine baselines on Entity F1, FC-Rate, and Or-Rate on CT-RATE-Report. Against the strongest general VLM (GPT-5, F1 26.26), TIF-GRPO gains +12.85 F1 points and more than doubles FC-Rate (20.37 vs 8.33). Against the strongest medical-specific model (CT-CHAT†, F1 26.03), TIF-GRPO improves F1 by +13.08 and Or-Rate by +8.41, despite using only ~45% of CT-CHAT's training data; CT-CHAT retains a slight edge on Loc-Acc (78.04 vs 70.23). On AMOS-MM-Report, TIF-GRPO (4B) leads all baselines with Entity F1 30.47 and FC-Rate 22.77, versus the next-best Hulu-Med (F1 7.60, FC-Rate 1.74). On the MCQ benchmarks, TIF-GRPO achieves the highest total average (69.67 on AMOS-MM-MCQ, 66.65 on CT-RATE-MCQ), surpassing both GRPO-with-accuracy-reward variants and all pre-trained VLMs, confirming that trajectory-integral credit assignment yields more generalizable clinical representations than surface-similarity or accuracy-only rewards.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) by replacing its scalar reward with a trajectory-integral, anatomy-aware signal derived from a new structured evaluation substrate (CABS), drawing conceptual inspiration from PID control theory. It departs from prior medical RL work (MedVLM-R1, Med-R1, ChestX-Reasoner, QoQ-Med) that relies on surface-similarity or sparse rubric rewards, arguing these induce 'Mechanistic Divergence' where policy gradients bypass clinical facts. CABS is positioned as a successor to RadGraph and RaTEScore, which the authors show remain proxy signals weakly correlated with true clinical competence. The base model follows the standard two-stage SFT+RL paradigm used across 3D medical VLMs (CT-CHAT, Hulu-Med, OmniCT).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CABS framework offers a more clinically grounded evaluation standard for medical VLMs, and the TIF-GRPO mechanism demonstrates that trajectory-level credit assignment (not just reward magnitude) is critical for aligning RL with diagnostic factuality. The cross-modal generalization to MIMIC-CXR (F1 25.3→70.8) suggests the approach is not CT-specific. However, a key open question is the circularity concern: CABS decomposition and reward computation both rely on LLMs (GPT-5, Qwen3-30B), so the 'clinical ground truth' is itself LLM-mediated rather than purely human-verified. Whether the framework scales to modalities with less structured ontologies (e.g., pathology, ophthalmology) remains untested.
