## Problem

Continuous diffusion language models are provably more expressive than discrete diffusion and looped transformers, yet empirically underperform them due to larger decision spaces, combinatorial decoding complexity, and deficient representation targets. Looped transformers, while expressive through latent reasoning, suffer from out-of-distribution issues at inference because intermediate states receive no supervision during training. The paper asks whether a single framework can simultaneously achieve the expressivity of continuous latent reasoning and the trainability and sample quality of explicit discrete tokens.

## Input / Output

At each denoising step, the model receives a partially masked token sequence x_t (where some positions are [MASK]) and a corresponding partially noised continuous embedding z_t (Gaussian-corrupted contextualized representations from Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B, dimension 32 per token in experiments). The output is a fully denoised text sequence, with the discrete head producing token logits and the continuous head producing clean embedding predictions. The number of denoising steps is flexible at inference: as few as 8 steps via ODE sampling for speed, or 512 steps via SDE sampling for maximum quality, with classifier-free guidance scale w controlling the trade-off.

## Method

CCDD defines a joint diffusion process on the product space X × Z, where the forward process independently corrupts discrete tokens via a CTMC (masked/absorbing noise) and continuous embeddings via a VP SDE (Gaussian noise). A single time-conditioned transformer network takes both (x_t, z_t) as input and produces two modality-specific heads: a discrete head predicting clean token distributions and a continuous head predicting clean embeddings, with cross-modal conditioning enabling the continuous representations to serve as implicit planning signals for token decoding. The training loss is a weighted sum of continuous ε-prediction MSE and discrete cross-entropy, providing dense intermediate supervision across all timesteps. Architecturally, three variants are proposed—MDiT (zero extra parameters), MoEDiT (mixture-of-experts routing), and MMDiT (dual-stream attention)—all built on DiT with rotary embeddings. A key inference technique is representation classifier-free guidance, where the continuous embeddings act as self-generated conditioning: the joint forward is treated as the conditional model and the discrete-only forward as the unconditional model, with guidance scale w interpolating between them.

## Results

On LM1B, CCDD-MDiT (92.1M params) reduces validation perplexity to ≤29.22 versus MDLM's ≤39.17 at the same parameter count, a 25% improvement achieved in only 40k iterations versus MDLM's 1000k (25× training acceleration). On OpenWebText with Qwen-2 tokenizer, CCDD-MoEDiT (104M params) achieves ≤21.90 PPL, a 35% reduction over MDLM (≤33.78). In few-step generation, CCDD with only 8 denoising steps outperforms MDLM with 256 steps. On complex reasoning, CCDD at depth 2 achieves 100% Sudoku, 91.9% 3-SAT, and 67.8% Countdown, surpassing all prior methods including looped transformers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CCDD consistently outperforms the masked discrete diffusion baseline MDLM (Sahoo et al., 2024) by 25–35% in validation perplexity across both LM1B and OWT at comparable parameter counts. Against the autoregressive GPT-2 baseline on OWT (GPT-2 tokenizer), CCDD-MoEDiT with RoBERTa embeddings (≤24.56) is slightly behind GPT-2 (23.40) but well ahead of MDLM (≤27.39) and GIDD+ (≤25.82); SEDD (≤24.10) is marginally better but was trained on 262B tokens versus CCDD's 131B. On complex reasoning tasks, CCDD at depth 2 matches looped transformer (LT) on Sudoku (100%) but surpasses it on 3-SAT (91.9% vs 91.3%) and Countdown (67.8% vs 60.6%), and at depth 3 extends the Countdown lead to 73.7% vs 68.2%. CCDD also dominates MDM (Ye et al., 2024) on all three reasoning benchmarks, achieving 100% Sudoku at depth 2 versus MDM's 88.6% at depth 2 (99.9% only at depth 20). In few-step generation, CCDD with 8 steps achieves lower generative NLL than MDLM with 256 steps, a 16× sampling acceleration.

## Relation to Prior Work

CCDD unifies three previously separate research threads—continuous diffusion (SDE/PF-ODE), discrete masked diffusion (CTMC-based MDLM/SEDD), and looped transformers—into a single joint denoising framework. Architecturally it builds directly on DiT and MM-DiT for the denoising backbone, borrows the masked CTMC forward process from MDLM, and adopts pretrained contextualized embeddings (Qwen3-Embedding) as the continuous generation space, drawing on the representation-guidance literature (REPA, RCG). The key departure is the theoretical proof that continuous diffusion strictly dominates discrete diffusion in trajectory-level expressivity and can simulate looped transformer rollouts, followed by the practical insight that the empirical underperformance of CDMs stems from trainability (large decision space, decoding complexity) rather than expressivity, which the joint discrete-continuous design resolves.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical hierarchy (CDM ≻ DDM ≻ LT) that explains why previously underperforming continuous diffusion models were not fundamentally limited but rather poorly trained, and offers a concrete recipe (joint denoising with pretrained embeddings) that closes the gap. The 8-step generation result beating 256-step MDLM is practically significant for inference efficiency in diffusion language models, and the complex-reasoning results (Sudoku, 3-SAT, Countdown) validate latent reasoning benefits at small scale. Open questions include whether the approach scales to billion-parameter models, whether the specific choice of pretrained embedding space is critical, and how the asynchronous noise schedule design generalizes to other modalities or longer sequences.
