## Problem

Existing robotic manipulation benchmarks emphasize reversible, high-tolerance tasks where intermediate errors can be recovered, and standard success metrics only check the final state. In scientific laboratories, small pose, force, or tilt errors cause irreversible spillage or equipment damage, so task success can mask unsafe execution throughout the trajectory. No prior benchmark combines calibrated fluid dynamics, physically verified task generation, and an interactive RL interface that penalizes unsafe intermediate states.

## Input / Output

Policies consume three RGB camera streams (head, chest, third-person at 480×640, resized to 224×224 for RL), proprioceptive joint positions and velocities from dual RealMan RM75-6F arms with PsiBot G0-R dexterous hands, and a natural-language task instruction. The residual RL outputs normalized joint-delta corrections in [-1,1] scaled by α=0.1 (≈15 mm / 0.1 rad) added to a frozen IL base policy. Evaluation produces per-trial Safe Success Rate (SSR) requiring zero safety violations across the full rollout, alongside domain-specific diagnostics: orientation deviation θ_dev (0.25 rad transport limit), peak contact force F_peak, and spatial residual ΔP (20 mm tolerance).

## Method

SafeLab is a three-component generative simulation framework built on Isaac Lab with GPU-parallel PBD fluid dynamics. A verified generative engine uses an LLM to propose YAML task configurations, then filters them through a four-stage cascade (schema validation, geometric grounding, causal logic, sim-in-the-loop dynamic feasibility) yielding a 44% end-to-end pass rate across 64 tasks and 63 calibrated assets. An automated expert generates 6,400 teleoperation-free demonstrations via cuRobo parallel trajectory optimization with jerk minimization. The safety-aware RL interface trains a bounded residual policy (DrQ-style SAC with NatureCNN encoders) on frozen IL base policies using a stage-wise progressive reward: sparse exponential curriculum bonuses (2^{k-1}) for sub-goal completion plus dense hybrid penalties on joint jerk, end-effector velocity, mechanical energy, pose residual, orientation deviation, and peak contact force. The key design choice is treating safety as a continuous trajectory property rather than a terminal label, so a policy cannot retroactively satisfy the safety criterion by reaching the final pose after an earlier spill or force violation.

## Results

Across all three evaluation domains (Liquid, Actuation, Spatial), Safe Success Rate (SSR) trails Success Rate (SR) by more than 30 percentage points for every baseline, confirming that task completion masks unsafe execution. π₀.₅ achieves the highest SR (91.2–95.0%) and SSR (48.6–55.0%) among the five baselines, yet still violates safety constraints in roughly half of its successful rollouts. Bounded residual RL post-training on frozen base policies improves domain-averaged SSR by 43.0 percentage points for DP (from 32.5% to 75.5%) and 33.1 percentage points for π₀.₅ (from 52.9% to 86.0%). Open-loop physical replay of 50 trajectories on PsiBot hardware confirms 86% agreement (κ=0.93) between simulated and observed safety labels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

π₀.₅ dominates all other baselines on both SR and SSR across every domain, reaching 91–95% SR and 49–55% SSR, yet still exhibits a 36–41 point SR-to-SSR gap, meaning roughly 40% of its successful rollouts contain safety violations. DP3 is the second-strongest method (72–86% SR, 36–49% SSR), followed by DP (59–78% SR, 20–41% SSR), ACT (52–66% SR, 15–27% SSR), and OpenVLA (50–64% SR, 12–24% SSR), with OpenVLA's single-camera protocol contributing to its lowest SSR. The critical finding is that no baseline closes the safety gap: even the best method (π₀.₅) violates trajectory-level constraints in over 40% of nominally successful episodes. After bounded residual RL (α=0.1), DP's domain-averaged SSR jumps from 32.5% to 75.5% (+43.0 pp) and π₀.₅'s from 52.9% to 86.0% (+33.1 pp), with liquid handling showing the largest absolute gains. Constrained RL variants (PPO-Lagrangian, PCPO) achieve marginally higher SSR on actuation and spatial tasks (+2–4 pp over the penalty method) but at 1.8–2.1× training cost, with negligible liquid-domain improvement, confirming that the dense penalty reward is the most cost-effective default for multi-phase laboratory tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

SafeLab sits at the intersection of three research threads: GPU-parallel simulation frameworks (Isaac Lab), data-augmented visual RL (DrQ-v2/SAC), and particle-based fluid simulation (Position-Based Dynamics). It departs from generalist manipulation benchmarks (LIBERO, RoboCasa, ManiSkill3) that emphasize reversible, high-tolerance rigid-body tasks, and from scientific benchmarks (AutoBio, LabUtopia, FluidLab) that model glassware and fluids but lack a unified trajectory-level safety-aware RL interface. The 'Propose-then-Verify' generative engine extends LLM-based task synthesis (GenSim, RoboGen) by adding a hierarchical physical verification cascade that rejects 56% of plausible LLM proposals, and the bounded residual RL on frozen IL policies follows the policy-correction lineage (PiCor, SafeVLA) but targets irreversible fluid and contact-force failures rather than household navigation constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The consistent >30-percentage-point gap between Success Rate and Safe Success Rate across all five baselines and all three domains provides concrete evidence that terminal-state metrics systematically overestimate laboratory readiness, a finding that should influence how the community designs evaluation protocols for safety-critical manipulation. The 86% sim-to-real agreement on 50 open-loop replays is encouraging but explicitly limited: false negatives concentrate in tall narrow containers where particle resolution underestimates peak sloshing, and the study validates label consistency rather than closed-loop policy transfer. Open questions include whether the residual RL correction generalizes to unseen task logic or asset affordances, whether the 0.25 rad transport threshold transfers across liquid viscosities beyond water-like defaults, and how the framework extends to chemical, thermal, or multi-agent deployment hazards that the simulator does not model.
