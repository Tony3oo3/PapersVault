## Problem

Applying diffusion models to text is challenging because existing approaches face a fundamental trade-off: Gaussian diffusion in continuous embedding spaces captures semantic relationships between tokens but requires a non-trivial decoding step from latent vectors back to discrete tokens, while categorical and simplex-based diffusion methods naturally preserve text discreteness but ignore semantic similarity during the noising process, leading to erratic information degradation. No prior method simultaneously respects both the discrete nature of text and the semantic structure of token embeddings within a unified diffusion framework.

## Input / Output

For unconditional generation, the model starts from a random Gaussian noise sample; for sequence-to-sequence tasks, it additionally conditions on a source sequence processed by a 6-layer Transformer encoder via cross-attention. The model input at each denoising step is a soft token distribution p_t (obtained by softmax over a noisy distance matrix), which is converted to a lower-dimensional representation via a weighted average of fixed BERT token embeddings (p_t·E) before being fed to the ~100M-parameter Transformer decoder. Output is a discrete token sequence decoded by argmax over the final clean distance matrix D_0, with padding tokens discarded post-generation.

## Method

SMOOTHIE represents each token as a vector of negative squared Euclidean distances between its BERT embedding and all vocabulary embeddings, creating a latent space where semantic proximity is directly encoded. The forward process applies non-Markovian Gaussian noising to this distance matrix with a timestep-dependent bandwidth σ_t; after noising, the matrix is converted to a probability distribution via softmax, which acts as a Nadaraya-Watson kernel estimator that progressively spreads probability mass from the true token to semantically similar tokens first. The reverse process trains a Transformer decoder (with UNet-style skip connections and self-conditioning) to predict the clean token embedding from the soft distribution, and a custom sampling solver (Eq. 9) reconstructs the distance matrix from the predicted embedding at each step, deliberately ignoring the previous latent state to avoid drift on the sparse text manifold. The paper proves that this distance-based latent space generalizes simplex diffusion (recovered as the trivial-metric special case) and that predicting embeddings rather than the distance matrix directly is equivalent in expectation (Theorem 4.1), enabling a stable MSE training objective.

## Results

On unconditional generation (ROCStories), SMOOTHIE achieves a MAUVE score of 80.7, surpassing GPT-2 (78.9) and TEncDM (76.2), while maintaining competitive perplexity (24.9 vs. GPT-2's 20.5). On sequence-to-sequence tasks, SMOOTHIE achieves BERTScore of 68.8 on XSum, 63.1 on Quasar-T, 83.9 on QQP, and J-Score of 51.7 on ParaDetox, consistently outperforming all other diffusion-based methods. Ablation comparisons show SMOOTHIE's distance-based latent space outperforms both embedding-based diffusion (e.g., 68.8 vs. 68.2 BERTScore on XSum) and simplex-based diffusion (68.8 vs. 63.8 on XSum) under identical architecture and training conditions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SMOOTHIE outperforms all diffusion-based baselines across all five evaluated tasks. On ROCStories, it achieves MAUVE 80.7 vs. TEncDM's 76.2 and GPT-2's 78.9, while embedding-based diffusion (41.5) and simplex-based diffusion (15.2) underperform dramatically, confirming that semantic-aware noising is critical. On XSum, SMOOTHIE (BS 68.8, R-L 26.0) edges out TEncDM (69.9, 25.3) on ROUGE and beats Embedding★ (68.2, 24.6) and Simplex★ (63.8, 23.0) on both metrics. On Quasar-T, SMOOTHIE (BS 63.1, BLEU 19.9) surpasses TESS-random (60.8, 19.0) and Embedding★ (62.0, 18.9), though BART (BS 66.2, R-L 38.8) remains stronger. On QQP, SMOOTHIE (BS 83.9, R-L 60.9) beats Embedding★ (83.4, 59.4) and TEncDM (83.8, 57.3) on BERTScore and ROUGE-L, though Embedding★ slightly leads on BLEU (31.3 vs. 30.8). On ParaDetox, SMOOTHIE achieves the best J-Score (51.7) among all diffusion methods, beating TEncDM (49.6) and Embedding★ (49.1), but still trails GPT-2 (60.4) and BART, indicating autoregressive models retain an edge on this small-scale detoxification task.

## Relation to Prior Work

SMOOTHIE carves new ground at the intersection of two established lines of text diffusion: Gaussian diffusion in continuous embedding spaces (Diffusion-LM, TEncDM) that captures semantics but requires explicit decoding, and categorical/simplex diffusion (SSD-LM, TESS) that respects discreteness but ignores token semantics. The key conceptual move is representing each token as a vector of negative squared Euclidean distances to all vocabulary embeddings, then applying Gaussian diffusion to this distance space — an idea borrowed from heat-dissipation/blurring diffusion in the image domain (Rissanen et al., 2023; Hoogeboom & Salimans, 2023). The paper proves that simplex diffusion is a strict special case of this distance-based formulation under a trivial metric, unifying the two approaches. It also introduces a sampling solver that re-applies the forward process to the model's prediction rather than updating from the previous latent state, departing from standard DDPM/DDIM solvers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The distance-based latent space provides a principled way to progressively dissolve semantic information while preserving a natural argmax decoding path, and the ablation showing it outperforms both embedding and simplex spaces under identical architecture validates the design. The finding that a solver ignoring the previous hidden state consistently (if marginally) beats DDPM and DDIM is conceptually interesting for sparse categorical data but lacks a rigorous theoretical explanation, leaving it as an open question. Key limitations include reliance on fixed BERT embeddings (no end-to-end training), O(V) distance computation per step that slows sampling, and the fact that performance gains over strong baselines like TEncDM are modest. The framework's stated extensibility to other categorical domains (graphs, protein sequences) with domain-appropriate metrics is promising but untested.
