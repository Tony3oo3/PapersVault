## Problem

Most existing VLN methods construct map representations (topological graphs, BEV grids, semantic maps) independently of the navigation policy, treating them as fixed inputs consumed by the decision-making module. This decoupling prevents maps from being refined through learning to align with instruction semantics or policy needs, creating a mismatch between encoded spatial information and actual navigation requirements. The paper argues that maps should be learned representations shaped directly by navigation objectives rather than exhaustive reconstructions designed by experts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262683/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a multi-frame monocular RGB observation history and a natural language instruction as input. The map module autoregressively generates a compact three-channel BEV image (occupancy, distance-to-goal, and landmark channels) at resolutions as small as 96×96 (36 tokens), which serves as the sole spatial interface passed to the VLN policy. The policy then outputs a sequence of N future continuous actions, enabling multi-step planning under partial observability.

## Method

MapDream formulates map construction as autoregressive BEV image synthesis using a Janus-Pro-based generator, decoupled from a NVILA-2B-based VLN policy through the BEV map as the only spatial representation between modules. Training proceeds in two stages: supervised pre-training with separate reconstruction loss for the map and cross-entropy loss for the policy establishes a reliable mapping-to-control interface, followed by joint reinforcement fine-tuning using GRPO where a combined probability ratio over both the map generation and action prediction distributions enables end-to-end credit assignment. The RL stage uses stepwise action rewards (longest correct prefix) and format rewards to directly shape both components toward encoding navigation-critical affordances rather than geometric reconstruction. Ablations show that even the most compact 96×96 map (36 tokens) achieves near-identical success rates to 384×384 while reducing inference latency from 12.7 s to 1.3 s per step.

## Results

On R2R-CE Val-Unseen, MapDream achieves 59.8% SR and 54.4% SPL with a navigation error of 4.59 m, setting a new state-of-the-art among monocular methods. On the more challenging RxR-CE Val-Unseen, it reaches 59.4% SR and 49.2% SPL (NE = 4.96 m), again leading all single-RGB-camera approaches. In zero-shot cross-dataset transfer (trained only on R2R-CE, evaluated on RxR-CE Val-Unseen), MapDream attains 27.8% SR and 23.3% SPL, outperforming all competitors including MonoDream (25.1% SR) and sim2real (25.5% SR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among monocular (single RGB camera) methods, MapDream outperforms all baselines on both benchmarks. On R2R-CE Val-Unseen, it surpasses the closest monocular competitor MonoDream (55.8% SR, 49.1% SPL) by +4.0 SR and +5.3 SPL, and NaVILA (54.0% SR, 49.0% SPL) by +5.8 SR and +5.4 SPL. On RxR-CE Val-Unseen, the gap widens: MapDream leads Uni-NaVid (48.7% SR, 40.9% SPL) by +10.7 SR and +8.3 SPL, and NaVILA (49.3% SR, 44.0% SPL) by +10.1 SR and +5.2 SPL. MapDream also beats map-augmented monocular methods MapNav (39.7% SR) and Dynam3D (52.9% SR) by large margins on R2R-CE, demonstrating that learned task-driven maps outperform hand-crafted or annotated map representations. In the zero-shot cross-dataset setting (Table 2), MapDream (27.8% SR, 23.3% SPL) edges out sim2real (25.5% SR, 21.2% SPL) and MonoDream (25.1% SR, 21.6% SPL), confirming that the learned maps capture transferable spatial structure rather than dataset-specific shortcuts.

## Relation to Prior Work

MapDream builds on the Janus-Pro autoregressive multimodal generation architecture (extended for multi-image and text conditioning) as its map generator, and on the NVILA-2B vision-language-action model as its navigation policy backbone. It departs from prior map-based VLN systems (BEVBert, GridMM, MapNav, Dynam3D) that treat maps as fixed, expert-designed inputs consumed by a separate policy, by placing the map generator inside the learning loop so that navigation rewards directly shape what the map encodes. The joint optimization via GRPO, where the policy ratio couples both the map module and the VLN policy, is a novel architectural choice not present in prior VLN or map-learning work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core claim—that maps for VLN should be learned task-driven representations rather than exhaustive reconstructions—is well-supported by the ablation showing that a 96×96 BEV map (36 tokens) matches the 384×384 version (576 tokens) in success rate while reducing inference latency from 12.7 s to 1.3 s, a practically significant efficiency gain for real-time deployment. The zero-shot transfer from simulator to a Unitree G1 humanoid in unseen indoor scenes suggests the learned spatial abstractions capture transferable structure. However, the framework currently maintains only a local egocentric BEV without explicit global memory or temporal consistency enforcement, limiting its applicability to very long-horizon or multi-room navigation; extending to persistent global maps and temporally coherent representations is the most natural next step.
