## Problem

Most existing time-series imputation methods formulate recovery as local conditional reconstruction over limited temporal windows, treating time as an indexing mechanism rather than a state-evolving dimension. This restricts temporal information propagation, biases models toward static correlations over genuine dynamic dependencies, and prevents explicit modeling of how long-range historical context constrains current missing states.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261056/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a multivariate time series partitioned into a historical segment of length L (up to 2048 timesteps) and an imputation target segment of length Δ, along with a binary missingness mask M. The history is encoded as L×C time-variable tokens and compressed into N=16 latent tokens, while the imputation targets are individual observed time-variable pairs (r tokens). Output is imputed values at missing positions within the target segment, evaluated via Masked MAE and Masked RMSE under both MCAR and MAR missingness mechanisms at 10%–50% missing rates.

## Method

CBiT operates under the Conditional Temporal Inference Paradigm (CTIP), which formulates imputation as conditional inference p(X_imp^missing | X_hist, X_imp^observed, M, Δ) rather than local reconstruction. A Temporal Latent Writer compresses L×C historical tokens into N latent tokens via Perceiver-style cross-attention (reducing complexity from O((LC)²) to O(NL)+O(N)²), using history-aware Fourier-based temporal encodings with relative normalization and variable-space encodings combining dynamic window statistics with static variable identities. A Dual-Manifold Cross-Block Attention module then computes global attention once over the unified [Lat; Imp] sequence and decomposes it into four directional blocks (hist→hist, hist→cur, cur→hist, cur→cur), each with its own value projection and block-wise attention sharpening to handle the asymmetric concentration of historical (diffuse) versus target (sparse) relations. A gated aggregation mechanism with max-pooling for history and average-pooling for targets fuses the block responses into a unified representation for final imputation. Training uses mask-aware losses under two settings: joint supervision (Masked MAE + weighted Observed MAE) and missing-only supervision (Masked MAE alone).

## Results

Under joint supervision, CBiT achieves the lowest Masked MAE and Masked RMSE across all four reported datasets (ETTh1, ETTh2, Weather, Power) and all missing rates (10%–50%). On ETTh1, CBiT reduces MAE by 35.4% and RMSE by 23.9% relative to the best baseline; on Power, the reductions reach 29.5% (MAE) and 14.0% (RMSE). Under the more challenging missing-only supervision, CBiT still leads on every dataset, with 22.0% MAE and 23.5% RMSE reductions on ETTh1. CBiT also attains the lowest FLOPs (0.0586 G) among all compared methods while using only 0.275 M parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CBiT outperforms all six baselines on every dataset and missing rate under both joint and missing-only supervision. The strongest baseline is SAITS (reconstruction-based), which CBiT surpasses by 10–35% in MAE depending on dataset and missing rate; for example, on ETTh1 at 30% missing, CBiT achieves 0.1959 MAE versus SAITS's 0.3110. The diffusion-based SSSD and unified UNiTS models degrade sharply at higher missing rates (e.g., UNiTS jumps from 0.4733 to 0.6304 MAE on ETTh1 between 30% and 50%), while CBiT's degradation is far more gradual (0.1959 → 0.2217). Among time-series forecasting models adapted for imputation, DeformableTST and XPatch consistently underperform CBiT by 15–40% in MAE. Notably, CBiT also achieves the lowest FLOPs (0.0586 G) and second-lowest parameter count (0.275 M) among all compared methods, offering the best accuracy–efficiency trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

CBiT builds on Perceiver-style latent compression (Jaegle et al., 2021b) to distill long-range history into compact tokens, and on standard Transformer multi-head attention for cross-token interaction, but departs from the dominant local-reconstruction paradigm (SAITS, ImputeFormer) by explicitly conditioning imputation on a compressed historical manifold rather than treating all tokens within a window as homogeneous. The Dual-Manifold Cross-Block Attention introduces a post-attention decomposition that separates historical and target semantics after a single global attention pass, a design not present in prior imputation Transformers. The theoretical framing (CTIP) connects to the information-theoretic argument that marginalizing over history yields a mixture conditional distribution, providing a principled justification for the architectural choice to retain history as an explicit conditioning signal rather than folding it into a shared reconstruction objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CTIP formulation offers a clean probabilistic justification for why history-conditioned imputation should outperform local reconstruction, and the empirical results (27.3% MAE reduction, consistent across four datasets and five missing rates under both MCAR and MAR) support this claim. The efficiency profile is practically notable: CBiT requires fewer FLOPs (0.0586G) and comparable parameters (0.275M) than SAITS while substantially outperforming it, making it attractive for resource-constrained deployment. Open questions include whether the Perceiver-style compression degrades gracefully at very long histories (beyond the 2048-token limit tested), how the method handles irregularly sampled or event-driven time series, and whether the block-wise sharpening mechanism generalizes to settings with more than two token manifolds (e.g., multi-horizon imputation).
