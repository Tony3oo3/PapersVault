## Problem

Existing generative recommendation methods that use RQ-VAE for item tokenization implicitly model the hierarchical relationships across codebook layers in Euclidean space, which distorts the intrinsic tree-like hierarchy induced by residual quantization and leads to low codebook utilization (e.g., only 22.6% usage in the first layer). This geometric mismatch between Euclidean space and the hierarchical codebook structure causes imbalanced codeword assignment, high collision rates, and inefficient representation capacity.

## Input / Output

The method takes user interaction histories (chronological item sequences, truncated to length 20) and item content information (titles, prices, categories, descriptions) as input. Item content is first encoded into semantic embeddings via Sentence-T5, then quantized into 3-layer discrete token sequences (e.g., [64,128,256] codebook sizes) by the hyperbolic RQ-VAE. The downstream Transformer encoder-decoder autoregressively generates next-item token sequences, which are decoded back into top-N recommended items via beam search (beam size 20).

## Method

HG-Rec introduces two complementary components. First, Hyperbolic RQ-VAE embeds latent discrete representations into the Poincaré ball to explicitly capture hierarchical relationships across codebook layers: at each quantization layer, residuals and codewords are mapped from tangent space to hyperbolic space via the exponential map for distance-based codeword selection, then mapped back to tangent space via the logarithmic map for stable residual subtraction. Second, the differential-length codebook strategy assigns codebook sizes following a pyramidal structure (K_ℓ ∝ K₁·γ^ℓ, with γ≈2) derived from the exponential volume growth of hyperbolic space, allocating more capacity to fine-grained layers while compressing the total codebook. The training pipeline first learns the hyperbolic RQ-VAE tokenizer (with hyperbolic reconstruction and quantization losses), then trains a 4-layer Transformer encoder-decoder with negative log-likelihood loss over the resulting token sequences.

## Results

HG-Rec achieves state-of-the-art performance across all three datasets (Beauty, Instruments, Yelp) on both Recall@N and NDCG@N. On Beauty, HG-Rec reaches R@5=0.0572 and N@10=0.0473; on Instruments, R@5=0.1058 and N@10=0.0945; on Yelp, R@5=0.0275 and N@10=0.0233. The improvements over the strongest baseline (ActionPiece) range from 4.8% to 13.5% across metrics and datasets, with the largest gains on Yelp (13.5% R@5, 11.8% N@5). Additionally, hyperbolic RQ-VAE reduces total training time by approximately 36×, 11×, and 38× on Beauty, Instruments, and Yelp respectively compared to vanilla RQ-VAE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HG-Rec consistently outperforms all 9 baseline groups across all three datasets and all four metrics. Against traditional sequential recommenders (Caser, HGN, SASRec, Bert4Rec), HG-Rec achieves substantially higher scores, e.g., 0.0572 vs. 0.0438 R@5 on Beauty for SASRec, confirming the advantage of the generative retrieval paradigm. Among ID-based tokenization methods (P5-RID/IID/TID/SemID/CID), the best variant P5-CID reaches 0.0481 R@5 on Beauty, still 15.6% below HG-Rec. Among codebook-based GR methods, TIGER (0.0502 R@5 Beauty), LC-Rec (0.0519), and LETTER (0.0533) are all outperformed by HG-Rec, with the gap widening on Yelp where HG-Rec gains 13.5% R@5 over TIGER. The strongest baseline is ActionPiece (context-aware tokenization), which HG-Rec surpasses by 6.2% R@5 on Beauty, 5.9% on Instruments, and 13.5% on Yelp, demonstrating that hyperbolic geometry and differential-length codebooks provide complementary benefits beyond context-aware tokenization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the codebook-based tokenization paradigm for generative recommendation established by TIGER (Rajput et al., 2023) and the RQ-VAE formulation of Singh et al. (2024), which encode items into hierarchical discrete token sequences via residual quantization in Euclidean space. The key departure is replacing Euclidean geometry with hyperbolic (Poincaré ball) geometry for the quantization process, motivated by the observation that residual quantization induces a tree-like hierarchy that is geometrically mismatched with Euclidean space. The differential-length codebook strategy is a direct corollary of hyperbolic volume growth, distinguishing this work from prior codebook-based methods (TIGER, LC-Rec, LETTER) that use uniform codebook sizes across layers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and well-documented problem in generative recommendation—codebook underutilization and high collision rates—by providing a geometrically principled solution. The 36× training time reduction and 100% codebook utilization are practically significant for deployment. However, open questions remain: codebook sizes must be manually specified per dataset, numerical instability near the Poincaré ball boundary limits scalability, and it is unclear whether downstream GR models can fully exploit the learned hierarchical structure without additional architectural changes. The authors note that replacing the Poincaré model with the Lorentz model could address stability concerns.
