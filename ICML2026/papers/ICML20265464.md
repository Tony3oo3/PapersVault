## Problem

Existing sparse attention methods optimize token selection locally—per head, per layer, or per decoding step—causing small selection errors to compound over the thousands of decoding steps required by long-horizon reasoning, leading to accuracy degradation and generation length inflation. The paper identifies that token importance in reasoning is actually a global and stable property shared across attention heads and decoding steps, a structure that prior methods fail to exploit.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265464/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard text prompt and a fixed token budget K (e.g., 2K–8K tokens) along with a static recency ratio r (default 0.25) that splits the budget between globally selected historical tokens and a recent-context window. During autoregressive decoding, at each step the model attends only to the K selected tokens from the growing KV cache rather than the full cache, producing logits and generated reasoning tokens. The method is training-free and architecture-agnostic, requiring only a small number of designated selection layers (e.g., Layer 12 for Qwen3-8B) where full attention is computed to estimate token importance.

## Method

LessIsMore enforces Cross-Head Unified Sparse Attention (CUSA), which replaces per-head independent token selection with a globally consistent selection: at a designated token selection layer, each attention head independently proposes its top-k candidates from exact query-key scores, these proposals are aggregated via UnionFlatten into a single ranked set, and only the top K(1−r) tokens are retained. A fixed fraction r of the budget is reserved for the most recent K·r tokens, forming a stable recency window that preserves step-by-step reasoning coherence. The resulting unified token set ρ is shared across all attention heads and reused across multiple subsequent sparse attention layers without re-selection, amortizing selection cost and preventing head-specific error accumulation. Instantiated on top of TidalDecode with two initial full-attention layers and one CUSA selection layer, the mechanism is kernel-optimized for GQA models, reducing redundant KV loading since all heads attend to the same token subset.

## Results

On AIME-24 with Qwen3-8B, LessIsMore achieves 73.8% Pass@1 at a 2K token budget (vs. 74.5% Full Attention) and 75.8% at 4K (exceeding Full Attention), while TidalDecode drops to 53.3% and Quest to 18.2% at 2K. LessIsMore maintains generation lengths nearly identical to Full Attention (14.8–15.8K tokens) whereas baselines inflate to 17–30K tokens. With kernel-level optimizations, LessIsMore delivers up to 1.6× end-to-end decoding speedup over Full Attention at 64K context and 1.3×–1.72× faster sparse attention computation versus TidalDecode. On HumanEval, LessIsMore reaches 89.63% Pass@1 at 2K budget, surpassing Full Attention (88.41%) and TidalDecode (85.36%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LessIsMore decisively outperforms TidalDecode on all reasoning benchmarks: on AIME-24 (Qwen3-8B, 2K budget) it achieves 73.8% vs. 53.3%, and at 4K it reaches 75.8% vs. 70.0%, while also generating shorter sequences (14.8K vs. 16.9K tokens). Against Quest, the gap is even wider—73.8% vs. 18.2% at 2K on AIME-24—and Quest's generation length inflates to 30K tokens (103% over Full Attention). The training-required SeerAttention-r is also surpassed: LessIsMore scores 73.8% vs. 58.2% at 2K and 75.8% vs. 71.4% at 4K on AIME-24, without any retraining. On long-context retrieval (Needle-in-the-Haystack, LongChat-7B), LessIsMore achieves 100% accuracy at a 256-token budget, dramatically outperforming eviction-based baselines H2O (1%) and TOVA (3%), and matching or exceeding TidalDecode (99%) and Quest (99%). StreamingLLM, which uses a fixed sliding window, falls below 65% attention recall on AIME-24 and is not competitive on reasoning tasks. In kernel-level efficiency, LessIsMore's unified cross-head selection reduces global-to-shared memory transfer from 2.34 MB to 1.04 MB (vs. TidalDecode/Quest), yielding 1.3×–1.72× lower attention kernel latency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on TidalDecode's selection-based sparse attention framework, which separates token selection from sparse attention computation, but departs fundamentally from the per-head independent selection paradigm shared by TidalDecode, Quest, and most prior methods. The key conceptual departure is the empirical finding that reasoning attention exhibits strong cross-head spatial locality—contradicting the standard assumption that attention heads serve highly specialized roles—motivating a unified cross-head token selection (CUSA) rather than head-local top-k. The work also positions itself against eviction-based methods (StreamingLLM, H2O, SnapKV) and training-required reasoning-specific approaches (SeerAttention-r), arguing that training-free, globally consistent selection is both simpler and more effective for long-horizon reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LessIsMore provides a practical, training-free recipe for deploying reasoning models at scale: 1.6× end-to-end speedup with no accuracy loss across multiple model families and benchmarks, which is directly relevant to the growing cost of test-time scaling in production. The cross-head locality observation, if it generalizes beyond the tested models, could reshape how sparse attention is designed more broadly. Open questions include whether the fixed recency ratio and single re-selection layer remain optimal at 100K+ context lengths, whether the approach extends to multi-modal or agentic reasoning settings, and whether the unified selection assumption holds for models with more extreme head specialization (e.g., very large MoE architectures).
