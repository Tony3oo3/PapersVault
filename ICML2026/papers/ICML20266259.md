## Problem

Functional data analysis faces a critical trade-off: classical functional regression models are constrained by linearity and offer limited region selection, while deep learning methods for functional inputs provide strong nonlinear approximation but operate as black boxes without interpretable, structured region selection. No existing framework simultaneously captures complex nonlinear function-to-scalar relationships and performs interpretable, uncertainty-quantified region selection on the functional domain with theoretical guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method takes functional curves (e.g., ECG waveforms, NIR absorbance spectra, daily power trajectories) observed on discrete grids and projects them onto locally supported B-spline bases to form a finite-dimensional spline feature vector of length J_n. It produces a scalar prediction, per-basis posterior inclusion probabilities (PIPs) that reflect both region location and signal strength, and an estimated active region expressed as a union of disjoint subintervals on the functional domain, obtained by merging the supports of selected B-splines.

## Method

sBayFDNN embeds functional predictors via B-spline projection into a feedforward ReLU network (depth H_n, constant hidden width) that estimates the composite single-index function h*(x) = g*(x^T w). A group-wise continuous spike-and-slab prior is imposed on each column of the first-layer weight matrix W_1: a Bernoulli inclusion indicator γ_j switches between a tight spike variance (shrinking the column toward zero) and a diffuse slab variance (permitting nonzero contributions), enabling region-wise selection in the spline-feature domain. Inference proceeds by MAP estimation with the inclusion indicators integrated out, followed by plug-in PIP computation from the MAP first-layer weights and thresholding to select active spline features, which are then mapped back to the functional domain via B-spline supports. The paper establishes the first theoretical guarantees for a Bayesian deep functional model, including non-asymptotic approximation error bounds, posterior contraction in Hellinger distance, and asymptotic consistency of both feature selection and region recovery.

## Results

sBayFDNN achieves the lowest RMSE across all four real-world datasets: 12.069 on ECG (vs. 12.991 for FNN), 2.138 on Tecator (vs. 2.217 for FNN), 0.618 on Bike Sharing (vs. 0.684 for SLoS), and 0.536 on IHPC (vs. 0.549 for FNN). On region-selection metrics, sBayFDNN attains F1 = 0.634 with perfect recall (1.000) on ECG and F1 = 0.339 with perfect recall on Tecator, substantially outperforming the next-best methods (cFuSIM F1 = 0.501 on ECG; SLoS F1 = 0.228 on Tecator). In simulation studies, sBayFDNN consistently achieves the highest mean F1 scores across all β(t) complexity levels, link functions, and SNR settings, with the advantage most pronounced under nonlinear links and lower SNR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

sBayFDNN outperforms all five baselines on predictive RMSE across every dataset. Against the two deep-learning baselines that do not perform region selection—FNN (Thind et al., 2023) and AdaFNN (Yao et al., 2021)—sBayFDNN achieves lower RMSE on all four datasets (e.g., 12.069 vs. 12.991 on ECG; 0.618 vs. 0.699 on Bike), confirming that structured sparsity does not sacrifice predictive accuracy. Against the three region-selection baselines, sBayFDNN dominates on both prediction and region recovery: on ECG it improves F1 from 0.501 (cFuSIM, Nie et al., 2023), 0.396 (BFRS, Zhu et al., 2025), and 0.412 (SLoS, Lin et al., 2017) to 0.634, while also achieving the lowest RMSE (12.069 vs. 17.677, 16.297, 16.258 respectively). On Tecator the gap is even wider in F1 (0.339 vs. 0.137, 0.211, 0.228) and RMSE (2.138 vs. 3.932, 2.691, 2.567). cFuSIM matches sBayFDNN's perfect recall on ECG but at the cost of substantially lower precision (0.334 vs. 0.464), reflecting its tendency to select broader active regions. Linear methods (BFRS, SLoS) degrade sharply under nonlinear link functions in simulations, whereas sBayFDNN maintains stable performance, underscoring the benefit of the deep architecture for capturing complex nonlinear dependencies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three threads: classical sparse functional regression (sparse FLR, functional single-index models with localized penalties), deep learning for functional inputs (FNN, AdaFNN, functional autoencoders), and structured sparsity in neural networks (group-sparse first layers, Bayesian DNNs with spike-and-slab priors). It departs from prior work by combining B-spline functional embedding with a Bayesian ReLU network whose first layer carries a group-wise spike-and-slab prior, thereby unifying nonlinear function approximation with interpretable, region-wise selection and posterior uncertainty quantification in a single framework. The theoretical contribution—posterior consistency and region selection consistency for a Bayesian deep functional model—is explicitly positioned as the first of its kind, building on the sparse DNN consistency results of Sun et al. (2022) but extending them to the functional, spline-embedded setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides the first rigorous statistical guarantees (approximation bounds, posterior contraction, exact region recovery) for a Bayesian deep functional model, giving practitioners a principled tool for nonlinear function-to-scalar regression where identifying *which* subregions of a curve drive prediction is scientifically essential (ECG morphology, spectral bands, power trajectories). Key limitations include dependence on a fixed B-spline resolution J_n, restriction to a single functional predictor with a single projection direction per region, and MAP-based plug-in PIPs rather than full posterior sampling. Open directions flagged by the authors include adaptive basis selection, multiple functional inputs with region-wise subspaces, joint modeling with discrete covariates, and robust loss functions for heavy-tailed settings.
