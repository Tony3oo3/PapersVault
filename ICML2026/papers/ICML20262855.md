## Problem

Existing cross-tokenizer knowledge distillation (CTKD) methods enter a 'Low-Overlap negative transfer regime' where vocabulary-mismatch-induced alignment noise causes the student to underperform a simple SFT baseline. Divergence-based objectives are especially sensitive to this noise, while prior OT-based approaches either collapse to token-level alignment (ignoring sequence structure) or incur quadratic costs from dense sequence-level transport with iterative Sinkhorn solvers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262855/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Given the same raw text, the method consumes temperature-scaled next-token distributions from both teacher and student at each supervised position, along with character-level span offsets exposed by each tokenizer. The distillation loss is computed over a sparse, deterministically constructed set of teacher-student token pairs (those whose character spans overlap), with no learned matching weights or auxiliary vocabulary-mapping storage. The final training objective combines this span-constrained transport cost with a standard cross-entropy loss, producing a trained student model.

## Method

E-SCOT replaces expensive dense OT with three components: (1) a vocabulary-agnostic ground metric using the 1D Wasserstein-1 distance on sorted probability values (inherited from ULD), which compares distribution shapes without requiring shared token indices; (2) span-anchored lexical alignment that constructs a sparse support set via character-span overlaps using a linear two-pointer sweep, assigning uniform weights 1/|N(i)| to all overlapping teacher tokens for each student token, reducing alignment complexity to O(|I|+|J|); and (3) Rényi-entropy adaptive reweighting that computes normalized Rényi entropy at orders {0.5, 1, 2} for both distributions and uses the summed discrepancy as an informativeness score to upweight positions with larger uncertainty-profile gaps. A smoothing parameter λ interpolates between uniform and fully adaptive weighting, and gradients are stopped through the reweighting coefficients so they act purely as loss multipliers.

## Results

On the Dolly-15K instruction-following setup (Rouge-L averaged over 5 benchmarks), E-SCOT achieves the highest average score across all four teacher–student pairs: 26.91 (Phi-3→Qwen2-0.5B), 28.47 (Llama-3→Gemma-2-2B), 23.75 (Phi-3→OPT-1.3B), and 25.50 (Mistral-7B→TinyLlama-1.1B). This corresponds to a 4.67% improvement over the best baseline (ULD, 25.68) for Qwen2-0.5B and a 4.32% improvement (vs. ULD, 27.29) for Gemma-2-2B. In the low-overlap regime (e.g., Phi-3→Qwen2-0.5B, overlap ≈ 30%), DSKD and MinED fall below the SFT baseline (negative transfer), while E-SCOT maintains a consistent +1.56 Rouge-L gain over SFT. On the additional Phi-4-14B→Qwen3-4B evaluation (UltraChat-200K, 4 capability benchmarks), E-SCOT reaches 0.6601 average, outperforming SFT (0.6498) and DSKD (0.6430).

## Baselines & Comparisons

E-SCOT outperforms all five baselines (SeqKD, ULD, MultiLevelOT, DSKD, MinED) on the average Rouge-L score across every teacher–student pair. Against the closest OT-based competitor ULD, E-SCOT gains +1.23 (Qwen2-0.5B), +1.18 (Gemma-2-2B), +0.85 (OPT-1.3B), and +0.75 (TinyLlama) in average score. The gap is most pronounced against explicit-alignment methods: DSKD drops to 24.26 (below SFT's 25.35) on the low-overlap Phi-3→Qwen2-0.5B pair, and MinED reaches only 24.34, both exhibiting the identified negative-transfer regime, whereas E-SCOT maintains a clear +1.56 margin over SFT. MultiLevelOT, despite its heavier Sinkhorn-based sequence-level optimization, is consistently outperformed (e.g., 24.56 vs. 26.91 on Qwen2-0.5B), confirming that the dense global transport plan does not compensate for the lack of explicit locality constraints. On the high-overlap Mistral-7B→TinyLlama pair (overlap ≈ 75.6%), E-SCOT still leads (25.50 vs. 25.29 for DSKD/MinED), and the LLM-as-Judge evaluation further confirms E-SCOT's advantage with win rates of 64–69% against SFT, MinED, ULD, and DSKD respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

E-SCOT sits at the intersection of two CTKD lineages: explicit-alignment methods (MinED, DSKD, CDM) that bridge vocabulary gaps via token mapping or hidden-state projection, and OT-based methods (ULD, MultiLevelOT) that avoid explicit mapping by comparing distribution shapes. It departs from ULD by replacing rigid index-to-index alignment with a span-anchored sparse support set built from character-offset overlaps, and from MultiLevelOT by eliminating iterative Sinkhorn optimization in favor of a deterministic linear-time two-pointer sweep. The Rényi-entropy adaptive reweighting is a novel addition not present in either lineage, drawing on information-theoretic uncertainty measures to modulate per-token distillation intensity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of the Low-Overlap negative transfer regime is practically important: it warns practitioners that CTKD can actively harm a student relative to plain SFT when tokenizers diverge substantially, a failure mode previously underappreciated. E-SCOT's linear-time span construction and parameter-free design make it attractive for deployment in heterogeneous LLM ecosystems where teacher-student tokenizer pairs are increasingly common. Open questions include whether the uniform matching prior within each span is optimal, whether overlap-aware tuning of λ (suggested by the high-overlap sensitivity study) can further improve performance, and whether the character-span assumption holds for tokenizers that do not expose reliable offset mappings (e.g., some subword or byte-level schemes).
