## Problem

Language models trained on web-scraped corpora memorize and reproduce copyrighted text at inference time, creating legal liability for developers and uncompensated use for creators. Existing mitigations are either prohibitively expensive (retraining frontier models), require restrictive model-pair assumptions (e.g., disjoint training shards for CP-FUSE), or lack formal safety guarantees. The paper asks whether a training-free, inference-time method can provably bound copyright leakage while preserving the utility of a high-capability model.

## Input / Output

At each decoding step, the method consumes the next-token (or next-byte) logit distributions from both a risky LM and a safe LM, along with a user-set per-step budget k and a debt window n. It outputs a single sampled token (or byte) drawn from a fused distribution that is a weighted geometric mean of the two models' distributions. The token-level variant requires both models to share a vocabulary; the byte-level variant (ANCHORED_Byte) operates over the 256-byte space via ByteSampler marginalization, removing the shared-vocabulary constraint. The global safety budget K = k·T_max (or k·B_max at the byte level) is the single user-facing knob controlling the risk-utility trade-off.

## Method

Anchored Decoding solves, at each step, a constrained optimization that minimizes KL divergence to the risky model subject to KL divergence to the safe model being at most k_t, yielding a closed-form weighted geometric mean of the two next-token distributions (Proposition 3.3). The Lagrange multiplier is found via a safeguarded Newton-Raphson 1D root-finding step. Two empirically effective adjustments make the budget allocation non-uniform: a one-time prefix debt δ_init(x) computed from the top-n log-likelihood-ratio outliers in the prompt (tightening the budget for memorization-triggering inputs), and an adaptive banking rule that rolls unspent budget from low-risk steps forward to cover later high-risk spikes. A key theorem (Theorem 3.1) shows that these per-step KL constraints compose via the chain rule into a global K-NAF guarantee, providing a provable sequence-level bound on divergence from the safe model. The byte-level extension (ANCHORED_Byte) applies the identical optimization over induced next-byte distributions from ByteSampler, enabling cross-tokenizer fusion between models with mismatched vocabularies (e.g., Comma 7B with Llama 3.1 70B).

## Results

At the high-protection operating point (NCR ≥ 75%), ANCHORED DECODING achieves the best utility across all six model pairs. For the token-level pair {TinyComma 1.8B, Llama 3.1 70B}, it reaches 0.53 factuality and 4.02 fluency, surpassing the strongest two-model baseline TokenSwap (0.44/3.77) and single-model baselines RCAD (0.37/3.38) and MemFree (0.37/3.18). At the byte level, ANCHORED_Byte DECODING achieves 0.52/4.23 for {Comma 7B, Llama 3.1 70B} and 0.56/4.46 for {Comma 7B, Llama 4 17B×16E}, consistently dominating all baselines. The method closes up to 75% of the measurable copying gap between the risky and safe references while adding only 1.1× inference overhead (195.9 ms TTFT vs. 181.3 ms for the risky model alone).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ANCHORED DECODING dominates all five baselines at the high-protection operating point (NCR ≥ 75%) across every model pair. Against the two-model baselines, it outperforms CP-FUSE by a wide margin (e.g., 0.53 vs. 0.20 factuality and 4.02 vs. 3.21 fluency on the token-level pair) and TokenSwap by a consistent but smaller gap (0.53 vs. 0.44 factuality; 4.02 vs. 3.77 fluency). Among single-model baselines, SYSTEM prompting fails to reach the 75% NCR threshold in any setting, while MemFree and RCAD either fall below the threshold or achieve it at substantially lower utility (RCAD: 0.37/3.38; MemFree: 0.37/3.18 on the token-level pair). Notably, RCAD requires two forward passes with the risky model (2.0× throughput slowdown), whereas ANCHORED DECODING pairs the large risky model with a much smaller safe model, incurring only 1.1× overhead. The method also generalizes across tokenizer-mismatched pairs via ANCHORED_Byte DECODING, where CP-FUSE and TokenSwap either underperform or fail entirely (TokenSwap does not reach the threshold for {Comma 7B, Llama 4}).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper operationalizes the K-NAF theoretical framework (Vyas et al., 2023) into a practical, closed-form decoding algorithm, departing from CP-FUSE (Abad et al., 2025) by removing the requirement for disjoint training shards and supporting asymmetric model pairs (a small safe anchor with a large risky model). The byte-level variant extends the ByteSampler framework (Hayase et al., 2025) to enforce K-NAF compliance across mismatched tokenizers, a setting no prior two-model copyright method handles. Unlike single-model baselines (MemFree, RCAD) that rely on retrieval-based blocklists, Anchored Decoding derives its safety signal purely from the distributional contrast between the two LMs, requiring no external datastore.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method is immediately deployable as a plug-and-play inference-time safeguard with only ~1.1× throughput overhead, making it attractive for retrofitting existing frontier models without retraining. The release of TinyComma 1.8B (a permissively trained model with a Llama 3.1-compatible tokenizer) lowers the practical barrier to adoption. However, K-NAF compliance is a mathematical bound relative to a chosen safe reference, not a legal certification of non-infringement, and the guarantee's practical meaning depends on the safe model being genuinely copyright-free. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multimodal generation, whether the safe-model requirement can be relaxed, and how the method interacts with instruction-tuning and RLHF layers.
