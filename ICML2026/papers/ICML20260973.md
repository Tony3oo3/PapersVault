## Problem

MLLMs generate continuous values autoregressively as discrete tokens optimized via next-token prediction, a paradigm fundamentally misaligned with numerical regression under long-tailed target distributions. Both token-level SFT and point-wise regression rewards bias optimization toward high-density regions, producing regression-to-the-mean behavior and poor tail performance. The paper identifies the absence of cross-sample relational supervision as the key limitation preventing existing MLLM training paradigms from preserving global distributional structure in deep imbalanced regression.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260973/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each sample consists of a single RGB image (face, movie poster, or pediatric hand radiograph) paired with a short textual prompt instructing the model to output only a numeric value. The model generates the prediction autoregressively as a token sequence, constrained to a single integer within a task-specific range (e.g., 0–100 for age, 0–216 months for bone age), wrapped in <answer></answer> tags. No chain-of-thought reasoning or explanatory text is permitted; the unified answer template ensures robust numeric extraction and decouples reward computation from free-form generation.

## Method

The paper proposes CCC-GRPO, a plug-and-play distribution-aware RL framework built on Group Relative Policy Optimization that requires no architectural modification. For each minibatch, GRPO samples K stochastic generations per input; rather than scoring each prediction independently against its ground truth, the method constructs a batch-level relational comparison vector by pairing the k-th sampled prediction with the empirical mean predictions of all other samples in the batch. The Concordance Correlation Coefficient (CCC) is then computed between this predicted vector and the corresponding ground-truth vector, simultaneously enforcing correlation, scale consistency, and mean alignment. A lightweight format-validity reward (constant 0.5 for well-formed outputs) is added to the CCC reward, and standard GRPO group-normalized advantages drive policy updates via LoRA adapters (rank 64, scale 128). Ablations confirm that the gains stem from the batch-level relational reward design rather than from the specific RL optimizer variant.

## Results

On AgeDB-DIR with Qwen2.5-VL-3B, CCC-GRPO achieves an overall MAE of 5.52, reducing medium-shot MAE from 7.67 (SFT) to 5.62 and few-shot MAE from 8.36 to 6.40, while maintaining competitive many-shot accuracy (5.42 vs. 5.78). On the more challenging BoneAge-DIR, CCC-GRPO yields a +23.55% overall MAE improvement over SFT (14.22 vs. 18.60) and a +34.35% few-shot MAE reduction. Across all four benchmarks and both 3B/7B model scales, CCC-GRPO consistently achieves the best or near-best MAE and GM in medium- and few-shot regimes, with the largest gains concentrated in sparse target regions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among MLLM-based baselines, CCC-GRPO outperforms SFT by 26.7% in medium-shot MAE and 23.4% in few-shot MAE on AgeDB-DIR (3B), with similar or larger gains on the other three datasets. SFT-Soft (Wang et al., 2025b) is essentially indistinguishable from standard SFT across all shot regimes, confirming that token-level soft labeling does not address distributional imbalance. VisualQuality (Wu et al., 2025) performs substantially worse than SFT on most datasets (e.g., 9.35 vs. 6.37 overall MAE on AgeDB), indicating that its ranking-based RL objective is poorly suited for continuous regression. The two closest MLLM competitors—Regression Reward (Tan et al., 2025) and DISCO MAE Reward (Zhou et al., 2025)—achieve competitive many-shot accuracy but remain 13–16% worse than CCC-GRPO in medium-shot MAE, confirming that point-wise value-level rewards still suffer from regression-to-the-mean. Classical DIR methods (Yang et al., 2021; Gong et al., 2022; Wang & Wang, 2023; Keramati et al.; Pu et al., 2025; Xiong & Yao, 2024; Nie et al.) are reported as reference only since they operate on non-generative CNN regression heads; the best classical method (ConR, 6.81 overall MAE) is still outperformed by CCC-GRPO (5.52) on AgeDB-DIR. Ablations with DrGRPO and RegGRPO variants confirm that the gains stem from the CCC reward design rather than the specific RL optimizer, as adding CCC to any GRPO variant yields consistent improvements.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) as its RL optimizer and repurposes the Concordance Correlation Coefficient (Lawrence & Lin, 1989) as a batch-level reward signal, but its conceptual departure is from two distinct lineages. From classical DIR methods (Yang et al., 2021; Gong et al., 2022; Wang & Wang, 2023), it moves away from explicit regression heads and feature-space regularization toward sequence-level supervision in autoregressive MLLMs. From existing MLLM regression work (architectural modifications like Rex-Omni/GEODE, reasoning-based approaches like OrderChain, and loss-level tweaks like SoftLabel), it avoids all architectural or decoding changes, instead operating purely at the reward level. The key positioning is that prior RL-for-MLLM efforts (Visual-RFT, VLM-R1, Perception-R1) and even frequency-aware reweighting (DISCO) remain point-wise, whereas this paper introduces cross-sample relational comparison as the supervision primitive.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified 129K-sample DIR benchmark with shot-aware evaluation fills a gap in systematic MLLM regression evaluation, and the plug-and-play nature (no architecture change, works with any GRPO variant) makes the approach broadly adoptable. However, the paper is limited to Qwen2.5-VL-3B/7B and four datasets, leaving open whether the batch-level CCC reward generalizes to larger models, more diverse numeric ranges, or multi-turn settings. The acknowledged head-tail trade-off (mild many-shot degradation on IMDB-Movie-DIR, GM increase on BoneAge-DIR) and the fairness concerns in demographic/medical subgroups suggest that distribution-level objectives alone are insufficient for safety-critical deployment. A key open question is whether the batch-level comparison principle can be extended beyond CCC to richer distributional metrics (e.g., Wasserstein, MMD) without the computational overhead of full distribution estimation.
