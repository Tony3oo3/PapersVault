## Problem

When the sampling budget T is smaller than the number of arms N, existing anytime BAI algorithms either assume each arm can be sampled at least once or rely on bracketing strategies that discard information from earlier subsets. In this data-poor regime, the dominant source of error shifts from estimation noise to the algorithm's ability to screen and retain viable candidates, yet no prior method explicitly optimizes this screening capacity while simultaneously maintaining within-set discrimination.

## Input / Output

B3 takes N arms with 1-sub-Gaussian reward distributions and operates without any prior knowledge of the total budget T, producing a valid ε-best arm recommendation at every stopping time. The algorithm introduces no tuning parameters; the sole structural constant r0 ≈ 1.728 is fixed by the theoretical optimality condition r0 + r0^1.5 = 4. The candidate set size c0 = |C| is a deterministic function of T and the algorithm's box structure, and the output is the arm residing at the highest non-empty level Box(L,0) with the largest empirical mean.

## Method

B3 organizes arms into a hierarchy of boxes Box(l,j) indexed by level l and deferment count j, each holding up to three arms. When a box fills, the ARRANGE_BOX procedure applies a ternary rule: the arm with the largest empirical mean is LIFTed (receives ⌈r0^l⌉ additional samples and is promoted to Box(l+1,0)), the median arm is SHIFTed to Box(l,j+1) without additional samples, and the weakest arm is permanently DISCARDED. The main loop alternates between a top-down sweep (prioritizing higher-level boxes for additional sampling) and base-level replenishment (introducing new arms into Box(0,0)). The key insight is that the recursive ternary comparison yields an effective halving rate of 1/2 at the population level (Σ 1/3^j = 1/2), matching Sequential Halving's screening efficiency while preserving information through deferred arms that are re-compared against progressively refined competitor sets. This hierarchical reuse enables B3 to achieve candidate set size c0 = Θ(T) — matching Uniform Sampling's screening capacity — while simultaneously attaining the same within-set misidentification bound as SH with optimal r0, removing the (ln T)² factor present in bracketing-based methods like BSH.

## Results

On the NYCCC 893 dataset (N=5,513 arms, T=10,000 budget), B3 achieves the lowest simple regret across all three noise regimes (high-noise N(μ,0.5²), moderate-noise N(μ,0.2²), and deterministic), outperforming BSH and BUCB by wide margins. In the data-rich regime (N=1,024, T=10,240), B3 attains an average simple regret of 0.0268 without knowing T, compared to 0.1531 for BSH and 0.2558 for BUCB, while remaining within 37% of the fixed-budget SH(r₀=1.7) which knows T (0.0195). Theoretically, B3 removes the sub-optimal (ln T)² factor present in BSH's misidentification bound, achieving exp(−c·Tε²/N) versus BSH's exp(−c·Tε²/(N(ln T)²)).

## Baselines & Comparisons

B3 is compared against three anytime baselines (US, BSH, BUCB) in the main experiments and two fixed-budget baselines (SH, UCB-E) in the appendix. Against BSH, B3 wins decisively: in the data-rich regime (N=1,024), B3 achieves 0.0268 average simple regret versus BSH's 0.1531 (a 5.7× improvement), and B3's theoretical bound removes BSH's (ln T)² penalty. Against BUCB, B3 is even more dominant (0.0268 vs. 0.2558, a 9.5× gap), as BUCB's bracketing strategy retains far fewer candidate arms (c₀ ≪ T). Against US, the comparison is regime-dependent: in the deterministic setting, US matches B3 because both have c₀ ≈ T and within-set misidentification is zero; however, under high or moderate noise, B3 outperforms US because its hierarchical ternary comparisons provide stronger within-set discrimination than US's flat uniform sampling. Against fixed-budget SH (which knows T), B3 is slightly worse (0.0268 vs. 0.0195 for SH with r₀=1.7) but competitive, and notably outperforms SH(r₀=2.0) at 0.0288, confirming that B3's optimal r₀≈1.728 choice is well-calibrated.

## Relation to Prior Work

B3 extends the Sequential Halving (SH) lineage by replacing binary elimination with a ternary comparison mechanism inspired by the remedian estimator from robust statistics, while operating in an anytime fashion without knowledge of the total budget. It directly addresses the data-poor regime studied by BSH (Zhao et al., 2023) and BUCB (Katz-Samuels & Jamieson, 2020), but departs from their bracketing-based approach by introducing an algorithm-dependent 'data-poor condition' tied to the candidate set size c₀ rather than the raw budget T. The key theoretical departure is showing that B3's hierarchical ternary comparisons achieve the same effective halving rate as SH (∑ 1/3^j = 1/2) while maintaining linear screening capacity c₀ ∝ T, in contrast to BSH's c₀ ∝ T/(ln T)².

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decomposition of total error into non-inclusion probability (best arm never enters the candidate set) and within-set misidentification probability provides a practical diagnostic for selecting anytime BAI algorithms: high-noise settings favor strong within-set discrimination, while low-noise settings favor large screening capacity. B3's parameter-free, anytime design with no (ln T)² overhead makes it a robust default for budget-constrained A/B testing and recommendation systems. Open questions include whether retaining and aggregating historical samples across levels (as in Kone et al., 2024) can further reduce estimation variance within B3's box structure, and whether the framework extends to other pure-exploration variants like top-k identification.
