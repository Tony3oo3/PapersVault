## Problem

When LLMs face knowledge conflicts (e.g., retrieved evidence contradicting parametric memory, or distractor narratives in role-play), they can silently drift from the target world-of-discourse toward incompatible priors, producing hallucinations that are locally fluent but globally inconsistent. Existing mitigation methods treat conflicts as token- or proposition-level competition and apply static or heuristic steering, without explicitly modeling the global stability of the hidden-state trajectory relative to a target discourse regime. This paper identifies the underlying failure as 'world misattribution'—a measurable geometric drift in activation space—and asks whether it can be detected early and corrected with minimal intervention.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a structured text prompt organized into three blocks ([SPEC] declaring the target world, [EVIDENCE] providing supporting or conflicting context, [QUESTION] containing the query) and extracts internal hidden states at pre-specified anchor token positions (block-ending markers) in a designated probe layer. It produces a standard autoregressive text response, but with sparse, entropy-gated rescaling of a small set of attention heads applied during decoding. The offline components (a linear world-posterior probe and a 17 MB score network) are trained on ~100 calibration samples per world, and the one-time geometric head-selection analysis takes ~43 minutes; online overhead is a single prefix entropy check plus per-step rescaling of K heads, adding 6–14% latency.

## Method

WET operates in two phases. Offline, it (i) trains a linear probe to estimate the posterior over discrete world labels from anchor states, whose entropy serves as a drift-risk signal, and (ii) trains a lightweight MLP via conditional denoising score matching to learn a world-conditioned vector field pointing toward high-density regions of each world's activation manifold, explicitly handling multi-modality. A geometric sensitivity analysis then identifies sparse tethering heads by counterfactually patching each head's anchor output to a within-world mean and measuring the induced perturbation in the score field (using an ℓ₂ norm to avoid directional cancellation). Online, WET computes the Anchor Entropy on the test prompt; only when this exceeds a threshold does it activate a smooth gate that rescales the top-K tethering heads by a factor proportional to their normalized sensitivity score, nudging the residual stream back toward the target world direction while leaving generation untouched when the model is already stable.

## Results

WET achieves the best performance across all three benchmarks and all eight metrics simultaneously. On TimeChara, WET reaches 69.4% CSC (a 22.4% relative improvement over the strongest baseline INSIDE at 56.7%) and 55.8% TWCC. On NeoQA, WET attains 91.1% accuracy and 92.5% TWCC, outperforming the next-best methods (JUICE at 86.0% Acc; IRCAN at 84.9% TWCC) by 5.1 and 7.6 points respectively. On TriviaQA, WET scores 86.2% EM, 86.8% F1, 88.2% Oracle EM, and 96.9% TWCC, leading the strongest baselines (SelfCheckGPT, IRCAN) by 3–5 points on every metric.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WET outperforms all six baselines on every metric across all three benchmarks, with no single metric where a baseline surpasses it. The closest competitors are JUICE (strongest on NeoQA Acc at 86.0% and TimeChara TWCC at 50.1%) and IRCAN (strongest on TriviaQA F1 at 83.5% and NeoQA TWCC at 84.9%), but WET leads both by 5+ points on their respective best metrics. SelfCheckGPT is the strongest on TriviaQA EM (81.1%) and TWCC (91.8%) among baselines, yet WET still exceeds it by 5.1 and 5.1 points respectively. INSIDE, despite improving TimeChara CSC to 56.7%, shows negligible TWCC gain (35.6% vs. 35.0% original), confirming that generic regularization cannot preserve the active discourse regime; WET's entropy-gated tethering closes this gap to 55.8%. PH3 and ITI are consistently the weakest, with PH3 dropping TimeChara TWCC to 23.9% and ITI degrading TriviaQA TWCC to 76.5%, indicating that untargeted head pruning or static vector injection can actively harm world consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

WET extends the inference-time activation-steering lineage (ITI, PH3, IRCAN, JUICE) by replacing heuristic or dual-run head selection with a principled geometric criterion: conditional denoising score matching learns a world-conditioned vector field, and a norm-based sensitivity analysis identifies sparse tethering heads. It also builds on the probing/monitoring tradition (semantic entropy, truth-direction vectors) but repurposes the probe to measure uncertainty over discrete discourse regimes rather than binary factuality. The key departure is framing knowledge conflicts as trajectory-level drift between separable world manifolds rather than local token- or proposition-level competition, which motivates the entropy-gated, minimal-intrusion intervention policy absent from prior steering methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The geometric framing of hallucination as drift from a target world manifold offers a unifying lens across RAG, role-play, and multi-source QA, and the lightweight inference-time design (17 MB score network, sparse head rescaling, no retraining) makes it practically deployable. However, the approach assumes a small number of discrete, linearly separable worlds and relies on a GPT-4o-mini judge for consistency metrics (96% human agreement), leaving open how it scales to continuous or highly overlapping discourse regimes. Whether the linear-separability premise holds in open-ended, multi-turn agentic settings—and whether the score-matching geometry generalizes beyond the four fictional worlds and three source domains tested—remains an important open question.
