## Problem

Discrete visual tokenizers have been considered inherently inferior to continuous ones for image generation, but this paper shows the gap is primarily due to insufficient bit allocation (compression ratio) rather than the quantization paradigm itself. A second, practical barrier is that standard autoregressive generators with cross-entropy heads become computationally intractable and statistically unreliable when codebook sizes scale beyond ~2^18, preventing discrete methods from exploiting the higher bit budgets needed to match continuous quality.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single ImageNet class label as conditioning input and synthesizes a 256×256 (or 512×512) RGB image. Generation proceeds in a discrete latent space of 256 tokens arranged on a 16×16 grid (for 256×256), where each token is a k-bit binary code (k ranges from 10 to 64 depending on codebook size). The autoregressive transformer processes tokens sequentially, and the MBM head decodes each token's bits through a progressive unmasking schedule of 2–6 steps, with optional classifier-free guidance applied via a linear schedule.

## Method

BAR decomposes autoregressive visual generation into two stages: a causal-attention transformer (built on RAR with RoPE, SwiGLU, RMSNorm) that models global token structure, and a lightweight Masked Bit Modeling (MBM) head (3-layer SwiGLU with adaLN) that predicts each token's k-bit binary representation via iterative unmasking. During training, a random subset of bits in the target token is masked and the head is trained with bit-wise cross-entropy; at inference, bits are revealed progressively according to a configurable unmasking schedule (e.g., [2,2,5,7]). This design reduces prediction-head memory from O(C) to O(log C), enabling arbitrary codebook sizes (up to 2^64) without the OOM failures of linear heads or the quality degradation of naive bit-prediction heads. The tokenizer uses FSQ with a SigLIP2-initialized encoder and DINO-discriminator GAN training, and the paper demonstrates that scaling the codebook to 16 bits per token (65536 total bits) allows the discrete tokenizer to surpass SD-VAE in reconstruction fidelity, which in turn enables BAR-L to achieve a gFID of 0.99 on ImageNet-256.

## Results

On ImageNet-256, BAR-B (415M params) achieves a gFID of 1.13 with classifier-free guidance, surpassing all prior discrete methods (RAR: 1.48, VAR: 1.92, LlamaGen: 2.18) and most continuous diffusion baselines (xAR: 1.24, DDT: 1.26, VA-VAE: 1.35, MAR: 1.55). The larger BAR-L (1.1B params) sets a new state-of-the-art gFID of 0.99, outperforming the closest continuous competitor RAE (1.13) by a clear margin. In terms of sampling throughput, BAR-B achieves 24.33 images/sec—3.68× faster than RAE (6.62) and 20.45× faster than MAR (1.19)—while the efficient variant BAR-B/2 reaches 150.52 images/sec at gFID 1.35, matching MeanFlow's speed with substantially better quality (1.35 vs. 2.20).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BAR-B (415M) decisively outperforms all prior discrete autoregressive methods: it beats RAR (1.48) by 0.35 FID with only 28% of the parameters, VAR (1.92) by 0.79, and LlamaGen (2.18) by 1.05. Against continuous diffusion baselines, BAR-B surpasses xAR (1.24), DDT (1.26), VA-VAE (1.35), and MAR (1.55), while tying RAE at gFID 1.13. The larger BAR-L (1.1B) breaks the tie, achieving gFID 0.99 vs. RAE's 1.13, and also leads without guidance (1.42 vs. 1.51). In sampling speed, BAR-B is 3.68× faster than RAE, 11.99× faster than xAR, and 20.45× faster than MAR; the efficient BAR-B/2 variant (150.52 img/s, gFID 1.35) matches MeanFlow's throughput (151.48 img/s) while delivering 0.85 FID better quality, and BAR-B/4 (445.48 img/s) is 2.94× faster than MeanFlow at comparable quality (2.34 vs. 2.20). The only baselines BAR does not clearly beat are pixel-space methods SiD2 (1.38) and JiT (1.82) in raw FID, though BAR-B still outperforms both.

## Relation to Prior Work

BAR builds on the RAR autoregressive generation framework and the FSQ quantizer, but its core departure is the Masked Bit Modeling (MBM) head, which replaces the standard linear prediction head with a progressive bit-wise unmasking mechanism inspired by MaskGIT's iterative decoding. Conceptually, the paper repositions the discrete-vs-continuous debate by introducing a unified 'Bit Budget' metric, showing that prior discrete methods (MaskBit, Infinity, VAR, LlamaGen) were handicapped by low compression ratios rather than by an inherent limitation of quantization. Unlike MaskBit, which still predicts codebook indices, and Infinity, which relies on an external bit-corrector, BAR is fully self-contained and compatible with arbitrary autoregressive formulations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that discrete tokenizers can match or surpass continuous ones under comparable bit budgets, this paper challenges the field's default assumption that diffusion-based continuous pipelines are necessary for high-fidelity generation. The MBM head provides a practical, lightweight solution to the vocabulary scaling bottleneck that has capped codebook sizes in prior discrete generators, and the resulting 3.7×–20× sampling speedups over leading continuous methods make high-quality generation substantially more accessible. Open questions include whether the bit-budget analysis and MBM head transfer to open-ended text-to-image or video generation, whether the advantage persists at higher resolutions (512+), and whether the FSQ-based tokenizer's reliance on a frozen SigLIP2 encoder limits its generality beyond ImageNet-style classification.
