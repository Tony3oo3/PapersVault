## Problem

Activation steering methods for LLMs consistently underperform prompt-based steering, yet the reasons for this gap remain unclear. The paper identifies that popular activation steering approaches apply a constant intervention strength across all token positions, which is fundamentally unfaithful to how prompt steering actually operates—prompt steering exerts strong interventions on some tokens (e.g., sentence boundaries, branching points) while barely affecting others. The central question is whether distilling the token-specific mechanics of prompt steering into a simpler, interpretable activation intervention can close this performance gap.

## Input / Output

At inference, the PSR model consumes the residual stream activations at one or more transformer layers, a fixed steering vector z_attr (obtained via difference-in-means or probe weights), and a user-set global steering coefficient α that controls overall attribute strength. The token-specific coefficient λ is computed on-the-fly from the activations themselves via a ReLU probe, so no additional prompt tokens are needed. The output is the modified residual stream activation, which is fed forward to produce attribute-aligned text. Training data consists of (x, x', y') triplets—original prompt, steered prompt, and the LLM's response to the steered prompt—filtered to retain only successful steering instances as judged by an LLM-based attribute and coherence scorer.

## Method

The paper formulates prompt steering as a form of activation steering (Equation 3), showing that the difference between prompt-steered and unsteered activations defines the intervention Δ_PS. It then proves that constant activation steering (A + αz) is a special case that requires two assumptions: a single steering direction and uniform intervention magnitude across tokens. The key relaxation (Assumption 3.2a) replaces the uniform-magnitude assumption with a learned function of the activations, yielding the PSR architecture: A_steered = A + α·λ(A; θ)·z_attr, where λ is a single-layer ReLU probe (λ = ReLU(A·w + b)) that estimates the token-specific steering coefficient. Two variants are proposed: S-PSR intervenes in a single layer, while A-PSR applies the intervention at every layer iteratively. Training uses either MSE between PSR-steered and prompt-steered activations (across the target layer and all subsequent layers) or loglikelihood of the steered response, with a regularization term preventing the ReLU from dying. The model is trained only on positive examples where prompt steering successfully elicited the target attribute, effectively distilling successful prompt-steering behavior into a lightweight rank-1 intervention.

## Results

On the Persona Vectors benchmark, A-PSR_MSE achieves trait alignment at prompt-steering coherence (TA@C_p) of 92.5 (Llama-3.2-3B), 96.4 (Llama-3.1-8B), and 83.9 (Qwen2.5-7B), outperforming prompt steering (91.5, 95.7, 71.6) on all three models. On AxBench, A-PSR_MSE sets a new state-of-the-art with steering scores of 0.871 (Gemma-2-2B L20) and 1.120 (Gemma-2-9B L20), surpassing prompt steering (0.731, 1.075) and HyperSteer (0.742, 1.091). On IFEval, PSR does not consistently outperform prompting (e.g., S-PSR_LL achieves 62.8% IF accuracy vs. 72.5% for prompting on Phi-3-mini), though combining PSR with prompting yields the best results (89.8% on Phi-3-mini).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Chen et al. (2025) (S-Const_DiM|R on Persona Vectors), PSR achieves a dramatic improvement: S-PSR_MSE reaches 91.1 TA@C_80 on Llama-3.2-3B versus 46.1 for the difference-in-means baseline, and A-PSR_MSE reaches 92.5 TA@C_p versus 28.9. Against Stolfo et al. (2025) on IFEval, S-PSR_LL substantially outperforms their method (62.8% vs. 30.1% IF accuracy on Phi-3-mini; 66.1% vs. 28.9% on Gemma-2-9B), though neither consistently beats prompting alone. On AxBench, A-PSR_MSE outperforms Wu et al. (2025a) baselines (prompt: 1.075, LoRA: 0.602 on 9B) and Wu et al. (2025b) methods (LoReFT-RePS: 0.757 on 9B), achieving 1.120. It also surpasses HyperSteer (Sun et al., 2025) by 0.029 on 9B (1.120 vs. 1.091) and by 0.129 on 2B (0.871 vs. 0.742). The one setting where PSR does not win is IFEval instruction-following accuracy against prompting, where rank-1 interventions are insufficient for complex multi-argument instructions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the constant activation steering family (ActAdd, CAA, ITI, ReFT-R1) by showing that its core assumption—applying a uniform steering coefficient across all token positions—is unfaithful to how prompt steering actually operates within the model. The key conceptual move is reframing prompt steering itself as a form of activation steering with token-specific interventions, then distilling those interventions into a lightweight ReLU-probe-based module (PSR) trained to match prompt-steered activations. This builds on Dherin et al.'s observation that prompting, activation steering, and PEFT are all low-rank weight updates, and on recent per-token steering work (Stolfo et al., Nguyen et al.) that also relaxes the constant-coefficient assumption, but differs in training objective (matching prompt steering activations rather than enforcing uniform projection) and in providing a faithfulness analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled diagnostic for why existing activation steering underperforms prompting: the interventions are structurally unfaithful to the model's own steering mechanics. The practical upshot is that PSR models, particularly A-PSR_MSE, close much of the gap and set a new SOTA on AxBench, while the faithfulness analysis (Section 5.2) reveals that the model can partially self-correct from unfaithful interventions in later layers—a useful insight for designing future steering methods. A key limitation is that rank-1 interventions cannot capture complex multi-constraint instructions (IFEval), pointing toward low-rank PSR variants as a natural next step. The framework also opens the door to using PSR as an interpretability tool: S-PSR reveals how an attribute is represented at a given layer, while A-PSR illuminates how that representation is computed across depth.
