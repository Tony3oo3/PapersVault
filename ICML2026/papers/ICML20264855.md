## Problem

Trajectory-level policy optimization for long-horizon language agents suffers from two compounding failures: credit misattribution, where correct early actions receive contradictory gradient signals depending on downstream stochasticity (over 40% of updates conflict), and sample inefficiency, where 39–47% of trajectories complete meaningful subgoals yet receive zero reward identical to complete failures. Existing denser-credit methods (process reward models, Monte Carlo estimation, state-recurrence grouping) introduce annotation costs, sampling overhead, or dependence on incidental state repetition that diminishes in long-horizon settings.

## Input / Output

The agent consumes text-based environment observations and a natural-language task instruction, producing a sequence of text actions through multi-turn interaction. A milestone indicator Φ operates on consecutive state transitions (s_t, a_t, s_{t+1}) to detect subgoal completion from observable environment feedback (e.g., object state changes in ALFWorld, page transitions in WebShop, explicit subgoal signals in ScienceWorld) without learned models or manual annotation. The environment provides only a sparse binary terminal reward R(τ) ∈ {0,1}; all intermediate credit is synthesized by BEACON's shaping and advantage estimation.

## Method

BEACON partitions each trajectory into segments at milestone boundaries, then applies temporal reward shaping within each completed segment (r_t = R_ms · γ^(t_k − t)) so that actions closer to milestone completion receive higher credit, converting sparse terminal signals into dense feedback that rewards partial progress. For advantage estimation, BEACON computes a dual-scale signal: a trajectory-level advantage (GRPO-style, comparing terminal outcomes across the group) captures global task performance, while a segment-level advantage compares per-step returns only among trajectories that reached the same milestone, isolating local action quality from downstream variance. Under the Milestone Markov Property (future segments depend on the milestone state, not full history), Proposition 3.2 guarantees that segment-level advantages are approximately uncorrelated with later segment returns, directly preventing credit misattribution. The final advantage is a weighted sum (λ = 1.0) of both scales, optimized with a standard clipped PPO surrogate objective.

## Results

On ALFWorld with Qwen2.5-1.5B, BEACON achieves 91.4% average success rate, surpassing GiGPO (86.1%) by 5.3 points and GRPO (72.8%) by 18.6 points. The advantage is most pronounced on long-horizon tasks (L* > 7), where BEACON reaches 92.9% versus GRPO's 53.5%—nearly a doubling. On WebShop, BEACON achieves 75.6% success (vs. 65.0% GiGPO, 56.8% GRPO), and on ScienceWorld it reaches 45.3% success (vs. 25.8% GiGPO, 21.1% GRPO). Effective sample utilization improves from 23.7% (GRPO) to 82.0% (BEACON), a 3.5× increase in trajectories providing useful gradient signal.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BEACON outperforms all eight baselines across every benchmark and both model scales (1.5B and 7B). Against the strongest RL baseline GiGPO, BEACON leads by 5.3 points on ALFWorld (91.4% vs. 86.1%) and 10.6 points on WebShop success (75.6% vs. 65.0%), with the gap widening on longer horizons (92.9% vs. 79.5% on ALFWorld Long). Against GRPO, the advantage is most dramatic on long-horizon ALFWorld tasks: 92.9% vs. 53.5%, a 73.6% relative gain, while on short tasks the gap is only 26.2% relative. On ScienceWorld, BEACON nearly doubles GiGPO's success rate (45.3% vs. 25.8%) and more than doubles GRPO's (45.3% vs. 21.1%). Notably, the 1.5B BEACON model surpasses both GPT-4o (48.0% ALFWorld avg) and Gemini-2.5-Pro (60.3%) under ReAct prompting, demonstrating that milestone-anchored credit assignment provides gains that model scale alone cannot match. PPO and RLOO trail BEACON by 37 and 22 points respectively on ALFWorld average, while prompting-only baselines (ReAct, Reflexion) remain far below all RL-trained methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

BEACON extends the group-based policy optimization lineage (GRPO, RLOO) by replacing flat trajectory-level credit assignment with milestone-anchored segment-level comparison. It departs from GiGPO's reliance on state recurrence for step-level grouping by instead exploiting the compositional structure of long-horizon tasks—verifiable subgoal completions that exhibit an approximate Markov property. Unlike process reward models or Monte Carlo value estimation, BEACON requires no learned critics, no step-level annotations, and no additional rollouts, relying solely on observable environment feedback to identify milestone boundaries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The near-doubling of success rate on long-horizon ALFWorld tasks (92.9% vs 53.5%) and the 3.5× improvement in effective sample utilization address two bottlenecks that have limited RL training of language agents as task complexity grows. Key limitations include the need for task-specific milestone detectors (pattern matching, page transitions, or explicit subgoal signals), which restricts applicability to environments with clear compositional structure and verifiable state changes. Open questions include automated milestone discovery, extension to continuous control or multi-agent settings, and whether the approach generalizes to tasks where subgoal boundaries are ambiguous or where resources carry across segments.
