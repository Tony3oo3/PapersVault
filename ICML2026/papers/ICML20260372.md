## Problem

Existing unlearnable example (UE) methods concentrate their effective perturbation signals in high-frequency components, causing their protective capability to collapse under simple low-pass filtering. This paper identifies this critical spectral vulnerability and argues that reliable UEs must remain effective across the full frequency spectrum so that no residual band can be exploited for semantic learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260372/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a clean RGB image (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100/SVHN, 224×224 for ImageNet) and a frozen surrogate classifier, and produces a perturbed image x' = x + δ where δ is generated by a lightweight encoder-decoder network (0.09M parameters) bounded by ‖δ‖∞ ≤ 8/255. The perturbation is designed to be spectrum-agnostic, meaning it degrades learnability regardless of which frequency bands are subsequently suppressed by filtering or compression.

## Method

FUSE combines two complementary mechanisms. First, Random Spectral Masking (RSM) applies a randomly sampled contiguous frequency-band dropout in the Fourier domain at each training step, forcing the perturbation to remain unlearnable regardless of which band is removed; this is paired with a spectral entropy regularizer that penalizes concentration of perturbation energy in narrow frequency regions. Second, Cross-Band Guidance (CBG) decomposes both the clean and perturbed images into low- and high-frequency components via a circular Fourier mask at radius r_c=0.5, then enforces mutual consistency: the high-frequency branch's classifier prediction serves as a soft target for the low-frequency branch (semantic guidance), while a hybrid cosine+SSIM similarity between clean and perturbed components is matched across bands with a stop-gradient (structural alignment). The full objective L_full = L_RSM + L_CBG is optimized jointly with the perturbation generator under the ε=8/255 budget, using a small U-Net-style generator with residual bottleneck blocks.

## Results

Under low-pass filtering (cutoff 0.5) on CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18, FUSE reduces test accuracy to 10.13%, compared to 54.16% (LSP), 52.16% (TUE), 41.97% (GUE), 23.56% (EMN), and 23.60% (PUE). In the unfiltered setting, FUSE achieves 9.41% on CIFAR-10/ResNet-18 and 1.10% on CIFAR-100/ResNet-18, both near random-guessing levels. On SVHN with ViT, FUSE reaches 6.55% unfiltered accuracy, approaching the 10% random-guess floor for a ten-class task. FUSE also maintains near-random accuracy (10.06–15.52%) across seven diverse target architectures (ResNet-50, VGG-11, DenseNet-121, ViT, ConvNeXt, MLP-Mixer, Swin-T) in cross-architecture transfer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FUSE dominates all five primary UE baselines (EMN, LSP, TUE, GUE, PUE) under low-pass filtering, where the gap is most dramatic: on CIFAR-10/ResNet-18, FUSE achieves 10.13% versus 54.16% for LSP and 52.16% for TUE, a gap of over 40 percentage points. Under unfiltered conditions, FUSE still leads but by smaller margins (9.41% vs. 10.03% for TUE and 10.62% for PUE on CIFAR-10), indicating that the spectral-agnostic design provides the largest advantage precisely when high-frequency perturbations are suppressed. Against the two model-free baselines AR and OPS (evaluated under defense strategies in Table 5), FUSE maintains 9.41–14.35% accuracy across Cutout, CutMix, Mixup, and Adversarial Training, while OPS collapses to 61.68–76.40% under Cutout/CutMix and AR degrades to 11.75–14.59%. Under UE-specific defenses (D-VAE, AN-SDA, ORTHO PROJ in Table 6), FUSE achieves 12.23%, 25.07%, and 10.50% respectively, whereas most baselines recover to 64–92% accuracy. FUSE also outperforms all baselines in cross-architecture transfer (Table 2), achieving the lowest accuracy on all seven target networks including non-standard architectures like ConvNeXt (10.06%) and MLP-Mixer (10.11%), where TUE and PUE degrade to 15.35% and 13.06% respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unlearnable examples (UE) literature (EMN, LSP, TUE, GUE, PUE) by identifying a shared frequency-domain vulnerability: all existing methods concentrate perturbation energy in high frequencies, causing unlearnability to collapse under simple low-pass filtering. Rather than building on a single prior method, FUSE repurposes signal-processing concepts—Fourier-domain masking, spectral entropy regularization, and cross-band structural consistency—into a unified generator training objective. The work also connects to frequency-domain adversarial robustness analysis (Li et al., 2024a) and the broader observation that DNNs exploit spectral shortcuts (Geirhos et al., 2018; 2020), positioning spectral balance as a necessary condition for robust data protection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that a single low-pass filter (cutoff 0.5) recovers 50%+ accuracy from 'protected' data across all major UE methods is a significant vulnerability finding that should prompt re-evaluation of UE-based privacy claims in deployed systems. FUSE's spectrum-agnostic design addresses this gap with a practical, lightweight generator (0.09M params, 4ms inference) that also extends to vision-language models (CLIP), JPEG compression, and cross-dataset transfer. Open questions include whether adaptive or learned spectral defenses can still neutralize full-spectrum perturbations, whether the approach generalizes to video or 3D data, and whether the CLIP results (4–6% accuracy drop) are sufficient for real-world multimodal privacy protection.
