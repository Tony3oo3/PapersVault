## Problem

RLVR scaling is bottlenecked by the scarcity of verifiable training data, as strict format requirements limit usable problems to those with automatically checkable solutions (e.g., math verifiers, sandboxed unit tests). Reasoning-rich corpora such as science textbooks, forum discussions, and coding problems lacking test cases are systematically excluded from prior RLVR pipelines, and models increasingly saturate on finite verifiable datasets, with only ~25% of ProRL's 136K samples still providing learning signal after prolonged training.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes raw source text (AoPS forum solutions, MegaScience textbook QA, rStar-Coder solutions without test cases, or noisy FineWeb scrapes) and produces a multiple-choice fill-in-the-middle task consisting of a masked passage with 10 options (1 ground-truth span + 9 distractors) presented in randomized order. For noisy sources, an intermediate LLM extraction step first distills a coherent, educationally valuable passage before masking is applied. Verification during RL is a simple exact-match check between the model's selected option letter and the ground-truth answer, requiring no external judge or sandbox.

## Method

The core trick is converting unverifiable text into verifiable MCQ tasks by masking a contiguous span of crucial reasoning steps and generating plausible distractors around it. GPT-5 is prompted to identify the masked span (which becomes the ground-truth answer) and produce at least 10 diverse distractors that are similar in style and length to the removed content but incorrect in context. For noisy web scrapes, a preliminary extraction step first summarizes the source into a coherent passage, and difficulty-based filtering then removes problems where the student model succeeds across all 16 rollouts. Nine distractors (10 total options) are specifically chosen to place problems in a medium-difficulty regime where both successful and failed rollouts occur, avoiding the elimination-strategy shortcut that plagues 3-option formats. The resulting GooseReason-0.7M dataset (0.7M+ tasks across math, code, and STEM) is seamlessly plugged into standard RLVR training using ProRLv2 (a GRPO variant with REINFORCE++ decoupled advantage normalization), requiring no modifications to the RL algorithm itself.

## Results

On Qwen3-4B-Instruct, adding GooseReason-0.7M for 270 additional RL steps after ProRL saturation yields 70.00 on AIME24, 63.96 on AIME25, and 89.16 on AMC, versus 62.29/55.21/87.65 when continuing with ProRL data alone (which actually degrades). On coding, the average improves from 53.89 (ProRL-only) to 55.70 (with GooseReason), and on GPQA Diamond from 62.87 to 66.79. In the cybersecurity domain, GooseReason-Cyber-4B achieves 78.99 average across CTI-Bench, CyberMetric, and SecEval after only 100 RL steps, surpassing the 8B Llama-Primus-Instruct (67.48) by 11.51 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the original ProRL data blend (Liu et al., 2025a), GooseReason-0.7M clearly wins: continuing ProRL-only training for 156 additional steps causes math to drop by 4.17 points (AIME24: 66.46→62.29) and STEM (GPQA) to drop by 1.52 points, while adding GooseReason-0.7M for 270 steps recovers and surpasses the peak (AIME24: 70.00, GPQA: 66.79). The paper reports absolute gains of 2.71% (math), 2.12% (coding), and 3.48% (STEM) for ProRL-1.5B-v2 with GooseReason versus 0.63%, 0.95%, and 0.13% with ProRL-only. Against RLVE (Zeng et al., 2025a), which uses procedurally generated adaptive environments, GooseReason is competitive on math but substantially outperforms on STEM (3.48% vs. 0.62% gain) and coding, where RLVE's handcrafted environments struggle to cover knowledge-intensive domains. In the cybersecurity domain, GooseReason-Cyber-4B (78.99 avg) decisively beats Llama-Primus-Instruct (67.48 avg, Yu et al., 2025) by 11.51 points despite being a 4B general model versus an 8B domain-specialized model that underwent extensive domain-specific pre-training and post-training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a data-centric complement to algorithmic RLVR scaling efforts like ProRL, BroRL, and ScaleRL, which focus on training recipes and rollout budgets. It departs from two prior data-synthesis paradigms: (1) human-annotated verifiable problems (Big-Math, Omni-Math, rStar-Coder), which are expensive and domain-limited, and (2) procedurally generated environments (Reasoning Gym, Reasoning Core, RLVE, SCALE-R), which require handcrafted verifiers and struggle to cover knowledge-intensive domains like medicine or cybersecurity. The key conceptual move is reframing unverifiable text as a source of verifiable tasks via a simple MCQ fill-in-the-middle transformation, bypassing the need for domain-specific verifiers or test-case generation entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and growing bottleneck: RLVR training saturates on finite verifiable data, and the gap is widest in STEM and specialized domains where human-annotated data is scarce. By demonstrating that a 4B model trained for only 100 RL steps on synthesized cybersecurity data surpasses a 7B domain-specialized model with extensive pre- and post-training, the work suggests a scalable path to domain-specific reasoning without expensive expert curation. Open questions include whether the MCQ format constrains the depth of reasoning the model can acquire (vs. open-ended generation), whether difficulty-based filtering is sufficient to prevent the model from learning surface-level pattern matching, and whether the approach generalizes to truly open-ended domains like legal reasoning or medical diagnosis where 'correct' answers are more ambiguous.
