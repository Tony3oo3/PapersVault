## Problem

Fine-tuning large vision-language models on long-tailed data leads to rapid overfitting on head classes while impairing tail class learning, and existing long-tailed techniques either require explicit distribution priors (per-class counts, imbalance ratios) that are unavailable in downstream LVLM scenarios like VQA, or are designed for classification rather than the next-token-prediction generation paradigm used by LVLMs.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard LVLM inputs: an RGB image encoded into N visual tokens by a vision encoder, concatenated with a text prompt, and produces either a class name (for classification, evaluated by exact string match) or a free-form text answer (for VQA, evaluated by multiple-choice accuracy). The key distinction from standard fine-tuning is that both the input visual token sequence and the output token sequence are modified during training: low-entropy visual tokens are stochastically removed from the input, and high-confidence output tokens are filtered from the loss while low-confidence ones are reweighted.

## Method

ATR introduces two complementary token-level operations. On the output side, the Bounded Adaptive Loss (BAL) combines (a) dynamic filtering of high-confidence tokens via an adaptively increasing threshold τ (updated per-token based on prediction confidence, Eq. 5) that excludes well-learned head tokens from the loss, and (b) adaptive reweighting of low-confidence tokens with a bounded factor w_t = min(1, ℓ_max/(−log P_t + ε)) that prevents sharp optimization and spurious correlation learning. On the input side, the Visual Token Mask (VTM) strategy computes per-token generation entropy for each visual token and randomly masks tokens below the k-th percentile of entropy with probability m, forcing the model to rely on more informative visual features rather than simplistic cues. The entire framework requires no distribution priors and is compatible with standard PEFT methods (LoRA, DoRA) and accelerated training (FlashAttention).

## Results

On ImageNet-LT, ATR (Qwen2.5-VL-7B + LoRA) achieves 84.1% overall accuracy (Head 90.2%, Medium 82.8%, Tail 71.5%), surpassing the best CLIP-based long-tailed method SynBalance (77.5%) by 6.6 points and the standard SFT baseline (78.2%) by 5.9 points. On Place-LT, ATR reaches 51.4% overall accuracy (Head 58.3%, Medium 49.2%, Tail 43.5%), outperforming SFT (43.8%) by 7.6 points and the prior-based Reweight baseline (47.4%) by 4.0 points. On VQA tasks (IconQA, ScienceQA) with Qwen2.5-VL-7B + LoRA, ATR achieves 77.3% and 76.1% average (finetune + generalization) accuracy respectively, improving over FisherSFT (74.9% / 73.8%) and MinorSFT (74.6% / 74.5%) by 2.4 and 1.6 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ATR outperforms all 12 compared baselines across both ImageNet-LT and Place-LT. Against traditional long-tailed recognition methods (cRT, MiSLAS, DisAlign, LiVT), ATR leads by 23–37 points in overall accuracy, reflecting the advantage of leveraging a powerful LVLM backbone. Among CLIP-based vision-language methods, ATR surpasses the strongest competitor SynBalance (Jiang et al., 2025) by 6.6 points on ImageNet-LT (84.1 vs. 77.5) and by 0.9 points on Place-LT (51.4 vs. 50.5), while also achieving substantially higher head accuracy (90.2 vs. 81.9 on ImageNet-LT), indicating that ATR does not sacrifice head performance to boost tail. Against the prior-based Reweight baseline (Du et al., 2023), ATR gains 4.8 and 4.0 points on ImageNet-LT and Place-LT respectively, demonstrating that explicit distribution priors are not necessary. Among LLM fine-tuning loss functions, ATR beats FisherSFT (Deb et al., 2025) by 3.6 points and MinorSFT (Xie et al., 2024) by 3.5 points on ImageNet-LT overall, with even larger margins on Place-LT (+4.2 and +6.0). On VQA tasks, ATR consistently improves over both FisherSFT and MinorSFT by 1.1–2.4 points in average accuracy across IconQA and ScienceQA with both LLaVA-1.5-7B and Qwen2.5-VL-7B architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of long-tailed learning and LVLM fine-tuning by operating at the token level rather than the sample or class level. It departs from traditional long-tailed recognition methods (cRT, MiSLAS, VL-LTR, etc.) that require explicit distribution priors, and from recent LLM fine-tuning loss modifications (FisherSFT, MinorSFT) that are not designed for imbalanced data. The bounded adaptive loss repurposes the focal-loss intuition of down-weighting easy examples but applies it adaptively at the token level with a dynamically increasing threshold, while the visual token mask strategy borrows from BERT-style masking but is guided by token entropy rather than applied uniformly. The paper positions itself as the first framework to address long-tailed LVLM fine-tuning in the absence of distribution priors, a gap it identifies between classification-oriented long-tailed methods and generative LVLM fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical relevance is significant: real-world LVLM deployments in e-commerce, medical diagnosis, and VQA inherently face long-tailed data without accessible distribution statistics, making prior-free methods essential. The token-level granularity of ATR (filtering output tokens, masking input visual tokens) offers a more fine-grained control than sample-level reweighting, and the approach is lightweight (only ~0.5h additional training time) and compatible with FlashAttention. Limitations include evaluation only on 7B models with two PEFT methods, weaker gains on Place-LT (attributed to the backbone's zero-shot weakness), and the fact that most CLIP-based competitors use additional synthetic data. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger models, whether it extends to multi-modal long-tail (both visual and textual tails), and how it interacts with RLHF or DPO-style alignment.
