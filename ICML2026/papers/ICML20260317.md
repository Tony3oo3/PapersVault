## Problem

Gated linear attention models use fixed-size memory states for in-context learning, but this finite capacity leads to catastrophic interference: new key-value pairs overwrite old ones (catastrophic forgetting), while overly concentrated posteriors prevent the model from integrating new information (catastrophic remembering). Existing metaplasticity methods from continual learning are unsuitable for transformers because they require demarcated tasks, growing memory, or lack differentiability, and current gated linear models (Mamba2, Deltanet, Longhorn) use a constant plasticity rate across all memory states, preventing true per-synapse metaplasticity.

## Input / Output

The model consumes an autoregressive text token sequence and produces next-token predictions, maintaining a fixed-size attention state S ∈ R^{d_v × d_k} that is updated in-place at each step regardless of sequence length. A second state, the importance/precision matrix I ∈ R^{d_v × d_k}, is tracked alongside the mean state to provide per-synapse learning rates. The output at each step is y_t = μ_t q_t, where μ_t is the posterior mean of the attention state. Unlike standard transformers that cache all KV pairs (growing linearly with sequence length), Palimpsa's state size is constant, making it suitable for edge deployment and long-context processing.

## Method

Palimpsa formulates self-attention as minimizing a variational free energy at test time, decomposed into three terms: a plasticity term (fitting new key-value data), a forgetting term (pulling toward a prior to prevent catastrophic remembering), and a stability term (penalizing deviation from the previous posterior, weighted by per-synapse uncertainty). The key mechanism is a dual-state update where the importance state I_t (a diagonal precision matrix) evolves as I_t = α_t I_{t-1} + (1−α_t) I_prior + β_t ⊗ k_t², and the mean state updates as μ_t = α_t (I_{t-1}/I_t) ⊙ μ_{t-1} + (1/I_t) ⊙ [(β_t ⊙ v_t) ⊗ k_t], where the ratio I_{t-1}/I_t acts as a per-synapse learning rate implementing metaplasticity. The forgetting gate α_t = exp(−Ad_t) is input-dependent and defines an effective memory window N_t, while β_t ∈ R^{d_v} provides per-row input gating. The paper proves Mamba2 is the asymptotic special case where forgetting dominates (I_t ≈ I_prior), enabling a practical pipeline: pre-train with fast Mamba2 kernels, then fine-tune into Palimpsa by gradually increasing β_t via a learnable b_scale parameter. The update is computed efficiently via a chunk-wise associative scan in fused Triton kernels, achieving speeds comparable to Mamba1.

## Results

At the 760M parameter scale, Palimpsa-D (fine-tuned) achieves 52.27% average commonsense reasoning accuracy, outperforming Gated Deltanet (51.50%) and Transformer++ (51.28%). At 2.7B parameters, the surgically upgraded Palimpsa-M variant improves average accuracy by +0.84 points over the Mamba2 baseline (60.06% vs 59.22%) and gains +1.1 points on LongBench overall. On the MQAR synthetic benchmark, metaplasticity consistently improves recall accuracy over non-metaplastic ablations, with the performance gap widening as sequence length increases from 128 to 1024 tokens. On RULER Variable Tracing at 32k context, Palimpsa achieves 24.4% vs 14.9% for the Mamba2 baseline, confirming superior long-range memory retention.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Gated Deltanet at 760M parameters, Palimpsa-D (fine-tuned) wins by +0.77 points in average commonsense reasoning accuracy (52.27% vs 51.50%), with the largest gap on LAMBADA perplexity (14.86 vs 17.06), confirming superior long-range context retention. Against Transformer++ at the same scale, Palimpsa-D (fine-tuned) leads by +0.99 points in average accuracy while also achieving lower WikiText perplexity (19.02 vs 18.91 is a near-tie, but LAMBADA PPL is substantially better at 14.86 vs 18.84). Against the Mamba2 baseline at 2.7B parameters, the surgically upgraded Palimpsa-M variant gains +0.84 points in average accuracy (60.06% vs 59.22%) and +1.1 points on LongBench overall, with the advantage most pronounced in the Medium context category (32k–128k). However, on RULER Variable Tracing at shorter contexts (4k–16k), the metaplastic variant underperforms the Mamba2 baseline (e.g., 33.5% vs 59.6% at 4k), only surpassing it at 32k (24.4% vs 14.9%), indicating that metaplasticity trades short-context performance for significantly improved long-context retention. On the MQAR synthetic benchmark, metaplasticity consistently improves over non-metaplastic ablations across all sequence lengths, with the gap widening as task difficulty increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

Palimpsa extends the Bayesian continual learning framework of MESU (Bonnet et al., 2025) into the attention mechanism, introducing per-state metaplasticity that dynamically adjusts each synapse's learning rate based on tracked importance. It departs from Longhorn (which uses a fixed plasticity rate per row) and Mesanet (which requires iterative conjugate-gradient inversion of the full covariance) by adopting a diagonal precision approximation that enables vector-valued input gating and scalable GPU kernels. The paper's key theoretical contribution is showing that Mamba2, Gated Deltanet, Longhorn, and Mesanet all emerge as specific posterior approximations or limiting cases of a single variational free energy objective, unifying previously disjoint architectures under one Bayesian framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical pipeline of pre-training with fast Mamba2 kernels and then fine-tuning into metaplastic Palimpsa layers offers a low-cost path to expand the effective memory of existing state-space models without retraining from scratch, validated at 2.7B parameters with surgical replacement of just 8 layers. However, the 3× training slowdown relative to Mamba2, the finding that fully training Palimpsa from scratch underperforms its fine-tuned counterpart, and the unexplained degradation on aggregation tasks (CWE/FWE) in RULER remain open questions. The framework also naturally provides output uncertainty estimates that could be leveraged for downstream calibration, a direction the authors flag for future work.
