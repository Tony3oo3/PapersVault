## Problem

State-of-the-art DP-SGD implementations for sequential data (e.g., LLMs) require per-sample gradient clipping with memory overhead O(B·min{T², d²}), where T is context length and d is layer width. This quadratic scaling becomes prohibitive as frontier LLMs push context lengths to O(100K) and model widths grow, limiting the practical deployment of differentially private training for large language models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264519/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequential text dataset, LLM parameters (specifically linear-like layers of shape (p,d) with d < p), a small projection dimension k (≈32 in experiments), and a noise multiplier σ. It produces a differentially private fine-tuned model with a formal (ε, δ)-DP guarantee computed via a PRV-based accountant that incorporates the randomized clipping's envelope distribution. The projection is applied along the larger dimension of each layer, so a p×d layer and a d×p layer incur identical overhead.

## Method

DP-SGD-RC replaces exact per-sample gradient norm computation with stochastic trace estimation: the norm ||A^T G||_F² is rewritten as trace(G^T A A^T G) and estimated via Hutchinson's estimator (random Gaussian projections P ∈ R^{p×k}) or its improved variant Hutch++, reducing memory from O(B·min{d², T²}) to O(BkT + pk) with no asymptotic overhead over non-private training. The privacy analysis shows that randomized clipping induces a random effective noise scale, so the single-step tradeoff function involves an envelope CDF over convex combinations of chi-squared random variables, characterized via stochastic orders and majorization theory. An efficient PRV-based accountant composes these envelope distributions across steps with subsampling amplification. Empirically, fine-tuning Llama 3.2 1B at context length 4096 achieves up to 40% peak memory reduction and 2× compute savings on the largest linear layer while matching DP-SGD utility across classification, summarization, and question-answering tasks.

## Results

On full fine-tuning of Llama 3.2 1B with projection dimension k=32, DP-SGD-RC achieves 96.40% accuracy on BBC (ε=9) and 95.60% (ε=2), matching or exceeding DP-SGD(FGC) (96.33% and 94.06% respectively). On BillSum (ROUGE-1) and HotpotQA (Exact Match), DP-SGD-RC trails DP-SGD(FGC) by only 0.0018 and 0.04% at ε=9, and 0.0035 and 0.04% at ε=2. Across all settings, the method reduces peak memory by 15–40% and FLOPs by up to 98% for the largest linear layer (8192×2048) compared to the DP-SGD(FGC) baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole quantitative baseline is DP-SGD with Fast Gradient Clipping (FGC; Lee & Kifer, 2021). On BBC classification, DP-SGD-RC actually outperforms DP-SGD(FGC) by 0.07% at ε=9 (96.40% vs. 96.33%) and by a more substantial 1.54% at ε=2 (95.60% vs. 94.06%), suggesting the stochastic norm estimation acts as a mild regularizer. On BillSum summarization, DP-SGD-RC is slightly behind DP-SGD(FGC) by 0.0018 ROUGE-1 at ε=9 and 0.0035 at ε=2. On HotpotQA, the gap is 0.02% EM at ε=9 and 0.04% at ε=2 — both within the reported standard deviations. The practical advantage of DP-SGD-RC is not in utility but in efficiency: it reduces peak memory by up to 40% (39.18% for the 8192×2048 layer at k=32) and FLOPs by 98% for that layer, while requiring only one forward pass and two backward passes (same as FGC) rather than k+1 forward passes needed by the closest prior method, DP-SGD-JL.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DP-SGD lineage (Bassily et al., 2014; Abadi et al., 2016) by replacing the exact per-sample gradient norm computation in Fast Gradient Clipping (Lee & Kifer, 2021) and Ghost Clipping (Li et al., 2021) with stochastic trace estimation via Hutchinson's estimator and Hutch++. The most closely related work is DP-SGD-JL (Bu et al., 2021), which uses Johnson-Lindenstrauss projections for norm estimation; this paper generalizes that approach from d=1 to d>1 (factored matrix gradients) and provides a substantially more complex privacy analysis involving envelope CDFs of convex combinations of chi-squared variables. The implementation leverages the same forward/backward hook architecture as FGC, requiring only one forward and two backward passes, while the privacy accounting builds on the f-DP tradeoff function framework and PRV accountant (Gopi et al., 2021).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As frontier LLMs push context lengths toward 100K tokens, the quadratic memory overhead O(B·min{d²,T²}) of existing DP-SGD implementations becomes a hard blocker for private training; DP-SGD-RC reduces this to near-linear O(BkT+pk) with k≈32, making DP fine-tuning of LLMs practically feasible at scale. The key open question is whether the current conservative privacy analysis for Hutch++ (which assumes the adversary knows intermediate computation state) can be tightened, and whether sparse or ±1-valued sketching matrices can further reduce overhead while preserving the privacy guarantee. The finding that Hutch++'s noisy norm estimation acts as a beneficial regularizer at low epsilon (≈3% accuracy gain) suggests an unexplored connection between estimation noise and generalization in private training.
