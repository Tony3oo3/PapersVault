## Problem

Existing training-free decoding methods for MLLM hallucination mitigation (e.g., VCD, ICD) apply uniform linear suppression of language priors, which the authors show can degrade performance even when priors are aligned with visual evidence. They identify this as a geometric failure mode they term Manifold Departure: structure-agnostic linear shifts push hidden states away from the high-density semantic manifold of normal decoding, destabilizing generation. The core gap is that prior methods treat language priors as uniformly harmful, ignoring their dual role as both confidence anchors and hallucination triggers.

## Input / Output

MGAP takes an RGB image and a text query as input and produces an autoregressively generated text response, intervening at the final-layer hidden state at each decoding step. The method is fully training-free and operates within a single forward pass per token, unlike contrastive baselines that require multiple forward passes with different contexts. A small offline construction phase (N=50 blind prompts, SVD with K=5 components) precomputes the prior subspace basis, adding only O(dK) memory and O(dK) per-token compute overhead.

## Method

MGAP (Manifold-Guided Adaptive Projection) models language priors as a low-rank subspace estimated via SVD from centered blind hidden states (null-image inputs), then performs subspace-selective intervention during decoding. At each step, the hidden state is projected onto the prior subspace, and only this projected component is attenuated by an adaptive coefficient β = α·γ·δ, where γ = tanh(λH) is an uncertainty gate derived from Shannon entropy of the token distribution, and δ = 1 − cos(h_orig, h_proj) is a consistency gate measuring mismatch between the state and its prior projection. When priors align with vision and the model is confident, both gates shrink β toward zero, reducing the operation to an approximate identity and preserving the orthogonal semantic components (Theorem 4.3). Three theoretical properties guarantee bounded step size, subspace-selective updates, and distance decrease toward ground-truth-aligned states under prior-aligned error conditions.

## Results

On the POPE benchmark, MGAP achieves the best F1 across all three splits for both backbones: 90.29/87.59/84.46 (Random/Popular/Adversarial) on LLaVA v1.5-7B and 91.94/86.84/84.17 on Qwen3-VL-8B. On CHAIR, MGAP dramatically reduces hallucinated objects, cutting CHAIRs from 47.4 to 26.2 (LLaVA) and from 33.6 to 26.8 (Qwen3-VL) while simultaneously improving precision to 85.9% and 83.9% respectively. On AMBER, MGAP achieves the lowest CHAIR (7.6) and hallucination rate (35.1) while maintaining the highest coverage (51.7). Inference cost is ~1.05 s/sample, roughly 50% faster than contrastive baselines (~2.1–2.2 s/sample).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MGAP outperforms all five baselines on the POPE Adversarial split for both backbones (F1: 84.46 vs. DeCo 83.71, ICD 83.13, MoD 83.02, HalTrapper 81.80, VCD 80.66 on LLaVA). On CHAIR, the advantage is most dramatic: MGAP reduces CHAIRs to 26.2 (LLaVA) and 26.8 (Qwen3-VL), roughly halving the hallucination rate compared to VCD (52.8/38.4), ICD (51.8/34.2), CODE (49.8/31.4), and HalTrapper (47.2/30.8), while also achieving the highest precision (85.9%/83.9%). Notably, VCD underperforms vanilla decoding on POPE Random and Popular splits (F1 87.79 vs. 89.76 on LLaVA), confirming the Manifold Departure diagnosis, whereas MGAP consistently improves over vanilla. On Qwen3-VL, DeCo edges out MGAP on POPE Random F1 (91.98 vs. 91.94) but MGAP dominates on Popular (86.84 vs. 83.81) and Adversarial (84.17 vs. 83.81). On AMBER, MGAP achieves the best CHAIR (7.6) and hallucination rate (35.1), beating HalTrapper (8.0/36.3), ICD (8.6/37.3), VCD (8.9/38.1), and CODE (9.0/39.5).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by reframing the hallucination-mitigation problem through a geometric lens: it identifies 'Manifold Departure' as a systematic failure mode of the dominant family of training-free contrastive decoding methods (VCD, ICD, CODE, HalTrapper, DeCo, MoD), which all perform structure-agnostic linear shifts in representation space. Rather than building on any single prior method, MGAP repurposes the manifold assumption from representation learning (Roweis & Saul; Tenenbaum; Bengio) and applies SVD-based low-rank subspace estimation to blind hidden states, yielding a subspace-selective correction that preserves orthogonal semantics. The adaptive gating mechanism (Shannon entropy + cosine consistency) is a novel combination not present in prior contrastive or penalty-based decoders.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Manifold Departure diagnosis provides a useful geometric explanation for why widely-adopted contrastive decoders can degrade performance below vanilla, and the subspace-selective correction offers a practical, training-free, single-pass alternative with ~50% lower latency than multi-branch contrastive methods. However, the evaluation is limited to two 7–8B backbones (LLaVA v1.5-7B, Qwen3-VL-8B), and the prior subspace is estimated from only 50 blind states with K=5 components, raising open questions about robustness across model scales, domains, and video modalities. Whether the learned subspace remains valid under distribution shift (e.g., new image domains or longer contexts) and whether the approach extends to autoregressive video MLLMs remain unaddressed.
