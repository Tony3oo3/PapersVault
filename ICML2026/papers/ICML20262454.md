## Problem

Current VLM agents that internalize world modeling via Chain-of-Thought reasoning rely on passive exploitation of visited states, lacking the epistemic drive to actively discover 'known unknowns' in sparse-reward environments. Standard curiosity methods decouple exploration from the agent's linguistic reasoning, and directly applying visual-to-visual prediction ignores the architectural shift where the world model is embedded in the policy itself. The paper asks whether a VLM agent can actively seek interactions where its linguistic hypothesis of the future fails to align with visual reality, thereby grounding its internal world model through active falsification.

## Input / Output

The agent consumes a visual observation (rendered image at 255×255 for Navigation, grid-based images for Sokoban/FrozenLake, or workspace images for PrimitiveSkill) along with a text instruction and the full action-observation history. At each turn it emits a structured reasoning trace — <observation>, <reasoning>, <prediction> — followed by an executable action sequence (up to 3–5 discrete actions per turn, or free-form SVG markup for the reconstruction task). The hidden state at the final </prediction> token is extracted as a 2048-d vector representing the agent's linguistic hypothesis of the next state, which is the key intermediate signal for the cross-modal alignment objective.

## Method

GLANCE introduces a self-supervised cross-modal prediction objective that projects the VLM's linguistic prediction (the hidden state at the </pred> token) through a lightweight 2-layer MLP projector into the visual feature space of a momentum-updated target encoder (EMA with decay α). The normalized MSE between the projected prediction and the stop-gradient target representation serves a dual role: it shapes the online visual encoder to capture semantically actionable features (gradients flow through the frozen LLM to update only the projector and visual encoder), and it provides an intrinsic curiosity reward r_t^i = β·L_explore that is added to the extrinsic reward for PPO policy optimization with Bi-Level GAE. To counteract 'curiosity drain' — the rapid overfitting of the projector to superficial features that causes the intrinsic reward to vanish — GLANCE employs a Curriculum Exploration mechanism that monitors the rate of change in prediction loss and periodically re-initializes the projector weights while preserving the evolved visual encoder, forcing the agent to re-examine familiar states at increasing granularity. The LLM backbone is frozen during the alignment step to prevent language drift, and the target network is updated via EMA at the end of each iteration, creating a slowly evolving regression anchor.

## Results

GLANCE-Full achieves an overall score of 0.86 across five agentic tasks (Sokoban, FrozenLake, Navigation, PrimitiveSkill, SVG Reconstruction), outperforming the strongest exploitation-based baseline VAGEN-Full (0.81) by 5 points. In the more challenging sparse-reward setting, GLANCE-Base reaches 0.80 overall versus VAGEN-Base's 0.76, with the largest gap on Sokoban (0.74 vs. 0.61). GLANCE also surpasses all zero-shot proprietary models (o4-mini 0.67, Gemini 2.5 Pro 0.67, GPT-4o 0.60) and open-source VLMs (Qwen2.5-VL-72B 0.55) using only a 3B backbone trained without human demonstrations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary baseline VAGEN-Full (dense extrinsic reward + Bi-Level GAE), GLANCE-Full wins on every task: Sokoban 0.85 vs 0.79, FrozenLake 0.78 vs 0.72, Navigation 0.87 vs 0.81, PrimitiveSkill 0.92 vs 0.90, and SVG 0.86 vs 0.81. In the sparse-reward setting (VAGEN-Base), the advantage widens substantially on Sokoban (0.74 vs 0.61) and PrimitiveSkill (0.94 vs 0.90), confirming that curiosity-driven exploration is most beneficial when extrinsic signals are scarce. GLANCE also improves over Turn-level PPO w/Mask (0.69 vs 0.58 overall) and GRPO w/Mask (0.69 vs 0.54), with the largest gains in PrimitiveSkill where sparse task rewards cause exploitation-based agents to stagnate. Compared to zero-shot proprietary models, GLANCE-Full (0.86) outperforms o4-mini (0.67), Gemini 2.5 Pro (0.67), GPT-4o (0.60), and Claude 4.5 Sonnet (0.64) by 12–26 points overall, despite using a 3B open-source backbone. The only setting where GLANCE does not clearly dominate is SVG reconstruction against Claude 4.5 Sonnet (0.86 vs 0.88), where the proprietary model's stronger generative prior gives it a slight edge on perceptual similarity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends curiosity-driven exploration (Pathak et al., 2017; BYOL-Explore, Guo et al., 2022) from pixel/proprioception-based domains to the semantic reasoning space of VLM agents, where the world model is internalized in the policy via chain-of-thought reasoning rather than existing as an external module. It builds directly on VAGEN (Wang et al., 2025) for the internalized world-modeling CoT framework and on BYOL (Grill et al., 2020) for the momentum target network architecture, but departs from both by using the VLM's linguistic prediction (the hidden state at the </pred> token) as the prediction source and the visual observation as the target, creating a cross-modal alignment objective that standard visual-to-visual curiosity methods cannot capture. The curriculum rejuvenation mechanism, which periodically re-initializes the projector to counteract 'curiosity drain,' is a novel practical contribution not present in prior bootstrapped exploration methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a lightweight cross-modal curiosity signal (frozen LLM + 2-layer MLP projector + updated visual encoder) can sustain effective exploration in sparse-reward VLM agent tasks without requiring dense LLM-as-a-judge supervision, reducing a major practical bottleneck in training VLM agents. The identification of 'curiosity drain' and its resolution via periodic projector rejuvenation provides a reusable mechanism for long-horizon self-supervised training. Key limitations include the frozen LLM backbone (which prevents reasoning-logic refinement) and the simple MLP projector (which may limit alignment granularity), and open questions remain about whether LoRA-based backbone updates or Transformer-based cross-attention projectors could further improve grounding without catastrophic forgetting.
