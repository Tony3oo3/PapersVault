## Problem

Autoencoder latent spaces lack explicit geometric structure, making it difficult to compute geodesic interpolation or extrapolation that both adheres to the data distribution and incorporates application-dependent Riemannian metrics. Existing approaches either require specific autoencoder architectures, rely on latent-space grids that suffer from the curse of dimensionality, or allow paths to leave the latent manifold. The paper addresses how to perform a practical, metric-flexible geodesic calculus on latent manifolds represented only by noisy point clouds, robust to the inevitable inaccuracies of any learned implicit representation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a trained autoencoder (encoder φ, decoder ψ), a point cloud of encoded samples approximating the latent manifold Z ⊂ ℝ^l, and either two endpoints z₀, z_K (for interpolation) or an initial point z₀ with discrete velocity v₀ = K(z₁ − z₀) (for extrapolation), plus a choice of local distance functional W (Euclidean, pullback metric, or KL-divergence-based). It produces a discrete geodesic path (z₀, …, z_K) with K+1 nodes constrained to Z, or a discrete exponential map Exp^K_{z₀}(v₀) = z_K. The projection network Π_σ is a fully connected MLP with ELU activations trained separately on the encoded point cloud; the geodesic solver then operates on the K−1 free interior nodes in ℝ^l, with the constraint ζ_σ(z_k) = z_k − Π_σ(z_k) ≈ 0 enforced via an augmented Lagrangian.

## Method

The framework has two components. First, the latent manifold is represented implicitly via a learned projection Π_σ obtained by minimizing a denoising loss Q(Π) = ∫∫ |z − Π(y)|² f_σ(z−y) dz dy over the encoded point cloud, yielding a Gaussian-weighted barycenter whose residual ζ_σ(z) = z − Π_σ(z) serves as the implicit function (with O(σ²) defect on the manifold interior). Second, geodesics are computed by minimizing a discrete path energy E^K = K Σ W(z_{k−1}, z_k) subject to ζ_σ(z_k) = 0 using an augmented Lagrangian method, where the penalty term naturally tolerates the inexactness of ζ_σ as long as it points approximately in the normal direction. For the exponential map, rather than integrating the geodesic ODE, the paper uses a variational ODE solver: given z_{k−1} and z_k, it iteratively minimizes a functional F(z_{k+1}, λ_k) combining the Euler–Lagrange residual of the discrete path energy with a manifold-constraint penalty, via BFGS. This variational discretization ensures the extrapolated path reproduces the discrete geodesic between its endpoints and converges to the continuous exponential map as K → ∞, while avoiding second-order differentiation of the decoder network.

## Results

The paper demonstrates that geodesic interpolation and extrapolation on learned latent manifolds produce qualitatively more realistic decoded outputs than linear interpolation across four experimental settings (SCAPE human poses, CMU motion capture, rotating cow images, and SMPL body poses). On the torus toy model, the discrete geodesic energy (4.7889) matches Stochman's parametric spline approach (4.7892) while being faster (1.89 s vs. 2.48 s). For the uncertainty-regularized VAE baseline, the proposed method computes geodesics in 4 s (Euclidean metric) versus 15 s for Arvanitidis et al. (2022), and unlike the baseline, the computed paths remain on the latent manifold. The method is robust to imperfect implicit representations, with the augmented Lagrangian scheme tolerating the O(σ²) defect of the learned projection.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Stochman (Detlefsen et al., 2021), the proposed variational discretization matches geodesic quality on the torus (energy 4.7889 vs. 4.7892) with faster runtime (1.89 s vs. 2.48 s), and produces a more stable exponential map in negatively curved regions where Stochman's ODE-based solver visibly deviates. Against Arvanitidis et al. (2022) (uncertainty-regularized VAE with KL-divergence metric), the proposed method wins on both speed (4 s vs. 15 s) and correctness: the baseline's geodesic leaves the data manifold and the optimization fails to converge, whereas the proposed constrained approach keeps paths on the latent manifold. Against Arvanitidis et al. (2018) (RBF-based pullback metric with k-NN graph), the proposed method is substantially faster (4 s vs. 66 s total) and avoids the need for grid-based precomputation that limits scalability. Against the LAND model (Arvanitidis et al., 2016), the proposed method computes five geodesics in under 10 s versus approximately 2 minutes, and produces cleaner paths without the artifacts (paths leaving the manifold neighborhood) observed in the LAND model, which is designed for volumetric data rather than manifolds with non-vanishing codimension.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the variational time-discrete geodesic calculus of Rumpf & Wirth (2015), originally developed for Riemannian shape spaces, and adapts it to the setting of implicitly defined latent manifolds. For the implicit representation, it adopts the denoising-autoencoder framework of Alain & Bengio (2014), interpreting the learned projection as a Gaussian-weighted barycenter. Conceptually, it departs from prior latent-space geodesic methods (Arvanitidis et al. 2018, 2022; Shao et al. 2018) that either parametrize the manifold via the decoder, rely on grid-based shortest paths, or extend the metric to penalize off-manifold points; instead, this work constrains paths directly to a learned implicit zero-level set via an augmented Lagrangian, making it robust to the imperfect representations that arise in practice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a practical, plug-and-play tool for geodesic interpolation and extrapolation on latent manifolds that is independent of the specific autoencoder architecture and supports arbitrary Riemannian metrics (Euclidean, pullback, Fisher-Rao). Key open questions include the lack of a full error analysis combining projection learning error with discretization error, untested scalability beyond l=84, and the requirement for efficient local distance evaluation on the data manifold. The connection between the denoising projection and score functions (σ⁻²(Π_σ(z)−z) ≈ ∇log p(z)) suggests a natural bridge to diffusion-model-style conditional denoisers for latent distributions, which the authors identify as a promising next step.
