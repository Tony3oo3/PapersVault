## Problem

Existing VLN methods condition the policy on the full long-form instruction at every timestep, causing irrelevant instruction segments to act as semantic noise that amplifies hallucinations and spurious actions in long-horizon navigation. This full-instruction conditioning also incurs substantial latency and memory overhead from repeatedly processing redundant textual and visual context, making efficient real-time deployment difficult.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265177/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During the thinking phase, the model consumes 8 uniformly sampled historical frames plus the full instruction to produce a concise sub-instruction (semantic anchor). During the action phase, it consumes only the most recent 3 egocentric RGB frames and the sub-instruction—explicitly excluding the global instruction—to output a micro-action sequence of N discrete actions (Forward, Turn_Left, Turn_Right, Stop at 15° increments). The thinking phase is not invoked every step but triggered adaptively by action-entropy signals, reducing total token consumption from ~147K to ~77K per episode.

## Method

IDEAL-VLN reformulates VLN as a two-step causal inference chain: Semantic Anchoring extracts a sub-instruction S_t from the global instruction and observation history, then Action Alignment generates actions conditioned solely on (I_t, S_t) via a do-operator intervention that blocks the direct instruction-to-action pathway, enforcing A_t ⊥ L | (S_t, I_t). An adaptive triggering mechanism monitors the predicted action sequence for confusion-induced dithering (simultaneous left/right turns) or scene transitions (k+ same-direction rotations) to decide when to re-anchor, adding zero extra computational cost. Training uses a Hierarchical Correction Mechanism combining Semantic-Level Thought Correction (word-overlap scoring with Thought Forcing for hallucinated sub-instructions) and Spatially-Aware Adaptive Intervention (sigmoid gating on geodesic distance to modulate expert intervention probability), jointly supervising both the reasoning and execution stages. Built on Qwen2.5-VL-7B with a 160K Instruction-Aligned Navigation Dataset, the method achieves SOTA SR of 59.8% on R2R-CE and 59.1% on RxR-CE Val-Unseen while halving inference token cost.

## Results

On R2R-CE Val-Unseen, IDEAL-VLN† achieves 59.8% SR, 52.9% SPL, 64.9% OS, and 4.62m NE using only monocular RGB input, setting a new state-of-the-art among all methods. On the more challenging RxR-CE Val-Unseen (longer instructions, more complex trajectories), IDEAL-VLN† reaches 59.1% SR, 50.7% SPL, and 64.5% nDTW. Even without additional training data, IDEAL-VLN attains 56.7% SR on R2R-CE and 55.9% SR on RxR-CE, surpassing StreamVLN (45.5%/51.8%) and NaVid (37.0%) that rely on extra datasets or longer context windows. The method also reduces per-episode token consumption from ~147K to ~77K, nearly halving inference cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IDEAL-VLN† outperforms all monocular RGB baselines on both benchmarks. On R2R-CE Val-Unseen, it leads NavFoM† (56.2% SR) by 3.6 points and InternVLA-N1† (55.4%) by 4.4 points, while also achieving the best SPL (52.9%) and lowest NE (4.62m). On RxR-CE Val-Unseen, IDEAL-VLN† (59.1% SR, 50.7% SPL, 64.5% nDTW) surpasses NavFoM† (57.4%, 49.4%, 60.2%) and StreamVLN† (51.8%, 45.0%, 61.9%) by a wide margin, with the nDTW gap of 4.3 points indicating substantially better path fidelity. Notably, IDEAL-VLN without extra data (56.7% SR on R2R) already exceeds StreamVLN† (55.7%) and NaVILA† (54.0%), demonstrating that the Think-Before-Act decomposition itself provides a structural advantage over simply scaling training data. Against multi-sensor baselines like ETPNav (57.0% SR with Pano+Depth+Odo), IDEAL-VLN† matches or exceeds performance using only a single RGB camera. The ablation confirms that the Think-Before-Act mechanism contributes +8.4% SR and the Hierarchical Correction Mechanism adds a further +4.8% over the end-to-end full-instruction baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant end-to-end VLN paradigm (NaVid, StreamVLN, VLN-R1) that conditions the policy on the full instruction at every timestep, instead reformulating navigation as a causal inference chain with an explicit do-operator intervention that blocks the direct instruction→action path. Conceptually, it extends the dual-system / slow-fast planning line (InternVLAN, OmniNav) by making the 'thinking' step a discrete, sparsely-triggered semantic anchoring operation rather than a continuous high-level planner, and it generalizes DAgger-style imitation learning by adding semantic-level thought correction on top of action-level correction. The causal mediation analysis framing (NIE vs. NDE decomposition) provides a theoretical justification for why full-instruction conditioning causes hallucinations, a problem prior work addressed only empirically through memory compression or context-window tricks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The instruction-interference problem is a core bottleneck for long-horizon embodied navigation, and the Think-Before-Act decomposition with entropy-based triggering offers a practical, zero-overhead mechanism that halves token cost while improving accuracy—a combination that matters for real-time robotic deployment. The 160K Instruction-Aligned Navigation Dataset fills a gap in frame-level instruction grounding that existing VLN benchmarks lack, and the sim-to-real validation on a Unitree Go2 quadruped demonstrates transferability beyond simulation. Open questions include whether the 7B backbone can be distilled to sub-2B models for fully onboard inference, how the approach handles genuinely unstructured spoken instructions with hesitations and self-corrections, and whether the causal-intervention framing generalizes to multi-agent or outdoor navigation settings.
