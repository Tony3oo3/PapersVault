## Problem

Standard semi-supervised LiDAR segmentation methods rely on a two-step paradigm where pseudo-labels are generated from a single distillation source, reinforcing confirmation bias and propagating errors during training. This is especially severe under high label scarcity, where erroneous pseudo-labels are amplified and models overfit to their own mistakes. No prior work trains multiple heterogeneous LiDAR representations collaboratively as coequal students within a single unified step to mitigate this issue.

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw LiDAR point clouds (x, y, z, intensity) and simultaneously transforms them into multiple structured representations—frustum-range-view images (64×512 or 32×480), polar bird's-eye-view images, and cylindrical voxel grids (240×180×20)—each processed by a dedicated architecture (FRNet, PolarNet, Cylinder3D). Cross-representation mappings (range-to-voxel, voxel-to-range) are defined through the point cloud as an intermediary to enable distillation between structurally different outputs. The final output is per-point semantic class labels, with each student model producing predictions in its own representation space.

## Method

CoLLiS trains multiple LiDAR representations as coequal students in a single forward step, eliminating the decoupled pseudo-labeling phase. Each student adaptively distills from all other participants using two reliability signals: Absolute Reliability (a linear schedule on epoch) controls the unlabeled loss weight, while Relative Reliability (a pairwise confidence-dominance count) sets per-source pseudo-label thresholds and distillation weights to softly resolve contradictory supervision. A consensus-driven augmentation mechanism dynamically adjusts the mixing probability based on inter-student prediction agreement, applying stronger augmentations to easy samples and weaker ones to hard samples. A KL-divergence regularization to a uniform distribution guards against overconfidence under extreme label scarcity. The composite loss combines labeled cross-entropy + Lovász loss, the adaptively weighted unlabeled distillation loss, and the regularization term.

## Results

CoLLiS is evaluated on three LiDAR segmentation benchmarks (nuScenes, SemanticKITTI, ScribbleKITTI) at 1%, 10%, 20%, and 50% label proportions, reporting mIoU. At the most challenging 1% label setting, CoLLiS achieves 63.2 mIoU (F-Range/FRNet) and 61.1 mIoU (Voxel/Cylinder3D) on nuScenes, 56.0 and 53.2 on SemanticKITTI, and 47.6 on both F-Range and Voxel for ScribbleKITTI. Gains are largest in low-label regimes: at 1% on nuScenes, CoLLiS surpasses the strongest single-representation baseline (FrustumMix) by +2.0 mIoU (F-Range) and +3.6 mIoU (Voxel), and outperforms the dual-representation IT2 by +3.6 mIoU on Voxel.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest single-representation baselines, CoLLiS consistently wins: on nuScenes at 1% labels, it beats FrustumMix by +2.0 mIoU (F-Range) and +3.6 mIoU (Voxel), and LaserMix by +4.5 and +5.8 mIoU respectively. Against the dual-representation IT2 (Liu et al., 2024), CoLLiS gains +3.6 mIoU on nuScenes Voxel 1% and +1.7 mIoU on SemanticKITTI Voxel 10%, though it narrowly trails IT2 by 0.3 mIoU on ScribbleKITTI Voxel 1% (47.6 vs. 47.9). On the significant-split protocol (Table 2), CoLLiS (Voxel) outperforms AIScene by +2.9 mIoU on nuScenes 1% and +0.3 on SemanticKITTI 1%, and beats DDSemi by +5.0 and +2.2 mIoU respectively. Older baselines (CPS, CBST, GPC, Lim3D) are surpassed by 5–8 mIoU in the 1% regime. The paper also shows that combining CoLLiS with IT2's contrastive objective yields further gains (e.g., 60.8 vs. 57.5 mIoU for Cylinder3D on nuScenes 1%), confirming the two approaches are complementary.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoLLiS extends the collaborative learning (CoL) paradigm—originally introduced by Deep Mutual Learning (Zhang et al., 2018) for two-network reciprocal supervision—into the semi-supervised LiDAR segmentation setting, where it had been largely absent. It departs from the dominant two-step LiDAR SemiSL pipeline (LaserMix, IT2, FrustumMix) that decouples pseudo-label generation from training and relies on a single distillation source, instead training multiple heterogeneous LiDAR representations (range-view, polar, voxel, raw points) as coequal students in a single forward step. The adaptive pseudo-labeling mechanism (combining absolute and relative reliability) and the consensus-driven augmentation (CDA) are novel components that address confirmation bias and augmentation scheduling, respectively, without requiring the contrastive objectives or multi-stage training of prior dual-representation methods like IT2.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical and scalable recipe for reducing annotation cost in LiDAR segmentation: it achieves consistent mIoU gains over SOTA across three benchmarks (especially at 1–10% labels) while being ~50% faster and using less memory than IT2, making multi-representation semi-supervised training more accessible. A key open question is whether the collaborative paradigm can close the gap on extremely rare long-tail classes (e.g., bicycle at 0.01% of annotations), where the paper acknowledges that data scarcity—not the learning paradigm—is the bottleneck. Future directions include integrating contrastive objectives across representations, multi-modality (image-LiDAR), and temporal cues, as well as efficient distillation of ensemble knowledge into lightweight deployable models.
