## Problem

Block-wise diffusion language models (DLMs) remain bottlenecked by memory-bound prefix attention in long-context settings, and naive sparse attention fails on them due to a KV Inflation problem: different queries in a block select different prefix KV positions, so the union of accessed KV pages can grow up to B times the per-query budget, negating the intended speedup. No prior method exploits the temporal structure unique to iterative denoising to reduce this union size.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265098/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each denoising step, the method consumes a fixed prefix KV cache of length L (K_p, V_p) and the current block's Q_b, K_b, V_b (B×d each), producing the merged attention output for all B block tokens. The prefix KV cache is immutable across denoising steps while block K/V are updated each step, and the method additionally caches per-token prefix attention statistics (o_p ∈ R^{B×d}, L_p ∈ R^B) between steps to enable reuse for stable tokens. The first denoising step of each block requires a full dense prefix-attention pass for initialization before sparse reuse begins.

## Method

LoSA exploits Locality of Representation Changes: between consecutive denoising steps, only a small fraction of block tokens (active tokens) undergo substantial hidden-state updates while the majority (stable tokens) remain nearly constant. It measures per-token MSE of query vectors across steps to partition tokens into an active set A (top-k by change) and stable tokens. For stable tokens, it directly reuses the cached prefix-attention statistics (o_p, L_p) from the previous step, preserving full dense-attention information without any KV reload. For active tokens, it applies QUEST sparse selection with per-query budget k and computes the union of selected KV indices only over A, shrinking the KV cache load from |∪_{i=1}^{B} I_i| to |∪_{i∈A} I_i|. Prefix and block contributions are then merged via online softmax, and updated statistics are cached for the next denoising step. This reduces average KV-cache density by ~1.54× versus naive sparse attention while simultaneously improving accuracy because stable tokens retain full prefix information rather than a sparse subset.

## Results

On LongBench (Trado-8B-Instruct), LoSA achieves 41.97% average accuracy at budget 128, outperforming QUEST (31.54%) by +10.43 points while maintaining 1.54× lower KV-cache density (2.58% vs 4.24%). At budget 256, LoSA reaches 42.14% vs QUEST's 34.64% (+7.50), approaching the Dense baseline of 44.86%. On SDAR-8B-Chat, LoSA similarly leads QUEST by +8.38 at budget 128 (40.95% vs 32.57%). In wall-clock latency on RTX A6000 (64K context, block size 16), LoSA achieves a 4.14× attention speedup over Dense, compared to 3.26× for QUEST.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoSA consistently outperforms QUEST (the primary sparse-attention baseline adapted from autoregressive LLMs) across all budget levels and both evaluated models. On Trado-8B-Instruct at budget 128, LoSA achieves 41.97% average LongBench accuracy versus QUEST's 31.54% (+10.43), and on SDAR-8B-Chat the gap is +8.38 (40.95% vs 32.57%). Against SparseD (which reuses a fixed sparse pattern computed at initialization), LoSA leads by +9.01 points at budget 128 on Trado-8B (41.97% vs 32.96%) and +8.27 on SDAR-8B (40.95% vs 32.68%), with the gap narrowing at higher budgets where both methods converge toward dense accuracy. LoSA also achieves 1.54× lower average KV-cache density than QUEST across all settings, meaning it loads fewer prefix KV positions while simultaneously producing more accurate outputs. On short-context commonsense reasoning (HellaSwag, WinoGrande, BoolQ), LoSA and QUEST perform comparably (within ~1 point), as expected since the KV Inflation problem is less severe under 1K-token contexts.

## Relation to Prior Work

LoSA builds directly on QUEST's page-based sparse KV selection and FlashAttention's online-softmax decomposition, repurposing both for the block-wise diffusion setting. It departs from concurrent DLM acceleration work (SparseD, which reuses a fixed sparse pattern; Sparse-dLLM, which evicts KV entries) by instead reusing cached attention *outputs* for stable tokens, preserving full dense-attention information for the majority of queries. The key conceptual departure is identifying the KV Inflation problem—where the union of per-query KV selections across a block negates sparsity gains—a failure mode absent in autoregressive decoding and specific to the multi-query block structure of DLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As block-wise diffusion LLMs (Trado, SDAR, Dream) gain traction as alternatives to autoregressive generation, their long-context inference bottleneck (repeatedly loading the full KV cache at every denoising step) becomes a practical deployment concern. LoSA's locality-aware reuse offers a training-free, plug-and-play speedup that also *improves* accuracy over naive sparse attention, making it attractive for practitioners already using these models. Open questions include whether the locality structure persists at larger block sizes or with more aggressive denoising schedules, whether integration with batched serving systems (vLLM, TensorRT-LLM) preserves the speedup, and whether the initial dense-attention overhead becomes problematic for very short generation sequences.
