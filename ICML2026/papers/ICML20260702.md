## Problem

Pixel-based RL agents are brittle to purely visual distribution shifts, but existing benchmarks entangle multiple sources of shift (background, geometry, dynamics, distractors) simultaneously, making it impossible to attribute performance degradation to specific visual factors. Additionally, many pixel-based RL environments are computationally expensive, limiting the large-scale ablations needed for systematic visual generalization analysis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260702/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent consumes a single 128×128×3 RGB frame with no history or frame stacking and outputs a discrete action encoded as a 3-bit bitmask (LEFT=1, RIGHT=2, JUMP=4), yielding 8 possible actions. The environment is configured entirely through a single YAML file with 93 controllable parameters organized into groups (background, character, npc, distractors, filters, effects, layout, physics). Evaluation produces per-configuration metrics (distance, progress, success rate, return) and their train-eval generalization gaps, with success defined as reaching a fixed distance threshold of 490 units.

## Method

KAGE-Env is a JAX-native 2D platformer that factorizes the observation process into independently controllable visual axes while holding latent dynamics P and reward r fixed across all configurations. The key theoretical result (Theorem 4.2) shows that for a reactive pixel policy, visual shift reduces exactly to a shift in the induced state-conditional action distribution π_ξ(a|s) = ∫ π(a|o) O_ξ(do|s), meaning any train-eval gap is attributable solely to how the renderer changes the policy's effective behavior in a fixed latent MDP. KAGE-Bench constructs 34 train-eval configuration pairs across 6 known-axis suites (agent, background, distractors, effects, filters, layout) where exactly one parameter within the target axis changes between train and eval. The fully vectorized JAX implementation (vmap + jit + lax.scan) scales to 2^16 parallel environments at up to 33M steps/sec on a single GPU, enabling exhaustive sweeps. The primary diagnostic baseline is PPO-CNN from CleanRL trained on single RGB frames, supplemented by robustness baselines (RAD-PPO, CURL, DrQ, SVEA) evaluated on the hardest configurations.

## Results

Using a PPO-CNN baseline across 34 train-evaluation configuration pairs, visual generalization is strongly axis-dependent: filters (ΔSR = 86.8%) and effects (ΔSR = 80.5%) cause the most severe success-rate collapses, followed by layout (62.8%) and background (53.3%), while agent appearance shifts are comparatively benign (ΔSR = 21.1%). A critical finding is that photometric and lighting perturbations preserve forward motion (distance drops only 11.7–20.8%) yet nearly eliminate task completion (SR drops from ~0.83 to 0.11–0.16), showing that return alone can obscure generalization failures. Background shifts uniquely impair both motion and completion simultaneously (30.5% distance drop alongside 53.3% SR drop). The fully vectorized JAX implementation reaches 33M environment steps/s on a single GPU with 2¹⁶ parallel environments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PPO-CNN (from CleanRL) serves as the primary diagnostic probe across all 34 configuration pairs and six axis suites. Among robustness baselines evaluated on the 8 hardest configurations, RAD-PPO with cutout-color augmentation is the only method that converges and improves over PPO-CNN on 7 of 8 configurations, though substantial success-rate gaps persist. In contrast, RAD-PPO with random crop-resize augmentation, CURL, DrQ, and SVEA all failed to converge in the platformer setting; the authors attribute these failures to spatial augmentations disrupting the screen-position information needed for cliff distance estimation, obstacle avoidance, and jump timing. The paper positions these comparisons as diagnostic: KAGE-Bench is designed to expose which controlled visual shifts break a standard pixel policy, and different methods may expose different failure modes. A simple RIGHT+JUMP reflexive policy reaches SR = 0.59 ± 0.03 but with much worse return (−5000.3) than trained policies, underscoring that success rate must be interpreted jointly with return rather than treated as task competence alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of two existing lines of work: visual generalization benchmarks for pixel-based RL (RL-ViGen, Procgen, DCS, DMC-VB) and JAX-native high-throughput RL environments (Brax, Jumanji, Craftax, Octax). The key conceptual departure is the 'known-axis' design principle: rather than sampling from a joint distribution over confounded visual and structural changes, KAGE-Bench factorizes the observation kernel into independently controllable axes and constructs train-eval pairs that vary exactly one axis, enabling unambiguous attribution of failures. The formal reduction of visual shift to induced state-policy shift (Theorem 4.2) provides a clean POMDP-theoretic justification for this design that prior benchmarks lacked. The paper does not build on a specific prior method but repurposes the standard PPO-CNN (CleanRL) as a diagnostic probe and evaluates robustness baselines (RAD, CURL, DrQ, SVEA) as secondary checks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

KAGE-Bench addresses a practical gap: existing visual generalization benchmarks entangle multiple shift sources, making it impossible to attribute failures to specific visual factors, and CPU-bound simulators make exhaustive axis sweeps infeasible. The 33M steps/sec throughput on a single GPU and the axis-isolated design together enable systematic, reproducible diagnosis that was previously impractical. The finding that photometric/lighting shifts collapse task completion (SR drops of 80–87%) while preserving forward motion (distance gaps of only 12–21%) is practically important because it shows return-based evaluation alone can mask severe generalization failures. Limitations include the single 2D platformer domain, stylized (non-photorealistic) shifts, and reliance on PPO-CNN as the primary probe; whether axis rankings transfer to 3D environments, geometric/perspective shifts, or stronger agents remains open.
