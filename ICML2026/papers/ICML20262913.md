## Problem

Existing continual test-time adaptation (CTTA) methods rely on dense models that encode all domain knowledge into a single global parameter set, making the adaptability-stability balance inherently difficult. While Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures offer sparsity that could improve this balance, their pretrained routers fail on unseen shifting domains (producing near-random expert assignments) and operate at the token level, unable to capture the domain-level distributional shifts characteristic of CTTA.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262913/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each discrete time step t, the method consumes a batch of N unlabeled test text samples drawn from the current shifting domain distribution and the M expert parameter vectors of the MoE model. It produces an N×M sample-expert allocation matrix π_t^s (computed online via As-COOT) that is row-normalized and used as a domain-level prior to calibrate the original token-level MoE routing logits, yielding a calibrated routing distribution z_As. The calibrated routing then drives entropy-minimization-based parameter updates, with a random restoration mechanism that resets a fraction of parameters to their initial source-domain values to prevent catastrophic forgetting.

## Method

TiME models test-time MoE routing as a sample-expert allocation problem via co-optimal transport (COOT), jointly optimizing an element allocation matrix and a feature alignment matrix. A Semantic Space Alignment (SSA) module, trained offline via bidirectional contrastive learning on sample-expert compatibility pairs (constructed from expert output entropy or original MoE routing), aligns sample and expert representations so that Euclidean distance in the aligned space serves as a physically meaningful allocation cost. The key innovation is As-COOT, which breaks COOT's symmetric mass conservation by relaxing the sample-side marginal via KL divergence (allowing high-cost noisy samples to be discarded with a penalty λ) while enforcing the expert-side marginal via a hard indicator function (guaranteeing uniform expert load). As-COOT is solved by block coordinate descent over two nested asymmetric OT subproblems, each converted to a smooth dual via the Fenchel-Rockafellar transform and solved with a modified Sinkhorn iteration where the sample-side scaling uses a fractional exponent λ/(λ+ε) to suppress mass for noisy samples, while the expert-side scaling uses the standard hard-constraint update.

## Results

On the Text Understanding task stream, TiME(OR) achieves the best average accuracy of 0.4707 on Qwen1.5-MoE-A2.7B and 0.4481 on DeepSeek-V2-Lite, outperforming the strongest baseline CTTA-T (0.4548 and 0.4300) by +1.59% and +1.81% respectively. Notably, several baselines (Tent, CoTTA) fall below the zero-shot source model (0.4449 on Qwen, 0.4256 on DeepSeek), and Tent collapses to 0% on XQuAD, indicating severe model degradation under continuous domain shifts. On the Medical Reasoning stream, TiME also achieves the best average score on Qwen, confirming consistent gains on specialized domains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TiME(OR) outperforms all seven CTTA baselines on both MoE models. On Qwen1.5-MoE-A2.7B, TiME(OR) (0.4707) beats the closest competitor CTTA-T (0.4548) by 1.59 points, and REM (0.4301) by 4.06 points. On DeepSeek-V2-Lite, TiME(OR) (0.4481) leads CTTA-T (0.4300) by 1.81 points and SoTTA (0.4275) by 2.06 points. Tent and CoTTA are the weakest baselines, both falling below the zero-shot source model on both architectures (e.g., Tent drops to 0.3887 vs. source 0.4449 on Qwen), with Tent completely collapsing to 0% on XQuAD. The two SSA variants (EOE and OR) achieve nearly identical performance (0.4661 vs. 0.4707 on Qwen), indicating the method is robust to the specific sample-expert compatibility construction strategy. All results are statistically significant under t-test (p < 0.01).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CTTA literature (Wang et al. 2022, Niu et al. 2023, Han et al. 2025), which has exclusively targeted dense models, by arguing that MoE architectures are structurally better suited for the adaptability-stability trade-off. For the routing mechanism, it departs from both training-based routers (which are frozen at test time and fail under domain shift) and training-free heuristics (hashing, random routing) by formulating test-time routing as a co-optimal transport allocation problem. The key methodological departure from standard COOT (Titouan et al. 2020) is the asymmetric modification: relaxing the sample-side mass constraint via KL divergence to discard noisy samples while enforcing a hard expert-side constraint for load balancing, solved via Sinkhorn scaling on the Fenchel-Rockafellar dual.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The architectural insight that MoE models are more robust than dense models under continuous test-time adaptation (even when the dense model is larger) is practically useful for practitioners choosing base architectures for CTTA pipelines. However, the performance gains over the strongest baseline (CTTA-T) are modest (~1.5–2% average), and the method introduces non-trivial computational overhead from solving the As-COOT optimization at each time step. Open questions include whether the offline-trained SSA module remains valid under extreme or multi-modal domain shifts, whether the approach scales to much larger MoE models with hundreds of experts, and whether the random-restoration regularization can be replaced by more principled stability mechanisms.
