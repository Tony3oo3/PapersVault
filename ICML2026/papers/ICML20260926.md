## Problem

Existing diffusion-based RL methods face a fundamental exploration-exploitation tradeoff: sampling-based approaches (e.g., QVPO) lose discriminative power as the policy concentrates, weakening the critic's ability to select better candidates, while gradient-based approaches (e.g., DIPO, QSM) exploit Q-gradients but collapse into unimodal policies with low diversity. Additionally, diffusion-based RL has not been successfully deployed in real-world robotic systems trained from scratch without large-scale human demonstrations.

## Input / Output

In simulation, the policy takes a continuous state vector and outputs a continuous action via a 20-step DDPM with ε-parameterization. In the real-world Franka Emika Panda setup, the input combines multi-view RGB images (a 672×376 eye-in-hand ZED Mini camera and a 640×480 fixed RealSense D435i) with proprioceptive state (end-effector Cartesian position, velocity, and gripper position), and the output is an end-effector Cartesian position command. Critically, the critic-guided sampler is used only during training to synthesize supervised targets; environment interaction and deployment use the standard unguided sampler to preserve control-loop latency.

## Method

CGPO replaces finite-candidate sampling with a single critic-guided target action per state, generated by running a reverse diffusion chain where the last G denoising steps are steered by the gradient of a conservative Q-critic evaluated at the predicted clean action x̂₀. The guidance direction is constrained via DSG (Diffusion with Spherical Gaussian) to lie on the typical-radius shell of the reverse Gaussian transition, preserving manifold structure and stochasticity through a convex mixture with the unconditional noise. The diffusion actor is then trained by weighted denoising regression on these guided targets, with a value-calibrated network V_φ(s) anchoring the weight scale to prevent drift, and a diffusion entropy regularization term preserving action diversity. The Q-signal is stabilized through DDQN target construction and truncated-quantile aggregation (discarding top quantiles from a distributional critic ensemble), ensuring the same critic provides both reliable guidance gradients and stable reweighting signals throughout training.

## Results

On five MuJoCo v3 locomotion tasks (10⁶ environment steps, 5 seeds), CGPO achieves the best episodic return on all tasks: 7272.1 on Ant-v3, 14368.6 on HalfCheetah-v3, 4170.5 on Hopper-v3, 7131.4 on Humanoid-v3, and 6482.6 on Walker2d-v3. The closest diffusion-based competitor, QVPO, trails by 12–34% depending on the task (e.g., +26.2% on HalfCheetah, +34.4% on Humanoid). On the real-world Franka Emika Panda cylindrical peg-in-hole task, CGPO reaches an 80% success rate (16/20 trials) versus 65% for the SAC baseline, with visibly smoother insertion trajectories.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CGPO outperforms all nine baselines on every MuJoCo task. Among diffusion-policy methods, QVPO (the authors' prior sampling-based work) is the strongest competitor, but CGPO improves upon it by 12–34% (e.g., 14368.6 vs. 11385.6 on HalfCheetah, 7131.4 vs. 5306.6 on Humanoid), confirming that critic-guided target synthesis overcomes the diminishing-contrast bottleneck of finite candidate sampling. DIPO and DACER, which use gradient-based or backprop-through-diffusion optimization, are consistently 10–25% behind CGPO, while QSM (score-matching) and SDAC (energy-based) underperform more substantially, with SDAC particularly weak on Ant (1404.6) and Humanoid (4429.6). Among conventional baselines, SAC is the strongest Gaussian-policy method but still trails CGPO by 15–35% on most tasks; TD3 (deterministic) and PPO/SPO are further behind. On the real-world Franka peg-in-hole task, CGPO (80%) beats the HIL-SERL-SAC baseline (65%) by 15 percentage points, and qualitatively produces smoother, less jittery insertion trajectories.

## Relation to Prior Work

CGPO sits at the intersection of diffusion-policy RL and training-free guidance. It inherits the conditional DDPM action-generation architecture from Diffusion Policy (Chi et al., 2023), the weighted denoising regression and state-reweighting machinery from QVPO (Ding et al., 2024), and the spherical-Gaussian-constrained guidance (DSG) from Yang et al. (2024). The key departure is from both sampling-based methods (QVPO, DIPO), which select among finite candidate actions and lose discriminative power as the policy concentrates, and gradient-based methods (DIPO, QSM, DACER), which backpropagate Q-gradients through the diffusion chain and risk mode collapse. CGPO instead repurposes training-free loss-guided diffusion (Song et al., 2023) as a training-time target generator: a single critic-guided action per state replaces the multi-candidate selection, and the guided action becomes the regression target for the denoiser.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most significant practical contribution is demonstrating that diffusion-based RL can be trained from scratch on a real robot (Franka Emika Panda) without large-scale human demonstrations, addressing a gap where prior diffusion-policy work in robotics has been limited to imitation learning. The training-time-only guidance design keeps deployment latency identical to vanilla diffusion-policy inference, which is critical for real-robot control loops. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher-dimensional action spaces (e.g., dexterous multi-fingered hands), how sensitive performance is to critic quality in long-horizon tasks, and whether the DSG constraint generalizes to flow-matching or other generative backbones beyond DDPM.
