## Problem

End-to-end training of early-stage rankers (ESR) in two-stage ranking systems is impractical with vanilla policy gradient (V-PG) because its variance grows exponentially with candidate-set size K, and it suffers from a credit-assignment issue: gradients are propagated to the joint probability of the entire candidate set, equally rewarding or penalizing all K items regardless of which specific item the late-stage ranker actually selected and which generated the observed reward.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262171/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The ESR receives a user context x (e.g., demographic profile) and a large item pool A (up to thousands of items), and outputs an unordered candidate set of K distinct items. The fixed LSR then selects a ranked list of L items from this candidate set, producing position-wise rewards r_l that are aggregated with position weights α_l (e.g., DCG-style 1/log₂(l+1)). The key structural constraint is that the ESR's action space is combinatorial (|A|^K possible candidate sets), and only one candidate set is observed per user interaction, making unbiased gradient estimation from a single sample intractable for large K.

## Method

CA-PG replaces the score function ∇log π_ESR(A^K|x) with ∇log π_ESR(S^K(a_l)|x), where S^K(a_l) is the set of all candidate sets containing the target item a_l, so the gradient is computed with respect to the marginal probability that a_l appears in any candidate set rather than a specific one. This marginalization shrinks the effective action space from |A|^K to |A|, eliminating the need for Monte-Carlo estimation over candidate-set compositions and resolving the credit-assignment issue by attributing reward only to the item the LSR actually selected. Theorem 3.1 decomposes V-PG into CA-PG plus a residual term, showing CA-PG is a variance-reduced component; Theorem 3.3 proves CA-PG learns the correct top-K item partition whenever the LSR is reasonably aligned (including any interpolation between uniform and optimal policies). For Plackett-Luce ESRs, the paper derives fully differentiable score functions, and the sampling-with-replacement approximation further reduces computation from O(KL) to O(L) for single-logit models (yielding TOP1-PG) or O(ML) for mixture-of-experts configurations.

## Results

On synthetic data with K=20 candidates, CA-PG-SwR (TOP1-PG) achieves a policy value of 8.33 at 50K gradient steps versus 7.88 for V-PG-SwR, reaching 95% of V-PG-SwR's final value ~3x faster. V-PG suffers catastrophic gradient overflow at K=20, while CA-PG maintains stable training. On the real KuaiRec dataset (K=200), CA-PG-SwR outperforms V-PG-SwR at 50K steps (7.10 vs. 5.92) and also at convergence (8.32 vs. 8.26), demonstrating that the credit-assignment variance reduction is most beneficial at large candidate-set sizes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary baseline is V-PG and V-PG-SwR from Ma et al. (WWW 2020), the vanilla policy gradient for two-stage recommender systems. CA-PG and CA-PG-SwR consistently outperform V-PG-SwR in early training (@50K) across all candidate-set sizes, with the gap widening as K grows (e.g., +0.45 policy value at K=20 on synthetic data, +1.18 at K=200 on KuaiRec). At full convergence (@500K), V-PG-SwR retains a slight edge in most settings due to its unbiasedness, but CA-PG-SwR matches or exceeds it on the real KuaiRec data at K=200 (8.32 vs. 8.26). Critically, V-PG without SwR suffers gradient overflow and training failure at K=20, whereas CA-PG trains stably throughout. The paper also shows that CA-PG exploits MoE candidate retrievers more effectively than V-PG-SwR (Table 3: 8.88 vs. 8.58 at M=5, L=1), indicating that the credit-assignment mechanism better leverages diverse expert models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the two-stage policy gradient framework of Ma et al. (2020), which introduced V-PG for training early-stage rankers, and on the sampling-with-replacement (SwR) approximation for Plackett-Luce policies from Chen et al. (2019). The key departure is identifying a credit-assignment problem in V-PG—where the gradient is propagated to the joint probability of the entire candidate set rather than to individual items—and proposing CA-PG, which marginalizes over candidate sets to assign credit only to the specific item that received reward. The theoretical contribution (Theorems 3.1 and 3.3) formally decomposes V-PG into CA-PG plus a residual term and characterizes when CA-PG suffices, while the computational analysis yields TOP1-PG as a simplified O(L) alternative for single-logit PL models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For practitioners building large-scale recommender or RAG systems, V-PG's exponential variance in candidate-set size K makes end-to-end ESR training impractical; CA-PG and TOP1-PG offer a principled, low-variance alternative with ~3× faster convergence at K=20. The finding that a simple top-1 selection probability suffices for single-logit PL models is a valuable practical simplification, though the MoE + full CA-PG combination still yields better exploration. Open questions include extending CA-PG to off-policy (logged-data) settings, handling reward interactions (e.g., diversity) within the final ranking, and understanding the bias-variance tradeoff more precisely as K grows. The paper's scope is limited to position-wise independent rewards and a fixed LSR, which constrains generality.
