## Problem

Unsupervised dense retrievers trained via contrastive learning optimize solely for semantic similarity, causing them to retrieve documents that are topically relevant but temporally misaligned (e.g., returning a 2023 president for a 2019 query). Existing temporal retrieval methods rely on supervised training with explicit timestamps or query rewriting, which are not scalable or feasible when temporal context is implicit. The paper asks whether temporal preference can be injected into an unsupervised retriever using only corpus-level temporal signals from document dumps collected at different times.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262152/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text query (containing explicit temporal markers like 'in 2019' or implicit ones like 'this year') and retrieves from a mixed-timestamp document index built from Wikipedia dumps spanning multiple years. The base encoder is BERT-base-uncased (110M parameters) with 256-token chunks. Beyond standard ranked retrieval, TPOUR supports two additional outputs: (1) continuous temporal alignment via time-vector interpolation between two fine-tuned checkpoints, and (2) document timestamp prediction via a mixture-of-TPOUR classifier that concatenates embeddings from multiple frozen period-specific retrievers into a linear head.

## Method

TPOUR augments standard MoCo contrastive learning with a Temporal Retrieval Preference Optimization (TRPO) loss that reinterprets DPO in the embedding space: given a query Q_i, a temporally aligned document D_i^t (preferred), and a misaligned document D_i^{t'} (less preferred), TRPO penalizes the model for failing to widen the similarity gap S_θ(y^w) − S_θ(y^l) relative to a momentum-updated reference encoder, yielding L_TRPO = −log σ(β[S_θ(y^w)−S_θ(y^l)−(S_ref(y^w)−S_ref(y^l))]). The total objective is L_total = λ·L_CE + (1−λ)·L_TRPO with λ=0.925, balancing semantic and temporal signals. For continuous temporal generalization, the paper extracts time vectors τ_t = θ_t − θ_base from retrievers fine-tuned on distinct periods and linearly interpolates them (θ^{t_mid} = θ_base + (1−α)τ_{t_start} + ατ_{t_end}), enabling alignment to intermediate or even future periods without retraining. The approach requires no labeled query-document pairs, no explicit timestamps in documents, and no query rewriting—temporal preference is learned purely from the distributional differences between Wikipedia dumps collected at different times.

## Results

On SituatedQA (explicit queries), interpolated TPOUR Contriever achieves nDCG@5 of 43.93 (2018), 32.87 (2019), 30.56 (2020), and 40.21 (2021), outperforming the strongest baseline Qwen3-Embedding-8B by +4.04 average nDCG@5 despite being ~72.7× smaller. On implicit queries, the average improvement over Qwen3-Embedding-8B grows to +4.98 nDCG@5. On RealTimeQA (December 2023, explicit), TPOUR reaches 48.24 nDCG@5, surpassing all baselines. Time-vector interpolation between 2018 and 2021 checkpoints matches or exceeds directly fine-tuned models on intermediate periods (e.g., 42.47 vs. 42.47 nDCG@5 on SituatedQA 2019/2020), and extrapolation to 2023 outperforms the most recent 2022 checkpoint (30.00 vs. 27.78 nDCG@5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TPOUR Contriever (interpolated) outperforms all eight baselines across nearly every test period. Against the unsupervised Contriever baseline, TPOUR gains +14.63 nDCG@5 on SituatedQA 2018 (43.93 vs. 29.30) and +12.39 on 2021 (40.21 vs. 37.85) for explicit queries. Against the supervised DPR, TPOUR leads by +15.26 on 2018 and +7.45 on 2021. The closest temporal-aware competitor, Temporal Contrastive, is beaten by +8.93 on 2018 and +4.24 on 2021; TimeR⁴ collapses on RealTimeQA December (8.94 vs. TPOUR's 48.24). Against the much larger Qwen3-Embedding-8B (8B params vs. 110M), TPOUR wins by +3.01 nDCG@5 on 2018 explicit (43.93 vs. 40.92 with query rewriting) and by +14.99 on 2018 implicit (44.11 vs. 29.12), confirming that scale alone does not resolve temporal misalignment without explicit temporal preference optimization. On timestamp prediction, the 2-encoder Mixture-of-TPOUR (220M) surpasses the larger Nomic-Embed v2 MoE (305M) by +21.53% year accuracy (74.56% vs. 53.03%). Interpolation between 2018 and 2021 checkpoints matches directly fine-tuned models on SituatedQA (42.47 vs. 42.47) and exceeds them on RealTimeQA (38.77 vs. 37.30 nDCG@5), demonstrating that no retraining is needed for intermediate periods.

## Relation to Prior Work

TPOUR sits at the intersection of three research threads: unsupervised dense retrieval via contrastive learning (Contriever, MoCo), preference optimization for alignment (DPO), and temporal representation learning (time vectors in finetuned LMs). The key conceptual departure is repurposing DPO's preference-pair framework from generation (where preferences are human-labeled) to retrieval (where preferences are defined by corpus-level temporal grounding), replacing log-likelihood ratios with embedding similarity gaps. The time vector interpolation mechanism, originally demonstrated for generative language models (Nylund et al., 2024), is extended here to encoder-based retrievers, enabling continuous temporal alignment without retraining. Unlike prior temporal retrieval methods (Berberich et al., TimeR4) that require explicit timestamps or supervised temporal labels, TPOUR derives temporal signals purely from the distributional differences between Wikipedia dumps collected at different times.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Temporal misalignment is a practical failure mode in domains like news search and legal retrieval where document relevance is time-dependent, yet most dense retrievers are trained solely on semantic similarity. TPOUR's unsupervised approach removes the need for labeled temporal data, making temporal awareness scalable to large document collections. The BEIR finding—that optimal interpolation weight correlates with dataset publication year—suggests temporal modeling may improve even general-purpose retrieval, opening a broader question about whether time-awareness is a universal retrieval signal. Key limitations include the requirement for temporally distributed document collections, heuristic selection of the interpolation coefficient α, and the observation that gains are more pronounced for explicit than implicit temporal queries, leaving the interpretation of implicit temporal expressions as an open challenge.
