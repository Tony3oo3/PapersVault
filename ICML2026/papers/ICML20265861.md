## Problem

The distortion-perception (D-P) tradeoff in Bayesian inverse problems is well-characterized theoretically, but principled and computationally efficient mechanisms for traversing the D-P curve at inference time in diffusion-based zero-shot inverse algorithms remain inadequately characterized. Existing approaches—adjusting sampling steps, sample averaging, or tuning hyperparameters—are either heuristic or prohibitively expensive, and the most recent theoretical treatment (VSDPS) requires Gaussian posterior assumptions and careful joint calibration of multiple hyperparameters.

## Input / Output

The method takes a degraded observation (noisy, downsampled, masked, or blurred image), the corresponding measurement operator A, and a pretrained diffusion score model (pixel-space or latent-space, e.g., Stable Diffusion v1.5). It produces a single reconstructed image whose position on the D-P curve is controlled by one scalar hyperparameter—the re-noising timestep t0. At t0=0 the output is the pure MAP estimate (maximum distortion, minimum perception error); as t0 increases toward T the output transitions toward full posterior sampling (minimum distortion, maximum perception error), enabling continuous traversal of the tradeoff with a single forward pass of the diffusion model.

## Method

MAP-RPS is a two-stage framework. Stage 1 performs MAP estimation via gradient-based optimization (AdamW with cosine annealing) where the log-prior gradient is estimated using Tweedie's formula at a small fixed timestep t1, yielding a low-distortion initialization that approximates the MMSE solution under strong log-concavity of the posterior (bounded error O(√(n_x/μ))). Stage 2 re-noises the MAP estimate to timestep t0 via the forward SDE, then runs a standard posterior sampling algorithm (DPS, Latent-DPS, PSLD, or Latent-DCDP) from that initialization back to t=0; the re-noising level t0 acts as a continuous knob controlling the W2 perception error, which is theoretically bounded by (ᾱ_{t0})^{1−L_s}·√(2n_x/μ) + ε_score. The latent-space variant LMAP-RPS performs both stages in the VAE latent space, approximating the latent likelihood as log p_{Y|Z}(y|z) ≈ log p_{Y|X}(y|D(z)), enabling plug-and-play use of large pretrained latent diffusion backbones such as Stable Diffusion without any task-specific retraining.

## Results

On MS-COCO, LMAP-RPS(0) achieves SOTA distortion and perception metrics across nearly all six inverse tasks. For example, on 50% inpainting it reaches PSNR 28.14 / FID 61.35, on 4× super-resolution PSNR 25.03 / SSIM 0.6999, and on HDR reconstruction PSNR 25.86 / FID 108.79. LMAP-RPS(600) further improves perception (e.g., FID 87.20 on SR4×, FID 85.01 on anisotropic deblurring) at a modest distortion cost. Inference time at t₀=0 is 45 s/image for inpainting, roughly 5× faster than the next-fastest baseline (Latent-DCDP at 110 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LMAP-RPS(0) outperforms all nine latent-diffusion baselines on PSNR and SSIM across all six MS-COCO tasks, with the closest competitor being Latent-SITCOM on inpainting PSNR (28.06 vs. 28.14) and LDIR on SR4× PSNR (25.01 vs. 25.03). On perception metrics (LPIPS, FID), LMAP-RPS(0) leads on five of six tasks; Latent-DCDP is competitive on anisotropic deblurring FID (81.27 vs. 90.80) but LMAP-RPS(600) recovers the lead (85.01). On the FFHQ D-P traversal experiments, both MAP-RPS and LMAP-RPS produce curves that lie closer to the ideal lower-left corner than VSDPS (Wang et al., 2025) and PSCGAN (Ohayon et al., 2021) across denoising, SR, inpainting, and deblurring. Inference speed is a clear advantage: LMAP-RPS(0) runs in 45 s/image on inpainting versus 242 s for Latent-DPS, 1102 s for ReSample, and 464 s for Latent-DAPS. The paper notes that on low-noise MS-COCO settings (σ_y=0.05), LMAP-RPS(600) can slightly degrade both distortion and perception on inpainting and HDR, where the concentrated posterior makes the two objectives more aligned.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the score-based SDE formulation of diffusion models (Song et al., 2021) and the DPS posterior sampling framework (Chung et al., 2023), but departs from prior D-P traversal strategies that modulate noise variance during sampling (VSDPS, Wang et al. 2025) or rely on expensive sample averaging. Instead, MAP-RPS introduces a two-stage decomposition: a MAP estimation stage that approximates the MMSE endpoint via gradient-based optimization using Tweedie's formula, followed by a re-noised posterior sampling stage whose perception quality is controlled by a single re-noising timestep t₀. The theoretical analysis connects to the MSE-W₂ closed-form tradeoff of Dror et al. (2021) and the Wasserstein contraction results of Kwon et al. (2022), but provides new bounds specific to the MAP-to-posterior-sampling pipeline under strong log-concavity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a principled, theoretically grounded mechanism for D-P traversal that is significantly more computationally efficient than sample-averaging approaches, requiring only one MAP optimization and one posterior sampling pass. The finding that the pure MAP output (t₀=0) already achieves highly competitive performance on MS-COCO challenges the assumption that iterative annealing schedules are always necessary for diffusion-based inverse solving. Key limitations include the strong log-concavity assumption (restricting applicability to unimodal posteriors), reliance on DPS-style score approximations that introduce estimation error, and observed degradation at very low observation noise where distortion and perception metrics become more aligned. Extending the analysis to multimodal posteriors and incorporating asymptotically consistent posterior samplers are identified as important future directions.
