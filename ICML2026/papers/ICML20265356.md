## Problem

Existing FAL algorithms have been analyzed primarily for convergence, leaving their generalization behavior under adversarial perturbations largely uncharacterized. The non-smooth inner maximization in adversarial loss couples the objective's regularity to the attack radius δ, causing SGD-based FAL to exhibit robust overfitting with generalization bounds that degrade as attacks strengthen. This paper asks whether a unified stability-based framework can explain this degradation and whether smoothing or gradient-free techniques can yield perturbation-independent generalization guarantees in the federated setting.

## Input / Output

Each of N federated clients holds n local samples (28×28 MNIST/CIFAR-10 images or high-dimensional tabular vectors from Webspam, Epsilon, SUSY) drawn from a client-specific distribution, along with a shared ℓ∞ perturbation budget δ. The min-max adversarial loss f_i(w_i; z_{i,j}) = max_{‖z−z'‖∞≤δ} g_i(w_i; z') is evaluated per sample, and the global empirical risk averages over all N·n samples. In the black-box FalZO variant, clients only receive function-value queries (no gradient access), so the method must operate purely through randomized finite-difference estimates over b₂ random directions, requiring b₂ > d for high-fidelity gradient approximation.

## Method

The paper establishes a unified ε-uniform stability framework for FAL, proving that the expected generalization gap is bounded by the stability parameter and then deriving concrete stability bounds for three algorithms. For standard SGD-based FAL, the bound scales as O(L((nN)⁻¹L + L_z δ)√T log T), explicitly showing linear dependence on attack strength δ. To eliminate this δ-dependence, FalME replaces the non-smooth adversarial loss with its Moreau envelope M_i(u_i; S_i) = min_{w_i}{(1/n)Σf_i(w_i;z_{i,j}) + (p/2)‖w_i − u_i‖²}, where p > ℓ (the weak-convexity constant) ensures strong convexity of the inner subproblem; the outer update u_i^{t+1} = u_i^t − α_t·p(u_i^t − w_i*) operates on a smooth surrogate, yielding a δ-free bound of O((Lp/(p−ℓ))((L/(Nn)) + 1/n)√T log T). FalZO further extends the analysis to zeroth-order optimization, where gradients are estimated via randomized finite differences over b₂ random unit vectors, introducing an additional approximation-error term μ into the stability bound while preserving the √T log T rate.

## Results

The paper's primary quantitative contribution is a set of uniform-stability generalization bounds for three FAL algorithms. The standard SGD-based FAL yields a perturbation-dependent bound of O((1/(nN) + δ) T^{1/2} log T), while the proposed FalME (Moreau-envelope) achieves a δ-independent bound of O(p/(p−ℓ) · (1/(Nn) + 1/n) T^{1/2} log T), and FalZO (zeroth-order) gives O((1/(nN) + μ + δ) T^{1/2} log T). Empirically, FalME exhibits the mildest degradation in adversarial generalization gap as the attack radius δ increases across MNIST, CIFAR-10, SVHN, Webspam, Epsilon, and SUSY, while maintaining runtime efficiency comparable to standard FAL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to Xing et al. (2021a), which uses on-average stability with a constant step size and yields a bound linear in ηT/n², the proposed FAL achieves a tighter T^{1/2} log T rate via uniform stability with a diminishing step size, and extends the analysis to the federated setting. Against Xiao et al. (2022), whose uniform-stability bound scales linearly in T (O(T/(L_w·n) + δT/L_w)), the paper's T^{1/2} log T rate represents a significant improvement in the dependence on training rounds. The most direct comparison is with Ding et al. (2025), the only prior federated adversarial learning work with stability guarantees: their SGD+SSA variant retains a δT log T perturbation-dependent term, and their SGD+RSA variant achieves only T^{3/4} scaling; in contrast, the proposed FalME eliminates the δ-dependence entirely (δ-independent bound) while recovering the canonical T^{1/2} log T rate, and FalZO extends the guarantee to the gradient-free black-box regime with an additional μ approximation-error term. Empirically, FalME shows the smallest generalization gap under increasing δ across all six datasets, confirming the theoretical advantage of the Moreau-envelope smoothing over both standard FAL and the RSA-based approach of Ding et al.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the algorithmic stability framework of Hardt et al. (2016) from centralized SGD to the federated adversarial setting, building on prior stability analyses of adversarial training by Xing et al. (2021) and Xiao et al. (2022) that established perturbation-dependent generalization bounds. It departs from Ding et al. (2025), the closest prior work on FAL generalization, by replacing randomized smoothness approximation (which retains a δ-dependent term) with Moreau-envelope smoothing that provably eliminates the attack-radius dependence. The zeroth-order extension (FalZO) builds on the federated ZO framework of Fang et al. (2022) and the black-box stability analysis of Nikolakakis et al. (2022), but is the first to combine ZO estimation with adversarial training in a federated stability analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The δ-independent generalization bound for FalME is a clean theoretical result showing that the irreducible error floor from adversarial perturbations (present in all prior FAL stability bounds) can be eliminated through proper smoothing, which has direct implications for designing robust federated systems under strong attacks. The zeroth-order extension addresses a practical gap in privacy-constrained federated deployments where gradient access is unavailable. However, the experimental validation is limited to simple CNNs on MNIST/CIFAR-10/SVHN and tabular data, and the theoretical bounds remain loose (O(√T log T)); open questions include whether the δ-independence holds under non-IID heterogeneity, deeper architectures, and whether the Moreau parameter p can be tuned adaptively without degrading the bound.
