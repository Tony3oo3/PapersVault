## Problem

Existing human motion reconstruction methods treat motion as a flat, single-scale sequence, failing to exploit the natural temporal hierarchy from low-frequency global trajectories to high-frequency local details. This limitation is especially acute under sparse observations (e.g., VR headsets and controllers), where models must infer full-body dynamics from minimal kinematic constraints, often producing floating artifacts, unwanted jitter, or ambiguous poses.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263087/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes three 6-DoF rigid-body tracking signals (head, left hand, right hand) expanded into 18-dimensional feature vectors combining pose with linear and angular velocities, forming an augmented matrix X ∈ R^{T×3×18} over T=20-frame windows. It outputs a full-body SMPL pose sequence θ ∈ R^{T×22×6} using continuous 6D rotation representations for 22 key joints, chosen for their mathematical continuity over quaternions or Euler angles during network training.

## Method

MotionMAR adapts Visual Autoregressive (VAR) next-scale prediction to temporal resolutions, generating motion progressively from coarse (T/4) to fine (T) scales. A Temporal Multi-scale VQ-VAE encodes motion into a 3-level discrete latent pyramid via iterative residual quantization with a shared codebook (V=1024, d=64), separating global trajectory from local jitter. A GPT-2-style Motion Autoregressive Network predicts token indices scale-by-scale under teacher forcing, conditioned by a Scale-aware Control Module that aligns sparse tracking features to each temporal resolution via 1D convolution and linear interpolation, injected through element-wise addition and AdaLN modulation. A Bi-GRU Motion Refinement Network then predicts residual corrections in continuous pose space to eliminate quantization artifacts, trained with axis-angle rotation loss and multi-scale velocity loss. The VQ-VAE is pre-trained and frozen; MAN and MRN are trained separately in two phases.

## Results

On the AMASS dataset (Setting S1, head + both hands as sparse input), MotionMAR achieves the best scores on 7 out of 8 metrics: MPJRE 2.39°, MPJPE 2.82 cm, MPJVE 16.23 cm/s, Hand PE 0.83 cm, Upper PE 1.22 cm, Lower PE 5.13 cm, and Root PE 2.58 cm. The only metric where it is not best is Jitter (5.17), where RPM achieves 4.20. MotionMAR also runs at 61.76 FPS with 42.36M parameters, comfortably exceeding the 30/60 FPS real-time threshold for VR/AR deployment, while diffusion-based SAGE requires 137.35M parameters and 4.11G FLOPs at only 13.81 FPS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MotionMAR outperforms all 12 baselines on 7 of 8 metrics in the primary Setting S1 evaluation. Against the strongest diffusion-based method SAGE, MotionMAR reduces MPJPE from 3.28 to 2.82 cm (−14%), MPJVE from 20.62 to 16.23 cm/s (−21%), and Hand PE from 1.18 to 0.83 cm (−30%), while also improving Lower PE by 15% and Root PE by 12%. Against the best Transformer regression baseline AvatarJLM, gains are even larger: MPJPE drops from 3.35 to 2.82 cm and Jitter from 8.39 to 5.17. The sole exception is the Jitter metric, where RPM's rolling-prediction strategy achieves 4.20 vs. MotionMAR's 5.17, though RPM pays a heavy price in reconstruction accuracy (MPJRE 3.25°, MPJPE 4.08 cm, Hand PE 3.61 cm). On the GORP real-VR dataset (Table 7), MotionMAR also leads with MPJPE 5.98 cm and MPJVE 10.54 cm/s, beating the next-best AvatarJLM (6.07 / 10.79) and RPM (6.83 / 10.93). The paper further shows that replacing SAGE's single-scale VQ with TMT (Table 6) yields only marginal gains (MPJPE 3.28→3.14), confirming that the full autoregressive multi-scale pipeline—not just the tokenizer—is responsible for the large improvements.

## Relation to Prior Work

MotionMAR adapts the Visual Autoregressive (VAR) framework—originally designed for spatial multi-resolution image generation via next-scale prediction (Tian et al., 2024)—to the temporal domain of human motion, replacing spatial resolution levels with temporal resolution levels (T/4, T/2, T). It also builds on the VQ-VAE-based discrete motion representation paradigm established by PoseGPT, MotionGPT, and T2M-GPT, but extends single-scale token prediction to a hierarchical, coarse-to-fine autoregressive process. The key conceptual departure from prior sparse-motion reconstruction methods (AvatarPoser, SAGE, AGRoL, RPM) is the explicit modeling of motion's intrinsic temporal hierarchy: rather than treating the sequence as a flat token stream or decomposing it spatially (upper/lower body), MotionMAR structures generation along time scales, letting low-frequency global trajectories condition high-frequency local dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, biologically motivated architecture for sparse motion reconstruction that achieves SOTA accuracy on AMASS while maintaining real-time inference speed (61.76 FPS), making it directly applicable to VR/AR pipelines. However, the authors acknowledge that the method still fails on extreme motions (e.g., backflips with inverted head orientation) and ambiguous arm-crossing configurations, and that the Motion Refinement Network is a data-driven regularizer rather than a true physics model. Open questions include whether combining spatial and temporal multi-scale hierarchies is feasible within real-time budgets (the paper estimates a ~369× FLOP increase), and whether explicit physics-based priors could close the remaining gap on rare or highly ambiguous motions.
