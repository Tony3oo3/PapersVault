## Problem

In standard decoder-only MLLMs, the causal attention mechanism prevents image tokens (placed before text) from attending to subsequent text tokens, so the visual representation remains static regardless of the user's question. This architectural blind spot is a fundamental cause of vision-language misalignment and object hallucinations, yet prior work has focused on data scaling or connector redesign rather than the attention mechanism itself.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264826/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image (resized to 384×384, encoded by SigLIP into 144 vision tokens via a Perceiver Resampler) and a text query, producing an autoregressive text response. The key operational detail is that Modality-Mutual Attention is applied only during the supervised finetuning stage, where a specific user question exists to condition image-token attention; during pretraining (caption-only pairs) the standard causal mask is retained to avoid information leakage between text tokens.

## Method

The core contribution is Modality-Mutual Attention (MMA), which modifies the causal attention mask M inside the LLM so that image tokens (positions 1…|V|) can attend to question text tokens (positions |V|+1…|V|+|T_Q|), while all other causal constraints remain intact. This is implemented purely by changing the mask entries from −∞ to 0 in the image-to-text block, introducing zero additional parameters and no extra FLOPs. A generalized formulation using a modality-mapping function φ(i) extends MMA to interleaved multi-modal sequences. The authors also study Dual-Order Training (DOT) as an intuitive baseline that trains on both I&T and T&I orders, showing it helps but at 2× training cost and with factorial scaling for n modalities. MMA is applied during SFT with a standard two-stage pipeline (BLIP3-KALE pretraining + multi-task SFT) across three LLM backbones (Phi-3.5-Mini, LLaMA-3.2-3B, Qwen2.5-3B), consistently outperforming CCA, Mixed Attention, and DOT by 1–28% on 12 benchmarks.

## Results

MMA achieves a +6.2% average improvement across 12 multimodal understanding benchmarks and 3 LLM backbones (LLaMA-3.2-3B, Phi-3.5-Mini, Qwen2.5-3B) without introducing any additional parameters. The largest gains appear on knowledge-intensive tasks (up to +28.4% on MMMU with LLaMA-3.2-3B) and general perception (up to +12.3% on MME^C with Phi-3.5-Mini). The full-scale AKI-4B model outperforms 7B-scale MLLMs (e.g., LLaVA-1.5-7B, Honeybee-C-7B) on most benchmarks despite using significantly less training data (6.7B vs. 1.4T pretraining tokens).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MMA consistently outperforms all baselines across nearly all 12 benchmarks for all three LLM backbones. Against the conventional causal attention pipeline (I&T)PT+(I&T)SFT (BLIP-3 style), MMA improves by 1–28% depending on benchmark and backbone. Against CCA (Xing et al., NeurIPS 2024), MMA wins on every benchmark for every backbone, with the largest margin on MMMU (+28.4% for LLaMA-3.2-3B, +12.3% for Phi-3.5-Mini) and MME^C (+12.0–12.3%). Against MA (Xie et al., ICLR 2025), MMA also dominates, particularly on MME^C and LLaVA^W (+9.6–10.4%). The intuitive Dual-Order Training (DOT) baseline shows some vision-centric gains but underperforms MMA on general and knowledge benchmarks (e.g., MMB drops from 64.9 to 43.8 for Phi-3.5-Mini) and requires 2× training time. In the SOTA comparison (Table 3), AKI-4B surpasses 7B models such as LLaVA-1.5-7B and Honeybee-C-7B on MME^P, MMB, SEED^I, LLaVA^W, POPE, MM-Vet, RealWorldQA, and CV-Bench, while remaining competitive on MMMU despite using far less training data (6.7B vs. 1.4T pretraining tokens).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the two dominant strategies for addressing vision-language misalignment in MLLMs—data-centric scaling of instruction tuning samples (Molmo, LLaVA-1.5) and model-centric redesign of the vision-language connector (Honeybee, Cambrian)—by instead identifying a fundamental architectural limitation in the causal attention mechanism of decoder-only LLMs: the standard image-before-text ordering prevents image tokens from attending to text tokens. The closest prior work is Concentric Causal Attention (CCA, Xing et al. 2024), which also modifies attention masks for image tokens but focuses on spatial centrality rather than cross-modal information flow, and Mixed Attention (Xie et al. 2025), which enables full attention among image tokens but still blocks image-to-text paths. MMA carves new ground by unlocking the attention mask to permit image-to-text attention during SFT, a modification that is orthogonal to both connector design and data composition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core insight—that the ubiquitous I&T ordering in causal-attention LLMs creates a one-way information bottleneck that hinders vision-centric reasoning—reframes how the community thinks about MLLM architecture and suggests a drop-in, zero-parameter modification that could be adopted broadly. A key limitation is that MMA is currently restricted to the SFT stage; applying it during pretraining causes information leakage because captioning data lacks the specific user queries that would constrain image-token attention, leaving an open question about how to safely enable cross-modal flow during pretraining. The approach also generalizes conceptually to interleaved and multi-modality settings (e.g., audio, video), but the paper does not yet validate these extensions empirically, and the modest training scale (5k SFT steps for main experiments) raises questions about whether gains persist at full-scale training budgets.
