## Problem

Most schema-grounded information extraction and knowledge graph construction systems assume the schema is already available, yet schema design, cross-source alignment, and maintenance remain expensive and inconsistent. No existing benchmark directly evaluates the upstream task of inducing a reusable schema graph from raw corpora and fusing it with an existing ontology, leaving the quality and auditability of such pipelines unmeasured.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261639/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes train-only raw text (induction_texts.jsonl with doc_id and text fields, chunked at 1200 whitespace tokens) and, in the fusion track, a fixed base ontology package with canonical labels and domain/range or role-signature constraints. It produces a schema graph of typed RE edges (head_type, rel_type, tail_type) and EE event-role edges (event_type, role, ARG) under a strict JSON contract where every item must link to mined candidates with evidence pointers; a fused schema/ontology artifact is additionally emitted when a base ontology is provided. All outputs are accompanied by parse/fallback logs, candidate retention/merging statistics, and run manifests for full auditability.

## Method

SCION is a three-stage auditable pipeline: (1) chunking and corpus bookkeeping over train-only text; (2) construction of a candidate space of entity/relation/event/role items, followed by contract-constrained LLM modules that name, merge, and filter items strictly within that candidate space under a JSON contract with evidence pointers and deterministic validation/fallback; and (3) optional conservative fusion with a base ontology via lexical+embedding+structural alignment with provenance tracking. The default SCION-lite variant mines candidates via aggregated LLM prompts without dependency parsing or embedding clustering, while SCION-full adds Hearst-style pattern mining, dependency/path predicates, and embedding-space clustering. SCION-RL replaces the black-box LLM schema engineer with a compact Qwen3-8B model fine-tuned via offline PPO using RLAIF, where the reward combines five deterministic signals (JSON validity, candidate-space linkage, evidence coverage, compactness, and structural consistency) computed from the generated JSON and mined candidate package, enabling train-only induction without gold schema access.

## Results

On the SCOPE core suite (24 sources: 15 RE + 9 EE), SCION-lite (GPT-5.2 Chat) attains the highest F1 among all compared systems under all four schema-graph similarity metrics: 0.7518 Literal, 0.9298 Fuzzy, 0.8909 Continuous, and 0.7888 Graph F1. The strongest non-SCION baseline (ETA, extract-then-aggregate) scores 0.7063/0.9141/0.8759/0.7629, yielding SCION-lite gains of +0.0455 Literal, +0.0157 Fuzzy, +0.0150 Continuous, and +0.0259 Graph F1. The compact open-model SCION-RL variant (Qwen3-8B) further improves to 0.8025/0.9310/0.9101/0.8228 F1, reducing reliance on proprietary LLMs. Downstream, a fixed extractor using SCION-lite's induced schema improves instance-level F1 from 0.5633 (released-schema reference) to 0.6800, and fusion further lifts it to 0.6900.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCION-lite outperforms all five baselines in Table 1 across all four schema-graph metrics. Against the closest competitor, the matched extract-then-aggregate baseline (ETA), SCION-lite gains +0.0455 Literal F1 (0.7518 vs. 0.7063) and +0.0259 Graph F1 (0.7888 vs. 0.7629), with smaller but consistent margins on Fuzzy (+0.0157) and Continuous (+0.0150). The LLM-only GPT-5.2 Chat baseline (direct ontology induction without candidate-space constraints) trails SCION-lite by +0.0682 Literal F1 and +0.0557 Graph F1, indicating that the candidate-space constraint and contract-based validation contribute meaningfully beyond raw LLM generation. The Text2Onto-style mining baseline (no LLM) is the weakest non-reference method, scoring 0.6299 Literal F1, roughly 12 points below SCION-lite. The released source schemas serve as a representation-formalism reference (not an oracle), and SCION-lite exceeds them by +0.1794 Literal F1, though the authors caution this reflects agreement with the unified typed-edge formalism rather than superiority over human ontology design. The DeepSeek-R1 LLM-only baseline underperforms GPT-5.2 Chat substantially (0.5664 vs. 0.6836 Literal F1), highlighting backend sensitivity in the LLM-only setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper inherits the mining-first ontology learning paradigm established by Hearst patterns and Text2Onto, but deliberately reframes it as a controlled, auditable pipeline rather than a new architecture. It extends the LLM-assisted ontology alignment literature (He et al., 2023; Hertling & Paulheim, 2023) by making fusion a first-class, provenance-tracked stage within an end-to-end corpus-to-schema protocol. The paper positions itself against the LLMs4OL/4OM challenges by arguing those benchmarks evaluate individual sub-tasks (term typing, taxonomy discovery) in isolation, whereas SCOPE evaluates the full induction-plus-fusion pipeline under a unified typed-edge schema-graph formalism. It also connects to schema-grounded IE work (Feng et al., 2024; Dagdelen et al., 2024; Bai et al., 2024) by addressing the upstream schema-construction step those systems assume is already available.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Schema graphs are the upstream bottleneck for schema-grounded IE and KG construction, yet most extraction systems silently assume the schema exists; SCOPE makes this induction step measurable and reproducible with a train-text-only protocol across 24 heterogeneous sources. The auditable pipeline (SCION) provides a practical reference point with explicit controllability statistics (parse success, fallback rates, evidence-linked outputs) that prior ontology learning work has not systematically reported. Key limitations include the inability to rule out pretraining contamination on popular public IE sources, the absence of inter-event temporal/causal links from the core EE target, and sensitivity to the chosen schema-graph formalism. Open questions include whether induced schemas can be maintained under domain drift, how they affect long-term downstream extraction quality, and whether the fusion track can be strengthened with larger human-audited mapping sets.
