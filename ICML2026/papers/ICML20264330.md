## Problem

Existing latent-space models for Neural Combinatorial Optimization either require expensive labeled data for supervised training (CVAE-Opt) or use an instance-independent latent prior that cannot adapt to specific problem instances (COMPASS). Moreover, standard NCO inference strategies (beam search, active search, evolutionary algorithms) lack principled convergence guarantees and often underperform in low-budget regimes. This paper addresses both gaps by introducing an instance-conditioned latent model trained purely with RL and a guided inference procedure with rigorous theoretical guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method takes a problem instance x = {x_i}_{i=1}^n ∈ R^{n×d_x} (node coordinates for TSP; coordinates plus demands for CVRP) and produces a feasible tour y = (y_1, …, y_T) with associated cost C(y,x). Internally, the encoder maps x to a d_z=100-dimensional Gaussian latent vector z, and the decoder autoregressively generates the node sequence conditioned on both x and z, enforcing feasibility via step-wise masking (visited-node exclusion for TSP; capacity tracking for CVRP). At inference, K interacting particles jointly explore the latent space, and the best decoded solution across all particles and iterations is returned.

## Method

LGS-Net is a latent-variable model where an instance-conditioned encoder p_φ(z|x) (6-layer multi-head attention producing a Gaussian mean and log-variance) maps the problem instance to a continuous latent space, and a single-layer attention decoder p_θ(y|x,z) generates solutions autoregressively. Training uses REINFORCE with a cost-weighted loss w^k = exp(−C(y^k,x)/τ) inspired by IWAE and best-of-many objectives, plus entropic regularization to encourage solution diversity, all optimized via Adam without any labeled data. At inference, Latent Guided Sampling (LGS) runs K interacting Metropolis-Hastings chains in latent space with proposals based on inter-particle differences (analogous to differential evolution), while simultaneously updating decoder parameters via Stochastic Approximation with a score-function gradient estimator. The key theoretical contribution is proving that the joint (z, y) iterates form a time-inhomogeneous Markov Chain that converges geometrically to the cost-reweighted target distribution, with explicit bounds decomposing mixing, tracking, and optimization errors.

## Results

On TSP-100, LGS-Net achieves an objective of 7.752 with a gap of only 0.002% to the Concorde optimum, matching the exact solver. On CVRP-100, LGS-Net reaches 15.524, actually beating the LKH3 solver (15.54) with a negative gap of −0.102%, and similarly outperforms LKH3 on CVRP-125 (17.496 vs. 17.50, gap −0.022%). Across all six problem settings (TSP/CVRP at n=100, 125, 150), LGS-Net consistently achieves the lowest gap among all learning-based NCO methods, with gaps ranging from 0.002% to 0.343%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LGS-Net outperforms all NCO baselines across every TSP and CVRP setting. On TSP, the closest competitor is COMPASS (gap 0.014% at n=100 vs. LGS-Net's 0.002%), while EAS and POMO(sampling) trail by roughly 0.19–0.21% at n=100; CVAE-Opt, ELG, and CNF lag further behind (0.35–1.45% gap). On CVRP, LGS-Net is the only learning-based method to beat the LKH3 solver, achieving negative gaps at n=100 (−0.102%) and n=125 (−0.022%), whereas COMPASS and EAS remain 0.13–0.26% above LKH3. Among inference strategies tested with the same trained model (Table 2), LGS (interacting MCMC + SA) achieves the best CVRP-100 objective (15.524), outperforming DE (15.561), CMA-ES (15.582), SGLD (15.607), and EAS (15.685), confirming that the combination of particle interaction and parameter adaptation is critical. The generalization-boosting methods ELG and CNF improve over POMO but still fall short of LGS-Net by 0.2–3.7% gap across settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the latent-space approach to Neural Combinatorial Optimization initiated by CVAE-Opt (Hottung et al., 2021) and COMPASS (Chalumeau et al., 2023), but departs from both in key ways: unlike CVAE-Opt, it requires no labeled data (trained via RL), and unlike COMPASS, its latent distribution is conditioned on the problem instance rather than being instance-independent. The encoder architecture follows the Attention Model of Kool et al. (2019), while the inference procedure replaces the differential evolution or CMA-ES used in prior latent-space methods with a novel interacting MCMC scheme coupled to stochastic approximation, for which the authors derive new convergence guarantees for time-inhomogeneous Markov chains where both the kernel and target distribution evolve.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LGS-Net sets a new benchmark among learning-based NCO methods on TSP and CVRP, notably achieving negative gaps (beating LKH3) on CVRP at n=100 and n=125, and the theoretical convergence analysis for time-inhomogeneous MCMC fills a gap in the adaptive MCMC literature. The finding that gradient-based inference (Adam, SGLD) underperforms MCMC-based exploration in the latent space challenges the common assumption that parameter updates via backpropagation are the natural inference-time strategy. Open questions include scaling to much larger instances (beyond n=150), handling global constraints that cannot be enforced by decoder-side masking, and determining the optimal frequency of parameter updates during inference to balance exploration and exploitation.
