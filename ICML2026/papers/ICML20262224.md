## Problem

Existing spectral operators for higher-order structures (Hodge Laplacian, Multiorder Laplacian, Dirac operator) decompose topology into separate rank-local operators, leaving practitioners to fuse multi-rank information back to vertices through ad hoc, application-specific heuristics. No principled construction yields a single vertex-level operator that encodes how higher-order cells mediate vertex dynamics, creating a persistent bottleneck for node-level tasks such as spectral clustering, signal smoothing, and molecular property prediction.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a finite higher-order complex (CW, simplicial, or combinatorial) specified by its boundary or incidence matrices between adjacent ranks, along with per-rank adjacency weights β_k and coupling weights γ_k. It produces a single (generally dense) vertex-level operator S_ε that is applied implicitly via sparse Conjugate Gradient solves rather than explicit matrix construction, yielding vertex-level signals, spectral features (eigenvalues/eigenvectors), cluster assignments, or positional encodings for downstream GNNs. The operator's density reflects non-local couplings induced by shared higher-order cells, and its structure depends on the specific topological choices in constructing the complex.

## Method

The paper constructs a Graded Laplacian L* as a block-tridiagonal operator on the full graded cochain space, with Hodge Laplacians as diagonal blocks and weighted boundary matrices as off-diagonal coupling blocks between adjacent ranks. The key step is partitioning L* into a vertex block A and a higher-order block C with coupling X, then computing the Schur complement S = A − XC^{-1}X^T to marginalize all higher-order degrees of freedom onto the vertex set. Tikhonov regularization (C + εI) handles the homological kernel of C, and a proposition bounds the approximation error as ε→0. The resulting operator is proven PSD with a spectral upper bound relative to the rank-0 Hodge Laplacian, and its action is applied implicitly via sparse CG to avoid densification, achieving O(M/m) speedup over higher-order message passing per forward pass.

## Results

On the Topotein protein segmentation task, the collapsed operator S_ε achieves 70.9% accuracy versus 46.9% for the standard graph Laplacian, a 24-point improvement in recovering interleaved secondary-structure elements. On the MANTRA Betti-number classification benchmark, the spectrum of S_ε reaches 0.95 accuracy on β_1 (vs. 0.92 for the graph Laplacian and 0.97 for the Multiorder Laplacian), substantially outperforming all GNN baselines (0.31–0.39). For transductive protein node classification, GT+SchurPE attains 0.573 on Contact and 0.789 on ResType, surpassing GT+LaplacePE (0.551 / 0.762). On real-world network clustering, S_ε yields consistent gains of +2–3% over L^G (e.g., 0.50 vs. 0.47 on Football, 0.66 vs. 0.64 on Les Misérables).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Multiorder Laplacian (Lucas et al., 2020), S_ε wins on 6 of 8 SBM variants (notably +8% on Dense-Cliques, +11% on Cliques+Noise, +7% on Unequal-Sizes) but loses on Large-Sparse (0.78 vs. 0.82); on real-world networks S_ε consistently beats L^(mult) (e.g., 0.66 vs. 0.52 on Misérables). Against the standard graph Laplacian L^G, S_ε ties or wins on all real-world networks and SBM variants, with the largest gap on Dense-Cliques (0.98 vs. 0.96) and Unequal-Sizes (0.98 vs. 0.92). On MANTRA β_1, S_ε (0.95) outperforms all GNN-based baselines by 56–83 points (GAT 0.31, GCN 0.31, TAG 0.32, UniMP 0.33, CellMP 0.39, DECT 0.32, SAN 0.12, SCCNN 0.03, SCN 0.21) and edges out CT (0.93) and SCCN (0.93), though it trails the Multiorder Laplacian spectrum (0.97). For protein node classification, SchurPE improves over LaplacePE in both GCN (0.494 vs. 0.480 on Contact) and GT (0.573 vs. 0.551 on Contact; 0.789 vs. 0.762 on ResType), confirming that the collapsed operator provides a stronger positional signal than the standard Laplacian eigenvectors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard Hodge Laplacian framework (Eckmann 1944; Lim 2020), which produces separate rank-local operators, by introducing a Graded Laplacian that couples all ranks simultaneously through off-diagonal boundary-map blocks. The key algebraic move is Schur complementation (Boyd & Vandenberghe 2004), a tool well-established in electrical network reduction and Gaussian graphical model marginalization, here repurposed to collapse topological ranks rather than spatial vertex subsets. The main empirical comparison is the Multiorder Laplacian (Lucas et al. 2020), which aggregates per-order vertex Laplacians additively; the paper's operator instead models higher-order cells as mediating conductance that is subtracted from pairwise coupling, yielding a fundamentally different spectral structure. In the Topological Deep Learning lineage (HOMP, cellular transformers), this work provides a principled vertex-level operator that avoids the multi-rank message-passing bottleneck, positioning itself as a preprocessing/positional-encoding component rather than a full architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The collapsed operator offers a single, principled construction for the 'fusion problem' of transferring higher-order topological information to vertex-level tasks, replacing ad hoc aggregation heuristics with a Schur-complement derivation that preserves positive semi-definiteness and admits clean spectral bounds (S ⪯ A). The practical impact is demonstrated across protein structure segmentation (24-point accuracy gain), noise-robust signal denoising, and spectral positional encodings for GNNs. Key limitations remain: the operator is typically dense (mitigated by implicit CG application), rank-local invariants like individual Betti numbers are partially lost under collapse, and Cheeger-type lower bounds do not transfer. Open directions include metric-aware per-cell weights, time-varying topologies, and learned effective operators that could adapt the collapse to task-specific needs.
