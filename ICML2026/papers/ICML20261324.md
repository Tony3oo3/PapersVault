## Problem

Optimizing expectations over categorical distributions requires differentiating through discrete sampling, which is non-differentiable. Existing continuous relaxations (Gumbel-Softmax, Straight-Through) impose a rigid bias-variance tradeoff controlled by a single temperature parameter, with no principled way to interpolate between the two extremes. The paper asks whether denoising diffusion models can provide a more flexible family of soft reparameterizations for categorical distributions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261324/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes categorical logits φ_θ ∈ R^{L×K} (e.g., 81×10 for Sudoku, 576×16384 for VQ-VAE image codes), a differentiable loss or reward function f, and a Gaussian noise sample X_1 ~ N(0, I_K)^{⊗L}. It produces a pathwise gradient estimate J_θ T_0^θ(X_1)^⊤ ∇_x f(X_0) and, in the guidance setting, steered discrete samples. Crucially, the denoiser is computed in closed form (no learned network), so the only tunable hyperparameters are the number of diffusion steps n and the first timestep t_1, which jointly control the relaxation strength.

## Method

REDGE exploits the fact that for a factorized categorical distribution on simplex vertices, the posterior-mean denoiser admits the closed-form expression x̂_0^θ(x_t, t) = softmax(φ_θ + α_t x_t / σ_t²), eliminating the need for a learned denoiser. It then applies DDIM deterministic reverse transitions iteratively to map Gaussian noise to an approximate, differentiable sample from π_θ, yielding a training-free reparameterization. The number of steps n and the first timestep t_1 play the role of a temperature: t_1 = 1 recovers the Straight-Through estimator, while more steps with smaller t_1 approach the true discrete distribution. Proposition 1 proves that as t_1 → 0 the Jacobian of the transport map vanishes almost surely, explaining why overly tight relaxations produce uninformative gradients. Two extensions are provided: REINDGE replaces the Straight-Through approximation inside the conditional expectation with the REINMAX estimator, and REDGE-COV uses a parameter-dependent diagonal-covariance Gaussian base distribution matched to the mean and variance of π_θ.

## Results

On Sudoku solving guided from a weak (1%) masked-diffusion checkpoint, REDGE raises the solve rate to 89%, outperforming the strongest baselines which plateau in the mid-80s; from a strong (90%) checkpoint, REDGE-COV further improves to 93%. On CLIP-guided image generation with a pretrained MaskGIT model (576 VQ-VAE codes, 16384-entry codebook), REDGE, REDGE-COV, and STRAIGHT-THROUGH achieve comparable CLIP scores that substantially surpass REINMAX and outperform REINDGE and GUMBEL-SOFTMAX. In a no-prior direct-optimization Sudoku variant, the best estimators reach mid-to-high 90s solve rates, though REDGE and GUMBEL-SOFTMAX lag behind in that specific setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against STRAIGHT-THROUGH, REDGE clearly wins on Sudoku (ST 'performs substantially worse' from the 1% checkpoint) but ties on CLIP-guided image generation, where both achieve comparable CLIP scores. Against GUMBEL-SOFTMAX, REDGE outperforms on both benchmarks: on Sudoku it reaches 89% vs. lower solve rates for GUMBEL-SOFTMAX, and on CLIP-guided generation it achieves higher CLIP scores. Against REINMAX, REDGE substantially surpasses it on CLIP-guided image generation and outperforms it on Sudoku from the 1% checkpoint (where REINMAX plateaus in the mid-80s). The REINDGE variant (REINMAX-based REDGE) also underperforms REDGE on CLIP-guided generation. Overall, REDGE is described as the most reliable and robust method across diverse objectives, while REDGE-COV can be stronger in favorable regimes but is more hyperparameter-sensitive.

## Relation to Prior Work

REDGE repurposes the DDIM deterministic reverse-transition framework (Song et al., 2021; Ho et al., 2020) for a fundamentally different purpose: instead of learning a denoiser with a neural network to generate data, it exploits the closed-form posterior-mean denoiser available for factorized categorical distributions to construct a training-free, differentiable sampling map from Gaussian noise to π_θ. This positions REDGE as a new continuous relaxation in the same family as Gumbel-Softmax (Maddison et al., 2017; Jang et al., 2017), but with the number of diffusion steps and the first timestep t_1 playing the role of the temperature parameter. The method elegantly unifies prior estimators: the one-step limit recovers both the hard Straight-Through estimator (Bengio et al., 2013) and REINMAX (Liu et al., 2023a), while the multi-step regime interpolates between them. The application to inference-time guidance in masked diffusion models (Shi et al., 2024; Sahoo et al., 2024) connects to the broader line of reward-guided discrete generation (Murata et al., 2025).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

REDGE provides a principled, training-free alternative to Gumbel-Softmax for differentiable categorical sampling, with a clear theoretical characterization (Proposition 1) of when gradients become uninformative—giving practitioners concrete guidance on the t_1 hyperparameter that plays the role of temperature. The method's practical impact is demonstrated across diverse settings (Sudoku solving, CLIP-guided image generation, direct discrete optimization), where it consistently matches or outperforms strong baselines with a broad operating range of hyperparameters. Open questions include combining REDGE with REBAR/RELAX-style learned control variates to further reduce residual bias, and extending the analysis to correlated (non-factorized) categorical structures. The parameter-dependent base distribution (REDGE-Cov) is promising but more hyperparameter-sensitive, suggesting the optimal initialization strategy remains an active design question.
