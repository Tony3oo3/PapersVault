## Problem

Existing dynamic computation allocation methods for LLMs rely on greedy routing—a binary execute-or-skip mechanism that causes irreversible information loss and makes short-sighted token selection decisions based solely on immediate performance impact. No prior method considers whether a skipped token's transformation can be cheaply approximated, leaving a gap between full computation and complete discarding that degrades both perplexity and reasoning accuracy at moderate sparsity levels.

## Input / Output

The method processes text sequences through a standard transformer LLM (Llama 3.1-8B or 3.2-3B), where each token embedding at every layer is routed either through the full self-attention or FFN module or through a corresponding Lightweight Feature Forecaster (a bottleneck MLP with hidden dimension r=100 vs. d=4096). The router produces per-token binary decisions via Gumbel-Softmax sampling with annealed temperature, and the system enforces a target global sparsity (e.g., 25% or 40%) via an L1 penalty on the compute fraction during training. The final output is generated text, with the routing mask also enabling optional KV-cache reduction by masking attention to LFF-routed tokens.

## Method

The paper introduces informed routing, which replaces the binary execute-or-skip paradigm with an execute-or-approximate policy guided by a Lightweight Feature Forecaster (LFF). Each computational unit (self-attention or FFN) is paired with a small bottleneck network (W₁∈R^{r×d}, W₂∈R^{d×r}, r≪d) trained to predict the unit's normalized output using cosine similarity and L₁ loss. A three-stage pipeline trains the system: (1) LFFs are fitted to their corresponding units with frozen LLM weights in massive parallelism, (2) routers (two-layer MLPs) are trained with Gumbel-Softmax to select between the original unit and its LFF per token, optimizing LM loss plus a sparsity penalty, and (3) optional LoRA fine-tuning polishes the final model. The router's intermediate hidden dimension is reduced by 200 to absorb the LFF parameters, keeping total parameter count identical to SkipGPT for fair comparison. The key insight is that the LFF's forecasting error provides a recoverability-based importance signal—tokens with simple, linearly approximable transformations (especially in self-attention) are routed to the LFF, while semantically rich content words retain full computation.

## Results

At 25% sparsity on Llama-3.1-8B, LFF-Router (without fine-tuning) achieves 64.62% average reasoning accuracy and 9.43 WikiText-2 perplexity, outperforming SkipGPT-Router (51.23% / 20.76) by over 13 points in accuracy and 11 points in PPL. After LoRA fine-tuning, LFF-LoRA reaches 66.16% accuracy and 8.91 PPL, surpassing SkipGPT-LoRA (60.43% / 10.53) on 15 of 16 tasks. At 40% sparsity, LFF-LoRA maintains 60.24% accuracy and 11.11 PPL versus SkipGPT-LoRA's 58.85% / 14.35. The pure-PyTorch inference pipeline achieves 1.5× speedup at 50% sparsity with 4096-token sequences, while the Triton custom-operator version delivers up to 1.62× speedup at 50% sparsity with batch size 8.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary dynamic-routing baseline SkipGPT, LFF-Router (no fine-tuning) achieves 64.62% average reasoning accuracy versus SkipGPT-Router's 51.23% at 25% sparsity—a 13.4-point gap—and 9.43 PPL versus 20.76. Notably, LFF-Router without any fine-tuning already surpasses the fully LoRA-fine-tuned SkipGPT-LoRA (64.62% vs. 60.43% accuracy) while saving over 50% of training time. After both methods receive LoRA fine-tuning, LFF-LoRA wins on 15 of 16 individual tasks, with the largest gaps on OBQA (+12.2 pts) and PIQA (+19.9 pts). Against static pruning baselines, LFF-Router outperforms SliceGPT (64.62% vs. 53.37%), Shortened-LLaMA (64.62% vs. 62.15%), and ShortGPT (64.62% vs. 62.35%) on average reasoning accuracy, though SliceGPT edges out LFF-Router by 0.21 on WikiText-2 PPL (9.22 vs. 9.43). Against other dynamic allocation methods, LFF-Router dominates MoD (64.62% vs. 44.98%) and D-LLM (64.62% vs. 41.91%) by 19–23 points in accuracy, with PPL gaps exceeding 25 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dynamic computation allocation (DCA) line initiated by Mixture-of-Depths (MoD) and D-LLM, and most directly builds on SkipGPT's sub-module-level (attention vs. FFN) routing granularity. The key conceptual departure is replacing the binary execute-or-skip decision with an execute-or-approximate policy, where a Lightweight Feature Forecaster (LFF) provides a low-rank bottleneck approximation of each unit's output for tokens the router deems predictable. This positions the work between prior DCA methods (which discard computation entirely) and error-compensation approaches like RECAP, PRUNE&COMP, and Olica (which compensate at the weight or channel level), by operating at the token level with a learned per-token approximation rather than a fixed structural correction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that self-attention's per-token transformation is 'linearly simple'—well approximated by a ~0.8M-parameter bottleneck—offers a concrete, linguistically grounded explanation for why attention is more redundant than FFN, and gives the router a recoverability-based criterion rather than a myopic importance score. Practically, the method delivers consistent 1.5×–1.62× wall-clock speedups with a Triton kernel and outperforms SkipGPT by 10–20% on reasoning tasks without requiring global fine-tuning. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the Llama family to architectures with grouped-query attention or MoE FFNs, how the three-stage training pipeline scales to 70B+ models, and whether the KV-cache reduction trade-off (2.3 PPL penalty) can be mitigated without sacrificing the approximation quality that drives the method's gains.
