## Problem

KL divergence regularization in optimal control and reinforcement learning becomes singular as process noise vanishes: the quadratic control penalty coefficient scales as Σ_w^{-1}, driving the optimal feedback gain to zero and yielding ill-posed problems in the deterministic limit. The paper asks whether replacing the Fisher-Rao geometry underlying the KL's dynamical formulation with state-space-aware transport geometries can produce divergences that remain finite and yield well-posed control across all noise regimes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264245/cropped_page5_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

For divergence computation, the method takes two probability densities (or elliptic distribution parameters m, Σ, g) and returns a scalar divergence value via a time-weighted action integral along G-dual geodesics. For control, it takes LTI system matrices (A, B), state cost Q, noise covariance Σ_w, and discount factor γ, and produces a closed-form optimal feedback gain F obtained by solving a discrete algebraic Riccati equation with a geometry-dependent control penalty R_∘. The key structural property is that R_KW = B^T(Σ_w + λI)^{-1}B smoothly interpolates between the singular KL penalty (λ→0) and the noise-independent WKL penalty (Σ_w→0), while R_O = B^T B is entirely independent of Σ_w.

## Method

The paper constructs state-space-aware KL divergences by replacing the Fisher-Rao Onsager operator in the dynamical formulation of KL with transport-based Onsager operators (Otto/Wasserstein, linearized Wasserstein, Kalman-Wasserstein, Stein), each defining a Riemannian metric on the infinite-dimensional density manifold. G-dual geodesics connecting two distributions are characterized as metric gradient flows of a quadratic potential energy, and for elliptic distributions the resulting mean-covariance ODEs admit closed-form solutions, yielding explicit divergence formulas. In the LQR setting, the divergence between the controlled transition distribution N(Ax+Bu, Σ_w) and the uncontrolled one N(Ax, Σ_w) reduces to a quadratic control penalty ½u^T R_∘ u, where R_∘ depends on the chosen geometry. The Kalman-Wasserstein choice R_KW = B^T(Σ_w+λI)^{-1}B provides a principled noise floor that regularizes the control problem without the ad-hoc covariance inflation used in ensemble Kalman methods, and the resulting Riccati equation yields a non-degenerate optimal gain F_KW in the zero-noise limit.

## Results

In the LQR setting with Gaussian process noise covariance Σ_w = ρI, the classical KL regularizer yields a control penalty R_FR = B^T Σ_w^{-1} B that diverges as ρ → 0, driving the optimal gain F_KL → 0 and the closed-loop spectral radius toward the unit circle (near-instability). In contrast, the WKL penalty R_O = B^T B is independent of ρ, and the KWKL penalty R_KW = B^T(Σ_w + λI)^{-1}B remains finite for all ρ ≥ 0. On the double integrator (Q = I, γ = 0.9, λ = 1), F_KW smoothly interpolates: lim_{ρ→0} F_KW = F_WKL (nonzero) while lim_{ρ→∞} F_KW = F_KL, and the closed-loop spectral radius stays well below 1 across all noise levels. On the nonlinear cart-pole, KL-regularized LQR produces large oscillations at low noise, whereas WKL- and KWKL-regularized controllers yield increasingly well-damped trajectories.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three divergence-based regularizers (KL/Fisher-Rao, WKL/Otto, KWKL/Kalman-Wasserstein) in the same LQR and path-integral control framework. The classical KL baseline (Kullback & Leibler, 1951) is the primary point of contrast: its quadratic control penalty scales as 1/ρ, so as process noise vanishes the optimal feedback gain collapses to zero and the closed-loop system approaches the stability boundary (spectral radius → 1). The WKL baseline (Datar & Ay, 2026a) avoids this singularity entirely since its penalty R_O = B^T B is noise-independent, but it treats all noise levels identically and does not recover the KL behavior in the high-noise regime. The proposed KWKL method wins on well-posedness: it remains finite at ρ = 0 (unlike KL) while smoothly recovering the KL penalty as ρ → ∞ (unlike WKL), giving a single parameter λ that interpolates between the two extremes. On the anisotropic-noise double integrator, KWKL additionally adapts direction-wise—matching WKL in low-noise directions and KL in high-noise directions—whereas WKL treats all directions identically and KL suppresses control in low-noise directions. The path-integral (trajectory-level) formulation confirms the same qualitative ordering: u_KL → 0 as ρ → 0 while u_KW → u_WKL ≠ 0.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the canonical contrast function framework of Felice & Ay (2021) and Ay & Amari (2015) by generalizing the construction from the Fisher-Rao geometry to arbitrary Riemannian geometries on the density manifold, including Wasserstein, Kalman-Wasserstein, linearized Wasserstein, and Stein geometries. The Wasserstein-specific case was previously studied by Ay (2024) and Datar & Ay (2026a) for Gaussians; here the authors derive closed-form expressions for the broader class of elliptic distributions and introduce the Kalman-Wasserstein variant. In the control setting, the paper departs from classical KL-regularized LQR (Todorov 2006; Kappen 2005) by replacing the Fisher-Rao-based penalty with transport-based divergences that remain finite in the deterministic limit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a concrete pathology in KL-regularized control: the quadratic control penalty coefficient scales as Σ_w^{-1} and diverges as process noise vanishes, making the problem ill-posed. The WKL and KWKL regularizers remove this singularity while preserving the variational (Riccati) structure, yielding well-posed problems across all noise regimes. A notable conceptual contribution is showing that the Kalman-Wasserstein divergence between equal-covariance Gaussians exactly recovers the 'additive covariance inflation' heuristic long used in ensemble Kalman filtering, providing a geometric justification for this ad-hoc technique. The main limitation is that validation is restricted to linear feedback policies on LTI systems with Gaussian noise; the authors explicitly frame integration into model-free or model-based deep RL as future work, leaving open whether these divergences offer practical benefits in high-dimensional, nonlinear policy optimization.
