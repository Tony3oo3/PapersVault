## Problem

BERT encoder representations become increasingly unexploitable by frozen probes as model scale increases, despite improved perplexity. This degradation originates in BERT's flat architecture, which couples representation learning to the token reconstruction loss, causing features to over-specialize on local reconstruction signals rather than developing versatile high-level abstractions. The paper asks whether separating these two objectives architecturally can restore monotonic scaling of frozen representation quality.

## Input / Output

The input is a tokenized text sequence partitioned into visible and masked token sets; the encoder processes only unmasked tokens with RoPE positional encoding, while the lightweight predictor receives <MASK> placeholders and their positional indices but never sees the masked tokens' content. The Complementary Masking Strategy augments each batch with its inverse mask, creating two complementary views processed in parallel with attention masks preventing information leakage between them. Outputs include contextualized token representations from the encoder (used as frozen features), reconstructed tokens from the predictor, and pooled sentence/document embeddings via a cross-attention adapter for downstream retrieval and classification tasks.

## Method

CrossBERT introduces a bipartite architecture that decouples representation learning (a deep encoder) from token reconstruction (a shallow, cross-attention-only predictor of roughly one-fourth the encoder depth), directly inspired by Masked Autoencoders in vision. The predictor's cross-attention-only design—lacking self-attention between masked tokens—forces it to act strictly as a readout interface querying encoder embeddings, ensuring the encoder optimizes for semantic abstraction rather than token prediction. The Complementary Masking Strategy processes the inverse mask in parallel within the same batch, collecting gradients from every token and doubling sample efficiency, while the high masking ratio (≥50%) reduces encoder compute by 1.5–2× compared to standard BERT. The predictor can be repurposed as a warm-started pooling adapter for contrastive finetuning on frozen features, accelerating convergence and improving MTEB scores by approximately 2%. A scaling sweep from 2×10¹⁸ to 1×10²¹ FLOPs demonstrates monotonic scaling of frozen representation quality, in sharp contrast to BERT's degradation at larger scales where frozen performance drops below that of the smallest model in the sweep.

## Results

On the GLUE linear probe (frozen backbone), CrossBERT achieves 73.8 average, surpassing BERT (67.4) by +6.4 points and ModernBERT (71.2) by +2.6 points, while using ~100× less compute than ModernBERT. On MTEB(eng, v1) with frozen contrastive finetuning on MS-MARCO, CrossBERT scores 53.6 overall versus 42.1 for BERT (+11.5) and 22.4 for Electra, with a particularly large gap on Retrieval (40.7 vs. 19.2). Under full finetuning, CrossBERT matches BERT on GLUE (86.6 vs. 86.4) and leads on MTEB(eng, v1) at 54.5, outperforming NeoBERT (51.3) and ModernBERT (46.9) despite using 4.1e19 FLOPs versus their 2.9e21 and 4.9e21 respectively. Training throughput reaches 207k tokens/sec (1.68× faster than BERT's 123k) thanks to the 50% masking ratio and Complementary Masking Strategy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against BERT (same backbone, flat MLM), CrossBERT wins decisively on frozen evaluations: +6.4 on GLUE linear probe and +11.5 on MTEB frozen overall, while matching on full finetuning GLUE (86.6 vs. 86.4) and slightly leading on unfrozen MTEB (54.5 vs. 53.9). Against Electra (RTD objective on flat backbone), CrossBERT loses on GLUE linear probe (73.8 vs. 76.9) but dominates on sentence-embedding tasks, scoring 53.6 vs. 22.4 on frozen MTEB and 54.5 vs. 49.0 on unfrozen MTEB, with Electra's Retrieval score collapsing to 0.0. Against ModernBERT and NeoBERT, which use 70–120× more compute, CrossBERT still leads on frozen GLUE (73.8 vs. 71.2 and 58.7) and on unfrozen MTEB (54.5 vs. 46.9 and 51.3), though it trails on full-finetuning GLUE (86.6 vs. 90.5 and 88.8). Against DeBERTaV3, CrossBERT trails on GLUE linear probe (73.8 vs. 81.3) and full-finetuning GLUE (86.6 vs. 91.4), but DeBERTaV3 collapses catastrophically on MTEB (27.1 unfrozen, Retrieval 4.0), confirming that GLUE classification alone masks representation-quality failures that MTEB exposes.

## Relation to Prior Work

CrossBERT directly transplants the Masked Autoencoder (MAE) paradigm from vision (He et al., 2022; Fu et al., 2024) into text, where the bipartite encoder–lightweight-predictor split had not previously been applied to encoder-only models. It departs from the flat BERT lineage (BERT, ModernBERT, NeoBERT, DeBERTaV3, OptiBERT) by arguing that their coupling of representation learning to token reconstruction is the root cause of frozen-feature degradation at scale, a failure mode documented by Dervishi et al. (2025) but not architecturally addressed. Unlike T5, which also uses a bipartite structure, CrossBERT's predictor is a shallow cross-attention-only module that never sees masked token content, strictly offloading reconstruction so the encoder optimizes for semantic abstraction. The paper also repositions evaluation from full finetuning to frozen probing (linear, kNN, contrastive), aligning text-encoder assessment with the standards already adopted in vision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that standard BERT frozen representations *degrade* monotonically with scale—dropping below the smallest model in the sweep at the largest configurations—challenges a core assumption that more compute always improves pre-trained features, and it is well-evidenced across 8–9 model sizes spanning 2×10¹⁸ to 10²¹ FLOPs on both GLUE and MTEB. Practically, CrossBERT's 50% masking with Complementary Masking Strategy yields ~1.5–2× throughput and ~2× sample efficiency, making large-scale encoder pre-training substantially cheaper. Open questions remain: the paper offers no theoretical account of *why* coupling representation and reconstruction degrades frozen features, all models are ≤5B parameters so behavior at decoder-scale is unknown, and the reconstruction objective (MLM) could potentially be swapped for alternatives better suited to representation learning. The empirical gap between frozen and unfrozen CrossBERT at large scales (frozen outperforming unfrozen BERT) also raises the question of whether heavy downstream contrastive adaptation is still necessary at all.
