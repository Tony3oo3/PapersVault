## Problem

Deep learning on real-world data faces a dual challenge of long-tailed class imbalance and label noise, where both rare tail samples and corrupted noisy samples produce similarly high loss values (scalar ambiguity), making them indistinguishable to standard optimizers. Existing meta-learning solutions (e.g., MW-Net, FMW-Net) resolve this via black-box neural re-weighters guided by clean validation sets, but such sets are unavailable in practice, creating a circular dependency that limits real-world applicability.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR, 224×224 for iNaturalist/Clothing1M) and outputs class labels via a standard classifier head. Internally, it intercepts per-class gradient vectors from the last residual block and classifier layer (or classifier-only for memory-constrained large-scale tasks like iNaturalist 2018), computing them via hook-based mechanisms with only ~4.8% time overhead. No external validation set is required at any point during training.

## Method

ProMeCD replaces the black-box neural meta-learner with a transparent proportional-integral (PI) controller that operates on a novel 'cognitive entropy' metric. Per-class gradient directions are modeled via von Mises-Fisher distributions, yielding a concentration parameter κ (directional consistency) and a magnitude m (signal strength); cognitive entropy combines the differential entropy of the vMF (directional uncertainty) with −log m (magnitude uncertainty) into a single scalar. The PI controller is decoupled: the integral loop accumulates magnitude deficits for classes with high directional consistency (κ > τ_κ), boosting under-represented tail classes, while the proportional loop immediately penalizes classes whose consistency collapses below the dynamic threshold, suppressing noisy samples. This self-referential feedback loop—driven entirely by internal gradient statistics—eliminates the need for clean validation sets, and the paper proves monotonic convergence and formal prevention of the minority initial drop under the condition that the effective gradient ratio remains below 1.

## Results

On CIFAR-100-LT (IF=100, ResNet-32), ProMeCD achieves 53.12% top-1 accuracy, outperforming the strongest static baseline PaCo (52.00%) and the meta-learner FMW-Net (42.32%) by +1.12% and +10.80% respectively. On the noisy-label benchmark CIFAR-10-N (40% flip noise), ProMeCD reaches 91.12%, surpassing FMW-Net (68.80%) by over 22 points. On the unified long-tailed noisy-label setting (CIFAR-10, IF=10, NR=40%), ProMeCD attains 90.45%, exceeding the specialized joint solver D-SINK (89.00%) by +1.45%. On iNaturalist 2018, ProMeCD achieves 72.94%, marginally below PaCo (73.20%) but well above LDAM-DRW (68.00%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProMeCD outperforms all static re-balancing methods (LDAM-DRW, BBN, LA, PaCo) on CIFAR-LT, with the largest margin over PaCo on CIFAR-100-LT IF=100 (+1.12%) and CIFAR-10-LT IF=10 (+3.49% over LPL). Against black-box meta-learners, ProMeCD achieves a decisive advantage: on CIFAR-100-LT IF=100 it beats FMW-Net by +10.80% (53.12% vs 42.32%), and even under the same 100-epoch schedule it leads by +5.2%. On noisy-label benchmarks the gap widens dramatically—under 40% flip noise on CIFAR-10, ProMeCD (91.12%) exceeds FMW-Net (68.80%) by over 22 points, and on mini WebVision it surpasses both MW-Net (71.64%) and FMW-Net (72.44%). In the unified LTNLL setting, ProMeCD consistently beats the specialized joint solver D-SINK across all IF/NR configurations (e.g., +1.45% on CIFAR-10 IF=10 NR=40%, +2.46% on CIFAR-100 IF=100 NR=60%). The sole exception is iNaturalist 2018, where PaCo (73.20%) marginally edges out ProMeCD (72.94%), though ProMeCD still leads all other methods by +4.94%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant 'black-box' meta-learning paradigm (MW-Net, FMW-Net) that trains a neural network to predict sample weights via bi-level optimization on a clean validation set. Rather than extending those methods, ProMeCD repurposes a classical proportional-integral controller from control theory as the meta-decision mechanism, guided by a novel 'cognitive entropy' metric derived from von Mises-Fisher gradient statistics. It also unifies long-tailed and noisy label learning in a single self-referential loop, whereas prior joint solvers (TABASCO, RCAL, D-SINK) rely on multi-stage heuristics or task-specific pipelines. The control-theoretic optimization literature (PID-Optimizer, PID-GAN) is acknowledged but distinguished: ProMeCD operates at the per-class gradient-dynamics level rather than at the parameter or global learning-rate level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The elimination of the clean-validation-set dependency addresses a fundamental practical bottleneck in meta-learning-based re-weighting, making the approach deployable in settings where no pristine holdout exists. The theoretical guarantees (monotonic convergence for tail classes, formal MID prevention) and the 4.8% overhead (vs. 27.5% for FMW-Net) make this a practically attractive alternative for large-scale training. Open questions include whether the vMF assumption on gradient directions holds under extreme architectural diversity or very high-dimensional feature spaces, whether the approach extends naturally to non-classification tasks (detection, segmentation), and how the fixed PI gains transfer to fundamentally different data regimes (e.g., open-set or few-shot settings).
