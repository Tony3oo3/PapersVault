## Problem

Predicting protein properties like binding affinity and thermostability from sparse experimental data remains challenging, particularly when methods must be data-efficient, provide calibrated uncertainty estimates, and support multi-task learning across multiple landscapes. Existing approaches either rely on heavy foundation models (ESM-2, ProteinMPNN) with millions of parameters or lack principled uncertainty quantification, and no simple sequence-based method has matched their performance across diverse evaluation regimes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261854/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes fixed-length one-hot encoded protein sequences (L positions × 20 amino acids) and, for the structure-conditioned CLOCK variant, 128-dimensional positional structure embeddings from Chroma. It produces a full Gaussian predictive distribution (mean and variance) for the scalar property at any query sequence, with the variance reflecting calibrated epistemic uncertainty that shrinks near training data and grows in extrapolatory regimes. Sequences must be of fixed length or alignable to a common reference length.

## Method

The LOCK kernel combines a multiplicative (non-linear) kernel and a linear kernel, both constructed from BLOSUM-derived correlation matrices raised to learnable exponents, plus an additional linear term: k = σ₁²·k_nl·k_lin + σ₂²·k̃_lin. The key theoretical insight is that BLOSUM matrices are infinitely divisible, so Hadamard powers preserve positive semi-definiteness, enabling gradient-based learning of exponents that adapt biophysical similarity to each landscape. The product structure k_nl·k_lin encodes local linearity—equivalent to a GP prior on position-dependent linear coefficients—so predictions are nuanced near training data but smoothly revert to a robust linear extrapolator far away. For multi-task learning, CLOCK maps foundation-model structure embeddings to position-specific correlation matrices via a ~49k-parameter linear map, creating zero-shot structure-conditioned kernels that are trained by maximizing the concentrated GP marginal likelihood across multiple landscapes simultaneously.

## Results

On 21 protein property landscapes, LOCK-GP achieves Spearman R of 0.655 and MAE of 0.496 with only 48 training points (cross-validation), and Spearman R of 0.867 / MAE of 0.210 with 1536 points, outperforming all 8 baselines in most regimes. In the extrapolation regime with 512 training points, LOCK-GP achieves a mean MAE 22% lower than the next-best model (Tanimoto-GP: 0.439 vs. 0.574). Notably, LOCK-GP uses only 210 parameters (a BLOSUM50 correlation matrix) while Kermut-GP relies on ESM-2 (650M params) plus ProteinMPNN (1.7M params), and LOCK-GP is 2–140× faster at training and inference. In multi-task learning across 371 thermostability landscapes, the structure-conditioned CLOCK-GP achieves Spearman R of 0.755 at N=50 landscape-local points, versus 0.498 for sequence-only LOCK-GP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LOCK-GP outperforms Tanimoto-GP (Gessner et al., 2024) across nearly all regimes: at N=48 cross-validation, LOCK-GP achieves Spearman 0.655 vs. 0.520, and at N=512 extrapolation, MAE 0.439 vs. 0.574. Against Kermut-GP (Groth et al., 2024), the top ProteinGym supervised method, LOCK-GP wins on Spearman in 16/21 datasets at N=192 and achieves substantially lower MAE in extrapolation (0.439 vs. 0.670 at N=512), despite using 650M fewer parameters. The ESM-2-based baselines (MLP-ESM2-LastLayer and Ridge-ESM2) are outperformed by LOCK-GP in most settings; Ridge-ESM2 is particularly fragile in the unseen-mutations regime (Spearman 0.476 vs. LOCK-GP's 0.610), highlighting the vulnerability of high-dimensional embeddings out-of-distribution. SigGLM-OH (Ding et al., 2024), a structure-based generalized linear model, is consistently outperformed (Spearman 0.550 vs. 0.655 at N=48). In the multi-task setting, CLOCK-GP (structure-conditioned) decisively outperforms the Chroma-based CNN baseline: CLOCK-GP trained on 10 landscapes roughly matches CNN-EMB-OH trained on 20 landscapes, and CLOCK-GP+CNN-ZS achieves the best overall performance by combining the GP's function prior with neural zero-shot features.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of GP-based protein property prediction (Romero et al. 2013; Fox et al. 2003) by constructing kernels from BLOSUM substitution matrices rather than foundation model embeddings, and introduces the concept of 'local linearity' as a principled kernel property. It departs from the dominant paradigm of using ESM-2/ProteinMPNN embeddings (as in Kermut, Groth et al. 2024) by showing that the infinite divisibility of BLOSUM matrices enables learnable exponents that adapt biophysical similarity to each landscape. The CLOCK kernel provides a clean, zero-shot mechanism for incorporating structure-conditioned information from foundation models (Chroma, AlphaFold2) without the full training cost of supervised fine-tuning, positioning GPs as a competitive alternative to neural approaches in data-scarce protein design settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for protein designers working with limited experimental data: a 210-parameter BLOSUM50 kernel frequently matches or exceeds 650M-parameter foundation model pipelines, with superior uncertainty estimates (CRPS, NLL) that enable reliable model-based design via Thompson sampling. Key limitations include the restriction to fixed-length or alignable sequences, and the multi-task CLOCK kernel still requires ~10+ landscapes for strong performance. Open questions include whether CLOCK kernels can be pre-trained on one property (e.g., thermostability) and fine-tuned via low-rank updates to transfer to related properties (e.g., binding affinity), and whether the approach extends to variable-length sequences without alignment.
