## Problem

The literature on targeted instruction selection for LLMs is fragmented: prior methods vary widely in data representations, similarity metrics, selection algorithms, candidate pools, and budgets, and often omit zero-shot baselines or entangle the contributions of key components. As a result, it remains unclear which design choices actually drive performance, when, and why, leaving practitioners without actionable guidance for selecting instruction data for their target tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263415/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a large candidate pool of instruction-response pairs (e.g., ~198K examples from Tulu V2), a small query set drawn from the target task (8-81 samples depending on the benchmark), a base LLM (primarily Llama-2-7B), and a training budget B in {500, 1000, 2500, 5000, 10000}. It produces a selected subset S of exactly B examples, which is then used for supervised fine-tuning of the base model. All representations are reduced to feature vectors (hidden-state means, sentence-encoder embeddings, or projected gradient features), and a cosine similarity matrix between query and candidate representations is the sole interface between the representation and selection stages.

## Method

The paper introduces a disentangled framework that independently varies (i) the data representation-RDS+ (position-weighted hidden-state means), EMBED (GTR-T5 sentence encoder), or LESS (LoRA gradient features with random projection to 8192 dimensions)-and (ii) the selection algorithm-Greedy Round-Robin, Doubly Greedy, KNN-Uniform, KNN-KDE, or a newly proposed Unbalanced Optimal Transport (UOT) method that explicitly solves an entropy-regularized UOT problem to obtain a transport plan and selects top-B candidates by transported mass. Empirically, LESS is the only representation whose subset-query distance consistently predicts both query loss and downstream performance across tasks and models, while greedy round-robin selection performs best at low budgets and OT-based methods (UOT, KNN-KDE) pull ahead at larger budgets. Theoretically, the paper unifies all selection algorithms as approximate minimizers of a distributional distance Dist(S, Q), proves a generalization bound (Theorem 6.1) showing that minimizing the 1-Wasserstein distance W1(P_S, P_Q) directly tightens an upper bound on target-task loss, and establishes (Theorem 6.2) that the advantage of query-aware selection over random sampling decays as B^{-1/d}, explaining the observed diminishing returns at larger budgets.

## Results

Across five target tasks (BBH, Codex, GSM8K, TyDiQA, MMLU-Pro) and budgets B ∈ {500, 1000, 2500, 5000, 10000}, LESS (gradient-based) representations paired with greedy round-robin (RR) selection achieve the lowest query-set cross-entropy loss on all five tasks and the best downstream performance on three of five (BBH, TyDiQA, MMLU-Pro), while RDS+ (RR) is best on GSM8K. When the data representation is fixed to LESS, the proposed UOT selection algorithm achieves the lowest query loss on three of five datasets and, together with KNN-KDE, outperforms RR at larger budgets (≥5000), whereas RR dominates at small budgets (≤1000). The doubly greedy (DG) algorithm from Xia et al. (2024) consistently underperforms, yielding the highest loss on three of five datasets. Notably, uniformly random sampling is a strong baseline that often matches or exceeds targeted selection methods at larger budgets, particularly on TyDiQA and MMLU-Pro.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Xia et al. (2024) (LESS + Doubly Greedy): the paper adopts LESS as the strongest data representation but finds that the DG selection algorithm consistently underperforms all other algorithms, yielding the highest query loss on three of five datasets and the worst downstream performance on three of five, suggesting DG over-concentrates on a subset of queries. Against Ivison et al. (2025) (RDS+ + Round-Robin): RDS+ (RR) is competitive and wins on GSM8K across all budgets, but underperforms LESS (RR) on BBH, TyDiQA, and MMLU-Pro, and can fall below the Random baseline at larger budgets on some tasks; the paper attributes RDS+'s relative strength on GSM8K to a bias toward selecting longer sequences. Against Liu et al. (2024b) (KNN-Uniform, KNN-KDE): these optimal-transport-inspired methods perform comparably to the newly proposed UOT at larger budgets (≥5000) but are outperformed by greedy RR at small budgets; the paper confirms that using cosine distance (as in its unified framework) yields similar trends to the original L2-distance implementation. The proposed UOT selection algorithm matches or slightly exceeds KNN-KDE at high budgets while offering a more principled formulation via explicit unbalanced OT, and the paper's theoretical analysis (Theorem 6.2) explains why all distance-minimizing methods converge toward random sampling as budget grows.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on three concurrent lines of targeted instruction selection work—LESS with Doubly Greedy (Xia et al., 2024), RDS+/EMBED with Round-Robin (Ivison et al., 2025), and KNN-Uniform/KNN-KDE (Liu et al., 2024b)—but departs from them by introducing a disentangled framework that isolates data representation from selection algorithm, enabling controlled comparisons that prior work could not support. Theoretically, it draws on unbalanced optimal transport (Chizat et al., 2018) to propose a new UOT selection algorithm and on domain adaptation bounds via Wasserstein distance (Redko et al., 2017) to prove generalization guarantees that unify greedy, density-based, and transport-based selection as approximate distance minimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides practitioners with the first systematic, controlled comparison of instruction selection components, revealing that the field's apparent progress is partly an artifact of confounded experimental design. The finding that random sampling is a surprisingly strong baseline—especially at larger budgets—challenges the assumed value of sophisticated selection methods and motivates more principled approaches. Open questions include how to reduce the computational cost of gradient-based representations (LESS requires forward and backward passes over the entire pool), whether the candidate-pool dependence observed with Dolci Instruct generalizes, and how to design selection algorithms that exploit the distance-minimization view while avoiding the diminishing returns characterized by Theorem 6.2.
