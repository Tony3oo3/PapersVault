## Problem

Shampoo's Kronecker-factored preconditioner requires expensive eigendecompositions for inverse-root updates, so practitioners resort to stale (fixed-frequency) refreshes. However, staleness-induced operator errors simultaneously degrade convergence and threaten numerical stability, and the damping parameter that could counteract these errors has been treated as a fixed heuristic with no theoretical justification for its interaction with the refresh interval.

## Input / Output

At each step, FOAM consumes the current gradient matrix G_t (m×n), the EMA-updated Kronecker statistics L_t (m×m) and R_t (n×n), the stale eigendecompositions (Q_L, D_L) and (Q_R, D_R) from the last refresh, and the current damping factor ε. It outputs updated inverse-root preconditioner factors for the parameter update, an adaptively adjusted damping value, and a binary decision on whether to trigger a full EVD refresh. Crucially, the error proxy h_t is evaluated entirely within the stale eigenspaces (via whitened drift norms), avoiding any fresh eigendecomposition at non-refresh steps.

## Method

FOAM treats the damping factor ε and the EVD refresh frequency as coupled control variables governed by a three-phase feedback loop: (1) Sensing — compute a relative operator error proxy h_t = (α(ε)/p)·RC(ε) in the stale eigenspaces, where RC measures the whitened drift ‖(L̂_t)^{-1/2}(L_t − L̂_t)(L̂_t)^{-1/2}‖_F and α(ε) is a spectral-conditioning ratio; (2) Adapting — multiplicatively update ε ← max(ε_0, ε·h_t/τ), increasing damping when the estimated error exceeds threshold τ and relaxing it toward the baseline ε_0 otherwise; (3) Frequency control — if ε exceeds a cap ε_max, trigger a full EVD refresh and reset ε to ε_0. The theoretical analysis shows that ε controls the Lipschitz constant of the inverse-root map (scaling as 1/(p·ε^{(p+1)/p})), so increasing ε suppresses staleness-induced operator gaps, while the discounted regret bound reveals a countervailing cost: the leading term ε·D² penalizes excessive damping by flattening the preconditioner. This trade-off motivates the adaptive, bounded control rather than a fixed damping choice.

## Results

The paper's main results are presented as wall-clock time curves (Figure 1, Figure 5) rather than a single numerical table. On ViT-small (ImageNet-1K), FOAM achieves lower training loss and higher top-1 validation accuracy in substantially less wall-clock time than stale Shampoo; on Conformer (LibriSpeech), FOAM reaches a lower WER in less time. A key quantitative finding is the reduction in eigendecomposition (EVD) calls: on ViT, the number of EVDs for the L and R Kronecker factors drops to approximately 10% and 5% of the stale baseline, respectively (i.e., ~90% and ~95% reduction). The wall-clock advantage is robust across a sweep of (f, τ, ε_max, ε_0) hyperparameters, with the majority of tested configurations falling within the superior-performance region.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is Stale Shampoo, which performs a full eigendecomposition at a fixed frequency f with a constant damping ε_0. FOAM consistently outperforms Stale Shampoo on wall-clock time while matching or exceeding final performance (training loss, validation accuracy, WER) across both ViT and Conformer benchmarks. The ablation study (Figure 4) isolates the two core mechanisms: removing adaptive damping (increasing f without ε compensation) causes a significant training loss increase due to uncontrolled spectral drift, while removing selective EVD refreshes (relying solely on ε control) reduces loss but at the cost of higher wall-clock time. Only the full FOAM configuration simultaneously minimizes both loss and computational overhead. The paper also reports comparisons with AdamW and SOAP in Appendix J, though these are secondary to the main Stale Shampoo comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

FOAM builds directly on Shampoo's Kronecker-factored preconditioning framework (Gupta et al., 2018) and its practical stale-update variant, which refreshes expensive eigendecompositions at a fixed frequency. It departs from the broader Shampoo family (SOAP, CASPR, Muon, SPlus) by reframing staleness not merely as a convergence-speed issue but as a fundamental numerical-stability threat, and by introducing adaptive inverse-root damping as a theoretically grounded countermeasure. The error proxy h_t draws on matrix perturbation theory (Davis-Kahan, Weyl) to estimate relative operator gap without a fresh EVD, distinguishing it from prior refresh triggers based on diagonalization residuals. The closest prior work on damping stale quantities is Damaskinos et al. (2018), which damps stale gradients in asynchronous SGD via a scalar weight, whereas FOAM adaptively tunes the inverse-root damping ε within Shampoo's Kronecker factors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: reducing EVD calls by over 80% while matching or exceeding stale Shampoo's final performance directly lowers wall-clock training time for large-scale Shampoo-based training, with validated results across vision (ViT/ImageNet), speech (Conformer/LibriSpeech), and language (GPT-2/Wikitext-103) modalities. The theoretical contribution—formally linking staleness to operator-gap instability and characterizing the damping trade-off (stability vs. preconditioning quality)—provides the first principled justification for the previously heuristic choice of refresh frequency and damping level. Open questions include extending the regret analysis to non-convex settings, reducing the two new hyperparameters (ε_max, τ), and adapting the framework to SOAP-style eigenspace-reuse where eigengap-dependent sensitivity dominates the error.
