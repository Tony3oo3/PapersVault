## Problem

Multimodal LLMs achieve near-human performance on standard OCR benchmarks but suffer severe perceptual degradation when text is intentionally concealed through physical deformation, visual camouflage, or cognitive suggestion in real-world adversarial content. No dedicated benchmark or methodology existed to evaluate or address this 'Implicit Text Reasoning' task, leaving content moderation systems blind to high-risk hidden text that evades detection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261407/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB adversarial image as input and produces a joint output tuple (ŷ, T̂): a binary presence label indicating whether implicit text exists, and the recognized hidden text string. Evaluation uses a dual-metric protocol — F1 score for the detection classification and a Text Match Score (TMS) computed via Normalized Edit Distance with a relaxed threshold τ=0.5 to accommodate visual distortions inherent in implicit text. The benchmark comprises 1,630 image-text pairs spanning four concealment categories (benign, physical deformation, visual camouflage, cognitive suggestion) with a temporal train/test split to simulate evolving adversarial techniques.

## Method

ImpText-Reader decomposes the task into a Visual Enhancer (selecting from 12 OpenCV-based image processing tools such as adaptive thresholding, anisotropic stretch, and JPEG purify) and a Semantic Reasoner (performing existence classification and text recognition), trained via a three-stage progressive paradigm. Phase I performs SFT warm-up on raw data to establish baseline recognition. Phase II uses oracle-guided co-training: all tools are exhaustively applied to each image, the warmed-up Reasoner scores candidates, and the best tool is used to fine-tune the Enhancer; simultaneously, the Reasoner is trained only on boundary samples where the pass rate P satisfies 0 < P < 1, filtering out trivially easy and impossible cases. Phase III applies GRPO reinforcement learning with a composite reward (format + classification + 1−NED) on edge cases mined from the Enhancer's actual policy, robustifying the Reasoner against inference-time tool deviations. A key design choice is decoupled optimization — training the Enhancer and Reasoner separately rather than jointly — which prevents task interference and training collapse.

## Results

On ImpText-Bench (1,630 samples), ImpText-Reader achieves an overall F1 Score of 87.97% and Text Match Score (TMS) of 48.26%, substantially outperforming the strongest proprietary baseline GPT-5 (F1 64.68%, TMS 35.79%) by over 12 percentage points in TMS. The best fine-tuned control (Qwen3-VL 8B + SFT) reaches only 37.63% TMS, while specialized OCR systems (PaddleOCR-VL, DeepSeek-OCR) collapse to 7.57% and 1.02% TMS respectively. ImpText-Reader maintains a Benign Accuracy of 96.76%, confirming it does not over-trigger on non-adversarial images.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ImpText-Reader (48.26% TMS, 87.97% F1) outperforms every baseline across all three adversarial categories. Against the strongest proprietary model GPT-5 (35.79% TMS, 64.68% F1), it gains +12.47 pp in TMS and +23.29 pp in F1; notably GPT-5.2 achieves a higher Cognitive Suggestion TMS (49.51%) but collapses to 0% on Physical Deformation, whereas ImpText-Reader maintains 25.30% there. The best fine-tuned control (Qwen3-VL 8B + SFT, 37.63% TMS) is surpassed by +10.63 pp, confirming that the three-stage training paradigm and boundary-aware data filtering contribute beyond simple SFT. Specialized OCR systems (PaddleOCR-VL 7.57%, DeepSeek-OCR 1.02%) and all open-source MLLMs (best: InternVL-3.5 at 26.79% TMS) are far behind, with most scoring below 20% TMS. In the supplementary tool-use comparison (Table 8), generic agentic baselines (ReAct+Tool at 33.33% TMS, Best-of-tool at 33.74%) also fall well short, and simply appending tools to GPT-5 or Gemini actually degrades their TMS, underscoring that task-specific tool selection and boundary-aware reasoner optimization—not raw tool access or larger inference budgets—drive the gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of visual text understanding, adversarial robustness, and content safety, but does not directly build on a single prior method. It departs from OCR robustness work (which assumes visible characters under noise) and from MLLM safety alignment (which targets output compliance rather than perceptual failure) by defining Implicit Text Reasoning as a distinct detect-then-recognize task under adversarial concealment. The ImpText-Reader framework repurposes the tool-augmented agentic paradigm and GRPO-based RL from general reasoning literature, but applies them in a novel three-stage decoupled pipeline with boundary-aware data filtering specifically calibrated to the capability frontier of the recognizer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a critical blind spot: models scoring >90% on standard OCR drop to <36% TMS on implicitly concealed text, revealing that current content moderation pipelines are fundamentally vulnerable to real-world adversarial obfuscation. The 1,630-sample dataset is Chinese-only and the regeneration pipeline, while privacy-preserving, may not fully capture the full diversity of real-world concealment strategies, limiting generalizability claims. Open questions include whether the tool-augmented + boundary-aware paradigm transfers to multi-lingual settings, whether the 12-tool OpenCV library is sufficient for novel obfuscation types, and whether the approach scales to video or multi-page documents where implicit text may be distributed across frames.
