## Problem

Sampling from high-dimensional non-log-concave distributions with only zeroth-order (function-value) access to the potential remains largely unexplored: standard ZO gradient estimators require batch sizes scaling as O(d), and no non-asymptotic convergence guarantees existed for non-log-concave targets. In the inverse-problems setting, black-box posterior sampling with score-based generative priors—where the forward model admits only input-output queries—lacked any rigorous convergence guarantees under standard probabilistic discrepancy measures such as Fisher information or total variation distance.

## Input / Output

The method consumes noisy measurements y ∈ R^m, a black-box forward operator A: R^d → R^m accessible only through function evaluations (no gradients, pseudo-inverses, or internal parametrization), and a pre-trained SGM score network S_θ(x, σ) that approximates the perturbed prior score at multiple noise scales. It produces posterior samples from π(x|y) ∝ ℓ(y|x)p(x) using only O(1) forward-model function evaluations per iteration, which are then averaged to yield reconstructions at various resolutions (256×256 MRI, 64×64 black-hole images, Navier-Stokes velocity fields). The SGM prior is frozen and queried at annealed noise levels σ_k, so no retraining or fine-tuning is required per inverse problem.

## Method

The core contribution is a variance-reduced ZO gradient estimator inspired by the PAGE algorithm: with probability p it computes a fresh large-batch ZO estimate, and with probability 1−p it updates the previous estimate using a small batch of fresh evaluations plus the difference between consecutive iterates, exploiting the strong correlation between x_{kγ} and x_{(k−1)γ}. This reduces the per-iteration cost from O(d) to O(1) function evaluations while preserving convergence. For posterior sampling, the estimator is embedded in an annealed Langevin Monte Carlo framework (ZO-APMC) that combines the ZO likelihood gradient with the SGM prior, using decaying noise schedules σ_k and annealing weights α_k to progressively shift from prior-dominated to likelihood-dominated sampling. The paper establishes the first non-asymptotic convergence guarantees for non-log-concave ZO sampling in relative Fisher information (O(ε^{-4}) iterations), squared total variation distance under a Poincaré inequality, and weak convergence with decaying parameters, extending all results to the posterior sampling setting while accounting for both annealing bias and SGM estimation error.

## Results

On MRI reconstruction (256×256, 4× acceleration), ZO-APMC achieves 35.29 dB PSNR and 0.966 SSIM using only O(1) function evaluations per iteration, closely matching the gradient-based APMC (36.55 dB PSNR) and outperforming all black-box baselines. On black-hole interferometric imaging (64×64), ZO-APMC achieves 26.71 dB PSNR and 5.42 χ²_cph, surpassing even the gradient-based APMC (26.23 dB PSNR, 11.78 χ²_cph) and all derivative-free competitors. The theoretical guarantee requires O(d⁷L_m⁴/ε⁴) iterations to reach ε-FI accuracy, though experiments show convergence in far fewer iterations in practice.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ZO-APMC outperforms all black-box baselines on both MRI and black-hole imaging. On MRI, it leads DPG (32.17 dB) by 3.1 dB PSNR, EnKG (31.32 dB) by 4.0 dB, and SCG (7.1 dB) by a wide margin; on black-hole imaging, it surpasses DPG (12.33 dB) by 14.4 dB PSNR and EnKG (22.86 dB) by 3.9 dB. Against the gradient-based APMC, ZO-APMC is slightly behind on MRI (35.29 vs 36.55 dB PSNR) but actually exceeds it on black-hole imaging (26.71 vs 26.23 dB PSNR, 5.42 vs 11.78 χ²_cph). Compared to DPS, ZO-APMC wins on both tasks (35.29 vs 34.38 on MRI; 26.71 vs 25.61 on black-hole), which the authors attribute to DPS's uncontrolled Jensen-gap approximation. PnPDM, while principled, is limited to the EDM framework and lags ZO-APMC on both benchmarks (30.81 vs 35.29 MRI PSNR; 26.48 vs 26.71 black-hole PSNR). The Forward-GSG and Central-GSG variants of EnKG underperform substantially (27.8 and 27.78 dB on MRI), confirming that heuristic ZO guidance without variance reduction degrades reconstruction quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the non-log-concave LMC theory of Balasubramanian et al. (2022) from the gradient-access setting to the zeroth-order (derivative-free) regime, and adapts the PAGE variance-reduction estimator from non-convex optimization (Li et al., 2021) to the sampling context. It departs from Roy et al. (2022), which provided ZO-LMC guarantees only for strongly log-concave targets with O(d) batch sizes, by handling non-log-concave distributions with O(1) per-iteration cost. For the inverse-problems component, the paper builds on the annealed PMC framework of Sun et al. (2024) (APMC) but replaces exact gradient access with variance-reduced ZO estimates, positioning itself as the first black-box posterior sampler with SGM priors to carry non-asymptotic convergence guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The first non-asymptotic convergence guarantees for ZO non-log-concave sampling and for black-box posterior sampling with SGM priors fill a significant theoretical gap, giving practitioners a principled foundation for derivative-free probabilistic inference in settings where gradients are inaccessible (PDE simulators, proprietary scanners, discontinuous physics). The O(1) function-evaluation cost per iteration is a substantial practical improvement over the O(d) scaling of standard ZO estimators, enabling scalable sampling in high-dimensional imaging problems. Key limitations include the restrictive Lipschitz-continuity assumption on the potential (Assumption 1), the high d^7 polynomial iteration complexity, and the need for a Poincaré inequality for TV-distance guarantees. Open directions noted by the authors include extending to underdamped Langevin dynamics and latent diffusion models for improved sampling efficiency.
