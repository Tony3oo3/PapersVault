## Problem

Existing ML+MIP approaches typically require complex modifications to the solver's internal branch-and-bound process, making them incompatible with commercial black-box solvers and difficult to tune due to low interpretability. This paper asks whether a simple, model-agnostic, and theoretically grounded mechanism can incorporate offline ML predictions into MIP solving without altering the solver's core architecture.

## Input / Output

The method takes a probabilistic prediction vector (one value in [0,1] per binary variable, from any supervised model such as logistic regression or a GNN) and a threshold τ to select high-confidence variables, then produces two linear branching constraints of the form Σ_{i∈U} y_i ≥ z₁ and Σ_{i∈L} y_i ≤ z₂ that partition the feasible region into four subproblems. In the data-free variant, the LP relaxation (or interior-point) solution replaces the ML prediction entirely. The output is either a single near-optimal solution (primal heuristic mode) or a certified optimal solution after solving all four subproblems with objective-cut pruning (exact branching mode).

## Method

PMVB's core insight is risk pooling: rather than fixing individual binary variables (where misclassification risk compounds exponentially), it aggregates predicted-1 and predicted-0 variables into two sum constraints whose validity is guaranteed by concentration inequalities (Hoeffding for the independent case, Chebyshev for the general case). A threshold τ ∈ (1/2, 1] filters out low-confidence predictions, and the hyperplane intercepts are set via a variance-based bound σ/√δ, yielding an interpretable trade-off between the number of variables included and the confidence level. The entire procedure reduces to adding two linear constraints to the MIP model (two lines of code), making it solver-agnostic and compatible with Gurobi, COPT, or any other MIP solver. The paper further provides a data-free variant that uses the LP relaxation solution as a surrogate prediction, with a theoretical guarantee for the knapsack problem showing the branching hyperplanes hold with high probability under uniform data assumptions.

## Results

As a primal heuristic, PMVB achieves up to 5× speedup on multi-knapsack (MKP) instances, up to 10× on set covering (SCP) instances, and over 50% average speedup on security-constrained unit commitment (SCUC) using logistic regression predictions. As an exact branching rule integrated with Gurobi, PMVB yields 10–31% SGM time reductions (e.g., MKP (30,500): 1283s → 979s, Σ=1.31). In the data-free setting (LP relaxation as prediction), PMVB achieves up to 19% SGM improvement on MIPLIB instances (δ=10⁻⁸, τ=0.9: Σ=1.19), though very conservative δ values are required to avoid infeasibility.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary external baseline is Predict-and-Search (P&S) from Han et al. (2023), a trust-region primal heuristic that also uses probabilistic binary predictions. On the ML4CO Load Balancing problem, PMVB consistently outperforms P&S across all four prediction models (e.g., 706.5 vs. 750.3 with Binary GCN; 706.1 vs. 749.6 with GCN), a gap of roughly 43–44 objective units. On the ML4CO Item Placement problem, the two methods are comparable, with P&S achieving marginally better values (15.32–15.38) than PMVB (15.36–15.44), suggesting PMVB's separate aggregation of predicted zeros and ones into two hyperplanes is less advantageous when the problem has a single partitioning constraint. Internally, PMVB is compared against the unmodified solver (Gurobi/COPT) as the baseline in all experiments; the speedup is measured as the ratio of time for the original solver to reach the same objective value that PMVB achieves in a shorter window. The paper also ablates against solver warm-start (Appendix E.4), confirming that PMVB's acceleration persists regardless of whether the rounded prediction is used as a MIP start.

## Relation to Prior Work

PMVB extends the classical multi-variable branching (MVB) / local branching framework of Fischetti & Lodi (2003) and Karamanov & Cornuejols (2011) by deriving the branching hyperplane coefficients from probabilistic ML predictions rather than hand-crafted or learned policies. The key conceptual departure is the 'risk pooling' argument: instead of fixing individual binary variables (where error compounds exponentially), PMVB aggregates predictions into sum-constraints whose validity is guaranteed by concentration inequalities (Hoeffding, Chebyshev), yielding interpretable hyperparameter choices. Within the broader ML+MIP landscape, PMVB deliberately avoids the complex solver modifications typical of prior work (neural diving, learned branching policies, trust-region heuristics like Predict-and-Search) in favor of a model-agnostic, two-line constraint addition that works as a black-box add-on to commercial solvers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The two-line, model-agnostic implementation makes PMVB one of the most accessible ML+MIP techniques to deploy in practice, and the statistical-learning-theory grounding provides a principled (if conservative) recipe for setting the branching threshold τ and confidence parameter δ. However, the main theoretical guarantee still relies on an independence assumption that rarely holds in practice, and the data-free LP-relaxation variant requires extremely conservative δ (10⁻⁸) to avoid infeasibility, limiting its practical utility. Open questions include whether the four-subproblem exact mode consistently outperforms the original solve at scale, how PMVB behaves on problems with strong variable correlations, and whether the approach extends naturally to general integer (non-binary) variables beyond binary encoding.
