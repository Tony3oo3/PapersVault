## Problem

In large-scale outdoor scenes, LiDAR geometric priors are spatially incomplete and uneven, yet existing 3DGS methods either use them only for initialization or apply them as uniform constraints that suppress valid camera-visible geometry in LiDAR-blind regions while failing to exclude floaters in verified free space. This paper addresses the need for a principled, spatially adaptive treatment of partial geometric observability that is rigid where sensor data is definitive and permissive where it is absent.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images, a LiDAR point cloud, and camera poses. The LiDAR is preprocessed into a discretized voxel grid via Euclidean Distance Transform, partitioning 3D space into occupied, free, and unknown regions with precomputed gradient fields. The output is a set of anisotropic 3D Gaussians (positions, covariances, opacities, spherical-harmonic colors) that support real-time novel-view rendering via differentiable rasterization.

## Method

EnerGS constructs a continuous geometric energy field with three spatially distinct regimes: a Welsch M-estimator attraction in occupied space (bounded-influence pull toward LiDAR surfaces), a Softplus/Boltzmann barrier in certified free space (monotonic repulsion pushing primitives out), and a high-variance weak prior in unobserved regions (near-zero force allowing photometric densification to proceed). The key architectural choice is a decoupled update rule: Gaussian positions are updated exclusively by the negative geometric gradient (guaranteeing monotonic energy descent and eliminating stable floaters in free space), while covariance, opacity, and color are updated by the photometric gradient with the position gradient explicitly blocked. A discrete pruning step every T iterations removes any primitives still trapped in free space as a secondary hard constraint. Theoretical analysis proves that degenerate solutions cannot form stable equilibria in free space, the update field has a finite Lipschitz constant, and the geometric constraint vanishes asymptotically in unknown regions as the prior variance grows.

## Results

On KITTI, EnerGS achieves the best PSNR (16.80 dB), highest occupied-space coverage (OccCov 25.8%), and lowest free-space leakage (Leak 9.2) among all compared methods, while using 1.20M Gaussians. On Waymo, it attains the best PSNR (24.81 dB), lowest Leak (0.2), and highest Margin (3.33), with 1.57M Gaussians. The method trades a modest SSIM reduction (0.841 on KITTI vs. 0.950 for Mip-Splatting) for substantially improved geometric consistency and fewer artifacts in LiDAR-unobserved regions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EnerGS outperforms vanilla 3DGS by +1.79 dB PSNR and reduces Leak from 12.5 to 9.2 on KITTI, and by +0.69 dB PSNR with Leak reduced from 0.3 to 0.2 on Waymo, while using fewer Gaussians (1.20M vs. 1.51M on KITTI). Against the strongest photometric baselines (Mip-Splatting, Taming-3DGS), EnerGS gains 2.89 dB PSNR on KITTI and 0.63 dB on Waymo, at the cost of lower SSIM (0.841 vs. 0.950 on KITTI), reflecting a deliberate trade-off favoring geometric validity over local structural similarity. Compared to geometry-aware methods (2DGS, GeoGaussian, Scaffold-GS), EnerGS achieves the best OccCov (25.8% vs. 19.3–19.8% on KITTI) and lowest Leak, confirming that its energy-field formulation more effectively concentrates primitives on true surfaces. Against the LiDAR-guided SplatAD pipeline, EnerGS reduces KITTI Leak from 22.1 to 7.0 and improves PSNR from 17.14 to 18.32, demonstrating particular benefit under sparse LiDAR. When integrated with the dynamic StreetGS framework, EnerGS improves full-scene PSNR from 25.58 to 25.94 and static-region PSNR from 25.86 to 26.22 while maintaining comparable dynamic-region quality, confirming plug-and-play compatibility.

## Relation to Prior Work

EnerGS builds directly on vanilla 3DGS as its scene representation but departs fundamentally from prior LiDAR-guided 3DGS methods (GeoGaussian, Taming-3DGS, LiGSM, LI-GS) that treat geometric supervision as uniform L2 attraction targets or initialization-only constraints. The key conceptual shift is modeling partially observed LiDAR as a spatially partitioned continuous energy field—Welsch M-estimator attraction in occupied space, Boltzmann barrier repulsion in certified free space, and a high-variance weak prior in unobserved regions—combined with a decoupled update rule that blocks photometric gradients from flowing to Gaussian positions. This positions the work at the intersection of robust statistics (M-estimators), statistical physics (Boltzmann distributions), and differentiable rendering, rather than as another depth-regularization variant.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical gap: LiDAR in autonomous driving is inherently sparse and spatially incomplete, yet most 3DGS methods either ignore free-space evidence or apply uniform geometric constraints that suppress valid camera-visible geometry in sensor blind spots. The decoupled optimization with theoretical guarantees (monotonic energy descent, exclusion of stable floaters in free space) provides a principled alternative to ad-hoc regularization, and the plug-and-play compatibility demonstrated with SplatAD and StreetGS suggests broad applicability. Key limitations include the static-scene assumption (dynamic objects violate the free-space/occupancy partition) and reliance on accurate LiDAR-to-camera calibration; extending the energy formulation to handle moving agents or other sensor modalities (e.g., radar, depth cameras) remains open.
