## Problem

Existing anchor-based incomplete multi-view clustering methods rely on shallow bipartite graphs that model only pairwise sample-anchor relations, making them structurally fragile when samples are missing in specific views. Additionally, these methods treat observed and missing instances indiscriminately, ignoring distributional shifts from non-random missingness that introduce systematic bias into consensus anchor learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261652/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes v heterogeneous views {X_p ∈ R^{d_p×n}} with per-view binary masks S_p indicating which samples are observed, and produces cluster assignments via K-Means on the learned m×n anchor graph Z. All views are projected into a shared l-dimensional consensus subspace (l, m ≪ n), and the hypergraph structure is dynamically rebuilt each iteration using T-nearest anchors per sample, keeping memory at O(n(m+T)).

## Method

HA-IMVC replaces the conventional bipartite anchor graph with a consensus anchor-guided hypergraph where each anchor induces a hyperedge over its T-nearest samples, explicitly modeling group-wise high-order interactions and preserving structural redundancy under missingness. A dual-adaptive reweighting mechanism calibrates importance at two levels: view-level weights α_p derived from completeness ratios, and sample-level weights via an exponential penalty M = Diag(e^{−γq_i}) on missingness scores q_i, plus a missing-rate regularizer Q that suppresses anchor assignments to unreliable samples. The unified objective is optimized by alternating closed-form SVD (Orthogonal Procrustes) updates for projections W_p and anchors A, a FISTA-based quadratic program with column-wise simplex projection for the anchor graph Z, a closed-form update for view weights β, and dynamic hypergraph Laplacian reconstruction. The per-iteration complexity is O(n(m log m + ml)), scaling linearly in n, which enables reliable operation on 60,000-sample datasets where non-anchor baselines fail.

## Results

On NGs, HA-IMVC achieves 94.20% ACC / 83.53% NMI at 30% missing rate, outperforming the next-best HCP-IMSC (93.40% ACC) and maintaining 87.42% ACC even at 70% missing. On the large-scale MNIST dataset (60,000 samples), HA-IMVC reaches 98.70% ACC at 30% missing, surpassing FDAGF (98.64%) and FastMICE (97.45%), while several non-anchor baselines (HCP-IMSC, SCSL, UOMVSC) fail entirely due to memory constraints. On Caltech101-20, HA-IMVC leads with 55.23% ACC at 30% missing, ahead of MVSC-HFD (51.09%) and SCSL (43.84%). The method consistently ranks first or second across all six datasets and three missing rates (30%, 50%, 70%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HA-IMVC outperforms all eight baselines on NGs at every missing rate, with a 2.2-point ACC margin over HCP-IMSC (91.20 vs. 89.00 at 50%) and a 38.3-point margin over the next-best anchor method FDAGF (91.20 vs. 52.93). On Caltech101-20, HA-IMVC leads by 7.3 ACC points over MVSC-HFD (55.34 vs. 48.01 at 50%) and 12.0 points over SCSL (55.34 vs. 43.39). On BDGP, the advantage is 2.1 ACC points over FDAGF (48.52 vs. 46.38 at 50%). On MNIST, HA-IMVC edges out FDAGF by 0.36 ACC points (98.70 vs. 98.64 at 30%) and FastMICE by 1.25 points (98.70 vs. 97.45). On Animal, HA-IMVC is competitive with MVSC-HFD (17.30 vs. 17.60 ACC at 30%), a near-tie where MVSC-HFD holds a slight 0.3-point edge. Notably, HCP-IMSC, SCSL, and UOMVSC all fail (N/A) on CCV, Animal, and MNIST due to out-of-memory errors, while HA-IMVC completes all six datasets efficiently.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the consensus anchor graph paradigm for incomplete multi-view clustering (Wang et al., CVPR 2022) by replacing the standard bipartite anchor-sample graph with a hypergraph structure that models group-wise interactions, and by introducing a dual-adaptive reweighting mechanism at both view and sample levels. It departs from completion-based approaches (diffusion, contrastive imputation) that are computationally prohibitive at scale, and from prior anchor-based methods that rely on fragile pairwise connections. The hypergraph Laplacian regularization and FISTA-based QP solver for the anchor graph update are the main technical novelties layered onto the existing anchor learning framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical path to scalable incomplete multi-view clustering on large datasets (up to 60K samples) where non-anchor methods simply fail due to memory constraints, and the reweighting mechanism addresses a realistic concern—non-random missingness causing systematic bias in anchor learning. However, the final clustering step still relies on K-Means on the anchor graph, the hypergraph construction is heuristic (T-nearest anchors), and the theoretical error bound in the appendix is somewhat informal. Open questions include whether the hypergraph structure can be learned end-to-end, how the method behaves under adversarial or structured missingness patterns, and whether the reweighting strategy generalizes to settings with view-level distribution shifts beyond simple completeness ratios.
