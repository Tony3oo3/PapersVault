## Problem

At 2-bit precision, scalar-quantization-based QAT suffers severe information loss, producing weak initializations that demand massive training data for recovery. Vector quantization offers substantially higher representational capacity, but its discrete codebook lookup is non-differentiable, preventing standard end-to-end backpropagation. Existing VQ-QAT workarounds (e.g., PV-Tuning's coordinate descent) introduce expensive, asynchronous parameter updates that limit scaling.

## Input / Output

The method takes pre-trained LLM weights (Qwen-3-1.7B/8B, LLaMA-3-3B/8B) and a relatively small training corpus (~4B tokens from FineWeb for foundation models, ~2B tokens from AM-Qwen3-Distilled for instruction tuning), plus 1,024 RedPajama calibration samples for PTQ initialization. It produces 2-bit weight-only quantized models where each weight matrix carries only 20 extra parameters (a 4×4 matrix A and a 4-dim vector B per layer). The forward pass is reformulated to reuse optimized Int2-FP16 MatMul kernels, yielding 1.68× throughput over FP16 on a single A100.

## Method

LC-QAT replaces the unconstrained VQ codebook with a linear-constrained parameterization c = Az + B, where z ∈ {0,1,2,3}^d is a discrete quaternary vector and A, B are shared floating-point parameters per weight matrix. This transforms the non-differentiable nearest-neighbor codebook lookup into simple round-and-clip discretization followed by an affine projection, so gradients flow through the linear mapping under standard backpropagation without any index search. The model is initialized via LDLQ-based PTQ with Hadamard incoherence processing, and the resulting integer weights are reverse-mapped to continuous proxy weights through an affine transformation aligned with Xavier statistics. During training, a Differentiable Gradient Estimator (DGE) replaces the Straight-Through Estimator to avoid the loss spikes that destabilize optimization near the favorable local minimum inherited from the VQ initialization. The forward pass is algebraically restructured as Y = Σ(X_i A)W_{z,i}^T + Σ(X_i B)1^T, allowing the integer matmul portion to leverage existing optimized kernels while the small d×d projection is absorbed into the activation side.

## Results

On Qwen-3-8B, LC-QAT achieves 72.18% average zero-shot QA accuracy (PER 0.97 vs. FP16), outperforming the VQ-QAT baseline PV-Tuning (70.99%, PER 0.96) and all PTQ methods. On the more challenging Qwen-3-1.7B, LC-QAT reaches 61.43% average (PER 0.96) versus PV-Tuning's 58.05% (PER 0.91). In the data-efficiency setting on LLaMA-3-8B, LC-QAT achieves 73.42% average using only 4B tokens, surpassing ParetoQ (72.07%) which requires 30B tokens (~10× more data). Against BitNet 2B4T on instruction-tuned benchmarks, LC-QAT attains 50.35% average versus 48.07%, using only ~0.1% of the training tokens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the only prior end-to-end VQ-QAT method, PV-Tuning, LC-QAT wins on both model sizes: 72.18% vs. 70.99% average on Qwen-3-8B and 61.43% vs. 58.05% on Qwen-3-1.7B, with the gap widening on the smaller model where compression is harder. Against the state-of-the-art SQ-QAT method ParetoQ (30B tokens), LC-QAT achieves higher average accuracy on both LLaMA-3-8B (73.42% vs. 72.07%) and LLaMA-3-3B (68.33% vs. 66.82%) while using only ~10% of the training data (4B tokens). On instruction-tuned benchmarks, LC-QAT outperforms BitNet 2B4T in average score (50.35% vs. 48.07%) using roughly 0.1% of its 4T-token training budget, though BitNet retains an edge on MMLU (53.17 vs. 46.77) and MATH (43.40 vs. 39.60). All SQ PTQ baselines (GPTQ, QuIP, OSTQuant) suffer catastrophic degradation at 2-bit (PER 0.50–0.78), while VQ PTQ methods (QuIP#, QTIP, YAQA) reach PER 0.91–0.97 without any training, confirming that LC-QAT's gains come from the end-to-end optimization on top of a strong VQ initialization rather than initialization alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

LC-QAT extends the linear-constrained codebook parameterization from UniSVQ (Wang et al., 2026), a PTQ method, into a fully differentiable QAT framework by replacing discrete codebook lookup with round/clip discretization followed by an affine projection. It builds on QuIP's LDLQ algorithm for initialization, QuIP#'s Hadamard transform for outlier mitigation, and the Differentiable Gradient Estimator from Wang et al. (2025) for stable gradient flow. The key departure is from both SQ-based QAT (ParetoQ, LLM-QAT), which suffers severe information loss at 2-bit, and from PV-Tuning, the only prior end-to-end VQ-QAT method, which relies on expensive asynchronous coordinate descent for codebook index updates. By making the forward pass lookup-free, LC-QAT enables synchronized, standard backpropagation over all VQ parameters simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: LC-QAT matches or surpasses ParetoQ (30B tokens) with ~4B tokens and approaches BitNet 2B4T (4T tokens) with ~0.1% of that budget, making 2-bit QAT feasible under realistic data constraints. The loss-landscape analysis provides a mechanistic explanation for the data efficiency—VQ initialization places the model in a low-loss basin with saddle-point structure similar to FP16, whereas SQ initialization lands in a high-loss region lacking nearby minima. Open questions include scalability beyond the 14B models tested, the interaction of the affine dequantization with highly optimized GEMM kernels at larger batch sizes, and whether the approach generalizes to sub-2-bit or mixed-precision settings.
