## Problem

Extending distributional reinforcement learning to multivariate return distributions is challenging because common metrics (categorical grids, quantile parameterizations, Wasserstein) either grow combinatorially or become computationally intractable in higher dimensions. Moreover, the multivariate setting introduces general matrix discounting for which no Bellman contraction results existed in the distributional RL literature, and existing approaches that do offer contraction (e.g., MMD with restricted kernels) fail to extend to anisotropic discount structures.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a transition tuple (s, a, r, s′) where r ∈ ℝ^d is a d-dimensional reward vector, a policy π, and a state-action-dependent discount-mixing matrix Γ(s,a) ∈ ℝ^{d×d} (which may be dense and anisotropic). It produces N particles {z_i} approximating the full multivariate return distribution Z^π(s,a) at each state-action pair, rather than a single scalar Q-value. In pixel-based experiments, observations are RGB images processed through a PQN backbone, and the critic outputs a fixed-size particle set whose discrepancy with the Bellman target is measured via sliced divergences.

## Method

SDRL lifts a one-dimensional base divergence Δ (Wasserstein, Cramér, or MMD) to multivariate return distributions by projecting particles onto random unit directions θ ∈ S^{d-1}, computing the 1D divergence on each projected set, and averaging over L directions (uniform slicing) or optimizing over the most discriminative direction (max slicing). The key theoretical insight is that the univariate sufficient conditions for Bellman contraction—translation nonexpansion, scale contraction, and mixture p-convexity—lift directly to the sliced objective, yielding contraction under shared scalar discounting for uniform slicing. For general dense matrix discounting, the paper introduces a max-sliced variant (MSDRL) and proves a norm-only contraction bound depending solely on the operator norm ‖Γ‖_op, a setting where uniform slicing and MMD provably fail to contract. Training follows a particle-based TD update: predicted particles from the critic are compared to target particles r + Γ·Z_φ⁻(s′,a′) via the sliced divergence, and gradients are backpropagated through the projection and base-divergence computation.

## Results

On the chain policy-evaluation benchmark, objectives satisfying the unbiased sample-gradient property (U) (sliced Cramér, sliced MMD) achieve low empirical W₂ under one-sample TD, while Wasserstein-based and max-sliced variants degrade substantially; under near-exact mixture TD, all objectives perform comparably. On the pixel-based Maze benchmark, sliced MMD and sliced Cramér achieve empirical W₂ on par with the non-sliced multivariate MMD baseline from Zhang et al. (2021). On the Atari subset (Asteroids, Gopher, MsPacman, Pong, Up'n Down), Sliced Cramér performs strongly for control, and Sliced Wasserstein-2 achieves competitive normalized scores despite exhibiting biased gradients and poor distributional matching in earlier experiments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the non-sliced multivariate MMD method from Zhang et al. (2021), which uses a particle-based critic with a direct multivariate MMD objective. On the Maze policy-evaluation benchmark, sliced MMD and sliced Cramér match MMD in empirical W₂ between Monte Carlo returns and the learned critic distribution, confirming that the slicing projection does not sacrifice distributional accuracy. On the chain benchmark, the comparison is more nuanced: under one-sample TD, MMD (which satisfies (U)) and sliced Cramér maintain low W₂, while Wasserstein-based and max-sliced variants that violate (U) show substantially higher W₂; under near-exact TD, this gap closes. On Atari control, Sliced Cramér is competitive with or exceeds MMD in aggregated normalized scores, while Sliced Wasserstein-2 surprisingly achieves strong control performance despite its known gradient bias, suggesting that accurate return-mean estimation may suffice for policy improvement even when the full distribution is poorly captured.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper lifts the Sliced Probability Divergence (SPD) framework from Nadjahi et al. (2020) and the max-slicing mechanism from Deshpande et al. (2019) into the distributional RL setting, extending the univariate Bellman contraction analysis of Bellemare et al. (2017a) to multivariate returns with general matrix discounting. It departs from prior multivariate DRL work (Zhang et al. 2021; Wiltzer et al. 2024a) by providing the first contraction guarantees under dense, state-action-dependent discount matrices, and by systematically characterizing which sliced divergences remain compatible with the standard one-sample TD bootstrap via the unbiased sample-gradient property. The Cramér distance's role as a gradient-unbiased alternative to Wasserstein (Bellemare et al. 2017b) is central to the paper's practical recommendations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical template (conditions T, S, M_p on the base divergence) that immediately yields contraction for any future base divergence, and identifies a concrete open question: no known divergence simultaneously achieves norm-only contraction under general anisotropic discounting, satisfies the unbiased sample-gradient property for one-sample TD, and admits dimension-friendly estimators. Practically, the finding that sliced Cramér is a strong default for multivariate distributional learning (efficient, gradient-unbiased, contractive under isotropic discount) gives practitioners a clear recommendation. The tension between max-slicing's superior contraction properties and its incompatibility with one-sample bootstrapping highlights a fundamental trade-off that will constrain future multivariate DRL designs.
