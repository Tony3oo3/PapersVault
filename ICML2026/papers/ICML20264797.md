## Problem

Existing LLM agent security defenses rely on retrospective analysis of historical context, which is insufficient against multi-turn attacks (jailbreaks and indirect prompt injections) that split malicious intent across turns to evade detection. This paper addresses the gap by proposing a proactive, predictive defense that speculates on future agent behaviors before they occur, rather than reacting to already-generated outputs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264797/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

SSH is triggered asynchronously whenever the target agent begins generating: it clones the agent's current message list as the root of a speculation tree and runs in parallel without blocking the agent. Upon receiving the agent's actual action, SSH prunes the tree via behavioral matching (exact match for tool calls, SimHash similarity for text) and computes a risk score as the fraction of remaining leaf nodes flagged by existing detectors. A dual-threshold alert mechanism (stricter τ₁ when speculation matches the real action, looser τ₂ when it does not) determines whether to trigger resampling, guidance injection, or termination.

## Method

SSH deploys three lightweight simulators (Assistant, User, Environment) on a single 3B uncensored LLM (Dolphin3-3B) via concurrent Multi-LoRA adapters, which asynchronously draft a speculation trajectory tree using a diversity-oriented Beam Search. This search employs behavioral clustering (SimHash N-gram overlap for text, exact function/argument matching for tool calls), bi-level random shuffling, and round-robin selection with quota re-allocation to downsample common behaviors and amplify rare, corner-case trajectories within a fixed budget M. The Differentiated Alignment Strategy trains the simulators via SFT on two complementary objectives: fidelity distillation from the target agent's raw trajectories (preserving error patterns) and benign-injection data synthesis that embeds normal instructions into tool-return results to make the Assistant Simulator deliberately more vulnerable to adversarial induction. Existing detectors (e.g., PromptGuard, LLM-based judgers) are then applied to leaf-node trajectories, and the resulting risk score across the pruned tree provides decision redundancy that reduces reliance on any single detector's precision.

## Results

On AgentDojo (IPI attacks), SSH combined with a Judger detector achieves 0% ASR across all six attack categories (Direct, System, Ignore, Important, Tool, InjecAgent) while maintaining 58.43% average Utility under Attack (UA), compared to 31.78% ASR and 56.59% UA with no defense. On ActorAttack (multi-turn jailbreak), SSH with M=5 samples reduces ASR from 41.5% (HarmBench) and 33.6% (Circuit Breaker) to 0.0% in both subsets, with a Risk Exposure Rate of 99.5%. On utility benchmarks, SSH introduces negligible degradation: BFCL-v3 accuracy drops only 0.25 points (45.13→44.88) and XSTest refusal rate remains at 0%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSH dominates all five baselines on the primary security metric (ASR): it achieves 0% ASR on AgentDojo, whereas the best prompting baseline (Tool Filter) still leaks 3.71% and the best guardrail (ProtectAI) leaks 6.42%. On ActorAttack jailbreaks, SSH (M=5) eliminates all attacks (0% ASR) while the unprotected Qwen3-235B agent suffers 41.5% ASR on HarmBench. Crucially, SSH also improves utility: Judger+SSH reaches 58.43% UA, exceeding the no-defense baseline (56.59%) and all other defenses, while PromptGuard+SSH (44.14% UA) recovers 8.12% UA over PromptGuard alone (36.02%). The paper highlights that even a lightweight 86M PromptGuard classifier, which alone achieves only 12.25% ASR, is elevated to 0% ASR when augmented by SSH, demonstrating that SSH provides decision redundancy that reduces reliance on any single detector's precision. On utility benchmarks (BFCL-v3, XSTest), SSH introduces no measurable degradation, confirming that the speculative overhead does not cause false-positive over-blocking.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the speculative decoding paradigm (Leviathan et al., 2023) from token-level inference acceleration to action-level security speculation, building on recent extensions like SSD, DSP, and Speculative Action that have already moved speculation to the plan/reasoning level. It departs from the entire line of multi-turn agent defenses (Sandwich, Spotlight, PromptGuard, ProtectAI, LLM judges) by inverting their temporal logic: rather than retrospectively analyzing historical context for risk, SSH asynchronously simulates future trajectories via a multi-agent honeypot and prunes them against the agent's actual actions. The diversity-oriented beam search with behavioral clustering and round-robin re-allocation is a novel search strategy not present in prior speculative decoding work, which optimizes for hit rate rather than risk-space coverage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design that elevates even an 86M classifier to 0% ASR suggests a new axis of defense: decision redundancy through trajectory diversity can substitute for detector precision, potentially reducing the arms race on ever-larger safety classifiers. Key limitations include the non-trivial compute cost of M concurrent speculative branches (mitigated but not eliminated by Multi-LoRA and vLLM batching), the thin boundary between ambitious and malicious intent in open-ended tasks, and the fact that the 8% hit rate on text-based jailbreaks means the defense there relies almost entirely on risk exposure rather than verification. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-tool-calling agents, how it behaves under adaptive attackers who probe the honeypot's simulation boundaries, and whether the differentiated alignment strategy (intentionally making the simulator more vulnerable) introduces its own attack surface.
