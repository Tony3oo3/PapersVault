## Problem

Antibody expression ranking is severely limited by the scarcity of labeled experimental data—public benchmarks contain only hundreds of sequences, mostly expressible, with minimal length diversity. Industrial immunization pipelines generate millions of unlabeled but predominantly expressible sequences, yet no existing framework can jointly exploit this large-scale weak positive supervision alongside the few hundred quantitative yield measurements in a single training objective.

## Input / Output

The method takes variable-length VHH antibody sequences (average ~123 residues, std ~13) as input, first aligned to a standardized 128-position IMGT coordinate system via ANARCI to handle length variability. It produces a scalar pseudo-log-likelihood score per sequence that simultaneously supports fine-grained yield ranking (via Kendall's τ / Spearman correlation) and binary expressibility prediction (via a percentile-based threshold derived from the training set), without requiring separate task-specific heads.

## Method

The core contribution is adapting Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) to masked protein language models, which lack tractable autoregressive likelihoods. Instead of computing pseudo-log-likelihood at every position (one forward pass per residue), the paper introduces a union-masked log-likelihood approximation: for a preference pair (y_w, y_l), only positions where the two sequences differ are jointly masked and evaluated in a single forward pass, since identical-position contributions cancel in the DPO score difference. Preference pairs are constructed from two heterogeneous sources—strong supervision (yield differences exceeding a margin δ=30) and weak supervision (immunization-derived sequences paired against zero-yield sequences)—and balanced via a sampling hyperparameter α. Training proceeds in two stages: continual pretraining with masked language modeling on 4.2M camelid-derived sequences to domain-align the backbone, followed by DPO fine-tuning on the unified preference dataset. The approach is evaluated across three transformer backbones (AntiBERTa2, IgBERT, ProtBERT) and shown to outperform supervised regression and classification baselines while exhibiting tighter generalization gaps and smoother optimization trajectories.

## Results

On the Exp-1K test set, the full two-stage framework (continual pretraining + DPO) achieves a composite Avg score of 45.0 with AntiBERTa2, 40.1 with IgBERT, and 40.3 with ProtBERT, consistently outperforming all baselines. The best binary expressibility metric (MCC) reaches 43.7 (AntiBERTa2), while the best yield-ranking metric (Kendall's τ) reaches 46.3 (AntiBERTa2) and the best Spearman correlation on expressible sequences (SCC) reaches 54.7 (IgBERT). Zero-shot PLL scoring performs near chance (MCC ≈ 0–17, τ ≈ −9 to 9), confirming that pretrained sequence likelihood alone is insufficient for expressibility prediction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four categories of baselines: zero-shot PLL scoring, supervised MSE regression (with and without continual pretraining), and its own Stage-II-only DPO variant. The full framework (Stage I + II) outperforms the best supervised regression baseline on the composite Avg metric by +16.0 (AntiBERTa2: 45.0 vs 29.0), +2.6 (IgBERT: 40.1 vs 37.5), and +9.2 (ProtBERT: 40.3 vs 31.1). On MCC, the gains are most pronounced for AntiBERTa2 (43.7 vs 29.1 best supervised) and ProtBERT (40.1 vs 26.1), while IgBERT shows a smaller margin (37.5 vs 36.8). Zero-shot baselines are uniformly weak, with MCC and τ near or below zero, confirming that pretrained sequence likelihood does not capture expressibility. Notably, DPO without continual pretraining (Stage II only) underperforms the full pipeline on all three backbones, demonstrating that domain-aligned representations are essential for preference optimization to succeed.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) from LLMs to protein language models (pLMs), building directly on the union-masked log-likelihood approximation from g-DPO (Ferragu et al., 2025) to make sequence scoring tractable for masked models. It departs from the dominant pLM-based developability paradigm—where a frozen pLM's embeddings feed a regression or classification head—by instead modifying the sequence-level likelihood itself through preference fine-tuning. The two-stage pipeline (continual pretraining / evo-tuning followed by DPO) and the integration of large-scale immunization-derived sequences as weak positive supervision represent the paper's primary conceptual contributions, positioning it at the intersection of preference learning, semi-supervised protein modeling, and antibody engineering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates a practical path to mitigating the severe labeled-data bottleneck in antibody developability prediction by showing that millions of unlabeled immunization sequences can be productively exploited through preference learning, with measurable gains in both ranking and classification. However, the reliance on two proprietary datasets (Exp-1K, Camel-4M) limits reproducibility and external validation, and the semi-quantitative yield measurements (SDS-PAGE band intensity) introduce noise that may cap achievable performance. Open questions include whether the framework generalizes to conventional VH-VL antibodies or other developability properties (aggregation, viscosity), and whether the observed regularization effect of weak data persists at even larger scales or with truly public corpora.
