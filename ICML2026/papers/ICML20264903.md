## Problem

Existing context-aware few-shot molecular property prediction methods face two critical gaps: they model only molecule-property edges in the context graph while ignoring property-property relational structure, and they indiscriminately incorporate all auxiliary properties, introducing task-irrelevant noise that degrades generalization. The paper provides a theoretical reformulation of the context graph learning objective that unifies relational knowledge extraction with information compression, then realizes this objective in a practical framework.

## Input / Output

The method consumes molecular graphs featurized with atomic numbers, chirality tags, bond types, and bond directions, encoded by a pretrained 5-layer GIN (300-dim embeddings), along with a target property task and auxiliary property labels that together form a bipartite context graph of molecular and property nodes. A 2-layer MPNN context encoder processes this graph, and the final prediction is a binary active/inactive label obtained by concatenating the molecule and target-task context representations into a 2-layer MLP. The context graph is the central data structure: its edges encode active, inactive, or unknown molecule-property relations, and the method operates on 2-way K-shot episodes (K=1 or 10) sampled per task.

## Method

RECOG introduces two complementary modules on top of a standard context-aware FSMPP pipeline. The cross-property relation learning (CPRL) module defines a triplet relation signal y_rel = (y_τ1 − y_τ2)² for each molecule paired with two properties, and trains a relation head (2-layer MLP over element-wise product of concatenated context representations) to predict this signal via MSE loss, thereby converting implicit property-property dependencies into explicit optimizable supervision. The context graph information bottleneck (CGIB) module decomposes the context graph into a task subgraph and an environment subgraph, then applies per-auxiliary-task Gumbel-Sigmoid gates (conditioned on the task subgraph representation) to stochastically retain or replace auxiliary node features with Gaussian noise, regularized by a variational mutual information upper bound that penalizes retained information. Training follows a MAML-style bi-level scheme: an inner loop adapts parameters on the support set prediction loss, while the outer loop jointly minimizes the query prediction loss, the relation MSE loss, and the MI compression term with a tunable β coefficient.

## Results

RECOG achieves state-of-the-art ROC-AUC across all five MoleculeNet benchmarks (Tox21, SIDER, MUV, ToxCast, PCBA) in both 10-shot and 1-shot settings. Headline results include 93.52% (10-shot) and 91.01% (1-shot) on Tox21, 95.94% (10-shot) and 81.96% (1-shot) on MUV, and 82.57% (10-shot) and 82.39% (1-shot) on PCBA. The largest relative improvement over the strongest baseline (Pin-Tuning) is 22.61% on MUV 10-shot (95.94% vs. 73.33%), and RECOG also shows an average relative improvement of 11.19% over Pin-Tuning across all datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RECOG outperforms all 16 baselines across every dataset and shot setting. The closest competitor is Pin-Tuning (NeurIPS 2024), which RECOG surpasses by 1.96% on Tox21 10-shot (93.52 vs. 91.56), 5.70% on SIDER 1-shot (90.06 vs. 84.36), 22.61% on MUV 10-shot (95.94 vs. 73.33), 3.91% on ToxCast 10-shot (88.85 vs. 84.94), and 10.41% on PCBA 1-shot (82.39 vs. 71.98). Among context-aware methods, Pre-KRGTS (Information Sciences 2025) and Pre-GS-Meta (IJCAI 2023) are the next strongest baselines, but RECOG leads them by 5.90% on MUV 10-shot and 14.27% on MUV 1-shot. Notably, RECOG's advantage is most pronounced on MUV, the most label-sparse dataset (only 0.31% active labels), where it achieves 95.94% (10-shot) versus 73.33% for Pin-Tuning and 74.47% for Pre-KRGTS. In the 1-shot regime, RECOG gains larger relative margins (e.g., +5.30% on Tox21, +5.70% on SIDER over Pin-Tuning), confirming its effectiveness under extreme label scarcity. On the additional Meta-MolNet benchmarks (JNK3, HIV), RECOG also outperforms Meta-GAT by 5.99% and 5.18% respectively in the 1-shot setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

RECOG extends the context-aware FSMPP paradigm (GS-Meta, Pin-Tuning, KRGTS) by addressing two identified gaps: prior methods model only molecule-property edges in the context graph while ignoring property-property relational structure, and they incorporate all auxiliary context indiscriminately. The method draws on MAML's bi-level optimization for episodic training, the Information Bottleneck principle for a unified theoretical objective, and the Graph Information Bottleneck's noise-injection strategy for compressing the auxiliary subgraph. The key departure is the introduction of a triplet-based cross-property relation signal (squared label difference) as an explicit auxiliary supervision target, combined with a Gumbel-Sigmoid gated bottleneck that adaptively filters task-irrelevant auxiliary nodes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical finding that increasing auxiliary tasks degrades existing context-aware methods but consistently improves RECOG (up to 25.74% on MUV) provides a concrete recipe for leveraging more context without the usual instability. The unified IB-derived objective (Eq. 8) offers a principled framework for balancing context exploitation vs. compression that could transfer to other graph-based few-shot settings. Limitations include relatively high variance on some datasets (attributed to stochastic episodic sampling and bi-level optimization overhead), and the approach is validated only on MoleculeNet benchmarks, leaving open whether the relational signal design generalizes to 3D molecular representations or non-binary property tasks.
