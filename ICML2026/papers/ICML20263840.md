## Problem

Rotary position encodings (RoPE) have become the dominant position encoding in LLMs and ViTs due to their translational invariance and compatibility with linear attention, but they require a canonical coordinate system that graphs lack. Existing graph position encodings either use absolute encodings (which fail to encode invariance) or bias-based relative encodings (which require instantiating the full N×N attention matrix, ruling out O(N) linear attention). The paper asks to what extent RoPE can be generalised to graph-structured data while preserving its key efficiency advantages.

## Input / Output

The method takes an undirected graph's adjacency matrix and node feature vectors, then computes the lowest m eigenvectors of the graph Laplacian (m typically 3–16, far smaller than the token dimension d) as per-node spectral coordinate vectors r_i ∈ R^m. These coordinates are projected through learnable frequency vectors ω_n ∈ R^m to produce rotation angles, which are applied as 2D rotations to consecutive pairs of elements in query and key vectors. The only learnable parameters are the dm/2 frequency components per layer, making WIRE a negligible parameter overhead relative to the rest of the network.

## Method

WIRE (Wave-Induced Rotary Encodings) generalises RoPE to graphs by using the lowest m Laplacian eigenvectors as positional coordinates: each node i receives a spectral feature r_i = [u_k[i]/√λ_k]_{k=1}^{m-1}, and standard 2D RoPE rotations with angles θ_n = ω_n^T r_i are applied to query and key vectors. Two key theoretical results distinguish WIRE: (1) on grid graphs (Cartesian products of paths), WIRE exactly recovers standard RoPE used in LLMs and ViTs (Theorem 2), and (2) with randomly sampled frequencies, the expected attention score is downweighted by exp(−ω²R(i,j)/2) where R(i,j) is the graph effective resistance (Theorem 3), providing a principled topological inductive bias without ever instantiating the N×N attention matrix. Because WIRE modifies tokens rather than attention logits, it is directly compatible with O(N) linear attention (Performer, SGFormer) and KV-caching, unlike bias-based RPEs. In practice, WIRE is a drop-in addition to existing graph transformer architectures such as GraphGPS, and can also use cheaper random-walk position encodings in place of spectral features at the cost of theoretical guarantees.

## Results

On the 12 graph benchmark datasets (Table 3), WIRE consistently improves O(N) Performer baselines by 0.1–2.6 points across all tasks. Most notably, WIRE Performers match or exceed the O(N²) Transformer on MNIST (98.10 vs. 98.05 accuracy), MalNet-Tiny (93.46 vs. 93.36), PascalVOC-SP (0.376 vs. 0.37 F1), and PATTERN (86.63 vs. 86.69), while remaining trainable on a single T4 12GB GPU. On the point cloud tasks (Table 4), WIRE achieves 93.4% classification accuracy on ModelNet40, outperforming both the NoPE baseline (91.8%) and Cartesian RoPE (91.8%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

WIRE improves upon the O(N) Performer baseline on all 12 graph benchmark datasets, with gains ranging from +0.02 (ogbg-code2) to +2.6 points (ogbg-molpcba, 0.238→0.264 Avg. Precision). Against the O(N²) Transformer baseline, WIRE Performers match or exceed full-attention accuracy on four datasets (MNIST, ogbg-ppa, PascalVOC-SP, MalNet-Tiny) while remaining linear in sequence length, and close the gap substantially on PATTERN (86.63 vs. 86.69) and CLUSTER (77.53 vs. 78.02). On point cloud classification (ModelNet40), WIRE (93.4%) outperforms both the NoPE PCT baseline (91.8%) and Cartesian RoPE (91.8%), and matches Cartesian RoPE on ShapeNet segmentation (93.0% vs. 93.2%). In the appendix, WIRE also provides consistent gains when added to SGFormer (e.g., +0.52 on MNIST, +0.7 on CIFAR-10) and BigBird (+0.84 on MNIST, +0.82 on CIFAR-10), confirming broad compatibility across efficient transformer architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

WIRE generalises rotary position encodings (RoPE), a mechanism popularised in LLMs and ViTs (Su et al., 2024; Heo et al., 2024), to graph-structured data by feeding graph Laplacian eigenvectors as the rotation coordinates. This departs from prior graph position encodings, which were either absolute (adding spectral features to tokens, e.g. Dwivedi & Bresson 2020; Kreuzer et al. 2021) or relative (biasing attention logits with shortest-path or effective-resistance distances, e.g. Graphormer/Ying et al. 2021). The key conceptual departure is that WIRE modifies tokens directly via rotations rather than attention logits, making it compatible with O(N) linear attention (Performer, Katharopoulos et al. 2020; Choromanski et al. 2021) — a property that bias-based RPEs fundamentally lack. The method is integrated into GraphGPS (Rampášek et al. 2022) and other efficient architectures (SGFormer, BigBird) for evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WIRE provides a lightweight, drop-in structural inductive bias for graph transformers that preserves O(N) complexity, addressing a long-standing tension between expressiveness (needing global structural information) and efficiency (avoiding N×N attention matrices). The theoretical connection to effective resistance (Theorem 3) and the recovery of standard RoPE on grid graphs (Theorem 2) give the method a principled foundation beyond empirical tuning. Open questions include whether the spectral features are truly necessary versus cheaper alternatives like RWPEs (which also work but lose theoretical guarantees), how WIRE interacts with graph properties like homophily, and whether the approach extends to dynamic or heterogeneous graphs. The modest parameter overhead (<1% of model size) and broad architectural compatibility make it a practical tool likely to see wide adoption in graph transformer pipelines.
