## Problem

Semi-supervised learning methods rely on either uniform distribution assumptions or explicit estimation of the unlabeled data distribution to generate pseudo-labels, both of which break down under extreme label scarcity combined with unknown, arbitrary unlabeled distributions. This failure produces massive false-positive pseudo-labels that trigger representation confusion, destroying the discriminability of learned features. The paper formalizes this under-explored regime as Universal Semi-supervised Learning (UniSSL) and asks how to learn robust representations without any distributional prior on the unlabeled data.

## Input / Output

The method takes a small labeled set (e.g., 4 images per class, 40 total for CIFAR-10) with ground-truth labels and a large unlabeled set (e.g., 11,650–100,000 images) whose class distribution is completely unknown and arbitrary. All images are resized to 32×32 RGB. At inference, the auxiliary head and projection head are discarded, and the primary classification head outputs a single class label via argmax over logits.

## Method

SAGE (Simplex Anchored Graph-state Equipartition) replaces distribution estimation with representation-level structural inference. It constructs a fixed simplex equiangular tight frame of K=d+1 unit-norm vectors in R^d as a rigid, distribution-agnostic coordinate frame; unlabeled representations are projected onto this frame to yield relational embeddings, from which a structural affinity matrix is built and diffused over β=5 graph steps to produce a structural consensus that guides a contrastive loss aligning pairwise instance similarities. A Distribution-agnostic Reliability Prioritization module weights pseudo-labels using truncated Gaussian kernels over max-confidence and top-two-margin statistics (tracked via EMA), while an auxiliary classification head isolates noisy pseudo-label gradients from the primary head. The full objective jointly optimizes supervised classification, structural contrastive loss, representation consistency across augmentation views, and the weighted auxiliary loss, all on a WRN-28-2 backbone.

## Results

SAGE achieves an average accuracy improvement of 8.52 pp over the strongest prior baseline across five benchmarks (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, Food-101, SVHN, STL-10). On CIFAR-10 under the arbitrary unlabeled distribution (N=40, M=11650), SAGE reaches 61.24% top-1 accuracy, exceeding CPG (50.24%) by 11.00 pp. On SVHN under the same arbitrary setting, SAGE achieves 84.11%, a gain of over 24 pp. Under the standard uniform SSL setting on CIFAR-10 (N=40, M=13980), SAGE attains 80.68%, surpassing CGMatch (72.83%) by 7.85 pp.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAGE outperforms all 12 baselines across every evaluated setting, with no losses in the pairwise t-test (136 wins, 59 ties, 0 losses out of 195 comparisons at α=0.05). Against the strongest SSL baselines, SAGE beats CGMatch by 7.85 pp on CIFAR-10 uniform (80.68% vs 72.83%) and FreeMatch by a wide margin on all datasets. Among LTSSL methods, SAGE surpasses CPG by 11.00 pp on CIFAR-10 arbitrary (61.24% vs 50.24%) and by 3.71 pp on CIFAR-100 (37.82% vs 34.11%), while the gap over SimPro and CDMAD exceeds 20 pp on CIFAR-100. On SVHN under the arbitrary setting, SAGE (84.11%) leads CPG (57.39%) by 26.72 pp and FixMatch (58.74%) by 25.37 pp. In the open-set experiment on CIFAR-10, SAGE (60.88%) outperforms CaliMatch (56.12%), SCOMatch (51.03%), and SSB (47.41%). On the large-scale ImageNet-127 benchmark, SAGE (53.83%) exceeds CPG (50.43%) by 3.40 pp and Meta-Expert (49.83%) by 4.00 pp.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SSL → LTSSL → ReaLTSSL lineage by formalizing Universal Semi-supervised Learning (UniSSL), which combines extreme label scarcity with truly unknown, arbitrary unlabeled distributions. It departs from prior methods that either assume uniform distributions (FixMatch, FreeMatch, CGMatch) or attempt to estimate the unlabeled distribution (CPG, SimPro), arguing both fail under UniSSL's conditions. The key conceptual departure is shifting from distribution estimation to representation-level structural inference: it repurposes the simplex equiangular tight frame (from neural collapse theory) as a fixed geometric coordinate frame and introduces graph-state relational inference to capture high-order inter-sample dependencies, bypassing the need for distributional priors entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical gap: in real deployments, unlabeled data distributions are inaccessible and arbitrary, yet existing methods silently assume they are known or uniform. The 8.52% average improvement (with >24pp gains on SVHN under arbitrary settings) demonstrates that structural consensus can substitute for distribution estimation, offering a more robust paradigm for label-scarce, distribution-shifted scenarios. Limitations include the assumption of a fixed, known number of classes and quadratic scaling of the structural affinity matrix with batch size, which constrains applicability to open-world and very large-scale settings. Future work on adaptive anchor generation and more efficient graph diffusion could broaden the method's reach.
