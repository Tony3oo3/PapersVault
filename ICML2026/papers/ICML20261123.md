## Problem

Chain-of-Thought reasoning in LLMs is structurally vulnerable to bias propagation: once a stereotypical prior is activated in an early reasoning step, autoregressive inertia compels the model to rationalize it through subsequent steps rather than correct it. Existing debiasing approaches—whether static training constraints or inference-time interventions like prompting and filtering—fail to interrupt this cascade at its generative origin, and empirical analysis shows that naive self-correction prompts actually degrade performance by disrupting logical coherence.

## Input / Output

In direct-generation mode, the model receives a text query containing sensitive attributes (e.g., BBQ or CEB questions) and produces a full CoT trajectory ending in an answer. In self-correction mode, the input additionally includes a biased CoT response (generated via a controlled bias-injection protocol) and an explicit debiasing instruction, and the model must output a structured critique within a think block followed by a corrected answer. The training data pairs each query with both a verified unbiased trajectory (y⁺) and a biased trajectory (y⁻), with the self-correction objective conditioning on the biased prefix plus instruction to produce the unbiased suffix.

## Method

SELF-DEBIAS reformulates debiasing as a corrective resource-allocation problem over the model's output probability mass, operating in three progressive stages. Stage I cold-starts via SFT with a joint objective that trains both direct unbiased generation and explicit self-correction from a biased prefix. Stage II performs trajectory-level preference optimization: it freezes valid reasoning prefixes and optimizes only the divergent suffix using a DPO-style log-ratio margin (Eq. 7), combined with a Jain's Fairness Index regularizer (Eq. 8) that penalizes variance across samples to prevent the model from neglecting hard, bias-prone cases. Stage III introduces an online self-improvement loop where bias injection on unlabeled queries generates failure trajectories, and a consistency-filtering mechanism selects stable self-corrected outputs as supervision, iteratively expanding the correction frontier. The key distinction from standard DPO is the suffix-level granularity (preserving valid context) and the distributional fairness constraint that enforces uniform debiasing capability rather than average performance.

## Results

SELF-DEBIAS Iter2 achieves an average score of 81.7 across eight benchmarks, surpassing the strongest reasoning baseline DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B (70.4) by 11.3 points and Qwen3-8B (77.5) by 4.2 points. Under self-correction, SELF-DEBIAS further improves to 82.1 (p<0.05), while all baselines degrade: Qwen3-8B drops 13.5 points, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct drops 9.5, and DeepSeek-R1-Distill drops 6.7. On utility benchmarks, SELF-DEBIAS maintains ARC-C at 93.1 and GSM8K at 87.6, confirming no fairness tax. On the primary fairness benchmark BBQ, it reaches 97.0, and on OOD CrowS-Pairs it achieves 71.2, both leading all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the four reasoning-model baselines, SELF-DEBIAS Iter2 leads on every benchmark: it outperforms DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B by 11.3 points on average (70.4→81.7), Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct by 4.3 points (77.4→81.7), Qwen3-8B by 4.2 points (77.5→81.7), and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct by 29.4 points (52.3→81.7). Critically, while all four baselines suffer severe degradation under self-correction (ranging from −6.5 to −13.5 points), SELF-DEBIAS consistently gains (+0.3 to +0.5), with Iter2+SC reaching 82.1. Among inference-time correction methods (Table 4), SELF-DEBIAS's intrinsic self-correction (+0.4) is the only positive-scaling strategy; Confirmation (−0.7), Denying (−1.3), Self-refine (−0.2), and ReVISE (−1.1) all degrade performance. Fairness-specific methods fare worse still: CAL drops 1.1 points and BiasFilter drops 3.1 points (with a precipitous CEB-Adult collapse from 67.1 to 54.5), confirming that external filtering sacrifices semantic integrity to satisfy superficial constraints.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on DPO-based preference optimization (Rafailov et al., 2023) and self-correction prompting strategies (Self-Refine, ReVISE), but departs from both in a key way: rather than applying coarse-grained penalties to full responses or relying on external inference-time interventions, it reformulates debiasing as a trajectory-level resource allocation problem where only the divergent suffix after a bias-activation step is optimized. The fairness regularizer is adapted from Jain's Index in network resource allocation (Jain et al., 1984), and the online self-improvement loop draws on curriculum learning principles (Bengio et al., 2009) combined with self-consistency filtering. Unlike prior debiasing methods (BiasDPO, CAL, BiasFilter) that treat bias as a static output property, SELF-DEBIAS targets the sequential rationalization cascade unique to CoT reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The empirical demonstration that standard reasoning models exhibit a 'detection-correction gap'—identifying injected biases in 12–33% of cases yet still rationalizing them—challenges the working assumption that self-awareness implies self-repair, and the finding that generic self-correction prompts are counter-productive (degrading all baselines by 6–14 points) has practical implications for anyone deploying CoT models in sensitive domains. The data efficiency (20k seed samples plus autonomous online refinement) offers a scalable pathway for fairness alignment without exhaustive annotation. Open questions include whether the approach extends to larger models (>70B), non-CoT architectures, and whether the consistency-filtering criterion generalizes to biases beyond the tested demographic dimensions.
