## Problem

Existing self-supervised learning methods for wrist-worn accelerometers treat sensor streams as unstructured time series, optimizing objectives over fixed-length windows with arbitrary boundaries rather than meaningful movement units. This overlooks the underlying biological structure of human motor control, where continuous wrist motion is composed of elementary submovements, and prevents the model from learning high-level temporal organization between coherent motion events. The paper asks whether grounding tokenization in the submovement theory of motor control provides the inductive bias needed for effective representation learning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw 3-axis wrist accelerometer signals (80 Hz for NHANES pretraining; 20–100 Hz across downstream benchmarks), first separating gravity via a 0.5 Hz Butterworth high-pass filter and then segmenting the linear acceleration at zero-crossings into variable-length movement segments (typically ~0.5 s), each resampled to a fixed 32-sample token. The output is a 1028-dimensional window-level representation (128-d from mean/std pooling of Transformer token embeddings concatenated with a 900-d flattened gravity residual), which feeds a multinomial logistic regression probe to produce activity class labels over 10-second windows.

## Method

Bio-PM tokenizes gravity-reduced acceleration into 'type 2' movement segments bounded by zero-crossings (with a two-stage hysteresis filter enforcing ≥50 ms duration and ≥0.01 g peak amplitude), treating each segment as a 'word' analogous to submovements as 'phonemes.' A 1D CNN encodes each resampled segment into a 60-d embedding, concatenated with a learned 3-d axis embedding and a scalar duration to form a 64-d token; tokens from all three axes are merged and sorted by midpoint time. A 5-layer Transformer encoder (1.4M parameters) models inter-segment dependencies using time-aware positional encodings—an MLP for absolute phase within the 10 s window plus learned relative-position embeddings indexed by discretized pairwise time offsets. Pretraining uses masked movement-segment reconstruction with a hybrid 50% masking policy (random + contiguous 1-s-bin masking) and 20% corruption of visible tokens to prevent trivial copying, minimizing an L1 loss upweighted 100× on masked tokens. Downstream, the frozen encoder's pooled token embeddings are fused with the low-pass gravity residual and classified via linear probing under subject-disjoint splits.

## Results

Bio-PM (1.4M params) achieves the highest Macro-F1 on all six subject-disjoint HAR benchmarks, with an average of 0.65 across UMH, PAMAP, WISDM, MHEALTH, WHARF, and HAD. The largest single-dataset gain is on MHEALTH (0.80 vs. 0.68 for the strongest baseline TF-C, a +0.12 improvement). Bio-PM also outperforms the capacity-matched equal-chunk masked-reconstruction baseline by +0.18 average Macro-F1, isolating the contribution of bio-inspired tokenization. A one-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test over 41 pooled folds confirms significance over TF-C (p < 0.0001, Cohen's d = 0.61).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest controlled baseline, TF-C contrastive learning (8.7M params), Bio-PM (1.4M params) wins on all six datasets with an average Macro-F1 improvement of +0.06, with the largest margin on MHEALTH (+0.12) and UMH (+0.04). Against AugPred (10.5M params, replicating Yuan et al. 2024), Bio-PM improves by +0.11 on average, showing that bio-inspired tokenization with masked reconstruction substantially outperforms augmentation-prediction objectives even at a fraction of the parameter count. Against the capacity-matched equal-chunk masked-reconstruction baseline (1.4M params, same architecture and objective but arbitrary time chunks instead of movement segments), Bio-PM gains +0.18 on average, directly attributing the improvement to the tokenization strategy rather than model size or objective. Against the generic time-series foundation models Moment (35M) and Chronos (8.3M), which are not pretrained on NHANES, Bio-PM achieves +0.06 and +0.23 average Macro-F1 respectively, confirming that domain-specific pretraining with biologically grounded tokens is critical for wearable HAR transfer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant SSL-for-wearables paradigm (contrastive learning, augmentation prediction, masked reconstruction over raw windows) by introducing a bio-inspired tokenization strategy grounded in the submovement theory of motor control. It adopts the movement segment concept from Daneault et al. (2023) and the type-2 zero-crossing boundary criterion from Simo et al. (2014), repurposing these clinical motor-control constructs as the atomic unit for sequence modeling. The SSL objective (masked reconstruction) and architecture (CNN + Transformer) are standard; the novelty lies entirely in what is tokenized and how temporal structure is encoded, shifting the learning focus from local waveform morphology to high-level compositional organization of movement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The strong, well-isolated ablation showing a −0.18 Macro-F1 drop when bio-inspired tokens are replaced with equal-length chunks provides compelling evidence that domain-informed tokenization is a critical inductive bias for wearable SSL, not merely a preprocessing detail. The fact that a 1.4M-parameter model consistently outperforms 8.7M and 10.5M baselines suggests that structural priors can substitute for model capacity in this setting. Key open questions include whether the approach extends to other body locations and sensor modalities (gyroscopes, magnetometers), whether incorporating gravity/postural context during pretraining (rather than only at probe time) further improves representations, and whether scaling to larger corpora like UK Biobank (>100k subjects) yields additional gains. The framework also opens a path toward clinical motor assessment in neurological conditions, where submovement features are already established biomarkers.
