## Problem

Most diffusion-based OOD detectors rely on score magnitude or local differential geometry (curvature, covariance spectra) and largely ignore the approximate group equivariances (flips, rotations, circular shifts) that pretrained diffusion score fields inherit from in-distribution data and convolutional backbones. This paper asks whether equivariance breaking of the learned score field—measured at noisy levels rather than in pixel space—can serve as a complementary and interpretable OOD signal, even when score magnitude remains unchanged.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (32×32 for CIFAR-scale benchmarks) or a 256×256 SAR patch as input, along with a frozen pretrained diffusion backbone (improved-diffusion U-Net). The input is consumed by first adding Gaussian noise at a small set of selected timesteps, then applying a finite group of spatial transformations (identity, flips, 90°/180° rotations, 1-pixel circular shifts) to the noisy version. The output is a scalar OOD score calibrated with ID-only statistics (KDE, z-score, or Mahalanobis), plus a spatial equivariance-breaking heatmap that localises where symmetry violations concentrate—useful for target localisation in SAR imagery.

## Method

GEPC is a training-free probe that, for each group element g and selected timestep t, computes the equivariance residual Δ_g s_θ(x_t, t) = P_g^{-1} s_θ(P_g x_t, t) − s_θ(x_t, t), measuring how consistently the score field transforms under the group. Residual energies are pooled spatially, averaged over group elements, aggregated across timesteps selected by an ID-only coefficient-of-variation stability criterion, and calibrated using ID-only density models. Theoretically, the paper defines a population-level equivariance-breaking functional B^(G)(p_t) and derives ID upper bounds and OOD lower bounds on the expected GEPC residual under mild score-error assumptions, plus cross-backbone pointwise bounds via a distance-to-manifold argument with Lipschitz and directional-contraction conditions. Practically, GEPC requires only (1+|G|)K forward passes per input (no Jacobians, no fine-tuning), placing it in a similar NFE regime as simple score-norm baselines while approaching the accuracy of more expensive trajectory and curvature-based methods.

## Results

Under a strictly shared frozen CelebA-32 improved-diffusion backbone, GEPC achieves an average AUROC of 0.908 across 9 standard 32×32 ID/OOD pairs (CIFAR-10, SVHN, CelebA as ID; CIFAR-100, CelebA, SVHN as OOD), using 16 forward passes and zero Jacobian-vector products. The lighter GEPC_lite variant (flips-only subgroup, 8F+0J) reaches 0.910 average AUROC, matching or exceeding the full GEPC score. On the cross-domain SAR task (LSUN-256 backbone applied to 256×256 HRSID patches without fine-tuning), GEPC produces strong ship/wake separation with interpretable equivariance-breaking heatmaps that concentrate on targets while remaining low on homogeneous sea clutter.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Under the shared CelebA-32 backbone, GEPC (0.908 avg AUROC) outperforms SCOPED (0.892) by 1.6 points and MSMA (0.861) by 4.7 points, while using no Jacobian-vector products (16F+0J vs. SCOPED's 2F+2J). GEPC trails DiffPath (0.918) by 1.0 point on average, though GEPC_lite (0.910) narrows this gap to 0.8 points at half the forward-pass cost. Against trajectory/likelihood-based baselines, GEPC dominates: NLL (0.581), IC-diffusion (0.536), DDPM-OOD (0.586), and LMD (0.515) all fall 30+ points below GEPC, indicating that score-magnitude and reconstruction-based signals are far less discriminative under a mismatched backbone. The ID-trained discriminative baselines (DoS 0.928, TT 0.832, IC 0.950 on CIFAR-10) are not directly comparable since they are trained per-ID-dataset, but GEPC's training-free protocol achieves competitive aggregate performance without any ID-specific training. On the SAR cross-domain task, GEPC outperforms the classical Reed-Xiaoli CFAR detector in both detection AUROC and localisation quality, with residual maps that visually highlight ships and wakes while remaining flat on clutter.

## Relation to Prior Work

GEPC builds on the DDPM score-field framework (Ho et al., 2020) but departs from the dominant diffusion-OOD paradigm that exploits score magnitude, trajectory energy, or local differential geometry (curvature, covariance spectra) as in DiffPath, SCOPED, and EigenScore. Its conceptual lineage is closer to equivariant score-based generative models (Chen et al., 2024; Hoogeboom et al., 2022), which treat equivariance as a training-time inductive bias; GEPC repurposes the same mathematical structure as a *test-time diagnostic* on a frozen backbone, measuring how consistently the learned score transforms under a finite group. The paper positions itself as complementary to iDECODe (Kaur et al., 2022), which wraps equivariance violations in a conformal layer, by instead analyzing the population-level equivariance-breaking functional of the diffusion score across timesteps and deriving ID/OOD bounds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GEPC offers a training-free, Jacobian-free OOD probe that is computationally lightweight (16 NFEs for the full variant, 8 for GEPC_lite) and produces spatially interpretable equivariance-breaking maps, which is practically valuable for sensing applications like SAR target detection where a pretrained backbone may not match the target domain. The theoretical contribution—ID upper and OOD lower bounds on the expected residual under mild score-error assumptions—gives a formal account of *when* posterior consistency should hold or break, including a cross-backbone distance-to-manifold analysis. Key limitations include reliance on approximate group invariances (performance may degrade for strongly oriented or structured data), dependence on a pretrained diffusion backbone, and the fact that empirical AUROC gains over DiffPath are modest (0.908 vs. 0.918 average). Open questions include extending to continuous or learned group actions, non-convolutional backbones (DiT), and multi-modal diffusion models.
