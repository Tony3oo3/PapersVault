## Problem

Existing feature-space tail distribution reconstruction methods for long-tailed recognition prescribe heuristic statistical updates (mean shifts, covariance blends) without theoretical guarantees that these updates preserve multi-class separability. Under extreme sample scarcity, tail statistics are unreliable, and unconstrained calibration can inadvertently shift or inflate the tail distribution along rival-aligned discriminative directions, worsening confusion with visually or semantically similar classes even when within-class fit improves.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262800/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates entirely in the frozen backbone feature space after Stage-1 training: it consumes per-class empirical means and covariances (computed from the limited tail samples), a set of top-M rival classes per tail class (identified via the frozen classifier's confusion matrix), and head-class statistics as transfer sources. It outputs calibrated tail-class means and covariances, a set of synthesized tail features (matched to the maximum head-class sample count) produced via Gaussian sampling or a class-conditional latent DDIM, and a Stage-2 fine-tuned linear classifier trained on the augmented frozen features.

## Method

RBDR derives a distribution-free pairwise confusion upper bound via the Cantelli inequality, links it monotonically to the Fisher separation ratio, and uses this surrogate-Fisher connection to define safe calibration directions. For mean calibration, it computes an OT-based (Sinkhorn) head-to-tail candidate direction and projects it onto a multi-rival supportive set—defined by the constraint that the update must not decrease Fisher separation against any rival—via a soft hinge-squared penalty solver. For covariance calibration, it performs a head-guided convex shrinkage (blending the tail covariance with a transport-weighted head covariance proxy) and then applies a low-rank contraction operator that selectively reduces variance along an orthonormal basis of rival-induced directions while preserving orthogonal diversity. The entire pipeline is single-round and offline: statistics are calibrated once, features are synthesized, and the classifier is fine-tuned, with a global potential function providing first-order safety guarantees across all tail classes simultaneously.

## Results

On CIFAR100-LT (ρ=200), RBDR achieves 49.02% Top-1 accuracy, outperforming the strongest feature-space reconstruction baseline FUR by +2.82% and the CE baseline by +14.22%. On ImageNet-LT, RBDR reaches 55.91% overall accuracy (38.25% Few), surpassing FDC (55.30%) and FUR (55.50%). On iNaturalist 2018, RBDR attains 72.92% overall accuracy, leading all reconstruction-based methods. Gains are largest under severe imbalance (ρ=200/100), where tail statistics are least reliable.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the directly comparable feature-space reconstruction methods, RBDR consistently outperforms all baselines across all three datasets. On CIFAR100-LT (ρ=200), RBDR (49.02%) beats FUR (46.20%) by +2.82%, FDC (45.80%) by +3.22%, and LADC (46.67%) by +2.35%; the gap narrows at ρ=10 where RBDR (66.36%) still leads FUR (61.80%) by +4.56%. On ImageNet-LT, RBDR improves over FDC/FUR on Medium (+1.45%/+1.15%) and Few (+0.45%/+0.45%) splits and overall (+0.61%/+0.41%), while GCL+H2T achieves a higher Few score (52.15%) but sacrifices Many accuracy (62.36% vs. 65.72%), yielding a lower overall result. On iNaturalist 2018, RBDR leads FDC by +0.72% and FUR by +0.32% overall, with consistent gains on all Many/Medium/Few splits. Against non-reconstruction baselines, RBDR substantially outperforms CE (+14.22% on CIFAR100-LT ρ=200, +11.51% on ImageNet-LT All) and also surpasses re-weighting methods like WD and DisA, as well as contrastive methods like FeatRecon, confirming that risk-bounded statistic calibration provides a distinct advantage over both loss-reweighting and feature-space contrastive approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

RBDR extends the feature-space distribution reconstruction line (GistNet, FDC, FUR, H2T, LADC) by replacing their heuristic tail-statistic update rules with a theoretically grounded calibration procedure. The method builds on Sinkhorn optimal transport (Cuturi, 2013) for head-to-tail mean transfer initialization and on DDIM (Song et al., 2020) as an optional diffusion backend, but its core departure is the derivation of rival-aware safety constraints via a distribution-free Cantelli surrogate linked to Fisher separation. Unlike prior reconstruction methods that prescribe how to shift tail means or shrink covariances without guaranteeing multi-class separability is preserved, RBDR characterizes a supportive set of update directions under which a surrogate discriminative risk bound is non-increasing, converting reconstruction cues into bounded calibration operators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled, distribution-free safety analysis for offline tail-distribution reconstruction, giving practitioners a modular plug-in (supportive projection + low-rank rival-subspace contraction) that can be layered onto existing two-stage pipelines without architectural changes. A notable practical finding is that the simple Gaussian/shrinkage variant outperforms the more flexible diffusion backend (RBDR-DDIM) across all three benchmarks, suggesting that in frozen feature spaces, moment-matched sampling with conservative variance control is harder to beat than generative diversity. Open questions include whether the single-round, frozen-feature calibration can be extended to online or iterative settings, whether the conservative Cantelli bound is overly restrictive for very high-dimensional features, and how the rival-set construction generalizes when confusion structure is non-stationary across training stages.
