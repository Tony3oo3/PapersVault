## Problem

Energy-based models offer flexible distribution modeling but suffer from unstable training and expensive joint-space MCMC sampling. Existing latent-variable EBM approaches either enforce rigid alignment between the latent and generator prior (restricting latent flexibility and weakening the energy), or rely on cumbersome contrastive training paradigms with slow sampling. This paper addresses how to guide EBM training with informative semantic latents while keeping MCMC confined to the data space and enabling efficient cooperative generator initialization.

## Input / Output

For unconditional generation, the model takes a Gaussian noise vector m ~ N(0,I) and produces an image after one generator forward pass followed by 15–16 MCMC refinement steps (or zero MCMC steps for one-step generation). For OOD detection, the joint energy score s(x) = −F(f_θ(x)) + γ·sim(g_ψ(f_φ(x)), h(x)) yields a scalar decision value. For zero-shot restoration, a degraded image y = Ax is processed via its pseudo-inverse A†y to obtain an initial estimate, which is then refined through alternating MCMC updates on the latent code m and a residual Y, producing a restored image at the target resolution (e.g., 256×256 on CelebA-HQ).

## Method

LGCEBM defines a joint energy E_θ(x,z) = F(f_φ(x)) − log p_φ,ψ(z|x) with a cosine-similarity posterior on the unit sphere, and proves (Proposition 3.1) that the training gradient requires MCMC only in the data space via the marginal energy E_θ(x) = −log ∫ exp(−E_θ(x,z)) dz, eliminating costly joint-space sampling. Target latent variables z are obtained by passing augmented data through a frozen pretrained SimCLR encoder, so the energy learns semantic data–latent relationships directly from real pairs without contrastive exclusion of fake pairs. The generator G_ζ is trained cooperatively (alternating with the energy) to initialize MCMC chains in one forward pass, using either energy distribution matching (EM, an MSE loss against MCMC-refined samples) or energy-and-real distribution matching (ERM, adding an ELBO term that aligns the generator's latent output with the SSRL encoder). A stochastic augmentation strategy applies random SSRL augmentations to generated negative samples with probability p before MCMC, preventing the energy from catastrophically forgetting low-density regions and improving OOD robustness.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, LGCEBM-EM achieves FID 4.23 with only 16 NFE, while the modified variant LGCEBM†-EM reaches FID 3.28 (16 NFE) and FID 3.48 in single-step mode (1 NFE), surpassing Consistency Models (FID 8.70) and ACT (FID 6.0) at the same NFE budget. On ImageNet 32, LGCEBM†-EM attains FID 3.90, outperforming Flow Matching (FID 5.02) and Energy Matching (FID 6.64). On CelebA-HQ 256, LGCEBM†-ERML achieves FID 6.99, beating DDGAN (7.64) and Score SDE (7.23). For OOD detection on CIFAR-10, the joint energy score yields AUROC 0.82 on CIFAR-100 and 0.97 on Constant, matching or exceeding specialized OOD methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LGCEBM-EM (FID 4.23, 16 NFE) substantially outperforms CLEL-Large (FID 8.61, 1200 NFE) and Dual-MCMC (FID 9.26, 31 NFE) on CIFAR-10, demonstrating that latent guidance plus cooperative training closes the gap with prior latent-variable EBMs at a fraction of the sampling cost. In single-step mode, LGCEBM†-EM w/o MCMC (FID 3.48) beats EC-VAE (5.20), Consistency Models (8.70), and ACT (6.0), establishing it as the strongest one-step EBM. The modified model LGCEBM†-EM (FID 3.28, 16 NFE) surpasses Energy Matching (3.34, 325 NFE) and CDRL (4.31, 96 NFE) with far fewer function evaluations, and is competitive with DDGAN (3.75, 4 NFE) and R3GAN (3.64, 1 NFE) while using fewer parameters (34M vs. 61M/41M). On ImageNet 32, LGCEBM†-EM (FID 3.90) is the first EBM to beat Flow Matching (5.02). For OOD detection, the joint energy score improves CIFAR-100 AUROC from 0.66 (marginal energy only) to 0.82, exceeding CLEL (0.72) and approaching specialized OOD EBM (0.87), while matching MPDR-S on SVHN (0.95 vs. 0.99). In zero-shot image restoration, LGCEBM-ERML achieves the best colorization FID (12.9) among EBM-based methods, outperforming EC-VAE (13.3) and approaching diffusion-based DDNM (26.4 FID) and DDRM (31.2 FID).

## Relation to Prior Work

LGCEBM extends the cooperative EBM training paradigm (Xie et al., 2020; Cui & Han, 2023) by introducing pretrained self-supervised representations (SimCLR) as target latent variables to guide the joint energy, departing from CLEL's contrastive latent encoder and Divergence Triangle's exact latent-prior alignment. The key theoretical departure is Proposition 3.1, which shows MCMC sampling is only required in data space rather than the joint (x,z) space, simplifying training and enabling direct learning of semantic data-latent relationships from real samples. The cosine-similarity posterior and stochastic augmentation strategy further distinguish this work from prior latent-variable EBMs that rely on Gaussian posteriors or contrastive exclusion of fake pairs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work narrows the long-standing performance gap between EBMs and mainstream generative models (GANs, diffusion), with the modified model becoming the first EBM to beat Flow Matching on ImageNet 32 and achieving competitive FID with far fewer parameters than DDGAN/R3GAN. The versatility across OOD detection, conditional sampling, and zero-shot image restoration demonstrates that latent-guided joint energy modeling provides genuine density estimation benefits beyond pure generation. Open questions include whether the dependency on GAN architectures (DDGAN discriminator, R3GAN generator) for best results limits the method's identity as a pure EBM, whether the SSRL pretraining requirement generalizes to non-image modalities, and whether the framework scales to ImageNet 256 or video generation.
