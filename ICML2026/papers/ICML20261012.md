## Problem

Post-hoc interpretability methods typically attribute model behavior to its components, training data, or training trajectory in isolation, and are often locked to a single granularity level along the local-to-global spectrum. This fragmentation means explanations miss key interactions across these dimensions, and the existing theoretical foundation (the Exact Path Kernel) does not cover modern training regimes involving AdamW, decoupled weight decay, dynamic learning rates, and mini-batching.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261012/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

ExPLAIND is a post-hoc decomposition requiring no retraining: it consumes parameter checkpoints at each training step, the corresponding optimizer states (AdamW first/second moment estimates, per-step learning rates, weight decay coefficient), the training dataset, and test samples. The output is a multi-dimensional influence tensor indexed by training steps, individual parameters, training samples, test samples, and output dimensions, from which parameter-wise, data-wise, and step-wise attributions are obtained by summing along selected axes. A key structural property is exact additivity: the influence scores sum precisely to the model's prediction change, and the framework supports arbitrary granularity by choosing which tensor axes to retain versus aggregate.

## Method

The core contribution is a theoretical extension of the Exact Path Kernel to AdamW and gradient descent with momentum, proving that a model's final prediction decomposes exactly into dot products between a test feature map (the integral of parameter gradients along the linear interpolation between consecutive checkpoints) and a train feature map (a weighted accumulation of per-sample loss gradients scaled by AdamW's exponential moving averages and second-moment normalization), plus a separate decoupled weight-decay term. From this decomposition, the paper defines a tensor of influences ψ(s, θ^(i), x_k, x)_j whose entries are additive and sum to the prediction, with a parallel regularization tensor for the weight-decay contribution. Different explanations—parameter attribution, data attribution, step-wise training dynamics—are obtained by accumulating this tensor over different subsets of its axes, enabling a unified view across granularities and perspectives. The framework is validated by showing the EPK representation reproduces model predictions exactly (100% classification accuracy match at 100 integration steps across CNN, Transformer, and ResNet settings) and that the resulting parameter importance scores yield competitive training-free pruning, outperforming iterative magnitude-based pruning in output fidelity (KL divergence) across sparsity levels from 70% to 99%.

## Results

The EPK representation exactly reproduces model predictions with 100 integration steps: 100% classification accuracy matching and near-zero KL divergence (≤0.0) on ResNet-9/CIFAR-2, Transformer/MOD-113, and CNN/MNIST (Table 1). For parameter pruning on the CNN/MNIST model, ExPLAIND's training-free score-based pruning achieves test accuracy competitive with Li et al. (2017) across 70%–99% sparsity while producing consistently lower KL divergence, indicating closer replication of the original model. On EuroLLM-1.7B, the influence-score decomposition reproduces the mean loss change (−6.05×10⁻⁴) with an average error of only 4.46×10⁻⁸ (std 3.12×10⁻⁸) across 37 checkpoints.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For parameter pruning on CNN/MNIST, ExPLAIND's training-free score-based pruning matches or approaches the test accuracy of Li et al. (2017)'s iterative magnitude-based retraining across all sparsity levels from 70% to 99%, while achieving substantially lower KL divergence at every sparsity level, indicating it preserves the original model's output distribution more faithfully. For causal data attribution on CIFAR-2 and MNIST (Appendix G.1), ExPLAIND-derived data influence scores perform roughly on par with TracIn (Pruthi et al., 2020) but are slightly outperformed by TRAK (Park et al., 2023); notably, all baselines including influence functions perform poorly in these settings. The paper explicitly frames the pruning comparison as validation of score quality rather than a SOTA pruning claim, and the data-attribution results are presented as preliminary, with the authors noting that ExPLAIND's primary strength lies in unified parameter- and step-level attribution rather than standalone data attribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Exact Path Kernel (EPK) of Bell et al. (2023) from simple gradient descent to realistic training regimes (AdamW, weight decay, dynamic learning rates, mini-batching), building on the broader gradient-path-kernel lineage from Domingos (2020) and its connection to the Neural Tangent Kernel. It departs from prior interpretability work by unifying three traditionally separate attribution perspectives—model components, training data, and training dynamics—into a single tensor-of-influences framework, rather than treating them in isolation as influence functions, TracIn, or checkpoint-based probing do. The Grokking analysis refines Nanda et al.'s (2023) three-phase narrative, and the LLM pretraining study extends the framework to a 1.7B-parameter model, going beyond the small-scale settings typical of prior kernel-based attribution work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ExPLAIND provides the first theoretically grounded, additive decomposition of model predictions along parameters, data, and training steps simultaneously, offering a principled alternative to heuristic or approximate attribution methods. The Grokking case study reveals a previously unreported 'alignment phase' where outer layers converge around a representation pipeline, supported by causal layer-swapping ablations, while the EuroLLM study suggests a two-phase pretraining dynamic (MLP-dominated → attention-dominated) that could guide future training analyses. Key limitations include substantial computational cost (comparable to a full training run), the non-causal nature of the scores, and modest performance on data attribution benchmarks relative to TRAK. Open questions include whether the Grokking alignment findings generalize to larger models and more complex tasks, whether ExPLAIND can identify mechanistic circuits, and how to make the method efficient enough for routine use on frontier-scale models.
