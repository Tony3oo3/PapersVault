## Problem

Diffusion models store a large amount of information in their neural networks, but it is unclear how this information is allocated between reconstructing fine-grained perceptual details and correlating generated samples with conditioning signals like class labels. This paper asks: what is the information in a diffusion model, and why does classifier-free guidance (CFG) work effectively even when the mutual information between images and labels is tiny compared to the total neural entropy?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263687/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on pixel-space RGB images at 28×28 (MNIST), 32×32 (CIFAR-10), and 64×64 (Tiny ImageNet) paired with discrete class labels. A trained entropy-matching diffusion model (U-Net with self-attention, VP forward process) is used as a black box; the DAE variant additionally takes an encoder q_φ(z|x) that produces two latent codes Z_per and Z_sem, each conditioned at different diffusion time intervals (s ∈ (0,τ) and s ∈ [τ,T)). Outputs include neural entropy curves S_NN(s), mutual information estimates I(X;Y) and I(Z;Y) via the score-difference formula, t-SNE visualizations of the latents, and CFG-generated samples at varying guidance strengths w.

## Method

The paper introduces an entropy-matching parameterization of the reverse SDE (e_θ instead of score s_θ) that makes the network's information content directly measurable as neural entropy S_NN. It decomposes S_NN^X into per-component marginal terms plus the total correlation TC(X), showing that TC(X) diverges when X lives on a low-dimensional manifold (as images do) and is dominated by nearest-neighbor pixel correlations that encode perceptual texture. The key theoretical result is that I(X;Y) = S_NN^{X|Y} − S_NN^X, and the divergent manifold-resolving components of the conditional and unconditional scores cancel in the score-difference formula, so I(X;Y) is sourced from early diffusion times (semantic structure) rather than late times (perceptual detail). Empirically, a diffusion autoencoder with split conditioning (Z_per for s < τ, Z_sem for s > τ) confirms that Z_per contains little class-specific information while Z_sem shows clear digit clustering, and CFG is shown to amplify I(X;Y) by strengthening the score difference vector at early times before tapering off as details are filled in.

## Results

The paper's central quantitative finding is that in pixel-space image diffusion models, the ratio I(X;Y)/S_NN^X is on the order of 10⁻⁴ to 10⁻³, meaning the mutual information between images and class labels is vanishingly small compared to the total neural entropy. For Tiny ImageNet, S_NN^X is roughly an order of magnitude larger than for MNIST, while I(X;Y) merely doubles, confirming that higher resolution inflates the perceptual-detail budget (TC(X)) far faster than the semantic budget. The DAE experiments on MNIST show that at τ=0.1T, the perceptual latent Z_per exhibits no class-specific clustering in t-SNE, while the semantic latent Z_sem shows clear digit separation, and I(Z_per;Y) is negligible compared to I(Z_sem;Y). In the joint Gaussian 'flattening' experiment, S_NN^X diverges as the effective rank of X decreases, yet I(X;Y) remains finite and is correctly estimated by the diffusion model, confirming the cancellation of divergent score components in Eq. (12).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical and empirical analysis paper rather than a method-comparison study, so it does not benchmark a proposed algorithm against competing baselines. Instead, it provides an information-theoretic decomposition (S_NN^X = marginal shifts + TC(X), with I(X;Y) stored on top) that retroactively explains several known phenomena: (1) the effectiveness of classifier-free guidance, since the CFG vector s_cl = e_θ(·;y) − e_θ(·) is strongest at early t where scores are well-behaved and I(X;Y) is concentrated, and saturates as perceptual details dominate; (2) the success of latent diffusion models (Rombach et al., 2022; Vahdat et al., 2021), which offload the TC(X) burden to a decoder and let the diffusion model handle only the semantic I(X;Y) component; and (3) the blurriness observed in distilled few-step models, which the paper attributes to insufficient fidelity in capturing the information concentrated at s < τ (the high-curvature manifold-approach phase). The mutual information estimation formula (Eq. 12) generalizes and re-derives results from Franzese et al. (2024) and Kong et al. (2024) to arbitrary perturbation kernels, and the DAE framework extends Preechakul et al. (2022) by splitting conditioning into time-resolved perceptual and semantic latents.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the neural entropy framework of Premkumar (2025) by decomposing the total information stored in a conditional diffusion model into two orthogonal components: the total correlation TC(X) that resolves the data manifold, and the mutual information I(X;Y) that binds the data to its conditioning signal. It builds on the mutual information estimation formulas of Franzese et al. (2024) and Kong et al. (2024), providing an alternative derivation and showing that the divergent score components cancel in the I(X;Y) formula. The diffusion autoencoder (DAE) of Preechakul et al. (2022) is repurposed as a probing tool, with a novel time-split conditioning scheme that separates perceptual from semantic information. The paper is the first to formally connect TC(X), I(X;Y), and the intrinsic dimensionality of the data manifold, and to draw an explicit analogy to renormalization group flow.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The separability result (TC(X) >> I(X;Y), with a ratio of ~10⁻⁴–10⁻³ for images) provides a principled explanation for why latent-space diffusion models work (offloading the manifold-resolution burden to a decoder) and why few-step distilled models lose fine details (they fail to capture the information concentrated in the high-curvature final steps). The connection to RG theory suggests a deeper structural link between generative modeling and multiscale physics that could inform future architecture design. However, the empirical validation is limited to small-scale datasets (MNIST, CIFAR-10, Tiny ImageNet) and toy Gaussians; whether the quantitative separability ratios hold for large-scale text-to-image or video models remains an open question, as does how the framework extends to continuous conditioning signals like text embeddings.
