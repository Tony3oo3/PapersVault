## Problem

Diffusion LLMs (dLLMs) rely on fixed, predefined block schedules for semi-autoregressive inference, but this rigid partitioning is agnostic to semantic difficulty: it forces premature commitment to low-confidence positions inside the active block while delaying high-confidence positions just outside the boundary. This mismatch degrades both generation quality and inference efficiency, and existing semantic-aware alternatives (e.g., delimiter-based AdaBlock) lack generality across domains and model scales.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and produces a decoded text response of a predefined length L, initialized as a fully masked [MASK] sequence. Decoding proceeds iteratively over denoising steps, with a confidence threshold (default 0.9) controlling which masked positions are committed in parallel at each step. Key hyperparameters include the initial block size S_init (default 32), maximum block size S_max, and minimum prefix-window length ℓ_pmin (24 for LLaDA, 4 for Dream), all set without any model retraining.

## Method

DSB maintains an active block [s, e) whose boundaries advance at different rates each denoising step: the left boundary slides to the first remaining masked position (or the right edge if the block is fully resolved), while the right boundary expands to keep at least S_init unresolved tokens, capped at S_max. This dynamic resizing lets DSB decode high-confidence positions near the boundary early and defer low-confidence interior positions until richer context is available, all in a training-free manner. DSB Cache addresses the KV-cache instability introduced by sliding boundaries by maintaining a prefix window immediately before the active block, refreshing its KV states jointly with the active block at every step, caching all remaining positions for reuse, and performing periodic global refreshes every S_init decoded tokens to re-synchronize cached states.

## Results

On LLaDA-8B-Instruct with DSB Cache, DSB (greedy) achieves 80.29% accuracy and 99.61 TPS on GSM8K, surpassing the vanilla Top-1 baseline (77.79%, 14.94 TPS) and the Naive Block + Dual Cache baseline (77.40%, 92.26 TPS) in both quality and speed. On HumanEval, DSB (const.) reaches 43.00% accuracy at 82.02 TPS with DSB Cache, outperforming Naive + Dual Cache (37.80%, 76.61 TPS) by 5.2 accuracy points. Across all five benchmarks (GSM8K, MATH, HumanEval, MBPP, BBH) and four model variants, DSB with DSB Cache consistently improves the quality–speed trade-off, with the largest gains on GSM8K (+2.9 acc over Naive+Dual) and BBH (+4.1 acc).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Fast-dLLM's confidence-aware parallel decoding with Naive Block (Wu et al., 2025b), DSB (greedy) improves GSM8K accuracy from 77.26 to 78.54 (no cache) and from 77.40 to 80.29 (with cache), while also increasing TPS from 92.26 to 99.61 under the Dual/DSB Cache setting. Against AdaBlock-dLLM (Lu et al., 2025), DSB (const.) outperforms on HumanEval by 2.44 accuracy points (42.07 vs. 39.63) and 14.0 TPS (124.6 vs. 110.6) without cache, and DSB (greedy) with DSB Cache leads on GSM8K by 2.12 points (80.29 vs. 78.17) and 19.2 TPS (99.61 vs. 80.42) over AdaBlock + Dual Cache. Against dKV-Cache (Ma et al., 2025), DSB with DSB Cache achieves substantially higher throughput (99.61 vs. 59.36 TPS on GSM8K) while also improving accuracy (80.29 vs. 77.56). On the Dream models, gains are less consistent without cache but DSB (greedy) + DSB Cache still leads on GSM8K (73.08 vs. 71.87 for dKV) and HumanEval (59.76 vs. 56.10) for Dream-v0-Instruct-7B.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the block-diffusion LLM inference paradigm introduced by LLaDA (Nie et al., 2025) and Fast-dLLM (Wu et al., 2025b), which impose fixed, predefined blocks to enforce approximate left-to-right causality during parallel denoising. It departs from both the naive fixed-block schedule and the delimiter-driven AdaBlock-dLLM (Lu et al., 2025) by proposing a training-free sliding block whose size and boundary adapt online to the unmasking state, and from existing KV-cache schemes (dKV-Cache, dual caching) by identifying that they cause severe degradation under sliding blocks and introducing a prefix-window refresh mechanism. Unlike the concurrent WeDLM (Liu et al., 2025a), which requires a new training paradigm, DSB is entirely training-free and operates as a drop-in scheduling replacement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As block-diffusion LLMs move toward practical deployment (LLaDA 2.0 at 100B, Mercury, Gemini Diffusion), the quality-speed trade-off of the inference schedule becomes a first-order concern; DSB offers a simple, training-free lever to improve both simultaneously without retraining. The paper's key practical insight—that existing KV-cache schemes break under sliding blocks and require a dedicated prefix-window refresh—will likely inform future dLLM inference systems. Limitations include inconsistent gains on the Dream model without caching and sensitivity of S_max across architectures, and open questions remain about whether DSB-like schedules can be incorporated into pretraining for further gains and how the approach scales to much longer generations or 100B+ models.
