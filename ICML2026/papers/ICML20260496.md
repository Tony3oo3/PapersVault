## Problem

State-of-the-art confusion-matrix-based truth inference methods suffer from two coupled bottlenecks: (1) individual confusion matrices are underfit when annotators label only a small subset of tasks or when classes are severely imbalanced, and (2) a single confusion matrix per annotator cannot capture complex, class-dependent annotator behaviors, causing class-level collapse on extremely difficult tasks. Addressing both simultaneously is nontrivial because it demands robustness to data sparsity and sufficient expressiveness for heterogeneous annotator patterns at the same time.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sparse annotator-task label matrix (T tasks, W annotators, K classes) where each task is labeled by a variable subset of annotators, with sparsity ranging from Median(wj)=2 (CF) to 56 (Face) and class distributions from balanced (Face, 4-way uniform) to highly imbalanced (Val7, one class at 1%). It produces per-task posterior truth distributions (φ_ik) from which the ground-truth class is read off, along with learned class-specific prototype label distributions (v_ks) and annotator-specific prototype weights (π_jk) that provide interpretable characterizations of annotator behavior.

## Method

CPBCC replaces the per-annotator confusion matrix with a dual-pathway generative model: for each class k, S prototype label distributions v_ks ~ Dirichlet(a_ks) are learned by pooling data across all annotators, and each annotator w_j is characterized by a class-specific prototype weight vector π_jk ~ Dirichlet(β_jk) over those S prototypes. At annotation time, the annotator first draws a prototype assignment x_jiz_i from π_jz_i, then draws the label y_ij from the corresponding prototype distribution v_{z_i, x_jiz_i}. Inference proceeds via mean-field variational inference with closed-form coordinate updates for all variational parameters (ν, η, μ, θ, φ) using digamma functions, iterating until convergence. A minimax error bound (Theorem 4.1) shows that the class-specific prototype structure maintains larger inter-class KL divergences than a single confusion matrix, tightening the lower bound on achievable error and formally explaining the expressiveness advantage.

## Results

CPBCC achieves an average accuracy of 74.11% across 10 real-world datasets, improving over the strongest baseline DS (68.73%) by 5.38 percentage points. The largest gain is on the extremely difficult and class-imbalanced Val7 dataset, where CPBCC reaches 48.00% accuracy versus 22.00% for DS (a 26-point improvement). On the data-sparse CF dataset (median 2 annotations per task), CPBCC attains 90.33% accuracy, outperforming DS (79.67%) and IBCC (88.33%). In Macro-F1, CPBCC also leads with 69.87% average versus 66.41% for DS and 62.88% for GOVERN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CPBCC outperforms all 10 baselines on average accuracy (74.11%), with the largest margins over confusion-matrix-based methods: IBCC (66.64%, +7.47 pts), CBCC (65.76%, +8.35 pts), and EBCC (67.37%, +6.74 pts). Against the strongest single-parameter baseline BWA (68.42%), CPBCC gains 5.69 points, and against the representation-learning baseline GOVERN (68.17%), it gains 5.94 points. On the hardest dataset Val7 (7 classes, extreme imbalance), CPBCC achieves 48.00% while all baselines remain below 22%, with DS and TiReMGE tied at 22.00% and CBCC lowest at 13.00%. On the data-sparse CF dataset (median 2 labels/task), DS drops to 79.67% while CPBCC reaches 90.33%, a 10.66-point gap that highlights DS's sensitivity to sparsity. On Web, IBCC catastrophically degrades to 70.41% (collapsing to 39.7% on class 2) while CPBCC maintains 85.11%, demonstrating the expressiveness advantage of class-specific prototypes over a single confusion matrix. GOVERN is competitive on Web (89.66%) but suffers on Adult (69.52%) and Val7 (14.70%), confirming its vulnerability to class imbalance and sparsity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the confusion-matrix-based truth inference lineage (DS → IBCC → EBCC) by replacing the per-annotator confusion matrix with a dual-pathway mixture architecture: class-specific prototype annotation patterns shared across all annotators, plus annotator-specific Dirichlet weights over those prototypes. Conceptually, it applies the prototype/mixture-model idea (familiar from clustering and few-shot learning) to the annotator-reliability modeling problem, while retaining the Bayesian Dirichlet-prior structure and mean-field variational inference of IBCC. The theoretical analysis builds on the minimax error bound of Imamura et al. (2018) for DS, specializing it to show that class-specific prototypes tighten the KL-divergence-based lower bound relative to class-agnostic confusion matrices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses two concrete failure modes of state-of-the-art truth inference—underfitting under data sparsity/class imbalance and class-level collapse on extremely difficult tasks—that are prevalent in real-world crowdsourcing but underexplored in the literature. The 26% accuracy gain on Val7 (22%→48%) is striking, though on a very small (100-task) and extremely imbalanced dataset, so generalizability to larger sparse settings remains to be confirmed. The authors acknowledge that in data-abundant, class-balanced scenarios, representation-learning-based methods (GOVERN, TiReMGE) may still outperform, and future work on multi-model annotation aggregation is noted as an open direction.
