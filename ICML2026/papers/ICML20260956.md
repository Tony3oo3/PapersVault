## Problem

Standard theoretical analyses of Empirical Risk Minimization (ERM) for neural networks operate in the real-RAM model, where even shallow networks are ∃R-complete and NP membership is unknown. This algebraic framework diverges from actual digital computation, where all parameters and activations are stored as finite bit strings. The paper asks whether hardness results for real-valued ERM survive when parameters are restricted to polynomially encodable rationals, and reveals that the answer depends sharply on the activation function.

## Input / Output

An ERM_bit instance specifies a feedforward DAG with rational edge weights/biases, a finite rational dataset, a poly-time computable loss, and a threshold γ; the decision is whether a rational parameter vector θ with binary encoding length at most C₁·|I|^C₂ achieves loss ≤ γ. For the backpropagation variants, the input additionally fixes a rational parameter point θ and a distinguished edge e*, and the output is the sign (Backprop-Sign) or the j-th least-significant bit (Backprop-Bit) of the partial derivative ∂L/∂w_{e*}(θ). All intermediate rational values during forward/backward passes are exact, with bit-length counted as computational cost.

## Method

The core mechanism is a multiplication gadget: for any non-linear polynomial σ ∈ ℚ[T] of degree μ ≥ 2, there exist fixed rational coefficients λ₀,…,λ_μ such that xy = Σⱼ λⱼ(σ(x+y+j) − σ(x+j) − σ(y+j)), allowing a deep {σ, id} network to simulate the +, −, × gates of a straight-line program. The #P-hardness reduction from BitSLP to ERM_bit uses auxiliary training samples that force any low-loss solution to implement the target SLP circuit, combined with a bit-extraction loss on the output; the backpropagation hardness embeds the SLP output into a single gradient coordinate via a square loss on a one-dimensional extension. In contrast, for piecewise-linear activations like ReLU, each layer performs only affine operations and comparisons so bit-length grows additively rather than multiplicatively with depth, yielding NP-completeness for ERM_bit and polynomial-time exact backpropagation.

## Results

The paper establishes a sharp complexity dichotomy in the bit-model ERM setting: for any non-linear polynomial activation σ ∈ ℚ[T] with degree ≥ 2, deciding ERM_bit on deep networks is #P-hard (Theorem 1.1), deciding a specific bit in the gradient is #P-hard, and deciding the sign of a single gradient coordinate is not in BPP under the Constructive Univariate Radical Conjecture and NP ⊄ BPP (Theorem 1.3). In contrast, for piecewise-linear activations such as ReLU, ERM_bit is NP-complete and exact backpropagation runs in polynomial time (Theorem 1.4). The hardness results hold even with a fixed promise gap and with all intermediate node values uniformly bounded by a constant, ruling out magnitude-based explanations for the intractability.

| Deep ERM, poly σ | . | ∉ NP* |

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or dataset comparisons. The central 'comparison' is between two activation families under the same bit-model ERM formulation: polynomial activations (e.g., x², any non-linear σ ∈ ℚ[T]) yield #P-hard ERM_bit and intractable backpropagation, while piecewise-linear activations (ReLU, leaky-ReLU) yield NP-complete ERM_bit with polynomial-time exact backpropagation. The paper also contrasts the bit model with the classical real-RAM (BSS) model, where even shallow ReLU networks are ∃R-complete and NP membership is unknown; in the bit model, shallow ERM is already NP-complete because rational witnesses are polynomially checkable. The hardness for polynomial activations is shown to persist even under bounded intermediate norms and fixed promise gaps, distinguishing it from the real-RAM ∃R-hardness which concerns the existence of exact real solutions rather than the computational difficulty of extracting bits from succinctly represented arithmetic.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard real-RAM (BSS) model of ERM complexity, where even shallow networks are ∃R-complete (Abrahamsen et al. 2021; Bertschinger et al. 2023), by introducing a finite-precision bit-model formulation (ERM_bit) in which parameters must have polynomially bounded rational encodings. The hardness reductions build directly on the SLP complexity literature—specifically the #P-hardness of BitSLP (Allender et al. 2009) and the conditional PosSLP lower bound under the Constructive Univariate Radical Conjecture (Bürgisser & Jindal 2024; Dutta et al. 2022)—embedding these arithmetic-circuit hardness results into deep network training via a polynomial-activation multiplication gadget. While NP-hardness of training small networks in the bit model is known (Judd 1988; Blum & Rivest 1988), this paper is the first to show that depth combined with polynomial activations pushes the problem beyond NP to #P-hardness, and to contrast this sharply with the tractability of piecewise-linear activations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first complexity-theoretic explanation for the empirical preference of piecewise-linear activations in modern deep learning: under finite-precision computation, polynomial activations can encode computationally hard arithmetic in their intermediate values, making even basic gradient sign determination intractable, while ReLU-type activations keep bit-length growth additive per layer. Open questions include where smooth activations (sigmoid, tanh, GELU) and non-Lipschitz functions (1/x) fall in this taxonomy, whether the #P-hardness persists at constant input/output dimensions without regularization, and how closely the bit model captures the behavior of specific floating-point formats (FP16, BF16) used in practice. The authors acknowledge the model remains an intermediate abstraction—closer to practice than real-RAM but not modeling per-operation rounding or mixed-precision training—so further bridging work is needed.
