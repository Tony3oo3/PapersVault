## Problem

Existing token reduction methods (pruning and merging) cause catastrophic performance collapse when applied to structurally enhanced vision Mamba variants like VMamba, dropping accuracy by over 50% on ImageNet-1K. The paper identifies the root cause: these methods are spatially agnostic, flattening 2D token grids into 1D sequences and thereby violating the spatially sensitive scanning topology that SS2D-based selective scan mechanisms require for faithful state propagation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263106/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

STORM operates as a training-free, plug-and-play wrapper on top of existing reduction operators (ToMe, EViT) applied to standard Mamba backbones (VMamba, PlainMamba, LocalMamba) processing RGB images at 224×224 or higher resolutions. Reduction is applied at designated pruning anchor layers within the backbone, controlling the final feature map resolution (e.g., 9×9 down to 5×5 for VMamba, corresponding to 12%–36% overall token reduction). The output format is unchanged from the base model: class logits for classification, or detection/segmentation predictions for dense tasks.

## Method

STORM reformulates token reduction from a globally flattened 1D operation into a two-stage dimensionally decomposed process: first reducing tokens independently along each row (horizontal), then along each column (vertical), so the output remains a regular sub-grid aligned with SS2D scanning paths. A localized windowing mechanism further confines each reduction decision to non-overlapping windows of length L (default 5) along the scanning dimension, preventing cross-region merging that would disrupt local semantic coherence. The framework is architecture-agnostic and criterion-agnostic: any base reduction operator (similarity-based merging, importance-based pruning, or even random selection) can be plugged in unchanged, with STORM only restructuring the spatial organization of the reduction. A theoretical analysis bounds the cumulative state error in the SSM recurrence by a monotonic function of window size, showing that compact windows rigorously suppress error accumulation that causes the collapse observed with spatially agnostic methods.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K classification (training-free), STORM(ToMe) limits accuracy loss to just 1.3% on VMamba-B (82.6% vs. 83.9% baseline) and 1.0% on PlainMamba-L2 (80.6% vs. 81.6%), while standard ToMe suffers catastrophic drops of 48.2% and 4.9% respectively. On COCO 2017 object detection, STORM(ToMe) recovers VMamba-B AP^b from a collapsed 2.8 (ToMe) to 45.7 (baseline 49.2). Against Mamba-specific reduction methods, STORM(ToMe) achieves 82.0%/83.3% top-1 on VMamba-S/B, surpassing the best prior method QuarterMap (64.2%/69.9%) by 17.8/13.4 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STORM decisively outperforms all five baselines across every backbone and task. Against the two general-purpose ViT reduction methods, the gap is extreme: on VMamba-S, STORM(ToMe) retains 80.9% top-1 while ToMe collapses to 32.9% (a 48-point gap), and STORM(EViT) reaches 80.5% versus EViT's 23.0%. Among Mamba-specific methods (Table 3), STORM(ToMe) achieves 82.0%/83.3% on VMamba-S/B, beating QuarterMap (64.2%/69.9%) by 17.8/13.4 points, MTR (47.9%/49.4%) by 34.1/33.9 points, and HSA (46.2%/45.0%) by 35.8/38.3 points. On COCO detection, the advantage is even more dramatic: STORM(ToMe) lifts VMamba-B AP^b from 2.8 (ToMe) to 45.7, a 42.9-point recovery. The paper also shows that even random token selection within STORM's structured framework (69.1% at 36% reduction) far exceeds ToMe's guided selection (11.7%), confirming that spatial structure preservation—not the specific reduction criterion—is the dominant factor.

## Relation to Prior Work

STORM repurposes existing token reduction operators (ToMe, EViT) by wrapping them in a spatially structured framework, rather than proposing a new reduction criterion. It departs from the line of Mamba-specific reduction work (HSA, MTR, QuarterMap) that adapted pruning/merging to vanilla Mamba but remained spatially agnostic, and from ViT-era methods (ToMe, EViT, DynamicViT) that assume self-attention's permutation invariance. The key conceptual departure is reframing reduction as a dimension-wise operation on rows and columns within localized windows, directly mirroring the SS2D scanning geometry of VMamba and similar architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

STORM provides a training-free, plug-and-play solution to a problem that currently renders vision Mamba models impractical to compress: standard reduction collapses VMamba accuracy by ~50 points, while STORM limits the drop to 1–4 points across classification, detection, and segmentation. The finding that spatial structure preservation dominates token selection quality (random selection within STORM's grid outperforms ToMe's global matching by 57 points) suggests a broader principle for any architecture with sequential or spatial inductive biases. Open questions include whether the framework extends to video Mamba (3D scanning), whether the window-size hyperparameter can be learned or auto-tuned, and whether the modest throughput overhead of structured row-column operations becomes prohibitive at very high resolutions.
