## Problem

Softmax attention in long-context settings suffers from two coupled failures: probability mass diffuses onto irrelevant tokens (attention noise) as key correlations accumulate with sequence length, and the standard remedy of lowering softmax temperature to sharpen focus causes vanishing gradients that block learning. No existing mechanism simultaneously achieves precise retrieval and stable gradient flow at standard entropy levels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261001/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

LUCID consumes the same Q, K, V matrices as standard attention (head dimension d, sequence length N) but additionally computes a lower-triangular preconditioner P = (M ∘ exp(K_RN K_RN^T / √d − √d))^{-1} from RMS-normalized keys, solved via forward substitution (cuBLAS TRSM). The output is the standard attention output O = softmax(QK^T/√d + M̂) · P^{-1}V, retaining O(N²d) complexity with only 0–5.5% training overhead. During autoregressive decoding, the preconditioner is applied incrementally to the KV cache, adding a single matrix-vector multiplication per new token.

## Method

LUCID derives its preconditioner by recasting attention as gradient descent in an RKHS: standard attention corresponds to a linear objective with state-independent additive updates, while LUCID optimizes a quadratic retrieval-error objective that yields the delta rule (erase-then-write) in the infinite-dimensional RKHS induced by the exponential kernel. This produces the parallel-form preconditioner (M ∘ exp(KK^T))^{-1}, which decorrelates keys in feature space where ⟨φ(k_i), φ(k_j)⟩ = exp(k_i^T k_j) > 0 always holds—unlike finite-dimensional DeltaNet where orthogonal keys yield trivial correction. The softmax operates at standard temperature (preserving non-zero Jacobian gradients), while the preconditioner provides retrieval sharpness through deconvolution, decoupling precision from temperature. A Theorem proves gradient preservation: ∂o/∂q ≠ 0 under mild conditions, resolving the sharpness-learnability dilemma. A LUCID-PaTH variant integrates PaTH positional encoding for length extrapolation beyond the training context.

## Results

On the MNIAH task at 2048 sequence length, LUCID achieves 76.6/55.8/43.6% accuracy for 2/4/6 needles, outperforming Standard Attention (74.2/51.0/38.8%) by up to 12%. On LongBench and SCROLLS, LUCID-PaTH achieves the best F1 on 4 of 6 tasks, including Qasper (11.70 F1) and QMSum ROUGE-1 (14.83). On BABILong, LUCID-PaTH maintains stable accuracy (0.21–0.25) across 32K–128K contexts while Standard and Diff attention degrade to near zero at 128K. The attention hitrate on needle tokens improves by 56.6% (0.2845 vs. 0.1817 for Standard), with only 0–5.5% training overhead and ~1.3% inference overhead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LUCID consistently outperforms Standard Attention across all needle-in-a-haystack benchmarks: on MNIAH at 2048 length, LUCID gains +2.4/+4.8/+4.8 points for 2/4/6 needles, and the gap widens to +19.8–47.3% at longer fine-tuning lengths. Against Differential Transformer, LUCID wins on MNIAH (e.g., 55.8 vs. 35.4 for 4 needles) and on most LongBench/SCROLLS tasks, though Diff is competitive on 2WikiMQA (0.263 vs. 0.274). Linear attention variants (DeltaNet, GLA, GSA) are substantially outperformed, with DeltaNet achieving only 0.036 F1 on 2WikiMQA versus 0.274 for LUCID. PaTH Attention is the closest competitor, achieving 0.283 on 2WikiMQA and 14.62 on QMSum R-1, but LUCID-PaTH surpasses it on Qasper (+1.13 F1) and QMSum R-1 (+0.21). A compute-matched ablation (Standard +10% extra steps) confirms that LUCID's gains are architectural rather than from additional training compute, with LUCID outperforming the compute-matched baseline by ~20% on multi-key retrieval.

## Relation to Prior Work

LUCID Attention is derived by viewing softmax attention through the lens of gradient descent in a Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Space (RKHS), building directly on the kernel-method reformulation of Katharopoulos et al. (2020). The key conceptual move is recognizing that the quadratic (retrieval-error) objective yields the delta rule in RKHS, making LUCID a natural infinite-dimensional generalization of DeltaNet (Yang et al., 2024b), which operates in finite d-dimensional token space. Unlike Differential Transformer, which subtracts two attention maps to cancel noise, LUCID targets the root cause—correlated keys—by applying a preconditioner derived from exponentiated key-key similarities. The LUCID-PaTH variant further integrates PaTH positional encoding (Yang et al., 2025) for length extrapolation, positioning the method as a drop-in enhancement for full-attention layers in hybrid architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupling of retrieval sharpness from softmax temperature is a clean theoretical contribution: LUCID achieves near-one-hot retrieval precision while preserving non-vanishing gradients, resolving a fundamental tension in standard attention. Practically, the 0–5.5% training overhead and ~1.3% inference overhead make this a low-cost architectural upgrade for long-context LLMs, with consistent gains across BABILong, RULER, LongBench, and SCROLLS. A key limitation is that the triangular structure of the preconditioner (enabling efficient cuBLAS TRSM solves) is specific to causal settings; bidirectional models such as diffusion transformers would lose this structure, making the linear solve prohibitively expensive. Open questions include whether the approach scales favorably to much larger models (the paper tests up to ~2B parameters) and whether the preconditioner can be adapted for non-causal or bidirectional attention.
