## Problem

Large reasoning models (LRMs) produce chains of thought that are frequently redundant and uncorrelated with correctness, yet prior work shows shorter responses often outperform longer ones. The paper asks whether LRMs implicitly know when to stop thinking and demonstrates that this capability exists but is obscured by current pass@1 sampling paradigms that fail to select the model's own high-confidence, concise reasoning paths.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261752/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

SAGE takes a math problem query x and a reasoning model policy π_θ, and produces r completions, each consisting of a concise reasoning chain t_i and a greedily derived answer a_i. The algorithm operates at the reasoning-step level (segments delimited by newline boundaries) rather than the token level, maintaining m candidate sequences and expanding each by one full sampled reasoning step per iteration. Termination of a branch occurs immediately when a sampled step ends with , and the process halts once r completions are collected or a maximum step budget T_max is reached.

## Method

SAGE (Self-Aware Guided Efficient Reasoning) is a step-wise beam search that scores candidate sequences by cumulative log-probability Φ (the average of per-token log-probs) rather than per-token probability φ, and accepts a branch as a completion the moment  appears within a sampled step. This design exploits the empirical finding that high-Φ branches consistently rank  first in the candidate token set, so no additional tolerance threshold is needed. SAGE-RL integrates this into RLVR (GRPO or GSPO) by replacing 2 of 8 rollouts per group with SAGE(2,2) completions while keeping the remaining 6 as standard random samples; the unchanged advantage estimation then naturally shifts the policy toward the shorter, higher-reward reasoning patterns discovered by SAGE, improving both pass@1 accuracy and token efficiency without modifying the optimization objective.

## Results

SAGE-RL (SAGE-GRPO/SAGE-GSPO) achieves the best or second-best token efficiency (TE = Pass@1/LEN) across all six mathematical benchmarks while simultaneously improving pass@1 over the base model. On DS-1.5B, SAGE-GSPO reaches 85.2% pass@1 with 2,921 tokens on MATH-500 (TE=29.2×10⁻³, +71.6% over base) and 27.1% on AIME 2025 (TE=3.78×10⁻³, +111.1% over base). On DS-7B, SAGE-GRPO achieves 93.0% pass@1 with only 2,141 tokens on MATH-500 (TE=43.4×10⁻³, +83.1% over base) and 55.3% on AIME 2024 (TE=8.61×10⁻³, +87.6% over base). Training overhead is modest: SAGE-GRPO adds ~3.1 hours (30.2h vs 27.1h) and negligible peak memory (+2 MiB) compared to vanilla GRPO on DS-1.5B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAGE-RL is the only method that simultaneously improves both pass@1 and token efficiency over the base model across all four benchmarks; every other baseline trades accuracy for compression. On DS-1.5B MATH-500, AdaptThink achieves the highest TE (31.4) but at a 2.8% pass@1 drop (80.4 vs 83.2), while SAGE-GSPO gains +2.0% pass@1 (85.2) with TE of 29.2. On AIME 2025, SAGE-GSPO delivers the largest accuracy gain (+6.2% to 27.1%) with the shortest response length (7,167 tokens), outperforming GRPO (+4.2%, 8,227 tokens) and all compression baselines. On DS-7B, SAGE-GRPO outperforms GRPO-LEAD by 2.2% pass@1 on AIME 2024 (55.3 vs 53.1) while producing 601 fewer tokens (6,422 vs 7,023). On Qwen3-8B, SAGE-GSPO achieves 94.4% pass@1 with 2,753 tokens on MATH-500 (TE=34.3, +105.3% over base), surpassing GRPO's 94.6%/4,342 tokens (TE=22.2). The key differentiator is that SAGE-RL's gains come solely from modifying 2 of 8 rollout samples per group, requiring no reward function changes or additional training infrastructure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the RLVR framework (GRPO, GSPO) and the beam-search algorithm, but departs from prior efficient-reasoning methods (LC-R1, ThinkPrune, AdaptThink, Efficient Reasoning, GRPO-LEAD) that modify reward functions, impose length penalties, or perform offline distillation. Instead, SAGE-RL intervenes solely in the rollout phase by replacing a subset of random samples with SAGE-generated completions, leaving the optimization objective untouched. The conceptual departure is the empirical claim that the model's self-confidence (captured via cumulative log-probability Φ) already encodes when to stop, and that standard pass@1 sampling obscures this signal rather than the model lacking the capability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a minimal rollout modification (2 of 8 samples) yields consistent gains in both accuracy and token efficiency across four models and six benchmarks suggests a low-cost, broadly applicable lever for efficient reasoning that does not risk reward hacking or training instability. However, the 'implicit knowledge' framing is somewhat interpretive—the mechanism is essentially cumulative-probability-guided beam search—and the evaluation is confined to mathematical reasoning with models up to 8B parameters. Open questions include whether the convergence behavior observed with increasing exploration width reflects a true capability ceiling or an artifact of the scoring function, and whether the approach transfers to non-mathematical reasoning, code generation, or much larger models where the exploration cost becomes prohibitive.
