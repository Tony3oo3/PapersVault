## Problem

Standard graph search algorithms (DFS, BFS, Random Walk) and existing GraphRAG pipelines rely on explicit edge connectivity to traverse knowledge graphs, but real-world KGs extracted from natural language are inherently noisy, sparse, and fragmented—semantically identical entities appear as disconnected nodes (e.g., 'butterflies' vs. 'adult butterflies'), breaking reasoning trails that rigid traversal cannot bridge. The paper asks whether embeddings and LLMs can be used to dynamically navigate and repair graph structure during search, rather than treating the graph as immutable.

## Input / Output

INSES takes a natural-language query and a property knowledge graph stored in Neo4j, where each node carries textual attributes, a type label, and a d-dimensional embedding (bge-base-en-v1.5), and each edge carries a relation label. A vector database (Qdrant) backs the Naive RAG path used by the router. The system outputs a set of evidence triples relevant to the query plus a final answer; the router additionally produces a confidence score from Naive RAG (threshold 0.8) that gates escalation to the full graph search. All LLM calls use temperature 0.0 for deterministic parsing.

## Method

INSES couples two mechanisms in an iterative loop (max 6 iterations, motivated by small-world theory): (1) an LLM navigator that, at each step, selects the most promising triples from the current frontier's neighborhood and prunes irrelevant ones, and (2) a similarity-based expansion that retrieves top-k semantically proximate nodes via HNSW approximate nearest-neighbor search (cosine threshold τ_sim = 0.8), dynamically creating virtual edges to bridge gaps in the explicit graph. Unlike offline knowledge-graph completion or link prediction, these virtual edges exist only for the duration of the query and are immediately re-verified by the LLM navigator in the next iteration, forming a generate-then-verify cycle that prevents noise propagation. A lightweight router precedes the search: an LLM classifies whether the query requires ≥3-hop reasoning; simple queries are answered by Naive RAG with a self-assessed confidence score, and only low-confidence or complex queries escalate to INSES, keeping overall cost close to Text RAG while reserving graph reasoning for cases where it adds value. Per-iteration complexity is O(k·log|V|) because the LLM bounds the active frontier to a small constant k and HNSW provides logarithmic similarity lookup.

## Results

INSES+Router achieves the best performance across all three multi-hop QA benchmarks on both EM and LLM Judge metrics: 0.46/0.47 on MuSiQue, 0.67/0.71 on 2WikiMultiHopQA, and 0.68/0.80 on HotpotQA. On the MINE benchmark, INSES demonstrates robustness across heterogeneous KG structures, yielding mean accuracy improvements of 4.7%, 9.1%, and 27.5% over the native retrievers of KGGEN, GraphRAG, and OpenIE respectively. Ablation confirms that similarity-based expansion is the dominant accuracy contributor (e.g., +0.12 EM on MuSiQue), while the router adds further gains by routing 86% of HotpotQA queries to the cheaper Naive RAG path.

## Baselines & Comparisons

INSES+Router outperforms all nine baselines on every dataset and metric. Against the strongest graph-based competitor SiReRAG, INSES leads by +0.02 EM on MuSiQue (0.46 vs 0.44), +0.19 EM on 2Wiki (0.67 vs 0.48), and +0.07 EM on HotpotQA (0.68 vs 0.61). LightRAG is the closest text/graph hybrid rival, reaching 0.67 EM on HotpotQA (matching INSES) but trailing by 0.09 EM on 2Wiki and 0.08 EM on MuSiQue. Notably, GraphRAG underperforms even Naïve RAG on MuSiQue (0.26 vs 0.33 EM) and HotpotQA (0.47 vs 0.67 EM), confirming that community-summary-based retrieval is ill-suited to short factoid QA. IRCoT, despite its iterative retrieval design, scores only 0.37 EM on HotpotQA—well below Naïve RAG—suggesting error accumulation in its chain-of-thought loop. On the MINE robustness benchmark, INSES improves over KGGEN's native retriever by 4.7%, over GraphRAG by 9.1%, and over OpenIE by 27.5%, with the largest gain on the noisiest, densest graph (OpenIE: avg. 189 nodes, 265 edges), validating the LLM navigator's role in pruning noise while similarity expansion bridges gaps in sparse graphs.

## Relation to Prior Work

INSES sits at the intersection of LLM-guided graph traversal (Think-on-Graph, HippoRAG) and embedding-based similarity retrieval, but departs from both by making similarity expansion a dynamic, query-specific mechanism rather than a static offline graph-completion step. Unlike Knowledge Graph Completion or Link Prediction methods that permanently materialize missing edges, INSES generates virtual edges on-the-fly during search and immediately subjects them to LLM-based pruning, avoiding the semantic drift that static densification introduces. The lightweight router further distinguishes the work from prior GraphRAG systems by explicitly acknowledging that text-based RAG and graph-based RAG occupy different applicability regimes, rather than assuming graph structure is uniformly superior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical gap: real-world KGs extracted from text (via OpenIE, GraphRAG, KGGEN) are inherently noisy and sparse, and rigid edge-based traversal fails on them. The dynamic-vs-static completion distinction offers a principled way to exploit embedding similarity without corrupting graph precision, and the router provides a deployable cost-control mechanism. Open questions include scalability to very large KGs (beyond the 1,000-query test sets used), sensitivity to the similarity threshold and LLM backbone quality, and whether the framework generalizes beyond QA to other graph reasoning tasks such as link prediction or subgraph retrieval. The authors also flag LLM API instability and prompt brittleness as unresolved engineering challenges for production deployment.
