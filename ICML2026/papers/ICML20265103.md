## Problem

Standard Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) in diffusion-based counterfactual generation applies a single global guidance weight uniformly across all conditioning attributes, causing spurious amplification of attributes that should remain invariant under a causal intervention. This 'attribute amplification' violates the intended causal graph, degrades identity preservation, and undermines the axiomatic soundness of inferred counterfactuals, yet prior work had not identified the guidance mechanism itself as the root cause.

## Input / Output

The method takes an observed image (64×64 RGB for CelebA-HQ; 192×192 grayscale for EMBED and MIMIC-CXR), a vector of structured semantic parent attributes (e.g., sex, age, disease status), and a causal intervention specification (which attributes to set to which values). It produces a single counterfactual image via DDIM inversion (abduction), attribute-space intervention (action), and guided reverse diffusion (prediction). Conditioning is encoded through per-attribute MLP embedders (1→32→32 each) concatenated into a K×32 vector, enabling selective null-tokenization of individual attributes at inference time without any architectural change to the diffusion backbone.

## Method

FCFG factors the CFG guidance signal across M causally or semantically defined attribute groups, assigning a separate guidance weight ω_m to each group. The guided noise prediction is computed as ε_FCFG = ε_θ(x_t, t, ∅) + Σ_m ω_m (ε_θ(x_t, t, c̲^(m)) − ε_θ(x_t, t, ∅)), where c̲^(m) is a masked conditioning embedding that retains only group m's attributes and nulls all others. For counterfactual generation, the paper primarily uses a two-group partition (affected vs. invariant attributes) informed by the causal graph, though the framework supports arbitrary groupings up to per-attribute control (M=K). The approach is justified as gradient ascent on a factorized proxy posterior under a group-level conditional independence assumption, and requires no retraining—only the inference-time guidance rule changes. FCFG is model-agnostic and composes with advanced guidance variants such as CFG++ and APG by applying the same group-wise decomposition to their score formulations.

## Results

On CelebA-HQ under do(Smiling) at ω=3.0, FCFG achieves +13.1 target AUC change with −1.5 off-target change, compared to +12.3/+3.0 for global CFG and +12.9/+3.0 for SA-DCG. On MIMIC-CXR under do(finding), FCFG reaches +18.8 target AUC with only +0.6 off-target, versus +17.1/+5.1 (HVAE), +17.3/+5.0 (HVAE-soft), and +17.8/+5.3 (CFG). FCFG also consistently improves FID over global CFG (e.g., 19.6 vs 21.3 for do(Smiling) at ω=3.0) and reduces reversibility errors (MAE 0.177 vs 0.263, LPIPS 0.122 vs 0.155 on CelebA-HQ).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FCFG consistently outperforms all four baselines on the critical off-target amplification metric while matching or exceeding target intervention effectiveness. Against global CFG, FCFG reduces off-target AUC change from +3.0 to −1.5 on CelebA-HQ do(Smiling) at ω=3.0, and from +10.7 to +0.6 on MIMIC-CXR do(sex), while achieving equal or higher target AUC. Against SA-DCG (the strongest diffusion-based baseline), FCFG achieves a slightly higher target (+13.1 vs +12.9) while cutting off-target amplification from +3.0 to −1.5 and improving reversibility (MAE 0.177 vs 0.221, LPIPS 0.122 vs 0.145). Against HVAE and HVAE-soft on MIMIC-CXR, FCFG reduces off-target change by roughly 90% (from +5.0–9.0 to +0.6) while matching or exceeding target effectiveness (e.g., +18.8 vs +17.1–17.3 under do(finding)). FCFG also requires no additional training objective or heavier architecture, operating purely as an inference-time modification with only 60.5M parameters and 140s sampling time versus SA-DCG's 72.6M and 180s.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends classifier-free guidance (CFG; Ho & Salimans, 2022) for diffusion-based counterfactual image generation by identifying that the single global guidance weight indiscriminately amplifies all conditioning attributes, including those that should remain invariant under a causal intervention. It departs from prior counterfactual diffusion methods (Sanchez & Tsaftaris, 2022; Ribeiro et al., 2023; Rasal et al., 2025) that adopt CFG as a black-box improvement to intervention effectiveness, by isolating the guidance mechanism itself as a source of spurious attribute changes. The proposed FCFG is positioned as a lightweight, inference-time, model-agnostic extension that is compatible with recent guidance refinements such as CFG++ (Chung et al., 2025) and APG (Sadat et al., 2025), and is distinct from the previously identified attribute amplification caused by predictor-based fine-tuning (Xia et al., 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FCFG provides a simple, drop-in remedy for a concrete failure mode in a widely-used technique, requiring no retraining and only M+1 denoiser evaluations per timestep. Its practical impact is strongest for structured-attribute counterfactual generation in medical imaging and fairness settings, where preserving invariant attributes is critical for axiomatic soundness. Key limitations include the need for a pre-specified causal or semantic grouping and empirically chosen guidance weights; open directions include adaptive weight selection per timestep or input condition, extending to uncertain or learned groupings, and generalizing the factoring principle to other conditional generation tasks and flow-matching models.
