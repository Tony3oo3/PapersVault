## Problem

Scaling test-time compute for long chain-of-thought reasoning faces a fundamental trade-off: RL and preference optimization amortize long-horizon control into model weights at high training cost and can collapse trajectory diversity, while sampling-based inference yields rapidly diminishing returns as additional samples remain correlated. The paper addresses how to achieve robust, diverse trajectory coverage at inference time without any retraining, under strict memory constraints imposed by quadratic attention and unbounded KV cache growth.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text query (math or code problem) and a frozen LLM, consuming input in fixed-length chunks (S=512 tokens) with unit-norm state anchors extracted from top-layer hidden states at each chunk boundary via a fixed linear projection onto the unit sphere. Output is a full reasoning trajectory (chain-of-thought text) terminated by standard stopping criteria, with the solution answer embedded in the final chunk. Unusually, the method requires direct access to internal hidden states for anchor extraction and applies fixed low-rank residual injections (rank r=8) at every transformer layer during both candidate scoring rollouts and final chunk generation, making it applicable only to open-weight or locally deployed models.

## Method

TGR segments reasoning into chunks and performs search at chunk boundaries in a continuous latent space, keeping the backbone LLM entirely frozen. At each boundary, K candidate anchors are sampled via tangent-space perturbation around the previous state anchor on the unit sphere, then scored by a soft geometric objective combining (i) foresight value estimated from a single short Monte Carlo rollout, (ii) a bumpiness penalty on second-order hidden-state differences to enforce smooth transitions, and (iii) a hinge-based uniformity penalty that repels candidates whose cosine similarity to the previous anchor exceeds a threshold. The highest-scoring anchor is injected into the frozen model via fixed low-rank residual streams at every transformer layer to steer the next chunk's generation, and chunk-wise KV cache resets bound peak memory to O(S) by discarding earlier history and transmitting long-range state solely through the latent anchor. The paper further demonstrates that hard geometric feasibility filtering suffers from exponentially vanishing acceptance rates in high-dimensional latent spaces (below 1.5% at 14B scale), motivating the soft regularization approach as a scalable alternative.

## Results

On Qwen3-8B, TGR-Latent achieves AUC (area under the Pass@k curve) of 34.8 on AIME25, 39.5 on OlympiadBench, and 52.8 on OmniMath for math, and 73.5 on HumanEval, 56.3 on BigCodeBench, and 44.4 on LiveCodeBench for code, matching or exceeding the best RL-tuned baseline (Dele-SimKO) on all seven benchmarks while consuming 18% fewer total forward tokens (7.0×10³ vs. 8.5×10³). The headline gain is up to 13 points in AUC over the base model, with an inference overhead of only ~1.1–1.3×. On code generation, TGR-Latent leads at high k (e.g., Pass@128 of 84.2 on HumanEval vs. 83.5 for Dele-SimKO), reflecting its ability to explore multiple valid coding solutions in latent space.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TGR-Latent outperforms all six baselines on AUC across math and code benchmarks. Against the strongest RL-tuned baseline Dele-SimKO (Aghajohari et al., 2025), TGR-Latent ties on AIME25 AUC (34.8) and OmniMath AUC (52.8) but wins on OlympiadBench (39.5 vs. 38.8) and HumanEval (73.5 vs. 73.2), while using 18% fewer tokens (7.0 vs. 8.5 ×10³). Against LCoT-SimKO (Peng et al., 2025), TGR-Latent gains +4.3 AUC on AIME25 and +4.4 on OlympiadBench. Against LCoT-GRPO (Guo et al., 2025), gains are larger: +7.3 AUC on AIME25 and +8.0 on OlympiadBench. Against the training-free PSampling baseline (Karan & Du, 2025), TGR-Latent leads by +6.4 AUC on AIME25 and +6.9 on OlympiadBench. Against token-space structured search methods ToT (Yao et al., 2023) and GoT (Besta et al., 2024), TGR-Latent gains +6.3 and +5.1 AUC respectively on AIME25, confirming that latent-space search provides stronger coverage than discrete token-level branching under matched budgets.

## Relation to Prior Work

TGR carves new ground by repurposing geometric intuitions from training-time manifold-constrained architectures (mHC, attention-as-geodesic-transport) into a training-free inference-time scoring function, while borrowing the chunk-boundary state interface from Delethink's Markovian state transitions but replacing learned discrete transitions with continuous tangent-space sampling. It departs from token-space search methods (ToT, GoT, Self-Consistency) by operating in a continuous latent anchor space, and from RL/preference-optimization approaches (GRPO, SimKO) by externalizing geometric guidance as a soft penalty rather than amortizing it into weights. The chunk-wise KV cache reset yielding O(S) memory is a practical systems contribution that complements but is distinct from KV-compression techniques.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that hard geometric feasibility filtering becomes exponentially infeasible in high-dimensional latent spaces (acceptance <1.5% at 14B, requiring >66× compute inflation) provides a concrete theoretical justification for soft regularization over hard constraints in latent-space search, a distinction that generalizes beyond this specific framework. The O(S) memory bound via chunk-wise resets is practically important for long-horizon reasoning where unbounded KV caching is prohibitive. Key limitations include the requirement for hidden-state access (ruling out closed-box APIs), the reliance on low-depth rollouts that may be insufficient under delayed credit, and the observation that heavily RL-optimized models leave less room for inference-time search gains. Open questions include whether adaptive budgeting at high-uncertainty boundaries and agentic intent-anchoring can extend the approach beyond verifiable math/code tasks.
