## Problem

Existing preference-based hallucination mitigation for MLLMs relies on human annotation or external large models to label hallucinated responses, producing off-policy preference data that violates DPO's on-policy assumption and creating a scalability bottleneck. This paper asks whether a model's own internal logits can provide a hallucination-sensitive confidence signal sufficient to construct high-quality preference pairs without any external supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261535/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes an RGB image paired with a text question and produces a single hallucination-mitigated text response via standard autoregressive decoding. During training, each image-text prompt is expanded into N=10 diverse candidate responses (temperature 1.0, top-p 0.9, top-k 50), from which the model's cached token-level logits are used to compute a POS-aware fuzzy semantic uncertainty score; the lowest- and highest-uncertainty candidates form a preference pair. Fine-tuning uses LoRA (rank 256, α=512) with a DPO objective at β=0.1, applied to LLaVA-v1.5-7B/13B, Qwen2.5-VL-3B, Qwen3-VL-8B, and InternVL3.5-8B backbones.

## Method

RLSF-V derives a hallucination-sensitive uncertainty signal from the model's internal logits by interpreting them as a fuzzy membership function: logits are min-max normalized per position, and a local fuzzy entropy (De Luca–Termini) measures ambiguity among competing candidates. This signal is provably invariant under positive affine logit rescaling, unlike Shannon entropy which saturates as the logit scale grows. A lightweight POS tagger restricts aggregation to content-bearing tokens (nouns, proper nouns, numerals, adjectives, verbs, adverbs, adpositions), and a top-R pooling step further concentrates on the most uncertain positions, reflecting the observation that hallucinations localize to a small subset of tokens. The resulting sentence-level score ranks diverse candidates, and the extreme pair (lowest vs. highest uncertainty) is used as a self-generated preference for DPO fine-tuning, eliminating the need for any external evaluator or human annotation.

## Results

On LLaVA-v1.5-7B, RLSF-V (10k self-generated pairs) achieves CHAIR 2.4, HalRate 5.1, and Cog 0.3 on AMBER Generative, HallusionBench All Acc 47.4, Object HalBench Resp 2.9 / Ment 4.0, and AMBER Discriminative Acc 78.8 / F1 84.0. On LLaVA-v1.5-13B, it further improves to CHAIR 2.0, HalRate 4.4, HallusionBench 48.1, Object HalBench Resp 1.2 / Ment 1.7, and AMBER DIS Acc 84.8 / F1 87.6. RLSF-V also transfers to Qwen2.5-VL-3B, Qwen3-VL-8B, and InternVL3.5-8B, consistently reducing hallucination rates (e.g., HalRate drops from 62.0 to 48.1 on Qwen3-VL) while preserving general multimodal capabilities (7/10 general benchmarks improve).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RLSF-V (10k self-generated pairs, no external models) substantially outperforms all baselines on AMBER Generative: on 7B it achieves HalRate 5.1 vs. OPA-DPO (12.1), SENTINEL (14.8), RLAIF-V (15.6), and LLaVA-RLHF (41.4); on 13B it reaches HalRate 4.4 vs. OPA-DPO (13.4) and SENTINEL (12.0). On HallusionBench, RLSF-V leads with 47.4 (7B) and 48.1 (13B), surpassing symMPO (45.3/45.1) by ~2–3 points and OPA-DPO (42.2) by 5+ points. On Object HalBench, RLSF-V achieves the best Resp and Ment scores (2.9/4.0 on 7B; 1.2/1.7 on 13B), beating OPA-DPO (3.8/5.7; 4.9/7.3) and SENTINEL (3.0/5.0; 2.4/4.0). The one area where RLSF-V trails is AMBER Discriminative F1: symMPO (86.5/89.0) and OPA-DPO (85.1/87.5) edge out RLSF-V (84.0/87.6), though RLSF-V matches or exceeds them on Accuracy for 13B (84.8 vs. 84.5/84.1). Notably, RLSF-V uses only 10k preference pairs with zero external supervision, compared to 4.8k–122k pairs requiring GPT-4V, DeepSeek-V3, human annotators, or CLIP/Grounding-DINO in the baselines, and it transfers directly to Qwen2.5-VL, Qwen3-VL, and InternVL3.5 without pipeline redesign.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DPO training objective (Rafailov et al., 2023) and the fuzzy-set-theoretic entropy of De Luca & Termini (1993), repurposing the latter as a token-level uncertainty signal for hallucination assessment. It departs from the dominant RLHF/RLAIF paradigm, where preference pairs are constructed via human annotation or external large-model evaluators (GPT-4V, LLaVA-Next, etc.), by deriving all preference signals from the target model's own internal logits. The key conceptual move is interpreting min-max-normalized logits as fuzzy membership values and restricting aggregation to POS-tagged content tokens, positioning the method as a fully self-contained, reference-policy alternative to off-policy external feedback.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that hallucination-mitigation preference data can now be generated without any external model API, human annotation, or proprietary system, making the pipeline trivially scalable to new backbones (demonstrated on LLaVA-v1.5, Qwen2.5-VL, Qwen3-VL, and InternVL3.5). The scale-invariance property (Proposition 3.2) provides a clean theoretical justification for why fuzzy semantics is more robust than Shannon entropy under logit rescaling. Open questions include whether the approach extends to purely textual LLMs, how it interacts with external feedback signals, and whether the backbone-specific top-R hyperparameter can be made architecture-agnostic. The authors also note that relation-type hallucinations remain under-addressed, as prepositional tokens carry weaker hallucination signals.
