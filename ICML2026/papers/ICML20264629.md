## Problem

Online Newton methods offer robust uncertainty quantification for streaming data, but solving the Newton system exactly costs O(d³) per step. While unaccelerated sketch-and-project solvers reduce this to O(d²), it remains unclear whether adding Nesterov's acceleration to the sketching solver degrades or preserves the statistical efficiency of the resulting inference. This paper answers that question by establishing the full asymptotic normality and covariance structure of online Newton with Nesterov-accelerated sketching.

## Input / Output

At each outer iteration the method consumes one streaming data sample to form a gradient and Hessian estimate, then draws τ random sketching matrices (Kaczmarz or Gaussian, with s ≪ d columns) to approximately solve the Newton system in O(d²) time. The acceleration parameters (μ_t, ν_t) governing the momentum can be recomputed every step or refreshed periodically (e.g., every 500 or 1000 iterations) without affecting asymptotic validity. The output is the last iterate x_t as a point estimate, a fully online covariance estimator Σ̂_t updated via recursive running sums (no matrix inversions or stored data), and asymptotically valid confidence intervals of the form wᵀx_t ± z_{1−q/2}√(φ_t wᵀΣ̂_t w).

## Method

The method combines Hessian averaging (B_t = (1/t)ΣH_i) with a Nesterov-accelerated sketch-and-project solver (NASketch) that maintains a state-co-state pair (z_j, v_j) and applies momentum parameters (α, β, γ) derived from the sketching condition number. The key technical contribution is modeling the τ-step accelerated solver as a product of random asymmetric 2d×2d block matrices, then using the Cayley-Hamilton theorem and Kronecker-product diagonalization to reduce the spectral-radius analysis to a family of 2×2 matrices G_t(z) indexed by eigenvalues of the expected projection. This yields a uniform contraction bound ‖K_t‖ ≤ 2τ(1−√(μ_t/ν_t))^{τ−2}, which drives global almost-sure convergence and, together with a Lyapunov equation whose right-hand side Γ* depends explicitly on the sketching distribution, characterizes the limiting covariance Σ*. A fully online weighted-sample covariance estimator is then shown consistent at rate O(1/√(tφ_t)), requiring only fourth-moment conditions on gradient and Hessian noise.

## Results

On linear and logistic regression (d=40), the accelerated sketched Newton method (τ=5 or 10 sketching steps) achieves near-nominal 95% coverage (92–98%) across all covariance structures and sketching distributions, while SGD suffers severe undercoverage (62.5%–88.5% for linear regression). Mean absolute error is comparable to exact Newton (τ=∞), and in several settings (Toeplitz, Equi-Corr covariance) the inexact method actually achieves 8.8%–37.5% lower MAE than exact Newton. Average confidence-interval lengths are modestly longer than exact Newton (e.g., 2.55 vs. 2.55 for Identity linear; 1.03 vs. 0.61 for Equi-Corr linear), reflecting the additional sketching randomness. The method attains competitive results with as few as 5 sketching steps, versus 10+ required by unaccelerated sketched Newton.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the SGD baseline, the accelerated sketched Newton method dramatically improves coverage: SGD achieves only 62.5% and 68.5% empirical coverage on linear regression with Identity and Toeplitz covariance (vs. 95% nominal), while the proposed method reaches 93–98%. Against exact Newton (τ=∞), the inexact method matches or beats MAE in most settings (e.g., 22.37 vs. 29.87 for Toeplitz linear; 13.55 vs. 20.11 for Equi-Corr linear), at the cost of slightly longer confidence intervals (e.g., 2.17 vs. 1.70 for Toeplitz linear). Against unaccelerated sketched Newton (Kuang et al., 2025), the accelerated version achieves comparable or superior results with at most 5 sketching steps versus 10+ steps needed by the unaccelerated method, yielding substantial per-iteration computational savings. QQ plots (Figure 1) confirm that the limiting covariance of accelerated and unaccelerated sketching are empirically indistinguishable, indicating that Nesterov acceleration does not degrade statistical efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of online Newton methods with Hessian averaging (Na et al. 2022; Leluc & Portier 2023), Nesterov-accelerated sketch-and-project solvers (Gower et al. 2018; Dereziński et al. 2025), and the recent line of work on online inference for sketched Newton methods (Na & Mahoney 2025; Kuang et al. 2025). The key departure is extending the online inference framework from unaccelerated to Nesterov-accelerated sketching, which fundamentally changes the solver's dynamics from a symmetric projection to a random, time-varying asymmetric 2×2 block operator, requiring new analytical tools (Cayley-Hamilton, Kronecker products, spectral radius recursions) not present in prior sketching or inference literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper makes Newton-based online inference practical at O(d²) complexity—matching first-order SGD—while preserving near-optimal statistical efficiency, and shows that Nesterov acceleration reduces the required sketching steps from 10+ to at most 5 without degrading coverage. This opens the door to second-order inference in streaming settings where exact Newton is infeasible. Open questions include extension to non-convex objectives, adaptive or data-dependent stepsize schedules, and whether the acceleration benefit persists in much higher dimensions (d >> 40) where the current experiments are limited.
