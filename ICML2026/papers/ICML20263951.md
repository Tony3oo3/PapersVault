## Problem

The standard statistical theory of high-dimensional data predicts near-orthogonality and hypersphere concentration, a degenerate geometry incompatible with the Manifold Hypothesis that underpins most dimension-reduction and TDA methods. Existing HDLSS theory targets the p/n → ∞ regime, rendering it irrelevant for the common practical case p ≤ n. This paper asks when and how high-dimensionality can instead be beneficial, providing the first unified framework explaining how latent topological and geometric structure emerges from noisy high-dimensional observations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a point cloud of n vectors in R^p and operates entirely through pairwise dot-products, Euclidean distances, and cosine similarities — the same quantities feeding MDS, kernel PCA, Isomap, t-SNE, UMAP, and persistent homology. It produces (i) explicit concentration bounds on all pairwise quantities at rate √(log n / p_eff), (ii) persistence diagrams whose Betti numbers estimate the latent manifold's topology, (iii) a scalar effective-dimension estimate p_eff = tr(Σ̂)/‖Σ̂‖ from the sample covariance, and (iv) an isometry correlation coefficient ρ obtained by regressing k-NN-graph geodesic distances in the data space against those in a known latent space. The isometry step additionally requires ground-truth physical location coordinates to define the latent metric space Z_n.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a generalized Hanson-Wright inequality bounding |X^T A X' − E[X^T A X']| for possibly dependent vectors X, X' (with pairs (X_j, X'_j) independent across j) and sub-Gaussian entries, yielding dot-product concentration at rate √(log n / p_eff) where p_eff = tr(Σ)/‖Σ‖ is the effective dimension. The paper embeds this in a random function model Y_i = Σ^{1/2}(z_i)X(z_i) + μ(z_i) + σE_i, distinguishing three notions of dimensionality: effective dimension p_eff (total variability across features), correlation rank r (number of Mercer eigenfunctions in the kernel E[Y^nf(z)·Y^nf(z')]), and latent intrinsic dimension d (manifold structure of Z). Under mean-square continuity of the signal, the GHW inequality implies the observed point cloud is Gromov-Hausdorff close to the latent feature map image M_n whenever p_eff ∈ ω(log n), and the bottleneck distance between persistence diagrams of p^{-1/2}Y_n and p^{-1/2}M vanishes when p_eff/log n → ∞ — establishing TDA consistency without requiring p ≫ n. Isometry between Z and M is then assessed empirically by constructing k-NN graphs on both Y_n and Z_n, computing shortest-path distances, and measuring the linear correlation ρ between them, with a positive intercept accounting for additive noise σ².

## Results

The paper establishes that under sub-Gaussian assumptions, dot-products concentrate at rate √(log n / p_eff), showing that p_eff ∈ ω(log n) suffices for faithful recovery of latent topology—far weaker than the traditional p/n → ∞ (HDLSS) regime. On the grid cell dataset (p=149, n=15,000), the estimated effective dimension is p̂_eff = 12.67, exceeding log n ≈ 9.62, placing it in the regime where the theory predicts geometric concentration. Persistent homology recovers Betti numbers H₀=1, H₁=2, H₂=1 (torus topology), and the isometry test between the neural manifold and the flat torus model of physical space yields a correlation coefficient ρ = 0.75, providing the first empirical evidence of global isometric structure in grid cell activity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is primarily a theoretical paper, so there are no direct method-vs-method benchmark comparisons. The key contrast is with the HDLSS literature (Hall et al. 2005; Ahn et al. 2007; Shen et al. 2016), which requires p/n → ∞ (or p/n² → ∞ under finite fourth moments) for dot-product concentration; the present work shows that under sub-Gaussianity, the much weaker condition p_eff ∈ ω(log n) suffices, yielding the faster √(log n/p_eff) rate versus the n/√p rate under mere finite fourth moments. In the grid cell application, the paper extends Gardner et al. (2022), who established toroidal topology via persistent homology, by additionally providing evidence of global isometry (ρ = 0.75) between the neural manifold and a flat torus model of physical space—a geometrically stronger claim. The authors position their work as complementary to conformal isometry analyses by Xu et al. (2024) and Schoyen et al. (2025), which assume conformal isometry as a modelling premise and derive predictions; here, the statistical framework explains when and why such geometric structure is recoverable from noisy finite-dimensional data without requiring p ≫ n.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the Hanson-Wright concentration inequality (Rudelson & Vershynin, 2013) from the quadratic form X^T A X to the bilinear form X^T A X' with dependent vectors, and applies it within the random function model of Whiteley et al. (2026) to derive dot-product concentration bounds governed by a new quantity, effective dimension p_eff. It departs from the HDLSS literature (Hall et al., 2005; Ahn et al., 2007) by showing that the classical p >> n regime is unnecessary: under sub-Gaussian assumptions, p_eff >> log n suffices for latent topology to emerge. The TDA consistency result combines existing bounds from Chazal et al. (2013) and Ivanov et al. (2016) with the new concentration estimates, and the neuroscience application extends Gardner et al. (2022) by testing a stronger geometric (isometric) relationship beyond topological (homeomorphic) recovery.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reframes high-dimensionality from a statistical impediment to a potential signal amplifier, providing practitioners with a concrete diagnostic (p_eff vs. log n) for when pairwise distances in high-dimensional data faithfully reflect latent geometry. The grid cell application offers the first empirical evidence that neural population activity is isometric to a flat torus model of physical space, though the moderate correlation (ρ = 0.75) and acknowledged estimation error in cohomological decoding temper the strength of this claim. Open questions include whether the sub-Gaussian assumption is essential in practice (the paper shows degradation under heavier tails), whether parametric extensions (e.g., Gaussian process latent variable models) yield sharper inference, and whether the isometry test generalizes beyond the single-experiment grid cell setting.
