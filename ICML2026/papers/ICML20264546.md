## Problem

Surgical video datasets are heavily imbalanced, with rare actions, tools, and steps severely under-represented, limiting the robustness of downstream surgical understanding models. Existing augmentation strategies (class sampling, pixel-level transforms) increase sample frequency but not diversity, and prior surgical video diffusion models either lack controllability or require dense per-frame segmentation masks that are rarely available in practice. The paper tackles the question of how to generate diverse, temporally coherent synthetic surgical videos of under-represented classes using only the sparse conditional signals (a few frames or masks) typically present in surgical datasets.

## Input / Output

The model accepts text prompts, sparse segmentation masks (collected at 1 fps), or 3–5 randomly sampled RGB frames from a 16-frame clip as conditional signals, which are channel-wise concatenated with a binary presence mask before entering the sparse visual encoder. It produces 4-second synthetic surgical videos at 512×512 resolution (16 frames), substantially higher than the 256×256 of SurV-Gen/SparseCtrl or 128×128 of Endora. Optical flow maps are precomputed offline with RAFT and consumed only during training as an additional denoising target; they are not required at inference, keeping the generation pipeline lightweight.

## Method

SurgiFlowVid builds on the SurV-Gen two-stage pipeline (Stable Diffusion image pre-training followed by temporal transformer insertion) and introduces two key modifications. First, a dual-prediction diffusion U-Net jointly denoises RGB latents and optical-flow latents (encoded as RGB via normalized magnitude and angle), with a combined noise-prediction loss weighted by λ_p=0.65; this forces the model to learn explicit motion structure from limited samples rather than relying solely on cross-frame attention. Second, a lightweight sparse visual encoder (SVE), adapted from SparseCtrl, propagates sparse conditional frames across time via spatial and temporal layers while the U-Net backbone remains frozen, enabling controllable generation without dense annotations. Spatial layers are frozen throughout the video training stages, and only the temporal attention blocks, dual-prediction input/output layers, and SVE are optimized, keeping the trainable parameter count at ~437–456 M and fitting within 1–2 GPUs.

## Results

On SAR-RARP50 surgical action recognition, SurgiFlowVid with segmentation-mask conditioning raises the mean Jaccard index from 0.46 (real data only) to 0.52, with under-represented classes improving by 12% (A1: 0.32→0.44), 8% (A5: 0.10→0.18), and 10% (A7: 0.32→0.42). On GraSP, the mean mAP increases from 0.47 to 0.52 with RGB-frame conditioning. For surgical tool presence detection on SAR-RARP50, the mean Dice score improves from 0.69 to 0.79, and on AutoLaparo laparoscope motion prediction, SurgiFlowVid achieves the highest F1 in both online and offline settings, surpassing all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SurgiFlowVid consistently outperforms all four baselines across the three primary tasks. Against Endora (unconditional), SurgiFlowVid wins on every metric: on SAR-RARP50 the mean Jaccard rises from 0.45 to 0.52, and on GraSP the mAP improves from 0.45 to 0.52, confirming that controllability is essential for targeted augmentation. Against SurV-Gen with rejection sampling (the strongest conditional baseline), SurgiFlowVid gains +4 points in mean Jaccard on SAR-RARP50 (0.48→0.52) and +3 points in mAP on GraSP (0.49→0.52), with the largest per-class gains on under-represented actions (e.g., A5: 0.14→0.18). Against SparseCtrl, SurgiFlowVid achieves a 5-point Dice improvement on tool presence detection (0.74→0.79) and a 3-point Jaccard gain on action recognition, attributed to the dual-prediction module's explicit motion supervision. Against VideoMix/TubeMix augmentation baselines, SurgiFlowVid's 0.52 mean Jaccard substantially exceeds their 0.45, demonstrating that generative augmentation provides genuine data diversity rather than mere pixel-level perturbation.

## Relation to Prior Work

SurgiFlowVid builds directly on SurV-Gen (Venkatesh et al., 2025a), a two-stage surgical video diffusion model that fine-tunes Stable Diffusion with temporal transformer blocks, and on SparseCtrl (Guo et al., 2024), which propagates sparse conditional signals across time in video diffusion. The key departure is the introduction of a dual-prediction U-Net that jointly denoises RGB frames and optical flow maps during training, providing a motion inductive bias that eliminates the need for SurV-Gen's rejection sampling strategy. Unlike SurgSora, which injects optical flow from off-the-shelf estimators as an external conditioning signal, SurgiFlowVid explicitly trains the generative model to predict flow alongside RGB, and unlike FlowVid (video-to-video translation on 10M videos), it generates new conditional videos from sparse inputs in a lightweight, healthcare-constrained setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Data imbalance in surgical video datasets is a persistent practical barrier to deploying robust surgical scene understanding models, and this paper demonstrates that a lightweight dual-prediction diffusion framework (437M params, 1–2 GPUs) can yield consistent 10–20% gains on under-represented classes across three datasets and three downstream tasks without requiring dense per-frame annotations. The finding that SurV-Gen's gains stem primarily from rejection sampling rather than the generative model itself, and that SurgiFlowVid's optical flow inductive bias achieves comparable or better results without that filtering step, is a useful conceptual insight for the surgical video generation community. Open questions include extending to longer-horizon tasks (surgical phase recognition), incorporating tool kinematics to reduce position drift observed with sparse masks, and whether the approach generalizes to even rarer or single-instance classes.
