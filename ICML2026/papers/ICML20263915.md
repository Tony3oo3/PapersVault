## Problem

Standard RL assumes the agent observes the environment state after every action, but in practice sensing is costly, unreliable, or action-dependent (e.g., invasive diagnostics in medicine, high-frequency data in trading). General POMDPs are computationally and statistically intractable, and existing work on delayed or missing observations treats the observation gaps as exogenous. This paper identifies and formalizes the missing case where the probability of observing the next state is endogenous—determined by the agent's chosen action—creating a coupled optimization over actions and information acquisition.

## Input / Output

The agent receives full state observations and cumulative undiscounted rewards only at stochastic data-bursts (triggered with action-dependent probability β(a)); between bursts it maintains an augmented state consisting of the last observed state concatenated with the sequence of actions taken since. The algorithm ATST-LSVI-UCB takes as input an ε-admissible approximation ψ̂ of a 2d-dimensional action-sequence feature map and a discount factor γ, and outputs a burst-dependent policy that commits to an entire (potentially infinite) action-sequence at each data-burst, executing it open-loop until the next observation. The regret is measured against the optimal augmented policy operating under the same observation constraints, yielding Õ(√(d³K(1−γ)⁻³)) over K episodes with geometrically distributed horizons.

## Method

The key structural insight is that data-bursts act as renewal points: the augmented state space X = S × A^{<N} resets to a singleton in S at each observation, so trajectories decompose into independent intervals between bursts. This motivates an action-sequence reformulation where the agent's decision at each burst is to commit to an infinite sequence a ∈ A^N to execute until the next observation. Under the linear MDP assumption, the paper constructs a 2d-dimensional feature map ψ(s, a) via discounted sums of action-matrix products M_a, such that the action-sequence value function K^π(s, a) = ⟨ψ(s,a), v_{1,2}^π⟩ is linear in ψ for every policy π. The feature map is estimated plug-in from ridge-regression estimates of the action-matrices and empirical means of the observation probabilities, achieving Õ(d³/ε²(1−γ)²) sample complexity in a reward-free manner. ATST-LSVI-UCB then performs backward value iteration over these action-sequences with UCB exploration bonuses, reducing the infinite-dimensional maximization over A^N to a finite-dimensional optimization over the compact image of ψ̂ in R^{2d}.

## Results

The main theoretical result is a regret bound of Õ(√(d³K(1−γ)⁻³)) for ATST-LSVI-UCB in episodic linear ATST-MDPs with geometric horizons, matching the known optimal rate for fully observable linear MDPs (Jin et al., 2020b). The action-sequence feature map estimation requires Õ(d³/(ε²(1−γ)²)) exploratory samples, independent of |S|. Empirically, on RiverSwim all observation probabilities β* ∈ {0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.5} converge to the optimal policy, with smaller β* converging faster due to longer open-loop commitments encouraging exploration. On RiverBalance, all settings improve and plateau, with larger β* achieving higher average reward because state-dependent corrections toward the center are more effective with frequent observations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare ATST-LSVI-UCB against competing RL algorithms empirically; the evaluation focuses on how observation frequency (β*) interacts with task structure across two environments. The key theoretical comparison is that the regret rate Õ(√(d³K(1−γ)⁻³)) matches the optimal rate for standard linear MDPs with full observability (Jin et al., 2020b), demonstrating that the action-triggered observation constraint does not degrade the fundamental learning rate under the linear MDP assumption. The empirical results highlight a qualitative distinction: in RiverSwim (where the optimal policy is open-loop), sparse observations (β*=0.05) accelerate convergence, while in RiverBalance (where closed-loop state-dependent corrections are needed), frequent observations (β*=0.5) yield the highest plateau reward. The paper positions ATST-MDPs as subsuming ACNO-MDPs (Nam et al., 2021) and intermittent feedback models (Hausknecht & Stone, 2015) as special cases, but does not run head-to-head comparisons with methods from those settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper introduces ATST-MDPs, a new formalization that sits at the intersection of POMDPs, delayed-observation MDPs (Walsh et al., 2009), and paid-observation models (Nam et al., 2021), but explicitly departs from all of them by making the observation probability an endogenous function of the chosen action. The augmented-state construction follows the delayed-observation MDP literature, while the learning algorithm adapts LSVI-UCB from the linear MDP framework (Jin et al., 2020b). The key conceptual departure from general POMDPs is the renewal structure: because data-bursts fully reveal the state, the problem decomposes into intervals between observations, enabling an action-sequence reformulation that avoids the intractability of belief-space planning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides the first provably efficient learning algorithm for a setting where the agent must strategically trade off information acquisition against task reward, with a regret bound matching fully observable linear MDPs. The empirical contrast between RiverSwim (sparse observations accelerate learning) and RiverBalance (frequent observations are necessary) highlights a non-trivial interaction between observation frequency and task structure that is absent from prior analyses. Significant open questions remain: the algorithm requires an optimization oracle over infinite action sequences, the feature map must be estimated in a separate reward-free phase, and the empirical validation is limited to small tabular environments, leaving scalability to continuous or high-dimensional settings unaddressed.
