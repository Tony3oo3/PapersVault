## Problem

While mean-field theory establishes an order-to-chaos phase diagram for randomly initialized deep networks, the effect of dropout on this picture was previously shown only to destroy the critical fixed point, leaving no well-defined correlation length or scaling structure. This paper asks what replaces the destroyed critical point and whether the resulting theory can yield a concrete, testable scheduling rule for allocating dropout across depth.

## Input / Output

The theoretical framework takes an activation function (smooth such as tanh/GELU, or kinked such as ReLU), a total dropout budget expressed as a mean field h̄, and network depth L as inputs, and produces a depth-dependent keep-probability profile ρ_ℓ, a set of critical exponents (β, δ, γ, ν_t, ν_ρ, θ_rel, α) characterizing the universality class, and a predicted depth correlation length ξ_eff. Empirically, the method is applied to MLPs and Vision Transformers on CIFAR-10/100 (32×32 RGB images), where the practical output is a front-loaded dropout schedule that reduces test cross-entropy by 18–35% (MLPs) or 4–6% (ViTs) over constant dropout at the same budget, with no additional computational cost.

## Method

The paper extends the deep information propagation mean-field recursion to include multiplicative inverted dropout, showing that independent Bernoulli masks per input shift the correlation map at perfect alignment by a field h = 1 − F̄_ρ(1), making c* = 1 no longer a fixed point for any ρ < 1. Expanding the deformed map around the displaced fixed point yields a Landau equation of state h = (g_ρ/2)m² − tm for smooth activations (analytic Taylor expansion) versus h = κm^{3/2} − tm for kinked activations (branch-point non-analyticity from the ReLU kink), producing distinct critical exponents and a universal two-parameter scaling collapse in detuning t and field h. The correlation length ξ_eff^{-1} ∝ (1/L)Σ_ℓ √(t² + 2g_ρ h_ℓ) is minimized under a fixed budget constraint, and because h^{1/2} is concave, Jensen's inequality forces the optimal allocation to saturate the box constraint into a step-like profile. A regularization-reach argument then breaks the permutation degeneracy: the cumulative downstream exposure D_ℓ ∝ h_ℓ ξ_c(1 − e^{−(L−ℓ)/ξ_c}) is maximized by filling the earliest layers first, yielding the front-loaded step schedule validated in experiments.

## Results

Front-loaded dropout schedules reduce final cross-entropy loss by 18–35% over constant dropout at fixed budget in MLP settings (e.g., +35.4% loss reduction for ReLU MLPs at h̄=0.1, +29.8% for GELU MLPs), and by 4–6% in Vision Transformer settings on CIFAR-10/100. Accuracy gains are consistent but smaller: +2.04 pp (5.0% relative) for ReLU MLPs and +0.66 pp (1.4% relative) for ViT on CIFAR-100 (p < 0.05). The theoretical framework predicts distinct critical exponents for smooth (tanh, GELU) versus kinked (ReLU) activations, with the smooth class showing ν_ρ = 1/2 and the kinked class ν_ρ = 1/3 for the dropout-field correlation length.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline throughout is constant (uniform) dropout at the same total budget, which is the standard practice in most deep learning pipelines. Front-loaded schedules (step-early, big-step-1/3, linear-decreasing) consistently outperform constant dropout across all tested architectures and activation classes: the largest gap is +35.4% loss reduction for ReLU MLPs, while the smallest is +4.2% for ViT on CIFAR-100. The advantage is largest in the MLP regime where mean-field assumptions are most valid (width ≫ depth, moderate dropout) and weakens in ViTs, consistent with the theory's prediction that the regularization-reach advantage scales with the ratio L/ξ_c. Gains persist across h̄-sweeps, large-width sweeps, and both smooth (GELU) and kinked (ReLU) activation classes, confirming the universality-class prediction. The paper does not compare against other scheduling strategies (e.g., curriculum dropout over training time or stochastic depth), positioning its depth-profile schedules as complementary rather than competing with those temporal or structural mechanisms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the mean-field signal-propagation framework of Poole et al. (2016) and Schoenholz et al. (2017) by incorporating dropout as a relevant perturbation of the critical fixed point. While Schoenholz et al. (2017) showed that dropout destroys the order-to-chaos critical point, this work demonstrates that a nontrivial c*<1 fixed point survives, enabling a full Landau equation of state and the extraction of critical exponents. The smooth-versus-kinked universality class distinction builds on the qualitative tanh/ReLU dichotomy observed in Hayou et al. (2019) and Yang & Schoenholz (2017), but here the scaling exponents are derived and the underlying Hermite spectral mechanism is identified. The representation-group coarse-graining language of Roberts et al. (2022) and Bahri et al. (2024b) provides the organizational framework for treating dropout as a depth-dependent dynamical field.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper yields a concrete, no-cost design rule—front-loaded dropout schedules—that reduces test loss by 18–35% in MLPs and 4–6% in Vision Transformers at fixed budget, with the theoretical mechanism (regularization reach) explaining why early layers are optimal. The universality class distinction between smooth and kinked activations, with different critical exponents and scaling collapses, provides a principled framework for understanding how activation choice interacts with regularization depth. Key limitations include the initialization-only scope (no training-time dynamics), the restriction to controlled wide-network regimes where mean-field assumptions hold, and the absence of a full backward critical theory or CNN/ResNet extensions. Open questions include whether the front-loading principle survives in large-scale training with feature learning, and how the theory extends to attention mechanisms and residual architectures.
