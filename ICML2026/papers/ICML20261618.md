## Problem

Existing motion-controlled video generation models treat user-provided trajectories as ground truth and rigidly execute them, producing unnatural or physically implausible results when inputs are sparse, imprecise, or causally incomplete. The critical gap is the absence of visual-context reasoning: models fail to infer secondary consequences such as chain reactions, coupled mechanical motion, or collision dynamics that the user implies but does not explicitly specify.

## Input / Output

The system takes an input RGB image, a set of sparse 2D point trajectories (normalized to [0,1] coordinates), and an optional text prompt describing coarse user intent (e.g., 'cut', 'pull away', 'rotate'). The VLM reasoning stage outputs a refined set of trajectories (corrected user paths plus hallucinated secondary paths for reacting objects or static anchors) and a detailed narrative prompt describing cause-and-effect dynamics. The video generator then consumes the input image, the motion volume (spatiotemporal Gaussian heatmaps projected through a 3D VAE encoder), and T5 text embeddings to synthesize the final video. Trajectories are represented as N point paths of length L placed as 2D Gaussian peaks in a zero-initialized volume M ∈ R^{L×H×W×3}, with σ scaled to video resolution.

## Method

MotiMotion is a reasoning-then-generation pipeline with two components. First, a training-free VLM (Gemini 3.1 Pro) interprets the image, trajectory rendering, and prompt to produce refined user trajectories (adjusting temporal spacing for physical forces), secondary trajectories for reacting objects, and a narrative prompt capturing causal consequences; this can be iterated by having the VLM inspect generated videos and propose corrections. Second, a confidence-aware motion-controlled generator built on Wan 2.2 I2V-A14B (flow-matching DiT with 3D VAE) injects trajectories as spatiotemporal Gaussian heatmaps encoded into the latent space and concatenated along the channel dimension with the noisy latent and reference image latent. The key mechanism is confidence modulation: each trajectory receives a score s ∈ [0,1] that scales its Gaussian kernel amplitude (G' = s·G), so high-confidence plans are followed tightly while low-confidence inputs degrade gracefully to the model's generative prior. During training, low-confidence trajectories are synthetically degraded via affine perturbation, temporal linearization, and Savitzky-Golay smoothing to teach the model the correlation between confidence and input fidelity. Fine-tuning updates only the expanded tokenization layer and self-attention layers (5K steps on OpenVid for motion control, then 3K steps with 50% degraded trajectories for confidence learning).

## Results

On MotiBench, MotiMotion achieves the highest VLM-judged scores across all three metrics: physical realism (0.302), photorealism (0.520), and semantic consistency (0.665), outperforming MagicMotion (0.157/0.550/0.343) and Wan-Move (0.218/0.483/0.511). In the 2AFC preference test, MotiMotion is preferred 78.0% of the time overall against MagicMotion and 73.8% against Wan-Move, with a striking 97.9% human preference rate over MagicMotion. On motion-following metrics (FVD/EPE) evaluated with 4 and 16 trajectories, the underlying motion-controlled generator achieves FVD of 724.4/712.8, substantially lower than Motion Prompting (1207.7/1322.0), DragAnything (1497.2/1282.8), and Image Conductor (1735.0/1838.9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MotiMotion is compared against two primary baselines on MotiBench: MagicMotion (Li et al., 2025a), which uses dense-to-sparse trajectory guidance, and Wan-Move (Chu et al., 2025), which employs latent trajectory guidance. MotiMotion wins decisively on physical realism (0.302 vs. 0.157 for MagicMotion, 0.218 for Wan-Move) and semantic consistency (0.665 vs. 0.343 and 0.511), though MagicMotion retains a slight edge on photorealism (0.550 vs. 0.520). In the 2AFC human study, MotiMotion is preferred 97.9% of the time over MagicMotion and 81.4% over Wan-Move, with the largest gaps in interaction plausibility (80.8% and 75.0% respectively). For motion-following fidelity (Table 7, appendix), the paper's motion-controlled generator achieves the best FVD (724.4 for 4 trajectories, 712.8 for 16) and competitive EPE (8.28, 7.89), outperforming Motion Prompting, DragAnything, and Image Conductor on FVD while matching or slightly trailing on EPE. Cross-method verification (Table 3) further shows that applying the VLM reasoning module to MagicMotion and Wan-Move improves their physical realism by 27% and 30% respectively, confirming the reasoning component's generality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends motion-controlled video generation (Wan-Move, MagicMotion, Motion Prompting) by inserting a training-free VLM reasoning stage that refines sparse user trajectories and hallucinates causally consistent secondary motions before the diffusion generator runs. It aligns with the modular video-generation paradigm (VideoDirectorGPT, Plan-X) where an LLM/VLM plans semantics before a renderer executes, but applies this specifically to the motion-control setting where prior work treats user trajectories as rigid ground truth. The confidence-aware control mechanism, which modulates trajectory-following strength via signal scaling and trajectory degradation during training, departs from prior physics-aware generation work (PhysCtrl, Force Prompting) that bakes physical constraints into training or inference rather than adapting to input quality at test time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: current motion-controlled generators faithfully execute sparse user trajectories but fail to trigger implicit causal consequences (dominoes falling, clock hands coupling), producing physically implausible videos. The confidence-aware control offers a reusable mechanism for any trajectory-conditioned generator to handle imperfect inputs gracefully, and the cross-method verification (Table 3) suggests the VLM reasoning module is broadly applicable. Key limitations include the small 62-sample MotiBench, ~50-second VLM inference latency, and dependence on external APIs (Gemini 3.1 Pro), which constrain real-time deployment. Open questions include whether reasoning can be distilled into the generator itself, whether smaller local VLMs suffice, and whether the approach generalizes to longer-horizon multi-event sequences.
