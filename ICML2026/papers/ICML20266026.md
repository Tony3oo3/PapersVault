## Problem

Rank-1 LoRA adaptation in multimodal large language models is notoriously unstable, yet prior work attributes this failure primarily to limited expressive capacity. This paper shows the instability is geometric: in high-dimensional activation spaces, a randomly initialized rank-1 direction is almost surely orthogonal to the dominant cross-modal (modality-gap) axis, suppressing early gradient flow by a factor of O(d^{-1/2}) and causing optimization collapse. The gap addressed is the lack of a principled, data-aware initialization that aligns the single update direction with the geometry-salient axis that actually drives early optimization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a frozen pretrained MLLM (primarily BLIP-2 OPT-2.7B), a small unlabeled calibration set of weakly paired image-text examples (default 256 samples; no precise alignment or supervision required), and a target LoRA rank. It produces layer-specific initialized LoRA matrices (B, A) where B is a unit vector aligned with the per-layer modality-gap direction and A is zero, ensuring ΔW = 0 at initialization. The entire gap estimation requires a single forward pass over the calibration set, incurring negligible overhead relative to fine-tuning.

## Method

Gap-Init is a training-free, geometry-aware initialization that replaces the random Gaussian initialization of LoRA's B matrix with a direction estimated from activation-space data. For each transformer layer ℓ, it computes the mean per-sample difference between aligned visual and textual hidden states over the calibration set to obtain a layer-specific gap vector g^(ℓ), optionally smoothed via a neighborhood-consistency procedure (SPOT-Gap). The LoRA B matrix at that layer is then set to g^(ℓ)/‖g^(ℓ)‖₂ while A is set to zero, so the pretrained function is preserved but the gradient with respect to A is projected directly onto the modality-gap axis from the first step. Unlike weight-space methods (PiSSA, DoRA) that infer subspaces from pretrained weight geometry, Gap-Init operates entirely in data-induced activation space, making it complementary to those approaches. An optional GG-Safe layer-selection strategy uses the SPOT-Gap score to pick the top-k most misaligned layers, though the paper shows Gap-Init is robust enough to work well under naive selection.

## Results

On COCO Captioning (Karpathy split), Gap-Init at rank 1 achieves 140.59 CIDEr and 41.87 BLEU-4, matching or slightly exceeding rank-8 Standard LoRA (138.49 CIDEr, 40.63 BLEU-4) while using 8× fewer trainable parameters. On zero-shot Flickr30k transfer, Gap-Init reaches 79.60 CIDEr, outperforming both rank-1 Standard LoRA (63.00) and rank-8 Standard LoRA (78.35). Multi-seed evaluation (5 seeds) shows Gap-Init achieves 140.57 ± 1.44 CIDEr versus 135.37 ± 7.10 for Standard LoRA at rank 1, confirming substantially reduced optimization variance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Standard LoRA at rank 1, Gap-Init wins decisively on every metric: CIDEr improves from 98.08 to 140.59 (+42.51) on COCO, VQA2 accuracy jumps from 15.58% to 57.23%, and POPE F1 rises from 50.1 to 92.4, indicating that random rank-1 initialization causes near-catastrophic optimization failure. Against PiSSA (SVD-based weight-space initialization) at rank 1, Gap-Init wins on COCO CIDEr (140.59 vs. 130.42), Flickr30k zero-shot CIDEr (79.60 vs. 76.00), and POPE F1 (92.4 vs. 86.9), but loses on in-domain VQA2 (57.23 vs. 63.35) and OK-VQA (21.03 vs. 26.56), suggesting weight-space spectral initialization retains an edge for in-domain task accuracy while activation-space gap alignment better supports transfer and hallucination robustness. Against rank-8 Standard LoRA, Gap-Init at rank 1 matches or slightly exceeds on COCO (140.59 vs. 138.49 CIDEr) and Flickr30k (79.60 vs. 78.35 CIDEr) with 8× fewer parameters, and against DoRA at rank 1 it achieves marginally higher CIDEr (140.59 vs. 140.51) and BLEU-4 (41.81 vs. 41.55). Overall, Gap-Init's primary advantage is stability: the standard deviation across seeds drops from 7.10 to 1.44 CIDEr, eliminating the 'lottery' behavior of random rank-1 initialization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PEFT initialization line (LoRA → PiSSA → DoRA) by shifting the geometric analysis from weight space to activation space. While PiSSA and DoRA select subspaces via SVD or magnitude-direction decomposition of pretrained weights, Gap-Init estimates a data-induced modality-gap direction from a small calibration set and aligns the rank-1 LoRA update with it. The conceptual departure is identifying the modality gap's translation component as the dominant optimization bottleneck at rank 1, building on the anisotropy and modality-gap literature (Liang et al., 2022; Ethayarajh, 2019) but repurposing it as an initialization signal rather than a diagnostic.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that initialization direction can matter as much as rank at the extreme low-rank limit offers a practical, parameter-free remedy for rank-1 instability that is immediately deployable. However, the effect is most pronounced on BLIP-2 OPT-2.7B; cross-backbone results (Qwen2-VL-7B, Gemma3-4B) show smaller gains, and the DoRA(r=1) comparison reveals only a marginal CIDEr difference (140.51 vs. 140.59), suggesting the advantage narrows as alternative initialization strategies mature. Open questions include whether the translation-dominant gap structure generalizes to non-contrastive or video-based multimodal models, and whether the benefit persists as rank increases beyond 1.
