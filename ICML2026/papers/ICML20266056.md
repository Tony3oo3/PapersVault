## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models can reproduce near-verbatim copies of training images when prompted with the original text, raising privacy and copyright concerns. Existing mitigation approaches are either unrealistic (training-time interventions on pretrained models), computationally expensive and fragile (finetuning-based unlearning), or rely on hard-coded attention redistribution rules (e.g., always suppressing the EOT token) that fail for template memorization. The paper seeks a dynamic, per-prompt, inference-time method that surgically reduces memorization without degrading image quality.

## Input / Output

At each denoising step, the method consumes a text prompt embedding and the current noisy latent, performing three U-Net evaluations (unconditional, memorized-conditional, and spike-attenuated-conditional) batched into a single forward pass to keep overhead close to standard inference. The output is a modified noise prediction that steers the denoising trajectory away from the memorized training image while preserving prompt alignment, ultimately yielding a generated RGB image. No weight modification is required; the entire intervention is applied on-the-fly during the forward pass.

## Method

GUARD modifies classifier-free guidance by adding a repulsion term (negative weight on the memorized prompt's noise prediction) and an attraction term (positive weight on a 'positive target' noise prediction), forming a contrastive guidance formula. The concrete instantiation, CA-in-GUARD, defines the positive target via a statistical cross-attention spike detector: for each prompt, it computes per-token maximum attention mass across spatial queries, derives Z-scores, and flags tokens exceeding a threshold τ as memorization-critical positions S(p). Cross-attention logits at these positions are then multiplicatively attenuated by a factor α before the softmax, applied selectively in the down and mid U-Net blocks at every denoising step. This makes the intervention dynamic (adapting to each prompt's unique attention pattern), surgical (leaving non-spike tokens untouched), and robust across both verbatim and template memorization types.

## Results

On SD v1.4 verbatim memorization, CA-in-GUARD achieves the lowest SSCD of 0.079 (vs. 0.113 for the best prior method, Ren et al.) while also attaining the best FID of 158.1. On SD v2.0 template memorization, CA-in-GUARD reduces SSCD to 0.193, a 26% improvement over the best prior method (Wen et al. at 0.260), with FID of 212.7. CA attenuation alone (the r=0 special case) already outperforms Ren et al. across all three settings, e.g., dropping SSCD from 0.356 to 0.193 on SD v2.0 template memorization. The method also maintains competitive CLIP scores (0.266–0.282) and incurs only ~1.4× the inference time of the no-mitigation baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CA-in-GUARD outperforms Ren et al. (2024), the most closely related prior CA-attenuation method, on SSCD across all three settings (e.g., 0.079 vs. 0.113 on SD v1.4 verbatim; 0.193 vs. 0.356 on SD v2.0 template), with the gap being most dramatic for template memorization on SD v2.0. Against Wen et al. (2024), CA-in-GUARD achieves lower SSCD on SD v1.4 verbatim (0.079 vs. 0.115) and SD v2.0 template (0.193 vs. 0.260), though Wen et al. retains a slight FID edge on SD v2.0 template (188.9 vs. 212.7). Han et al. (2025) is the only baseline that beats CA-in-GUARD on SSCD in one setting (SD v1.4 template: 0.479 vs. 0.517), but is outperformed on the other two settings and on FID overall. RTA (Somepalli et al., 2023) is consistently the weakest mitigation method, achieving SSCD values 2–4× higher than CA-in-GUARD across all settings. The paper's key claim is robustness: no single prior method dominates across all settings, whereas CA-in-GUARD achieves the best or near-best SSCD in every configuration tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classifier-free guidance (CFG) framework from Stable Diffusion (Rombach et al., 2022) by introducing a contrastive 'attraction-repulsion' modification (GUARD) that steers denoising away from memorized content while steering toward a safe alternative. Its core technical contribution builds directly on Ren et al. (ECCV 2024), which identified that memorization is linked to peaky cross-attention on summary tokens (EOT, padding) and proposed hard-coded attention redistribution; here, the authors replace that fixed strategy with a dynamic, per-prompt statistical spike detector that identifies memorization-critical positions on-the-fly. The work also builds on the trigger-token discovery of Somepalli et al. (2023) and Wen et al. (2024), but departs from them by showing that no single fixed token position or category reliably captures memorization triggers across both verbatim and template memorization types.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GUARD/CA-in-GUARD offers a practical, weight-agnostic inference-time solution for T2I model operators who cannot retrain or finetune, and its robustness across SD v1.4, v2.0, and v3.0 (including a transformer-based backbone) makes it broadly applicable. A key open limitation is that inference-time mitigation does not erase memorized information from weights, leaving white-box extraction attacks unaddressed; the authors explicitly call for integrating their mechanistic insights into training-time or finetuning-time methods. The finding that template memorization is structurally harder to mitigate than verbatim memorization—and that prior methods optimized for one type actively underperform on the other—reframes the evaluation landscape and suggests future work should treat these as distinct sub-problems.
