## Problem

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems for non-parametric continual learning store verbatim document chunks and force the LLM to re-process the same passages at every query, incurring high test-time compute while injecting irrelevant context that increases hallucination. Existing structure-augmented methods (RAPTOR, GraphRAG, HippoRAG) improve retrieval but still feed long passages to the answer model and lack reliable abstention when evidence is insufficient. The paper identifies three under-addressed criteria for NPCL—supported performance, inference-time efficiency, and reliable abstention—and shows no existing system satisfies all three simultaneously.

## Input / Output

At write time, each source document is independently encoded into a Generative Semantic Workspace (GSW): a small directed graph of entity nodes (with role/state annotations), verb-phrase/event nodes, and question-answer pairs as labeled edges, indexed via a sparse BM25 entity index and a dense vector index over all QA pairs. At read time, a single natural-language query is decomposed into an ordered sequence of atomic sub-questions by one LLM call; the system then performs purely non-parametric beam-search retrieval over the GSW store and passes only the deduplicated QA pairs (typically ~200–450 tokens) to the answer model, which outputs either a grounded answer or the canonical abstention token 'N/A'.

## Method

PANINI's core contribution is a two-phase design that trades write-time compute for read-time efficiency. At write time, each document is converted into a GSW—an entity- and event-aware network where QA pairs form directed edges from verb-phrase nodes to entity nodes, making the set of entities associated with each event explicit in short atomic units. At read time, Reasoning Inference Chain Retrieval (RICR) decomposes the query once into parallel sequences of single-hop sub-questions, then iteratively resolves each hop via a dual-search subroutine (BM25 entity lookup + dense QA-pair retrieval, merged and reranked by a cross-encoder). Multiple candidate chains are maintained in parallel (beam width B=5), scored by the geometric mean of per-hop relevance scores to penalize any weak link, and pruned at each step to retain only the top-B chains with unique current answers. The final deduplicated QA pairs from surviving chains are passed to the answer model, which generates a response grounded exclusively in these compact factual units rather than raw document passages.

## Results

PANINI achieves the highest average F1 of 56.1 across six QA benchmarks (NQ, PopQA, MuSiQue, 2Wiki, HotpotQA, LV-Eval), outperforming the strongest structure-augmented baseline HippoRAG2 (53.3) and dense retrieval with reranking (50.5). Gains are most pronounced on multi-hop tasks: PANINI scores 52.3 on MuSiQue and 72.4 on 2Wiki versus 49.3 and 69.7 for HippoRAG2. Simultaneously, PANINI uses only 320 average inference-time tokens—2.2× fewer than chunk-based retrieval (705 tokens) and 25–33× fewer than structure-augmented (GraphRAG: 8,122) and agentic (IRCoT: 10,745) methods. On the curated Platinum reliability splits, PANINI achieves the best answerable accuracy (79.9 F1) while maintaining strong refusal accuracy (72.8%), breaking the typical accuracy–abstention trade-off observed in baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PANINI outperforms all seven baselines on average F1, with the largest margins on multi-hop benchmarks: +3.0 over HippoRAG2 on MuSiQue (52.3 vs 49.3), +2.7 on 2Wiki (72.4 vs 69.7), and +12.8 over dense retrieval with reranking on MuSiQue (52.3 vs 43.7). On simple QA (NQ, PopQA), PANINI leads on NQ (67.4 vs 61.4 for Qwen3+rerank) but trails slightly on PopQA (57.6 vs 59.9), suggesting structured memory is most beneficial when multi-hop composition is required. In terms of efficiency, PANINI uses 320 tokens on average versus 705 for chunk-based methods (2.2× fewer), 8,122 for GraphRAG (25× fewer), and 10,745 for IRCoT (33× fewer), while agentic Search-R1 uses 2,458 tokens (7.7× more). On the Platinum reliability evaluation, PANINI achieves 79.9 answerable F1 and 72.8 refusal accuracy, dominating HippoRAG2 (72.5 / 58.5) and dense retrieval (61.7 / 65.2); BM25 achieves higher refusal accuracy (79.1) but at the cost of very low answerable accuracy (36.4), confirming the trade-off that PANINI breaks. The paper also shows that swapping Search-R1's chunk retrieval with PANINI's GSW index (without retraining) improves performance on 4 of 6 datasets, indicating the structured memory is transferable across retrieval paradigms.

## Relation to Prior Work

PANINI adapts the Generative Semantic Workspace (GSW) representation from Rajesh et al. (2025) and Holur et al. (2024)—originally designed for episodic memory over continuous narratives—into a fact-centric, document-local structure for multi-hop QA. It departs from the dominant NPCL paradigm (RAG and its variants like HippoRAG, GraphRAG, RAPTOR) by investing equally in what is written to memory (structured entity-event QA networks) and how it is read (beam-search chain-following via RICR), rather than focusing primarily on retrieval improvements. Unlike agentic systems (IRCoT, Search-R1) that interleave multiple LLM calls with retrieval, PANINI performs a single decomposition call and then executes all subsequent hops as non-parametric retrieval and scoring, shifting computational cost from read time to a one-time write-time indexing step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2–30× reduction in inference-time tokens while simultaneously improving accuracy and abstention reliability is practically significant for deployment of retrieval-augmented systems at scale, and the Platinum benchmark (separating answerable from unanswerable questions) fills a gap in evaluating hallucination control under missing evidence. The finding that structured write-time representations can match or exceed agentic multi-step retrieval without iterative LLM calls suggests a viable path to reducing serving costs for multi-hop QA. Open questions include handling knowledge conflicts and entity evolution over time, reducing write-time cost with smaller open-source models, and extending the framework to narrative-heavy or multimodal settings where cross-document reconciliation becomes more critical.
