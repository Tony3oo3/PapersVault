## Problem

Existing spatio-semantic inference methods for object localization assume access to a complete global image and rely on dense pixel-wise computation or task-specific regression heads, incurring high computational cost and conflicting with the local, viewpoint-dependent perception of embodied AI agents. No lightweight framework explicitly structures latent space geometry to jointly encode object identity and fixation-centered spatial proximity from a small set of local observations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261971/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 256×256 grayscale or RGB face image and a fixed set of N_F=9 periodic fixation points (spaced 64 pixels apart). At each fixation point, two multiscale patches (27×27 and 108×108) are extracted, resized, and channel-concatenated into a single 2C×27×27 observation. The output is a set of landmark coordinates normalized to (−1,1) for all |C^tot| classes, optionally refined by a per-landmark 27×27 heatmap that constrains the offset to at most 1–2 pixels.

## Method

WorldComp2D comprises three components: a proximity-dependent encoder (PdEnc), a localizer (Loc), and an optional auxiliary localizer (AuxLoc). PdEnc is a six-layer CNN that maps each fixation-centered multiscale observation to a 256-dimensional L2-normalized latent vector, trained with a proximity-weighted contrastive loss (PWConLoss) in which pairwise similarity weights w_ij = 1 + exp(−0.025·d_ij) continuously modulate the contrastive objective by real-world distance to the fixation point, replacing binary positive/negative pairs. Loc is a two-layer MLP that jointly aggregates N_F paired (z, F) representations to predict all landmark coordinates in a single forward pass, avoiding pixel-wise computation. AuxLoc is a depthwise-separable CNN that takes a class-conditioned local patch at each predicted coordinate and outputs a refinement heatmap, providing a flexible accuracy–speed trade-off. The key insight is that continuous proximity weighting in the contrastive objective explicitly encodes spatial structure into latent geometry, enabling distance-preserving embeddings that support efficient localization from limited local views.

## Results

On AFLW, WorldComp2D achieves NME_Diag of 1.52±0.01, outperforming LAB (1.85), AWing (1.53), ODN (1.63), and HRNet (1.57), though trailing PIP (1.42), RHT-R (1.18), and PoPos (1.43). On COFW and 300W, WorldComp2D reports NME_IO of 5.16±0.05 and 5.06±0.01 respectively, which are higher than regression-based SoTA methods (e.g., HIH 3.21 on COFW, RHT-R 2.82 on 300W). The framework uses only 2.4M parameters and 256.9–546.8M FLOPs, achieving a 4.0× parameter reduction and 2.2× FLOP reduction compared to the lightweight PoPos (9.7M params, 1.2G FLOPs), while running at 78–164 FPS on CPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On AFLW, WorldComp2D (NME_Diag 1.52) outperforms LAB (1.85), AWing (1.53), ODN (1.63), and HRNet (1.57), but is surpassed by PIP (1.42), RHT-R (1.18), and PoPos (1.43). On COFW and 300W, WorldComp2D trails all regression-based SoTA methods by a substantial margin (e.g., 5.16 vs. 3.21 for HIH on COFW; 5.06 vs. 2.82 for RHT-R on 300W), which the authors attribute to the absence of direct supervised regression training. The primary advantage is computational efficiency: WorldComp2D uses 2.4M parameters and <550M FLOPs, a 4.0× parameter reduction and 2.2× FLOP reduction over the next-lightest competitor PoPos (9.7M params, 1.2G FLOPs), while maintaining >78 FPS on CPU. In cross-dataset transfer (300W→COFW-68), WorldComp2D degrades by only 20.2% in NME, compared to 27–32% for LAB, ODN, and PIP, suggesting more robust spatio-semantic representations. The optional AuxLoc module adds ~100M FLOPs and improves NME by up to 4.6% (COFW), but removing it nearly doubles FPS (e.g., 344 vs. 138 on COFW), offering a flexible accuracy–speed trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

WorldComp2D departs from two main lines of prior work. First, it contrasts with embodied AI world models (Ha & Schmidhuber, 2018; Hafner et al., 2019) that shape latent spaces through temporal prediction objectives, instead explicitly encoding spatial proximity and object identity as intrinsic geometric properties of the representation. Second, it moves away from conventional binary contrastive learning by introducing a continuous proximity-weighted affinity that modulates pairwise interactions based on real-world distance to the fixation point. For object localization, the framework replaces full-image pixel-wise processing (anchor-based detectors, heatmap regression) with a fixation-centered local observation pipeline, repurposing a simple CNN encoder and MLP localizer to operate on compact latent vectors rather than dense feature maps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicitly structured latent geometry can substitute for exhaustive full-image computation in localization tasks, achieving 4× parameter and 2.2× FLOPs reductions over the lightest SoTA while maintaining real-time CPU inference. This opens a path toward resource-constrained embodied perception where agents must reason from sparse local views rather than global images. However, validation is limited to 2D facial landmarks with a fixed periodic fixation policy, and accuracy on COFW/300W remains notably below regression-based SoTA. Key open questions include whether the approach generalizes to 3D scenes, arbitrary object categories, and adaptive (learned) fixation strategies, and whether the structured latent space can support downstream planning or manipulation tasks beyond localization.
