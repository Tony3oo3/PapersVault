## Problem

Existing LLM-enhanced Bayesian optimization methods use LLMs only at initialization or as partial signal sources, failing to exploit the fact that LLM evaluations are orders of magnitude cheaper than real experiments. This limits their ability to support broad, low-cost exploration across the search space. The paper asks whether LLMs can be systematically integrated into the BO loop to enable more effective exploration and improve sample efficiency under fixed experimental budgets.

## Input / Output

The method consumes task-specific prior knowledge (literature, physical constraints, semantic descriptions), a bounded parameter space, an optimization objective, and accumulated historical observations, all packaged into a structured prompt (fixed system prompt + dynamic user prompt) that constrains LLM output to a strict JSON schema with predicted target values clipped to valid ranges. The real-fidelity experiment function returns ground-truth measurements for selected candidates. The final output is the best formulation parameter vector and its corresponding objective value, with the optimization loop terminating when the real-fidelity budget is exhausted.

## Method

LABO is a dual-fidelity BO framework built on the Kennedy-O'Hagan decomposition f_R(x) = ρ·f_L(x) + δ(x), where f_L is a GP trained on LLM predictions and δ is a discrepancy GP trained on residuals between real observations and scaled LLM predictions. A gating criterion p_Δ(x) = σ²_δ(x)/(ρ²σ²_L(x) + σ²_δ(x)) dynamically decides whether a candidate's uncertainty is dominated by the discrepancy (triggering a costly real experiment) or by the LLM-fidelity component (allowing a free LLM update to the surrogate). During warm-start, the LLM proposes high-potential candidates for initial real experiments while Latin Hypercube Sampling generates a broad set of LLM-fidelity predictions covering the search space. In the optimization loop, q-UCB selects candidates, the LLM is queried at each, and only those failing the gate (p_Δ > τ) trigger real experiments. The paper provides a cumulative regret bound showing that the effective exploration region shrinks to a limiting subset X_R* where real queries concentrate, yielding sublinear regret with the dominant term governed by Ψ_T(X_R*) ≪ Ψ_T(X).

## Results

Across six scientific formulation optimization tasks (COF 14D, Fullerene 3D, PCE10 4D, P3HT 5D, Flow Battery 3D, Sandwich 20D), LABO consistently achieves the best final objective value under a fixed real-fidelity budget of 50 evaluations, with notably lower variance across seeds than all baselines. On the HPOBench AutoML benchmarks, LABO reaches a final objective of 0.909±0.016 (SVM) and 0.941±0.009 (MLP), requiring only 33.8±18.8 and 30.0±10.2 iterations to reach 90% of the best value, respectively. The gating criterion (τ=0.75) yields an LLM-to-real-fidelity query ratio of ~2.7× on COF and ~3.9× on Fullerene, confirming that LABO substantially reduces expensive experiments while maintaining optimization quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LABO outperforms all three LLM-guided baselines (LLAMBO, BOPRO, CAKE) and vanilla BO across all six scientific tasks. LLAMBO and BOPRO benefit from LLM-suggested initialization and show strong early progress, but both tend to get stuck in local optima in later iterations, whereas LABO's globally informed LLM-fidelity guidance enables escape from suboptimal regions. CAKE demonstrates consistent exploratory behavior but exhibits higher variance across seeds, likely due to instability from adaptive kernel updates; LABO shows significantly lower variance, reflecting more consistent search behavior. On high-dimensional tasks (COF 14D, Sandwich 20D), LABO's advantage is most pronounced, as the gating criterion concentrates expensive real-fidelity evaluations in a reduced effective region. On low-dimensional tasks (PCE10 4D, Flow Battery 3D) where vanilla BO converges quickly, LABO still delivers better final performance with more stable convergence. The ablation confirming that LABO's gains are not solely from LLM initialization (Figure 3B) and that random-fidelity predictions degrade performance validates that the advantage comes from informative LLM predictions integrated through the KOH discrepancy model.

## Relation to Prior Work

LABO builds directly on the Kennedy-O'Hagan (2000) multi-fidelity GP decomposition, repurposing it to treat LLM predictions as a 'low-fidelity' signal analogous to a fast simulation, with a discrepancy process capturing LLM-specific bias. It departs from prior LLM+BO methods (LLAMBO, BOPRO, CAKE, ChemBOMAS) that use LLMs only for initialization, candidate suggestion, or kernel design, by integrating LLM predictions as a continuous, per-iteration fidelity source governed by a discrepancy-dominance gating criterion. The theoretical analysis extends standard GP-UCB regret bounds to the KOH multi-fidelity setting with a dynamic fidelity-allocation policy, a setting not previously formalized for LLM-driven optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For scientific domains where each experiment costs hours to days (drug discovery, catalyst design, materials engineering), reducing the number of real-fidelity evaluations while maintaining optimization quality has direct practical impact. The gating criterion provides a principled, adaptive mechanism for deciding when LLM 'intuition' is trustworthy versus when ground-truth data is needed, and the regret bound guarantees that LABO degrades gracefully to vanilla BO when LLM predictions are uninformative. Open questions include how the KOH linear-scaling assumption (f_R = ρ·f_L + δ) holds when LLM predictions are systematically biased in input-dependent ways, whether the fixed gating threshold τ generalizes to tasks with very different LLM reliability profiles, and how the framework extends to discrete or mixed-variable search spaces common in molecular design.
