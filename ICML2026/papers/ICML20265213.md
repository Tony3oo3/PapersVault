## Problem

Existing generalist graph anomaly detection (GAD) methods rely on scarce and costly anomaly labels for training and often require few-shot support at inference, limiting robustness to diverse unseen anomaly patterns. This paper asks whether a generalist GAD model can be trained once in a fully unsupervised manner—leveraging only the abundant normality in unlabeled data—and then transferred zero-shot to entirely unseen graphs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265213/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a collection of attributed training graphs (node feature matrices and adjacency matrices) with no anomaly labels, and a frozen pre-trained self-supervised GNN teacher. At inference, it accepts a single unseen attributed graph and produces a per-node anomaly score by combining prototype-level distillation bias (KL divergence between teacher and student soft-label distributions) and geometric deviation (squared distance between embeddings and their nearest quantized prototypes). No retraining, fine-tuning, or support samples are required at test time.

## Method

ProMoS distills normality priors from a frozen self-supervised GNN teacher into a Mixture-of-Students (MoS) architecture comprising a shared branch for global regularities and a sparsely activated personalized branch (Top-K routing over N lightweight students) for diverse local patterns. A prototype-guided soft-label distillation loss aligns teacher and student predictions in a shared learnable prototype space (initialized via k-means), avoiding instance-level overfitting. A discrepancy-aware commitment and refinement objective uses sample-reliability weights—derived from the KL divergence between each node's prototype distribution and its nearest prototype's relational pattern—to downweight anomalous nodes while simultaneously pulling teacher features toward stable prototype anchors and refining prototypes to capture transferable semantics. A theoretical result proves that under mean-alignment and equicorrelation assumptions, the MoS ensemble achieves no larger expected prediction error than any individual student.

## Results

ProMoS achieves the best zero-shot AUROC on 9 out of 11 test datasets, with an average improvement of 14.12% over the strongest unsupervised baseline (DOMINANT). On AUPRC (more informative under severe class imbalance), ProMoS ranks first on 9 of 11 datasets, outperforming the best competitor by 33.73% on Cora. The method also demonstrates sub-linear inference scaling (T ∝ |E|^0.3) and runs 4.8× faster than GCN during training and 1.4× faster during inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest unsupervised baseline DOMINANT, ProMoS wins on 10 of 11 datasets (e.g., Cora 84.56 vs. 66.53, CiteSeer 90.77 vs. 69.47, CS 88.85 vs. 60.61), with DOMINANT narrowly ahead only on Weibo (92.88 vs. 91.74) and running out of memory on T-Finance. TAM, the second-best unsupervised method, is outperformed on all 11 datasets, with gaps ranging from 2.29 (Tolokers) to 26.48 (BlogCatalog). Among supervised generalist baselines, UNPrompt and AnomalyGFM are consistently surpassed; ProMoS leads UNPrompt by 17.83 on T-Finance (71.62 vs. 22.14) and AnomalyGFM by 23.18 on CS (88.85 vs. 48.24). In the few-shot comparison (Table 9), ProMoS (fully unsupervised) beats ARC on 7 of 11 datasets (e.g., ACM 89.47 vs. 79.88, Weibo 91.74 vs. 88.85, T-Finance 71.62 vs. 64.10) while ARC retains a small edge on Cora (87.45 vs. 84.56) and CiteSeer (90.95 vs. 90.77). Conventional supervised GNNs (GCN, GAT, BGNN, BWGNN, GHRN) all fall well below ProMoS, with average AUROC gaps exceeding 20 points, confirming that label-driven approaches fail to generalize to unseen anomaly patterns.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the knowledge distillation paradigm (Hinton et al., 2014) from model compression to graph anomaly detection, repurposing a frozen self-supervised GNN as a teacher that encodes normality priors. It departs from prior generalist GAD methods (ARC, UNPrompt, AnomalyGFM), which all require labeled supervision during training and sometimes few-shot support at inference, by eliminating labels entirely. It also diverges from conventional unsupervised GAD (DOMINANT, CoLA, TAM) that learn from scratch on a single graph, by introducing a mixture-of-students architecture with prototype-guided alignment to bridge cross-graph heterogeneity. The theoretical guarantee that MoS prediction error is bounded by any individual student's error provides a formal justification for the ensemble design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a fully unsupervised, zero-shot generalist GAD model can outperform both supervised and unsupervised baselines by 14%+ on average, this work challenges the prevailing assumption that anomaly labels are necessary for cross-graph transfer. The practical impact is significant for high-stakes domains (finance, cybersecurity) where labels are scarce, costly, and cannot enumerate evolving anomaly types. Open questions remain: whether the SSL teacher's quality is a hard bottleneck, whether the prototype space adequately captures rare or novel anomaly patterns, and whether the two-signal anomaly score (KL divergence + geometric deviation) generalizes beyond the tested graph types. The method's sub-linear scaling (α≈0.3) is encouraging but untested on graphs with millions of edges.
