## Problem

EEG is the gold standard for assessing brain activity during sleep, but its acquisition via wet electrodes and headbands is labor-intensive, artifact-prone, and disruptive to sleep itself. While breathing is far easier to capture (via belts or contactless RF sensors), no prior work has demonstrated that full sleep EEG spectrograms can be reconstructed from respiration alone, leaving a fundamental gap between the diagnostic value of EEG and the accessibility of respiratory signals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265727/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a continuous nocturnal breathing waveform (from a wearable belt or RF reflection) divided into non-overlapping 4-minute segments, each linearly projected into a dense continuous embedding. The output is a 256×512 time-frequency spectrogram of the C4-A1 EEG channel, produced by predicting a grid of discrete tokens (each spanning ~4 Hz in frequency and ~4 min in time) that are decoded back to a spectrogram via a frozen VQGAN. The 30-second spectrogram window aligns with the AASM standard epoch length for sleep scoring.

## Method

The framework uses an asymmetric embedding strategy to bridge the complexity gap between the two modalities: the breathing signal is preserved as raw waveform embeddings via a minimal linear projection (heavy encoders or time-frequency transforms degraded performance), while the EEG target is converted to a multitaper spectrogram and discretized by a VQGAN with an 8192-entry, 32-dimensional codebook and a StyleGAN-style discriminator augmented with a correlation regularization term. A ViT-Small transformer (8 blocks, 768-dim, 8 heads) then performs masked generative translation over a concatenated sequence of breathing tokens and partially masked EEG tokens, trained with cross-entropy loss and a variable masking ratio sampled from a truncated Gaussian (μ=0.55, clipped to [0.5, 1.0]). At inference, the model predicts the full EEG token sequence from breathing alone (γ=1.0), which the frozen VQGAN decodes into the final spectrogram. This discrete-token formulation converts the synthesis problem from an ill-posed continuous regression into a tractable classification over a finite vocabulary of physiologically meaningful EEG patterns, avoiding the mode-averaging blur that plagues pixel-wise regression baselines.

## Results

The model achieves an average EEG spectrogram reconstruction MAE of 0.068 (internal) and 0.067 (external) with ~14 dB SNR, demonstrating consistent performance across 14 datasets and both belt-based and RF-based breathing inputs. On downstream tasks, synthesized EEG closely matches ground-truth EEG: age estimation MAE 5.00 vs. 5.14 years, sex classification AUROC 0.814 vs. 0.819, and sleep staging accuracy 0.839 vs. 0.882. From contactless RF reflections alone, the model achieves 0.075 MAE reconstruction and supports downstream tasks at slightly lower accuracy (age MAE 8.83, sex AUROC 0.703, sleep staging Acc. 0.814 on MGH).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary external baseline is He et al. (2025), the current SOTA for breathing-based sleep staging using a ResNet-style model. The proposed breathing→synthesized EEG→staging pipeline outperforms this baseline by 2.7 percentage points in overall accuracy (0.839 vs. 0.812) across all five evaluation datasets, while narrowing the gap to ground-truth EEG (0.882) to just 4.3 points. For age estimation, synthesized EEG (MAE 5.00 years) substantially outperforms the breathing ViT baseline (7.66 years) and even slightly beats GT-EEG (5.14 years). For sex classification, synthesized EEG (AUROC 0.814) exceeds the breathing ViT baseline (0.768) by 4.6 points and nearly matches GT-EEG (0.819). The paper also ablates against a breathing-spectrogram input (bypassing EEG translation), which degrades age MAE to 14.0 and sex AUROC to 0.70, confirming that the gains come from genuine cross-modal translation rather than merely using a time-frequency representation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the masked generative modeling paradigm from vision (MaskGIT, MAGE) and the VQGAN tokenizer from image synthesis to a new cross-physiology translation setting: converting continuous respiratory waveforms into discrete EEG spectrogram tokens. Prior cross-modal physiological generation work (EEG→fMRI, PPG→ECG, PPG→ABP) operates within a single physiological domain where signals share tight coupling; this paper is the first to bridge the pulmonary-to-neurological domain gap, where the input is a low-frequency mechanical waveform and the target is a high-frequency stochastic neural signal. The asymmetric embedding strategy—raw linear projection for breathing versus learned discrete tokenization for EEG—is the key architectural departure, motivated by the need to preserve fine-grained respiratory context while constraining the EEG search space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that breathing dynamics carry a latent 'fingerprint' of brain activity sufficient to synthesize functionally useful sleep EEG, opening a path toward scalable, contact-free neurological monitoring that bypasses the discomfort and artifact issues of traditional polysomnography. The RF-based result (8% MAE vs. 7% for belt) is particularly notable as the first demonstration of EEG inference from passive wireless reflections. Key limitations include the single-channel focus (C4-A1), a residual gap to ground-truth EEG on downstream tasks, hallucination risk in generative outputs, and degraded performance under severe sleep apnea where respiratory-neurological coupling breaks down. Open questions include multi-channel extension, apnea-aware conditioning, and whether the learned mapping generalizes to pathological EEG patterns not well-represented in the training cohort.
