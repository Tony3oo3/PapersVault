## Problem

Existing convergence analyses for multi-block ADMM on nonconvex problems rely on a global range assumption (range(A_i) ⊆ range(A_n)) on the full constraint matrix, which fails for problems with block anti-upper triangular constraints such as consensus, block-angular, and robust regression models. This paper fills that gap by proving global convergence of proximal ADMM under a weaker, structure-specific assumption tailored to the block anti-upper triangular pattern.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes n block variables X_i ∈ R^{m_i×p_i} with proper closed objective functions f_i, a block anti-upper triangular system of linear constraints where row i involves only variables X_1 through X_{n-i+1} via operators A_{i,j}, penalty parameters β_i, and a sequence of positive semidefinite proximal operators Q_i^{(k)}. It outputs a sequence of primal-dual iterates (X̄^{(k)}, Λ̄^{(k)}) that converges to a stationary point satisfying the KKT conditions, with the constraint that the block structure (m constraint rows, n variable blocks) governs which subproblems are smooth versus nonsmooth.

## Method

The proposed proximal ADMM (Algorithm 1) alternates between block-wise minimization of the augmented Lagrangian plus a proximal quadratic term and a standard multiplier update, exploiting the block anti-upper triangular constraint structure. The key theoretical contribution is a novel assumption A(2) requiring range(A_{i,j}) ⊆ range(A_{i,n-i+1}) per constraint row rather than a global range condition, combined with injectivity of the diagonal operators A_{i,n-i+1}. For nonconvex problems, global convergence is established via a merit function whose sufficient decrease is proven using a recursive bounding technique (Lemma 5) that propagates multiplier errors through the triangular structure, followed by a KL-property argument. For strongly convex problems, Q-linear and R-linear convergence rates are derived under four scenarios combining different strong convexity and Lipschitz conditions on the objective components, with the penalty parameter constrained by a condition number bound involving the block structure constants.

## Results

The paper's experiments are primarily convergence-speed comparisons presented as plots (Figures 1 and 3) rather than single summary tables with fixed numerical values; detailed numbers are deferred to Appendix Tables 5–8. On the nonconvex robust regression problem (Leukemia: n=72, p=7129; Alzheimer's MRI: n=200, p=4200), pADMM-MCP and pADMM-SCAD reduce both the primal residual L₁ and the relative iterate change L₂ faster than all ADMM-type baselines in wall-clock CPU time. On the strongly convex block-angular problem (synthetic BA1/BA2 and NMCNF tripart1/tripart2), pADMM reaches the target accuracy (r₁ ≤ 10⁻⁶) more efficiently than the compared baselines, and the convergence behavior remains stable across different proximal parameter choices (Figure 4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Three ADMM-type baselines are compared: (1) Classical ADMM from Chen et al. (2025), (2) Linearized ADMM from Yashtini (2022), and (3) Semiproximal ADMM from Lam et al. (2021b). On the nonconvex robust regression experiments (Leukemia and Alzheimer's MRI), pADMM with both MCP and SCAD penalties converges faster in CPU time than all three baselines, reducing both the primal residual and the relative error more quickly (Figure 1). On the strongly convex block-angular experiments (synthetic BA1/BA2 and real-world NMCNF tripart1/tripart2), pADMM again outperforms the semiproximal ADMM and other baselines in reaching the target accuracy (Figure 3). The paper also demonstrates robustness: varying the proximal parameter Q_i does not degrade convergence (Figures 2 and 4), whereas the baselines do not have this tunable proximal term. Overall, pADMM wins on all tested instances in terms of convergence speed, with no reported cases where a baseline outperforms it.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical ADMM framework (Gabay & Mercier, 1976) and its proximal variant to multi-block problems with block anti-upper triangular constraints, building on the KL-property-based convergence machinery of Yashtini (2022) and the multi-block linear convergence analysis of Lin et al. (2015a) and Deng & Yin (2016). The key departure is that all prior nonconvex convergence results for multi-block ADMM require a global 'range assumption' (range(A_i) ⊆ range(A_n)) on the full constraint matrix, which is infeasible for the block anti-upper triangular structure. The authors introduce a weaker, structure-aware assumption (range(A_{i,j}) ⊆ range(A_{i,n-i+1})) that exploits the triangular block pattern, enabling convergence guarantees for problems like consensus, block-angular, and robust regression that were previously outside the theoretical scope of ADMM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work removes a significant theoretical barrier: many practically important multi-block problems (consensus optimization, block-angular programming, nonconvex image processing, robust regression) have constraint structures that violate the classical range assumption, leaving their ADMM-based solutions without convergence guarantees. By establishing the first global convergence result for proximal ADMM on multi-block nonconvex problems under the weaker block-specific assumption, the paper legitimizes the use of ADMM for a broader class of structured problems. Open questions include whether the assumption can be further relaxed, whether the results extend to other constraint geometries (e.g., block lower-triangular or more general sparsity patterns), and whether the penalty parameter conditions in A(4) can be made more transparent or automatically satisfiable in practice.
