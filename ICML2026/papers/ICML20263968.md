## Problem

While empirical studies consistently show that adaptive step-size methods (SPS, NGN, SPP) tolerate much larger learning rates than SGD, optimization theory has focused on convergence rates that only require existence of some optimal step size, leaving the sensitivity to step-size magnitude unquantified. This paper fills that gap by providing a unified theoretical framework that explains *why* and *how much* adaptive methods are more robust to large step sizes.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a stochastic loss f(x,s) with a user-specified step size α_t and a model f_x(·,s) around the current iterate, and produces a per-iteration stability index δ_t whose scaling in α_t determines the method's robustness. The final output is a suboptimality (or stationarity) bound of the form D²/(2Σα_t) + (Σα_t E[δ_t])/(Σα_t), where the second term's growth rate in α is the key diagnostic: linear for SGD, sub-linear for SPS/NGN/SPP. The stability index is computable from logged gradient norms and batch losses, enabling direct tracking during training.

## Method

The paper introduces the stability index δ_t := f(x_t,s_t) − f_{x_t}(x_{t+1},s_t) − (1/2α_t)‖x_{t+1}−x_t‖² within a model-based proximal update framework, and proves via a base descent lemma that E[δ_t] directly controls the variance term in suboptimality bounds. They derive closed-form δ_t for four methods: SGD gives δ_t = (α_t/2)‖g_t‖² (linear in α), while SPS gives δ_t ≤ min{α_t‖g_t‖², f(x_t,s_t)−C_t}, NGN gives δ_t = (γ_t/2)‖g_t‖² with γ_t saturating at 2f_t/‖g_t‖² as α→∞, and SPP gives δ_t ≤ min{α_t‖g_t‖²/(2(1−α_tη̄)), f(x_t,s_t)−inf f(·,s_t)}. This proves δ_t^SPS, δ_t^NGN, δ_t^SPP ≤ δ_t^SGD, establishing that adaptive methods are always at least as stable. The analysis extends to weakly convex problems via Moreau envelope stationarity bounds, and experiments on ResNet-20/CIFAR-10 and convex regression tasks confirm that the theoretical bound qualitatively tracks actual training loss across orders of magnitude in α.

## Results

The paper's central theoretical result is the derivation of a stability index δ_t for four methods, showing that SGD's index scales linearly with step size (δ_t^SGD = α_t/2 · ‖g_t‖²), while SPS, NGN, and SPP all achieve sub-linear scaling. In experiments on ResNet20/CIFAR-10, SGD degrades for base learning rates α ≥ 1, whereas SPS and NGN maintain good training loss up to α ≈ 10. On synthetic linear regression, SGD fails at α ≈ 0.03 while SPS and SPP remain stable up to α = 10². The theoretical bound Ω_T from Theorem 3.4 qualitatively matches actual training loss across all tested tasks, including non-convex deep learning where the theory does not formally apply.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SGD (Shamir & Zhang, 2013), the paper proves that SPS, NGN, and SPP are always at least as stable: their stability indices satisfy δ_t ≤ δ_t^SGD for all step sizes, with the gap determined by the interpolation constant σ² and lower-bound estimation error ε_lb. Against the SPS analysis of Loizou et al. (2021), this paper provides the first convergence result in the convex non-smooth setting and identifies the precise quantities (interpolation and lower-bound quality) that govern the stability gain; Loizou et al.'s smooth-case bound increases linearly in α for α ≥ 1/(2L_max), failing to explain SPS's empirical robustness. Against NGN (Orvieto & Xiao, 2024), the paper supplies the first convex non-smooth convergence analysis and shows δ_t^NGN converges to a finite limit as α_t → ∞ (specifically 2f_t/‖g_t‖²), confirming extreme large-step-size stability. Against SPP (Milzarek et al., 2024), the paper proves a step-size stability improvement and shows the bound is additionally capped by sub-optimality plus the interpolation constant, explaining why larger batch sizes (reducing σ²) improve SPP's stability as observed empirically.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the model-based stochastic optimization framework of Asi & Duchi (2019a) and Davis & Drusvyatskiy (2019), which established that well-chosen surrogate models yield stable iterates, by introducing the stability index δ_t as a concrete, method-specific quantity that quantifies *how* performance degrades with step size. Prior convergence analyses (e.g., Loizou et al. 2021 for SPS) could not explain why adaptive methods tolerate larger learning rates, as their bounds still grew linearly in α for large step sizes. The paper also builds on the empirical observations of Schaipp et al. (2023, 2024) and Orvieto & Xiao (2024) that SPS and NGN are robust in practice, providing the first non-asymptotic theoretical proof that their stability index is always bounded above by that of SGD.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The stability index gives practitioners a principled, computable diagnostic for understanding *why* a particular optimizer run is unstable and *which* method should be preferred for a given problem structure (interpolation constant, lower-bound quality). The qualitative match between the convex-theory bound and actual non-convex training loss (ResNet20/CIFAR10) suggests the framework may generalize beyond its assumptions, though this remains unproven. Key limitations include the exclusion of momentum, preconditioning (Adam), and weight decay—all prevalent in practice—and the open question of whether tracking δ_t online could enable adaptive early-stopping or step-size correction heuristics.
