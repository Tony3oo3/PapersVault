## Problem

While MLLM hallucination has been attributed largely to language-side biases such as text inertia, the visual perceptual mechanism underlying these failures remains poorly understood. This paper reveals that hallucinations are strongly associated with a human-like attention distraction phenomenon, manifesting in models as spatial inconsistency across multi-head attention and progressive temporal fading of visual attention during autoregressive decoding, and provides the first theoretical characterization linking cross-head attention dispersion to degraded generalization.

## Input / Output

AFIP operates as a training-free, plug-and-play module on standard MLLM architectures (LLaVA-1.5, Qwen-VL, MiniGPT-4, Shikra) that takes an RGB image (encoded into visual patch tokens) and a text prompt, and produces a generated text response. The method intercepts pre-softmax attention scores at intermediate and deeper Transformer layers (l ∈ [l_e, L]) during inference, requiring no additional training data, model retraining, or external retrieval, and imposes no measurable inference latency overhead.

## Method

AFIP corrects attention distraction through two complementary mechanisms applied to pre-softmax attention scores. First, head-level correction selects the top-k attention heads by response magnitude, computes their mean and variance over image tokens, and adds a regularization term (Avg − α·Var) that reinforces concentrated attention while penalizing spatially diffuse regions. Second, dynamic historical attention enhancement maintains a fixed-length window (W=8) of past visual attention scores, identifies the most influential historical text token, and fuses its associated visual attention into the current step to counteract temporal perceptual decay. A dynamic gating function, driven by the peak text-visual attention dominance gap, selectively activates corrections only when visual attention is insufficiently dominant, preventing over-adjustment in text-constrained decoding regimes. Theoretically, the paper proves via Rademacher complexity bounds that cross-head attention heterogeneity increases model complexity and weakens token-level discrimination, formalizing why attention consistency is necessary for reliable generation.

## Results

On the CHAIR benchmark (max new token 512), AFIP achieves an average C_S of 17.5 and C_I of 5.9 across five MLLMs, outperforming the strongest baseline HGAI (C_S 26.7, C_I 7.5) by 9.2% and 1.6% respectively. On LLaVA-1.5-7B, AFIP reduces C_S from 55.4 (greedy) to 16.8 and C_I from 14.4 to 4.4, a >50% improvement over vanilla decoding. On the POPE benchmark, AFIP attains the highest average accuracy (87.11%) and F1 (87.82%) on LLaVA-1.5-7B, surpassing HGAI (85.72% / 86.01%) and MemVR (85.18% / 86.07%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AFIP outperforms all four baselines across every model and metric on both CHAIR and POPE. Against HGAI (the closest competitor, an attention-intervention method from CVPR 2025), AFIP reduces average C_S by 9.2% (17.5 vs. 26.7) and C_I by 1.6% (5.9 vs. 7.5), with the largest gap on Shikra-7B (C_S 18.0 vs. 32.8, a 14.8% relative improvement). Against VCD (contrastive decoding, CVPR 2024), AFIP achieves substantially lower hallucination rates, e.g., C_S of 16.8 vs. 54.6 on LLaVA-1.5-7B. Against MemVR (hidden-state intervention, ICML 2025), AFIP reduces C_S from 50.4 to 16.8 on LLaVA-1.5-7B and from 38.2 to 16.6 on Qwen-VL. Against OPERA (beam-search-based decoding, CVPR 2024), AFIP lowers C_S from 48.2 to 16.8 on LLaVA-1.5-7B. The only exception is MiniGPT-4 on POPE, where AFIP's average F1 (75.90) is marginally below HGAI (76.10), though AFIP still leads on CHAIR for that model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by reframing MLLM hallucination through a human-perception analogy (attention distraction causing visual blur) and decomposing it into two mechanistic factors—spatial inconsistency across multi-head attention and temporal fading of visual attention during decoding—that prior work had not jointly addressed. It departs from existing attention-intervention methods (e.g., HGAI) by simultaneously correcting cross-head divergence and reintegrating historical visual attention, and from hidden-state approaches (e.g., MemVR) by operating directly on pre-softmax attention scores. The theoretical contribution, bounding Rademacher complexity as a function of inter-head parameter heterogeneity, is a novel formalization not present in prior hallucination-mitigation literature, though it draws on standard tools from Edelman et al. (2022) and information-theoretic generalization bounds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AFIP offers a training-free, zero-overhead plug-and-play module that practitioners can immediately deploy on existing MLLMs, and its dual spatial-temporal correction addresses a gap that single-axis methods leave open. The theoretical results provide a principled explanation for why cross-head inconsistency degrades token discrimination, potentially guiding future architectural or training choices. However, the theory is developed for a simplified single-layer transformer with a CLS token, and the human-perception analogy, while motivating, remains largely qualitative. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video or multi-image inputs, whether the Rademacher bound is tight enough to be practically informative, and whether the gains persist on more recent, larger MLLMs beyond the five evaluated here.
