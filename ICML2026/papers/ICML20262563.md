## Problem

LLM-based molecule generation produces high-quality molecules but their dense, entangled representations make fine-grained control over specific substructures or property levels extremely difficult. Existing controllable generation methods offer only coarse-grained control (presence/absence of fragments or properties) without enabling site-specific edits or precise tuning of physicochemical values within defined ranges, which is essential for real-world molecular design tasks like scaffold optimization and multi-constraint drug discovery.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262563/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes molecular sequences in Group SELFIES format (where each token corresponds to a single atom or functional group) along with a control specification: either a target atom/group at a designated token position (local control) or a property constraint with a target range (global control), plus a scalar strength parameter α ∈ [0,1]. It outputs a generated molecule in Group SELFIES that satisfies the specified constraint, with the edit applied purely at inference time via activation modification at specific transformer layers (layer 22 for local, layer 10 for global) without any parameter updates to the base LLM.

## Method

SpaRE trains a sparse autoencoder (SAE) on LLM hidden activations to project them into a 40× overcomplete sparse latent space where individual dimensions correspond to chemically interpretable concepts (e.g., specific functional groups, aromaticity, solubility). Two complementary editing schemes operate in this space: (1) local control uses a forward hook to retrieve token-specific activations at the target position, then amplifies or suppresses the associated concept vector to steer generation of a specific atom or group at that site; (2) global control computes a contrastive direction by subtracting average SAE activations of negative from positive property exemplars, then injects this direction at every generative step to accumulate a property shift. The key architectural insight is a representational hierarchy—shallow layers encode global molecular properties while deeper layers encode local substructural fragments—enabling both control types from a single SAE framework. The entire approach requires no fine-tuning of the LLM at inference time; the precomputed concept vectors are injected via forward hooks, yielding the fastest runtime among all baselines.

## Results

On the ChEBI-20 site-specific generation benchmark, SpaRE achieves a 98.92% control success rate, far surpassing the best baseline RetMol (62.58%), while maintaining 100% validity, 92.10% novelty, and the lowest SA score (3.78). On GEOM-DRUGS, SpaRE reaches 96.77% success rate and 98.57% novelty, again leading all 13 baselines. In multi-objective drug discovery (GSK3β/JNK3), SpaRE achieves 98.1% success with 100% validity and 96.2% novelty, outperforming the next-best Atomas (95.2% success, 97.6% validity). SpaRE is also the fastest method at 12.19 ms per molecule, roughly 2× faster than the next-best autoregressive baseline MolXPT (25.23 ms) and ~30× faster than GNN-based methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpaRE dominates the control success rate metric across all baselines: 98.92% on ChEBI-20 vs. RetMol's 62.58% (the strongest controllable baseline), Llamole's 46.26%, and MARS's 50.37%. On generation quality, SpaRE matches BioT5/BioT5+ at 100% validity while exceeding them in novelty (92.10% vs. 89.63%) and atom stability (97.24% vs. 93.71%). Among autoregressive baselines, Atomas achieves the closest novelty (90.91%) but trails SpaRE by 1.19 points and has a much higher SA score (4.72 vs. 3.78). Diffusion-based methods (LDMol, TGM-DLM, CDGS, JODO) all fall below 36% success rate and are 2–25× slower than SpaRE. GNN-based methods (MARS, MolEvol) are the slowest (231–384 ms) with the weakest control (37–50%). In the multi-objective GSK3β/JNK3 task (Table 2), SpaRE achieves 98.1% success and 100% validity, outperforming Atomas (95.2%/97.6%), TGM-DLM (94.5%/99.7%), and RetMol (93.8%/98.7%). SpaRE is also the fastest method overall at 12.19 ms, roughly 2× faster than MolXPT (25.23 ms) and an order of magnitude faster than all GNN and diffusion baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

SpaRE repurposes sparse autoencoders (SAEs) from the LLM interpretability literature (Olshausen & Field, 1997; Huben et al., 2024; Templeton et al., 2024) as a mechanism for controllable molecule generation, a domain where prior methods (RetMol, Llamole, Atomas, TGM-DLM) rely on retrieval, cross-modal alignment, or auxiliary conditioning modules that offer only coarse-grained control. The base model is MolXPT (a GPT-2-based molecular LLM), fine-tuned on Group SELFIES representations, and the SAEs are trained independently per layer to disentangle dense activations into an overcomplete sparse feature space. The key departure from prior controllable generation work is that SpaRE operates entirely at inference time via activation steering—no fine-tuning or additional conditioning modules are needed—enabling both site-specific local edits (via forward-hook retrieval) and global property modulation (via contrastive guidance) within a unified framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a small number of sparse latent features (~15 for a single atom, ~30 for a global property) can capture chemically meaningful concepts, bridging interpretability and actionable generative control in a way that prior controllable generation methods could not. The 98.92% control success rate (vs. 62.58% for the best baseline) and 12 ms per-molecule runtime suggest a practical path toward fine-grained molecular design in drug discovery pipelines. Open questions include whether the concept alignment generalizes to 3D structural properties, whether the approach scales to larger foundation models, and whether the linearly separable concept structure persists for more complex, multi-property constraints beyond the Lipinski-style heuristics tested here.
