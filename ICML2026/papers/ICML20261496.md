## Problem

Autoregressive Video LLMs face a dual efficiency bottleneck: causal attention imposes an asymmetric receptive field that structurally prevents early visual tokens from attending to later context, limiting global spatiotemporal aggregation (understanding efficiency), while serial token-by-token decoding scales latency linearly with output length (generation efficiency). The paper formalizes this gap via two propositions showing AR decoding admits a strictly lower information capacity bound than bidirectional decoding, and empirically demonstrates the resulting positional biases in both intra-frame and inter-frame settings.

## Input / Output

The model takes a video (sampled at up to 64 frames) and a text prompt as input. Video frames are encoded by SigLIP2 SO400M, projected, and spatially pooled via 2×2 bilinear downsampling to reduce the visual token sequence by 4× before being unrolled into a 1D spatiotemporal token sequence. The output is a text response generated through iterative masked denoising, where multiple tokens are predicted in parallel at each diffusion step rather than one at a time, enabling the model to produce readable text from the first decoding step.

## Method

VidLaDA replaces the autoregressive backbone with a Diffusion Language Model (DLM) that uses full bidirectional attention, allowing every visual and textual token to attend to the entire sequence simultaneously and eliminating the causal visibility asymmetry. For inference, MARS-Cache prunes redundant computation through three mechanisms: (i) frame-wise chunk attention that restricts visual token interactions to temporal neighbors (F_{n-1} ∪ F_n ∪ F_{n+1}), reducing attention cost from quadratic to linear; (ii) adaptive anchor token searching that identifies a small set of globally influential tokens via equidistant query subsampling and makes them visible to all tokens to restore long-range connectivity; and (iii) modality- and depth-wise asynchronous cache refreshing that updates stable visual hidden states less frequently than volatile textual states, and updates shallow layers less frequently than deep layers, with a synchronization constraint ensuring shallow updates trigger simultaneous deep-layer refreshes. Training follows a three-stage curriculum (short-clip pre-training → temporal scaling warm-up → long-form video expansion) initialized from LLaDA-V, with the ViT frozen in the final stage.

## Results

VidLaDA (8B, 64 frames) achieves 64.1 on Video-MME, 61.4 on LongVideoBench, 69.2 on MLVU-dev, and 53.4 on MLVU-test, consistently outperforming all DLM baselines (LLaDA-V, Dream-VL, SDAR-VL) across every reported benchmark. Against the strongest AR baseline Qwen2.5-VL (7B, 64F), VidLaDA wins on LongVideoBench (+1.2), MLVU-dev (+6.4), MLVU-test (+8.1), and Video-MME (+0.2), while trailing on MVBench (59.4 vs 69.6) and EgoSchema (64.5 vs 65.0). With MARS-Cache under CoT inference, VidLaDA reaches 33.6 TPS on EgoSchema and MLVU-test (vs 2.7 TPS vanilla DLM), a >12× speedup, while matching or exceeding the AR baseline LLaVA-OneVision's throughput (~27 TPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest AR baseline Qwen2.5-VL (7B, 64F), VidLaDA wins on LongVideoBench (61.4 vs 60.2), MLVU-dev (69.2 vs 62.8), MLVU-test (53.4 vs 45.3), and Video-MME (64.1 vs 63.9), but trails on MVBench (59.4 vs 69.6), EgoSchema (64.5 vs 65.0), LVBench (44.7 vs 45.3), and Video-MMMU (46.6 vs 47.4). Against LLaVA-Video (7B, 64F), VidLaDA wins on 7 of 8 benchmarks, with the largest gaps on Video-MMMU (+9.5) and EgoSchema (+7.2), losing only on MLVU-dev (69.2 vs 70.8). Against LLaVA-OneVision (7B, 32F), VidLaDA wins on all 8 benchmarks, with margins of +12.7 on Video-MMMU and +5.6 on Video-MME. Among DLM baselines, VidLaDA dominates LLaDA-V (8B, 32F) on every benchmark (e.g., +3.3 on Video-MME, +9.8 on MLVU-dev), and outperforms Dream-VL and SDAR-VL on the overlapping MLVU-dev and Video-MME metrics. The paper also shows that at a matched 32-frame setting (Appendix F.3), VidLaDA still outperforms LLaVA-OneVision across all Table 1 benchmarks, confirming the gains are not solely attributable to using more input frames.

## Relation to Prior Work

VidLaDA extends the masked diffusion language model framework (LLaDA, Nie et al. 2025) and its multimodal instantiation (LLaDA-V, You et al. 2025) to the video domain, replacing the standard autoregressive Video LLM recipe (vision encoder → projector → causal LLM decoder) with a bidirectional DLM backbone. It departs from text-only DLM acceleration (Fast-DLLM, Wu et al. 2025) by introducing MARS-Cache, which exploits modality-specific state stability and frame-wise temporal locality unique to video inputs. Conceptually, the paper positions itself against the entire AR Video LLM lineage (Qwen2.5-VL, LLaVA-Video, LLaVA-OneVision) by providing a formal information-theoretic argument (Propositions 3.1–3.2) that causal attention structurally limits spatiotemporal understanding, rather than treating diffusion as a drop-in replacement for AR decoding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VidLaDA establishes that DLM-based Video LLMs can rival SOTA AR systems on complex spatiotemporal benchmarks, validating bidirectional attention as a viable alternative paradigm for video understanding. MARS-Cache's 12× throughput gain without accuracy loss is practically significant for deploying DLM-based video models at scale, and the technique generalizes across DLM backbones (demonstrated on both LLaDA-V and VidLaDA). Open questions include whether the modest accuracy edge over AR persists at larger model scales, whether MARS-Cache's assumptions (visual state stability, frame-wise locality) hold for higher-resolution or longer videos, and whether the DLM paradigm can match AR on tasks requiring precise sequential generation (e.g., structured output, code).
