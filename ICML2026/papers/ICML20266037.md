## Problem

LLMs can generate syntactically valid optimization programs but often fail to commit to a coherent high-level modeling paradigm (e.g., flow-based vs. assignment-based vs. time-indexed), leading to incorrect index domains, runtime failures, and solver-inefficient formulations. This paradigm-level decision—termed the Modeling Strategy—remains implicit in both training data and optimization objectives, making it difficult for models to learn when and how to choose a formulation structure that is both correct and computationally efficient.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language optimization problem description as input and produces a structured reasoning trace followed by executable Gurobi Python code. The reasoning trace is enforced into three ordered segments within a  block: <strategy> (paradigm selection and core variable commitment), <modeling> (instantiation of sets, parameters, variables, objective, and constraints), and <check> (consistency and redundancy verification). This template is mandatory during both RL training and inference, and the final code block must be a complete, solver-executable Gurobi program.

## Method

SAGE is a two-phase framework. In Phase 1, a teacher model (DeepSeek-R1) generates K candidate modeling strategies per problem, produces strategy-conditioned reasoning traces and Gurobi code, filters outputs via solver execution against ground-truth answers, and deduplicates semantically redundant strategies using an LLM-as-Judge, yielding a 100K-sample multi-strategy SFT corpus. In Phase 2, the student model (initialized from DeepSeek-R1-distill-Qwen-14B) undergoes full-parameter SFT followed by Segment-Weighted GRPO, a modification of standard GRPO that assigns higher token-level weights to the strategy segment (α=2.0) than to modeling (α=1.5) or check (α=1.0) segments, improving credit assignment for early paradigm decisions. The RL objective optimizes a composite reward combining format compliance (0.2 per correctly detected structural tag), solver outcome (0 to 1.0 based on executability and correctness), and a tanh-shaped efficiency term computed from solver iterations (LP) or normalized optimality gap plus branch-and-bound node count (MILP), applied only to correct solutions.

## Results

SAGE-DS-14B achieves 80.3% average Pass@1 across eight optimization modeling benchmarks, improving over the strongest open-source baseline SIRL-Q2.5-7B (72.7%) by 7.6 points. The gap is most pronounced on complex benchmarks (MAMO-Complex, ComplexOR, IndustryOR, OptMATH), where SAGE gains approximately 15.4% over SIRL on average. SAGE also surpasses its own teacher model DeepSeek-R1 (68.1%) by 12.2 points despite using far fewer parameters, and outperforms GPT-4o (64.9%) by 15.4 points. At pass@16, SAGE discovers more distinct correct formulations, improving component-level diversity by 19–29% over baselines, and produces formulations with 14.2% fewer constraints at the largest problem scale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAGE decisively outperforms both online-RL baselines: it beats SIRL-Q2.5-7B (72.7%) by 7.6 points and StepORLM-Q3-8B (71.7%) by 8.6 points on average Pass@1. The advantage is concentrated on complex benchmarks—on ComplexOR SAGE scores 61.1% vs. 44.4% (SIRL) and 50.0% (StepORLM), and on OptMATH it reaches 45.8% vs. 27.1% and 13.9% respectively. Against zero-shot generalist LLMs, SAGE surpasses DeepSeek-V3 (71.8%) by 8.5 points and GPT-4o (64.9%) by 15.4 points, while also exceeding its own teacher DeepSeek-R1 (68.1%) by 12.2 points despite using a 14B student rather than the full R1 model. On easy benchmarks (NL4OPT, MAMO Easy, NLP4LP, OptiBench), SAGE is competitive but not uniformly best—SIRL and StepORLM edge it out on NL4OPT (94.8/96.7 vs. 94.3) and MAMO Easy (98.0/95.4 vs. 94.7)—indicating that the strategy-aware training primarily benefits structurally complex problems. Agent-based methods (Chain-of-Experts, OptiMUS) and offline-learning baselines (ORLM, LLMOpt, OptM) are substantially outperformed, with SAGE leading by 19+ points over the best agent-based average. The Qwen3-8B variant of SAGE (77.0% avg.) also confirms the framework's robustness across backbone scales, outperforming SIRL and StepORLM on the same 8B-class comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAGE extends the GRPO framework from DeepSeekMath (Shao et al., 2024) by introducing segment-weighted credit assignment that upweights strategy-level tokens over surface-level modeling tokens, addressing the credit-assignment problem in long-horizon optimization reasoning. Conceptually, it departs from prior optimization-modeling systems (ORLM, LLMOpt, SIRL, StepORLM, OptiMUS) that treat formulation as implicit in end-to-end code generation, by elevating the paradigm-level Modeling Strategy (flow-based, assignment-based, time-indexed, etc.) to an explicit, trainable planning layer with its own structured reasoning segment and dedicated reward signal. The composite reward design—combining format compliance, solver-verified correctness, and solver efficiency—extends SIRL's feasibility/optimality feedback by adding an efficiency term that incentivizes sparser constraint systems, a signal largely absent from prior solver-informed RL work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By making Modeling Strategy an explicit first-class object in both data construction and post-training, SAGE provides a practical recipe for improving automated OR modeling that mirrors how human experts actually work: commit to a paradigm first, then instantiate. The efficiency reward and the finding that strategy-aware models produce 14.2% fewer constraints (via extended-formulation-style sparsification) open a path toward solver-efficient automation, not just correctness. Key limitations include reliance on a strong teacher (DeepSeek-R1) for data synthesis, dependence on ground-truth answers for solver verification, and specialization to Gurobi-based LP/MILP settings; open questions include whether strategy-explicit modeling transfers to open-ended problems without ground truth, to weaker-teacher or self-evolving pipelines, and to broader structured-reasoning domains beyond OR.
