## Problem

Extending the foundation-model paradigm to reinforcement learning remains challenging because training a universal RL FM requires a massive, heterogeneous corpus of environments, and existing deep RL methods (DQN, PPO, TRPO) are brittle, hyperparameter-sensitive, and computationally expensive due to backpropagation. This paper asks whether a general-purpose regression foundation model, pre-trained on entirely unrelated synthetic tabular data, can be repurposed as a Q-function approximator for RL without any gradient-based training or fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a context of (state, action, reward) tuples collected from environment episodes, filtered by a high-reward gate (95th percentile of historical undiscounted returns) and bounded by a fixed token budget B. At each decision step, the query is the current state paired with every action in the discrete action set, processed in a single forward pass alongside the full context. The output is a vector of Q-value estimates from which the greedy action is selected; the context is then iteratively 'refit' by replacing rewards with bootstrapped Bellman targets (r + γ max Q) over K feedforward passes, with no parameter updates at any point.

## Method

ICR-RL reduces the RL problem to in-context regression by instantiating Fitted Q-Iteration (FQI) with a pre-trained transformer (TabPFN) as a black-box regression oracle, eliminating all gradient computation. The core mechanism, ICR-FQI, iteratively updates Bellman targets within the context by running the frozen transformer in feedforward mode and replacing each transition's reward with r + γ max_a' Q_{k-1}(s', a'), effectively 'refitting' the data without touching weights. The online agent (ICR-RL) wraps this in an ε-greedy policy with a decaying exploration schedule, a high-reward gate for episode selection, and context-engineering heuristics (notably Naive De-duplication) to manage the fixed context budget when it is exhausted. The key insight is that TabPFN, trained exclusively on i.i.d. synthetic tabular regression tasks with no exposure to MDP dynamics or RL trajectories, generalizes zero-shot to control tasks, achieving performance competitive with DQN and superior to PPO and TRPO on Gymnasium classic-control benchmarks.

## Results

On Gymnasium classic-control benchmarks (CartPole-v1, MountainCar-v0, Acrobot-v1), ICR-RL achieves competitive reward-per-episode performance with DQN while outperforming both PPO and TRPO across all three environments over 250 episodes (10 seeds each). Context engineering with Naive De-duplication (ND) enables continued learning after the context budget is exhausted, and using only 512 context samples matches the performance of 8096 samples. Proper context engineering reduces runtime by nearly 40% while maintaining similar performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ICR-RL is compared against four baselines: DQN, PPO, TRPO, and KNN-TD. Against DQN, ICR-RL achieves comparable reward-per-episode performance on all three environments without any gradient-based training or hyperparameter tuning of a neural network. Against PPO and TRPO, ICR-RL clearly outperforms both, with the gap widening over the course of training as the gradient-based methods plateau or degrade. Against KNN-TD (a traditional non-deep RL method), ICR-RL significantly outperforms, demonstrating the advantage of using a transformer-based in-context regression backbone over nearest-neighbor Q-value estimation. Notably, ICR-RL requires no backpropagation, no target network updates, and no clipping/entropy hyperparameters, making it substantially simpler to configure than any of the gradient-based baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by reducing reinforcement learning to in-context regression (ICR), repurposing a general-purpose tabular regression foundation model (TabPFN) as a gradient-free Q-function approximator via Fitted Q Iteration. It departs from transformer-based RL methods (Decision Transformer, Trajectory Transformer) that still require backpropagation, and from meta-RL approaches (RL², Algorithm Distillation, OmniRL) that demand RL-specific pre-training on large corpora of MDPs. The key conceptual departure is that no RL-specific training occurs at any stage: the ICR backbone was trained on synthetic i.i.d. tabular tasks and is applied zero-shot to sequential decision-making, with the Bellman operator's iterative structure handled entirely at inference time through context re-labelling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reduction hypothesis—that improvements in general-purpose regression FMs directly translate to RL capability—opens a path to RL foundation models without the data-diversity bottleneck of curating massive environment corpora. However, the empirical scope is narrow (three classic-control environments, all requiring dense reward shaping), and the method struggles with sparse rewards, high-dimensional inputs, and the quadratic transformer cost. Open questions include whether the prior-data mismatch (i.i.d. pretraining vs. non-stationary bootstrapped targets) can be resolved through targeted fine-tuning, whether one-shot latent FQI can eliminate the explicit iteration loop, and whether the approach scales to visual or robotic state spaces.
