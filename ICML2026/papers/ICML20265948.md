## Problem

In the standard SFT→RL post-training pipeline for reasoning LLMs, the offline SFT stage is optimized in isolation to maximize offline accuracy, yet the paper demonstrates that stronger SFT checkpoints can significantly underperform weaker ones after identical RL training. The root cause is a distribution mismatch: offline SFT learns from data generated by a behavior policy, while online RL learns from the model's own rollouts, so uniformly training on all logged tokens reinforces transitions the RL phase will rarely revisit.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265948/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes prompt-response pairs from a known (or proxy) behavior policy π_β, along with per-token log-probabilities from both π_β and the target model π_θ, and produces a reweighted per-token loss that is a drop-in replacement for standard NLL or KL-distillation objectives. The reweighting is computed in log-space with per-decision ratio clipping (e.g., [−0.08, 0.3]) and final weight clipping (e.g., [G_min, G_max]) for numerical stability. When exact π_β access is unavailable, a proxy model or ensemble of proxies from the same family can substitute, with performance scaling with the proxy's token-level correlation to the true generator.

## Method

PEAR (Policy Evaluation-inspired Algorithm for Offline Learning Loss Reweighting) corrects the offline-to-online distribution mismatch by reweighting each token's SFT loss with an importance ratio derived from off-policy evaluation. The core token-level weight is a discounted suffix likelihood ratio G_t = γ^(T−t) ∏_{j=t+1}^T Δ_j, where Δ_j = π_θ(y_j|x,y_<j)/π_β(y_j|x,y_<j), which down-weights tokens whose logged continuations are implausible under the target policy and up-weights those that remain plausible. Three granularity variants are provided: sequence-level (single global product applied uniformly), token-level (B=1, the default), and block-level (contiguous blocks of size B sharing one weight, trading granularity for reduced multiplicative variance). An optional repulsive term on verified-failure trajectories further stabilizes training. The approach is model-agnostic (works with NLL or KL-distillation losses), requires only a one-time collection of offline log-probabilities, and consistently improves post-RL performance by up to +30 pp Pass@8 on AIME-2025 over canonical SFT initialization.

## Results

After identical GRPO training, PEAR-initialized models outperform SFT-initialized models by up to +25 pp absolute Pass@1 on SynLogic logic games (Qwen3-1.7B-Base: 13.1% → 38.3%) and up to +30 pp Pass@8 on AIME-2025 (DS-Qwen-1.5B: 5% → 35%). Across four model families (Qwen2.5-1.5B-Math, DS-Qwen-1.5B, Qwen3-4B-Base, Qwen3-8B-Base), PEAR consistently improves Pass@1 average by +4 to +25 pp and Pass@8 average by +4 to +33 pp over SFT+GRPO on math benchmarks. On instruction-following, PEAR+GRPO achieves 57.67% on IF-Eval vs. 37.34% for SFT+GRPO (+20.3 pp).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PEAR dominates both concurrent importance-sampling baselines DFT (Wu et al., 2025) and Proximal-SFT (Zhu et al., 2025c) across all model families and benchmarks on both math reasoning (AIME-24, AIME-25, AMC-23) and SynLogic, with the gap widening for smaller models. Against CHORD (Zhang et al., 2025a) on SynLogic with Qwen3-0.6B-Base, PEAR achieves 12.5% Pass@1 and 42.5% Pass@8 versus CHORD's 11.4% and 31.25%, though the two methods target different pipeline stages (SFT vs. RL). PEAR also outperforms all probability-based SFT objectives from Li et al. (2025a) (TopP, BottomP, TopLogP, BottomLogP, GeneralFamily-α) in post-RL performance: for example, TopLogP achieves the best offline Pass@1 on Qwen3-1.7B-Base but the worst post-RL Pass@1 (10%), while PEAR reaches 45% online Pass@1. A single-step importance weighting baseline (weighting each token only by its own π_θ/π_β ratio) is consistently weaker than PEAR's suffix-based weighting, confirming that future-continuation plausibility matters more than per-token likelihood for RL readiness.

## Relation to Prior Work

PEAR repurposes off-policy evaluation (OPE) machinery—specifically suffix likelihood ratios and sequence-level importance weights from classical RL (Precup et al., 2000; Thomas & Brunskill, 2016; Jiang & Li, 2016)—as a reweighting scheme for the SFT stage of LLM post-training. It departs from concurrent importance-weighted SFT methods (DFT, Proximal-SFT, OPC-SFT/CHORD) that use probability-ratio weights primarily as trust-region or stabilization heuristics to keep SFT well-behaved under distribution shift; PEAR instead frames the SFT stage as an offline-to-online RL problem where the weight encodes how compatible a logged continuation is with the target policy's future rollouts. The empirical motivation builds on Kang et al. (2025) and Li et al. (2025a), who showed that SFT objectives optimized in isolation can mislead, but PEAR provides the first principled OPE-based correction rather than ad-hoc probability-shaping.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that offline SFT performance is an unreliable—and sometimes inversely correlated—proxy for post-RL performance challenges a widely held implicit assumption in the LLM post-training community, and the 20–30 pp gains on math benchmarks demonstrate that the correction is not merely theoretical. PEAR is a lightweight plug-in (one forward pass for log-probabilities, then a backward scan for suffix weights) that can be layered onto any existing SFT or KD objective, and its generality is supported by experiments across six model families, three task domains (logic, math, instruction-following), and two RL algorithms (GRPO, DAPO). Open questions include whether the approach extends to RLHF with learned reward models (where the 'verifiable' signal is absent), how sensitive it is to the quality of the behavior-policy proxy in production settings, and whether the block-size and discount hyperparameters transfer cleanly to much longer reasoning traces (e.g., 10K+ token agentic tasks).
