## Problem

Algebraic multigrid (AMG) achieves optimal linear scaling for sparse linear systems, but its convergence is limited by the sparsity–quality trade-off in coarse-grid operators: smoothed aggregation improves convergence at the cost of stencil growth, while non-Galerkin sparsification methods like SpSA have algorithmic bottlenecks that scale poorly with node degree. Prior neural approaches to AMG either handle only two levels, require per-iteration inference, or produce dense operators, leaving no method that combines strict sparsity by construction, scaling to deep hierarchies and million-node domains, and setup-only neural inference.

## Input / Output

RAPNet takes a sparse system matrix A together with its unsmoothed aggregation hierarchy (binary prolongation P_g and restriction R_g) as input, and emits sparse additive corrections ΔP, ΔR, and ΔA_c that are constrained to the non-zero entries of the aggregation pattern. The corrections are applied once during solver setup; the subsequent solve phase uses standard AMG V-cycles (or GMRES preconditioned by the augmented hierarchy) with no additional neural inference. Training uses small subgraphs of ~4000 nodes extracted from larger graphs or small 2D PDE discretizations, while evaluation generalizes to million-node 3D unstructured meshes and deeper hierarchies than seen in training.

## Method

RAPNet is a weight-shared GNN that processes the AMG hierarchy as a sequence of level pairs, each represented as a composite graph where fine and coarse nodes are vertices and the off-diagonals of A_l, A_{l+1}, P_l, R_l form intra- and inter-level edges. The architecture follows an encode–process–decode paradigm: MLP encoders map node/edge features to 64-dimensional latent states, three residual gated graph convolutional (RGGCN) layers propagate messages, and an MLP decoder projects edge embeddings back to scalar corrections. A key mechanism is a level-wise mixing step that concatenates the previous level's coarse hidden states with the current fine-level states via a learned linear projection, acting as a recurrent unit over hierarchy depth and enabling generalization to more levels at inference than at training. Training is self-supervised: random solutions are pre-smoothed with a random number of AMG V-cycles to generate algebraically-smooth residuals, and the loss is the relative L2 error between the predicted and true error after a single augmented V-cycle (with Jacobi replacing the coarse solve for gradient stability). The network is trained on small subgraphs and generalizes to larger problems and deeper hierarchies, exploiting the local nature of multigrid convergence as analyzed by local Fourier analysis.

## Results

RAPNet reduces average V-cycle iterations by 40–90% over plain aggregation (AGG) and 30–80% over sparsified smoothed aggregation (SpSA) across seven benchmark families. As a standalone solver on 3D advection-diffusion (524K vars), RAPNet converges in 62±15 iterations versus 906±285 for AGG and 875±324 for SpSA. As a GMRES preconditioner on Watts-Strogatz graphs (1M vars), RAPNet requires 130±20 iterations while SpSA fails to converge on 49% of test cases. RAPNet achieves 100% convergence on all evaluated datasets, whereas SpSA drops to 18% convergence on 1M-node Watts-Strogatz graphs and 6% on 300K-node Social Hub graphs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAPNet is compared against two classical AMG baselines: plain unsmoothed aggregation (AGG) and sparsified smoothed aggregation (SpSA, Treister & Yavneh 2015). Against AGG, RAPNet wins decisively on every benchmark, reducing standalone iterations by roughly 40–90% (e.g., 62 vs. 906 on 3D advection-diffusion at 524K vars). Against SpSA, RAPNet achieves substantially lower iteration counts on graph Laplacian benchmarks—Watts-Strogatz (325 vs. non-converging at 1M vars), Social Hub (18 vs. 978 at 300K vars), and Temporal Barabási-Albert (72 vs. 467 at 200K vars)—and on 3D advection-diffusion (62 vs. 875 at 524K vars). The one area where SpSA retains an edge is 3D anisotropic diffusion as a standalone solver (132–164 vs. 202–251 iterations), though as a GMRES preconditioner the gap narrows to 1–2 iterations. Critically, RAPNet achieves 100% convergence across all 100 test instances on every dataset, while SpSA's convergence rate drops to 18% (Watts-Strogatz 1M) and 6% (Social Hub 300K), indicating that RAPNet's learned corrections provide robustness that classical sparsification heuristics cannot match on high-connectivity or anisotropic topologies.

## Relation to Prior Work

RAPNet extends the non-Galerkin AMG framework of Treister & Yavneh (2015) and Falgout & Schroder (2014), which replace dense Galerkin coarse operators with sparser spectrally-equivalent ones, by learning the corrections via a GNN rather than hand-crafted heuristics. It departs from prior neural AMG work (Greenfeld et al. 2019; Luz et al. 2020), which learned prolongation operators but were restricted to two-level hierarchies, by introducing a level-wise shared-weight architecture that generalizes across arbitrary hierarchy depths. The method also contrasts with Huang et al. (2024), which used convolutions on structured grids to predict coarse stencils, by operating on unstructured graphs via message passing and preserving the unsmoothed aggregation sparsity pattern by construction rather than learning a new pattern.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RAPNet addresses a practical bottleneck in scientific computing: the sparsity-convergence trade-off in AMG that limits parallel scalability and multi-query efficiency (eigenproblems, time-dependent simulations, inverse problems). The ability to train on 4K-node graphs and generalize to million-node systems, including 2D-to-3D transfer for PDEs, makes the approach broadly applicable without per-problem retraining. Key limitations include the absence of theoretical convergence guarantees for the learned corrections, the use of classical (unmodified) smoothers, and the reliance on fixed aggregation patterns. Open questions include whether the aggregation step itself can be learned, whether theoretical guarantees can be established beyond the small-perturbation regime of non-Galerkin theory, and whether the approach extends to saddle-point or non-symmetric systems beyond the supplementary elasticity/Stokes experiments.
