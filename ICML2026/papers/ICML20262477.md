## Problem

Existing incomplete multi-view multi-label classification methods treat labels solely as supervision signals at the output layer, leaving the semantic structure in partial annotations (e.g., label co-occurrence patterns) unexploited. This results in label-agnostic latent representations and category-agnostic fusion that cannot capture how different categories depend on distinct view contributions, producing suboptimal predictions under dual view and label incompleteness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262477/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each sample provides m heterogeneous views (e.g., 6 image descriptors like DenseHue/SIFT/GIST, 2 DenseNet-121 embeddings from frontal/lateral chest radiographs, or 6 tabular sports-statistic groups) with a subset randomly masked per the Partial Example Ratio, plus a binary label vector of length C with a fraction of entries unobserved per the Label Missing Ratio. The method outputs a shared d_z-dimensional latent representation and a full C-dimensional multi-label prediction, where each category's probability is computed through a category-conditioned posterior and aggregated across (1+|V|) prediction sources via confidence-weighted softmax.

## Method

LGRL introduces three coupled mechanisms: (1) a semantic-informed mixture prior where each category c has a learnable Gaussian prototype N(μ_c, diag(σ_c²)) generated from trainable one-hot embeddings via MLPs, with a contrastive alignment loss pulling representations toward positive prototypes and away from negative ones; (2) a decomposed information-bottleneck objective separating feature-level (reconstruction + encoder-consistency KL) and label-level (classification + classifier-consistency KL) terms, each with derived variational bounds; and (3) category-specific conditional posteriors in which the category prototype acts as an additional precision-weighted expert in a Product-of-Experts fusion, so that each label hypothesis biases the latent representation toward its category-relevant subspace before a per-category classifier evaluates the evidence. Predictions from the full-view posterior and each single-view posterior are combined via a confidence-weighted aggregation that favors decisive outputs, and the overall loss jointly optimizes reconstruction, consistency, semantic alignment, and binary cross-entropy over observed labels.

## Results

At the standard 50% PER / 50% LMR setting, LGRL achieves the best average ranking (AVE) on 5 of 6 benchmark datasets, ranking only second on OBJECT. On Corel5k, LGRL attains an AP of 0.469, surpassing the runner-up TDLSR (0.450) by 4.2% and MTD/SIP (0.414) by 13.3%. Under extreme 90% PER / 90% LMR, the advantage widens: on Corel5k LGRL reaches AP 0.360 versus 0.323 for TDLSR (+11.5%) and 0.242 for SIP (+48.8%). On the real-world CheXpert-MV and NBA datasets, LGRL consistently achieves the highest AP across all missing-ratio settings from 30% to 90%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LGRL outperforms all nine baselines on 5 of 6 datasets (all except OBJECT, where it ranks second behind TDLSR). The most competitive baselines are TDLSR, SIP, and MTD, which frequently occupy top-3 positions; LGRL surpasses TDLSR by 4.2% AP on Corel5k (0.469 vs 0.450), 8.2% on ESPGame, and 8.1% on IAPRTC12, and beats MTD/SIP by 13.3% on Corel5k. Under extreme 90% missingness, the gap widens substantially: LGRL gains 11.5% over TDLSR and 48.8% over SIP on Corel5k AP, confirming that label-guided priors provide the largest benefit when supervision is sparse. Older matrix-factorization methods (iMVWL) and the non-missing-data method LVSL (which requires imputation) consistently rank last, with AVE rankings around 7.3. The ablation confirms that the category-specific conditional posteriors (S₃) contribute the largest single improvement, followed by the consistency constraints (S₂) and the semantic alignment loss (S₁).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the information bottleneck framework for multi-view multi-label classification (SIP, Liu et al. 2024) by decomposing the unified objective into separate feature-level and label-level components, and grounds its variational architecture in the standard VAE formulation (Kingma & Welling, 2014). Its key departure from prior iMvMLC methods is treating label semantics as structural priors rather than mere supervision signals: learnable category prototypes impose geometric structure on the latent space via contrastive alignment, and these same prototypes act as Bayesian experts within a Product-of-Experts fusion, enabling category-conditional multi-view aggregation. This contrasts with prior fusion approaches (LMVCAT, MFD, URDF) that aggregate views into a single category-agnostic representation, and with label-semantic methods (NAIM3L, TDLSR) that model label correlations without integrating them into the generative/fusion mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The category-specific PoE fusion provides a principled mechanism for hypothesis-driven multi-label reasoning that naturally handles missing views and adapts view contributions per category, which is practically relevant for domains like medical imaging where modalities are often unavailable. However, prototype learning scales linearly with the number of categories C, limiting applicability to extreme multi-label settings (thousands of labels); the authors acknowledge this and suggest prototype clustering as a natural extension. An open question is whether the dual-level supervision (per-view classification loss plus fused classification loss) is necessary or whether a single-level approach with the category-specific posteriors would suffice, as the ablation shows S1 (semantic alignment) contributes less than expected on larger label spaces.
