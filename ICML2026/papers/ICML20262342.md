## Problem

Unpaired image inverse problems—where only independent sets of noisy measurements and clean signals are available for training—remain challenging because existing OT-based solvers enforce exact marginal-matching constraints, making them brittle to multi-level observation noise, class imbalance between source and target distributions, and noise-type mismatches. No prior OT-based method had incorporated a likelihood-based cost function tailored to the measurement model, nor had the Unbalanced OT framework been applied to inverse problems.

## Input / Output

The method takes a noisy measurement image y (128×128 RGB) produced by a known corruption operator A (Gaussian deblurring, 4× patch downsampling, HDR clipping, or a pretrained nonlinear neural operator) plus additive noise of unknown or multi-level intensity, and produces a clean reconstructed image x at the same spatial resolution. In the unpaired setting, mini-batches of measurements Y and clean signals X are independently sampled with no correspondence between them. For super-resolution, y has lower spatial dimensions than x, so a bicubic interpolation Q is applied to y before computing the quadratic cost term.

## Method

UOTIP formulates the inverse problem solver as a neural Unbalanced Optimal Transport map T* from the noisy measurement distribution μ to the clean signal distribution ν, trained via the semi-dual UOT objective with a combined cost c(y,x) = τ(||A(x)−y||² + ||y−x||²). The likelihood cost c_l encodes the measurement model (proportional to negative log-likelihood under Gaussian noise), while the quadratic cost c_q guarantees the twist condition and thus existence/uniqueness of the OT map even for ill-posed operators (Proposition 3.1: injectivity holds when the quadratic weight λ exceeds the Lipschitz constant of A). Training alternates between minimizing a transport loss for the generator T_θ and a KL-based f-divergence loss (Ψ*(t)=e^t−1) for the potential v_φ, following the UOTM framework. The unbalanced relaxation—replacing exact marginal constraints with soft f-divergence penalties—enables the map to rescale source samples, providing robustness to multi-level noise, class imbalance, and diverse noise types without architectural changes.

## Results

On four unpaired inverse problem benchmarks (Gaussian deblurring, 4× super-resolution, HDR reconstruction, nonlinear deblurring) across FFHQ and AFHQ-dog, UOTIP achieves the best or near-best scores on nearly all metrics. Headline numbers include PSNR of 28.52 / SSIM 0.8841 / FID 11.37 on FFHQ nonlinear deblurring, and PSNR 26.02 / SSIM 0.8642 / FID 20.84 on FFHQ HDR reconstruction. On AFHQ, UOTIP reaches FID as low as 5.11 (nonlinear deblurring) and 7.90 (HDR), substantially outperforming the strongest OT-based baseline OTUR (FID 7.65 and 7.23 respectively). The method also demonstrates superior robustness under multi-level noise (FID 58.4 vs. 70.2 for OTUR on 4× SR) and class imbalance settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UOTIP outperforms all three OT-based baselines (NOT, OTUR, RCOT) on the majority of metrics across all four tasks and both datasets. Against OTUR (the strongest baseline), UOTIP gains +0.24 dB PSNR and −3.1 FID on FFHQ Gaussian deblurring, +0.26 dB PSNR and −3.3 FID on FFHQ 4× SR, +0.70 dB PSNR on FFHQ HDR, and +1.58 dB PSNR and −1.2 FID on FFHQ nonlinear deblurring. RCOT is consistently the weakest OT baseline, with FID scores often exceeding 100 on FFHQ (e.g., 123.7 on Gaussian deblurring, 118.8 on SR), indicating severe distributional mismatch. In the additional Appendix F comparisons, UOTIP also surpasses the diffusion-based DPS by large margins (e.g., PSNR 24.06 vs. 18.91 on Gaussian deblurring; FID-Total 21.2 vs. 4.3, though DPS's low FID-Total suggests memorization) and the more recent KIDOT method (PSNR 24.06 vs. 21.98 on Gaussian deblurring). The only near-tie is AFHQ 4× SR PSNR, where RCOT (25.04) marginally exceeds UOTIP (24.97), though UOTIP still leads on FID (15.9 vs. 69.1).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Neural UOT framework (UOTM, Choi et al. 2023) from generative modeling to unpaired inverse problems by introducing a likelihood-based cost function c_l(y,x) = ‖A(x)−y‖² that encodes the measurement model. It departs from prior OT-based inverse problem solvers (NOT, RCOT) by replacing the standard balanced OT with Unbalanced OT, and from GAN-based approaches (OTUR) by retaining the semi-dual OT map parameterization. The theoretical contribution—proving that adding a quadratic cost term restores the twist condition for ill-posed operators—complements the existing OT existence theory (Fathi & Figalli, 2010) in a setting where the likelihood cost alone fails injectivity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UOTIP provides the first principled UOT formulation for unpaired inverse problems, offering practical robustness to multi-level observation noise, class imbalance between source and target datasets, and noise-type mismatch—properties that are natural consequences of the relaxed marginal constraints but had not been exploited for inverse problems before. The twist-condition guarantee is a clean theoretical result that ensures well-posedness of the learned transport map even when the forward operator is non-invertible. A key limitation is that the cost function is fixed-form (Gaussian likelihood) without learned capacity, and the cost intensity τ is set heuristically; adapting the cost to the actual noise distribution or learning it end-to-end remains open. The quadratic-only blind variant outperforming OTUR suggests the OT map structure itself carries substantial inductive bias, which could inform future blind inverse problem solvers.
