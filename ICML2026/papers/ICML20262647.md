## Problem

Batch multi-objective Bayesian optimization (MOBO) must simultaneously acquire high-quality Pareto-optimal candidates and diverse points that cover the full Pareto front, yet existing acquisition functions handle these two requirements only indirectly. EHVI is biased by reference-point choice, CDF-based methods (e.g., BOtied) exhibit myopic oversampling in local Pareto regions, and entropy-based diversity methods (e.g., QSVGD) impose a fixed exploration preference that is mismatched to the unknown objective structure. The paper identifies the absence of a principled probabilistic quantity that directly measures the likelihood that an acquired batch matches the true Pareto optimal set in both quality and coverage.

## Input / Output

The method consumes m black-box objective functions over a design space X, a set of n prior observations D_n, and a batch size q, and outputs a batch of q query points selected by jointly maximizing the POM acquisition function under a GP surrogate. Unlike objective-space diversity methods, POM operates its diversity estimation entirely in the design space, where the feasible region is explicitly defined and no validity concerns arise. The batch is selected via joint (non-sequential) optimization within BOTorch using 512 Monte Carlo samples per iteration, and the evaluation budget is fixed (e.g., 80 queries for the alloy task, 60 for the GM benchmark) with 10 random initial points.

## Method

POM factorizes the probability that a batch X equals the true Pareto optimal set X* into two components: Pareto Optimality P(X ⊆ X*), the product of per-candidate non-domination probabilities, and Coverage P(X* ⊆ X), estimated via either a hypervolume indicator (POMH) or an empirical CDF indicator (POMC). The key mechanistic innovation is Pareto Optimal Region Sampling (PORS): instead of drawing candidates from the full GP posterior, the design space is partitioned into boxes defined by the current Pareto front, each box's probability mass is computed as a truncated multivariate Gaussian CDF, and a multinomial distribution over box-conditional expected locations is constructed to generate samples only from regions that are potentially Pareto optimal. This conditional sampling eliminates the myopic bias of standard acquisition estimation by structurally excluding regions where no Pareto-optimal solution can exist. The two variants differ only in the coverage indicator: POMH assigns uniform weight across the objective space (favoring uniform Pareto front distribution), while POMC weights by realized candidate density (favoring recovery of the true Pareto front density). Complexity is O(max{qm, (2^q−1)m}) per iteration in early stages, growing to O(mn(ln n)^(m−1)) as the Pareto set accumulates.

## Results

On the three primary synthetic benchmarks (Gaussian Mixture, RE4-7-1, Penicillin), POMH achieves the best hypervolume across all problems: 0.156 on GMM, 325 on RE4-7-1, and 3.42×10⁵ on Penicillin, outperforming the next-best baselines (qEHVI-SF at 0.149/329/3.20×10⁵). On the new Expected Minimum Distance (EMD) metric, POMH achieves 0.013 on GMM and 0.489 on RE4-7-1, while POMC achieves the best EMD of 0.413 on Penicillin. In the alloy inverse design case study (6 objectives, 1000 candidates, 80 evaluations), POM-based acquisitions consistently achieve the highest Pareto optimal rediscovery ratio across all bi-objective, tri-objective, and six-objective task configurations, with stable performance even at small batch sizes (q=2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

POMH outperforms qEHVI (Emmerich et al., 2006) on hypervolume across all six synthetic problems (e.g., 325 vs. 271 on RE4-7-1; 3.42 vs. 2.95 on Penicillin) and on EMD (0.489 vs. 0.585 on RE4-7-1), with the gap widening for small batch sizes where qEHVI suffers from local oversampling. Against QSVGD (Gong et al., 2019), POMH achieves consistently higher rediscovery ratios in the alloy design study and better hypervolume on all benchmarks, while QSVGD's entropy-based diversity term requires careful hyperparameter tuning and can degrade performance when the diversity term dominates. POMH also surpasses qCDF/BOtied (Park et al., 2023) on hypervolume for RE4-7-1 (325 vs. 269) and Penicillin (3.42 vs. 2.72), though qCDF achieves an anomalously high 0.932 on GMM (likely due to the CDF metric's sensitivity to the specific landscape structure). Among the remaining baselines, POMH leads qParEGO (Knowles, 2006), qJES (Tu et al., 2022), qEMMI (Olofsson et al., 2018), qPOTS (Renganathan & Carlson, 2023), DGEMO (Neumann et al., 2019), and qHVKG (Daulton et al., 2023) on hypervolume for the complex multi-region problems (GMM, RE4-7-1, Penicillin, RE3-5-4), while on simpler unimodal landscapes (DTLZ3, ZDT3) the advantage narrows or reverses (e.g., qPOTS achieves 21.6 vs. POMH's 21.0 on ZDT3). POMC complements POMH by achieving the best EMD on Penicillin (0.413) and RE4-7-1 (0.457), demonstrating that the CDF-based coverage indicator is particularly effective for design-space diversity when the Pareto front has non-uniform density.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-objective Probability of Optimality concept (Hennig & Schuler, 2012) to the multi-objective batch setting by factorizing the matching probability into Pareto Optimality and Coverage components, providing precise probabilistic definitions where prior work (EHVI, BOtied) treated these as informal, correlated notions of proximity to the Pareto front. It departs from standard batch MOBO acquisition functions (qEHVI, qCDF) by introducing a conditional sampling mechanism (Pareto optimal region sampling) that restricts candidate generation to regions where the current front could still be improved, rather than sampling from the full surrogate posterior. The approach also repurposes hypervolume and CDF indicators not as standalone acquisition functions but as coverage estimators within a unified probabilistic framework, and introduces the Expected Minimum Distance (EMD) metric for design-space coverage evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The POM framework offers a principled, adaptive mechanism for balancing exploitation and diversity in batch MOBO without requiring manual hyperparameter tuning (unlike QSVGD's entropy weight), and its batch-size insensitivity makes it robust across different parallel evaluation budgets. The EMD metric fills a gap in MOBO evaluation by measuring design-space coverage rather than objective-space coverage, providing a stricter and more actionable measure for applications like materials discovery. Key limitations include the independence assumption in Pareto optimality estimation, the assumption that the current front is strictly dominated by the ground truth, and computational challenges in high-dimensional many-objective settings (GAA results are partial). Open questions include whether the conditional sampling strategy generalizes to constrained or mixed-variable design spaces, and how POM performs when the true Pareto front is genuinely unimodal, where its diversity mechanism may be unnecessary overhead.
