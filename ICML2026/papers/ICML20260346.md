## Problem

GUI agents frequently make policy-induced errors during execution (incorrect grounding, misinterpretation of screen state, wrong subgoals) but lack the ability to detect and recover from them, hindering real-world deployment. Existing benchmarks and training data are misaligned with real failure modes: they overrepresent low-level, immediately-identifiable errors while actual policy-induced failures are often compositional, high-level, and only become apparent after multiple steps. This paper addresses both the evaluation gap (no fine-grained benchmark measuring error awareness and long-horizon recovery) and the data gap (no scalable method to generate diverse failure-recovery trajectories matching the policy-induced error distribution).

## Input / Output

The agent consumes a natural-language task instruction, a sequence of GUI screenshots, and the prior action history, and produces a ReAct-style CoT reasoning trace followed by an executable PyAutoGUI action (click, type, drag) with coordinates normalized to [0,1] in the AgentNet action space. For GUI-RobustEval evaluation, the agent is additionally injected with an erroneous prefix trajectory at a controlled error depth (0, 1, 3, or 5 steps after the root-cause action) and must both recognize the error and recover to complete the task. The RoTS synthesis pipeline outputs 800k step-level training instances—each a (instruction, history, screenshot, action) tuple—split into 720k reflection-agnostic and 80k reflection-related samples after critic-based filtering and MinHash deduplication.

## Method

The core technical contribution is RoTS, a tree-based online data synthesis framework that organizes all rollouts for each task into a replayable trajectory tree and performs Explore-Recovery Co-Expansion over 32 rounds. On successful branches, Fragility-Driven Exploration (FDE) scores each node with a UCB-style criterion combining the progress critic's estimated error probability and an exploration bonus, then replays to the selected fragile state and branches out to proactively discover new failure modes while reusing the correct prefix. On failed branches, Experience-Informed Recovery (EIR) localizes the root-cause error using a reflector conditioned on the failed trajectory plus aggregated experiences from sibling-branch trajectories, then performs an advice-conditioned recovery rollout from the identified error state. A post-processing pipeline applies VLM-based state-transition consistency filtering, progress and action critics for step-level correctness masking, a reflection validator to partition data into reflection-agnostic and reflection-related subsets, and MinHash-based deduplication. The final 800k-sample dataset fine-tunes Qwen2.5-VL-7B/32B via SFT with a mixture ratio λ_ref=0.1, while the companion benchmark GUI-RobustEval (1,216 cases across 11 error types and 4 depths) measures Error-Awareness Rate and Post-Error Success Rate to provide fine-grained diagnostics beyond overall task success.

## Results

On OSWorld (max steps 50), RoTS-32B achieves a 47.4% success rate and 33.8% All-Pass@4, setting a new state-of-the-art among open-weights GUI agents and showing a much lower accuracy drop (↓28.7%) compared to OpenCUA-32B (↓54.5%). RoTS-7B reaches 36.3% success rate and 26.3% All-Pass@4, surpassing all comparable open-weights 7B models. On the paper's own GUI-RobustEval benchmark, RoTS-32B achieves 58.8% error-awareness rate and 33.2% post-error success at the hardest depth (d=5) with the lowest performance drop (↓33%), while RoTS-7B reaches 51.9% awareness and 26.7% success (↓38%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On OSWorld, RoTS-32B (47.4%, 33.8% All-Pass@4) substantially outperforms the strongest open-weights baselines: OpenCUA-32B (34.1%, 15.5%) by +13.3 points in success rate and +18.3 in All-Pass@4, and Qwen3-VL-32B-Thinking (41.0%, 21.1%) by +6.4 and +12.7 respectively. RoTS-7B (36.3%, 26.3%) similarly dominates the 7B/8B tier, beating Qwen3-VL-8B-Thinking (33.9%, 21.6%) and OpenCUA-7B (28.2%, 12.5%) by a wide margin. On GUI-RobustEval, RoTS-32B leads all open-source models in both error awareness (58.8% vs. 50.3% for OpenCUA-32B) and post-error success at depth 5 (33.2% vs. 25.9%), with the smallest performance degradation across depths (↓33% vs. ↓53% for OpenCUA-32B). RoTS-7B also surpasses OpenCUA-7B on awareness (51.9% vs. 46.3%) and depth-5 success (26.7% vs. 19.0%). However, both RoTS models remain behind top proprietary systems such as Claude 4.5 Sonnet (58.1% on OSWorld) and Qwen3-VL-Flash (41.6%), indicating that the gap to closed-source agents persists despite the robustness gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GUI agent evaluation and training literature by isolating policy-induced error recovery as a first-class objective, departing from prior benchmarks that focus on grounding accuracy, planning, or overall task success, and from robustness benchmarks that inject external noise or adversarial perturbations. On the training side, it builds on the self-reflection paradigm of AgentR (Yuan et al., 2025) and the WebJudge reward framework (Xue et al., 2025), but replaces offline-augmented or human-curated reflection data with a tree-based co-expansion pipeline (Fragility-Driven Exploration + Experience-Informed Recovery) that proactively discovers diverse failure modes and synthesizes long-horizon recovery trajectories. The action space and CoT format follow AgentNet (Wang et al., 2025), and the UCB exploration constant follows AgentR, but the joint FDE+EIR tree growth over a replayable GUI environment is the paper's core methodological contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As GUI agents move toward real-world deployment, the inability to detect and recover from one's own mistakes is a critical bottleneck that existing training data and benchmarks under-address; GUI-RobustEval and the 800k RoTS dataset provide the field with both a fine-grained diagnostic tool and a scalable data source to close this gap. The finding that error-recovery training disproportionately improves consistency (All-Pass@4 more than doubles vs. OpenCUA-32B) suggests that robustness and peak performance are coupled through long-horizon reflection, a useful insight for agent design. Limitations include desktop-only scope, cross-format conversion overhead in evaluation, and performance saturation at ~1M samples under the current tree-expansion settings, leaving open whether larger N, more rounds, or RL-based self-evolution can push further.
