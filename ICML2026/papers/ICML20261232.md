## Problem

Neural Flows model irregular multivariate time series via one-step mappings that bypass iterative ODE solvers, but this collapses continuous-time dynamics into a single application of interaction effects, removing the iterative refinement of inter-variable dependencies that Neural ODEs naturally provide. Most existing approaches treat variables independently, and incorporating graph-structured interactions into a one-step flow in a way that directly governs trajectory evolution remains largely unexplored, especially under missing observations where interaction learning and trajectory prediction must be jointly resolved.

## Input / Output

The method takes irregularly sampled multivariate time series where each observation is a D_x-dimensional vector recorded at non-uniform timestamps with a binary mask indicating which variables are present (missing values are zero-imputed during inference). It produces a binary class label (e.g., in-hospital mortality or sepsis onset) alongside learned latent trajectories and a time-invariant interaction graph. The interaction graph is inferred via segment-level self-attention over C consecutive temporal segments, aggregated into a global posterior weighted by KL divergence, while the initial latent state is inferred by back-propagating each observed state to t_0 through the inverse flow conditioned on its segment's adjacency matrix.

## Method

GSNF conditions a ResNet-style one-step flow on an interaction graph A, computing the residual as the element-wise product of an MLP and a GCN applied to the concatenated state and time encoding, with a Time-Fourier scaling factor φ ensuring the initial condition. Invertibility is guaranteed by spectral normalization on all linear layers, making the flow bi-Lipschitz. Two auxiliary trajectory-level self-supervision signals compensate for the absence of iterative interaction refinement: (i) Interaction-Aware Trajectory Generation (ITG) re-initializes the flow at an intermediate time t_0* to create a divergent auxiliary trajectory, penalized by a margin-based loss with a theoretically derived data-dependent lower bound on the separation margin; (ii) Reverse-Time Trajectory Generation (RTG) applies the learned flow in reverse from the final state and enforces forward-backward consistency via a reconstruction loss. The overall framework is a VAE that jointly infers the interaction graph and initial latent state, trained with a combined ELBO, cross-entropy classification loss, and the two trajectory-level regularizers.

## Results

GSNF(δ_lb) achieves the best AUPRC on all five irregular time series classification benchmarks: 56.9 (PhysioNet12), 56.3 (P12), 57.9 (P19), 55.5 (MIMIC-IV), and 56.1 (eICU), with corresponding AUROC values of 86.7, 87.5, 91.2, 85.3, and 85.2. The largest margin over the second-best method is 1.2 AUPRC points on P19, the most class-imbalanced dataset. Among top-performing methods (AUPRC ≥ 55), GSNF has the lowest training time at 9 s/epoch and 6349 MB peak GPU memory, compared to 17 s/7356 MB for FlowPath and 37 s/6743 MB for DualDynamics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GSNF(δ_lb) outperforms all 13 baselines on AUPRC across every dataset, with the most notable margin of +1.2 AUPRC over DualDynamics (57.9 vs 56.7) on P19, the most imbalanced benchmark. Among continuous-time flow baselines, GSNF surpasses IVP-VAE by 3.7–4.2 AUPRC points and NeuralFlow by 5.4–6.2 points, confirming that explicit graph-structured interaction modeling within the one-step flow is the key differentiator. Among graph-based methods, GSNF beats GNeuralFlow by 1.2–2.4 AUPRC points and Hi-Patch by 1.0–1.7 points, while also matching or exceeding their AUROC. In terms of efficiency, GSNF trains in 9 s/epoch—roughly 2× faster than FlowPath (17 s) and 4× faster than DualDynamics (37 s)—while IVP-VAE, though fastest at 3 s/epoch, falls into a lower performance tier (AUPRC 46–54). The theoretically derived margin variant GSNF(δ_lb) further outperforms the manually tuned GSNF(δ) on 4 of 5 datasets without any dataset-specific hyperparameter search.

## Relation to Prior Work

GSNF sits at the intersection of two lines of work: Neural Flows (Biloš et al., 2021), which replace iterative ODE solvers with one-step trajectory mappings, and graph-structured continuous-time models (Poli et al., 2019; Huang et al., 2020), which condition dynamics on interaction graphs but rely on multistep numerical integration. The key departure is from GNeuralFlow (Mercatali et al., 2024), which uses a learned DAG only to parameterize the initial condition rather than to modulate the flow dynamics throughout trajectory evolution. GSNF embeds graph-conditioned interactions directly into the one-step flow mapping and compensates for the loss of iterative refinement with two trajectory-level self-supervision signals (ITG and RTG), building on the IVP-VAE parallel inference paradigm (Xiao et al., 2024) for initial state estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine tension in the field: one-step flows are computationally efficient but inherently limit how inter-variable interactions are refined along trajectories, a gap that iterative ODE-based graph models handle naturally. The theoretical lower bound on trajectory divergence (Theorem 5.1) provides a principled, parameter-free alternative to manual margin tuning, which is practically useful for deployment across heterogeneous datasets. However, the current formulation assumes a static interaction graph at inference time, limiting applicability to non-stationary or rapidly evolving systems, and scalability to very long sequences remains an open question. Future extensions to time-varying graphs and larger-scale non-stationary settings would strengthen the framework's broader relevance.
