## Problem

Standard additive explanation methods (e.g., SHAP) are restricted to scalar outcomes and cannot capture the non-additive, time-indexed structure of survival and hazard functions. Feature interactions in machine learning survival models have remained largely unquantified, with no principled decomposition that separates time-dependent from time-independent effects or characterizes how transformations between prediction scales (log-hazard → hazard → survival) induce spurious interactions absent on the additive log-hazard scale.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a trained survival model (GBSA, CoxPH, or DeepHit), a single individual's feature vector, a set of time points of interest, and a choice of reference distribution (marginal/interventional or conditional/observational). Outputs are not single attribution values but continuous curves over time: individual effect curves, pairwise interaction effect curves, and a partition of all feature subsets into time-dependent vs. time-independent components. For multi-modal inputs (e.g., histopathology patches + clinical features), the output is a network graph where node sizes encode individual effects and edge weights encode pairwise interactions at a chosen timepoint.

## Method

SurvFD generalizes functional decomposition to survival prediction functions by partitioning the power set of features into time-dependent and time-independent subsets, yielding an additive decomposition F(t|x) = f_∅(t) + Σ f_M(t|x) + Σ f_M(x). The paper proves that on the log-hazard scale, SurvFD recovers ground-truth interactions exactly under linearity or additivity assumptions (Thm. 3.2), but a single time-dependent interaction can propagate downward to lower-order subsets (Thm. 3.3), and the exponential transformation to hazard/survival scales induces both downward and upward propagation plus spurious higher-order interactions (Cor. 3.4, Prop. 3.5). SurvSHAP-IQ operationalizes this by extending n-Shapley values (grounded in the Shapley interaction index) to time-indexed value functions, computing discrete derivatives Δ_K(M) at each timepoint to obtain axiomatic interaction attributions up to order k. Practical computation uses kernel regression, Monte Carlo, SVARM, or permutation approximators extended to the survival setting, with a time-dependent local accuracy metric for evaluation.

## Results

On simulated data (10 scenarios, n=1,000), SurvSHAP-IQ achieves near-perfect local accuracy: below 0.001 for survival functions and below 0.00001 for hazard and log-hazard decompositions of ground-truth functions; for model-predicted survival functions (CoxPH, GBSA), local accuracy remains below 0.015. On the uveal melanoma GBSA model (100 trees, depth 4, 9 features), the validation C-index is 0.758. In the scalability benchmark across four datasets (10–16 features), the regression-based (kernel) approximator is the most faithful once the sampling budget exceeds the number of interaction effects, while Monte Carlo, SVARM, and Permutation methods show smaller standard errors at low budgets (trading bias for variance).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark SurvSHAP-IQ against competing interaction-explanation methods (e.g., SHAP-IQ, KernelSHAP-IQ, SVARM-IQ) in a head-to-head accuracy comparison; instead, it extends those approximators to the survival setting and compares them on scalability. In the scalability benchmark (Fig. 7, Fig. B.12) on four datasets with 10–16 features, the kernel (regression-based) approximator achieves the lowest error once the budget exceeds 2^6, while sampling-based methods (Monte Carlo, SVARM, Permutation) exhibit smaller standard errors at low budgets. For model-level comparison on simulated scenario (8), GBSA (C-index 0.70, IBS 0.15) outperforms CoxPH (C-index 0.66, IBS 0.16), and GBSA's SurvSHAP-IQ attribution curves more closely match the ground-truth survival attributions. The multi-modal TCGA-BRCA experiment (990 patients, 6 WSI patches + 8 clinical features) demonstrates that SurvSHAP-IQ recovers clinically established interactions (e.g., N0 × lumpectomy) and reveals that interaction structures differ between the survival probability and PMF prediction scales, empirically extending Prop. 3.5 beyond the multiplicative-hazards assumption.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the functional decomposition (Hoeffding, 1948; Hooker, 2004) and Shapley interaction quantification (Bordt & von Luxburg, 2023; Fumagalli et al., 2023) frameworks from scalar prediction targets to time-indexed survival functions. Prior survival explanation methods (SurvSHAP(t), SurvLIME, GradSHAP(t)) quantify only first-order feature attributions, while the only prior FD in survival (Huang et al., 2000) is restricted to the log-hazard scale. The key conceptual departure is formalizing how the exponential transformation from log-hazard to hazard/survival induces spurious interactions and time-dependence, and providing the first axiomatic interaction estimator (SurvSHAP-IQ) that operates directly on survival predictions across multiple scales.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical result that even a linear CoxPH model with no specified interactions produces non-zero higher-order effects on the hazard and survival scales (Prop. 3.5) has direct practical implications: clinicians and practitioners relying on additive explanations for survival models are systematically missing interaction structure that is an artifact of the prediction scale. The framework also clarifies when additive explanations are valid (log-hazard, independent features, linear/additive G) versus when they fail, providing a principled diagnostic. Open questions include scaling to high-dimensional genomics (exponential growth in interaction terms), extending theory beyond the multiplicative hazard assumption to competing risks and cumulative incidence functions, and developing effective visualizations for time-varying interaction networks.
