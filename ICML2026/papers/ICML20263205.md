## Problem

Latent categorical variables in deep learning models are typically parameterized by softmax, which induces a dense Fisher Information Matrix and thus geometric distortions that degrade gradient-based optimization. While prior work has focused on improving gradient estimators (REINFORCE, Gumbel-Softmax, control variates), the parameterization function itself has been largely overlooked from an information-geometric perspective. This paper asks whether a different parameterization can intrinsically flatten the statistical manifold and make standard gradient descent more effective.

## Input / Output

The method takes an unnormalized score vector s ∈ R^K (produced by an upstream neural network) and outputs a categorical probability vector p ∈ Δ^(K−1) over K classes. Each score s_i is mapped through an activation function a: R → [0,1] to a binary probability at a unique node in a balanced binary tree of depth H = log₂(K); the final categorical probability for class k is the product of binary probabilities along the root-to-leaf path identified by k's binary representation. The natural activation ν(x) is a piecewise sine function with parameters C (center, set to 0) and A (width, set to 2π to match sigmoid's slope at zero), requiring no additional hyperparameter tuning.

## Method

Catnat replaces the single softmax layer with a hierarchical tree of binary decisions: each score s_i corresponds to a node at some hierarchy level h, and the categorical probability for class k is the product of a(s_i) or (1−a(s_i)) along the path from root to the leaf for k. The key theoretical result (Theorem 4.2) proves that this parameterization yields a diagonal Fisher Information Matrix, in contrast to softmax's dense FIM with off-diagonal entries −p_i·p_j. A further 'natural activation' ν(x) = (1+sin(π(x−C)/A))/2 is proposed so that the diagonal FIM entries become P(a_i)·(π/A)², eliminating dependence on the local score s_i (Corollary 4.3). This diagonal structure means the natural gradient reduces to a coordinate-wise rescaling of the ordinary gradient, which adaptive optimizers like Adam approximate naturally, and the pulled-back FIM with respect to network parameters loses all cross-score coupling terms. The approach is a drop-in replacement for softmax, compatible with Gumbel-Softmax, REINFORCE, and other standard training techniques, and is validated across graph structure learning, categorical VAEs, and Atari RL.

## Results

On the categorical VAE (MNIST, N=10, K=32), catnat with natural activation achieves 96.9±0.4 NLL, outperforming softmax (98.6±0.7) and sparsemax (101.3±0.8). In Graph Structure Learning, the natural activation recovers the true latent parameters with MAE on θ of 0.0023–0.0061 across entropy settings, versus 0.0077–0.0146 for softmax. In Reinforcement Learning, catnat with natural activation yields 2164±533 episodic return on Seaquest versus 1875±312 for softmax, and 406±34 versus 398±25 on Breakout.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Catnat (both sigmoid and natural activation variants) consistently outperforms both the softmax and sparsemax baselines across all three experimental domains and all tested configurations. In the VAE experiments (Table 3), catnat achieves 1.5–4.7 NLL lower than softmax and 3–8 NLL lower than sparsemax across the 18 (N, K, dataset) settings, with the gap widening as K increases. In Graph Structure Learning (Table 2), the natural activation reduces MAE on θ by roughly 30–80% relative to softmax (e.g., 0.0023 vs 0.0108 at θ*=0.9) and also improves ES loss and point-prediction errors. In RL (Table 4), catnat with natural activation improves episodic return by ~15% on Seaquest (2164 vs 1875) and ~2% on Breakout (406 vs 398) over softmax. Between the two catnat variants, the natural activation ν slightly edges out the sigmoid σ in most settings but the two are statistically equivalent overall, confirming that the hierarchical structure itself (diagonal FIM) is the primary source of the gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper draws on information geometry (Amari, 1998; Amari & Douglas, 1998) to analyze the softmax parameterization of categorical distributions, identifying its dense Fisher Information Matrix as a source of geometric distortion in gradient-based optimization. Structurally, catnat generalizes hierarchical classifiers (Morin & Bengio, 2005; Mnih & Hinton, 2008) that decomposed multiclass prediction into binary splits for computational efficiency, but here the decomposition is motivated by the desire to produce a diagonal FIM rather than to reduce prediction cost. The paper positions itself as complementary to the large body of work on gradient estimators for discrete variables (REINFORCE, Gumbel-Softmax, REBAR, etc.), changing the parameterization function rather than the estimator, and is explicitly compatible with those techniques.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Catnat offers a simple, drop-in replacement for softmax in any architecture with categorical latent variables, requiring no changes to the training pipeline and remaining compatible with standard stabilization techniques. The consistent (if modest) gains across three diverse domains—graph structure learning, VAEs, and Atari RL—suggest the benefit is a general property of the diagonal FIM rather than an artifact of a specific setup. Open questions include whether the approach extends to other distribution families (continuous, mixed), how the relative advantage scales with action-space size or model capacity, and whether application-specific state-of-the-art methods can be improved with minimal effort. A key limitation is that the diagonal FIM is local to the categorical scores, not the full network parameters, so the geometric benefit is partial rather than complete.
