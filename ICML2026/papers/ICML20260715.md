## Problem

Diffusion models frequently produce hallucinated samples with semantic inconsistencies (e.g., extra fingers, mixed-up objects) due to trajectories traversing low-density valleys between data modes during sampling. Existing inference-time guidance methods like CFG apply a single scalar magnification to the conditional-unconditional residual in a geometry-agnostic manner, ignoring the local directional structure of the data manifold and potentially distorting the denoising trajectory. The paper asks: which directional components of the score update actually carry semantic structure, and can selectively amplifying them reduce hallucinations without extra network evaluations?

## Input / Output

TAG is a training-free, plug-and-play inference-time modification that operates on the solver update at each denoising step of any pretrained diffusion backbone (SD v1.5/2.1/XL/3, Qwen-Image, Wan2.2). It consumes the same inputs as the base model (text prompts for conditional generation, empty prompts for unconditional, or reference images for I2I/I23D) and produces the same outputs (512×512 or 1024×1024 images, 49-frame video clips, or 3D shapes) without requiring additional denoising evaluations or architectural changes. The only added computation is a per-step vector decomposition (normalization, dot product, subtraction) on the latent tensor, adding roughly 9.4% latency overhead compared to the baseline.

## Method

TAG decomposes the single-step solver increment Δ_{k+1} into a radial component (parallel to x_{k+1}, governing noise-level progression) and a tangential component (orthogonal to x_{k+1}, governing within-manifold refinement) using the projectors P_M = x̂x̂ᵀ and P_M^⊥ = I − P_M. It then amplifies only the tangential component by a factor η ≥ 1 while leaving the radial component unchanged, yielding the update x_k = x_{k+1} + P_M Δ_{k+1} + η P_M^⊥ Δ_{k+1}. A first-order Taylor analysis (Theorem 4.1) proves this monotonically increases the local log-likelihood gain because the DDIM coefficient α̃_k ≤ 0, so the tangential amplification term contributes a non-negative quadratic form ‖P_M^⊥ ε_θ‖². For classifier-free guidance, TAG additionally extracts the tangential residual g_k^⊥ = P_M^⊥(ε_c − ε_u) between conditional and unconditional scores, projects the conditional prediction onto span(g_k^⊥), and adds this aligned tangential term to the standard CFG update, reconciling conditional-unconditional incoherence that is fundamentally tangential in nature.

## Results

On COCO 2014 conditional generation, TAG consistently improves FID across all Stable Diffusion backbones without additional NFEs: SDv1.5 (33.49→26.61), SDv2.1 (26.12→21.59), SDXL (29.28→28.53), and SD3 (29.02→27.54). Unconditional generation shows even larger FID gains (e.g., SDv1.5: 58.41→46.20; SDXL: 119.14→90.71). Against geometry-aware CFG baselines on COCO@10K, TAG_cfg achieves the lowest FID (19.29) among TCFG (20.72) and APG (19.52), though APG yields a slightly higher CLIPScore (26.71 vs. 26.23). On T2I-CompBench Spatial-Val300, TAG_cfg improves 2D Spatial from 0.1857 to 0.1980 and ImageReward from 0.800 to 0.911 on SDXL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PAG (Ahn et al., 2024), stacking TAG on top yields consistent but modest gains: FID improves from 53.72 to 52.61 (unconditional SDv1.5) and from 30.24 to 30.22 (conditional SDXL), confirming plug-and-play compatibility. Against SEG (Hong, 2024), TAG+SEG shows a larger FID improvement (47.69→42.71 unconditional), suggesting TAG complements attention-smoothing guidance well. Against geometry-aware CFG methods, TAG_cfg achieves the lowest FID (19.29) versus TCFG (20.72) and APG (19.52) on COCO@10K, but APG edges out TAG_cfg on CLIPScore (26.71 vs. 26.23), indicating a trade-off between distributional fidelity and text-image alignment. Against stronger ODE solvers on ImageNet, TAG standalone (FID 52.83) outperforms DPM++ (55.40) but trails UniPC (50.82); however, UniPC+TAG achieves the best FID (48.34), demonstrating that TAG is complementary to solver improvements. Against SSG (Zhang et al., 2026), TAG+SSG improves FID from 49.02 to 48.53 (unconditional) and ImageReward from 0.449 to 0.460 (conditional), confirming orthogonality to token-swap guidance.

## Relation to Prior Work

TAG builds on the score-based diffusion SDE/ODE framework (Song et al., 2021b) and classifier-free guidance (Ho & Salimans, 2021), but departs from the dominant residual-scaling paradigm by operating directly on the geometry of the single-step solver update rather than on the cond-uncond score difference. It is conceptually distinct from CFG-specific projection methods (APG, TCFG), nonlinear CFG corrections (Characteristic Guidance), and task-specific manifold constraints (MPGD, SDDM), which all modify the guidance signal itself; TAG instead decomposes the base solver increment into radial and tangential components relative to the iso-noise manifold and amplifies only the tangential part. The theoretical grounding draws on Tweedie's formula and the Gaussian annulus theorem to justify why the tangential direction encodes semantic refinement while the radial direction is governed by the noise schedule.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TAG offers a practically attractive option for diffusion practitioners: a training-free, architecture-agnostic, plug-and-play refinement with only ~9% latency overhead (vs. >100% for attention-based methods like PAG) that consistently improves FID, CMMD, and human-preference metrics across SD1.5/2.1/XL/3, Qwen-Image, and Wan2.2 video. The geometric framing—hallucinations as off-manifold drift corrected by tangential amplification—provides a unifying lens that could inform future trajectory-level analyses. Key limitations include the purely local first-order guarantee (no full-trajectory characterization), the heuristic nature of η selection and timestep windowing, and the simplification of the data manifold to a spherical shell, which may not capture complex multi-modal geometry.
