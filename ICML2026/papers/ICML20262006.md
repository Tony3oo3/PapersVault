## Problem

Pre-trained VLMs map inputs to single fixed points in a high-dimensional embedding space, providing no intrinsic mechanism to quantify epistemic uncertainty (model ignorance). Existing epistemic UQ approaches for VLMs—deep ensembles, Monte Carlo dropout, or probabilistic adapters—are either computationally prohibitive at scale or yield unstable, batch-dependent estimates, leaving a gap for a scalable, intrinsic confidence metric.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262006/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a frozen VLM's ℓ2-normalized embedding (512-d for CLIP ViT-B/32, 768-d for SigLIP ViT-B/16) together with a binary modality indicator (image or text), and outputs a scalar epistemic uncertainty score U_ep(x) = −log p(z|c). Training requires a proxy dataset of 50K–1M image-caption pairs distinct from any downstream task, making the uncertainty estimate task-agnostic. At inference, only 5 ODE integration steps are needed, adding ~0.55 GFLOPs of overhead beyond the backbone forward pass.

## Method

REPVLM learns a single modality-conditioned vector field v_t(z, c; φ) via Conditional Riemannian Flow Matching that transports a uniform base distribution on the hypersphere S^{d-1} to the empirical embedding distribution for each modality. The key geometric choice is geodesic (slerp) interpolation between base and target points, ensuring the probability path respects the intrinsic curvature of the ℓ2-normalized embedding space rather than cutting through its interior. The network is a 6-block residual architecture with Adaptive Layer Normalation conditioned on sinusoidal time encoding and a learnable modality embedding, with outputs projected onto the tangent space at each point. At inference, the log-density is recovered by integrating the Riemannian divergence of the learned vector field along the reverse ODE, using a manifold-constrained Hutchinson trace estimator to avoid O(d) backward passes. The negative log-density then serves as the epistemic uncertainty score, with the theoretical justification linking low embedding density to high parameter-posterior covariance via a three-link chain (Jacobian norm → data density → embedding density).

## Results

Using a frozen CLIP ViT-B/32 backbone trained on Conceptual Captions, REPVLM achieves near-perfect Spearman correlation (S ≥ 0.988) on all standard benchmarks and S = 1.000 across all nine distribution-shift cells (ObjectNet, ImageNet-R, ImageNet-Sketch × 3 proxies), making it the only method to do so. It attains the best Acc@90 on ImageNet-1K (0.733), CIFAR-100 (0.873), and ImageNet-R (0.885), with only a 0.55 GFLOP inference overhead beyond the backbone (9.80 GFLOPs total vs. 92.50 for MC-Dropout). Under the noisier LAION proxy, margins narrow but REPVLM remains the only method maintaining S = 1.000 on all shift benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

REPVLM outperforms all five baselines on Acc@90 for ImageNet-1K, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet-R under the Conceptual Captions proxy, with the largest margin over ProbVLM (0.733 vs. 0.661 on ImageNet-1K) and GMM (0.873 vs. 0.812 on CIFAR-100). Conformity is the closest competitor on Spearman (matching S = 1.000 on most cells) but trails on Acc@90 (e.g., 0.704 vs. 0.733 on ImageNet-1K) and collapses on CIFAR-100/DataComp (S = 0.042). ProbVLM is the most unstable baseline, with Spearman degrading to 0.290 on DataComp and near-zero under LAION on shift benchmarks. Embedding Norm, while simple, loses discriminative power under distribution shift (S drops to 0.430 on ImageNet-R). MC-Dropout achieves competitive Spearman on in-distribution data but is 9.5× more expensive (92.50 vs. 9.80 GFLOPs) and exhibits high variance on ObjectNet (S = 0.743 ± 0.472). GMM is the least reliable, producing negative Spearman on Food101 (S = −0.479) and ImageNet-Sketch (S = −0.993), indicating its Euclidean parametric assumption is fundamentally mismatched to the hyperspherical embedding geometry.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Riemannian Flow Matching (Chen & Lipman, 2024) from a generative modeling tool to a density-estimation mechanism for uncertainty quantification, applying it to the hyperspherical geometry of ℓ₂-normalized VLM embeddings. It departs from the dominant UQ-for-VLMs literature, which predominantly targets aleatoric uncertainty via probabilistic embeddings (ProbVLM, PFE, PCME+) or relies on expensive parameter-space sampling (deep ensembles, MC dropout), by instead estimating the embedding density itself and using negative log-density as an epistemic uncertainty proxy. The theoretical chain linking Bayesian epistemic uncertainty to embedding density (via Jacobian norms and data density) is novel, though the authors explicitly tag each link as an assumption rather than a formal identity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

REPVLM provides a practical, single-pass (5 ODE steps, ~0.55 GFLOPs overhead) method for epistemic UQ in frozen VLMs, filling a gap where existing approaches are either computationally prohibitive or batch-dependent. However, the method's applicability is bounded: it requires a proxy dataset representative of the pre-training domain, assumes ℓ₂-normalized embeddings from cosine-similarity-trained encoders, and the SigLIP results show degradation when the embedding geometry is shaped by multi-objective losses beyond pure alignment-uniformity. The conflation of rarity with ignorance also raises fairness concerns for underrepresented groups in automated curation. Extending the density-based framework to encoders with richer geometries or non-spherical embedding spaces remains an open direction.
