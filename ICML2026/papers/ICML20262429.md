## Problem

Reinforcement learning for LLM reasoning typically distributes sequence-level rewards uniformly across all tokens, blurring the distinction between pivotal reasoning steps and routine elaboration. The internal structure of how LLMs organize their reasoning—when they plan, when they anchor, and which tokens carry disproportionate downstream influence—remains opaque, leaving no principled signal for targeted credit assignment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262429/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text prompt and its autoregressively generated response, then performs a single auxiliary forward pass through a dedicated attention-retaining model (actor_attn) to extract full attention maps from five evenly spaced middle layers (since flash attention in vLLM/Megatron discards them). From these maps it computes per-token WAAD and FAI scores, which are used to select top-quantile preplan and anchor tokens. The output is a set of per-token scaling coefficients γ_t that multiply the signed GRPO advantages before the standard clipped policy-gradient update, adding only 2–3% wall-clock overhead relative to generation.

## Method

The paper first classifies attention heads into local-focused (small attention-weighted mean backward distance) and global-focused (large distance) groups, then defines two metrics: WAAD, which measures how far a token looks back within a clipped window (peaks mark chunk-boundary preplan tokens with high entropy), and FAI, which measures the average attention a token receives from subsequent positions (peaks mark anchor tokens with broad downstream influence). The key empirical finding is a recurring 'preplan-and-anchor' rhythm: WAAD spikes (long-range consultation at semantic boundaries) are followed by or coincide with high-FAI anchor tokens that are persistently revisited by future positions. Building on this, three RL credit-assignment strategies rescale per-token advantages as Ã_t = A_t · γ_t: (1) local-chunk credit amplifies tokens at WAAD peak-valley transitions, (2) global-anchor credit amplifies top-FAI tokens, and (3) coupled rhythm credit combines both and back-allocates a fraction α of the amplification bonus from locally-dominated anchors to their preceding introductory tokens, promoting coherent chunk-level credit rather than overfitting a single position. All shaping signals are detached from gradients and applied to both positive and negative advantages within the standard GRPO objective.

## Results

On Qwen3-8B-Base (1K context), the coupled rhythm credit strategy achieves an average of 43.2 across five math benchmarks (AIME24, AIME25, AMC23, MATH500, OlympiadBench), improving over the GRPO baseline (39.4) by +3.8 points, with the largest gains on AIME25 (+5.0) and AMC23 (+6.3). On the Countdown puzzle, coupled rhythm credit reaches 63.1% accuracy versus 52.6% for GRPO (+10.5). Gains are consistent across model scales (Qwen3-4B: +2.1 avg; Qwen3-8B: +3.8 avg) and context lengths (8K: +2.5 avg), and extend to other model families (Llama3.2-3B: +18.8% relative; DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B: +4.36% relative).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The coupled rhythm credit strategy outperforms all four compared baselines on the Qwen3-8B-Base math reasoning suite (avg 43.2). Against GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), the primary baseline, it gains +3.8 points on average, with the largest per-benchmark improvements on AIME25 (+5.0) and AMC23 (+6.3). Against the high-entropy token selection method (Wang et al., 2025b, avg 40.9), it leads by +2.3 points, indicating that attention-derived preplan/anchor signals identify more decisive positions than entropy alone. Against CAPO (Xie et al., 2025, avg 41.4), which uses verifiable generative credit assignment, it leads by +1.8 points. Against ThinkPRM-1.5B (Khalifa et al., 2025), a process reward model approach, it also achieves superior average performance. Notably, the simpler random-credit and high-entropy-credit ablations provide only marginal or no improvement over GRPO, confirming that the specific attention-derived structure (not just any reweighting) drives the gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) as its RL backbone and departs from prior token-level credit assignment work (entropy-based selection in Wang et al., 2025b; critical-token identification in Vassoyan et al., 2025; CAPO in Xie et al., 2025) by deriving its reweighting signals from attention dynamics rather than external heuristics or predictive uncertainty. It connects to mechanistic interpretability literature on iteration heads, receiver heads, and attention sinks (Cabannes et al., 2024; Bogdan et al., 2025; Xiao et al., 2023), but repurposes these descriptive findings into a concrete training recipe: two new metrics (WAAD, FAI) computed from a single auxiliary forward pass identify 'preplan' and 'anchor' tokens whose advantages are amplified during policy updates. The key conceptual departure is framing attention patterns as a recurring 'preplan-and-anchor rhythm' that can be exploited for process-aware credit assignment within standard RLVR pipelines, rather than treating attention as a post-hoc explanation tool.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a low-overhead (2–3% wall-clock), drop-in modification to existing GRPO/RLVR pipelines that consistently improves reasoning performance across model families (Qwen3, Llama3, DeepSeek-R1-Distill) and scales (0.6B–8B), suggesting the attention-derived signal is architecture-agnostic. The preplan-and-anchor framing provides a more interpretable account of *where* in a trajectory credit should concentrate, potentially improving sample efficiency in long-horizon reasoning where uniform sequence-level rewards are most wasteful. Limitations include modest absolute gains on the hardest benchmarks (AIME24/25), attenuated rhythm in knowledge-retrieval tasks, and the acknowledged caveat that attention maps are filtered signals rather than causal explanations of reasoning. Open questions include whether the rhythm persists in much larger models, whether the specific head-grouping and middle-layer heuristics are optimal, and whether the approach transfers to non-verifiable or multi-turn agentic settings.
