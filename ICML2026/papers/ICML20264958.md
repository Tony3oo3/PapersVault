## Problem

Open-Vocabulary Aerial Detection (OVAD) and Remote Sensing Visual Grounding (RSVG) are studied as separate paradigms with complementary but incomplete capabilities: OVAD supports multi-target detection but only at coarse category-level semantics, while RSVG handles complex referring expressions but is structurally restricted to single-target localization. No existing framework can simultaneously accept multi-granular textual inputs (from simple category names to complex spatial descriptions) and retrieve all matching targets in a single inference pass.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264958/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an aerial image, a set of text queries (each being either a category name or a full referring expression), and a pre-extracted fine-grained attribute set (decomposed offline via an LLM into typed attributes like color, spatial relation, state). Text is encoded offline with frozen SigLIPv2, so at inference only the image passes through the network. Outputs are dual-granularity: for each predicted box, the model returns a query-level similarity score (matching the holistic expression) and per-attribute similarity scores, enabling users to filter by either the full description or flexible attribute combinations via a hierarchical mapping matrix.

## Method

OTA-Det extends the RT-DETR/DEIMv2 architecture from closed-set to open-text detection through two key strategies. First, a Task Reformulation Strategy restructures RSVG from single-target localization into a joint classification-localization problem and aggregates all expressions per image into a dense query set (Image-Level Annotation Aggregation), enabling unified contrastive supervision across OVAD and RSVG datasets. Second, a Dense Semantic Alignment Strategy introduces an LLM-based Attribute-Level Data Decomposition that parses expressions into typed attribute sets, a Unified Correspondence Matrix that encodes multi-label object-to-semantic correspondences, and a Decoupled Multi-Granular Head that computes independent cosine-similarity logits for holistic queries and individual attributes, optimized with a Matchability-Aware Loss that modulates supervision by IoU. The image encoder is DINOv3-STAs and the text encoder is frozen SigLIPv2, keeping the added inference overhead to only 4 FPS over the closed-set base, yielding 34 FPS real-time performance.

## Results

OTA-Det-L achieves state-of-the-art performance across all six benchmarks: 93.1 AP50 on DIOR, 52.0 mAP on DOTA, 31.0 mAP on LAE-80C for OVAD, and 86.5 / 85.1 / 53.9 Acc@0.5 on OPT-RSVG / DIOR-RSVG / AerialVG for RSVG. The smallest variant (OTA-Det-S) maintains real-time inference at 34 FPS on a single RTX 4090, roughly 3× faster than LAE-DINO (10 FPS) and 5× faster than AerialVG (7 FPS). On the challenging AerialVG benchmark with complex spatial referring expressions, OTA-Det-M surpasses the previous best (AerialVG, 50.0) by 4.9 points under task-specific training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On OVAD benchmarks, OTA-Det-L outperforms the strongest dedicated OVAD method LAE-DINO by +7.6 AP50 on DIOR, +5.2 mAP on DOTA, and +10.8 mAP on LAE-80C, while also being 3× faster (28 vs. 10 FPS). On RSVG benchmarks, OTA-Det-L surpasses the best dedicated grounding method LPVA by +8.5 Acc@0.5 on OPT-RSVG and +2.8 on DIOR-RSVG, and beats AerialVG (ICCV'25) by +3.9 on the AerialVG benchmark. When existing OVAD detectors (G-DINO-T, LAE-DINO) are adapted to RSVG via the Naive Reformulation strategy and jointly trained on OTA-Mix, OTA-Det still leads on all six benchmarks, confirming that the gains come from the dense semantic alignment strategy rather than merely joint training. Against remote sensing VLMs (VHM, EarthGPT, GeoGround) on DIOR-RSVG, OTA-Det-L achieves 85.1 Acc@0.5 versus 77.7 for GeoGround, while maintaining real-time inference at 28 FPS compared to the non-real-time VLMs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the RT-DETR/DEIMv2 real-time detection architecture and extends it from closed-set to open-text detection, while drawing on the OVAD line (LAE-DINO, G-DINO, OVA-DETR) for contrastive vision-language alignment and the RSVG line (TransVG, LPVA, AerialVG) for expression comprehension. Conceptually, it departs from prior unified detection-grounding efforts (GLIP, Grounding DINO, YOLO-World) that unify detection with *phrase grounding* (localizing individual noun phrases), instead unifying OVAD with REC-style visual grounding where holistic referring expressions with spatial relationships must be understood. The key methodological departure is the task reformulation that converts RSVG from localization-only to joint classification-localization, combined with an attribute-level dense alignment strategy that addresses what the authors term 'semantic pseudo-alignment'—a problem not explicitly tackled by prior aerial grounding methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to simultaneously support category-level multi-target detection and complex expression-based grounding in aerial imagery at real-time speed (34 FPS), addressing a practical gap where users naturally mix simple category queries with rich referring expressions. The identification and mitigation of semantic pseudo-alignment (where models bind features to whole sentences without understanding constituent attributes) is a meaningful conceptual contribution, validated by the dramatic Attr-Align drop (44%→14%) when dense alignment is removed. However, the training data scale (<0.1M grounding annotations) is orders of magnitude smaller than natural-image counterparts (20M+ for Grounding DINO 1.5), and the authors acknowledge that attribute-guided feature enhancement and attribute-importance weighting remain open. The framework's practical value for UAV and remote sensing deployment is significant, but broader impact depends on whether the unified formulation scales with data.
