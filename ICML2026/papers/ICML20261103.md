## Problem

While machine unlearning of negative examples has been shown empirically to improve preference alignment in LLMs, no systematic quantitative framework exists to measure how unlearning specific samples affects alignment performance. Critically, it remains unclear which negative examples should be unlearned and at what intensity to maximize alignment, as indiscriminate unlearning of all negative samples does not guarantee improvement.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261103/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of negative preference samples (e.g., harmful or low-quality responses from SafeRLHF, UltraFeedback, or HaluEval) along with the parameters of a pre-trained LLM (LLaMA2-7B, LLaMA3-8B, or Qwen2.5-14B). It outputs the updated model parameters after selective unlearning, a sparse subset of negative samples identified for removal, and per-sample unlearning weights constrained to the probability simplex. The L_{1/2}-norm sparsity term ensures only a small fraction of the candidate negative set receives non-zero weight, making the unlearning set compact.

## Method

U2A formulates preference alignment via unlearning as a bi-level optimization: the inner problem minimizes a weighted gradient-ascent unlearning loss plus parameter regularization over model parameters, while the outer problem maximizes the PA reward objective over the unlearning weight vector. A first-order Taylor expansion (Proposition 4.2) yields a closed-form influence score for each sample as the negative inner product of the PA gradient and the unlearning gradient, revealing that some negative samples actually degrade alignment. The algorithm iteratively selects samples by marginal gain (a cone-constrained matching pursuit variant) and updates weights via mirror descent with negative entropy, producing a closed-form softmax-like reweighting rule. An L_{1/2}-norm sparsity regularizer is chosen over L_0 or L_1 because it is both strictly convex and smooth for non-negative weights, balancing sparsity control with tractable optimization.

## Results

On UltraFeedback with LLaMA3-8B, GA&U2A achieves a Reward-V of 1.105, surpassing DPO (-0.277) and PPO (-1.302), while also attaining the highest GPT4-WR (21.33% vs. 19.08% and 19.41%). On SafeRLHF with LLaMA3, GA&U2A reduces ASR-K from 0.982 (GA) to 0.246 and improves Reward-V from -18.011 to -7.609, substantially outperforming the Retrain baseline (-17.911). Across all three models (LLaMA2, LLaMA3, Qwen2.5-14B), U2A variants consistently outperform their corresponding unlearning baselines (GA, GradDiff, NPO) on PA metrics while maintaining competitive unlearning performance (MIA, PPL).

## Baselines & Comparisons

U2A is compared against four unlearning baselines (Retrain, GA, GradDiff, NPO) and two PA baselines (PPO, DPO). Against unlearning baselines, U2A variants consistently win on PA metrics: on SafeRLHF/LLaMA3, GA&U2A improves Reward-V by 10.4 points over GA (-7.609 vs. -18.011) and 10.3 over Retrain, while reducing ASR-K from 0.982 to 0.246. Against PA baselines on UltraFeedback, GA&U2A on LLaMA3-8B beats both DPO and PPO on Reward-V (1.105 vs. -0.277 and -1.302) and GPT4-WR (21.33% vs. 19.08% and 19.41%), though DPO edges ahead on LC-WR (25.30% vs. 24.79%). On LLaMA2-7B and Qwen2.5-14B, U2A is competitive but does not uniformly surpass DPO/PPO on all metrics, which the authors attribute to the absence of positive-sample guidance. NPO&U2A shows the weakest gains among U2A variants, suggesting that the choice of underlying unlearning method matters for the final PA outcome.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously independent research threads—machine unlearning (MU) for LLMs and preference alignment (PA)—by formulating a bi-level optimization framework that quantifies how unlearning individual negative samples affects PA performance. Theoretically, it draws on influence functions (Koh & Liang, 2017) to derive closed-form expressions for sample impact, and on cone-constrained matching pursuit (Locatello et al., 2017) for the incremental sample-selection algorithm. Empirically, it extends existing MU baselines (gradient ascent, GradDiff, NPO) by adding a learned reweighting mechanism, and positions the resulting U2A framework as a negative-sample-only alternative to RLHF-based PA methods (PPO, DPO) that require costly positive preference data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is clear for low-resource alignment: U2A avoids the need for large-scale positive preference annotations and the computational instability of full RLHF, operating at fine-tuning-level cost. However, the paper's own experiments reveal a key limitation—U2A does not consistently outperform DPO/PPO, particularly on LLaMA2 with the noisier SafeRLHF dataset, suggesting that negative-only unlearning cannot fully replace positive-sample guidance for learning what to generate. Open questions include how to optimally combine selective unlearning with partial positive supervision, whether the Hessian-free assumption holds at larger scales, and whether the marginal-gain-based selection generalizes beyond the tested 7B–14B model range.
