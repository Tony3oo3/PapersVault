## Problem

Existing visual privacy detection datasets suffer from limited scale (typically under 7K images), coarse-grained annotations, and narrow domain coverage that omits critical leakage sources like on-screen PII and fine-grained human attributes. This data gap forces models to rely on broad categories and prevents robust generalization to unconstrained environments such as live streaming, where small, camouflaged sensitive objects (e.g., verification codes, distant ID cards) must be detected in real time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261682/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (over half exceeding 1080p resolution) and live-streaming video frames, producing bounding boxes annotated with one of 33 fine-grained privacy classes spanning four domains: Human Presence (8 classes by age and environment), On-Screen PII (9 classes), Physical Identifiers (12 classes), and Location Indicators (4 classes). The dataset contains 100,000 images with over 190,000 object instances, featuring long-tailed class distributions, small normalized object sizes (<10% of image area), and high within-image scale disparity that challenge multi-scale detection.

## Method

Built on YOLOv10, the paper introduces a Frequency-Enhanced Mechanism (FEM) that extends the neck architecture from a purely spatial domain to a spatial-frequency dual-stream. The FDAF module applies per-channel DFT to high-level feature maps, then fuses the modulated spectrum back via IDFT with a residual connection and 1×1 convolution. An Adaptive Spectral Gating mechanism uses a learnable weight tensor with sigmoid activation as a soft band-pass filter, allowing the network to selectively amplify frequency components relevant to specific privacy object types (e.g., horizontal/vertical strokes for text). A Frequency-Consistency Loss (β=0.05) penalizes weighted Euclidean distance between predicted and ground-truth features in the frequency domain, with weights increasing linearly with frequency radius to prioritize high-frequency boundary fitting. Ablations show each component contributes incrementally: DFT fusion alone adds +2.2% AP, gating adds +2.4%, and the frequency loss adds +1.2%, yielding 58.6% AP on the image benchmark at 7.51ms latency.

## Results

On the VPD-100K image test set, YOLOv10L+our FEM achieves the best AP of 58.6%, AP50 of 73.4%, and AP_S (small objects) of 36.5%, with an F1-Score of 0.81 at only 7.51 ms latency (132 GFLOPs). The lightweight variant (YOLOv10s+our FEM) reaches 52.1% AP and 0.71 F1 at just 2.71 ms latency (26 GFLOPs). On the live-streaming video benchmark, the full model maintains 57.7% AP and 0.81 F1, while the small variant achieves 51.8% AP at 2.71 ms, enabling >130 FPS real-time processing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

YOLOv10L+our FEM outperforms the strongest baseline YOLOv10L by 4.8% AP (58.6 vs. 53.8), 3.8% AP₅₀ (73.4 vs. 69.6), and 2.9% AP_S (36.5 vs. 33.6), while also improving F1 from 0.73 to 0.81 at nearly identical latency (7.51 vs. 7.42 ms). Against the open-set detector Grounding-DINO, the proposed method gains 10.5% AP (58.6 vs. 48.1) with 16× lower latency (7.51 ms vs. 119.5 ms) and 3.5× fewer GFLOPs (132 vs. 464). The lightweight variant (YOLOv10s+FEM) improves over vanilla YOLOv10s by 5.8% AP (52.1 vs. 46.3) and 4.0% AP_S (30.1 vs. 26.1) at only 0.18 ms additional latency, making it competitive with heavier models like Gold-YOLO-L (52.7% AP) while being 4× faster. On the streaming video benchmark, the full model leads all baselines in AP (57.7%) and F1 (0.81), confirming that the frequency-enhanced mechanism generalizes from static images to dynamic video without architectural changes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the privacy-sensitive object detection line initiated by PrivacyAlert, DIPA, and BIV-Priv, which the authors identify as limited in scale (≤6.8K images), annotation granularity (coarse image-level tags), and domain coverage (missing on-screen PII and location indicators). Methodologically, it builds directly on YOLOv10's NMS-free detection architecture, inserting a frequency-domain attention fusion module and adaptive spectral gating into the neck to exploit the high-frequency texture cues that privacy-sensitive objects (tiny text, verification codes, small faces) depend on. The frequency-domain perspective draws on the broader observation that CNNs are biased toward texture and that DFT-based feature manipulation can amplify subtle structural details, but the specific combination of learnable spectral gating with a frequency-consistency loss for privacy detection is new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VPD-100K is a 15× scale jump over the largest prior privacy dataset (PrivacyAlert at 6.8K) and introduces the first comprehensive four-domain taxonomy with 33 fine-grained classes, making it a practical benchmark for the live-streaming privacy protection community. The frequency-enhanced mechanism achieves 7.51 ms latency at 130+ FPS, which is directly deployable in real-time streaming pipelines, and the 8.9% AP gain over the second-best baseline is meaningful for a task where small-object recall is critical. Open questions include whether the frequency modules transfer to non-YOLO backbones, whether the ethically reconstructed PII screenshots generalize to the full diversity of real user interfaces, and whether the long-tailed class distribution (e.g., passport vs. face) requires additional re-balancing strategies at inference time.
