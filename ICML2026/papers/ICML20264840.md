## Problem

Concept erasure in next-scale autoregressive (AR) image generation remains largely unexplored. Unlike diffusion models that generate at a fixed resolution, next-scale AR models compress semantic information into a limited number of tokens at early scales, causing severe entanglement between unsafe and unrelated semantics within individual tokens. This entanglement makes it difficult to precisely erase unsafe concepts without degrading general generative capability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264840/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a text prompt, an unsafe concept (e.g., 'nudity'), a corresponding safe concept (e.g., 'a person wearing clothes'), and a set of unrelated concepts (e.g., 'background, sky, beach'), the method produces a standard RGB image in which the unsafe concept has been suppressed. The method operates at inference time on the Infinity-2B next-scale AR model (which uses Binary Spherical Quantization with D independent bit channels per token), requiring two additional forward passes conditioned on the safe and unsafe concepts respectively, with an early-stop strategy that terminates these passes at an intermediate scale to limit overhead to ~9% latency increase.

## Method

ScaleErasure performs minimal intervention by selectively guiding predicted logits that are most relevant to the unsafe concept, operating across three orthogonal dimensions. At the scale level, guidance is restricted to intermediate scales (k_start=3 to k_end=8), skipping the first two scales that encode global structure and the last few that only refine details. At the token level, spatial locations are identified by computing cross-attention similarity scores between visual queries and projected keys for the unsafe concept versus unrelated concepts, then thresholding the softmax-normalized relevance. At the bit-channel level, channels are selected by comparing absolute logit differences between prompt-conditioned and unsafe-conditioned forward passes, isolating channels that encode unsafe semantics within each token. For selected logits, safe guidance (Eq. 6) replaces the prompt-conditioned term in the CFG formulation with the safe-conditioned term and subtracts the unsafe-conditioned term, explicitly redirecting activations away from the unsafe concept while an early-stop strategy at k_end keeps the two additional forward passes computationally affordable.

## Results

On the I2P nudity erasure benchmark, ScaleErasure achieves the lowest total NudeNet detection count of 182 (vs. 1042 for the base model and 296 for the next-best ESD-u), while simultaneously attaining the best FID (2.91) and CLIP score (30.68) on MS-COCO, indicating superior preservation of general generative capability. For copyrighted character erasure on SMP, ScaleErasure achieves the best averaged H_a score (8.62) across Pikachu and SpongeBob, with the lowest CLIP-E on SpongeBob (23.32) and competitive CLIP-E on Pikachu (22.48). The method incurs only a 9.01% latency increase over the base model (2.54s vs. 2.33s per sample) thanks to its early-stop strategy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ScaleErasure decisively outperforms all baselines on nudity erasure effectiveness, reducing the total NudeNet count from 1042 (base) to 182, a 5.7× improvement over the next-best ESD-u (296) and 2.6× over SLD-strong (474). On general generation quality, ScaleErasure achieves the best FID (2.91) and CLIP score (30.68), substantially outperforming fine-tuning baselines ESD-u (FID 6.41) and ESD-x (FID 8.81), as well as closed-form editing methods UCE (FID 13.51) and RECE (FID 21.59). SLD-medium preserves structure best (SD 48.65, B-PSNR 18.22) but erases far less effectively (744 total detections), while ScaleErasure achieves near-comparable preservation (SD 58.24, B-PSNR 17.27) with dramatically better erasure. For copyrighted erasure, ScaleErasure achieves the best H_a on SpongeBob (8.64) and is competitive on Pikachu (8.60 vs. RECE 9.33), yielding the best averaged H_a (8.62) across both characters, whereas UCE and RECE show inconsistent performance across targets. Overall, ScaleErasure provides the best trade-off between erasure effectiveness and generative fidelity, with no single baseline dominating across all metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the concept erasure literature—previously dominated by diffusion-model methods (ESD, UCE, RECE, SLD)—to the next-scale autoregressive (VAR/Infinity) paradigm, where semantic entanglement at low-resolution scales creates a fundamentally different challenge. It builds directly on VAR's coarse-to-fine multi-scale generation structure and Infinity's Binary Spherical Quantization bit channels, repurposing the CFG formulation into a safe-guidance mechanism that operates selectively across scales, tokens, and bit channels. Unlike prior inference-time methods that modulate a single denoising trajectory, ScaleErasure exploits the hierarchical scale structure to restrict intervention to an intermediate scale window and uses cross-attention contrastive scoring for spatial token localization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As next-scale AR models (Infinity, VAR) gain traction as an alternative to diffusion, this work provides the first practical safety mechanism tailored to their architecture, with only ~9% latency overhead and no parameter modification. The dramatic ablation showing FID collapsing from 2.91 to 73.11 without token-level selection underscores how critical selective intervention is under semantic entanglement—a problem absent in fixed-resolution diffusion. Open questions include whether the three-dimensional selection strategy generalizes to other AR tokenization schemes (e.g., VQ-based VAR), whether the manually specified safe/unrelated concept sets can be learned automatically, and whether the method remains effective against adaptive adversarial prompts beyond the two attack settings tested.
