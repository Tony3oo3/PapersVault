## Problem

Robot world models that condition on explicit action labels produce disjoint action representations across different embodiments, limiting cross-embodiment knowledge transfer and degrading performance when adapting to unseen robots. The paper asks whether learning a unified latent action space shared across heterogeneous embodiments (human hands, different robot arms) can enable a world model to scale positively with pretraining diversity and adapt more effectively to new robot forms.

## Input / Output

The model takes 256×256 RGB frames encoded by a frozen V-JEPA2 tokenizer and raw robot actions (e.g., 25D for the BFA setup: 16 finger joint angles + 3D end-effector position + 6D rotation), chunked into 5-step sequences to downsample observation frequency. At inference, a lightweight two-layer MLP action projector maps these raw actions into the 64-dimensional latent action space, and the forward dynamics model produces 8-frame future image predictions in V-JEPA2 embedding space. For planning, the model scores N candidate action sequences from a VLA by rolling out predictions and selecting the top-k whose final predicted frame is closest to a subgoal image embedding.

## Method

LAC-WM comprises four modules: an Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM) that encodes consecutive V-JEPA2 frame embeddings into a 512-dim latent action via causal and cross-attention layers, a Forward Dynamics Model (FDM) that predicts the next frame embedding conditioned on the current frame and latent action, a Motion Decoder that reconstructs explicit motion labels (end-effector poses, camera motion) from latent actions to ground the representation, and an Action Projector that maps raw robot actions into the latent space at inference. Training combines a self-supervised image-latent reconstruction loss (with cross-augmentation to prevent the IDM from encoding future information as a shortcut) and a supervised motion-decoding MSE loss. Pretraining is done jointly on cross-embodiment data (EgoDex human manipulation, Agibot bimanual humanoid, Droid single-arm Franka), and adaptation to an unseen embodiment uses a three-stage LoRA fine-tuning procedure (joint IDM+FDM, then action projector, then joint projector+FDM). The key finding is that this unified latent space scales positively with the number of pretraining embodiments, whereas explicit-action-conditioned baselines with separate encoders per embodiment show the opposite trend.

## Results

On the BFA dexterous manipulation benchmark, LAC-WM achieves an average task success rate of 22% (Unseen Instances: 25%, Unseen Categories: 18%), outperforming EAC-WM (15%) by 46.7% and VLA-mean (17%) by 29.0%. On the modified LIBERO benchmark with unseen objects and locations, LAC-WM reaches 92.0% average success rate versus 82.4% for EAC-WM (a 9.6-point absolute gain). For action-conditioned imagined rollouts, LAC-WM achieves the best image/video quality (PSNR 27.48, LPIPS 0.037, FVD 23.21 on Unseen Instances). Notably, LAC-WM's downstream performance scales positively with the number of pretraining embodiments, while EAC-WM's performance degrades as more embodiments are added.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is between LAC-WM (unified latent action space) and EAC-WM (disjoint explicit action encoders per embodiment), both pretrained on EgoDex, Agibot, and Droid then fine-tuned on the unseen BFA robot. LAC-WM wins decisively: it achieves 22% average S.R. vs. EAC-WM's 15% on BFA (a 46.7% relative improvement) and 92.0% vs. 82.4% on LIBERO (9.6-point absolute gain). Strikingly, EAC-WM actually underperforms the VLA-only baselines (VLA-mean: 17%, VLA-random: 15%), suggesting its disjoint action space actively harms cross-embodiment pretraining. LAC-WM also outperforms EAC-WM-S (trained from scratch, no pretraining) by 22.2% on S.R., confirming the value of cross-embodiment pretraining when a unified action space is used. On the scaling axis, LAC-WM's S.R. improves monotonically as pretraining embodiments increase (1→2→3), while EAC-WM's S.R. decreases, directly demonstrating that the unified latent space is the critical enabler of positive cross-embodiment scaling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two lines of work: latent action models (LAMs) that learn action representations from video (Ye et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2024; Schmidt & Jiang, 2024) and robot world models that predict future observations conditioned on actions (Gupta et al., 2022; Wu et al., 2024; Assran et al., 2025). Prior LAM work has focused on policy learning from unlabeled video, while robot world models have typically avoided cross-embodiment action conditioning during pretraining, relying instead on action-free video prediction or single-embodiment action spaces. LAC-WM departs from both by using labeled cross-embodiment data to learn a unified latent action space that conditions a forward dynamics model, and by providing the first systematic comparison between latent-action conditioning and explicit-action conditioning for cross-embodiment world models. The motion decoding loss (Nikulin et al., 2025; Liang et al., 2025) and action projector for inference-time use (Gao et al., 2025) are adopted from prior work but repurposed within a cross-embodiment world model setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that explicit-action-conditioned world models *degrade* in performance as more embodiments are added during pretraining—while latent-action-conditioned models improve—challenges the standard assumption that greater data diversity is always beneficial and provides a concrete mechanism (disjoint action representations) explaining why cross-embodiment transfer fails. This has direct practical implications for how the robotics community should structure pretraining data and action spaces for foundation world models. Key limitations include simulation-only evaluation (RoboCasa, LIBERO), low absolute success rates (22% on dexterous manipulation), and testing on only three pretraining embodiments plus one target embodiment. Open questions include whether the unified latent action space approach scales to truly large-scale unlabeled video pretraining, whether it generalizes to locomotion or navigation tasks with fundamentally different action structures, and whether real-world hardware validation confirms the simulation results.
