## Problem

The learning rate in SGD is notoriously expensive to tune via grid search, especially for large-scale models, yet standard convergence analyses show that overestimating the stepsize by a multiplicative factor ρ degrades the rate linearly in ρ for both fixed and inverse-square-root schedules. This paper asks whether annealing schedules (e.g., cosine decay) can break this linear dependence, providing a theoretical justification for their practical robustness under coarse grid searches.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a convex objective f accessed through an unbiased subgradient oracle with bounded second moment (Lipschitz case) or bounded variance (smooth case), a differentiable p-Lipschitz annealed schedule h:[0,1]→[0,1] (monotonically non-increasing, h(u)=0 iff u=1), and a base stepsize η that may overestimate the tuned value η_tu by a factor ρ≥1. The output is a last-iterate convergence bound of the form Rate_tu · inf_τ {H_h(0)/(ρH_h(τ)) + ρQ_h(τ)/Q_h(0)}, where H_h and Q_h are integral functionals of the schedule. Unlike standard analyses that unroll from x_1, the bound implicitly selects an optimal 'restart' fraction τ∈[0,1) that depends on ρ and the schedule's tail behavior.

## Method

The key insight is to analyze the suffix of SGD iterates starting from an intermediate point x_k where the stepsize has decayed sufficiently to absorb the initial overestimation, rather than unrolling the full trajectory from x_1. To handle the unknown optimal k, the authors switch from average-iterate to last-iterate analysis (building on Zamani & Glineur, 2025 and Liu & Zhou, 2024), so the final iterate x_{T+1} is independent of the choice of k. A technical bridge (Lemma 3) converts the discrete stepsize sums in the last-iterate bound into continuous integrals H_h(τ) and Q_h(τ) of the schedule, introducing only O(p/T) discretization error. The main theorem then minimizes the resulting two-term expression over τ, yielding sublinear ρ-dependence: ρ^{1/(2p+1)} for polynomial decay of degree p and ρ^{1/5} for cosine annealing, versus the unavoidable linear ρ dependence for fixed and 1/√t schedules (proven via explicit oscillation lower bounds in Section C). Experiments on synthetic logistic regression and CIFAR-10 with WideResNet-28-10 confirm that annealing schedules degrade less than fixed stepsize under coarse geometric grids.

## Results

On the synthetic logistic regression task, with a grid factor of ~100, the fixed learning rate (with iterate averaging) degrades by 0.08 in test loss, while cosine annealing, linear decay, and quadratic decay degrade by only 0.01, 0.014, and 0.08 respectively. On CIFAR-10 with Wide ResNet 28-10, at a grid factor of ~22, the fixed learning rate degrades by 0.61 in top-1 test error, compared to drops of 0.3 (cosine annealing), 0.35 (linear decay), and 0.25 (quadratic decay). At high grid resolutions (factor ≤ 10), all schedules perform comparably, but the gap between fixed and decaying schedules widens monotonically as the grid becomes coarser.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four learning rate schedules—fixed stepsize (baseline), cosine annealing, linear decay, and quadratic decay—under geometric grid searches of varying resolution on two tasks. On the synthetic logistic regression task, cosine annealing and linear decay substantially outperform the fixed stepsize baseline at coarse grids (0.01 and 0.014 degradation vs. 0.08 at grid factor ~100), while quadratic decay matches the fixed stepsize. On CIFAR-10, all three decaying schedules outperform the fixed stepsize at grid factor ~22 (0.25–0.35 vs. 0.61 degradation), with quadratic decay showing the smallest drop. At fine grid resolutions (factor ≤ 10), all schedules perform comparably, confirming that the robustness advantage of annealing is specifically relevant under limited computational budgets for hyperparameter search. The theoretical prediction that cosine annealing and quadratic decay share the same ρ^{1/5} dependence (due to matching tail behavior) is partially confirmed, though cosine annealing empirically performs slightly better, which the authors attribute to its slower initial decay phase.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the last-iterate convergence analysis framework of Zamani & Glineur (2025) and Liu & Zhou (2024), which provides the technical foundation for bounding SGD's final iterate with varying stepsizes. It departs from prior stepsize scheduling work (Jain et al., 2019; Defazio et al., 2024a,b) that focused on optimality under well-tuned stepsizes, by introducing the robustness-to-misspecification perspective: how convergence degrades when the base stepsize is overestimated by a multiplicative factor ρ. The key conceptual departure is showing that annealing schedules implicitly 'self-correct' overestimation by decaying the stepsize, enabling analysis from an intermediate iterate where the effective misspecification is reduced—a technique not present in prior schedule analyses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result provides a principled justification for using annealing schedules (cosine, polynomial decay) over fixed or inverse-square-root stepsizes when computational budgets limit grid search resolution—a practically critical concern for training billion-parameter models. The sublinear ρ^(1/(2p+1)) dependence means that even a 10× overestimation of the learning rate costs only ~1.4× in convergence rate under cosine annealing, versus 10× under fixed stepsize. Key limitations include the restriction to convex objectives (Section E shows a last-iterate impossibility for general non-convex smooth optimization) and dependence on the domain diameter D rather than the actual distance to the optimum. Extending the robustness analysis to non-convex settings with additional structure remains an important open direction.
