## Problem

Existing benchmarks for MLLM-based computer-use agents evaluate performance in clean environments or under adversarial attacks, but overlook robustness against common, non-adversarial corruptions (pop-ups, resolution changes, accidental touches, network errors) that routinely disrupt real-world desktop workflows. This gap leaves a critical blind spot: even state-of-the-art agents like UI-TARS-1.5-7B suffer up to 57% relative performance decline under minor corruptions, yet no benchmark provides configurable, executable environments to systematically measure this fragility.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a 1920×1080 GUI screenshot, a natural-language user instruction, and a history of up to 15 past screenshots paired with onlooker-generated behavioral summaries (concise text descriptions of what changed between consecutive frames). It outputs a single pyautogui action (click, drag, type, hotkey, scroll, wait, finished, or call_user) with pixel coordinates. The onlooker additionally produces a pre-execution environment validity report (flagging network disconnection or login-verification states) and per-step behavioral summaries that replace raw action tokens in the action generator's context, reducing distraction from irrelevant UI elements triggered by unexpected operations.

## Method

AgentHijack-Agent couples two components: (1) an action generator fine-tuned via Data-Augmented Group Relative Policy Optimization (DA-GRPO), which extends GRPO by sampling rollouts from multiple randomly corrupted environments per task so the policy learns grounding invariance across visual disruptors, and (2) an onlooker agent that continuously summarizes environmental changes into brief descriptions and performs initial environment checking against an error repository before task execution. DA-GRPO uses a structured reward (binary task success + format penalty) with an experience replay buffer that injects cached successful trajectories when an entire GRPO batch yields zero rewards, mitigating sparse-reward stagnation. The onlooker transforms the action generator's context from raw {observation, action} pairs into {observation, summary} pairs, decoupling the agent's planning from irrelevant UI state changes caused by accidental touches or app minimization. The base model is UI-TARS-1.5-7B (Qwen-2.5-VL architecture), trained with the VERL framework over 15 epochs on 128 sampled tasks with batch size 1, 4 rollouts, and AdamW at 1e-6 learning rate.

## Results

On the AgentHijack benchmark (3,321 tasks across 9 corruption types applied to OSWorld), AgentHijack-Agent achieves a 22.89% average success rate, outperforming the strongest baseline UI-TARS-1.5-7B (18.74%) by +4.15% on average. On clean (uncorrupted) tasks, the proposed method reaches 27.80% versus 24.21% for UI-TARS-1.5-7B. The largest per-corruption gains are on Pop Ups (+11.23%) and Verification (+9.67%), while the smallest is on Resolution Change (+0.84%). All other evaluated agents (GPT-4o, Claude-3.7-Sonnet, Gemini-2.5-Pro, Qwen2.5-VL-72B, Llama-3.2-90B, GLM-4.5V, UI-TARS-7B/72B-DPO) score between 1.63% and 16.96% average, confirming that even state-of-the-art GUI agents degrade substantially under common corruptions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AgentHijack-Agent outperforms all nine baselines on the average success rate across the 9 corruption types. Against the strongest baseline UI-TARS-1.5-7B (18.74% avg), it gains +4.15% overall, with the most substantial improvements on Pop Ups (+11.23%) and Verification (+9.67%), while the gain on Resolution Change is modest (+0.84%). Compared to the larger UI-TARS-72B-DPO (16.96% avg), the proposed 7B-parameter framework still leads by +5.93%, demonstrating that the DA-GRPO training and onlooker design are more effective than simply scaling model size. Among general-purpose MLLMs, Qwen2.5-VL-72B-Instruct (7.47%) and Gemini-2.5-Pro (5.82%) are the strongest, yet both remain far below the specialized GUI agents; GPT-4o (3.69%), Claude-3.7-Sonnet (2.45%), GLM-4.5V (3.05%), and Llama-3.2-90B-Vision (1.63%) all score below 4% average, highlighting that off-the-shelf multimodal models are particularly fragile under environmental corruptions. The paper also ablates the onlooker component, showing that replacing the fine-tuned UI-TARS-1.5-7B onlooker with Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B-Instruct yields further gains, confirming that a stronger summarization model benefits the action generator.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the agent robustness evaluation line by introducing 'corruption robustness' as a distinct concept from the adversarial robustness studied by InjecAgent, R-Judge, Agent-SafetyBench, and RiOSWorld, and by moving beyond the QA-format evaluations of Env.Distractions and GUI-Robust into a fully executable virtual-machine environment. Methodologically, it builds on UI-TARS as the base GUI agent, extends GRPO (from DeepSeek-R1) into Data-Augmented Group Relative Policy Optimization by rolling out from multiple corrupted environments, and adopts the experience replay buffer from ARPO to mitigate sparse reward signals. The multi-agent architecture (action generator + onlooker) draws on the broader trend of decomposing monolithic agent pipelines into specialized sub-agents, but applies it specifically to environmental state tracking rather than planning or tool use.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark fills a clear and practically important gap: prior robustness benchmarks either focused on adversarial attacks or used non-executable QA formats, leaving the question of how agents handle everyday environmental noise (pop-ups, resolution shifts, accidental clicks, network drops) largely unquantified. The finding that verification and resolution changes cause ~40–50% relative performance drops in state-of-the-art agents underscores that current GUI agents are far from deployment-ready in realistic settings. Limitations include evaluation restricted to OSWorld (Ubuntu desktop), a single 7B base model for the proposed method, and modest absolute gains (+4.15% average), leaving open whether the onlooker design and DA-GRPO transfer to mobile, web, or multi-platform agents and whether the 9 corruption types adequately capture the full spectrum of real-world environmental perturbations.
