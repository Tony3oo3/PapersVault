## Problem

Aligning LLMs to human preferences is inherently multidimensional, yet most pipelines collapse heterogeneous signals (verifiable correctness, subjective preferences, interactive engagement) into a single scalar objective. Existing multi-objective approaches are either computationally expensive (retraining per objective combination) or apply fixed weights at training time, limiting user control at inference. The paper asks what it takes to simultaneously align a model across verifiable, non-verifiable, and interactive domains while preserving fine-grained, adjustable control over each objective.

## Input / Output

At training time, the method consumes H objective-specific preference datasets (e.g., 5.5k accuracy pairs and 7.9k engagement pairs for Math; ~57k pairs per value dimension for Human Values) routed to dedicated heads, plus a shared SFT backbone. At inference, the user specifies per-objective ensemble weights (w_i) over the H action heads and optionally activates a trained PRM for step-level candidate scoring; the system produces a single aligned text response whose behavior shifts smoothly along the multi-objective trade-off frontier as weights change, without any retraining.

## Method

MAHALO combines three components: (1) a standardized PRM training pipeline that handles verifiable domains via hindsight relabeling (combining step-level rewards with discounted terminal correctness to form value targets) and non-verifiable domains via calibrated LLM-as-Judge rollout labeling or Bradley-Terry reward models; (2) Multi-Action-Head DPO, which extends a single shared LLM backbone with H lightweight linear projection heads, each trained with an objective-specific DPO loss while the backbone receives a combined gradient, preserving multidimensional preference structure and enabling inference-time logit ensembling with arbitrary weights; and (3) PRM-guided decoding with a continuing KV cache, where at each step boundary K candidate continuations are sampled from the running hidden state, scored by the PRM, and the best is committed—avoiding the discontinuities of re-encoding the full prompt at every step and yielding a 4× speedup over re-encode baselines.

## Results

On Math (MATH-500), MAH-DPO Ensemble achieves 0.7247 accuracy and 0.8733 engagement, substantially outperforming MODPO (0.7280/0.7367) and Single-Head DPO (0.7253/0.7160) on engagement while maintaining competitive accuracy. On Human Values (UltraFeedback), the MAH-DPO Ensemble leads all three dimensions simultaneously: 0.6389 helpfulness, 0.3687 honesty, and 0.2478 truthfulness, versus MODPO's 0.6175/0.3477/0.2325. On Socratic Mind tutoring, MAH-DPO Ensemble reaches 0.6893 accuracy and 0.4513 engagement, improving engagement by ~9 points over MODPO (0.3600) while retaining comparable accuracy. Combining MAH-DPO with PRM-guided decoding at test time further pushes the frontier, e.g., reaching 0.8000 accuracy and 0.8553 engagement on Math simultaneously.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAH-DPO Ensemble consistently outperforms all three baselines across every domain and metric. Against Single-Head DPO, MAH-DPO gains +0.157 on Math engagement (0.8733 vs 0.7160), +0.035/+0.063/+0.046 on helpfulness/honesty/truthfulness in Human Values, and +0.005 on Socratic Mind engagement. Against MODPO, the gains are even larger: +0.137 on Math engagement (0.8733 vs 0.7367), +0.021/+0.021/+0.015 on the three Human Values dimensions, and +0.091 on Socratic Mind engagement (0.4513 vs 0.3600), where MODPO notably underperforms even Single-Head DPO. Against DPO Soup, MAH-DPO leads by +0.138 on Math engagement and +0.026/+0.047/+0.033 on the Human Values dimensions. The specialized heads further demonstrate that MAH-DPO supports targeted control: the Math Acc Head reaches 0.7353 accuracy (best among all methods) while the Eng Head reaches 0.8840 engagement, showing that head-level specialization does not sacrifice the non-target objective.

## Relation to Prior Work

MAHALO extends the multi-objective DPO line (MODPO, Zhou et al. 2024) by replacing scalarized fixed-weight training with a multi-action-head architecture where each objective gets a dedicated projection head on a shared backbone, enabling inference-time weight adjustment without retraining. It departs from post-hoc parameter merging (DPO Soup / Personalized Soup, Jang et al. 2023; Rewarded Soups, Rame et al. 2023) by learning objectives jointly rather than separately. The PRM-guided decoding component builds on process reward models (Lightman et al. 2024; Math-Shepherd, Wang et al. 2024) and hindsight relabeling (Andrychowicz et al. 2018), but standardizes PRM training across verifiable and non-verifiable domains and introduces a continuing KV-cache mechanism to avoid the re-encoding discontinuity that plagues prior reward-guided decoding (Khanov et al. 2024; Deng & Raffel 2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical recipe for deploying assistants that must simultaneously satisfy accuracy, safety, and engagement constraints, with the key practical insight that reward verifiability should guide where optimization effort is spent (test-time search for verifiable rewards, multi-head training for subjective ones). Limitations include reliance on calibrated LLM-as-Judge for non-verifiable PRM labels (75–81% accuracy), evaluation on 7B/8B models only, and the need for careful boundary detection heuristics in PRM-guided decoding. Open questions include whether the shared-backbone multi-head design scales to dozens of objectives or to 70B+ models, whether cross-objective transfer in the backbone is genuinely synergistic or merely non-interfering, and how the approach interacts with online RLHF or iterative alignment loops.
