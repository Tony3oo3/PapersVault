## Problem

Current spatial transcriptomics prediction methods from histology are largely restricted to single-tissue models, failing to leverage biological principles shared across cancer types. While pan-cancer training offers a solution, the extreme morphological heterogeneity of diverse tissues introduces conflicting learning signals that cause monolithic architectures to struggle with parameter interference, and existing generative approaches (diffusion or single-flow) lack mechanisms to resolve this phenotypic diversity within a shared parameter space.

## Input / Output

The method takes H&E histology patches (processed through the UNI-v2 pathology foundation model to extract visual features) and a one-hot encoded cancer type label as conditioning inputs. It produces a log1-normalized spatial gene expression vector over a curated panel of 1,386 genes (the union of 50 MSigDB Hallmark pathway genes and top-50 cancer-specific Highly Variable Genes). At inference, a single Euler ODE step with Classifier-Free Guidance (scale w selected via a Filter-and-Rank protocol balancing Wasserstein realism against cosine pattern fidelity) maps Gaussian noise to the latent gene manifold, which is then decoded to expression space by the frozen VAE decoder.

## Method

MoLF is a two-stage generative framework. Stage I trains a Transformer-based VAE (4 heads, hidden dim 512, latent dim 128) to compress high-dimensional gene expression into a structured latent manifold, which is then frozen. Stage II learns a Conditional Flow Matching vector field that transports isotropic Gaussian noise to this latent manifold, conditioned on histological image features (concatenated with the noisy state) and cancer type (injected via cross-attention). The key architectural contribution is parameterizing the velocity field as a sparse Mixture-of-Experts (6 experts, Top-2 routing) where a gating network dynamically routes each input to specialized sub-networks, decoupling the optimization of conflicting tissue-specific transport directions. Training minimizes the Optimal Transport CFM regression loss plus a gene-reconstruction consistency loss (projecting the flow trajectory to t=1 and penalizing MSE against ground-truth expression through the frozen decoder) plus a standard load-balancing auxiliary loss. This design allows the model to capture both shared biological motifs and tissue-specific nuances without the averaging artifacts of a monolithic velocity predictor.

## Results

On the HEST-1k pan-cancer benchmark, MoLF achieves a mean PCC of 0.382 on Top-50 Highly Variable Genes (HVG) across 10 cancer types, outperforming the best baseline STPath (0.361) and the flow-matching baseline STFlow (0.312). On variance-stratified Hallmark pathway genes, MoLF reaches 0.185 overall PCC, surpassing MLP (0.171), STPath (0.143), and STFlow (0.122). In zero-shot cross-species transfer to mouse melanoma, MoLF attains 0.202 PCC, substantially exceeding STFlow (0.126) and STPath (0.090).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoLF consistently outperforms both the flow-matching baseline STFlow (Huang et al., 2025a) and the foundation-model baseline STPath (Huang et al., 2025b) across all evaluation settings. On Top-50 HVG PCC, MoLF (0.382) beats STPath (0.361) by ~2.1 points and STFlow (0.312) by ~7 points on average; the gap is largest on COAD (+1.3 over STPath) and SKCM (+1.8 over STPath). On Hallmark pathway genes, MoLF (0.185) leads STPath (0.143) by 4.2 points and STFlow (0.122) by 6.3 points, with the advantage most pronounced on low-variance genes (0.242 vs. 0.177 for STPath). In the zero-shot cross-species mouse melanoma task, MoLF (0.202) surpasses STFlow (0.126) by 7.6 points and STPath (0.090) by 11.2 points, indicating that STPath's masked-prediction pretraining overfits to species-specific artifacts while MoLF's MoE routing captures more transferable morphological primitives. Notably, STPath remains competitive on HVG (second-best) but degrades sharply on lower-variance pathway genes and cross-species transfer, whereas STFlow is consistently outperformed by MoLF in every setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

MoLF extends the flow-matching lineage for spatial transcriptomics (STFlow, Huang et al. 2025a) by replacing the monolithic velocity network with a sparse Mixture-of-Experts parameterization, motivated by the observation that pan-cancer morphological heterogeneity creates conflicting optimization signals in shared parameter spaces. It also builds on the conditional flow matching framework of Lipman et al. (2022) and the VAE-based latent space compression, but departs from diffusion-based generative approaches (STEM) on sampling efficiency and from the BERT-style masked-prediction paradigm of STPath by adopting an explicitly generative, distribution-matching objective. The two-stage design (VAE manifold + MoE flow) is the paper's core architectural contribution, positioning it between single-tissue regression methods and large-scale foundation models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The pan-cancer setting is practically critical because most real-world histology data spans multiple cancer types, yet prior methods were restricted to single-tissue models; MoLF's MoE routing offers a principled mechanism to handle this heterogeneity without the compute cost of foundation-model pretraining. The zero-shot cross-species generalization (human-trained model predicting mouse melanoma) and the finding that the large STPath foundation model underperforms a simple MLP on low-variance Hallmark pathway genes are both notable, suggesting that scale and masked-reconstruction objectives do not automatically capture fine-grained biological signals. Key open questions include the interpretability of what each expert actually encodes (the authors acknowledge this gap), whether the collaborative (non-cancer-specific) routing strategy generalizes beyond HEST-1k, and whether the approach scales to the full 38k-gene panels that foundation models target.
