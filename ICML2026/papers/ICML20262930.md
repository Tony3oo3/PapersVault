## Problem

Designing dense, task-instructive reward functions for reinforcement learning in robotics is labor-intensive, requires domain expertise, and does not scale across tasks. This paper addresses the gap by showing that informative step-wise rewards can be learned entirely from passive, action-free expert videos—no manual reward engineering, no action labels, and no privileged environment access needed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262930/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The progress model F_θ takes an ordered pair of RGB frames (e.g., two consecutive observations from an agent rollout) as input, ences each frame through a CLIP-pretrained ViT-B/16 backbone, concatenates the 512-dim features, and passes them through a linear head to predict a two-hot distribution over 20 bins spanning [-1,1]. The output is a scalar temporal distance in [-1,1] that directly serves as a dense step-wise reward: positive values indicate forward task progress, negative values indicate regression, and the magnitude encates the amount of progress per step.

## Method

TimeRewarder trains a self-supervised frame-pair model to predict normalized temporal distance d_uv = (v−u)/(T−1) between two ordered frames from expert trajectories, using three key design choices: (1) implicit negative sampling, where reverse-ordered pairs yield negative targets in [-1,1], enforcinging antisymmetric structure and suboptimality awareness; (2) weighted pair sampling with P(Δ) ∝ 1/Δ to emphasize fine-grained adjacent-frame differences; and (3) two-hot discretization (K=20 bins) for numerical stability and sharp progress boundaries. During downstream RL (DrQ-v2), the frozen model scores every adjacent observation pair as a dense reward, combined additively with a sparse binary success signal scaled by an adaptive factor α. A theoretical analysis shows that under deterministic transitions with per-step penalty, the optimal value function is a monotonic function of time-to-go, so temporal distance is a natural potential for reward shaping.

## Results

On 10 Meta-World manipulation tasks with 100 action-free expert demonstrations per task, TimeRewarder achieves nearly perfect success rates on 9/10 tasks using only 200,000 environment interactions, outperforming both all learned-reward baselines and the manually designed environment dense reward on 9 tasks in both final success rate and sample efficiency. On the Value-Order Correlation (VOC) metric for temporal coherence on held-out expert videos, TimeRewarder consistently achieves the highest scores, confirming strong generalization beyond memorization. In the cross-domain setting (1 in-domain video + 20 real-world human videos per task), combining both sources substantially improves success rates over either source alone, demonstrating scalability to diverse video inputs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TimeRewarder outperforms all three progress-based reward baselines (PROGRESSOR, Rank2Reward, VIP) on 9/10 tasks in final success rate and sample efficiency. PROGRESSOR saturates after partial progress (e.g., grasping) and produces spurious early reward spikes; Rank2Reward, limited to pairwise orderings without explicit temporal distance, fails to produce consistent distinctions between success and failure; VIP ignores partial progress and is misled by visual similarity in out-of-distribution rollouts. Against ILfO methods (GAIfO, OT, ADS), TimeRewarder wins decisively, as these methods rely on near-identical initial states for trajectory alignment—a shortcut that breaks under the paper's deliberately diverse initialization protocol. Notably, TimeRewarder also surpasses the environment's ground-truth dense reward on 9 tasks, attributed to the hand-crafted reward providing limited feedback on pre-contact progress. In appendix comparisons, TimeRewarder also outperforms TemporalOT and ORCA, which suffer from frame-skipping and compounding alignment errors, and BCO (behavior cloning from observation), which fails entirely under the 200K-step budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

TimeRewarder sits within the progress-based reward learning subfield of inverse RL, building directly on the potential-based reward shaping framework of Ng et al. (1999) and the two-hot discretization technique from Wang et al. (2024). It departs from prior progress estimators—VIP (goal-conditioned, unbounded objective), Rank2Reward (pairwise ordering without explicit distance), and PROGRESSOR (triple-based, forward-only)—by formulating progress as a signed, antisymmetric temporal distance prediction between arbitrary frame pairs, requiring no goal image and naturally encoding both progression and regression. The use of implicit negative sampling (reverse-ordered pairs) and weighted pair sampling (biasing toward short intervals) are the key design choices that distinguish its training objective from these predecessors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a simple frame-pair temporal distance model trained on 100 action-free videos produces rewards that outperform both prior learned-reward methods and the hand-crafted environment dense reward on 9/10 Meta-World tasks, suggesting that carefully engineered rewards may not be necessary when temporal structure in demonstrations is properly exploited. The cross-domain result—where 20 unlabeled human videos combined with a single robot demonstration substantially improve policy learning—points toward a scalable 'watch-to-act' paradigm. Key limitations remain for tasks with frequent back-and-forth motions (visual aliasing in single-frame observations), which the authors suggest could be addressed with history-augmented inputs or hierarchical progress models; broader validation beyond Meta-World and ManiSkill3 (e.g., real-robot deployment) is still open.
