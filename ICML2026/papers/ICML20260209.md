## Problem

Estimating the Riemannian geometry of high-dimensional data typically relies on k-NN graphs or kernel methods whose computational and memory costs grow super-linearly with dataset size and which break down in high dimensions due to the curse of dimensionality. These graph-based approaches also require recomputing nearest neighbors at inference time, preventing efficient out-of-sample extension. The paper asks whether a neural network can learn the carré du champ operator directly from samples, enabling amortized, dataset-size-independent geometric inference.

## Input / Output

The network takes a data point x ∈ R^D (e.g., a 28×28 grayscale MNIST image, 32×32 RGB CIFAR-10, 64×64 CelebA, or 256×256 FFHQ image) together with a scalar noise/bandwidth ε, and outputs a low-rank factor M ∈ R^{r×D} (r ≪ D) whose product M^T M gives the D×D carré du champ matrix at that point. From this single matrix one recovers the tangent space (top-d eigenvectors), intrinsic dimension (eigenvalue spectrum), intrinsic gradients (matrix-vector product with the ambient Jacobian), and on-manifold interpolating paths (Riemannian gradient descent). The network is conditioned on ε via Fourier feature embeddings so a single model captures geometry across multiple scales.

## Method

The key insight is that the carré du champ operator Γ_ε(f,h)(y) can be rewritten as a conditional expectation E[Γ_ε(f,h)(X,y) | Y=y] over Gaussian perturbations Y~N(X,εI) of data points X~p, yielding a tractable denoising-style loss L = E_{X,Y|X}‖Γ^θ(Y) − (X−Y)(X−Y)^T/(2ε)‖_F² that is sample-wise, O(1) per pair, and trivially parallelizable. A theorem (Theorem 3.1) proves this conditional loss equals the intractable marginal loss up to a θ-independent constant, so their gradients are identical. The network is parameterized to output a low-rank factor M ∈ R^{r×D} (with r ≥ 2d−1 guaranteed sufficient by Theorem 4.4), and the Frobenius loss is algebraically decomposed so that no D×D matrix is ever materialized, reducing complexity from O(D²) to O(D). The architecture is a FiLM-conditioned residual MLP for synthetic data or a timestep-conditioned UNet for images, trained with Adam/AdamW on mini-batches of (X, Y, ε) triples. A convergence theorem (Theorem 4.1) guarantees that at the loss minimum, the learned CDC converges to the true Riemannian metric as ε→0 when data is locally manifold-supported, and the ambient-coordinate CDC matrix converges to the tangent-space projector.

## Results

On synthetic 8-dimensional spheres in R^64, metric matching achieves 46% lower Frobenius distance in tangent space prediction compared to the k-NN baseline for datasets under 4M points, while closely matching it at larger scales. Inference throughput reaches 400× faster than k-NN-based CDC at 8M points (82× at 2M points). On high-dimensional image datasets (MNIST D=784, CIFAR-10 D=3072, CelebA D=12288, FFHQ D=196608), metric matching achieves the best inception feature stability under tangent perturbation across all four datasets, while requiring only 2.7–513.9 MB memory versus OOM for the Score Jacobian baseline on CelebA and FFHQ.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the k-NN diffusion maps baseline (Coifman & Lafon, 2006), metric matching achieves 46% lower tangent-space Frobenius distance on synthetic spheres for datasets under 4M points and is 400× faster at 8M points, while closely matching accuracy at the largest scales. Against the Stein Score metric (Azeglio & Di Bernardo, 2025), metric matching consistently achieves lower inception feature perturbation across all four image datasets; the Stein score's rank-1 correction to the identity provides only marginal gains in low dimensions (MNIST, CIFAR-10) and virtually no improvement on CelebA and FFHQ. Against the Score Jacobian metric (Kharitenko et al., 2025), metric matching outperforms on inception feature stability on both MNIST and CIFAR-10, and is the only method that runs on CelebA and FFHQ (the Score Jacobian OOMs despite 80 GB GPU memory, and is ~62× slower per evaluation on CIFAR-10). The paper attributes the Score Jacobian's underperformance to its full-rank metric lacking the regularization effect of metric matching's low-rank training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate lines of work: classical diffusion maps (Coifman & Lafon, 2006) and diffusion geometry (Jones, 2024a), which estimate Riemannian geometry via kernel-weighted neighborhood graphs, and modern denoising-based neural network training (Ho et al., 2020; Kharitenko et al., 2025), which extract geometry from pretrained model Jacobians. The key conceptual move is rewriting the intractable marginal carré du champ as a conditional expectation over Gaussian perturbations, yielding a sample-wise denoising loss that is provably equivalent (up to a constant) to the original objective. This connects directly to higher-order score matching (Meng et al., 2021) via the identity εA*(y) = I − 2Γ*(y), but differs in that the CDC target remains bounded as ε→0 while the second-order score diverges, and in the low-rank parameterization that enables high-dimensional scaling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: by replacing O(N²) graph construction with a single forward pass, metric matching makes diffusion-geometric analysis (intrinsic dimension, tangent spaces, Riemannian optimization) feasible on high-dimensional image data (up to 196,608 dimensions) where k-NN methods fail entirely. The theoretical guarantee that the learned CDC converges to the true Riemannian metric under local manifold assumptions provides a principled foundation absent from prior neural-geometry approaches. Open questions include: why the neural estimator generalizes better than k-NN at moderate sample sizes (the paper attributes this to inductive biases but does not characterize them), whether the approach handles genuinely non-manifold data (branching, varying dimension) in practice, and whether the low-rank constraint (r ≥ 2d−1) is tight or overly conservative for typical data geometries.
