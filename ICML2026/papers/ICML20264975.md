## Problem

Graph Domain Adaptation (GDA) methods implicitly assume reliable graph structures and align node features or representations, but they leave cross-domain node homophily mismatch fundamentally unresolved. When source and target graphs exhibit substantially different homophily distributions, message passing in GNNs is biased at the structural level in a way that feature-level alignment alone cannot correct, severely degrading cross-domain transfer even when node attributes are well aligned.

## Input / Output

The method takes a labeled source graph (V_s, E_s, X_s, Y_s), an unlabeled target graph (V_t, E_t, X_t), and a prescribed homophily level h ∈ (0,1] as input, and produces target node class predictions along with adjusted (generally directed) adjacency matrices A_s' and A_t' and domain-aligned node representations. Because node-specific adjustment strengths α_u differ across connected nodes, the adjusted source graph becomes directed, requiring row-normalized message passing rather than the standard symmetric normalization. The target-side refinement is restricted to an agreement subset V_t^a whose size adapts automatically during training, ranging from roughly 17% to 83% of target nodes.

## Method

PSAHS consists of three coordinated modules operating in a progressive loop. Source-side adjustment (performed once with ground-truth labels) raises the homophily of every low-homophily node to exactly h by upweighting homophilous edges, downweighting heterophilous edges, and inserting new intra-class edges, with a node-specific strength α_u = (h − h_s(u))/(1 − h·h_s(u)) derived in closed form. Target-side adjustment (applied iteratively) restricts refinement to nodes where a structure-aware GNN and an attribute-only MLP produce agreeing pseudo-labels, estimates homophily using only agreement neighbors, and applies the same closed-form rewiring to low-homophily nodes within this reliable subset. Cross-domain representation alignment via domain-adversarial training reduces residual feature-level mismatch. The three modules alternate progressively: improved representations yield more accurate pseudo-labels enabling safer structural updates, and the refined structure in turn reduces aggregation noise for better representation learning. A homophily-aware error analysis under the Simplified Graph Network model decomposes target risk into source margin loss, a W₁ homophily-discrepancy term, and an IPM representation-discrepancy term, establishing a one-to-one correspondence between each PSAHS module and a specific error component.

## Results

PSAHS achieves the best accuracy on all 15 GDA tasks across four benchmark suites (Citation, Airport, Blog, Twitch). The most dramatic gains appear on the low-homophily Blog dataset (average homophily 0.38), where PSAHS reaches 88.05% (B1→B2) and 89.64% (B2→B1), surpassing the strongest baseline HGDA (68.30% / 67.70%) by approximately 20–22 percentage points. On higher-homophily datasets such as Citation (A→D: 82.61% vs. 79.10%) and Airport (U→E: 59.20% vs. 57.20%), PSAHS still leads by 2–3 points over the best competing method. Synthetic experiments confirm that PSAHS maintains stable performance as homophily divergence increases from 0 to 0.7, while baselines without explicit homophily correction degrade sharply.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PSAHS outperforms all eight baselines on every one of the 15 evaluated GDA tasks. The largest margins are on the Blog dataset, where PSAHS (88.05–89.64%) exceeds the next-best method HGDA (68.30–67.70%) by roughly 20–22 points, demonstrating the critical benefit of explicit homophily correction under severe structural mismatch. Among feature-alignment baselines, GraphAlign and ADAlign are competitive on Citation and Airport (within 1–4 points of PSAHS) but collapse on Blog (47.14% and 64.69% respectively), confirming that feature-level alignment alone cannot compensate for homophily shift. Structure-shift methods StruRW and PairAlign perform moderately on Airport and Twitch but lag PSAHS by 3–6 points on most tasks. The homophily-specific baseline HGDA is the strongest competitor on higher-homophily datasets (Citation, Airport) yet fails dramatically on low-homophily Blog (68.30%) and several Twitch tasks (e.g., 48.25% on DE→PT), where PSAHS's targeted structural adjustment provides a decisive advantage. On Twitch, PSAHS leads by 1–4 points over the best baseline (PairAlign or JHGDA), with the gap widening on more heterogeneous regional pairs.

## Relation to Prior Work

PSAHS departs from the dominant GDA paradigm of aligning node features or learned representations (UDA-GCN, ASN, GraphAlign, ADAlign) by intervening directly on graph structure to correct node-level homophily mismatch. It also distinguishes itself from single-graph structure learning (Gao et al. 2019, Yu et al. 2020, Li et al. 2023) and prior homophily-aware GDA (HGDA, Fang et al. 2025b) by treating homophily as an explicitly controllable quantity with a closed-form node-specific adjustment strength, rather than a passive statistic addressed indirectly. The GNN-MLP prediction-agreement mechanism for reliable pseudo-labeling under label scarcity is a novel practical contribution not present in prior GDA or semi-supervised graph learning work. The theoretical analysis follows the SGN abstraction of Mao et al. (2023) and the domain-adaptation bound framework of Ben-David et al. (2010), extending them to incorporate homophily distributions as a first-class error term.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that homophily shift is a distinct and addressable source of GDA failure that feature-level alignment alone cannot fix, providing both a clean theoretical decomposition (each PSAHS module maps to a specific error term in the target risk bound) and strong empirical evidence across 15 GDA tasks. The GNN-MLP agreement mechanism offers a practical, parameter-free strategy for reliable pseudo-labeling that could transfer to other semi-supervised graph tasks. Key limitations include the SGN-based theory (not general multi-layer GNNs), the binary-classification focus in proofs, and the assumption that GNN and MLP errors are weakly correlated. Open questions include whether the progressive structure-adjustment loop converges efficiently on very large graphs, how the method behaves under multi-class or open-set settings, and whether the prescribed homophily level h can be learned rather than tuned.
