## Problem

Existing IB-based multimodal learning methods (MIB, MCIB, OMIB) rely on a VAE-style surrogate that enforces a fixed Gaussian prior on latent features, an assumption that does not hold for complex DNNs. Moreover, these methods apply the IB principle only to the fused multimodal representation, neglecting the complementary information in modality-specific representations that could guide each encoder to suppress modality-specific noise and redundancy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264628/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

IBMA consumes heterogeneous multimodal pairs (e.g., log-Mel spectrograms + facial video frames + BERT-encoded text for emotion recognition; ViT-B-encoded chest X-rays + ClinicalBERT-encoded radiology reports for disease classification) and produces a 512-dimensional fused multimodal representation for downstream classification or anomaly scoring. The IBB regularization is separable across training samples, so the composite loss (cross-entropy + η·IBB) is optimized with standard minibatch SGD without requiring a separate VAE or contrastive network. The number of learnable prototypes C is set larger than the number of classes (selected from C′ to 20C′ by cross-validation) to better capture the latent feature distribution.

## Method

IBMA introduces modality-specific representation alignment: for each modality j, an IB loss IB^(j) = I(Z^(j), X^(j)) − I(Z^(j), Z^(j′)) is minimized, treating the complementary modality's representation as cross-modal supervision so that each encoder retains only task-relevant, semantically aligned signal. To make this tractable without distributional assumptions, the paper derives IBB, a variational upper bound for the IB loss using prototype-based feature distribution modeling (softmax over C learnable prototypes), which is separable across samples and has O(nT₀ + nC²) complexity versus O(n²T₀) for CLUB. The variational conditional Q^(j)(Z^(j)∈a | Z^(j′)∈y) is computed in closed form via empirical conditional probabilities (Algorithm 3), eliminating the need to train a separate VAE. The full training loss combines cross-entropy on modality-specific and fused representations with η·(IBB^(1) + IBB^(2) + IBB^(fuse)), optimized end-to-end over modality-specific encoders (ResNet-18, BERT, ViT-B) and a single cross-modal attention fusion block.

## Results

On multimodal emotion recognition, IBMA achieves 65.4% accuracy on CREMA-D, 66.3% on MELD, and 75.7% on IEMOCAP, outperforming the strongest baseline OMIB by 1.8%, 2.0%, and 1.5% respectively. On multimodal disease classification, IBMA reaches 72.7% mAUC on MIMIC-CXR and 91.1% on CheXpert, surpassing OMIB by 1.7% and 1.8%. On sentiment analysis (CMU-MOSI), IBMA attains 50.1% Acc-7 and 88.3% F1, exceeding the best baseline by 1.5% and 1.2%. The proposed IBB upper bound also matches or exceeds CLUB in accuracy (65.4% vs 64.1% on CREMA-D) while using only 52.6% of CLUB's training time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IBMA outperforms all 15 compared baselines across every dataset and task. The strongest competing method is OMIB (Wu et al., 2025), which IBMA surpasses by 1.8% on CREMA-D, 2.0% on MELD, 1.5% on IEMOCAP, 1.7% mAUC on MIMIC-CXR, 1.8% on CheXpert, 1.5% F1 on MELD sentiment, and 2% AUC on anomalous tissue detection. Among IB-based methods, MCIB (Wang et al., 2026) and MIB (Mai et al., 2023a) are the next strongest, trailing IBMA by 2.0–2.2% on emotion recognition. Contrastive alignment methods (CLFA, DCLF) and early-fusion baselines (Concat, BiGated) are consistently 2–12% below IBMA. In the dedicated IB-bound comparison (Table 5), IBB achieves 65.4% accuracy on CREMA-D versus 64.1% for CLUB, 63.9% for VIB, and 63.8% for APIB, while training 47% faster than CLUB and only 5% slower than VIB/APIB.

## Relation to Prior Work

IBMA extends the Information Bottleneck principle from the fused multimodal representation (as in MIB, MCIB, and OMIB) to modality-specific representations, introducing a cross-modal supervision signal that guides each encoder to retain only information semantically aligned with the complementary modality. It departs from prior IB-based multimodal methods by replacing their VAE-style Gaussian surrogate for the latent distribution with a prototype-based, distribution-free variational upper bound (IBB) inspired by prototypical learning (Snell et al., 2017; Yang et al., 2018). The theoretical foundation traces back to Tishby's IB principle and Deep VIB (Alemi et al., 2017), while the fusion architecture follows the standard cross-modal attention paradigm used in recent multimodal IB work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The distribution-free IBB bound is a practically useful contribution: it avoids the unrealistic Gaussian assumption on DNN hidden features that plagues VIB-style methods, and its O(nT₀ + nC²) complexity makes it substantially cheaper than CLUB's O(n²T₀) on large multimodal datasets. The modality-specific alignment mechanism offers a principled alternative to contrastive or distillation-based cross-modal alignment, with the Grad-CAM visualizations providing intuitive evidence that it steers attention to task-relevant regions. Open questions include whether the prototype-based MI estimation remains consistent when the number of prototypes is small relative to feature dimensionality, how the method scales beyond four modalities in practice, and whether the IBB bound transfers effectively to continuous-valued regression or self-supervised pre-training settings beyond the supervised classification tasks evaluated here.
