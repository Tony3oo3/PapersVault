## Problem

Long-horizon data science agent workflows suffer from cascading failures in static, chain-like execution plans, where minor early-stage errors propagate and collapse the entire reasoning chain. Existing remedies are polarized: exhaustive search or multi-agent collaboration achieves resilience at prohibitive computational cost, while lightweight heuristic retries lack the semantic awareness to rectify deep logical contradictions, leading to silent failures. The paper addresses the gap of achieving search-level resilience without sacrificing operational efficiency in resource-constrained settings.

## Input / Output

The system takes a natural-language task description (global intention) and raw dataset files spanning tabular, NLP, vision, audio, and signal-processing modalities (scales from <10 MB to >30 GB) as input. It produces an executable code pipeline that yields a valid competition submission file, alongside a dynamically evolved directed acyclic graph (DAG) representing the workflow topology. Notably, the entire pipeline runs on a single consumer-grade RTX 3090 GPU with Qwen3-Max under a strict <1-hour budget and ~104k tokens per task, in contrast to standard MLE-Bench protocols that rely on GPT-4o/5 with 12–24-hour budgets and 36 vCPUs.

## Method

R³DAO constructs a dynamic closed-loop of hierarchical exploration, iterative recovery, and empirical convergence through three coupled mechanisms. First, the Dynamic Hierarchical Task Network (D-HTN) recursively decomposes global intent into macro-logical anchors (parent tasks) and micro-operators (atomic code blocks), performing logic-layer dimensionality reduction that shrinks effective search depth from n to k (k ≪ n) via a domain expert library. Second, the Reactive Topology Reconfiguration (RTR) mechanism uses a semantic reflection operator to classify runtime anomalies and emit structured diagnostic signals, then applies one of three localized topological repairs—Refine (in-place code correction), Insert (inject a compensatory predecessor node for missing dependencies), or Skip (bounded local reconstruction)—replacing expensive global resets. Third, a dual-loop Semantic Experience Distillation compresses execution trajectories into constant-scale structured priors (metadata invariants, file-path constraints), pruning the search space in the inner loop and persisting validated cases into the expert library in the outer loop, reducing token overhead from O(N) to O(1).

## Results

On a 17-task subset of MLE-Bench, R³DAO achieves a Grand Average comprehensive score of 0.646 under a strict agile setting (Qwen3-Max, <1h per task, single RTX 3090), outperforming all reproduced baselines (AIDE: 0.456, DataInterpreter: 0.321) and high-resource leaderboard entries (R&D-Agent: 0.540, OpenHands: 0.410, MLAB: 0.357). R³DAO attains a 94.1% task success rate versus 53% for R&D-Agent and AIDE, while consuming only 103k tokens per task (≈1/30th of R&D-Agent's ~3000k) and averaging 21.7 minutes execution time (≈36× faster than R&D-Agent's 12h). The advantage is most pronounced in High-complexity tasks (>10 steps), where R³DAO scores 0.495 versus 0.286 (AIDE) and 0.566 (R&D-Agent), and in failure-prone tasks like TF-Speech and COVID-Vax where MLAB scores 0.0.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AIDE (tree-search, 16 trajectories), R³DAO wins on every metric: Grand Average score 0.646 vs 0.456 (+41.7%), success rate 94.1% vs 53%, and token cost 103k vs 150k, while also running 3× faster (21.7 vs 67.4 min). Against DataInterpreter (dynamic chain, 10-round horizon), R³DAO dominates on score (0.646 vs 0.321) and success rate (94.1% vs 24%), though DataInterpreter is faster per task (4.0 vs 21.7 min) at the cost of near-zero performance on complex tasks. Against the high-resource baselines MLAB (GPT-4o, 24h) and OpenHands (GPT-4o, 24h), R³DAO achieves substantially higher scores (0.646 vs 0.357 and 0.410) and success rates (94.1% vs 47% and 59%) in roughly 1/67th of the time. Against R&D-Agent (GPT-5, 12h, ~3000k tokens), R³DAO matches or exceeds the comprehensive score (0.646 vs 0.540 in Table 2; 0.42 vs 0.44 in the Overall metric of Table 3) while achieving a 77.36% higher success rate (94.1% vs 53%), 36× lower execution time, and ~29× fewer tokens. Notably, R³DAO is the only system that successfully completes TF-Speech and COVID-Vax (where MLAB scores 0.0) and achieves a 0.910 on DocDn versus MLAB's 0.264, demonstrating that architectural resilience (RTR self-healing) compensates for the weaker base model (Qwen3-Max vs GPT-4o/5).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of hierarchical task planning (HTN from classical AI) and reactive LLM-agent orchestration. It departs from the dominant paradigm in data-science agents—where frameworks like AIDE and R&D-Agent rely on tree-search or multi-chain fusion with global resets—by treating the execution graph as a mutable topology that can be locally repaired via semantic reflection (Refine/Insert/Skip). The experience-distillation component draws on case-based reasoning ideas from DS-Agent and AutoKaggle but formalizes a dual-loop (inner consistency / outer library) compression that prior agents lack. Conceptually, the paper repurposes the HTN decomposition operator and the reactive-control notion of mapping physical anomalies into diagnostic signals, but applies them to LLM-driven code execution rather than classical planning domains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: demonstrating that a consumer-grade GPU with a mid-tier open model (Qwen3-Max) can match or exceed GPT-4o/5-based systems on MLE-Bench tasks by spending 30× fewer tokens and 33× less time suggests that orchestration architecture, not raw model scale, is the binding constraint for reliable long-horizon data-science automation. Key limitations temper the claim: the evaluation covers only 17 tasks, the 'agile' comparison is asymmetric (different models, budgets, and time limits), and RTR's effectiveness depends on the LLM's ability to correctly diagnose root causes—a failure mode the authors acknowledge but do not stress-test. Open questions include whether the reactive topology approach generalizes to truly open-ended research tasks (beyond Kaggle-style competitions), whether RL-based policy learning can replace heuristic reflection, and whether multi-agent topology evolution can handle extreme environmental volatility.
