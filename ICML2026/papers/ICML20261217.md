## Problem

Existing RL methods for LLM reasoning perform policy optimization at either the token level (PPO) or the sequence level (GRPO), both of which misalign with the natural step-wise structure of chain-of-thought reasoning. Token-level credit assignment produces noisy, inconsistent learning signals within a single reasoning step, while sequence-level assignment uniformly penalizes or rewards entire trajectories, suppressing correct intermediate steps. No prior method treats coherent reasoning steps as the fundamental unit of policy update.

## Input / Output

The method accepts a text prompt (optionally paired with an RGB image for multi-modal reasoning) and generates a chain-of-thought response terminated by a final answer. During generation, per-token entropy values are recorded to identify segment boundaries via a top-k% threshold (k=30), partitioning the response into a variable number of coherent reasoning segments. The final answer is verified by an outcome reward (e.g., exact match or \boxed{} extraction), with no intermediate rewards assigned to individual segments.

## Method

SAPO reformulates the language generation MDP so that each action corresponds to generating an entire reasoning segment rather than a single token, with states defined at segment boundaries. It computes temporal-difference errors and GAE advantages over segments, then assigns the resulting segment-level advantage uniformly to all tokens within that segment, ensuring consistent credit assignment. For off-policy correction, SAPO uses a geometric-mean importance ratio over each segment (the L-th root of the product of token-level likelihood ratios), which length-normalizes the update and prevents variance explosion on long segments. An entropy-based adaptive segmentation mechanism identifies step boundaries by selecting tokens whose generation entropy falls in the top-k% quantile, requiring no external formatting or annotations. The clipped PPO surrogate objective is retained but operates with segment-level ratios and advantages, while the value function is trained only at segment boundaries to match the step-wise temporal abstraction.

## Results

On six multi-modal reasoning benchmarks, SAPO achieves the best average accuracy at both model scales: 40.23% (3B) and 46.90% (7B), outperforming GRPO (37.97% / 45.96%), PPO (31.19% / 42.39%), and VC-PPO (35.24% / 44.20%). The largest gains appear on Geo3K, where SAPO improves over PPO by +24.46% (3B) and +10.15% (7B), and on WeMath, where it surpasses GRPO by +6.76% (3B) and +1.81% (7B). On text-only reasoning, SAPO leads on both GSM8K and MATH500, e.g., +9.4% over GRPO and +5.3% over PPO on GSM8K with DeepSeekMath-7B, and +7.8% over GRPO on MATH500 with RhoMath-1.1B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAPO consistently outperforms all eight baselines across both multi-modal and text-only reasoning. Against token-level PPO, SAPO gains +9.04% average (3B) and +4.51% (7B) on multi-modal benchmarks, with the most dramatic improvement on Geo3K (+24.46% for 3B). Against sequence-level GRPO, SAPO leads by +2.26% (3B) and +0.94% (7B) on average, with larger margins on harder long-horizon tasks like WeMath (+6.76% for 3B). Against the value-calibrated variant VC-PPO, SAPO improves by +4.99% (3B) and +2.70% (7B) on average. On text-only reasoning, SAPO surpasses the prior segment-level methods VinePPO by +4.1% (GSM8K) and +2.6% (MATH500), and SPO by +6.0% (GSM8K) and +4.8% (MATH500), indicating that the gains stem from the overall step-wise MDP formulation rather than segmentation alone. SAPO also outperforms RL-free baselines (RestEM, DPO+) and the sequence-level RLOO on both GSM8K and MATH500, confirming that structured step-level credit assignment is more effective than preference-based or uniform sequence-level optimization for multi-step reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAPO directly reformulates PPO's clipped surrogate objective, GAE-based advantage estimation, and value function at the granularity of reasoning segments rather than individual tokens, while also departing from sequence-level methods like GRPO that assign uniform credit across entire trajectories. It positions itself as a principled middle ground between the two extremes, and distinguishes itself from recent segment-aware approaches (VinePPO, SPO, segment policy optimization) by making the MDP itself step-wise—so that value estimation, advantage computation, and importance sampling are all natively defined over reasoning segments rather than being retrofitted onto token- or sequence-level formulations. The entropy-based adaptive segmentation mechanism draws on the observation that high-entropy tokens mark decision-critical branching points, connecting to the broader literature on token-level uncertainty in LLM generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental granularity mismatch in RL-based reasoning optimization that has been implicitly acknowledged but not systematically resolved: token-level methods produce noisy within-step credit assignment, while sequence-level methods suppress correct intermediate reasoning. SAPO's consistent outperformance across model scales (3B, 7B, 1.1B), task types (geometry, math, logic), and modalities (text-only, multi-modal) suggests the step-wise MDP abstraction is a broadly applicable design principle rather than a dataset-specific trick. Open questions include whether the entropy-based segmentation (top-k% heuristic) generalizes to non-mathematical reasoning domains, whether the geometric-mean importance sampling bias becomes problematic at scale, and whether the approach can be extended to settings with dense intermediate rewards rather than sparse terminal rewards.
