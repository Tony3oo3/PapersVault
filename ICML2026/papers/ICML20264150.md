## Problem

Existing sleep foundation models rely on contrastive pre-training but lack a systematic understanding of which pre-training objectives, augmentation strategies, and scaling choices actually drive generalization across cohorts, devices, and channel sets. The paper further reveals that current sleep FMs fail to generalize when channels are missing at inference time—a common real-world scenario in home-based and micro-event studies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264150/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 12-channel polysomnography signals (4 EEG/EOG, 4 respiratory, 1 ECG, 3 EMG) segmented into non-overlapping 30-second epochs, resampled to 64 Hz with zero-padding for absent channels. It produces a fixed-dimensional epoch-level embedding that supports 4-class sleep staging, four binary sleep-event detections (arousal, hypopnea, oxygen desaturation, central apnea), and—after temporal aggregation—patient-level disease risk scores for coronary disease, diabetes, and hypertension.

## Method

OSF is built on DINO self-distillation with a ViT-85M Transformer encoder, but its key differentiator is a two-stage masking augmentation: random channel masking (dropping 50% of the 12 channels) combined with temporal block masking (ratio sampled from [0.3, 0.6]). This forces the model to learn channel-invariant representations, which the paper shows is critical for robustness under missing-channel inference and for strong downstream transfer. Pre-training is conducted on SleepBench, a 166,500-hour corpus aggregated from nine public NSRR datasets, and the paper demonstrates that jointly scaling pre-training data, model capacity (1M→85M), and multi-source data mixture yields consistent downstream gains. For patient-level tasks, OSF uses a top-k selection module (trained with straight-through estimation) to aggregate epoch embeddings, outperforming mean pooling and LSTM-based aggregation.

## Results

On the out-of-domain MROS cohort, OSF achieves state-of-the-art linear-probing performance across all five epoch-level tasks: Sleep Staging (AUC 97.3, AUPRC 90.4), Arousal (AUC 92.8, AUPRC 88.3), Hypopnea (AUC 77.7, AUPRC 62.2), Oxygen Desaturation (AUC 81.5, AUPRC 81.0), and Central Apnea (AUC 97.3, AUPRC 70.7). Under full fine-tuning, OSF further improves to Sleep Staging AUC 97.9 and Hypopnea AUC 85.0. Across all seven external evaluation cohorts (CCSHS, CFS, NCHSDB, WSC, CHAT, MESA, SOF), OSF achieves the best linear-probing sleep staging AUC, ranging from 95.8 (NCHSDB) to 98.6 (CCSHS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OSF (DINO-based with channel-invariant masking) outperforms the prior sleep foundation model SleepFM (Thapa et al., 2026) on every epoch-level task under linear probing: Sleep Staging AUC 97.3 vs. 96.4, Arousal 92.8 vs. 90.3, and Hypopnea 77.7 vs. 75.5 on MROS. Among the four SSL families, vanilla SimCLR performs worst (Sleep Staging AUC 81.4), while reconstruction-based MAE (96.5) and VQ-VAE (95.8) are the strongest non-OSF baselines, yet OSF still leads by 0.8 and 1.5 AUC points respectively. Under full fine-tuning, OSF achieves Sleep Staging AUC 97.9 and Hypopnea AUC 85.0, matching or exceeding SleepFM (97.0 and 85.0) and surpassing all other methods. Against five domain-specific physiological pre-training methods (TS2Vec, ST-MEM, LSM-2, PedSleepMAE, SleepGPT), OSF leads on both Sleep Staging (97.3 vs. best 96.1 for ST-MEM) and Hypopnea (77.7 vs. best 75.4 for ST-MEM) on MROS. OSF also demonstrates superior robustness under missing-channel inference, outperforming SleepFM by 1–4 AUC points across four realistic deployment scenarios (head-band, in-home, micro-event, and sleep-disorder study configurations).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SleepFM (Thapa et al., 2026), the first multimodal sleep foundation model that used contrastive objectives to align modality-group embeddings, and on the broader SSL literature (SimCLR, DINO, MAE, VQ-VAE, autoregressive modeling). Rather than proposing a single new architecture, it departs from prior sleep FM work by conducting a controlled, ablation-driven study of which pre-training design choices—augmentation strategy, data mixture, model capacity—actually drive downstream transfer, particularly under the practically critical but previously unstudied setting of missing-channel inference. The resulting OSF model family uses DINO as its base objective combined with the identified channel-masking augmentation recipe, positioning it as a systematic refinement of the SleepFM paradigm rather than a fundamentally new approach.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that existing sleep FMs degrade substantially when channels are missing at inference time is a practical concern for real-world deployment (home studies, sensor dropouts) that the field had not systematically addressed. The 166,500-hour fully open SleepBench benchmark and the clear scaling guidelines (channel-invariant SSL + multi-source data + larger models) provide a concrete recipe for building more generalizable sleep FMs. However, a meaningful performance gap remains when task-critical channels (e.g., EEG for staging) are entirely absent, and the paper explicitly calls for further work on robustness under channel missingness. Clinical deployment would require prospective validation and subgroup monitoring, which remain open.
