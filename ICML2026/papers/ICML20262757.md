## Problem

Long-horizon robotic manipulation requires preserving global task intent while executing precise low-level control, but existing coarse-to-fine action generation methods struggle to maintain consistency across hierarchical scales. Discrete codebook approaches introduce quantization errors that disrupt action continuity, while continuous autoregressive policies fail to preserve coherent semantics across different temporal resolutions. The paper addresses the unresolved tension between action continuity, cross-scale probabilistic consistency, and real-time inference efficiency in multi-scale action generation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-modal observations—2D RGB images (224×224 or 320×240 from multiple camera views), 3D point clouds (1024×6), and language instruction embeddings—encoded into a single observation condition vector C. It produces a continuous action sequence of 32 steps (7-dim for LIBERO, 14-dim for RoboTwin), decoded from the finest-scale latent via a VAE decoder. Action chunks are first compressed into latent sequences of length 8 (downsampling factor F=4), then further downsampled into four temporal scales of lengths [1, 2, 4, 8], with each scale generated via a single MeanFlow function evaluation (1-NFE).

## Method

MSP operates in two stages: first, a causal Transformer within a VAE framework encodes action trajectories into a smooth continuous latent space (avoiding discrete tokenization), and second, a probabilistically consistent multi-scale generation pipeline refines actions coarse-to-fine. The finest-scale latent is deterministically downsampled into four scales (factors 8, 4, 2, 1), and a scale-wise autoregressive DiT Transformer generates semantic conditions at each scale by processing the observation condition and upsampled coarser-scale latents. A lightweight three-layer MLP ResNet implements MeanFlow at each scale, learning the average velocity field over time intervals to enable single-step (1-NFE) generation from Gaussian noise to the target latent. Classifier-free guidance is integrated into the MeanFlow objective to strengthen semantic conditioning, and scale-weighted losses (proportional to latent size) balance coarse semantic modeling against fine dynamic refinement. Cross-scale probabilistic consistency is naturally induced by constructing all scale targets from the same finest-scale latent via deterministic downsampling, eliminating the need for explicit distribution-matching losses while ensuring different temporal resolutions represent the same underlying action trajectory.

## Results

On LIBERO, MSP achieves a 96.9% average success rate across four suites, outperforming DSP (93.15%) and π₀ (94.15%), with the largest gap on LIBERO-Long (96.8% vs. 90.0% for DSP, +6.8%). On RoboTwin2.0, MSP attains 82.25% average success with 3D point-cloud inputs, surpassing the next-best method DSP3D (64.125%) by 18.1 points, with the advantage growing as task horizons extend from short to extra-long. MSP uses only 17.2M action-head parameters (~60% of DSP's 28.77M) while generating 32 actions per forward pass versus 16 for DSP, and achieves 94.2% on Push-T and 69.63% on RoboTwin1.0 (vs. H³DP's 57.38%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MSP outperforms all 12 baselines on the primary LIBERO benchmark, with the most significant margins on LIBERO-Long: +6.8% over DSP (96.8 vs. 90.0), +11.6% over π₀ (96.8 vs. 85.2), and +36.8% over CARP (96.8 vs. 60.0). On RoboTwin2.0, MSP (82.25% with 3D inputs) leads DSP3D (64.125%) by 18.1 points and π₀ (53.375%) by 28.9 points, with the gap widening on extra-long-horizon tasks (79% vs. 60% for DSP3D). The only suite where MSP does not rank first is LIBERO-Goal, where π₀ edges ahead (95.8% vs. 93.8%), though MSP still surpasses DSP (90.8%) and all other baselines. In real-world experiments, MSP consistently outperforms both DP and DSP across all five multi-stage tasks, with the advantage growing from ~10 points on single-stage tasks to ~48 points on the final stage of Make tea (28.33 vs. 0.00 for both baselines). MSP also achieves +12.25% over H³DP on RoboTwin1.0 (69.63% vs. 57.38%) and +3.2% over DP-C on Push-T (94.2% vs. 91.0%), while using roughly 60% of DSP's parameters and generating twice the action horizon per inference call.

## Relation to Prior Work

MSP extends the scale-wise autoregressive generation paradigm from FlowAR (Ren et al., 2025) from image generation to continuous robot action sequences, and adopts MeanFlow (Geng et al., 2025) as the per-scale generative backbone to achieve 1-NFE sampling. It departs from the closest prior work, CARP (Gong et al., 2025), by replacing discrete codebook tokenization with a continuous VAE latent action space, thereby avoiding quantization errors that disrupt fine-grained temporal control. The multi-scale construction via simple temporal downsampling (rather than residual VQ-VAE hierarchies) and the explicit cross-scale probabilistic consistency formulation distinguish MSP from both discrete autoregressive policies (VQ-BeT, QueST) and single-scale flow/diffusion policies (Diffusion Policy, FlowPolicy, MP1).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The combination of 1-NFE MeanFlow generation with a lightweight continuous latent space offers a practical path to real-time long-horizon robot control, reducing both parameter count (~60% of DSP) and inference latency while improving success rates on multi-stage tasks. The cross-scale probabilistic consistency principle provides a clean theoretical framing for why coarse-to-fine generation should work, though the paper acknowledges that real-world sequences with weakly related segments (pauses, task switches) may violate the assumed coherent temporal structure. Open questions include whether the framework extends to closed-loop replanning, uncertainty-aware execution, and joint optimization in visual latent spaces, as well as robustness under distribution shift in perception and dynamics.
