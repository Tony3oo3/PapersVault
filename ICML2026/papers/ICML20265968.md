## Problem

Merging multiple LoRA adapters into a single multi-task model is a practical bottleneck: existing methods either materialize dense weight deltas or perform expensive subspace factorizations (SVD, core-space decomposition) whose cost scales poorly with the number of tasks and layers. The paper asks whether one can merge LoRA adapters in a compressed sketch space while preserving cross-task interactions, and provides a theoretical link between merging quality and the effective rank of the merged matrices.

## Input / Output

The method takes T task-specific LoRA factor pairs (A^(t) ∈ R^{d×r}, B^(t) ∈ R^{r×d}) for a frozen backbone layer of width d and rank r (typically r=16), along with a shared sketch size s (default s=8). It produces a single merged LoRA pair (A*, B*) of the same rank r that can be loaded as a standard LoRA module without modifying inference code. The key constraint is that the shared circulant transform C ∈ R^{r×r} and the row/column index sets I, J are fixed per layer and shared across all tasks, ensuring comparable sketches.

## Method

CircuMerge reparameterizes each task's LoRA update as (A^(t)C)(C^{-1}B^(t)) using a shared invertible circulant matrix C in the rank space, placing all tasks in a common coordinate system via structured mixing. It then constructs compact s×r and r×s factor sketches by uniformly sampling s rows and columns (shared across tasks), applies any merging rule (TA, TIES, DARE, TSV, Iso-C) directly on these small sketches, and reconstructs full factors by overwriting sampled positions with merged sketches while averaging unsampled positions across tasks. A final circulant inverse maps the result back to standard LoRA parameters. The theoretical contribution shows that the circulant transform increases the effective rank (spectral uniformity) of the merged matrix, which provably raises the expected Subsection Alignment Ratio under a left-isotropy assumption, explaining why structured rank-space mixing before sketching improves merging quality. The circulant structure enables O(r log r) FFT-based multiplications instead of O(r²) dense operations, yielding an overall per-layer complexity of O(nTr log r) versus O(n³T) or O(n³T²) for prior subspace-based methods.

## Results

On the 6-task NLI benchmark with Llama 3 8B, CircuMerge achieves 93.37% average normalized accuracy with TIES (vs. 93.30% for Core Space and 92.53% for KnOTS) while reducing merge time from 8.3 s (Core) to 3.7 s and from 2963 s (KnOTS) to 3.7 s. On the 8-task CLIP vision benchmark with ViT-B/32, CircuMerge reaches 77.53% average normalized accuracy with TSV+Iso-C, outperforming Core Space (76.43%) and KnOTS (74.02%). Across all configurations, CircuMerge reduces overall merging time by at least 44% compared to the fastest prior method (Core Space) while maintaining optimal or near-optimal accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TA (Ilharco et al., 2023), CircuMerge consistently improves average normalized accuracy by 2–4 points across all merging rules on NLI tasks (e.g., 93.37 vs. 90.38 with TIES) while being 2.6× faster. Against TIES (Yadav et al., 2023) and DARE-TIES (Yu et al., 2024), CircuMerge matches or slightly exceeds Core Space accuracy (93.37 vs. 93.30; 93.64 vs. 93.32) at roughly half the merge time (3.7 s vs. 8.3 s; 4.7 s vs. 10.3 s). Against TSV (Gargiulo et al., 2025), CircuMerge is within 0.03 points of Core Space (94.13 vs. 94.16) but 3.5× faster (4.1 s vs. 14.3 s), and substantially outperforms KnOTS (94.13 vs. 90.87). Against Iso-C (Marczak et al., 2025), CircuMerge trails Core Space by 0.96 points on NLI (89.37 vs. 90.33) but is 2.3× faster (5.1 s vs. 11.6 s); on the vision suite, CircuMerge actually surpasses Core on most Iso-C combinations (e.g., 77.53 vs. 76.43 for TSV+Iso-C). Against CART (Choi et al., 2025, vision only), CircuMerge is within 0.12 points of Core (73.32 vs. 73.44) while being faster. Overall, CircuMerge never degrades accuracy by more than ~1 point relative to the best prior method in any configuration, while uniformly reducing merge time by 44–99%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the core-space merging line (KnOTS, Core Space Merging), which exploits LoRA's low-rank structure to avoid operating on full dense weight deltas, but departs from those methods by replacing expensive shared-basis factorizations (SVD, subspace projections) with a shared circulant transform applied via FFT plus random row/column sketching. The theoretical analysis extends the Subsection Alignment Ratio (SAR) framework from Iso-C (Marczak et al., 2025) by connecting it to the participation-ratio effective rank, providing a spectral-uniformization mechanism that justifies structured rank-space mixing before sketching. The sketching component draws on the circulant Johnson-Lindenstrauss literature (Krahmer et al., 2014; Yin et al., 2020b), repurposing it from dimensionality reduction of single matrices to cross-task interaction capture at merge time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 44%+ merge-time reduction while matching or exceeding accuracy of factorization-based baselines makes multi-task LoRA merging substantially more practical for deployment pipelines that must compose many off-the-shelf adapters. The theoretical link between effective rank and expected SAR gives a verifiable, layer-wise diagnostic for whether a given transform is improving merging quality, which is useful beyond this specific method. However, the theory is scoped to Task Arithmetic under a strong left-isotropy assumption, and the empirical extension to TIES, TSV, Iso-C, etc. lacks a corresponding guarantee; whether the circulant structure is truly optimal versus other O(r log r) structured transforms (e.g., Hadamard-based) remains open. The non-monotonic sketch-size behavior (s=8 optimal, larger s degrades accuracy) is intriguing but not fully explained theoretically.
