## Problem

Applying the MeanFlow paradigm (single-step generative inference) to robotic manipulation is impeded by three theoretical pathologies: spectral bias from temporal averaging that attenuates high-frequency directional adjustments, gradient starvation where MSE loss provides vanishing directional supervision in low-velocity regimes common to fine manipulation, and prohibitive memory overhead from nested Jacobian-Vector Product computations that prevent training large backbones on standard hardware.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261066/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The policy consumes 3D point clouds downsampled to 512 or 1024 points via Farthest Point Sampling (or 84×84 RGB images) together with a 2-step proprioceptive observation history, and outputs a multi-step action trajectory in a single forward pass (NFE=1). In simulation the prediction horizon is 4 steps with 3-step execution; in real-world deployment the horizon extends to 16 prediction steps with 8-step execution, all generated from a single noise-to-action mapping without iterative denoising.

## Method

OMP builds on the MeanFlow Identity, which models interval-averaged velocities to enable single-step inference, and introduces two key mechanisms to overcome its pathologies. First, a Directional Alignment loss (L_DA = -log((cos α + 1)/2)) explicitly decouples direction from magnitude by maximizing cosine similarity between the predicted velocity and the true mean velocity v₀, guaranteeing non-vanishing angular gradients even as ‖v₀‖→0. Second, a Differential Derivation Equation (DDE) replaces the exact JVP operator in the MeanFlow Identity with a central finite-difference approximation, decoupling forward and backward passes and reducing activation memory from ~2× standard backpropagation to standard levels. The composite training objective combines MSE reconstruction, a Dispersive Loss for latent feature discrimination, and the Directional Alignment term, with the DDE variant trading a small accuracy cost (~1.5% average) for substantial GPU memory savings (e.g., 26.7 GB → 19.2 GB on the Place Bottle task).

## Results

On the combined Adroit + Meta-World benchmark (37 tasks, 3 seeds), OMP-JVP achieves an average success rate of 82.3% ± 1.6% with NFE=1, outperforming the closest single-step baseline MP1 (78.9% ± 2.1%) by 3.4% and FlowPolicy (71.6% ± 3.5%) by 10.7%. The gains are most pronounced on harder tasks: OMP improves over MP1 by 9.4% on Meta-World Medium, 4.4% on Hard, and 10.6% on Very Hard tasks. On real-world manipulation (Place Bottle, Clean Table, Slip Ring), OMP achieves 80%, 75%, and 70% success respectively, surpassing MP1 by 10–15%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OMP outperforms all six baselines across every benchmark category. Against the strongest single-step competitor MP1 (Sheng et al., AAAI 2026), OMP-JVP gains 3.4% in average success (82.3% vs. 78.9%), with the largest margins on Meta-World Very Hard (+10.6%) and Medium (+9.4%) tasks where fine-grained control is critical. Against FlowPolicy (Zhang et al., AAAI 2025), the improvement is 10.7% on average, with OMP also achieving higher success on Adroit Pen (60% vs. 54%) and Meta-World Very Hard (77.8% vs. 52.2%). Multi-step diffusion baselines DP3 (Ze et al., RSS 2024) and DP (Chi et al., RSS 2023) trail by 13.6% and 47.1% respectively, despite using 10× more function evaluations (NFE=10 vs. 1). The consistency-distillation baseline CP (Prasad et al., 2024) and the variance-adaptive flow method AdaFlow (Hu et al., NeurIPS 2024) are substantially weaker (50.1% and 35.6% average), confirming that the MeanFlow-based formulation with directional alignment provides a clear advantage over prior single-step approaches. On real-world tasks, OMP leads MP1 by 10% on Place Bottle and Clean Table, and by 15% on the most challenging Slip Ring task (70% vs. 55%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MeanFlow paradigm (Geng et al., 2025) and its robotics adaptation MP1 (Sheng et al., 2025) by identifying three specific theoretical pathologies—spectral bias from temporal averaging, gradient starvation in low-velocity regimes, and prohibitive memory costs from nested JVP computation—that impede direct application to high-precision manipulation. The proposed Directional Alignment loss decouples directional learning from magnitude regression, while the Differential Derivation Equation (DDE) replaces exact Jacobian-Vector Products with finite-difference approximations, drawing on ideas from Wang et al. (2025c). The composite objective also incorporates the Dispersive Loss from Wang & He (2025), positioning OMP as a targeted fix within the existing MeanFlow robotics lineage rather than a departure from it.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Single-step (NFE=1) generative policies are practically critical for real-time robotic control, and OMP's 3.4% average improvement over MP1—concentrated on harder tasks (9.4% on Medium, 10.6% on Very Hard)—demonstrates that the identified pathologies are not merely theoretical but actively limit performance in precision manipulation. The DDE's ~28% GPU memory reduction (e.g., 26.71→19.19 GB) makes training with larger point clouds or longer horizons feasible on standard hardware, a practical bottleneck the field has not fully addressed. Open questions include whether the spectral bias can be fundamentally overcome (OMP still attenuates high-frequency components), whether the method scales to more complex multi-task or cross-embodiment settings, and whether the modest real-world evaluation (3 tasks, 50 demos) generalizes to broader deployment scenarios.
