## Problem

Static negative guidance in text-to-image diffusion models forces a safety-fidelity trade-off: a fixed scale either distorts benign images when the target concept is absent or fails to suppress robust adversarial prompts. Dynamic variants like DNG reweight guidance via posterior-odds signals, but these are brittle for open-vocabulary compositional prompts where the complement of a negative concept is not a well-defined class, and lightweight similarity-based methods ignore evolving image evidence along the denoising trajectory.

## Input / Output

The method takes a user text prompt c and a user-specified negative prompt c- (a set of terms characterizing the unwanted concept, e.g., 'Nudity, Pornography, Erotic Art') as inputs to a pre-trained diffusion model (SD v1.4, SDXL, or SD v3). At each denoising step it consumes three noise predictions (unconditional, conditionally on c, and conditionally on c-) plus the realized trajectory history to compute a scalar concept-presence score, then outputs a modified noise prediction that steers the final image away from the target concept. No auxiliary classifiers, external datasets, or model fine-tuning are required; the only additional cost is one extra forward pass per step relative to the backbone.

## Method

CRG derives a training-free concept presence (CP) score by decomposing the log-likelihood of the negative concept into a difference of two KL divergences between conditional/unconditional and conditional/negative-concept distributions, each estimated as an expected squared discrepancy between noise predictions (following Kong et al. 2024 and Wang et al. 2025). At an intermediate timestep t, a Tweedie-based look-ahead term (scaled by hyperparameter k) captures the predicted clean sample's alignment with the negative concept, while a trajectory-accumulation term sums per-step alignment over remaining steps. The guidance update is formulated as a constrained optimization: minimize deviation from the original conditional noise prediction subject to CP ≤ τ. Because the constraint is affine in the noise prediction, the solution is a closed-form Euclidean projection onto a half-space, yielding a guidance weight ω_CRG = (CP − τ) / (k‖Δε_t‖²) that is linearly proportional to the concept surplus and gated by the threshold, fundamentally differing from DNG's odds-based modulation which is sensitive to posterior estimation error near the boundary.

## Results

On the primary nudity concept removal task (SD v1.4, 50-step DDPM), CRG achieves the lowest Attack Success Rate (ASR) on 5 of 6 adversarial benchmarks: 0.011 (Ring-A-Bell 16), 0.021 (Ring-A-Bell 38), 0.021 (Ring-A-Bell 77), 0.026 (P4D), and 0.059 (UnlearnAtk), while remaining competitive on MMA-Diff (0.164 vs. DNG's 0.139). CRG simultaneously attains the best FID (36.05) and a competitive CLIP score (30.85) on the benign COCO set, indicating that safety gains do not come from blanket degradation. On SDXL and SD v3, CRG again achieves the lowest ASR across P4D, UnlearnAtk, and MMA-Diff while preserving fidelity close to the unmodified backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CRG outperforms all fine-tuning baselines (ESD, CA, MACE) and weight-editing baselines (UCE, RECE) by a wide margin on every adversarial benchmark; for example, on P4D CRG achieves ASR 0.026 versus ESD's 0.152 and MACE's 0.159. Among inference-time methods, CRG dominates SAFREE (0.529→0.026 on P4D), TraSCE (0.291→0.026), STG (0.331→0.026), and static Negative Prompting (0.338→0.026). The closest competitor is DNG, which CRG beats on five of six ASR benchmarks (e.g., 0.026 vs. 0.225 on P4D, 0.059 vs. 0.183 on UnlearnAtk) but loses narrowly on MMA-Diff (0.164 vs. 0.139). In terms of benign fidelity, CRG achieves the lowest FID (36.05) by a large margin over all baselines (next best: NP at 51.74), while maintaining a CLIP score (30.85) within 0.25 of the unmodified backbone (31.10), confirming that its safety gains are not purchased through indiscriminate quality degradation.

## Relation to Prior Work

CRG extends the negative-guidance lineage from classifier-free guidance (Ho & Salimans, 2021) by replacing the fixed or posterior-odds-based weight with a KL-divergence-derived concept presence score, building directly on the noise-prediction-based KL estimation of Kong et al. (2024) and Wang et al. (2025). It departs from Dynamic Negative Guidance (Koulischer et al., 2025), whose posterior-odds formulation is ill-posed for open-vocabulary compositional concepts, and from classifier-based methods (STG/Na et al., 2025) that require auxiliary models, by deriving all signals from the diffusion model's own noise predictions. The constrained-optimization framing (Euclidean projection onto a half-space) is a clean reformulation that yields a closed-form update, distinguishing it from both static prompting and gradient-based trajectory steering (TraSCE).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CRG provides a practical, training-free safety layer that dominates the safety-fidelity Pareto frontier across multiple architectures (SD v1.4, SDXL, SD v3) and concept types (nudity, violence, artist style), making it immediately deployable without retraining or external classifiers. Key open questions include: (1) robustness to adaptive or novel adversarial prompts not covered by the fixed negative-prompt set, (2) automated or adaptive construction of negative prompts in open-vocabulary settings, and (3) extension to video and 3D diffusion where the concept-presence signal may behave differently. The paper acknowledges that the CP score is a surrogate rather than a definitive harmfulness indicator, and that CRG is a complementary layer rather than a standalone solution.
