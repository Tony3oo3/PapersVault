## Problem

Self-play LLM training frameworks like R-Zero exhibit non-sustained improvement: performance gains early in the evolution loop but plateaus or degrades after a few iterations. The paper diagnoses this as a 'Diversity Illusion' where the Challenger satisfies surface-level diversity constraints while the Solver's training signals progressively collapse into recurring underlying patterns, decomposed into Local Diversity Illusion (within-batch diversity enforcement causes cross-iteration mode cycling) and Surface Diversity Illusion (questions vary textually but exercise near-identical reasoning skills).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262452/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a base LLM (Qwen3-4B-Base or Qwen3-8B-Base) and iteratively co-evolves a Challenger (question generator) and a Solver (answer generator) over five evolution iterations, each comprising 5 Challenger training steps and 15 Solver training steps. The Challenger produces natural-language math problems targeting the Solver's zone of proximal development (consistency score near 0.5), while the Solver produces step-by-step reasoning chains with boxed final answers. A persistent memory bank of skill-aware embeddings accumulates across iterations, and a code-generation model (Qwen2.5-Coder-7B) plus code encoder (Jina-Code-Embeddings-1.5B) serve as auxiliary components for diversity measurement.

## Method

R-Diverse introduces two aligned mechanisms integrated into the R-Zero self-play framework with GRPO optimization. Memory-Augmented Penalty (MAP) equips the Challenger with a persistent memory bank and applies a dual-perspective penalty—max-similarity to prevent direct recycling of specific past questions and mean-similarity to prevent drifting back into densely explored regions—thereby enforcing cross-iteration exploration; a memory replay mechanism (30% historical samples) stabilizes the Solver against distribution shift. Skill-Aware Measurement (SAM) redefines diversity at the level of reasoning skills by mapping each question to a canonical Python solver function via Qwen2.5-Coder-7B (with symbolic anonymization and procedure extraction constraints), then computing cosine similarity in a code-embedding space via Jina-Code-Embeddings-1.5B, so that superficially different but procedurally identical questions are correctly identified as redundant. Both penalties operate under the unified skill-aware similarity function, replacing R-Zero's BLEU-based lexical clustering.

## Results

On Qwen3-4B-Base, R-Diverse achieves a Math AVG of 52.59 and Overall AVG of 36.68 after 5 evolution iterations, improving over the base model by +10.01 and +9.58 respectively. On Qwen3-8B-Base, it reaches 56.46 Math AVG and 40.75 Overall AVG (+7.28 and +6.26 over base). Extending from 3 to 5 iterations (R-Diverse* → R-Diverse) yields additional gains at both scales (50.68→52.59 on 4B; 55.40→56.46 on 8B), confirming sustained rather than plateauing improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

R-Diverse outperforms all four baselines on both Math AVG and Overall AVG at both model scales. Against the primary baseline R-Zero, R-Diverse gains +3.52 Math AVG and +2.04 Overall AVG on Qwen3-4B-Base, and +1.77 / +2.02 on Qwen3-8B-Base. On the 8B scale, R-Diverse also surpasses Socratic-Zero (which uses an external API for question generation) by +0.36 Math AVG and +1.60 Overall AVG. Against Absolute Zero and SPIRAL (which use continuous online training rather than discrete iterations), R-Diverse leads by +6.17 / +3.07 and +5.59 / +2.46 on Math / Overall AVG respectively on the 4B model. Notably, R-Diverse* (3 iterations, matching R-Zero's evolution budget) already beats R-Zero at both scales, indicating the gains come from the diversity mechanisms rather than simply more training steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on R-Zero's Challenger-Solver self-play framework for reasoning LLMs, identifying a specific failure mode (Diversity Illusion) in its repetition penalty mechanism and proposing two targeted fixes. Conceptually, it departs from prior self-play methods (Absolute Zero, SPIRAL, Socratic-Zero) by shifting the diversity objective from within-batch surface metrics (BLEU) to cross-iteration, skill-level measurement. The SAM component repurposes code generation as a semantic bottleneck for diversity measurement, drawing on auto-formalization ideas but applying them to a novel measurement role rather than verification. The MAP component introduces memory as a repulsive regularizer in the optimization loop, distinct from prior memory-augmented agent work that uses memory for inference or single-agent learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Self-play is a promising but fragile route to reasoning LLMs, and this paper provides a clear diagnosis (Diversity Illusion) for why it collapses after a few iterations, along with a principled fix that sustains improvement across five iterations at two model scales and a second model family. The practical recipe (MAP + SAM) is relatively lightweight—replacing BLEU clustering with code-embedding similarity actually reduces compute—making it accessible to practitioners. A key limitation is that SAM relies on code as a semantic bottleneck, which the authors acknowledge may not generalize to domains difficult to formalize; the code-free variant (+1.04 over w/o SAM) offers a partial path but is weaker. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to non-mathematical reasoning domains and whether the memory bank can be further compressed without losing the cross-iteration signal.
