## Problem

Standard additive optimizers like Adam fail to make progress when master weights are stored in low-precision formats (BF16, FP8, FP4), because updates smaller than the local quantization step are rounded to zero at large weight magnitudes. Multiplicative optimizers like Madam fix this but cannot change signs or escape exact zero. The paper identifies these as complementary failure modes and asks whether combining both update types can enable stable low-precision training without FP32 master weights.

## Input / Output

At each step, M+Adam consumes the stochastic gradient g = ∇_w L(w_t) and the current weights w_t stored in a target low-precision format (BF16, FP8, or NVFP4), and produces updated weights w_{t+1} that are immediately rounded back to that same format before the next iteration. The method maintains three per-parameter optimizer states (additive first moment, additive second moment, and multiplicative second moment) in BF16, and all arithmetic is element-wise. A threshold τ prevents division by near-zero weights in the multiplicative branch, and optional clipping bounds the relative multiplicative factor to keep log(1+u^(m)) numerically stable.

## Method

M+Adam composes two complementary branches into a single update w_{t+1} = w_t + w_t·u^(m) + u^(a). The additive branch is a standard Adam-style preconditioned step u^(a) = -η_a·û/(√v̂+ε) that handles sign changes, zero recovery, and small-magnitude corrections. The multiplicative branch computes the exponent-space gradient g^(e) = (ln 2)·w_t·g, normalizes it with a dedicated second-moment accumulator, and converts the result into a relative scale change u^(m) = -η_m·g^(e)/(√v̂^(e)+ε) / max(|w_t|, τ), which preserves progress at large magnitudes where additive steps vanish under rounding. The paper proves monotone descent for the idealized scale-then-add map under a local smoothness bound in (additive, exponent) coordinates, and demonstrates consistent perplexity improvements over AdamW, AdamW+SR, and AdamW+Kahan across 60M–1B LLaMA-style models, 1–8× Chinchilla budgets, and BF16/FP8/NVFP4 master-weight regimes.

## Results

On C4/EN validation perplexity at 1× Chinchilla, M+Adam consistently outperforms AdamW across all four precision regimes (BF16/BF16, BF16/FP8, FP8/FP8, NVFP4/FP8) and all model sizes (60M–1B). The largest gains appear in the most aggressive low-precision setting: NVFP4/FP8, where M+Adam reduces perplexity by 8.7%, 7.3%, 16.6%, and 7.2% over AdamW for 60M, 130M, 350M, and 1B models respectively. In BF16/BF16, M+Adam also beats the stronger additive baselines AdamW+SR and AdamW+Kahan (e.g., 16.607 vs. 18.720 and 18.758 perplexity at 350M). The FP32/TF32 reference (e.g., 15.955 at 350M) shows that M+Adam with FP8 weights (19.395) narrows the gap to full-precision training substantially compared to AdamW with FP8 weights (21.674).

## Baselines & Comparisons

M+Adam wins against AdamW in every precision regime and at every model size tested, with relative perplexity reductions ranging from 2.7% (FP8/FP8, 130M) to 16.6% (NVFP4/FP8, 350M). Against AdamW+SR (stochastic rounding, Connolly et al. 2021), M+Adam achieves lower perplexity in BF16/BF16 at all four model sizes (e.g., 16.607 vs. 18.720 at 350M), indicating that the multiplicative branch provides benefits beyond what unbiased rounding alone can achieve. Similarly, M+Adam outperforms AdamW+Kahan (Kahan-style compensated accumulation, Zamirai et al. 2021) in BF16/BF16 (e.g., 14.139 vs. 14.854 at 1B), confirming that the additive-multiplicative geometry addresses a different failure mode than residual compensation. The gap between M+Adam and AdamW widens as precision decreases, supporting the paper's central claim that combined updates become increasingly valuable when quantization bins are coarse. Notably, M+Adam with FP8 weights at 350M (19.395) approaches the FP32/TF32 AdamW reference (15.955) more closely than AdamW with FP8 weights (21.674) does, suggesting the multiplicative branch partially recovers the accuracy lost to aggressive weight quantization.

## Relation to Prior Work

M+Adam directly combines two established optimizer families: the Adam/AdamW additive update (Kingma & Ba, 2015; Loshchilov & Hutter, 2019) and the Madam multiplicative update (Bernstein et al., 2020), which prior work has shown effective under extreme low-precision formats like the logarithmic number system (Zhao et al., 2022). The key departure is recognizing that these two update types have complementary failure modes under standard floating-point low-precision storage—additive updates vanish at large magnitudes while multiplicative updates stall near zero and cannot flip signs—and composing them into a single optimizer. This contrasts with prior low-precision training work that addresses the same rounding problem through accumulation-level fixes (stochastic rounding, Kahan compensation) or optimizer-state compression (GaLore, Apollo, 8-bit moments) without modifying the update geometry itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

M+Adam provides a principled path toward eliminating FP32 master weights in LLM pretraining, with the largest gains (up to 16.6% perplexity reduction) appearing in the most aggressive NVFP4/FP4 regime where additive updates are most severely degraded. The clean complementary-failure-mode analysis and controlled toy diagnostics make the mechanism transparent, and the Apollo-E compression results show the added optimizer state can be kept near BF16-AdamW memory. Key open questions include whether the gains persist at 7B+ scale and longer training horizons, whether M+Adam can be combined with stochastic rounding or Kahan compensation for further improvement, and whether native fused low-precision update kernels can reduce the current 6–34% runtime overhead.
