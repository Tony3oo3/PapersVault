## Problem

Existing quantization error reconstruction (QER) methods devote the entire low-rank budget to approximating the quantization residual, which is suboptimal in low-bit regimes where the activation-scaled weight SW has strong low-rank structure and quantization disproportionately corrupts dominant singular directions. This forces a rank-limited correction to spend capacity repairing avoidable distortion in the most informative subspace rather than addressing genuinely high-rank quantization noise. The paper asks: under a fixed rank budget r, how should rank be split between preserving dominant structure before quantization and reconstructing the resulting quantization error?

## Input / Output

SRR takes a full-precision weight matrix W ∈ R^{m×n}, an activation-derived scaling matrix S ∈ R^{m×m}, a quantizer Q(·) (e.g., MXINT, GPTQ, QuIP#), and a total rank budget r. It outputs a quantized weight Q_k plus a single low-rank factorization L_k R_k (rank ≤ r) whose two sub-components have distinct roles: a rank-k* preserved component (bypassing quantization) and a rank-(r−k*) reconstructed component (correcting quantization error). The split point k* is selected per layer via a lightweight one-shot random-matrix probe, making the allocation matrix-specific without expensive enumeration.

## Method

SRR implements a preserve-then-quantize pipeline: it first extracts the top-k singular subspace of SW via truncated SVD and maps it back to the original weight space, quantizes only the residual W − L_k^(1)R_k^(1), and then uses the remaining r−k ranks to reconstruct the induced quantization error via SVD in the scaled space. The key theoretical contribution is a closed-form criterion for selecting k*: minimizing the product ρ_k(SW)·ρ_{r−k}(SE), where ρ_p(A) is the unrecoverable energy ratio beyond rank p, with the second term approximated by a single random-matrix probe whose spectral profile is k-insensitive. For QPEFT, SRR initializes a two-component LoRA adapter from the decomposition and stabilizes training by attenuating gradients along the preserved top-k* directions (scaling factor γ ∈ (0,1)), preventing drift in the high-energy subspace while allowing the residual directions to adapt freely.

## Results

Under 3-bit MXINT PTQ, SRR reduces WikiText-2 perplexity by up to 27.1% relative to the corresponding QER baseline on LLaMA-2 7B (e.g., QERA-exact drops from ~9.04 to ~8.91 at r=64), with consistent gains across six models (TinyLlama 1.1B through LLaMA-3.1 70B) at both r=32 and r=64. In QPEFT on GLUE (RoBERTa-base), SRR achieves a 5.9 percentage-point average gain over QERA at 2-bit (78.43 vs. 72.51) and outperforms iterative methods LoftQ and LQ-LoRA by over 10 points at the same setting. On SlimPajama/GSM8K with LLaMA-2 7B at 2-bit, SRR attains 10.72 PPL and 18.29% accuracy, beating QERA (10.87 PPL, 16.83%) and all other baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SRR is applied as a plug-in on top of three QER methods (LQER, QERA-approx, QERA-exact) and consistently reduces perplexity across all six models in PTQ, with the largest relative gain (27.1%) on LLaMA-2 7B using QERA-exact. In QPEFT, SRR outperforms QERA by 1.5, 4.7, and 5.9 percentage points on average GLUE accuracy at 4-bit, 3-bit, and 2-bit respectively, and surpasses the iterative baselines LoftQ and LQ-LoRA by over 10 points at 2-bit without requiring multiple quantization/reconstruction iterations. On SlimPajama and GSM8K, SRR beats QLoRA, LoftQ, QERA, and LQ-LoRA on both perplexity and accuracy at both 4-bit and 2-bit, with the margin widening at 2-bit (e.g., +1.46 pp GSM8K over QERA on LLaMA-2 7B). The 16-bit LoRA baseline remains the upper bound, but SRR at 4-bit nearly matches it (34.18% vs. 35.41% GSM8K on LLaMA-2 7B), while at 2-bit SRR substantially narrows the gap to full precision. SGP gradient scaling (α=5) achieves performance comparable to SRR's fixed γ=0.1 scaling, confirming that the primary gain comes from the SRR initialization rather than the specific scaling rule.

## Relation to Prior Work

SRR extends the Quantization Error Reconstruction (QER) lineage initiated by ZeroQuant-V2 and refined by activation-aware methods like LQER and QERA, which all devote the full low-rank budget to post-hoc error correction. The key conceptual departure is the 'preserve-then-quantize' principle: rather than quantizing the entire weight and then spending rank capacity repairing the resulting error, SRR first extracts and preserves the dominant singular subspace of the activation-scaled weight, quantizes only the residual, and allocates the remaining rank to reconstruct the (now smaller) induced error. This positions SRR as a rank-allocation framework that unifies the k=0 extreme (standard QER) and the k=r extreme (LQ-LoRA, SVDQuant) under a single theory-guided criterion, and it extends naturally to QPEFT by initializing LoRA-style adapters with the two-component decomposition and stabilizing training via gradient scaling on preserved directions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SRR provides a principled, plug-and-play mechanism for improving low-bit PTQ and QPEFT with negligible computational overhead (~6% on the reconstruction stage), making it immediately applicable to existing QER pipelines. The 5.9 percentage-point average GLUE gain at 2-bit QPEFT and consistent perplexity reductions across six models demonstrate that the rank-allocation perspective yields tangible benefits even when the underlying QER method is already an exact solution. Open questions include whether the random-matrix proxy for the quantization error spectrum (Assumption 4.2) remains valid for more structured quantizers beyond MXINT, whether the approach extends to mixed-precision or channel-wise quantization schemes, and whether the preserve-then-quantize principle generalizes to other compression paradigms such as pruning or sparsity.
