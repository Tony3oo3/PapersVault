## Problem

Multimodal In-Context Learning (ICL) suffers from high inference latency due to long interleaved image-text demonstration prompts and instability from sensitivity to demonstration formatting, ordering, and content. Existing vector-based alternatives (e.g., LIVE) compress demonstrations into uniform hidden-state shifts but fail to capture query-adaptive, head-level attention redistribution and do not directly calibrate the attention distribution itself.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260071/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, Hyper-ICL consumes only the query (an RGB image paired with a text question or captioning prompt) with no demonstrations, producing a text answer or caption. During training, a frozen teacher processes the full demonstration-conditioned prompt (32 ICDs for Idefics-9b, 8 for Idefics2-8b) while the student sees only the query, so the student must recover demonstration effects purely through learned attention interventions. The adapter adds only 1.18M trainable parameters and operates at near zero-shot inference cost (0.955T FLOPs, 59.32 ms).

## Method

Hyper-ICL introduces a parameter-efficient low-rank logit-level adapter that injects a learnable bias Δ^{(l,h)} = (A B^T)/√r into pre-softmax attention logits at each layer l and head h, where A and B are rank-r projections of queries and keys (2d_h·r parameters per head vs. d_h² for a full matrix). A query-adaptive token-wise modulation vector g_{l,h} = σ(LN(Q)w + b) is applied via diagonal scaling to control intervention strength per token, per layer, and per head, enabling selective calibration that suppresses unnecessary interference. Training uses a layer-wise hyperbolic anchor distillation loss that maps student and teacher hidden states onto the Lorentz hyperboloid via the exponential map at the origin and minimizes squared geodesic distance across all layers, preserving the demonstration-conditioned relational geometry that Euclidean alignment would distort. The final objective combines this hyperbolic anchor loss with a standard cross-entropy supervision term weighted by λ.

## Results

On Idefics-9b, Hyper-ICL achieves 62.08 on VQAv2, 55.31 on OK-VQA, and 117.44 on COCO Caption, outperforming the strongest learnable baseline MimIC (59.64, 52.05, 114.89) and the 32-shot ICL baseline (56.18, 48.48, 105.89) with only 1.18M trainable parameters. On Idefics2-8B-base, Hyper-ICL reaches 71.17 (VQAv2), 62.24 (OK-VQA), and 135.66 (COCO), surpassing MimIC (69.29, 58.74, 132.87) and 8-shot ICL (66.20, 57.68, 122.51). Inference cost remains near zero-shot (0.955T FLOPs, 59.32 ms) versus 23.364T FLOPs and 468.55 ms for 32-shot ICL, confirming demonstration-free efficiency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Hyper-ICL consistently outperforms all six baselines across both MLLM backbones and all three primary benchmarks. Against the strongest learnable competitor MimIC, Hyper-ICL gains +2.44 on VQAv2 and +3.26 on OK-VQA for Idefics-9b, and +1.88 / +3.50 / +2.79 on VQAv2 / OK-VQA / COCO for Idefics2-8B-base. Compared to LIVE, the advantage is larger on OK-VQA (+9.26 on Idefics-9b, +7.38 on Idefics2-8B-base), reflecting LIVE's inability to model head-level and query-adaptive effects. Non-learnable vector methods (TV, FV) remain far below few-shot ICL, confirming that heuristic ICV extraction fails on complex multimodal tasks. RICES, the retrieval-based ICD selector, improves over random selection on Idefics-9b but degrades below random on Idefics2-8B-base for OK-VQA and COCO, highlighting architecture-dependent sensitivity. LoRA, while competitive on VQAv2, requires 21.2× more parameters than Hyper-ICL and lags on OK-VQA and COCO, underscoring the efficiency advantage of the logit-level adapter approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

Hyper-ICL extends the training-based in-context vector (ICV) distillation paradigm introduced by LIVE (Peng et al., 2024), which learns layer-wise shift vectors from demonstration-conditioned outputs. The key conceptual departure is moving intervention from the value stream (adding a vector to attention output) to the logit level, directly calibrating where attention is distributed rather than where the output lands. The paper also repurposes the Lorentz model of hyperbolic geometry (Law et al., 2019) as a distillation alignment space, applying geodesic distance to intermediate hidden states rather than using it for representation learning as in prior hyperbolic embedding work. This positions Hyper-ICL between the lightweight vector-based ICL line (Task Vector, Function Vector, LIVE, MimIC) and full fine-tuning methods like LoRA, occupying a middle ground of parameter-efficient, demonstration-free inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: Hyper-ICL achieves near zero-shot inference latency (~59 ms) while matching or exceeding 32-shot ICL quality, which is critical for deploying multimodal models in latency-sensitive applications. The logit-level attention calibration with query-adaptive gating is a clean, modular design that could be adopted in other attention-based adaptation settings beyond ICL. A key limitation is that the adapter is task-specific and requires retraining for substantially different domains or backbones, limiting its plug-and-play utility. Open questions include whether the hyperbolic alignment generalizes to longer sequences or video inputs, and whether the logit-level intervention can be made fully task-agnostic through meta-learning or multi-task training.
