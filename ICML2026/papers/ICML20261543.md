## Problem

Learning distributions over the symmetric group S_N is difficult because the state space grows factorially and natural discrete transitions (e.g., riffle shuffles) produce abrupt, non-smooth trajectories that degrade rapidly as sequence length N increases. Existing permutation diffusion methods define forward noising directly on S_N via shuffle-based random walks, yielding jumpy paths that are increasingly hard to denoise in the long-N regime, with performance collapsing near chance level for N > 100.

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of N items with d-dimensional feature vectors X = (x_1, …, x_N) ∈ R^{N×d}—concretely 4-digit MNIST images, 2D point sets for TSP, or image patches for jigsaw reconstruction—and produces a permutation σ ∈ S_N specifying the desired ordering. Internally, each item is assigned a continuous soft rank in [0,1], and the permutation is recovered by a simple argsort of the rank vector, so the output is always a valid discrete permutation regardless of the continuous latent state.

## Method

Soft-Rank Diffusion lifts each permutation to a continuous soft-rank vector Z_0 ∈ [0,1]^N via a uniform grid mapping, then defines the forward noising process as a coordinate-wise reflected Brownian bridge (VE diffusion bridge) on [0,1]^N, with the permutation at time t recovered by argsort(Z_t). For the reverse process, a σ_0-prediction neural network (an encoder-decoder Transformer) predicts the clean permutation from the noisy permuted instance, and the one-step backward update combines this discrete denoiser with a tractable Gaussian bridge conditional in soft-rank space, followed by reflection and re-sorting. The denoiser is parameterized as a contextualized Generalized Plackett-Luce (cGPL) model where stagewise logits depend autoregressively on the evolving prefix, with a Pointer-cGPL variant using bi-affine cross-attention over encoded items. Training maximizes the standard variational lower bound, reducing to a sum of cross-entropies over feasibility-masked scores at each position.

## Results

On 4-digit MNIST sorting, Soft-Rank Diffusion with Pointer-cGPL achieves Kendall-Tau of 0.6048 and correctness of 0.6607 at N=200, where SymmetricDiffusers collapses to near-chance (Kendall-Tau 0.0022, correctness 0.0061). On TSP-50, Pointer-cGPL reduces the optimality gap from 1.2618 (SymmetricDiffusers) to 0.0047, a ~268× improvement, with tour length dropping from 12.85 to 5.72. On CIFAR-10 8×8 jigsaw, Pointer-cGPL reaches 52.6% correctness versus 5.0% for SymmetricDiffusers, which effectively fails at this scale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SymmetricDiffusers (Zhang et al., 2025), the primary discrete-diffusion baseline, Soft-Rank Diffusion wins decisively at every sequence length beyond N=32 on MNIST sorting: at N=200, Pointer-cGPL achieves 0.6048 Kendall-τ versus 0.0022 for SymmetricDiffusers, and 66.1% correctness versus 0.6%. On TSP-50, Pointer-cGPL cuts the optimality gap from 1.2618 to 0.0047 (≈268×) and tour length from 12.85 to 5.72. Against DiffSort (Petersen et al., 2022) and Error-free DiffSort (Kim et al., 2024), both differentiable-sorting baselines, Soft-Rank Diffusion dominates at all N≥32; Error-free DiffSort in particular collapses to near-zero accuracy beyond N=32 while Pointer-cGPL retains 49.1% accuracy at N=52. The Pointer-cGPL variant consistently outperforms the vanilla cGPL variant, with the gap widening at larger N (e.g., 0.6048 vs. 0.3982 Kendall-τ at N=200), confirming that prefix-conditional scoring and bi-affine cross-attention are critical for long-sequence and sequential tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from SymmetricDiffusers (Zhang et al., 2025), the most direct prior work, which defines the forward noising process as a random walk on S_N via riffle shuffles and parameterizes the reverse with prefix-agnostic Plackett-Luce (PL) or Generalized PL (GPL) models. The key conceptual shift is lifting permutations into a continuous soft-rank space [0,1]^N and running a reflected Brownian bridge there, rather than diffusing within the discrete group. The reverse denoiser is further generalized from GPL to a contextualized GPL (cGPL) with an encoder-decoder Transformer, and a pointer-cGPL variant borrows the bi-affine attention mechanism from Pointer Networks (Vinyals et al., 2017) to score candidates against the evolving prefix. The reflected-diffusion machinery (Lou and Ermon, 2023; Xie et al., 2024) is repurposed to keep soft-rank trajectories within [0,1]^N, and the variational lower bound training objective follows the standard discrete-diffusion recipe (Austin et al., 2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central practical takeaway is that the choice of forward-process geometry—continuous soft-rank diffusion versus discrete riffle-shuffle walks—determines whether permutation generation scales: the discrete baseline collapses to chance-level accuracy beyond N≈100, while the continuous approach degrades gracefully to N=200, with the gap widening superlinearly. The cGPL/pointer-cGPL parameterization offers a reusable, likelihood-based decoder for any sequential permutation task where next-step decisions depend on the partial solution (TSP, routing, constrained ranking). Open questions include extending the framework to constrained permutation generation (partial orders, forbidden assignments), to other combinatorial structures beyond S_N, and to settings where the reference distribution or noise schedule must be adapted to highly non-uniform target distributions. The paper does not address multi-objective or preference-based permutation learning, and the autoregressive cGPL decode, even with KV caching, remains slower than encoder-only GPL at large N.
