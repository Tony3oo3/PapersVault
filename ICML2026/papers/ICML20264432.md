## Problem

Coupled cluster (CC) theory, particularly CCSD, is the gold standard for high-accuracy molecular property prediction but scales as O(N⁶), restricting its use to small molecules. While machine-learned interatomic potentials (MLIPs) can predict energies at DFT accuracy, they cannot match CC-level precision. No prior model directly predicts the CC excitation amplitudes—the core mathematical objects encoding the full correlated electronic response—from molecular orbital inputs, leaving a gap between DFT-accuracy ML surrogates and the high-accuracy CC methods they aim to replace.

## Input / Output

The model takes as input the full set of localized Hartree-Fock molecular orbital coefficients (padded to a uniform per-atom dimension) together with nuclear coordinates, requiring a prior HF calculation and IBO/VV-HV orbital localization. It outputs the complete CCSD T₁ (single) and T₂ (double) excitation amplitude tensors, from which correlation energy, the 1-RDM, electron density, and other one-particle properties can be derived. Training uses Δ-MP2 learning, so the network predicts only the CCSD-minus-MP2 amplitude correction, with the MP2 amplitudes added at inference time for free since the two-electron integrals are already available.

## Method

MoLe is an SO(3)-equivariant neural network that processes one graph per molecular orbital with shared weights, alternating between an MO-Attention block (cross-orbital interaction via L₂-normalized inner products without softmax, preserving sign equivariance) and an Odd-MACE block (within-orbital mixing via tensor polynomials restricted to odd monomial orders to enforce sign equivariance). The architecture is designed to satisfy four physical constraints: rotation equivariance of MO coefficients, rotation invariance of amplitudes, sign equivariance under orbital sign flips, and size extensivity (zero cross-fragment amplitudes for separated molecules). A readout layer combines paired orbital features via Clebsch-Gordan couplings and an Odd-MLP to produce T₁ and T₂ amplitudes. The key insight is that amplitude prediction provides a high-dimensional, spatially resolved supervision signal per molecule (O(N⁴) targets for T₂ alone) rather than a single scalar energy, yielding substantially better data efficiency than MLIPs even in the 100-sample regime.

## Results

MoLe achieves a QM7 test-set energy MAE of 0.12 mHa (full training) and 0.66 mHa (100-molecule subset), outperforming all Δ-MP2 MLIP baselines in the ultra-low-data regime by 2.5–10×. On out-of-distribution size extrapolation (Amino Acids, PubChem) and off-equilibrium geometries (Diels-Alder, dihedral scan, chair-to-boat), MoLe-100 attains 0.67–2.80 mHa versus 0.76–17.41 mHa for the best MLIP baselines. The predicted amplitudes also yield electron-density Frobenius errors of 0.13 (Amino Acids) and 0.36 (PubChem), substantially better than MP2 (0.59 and 0.90), and reduce CCSD solver iterations by 40–50% (e.g., 7.61→3.83 cycles on Amino Acids).

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the full-data regime (5732 molecules), MoLe (0.12 mHa) edges out MACE Δ-MP2 (0.16) and eSEN Δ-MP2 (0.13) on QM7, and leads on PubChem (1.63 vs. 2.24 for MACE) and chair-to-boat (0.08 vs. 0.19 for GemNet), though GemNet is slightly better on the dihedral scan (0.16 vs. 0.22). In the critical 100-molecule regime, MoLe-100 dominates every baseline across all six benchmarks: it achieves 0.66 mHa on QM7 versus 1.48–6.60 for the MLIPs, and 0.67 mHa on Amino Acids versus 5.33–12.16. The gap is most dramatic on PubChem (2.80 vs. 5.29–17.41) and the Diels-Alder reaction (1.50 vs. 3.17–11.33), where TensorNet-100 degrades to 11.33 mHa. For one-particle properties, MoLe's density-matrix error (0.13/0.36 Frobenius norm) is 4.5× and 2.5× lower than MP2 on Amino Acids and PubChem respectively. For CCSD cycle reduction, MoLe's amplitudes cut iterations by ~50% (7.61→3.83 on Amino Acids; 10.11→6.3 on PubChem) and rescue 2 of 3 PubChem systems that fail to converge with the default MP2 guess.

## Relation to Prior Work

MoLe extends the equivariant GNN/transformer architecture lineage (Tensor Field Networks, MACE, EquiformerV2) into a fundamentally different input-output regime: rather than mapping atomic coordinates to scalar energies or forces as in MLIPs, it maps localized Hartree-Fock molecular orbital coefficients to the full CCSD T1/T2 excitation amplitude tensors. The core message-passing component is a modified MACE layer ('Odd-MACE') that restricts tensor polynomial monomials to odd orders to enforce sign equivariance of orbital coefficients, and the inter-orbital mixing uses a custom MO-Attention mechanism with L2-normalized queries/keys to preserve both rotation invariance and sign equivariance. The paper also builds on the Δ-learning paradigm (Ramakrishnan et al., 2015) by training the model to predict only the CCSD−MP2 amplitude correction, and on the IBO/AVAS orbital localization literature to obtain a sparse, chemically interpretable orbital basis that serves as a critical inductive bias.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central practical impact is that MoLe achieves CCSD-level energies at roughly 1000× lower cost than CCSD itself (empirical O(N^3.3) vs O(N^5.9)), while being dramatically more data-efficient than MLIPs in the ultra-low-data regime (0.66 mHa with 100 molecules vs 1.48–19.15 mHa for the best MLIPs). Because the T1/T2 amplitudes are the only objects needed to compute energies at any CC level (CCSDT, CCSDTQ, etc.), this approach opens a path to learning from very high levels of theory with the same data efficiency, which would otherwise be computationally prohibitive. Key limitations and open questions include: the model is validated only on def2-SVP basis sets and small organic molecules (≤15 heavy atoms); the O(N^4) number of amplitudes still imposes cubic memory scaling that the authors flag for future sparse prediction; and the λ-tensor needed for rigorous response properties (beyond XCCSD) has not yet been predicted. The 40–50% reduction in CCSD solver iterations and the ability to rescue non-converging systems demonstrate practical utility, but the approach has not yet been tested on transition metals, open-shell systems, or periodic materials.
