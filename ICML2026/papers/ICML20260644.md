## Problem

Graph neural networks suffer from structural entanglement, where spurious correlations from semantically irrelevant neighbors contaminate node embeddings and blur decision boundaries. Existing robust GNN methods treat all nodes uniformly, overlooking that boundary nodes—those near class decision boundaries—are disproportionately affected by amplified structural noise. This paper identifies boundary-region entanglement as the primary bottleneck and asks how to selectively suppress spurious structural noise at decision boundaries with minimal model perturbation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260644/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

BES is a plug-in module that consumes pre-trained GNN encoder embeddings (concatenated from multiple encoders such as GCN, GAT, GraphSAGE) along with the graph topology and training-node labels to identify boundary regions. It produces refined, structurally disentangled node embeddings that are fed to a Chebyshev convolution decoder for node classification or link prediction. Notably, boundary identification uses embedding-space k-nearest neighbors rather than original graph topology for shift score computation, and only training labels are used—test nodes and labels are strictly excluded.

## Method

BES trains a lightweight attention layer A^(B) via boundary-focused contrastive learning grounded in a latent variable model that decomposes node representations into invariant (class-determining) and variant (structural noise) components. The method first identifies a boundary region via a margin-based slab between Gaussian cluster centers, then selects boundary nodes with high structural variability using a normalized shift score. A gravity-based contrastive loss pulls boundary node embeddings toward their class centroid and away from other class centroids, with the pairwise objective approximated by centroid-based similarity (proven gradient-equivalent) for O(N) efficiency. An adaptive pre-update mechanism computes a virtual parameter update, measures the resulting global embedding perturbation Δ^(B), and inversely scales the actual update to prevent destabilizing changes—operationalizing the theoretical error bound that disentanglement error is inversely proportional to boundary margin. Multiple attention layers are trained sequentially with residual connections to prevent feature-level coupling, and the entire module adds minimal parameters on top of any existing GNN encoder.

## Results

BES achieves the best node classification accuracy across all four homogeneous benchmarks: 89.46% on Cora, 78.40% on CiteSeer, 91.03% on Pubmed, and 85.52% on WikiCS. Compared to the GCN baseline, BES improves node classification by an average of 3.3% (up to 5.0% on WikiCS, from 80.48% to 85.52%). For link prediction, BES also leads on all datasets, reaching 94.45% (Cora), 96.27% (CiteSeer), 97.89% (Pubmed), and 92.27% (WikiCS). Additional experiments on heterogeneous (Chameleon, Cornell, Texas), heterophilic (Roman-empire), and large-scale OGB (ogbn-arxiv, ogbl-collab) datasets confirm consistent gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BES outperforms all 15 baselines on both node classification and link prediction across the four homogeneous datasets. On WikiCS node classification, BES (85.52%) surpasses the strongest baselines MPNN-SAGE (85.40%) and MPNN-GCN (84.05%) by 0.12% and 1.47% respectively, and dramatically outperforms H2GCN (76.27%) and SGNN (74.36%) which struggle with boundary discrimination. On Pubmed, BES (91.03%) leads MPNN-SAGE (89.99%) by 1.04% and GCN (87.36%) by 3.67%. For link prediction, BES achieves the best results on all four datasets, with the largest margin on Pubmed (97.89% vs. MotifRGC-GAT's 97.64%) and CiteSeer (96.27% vs. SGNN's 96.07%). Notably, GraphECL, while competitive on node classification, suffers a severe drop in link prediction on WikiCS (51.99%), whereas BES maintains strong performance (92.27%), demonstrating that boundary-focused shaping preserves edge-level representations more effectively than uniform contrastive approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the identifiability framework of von Kügelgen et al. (2021) from image contrastive learning to graph structural disentanglement, adapting the latent variable model to decompose node embeddings into invariant (class-determining) and variant (topological noise) components. It departs from prior graph contrastive learning methods (GraphECL, IGCL) that treat all nodes uniformly by introducing a boundary-focused hard example mining strategy, and from disentangled GNNs (Ma et al., 2019) by targeting structure-level rather than feature-level entanglement. The center-based gradient-equivalent approximation of the pairwise contrastive loss, combined with an adaptive pre-update mechanism, represents a practical bridge between the theoretical identifiability guarantees and scalable graph training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BES provides a principled, theoretically grounded plug-in that selectively refines decision boundaries without perturbing the full model, making it attractive for practitioners who want to improve existing GNN pipelines with minimal architectural changes. The identification of boundary nodes as the primary source of structural entanglement damage offers a clear diagnostic lens for understanding GNN failure modes. However, the method relies on training labels for boundary identification and shift score computation, limiting its applicability to low-label or self-supervised settings—a gap the authors acknowledge as future work. Scalability to very large graphs remains a practical concern given the O(L·QNKD²) complexity of the adaptive update step.
