## Problem

Existing video understanding benchmarks focus on general-purpose tasks (action recognition, commonsense QA, spatial-temporal reasoning) or long brand advertisements, leaving the high-revenue domain of conversion-oriented e-commerce short videos largely unexplored. These videos pack dense, tightly synchronized multi-modal signals—rapid visual edits, continuous speech, and on-screen text overlays—into brief clips with a clear commercial conversion goal, creating a qualitatively harder setting that current MLLMs struggle with. No dedicated benchmark systematically evaluates commercial-intent reasoning (selling points, target audiences, regulatory compliance) at scale for this domain.

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes e-commerce short videos (average ~45 s, sampled at 1 FPS) together with a text question, and the model must produce an open-ended answer preceded by an explicit reasoning trace in <think>...<answer>...</answer> format. Each video is pre-processed into a structured time-aligned context C = {⟨f_t, α_t, γ_t⟩} ⊗ M, where frames, per-second ASR chunks (from Whisper-v3-large), and de-duplicated OCR text (from Qwen2.5-VL 32B) are merged on a 1-second timeline with product metadata. Evaluation uses an LLM-as-a-judge (Qwen3-Coder-Plus) that scores responses on a five-level rubric (0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1) by cross-referencing the answer against visual, ASR, and OCR evidence, reporting Strict, Relaxed-3, and Relaxed-5 metrics.

## Method

The paper introduces E-VAds, a benchmark of 3,961 Taobao videos and 19,785 open-ended QA pairs spanning five tasks (Basic Perception, Cross-Modal Detection, Marketing Logic, Consumer Insight, Regulatory Compliance) organized under Perception and Cognition & Reasoning dimensions, constructed via a multi-agent annotation system (role-specific agents + primary judge) with strict evidence-traceability constraints and human expert verification. To quantify domain difficulty, a multi-modal information density framework defines visual dynamic density (DINOv3 frame-similarity based), audio density (ASR words/second), and textual density (OCR words/second), showing E-VAds substantially exceeds mainstream datasets on all three. For model training, E-VAds-R1 uses a two-stage pipeline (SFT for format alignment, then RL) and proposes MG-GRPO, a multi-grained reward that averages Strict, Relaxed-3, and Relaxed-5 scores to provide dense feedback during early exploration while creating a non-linear incentive jump toward fully correct, evidence-grounded answers, achieving a 109.2% relative gain in commercial intent reasoning over the base model with only ~980 RL samples.

## Results

On E-VAds, E-VAds-R1 (Qwen3-VL 8B) achieves 0.320 on the ALL metric under the strict (S) scoring, a 109.2% relative improvement over its base model Qwen3-VL-8B Instruct (0.153). The largest gain is on Regulatory Compliance (RC), where E-VAds-R1 reaches 0.279 (S), approximately 16× higher than the base model (0.017) and surpassing both GPT-5.2 (0.105) and Gemini3-Flash (0.222). Under the relaxed R5 metric, E-VAds-R1 scores 0.736 on ALL, while human experts achieve 0.871, indicating a remaining gap in commercial reasoning. The best closed-source model, Gemini3-Flash, leads overall at 0.350 (S) on ALL but is outperformed by E-VAds-R1 on RC and CI.

## Baselines & Comparisons

E-VAds-R1 (Qwen3-VL 8B) outperforms all open-source baselines on the ALL metric under strict scoring (0.320 vs. 0.153 for Qwen3-VL-8B Instruct, 0.186 for Qwen3-VL-8B Thinking, 0.206 for Keye1.5-VL, and 0.155 for Mimo-VL-RL). Against closed-source models, E-VAds-R1 surpasses GPT-5.2 on ALL (0.320 vs. 0.278), RC (0.279 vs. 0.105), and CI (0.126 vs. 0.074), but trails Gemini3-Flash on ALL (0.320 vs. 0.350) and CM (0.252 vs. 0.391). The most dramatic improvement is on RC, where E-VAds-R1 achieves 0.279 compared to 0.017 for its base model and 0.105 for GPT-5.2, a roughly 16× gain over the base. Scaling alone does not close the reasoning gap: Qwen2.5-VL-32B scores only 0.142 on ALL (S), well below the 7B E-VAds-R1 at 0.193, and Qwen3-VL-30BA3B Instruct reaches 0.196, still below E-VAds-R1's 0.320. Human experts remain a strong upper bound at 0.535 (S) on ALL, with the largest model–human gap on ML (0.313 vs. 0.173) and CI (0.126 vs. 0.250), indicating that commercial intent reasoning remains an open challenge.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the video QA benchmark lineage (VideoMME, MVBench, EgoSchema, ActivityNetQA) and the advertising video line (AdsQA, VideoAds) by targeting a distinct sub-domain: conversion-oriented e-commerce short videos with dense, tightly synchronized multimodal signals. Methodologically, it builds on GRPO from DeepSeekMath (Shao et al., 2024) by introducing MG-GRPO, a multi-grained reward ensemble that calibrates strictness levels to address sparse supervision in open-ended commercial reasoning. The multi-agent annotation pipeline and LLM-as-a-judge evaluation follow recent trends in automated benchmark construction, but the key departure is the explicit focus on commercial intent reasoning (selling points, audience inference, regulatory compliance) rather than general spatiotemporal understanding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

E-VAds fills a genuine gap: no prior benchmark systematically evaluates MLLMs on the dense, goal-driven format of e-commerce short videos, and the density metrics (especially OCR at 18.66 vs. ~4–5 for general datasets) quantify why this domain is qualitatively harder. The large remaining gap between SOTA models and human experts on Marketing Logic and Consumer Insight (even GPT-5.2 scores below 0.21 under strict scoring) highlights that commercial reasoning is not yet solved by scaling or general video understanding. Open questions include whether the MG-GRPO reward design generalizes beyond e-commerce, whether the Taobao-centric data limits cross-platform transferability, and whether the inherent commercial biases in source videos can be mitigated at scale.
