## Problem

Self-distillation (retraining a model on a mixture of ground-truth labels and its own predictions) empirically improves generalization, but formal guarantees have been limited to fixed-design, well-specified linear models with the mixing weight ξ restricted to [0,1]. No prior work established pointwise strict improvement at a fixed, suboptimal regularization level λ, characterized when the optimal ξ* becomes negative (over-regularized regimes), or provided an efficient tuning procedure that avoids grid search and sample splitting.

## Input / Output

The method takes a training dataset (X, y) ∈ R^{n×p} × R^n and a fixed ridge penalty λ > 0, and produces the optimal unconstrained mixing weight ξ*(λ) ∈ R (which can be negative), the self-distilled ridge predictor, and a one-shot risk estimate R̂_sd*(λ). The one-shot estimator computes ξ* in closed form from GCV-corrected residuals of a single teacher fit and a single pure-distillation refit at the given λ, requiring no grid search over candidate ξ values, no repeated retraining, and no hold-out split. The risk estimate is consistent in proportional asymptotics (n, p → ∞ with p/n → γ).

## Method

The core insight is that the self-distilled ridge predictor satisfies the affine identity f_sd,λ,ξ = (1−ξ)f_λ + ξ f_pd,λ, making the SD risk a quadratic in ξ. This yields the closed-form ξ* = −(λ/2)R'(λ)/D(λ), where D(λ) = E[(f_λ(x₀) − f_pd,λ(x₀))²|D] is the prediction separation, immediately implying strict improvement whenever R'(λ) ≠ 0 and sign(ξ*) = −sign(R'(λ)). For quantifying gains, the paper derives exact deterministic equivalents for R_sd*(λ) and ξ*(λ) in proportional asymptotics under general anisotropic covariance and deterministic signals, with the spectrum entering through resolvent traces t₂, t₃, t₄ and the signal through alignment functionals q₂, q₃, q₄. For practical tuning, a one-shot GCV-based estimator plugs GCV-corrected residuals into the closed-form ξ* formula, achieving consistency without grid search, refitting, or data splitting. A curvature-based sufficient condition (Proposition 2.3) further characterizes when the global minimum of the SD risk curve falls strictly below the teacher's global minimum.

## Results

The paper's central result (Theorem 2.2) proves that for ridge regression, the optimally self-distilled student strictly improves the teacher risk at every regularization level λ where R′(λ) ≠ 0, with the optimal mixing weight satisfying sign(ξ*) = −sign(R′(λ)). In the isotropic-signal proportional asymptotics regime (Corollary 3.3), the unique stationary point is λ* = γσ²/r², so ξ* > 0 for under-regularized λ < λ* and ξ* < 0 for over-regularized λ > λ*. The proposed one-shot GCV estimator (Theorem 4.1) is consistent in proportional asymptotics and avoids grid search or sample splitting. Empirically, on BlogFeedback, Communities and Crime, and CIFAR-10 (ResNet-18 features), R_sd*(λ) < R(λ) at every nonstationary λ, and the one-shot risk estimate R̂_sd*(λ) closely tracks the true test risk; the curvature test (Proposition 2.3) predicts a strictly lower global minimum for SD on BlogFeedback and Air Quality but not on Communities and Crime or CIFAR-10, matching observed behavior exactly.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares optimal unconstrained SD (ξ* ∈ ℝ) against four baselines: (1) the standard ridge teacher, (2) pure distillation (ξ = 1), (3) constrained SD with ξ restricted to [0,1], and (4) grid-search cross-validation for tuning ξ. Against the ridge teacher, optimal SD achieves strict pointwise improvement at every nonstationary λ (Theorem 2.2), a guarantee not available in prior work (Das & Sanghavi, 2023; Pareek et al., 2024), which only established global-minimum improvements under fixed-design and well-specified linear models. Against pure distillation (ξ = 1), optimal SD is strictly better in over-regularized regimes where ξ* < 0 (termed 'pro-learning'), a regime where pure distillation can actually be worse than the teacher (Moniri & Hassani, 2025). Against constrained SD (ξ ∈ [0,1]), the unconstrained version captures the negative-ξ correction for over-regularization that the constrained version misses entirely (Figure 16). Against grid-search CV, the one-shot GCV estimator matches the true optimal ξ* consistently in proportional asymptotics while requiring only a single refit and no hold-out set, avoiding the statistical inefficiency of sample splitting in high dimensions. Relative to Ildiz et al. (2025), who analyze pure distillation in the ridgeless limit, this paper captures the full λ–ξ interaction for λ > 0 under general anisotropic covariance and deterministic signals.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-distillation analysis of Das & Sanghavi (2023) from fixed-design, well-specified linear models with ξ > 1 ('anti-learning') to random design, arbitrary squared prediction risks, and fully unconstrained ξ ∈ ℝ, revealing a new negative-ξ regime ('pro-learning') that corrects over-regularization. The structural identities build on the affine path property of ridge regression in the response, while the proportional asymptotics leverage random matrix techniques from the high-dimensional ridge literature (Dobriban & Wager, 2018; Hastie et al., 2022) extended to dependent resolvent quantities via block linearization. The one-shot GCV tuning method is a direct application of the consistent risk estimation framework of Patil et al. (2021) and Bellec et al. (2025) to the SD setting, avoiding the grid-search and sample-splitting overhead of prior tuning approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The pointwise strict improvement guarantee at every nonstationary λ is practically significant because finding the globally optimal ridge penalty is known to be non-convex and difficult; knowing that optimal SD helps at any suboptimal λ provides a cheap correction mechanism. The one-shot GCV estimator offers a computationally efficient alternative to expensive grid-search CV for tuning the mixing weight, which is especially valuable in high dimensions where sample splitting is statistically costly. Key limitations include the restriction to ridge-type smoothers (extensions to kernel ridge and generalized ridge are sketched but not fully developed), the proportional asymptotics requiring specific moment and spectral conditions, and the fresh-X setting remaining only partially analyzed. Open questions include whether the regularization-path geometry and sign structure of ξ* persist for classification, nonlinear learners, and more general distillation variants.
