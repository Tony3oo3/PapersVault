## Problem

State-of-the-art explicit surface reconstruction methods like OffsetOPT achieve high geometric fidelity but rely on per-instance test-time optimization with hundreds of gradient steps, resulting in minutes-level latency per scene. This makes them impractical for real-time or large-scale scene-level applications. The paper asks whether the iterative offset refinement can be amortized into a small, fixed number of learnable steps without sacrificing reconstruction quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262981/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a dense 3D point cloud as input; for large-scale scenes, points are first voxelized using the maximum nearest-neighbor distance as the grid scale to regularize the input. It produces a 3D triangle mesh extracted via a Top-2 probability rule with confidence thresholds (0.8 and 0.5) and an angle constraint (>120°) to prevent face folding. Internally, the OON predicts per-point 3D offsets at a coarse bottleneck resolution, which are then upsampled to full resolution via inverse distance weighting plus a learnable residual.

## Method

FastSESR is a two-stage feed-forward framework. Stage 1 (TCN) processes KNN patches (K=49) through TransGNN blocks (GNN message passing + multi-head self-attention with relative position bias) and predicts triangle-candidate probabilities using an edge-factorized parameterization that decomposes K² pairwise logits into unary scores plus a low-rank (r=32) pairwise term, reducing parameters by 94% over dense prediction. Stage 2 (OON) is a U-Net-style network with cross-scale EdgeConv downsampling; at the coarsest level it unrolls T weight-shared OffsetFormer steps (vector attention + MLP) that predict per-point offsets, conditioned on TCN-derived connectivity statistics (max, mean, entropy, top-2 gap). The OON is trained unsupervised: the frozen TCN generates pseudo-labels via a Top-2 rule on the offset-deformed points, and the loss combines BCE on connectivity with an L2 offset regularizer. At inference, a single forward pass through both stages replaces the hundreds of TTO iterations, yielding ≥20× speedup.

## Results

On scene-level benchmarks, FastSESR achieves at least 20× speedup over OffsetOPT (24.5× on ScanNet, 21.9× on Matterport3D, 26.0× on CARLA) while maintaining competitive reconstruction quality. On CARLA, FastSESR attains the best overall performance among all compared methods (CD1=0.114, CD2=0.171, F1=0.990, NC=0.967, NR=4.93°), surpassing OffsetOPT on every metric. On ScanNet and Matterport3D, FastSESR matches or slightly trails OffsetOPT on Chamfer Distance (e.g., CD1=0.154 vs 0.147 on ScanNet) but leads in Normal Consistency and Normal Reconstruction Error. On shape-level ABC, FastSESR ties OffsetOPT exactly (CD1=0.283, CD2=0.540, F1=0.951) with a marginally lower NR (1.311° vs 1.318°).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary SOTA baseline OffsetOPT (CVPR 2025), FastSESR achieves a 20–26× speedup (e.g., 24.4 s vs 599 s per ScanNet scene) while remaining within ~5% on Chamfer Distance; on CARLA it actually surpasses OffsetOPT on all five metrics (CD1 0.114 vs 0.124, NR 4.93° vs 5.53°). Against implicit methods NKSR and SPSR (both requiring GT normals), FastSESR wins decisively on every scene dataset without any normal input, e.g., on ScanNet CD1 drops from 0.423 (NKSR) / 5.428 (SPSR) to 0.154. On shape-level ABC, FastSESR ties OffsetOPT exactly (CD1=0.283, F1=0.951) and marginally improves NR (1.311° vs 1.318°); on FAUST and MGN it trades a small CD1 increase (0.222 vs 0.217, 0.284 vs 0.278) for lower NR (3.73° vs 4.04°, 2.69° vs 2.97°). Against earlier explicit methods (CircNet, DSE, PointTriNet, Ball-Pivoting), FastSESR consistently leads on all metrics across both scene and shape benchmarks, with the largest gaps over SPSR and Ball-Pivoting (e.g., F1 0.999 vs 0.194 on ScanNet). The ablation in Table 4 further shows that replacing the learned OffsetFormer with SGD-based test-time optimization degrades CD1 from 0.114 to 6.745 on CARLA, confirming the learned update rule is essential.

## Relation to Prior Work

FastSESR builds directly on OffsetOPT (Lei, 2025), the current state-of-the-art for explicit surface reconstruction without normals, but replaces its per-instance test-time optimization (hundreds of gradient steps) with a two-stage feed-forward pipeline. The first stage, a triangle candidate network (TCN), departs from OffsetOPT's dense K×K pairwise logit prediction by introducing an edge-factorized parameterization that decomposes connectivity scores into unary and low-rank pairwise terms, reducing parameters by ~94%. The second stage, an offset optimization network (OON), amortizes the iterative offset refinement into a small number of weight-shared OffsetFormer steps guided by TCN connectivity signals, drawing on multi-scale GNN architectures (EdgeConv, TransGNN) and vector attention from the point cloud literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 20×+ speedup (minutes to seconds for scene-level reconstruction) makes explicit surface reconstruction practical for real-time applications in robotics, autonomous driving, and digital content creation, where OffsetOPT's iterative optimization is prohibitive. The ablation showing that a few learned OffsetFormer steps dramatically outperform even 10 steps of generic SGD-based TTO (CD1: 0.114 vs. 6.7) provides strong evidence that amortized optimization captures a more efficient update rule than generic gradient descent. Open questions include robustness to strong Gaussian noise (where FastSESR degrades similarly to OffsetOPT), cross-dataset generalization at scene scale, and whether the TCN's reliance on uniform sampling limits applicability to raw, irregularly-sampled point clouds in the wild.
