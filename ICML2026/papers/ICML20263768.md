## Problem

Existing literature addresses unconstrained OCO with movement costs and dynamic regret with movement costs in isolation, and all prior works assume fixed movement penalties. This paper tackles the unexplored intersection: achieving near-optimal comparator-adaptive dynamic regret in unconstrained domains where movement cost coefficients λ_t vary arbitrarily over time, a setting motivated by applications like portfolio rebalancing with fluctuating transaction fees.

## Input / Output

At each round t, the algorithm receives a gradient vector g_t ∈ R^n and the next-round movement cost coefficient λ_{t+1} ≥ 0, and outputs a decision vector w_t ∈ R^n in the unconstrained space. The algorithm is fully parameter-free: it requires no prior knowledge of comparator norms M, path length P_T, gradient norms, or the sequence of λ_t values (beyond a standard Lipschitz constant G and a doubling trick for λ_max). The regret guarantee adapts simultaneously to the realized gradient sequence, the individual λ_t values, and the comparator complexity (M, P_T).

## Method

The core algorithm is a three-layer construction. The base learner (Algorithm 1) is a Composite Mirror Descent step using a log-linear regularizer ψ(w) = (2/η)∫₀^‖w‖ log(x/α+1)dx combined with a correction term φ_t(w) = (ηβ_t² + γ)‖w‖ where β_t = ‖g_t‖ + λ_{t+1}, so the penalty dynamically scales with both gradient magnitude and the upcoming movement cost. A meta-algorithm (Algorithm 2) runs parallel instances with geometrically spaced learning rates and plays their sum, achieving parameter-free adaptivity. The key refinement (Algorithm 3) is an adaptive batching layer that accumulates gradients within epochs and only triggers a base-learner update when the cumulative gradient norm exceeds the current λ_{t+1}, improving the movement-cost dependence from the suboptimal ∑λ_t² to the first-order ∑λ_t‖g_t‖. This first-order dependence is essential for the paper's applications: it enables a novel reduction from OCO with delayed feedback (where λ_t = G·|m_t| is the number of missing gradients) and from OCO with time-varying memory (where λ_t = G·ξ_t encodes the memory structure), yielding the first parameter-free dynamic regret bounds in both unconstrained settings.

## Results

The paper establishes the first comparator-adaptive dynamic regret bound for unconstrained OCO with time-varying movement costs: $\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}\!\left(\sqrt{(M+P_T)\sum_t(\|g_t\|^2+\lambda_t\|g_t\|)\|u_t\|}\right)$ (Theorem 4.1), achieving first-order (linear) dependence on movement costs rather than the quadratic $\lambda_t^2$ of the initial algorithm (Theorem 3.1). For delayed feedback, the bound is $\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}\!\left(\sqrt{(M^2+MP_T)(T+d_{\mathrm{tot}})}\right)$ (Theorem 5.2), and for time-varying memory it is $\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}\!\left(\sqrt{(M^2+MP_T)(H^2T+GH\sum_t b_t^2)}\right)$ (Theorem 5.7). All bounds are parameter-free, adapting to comparator norm $M$, path length $P_T$, realized gradient norms, and time-varying coefficients without prior knowledge.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Wan et al. (2024) for delayed feedback, this paper improves the delay dependence from $d_{\max}\!\cdot\!T$ to $d_{\mathrm{tot}}$ (where $d_{\mathrm{tot}}\leq d_{\max}\!\cdot\!T$) and removes the restrictive in-order feedback assumption required by Wan et al. to achieve the $d_{\mathrm{tot}}$ rate; additionally, the result extends to unconstrained domains where Wan et al. only handle bounded convex sets. Against Zhao et al. (2023) for OCO with memory, this paper handles time-varying memory lengths $b_t$ (vs. fixed $B$) and unconstrained domains (vs. bounded diameter $D$), while improving the dependence on the Lipschitz constant from $\sqrt{G}H^2B$ to $GH\sum_t b_t^2$. Against Zhang et al. (2022b) in the 1-D static special case, the adaptive sum $\sum_t\|g_t\|^2+\lambda\sum_t\|g_t\|$ replaces the worst-case $G^2T+\lambda GT$, yielding tighter bounds when gradients are small. The paper also introduces a novel reduction from delayed feedback to time-varying movement costs (Lemma 5.1), which is of independent interest and has no direct analogue in prior work.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two previously separate lines of work: unconstrained OCO with movement costs (Zhang et al., 2022a,b) and dynamic regret with movement costs (Zhang et al., 2021), explicitly noting that their intersection 'remains unexplored.' The algorithmic core extends the composite mirror descent framework of Jacobsen & Cutkosky (2022) with a log-linear regularizer and a correction term scaled by (‖g_t‖ + λ_{t+1})², while the adaptive batching meta-algorithm (Algorithm 3) generalizes the static-regret batching of Zhang et al. (2022b) to handle both time-varying coefficients and drifting comparators. The key conceptual departure from all prior movement-cost work is allowing λ_t to vary arbitrarily over time, which is essential for the delayed-feedback and time-varying-memory applications.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The first-order dependence on movement costs (λ_t‖g_t‖ rather than λ_t²) is the technical linchpin that makes the delayed-feedback reduction work, yielding a d_tot-dependent bound without the in-order feedback assumption required by Wan et al. (2024). The reduction from delayed feedback to time-varying movement costs—treating the number of missing gradients as the movement scale—is described as a novel connection of independent interest, potentially opening a new analytical lens for other delayed-feedback variants. Limitations include the need for prior knowledge of G and λ_max (mitigated by a doubling trick) and the fact that the bounds, while near-optimal in form, have not been shown to be minimax tight in the time-varying setting. Open questions include whether the adaptive batching can be made fully parameter-free (removing the L requirement) and whether the framework extends to non-convex or bandit settings.
