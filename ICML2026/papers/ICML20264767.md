## Problem

Existing dynamic data selection methods for LLM pre-training score candidates in raw gradient space, implicitly assuming SGD dynamics, while modern training relies on adaptive optimizers (AdamW, Muon) that precondition and reshape the effective update direction. This misalignment between scoring geometry and actual optimizer trajectory causes suboptimal selection, and static filters ignore the model's evolving state entirely. OPUS addresses this by defining data utility in the optimizer-induced update space, making selection principled under the specific optimizer geometry.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264767/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At each training step, OPUS consumes a candidate buffer of N text sequences (scored using only 512 tokens per sequence for efficiency), a proxy mini-batch drawn from a pre-constructed BENCH-PROXY pool (benchmark-aligned documents retrieved from the corpus via a frozen sentence encoder), and the current optimizer state (momentum and variance buffers). It outputs a selected subset of K = ⌊ρN⌋ sequences for the parameter update, along with per-candidate scalar utility scores that combine proxy-direction alignment and an in-step geometric redundancy penalty. The utility scores are also usable as a static filter to flag toxic or corrupted documents without any training.

## Method

OPUS derives closed-form optimizer-induced preconditioners P_t for AdamW (diagonal, from frozen RMS geometry) and Muon (dense, from a frozen Newton-Schulz linearization), then scores each candidate by projecting its preconditioned gradient onto a proxy descent direction, minus a redundancy term penalizing alignment with already-selected updates. To avoid materializing full per-sample gradients, it exploits the rank-1 outer-product structure of linear-layer gradients (ghost technique) and applies CountSketch random projections to reduce inner-product computation to a low-dimensional sketch space, incurring only 4.7% additional compute overhead. The proxy direction is constructed via BENCH-PROXY, which retrieves the most semantically similar pre-training documents to benchmark validation samples using a frozen text encoder, yielding a stable in-distribution target. Finally, Boltzmann soft sampling with temperature τ replaces greedy top-k selection to preserve data diversity and avoid over-concentration on noisy proxy estimates.

## Results

On FineWeb with 30B update tokens, OPUS achieves the best compute-matched average of 41.75 on GPT-2 XL with Muon (vs. 40.29 for random) and 41.43 on GPT-2 Large with AdamW (vs. 39.29 for random), outperforming all static and dynamic baselines. On the stricter FineWeb-Edu setting where OPUS selects from the lower-quality score-3 subset while baselines train on score-4+5, OPUS reaches 44.99 average, exceeding every baseline trained on the superior data. In continued pre-training of Qwen3-8B-Base on SciencePedia, OPUS reaches the best SciAssess/OlympicArena performance using only 0.5B tokens, already surpassing random CPT trained for 3B tokens (6× data efficiency).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OPUS outperforms all seven baselines on average in both optimizer settings: on GPT-2 XL + Muon it reaches 41.75 vs. the best static baseline FineWeb-Edu (40.74) and best dynamic baseline QuRating (40.72); on GPT-2 Large + AdamW it reaches 41.43 vs. UltraFineweb (40.38) and FineWeb-Edu (40.24). Notably, OPUS at 30B tokens matches or exceeds Random trained at 60B tokens (41.75 vs. 41.29 on GPT-2 XL/Muon; 41.43 vs. 39.60 on GPT-2 Large/AdamW), indicating roughly 2× compute savings. On the stricter FineWeb-Edu experiment (Table 2), OPUS selecting from the lower-quality score-3 subset achieves 44.99 average, beating every baseline trained on the superior score-4+5 partition (best: PPL at 42.27, DCLM-FastText at 42.24). On out-of-distribution benchmarks (Table 4), OPUS leads with 40.07 average vs. GREATS (39.42) and FineWeb-Edu (39.08), confirming gains are not proxy-overfitting. In continued pre-training, OPUS at 0.5B CPT tokens already surpasses random CPT at 3B tokens on both SciAssess and OlympicArena, a 6× data-efficiency gain over the strongest baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

OPUS extends the dynamic data selection framework introduced by GREATS (Wang et al., 2024), which first formalized per-step utility scoring via Taylor-approximated validation loss reduction and the ghost technique for avoiding per-sample gradient materialization. The key departure is that all prior dynamic selectors (GREATS, MATES, Group-MATES) score candidates in raw gradient space, implicitly assuming SGD-like update geometry, whereas OPUS re-derives the utility objective under the optimizer-induced preconditioner (diagonal for AdamW, dense Newton-Schulz for Muon), aligning the scoring signal with the trajectory the optimizer actually follows. The BENCH-PROXY retrieval-based proxy construction and the CountSketch + Boltzmann sampling pipeline are novel engineering contributions layered on top of this theoretical re-framing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As high-quality public text approaches exhaustion (the Data Wall), the ability to extract more learning signal per token becomes critical, and OPUS's 4.7% overhead makes optimizer-aware selection practically deployable at scale. The finding that selecting from mid-quality data (FineWeb-Edu score 3) can outperform training on high-quality data (score 4+5) suggests that dynamic, model-aware selection can partially substitute for static quality filtering, which has implications for how training pipelines are structured. Open questions include whether the optimizer-geometry advantage persists at frontier model scale (100B+), whether the isotropic Hessian approximation (H≈I) remains valid in later training stages, and how the method extends to multi-domain data mixtures where a single proxy direction may be insufficient.
