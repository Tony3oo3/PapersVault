## Problem

Despite widespread adoption of Mixture-of-Experts architectures in frontier LLMs, it remains unclear how close existing designs are to optimal accuracy per FLOP and per parameter. Prevailing MoE architectures are motivated primarily by high-level sparsity arguments and optimized for offline throughput, with limited consideration for the memory-bandwidth and all-to-all communication bottlenecks that dominate latency-critical and distributed inference. The paper identifies structural inefficiencies in how expert computation is parameterized at the full hidden dimension and how routing-induced communication scales with model size.

## Input / Output

The model consumes sequences of text tokens (up to 8192 tokens per sequence) and produces next-token predictions in a standard autoregressive language-modeling setup. Internally, each input token of dimension d is down-projected to a latent dimension ℓ = d/α before expert routing and computation, then up-projected back to d after expert aggregation. The architecture is validated at scales from 16B to 95B total parameters (2B–8B active) and projected to the trillion-parameter regime, with inference evaluated on H100 and GB200 hardware using FP4/FP8 quantization.

## Method

LatentMoE decouples expert routing and computation from the model hidden dimension by projecting each token x ∈ ℝ^d into a lower-dimensional latent space ℝ^ℓ via a learnable down-projection W↓, performing all routed expert computation (FC1, gate, FC2) in the latent space, and projecting back via W↑. This reduces both all-to-all communication volume and per-expert weight-loading bandwidth by a factor α = d/ℓ. The freed budget is reinvested by scaling the total expert count N and top-K active experts by the same factor α (the ℓ-MoE_acc variant), which exponentially expands the combinatorial specialization space C(αN, αK) while keeping the effective nonlinear budget K·m and total inference cost unchanged. A second variant, ℓ-MoE_eff, keeps K fixed and achieves the same accuracy at substantially fewer FLOPs. The design is grounded in a roofline-guided analysis identifying memory bandwidth (latency regime) and all-to-all communication (throughput regime) as the binding bottlenecks, with a theoretical lower bound r_eff on the latent dimension to prevent information loss.

## Results

On the 95B-parameter Transformer (95BT-8BA), ℓ-MoE_acc achieves 34.91 MMLU Pro, 62.23 MMLU, 41.50 Code, 64.88 Math, and 75.18 Commonsense, outperforming the standard MoE baseline (29.26 / 58.95 / 40.33 / 64.39 / 74.32) by +5.65, +3.28, +1.17, +0.49, and +0.86 points respectively at iso-parameter count. On the 73B Hybrid Mamba-Attention model trained for 1T tokens, ℓ-MoE_acc reaches 52.87 MMLU Pro and 55.14 Code, surpassing the baseline (48.30 / 51.95) by +4.57 and +3.19 points. The efficiency variant ℓ-MoE_eff matches or approaches baseline accuracy while using only 5.62B active parameters (vs. 8.47B), a ~34% reduction in per-token FLOPs. At projected trillion-parameter scale, KIMI-K2-1T-LATENTMOE is 1.24×–3.46× faster than the iso-accuracy standard MoE (KIMI-K2-1.35T) across the throughput-latency Pareto frontier.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is a standard MoE architecture with identical total parameter count but full hidden-dimension expert computation (no latent projection). ℓ-MoE_acc wins on every evaluated benchmark at both the 95B Transformer scale (e.g., +5.65 MMLU Pro, +3.28 MMLU) and the 73B Hybrid Mamba-Attention scale (e.g., +4.57 MMLU Pro, +3.19 Code), confirming that the latent-space routing plus expanded expert diversity yields consistent accuracy gains at iso-parameter and iso-FLOP budgets. ℓ-MoE_eff, which keeps K unchanged rather than scaling it by α, achieves near-baseline accuracy (within ~1 point on most tasks) while reducing active parameters by ~34%, making it a strong efficiency-oriented option. At the projected 1T-parameter scale, matching ℓ-MoE_acc's accuracy with standard MoE scaling requires ~350B additional parameters (KIMI-K2-1.35T), resulting in a 1.24×–3.46× throughput penalty across the serving Pareto frontier. The paper also notes that ℓ-MoE_acc incurs at most a ~6% throughput drop relative to standard MoE at higher batch sizes on H100 GPUs, meaning the accuracy gains come at negligible inference-speed cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DeepSeek-V2 MoE architecture as its base configuration but fundamentally departs from the prevailing design paradigm of running all expert computation at the full model hidden dimension. The closest prior work—MoLAE (post-training low-rank expert compression) and BigMac (lower-dimension token dispatch with FFN width expansion)—both project into a latent space but reinvest the freed budget differently: MoLAE restricts compression to a subset of expert components, while BigMac enlarges the intermediate dimension. LatentMoE instead scales both total and active expert counts by the compression ratio, a choice grounded in a roofline-guided hardware-software co-design analysis that prior MoE work largely neglected. The paper also positions itself as complementary to MLA (attention-side KV-cache compression) and mHC (residual connectivity), which target orthogonal bottlenecks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LatentMoE has already been adopted in the Nemotron-3 Super and Ultra frontier models, and the projected trillion-parameter results (a 1T LatentMoE matching a 1.35T standard MoE, eliminating ~350B parameters of serving cost) suggest a practical path to reducing inference energy and GPU requirements for frontier-quality models. The roofline-guided design framework—identify the binding bottleneck, derive an architectural lever, validate at scale—is explicitly framed as a reusable template for future MoE modifications. Open questions include whether the EPM-based iso-accuracy extrapolation holds beyond the non-saturating MMLU regime, whether kernel-level optimizations can close the remaining ~6% throughput gap at small batch sizes, and whether the approach generalizes to hardware platforms with different compute-to-bandwidth ratios.
