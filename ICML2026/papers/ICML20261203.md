## Problem

Hypergraph generation is limited by the combinatorial explosion of possible hyperedges (O(2^|V|)), forcing existing methods to rely on either ambiguous representations (clique expansion, as in HyperPLR) or iterative local expansion (as in HYGENE) that accumulates errors. These compromises degrade generation quality in both local structural patterns and global graph-level validity, leaving a gap for an exact, tractable generative model for hypergraphs.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a noised graph-superposition: L binary adjacency matrices (one per layer) sharing the same N-node set, progressively corrupted by a discrete forward diffusion process. It outputs a clean graph-superposition from which the synthetic hypergraph is recovered exactly by extracting maximal cliques from each layer. The representation is injective—each graph-superposition maps to exactly one hypergraph—so no information is lost in the round trip, unlike clique expansion where one graph can correspond to multiple hypergraphs.

## Method

SuperHype introduces a graph-superposition decomposition that embeds a hypergraph into L layered graphs (typically 1–6 layers) such that each hyperedge becomes a maximal clique in exactly one layer, reducing representation cost from O(|V|·2^|V|) to O(|V|²L) while preserving exactness. A greedy projection algorithm (Algorithm 1) assigns hyperedges to layers by checking that adding a hyperedge's pairwise edges creates exactly one new maximal clique without disturbing existing ones. The Graph-Superposition Transformer extends DiGress-style discrete diffusion with a dual-parameter design: shared θ_single blocks process each layer independently (preserving layer-specific edge assignments), while θ_multi blocks operate on summed cross-layer node and graph embeddings to capture inter-layer dependencies. Triplet aggregation (adapted from Hussain et al., 2024) is integrated into the layer-specific module to model dependencies between node pairs via shared neighbors, improving maximal-clique formation. Hypergraph-specific auxiliary features—counts of 3-cliques and 4-cliques at node, edge, and graph levels—further guide the denoising process, and the model is trained with a cross-entropy loss over all binary edge entries across all layers.

## Results

On the SBM dataset, SuperHype achieves 88.3% valid hypergraphs with a node-degree Wasserstein distance of 0.68 and spectral MMD of 0.003, substantially outperforming HYGENE (63% valid, 1.7 node-degree, 0.026 spectral). On the Tree dataset, SuperHype reaches 90% validity with near-zero errors (node-degree 0.0037, edge-size 0.0032, spectral 0.00031), compared to HYGENE's 79.2% validity and 0.0079 spectral. On Erdős-Rényi, SuperHype leads on four of five metrics (spectral 0.00092 vs. HYGENE's 0.0047; edge-size 0.006 vs. 0.14). On the larger ModelNet40 Bookshelf dataset, all methods struggle, with SuperHype (node-degree 3.2, spectral 0.029) performing comparably to HyperPLR (3.0, 0.0019) and better than HYGENE (8.2, 0.061) on node-degree and spectral.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SuperHype outperforms HYGENE in 12 out of 15 metric-dataset scenarios across SBM, Ego, and Tree. The most dramatic gains are on the Tree dataset, where SuperHype achieves 90% validity versus HYGENE's 79.2%, and a spectral distance 25× lower (0.00031 vs. 0.0079). On SBM, SuperHype improves validity from 63% to 88.3% and reduces spectral distance by ~9× (0.003 vs. 0.026). The one clear loss is Ego validity (50.6% vs. HYGENE's 95.6%), which the authors attribute to maximal-clique splitting during denoising; however, SuperHype still beats HYGENE on all other Ego metrics (node-degree 0.079 vs. 0.15, edge-size 0.008 vs. 0.45, spectral 0.0013 vs. 0.0056). Against HyperPLR, SuperHype is consistently superior on all datasets except ModelNet40 Bookshelf, where HyperPLR achieves a lower spectral distance (0.0019 vs. 0.029) and edge-size error (0 vs. 0.076), though both methods produce node-degree errors too large for practical use on that dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

SuperHype extends the discrete graph diffusion framework of DiGress (Vignac et al., 2023) from standard graphs to hypergraphs by introducing a graph-superposition decomposition that maps each hypergraph to a small set of layered graphs with an injective (lossless) projection. It departs from prior hypergraph generators—HyperPLR, which relies on ambiguous clique expansion followed by greedy reconstruction, and HYGENE, which uses an exponential-cost bipartite representation with iterative local expansion—by operating directly on an exact, tractable multi-layer graph state space. The triplet-aggregation mechanism is adapted from Hussain et al. (2024) to encourage consistent edge decisions within node triples, addressing the specific challenge of maximal-clique formation in the superposition layers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The graph-superposition decomposition resolves a fundamental tension in hypergraph representation: the bipartite form is exact but scales as O(2^|V|), while clique expansion is tractable but lossy. By showing that at most six layers suffice for all tested datasets and that the projection is injective, the paper provides a practical path to exact hypergraph generation via standard graph diffusion tooling. Key open questions include scalability beyond ~200 nodes (the dense implementation is the bottleneck), whether hard combinatorial constraints could eliminate the observed clique-splitting failure mode on ego hypergraphs, and whether sparse discrete diffusion formulations can be integrated to reduce the O(N^2.37) per-layer cost.
