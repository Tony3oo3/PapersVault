## Problem

FTRL is the natural framework for curved losses (e.g., linear and logistic regression) and for understanding Adam's momentum structure, yet existing dynamic regret analyses are largely built around OMD-type methods and fail to cover these FTRL-based settings. Moreover, the prior Adam convergence analysis via the discounted-to-dynamic (D2D) reduction (Ahn & Cutkosky, 2024) is restricted to the coupled parameter choice β₂ = β₁², which is narrower than the (β₁, β₂) pairs used in practice.

## Input / Output

In the online regression protocol, the learner sequentially receives feature vectors z_t ∈ ℝ^d and labels y_t, and must output a decision x_t before seeing the label; the analysis is stated for unconstrained decision sets (x_t ∈ ℝ^d) with bounded comparators. For the Adam analysis, the input is a sequence of stochastic gradients g_t with bounded norm G and variance σ², and the output is an EMA iterate x̄ whose expected (c,ε)-stationarity norm is bounded. A notable structural detail is that the clip-free Adam variant introduces an extra damping term γμ(1−β₁ᵗ) in the denominator, making it a damped approximation of standard Adam rather than a clipped one.

## Method

The core contribution is a modular D2D reduction that operates at the level of untuned upper-bound templates on rescaled losses (Theorem 1), keeping stability terms Λ_t, comparator-dependent quantities φ_t(u), and path-variation terms F_t^{β,φ} explicit throughout the derivation rather than collapsing them into a single tuned bound. For curved losses, a rescaling trick (z̃_t = β^{−t/2} z_t) converts the discounted FTRL into a standard FTRL on rescaled data, after which the modular reduction directly yields dynamic regret bounds; this streamlines the linear regression proof and produces a new O(dB log(BT) + √(dBTP_T^β)) guarantee for logistic regression without the exponential e^B dependence. For Adam, the D2D reduction is combined with the exponentiated O2NC conversion, and a new self-confident tuning lemma handles the non-monotone learning rates that arise when β₂ ≠ β₁², while a scale-free-style argument (Orabona & Pal, 2018) controls the step-size deviation |η_t − η_{t−1}| to derive margin-style conditions on β₂ − β₁². This yields optimal convergence rates for both clipped and clip-free Adam under flexible (β₁, β₂) choices, relaxing the prior β₂ = β₁² restriction to β₂ ≥ β₁⁴ (clipped) or β₂ ≥ β₁² (clip-free).

## Results

The paper's primary contributions are theoretical: for online logistic regression, the discounted AIOLI algorithm achieves O(dB log(BT) + √(dBTP_T)) dynamic regret with Õ(d²) per-round cost, avoiding the exponential e^B dependence of prior methods. For online linear regression, the discounted VAW forecaster recovers the optimal O(d log T + √(dTP_T^β)) dynamic regret. For Adam optimizers in stochastic non-convex non-smooth settings, the paper establishes optimal convergence rates to (c,ε)-stationary points under flexible (β₁,β₂) choices, with iteration complexity O(max{(G+σ)²F*c^{1/2}ε^{-7/2}, (G+σ)³ε^{-3}, (G+σ)ν^{-1}}). Empirically, on ResNet-18/CIFAR-10 with λℓ₁ regularization, both clipped and clip-free Adam variants consistently outperform default Adam, with the gap widening as non-smoothness (λ) increases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For online logistic regression, the proposed discounted AIOLI (Theorem 3) achieves O(dB log(BT) + √(dBTP_T)) dynamic regret, which is linear in B rather than exponential (e^B) as in Baby & Wang (2021), and has a √T dependence on the path length P_T rather than the T^{1/3}P_T^{2/3} scaling of both Baby & Wang (2021) and Zhang et al. (2025). The per-round computational cost is Õ(d²), substantially lower than Õ(d³) for Baby & Wang (2021) and the prohibitively expensive Õ(d^{12}T^{11}+T^{23}) for Zhang et al. (2025). For Adam convergence, the paper extends beyond Ahn & Cutkosky (2024), which is restricted to the coupled choice β₂=β₁², by providing optimal rates under the more general conditions β₂ ≥ max{1−ν/(G+σ), β₁⁴} (clipped) or β₂ ≥ max{1−ν/(G+σ), β₁²} (clip-free), at the cost of assuming bounded stochastic gradients. Empirically on ResNet-18/CIFAR-10, both the clipped and clip-free Adam variants outperform default Adam (Kingma & Ba, 2015) across all tested regularization strengths, with the advantage growing as λℓ₁ non-smoothness increases, while bias correction only affects early-stage behavior without altering final convergence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the discounted-to-dynamic (D2D) reduction of Ahn et al. (2024), which connected Adam's momentum parameters to discounted FTRL regret, and on the O2NC conversion framework of Cutkosky et al. (2023) and Zhang & Cutkosky (2024) for non-smooth non-convex optimization. The key methodological departure is working at the level of untuned upper-bound templates on rescaled losses rather than plugging in a pre-tuned closed-form bound, making the reduction modular and reusable across algorithmic families. For curved losses, it streamlines the Bregman-divergence-based analysis of Jacobsen & Cutkosky (2024) for linear regression and extends the framework to logistic regression via a discounted AIOLI variant, where prior FTRL dynamic regret results were unavailable. For Adam, it generalizes the restricted β₂=β₁² analysis of Ahn & Cutkosky (2024) to flexible (β₁,β₂) regimes for both clipped and clip-free variants.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The modular D2D template provides a reusable analytical tool for deriving dynamic regret guarantees of FTRL-based methods in nonstationary settings, potentially applicable beyond the two curved losses studied here. The flexible (β₁,β₂) convergence conditions for Adam are practically relevant: they clarify that clipping can weaken the momentum coupling (down to β₂≥β₁⁴) and that the clip-free damping variant achieves comparable guarantees, offering practitioners more freedom in hyperparameter selection. Limitations include the requirement of bounded stochastic gradients for the flexible-parameter results (a trade-off not present in the β₂=β₁² special case) and the open question of whether the approach recovers optimal dynamic regret for general convex losses. The empirical validation is limited to a single architecture (ResNet-18) and dataset (CIFAR-10) with three seeds, leaving broader practical impact untested.
