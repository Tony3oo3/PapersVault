## Problem

Existing knowledge augmentation methods for LLMs operate on the simplistic premise that correct responses equate to internal knowledge and errors equate to knowledge deficits, ignoring the knowledge-confidence gaps that cause overconfident hallucinations or unwarranted uncertainty on correct answers. This misalignment between subjective confidence and objective accuracy means that blindly supplementing models with external knowledge fails to resolve—and may even exacerbate—the underlying cognitive miscalibration.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a text question as input and, during the CGKE phase, retrieves top-5 Wikipedia passages via UltraRAG (Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B) to construct region-specific augmented training samples. During the CDKC phase, the model generates K=16 stochastic reasoning paths per query to build a behavioral profile of (answer, uncertainty, correctness) tuples, which drives both the meta-cognitive region assignment and the entropy-based calibration signal. The final output is an answer text whose internal confidence distribution has been explicitly calibrated so that high-entropy states correspond to genuinely uncertain knowledge and low-entropy states to verified correct reasoning.

## Method

The framework has two stages. First, Cognition-Guided Knowledge Expansion (CGKE) probes the model via stochastic decoding to partition each training instance into Mastered, Confused, or Missing regions using a meta-cognitive assignment prompt, then applies region-specific augmentation: epistemic foundation (instantiating missing facts from retrieved passages), structural disambiguation (resolving fragmented reasoning via decomposed sub-questions), or boundary expansion (extending stable knowledge through cross-concept connections), followed by unified SFT with LoRA. Second, Cognition-Driven Knowledge Calibration (CDKC) introduces a Cognitive Consistency Alignment loss that modulates sequence-level entropy with a binary performance-contingent multiplier: α=+1 for correct paths (reinforcing peaked confidence) and α=−1 for incorrect paths (incentivizing uniform hesitation). This calibration loss is integrated into GRPO as L_total = L_pg + λ₁·L_KL + λ₂·L_cal, where the entropy gradient acts as a corrective vector field that counteracts the KL-driven 'refusal trap' identified in the theoretical analysis. The entire pipeline can be iterated (2-round variant) so that calibrated uncertainty from one cycle guides more targeted expansion in the next.

## Results

On 11 QA benchmarks spanning weakly-, partially-, and well-grounded knowledge states, CDKC achieves an average accuracy of 44.97% on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and 51.44% on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, outperforming the strongest baseline GRPO by 1.06% and 1.73% respectively. The two-round iterative variant (CDKC w/2 round) further elevates performance to 48.29% and 55.50%, representing gains of 3.32% and 4.06% over single-round CDKC. On the self-knowledge benchmark, CDKC achieves a Cognitive Balance Score of 73.43% and Cognitive Alignment Efficiency of 68.18%, substantially surpassing all baselines in distinguishing knowns from unknowns.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CDKC outperforms all eight baselines across both backbone models. Against the strongest baseline GRPO, CDKC gains 1.06% average accuracy on Qwen2.5-7B (44.97 vs. 43.91) and 1.73% on Llama-3.1-8B (51.44 vs. 49.71), with the two-round variant extending these gaps to 4.38% and 5.79%. CDKC also surpasses BARREL (33.04/45.66) by 11.93%/5.78%, CRew-DPO (30.26/38.56) by 14.71%/12.88%, and Know What (28.88/35.58) by 16.09%/15.86% in average accuracy. In the knowledge expansion stage, CGKE (29.15/36.52) modestly outperforms Vanilla SFT (27.98/34.69) and LLKD-SFT (28.65/34.44), while RAG (37.11/37.71) remains competitive on weakly-grounded tasks but is surpassed by CDKC on the overall average. Notably, CDKC achieves superior performance to RAG without any retrieval overhead at inference time, and on the self-knowledge benchmark, CDKC's 63.37% True Positive rate on answerable questions far exceeds Know What's 2.05%, demonstrating that calibration-based methods avoid the over-conservative refusal failure mode of supervised abstention approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends GRPO (DeepSeek-R1) by adding a bidirectional entropy-based calibration loss (CDKC) that modulates confidence based on per-trajectory correctness, and pairs it with a meta-cognitive data augmentation stage (CGKE) that partitions training instances into mastered/confused/missing regions before SFT. Conceptually, it departs from prior knowledge augmentation work (SFT, RAG, distillation) that treats all errors as uniform knowledge deficits, and from calibration methods (Know What, CRew-DPO, BARREL) that apply uniform confidence penalties, by grounding both expansion and calibration in the model's own internal uncertainty signals. The 'Structural Decay Law'—an empirical exponential relationship between sequence-level NLL and accuracy—serves as the theoretical bridge justifying why these internal signals can reliably index knowledge state.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The meta-cognitive framing offers a principled diagnostic for *where* to intervene (missing vs. confused vs. mastered) rather than blindly augmenting, and the iterative expansion-calibration loop provides a practical recipe for practitioners. The substantial ECE reduction (>59%) and the self-knowledge results (63% TP vs. 98% FN for Know What) demonstrate that the model learns to genuinely distinguish knowns from unknowns rather than defaulting to refusal. However, the raw accuracy gains over GRPO alone are modest (~1–2%), the region assignment relies on a prompted self-evaluation that may not generalize beyond QA, and experiments are limited to 7B–8B models on English QA benchmarks, leaving open whether the framework scales to larger models or non-English/multilingual settings.
