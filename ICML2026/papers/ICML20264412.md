## Problem

Prior token cramming work showed a single embedding can reconstruct up to 1568 tokens at ~99% teacher-forced accuracy, but this protocol masks a critical failure mode: residual errors concentrated at the earliest token positions cause near-total collapse under autoregressive generation. Beyond this methodological gap, it remains unclear whether cramming discovers dense semantic representations the model can interpret or merely exploits brittle steering through attention mechanisms. The paper addresses both: establishing a reliable 100%-reconstruction evaluation protocol and probing what perfect reconstruction actually encodes.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text token sequence (e.g., from PG19) and produces a single learned compression embedding in the frozen LLM's input embedding space (dimension 2048–4096 depending on model). The embedding is prepended as the sole input to the frozen autoregressive decoder, which must reconstruct the original sequence from it alone. In the progressive variant, the output is a trajectory of embeddings {e^(k)} indexed by prefix length, each guaranteed to perfectly reconstruct its prefix under greedy decoding; the procedure halts at the first prefix length where perfect reconstruction is unattainable within the fixed per-token optimization budget.

## Method

Progressive cramming grows the reconstruction target one token at a time: at each stage k, the compression embedding is warm-started from the previous stage's converged solution and optimized until the length-k prefix achieves 100% token-argmax match, then a new token is appended. A low-dimensional projection (e = Wz + b, k ≪ d) constrains the embedding to a rank-k affine subspace, stabilizing optimization and reducing learning-rate sensitivity. The paper then uses this reliable compression tool to probe the mechanism: PCA analysis reveals trajectories occupy only 30–100 dimensions; attention-knockout interventions causally localize downstream capability loss to the embedding's interactions in early transformer layers rather than the late layers carrying the most attention mass; and evaluation on HellaSwag, ARC, and MMLU shows that perfect reconstruction coincides with significant capability degradation, indicating cramming achieves reconstruction through brittle attention-based steering rather than transferable semantic encoding.

## Results

Progressive cramming guarantees 100% teacher-forced and 100% greedy reconstruction accuracy, whereas full cramming (Kuratov et al., 2025) achieves only ~99% TF accuracy and collapses to ~40% greedy accuracy on Llama-3.1-8B (40.44±48.63%) and Pythia-1.4B (44.19±44.82%). In terms of compressed token count, progressive cramming slightly underperforms: 1438±380 tokens on Llama-3.1-8B vs. 1568 for full cramming, and 430±65 vs. 512 on Pythia-1.4B. On downstream multiple-choice evaluation (HellaSwag, ARC-Easy), prepending a crammed embedding causes a moderate but consistent accuracy drop (e.g., Llama-3.1-8B HellaSwag: 61.91% baseline → 40.80% progressive, 40.00% full), and under generative 5-shot MMLU evaluation the drop becomes a near-complete collapse to near-zero accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against full cramming (Kuratov et al., 2025), progressive cramming trades a small reduction in compressed token count (1438 vs. 1568 on Llama-3.1-8B; 430 vs. 512 on Pythia-1.4B) for a qualitative guarantee: 100% reconstruction under autoregressive greedy decoding, where full cramming's residual ~1% teacher-forcing error (concentrated at the first two token positions) causes greedy accuracy to collapse to ~40%. On downstream multiple-choice benchmarks (HellaSwag, ARC-Easy), progressive and full cramming perform comparably, both causing a moderate but consistent accuracy drop of roughly 15–20 percentage points below the uncompressed baseline even when the original prefix remains in context. Against prefix tuning (Li & Liang, 2021), progressive cramming is far less parameter-efficient: prefix tuning compresses up to 8k tokens with perfect reconstruction on Llama-3.1-8B (vs. ~1438 for progressive cramming) and ~4096 tokens on Llama-3.2-1B (vs. ~402), but at the cost of L× more trainable embeddings (one per layer). The paper's key finding is that neither cramming variant preserves downstream semantic utility—reconstruction accuracy alone is insufficient for meaningful compression.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on token cramming (Kuratov et al., 2025), which demonstrated that a single learned embedding can reconstruct up to 1,568 tokens through autoregressive decoding with a frozen LLM. The key methodological departure is progressive cramming: rather than optimizing for a fixed token budget at a 99% accuracy threshold, the target prefix grows token-by-token until 100% reconstruction is no longer achievable, combined with a low-dimensional affine projection that stabilizes optimization. The paper also positions itself against the broader context-compression literature (AutoCompressors, ICAE, Activation Beacon), which targets semantic preservation for downstream tasks, and against prefix tuning as the general family of learned-embedding steering, using the latter as a control to isolate cramming-specific attention effects.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that perfect token reconstruction coexists with a consistent downstream capability drop (and near-total collapse under generative evaluation) even when the original prefix remains in context—challenges the implicit assumption in prior cramming work that high reconstruction accuracy reflects dense, transferable semantic encoding. Causal attention-knockout interventions localize the degradation to early-layer interactions, showing that the compression embedding steers the model through brittle attention capture rather than encoding information the model can flexibly reuse. The open problem of reconciling perfect reconstructability with semantic faithfulness, combined with the finding that compression capacity is jointly governed by the frozen reconstructor's depth and width, points toward architecture-aware compression design as a concrete future direction.
