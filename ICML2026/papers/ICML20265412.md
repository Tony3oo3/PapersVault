## Problem

Head-adaptive KV cache compression methods (e.g., Top-p nucleus sampling) dynamically fluctuate memory budgets per attention head, yielding superior accuracy over fixed Top-k eviction. However, this dynamic, context-dependent allocation is fundamentally incompatible with modern inference engines that rely on static tensor shapes (CUDA Graphs), uniform block contiguity (PagedAttention), and predictable memory layouts (continuous batching). The paper identifies and resolves this 'Static-Dynamic' mismatch, which prevents theoretical compression gains from translating into actual throughput improvements.

## Input / Output

The method consumes KV cache pairs, raw attention logits, and query vectors during the decoding phase, operating on a sliding window of the most recent tokens. It produces a three-tier compressed KV cache (Dense for recent tokens, Sparse for adaptively selected tokens, Condensed for archived/merged tokens) along with head-wise sparse selection masks packed in uint8 format. A key constraint is that all output indices must be regularized into fixed-shape, contiguous physical block layouts compatible with CUDA Graphs, meaning the system must rewrite or mask fragmented selections into a static buffer before each attention kernel launch.

## Method

HARD-KV introduces a Cascade Cache hierarchy that manages the token lifecycle across three tiers (Dense → Sparse → Condensed), with a Cautious Update strategy that treats the dense window as a voting period before compressing blocks. The Logits Calibration mechanism normalizes heterogeneous selection metrics (max-pooling in SNAPKV, pairwise similarity in R-KV, raw attention in H2O) into a unified probability space by solving for a per-head temperature T that preserves the Top-k selection order while matching the probability mass of raw attention, enabling consistent Top-p budgeting across all heads. At the system level, three index regularization operations—Rewrite Cache (copying selected blocks into contiguous ranges), Sparse Loading (applying hardware attention masks), and Flatten Index (unifying per-head indices to max or sum length)—are combined to produce CUDA Graphs-compatible layouts, with a dual-threshold trigger (L_lower for sparse loading, L_upper for full rewrite) balancing preparation cost against per-step decoding latency. The entire pipeline is implemented in a custom NANO-VLLM fork using FlashInfer's BSR-format kernels and composable Attention State algebra to compute exact full-sequence attention from the three physically separated tiers.

## Results

On math-reasoning benchmarks (AIME24, AIME25, U-MATH) with Qwen3-8B, HARD-KV achieves up to a 1.5× accuracy gain over static Top-k baselines at the 4096 budget (e.g., R-KV on AIME25: 40.0% → 56.7%; SnapKV on AIME25: 27.9% → 42.3%). In dynamic Top-p settings, HARD-KV raises sparsity utilization from ~30–47% (baselines) to ~80–98% while maintaining comparable accuracy, yielding up to 2× throughput improvement (e.g., 732 vs. 279 tokens/s for Vanilla on AIME24 at Top-p=0.90). Full Attention references are 79.9% (AIME24), 63.3% (AIME25), and 50.4% (U-MATH).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SnapKV, HARD-KV delivers substantial gains on AIME25 (42.3% vs. 27.9%, +14.4 pp) and U-MATH (49.4% vs. 46.7%, +2.7 pp) at the 4096 budget, but slightly trails on AIME24 (56.7% vs. 57.7%). Against R-KV, HARD-KV wins decisively on AIME25 (56.7% vs. 40.0%, +16.7 pp) and U-MATH (50.3% vs. 39.9%, +10.4 pp), and also on AIME24 (57.9% vs. 43.3%, +14.6 pp). Against the Vanilla (H2O + StreamLLM) baseline, HARD-KV improves on AIME24 (33.5% vs. 29.7%) and U-MATH (36.1% vs. 32.1%) but underperforms on AIME25 (20.7% vs. 24.8%), which the authors attribute to noise accumulation in raw attention logits at higher budgets. In dynamic Top-p throughput experiments, HARD-KV matches or exceeds baseline accuracy while achieving 2–3× higher sparsity utilization (e.g., 90.3% vs. 30.7% for SnapKV on AIME24), translating to ~2× throughput (632 vs. 301 tokens/s).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed but previously disconnected lines of work: head-adaptive KV cache compression (H2O, StreamLLM, SNAPKV, R-KV, AdaKV, HeadKV) and rigid inference-engine optimizations (PagedAttention, CUDA Graphs, continuous batching). It builds directly on NANO-VLLM as its engine substrate and on FlashInfer's composable Attention State algebra for the Cascade Attention kernel. The key departure is identifying and resolving the 'Static-Dynamic mismatch'—prior compression methods produce fragmented, variable-size indices that break CUDA Graphs and PagedAttention assumptions, while prior system work assumes uniform allocation. The Logits Calibration mechanism (temperature-based normalization of heterogeneous importance scores into a unified Top-p probability space) and the three-tier Cascade Cache hierarchy are novel contributions that unify previously incompatible algorithmic and system-level design choices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a concrete deployment gap: head-adaptive KV compression methods have demonstrated superior accuracy in research settings but have been effectively undeployable in production inference engines due to system incompatibilities. The demonstrated 2× throughput improvement while preserving accuracy on 10k+ token math-reasoning tasks makes head-adaptive compression practically viable for long-context serving. Limitations include evaluation restricted to math-reasoning benchmarks (AIME, U-Math) and the Qwen3 model family, leaving open whether the findings generalize to other task types (e.g., code generation, multi-turn dialogue) and architectures. The authors also flag future directions in streaming compression and sequence-level hybrids of constant-sized and linear-sized memory, and the LSE-preserving merging analysis in Appendix G, while theoretically interesting, remains preliminary without full integration into the main pipeline.
