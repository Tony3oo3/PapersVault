## Problem

Existing unlearning methods for diffusion models combine retention and forgetting objectives via ad hoc weighted sums, making the tradeoff sensitive to hyperparameters and poorly generalizable across scenarios. No principled framework exists that explicitly characterizes the optimal retention-unlearning tradeoff or provides theoretical guarantees for the resulting solutions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained diffusion model (score function), one or more unlearning distributions (either as associated diffusion models for reverse KL/likelihood formulations, or as raw samples for the forward KL formulation), and user-specified constraint thresholds (minimum KL divergence b_i or maximum likelihood ε_i). It produces an unlearned score function that generates images free of the target concepts while preserving the rest of the distribution. Experiments use Stable Diffusion v1.4 for text-to-image concept unlearning and an unconditional DDPM on CelebA-HQ for data unlearning of specific memorized images.

## Method

The paper formulates three constrained distribution optimization problems—reverse KL-constrained (RU), forward KL-constrained (FU), and likelihood-constrained (LU) unlearning—each minimizing deviation from the pretrained model subject to explicit separation constraints from unlearning distributions. Despite the nonconvexity of the KL constraints, the authors prove strong duality for all three problems by exploiting the convexity of the image of a non-atomic vector measure via Lyapunov's convexity theorem, yielding explicit optimal solutions: a ratio of distributions (RU), a difference of distributions (FU), and an exponential tilt (LU). Primal-dual algorithms alternate between gradient descent on the Lagrangian (score-matching or noise-prediction losses) and subgradient ascent on dual variables, with the dual variables dynamically assigning weights that are large for hard-to-unlearn targets and small for easy ones. The constrained thresholds provide direct semantic control over the degree of unlearning, unlike penalty weights in regularized formulations.

## Results

The paper presents results as retention-unlearning tradeoff curves rather than single-point numbers. In likelihood-constrained concept unlearning (Section 4.1), at the same unlearning-likelihood level, the proposed method achieves a smaller KL divergence to the retained modes compared to concept erasing (Gandikota et al., 2023). In forward KL data unlearning on CelebA-HQ (Section 4.2), at the same unlearning level measured by max SSCD, the constrained approach yields a lower KID (better retention) than the equal-weight baseline, achieved by dynamically assigning larger weights to harder-to-unlearn samples. In reverse KL multi-concept unlearning (Section 4.3), the constrained tradeoff curve lies strictly below the unconstrained baseline in both KL divergence to the pretrained model and max CLIP similarity to unlearning concepts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines. Against concept erasing (Gandikota et al., 2023) in Sections 4.1 and 4.3, the proposed constrained methods achieve the same degree of unlearning (same likelihood or KL separation from unlearned concepts) while deviating less from the pretrained model, as measured by lower KL divergence and lower KID to the retained distribution. The key advantage is that the reverse KL-constrained solution pushes retained modes away from the unlearned concept, whereas the likelihood-constrained solution does not, yielding a better retention-unlearning Pareto frontier. Against the data unlearning baseline of Alberti et al. (2025) in Section 4.2, which assigns equal weights to all samples to be removed, the proposed forward KL-constrained method achieves the same unlearning effectiveness (same max SSCD) but with significantly lower KID, because it learns optimal per-sample weights that allocate more capacity to harder-to-forget samples. In all three experimental settings, the constrained approach dominates the weighted-sum baselines on the retention-unlearning tradeoff curve.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the constrained diffusion framework of Khalafi et al. (2024) from convex expectation constraints to the nonconvex setting of KL-divergence and likelihood constraints for unlearning. It departs from the dominant weighted-sum regularization paradigm (Gandikota et al. 2023, Alberti et al. 2025, Wu & Harandi 2025) by recasting unlearning as a constrained distribution optimization problem, drawing on the constrained relative-entropy minimization framework of Koyejo & Ghosh (2013) but choosing unlearning-distribution likelihoods as moment functions. The key theoretical departure is proving strong duality for nonconvex KL-constrained problems via a novel application of Lyapunov's convexity theorem to a non-atomic vector measure, rather than relying on standard convex duality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified constrained framework provides interpretable, user-specified thresholds for unlearning (e.g., minimum KL separation or maximum likelihood) rather than opaque penalty weights, and the strong duality results yield explicit closed-form unlearning targets that guide practical algorithm design. The likelihood-constrained formulation is a genuinely new formulation that empirically achieves better retention-unlearning tradeoffs than both KL-based variants. However, the paper is limited to image generation experiments, lacks convergence and sample-complexity guarantees for the primal-dual algorithms, and the gap between the theoretical distribution-space targets and what a finite-capacity score function can approximate (the 'parametrization gap') remains unquantified.
