## Problem

Traditional Influence Maximization assumes social influence directly translates into participation, but spatial crowdsourcing tasks require offline physical execution, so a socially influential user may fail to mobilize followers for a task located far away. Moreover, finite task demand creates a non-submodular 'saturation trap' where marginal utility drops to zero once demand is met, invalidating greedy heuristics that rely on submodularity. The paper formulates Effective Task Satisfaction (ETS) Maximization to jointly address task heterogeneity and demand saturation in seed selection.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a directed social graph with per-edge influence probabilities, a set of spatial tasks each specified by location, quality demand, and reward, and worker trajectory histories from which quality potential (Euclidean proximity) and task affinity (interaction frequency × reward) are derived. The output is an ordered sequence of at most K seed worker-task pairs rather than a flat set of seed nodes, subject to a per-worker workload cap U_max. The ETS objective applies a per-task min(C/d, 1) truncation, rendering the global objective non-submodular and #P-hard to evaluate exactly, which is why Monte Carlo or learning-based approximation is required.

## Method

GKD-Recruiter constructs a two-view representation: an Influential GAT (IGAT) that models directional social influence by conditioning attention coefficients on base propagation probabilities and separately aggregating in- and out-neighborhoods, and a Relational GCN augmented with a Correlation Layer that encodes worker-task affinity on a pruned heterogeneous graph (each worker linked to its top-m tasks by quality potential). A Graph Knowledge Distillation module fuses these views through a gating-based teacher model that mutually supervises single-view student models via a joint BPR + KD loss, producing a unified embedding that retains cross-view consensus while suppressing view-specific noise. Seed selection is then cast as an MDP solved by Rainbow DQN, where the reward is the marginal ETS gain of adding a new worker-task pair, allowing the agent to learn long-term strategies that reserve versatile workers and diversify across unsaturated tasks. This design shifts the heavy Monte Carlo simulation burden to offline training, reducing online inference complexity from O(K·|V|·R) to O(K·(|V|+|E|)).

## Results

On the Gowalla dataset (|V|=3000 and |V|=5000), GKD-Recruiter achieves the highest Effective Task Satisfaction (ETS) across all seed budgets. At K=150, it reaches ETS of 0.413 (|V|=3000) and 0.405 (|V|=5000), surpassing the strongest baseline CELF by 24.4% and 38.1% respectively. Inference time scales near-linearly from 1.45s (K=25) to 6.20s (K=150), substantially faster than CELF (~38s) and TSIM (~37s) while delivering markedly higher solution quality than RL-based baselines MAIM and DQNSelector.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GKD-Recruiter outperforms all six baselines on ETS across all budgets. Against CELF (Leskovec et al., 2007), the gap widens dramatically with budget: at K=150 on |V|=5000, GKD-Recruiter achieves 0.4051 vs. CELF's 0.2933 (+38.1%), directly demonstrating the failure of greedy submodular heuristics under the non-submodular ETS objective. TSIM (Qiu et al., 2020) is also clearly outperformed in both ETS and inference speed (37s vs. 6.2s at K=150). Among RL-based methods, GKD-Recruiter beats MAIM (Liu et al., 2021) by 18.1% and 15.6% in average ETS on the two scales, and similarly surpasses DQNSelector (Gao et al., 2024), validating the benefit of fine-grained worker-task pair modeling over coarse dual-embedding schemes. The heuristic baselines NDD (Chen et al., 2009) and FastSelector (Wang et al., 2019) are far behind in ETS (e.g., GKD-Recruiter's margin over DegGreedy grows from 28.1% at K=25 to 66.6% at K=150), though they run in milliseconds versus GKD-Recruiter's seconds-scale inference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classic Influence Maximization (IM) lineage (Kempe et al., 2003) by introducing task-specific heterogeneity and a saturation constraint that breaks submodularity, moving beyond both traditional IM and Topic-Aware IM variants that assume interest implies participation. Methodologically, it departs from prior DRL-based recruitment work (e.g., DQNSelector) by operating at the fine-grained worker-task pair level rather than the coarse worker level, and replaces shallow dual-embedding fusion with a graph knowledge distillation framework that jointly supervises social-view (IGAT) and task-view (RGCN + correlation) representations. The directional attention mechanism in IGAT and the use of Rainbow DQN for non-submodular combinatorial search are specific architectural choices that distinguish it from standard GNN+DQN pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The formal proof that ETS-Maximization is non-submodular and that greedy strategies achieve only 50% of optimal utility under capacity constraints provides a clear theoretical justification for abandoning CELF-style heuristics in spatial crowdsourcing, a gap that has been informally noted but not rigorously established. The practical implication is significant for platforms like Meituan or Uber that must allocate limited recruitment budgets across heterogeneous spatial tasks in real time. However, the evaluation is limited to a single dataset (Gowalla) with a simplified task-affinity model, and the ETS metric's real-world validity (e.g., whether demand caps are truly hard constraints in practice) remains an open question. Scaling to millions of users and handling truly dynamic task arrivals would be important next steps.
