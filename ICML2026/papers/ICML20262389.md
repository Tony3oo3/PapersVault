## Problem

Existing diffusion models for image restoration rely on large corpora of clean images for supervised training, which is often unavailable in practice. Self-supervised alternatives like SURE can only learn denoisers at or above the measurement noise level, and methods for incomplete measurements require multiple complementary acquisition operators. This paper addresses the gap of learning diffusion models from noisy measurements of a single operator, specifically enabling denoiser learning below the measurement noise level without any clean data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262389/cropped_page5_idx4.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a dataset of noisy measurements {y_i = Ax_i + σ_n η_i} all acquired through the same forward operator A at the same noise level σ_n, with no access to clean images. For denoising, A is the identity; for inverse problems (inpainting, demosaicing, MRI), A is a rank-deficient operator. The output is either a one-step MMSE denoised image or a posterior sample generated by running a reverse SDE in measurement space (K=25 steps by default) followed by a final denoising step to produce an image-space sample. Experiments use 128×128 or 64×64 images with a 55M-parameter UNet.

## Method

The core insight is that MMSE denoisers exhibit a weak form of normalization equivariance: rescaling the signal amplitude by α induces a corresponding rescaling of the effective noise level. Theorem 3.1 formalizes this by showing that for priors whose magnitude and direction are approximately independent, the MMSE denoiser is approximately equivariant under scaling, with error bounded by the Fisher divergence to the nearest positively homogeneous prior. The NE-SURE loss (Eq. 8) enforces this property by randomly sampling scale α~U(0,1) and shift μ~U(0,1) during training, applying them to the noisy input with correspondingly scaled noise, and computing SURE at the scaled level—enabling unbiased denoiser learning below σ_n. For linear inverse problems, the loss is augmented with an equivariant imaging term that uses geometric transformations (translations, rotations) to create virtual operators from the single A, providing self-supervised guidance in the nullspace. Sampling proceeds via a reverse SDE in measurement space (Algorithm 1) from σ_n down to σ_min, followed by a final denoising step to obtain the image-space posterior sample.

## Results

On FFHQ (σn=0.075), the proposed self-supervised method achieves FID 22.57 and LPIPS 0.015, approaching the supervised EDM baseline (FID 19.91, LPIPS 0.012) while being trained exclusively on noisy measurements. On AFHQ at σn=0.05, the method reaches FID 5.08 and LPIPS 0.011, within ~1.5 FID points of supervised EDM (FID 3.62). For inpainting (σn=0.075), the method achieves FID 21.11 and LPIPS 0.031, outperforming both EI (FID 25.26) and Ambient Diffusion (FID 44.92) in perceptual quality. The method also demonstrates strong sample efficiency, with MSE scaling approximately as σt²/N^(1/3) even below the measurement noise level.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CD (Consistency Diffusion, Daras et al. 2024a), the proposed method wins decisively across all datasets and metrics: on FFHQ it achieves PSNR 32.76 vs. 28.92 and FID 22.57 vs. 59.37, and on AFHQ at σn=0.10 it reaches FID 8.88 vs. 20.65, indicating CD struggles severely below the measurement noise level. Compared to supervised EDM (Karras et al. 2022), the self-supervised method is close in perceptual quality (e.g., FID 22.57 vs. 19.91 on FFHQ; FID 5.08 vs. 3.62 on AFHQ σn=0.05) but slightly trails in LPIPS, confirming the normalization-equivariance assumption enables near-supervised generalization without any clean data. Against EI (Chen et al. 2022) on inpainting and demosaicing, the proposed method trades a small PSNR loss (27.74 vs. 27.92; 26.05 vs. 26.48) for substantially better perceptual quality (LPIPS 0.031 vs. 0.043; FID 21.11 vs. 25.26 on inpainting). Against Ambient Diffusion (Daras et al. 2023), the advantage is overwhelming: on demosaicing the proposed method achieves PSNR 26.05 vs. 7.77 and FID 42.29 vs. 232.95, as Ambient Diffusion lacks multi-operator coverage needed to constrain the nullspace. Against SURE alone (Metzler et al. 2020), the normalization-equivariant extension (NE-SURE) provides significantly better denoising at noise levels below σn, where standard SURE is theoretically invalid.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-supervised denoising literature (SURE, Noise2Noise) by identifying a weak scale-equivariance property of MMSE denoisers that enables learning below the measurement noise level—a gap that prior SURE-based methods cannot fill. It integrates this insight with equivariant imaging (Chen et al., 2022) to handle rank-deficient single-operator measurements, and departs from prior normalization-equivariant architectures (Herbreteau et al., 2024) by enforcing equivariance through a modified loss (NE-SURE) rather than architectural constraints. The diffusion sampling framework follows EDM (Karras et al., 2022), adapted to operate in measurement space via a custom reverse SDE solver (Algorithm 1).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to learn a full diffusion posterior sampler from noisy, incomplete measurements of a single operator without any clean data or multiple acquisition processes, addressing a practical bottleneck in medical imaging, remote sensing, and other domains where clean references are unavailable. The theoretical guarantee (Theorem 3.1) that approximate scale equivariance holds for priors with a radial/angular factorization provides a principled justification, while the sample-complexity finding (MSE ∝ σ_t²/N^{1/3} below the measurement noise) contradicts prior hypotheses that learning below the noise floor requires prohibitive data. Open questions include extension to few-step/distilled samplers, non-Gaussian noise, and whether the weak equivariance assumption holds for higher-dimensional or 3D imaging tasks.
