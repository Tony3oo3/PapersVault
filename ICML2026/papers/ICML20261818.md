## Problem

Active regression for single-index models with an unknown 1-Lipschitz link function had only been solved for the special case p=2 with a constant-factor approximation (Gajjar et al., 2024). This paper closes the gap by providing a (1+ε)-approximation for all p≥1 and establishing a nearly tight non-adaptive lower bound for p>2, showing the query complexity is only log n factors larger than the known-link-function case.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives the full data matrix A ∈ R^{n×d} but only coordinate-query access to the label vector b ∈ R^n, meaning it must decide which entries of b to read before seeing their values (non-adaptive). It outputs a pair (f̂, x̂) where f̂ is a 1-Lipschitz function satisfying f̂(0)=0 and x̂ ∈ R^d, jointly minimizing ‖f̂(Ax̂)−b‖_p^p. The number of coordinate queries is O(d^{max(p/2,1)}/ε^{max(p,2)} · polylog(n/ε)), and the link function may be restricted to any subset of Lip₁.

## Method

The algorithm is a non-adaptive Lewis-weight sampling scheme: it computes Lewis weights of A, splits each row into k_i = ⌈nw_i/d⌉ copies, samples each copy independently with probability α, rescales, and solves a regularized single-index regression min_{f,x} ‖Sf(Ax)−Sb‖_p^p + ε‖Ax‖_p^p on the sketched instance. The main technical contribution is the analysis, which bounds a uniform sampling error via Dudley's integral over a Rademacher process indexed by Lip₁ × R^d. The key new ingredient is a covering-number bound for Lip₁ under a norm defined as the maximum of weighted L^∞-norms over possibly different intervals (Theorem D.1), which avoids the explicit discretization used in prior p=2 work and generalizes cleanly to all p. Because the index set T is not rectangular in the (1+ε)-approximation regime, the decoupling of f and x is introduced mid-chain rather than at the index-set level. A bootstrapping argument then converts the uniform error bound into the final (1+ε)-approximation guarantee.

## Results

On the Communities and Crime dataset (1994×127), Lewis weight sampling achieves up to 126.3× speedup over the full-dataset Isotron solver at 10% sampling fraction for p=1 (median 5.224s vs. 659.961s) and 126.0× for p=3 (median 5.177s vs. 652.508s). Lewis weight sampling consistently yields smaller approximation parameter ε than uniform sampling across all tested sampling fractions (10%–30%) for both p=1 and p=3, with noticeably tighter interquartile ranges indicating more stable approximation quality. The advantage of Lewis weight sampling over uniform sampling is particularly pronounced for p=3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares Lewis weight sampling against uniform sampling as the baseline row-sampling strategy for constructing the sketched single-index regression problem. Lewis weight sampling consistently outperforms uniform sampling in terms of the achieved approximation parameter ε (smaller is better) across most sampling fractions for both p=1 and p=3, on both the synthetic ReLU dataset and the Communities and Crime dataset. The improvement is accompanied by tighter interquartile ranges, indicating more stable approximation quality at comparable sketch sizes. The advantage is most pronounced for p=3, where the ℓ_p-norm geometry makes importance sampling more critical. In terms of runtime, both sampling strategies yield comparable solving times for the sketched problem (the paper omits uniform-sampling runtimes as they are similar), so the primary benefit of Lewis weight sampling is purely in approximation quality rather than speed.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two prior lines of work: Li & Tai (2025) established near-optimal active regression for single-index models with a *known* link function and general p≥1, while Gajjar et al. (2024) handled *unknown* link functions but only for p=2 with a constant-factor approximation. The present work unifies these by achieving a (1+ε)-approximation for unknown 1-Lipschitz link functions at general p≥1, with query complexity only polylog n factors above the known-f case. The key technical departure is a new covering-number bound for Lip₁ under a family of weighted sup-norms (Theorem D.1), which replaces the explicit discretization arguments of Gajjar et al. (2024) and enables the Dudley's integral to be controlled for all p.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This closes a significant gap in the active ℓ_p-regression literature: before this work, the unknown-link-function setting was completely open for p≠2, and even for p=2 only a constant-factor guarantee was available. The non-adaptive lower bound for p>2 (Theorem 4.3) is important because it certifies that the standard Lewis-weight row-sampling paradigm cannot be substantially improved in that regime, guiding future work toward adaptive or fundamentally different sampling strategies. Open questions include whether the polylog n factors in the upper bound can be eliminated (the authors note that the intrinsic log n from Lipschitz covering numbers resists the net-argument trick used in the known-f case) and whether the lower bound extends to adaptive queries. The covering lemma (Theorem D.1) may also find independent use in other problems involving metric entropy of function classes under nonstandard norms.
