## Problem

Existing LLM-based communicative embodied agents lack effective mechanisms to align their behavior with both cooperation partners and task specifications, and they cannot self-evolve through interaction without supervision from stronger models or ground-truth annotations. This leads to misaligned coordination, redundant actions, and degraded task success in partially observable multi-agent settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266165/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each replanning step, the agent receives a structured prompt containing the task description, parsed environment observations, accumulated action history, partner messages from an event buffer, and the full set of K learned laws (each a name–description pair). The model autoregressively generates a single law-referencing thought paragraph followed by one action token (e.g., Navigate, Pick, Place, Talk[message], Done), where the thought must explicitly cite the most relevant law by its exact name. Communication is implemented as a Talk primitive that encodes the speaker ID and message content into the environment's event buffer for broadcast to the partner in subsequent steps.

## Method

LLawCo is a three-stage law-guided training pipeline. First, failure-driven law extraction analyzes the chain-of-thought traces of failed episodes with an LLM to identify specific failure reasons, then aggregates these reasons to induce at most K high-level behavioral laws (e.g., 'Plan Before Acting', 'Avoid Infinite Loops'). Second, law-aligned trace selection filters successful episodes by prompting the LLM to judge whether each episode's action histories are consistent with the induced laws, retaining only episodes that are both successful and law-aligned. Third, law-guided supervised fine-tuning generates, for each retained episode, a law-grounded reasoning trace that justifies each action by referencing the single most relevant law, and fine-tunes the backbone LLM on these (observation, thought, action) triples with standard next-token prediction. At inference, the fine-tuned model receives the law set in the prompt and produces a law-referencing thought before each action, making the laws an interpretable and human-editable control interface that can be modified at inference time to redirect behavior without retraining.

## Results

On PARTNR-Dialog, LLawCo achieves an average success rate improvement of 4.5% over the strongest communicative baseline across four LLM backbones (LLaMA-3.1-8B, LLaMA-3.1-70B, Gemma-3-12B, Qwen-3-14B). On TDW-MAT, the average improvement is 6.8% over the strongest baseline. Notably, Qwen-3-14B with LLawCo reaches 0.74 success rate on PARTNR-Dialog, surpassing all baselines built on the substantially larger LLaMA-3.1-70B (best baseline: 0.70 CommPARTNR), and achieves 0.80 overall success on TDW-MAT versus 0.73 for CoELA with the same backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LLawCo consistently outperforms all three communicative baselines (RoCo, CoELA, CommPARTNR) across every LLM backbone on both PARTNR-Dialog and TDW-MAT. On PARTNR-Dialog with Qwen-3-14B, LLawCo achieves 0.74 success rate versus 0.70 (CommPARTNR), 0.69 (RoCo), and 0.62 (CoELA); on TDW-MAT with the same backbone, it reaches 0.80 versus 0.73 (CoELA) and 0.65 (RoCo). Against the non-communicative ReAct baseline, LLawCo wins on three of four backbones (e.g., 0.75 vs. 0.73 on LLaMA-70B, 0.74 vs. 0.64 on Qwen-14B) but slightly underperforms on LLaMA-3.1-8B (0.57 vs. 0.62), a gap the authors attribute to the small model's limited ability to use the Talk action—a limitation shared by all communicative baselines on that backbone. The largest absolute gains appear on TDW-MAT with LLaMA-3.1-70B (0.82 vs. 0.76 RoCo, +6 pts) and with Qwen-3-14B (0.80 vs. 0.73 CoELA, +7 pts), demonstrating that the law-guided SFT provides substantial self-improvement even on already-strong models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ReAct planning framework and the PARTNR embodied multi-agent benchmark by adding a communication layer (Talk action, event buffers) to create PARTNR-Dialog, and by introducing a law-guided self-alignment mechanism inspired by Constitutional AI and Self-Align. Unlike prior communicative agent frameworks (CoELA, RoCo, CommPARTNR) that rely on carefully designed non-learning pipelines or distillation from stronger models, LLawCo autonomously extracts high-level behavioral laws from failure traces and embeds them into the LLM via supervised fine-tuning, enabling self-improvement without external supervision. The conceptual lineage traces from principle-driven LLM alignment (Constitutional AI, Self-Align) to embodied multi-agent cooperation, grounding abstract laws in concrete interaction traces rather than purely textual preferences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The PARTNR-Dialog benchmark (2,000 training episodes) addresses a critical data scarcity bottleneck that has limited learning-based research in communicative embodied agents, where prior benchmarks like TDW-MAT offer only 24 training episodes and C-WAH provides no training split at all. The law-based mechanism offers a practical controllability knob—humans can edit or replace laws at inference time to enforce domain-specific constraints—while the self-improvement loop removes the dependency on access to stronger teacher models. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond two-agent settings (longer dialog histories, complex credit assignment), whether induced laws can encode unsafe behaviors in deployment, and whether the moderate gains (4.5–6.8%) compound over repeated self-improvement cycles in long-horizon settings.
