## Problem

Hyperbolic metric learning has been almost entirely dominated by the conformal Poincaré model, whose curved geodesics create a fundamental geometric misalignment with the linear projection operations that dominate modern neural network backbones like Vision Transformers. The Klein model, a projective model of hyperbolic geometry whose geodesics are Euclidean straight lines, offers a structurally aligned alternative but has been largely under-explored in the metric learning literature.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images as input, processed through a pre-trained ViT-based backbone (ViT-S, DeiT-S, or DINO) to produce 384-dimensional Euclidean feature vectors. These are projected into a 128-dimensional Klein model via an exponential map, with a feature clipping radius (r ≈ 4.8–4.9) applied before projection to prevent boundary instability. The final output is a hyperbolic embedding used for fine-grained classification and retrieval, evaluated via Recall@K on four benchmarks (CUB, Cars, SOP, In-Shop).

## Method

The paper formalizes a Klein-model-based metric learning framework built on Einstein gyrovector space, where the key insight is that straight-line geodesics in the Klein model are naturally congruent with the linear output spaces of Transformer backbones, eliminating the geometric distortion inherent in conformal models. For distance computation, the authors derive a numerically stable gyrovector distance (Eq. 4) that avoids the vanishing-gradient issues of the standard arcosh-based Klein distance (Eq. 3), verified both analytically and empirically. Two loss functions are proposed: a Pairwise Cross-Entropy (PCE) loss operating on N-way m-shot batches with temperature-scaled gyrovector distances, and a Hierarchical Regularization (HIER) loss that combines a hyperspherical proxy-anchor term with Klein-model hierarchical anchoring via learnable LCA proxies. A feature clipping mechanism adapted from the Poincaré setting mitigates vanishing gradients at the manifold boundary, with the optimal clipping radius naturally shifting to a larger value due to the Klein model's more moderate metric expansion.

## Results

On the 128-dimensional hyperbolic embedding (evaluated with intrinsic Klein distance), Klein-DINO achieves 86.9% Recall@1 on Stanford Cars, a 3.4-point absolute improvement over Poincaré-DINO (83.5%), and Klein-ViT-S reaches 93.5% Recall@1 on In-Shop, surpassing both Poincaré (92.5%) and Spherical (89.6%) baselines. On CUB-200-2011, Klein-ViT-S attains 83.4% Recall@1, matching Poincaré-ViT-S (83.0%) and exceeding Spherical-ViT-S (83.3%). In the 384-dimensional encoder-feature evaluation (cosine similarity), Klein-DINO leads on Cars with 89.2% Recall@1 vs. 87.5% for Poincaré-DINO, confirming that the Klein geometry also improves upstream encoder representations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Poincaré and Spherical baselines from Ermolov et al. (CVPR 2022), the Klein model wins decisively on Stanford Cars (e.g., Klein-DINO 86.9% vs. Poincaré-DINO 83.5% Recall@1 at 128D) and In-Shop (Klein-ViT-S 93.5% vs. Poincaré-ViT-S 92.5%), while remaining competitive on CUB and SOP where Poincaré holds a marginal 0.4–0.8-point edge at Recall@1. Against the HIER-Poincaré framework from Kim et al. (CVPR 2023), HIER-Klein achieves higher Recall@1 on Cars (88.7% vs. 88.4% for DINO-128D; 91.5% vs. 91.3% for DINO-384D) and In-Shop (92.9% vs. 92.8% for ViT-S-384D), while Poincaré retains a small advantage on CUB (84.2% vs. 83.8% for ViT-S-128D) and SOP. The paper also demonstrates that the Klein model's 384-dim encoder features outperform Poincaré-trained features (e.g., 89.2% vs. 87.5% on Cars-DINO), indicating that the straight-line geodesic geometry produces more stable backpropagated gradients that benefit the upstream ViT backbone. Overall, the Klein model is architecture-agnostic, consistently matching or exceeding Poincaré across DeiT-S, DINO, and ViT-S backbones without adding parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the hyperbolic metric learning line initiated by Ermolov et al. (2022) and Kim et al. (2023), which have almost exclusively relied on the conformal Poincaré model. The key departure is the introduction of the projective Klein model, formalized through Einstein gyrovector space operations (Ungar, 2008; Ganea et al., 2018), as a geometrically aligned alternative whose straight-line geodesics match the linear projection operations of modern ViT backbones. The paper also adapts the feature clipping stabilization technique from Guo et al. (2022) to the Klein setting and re-derives the Riemannian metric inverse via the Sherman-Morrison formula to address vanishing gradients.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled geometric argument for why the choice of hyperbolic model should be matched to the encoder's inductive bias, rather than treating all negative-curvature spaces as interchangeable. Practically, it offers a drop-in alternative to the Poincaré model that achieves competitive (and in some cases superior) performance without additional parameters, and the GDE analysis quantitatively validates the alignment claim. Limitations include a fixed curvature parameter, evaluation restricted to fine-grained classification, and the observation that gains are dataset-dependent (Klein underperforms Poincaré on CUB in some settings). Open questions include learnable curvature, adapting standard Euclidean loss functions to the Klein model, and whether the alignment advantage persists in non-ViT architectures.
