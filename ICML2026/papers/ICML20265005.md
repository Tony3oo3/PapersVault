## Problem

In weak-to-strong generalization (W2SG), a strong student is trained on imperfect labels from a weak teacher, yet can outperform the teacher. The central open question is whether this improvement can occur at the level of the scaling law exponent, not just in absolute error. The only prior theoretical result relating W2SG to scaling laws (Ildiz et al., 2025) is negative for ridgeless linear regression, showing no exponent improvement is possible. This paper asks whether regularization and over-parameterization in a non-linear model can change this picture.

## Input / Output

The two-stage pipeline takes n_t ground-truth labeled samples to train a teacher RFRR model (with p_t random features and ridge parameter λ_t), then draws n_s fresh unlabeled inputs that are labeled by the teacher to train a student RFRR model (with p_s features and ridge parameter λ_s). The output is a dimension-free deterministic equivalent R_s for the student's excess test error, expressed as a function of the feature map eigenvalue spectrum Σ, target coefficients β_*, and fixed points (μ_{t,1}, μ_{t,2}) and (μ_{s,1}, μ_{s,2}) solving self-consistency equations. Under power-law source-capacity conditions, this yields explicit scaling law exponents γ_{s,bias} and γ_{s,var} as functions of the hyperparameter scaling exponents (γ_{p_t}, γ_{λ_t}, γ_{p_s}, γ_{n_s}, γ_{λ_s}).

## Method

The core technical contribution is a dimension-free deterministic equivalent for the student test error in a two-stage RFRR pipeline, proved via a two-step argument: first, a deterministic equivalent conditional on the teacher's coefficient vector β_t (using asymmetric functional bounds for random features), and second, a deterministic equivalent for β_t itself (reusing the teacher's deterministic equivalent machinery). The resulting expression involves coupled operators Λ and Λ_0 that depend on both teacher and student fixed points, capturing cross-terms absent in single-stage analysis. Under source-capacity conditions (power-law spectrum Σ_{k,k}=k^{-α} and target coefficients β_{*,k}=k^{-(1+2αr)/2}), the paper derives closed-form scaling exponents and identifies W2SG regimes: when z_t > z_s (the teacher is 'richer' than the student), the student can improve the scaling law via variance reduction (always possible if the teacher is variance-dominated and sub-optimal) or bias reduction (possible in specific parameter regions when the teacher is bias-dominated). Strikingly, the student can attain the minimax-optimal rate n_t^{-2α(r∧1)/(1+2α(r∧1))} even when the teacher's error does not decay with sample size.

## Results

The paper establishes that the student's optimal scaling exponent equals the teacher's: γ* = 2α(r∧1)/(1+2α(r∧1)), matching the minimax rate n_t^{−2αr/(1+2αr)} when r ≤ 1. Crucially, the student can achieve this minimax rate even when the teacher is suboptimal: in the variance-dominated regime (Figure 1a), the teacher's test error has zero decay rate (γ_{t,V} = 0) while a properly tuned student attains the full minimax exponent. In the bias-dominated regime with τ_t² = 0 (Figure 1b), two of three student configurations reduce the bias and improve the scaling law. The deterministic equivalent predictions (Theorems 1–2) match empirical test errors across all three scenarios in Figure 1, validated on a Gaussian linear model with d = 20,000 features over 25 independent runs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the teacher scaling law of Defilippis et al. (2024), the student strictly improves the exponent whenever the teacher is suboptimal: in the variance-dominated regime (Corollary 2), the student always improves by reducing variance, and in the bias-dominated regime (Corollary 3), improvement is possible when the student width exceeds the teacher width (γ_{p_s} > γ_{p_t}) and r > 1/2. When the teacher is optimally regularized (z_t = 1/(1+2α(r∧1))), no student can improve the scaling law (Figure 1c), confirming that the teacher's optimal rate is a hard ceiling. Against the Caponnetto & De Vito (2007) minimax rate n_t^{−2αr/(1+2αr)}, the student matches it in both the variance-dominated and bias-dominated W2SG regimes, even in the extreme case where the teacher's error does not decay with n_t at all. The key distinction from prior work (Ildiz et al., 2025) is that ridge regularization and over-parameterization in RFRR enable exponent-level improvements, whereas ridgeless linear regression cannot improve the scaling law exponent.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dimension-free deterministic equivalent framework from Defilippis et al. (2024) and Misiakiewicz & Saeed (2024) from single-stage to two-stage (teacher-student) learning, introducing new asymmetric cross-terms absent in the one-stage setting. It directly departs from the negative result of Ildiz et al. (2025), who showed that ridgeless linear regression on teacher labels cannot improve the scaling law exponent, by demonstrating that ridge regularization combined with over-parameterization in RFRR enables such improvement. The paper also generalizes prior W2SG analyses (Medvedev et al. 2025, Dong et al. 2025, Moniri & Hassani 2025) that identified specific mechanisms (early stopping, intrinsic dimension, under-regularization) by showing W2SG at the scaling-law level is possible in both bias-dominated and variance-dominated regimes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a student can attain the minimax-optimal decay rate even when the teacher's error does not decay with sample size fundamentally reframes the potential of weak-to-strong generalization: the student is not bounded by the teacher's asymptotic quality, only by its own regularization and capacity choices. The deterministic equivalent for two-stage learning is a reusable technical tool applicable to other multi-source problems (distribution shift, transfer learning). Key open questions include whether the additive approximation bound can be sharpened to multiplicative, whether the framework captures early stopping (a known W2SG mechanism), and whether the results extend beyond RFRR to deeper architectures or non-ridge regularizers.
