## Problem

De novo structural elucidation from tandem mass spectra remains limited by poor scalability at both training and inference time: autoregressive decoders lack mechanisms for automatic correction of erroneous substructures, while graph diffusion decoders are too slow for practical iterative refinement. No existing method combines large-scale pretraining on unlabeled structures with an efficient inference-time correction loop that leverages forward spectral simulation to identify and fix spectrum-inconsistent fragments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260310/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an MS/MS spectrum (converted to a 4096-bit radius-2 Morgan fingerprint via the MIST encoder) and a chemical formula (typically from high-resolution MS1) as conditioning signals, and produces a molecular structure as a BPE-tokenized SAFE sequence (max length 256) that is deterministically converted to SMILES. A lightweight NGBoost model predicts the target SAFE sequence length from the formula, and generated candidates are filtered by exact formula match before ranking by Tanimoto similarity to the predicted fingerprint.

## Method

FRIGID-base is a 12-layer masked diffusion language model (MDLM) that generates SAFE sequences non-autoregressively, conditioned on the chemical formula and MIST fingerprint injected via cross-attention in every Transformer block; it is pretrained self-supervised on up to 1 billion unlabeled SAFE structures for a single epoch. For inference-time scaling, FRIGID introduces a cycle-consistent refinement loop: ICEBERG simulates a spectrum for each generated candidate, simulated peaks are matched to observed peaks within 20 ppm, and unmatched (hallucinated) peaks yield negative atom-wise consistency scores that are aggregated to token-level masking probabilities. Tokens with non-positive scores are selectively re-masked and re-denoised by the same MDLM, targeting only spectrum-inconsistent substructures rather than applying uniform random masking. This sequence-based design achieves 20× faster inference than graph diffusion baselines (6.58 s vs. 131.2 s per spectrum), making multi-round iterative refinement computationally practical and yielding log-linear accuracy gains with increasing inference compute.

## Results

On NPLIB1, FRIGID achieves 25.03% Top-1 exact-match accuracy and 0.58 Top-1 Tanimoto similarity, tripling the Top-1 accuracy of the best prior method (DiffMS at 8.34%). On the more challenging MassSpecGym benchmark, FRIGID is the first method to surpass 18% Top-1 accuracy (18.29%), with a 70% relative improvement over the next-best method (MIST+MolForge at 10.73%). Even without inference-time scaling, FRIGID-base alone reaches 19.80% Top-1 on NPLIB1 and 16.09% on MassSpecGym, already outperforming all autoregressive and graph-diffusion baselines. FRIGID also achieves the best Top-10 accuracy on both benchmarks (33.37% on NPLIB1, 22.00% on MassSpecGym) and the lowest MCES graph edit distance on NPLIB1 (7.10).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FRIGID decisively outperforms all eight baselines on Top-1 exact-match accuracy across both benchmarks: on NPLIB1 it triples the best prior result (25.03% vs. DiffMS's 8.34%) and on MassSpecGym it nearly doubles the next-best (18.29% vs. MIST+MolForge's 10.73%). Among autoregressive baselines, MS-BART (7.45% NPLIB1, 1.07% MassSpecGym) and MIST+MSNovelist (5.40%, 0.00%) are the strongest but remain far behind FRIGID-base alone. Among graph-diffusion baselines, DiffMS (8.34% NPLIB1, 2.30% MassSpecGym) and MADGEN (2.10%, 1.31%) are outperformed by a wide margin on accuracy, though DiffMS and FOAM retain a slight edge on the MCES graph-edit-distance metric on MassSpecGym (12.21 for FOAM vs. 13.49 for FRIGID). The paper also demonstrates that applying ICEBERG-guided refinement to DiffMS improves it from 8.34% to 13.33% Top-1 on NPLIB1, confirming the refinement strategy is model-agnostic, but at 1423 s/sample versus FRIGID's 86 s/sample. In terms of inference speed, FRIGID is 20× faster than DiffMS (6.58 s vs. 131.2 s per spectrum) and 3.6× faster than MADGEN, making iterative refinement practical at scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

FRIGID builds on the masked diffusion language model (MDLM) framework of Sahoo et al. (2024) and the SAFE-sequence generation paradigm of GenMol (Lee et al., 2025), repurposing them for the MS/MS structural elucidation setting. It adopts the now-standard two-stage spectra-to-fingerprint-to-structure pipeline (MIST encoder + decoder) but replaces the autoregressive SMILES decoders (MSNovelist, MS-BART, MolForge) and slow graph diffusion decoders (DiffMS, MADGEN) with a sequence-based DLM over BPE-tokenized SAFE strings. The key departure from prior work is the inference-time scaling mechanism: rather than simply increasing denoising steps or sampling more candidates, FRIGID uses the forward fragmentation model ICEBERG to identify spectrum-inconsistent subgraphs and perform targeted remasking, a capability absent from all prior de novo generators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 20× inference speedup over graph diffusion (6.6 s vs. 131 s per spectrum) is what makes iterative refinement computationally practical, and the demonstrated log-linear scaling of Top-1 accuracy with inference compute opens a clear path for continued improvement without retraining. The finding that the ICEBERG-guided refinement is model-agnostic (it also improves DiffMS) but only becomes practical with a fast sequence-based decoder highlights an important architectural trade-off for the field. Limitations include dependence on a known chemical formula (performance drops ~13% on MassSpecGym with MIST-CF formula hypotheses) and the still-modest absolute accuracy on the harder MassSpecGym benchmark (~18% Top-1). Open questions include whether combining test-time tuning (Mismetti et al., 2026) with inference-time scaling yields further gains, and whether the log-linear scaling trend persists at much larger compute budgets.
