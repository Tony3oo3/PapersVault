## Problem

State-of-the-art MLLMs exhibit 'geometric blindness' in medical imaging: they generate semantically fluent reports that contradict the actual spatial, topological, and pixel-level structure of scans. This stems from training paradigms (SFT, standard RL) that optimize for linguistic plausibility without explicit mechanisms to penalize geometric violations, leaving models unable to ground outputs in objective visual constraints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262797/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes unlabeled medical images (CT, MRI, X-ray) and algorithmically transforms them into three geometric proxy tasks without any expert annotation. For Task A, the model receives a global image plus three resized local crops and must output scale-level labels and normalized bounding boxes; for Task B, a 2×2 shuffled grid and must output the original index sequence; for Task C, a 4×4 grid with one swapped patch and must output the anomaly grid index. Downstream, the aligned model produces standard medical VQA responses and free-text radiology reports.

## Method

Med-Scout is a geometry-aware RL post-training framework built on GRPO that cures geometric blindness by deriving verifiable supervision signals from unlabeled images through three automatically generated proxy tasks: Hierarchical Scale Localization (multi-scale crop-to-global coordinate mapping), Topological Jigsaw Reconstruction (2×2 grid permutation recovery), and Anomaly Consistency Detection (cut-paste anomaly localization on a 4×4 grid with modality-specific hard-negative mining). The key technical innovation is a Dense Geometric Reward (DGR) mechanism that replaces sparse binary feedback with continuous, task-specific metrics—IoU for bounding boxes, Euclidean distance decay for anomaly localization, and element-wise alignment for topological sequences—providing granular gradient signals that guide progressive geometric refinement. Training uses 97,200 automatically generated samples over 7,200 GRPO steps with a KL coefficient of 0.04, and the framework is applied as a post-training stage to both general-purpose (Qwen3-VL) and medical-specialist (Lingshu, HuatuoGPT) backbones, yielding over 40% improvement on the newly introduced Med-Scout-Bench and consistent gains on radiological VQA and report generation benchmarks.

## Results

On Med-Scout-Bench, Med-Scout improves Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct from 39.7% to 83.6% average DGR score (+43.9%) and Lingshu-7B from 31.9% to 71.9% (+40.0%), with open-source models surpassing proprietary GPT-5 (63.6%) and Gemini-3-Flash (66.1%). On radiological VQA, Qwen3-VL-4B gains +4.2% on Rad-VQA and +3.0% on VQA-RAD, while Lingshu-7B+Med-Scout (71.0%) surpasses Gemini-3-Flash (70.2%) on VQA-RAD. On report generation, Lingshu-7B+Med-Scout achieves a SOTA CIDEr of 105.2 on MIMIC-CXR, dramatically outperforming GPT-5 (89.4) and Gemini-3-Flash (90.0). Generalization to broader medical VQA (PMC-VQA, OmniMedVQA, MedXpertQA) shows consistent 0.4–3.3% gains across all backbones.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Med-Scout consistently outperforms all open-source baselines across every benchmark. On Med-Scout-Bench, the aligned Qwen3-VL-4B (84.4%) and Qwen3-VL-8B (83.6%) surpass proprietary GPT-5 (63.6%) and Gemini-3-Flash (66.1%) by over 17 points, demonstrating that targeted geometric RL post-training unlocks capabilities absent even in the strongest commercial models. On VQA-RAD, Lingshu-7B+Med-Scout (71.0%) edges out Gemini-3-Flash (70.2%), and on MIMIC-CXR report generation, Lingshu-7B+Med-Scout achieves CIDEr 105.2 versus 89.4 (GPT-5) and 90.0 (Gemini-3-Flash). Against general-purpose MLLMs (InternVL3-8B, Qwen2.5-VL series), Med-Scout-aligned models lead on all radiological VQA benchmarks by 2–4%. Compared to existing proxy-task methods (Jigsaw-R1, ViCrit), Med-Scout with sparse rewards still achieves +2.5% on Rad-VQA and +1.8% on generalization benchmarks, confirming that medical-specific geometric tasks are essential rather than generic spatial awareness. Medical specialists (HuatuoGPT, Lingshu) show smaller absolute gains than general models but still improve, with Lingshu-7B breaking through its prior SOTA ceiling on multiple benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the proxy-task RL paradigm established by Jigsaw-R1 and ViCrit (which target general-domain visual grounding) to the medical imaging domain, where geometric constraints carry direct clinical meaning. It builds on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) as the optimization backbone but departs from prior proxy-task work in two key ways: (1) the three proxy tasks (hierarchical scale localization, topological jigsaw, anomaly consistency detection) are specifically designed around clinical reading patterns rather than generic spatial puzzles, and (2) the Dense Geometric Reward mechanism replaces the sparse binary feedback used in prior methods with continuous, distance-based signals. The paper also positions itself against the broader medical MLLM literature (Lingshu, HuatuoGPT, MedGemma, LLaVA-Med), arguing that both general-purpose and domain-adapted models share a common 'geometric blindness' that neither SFT nor standard RL addresses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of geometric blindness as a distinct, measurable failure mode in medical MLLMs provides the field with a concrete diagnostic axis that prior evaluation suites (VQA-RAD, SLAKE, etc.) do not capture. The finding that SFT achieves comparable internal benchmark scores but fails to generalize externally—while RL does generalize—offers a practical guideline for post-training strategy in safety-critical domains. Open questions include whether the 2D geometric constraints captured here transfer to 3D volumetric reasoning (CT/MRI stacks), whether the proxy tasks cover all clinically relevant geometric relationships (e.g., depth ordering, occlusion), and whether the modest 2–4% external benchmark gains scale with larger models or longer training. The energy-landscape analysis also opens a path toward using energy-based diagnostics as a routine hallucination-detection tool in clinical deployment.
