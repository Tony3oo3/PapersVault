## Problem

In standard causal attention, each token's query, key, and value are static projections of its own representation and can only encode preceding context. This causal mask prevents earlier tokens from incorporating information that appears later in the sequence, limiting the model's ability to capture global dependencies, resolve structural ambiguities (e.g., garden-path sentences), and handle tasks where the relevant focus appears at the end of the input. The paper asks whether keys can be dynamically updated as context unfolds to incorporate later information while strictly preserving the autoregressive property.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text token sequence of length L and produces next-token predictions autoregressively, identical in interface to standard causal attention. The key distinction is internal: at each generation step t+1, the keys of all preceding tokens s ≤ t are updated to additionally encode information from tokens s+1 through t, so the attention scores reflect a richer, context-aware representation. At inference, the model maintains a UQ-KV cache (lookahead keys U^t, lookahead queries Q_t^U, causal keys K_t^C, and causal values V_t^C) rather than the standard KV cache, with O(td) memory per step during decoding.

## Method

CASTLE partitions each head's keys into two equal groups: causal keys (static projections of x_s, identical to standard attention) and lookahead keys (dynamically updated representations that incorporate information from subsequent tokens via a sigmoid-weighted attention over tokens s+1 to t). The final attention weights combine causal-key scores and SiLU-gated lookahead-key scores through a single softmax, where the SiLU nonlinearity is critical for training stability. A central theoretical contribution is a parallel training formulation (Theorem 1.1) that expresses the entire sequence of outputs as a single masked attention computation with an auxiliary low-rank term S^U, avoiding the O(L³d) cost of materializing lookahead keys at every position and enabling FlashAttention-style O(L²d) training. A variant, CASTLE-SWL, restricts lookahead keys to a sliding window of size W (128–512 tokens), reducing FLOPs and cache size while preserving performance. At inference, lookahead keys are updated via a rank-1 update from the previous step, yielding O(td) per-step cost.

## Results

CASTLE and CASTLE-SWL consistently outperform standard causal attention across all four model scales (0.16B–1.3B) after 50B training tokens on FineWeb-Edu. At the XL scale (1.3B), CASTLE-SWL-XL achieves a validation perplexity of 10.900 versus 11.309 for the baseline (a 0.037 loss reduction), and an average 5-shot downstream accuracy of 60.45% versus 58.11%. On long-context RULER S-NIAH tasks at 16K sequence length, CASTLE-SWL-XL-16K reaches 99.2% on S-NIAH-1 and 92.8% on S-NIAH-2, compared to 85.4% and 60.2% for the baseline, respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is standard causal attention with a LLaMA-style architecture (SwiGLU, RoPE, RMSNorm), trained under identical hyperparameters and token budgets. CASTLE and CASTLE-SWL win on every metric at every scale: at XL, CASTLE-SWL-XL reduces validation loss by 0.037 (2.389 vs. 2.426) and perplexity by 0.409 (10.900 vs. 11.309), while improving average 5-shot downstream accuracy by 2.34 points (60.45% vs. 58.11%). The gains grow with model size, from a modest 0.008 loss reduction at 0.16B to 0.037 at 1.3B. On long-context RULER S-NIAH-3 at 16K, the baseline collapses to 15.8% accuracy while CASTLE-SWL-XL-16K maintains 59.4%, and after YaRN extrapolation to 32K the gap widens further (6.2% vs. 0.0%). Ablations confirm the gains come from the lookahead-key mechanism itself rather than from the slightly higher key count (18 vs. 16) or from the sliding-window component alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

CASTLE extends standard causal attention (Vaswani et al., 2017) by splitting each head's keys into a static causal half and a dynamic lookahead half that incorporates information from subsequent tokens, while preserving autoregressiveness. It addresses the well-documented limitation that causal masking restricts global context capture (Li & Li, 2024; Du et al., 2022; Springer et al., 2025), but unlike prior work on sentence embeddings (BeLLM, Echo Embeddings, RE2) that modifies decoding or duplicates inputs, CASTLE operates directly within the pretraining attention layer. The rank-1 key update at inference echoes PaTH Attention (Yang et al., 2025), but CASTLE's parallel training equivalence (Theorem 1.1) and FlashAttention-style O(L²d) kernels distinguish it from ENTP (Ewer et al., 2024), whose per-token compute scales cubically over full sequences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The mathematical equivalence between the recurrent lookahead-key definition and a closed-form parallel expression is the key enabler for practical adoption: it means CASTLE can be dropped into existing LLaMA-style training pipelines with O(L²d) complexity rather than the naive O(L³d) cost. The dramatic long-context retrieval gains (e.g., S-NIAH-3 at 16K: 15.8% → 59.4%) and robust YaRN extrapolation suggest lookahead keys may be a low-cost lever for extending effective context windows. Open questions include whether values can be similarly updated, the optimal causal-to-lookahead key ratio, and whether the SiLU stabilization requirement generalizes to larger scales or different initializations.
