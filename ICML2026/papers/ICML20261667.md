## Problem

Existing deep learning models for structure-based drug design operate as one-shot generators that lack feedback mechanisms, leaving the iterative Design-Make-Test-Analyze (DMTA) cycle disconnected. The paper identifies three systemic gaps: no mechanism to prioritize high-potential molecular lineages (Policy Disconnect), chemical modification rules hidden inside black-box token generation (Translation Disconnect), and sparse scalar docking scores that prevent fine-grained credit assignment to specific structural changes (Feedback Disconnect). K-BTS addresses all three by formalizing iterative SBDD as a Dynamic Hierarchical Multi-Armed Bandit problem.

## Input / Output

The method takes a target protein binding site and an initial molecular pool of 100 ligands with pre-computed docking scores (either randomly sampled from CrossDocked2020 training data or generated by ALIDiff), plus a warm-started knowledge base of scaffold-conditioned chemical actions extracted from intra-scaffold transitions in the initial pool. At each of 100 iterations, the system outputs one new SMILES string generated by DeepSeek-V3.2, which is then docked via smina to produce a scalar reward. The growing molecular pool and knowledge base persist across iterations, with the LLM explicitly conditioned on a forbidden list of previously evaluated derivatives to prevent regeneration.

## Method

K-BTS decouples each DMTA iteration into two Thompson Sampling levels: an upper level that selects a seed molecule from the pool using a Boltzmann-weighted score S(m) = θ̂_m · exp(−y_m/T) where θ̂_m ~ Beta(α_m, β_m) captures lineage refinement potential, and a lower level that retrieves a scaffold-conditioned modification action from a dynamic knowledge base by maximizing expected utility U(a) = θ̂_a · R̄_pos + (1−θ̂_a) · R̄_neg, gated by a scaffold-similarity threshold τ. The retrieved action serves as a semantic anchor in a single-step chain-of-thought prompt to DeepSeek-V3.2, which outputs a rationale and a new SMILES. After docking, a bi-level Bayesian update decomposes the scalar reward: the seed's Beta parameters receive a magnitude-sensitive increment ω_m = λ|Δy|, while the action's parameters are updated as a Bernoulli trial; additionally, all atomic sub-actions extracted bidirectionally from the structural diff receive proportional credit (ω = 1/n per modified site), enabling fine-grained experience accumulation that transforms sparse docking feedback into reusable chemical knowledge.

## Results

On the CrossDocked2020 benchmark (100 test targets, 100 candidates per target), K-BTS-diff achieves a state-of-the-art Top-1 average Vina docking score of −10.23 kcal/mol, outperforming the strongest iterative baseline ELILLM-diff (−9.80) by 0.43 and the SOTA diffusion model ALIDiff (−9.37) by 0.86. Notably, K-BTS-diff's Top-20 average score (−9.44) surpasses ALIDiff's Top-1 score (−9.37), indicating systematic improvement across the entire candidate pool rather than isolated outliers. Under the more challenging pocket-agnostic initialization (K-BTS-rand), the method still reaches −9.60 Top-1, exceeding both ALIDiff and TargetDiff. All improvements are statistically significant with Holm-Bonferroni corrected p-values < 2.01×10⁻⁷.

## Baselines & Comparisons

K-BTS-diff (−10.23 Top-1) decisively outperforms all eight baselines: it beats the closest iterative competitor ELILLM-diff (−9.80) by 0.43 kcal/mol, LMLF-diff (−9.29) by 0.94, and the best one-shot diffusion model ALIDiff (−9.37) by 0.86. Under the harder random-initialization setting, K-BTS-rand (−9.60) still surpasses ELILLM-rand (−9.33) by 0.27 and LMLF-rand (−8.66) by 0.94, and notably exceeds both ALIDiff (−9.37) and TargetDiff (−9.38) despite starting from pocket-agnostic ligands. The advantage is not limited to the best single molecule: K-BTS-diff's Top-20 average (−9.44) exceeds ALIDiff's Top-1 (−9.37), confirming a distributional shift rather than a single lucky outlier. In the ablation, removing the upper-level seed selection (w/o upper) causes the largest regression (−8.95 vs. −9.60 for K-BTS-rand), while removing the knowledge base (w/o knowledge) still retains moderate performance (−9.33), indicating that the curated action manifold itself provides substantial directional guidance even without the Thompson Sampling retrieval mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical Thompson Sampling / multi-armed bandit framework (Thompson 1933; Robbins 1952) into a dynamic hierarchical setting tailored to iterative molecular optimization, and positions itself against the recent wave of LLM-based iterative refinement methods (LMLF, ELILLM) that treat the LLM as a black-box generator without explicit action decomposition. The key conceptual departure is the formalization of the DMTA cycle as a Dynamic Hierarchical Multi-Armed Bandit, where the upper level selects molecular lineages and the lower level retrieves explicit chemical rules from a growing knowledge base, rather than relying on implicit token-level generation. The warm-start knowledge extraction from initial docking data and the bidirectional action decomposition (forward/backward with scaffold-aware weighting) are novel mechanisms not present in prior LLM-SBDD work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a principled, interpretable alternative to black-box iterative LLM refinement, with the bi-level Bayesian update converting sparse scalar docking feedback into reusable chemical knowledge — a step toward closing the DMTA loop that prior methods left open. The finding that pocket-agnostic random initialization can outperform target-aware diffusion models (ALIDiff, TargetDiff) suggests that strategic iterative refinement may be more important than specialized generative priors, though this is demonstrated on a single benchmark with docking scores as proxy. Open questions include whether the knowledge base generalizes across chemically distant targets, whether the theoretical convergence guarantees hold under realistic docking noise, and whether the approach scales to multi-objective optimization (solubility, selectivity, toxicity) beyond single-score docking.
