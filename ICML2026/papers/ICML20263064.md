## Problem

Training-free test-time adaptation (TTA) for vision-language models improves zero-shot classification under mild shifts but degrades sharply under severe style or environment variation. The paper identifies two shared structural failure modes across existing training-free pipelines: retrieval confounding, where feature similarity is dominated by nuisance/style cues rather than semantic identity, and environment-biased priors, where VLM logits exhibit environment-dependent centered shifts that distort gating and prior-like terms. No prior training-free method explicitly debiases both of these inference-time objects simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a set of text prompts (one per class) as input, producing a final class label. Internally, D²O maps each test sample to three inference-time objects: a retrieval-oriented content feature (d-dimensional, ℓ₂-normalized) for semantic matching, a style-aware routing coordinate (r-dimensional, with r=8 by default) for environment clustering, and a debiased logit vector (C-dimensional) serving as a corrected prior. The operator requires a short warm-up buffer of N_S=128 unlabeled test samples for subspace initialization and operates without any backpropagation or parameter updates, supporting both online (sequential, batch size 1) and transductive (full test set) protocols.

## Method

D²O is a strictly training-free, plug-and-play debiasing operator that decomposes each test sample's CLIP image feature into inference-oriented style and content signals. During a warm-up phase, paired weak augmentations (e.g., brightness ±ε, contrast ±ε) produce finite-difference responses whose covariance reveals a low-rank nuisance-sensitive subspace; the top-r eigenvectors define a projector. At inference, the feature is projected onto this subspace to yield a style-aware routing coordinate, while the residual (softly suppressed by λ_proj) is renormalized into a retrieval-oriented content feature. Environment-specific centered-logit bias is tracked via K_env online clusters in the routing space, each maintaining an EMA-updated bias template in centered-logit space; the debiased logit is obtained by subtracting the routed cluster's bias template from the raw CLIP logits. The operator is instantiated on two host families—TDA (cache-based retrieval, where f_cnt drives retrieval and s_deb replaces the CLIP prior) and ADAPT (Gaussian posterior, where s_deb serves as a debiased prior multiplied by a Gaussian likelihood and a retrieval-oriented bank evidence term). The paper further provides operator-to-decision guarantees: finite-difference covariance recovers the nuisance subspace with a Davis-Kahan-type bound, routing-based EMA controls centered-logit template estimation with a martingale concentration bound, and these errors propagate to bounded pairwise score perturbations yielding a margin-based sufficient condition for label invariance.

## Results

On PUG (style/environment-dominant shift), D²O+ADAPT achieves 58.28% top-1 accuracy in the Online setting, outperforming ADAPT (51.87%) by +6.41% and TDA (51.50%) by +6.78%. On the four ImageNet OOD variants, D²O+ADAPT reaches 65.92% OOD average (Online) and 66.53% (Transductive), the strongest among all compared methods. On ImageNet-C corruption, D²O+ADAPT attains 29.15% (Online) and 31.27% (Transductive), and on the 10 fine-grained datasets it achieves 71.12% (Online) and 72.62% (Transductive) average accuracy. Gains are largest under style/environment-dominant shifts (PUG: +6.41%) and smaller but consistent on standard OOD (+0.48%), corruption (+0.59%), and fine-grained (+0.36%) benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

D²O+ADAPT outperforms its direct host ADAPT across all four task families: +6.41% on PUG (Online), +0.48% on ImageNet OOD (Online), +0.59% on ImageNet-C (Online), and +0.36% on fine-grained (Online). Against the retrieval-based host TDA, D²O+TDA yields smaller but positive gains (+0.72% on PUG, +0.26% on OOD, +0.25% on corruption, +0.49% on fine-grained). Among all 29 compared baselines, D²O+ADAPT achieves the best OOD average (65.92% Online, 66.53% Transductive), surpassing the next-best training-free methods ADAPT (65.44%/65.97%), DOTA (64.53%), and ZERO (64.86%). On PUG, the gap to the strongest baseline ADAPT widens to +6.41%, with particularly large improvements on Lighting (+16.41%) and Texture (+5.72%), confirming the method's design target of style/environment-dominant shifts. On fine-grained datasets, D²O+ADAPT edges out ADAPT (71.12% vs 70.76%) and DMN (70.30%), with the largest per-dataset gains on OxfordPets (+0.47%) and Flowers102 (+1.18%). The method also demonstrates plug-and-play scope: when inserted into CoOp, MTA, ZERO, and TPT, it yields +5.80%, +0.34%, +0.54%, and +6.24% on PUG respectively, confirming it operates as an upstream debiasing layer rather than a host-specific modification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions D²O as an operator-level debiasing layer that sits upstream of existing training-free TTA hosts, rather than proposing yet another host-specific adapter. It builds directly on TDA (Karmanov et al., CVPR 2024) for cache-based retrieval and ADAPT (Zhang et al., NeurIPS 2025) for closed-form Gaussian posterior alignment, inserting its three inference-time objects (retrieval-oriented content feature, style-aware routing coordinate, debiased logits) into their pipelines without altering their internal mechanics. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant paradigm of redesigning the adaptation procedure itself by instead diagnosing two shared failure modes—retrieval confounding and environment-biased priors—and correcting the corrupted inference objects before they reach the host adapter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play operator framing makes D²O practically attractive: it can be inserted into any training-free pipeline without backpropagation, and the efficiency comparison (≈4× faster than TPT, 33% faster than ADAPT) supports deployment in resource-constrained settings. However, the clearest gains are concentrated on PUG-style shifts (+6.41% online), while improvements on standard OOD, corruption, and fine-grained benchmarks are modest (+0.2–0.9%), limiting the method's perceived generality. Open questions include whether the low-rank finite-difference subspace assumption holds under high-rank or adversarial shifts, whether fixed-capacity routing (K_env clusters) suffices for highly imbalanced mixed-style streams, and whether the transductive memory overhead can be reduced for large-scale deployment.
