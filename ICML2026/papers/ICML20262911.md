## Problem

Current audio-visual large language models (AV-LLMs) are restricted to 2D perception using RGB video and monaural audio, creating a fundamental dimensionality mismatch that precludes reliable sound-source localization and spatial reasoning in 3D environments. Existing work addresses visual depth or spatial audio in isolation, and no unified end-to-end framework jointly performs 3D grounding and audio-visual reasoning with explicit geometric and directional cues.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262911/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a single RGB-D frame (1920×1080 with aligned metric depth and calibrated camera intrinsics/extrinsics), a 4-channel first-order ambisonics (FOA) waveform, and a natural-language instruction. Outputs are task-specific: spherical DoA angles (azimuth in [−180°, 180°], elevation in [−90°, 90°] under a right-handed camera frame), a 3D axis-aligned bounding box encoded as the structured token sequence bbox(c, x, y, z, sx, sy, sz) in camera coordinates, or a discrete speaker-identity label (e.g., left/center/right) for multi-speaker matching.

## Method

JAEGER initializes from Qwen2.5-Omni and is adapted via LoRA (r=64, α=128) with a dual-stream architecture. The visual stream fuses RGB semantic tokens with 3D-aware sinusoidal positional encodings derived from depth-projected metric point clouds, explicitly grounding visual features in metric space. The audio stream uses a dual-path design: semantic content is extracted from the omnidirectional FOA channel (W), while spatial directional cues come from either a Classical Intensity Vector (STFT cross-spectrum of W with X/Y/Z channels) or the proposed Neural IV, which replaces the STFT with a learnable 7-layer 1D-CNN (data2vec frontend) and computes element-wise products between omnidirectional and directional latent features before an MLP projection. Training is supervised instruction tuning on the 61k-sample SpatialSceneQA benchmark, with selective fine-tuning of modality-specific encoders and projectors while the LLM is updated only through LoRA. Ablations show that removing the FOA spatial branch collapses reasoning accuracy to near-random (~44–48%), confirming that monaural representations are fundamentally insufficient for 3D spatial disambiguation.

## Results

On the SpatialSceneQA benchmark, JAEGER (Neural IV) achieves a median angular error (MAE) of 2.21° for single-source DoA and 4.11° for overlapping-source DoA, a 3D IoU of 0.32 with a median visual offset of 0.16 m for 3D bounding-box grounding, and 99.5%/99.2% accuracy on single- and two-speaker audio-visual reasoning. The Classical IV variant is slightly weaker on audio (2.95°/6.44° MAE) but matches on visual grounding and reasoning (99.5%/98.6%). On the small-scale STARSS23 real-world transfer test, SpatialSceneQA pretraining reduces Neural IV's median elevation error from 7.3° to 4.8° compared to training from scratch.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the specialized audio model BAT, JAEGER (Neural IV) is marginally worse on single-source DoA (2.21° vs. 2.16°) but dramatically outperforms it on the overlapping-source task (4.11° vs. 19.09° MAE), a 4.6× reduction in angular error; BAT also cannot perform 3D grounding or audio-visual reasoning. Against the 2D omni model Qwen2.5-Omni, JAEGER dominates across every task: Qwen2.5-Omni is entirely incapable of DoA estimation (monaural input), achieves 0.00 3D IoU, and scores only 35.8%/44.0% on reasoning versus JAEGER's 99.5%/99.2%. Against the RGB-only Qwen3-VL-8B, JAEGER improves 3D IoU from 0.01 to 0.32 and reduces visual offset from 1.11 m to 0.16 m, while also handling all audio tasks that Qwen3-VL cannot. Against N3D-VLM (RGB-D only), JAEGER lifts 3D IoU from 0.00 to 0.32 and cuts visual offset from 2.04 m to 0.16 m, again while adding full audio-visual reasoning capability. Overall, JAEGER is the only model evaluated that simultaneously handles all six task types, and it sets the best score on five of six metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

JAEGER unifies two previously fragmented research threads—3D visual grounding via RGB-D depth encoding (following Video-3D LLM and N3D-VLM) and spatial audio understanding via intensity-vector representations (following Tang et al., 2024)—into a single end-to-end AV-LLM initialized from Qwen2.5-Omni. It departs from prior cascaded or modular pipelines (e.g., SAVVY's signal-processing-based localization, Hear You Are's single-source assumption) by jointly learning spatial audio and visual geometry within one model, and introduces the Neural IV as a learned replacement for the fixed STFT-based Classical IV. The 3D bounding-box output format and sinusoidal depth-projected positional encoding are directly inherited from N3D-VLM and Video-3D LLM, respectively, while the dual-path audio design (semantic W-channel + spatial X/Y/Z channels) extends the intensity-vector formulation into a trainable CNN latent space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first large-scale (61k-sample) benchmark with degree-level azimuth/elevation supervision and dense 3D object annotations for joint audio-visual spatial reasoning, filling a clear gap left by RGB-only or single-source datasets like STARSS23. The ablation showing near-random accuracy collapse (~44–47%) when FOA is removed—even after fine-tuning—empirically validates that monaural audio is fundamentally insufficient for 3D spatial disambiguation, a claim that has been argued but not previously demonstrated at this scale. Key open questions include sim-to-real transfer (only a 753-clip sanity check on STARSS23 is provided), whether the near-ceiling reasoning accuracy on 2–3 candidates generalizes to realistic distractor counts (Appendix D shows significant degradation at 4–6 candidates), and whether the Neural IV's advantage over Classical IV persists under real reverberant acoustics rather than controlled simulation.
