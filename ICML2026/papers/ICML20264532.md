## Problem

Existing group counterfactual methods optimize counterfactuals point-wise for a fixed group, so they cannot generalize to new group members without expensive re-optimization, and enforcing bi-Lipschitz geometry preservation makes the problem non-convex and prone to non-convergence. No prior approach learns a single generalizable mapping function that simultaneously minimizes total transport cost and controls group geometric distortion in a convex, tractable way.

## Input / Output

The method takes a user-defined group of n tabular instances in R^d, a linear (or linearizable) classifier with a score function and threshold α, a target counterfactual label c', and user-specified bi-Lipschitz constants K, k ≥ 1. It outputs a learned OT map ĝ: X→X parameterized as either an affine transform (matrix A ∈ R^{d×d}, vector b ∈ R^d) or a Gaussian distribution (μ_Q, Σ_Q), which can be applied to any new instance without re-optimization. The number of parameters scales with feature dimension d (e.g., d²+d for affine) rather than with group size n, enabling real-time recourse generation for unseen group members.

## Method

The core contribution is reframing group counterfactuals as learning a Monge OT map ĝ that minimizes the empirical W₂ transport cost subject to classification and bi-Lipschitz constraints, reducing the optimization from nd point-wise variables to a small set of map parameters. For affine maps with a PSD constraint on A, the bi-Lipschitz condition is expressed as a Loewner-order LMI (1/k·I ≼ A ≼ K·I), yielding a convex SDP; diagonal A reduces this to a QP with simple box constraints. For Gaussian parameterizations, the closed-form W₂ expression between multivariate normals (Givens & Shortt) is optimized via SDP, with further simplifications to QCQP or QP under commutativity or scaling assumptions. The paper proves that the functional formulation is convex for linear classifiers while the point-wise bi-Lipschitz baseline is non-convex, and establishes density-preservation bounds linking bi-Lipschitz constants to Jacobian determinants. When linearity cannot be exploited, the same parameterizations are optimized via NSGA-II multiobjective metaheuristics, where the reduced parameter space yields significantly better hypervolume than baselines.

## Results

Across 15 binary classification datasets (300 groups total), the dense OT maps (PSD affine, Gaussian) cover 90% of experiments within a factor of 1.7 of the optimal W2 distance, while the group-with-bi-Lipschitz baseline fails to converge in 50% of experiments at K=1.01 (35% overall). On the efficiency metric (W2² × log(#parameters)), all proposals surpass the bi-Lipschitz baseline in 60% of experiments, with the baseline requiring a performance factor of 2.2 versus 1.1–1.9 for the proposed methods. In the multiobjective (NSGA-II) setting, all proposals achieve significantly larger Pareto hypervolume than any baseline, with sparser maps (diagonal affine, scaled Gaussian) performing best at ratios of ~1.01 and ~1.4 across 80% of experiments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Independent (Wachter) baseline, the proposed OT maps incur only ~30% higher W2 distance while enforcing the bi-Lipschitz lower bound that the baseline ignores; the Independent method achieves the lowest raw W2 but provides no geometric distortion control. Against the Group-with-Lipschitz baseline (Carrizosa et al., 2024b), the proposed methods are slightly worse in raw W2 for sparse transforms (factor ~2.3 at 80%) but significantly better in efficiency due to fewer parameters (d²+d vs. nd). Against the Group-with-bi-Lipschitz baseline (the direct comparison), the proposed methods win decisively: the baseline fails to converge in 50% of experiments at K=1.01 and 35% overall, while all proposed methods always converge. On the efficiency metric, the bi-Lipschitz baseline requires a factor of 2.2 relative to the best method, whereas the proposals stay within 1.1–1.9. In the multiobjective NSGA-II experiment (simulating black-box classifiers), all proposed maps yield significantly larger hypervolume than all three baselines, with the Gaussian maps outperforming their affine counterparts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the group counterfactual explanation framework of Carrizosa et al. (2024b), which formulates the problem as a point-wise QCQP with nd decision variables, by replacing discrete counterfactual points with an explicit optimal transport map (affine or Gaussian) that generalizes to unseen group members. The closed-form W2 expression for Gaussian distributions from Givens & Shortt (1984) underpins the Gaussian parameterization, while the bi-Lipschitz geometry constraint is recast as a convex semidefinite program rather than the non-convex pairwise distance constraints of the baseline. The paper also draws on Brenier's theorem for the optimality of deterministic Monge maps and introduces Bayesian network surrogates for post-hoc interpretability of the learned transport function.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The shift from per-instance optimization to a learnable OT map addresses a practical gap: existing group counterfactual methods cannot generalize to new group members without expensive re-optimization, and the bi-Lipschitz geometry constraint renders the baseline non-convex (failing to converge in ~35% of experiments). The convexity guarantees for linear classifiers and the parameter reduction (from nd to d²+d or fewer) make the approach scalable and interpretable. Key limitations include the reliance on linear/linearizable classifiers for convex optimization, the potential insufficiency of affine maps for complex modalities (images), and the fact that the framework is less useful when congestion or uniformity in recourse are not concerns. Open questions include extending to autoregressive or neural OT maps for non-linear classifiers and high-dimensional data.
