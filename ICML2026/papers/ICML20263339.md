## Problem

Answer-Level Fine-Tuning (ALFT) requires optimizing a language model based on the correctness or distributional properties of its final answers rather than specific reasoning traces, but computing gradients of the marginal answer likelihood is intractable because it requires summing over the vast combinatorial space of latent reasoning paths. Standard policy-gradient estimators like REINFORCE suffer from extreme variance in this many-to-one setting, and recent heuristic alignment methods (inverse-frequency rewards for diversity, consensus-based self-improvement) lack a unified theoretical justification.

## Input / Output

The method consumes text prompts paired with task-preserving paraphrases (forming an 'orbit' of semantically equivalent inputs) and generates groups of K reasoning traces per prompt. A deterministic extraction function E maps each trace to a discrete answer (e.g., a number or multiple-choice letter), or a learned pairwise semantic distance d(y,y') replaces E for open-ended domains where surface-form matching is brittle. The output is a fine-tuned language model (via QLoRA on 3B-class LLMs) whose induced answer distribution satisfies the chosen alignment objective—coherence, diversity, or safety constraints.

## Method

The paper formulates ALFT as a two-player min-max game between a Policy π (the generator) and an auxiliary Target distribution q, derived via Fenchel duality of the answer-level objective. The key theoretical result is that the Nash equilibrium of this game exactly recovers the solution to the original intractable marginalization problem, with the optimal policy taking the closed form π*(y|x) ∝ π₀(y|x)·q*(E(y)|x), i.e., a KL projection of the reference onto traces compatible with the target. The policy update is instantiated as GRPO, where rewards are defined as R(y) = log q*(E(y)) and advantages are standardized within each group, converting the game into a tractable alternating best-response loop. Specific target strategies yield distinct algorithms: COHERENCE-GRPO uses orbit-level majority vote for discrete answers, PAIRWISE-GRPO uses average semantic centrality for open-ended generation, DIVERSITY-GRPO recovers the inverse-frequency reward as the exact equilibrium of a max-entropy game, and SAFETY-GRPO uses a primal-dual update where Lagrange multipliers act as dynamic penalty weights for distributional constraints.

## Results

On GSM8K (greedy decoding), both PAIRWISE-GRPO and COHERENCE-GRPO yield consistent absolute improvements of +3.18 to +9.18 percentage points across three 3B-class models (Qwen2.5-3B, Llama-3.2-3B, Phi-3-mini-4k), with the largest gain being +9.18 pp for PAIRWISE-GRPO on Phi-3 (73.69% → 82.87%). On TriviaQA, PAIRWISE-GRPO achieves up to +42.06% relative Exact Match improvement (Phi-3: 32.03% → 45.50%), while COHERENCE-GRPO produces negligible or slightly negative changes due to its reliance on exact-match extraction. A synthetic 1000-to-1 redundant mapping experiment confirms that GAME-GRPO's group-relative baseline reduces gradient variance from σ≈0.4 (REINFORCE) to near zero, enabling convergence to 100% accuracy versus REINFORCE's ~80% plateau. The SAFETY-GRPO pilot on Qwen2.5-3B reduces harmful mean toxicity from 0.0733 to 0.0677 while maintaining a 99% benign non-refusal rate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against other alignment methods (e.g., DPO, standard RLHF, or self-consistency decoding); instead, all comparisons are against the unmodified base model before fine-tuning. On GSM8K, both proposed algorithms (PAIRWISE-GRPO and COHERENCE-GRPO) consistently outperform the base model across all three architectures, with PAIRWISE-GRPO achieving the largest absolute gain (+9.18 pp on Phi-3). On TriviaQA, PAIRWISE-GRPO substantially outperforms COHERENCE-GRPO (up to +42.06% vs. −0.15% relative EM on Phi-3), demonstrating that the pairwise semantic distance metric is critical for open-ended domains where exact-match extraction fails. In the controlled synthetic experiment, GAME-GRPO decisively outperforms REINFORCE with a global moving-average baseline: it converges to 100% accuracy with near-zero gradient variance, while REINFORCE plateaus at ~80% with σ≈0.4 due to its inability to normalize heterogeneous difficulty. The SAFETY-GRPO pilot shows the primal-dual update successfully enforces both toxicity and benign-refusal budgets simultaneously, reducing harmful toxicity by 0.0056 while keeping benign non-refusal at 99%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies several previously separate alignment paradigms—outcome-based RL, diversity promotion via inverse-frequency rewards (Li et al., 2025), coherence-based self-improvement (Mohri et al., 2025), and constrained safety optimization—under a single Fenchel-duality min-max game between a Policy and a Target distribution. It builds directly on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) as the policy-gradient solver and on the Bregman projection framework of Mohri et al. (2025) for the coherence objective, but departs from both by deriving all reward signals from the game's equilibrium target rather than from ad-hoc heuristics. The paper also establishes a formal duality with DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023), showing that learning the optimal target distribution is equivalent to learning the implicit reward function, positioning the framework as the 'dual' of preference-based alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a principled replacement for reward engineering in answer-level alignment: instead of designing heuristics per task, one selects a convex functional R and the optimal reward follows from the game equilibrium. This is practically useful for practitioners choosing between COHERENCE-GRPO (discrete answers) and PAIRWISE-GRPO (open-ended answers), and the safety instantiation offers a theoretically grounded alternative to Lagrangian RL. Key limitations include evaluation only on 3B-class models across two benchmarks, pilot-scale safety experiments with small absolute changes, and the use of majority-vote (a coarse approximation to the ideal geometric-mean target) that may underperform in highly multimodal settings. Open questions include whether the framework scales to larger models, whether the O(1/√T) no-regret convergence rate is tight in practice with stochastic GRPO updates, and how to handle continuous or unbounded answer spaces where the simplex parameterization breaks down.
