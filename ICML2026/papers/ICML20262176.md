## Problem

The Expected Information Gain (EIG) in Bayesian Experimental Design is a doubly intractable nested expectation, making its estimation and gradient computation prohibitively expensive. While closed-form or singly-nested EIG expressions are known for Gaussian likelihoods with Gaussian priors, no general characterization existed for when the EIG collapses to a tractable form. This paper identifies the precise structural condition—posterior belonging to an exponential family with design-dependence only through natural parameters—that guarantees this collapse.

## Input / Output

The method requires a likelihood expressible in exponential family form p(y|θ,ξ) = h_ℓ(y,ξ) exp(⟨λ(y,ξ), T_p(θ)⟩ − Ψ_ℓ(T_p(θ))) with a log-partition function independent of ξ, an arbitrary prior p(θ), and a reparameterization g(ε,θ,ξ) for sampling y. It produces unbiased singly-intractable Monte Carlo estimators for both the EIG (requiring only outer samples y ~ p(y|ξ)) and its reparameterized gradient (requiring joint samples (θ,ε) ~ p(θ)p(ε)), which converge at O(1/T) rate. When the prior predictive is discrete and bounded, both quantities become fully analytic.

## Method

The core result (Theorem 3.1) shows that when the posterior p(θ|y,ξ) always belongs to a fixed exponential family with natural parameters λ(y,ξ), the EIG reduces to E_{p(y|ξ)}[(λ(y,ξ)−λ₀)ᵀμ(y,ξ) − Ψ_p(λ(y,ξ))] up to design-independent constants, by expressing the EIG as an expected KL divergence between the posterior and a fixed reference distribution in the same EF and applying the closed-form EF KL formula. The gradient is further simplified via the reparameterization trick to E_{p(θ)p(ε)}[(λ(y,ξ)−λ₀)ᵀ∇_ξ μ(y,ξ)], where the ∇_ξλ terms cancel using the mean-parameter identity μ = ∇_λΨ. Theorem 4.1 provides a sufficient condition on the likelihood (EF form with ξ-independent log-partition) that guarantees the required posterior structure for any prior, and Lemma 4.2 extends this to sequential settings via an expanded conjugate EF. The paper additionally characterizes degeneracies: EIG independence of ξ under invertible design-removing transformations, element-wise monotonicity in an exposure function c(ξ) (Theorem 5.1), and convexity over convex design spaces when the reparameterized posterior mean is affine in ξ (Theorem 5.2).

## Results

In the 100-D Gaussian linear regression convergence study, the singly-intractable (SI) estimator achieves an MSE of ~10⁻⁴, while both NMC and PCE fail to reach an MSE below 10⁴ — an 8-order-of-magnitude gap. In the sequential Poisson and Wishart design experiments, NMC and PCE with ~25× more total samples still achieve only about half the cumulative utility of SI with 100 outer samples. In the active GP regression (EPIG) experiment, NMC requires 500 outer × 500 inner samples (250× the MC budget of SI's 1000 outer samples) to match SI's optimization performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its singly-intractable (SI) estimator against two nested Monte Carlo baselines: NMC (Rainforth et al., 2018) and PCE (Foster et al., 2021). SI dominates both baselines across all four experimental settings. In the convergence-rate study (1-D, 10-D, 100-D Gaussian regression), SI follows the theoretical O(1/T) rate with MSE scaling linearly in dimension, while NMC and PCE exhibit bias-induced MSE floors that worsen with dimension; in 100-D the gap reaches 8 orders of magnitude. In the sequential Poisson and Wishart design tasks, NMC and PCE with (50,50) inner/outer samples (~25× SI's budget) still yield roughly half the cumulative utility of SI with just 100 outer samples. In the GP EPIG experiment, NMC with 20 outer × 50 inner samples produces visibly inferior batch designs, and even 500×500 NMC only matches SI's 1000-sample performance. PCE consistently tracks NMC closely in all experiments, confirming that the nested structure — not the specific inner-estimator variant — is the bottleneck.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the well-known tractability of EIG in the Gaussian/GP setting (Houlsby et al., 2011) to all exponential family posteriors whose design-dependence flows through natural parameters. The core derivation builds on the KL-divergence formula for same-family EF distributions (Nielsen & Nock, 2010) and the nested Monte Carlo estimation framework (Rainforth et al., 2018), showing that the doubly-nested structure collapses to a single expectation under the EF posterior condition. A key departure from prior work is Theorem 4.1, which identifies a likelihood-form condition that is both necessary and sufficient for the EF posterior to arise, and reveals that this condition implies conjugacy to *any* prior — a broader result than the standard conjugate-pairing framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This answers a long-standing open question in Bayesian experimental design: beyond Gaussians, which models admit tractable EIG? The practical impact is significant for the applicable model class, reducing estimation cost from O(NM) to O(N) and enabling gradient-based optimization of designs that was previously infeasible with nested estimators. The characterization of degenerate behaviors (EIG monotonicity in exposure, convexity under affine posterior means) is an important caveat for practitioners, showing that naive EF model choices can make the optimization problem ill-posed. Open questions include whether basis-expansion approximations of non-EF likelihoods can recover the singly-intractable form at acceptable cost, and whether EF-based control variates can improve the standard NMC/PCE estimators when the EF condition does not hold exactly.
