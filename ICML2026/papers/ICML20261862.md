## Problem

Robotic RL policies deployed in the wild encounter post-training faults—actuator degradation, unmodeled dynamics shifts, environmental changes—where retraining, exploration, or system identification are infeasible due to safety constraints and computational cost. Existing robustness approaches (domain randomization, fault-aware policies, online system identification) require prior fault exposure during training or explicit dynamics inference, leaving a gap for unexpected, unknown faults that arise after deployment. This paper asks whether a frozen policy can be augmented at inference time with lightweight, conservative correction that recovers performance without modifying base policy parameters.

## Input / Output

The residual controller consumes proprioceptive joint positions and velocities, a phase-indexed reference joint acceleration from a precomputed nominal rollout, and the frozen policy's nominal action at each step. It produces an additive residual correction (bounded, clipped, and directionally gated) that is summed with the nominal action to yield the executed control signal. For persistent faults, the stabilized slow-pathway weights are additionally consolidated offline into a static linear adapter that reproduces the corrective mapping without online plasticity, freeing the residual pathway for future faults.

## Method

The architecture instantiates three cerebellar principles: (1) high-dimensional pattern separation via a fixed random ReLU feature expansion (M=2500) with dual exponential eligibility traces at distinct timescales (excitatory α_E=Δt/0.03, inhibitory α_I=Δt/0.30); (2) phase-conditioned microzones (K=4) that restrict learning to the current gait phase, preventing cross-context interference; and (3) separate fast and slow linear weight heads per microzone updated by a normalized LMS rule driven by composite tracking error, with deadzones and weight projection for boundedness. A meta-controller monitors an exponential moving average of task reward and modulates residual gain and learning rates conservatively—remaining inactive under nominal performance and activating only upon sustained degradation—while a directional-consistency gate suppresses residual actions opposing the base policy. Under persistent faults, the slow-head corrections are consolidated via ridge regression into a static adapter, transferring fault structure into fixed parameters and regenerating adaptive capacity for future disturbances.

## Results

Under moderate post-training faults, the proposed cerebellar residual controller (SAC+Ours) achieves the highest episodic return across all evaluated settings: on HalfCheetah-v5 it reaches 7239±2159 (actuator), 7502±1281 (dynamic), and 19517±3834 (environment), and on Ant-v5 it reaches 5622±1137, 4728±1447, and 4551±2132 respectively. On the high-dimensional Humanoid-v5 task, it achieves 13981±5624 (actuator), 15282±5979 (dynamic), and 19825±7932 (environment), outperforming all baselines in every fault family. The paper reports headline improvements of up to +66% over the frozen SAC baseline on HalfCheetah-v5 and +53% on Humanoid-v5 under moderate faults, with graceful degradation under severe whole-body perturbations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method (SAC+Ours) ranks first or second in all 12 evaluated settings (2 environments × 3 fault types × 2 tables) and is the only method to consistently outperform the frozen SAC baseline across every fault family and environment. Against Robust SAC (domain randomization, Tobin et al.), the proposed method wins decisively on HalfCheetah-v5 actuator faults (7239 vs. 3093) and dynamic faults (7502 vs. 3491), where training-time randomization actually degrades performance below the nominal policy. Against SAC+OSI (online system identification, Yu et al.), the proposed method matches or exceeds performance on HalfCheetah-v5 (7239 vs. 7548 on actuator, 7502 vs. 8810 on dynamic) while substantially outperforming it on Ant-v5 (5622 vs. 3128 on actuator, 4728 vs. 3376 on dynamic), where OSI's low-dimensional fault parameterization breaks down. Against the offline residual baseline (Johannink et al.), the proposed method achieves roughly 2× improvement on HalfCheetah-v5 actuator faults (7239 vs. 3898) and environment faults (19517 vs. 12977). Against SAC+CMAC (Hovakimyan & Cao) and SAC+Online LMS (Slotine & Li), the proposed method consistently leads, with the largest gaps on HalfCheetah-v5 environment faults (19517 vs. 11011 and 11672) and Ant-v5 actuator faults (5622 vs. 5324 and 4273). On Humanoid-v5 (Table 2), the proposed method achieves the highest return in all three fault families (13981, 15282, 19825), outperforming Robust SAC (11620, 12597, 14694) and SAC+OSI (5754, 7790, 9265) by 20–115%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes core cerebellar computational principles—high-dimensional random-feature pattern separation, phase-conditioned microzone organization, and dual-timescale eligibility traces—from classical Feedback Error Learning models (Albus, Kawato & Gomi, Nakanishi & Schaal) into an inference-time residual architecture layered atop a frozen deep RL policy. It departs from prior residual learning (Johannink et al., Silver et al.) by being fully online and performance-gated rather than offline-trained, and from classical adaptive control (Hovakimyan & Cao, Slotine & Li) by requiring no explicit system model or low-dimensional uncertainty parameterization. The consolidation mechanism, which transfers stabilized slow-pathway corrections into a static linear adapter, extends the standard residual-learning paradigm with a cerebellar-cortical two-timescale lifecycle that prior inference-time adaptation work (Sun et al., Wang et al.) does not address in a closed-loop control setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical deployment gap—recovering from post-training faults without retraining, exploration, or system identification—while providing a clear do-no-harm guarantee through performance-gated default inactivity. Key limitations include simulation-only evaluation (no real-robot validation), absence of formal stability or convergence guarantees, and graceful-but-incomplete degradation under severe whole-body perturbations that exceed local corrective capacity. Open questions include whether the consolidation protocol generalizes to non-stationary or multi-fault scenarios, whether the phase-portrait estimator transfers to non-periodic tasks beyond the PandaReach adaptation, and whether the bounded-authority design can be made formally certifiable for safety-critical deployment.
