## Problem

Existing circuit discovery methods either identify global capability circuits (e.g., for IOI) or rely on surrogate models like sparse autoencoders and cross-layer transcoders for instance-level explanations, which may fail to faithfully reconstruct model computations. This paper introduces query circuit discovery: finding a sparse sub-network directly within the LLM that explains how it answers a single user query, without dependence on surrogates. Two technical obstacles are identified: the widely used Normalized Faithfulness Score (NFS) becomes unstable on general datasets, and vanilla attribution-patching methods require far too many edges to form a nontrivial circuit for a single complex query.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural language query q, an edge budget N, and p paraphrases of q (generated by GPT-4o for MCQs, by operand permutation for arithmetic, or by random dataset sampling for IOI), along with a corrupted query q' used to define the performance gap. It produces a sparse query circuit C_q as a subset of the LLM's edges (|E_q| = N) and an NDF faithfulness score in [0,1]. The circuit is evaluated by running only its edges in a forward pass and measuring the logit difference between the correct and incorrect answer tokens, normalized by the full model's performance gap between q and q'.

## Method

The core technique is Best-of-N (BoN) sampling, motivated by the observation that EAP-IG score matrices from a query's paraphrases act as perturbations of the original score matrix, and one of the resulting p+1 candidate circuits often recovers model performance even when the original query's circuit fails. BoN computes edge importance scores via EAP-IG (integrated gradients, step m=20) on the original query and p paraphrases, constructs p+1 circuits by greedy edge selection at budget N, and selects the circuit with the highest NDF. Two extensions accelerate multi-budget discovery: iBoN interpolates between two previously found circuits by augmenting the smaller with top-scoring edges from the larger, and BoN-CSM builds a tier-then-score ranking from all previously discovered circuits to form new circuits without additional forward passes. The NDF metric, derived from the MIB benchmark's CMD integrand via clipping and reversal, provides a bounded, symmetric faithfulness measure that remains stable on general datasets where NFS fluctuates outside [0,1].

## Results

On MMLU (Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct, 386,713 edges), BoN discovers a query circuit with only 5,000 edges (1.3% of all edges) that achieves an average NDF of 0.6, whereas vanilla EAP-IG (single-query baseline) misleadingly suggests 200k edges (51.7%) are needed to reach the same faithfulness level. On IOI (GPT-2 Small, 32,491 edges), directly applying capability circuit methods yields query circuits recovering less than 50% of per-query performance, while BoN substantially closes this gap. Scaling to Llama-3-8B-Instruct (1,592,881 edges), BoN finds a 5,000-edge circuit (0.3% of all edges) achieving NDF 0.4 on MMLU Astronomy, while vanilla EAP-IG suggests even a 300k-edge circuit (18%) cannot match this.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares BoN (and its variants iBoN, BoN-CSM) against two baselines derived from EAP-IG: (i) Single Query, which scores edges using only the original query, and (ii) Averaging, which scores edges as the mean over the query and its paraphrases (the standard capability-circuit approach). BoN substantially outperforms both baselines across all six evaluated datasets (IOI, arithmetic addition, arithmetic multiplication, ARC Challenge, and MMLU), requiring orders of magnitude fewer edges to reach non-trivial NDF scores. Notably, the Averaging baseline does not outperform Single Query, because averaging can downweight edges that are crucial specifically to the original query. Against ACDC (activation patching), the paper shows a runtime advantage of roughly 1800×: ACDC takes ~18,000 seconds (5 hours) per query on GPT-2 Large, while EAP-IG-based methods complete in under 10 seconds. Against random edge selection, BoN with 5k edges on MMLU achieves NDF 0.6, while random selection remains near NDF 0.1–0.2 until circuit size exceeds 50k edges.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends circuit discovery from the capability level (e.g., IOI, greater-than) to the query level, and from surrogate models (Circuit Tracing's CLTs) to the original LLM itself. It builds directly on EAP-IG (Hanna et al., 2024) for edge scoring and on the MIB benchmark's circuit-model distance (Mueller et al., 2025) to derive the NDF metric. The key conceptual departure is that prior instance-level circuit work (Circuit Tracing, Marks et al. 2025) defines circuits entirely within cross-layer transcoders, whereas query circuits are identified in-place, making SAEs optional post-hoc annotations rather than foundational infrastructure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a 1.3%-edge sub-network recovers ~60% of model behavior on MMLU provides concrete evidence that LLM responses to individual queries are governed by highly sparse information flow, advancing the field toward practical, prompt-level explanations for high-stakes deployments. NDF offers a broadly applicable, bounded faithfulness metric that addresses a real evaluation gap (NFS instability on general datasets). Key open questions include extending to multi-token generation, scaling to frontier-scale models, and building automated pipelines for labeling circuits with human-readable concepts rather than relying on manual SAE inspection.
