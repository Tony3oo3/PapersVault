## Problem

SVD-based LLM compression methods either assign homogeneous ranks to similarly sized matrices despite large differences in per-layer loss sensitivity, or rely on expensive iterative optimization to determine per-matrix ranks. The core gap is that truncation decisions are made based on local reconstruction error (matching W or WX) rather than directly characterizing how each singular component's removal changes the end-to-end calibration loss, including error propagation through subsequent layers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260004/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained LLM's transformer linear weight matrices (attention Q/K/V/O projections and MLP gate/up/down layers) along with a small calibration set of 256 WikiText-2 sequences of length 2048. It produces per-layer low-rank factor pairs (W_u' ∈ R^{m×k}, W_v' ∈ R^{k×n}) with heterogeneous ranks k determined globally by the zero-sum selection rule, stored in a packed format compatible with Dobi-SVD remapping or a half-prune-plus-quantize (HQ) scheme for aggressive compression ratios ≥50%.

## Method

ZS-SVD computes the SVD of each whitened weight matrix A = WS (where S is the Cholesky factor of the activation covariance XX^T + λI), then scores every singular value σ_i by its first-order directional derivative of the calibration loss: ΔL_i ≈ −σ_i · u_i^T H v_i, where H = ∇_W L · S^{−T} is the whitened gradient. A greedy global selection rule maintains two min-heaps (positive and negative ΔL candidates) and iteratively removes the next-smallest-σ component from whichever heap counteracts the current running loss drift, keeping the cumulative predicted loss change near zero and automatically inducing heterogeneous ranks without solving a rank-allocation optimization. An optional truncate-correct-re-truncate step applies a single projected-gradient update (projecting the truncation residual onto the gradient direction, which is low-rank) to recover calibration loss, then re-truncates back to the target rank with negligible projection error.

## Results

On LLaMA-7B at 20% pruning (ratio 0.8), ZS-SVD with remapping achieves WikiText-2 PPL of 5.90 and average zero-shot accuracy of 0.54 (only 1.8% drop from the uncompressed baseline of 0.55), compared to Dobi-SVD* (PPL 6.08, 7.3% drop), SVD-LLM (PPL 7.94, 20.0% drop), and ASVD (PPL 11.14, 21.8% drop). At 60% pruning (ratio 0.4), ZS-SVD with HQ achieves PPL 6.73 and 7.3% accuracy drop, while SVD-LLM degrades to PPL 53.74 (43.6% drop) and Dobi-SVD* to PPL 9.95 (27.3% drop). Across OPT-6.7B, Vicuna-7B, and LLaMA-30B at 20% pruning, ZS-SVD consistently achieves the best PPL-accuracy trade-off, e.g., 11.40 PPL / 0.51 acc on OPT-6.7B vs. 16.04 / 0.41 for SVD-LLM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ZS-SVD consistently outperforms all SVD-based baselines (ASVD, SVD-LLM, Dobi-SVD) across every compression ratio tested on LLaMA-7B, with the largest margins at aggressive compression: at 60% pruning, ZS-SVD† achieves 7.3% accuracy drop versus 27.3% for Dobi-SVD* and 43.6% for SVD-LLM. Against the gradient-informed Dip-SVD at 30% pruning, ZS-SVD improves WikiText-2 PPL from 9.4 to 8.2 on LLaMA-7B and from 12.1 to 10.2 on Vicuna-7B. On structured pruning comparisons (LLaMA-2-7B and LLaMA-13B), ZS-SVD* matches or exceeds LLM-Pruner, SliceGPT, Bonsai, Wanda-SP, and FLAP on average commonsense accuracy at both 40% and 60% pruning. Against SVD-LLMv2 at 20% pruning, ZS-SVD improves WikiText-2 PPL from 7.12 to 6.74 on LLaMA-7B and from 13.46 to 11.40 on OPT-6.7B. In inference benchmarks, ZS-SVD delivers the highest throughput among compressed methods (731 tok/s at 40% on RTX A5000, a 5.63× speedup over the uncompressed baseline) while matching or slightly beating SVD-LLM and Dobi-SVD on peak memory.

## Relation to Prior Work

ZS-SVD builds directly on the activation-whitening framework of SVD-LLM (Cholesky-based whitening of input activations followed by truncated SVD in the whitened space) and adopts Dobi-SVD's packed-storage remapping for footprint accounting. Its key departure is replacing per-layer or homogeneous rank allocation with a global, loss-aware selection rule: each singular value is scored by its first-order directional derivative of the calibration loss in whitened coordinates, and a greedy zero-sum policy alternates positive- and negative-loss-change components to keep cumulative predicted drift near zero. The optional correction step draws on the low-rank gradient literature (PowerSGD, LoRA, GaLore) by projecting the truncation residual onto the gradient direction, exploiting the empirically small effective rank of gradients near pretrained solutions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ZS-SVD offers a practical, hardware-agnostic post-training compression pipeline that is dramatically faster than optimization-heavy alternatives (15.9 min vs. 19.25 hr for Dobi-SVD on LLaMA-7B) while achieving consistently better perplexity and zero-shot accuracy across LLaMA, OPT, and Vicuna at multiple scales. The zero-sum selection rule elegantly avoids solving an explicit rank-allocation optimization while naturally producing heterogeneous per-layer ranks, and the single-step correction adds negligible compute yet yields meaningful recovery under aggressive compression. Open questions include whether richer curvature approximations (e.g., Kronecker-factored Hessian) could further improve the first-order importance scores, how to jointly optimize the truncation-quantization budget (remapping vs. HQ), and whether the approach extends to non-transformer architectures or multimodal models.
