## Problem

Detecting whether a third-party student model has been fine-tuned on a teacher LLM's outputs requires embedding a detectable fingerprint in the teacher's generations, but existing red-and-green-list watermarking schemes apply uniform heuristic logit biases that ignore how the student actually updates its parameters. This creates a steep trade-off: strong enough perturbation to be internalized by the student severely degrades generation quality, while mild perturbation leaves the fingerprint too weak to detect. The paper asks whether the fingerprinting objective can be aligned with the student's learning dynamics to break this trade-off.

## Input / Output

At each autoregressive step, the sampling algorithm consumes the teacher's raw logits, the proxy model's predicted distribution over the vocabulary, the current green list (computed via a hash of the last w tokens and a secret key), and a penalty multiplier λ, then outputs a single sampled token. For detection, the algorithm takes a student model (open-weight logits or closed-weight sampled tokens), the same secret key, and an evaluation dataset of contexts with distinct last-w-token windows, and outputs a single conservative p-value via Hoeffding's inequality on the average green-list token probability (GTP). The evaluation dataset must be related to but distinct from the student's fine-tuning data in the unsupervised setting.

## Method

ADFP replaces the uniform red-and-green-list logit boost with a gradient-derived perturbation Δ^ADS_t = q_t · (𝕀[t∈S] − L), where q_t is the proxy model's probability for token t, S is the green list, and L = Σ_{t∈S} q_t is the current green-list probability mass. This is derived by computing the gradient of the per-step green-list probability loss in logit space and applying an isotropic approximation K ≈ cI to the Gram matrix of logit gradients, which is exact when the proxy has only a trainable final linear layer. The key distinction from the baseline is that Δ^ADS selectively amplifies high-likelihood green tokens and suppresses high-likelihood red tokens (weighted by q_t), rather than uniformly shifting all green tokens, so the perturbation targets tokens the student is most likely to internalize during fine-tuning. Detection then tests whether the student's average GTP significantly exceeds the null mean γ using a Hoeffding-based p-value, requiring no knowledge of the student's architecture or weights beyond a proxy model.

## Results

On GSM8K, at a fixed teacher accuracy of ~52%, ADFP (λ=256) achieves a mean log p-value of −4.01 (open-weight) and −3.48 (closed-weight) under LoRA fine-tuning, compared to −1.13 and −1.74 for red-and-green-list fingerprinting (δ=7, 47% accuracy), representing a roughly 3–4× stronger fingerprint signal. In the most practical setting (unsupervised, closed-weight student ≠ proxy), ADFP reaches a TPR of 55% at 0% FPR, while red-and-green-list achieves only 24% TPR at the same FPR. Across all three domains (GSM8K, OASST1, MBPP) and both proxy=student and proxy≠student configurations, ADFP achieves a consistent Pareto improvement over the red-and-green-list baseline in the p-value vs. generation-quality trade-off.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the red-and-green-list (RGL) fingerprinting scheme of Kirchenbauer et al. (2023) / Sander et al. (2024), which applies a uniform logit boost δ to all green-list tokens at each step. ADFP wins across every experimental configuration tested: on GSM8K, OASST1, and MBPP, under both unsupervised and supervised evaluation, with both open-weight and closed-weight students, and whether the proxy model matches the student or not. The margin is largest under unsupervised evaluation with a mismatched proxy (the most realistic threat model), where ADFP achieves a TPR of 55% at 0% FPR versus 24% for RGL. Under supervised evaluation with proxy ≠ student, the gap narrows and the two methods become comparable, suggesting ADFP's gradient-based targeting is most beneficial when the evaluation distribution differs from the fine-tuning data. ADFP also produces qualitatively more coherent and less repetitive text at matched fingerprinting strength, as shown in the qualitative examples in Section B.4.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the antidistillation sampling (ADS) framework of Savani et al. (2025), originally designed to degrade distilled student performance, into a fingerprinting mechanism that embeds detectable identification signals. It builds on the red-and-green-list watermarking scheme of Kirchenbauer et al. (2023) and the 'radioactive model' transfer results of Sander et al. (2024, 2025), but departs from their heuristic uniform logit boost by deriving a gradient-based perturbation (Δ^ADS) that directly optimizes the expected green-list token probability on a proxy model. The key conceptual shift is aligning the fingerprinting objective with the student's optimization trajectory rather than relying on incidental absorption of untargeted biases.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ADFP provides model owners with a statistically grounded (Hoeffding-based p-value) tool for detecting unauthorized distillation, addressing a real and growing IP-protection need in the frontier LLM ecosystem. The Pareto improvement (~10× lower p-value at matched quality) and the qualitative finding of more coherent outputs make the technique practically deployable without sacrificing user-facing quality. Open questions include robustness to adversarial fingerprint removal, behavior under multi-round or iterative distillation, and whether the isotropic approximation degrades for very large or architecturally dissimilar proxy-student pairs.
