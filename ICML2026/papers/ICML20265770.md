## Problem

Existing SLAM systems and perception pipelines operate in isolation: geometry-based SLAMs lack semantic expressiveness, neural implicit representations fail to scale to large scenes, and descriptor-based methods optimize for localization rather than reusable perception. Meanwhile, temporal aggregation strategies for multi-frame perception suffer from unbounded memory growth and weak spatial alignment. The paper addresses how to construct a single persistent spatial representation that simultaneously supports accurate localization and directly enables downstream perception without redundant feature extraction.

## Input / Output

The system consumes sequential RGB images paired with LiDAR point clouds (outdoor, SemanticKITTI) or RGB-D frames (indoor, ScanNet), processing them online at approximately 5.3 fps on a single RTX 3090 with ≤1.4 GB GPU memory. Beyond pose estimates, the primary output is a persistent neural-descriptor map storing 256 learned 3D feature vectors per keyframe (keyframes comprising ~11.9% of frames at ~6 m spacing), which downstream perception heads query directly via spatial ball-search without re-encoding raw sensor data. Detection and segmentation heads consume these Spatial Aggregated Descriptors as spatial queries or bottleneck injections, producing 3D bounding boxes and per-point class labels respectively.

## Method

UniMapping extracts neural-descriptors via a Spatial-Aware Deformable Transformer (SADT) that injects an explicit inverse-depth scaling factor (α = 1/z) into deformable attention offsets, ensuring a constant physical receptive field across varying depths and achieving scale-invariant feature sampling by design. A Spatial Fusion module then aggregates multi-view descriptors from the persistent map using multi-scale ball search with relative-offset encoding and max-pooling, replacing temporal memory banks with a finite, spatially-indexed representation that naturally handles looped trajectories. Map reconstruction follows a pose-graph framework with a learned registration network (cross-attention pairing + offset prediction solved via weighted orthogonal Procrustes with Geman-McClure robust kernel), loop-closure detection, and optional distance-based keyframe pruning. Training uses InfoNCE contrastive loss for descriptor discrimination, Mahalanobis offset loss for registration, and a sequential spatial clip strategy with gradient accumulation to maintain i.i.d. assumptions while learning spatially consistent representations.

## Results

On the KITTI odometry benchmark (outdoor, LiDAR+Camera), UniMapping achieves competitive translation errors of 0.38–0.91 m/100m and rotation errors of 0.20–0.42°/100m across sequences 06–10, matching or exceeding the best descriptor-based method DeepPointMap2 on 4 of 5 sequences. On ScanNet (indoor, RGB-D), UniMapping attains ATE of 3.7–13.7 cm, outperforming all baselines on sequences 106 and 181. For downstream perception, Spatial Fusion yields +3.4% mAP25 (60.8 vs. 57.4) over V-DETR on ScanNet detection and +9.6% mIoU (60.3 vs. 50.7) over SPVCNN on SemanticKITTI segmentation, demonstrating the value of the persistent spatial map over single-frame or temporal-buffer approaches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On KITTI outdoor SLAM, UniMapping is competitive with the strongest geometric baselines DVLO/DVLO4D, winning on sequences 07, 08, and 09 in translation error (e.g., 0.73 vs. 0.95 on seq 08) but trailing on seq 06 (0.44 vs. 0.32) and seq 10 (0.91 vs. 0.76). Among descriptor-based methods, UniMapping matches or slightly exceeds DeepPointMap2 on 4 of 5 sequences (e.g., 0.38 vs. 0.39 on seq 07) and substantially outperforms LO-Net, DELO, and TransLO. On ScanNet indoor SLAM, UniMapping outperforms all 14 baselines on sequences 106 (5.7 cm vs. best 4.9 cm for voxel s=10cm, or 8.3 cm for Loopy-SLAM) and 181 (3.7 cm vs. 8.5 cm for LoopSplat), but trails Loopy-SLAM on seq 000 (13.7 vs. 4.2 cm). For 3D detection on ScanNet, UniMapping with Spatial Fusion achieves 60.8 mAP25, surpassing V-DETR (57.4) by +3.4 points and the temporal-fusion variant (59.2) by +1.6 points. For semantic segmentation on SemanticKITTI, Spatial Fusion lifts mIoU from 53.7 (no fusion) to 60.3, a +9.6 point gain over the SPVCNN baseline (50.7), with particularly large improvements on bicyclist (+34.8) and person (+23.5) classes.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniMapping extends the neural-descriptor SLAM lineage established by DeepPointMap and DeepPointMap2 (Zhang et al., 2024a,b), which demonstrated learned descriptors for LiDAR localization, by repurposing those descriptors as a reusable perceptual foundation for downstream tasks. The Spatial-Aware Deformable Transformer (SADT) departs from standard deformable attention (Zhu et al., 2020) by injecting an explicit inverse-depth scaling factor derived from pinhole geometry, ensuring scale-invariant sampling that prior deformable-attention-based SLAM methods lacked. The Spatial Fusion module conceptually replaces the temporal memory banks used in multi-frame 3D detection (e.g., MSF, BEVFormer) with a spatially-indexed aggregation over a persistent map, arguing that scene extent is finite while observation sequences are unbounded. For perception heads, the paper integrates V-DETR and SPVCNN with minimal modification, bypassing their original backbones in favor of the map's accumulated descriptors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The map-centric perception paradigm offers a principled alternative to temporal fusion: because spatial extent is bounded, memory and computation scale with scene size rather than sequence length, enabling long-term context without the memory growth of RNN-based or sliding-window approaches. The demonstrated robustness to pose noise (only 1.2% mAP drop at 15 cm perturbation) suggests practical deployability in real-world systems with imperfect odometry. Key limitations remain: the framework requires separate trained weights for indoor and outdoor scenes due to data heterogeneity, and the current scan-wise descriptor replacement may discard useful historical context that a descriptor-wise fusion strategy could preserve. A unified model generalizing across diverse scene types without retraining would be a significant next step.
