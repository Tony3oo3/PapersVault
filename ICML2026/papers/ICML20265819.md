## Problem

Discrete text diffusion models lack effective inference-time guidance for controlling diversity, because existing methods (temperature scaling, D-CFG) operate exclusively in logit space and ignore cross-token semantic correlations. This forces a rigid fidelity-diversity trade-off where diversity is achieved only through indiscriminate probability flattening rather than structured semantic exploration, limiting performance on tasks requiring diverse reasoning paths.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained discrete diffusion language model (e.g., LLaDA-8B), a text prompt, and the model's vocabulary embedding matrix E ∈ R^{|V|×d} as inputs, and produces a generated text sequence of length L. At each of T diffusion steps, the model's intermediate token embeddings H ∈ R^{d×L} are used to compute the guidance signal, which is added to the base logits before sampling. The method is entirely training-free and introduces only ~7% inference overhead relative to unguided decoding, with linear complexity in sequence length.

## Method

SAKE computes the order-2 Rényi entropy of a normalized Hadamard product of a semantic Gaussian RBF kernel (over token embeddings) and a positional attention kernel, treating the result as a quantum density matrix. To make this tractable, the paper linearizes the diversity objective in embedding space via a first-order Taylor expansion, reducing the guidance signal to a single backward pass: the gradient of the Rényi entropy with respect to each token embedding, projected onto the vocabulary through the embedding matrix. This yields a weighted sum of repulsive forces where each contextual token pushes the current token away with strength proportional to the squared product of semantic and attention kernels. The resulting signal acts as an adaptive distribution modulator: it flattens the sampling distribution (increasing entropy) when the base prediction is semantically redundant, and sharpens it (decreasing entropy) when intrinsic diversity is already high, enabling bi-directional entropy control without retraining.

## Results

On LLaDA-8B, SAKE achieves a substantial improvement in multi-sample code generation: HumanEval Pass@32 rises from 41.1 (base) to 55.8, and MBPP Pass@32 from 48.2 to 56.1, while Pass@1 remains competitive (32.0 vs. 32.9). On GSM8K 5-shot self-consistency, SAKE reaches 75.1 (strict) and 73.5 (flexible), surpassing both the base model (71.5/68.8) and D-CFG (73.2/69.7). In terms of inference efficiency, SAKE retains ~93% of the unguided baseline throughput (44.09 vs. 47.41 tokens/sec), whereas D-CFG drops to 31.83 tokens/sec (−33%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is D-CFG (Schiff et al., 2024), a discrete classifier-free guidance method that relies on a token-independence assumption. SAKE decisively outperforms D-CFG on all multi-sample and reasoning metrics: HumanEval Pass@32 (55.8 vs. 41.2, +14.6), MBPP Pass@32 (56.1 vs. 45.9, +10.2), GSM8K strict self-consistency (75.1 vs. 73.2), and GSM8K flexible self-consistency (73.5 vs. 69.7). On single-sample metrics, SAKE is slightly below D-CFG on HumanEval Pass@1 (32.0 vs. 33.7) but ahead on MBPP Pass@1 (39.0 vs. 37.3), indicating that the diversity guidance trades a small amount of single-sample confidence for large gains in candidate-set diversity. In inference speed, SAKE averages 44.09 tokens/sec versus D-CFG's 31.83 tokens/sec, a ~39% throughput advantage, with the gap widening at longer contexts (21.51 vs. 11.98 tokens/sec at 1024-token prompts). Overall, SAKE dominates D-CFG on every headline metric while also being substantially faster at inference time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the discrete diffusion language model (DLM) line of work (LLaDA, MDLM, BD3LM) by introducing a training-free diversity guidance mechanism that departs from the token-independence assumption underlying D-CFG (Schiff et al., 2024). It draws on continuous diffusion guidance (score-based, CFG) and kernel/Rényi entropy methods from the diversity-evaluation literature, but adapts them to the discrete categorical setting via an embedding-space linearization that avoids the intractable V^L partition function. The unified entropy-dynamics framework (Proposition 4.1) positions temperature scaling, D-CFG, and the proposed SAKE as special cases of a single tilted-distribution family, clarifying why prior methods are structurally limited for diversity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SAKE is the first training-free, semantically-aware diversity guidance for discrete text diffusion, and its ~7% inference overhead (vs. ~33% for D-CFG) makes it practical if DLMs gain broader adoption. The finding that diversity-guided chain-of-thought substantially improves deterministic reasoning (HumanEval Pass@32: 41.1→55.8) reframes diversity from a creative-writing concern to a robustness mechanism for code and math. Open questions include a tighter theoretical characterization of how the guidance scalar γ shapes the full output distribution, whether the first-order Taylor linearization degrades under large γ or in low-dimensional embedding regimes, and whether the approach transfers to other discrete diffusion architectures beyond LLaDA-style masked diffusion.
