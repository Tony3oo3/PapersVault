## Problem

Achieving discrete roto-reflection equivariance in vision transformers has been complicated by the need for relative position encoding (RPE) in self-attention to preserve spatial symmetry, adding significant computational overhead and architectural complexity. Most prior work on roto-reflection equivariant models for 2D image classification has focused on CNNs rather than transformers, leaving a gap in equivariant transformer design that is both simple to implement and competitive in performance.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 2D images of varying sizes (28×28 grayscale for Rotated MNIST, 96×96 RGB for PatchCamelyon, 32×32 RGB for CIFAR-10, and 224×224 RGB for ImageNet-1K) and outputs a class label. Unusually, the 2D input is first lifted via rotational convolutions into a 3D position-orientation feature space (ℤ² × C_N) before any attention is applied, so the network internally operates on N rotated copies of each spatial location rather than on flat pixel patches.

## Method

REViT replaces standard ViT self-attention with Group Convolutional Self-Attention (G-CSA) and removes all position encoding by adopting convolutional patch embedding and convolutional Q/K/V projections (inspired by CvT). A lifting layer applies rotational convolutions to map the 2D input into a lifted feature map over discrete positions and N group orientations, and G-CSA then computes attention over both spatial and group dimensions using 3D convolutions (two spatial + one group-element axis) for the query, key, and value projections. The paper proves G-CSA is exactly equivariant under the E(2,N) roto-reflection group, and the resulting architecture requires no relative position encoding, yielding roughly half the multiply-add operations and peak memory of the G-SA-with-RPE baseline at comparable accuracy. On ImageNet-1K, the design is extended with a hierarchical lifting stem and windowed G-CSA to handle 224×224 inputs efficiently.

## Results

On Rotated MNIST, REViT with p8-CSA achieves 98.92% accuracy, outperforming the p8-CNN baseline (98.5%) and G-SA with RPE (97.90%). On PatchCamelyon, p4m-CSA reaches 90.75% accuracy versus 88.36% for p4m-CNN and 84.76% for p4m-SA, while using fewer parameters (94.35K vs 124.21K). On ImageNet-1K, REViT achieves 79.27% Top-1 accuracy, surpassing both ViT-S with augmentations (72.08%) and the equivariant RE-ResNet (77.37%). REViT also reduces computational cost by roughly 2× in multiply-add operations and peak memory compared to G-SA with RPE (e.g., 116.37M vs 232.49M Mul-Add for p4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against G-SA with RPE (Romero & Cordonnier, 2021), REViT matches or exceeds accuracy on all tested configurations (e.g., p8-CSA 98.03% vs p8-SA 97.90% on Rotated MNIST; p4m-CSA 90.75% vs p4m-SA 84.76% on PatchCamelyon) while using fewer parameters (44.28K vs 44.67K) and roughly half the multiply-add operations and peak memory. Against G-CNNs (Cohen & Welling, 2016), REViT consistently wins: p8-CSA beats p8-CNN by 0.42% on Rotated MNIST, p4m-CSA beats p4m-CNN by 0.21% on CIFAR-10, and p4m-CSA beats p4m-CNN by 2.39% on PatchCamelyon. Against the attentive G-CNN variants (Romero et al., 2020), REViT's p4m-CSA outperforms αF-p4m-CNN by 1.63% on PatchCamelyon (90.75% vs 89.12%). Against vanilla ViT (Dosovitskiy et al., 2021), REViT achieves substantially higher accuracy (98.03% vs 81.25% without augmentation, 98.03% vs 91.67% with augmentation on Rotated MNIST) and 79.27% vs 72.08% Top-1 on ImageNet-1K. Against CvT (Wu et al., 2021a), REViT with Z2-CSA improves from 91.46% to 95.97% on Rotated MNIST. Against RE-ResNet (Veeling et al., 2018), REViT achieves 79.27% Top-1 vs 77.37% on ImageNet-1K with 18M vs 11M parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the group equivariant CNN framework of Cohen & Welling (2016) and the rotational convolution/lifting layer concepts from Bekkers et al. (2018), but departs from the dominant transformer-based approach of Romero & Cordonnier (2021), which required relative position encoding (RPE) to achieve group equivariance in self-attention. Instead, REViT adopts the convolutional self-attention mechanism from CvT (Wu et al., 2021a), which inherently preserves spatial structure without explicit position encoding, thereby eliminating the RPE complexity that made prior equivariant transformers difficult to implement. The lifting layer bridges the CNN equivariance literature with the transformer architecture, and the group convolutional self-attention (G-CSA) is the novel architectural contribution that unifies these ideas.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

REViT demonstrates that equivariant vision transformers can be substantially simpler (no RPE) and more computationally efficient (~50% fewer operations, ~75% less peak memory for p4/p8) while matching or exceeding prior equivariant approaches, and it scales to ImageNet-1K where no prior equivariant transformer had been demonstrated. The key limitation is that computational and memory cost still scales linearly with group cardinality, making high-order groups (p12, p16) impractical for large-scale deployment. Open questions include whether REViT can serve as a pretrained equivariant backbone for oriented object detection and segmentation, and whether the interpolation-induced equivariance errors for non-grid-aligned rotations (p8, p12) can be mitigated with sub-pixel or continuous rotation formulations.
