## Problem

Video object-centric learning relies on learned temporal dynamics modules (transitioners) to maintain object identity across frames, assuming that identity preservation requires explicit simulation of physical dynamics. This paper demonstrates that these high-capacity predictors are computationally expensive approximations of a discrete correspondence problem, and that modern self-supervised vision backbones already encode sufficient instance-discriminative features to eliminate the need for learned temporal prediction entirely.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB video frames (336×336 for MOVi-D/E, 518×518 for YouTube-VIS) processed through a frozen DINOv2 ViT-B backbone (768-dim patch features, patch size 14), with no gradient flowing through the encoder. It produces per-object segmentation masks via Slot Attention (15 slots for synthetic benchmarks, 7 for YouTube-VIS) with temporally consistent slot identities maintained by a parameter-free Hungarian matcher. The entire temporal modeling pipeline requires zero learnable parameters, in contrast to the Transformer or recurrent transitioners used by prior methods.

## Method

Grounded Correspondence replaces learned temporal transitioners with two parameter-free components. First, Grounded Saliency Initialization seeds slot queries at local maxima of a saliency field computed from DINOv2 patch features: local instance consistency (mean cosine similarity over a spatial neighborhood) minus a global background penalty, with iterative mode selection and feature suppression to ensure distinct object centroids. Second, Hungarian Correspondence maintains frame-to-frame identity by solving a Linear Sum Assignment Problem on cosine distances between slot representations, recovering the permutation that aligns current-frame slots to previous-frame indices. The key insight is that Slot Attention's permutation equivariance means temporal consistency reduces to index alignment rather than state prediction, and empirical ablations confirm that learned predictors in existing methods approximate identity mappings with negligible performance benefit over simple slot propagation.

## Results

On synthetic benchmarks, Grounded Correspondence substantially outperforms SlotContrast: +15.7 ARI on MOVi-D (73.7 vs. 58.0) and +6.8 ARI on MOVi-E (75.7 vs. 68.9), while achieving slightly lower mBO (28.4 vs. 30.0 on MOVi-D; 23.4 vs. 26.6 on MOVi-E). On the real-world YouTube-VIS 2021 benchmark, the method is competitive but slightly behind: 30.1 ARI vs. 32.1, 33.1 FG-ARI vs. 36.3, and 29.3 mBO vs. 29.9. All results are reported as mean ± std over 3 seeds, and Grounded Correspondence requires zero learnable parameters for temporal modeling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against a single external baseline, SlotContrast (Manasyan et al., 2025), the current state-of-the-art for temporally consistent object-centric learning. On synthetic benchmarks, Grounded Correspondence decisively wins: +15.7 ARI on MOVi-D and +6.8 ARI on MOVi-E, attributed to more compact object masks that avoid the over-segmentation tendency of SlotContrast. However, this comes at a cost in mask overlap quality (mBO drops by 1.6 on MOVi-D and 3.2 on MOVi-E), suggesting slightly less precise boundary localization. On YouTube-VIS 2021, the comparison is closer: Grounded Correspondence trails by 2.0 ARI and 3.2 FG-ARI but is within 0.6 mBO, indicating competitive real-world performance despite using no learned temporal parameters. The paper also shows internally that removing SlotContrast's learned temporal predictor (replacing it with identity propagation Q_t = S_{t-1}) causes negligible degradation, supporting the claim that the predictor is architecturally redundant.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repositions video object-centric learning by challenging the dominant predictive paradigm, where learned temporal transitioners (GRUs, Transformers) forecast future slot states to maintain identity. It builds directly on Slot Attention (Locatello et al., 2020) as the grouping mechanism and on SlotContrast (Manasyan et al., 2025) as the immediate architectural baseline, while drawing on the finding that DINOv2 features encode emergent object-binding signals (Li et al., 2025c) to motivate saliency-grounded slot initialization. The key conceptual departure is reframing temporal consistency as a discrete correspondence problem solvable by parameter-free Hungarian matching rather than a prediction problem requiring high-capacity dynamics modules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that identity propagation (Q_t = S_{t-1}) matches learned predictors across three backbone families challenges a core architectural assumption in video OCL and suggests substantial computational savings are possible. However, the framework has notable limitations: it cannot recover identities after occlusion, requires a fixed slot count, and the Hungarian solver's O(K³) complexity becomes problematic at scale. On real-world YouTube-VIS, the method achieves competitive but not superior performance, with the large gains (+15.7 ARI) confined to synthetic Kubric benchmarks. Open questions include whether correspondence-based tracking can be extended to handle occlusions and variable object counts, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the SlotContrast architecture to other video OCL formulations.
