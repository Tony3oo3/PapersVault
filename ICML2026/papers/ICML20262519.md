## Problem

RL-trained LLMs suffer from length inflation, producing unnecessarily verbose or over-extended reasoning trajectories that inflate inference costs without proportional quality gains. Existing mitigations rely on additive length penalties that create compensatory optimization shortcuts (the model can trade task accuracy for brevity) or heuristic gating that is restricted to binary rewards, leaving a gap for a general, lossless length-control mechanism applicable to both RLVR and continuous-reward RLHF settings.

## Input / Output

The method operates within a standard GRPO training loop: for each text prompt, a group of G autoregressive responses is sampled from the current policy, each receiving a scalar task reward R(x,y) (binary correctness for RLVR, or a sigmoid-shaped reward-model score for RLHF). The rescaled reward R̂ = R · S is computed per response using its token length ℓ relative to the group mean ℓ̄, and the resulting group-normalized advantages drive the clipped PPO-style policy update. The trained policy outputs text responses that are substantially shorter (e.g., 40%+ token reduction on AIME-25) while matching or exceeding the accuracy of standard GRPO.

## Method

GR3 replaces additive length penalties with a multiplicative rescaling factor S = 1/(1 + α·ℓ/ℓ̄), which acts as a continuous, reward-dependent gate: the length signal's influence on the advantage is proportional to R itself (∂R̂/∂S = R), so low-reward trajectories receive negligible length pressure while high-reward ones are compressed. The group-relative normalization against the on-policy mean ℓ̄ (rather than a fixed threshold) dynamically adapts the length budget to each prompt's difficulty and the model's current generation distribution. A third component, advantage-aware calibration, selects the penalty coefficient α via a short calibration phase that maximizes α subject to a Constraint Satisfaction Rate (CSR ≥ 99.9%) ensuring a representative high-quality trajectory (R_max at average length) retains non-negative group-normalized advantage. Theoretical analysis (Propositions 3.1–3.2) shows that under group normalization, additive shaping injects the length deviation with a fixed weight λ independent of task signal, while multiplicative shaping decomposes the advantage so the length term is scaled by R, eliminating the compensatory shortcut and generalizing binary gating to continuous rewards.

## Results

On 7B mathematical reasoning (Table 3), GR³ achieves 60.1 Avg@32 on AIME24 and 46.9 on AIME25, surpassing standard GRPO (57.1 and 44.7) while reducing average token count by 28–40% (e.g., 7,923 vs. 11,079 on AIME24; 8,582 vs. 12,540 on AIME25). On MATH500, GR³ reaches 94.0 Avg@4 with only 1,764 tokens, compared to GRPO's 92.1 at 5,006 tokens. In the RLHF setting (Qwen3-8B), GR³ improves Arena-Hard-Auto from 77.2 to 92.8 while keeping response length nearly unchanged (1,178 vs. 1,171 initial tokens), whereas GRPO inflates length to 2,343 tokens for a lower score of 90.6.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GR³ outperforms standard GRPO on all four math benchmarks at 7B scale (e.g., +3.0 on AIME24, +2.2 on AIME25, +2.7 on AMC23, +1.9 on MATH500) while using 28–65% fewer tokens, demonstrating that length reduction does not come at a capability cost. Against length-oriented baselines (LC-R1, Laser, AdaptThink, DLER), GR³ achieves substantially higher accuracy: on AIME25, none of these baselines surpass the initial model's 39.4, whereas GR³ reaches 46.9; on AIME24, GR³'s 60.1 exceeds the best length-oriented method (Laser at 51.7) by 8.4 points. In the RLHF setting, GR³ matches or exceeds GRPO on Arena-Hard-Auto (92.8 vs. 90.6 on Qwen3-8B) and AlpacaEval (55.8 vs. 53.5) while keeping response length at the initial policy level (~1,178 tokens) instead of GRPO's ~2× inflation. The threshold-based truncation baseline (Hou et al.) degrades AIME24 accuracy from 41.0 to 32.5 at 4k tokens, while GR³ improves it to 44.0 at 8,760 tokens. The group-relative 'Efficiently' method (Arora & Zanette) shows difficulty over-adaptation, dropping MATH500 from 87.2 to 60.5 at λ=0.2, whereas GR³ maintains 88.7.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), exploiting its group-normalized advantage structure to construct a length-regularized variant. It departs from the dominant additive reward-shaping paradigm (DAPO, LC-R1, Efficiently, Kimi-k1.5) by replacing additive penalties with multiplicative reward rescaling, which the authors show is mathematically equivalent to heuristic gating in binary-reward RLVR but generalizes naturally to continuous-reward RLHF. The group-relative normalization (using within-group mean length rather than fixed thresholds) and the advantage-aware calibration procedure for selecting the penalty coefficient are the two additional design choices that distinguish GR3 from prior multiplicative or gated approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Length inflation is a practical bottleneck for deploying reasoning LLMs, and GR3 offers a drop-in modification to GRPO that reduces token usage by 40%+ while matching or exceeding baseline accuracy across math, code, and chat tasks. The finding that compression can *improve* rather than merely preserve performance (via higher reward signal density per token) challenges the implicit assumption in prior work that longer reasoning is at worst neutral. Open questions include whether the calibration procedure generalizes to multi-turn or tool-use settings, whether the approach interacts well with other recent GRPO modifications (e.g., dynamic clipping, entropy bonuses), and whether the gains persist at much larger model scales where the base policy is already more efficient.
