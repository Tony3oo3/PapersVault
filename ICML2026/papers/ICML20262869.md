## Problem

Few-step image generation quality fundamentally depends on the uniqueness of the noise-to-data trajectory mapping, yet existing efficient methods address this primarily through distillation algorithm improvements—a challenging, model-dependent process. The paper asks whether probability flows can be made intrinsically more unique and learnable through structured model architecture, rather than relying solely on better distillation strategies.

## Input / Output

The model takes an ImageNet class label as input and produces a 256×256 RGB image. The image is decomposed into a 2×2 grid of patches (each patch comprising 8×8 tokens in the 16×16 latent space), and generation proceeds autoregressively patch-by-patch with a progressive denoising schedule (5→4→3→2 steps), totaling 14 NFEs. The first patch establishes a full denoising trajectory that serves as a conditioning prior for all subsequent patches.

## Method

XYZFlow enhances flow matching expressivity through two orthogonal conditioning dimensions: temporal scaling conditions each denoising step on the patch's own full generation history (non-Markovian, via KV cache), while spatial scaling (Next Shortcut Prediction) conditions each patch on the complete denoising trajectories—not just final outputs—of all previously generated patches. The training objective distills a 50-step teacher's ODE trajectories into a student that performs one-step Euler updates conditioned on both the intra-patch temporal history and inter-patch spatial trajectory context, using a block-wise causal attention mask in a transformer. This multidimensional conditioning transforms the reverse process from an ambiguous one-to-many mapping into a nearly deterministic one-to-one function, straightening probability paths and enabling aggressive step reduction. An optional adversarial (GAN) loss on the final patch further enhances high-frequency details, allowing the student to potentially exceed teacher quality.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, XYZFlow achieves FID 1.63 (Base, 172M), 1.25 (Large, 608M), and 1.22 (Huge, 1.1B) with GAN fine-tuning, using a progressive 5→4→3→2 denoising schedule across 4 patches. The Base model runs in 0.018s per image (36.1× speedup over its 50-step teacher), matching the inference speed of the 676M-parameter one-step MeanFlow-XL/2+ while achieving a substantially lower FID (1.63 vs. 2.20). The Large and Huge models achieve 9.9× and 4.0× speedups respectively, with FIDs competitive with or exceeding their respective teachers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

XYZFlow-B (172M) outperforms all same-scale baselines: it beats xAR-B (FID 1.67) with a 36.1× speedup vs. 5.0×, and substantially outperforms FlowAR-S (FID 3.70) and MAR-B (FID 2.31–2.39) in both quality and speed. Against larger models, XYZFlow-B matches the inference speed of 676M MeanFlow-XL/2+ (0.018s) while achieving a 0.57 FID improvement (1.63 vs. 2.20), and beats 820M DART-FM by 2.19 FID (1.63 vs. 3.82) at 17.8× faster inference. At the Large scale, XYZFlow-L (FID 1.25) surpasses its teacher xAR-L (FID 1.28) while running 7.9× faster, and outperforms ARD w/ GAN (FID 1.84) by 0.59 FID at 3.2× lower latency. At the Huge scale, XYZFlow-H (FID 1.22) slightly beats xAR-H (FID 1.24) with an 8.5× speedup, though VAR-d30 (2.0B) achieves a lower inference time (0.039s) at the cost of higher FID (1.92). Overall, XYZFlow consistently wins on the FID–latency trade-off across all three model scales, with the largest margins against distillation-based methods (DART Distill, ARD) that rely on iterative multi-step sampling.

## Relation to Prior Work

XYZFlow sits at the intersection of flow matching (Lipman et al., 2023), autoregressive continuous-latent generation (xAR, Ren et al., 2025), and shortcut model distillation (Frans et al., 2025), but reframes the problem from improving distillation algorithms to enhancing the intrinsic expressivity of probability flows through structured multidimensional conditioning. The key conceptual departure is treating autoregressive patch generation not merely as a sequential prediction strategy but as an implicit mechanism for flow straightening, where the full denoising trajectory of preceding patches (not just their final outputs) serves as a spatial prior. This 'intensive scaling' of constraint dimensionality is positioned as complementary to the prevailing 'extensive scaling' path of better distillation objectives (consistency models, flow map matching, progressive distillation).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a principled alternative to the dominant distillation-centric paradigm for few-step generation, showing that a 172M-parameter model can match the inference speed of a 676M one-step model (MeanFlow-XL/2+) while achieving better FID (1.63 vs. 2.20), and that the architecture itself provides robustness under weaker teachers (FID 1.74 vs. 3.37 for standard 5-step distillation from DiT/XL-2). However, evaluation is limited to class-conditional ImageNet 256×256, and the theoretical appendix, while suggestive, relies on loose bounds (EPI, Cramer-Rao) that do not constitute tight guarantees. Open questions include whether the patch-based trajectory transfer generalizes to text-to-image or higher-resolution settings, and whether the GAN component (which contributes ~0.4 FID) can be replaced by other quality-enhancement strategies.
