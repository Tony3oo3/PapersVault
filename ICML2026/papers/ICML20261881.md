## Problem

Prior music-to-motion systems are optimized for offline generation with global audio context and non-causal inference, causing them to degrade in streaming rollouts where the model must act causally on incomplete evidence, commit to outputs without revision, and meet strict per-frame latency budgets. The paper identifies a structural mismatch: streaming execution changes the learning and inference problem because non-stationary audio (tempo shifts, drops, user edits) interacts with autoregressive error accumulation to produce delayed reactions, weakened beat synchronization, and accumulating artifacts over long horizons. No prior work provides a deployment-faithful end-to-end system that couples causal audio encoding, streaming generation, and online execution under matched causality and latency constraints.

## Input / Output

The system consumes a live music stream at 16 kHz processed through a 6-second causal sliding window (with zero-padding cold start) and a finite motion history buffer of 60 latent tokens, producing 272-D motion frames at 30 FPS. Each 272-D frame encodes root XZ-plane linear velocities, a 6D root angular velocity, and per-joint positions, velocities, and 6D rotations in the root frame for 22 SMPL joints, enabling direct forward-kinematics reconstruction without per-frame inverse kinematics. Outputs are delivered via ROS2 to either a Unity avatar platform for interactive visualization or a Unitree G1 humanoid robot through GMR retargeting and whole-body control at 50 Hz.

## Method

DiscoForcing couples a causal VQ-PAE music encoder that decomposes audio into discrete rhythmic tokens (via residual vector quantization over a dilated 1D convolutional latent) and continuous phase-alignment features (via FFT-based periodic reconstruction with learned amplitude, frequency, and phase parameters) with a latent diffusion-forcing transformer trained under heterogeneous per-token noise levels. A motion VAE compresses 272-D frames into a 4-D latent with 4× temporal downsampling, and the flow-matching backbone (8-layer, 1024-dim transformer) is trained with a hybrid temporal schedule that stochastically mixes random, monotonic, and trapezoid noise profiles to bridge training and streaming inference. At inference, a FIFO denoising window of fixed length l advances one token per frame, and temporal guidance combines an unconditional history-based velocity (monotonic schedule) with an audio-conditioned velocity (trapezoid schedule that re-noises distant history) at scale ω=2.5, explicitly trading off responsiveness to incoming audio against long-horizon drift suppression. The full pipeline achieves 30 FPS on an RTX 4090 with 10 denoising steps per frame.

## Results

On FineDance, DiscoForcing achieves the best motion quality with FIDk=23.84 and FIDg=8.62, substantially outperforming the next-best baselines (Lodge: FIDk=50.00, FIDg=35.52; MEGA: FIDk=50.00, FIDg=13.02). On AIST++, DiscoForcing again leads in kinetic quality (FIDk=18.87 vs. MEGA's 25.89) and geometric quality (FIDg=11.57 vs. MEGA's 12.62), while achieving the highest Beat Alignment Score (BAS=0.244). The method maintains competitive diversity (Divk=5.98, Divg=5.99 on FineDance) and achieves real-time throughput at ~26 ms/frame with 10 denoising steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiscoForcing decisively outperforms all five baselines on motion quality (FIDk and FIDg) across both FineDance and AIST++, with the largest margins over autoregressive methods FACT (FIDk 113.38→23.84 on FineDance) and Bailando (FIDk 82.81→23.84). Against the strongest diffusion baselines, DiscoForcing halves FIDk relative to Lodge (50.00→23.84) and MEGA (50.00→23.84) on FineDance, and improves FIDk by ~27% over MEGA on AIST++ (25.89→18.87). On beat alignment (BAS), DiscoForcing matches or slightly trails Lodge and MEGA by 0.001 on FineDance (0.225 vs. 0.226) but leads all baselines on AIST++ (0.244 vs. Lodge's 0.242). Lodge achieves the best foot-skating score (FSR=0.028) and diversity closest to ground truth, while DiscoForcing trades a modest FSR penalty (0.142) for substantially better overall motion realism. The key differentiator is that DiscoForcing is the only method evaluated under strict streaming/causal constraints, whereas all baselines were originally designed for offline, global-context generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Diffusion Forcing framework (Chen et al., 2024a) from static-condition sequence modeling to non-stationary audio-driven streaming control, combining per-token heterogeneous noise with a hybrid temporal schedule (random, monotonic, trapezoid) and a temporal-guidance inference strategy that interpolates between history-based and audio-conditioned predictions. It departs from prior music-to-motion systems (FACT, Bailando, EDGE, Lodge, MEGADance), which are offline and non-causal, by formulating the problem as bounded-latency streaming generation with a causal VQ-PAE music encoder and a 272-D canonicalized motion representation that avoids costly per-frame IK. The streaming inference design draws on FIFO-Diffusion and Rolling Forcing for the sliding-window denoising mechanism, while the temporal guidance mechanism is analogous to classifier-free guidance but applied along the temporal axis rather than the condition axis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first end-to-end system that closes the loop from streaming audio ingestion through diffusion-forcing motion generation to both virtual avatar playback and physical humanoid execution (Unitree G1 via ROS2), providing a deployment-faithful testbed that prior offline music-to-motion work lacks. The temporal guidance mechanism offers a practical, tunable knob for the responsiveness–stability trade-off under non-stationary audio, a problem that is underexplored in the streaming diffusion literature. Open questions include quantitative streaming-specific evaluation (the paper relies largely on qualitative demos for long-horizon rollouts), robustness under more extreme distribution shifts, extension beyond dance to general locomotion, and tighter physical feasibility guarantees for the robot deployment pipeline.
