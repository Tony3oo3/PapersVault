## Problem

Existing 3D scene generation methods are tailored for household indoor environments, optimizing visual plausibility and geometric consistency while ignoring the protocol grounding and layout-level chemical-safety constraints that are fundamental to laboratory settings. This visual-functional gap means generated scenes cannot support safe multi-step experimental workflows or autonomous robotic navigation in cluttered lab environments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260412/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a free-form natural-language experimental description and compiles it into a structured protocol grounded in a curated asset knowledge base of 176 laboratory entities (each annotated with geometry, bilingual semantics, and boolean hazard attributes). The output is a 3D laboratory layout exported as a structured JSON scene directly importable by Isaac Sim (Z-up coordinate convention, 6-DoF poses for room-level assets, local-frame poses for desktop items), accompanied by quantitative navigation feasibility and chemical safety scores.

## Method

LabBuilder couples three modules: LabForge uses retrieval-augmented generation over chemistry databases (CHEMTRANS, OPENEXP) plus an LLM to synthesize asset-grounded protocols with explicit spatial and safety constraints; LabGen factorizes layout synthesis into room-level zoning and desktop-level organization via hierarchical LLM sampling, then iteratively refines the layout through a violation-prioritized optimization loop that combines deterministic FastRepair (boundary/collision) with LLMAdjust (safety/workflow) under a multi-objective reward, followed by a Navigation-Aware Refinement stage that projects the scene to a 2D occupancy grid, runs A* over protocol-derived start-goal pairs, and applies rotation/translation fixes to eliminate reachability failures; LabTouchstone provides a four-axis evaluation suite (geometry, feasibility, chemistry, semantics) plus point-goal navigation benchmarking. The key design choice is treating protocol grounding and safety as generation-time constraints rather than post-hoc checks, with a two-level (room → desktop) search that reduces the combinatorial configuration space.

## Results

On 30 curated chemical experiments, LabBuilder achieves near-zero geometric violations (OB=0.07, CN=0.17) compared to Holodeck (OB=10.8, CN=0.20) and SceneWeaver (OB=5.61, CN=0.35), while attaining the highest asset availability (0.833) and navigation feasibility (0.966). Across all four chemical safety constraints, LabBuilder leads: flammable isolation (0.725), reagent storage (0.801), incompatible separation (0.716), and glassware edge avoidance (0.364). LLM-based semantic scores also favor LabBuilder substantially (Realism=8.23, Layout=9.00, Completeness=9.10 vs. best baseline 6.27/5.61/5.20). For point-goal navigation in the generated scenes, NavDP achieves 0.517 SR / 0.446 SPL after navigation-aware refinement, outperforming iPlanner (0.425 / 0.412).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LabBuilder dominates all three scene-generation baselines on every reported metric. Against Holodeck (CVPR 2024), the gap is most dramatic in geometric compliance: boundary violations drop from 10.8 to 0.07, and chemical safety improves across the board (e.g., incompatible reagent separation from 0.087 to 0.716). SceneWeaver (2025) performs worst overall, with near-zero storage safety (0.000) and only 0.226 asset availability, reflecting its geometry-oriented design that ignores protocol grounding. The Holodeck+ variant, which receives the same structured protocol information as LabBuilder, improves flammable isolation (0.239→0.723) and incompatibility separation (0.087→0.613) but worsens boundary violations (10.8→15.2) and still scores 0.000 on storage, confirming that richer prompting alone cannot jointly satisfy geometric and safety constraints. For navigation, NavDP (2025) consistently outperforms iPlanner (RSS 2023) in the generated laboratory scenes (0.517 vs. 0.425 SR after refinement), with the gap attributed to NavDP's stronger learned traversability priors versus iPlanner's brittle monocular cues in dense layouts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of LLM-based 3D scene generation (Holodeck, SceneWeaver, LayoutGPT) that targets household indoor environments and optimizes for visual plausibility and geometric consistency. It also distinguishes itself from laboratory automation work (XDL/Chemputer, AutoDSL) that digitizes chemical procedures without grounding them in spatial layouts, and from embodied-agent environment generation (ProcTHOR, Behavior-1K) that emphasizes task diversity over protocol-specific safety. LabBuilder's core conceptual move is to treat experimental protocols as first-class constraints during generation—via a hierarchical room/desktop factorization, a multi-objective geometric-and-chemical reward, and iterative navigation-aware refinement—rather than as post-hoc evaluation criteria.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As self-driving laboratories scale, the ability to automatically generate safe, protocol-consistent 3D environments is a practical bottleneck; LabBuilder provides the first end-to-end system and evaluation suite (LabTouchstone) for this setting. However, the paper explicitly acknowledges that it does not validate equipment specification-level requirements (heating ranges, pipette volumes, fumehood ratings) or physical manipulation feasibility for robotic execution, leaving a gap between layout-level safety and operational safety. Open questions include whether the constraint-optimization loop generalizes to multi-protocol laboratories, how it interacts with regulatory constraints, and whether sim-to-real transfer of generated layouts is feasible.
