## Problem

Standard expected-return RL objectives inevitably cause outcome-level mode collapse in multimodal settings where multiple terminal outcomes achieve comparable reward. The paper proves this collapse is not an artifact of insufficient exploration or weak regularization but a structural consequence of the probability multiplier inside the expectation, which creates a rich-get-richer feedback loop driving probability ratios to diverge exponentially regardless of exploration strategy or entropy bonuses.

## Input / Output

The method operates on episodic RL with terminal-only rewards, consuming a group of G sampled trajectories per update (from text prompts in LLM reasoning tasks, target protein reward functions in molecular generation, or initial grid states in controlled environments) and producing a policy whose terminal-state distribution is proportional to reward. A key practical constraint is that outcomes must be discrete and enumerable within each sampled group for empirical frequency estimation; the output is a policy that maintains stable coverage across all high-reward modes rather than collapsing onto a single dominant one.

## Method

The core contribution is Inverse Probability Scaling (IPS), which replaces each terminal reward r(o) with r(o)/p_θ(o), removing the probability multiplier that causes frequency-weighted amplification in standard expected-return optimization. Under gradient flow, this changes the log-ratio dynamics from divergent (driven by p_i·a_i − p_j·a_j) to convergent (driven by (r_i − r_j) − (p_i − p_j)·Σr_k), yielding a provable stationary solution where p_i* ∝ r_i. The method is instantiated as IPS-GRPO, a drop-in modification to GRPO that scales each trajectory's reward by the inverse of its empirical group frequency (clipped at ε for stability) before the standard group-relative policy gradient update. No auxiliary models, architectural changes, or explicit distribution-matching constraints are required; the method is fully compatible with existing PPO-style clipping and KL regularization, where KL now serves its intended role of interpolation between reward-proportional behavior and reference-policy adherence rather than fighting collapse.

## Results

On the HypoSpace benchmark, IPS-GRPO achieves recovery rates of 43.91% (Causal Inference), 90.00% (3D Reconstruction), and 60.74% (Boolean/DNA), substantially outperforming GRPO (16.02%, 31.61%, 9.50%) and FlowRL (12.82%, 26.85%, 10.74%). On the controlled hyper-grid task, IPS-GRPO reduces the ℓ₁ distance to the target distribution to 3.0×10⁻³ (n=2) and 3.7×10⁻⁴ (n=4), versus 2.8×10⁻² and 5.2×10⁻⁴ for both GRPO and FlowRL. In molecular generation, IPS-GRPO discovers 7,371 unique high-reward molecules under SYNTH (vs. 5,695 for REINVENT) and 1,478 under ALL-AMIDE (vs. 1 for REINVENT) within the same oracle budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IPS-GRPO is compared against three baselines: GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), FlowRL (Zhu et al., 2025), and REINVENT (Olivecrona et al., 2017). Against GRPO, IPS-GRPO achieves 2.7×–6.4× higher recovery rates across all three HypoSpace subtasks (e.g., 90.00% vs. 31.61% on 3D Reconstruction) and a 9.3× lower ℓ₁ distance on the n=2 hyper-grid. Against FlowRL, the advantage is similar in magnitude (3.4×–5.6× on HypoSpace), indicating that flow-matching objectives also suffer from the same outcome-frequency bias. Against REINVENT on molecular generation, IPS-GRPO yields 3.8× more unique high-reward molecules under the ALL-AMIDE reward at threshold 0.80 (1,478 vs. 1) and 3.8× fewer oracle calls to find 100 molecules under SYNTH at threshold 0.85 (1,178 vs. 2,934). IPS-GRPO wins on every reported metric across all three baselines and all evaluated domains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) as its base optimizer, proposing a one-line reward rescaling (inverse probability scaling) as a drop-in modification. It departs conceptually from two dominant prior lines: (1) exploration-based remedies (entropy regularization, KL constraints, exploration bonuses) that the authors argue only delay rather than prevent collapse, and (2) flow-based or energy-based methods (GFlowNets, FlowRL) that enforce explicit distributional constraints over terminal outcomes. The key positioning is that mode collapse is not an optimization or exploration failure but a structural property of the expected-return objective itself, making the correction a matter of objective design rather than algorithmic augmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical result that outcome-level mode collapse is inevitable under expected-return gradient flow—regardless of exploration strategy, entropy bonuses, or KL regularization—reframes a long-standing practical problem in LLM post-training and multimodal RL from a tuning challenge to an objective-design problem. The practical impact is significant because the fix is minimal (a single reward rescaling step in GRPO) and requires no auxiliary models. Key limitations include reliance on empirical probability estimates from finite groups (introducing a bias-variance trade-off), inapplicability to actor-critic methods with learned value functions, and restriction to terminal-reward settings; extending IPS to dense rewards, continuous outcome spaces, and value-based methods remains open.
