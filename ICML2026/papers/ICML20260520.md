## Problem

Fine-tuning aligned LLMs on data containing adversarial or harmful samples can strip away safety alignment, yet existing defenses either treat safe data as a soft regularizer with weak, hard-to-tune control over the safety-utility tradeoff, or select safe samples randomly without accounting for task relevance. The paper addresses the need for a principled defense that explicitly enforces safety constraints during optimization while selecting compact, task-aligned, and diverse safety data to maximize corrective signal.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained aligned LLM (e.g., Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct, LLaMA-3.2-3B-Instruct), a fine-tuning dataset that may contain 10% injected harmful samples, and a candidate safety pool (GeneralSafe) built from harmful queries paired with safe responses. It produces LoRA-adapted model parameters (rank 32, alpha 4) that maintain downstream task accuracy while satisfying a safety loss threshold τ. The safety dataset is a compact DPP-selected subset fixed at 3% of the fine-tuning data size, and the safety mini-batch during projection uses batch size 1.

## Method

SPARD combines two components. First, SPAG (Safety-Projected Alternating Gradient) formulates fine-tuning as a safety-constrained optimization problem and alternates between utility-driven gradient steps on the fine-tuning data and explicit closed-form projections onto a linearized safety half-space derived via first-order Taylor expansion and KKT conditions; a trust-region radius η_safe caps the projection magnitude to prevent overshoot. Second, a Relevance-Diversity DPP selects the safety subset by constructing a kernel K̂_ij = (q_i·q_j)^β · K(x_i, x_j) that multiplicatively weights intrinsic embedding similarity by task-relevance scores (max cosine similarity to fine-tuning data), then applies greedy MAP inference with incremental Cholesky updates in O(Nk²) time. The exponent β amplifies subtle relevance differences among highly similar candidates, and the determinant structure naturally enforces diversity, avoiding the redundancy that plagues relevance-only selection.

## Results

On GSM8K with Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct under four harmful fine-tuning attacks, SPARD achieves the lowest average Attack Success Rate (ASR) of 9.45% and Harmfulness Score (HS) of 1.32, while maintaining 85.77% GSM8K accuracy. This represents a 78.5% reduction in average ASR over standard SFT (87.93%) and a 50.6% reduction over the strongest optimization-based baseline Lisa (19.12%), which also suffers a 7.32% accuracy drop. On LLaMA-3.2-3B-Instruct, SPARD similarly achieves the lowest average ASR (12.09%) and HS (1.38) with 71.23% accuracy, and on OpenBookQA it reaches 14.54% ASR with 83.25% accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPARD outperforms all five baselines on average ASR across all four attacks. Against SFT (87.93% avg ASR) and PTST (74.64%), SPARD reduces ASR by over 65 percentage points, demonstrating that explicit safety projection is far more effective than inference-time prompting. Compared to SafeInstr (73.59% avg ASR), which randomly mixes 3% safe samples, SPARD's relevance-diversity DPP selection yields a 64.14-point ASR reduction, confirming that principled data selection is critical. Against the strongest optimization-based baseline Lisa (19.12% avg ASR, 78.45% accuracy), SPARD achieves a 9.67-point ASR reduction while simultaneously improving GSM8K accuracy by 7.32 points, showing that SPAG's closed-form projection better balances safety and utility than Lisa's proximal bi-state approach. Relative to SafeGrad (30.18% avg ASR, 85.71% accuracy), SPARD reduces ASR by 20.73 points with marginally better accuracy, validating that projecting onto a safety half-space is more effective than gradient surgery for enforcing safety constraints.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPARD departs from the dominant penalty-based paradigm for safety-constrained fine-tuning (used by Lisa, SafeInstr, and SafeGrad) by replacing soft λ-weighted regularization with a closed-form projection onto a linearized safety half-space, drawing on trust-region optimization (Schulman et al., 2015) to bound the correction step. For data selection, it extends classical Determinantal Point Processes (Kulesza et al., 2012; Chen et al., 2018) by embedding task-relevance scores directly into the DPP kernel, bridging the gap between relevance-only retrieval and diversity-only DPP sampling. The greedy MAP inference with incremental Cholesky updates follows Chen et al. (2018), while the overall safety-constrained formulation is novel in its alternating utility-update / safety-projection structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SPARD offers a hyperparameter-free, geometrically interpretable mechanism for enforcing safety during fine-tuning, removing the need to tune penalty weights that plague prior methods. The empirical finding that extreme task-relevance in safety data actually degrades defense (due to redundancy) motivates the relevance-diversity tradeoff and suggests that future safety data curation should explicitly model coverage of the risk space. Open questions include whether the first-order projection remains valid under large distribution shifts, how the method scales to multi-turn or agentic safety settings, and whether the DPP selection generalizes when the fine-tuning domain is far from any available safety corpus (where relevance scores become near-uniform).
