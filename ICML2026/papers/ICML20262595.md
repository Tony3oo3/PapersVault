## Problem

Most fairness-aware graph learning methods require sensitive attributes (e.g., race, gender) to be observed for all training nodes, which is often unrealistic due to privacy constraints or incomplete demographic data. This paper introduces the underexplored problem of graph fairness adaptation: transferring fairness knowledge from a fully labeled source graph to a completely unlabeled target graph without any target sensitive labels, while simultaneously addressing sensitive-group distribution shift and preventing alignment collapse caused by entangled task and sensitive information.

## Input / Output

The method takes a labeled source graph (node features, adjacency, binary task labels, and binary sensitive attributes) and a fully unlabeled target graph (node features and adjacency only), with a unified feature space constructed across both domains. It produces binary node classification predictions for target nodes, evaluated via ACC, ROC-AUC, demographic parity (ΔDP), and equalized odds (ΔEO). Internally, the method generates pseudo-labels for both task labels and sensitive attributes on target nodes, which are then filtered by per-class adaptive confidence thresholds before being used for supervision.

## Method

CELL builds a Structural Causal Model (C→G←S, C→Y) to formalize graph generation and uses a dual GNN encoder — a sensitive encoder and a causal encoder — to disentangle task-relevant and sensitive factors, enforced by minimizing I(Z_s; Z_c) via a CLUB upper bound and maximizing I(Z_c; D | Z_s) via a Conditional InfoNCE lower bound. For the unlabeled target graph, the method generates pseudo-labels and partitions nodes into Intra-Group, Sensitive Inter-Group, and Target Inter-Group sets, then applies group-aware contrastive losses that pull same-class/different-sensitive pairs together and push same-sensitive/different-class pairs apart. A fairness-aware bipartite graph is constructed by linking source-target node pairs sharing the same task label but different sensitive attributes, and a spectral clustering constraint (Frobenius norm of (I−L)−PPᵀ) is imposed to align cross-domain representations while decorrelating sensitive information. The overall objective combines the MI disentanglement loss, group-acquired unbiased loss, bipartite alignment loss, and a confidence-filtered pseudo-label classification loss, with theoretical guarantees bounding ΔEO in terms of the MI between sensitive and causal representations.

## Results

CELL achieves rank 1 on all four datasets (Bail-t, German-t, Pokec-n, syn-t) under the composite score c = ACC + ROC-AUC − ΔDP − ΔEO. On Bail-t, CELL attains 94.21% ACC and 97.96% ROC-AUC with ΔDP = 3.88 and ΔEO = 4.98, outperforming the strongest baseline DANCE (84.43% ACC, 95.96% ROC-AUC). On German-t and syn-t, CELL achieves the best fairness (ΔDP = 1.83/3.36, ΔEO = 2.27/0.42) at the cost of lower accuracy than DANCE (74.29%/86.87% ACC), while on Pokec-n it leads on both utility (68.93% ACC) and fairness (ΔDP = 0.37, ΔEO = 0.71).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the traditional GCN baseline, CELL substantially improves fairness (e.g., ΔDP drops from 7.37 to 3.88 on Bail-t, from 2.76 to 0.37 on Pokec-n) while matching or exceeding accuracy. IID fairness-aware GNNs (NIFTY, FairVGNN, FairSIN, SFG) degrade significantly under domain shift: FairVGNN drops to 50.51% ACC on German-t and SFG to 53.61% on Pokec-n, confirming that IID-tuned fairness methods do not transfer. General domain adaptation methods (SPA, SGDA) achieve moderate utility but poor fairness (e.g., SGDA yields ΔDP = 29.35 on syn-t) or fail entirely (SGDA OOMs on Pokec-n). Among OOD fairness-aware GNNs, DANCE achieves the highest raw accuracy on German-t (74.29%) and syn-t (86.87%) but at severe fairness cost (ΔDP = 24.76/25.34, ΔEO = 21.89/23.13), whereas FatraGNN shows intermediate performance. CELL wins the composite utility-fairness ranking on all four datasets, dominating on fairness metrics (best ΔDP and ΔEO on German-t, Pokec-n, and syn-t) and on utility on Bail-t and Pokec-n, while trading some accuracy on German-t and syn-t for dramatically lower bias.

## Relation to Prior Work

CELL sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) fair GNNs under IID assumptions (NIFTY, FairVGNN, FairSIN, SFG), which require observed sensitive attributes for all nodes; (2) graph domain adaptation (SGDA, SPA), which aligns source and target representations but ignores fairness; and (3) fairness under distribution shift (FatraGNN, DANCE), which is the closest prior work but still assumes target sensitive labels are available. The paper departs from all three by formalizing a setting where target sensitive attributes are entirely unavailable, and by grounding the disentanglement objective in a Structural Causal Model rather than purely adversarial or contrastive heuristics. Technically, it repurposes CLUB (Cheng et al., 2020) for the MI upper bound, Conditional InfoNCE (Gupta et al., 2021) for the MI lower bound, and the bipartite spectral alignment loss from DREAM (Zhang et al., 2025) for cross-domain structural supervision, combining them with a group-aware pseudo-labeling strategy adapted from DANCE (Wang et al., 2025).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: in real deployment (credit scoring, criminal justice), target-domain demographic labels are often unavailable due to privacy regulations, yet fairness guarantees are still required. By showing that fairness knowledge can be transferred via causal disentanglement and pseudo-labeling without target sensitive attributes, CELL opens a path toward deployable fair GNNs in cross-domain settings. However, several limitations temper the impact: the method is restricted to binary sensitive attributes, requires careful per-dataset hyperparameter tuning (top-K, τ, δ, β, γ all vary substantially across datasets), and the accuracy trade-offs on German and syn datasets (where CELL underperforms DANCE on ACC by 10+ points) suggest the fairness gains come at a non-trivial utility cost. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-class sensitive attributes, whether the causal graph assumptions hold under more severe distribution shifts, and whether the pseudo-labeling pipeline remains reliable when source and target domains are highly dissimilar.
