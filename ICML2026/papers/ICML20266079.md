## Problem

Existing unified medical multimodal models treat understanding and generation as disjoint objectives, training the understanding branch on generic recognition-style tasks (e.g., lesion VQA) that are only weakly related to what cross-modality image synthesis actually requires—fine-grained slice-level correspondence, explicit modality control, and mapping-direction constraints. The paper asks: what form of understanding truly benefits medical image generation, and how can the two objectives be made to synergize rather than merely coexist?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266079/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For generation, the model takes a source medical image slice plus a text prompt specifying the target modality (e.g., 'Convert a non-contrast pelvic CT slice to a CBCT-like appearance') and produces a synthesized slice in the target modality; inference is slicewise and results are stacked back into 3D volumes for volume-level metric computation. For understanding, the model consumes one or more image panels plus a multiple-choice text prompt and outputs a short answer string (a single option letter or a modality label such as 'T2' or 'CBCT'). The 22 directed synthesis tasks span CT, CBCT, PET, and multi-sequence MRI (T1, T1CE, T2, FLAIR) across brain, pelvis, and whole-body anatomies.

## Method

SynerMedGen builds on the Bagel unified architecture, which uses a Mixture-of-Transformer-experts (MoT) with separate understanding and generation experts fed by ViT and VAE encoders respectively. The key contribution is a two-stage training schedule that aligns understanding with generation: Stage I (GAU) trains the understanding branch on three tasks derived directly from the paired synthesis data—Conditional Target Selection (CTS, matching the correct paired slice among hard negatives from neighboring slices), Modality Identification (MI, recognizing modality including confusable pairs like CT vs. CBCT), and Transformation Instruction Alignment (TIA, selecting the text description that matches the observed paired change in direction and constraints)—all formulated as masked next-token prediction. Stage II (UCG) then initializes from Stage I and trains conditional synthesis via flow matching in VAE latent space, with the generation expert attending to source-image and text conditioning through cross-attention. The central design principle is that understanding tasks are co-defined with generation objectives from the same paired corpus, so the learned representations are explicitly optimized to preserve slice-level correspondence, disentangle patient content from modality, and encode mapping direction—properties directly required for faithful cross-modality synthesis.

## Results

On the SynthRAD2023/AutoPET benchmark (10 cross-modality tasks), SynerMedGen achieves SSIM scores ranging from 73.08 (Brain CT→CBCT) to 91.10 (Whole-Body CT→PET), consistently outperforming the strongest specialized baseline RCD (e.g., 91.10 vs. 88.90 on CT→PET; 87.15 vs. 85.97 on Brain CBCT→CT). On BraTS (12 MRI sequence translations), SynerMedGen reaches SSIM up to 92.45 (T2→T1CE), surpassing SynDiff by 4.3% on that task. Even with understanding training alone (Stage I, zero-shot), SynerMedGen-GAU already achieves SSIM of 61–87 across tasks, far exceeding the untrained Bagel baseline (12–47 SSIM). On standard medical VQA benchmarks, SynerMedGen also leads (e.g., 78.9 on OmniMedVQA vs. 74.4 for HealthGPT), confirming that generation-aligned understanding does not compromise general comprehension.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SynerMedGen wins across all 22 synthesis tasks against every baseline. Against the strongest specialized synthesis methods, it leads RCD by 1.2–2.2 SSIM on SynthRAD2023/AutoPET (e.g., 91.10 vs. 88.90 on CT→PET) and by 1.2–4.3 SSIM on BraTS (e.g., 92.45 vs. 88.15 on T2→T1CE); it also surpasses SynDiff and ResViT by similar margins. Against the unified medical MLLMs, the gap is far larger: HealthGPT trails by 29–35 SSIM on CBCT↔CT tasks (e.g., 87.15 vs. 57.37 on Brain CBCT→CT) and by 24.6 SSIM on Whole-Body CT→PET (91.10 vs. 66.54), while UniMedVL lags by 35–41 SSIM on the same tasks (e.g., 87.15 vs. 51.48). The untrained Bagel architecture baseline scores only 12–47 SSIM, and even after Stage I (GAU) alone, SynerMedGen-GAU jumps to 61–87 SSIM, demonstrating that generation-aligned understanding transfers strongly to synthesis without any generation-specific training. On standard medical VQA, SynerMedGen also outperforms HealthGPT (78.9 vs. 74.4 on OmniMedVQA; 69.4 vs. 64.5 on SLAKE) and Bagel (78.9 vs. 71.1 on OmniMedVQA), indicating no trade-off between generation-aligned understanding and general medical comprehension.

## Relation to Prior Work

SynerMedGen builds on the Bagel unified multimodal architecture (Deng et al., 2025) but departs from it conceptually by introducing a generation-aligned understanding principle: rather than training the understanding branch on generic medical VQA or recognition tasks (as in HealthGPT and UniMedVL), it derives understanding supervision directly from paired synthesis data. This positions the paper at the intersection of unified multimodal models (Bagel, Janus, Show-o) and specialized medical image synthesis (ResViT, SynDiff, RCD), arguing that the task misalignment between understanding and generation objectives is the key bottleneck in unified medical MLLMs. The three proposed tasks (CTS, MI, TIA) are a novel task-design contribution rather than an architectural one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that generation-aligned understanding training alone yields strong zero-shot cross-modality synthesis (without any explicit generation training) challenges the assumption that understanding and generation must be jointly optimized, and suggests a general principle for designing understanding objectives that transfer to generation. The released SynerMed dataset (1M paired samples, 2M understanding instances) provides a useful testbed for studying understanding-generation synergy. Limitations include the restriction to 2D slice-level synthesis, dependence on paired data, and evaluation limited to cross-modality translation; extending to 3D volumetric modeling and unpaired or text-to-image settings remains open.
