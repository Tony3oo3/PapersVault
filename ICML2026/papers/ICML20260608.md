## Problem

Features of the same sample extracted by different pretrained models (e.g., DINOv2 vs. CLIP) follow fundamentally distinct distributions, making direct contrastive or fusion-based multiview clustering inapplicable. Existing unsupervised transfer learning methods either fine-tune a single backbone or treat pretrained features as homogeneous, failing to exploit the complementary semantic information preserved across heterogeneous views. The paper addresses how to learn invariant, view-consistent representations from large-scale unlabeled visual data in a fully unsupervised transfer manner without requiring labeled samples or class-level textual descriptions.

## Input / Output

The method takes unlabeled RGB images and processes them through L frozen pretrained backbones (e.g., DINOv2 ViT-g/14 and CLIP ViT-L/14) to obtain fixed-dimensional feature vectors, on top of which a trainable linear projection (W ∈ R^{d×C}) is stacked per view. The batch size B constrains the self-representation neighborhood: each feature is aggregated only over the B−1 other samples in the same mini-batch via attention, so B must be a multiple of the number of clusters C. The output is a per-sample cluster pseudolabel y_i = argmax_j [ρ_θ(x_i)]_j, where ρ_θ is the element-wise average of the softmax assignment probability distributions across all L linear models.

## Method

MSRL introduces an information-passing mechanism in which each low-dimensional feature h_i is expressed as a linear combination of its batch-mates (self-representation, Eq. 3), with attention-based coefficients a_ij (Eq. 4) that adaptively weight the most linearly related neighbors within the same category. The aggregated representation z_i = Σ_j a_ij h_j + h_i (Eq. 5) is then passed through a softmax to yield a per-view assignment probability distribution s_i^(l). To enforce invariance across heterogeneous views, three losses are jointly optimized: a semantic pseudolabel loss L_s that pulls each view's distribution toward the consensus ρ_θ, a cluster diversity loss L_a that maximizes marginal entropy to prevent collapse, and a cross-view consistency loss L_c that minimizes pairwise cross-entropy between views' distributions (Eq. 11–12). Theoretically, the paper proves that the consensus distribution is a stable attractor (Theorem 3.4) and that entropy variation from adding a new view decays as O(ε/(L+1)) (Theorem 3.5), providing guarantees that the fused assignment remains bounded and stable as more heterogeneous views are incorporated.

## Results

MSRL achieves the best clustering accuracy across all eight vision datasets, reaching 95.83% ACC on Pets, 49.78% on GTSRB, 62.34% on DTD, 37.65% on Aircraft, 99.71% on Flowers, 99.57% on CIFAR-10, 91.00% on CIFAR-100, and 73.24% on ImageNet-1K. On the two largest datasets, MSRL outperforms the strongest baseline (TURTLE) by 1.90% ACC on CIFAR-100 (91.00% vs. 89.10%) and 3.14% on ImageNet-1K (73.24% vs. 70.10%). The method also leads on NMI and ARI across all datasets, with the largest margins on GTSRB (ARI 46.65 vs. 36.89 for TURTLE) and DTD (ARI 49.03 vs. 42.69).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MSRL outperforms all eight baselines on every dataset and every metric (ACC, NMI, ARI). The closest competitor is TURTLE (Gadetsky et al., 2024), an unsupervised transfer method that maximizes margins of independent linear classifiers on frozen pretrained models; MSRL beats it by 3.24% ACC on Pets, 6.77% on GTSRB, 4.79% on DTD, 1.90% on CIFAR-100, and 3.14% on ImageNet-1K. Among traditional multiview clustering methods, BONE (Xu et al., 2026) and CPP (Chu et al., 2024) are the strongest, but they trail MSRL by 3–10% ACC on most datasets and fail entirely on ImageNet-1K (CMVC, HSACC) or achieve very low scores (SparseMVC at 14.44%). The gap is most pronounced on GTSRB, where MSRL's 49.78% ACC exceeds the next-best TURTLE (43.01%) by nearly 7 points, and on DTD where MSRL reaches 62.34% vs. BONE's 58.04%. On Flowers and CIFAR-10, where most methods already saturate above 97%, MSRL still edges out TURTLE by 0.15% and 0.07% respectively, confirming consistent but smaller gains in easier regimes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-representation learning framework (Chen et al., 2022) from homogeneous multiview subspace clustering to the setting of heterogeneous views derived from frozen pretrained models (DINOv2, CLIP ViT-L/14). It departs from both traditional multiview clustering methods (CMVC, SparseMVC, BONE) that assume shared feature spaces, and from contrastive learning approaches that require augmented views from the same model, by introducing an attention-based information-passing mechanism and an assignment probability distribution consistency loss to bridge fundamentally different feature distributions. The unsupervised transfer learning paradigm (TURTLE, MIM-Refiner) provides the backbone-freezing strategy, while the cross-view consistency objective draws on ideas from self-supervised learning but applies them to the probability simplex rather than feature space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important problem: how to combine multiple pretrained models for clustering without expensive fine-tuning, and provides theoretical guarantees (bounded multiview consistency, incremental view attractivity) that are rare in the multiview clustering literature. A notable practical finding is that not every additional pretrained model helps—lower-quality backbones like ViT-B/32 can degrade performance—highlighting an open question about principled model selection. Limitations include reliance on frozen backbones (no adaptation), the very low CKA similarity between transformer and convolutional backbones (0.04–0.19) suggesting the method may struggle with truly heterogeneous architectures, and the evaluation being limited to clustering accuracy rather than downstream task transfer.
