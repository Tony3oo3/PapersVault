## Problem

Cross-task generalization in robotic manipulation requires transferring manipulation knowledge from seen tasks to entirely unseen tasks without parameter updates. Existing in-context learning approaches (e.g., X-ICM) provide only low-level continuous action sequences as context, causing LLMs to degenerate into superficial trajectory pattern matching rather than reasoning about composable skills, which limits generalization to tasks with novel skill compositions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265933/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB scene observation, a text task instruction, and a library of seen-task demonstrations (each segmented into keyframe pairs with 7-DoF actions) as input. Actions are discretized into 7-tuples: 3D voxel indices (V=100 bins per axis), Euler angle bins (5° resolution), and a binary gripper command. The LLM outputs only executable action sequences (no skill labels), ensuring direct compatibility with the robot controller while skill annotations in the prompt guide internal reasoning.

## Method

The framework is a training-free pipeline with three components: (1) Atomic Skill Collection uses a VLM to annotate each demonstration segment with interpretable skill labels (Verb[obj] format) constrained by gripper transitions, producing skill-action aligned pairs; (2) Dual-Library Demonstration Retrieval combines a dynamic library (fusing DINOv3 visual similarity with Jaccard-based plan-skill matching from a planning agent) and a coverage-aware static library (IDF-weighted token selection that greedily fills missing skill patterns from the predicted plan); (3) Skill-Augmented In-Context Learning formats the 20 retrieved demonstrations (17 dynamic + 3 coverage) with explicit skill-action alignment, prompting the LLM to reason compositionally over skill sequences while outputting only executable actions. The key insight is that elevating context from raw numerical trajectories to composable skill structures explicitly activates the LLM's reasoning over skill composition and execution ordering rather than trajectory imitation.

## Results

On the AGNOSTOS benchmark (23 unseen tasks), Decompose and Recompose achieves 32.5% (Level-1) and 18.5% (Level-2) success rates, yielding a 26.4% overall average across 75 episodes per task. The method exceeds 60% success on four distinct tasks (Microwave 62.7%, Seat 72.0%, LampOff 67.0%, and a Level-2 task), whereas no single baseline surpasses 60% on more than three tasks. In real-world experiments on a UFACTORY xArm6, the method achieves a 36% average success rate across five zero-shot manipulation tasks, with the best result being 70% on 'put a bunch of grapes on a plate.'

## Baselines & Comparisons

The closest and most directly comparable baseline is X-ICM (Zhou et al., 2025), the only other ICL-based cross-task method. Decompose and Recompose outperforms X-ICM by +3.9% overall (26.4% vs. 23.5%), with particularly large gains on Seat (+24.0 pp), LampOff (+8.3 pp), and Microwave (+17.4 pp), though X-ICM retains an edge on Knife (+5.4 pp) and Phone (+14.6 pp). Among Foundation VLA models, π₀ is the strongest baseline at 17.5% overall, still 8.9 pp below the proposed method; SAM2Act (14.0%) and VoxPoser (15.6%) trail further behind. In-Domain baselines (PerAct 14.0%, RVT 12.8%) and Human-Video baselines (D4R 12.6%, R3M 9.7%) all fall well below, confirming that task-specific training or human-video pretraining does not close the cross-task gap. The ablation (Table 2) further shows that each component contributes incrementally: random selection (21.6%) → dynamic retrieval (23.3%) → coverage-aware static library (24.9%) → skill-augmented ICL (26.4%), with the skill-action alignment providing the largest single gain (+1.5 pp). On real-world tasks, no direct baseline comparison is reported, but the 36% average across five zero-shot tasks demonstrates practical viability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the in-context learning paradigm for robotic manipulation, building most directly on X-ICM (Zhou et al., 2025), which first demonstrated zero-shot cross-task action prediction by prompting LLMs with seen-task demonstrations. The key departure is replacing raw numerical action sequences as in-context examples with atomic skill-action aligned pairs, elevating the intermediate representation from trajectory shapes to composable skill structures. The dual-library retrieval strategy (dynamic visual-semantic retrieval plus coverage-aware static supplementation with IDF-weighted token selection) is a novel combination not present in prior ICL-for-robotics work, and the entire pipeline is training-free, leveraging pretrained DINOv3 encoders and Qwen-2.5-7B without any task-specific fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical gap: deploying robots on tasks never seen during training, which is essential for open-world general-purpose manipulation. The training-free, skill-decomposition approach offers a modular path that could be combined with stronger VLA backbones as they improve. However, absolute success rates remain modest (26.4% overall on AGNOSTOS, 36% average in real-world), and the authors themselves identify 3D spatial reasoning and object affordance understanding as unresolved limitations. Open questions include whether the skill-action alignment representation scales to longer-horizon tasks with more complex skill compositions, and whether the coverage-aware retrieval strategy generalizes beyond the relatively constrained RLBench/AGNOSTOS task space.
