## Problem

Low-rank optimal transport (LR-OT) is a non-convex, NP-hard co-clustering problem for which existing solvers rely on complex multi-variable local optimization without provable approximation guarantees. While Scetbon & Cuturi (2022) showed that K-means reduces to LR-OT, the reverse reduction—showing LR-OT is no harder than a standard clustering problem—was unexplored, leaving a gap between the rich theory of K-means (O(log K) and (1+ε) approximations) and the heuristic-only landscape of LR-OT algorithms.

## Input / Output

The method takes a cost matrix C ∈ R^{n×n} induced by a cost function c(x_i, y_j) between two datasets X and Y of equal size n, along with a target rank K. It outputs a pair of low-rank factors (Q, R) where Q ∈ {0,1}^{n×K} is a hard assignment matrix and R = P_σ*^T Q, together with the co-clustering partitions of X and Y. For unequal dataset sizes (n ≠ m), a Kantorovich registration variant replaces the Monge permutation with the optimal soft transport plan, yielding a soft generalized K-means subproblem.

## Method

The key insight is a reparameterization: since the hard assignment factors Q and R share matching row and column sums, R = P_σ^T Q for some permutation σ, collapsing the two-variable co-clustering problem into a single-variable generalized K-means problem on the Monge-registered cost C·P_σ*^T. Transport Clustering (TC) is a two-step algorithm: (1) compute the optimal full-rank Monge map P_σ* via the Hungarian or Sinkhorn algorithm, then (2) solve generalized K-means on the registered cost to obtain Q, with R recovered automatically. The paper proves constant-factor approximation guarantees—(1+γ) for negative-type metrics, (1+γ+√(2γ)) for kernel costs, and (1+γ+ρ) for general metrics—where γ is the ratio of full-rank to rank-K optimal costs. A transport-registered initialization (Algorithm 2) that takes the best of K-means on X or the Monge-registered K-means on Y preserves these guarantees when combined with standard (1+ε)-approximation K-means or K-medians solvers, eliminating the need for a global generalized K-means solver.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (n=60,000), TC achieves the lowest OT cost (231.20) versus LOT (234.73) and FRLC (235.95), with the highest AMI (0.478/0.476) and ARI (0.358/0.356) across both splits. On the largest single-cell alignment (n=131,040 mouse embryo cells), TC achieves OT cost 0.389 and CTA 0.564, while LOT fails to compute an alignment at this scale. For Wasserstein distance estimation on the fractured hypercube (d=30, K=10), TC achieves error 0.784 at n=54 samples, compared to FRLC (1.745), FactoredOT (2.774), and full-rank OT (13.485). On synthetic benchmarks, TC obtains a 23% average relative cost improvement over LOT on Shifted Gaussians and 4% on the Stochastic Block Model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TC outperforms LOT (Scetbon et al., 2021) on OT cost, AMI, ARI, and CTA across all three main datasets; notably, LOT fails to compute an alignment beyond ~45,360 cells while TC scales to 131,040. Against FRLC (Halmos et al., 2024), TC achieves lower OT cost and higher clustering metrics on every dataset, with the largest gap on the E8.5→E8.75 single-cell alignment (CTA 0.722 vs 0.525). On synthetic benchmarks, TC beats LatentOT (Lin et al., 2021) on OT cost across all noise levels and ranks, though LatentOT marginally outperforms TC on ARI/AMI for co-cluster recovery on Shifted Gaussians (1.00/1.00 vs 0.97/0.99). For Wasserstein distance estimation, TC substantially outperforms FactoredOT (Forrow et al., 2019), achieving error 0.78±0.13 at n=200 on Gaussian mixtures (d=50) versus 2.45±0.27 for FactoredOT. The main trade-off is runtime: TC takes 90.51s on CIFAR-10 versus 6.84s for LOT, reflecting the cost of the full-rank transport registration step.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper inverts a key reduction established by Scetbon & Cuturi (2022), who showed that K-means is a special case of low-rank OT. Here, the authors prove the reverse: low-rank OT (a co-clustering problem over two datasets) reduces to generalized K-means (a single-dataset clustering problem) via a Monge registration step that permutes the cost matrix. The optimization formulation builds directly on the low-rank Kantorovich parameterization of Scetbon et al. (2021), but the reparameterization trick—eliminating the auxiliary variable R by expressing it as a permutation of Q—converts a three-variable non-convex problem into a single-variable clustering subroutine. This positions TC as the first algorithm for low-rank OT with provable constant-factor approximation guarantees, in contrast to prior solvers (LOT, FRLC, LatentOT) that only converge to stationary points.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The first constant-factor approximation guarantees for low-rank OT (up to 2-approximation for negative-type metrics, (2+√2) for kernel costs) close a significant theoretical gap and enable the transfer of mature K-means/K-medians approximation and statistical guarantees to the LR-OT setting. Practically, TC consistently outperforms existing solvers across synthetic benchmarks, CIFAR-10 co-clustering, and large-scale single-cell transcriptomics (up to 131k cells), with the strongest gains in cross-domain class-transfer accuracy. Key limitations include a substantial runtime penalty (~90s vs ~7s on CIFAR-10) due to the full-rank OT registration step, theoretical guarantees proven only for the Monge (equal-size, uniform-marginal) case rather than the general Kantorovich setting, and an uncharacterized interaction with entropy regularization. Extending the guarantees to the Kantorovich case and developing linear-time registration are the most natural next steps.
