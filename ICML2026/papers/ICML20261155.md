## Problem

Nonparametric conditional independence (CI) testing is fundamentally challenging: no test can be uniformly valid and powerful across all distributions (Shah & Peters, 2020), and existing kernel-based tests (KCIT, RCIT) suffer from poor adaptivity and scalability as conditioning dimension grows. The paper asks whether learned spectral representations of the partial covariance operator can overcome these limitations while retaining rigorous statistical guarantees.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261155/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an i.i.d. sample of (X, Y, Z) triplets split into a training set (size m) for representation learning and a held-out test set (size n) for computing the statistic. In the breast cancer application, X ∈ ℝ³ (Her2/Luminal/Basal metagene scores), Y ∈ {0,1} (3-year death vs. 5-year survival), and Z ∈ ℝ³⁸⁴ (Path Foundation model embeddings of H&E-stained 244×244 tiles). The output is a scalar test statistic T_n = n‖Ĉ_UV − Ĉ_UW Ĉ_WV‖_F² compared against the 1−α quantile of χ²(d²), yielding a reject/fail-to-reject decision and an approximate p-value.

## Method

SpectralCIT learns the leading singular functions of the partial covariance operator Σ_{XẎ·Z} via a bi-level contrastive algorithm: an outer problem optimizes feature maps u_θ(X) and v_θ(Ẏ) to capture the cross-covariance structure, while an inner problem optimizes w_θ(Z) to implicitly handle the residualization term Σ_XZ Σ_ZẎ that cannot be directly estimated from data. The empirical losses are U-statistics derived from the Eckart-Young-Mirsky variational formulation, augmented with orthonormality (whitening) regularization, followed by a post-hoc whitening step that enforces empirical orthonormality of the learned features. The resulting test statistic T_n = n‖Ĉ_UV − Ĉ_UW Ĉ_WV‖_F² is a simple Frobenius norm of a d×d matrix; under the null it converges to χ²(d²) (governed by the orthonormality error E_m^val), and under the alternative its power is governed by how well the learned representations approximate the rank-d truncation of Σ_{XẎ·Z} (error E_m^pow). This design decouples the statistical validity conditions from full regression estimation, requiring only moment control of bounded (e.g., Tanh-activated) network outputs.

## Results

On the TCGA-BRCA breast cancer dataset (N=1341 triplets with 384-dim image features), SpectralCIT strongly rejects conditional independence (p < 10⁻³), while KCIT (p = 6.8×10⁻²) and NNLSCIT (p = 3.8×10⁻¹) fail to reject at α = 0.05. An XGBoost model confirms residual predictive signal: accuracy rises from 0.91 (Z alone) to 0.95 (X + Z), validating SpectralCIT's detection of non-linear conditional dependence. On synthetic post-nonlinear benchmarks (d_Z up to 300, N=1000), SpectralCIT maintains type I error at or below α = 0.05 across all dimensions while achieving high power, whereas KCIT, RCIT, GCIT, and DGCIT all fail type I error control.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpectralCIT outperforms all six baselines on the breast cancer task: it is the only method to reject the null (p < 10⁻³ vs. KCIT's 6.8×10⁻² and NNLSCIT's 3.8×10⁻¹), correctly identifying non-linear conditional dependence that linear partial correlations (all |r| < 0.09) and logistic regression miss. On synthetic post-nonlinear benchmarks, KCIT, RCIT, and GCIT achieve high power but fail type I error control across all conditioning dimensions, while DGCIT completely loses calibration; SpectralCIT matches NNLSCIT's robust type I error control while also maintaining high power. In the nonsmooth high-dimensional experiment, NNLSCIT's type I error breaks down entirely due to its smoothness assumptions, whereas SpectralCIT remains calibrated. Against kernel-based methods in the scalability experiment (d_Z up to 300), KCIT achieves slightly higher power at d_Z ∈ {250, 300} but at the cost of invalid type I error, and GCM preserves validity but exhibits very low power; LPCIT was excluded due to excessive runtime. Overall, SpectralCIT is the only method that simultaneously controls type I error and retains competitive power across all tested settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate lines of work: kernel-based conditional independence tests (Fukumizu et al., 2007; Zhang et al., 2011) that model dependence through the partial covariance operator, and spectral representation learning (Kostic et al., 2024) that learns leading singular functions of covariance operators via contrastive objectives. The key technical departure is a bi-level optimization formulation: because the partial covariance operator Σ_{XȲ·Z} involves the composition Σ_{XZ}Σ_{ZȲ}, which is not directly estimable from data, the authors introduce an inner optimization over auxiliary features w(Z) to handle the residualization implicitly. This contrasts with regression-based tests (KCIT, RCIT) that require explicit conditional expectation estimation, and with local permutation tests (NNLSCIT) that rely on smoothness assumptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled path toward scalable CI testing that avoids the power loss of kernel methods in high dimensions (Ramdas et al., 2015) while maintaining the operator-theoretic guarantees of the partial covariance framework. The breast cancer application is practically notable: SpectralCIT detected non-linear conditional dependence (p < 10⁻³) between molecular profiles and survival given 384-dim histology features, where KCIT and NNLSCIT failed to reject, suggesting learned spectral features capture complex dependencies invisible to fixed-kernel or permutation-based approaches. Key open questions include obtaining explicit non-asymptotic rates for the learned representations (the guarantees depend on the E_m terms), resolving the lack of standard hyperparameter protocols in CI testing, and understanding when the conservative calibration observed in practice can be tightened without sacrificing validity.
