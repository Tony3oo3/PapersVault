## Problem

LLMs have demonstrated strong capabilities in mathematical reasoning and code generation, yet they consistently fail to produce feasible solutions for open-ended engineering problems, where solutions must satisfy physical laws, safety limits, operational rules, and data consistency simultaneously. Prior work on autonomous research, mathematical modeling, and code generation advances correctness and expressiveness but neglects feasibility, with empirical results showing fewer than 10% of generated solutions are actually implementable under real-world constraints. This paper identifies four recurring failure modes—fancy but vague modeling, altering key data, violating physical laws, and over-constraining the model—and asks how to build a system that explicitly prioritizes feasibility at every stage of the engineering workflow.

## Input / Output

The system takes self-contained natural-language engineering problem statements (drawn from four domains: market/multi-agent decision-making, scheduling/resource allocation, planning/design, and control/autonomous systems) that include background context, numerical data tables, and explicit engineering constraints. It produces executable Pyomo optimization code, concrete numerical solutions, and a multi-dimensional evaluation report. Feasibility is assessed as a binary pass/fail via executable constraint verification confirmed by human experts (not LLM-based scoring), while modeling quality is scored 0–10 across four dimensions: information extraction, domain-specific reasoning, multi-objective decision-making, and uncertainty handling.

## Method

EngiAgent is a multi-agent system with five specialized agents—Analyzer (semantic parsing into structured JSON blueprints), Modeler (template-based Pyomo code generation), Verifier (hierarchical consistency checking with dynamic tolerance adjustment), Solver (backend selection and execution), and Evaluator (multi-perspective quality scoring)—orchestrated by a Fully Connected Coordinator. The key architectural distinction from prior pipeline-based reflection methods is that the coordinator dynamically routes feedback among any pair of agents based on real-time error diagnosis, rather than following a fixed sequential order; it employs a hybrid control strategy that grounds LLM-based autonomous routing decisions within structured engineering protocols. State-aware memory tracks error history, and a forced agent-switching mechanism triggers when repetitive failures are detected, preventing infinite debugging loops and promoting convergence. The Verifier distinguishes catastrophic directional errors (immediate rejection) from pragmatic modeling deviations (accepted with minimal correction suggestions), and its tolerance automatically relaxes after consecutive verification failures to avoid unproductive retry cycles.

## Results

On the EngiAgent Benchmark (53 open-ended engineering problems across four domains), EngiAgent with the fully connected coordinator achieves feasibility rates of 64.15% (GPT-4o), 50.94% (Gemini-2.5 Flash), and 75.47% (DeepSeek-V3-671B), representing an average seven-fold improvement over the best prior baselines. The numerical-to-feasible gap is reduced to as low as 1.89% (GPT-4o and Gemini-2.5 Flash) and 3.78% (DeepSeek-V3-671B), indicating that nearly all generated numerical solutions satisfy real-world constraints. On average quality scores (0–10 scale), EngiAgent (Coord.) achieves 7.72 (GPT-4o), 6.89 (Gemini-2.5 Flash), and 7.24 (DeepSeek-V3-671B) across the four evaluation dimensions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EngiAgent (Coord.) decisively outperforms all three baselines on the key feasibility metric across all three LLMs. On GPT-4o, feasibility jumps from 7.55% (MM-Agent, best baseline) to 64.15%, an 8.5× improvement; on Gemini-2.5 Flash, from 3.77% to 50.94% (13.5×); and on DeepSeek-V3-671B, from 28.30% (DS-Agent) to 75.47% (2.7×). DS-Agent produces the highest numerical solution rates among baselines (62.26% on GPT-4o, 77.36% on DeepSeek) but most outputs violate constraints, yielding feasibility rates of only 5.66% and 28.30% respectively. MM-Agent achieves the best baseline feasibility on GPT-4o (7.55%) and the highest average quality scores among baselines (8.12 on GPT-4o, 8.34 on DeepSeek for feasible solutions), but generates too few numerical results overall (13.21% and 11.32% numerical rates). ResearchAgent performs worst across all settings, with feasibility at or near 0% on two of three LLMs. The fully connected coordinator itself adds over 10% feasibility improvement on average compared to the fixed-pipeline variant of EngiAgent, confirming that flexible routing is critical for robust engineering problem solving.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant fixed-pipeline paradigm in multi-agent LLM systems (sequential planners, hierarchical decomposers, and role-based coordinators) by introducing a fully connected coordinator that dynamically routes feedback among specialized agents. It also carves new ground relative to prior LLM-for-engineering work, which has focused on closed, domain-specific tasks (power dispatch, materials, structures) using fixed templates, by targeting open-ended problems where the formulation itself must be discovered. The feasibility-first evaluation criterion is a deliberate departure from mathematical modeling agents (e.g., MM-Agent) that prioritize formulation elegance and from code-generation agents (e.g., DS-Agent) that prioritize executability, neither of which treats physical and operational constraint satisfaction as the primary success metric.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By establishing feasibility as the central evaluation axis and demonstrating that a flexible coordination architecture can close the gap between numerical output and practical validity, the paper provides a concrete template for deploying LLM agents in safety-critical engineering contexts where infeasible solutions are unacceptable. The 53-problem benchmark across four domains is a useful but limited resource; open questions include whether the approach generalizes to genuinely novel engineering problems outside the four covered domains, how it scales to problems with hundreds of constraints, and whether the 50–75% feasibility ceiling can be pushed higher. The finding that text-based LLM-as-judge scores poorly correlate with feasibility also has broader implications for how the community evaluates open-ended technical tasks.
