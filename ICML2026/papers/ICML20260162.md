## Problem

LLM-based pairwise judges are prone to position bias and miscalibration, yet no existing method provides a principled, finite-sample guarantee that the error rate among accepted judgments stays below a user-specified level. Heuristic confidence thresholding offers no statistical validity on new data, while methods with formal guarantees (e.g., Clopper-Pearson bounds) are so conservative that they reject most queries, sacrificing coverage.

## Input / Output

The system consumes a user instruction paired with two candidate responses, a target risk level α ∈ (0,1), and a labeled calibration set of human preferences. At test time it outputs either a pairwise preference (A or B) or an abstention symbol, with the guarantee that the fraction of incorrect accepted judgments is at most α. BPE requires exactly two deterministic forward passes per pair (one per response ordering) at temperature 0, extracting softmax probabilities over the two preference tokens only.

## Method

SCOPE combines two components: (1) Bidirectional Preference Entropy (BPE), which queries the judge under both response orderings, averages the resulting preference probabilities into a bias-neutral score p̄, and applies binary entropy as the uncertainty signal—penalizing position bias through order disagreement rather than measuring it directly; and (2) a conformal calibration step that selects the largest threshold λ̂ satisfying a linear-expectation constraint (Σ S(xᵢ,λ̂)·(E(xᵢ)−α) ≤ −1) on the calibration set, yielding a finite-sample FDR guarantee under exchangeability. The linearized loss reframes risk control as a budgeting problem where correct accepted predictions build a safety margin and incorrect ones deplete it, and the −1 correction absorbs the worst-case contribution of a single unseen test point. The resulting threshold is a drop-in calibration layer requiring no retraining, exposing α as the sole user-tunable knob trading coverage against certified error rate.

## Results

At α=0.10, SCOPE achieves empirical FDR of 0.097–0.099 across all four judges and three main benchmarks, consistently meeting the target risk bound. Coverage ranges from 0.246 (Qwen-7B, MT-Bench) to 0.983 (Qwen-32B, RewardBench), substantially exceeding the Naïve calibration baseline (e.g., 0.246 vs. 0.102 for Qwen-7B on MT-Bench, a 2.4× improvement). For uncertainty estimation, BPE achieves the best AUROC (0.685–0.822) and AUPRC (0.855–0.975) across model scales and datasets, with ECE as low as 0.062 (Qwen-7B, RewardBench), outperforming all four baselines in calibration and discrimination.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the four uncertainty baselines, BPE strictly improves over Swap-and-Aggregate (its closest relative, sharing the same bidirectional prediction rule) on ECE, AUROC, and AUPRC in every model–dataset cell, confirming that probability-level aggregation yields a richer signal than discrete vote agreement. BPE also outperforms Predictive Probability and Verbalized Confidence on AUROC and AUPRC across all settings, though Verbalized Confidence occasionally achieves slightly lower ECE (e.g., 0.096 vs. 0.145 on MT-Bench with Llama-70B) at the cost of near-chance discrimination (AUROC ≈ 0.48). Against Simulated Annotators, BPE delivers substantially stronger discrimination (AUROC 0.685 vs. 0.594 on MT-Bench/Qwen-7B; 0.809 vs. 0.627 on RewardBench/Qwen-14B) with only two forward passes versus five sampled generations. For selective prediction, SCOPE dominates Naïve calibration on coverage (up to 2.4× more accepted judgments) while both stay near the risk bound, and it achieves 1.1–3.2× higher coverage than Trust-or-Escalate (ToE) under the same α=0.10 constraint, because BPE's continuous entropy score avoids the discrete-confidence blocks that cap ToE's coverage at 17–23%.

## Relation to Prior Work

SCOPE sits at the intersection of conformal risk control (Angelopoulos et al., 2024) and selective prediction for LLM judges, adapting the linear-expectation-constraint calibration of Wang et al. (2025b) to the pairwise judging setting. The bidirectional scoring idea draws directly from the swap-and-aggregate protocol of Zheng et al. (2023), but replaces discrete vote agreement with a continuous probability-level entropy signal. The closest prior work with formal guarantees is Trust-or-Escalate (Jung et al., 2025), which uses Clopper-Pearson bounds and fixed-sequence testing; SCOPE departs by using a less conservative linear-expectation constraint paired with a bias-neutral uncertainty score, trading some of ToE's per-prefix conservatism for substantially higher coverage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SCOPE provides the first finite-sample FDR guarantee for single-model pairwise LLM judging as a drop-in calibration layer, which is practically important for RLHF reward pipelines and leaderboard systems where even small systematic error rates compound. Key limitations include the exchangeability assumption (demonstrated to break under cross-benchmark transfer), the white-box requirement for logit access, and restriction to binary pairwise settings. Open questions include extending guarantees to pointwise scoring, multi-response ranking, and black-box API-only judges, as well as understanding how the framework behaves under adversarial or distribution-shifted deployment.
