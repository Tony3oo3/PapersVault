## Problem

Existing neural network architectures fix their connectivity topology at either the sparse extreme (FractalNet, with O(1) average in-degree independent of scale) or the dense extreme (DenseNet, with O(N) average in-degree growing linearly with node count), implicitly hard-coding how information is aggregated. No principled framework exists to systematically interpolate between these regimes or to treat network topology itself as a controllable, first-class design object with theoretical guarantees on acyclicity and complexity.

## Input / Output

For image classification, the method consumes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for ImageNet-1K) and outputs class labels (10, 100, or 1000 classes). For human activity recognition, it takes multi-channel IMU sensor sequences (9 channels of length 128 for UCI-HAR; hand/chest/ankle IMU data segmented into non-overlapping windows of length 256 for PAMAP2) and outputs activity class labels. A notable design constraint is that KGN nodes can receive multiple input branches of different spatial/channel shapes, which must be fused into a unified tensor before processing.

## Method

KGNs recursively construct directed acyclic graph topologies via the Kronecker-based transform P_i = I_{n_i} ⊗ P_{i-1} + P̃_i ⊗ C_i, where strictly upper-triangular inducer matrices P̃_i and binary connector matrices C_i control how the previous graph is duplicated and cross-linked at each depth. This algebraic generation rule yields families of DAGs whose average in-degree scales as O(log n) under bounded inducer sizes, interpolating between the O(1) and O(n) extremes of FractalNet and DenseNet, which are recovered as specific inducer choices. The paper proves acyclicity is preserved under the transform, derives closed-form expressions for node and edge counts, and establishes that fully connected inducers yield maximal expressiveness. Practically, KGNs are instantiated as encoder-decoder architectures where the encoder stacks shallow KGN blocks (typically depth-2) with fusion layers (Inception-Styled, Residual-Style, or Biased Residual-Style) that handle multi-input nodes of heterogeneous shapes, while the decoder maps top-level features to task-specific outputs. Concrete instantiations include KGN-Helix5 (a 2-layer, 20-node topology with 0.21–0.41M parameters for CIFAR) and KGN-HAR/KGN-PAMAP2 (2-layer models with 0.11–0.21M parameters for sensor-based activity recognition).

## Results

On CIFAR-10, KGN Helix5-C10-L achieves 96.31% top-1 accuracy with only 0.373M parameters, and on CIFAR-100, Helix5-C100 reaches 79.55% with 0.408M parameters. On ImageNet-1K, Helix5-ImageNet attains 74.89% top-1 and 92.23% top-5 accuracy with 4.63M parameters and 2.18 GFLOPs. For human activity recognition, KGN-HAR achieves 97.10% on UCI-HAR (0.111M params) and KGN-PAMAP2 reaches 95.60% on PAMAP2 under subject-independent evaluation (0.206M params).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On CIFAR-10, KGN Helix5-C10-L (96.31%, 0.373M) outperforms FractalNet Plain-20 (94.78%, 38.6M) by 1.53 points with 103× fewer parameters, and beats DenseNet-12 (94.76%, 1.0M) and CondenseNet-light-94 (95.00%, 0.33M) while using comparable or fewer parameters. On CIFAR-100, Helix5-C100 (79.55%) surpasses FractalNet (76.70–77.51%), DenseNet-12 (75.58%), CondenseNet (75.92%), and CPT-SNN (78.74%, 11.179M) by 1–4 points. On ImageNet-1K, Helix5-ImageNet (74.89% top-1, 4.63M) exceeds DenseNet-121 (74.43%, 7.98M), MobileNetV2 (72.0%, 3.4M), and CondenseNet (73.8%, 4.8M), and slightly edges out MobileNetV2-1.4 (74.7%, 6.9M) with 33% fewer parameters. On UCI-HAR, KGN-HAR (97.10%, 0.111M) trails HS-ResNet (97.38%, 0.67M) by 0.28 points but uses 6× fewer parameters. On PAMAP2, KGN-PAMAP2 (95.60%, 0.206M) outperforms HS-ResNet (94.29%, 1.20M) by 1.31 points with nearly 6× fewer parameters, and exceeds all other baselines under the subject-independent protocol.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by treating neural network topology as a first-class algebraic object generated through recursive Kronecker product rules, rather than fixing connectivity at design time or selecting it from a dense parent network. It draws conceptual inspiration from Kronecker graph generation (Leskovec et al., 2010) but departs fundamentally by producing directed acyclic computation graphs with learnable nodes and controllable information flow. The framework unifies FractalNet (bounded-degree, self-similar topology) and DenseNet (linearly scaling dense aggregation) as special cases of the same generative process, while explicitly positioning itself apart from Kronecker-based parameterization work (Jagtap et al., 2022; Gao et al., 2020) that operates on weights under a fixed graph, and from sparse-topology selection methods (Frankle & Carbin, 2019; Evci et al., 2020) that prune rather than generate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The KGN framework offers a principled, theoretically grounded dial for interpolating between sparse and dense connectivity regimes, with provable acyclicity, O(log n) in-degree bounds under bounded inducers, and explicit complexity formulas that let practitioners trade off aggregation richness against parameter cost. However, the empirical validation is limited to relatively small models (≤4.6M parameters), the ImageNet result (74.89% top-1) is competitive but not state-of-the-art, and the automated architecture search formulation remains only a brief sketch with a small six-candidate experiment. Open questions include whether the framework scales to very deep or very wide networks, whether the topological design space is rich enough to match hand-tuned architectures at scale, and whether the Kronecker generation rule can be extended to non-convolutional backbones (transformers, state-space models) or generative tasks.
