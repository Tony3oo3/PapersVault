## Problem

Existing automated theorem proving benchmarks (PutnamBench, miniF2F) are saturated, focused on competition math rather than real-world formalization workflows, and vulnerable to training-data contamination. This paper introduces SorryDB, a dynamically-updating benchmark of open proof obligations (sorry placeholders) drawn from 78 active Lean formalization projects on GitHub, designed to evaluate provers on tasks that are genuinely unsolved, contextually rich, and aligned with community needs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262386/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The task is fill-in-the-sorry: given a specific sorry keyword inside a Lean theorem, the prover must produce a proof term that discharges only that obligation, not the entire theorem. The input includes the local goal state at the sorry location (extracted via LeanInteract) plus the surrounding project repository for context. Verification is deterministic: the proposed term replaces the sorry, the file is compiled, and success requires the sorry count to decrease by exactly one with all remaining sorry goals unchanged—preventing trivial exploits like inserting sorryAx.

## Method

SorryDB is constructed by indexing all sorry keywords from 78 active repositories in the Lean Reservoir registry, filtering for reproducible, prop-valued obligations, and deduplicating by goal state. The evaluation framework compares deterministic tactics (trivial, ring, linarith, simp, grind), general-purpose LLMs (GPT 5.2, Claude Opus 4.5, Gemini Flash/Pro 3, Qwen 3), specialized RL-trained provers (Kimina Prover 8B, Goedel Prover V2 32B), self-correcting loops (up to 16 iterations with build-error feedback), and a ReAct-style agentic approach with LeanSearch library retrieval. The key empirical finding is that iterative compilation feedback is the dominant performance factor—self-correcting and agentic approaches (27–30%) far exceed any single-shot pass@k—while individual provers solve complementary subsets, with the combined union reaching 35.7%.

## Results

On the 1000-task SorryDB-2601 snapshot, the best single configuration is Gemini Flash 3 with an agentic ReAct loop (LeanSearch tool, 16 iterations), solving 30.3% of sorry statements. Self-correcting variants of Gemini Flash 3 and Claude Opus 4.5 achieve 27.9% and 27.1% respectively, far exceeding the best single-shot pass@32 score of 20.5% (Gemini Flash 3 / Gemini Pro 3). Deterministic tactics solve 8.4%, and the combined union of all provers reaches 35.7%, confirming strong complementarity across methods. Specialized RL-trained provers (Goedel Prover V2 32B: 11.3% pass@32; Kimina Prover 8B: 7.7% pass@32) underperform general-purpose LLMs on these real-world tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among general-purpose LLMs, Gemini Flash 3 and Gemini Pro 3 lead at 20.5% pass@32, outperforming Claude Opus 4.5 (15.4%), GPT 5.2 (13.2%), and Qwen 3 (8.1%). In self-correcting mode (16 iterations with build-error feedback), Claude Opus 4.5 (27.1%) and Gemini Flash 3 (27.9%) nearly triple their single-shot scores, while the agentic variant of Gemini Flash 3 (with LeanSearch tool calls) edges ahead at 30.3%. Specialized RL-trained provers—Goedel Prover V2 32B (11.3% pass@32) and Kimina Prover 8B (7.7% pass@32)—are substantially outperformed by every general-purpose LLM, likely due to their training focus on competition math and version mismatch with current Lean. Deterministic tactics (8.4%) solve a disjoint subset of problems, and the combined union of all provers (35.7%) exceeds any single method, demonstrating that no current approach dominates across all task types.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the SWE-bench paradigm—evaluating AI systems on real, unresolved GitHub issues—into the domain of formal theorem proving, where the 'issues' are unproven sorry statements in active Lean repositories. It extends prior fill-in-the-sorry evaluations (FormalML on 2 projects, RLMEval on 6 projects) from small, field-specific, static collections to a large-scale (78 repositories, 5,663 tasks), dynamically updating benchmark spanning all Lean project categories. Unlike competition-math benchmarks (PutnamBench, miniF2F, ProofNet) that are now saturated and susceptible to training-data leakage, SorryDB targets novel, unsolved proof obligations by construction, and unlike miniCTX which also sources from real projects, it explicitly excludes already-proven theorems to mitigate contamination.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SorryDB addresses three critical gaps in automated theorem proving evaluation: benchmark saturation, misalignment with practical use, and data contamination. The finding that iterative feedback (self-correction/agentic loops) is the dominant performance factor—outperforming pass@32 with double the compute—provides a clear practical signal for how to deploy provers. Open questions include estimating the true ceiling (how many tasks are genuinely unsolvable), whether the benchmark remains discriminative as provers improve, and whether the current verifier's restrictions (no new imports, no auxiliary lemmas) artificially cap performance. The benchmark is likely to become the de facto standard for evaluating Lean provers on real-world utility, analogous to SWE-bench's role for code agents.
