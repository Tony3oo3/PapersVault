## Problem

Classic Bayesian persuasion theory operates on discrete, finite signal and action spaces, limiting its application to heavily abstracted toy games (e.g., binary student quality, binary recommend/not-recommend). Real-world persuasion involves natural language with infinite-dimensional signal spaces where classic solution concepts break down, and LLM parameter spaces are too high-dimensional and non-convex for direct strategy optimization. This paper bridges the gap by formulating BP directly in the natural language domain.

## Input / Output

The method takes natural language state text (e.g., a detailed student background description), compact discrete prompt objects (category-value style pairs or small dictionaries of prompt slots), and numerical utility functions with a shared prior as input. It produces an optimal signaling scheme encoded as a public style prompt string, an equilibrium meta-strategy (probability distribution over the prompt library), the realized persuasive signal text generated by the sender LLM, and the receiver's action decision. Unusually, the 'action space' for both agents is the set of prompts rather than neural network parameters, and the signal space is open-ended natural language text scored by an LLM judge.

## Method

VBP reformulates BP as a verbalized mediator-augmented extensive-form game (MAG) where states, signals, and actions are all text, then solves it via a Prompt-PSRO loop: a meta-solver computes an equilibrium over a growing library of prompt strategies, and OPRO (static) or FunSearch (multistage, where the strategy is an executable Python prompt-generating function conditioned on interaction history) generates approximate best responses. Three key mechanisms ensure BP-specific structure: (1) verbalized commitment, where the sender's style string and meta-strategy weights are injected into the receiver's prompt as common knowledge, with a judge-LLM verifier penalizing non-adherence; (2) verbalized obedience constraints, enforced as a soft penalty using a Monte Carlo estimate of counterfactual one-step deviations selected via a theory-of-mind LLM query; and (3) information obfuscation, where an evaluator LLM scores the signal on omission, distortion, ambiguity, and misdirection, triggering self-reflection revisions below a threshold. The framework provides a convergence guarantee to an ε-approximate Bayes correlated equilibrium (static) or Bayes-Nash equilibrium (multistage).

## Results

On Full Press Diplomacy (S4), VBP(GPT-5) achieves a Game Score of 41.9 (±2.5) and 17.0% win rate, outperforming GPT-5 (41.0, 15.0%), Gemini 2.5 Pro (39.4, 11.0%), and DeepSeek-R1 (39.0, 10.0%). VBP reduces invalid orders to 3.1% (vs. 5.2–6.1% for baselines), lowers hold rate to 26.8% (vs. 31.8–34.9%), and improves support success to 59.8% (vs. 49.2–52.7%). On the classic REL task, VBP achieves a sender reward of 0.48 (±0.03) with a lying probability of 0.22, matching the analytical BCE equilibrium while DeepSeek-R1 achieves only 0.42 sender reward. VBP also reduces expected calibration error (ECE) to 0.07 (±0.01) and obedience penalty to 0.12 (±0.05), roughly 40% and 60% lower than GPT-5's 0.12 and 0.31 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the analytical BCE baseline (Kamenica & Gentzkow, 2011), VBP successfully reproduces the theoretical equilibrium on all three classic static BP problems (REL, COR, LAE), matching the predicted lying/honesty probabilities and sender/receiver rewards within noise. Compared to the MARL baseline (Lin et al., 2023), VBP achieves comparable or slightly better sender rewards while requiring no gradient-based training, relying instead on prompt optimization. In the S4 Diplomacy setting, VBP(GPT-5) beats GPT-5 by +0.9 Game Score and reduces invalid orders from 5.2% to 3.1% and betrayal rate from 34.7% to 24.9%, with the Game Score improvement being statistically marginal but the reliability metrics showing significant gains. Against DeepSeek-R1 on the REL task, VBP achieves higher sender reward (0.48 vs. 0.42) through more strategic lying (0.22 vs. 0.13), while DeepSeek-R1 yields higher receiver reward (0.25 vs. 0.19), confirming that game-theoretic optimization produces more nuanced information manipulation than raw reasoning. On posterior calibration (Table 1c), VBP-G5 reduces ECE by ~40% relative to GPT-5 (0.07 vs. 0.12) and cuts the obedience penalty by more than half (0.12 vs. 0.31), demonstrating that verbalized commitment and posterior writing tighten the belief-action coupling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Prompt-PSRO framework (Gemp et al., 2024) from general game solving to the specific structure of Bayesian persuasion, reformulating BP as a verbalized mediator-augmented extensive-form game (Zhang & Sandholm, 2022) where states, signals, and actions are all natural language. It departs from classic BP solvers (linear programming, MARL) that require discrete, finite signal spaces by using OPRO and FunSearch as best-response oracles over prompt strings, and introduces conditional prompt optimization for multistage settings. The verbalized commitment assumption, obedience constraints, and information obfuscation mechanism are novel operationalizations of classical BP concepts in the LLM-agent setting, distinguishing VBP from both cheap-talk learning and prior LLM persuasion studies that lack formal equilibrium guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VBP is the first framework to solve BP problems directly in natural language with a convergence guarantee to Bayes correlated/Bayes-Nash equilibria, opening the door to studying persuasion in realistic multi-stage, multi-agent scenarios (e.g., full-press Diplomacy) that were previously intractable for classical methods. Key limitations include reliance on LLMs as imperfect proxies for human decision-makers, significant computational overhead from iterative prompt optimization and rollout evaluation, and the restrictiveness of prompt-based control compared to fine-tuning. Open questions include extending to multi-sender settings, validating external validity through human studies, and connecting the emergent bargaining dynamics in multistage BP to established bargaining theory.
