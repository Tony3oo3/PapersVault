## Problem

Mainstream value-function-free RL algorithms for LLM reasoning (GRPO, DAPO) assign a single uniform reward to every token in a sequence based solely on the final outcome, resulting in coarse-grained credit assignment that penalizes correct intermediate steps in wrong answers and rewards flawed reasoning in correct ones. While prior work recognizes policy entropy as a signal of pivotal reasoning steps, it treats entropy only as a regularization constraint or a binary selection mask, failing to reshape the reward signal itself for fine-grained supervision.

## Input / Output

The method takes a mathematical problem prompt and samples a group of G=16 response sequences (max 4096 tokens, temperature 1.0, top-p 1.0) from the current policy. For each token, the old policy's generation entropy H_{i,t} is computed from its full vocabulary probability distribution, and these entropy values are normalized across all sequences active at that timestep. The output is a trained policy that produces a chain-of-thought reasoning trace ending in a boxed numerical answer, evaluated via Pass@k and Mean@32 on AIME and MATH 500.

## Method

The core mechanism is Dynamic Entropy Weighting, which redistributes the terminal reward signal proportionally to token-level or sequence-level policy entropy. GTPO (token-level) assigns each token in a correct sequence a bonus proportional to its normalized entropy H_{i,t}/ΣH_{k,t} scaled by the count of active correct sequences d_t, while tokens in incorrect sequences receive penalties weighted by inverse entropy (1/H_{j,t}), thereby rewarding valuable exploration in success and punishing confident errors in failure. GRPO-S (sequence-level) applies the same principle using each sequence's average entropy Ĥ_k, modulating the global sequence reward for computational efficiency. Both algorithms use PPO-style clipped surrogate losses with token-level normalization (1/Σ|o_k|), and the paper proves reward-mass conservation (total shaped reward equals original reward when α₁+α₂=1) and asymptotic gradient consistency under an Entropy Consolidation condition, establishing that GTPO and GRPO-S share the same global optimum as DAPO.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-7B, GTPO achieves 34.23 Mean@32 and 64.89 Pass@32 on AIME 2024, and 16.67 Mean@32 on AIME 2025, while GRPO-S reaches 34.67 Mean@32 and 18.33 Mean@32 on AIME 2025. On Qwen2.5-32B, GTPO attains 35.21 Mean@32 and 68.91 Pass@32 on AIME 2024, and 26.89 Mean@32 on AIME 2025; GRPO-S reaches 35.52 Mean@32 on AIME 2024 and 85.79 Mean@32 on MATH 500. Both methods establish new state-of-the-art across all three benchmarks (AIME 2024, AIME 2025, MATH 500) for both model sizes, with the largest absolute gain being +29.4 points on Pass@32 (AIME 2024, 7B) over DAPO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Both GTPO and GRPO-S consistently outperform all three baselines (GRPO, DAPO, and DAPO w/ Forking Tokens) across every benchmark and model size. Against GRPO, GTPO gains +5.87 Mean@32 on AIME 2024 (7B) and +3.50 on AIME 2025 (32B), while GRPO-S gains +5.50 and +7.40 respectively. Against DAPO, the gains are even larger: GTPO improves AIME 2024 Pass@32 by +29.38 (7B) and +9.89 (32B), and AIME 2025 Mean@32 by +3.34 (7B) and +5.22 (32B). Against the strongest baseline DAPO w/ Forking Tokens, GTPO still leads by +3.46 Pass@32 on AIME 2024 (7B) and +4.65 Mean@32 on AIME 2025 (32B), confirming that continuous entropy-weighted reward shaping outperforms the binary forking-token mask. On MATH 500, both methods achieve modest but consistent gains of ~1 point over DAPO w/ Forking Tokens, indicating the entropy mechanism is most impactful on harder, longer-horizon reasoning tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GRPO/DAPO lineage of value-function-free policy optimization for LLM reasoning by introducing Dynamic Entropy Weighting as a continuous reward-shaping mechanism. It departs from prior entropy-based approaches (DAPO w/ Forking Tokens, which uses entropy as a binary token mask, and Cui et al., which uses it as a regularization term) by repurposing policy entropy as a proportional redistribution signal that assigns unique per-token or per-sequence rewards. The theoretical grounding builds on variance-reduction arguments showing that token-level normalization (as in DAPO) is statistically superior to GRPO's sequence-level averaging, and the paper formalizes reward conservation and asymptotic consistency properties that connect GTPO/GRPO-S back to the DAPO objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a widely recognized but under-addressed limitation in RL for LLMs—coarse-grained credit assignment—without requiring an explicit value network, making it a practical drop-in improvement for existing GRPO/DAPO training pipelines. The observed 'entropy rebound' phenomenon (where policy entropy initially drops then recovers, unlike DAPO's monotonic collapse) provides mechanistic evidence that the reward shaping actively sustains exploration, which is critical for long CoT reasoning. Open questions include whether the optimal entropy comparison is column-wise, row-wise, or matrix-wise (the paper only tests one), whether the approach generalizes beyond mathematical reasoning to other long-horizon tasks, and whether the hyperparameter sensitivity (particularly the entropy bonus weight) remains stable at larger scales or with different base models.
