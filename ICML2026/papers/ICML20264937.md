## Problem

Federated Prompt Learning for medical images relies on a 'textual tuning' paradigm that assumes the frozen visual encoder already provides well-structured, discriminative feature geometry. The paper identifies two geometric pathologies that break this assumption in medical settings: intra-client manifold collapse (subtle morphological differences compress class features into low-rank subspaces) and inter-client topological misalignment (heterogeneous acquisition protocols produce inconsistent neighborhood structures across hospitals), making text-side boundary shifting unreliable and global aggregation unstable.

## Input / Output

Each client provides 224×224 medical images (dermoscopy, histopathology WSI, or ultrasound) with class labels; the frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 backbone processes them with 1 learnable visual prompt token prepended at each of 12 transformer layers. A GPT-4o-generated semantic codebook (10 attribute anchors per class, encoded by the frozen CLIP text encoder) serves as a client-invariant reference that is broadcast but never updated. The output is a class label predicted via temperature-scaled cosine similarity between the prompted visual embedding and the frozen semantic anchors, with only the 9.2K visual prompt parameters exchanged per federated round (0.07 MB in FP16).

## Method

FedMAP shifts federated prompt learning from textual tuning to visual manifold anchoring by injecting depth-aware visual prompts into the frozen CLIP image encoder and regularizing the resulting feature geometry with two complementary mechanisms. Manifold Semantic Anchoring (MSA) computes classification loss against an LLM-derived semantic codebook (attribute-augmented anchors averaged and L2-normalized in CLIP text space), whose gradient always lies in span(A), thereby expanding the effective feature subspace to counteract intra-client rank collapse. Topology Structural Alignment (TSA) maintains an EMA-updated prototype bank per client and minimizes the Frobenius-norm distance between the visual prototype Gram matrix and the frozen text relation matrix, enforcing consistent inter-class similarity structure across heterogeneous clients. The total per-client objective is L_CE + λ·L_struct (λ=10), optimized with SGD (lr=1e-3) for 50 FedAvg rounds with 1 local epoch each. A theoretical analysis proves that under manifold collapse no text-side tuning can create discriminative directions outside the collapsed subspace, while MSA injects anchor-span descent directions and TSA guarantees neighborhood-level topology consistency when the structural loss is below a margin threshold.

## Results

On FedISIC (dermoscopy, 6 sites), FedMAP achieves 73.29% ± 1.29% accuracy and 59.47% ± 1.98% Macro-F1, improving over the strongest text-prompt baseline (PromptFL) by +7.16% accuracy and +19.56% Macro-F1. On FedCamelyon17 (histopathology, 5 hospitals), it reaches 93.15% / 92.99%, surpassing FedAPT by +2.62% / +2.01%. On the private fetal ultrasound dataset (3 hospitals, 13 classes), FedMAP attains 89.60% / 88.04%, outperforming FedAPT by +18.85% / +20.84%. FedMAP also uses only 9.2K trainable parameters and 0.07 MB per communication round, the lightest among all compared visual/multimodal adaptation methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedMAP consistently outperforms all eight baselines across all three benchmarks. Against the strongest text-prompt baseline PromptFL (65.11% avg on FedISIC), FedMAP gains +7.16% accuracy and +19.56% Macro-F1, with the gap widening on the most heterogeneous sites (MSK: +12.29%, RS: +5.35%). Against the best lightweight adaptation method FedAPT (64.95% on FedISIC, 90.53% on FedCamelyon17, 70.75% on Private), FedMAP leads by +8.34%, +2.62%, and +18.85% accuracy respectively, with the largest margin on the ultrasound dataset where class count is highest (13 classes) and manifold collapse is most severe. The OOD-robust method FOCoOP performs worst among trainable baselines (51.53% on FedISIC, 34.99% on Private), suggesting its distribution-level separation strategy is ill-suited to the fine-grained morphological differences in medical imaging. FedMVP, despite using 9.85M parameters (1070× more than FedMAP's 9.2K), still trails FedMAP by 11.31% on FedISIC and 37.88% on Private, confirming that the gain comes from geometric anchoring rather than model capacity. On FedCamelyon17, where all methods achieve >85% accuracy, FedMAP still leads by 2.62% over FedAPT and 4.34% over FedCLIP, and is the only method to exceed 93% on all five hospitals simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant 'textual tuning' paradigm in Federated Prompt Learning (PromptFL, FedAPT, FedMVP), which freezes the visual encoder and adapts only text-side decision boundaries. It builds on Visual Prompt Tuning (Jia et al., 2022) but repurposes it as the primary adaptation mechanism in a federated medical setting, arguing that the frozen CLIP visual geometry is fundamentally distorted for medical images. The key conceptual departure is introducing an LLM-derived semantic codebook as a client-invariant structural reference, combined with two geometric regularizers (Manifold Semantic Anchoring and Topology Structural Alignment) that explicitly restructure the visual feature space rather than passively shifting text boundaries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The geometric diagnosis—formalizing why text-only tuning is intrinsically ill-conditioned when visual features collapse into low-rank subspaces—provides a new lens for understanding FPL failures beyond average accuracy, and the theoretical proof (Theorem A.1) makes this rigorous. The method's extreme lightness (9.2K parameters, 0.07 MB/round) makes it practical for resource-constrained medical deployments. Open questions include the robustness of LLM-generated anchors to domain-specific terminology, whether the codebook can be iteratively refined with clinical feedback, and whether the geometric framing generalizes to other modalities (e.g., 3D volumetric imaging) or supervision regimes beyond classification.
