## Problem

Existing multi-hop QA methods adopt a single reasoning strategy for all question types: LLM-based fact verification excels at parallel fact aggregation but breaks sequential reasoning chains, while KG path-based construction handles chained inference well but suffers exponential path explosion on parallel tasks. This 'strategy-task mismatch'—where the reasoning architecture is misaligned with the question's logical topology—remains unaddressed because no prior framework dynamically classifies and routes questions to type-appropriate processing.

## Input / Output

The system takes a natural-language multi-hop question and the Wikidata knowledge graph as input. A 6-shot LLM prompt first classifies the question into parallel or chained type, then SPARQL queries retrieve candidate triplets from Wikidata. The output is a final answer strictly grounded in the selected KG triplets (ensuring interpretability), evaluated via Exact Match and a BERTScore-based Semantic Match Accuracy. The backbone LLM is Llama 3:8B, with BGE-Large-v1.5 providing embedding vectors for the two-stage hybrid scoring.

## Method

DTKG implements a two-stage pipeline inspired by Dual Process Theory: a lightweight few-shot LLM classifier (emulating 'unconscious processing') routes each question to one of two specialized branches (emulating 'conscious processing'). The parallel branch decomposes the model response into atomic facts, entity-links them to Wikidata QIDs, retrieves candidate triplets, and applies a two-stage hybrid scoring (cosine similarity followed by a reranking model with α=0.75 fusion) to verify or correct each fact independently. The chained branch identifies a central entity, retrieves head and tail relations, then performs depth-first expansion with dynamic pruning (max depth 3, width 3, threshold filtering, and LLM-based relation selection) plus early stopping via an information-sufficiency check. A task-aware denoiser further prunes noise: static keyword filtering removes administrative relations, while an LLM-based reasoning-necessity score discards contextually irrelevant attribute relations specific to each question type.

## Results

DTKG achieves the highest EM and ACC across all six multi-hop QA datasets under the Llama 3:8B backbone, with headline improvements of 5.0%–29.5% over the original baselines. The most striking gain is on MuSiQue, where DTKG reaches 83.0% ACC versus 53.5% for the original model (+29.5%), and on Mintaka where it attains 93.9% ACC, the highest among all methods. On the complex CWQ dataset, DTKG achieves 46.3% EM and 90.0% ACC, surpassing the strongest KG-verified baseline KGR (45.0% EM, 85.0% ACC) by 1.3 and 5.0 percentage points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DTKG outperforms all five baselines on every dataset in both EM and ACC. Against the LLM-centric baselines (COT, CRITIC), DTKG leads by 2.6–13.5 EM points and 2.8–21.5 ACC points, with the largest gap on MuSiQue (83.0 vs. 75.0 ACC for COT; 83.0 vs. 61.5 for CRITIC). Against the KG-verified parallel-centric method KGR, DTKG gains 1.3 EM and 5.0 ACC on CWQ, and 5.3 EM and 1.9 ACC on Mintaka, confirming that dynamic strategy selection beats a fixed fact-verification pipeline. Against the KG path-centric method TOG, DTKG's advantage is most pronounced on parallel-heavy Mintaka (67.6 vs. 58.5 EM, +9.1) and GraphRAG-Bench (87.1 vs. 84.5 ACC), while on QALD10-en the two methods tie in EM (50.0%) but DTKG leads in ACC (85.0 vs. 81.0). In the supplementary cross-baseline comparison on Llama 3.1:8B (Table 10), DTKG also surpasses the SOTA CoRAG method by 1.9 EM / 1.7 F1 on HotpotQA and 2.6 EM / 2.8 F1 on MuSiQue.

## Relation to Prior Work

DTKG carves new ground by synthesizing two previously separate paradigms—LLM-centric post-hoc fact verification (KGR, CRITIC) and KG-centric iterative path construction (ToG)—into a unified dual-track architecture, rather than extending either lineage directly. Its conceptual departure is the introduction of a lightweight few-shot classification stage (framed via Dual Process Theory and Tversky & Kahneman's extensional/intuitive reasoning taxonomy) that dynamically routes questions to the appropriate track, addressing what the authors term 'strategy-task mismatch.' The two-stage hybrid scoring (embedding cosine similarity + reranking) and task-aware denoising are borrowed from retrieval literature but recontextualized with type-specific noise signatures (cross-redundant triplets vs. out-of-chain paths).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, training-free recipe for multi-hop QA that practitioners can adopt with off-the-shelf LLMs and Wikidata, and the ablations convincingly show the classifier is not a cosmetic addition (random routing drops ACC by ~13 points). However, significant open questions remain: hybrid questions requiring both parallel and chained reasoning (14% of errors) are handled suboptimally by forcing a single track, temporal qualifiers in Wikidata are underutilized, and the 91.5% classifier accuracy means a non-trivial misclassification rate that cascades into 22.4% average ACC drops. Future work on multi-stage routing, temporal logic integration, and large-scale set aggregation are identified but unresolved.
