## Problem

Standard Prefill-Decode (PD) disaggregation, the dominant LLM inference architecture, suffers two critical inefficiencies under multi-turn conversations: every follow-up turn forces the prefill node to recompute the entire conversation's KV cache (up to 99% of multi-turn prefill cost) and then transfer it back to the decode node, saturating network bandwidth. The paper identifies that no single fixed routing strategy for these append-prefill operations simultaneously satisfies TTFT, TPOT, and throughput SLOs, motivating a dynamic per-request routing mechanism.

## Input / Output

At inference time, each Turn 2+ request is characterized by a feature tuple (turn number, new-input tokens, expected output tokens, accumulated context length, current system QPS), which is discretized along three axes—context class (≤512, 512–4096, >4096 tokens), workload type (nine input/output ratio categories), and QPS bin—and mapped to a precomputed binary routing decision (x=0: route through P-node with KV transfer; x=1: process locally on D-node using cached prefix) returned in <1 ms. Turn 1 requests always take the PD path since no cached KV exists. The operator-facing control surface is a single weight ratio w_tpot/w_ttft that smoothly interpolates between the x=1 regime (maximum TTFT reduction) and the x=0 baseline (maximum TPOT stability).

## Method

PPD (Prefill Prefill-capable Decode) disaggregation is a two-phase dynamic routing system grounded in the empirical finding that append-prefill causes only ~2% decode TPOT slowdown versus ~48% for full prefill at batch size 200—an order-of-magnitude gap that makes local processing on decode nodes viable. In Phase 1 (offline), the system measures Turn 2 TTFT and TPOT at both routing extremes (x=0 and x=1) across a coarse workload grid and stores the sign of a score S(ψ;π,w) = w_ttft·Δ_ttft − w_tpot·Δ_tpot for each cell. In Phase 2 (online), each Turn 2+ request is snapped to the nearest grid cell and the precomputed decision is returned, routing locally when S>0 and through P otherwise. This formulation makes traditional PD the special case x≡0 and decouples the P:D node ratio (which governs Turn 1 prefill capacity) from the Turn 2+ SLO operating point (selected by operator weights), enabling independent tuning of the two concerns. The prototype builds on vLLM's disaggregated serving infrastructure, reusing its KV transfer protocol and prefix cache with a routing proxy that maintains session tables for conversation-to-server mappings.

## Results

PPD reduces Turn 2+ TTFT by 48–73% on synthetic sweeps and ~68% on real-world workloads (ShareGPT, WildChat) while maintaining competitive TPOT. On the 27-point WildChat evaluation (3 configs × 9 QPS), PPD achieves the most TTFT wins (14/27, surpassing x=1's 13) and the most TPOT wins (12/27, surpassing x=0's 10), while matching x=1's 100% success rate (27/27) versus x=0's 4/27. PPD also resolves service instability: x=0 baselines degrade (SR < 95%) in 13/27 test points, whereas PPD maintains 100% success across all configurations and QPS levels. Under simulated slower interconnects (NVLink → 100GbE), PPD's relative TTFT advantage widens from 64% to 70%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PPD is compared against three static routing baselines: traditional PD (x=0, all Turn 2+ routed through P), Full AP-to-D (x=1, all Turn 2+ processed locally on D), and partial routing (0<x<1). Against x=0, PPD dominates on every metric: it wins TTFT 14-to-0, TPOT 12-to-10, and success rate 27-to-4, with the gap widening under high QPS and slower networks. Against x=1 (the strongest static disaggregated baseline), PPD wins TTFT 14-to-13 and TPOT 12-to-5 while matching its 100% success rate, confirming that dynamic per-request routing strictly improves over the simplest 'always-local' heuristic. Against Replica (4R), PPD trades a small TTFT penalty for large TPOT and throughput gains, since Replica's lack of workload isolation causes prefill-decode interference. The paper also shows that no single fixed x (including x=1/3, 1/2, 2/3) dominates across all objectives simultaneously (92.2% of workload-QPS combinations have different optima for TTFT vs. TPOT), which is the core motivation for PPD's dynamic routing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PD disaggregation architecture (DistServe, Splitwise) to the multi-turn conversation setting, where the standard one-way P→D KV transfer protocol forces redundant recomputation of conversation history on every turn. Rather than adding external KV cache tiers (CachedAttention, Mooncake, MemServe) or relying on prefix caching within a single instance, PPD adjusts the routing decision itself: it exploits the finding that append-prefill (processing only new tokens over cached KV) causes an order-of-magnitude less decode interference than full prefill, making local execution on decode nodes viable. The concurrent work AMPD (He et al., 2026) shares the high-level intuition of routing incremental prefills to decode nodes, but PPD distinguishes itself through a rigorous microarchitectural interference analysis, a formal optimization formulation in which traditional PD is the special case x≡0, and an offline-computed lookup table rather than real-time queue-state estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Multi-turn conversations are the dominant interaction pattern in production chatbots and agentic systems, yet standard PD disaggregation was designed for independent single-turn queries; the ~68% Turn 2+ TTFT reduction and elimination of KV transfer saturation address a real operational bottleneck. The single-knob control surface (w_ttft, w_tpot) gives operators a predictable offline tuning mechanism rather than coupled online parameters, and PPD gracefully recovers the default PD configuration at extreme settings. Key limitations include the offline lookup table's sensitivity to workload drift, evaluation limited to intra-node NVLink (with bandwidth simulation as a proxy for multi-node RDMA), and the absence of closed-loop SLO enforcement. Open questions include integration with higher-level admission controllers, heterogeneous GPU clusters, and joint deployment with distributed KV cache layers.
