## Problem

In LLM post-training under budgeted data acquisition, practitioners must select which auxiliary datasets to include, but commonly used gradient alignment scores treat each dataset independently and ignore redundancy among them. This paper addresses the gap by formulating dataset valuation as a joint subset selection problem that accounts for both target-task alignment and inter-dataset redundancy, motivated by emerging dataset marketplaces where data is acquired as indivisible units under cost constraints.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pretrained model θ₀, a small preview (m samples) from each of N auxiliary datasets, and a target dataset, operating under partial observability where full auxiliary data is unavailable before the acquisition decision. It produces signed, comparative valuation scores w ∈ ℝᴺ (positive for beneficial, negative for harmful transfer) that induce a ranking; the buyer selects the top-k datasets with strictly positive scores. Because N (tens to hundreds) is typically far smaller than the gradient dimensionality d (billions), the Gram-space formulation reduces the problem to an N×N quadratic program, making the computation tractable despite the high-dimensional parameter space.

## Method

The core contribution is a convex quadratic program derived from a second-order Taylor expansion of the target loss after a combined gradient step: minimizing ‖Gw − λg_tar‖² subject to ‖w‖₁ ≤ k, where G stacks auxiliary dataset gradients. In Gram space this becomes min ½wᵀKw − λβᵀw, where the linear term βᵀw rewards alignment with the target gradient and the quadratic term wᵀKw penalizes redundancy among selected datasets (analogous to the risk term in Markowitz portfolio optimization). Unlike the original KMM for covariate shift, the formulation allows signed weights without simplex constraints, enabling representation of negative transfer. The method is agnostic to the gradient representation (one-step gradients or multi-step task vectors) and post-training paradigm (SFT or RL-GRPO), and comes with a finite-sample guarantee showing estimation error scales as Õ(kN/(μ√m)) under bounded gradients and a well-conditioned Gram matrix.

## Results

On SFT (Qwen3-1.7B), One-Step+KMM achieves a Borda count of 29.0 on Danish and 31.0 on Marathi, substantially outperforming the uncorrected One-Step baseline (17.5 and 19.0 respectively). On GRPO (Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, Thai target), One-Step+KMM reaches a Borda of 14 versus 6 for One-Step alone, with best-k MGSM scores of 41.6 vs 39.2. KMM-enhanced methods match DataModel's best-k performance at over 100× lower wall-clock time, and consistently improve over the TV baseline across model scales from 1B to 70B (gains of +0.36 to +2.04 on IFEval).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DataModel (Ilyas et al., 2022), KMM-enhanced methods achieve comparable or superior best-k performance at over 100× lower computational cost: on Danish, One-Step+KMM (Borda 29.0) and DataModel-CS (27.5) both outperform vanilla DataModel (7.0), while on Marathi, DataModel (33.0) narrowly matches TV+KMM (33.5) on Borda but KMM wins on best-k (34.60 vs 34.52). Against GradEX (Li et al., 2024), KMM methods dominate clearly: on Danish, One-Step+KMM (29.0) beats GradEX-FS (24.5) and GradEX-RE (25.0) by 4–5 Borda points, and on Marathi the gap widens dramatically with One-Step+KMM (31.0) and TV+KMM (33.5) outscoring GradEX-FS (13.0) and GradEX-RE (11.5) by roughly 18–22 points. The paper also shows that KMM's advantage is robust across regularization strengths and that valuation scores computed on a 1B model transfer effectively to 70B models, a property neither DataModel nor GradEX provides.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Kernel Mean Matching (KMM), originally developed for covariate shift correction (Gretton et al., 2009), as a dataset-level valuation mechanism in gradient space, dropping the nonnegativity and simplex constraints to allow signed weights that capture negative transfer. It departs from prior data valuation methods (DataModel, GradEX, LESS) that operate at the instance level or target pretraining, by formulating a convex quadratic program over dataset-level gradients that explicitly penalizes redundancy via a Gram-matrix term. The resulting QP structure is shown to be algebraically identical to Markowitz mean-variance portfolio optimization, providing an intuitive interpretation where the linear term rewards target alignment and the quadratic term penalizes correlated (redundant) auxiliary datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a practically deployable tool for budget-constrained post-training: the KMM correction adds negligible computational overhead over gradient baselines (O(N²) for N datasets vs. O(Nd) for d-dimensional gradients), yet consistently improves selection quality across SFT, GRPO, multiple languages, and model scales up to 70B. The cross-model transferability result—valuation scores computed on a 1B model guiding selection for 70B models—suggests practitioners can run the lightweight valuation stage once and reuse it, reducing the cost of data acquisition planning. Open questions include whether the isotropic curvature assumption can be relaxed in practice (the diagonal Fisher variant performed slightly worse), how the method behaves when auxiliary data overwhelms the target signal, and whether the approach extends to non-LoRA full fine-tuning or multi-objective post-training settings.
