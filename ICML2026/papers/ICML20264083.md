## Problem

Learning the structure of a sparse Gaussian graphical model from a single Glauber dynamics trajectory without waiting for the chain to mix is an open problem. In the i.i.d. setting, sublinear-in-n sample guarantees are known but polynomial-time algorithms remain open, and prior work on Glauber trajectories (Tirukkonda et al., 2025) required additional regularity assumptions that essentially implied fast mixing. This paper asks whether structure learning is possible from a single trajectory with no mixing-time dependence and no assumptions beyond sparsity and a minimum edge-strength condition.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a single continuous-time Glauber trajectory (a piecewise-constant process performing n single-site updates per unit time in expectation) of length T = Ω(d³ log(n/δ)/α⁵), along with the sparsity bound d and edge-strength parameter α. It outputs the sparsity pattern (support of the precision matrix) with probability 1−δ, or more precisely a (1±ε)-multiplicative estimate of every entry of the precision matrix. The trajectory need not be stationary at the start and no mixing-time assumption is imposed; the initial state can be arbitrary or worst-case.

## Method

The algorithm has three components. First, it estimates diagonal entries Θ_ii by identifying short 'ii' windows (two i-updates with no neighbor updates between them), where the difference of the two updated values is N(0, 2/Θ_ii), and applies robust variance estimation to handle hidden-neighbor contamination; the trajectory is then rescaled to (approximately) unit diagonal. Second, for each candidate edge (i,j), it searches for 'iiji' windows (i updates in quarters 1, 2, and 4; j updates in quarter 3) rather than the naive 'iji' pattern, because the extra leading i-update acts as a refresh that breaks a subtle dependence between the first i-update's noise and the later j-state, yielding a ratio statistic centered at −Θ′_ij with bounded variance after conditioning on a large j-increment. Third, since the unknown graph makes it impossible to verify window cleanliness, it aggregates many windows using a robust median estimator and proves concentration via martingale inequalities that account for the temporal dependence inherent in a single trajectory.

## Results

The main no-mixing result (Theorem 1.3) recovers the sparsity pattern of a d-sparse GGM from a single Glauber trajectory of length T = Ω(d³ log(n/δ) / α⁵), with no dependence on the mixing time and no additional assumptions beyond the non-degeneracy condition |Θᵢⱼ|/√(ΘᵢᵢΘⱼⱼ) ≥ α. The parameter-learning variant (Theorem 1.4) achieves multiplicative ε-estimation of all precision matrix entries with T = Ω(d³ log(n/δ) / (α⁵ ε⁵)). When mixing is available (Theorem 1.5), the bound improves to T = Ω(log(n/δ)/α² · (t_mix(cα) + d²/α²)), which in the fast-mixing regime reduces to O(d² log(n/δ)/α⁴), a d/α improvement over the no-mixing bound. An information-theoretic lower bound (Theorem E.3) shows Ω(log(n)/α²) continuous-time trajectory length is necessary, confirming the correct logarithmic dependence on n.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The most direct comparison is with Tirukkonda, Rayas, and Dasarathy (ISIT 2025), the first algorithmic guarantees for GGM structure learning from a single Glauber trajectory. This paper improves on that work in two key ways: (1) it removes three additional regularity assumptions (bounded edge strength β_max, bounded marginals, and the sample-decay condition dβ_max < 1−Ω(1)) that in the diagonal-1, d=O(1) regime imply fast mixing via Gershgorin's theorem, and (2) it achieves an improved dependence on log(1/δ). The no-mixing algorithm (Theorem 1.3) is the first to handle arbitrarily slow mixing with only the non-degeneracy condition, at the cost of a d³/α⁵ trajectory-length dependence versus the d²/α⁴ dependence available when mixing is fast. Relative to the i.i.d. setting, the mixing-based algorithm (Theorem 1.5) effectively requires log(n/δ)/α² nearly independent samples, matching the i.i.d. lower bound of Wang et al. (2010). The remaining gap between the no-mixing upper bound (d³/α⁵) and the information-theoretic lower bound (log n/α²) in the polynomial dependence on d and α is identified as an open problem.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the first algorithmic guarantees for GGM structure learning from a single Glauber trajectory (Tirukkonda et al., 2025) by removing all additional regularity assumptions (bounded edge strength, bounded marginals, sample decay) and eliminating dependence on the mixing time entirely. The technical approach draws on the pattern-based window analysis developed in the discrete Glauber-dynamics learning literature (Bresler et al., 2017; Gaitonde et al., 2025a,b) but requires new ideas for the continuous Gaussian setting, particularly the 'iiji' pattern to break a subtle dependence that invalidates the naive 'iji' statistic. The robust median-based aggregation under adversarial corruption, combined with martingale concentration for dependent trajectory samples, is the key methodological contribution that distinguishes this work from both the i.i.d. GGM literature (Misra et al., 2020) and the discrete-model Glauber learning line.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The main result establishes that sparse GGM structure is learnable from a single trajectory even when the mixing time is arbitrarily large, resolving a natural open question and showing that temporal correlation does not fundamentally obstruct structure recovery under sparsity and non-degeneracy alone. However, the authors explicitly note that the trajectory-length constants are too large for any practical application, and the polynomial dependence on d and α (d³/α⁵) leaves a significant gap to the information-theoretic lower bound (log n/α²). Closing this gap, and finding algorithms with practical constants, are identified as important open problems. The improved lower bound (Ω(n log n/α²) single-site updates) also sharpens the understanding of what is fundamentally required.
