## Problem

Existing multimodal biomolecular co-design methods impose either synchronous coupling (all modalities evolve at a shared timestep) or random coupling (independent timesteps per modality), both of which introduce suboptimal training dynamics—high model bias or high target variance, respectively. The temporal coupling schedule between heterogeneous modalities (continuous 3D coordinates vs. discrete sequences) has been treated as a fixed heuristic rather than a learnable degree of freedom, leaving a critical gap in how generative progress is coordinated across modalities with different convergence rates.

## Input / Output

For structure-based drug design, the method takes a protein binding pocket (3D coordinates) and generates a complete ligand molecule with atom types and 3D positions; for unconditional protein co-design, it takes only a target residue length and jointly produces a protein sequence and its 3D backbone structure. The generative process operates on a product manifold with continuous coordinates r ∈ R^{N×3} and discrete node features h ∈ R^{N×K}, where the learned coupling is a monotonic time-warping function γ: [0,1] → [0,1]² that maps a single global progress variable to modality-specific noise levels, enabling asynchronous but coordinated denoising.

## Method

GeoCoupling reformulates multimodal generation as a Temporal Optimal Transport problem, where the coupling schedule γ is a geodesic on the product manifold minimizing cumulative transport cost (the integrated MSE loss along the generative trajectory). A bilevel optimization strategy decouples the problem: the inner loop trains the vector field θ via standard MSE regression under a fixed coupling, while the outer loop searches for the coupling that minimizes the converged loss (equivalently, the conditional variance of the regression target). The outer loop is solved efficiently using a Gaussian Process surrogate over the 2D time-pair cost surface, with dynamic programming to extract the geodesic path, achieving a 56× speedup over brute-force grid search. Training stability is maintained through EMA smoothing on the learned schedule, a trust-region perturbation around the current geodesic, and a mixture policy that blends the optimized coupling with random sampling to prevent distribution shift.

## Results

On structure-based drug design (CrossDock), GeoCoupling achieves the best binding affinity across all Vina metrics (Vina Score −7.16, Vina Min −7.68, Vina Dock −8.32) while maintaining 94.3% PoseBusters validity and the highest synthetic accessibility (SA 0.77). On unconditional protein co-design (PDB-derived), GeoCoupling attains the top co-designability (0.79, i.e., 79% of generated proteins fold back within 2 Å via ESMFold) with competitive structural diversity (1−pairwise TM 0.63) and novelty (max TM 0.83). The learned coupling also demonstrates superior OOD robustness, maintaining >0.6 co-designability at length 500 where MultiFlow drops below 0.3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoCoupling outperforms all synchronous-coupling baselines on binding affinity: it improves Vina Score by 0.61 over the best synchronous method (MolCRAFT, −6.55) and by 2.02 over DrugFlow (−5.12), while also achieving the highest SA (0.77 vs. 0.76 for Pocket2Mol). Against the strongest random-coupling baseline MolPilot (Vina Score −6.88, PB-Valid 95.9%), GeoCoupling gains 0.28 in Vina Score and 0.40 in Vina Dock, at a small cost in PB-Valid (94.3% vs. 95.9%) and scRMSD<2Å (43.1% vs. 46.8% for MolCRAFT). On protein co-design, GeoCoupling achieves the highest co-designability (0.79) versus La-Proteina-tri (0.77) and MultiFlow (0.72), but trails La-Proteina-tri on pLDDT (80.15 vs. 85.32) and MultiFlow on Foldseek cluster diversity (0.48 vs. 0.56). The post-hoc variant (applying the learned schedule to a pre-trained MultiFlow checkpoint) recovers sequence diversity (FS Clusters 0.73) while retaining competitive co-designability (0.74), demonstrating plug-and-play utility. Across OOD protein lengths (≥400 residues), GeoCoupling's advantage widens: it sustains >0.6 co-designability at length 500 where MultiFlow collapses below 0.3.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of multimodal biomolecular co-design (MultiFlow, MolCRAFT, MolPilot) by reframing the temporal coupling between heterogeneous modalities as a learnable geometric object rather than a fixed heuristic. It builds directly on MolPilot's variational lower-bound optimal scheduling (VOS), which performed post-hoc test-time schedule search, by promoting the schedule to a training-time bilevel optimization problem solved via a Gaussian Process surrogate. Conceptually, it departs from the sample-space optimal transport literature (rectified flow, EOT) by operating orthogonally in the temporal domain, unifying synchronous, random, and learned couplings under a single transport-cost minimization over the product manifold.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The model-agnostic bilevel framework offers a drop-in mechanism for any multimodal generative model (diffusion, flow matching, BFN) to discover modality-aware curricula, with negligible inference overhead and a 56× speedup over brute-force schedule search. The consistent 'structure-first' geodesic across both small-molecule and protein design suggests a universal geometric principle that could inform architecture design and curriculum strategies more broadly. Open questions include whether the GP surrogate scales to more than two modalities or highly non-stationary training dynamics, and whether the bilevel approximation (stochastic estimator of the bias-variance ordering) can be tightened with theoretical guarantees beyond the sufficient-capacity assumption.
