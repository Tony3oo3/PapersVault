## Problem

Few-shot anomaly image generation must simultaneously achieve high fidelity to real defect patterns and structural diversity, yet existing fine-tuning-based diffusion methods suffer from distributional misalignment and semantic incoherence, while training-free alternatives incur prohibitive inference latency. A critical gap is the absence of an explicit distributional alignment mechanism that can be optimized without costly human-annotated preference pairs, which standard DPO/RLHF pipelines require.

## Input / Output

The method takes a small reference set of real anomaly images (1/3 per MVTec category, ~12 images) paired with text prompts, and a frozen Stable Diffusion v1-4 backbone. It produces 1,000 synthesized anomaly images per category via DDIM (100 steps) with hierarchical guidance scales (s_text=6.5, s_align=3), and additionally yields a pixel-level anomaly localization map derived by aggregating and upsample the latent-space alignment deviation across timesteps, followed by min-max normalization and a 3×3 Gaussian smooth.

## Method

APO reformulates few-shot anomaly generation as a constrained policy optimization problem where the implicit reward is defined by the difference in denoising error between the policy and reference model on real anomalies, eliminating the need for human-annotated preference pairs. The training loss is a time-weighted sigmoid of the denoising deviation Δ = ‖ε_θ − ε‖² − ‖ε_ref − ε‖², scaled by β_t = −½βλ'_t, which naturally up-weights low-noise timesteps where fine-grained fidelity matters. A Time-Aware Capacity Allocation (TACA) module gates the LoRA rank along the diffusion timeline (k_min=4 at high noise → k_max=32 at low noise) to preserve structural diversity early and enhance fine-grained anomaly detail late. At inference, a hierarchical sampling strategy decomposes the denoising prediction into an unconditional prior, a text-conditioned semantic term, and an anomaly-alignment term, enabling independent control over coherence and defect injection.

## Results

On MVTec AD, APO achieves an average pixel-level AUROC of 99.6% and AP of 87.7% when a U-Net is trained on its generated anomalies, outperforming the best unsupervised detector (DRAEM: 97.7/69.0) and the best supervised detector (PRN: 99.0/78.6). For generation quality, APO attains an average Inception Score of 2.14 and IC-LPIPS of 0.41, surpassing the strongest baseline (AnomalyAny: 2.02/0.33). On the downstream classification task with ResNet-34, APO reaches 72.06% average accuracy, exceeding the best competing generator (AnomalyDiffusion: 70.25%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

APO outperforms all 15 baselines on the primary detection metric (pixel-level AUROC/AP). Against the strongest unsupervised detector DRAEM (97.7/69.0), APO gains +1.9 AUROC and +18.7 AP; against the strongest supervised detector PRN (99.0/78.6), it gains +0.6 AUROC and +9.1 AP. Among generation-method baselines, APO beats AnomalyDiffusion (99.1/81.4), DualAnoDiff (99.1/84.5), SeaS (98.7/83.1), and DFMGAN (90.0/62.7) on both AUROC and AP, with the largest margins on challenging categories like capsule (+13.4 AP over PRN), leather (+22.0 AP), and zipper (+16.1 AP). On generation quality (Table 1), APO achieves the best average IS (2.14 vs. 2.02 for AnomalyAny) and IC-LPIPS (0.41 vs. 0.38 for DualAnoDiff). On ResNet-34 classification (Table 3), APO leads with 72.06% average accuracy versus 70.25% for AnomalyDiffusion and 65.55% for DualAnoDiff, with particularly large gains on zipper (+1.15 pp over DualAnoDiff) and pill (+24.14 pp over SeaS).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) from NLP to diffusion-based anomaly generation, but makes a critical departure: instead of requiring human-annotated preference pairs, it derives implicit preference signals from denoising trajectory deviations on real anomaly samples, eliminating the annotation bottleneck. On the generation side, it builds on the Stable Diffusion latent-space framework with LoRA fine-tuning, but introduces a Time-Aware Capacity Allocation (TACA) mechanism that dynamically modulates LoRA rank along the diffusion timeline—a scheduling strategy absent from prior fine-tuning-based anomaly generators (AnomalyDiffusion, DualAnoDiff, SeaS). The hierarchical sampling strategy at inference also departs from standard classifier-free guidance by decomposing the denoising signal into text-conditioned and anomaly-alignment components with independent scaling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The implicit preference alignment mechanism offers a practical path to distributional alignment in few-shot anomaly synthesis without the costly human annotation that standard DPO/RLHF requires, which is particularly relevant for industrial settings where expert labeling is prohibitively expensive. However, the evaluation is confined to a single benchmark (MVTec AD) with 15 categories, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to more diverse anomaly types (e.g., medical imaging, natural scenes) or to truly zero-shot settings. The TACA mechanism, while effective here, is a relatively simple linear schedule and its interaction with more complex LoRA variants or multi-step fine-tuning remains unexplored. A key open question is whether the denoising-deviation-based preference signal remains informative when the few-shot reference set is extremely small (1-2 samples per category).
