## Problem

Existing task-oriented dialogue systems rely on supervised fine-tuning over static corpora and evaluate success with a monolithic resolution rate, failing to penalize 'false successes' achieved through excessive compensation or protracted dialogue. This leaves a critical gap: no method simultaneously optimizes empathetic communication quality, task resolution, and global operational cost budgets within a single training objective.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261239/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each turn the agent receives the cumulative dialogue history augmented with real-time business signals (order ID, merchant, food items, FAQ type, courier status) and system SOP instructions, and must emit a structured composite action: a chain-of-thought reasoning block in <think> tags, a natural-language reply in <response> tags, and a discrete executable decision (chat or voucher) in <action> tags. The interaction is multi-turn and stochastic, driven by an LLM user simulator conditioned on persona profiles (intrinsic traits and extrinsic demands), and terminates when the user signals satisfaction, explicit refusal, or a 15-turn cap is reached.

## Method

InteractCS-RL reframes TOD as a Constrained MDP and trains the policy via Cost-aware Multi-turn Policy Optimization (CMPO), which extends GRPO with a hybrid advantage that sums three signals: a session-level outcome reward (user satisfaction from the simulator), a turn-level process reward from a generative reward model that scores each response against domain principles on a discrete {0, 0.5, 1} scale, and a dynamic cost penalty on high-expenditure actions (e.g., voucher issuance). The cost penalty coefficient λ is updated by a PID-Lagrangian controller that tracks the gap between the running average voucher rate and a global budget threshold, correcting both instantaneous violations (proportional term) and long-term drift (integral term) to keep the policy on the Pareto boundary. The training environment is a closed-loop User-Centric Interaction Framework where an LLM user simulator, conditioned on a bi-level persona bank (four intrinsic trait dimensions × four extrinsic demand patterns), generates diverse, non-cooperative user behaviors for online exploration rather than relying on static trajectory imitation.

## Results

On the FoodDeliverService Hard scenario, Qwen-2.5-14B trained with InteractCS-RL achieves a user satisfaction of 3.05/5, 100% dialogue finish rate, communication quality of 27.43/28, logic quality of 11.34/12, and a voucher rate of 27.5% (within the <30% budget constraint). This surpasses the strongest closed-source model (LongCat-Flash: Sat. 2.18, V-Rate 93.3%) by ~40% in satisfaction while reducing cost by over 70 percentage points. On τ²-bench, the 14B InteractCS-RL model improves average Pass@1 by 5.6% over SFT across Retail (47.4% vs 43.9%), Airline (28.0% vs 20.0%), and Telecom (24.6% vs 20.2%) domains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InteractCS-RL (14B) decisively outperforms all closed-source LLMs on the Hard FDS scenario: it beats LongCat-Flash (Sat. 3.05 vs 2.18, V-Rate 27.5% vs 93.3%), GPT-4.1 (Sat. 3.05 vs 1.91, FR 100% vs 83.8%), DeepSeek-v3.2 (Sat. 3.05 vs 1.96, V-Rate 27.5% vs 76.7%), and Qwen3-235B (Sat. 3.05 vs 1.73, FR 100% vs 62.5%), with the most dramatic gap in cost control—every large model exceeds the 30% voucher budget by 40+ percentage points while InteractCS-RL stays within it. Against the SFT baseline (Qwen-2.5-14B-SFT), InteractCS-RL improves satisfaction by 0.90 points (3.05 vs 2.15), communication by 2.80 points, logic by 1.23 points, and reduces voucher rate from 45.0% to 27.5%. Among RL baselines on the 7B model, InteractCS-RL (CMPO) beats token-level PPO (Sat. 2.74 vs 2.29), session-level GRPO (2.74 vs 2.67), and turn-level CAPO (2.74 vs 2.64) on satisfaction while also achieving the lowest voucher rate (30.8% vs 31.7%, 31.2%, 32.4%). On τ²-bench, InteractCS-RL (14B) improves Pass@1 over SFT by +3.5% (Retail), +8.0% (Airline), and +4.4% (Telecom), and over the base model by +2.7%, +12.0%, and +7.1% respectively, confirming cross-domain generalizability without domain-specific training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) as its policy optimization backbone and on PID-Lagrangian methods (Stooke et al., 2020) for constraint enforcement, but departs from both in important ways. Rather than applying GRPO with sparse terminal rewards as in standard RLVR settings, it introduces a hybrid advantage that fuses session-level outcome utility, turn-level generative process credits (via GenRM), and a dynamically regulated cost penalty. It also departs from prior TOD work that relies on static SFT corpora or offline RL by constructing a closed-loop interactive training environment with persona-driven user simulators, and from prior safe-RLHF literature by treating operational budget (voucher issuance rate) as the constrained resource rather than content safety.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical gap: most TOD systems optimize for resolution rate while ignoring the economic cost of achieving it, leading to 'false successes' through excessive compensation. The PID-Lagrangian cost controller and the persona-driven interaction gym together offer a reusable recipe for training cost-aware service agents, and the cross-domain transfer to τ²-bench (Retail, Airline, Telecom) without domain-specific data suggests the learned reasoning is not merely pattern-matching. Open questions include how the framework scales to multi-constraint settings (e.g., simultaneous budget, latency, and fairness constraints), whether the LLM-based user simulator introduces systematic biases that limit the learned policy, and whether the relatively simple binary cost signal (voucher vs. no voucher) generalizes to richer cost structures in other service domains.
