## Problem

Existing end-to-end learning-based primal heuristics (E2EPH) for integer linear programming cannot independently generate feasible solutions and instead delegate feasibility to external solvers, while also being limited to binary variables. This paper addresses the gap by proposing the first E2EPH that independently produces feasible solutions for general ILP (including non-binary integers), grounded in a theoretical reward-feasibility alignment.

## Input / Output

The method takes an ILP instance (constraint matrix A, objective vector c, RHS vector b, variable bounds) and an initial solution from either LP-relaxation or random assignment. At each timestep, only a subset of ñ = 2⌈log₂n⌉ variables is selected as changeable via a feasibility-aware strategy, and the agent outputs discrete actions (+1, 0, −1) for each. The final output is the best feasible incumbent solution x_b and its objective value, updated whenever a new feasible solution improves upon the current incumbent.

## Method

RL-SPH frames ILP solving as an actor-critic RL problem where the agent (ILP-GT, a Graph Transformer with periodic embeddings for variable values and reward context tokens) learns to adjust variable values step-by-step. A two-phase reward design enforces strict priority—variable bounds over linear constraints over objective value—with Proposition 1 providing a theoretical guarantee that reward maximization from an infeasible state necessarily reaches feasibility in finite steps. A feasibility-aware search strategy selects seed variables from violated constraints and their co-occurring neighbors to focus the agent's attention, reducing the effective search space. The architecture uses phase-separated actor/critic layers, equilibration scaling for structural information, and a heavy exploration penalty to prevent stagnation, achieving 100% feasibility across five benchmarks with a single hyperparameter configuration.

## Results

RL-SPH achieves a 100% feasibility rate (FR) across all five benchmarks (IS, CA, SC, MVC, NBI), matching only the Rounding Heuristic Family (RHF) among baselines. Compared to the 100%-FR baselines, RL-SPH(LP) attains a 28.6× lower primal gap (PG), a 2.6× lower primal integral (PI), and a 2.5× faster first-feasible time (FT) on average. On the non-binary integer (NBI) benchmark, RL-SPH(LP) achieves a PG of 0.22% and PI of 11.3, outperforming all other methods except RHF's PG of 0.00% (where RL-SPH's PI is comparable at 11.3 vs 11.3). The LP-free variant RL-SPH(Random) finds the first feasible solution within 2 seconds on all datasets, a 180× average speedup in FT over 100%-FR baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the four classical SPH baselines (DHF, FP, RHF, RENS), RL-SPH(LP) dominates on PG and PI across nearly all datasets: e.g., on IS it achieves PG 0.14% vs RHF's 18.09% and FP's 20.60%, and on MVC it reaches PG 0.81% vs RHF's 8.01%. The only exception is NBI, where RHF attains PG 0.00% (perfect) while RL-SPH(LP) records 0.22%, though their PI values are identical (11.3). Against the three E2EPH baselines (PAS, DDIM, DiffILO), RL-SPH wins decisively on feasibility: PAS achieves 0% FR on CA and IS, DiffILO achieves 0% FR on SC and CA, and DDIM reaches only 99% FR on SC, whereas RL-SPH maintains 100% FR everywhere. On solution quality where all methods find feasible solutions, RL-SPH(LP) outperforms DDIM by a wide margin (e.g., PG 3.82% vs 22.15% on CA; PI 21.9 vs 71.5 on CA) and also beats RHF on PG for IS, CA, SC, and MVC. The LP-free variant RL-SPH(Random) further demonstrates that feasibility can be achieved without any LP solve, finding the first feasible solution in under 2 seconds on all datasets—a 180× speedup in FT over the 100%-FR classical baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

RL-SPH extends the actor-critic local rewriting paradigm of NeuRewriter (Chen & Tian, 2019) from improving already-feasible solutions to navigating from infeasible to feasible, directly realizing a vision that prior work identified but did not address. It departs from the dominant E2EPH lineage (PAS, DDIM, DiffILO) that delegates feasibility to external ILP solvers, instead positioning itself as a standalone start primal heuristic with a theoretically grounded reward-feasibility alignment. Architecturally, it replaces the MPNN-based encoders common in prior E2EPH work with a Graph Transformer (ILP-GT) to capture long-range variable dependencies, and introduces a feasibility-aware variable selection strategy that borrows concepts from RENS and LNS but adapts them for the no-initial-feasible-solution setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first E2EPH method to independently achieve a 100% feasibility rate across multiple benchmarks including non-binary integer variables, resolving a gap that supervised E2EPH methods (which require near-optimal labels) and prior RL-free approaches have left open. The conditional feasibility guarantee (Proposition 1) provides theoretical grounding absent from prior heuristic-based approaches. However, the paper explicitly scopes its contribution to feasibility rather than optimality, leaving open whether the same RL framework can be extended to competitive optimality gaps. The consistent hyperparameter configuration across five diverse benchmarks and generalization to unseen MIPLIB instances suggest practical utility, but scaling to very large ILPs (beyond ~4,000 variables) and integration into full solver pipelines remain to be validated.
