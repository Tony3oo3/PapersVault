## Problem

Single-step adversarial training methods suffer from Catastrophic Overfitting (CO), where robustness to multi-step attacks collapses to near zero while single-step accuracy paradoxically rises. The paper identifies that fixed perturbation magnitudes and directions cause a previously unrecognized failure mode—Epsilon Overfitting—and that no existing method prevents CO with a single fixed set of hyperparameters across diverse datasets and architectures, undermining the practical goal of fast adversarial training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264427/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images at various resolutions (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for TinyImageNet, 224×224 for ImageNet-100, 28×28 for PathMNIST/TissueMNIST) and outputs class labels via cross-entropy. During training, each input is perturbed with a random start η ~ U(−ε,+ε)^d followed by an element-wise step-size sampled from [0, α*] in the sign-gradient direction, with the resulting adversarial example projected back to the valid pixel range [0,1]. The perturbation budget is ε = 8/255 in the ℓ∞ norm, and the adaptive step-size α* is computed from the previous batch's backpropagation gradient, introducing a one-batch temporal lag.

## Method

SORA derives an optimal attack step-size by locally approximating the loss landscape with a quadratic model and solving for the stationary point along the sign-gradient direction, yielding α* = α₀ / (1 − pᵀg′/‖g‖₁), where g′ is the backpropagation gradient on the adversarial example. This formula is applied with a one-batch lag (exploiting second-order information for free, analogous to Free AT's temporal gap) and stabilized via an exponential moving average of the linearity coefficient. To prevent Epsilon Overfitting, per-pixel step-sizes are sampled uniformly from [0, α*], introducing perturbation magnitude diversity that avoids the model memorizing a single perturbation scale. The paper also introduces PertAlign, a zero-overhead metric measuring cosine similarity between the attack gradient and the backprop gradient, which theoretically captures the Hessian component orthogonal to the gradient and reliably predicts CO onset before single-step and PGD accuracies diverge.

## Results

On PATHMNIST with PreActResNet-18 (ε=8/255), SORA achieves 84.88% clean accuracy and 35.54% AutoAttack robust accuracy, dramatically outperforming the strongest single-step baselines N-FGSM (74.86% clean, 1.90% AA) and AAER (81.43% clean, 1.89% AA). On ImageNet-100, SORA reaches 57.26% clean and 18.56% AA, again surpassing N-FGSM (49.38%, 15.52%) and AAER (48.26%, 17.18%). Across all six datasets and four architecture families, SORA is the only fast method that consistently matches or exceeds the best-performing method in each setting while maintaining higher clean accuracy than competitors with comparable robustness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SORA decisively outperforms all single-step baselines on the challenging medical datasets: on PATHMNIST it achieves 35.54% AutoAttack accuracy versus 1.90% for N-FGSM and 1.89% for AAER, a gap of over 33 percentage points. On ImageNet-100, SORA leads N-FGSM by 3.04% AA and AAER by 1.38% AA while also achieving the highest clean accuracy (57.26% vs. 49.38% and 48.26%). Against standard-benchmark methods like FGSM-RS, GradAlign, Free AT, NuAT, ZeroGrad/MultiGrad, ATAS, and ELLE, SORA consistently attains the highest relative AutoAttack accuracy across all dataset-architecture combinations in Figure 6, whereas several of these methods suffer catastrophic overfitting (near-0% robust accuracy) on PATHMNIST and TissueMNIST. SORA also matches or approaches the multi-step upper bounds (PGD-10, TRADES) with negligible additional computational or memory overhead compared to other fast methods. The ablation (Table 2) confirms that removing the optimal step-size selection collapses PGD-10 accuracy from 45.56% to 17.23% on PATHMNIST, underscoring that the second-order adaptation—not merely randomization—is what enables SORA's advantage over noise-based baselines like N-FGSM.

## Relation to Prior Work

SORA sits squarely within the Fast Adversarial Training (FAT) paradigm initiated by Wong et al. (2020) and borrows the temporal-gap trick from Free AT (Shafahi et al., 2019) to exploit second-order information at zero additional cost. It departs from prior CO-mitigation strategies—randomization (N-FGSM), regularization (GradAlign, ELLE, AAER), and heuristic adaptive step-sizes (ATAS, ZeroGrad)—by grounding step-size selection in a second-order Taylor approximation of the loss landscape, formalized through the new Epsilon Overfitting (EO) perspective. PertAlign, while structurally similar to GradAlign, is repositioned from a training regularizer to a zero-overhead monitoring metric, and the optimal step-size formula (Lemma 5.1) provides a Newton-like justification absent in prior adaptive methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that SORA is the first single-step method to maintain a single fixed hyperparameter set across six datasets and four architectures without CO, addressing a key deployment barrier in FAT. PertAlign offers a free, theoretically grounded early-warning signal for CO that practitioners can plug into any existing single-step pipeline. Open questions include whether the quadratic approximation remains valid under very large perturbations or in the regime of very deep/wide networks, and whether the temporal-gap assumption (weights change negligibly between batches) holds under aggressive learning-rate schedules. The paper also acknowledges that evaluation on medical imaging (PathMNIST, TissueMNIST), while valuable, involves small datasets that may not fully reflect the challenges of large-scale deployment.
