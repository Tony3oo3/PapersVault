## Problem

Standard draft model training for speculative decoding minimizes KL divergence as a proxy for acceptance rate, but capacity-limited draft models (1–5% of target parameters) converge to suboptimal solutions where minimizing KL does not guarantee maximizing acceptance rate. While both objectives share the same global optimum, the gap between proxy and direct optimization becomes significant precisely in the regime where draft models operate, leaving a largely unexplored direction for directly optimizing acceptance.

## Input / Output

The draft model consumes the input text context along with hidden states from intermediate layers of the frozen target model (fused as input features in EAGLE-3-style architectures) and produces a chain of K draft tokens (K=6 or 7) that are verified sequentially by the target model in a single parallel forward pass. The draft operates in the target model's hidden state space with a truncated vocabulary (e.g., 64k tokens), and the output is a fixed-length token sequence where each position's acceptance probability is independently optimized during training.

## Method

The paper proposes two LK loss variants that directly target acceptance rate rather than KL divergence. The likelihood-based loss LK^α minimizes negative log-acceptance rate (−log Σ min(p(x),q(x))), which gradient analysis shows is equivalent to TV optimization with an automatic 1/α scaling factor that resolves TV's vanishing-gradient problem at random initialization (restoring O(1/√k) magnitude). The hybrid loss LK^λ blends KL and TV with an adaptive schedule λ = exp(−η·sg[α]) that shifts from KL-dominated (smooth gradients) early in training to TV-dominated (direct acceptance optimization) as alignment improves, analogous to trust-region methods. Gradient analysis reveals that pure TV gradients scale as O(√k/V) for large vocabularies, making them impractical from random initialization, while the hybrid and likelihood formulations overcome this by leveraging KL's well-conditioned gradients to navigate into the trust region before TV takes over.

## Results

Across six target models (8B–685B parameters) and three benchmarks (MT-Bench, HumanEval, GSM8K), the hybrid LK loss (L_K^λ, η=3) consistently outperforms the standard KL baseline in average acceptance length τ. Under stochastic sampling (T=1), gains range from +3.5% (Llama-3.3-70B) to +8.2% (Qwen3-235B-A22B), with the largest improvement of +7.7% on GPT-OSS-120B. Under greedy decoding (T=0), gains are smaller but still positive, ranging from +0.5% to +1.8%. For DeepSeek-V3's native MTP module, LK fine-tuning adds an additional +5.6% over KL fine-tuning under stochastic sampling, pushing mean τ from 4.43 to 4.68.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against public EAGLE-3 HuggingFace checkpoints (RedHatAI, yuhuili, zhuyksir), the LK-trained models achieve substantially higher acceptance lengths across all target models. For example, on Llama-3.1-8B at T=1, the best public checkpoint (ZK-8B) reaches mean τ ≈ 3.46, while the LK-trained EAGLE-3 achieves 4.01 (+16%); on Qwen3-235B, the public RH-235B checkpoint scores mean τ ≈ 3.31 at T=1 versus 4.08 for LK-trained (+23%). Against the original DeepSeek-V3 MTP module (which was not explicitly trained for multi-token speculation), LK fine-tuning improves mean τ from 3.09 to 4.68 at T=1 (+51%), far exceeding the KL fine-tuning result of 4.43. The paper also shows that within the LK family, the adaptive hybrid objective (η=3) outperforms both the likelihood-based L_K^α loss and fixed-weight (λ=0.5) mixing, while pure TV loss performs substantially worse than KL due to vanishing gradients from random initialization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the speculative decoding framework of Leviathan et al. (2023), which established the draft-then-verify paradigm and the exact relationship α = 1 − TV(p,q) between acceptance rate and total variation distance. It departs from the dominant knowledge-distillation framing (KL or cross-entropy as training objective) by arguing that at the suboptimal solutions capacity-limited drafts actually reach, KL minimization does not guarantee acceptance-rate maximization. The closest prior work, DistillSpec (Zhou et al., 2024), explored TV distance for pretrained external speculators, while AdaSPEC (Hu et al., 2025) addressed selective distillation for standalone drafters; this paper instead targets native draft modules trained from scratch and provides the gradient-level analysis explaining why pure TV is impractical in that regime, motivating the hybrid KL+TV objective with an adaptive trust-region-style schedule.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LK losses are a zero-overhead, drop-in replacement for KL in any existing speculator training pipeline, and the consistent 3–10% gains across six target models (8B–685B) and four draft architectures make them practically attractive for production inference stacks. The observation that low-capacity architectures (MEDUSA, MLP) benefit roughly twice as much as the more powerful EAGLE-3 aligns with the theory and suggests the technique is most valuable precisely where draft models are most constrained. Open questions include whether per-position acceptance improvements transfer to tree-based or block-verification inference schemes, how training-data diversity interacts with epoch count, and whether directly optimizing the end-to-end acceptance length τ (rather than per-position α) yields further gains.
