## Problem

Objective evaluation of Bayesian imaging models—selecting among competing likelihoods or data-driven priors and diagnosing misspecification—requires ground truth or multiple independent measurements of the same scene, neither of which is available in practice. Existing unsupervised approaches such as marginal likelihood estimation via nested sampling are computationally prohibitive in high dimensions and incompatible with modern implicit priors encoded by diffusion or plug-and-play models, while Bayesian cross-validation, the natural remedy, has remained unexplored in imaging because it presupposes access to multiple data points.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single noisy measurement y ∈ ℝ^m, the forward operator A, and noise model parameters (e.g., Gaussian variance or Poisson scaling), with no access to the underlying image x⋆. It produces a scalar score per candidate model (lower is better for the likelihood-based rule, lower is better for the posterior-based rule) that can be compared across models to yield a ranking or thresholded against a reference distribution to produce a binary OOD detection decision. The splitting parameter α ∈ (0,1) and the number of noise realizations K and posterior samples N per split control the accuracy–cost trade-off; typical settings use K=10 and N=20–100 samples.

## Method

The core mechanism is a Bayesian cross-validation procedure enabled by data fission: a single measurement y is split into two conditionally independent noisier measurements y⁺ = y + c_α w and y⁻ = y − w/c_α (with c_α = √(α/(1−α)) and w ~ N(0,Σ)), so the model is fit to y⁻ and its predictive performance is evaluated on y⁺, with the expectation over w playing the role of randomization in K-fold CV. Two scoring rules are proposed: a likelihood-based rule Φ¹ that averages the log-likelihood of y⁺ under posterior samples from x|y⁻, and a posterior-based rule Φ² that measures the average embedding-space discrepancy (LPIPS, DINOv2, CLIP, or DreamSim) between posterior samples drawn from x|y⁺ and x|y⁻, making it sensitive to prior misspecification even when the likelihood is uninformative. Proposition 1 establishes that as α→0⁺, the likelihood-based formulation converges to the marginal likelihood p(y), positioning the method as a computationally tractable relaxation of the gold-standard Bayes factor. The approach is sampler-agnostic and integrates directly with Langevin, SK-ROCK, DiffPIR, and other modern Bayesian imaging samplers, requiring only posterior sampling from the two split measurements.

## Results

For likelihood-based model selection (blur kernel identification), the proposed method achieves 86.7% single-shot accuracy and 100% few-shot accuracy, substantially outperforming residual-based baselines (40–60%). For OOD detection on natural image deblurring, the posterior-based estimator Φ̂² achieves Type I error rates of 0–6.7% (targeting 5%) and detection power of 100% for strong OOD and 60–87% for moderate OOD across blur levels σ_κ ∈ {0.5, 2, 5}. On MRI reconstruction (brain vs. knee), both estimators achieve 94–100% power with 0–6% Type I error at acceleration factors R = 4 and R = 8. Model rankings from the proposed estimators match those of the Bayesian log evidence computed via ProxNest nested sampling on galaxy denoising.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Bayesian residual method of Vidal et al. (2021), the proposed Φ̂¹ estimator more than doubles single-shot accuracy (86.7% vs. 40.0%) and achieves perfect few-shot accuracy (100% vs. 40.0%), a gap that persists even though the baseline benefits from automatic parameter calibration. The empirical Bayesian variant (Vidal et al., 2020) narrows the gap somewhat (46.7% single-shot, 60.0% few-shot) but still trails the proposed method by 40+ percentage points in the few-shot setting. Against ProxNest (McEwen et al., 2023), which estimates the full Bayesian marginal likelihood via nested sampling, the proposed estimators reproduce the same model ranking (DRUNet > DnCNN > Wavelet) on galaxy denoising, validating the approach while requiring orders of magnitude fewer posterior samples (210 total samples vs. the thousands needed by nested sampling). The key practical advantage is that the proposed method is compatible with any modern sampler (DiffPIR, SK-ROCK, PnP-Langevin) and operates from a single measurement, whereas ProxNest is computationally expensive and difficult to scale to high-dimensional imaging problems.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines two previously independent ideas—data fission (Leiner et al., 2025; Pang et al., 2021), a noise-injection technique for splitting a single measurement into conditionally independent halves, and Bayesian cross-validation (Vehtari & Ojanen, 2012), a partial predictive framework for model assessment—into a unified methodology for imaging inverse problems. It departs from the standard Bayesian model selection literature, which relies on the marginal likelihood (computationally intractable in high dimensions) or posterior predictive checks (overly optimistic due to data reuse), by introducing a randomized hold-out mechanism that works from a single observation. The approach is also positioned against existing OOD detection methods for diffusion models (Shoushtari et al., 2025; Graham et al., 2023), which require multiple measurements or span the full signal space, whereas this method operates on a single measurement and is agnostic to the sampler or prior class.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in quantitative and scientific imaging: the inability to objectively validate or compare Bayesian models (especially those with data-driven priors) when ground truth is unavailable, which is the norm in clinical and scientific settings. The demonstrated compatibility with modern samplers (DiffPIR, SK-ROCK) and the low computational cost (K=10 splits, N=20 samples) make it a deployable diagnostic tool rather than a purely theoretical construct. Open questions include the optimal choice of α in practice, the behavior under more complex or correlated noise models, whether the method extends to 3D volumetric imaging, and how to formally combine multiple scoring rules or embeddings into a single decision criterion.
