## Problem

Post-training of flow matching models to align with a high-quality target distribution is equivalent to imitation learning, but existing approaches have critical limitations: Supervised Fine-Tuning (behavioral cloning) suffers from compounding distribution shift in unseen states, while preference optimization methods (RLHF, DPO) require costly preference pairs or reward models that are difficult to obtain at scale. This paper asks whether adversarial imitation learning can provide a reward-free, pair-free alignment signal that avoids both the drift of SFT and the data overhead of preference methods.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a base flow matching model (FLUX.1-dev), a set of 13,000 text prompts, and a single expert demonstration image per prompt generated by Gemini 3 Pro at 512×512 resolution. It produces a fine-tuned flow matching policy whose output distribution matches the expert, evaluated on prompt-following (UniGen-Bench, DPG-Bench) and aesthetic alignment (HPSv3, UnifiedReward) benchmarks. The framework also extends to discrete autoregressive image generation (Xomni) and video generation (Wan2.1) without architectural modification, using the same single-expert-per-prompt constraint.

## Method

FAIL formulates post-training as a GAN-style minimax game between a flow matching policy π_θ and a discriminator D_ω, minimizing Jensen-Shannon divergence between policy and expert distributions without explicit rewards. Two gradient estimation strategies are derived: FAIL-PD exploits the differentiability of the ODE solver to backpropagate the discriminator's signal through a single-step denoising approximation (avoiding full trajectory unrolling), yielding low-variance pathwise gradients analogous to DDPG; FAIL-PG treats the discriminator output as a scalar reward and uses Flow Policy Optimization (FPO) with a CFM-loss-based likelihood ratio and PPO-style clipped updates, making it applicable to discrete or black-box settings. The discriminator is initialized from a pre-trained VLM (Qwen3-VL-2B), VFM (DINOv3), or the flow model itself, and training is stabilized via hybrid imitation (mixing expert and policy samples), discriminator warmup, and KL-constrained policy updates.

## Results

Fine-tuning FLUX.1 Dev with only 13,000 Gemini 3 Pro demonstrations, FAIL-PD achieves 73.70 on UniGen-Bench (vs. 61.30 baseline) and 87.32 on DPG-Bench (vs. 83.84 baseline), surpassing closed-source models like FLUX.1 Ultra (70.67) and Imagen-3.0 (71.85) on prompt following. On aesthetic alignment (HPDv3), FAIL-PD reaches 11.28 overall, outperforming the FLUX.1 Dev baseline (10.43) and all other open-source models. FAIL-PG converges faster (reaching competitive scores in 400 steps) but suffers from mode collapse beyond ~450 steps, while FAIL-PD maintains monotonic improvement to 2,000+ steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the FLUX.1 Dev base model, FAIL-PD improves UniGen-Bench from 61.30 to 73.70 (+12.4) and DPG-Bench from 83.84 to 87.32 (+3.5), a substantial gain from only 13K single-sample demonstrations. FAIL-PD outperforms FLUX.1 Ultra (70.67) and Imagen-3.0 (71.85) on UniGen-Bench, but trails the strongest frontier models—Qwen-Image (78.81) and Seedream 3.0 (78.98)—by roughly 5 points, with the largest gap in the Text rendering sub-category (52.87 vs. 76.14 for Qwen-Image), which the authors attribute to limitations in the base model's pre-training. Against preference-optimization baselines using the same expert data, FAIL-PG (64.75 UniGen at 400 steps) beats Online DPO (62.83) and both RLHF variants (which degrade after 50 steps due to reward hacking). On DPG-Bench, FAIL-PD (87.32) approaches Seedream 3.0 (88.27) and Qwen-Image (88.32), confirming competitive zero-shot generalization. When combined with HPSv3 reward optimization, FAIL-PD acts as a regularizer that prevents reward hacking, achieving the best joint score (HPSv3 12.94, UnifiedReward 3.4832) versus standalone reward gradient (HPSv3 12.21, UnifiedReward 3.4254).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Adversarial Imitation Learning (GAIL, Ho & Ermon 2016) — originally developed for discrete MDPs in robotics — as the post-training paradigm for continuous-time flow matching models, arguing that SFT is Behavioral Cloning and preference optimization is IRL, with AIL as the natural middle ground that avoids both distribution shift and reward modeling. The key technical departure is exploiting the differentiability of the ODE solver to compute pathwise derivatives (FAIL-PD), which has no direct analogue in standard GAIL where environment dynamics are unknown. For the policy gradient variant, the paper substitutes TRPO with Flow Policy Optimization (FPO, McAllister et al. 2025) and GRPO-style advantage normalization, adapting the GAIL recipe to the flow matching likelihood structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FAIL offers a practical alternative to DPO/RLHF that requires only single-sample expert demonstrations (13K here) rather than preference pairs or trained reward models, lowering the data barrier for post-training alignment. Its demonstrated utility as a regularizer against reward hacking when combined with explicit reward optimization addresses a persistent pain point in RLHF for generative models. Open questions include scaling beyond 13K demonstrations, whether the adversarial training instability can be further mitigated for longer horizons, and whether the framework's effectiveness depends on the quality gap between base and expert models. The persistent text-rendering gap (52.87 vs. 76.14) underscores that alignment methods refine but cannot synthesize capabilities absent from pre-training.
