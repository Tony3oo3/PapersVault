## Problem

Hyper-Connections (HC) expand the residual stream width and diversify connectivity to improve performance, but the unconstrained residual mapping H_res breaks the identity mapping property across layers, causing unbounded signal amplification (Amax Gain Magnitude peaks of ~3000), training instability, and restricted scalability. Additionally, the widened residual stream introduces significant memory access overhead that degrades training throughput.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard text token sequences as input and produces next-token distributions for language model pretraining. Internally, the residual stream is expanded from C dimensions to n×C dimensions (n=4), so each layer operates on an n×C hidden matrix representing n parallel streams. The initial multi-stream input is constructed by replicating the token embedding n times along the stream dimension, and the layer input is a C-dimensional vector obtained by a learned weighted combination of the n streams.

## Method

mHC projects the residual mapping H_res onto the Birkhoff polytope (the manifold of doubly stochastic matrices) using the Sinkhorn-Knopp algorithm (20 iterations), ensuring norm preservation, compositional closure under matrix multiplication, and convex-combination signal propagation that conserves feature means across arbitrary depths. Non-negativity constraints via sigmoid are additionally imposed on H_pre and H_post to prevent signal cancellation. The dynamic mappings are computed from the full nC-dimensional flattened input rather than per-stream, enabling cross-stream context in coefficient calculation. Infrastructure optimizations include aggressive kernel fusion (reducing memory reads by 2× and writes by 4×), selective recomputation with analytically optimal block size L_r* ≈ √(nL/(n+2)), and extended DualPipe scheduling to overlap pipeline communication with mHC recomputation, achieving only 6.7% additional training overhead at n=4.

## Results

On the 27B MoE model, mHC achieves consistent improvements across all 8 downstream benchmarks, with headline gains of +7.2 EM on BBH (51.0 vs. 43.8 baseline), +6.9 F1 on DROP (53.9 vs. 47.0), and +4.4 Acc. on MMLU (63.4 vs. 59.0). Compared to HC, mHC further improves BBH by 2.1 points and DROP by 2.3 points, while matching HC on MATH (26.0 EM). In terms of training stability, mHC achieves a final loss reduction of 0.021 over the baseline and avoids the loss surge observed in HC around step 12k, with only 6.7% additional training time overhead at expansion rate n=4.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares mHC against two baselines: the standard residual connection (Baseline) and the unconstrained Hyper-Connections (HC). Against the Baseline, mHC wins on all 8 benchmarks, with the largest gains on BBH (+7.2 EM), DROP (+6.9 F1), and MMLU (+4.4 Acc.), confirming that the multi-stream residual design provides substantial representational benefits. Against HC, mHC wins on 7 out of 8 benchmarks (BBH +2.1, DROP +2.3, GSM8K +0.6, HellaSwag +0.4, MMLU +0.4, PIQA +0.6, TriviaQA +1.3) and ties on MATH (26.0 EM). The paper attributes mHC's advantage over HC to the doubly stochastic constraint restoring the identity mapping property, which eliminates the signal amplification (Amax Gain Magnitude peaks of ~3000 in HC vs. ~1.6 in mHC) and the associated training instability (HC exhibits a loss surge around step 12k that mHC avoids). Scaling experiments across 3B, 9B, and 27B parameters confirm that mHC's performance advantage over the baseline is maintained at higher compute budgets with only marginal attenuation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Hyper-Connections (HC, Zhu et al., ICLR 2025), which expanded the residual stream width and introduced learnable mixing matrices (H_pre, H_post, H_res) to increase topological complexity without changing per-unit FLOPs. The key departure is that HC's unconstrained H_res breaks the identity mapping property across layers, causing signal amplification (Amax gain up to 3000×) and training instability at scale. mHC restores this property by projecting H_res onto the Birkhoff polytope of doubly stochastic matrices via the Sinkhorn-Knopp algorithm, a classical optimization tool repurposed here as a per-layer architectural constraint. The infrastructure layer builds on DeepSeek-V3's MoE architecture, the DualPipe pipeline schedule, and TileLang for fused mixed-precision kernels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

mHC provides a principled, minimal constraint that makes multi-stream residual architectures trainable at scale (27B parameters) with only 6.7% overhead, effectively introducing residual stream width as a practical new scaling dimension alongside FLOPs and data size. The compositional closure property of doubly stochastic matrices guarantees stability at arbitrary depth without per-layer tuning, which is critical for very deep models. Open questions include whether other manifold constraints (beyond doubly stochastic) could better balance plasticity and stability, whether the geometric structure of individual streams (shown to be highly heterogeneous) can be exploited for further gains, and how the framework interacts with other architectural innovations like MoE routing or attention variants at even larger scales.
