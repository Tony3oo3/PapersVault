## Problem

Dynamic facility location with non-uniform opening costs in high-dimensional Euclidean spaces had no known efficient algorithm: prior work either handled uniform costs in high dimensions or non-uniform costs in general metric spaces, but the invariant structure from the general metric approach incurs an unavoidable Ω(m) per-client overhead that is prohibitively expensive in the Euclidean setting. This paper closes that gap by providing the first efficient dynamic algorithm for non-uniform facility location in high-dimensional Euclidean spaces with logarithmic recourse.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a fixed set of m facilities (each with a distinct opening cost polynomially bounded by m²) and a dynamic set of n clients in d-dimensional Euclidean space, processing a sequence of client insertions and deletions. It maintains a solution consisting of a subset of open facilities and a client-to-facility assignment, guaranteeing a γ-approximation ratio, O(log m) amortized recourse (counting both client reassignments and facility open/close events), and poly(d)·(m+n)^{O(1/γ)} amortized update time against an adaptive adversary. The facility set never changes; only the client set evolves over time.

## Method

The algorithm dynamizes the static greedy facility location algorithm by maintaining a hierarchical partition of satellite and regular clusters across O(log m) levels, governed by five invariants derived from the greedy process. Crucially, it removes a problematic invariant from the general metric space approach (which required checking all m facilities per client move) and instead allows multiple clusters to share the same facility center, paying the opening cost in each. Two novel dynamic data structures power the algorithm: a bi-chromatic closest pair oracle (maintaining a maximal matching in a threshold bipartite graph with dynamic nearest-neighbor oracles) and a cheapest cluster oracle (using pseudo-average costs and dynamic ball-size tracking via LSH-based structures). A token-based amortized analysis, where each client stores 3 tokens per level and regular clusters maintain bank accounts, proves the O(log m) amortized recourse bound, while the update time follows from the poly(d)·(m+n)^{O(1/γ²)} guarantees of the underlying oracles.

## Results

The paper's main result (Theorem 1.1) is a randomized algorithm for dynamic facility location in d-dimensional Euclidean spaces achieving a γ-approximation ratio, O(log m) amortized recourse, and poly(d)·(m+n)^{O(1/γ)} amortized update time, for every sufficiently large constant γ ≥ 1. This is the first efficient dynamic algorithm for the non-uniform dynamic facility location problem in high-dimensional Euclidean spaces. The algorithm works against an adaptive adversary and handles each update correctly with high probability. The approximation ratio and update time are near-optimal given current LSH-based tools, as improving the O((m+n)^{O(1/γ)}) update time would also improve the best known static sequential algorithm.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper positions itself against two key prior results. Compared to Bhattacharya et al. 2024 (ICML), which handles uniform costs in high-dimensional Euclidean spaces with O(1) facility recourse, this paper extends to non-uniform opening costs and provides a stronger recourse guarantee of O(log m) (client + facility recourse), though at the cost of a γ-approximation rather than O(1). Compared to Bhattacharya et al. 2022 (NeurIPS), which handles non-uniform costs in general metric spaces with O(1) approximation and Õ(m) update time, this paper achieves a much better update time of poly(d)·(m+n)^{O(1/γ)} in the Euclidean setting, trading a constant approximation for a γ-approximation. The paper notes that the Ω(m) update time lower bound for general metric spaces is bypassed by exploiting Euclidean structure via LSH-based oracles. No empirical comparisons are conducted, as the contribution is entirely theoretical.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper dynamizes the static greedy facility location algorithm of Jain et al. (2003) in the high-dimensional Euclidean setting, building directly on two prior lines: Bhattacharya et al. (2022) for non-uniform dynamic facility location in general metric spaces (with unavoidable Ω(m) update time), and Bhattacharya et al. (2024) for uniform-cost dynamic facility location in high-dimensional Euclidean spaces. The key technical departure from the 2022 approach is the elimination of an invariant that required checking all m facilities per client move, replaced by novel bi-chromatic closest pair and cheapest cluster oracles built on adaptive-adversary-robust dynamic nearest-neighbor structures (Bateni et al., 2024). The paper thus occupies the previously unexplored intersection of non-uniform costs and high-dimensional Euclidean geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first efficient dynamic algorithm for non-uniform facility location in high-dimensional Euclidean spaces, closing a gap between the general-metric and uniform-cost Euclidean results. The near-optimality of the approximation/update-time trade-off (matching the best known static sequential bounds up to the LSH exponent) suggests that further improvements would require breakthroughs in locality-sensitive hashing itself. Open problems include extending the framework to capacitated or prize-collecting variants, and proving whether Ω(log m) recourse is inherently necessary for sub-polynomial update times. Practically, the large constant γ required by theoretical ANNS is an artifact; combining the framework with graph-based ANNS indices could yield highly efficient implementations without super-polynomial time dependencies.
