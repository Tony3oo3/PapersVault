## Problem

Autonomous cyber defence (ACD) agents are typically trained with dense, hand-crafted reward functions that combine many arbitrary penalties and incentives, yet no prior work has systematically evaluated how reward structure impacts agent performance, worst-case risk, and training reliability. Additionally, standard cyber gym evaluation using mean episodic rewards cannot fairly compare agents trained under different reward functions and fails to capture intra-step node compromises that are resolved before the next observation, obscuring critical performance differences between policies.

## Input / Output

The agents consume network state observations (a vector embedding the adjacency matrix plus per-node vulnerability and compromise status in YT; a 52-bit vector in MiniCAGE) and output discrete defensive actions (scan/restore/decoy in YT's extended space; 145 expanded actions in CAGE). The key evaluation output is the ground truth score Score_GT, defined as the maximum number of compromised nodes across both intra-step and end-step, which is reward-independent and captures events invisible to standard MDP evaluation. This is supplemented by CVaR-based fixed-policy risk (RF at α=0.05) and standardized training reliability metrics (dispersion variability across time DT and across runs DR'), all reported with 95% confidence intervals over 25 training runs and 1000 evaluation episodes.

## Method

The paper introduces a ground truth scoring mechanism (Score_GT = max(m_intra, m_end)) that resolves the intra-step compromise problem by tracking the peak number of compromised nodes within each time step, enabling direct comparison between agents trained under different reward functions. Using this metric, the authors systematically evaluate five reward structures (sparse positive, sparse negative, sparse positive-negative, dense negative, complex dense negative) across two cyber gyms (Yawning Titan with 2–50 node linear networks and MiniCAGE with a 13-host enterprise topology), two DRL algorithms (PPO and DQN), and three intra-step agent orderings (red-then-blue, blue-then-red, random). A standardized DR' metric restricted to the final 20% of training steps quantifies cross-run reproducibility, while CVaR-based RF captures worst-case policy risk. The central finding is that sparse, goal-aligned rewards (SP and SPN) uniquely achieve the lowest Score_GT, lowest worst-case risk, and highest training reliability across nearly all configurations, and in MiniCAGE produce policies that confine adversary access to low-privilege user hosts while using costly restore actions more sparingly than dense-reward agents.

## Results

In Yawning Titan (YT, extended action space, averaged across 2–50 node networks and all agent orders), the sparse positive-negative (SPN) reward achieves the lowest ground truth score (Score_GT = 2.00), followed by sparse positive (SP, 2.69), while dense negative (DN, 6.29) and complex dense negative (CDN, 6.21) are roughly 3× worse. In MiniCAGE, SP (Score_GT = 1.29) and SPN (1.35) outperform the standard CAGE default reward (1.41). Sparse rewards also yield an order-of-magnitude improvement in training reliability (DT ≈ 0.08–0.11 × 10⁻³ vs. 2.33–2.45 × 10⁻³ for dense rewards), and in the 50-node blue-then-red setting, SP and SPN agents reach the analytically optimal Score_GT of 0 in 23–24 out of 25 runs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the standard CAGE 2 default reward (CDN, Kiely et al. 2023), the proposed sparse rewards win on Score_GT in both environments: in MiniCAGE, SP (1.29) and SPN (1.35) beat CDN (1.41), and in YT, SPN (2.00) and SP (2.69) beat CDN (6.21) by a factor of ~3. Against the YT-derived dense reward (Andrew et al. 2022, also labelled CDN here), the same pattern holds: SPN and SP reduce worst-case risk (Upper RF) from 6.52 to 2.16 and 2.87 respectively. Sparse rewards also dominate on training reliability: DT drops from 2.45×10⁻³ (CDN) to 0.08×10⁻³ (SPN), a ~30× improvement, and DR' is lower for SP (0.12) than CDN (0.31). The one exception is in MiniCAGE where CDN achieves a slightly lower Upper RF (2.02 vs. 3.11 for SP), but SP's 95% CI upper bound (1.34) is still below CDN's mean Score_GT (1.41), confirming SP's overall superiority. In the most realistic random agent-order setting, SPN's advantage over CDN widens further (Score_GT 4.50 vs. 11.79 in YT), underscoring that dense rewards are particularly fragile under adversarial timing uncertainty.
