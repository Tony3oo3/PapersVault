## Problem

RAG systems are highly vulnerable to poisoning attacks that inject a small number of malicious passages into the retrieved context, yet existing defenses (perplexity filtering, vigilant prompting, reranking) suffer from limited efficacy or high false positive rates. The paper challenges the assumption that existing attacks are stealthy, arguing that successful low-corruption attacks must disproportionately influence the LLM's response and therefore leave detectable traces in intermediate representations. This motivates a principled, attention-based approach to both quantify stealth and detect poisoning at the generation stage.

## Input / Output

The method takes a query, a set of k=10 retrieved passages, and the LLM's multi-layer, multi-head attention weights computed during response generation; for closed-source models like GPT-4o, an auxiliary open-source model (Mistral-7B) computes attention scores as a proxy. The NPAS is computed by summing attention from all response tokens to the top-α most attended tokens within each passage (α∈{5,10,∞}), then normalizing across all k passages to yield a length-invariant percentage score. The output is a filtered passage set (with anomalously high-attention passages removed) and the final RAG response generated from the remaining passages.

## Method

The paper introduces the Normalized Passage Attention Score (NPAS), which aggregates token-level attention (averaged across all decoder layers and heads) to quantify each passage's influence on the generated response, focusing on top-α tokens to capture 'Heavy Hitter' tokens while eliminating passage-length bias. Building on NPAS, the Attention-Variance Filter (AV Filter) iteratively removes the passage with the highest NPAS until the variance across remaining passages drops below a threshold δ, exploiting the observation that benign retrievals distribute attention broadly while successful low-corruption attacks concentrate it in a small subset, producing a high-variance signature. The paper formalizes stealth via the Stealth Attack Distinguishability Game (SADG), where a defender must identify the corrupted set from two retrieved sets presented in random order, and shows the AV-based defender achieves an average win rate of 0.78 across models and datasets. To probe the limits of this defense, the paper develops adaptive attacks extending GCG and AutoDAN jailbreak methods to jointly minimize cross-entropy loss for the target response and attention variance, achieving up to 35% ASR but requiring ~10³× baseline runtime and full model access.

## Results

The AV Filter reduces average Attack Success Rate (ASR) from up to 88.2% (Vanilla RAG, Llama2-C on RQA under PIA) to ~6.6% on RQA-MC, while achieving up to 20% higher Robust Accuracy (RACC) than baseline defenses (e.g., 73.8% vs. 55.0% for Keyword on Mistral-7B/RQA-MC/Poison). Clean accuracy drops are minimal at only 4–6% compared to 15–30% for Keyword and Decoding. The defender D_AV wins the Stealth Attack Distinguishability Game with an average Corruption Identification Rate (CIR) of 0.78 across all models and datasets. Adaptive attacks (GCG/AutoDAN) can raise ASR to 35% against AV Filter but require ~10³× more runtime than baseline attacks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Certified Robust RAG (Keyword and Decoding, Xiang et al. 2024), AV Filter achieves substantially higher RACC in most settings (e.g., 73.8% vs. 55.0% on Mistral-7B/RQA-MC/Poison) while maintaining comparable ASR; Keyword achieves lower ASR in 23/30 cases but underperforms on RACC in 17/30 cases due to aggressive pruning. On multi-hop HotpotQA, AV Filter outperforms Keyword in ASR across all 6/6 settings because Keyword's isolate-then-aggregate design fails when individual passages cannot answer the query alone. Against Perplexity Filtering (Alon & Kamfonas 2023), AV Filter achieves lower ASR in 6/9 Poison and 9/9 Paradox cases (e.g., 8.4% vs. 31.6% on Mistral-7B/RQA-MC/Poison). Against Vigilant Prompting (Pan et al. 2023), AV Filter consistently outperforms (e.g., 8.4% vs. 28.6% ASR on Mistral-7B/RQA-MC/Poison). Against Reranking methods (Glass et al. 2022, ColBERTv2/T5), AV Filter achieves lower ASR in most Poison and Paradox settings, though rerankers perform well on PIA because the verbatim query in poisoned passages makes them trivially detectable by relevance scoring.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RAG poisoning defense literature (PoisonedRAG, Certified Robust RAG) by introducing an attention-based detection signal rather than the isolate-then-aggregate strategy of prior certified defenses. It repurposes attention interpretability concepts (Heavy Hitters from H2O, multi-head attention aggregation) into a lightweight passage-influence metric (NPAS) and a variance-based filter, departing from perplexity-based or keyword-based filtering approaches. For the adaptive attack side, it extends jailbreak optimization methods (GCG, AutoDAN) by adding an attention-variance regularization term, creating a new class of stealth-aware poisoning attacks that prior work did not consider.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central insight—that successful low-corruption attacks must concentrate influence in a small subset of passages, making them inherently detectable—reframes the RAG security threat model and challenges prior claims that single-passage poisoning is undetectable. The AV Filter is practical (no extra forward passes, composable with other defenses like Certified Robust RAG) and the SADG formalization provides a rigorous framework for quantifying attack stealth. Key open questions include whether computationally infeasible-to-detect attacks exist (information-theoretic signal present but polynomial-time detection impossible), whether the approach generalizes beyond QA to other RAG behaviors (style manipulation, data exfiltration), and how to handle majority-corruption regimes where the variance signal degrades.
