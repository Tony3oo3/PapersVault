## Problem

In-context reinforcement learning (ICRL) agents, such as the Decision-Pretrained Transformer (DPT), implement learning algorithms within their context window, making them vulnerable to test-time reward poisoning attacks that corrupt observed rewards to manipulate future actions. Prior reward poisoning literature focused exclusively on training-time attacks in single-task RL, leaving the meta-RL/ICRL setting—where the attack targets the learning algorithm itself rather than a fixed policy—entirely unexplored.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a sequential context of (state, action, reward, next-state) tuples accumulated during the episode, plus a query state, and outputs a categorical distribution over a discrete action space. Unusually, the reward field in the context may be adversarially replaced (with probability ε per timestep) by a learned attacker who observes the true reward but whose output is bounded by a soft L2 budget constraint. The attacker's context contains the true rewards, while the victim's context contains the corrupted ones, creating an information asymmetry the agent must overcome.

## Method

AT-DPT extends DPT with a three-phase training procedure: standard supervised pretraining on clean optimal actions, followed by adversarial training where a population of task-specific attackers (parameterized by φ) are jointly optimized with the DPT (parameterized by θ) over N rounds. In each round, the DPT is rolled out in M parallel environments under ε-contamination from its dedicated attacker; the attacker is updated via REINFORCE to minimize the agent's true return subject to a soft budget penalty on the mean and variance of its reward perturbations, while the DPT is updated via NLL loss to predict oracle-provided optimal actions from the poisoned context. At test time, the DPT processes the (potentially corrupted) context in-context, updating it after every timestep to support robustness against adaptive attackers. The key mechanism is that simultaneous optimization approximates a Nash equilibrium, causing the DPT to implicitly learn a corruption-robust posterior-sampling algorithm that generalizes to unseen attack distributions.

## Results

In the 5-armed bandit setting (ε=0.4, B=3), AT-DPT achieves a cumulative regret of 24.2±1.2 against its own attacker, compared to 63.6±8.6 for frozen DPT, 106.3±3.8 for Thompson Sampling, and 86.0±4.4 for corruption-robust crUCB — a 2.5–4.4× reduction in regret. In the Darkroom2 MDP (ε=0.4, B=10), AT-DPT attains an average episode reward of 242.2±11.9, outperforming DPT (216.1), NPG (237.2), and Q-learning (198.1). In the linear bandit setting, AT-DPT achieves a regret of 2.49±1.06 versus 70.29 for DPT and 37.45 for CRLinUCB, a >90% improvement over the corruption-robust baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AT-DPT consistently outperforms all baselines under adversarial reward poisoning. In the bandit setting, AT-DPT (regret ≈24) beats frozen DPT (≈60) by 2.5×, Thompson Sampling (≈100) by 4×, and the best corruption-robust baseline crUCB (≈82) by 3.4×. In the linear bandit setting, AT-DPT (regret 2.49) surpasses CRLinUCB (37.45) by over 90%, while DPT without adversarial training collapses to 70.29. In the Darkroom2 MDP, AT-DPT (reward 242.2) edges out NPG (237.2) and clearly beats DPT (216.1) and Q-learning (198.1); NPG is the closest competitor, consistent with prior findings of its bounded-reward robustness. AT-DPT generalizes across attacker targets — its regret stays in the 24–30 range regardless of whether the attacker was trained against AT-DPT, DPT, TS, or crUCB — whereas baselines show large variance (e.g., TS ranges from 89.6 to 106.3). The main trade-off is in the clean environment, where AT-DPT (13.0) slightly underperforms TS (8.7) and DPT (11.5), reflecting a small cost of adversarial training when no attack is present.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Decision-Pretrained Transformer (DPT, Lee et al., 2023) by inserting an adversarial training phase between pretraining and in-context learning, creating a minimax game between a population of learned attackers and the DPT agent. It departs from prior reward poisoning work (Lin et al., 2017; Zhang et al., 2020b; Nika et al., 2023), which models poisoning as a training-time attack on single-environment RL policies, by introducing test-time poisoning in the meta-RL/ICRL setting where the agent's in-context learning algorithm itself is the target. The approach also contrasts with corruption-robust bandit/RL theory (Lykouris et al., 2018; Niss & Tewari, 2020; Ding et al., 2022), which provides suboptimality guarantees for single-task algorithms, by instead learning a corruption-robust algorithm in-context via a transformer that leverages the multi-task distribution as a prior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first formalization of test-time reward poisoning in meta-RL, opening a new security dimension for ICRL-based agents (including LLM-based decision-makers using RAG or external tools). The key open question is whether the approach generalizes beyond DPT to other ICRL methods (e.g., Algorithm Distillation, Vintix) and whether a complete theoretical account of convergence to the bi-level equilibrium can be established. Practical limitations include the need for oracle actions during training and the observation that performance degrades with suboptimal demonstrations, suggesting the method is most applicable in settings where expert supervision is available. The finding that the multi-task prior makes implausible corruptions ineffective points toward a broader principle: meta-learners can be inherently more robust than single-task algorithms when the task distribution is informative.
