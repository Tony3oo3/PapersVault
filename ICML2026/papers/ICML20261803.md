## Problem

Existing LDM-based inverse problem solvers suffer from instability that produces artifacts and degraded reconstructions. Prior work attributes this to off-manifold drift under a linear manifold assumption, but this assumption fails in latent space due to highly nonlinear decoders. The paper instead characterizes instability as a measurable discrepancy (KL divergence) between the solver-induced dynamics and the stable reverse diffusion dynamics defined by the pretrained model's time-marginal distributions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261803/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

MCLC is a plug-and-play module inserted into existing LDM-based solvers (LDPS, PSLD, ReSample, LatentDAPS) after their measurement-consistency step. It operates on 512×512 RGB images in the 64×64×4 latent space of a pretrained LDM (e.g., Stable Diffusion v1.5), consuming the latent state and the measurement-consistency gradient already computed by the host solver. It requires only a forward pass of the LDM score network plus simple algebraic projection—no backward computation or additional memory—making it lightweight to integrate.

## Method

MCLC applies a Langevin corrector step after each measurement-consistency update to push the latent state back toward the diffusion model's time-marginal distribution p_t, provably reducing the KL divergence between solver dynamics and stable reverse dynamics. To avoid disturbing measurement fidelity, the Langevin update (both drift and noise) is projected onto the orthogonal complement of the normalized measurement-consistency gradient via P_⊥g = I − ggᵀ, ensuring first-order preservation of the measurement residual. A theoretical bound (Theorem 3.4) shows that when the step-size parameter λ satisfies λ ≤ √(k/(d+2))/σ_t, the expected measurement perturbation is O(k) while KL monotonically decreases. The method is training-free, requires no modification to the host solver's architecture, and a single default hyperparameter set (k=5, N_c=1, λ=0.1) generalizes across tasks, solvers, and degradation types.

## Results

MCLC is evaluated as a plug-and-play module on four LDM-based inverse solvers (LDPS, PSLD, ReSample, LatentDAPS) across six tasks on FFHQ and ImageNet. Headline gains are most dramatic on FID and P-FID (stability/artifact metrics): e.g., on Gaussian Deblur with LDPS on FFHQ, FID drops from 100.10 to 80.83 and P-FID from 93.55 to 54.74, while PSNR improves from 27.61 to 28.14. On 4× Super Resolution with ReSample, FID drops from 70.16 to 53.85 and P-FID from 133.15 to 78.08. Under severe degradations (92% inpainting, 12× SR), MCLC still yields large improvements (e.g., PSNR 21.51→25.01 on 92% inpainting).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MCLC is compared against four base solvers (LDPS, PSLD, ReSample, LatentDAPS) and six competing stabilization methods. Against the primary plug-and-play competitor DiffStateGrad (Zirvi et al., 2025), MCLC consistently wins on FID and P-FID across nearly all tasks and solvers (e.g., P-FID 54.74 vs. 94.14 on Gaussian Deblur/LDPS), while DiffStateGrad often degrades PSNR or FID relative to the base. Against non-pluggable methods MPGD (He et al., 2024), SILO (Raphaeli et al., 2025), and SITCOM (Alkhouri et al., 2025), MCLC achieves competitive or superior FID (e.g., 62.12 vs. 77.90 on 4× SR) while retaining plug-and-play applicability. MCLC also improves the recent TReg solver (FID 37.12→27.62 on Gaussian Deblur) and the flow-based FlowChef solver (FID 185.44→100.40 on Motion Deblur), demonstrating broad applicability. The one notable exception is LatentDAPS, where MCLC yields only marginal gains because its decoupled re-initialization breaks the reverse-dynamics structure that MCLC targets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant 'off-manifold' framing of instability in diffusion-based inverse solvers (Chung et al. 2022, 2023; He et al. 2024; Zirvi et al. 2025), which relies on a linear manifold assumption that the authors argue fails in latent space due to highly nonlinear decoders. Instead, it recharacterizes instability as a measurable KL divergence gap between solver-induced dynamics and the learned time-marginal distributions of the diffusion model, grounding the correction in Langevin convergence theory (Vempala & Wibisono, 2019). The resulting MCLC module is conceptually related to constrained Langevin dynamics (Bubeck et al., 2018; Lamperski, 2021) but applies a local, state-dependent projection onto the orthogonal complement of the measurement gradient rather than a global feasible-set constraint, and is explicitly distinguished from predictor-corrector samplers that target discretization error rather than measurement-consistency-induced drift.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MCLC provides a theoretically grounded, plug-and-play stabilization mechanism that avoids the linear manifold assumption, making it applicable across diverse LDM-based solvers (LDPS, PSLD, ReSample, TReg) and even latent flow-based solvers (FlowChef) without modifying their core algorithms. The practical impact is significant for the growing community of zero-shot inverse problem solvers, where instability artifacts remain a persistent quality bottleneck. Limitations include the need for some hyperparameter tuning (step size, number of corrector steps), reduced effectiveness with solvers that break reverse dynamics (e.g., LatentDAPS), and limited validation in physics or medical imaging domains where domain-specific LDM priors are scarce. Open questions include adaptive strategies for the correction strength and whether the KL-gap characterization can be exploited for more principled, task-aware stabilization.
