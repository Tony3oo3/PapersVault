## Problem

Existing video understanding benchmarks focus on literal perception tasks like object recognition and event description, leaving a gap in systematically evaluating whether MLLMs can perform the high-order cognitive process of metaphorical video understanding—transforming perceived visual signals into deeper implicit semantics through cross-domain mapping. This paper identifies that even the strongest MLLMs (Gemini-3-Pro, GPT-5) score only ~64/100 versus ~83 for humans, with over 80% of failures stemming from defective cross-domain mapping rather than recognition errors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262398/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives video frames (extracted from short videos averaging 37.2 seconds, converted to base64 for MLLM compatibility) concatenated with a brief title providing necessary background context. The output is a two-part response: a thinking process where the model recognizes visual elements, establishes links to underlying concepts, and reveals implicit meanings, followed by a structured interpretation specifying which visual elements convey which implicit meanings in a fixed format. Evaluation uses DeepSeek-V3.2 as an LLM judge scoring accuracy of metaphorical grounding and meaning revelation on a 0–100 scale (Pearson r=0.85 with human judges).

## Method

The paper contributes two components. First, MetaphorVU-Bench is the first systematic benchmark for metaphorical video understanding, containing 860 videos from Kuaishou spanning 8 taxonomy types grounded in multimodal metaphor theory, constructed via multi-stage filtration (comment threshold → GPT-5 text analysis → Gemini-3-Pro visual verification → human annotation with cross-validation). Second, MetaphorBoost is an inference-time enhancement framework that constructs the first metaphorical knowledge graph (54,687 concept nodes, 200,268 edges) by extracting source-target metaphorical concept pairs from textual metaphor datasets using DeepSeek-V3.2. At inference time, MetaphorBoost first prompts the MLLM to identify visual elements as a keyword list, then queries the knowledge graph with h=2 hops to retrieve the top-z=10 target nodes that simultaneously link to the most identified keywords, and finally feeds these retrieved concepts back to the MLLM as external cognitive scaffolding to promote accurate cross-domain mapping—achieving consistent improvements (+2.3 to +4.1 points) across multiple base models without any training.

## Results

On MetaphorVU-Bench (860 videos, 8 metaphor types), the evaluation metric is a 0–100 LLM-judge score measuring accuracy in grounding metaphorical visual elements and revealing implicit meanings. The best close-source MLLM, Gemini-3-Pro, achieves 63.8 average, while GPT-5 scores 63.7; both lag the human upper bound (83.4) by roughly 20 points. MetaphorBoost applied to Gemini-3-Pro reaches 66.1 average (+2.3), and applied to Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct improves from 33.8 to 37.9 (+4.1), consistently outperforming all reasoning-enhanced baselines. Error analysis shows over 80% of MLLM failures stem from defective cross-domain mapping (missing, superficial, or improper) rather than recognition errors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MetaphorBoost (Gemini-3-Pro) achieves the best overall score of 66.1, surpassing GPT-5 (63.7) and Gemini-3-Pro (63.8) by 2.4 and 2.3 points respectively, and outperforming all other close-source MLLMs (GPT-4o 56.8, Qwen3-VL-Plus 61.4, Gemini-2.5-Pro 61.8, Doubao-1.5-Vision-Pro 50.5). Among open-source MLLMs, Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B-Thinking (58.6) and GLM-4.5V (56.8) lead, but both are substantially below the close-source frontier. All post-training reasoning-enhanced methods (VideoRFT 35.6, Vision-R1 33.1, ReAd-R 36.8) fail to meaningfully improve over their base model Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct (33.8), and inference-time scaling methods LTR (44.5) and ViTCoT (46.2) actually degrade Qwen3-VL-8B-Thinking's base score of 52.0. MetaphorBoost consistently improves its base models: +4.1 over Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct, +3.8 over Qwen3-VL-8B-Thinking, and +2.3 over Gemini-3-Pro, with the largest gains on Causal Montage (+7.8) and Surreal Narrative (+6.2) where cross-domain mapping is most demanding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of metaphor understanding (previously studied in text and image domains) and video understanding (previously focused on literal perception tasks like object recognition and event description). Prior video metaphor work was confined to the advertising domain, while the authors generalize to a comprehensive 8-type taxonomy grounded in multimodal metaphor theory and film studies, sourced from diverse real-world Kuaishou videos. The MetaphorBoost method repurposes retrieval-augmented generation concepts by constructing a metaphor-specific knowledge graph (rather than a general commonsense graph like ConceptNet) and using it as an inference-time scaffold for cross-domain mapping, departing from both post-training approaches (VideoRFT, Vision-R1) and generic inference-time scaling (LTR, ViTCoT) that the paper shows are ineffective for this task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic benchmark for metaphorical video understanding, filling a gap that limits both the practical deployment of MLLMs in content-rich real-world scenarios and the assessment of their high-order cognitive capabilities. The key diagnostic finding—that over 80% of failures stem from defective cross-domain mapping rather than visual recognition—redirects optimization efforts away from perception and toward semantic linking, a distinction with broader implications for how MLLMs are improved. Open questions include whether the 860-sample benchmark is sufficient for robust conclusions, whether the textual metaphor knowledge graph adequately captures video-specific metaphorical logic, and whether the ~20-point human-MLLM gap can be closed through training rather than inference-time augmentation alone.
