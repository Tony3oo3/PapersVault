## Problem

Existing single-image-to-3D methods produce facial meshes with inaccurate geometry and irregular structures, and separately struggle to synthesize realistic, cross-view consistent textures for those meshes. No prior pipeline simultaneously generates a structured, identity-faithful facial mesh and a fine-grained, multi-view-consistent texture from a single input image.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264640/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB avatar image (background removed, foreground centered, encoded by DINOv2 at 518×518) and produces an explicit watertight 3D facial mesh extracted via Marching Cubes from a decoded occupancy field, together with a single UV texture map baked from super-resolved multi-view projections. The texturing stage internally generates 6 identity-consistent face views at zero elevation (azimuths 0°, ±45°, ±90°, 180°) as priors, and the final texture is a unified UV map aligned to the mesh surface.

## Method

SAMT is a two-stage framework. Stage 1 uses a DiT-based 3D latent diffusion model with DoraVAE (sharp-edge sampling + dual cross-attention) to generate facial geometry; it is pretrained on ObjaverseXL then finetuned on 35K curated face meshes via jointly optimizing the DiT backbone and LoRA adapters (rank 32) with a grouped learning-rate schedule. Stage 2 synthesizes textures with a pretrained geometry-conditioned 2D texturing diffusion, augmented by a facial-multi-view-aware strategy that injects 6 identity-consistent views from FaceLift as explicit priors paired with rendered geometry conditions. A derivative-free Powell optimization aligns the mesh to the prior views by minimizing silhouette IoU mismatch across all 6 views, and a view-matched one-to-one denoising scheme feeds each prior view with its corresponding geometry condition to enforce cross-view consistency before baking into a single UV map.

## Results

On facial mesh generation (Table 1), SAMT achieves the best ULIP-I (0.149), ULIP-T (0.084), Uni3D-T (0.251), Chamfer Distance (0.105), and F1 score (0.216) among all compared methods, with only Uni3D-I (0.323) slightly trailing Trellis 2 (0.327). On facial texture synthesis (Table 2), SAMT leads all four metrics: CLIP-score (0.839), FID-CLIP (32.211), CMMD (2.805), and ArcFace distance (0.316), outperforming the strongest baseline Hunyuan3D 2.1 by 0.018 CLIP-score and 1.19 FID-CLIP. Evaluations use 50 test cases for cross-modal similarity (ULIP, Uni3D) and RenderMe-360 samples for geometric accuracy (CD, F1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CraftsMan3D, SAMT wins on every mesh-generation metric, with the largest gains in F1 (0.216 vs. 0.166, +30%) and ULIP-T (0.084 vs. 0.061, +38%), indicating substantially better identity preservation and text-image alignment. Against Hunyuan3D 2.1, SAMT leads on ULIP-I, ULIP-T, Uni3D-T, CD, and F1 but trails slightly on Uni3D-I (0.323 vs. 0.325); the CD improvement (0.105 vs. 0.121) and F1 improvement (0.216 vs. 0.171) are the most notable, reflecting more accurate geometry. Against Trellis 2, SAMT similarly wins on five of six mesh metrics (ULIP-I, ULIP-T, Uni3D-T, CD, F1) while losing narrowly on Uni3D-I (0.323 vs. 0.327); the F1 gap (0.216 vs. 0.183) is the largest single-metric advantage. For texture synthesis, SAMT outperforms TEXGen by a wide margin on all four metrics (e.g., CLIP-score 0.839 vs. 0.697, ArcFace 0.316 vs. 0.442), and edges out both Hunyuan3D 2.1 and Trellis 2 on every texture metric, with the most significant advantage over Trellis 2 in ArcFace identity distance (0.316 vs. 0.388, an 18% reduction).

## Relation to Prior Work

SAMT builds on the 3D latent diffusion paradigm (CraftsMan3D, Hunyuan3D 2.1, Trellis 2) that encodes shapes via a VAE and denoises in latent space with a DiT backbone, but departs from these general-purpose systems by specializing the pipeline for faces through domain-specific finetuning with LoRA adapters on a curated 35K-face dataset. For the VAE, it adopts DoraVAE's Sharp Edge Sampling and dual cross-attention to better preserve high-curvature facial micro-structures that uniform sampling under-represents. For texturing, it extends geometry-conditioned 2D diffusion (as in Hunyuan3D 2.1) by injecting explicit multi-view facial priors from FaceLift and introducing a derivative-free mesh-image alignment step, moving beyond both UV-space texturing (TEXGen) and 3D-voxel texturing (Trellis 2) paradigms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SAMT addresses a practical gap in the 3D avatar pipeline: producing an explicit, textured mesh (directly usable in AR/VR engines and game engines) from a single photo, rather than a neural radiance field or parametric model that requires per-identity optimization. The 35K curated face dataset spanning 1,300+ identities is a useful community resource. However, the paper acknowledges that out-of-distribution inputs (strong directional lighting, heavy occlusions, extreme profile views) still degrade both geometry and texture quality, and the approach is limited to frontal-dominant single-image inputs. Open questions include whether the multi-view prior injection strategy generalizes to other body parts or object categories, and whether the two-stage pipeline can be unified into a single end-to-end model.
