## Problem

Existing spatial transcriptomics models analyze cellular morphology, gene expression, and spatial context in isolation or at spot/patch resolution, failing to learn unified cell-level representations that capture how local niches and tissue architecture jointly shape cell identity. A critical unmet need is modeling microenvironment-dependent morphological transitions (e.g., DCIS-to-invasive progression, immune-cold-to-hot remodeling) without the paired before/after observations that destructive spatial transcriptomics precludes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262194/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

SPATIA consumes 256×256 px cell morphology crops (from 0.2125 μm/px tissue sections), per-cell gene expression vectors, spatial coordinates, and a precomputed transition descriptor (mean gene-expression shift Δg and mean CellProfiler morphology shift Δm for a given transition type). For generation, it outputs a target-state cell morphology image conditioned on the control cell and transition type; for prediction tasks, the same unified embedding supports cell-type annotation, clustering, gene expression regression, and ER/PR/HER2 biomarker classification. Notably, at inference the model requires only the control image and the transition-level descriptor—no per-sample target features are used, avoiding target leakage.

## Method

SPATIA employs a three-level hierarchical architecture: at the cell level, a ViT-MAE image encoder and a scPRINT gene encoder produce tokens fused via cross-attention; at the niche level (256×256 px patches containing 10–30 cells), a transformer models local cell–cell interactions; at the tissue level, a global transformer aggregates niche embeddings with positional encodings for long-range context. For spatially conditioned morphology generation, entropy-regularized optimal transport in PCA-reduced gene expression space constructs weak control–target pairs, and a confidence-aware flow-matching objective reweights velocity-field supervision by OT coupling strength to downweight uncertain matches. A morphology-profile alignment loss (sliced Wasserstein distance in frozen CellProfiler feature space) enforces distributional fidelity of generated cells to real target-state phenotypes, while a condition-contrastive regularization term ensures the model distinguishes different transition types. The entire system is pretrained via self-supervised dual reconstruction (image + gene expression) before downstream adaptation.

## Results

On the primary conditional morphology generation task (DCIS-to-invasive and immune-cold-to-hot transitions), SPATIA achieves FID 58.5±1.05 and KID 2.01±0.07, outperforming the best baseline GeneFlow (FID 62.4, KID 2.20) by ~6% in FID and ~8% in KID. On morphological correctness, SPATIA reaches Wasserstein correlation 0.94 and KS statistic 0.65, versus 0.87/0.58 for GeneFlow. On predictive tasks, SPATIA achieves 0.902 AUC for ER receptor prediction (vs. 0.891 for UNI), 0.725 F1 for cell annotation (vs. 0.709 for CellPLM), and 0.870 ARI for clustering (vs. 0.867 for CellPLM), representing up to 3% gains over the strongest baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPATIA outperforms all three generative baselines on every metric: GeneFlow (FID 62.4, KID 2.20, KS 0.58), CellFlux (FID 64.1, KID 2.31, KS 0.57), and MorphDiff (FID 70.5, KID 2.52, KS 0.54) are all beaten by the full SPATIA model (FID 58.5, KID 2.01, KS 0.65), with the largest gap over MorphDiff (~17% FID reduction). On biomarker prediction (BCNB), SPATIA exceeds UNI (0.902 vs. 0.891 ER AUC; 0.744 vs. 0.732 HER2 AUC), CONCH (0.902 vs. 0.881 ER AUC), GigaPath (0.902 vs. 0.841 ER AUC), and Hibou (0.902 vs. 0.832 ER AUC) across all three receptors. For cross-platform clustering, SPATIA achieves 0.735 ARI / 0.806 NMI on Xenium (vs. scGPT 0.730/0.678, scFoundation 0.727/0.754, UCE 0.618/0.718) and 0.542 ARI / 0.490 NMI on CosMx (vs. scFoundation 0.530/0.560, UCE 0.516/0.555), with the CosMx NMI slightly below scFoundation. On cell annotation (MS dataset), SPATIA reaches 0.725 F1 / 0.734 Precision, surpassing CellPLM (0.709/0.702), scGPT (0.703/0.729), CellTypist (0.667/0.693), and scBERT (0.599/0.604). For clustering, SPATIA (0.870 ARI / 0.831 NMI) edges out CellPLM (0.867/0.823) and scGPT (0.856/0.828), while Geneformer (0.461/0.586) and Nicheformer (0.256/0.345) perform substantially worse. Overall, SPATIA wins or ties on every reported benchmark, with the most substantial margins on the generative task and the smallest on predictive tasks where specialized pathology models (UNI, CONCH) are strong.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPATIA extends the flow-matching generative framework (Lipman et al., 2022) with a confidence-aware OT reweighting mechanism and builds its gene-expression backbone on scPRINT (Kalfon et al., 2025), a pretrained single-cell encoder. It departs from prior spatial transcriptomics models (CellPLM, scGPT-spatial, Nicheformer) that operate at spot or patch resolution by performing true cell-level multimodal fusion of morphology, transcriptomics, and spatial coordinates via hierarchical cross-attention. Compared to existing cell-morphology generative models (CellFlux, MorphDiff, GeneFlow), SPATIA introduces spatial niche and tissue-level conditioning and a morphology-profile alignment objective that constrains generation in an interpretable CellProfiler feature space rather than pixel space alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MIST atlas (25.9M cells across 74 sources, 4 platforms, 17 tissues) is a substantial resource for benchmarking multi-scale spatial omics models, and the confidence-aware OT reweighting offers a practical recipe for handling the inherent noise in weakly paired destructive ST data. However, the generative evaluation is limited to two biologically defined transition axes (DCIS-to-invasive, immune-cold-to-hot), and predictive gains over specialized pathology models (UNI, GigaPath) are modest (≤3%), leaving open whether the spatial conditioning provides meaningful advantage for more complex, multi-step, or cross-tissue perturbations. A key open question is whether the OT-based weak pairing strategy generalizes to transitions without clear lineage constraints or to platforms lacking high-resolution cell segmentation.
