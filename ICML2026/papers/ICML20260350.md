## Problem

Existing diffusion-based O2O-RL methods either use diffusion planners solely for data augmentation (leaving the policy as a non-diffusion model) or use diffusion policies that lack long-term planning capability. Moreover, these policies provide only aleatoric uncertainty and become overconfident under distribution shift, hindering exploration in the online phase. The paper asks whether one can inherit the planner's long-horizon expressiveness in a fast-sampling actor while providing principled epistemic uncertainty to balance exploration and exploitation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single environment state s as input and outputs an action a sampled from a Gaussian policy π_θ(a|s) whose mean is the first action of a trajectory sampled from the diffusion planner conditioned on s, and whose variance combines epistemic uncertainty (aggregated over M Laplace-approximated sub-policies) with a shift-awareness term measuring the distance between the diffusion-based transition model's predicted next state and the true next state. The transition model p(s'|s,a) is extracted for free from the same diffusion planner via marginalization over the trajectory distribution.

## Method

DUAL operates in two phases. In the offline phase, a diffusion planner is pre-trained on offline trajectories, then the actor policy is extracted from the planner's trajectory distribution (via marginalization over future state-action pairs) and progressively distilled to reduce denoising steps from N=32 to N=1 for fast inference; the actor is further trained with a Q-weighted policy gradient loss. For uncertainty quantification, a last-layer Laplace approximation is applied to the actor loss (rather than the ELBO), which the paper proves yields a valid weighted posterior p(θ|D) ∝ exp(-L_π(θ)), enabling M Monte Carlo sub-policies whose aggregated variance provides principled epistemic uncertainty. A second uncertainty term uses the diffusion-based transition model to detect transition-state distribution shifts, increasing policy variance when the predicted next state diverges from the observed one. The key theoretical distinction is that applying Laplace to the ELBO (as in prior diffusion UQ work) does not align with the policy's decision-making objective, whereas the actor-loss-based approximation does.

## Results

On D4RL MuJoCo locomotion (9 environments), DUAL achieves an average online expected return of 89.93 after 1M online steps, outperforming the strongest diffusion baseline DACER (88.95) and Diff-QL (86.82). On AntMaze (6 long-term planning environments), DUAL achieves 81.90, a ~10-point improvement over the next-best DACER (71.71) and Diff-QL (70.67). On Adroit (4 dexterous manipulation tasks under IQL), DUAL reaches 46.71 vs. 37.51 for Diff-QL. In off-dynamic RL (ODRL) settings with transition shifts, DUAL achieves 62.62 average return under Cal-QL vs. 57.51 for Diff-QL, confirming the shift-awareness component's importance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DUAL consistently outperforms all seven baselines (IQL, EDIS, Diffuser, Diff-QL, DACER, Cal-QL Base, SO2+OFF2ON Base) across MuJoCo locomotion, AntMaze, Adroit, and ODRL environments. The most dramatic gains appear in long-term planning: on AntMaze, DUAL's 81.90 average exceeds DACER (71.71) by ~10 points, and on antmaze-umaze-diverse-v2 specifically, DUAL reaches 78.06 vs. 51.73 for DACER. On MuJoCo locomotion, DUAL (89.93) edges out DACER (88.95) and Diff-QL (86.82), with the largest single-environment win on hopper-medium-v2 (88.23 vs. 83.05 for DACER). In off-dynamic RL settings where transition dynamics shift (friction, gravity, kinematic, morphology), DUAL's shift-awareness term yields a 5.1-point advantage over Diff-QL under Cal-QL (62.62 vs. 57.51). On Adroit manipulation tasks, DUAL improves over Diff-QL by ~9 points under IQL (46.71 vs. 37.51). DUAL is slightly slower than the N=1 distilled variants (D-Diffuser, D-Diff-QL) due to MC sampling for epistemic UQ, but achieves substantially higher returns; it is also far faster than DACER, which requires 200 action samples per step for entropy-based UQ.

## Relation to Prior Work

DUAL unifies two previously separate threads in diffusion-based RL: the diffusion planner (Diffuser, EDIS) used for long-horizon trajectory modeling and data augmentation, and the diffusion policy (Diff-QL, DACER) used as a single-step action generator. Prior O2O-RL work with diffusion (EDIS, Huang et al. 2025) treats the diffusion planner purely as a data synthesizer while keeping a non-diffusion actor, whereas Diff-QL/DACER use a diffusion policy but lack lookahead planning and provide only aleatoric uncertainty. DUAL departs from both by extracting a diffusion actor policy directly from the pre-trained diffusion planner (via Theorem 3.1), distilling it to N=1 steps for fast inference, and applying a Laplace approximation to the Q-weighted actor loss rather than the ELBO loss—yielding a principled Bayesian posterior that existing diffusion UQ methods (BayesDiff, DiffUQ) cannot provide because the ELBO is not interpretable as a log-likelihood.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical justification (Theorem 3.4) for why applying Laplace approximation to the actor loss—rather than the diffusion ELBO—yields a valid epistemic uncertainty estimate, which is a practical and conceptual correction to existing diffusion UQ approaches. Empirically, the combination of planner-prior extraction and epistemic UQ produces the largest gains in long-horizon planning (AntMaze diverse: +26 over DACER) and transition-shift settings (ODRL: +5 over best baseline), suggesting that proper model uncertainty is particularly critical when the environment dynamics change. Limitations include the Gaussian policy assumption, MC sampling latency in the online phase, and additional training cost (~1 hour for distillation + Laplace). Open questions include whether the framework extends to multimodal policies without prohibitive cost, how it scales to higher-dimensional action spaces, and whether diffusion language models can inherit similar uncertainty-aware formulations for RL.
