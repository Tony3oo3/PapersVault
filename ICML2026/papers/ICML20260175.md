## Problem

LLM-based CAD generation has largely focused on one-shot model synthesis, leaving operation-level, step-by-step modeling underexplored. This gap is driven by the scarcity of complex, multi-operation CAD datasets (existing benchmarks like DeepCAD average only 2 operations per model and cover mostly extrusion), the absence of fine-grained parsing that decomposes modeling sequences into discrete operations with topological relationships, and the lack of evaluation metrics for advanced edge-based operations such as chamfer and fillet.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260175/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For the Step-level Geometric Modeling (SGM) task, the model receives a single textual shape description and must output an executable CadQuery script that constructs a 2D sketch and applies an extrusion or revolution. For the Sequence-level Spatial Modeling (SSM) task, the model additionally receives the full prior CadQuery script history and a sequence-level spatial instruction, and must generate an incremental script that resolves workplane localization and topological interaction (boolean cut/join or fillet/chamfer). Outputs follow a strict structured format: shape-then-boolean blocks for constructive operations and edge-select-then-operate blocks for modification operations, with workplanes always constructed explicitly via Plane and Vector objects rather than string shortcuts.

## Method

The paper introduces Op-CAD, a 128k-instance dataset spanning four operations (extrude, revolve, chamfer, fillet) and five modalities (B-Rep geometry, CadQuery code, command sequences, multi-view renderings, text), built from 27k+ professional CAD models with an average sequence length of 14.3 (8× DeepCAD). A CAD Parsing Module decomposes each raw operation into a structured triplet (isolated geometry, topological interaction, cumulative prior state) and extracts geometric, spatial, and topological priors that guide a hierarchical VLM annotation pipeline (Gemini 2.5-Pro), reducing hallucination by conditioning on parsed shape categories and bounding-box positions. A Salient Context Window (capacity K=5) selects the most relevant prior operations to mitigate attention decay in long sequences. For evaluation, the paper defines a novel CF-IoU metric that measures edge-set overlap for chamfer/fillet operations, complementing standard Chamfer/Hausdorff distances. The paper further proposes Chain-of-Operation (COOP) prompting, which appends sequence-level natural-language annotations to the modeling history to emulate human-engineer reasoning, and demonstrates that fine-tuning Llama-3.1-8B on Op-CAD (Op-Llama-8B) outperforms the 8×-larger Llama-3.1-70B on SSM tasks.

## Results

On the Op-CAD benchmark, even state-of-the-art models achieve only ~80% Pass@1 on both SGM and SSM tasks: GPT-4o reaches 0.836 (SGM) and 0.811 (SSM), while Gemini-2.5-pro attains the best 3D geometry metrics (Mean CD 20.229, Mean HD 179.538 on SSM). DeepSeek-R1 achieves the highest CF-IoU (0.510) among all evaluated models, yet this remains far below the ideal value of 1.0. The fine-tuned Op-Llama-8B (Llama-3.1-8B trained on Op-CAD) improves SSM Pass@1 by 25.5% over its base and increases CF-IoU by 172.7%, outperforming the 8× larger Llama-3.1-70B (0.783 vs. 0.711 SSM Pass@1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among large-scale closed-source models, GPT-4o leads on SGM Pass@1 (0.836) but Gemini-2.5-pro achieves the best 3D geometry fidelity (SSM Mean CD 20.229, Mean HD 179.538) and a substantially higher CF-IoU (0.423 vs. GPT-4o's 0.133). DeepSeek-R1 attains the best CF-IoU (0.510) across all models, indicating superior chamfer/fillet edge localization, though its overall Pass@1 (0.773 SSM) trails GPT-4o. In the medium-scale tier, Qwen3-Coder-30B (SGM Pass@1 0.834) surpasses the much larger Llama-3.1-70B (0.746), and Qwen3-VL-30B achieves the highest SGM Pass@1 (0.878) among all models. The fine-tuned Op-Llama-8B outperforms Llama-3.1-70B on SSM Pass@1 (0.783 vs. 0.711) and CF-IoU (0.120 vs. 0.032), demonstrating that Op-CAD training enables an 8B model to exceed a 70B model on spatial reasoning. On the DeepCAD benchmark (Table 6), DeepCAD-Op (trained on Op-CAD) improves Mean CD by 86.39% and invalid ratio by 32.19% over the original DeepCAD model on Op-CAD test data, while also slightly improving on the original DeepCAD test set (Mean CD 6.64 vs. 7.10). Overall, the paper reveals that no single model dominates all metrics: geometry accuracy (CD/HD) and edge-operation accuracy (CF-IoU) are achieved by different models, and small-scale models show a clear decoupling between geometric and spatial reasoning abilities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant one-shot CAD generation paradigm (DeepCAD, LLM4CAD, Query2CAD, CAD-MLLM) by reframing the task as operation-level, step-by-step modeling with explicit geometric and spatial decomposition. It builds on the procedural CAD modeling lineage (DeepCAD, CadParser) and the hierarchical VLM annotation approach from CAD-Llama, but introduces a novel CAD parsing module that decomposes each operation into geometric shape, topological interaction, and cumulative prior state—granularity absent from all prior datasets. The CF-IoU metric and the Chain-of-Operation (COOP) prompting strategy are entirely new contributions with no direct predecessor in the CAD or broader code-generation literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Op-CAD (128k instances, 5 modalities, 4 operation types, 8× longer sequences than DeepCAD) is likely to become the standard benchmark for operation-oriented CAD generation, filling a critical gap where prior datasets were too simple to stress-test spatial reasoning. The finding that even GPT-4o and Gemini-2.5-Pro achieve only ~80% Pass@1 and CF-IoU scores below 0.5 reveals that edge-based operations (chamfer, fillet) remain a major unsolved challenge. Open questions include whether the geometric-spatial decoupling observed in small models can be exploited architecturally, how COOP generalizes to even longer sequences, and whether the dataset's reliance on CadParser-sourced models limits coverage of industrial design diversity.
