## Problem

State-of-the-art Audio-Visual Segmentation methods rely on dense cross-modal attention with quadratic complexity in spatial tokens, making them impractical for mobile and edge deployment. Prior efficiency-oriented work has focused on swapping heavy backbones for lighter ones but has overlooked the cross-modal interaction module itself as the dominant latency bottleneck.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266140/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes synchronized video frames (resized to 224×224, T frames) and a 16 kHz mono audio waveform, which is converted to a log-mel spectrogram (64 mel bins) and aggregated into a spatially global, temporally aligned audio state with one-to-one correspondence to visual frames. The output is a pixel-level binary segmentation mask delineating the sound-emitting object(s) at full input resolution. The entire pipeline is optimized for on-device inference, achieving 163.4 ms per frame on a Snapdragon 8 Elite CPU via the TNN framework.

## Method

LightAVSeg decouples cross-modal interaction into two linear-complexity stages: a Reciprocal Audio-Visual Encoder that iteratively refines a global audio token using max-pooled visual descriptors and h-sigmoid gated reweighting (semantic filtering: 'what is sounding'), then injects the refined audio state into visual features via channel-wise broadcast residual (spatial grounding: 'where'); and a Cross-Modal Fusion Decoder that maintains a recurrent audio state path during upsampling, re-grounding it at each scale with visual context before broadcasting back into the visual stream. This design replaces O(N²) dense attention with O(N) pointwise projections and broadcasting, using SeaFormer-Large (visual) and a frozen MobileNetV2 (audio) backbone. A training-only Multi-Scale Audio-Visual Alignment Loss (L_msa) computes temperature-scaled cosine similarity between ℓ₂-normalized audio and visual features at multiple decoder scales, maps scores through sigmoid, and supervises them with pixel-wise BCE against the foreground mask, providing a stable structural prior that is discarded at inference with zero overhead.

## Results

On the S4 benchmark, LightAVSeg achieves 75.6 mIoU / 86.2 F-score with only 20.5M parameters, rivaling the heavy AVSegFormer-R50 (76.5 mIoU, 151.1M params) while running 7.8× faster on mobile (163.4 ms vs. 1271.4 ms on Snapdragon 8 Elite). On the more complex MS3 benchmark, LightAVSeg reaches 50.4 mIoU / 62.6 F-score, surpassing AVSegFormer-R50 (49.5 mIoU) and outperforming all lightweight baselines by a wide margin. On the large-scale AVSS semantic segmentation benchmark, LightAVSeg achieves 30.6 mIoU, nearly doubling the AVSegFormer-Sea baseline (15.8 mIoU) and approaching SelM-R50 (31.9 mIoU).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AVSegFormer (Gao et al., 2024), LightAVSeg wins on MS3 (50.4 vs. 49.5 mIoU) and AVSS (30.6 vs. 24.9 mIoU) while being 7.8× faster on mobile; on S4 it trails by a narrow 0.9 mIoU (75.6 vs. 76.5) but uses only ~1/7 the parameters (20.5M vs. 151.1M). Against SelM (Li et al., 2024), LightAVSeg loses on MS3 (50.4 vs. 54.5 mIoU) and AVSS (30.6 vs. 31.9 mIoU) when SelM uses a ResNet-50 backbone, but decisively outperforms the lightweight SelM-Sea variant on all three benchmarks (e.g., 50.4 vs. 45.7 mIoU on MS3, 30.6 vs. 20.6 on AVSS). Against the original AVSBench (Zhou et al., 2022), LightAVSeg dominates across all settings: on MS3 it gains +2.5 mIoU over AVSBench-R50 and +15.2 mIoU over AVSBench-Sea, while also achieving the lowest mobile latency (163.4 ms vs. 753.5 ms and 237.1 ms respectively). Overall, LightAVSeg establishes the best accuracy–efficiency trade-off among all lightweight methods and is competitive with or superior to heavy ResNet-50-based SOTA on the harder multi-source and semantic benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the AVS lineage established by AVSBench (Zhou et al., 2022) and the Transformer-based fusion paradigm of AVSegFormer (Gao et al., 2024) and SelM (Li et al., 2024), but deliberately departs from their reliance on dense cross-modal attention. Rather than following the common efficiency strategy of swapping in a lighter backbone while retaining the quadratic interaction module, LightAVSeg identifies the interaction module itself as the primary bottleneck and replaces it with hierarchical channel-wise modulation (linear complexity), drawing design inspiration from mobile vision transformers (SeaFormer, TopFormer) and deep supervision. The auxiliary multi-scale alignment loss repurposes the deep-supervision principle (Lee et al., 2015; Xie & Tu, 2015) for cross-modal consistency, replacing the KL-divergence-based alignment in AVSBench with a more stable BCE objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: an 8× mobile speedup (163 ms vs. 1271 ms on Snapdragon 8 Elite) makes pixel-level AVS feasible for on-device applications like video editing and AR, a gap that prior SOTA methods could not bridge. The conceptual finding that a global channel-wise bias suffices to guide spatial segmentation—outperforming dense token-to-token attention on MS3—challenges the working assumption that fine-grained cross-modal affinity matrices are necessary. Open questions include whether knowledge distillation from heavy teachers can close the remaining fine-grained detail gap, whether the alignment principle transfers to other dense-prediction tasks beyond AVS, and how the method scales to higher resolutions or longer video sequences where the linear-complexity advantage becomes more pronounced.
