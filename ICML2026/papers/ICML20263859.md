## Problem

Parameter-based knowledge editing methods (ROME, MEMIT, AlphaEdit, etc.) appear to achieve near-perfect factual updates under narrow, teacher-forcing evaluation protocols, yet they lack a theoretical explanation for why localized weight modifications cause global capability degradation, and they have rarely been tested under realistic conditions such as sequential editing, event-level knowledge, and reasoning preservation. This paper asks two questions: what geometric mechanism makes LLM representations vulnerable to small parameter perturbations, and how do these methods actually perform when evaluated holistically across reliability, generalization, locality, portability, and downstream task retention?

## Input / Output

The editing input is a factual triple (h, r, t*) or a multi-prompt event-level knowledge set [(x₁,y₁),…,(xₘ,yₘ)] applied to a pretrained LLM's parameters; evaluation uses autoregressive decoding (not teacher-forcing) with semantic consistency judged by Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct, a deliberate departure from the token-level matching common in prior work. The output is either modified model weights (parameter-based methods) or an external knowledge store (retrieval-based SCR/GRACE), scored across four editing dimensions plus general-task retention on mathematical reasoning (AIME), GPQA-Diamond, ARC_c, and MMLU-Pro after 10 and 100 sequential edits.

## Method

The theoretical contribution builds a geometric framework on the Dimensional Collapse Hypothesis: SVD of hidden-state matrices reveals highly anisotropic singular-value spectra (condition numbers >10⁶, effective rank ~25–80% of d), creating fragile low-variance directions. A first-order Taylor expansion of the FFN layer shows that the directional relative change rate R_k = √n·|c_k|/σ_k is inversely proportional to the singular value, so even modest perturbations are disproportionately amplified in low-σ directions (Theorem 4.5); under sequential editing, distortions accumulate linearly as T·R_min^(1), and empirical measurements confirm that principal directions themselves drift, compounding the instability. The empirical contribution systematically evaluates nine editing methods (ROME, MEMIT, PMET, RECT, AlphaEdit, FT-L, MEND, AdaLoRA, WISE) plus two external-memory baselines (GRACE, SCR) across four LLMs (Llama-2-7B-Chat, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Mistral-7B-Instruct, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B) on three datasets (ZsRE, WikiData_counterfact, ELKEN) under single and sequential editing, revealing that all parameter-based methods collapse under sequential edits while the simple retrieval-based SCR baseline consistently outperforms them on every dimension.

## Results

On the ZsRE dataset with DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B, the retrieval-based baseline SCR achieves an average score of 60.19 under sequential editing (Rel. 90.00, Gen. 90.00, Loc. 15.50, Port. 45.26), far exceeding the best parameter-editing method AlphaEdit (24.16 avg). Under sequential editing, most parameter-based methods (ROME, RECT, MEND, AdaLoRA, FT-L) collapse to near-zero scores across all four dimensions, while even AlphaEdit's Locality and Portability drop to 8.00 and 7.62 respectively. On Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, AlphaEdit achieves Reliability 69.49 and Generalization 55.50 but only 8.38 Locality and 8.58 Portability, confirming that parameter edits systematically degrade core LLM capabilities regardless of the specific algorithm.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCR (selective contextual reasoning, a retrieval-based method) decisively outperforms all parameter-editing baselines across every evaluation dimension and editing setting, achieving 60.19 average under sequential editing versus 24.16 for the best parameter method (AlphaEdit). Among parameter-based methods, AlphaEdit is the most robust, maintaining non-zero scores under sequential editing (46.00 Rel., 35.00 Gen.), but its Locality (8.00) and Portability (7.62) remain critically low, indicating it cannot generalize edited knowledge to paraphrased or downstream reasoning queries. ROME, RECT, MEND, AdaLoRA, and FT-L all collapse to 0.00 or near-zero across all dimensions after sequential editing, representing complete failure. GRACE (external memory with discrete key-value adaptors) preserves Locality (15.50) but achieves only 3.00 Generalization, showing it cannot extend edits beyond the exact training prompts. On reasoning-oriented LLMs (DeepSeek-R1), all parameter methods perform worse than on general-purpose LLMs; for example, AlphaEdit's sequential average drops from 35.48 (Llama-3.1) to 24.16 (DeepSeek-R1), while SCR maintains 60.19. The paper also shows that after 100 sequential edits, ROME and RECT reasoning accuracy drops to near zero on mathematical benchmarks, while AlphaEdit retains most reasoning ability but still lags SCR in knowledge editing quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by providing a unified theoretical framework (the Dimensional Collapse Hypothesis) that explains why previously fragmented observations of editing failures—ripple effects (Wang et al., 2025), reasoning degradation (Gu et al., 2024b), model collapse (Yang et al., 2024b), and representation shattering (Nishi et al., 2025)—share a common geometric root cause: disproportionate amplification of perturbations in low-variance singular-value directions. Rather than proposing a new editing method, it repurposes SVD-based representation geometry analysis to characterize the fundamental limits of all parameter-modifying approaches (locate-then-edit, meta-learning, additional-parameter). Empirically, it departs from prior work by evaluating under autoregressive inference (not teacher-forcing), sequential editing, event-level knowledge, and reasoning-oriented LLMs—settings largely absent from prior benchmarks as summarized in their Table 4.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a simple retrieval-based baseline (SCR) consistently outperforms all parameter-editing methods across every evaluated condition challenges the field's implicit assumption that localized weight modification is the principled path for knowledge updates, suggesting the stability-efficiency trade-off may be fundamental rather than an engineering gap. The theoretical framework identifies a concrete, falsifiable mechanism (relative amplification in collapsed directions) that could guide future work toward regularization or architectural changes that protect low-variance subspaces. Key open questions include whether the dimensional collapse assumption holds universally across model scales and training regimes, whether improved constraints (e.g., null-space projection as in AlphaEdit) can mitigate the effect without sacrificing reliability, and whether the evaluation protocol (autoregressive + semantic consistency via a 72B judge) introduces its own biases. The authors explicitly caution against wholesale rejection of parameter editing, noting it may remain effective under carefully constrained conditions.
