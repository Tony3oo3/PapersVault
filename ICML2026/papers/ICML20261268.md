## Problem

Existing prior-free methods for non-stationary bandits, such as MASTER, are theoretically order-optimal but practically infeasible (requiring horizons of ~143 billion steps before triggering), while most other approaches are prior-based and assume knowledge of the non-stationarity structure. Additionally, the literature emphasizes theory over empirical evidence: order-optimal non-stationary methods are evaluated almost exclusively on synthetic data, and contextual bandits lack real-world experiments altogether. DAL addresses both gaps by providing a practical, hyperparameter-free black-box framework that detects actual reward-model changes rather than violations of stationary regret bounds.

## Input / Output

DAL takes as input any stationary bandit algorithm with order-optimal regret (e.g., LinUCB, GLM-UCB, SquareCB, REDS), a change detector (GLR or GSR test), a finite covering action set A_e constructed from structural properties of the reward class (d linearly independent actions for parametric bandits, δ_T-cover centers for kernelized bandits, or the full finite action set for contextual bandits), and a context set C. At each round it observes a context C_t and outputs either a forced-exploration action from A_e or the action chosen by the stationary algorithm, plus a binary restart signal that triggers a full reset of the bandit algorithm and clearing of all detection histories. The exploration frequency α_k = √(k|C|N_e)/(2√T log²T) is set entirely by theory, making the framework hyperparameter-free.

## Method

DAL is a modular black-box framework with three components: (1) a forced exploration scheme that periodically plays each action in a finite covering set A_e to maintain a stream of reward observations for change monitoring, (2) a change detector (Generalized Likelihood Ratio or Generalized Shiryaev-Roberts test) that monitors these reward sequences for mean-shifts using KL-divergence-based statistics with theoretically calibrated thresholds, and (3) the stationary bandit algorithm itself, which is fully reset whenever the detector flags a change. The key distinction from all prior prior-free methods is the 'detect-the-model' paradigm: DAL monitors actual reward distributions on structurally informative action pairs rather than checking for violations of stationary regret guarantees, which is what makes it practically triggerable. The covering set is designed so that any change in the reward function necessarily induces a detectable mean-shift on at least one monitored context-action pair, with |A_e| bounded by the structural complexity of the reward class (d for parametric, γ_T for kernelized, |A| for contextual). Under a mild change-point separation assumption, DAL inherits the order-optimal regret of its stationary input, matching state-of-the-art bounds for PS-LBs, PS-GLBs, and PS-CBs while improving the best known bounds for PS-SCBs and PS-KBs.

## Results

DAL achieves order-optimal dynamic regret across all bandit settings, matching state-of-the-art bounds for PS-LBs (Õ(d√(N_T T))), PS-GLBs (Õ(d√(N_T T))), and PS-CBs (Õ(√(|A|N_T T log|Π|))), while improving the best known bounds for PS-SCBs from Õ(d^{2/3}T^{2/3}N_T^{1/3}) to Õ(d√(N_T T)) and for PS-KBs from Õ(√(dγ_T N_T T)) to Õ(√(γ_T N_T T)). Empirically, DAL attains the lowest dynamic regret and highest cumulative reward across all 9 real-world benchmarks (micro-prefetcher, stock markets, COVID-NMA, Yahoo! R6A/R6B, Criteo, Bioliq, Zozo) and all synthetic PS and drifting scenarios, with runtime competitive with the fastest baselines and substantially faster than MASTER.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAL outperforms all six baselines in every synthetic and real-world experiment. Against MASTER (Wei & Luo, 2021), the only prior-free black-box method with order-optimal regret, DAL achieves substantially lower dynamic regret because MASTER's detection mechanism requires a horizon of at least 143 billion steps before triggering, making it practically inapplicable; DAL is also substantially faster in runtime. Against ADA-OPKB (Hong et al., 2023) for NS-LBs/NS-KBs, DAL wins on dynamic regret in all synthetic and real-world settings, and unlike ADA-OPKB requires no hyperparameter tuning (7 hyperparameters for ADA-OPKB vs. zero for DAL). Against ADA-ILTCB+ (Chen et al., 2019) for NS-CBs, DAL achieves lower dynamic regret on both Yahoo! R6A and R6B benchmarks. Against the prior-based WeightUCB (Wang et al., 2023), DAL outperforms it in PS settings across all parametric and contextual benchmarks, and in drifting settings DAL wins for small drift magnitudes (where variation is absorbed into noise) while WeightUCB wins for large drifts. Against WGP-UCB (Deng et al., 2022) for drifting KBs, DAL achieves lower dynamic regret on both stock market benchmarks. Against D-UCB (Kocsis & Szepesvári, 2006) in the micro-prefetcher benchmark, DAL achieves higher cumulative reward.

## Relation to Prior Work

DAL extends the change-detection-based restart paradigm from PS-MABs (Huang et al., 2025; Besson et al., 2022) to general bandits (linear, generalized linear, self-concordant, kernelized, and contextual) via a black-box reduction: any order-optimal stationary algorithm can be plugged in. The key conceptual departure is from all prior prior-free methods (notably MASTER, the only other black-box order-optimal approach), which detect violations of stationary regret bounds; DAL instead detects actual mean-shifts in reward distributions using GLR/GSR change-point tests, a 'detect-the-model' paradigm that the authors argue is both more practical and more effective. The theoretical framework builds directly on the detection-latency analysis of Huang & Veeravalli (2025) and the modular regret decomposition of Huang et al. (2025), but requires new analysis to handle general bandit feedback and the covering-set construction for infinite action spaces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DAL closes a significant gap between theory and practice in non-stationary bandits: MASTER, the only prior-free black-box method with order-optimal regret, requires a horizon of ~143 billion steps before its detector can trigger, rendering it useless in practice. By providing the first practical prior-free black-box framework with order-optimal guarantees across all major bandit variants, and by delivering the first comprehensive real-world evaluation of order-optimal NS bandit methods (9 datasets spanning microarchitecture, finance, clinical trials, and advertising), DAL makes these theoretically optimal methods accessible to practitioners. Open questions include: (i) regret guarantees for drifting (non-piecewise) environments remain unproven, (ii) the change-point separation assumption (Assumption 4.6) limits the scope of the theoretical optimality claim, and (iii) extension to reinforcement learning is identified but not yet pursued.
