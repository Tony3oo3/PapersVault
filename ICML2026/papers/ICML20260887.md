## Problem

Existing visual preference DPO methods for mitigating multimodal hallucinations suffer from two critical flaws: (1) a theoretically inconsistent objective, where contrasting different images causes partition functions to fail to cancel, introducing an intractable bias into the DPO loss; and (2) reliance on coarse-grained negatives from retrieval or text-to-image synthesis, which exhibit global stylistic shifts that enable shortcut learning rather than fine-grained visual grounding.

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes a single RGB image and a text prompt and produces a text response, matching standard LVLM usage. During training, however, the method constructs a shared multi-image context M = [m, m'] containing both the original and contrastive images, appended with a positional anchor prompt (e.g., 'respond based on the first image') to disambiguate the target. The image order is randomized per sample to eliminate position bias, and token-level masks are applied to edited evidence tokens for fine-grained preference scoring on the single-image branch.

## Method

IC-VCO places both the original image m and contrastive image m' within a single shared multi-image context, so that chosen and rejected responses are scored under the identical conditioning (M, x̂), ensuring the partition function Z(M, x̂) cancels exactly and yielding a theory-consistent DPO objective. A Visual Contrast Distillation (VCDist) regularizer then bridges the train-inference gap by using the multi-image preference distribution as a soft teacher to calibrate the single-image branch, gated by a correctness filter (p_multi > 0.5) and a confidence filter (p_single < p_multi) with stop-gradient on teacher logits. To generate hard negatives that prevent shortcut learning, a Contrastive Sample Editing pipeline uses QwenVL-Plus as an expert planner to formulate surgical edit instructions (existence, attribute, or relation changes), applies them via Qwen-Image-Edit with a reversible padding procedure to preserve spatial alignment, and verifies fidelity—producing 19,453 fine-grained contrastive samples with 91% success rate and a mean CLIP similarity of 94.72 to originals versus 72.88 for synthetic baselines. The full objective jointly optimizes multi-image and single-image DPO branches with anchor losses, VCDist, and symmetrical preference pairs, plus token-level masking on the single-image branch to concentrate supervision on edited visual evidence.

## Results

On LLaVA-NeXT-Interleave-Qwen-7B with contrastive edited samples, IC-VCO achieves the best overall score of 63.35, outperforming the strongest baseline (SymMPO, 62.11) by 1.24 points. On LLaVA-OneVision-Qwen2-7B with the same edited samples, IC-VCO reaches 66.82 overall, surpassing the best baseline (S-VCO, 66.34) by 0.48 points. IC-VCO attains the best AMBER-Attr score in every setting (e.g., 82.24 on LLaVA-NeXT-Interleave with edited samples) and consistently improves BLINK perception scores (49.44 vs. 45.13 base). Replacing synthetic contrastive images with the proposed edited hard negatives improves all methods, with baseline gains ranging from 0.08 to 1.23 points across the two backbones.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IC-VCO achieves the best overall score across all settings on both backbones. Against DPO (standard textual preference), IC-VCO gains +2.95 points on LLaVA-NeXT-Interleave and +0.74 on LLaVA-OneVision with edited samples, confirming that explicit visual contrastive supervision is critical for hallucination mitigation. Against mDPO and V-DPO, which introduce visual contrast but suffer from partition function mismatch, IC-VCO leads by +1.33/+2.97 (NeXT) and +0.58/+0.51 (OneVision) respectively. Against S-VCO, the closest symmetrical visual contrastive baseline, IC-VCO wins by +1.94 (NeXT) and +0.48 (OneVision), with the gap attributable to the shared-context formulation eliminating the intractable partition function bias. Against SymMPO, which also addresses theoretical consistency but via separate single-image conditions, IC-VCO leads by +1.24 (NeXT) and +0.69 (OneVision), benefiting from in-context visual comparison and the VCDist regularizer. IC-VCO is strongest on attribute and existence grounding (AMBER-Attr, AMBER-Exist, CRPE-Exist) but does not uniformly dominate on relation-centric metrics (AMBER-Rel, CRPE-Rel), likely due to the edited dataset containing only 828 relation-type samples out of 19,453.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the visual preference optimization line initiated by mDPO, V-DPO, S-VCO, and SymMPO, which all attempt to inject explicit visual supervision into DPO-style training for hallucination mitigation. Its key departure is twofold: (1) it identifies that prior visual DPO formulations (S-VCO, V-DPO, and the visual-preference variant of SymMPO) suffer from a partition function mismatch that makes their objectives theoretically inconsistent with the original DPO derivation, and fixes this by placing both contrastive images in a shared multi-image context so the partition function cancels; (2) it introduces a surgical contrastive sample editing pipeline that generates fine-grained hard negatives, in contrast to the retrieval- or synthesis-based coarse negatives used by prior methods. The VCDist distillation regularizer bridges the train-inference gap between the multi-image training context and single-image inference, a component absent from all prior visual DPO methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The partition function consistency argument provides a cleaner theoretical foundation for visual preference optimization, and the contrastive editing pipeline is broadly applicable—experiments show it improves all baseline methods, not just IC-VCO, suggesting it could be adopted as a general data-quality upgrade for the field. However, practical gains remain modest (≤1.24 points overall), the edited dataset is heavily skewed toward attribute and existence edits (relation edits are only 828/19,453 samples), and the pipeline depends on external expert VLMs and image-editing models, adding cost and potential bias. Open questions include whether the in-context multi-image formulation generalizes to longer multi-image sequences or video, whether the editing strategy scales to more complex compositional hallucinations, and whether the modest partition-function bias actually matters at larger model scales where the residual ratio may be negligible.
