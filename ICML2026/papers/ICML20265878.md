## Problem

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have shown strong general video understanding but remain untested on interpreting involuntary, spatiotemporally evolving pathologic motor behaviors such as seizure semiology. The field lacks a large-scale dataset with dense expert annotations, a comprehensive task suite spanning perception to clinical reasoning, and evaluation metrics aligned with clinical priorities like laterality and temporal evolution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265878/cropped_page2_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

Input consists of full-body seizure videos (1920×1080, 1280×720, or 640×480; average duration ~120s) processed under task-specific frame-sampling protocols: 2 FPS sliding windows (30s, 5s overlap) for detection and description tasks, 60s event-centric clips at 1 FPS for lateralization and temporal localization, and 60–120 uniformly sampled frames for global diagnosis. Outputs span a seven-task hierarchy from binary yes/no labels on 20 ILAE-defined features, through free-text justifications, left/right laterality calls, MM:SS onset/offset timestamps, chronologically ordered symptom sequences, and narrative clinical reports scored by the Seizure-RQI, up to a final ES-vs-NES diagnosis. An optional clinician-written report can be co-fed to the model for report-augmented diagnosis.

## Method

The paper introduces a 438-video, 35,000+-label dataset annotated by epileptologists and trained annotators (Kappa 0.84) across 20 ILAE semiological features, paired with a seven-task hierarchical benchmark and a clinically grounded Report Quality Index (Seizure-RQI) that weights structural completeness, symptom coverage, key localizing features, and temporal fidelity while penalizing hallucinations, off-topic content, verbosity, and hazardous statements. For model improvement, the authors finetune Qwen2.5-Omni-7B via LoRA-based SFT and GRPO reinforcement learning with task-specific reward functions (accuracy, BLEU/ROUGE, temporal proximity, LCS ratio), yielding 12–15% average relative gains across six of seven tasks. The key architectural contribution is a two-stage neuro-symbolic classifier: the MLLM acts as a perception module producing a 20-dimensional binary semiology vector, which is then fed to a Random Forest for ES/NES diagnosis, decoupling visual recognition from logical classification and achieving F1 = 0.96—substantially outperforming both direct MLLM inference (F1 ≈ 0.70) and report-augmented MLLM diagnosis (F1 ≈ 0.79). Extensive baselines across 11 open-weight MLLMs (Qwen-VL, InternVL, Qwen-Omni, Audio-Flamingo-3, Lingshu) reveal systematic failures in spatial laterality (mean F1 < 0.2), temporal localization (best MAE 8.19s), and symptom sequencing, confirming that scaling alone does not close the clinical gap.

## Results

On the seven-task Seizure-Semiology-Bench, the two-stage neuro-symbolic framework (MLLM feature extraction + Random Forest) achieves an F1 of 0.96 for epileptic vs. non-epileptic seizure classification using the seizure_omni_sft model, improving the average F1 from 0.70 (direct MLLM) to 0.86 across all models. For narrative report generation (Task 6), Lingshu-32B attains the highest Seizure-RQI of 39.80, closely followed by Qwen3-VL-32B (39.13), while conventional NLP metrics (BLEU, ROUGE-L, BERTScore) show negligible correlation with expert judgment (Pearson r ≤ 0.10) versus Seizure-RQI's r = 0.57. Spatial laterality reasoning (Task 3) remains a critical failure point for all models, with mean F1 consistently below 0.2 even under explicit patient-perspective prompting. Seizure-specific finetuning (SFT/GRPO on Qwen2.5-Omni-7B) yields a 12–15% average relative improvement across six of seven tasks, with Task 1 mean F1 rising from 0.38 to 0.47 (SFT).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks 11 open-weight MLLMs (Qwen2.5-VL 7B/32B/72B, Qwen3-VL 8B/32B, InternVL3.5 8B/38B, Qwen2.5-Omni-7B, Qwen3-Omni-30B, Audio-Flamingo-3, Lingshu-32B) plus two seizure-finetuned variants. The Qwen-VL family consistently outperforms InternVL across all scales (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-32B vs. InternVL3.5-38B on Task 1 F1), and scaling from 7B to 72B yields only marginal Task 1 gains (~0.42–0.45 mean F1), indicating architectural rather than capacity bottlenecks. Qwen3-Omni-30B outperforms both vision-only Qwen2.5-VL-32B and audio-only Audio-Flamingo-3 on sound-associated features, validating multimodal fusion. Lingshu-32B achieves the highest RQI (39.80) in video-only report generation but drops sharply to F1 0.60 with report augmentation (vs. 0.84 video-only), revealing limited language-reasoning capacity despite strong medical visual priors. The two-stage classification strategy uniformly outperforms direct MLLM diagnosis: average F1 rises from 0.70 (w/o report) to 0.86 (two-stage), and even surpasses report-augmented MLLM classification (0.79 → 0.86). Seizure-specific SFT finetuning of Qwen2.5-Omni-7B achieves the best Task 1 mean F1 (0.47), exceeding the much larger Qwen2.5-VL-72B, while GRPO further reduces Task 4 MAE from 25.50s to 20.02s (21.5% improvement) but suffers from BLEU/ROUGE-based reward misalignment causing repetitive outputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the narrow, task-specific pipelines that have dominated computational seizure analysis (3D CNNs for tonic-clonic detection, accelerometry, optical flow segmentation, single-label classifiers) by reframing seizure semiology understanding as a structured, generative, multi-level problem amenable to MLLMs. It also carves new ground relative to general video understanding benchmarks (ActivityNet-QA, MotionBench) and medical video datasets (MedVidQA, SV-RCNet), which assume intentional, goal-directed actions with prescriptive temporal structure, whereas seizure semiology involves involuntary, concurrent, and spatiotemporally evolving pathologic motor behaviors. The two-stage neuro-symbolic framework (MLLM as structured feature extractor + Random Forest classifier) repurposes the MLLM's perceptual strengths while offloading diagnostic logic to an interpretable statistical model, a design choice not seen in prior end-to-end seizure classifiers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The S³ suite provides the first large-scale, expert-annotated resource (438 videos, 35,000+ labels across 20 ILAE features) and a clinically grounded 7-task benchmark that exposes fundamental MLLM limitations in spatial laterality reasoning, temporal localization, and faithful clinical reporting—gaps that are safety-critical in epilepsy diagnosis. The finding that scaling from 7B to 72B parameters yields negligible improvement (~0.42–0.45 mean F1) challenges the assumption that model size alone resolves domain-specific perceptual deficits, pointing instead to architectural or data-coverage bottlenecks. Key limitations include single-center adult cohort, low-framerate processing constraints, and the catastrophic forgetting of spatial reasoning during finetuning on limited laterality samples. Open questions include whether agentic iterative refinement, 3D pose fusion, or EEG-multimodal integration can close the temporal and spatial gaps that current feed-forward MLLMs cannot.
