## Problem

Existing automated LLM probing methods discover isolated failure cases without principled budget allocation, lack benchmark-agnosticism, and rely on unvalidated test cases that conflate genuine model weaknesses with evaluation noise. Static benchmarks cannot track shifting error distributions as models evolve, and prior automated approaches remain case-centric rather than identifying the structured, recurring failure modes that explain how and why models systematically fail.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261704/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

PROBELLM takes a target LLM, seed test cases from any existing benchmark (MMLU, SuperGLUE, MBPP, HellaSwag, TruthfulQA), and a fixed simulation budget (100 per benchmark) as input. It produces individual verified failure cases, HDBSCAN-clustered failure modes, and natural-language failure-mode descriptions generated via boundary-aware induction that pairs central failures with contrastive boundary/non-failure pairs. The framework is benchmark-agnostic and restricted to test cases with verifiable ground-truth answers to ensure reliable failure signals.

## Method

PROBELLM formulates adaptive probing as a two-level hierarchical MCTS: the top level uses UCB to allocate budget between MACRO (broad topical exploration via embedding-space clustering to steer generation toward under-represented regions) and MICRO (local perturbation around promising seeds to densify failure evidence), while the second level performs standard MCTS traversal with UCB selection within each regime's tree. Test case generation is tool-augmented (Python execution, web retrieval) to ground question construction and verify ground-truth answers, reducing spurious failures. Discovered failures are consolidated into interpretable failure modes via failure-aware embeddings (concatenating problem description with LLM-derived error descriptions), HDBSCAN clustering, and boundary-aware induction that selects central failures plus boundary failures paired with their nearest verified non-failures to elicit concise, non-over-generalized mode descriptions.

## Results

PROBELLM achieves error rates of 71–90% across five benchmarks (HellaSwag, MBPP, MMLU, SuperGLUE, TruthfulQA) with GPT-5.2 as generator and Llama-3.1-8b-ins. as target, substantially exceeding AutoDetect (38–67%) and PAIR (35–46%). In terms of failure-mode structure, PROBELLM discovers 10–19 clusters with average sizes of 10.6–13.6, compared to only 2–6 clusters with average sizes of 25–114 from static benchmarks, indicating finer-grained and more diverse failure modes. Noise rates are 31.7–50.5% for PROBELLM versus 60.9–77.2% for AutoDetect, and cluster-size standard deviations are 3.2–9.2 versus 13.2–22.9, confirming cleaner and more balanced cluster structures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PROBELLM outperforms both baselines on all five datasets. Against AutoDetect, PROBELLM gains 19–44 percentage points in error rate (e.g., 90% vs. 46% on MBPP, 75% vs. 45% on HellaSwag), and also achieves substantially lower noise rates (31.7–50.5% vs. 60.9–77.2%) and tighter cluster-size standard deviations (3.2–9.2 vs. 13.2–22.9), indicating fewer spurious discoveries and more coherent failure modes. Against PAIR, PROBELLM gains 27–52 percentage points in error rate (e.g., 90% vs. 38% on MBPP, 86% vs. 46% on MMLU). Notably, PAIR achieves very high final attack success rates (89.5–99.1%) yet induces much lower error rates, suggesting that high attack success does not translate to broader failure discovery. PROBELLM's advantage is further reflected in its ability to produce more clusters (10–19) with smaller average sizes (10.6–13.6) compared to static benchmarks (2–6 clusters, avg. size 25–114), demonstrating finer-grained failure-mode resolution.

## Relation to Prior Work

PROBELLM repurposes hierarchical Monte Carlo Tree Search—previously applied to LLM reasoning and planning (Xie et al., 2024; Feng et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2024b)—as a budgeted exploration mechanism over the failure space, a use case absent from prior MCTS-in-LLM work. It departs from case-centric automated probing methods like AutoDetect (Cheng et al., 2024) and PAIR (Chao et al., 2025) by explicitly formulating probing as a two-level MACRO/MICRO search that allocates budget between global coverage and local refinement, and by consolidating discovered failures into structured failure modes via failure-aware embeddings and boundary-aware induction rather than leaving them as isolated error instances. The tool-augmented generation with verifiable ground-truth answers distinguishes it from prior pipelines that rely on unvalidated LLM-generated test cases.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PROBELLM provides a practical, low-cost (<$0.10 per high-error-rate test case) pathway from raw failure cases to interpretable, actionable failure modes that can directly inform targeted fine-tuning (demonstrated via GRPO), shifting LLM evaluation from static snapshots toward dynamic, mode-centric diagnosis. Key limitations include the restriction to verifiable ground-truth answers (open-ended generation is deferred to future work) and reliance on an LLM-based judge whose reliability, while validated, introduces a single point of failure. Open questions include whether discovered failure modes transfer across model families, how the approach scales to agentic or multi-turn settings, and whether the boundary-aware induction generalizes beyond the HDBSCAN clustering regime.
