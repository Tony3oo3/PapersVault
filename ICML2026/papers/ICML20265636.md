## Problem

High-curvature regions in 3D point clouds (corners, edges, fine details) are sparsely distributed yet carry critical geometric semantics, yet Euclidean feature spaces with polynomial volume growth force these rare, complex structures to compete for limited representational capacity against ubiquitous low-curvature surfaces. This capacity imbalance causes 'representation crowding,' where fine-grained geometric cues are compressed into restricted subspaces and subsumed by dominant global patterns, degrading boundary localization and class separability in geometrically complex regions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud P ∈ R^{N×3} and optional auxiliary features F ∈ R^{N×M} (channel-wise concatenated), from which it extracts PCA-based point-wise curvature κ ∈ R^N and initial semantic embeddings via a backbone encoder (default: CamPoint). The CHR module is a plug-and-play block inserted after the backbone's main feature extraction stage, producing rectified Euclidean features H_out ∈ R^{N×C'} that feed into a task-specific decoder for segmentation or classification. Curvature is normalized via min-max clipping with κ_max=0.2 to suppress outliers, and the module operates with a numerical stability margin ε=10^{-5} to keep embeddings within the Poincaré ball.

## Method

PointCHR introduces a Curvature-Aware Hyperbolic Rectification (CHR) module with three stages. First, Hyperbolic Semantic Transformation (HST) lifts Euclidean features onto the Poincaré ball via the exponential map and applies Möbius linear layers with Möbius GELU activations to perform semantic mixing while preserving the conformal geometry of the manifold. Second, Point-wise Curvature-Adaptive Perception (PCP) uses a lightweight MLP to fuse the PCA-estimated curvature with local semantic context, producing a point-wise gating factor that is combined with curvature via s_i = 1 + α·g_i·κ_i^γ to determine per-point radial dilation strength. Third, Closed-Form Geodesic Dilation (CGD) scales each point's geodesic distance from the origin by s_i using a tangent-space-free Möbius scalar multiplication (z' = (1/√c)·tanh(s_i·arctanh(√c·r̃))·z/r̃), which is intrinsically bounded within the ball and avoids numerical overflow near the asymptotic boundary. The rectified embeddings are then projected back to Euclidean space via the logarithmic map and LayerNorm, enabling seamless integration with downstream decoders. The key insight is that hyperbolic space's exponential volume growth near the boundary provides a principled mechanism to allocate disproportionate representational capacity to sparse high-curvature regions, fundamentally resolving the crowding bottleneck that isotropic Euclidean scaling cannot address.

## Results

On S3DIS semantic segmentation (the primary task), PointCHR achieves 86.0% mIoU on Area 5 and 89.1% on 6-fold cross-validation, surpassing the previous best (CamPoint, 83.3% Area 5) by 2.7 points. On ShapeNetPart part segmentation, PointCHR reaches 87.0% instance mIoU and 85.7% class mIoU. For shape classification, PointCHR attains 93.7% OA / 92.0% mAcc on ModelNet40 and 92.7% OA / 91.7% mAcc on the challenging ScanObjectNN PB_T50_RS variant, all setting new state-of-the-art results.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PointCHR outperforms all 10 compared baselines on S3DIS, with the closest competitor CamPoint (CVPR'25) trailing by 2.7 mIoU on Area 5 (83.3% vs 86.0%) and Sonata (CVPR'25) trailing by 6.8 mIoU on 6-fold (82.3% vs 89.1%). On ShapeNetPart, PointCHR edges out PCM (AAAI'25) by 0.1% instance mIoU (87.0% vs 86.9%) and 0.7% class mIoU (85.7% vs 85.0%). On ModelNet40, PointCHR ties PointSD (ICCV'25) at 93.7% OA but leads in mAcc (92.0% vs not reported), and on ScanObjectNN PB_T50_RS it surpasses CamPoint by 0.6% OA (92.7% vs 92.1%) and 0.6% mAcc (91.7% vs 91.1%). The gains are most pronounced in high-curvature regions: curvature-stratified analysis shows a +10.4% mIoU improvement in the highest-curvature bin (2.13% of points) versus only +2.2% in the lowest-curvature bin, confirming the method's targeted benefit on geometrically complex structures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: hyperbolic deep learning (Poincaré embeddings, Möbius transformations, hyperbolic vision transformers) and curvature-aware point cloud analysis (RepSurf, CurveNet, CanonNet). While prior hyperbolic methods have been applied to graphs, images, and 2D vision, this is the first to exploit the exponential volume growth of the Poincaré ball specifically to resolve representation crowding in 3D point clouds. The key conceptual departure from prior curvature-based methods is that rather than using curvature as an input feature or attention weight within a Euclidean backbone, PointCHR uses curvature to dynamically control radial positioning on a hyperbolic manifold, thereby reallocating representational capacity in a geometry-aware manner. The closed-form geodesic dilation (CGD) component also departs from standard tangent-space hyperbolic operations by avoiding the numerical instability that plagues boundary regions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design (consistent gains when inserted into PointMLP, DeLA, and PointNext-s) makes this a broadly applicable module for any point cloud backbone, and the curvature-stratified analysis (+10.4% mIoU in the highest-curvature bin) provides mechanistic evidence that the method targets the right bottleneck. The negative result on curvature-guided resampling (CGR degrades performance to 79.54% vs. 83.30% baseline) is practically important: it demonstrates that the limitation is in feature-space capacity rather than data density, ruling out a simpler data-level fix. Open questions include whether the hyperbolic formulation remains advantageous at very high embedding dimensions where Euclidean capacity may suffice, how the method behaves on outdoor/autonomous-driving point clouds with different curvature distributions, and whether the CamPoint backbone dependency limits the generality of the reported SOTA numbers.
