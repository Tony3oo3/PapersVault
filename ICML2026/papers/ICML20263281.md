## Problem

Existing single-image watermark removal attacks optimize for high removal success and low perceptual distortion but rely on expensive iterative optimization or surrogate models, making them impractical under realistic compute budgets. The paper demonstrates that no current approach simultaneously achieves all three desirable properties—removal success, low distortion, and low computational cost—and identifies this trade-off as fundamental to the single-image, no-box threat model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263281/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

DAWN takes a single watermarked RGB image as input with no access to the watermarking algorithm, embedding key, detector, generative model parameters, or additional watermarked exemplars. It produces a watermark-removed image that evades detection while preserving semantic content. The attack is entirely inference-only at deployment: the frequency-domain UNet is trained once offline on 20k clean images (MS-COCO + Stable Diffusion Prompts), and the diffusion refiner uses frozen pretrained weights, so the per-image cost is dominated by a small number of feed-forward passes rather than gradient-based optimization.

## Method

DAWN is a three-stage, training-free-at-inference pipeline that projects a watermarked image onto natural-image priors in two complementary domains. Step 1 applies 8×8 blockwise DCT and denoises the coefficients with a lightweight frequency-domain UNet (trained offline with synthetic structured spectral noise and a learnable frequency mask under an ℓ₁ loss) to suppress the narrow-band spectral artifacts characteristic of watermark signals, then applies IDCT. Step 2 refines the reconstructed image with a pretrained Stable Diffusion v2 img2img model (~50 diffusion steps) to restore global semantic coherence and further erode watermark evidence aligned with high-level structure. An optional Step 3 applies channel-wise mean-variance matching (or neural style transfer) to correct residual hue/tone shifts without reintroducing watermark evidence. Ablations confirm that frequency-domain reconstruction is the dominant driver of watermark weakening—removing it collapses success from ~77% to ~27%—while semantic refinement stabilizes structure and perceptual alignment improves visual fidelity with only a minor success trade-off.

## Results

DAWN achieves >95% attack success on classical pixel/frequency watermarks (DWTDCT 99.8%, RIVAGAN 100%, SSL 95.8–97.0%) with PSNR ≈ 28 dB and LPIPS ≈ 0.47–0.53. On semantic watermarks, success is 70.2–77.4% for TREE-RING, 92.2–94.4% for ZODIAC, and 65.6–67.8% for PRC-WATERMARK, at the cost of lower PSNR (14.6–16.9 dB) and higher LPIPS (0.54–0.67). Luminance-based metrics (SSIM_lum, CLIP_lum) remain near 0.99 across all schemes, confirming that deviations are primarily chromatic rather than structural.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the diffusion-based regeneration baseline (Zhao et al., 2024), DAWN wins decisively on watermark removal: pixel-only img2img regeneration universally fails on TREE-RING (median p-value 5.1×10⁻³¹, i.e., watermark still strongly detected) and ZODIAC, whereas DAWN achieves 70–77% success on TREE-RING and 92–94% on ZODIAC. Regeneration does preserve higher perceptual fidelity (LPIPS 0.10 vs. 0.64, CLIP 0.99 vs. 0.73 on TREE-RING), but this comes at the cost of near-zero attack success. Against the optimization-based imprint-removal attack (Müller et al., 2025), DAWN trades a small amount of perceptual quality for a ~16× reduction in FLOPs per image (50 inference-only diffusion steps vs. ~100 iterative optimization steps with a surrogate generator). Imprint-removal achieves slightly higher PSNR/SSIM on TREE-RING but requires substantially more compute and a surrogate model, while DAWN operates training-free and model-agnostic. The paper frames this as DAWN occupying the low-compute, moderate-distortion corner of the removability–distortion–compute tradeoff, whereas imprint-removal sits in the high-success, high-quality, high-compute corner and regeneration in the low-compute, high-fidelity, near-zero-success corner.

## Relation to Prior Work

DAWN builds directly on the frequency-domain UNet architecture from Xu et al. (2020, 'Learning in the frequency domain'), repurposing it not as a restoration tool but as a watermark-agnostic spectral projector that enforces natural 1/f^α decay. Conceptually, it departs from the two dominant prior attack families—pixel-space regeneration (Zhao et al., 2024) and iterative latent optimization (Müller et al., 2025)—by reframing watermark removal as a single-shot projection onto natural-image priors in complementary frequency and semantic spaces, explicitly targeting the low-compute regime that prior methods ignore. The dual-domain combination (DCT-block UNet + frozen diffusion img2img) is a novel pipeline, though each component individually predates this work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a concrete removability–distortion–compute tradeoff for single-image watermark attacks and shows that no existing method occupies all three favorable corners simultaneously, a framing that should guide both watermark designers and security auditors. Practically, it demonstrates that widely deployed semantic watermarks (TREE-RING, ZODIAC) can be weakened 60–90% of the time with a training-free, ~50-step inference pipeline at roughly 1/16 the FLOPs of optimization-based attacks, raising the bar for what 'robust' means in provenance tracking. Key open questions include whether distribution-preserving latent schemes (Gaussian Shading, SFWMark) represent a genuine defense or merely a gap that geometric/latent-manifold attacks will close, and whether multi-hierarchy embedding (across spectral bands and semantic levels) can resist frequency-projection attacks without sacrificing generation quality.
