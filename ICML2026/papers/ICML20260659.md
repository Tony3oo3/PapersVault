## Problem

Existing agentic task synthesis pipelines scale data by quantity or difficulty but remain confined to narrow task families and fixed toolsets (e.g., deep-research with web search), causing trained agents to over-rely on rigid routines and exhibit poor or even negative transfer on unseen task types and toolsets. The core tension is that scaling structural diversity (heterogeneous tool types, toolset combinations, and multi-step patterns) while maintaining grounded validity (every task must be executable and verifiable under its specific toolset) has not been reconciled by prior approaches.

## Input / Output

At inference, the agent receives a natural-language task query and a per-task toolset of 15–50 tools drawn from a 373-tool pool spanning five domains (Finance, Medical, Academic, Biological, General), and produces an interleaved reasoning-and-tool-call trajectory (up to 100 turns) ending in a concise factual answer. During synthesis, the pipeline consumes a seed concept, a sampled toolset, and 3–5 query-only exemplars, and outputs query–answer–toolset triples where the answer is strictly entailed by real tool execution traces, enabling deterministic verification for both SFT rejection sampling and RL reward computation.

## Method

DIVE inverts the standard query-first synthesis order: an evidence collector agent first executes real tools under a sampled toolset to accumulate grounded execution traces, then a task generator reverse-derives a query–answer pair strictly entailed by those traces, guaranteeing executability and verifiability by construction. Structural diversity is scaled along two controllable axes—tool-pool coverage (expanding from 1 to 4 expert domains, 174→373 tools) and per-task toolset variety (random 15–50 tool subsets)—while an iterative Evidence Collection–Task Derivation loop (K=3 rounds) progressively induces richer multi-step patterns (retrieval-only → retrieval-then-process → mixed topologies). Training is two-stage: a supervised cold start (48k SFT trajectories via GPT-OSS-120B teacher with rejection sampling) followed by GRPO reinforcement learning on 3.2k frontier tasks (1–5 successes in pass@8), using a composite reward of answer correctness plus format penalty. Controlled scaling experiments show that diversity scaling (even with 4× less data) consistently outperforms quantity scaling for OOD generalization, and RL amplifies the diversity-scaling trend beyond what SFT imitation alone achieves.

## Results

DIVE-8B (RL) improves by +22.2 points on average across 9 OOD benchmarks over the Qwen3-8B base model, outperforming the strongest 8B baseline (EnvScaler-8B) by +68%. On in-distribution DIVE-Eval, DIVE-8B (RL) reaches 42.5% vs. 13.0% for the base model. On specialized L3 benchmarks, DIVE-8B (RL) achieves 34.0% on Finance Agent Benchmark, 57.3% on MedAgentBench, 18.3% on SWE-bench Verified, and 8.3% on Toolathlon (zero-shot, per-task MCP toolsets), approaching GPT-OSS-120B (9.8%) and Gemini-2.5-Pro (10.5%) on Toolathlon despite being an 8B model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against 8B baselines, DIVE-8B (RL) dominates across all 9 OOD benchmarks, outperforming the strongest 8B baseline (EnvScaler-8B) by +68% on average. On GAIA, DIVE-8B (RL) scores 61.2% vs. 50.0% for WebExplorer-8B (a specialist for deep research), while WebExplorer-8B exhibits negative transfer on L3 benchmarks (dropping 5.8 points below the base model on FAB). DIVE-8B (RL) also surpasses SWE-Dev-8B on SWE-bench Verified (18.3% vs. 19.5% is a near-tie, but DIVE leads on all other benchmarks by wide margins). Compared to frontier models, DIVE-8B (RL) is competitive with GPT-OSS-120B on FinSearchComp (67.3% vs. 61.0% on FSC₂; 37.3% vs. 22.0% on FSC₃) and approaches Gemini-2.5-Pro on Toolathlon (8.3% vs. 10.5%), but trails Gemini-3-Pro and Claude-4-Sonnet on most benchmarks, particularly HLE (17.8% vs. 42.9%/20.8%) and BrowseComp (16.4% vs. 49.0%/12.8%). The paper also reports that DIVE achieves a 3.2× larger OOD lift compared to EnvScaler-8B, validating the evidence-first synthesis approach over query-first synthesis in simulated environments.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant query-first paradigm in agentic task synthesis (ToolLLM, TaskBench, MCP-AgentBench), where tasks are generated first and validity is checked post hoc, by inverting the synthesis order: executing diverse real-world tools first and reverse-deriving tasks strictly entailed by the resulting traces. It also contrasts with simulated-environment approaches (AgentInstruct, SynthTools, EnvScaler) that sacrifice verifiability for scalability, and with fixed-toolset pipelines (WebExplorer, WebSailor) that scale quantity but not structural diversity. The conceptual lineage draws on ReAct-style interleaved reasoning for the agent formulation and GRPO for the RL stage, but the core contribution is the evidence-driven synthesis loop that guarantees executability and verifiability by construction while scaling two controllable diversity axes (tool-pool coverage and per-task toolset variety).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that diversity scaling consistently outperforms quantity scaling for OOD generalization—even with 4× less data—challenges the default assumption that more homogeneous data is always better, and provides a concrete, actionable recipe (evidence-first synthesis on real tools) that practitioners can adopt to improve tool-use generalization without massive compute. The RL amplification effect (the RL-SFT gap widens with diversity) suggests that exploration-based training benefits disproportionately from structurally diverse data, opening questions about optimal diversity-quantity tradeoffs at larger model scales. Limitations include reliance on a single 8B backbone, five expert domains, and Claude-4-Sonnet as the synthesis oracle; whether the approach transfers to non-API tools, multimodal tool use, or models beyond 8B remains open.
