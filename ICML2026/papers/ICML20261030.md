## Problem

Asynchronous SGD (ASGD) suffers from stragglers: slow workers introduce stale gradients that degrade convergence, and the oracle complexity of vanilla ASGD depends on the maximum delay. While gradient clipping empirically stabilizes asynchronous deep learning training, no prior work provided a theoretical justification for this effect, and no high-probability convergence guarantees existed for any asynchronous optimization algorithm.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a set of n stochastic gradient oracles (workers), a clipping radius c, a constant step size η, and a concurrency parameter τ_C ≤ n. Workers return clipped stochastic gradients g_t^i(x_t) = clip_c(∇F_i(x_t, ξ_t^i)), and the server updates the model using the most recently completed gradient. The output is a sequence of iterates whose average gradient norm converges to ε-stationarity, with oracle complexity bounds that are independent of the maximum delay τ_max. The gradient noise is modeled as sub-Weibull (generalizing sub-Gaussian and sub-exponential), with tail parameter θ and scale σ.

## Method

The core insight is that gradient clipping bounds the difference between a virtual sequence (evolving like serial SGD) and the actual asynchronous iterates by ηcτ_C, regardless of individual delays. This is established via perturbed iterate analysis: since all clipped gradients have norm at most c, the virtual-actual gap is a sum of at most τ_C gradient steps, each bounded by c. The convergence proof splits into two cases—‖∇f(x_t)‖ ≤ c/2 where clipping introduces a controllable bias bounded via sub-Weibull tail probabilities, and ‖∇f(x_t)‖ > c/2 where clipping is inactive or partially active. For the first high-probability result in asynchronous optimization, the martingale difference term is bounded using Freedman's inequality, where clipping is essential to uniformly bound the norm of the inner product arguments. The resulting oracle complexity is Õ(σ²/ε⁴ + στ_C/ε³ + τ_C/ε²) in expectation and Õ(σ²log^{2θ}(1/δ)/ε⁴ + στ_C log^θ(1/δ)/ε³ + τ_C/ε²) with high probability, both independent of τ_max.

## Results

Theoretically, Clipped ASGD achieves an oracle complexity of Õ(σ²/ε⁴ + στ_C/ε³ + τ_C/ε²) in the homogeneous case and Õ((σ²+ζ²)/ε⁴ + (σ+ζ)τ_C/ε³ + τ_C/ε²) in the heterogeneous case, both independent of the maximum delay τ_max. Empirically, on CIFAR-10 (ResNet-18, 80% accuracy target) with D=4 delay factor, Clipped ASGD reduces minimum wall-clock time by 1.8× over Vanilla ASGD and 1.5× over Delay-adaptive and Ringmaster ASGD. On Shakespeare (LSTM, perplexity 5.0) with D=8, Clipped ASGD is 2× faster than Vanilla ASGD and 2.2× faster than Delay-adaptive ASGD. In the heterogeneous label-skew CIFAR-10 setting, Clipped ASGD is 1.2–1.3× faster than both Vanilla ASGD and Ringleader ASGD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to Vanilla ASGD (Koloskova et al., 2022), Clipped ASGD consistently wins across all settings: it removes the √(τ_max·τ_C)/ε² dependence on maximum delay in the oracle complexity, and empirically achieves 1.8–2× speedups in wall-clock time on both CIFAR-10 and Shakespeare as delay factor D increases from 4 to 8. Against Delay-adaptive ASGD (Cohen et al., 2021; Koloskova et al., 2022; Mishchenko et al., 2022), Clipped ASGD is 1.5–2.2× faster empirically and, unlike delay-adaptive methods, also converges in the heterogeneous setting where delay-adaptive schemes introduce bias toward faster workers. Against Ringleader/Ringmaster ASGD (Maranjyan et al., 2025; Maranjyan & Richtárik, 2026), Clipped ASGD is 1.2–1.8× faster in the homogeneous setting and 1.2× faster in the heterogeneous label-skew setting, while requiring no delay-threshold hyperparameter tuning. The key structural advantage is that Clipped ASGD achieves τ_max-independence without discarding stale gradients or adapting step sizes to delays, making it simpler to implement and tune.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the perturbed iterate analysis framework for asynchronous SGD (Koloskova et al., 2022; Mishchenko et al., 2022) by showing that the simple act of clipping gradients — a widely used but previously untheorized heuristic in the asynchronous setting — provably removes the dependence on maximum delay in oracle complexity. It departs from the delay-adaptive line of work (Picky SGD, adaptive step-size methods) by achieving the same robustness without discarding or reweighting stale gradients, which is critical in heterogeneous/federated settings where such strategies introduce bias toward faster workers. The noise model (sub-Weibull) is borrowed from recent serial SGD analyses (Li & Liu, 2022; Madden et al., 2024) and is the first time it is applied to an asynchronous algorithm, enabling the paper's high-probability convergence guarantee via Freedman's inequality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result provides a clean theoretical explanation for the long-observed empirical phenomenon that gradient clipping 'stabilizes' asynchronous training (Chen et al., 2016), and offers a bias-free, implementation-simple alternative to delay-adaptive methods for federated learning where stragglers are severe. The first high-probability convergence guarantee for any asynchronous optimizer is particularly relevant to real-world FL deployments where retraining is expensive and single-run reliability matters. Open questions include whether the benefits extend to weaker smoothness assumptions like (L₀,L₁)-smoothness, and whether norm-constrained optimizers (Muon, Scion) inherit similar straggler robustness in asynchronous settings.
