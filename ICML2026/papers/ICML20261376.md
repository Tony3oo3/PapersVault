## Problem

Large-scale visual generative models embed rich visual knowledge in their weights, yet visual signals remain external to the model as pixels, latents, or tokens that must be separately encoded and fed in. This separation creates redundancy and prevents the model from directly storing or reusing past visual information. The paper asks whether a visual signal can instead be compressed as a *function*—a generation process—attached to the frozen generative model itself, thereby exploiting the model's pretrained knowledge for compact representation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261376/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At encoding time, the method takes an 81-frame 480p video (center-cropped and resized to 832×480 to match Wan-2.1 constraints) and a detailed text caption produced by a VLM (GPT-5.1); it outputs a single compact vector obtained by hashing all LoRA adaptation parameters across layers via a fixed PRNG projection, then quantized to roughly 1–3 bits per element with a factorized entropy model. At decoding time, the compressed vector (plus negligible caption and entropy-model overhead) is expanded back into LoRA weights on the frozen foundation model, which then generates the reconstructed video. The same adaptation can also be reused with modified text prompts to produce edited or composited outputs without re-encoding.

## Method

The core mechanism is to overfit a LoRA adaptation on a frozen flow-matching diffusion foundation model (Wan-2.1-1.3B or Qwen-Image) using the standard flow-matching objective so that the adapted model's generation process reproduces the target signal; the adaptation parameters constitute the implicit visual representation. To compress the adaptation into a single vector, all per-layer LoRA matrices are projected into one shared vector through a fixed pseudo-random hash (the hashing trick / Uni-LoRA), and a factorized entropy model with learnable scale and training-aware uniform quantization provides the rate constraint. The training objective is justified from a minimum-description-length perspective: minimizing the flow-matching loss is equivalent to finding the path measure that deviates least (in KL) from the pretrained model's path measure while conditioning on the terminal state, which has a closed-form solution via Doob's h-transform. A key additional contribution is an inference-time scaling algorithm that branches the SDE trajectory with multiple particles at each denoising step and selects via importance sampling against the analytically known optimal denoising kernel, yielding substantial reconstruction gains at negligible bitrate overhead. The functional nature of the representation also enables a free distortion–perception trade-off by early-stopping the denoising process and applying Tweedie's formula for the final reconstruction step.

## Results

On the UVG dataset (81-frame 480p clips), VOV achieves strong perceptual video compression at extremely low bitrates, outperforming both neural and traditional codecs on DISTS and FVD. With inference-time scaling (100 steps, 2^18 samples per step), VOV yields substantial additional gains in reconstruction fidelity with only a marginal bitrate increase. Against the INR-based baseline NVRC, VOV shows clear improvements on perceptual metrics (DISTS, LPIPS) and competitive or superior PSNR at extremely low bitrates, indicating the diffusion backbone provides richer visual priors than standalone INR networks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VOV is compared against five baselines on the UVG dataset. Against the MSE-optimized neural codec (Jia et al., 2025) and the perceptually optimized neural codec (Qi et al., 2025), VOV achieves lower DISTS and FVD at comparable or lower bitrates, demonstrating superior perceptual quality at extremely low bitrates where pixel-wise fidelity (PSNR) is less informative. Against traditional codecs H.265/HM and H.266/VTM, VOV again leads on perceptual metrics (DISTS, FVD) in the ultra-low-bitrate regime, though it exhibits lower PSNR — a trade-off the authors attribute to the stochastic generative reconstruction process. Against the INR-based video codec NVRC (Kwan et al., 2024), VOV shows clear gains on DISTS and LPIPS and matches or surpasses PSNR at the lowest bitrates, confirming that the large-scale diffusion prior encodes richer visual semantics than a small standalone INR network. Inference-time scaling further widens the gap over all baselines, with the 100-step/2^18-sample configuration providing the best rate-distortion trade-off without incurring decoding-time cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA) from the personalized-generation literature (DreamBooth, Multi-Concept Customization, Customize-A-Video) into a compression mechanism, where the adaptation parameters themselves serve as the implicit visual representation. It departs from prior INR-based codecs (COIN, NeRV, NVRC) that train small standalone networks from scratch, instead leveraging the rich visual priors already embedded in large-scale diffusion/flow-matching foundation models (Wan-2.1, Qwen-Image). The one-vector hashing trick draws on Uni-LoRA (Li et al., 2025) and classical neural network compression, while the inference-time scaling algorithm connects to Sequential Monte Carlo and relative entropy coding (Diff-C, Theis et al., 2022). The MDL/Doob's h-transform justification for the overfitting objective provides a theoretical grounding absent from prior LoRA-for-personalization work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper proposes a genuinely unifying perspective: compression and generation are two views of the same functional representation, enabling a single encoded vector to support reconstruction, inference-time quality refinement, and downstream personalized editing. Key open questions include whether an amortized encoder/decoder can eliminate the prohibitively slow per-signal overfitting, whether the random hashing map can be replaced by a learned one to better capture parameter correlations, and how the framework scales to much larger backbones (the authors note RD performance may degrade). The hallucination problem at extreme low bitrates—where the diffusion prior fills in plausible but inaccurate details—remains a fundamental tension between perceptual quality and fidelity that the paper acknowledges but does not resolve.
