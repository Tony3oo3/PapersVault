## Problem

Existing 3D indoor scene generation methods face a fundamental scalability bottleneck: learning-based approaches are capped by training data complexity and struggle as object counts grow because every object is modeled as globally dependent on all others, while LLM/VLM-based methods lack precise spatial reasoning for dense layouts. The paper asks whether global scene distribution modeling is truly necessary, arguing that object placement is governed primarily by a small subset of proximal neighbors rather than the entire scene context.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263822/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt or scene type plus a 3D asset library of point clouds (sampled to 8,192 points per object) as input. At inference, the model is queried per relational tuple (dependent asset point cloud, support anchor bounding box, optional functional anchor bounding box) and outputs a Mixture-of-Logistics distribution over the dependent object's 12-dimensional oriented bounding box (center, dimensions, 6-DoF rotation). The final scene is assembled procedurally as a set of placed bounding boxes, with object counts unconstrained by training data density.

## Method

Pair2Scene decomposes scene generation into local relational rules captured by two relation types—support (physical grounding) and functional (semantic proximity)—and learns them via a Geometry-Aware Layout Predictor that fuses Point-MAE geometric features with anchor bounding-box embeddings through cascaded Transformer blocks combining Relational Self-Attention and per-object Geometry-Aware Cross-Attention, outputting a 4-component Mixture-of-Logistics distribution trained with NLL plus entropy regularization on the curated 3D-Pairs dataset (~138K relational tuples). At inference, scene hierarchies (a Support Tree traversed by BFS and per-node Functional Trees traversed by DFS) are serialized into an ordered sequence of relational tuples, which are placed sequentially via the learned local distributions. Global consistency is enforced through collision-aware rejection sampling that truncates the predicted distribution to physically feasible configurations, followed by a brief gravity simulation to refine contact surfaces, enabling the framework to generate scenes of arbitrary complexity beyond the training distribution.

## Results

On the 3D-Front only setting, Ours-Fit achieves the best FID (65.92) and KID (22.14×10⁻³), outperforming ATISS (71.24 / 42.18) and DiffuScene (67.45 / 31.72). The Ours-Beyond variant generates scenes with 14.15 objects on average (vs. ~7 for baselines) while maintaining competitive FID (75.88), demonstrating extrapolation beyond the training distribution. In the multi-source setting, Pair2Scene achieves the highest Contextual Fidelity Score (4.20) in the user study, nearly doubling the next-best method (LayoutVLM at 1.72).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Pair2Scene (Ours-Fit) outperforms all four learning-based baselines on both FID and KID in the 3D-Front only setting: it beats ATISS by 5.3 FID and 20.0 KID, DiffuScene by 1.5 FID and 9.6 KID, LayoutVLM by 55.0 FID and 116.4 KID, and FactoredScenes by 38.2 FID and 107.3 KID. In the multi-source user study, Pair2Scene dominates all five baselines on every qualitative metric, achieving a CFS of 4.20 versus LayoutVLM (1.72), Infinigen-Indoors (1.62), Holodeck (1.45), and FactoredScenes (0.68). The largest gaps appear in Physical Plausibility (4.32 vs. 2.32 for LayoutVLM) and Scene Complexity (4.73 vs. 3.18 for LayoutVLM), indicating that LLM- and procedural-based methods struggle with dense, physically grounded layouts. Inference time remains competitive: Pair2Scene generates multi-source scenes in 69.19 s, compared to 373.69 s for LayoutVLM and 210.23 s for Holodeck.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in 3D indoor scene generation—whether end-to-end learning-based (ATISS, DiffuScene, LayoutVLM) or LLM/VLM-guided (Holodeck, Infinigen-Indoors)—by arguing that global joint-distribution modeling is unnecessary and instead decomposes scene synthesis into local pairwise relational rules (support and functional). It repurposes the Mixture-of-Logistics density head from ATISS and WaveNet, the Point-MAE encoder from self-supervised point cloud learning, and Transformer-style attention blocks, but recombines them within a hierarchical procedural assembly pipeline with physics-based rejection sampling that no prior scene-generation work has integrated in this way. The 3D-Pairs dataset (~140K relational tuples) is curated from 3D-Front, MesaTask, and InternScenes, bridging the data-scarcity gap that limits purely learning-based approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The local-relational decomposition offers a principled escape from the data-scarcity and combinatorial-scaling bottlenecks that constrain current scene generators, and the demonstrated ability to produce scenes with roughly double the object count of the training distribution while maintaining physical and semantic plausibility is practically valuable for game, VR, and embodied-AI pipelines. However, the 'beyond-distribution' claim is somewhat self-defined (the authors control the expansion factor), and the multi-source baselines (Infinigen-Indoors, Holodeck) are not direct learning-based competitors, leaving the strongest comparison (vs. ATISS/DiffuScene) confined to the 3D-Front-only setting. Open questions include handling multi-anchor objects, hanging/suspended items, stylistic consistency across retrieved assets, and whether the local-relational paradigm extends to outdoor or multi-room scenes.
