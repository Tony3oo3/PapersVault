## Problem

Existing safety alignment methods (RLHF, DPO, Shallow Alignment, STAIR) protect only the generation prefix or initial tokens, leaving the multi-step reasoning trajectory itself unsecured. Indirect jailbreak attacks (DRA, DrAttack, ReNeLLM) exploit this gap by embedding harmful intent behind 20+ tokens of seemingly benign reasoning, coercing the model into autonomously generating unsafe content before any surface-level defense can trigger. The paper asks how to internalize step-wise safety monitoring directly into the generation process so that latent risks are intercepted at any intermediate reasoning step.

## Input / Output

The model receives a standard text query (user prompt), which may contain hidden malicious intent encoded through indirect jailbreak patterns such as letter-extraction puzzles or nested instructions. The output is a safe text response structured as a trajectory: an initial (potentially unsafe) prefix, followed by an embedded reflection segment marked by <|reflect|> and <|explore|> tokens that identify the risk and chart a safe path, and concluding with a safe continuation. Notably, the reflection mechanism is internalized—suppressing the explicit <|reflect|> token during decoding causes only a ~1.5% DSR drop, confirming the correction behavior is not dependent on surface-level template cues.

## Method

REFLECTOR formulates safety as a trajectory-level property (cumulative validity across all reasoning steps y_t) and trains via a two-stage paradigm. Stage 1 (SFT) uses a GPT-5 teacher to synthesize reflection trajectories: a randomly truncated unsafe prefix is paired with a structured reflection segment (z^reflect identifying the ethical breach, z^explore providing safe guidance) and a safe continuation, injecting the 'search-and-recovery' inductive bias absent from pretraining. Stage 2 (RL) employs GDPO (Group reward-Decoupled Normalization Policy Optimization) with a dual reward: r_safety is a binary HarmCLS consensus (HarmBench classifier AND GPT-OSS-120B generative judge) on the final output, while r_reflect (±λ, λ=0.3) rewards reflection that leads to safe refusal and penalizes reflection that still produces harmful content. GDPO normalizes each reward component separately within groups before aggregation, preserving fine-grained signal differences that standard GRPO would lose. The framework achieves >90% DSR across all indirect attack categories while simultaneously improving general utility (GSM8K +5.85%, AdvGLUE +9.96%), demonstrating that trajectory-level safety reflection and reasoning capability are mutually reinforcing.

## Results

REFLECTOR achieves Defense Success Rates (DSR) exceeding 90% across all four indirect jailbreak categories (DRA, PAP, ReNeLLM, DrAttack) on both LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct and Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct, with DRA improving from 10.04% to 92.31% (LLaMA) and 8.65% to 90.38% (Qwen). It reaches 100% DSR on XSTest and AutoDAN, and 96.04% on PAIR. On utility, REFLECTOR yields a 5.65% absolute gain on GSM8K (84.50% → 90.15%), sets new peaks on MMLU-Pro (45.20%) and AdvGLUE (68.29%), and improves SimpleQA from 2.52% to 6.45%, demonstrating that safety alignment need not sacrifice general capability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

REFLECTOR decisively outperforms all five alignment baselines on indirect jailbreaks: STAIR (the strongest baseline) reaches only 55.83% DSR on DRA versus REFLECTOR's 92.31%, a 36.5-point gap; Shallow-Align (48.90%), Self-Critique (15.00%), DPO (13.30%), and SFT (12.50%) are all far below. On direct attacks, REFLECTOR also leads, achieving 100% on AutoDAN and 96.04% on PAIR versus STAIR's 99.04% and 89.24% respectively. Against reasoning-oriented LLMs (Table 7), REFLECTOR surpasses LLaMA-o1 (StrongREJECT 0.8931 vs 0.5771), Skywork-o1 (0.8931 vs 0.6865), and DeepSeek-r1-Distilled (0.8931 vs 0.5551) on safety, while QwQ-32B-Preview is competitive on StrongREJECT (0.8800) but degrades sharply under PAIR (0.3195 vs REFLECTOR's 0.7665) and PAP (0.5978 vs 0.9328). On utility, REFLECTOR matches or exceeds STAIR on GSM8K (90.15% vs 87.60%), MMLU-Pro (45.20% vs 44.92%), and AdvGLUE (68.29% vs 67.75%), confirming no alignment tax.

## Relation to Prior Work

REFLECTOR extends the GRPO/GDPO reinforcement learning framework (Shao et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2026) from mathematical reasoning to safety alignment, repurposing group-relative policy optimization with a novel dual-reward structure (safety + reflection validity). Conceptually, it departs from the dominant prefix-level safety paradigm (RLHF, DPO, Shallow Alignment, STAIR) by reformulating safety as a trajectory-level property and internalizing a 'search-and-recovery' reflection mechanism directly into the generation policy via a two-stage SFT-then-RL pipeline. The teacher-guided data construction borrows from imitation learning (Ho & Ermon, 2016) and reflective RL (Ecoffet et al., 2019), but applies them specifically to the cold-start problem of bootstrapping reflection behavior in base LLMs that lack any inductive bias for mid-generation self-correction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and growing vulnerability—indirect jailbreaks that weaponize the model's own reasoning chain—where existing defenses (prefix refusals, shallow alignment, external classifiers) are structurally insufficient. The finding that safety reflection training *improves* general utility (GSM8K +5.85%, AdvGLUE +9.96%) rather than imposing the typical alignment tax is practically significant and challenges the assumed safety-capability tradeoff. Key limitations include reliance on proprietary teacher (GPT-5) and reward (GPT-OSS-120B) models, evaluation only on 7–8B backbones, and the open question of whether trajectory-level reflection generalizes to truly novel attack paradigms beyond the four indirect methods used in training. Scaling to larger models and reducing dependence on external reward models are natural next steps.
