## Problem

Existing large-scale pre-trained traffic models (e.g., TFM, UniST, UrbanGPT) either require substantial computational resources impractical for fine-grained deployment, overlook the specific requirements of lane-level prediction, or fail to explicitly model road-lane correlations. The scarcity of lane-level data relative to abundant road-level data further hinders the training of generalizable fine-grained models. MiniTraffic addresses this by proposing the first mini-sized (~119k parameters) pre-trained model specifically designed for multi-granularity fine-grained traffic prediction that can be trained on a single A100 GPU.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262634/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes historical speed time series (18-timestep windows) over road or lane segment graphs, where road-level pre-training uses multi-source domains (METR-LA, PeMS-Bay, PeMS-Road, PeMSF-Road, HuaNan-Road) and lane-level fine-tuning uses limited target data. Inputs are patchified (patch length 3) and randomly masked (40% rate) during pre-training. Outputs are future speed predictions at horizons of 3, 6, 12 (and up to 48) steps, with the model supporting both road-level and lane-level prediction from a single frozen backbone via granularity-specific lightweight heads (1.8k–16.3k fine-tunable parameters).

## Method

MiniTraffic's core mechanism is a three-stage pipeline: (1) Frequency Domain Stability Augmentation (FDA) applies DFT to road-level signals, injects bounded Gaussian perturbations on magnitude and phase constrained by a learnable spectral mask Γ(f) and amplitude threshold λ·max A(f), then recovers time-domain signals via inverse DFT—simulating multi-lane variability while preserving spectral consistency; (2) a contrastive clustering backbone uses InfoNCE loss to learn patch-level similarity, constructs k-NN sparse adjacency matrices, and applies localized graph attention (reducing complexity from O(N²) to O(kN)); (3) granularity-aware fine-tuning freezes the backbone and swaps in task-specific modules—Extension+Pooling for road-level, retained FDA with lightweight Adaptive/Reduction Heads for lane-level. Pre-training optimizes a combined reconstruction + contrastive objective, and the entire model requires only ~119k trainable parameters, enabling few-shot adaptation to new lane-level domains with as little as 10% labeled data.

## Results

On the six fine-grained traffic prediction datasets, MiniTraffic (119k trainable parameters) achieves the best MAE across all tasks and horizons. On lane-level prediction, it reduces MAE by 7–24% over the strongest multi-task baseline McgVAE on PeMS-Lane (e.g., 3.94 vs. 4.89 at h=6) and by 24–39% on HuaNan-Lane (4.11 vs. 5.72 at h=6). On road-level prediction, it outperforms the best pre-trained plugin GPT-ST by ~10–15% MAE (e.g., 3.12 vs. 3.72 on PeMS-Road at h=6; 3.16 vs. 5.28 on HuaNan-Road at h=6). The model also demonstrates strong few-shot transfer, achieving reasonable accuracy with only 10% of lane-level fine-tuning data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MiniTraffic is compared against 29 baselines spanning 14 lane-level models, 12 road-level models, and 3 pre-trained/multi-task models (McgVAE, FlashST, GPT-ST). On lane-level tasks, MiniTraffic decisively outperforms all 14 lane-specific baselines and the multi-task McgVAE: on PeMS-Lane (h=6) it achieves MAE 3.94 vs. ST-ABC's 4.94 and McgVAE's 4.89; on HuaNan-Lane (h=6) it reaches 4.11 vs. McgVAE's 5.72 (a 28% reduction). On road-level tasks, MiniTraffic surpasses the strongest pre-trained plugins FlashST (3.76) and GPT-ST (3.72) on PeMS-Road (h=6) with MAE 3.12, and dramatically outperforms McgVAE (5.28) on HuaNan-Road (h=6) with 3.16. On the irregular-lane PeMSF datasets (Table 3), MiniTraffic again leads: 4.15 MAE on PeMSF-Lane (h=6) vs. McgVAE's 4.40, and 3.17 on PeMSF-Road (h=6) vs. GPT-ST's 3.56. MiniTraffic wins on every dataset, every horizon (3/6/12), and every metric (MAE/RMSE/MAPE) tested, with the largest margins on HuaNan-Lane and HuaNan-Road where data scarcity and irregular structure are most pronounced.

## Relation to Prior Work

MiniTraffic adapts frequency-domain augmentation from generic time-series methods (FrAug, SpecMix) by imposing road-lane-specific spectral constraints (learnable frequency mask, amplitude threshold) to simulate multi-lane variability from abundant road data. It borrows the RevIN normalization/denormalization mechanism to handle distribution shift and uses kNN-based similarity (Liu et al., 2024) to construct sparse patch-level graphs via contrastive clustering, departing from the global GNN architectures (DCRNN, MTGNN, STGCN) that dominate traffic prediction. The pre-training-on-road / fine-tuning-on-lane transfer paradigm positions it between task-specific models and large-scale pre-trained plugins (FlashST, GPT-ST), but at a fraction of the parameter count (~100k vs. millions).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a 100k-parameter model trainable on a single A100 GPU can outperform both task-specific GNNs and much larger pre-trained plugins across six fine-grained traffic datasets, offering a practical path for resource-constrained deployment in lane-level and road-level prediction. Open questions include whether the framework generalizes beyond speed prediction (e.g., flow, occupancy), how it behaves under truly cross-city distribution shifts (current results show consistent degradation), and whether the contrastive-clustering graph construction scales to city-wide networks with tens of thousands of nodes. The finding that naive lane-to-road averaging (Table 9) consistently underperforms dedicated road-level modeling also suggests that compositional relationships between granularities are non-trivial and warrant further study.
