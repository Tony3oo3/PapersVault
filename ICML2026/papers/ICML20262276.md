## Problem

Imitation learning in Mean Field Games has been studied only for deterministic mean fields, where the population distribution follows a fixed trajectory. When common noise is present, the mean field becomes a stochastic process, making vanilla state-only policies suboptimal and requiring agents to condition on the fluctuating population distribution. No prior work provides theoretical guarantees or algorithms for learning population-aware policies from expert data in this stochastic setting.

## Input / Output

The learner observes state-action trajectories of expert agents playing a Nash equilibrium under shared common noise realizations, along with the empirical population distribution at each time step. The output is a population-aware policy mapping (individual state, mean-field distribution) → action distribution, contrasted with a population-unaware baseline mapping state → action distribution. The method also computes two imitation divergences (BC and ADV) and the exploitability of the learned policy, all evaluated via Monte Carlo over common noise trajectories.

## Method

The paper establishes finite-sample bounds showing that minimizing either the Behavioral Cloning (BC) or Adversarial (ADV) divergence controls both the policy's exploitability and its performance gap relative to the expert, with ADV yielding polynomial rather than exponential error propagation in the horizon. For computing the expert Nash equilibrium, the authors generalize Fictitious Play to the common noise setting by training best responses against an aggregated mean-field sequence, then distill the resulting policy sequence into a single deployable policy via a mean-field imitation loss (Algorithm 2). For the learner, an interactive imitation algorithm (Algorithm 3) places a single agent in an environment populated by expert agents, training a neural network (or Nadaraya-Watson kernel regression for small spaces) to match expert actions conditioned on the empirical population distribution, thereby internalizing the relationship between common-noise-induced mean-field shifts and optimal actions.

## Results

Across all three environments (2-state/2-action, Beach Bar, Night Clubs) and all tested parameter regimes (varying α and η), the population-aware adaptive policy consistently achieves lower BC and ADV imitation divergences, higher reward relative to the expert, and lower exploitability than the population-unaware vanilla policy. The problem exhibits significant parameter sensitivity: performance gaps widen as the common noise intensity (η) and social coupling (α) increase. In the Night Clubs environment, the adaptive policy's advantage on reward matching (Q2) is less pronounced than in the other two settings, though it still dominates on equilibrium quality (exploitability). No single scalar headline number is reported; results are presented as full parameter-grid sweeps (5×5 grids) with 5 independent runs per configuration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external imitation learning or RL methods; instead, the central comparison is between two policy classes learned from the same expert data: a population-unaware vanilla policy (mapping state → action) and a population-aware adaptive policy (mapping state + mean field → action). The adaptive policy wins on every reported metric—BC divergence, ADV divergence, reward vs. expert, and exploitability—across all three environments and all parameter settings. The vanilla policy effectively captures only the time-averaged expert action and fails to react to mean-field fluctuations, resulting in systematically higher imitation error and higher exploitability. The gap is most dramatic in the 2-state environment (where the expert's policy is highly sensitive to the mean-field variable ρ) and in the Beach Bar environment. In the Night Clubs environment, the reward-matching advantage narrows (the vanilla policy's average action happens to be closer to optimal in that gathering-incentive setting), but the adaptive policy still achieves substantially lower exploitability, confirming that population awareness is critical for equilibrium recovery even when raw reward differences are modest.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Ramponi et al. (2023) on imitation learning in deterministic MFGs to the common noise setting, where the mean field becomes a stochastic process. The theoretical bounds (Theorems 3.1–3.2) reduce exactly to Ramponi et al.'s results when the policy Lipschitz constant L_E is set to zero, confirming a clean generalization. For computing the expert Nash equilibrium, the authors adapt the Fictitious Play algorithm from Perrin et al. (2022) to handle stochastic mean fields, and use the dynamic programming best-response approach of Gu et al. (2024) for the simplest environment. The broader MFG-with-common-noise theory (Carmona et al., 2016) provides the foundational framework, but no prior work addresses learning from expert data in this setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic treatment of imitation learning in MFGs with common noise, filling a clear gap between the rich theory of stochastic MFGs and the emerging IL literature for deterministic MFGs. The key practical insight—that population-unaware policies systematically fail to capture equilibrium dynamics under stochastic mean fields—has direct implications for domains with systemic shocks (financial markets, energy grids, crowd dynamics). Limitations include the restriction to finite state spaces, relatively small environments (≤20 states), and theoretical bounds that grow exponentially in horizon for the BC proxy. Open questions include extending to state-action mean field interactions, continuous state spaces, and applying the framework to real-world data where the common noise structure is unknown.
