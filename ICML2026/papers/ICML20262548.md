## Problem

In Safe RL, real-world safety constraints are often implicit, trajectory-based, and difficult to specify explicitly. While preference-based constraint inference offers a data-efficient alternative, the paper identifies that standard Bradley-Terry models infer symmetric cost distributions that fail to capture the heavy-tailed nature of safety costs (caused by cascading failures), leading to systematic risk underestimation and dangerously aggressive policies. Additionally, prior work largely ignores how the learned cost model's output characteristics affect downstream policy optimization quality.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pairwise trajectory-level preferences (which of two trajectories is safer) and binary safety labels (safe/unsafe) collected from a human annotator or synthetic oracle, along with state observations from the environment. It produces a learned cost function ĉ(s,a) parameterized by a 3-layer MLP (64 hidden units, ReLU) whose expectation over trajectories defines the safety constraint (threshold set to 0), and a safe policy π_θ trained via PPO-Lag. The preference data is coarse-grained at the trajectory level rather than requiring dense state-level annotations, and the total query budget is split between offline pre-training (18,000 queries) and online finetuning (2,000 queries).

## Method

PbCRL extends the Bradley-Terry model with a composite loss L_PbCI = L_pair + L_safe^DZ + L_SNR. The key innovation is a dead zone mechanism in the safety loss: instead of merely requiring unsafe trajectories to have estimated cost Ĉ > 0, it enforces Ĉ > δ, pushing the right tail of the cost distribution further out. The paper proves (via Lemma 3.1 → Theorem 3.2 → Corollary 3.3) that this strictly increases the gradient magnitude for unsafe trajectories, induces multi-step cost dominance, and yields a strictly heavier right tail in the learned distribution, mitigating the BT model's symmetric-distribution bias. A Signal-to-Noise Ratio loss (−ζ·Var(Ĉ)/H(p(μ))) regularizes the cost model to maintain sufficient output variance, which the paper shows (via Cauchy-Schwarz on the policy gradient) raises the upper bound on the policy gradient norm for more effective exploration. Training follows a two-stage strategy: offline pre-training on a fixed δ, then online finetuning where δ is adaptively calibrated by matching the model's predicted violation rate to the empirical violation rate from a small batch of online labels, reducing annotation burden while tracking distribution shift as the policy evolves.

## Results

On Safety Gymnasium navigation tasks, PbCRL achieves returns within ~1% of the PPO-Lag oracle (e.g., Goal: 23.41 vs 23.50; Button: 8.73 vs 8.96) while keeping true cost below the safety threshold (Goal: 18.50 vs threshold 20; Button: 37.51 vs threshold 40). In contrast, PPO-BT matches or exceeds oracle returns but violates the safety constraint (Goal cost: 26.97 > 20; Button cost: 50.78 > 40), and RLSF is overly conservative (Goal return: 5.78 vs 23.41). On language model alignment (Llama-3.2-1B), PbCRL achieves 80.7% helpfulness and 82.1% harmlessness win rates, outperforming Safe RLHF (79.4%/76.5%) and PPO (72.5%/60.7%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PbCRL is compared against three baselines: PPO-Lag (oracle with ground-truth costs), PPO-BT (standard BT-based cost learning adapted from Safe RLHF), and RLSF (BT-based binary cost from segment-level feedback). Against PPO-BT, PbCRL wins decisively on safety: PPO-BT consistently exceeds the true safety threshold (e.g., Goal cost 26.97 vs threshold 20; Button cost 50.78 vs threshold 40), while PbCRL stays below it with comparable returns. Against RLSF, PbCRL achieves dramatically higher returns (Goal: 23.41 vs 5.78; Push: 3.20 vs 1.07; Button: 8.73 vs -2.01) because RLSF's segment-level cost learning overestimates risk, producing overly conservative policies. Against the PPO-Lag oracle, PbCRL matches within ~1% on return while maintaining costs slightly below the threshold, confirming the learned constraint is a reliable surrogate. On language model alignment, PbCRL outperforms Safe RLHF by 1.3% on helpfulness and 5.6% on harmlessness win rates, and outperforms PPO by 8.2% and 21.4% respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Bradley-Terry preference model (Bradley & Terry, 1952) as used in recent preference-based constraint inference methods (RLSF, Reddy Chirra et al. 2024; Safe RLHF, Dai et al. 2024) by identifying a fundamental distributional mismatch: standard BT models produce symmetric cost distributions while true safety costs are heavy-tailed due to cascading failure effects. The dead zone mechanism is a targeted modification to the BT safety loss that provably recovers heavy-tailed structure, while the SNR loss draws on recent findings about reward model quality (Razin et al., 2025) to ensure informative cost signals for policy gradients. The two-stage offline-to-online training strategy adapts the paradigm from RLHF reward model training (Ouyang et al., 2022) to the constraint learning setting, reducing online labeling burden.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that high classification accuracy (85-87%) in BT-based cost models does not guarantee correct constraint satisfaction—due to systematic underestimation of expected costs from symmetric distributions—has direct implications for safety-critical deployments where Type II Errors (false safety) are dangerous. The dead zone fix is simple and theoretically grounded, but the framework assumes noise-free human feedback and is validated primarily on simulated benchmarks with oracle-generated labels, leaving open questions about robustness to noisy or inconsistent human judgments in real-world settings. The two-stage training with adaptive dead zone calibration offers a practical path toward reducing annotation costs, though its transferability to truly open-ended safety criteria remains to be demonstrated.
