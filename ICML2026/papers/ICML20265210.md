## Problem

Closed-loop neuromodulation requires controlling high-dimensional spatiotemporal fields on irregular 3D neuronal morphologies under hard safety constraints, but existing approaches fail to simultaneously handle: unknown biophysics on irregular shapes (not known PDEs on regular grids), sparse sensing that hides most of the field, hard full-field safety constraints that can be violated at unsensed nodes, and millisecond-scale latency requirements that rule out high-fidelity simulators inside every planning step. No prior framework addresses all four requirements together in a single auditable control loop.

## Input / Output

At each control step the method consumes a finite history window of sparse measurements (e.g., LFP, beta power, mean Ca²⁺, mean K⁺, mean voltage) and previously executed actions, an episode-specific heterogeneous morphology graph with typed edges (cable adjacency, spatial neighborhoods, membrane-ECS coupling) and per-node 3D geometry descriptors, and a preference weight vector over tracking/energy/burden objectives. It outputs a single low-dimensional stimulation action (C_w ≪ N nodes) that has been projected to satisfy both actuator bounds and a learned field-level safety barrier. The morphology graph varies across episodes in node count (~600 to ~20K), discretization, and sensor placement, so the controller must generalize across instances without retraining.

## Method

NEURONCTRL decomposes the feedback loop into four trained modules: (1) a GRU-based history-conditioned observer that reconstructs the full N-node latent field from sparse measurements and past actions; (2) a heterogeneous message-passing neural operator that predicts one-step field dynamics on typed morphology graphs, injecting control through a morphology-aware influence kernel that preserves stimulation locality across instances; (3) a preference-conditioned flow-matching policy that generates a raw action by integrating a learned velocity field from noise to the action space, with FMBF projection at each integration step to enforce actuator bounds; and (4) a learned field-level safety filter that computes a discrete-time CBF condition under the frozen surrogate dynamics and applies a closed-form minimal-intervention correction (a single half-space projection along the barrier gradient) only when the predicted next field would violate a constraint. Modules are trained in stages (simulator → observer → barrier → flow policy) with supervised losses, and the safety guarantee is model-relative: it enforces the barrier under the learned surrogate and learned barrier functional, with trust-region margin tightening to account for estimation and model mismatch.

## Results

On the three 3D neuromodulation benchmarks, NEURONCTRL achieves the best cost, safety, and competitive latency simultaneously. On DBS3D it reaches cost 0.095 and safety 0.910 at 3.9 ms per step; on ECS3D, cost 0.100 and safety 0.895 at 5.6 ms; on KDyn3D, cost 0.009 and safety 0.880 at 7.9 ms. The closest graph-based baselines (GINO, MeshGraphNet, GraphCast) trail by 12–17% in cost and 3–5 points in safety, while non-graph methods (Geo-FNO, DeepONet, TranSolver) lag by 30–50% in cost. Ablating the field-level safety filter increases unsafe rates from 0.8–1.4% to 6.0–9.5% with negligible cost change, confirming the filter's role as a targeted correction rather than a training regularizer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NEURONCTRL outperforms all 17 baselines on both cost and safety across all three benchmarks. Against the strongest graph-based surrogates (GINO, MeshGraphNet, GraphCast, HAMLET, RiGNO, GOAT), it reduces cost by 12–17% (e.g., 0.095 vs. 0.112 on DBS3D; 0.100 vs. 0.118 on ECS3D; 0.009 vs. 0.011 on KDyn3D) and improves safety by 3–5 points (0.910 vs. 0.875–0.878 on DBS3D), while remaining within ~1 ms of their latency. Against non-graph neural operators (Geo-FNO, Geom-DeepONet, TranSolver), the gap widens to 30–50% in cost and 8–12 points in safety, confirming that typed morphology edges capture physical couplings that coordinate-only methods miss. Generative planners (SafeDiffCon, RDM Planner, CFM-Flow, Latent-FM) achieve moderate safety (0.81–0.87) but at 1.5–3× the latency of NEURONCTRL, and Latent-FM in particular is the slowest at 9.8–13.4 ms. Classical MPC and offline RL (BC, BPPO) are the fastest (2.1–3.4 ms) but trail by 40–60% in cost and 10–20 points in safety, indicating that observation-only feedback without full-field reconstruction is fundamentally limited. Under partial observability and OOD shifts (Table 4), NEURONCTRL degrades least (ECS3D OOD safety 0.674 vs. 0.581–0.654 for baselines), and under zero-shot ×4 super-resolution it retains the best cost (0.150) and safety (0.865) among all methods tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

NEURONCTRL synthesizes three largely separate research threads—neural operators for PDE surrogate dynamics (Geo-FNO, GINO, MeshGraphNet, GraphCast), flow matching for preference-conditioned action generation (Lipman et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2023), and control barrier functions for hard safety enforcement (Ames et al., 2019)—into a single modular closed-loop controller tailored to irregular neuronal morphologies. It departs from prior safe-control work by enforcing safety at two complementary levels (action-space FMBF projection during flow integration and a learned field-level barrier filter with minimal-intervention correction) rather than relying on a single penalty or shield, and from prior neural-operator work by coupling the surrogate to an explicit observer and preference-conditioned generator rather than using it solely for planning. The morphology-aware heterogeneous graph with typed edges (cable, spatial, coupling) extends standard graph neural operators by encoding biophysical coupling structure directly into the message-passing topology.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The three high-fidelity 3D benchmarks (DBS3D, ECS3D, KDyn3D) and the modular observer–surrogate–policy–filter decomposition provide a reusable evaluation substrate and architectural template for safe closed-loop control of high-dimensional fields on irregular domains, a setting where classical PDE control theory does not directly apply. The honest reporting of a plant-level safety gap on DBS3D (safety score 0.742 vs. 0.880 under the learned surrogate) underscores that model-relative guarantees are not yet sufficient for deployment, leaving calibrated uncertainty margins and real-morphology validation as critical open problems. Broader implications include whether the typed-graph + flow-matching + CBF pattern generalizes to other biophysical or engineering systems with irregular geometry and hard state constraints.
