## Problem

In low-SNR infrared sequences, small targets are indistinguishable from cluttered backgrounds in individual frames, causing both task-specific multiframe detectors (lacking large-scale pretraining) and SAM-based extensions (relying on unreliable explicit prompts) to fail. The key unexploited observation is that targets gradually emerge from background dynamics over time, but no existing method explicitly models this temporal-emergence to guide a segmentation foundation model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265182/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a temporal clip of 7 infrared frames (window length 2T+1 with T=3) centered at the target frame, resized to 1024×1024, and produces a binary segmentation mask of the same spatial resolution for the target frame. Only a single frame is fed into the frozen SAM encoder; temporal information is injected as learned feature modulations and prompt tokens, eliminating the need for any user-provided points or boxes. Boundary frames are replicated for leading/trailing frames in the sequence.

## Method

TEP-SAM comprises three modules: (1) a Discrepancy-Enhanced Temporal Encoder (DETE) that extracts temporally emerged features via a Global-Local Temporal Modeling branch (ConvGRU for global background dynamics + patch-wise temporal attention for local motion deviations) and a Temporal Discrepancy Modulation branch (cosine-similarity-based dissimilarity map to enhance target-frame regions differing from adjacent frames); (2) a Temporal Feature Injection (TFI) module that adds layer-specific MLP-projected temporal features into each frozen SAM encoder transformer block, preserving pretrained semantics while enriching spatial representations; and (3) a Temporal Prompt Generator (TP-Gen) that uses 4 learnable query tokens with self-attention and cross-attention over the temporal features to produce prompt tokens concatenated with SAM output tokens for the decoder. Training is supervised with BCE + Dice loss on the main mask plus an auxiliary loss on a DETE-side prediction head, with the SAM encoder kept frozen throughout.

## Results

On NUDT-MIRSDT (All), TEP-SAM (SAM-B) achieves 86.15% IoU, 86.28% nIoU, 99.71% Pd, and 0.51×10⁻⁶ Fa, surpassing the best prior multi-frame method (DNANet+DTUM, 83.35% IoU) by 2.8 IoU. On TSIRMT (All), it reaches 74.34% IoU and 73.21% nIoU, outperforming LMAFormer (65.89% IoU) by 8.45 IoU. On the harder subsets (SNR ≤ 3 for NUDT, TSNR ≤ 10 for TSIRMT), TEP-SAM gains 4.36 and 10.07 IoU over the respective best baselines, demonstrating superior robustness under severely low-SNR conditions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TEP-SAM (SAM-B) outperforms all 18 baselines on both datasets across all four metrics. On NUDT-MIRSDT, it beats the strongest non-SAM multi-frame method DNANet+DTUM (83.35% IoU, 3.61 Fa) by 2.8 IoU and 3.1 Fa, and also surpasses DeepPro-Plus (83.17% IoU) by 2.98 IoU. On TSIRMT, the margin is larger: TEP-SAM exceeds LMAFormer (65.89% IoU) by 8.45 IoU and SAM-I2V-box (63.49% IoU) by 10.85 IoU, while also reducing Fa from 878 to 108.97. Among SAM-based methods, TEP-SAM dominates: it improves over SAM-SPL+DTUM (76.28% IoU on NUDT) by 9.87 IoU and over SAM2-Box (54.99% IoU) by 31.16 IoU. On the Hard subsets, TEP-SAM (SAM-B) achieves 71.19% IoU on NUDT-Hard (vs. 63.33% for DNANet+DTUM) and 65.22% IoU on TSIRMT-Hard (vs. 55.56% for LMAFormer), confirming its advantage is most pronounced under the lowest SNR conditions. The only metric where a TEP-SAM variant is not the absolute best is TSIRMT Pd, where TEP-SAM (SAM2-L) achieves 93.18% vs. 92.04% for SAM-B, but the SAM-B variant still leads on all other TSIRMT metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

TEP-SAM builds on the SAM/SAM2 segmentation foundation model as a frozen backbone and departs from two prior lines of work: (1) task-specific M-IRSTD architectures (3D CNNs, ConvLSTM, direction-coded convolutions) that lack large-scale semantic pretraining, and (2) generic SAM extensions to video (SAM2, XMem, SAM-I2V, TSP-SAM) that rely on explicit user or detector prompts and generic temporal memory. The key conceptual departure is the 'temporal-emerged' framing: rather than aggregating frames or propagating masks, the paper explicitly models the motion discrepancy between a target and background dynamics (via a ConvGRU global branch, patch-level temporal attention, and a cosine-similarity dissimilarity map) to generate cues that both modulate SAM's spatial features and produce automatic prompts, eliminating the need for interactive prompting in a domain where reliable single-frame prompts are unavailable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete gap: SAM-based methods fail in low-SNR infrared sequences because targets are indistinguishable in individual frames, and the experiments show that even combining SAM-SPL with the task-specific DTUM temporal module still underperforms TEP-SAM by a wide margin (e.g., 61.17 vs. 74.34 IoU on TSIRMT), indicating that generic temporal aggregation is insufficient. Open questions include applicability to stationary or very slowly moving targets (where motion discrepancy vanishes), robustness under strong ego-motion or parallax, and whether the temporal-emerged prompting paradigm generalizes beyond infrared to other low-contrast domains. The computational overhead of running a full SAM encoder per frame (12–30 FPS depending on variant) also limits real-time deployment.
