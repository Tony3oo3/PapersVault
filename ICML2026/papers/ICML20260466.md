## Problem

The Fisher Information Matrix provides a principled second-order measure of parameter sensitivity in neural networks, but its full computation is infeasible at scale, forcing most compression and pruning methods to rely on diagonal approximations that discard cross-parameter correlations. This paper addresses the gap by deriving a GPU-tractable Kronecker-factored approximation of the FIM that captures both row-wise and column-wise dependencies, and proving that under a Matrix-Variate Normal assumption this yields a unique closed-form optimal low-rank layer decomposition.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained model's weight matrices and a calibration dataset (e.g., 9,600 FineWeb samples processed in mini-batches of 128) from which per-sample weight gradients are collected. It produces, for each linear layer, two low-rank factor matrices W1 ∈ R^{n×r} and W2 ∈ R^{r×m} that replace the original dense weight W ∈ R^{n×m}, with the rank r chosen per layer via an importance score. Crucially, the full FIM (of size O(m²n²)) is never materialized; all computation is reduced to matrix-vector products of layer-sized objects, making the procedure tractable even for 70M-parameter layers in 13B-parameter models.

## Method

The core algorithm, Matrix-free Fisher Factorization (MFF), estimates the Kronecker factors A and B of the empirical FIM by solving a rank-1 approximation of the permuted Fisher matrix via the Lanczos method, where each matvec is computed as a sum of compact products G_i^T Z G_i rather than forming the full O(m²n²) matrix. Under the MVN assumption that layer weights follow MN(W*, B^{-1}, A^{-1}), Theorem 3.1 proves the optimal rank-r compression is obtained by taking the truncated SVD of the auxiliary matrix L_B^T W* L_A (where L_A, L_B are Cholesky factors of A and B) and recovering the compressed weights as L_B^{-T} Ŵ_r L_A^{-1}. This yields GFWSVD, which applies full non-diagonal transformations on both sides of the weight matrix, generalizing FWSVD (diagonal left factor only) and standard SVD (identity on both sides). The method is purely analytical with no stochastic steps, and can serve as a drop-in initialization for subsequent fine-tuning pipelines such as Dobi-SVD.

## Results

On Llama 3.1 8B at 20% compression, GFWSVD achieves 22.57 WikiText-2 perplexity and 0.53 average reasoning accuracy, compared to 354/0.35 for FWSVD, 145/0.35 for ASVD, and 18.54/0.52 for Basis Sharing. At 50% compression, GFWSVD maintains 176 WT2 perplexity versus 18,992 for FWSVD and 4,039 for ASVD. On BERT (GLUE macro-average), GFWSVD reaches 0.66 at 33% compression versus 0.47 (SVD), 0.46 (ASVD), and 0.56 (FWSVD). When used to initialize Dobi-SVD fine-tuning on Llama 2 7B at 40% compression, GFWSVD reduces WT2 perplexity from 10.56 to 10.29 and improves ARC-E accuracy from 0.58 to 0.60.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GFWSVD substantially outperforms the diagonal Fisher baseline FWSVD at every compression level: on Llama 3.1 8B at 20% compression, GFWSVD achieves 22.57 WT2 perplexity versus 354 for FWSVD (a 15× gap), and at 50% the gap widens to 176 vs. 18,992. Against the activation-based ASVD, GFWSVD wins on perplexity across all compression ratios (e.g., 22.57 vs. 145 at 20% on Llama 3.1) and on most reasoning tasks, though ASVD occasionally edges out GFWSVD on individual tasks like SST2 under aggressive BERT compression. Compared to SVD-LLM on Llama 2 7B, GFWSVD achieves lower perplexity at 20% (11.1 vs. 12.10 WT2) and higher average accuracy (0.50 vs. 0.48). Against the cross-layer Basis Sharing method, GFWSVD outperforms on Llama 3.1 8B at all compression levels (e.g., 0.53 vs. 0.52 avg accuracy at 20%) and on Llama 2 7B at 20% and 40%, though the two methods are competitive at 50%. When used as initialization for the Dobi-SVD fine-tuning pipeline, GFWSVD consistently improves over standard SVD initialization, reducing WT2 perplexity from 10.56 to 10.29 and ARC-E accuracy from 0.58 to 0.60 at 40% compression.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the Kronecker-factored Fisher structure from K-FAC (Martens & Grosse, 2015) and Koroko et al. (2022), which were designed for training-time optimization with dynamic moving averages, to the fundamentally different setting of post-training decomposition on a fixed calibration set. It proves that FWSVD (Hsu et al., 2022), the standard diagonal Fisher-weighted SVD for compression, is a special case of its generalized Kronecker framework, and introduces a matrix-free Lanczos algorithm (MFF) that makes the full Kronecker factorization tractable on GPU for layers with billions of Hessian entries. The theoretical bridge through the Matrix-Variate Normal distribution connects the Fisher geometry to a closed-form optimal low-rank solution, going beyond the heuristic weighting used in activation-based methods like ASVD and SVD-LLM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretically grounded (not merely heuristic) justification for using full Kronecker-structured Fisher information in post-training compression, and the matrix-free algorithm makes this practical for 7B–13B parameter models. Key limitations include the MVN assumption on weight distributions (unlikely to hold exactly in deep networks), the rank-1 Kronecker approximation that leaves unexplained curvature in some layers, and the absence of cross-layer coordination during compression. Open questions include whether multi-term Kronecker extensions or joint cross-layer compression strategies can close the remaining gap to cross-layer methods like Basis Sharing on certain task categories, and whether the approach transfers to quantization or pruning settings beyond low-rank factorization.
