## Problem

Existing MLLM unlearning benchmarks are limited in scale and diversity (e.g., only 20 concepts or a single entity type), making them unsuitable for evaluating the cumulative effects of sequential unlearning requests. More fundamentally, no prior work has identified that MLLM lifelong unlearning faces a unique constraint absent in unimodal settings: unlearning operations on one modality can catastrophically disrupt the multimodal alignment between vision and language components, causing the entire model to degrade.

## Input / Output

At inference, the system receives an RGB image paired with a text question (e.g., 'Who directed this film?') and produces a text response that is either a high-quality refusal (for forgotten entities) or a factual answer (for retained entities). A gate module (GLM-4V-Plus) first extracts the entity name from the multimodal input, then routes the query to the appropriate LoRA adapter or the untouched base model. The evaluation uses GPT-4o as a judge assigning scores of {0, 1, 2} for both forget quality (rejection score) and model utility (correctness score), with four semantically equivalent prompt variants tested per question to assess robustness.

## Method

LUMoE treats each unlearning task as a specialized expert in a Mixture-of-Experts framework, where the 'experts' are LoRA adapters trained individually via Preference Optimization (PO), a DPO variant that teaches the model to prefer refusal responses over factual answers for target entities. A gate module (GLM-4V-Plus) performs two-step routing: it extracts the entity from the multimodal input, then matches it against previously unlearned task sets to select the corresponding adapter. If no match is found, the input bypasses all adapters and is processed by the original MLLM, preserving utility. The key design insight is that by isolating task-specific modifications into switchable adapters rather than repeatedly altering the base model's weights, LUMoE protects the multimodal alignment from cumulative damage. An error-handling mechanism instructs the router to output 'None' when uncertain, defaulting such queries to the base model to avoid false unlearning.

## Results

On MLUBench with LLaVA-7B, LUMoE maintains Forget Quality at 0.95–1.00 and Model Utility at 0.88–0.95 across all four sequential unlearning tasks, with no cumulative degradation. In stark contrast, all four baselines suffer catastrophic collapse: GA's Forget Quality on Task A drops from 0.38 (immediately after unlearning) to 0.01 (after all tasks), and NPO's Model Utility on Task A falls from 0.24 to 0.00. On LLaVA-13B, LUMoE similarly sustains Forget Quality ≥0.95 and Model Utility ≥0.90, while baselines degrade to near-zero on previously unlearned tasks. On the MLLMU-Bench (153 celebrity profiles, 3 tasks), LUMoE achieves Forget Quality of 0.98–1.00 and Model Utility of 0.86–0.95, versus GA's Forget Quality dropping from 0.27 to 0.12 and GD's from 0.30 to 0.07.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LUMoE decisively outperforms all four baselines on both Forget Quality and Model Utility across every task and every point in the sequential unlearning sequence. GA (Gradient Ascent, Yao et al. 2023) initially achieves the highest Forget Quality among baselines on Task A (0.38) but degrades to 0.01 after subsequent unlearning, while its Model Utility collapses from 0.12 to 0.01. GD (Gradient Difference, Liu et al. 2022) shows a similar trajectory, dropping from 0.33 to 0.00 Forget Quality on Task A. KL Minimization (Yao et al. 2024) is the weakest baseline, with Forget Quality falling from 0.28 to 0.00 and Model Utility to 0.00 on all previously unlearned tasks. NPO (Negative Preference Optimization, Zhang et al. 2024b) is the most catastrophic: after unlearning Task A, its Forget Quality on Task A is 0.42, but after Task B it drops to 0.005, and Model Utility on all tasks becomes exactly 0.000 — the model essentially refuses to answer everything. LUMoE, by contrast, never drops below 0.95 Forget Quality or 0.88 Model Utility at any point in the sequence, demonstrating that isolating unlearning modifications via switchable LoRA adapters with a gate router effectively prevents the cumulative degradation that plagues weight-modification baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sequential/lifelong unlearning literature from LLMs (Gao et al., 2024; Shi et al., 2024b) to the multimodal setting, arguing that MLLM lifelong unlearning is qualitatively distinct due to the multimodal alignment constraint. For the unlearning training procedure, it builds directly on the Preference Optimization (PO) method from Maini et al. (2024, Tofu) to train individual LoRA adapters, while the MoE-style routing architecture draws on continual learning ideas (Kirkpatrick et al., 2017) and prior MoE-for-continual-learning work. It departs from existing MLLM unlearning benchmarks (MMUBench, FIUBench, MLLMU-Bench) by being substantially larger in scale and entity diversity, and by explicitly organizing data into a sequential task structure to evaluate cumulative effects rather than single-shot unlearning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MLUBench fills a clear gap as the first large-scale, multi-class benchmark for MLLM lifelong unlearning, and the finding that all standard unlearning methods (GA, GD, KL, NPO) suffer near-total cumulative collapse provides a strong motivation for new approaches. The identification of multimodal alignment preservation as the fundamental constraint—supported by the modality gap measurements and the Unlearn-LLM-Only / Unlearn-Vision-Only ablations—offers a concrete design principle for future methods. Key open questions include: whether LUMoE's reliance on a powerful commercial router (GLM-4V-Plus) for entity identification is practical at scale, whether the isolation-based approach generalizes to fine-grained or overlapping unlearning requests, and whether the alignment-preservation insight transfers to other multimodal architectures beyond LLaVA-style models.
