## Problem

Under KV-cached inference, next-token decisions are computed from single-token decode passes, yet most activation-steering and interpretability methods estimate intervention directions from prefill activations. This estimator-intervention mismatch is a suspected source of steering brittleness, but no prior work has identified or causally validated a task-general subspace that is specific to the decode-time decision state. The paper asks whether a compact, cross-task shared subspace exists in decode-time hidden states and whether it is causally necessary for decisions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes KV-cached decode-time hidden states (single-token forward passes, seq_len=1) collected at a fixed layer ℓ across 128 calibration prompts per task from a heterogeneous benchmark suite (arithmetic, commonsense QA, verification, physical reasoning). It produces a low-dimensional orthonormal shared basis Q_ℓ^(S) (typically ~100 directions out of d≈4096), causal accuracy drops under decode-only projection removal, and optionally a repaired steering vector v_α = (I − αQQ^⊤)v. A key constraint is that all estimation and intervention are aligned to the decode-time distribution; prefill states are used only as a contrast for the H3 transfer test.

## Method

DECODESHARE pools task-centered decode-time hidden states across tasks, runs a single pooled PCA (via SVD) to obtain a shared coordinate system, then identifies the shared core by thresholding per-task relative variance contributions r_{ℓ,t,i} rather than simply taking top-variance components. Causal relevance is tested by projecting out the shared subspace from h_ℓ^(s) only during KV-cached decode steps (Eq. 1), with dimension- and energy-matched non-shared controls to rule out generic perturbation effects. Sufficiency and specificity are confirmed via patchback: restoring only the removed shared component rescues a large fraction of ablation-induced flips, while energy-matched random or non-shared patches do not. A 2×2 estimator-intervention grid (decode-est vs. prefill-est × decode-intervene vs. prefill-intervene) demonstrates that prefill-estimated bases fail to reproduce the decode-time causal effect under matched budgets, establishing a distribution-alignment requirement. The protocol is then applied to steering: projecting the shared component out of arbitrary steering vectors reduces template sensitivity, and decode-stage validation of candidate vectors yields better held-out decode utility than prefill-stage or mixed-stage ranking.

## Results

Removing the identified decode-time shared subspace (k=48, layer ℓ=10) causes a mean accuracy drop of −19.5% across 7 tasks (e.g., CSQA: 53.3→23.1%, QASC: 48.9→13.7%), while dimension-matched random controls (mean +0.3%) and prefill-estimated basis removal (mean +0.2%) remain near baseline. Patchback specificity is near-perfect: restoring only the shared component rescues 84–100% of ablation-induced flips across models and layers, whereas energy-matched random subspaces rescue only 0–50% and non-shared patches rescue 0%. The shared core is compact (≈2–3% of hidden width d) yet statistically significant under both permutation and scramble nulls (p ≤ 5×10⁻⁴) across Llama-2-7B, Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen2.5-7B, and Falcon-7B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is between the decode-estimated shared subspace and three control conditions under identical intervention budgets (same dimension k, same layer, same α). The decode-estimated shared basis produces a mean −19.5% accuracy drop at decode, while the prefill-estimated basis (H3 control) yields only +0.2% and the random dimension-matched subspace yields +0.3%, confirming the effect is direction-specific rather than a generic rank-reduction artifact. Energy-matched controls (α-match at fixed k, and k-match at α=1) further confirm the gap: at α=1, shared removal drops CSQA to 21.7% while the α-matched control stays at 47.8% and the k-matched control at 54.0% (Table 9). For patchback, targeted restoration of the shared component rescues 84–100% of flips, whereas random vectors within the shared subspace rescue only 0–37%, random subspaces of matched dimension rescue 0–50%, and non-shared patches rescue 0% across all model/layer combinations (Table 1). Prefill-stage interventions are negligible across all conditions (mean +0.7%), ruling out prompt-processing confounds. Overall, the shared decode-time subspace is the only condition that consistently and substantially degrades decision accuracy, establishing it as a high-leverage causal channel distinct from all matched controls.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of activation steering (Turner et al. 2023; Rimsky et al. 2024; Zou et al. 2023), causal intervention/patching (Vig et al. 2020; Meng et al. 2022; Wang et al. 2023), and shared-computation/circuit-reuse analyses (Merullo et al. 2024; Kaushik et al. 2025; Wang et al. 2025). It departs from prior work in two specific ways: (1) it estimates and intervenes exclusively on KV-cached decode-time hidden states (sequence-length-1 forward passes) rather than prefill activations, and (2) it pairs shared-subspace ablation with energy-matched non-shared controls and patchback specificity tests to isolate direction-specific causal effects from generic energy-removal confounds. The protocol repurposes pooled PCA + thresholded sharedness scoring + projection ablation in a decode-aligned setting that prior steering and circuit work had not explicitly targeted.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that prefill-estimated shared subspaces largely fail to reproduce decode-time causal effects (behaving like random controls under matched budgets) directly challenges a core assumption in the representation-engineering literature that prefill activations are valid proxies for decision-time geometry. Practically, the decode-aligned evaluation protocol provides a concrete, reproducible method for ranking steering vectors by held-out KV-cached decoding utility, with measurable gains over prefill-based selection (lower flip rates, lower regret). Limitations include the white-box, per-model estimation requirement, primary validation at 7B scale (with 3B/13B/70B checks deferred to appendix), and the fact that the shared channel's computational content remains partially characterized (procedural/symbolic tokens vs. open-ended semantics). Open questions include whether the shared decode subspace has a stable functional identity across model families, how it interacts with task-specific subspaces during multi-step reasoning, and whether decode-aligned steering repair generalizes to longer-horizon generation beyond the tested K-step horizon.
