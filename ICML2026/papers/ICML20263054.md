## Problem

Existing sleeping experts algorithms achieve regret bounds of Õ(√(KT)), which become vacuous (linear in T) when the number of experts K equals the time horizon T. This paper introduces the Awakening Crowd of Experts (ACE) problem, where a new expert arrives at every round and never leaves, and shows that this monotonic awakening structure fundamentally simplifies the problem, enabling sublinear regret in the stochastic case and near-optimal α-regret in the adversarial case.

## Input / Output

At each round t, a new expert awakens and joins the nested set S_t of available experts; the learner selects one expert from S_t and then observes the rewards of all awake experts (full-information feedback). The baseline is not a single expert but the best *ordering* σ* of all T experts, where the induced policy picks the highest-ranked available expert each round. In the adversarial variant, the learner also receives a parameter α ∈ (0,1) controlling the target competitive ratio, and performance is measured as α-regret rather than standard regret.

## Method

For stochastic ACE, the paper proposes a pessimistic LCB policy that selects the expert with the highest *lower* confidence bound, deliberately avoiding the overestimation of new experts that plagues optimistic UCB methods; this yields Õ(√(Υ*·T)) regret where Υ* is the number of switches of the optimal ordering, and Õ(T^{2/3}) in the worst case. For adversarial ACE, the algorithm randomizes between two strategies: with probability M/(M+1) it samples uniformly from the M most recently awakened experts (handling short optimal intervals via a competitive-ratio argument), and with probability 1/(M+1) it follows a meta-regret minimizer (Fixed Share) over a set of base learners with doubling window sizes M·2^j, which reduces the expert-pool dependence from log(T) to log(log T) and achieves α-regret of O(√(log(1/α))·α^{3/2}·T·√(log log T)), nearly matching the Ω(α²T) lower bound.

## Results

In the stochastic ACE problem, the proposed pessimistic LCB policy achieves an expected ordering regret of Õ(T^{2/3}), which is tight up to logarithmic factors (matching lower bound of Ω(T^{2/3})). In the adversarial ACE problem, the paper establishes a fundamental lower bound of Ω(α²T) on α-regret for any constant α ∈ (0,1), and provides an almost-matching upper bound of O(√(log(α⁻¹)) · α^{3/2} · T · √(log log T)) via a meta-regret minimizer. As a corollary, when the optimal ordering's cumulative reward is linear in T, the algorithm achieves a Õ(log log T) competitive ratio. Experimentally, on Yahoo Finance stock data (T=1500 days), the LCB policy substantially outperforms standard UCB in cumulative ordering regret and switches experts far less frequently (tracking optimal policy switches rather than switching almost every 1.5 days as UCB does).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary theoretical comparison is against the sleeping experts framework of Kleinberg et al. (2010), whose optimal regret bound of Õ(√(KT)) becomes vacuous (i.e., O(T)) when K=T as in the ACE setting. The proposed LCB algorithm bypasses this limitation by exploiting the awakening structure, achieving sublinear Õ(T^{2/3}) regret in the stochastic case. In the adversarial case, the paper shows that no algorithm can achieve sublinear α-regret for constant α (a result that also precludes Kleinberg et al.'s approach), and the proposed randomized meta-learner nearly matches the lower bound. Experimentally, the LCB policy is compared against standard UCB on Yahoo Finance data (2018–2024, T=1500) and simulated stochastic environments (T=5000, 20 runs). UCB switches experts excessively (nearly twice every three days) and exhibits superlinear cumulative regret growth, while LCB successfully tracks the switches of an optimal ordering policy and maintains substantially lower regret across all tested instances (first-best, uniform experts, and frequent-switches scenarios).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sleeping experts framework (Kleinberg et al., 2010) to the special case where the number of experts equals the time horizon (K=T) and experts only awaken (never sleep), a structure the authors call the Awakening Crowd of Experts (ACE) problem. While prior sleeping experts results yield vacuous O(T) regret bounds when K=T, the paper shows the nested awakening structure fundamentally changes the problem: sublinear regret becomes achievable in the stochastic regime via a pessimistic LCB policy (unusual, as optimistic UCB methods fail here), while the adversarial regime admits only linear α-regret. The adversarial algorithm draws on weakly-adaptive regret minimization (Hazan & Seshadhri, 2007; Castiglioni et al., 2024) and a meta-learner over doubling-scheme base minimizers to reduce the expert-pool dependence from log T to log log T.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean separation between stochastic and adversarial ACE: sublinear regret is achievable in the former but provably impossible (for constant α) in the latter, revealing that the awakening structure alone is insufficient to overcome adversarial hardness. The open problem of whether the log log T factor in the adversarial upper bound is inherent or an artifact of the doubling scheme remains unresolved. Practically, the framework captures real settings like Q&A platforms, social proof marketing, and financial forecasting under regime shifts, where new 'experts' arrive continuously and the learner must balance exploiting proven veterans against adopting newcomers.
