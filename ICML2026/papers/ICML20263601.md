## Problem

Uncertainty quantification in GNN node regression remains underexplored: existing frequentist methods rely on exchangeability assumptions often violated by graph structure, while resampling and post-hoc calibration (e.g., conformal prediction) are costly or unstable. Standard quantile regression, a natural alternative, requires explicit quantile-level inputs and suffers from quantile crossing and oversmoothing when combined with graph message passing, yielding poorly calibrated and non-compact intervals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263601/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a graph G=(V,E) with node feature matrix X ∈ R^{n×d} and scalar regression targets y ∈ R^n, and produces a per-node prediction interval [ŷ_v − d̂_v, ŷ_v + d̂_v] where the center ŷ_v and half-width d̂_v > 0 are predicted by separate linear heads over shared GNN embeddings. No quantile parameter τ, calibration set, or resampling is required at inference; the only hyperparameter controlling interval tightness is the width penalty λ_width, tunable per dataset via Bayesian optimization.

## Method

QpiGNN employs a dual-head GraphSAGE encoder: one linear head predicts the regression center ŷ, and a second head with Softplus activation predicts the interval half-width d̂, structurally decoupling prediction from uncertainty to mitigate oversmoothing and gradient interference. Training uses a quantile-free joint loss L_total = (ĉ − (1−α))² + ℓ̂_viol + λ_width · E[ŷ^up − ŷ^low], where the coverage term penalizes deviation from the target 1−α plus the magnitude of violations, and the L1 width term encourages compactness—directly optimizing both objectives from label-only supervision without any quantile input. Theoretically, the loss is interpreted as a Lagrangian relaxation of the constrained width-minimization problem, yielding asymptotic coverage consistency via WLLN, finite-sample concentration bounds via McDiarmid's inequality under localized message passing, and near-optimal interval width under symmetric conditional distributions.

## Results

Across 19 synthetic and real-world node regression benchmarks (target coverage 90%), QpiGNN with Bayesian-optimized λ achieves average 22% higher PICP and 50% narrower MPIW than the best baseline. On synthetic graphs, QpiGNN(λ^opt.) attains PICP of 0.90–0.99 with MPIW of 0.30–0.93, while on real-world datasets it reaches PICP of 0.93–1.00 with MPIW of 0.16–0.77. The method maintains valid coverage under feature noise (σ up to 0.3), target noise, and edge dropout (p up to 0.6), outperforming SQR-GNN and RQR^adj.-GNN in all perturbation settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QpiGNN(λ^opt.) dominates BayesianNN on interval width by a factor of ~5–10× (e.g., MPIW 0.30 vs. 3.01 on Basic; 0.23 vs. 2.95 on Chameleon) while maintaining comparable coverage (≥0.90). Against MC Dropout, QpiGNN achieves substantially higher PICP (e.g., 0.98 vs. 0.76 on ER; 0.96 vs. 0.31 on Squirrel) at only modestly wider intervals, making it far more reliable. On heterophily graphs (Chameleon, Squirrel, Crocodile), SQR-GNN collapses to PICP of 0.22–0.44, whereas QpiGNN maintains 0.96–0.97, a gap of over 50 percentage points. CF-GNN achieves valid coverage but produces extremely wide intervals (MPIW up to 68.27 on BA), while QpiGNN keeps MPIW below 1.0 across all datasets. RQR^adj.-GNN is the closest competitor on real-world data but still trails QpiGNN by 5–15% in PICP on several datasets (e.g., Education: 0.87 vs. 0.99) and produces wider intervals. Overall, QpiGNN is the only method that simultaneously meets the 90% coverage target and keeps intervals compact across all 19 benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

QpiGNN builds on the quantile-free interval estimation idea from Relaxed Quantile Regression (RQR, Pouplin et al. 2024), which was designed for MLPs, and adapts it to GNNs by introducing a dual-head architecture (borrowed from heteroscedastic regression, Kendall & Gal 2017) and a decoupled joint loss that separates coverage from width. It departs from standard quantile regression (Koenker & Bassett 1978) and SQR (Tagasovska & Lopez-Paz 2019), which require explicit quantile-level inputs and suffer from quantile crossing in graph settings, and from conformal prediction methods (CF-GNN, Huang et al. 2023) that rely on exchangeability assumptions often violated by graph structure. The key conceptual move is showing that RQR's single-head, entangled loss fails under GNN message passing (collapsing to ~0.19 PICP on BA graphs) and that decoupling prediction from uncertainty via separate heads with an L1 width penalty resolves this.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: GNN-based UQ in high-stakes domains (healthcare, criminal justice) currently requires either expensive resampling, Bayesian approximation, or post-hoc calibration with strong exchangeability assumptions. QpiGNN offers a single-pass, assumption-light alternative with theoretical coverage guarantees, which is practically valuable for deployment. However, the theoretical guarantees rely on idealized conditions (node independence, symmetric conditional distributions) that are only approximately satisfied in real graphs, and the method's sensitivity to the underlying GNN backbone and to asymmetric/heavy-tailed targets remains unaddressed. Open questions include extending to dynamic graphs, disentangling aleatoric from epistemic uncertainty, and formalizing concentration under graph-dependent mixing conditions.
