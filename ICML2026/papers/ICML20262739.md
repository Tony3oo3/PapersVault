## Problem

Deterministic fine-tuning methods like LoRA cause LLMs to become systematically overconfident, especially on small datasets, degrading calibration in ways that are dangerous for safety-critical deployments. Full Bayesian inference is computationally infeasible at LLM scale, and post-hoc calibration methods (e.g., temperature scaling, Laplace approximations) fail under distribution shift because they learn a fixed rescaling that becomes stale. The paper asks whether LoRA's low-rank structure can be naturally embedded in a probabilistic framework that optimizes calibration end-to-end during training.

## Input / Output

For classification tasks (commonsense reasoning, math), the method takes a text prompt with discrete answer options (2–5 candidates) and produces a calibrated probability distribution over those options via length-normalized log-likelihood scoring, from which a predicted label and confidence are derived. For generative language modeling (WikiText-2), it takes a text sequence and produces per-token probability distributions. In both settings, N Monte Carlo samples of the inducing variables yield multiple weight realizations whose spread encodes epistemic uncertainty; inputs are truncated to 1024 tokens, and the model supports a zero-overhead deterministic inference mode (merging the posterior mean) or an uncertainty mode with N=2–4 forward passes.

## Method

Bayesian-LoRA identifies a structural isomorphism between Kronecker-factored Sparse GP posteriors and LoRA's factorization: the conditional mean M_W(U) = T_r U T_c shares the same bilinear form as ΔW = (α/r)BA, with projection operators T_r, T_c playing the roles of B and A, and a low-dimensional inducing matrix U (r×c, with r=c=LoRA rank) replacing the deterministic core. A Gaussian prior with Kronecker-factored covariance K_c ⊗ K_r is placed on U, and the variational posterior is enriched by a lightweight Masked Autoregressive Flow (MAF) over a diagonal-Gaussian base, keeping the parameter overhead to only ~0.42M. Training maximizes a flow-augmented ELBO with three terms: a Monte Carlo-approximated expected log-likelihood, a KL over inducing variables (invariant under the flow transform by Proposition 3.1, so optimization in U-space is equivalent to ΔW-space), and a closed-form conditional KL that is independent of U and requires no Hessian computation. In the limit of a point-mass posterior and vanishing conditional noise (λ→0), the method reduces exactly to deterministic LoRA, making standard LoRA a special case.

## Results

On six commonsense reasoning benchmarks with Llama-2-7B, Bayesian-LoRA (N=4) achieves the highest accuracy on 5 of 6 datasets, reaching 70.90% on WinoGrande-S (+2.9 over LoRA MAP), 81.60% on OBQA (+3.9 over MAP), and 86.10% on BoolQ. It attains the best ECE on WinoGrande-S (4.90 vs. 30.80 for MAP, an 84% reduction) and WinoGrande-M (3.00), and the best NLL on four benchmarks (e.g., 0.29 on BoolQ vs. 0.43 for MAP, a 76% reduction). On WikiText-2 language modeling, Bayesian-LoRA (N=2) reduces test ECE from 1.48 (LoRA MAP) to 1.41 and top-5%-uncertain-token ECE from 1.60 to 1.26. On MATH with Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct, it achieves CoT-NLL of 0.513 and CoT-ECE of 5.81 with 51.1% accuracy, outperforming all baselines including BLoB (N=10).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Bayesian-LoRA outperforms standard LoRA (MAP) on accuracy for 5 of 6 benchmarks (e.g., +2.9 on WinoGrande-S, +3.9 on OBQA) and dramatically on calibration: ECE drops from 30.80 to 4.90 on WinoGrande-S (84% reduction) and NLL from 0.43 to 0.29 on BoolQ (76% reduction). Against BLoB (N=10), the closest Bayesian competitor, Bayesian-LoRA wins on accuracy for 5 of 6 benchmarks (e.g., 70.90 vs. 69.07 on WinoGrande-S, 81.60 vs. 81.52 on OBQA) while using only N=4 samples versus BLoB's N=10, and achieves lower ECE on WinoGrande-S (4.90 vs. 9.35) and WinoGrande-M (3.00 vs. 3.01). Post-hoc Laplace methods (LA, LLLA) achieve competitive ECE on in-distribution data (e.g., LA: 3.40 on ARC-E vs. Bayesian-LoRA's 5.30) but lose on accuracy across all six benchmarks and degrade under large distribution shift (Table 6: Bayesian-LoRA ECE 11.1 on CS vs. LA's 14.5). Temperature scaling wins on ECE for ARC-C (4.60 vs. 9.20) and ARC-E (4.70 vs. 5.30) but provides no accuracy gain and no true uncertainty quantification. BBB (Bayesian Backpropagation) shows high variance (e.g., 56.54±7.87 ACC on WinoGrande-S) and is outperformed on all metrics by Bayesian-LoRA. Dropout and checkpoint ensembles are consistently outperformed on both accuracy and calibration, with Bayesian-LoRA achieving 1.23× training time versus ~4× for Dropout and ~3× for Deep Ensembles.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper identifies a structural isomorphism between Kronecker-factored Sparse Gaussian Process (SGP) posteriors and LoRA's low-rank factorization, positioning LoRA as a deterministic limiting case of a probabilistic inducing-variable model. It builds directly on the FFG-U inducing-weight framework (Ritter et al., 2021) and the SGP variational machinery (Titsias, 2009), while departing from BLoB (Wang et al., 2024b) by replacing mean-field Gaussian posteriors over the full LoRA parameter space with a low-rank GP posterior enriched by a normalizing flow. Unlike post-hoc calibration methods (LA, LLLA) that apply corrections after training, Bayesian-LoRA optimizes calibration end-to-end within the adapter subspace, and unlike full Bayesian neural networks, it keeps overhead at PEFT level (~0.42M extra parameters, ~1.2× training cost).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Calibrated uncertainty in fine-tuned LLMs is essential for safety-critical deployment (medical diagnosis, autonomous driving), and this paper shows that meaningful calibration gains (up to 84% ECE reduction) are achievable with minimal compute overhead that scales to 30B MoE architectures. The end-to-end Bayesian training proves more robust than post-hoc rescaling under large distribution shift, addressing a known failure mode of temperature scaling. Open questions include whether per-layer independent inducing matrices can be replaced by hierarchical priors capturing inter-layer correlations, whether the approach extends to RLHF/instruction-tuning pipelines, and whether tighter theoretical bounds on the sparse inducing approximation can be established.
