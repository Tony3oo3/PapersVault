## Problem

GFlowNets for molecular generation must truncate trajectories at a fixed maximum length to keep the chemical space tractable, but this forces incomplete fragments at the boundary to be treated as terminal states alongside valid molecules. The resulting 'flow leakage'—probability mass allocated to incomplete structures—distorts the learned distribution and destabilizes training, yet no prior work directly addresses this target-space mismatch induced by truncation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262097/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

For de novo generation the model takes no input and builds molecules by sequentially attaching fragments from a 105-building-block vocabulary onto a growing molecular graph, with a default budget of 8 fragments (L_max=8). In pocket-conditioned mode a protein binding pocket is encoded and fed to the shared graph transformer; in multi-objective mode a preference vector ω∈Δ^{K-1} conditions a hypernetwork that generates head weights. Output is a molecular graph scored by task-specific oracles (random forests for JNK3/GSK3β, SVM for DRD2, RDKit for QED/SA), with evaluation metrics computed on raw oracle scores rather than the transformed training rewards.

## Method

LEAKGFN decomposes the GFlowNet flow into a chemical head F_chem (log-scale flow over all transitions, including continuation past the truncation boundary) and a valid head λ (sigmoid-gated fraction reaching accessible terminals), so that the sampling policy uses F_valid = F_chem · λ for add actions. The key mechanism is asymmetric terminal treatment: at forced terminals F_chem enforces flow conservation (treating them as non-terminals with positive outgoing add-flow), while F_valid applies a terminal loss equal to the oracle reward, which jointly drives λ→0 at the boundary without explicit reachability labels. Both heads are trained with a flow-matching objective (squared log-ratios of in/out flow), with the valid head additionally incurring a terminal loss at both valid and forced terminals, and an auxiliary absorbing stop transition calibrates F(s,stop)=R(s). Exploration uses the chemical-flow policy rather than uniform random actions, keeping exploratory steps on chemically plausible transitions. The paper proves that at a reward-proportional fixed point the sampler is exact over accessible molecules conditioned on valid completion, and gives a closed-form expression for the residual leakage mass on forced terminals.

## Results

On five single-objective molecular optimization tasks, LEAKGFN achieves the best or tied-best harmonic mean (HM) on four of five: JNK3 (0.653), GSK3β (0.760), QED (0.926), and SA (0.923). On DRD2, balance-based objectives GFN-DB (0.934) and GFN-TB (0.933) outperform LEAKGFN (0.797) at L_max=8, but LEAKGFN degrades far more gracefully at larger L_max (0.435 at L_max=12 vs. 0.096–0.107 for balance-based methods). The most dramatic gain is on JNK3, where LEAKGFN raises HM from 0.403 (GFN-FM) to 0.653 while reducing seed-to-seed standard deviation from 0.218 to 0.068. In pocket-conditioned generation (CrossDocked), integrating LEAKGFN into TacoGFN improves mean Vina Dock from −8.237 to −8.589 kcal/mol, and in multi-objective GSK3β+JNK3 it raises hypervolume from 0.550 (HN-GFN) to 0.673.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LEAKGFN outperforms all non-GFlowNet baselines (Graph-GA, REINVENT, GEGL) on every task, with the largest gaps on QED and SA where diversity matters most. Against the closest GFlowNet competitor GFN-FM (the direct flow-matching baseline), LEAKGFN wins decisively on JNK3 (0.653 vs. 0.403) and GSK3β (0.760 vs. 0.716), ties on QED (0.926 vs. 0.926) and SA (0.923 vs. 0.922), and edges ahead on DRD2 (0.797 vs. 0.775). Against balance-based GFlowNet variants (GFN-DB, GFN-TB, GFN-SubTB), LEAKGFN wins on JNK3, GSK3β, QED, and SA but loses on DRD2 at L_max=8 (0.797 vs. 0.934/0.933/0.845); however, at L_max=12 LEAKGFN retains 0.435 while all three collapse to 0.096–0.107, demonstrating substantially better hyperparameter robustness. In pocket-conditioned generation, LEAKGFN integrated into TacoGFN beats TargetDiff, Pocket2Mol, DecompDiff, and vanilla TacoGFN on Vina Dock, QED, SA, and novel-hit rate, with only a marginal diversity drop (0.514 vs. 0.530). In multi-objective GSK3β+JNK3, LEAKGFN integrated into HN-GFN achieves hypervolume 0.673 vs. 0.550 for HN-GFN and 0.545 for PS-GFN, while maintaining comparable diversity (0.786 vs. 0.797).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GFlowNet framework (Bengio et al., 2021; 2023) by identifying and formally defining 'flow leakage'—the probability mass a truncated GFlowNet allocates to incomplete fragments at the trajectory boundary—as a distinct problem from the training objective or exploration strategy. Prior work has addressed related challenges through alternative objectives (trajectory balance, subtrajectory balance, detailed balance), exploration strategies (Thompson sampling, local search, genetic operators), and forward-looking credit assignment, but none directly model the target-space mismatch induced by truncation. LEAKGFN's dual-head decomposition (chemical flow × valid fraction) is orthogonal to the training objective and integrates as a plug-and-play module into both flow-matching and trajectory-balance frameworks, as demonstrated with TacoGFN and HN-GFN.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is strongest in the robustness finding: balance-based GFlowNet objectives (DB, TB, SubTB) collapse catastrophically when L_max is set conservatively larger (e.g., GFN-DB drops from 0.934 to 0.096 on DRD2 going from L_max=8 to 12), while LEAKGFN degrades gracefully—a critical property for real-world drug discovery where target molecule size is unknown a priori. The modular design (22% parameter increase, 2.8% inference overhead) makes it broadly applicable across GFlowNet variants. Open questions include whether the approach extends to atom-level construction and continuous action spaces, whether λ can drive adaptive truncation, and the absence of formal convergence guarantees for the training dynamics that underpin the empirical gains.
