## Problem

Constructing fast surrogate models for parametric PDEs from sparse, irregularly sampled observations with well-calibrated uncertainty remains challenging because existing methods cannot simultaneously handle variable observation patterns, enforce physics consistency, and preserve predictive variance. Two compounding obstacles are identified: fixed collocation points cause discretization overfitting (memorizing sampling patterns rather than learning continuous physics), and applying PDE constraints directly to stochastic predictions induces variance collapse (models suppress uncertainty to minimize residuals).

## Input / Output

The model takes a variable-sized context set of sparse, noisy point observations at irregular spatial or spatiotemporal locations (Nc ranging from 10 to 128), a vector of PDE parameters λ (e.g., diffusion coefficients, forcing terms), and arbitrary query locations T. It produces continuous-domain predictive marginals at any query point, yielding both a mean solution field and spatially-varying heteroscedastic standard deviations that distinguish epistemic uncertainty (reducible with more observations) from aleatoric measurement noise. Because BSNP is trained as a meta-learner across a distribution of PDE instances, a single trained model amortizes inference across the entire parametric family without per-instance retraining.

## Method

BSNP extends Convolutional Neural Processes by integrating weak structural priors (translation equivariance via CNN, permutation invariance and locality via RBF kernel deposition onto a regular latent grid) with strong physical priors (PDE and boundary residuals) through two key mechanisms. First, stochastic collocation resamples residual evaluation points i.i.d. from the domain each training step, providing an unbiased Monte Carlo estimate of the domain-integrated physics loss and eliminating discretization overfitting. Second, mean-field enforcement evaluates PDE residuals only on the deterministic predictive mean conditioned on the context-only posterior mean z_phys, rather than on stochastic samples, which provably prevents variance collapse (Proposition 4.4) while still guiding the mean toward physical consistency. The training objective combines a data ELBO (using a target-conditioned latent z_data sampled via reparameterization) with weighted physics and boundary residual penalties, and the paper provides an ELBO interpretation showing the physics term corresponds to a residual pseudo-observation likelihood in a hierarchical generative model.

## Results

On the 1D Nonlinear Poisson benchmark, BSNP achieves the best MNSE of 9.62×10⁻⁵, NLL of 0.37, and ECP(90%) of 90.4%, outperforming the next-best probabilistic method PDDLVM (MNSE 5.44×10⁻⁴) by ~5.7×. On the Burgers equation, BSNP achieves MNSE 8.73×10⁻⁴, NLL 0.68, and ECP(90%) 88.7%, beating PDDLVM (MNSE 3.58×10⁻¹) by ~410× and GRNP (MNSE 2.77×10⁻³) by ~3.2×. BSNP is the only method reporting all three metrics (accuracy, probabilistic quality, and calibration) while remaining competitive on inference time (11.8 s on Poisson, 101.8 s on Burgers).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BSNP outperforms all seven baselines on MNSE for both the 1D Nonlinear Poisson and Burgers equations. Against the closest deterministic competitor MT-DeepONet, BSNP achieves 2.2× lower MNSE on Poisson (9.62×10⁻⁵ vs. 2.14×10⁻⁴) and 1.75× lower on Burgers (8.73×10⁻⁴ vs. 1.53×10⁻³). Among probabilistic methods, BSNP beats PDDLVM by 5.7× on Poisson MNSE and by ~410× on Burgers MNSE, while also achieving better NLL (0.37 vs. 0.42 on Poisson; 0.68 vs. 0.79 on Burgers) and higher ECP (90.4% vs. 87.6%; 88.7% vs. 84.2%). GRNP is the second-best probabilistic baseline but still trails BSNP by 5× on Poisson MNSE and 3.2× on Burgers MNSE. The per-instance retraining methods PINN and FBPINN are substantially worse (MNSE 4.93×10⁻² and 5.26×10⁻³ on Poisson; 3.73 and 0.937 on Burgers) and 6–7× slower at test time. FFNet and DeepONet, which lack explicit physics constraints, show the largest errors among non-PINN baselines, confirming the critical role of PDE residual enforcement in sparse-data regimes.

## Relation to Prior Work

BSNP extends Convolutional Neural Processes (ConvNP, Gordon et al. 2020) by injecting strong PDE residual constraints into the probabilistic meta-learning framework, bridging the gap between weak architectural priors (translation equivariance, locality) and strong physical priors (governing equations). It departs from PINNs (Raissi et al. 2019) by providing amortized inference across parametric families rather than per-instance retraining, and from neural operators (Li et al. 2020, Kovachki et al. 2023) by handling irregular, variable-cardinality observations without requiring structured grids. The two key mechanisms—stochastic collocation to prevent discretization overfitting and mean-field enforcement to prevent variance collapse—address specific failure modes identified in prior physics-informed probabilistic methods (Yang et al. 2021; Liu et al. 2024) that had not been simultaneously resolved.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled resolution to two compounding failure modes (discretization overfitting and uncertainty collapse) that have limited the practical utility of physics-informed probabilistic models in sparse-data regimes, with theoretical guarantees (ELBO interpretation, variance collapse proof, O(N_r^{-1/2}) convergence) that go beyond empirical observation. The mean-field enforcement strategy—applying PDE constraints only to predictive means while preserving learned uncertainty—is a clean design principle likely to be adopted in future physics-informed probabilistic architectures. Limitations include reliance on differentiable PDE operators, sensitivity to physics loss weights requiring per-problem tuning, and validation primarily on low-dimensional benchmarks (1D Poisson, 2D Burgers/Navier-Stokes); open questions include adaptive collocation strategies, extension to inverse problems with joint parameter-solution inference, and scaling to higher-dimensional or multi-physics settings.
