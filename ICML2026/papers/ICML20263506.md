## Problem

Gölz et al. (2025) showed that RLHF distortion can scale exponentially with the Bradley-Terry temperature β, suggesting a catastrophic failure mode for utilitarian alignment. However, this exponential bound arises only in the limit of extreme distribution mismatch (B→∞) between the preference data distribution μ and the KL reference policy π_ref, leaving open whether RLHF is fundamentally a poor utilitarian aligner or whether the distortion is merely a consequence of off-policy data. This paper asks: under what conditions is RLHF distortion reasonably controlled, and what portion is algorithm-independent and unavoidable?

## Input / Output

The analysis takes as input a set of paired preference comparisons generated under a Bradley-Terry model with temperature β, sampled from a data distribution μ over alternatives, along with a reference policy π_ref and a KL budget τ. The key quantitative parameter is B = max_x log(μ(x)/π_ref(x)), the maximum log density ratio between the data and reference distributions. The output is the RLHF policy π_RLHF (the KL-constrained reward maximizer) together with tight upper and lower bounds on its distortion, i.e., the multiplicative gap between the optimal average utility within the KL ball and the utility achieved by π_RLHF.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a fine-grained distortion analysis that decomposes RLHF's suboptimality into two multiplicative factors: the unavoidable BT nonlinearity (β) and the distribution mismatch (B). The proof introduces an 'effective utility' that retains only the components of true utility informative to pairwise comparisons (zeroing out alternatives that lose to a μ-majority and capping large utilities at cβ⁻¹), then 'sandwiches' the learned reward between the effective utility and the true utility via localized sigmoid linearization. In the AI alignment setting with a KL constraint, the key difficulty is that the data distribution μ may lie outside the KL ball around π_ref; this is resolved by constructing an interpolating distribution π' = λμ + (1−λ)π_ref with λ = min{B⁻¹τ, 1} that provably lies within the KL ball, yielding the bound O(min{e^B τ, B, Bτ⁻¹} · β). Reward clipping to the interval [r_min, r_max] focuses optimization on the informative reward regime and is essential for achieving the tight O(Bβ) rate. Matching lower bounds (up to log factors) confirm that Ω(Bβ) distortion persists even under exponentially small KL budgets, and an algorithm-independent Ω(β) lower bound holds for all τ when μ = π_ref.

## Results

In the social choice setting (no KL constraint), the paper proves an O(β) distortion bound for RLHF (Theorem 3.1), improving upon the prior O(β²) bound of Gölz et al. (2025). In the AI alignment setting with KL constraint, the distortion is bounded by O(min{e^B·τ, B, B/τ} + 1)·β (Theorem 4.1), where B is the maximum log density ratio between the preference data distribution μ and the reference policy π_ref. When there is no distribution mismatch (μ = π_ref, i.e., B = 0), RLHF achieves the optimal O(β) distortion, matching the algorithm-independent lower bound up to a constant. Matching lower bounds of Ω̃(Bβ) (Theorem 5.1) and Ω(β) (Theorem 5.2) confirm tightness up to logarithmic factors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Gölz et al. (2025), the paper improves the social choice distortion bound from O(β²) to O(β) by introducing an effective utility that avoids double-linearizing the Bradley-Terry sigmoid, and replaces the exponential e^{Ω(β)} AI alignment bound with a polynomial O(Bβ) bound by isolating distribution mismatch as the driver. The exponential bound is shown to arise only in the limit B→∞, not as a fundamental property of RLHF. Against Boutilier et al. (2012), the paper's O(β) social choice bound is finite and applies to the stochastic Bradley-Terry setting, whereas Boutilier et al.'s Ω(m^{1/2}) lower bound applies to the deterministic (β=∞) limit. The empirical validation (Section 6) confirms the theory: in a synthetic experiment with β=10 and B=11.5, distortion converges to 1 when μ=π_ref but grows unboundedly when μ≠π_ref, and practical reward models (Skywork, UltraRM) exhibit reward scales (max Δr up to 108.8) indicating operation in the nonlinear BT regime where distortion effects are non-negligible.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the distortion framework for AI alignment introduced by Gölz et al. (2025), who showed RLHF suffers exponential distortion e^Ω(β) and left the μ=π_ref case as an open question. It also builds on Siththaranjan et al. (2023) for the ordering equivalence between Bradley-Terry rewards and Borda scores. The key conceptual departure is identifying distribution mismatch B between the preference data distribution μ and the KL reference policy π_ref as the true source of RLHF's poor distortion, rather than the nonlinearity of the BT model alone. The paper introduces 'effective utility' as an analytical tool to achieve tight O(Bβ) bounds, improving upon the prior O(β²) social choice bound and the e^Ω(β) AI alignment bound.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This result reconciles the theoretical pessimism about RLHF (exponential distortion) with empirical observations that RLHF performs reasonably well in practice, by showing the exponential regime only arises under extreme distribution mismatch. The practical implication—that on-policy data collection or fine-tuning the base model on preference data before RLHF mitigates distortion—provides actionable guidance for training pipelines. Open questions remain about how to efficiently precondition off-policy preference data (filtering, reweighting) at scale, and when the additional robustness of methods like NLHF justifies their computational cost over RLHF. The analysis is primarily theoretical with only small-scale synthetic validation, leaving the question of whether these bounds are tight in realistic LLM settings.
