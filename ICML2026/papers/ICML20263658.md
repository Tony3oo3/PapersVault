## Problem

Existing infinite-dimensional generative diffusion models rely on time-reversing a forward noising SDE, which requires the noising process to converge to a tractable reference measure over a sufficiently long time horizon. In infinite-dimensional Hilbert spaces, this convergence can fail or be impractically slow, causing biased or degenerate sampling. The paper asks whether one can define a generative diffusion that is well-posed for arbitrary time horizons without depending on long-time convergence of a noising process.

## Input / Output

The method takes i.i.d. training samples y_1,…,y_N from a target measure μ on an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space H, together with a predetermined trace-class covariance operator C defining the Gaussian reference measure N(0,C). For the conditional (Bayesian inverse) setting, an observation y is additionally encoded as a conditioning input to the score network. Outputs are samples from μ (or the posterior X|Y=y in the inverse-problem setting), generated by first Langevin-sampling from a modified initial distribution μ_0^h and then forward-simulating the forced SPDE. On MNIST-SDF, the model produces 64×64 or 128×128 signed-distance-function images; on the seismic imaging task it produces posterior mean fields and marginal standard deviations from 1024 generated samples.

## Method

The core construction replaces time reversal with Doob's h-transform: a reference VP-SPDE (an inhomogeneous Ornstein-Uhlenbeck equation with unbounded drift A = ½C^{−γ} and noise Q = C^{1−γ}) is forced to hit the target μ at an arbitrary time T via the exponential change of measure dP^h_t = h(t,X_t) dP_t, where h(t,x) = ∫ p(t,x;T,y)/p_T(y) dμ(y). Under P^h the process satisfies a new SDE with an additive steering term β(t)Q s(t,X_t), where s(t,x) = D_x log h(t,x). The steering function is learned by minimising a score-matching-type loss that is shown to be equivalent to the KL divergence between the true forced path measure and a parameterised approximation; for the linear VP-SPDE the loss is evaluated on tractable Ornstein-Uhlenbeck bridge samples. At inference, sampling proceeds in two stages: (i) Langevin sampling from the h-corrected initial distribution μ_0^h (which absorbs any mismatch between the prior and the back-noised data when T is short), and (ii) forward integration of the forced SDE. This design guarantees exact marginal matching at time T regardless of horizon length, and the paper derives a Wasserstein-2 error bound decomposing initialisation, score-estimation, and numerical-discretisation errors.

## Results

On MNIST-SDF at 64×64 resolution, the VP-SPDE achieves a FID of 3.12, competitive with the DDO baseline (2.74) and substantially outperforming MultiDiff (35.09) and GANO (3.41). On the synthetic Gaussian mixture (D=500), the forced noise-scheduled model (FNS) achieves sliced Wasserstein distances of 0.055 (T=1) and 0.053 (T=0.2), demonstrating robustness to short noising horizons where the finite-dimensional ND baseline degrades from 0.274 to 0.692. On the seismic imaging inverse problem, VP-SPDE achieves a relative error of 0.09 and mean absolute error of 26.82, compared to 0.17 and 37.80 for the noising-denoising baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On MNIST-SDF, VP-SPDE (FID 3.12) outperforms GANO (3.41) and MultiDiff (35.09) but falls slightly short of DDO (2.74), which the authors attribute to limited hyperparameter tuning. On the Gaussian mixture experiment, the key advantage is robustness to short noising time T: at T=0.2, FNS achieves a sliced Wasserstein distance of 0.053 while ND degrades to 0.692 and HND to 0.51, confirming that the h-transform approach avoids the pathologies of time-reversed diffusions under insufficient noising. On the seismic imaging inverse problem, VP-SPDE achieves a relative error of 0.09 versus 0.17 for the noising-denoising baseline of Baldassari et al. (2023), and a mean absolute error of 26.82 versus 37.80, while also producing more uniform posterior uncertainty estimates. The finite-dimensional ND baseline diverges as state dimension D increases, whereas both forced VP-SPDE variants remain stable, validating the infinite-dimensional formulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant noising-denoising paradigm (Song et al., 2021; Pidstrigach et al., 2024) that relies on time-reversing a forward SDE whose marginals must converge to a tractable reference measure. Instead, it draws on the classical Doob's h-transform from the infinite-dimensional SDE literature (Fuhrman, 2003; Pieper-Sethmacher et al., 2025a) to force a reference VP-SPDE directly to the target at an arbitrary time T, bypassing the need for long-time convergence. The approach is conceptually related to the non-denoising forward-time diffusions of Peluchetti (2023b) and the Schrödinger bridge framework of De Bortoli et al. (2021), but differs in that it uses a single unified change of measure rather than iterative projections or mixture constructions, and it extends to time-dependent and nonlinear drifts with unbounded operators. Compared to the closest infinite-dimensional h-transform work of Park et al. (2024), which learns bridges between prescribed endpoint distributions via optimal control, this paper targets the generative modeling task and provides explicit well-posedness conditions and Wasserstein bounds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical advantage is robustness to short noising horizons: the h-transform's correction of the initial distribution via μ₀ʰ prevents the bias and degenerate sampling that plague time-reversed diffusions when T is insufficient, a problem documented in Franzese et al. (2023b). This is particularly relevant for Bayesian inverse problems and function-space generative modeling where the natural time scale of the reference process may not align with the data structure. Open questions include the optimal choice of the roughness parameter γ and covariance operator C, adaptive selection of T and discretization parameters, and the connection between the h-function and infinite-dimensional stochastic optimal control or Schrödinger bridge formulations. The empirical results are competitive but not dominant (FID 3.12 vs. DDO's 2.74 on MNIST-SDF), and the authors acknowledge limited hyperparameter tuning, suggesting the method's full potential remains unrealized.
