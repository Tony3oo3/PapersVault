## Problem

While Montanari & Urbani (2025) identified feature unlearning under continuous-time gradient flow, the underlying mechanism of time-scale separation driving the loss of alignment remained incompletely characterized, and the analysis did not extend to discrete-time SGD. This paper asks whether feature unlearning persists under stochastic gradient descent and seeks to rigorously identify the geometric conditions—expressed in terms of the data nonlinearity and initialization—that determine whether a network retains or loses its learned features over long training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261817/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a two-layer neural network (width m, first-layer W ∈ R^{d×m}, second-layer a ∈ R^m) trained by one-pass online SGD on data drawn from a single-index teacher model with Gaussian inputs (x ~ N(0, I_d), y = σ_*(⟨w_*, x⟩/√d) + ε). It produces a deterministic 2D ODE for the alignment R_τ and second-layer scale a_τ, from which a critical manifold in the R-a plane, explicit unlearning conditions parameterized by Hermite coefficients, and power-law scaling exponents for the decay of alignment are derived. The analysis operates in the joint limit n,d→∞ (with n/d→δ) followed by m→∞, yielding population-level dynamics rather than finite-sample overfitting.

## Method

The core technique is a two-stage reduction: Tensor Programs are first used to derive a closed-form ODE for macroscopic variables (R, a) in the infinite-width limit, and a singular perturbation ansatz then formalizes the numerically observed fast-slow separation where R evolves rapidly while a is nearly frozen. The fast dynamics collapse trajectories onto a critical manifold S parameterized by a = h(R) = 2S'(R)/T'(R), and the subsequent slow flow along S is governed by the sign of α(R) = S(R)T'(R) − 2S'(R)T(R), which encodes the interplay between teacher and student nonlinearities. Under explicit conditions on the Hermite coefficients (Assumption 6) and the initialization scale (Assumption 7), the slow flow drives R→0 and a→∞, constituting feature unlearning, with scaling laws R = Θ(τ^{−1/(2k₁)}) or Θ(τ^{−1/(k₀+1)}) depending on the relative polynomial degrees. A complementary nullcline-based proof (Theorem 4) establishes the same unlearning result directly on the original ODE without relying on the fast-slow ansatz.

## Results

The paper proves that under explicit conditions on the Hermite coefficients of the teacher link and student activation (Assumptions 6–7), the alignment R_{τ_s} → 0 and the second-layer scale a_{τ_s} → ∞ as τ_s → ∞, with the product R_{τ_s}·a_{τ_s} → c_{★,1}/c_1 (Theorem 2). The scaling law (Theorem 3) shows R decays as Θ(τ_s^{-1/(2k_1)}) in case (i) and Θ(τ_s^{-1/(k_0+1)}) in case (ii), where k_0 and k_1 are the lowest-order nonlinearities present in the teacher-student interaction and the student activation, respectively. Numerical simulations confirm the predicted power-law exponents: log-log tail slopes of R_τ and a_τ approach ±1/4 (case i) and ±1/3 (case ii). A direct nullcline analysis (Theorem 4) recovers the same unlearning conclusion for the original ODE without the fast-slow ansatz, and real-network SGD experiments (m = 500–1000, n = d = 10⁴) show the predicted fast-slow alignment decay persists at finite width.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper without traditional baseline comparisons. The closest comparison is with Montanari & Urbani (2025), who identified feature unlearning under full-batch gradient flow in the infinite-width limit. The present work extends that analysis to discrete-time online SGD with a large-batch limit, showing the same fast-slow mechanism and critical-manifold structure persist. The key distinction is interpretive: Montanari & Urbani frame unlearning as a finite-sample overfitting effect, whereas this paper shows it arises from population-level geometry of the ODE dynamics (no training-test gap is involved). The paper also contrasts with Berthier et al. (2025), who assume the second-layer weights evolve on a faster timescale; here the first-layer alignment is the fast variable. No quantitative head-to-head benchmark is provided, as the contribution is a rigorous derivation of conditions and scaling laws rather than an empirical method comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the feature unlearning analysis of Montanari & Urbani (2025) from continuous-time gradient flow to discrete-time stochastic gradient descent, using the Tensor Programs framework (Yang, 2019a;b; 2020a; Yang & Hu, 2021) to derive a closed-form ODE for macroscopic variables in the infinite-width limit. A key conceptual departure is the reinterpretation of unlearning: while Montanari & Urbani attribute it to dataset-dependent overfitting in finite-sample settings, this paper shows it arises from population-level geometry of the dynamics, specifically the slow drift along a critical manifold in the alignment–second-layer-weight space. The singular perturbation / fast-slow decomposition (Fenichel, 1979; Hek, 2010) is borrowed from applied mathematics and adapted here, following a similar approach in Berthier et al. (2025) and Nishiyama & Imaizumi (2025) for other network architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that feature unlearning is a population-level phenomenon—occurring even without finite-dataset memorization—reframes the mechanism as an intrinsic property of the training dynamics rather than a statistical artifact, which has implications for understanding long-training behavior in practice. The derived scaling laws (Theorem 3) and explicit conditions (Assumptions 6–7) give quantitative predictions about when and how fast alignment decays, which could guide practitioners on initialization choices (e.g., larger initial second-layer scale mitigates unlearning). A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that the analysis is restricted to two-layer networks with single-index teachers and symmetric initialization; connecting the population-level unlearning to the finite-sample overfitting picture of Montanari & Urbani, and extending to deeper or multi-index settings, remain open.
