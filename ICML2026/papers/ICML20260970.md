## Problem

Monolithic models for unified Fake Image Detection (FID) across four subdomains (Deepfake, AIGC, IMDL, Doc) consistently underperform because heterogeneous, domain-specific artifacts collapse into an overlapping low-dimensional representation space when forced through a shared backbone. The paper identifies this as the 'Ji-Zhe phenomenon'—artifacts are highly distinct and non-transferable across subdomains—making the core challenge a 'unified-yet-discriminative' reconstruction of the artifact feature space that no prior method has addressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260970/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image (no domain label, no auxiliary input) and outputs a binary real/fake prediction at the image level. Evaluation is organized by 98 fine-grained faking types across 15 primary types, with a strict split of 26 types for training and 72 held-out types for testing to measure generalization to unseen faking methods. Although the dataset retains pixel-level masks for IMDL and Doc, SICA itself is an image-level classifier rather than a pixel-level localizer.

## Method

SICA freezes a CLIP ViT-L/14 backbone to provide a stable semantic manifold where inputs are naturally clustered by subdomain (faces, documents, natural scenes), then injects LoRA low-rank updates (r=8, α=16) into self-attention layers to co-adapt semantics and artifacts. The key mechanism is that the low-rank constraint selectively bridges the distribution gap between the frozen semantic reference and training data while preserving the manifold structure, preventing the feature space collapse that full fine-tuning causes. Spectral analysis via SVD confirms that LoRA updates allocate significantly more energy outside the principal semantic subspace (higher outside-energy ratio, lower cosine similarity) compared to full fine-tuning, reducing semantic shortcut risk while preserving discriminative artifact features. The result is a near-orthogonal reconstruction of the artifact feature space where each subdomain's artifacts occupy distinct subspaces within a unified representation.

## Results

On the OpenMMSec benchmark (330K+ images, 98 fine-grained faking types, 4 domains), SICA achieves an overall macro-average accuracy of 85.4%, outperforming all 15 baselines. Domain-level accuracies are 88.4% (Deepfake), 94.0% (AIGC), 85.3% (IMDL), and 73.8% (Doc). SICA also leads on AUC (87.5%), AP (79.0%), and F1 (69.7%) across all four evaluation metrics, with the largest margins over the next-best method (Effort, 80.1% ACC) in the AIGC domain (+12.1 points).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SICA outperforms all 15 baselines on overall accuracy (85.4% vs. 80.4% for the next-best SegFormer), achieving the best result in three of four domains and second-best on Doc (73.8% vs. CLIP-FFT's 73.9%). The largest margin is in AIGC detection, where SICA reaches 94.0% accuracy versus FFDN's 92.4% and Effort's 81.9%, a 12.1-point gap over Effort. In Deepfake, SICA (88.4%) surpasses the strongest domain-specific detector Effort (85.0%) by 3.4 points, while in IMDL it leads Effort (83.7%) by 1.6 points. General-purpose backbones (Swin, ConvNeXt, SegFormer) cluster around 79–80% overall, confirming the paper's claim that domain-agnostic architectures suffer from feature space collapse. The CLIP-FFT ablation (80.2%) shows that full fine-tuning of the CLIP backbone without low-rank constraints underperforms SICA by 5.2 points, validating the constrained adaptation design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against two prior FID paradigms: model ensembling (Huang et al., 2025), which suffers from the 'barrel effect' of routing bottlenecks, and naive monolithic training on aggregated subdomain data, which the authors diagnose as failing due to artifact feature space collapse (the 'Ji-Zhe phenomenon'). Methodologically, SICA builds on CLIP's pretrained ViT as a frozen semantic backbone and LoRA for parameter-efficient adaptation, but departs from Effort (Yan et al., 2024a) by replacing rigid SVD-based orthogonal decomposition with a softer low-rank constraint that co-adapts semantics and artifacts. The key conceptual departure is repurposing high-level semantics—long dismissed as inferior features in every FID subdomain—as a structural inductive bias rather than a direct input feature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SICA is the first monolithic FID model to simultaneously outperform 15 baselines across all four domains, and OpenMMSec (330K+ samples, 98 faking types) is the first comprehensive cross-domain FID benchmark, both of which will likely become standard references. However, the Doc domain remains the weakest (73.8% ACC), the model is image-level only (no pixel-level localization), and performance degrades on semantic contexts poorly covered by CLIP's pretraining—suggesting the approach is bounded by the quality of the frozen semantic manifold. Open questions include whether stronger or domain-adaptive semantic backbones can close the Doc gap, whether the paradigm extends to video forensics, and whether the near-orthogonal reconstruction property holds under adversarial or distribution-shift conditions.
