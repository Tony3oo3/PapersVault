## Problem

Existing unlearnable example (UE) schemes suffer from two critical limitations that undermine their robustness: heuristic shortcut patterns that are easily defeated by adaptive defenses, and single-domain (spatial) perturbation optimization that confines perturbations to a limited feature space, making them vulnerable to noise-suppressing defenses like ISS-J and ECLIPSE. This paper asks how to design UEs that remain effective against the full SOTA defense toolbox.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264885/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes clean RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10, 224×224 for ImageNet-100) with their ground-truth class labels and produces unlearnable examples by combining a per-sample spatial perturbation (ℓ∞-bounded, ε=8/255) with a class-wise color perturbation (distinct luminance offsets applied to each RGB channel). The spatial perturbation is unique per sample, while the color perturbation is shared across all samples within the same class, creating a class-wise offset structure. A gallery of 5 pre-trained ResNet18 checkpoints (from different training epochs) guides the optimization.

## Method

DUNE decomposes the joint dual-domain unlearnable objective into two independent sub-optimizations: a spatial branch that uses T-step PGD to push per-sample features toward shift-induced labels (y* = (y+Δy) mod k) under an ℓ∞ constraint, and a color branch that applies class-wise luminance shifts to the DC component of each RGB channel, optimized via gradient-free particle swarm optimization (PSO) with PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS perceptual constraints. The key design insight is that spatial perturbations concentrate energy in high-frequency AC components while color perturbations target the low-frequency DC component, creating an orthogonal dual-domain structure that forms a coupled cross-domain bias rather than a simple additive combination. An unlearnability-enhancing ensemble module further aggregates gradients (spatial branch) or losses (color branch) across M pre-trained models, smoothing the loss landscape and preserving only cross-model shortcut directions. This ensemble mechanism reduces model-specific biases, yielding perturbations that are more stable against diverse defense strategies.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet18, DUNE achieves the lowest average test accuracy of 23.29% across 7 defenses, substantially outperforming the next-best baselines CUDA (39.58%) and SEM (40.90%). Without any defense, DUNE reduces test accuracy to 13.26% (ResNet18) and 13.23% (VGG19), approaching random-guessing level. On ImageNet-100, DUNE achieves an average test accuracy of 16.67% versus 31.03% (EM), 34.14% (LSP), and 31.50% (REM). The highest accuracy DUNE suffers under any single defense is 57.49% (ECLIPSE), still well below the 80–90% of clean-trained models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DUNE achieves the lowest average test accuracy (23.29% ResNet18, 22.77% VGG19) across all 7 defenses, outperforming the strongest single-domain baselines CUDA (39.58%/38.53%) and SEM (40.90%/55.63%) by 16–33 percentage points on average. Under adversarial training (AT), DUNE (24.96%) beats the next-best OPS (40.28%) by 15.3 points; under ISS-J (high-frequency squeezing), DUNE (28.88%) beats CUDA (43.31%) by 14.4 points. However, DUNE is outperformed by CUDA under ECLIPSE (57.49% vs. 34.18%) and is nearly tied with GUE under COIN (19.21% vs. 18.82%), indicating that diffusion-based purification and random-matrix defenses remain partially effective. On ImageNet-100, DUNE (16.67% AVG) dominates EM (31.03%), LSP (34.14%), and REM (31.50%) by 14–17 points. The ablation confirms that removing the ensemble module (UEE) degrades average accuracy by 13.74% on ResNet18, while removing either the spatial or color branch alone yields 31–57% average accuracy, validating the dual-branch design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unlearnable examples (UE) literature by departing from the single-domain (typically spatial ℓ_p-norm) perturbation paradigm that characterizes prior methods like EM, REM, LSP, and SEP. DUNE introduces a dual-branch design that simultaneously optimizes spatial perturbations (via PGD toward shift-induced labels) and color perturbations (via gradient-free PSO targeting the DC/luminance component), exploiting the orthogonality between AC and DC image components to increase perturbation diversity. The ensemble module, which aggregates gradients from multiple pre-trained checkpoints during optimization, repurposes the self-ensemble idea from SEP (Chen et al., 2023) but applies it to the perturbation generation process rather than to the training process, and the class-wise shift-induced label mapping generalizes the error-minimization objective of EM (Huang et al., 2021) into a structured inter-class displacement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DUNE addresses a practical gap in data protection: existing UEs are defeated by mainstream defenses (ECLIPSE, ISS-J, COIN), rendering them unreliable for real-world data publishers. The dual-domain + ensemble design consistently maintains test accuracy below 60% under all seven evaluated defenses, whereas most baselines exceed 80%, suggesting a meaningful step toward deployable data-protection mechanisms. However, the paper acknowledges that adaptive defenses with full knowledge of the spatial-color decomposition can partially recover accuracy (up to ~68% under joint normalization + ISS-J), and the method is currently limited to image classification. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-modal or 3D data, whether the PSO-based color optimization scales to high-resolution images, and whether the ensemble overhead becomes prohibitive for very large model galleries.
