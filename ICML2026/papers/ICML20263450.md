## Problem

Diffusion LLMs (dLLMs) are widely assumed to benefit from arbitrary-order token generation for reasoning, and existing RL methods invest considerable complexity (combinatorial trajectory handling, intractable likelihood approximations) to preserve this flexibility. This paper shows that for general reasoning tasks (math, code), arbitrary order actually narrows the reasoning potential by causing 'entropy degradation' at logical fork tokens, making the elaborate diffusion-specific RL adaptations unnecessary and even counterproductive.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text query (a math problem or code prompt) and produces a generated text sequence (reasoning chain or Python code solution) of up to 256 tokens. During RL training, generation is strictly left-to-right (one token per step), but at inference the model retains full compatibility with parallel decoding via semi-autoregressive blocks (e.g., 32 tokens per step) or training-free samplers like the EB-sampler, decoupling the AR training scaffold from the parallel inference capability.

## Method

JustGRPO defines a surrogate autoregressive policy π_θ^AR on top of the dLLM backbone by constructing input states where all past tokens are observed and all future tokens are [MASK], then extracting the next-token logits at position k as the AR policy (Eq. 6). This converts the intractable marginalization over O(N!) denoising trajectories into an exactly computable product of per-token probabilities, enabling direct application of standard GRPO (clipped surrogate + KL penalty) without any diffusion-specific modifications. The AR constraint is applied exclusively during RL training as an exploration scaffold; no causal masking or architectural changes are imposed, so the model's bidirectional attention and discrete diffusion formulation remain intact. A lightweight variant, JustGRPO-Fast, further accelerates training by computing importance ratios only at the top-25% highest-entropy positions, exploiting the finding that reasoning is steered by a sparse set of forking tokens.

## Results

On LLaDA-Instruct with generation length 256, JustGRPO achieves 89.1% on GSM8K, 45.1% on MATH-500, 49.4% on HumanEval, and 52.4% on MBPP, outperforming all diffusion-specific RL baselines on mathematical reasoning and coding. In the unified-protocol reproduction (Table 2), JustGRPO leads by +2.2/+3.3 points over SPG* on GSM8K/MATH-500 and by +4.7/+7.8 points over ESPO* on HumanEval/MBPP. The method also preserves parallel decoding: accuracy gains over the base model grow with parallelism (e.g., +10.6% at 1 token/step to +25.5% at ~5 tokens/step on MBPP).

## Baselines & Comparisons

JustGRPO outperforms all eight diffusion-specific RL baselines on GSM8K (89.1% vs. best prior 88.1% by LLaDOU) and MATH-500 (45.1% vs. best prior 41.1% by LLaDOU), with margins of +3.0 and +4.0 points respectively. On coding, JustGRPO leads ESPO by +7.3 points on HumanEval (49.4% vs. 42.1%) and by +7.8 points on MBPP (52.4% vs. 44.6%), and surpasses GDPO by +9.8 and +1.8 points on the same benchmarks. The only method that beats JustGRPO on HumanEval is LLaDOU (59.1%), which introduces an auxiliary trainable module and thus modifies the base architecture. Under the unified reproduction protocol (Table 2), where d1, ESPO, and SPG are re-run with identical settings (full fine-tuning, one token per step, length 256), JustGRPO maintains clear margins of +2.2 to +7.8 points across all four benchmarks, confirming that the gains are not an artifact of heterogeneous evaluation settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of RL-for-dLLM work (d1, LLaDOU, ESPO, SPG, GDPO) that treats arbitrary-order generation as essential and builds increasingly complex machinery—combinatorial trajectory handling, ELBO surrogates, auxiliary position-selection policies—to preserve it. Instead, it repurposes the standard GRPO objective (Shao et al., 2024) by defining a surrogate autoregressive policy on top of the dLLM backbone, eliminating all diffusion-specific RL adaptations. The mechanistic explanation draws on the 'forking token' / high-entropy branching literature (Wang et al., 2025; Cheng et al., 2026; Huang et al., 2025a) to argue that adaptive unmasking suppresses the very uncertainty that exploration requires.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that arbitrary-order generation *reduces* Pass@k reasoning potential inverts a core design assumption of the dLLM community and suggests that an entire subfield of diffusion-specific RL formulations may be solving the wrong problem for general reasoning. Practically, JustGRPO offers a drop-in, architecture-agnostic recipe (standard GRPO, no causal masking, no auxiliary modules) that preserves parallel decoding at inference, lowering the barrier to RL training for dLLMs. Open questions include whether the result extends to tasks where non-sequential generation genuinely helps (constraint satisfaction, planning), to larger model scales, and to dLLM architectures with different attention patterns; the paper also notes that the AR scaffold during training and parallel decoding at inference are decoupled, but the interaction between the two regimes under prolonged training or multi-task settings remains unexplored.
