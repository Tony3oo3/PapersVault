## Problem

Depth pruning of Vision Transformers—removing entire layers—delivers higher speedups than width pruning but suffers severe accuracy degradation that existing methods cannot recover. The paper identifies the root cause as the neglect of heterogeneity between attention layers and GELU activation layers, which exhibit fundamentally different gradient magnitudes during backpropagation and asymmetric accuracy-recovery dynamics after pruning, making conventional cross-type importance metrics and short-sighted accuracy-based criteria ineffective.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264268/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on pre-trained ViTs (DeiT-S/B, Swin-S/B, DINO-V2-Giant) consuming standard 224×224 RGB images and producing task-specific outputs: 1000-class labels on ImageNet-1K, 100-class labels on CIFAR-100, bounding boxes on COCO2017, and 150-class segmentation masks on ADE20K. Unusually, the pruning target is not just attention blocks but also the GELU nonlinearity inside FFN sub-layers, after which adjacent linear layers are algebraically merged (D→4D→D collapses to D→D), so the pruned model's output is mathematically identical to the pre-merge pruned model.

## Method

HetDPT is a two-stage heterogeneous depth pruning pipeline. Stage 1 first allocates the pruning budget between attention and activation layers using a Model Accuracy Predictor (MAP)—a low-degree bivariate polynomial fitted via lightweight iterative prune-finetune-evaluate data collection and Leave-2-out cross-validation—then selects specific layers within each homogeneous type using learnable importance parameters trained with gradients, avoiding cross-type comparison. Stage 2 fine-tunes the pruned model (optionally with self-distillation from the original ViT) and merges adjacent linear layers that lost their intervening GELU, reducing depth while preserving exact dimension alignment. The theoretical analysis proves existence of an optimal configuration, uniform polynomial approximability of the accuracy surface via Stone-Weierstrass, and consistency of MAP under constant bias from fast fine-tuning. HetDPT+ further combines the depth-pruned model with isomorphic width pruning for extreme compression, achieving up to 9.19× speedup on DeiT-B.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, HetDPT achieves 81.8% top-1 accuracy with a 1.58× speedup on DeiT-B (10 layers pruned, 10.5G MACs) and 79.7% with 1.39× speedup on DeiT-S (8 layers pruned, 3.0G MACs), both matching or exceeding the dense baseline accuracy. The extended HetDPT+ pipeline reaches 5.19× speedup at 2.4G MACs with 81.4% accuracy and 9.19× speedup at 1.1G MACs with 77.4% accuracy, setting new state-of-the-art in extreme ViT compression. On COCO2017, HetDPT (10 layers) achieves 32.4% mAP (0.4% below the 32.8% DeiT-B baseline), and on ADE20K it reaches 46.4% mIoU with 8 layers pruned (0.6% below baseline).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HetDPT consistently outperforms the depth-pruning baseline NOSE across all architectures: on DeiT-B, HetDPT (10 layers) achieves 81.8% at 1.58× speedup versus NOSE (6 layers) at 81.5% and 1.24×; on DeiT-S, HetDPT (8 layers) reaches 79.7% at 1.39× versus NOSE (5 layers) at 79.6% and 1.22×. Against the joint depth-width pruning method DW-Pruning (Yu et al., 2022), HetDPT wins on every backbone, e.g., 81.8% at 1.58× on DeiT-B versus DW-Pruning's 80.9% at 1.40×. For extreme compression, HetDPT+ surpasses Isomorphic Pruning (Fang et al., 2024) at every compression level: 81.4% at 5.19× (2.4G) versus 81.1% at 4.24× (2.6G), and 77.4% at 9.19× (1.1G) versus 77.5% at 8.61× (1.2G). HetDPT also outperforms NViT (Yang et al., 2023) in the moderate-compression regime (82.5% at 3.73× vs. 82.2% at 3.23×). Among token-pruning/merging baselines (DynamicViT, Evo-ViT, EViT, TPS, ToMe, GTP-ViT), HetDPT (10 layers) on DeiT-B achieves 81.8% at 1.58×, exceeding all of them in both accuracy and speedup (e.g., ToMe: 80.6% at 1.28×; GTP-ViT: 81.5% at 1.29×). On Swin-B, HetDPT (82.9%, 1.60×) edges out SAViT (82.6%, 1.53×) in both accuracy and throughput.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends ViT depth pruning (previously studied by NOSE, which prunes only attention layers using Transfer Entropy) by introducing the pruning of GELU activation function layers as a second, heterogeneous pruning target. The key conceptual departure is identifying 'heterogeneity'—specifically gradient disparity and recovery asymmetry between attention and activation layers—as the root cause of depth pruning's accuracy collapse, rather than the coarse-granularity explanation offered by prior work. The HetDPT+ variant builds on Isomorphic Pruning's width-pruning pipeline, and the layer-merging mechanism draws a parallel to DepthShrinker's CNN activation removal, though the authors explicitly differentiate their approach by formulating a mixed heterogeneous allocation problem unique to ViTs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The heterogeneity insight (gradient disparity + recovery asymmetry) provides a principled explanation for why naive depth pruning fails and guides the design of the MAP-based budget allocation, which is a reusable framework for any future heterogeneous pruning problem. Practically, HetDPT+ pushes extreme ViT compression to 9.19× speedup with near-lossless accuracy, making high-capacity ViTs deployable on resource-constrained hardware. A stated limitation is that the MAP polynomial must be refit per architecture family, and extending the approach to language models or other modalities remains open. The orthogonality results with token pruning/merging suggest the method can be stacked with other compression axes, but the interaction with more aggressive token reduction at very high sparsity is not fully explored.
