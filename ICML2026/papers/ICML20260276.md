## Problem

Despite the widespread deployment of Transformer models, it was unclear whether the parameters of softmax-attention layers could be provably recovered from black-box access to model outputs (value queries). Prior model-extraction theory focused on feedforward and ReLU networks, leaving parameter-recovery guarantees for attention mechanisms entirely unexplored. This paper initiates a systematic study of learnability for attention-based sequence models under adaptive query access.

## Input / Output

The learner adaptively queries a value oracle VQ(X) = f_{W*,v*}(X) with any sequence X ∈ R^{N×d} of variable length N ≥ 1 and receives the exact (or approximate) scalar output. The target is to recover the attention score matrix W* ∈ R^{d×d} and value vector v* ∈ R^{d} exactly or to ε-accuracy. A key structural detail is that the last token in the sequence acts as the query token, and the softmax produces attention weights over all positions, so the learner exploits length-1 and length-2 sequences as specially designed probes.

## Method

The central insight is that length-2 sequences reduce the N-way softmax to a two-way sigmoid σ(uᵀw_j), which is invertible, turning each oracle response into a linear equation in the unknown column of W*. For single-head attention, d basis-vector queries recover v*, and d² two-row probes (one per matrix entry) recover W* exactly in O(d²) queries. In the low-rank regime (rank(W*) ≤ r ≪ d), the same probes yield rank-one measurements ⟨abᵀ, W*⟩, and nuclear-norm minimization (a convex program from the ROP matrix-sensing framework) recovers W* in O(rd) queries. For noisy oracles, probes are scaled so that the sigmoid argument stays in a bounded interval where σ⁻¹ is Lipschitz, yielding polynomial-query ε-approximation under norm and margin conditions. For multi-head attention, the paper proves non-identifiability (distinct parameter sets induce identical input-output maps) and shows that an orthogonal-subspace assumption on head parameters restores identifiability up to permutation.

## Results

The paper establishes that single-head attention parameters (W*, v*) are exactly recoverable with O(d²) value queries (Theorem 4.1), and in the low-rank regime where rank(W*) ≤ r ≪ d, recovery is possible with O(rd) queries via compressed sensing (Theorem 5.1). Under additive noise tolerance τ = O(min{μ, ε_v/√d, με_W/(W²d)}), parameters are recoverable to ε accuracy with the same O(d²) query complexity (Theorem 6.1). For multi-head attention, the paper proves a negative result: parameters are not identifiable from value queries in general (Proposition 7.1), as distinct parameter settings can induce identical input-output maps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or comparison tables. The authors position their results against prior work qualitatively: they note that learning a one-hidden-layer ReLU MLP with queries (Chen et al., 2021) is substantially more challenging and typically requires distribution-dependent assumptions, whereas their single-head attention recovery is distribution-free and achieves exact parameter identification. They also contrast with passive learning from random examples (Wang et al., 2024; Tian et al., 2023; Arnaboldi et al., 2025), which requires nontrivial data model assumptions and non-convex optimization, versus their query-based approach which is elementary and polynomial-time. The low-rank result builds directly on the ROP matrix sensing framework of Cai & Zhang (2015), adapting it to the attention-specific probe structure. No head-to-head numerical comparisons are provided.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of classical query-based learning theory (Angluin, 1988) and the model-extraction/security literature (Tramer et al., 2016; Jagielski et al., 2020), but departs from both by targeting softmax attention specifically. Prior provable guarantees for parameter recovery with queries have been established for ReLU feedforward networks (Chen et al., 2021; Milli et al., 2019; Daniely & Granot, 2023), while the attention-learning literature has focused almost exclusively on passive learning from random examples (Wang et al., 2024; Tian et al., 2023). The low-rank recovery section directly repurposes the ROP matrix-sensing framework of Cai & Zhang (2015), translating attention probes into rank-one linear measurements of the weight matrix.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide provable parameter-recovery guarantees for softmax attention from black-box queries, establishing that single-head attention is surprisingly tractable (O(d²) queries) while multi-head attention is fundamentally non-identifiable without structural assumptions. The security implications are direct: if a model exposes an inference API, its attention weights may be recoverable with polynomially many queries. Key open questions include whether multi-head attention is learnable under orthogonal-subspace or other structural conditions, whether functional equivalence (as opposed to parameter recovery) is achievable, and whether distribution-dependent PAC guarantees can be established for the multi-head case.
