## Problem

Existing differentially private synthetic data generation methods incorporate public data only indirectly—through pre-trained models or post-processing—while the underlying DP mechanism (Gaussian or Laplace noise) remains domain-agnostic, perturbing the private distribution in arbitrary directions on the probability simplex. This paper asks whether public data can be used directly to design the noise distribution itself, yielding a domain-aware DP mechanism with a fundamentally better privacy-utility tradeoff.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264555/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The mechanism takes a private dataset summarized as a normalized histogram over d categories, a public dataset from the same domain (also a normalized histogram), privacy parameters (ε, δ), a target sample size m, and a proximity parameter γ defining a TV ball around the public distribution. It outputs m i.i.d. synthetic samples drawn from a mixed distribution P_syn = (1−β*)P̄_prv + β*P*_nse. Crucially, the parameters β* and P*_nse are pre-computed from public data alone and are independent of the private dataset; the mechanism releases only m samples rather than the full perturbed histogram, exploiting the 2-point sparsity of neighboring histograms for tighter privacy accounting.

## Method

PUBMIX constructs the synthetic distribution via linear mixing P_syn = (1−β)P̄_prv + βP_nse, where the noise distribution P_nse is designed from public data rather than being a fixed Gaussian or Laplace. The key theoretical result shows that the asymptotically optimal P_nse is a floor-raised version of the public distribution P̄_pub: all probability masses below a threshold t are raised to t, with the added mass redistributed from larger entries, balancing two competing objectives—staying close to P̄_pub to minimize worst-case TV distance while maximizing the minimum probability mass to reduce the required mixing weight β_min. Two computable floor-raising procedures are provided: Water-Filling (uniformly leveling excess mass) and Maximum-Invariance Floor Raising (concentrating removal on a subset to preserve as many coordinates unchanged as possible). The minimum mixing weight β_min depends on P_nse only through its two smallest probability masses, and the privacy constraint is characterized via the hockey-stick divergence between m-fold product distributions, leveraging the fact that neighboring histograms differ in at most two coordinates. PUBMIX serves as a drop-in replacement for the DP histogram-sampling subroutine in existing pipelines (e.g., Private Evolution), requiring no changes to the surrounding architecture.

## Results

On the Person Activity tabular dataset, PUBMIX (integrated into the TABPE pipeline) achieves 66.40% downstream classification accuracy at ε=1, 66.47% at ε=2, and 66.85% at ε=4, outperforming the strongest baseline TABPE by +5.6, +3.3, and +2.6 points respectively. On the Yelp Reviews text dataset, PUBMIX reduces rating prediction RMSE from 0.90 to 0.81 at ε=1 (vs. PE baseline) and improves category classification accuracy from 69.93% to 72.35% at ε=4. On the ACS PUMS dataset, PUBMIX maintains lower TV distance between the private and synthetic distributions than both the Gaussian mechanism and LINMIX (no public data) as the number of synthetic samples m increases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PUBMIX consistently outperforms all eight tabular baselines on Person Activity across all privacy budgets. The largest gains are over GEM (+38.2 pts at ε=1) and PrivSyn (+39.0 pts at ε=1), which use domain-agnostic noise mechanisms. Against the strongest public-data-aware baselines, PUBMIX beats JAM-PGM by +10.4 pts and PMWpub by +17.2 pts at ε=1, showing that incorporating public data directly into the noise distribution (rather than only in pre/post-processing) yields substantially better utility. Against the closest baseline TABPE (which uses Gaussian-noised histograms within a Private Evolution pipeline), PUBMIX gains +5.6 pts at ε=1, +3.3 at ε=2, and +2.6 at ε=4, with the advantage growing under stricter privacy. On Yelp text synthesis, PUBMIX beats the PE baseline on rating RMSE at all ε (0.81 vs. 0.90 at ε=1) and on category accuracy at ε=2 and ε=4 (71.57% vs. 70.69% and 72.35% vs. 69.93%), though it slightly trails PE on category accuracy at ε=1 (71.01% vs. 71.67%). On the PUMS illustration, PUBMIX achieves lower TV distance than both the Gaussian mechanism and LINMIX (no public data), with the gap widening as m increases, confirming the theoretical prediction that domain-aware noise directions preserve utility better under sample-dependent privacy accounting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard paradigm in DP synthetic data generation where public data is used only indirectly (via pre-trained models, statistic selection, or post-processing) while the underlying DP mechanism remains domain-agnostic (Gaussian or Laplace noise). It extends the DP multi-sampling setting of Cheu & Nayak (2025) by incorporating public data directly into the noise distribution design, and is conceptually related to private prediction methods (Flemings et al. 2024; Ginart et al. 2022) that also use linear mixing with public distributions, but differs by jointly optimizing both the mixing weight and the noise distribution rather than fixing the public distribution as noise. The resulting PUBMIX mechanism is positioned as a drop-in replacement for the DP histogram-sampling subroutine in existing pipelines like Private Evolution (Xie et al. 2024) and TABPE (Tran et al. 2026).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled theoretical framework for designing domain-aware DP mechanisms, showing that the asymptotically optimal noise distribution is a floor-raised version of the public distribution—a clean characterization that connects information-theoretic privacy accounting to a concrete, computable construction. Practically, PUBMIX offers a modular upgrade for histogram-based synthesis pipelines without requiring architectural changes. Key limitations include restriction to linear mixing mechanisms, histogram-based estimators, and the requirement for same-domain public data; extending to complex distribution estimators (e.g., Gaussian mixtures, deep generative models) and to settings where public data is only partially overlapping remains open.
