## Problem

Deep neural networks consistently underperform tree-based models on tabular data, and while prior work identified robustness to irrelevant features, axis alignment, and localized irregularities as key inductive biases, the paper argues that feature heterogeneity—the mixing of raw measurements with derived metrics at different abstraction levels—is a fourth, overlooked factor that standard architectures fail to handle. Additionally, existing sparse neural methods like LassoNet suffer from unstable joint optimization that undermines their feature-selection guidance, making them unreliable alternatives to GBDTs.

## Input / Output

The model consumes tabular data where numerical features are encoded via Piecewise Linear Encoding (PLE) into fixed bin coordinates and categorical features via one-hot encoding, each processed by an independent per-feature ResNet block. It produces a class label or continuous prediction alongside interpretable feature importance scores (the Lasso skip weights β), which the paper validates achieve AUC-ROC of 0.84–0.92 in distinguishing true features from injected noise. Training uses mini-batch SGD with O(batch) memory and O(d) feature scaling, avoiding the O(n²) context-length bottleneck of foundation models.

## Method

LassoFlexNet replaces LassoNet's raw-input linear skip with a Tied Group Lasso operating on learned nonlinear Per-Feature Embeddings: a single scalar β_i scores the mean-pooled embedding of feature i while simultaneously bounding the entire outgoing weight block from that feature's non-pooled embedding into the first MLP-Mixer feature-mixing layer, enabling selection on rich nonlinear representations. A soft curriculum scalar τ scales the Mixer output to prevent the expressive module from dominating early training and bypassing feature selection. For optimization, the paper replaces LassoNet's joint Hier-Prox with Seq-Hier-Prox-Adam-EMA, which sequentially updates Lasso gates first (via soft-thresholding with per-coordinate Adam rates) before enforcing the induced ℓ∞ constraint on first-layer weights, with exponential moving averages stabilizing the constraint-boundary decisions. Theoretically, the paper proves that PLE breaks the rotational invariance of neural networks and provides a fixed-knot linear spline basis for localized univariate irregularities, while stochastic dynamics analyses identify a sign-flip jump discontinuity in the original Hier-Prox that the sequential, EMA-smoothed design mitigates.

## Results

Across 52 datasets spanning three benchmarks, LassoFlexNet achieves the best performance in 15 of 35 TabZilla datasets (vs. 0 for XGBoost, 4 for CatBoost, 7 for LightGBM, 7 for TabM) and 5 of 8 TabRed tasks. On TabRed, it outperforms TabPFN and TabICL on 5 of 8 datasets (homesite-insurance AUC 0.9605 vs. 0.9207; cooking-time RMSE 0.4658 vs. 0.4673; weather RMSE 1.5591 vs. 1.6006) using only mini-batch training with O(batch) memory. On the California Housing ablation, LassoFlexNet achieves RMSE 0.4047 vs. 0.5263 for LassoNet, a ~23% relative improvement. The Friedman omnibus test confirms statistical significance (χ²=72.84, p=2.62e-14), with LassoFlexNet holding the best average rank (2.161).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GBDT baselines, LassoFlexNet wins 15 of 35 TabZilla datasets (vs. 0 for XGBoost, 4 for CatBoost, 7 for LightGBM) and 5 of 8 TabRed tasks, with the largest margins on cooking-time (RMSE 0.4658 vs. CatBoost 0.4718) and weather (1.5591 vs. 1.5922); it trails CatBoost by only 0.0005 RMSE on delivery-eta. Against the deep tabular model TabM, LassoFlexNet achieves substantially lower RMSE on cooking-time (0.4658 vs. 0.5629) and weather (1.5591 vs. 2.2434), while TabM-mini leads on homecredit-default (AUC 0.8413 vs. 0.8365). Against the direct predecessor LassoNet, LassoFlexNet improves California Housing RMSE from 0.5263 to 0.4047 (~23% relative gain) and wins 15 vs. 0 TabZilla datasets, with pairwise Wilcoxon tests confirming significance (p=2.33e-09). Against tabular foundation models, LassoFlexNet outperforms TabPFN on 5 of 8 TabRed datasets (e.g., homesite-insurance AUC 0.9605 vs. 0.9207) and TabICL on the same 5, while TabPFN leads on homecredit-default (AUC 0.9670 vs. 0.8365) and ecom-offers (0.7069 vs. 0.6322). In the feature-selection benchmark, LassoFlexNet (operating blindly without knowing the number of relevant features) outperforms oracle two-stage pipelines that are explicitly given the ground-truth feature count k, achieving top-3 in 8/12 datasets at 50% noise injection.

## Relation to Prior Work

LassoFlexNet is a direct architectural and optimization successor to LassoNet (Lemhadri et al., 2021), which first introduced hierarchical proximal constraints to enforce feature sparsity in neural networks. The key departures are twofold: feature selection is moved from raw inputs to learned nonlinear Per-Feature Embeddings (via PLE + per-feature ResNet blocks), and the joint Hier-Prox update is replaced by a sequential, EMA-stabilized proximal adaptive gradient (Seq-Hier-Prox-Adam-EMA). The interaction module borrows from MLP-Mixer (Tolstikhin et al., 2021) rather than attention-based designs (FT-Transformer, TabNet), and the overall design is motivated by the inductive-bias analysis of Grinsztajn et al. (2022) on why trees outperform deep nets on tabular data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LassoFlexNet offers a practical, scalable alternative to both GBDTs and tabular foundation models: it matches or beats XGBoost/CatBoost/LightGBM across 52 datasets while providing native end-to-end feature interpretability via the Tied Group Lasso, and it remains competitive with TabPFN/TabICL on distribution-shift benchmarks without requiring foundation-model pretraining or O(n²) context-length inference. Key limitations include restriction to numerical or mixed-type features (purely categorical datasets are out of scope), the absence of an end-to-end convergence proof for the full Seq-Hier-Prox-Adam-EMA optimizer, and no wall-clock training-time comparisons against highly optimized GBDT libraries. Open questions include whether the architecture extends to purely categorical settings, whether the PLE knot selection can be made adaptive, and whether the sequential proximal dynamics admit a formal convergence guarantee.
