## Problem

Self-attention's quadratic scaling with sequence length is the dominant bottleneck for long-context LLM inference, and existing sparse attention methods face a persistent trade-off: training-based approaches lack plug-and-play generality, while inference-time heuristics either rely on expensive attention-like proxies or oversimplified, modality-specific assumptions that degrade on complex multi-modal tasks. The paper asks whether a structurally simple, hardware-friendly proxy can generate effective sparse patterns across diverse modalities without model retraining or task-specific tuning.

## Input / Output

UniSparse consumes the standard Q, K, V tensors (each L×d_k) from a pre-trained LLM's attention layer during the prefill phase, along with configurable compression factors (c_q, c_k, c_h) and a Top-P threshold P∈(0,1]. It produces the attention output tensor O∈R^{L×d_k} and a binary block-sparse mask M∈{0,1}^{N×N} (where N=L/S blocks) that restricts which query-key block pairs are computed. The method is a drop-in replacement requiring no architectural changes, operates on sequences up to 256K tokens, and supports causal masking for autoregressive models.

## Method

UniSparse introduces composite tokens—coarse-grained summary vectors obtained by average-pooling consecutive fine-grained tokens along the sequence dimension (and optionally the head dimension)—to construct a drastically compressed proxy space. In this compressed space, attention scores are computed at O(L²hd_k/(c_q·c_k·c_h)) cost, aggregated to block-level importance via summation over compressed token indices, and filtered through a Top-P threshold that retains blocks until a specified fraction P of total attention mass is accumulated. The resulting sparse mask guides standard block-sparse FlashAttention kernels for the final computation. The key theoretical insight (proven in Appendix A) is that block selection is fundamentally a ranking problem: average pooling preserves pre-softmax block rankings exactly by bilinearity, and post-softmax rank inversions are bounded to near-equal-importance blocks whose mean-logit gap is below a small threshold determined by intra-block variance. This makes the proxy modality-agnostic, as spatial pooling is a universal operation applicable to text tokens, video patches, or audio segments alike.

## Results

On RULER (4K–128K tokens), UniSparse-0.95 achieves 92.56 overall accuracy on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (46.33% sparsity) and 84.26 on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (47.25% sparsity), retaining ≥99% of FlashAttention's accuracy while computing less than half of attention blocks. On HELMET, UniSparse-0.95 reaches 56.21 (Llama) and 47.03 (Qwen), and on Video-MME it achieves 65.0/69.7 (without/with subtitles), even surpassing full attention in the subtitle setting. End-to-end, UniSparse delivers up to 2.61× faster attention computation than FlashAttention at 128K tokens, and on LongBench v2 it attains 30.4 overall accuracy, exceeding FlashAttention's 29.8.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniSparse-0.95 outperforms all five sparse baselines on Qwen2.5-7B across RULER (84.26 vs. MInference 83.58, XAttention-0.95 83.63, FlexPrefill-0.99 82.80, SpargeAttention 81.21) and HELMET (47.03 vs. XAttention-0.95 46.69, MInference 45.77, FlexPrefill-0.99 45.55, SpargeAttention 45.76), with the largest margins over SpargeAttention (+3.05 on RULER, +1.27 on HELMET). On Llama-3.1-8B, UniSparse-0.95 leads on HELMET (56.21 vs. FlexPrefill-0.99 56.01, MInference 55.37) and is within 0.03 of FlashAttention on RULER (92.56 vs. 92.87), while MInference edges ahead slightly on RULER-Llama (92.35 vs. 92.44 is actually UniSparse winning by 0.09). On Video-MME, UniSparse-0.95 matches or exceeds FlashAttention (69.7 vs. 69.6 with subtitles; 65.0 vs. 65.3 without) and clearly beats XAttention-0.95 (63.9) and FlexPrefill-0.99 (65.0). On LongBench v2, UniSparse-0.95 achieves the highest overall score (30.4), surpassing FlashAttention (29.8) and MInference (30.0), with the largest gap on the Long subset (29.6 vs. 26.9–27.8 for all other methods). In terms of efficiency, UniSparse-0.95 achieves 2.52× end-to-end attention speedup over FlashAttention at 128K (2.61× with head compression), outperforming XAttention-0.95 and FlexPrefill-0.99 in both selection overhead and total latency.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniSparse sits within the inference-time dynamic sparse attention lineage, sharing the block-sparse mask formulation with MInference, XAttention, FlexPrefill, and SpargeAttention, and building on FlashAttention's tile-based GPU execution. Its conceptual departure is the introduction of composite tokens—coarse-grained summaries formed by average pooling along sequence and head dimensions—as a universal proxy for block importance, replacing either expensive attention-like proxies (MInference's offline search, SeerAttention's learned gating) or oversimplified structural heuristics (XAttention's anti-diagonal scoring, FlexPrefill's last-block probing). The theoretical contribution (exact pre-softmax rank preservation under bilinear compression, bounded post-softmax distortion via Taylor expansion, and MSE-optimality of the centroid) reframes block selection as a ranking problem rather than a scoring problem, providing a principled justification for why a simple pooling operation suffices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UniSparse offers a training-free, plug-and-play acceleration for long-context prefill that generalizes across text and video modalities without task-specific tuning, addressing a practical gap where existing methods either require retraining or rely on modality-specific assumptions. The 2.61× attention speedup with >99% accuracy retention is directly deployable on current GPU hardware via standard block-sparse FlashAttention kernels. Open questions include tight distribution-free worst-case bounds on rank inversion (the current analysis relies on LayerNorm-induced isotropy), whether the approach extends to non-autoregressive or diffusion architectures, and how performance degrades at very short sequences (<16K) where compression overhead is poorly amortized.
