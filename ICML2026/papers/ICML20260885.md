## Problem

Existing VLMs for spatial reasoning treat the model as a passive observer that receives all views simultaneously, which is impractical for real-world embodied scenarios where scenes are large and task-irrelevant. Additionally, reinforcement learning for spatial reasoning relies on sparse 0/1 final-answer rewards, providing no dense feedback for the multi-step intermediate reasoning that spatial tasks demand.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260885/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a spatial reasoning question, a set of up to four cross-view RGB images, and free-form text descriptions of how each view relates to others (e.g., 'image 2 was taken after turning 90° right from image 1'). Rather than consuming all views at once, the model iteratively selects which view to retrieve next. Beyond the final multiple-choice answer, the model produces a dynamic cognitive map (object positions and orientations in a 10×10 top-down grid) and Spatial Assertion Codes—Python expressions with boolean outputs that encode each intermediate spatial claim.

## Method

The paper formulates spatial reasoning as a sequential decision-making problem where a VLM policy iteratively (1) retrieves a relevant view, (2) updates a dynamic cognitive map parameterizing scene layout as object positions and orientations in a unified top-down coordinate system, and (3) finally answers the question. The key innovation is Spatial Assertion Code (SAC): the model generates Python expressions (e.g., `obj1 in obj0.left(view=v4)`) alongside natural-language reasoning, which are executed against the cognitive map to verify intermediate steps and yield dense reward signals. Training uses a two-stage pipeline—supervised fine-tuning on synthetic data covering retrieval, map updating, and SAC generation, followed by GRPO-based reinforcement learning with a composite reward that gates dense intermediate scores (retrieval relatedness, cognitive map correctness, SAC verification) behind final-answer correctness to prevent reward hacking.

## Results

On the MindCube-Tiny benchmark (1,050 QA pairs), the proposed agentic pipeline achieves 80.5% overall accuracy, surpassing the previous best method 3DThinker (75.2%) by 5.3 points. The most striking gain is on the ROTATION subset, where the method reaches 85.0% accuracy versus 55.5% for 3DThinker — a 29.5-point absolute improvement (53.2% relative). On the AMONG subset, the method scores 81.0%, slightly below 3DThinker's 81.8% (−0.8), while on AROUND it edges ahead at 75.6% vs. 75.2%. A secondary evaluation on EmbodiedBench shows the model generalizes to embodied navigation in BASE and VISUAL settings but drops to zero on language-heavy COMMON and COMPLEX tasks without an auxiliary language model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest spatial VLM baseline, 3DThinker (Chen et al., 2026), the proposed method wins on overall accuracy (80.5 vs. 75.2, +5.3) and dramatically on ROTATION (85.0 vs. 55.5, +29.5), but loses slightly on AMONG (81.0 vs. 81.8, −0.8) and matches on AROUND (75.6 vs. 75.2, +0.4). Compared to MindCube (Yin et al., 2025), which uses a static cognitive map under passive perception, the method improves overall accuracy by 9.8 points (80.5 vs. 70.7) and ROTATION by 37.0 points (85.0 vs. 48.0). Against proprietary models, the method substantially outperforms GPT-4o (80.5 vs. 38.81 overall) and Claude-4-Sonnet (80.5 vs. 44.75), demonstrating that task-specific agentic training with dense rewards far exceeds general-purpose VLM prompting. Earlier spatial VLMs — Spatial-MLLM (32.06) and Space-Qwen (33.28) — are far behind, confirming that the combination of active perception, dynamic cognitive maps, and SAC-based dense rewards is the key differentiator rather than any single architectural component.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MindCube line of work (Yin et al., 2025), which introduced a static cognitive map for spatial reasoning under passive perception, by making the cognitive map dynamic and continually updated through an agentic retrieval loop. It also builds on the GRPO reinforcement learning framework (Shao et al., 2024) used in recent RLVR approaches for VLMs, but departs from the sparse 0/1 final-answer reward by introducing Spatial Assertion Codes (SAC) — executable Python expressions that enable programmatic verification of intermediate reasoning steps and thus dense reward signals. The conceptual departure from prior spatial VLMs (3DThinker, Spatial-MLLM, MindCube) is the shift from passive, all-at-once perception to active, iterative view retrieval inspired by pigeon navigation behavior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The active perception paradigm is more aligned with real-world embodied AI where agents cannot perceive entire scenes simultaneously, and the 29.5-point gain on the ROTATION subset (which simulates fragmented egocentric views with minimal overlap) suggests this paradigm shift is critical for navigation-like tasks. The SAC mechanism offers a general recipe for dense rewards in spatial reasoning that avoids the hallucination and overconfidence problems of LLM-as-a-judge. However, evaluation is limited to the synthetic MindCube benchmark, and the EmbodiedBench results reveal a task mismatch (zero on commonsense/complex linguistic subtasks), leaving open whether the framework generalizes to real-world 3D environments with continuous action spaces and multi-agent settings.
