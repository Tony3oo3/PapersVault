## Problem

Standard meta-RL algorithms (MAML, RL², PPO pretraining) assume that observed actions, states, and rewards are not simultaneously influenced by unobserved confounders. When this no-unmeasured-confounding condition is violated—as in environments where hidden variables (e.g., wind) affect both the demonstrator's actions and the resulting transitions—the policy gradient becomes biased and meta-learners fail to generalize, sometimes performing worse than training from scratch. No systematic approach existed for robust meta-RL under unmeasured confounding in sequential decision-making with finite state-action spaces.

## Input / Output

The method consumes purely observational (passive) trajectories collected from a demonstrator who can sense the confounders, across multiple source CMDP environments with discrete finite state and action spaces and a fixed horizon H. It produces a meta-policy parameter initialization θ for a neural network policy; this initialization is not directly deployable but requires a few gradient-descent adaptation steps in the target environment to yield a task-specific policy. The target domain may contain novel elements (e.g., new object colors) not present in the source domain, testing out-of-distribution generalization.

## Method

The core contribution is a counterfactual bootstrap augmentation procedure that replaces biased observational gradients with unbiased interventional ones. Given confounded observations, the method constructs a feasible region of candidate CMDPs using Manski-style partial identification bounds on transition and reward functions (Eqs. 5–8), leveraging the canonical CMDP representation to ensure the latent exogenous space is finite. It then samples counterfactual environments from this posterior, collects experimental trajectories by simulating the current policy in the sampled environment, and uses these trajectories for the standard MAML inner-loop and outer-loop gradient updates (Algorithm 1, CAUSAL-MAML). The key insight is that sampling from the posterior over environments consistent with observations yields a consistent estimate of the optimal meta-initialization in expectation, even though the true dynamics are non-identifiable. Theoretical analysis (Theorem 1) establishes convergence to an ε-approximate first-order stationary point with O(ε⁻⁴) total stochastic gradient evaluations under standard smoothness assumptions on the policy network.

## Results

The paper evaluates CAUSAL-MAML on three Windy Gridworld meta-RL tasks (Pick-Up-Key, Go-To-Door, Go-To-Goal) using cumulative return during few-shot adaptation as the primary metric. Results are presented as learning curves rather than a single numerical table. The headline finding is that under unobserved confounding (wind as a latent variable), standard MAML, PRETRAINED-PPO, and RL² all fail to outperform vanilla PPO from random initialization, while CAUSAL-MAML consistently achieves faster adaptation and lower variance than PPO across all three tasks. In the Go-To-Goal task (Experiment 3), CAUSAL-MAML also outperforms MAML in both adaptation speed and variance reduction, whereas PRETRAINED-PPO and RL² fail to converge entirely.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAUSAL-MAML outperforms all four baselines across the three Windy Gridworld tasks. Against PPO (random initialization), CAUSAL-MAML adapts more quickly with lower variance in all experiments, confirming that the counterfactual bootstrap provides a meaningful initialization advantage. Against standard MAML, CAUSAL-MAML wins decisively in Pick-Up-Key and Go-To-Door (where MAML fails to learn from confounded data), and also wins in Go-To-Goal where MAML can identify the correct path but adapts more slowly. Against PRETRAINED-PPO, CAUSAL-MAML consistently achieves higher returns; PRETRAINED-PPO is hurt by confounding bias and fails to converge in Go-To-Goal. Against RL², CAUSAL-MAML also wins across all tasks; RL² fails to learn effectively from confounded observational data in Experiments 1 and 2 and is unable to converge in Experiment 3. The appendix (Fig. 7) further shows that applying the counterfactual bootstrap to PPO, RL², and DPT also improves each over its non-causal counterpart, confirming the generality of the augmentation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of meta-RL (MAML, RL², DPT) and causal reinforcement learning, which has previously addressed unmeasured confounding in single-task settings (bandits, off-policy evaluation, imitation learning). The key departure is that all standard meta-RL methods assume the no-unmeasured-confounding condition, while prior causal RL work does not operate in the multi-task meta-learning regime. The paper bridges this gap by importing partial identification tools (Manski bounds, canonical CMDPs from Zhang et al. 2022) into the MAML training loop, constructing a feasible region of counterfactual environments consistent with confounded observations and sampling from it to generate unbiased training trajectories.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that standard meta-RL methods can perform worse than vanilla PPO from scratch when trained on confounded observational data highlights a critical, previously unaddressed failure mode of meta-learning in realistic settings where data is passively collected. The counterfactual bootstrap is general in principle (demonstrated on MAML, PPO, RL², DPT) and comes with convergence guarantees, but the experimental validation is confined to discrete gridworlds with a single confounding mechanism (wind). Open questions include whether the approach scales to high-dimensional continuous state-action spaces, whether the Manski-bound-based sampling remains tractable as the observed domain grows, and how the method behaves under more complex or time-varying confounding structures.
