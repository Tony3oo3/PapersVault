## Problem

RL-trained search agents for knowledge-intensive QA rely on sparse, outcome-based rewards (e.g., final answer correctness), which provide poor credit assignment for intermediate reasoning steps. A single early mistake—such as a misleading search query—can cascade into an incorrect answer because the agent has no mechanism to self-assess retrieved evidence and recover mid-episode. Additionally, existing evaluation benchmarks are vulnerable to data contamination, making it difficult to distinguish genuine procedural reasoning from memorized parametric knowledge.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a natural language question and interacts with a retrieval backend (E5 dense embeddings over the Wiki-18 corpus, top-k=3, or a live Google Search API) for up to T=4 tool-use turns. Output is a structured trajectory of tagged actions—<think>, <search>, <judge> (Yes/No), and <answer>—enforced by a strict prompt template that mandates a judgment after every retrieval step and conditions subsequent actions on that judgment. The final answer is extracted from the <answer> tag and scored via Exact Match against ground truth.

## Method

ReSeek introduces a JUDGE action into the agent's action space, converting the reasoning chain from a static linear sequence into a dynamic self-correction loop: after each retrieval, the agent emits a binary label (Yes/No) that is appended to the context as a 'soft removal' signal, guiding the policy to attend to or disregard the preceding evidence. Training uses GRPO with a two-component reward: a terminal Exact-Match answer reward and a dense intermediate self-correction reward (R_judge) that compares the agent's judgment against an 'ideal' label derived from BGE-reranker-large semantic similarity between the retrieved observation and the ground-truth answer (threshold τ=0.7). The penalty is asymmetric—accepting useless information incurs −0.6 while discarding useful information incurs −0.3—biasing the agent toward continued searching over premature commitment to noisy context. The framework is trained on Qwen2.5-3B/7B-Instruct backbones over a unified NQ+HotpotQA training set, and is evaluated on eight open-domain QA benchmarks plus the newly introduced FictionalHot, a contamination-resistant benchmark built by replacing real entities with fictional ones via GPT-5 paraphrasing and synthetic Wikipedia-style documents.

## Results

ReSeek achieves the highest average Exact Match (EM) across eight open-domain QA benchmarks: 0.377 with Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and 0.312 with Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct, outperforming the strongest baseline ZeroSearch (0.346 and 0.281 respectively). Gains are most pronounced on multi-hop datasets (HotpotQA: 0.389 vs 0.346; Bamboogle: 0.392 vs 0.278 for 7B) and on the contamination-resistant FictionalHot benchmark (0.061 vs 0.031), where ReSeek nearly doubles the score of the next-best method. Ablations confirm the JUDGE action contributes +0.9 avg EM and the dense self-correction reward (R_judge) adds a further +1.5 avg EM over the answer-only baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReSeek outperforms the strongest RL-based baseline ZeroSearch by +3.1 avg EM (7B: 0.377 vs 0.346) and +3.1 avg EM (3B: 0.312 vs 0.281), with the largest margins on multi-hop HotpotQA (+4.3 EM for 7B) and Bamboogle (+11.4 EM for 7B), while ZeroSearch retains a slight edge on TriviaQA and 2Wiki where single-pass retrieval suffices. Against Search-R1, ReSeek gains +3.5 avg EM (7B) and +2.4 avg EM (3B), with particularly large improvements on FictionalHot (0.061 vs 0.034) confirming that self-correction helps most when the corpus is closed and contamination-free. ReSeek also substantially surpasses non-RL agentic baselines: Search-o1 (0.183 avg) and single-pass RAG (0.267 avg) for 7B, and DeepSeek-R1 without search (0.238 avg). On the contamination-resistant FictionalHot benchmark, ReSeek nearly doubles ZeroSearch's score (0.061 vs 0.031) and is the only method to exceed 0.05, validating that the gains reflect genuine procedural reasoning rather than memorization. Statistical significance tests (paired t-tests, 5 seeds) confirm ReSeek's improvements over ZeroSearch are significant (p < 0.05) on NQ, PopQA, HotpotQA, 2Wiki, Bamboogle, and FictionalHot for the 7B model.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReSeek extends the RL-trained search agent paradigm established by Search-R1 and ZeroSearch, which rely on sparse, outcome-based rewards (final answer correctness) that provide poor credit assignment for intermediate reasoning steps. Its key departure is the JUDGE action: rather than training a separate process reward model (as in AgentPRM) or performing post-hoc backtracking correction (Feng et al., 2025), ReSeek embeds self-correction as an internal action within the episode, guided by a dense, answer-conditioned reranker reward. The FictionalHot benchmark complements prior contamination-aware efforts like MDBench by reducing data leakage at the source through fictional entity replacement rather than synthetic multi-document construction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The JUDGE action offers a practical, lightweight mechanism for in-episode error recovery that could be adopted by other agentic search systems, and the FictionalHot benchmark provides the community a cleaner evaluation signal for procedural reasoning versus memorization. However, the reward signal's reliance on answer-conditioned reranker scores limits its applicability to deep-search scenarios with long evidence chains or abstract bridge entities, where useful intermediate evidence is only indirectly related to the final answer. Open questions include whether richer per-step evidence-level supervision or LLM-as-a-judge signals can strengthen the JUDGE mechanism in these harder settings, and whether the approach generalizes beyond QA to broader agentic tasks.
