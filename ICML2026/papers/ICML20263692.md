## Problem

LLM-based scientific agents typically operate within a fixed hypothesis space with immutable prior principles, causing them to discard anomalous observations as noise rather than signals for theoretical refinement. This rigidity restricts discovery to phenomena consistent with initial priors, wastes computation on repetitive exploration of inadequate knowledge, and prevents agents from expanding their conceptual boundaries when encountering unexpected experimental results.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263692/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

PIEVO takes a scientific task description (e.g., optimize nanohelix circular dichroism), a high-fidelity surrogate model f* that maps concrete structure parameters to scalar observations, and an LLM backbone (Qwen3-32B or Gemini-2.5-Flash in non-thinking mode) as inputs. It operates under a strict budget of 24 experimental trials with 10-message context truncation per iteration. The output is a sequence of optimized hypotheses (concrete numerical structure parameters) alongside a dynamically evolved set of natural-language scientific principles, with performance measured by Solution Quality normalized to a task-specific reference maximum.

## Method

PIEVO reformulates scientific discovery as Bayesian optimization over an evolving principle space rather than static hypothesis search, decomposing the problem into dual uncertainty minimization: Principle→Hypothesis (an LLM generates testable candidates conditioned on the current MAP principle) and Evidence→Principle (Gaussian Process experts maintain calibrated likelihoods p(y|h,P) for posterior updates). Hypothesis selection uses Information-Directed Sampling to minimize the ratio of squared expected regret to information gain, with GP experts trained on semantic alignment features φ(h,P)=[e_h·e_P, ‖e_h−e_P‖₂] that bridge textual propositions and numerical kernels. When the surprisal-based Anomaly Score exceeds a threshold, a Coherent Augmentation mechanism triggers the Principle Agent to propose new candidate principles, expanding the active set while maintaining Bayesian coherence via a single fixed prior over the universal principle space. The framework provides sublinear regret guarantees Õ(√T) with the identification cost scaling with principle entropy H(P) rather than hypothesis-space size log|H|, yielding an 83.3% convergence speedup over the static-principle baseline PiFlow.

## Results

PIEVO achieves an average Solution Quality (SQ) of 90.81% (Qwen3-32B) and 93.15% (Gemini-2.5-Flash) across four scientific discovery benchmarks (MBO, NHO, SPO, TMC), representing a 29.7%–31.1% improvement over the strongest baseline PiFlow (70.03%–71.07%). On the MBO task, PIEVO exceeds 149% SQ (Qwen) and 153% SQ (Gemini), surpassing the empirical reference maximum. PIEVO also attains an 83.3% speedup in convergence steps versus PiFlow, with a fitted regret coefficient of 13.49√T versus 37.91√T, and achieves 2.4× higher exploration diversity (APD) and 1.5× higher exploitation efficiency (AUOC) on average.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PIEVO dominates all five baselines across every benchmark and both LLM backbones. Against the closest competitor PiFlow (static principle-guided search), PIEVO gains +20.78 SQ points on average with Qwen (90.81 vs. 70.03) and +22.08 with Gemini (93.15 vs. 71.07), with the largest margin on MBO (+52.96 / +73.35). Against autonomous scientific agents (AI Scientist v1/v2, AI Researcher), PIEVO leads by 27–31 SQ points on average, confirming that dynamic principle evolution outperforms both static hypothesis search and unstructured multi-agent reasoning. The gap is smallest on SPO (superconductor), where all methods cluster around 30–38% SQ due to the inherently difficult discrete search space, but PIEVO still leads by 3–4 points. In exploration-exploitation balance (Table 2), PIEVO achieves 2.4× higher APD and 1.5× higher AUOC than PiFlow on average, indicating it explores more broadly while converging faster. Against domain-agnostic optimizers (Random Search, GA, BO, DE), PIEVO's average SQ of 90.8–93.2% far exceeds the best optimizer (BO at 63.5%), demonstrating that the learned principle prior provides a substantial zero-shot advantage over from-scratch response surface modeling.

## Relation to Prior Work

PIEVO extends the line of LLM-based scientific discovery agents (The AI Scientist, AI Researcher, PiFlow) by shifting the optimization target from a static hypothesis space to a dynamically evolving principle space. It draws on Information-Directed Sampling (Russo & Van Roy, 2014) and Gaussian Process active learning (BALD) as the theoretical backbone, but departs from prior self-evolving agent work (RAGEN, EvolveR, Agent0) by applying Bayesian updates to semantic scientific principles rather than procedural parameters or policy weights. The most direct predecessor, PiFlow (Pu et al., 2025), treats principles as immutable constraints; PIEVO's key conceptual departure is modeling them as probabilistic beliefs that can be augmented via anomaly-driven discovery, formalized through a coherent augmentation mechanism that preserves a single fixed prior over a universal principle space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The principle-evolution paradigm offers a principled way to address the 'reward trap' problem where agents hill-climb within established paradigms and miss counterintuitive regimes, with the 83.3% convergence speedup suggesting that optimizing over a compact principle space is fundamentally more sample-efficient than searching the full hypothesis space. The case study autonomously identifying toroidal anapole resonance in nanohelices (corroborated by FDTD) demonstrates potential for genuine mechanistic discovery, not just optimization. However, all experiments rely on high-fidelity surrogate models rather than wet-lab or simulation feedback, the universal principle space is somewhat idealized (countable, fixed prior), and the GP experts use a simple 2D semantic-alignment proxy for likelihoods—open questions include whether the framework scales to truly open-ended discovery, multi-principle interactions, and real experimental noise regimes.
