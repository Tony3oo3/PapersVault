## Problem

While large reasoning models (LRMs) substantially outperform their base LLM counterparts on challenging reasoning benchmarks, it remains poorly understood where base models go wrong during token-by-token generation and how to efficiently narrow this gap. The paper asks whether the base-reasoning performance gap is uniformly distributed across all generated tokens or concentrated at a small set of critical decision points, and whether selectively correcting only those points can recover strong-model reasoning under a minimal token budget.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264077/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (a math problem wrapped in a chain-of-thought template) and two autoregressive models sharing a vocabulary: a small base model (e.g., Qwen3-0.6B-Base) for rollout generation and a larger reasoning model (e.g., Qwen3-8B Thinking) for guidance. At each decoding step, both models produce next-token distributions over the shared vocabulary; the output is a single generated reasoning trajectory (up to 16,384 tokens) where the vast majority of tokens are sampled from the base model and only a sparse subset (~4–13%) are sampled from the reasoning model via one-token takeover. No model parameters or hidden states are modified; the mechanism is purely an inference-time gating rule.

## Method

The core contribution is a disagreement-guided token intervention that performs sparse, one-token takeover by a stronger reasoning model at positions of high token-level distributional disagreement. At each step t, a disagreement score s_t is computed as the cross-entropy (or reverse KL) between the base model's and reasoning model's next-token distributions given the shared context. A two-part gate triggers takeover only when s_t exceeds both a globally calibrated threshold τ (the (1−r)-quantile from a held-out calibration set) and a local relative spike condition (s_t > λ · s̄_{t,W}, where s̄ is a sliding-window mean and λ is a tail-to-mean scale factor). When triggered, the reasoning model generates exactly one token and control immediately returns to the base model. The paper's key empirical finding is that this disagreement is extremely sparse (Gini ≈ 0.936), front-loaded to early positions, aligned with base-model uncertainty, and enriched 17× for planning-related tokens—meaning that correcting just a few early planning decisions redirects the entire downstream trajectory and recovers up to 157% of the Pass@8 gap to a same-size reasoning model.

## Results

On six mathematical reasoning benchmarks (GSM8K, MATH500, AMC23, OlympiadBench, AIME24, AIME25), disagreement-guided token intervention at a realized budget of ρ≈0.13 lifts Qwen3-0.6B-Base from 13.0/36.0 (Acc/Pass@8) to 52.4/80.0, surpassing the same-size Qwen3-0.6B(Thinking) variant (43.4/64.1) and recovering 157% of the Pass@8 gap to the 8B reasoning model. For Qwen3-1.7B, guided decoding at ρ≈0.16 reaches 62.1/83.8, essentially matching its thinking counterpart (64.1/80.3; 112% recovery). Only ~8% of generated tokens carry the salient base–LRM disagreement, and these tokens are 17× enriched in planning-related markers, confirming that a sparse set of early planning decisions drives the reasoning gap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the same-size reasoning variant (Qwen3-0.6B Thinking), guided intervention at ρ≈0.13 wins on every benchmark in both Accuracy and Pass@8 (e.g., 52.4 vs. 43.4 average Acc; 80.0 vs. 64.1 Pass@8), achieving 157% gap recovery. Against the larger Qwen3-8B(Thinking) upper bound, the guided 0.6B model still trails (52.4/80.0 vs. 78.1/87.3), but the entropy-triggered practical variant at ρ≈0.08 nearly matches the 8B Pass@8 (87.2 vs. 87.3) while querying the strong model at only ~8% of steps. In matched-budget comparisons, guided intervention at ρ≈0.04 (29.1/61.4) dominates both the random-replacement baseline (26.4/55.2) and the early-only baseline (25.7/58.4) at a 6× larger budget (ρ=0.25), confirming that the gain comes from selecting high-disagreement positions rather than from injecting strong-model tokens indiscriminately. For Qwen3-1.7B, guided decoding at ρ≈0.16 (62.1/83.8) essentially matches its thinking counterpart (64.1/80.3) on Pass@8 while exceeding it on Accuracy. Cross-family, the same protocol on LLaMA-3.1-8B / DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B recovers ~91% of the Pass@8 gap at ρ≈0.20, and on GPQA-Diamond the guided 0.6B model exceeds the 8B reasoning model's Pass@8 (88.0 vs. 78.0) at ρ≈0.05.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) the latent-capability view of reasoning, where base models are argued to already encode reasoning machinery that post-training merely activates (activation steering, one-example RL, confidence-based self-rewarding); (2) token-level distributional signals (cross-entropy, KL divergence, entropy) used in distillation and RLVR to identify decision-critical positions; and (3) selective inference-time intervention (RouteLLM, RelayLLM, AdaSwitch) that delegates to stronger models at coarse granularities (request-level or step-level). The paper departs from prior work by providing a quantitative, token-level *diagnostic* of where the base-reasoning gap actually lives—showing it is sparse (~8% of tokens), front-loaded, and planning-enriched—and then using that diagnostic as a fine-grained gate for one-token takeover, a granularity finer than any prior selective-intervention scheme.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a mechanistic, token-level account of why reasoning post-training works: a handful of early planning commitments steer the entire trajectory, and correcting them is sufficient to recover most of the reasoning gap. Practically, the entropy-only variant (Appendix C.3) means one need not query the reasoning model at every step, making the approach deployable. Key limitations include the primary focus on math benchmarks and the Qwen3 family (with only preliminary LLaMA and GPQA evidence), the computational cost of querying both models at every decoding step for the full disagreement gate, and the open question of whether the sparse-control picture holds for code generation, agentic reasoning, or multi-hop QA where the base-reasoning gap may be structured differently.
