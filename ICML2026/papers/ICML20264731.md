## Problem

Interpretable time series deep learning systems are commonly audited by checking whether explanations remain temporally consistent, implicitly treating explanation stability as evidence of decision robustness. This paper shows that assumption fails: an adversary can simultaneously force a target misclassification and steer the explainer to highlight a plausible, reference-consistent rationale, decoupling prediction from explanation. No prior work systematically demonstrated that time series explainers can be steered to match prescribed rationales under targeted misclassification, exposing a critical gap in coupled predictor-explainer robustness evaluation.

## Input / Output

The attack takes a multivariate time series X ∈ R^{T×D}, a target class label y′ ≠ y, and a reference explanation mask A′ (either ground-truth temporal annotations or the top-k% salient features from the clean explanation of the same interpreter), and produces an adversarial series X̃ = X + δ satisfying ‖δ‖∞ ≤ ε′ where ε′ = ε·(max(X) − min(X)) is an instance-wise budget. The threat model is white-box, requiring gradients of both the frozen transformer classifier and the explainer (TIMEX, TIMEX++, or Integrated Gradients). The output is a single perturbed time series that simultaneously achieves the target prediction and explanation alignment.

## Method

TSEF resolves a theoretical 'high-dimensional paradox'—proven via Theorem 4.1 that dense ℓ∞ steps cause off-target attribution mass to scale linearly with dimension d−|Ω|, making unstructured attacks incompatible with localized explanation targets—by decoupling the attack into two structured stages. The Temporal Vulnerability Mask M_t ∈ [0,1]^{T×D} localizes sparse, connected vulnerable segments via Gumbel-Sigmoid relaxation with a KL-divergence sparsity regularizer (against a Bernoulli prior) and a temporal connectivity penalty, using amplitude-invariant sign updates. The Frequency Perturbation Filter M_f ∈ [0,2]^{K×D} then edits the masked segment's Fourier spectrum element-wise, with adaptive scaling α_freq to satisfy the ℓ∞ budget and energy-invariant sign updates on the filter logits Θ_f. The two stages alternate iteratively: the inner loop re-identifies vulnerable patterns on the current adversarial example, while the outer loop refines the spectral perturbation, jointly minimizing a weighted sum of explanation-alignment distance and target-class classification loss.

## Results

TSEF achieves targeted misclassification (ASR) comparable to the strongest prediction-only attacks while dramatically preserving explanation alignment. On LOWVAR with TIMEX++, TSEF reaches ASR 0.848 / F1 0.837 with AUPRC 0.845, AUP 0.760, AUR 0.800, whereas PGD achieves similar ASR (0.846) but only AUPRC 0.258. On PAM with TIMEX++, TSEF attains ASR 0.614 and AUPRC 0.876, outperforming both PGD (0.597 / 0.696) and ADV² (0.585 / 0.820). On Epilepsy with TIMEX, TSEF pushes AUPRC to 0.981 versus 0.876 (PGD) and 0.922 (ADV²), confirming that explanation-preserving targeted attacks are feasible across diverse datasets and explainer families.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TSEF matches or slightly exceeds the ASR of PGD (the strongest prediction-only baseline) across all six datasets while achieving substantially higher explanation alignment: on LOWVAR+TIMEX++, AUPRC jumps from 0.258 (PGD) to 0.845 (TSEF) at nearly identical ASR (0.846 vs 0.848). Against ADV², the only other joint prediction-explanation attack, TSEF wins on both ASR and AUPRC on PAM (0.614/0.876 vs 0.585/0.820) and Epilepsy (0.199/0.912 vs 0.190/0.837 under TIMEX++). Prediction-only attacks BlackTreeS and SFAttack achieve competitive ASR but produce diffuse, temporally incoherent attributions that fail to match the reference explanation, yielding AUPRC values well below TSEF. Random and Local/Global Gaussian perturbations serve as sanity checks: they occasionally produce non-trivial explanation scores but almost never reach the target label (ASR < 0.10 in most settings). Overall, TSEF is the only method that simultaneously satisfies both the targeted prediction objective and the explanation-preservation constraint, exposing a coupled robustness gap that no single-objective baseline can address.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of time series adversarial attacks and explainer robustness. Prior time series attacks (PGD, BlackTreeS, SFAttack) target predictions without controlling post-attack explanations, while joint prediction-explanation attacks exist only in vision (ADV²) and NLP (TEF) without accounting for temporal structure. The paper introduces a modality-specific theoretical result (the high-dimensional paradox) explaining why naive dense ℓ∞ attacks fail at explanation control, then proposes TSEF which decouples the attack into a temporal vulnerability mask (when to perturb) and a frequency perturbation filter (how to perturb), drawing on Gumbel-Softmax relaxation, mutual information bounds for sparsity, and Fourier-domain editing in a way not previously combined for time series.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges a widely held assumption in interpretable ML that explanation stability serves as a reliable proxy for decision robustness, showing that targeted misclassification can be induced while explanations remain consistent with a reference rationale—effectively enabling an attacker to pass explanation-based audits. This has direct implications for high-stakes deployments (ECG monitoring, fault detection) where clinicians and operators rely on explanations to validate model outputs. Key limitations include the white-box requirement, restriction to differentiable explainers, and preliminary transfer results; open questions include coupling-aware defenses, black-box joint attacks, and extending the framework to perturbation- or surrogate-based explainers.
