## Problem

Existing LLM unlearning methods face a fundamental trade-off: Knowledge Deletion (KD) suppresses specific token sequences, causing biased removal, catastrophic forgetting, and incoherent outputs, while Distinguishable Refusal (DR) leaves harmful knowledge intact in the model, making it vulnerable to adversarial jailbreaks. No prior approach simultaneously achieves reliable distribution-level knowledge erasure and coherent, attack-resistant refusal behavior.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265457/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained LLM (θ_o), an unlearning set of prompt-response pairs to forget, and a retention set to preserve, and produces updated model parameters θ_u. At inference, a text query is processed in a single forward pass to compute a sample-wise free energy score (top-k token-level energies averaged for length invariance); if this score exceeds a model-adaptive threshold τ_energy, the response is replaced with a question-aware refusal template, otherwise the normal generation is returned. No additional sampling or generation is required for the energy computation, keeping overhead minimal compared to sampling-based estimators.

## Method

The D² paradigm reframes unlearning from token-level logit suppression to distribution-level control of knowledge presence, quantified by a free-energy index E(x, y^{<i}) = −T·log Σ_v exp(z(v|x,y^{<i})/T) derived directly from the softmax denominator. EUA training minimizes a combined objective: standard cross-entropy on retention data plus a squared-hinge energy loss that pushes unlearned-sample energies above a margin m_u and retained-sample energies below a margin m_r. These margins are not hand-set but derived from the pretrained model's own logit geometry via a self-preference split (top-50% logits define m_r, bottom-50% define m_u), ensuring model-adaptive boundaries that avoid the instability of fixed thresholds. At inference, the top-k token-level free energies are aggregated into a length-invariant sample score, compared against τ_energy (the average of sample-wise margins over both datasets), and a refusal is triggered when the threshold is exceeded, unifying erasure and refusal in a single energy-based decision.

## Results

On TOFU Forget 10%, EUA achieves the best aggregate score (Agg.) across all six model backbones, reaching 8.665 on LLaMA-3.2-3B and 8.665 on Qwen2.5-7B, with average gains of 24.57% over the best KD baseline on LLaMA and 33.27% on Qwen. EUA also attains the highest Erasing Quality (EQ ≈ 9.5) and Linguistic Quality (LQ ≈ 8.6–8.9), indicating strong forgetting without sacrificing response coherence. Against DR-based methods, EUA achieves 100% detection accuracy both before and after jailbreak attacks, whereas ICUL drops from 77% to 32% and VP from 71% to 49%. On WMDP and MUSE, EUA achieves the best utility preservation (50.45 on MUSE-Books, 39.12 on MUSE-News) while driving verbatim and factual memory to near-zero.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EUA dominates all KD-based baselines (GradDiff, DPO, NPO, SimNPO, RMU, WGA, SatImp) on TOFU across every model backbone, with the largest gaps in Linguistic Quality (LQ): EUA scores 8.651 on LLaMA-3.2-3B versus 0.049 for GradDiff and 0.600 for SatImp, indicating that prior methods produce incoherent or hallucinated outputs. Among KD methods, WGA and SatImp are the closest competitors on EQ (8.711 and 8.175 vs. EUA's 9.478 on LLaMA-3.2-3B), but both sacrifice LQ severely. Against DR-based methods (ICUL, VP, FP, Guardnail), EUA achieves 100% detection accuracy both before and after adversarial attacks, while ICUL collapses to 32% and VP to 49% post-attack; Guardnail is the most robust DR baseline (95–96%) but still trails EUA and degrades slightly. On WMDP, EUA achieves the best MMLU retention (0.5263 vs. 0.5152 for SatImp) with competitive Bio/Cyber unlearning accuracy. On MUSE, EUA matches the best unlearning scores (0.000 VerbMem/KnowMem on Books) while achieving the highest utility preservation (50.45 vs. 45.77 for SatImp on Books; 39.12 vs. 36.63 for SimNPO on News).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the two dominant LLM unlearning paradigms—Knowledge Deletion (token-level logit suppression via gradient ascent, DPO, NPO, WGA, SatImp) and Distinguishable Refusal (prompt-based or detector-based inference-time filtering)—by reframing unlearning as a distribution-level constraint on knowledge presence rather than a token-level objective. It builds directly on the energy-based model framework (LeCun et al., 2006), repurposing the Helmholtz free energy of the softmax logit distribution as a scalar index of whether the model 'knows' an answer, and unifies deletion and refusal under a single energy-boundary training objective (EUA). Conceptually, it bridges the KD and DR paradigms: energy-based training performs the deletion, while an energy threshold at inference triggers coherent refusal, eliminating the need for separate detectors or prompt engineering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a core practical tension in LLM unlearning: KD methods produce unstable, incoherent outputs (gibberish, hallucinations) while DR methods remain vulnerable to adversarial jailbreaks because the knowledge is never actually removed. By achieving 100% detection accuracy under adversarial attacks (vs. 32–49% for DR baselines) while simultaneously improving linguistic quality, EUA offers a more deployable unlearning pipeline. Key limitations include the dependence on a retain regularization set (limiting applicability when such data is unavailable), sensitivity to the hyperparameter λ, and the fact that the energy margin construction still involves manual design choices (top/bottom 50% logit split). Open questions include whether the energy-based separation generalizes to large-scale pretraining distributions and whether formal theoretical guarantees beyond the current empirical validation can be established.
