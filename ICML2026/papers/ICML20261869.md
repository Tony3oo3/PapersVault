## Problem

Constrained diffusion models on nonconvex feasible sets face a fundamental tension: projection-based methods require per-step Newton solves that are expensive, may fail on nonconvex geometries, and scale poorly with dimension, while reparameterization and penalty approaches introduce bias, require domain-specific knowledge, or add hard-to-tune hyperparameters. This computational bottleneck is especially acute for real-time applications like robotics where trajectories must be generated on resource-constrained hardware, and for complex mixed equality-inequality constraints where exact projections are numerically unstable or undefined.

## Input / Output

The method takes a data distribution supported on a stratified manifold Σ ⊂ ℝᵈ defined by smooth equality constraints h(x)=0 and inequality constraints g(x)≤0 (satisfying rCRCQ), an energy function f, and a noise schedule σ(t). At inference, it starts from a prior sample (position on Σ and, for ULLA, a Gaussian momentum projected onto T_xΣ) and iteratively denoises over N steps to produce a sample on Σ. The output is a point satisfying both equality and inequality constraints, with constraint violations decaying exponentially along the trajectory; in practice, a single optional terminal Newton projection can be applied for final numerical cleanup, but the landing mechanism keeps violations at O(10⁻⁵)–O(10⁻⁹) even without it.

## Method

The core mechanism is a 'landing' drift term −ασ(t)²∇J(x)ᵀG(x)†J(x) added to the normal component of the Langevin dynamics, which enforces exponential decay of constraint violations (dJ(X_t) = −ασ(t)²J(X_t)dt) without any per-step projection or Newton iteration. The paper derives both overdamped (OLLA) and underdamped (ULLA) closed-form SDEs via Lagrangian multipliers, where ULLA's ballistic momentum dynamics accelerate mixing toward the prior, reducing the required forward trajectory length N by up to 47× compared to projection-based baselines. For training, the authors introduce Conditional Wasserstein Path Matching (CWPM), a variational bound based on 2-Wasserstein distance rather than KL-divergence, which remains well-defined even when intermediate samples exhibit minor constraint violations off Σ—resolving a theoretical singularity that breaks standard score-matching or DT-ELBO losses. The ULLA discretization uses a memory-efficient OBA splitting integrator that collapses the (x,p) dynamics into a second-order Markov chain in positions alone, halving memory storage for long forward trajectories during training.

## Results

On Earth & Climate (S²), ULLA with N=50 achieves JSD of 0.125/0.099/0.110/0.069 (Volcano/Earthquake/Flood/Fire), comparable to RDDPM (N=400) at 0.123/0.093/0.106/0.051 while using 8× fewer forward steps. On 3D Mesh data, ULLA (N=30) achieves JSD of 0.029/0.033/0.044/0.036 (Bunny-50/100, Cow-50/100), matching or exceeding RDDPM (N=400) at 0.032/0.034/0.046/0.034. Training time drops from 12,388 s (RDDPM) to 1,021 s (ULLA) on Earth and from 1,916 s to 360 s on Mesh, yielding up to 5× faster training and 47× faster sampling. On the mixed-constraint 7-DOF robot arm task, ULLA achieves JSD of 0.275–0.391 across dimensions d=140–560, substantially outperforming the best Euclidean baseline (0.498–0.750).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The two primary Riemannian baselines are RFM (Chen & Lipman, ICLR 2024) and RDDPM (Liu et al., 2026). On Earth & Climate, ULLA (N=50) matches RDDPM (N=400) within 0.003–0.018 JSD on all four sub-datasets while requiring 8× fewer forward steps, and is within 0.009–0.011 of RFM (N=1000). On Mesh data, ULLA (N=30) achieves the best JSD on Bunny-50 (0.029 vs. 0.032 for RDDPM) and Cow-50 (0.044 vs. 0.046), and is within 0.001–0.002 on the remaining meshes. The critical advantage is computational: ULLA trains in 1,021 s vs. 12,388 s (RDDPM) and 4,019 s (RFM) on Earth, and 360 s vs. 1,916 s (RDDPM) and 145,424 s (RFM) on Mesh, a 5–40× speedup. On the mixed-constraint 7-DOF robot arm task, ULLA (JSD 0.275–0.391) outperforms the best Euclidean baseline (Guided, 0.499–0.740) by 0.22–0.36 JSD, while the Projected Euclidean variant fails entirely (NaN) due to projection breakdowns. On Alanine dipeptide, ULLA achieves JSD 0.031/0.035 (ψ/RMSD) versus 0.150/0.057 for Euclidean, with constraint violations ~10⁻⁵–10⁻¹¹ versus ~10⁻²–10⁻³ for baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the 'landing' technique from constrained optimization (Muehlebach & Jordan, 2022; Jeon et al., 2025) into the diffusion-model setting, replacing the per-step Newton projections used by Riemannian DPM (RDDPM) and the retraction-based steps in Riemannian Flow Matching (RFM) with a cheap first-order normal correction that enforces exponential decay of constraint violations. It further extends the SDE-based diffusion framework of Song et al. (2021) to nonconvex feasible sets defined by mixed equality and inequality constraints, and introduces underdamped Langevin dynamics to accelerate mixing toward the prior. A new training objective, Conditional Wasserstein Path Matching (CWPM), is derived to handle the fact that landing-based samplers do not guarantee exact on-manifold intermediate states, avoiding the singularity issues of KL-based losses like DT-ELBO.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for applications where constrained generation must run on resource-limited hardware (robotics, molecular design): up to 47× faster sampling and 5× faster training versus projection-based baselines, with constraint violations at the 10⁻⁵–10⁻⁹ level. Open questions include whether the O(dN) memory cost of storing forward trajectories can be reduced, how the method behaves on disconnected feasible sets where the prior must cover multiple components, and whether landing can be composed through decoder networks in latent-space diffusion for high-dimensional scalability. The finding that second-order curvature corrections are empirically negligible suggests the first-order landing term is the essential ingredient, simplifying implementation.
