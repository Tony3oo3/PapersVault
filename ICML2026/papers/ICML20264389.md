## Problem

Existing learning-to-rank objectives suffer from two structural limitations: surrogate losses (pairwise, listwise, differentiable sorting) compress supervision into low-dimensional statistics that provably cannot distinguish distinct full-order distributions at the gradient level, while Plackett-Luce-style probabilistic models introduce an order-reversal asymmetry that makes the learning objective depend on an arbitrary generation direction. Directly optimizing full-order probabilities is intractable due to factorial growth, leaving a gap between principled order-level modeling and practical optimization.

## Input / Output

The model takes item feature vectors as input (structured tabular features for Web30K/Istella, or 4-digit composite MNIST images for the synthetic permutation task) and outputs, per item, a location parameter μ_i and a scale parameter σ_i defining a latent score distribution. At inference, only the deterministic scores μ_i are used to rank items; the scale σ_i is a training-time auxiliary that captures per-item uncertainty. For the MNIST task, list lengths range from 4 to 32 with fully ordered labels, while Web30K/Istella use list length 64 with five-level graded labels containing ties.

## Method

The Full-Order Bound (FOB) constructs a tractable lower bound on the full-order probability P(z_1 ≤ … ≤ z_n) by introducing n−1 shared cut points that partition the real line into ordered intervals, constraining each latent score z_i to its designated interval so the event factorizes across items while preserving order-reversal invariance by design. Training is bilevel: an inner loop tightens the bound over cut points using Safe-Region Gradient Ascent (SRGA), which applies an unconstrained gradient step followed by clamping each cut point to a local interval between its neighbors, automatically preserving the ordering constraint without explicit projection; an outer loop then updates model parameters via the tightened objective. Under log-concave latent densities (Gaussian, Laplace, logistic), the inner problem is provably convex, and a list-length warmup (growing sublist size from 4 to n) stabilizes early training. The paper further proves that low-dimensional surrogates are gradient-non-separable and that PL-style objectives violate order-reversal invariance, properties that FOB satisfies by construction.

## Results

On the MNIST permutation task, FOB achieves the highest exact permutation accuracy (ACC) across all list lengths, with the gap over baselines widening as n grows: 68.20% at n=8 (vs. 68.13% for EF-DSF, 66.54% for DiffSort, 47.88% for RankNet) and 1.25% at n=32 (vs. 0.45% for EF-DSF and 0.23% for DiffSort). On the large-scale Web30K and Istella benchmarks, FOB is competitive on NDCG@10 (51.11% and 73.44% respectively, within ~0.2% of LambdaRank) and attains the best Kendall's τ on Istella (86.21%), indicating superior full-list ordering consistency. The optional LAMBDAFOB variant recovers the NDCG gap (51.28% on Web30K, 73.89% on Istella) while remaining comparable in Kendall's τ.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FOB outperforms all nine baselines on the MNIST permutation task at every list length, with the most dramatic margins at longer lists: at n=16, FOB reaches 27.55% ACC versus 26.51% (EF-DSF), 25.28% (DiffSort), and 3.34% (RankNet); at n=32, FOB is the only method above 1% (1.25% vs. 0.45% for EF-DSF). Among differentiable-sorting baselines, FOB consistently edges out EF-DSF and DiffSort by 0.5–2.3% ACC, while NeuralSort and Gumbel-Sinkhorn degrade sharply beyond n=8. On Web30K and Istella, FOB is within 0.1–0.2% NDCG@10 of LambdaRank (51.11% vs. 51.22% on Web30K; 73.44% vs. 73.88% on Istella) but achieves the highest Kendall's τ on Istella (86.21% vs. 85.96% for RankNet and 85.69% for LambdaRank), confirming its advantage on full-list consistency. Pairwise baselines (RankNet, PairwiseHinge) and the PL-style ListMLE are clearly outperformed on both NDCG and Kendall's τ across both benchmarks. The metric-aligned LAMBDAFOB variant matches or slightly exceeds LambdaRank on NDCG (51.28% vs. 51.22% on Web30K; 73.89% vs. 73.88% on Istella) while remaining within 0.3% on Kendall's τ, demonstrating that the FOB framework can be adapted to top-weighted metrics without sacrificing its structural properties.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of two established learning-to-rank paradigms: surrogate objectives (RankNet, LambdaRank, NeuralSort, DiffSort) that compress supervision into low-dimensional pairwise or position-wise statistics, and probabilistic listwise models (ListMLE, ListNet) that assign full-order likelihoods via Plackett-Luce sequential factorizations. Rather than building on either lineage directly, FOB introduces a cut-point construction over latent scores that factorizes across items (reminiscent of surrogate tractability) while preserving the full-order event structure and order-reversal symmetry that both prior families abandon. The bilevel tightening with safe-region gradient ascent is a novel optimization mechanism not present in either surrogate or PL-style training procedures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean structural diagnosis of why existing objectives are limited (gradient-level non-separability of low-dimensional surrogates; order-reversal asymmetry of PL factorizations) and offers a principled alternative that is computationally on par with standard baselines. However, the practical NDCG gains on Web30K/Istella are modest and require the LAMBDAFOB variant to match LambdaRank-level metric alignment, while the most dramatic results (MNIST permutation accuracy) come from an extremely stringent synthetic metric. Open questions include whether the full-rank conjecture holds beyond n=6, whether FOB's advantage persists at industrial list lengths (thousands of items), and whether the bilevel structure can be further simplified or replaced by closed-form cut-point solutions.
