## Problem

Sequential Monte Carlo (SMC) search for policy improvement in model-based RL suffers from two critical failures as search depth increases: estimator variance grows exponentially with the number of possible trajectories, and path degeneracy causes all particles to collapse onto a single root action, rendering additional search obsolete and producing degenerate delta-distribution policy targets. While MCTS scales well with sequential compute, its sequential tree structure resists the parallelization and GPU acceleration that SMC naturally supports. The paper asks whether SMC can be redesigned to scale favorably with depth while retaining its parallelizability advantages.

## Input / Output

Given a current state s, a learned or exact dynamics model (P, R), a prior policy network π_θ, and a value network v_φ, TSMCTS produces an improved policy π_search(s) and a root value estimate V_search(s) over a fixed search budget B = N·T (N particles × T depth steps). The improved policy is used as a cross-entropy training target for π_θ and the value estimate feeds TD-λ bootstraps for the critic v_φ. The method is agnostic to discrete versus continuous action spaces: in discrete settings it uses the Gumbel-top-k trick to sample m_1 root actions without replacement, while in continuous settings actions are simply sampled from π_θ.

## Method

TSMCTS combines two key mechanisms. First, SMCTS shifts the SMC perspective from estimating a distribution over trajectories to tracking action values at the root via a backpropagation step: at each depth t, bootstrapped returns are aggregated into a running average Q̄_t(s, a) per root action, which reduces variance (by mixing returns of different horizons) and prevents path degeneracy (information about actions with no remaining particles is preserved). Second, TSMCTS wraps SMCTS in a Sequential Halving (SH) bandit loop at the root: starting with m_1 actions sampled from π_θ, each iteration searches the surviving actions independently in parallel with N/m_i particles, halves the action set by selecting the top m_i/2 actions by inverse-variance-weighted value estimates, and doubles the per-action particle budget. This structure provides additional variance reduction (shorter per-iteration depth T_SH = T/log₂m_₁, repeated independent searches, and inverse-variance aggregation across iterations) while maintaining O(N) space and O(B) runtime complexity identical to vanilla SMC.

## Results

TSMCTS significantly outperforms all search-based baselines (SPO, TRT SMC, GumbelAZ) and the model-free PPO baseline across discrete (Snake, RubiksCube) and continuous (Ant, HalfCheetah, Humanoid) environments, with the sole exception of RubiksCube where it matches GumbelMCTS at the end of training. TSMCTS is the only SMC variant that scales favorably with increased search depth T, while the SMC baseline degrades beyond T=6 due to high variance and path degeneracy. TSMCTS reduces the variance of the root value estimator substantially compared to both the SMC baseline and SMCTS, and maintains a constant number of active actions in the policy target (m₁=4) versus the SMC baseline's collapse to a single action. Runtime overhead is modest compared to the SMC baseline and significantly lower than GumbelMCTS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the SMC baseline (SPO's CAI-SMC with μ=π_θ), TSMCTS significantly outperforms in all environments in an equal-footing comparison (Figure 2, Figure 5), and is the only variant that benefits from increased depth (Figure 1 left). Against TRT SMC, TSMCTS outperforms overall, and a TSMCTS+TRT hybrid further improves in RubiksCube and HalfCheetah where TRT's trust-region and revived-resampling contributions are beneficial. Against GumbelAZ (GumbelMCTS), TSMCTS outperforms in most environments but matches MCTS performance in RubiksCube at the end of training; TSMCTS also demonstrates significantly better runtime efficiency than GumbelMCTS (Figure 4). Against PPO (model-free reference), TSMCTS-based agents achieve substantially higher returns across all environments, confirming the benefit of model-based search for policy improvement. In the variance and path-degeneracy analyses (Figure 1 center/right), TSMCTS achieves the lowest root-estimator variance and maintains the highest number of active root actions (constant at m₁=4) compared to all other SMC variants.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SMC-for-search line initiated by Piché et al. (2019) and operationalized for policy improvement by Macfarlane et al. (2024, SPO) by (i) reformulating the target distribution outside the Control-as-Inference framework into a simpler, general greedification-based form (RL-SMC), (ii) shifting the search objective from trajectory estimation to root action-value estimation with backpropagation (SMCTS), and (iii) wrapping the root-level action selection in a Sequential Halving (Karnin et al., 2013) loop inspired by the simple-regret framing of Danihelka et al. (2022, GumbelMCTS). The resulting TSMCTS is positioned as a drop-in replacement for MCTS in AlphaZero/MuZero-style training loops, retaining SMC's O(N) space and embarrassingly-parallel structure while addressing the two known failure modes of SMC search—variance explosion and path degeneracy—that prevented it from scaling with depth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TSMCTS offers a practical path to GPU-accelerated, depth-scalable search for model-based RL without the sequential bottleneck and tree-memory overhead of MCTS, which is significant as modern deep RL increasingly relies on JAX-style parallelism. The value-based perspective shift (estimating Q-values rather than trajectory distributions) is a clean conceptual contribution that also subsumes the path-degeneracy mitigation of de Vries et al. (2025, TRT SMC) while additionally reducing variance. Open questions include whether the inverse-variance weighting assumption (equal per-particle variance) holds when DNN value estimates are used, whether the SH stationarity guarantee is truly satisfied in practice with learned critics, and whether the approach extends to very large or continuous action spaces where m₁ must be chosen carefully. The paper does not explore learned (imperfect) dynamics models, leaving the most practically relevant setting for model-based RL untested.
