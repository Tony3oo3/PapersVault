## Problem

Time series foundation models (TSFMs) generate forecasts through a single forward pass, processing all temporal information uniformly, which leads to systematic errors during drought events and regime shifts in regional climate prediction. Expert climatologists, by contrast, decompose multi-scale climate patterns and iteratively refine predictions through structured coarse-to-fine analysis. The paper asks whether frozen TSFMs can be adapted to perform this hierarchical refinement at inference time without updating backbone parameters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261219/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a multivariate input window of length W (70% of available series) with D channels—first channel being historical SPEI, remaining channels carrying NCEP-NCAR Reanalysis 1 covariates and large-scale climate indices (ENSO, IOD, SAM)—and produces an H-step SPEI forecast (primarily H=1 month). At inference, the same context is projected through five block-average/repeat operators at strides {12, 6, 3, 2, 1} months to generate multi-resolution backbone proposals, while lightweight Ridge residual predictors (fitted offline on 12-month short windows) supply the correction signals. No ground-truth targets are accessed at test time; the only test-time residual is the predicted residual from the frozen Ridge regressor.

## Method

RGMR is a two-stage inference-time wrapper: (1) offline calibration fits per-level closed-form Ridge residual predictors on short-window (12-month) projected inputs to capture systematic level-specific biases, and (2) at inference, the frozen backbone is queried at each of K=5 resolution levels from coarsest (12-month stride) to finest (1-month stride), and predictions are refined via a boosting-style update that mixes the current raw proposal with the previous refined prediction plus an adaptive residual correction. The adaptive weights are elementwise sigmoids of the predicted residual magnitude minus a validation-selected threshold, clipped to [10⁻³, 1], so corrections apply only where errors are likely large. A monotone mixing schedule α^(k) = 0.3 + 0.5(1 − r_k/12) gives finer resolutions more anchoring weight while preserving a strict contraction factor ρ^(k) < 1 at every level, yielding a geometric decay of expected squared error to a bounded noise floor. The framework is architecture-agnostic (validated on TimesFM, TimeGPT, and TabPFN) and requires no backbone parameter updates, with only ~60 ms additional GPU latency per forecast origin.

## Results

On one-month-ahead SPEI forecasting across three South Australian sites, TimesFM v1.0 + RGMR achieves the best MSE at every location: 0.318 (Loc 1), 0.258 (Loc 2), and 0.426 (Loc 3), reducing MSE by 18.7%, 18.9%, and 18.7% respectively over the frozen TimesFM baseline (mean ≈ 18.7%). RGMR is architecture-agnostic: TimeGPT-1 + RGMR and TabPFN-ts + RGMR also consistently outperform their respective frozen backbones and all generic deep baselines (Transformer, Autoformer, PatchTST, TimesNet, etc.) and multi-resolution baselines (N-BEATS, Scaleformer). The ablation (Table 2) shows a monotonic progression: coarse projection → multi-resolution → residual-guided update → adaptive weighting, with the full RGMR achieving the lowest MSE (0.334 mean) and highest R² (0.655 mean) across the three sites.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TimesFM + RGMR dominates all 14 baselines on MSE, MAE, and R² at every one of the three South Australian sites. Against the strongest generic deep baselines (DLinear at Loc 2 with MSE 0.423, iTransformer at Loc 1 with 0.524), TimesFM + RGMR (0.258–0.426) leads by 15–40% in MSE. Against multi-resolution architectures, TimesFM + RGMR beats N-BEATS (best MSE 0.439 at Loc 2) and Scaleformer (best 0.445 at Loc 2) by 42% and 42% respectively at Loc 2, and by 32–36% at the other sites. Among the three frozen foundation models, TimesFM is the strongest backbone (MSE 0.318–0.524), and RGMR improves each backbone: TimesFM by ~18.7% mean MSE, TimeGPT-1 by ~18.5%, and TabPFN-ts by ~18.2%. The ablation confirms that each RGMR component contributes: multi-resolution projection alone improves over the frozen backbone, residual-guided updates add further gains, and adaptive residual weighting yields the final ~3–5% MSE reduction on top of the unweighted variant.

## Relation to Prior Work

RGMR carves out a distinct niche by applying multi-resolution refinement at inference time on frozen time-series foundation models, rather than through dedicated architectures (N-BEATS, Scaleformer, TimeMixer) or retraining. Conceptually, it draws on iterative refinement paradigms from language models (chain-of-thought, self-refine) and the climatological practice of coarse-to-fine multi-scale analysis, but instantiates them with lightweight Ridge residual predictors and block-average/repeat projection operators. The key departure from prior multi-resolution work is that the backbone remains entirely frozen and the refinement is a plug-and-play wrapper, while the contraction-style theoretical analysis (Theorem 4.1) provides a formal guarantee absent from most empirical multi-scale forecasting methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that operational climate forecasting pipelines can adopt frozen TSFMs with a ~60 ms inference overhead and no retraining, which is critical for regional drought early-warning systems in data-limited settings. The architecture-agnostic consistency across TimesFM, TimeGPT, and TabPFN suggests the wrapper could transfer to other TSFMs and climate indices beyond SPEI. Open questions include whether the gains persist at longer horizons (H=6, 12), across more geographically diverse regions, and for probabilistic rather than point forecasts; the current evaluation is limited to monthly SPEI at a handful of sites with H=1 as the primary metric. The contraction bound, while clean, relies on bounded-noise assumptions that may not hold under severe distribution shift (e.g., unprecedented drought regimes).
