## Problem

Existing offline hierarchical RL methods for long-horizon goal-conditioned tasks rely on separate high-level and low-level networks that generate only a single intermediate subgoal, causing loss of final-goal awareness when subgoals are erroneous and preventing end-to-end gradient flow across decision stages. This structural limitation makes them inadequate for complex tasks requiring coordination of multiple intermediate decisions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260734/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a state observation (a state vector of dimension 2–46 depending on the environment, or a 64×64×3 RGB frame for pixel-based tasks) and a goal state as input, and autoregressively produces a sequence of H latent subgoals (encoded future states in a shared latent space) followed by a primitive action. The token sequence has a fixed length of T = 2 + H + 1 (state embedding, goal embedding, H subgoal placeholders, and one action placeholder), and subgoals are generated in reverse order—from the one farthest from the current state to the nearest—so that the nearest subgoal aggregates the most comprehensive reasoning context before the action is predicted.

## Method

CoGHP reformulates hierarchical decision-making as autoregressive sequence generation within a single unified MLP-Mixer architecture, where each latent subgoal acts as a chain-of-thought-style reasoning token that conditions subsequent predictions. The backbone alternates token-mixing, a learnable lower-triangular causal token-mixer (which lets each token attend to all previously generated tokens), and channel-mixing MLP layers, with parameters shared across all H+1 prediction steps. Training uses a shared goal-conditioned IQL value function V_ψ(s, e_g) to compute advantage-weighted regression objectives for every subgoal prediction and the final action, aggregated into a single end-to-end loss with per-subgoal discounting. Teacher forcing with ground-truth subgoal embeddings is applied during training to prevent error accumulation, and subgoal supervision targets are sampled at fixed k-step intervals along offline trajectories.

## Results

CoGHP reports average success rate (%) across 5 predefined state-goal pairs per environment, evaluated over 8 random seeds. On the most challenging long-horizon navigation tasks, CoGHP achieves 79% on pointmaze-giant and 78% on antmaze-giant, outperforming the next-best baselines (OTA at 72% and 77% respectively). On manipulation tasks, CoGHP's advantage is most pronounced: 54% on cube-double and 42% on cube-triple versus 19% and 17% for the strongest baseline (SAW), and 78% on scene versus 63% for SAW. On simpler tasks (antmaze-medium, cube-single), CoGHP is competitive but not always the top performer (e.g., GCIQL reaches 99% on cube-single vs. CoGHP's 97%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoGHP outperforms all seven baselines on the majority of environments, with the largest margins on multi-object manipulation (cube-double: 54% vs. 19% for SAW; cube-triple: 42% vs. 17% for SAW) and the scene task (78% vs. 63% for SAW). Against HIQL, the closest hierarchical baseline, CoGHP gains 33 points on scene (78% vs. 38%) and 33 points on cube-double (54% vs. 2%), confirming that HIQL's separate high/low-level networks lose final-goal awareness during manipulation. GCIQL is the only method that beats CoGHP on a single task (cube-single: 99% vs. 97%), but it collapses on all multi-object and long-horizon tasks (≤23% on cube-double, 0% on pointmaze-giant). OTA is the strongest recent navigation baseline, matching or slightly exceeding CoGHP on antmaze-large (92% vs. 90%) and antmaze-giant (77% vs. 78%), but it trails CoGHP by 15–35 points on all manipulation tasks. GCBC, QRL, and CRL are consistently weak across the board, with QRL and CRL dropping below 10% on most manipulation environments.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoGHP builds directly on HIQL's shared goal-conditioned value function and advantage-weighted regression framework, but departs fundamentally from its two-network hierarchical design by unifying all hierarchy levels into a single MLP-Mixer backbone. The autoregressive multi-subgoal generation draws an explicit analogy to chain-of-thought reasoning in LLMs, extending a paradigm previously applied to vision-language-action models (Zawalski et al., 2024) into the offline goal-conditioned RL setting. The choice of MLP-Mixer over Transformer as the sequence backbone is motivated by the fixed, position-dependent semantic roles of tokens (state, goal, subgoals, action), a structural property the authors argue makes self-attention's data-dependent mixing less beneficial than simple feedforward cross-token communication.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CoGHP addresses three structural limitations of prior offline hierarchical RL—single-subgoal constraints, loss of final-goal awareness under erroneous high-level guidance, and fragmented gradient flow across separate networks—within a single end-to-end architecture. The finding that MLP-Mixer substantially outperforms a capacity-matched Transformer on structured, position-dependent sequences (e.g., 42% vs. 2% on cube-triple) offers a useful architectural principle beyond this specific task. Key open questions remain: the number of subgoals and their temporal spacing must be hand-tuned per task, subgoals are restricted to encoded future states, and only unidirectional autoregressive generation is explored. Adaptive subgoal counting, richer subgoal representations (skill primitives, language embeddings), and bidirectional or diffusion-style subgoal refinement are natural next steps.
