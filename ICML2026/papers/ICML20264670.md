## Problem

Hybrid artifact-semantic MLLM detectors (e.g., AIGI-Holmes) naively concatenate artifact features (NPR) with semantic features (CLIP-ViT) before the LLM, but the high intra-feature similarity of artifact representations causes near-uniform attention maps after softmax (attention dilution), preventing effective transfer of fine-grained texture-level forgery cues into the semantic space and limiting detection accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264670/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input, which is processed by two parallel encoders: NPR (artifact encoder, operating on upsampled-downsampled residuals) and CLIP-ViT (semantic encoder, operating on the original image resized to 256×256). The output is a binary Real/Fake classification produced autoregressively by the LLM, constrained to emit only the single token 'Real' or 'Fake' in response to a fixed detection prompt.

## Method

TranX-Adapter is a lightweight bidirectional fusion module (40–160M trainable parameters) placed between the visual encoders and a frozen LLM. For Artifact→Semantic transfer, TOP-Fusion computes the Jensen-Shannon divergence between per-patch fake-detection probabilities of the two encoders and uses this as a cost matrix in Sinkhorn optimal transport, selectively amplifying patches where artifact and semantic predictions diverge. For Semantic→Artifact transfer, X-Fusion applies a 3-layer cross-attention stack where artifact features serve as queries and semantic features as keys/values, injecting semantic context into artifact representations. The asymmetric design is motivated by the finding that dot-product attention fails in the Artifact→Semantic direction due to intra-feature similarity, while cross-attention works well in the reverse direction; only the adapter is trained via a language-modeling objective, leaving the LLM untouched.

## Results

On the GenImage benchmark (Protocol I), TranX-Adapter with LLaVA-1.6-mistral 7B achieves 91.9% mean accuracy across eight generators, surpassing AIGI-Holmes (89.8%) by 2.1% and NPR (88.3%) by 3.6%. On the Chameleon benchmark (Protocol II), the same model reaches 85.1% accuracy when trained on the full GenImage dataset, outperforming the strongest baseline (Qwen3-VL 4B†, 78.3%) by 6.8%. On RRDataset, Qwen3-VL 4B with TranX-Adapter attains 90.9% average accuracy, exceeding GPT-4o zero-shot (84.1%) by 6.8% and the direct-concatenation baseline (85.6%) by 5.3%. The paper reports an average gain of 4.7% over baselines across all benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TranX-Adapter consistently outperforms all 20 baselines across the three evaluation protocols. On GenImage, LLaVA-1.6-mistral 7B with TranX-Adapter (91.9% mean) beats AIGI-Holmes (89.8%) by 2.1%, NPR (88.3%) by 3.6%, AIDE (86.9%) by 5.0%, and the direct-concatenation LLaVA baseline (87.3%) by 4.6%. On Chameleon, the method achieves 85.1% (full GenImage training) versus 78.3% for Qwen3-VL 4B† and 72.1% for PatchAll/DINOv2, a margin of 6.8% and 13.0% respectively. On RRDataset, Qwen3-VL 4B with TranX-Adapter (90.9%) surpasses GPT-4o zero-shot (84.1%) by 6.8%, Claude-3.7-sonnet (82.5%) by 8.4%, and the strongest fine-tuned detector DIRE (79.4%) by 11.5%. Against PEFT methods (LoRA, Adapter) on Chameleon, TranX-Adapter with 160M parameters (75.8%) matches full fine-tuning (76.8%) while using only 2.2% of the parameters, outperforming LoRA-160M (74.4%) and Adapter-160M (72.5%) by 1.4% and 3.3% respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on AIGI-Holmes (Zhou et al., 2025), which first demonstrated that concatenating NPR artifact features with CLIP-ViT semantic features inside an MLLM (LLaVA-1.6-mistral) yields strong AIGI detection. The key departure is a diagnostic finding: artifact features exhibit high intra-feature similarity, causing near-uniform attention maps (attention dilution) that prevent effective Artifact→Semantic information transfer under standard self-attention. To address this, the authors introduce TranX-Adapter, a lightweight bidirectional fusion module that replaces dot-product attention with Jensen-Shannon-divergence-guided optimal transport (TOP-Fusion) for Artifact→Semantic and cross-attention (X-Fusion) for Semantic→Artifact, positioning itself within the hybrid artifact-semantic methods family alongside CO-SPY and AIGI-Holmes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The attention dilution diagnosis provides a concrete mechanistic explanation for why prior hybrid MLLM-based detectors underperform, and the optimal-transport solution is a creative repurposing of a tool from a different subfield. Practically, the adapter requires only 40–160M trainable parameters versus 7.2B for full fine-tuning while matching or exceeding full fine-tuning accuracy, making it accessible for practitioners. Open questions include whether the approach extends to fine-grained localization and explainability (mentioned as future work), whether it generalizes to the newest generative models (e.g., FLUX), and whether the asymmetric fusion design (TOP-Fusion vs. X-Fusion for different directions) reflects a deeper principle about feature-space geometry or is specific to the NPR/CLIP-ViT pairing.
