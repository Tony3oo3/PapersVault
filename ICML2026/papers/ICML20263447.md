## Problem

Existing epistemic uncertainty methods face a fundamental dilemma: Bayesian approaches provide principled estimates but are computationally intractable for modern networks, while efficient second-order predictors like evidential deep learning (EDL) lack rigorous connections between their training objectives and epistemic uncertainty, sometimes exhibiting pathological behaviors such as increasing uncertainty with more data. The paper identifies that both paradigms are grounded in probability theory's complete mass allocation, which conflicts with the incomplete-knowledge nature of epistemic uncertainty, and turns to possibility theory as a principled alternative.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard classification inputs (RGB images at 32×32 for CIFAR, 64×64 for TinyImageNet, or text questions for LLM fine-tuning) and produces a vector of Dirichlet parameters α ∈ (1,∞)^K via softplus(logits)+1. From α, aleatoric uncertainty is computed as 1 − max_k α_k/α_0 (small when one class dominates) and epistemic uncertainty as K/α_0 (large when total evidence is low). The practical interface is minimal: the network architecture remains unchanged and only the cross-entropy loss is replaced by the DAPPr loss (~10 lines of PyTorch code).

## Method

DAPPr defines a possibilistic posterior over parameters as the relative likelihood exp(−L(θ;D)) normalized by its supremum, then projects it to prediction space via the possibilistic change-of-variable rule (supremum over all parameters yielding a given prediction). Under the high-capacity assumption for overparameterised networks, this projected posterior simplifies to a tractable surrogate proportional to exp(−ℓ(p,y)), and the training objective becomes minimising the maxitive pseudo-divergence between a learned Dirichlet possibility function and this surrogate. Applying Danskin's theorem yields a closed-form inner maximiser p* = (α − y)/(α_0 − 1), reducing the loss to log g_ψ(p*|x) + λ‖(1−y)⊙α‖²₂, where the spurious evidence regulariser enforces the minimum commitment principle by suppressing evidence on incorrect classes. This principled derivation from possibility theory distinguishes DAPPr from EDL, which post-hoc interprets the Dirichlet concentration parameter without formally targeting epistemic uncertainty.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, DAPPr achieves 92.00% test accuracy and 99.23% AUPR for confidence estimation, outperforming the strongest EDL variant F-EDL (91.19% / 99.10%) and all prior second-order predictors. On CIFAR-100, DAPPr reaches 70.85% accuracy and 94.39% confidence AUPR, again surpassing F-EDL (69.40% / 94.01%). On the Mistral-7B LLM benchmark (OBQA), DAPPr attains 89.47% accuracy and 90.42% OOD AUROC on ARC-C, exceeding IB-EDL (88.73% / 88.58%) while maintaining competitive ECE (3.11% vs. 2.27%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAPPr outperforms all EDL variants (EDL, I-EDL, R-EDL, DAEDL, F-EDL) on both accuracy and confidence AUPR across CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and CIFAR-10-LT, with the largest margin over F-EDL on CIFAR-10-LT (68.81% vs. 63.73% accuracy at ρ=0.01). It also surpasses non-EDL second-order predictors including RS-NN (91.91% vs. 92.00% accuracy on CIFAR-10; DAPPr wins on confidence and OOD), NatPN, PostNet, and DUQ across all benchmarks. On the LLM setting (Mistral-7B/OBQA), DAPPr beats IB-EDL on accuracy (89.47% vs. 88.73%), NLL (0.36 vs. 0.41), and OOD AUROC on all three test sets, though IB-EDL achieves lower ECE (2.27% vs. 3.11%). DAPPr is competitive with 10-model ensembles: it matches or exceeds them on CIFAR-10 OOD (91.72 vs. 86.90 on SVHN) but trails slightly on CIFAR-100 OOD (73.32 vs. 75.09 on SVHN); combining DAPPr with ensembling yields the best overall results (94.73% SVHN OOD on CIFAR-10). On TinyImageNet, DAPPr achieves 58.19% accuracy, substantially outperforming the next-best R-EDL (49.76%) and matching the plain cross-entropy baseline (58.28%) while providing principled uncertainty scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

DAPPr positions itself at the intersection of possibility theory (Zadeh, 1978; Dubois & Prade, 1988) and second-order predictive modeling, drawing directly on the possibilistic change-of-variable rule (Baudrit et al., 2008), the maxitive pseudo-divergence (Singh et al., 2025), and the recent decoupling of epistemic/aleatoric uncertainty via possibility theory (Hieu et al., 2025). It departs from evidential deep learning (EDL and its variants), which also uses Dirichlet parameters but grounds its objective in subjective logic and Dempster-Shafer theory without a rigorous link to epistemic uncertainty. The key conceptual departure is replacing Bayesian marginalization (integration over parameters) with a supremum-based projection, yielding a closed-form training objective that is both principled and as simple as a cross-entropy replacement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a long-standing tension in uncertainty quantification: Bayesian methods are principled but intractable, while efficient second-order predictors lack rigorous justification. By grounding second-order predictors in possibility theory, DAPPr provides a principled target (the projected possibilistic posterior) that is tractable to approximate, and the resulting ~10-line loss replacement is immediately deployable. The empirical demonstration that EDL's epistemic uncertainty pathologically increases with more training data (Figure 5) and that DAPPr's regulariser is inapplicable to EDL (near-zero accuracy in Table 6) strengthens the case that the two frameworks are fundamentally different. Open questions include whether the high-capacity approximation (Eq. 11) degrades in low-capacity or few-shot settings, and whether the possibilistic projection strategy extends naturally to regression, sequence modeling, or multi-modal tasks beyond classification.
