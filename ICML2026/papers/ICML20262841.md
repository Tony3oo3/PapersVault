## Problem

Most existing quantum algorithms for regression have focused exclusively on linear regression, leaving practically important variants such as Lasso, Ridge, Huber, ℓ_p, and γ_p regression without known quantum speedups. This paper asks whether the advantages of quantum computing can be extended to a unified framework covering this entire family of regression tasks, and answers affirmatively by quantizing the classical GLM sparsification breakthrough of Jambulapati et al. (2024).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262841/cropped_page11_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives quantum oracle access to a data matrix A ∈ ℝ^{m×n} with row sparsity r, a response vector b, a family of (L,θ,c)-proper loss functions f_i, and parameters ε, s_min, s_max. It outputs a non-negative weight vector w with at most Õ(log(s_max/s_min)·n/ε²) nonzero entries, forming an ε-approximate sparsifier that preserves the total loss within a (1±ε) factor over the range [s_min, s_max]. The sparsifier must be smaller than m (requiring ε = Ω(√(n/m))), and the final regression solution x is then obtained by solving the small sparsified instance classically in poly(n, 1/ε) time.

## Method

The core contribution is a fully quantum pipeline for GLM sparsification built around three components. First, a Quantum Multiscale Leverage Score Overestimates (QMLSO) procedure iteratively refines an initial coarse weight vector over Õ(1) contractive rounds using quantum leverage score approximation (Apers & Gribling, 2024), then recursively computes weights at smaller scales to produce a single overestimate vector z that upper-bounds each row's statistical contribution across all scales. Second, a quantum initialization step constructs the starting weight by locating anchor points on each loss function, estimating leverage scores at those anchors, and adding a quadratic bump to the losses to ensure well-conditioning. Third, importance sampling is performed via Hamoudi's (2022) multiple quantum state preparation technique to draw M samples proportional to z, combined with quantum sum estimation (Li et al., 2019) to approximate the normalization constant for correct reweighting. The resulting sparsifier achieves Õ(r√(mn)/ε + poly(n, 1/ε)) quantum time, yielding a quadratic speedup in m over the classical mr runtime for constant ε.

## Results

The paper presents a unified quantum GLM sparsification framework achieving Õ(r√(mn)/ε + poly(n, 1/ε)) quantum time for a broad class of regression problems (linear, multiple, Lasso, Ridge, Huber, ℓ_p, and γ_p regression), where r is the row sparsity of the data matrix. This yields a quadratic speedup in the number of samples m over the best classical algorithms (which run in Õ(mr + poly(n, 1/ε))) whenever ε = Ω(√(n/m)), the regime where the sparsifier is smaller than the input. The constructed sparsifier has size Õ(log(s_max/s_min) · n/ε²), and the algorithm is shown to be near-optimal in m and ε via quantum lower bounds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the classical baselines of Nelson & Nguyen (2013) and Clarkson & Woodruff (2017) for linear/multiple regression (Õ(mr + n³)), this work achieves Õ(r√(mn)/ε + n³), a quadratic improvement in m when ε is constant and m ≫ n. For ridge regression, the classical bound of Avron et al. (2017) (Õ(mr + poly(n,1/ε))) is improved to Õ(r√(mn)/ε + n³). For Lasso regression, the classical LARS-based bound of Efron et al. (2004) (Õ(mn² + n³)) is improved to Õ(r√(mn)/ε + poly(n,1/ε)), and for Huber, ℓ_p, and γ_p regression, the classical bound of Jambulapati et al. (2024) (Õ(mr + poly(n,1/ε))) is similarly improved to Õ(r√(mn)/ε + poly(n,1/ε)). Against the only prior quantum baseline, Song et al. (2023) with runtime Õ((mn)^{3/4}/ε + poly(n,1/ε)) for linear/multiple/ridge regression, this work's Õ(r√(mn)/ε + poly(n,1/ε)) strictly subsumes it since r√(mn) ≤ n√(mn) < (mn)^{3/4} when m > n and r < n. The paper also notes that for Lasso, Huber, ℓ_p, and γ_p regression, no prior quantum algorithm existed, making these the first quantum speedups for these tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper quantizes the classical GLM sparsification framework of Jambulapati et al. (2024), which itself generalized Lewis-weight-based sparsification to arbitrary proper loss functions. The quantum implementation relies on two key building blocks: the quantum leverage score approximation of Apers & Gribling (2024) for computing importance scores, and the multi-copy state preparation technique of Hamoudi (2022) for efficient importance sampling. Prior quantum regression work (Song et al., 2023) handled only linear, multiple, and ridge regression via spectral sparsification; this paper extends the quantum speedup to Lasso, Huber, ℓ_p, and γ_p regression by introducing Multiscale Leverage Score Overestimates (MLSO) as a quantum data structure that captures the multiscale structure needed for non-homogeneous losses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first unified quantum framework covering a broad family of regression objectives, closing a gap where quantum speedups were known only for the p=2 (linear/ridge) case. The near-optimality of the m and ε dependence (matching quantum lower bounds from Apers & Gribling, 2024 and Apers & de Wolf, 2022) suggests the approach is essentially tight for this model. However, practical impact is constrained by the QRAM assumption and the current state of quantum hardware; the algorithm's utility will depend on future advances in scalable quantum memory. Open questions include whether the quadratic speedup in m can be improved, whether the framework extends to non-convex or structured losses beyond the (L,θ,c)-proper class, and whether the QRAM requirement can be relaxed.
