## Problem

Current VLM causal reasoning benchmarks contain two blind spots: they presuppose temporal constitution (understanding time as the medium through which causes bring about effects) without testing it, and they provide scaffolding through prompts that may substitute for internalized capability rather than accessing it. This allows the plausibility-faithfulness gap to persist—models generate fluent causal narratives through pattern matching while lacking the temporal-constructive and symbolic foundations that genuine causal reasoning requires.

## Input / Output

The diagnostic protocol takes 500 COCO validation images, each paired with 9 causal reasoning questions spanning Pearl's three levels (3 association, 3 intervention, 3 counterfactual), and evaluates three LLaVA variants under three scaffolding conditions (full with worked examples, partial without examples, minimal with only task description). Each VLM response is scored on two independent 0-10 scales—text quality and causal chain validity (arrow-notation chains requiring ≥1 link for Level 2, ≥2 for Level 3)—by two automated judges (GPT-4o and Claude 4.5 Sonnet), yielding per-condition CDD and SS scores that are then compared across conditions to detect scaffolding dependence.

## Method

The paper proposes a two-metric diagnostic framework grounded in the distinction between camera-like single-frame extraction and Cézanne-like temporal construction of understanding. Causal Depth Disparity (CDD) quantifies the gap between text fluency and causal chain validity, testing whether models construct causal structure or merely pattern-match to causal language. Scaffolding Sensitivity (SS) measures how chain validity shifts when scaffolding is systematically removed, testing whether causal frameworks are constitutively internalized or remain external dependencies. The hierarchical logic is that temporal constitution is a prerequisite (H1) and scaffolding-invariance is the diagnostic signature (H2): a model lacking temporal foundation cannot exhibit scaffolding-invariance. Preliminary evidence from three LLaVA variants reveals three qualitatively distinct scaffolding-response patterns (necessary support, interference, format violation), with no model achieving scaffolding-invariance, supporting the claim that current VLMs operate in camera-like processing mode rather than constructing causal understanding through temporal integration.

## Results

On 500 COCO images with 9 causal questions each (4,500 items per model per condition), LLaVA-NeXT 13B achieves the strongest text-chain alignment under full scaffolding (CDD = 0.00; text 7.85, chain 7.42) but degrades monotonically as scaffolding is removed (CDD rises to 0.16; chain drops 25% to 5.59). LLaVA-CoT 11B shows high text fluency (7.16) but very poor chain validity (1.34) under full scaffolding, yielding CDD of 0.66–0.86 across conditions. LLaVA-RLHF 13B generates 2,467 blank responses under full scaffolding (chain score 1.11), with CDD spiking to 0.90 under partial scaffolding. Scaffolding Sensitivity values diverge across models (+1.83, +0.19, −1.07), indicating three qualitatively different patterns of external dependence rather than a single uniform sensitivity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three LLaVA-family models that share architectural lineage but differ in training approach. LLaVA-NeXT 13B (dynamic resolution + enriched instruction tuning) achieves the highest chain quality (7.42 under full scaffolding) and the lowest CDD (0.00), but its positive SS (+1.83) reveals that this performance is scaffolding-dependent, with chain scores declining 25% as scaffolding is removed. LLaVA-CoT 11B (chain-of-thought supervision) produces fluent text (7.16) yet generates nearly invalid causal chains (1.34), and its negative SS (−1.07) shows that external scaffolding actually interferes with its CoT-trained patterns. LLaVA-RLHF 13B (RLHF with frozen image encoder) exhibits a qualitatively distinct failure mode: full scaffolding triggers catastrophic blank outputs (2,467 blanks), while removing worked examples partially recovers text scores (6.39) but chain validity remains near zero (0.47). No model achieves scaffolding-invariance; the divergent SS patterns (+1.83, +0.19, −1.07) demonstrate that each model relates to causal frameworks externally in ways shaped by its specific training history, supporting the paper's central claim that current benchmarks miss genuine capability deficits.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the growing literature on VLM causal reasoning evaluation (CLEVRER, CELLO, CausalVL Bench, TimeCausality) but departs from it by treating scaffolding as an experimental variable rather than fixed infrastructure, drawing on the prompt-sensitivity literature (Zhao et al., Sclar et al.) and the plausibility-faithfulness gap in explainable AI (Lyu et al., Agarwal et al.). Its theoretical framing is distinctive: it imports Cézanne's multi-vantage-point painting technique as an analogy for temporal constitution in visual understanding, and Lacan's theory of symbolic constitution as a diagnostic logic for distinguishing internalized capability from external dependency. The dual-probe approach (text + causal chain) extends Zhang et al.'s chain-selection benchmark to chain generation, and the CDD/SS metrics formalize what prior work identified qualitatively as a gap between fluent output and structured reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete diagnostic gap: current VLM causal benchmarks cannot distinguish pattern-matched plausible text from genuinely constructed causal structure, and they cannot detect whether a model's performance depends on the specific scaffolding format. The divergent scaffolding responses across three LLaVA variants (one degrades, one improves, one catastrophically fails) suggest that training objectives shape models' relationships to symbolic structure in ways that fixed-format evaluation renders invisible. However, the evidence is limited to three models within a single architectural family, absolute chain scores are very low across all models (suggesting the task may be too rigid or the format too demanding), and the LLaVA-RLHF blank-response artifact complicates interpretation. Open questions include whether the patterns generalize to other model families, whether video-language models exhibit different temporal capacity, and whether the proposed structure-first training objectives or scaffolding curricula can actually produce scaffolding-invariant causal reasoning.
