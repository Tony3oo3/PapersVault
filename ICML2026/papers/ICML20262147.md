## Problem

Score-based diffusion models and other deep generative architectures assume fully specified inputs, yet real-world tabular data frequently contain missing values. Existing workpatches this gap with constant placeholders (which create deterministic missingness cues) or iterative self-imputation (which introduces non-stationary training dynamics), but neither cleanly separates the role of filled values as conditioning context from their role as supervised targets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262147/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes partially observed mixed-type tabular rows (continuous and categorical features) together with a binary observation mask indicating which entries are present. Missing entries are filled once offline via per-feature conditional LightGBM models (heteroskedastic Gaussian for continuous, smoothed categorical for discrete) that condition on all observed covariates and the mask itself, producing a single fixed augmented dataset used throughout training. The output is complete synthetic tabular rows generated by the trained feature-space denoiser, evaluated on distributional fidelity (precision, recall, trend, shape) and downstream ML utility (AUROC/RMSE).

## Method

AugMask is a plug-and-play two-step training rule for missing-unaware diffusion backbones: (1) stochastic conditional augmentation samples each missing entry from a context-dependent distribution (per-feature LightGBM regressors for mean and log-variance, or a classifier for categoricals), and (2) the denoising loss is masked so that only originally observed coordinates contribute to supervision. Augmented missing values thus enter the denoiser as uncertain conditioning context through the noised input but are never treated as reconstruction targets. A Rao-Blackwellized analysis shows that marginalizing over the augmentation draws yields a variance-weighted sensitivity penalty: high conditional variance on a missing coordinate penalizes the denoiser's reliance on that fill, while low variance permits stronger use as context. This mechanism is what distinguishes AugMask from both constant-placeholder baselines (zero variance, no regularization) and AugFull (which supervises on the augmented values and inherits pseudo-target bias when the augmenter is misspecified).

## Results

Across 90 MCAR scenarios (6 datasets × 5 missing ratios × 3 seeds), AugMask applied to feature-space diffusion backbones achieves the best overall Summary Rank: TabDiff+AugMask reaches 3.2** and CDTD+AugMask reaches 3.8**, both statistically superior (Wilcoxon p<0.05) to the best missing-aware baseline (ForestDiff, rank 8.1). The gain from loss-masking over augmentation alone is consistent: TabDiff improves from rank 5.2 (AugFull) to 3.2 (AugMask), and CDTD from 5.8 to 3.8. Downstream ML utility also improves substantially, with TabDiff+AugMask achieving rank 3.0 on the ML Task metric versus 11.1 for the unmodified TabDiff baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AugMask (applied to TabDiff and CDTD) outperforms all four missing-aware baselines on Summary Rank: TabDiff+AugMask (3.2) and CDTD+AugMask (3.8) beat ForestDiff (8.1), MIWAE (8.9), DiffPuter (13.7), and MissDiff (15.8), with the gap being statistically significant (\*\* p<0.05) against the entire missing-aware group. Among missing-unaware baselines, AugFull alone already yields large gains: TabSyn improves from rank 13.7 to 6.3, ARF from 18.5 to 12.4, and TVAE from 17.7 to 11.2, confirming that stochastic augmentation is broadly useful. Adding loss-masking (AugMask) provides further improvement specifically for feature-space diffusion models: TabDiff drops from 5.2 to 3.2 and CDTD from 5.8 to 3.8, while latent-space TabSyn (which cannot use observed-only supervision) benefits only from AugFull. The advantage of AugMask over AugFull is most pronounced at high missingness (p_miss=0.9), where treating completions as targets amplifies errors. AugMask is also more robust to augmenter misspecification: when the conditional bias η or stochasticity λ is perturbed, the AugMask–AugFull quality gap remains positive across all 25 perturbation settings, whereas AugFull degrades more sharply.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on score-based diffusion models for tabular data (TabDDPM, TabDiff, CDTD, TabSyn) and the line of missing-aware generative modeling (MIWAE, MissDiff, DiffPuter, ForestDiff), but departs from both by proposing a training-level fix rather than an architectural one. It connects conceptually to marginal score matching (Givens et al., 2025), which integrates out missing entries in the objective, and to data augmentation as implicit regularization (Bishop, 1995; Dao et al., 2019), but reframes stochastic augmentation specifically as uncertainty-aware conditioning context rather than as a standalone imputer. The key departure from prior missing-data diffusion work is the explicit separation of conditioning (stochastic completions enter the input) from supervision (loss is masked to observed coordinates only), formalized through a Rao-Blackwellized objective that yields a variance-weighted sensitivity penalty.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AugMask provides a practical, plug-and-play recipe that lets standard feature-space diffusion backbones handle incomplete tabular data without architectural modifications, and the empirical results show it outperforming purpose-built missing-aware baselines across six datasets and five missingness ratios. The theoretical analysis (variance-weighted sensitivity regularization) gives a principled explanation for why stochasticity in the augmentation matters beyond point-estimate accuracy, and the robustness study (Figure 5) shows the method degrades gracefully under augmenter misspecification. Key limitations include restriction to feature-space (not latent) diffusion, focus on MCAR/MAR (not MNAR), and the factorized augmentation ignoring cross-coordinate interactions under block missingness. Extending the observed-only supervision principle to latent diffusion, VAEs, or adversarial objectives remains an open and nontrivial direction.
