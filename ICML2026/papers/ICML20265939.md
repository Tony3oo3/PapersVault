## Problem

Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) for vision-language models suffers from two critical limitations: uniform sampling wastes the majority of the trajectory budget on sequences doomed to fail due to early visual perception errors, and sparse binary task rewards cannot distinguish whether a failure originates in the visual description stage or the downstream reasoning stage, creating a credit assignment ambiguity that hinders targeted optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265939/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a textual query as input and produces a structured three-part response: a visual description segment (in <description> tags), a step-by-step reasoning chain (in <think> tags), and a final answer (in \boxed{}). The key structural detail is that the description segment is identified and scored independently before any reasoning is generated, enabling a two-stage sampling pipeline where description quality gates the allocation of reasoning exploration budget. Responses are constrained to max 2048 tokens with prompts up to 4096 tokens.

## Method

MIRL introduces mutual information (MI) between generated description tokens and the visual input as a cheap pre-screening signal, computed via two forward passes (with and without the image) to estimate conditional entropy differences. The sampling strategy proceeds in three phases: pre-sample N description candidates, compute MI scores and select the top-K highest-MI descriptions, then fork M additional reasoning branches from each selected description, yielding K×(M+1) complete trajectories while discarding low-MI descriptions from reasoning sampling. A decoupled reward mechanism assigns MI-based rewards (clipped to [0, 0.5] and normalized) exclusively to description tokens and standard task-correctness rewards exclusively to reasoning tokens, with group-relative advantages computed separately for each segment. This is implemented as a modular extension to DAPO/GRPO, modifying only the rollout sampling logic and reward computation without altering the underlying policy gradient update, and achieves 70.22% average accuracy across six benchmarks with 25% fewer complete trajectories than uniform sampling of 16.

## Results

MIRL-12 (10 pre-samples → top-6 selected × 2 forks) achieves 70.22% average accuracy across six vision-language reasoning benchmarks, surpassing DAPO-16 (69.76%) while using 25% fewer complete trajectories (12 vs. 16). MIRL-8 (8→4×2) reaches 68.38% average, outperforming the same-budget DAPO-8 (66.27%) by 2.11 absolute points. On individual benchmarks, MIRL-12 leads on MathVerse (70.08 vs. 68.12), We-Math (71.54 vs. 71.20), and RealWorldQA (60.34 vs. 58.78), while DAPO-16 retains a slight edge on MathVista (77.89 vs. 78.42) and M3CoT (75.14 vs. 74.35).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DAPO-16 (the strongest baseline with 16 complete trajectories), MIRL-12 achieves a higher average accuracy (70.22% vs. 69.76%) while generating only 12 complete trajectories—a 25% reduction in sampling cost. MIRL-12 wins on MathVerse (+1.96), We-Math (+0.34), and RealWorldQA (+1.56), but trails DAPO-16 on MathVista (−0.47) and M3CoT (−0.79). Against the same-budget DAPO-8, MIRL-8 improves average accuracy by 2.11 points (68.38% vs. 66.27%), confirming that MI-guided selection adds value beyond simply matching trajectory counts. Compared to the untrained Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct base model (54.81% avg), both MIRL configurations yield substantial gains of 13.6–15.4 points, with the largest improvements on MathVerse (+33–35 points) and We-Math (+24–25 points), indicating that RLVR with MI-guided allocation is particularly effective for mathematically intensive visual reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

MIRL extends the RLVR paradigm (GRPO, DAPO) by repurposing mutual information—a signal previously used only at inference time for hallucination mitigation (M3ID, CMI-VLD)—as a training-time mechanism for both sampling budget allocation and reward computation. The key conceptual departure is the 'forking' strategy: rather than uniformly sampling G complete trajectories, MIRL pre-samples cheap description segments, scores them by MI, and concentrates full reasoning exploration only on top-K high-MI descriptions. The decoupled reward design (MI reward on description tokens, task reward on reasoning tokens) is a novel application of the data processing inequality argument to credit assignment in VLM policy optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 25% reduction in complete trajectories while surpassing DAPO-16's accuracy is a practical efficiency gain for VLM RL practitioners, and the decoupled reward mechanism offers a principled solution to the credit assignment ambiguity that plagues sparse-reward RLVR. However, the full training pipeline is validated only on Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct with static images and math/science benchmarks; generalization to other architectures (LLaVA, InternVL), video, and open-ended visual tasks remains untested. Open questions include whether the MI-accuracy correlation holds in domains where visual and textual information are redundant, and whether the two-forward-pass MI computation overhead becomes prohibitive at scale.
