## Problem

Automated GPU kernel generation via LLMs remains limited by the vast, hardware-dependent optimization space (up to 10^9 configurations for a single subgraph). Existing RL-based methods suffer from scarce training data and low correctness rates, while agent-based systems rely on coarse-grained execution-time feedback, leading to unstable trial-and-error exploration. The core gap is the absence of structured, expert-level optimization guidance that constrains the design space and directs LLM decisions toward high-performance implementations.

## Input / Output

The method takes a PyTorch reference operator following KernelBench's standardized interface (a Model class with forward(), get_inputs(), and get_init_inputs()) along with the target GPU specification, and produces a complete Triton kernel implementation consisting of @triton.jit-decorated kernel functions, a wrapper with grid calculation, and a ModelNew(nn.Module) class. The generated kernel must compile, pass numerical correctness verification against the PyTorch reference, and be benchmarked for speedup. The framework operates on Triton specifically (rather than raw CUDA) because its tile-based abstraction provides a more tractable optimization space for LLM-driven generation.

## Method

EGG decomposes kernel generation into two hierarchical stages mirroring expert workflows: (1) algorithmic structure design, which combines multi-seed search over diverse algorithmic paradigms with semantics-preserving structural refinements (e.g., operator fusion, algorithm reformulation), and (2) hardware-specific tuning through three sequential sub-stages—parallel mapping (dimension-to-grid assignment), tensor tiling (BLOCK_M/N/K selection), and memory optimization (coalesced loads, software pipelining via num_stages/num_warps). A stage-aware multi-agent collaboration mechanism coordinates three specialized agents (code, profile, debug) within each stage via structured JSON interfaces, while inter-stage context propagation retains only finalized decisions to prevent cross-stage interference. Nsight Compute profiling metrics (SM throughput, DRAM bandwidth, L2 hit rate, memory-dependency stalls) are collected at each stage to drive the profile agent's bottleneck diagnosis. The approach requires no model fine-tuning or RL training, instead encoding expert optimization principles directly into stage-specific prompts that constrain the LLM's search to well-scoped sub-problems with explicit objectives.

## Results

On KernelBench, EGG achieves a 100% success rate across all three difficulty levels and a 2.13× average speedup over PyTorch Eager. The Fast₁ rate (fraction of kernels beating PyTorch Eager) reaches 72% on Level 1, 100% on Level 2 (fused operators), and 94% on Level 3 (full networks), with corresponding speedups of 1.83×, 2.73×, and 1.52×. On real-world TritonBench operators, EGG surpasses hand-tuned production kernels by 1.24× (Flash Attention), 1.63× (RoPE Embedding), and 1.08× (INT8 Dequant MatMul).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EGG outperforms all six baselines on every KernelBench level. Against the closest competitor CudaForge (multi-agent with hardware feedback), EGG gains +0.40×, +0.73×, and +0.22× speedup on Levels 1–3 respectively, with the largest gap on Level 2 fused operators where EGG reaches 100% Fast₁ versus CudaForge's 90%. Against Torch Compile (default mode), EGG improves speedup by 0.74×, 1.35×, and 0.16× on Levels 1–3; even against Torch Compile in max-autotune mode (Appendix B), EGG still leads by 0.79×, 1.22×, and 0.04×. General-purpose LLMs (DeepSeek V3.2, ChatGPT 5.1) achieve sub-1× average speedup and 34–66% success rates, confirming that raw LLM capability is insufficient for kernel optimization. The RL-based AutoTriton suffers from low correctness (36–56% success) and often produces kernels slower than PyTorch Eager (0.83–0.96× speedup). Against the compiler baseline TVM Relax (Appendix B), EGG achieves a 1.56× geometric-mean speedup advantage, with the gap narrowing on Level 3 (1.03×) where TVM Relax benefits from graph-level optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the LLM-based GPU kernel generation literature, which splits into RL-fine-tuned approaches (AutoTriton, TritonRL, Kevin) and agent-based iterative refinement systems (CudaForge, AI CUDA Engineer). EGG departs from both by imposing a hierarchical, expert-workflow-mirrored decomposition—algorithmic structure design followed by sequential hardware-specific tuning (parallel mapping → tensor tiling → memory optimization)—rather than treating kernel generation as a flat end-to-end optimization or an undifferentiated multi-turn refinement loop. The multi-agent collaboration (code, profile, debug agents) is structured around these stages with explicit inter-stage context filtering, distinguishing it from CudaForge's single-loop Coder/Judger design. The target language is Triton rather than raw CUDA, leveraging its tile-based abstraction to make the optimization space more tractable for LLM-driven search.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 100% correctness rate combined with a 2.13× average speedup over PyTorch Eager (and 1.60× over Torch Compile) demonstrates that structured, expert-guided search can substantially outperform both general-purpose LLMs and prior agent/RL systems, while also being more token-efficient (50k vs. 110k tokens per kernel vs. CudaForge). The finding that EGG surpasses hand-tuned production Triton kernels on operators like RoPE (1.63×) suggests that even expert manual tuning leaves performance on the table, validating automated generation as a practical tool. Key limitations include dependence on hand-crafted expert priors that may bias exploration toward known patterns, and the sequential stage decomposition that precludes joint cross-stage optimization—both flagged as open directions. The framework's generality across GPU architectures (RTX 4090, 5090, H20, RTX PRO 6000) and LLM backends (GPT-5.1, Claude Opus 4.5) strengthens its practical relevance.
