## Problem

Learnable 2D RoPE variants like Mix-RoPE in vision transformers suffer from anisotropic frequency direction distributions: strided patch tokenization introduces an axial spectral bias in token features, which during training drives learned frequency orientations to align with a narrow subset of directions (frequency collapse). This directional anisotropy degrades resolution extrapolation, causing performance to drop sharply when inference resolution deviates from the training resolution.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images tokenized into a regular patch grid (e.g., 14×14 for 224×224 input with 16×16 patches) and encodes each token's centered position in polar coordinates (r, ϑ) rather than cartesian (x, y). Evaluation follows a fixed-protocol extrapolation setup: models are trained at 224×224 and tested without fine-tuning at resolutions ranging from 144 to 720, with outputs spanning classification labels, detection bounding boxes, segmentation masks, and generated images depending on the downstream task.

## Method

Compass-RoPE replaces the cartesian frequency vector (ωx, ωy) in Mix-RoPE with a polar parameterization ω = ρ(cos φ, sin φ), explicitly decoupling frequency scale from orientation. Orientation angles φ are initialized uniformly over [0, 2π) to guarantee isotropic directional coverage at the start of training, and the phase for each token is computed as θ = ρ·r(p)·cos(ϑ(p) − φ), where (r, ϑ) are the polar coordinates of the centered token position. To further prevent within-head directional collapse during training, a unitary DFT is applied across the D_pair frequency pairs within each attention head, so that each mixed frequency component nests multiple angular patterns, enriching angular expressiveness without adding parameters. The method introduces negligible computational overhead (identical FLOPs to Mix-RoPE) and is validated across ViT-Small, ViT-Base, Swin-Tiny, and DiT backbones.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K with ViT-Small pre-trained at 224², Compass-RoPE achieves 77.6% average Top-1 accuracy across nine evaluation resolutions (144–720), outperforming Mix-RoPE (75.4%), ViT with APE (74.8%), and 2D-RoPE (73.6%). The advantage is most pronounced at high resolutions: at 720, Compass-RoPE reaches 70.2% versus Mix-RoPE's 62.2% (+8.0 pts) and ViT(APE)'s 66.8% (+3.4 pts). On downstream tasks, Compass-RoPE also leads: 79.5% mAP on Pascal VOC (vs. 79.1% Mix-RoPE, 78.3% APE) and 43.4% mIoU on ADE20K (vs. 43.2% Mix-RoPE, 41.1% APE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compass-RoPE consistently outperforms all RoPE-based baselines. Against Mix-RoPE (Heo et al., ECCV 2024), the closest competitor, Compass-RoPE gains +2.2 pts average and up to +8.0 pts at 720 resolution; Mix-RoPE actually falls below ViT(APE) at the largest evaluation sizes. Against 2D-RoPE (same source), the gap widens to +4.0 pts average and +10.3 pts at 720. Among recent visual RoPE variants on ImageNet-100 (Table 8), Compass-RoPE (72.9% AVG) beats Spherical RoPE (71.7%), LieRE (72.0%), and ComRoPE (72.4%). The non-RoPE method LookHere (Fuller et al., NeurIPS 2024) achieves stronger high-resolution extrapolation (70.3% at 720 vs. 67.8%), but Compass-RoPE is better at lower resolutions; their combination yields the best overall score (74.3% AVG), confirming the methods are complementary. On the hierarchical Swin-Tiny backbone (Appendix G), Compass-RoPE (76.6% AVG) also surpasses both the original RPB (75.7%) and Mix-RoPE (74.8%), indicating the isotropy benefit transfers beyond plain ViT.

## Relation to Prior Work

Compass-RoPE directly extends Mix-RoPE (Heo et al., ECCV 2024), which introduced learnable 2D rotary frequencies for ViTs, by replacing its cartesian (ωx, ωy) frequency parameterization with a polar (ρ, φ) form and adding intra-head unitary DFT mixing. The underlying RoPE rotation mechanism is inherited from RoFormer (Su et al., 2024), and the paper positions itself within the broader lineage of resolution-robust positional encodings for ViTs (APE interpolation, relative position bias, CPE/PEG, LookHere). The key conceptual departure is the diagnosis that strided patch tokenization induces an axial spectral bias that systematically skews learned frequency orientations toward axis-aligned directions, and the remedy of enforcing isotropic directional coverage through uniform angle initialization and DFT-based angular enrichment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Resolution extrapolation (training at 224², testing at up to 720²) is a practical bottleneck for deploying ViTs at variable input sizes, and Compass-RoPE offers a nearly zero-overhead fix that consistently improves high-resolution accuracy by 3–8 points over Mix-RoPE. The identification of axial spectral bias as the root cause of frequency anisotropy provides a useful diagnostic lens for other learnable positional encoding designs. However, validation on larger backbones (ViT-Base/Large) is limited to ImageNet-100 rather than full ImageNet-1K due to compute constraints, and the paper acknowledges that full-scale confirmation at ViT-Large remains open. The complementary combination with non-RoPE methods like LookHere suggests directional isotropy is an orthogonal axis of improvement that can stack with other extrapolation techniques.
