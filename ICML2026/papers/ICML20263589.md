## Problem

Existing data-free model merging methods (e.g., TIES, DARE, WUDI) select parameters to keep using element-wise heuristics—magnitude trimming and sign election—that treat each weight as an isolated decision variable. This localist bias ignores the compositional hierarchy of deep networks, where a parameter's functional importance depends on its position within cross-layer connectivity pathways, leading to inadvertent removal of structurally critical weights and persistent task interference in the merged model.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pretrained base model θ₀ and N independently fine-tuned expert checkpoints {θₙ} (or equivalently their task vectors τₙ = θₙ − θ₀), with no access to any training, validation, or unlabeled calibration data during merging. It outputs a single merged checkpoint θ* = θ₀ + Σₙ τₙ^(T) that performs across all N tasks simultaneously. For LoRA experts, the input is the set of low-rank factor pairs {Aₙ, Bₙ} per adapted layer, and the output is a merged adapter set preserving the original rank-r parameterization.

## Method

SA-Merging reformulates SynFlow's connectivity score Rₙ(θ₀, τₙ) = 1ᵀ(∏ₗ|θ₀ˡ + τₙˡ|)1 as a data-free proxy for each task vector's end-to-end signal transmission capacity, then computes a saliency score Sₙ = (∂Rₙ/∂τₙ) ⊗ (Σᵢ τᵢ) that couples structural sensitivity (how much a parameter contributes to global connectivity) with cross-expert agreement (implicit sign election against the aggregate merge direction). An iterative pruning loop (T=10 iterations, per-step ratio p=0.2, retaining ~10% of parameters) progressively masks low-saliency coordinates per tensor, so that structurally redundant or sign-conflicting updates are removed while preserving end-to-end pathways. For LoRA, the method decomposes each update into rank-1 components and assigns a rank-wise saliency sₙ,ₖ = |γₙ,ₖ · ηₙ,ₖ| (connectivity importance × cross-expert agreement along that direction), pruning entire rank components via diagonal masking of A and B factors to maintain the low-rank structure without materializing ΔW.

## Results

On the 8-task vision suite with CLIP ViT-L/14, SA-Merging achieves 93.4% average top-1 accuracy, nearly matching traditional multi-task learning (93.5%) and individual experts (94.2%) without any joint training or data access. On the smaller ViT-B/32 backbone, SA-Merging reaches 85.9% average accuracy, outperforming the best data-free baseline WUDI-Merging (85.2%) and data-assisted AdaMerging++ (81.1%). On the 8-task GLUE benchmark, SA-Merging achieves 87.1% (RoBERTa-Base) and 90.2% (RoBERTa-Large) average normalized score, surpassing WUDI-Merging by 1.8 and 1.4 points respectively. For LoRA merging on Flan-T5-base, SA-Merging attains 82.5% average, and on Qwen-14B it reaches 56.33% average across four diverse tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SA-Merging consistently outperforms all 11 baselines across every evaluated setting. Against the strongest data-free competitor WUDI-Merging, SA-Merging gains +0.8 points on ViT-L/14 (93.4 vs 92.6), +0.7 on ViT-B/32 (85.9 vs 85.2), +1.8 on RoBERTa-Base GLUE (87.1 vs 85.3), and +1.4 on RoBERTa-Large (90.2 vs 88.8). The advantage over magnitude-based heuristics is substantial: TIES-Merging trails by 7.4 points on ViT-L/14 (86.0 vs 93.4) and 22.4 points on RoBERTa-Base GLUE (64.7 vs 87.1), while Task Arithmetic lags by 8.9 and 19.3 points respectively. Data-assisted methods that require calibration data (AdaMerging++ at 91.0, Rep.Surgery at 89.0 on ViT-L/14) are also surpassed by SA-Merging's strictly data-free procedure. On LoRA merging, SA-Merging edges out WUDI by +1.3 points on Flan-T5-base GLUE (82.5 vs 81.2) and +0.64 on Qwen-14B (56.33 vs 55.69), confirming the rank-wise saliency extension generalizes to larger models. The only setting where the gap narrows is Qwen-14B LoRA, where the low-rank parameterization and initialization noise limit the benefit of connectivity-based pruning.

## Relation to Prior Work

SA-Merging extends SynFlow's connectivity-based saliency (originally designed for single-model structural pruning) into the data-free model merging setting, where it is applied to task vectors relative to a shared base model rather than to a single network's weights. The task-vector formulation from Ilharco et al. (2023) provides the merging substrate, but the key departure from prior data-free methods (TIES, DARE, WUDI, PCB) is the rejection of the element-wise independence assumption: SA-Merging scores each parameter by its contribution to end-to-end cross-layer signal flow and modulates this with cross-expert directional agreement, rather than relying on magnitude or sign heuristics alone. The iterative pruning schedule is borrowed from SynFlow's progressive sparsification, and the LoRA extension introduces a rank-wise decomposition that preserves low-rank structure during masking.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By matching or exceeding data-assisted methods (e.g., AdaMerging++) and nearly closing the gap to joint multi-task training on ViT-L/14 (93.4% vs. 93.5% MTL) without any calibration or validation data, SA-Merging demonstrates that structural connectivity signals can substitute for empirical data feedback in merging. The consistent gains across vision (CLIP ViT), language (RoBERTa/GLUE), and LoRA (Flan-T5, Qwen-14B) settings suggest the formulation is architecture-agnostic. Open questions include whether the connectivity surrogate correlates well with downstream accuracy for non-Transformer architectures, how computational cost scales to hundreds of experts or very large models, and whether incorporating small amounts of calibration data could further tighten the data-free-to-data-assisted gap.
