## Problem

Existing VLA failure-handling methods suffer from two interrelated limitations: coarse-grained failure correction that lacks precise geometric localization (leading to accumulated deviations and repeated failures) and unreliable failure prevention that relies on qualitative VLM self-reflection without explicit trajectory modeling (leading to missed hazards or unnecessary interventions). These weaknesses make VLA models brittle when deployed in novel tasks and environments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262819/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB observation image and a text task instruction at each iteration. It produces a policy code block that directly invokes parameterized control-primitive APIs to execute the next action step, along with introspective Embodied Chain-of-Thoughts (ECoTs) derived from program execution traces (intermediate variable dictionaries from visual programming and trajectory modeling). The ECoTs are not free-form VLM text but are strictly rule-constrained conversions of executable program outputs, ensuring they are grounded in metric-level geometric evidence.

## Method

NeurVLA is a neural-symbolic framework with three sequential components per iteration. First, Observation-Grounded Failure Correction (C_OG) instantiates an enhanced visual programming language augmented with geometry functions in the Omni3D coordinate system; when 3D spatial reasoning is needed, it invokes 3D-MOOD for monocular open-set 3D object detection to infer precise object and gripper coordinates, then verifies action completion and injects metric-level corrective steps. Second, Trajectory-Modeling Failure Prevention (P_TM) invokes ATM to predict action-induced motion trajectories, visualizes them on the observation, and verifies via the enhanced VP whether the predicted trajectories cause collisions or failures, iteratively refining the plan until safe. Third, Reasoning-Guided Action Learning (L_RG) converts the executed programs and their intermediate variables into introspective ECoTs via a VLM-based generator with strict rule-based constraints, then trains the VLA backbone using SFT on successful samples and contrastive learning on paired failed/successful iterations across three formats (Think, Act, Follow), internalizing failure-handling as an intrinsic model capability rather than an external intervention.

## Results

On LIBERO, NeurVLA achieves an average success rate of 96.1% (NORA backbone) and 96.6% (InstructVLA backbone), improving by 0.9 and 2.4 points over the strongest regression/classification and flow-matching baselines respectively. On SimplerEnv, NeurVLA reaches 73.7% and 83.8% average success, outperforming the best baselines by ~10.9% (regression) and ~12.7% (flow-matching) on WidowX. On CALVIN, NeurVLA extends average completion length to 4.09 and 4.17 tasks, surpassing the strongest baselines (FailSafe at 3.98, UniCoD at 4.11). In real-world experiments, NeurVLA improves average success by 51.9 points over the NORA backbone and 34.7 points over π0.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeurVLA consistently outperforms all 12 baselines across LIBERO, SimplerEnv, and CALVIN. On LIBERO, it beats the strongest regression baseline UniVLA (95.2) by 0.9 points and the strongest flow-matching baseline π0 (94.2) by 2.4 points in average success rate, with the largest gap on the Long suite (92.2 vs. 92.0 for UniVLA, 92.4 vs. 85.2 for π0). On SimplerEnv, NeurVLA surpasses FPC-VLA (74.2) by 9.6 points and SpatialVLA (56.7) by 27.1 points in average success, with particularly large gains on WidowX tasks (e.g., Put Eggplant: 91.2 vs. 75.0 for FPC-VLA). On CALVIN, NeurVLA exceeds FailSafe (3.98) by 0.11 in average completion length and UniCoD (4.11) by 0.06, while also achieving the highest 5-task success rate (69.6% vs. 67.0% for UniCoD). Among failure-handling methods specifically, NeurVLA outperforms both CollabVLA and FailSafe across all three benchmarks and both action-modeling paradigms, confirming that its programmatic, geometry-grounded approach to failure correction and prevention is more effective than VLM-based semantic reflection or externally invoked recovery models.

## Relation to Prior Work

NeurVLA extends the visual programming paradigm (Gupta & Kembhavi, 2023) by augmenting it with 3D geometric functions and integrating it with trajectory prediction (ATM) and monocular 3D detection (3D-MOOD) to ground failure analysis in executable, coordinate-level reasoning. It departs from prior failure-handling methods (FPC-VLA, CollabVLA, FailSafe) that rely on VLM-based semantic judgments for correction or prevention, instead using programmatic verification to produce metric-level corrective actions and explicit trajectory-based hazard checking. The ECoT framework (Zawalski et al., 2024) is repurposed here not merely as an inference-time reasoning aid but as a training signal: program execution traces are converted into introspective ECoTs that are used for SFT and contrastive learning to internalize failure-handling into the VLA backbone itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a critical deployment gap: VLA models that achieve high success on seen tasks remain brittle in open-ended environments where execution failures are inevitable. By internalizing failure correction and prevention as intrinsic model capabilities rather than relying on external recovery modules, NeurVLA offers a path toward more robust and generalizable robotic deployment. Open questions include the dependency on external specialist models (3D-MOOD, ATM) that may not generalize to all embodiments or sensor configurations, the computational overhead of the programmatic pipeline at inference time, and whether the contrastive learning signal from paired failure/success iterations scales to more diverse failure modes. The finding that program-derived ECoTs substantially outperform LLM-generated ones suggests a broader principle about grounding reasoning supervision in verifiable execution traces.
