## Problem

Long Chain-of-Thought reasoning dramatically improves LLM performance on complex tasks but incurs quadratic attention cost and linear KV-cache growth, making extended reasoning impractical at scale. Prior compression approaches (token eviction, fixed-size chunking, heuristic summarization) impose external schedules or rule-based segmentation that disrupt natural reasoning flow and do not teach the model to self-regulate compression. The paper asks whether an LLM can learn, end-to-end, when and how to summarize its own reasoning so that the compressed context is sufficient for continued problem-solving.

## Input / Output

The model takes a text math problem as input and produces a structured reasoning trace in which each detailed reasoning segment is followed by a concise step summary enclosed in <step>...</step> tags, concluding with a final answer. Two special tokens are added to the vocabulary to delimit steps, and generation halts at </step> or EOS. In Fold mode, after each summary is emitted the detailed segment is pruned from the KV cache, so the model's visible context for the next step is only the original query plus the sequence of prior summaries (reducing peak token length by ~50–60% versus full-context CoT).

## Method

Accordion-Thinking is a two-stage training pipeline. First, a cold-start SFT stage fine-tunes the base model on ~3,900 synthetically generated Accordion-format examples (long CoT traces rewritten by DeepSeek-V3.2 into segmented steps with summaries, filtered by structural and length rules). Second, RL training using GRPO (without KL penalty, following Dr. GRPO) is conducted in three regimes: Unfold (full context), Fold (dynamic context pruning triggered at each </step> tag during rollout, discarding the detailed segment and retaining only summaries), and Mixed (both modes interleaved per training step). The critical mechanism is that in Fold mode the model must encode all necessary reasoning state into the summary before the detailed trace is pruned; if the summary is lossy, subsequent steps fail and the trajectory receives zero reward. The key empirical finding is a 'Gap-Vanishing' phenomenon: the accuracy gap between Fold and Unfold modes (initially ~27–42 points) progressively narrows and disappears during Mixed-RL training, demonstrating that the model learns lossless self-compression. The resulting model achieves ~3× throughput under 48 GB GPU memory while matching or exceeding full-context CoT accuracy, and the step summaries serve as a human-readable substitute for the full solution.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-Math-7B, Mix-RL in Fold mode achieves a macro average of 52.8% (Pass@1, Avg@32) across AIME24, AIME25, MATH-500, AMC23, and MinervaMath, matching or slightly exceeding the full-context Unfold-RL baseline (52.2%) while operating with ~50–60% fewer peak tokens. On Qwen3-4B-Base, Mix-RL (Fold) reaches 52.1% macro, again on par with Unfold-RL (52.0%). Under a 48 GB GPU memory constraint, Fold-mode inference delivers a ~3× throughput speedup (5,888 vs. 1,483 tokens/s) compared to standard Unfold CoT, with peak sequence length reduced from ~12.3k to ~5.7k tokens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to H2O (heavy-hitter KV eviction), Accordion-Thinking's Mix-RL in Fold mode outperforms by 7.9 points macro on Qwen2.5-Math-7B (52.8 vs. 44.9) and 7.4 points on Qwen3-4B-Base (52.1 vs. 44.7), as H2O's heuristic token eviction discards reasoning-critical tokens without compensatory summarization. Against LightThinker (step-compression via distillation), the gap is 5.0 and 4.5 points macro respectively, reflecting LightThinker's reliance on a separate compression model rather than learned self-summarization. DeleThink (fixed-size chunking with half-chunk eviction) is the closest competitor, achieving 52.3 and 51.5 macro, but it still lags Mix-RL by 0.5 and 0.6 points and does not produce human-readable step summaries. Critically, both Fold-RL and Mix-RL in compressed Fold mode match or slightly exceed the full-context Unfold-RL baseline (the 'gap-vanishing' phenomenon), whereas all three external-compression baselines (H2O, LightThinker, DeleThink) show measurable degradation when forced into compressed inference, confirming that learned self-regulated summarization via RL is necessary for lossless compression.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the GRPO/Dr. GRPO reinforcement learning framework for verifiable-reward training and borrows the data synthesis pipeline concept from InftyThink, but departs fundamentally from prior efficient-reasoning work (H2O, LightThinker, DeleThink) by treating context compression as a *learned, self-regulated* behavior rather than an external heuristic or fixed chunking rule. Where prior methods impose compression schedules externally or use attention-based token eviction at inference time, Accordion-Thinking integrates summarization into the reasoning loop itself via RL, so the model learns *when* and *what* to compress. The key conceptual shift is from 'compression as a separate engineering stage' to 'compression as a skill co-optimized with reasoning.'

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3× throughput gain at 48 GB with maintained accuracy is a practically significant efficiency win for deploying long-CoT reasoning models under memory constraints, and the structured step summaries offer a free interpretability bonus. However, the evaluation is confined to mathematical reasoning (plus two OOD knowledge benchmarks) and two small model sizes (4B, 7B), leaving open whether the gap-vanishing phenomenon and compression quality hold at larger scales or for open-ended, non-verifiable reasoning tasks. A key open question is whether the learned summaries remain faithful when the reasoning involves backtracking or multi-branch exploration, which the current step-by-step format may not capture well.
