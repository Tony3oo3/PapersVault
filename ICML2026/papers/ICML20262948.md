## Problem

Reconstructing unified continuous spatiotemporal dynamics from sparse, non-consecutive, and unpaired point cloud snapshots remains unsolved: scene flow methods require dense paired sequences, Optimal Transport approaches compute only discrete static matchings without continuous velocity fields, and propagation-based Neural ODE/CDE models accumulate errors through frame-to-frame Markovian transitions. Additionally, most existing methods focus solely on geometric alignment and cannot jointly model multi-attribute dynamics such as gene expression, cell type specification, and population changes (birth, death, proliferation).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262948/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of N sparse, non-consecutive, unpaired point cloud snapshots at irregular time points, where each snapshot contains a variable number of points with spatial coordinates, continuous gene expression values, and discrete cell type labels. The output is a continuous latent trajectory z(t) that, when decoded at any arbitrary time t, simultaneously produces point cloud geometry, cell type logits, gene expression profiles, and per-point existence logits for population dynamics. The model operates as a non-causal smoother for retrospective reconstruction rather than a real-time causal forecaster, and handles variable point cardinality across time via an existence predictor rather than fixed-size outputs.

## Method

FlowCloud is a variational Neural ODE (VAE-ODE) generative framework whose key architectural insight is aggregating all observed snapshots into a single global context vector via a Point Swin Transformer encoder and a sequence Transformer, which then parameterizes the initial state z(t_0) of a Neural ODE dz/dt = f_θ(z(t),t), thereby avoiding the cumulative errors of frame-to-frame propagation. The decoder uses N_max learnable slot queries (inspired by slot attention) that cross-attend to the projected latent state, producing variable-cardinality point clouds through four parallel MLP heads for geometry, cell type, gene expression, and existence. Training is supervised end-to-end with a composite loss: Sinkhorn distance for global distribution alignment, Chamfer distance for local geometric fidelity, a hybrid trajectory consistency loss that aligns the learned velocity field with empirical velocities computed via spatial+feature nearest-neighbor matching, latent acceleration regularization for smooth dynamics, and focal/MSE/BCE losses for multi-attribute prediction. The variational component introduces a Gaussian posterior over z(t_0) conditioned on the global context, regularized by KL divergence with annealed β.

## Results

FlowCloud achieves the best Chamfer Distance (CD) across all four datasets: 0.1369 (Simulation 2D), 0.2261 (Ambystoma 2D), 0.4772 (Drosophila 3D), and 0.0268 (Human 3D), with the Human 3D CD representing a 43.0% reduction over the best baseline (SPCMNet, 0.0470). On attribute fidelity, FlowCloud leads in Cell Type Accuracy (CTA) and Spearman's Correlation (SCC) on all biological datasets, reaching CTA 0.897 and SCC 0.627 on Simulation 2D. For short-term extrapolation (10% future horizon on Simulation 2D), FlowCloud achieves CD 0.1119 and SCC 0.569, substantially outperforming all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlowCloud outperforms all seven baselines on CD across every dataset, with the largest margin on Simulation 2D (0.1369 vs. SPCMNet's 0.2312, a 41% reduction) and Human 3D (0.0268 vs. SPCMNet's 0.0470, 43% reduction). On OT, FlowCloud leads on Simulation 2D (0.0147 vs. Spateo's 0.0179) and Drosophila 3D (0.2238 vs. Moscot's 0.2540), but DifFlow3D achieves a lower OT on Ambystoma 2D (0.1748 vs. 0.2868), indicating that the Sinkhorn-based global alignment is not uniformly dominant. Among scene-flow baselines (FlowNet3D, DifFlow3D, MeteorNet, SPCMNet, VoteFlow), FlowCloud consistently wins on CD by 20–90% depending on dataset, while OT results are more mixed. Among OT-based biological baselines (Spateo, Moscot), FlowCloud dominates on CD (e.g., 0.1369 vs. 1.6060 for Spateo on Sim 2D) and on all attribute metrics (CTA 0.897 vs. 0.572 for Spateo; SCC 0.627 vs. 0.503). The NicheFlow comparison (Appendix F.1) further confirms FlowCloud's advantage in unified geometry+attribute reconstruction (CD 0.1369 vs. 0.2352; SCC 0.627 vs. 0.412 on Sim 2D).

## Relation to Prior Work

FlowCloud sits at the intersection of three established lines of work: dense scene flow estimation (FlowNet3D, SPCMNet), discrete Optimal Transport-based trajectory inference (Moscot, Spateo), and continuous-time Neural ODE dynamics (Chen et al., 2018; TrajectoryNet, stVCR). It departs from scene flow by eliminating the requirement for dense, strictly paired frames, from OT methods by learning a continuous velocity field rather than static discrete couplings, and from propagation-based Neural ODE/CDE models by replacing frame-to-frame Markovian transitions with a single global context vector that initializes the ODE, thereby avoiding cumulative drift. The variational (VAE) formulation and the multi-head decoder for joint geometry/attribute/population prediction are borrowed from the generative modeling literature but applied here in a unified spatiotemporal setting that no prior method addresses simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first end-to-end framework that jointly reconstructs continuous geometry, discrete cell types, gene expression, and population dynamics from sparse unpaired snapshots, which is the natural data regime in developmental biology (e.g., spatial transcriptomics with irregular sampling). Key open questions remain: long-horizon extrapolation still suffers from Neural ODE drift, the simplified existence predictor does not enforce mass conservation (unbalanced OT would be more physically grounded), and scaling to million-point clouds or very long sequences is computationally challenging. The finding that gene features must be included in trajectory matching to recover accurate geometry (w_spatial=1.0 degrades CD) suggests a broader principle that multimodal coupling is essential for physical plausibility in biological dynamics, which could inform future generative models beyond this specific architecture.
