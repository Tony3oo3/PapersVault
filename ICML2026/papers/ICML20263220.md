## Problem

Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) excel at complex mathematical reasoning but perform significantly worse on factual questions because their step-by-step reasoning behavior introduces unnecessary associations and unverified information that hinder direct knowledge retrieval. While token-conditional generation can transiently shift an LRM's behavior at inference time by conditioning on a short prefix from a direct-answer response, this adaptation is unstable, requires external prefix examples, and does not persist without continuous conditioning. The paper asks how to internalize this exposed behavioral plasticity into stable, autonomously invocable behavioral patterns without degrading the model's existing reasoning capabilities.

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes factual query-answer pairs (10K) and math query-answer pairs (10K) alongside pre-generated token prefixes (3–8 tokens) extracted from an instruct model's direct-answer response. The token prefix is constructed as the  token concatenated with the first k−2 tokens of the instruct model's output, which are force-appended to the LRM's generation to steer it toward a direct-answer trajectory. The output is a standard LRM response: a  reasoning block followed by a  summary containing the final answer, where the emergent behavior for factual problems is a 'recalibrative reasoning' pattern—repeatedly generating and revising answer candidates before committing to a final summary.

## Method

ToCoRL (Token-Conditional Reinforcement Learning) is an on-policy RL framework that incorporates token-conditional generation directly into the rollout stage via a mixed policy π_mix = (π + λ·π_TC)/(1+λ), where π_TC is the policy induced by force-appending the instruct model's token prefix. The optimization objective combines a standard REINFORCE policy-gradient term with a customized KL divergence against a reference policy π̃_TC that upweights correct responses and downweights incorrect ones from the token-conditional rollouts, providing correctness-aware guided exploration. A key theoretical result (Theorem 4.2) shows this KL term is gradient-equivalent to a tractable policy-gradient-style objective over π_TC samples, and Theorem 4.4 proves the practical surrogate (using a single mixed-policy rollout) lower-bounds the original two-policy objective. The KL coefficient λ regulates the balance between exploration (injected target behavior) and exploitation (policy improvement), and the framework is instantiated with LLM-as-a-judge binary rewards for factual problems, jointly trained with GRPO on math problems to preserve reasoning capability.

## Results

Starting from Qwen3-30B-A3B-2507-Thinking (SimpleQA 18.9, AIME'25 80.5), ToCoRL improves factual accuracy on SimpleQA to 28.3 (+9.4) and AA-Omniscience to 18.6 (+2.5), while simultaneously improving complex math on AIME'25 to 81.5 (+1.0) and AIME'24 to 89.0 (+4.2). The method induces an emergent 'recalibrative reasoning' behavior where the model first gives a direct answer, then selectively revises it, reducing median response length from 1128 to 477 tokens on SimpleQA. Ablations confirm robustness: performance varies by less than 1.1 points across λ ∈ {0.5, 1, 2} and prefix length k ∈ {6, 7, 8}, and using a smaller prefix provider (Qwen2.5-14B) still yields 27.8 on SimpleQA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ToCoRL outperforms all three baselines on factual benchmarks. Against GRPO (the standard RLVR algorithm), ToCoRL gains +4.7 on SimpleQA (28.3 vs 23.6) and +1.9 on AA-Omniscience (18.6 vs 16.7), while matching or slightly exceeding GRPO on math (AIME'25: 81.5 vs 81.4; AIME'24: 89.0 vs 89.5). Against Adaptive-Thinking (length-penalty GRPO), ToCoRL leads by +4.4 on SimpleQA and +2.6 on AIME'25 (81.5 vs 78.9), showing that merely penalizing long reasoning degrades math capability without achieving the same factual gains. Against Instruct-Tuning GRPO (prompt-based adaptive reasoning), ToCoRL wins on every metric, with the largest gap on SimpleQA (+7.2) and AA-Omniscience (+3.8), demonstrating that prompt-level instructions are insufficient to stably induce the desired behavioral shift. Notably, ToCoRL is the only method that improves both factual and math scores simultaneously, whereas Adaptive-Thinking and Instruct-Tuning GRPO sacrifice math performance (AIME'25 drops to 78.9 and 80.4 respectively) while gaining less on factual tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the REINFORCE policy-gradient objective and the GRPO/RLVR framework for LLM training, but departs from both by incorporating token-conditional generation into the rollout stage via a customized KL divergence that shapes exploration toward desired behaviors. It positions itself against parameter-update steering methods (SFT, RLHF, DPO) that merely elicit pre-existing patterns, and against prompt-based approaches (instruct-tuning, adaptive-thinking length penalties) that lack stability and precision. The conceptual lineage connects to KL-constrained exploration in traditional RL (Rajeswaran et al., 2017), but the specific mechanism—using a token-conditioned policy as a reference distribution rather than an expert—is novel to the LLM setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central claim—that performance gaps between specialized LLMs often reflect behavioral patterns rather than knowledge gaps—has broad implications for how the field approaches model specialization, suggesting unified models can be 'programmed' with multiple behaviors rather than trained separately. The transferability result (SFT with ToCoRL-generated responses outperforms SFT with instruct-model responses) offers a practical recipe for building versatile reasoning models. However, the evidence is confined to a single model family (Qwen3) and one task pair (math + factual QA), leaving open whether the token-conditional plasticity mechanism generalizes to other behavioral axes (e.g., code generation, multi-turn dialogue) or model architectures. The reliance on LLM-as-a-judge for reward signals also introduces a potential vulnerability to reward hacking that the authors acknowledge but do not fully mitigate.
