## Problem

Early-exit neural networks (EENNs) rely on confidence thresholds to decide when to stop computation, and the prevailing assumption is that better calibration of intermediate classifiers improves the cost-accuracy trade-off. This paper shows theoretically and empirically that calibration is fundamentally misaligned with EENN design: it is a per-exit, isolated notion that ignores the sequential structure of exits and the computational cost of continuing inference, and miscalibrated EENNs can actually outperform calibrated ones.

## Input / Output

The EENN backbone (ResNet-34, ViT-Tiny/Small, EfficientNet-B2, MSDNet) consumes standard RGB images (64×64 for CIFAR-100/TinyImageNet, 224×224 for ImageNet-1k) and produces a class label at whichever intermediate classifier triggers an exit. The proposed confidence-correction module is a lightweight 2-layer MLP (hidden dim 128) that takes the top-k class confidences from the current exit (k=5 by default) and, in the 'history' variant, the top-k confidences from all previous exits as input, outputting a single scalar stopping probability that replaces the raw classifier confidence for threshold-based exit decisions.

## Method

The paper introduces Early-Exit Failure Prediction (EEFP), which redefines the exit criterion from 'is this prediction confident?' to 'is exiting now optimal given that no subsequent exit would succeed?'. Formally, the optimal stopping target at exit m is 1 if the current prediction is correct OR all remaining exits would be wrong, and 0 otherwise. A lightweight meta-classifier (2-layer MLP) is trained post-hoc with binary cross-entropy to predict this target from top-k confidences (optionally augmented with the history of prior exits' confidences), and its output replaces the raw classifier confidence for threshold-based exiting. Theoretically, the paper proves that canonically calibrated EENNs must be trivial (all exits identical), that confidence calibration does not bound log-loss excess risk, and that a group-conditional anytime property requires the per-exit grouping functions to form a refinement chain—properties that standard calibration provides no guarantee of. Empirically, the EEFP-motivated correction consistently improves cost-accuracy trade-offs over temperature calibration across five architectures and four datasets, while the proposed EEFP score (AUROC of stopping decisions) reliably ranks EENN quality where ECE does not.

## Results

Across three model-dataset combinations (MSDNet-CIFAR-100, ViT-Small-ImageNet-1k, EfficientNet-B2-ImageNet-1k), the proposed EEFP confidence correction consistently achieves the best cost-accuracy trade-off. For example, on MSDNet-CIFAR-100 at 25% FLOPs, the method reaches 73.27% accuracy versus 71.92% for temperature-calibrated and 71.38% for the baseline. The EEFP score (AUROC of stopping decisions) reliably tracks end-to-end performance (e.g., 0.86 for the corrected MSDNet vs. 0.83 calibrated vs. 0.82 baseline), whereas ECE misleadingly favors the calibrated model (0.02 vs. 0.15). The computational overhead of the correction module is negligible (~0.001% additional FLOPs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is temperature calibration (Guo et al., 2017), applied independently to each exit head. The proposed EEFP confidence correction outperforms temperature calibration on all three model-dataset combinations at every FLOPs budget: on MSDNet-CIFAR-100 it gains +1.35 pp at 25% FLOPs (73.27 vs. 71.92) and +0.36 pp at 50% FLOPs (75.66 vs. 75.30); on ViT-Small-ImageNet-1k it gains +0.78 pp at 25% and +0.41 pp at 50%; on EfficientNet-B2-ImageNet-1k it gains +0.80 pp at 25% and +0.47 pp at 50%. Notably, temperature calibration reduces ECE to ~0.02 (near-perfect calibration) yet still underperforms the EEFP-corrected model, which has a higher ECE of ~0.15. This demonstrates the paper's central claim: lower ECE does not translate to better early-exit performance, while the EEFP score correctly ranks the corrected model above the calibrated one. The paper also shows that deliberately overconfident (decalibrated) models can outperform calibrated ones on cost-accuracy, further undermining calibration as a performance proxy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the failure prediction framework of Corbière et al. (2019), repurposing it from a single-model confidence-estimation task to a sequential, structure-aware stopping problem for early-exit networks. It departs from the dominant EENN calibration literature (Qendro et al., 2021; Pacheco et al., 2023; Meronen et al., 2024; Mofakhami et al., 2024), which treats per-exit confidence calibration as the primary lever for improving cost-accuracy trade-offs, by showing theoretically that calibration is fundamentally misaligned with the sequential, compute-aware nature of EENNs. The theoretical analysis draws on the general calibration framework of Dawid (1982) and Vaicenavicius et al. (2019), while the anytime-computation perspective connects to Zilberstein (1996) and Jazbec et al. (2023). The learning-to-defer literature (Mozannar & Sontag, 2020; Verma et al., 2023) provides a parallel assumption that future model quality need not be fully encoded, which the authors adapt to the EENN setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges a widely-held assumption in the EENN community—that improving classifier calibration improves overall adaptive-computation performance—and provides a clean theoretical explanation for why this fails (confidence calibration controls only one output coordinate, leaving log-loss excess risk unbounded). The EEFP score offers a more reliable diagnostic for comparing EENNs, and the lightweight confidence-correction procedure is a practical drop-in replacement for calibration. Key limitations include the absence of a method for imposing the inter-exit grouping-function refinement structure required for true anytime computation, and the abstract nature of the grouping functions in the theory. The authors note potential applicability to adaptive computation in LLMs and chain-of-thought reasoning, which could broaden the impact beyond vision.
