## Problem

Self-play alignment methods like SPPO formulate preference optimization as a two-player game but largely lack explicit regularization to the reference policy, making them susceptible to over-optimization when the preference model is misspecified. While Nash-MD incorporates reverse KL divergence, its training protocol is intrinsically tied to that specific divergence and requires sampling from a geometric mixture policy, leaving the broader question of which regularization strategies are effective in self-play unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264393/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes text prompts (from the Ultrafeedback dataset, ~60K prompts split into three subsets per iteration) and produces fine-tuned LLM policies that generate text responses. During training, K=5 responses are sampled per prompt from the current policy, and a 0.4B PairRM preference model scores all pairwise comparisons to estimate the utility. The reference policy μ (the base instruct model) is fixed throughout training and serves as the regularization anchor.

## Method

RSPO defines a unified loss as the sum of a mean-squared self-play term (derived from Multiplicative Weights Update / exponentiated gradient descent) and a weighted plug-and-play regularization term λR(π_θ, μ), where R can be any divergence (forward KL, reverse KL, chi-squared, or linear combinations). The update direction G is set to P(y ≻ π_t) with baseline B=1/2, making the self-play component identical to SPPO; regularization is added as a single extra term without modifying existing loss functions or hyperparameters. Theoretically, RSPO is shown to be equivalent to Generalized Magnetic Mirror Descent (GMMD), a novel extension of Magnetic Mirror Descent that accommodates general regularizers beyond reverse KL, yielding last-iterate convergence to the Nash equilibrium of the regularized preference game. Empirically, combining forward and reverse KL regularization (IS-For.+Rev.) achieves the best AlpacaEval-2 LCWR, with forward KL reducing response length and reverse KL improving raw win rate through its mode-seeking behavior.

## Results

RSPO with IS-Forward+Reverse KL regularization achieves 35.44% LCWR on AlpacaEval-2.0 with Mistral-7B (vs. 28.53% for SPPO Iter3), 43.66% with LLaMA-3-8B (vs. 38.77%), and 51.83% with Gemma-2-2B (vs. 50.54%). RSPO also achieves consistently superior performance on Arena-Hard-v0.1, MT-Bench, ArmoRM, and response diversity (Self-BLEU). Notably, RSPO-LLaMA-8B paired with PairRM-0.5B (8.5B total parameters) matches SPPO-LLaMA-GPM (16B total parameters), demonstrating a 47% parameter reduction while maintaining competitive performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RSPO consistently outperforms SPPO across all three base models: +6.91 pp LCWR on Mistral-7B (35.44% vs. 28.53%), +4.89 pp on LLaMA-3-8B (43.66% vs. 38.77%), and +1.29 pp on Gemma-2-2B (51.83% vs. 50.54%). Against Snorkel (iterative DPO, Tran et al. 2023), RSPO gains +9.05 pp LCWR on Mistral-7B (35.44% vs. 26.39%), and also surpasses the strong offline method SimPO by +3.34 pp (35.44% vs. 32.1%). The unregularized SPPO baseline degrades after iteration 3 due to over-optimization (LCWR drops from 29.17% at iter 3 to 22.94% at iter 8), while RSPO with regularization maintains or improves performance across iterations. In terms of parameter efficiency, RSPO-LLaMA-8B with the 0.5B PairRM (8.5B total) achieves performance comparable to SPPO-LLaMA with the 8B GPM preference model (16B total), a 47% reduction in model size. On AlpacaEval-2.0, the RSPO-trained Mistral-7B (LCWR 35.44%) also outperforms GPT-4 0314 (35.3%) and LLaMA-3-70B-Instruct (34.4%), and matches Claude 3 Opus on raw win rate (38.31% vs. 29.1%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-play alignment line initiated by SPPO (Wu et al., 2024) and Nash-MD (Munos et al., 2023), which frame preference optimization as a two-player game solved via Multiplicative Weights Update or Mirror Descent. The key departure is that existing regularized methods (Nash-MD, OMD) are architecturally locked to reverse KL divergence because their policy updates require sampling from a geometric mixture policy, whereas RSPO decouples regularization from the update mechanism by simply adding a divergence term to the MSE-based self-play loss. Theoretically, the paper builds on Magnetic Mirror Descent (Sokota et al., 2022) and generalizes it to accommodate arbitrary convex regularizers, proving that SPPO, Nash-MD, and OMD are all special cases of the unified RSPO loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play regularization framework means practitioners can immediately regularize any existing self-play method (SPPO, SPO, etc.) without modifying its loss function or sampling procedure, addressing a practical gap where most self-play methods were deployed unregularized and susceptible to over-optimization. The empirical finding that forward KL reduces response length while reverse KL boosts win rate—and that their combination is optimal for Mistral-7B—provides actionable guidance for practitioners tuning alignment pipelines. The parameter-efficiency result (RSPO with a 0.5B preference model matching SPPO with an 8B model) suggests regularization can partially compensate for weaker preference models. Open questions include whether the on-policy assumption is too restrictive for large-scale deployment, whether the specific divergence combinations generalize to larger models (70B+), and whether the theoretical convergence guarantees hold under the off-policy approximations used in practice.
