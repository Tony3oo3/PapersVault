## Problem

Existing multimodal reasoning approaches face a structural dilemma: pre-reasoning visual-to-text conversion irreversibly compresses fine-grained visual details before the reasoning trajectory unfolds, while unified vision-language representation paradigms suffer from language-prior-dominated self-attention that systematically biases visual tokens and weakens grounding in image evidence. The paper identifies the central challenge as determining *when* and *how* visual evidence should be introduced into the reasoning process, rather than treating it as a one-time conversion or a fixed joint representation.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a textual question as input and produces a free-form text answer. The image is never directly fed to the reasoning LLM; instead, it is accessed exclusively through the independent VLM perception module, which returns textualized visual evidence in response to targeted queries. The reasoning state is maintained as a growing concatenation of intermediate reasoning traces and returned visual evidence, and the loop terminates either when the reasoning core judges the evidence sufficient or when a maximum token budget (T_max = 6000) is reached.

## Method

CSMR decouples perception from reasoning into two modules: a Cognitive Reasoning Core (CRC), instantiated as a pure LLM (e.g., Qwen2-7B), and a Primary Visual Perception Module (PVP), an independent VLM (e.g., Qwen2-VL-7B). At each step, the CRC produces an intermediate output that a routing function parses into either a new visual query or a final answer; if a query is issued, the PVP independently analyzes the original image and returns textualized evidence, which is appended to the reasoning state. This dynamic, state-driven querying replaces both one-shot captioning and pre-planned sub-question decomposition, allowing the model to iteratively refine its visual evidence as the reasoning trajectory unfolds. The paper further provides an empirical analysis showing that in unified VLMs, text tokens receive roughly 2.5× higher pre-softmax attention than visual tokens across all transformer layers, and that long CoT chains further dilute visual attention, explaining why the vision encoder is updated primarily by language-prior-dominated gradients. The entire framework is training-free and requires no fine-tuning, with all gains attributed to the structural decoupling and dynamic scheduling mechanism.

## Results

CSMR achieves the best performance across all three benchmarks under zero-shot settings: 45.7% accuracy on M3CoT, 78.2% on ScienceQA, and 34.3 ROUGE-L on LLaVA-Bench In-the-Wild. The largest gain is on ScienceQA, where CSMR outperforms the strongest text-centric baseline DDCoT (71.9%) by 6.3 points. On M3CoT, CSMR surpasses the strongest unified-paradigm baseline ICoT (44.1%) by 1.6 points, and on LLaVA-W it edges out ICoT (34.2) by 0.1 ROUGE-L. All gains are achieved without any additional training or fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CSMR outperforms all seven baselines on every benchmark. Against the unified multimodal representation paradigm, CSMR beats ICoT (the strongest unified baseline) by 1.6 points on M3CoT (45.7 vs. 44.1) and by 0.1 ROUGE-L on LLaVA-W (34.3 vs. 34.2), while ICoT leads on ScienceQA (56.8 vs. 78.2 is not applicable since ICoT uses a different reasoning backbone). Against the text-centric pre-reasoning paradigm, CSMR achieves a substantial 6.3-point gain over DDCoT on ScienceQA (78.2 vs. 71.9) and a 6.7-point gain on M3CoT (45.7 vs. 39.0), while also improving LLaVA-W ROUGE-L by 7.9 points (34.3 vs. 26.4). The simple Caption baseline is consistently the weakest, confirming that static one-shot visual-to-text conversion loses critical fine-grained evidence. Notably, CSMR's advantage over ICoT on M3CoT demonstrates that tightly coupled cross-modal fusion is not a necessary condition for strong multimodal reasoning, and the 6.3-point ScienceQA margin over DDCoT highlights the benefit of dynamically scheduled visual querying over pre-planned sub-question decomposition.

## Relation to Prior Work

CSMR departs from both dominant multimodal reasoning paradigms—text-centric pre-reasoning (e.g., DDCoT, Caption) and unified vision-language representation (e.g., ICoT, CCoT, SCAFFOLD)—by structurally decoupling perception from reasoning into separate LLM and VLM modules. Conceptually, it repurposes the tool-augmented reasoning idea from DDCoT but replaces static, pre-planned sub-question decomposition with a reasoning-state-driven scheduling mechanism inspired by working memory theory. The empirical attention analysis builds on prior observations of text-dominant attention in VLMs (Deng et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2024a) but extends them to explain *why* the unified paradigm structurally undermines visual faithfulness during training, providing a mechanistic account rather than merely documenting the phenomenon.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the prevailing assumption that tight cross-modal fusion is necessary for strong multimodal reasoning, showing that principled evidence scheduling in a decoupled architecture can match or exceed unified models without any training. This opens a practical capability-scaling path: upgrading only the reasoning LLM improves performance without retraining the perception module, and new modalities can be attached as additional perception modules. Key open questions include whether the training-free ceiling limits performance in high-accuracy or domain-specific settings, how to reduce the multi-model inference overhead, and whether the cognitive scheduling mechanism can itself be learned through training to further improve information-seeking behavior.
