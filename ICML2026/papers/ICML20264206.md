## Problem

Multi-modal diffusion language models (MDLMs) encode visual information into static features at initialization, so the denoising process drifts toward language priors and produces visual hallucinations without any mechanism to re-examine specific image regions. Unlike autoregressive models, MDLMs cannot dynamically insert visual tokens or assess reasoning progress mid-generation, and final token confidence is unreliable because hallucinated tokens can converge to stable but incorrect predictions with high confidence scores.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264206/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a text prompt as primary input. During the inference-time refinement loop, it additionally consumes a local image crop extracted by Grounding DINO (with 1.5× proportional expansion for regions under 5% of image area) as a second visual view, concatenated with global vision tokens via a shared vision encoder. The output is a refined image caption produced iteratively: one uncertain span is remasked and regenerated per round (up to 6 rounds), with the CF scores updated after each pass to select the next candidate.

## Method

VGR (Visual-Guided Refinement) introduces the Confidence Fluctuation (CF) metric, which quantifies non-monotonic oscillation in a token's confidence trajectory across denoising steps, revealing visual-linguistic conflicts invisible in final confidence. Training fine-tunes the MDLM (via LoRA on attention and FFN) on a ~37k-sample VGR-Instruct dataset built from RefCOCO+, teaching the model to recover masked local descriptions conditioned on dual-view inputs (global image + local crop). At inference, a three-stage loop operates: (1) syntax-aware localization maps the highest-CF token to its semantic core using spaCy dependency parsing; (2) Grounding DINO retrieves the corresponding bounding box, gated by a confidence threshold τ=0.4; (3) the uncertain span plus k=2 adjacent tokens are remasked and regenerated with both global and local visual evidence, exploiting the native bidirectional attention of the diffusion architecture without any structural modification.

## Results

LLaDA-VGR achieves state-of-the-art performance among multi-modal diffusion language models (MDLMs) across all five benchmarks. On CapArena-Auto, it improves over the LLaDA-V baseline by 25.00 points (−77.17 → −52.17), and on DetailCaps-4870 CAPTURE by 2.73 points (59.62 → 62.35). On AMBER-g, hallucination metrics drop substantially: CHAIR from 8.2 to 6.0, Hal from 44.9 to 37.2, and Cog from 4.2 to 2.2, while Cover increases from 61.8 to 64.5. On MMHal-Bench, LLaDA-VGR attains the highest Score (2.92) and ties for the lowest Hal-Rate (0.60) among all MDLMs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary MDLM baseline LLaDA-V, LLaDA-VGR wins on every metric: CapArena-Auto improves by 25.00 points, CAPTURE by 2.73, CHAIR drops by 2.2, Hal by 7.7, and Cog by 2.0. Among other MDLMs, LLaDA-VGR outperforms FUDOKI, LaViDa, and MMaDA on all five benchmarks, with the largest margin over MMaDA (e.g., CHAIR 6.0 vs. 44.9, Cover 64.5 vs. 26.0). Compared to AR models, LLaDA-VGR remains behind Qwen2.5-VL-7B on CapArena-Auto (−52.17 vs. −16.83) and CapMAS (60.84 vs. 73.51), but is competitive on DetailCaps-4870 CAPTURE (62.35 vs. 60.61) and achieves a lower Hal-Rate on MMHal-Bench (0.60 vs. 0.73). On AMBER-g, LLaDA-VGR surpasses Qwen2.5-VL-7B on CHAIR (6.0 vs. 10.3) and Hal (37.2 vs. 48.6), though it trails on Cover (64.5 vs. 65.9). The generalization study (Table 5) confirms that applying VGR to LaViDa and MMaDA also yields consistent improvements, with MMaDA benefiting most (CHAIR drops from 44.9 to 10.5, Cover rises from 26.0 to 54.9).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on LLaDA-V and other masked diffusion language models (LaViDa, MMaDA) for vision-language understanding, but departs from the autoregressive hallucination-mitigation literature (tool-based region focusing, interleaved visual tokens, 'thinking with images') that cannot be directly transferred to the non-sequential, fixed-length generation dynamics of MDLMs. Its key conceptual departure is identifying Confidence Fluctuation (CF) as a diffusion-intrinsic uncertainty signal, grounded in a theoretical model of dynamic competition between language priors and visual evidence during denoising, rather than relying on final-step token confidence as in AR models. The VGR framework repurposes the inherent remasking mechanism of diffusion models for targeted in-place correction, combined with an external open-vocabulary detector (Grounding DINO) and syntax-aware anchoring (spaCy), without any architectural modification to the base MDLM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that final token confidence is misleading in MDLMs (hallucinated tokens converge to high confidence when language priors dominate) challenges a natural assumption that AR-style confidence-based uncertainty estimation transfers to diffusion models, and the CF metric offers a practical, architecture-agnostic alternative. The approach is broadly applicable across masked MDLMs (validated on LLaDA-V, LaViDa, MMaDA) and operates purely at inference time, making it a low-cost plug-in for existing systems. However, the method's accuracy is bounded by external tools (Grounding DINO, spaCy), and the underlying tendency of MDLMs to drift toward language priors is alleviated but not eliminated at the model level. A key open question is whether visual re-grounding can be internalized as an intrinsic behavior of the denoising process, removing the need for an external refinement loop entirely.
