## Problem

Decentralized optimization with affine constraints has been studied for consensus constraints and coupled constraints separately, but the general case combining coupled, local, and shared-variable constraints (mixed constraints) lacked a unified complexity analysis. This paper fills that gap by establishing tight lower bounds and corresponding optimal first-order algorithms for the smooth strongly convex regime, and near-optimal bounds for nonsmooth and non-strongly convex settings, revealing how the joint structure of distributed constraints and network topology governs optimization difficulty.

## Input / Output

Each node i locally holds its convex function f_i, coupled constraint matrix A_i, local constraint matrix C_i, shared-variable constraint matrix C̃_i, and corresponding right-hand-side vectors, and can only communicate with its neighbors via the gossip matrix W. The algorithm outputs an approximate solution u^N with explicit three-way complexity guarantees: number of gradient evaluations, number of matrix-vector multiplications (by A, C, C̃), and number of communication rounds, each expressed in terms of problem-specific condition numbers (κ_f, κ_W, κ̂_A, κ̃_AC, κ̂_C̃^T) and the target accuracy ε.

## Method

The paper reformulates the mixed-constraint problem (P) into a single affine constraint Kz = v where K = diag(B, B̃) is block-diagonal: B = [[A, αW],[βC, 0]] encodes coupled and local constraints, while B̃^T = (C̃^T, γW) encodes shared-variable constraints. The key algorithmic mechanism is applying Chebyshev acceleration independently to each diagonal block (and to W within each block), which reduces the effective condition number of K to O(1) at a cost of O(√κ̂) matrix-vector multiplications per oracle call. For the smooth strongly convex case, the accelerated APAPC algorithm is applied to the preconditioned problem, yielding optimal rates; for nonsmooth cases, Gradient Sliding with a quadratic penalty on the constraint residual is used; for smooth non-strongly convex cases, the Penalty Similar Triangles Method (PSTM) is employed. New mixed condition numbers (κ̃_AC for coupled-local interaction, κ̂_C̃^T for shared-variable constraints) are introduced to capture how the interplay between distributed constraint matrices and network topology affects convergence, with Lemma 4.4 providing the critical upper bound κ_B = O(κ̃_AC·κ_W + κ̃_AC·κ_C).

## Results

For the smooth strongly convex mixed-constraint problem (P), the paper establishes optimal first-order complexity bounds: O(√κ_f) gradient calls, O(√κ_f √κ̂_AC) multiplications by A, O(√κ_f √κ̂_AC √κ_C) by C, O(√κ_f √κ̂_C̃^T) by C̃, and O(√κ_f (√κ̂_AC + √κ̂_C̃^T) √κ_W) communication rounds (all times log(1/ε)). These bounds are tight, matching newly proved lower bounds. The communication complexity is governed by the joint conditioning of the coupled constraint matrix A, local constraint matrix C, and network topology W, as characterized by Lemma 4.4: κ_B = O(κ̃_AC κ_W + κ̃_AC κ_C).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's mixed-constraint results (Theorem 4.6) strictly generalize all three prior baselines as special cases. When C = 0 (no local constraints), κ̃_AC reduces to κ̂_A and the mixed bounds recover exactly the coupled-constraints rates of Yarmoshik et al. (2024b). When A = 0 (no coupled constraints), the bounds reduce to the shared-variable-constraint rates, extending Rogozin et al. (2022) from identical to non-identical local constraint matrices C̃_i. When both A = 0 and C = 0, the results collapse to the consensus-optimization rates of Scaman et al. (2017). The key new contribution is that the mixed case does not simply add the individual complexities: the communication bound involves the sum (√κ̃_AC + √κ̂_C̃^T) rather than a product, and the joint spectral parameter κ̃_AC (Definition 4.2) can be strictly smaller than κ̂_A when local constraints C restrict the feasible subspace, yielding genuinely improved rates over treating the constraint types independently. All bounds are proven optimal via matching lower bounds constructed using Nesterov's worst-case function.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate lines of decentralized optimization—consensus optimization (Scaman et al., 2017; Kovalev et al., 2020), coupled constraints (Yarmoshik et al., 2024b), and shared-variable constraints with identical local constraints (Rogozin et al., 2022)—under a single 'mixed affine constraints' formulation. The algorithmic backbone (APAPC from Salim et al., 2022; Gradient Sliding from Lan, 2020; PSTM from Dvinskikh & Gasnikov, 2021) is borrowed from centralized affine-constrained optimization, with the paper's core technical contribution being the decentralized reformulation and block-structured Chebyshev acceleration of the constraint matrices. The key departure from prior work is extending Rogozin et al. from identical to non-identical local constraints on shared variables, extending Yarmoshik et al. from pure coupled to coupled-plus-local constraints, and then combining both extensions into the full mixed problem with matching lower bounds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a complete complexity characterization (optimal upper bounds with matching lower bounds) for the first-order decentralized optimization landscape across all combinations of constraint types, giving practitioners a principled way to predict how constraint structure and network topology jointly affect convergence. The mixed condition numbers (κ̂_A, κ̃_AC, κ̂_C̃^T) serve as diagnostic tools for understanding when a particular constraint formulation is well- or ill-conditioned. Open questions include whether constraints that mix local and global variables (∑(A_i x_i + Ã_i x̃_i − b_i) = 0) can be handled within this framework, and whether different affine reformulations of the same problem yield different optimization complexities—a question the authors explicitly flag as the logical next step.
