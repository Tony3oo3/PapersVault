## Problem

Existing RLVR methods for LLM reasoning perform on-policy optimization from scratch, incurring high sampling costs and failing to exploit the rich experience encoded in prior RL-optimized policies. Recent attempts to reuse experience as fixed reasoning trajectories suffer from policy mismatch as the model's capabilities evolve during training, rendering static trajectories unreliable. The paper argues that experience should be represented in a policy-adaptive, action-level manner rather than as static trajectories.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text question and a frozen prior RL-optimized policy pi_RL (obtained by running DAPO on the same base model) as inputs, and produces a reasoning trajectory with a final answer enclosed in a boxed delimiter. During rollout, the current policy pi_theta generates tokens autoregressively while pi_RL is consulted in parallel to compute per-token log-likelihood discrepancies; only tokens where the current policy is overconfident yet disfavored by pi_RL (exceeding a threshold tau) are resampled from pi_RL. The maximum response length is 20,480 tokens for Qwen3-8B and 8,192 for Qwen-2.5-Math-7B, and each rollout group of G=16 contains 15 on-policy responses plus 1 experience-augmented response.

## Method

EAPO represents RL experience as an action-level prior over critical decisions rather than static trajectories, leveraging a prior RL-optimized policy pi_RL to guide the current policy pi_theta. At each decoding step, EAPO computes a token-level discrepancy delta_t (the log-likelihood ratio between pi_theta and pi_RL) and identifies critical decision points where the current policy is overconfident yet strongly disfavored by pi_RL (delta_t > tau); at these sparse points, the token is resampled from pi_RL, with block-wise speculative verification (K=20 tokens) to amortize the computational cost. For stable optimization, EAPO applies positive experience filtering (only experience-augmented trajectories with correct answers contribute to gradients), a response-level smoothed importance sampling ratio that interpolates between pi_old and pi_RL based on the fraction of resampled tokens, and experience annealing that disables resampling after T=60 training steps to restore pure on-policy exploration.

## Results

On in-domain math reasoning (AIME'24, AIME'25, AMC), EAPO achieves 43.34% average Pass@1 and 62.41% average Pass@16 with Qwen-2.5-Math-7B, and 49.71% / 72.81% with Qwen-3-8B-Base, outperforming all baselines. On out-of-domain science benchmarks (GPQA, MMLU-Pro), EAPO reaches 45.38% average with Qwen-2.5-Math-7B and 60.58% with Qwen-3-8B-Base, again leading all compared methods. The largest single-benchmark gain is on AIME'24 Pass@1, where EAPO scores 40.14% (7B) and 41.66% (8B) versus 37.19% and 36.67% for DAPO respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EAPO outperforms all five baselines on both model scales across all benchmarks. Against the strongest baseline DAPO, EAPO gains +2.78 points in average Pass@1 on Qwen-2.5-Math-7B (43.34 vs. 40.56) and +4.40 points on Qwen-3-8B-Base (49.71 vs. 45.31). Token-level distillation methods (OPD, MOPD) are consistently weaker: MOPD, the best distillation baseline, trails EAPO by 2.23 points (7B) and 3.31 points (8B) in average Pass@1. Trajectory Replay (ExGRPO) is the closest competitor on the 7B model (42.08 vs. 43.34 avg Pass@1), but EAPO still leads by 1.26 points and by a wider margin on the 8B model where Trajectory Replay was not evaluated. On out-of-domain science benchmarks, EAPO leads DAPO by 3.70 points (7B) and 1.80 points (8B) in average score, confirming that the gains are not limited to in-domain math.

## Relation to Prior Work

EAPO builds directly on the DAPO/GRPO RLVR optimization framework and departs from two prior directions of experience reuse in LLM reasoning: (1) trajectory-level replay methods (e.g., ExGRPO) that inject static reasoning paths and suffer from policy mismatch as the model evolves, and (2) distillation-based approaches (OPD, MOPD) that rely on an external stronger model. The key conceptual departure is reframing experience as a policy-adaptive, action-level prior encoded in a prior RL-optimized policy π_RL, with sparse token-level resampling at critical decision points identified via log-likelihood ratio, rather than wholesale trajectory injection. The smoothed importance sampling scheme and block-wise speculative verification mechanism are novel technical contributions that make this action-level intervention tractable within standard on-policy RL loops.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EAPO addresses a practical bottleneck in RLVR training—expensive exploration from scratch—by showing that only ~4% of tokens at critical decision points need correction from a prior RL policy to yield consistent gains, making experience reuse cheap and scalable. The finding that token-level experience outperforms trajectory-level replay despite the latter having lower KL divergence to the experience model challenges the intuition that closer distributional matching is always better. Open questions include whether multiple or dynamically evolving experience policies can further improve results, whether the fixed threshold and annealing schedule can be made adaptive, and whether the approach extends to tasks with weaker or noisier verifiable rewards.
