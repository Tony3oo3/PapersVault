## Problem

Existing pure exploration methods (UCB-based, elimination-based, entropy-based) allocate samples by regulating local estimation error or optimism, but do not explicitly quantify how uncertainty in each stochastic component propagates to the final decision. In highly stochastic, heteroscedastic environments, this mismatch causes these methods to mistake noise for evidence and misallocate budget away from the components that most affect the final recommendation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes bounded stochastic reward samples (in [a,b]) from arms, actions, or state-action pairs, and in the MCTS/RL settings also requires a forward model or environment transitions to generate trajectories. At each step it produces a sample allocation decision (which component to pull next), and at the end of the budget it outputs a final recommendation: the best arm, the best root action, or a greedy policy. A key design choice is that in the RL setting the variance signal is computed from one-step TD targets (reward + bootstrapped value) rather than cumulative returns, since the latter are intractable in infinite-horizon MDPs.

## Method

VarDE formalizes the final decision as a smooth function f of local empirical means (using LogSumExp as a differentiable surrogate for max), then applies a first-order Taylor expansion to decompose the decision variance Var[f(μ̂)] into an influence-weighted sum of local variances: Σ w_i(μ)² σ_i²/N_i, where w_i = ∂f/∂μ_i are the influence weights. The sampling rule greedily selects the component maximizing w_i(μ̂)² σ̃_i² / (N_i(N_i+1)), combining how strongly the decision depends on that component with how noisy it currently is (with a variance floor σ̄² for stability). This yields a unified allocation principle across BAI, MCTS, and Q-learning: in BAI the influence weights are softmax probabilities over empirical means; in MCTS they are computed per-node over action values; in Q-learning they are computed over Q-values at each state with TD-target variance as the noise signal. Theoretically, the method achieves O(1/T) decision-variance decay with the optimal first-order allocation constant C* = (Σ|w_i|σ_i)², exponential misidentification probability in BAI and MCTS, and almost-sure convergence to an optimal policy in the RL setting.

## Results

On four fixed-budget BAI benchmarks, VARDE-BAI achieves the lowest error probability across all settings: 12.87% (BAI.1, 1200 pulls), 17.27% (BAI.2, 1000 pulls), 7.34% (BAI.3, 200 pulls), and 11.83% (BAI.4, 150 pulls), outperforming the best baselines (UCBE2, CR-A) by 2.8–4.3 percentage points. In MCTS, VARDE-MCTS attains the highest Monte Carlo value estimates on SAILING, TAXI, and FROZENLAKE and the lowest root value estimation error on the synthetic tree benchmark. In BPI, VARDE-Q-LEARNING maintains near-optimal normalized value proximity across all state sizes on RIVERSWIM and FORKEDRIVERSWIM, with tighter confidence intervals than all model-free and model-based baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In BAI, VARDE-BAI (τ=0.05) beats the strongest CI-based baselines UCBE2 (15.67%) and UGAPE2 (15.64%) by ~2.8 pp on BAI.1, and outperforms the best reject-based method CR-A (9.83%) by 2.5 pp on BAI.3 (τ=0.1 gives 7.34%). On BAI.4 with only 150 pulls, VARDE (τ=0.05) at 11.83% narrowly edges out CR-A (11.93%), while all UCB/UGAPE variants with larger α degrade substantially (19–26%). In MCTS, VARDE-MCTS consistently outperforms UCT, MENTS, RENTS/TENTS, DENTS, and BTS in both value estimation quality and root-action accuracy, with the largest gaps in the highly stochastic SAILING environment and the synthetic tree (k=200, d=2) where several baselines show unstable search. In BPI, VARDE-Q-LEARNING surpasses model-free baselines (Q-UCB, PSRL, MF-BPI, ε-Q-Learning) and model-based methods (MDP-NAS, PS-MDP-NAS, OBPI) in normalized value proximity, particularly as |S| grows, where baselines deteriorate rapidly while VARDE remains near-optimal with tight confidence intervals.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the standard Q-learning update rule (Watkins & Dayan, 1992) and the MCTS selection-simulation-backpropagation framework (Kocsis & Szepesvári, 2006), but departs from the dominant optimism-based exploration paradigm that governs both BAI (UCBE, UGAPE, Successive Halving, Continuous Rejects) and MCTS (UCT, MENTS, RENTS/TENTS/DENTS, BTS). The key conceptual departure is reframing pure exploration as decision-level variance minimization: rather than regulating local estimation error via confidence radii or elimination schedules, VarDE uses a smooth LogSumExp surrogate of the final decision and a first-order sensitivity decomposition to allocate samples by (influence weight)² × (empirical variance), directly targeting the uncertainty of the output recommendation. This positions the work as a unifying methodology across bandits, planning, and RL that is orthogonal to and complementary with existing optimism/entropy mechanisms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean, modular, and theoretically grounded alternative to optimism-based exploration that is particularly well-suited to highly stochastic and heteroscedastic environments where confidence-bound heuristics can mistake noise for signal. The unified variance-decay analysis (Theorem 3.7) and the characterization of the optimal first-order allocation constant (Remark 3.8) give practitioners a principled target for sample allocation. Key limitations include the reliance on a first-order Taylor approximation (most accurate once estimates concentrate), the asymptotic (rather than finite-sample) RL guarantee, and the diagonal variance view that does not capture trajectory coupling or bootstrapping dependencies. Open directions include non-asymptotic BPI bounds, correlated-uncertainty (full covariance) allocation, adaptive temperature schedules, and extension to function-approximation settings.
