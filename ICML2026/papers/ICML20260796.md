## Problem

Existing fair regression methods rely on squared error loss, making them fragile under heavy-tailed noise common in real-world data. While the Wasserstein barycenter transformation (Chzhen et al., 2020) provides a closed-form fair predictor for squared loss, no unified framework guarantees demographic parity under general robust loss functions (Cauchy, Huber, LAD, quantile, Tukey), and no theoretical optimality results exist for such settings.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained robust regression predictor f̂ (typically a DNN) along with the sensitive attribute S and covariates X, and produces a demographic-parity-compliant prediction g_Q(x,s) = Q(F_{f̂|s}(f̂(x,s))). The output is a rank-preserving fair predictor whose intra-group ordering matches the base model. An interpolation parameter λ ∈ [0,1] further allows producing a family of predictors trading off risk (decreasing in λ) against unfairness (increasing linearly in λ), with the unfairness measured via the Wasserstein-1 distance between group-conditional prediction distributions.

## Method

The framework is a three-step post-processing procedure: (1) train a robust predictor f̂ by minimizing empirical risk under a chosen robust loss; (2) apply the demographic parity transformation g_Q(x,s) = Q(F_{f̂|s}(f̂(x,s))), which satisfies DP for any quantile function Q (generalizing the Wasserstein barycenter to an arbitrary target distribution); (3) learn the optimal Q by minimizing the robust risk over a space of monotone I-splines (non-negative coefficients ensuring monotonicity), solved via constrained L-BFGS-B. The key theoretical result is that this transformation is the unique risk minimizer among all rank-preserving fair predictors for any robust loss. An interpolation predictor g_λ* = λf* + (1−λ)g_Q* then provides a provable linear trade-off: unfairness scales exactly as λ·U(f*) while risk is non-increasing in λ under convex losses.

## Results

On the CRIME dataset (1,994 US communities), the proposed method achieves the lowest unfairness across all five robust loss functions, with values ranging from 0.0093 (LAD) to 0.0107 (Quantile), compared to 0.0615–0.0721 for FRWB and 0.3950–0.4649 for RDNN. In terms of empirical risk, the method remains highly competitive with FRWB (e.g., LAD risk 0.1294 vs. 0.1330) while enforcing a substantially stricter fairness constraint. In simulation (Table 1), the method consistently outperforms FRWB on risk across all losses (e.g., Cauchy: 1.0469 vs. 1.2354; LAD: 1.5718 vs. 1.7207) while achieving roughly 2–3× lower unfairness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: RDNN (robust DNN regression without fairness) and FRWB (Wasserstein barycenter fair regression, adapted to use robust loss). Against RDNN, the proposed method reduces unfairness by roughly 35–40× (e.g., 0.0106 vs. 0.3993 under Cauchy loss) at the cost of higher empirical risk, which is expected since RDNN ignores the fairness constraint entirely. Against FRWB, the proposed method achieves approximately 6× lower unfairness (0.0093–0.0107 vs. 0.0615–0.0721) while maintaining comparable or slightly lower risk in several settings (e.g., LAD: 0.1294 vs. 0.1330; Cauchy simulation: 1.0469 vs. 1.2354). In the simulation study, the method consistently outperforms FRWB on risk across all five loss functions while simultaneously achieving lower unfairness, demonstrating a strictly superior fairness–accuracy trade-off. The interpolation parameter λ provides a continuous trade-off: at λ=0 the method is perfectly fair, and as λ increases, risk decreases monotonically while unfairness grows linearly, a property not available in either baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the fair regression line initiated by Chzhen et al. (2020), who derived a closed-form demographic-parity transformation via the Wasserstein barycenter for squared error loss, and Liu et al. (2022), who extended it to quantile regression. The key conceptual departure is showing that the Wasserstein barycenter target is suboptimal under general robust losses (demonstrated via a toy LAD example), and instead learning an optimal quantile function Q over a monotone I-spline space that minimizes the robust risk among all rank-preserving fair predictors. The robust regression component builds on DNN-based M-estimation (Shen et al., 2021), while the interpolation mechanism and conditional demographic parity extension are novel contributions not present in prior fair regression work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to provide optimality guarantees for fair regression under a broad class of robust M-estimators (Cauchy, Huber, LAD, quantile, Tukey), which is practically important given that high-stakes regression data (credit, crime) frequently exhibit heavy-tailed errors. The linear fairness-accuracy trade-off (Theorem 3.5) gives policymakers a transparent, interpretable knob rather than a binary fair/unfair choice. Limitations include the rank-preservation constraint (which restricts the admissible predictor class), the post-processing nature (performance bounded by the base predictor), and the requirement of a continuous conditional CDF. Open questions include whether the rank-preservation assumption is too restrictive in practice, how to handle continuous or multi-valued sensitive attributes, and whether the spline-based Q estimation scales to very large group counts.
