## Problem

Differentially private (DP) submodular maximization under a knapsack constraint (SMK) had only been addressed by a single prior work (Sadeghi & Fazel, 2021), which required exponential query complexity to evaluate the multilinear extension, was restricted to monotone objectives, and exhibited suboptimal additive error scaling with the ground set size and minimum element cost. This paper closes all three gaps, providing the first DP algorithm for non-monotone SMK with provable guarantees and substantially improved utility and query complexity for the monotone case.

## Input / Output

The algorithms take a sensitive dataset D, a submodular function f_D: 2^N → R_+ accessed via a value oracle with bounded sensitivity Δ_f (the maximum change in f_D(S) across neighboring datasets), a positive cost function c: N → R_{>0}, a budget B (normalized to 1), and privacy parameters (ε, δ). They output a feasible subset S ⊆ N satisfying c(S) ≤ B, with the objective value f(S) guaranteed to approximate the optimum up to an additive error term that depends on Δ_f, k (max feasible cardinality), ε, δ, and n (ground set size). The submodular function is consumed exclusively through oracle queries, and the total query count serves as the computational complexity measure.

## Method

For monotone SMK, the paper introduces DP-2GG (Algorithm 2), a privatized Two-Guess-Greedy that uses the Generalized Report Noisy Max (GRNM) mechanism of Raskhodnikova & Smith (2016) for each density-greedy selection step, thereby avoiding the worst-case sensitivity bound that would arise from uniform-cost analysis. The O(n²) enumeration over guessed pairs is handled via the generalized private selection framework of Cohen et al. (2023), which randomizes the number of invocations per candidate and yields a global privacy loss independent of the enumeration size, achieving the optimal (1−1/e)-approximation with polylogarithmic dependence on n. A faster variant, DP-DG+ (Algorithm 7), runs a single density-greedy pass and uses RNM to select among partial solutions and their single-element extensions, achieving a 1/2-approximation in O(nk) queries. For non-monotone SMK, DP-SDG (Algorithm 3) adapts the SmkRan algorithm by performing GRNM-based density selections with randomized 1/2 discarding, continuing to add elements even when marginal gains are negative (to handle DP noise), and using RNM for final candidate selection; a concentration bound showing the iteration count is stochastically dominated by a sum of geometric random variables concentrates around O(k) resolves the composition bottleneck, yielding a 1/4-approximation in expectation.

## Results

The paper provides three DP algorithms for SMK with a Δ-sensitive objective. For monotone objectives, DP-2GG (Algorithm 2) achieves the optimal (1−1/e)-approximation with additive error O(Δk^{1.5}/ε · √(log(n/δβ)) · log(n/β)) and O(β^{-1}n³k) queries, while the faster DP-DG+ (Algorithm 7) achieves a 1/2-approximation with O(nk) queries. For non-monotone objectives, DP-SDG (Algorithm 3) achieves a 1/4-approximation in expectation with additive error O(Δk/ε · √((k+log(1/δ))log(1/δ)) · log(n/β)) and O(nk) queries. Empirically on 100,000 Uber pickup locations in Manhattan, DP-2GG is within 2% of non-private 2GG at ε=0.2, DP-DG+ is within 8% of DG+ at ε=0.2, and DP-SDG is within 0.1% of SmkRan at ε=0.4, while the Random baseline is 49–61% lower.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the only prior DP SMK algorithm (Sadeghi & Fazel, 2021), the proposed methods improve the additive error from polynomial in n (with a 1/c_min factor) to polylogarithmic in n (scaling with k instead), reduce query complexity from exponential O(2^n) to polynomial O(n³k) or O(nk), and extend coverage to non-monotone objectives. In the empirical monotone comparison, DP-2GG is within 2% of the non-private Two-Guess-Greedy (Feldman et al., 2023) at ε=0.2, and DP-DG+ is within 8% of the non-private Density-Greedy+ (Yaroslavtsev et al., 2020) at the same privacy level, while the Random baseline is 49% lower in utility. In the non-monotone setting, DP-SDG is within 0.1% of the non-private SmkRan (Han et al., 2021) at ε=0.4, whereas Random is 61% lower. On scalability, DP-DG+ requires 6000× fewer oracle queries than DP-2GG at n=60, and DP-SDG exhibits linear query growth in n, confirming the theoretical O(nk) complexity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DP submodular maximization literature (Mitrovic et al. 2017 for cardinality constraints; Rafiey & Yoshida 2020 for matroids) to the knapsack constraint setting, building directly on the non-private Two-Guess-Greedy algorithm of Feldman et al. (2023) and the SmkRan algorithm of Han et al. (2021). The key technical departure from prior DP work is the use of the Generalized Report Noisy Max mechanism (Raskhodnikova & Smith 2016) to handle the varying sensitivities of density scores, and the generalized private selection framework of Cohen et al. (2023) to bypass the O(n²) composition bottleneck inherent in Two-Guess-Greedy's enumeration. The only prior DP SMK result (Sadeghi & Fazel 2021) required exponential query complexity via exact multilinear extension evaluation and was restricted to monotone objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work establishes the first provable DP guarantees for non-monotone SMK and achieves the optimal (1-1/e) approximation for monotone SMK with polynomial query complexity, closing a significant gap over the prior exponential-complexity result. The additive error's polylogarithmic dependence on ground set size (vs. polynomial in prior work) and its scaling with maximum feasible solution size (vs. minimum element cost) make the guarantees practically meaningful. Open questions include whether the √k dependence in the error term is tight under bounded sensitivity, whether decreasing-threshold techniques from the non-private setting can be adapted to DP without worsening k-dependence, and whether the approach extends to combined knapsack-matroid constraints.
