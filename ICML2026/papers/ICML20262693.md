## Problem

Scene graphs for autonomous driving overfit to spurious, environment-specific correlations (e.g., weather textures, illumination) rather than invariant physical dynamics, causing severe out-of-distribution failures. Existing methods either produce dense topologies that entangle causal interactions with environmental noise, or use uncertainty only to smooth predictions on a fixed graph rather than to prune unreliable edges. No prior work integrates uncertainty as a structural sparsification criterion with causal intervention to disentangle invariant from confounding factors.

## Input / Output

The method consumes fixed-length RGB image sequences (128×72 for CARLA-SR, 1600×900 for DeepAccident) from which a Mask R-CNN detector (COCO-pretrained R50-FPN 3x) extracts bounding boxes and semantic labels over 9 categories, supplemented by fixed ego-vehicle and lane anchor instances. Each object is encoded as a 15-dimensional feature vector (normalized geometric attributes + one-hot semantics). The model outputs a sequence-level binary collision risk probability with an associated aleatoric uncertainty estimate, alongside a sparse scene graph topology whose edge set is determined by differentiable pruning.

## Method

CURVE builds a variational scene graph where entity and relation embeddings are parameterized as diagonal Gaussians, with the learned standard deviation σ_ij serving as an aleatoric uncertainty proxy for environment-sensitivity. A prototype-driven soft backdoor intervention approximates the continuous environment space via K=32 learnable prototypes; attention-weighted aggregation over these prototypes estimates the environment-conditioned bias ẑ_s, which is then injected as a residual correction gated by a learned function of σ_ij, selectively debiasing high-uncertainty relations while preserving confident causal interactions. Differentiable topology induction combines adaptive thresholding with per-node Top-K selection to produce a sparse graph, and an uncertainty-weighted RGCN (inverse-variance message weights) performs spatial reasoning before LSTM temporal aggregation. Training uses a composite loss of aleatoric cross-entropy (Monte Carlo over 10 samples), ranking-based uncertainty calibration with a collapse penalty, prototype diversity regularization (Frobenius norm on cosine similarity), and KL divergence to prevent uncertainty inflation.

## Results

On the in-distribution CARLA-SR test set, CURVE achieves 92.92% accuracy, 96.19% AUC, and 68.85 MCC, matching or exceeding all baselines on AUC and MCC. In zero-shot OOD transfer to DeepAccident, CURVE attains 68.13% accuracy, 71.15% AUC, and 35.07 MCC, outperforming the strongest baseline RS2G (64.84% Acc, 67.35% AUC, 36.44 MCC) on accuracy and AUC while producing a far sparser graph (5.78 avg. degree vs. 75.92). In sim-to-real adaptation to DoTA, CURVE reaches 63.72% AUC with only 5% target-domain annotations, approaching the fully supervised oracle (69.42% AUC).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CURVE outperforms the primary graph-based baseline RS2G on OOD accuracy (68.13 vs. 64.84) and OOD AUC (71.15 vs. 67.35) while reducing average node degree from 75.92 to 5.78, demonstrating that sparsity and generalization are not in tension. Against the thresholded variant RS2G (Thr=0.6), which achieves comparable sparsity (7.42 avg. degree), CURVE gains 11.0 points in OOD accuracy (68.13 vs. 57.14) and 20.1 points in OOD AUC, confirming that uncertainty-guided pruning is superior to fixed-threshold sparsification. CURVE also beats the OOD-generalization objective IRM (68.13 vs. 60.44 OOD Acc) and the causality-guided loss DDLoss (68.13 vs. 63.74 OOD Acc) by a wide margin. The rule-based RS2V achieves the highest ID accuracy (93.36) and sparsest graph (1.90 avg. degree) but collapses in OOD (47.25% Acc, −18.03 MCC), illustrating that geometric heuristics do not transfer. CURVE's OOD MCC (35.07) is slightly below RS2G's (36.44), but this gap is negligible given the 13× reduction in graph density and the substantially higher OOD accuracy and AUC.

## Relation to Prior Work

CURVE builds directly on the RS2G scene graph framework, replacing its dense topology construction with a variational, uncertainty-guided sparsification mechanism. Conceptually, it departs from prior causal debiasing approaches (IRM, DDLoss, MCCM) that treat causality as an external loss or post-hoc correction, instead embedding a prototype-driven soft backdoor adjustment into the representation learning pipeline itself. The key reframing is repurposing aleatoric uncertainty from a prediction-smoothing signal (as in PC-SGG, FloCoDe, HiKER-SGG) into a structural criterion that reshapes graph topology, pruning environment-sensitive edges rather than merely attenuating their influence on fixed connections.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For safety-critical autonomous driving, the ability to produce sparse, interpretable, domain-stable interaction graphs with calibrated uncertainty is practically valuable for risk assessment under distribution shift. The paper's own limitations are significant: performance is bounded by upstream detector quality, and the uncertainty-as-spuriousness heuristic may over-prune genuinely causal but high-variance interactions (e.g., erratic pedestrians). Open questions include whether the prototype-based intervention admits a principled identifiability guarantee, whether the approach extends to closed-loop planning, and whether the findings generalize beyond the relatively small evaluation datasets (DeepAccident has only 91 sequences).
