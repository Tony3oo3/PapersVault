## Problem

Most open deep research agents are trained via reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) on short-form QA, which does not transfer to realistic long-form research tasks. Defining reliable reward signals for open-ended, long-form deep research is challenging because quality criteria are under-specified, require grounding in up-to-date external knowledge beyond parametric memory, and static rubrics are vulnerable to reward hacking.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264042/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a natural-language research question (long-form or short-form) and autonomously plans a sequence of actions over an action space of {think, tool, answer, cite}, invoking up to 10 tool calls per rollout across three search backends (google_search, web_browse, paper_search). The output is a long-form, report-style answer with snippet-level citations (not just URL-level links) that directly support individual claims, enabling verifiability. The same unified inference pipeline handles both long-form and short-form queries without task-specific customization.

## Method

The core contribution is Reinforcement Learning with Evolving Rubrics (RLER), which creates a generation-verification gap by giving the rubric generator privileged information unavailable to the policy: (1) external knowledge retrieved from multiple search rollouts for fact verification, and (2) multiple independently sampled model responses for contrastive quality assessment. At each training step, a rubric LM generates new positive and negative rubrics by contrasting the current rollouts against the existing rubric buffer; a buffer management strategy then prunes low-variance rubrics and retains the top-K most discriminative ones. Training proceeds in two stages: an SFT cold start distilling GPT-5 search trajectories into Qwen3-8B, followed by online RL using a customized GRPO variant with asynchronous tool calls, token-level loss, and tool-output masking. Three auxiliary rewards (format, search count, and citation F1) supplement the rubric-based reward, and the entire pipeline is built on dr-agent-lib, an MCP-based multi-tool agent infrastructure supporting concurrent, rate-limit-aware search execution.

## Results

DR Tulu-8B (RL) achieves an average score of 65.6 across four long-form deep research benchmarks (SQAv2, HealthBench, ResearchQA, DeepResearchBench), outperforming the best prior open model (Tongyi DR-30B, 50.0) by 15.6 points and matching OpenAI Deep Research (64.9) on average. On SQAv2, DR Tulu-8B achieves 88.3, the highest score among all evaluated systems, surpassing OpenAI DR (79.6) by 8.7 points. The model is approximately 1000× cheaper per query than OpenAI DR ($0.0019 vs. $1.80 on SQAv2). RLER training on top of the SFT checkpoint yields gains of 6.4–16.0 points across the four benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest open baseline, Tongyi DR-30B (50.0 avg), DR Tulu-8B wins on all four benchmarks by 4.8–41.8 points (e.g., 88.3 vs. 46.5 on SQAv2, 52.8 vs. 46.2 on HealthBench). Among other open models, DR Tulu-8B substantially outperforms WebExplorer-8B (44.4), WebThinker-32B-DPO (28.9), Search-R1-7B (14.9), and ASearcher-7B (10.3), with the gap largest on SQAv2 where prior open models produce no citations. Against the fixed-pipeline Ai2 ScholarQA (Claude Sonnet, 57.7 avg), DR Tulu-8B wins on all four benchmarks, most notably on HealthBench (52.8 vs. 32.0) and DRB (45.4 vs. 36.1), while narrowly beating it on SQAv2 (88.3 vs. 87.7). Versus proprietary systems, DR Tulu-8B matches OpenAI DR on average (65.6 vs. 64.9) and wins on SQAv2 (88.3 vs. 79.6) but trails on HealthBench (52.8 vs. 53.8), ResearchQA (75.7 vs. 79.2), and DRB (45.4 vs. 46.9); it also trails GPT-5+Search (65.8 avg) by a narrow margin, losing primarily on HealthBench (52.8 vs. 59.5) and DRB (45.4 vs. 50.7). On the short-form generalization analysis, DR Tulu-8B (RL) achieves 61.3 average on SimpleQA/2Wiki/WebWalkerQA, outperforming the Qwen3-8B naive RAG baseline (26.8) and QwQ-32B (33.8), confirming cross-task transfer from long-form RL training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RLVR line of work (Search-R1, WebExplorer, Tongyi DR) that trains search agents via reinforcement learning, but departs from its reliance on verifiable short-form QA rewards by introducing rubrics-as-rewards for open-ended long-form generation. It builds on RaR (Gunjal et al., 2025) for using instance-specific rubrics as RL rewards, but replaces static, closed-book rubrics with search-grounded rubrics that co-evolve with the policy model during training (RLER). The multi-tool, citation-generating agent design also extends beyond prior open systems that typically use a single search tool and omit citations, while the SFT-then-RL recipe with asynchronous tool calling builds on infrastructure from OpenInstruct and VerLTool.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DR Tulu-8B is the first fully open (model, data, code, infrastructure) deep research agent trained end-to-end for long-form tasks, and its 1000× cost advantage over proprietary systems makes long-form research capabilities accessible at scale. The RLER framework offers a general recipe for RL training on tasks lacking verifiable rewards, and the finding that it works with the same open model as both judge and rubric generator (no proprietary distillation needed) broadens its applicability. Key open questions include the train-test mismatch between evolving training rubrics and fixed evaluation rubrics, whether scaling privileged information to the verifier (more contrastive rollouts, deeper search traces) yields further gains, and how to extend the framework to domain-specific structured tools (genomics, molecular databases) beyond natural-language search.
