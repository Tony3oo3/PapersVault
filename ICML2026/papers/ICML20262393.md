## Problem

Traditional neural topic models reconstruct sparse Bag-of-Words representations, capturing only statistical word co-occurrences while ignoring contextual and compositional meaning — a limitation that is especially severe for short texts. Although language models encode rich semantic knowledge, they cannot directly model large corpora due to context-length constraints, and prior LLM-based topic modeling approaches either produce non-probabilistic binary assignments or require complex, costly pipelines impractical for large-scale use.

## Input / Output

The method takes a raw text document paired with a short thematic instruction prompt (e.g., 'Generate a single-word label that captures the document theme') and feeds them to a frozen small language model. The SLM's next-token logits are restricted to a predefined vocabulary of |V|=2000 single-token words and converted into a dense soft-label distribution via temperature-scaled softmax (τ=3), while the SLM's final hidden state at the last token position serves as the document representation. The trained topic model outputs explicit document-topic proportions θ, topic-word distributions, and a vocabulary-level predictive distribution, preserving the full probabilistic structure of LDA-family models.

## Method

The core mechanism is a distillation pipeline: a frozen SLM (e.g., Llama-3.1-8B, ERNIE-4.5-0.3B) is prompted with a thematic instruction, and the next-token logits immediately following the prompt are projected onto the topic-modeling vocabulary and passed through a temperature-scaled softmax to produce dense, semantically grounded soft-label targets that assign probability mass to theme-relevant words absent from the document. The SLM's final hidden state is used as the document input to a standard VAE-based topic model (ProdLDA, ECRTM, or FASTopic architecture, used without modification). Training replaces the conventional sparse BoW reconstruction loss with a KL-divergence term between the dense soft-label target and the topic model's predicted vocabulary distribution, while preserving each backbone's original regularization (latent prior, embedding clustering, or optimal transport). The key insight is that the prompt-conditioned next-token distribution functions as an implicit posterior predictive distribution over latent thematic concepts, and the KL objective distills this unstructured semantic signal into the structured, interpretable document-topic and topic-word parameters of the topic model.

## Results

Across three datasets (20NewsGroups, TweetTopic, StackOverflow) and four metrics (C_V, LLM rating, I-RBO, Purity), every DSL backbone–SLM combination significantly outperforms all eight baselines on Purity by a large margin. The most dramatic gains appear on short-text StackOverflow, where Purity jumps from .062 (ECRTM) to .805 (ECRTM+DSL with Qwen-3.5-0.8B) and from .265 (ProdLDA) to .803 (ProdLDA+DSL with Phi-3-mini). On 20NewsGroups, ECRTM+DSL achieves the best C_V (.423), ProdLDA+DSL the best LLM rating (2.89), and FASTopic+DSL maintains perfect I-RBO (1.000) on all three datasets. In retrieval evaluation, ECRTM+DSL with Phi-3-mini reaches .867 P@5 on StackOverflow, nearly tripling the best baseline (.306 BERTopic).

## Baselines & Comparisons

All 15 DSL variants (3 backbones × 5 SLMs) outperform every baseline on Purity across all three datasets, with the largest margins on short-text StackOverflow (e.g., ECRTM+DSL: .062 → .805; ProdLDA+DSL: .265 → .803). Against the strongest baselines, ECRTM+DSL (Qwen-0.8B) achieves the highest C_V (.423 on 20NewsGroups vs. .374 BERTopic, .373 ECRTM) and Purity (.561 vs. .416 FASTopic), while ProdLDA+DSL (Llama-1B) leads on LLM rating (2.89 vs. 2.59 FASTopic, 2.57 CombinedTM). FASTopic+DSL is the one exception: it trails the FASTopic baseline on C_V and LLM rating for 20NewsGroups (.344–.347 vs. .358; 2.04–2.24 vs. 2.59) due to a target–solver mismatch between the dense DSL target and FASTopic's OT-based DSR loss, though it still substantially improves Purity on TweetTopic and StackOverflow. The smallest SLM (ERNIE-4.5-0.3B) with DSL outperforms ZeroShotTM, CombinedTM, BERTopic, and FASTopic—which all use a comparably sized GTE-large-en-v1.5 encoder—demonstrating that distilling semantic labels from an SLM is more effective than relying on a sentence-encoder representation alone. In retrieval (Table 2), ECRTM+DSL and ProdLDA+DSL dominate all baselines by wide margins (e.g., .867 vs. .306 P@5 on StackOverflow), while FASTopic+DSL underperforms even the FASTopic baseline on TweetTopic retrieval (.627 vs. .811 P@5).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VAE-based neural topic model lineage (ProdLDA, ECRTM, FASTopic) by replacing their sparse Bag-of-Words reconstruction target with a dense, semantically-grounded soft-label distribution derived from a Small Language Model's next-token probabilities under a thematic prompt. It departs from prior LLM-for-topic-modeling work (TopicGPT, Mu et al., Yang et al.) that uses LLMs for evaluation, text augmentation, or direct topic generation, instead treating the LM purely as a frozen teacher whose output distribution is distilled into the existing probabilistic architecture via KL divergence. The use of the LM's final hidden state as the document input (rather than a separate sentence encoder) aligns the input representation with the target distribution, a design choice motivated by the implicit Bayesian interpretation of next-token prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The approach is remarkably simple—a single prompt and a forward pass through a sub-1B model—yet yields large, consistent gains in topic assignment accuracy (Purity) and retrieval precision across three datasets and five SLM families, while preserving the interpretable document-topic and topic-word structure that LLM-based alternatives sacrifice. The introduction of a retrieval-based evaluation metric (KL-divergence over topic distributions) opens a new axis for comparing topic models on downstream utility. Open questions include whether the target-solver mismatch observed with FASTopic's optimal-transport loss generalizes to other regularized architectures, how the method scales to very large corpora where per-document SLM inference becomes costly, and whether more sophisticated prompting (few-shot, CoT) or multi-modal extensions can further improve the soft-label quality.
