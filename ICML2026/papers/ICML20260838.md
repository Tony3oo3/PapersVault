## Problem

Epidemiological modeling pipelines rely heavily on manual model design: when policies change, variants emerge, or immunity assumptions shift, experts must redesign compartmental structures, revise transition logic, and re-implement simulators from scratch. While LLM-based agentic systems have been applied to code synthesis and automated research, they have not been explored for epidemiological modeling, and naively prompting LLMs to generate compartmental models produces frequent structural inconsistencies (invalid transitions, missing waning pathways, broken mass conservation) that are invisible to trajectory fitting alone.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes natural-language scenario specifications (defining vaccination policies, immune escape rates, and population heterogeneity), weekly epidemiological time series (infections, deaths across spatial or age-stratified patches), and a curated epidemiological knowledge base accessed via FAISS vector retrieval. It produces a verified directed flow graph (compartments as nodes, parameterized transitions as edges), a differentiable PyTorch simulator completing a fixed skeleton interface, calibrated disease parameters shared across scenarios, counterfactual projections with uncertainty intervals, and structured V&V reports. A key structural constraint is that the skeleton code fixes the execution interface (module layout, time indexing, output format) while leaving all epidemiological logic to the agent, and parameters are shared across scenarios unless explicitly overridden.

## Method

EPIAGENT's central design choice is an explicit epidemiological flow graph intermediate representation that decouples structural validation from equation synthesis, preventing invalid compartmental structures from propagating into executable code. The pipeline proceeds in stages: (1) scenario descriptions are embedded and augmented with retrieved epidemiological knowledge via RAG; (2) an LLM generates a directed flow graph under hard structural constraints (e.g., no direct S→R recovery, death only from severe states), and an iterative verification loop enforces valid transitions, conservation laws, and scenario-specific mechanisms (waning, immune escape, vaccination eligibility) before any code is written; (3) a planner agent compiles the verified graph into a differentiable ODE-based simulator within a fixed PyTorch skeleton, with an error-recovery module handling runtime failures; (4) parameters are calibrated via gradient-based optimization (Adam, MSE over levels and first-order differences); and (5) a multi-agent V&V stage with specialized verification, validation, reasoning, and feedback agents enforces non-negativity, mass conservation, monotonicity, and scenario fidelity, injecting actionable structural feedback (e.g., 'add waning transition') to drive iterative refinement. Ablations show that removing the flow graph drops correctness to ~42%, removing verification causes >87% of incorrect projections, and removing RAG increases generation iterations by ~20%.

## Results

On the COVID-19 Scenario Modeling Hub (Case Study I), EPIAGENT's time-variant dynamical model achieves a state-averaged MAE of 8.90×10⁻⁴ and RMSE of 1.10×10⁻³, while the neural-incorporated variant further reduces these to 6.27×10⁻⁴ MAE and 8.00×10⁻⁴ RMSE. At the nationwide level, the neural-incorporated model reaches 2.22×10⁻² MAE and 2.67×10⁻² RMSE. Ablations show that removing the flow-graph intermediate representation drops correctness to ~42%, removing verifier feedback to ~53%, and removing hard-coded constraints to ~69%, while omitting graph-level verification leads to incorrect scenario projections in over 87% of cases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EPIAGENT is compared against two general-purpose LLM agent frameworks: MLGym (Nathani et al., 2025) and HDTwinGen (Holt et al., 2024). MLGym defaults to naive SEIRD models and, despite explicit prompting, cannot reflect scenario-level differences such as vaccination policies or immune escape (Figure 18a). HDTwinGen produces structurally incorrect models with non-meaningful state flows that violate core mechanistic logic, such as impossible transitions (Figure 18b). Additionally, zero-shot prompting of frontier models (GPT-5.2 and Claude Opus 4.6) without the EPIAGENT pipeline yields plausible but epidemiologically imprecise simulators: GPT-5.2 shows systematically incorrect scenario ordering and breaks mass conservation, while Claude Opus 4.6 introduces extra states not specified in the scenario and fails to differentiate scenarios E/F. EPIAGENT's agentic pipeline with flow-graph verification and multi-agent V&V outperforms all baselines on structural correctness, scenario fidelity, and counterfactual consistency, achieving 100% valid model generation by iteration 5 versus the baselines' persistent structural failures.

## Relation to Prior Work

EPIAGENT extends the agentic code-generation paradigm (ReAct, Self-Refine, MetaGPT) to the domain of mechanistic epidemiological modeling, where prior agentic systems have targeted syntactic correctness and empirical performance but lack domain-specific structural constraints. The key architectural departure is the introduction of an epidemiological flow graph as an intermediate representation between natural-language scenario specifications and executable simulator code, enabling modular structural verification before any code is generated. This contrasts with symbolic regression and system identification approaches that recover equations purely from data, and with physics-informed neural networks that embed black-box components; EPIAGENT maintains full mechanistic interpretability while supporting scenario-conditioned counterfactual reasoning. The retrieval-augmented generation component grounds model construction in curated epidemiological literature, analogous to RAG in general LLM applications but specialized to compartmental transition semantics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a genuine bottleneck in public health modeling: the manual, iterative redesign of compartmental simulators whenever variants, policies, or immunity assumptions shift. The finding that 87% of unverified flow graphs produce incorrect projections, and that even frontier LLMs fail at zero-shot epidemiological model generation, underscores the necessity of the structural verification layer. Limitations include a fixed optimization strategy that can cause late-stage regression, bounded retry limits on error recovery, and the requirement for explicit scenario-relationship specification to enable cross-scenario counterfactual inference. Open questions include whether the flow-graph intermediate representation generalizes to other scientific modeling domains (e.g., climate, ecology), whether fine-tuning can reduce verification iterations, and how to handle genuinely incomplete or ambiguous scenario descriptions without under-specification.
