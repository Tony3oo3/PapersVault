## Problem

Prevailing RL paradigms for Large Vision-Language Models (e.g., PPO, GRPO) optimize solely for outcome correctness without enforcing cross-modal causal consistency, causing models to exploit language priors and spurious correlations rather than genuinely grounding reasoning in visual evidence. This manifests as a spectrum of failures—saliency deficiency (ignoring visual cues), misalignment (attending to irrelevant salient regions), and inertia (failing to update reasoning under hypothetical interventions)—that existing inference-time or input-level masking approaches cannot fundamentally rectify.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images and text queries as multimodal input to a Qwen2.5-VL-3B backbone, producing autoregressive text responses structured as chain-of-thought reasoning enclosed in think tags followed by a final answer in \boxed{}. Critically, the counterfactual intervention operates internally at the multi-head attention output level (the Z tensor), not at the input pixel level, so the model's forward pass is modified in-place during training without altering the external input format or requiring additional supervision signals.

## Method

CFPO introduces a Cross-Modal Counterfactual Intervention into the RL policy update loop by decomposing the attention matrix into High and Low Saliency regions via a statistical outlier mask (mean + 2σ threshold on the cross-modal attention sub-matrix). It then constructs a counterfactual attention output Z_cf by replacing the value vectors V in high-saliency regions with the global mean of all image token values, effectively suppressing specific visual cues while preserving the low-saliency context. The resulting KL divergence between the factual policy π_θ(o|Z) and the counterfactual policy π_cf(o|do(Z=Z_cf)) serves as a Counterfactual Regularization term (weighted by γ) added to the standard GRPO or DAPO objective, incentivizing the model to maximize the information gain from visual evidence. This representation-level intervention precisely isolates fine-grained semantic features driving reasoning, unlike prior input-level random masking (PAPO), and requires no external reward models or supervised fine-tuning.

## Results

On the overall average across 10 multimodal reasoning benchmarks (5 RealWorld-centric, 5 Mathematic-centric), CFPO_D achieves 58.49% accuracy, surpassing DAPO (55.60%) by +6.25% relative and PAPO_D (57.24%) by +2.13% relative. CFPO_G reaches 55.30%, improving over GRPO (53.88%) by +3.17% relative and PAPO_G (54.59%) by +1.32% relative. The largest individual gains appear on Geo3k (+15.22% over DAPO) and LogicVista (+12.26% over DAPO), indicating CFPO's particular strength in tasks demanding precise visual grounding for multi-step logical reasoning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), CFPO_G achieves a +3.17% relative overall gain (55.30 vs 53.88), with the largest per-benchmark improvements on Geo3k (+6.69%) and LogicVista (+5.03%), while remaining competitive on all 10 benchmarks. Against DAPO (Yu et al., 2025), CFPO_D delivers a substantially larger +6.25% relative gain (58.49 vs 55.60), with standout improvements on Geo3k (+15.22%) and LogicVista (+12.26%), suggesting that DAPO's removal of the KL reference penalty makes the policy more susceptible to spurious correlations that CFPO's counterfactual regularization effectively constrains. Against the state-of-the-art perception-aware method PAPO (Wang et al., 2025b), CFPO secures consistent but more modest gains of +1.32% (GRPO variant) and +2.13% (DAPO variant) relative, winning on 9/10 benchmarks in the DAPO setting (losing only marginally on Geo3k by −0.35% and MMMU-Pro by −0.27%). The paper attributes CFPO's edge over PAPO to performing intervention at the latent attention-output level rather than at the input pixel level, which PAPO uses via random masking, thereby preserving the structural integrity of semantic representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

CFPO extends the GRPO/DAPO policy optimization lineage by adding a counterfactual regularization term that performs causal intervention at the multi-head attention output level, rather than at the input pixel level as in PAPO. It draws on the structural causal model (do-operator) framework and connects to prior counterfactual decoding methods (VCD, ICD) and data-driven alignment approaches (DeFacto, CFVLM), but uniquely internalizes the causal necessity test into the RL training loop without external reward models or curated counterfactual datasets. The key departure from PAPO is the intervention granularity: CFPO operates on latent attention representations using a statistically-derived saliency mask, whereas PAPO applies coarse random input masking that can corrupt semantic structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CFPO offers a principled, plug-and-play mechanism for enforcing cross-modal causal consistency in LVLM RL training, addressing a recognized gap where outcome-based rewards cannot distinguish genuine visual grounding from linguistic-prior shortcuts. The modest but consistent gains over PAPO (1.32%–2.13%) and the notably larger DAPO-specific improvements (up to 15.22% on Geo3k) suggest the method is particularly valuable when the policy lacks a reference KL anchor. Key limitations include the ~50% training-time overhead from breaking fused attention kernels, evaluation restricted to Qwen2.5-VL-3B/7B and Qwen3-VL-2B, and the open question of whether the saliency-mask threshold and value-intervention strategy generalize to architectures with fundamentally different attention mechanisms (e.g., sliding-window or linear attention).
