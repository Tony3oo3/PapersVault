## Problem

Existing nonconvex convergence guarantees for LMO-based optimizers (Normalized SGD, SignSGD, Muon) rely on uniformly bounded variance or bounded p-th moments, which can fail even for simple finite-sum objectives on unbounded domains. This limitation obscures nontrivial batch-size effects and leaves a gap between the empirically observed batch-size sensitivity of modern optimizers and available theory. The paper addresses this by developing a unified convergence analysis under the more general ABC (expected smoothness) condition, which is faithful to subsampling regimes.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an unbiased stochastic gradient oracle (with mini-batch size b under τ-nice sampling), a momentum parameter α, and a step size γ, and operates in a general norm geometry (ℓ₂, ℓ∞, or spectral norm) rather than being restricted to Euclidean space. It produces an explicit nonconvex convergence rate bound of Õ(K^{-1/4}), a critical mini-batch size b* that minimizes total sample complexity, and closed-form scaling laws predicting how the optimal momentum α*(b) and step size γ*(b) grow with batch size. The ABC constants (A, B, C) are the key problem-dependent inputs that replace the single variance bound σ², and under τ-nice sampling they explicitly encode the finite-population correction (n−b)/(n−1).

## Method

The paper introduces a generic momentum LMO update template (m^{k+1} = (1−α)m^k + αg^k; d^k ∈ LMO(m^{k+1}); x^{k+1} = x^k + γd^k) that unifies Normalized SGD, SignSGD with momentum, and Muon as special cases determined by the choice of norm. The central technical novelty is a self-bounding closure argument: under ABC, the stochastic noise magnitude depends on the evolving function gaps Δ^k, creating a circular dependency between the momentum tracking error and the optimization history. This is resolved by deriving a crude upper bound U ≤ D + E√U on the average gap, solving the resulting quadratic to obtain √U ≤ E + √D, and substituting back to close the loop. Under τ-nice sampling, the ABC constant A_b = (n−b)/(b(n−1))·L_max makes the finite-population correction explicit, yielding the prediction that optimal momentum scales as α* ∝ √(b(n−1)/(n−b))·K^{-1/2} and step size as γ* ∝ (b(n−1)/(n−b))^{1/4}·K^{-3/4}, with a unique critical batch size b* ≈ nH/(1+H) that balances increasing per-step cost against decreasing stochastic noise.

## Results

The paper establishes a unified O(K^{-1/4}) nonconvex convergence rate (i.e., O(ε^{-4}) iterations) for the momentum LMO family (Normalized SGDM, SignSGD, Muon) under the ABC condition, via a novel self-bounding closure that handles momentum-induced history coupling. Under τ-nice sampling, the theory predicts a critical batch size b*(ε) ≈ H_norm(ε)/(1+H_norm(ε))·n and scaling laws α*(b) ∝ √(b(n−1)/(n−b))·K^{-1/2} and γ*(b) ∝ (b(n−1)/(n−b))^{1/4}·K^{-3/4}. Empirically, on linear regression the critical batch size shifts from b*=1 (α≤0.5) to larger values as α increases; on matrix regression with Muon, b_crit grows from 1 to 16 as α goes from 0.1 to 0.9; and on WikiText-103 (~6M-param Nano-GPT), a U-shaped sample-complexity curve is observed with the optimal step size increasing with batch size, matching the theoretical diagonal shift.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between the classical bounded-variance (BV) noise model and the ABC (expected smoothness) model for predicting batch-size behavior. Under BV, the sample complexity T_BV(b,ε) = Õ(b/ε² ∨ √b/ε ∨ 1/ε³ ∨ 1/ε⁴) is nondecreasing in b, predicting that mini-batching offers no sample-complexity benefit (optimal at b=1). Under τ-nice sampling, the ABC-based bound T_τ(b,ε) = Õ(b/ε² ∨ b/ε ∨ (n−b)/(n−1)·1/ε³ ∨ (n−b)/(n−1)·1/ε⁴) retains the finite-population correction (n−b)/(n−1), creating a genuine trade-off and a nontrivial critical batch size. The empirical experiments on linear regression, matrix regression (Muon), and WikiText-103 text prediction all confirm the ABC predictions: the optimal momentum and step size increase with batch size (a 'diagonal shift' in the α–b landscape), and a U-shaped sample-complexity curve appears that the BV analysis cannot explain. The paper also notes that the common LLM-training practice of large mini-batches with small momentum corresponds to a 'pseudo-critical' batch regime identified in the harder (smaller ε) instances.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the convergence analysis of momentum-based LMO optimizers (normalized SGD, SignSGD, Muon) from the classical bounded-variance noise model to the more general ABC (expected smoothness) condition of Khaled & Richtárik (2020). Prior analyses of this family — Cutkosky & Mehta (2020) for normalized SGDM, Pethick et al. (2025a) for the general LMO family, and Kovalev (2025) for Muon via trust-regions — all rely on uniformly bounded variance or bounded p-th moments, which the paper shows can fail even for simple finite-sum quadratics on unbounded domains. The key technical departure is a 'self-bounding closure' that resolves the history-coupling between momentum and the ABC-controlled noise, a step that is unnecessary under bounded variance where the noise level is a constant. The paper also connects to the broader SGD noise-growth literature (strong growth, gradient confusion, expected smoothness) but is the first to bring these conditions to the momentum-LMO family.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theory explains a practically observed but previously unexplained phenomenon: the optimal momentum must increase with batch size to maximize sample efficiency, a coupling invisible under bounded-variance analysis. The derived critical batch size formula and the 'U-shaped' sample complexity curves provide concrete, testable predictions for optimizer hyperparameter tuning in finite-sum regimes, with empirical confirmation on linear/matrix regression and a 6M-parameter language model. Open questions include whether the critical-batch and momentum-scaling predictions hold at LLM scale (billions of parameters), whether the self-bounding closure extends to distributed or compressed settings, and whether the pseudo-critical batch size observed for small momentum at large b (linked to LLM training practice) can be captured more precisely by the theory.
