## Problem

Current AI systems cannot independently perform operational extreme weather diagnosis, which requires multi-step logical reasoning, dynamic tool invocation, and expert-level prior judgment to identify physically meaningful anomalies from fragmented, multivariate datasets. Existing agentic frameworks lack sufficient domain-specific expertise integration, a heuristic reasoning loop for purposeful evidence collection, and fine-grained evaluation systems for complex meteorological workflows under extreme conditions.

## Input / Output

The system takes a natural-language diagnostic query (e.g., 'Analyze the heavy rain on May 8, 2014 in southern China') and retrieves corresponding meteorological reanalysis data from WeatherBench2/ERA5, classifying the event into one of five categories (rainstorm, snowstorm, gale, heat wave, cold wave). It produces a structured diagnostic report containing a reasoning trace, at least five confirmed anomalous factors (indices and figures), and supporting visualizations, with the closed-loop HVR mechanism iterating up to 50 turns until sufficient evidence is gathered. The benchmark evaluates outputs at three granularities: atomic index calculation (150 QA pairs across 30 index types), atomic figure generation (100 QA pairs across 20 figure types), and full-pipeline diagnosis (100 events).

## Method

HVR-Met is a seven-agent multi-agent system (Decomposer, Data Specialist, Code Executor, Plotter, Image Checker, Diagnostician, Reporter) built on the AG2 framework, whose central innovation is a Hypothesis-Verification-Replanning closed loop. The Decomposer formulates an initial hypothesis by selecting five diagnostic factors from a semi-automatically constructed Guideline Library (extracted from 584 extreme weather papers via MinerU and validated by five senior forecasters), then the Data Specialist computes indices using MetPy and the Plotter renders figures using Cartopy/Matplotlib, both supported by RAG modules (Index Knowledge Base and Visualization Code Repository). The Diagnostician evaluates multimodal evidence for anomalies; if fewer than five factors confirm the expected anomalies, the system records negative results, retrieves alternative causal logic from the Guideline Library, and triggers a new verification iteration. This replanning mechanism simulates the iterative cognitive workflow of human forecasters, distinguishing the system from single-pass or fixed-path agentic approaches.

## Results

Using GPT-5 as the backbone, HVR-Met achieves the highest scores across all five diagnostic stages: Hypothesis (4.70), Data (4.92), Index (4.07), Figure (4.13), and Final Report (3.94) on a 0–5 scale where >4 is a pass. At the atomic sub-task level, the system attains pass rates of 71.86% for meteorological index calculation (150 tasks, 30 index types), 79.52% for figure generation (100 tasks, 20 figure types), and 85% for full-pipeline diagnostic reporting (100 extreme weather events). Ablation studies confirm that removing the Guideline Library collapses the Final Report score from 3.94 to 0.40, and removing the Decomposer drops it to 1.12, underscoring the critical role of domain knowledge and structured planning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against other agentic weather-diagnosis systems (e.g., Zephyrus, ClimateAgent, EWE) but instead compares four LLM backbones powering the identical HVR-Met multi-agent framework. GPT-5 leads on every dimension, with the largest margins over Qwen3-Coder in the Figure stage (4.13 vs. 2.46) and Final Report stage (3.94 vs. 2.33). Gemini-3-Pro-Preview-Thinking is a close second, trailing GPT-5 by only 0.07 on Hypothesis and 0.18 on Final Report. DeepSeek-R1-0528 and Qwen3-Coder-480B both fall below the 4.0 pass threshold on Final Report (2.97 and 2.33, respectively), with execution logs attributing the gap to failures in dimension/unit conversion and weak error-correction resilience when calling specialized meteorological libraries (MetPy, Cartopy). On the atomic index-calculation sub-task at the Hard difficulty level, reasoning-enhanced models (Gemini-3-Pro-thinking 46.43%, DeepSeek-R1 42.31%) actually surpass GPT-5 (31.25%), suggesting that multi-step derivations benefit from explicit chain-of-thought reasoning even when the overall agentic workflow favors GPT-5.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the emerging line of meteorological agentic frameworks (Zephyrus, ClimateAgent, EWE) by introducing a domain-specific multi-agent architecture with a closed-loop Hypothesis-Verification-Replanning mechanism, departing from EWE's predefined thought paths and Zephyrus's general-purpose tool integration. Its key conceptual departure is the semi-automatically constructed Guideline Library (extracted from 584 papers and validated by senior forecasters), which serves as a structured knowledge backbone that prior agentic weather systems lacked. The system is built on the AG2 multi-agent framework and leverages RAG with domain-specific knowledge bases (MetPy documentation, Cartopy plotting scripts), repurposing standard retrieval-augmented generation for a highly specialized scientific workflow.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine operational gap: no prior system could autonomously execute the full diagnostic chain (data retrieval → index computation → visualization → abductive reasoning → report) for extreme weather events, and the introduced benchmark (100 events, 150 index QA pairs, 100 figure QA pairs) fills a critical evaluation void for atomic-level meteorological sub-tasks. A key limitation is the reliance on reanalysis data; real operational deployment must handle biased NWP model outputs (GRAPES, GFS, ECMWF), which could cascade errors through the diagnostic pipeline. Open questions include whether the HVR loop's 50-turn cap is sufficient for rare compound events, how the system generalizes beyond the five weather types covered, and whether the LLM-based scoring rubric (validated at 30% human sampling) remains reliable as model capabilities shift.
