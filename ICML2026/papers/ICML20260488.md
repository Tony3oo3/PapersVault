## Problem

Most CT reconstruction and generation pipelines require training a CT-specific VAE from scratch or heavily fine-tuning one on large medical corpora, incurring substantial compute cost and engineering complexity while degrading under heterogeneous scanners, protocols, and disease patterns. This paper asks whether a single Foundation VAE pretrained at scale on natural images and videos can be reused as a unified, training-free CT interface for reconstruction, augmentation, and generation without any medical fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

For reconstruction and augmentation, the method takes a 3D CT volume (512×512 per slice, 100–600 slices, intensities clipped to [−1000, 1000] HU) and produces a reconstructed volume via the frozen encoder-decoder, which is then paired with the original labels for segmentation training. For generation, the input is a composite conditioning signal: an organ mask (124 structures from VISTA3D + TotalSegmentator) plus an optional disease mask, concatenated into a single mask volume and encoded by the same frozen VAE encoder into a latent embedding, together with a radiology report processed by a frozen text encoder. The output is a 3D CT volume at 512×512×128 resolution, generated by a denoising U-Net operating in the fixed VAE latent space.

## Method

The core mechanism is a single frozen Foundation VAE (e.g., WAN2.1/2.2 video VAE) whose encoder E and decoder D are never updated on medical data; the reconstruction operator T(x)=D(E(x)) acts as a boundary-stable denoiser that suppresses acquisition noise while preserving anatomical geometry, enabling reconstruction-based augmentation where segmentation models are trained on (T(x), y) instead of (x, y). For CT generation, a conditional latent diffusion model is trained in the fixed VAE latent space with three conditioning channels: (1) the mask volume is encoded by the same frozen VAE encoder to produce a latent embedding z_m that is concatenated with the noised latent z_t along the channel dimension at every denoising block, providing explicit spatial grounding; (2) radiology report embeddings from a frozen text encoder are injected via cross-attention for semantic control; and (3) a lightweight 3D consistency module applies a learnable slice-axis convolution (initialized as a Dirac delta) across F consecutive axial slices to enforce volumetric coherence. A theoretical analysis shows that under a task-relevant reconstruction stability condition, the segmentation risk gap from training on reconstructions is bounded by 2L_ε·ε_φ, where ε_φ measures preservation of task-relevant geometric structure.

## Results

For CT generation, the conditional latent diffusion model in the frozen Foundation VAE latent space achieves FVD 0.30 and FID 2.29 on normal CT, and FVD 0.51 and FID 4.35 on disease CT, with CT-CLIP semantic scores of 59.35 and 51.49 respectively—substantially outperforming all baselines. For CT reconstruction, off-the-shelf video VAEs (e.g., IVVAE) achieve PSNR 31.78 (Lung) and 40.43 (Pancreas) on MSD, surpassing CT-specific VAEs MedVAE (30.06/36.00) and MAISI (29.78/36.97). Reconstruction-based augmentation improves segmentation NSD by 3.9% on average for pancreatic and lung tumors, and 500 synthetic volumes improve downstream multi-label classification mean AUC from 67.95 to 70.71 (+2.76).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MAISI (the strongest baseline, which also uses mask conditioning), the proposed method wins on FVD (0.30 vs 0.44 normal; 0.51 vs 0.64 disease), FID (2.29 vs 8.16 normal; 4.35 vs 6.45 disease), and organ-mask adherence (Vessel Dice 63.38 vs 13.50; Rib Dice 70.23 vs 15.20), while MAISI does not report CT-CLIP scores. Against GenerateCT and MedSyn (text-only conditioning), the gap is even larger: FID drops from 15.40/12.17 to 2.29 on normal CT, and CT-CLIP jumps from 2.35/25.40 to 59.35, indicating that joint mask+text conditioning is critical for semantic fidelity. Against MedVAE (a CT-specific VAE), the ablation in Table 6 shows the Foundation VAE reduces FID from 11.28 to 2.19 (normal) and boosts CT-CLIP from 20.76 to 59.35, confirming that the latent-space quality of the pretrained video VAE is the primary driver of generation performance. On downstream classification (Table 5), the proposed method achieves the best mean AUC of 70.71, outperforming MedSyn (69.70) and GenerateCT (69.80), though individual disease categories show complementary strengths across generators.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard CT generation pipeline, which requires training a CT-specific VAE (e.g., MedVAE, MAISI) before learning a diffusion model in its latent space, by showing that a frozen Foundation VAE pretrained on natural images and videos (primarily the Wan video model family) can serve directly as the CT encoder-decoder without any medical fine-tuning. It builds on the latent diffusion framework established by GenerateCT and MedSyn for text-conditioned CT synthesis, and on mask-conditioned approaches like MAISI, but replaces the domain-specific representation stage with an off-the-shelf natural-domain VAE. The theoretical analysis connects to denoising autoencoder and plug-and-play reconstruction literature, framing the frozen VAE as a boundary-stable reconstruction operator rather than a learned medical feature extractor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Eliminating the CT-specific VAE training stage (which for MAISI required 37K volumes, 8 V100 GPUs, and 300 epochs) substantially lowers the compute and engineering barrier to CT generation, and the finding that natural-video VAE latents outperform purpose-built medical VAEs (MedVAE) in the same diffusion pipeline suggests a broader principle about transferability of large-scale reconstruction priors. However, the scope is currently limited to chest CT, controllability depends heavily on input mask quality, rare and long-tail disease categories remain challenging, and institution-specific scanner characteristics may limit generalization. Open questions include whether this training-free VAE reuse extends to other modalities (MRI, ultrasound) and body regions, and whether the 3D consistency module can be replaced by more principled volumetric inductive biases.
