## Problem

Existing diffusion-based generative image steganography methods (e.g., CRoSS, DiffStega) confine secret data to a single fixed hiding space (the initial noise), which caps capacity at one secret image, causes severe inter-secret interference when multiple images are forced into the same space, and leaves the concealed data extractable by any party who knows the hiding location. No prior method provides a receiver-side authentication mechanism to restrict extraction to authorized parties.

## Input / Output

The method takes one or more secret RGB images (256×256 in main experiments, 512×512 for generalization), a reference image for IP-Adapter-based guidance, and a symmetric key shared between sender and receiver. It produces a single stego image generated entirely from Gaussian noise via the diffusion model (no external cover image required), and upon authenticated extraction, recovers the original secret image(s). Multiple secret images can be concealed within one stego image by assigning each to distinct timesteps in the diffusion trajectory.

## Method

The framework decomposes each secret image's latent representation into C channel fragments and dynamically assigns each fragment to the most similar channel of an intermediate diffusion latent (selected via cosine similarity across all timesteps and channels), then refines the selected cover with a 5-layer DenseNet to minimize secret-cover interference. A Signature and Authentication Controller embeds a digital signature (generated from the symmetric key via a lightweight CNN) into the hiding space using a watermark network; the receiver must authenticate this signature channel-by-channel before extraction, enforcing receiver isolation. The EDICT coupled-transformation framework provides deterministic forward and inverse diffusion trajectories, enabling exact recovery of intermediate latents at both conceal and reveal time. Training uses a composite L1 loss combining conceal fidelity, reveal reconstruction, and signature consistency terms, applied on top of pretrained Stable Diffusion V1.5.

## Results

On the UniStega dataset, the proposed method achieves 26.59 dB PSNR and 0.80 SSIM for authenticated receiver recovery, surpassing CRoSS (21.25 dB / 0.71) and DiffStega (23.29 dB / 0.77). For stego/secret similarity (lower is better), it reduces PSNR to 9.48 dB and SSIM to 0.21, compared to 19.03/0.66 (CRoSS) and 18.61/0.59 (DiffStega), indicating far less resemblance between stego and secret images. For unauthenticated receivers, the method limits recovery to 9.11 dB PSNR versus 17.53 dB (DiffStega), confirming effective receiver isolation. In multi-image settings, concealing 2 images yields 24.08 dB (matching DiffStega's single-image 23.92 dB), and 4 images yields 22.61 dB (exceeding CRoSS's single-image 21.25 dB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CRoSS (NeurIPS 2024), the proposed method wins decisively on all metrics: +5.34 dB PSNR for authenticated recovery (26.59 vs 21.25), −9.55 dB PSNR for stego/secret similarity (9.48 vs 19.03), and uniquely supports multi-image concealment (up to 8 images) while CRoSS is limited to one. Against DiffStega (IJCAI 2024), the gains are +3.30 dB PSNR for authenticated recovery (26.59 vs 23.29) and −9.13 dB for stego/secret similarity (9.48 vs 18.61); critically, the unauthenticated receiver PSNR drops from 17.53 dB (DiffStega) to 9.11 dB (Ours), demonstrating that DiffStega leaks secret information to unauthorized parties while the proposed method does not. Against cover-based baselines ISN (CVPR 2021) and HINET (ICCV 2021), the proposed method achieves superior naturalness (NIQE 5.49 vs 11.28 and 5.35) and lower steganalysis detection accuracy (50.57–50.71% vs 53–55% for generative methods and 76–86% for cover-based), confirming that generative steganography inherently resists detection better than cover-based approaches. The proposed method also outperforms UDH (NeurIPS 2020) in steganalysis resistance (50.57% vs 76.34% SRNet detection). Notably, the multi-image capability is unique: concealing 4 images at 22.61 dB exceeds CRoSS's single-image 21.25 dB, demonstrating that capacity expansion does not come at the cost of per-image quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends diffusion-based generative steganography (CRoSS, DiffStega) by replacing the fixed initial-noise hiding space with a dynamic, multi-timestep and multi-channel hiding strategy, leveraging EDICT's exact inversion to recover intermediate latents deterministically. It departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) decomposing secret images into per-channel fragments and assigning each to a separately selected and optimized cover channel, and (2) introducing a cryptographic signature-and-authentication layer that gates extraction to authorized receivers only. The cover optimization module (a small DenseNet) and the cosine-similarity-based channel selection are novel architectural components not present in prior generative steganography pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first generative steganography framework to enforce receiver-side authentication, bridging image steganography and cryptographic access control in a way that prior coverless methods lacked. The multi-image capacity (up to 8 secrets per stego image) with near-single-image reconstruction quality addresses a practical bottleneck for high-bandwidth covert communication. Open questions include whether the symmetric-key authentication scales to large receiver groups without key-management overhead, how the method performs under adversarial re-encoding or model-swap attacks, and whether the dynamic hiding strategy generalizes to video or 3D content where the temporal dimension adds another axis of hiding space.
