## Problem

RLVR optimization for LLMs has largely inherited AdamW from pretraining and SFT, despite fundamental differences in training dynamics (non-stationary data, O(1)-bit reward signals, low-dimensional update geometry). This paper asks whether AdamW's two key components—momentum and per-parameter adaptive learning rates—are actually necessary for effective RLVR, or whether the simpler and more memory-efficient SGD suffices.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained LLM (Qwen3-1.7B, Qwen3-8B, or Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct) and trains it via RLVR using verifiable reward signals from math (NuminaMath-CoT), coding (APPS/CodeContests/TACO/Codeforces), or RLVE environments. A notable practical detail is that SGD requires a learning rate of 10⁻¹ versus AdamW's 10⁻⁶, because AdamW's effective per-parameter rates (η/√v) span 10⁻¹ to 10² despite its nominal rate. The output is an RLVR-fine-tuned model whose parameter updates are extremely sparse under SGD (only 0.02%–0.46% of weights change), with no explicit sparsity regularization applied.

## Method

The paper conducts a controlled ablation of AdamW's components in RLVR by comparing four optimizers—SGD, SGD+Momentum, RMSProp, and AdamW—across two RL algorithms (GRPO and PPO), three verifiable domains, and two model families. Two analytical observations motivate the hypothesis: (1) the second-moment distribution √v in RLVR has ~22× lower standard deviation than in SFT, making per-parameter adaptive rates less informative, and (2) the cosine similarity between accumulated momentum and the current gradient drops from 0.997 in SFT to −0.007 in RL, indicating momentum points in misaligned directions due to non-stationarity. Experiments confirm that neither momentum nor adaptive learning rates consistently improve performance, with momentum often being detrimental. The most striking finding is that SGD induces updates with 99.9%+ sparsity and substantially lower effective rank than AdamW, attributed to the absence of adaptive rescaling that would otherwise amplify small-magnitude updates above the floating-point rounding threshold.

## Results

On mathematical reasoning (Table 2), SGD matches or slightly outperforms AdamW across all three model families: on Qwen3-8B at 8K rollout, SGD achieves a mean score of 70.0 vs. AdamW's 69.5; on Qwen3-1.7B at 3K, SGD scores 48.6 vs. AdamW's 46.0. On code generation (Table 3), SGD consistently outperforms AdamW, achieving an average pass@1 improvement of +8.7% (e.g., 49.3% vs. 44.0% on HumanEval for Qwen3-1.7B at 4K). Remarkably, SGD updates only 0.01%–0.46% of model parameters (99.99% sparsity) versus ~10% for AdamW, and reduces peak GPU memory by 15.7 GB on Qwen3-1.7B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AdamW (Loshchilov & Hutter, 2019), SGD matches or outperforms across all model families and domains: on math, SGD's mean scores are within 0.2–2.6 points of AdamW (e.g., 70.0 vs. 69.5 on Qwen3-8B at 8K), while on coding SGD achieves a clear +8.7% pass@1 advantage (49.3% vs. 44.0% on HumanEval). SGD+Momentum consistently underperforms both SGD and AdamW on math (e.g., 47.1 vs. 55.6 mean on Qwen3-8B at 3K), confirming that momentum is counterproductive in RLVR. Against RMSProp (Ruder, 2017), which isolates the adaptive learning rate component, results are mixed: RMSProp slightly edges out SGD on Qwen3-8B at 8K (71.1 vs. 70.0) but underperforms on Llama-3.1-8B (28.4 vs. 29.6 at 8K), indicating that adaptive learning rates do not consistently help. In terms of update sparsity, SGD updates only 0.01%–0.46% of parameters versus ~9–13% for AdamW, a >500× difference, while RMSProp produces sparsity levels comparable to AdamW, confirming that the absence of per-parameter adaptive learning rates is the key driver of SGD's extreme sparsity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant practice of using AdamW for all LLM training stages by questioning whether its two key components—momentum and per-parameter adaptive learning rates—are actually beneficial in the RLVR regime. It builds directly on recent empirical findings that RLVR induces fundamentally different training dynamics than SFT: sparser parameter updates (Mukherjee et al., 2025), concentration in off-principal directions (Zhu et al., 2025), and reduced catastrophic forgetting (Chen et al., 2025; Shenfeld et al., 2025). The paper repurposes the standard ablation framework (removing momentum → RMSProp; removing adaptive LR → SGD+momentum; removing both → SGD) to isolate which AdamW components, if any, are load-bearing in RLVR, in contrast to the extensive prior literature establishing AdamW's superiority over SGD in supervised transformer training (Pan and Li, 2023; Zhao et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that SGD matches or outperforms AdamW in RLVR while updating fewer than 0.02% of parameters (over 1000× sparser) without any sparsity regularization provides a mechanistic explanation for why RL-trained models forget less and depend heavily on pretrained capabilities. Practically, the 15.7 GB memory reduction on a 1.7B model is immediately actionable and could democratize RL-based post-training for resource-constrained labs. Open questions include whether these findings hold at frontier scale (70B+), whether the extreme sparsity can be exploited for RL-specific parameter-efficient fine-tuning, and whether purpose-built RL-native optimizers (e.g., leveraging the low-dimensional update geometry) can further improve efficiency. The paper's scope is limited to models up to 8B and two model families, and the required SGD learning rate (0.1) is 10^5–10^6× AdamW's nominal rate, though the authors show this is consistent with AdamW's own effective per-parameter rates.
