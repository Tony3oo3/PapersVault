## Problem

Existing speculative decoding draft models, including the state-of-the-art EAGLE series, condition solely on the current prefix, causing error accumulation where draft predictions drift from the target model's distribution over successive steps. No prior method equips the draft model with information about the target model's planned future generation direction, leaving a systematic gap in how well draft tokens align with the target's intended trajectory.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263746/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The target model receives the input prefix augmented with learnable soft prompt tokens (prepended to the KV cache) and one contemplate token per draft node in the speculative tree, producing both verification logits and a continuous future prediction vector f (the hidden state of the contemplate token). The lightweight single-layer Transformer draft model conditions on the target's down-projected hidden states, its own autoregressive hidden states, and the fixed future prediction vector f appended as an auxiliary token, outputting a tree of candidate draft tokens. The MoE modules that generate the dynamic contemplate token embedding [con] and future token embedding [f] take as input the hidden state of the most recently accepted token (from the target or draft model respectively), making the guidance context-dependent rather than static.

## Method

ConFu introduces three coupled mechanisms: (1) contemplate tokens and soft prompts that, with the target model frozen, are trained to expose the target's intermediate reasoning as a continuous embedding at negligible inference cost (one extra token per draft node); (2) a Mixture-of-Experts module that replaces the static contemplate token embedding with a dynamic one, where a linear router over the last accepted token's hidden state selects top-K learnable expert embeddings and outputs their weighted sum, enabling context-adaptive future direction signals; (3) a training framework using anchor token sampling (inserting contemplate tokens only at K_train randomly sampled positions to avoid doubling sequence length) and future prediction replication (reusing the anchor's future prediction f_t for a window of l nearby non-anchor tokens to encourage positional robustness). The draft model is initialized from EAGLE-3 checkpoints and further trained with a KL-divergence objective under the train-time testing paradigm, with the future prediction vector f as an additional conditioning input. During inference, the target model verifies the draft tree and generates a distinct future prediction for every draft node in parallel via modified tree attention, then passes the prediction corresponding to the last accepted token to the draft model for the next iteration.

## Results

On SpecBench, ConFu consistently outperforms EAGLE-3 across all target models, temperatures, and draft tree sizes. For Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (30 nodes, T=0), ConFu raises average accepted length τ from 4.59 to 5.01 (+9.2%) and speed-up ratio from 2.36 to 2.69 (+14.0%). For Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct, average τ improves by ~11% and SR by ~8.2% across configurations. On Qwen3-4B, the gains are largest: ConFu achieves a 21.0% improvement in τ and 19.8% in SR over EAGLE-3 on average, with the gap peaking at 20.7% τ improvement (4.60 vs 3.81) at 60 nodes, T=0.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is EAGLE-3 (Li et al., NeurIPS 2025), the current state-of-the-art speculative decoding draft model. ConFu wins on every single configuration tested — all three target models (Llama-3.2-3B, Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen3-4B), all temperatures (0.0, 0.7, 1.0), and both draft tree sizes (30, 60 nodes) — on both metrics (accepted length τ and speed-up ratio SR). The advantage is most pronounced at low temperature (T=0), where the target distribution is sharper and future direction is easier to anticipate: e.g., on Llama-3.1-8B with 30 nodes, τ improves from 4.59→5.01 and SR from 2.36→2.69. On Qwen3-4B the gap widens substantially to ~20% in both τ and SR, suggesting ConFu's future-aware signals are especially beneficial for models where EAGLE-3's prefix-only conditioning accumulates more error. Ablations confirm both the MoE dynamic contemplate tokens (+0.05 τ, +0.02 SR) and future prediction replication (+0.17 τ) contribute independently, and peak GPU memory overhead is negligible (e.g., 17.12 GB vs 17.08 GB for the 8B model).

## Relation to Prior Work

ConFu extends the EAGLE-3 speculative decoding framework by augmenting the draft model with future-oriented signals extracted from the target model via contemplate (pause) tokens and learnable soft prompts, drawing on the pause-token literature (Goyal et al., 2024) and BiTA (Lin et al., 2025). The key conceptual departure is bridging speculative decoding with continuous latent reasoning: rather than conditioning the drafter solely on the current prefix (as in EAGLE-1/2/3), ConFu provides the drafter with a 'future prediction' vector that captures the target model's intermediate thought, reducing error accumulation over autoregressive draft steps. The dynamic MoE parameterization of contemplate tokens and the anchor-token-sampling training strategy are novel architectural and training contributions layered on top of the EAGLE-3 draft head.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ConFu demonstrates that equipping draft models with lightweight future-aware signals yields consistent 8–11% (Llama-3) and ~20% (Qwen3-4B) gains in acceptance length and speed-up over EAGLE-3 at negligible memory overhead (<0.05 GB), making it a practical drop-in improvement for production speculative decoding pipelines. The paper opens a promising direction of integrating latent/continuous reasoning tokens into inference acceleration, but leaves open questions about scaling the number of contemplate tokens, generalization to non-instruction tasks, and whether the gains persist at higher temperatures where the target distribution is flatter. The modest ablation gain from MoE (0.05 in accept length) versus the larger gain from future prediction replication (0.17) suggests the core mechanism is the future signal itself, with dynamicity being a secondary refinement.
