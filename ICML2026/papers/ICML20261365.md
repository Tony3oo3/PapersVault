## Problem

Federated multi-view clustering (FMVC) for remote sensing data remains entirely unexplored. Centralized MVC methods require aggregating petabytes of multi-view imagery on a single server, which is infeasible due to strict data-privacy constraints on sensitive geospatial information and prohibitive communication costs. The core technical challenge is achieving globally consistent cluster assignments across distributed clients without transmitting raw data or intermediate features, which deprives the system of a unified semantic reference space and causes severe prototype misalignment.

## Input / Output

Each client holds a distinct view (e.g., HSI, SAR, LiDAR, DSM) of the same geographical area as raw imagery of shape H×W×D. A superpixel segmentation map (M superpixels via SLIC) computed on the first view is shared with all clients, who then aggregate features within each superpixel region to form local matrices of shape M×D. Only privatized prototypes (K×d) and co-occurrence matrices (K×K) are uploaded to the server; the final output is a unified hard-label assignment over all M superpixels obtained by averaging aligned soft assignment matrices across clients.

## Method

On the client side, a graph autoencoder with graph convolutional layers encodes superpixel features into a low-dimensional latent space while preserving spatial context; local K-means prototypes are then obfuscated via adaptive noise generated by a dual-branch MLP conditioned on the prototypes (reparameterization trick) before transmission. On the server side, two modules resolve cross-client misalignment: (1) Federated Co-occurrence Structural Alignment (FCSA) computes co-occurrence matrices (Gram matrices of soft assignments) and finds optimal permutation matrices via a differentiable Hungarian-algorithm surrogate to structurally align prototypes across views; (2) Wasserstein Prototype Contrastive Learning (WPCL) models each prototype as an isotropic Gaussian N(μ, σ²I) and minimizes an InfoNCE loss built on the closed-form 2-Wasserstein distance, pulling corresponding prototype distributions together while pushing non-corresponding ones apart. The total loss jointly optimizes local reconstruction, FCSA alignment, and WPCL contrastive objectives, with only gradients transmitted back to clients, and a theoretical bound (Theorem 1) connects the WPCL loss to the mutual information between view prototype distributions.

## Results

FedRSMVC achieves state-of-the-art federated multi-view clustering across all four remote sensing benchmarks. On Trento, it reaches 87.0% ACC and 77.0% NMI, outperforming the best baseline (FedDMVC) by 15.3% in ACC and 12.9% in NMI. On the highly heterogeneous Augsburg dataset (HS + SAR + DSM), FedRSMVC attains 70.1% ACC and 44.1% NMI, surpassing the next-best method (Fed-MVKM) by 28.0% in ACC and 26.9% in NMI. On Salinas, it achieves 73.3% ACC and 74.9% NMI, a 19.6% ACC gain over FedDMVC. Three competing deep methods (FMVC-IMK, TensorFMVC, MGCD) fail entirely with out-of-memory errors on all four datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedRSMVC decisively outperforms all seven baselines on three of four datasets. Against FedDMVC (Chen et al., ACM MM 2023), the closest competitor, FedRSMVC gains +15.3% ACC on Trento (87.0 vs 71.7), +19.6% ACC on Salinas (73.3 vs 53.7), and +23.5% ACC on Augsburg (70.1 vs 46.6); on XuZhou the gap narrows to +1.3% ACC (61.0 vs 59.7), where FedDMVC retains a slight edge in KAPPA (54.0 vs 52.3) and NMI (54.5 vs 51.5). Fed-MVKM (Yang & Sinaga, TPAMI 2025) is the second-strongest baseline on Augsburg but trails FedRSMVC by 28.0% in ACC. The traditional methods Fed-MVFCM and Fed-MVFPC (Hu et al., TFS 2024) and CeFMC (Liu et al., TPAMI 2026) all underperform by 15–35% in ACC across datasets, reflecting the limited representational capacity of non-deep approaches. Three deep baselines—FMVC-IMK and TensorFMVC (Feng et al., IJCAI 2024) and MGCD (Sun et al., AAAI 2025)—encounter out-of-memory failures on every dataset, highlighting the computational infeasibility of their architectures for remote sensing-scale data. FedMVL (Huang et al., PR 2022) performs worst among runnable methods, with ACC below 42% on all datasets where it completes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: centralized deep multi-view clustering for remote sensing (autoencoder-based and contrastive-learning-based methods) and general-domain federated multi-view clustering (FedMVL, FedDMVC, FMVC-IMK, etc.). It departs from prior FMVC by transmitting only noise-perturbed prototypes rather than intermediate features, and from prior remote sensing MVC by introducing a federated setting with superpixel-based dimensionality reduction. The differentiable Hungarian-algorithm substitution from PVC (Huang et al., 2020) is repurposed here to resolve prototype permutation ambiguity across clients, while the Wasserstein-prototype contrastive objective extends InfoNCE from point-based to distribution-based prototype comparison.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical gap: geospatial data is inherently siloed across institutions due to privacy regulations, yet centralized MVC methods assume full data access. By demonstrating that federated clustering can approach centralized performance (87% ACC on Trento vs. 71.7% for the best prior federated method) while transmitting only K prototypes per client, the work provides a viable path for multi-institutional remote sensing analysis. Open questions include scalability to hundreds of clients, handling genuinely non-aligned or partially overlapping geographic regions, and whether the superpixel alignment assumption (identical segmentation across all views) holds in real multi-sensor deployments. The OOM failures of several strong baselines on three of four datasets also highlight that the field lacks methods that scale to the petabyte-scale data volumes typical of Earth observation.
