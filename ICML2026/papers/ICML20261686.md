## Problem

Math reasoning benchmarks (MATH, AIME) have become the de facto measure of LLM progress, yet it remains unclear whether gains on these narrow, verifiable tasks reflect broader problem-solving ability or merely overfitting to a specific domain. This paper asks whether and why math-tuned models transfer their reasoning improvements to other reasoning domains (coding, scientific QA, agent planning) and to non-reasoning tasks (instruction following, conversational QA), and identifies the fine-tuning paradigm as the decisive factor.

## Input / Output

The controlled study consumes 47K math problems (stratified from DeepScaler and SimpleRL) as training input; for SFT, teacher-generated CoT traces from Qwen3-32B-Instruct are produced via rejection sampling, while for RL, answer correctness serves as the scalar reward. The output is a fine-tuned model (UniReason) scored on three benchmark groups, with the proposed Transferability Index (TI) quantifying the ratio of non-math improvement to math improvement after z-normalization, signed square-root compression, and difficulty weighting. Diagnostic outputs include per-layer PCA shift magnitudes and token-level KL-divergence values that measure how much internal representations and output distributions drift from the base model.

## Method

The paper's core technical contribution is a controlled comparison of on-policy RL (GRPO with answer-correctness reward) versus off-policy SFT (cross-entropy on teacher CoT) applied to the same Qwen3-14B-Base model on identical math-only data, revealing that on-policy sampling—not the RL-vs-SFT label per se—is the mechanism enabling cross-domain transfer. To explain why, they deploy two diagnostic tools: PCA shift analysis on hidden states across all layers (measuring Euclidean distance between pre- and post-training representation centroids) and KL-divergence plus token-rank-shift analysis on output distributions, showing that off-policy SFT induces large, scattered drift especially on non-reasoning inputs while on-policy RL produces small, targeted shifts concentrated on task-relevant tokens. An ablation study then decomposes the unified surrogate loss into four levers—sampling distribution (on/off-policy), credit assignment (uniform vs. advantage-weighted), KL regularization, and negative gradient—and finds that on-policy sampling and advantage-weighted credit assignment jointly drive transferability, while KL regularization contributes only marginally. The Transferability Index itself is constructed by z-normalizing per-benchmark gains within each task group, applying a signed square-root transform for robustness, weighting by base-model difficulty, and taking the ratio of the resulting Domain Index to the math group's Domain Index.

## Results

In the controlled study on Qwen3-14B-Base with math-only training data, the RL-tuned UniReason model achieves 53.8% average math accuracy (vs. 49.8% for SFT-think and 32.3% for SFT-no-think), 51.0% on other reasoning (TI_other = +82.3), and 53.2% on non-reasoning tasks (TI_non = +52.2). In stark contrast, SFT models show severe negative transfer on non-reasoning tasks: SFT-think drops to 21.1% average (TI_non = −104.1) and SFT-no-think to 29.0% (TI_non = −278.9), while the base model scores 45.7%. The RL model also outperforms SFT by +17.1 points on LiveCodeBench2 and +27.7 on IFEval, confirming that on-policy RL preserves general capabilities while improving reasoning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SFT-based baselines, UniReason-RL dominates on transferability: it outperforms DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B (TI_other 60.4, TI_non −4.2), OpenThinker2-7B (TI_other 52.0, TI_non −76.2), and S1.1-7B (TI_other −65.5, TI_non −213.3) on both TI_other and TI_non, with all SFT models showing negative or near-zero non-reasoning transfer. Against RL-based baselines, UniReason-RL (TI_other 82.3, TI_non 52.2) outperforms SimpleRL-7B (TI_other 15.4, TI_non 34.4) on both metrics, but trails General-Reasoner-14B-RL (TI_other 121.6, TI_non 66.5), which is trained on a broader 232K multi-domain dataset rather than math-only data. The key finding is that among math-only-trained models, RL consistently achieves positive transfer to non-reasoning tasks while SFT induces catastrophic forgetting, with the gap most dramatic on IFEval (+27.7) and HaluEval (+37.4) for RL over SFT.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of the post-training paradigm debate (SFT vs. RL for reasoning models) and the catastrophic forgetting literature. It builds on prior observations that fine-tuning can distort pretrained features (Kumar et al., 2022) and that 'SFT memorizes, RL generalizes' (Chu et al., 2025), but departs by providing a strictly controlled, math-only comparison that isolates the sampling distribution as the operative mechanism rather than the reward signal or KL constraint. The Transferability Index formalizes what prior work had only anecdotally reported, and the PCA/KL diagnostic toolkit repurposes representation-shift analyses (Xu et al., 2025a) to explain *why* on-policy training preserves general-domain structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that on-policy sampling—not KL regularization or the RL reward signal per se—drives cross-domain transfer has immediate practical implications: the community's heavy reliance on off-policy SFT-distilled reasoning data (e.g., DeepSeek-R1 distills, OpenThoughts) may be systematically degrading general capabilities. Limitations include the math-only training setting, a single base model family in the main study, and no exploration of hybrid SFT→RL or multi-stage pipelines. Open questions include whether these conclusions hold for multi-modal reasoning, long-context tasks, and whether adaptive regularization can further close the gap for off-policy settings where on-policy rollouts are computationally prohibitive.
