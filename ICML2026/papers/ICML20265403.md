## Problem

Existing RAG data extraction attacks rely on local semantic continuity or strong prior knowledge of corpus content, causing them to become trapped in local optima when the target knowledge base is heterogeneous and spans multiple semantically disjoint domains. No prior method can systematically navigate across domain boundaries to achieve whole-corpus reconstruction under a no-prior, black-box setting with a fixed query budget.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265403/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single seed query and black-box access to a RAG system's query interface (issuing queries and receiving top-K retrieved chunks) as input, with no knowledge of the corpus composition, retriever architecture, or embedding space. On the attacker side, it uses a publicly available surrogate encoder (GTR-T5) and a BART decoder to plan and invert queries. The output is a set of reconstructed corpus chunks maximizing coverage of the underlying knowledge base within a fixed budget of 2000 queries (top-K=2 per query), with the intermediate artifacts being natural language queries synthesized via similarity-guided inverse decoding.

## Method

GeoEx shifts extraction planning from the discrete text domain to a continuous surrogate embedding space, operating as a Retrieve-Plan-Invert loop: retrieved chunks are encoded by a surrogate encoder, a geometric planner computes target latent vectors, and a similarity-guided inverse decoder (BART with cosine-similarity-biased re-ranking at each token step) projects vectors back into fluent natural language queries. The core geometric strategy alternates between two modes: (1) Global Exploration, which generates N orthogonal target directions via iterative Gram-Schmidt projection (removing components along the current context and all previously found directions) scaled by step size β, forcing semantically disjoint jumps across domain boundaries; and (2) Local Densification, which injects isotropic Gaussian noise (scale σ) around newly discovered anchor embeddings to densely mine the neighborhood within each cluster. This composite strategy enables the system to escape local semantic basins while exhaustively covering identified regions, achieving 19.94% average coverage improvement over the best baseline across three retrievers and five LLMs.

## Results

Across three retrievers (Contriever, BGE, E5) and five LLMs, GeoEx achieves average coverage of 49.35% (aligned) and 44.21% (misaligned) on Contriever, 45.93%/43.96% on BGE, and 45.68%/43.65% on E5, with corresponding efficiency scores of 76.47%, 72.77%, 71.30%, 68.70%, 70.17%, and 69.07%. The paper reports an average coverage improvement of 19.94% over the best-performing baseline. Efficiency (unique chunks per query) consistently exceeds 68% for GeoEx, compared to 19–51% for baselines, indicating substantially less redundant retrieval.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoEx dominates all four baselines across every retriever–LLM combination. Against the strongest baseline DGEA-A (Cohen et al., 2024), GeoEx(A) gains approximately 26–29 percentage points in coverage (e.g., 49.35% vs. 20.10% on Contriever, 45.93% vs. 23.16% on BGE, 45.68% vs. 19.30% on E5). Even in the misaligned setting where the surrogate encoder differs from the target retriever, GeoEx(M) still outperforms DGEA(M) by roughly 31–32 points (44.21% vs. 12.71% on Contriever). GAB (Zeng et al., 2024) and RAG-thief (Jiang et al., 2025) achieve moderate coverage (18–29%) but suffer from low efficiency (19–37%), indicating heavy redundant retrieval; GeoEx more than doubles their efficiency. Pine (Qi et al., 2025) is the weakest baseline, achieving only 6.65–14.58% coverage, as its prompt-injection strategy is limited to targeted extraction rather than systematic corpus traversal. The aligned variant (A) consistently edges out the misaligned variant (M) by 3–5 points, confirming that surrogate–retriever alignment helps but is not essential for GeoEx's effectiveness.

## Relation to Prior Work

GeoEx departs from prior RAG extraction attacks (GAB, DGEA, PINE, RAG-thief) that operate in discrete text space using heuristic query rewriting or targeted prompting, by shifting the search planning into a continuous surrogate embedding space where geometric operations (Gram-Schmidt orthogonal projection, Gaussian perturbation) can be precisely defined. It repurposes the embedding inversion literature (Morris et al. 2023; Chen et al. 2024, 2025) not for reconstructing a single text from a vector, but as a navigation mechanism that projects planned latent trajectories back into executable natural-language queries. The key conceptual departure is treating heterogeneous multi-domain corpus reconstruction as a geometric coverage problem in embedding space rather than a sequential text-relevance expansion problem, which prior agent-based methods (Jiang et al. 2025; Di Maio et al. 2024) handle only for single-domain homogeneous corpora.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As RAG systems increasingly aggregate sensitive multi-domain corpora (medical, legal, financial), this work demonstrates that a black-box attacker with no prior knowledge can reconstruct roughly 44–49% of a heterogeneous knowledge base using only 2000 queries, and that common defenses (reranking, prompt rewriting, threshold filtering) provide only marginal protection. The robustness to surrogate-retriever encoder mismatch is practically important because it means the attack does not require knowledge of the target retriever's architecture. Open questions include whether the assumption of consistent semantic topology across retrievers holds for very different model families, how coverage scales to corpora with dozens of domains or millions of chunks, and whether adaptive defenses that detect geometric exploration patterns can meaningfully reduce extraction coverage.
