## Problem

Existing neural PDE solvers and classical numerical methods degrade substantially or fail entirely under extremely sparse observations (below 3% observation rates), while diffusion-based approaches that do handle sparsity incur prohibitive inference costs at high resolutions. No unified framework existed that could efficiently solve both forward and inverse PDE problems in a single model under such extreme data scarcity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262507/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an irregular point cloud of as few as 500 sparse observations (3% of a 128×128 grid) of either the PDE coefficient field or the solution state, along with boundary conditions, and produces both the full solution field and the full coefficient field simultaneously (bidirectional inference). Because the GINO decoder operates on continuous spatial coordinates, the model can also generate zero-shot super-resolution predictions by querying arbitrary spatial locations at resolutions finer than the training grid, without any retraining.

## Method

Di-BiLPS combines three modules operating in a compressed latent space: (1) a GINO-based VAE that maps irregular point-cloud inputs to a structured latent grid and back, enabling continuous-domain decoding; (2) a contrastive learning module (GINO-ViT encoder) that aligns embeddings of sparse observations with those of full observations, producing conditioning vectors for the diffusion process; and (3) a U-Net-based latent diffusion model trained with standard noise-prediction loss. The key technical contribution is a PDE-informed denoising algorithm built on the DDIM variance-preserving schedule, which at each reverse step injects two additional gradient signals: a PDE residual term (N_pde) computed by decoding the current latent estimate onto a grid and evaluating the governing equation, and a sparse-observation term (N_obs) that pulls the decoded values at observed points toward their measured values. This guided sampling runs entirely in the low-dimensional latent space, yielding a ~90% reduction in inference time compared to pixel-space diffusion baselines while achieving lower relative ℓ₂ error across all five benchmark PDEs.

## Results

Under 3% sparse observations, Di-BiLPS achieves relative ℓ₂ errors as low as 0.013 (inverse Darcy Flow) and 0.021 (forward Darcy Flow / Bounded NS), outperforming the closest diffusion-based baseline DiffusionPDE on 8 out of 10 forward/inverse tasks. Inference runtime is reduced by 86–93% (≈19–36 s vs. ≈250–256 s per sample). Against classical operator-learning baselines (FNO, DeepONet, PINO, PINNs), Di-BiLPS reduces error by 1–2 orders of magnitude across all five PDE benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DiffusionPDE (the strongest diffusion-based baseline), Di-BiLPS wins on 8 of 10 tasks, with the largest gains on forward Helmholtz (0.025 vs. 0.088, −72%) and inverse Darcy Flow (0.013 vs. 0.032, −59%); it loses narrowly on the two inverse Navier-Stokes tasks (0.116 vs. 0.104 for Non-B NS; 0.032 vs. 0.027 for Bounded NS) but still runs 86–93% faster. Against the four classical operator-learning baselines—FNO, DeepONet, PINO, and PINNs—Di-BiLPS dominates by 1–2 orders of magnitude on every task (e.g., forward Poisson: 0.038 vs. 1.009–1.555), confirming that standard neural operators fail under 3% sparsity. GraphPDE, compared qualitatively in Fig. 5 on the inverse Bounded NS problem, is outperformed by Di-BiLPS in capturing the dynamical states around the cylinder, yielding a lower overall prediction error. Overall, Di-BiLPS is the only method that maintains sub-0.15 relative ℓ₂ error across all ten forward/inverse tasks under extreme sparsity.

## Relation to Prior Work

Di-BiLPS extends the latent diffusion paradigm (Rombach et al., 2022) and the PDE-guided denoising framework of DiffusionPDE (Huang et al., 2024) by relocating the entire diffusion process into a VAE-compressed latent space, using GINO (Li et al., 2023) to handle irregular point-cloud inputs and enable continuous-domain decoding. The key conceptual departure from DiffusionPDE is the introduction of a contrastive learning module (GINO-ViT) that aligns sparse observation embeddings with full-observation embeddings, providing a learned conditioning signal that replaces the need for dense inputs. The DDIM-based deterministic sampling (Song et al., 2020a) and DPS-style measurement guidance (Chung et al., 2022) are adapted to the latent setting, but the paper's ablation reveals that the PDE-guided term contributes negligibly, shifting the effective burden to the observation-guidance and condition-guidance terms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important regime—extremely sparse PDE observations (as low as 3%)—where both classical solvers and prior neural operator methods fail, and demonstrates a 90% inference-time reduction over DiffusionPDE while matching or exceeding accuracy. The zero-shot super-resolution capability via GINO's continuous decoder is a useful byproduct for applications requiring resolution generalization without retraining. However, the ablation showing that the PDE-guided denoising term (the paper's titular contribution) provides negligible improvement is a notable caveat, and the model's breakdown at 0.1% sparsity leaves the minimal viable observation rate an open question. The contrastive alignment + latent compression recipe may generalize to other latent neural PDE frameworks, but the current evaluation is limited to 2D unit-square domains and five standard benchmarks.
