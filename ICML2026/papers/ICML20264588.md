## Problem

In differentially private ridge regression and ERM, the single regularization parameter α must simultaneously control statistical bias (where smaller α is preferred) and global sensitivity (where larger α reduces the DP noise scale), creating an irreconcilable privacy-accuracy bottleneck. No prior work provides a principled two-parameter formulation that separates these roles or derives explicit optimal tuning rules across multiple (n, p) regimes.

## Input / Output

The method consumes covariate-response pairs (x_i, y_i) along with a covariance proxy Σ (for whitening), bounding radii R_x, R_y, R_θ (for deterministic clipping), and privacy parameters (ε, δ). It produces a (ε, δ)-DP parameter estimator θ̃_{α,c} in the original coordinate system, obtained by whitening, clipping, solving a stabilized ridge system, adding calibrated Gaussian noise, and back-transforming. The key design choice is the pair (α, c): α sets the statistical ridge level while c enforces a curvature floor on the empirical Gram matrix, so the DP noise denominator is (c+α)² rather than α².

## Method

The core mechanism is an eigenvalue-boosting operator (X̃X̃ᵀ) ∨ (nc)I that raises the minimum eigenvalue of the whitened Gram matrix to at least nc, ensuring a uniform sensitivity bound of O(1/(n(c+α))) regardless of how ill-conditioned the realized design is. This decouples the roles: α governs the statistical bias-variance trade-off of the ridge solution, while c acts as a worst-case guardrail that activates only on rare pathological datasets where λ_min(X̃X̃ᵀ) < c. In the proportional asymptotics regime (p/n → γ), the effective ridge level is λ(α,c) = α + (c − a_γ)₊, so choosing c ≤ a_γ boosts curvature (reducing DP noise) without inflating statistical bias. The paper derives a closed-form bias-variance-DP-variance risk decomposition under the Marchenko-Pastur spectral limit, yielding explicit optimal (α, c) as functions of n, p, ε, δ, and sample bounds—eliminating the need for privacy-wasting hyperparameter sweeps. The same decoupling principle extends to general ridge-regularized ERM by replacing the Gram-matrix eigenvalue floor with a Hessian curvature floor α_c(Z) = α + (c − n⁻¹Σ h_f(z_i))₊.

## Results

On four real datasets (California Housing, Appliances Energy, Wine Quality, Boston Housing) with ε=1 and δ=10/n, DADP achieves the smallest normalized parameter error ‖θ̂−θ₀‖_Σ/var(y) on all four: 0.31 (California), 0.11 (Energy), 0.22 (Wine), and 0.63 (Boston), with the smallest standard deviations as well. In simulation studies (p=n^0.4/2 and p=n^0.6/5, ε=0.5), DADP attains the lowest parameter error ‖θ̂−θ*‖₂² across the full range of n from 1000 to 25000, with the gap widening in the more challenging high-dimensional regime. Varying ε from 2⁻³ to 8 at n=20000, DADP maintains the smallest error at every privacy level, with the most pronounced gains in the high-privacy (small ε) region where DP noise dominates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DADP outperforms all three baseline families on every dataset. Against CWZ (Cai et al., 2021), the gap is dramatic: on California, DADP's error of 0.31 is roughly 100× smaller than CWZ-LD (31.9) and CWZ-HD (36.4); on Boston, DADP (0.63) beats both CWZ variants (22.9 and 26.2) by over 35×. Against Objective Perturbation (Kifer et al., 2012), DADP wins on all four datasets, with the largest margin on Boston where ObjPert-LD exceeds 100 while DADP stays at 0.63; on Energy the gap is smaller (0.11 vs. 0.14 for ObjPert-LD) but DADP still leads with a tighter standard deviation (0.01 vs. 0.11). Against the JL projection estimator (Sheffet, 2017), DADP dominates on California (0.31 vs. >1e5), Wine (0.22 vs. >1e3), and Boston (0.63 vs. >1e2), while on Energy JL is competitive (0.51 vs. 0.11) but DADP still achieves the lower error. In simulation, DADP consistently achieves the smallest ‖θ̂−θ*‖₂² across all sample sizes and privacy budgets, with the advantage most visible at small ε where the decoupling of α from c reduces the DP noise floor.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adaptive DP regression line initiated by Wang (2018), who privately estimated dataset sensitivity to reduce worst-case conservatism, by formalizing a clean two-parameter decoupling: the ridge level α governs statistical bias-variance trade-off while a separate stabilization parameter c enforces a curvature floor for sensitivity control. The theoretical analysis builds on the Marchenko-Pastur proportional-asymptotics framework of Wu & Xu (2020) and the private ERM output-perturbation machinery of Kifer et al. (2012), but departs from both by treating regularization not as a fixed component of the learning rule but as an explicit object to be optimized jointly with the privacy guardrail. The eigenvalue-boosting operator (Q ∨ cI) is the paper's key technical device, generalizing the scalar max{cn, Σx²} guardrail from the univariate appetizer to the multivariate setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The explicit closed-form tuning rules for (α, c) as functions of n, p, ε, δ, and data bounds eliminate the need for privacy-leaking hyperparameter sweeps, which is a practical bottleneck in deployed DP systems. The results match minimax lower bounds across three dimensional regimes and provide new rates in the proportional asymptotics setting (p/n → γ), where prior minimax theory did not directly apply. Limitations include the requirement of a (possibly approximate) covariance proxy Σ, the restriction to ridge-type losses (the LASSO/sparse extension is only sketched), and the ERM generalization (Section 5) remaining largely conceptual without a full risk decomposition. Open questions include extending the decoupling to non-convex or non-ridge objectives, handling the unknown-Σ case with a privatized covariance estimate, and whether the eigenvalue-boosting idea transfers to kernel methods or deep linear models under DP.
