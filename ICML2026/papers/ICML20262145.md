## Problem

Standard ℓ2 K-means, the core codebook-learning primitive in most vector quantization pipelines for KV cache compression, suffers from centroid shrinkage in high dimensions: Euclidean averaging of angularly dispersed vectors produces centroids with reduced norm, which weakens the angular alignment term in the ℓ2 distortion and degrades directional fidelity. This effect is amplified in multi-stage residual quantization, where each stage operates in an under-capacity regime with small codebooks covering broad, weakly structured residual distributions, yet prior work has not identified or addressed this failure mode.

## Input / Output

The method consumes per-layer key and value vectors from LLM attention (concatenated across heads into 1024-dim vectors, then partitioned into D-dimensional subspaces with D=128 or 256) along with per-token gradient sensitivity weights computed as the ℓ2 norm of ∂L/∂x_i. It produces a quantized KV cache represented as codebook indices (K=256) across L residual stages, achieving 0.375–2 bits per activation. The output is decoded on-the-fly during autoregressive inference via fused Triton kernels that unpack, dequantize, apply RoPE, and compute attention without materializing intermediate FP16 tensors.

## Method

GSKM replaces the standard K-means centroid update with a gain-shape decomposition: each centroid is parameterized as μ_k = g_k · s_k where s_k is a unit-norm shape and g_k ≥ 0 is a scalar gain. The shape is updated by normalizing the mean of per-sample direction vectors (x_i/‖x_i‖), yielding a magnitude-invariant angular estimate that avoids the shrinkage-alignment coupling of raw ℓ2 averaging; the gain is then set to the mean projection of assigned points onto s_k. This is integrated into a PQ+RQ pipeline (GSRQ) where every K-means call is replaced by a weighted GSKM variant, with per-sample weights derived from log-smoothed gradient norms (w_i = log(1 + λ‖∇_{x_i} L‖₂)) to robustly emphasize high-sensitivity tokens without being dominated by heavy-tailed outliers. The method introduces no additional hyperparameters beyond the standard K-means iteration count and the log-smoothing scale λ, and maintains the same O(NKD) per-iteration complexity as vanilla K-means.

## Results

On LongBench (LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct), GSRQ at 1-bit achieves 33.54 average accuracy, a 22.20 pp gain over VQLLM (11.34) and a 27.57 pp gain over KIVI-1 (5.97). On perplexity, GSRQ consistently achieves the lowest PPL across LLaMA-2-7B, LLaMA-3-8B, and Mistral-7B at all bit rates; at 0.375 BPA on LLaMA-3-8B WikiText-2, GSRQ reaches 12.77 versus 22.80 (CQ) and 17.70 (AnTKV). On few-shot commonsense/math benchmarks (Table 2), GSRQ at 1-bit scores 57.78 average accuracy versus 44.60 for VQLLM at 1-bit, and GSRQ at 0.75-bit (53.38) already surpasses VQLLM at 1-bit.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GSRQ outperforms all four baselines across every evaluated setting. Against CQ (PQ-style block quantization, Zhang et al. 2024) and AnTKV (anchor-token-aware sub-bit VQ, Li et al. 2025), GSRQ achieves substantially lower perplexity at all bit rates; the gap widens dramatically below 1-bit, e.g., at 0.375 BPA on LLaMA-3-8B C4, GSRQ reaches 20.17 while CQ and AnTKV both hit 103.5. Against VQLLM (RQ with EMA codebook learning, Kumar 2024), GSRQ wins on every downstream benchmark: at 1-bit on LongBench, GSRQ scores 33.54 vs. VQLLM's 11.34, and on few-shot commonsense/math the average is 57.78 vs. 44.60; GSRQ at 0.75-bit (53.38) already exceeds VQLLM at 1-bit. Against KIVI (scalar per-channel/per-token quantization, Liu et al. 2024), GSRQ is far superior in the 1-bit regime: on LongBench, 33.54 vs. 5.97, and on MATH-500, 14.2 vs. 0.8, indicating that vector-quantized reconstruction with GSKM preserves directional structure that scalar quantization cannot capture at extreme compression.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits within the VQ/RQ-based KV cache compression line that includes CQ (PQ-style block quantization), VQLLM (RQ with EMA codebook learning), and AnTKV (anchor-token-aware weighted K-means). Its core departure is identifying centroid shrinkage—a high-dimensional averaging effect in standard ℓ₂ K-means—as a previously overlooked failure mode that degrades directional fidelity, and proposing Gain-Shape K-means (GSKM) as a drop-in re-parameterization of the centroid update that decouples direction from magnitude. The gain-shape idea is borrowed from classical signal-processing GSVQ (Sabin & Gray, 1984) but repurposed not as a product code with separate gain/shape codebooks, and the gradient-weighting scheme adapts SqueezeLLM's sensitivity-based weighting with a logarithmic smoothing step to handle heavy-tailed gradient norms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Sub-1-bit KV cache compression is critical for making long-context inference feasible on a single GPU, and the 22-point LongBench accuracy gain at 1-bit (11.34 → 33.54 over VQLLM) demonstrates that the clustering primitive, not just the quantization architecture, is a first-order bottleneck. The centroid-shrinkage diagnosis is a reusable insight applicable to any high-dimensional K-means-based codebook learning, and the drop-in nature of GSKM makes adoption trivial. Open questions include whether the gain-shape re-parameterization helps in other VQ contexts (e.g., retrieval, speech coding), whether the gradient weighting is essential or merely helpful at higher bit-rates, and whether the heuristic (non-monotone) convergence of GSKM can be formalized or replaced with a provably convergent variant.
