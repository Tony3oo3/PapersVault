## Problem

Block-scaled FP4 formats like NVFP4 use a single fixed micro-format (E2M1) for all blocks, but LLM tensor statistics are highly heterogeneous at the block level: outlier-heavy blocks benefit from exponent-rich spacing while flat blocks benefit from uniform INT-like spacing. No single 4-bit codebook is universally optimal across all blocks, layers, and modules, and this mismatch becomes more pronounced when orthogonal transforms (e.g., Hadamard) reshape local distributions during inference or training.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard LLM weight matrices and input activations (GEMM-heavy linear projections such as Q/K/V/O and MLP up/gate/down) and produces 4-bit quantized tensor blocks of size 16, each tagged with a per-block format selection between E2M1 and E1M2. The selection bit is stored with zero additional overhead by repurposing the sign bit of the existing FP8 E4M3 per-block scale, so the total storage footprint is identical to vanilla NVFP4. The two-level scaling hierarchy (FP32 per-tensor + E4M3 per-block) and the block-scaled MMA/GEMM execution path remain unchanged.

## Method

MixFP4 extends NVFP4 by allowing each 16-element block to choose between two FP4 micro-formats—E2M1 (exponent-heavy, suited to dynamic-range-stressed blocks) and E1M2 (uniform INT-like spacing, suited to flat blocks)—selected via per-block MSE comparison under the same scaling procedure. The format choice is encoded by repurposing the redundant sign bit of the E4M3 block scale, incurring zero extra storage or bandwidth cost. On the hardware side, both micro-formats are decoded at the tensor-core front-end into a unified E2M2 internal representation (E2M1 via zero-padding, E1M2 via a small lookup table), so downstream computation uses a single datapath without format-specific duplication. The E1M2 codebook, when remapped by a constant ×2 factor, yields an exact symmetric INT4 lattice {−7,…,7}, bridging float and integer quantization semantics under the same block-scaling hierarchy. The resulting tensor-core slice replaces eight E2M1 MAC units with eight E2M2 MAC units plus lightweight per-lane decode logic, adding only 3.1% area and 1.5% power overhead relative to the baseline NVFP4 slice.

## Results

On WikiText perplexity (lower is better) with round-to-nearest PTQ, MixFP4 achieves the best or near-best scores across 7 models: e.g., 8.06 on Llama-3.1-8B (vs. 8.26 NVFP4, 8.15 4/6), 12.39 on Qwen3-8B (vs. 12.74 NVFP4, 12.56 4/6), and 11.03 on Qwen3-14B (vs. 11.31 NVFP4, 11.21 4/6). With random Hadamard transform, the margin widens substantially (e.g., 13.53 vs. 15.09 on Llama-3.2-1B). In pre-training (114M and 476M Qwen3-style models), MixFP4 maintains a smoother loss trajectory and reaches lower final loss than both NVFP4 and 4/6. Hardware synthesis at TSMC 28nm/1GHz shows only 3.1% area and 1.5% power overhead on the tensor-core compute datapath.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NVFP4 (the standard block-scaled FP4 format), MixFP4 reduces WikiText perplexity on 6 of 7 models, with the largest gains on Qwen3-8B (12.39 vs. 12.74, −0.35) and Qwen3-14B (11.03 vs. 11.31, −0.28); the sole exception is Qwen3-4B where NVFP4 edges out MixFP4 (17.34 vs. 17.42). Against 4/6 (the SOTA NVFP4 improvement from Cook et al., 2025), MixFP4 wins on all 7 models, with consistent 0.05–0.28 perplexity reductions (e.g., 8.06 vs. 8.15 on Llama-3.1-8B, 12.39 vs. 12.56 on Qwen3-8B). Under random Hadamard transform, MixFP4's advantage over NVFP4 expands dramatically (e.g., 13.53 vs. 15.09 on Llama-3.2-1B, 12.77 vs. 13.57 on Qwen3-8B), demonstrating robustness to distribution shifts induced by orthogonal mixing. In pre-training, MixFP4 outperforms both NVFP4 (which shows unstable optimization) and 4/6 in final loss for both the 114M and 476M Qwen3-style models. The hardware cost of enabling this mixed-format capability is minimal: 3.1% area and 1.5% power overhead relative to the baseline NVFP4 tensor-core slice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends NVFP4 (Abecassis et al., 2025), the block-scaled FP4 format targeting Blackwell tensor cores, by allowing each 16-element block to choose between two FP4 micro-formats (E2M1 and E1M2) rather than committing to a single codebook. It is conceptually adjacent to the Four-over-Six (4/6) method (Cook et al., 2025), which also refines NVFP4's block scaling, and to the broader adaptive-numerical-type line (ANT, M-ANT, BitMoD) that argues for format flexibility at fine granularity. The key departure is the zero-storage-overhead encoding trick—repurposing the unused sign bit of the E4M3 block scale as a per-block format selector—and the hardware co-design that unifies both micro-formats into a single E2M2 internal representation, avoiding duplicated compute datapaths.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MixFP4 offers a practically deployable improvement to the emerging NVFP4 ecosystem: consistent perplexity and downstream-task gains over NVFP4 and 4/6 across Llama, Qwen, and Mamba models, with only 3.1% area and 1.5% power overhead on the tensor-core datapath. The pre-training stability result (smooser loss trajectory vs. unstable NVFP4 optimization) suggests the mixed codebook may be important for scaling FP4 pre-training beyond the 476M pilot. However, the paper explicitly defers native tensor-core kernel engineering and end-to-end throughput measurement to future work, and the accuracy gains, while consistent, are modest (typically 0.1–0.5 perplexity), leaving open whether the benefit persists at frontier model scale or under more aggressive quantization settings.
