## Problem

Zeroth-order (ZO) optimization enables memory-efficient LLM fine-tuning using only forward passes, but suffers from high-variance gradient estimates that degrade with parameter dimensionality. Sparse ZO updates mitigate this by perturbing only a subset of parameters, yet existing methods lack a principled, online mechanism to select which parameters to perturb—relying instead on predefined patterns, random masks, or pre-computed statistics that undermine ZO's memory-efficiency advantage.

## Input / Output

The method consumes only scalar loss values from two forward passes per ZO query (no backpropagation, no stored activations or gradients) along with the current model parameters and a training minibatch. It operates at the block level, where each block corresponds to one native parameter tensor of the model, producing per-block curvature scores, a sparse binary perturbation mask, and updated parameters. The perturbation budget B is not fixed but adapts dynamically each iteration based on the evolving curvature score distribution, and the entire pipeline requires only ZO-level memory (≈5.91 GB for OPT-2.7B, ≈13.95 GB for OPT-6.7B).

## Method

CurvZO tracks an online curvature score s_i = Δ²v_i² from each scalar ZO response, normalizes it by total perturbation energy, and smooths it via exponential moving average to obtain stable per-block curvature signals. It then applies a Horvitz-Thompson inverse-probability correction to the sparse gradient estimator to remove sampling bias, and derives a variance-minimizing sampling rule π_i ∝ √S_i by solving a Lagrangian optimization under a fixed perturbation budget. An adaptive budget mechanism dynamically adjusts B each iteration using two statistics of the score distribution—effective support size (a participation-ratio-like quantity) and normalized Shannon entropy—so that early noisy training uses a larger budget for exploration while later sharper distributions allow a smaller, more focused budget. The full method is formulated at the block level (one block per parameter tensor) to keep overhead negligible, and a convergence analysis establishes an O(1/T) rate for the expected squared gradient norm with a variance floor of O(M²/B) and a smoothing bias of O(dε²).

## Results

On OPT-2.7B fine-tuning with 1,000 training samples across 8 SuperGLUE/SQuAD/DROP tasks, CurvZO achieves an average accuracy of 66.8%, outperforming MeZO (64.2%) and DiZO (61.4%) while remaining competitive with first-order FT (72.2%) and LoRA (71.8%). CurvZO reaches the same accuracy as MeZO in approximately 2.4× fewer optimization steps on BoolQ and 2.0× fewer on RTE, reducing total GPU hours by up to 59% (BoolQ) and 51% (RTE). Memory usage is identical to MeZO (5.91 GB for OPT-2.7B, 13.95 GB for OPT-6.7B), confirming ZO-level memory efficiency. Gains extend to OPT-6.7B (Table 2) and Llama2-7B/13B (Figure 2), with up to 4.4-point accuracy improvements over ZO baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary ZO baseline MeZO (Malladi et al., 2023), CurvZO wins on 7 of 8 tasks in the full-parameter setting (66.8% vs 64.2% average), with the largest gains on WSC (+4.3 pts), SQuAD (+2.5 pts), and BoolQ (+2.2 pts); in the LoRA setting, CurvZO+LoRA (65.1%) also surpasses MeZO+LoRA (62.1%) by 3.0 points. Against DiZO (Tan et al., 2026), CurvZO leads by 5.4 points on average (66.8% vs 61.4%), with particularly large margins on SQuAD (+19.4 pts) and DROP (+5.1 pts). In the extended comparison (Table 5), CurvZO outperforms all four additional ZO baselines: Sparse-MeZO (64.1%), SensZOQ (61.0%), MeZO-BCD (63.8%), and SubZero (58.7%). Against first-order baselines, FT (72.2%) and LoRA (71.8%) remain stronger in absolute accuracy, but CurvZO narrows the gap to within 5.4 points of FT while using only 5.91 GB of GPU memory (vs. >80 GB for FT on OPT-6.7B). On Llama3.1-8B with challenging benchmarks (Dolly-15k, GSM8K), CurvZO (25.4 ROUGE-L, 72.4 EM) consistently outperforms MeZO (23.8, 70.2) but still trails first-order LoRA (30.7, 75.7).

## Relation to Prior Work

CurvZO extends the sparse zeroth-order fine-tuning line initiated by MeZO and refined by Sparse-MeZO, SubZero, and SensZOQ. Where prior sparse ZO methods rely on predefined sparsity patterns (Sparse-MeZO's small-magnitude weights, SubZero's low-rank structure) or pre-computed offline Fisher statistics (SensZOQ), CurvZO departs by tracking curvature signals entirely online from scalar ZO feedback, requiring no gradient access or pre-training computation. The method also introduces a Horvitz-Thompson bias correction for Bernoulli-masked sparse perturbations and derives a variance-minimizing sampling rule (π_i ∝ √S_i) via a Lagrangian argument, providing a principled connection between the curvature score and the Fisher diagonal that prior heuristic sparsity choices lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CurvZO offers a plug-and-play mechanism that improves ZO fine-tuning accuracy by up to 4.4 points and reduces GPU hours by up to 2× while maintaining MeZO-level memory, making it practically relevant for resource-constrained LLM adaptation. The key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that per-coordinate/block curvature scores capture only local sensitivity and ignore parameter coupling, potentially leaving gains unrealized in regimes where interaction-aware second-order information matters. An open question is whether the adaptive budget mechanism and curvature-guided sampling generalize beyond the SuperGLUE-scale tasks tested, particularly to very large models or tasks with highly non-stationary curvature landscapes during training.
