## Problem

Current Video-LLM approaches to Video Temporal Grounding treat the task as direct timestamp regression from a flattened visual-token stream, forcing the model to map a discrete token space onto a continuous temporal domain. This black-box formulation produces brittle, high-variance boundaries and offers no verifiable intermediate evidence. The paper argues that grounding should follow a human-like Identify-then-Measure routine: first commit to a discrete event segment, then refine its precise boundaries under that hypothesis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266108/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an untrimmed video sampled at 1 FPS and a natural-language query. Before the LLM sees the query, a perception pipeline extracts a Top-K=8 evidence pool where each unit consists of a discrete span ID, a coarse metric interval, and 4 segment-local visual evidence tokens (produced by a Q-Former-style Span Evidence Encoder), adding only ~100–200 tokens to the context. The output is a single structured response containing a natural-language answer, precise start/end timestamps, and exactly one cited span ID, making every boundary prediction attributable to a specific event hypothesis.

## Method

F2G is a three-stage pipeline. Stage 1 pretrains a temporal module via multi-view latent prediction (V-JEPA-style) with a sliced isotropic Gaussian regularizer (SIGReg), encouraging the backbone to encode boundary-sensitive transition cues rather than raw pixel dynamics. Stage 2 trains a lightweight self-attention + MLP proposal head to emit per-timestep segment spans and objectness scores, from which a Top-K candidate pool is selected. Stage 3 fine-tunes the Video-LLM (via LoRA) and the Span Evidence Encoder jointly, supervising the decoder to cite exactly one span ID (Identify) and then generate precise timestamps conditioned on the cited evidence (Measure), with a combined loss over sequence cross-entropy, ID-token supervision, timestamp-token supervision, and a small proposal-regression term to keep the evidence pool improving. The key architectural insight is that grounding becomes a verifiable, citation-based decision over an explicit evidence pool rather than unconstrained numeric regression over a flat token stream.

## Results

On the backbone-controlled Qwen3-VL-8B setting, F2G achieves mIoU 47.2 (R@0.7 = 25.7) on Charades-STA and mIoU 45.7 (R@0.7 = 28.4) on ActivityNet-Captions, outperforming the best prior Video-LLM method (NumPro: mIoU 41.4 / 38.8) by +5.8 and +6.9 points respectively. On QVHighlights, F2G reaches mAP 29.7 and HIT@1 45.6, surpassing NumPro (25.0 / 37.2). The same +F2G-FT recipe transfers to LLaVA-NeXT-7B and Qwen2.5-VL-7B, yielding consistent R@0.7 gains of +3.9 to +5.9 points across backbones, while general video QA (VideoMME) is preserved at 70.9% vs. 71.4% baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

F2G outperforms all prior Video-LLM VTG methods on every reported metric. Against the strongest prior method NumPro (LongVA-7B-DPO), F2G gains +7.5 R@0.3, +8.4 R@0.5, +5.1 R@0.7, and +5.8 mIoU on Charades-STA, and +8.2 R@0.3, +8.6 R@0.5, +7.8 R@0.7, and +6.9 mIoU on ActivityNet-Captions; on QVHighlights it improves mAP by +4.7 and HIT@1 by +8.4. Against VTimeLLM (CLIP+Vicuna-13B), the gains are even larger (+16.0 R@0.3, +16.1 R@0.5, +11.0 R@0.7, +12.6 mIoU on Charades-STA). Against TimeChat (ViT-G+LLaMA-7B), F2G leads by +23.6 R@0.3 and +15.2 mIoU on Charades-STA. On the stricter TimeLens-Bench, F2G (74.2 R@0.3, 36.3 R@0.7 on Charades-TimeLens) is competitive with the specialized TimeLens-8B (76.6 / 35.2) while using only SFT without RL post-training. Cross-backbone, F2G consistently improves LLaVA-NeXT-7B (+4.6 R@0.7), Qwen2.5-VL-7B (+3.9 R@0.7), and Qwen3-VL-8B (+9.8 R@0.7), confirming the recipe is backbone-agnostic. General video understanding is preserved: VideoMME overall drops only 0.5 points (71.4→70.9), indicating no over-specialization.

## Relation to Prior Work

F2G builds on the JEPA family of latent-predictive video pretraining (V-JEPA, V-JEPA 2) and the SIGReg geometry regularizer from LeJEPA to learn boundary-sensitive temporal representations, but repurposes them not as a general transferable encoder specifically as a proposal generator for a video-wide evidence pool. It departs from the dominant Video-LLM VTG paradigm—direct timestamp regression over a flattened visual-token stream (TimeChat, VTimeLLM, Momentor, NumPro)—by reframing grounding as a verifiable Identify-then-Measure problem where the LLM must cite a discrete span ID before emitting timestamps. This positions F2G between timestamp-tokenization methods (which improve the time-to-text interface but still perform stream-to-time mapping) and segment-structured decoding methods (which add structure but keep the intermediate hypothesis implicit and unsupervised), by making each candidate segment an explicit, supervised, and attributable evidence unit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Identify-then-Measure decomposition offers a general design principle for Video-LLM grounding: expose intermediate evidence explicitly before requesting precise continuous outputs, which improves both accuracy and inference stability (reducing stochastic collapse) while keeping predictions auditable. The recipe transfers across LLaVA-NeXT, Qwen2.5-VL, and Qwen3-VL backbones without architectural changes and preserves general video QA (VideoMME), suggesting broad applicability. Open questions include extending to multi-span and compositional queries, spatio-temporal grounding with region-level evidence, and audio-visual multimodal grounding, all of which the authors identify as natural next steps. The citation-gap analysis revealing that remaining errors are dominated by proposal coverage rather than LLM mis-selection points to proposal quality as the key lever for further gains.
