## Problem

Real-world dynamic graphs suffer from tightly entangled multi-domain imperfections—structural incompleteness, noise perturbations, and temporal redundancy—that cascade and reinforce one another over time. Existing DGNNs lack a principled framework to systematically decouple these coupled domains, causing representation drift, spectral distortion, and accumulated temporal redundancy that severely degrade robustness under adversarial conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263323/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a discrete-time sequence of T graph snapshots (each with an N×N binary adjacency matrix and N×d node feature matrix, where d=32) plus the current node features at time T+1, and outputs a predicted adjacency matrix for dynamic link prediction at T+1. The three evaluation datasets span multi-scale temporal granularities (16 yearly, 24 monthly, 30 daily snapshots) with 13K–23K nodes and 65K–202K edges. Internally, the pipeline produces robust node embeddings at each stage (spatially decoupled, spectrally purified, and temporally redundancy-suppressed) that are fused into a final representation before the link predictor.

## Method

DeR-Mamba performs three-stage multi-domain decoupling. First, MP-K²alman achieves spatial decoupling by sampling M particle groups of orthogonalized random projections into kernel subspaces (approximating the Softmax kernel via Positive Random Features), computing per-particle Q/V statistics, and fusing them through a Kalman-style LMMSE gain that adaptively weights confidence across particles, followed by multi-hop degree-normalized relational bias injection. Second, AFDM achieves spectral decoupling by applying a differentiable non-decimated 1D DWT along the temporal axis to separate low- and high-frequency components, then generating adversarial gating weights via an entropy-regularized convex optimization (solved in closed form as a Gibbs distribution) that adaptively suppresses noise-dominant high-frequency responses while preserving meaningful abrupt changes. Third, a Dynamic Graph Redundancy-aware Scanning (DGRS) selective state-space mechanism achieves temporal decoupling by discretizing SSM transition parameters with input-dependent step sizes modulated by inter-graph structural variation, using zero-order-hold Taylor expansion, and performing selective snapshot scanning to filter redundant cross-time pathways. Training minimizes a composite loss combining cross-entropy link prediction with an entropy-plus-KL-divergence regularizer that enforces distributional consistency between the state-space output and the decoupled representation.

## Results

On COLLAB (the primary benchmark), DeR-Mamba achieves 94.83% AUC on clean data and limits average performance degradation to 18.0% under targeted evasion attacks and 18.5% under poisoning attacks—the smallest degradation among all 12 baselines. Under non-targeted feature attacks at the strongest level (γ=1.5), it retains 57.50% AUC on COLLAB. In efficiency, DeR-Mamba reduces training latency by up to 49.8% and GPU memory by 56.5% compared to DGIB, while maintaining linear O(T(|V|+|E|)) complexity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeR-Mamba achieves the smallest average degradation across all targeted attack settings on COLLAB (18.0% evasion, 18.5% poisoning), outperforming the closest competitor DG-Mamba (which has a higher clean AUC of 96.67% but shows more variable and larger per-step degradations, e.g., 30.6% at n_p=2). DGIB is the second most robust baseline with ~12.8% average degradation but starts from a lower clean score (90.12%). Static GNNs (GAT at 85.07% clean) and basic dynamic GNNs (DySAT at 73.29%, WinGNN at 78.52%) suffer substantially larger degradations (20–33%) under targeted attacks. Under non-targeted feature attacks (γ=1.5), DeR-Mamba retains 57.50% AUC on COLLAB, while most baselines drop below 50%. The ablation confirms that both MP-K²alman (spatial decoupling) and AFDM (spectral decoupling) are necessary: removing either causes 3–5% additional degradation under targeted attacks.

## Relation to Prior Work

DeR-Mamba sits at the intersection of three research threads: selective state-space models (Mamba, SpoT-Mamba) for long-range temporal modeling, kernel-based random feature methods (Performer/PRF) for efficient attention approximation, and Kalman filtering for state estimation under uncertainty. It departs from prior robust DGNN work (DGIB, DG-Mamba, WinGNN) by explicitly framing the robustness problem as a multi-domain entanglement—spatial incompleteness, spectral noise, and temporal redundancy—that must be decoupled simultaneously rather than addressed from a single perspective. The Kalman-style multi-particle fusion draws on the Generalized Product of Experts framework, while the wavelet-based frequency decoupling extends graph signal processing ideas (previously applied to static graphs in KalmanNet, PKF) into the dynamic and adversarial setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The systematic three-domain decoupling framework with accompanying theoretical guarantees (information-theoretic stability, spectral non-expansiveness) provides a principled template for building robust dynamic graph models, moving beyond ad-hoc denoising. The linear-complexity design (O(T(|V|+|E|))) makes it practical for large-scale dynamic graphs, and the 49.8% training-time and 56.5% memory reductions over DGIB are practically significant. However, evaluation is limited to three datasets and standard adversarial settings (NETTACK, random perturbations); the paper does not test against stronger adaptive attacks or continuous-time dynamic graphs, which the authors themselves flag as future work. Whether the multi-domain decoupling narrative generalizes beyond link prediction to other dynamic graph tasks (node classification, community detection) remains open.
