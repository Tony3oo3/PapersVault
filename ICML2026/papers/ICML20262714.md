## Problem

Traditional knowledge distillation for fine-grained visual classification relies on dataset-specific visual teachers, which can transfer spurious correlations and require expensive training. Although LLMs encode broad, modality-agnostic conceptual knowledge, their potential to supervise vision-only student models without paired multimodal data remains largely unexplored. The paper asks whether structured conceptual knowledge from a language-only teacher can be transferred to improve fine-grained visual recognition and robustness.

## Input / Output

The student model takes a standard RGB image (3×H×W) and produces a class label via its main classification head, plus an auxiliary Q×M logit matrix aligned with the LLM's question space. The LLM teacher receives only text prompts (class names instantiated into generated multiple-choice questions) and outputs pre-softmax logits over M answer options for each of Q questions. Critically, the LLM supervision is generated once per dataset and reused throughout training, so no per-sample LLM inference is needed during student training.

## Method

LaViD operates in two stages. First, a frontier LLM (GPT-4o) generates Q multiple-choice questions from dataset metadata, each containing a <object> placeholder and M answer options that avoid class names; a language-only LLM (Qwen2.5-7B) then produces pre-softmax logits over the answer tokens for each class-instantiated question, yielding a Q×M soft-label matrix per class that encodes multi-dimensional semantic relationships. Second, the visual student (ResNet, MobileNetV2, ShuffleNetV2, ViT, or CLIP) is augmented with an auxiliary linear head projecting its final feature vector to QM dimensions, reshaped into a Q×M matrix. Training minimizes standard cross-entropy plus an MSE distillation loss between the student's auxiliary predictions and the fixed LLM-derived class targets, weighted by λ. The key distinction from conventional KD is that supervision is class-level and conceptually structured across diverse semantic dimensions (color, shape, behavior, etc.) rather than instance-level logit matching, acting as a semantic regularizer that encourages representations aligned with world knowledge.

## Results

On six fine-grained benchmarks (CUB-200, Caltech-101, 102 Flowers, FGVC Aircraft, Oxford Pets, Stanford Cars), LaViD with a Qwen2.5-7B language teacher achieves top-1 accuracy of 70.15% on CUB-200, 81.51% on Caltech-101, and 88.59% on Stanford Cars with a ResNet-18 student, outperforming all MLLM-based baselines (MaKD, FitNet+LLaVA, CRD+LLaVA) despite never accessing training images. Combined with logit standardization (LS), LaViD reaches 72.46% on CUB and 91.20% on Cars, surpassing the strongest visual-teacher baselines (DKD, MLKD). On Waterbirds, LaViD dramatically improves worst-group accuracy from 14.29% (independent student) to 55.39% (ResNet-18), demonstrating robustness to spurious correlations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LaViD (Qwen teacher) outperforms all three MLLM-based baselines across all six datasets: it beats MaKD (InternVL) by 1.96 pts on CUB (70.15 vs. 68.19), 1.60 pts on Aircraft (83.22 vs. 80.45), and 0.63 pts on Cars (88.59 vs. 87.96); it surpasses CRD+LLaVA by 0.68 pts on CUB and 1.73 pts on Aircraft; and it exceeds FitNet+LLaVA by 7.20 pts on CUB and 2.69 pts on Aircraft. Against visual-teacher KD methods, LaViD (Qwen) beats KD by 5.39 pts on CUB and DKD by 2.48 pts on CUB, though it trails MLKD (68.76) and LS (70.42) on CUB by 1.39 and 0.27 pts respectively. When combined with logit standardization (QN+R50 w/ LaViD), the method achieves the best results on all six datasets, exceeding MLKD by 3.70 pts on CUB and LS by 2.04 pts on CUB. On Waterbirds, LaViD's worst-group accuracy of 55.39% (ResNet-18) is 41+ points higher than all visual-teacher KD baselines (ranging from 2.26% for MLKD to 13.03% for DKD), highlighting its unique robustness advantage from language-derived conceptual supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

LaViD carves new ground at the intersection of knowledge distillation and cross-modal transfer by repurposing the KD framework for a fundamentally different purpose: instead of mimicking a task-trained visual teacher's instance-level predictions, it injects external world knowledge from a language-only LLM into a vision-only student. It departs from traditional KD (Hinton, DKD, MLKD) which requires a dataset-specific visual teacher, and from cross-modal KD (MaKD, LLaVA-based FitNet/CRD) which relies on paired multimodal inputs or shared embedding spaces. The conceptual motivation draws on the Platonic Representation Hypothesis (Huh et al., 2024), but the mechanism—eliciting structured semantic supervision via MCQ-generated logit distributions—is novel and does not build on a specific prior method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a language-only teacher (no image access) consistently outperforms multimodal MLLM-based distillation across six fine-grained benchmarks challenges the assumption that visual supervision requires visual data. The substantial worst-group accuracy improvement on Waterbirds (e.g., 14%→55% for ResNet-18) suggests conceptual regularization can mitigate spurious correlations, a practically important property for deployment. Open questions include scalability to very large or abstract class spaces where verbalizable distinctions are limited, and whether the approach transfers to non-classification tasks. The method's simplicity (one-time MCQ generation, no paired data, no multimodal pretraining) makes it broadly applicable, but its dependence on interpretable class names limits generality.
