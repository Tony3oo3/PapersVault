## Problem

The design space for native multimodal foundation models remains opaque because most prior work initializes from pretrained language models, confounding what is learned from unified training versus inherited from language pretraining. This makes it impossible to isolate the factors governing multimodal pretraining or to understand the fundamental scaling dynamics between vision and language. The paper addresses this by conducting controlled, from-scratch pretraining experiments that systematically vary one factor at a time across representations, data, architecture, and scaling.

## Input / Output

The model consumes text as BPE tokens (LLaMA-3 tokenizer), images and video frames as 256 latent tokens (16×16 grid) produced by a frozen visual encoder at 224×224 resolution (default SigLIP 2 So400M), and navigation actions as plain text tokens (e.g., 'dx=+4.96, dy=+1.50'). A hybrid attention mask applies causal masking to text but block-wise causal masking to vision (bidirectional within a frame, causal across frames). Outputs include autoregressive text, images generated via a 25-step Euler sampler with CFG=3.0 decoded by a pretrained RAE decoder, and predicted next visual frames for world modeling tasks.

## Method

The paper trains a decoder-only Transformer from scratch using the Transfusion framework: standard next-token prediction for text and frame-wise flow matching for vision (z_t = (1-t)ε + tz_0, minimizing velocity prediction), combined as L = 1.0·L_LM + 3.0·L_flow. The architecture uses modality-specific FFNs (separate text and vision FFNs per layer) with simple linear projections instead of a U-Net, and a single RAE encoder (SigLIP 2) for both understanding and generation rather than the dual-encoder approach used by prior work. The key architectural contribution is a Mixture-of-Experts design with per-modality shared experts and fine-grained routing (G=16, 256 experts, top-16 active), which naturally induces modality specialization and harmonizes the discovered scaling asymmetry where vision is significantly more data-hungry than language. The paper derives IsoFLOP scaling laws for both modalities, showing MoE narrows the vision-language exponent gap from 0.10 (dense) to 0.05, and demonstrates that world modeling capabilities (navigation prediction) emerge from general multimodal pretraining with as little as 1% domain-specific data.

## Results

The best configuration (MoE + SigLIP 2 RAE + x-prediction) achieves a DCLM text perplexity of 12.49 and a DPGBench score of 0.65, substantially outperforming the dense Transfusion baseline (PPL 15.93, DPG 0.45). At 10²¹ FLOPs, the unified dense multimodal model matches or exceeds unimodal baselines on all four metrics (DCLM PPL 13.3 vs. 13.6 text-only; DPG 0.622 vs. 0.598 T2I-only). The MoE variant at the same budget achieves DCLM PPL 12.3 and FID 39.2, closely tracking unimodal MoE baselines (PPL 12.0, FID 39.8). Scaling-law analysis reveals vision is significantly more data-hungry than language (exponent b≈0.63 vs. 0.53 for dense), a gap that MoE narrows from 0.10 to 0.05.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT, Liang et al. 2024), the paper's MoE approach achieves the lowest perplexity (12.49) and highest DPG score (0.63), outperforming MoT on both metrics; the paper attributes this to learned routing adapting capacity per token versus MoT's fixed architectural separation. Against the dual-encoder strategies used by Bagel (Deng et al. 2025) and Janus (Wu et al. 2025), which employ separate VAE and SigLIP 2 encoders for generation and understanding respectively, the single SigLIP 2 RAE encoder significantly outperforms on both DPG (0.57 vs. lower) and perplexity (15.06 vs. higher), demonstrating that the understanding/generation dichotomy is unnecessary. The unified dense multimodal model also matches or exceeds unimodal baselines at equal compute (DCLM PPL 13.3 vs. 13.6 text-only; DPG 0.622 vs. 0.598 T2I-only at 10²¹ FLOPs), and the MoE variant tracks unimodal MoE baselines within 0.3 PPL and 0.6 FID, confirming that joint training incurs no modality tax with the right architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Transfusion framework (Zhou et al., 2025) for unified multimodal pretraining with next-token prediction for language and flow matching for vision, and on recent Representation Autoencoder (RAE) results (Zheng et al., 2026; Tong et al., 2026) showing diffusion can operate in high-dimensional semantic latent spaces. It departs from the dominant paradigm of initializing from pretrained LLMs (as in Janus, BAGEL, and most current unified models) by training entirely from scratch, thereby isolating what unified multimodal training itself contributes. The MoE design extends language MoE (DeepSeek, Kimi K2) to the multimodal setting with modality-specific shared experts and granularity analysis, while the IsoFLOP scaling analysis extends Chinchilla-style laws to joint vision-language training for the first time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic, controlled decomposition of the unified multimodal pretraining design space, yielding actionable guidance: a single semantic encoder (RAE/SigLIP 2) suffices for both understanding and generation, eliminating the dual-encoder complexity in prior work; vision is significantly more data-hungry than language (scaling asymmetry), and MoE naturally harmonizes this by shifting language toward a data-intensive regime; and world modeling capabilities emerge from general pretraining with as little as 1% in-domain data. Open questions include whether these findings hold at much larger scale (beyond ~1T tokens), how interleaved data and post-training (RL) interact with these pretraining dynamics, and whether the MoE hardware utilization bottleneck (uneven expert loading) can be resolved for production deployment. The practical impact is substantial: the findings simplify architecture (single encoder, MoE routing) while revealing that the 'modality tax' is largely an artifact of specific design choices rather than an inherent limitation.
