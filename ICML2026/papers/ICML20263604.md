## Problem

Standard neural operators produce deterministic point predictions and cannot capture the aleatoric uncertainty inherent in stochastic PDEs or the epistemic uncertainty in deterministic surrogate modeling. Existing probabilistic alternatives either model uncertainty in parameter space (Bayesian neural operators), prescribe a fixed output distribution (PNO with pointwise Gaussians), or perform diffusion directly in pixel space at high computational cost. The paper asks whether a lightweight, modular probabilistic head can be attached to any neural operator backbone to learn a full conditional output distribution efficiently.

## Input / Output

The method takes a conditioning function field a on a regular grid (1D: 256 points; 2D: 128×128) and produces an ensemble of K=32 output field samples drawn from the learned conditional distribution p(u|a). Sampling occurs in a fixed r=64-dimensional coefficient space via a 10-step ODE integration, and each coefficient vector is decoded into a full-resolution field through input-dependent basis functions Φ_a produced by the frozen neural operator backbone, so the output resolution is determined by the backbone rather than the diffusion head.

## Method

DLL is a two-stage pipeline. First, an operator encoder is trained by minimizing a reconstruction loss: a neural operator NO_ψ maps the input a to r input-dependent basis functions Φ_a, and a neural functional encoder NF_φ maps the target u to a coefficient vector ξ, so that û = ξᵀΦ_a approximates u. Under idealized assumptions, this recovers the optimal rank-r uncentered Karhunen-Loève subspace conditioned on a (Proposition 4.1). Second, with the encoder frozen, a conditional flow-matching model (a 3-layer MLP velocity network of width 512, conditioned on FNO embeddings of a) is trained in the r-dimensional coefficient space using the standard velocity-matching objective with a linear noise schedule. At inference, coefficients are sampled by integrating the learned probability-flow ODE and decoded through the input-dependent basis, yielding diverse output fields while preserving the backbone's discretization invariance and geometry awareness.

## Results

On stochastic Burgers' equation, DLL achieves the best energy distance (ED = 1.285) and sliced Wasserstein distance (SWD = 0.213) among all methods, outperforming pixel-space diffusion (DM: ED = 1.355, SWD = 0.239) and latent diffusion (LDM: ED = 1.373, SWD = 0.249). On the deterministic Kuramoto-Sivashinsky rollout, DLL attains the lowest NRMSE (0.343) and CRPS (0.470) with a spread skill ratio of 0.949, improving over the FNO backbone (NRMSE = 0.404). On Kolmogorov flow, pixel-space diffusion (DM) achieves the best NRMSE (0.369) and CRPS (0.692), while DLL (NRMSE = 0.426, CRPS = 0.822) still improves over the FNO backbone and attains the best SSR (0.620).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the deterministic FNO backbone, DLL dramatically reduces distributional error (ED drops from 6.491 to 1.285, SWD from 0.426 to 0.213) at the cost of a modest increase in mean-field NRMSE (0.146 → 0.252), since FNO's point estimate is well-calibrated in mean but captures no spread. Compared to the Monte Carlo dropout variant FNO-d, DLL improves ED by ~4.8× and NRMSE_s from 0.755 to 0.289, indicating far more informative uncertainty. Against the probabilistic neural operator PNO, DLL wins on ED (1.285 vs 1.766), SWD (0.213 vs 0.253), and spread error (0.289 vs 0.457), though PNO retains a slight edge in mean error (NRMSE_m 0.215 vs 0.252). Versus the grid-based generative baselines DM and LDM, DLL achieves the best ED and SWD on both stochastic benchmarks and the best NRMSE_s, while DM edges out DLL on NRMSE_m for Darcy (0.353 vs 0.355) and on NRMSE/CRPS for Kolmogorov flow (0.369/0.692 vs 0.426/0.822), which the authors attribute to the stronger spatial inductive bias of the U-Net used by DM. On the KS rollout, DLL beats all baselines on both NRMSE and CRPS, with SSR (0.949) close to the ideal value of 1, whereas PNO's SSR collapses to 0.550.

## Relation to Prior Work

DLL sits at the intersection of neural operator learning (FNO, DeepONet) and conditional generative modeling via flow matching/diffusion. It departs from standard latent diffusion by replacing the VAE autoencoder with an operator encoder that produces input-dependent basis functions (a learned, uncentered Karhunen-Loève-type expansion), and from pixel-space diffusion by performing the generative process in a low-dimensional coefficient space. Conceptually, it is closest to spectral diffusion processes (Phillips et al., 2022) and function-space flow matching (Kerrigan et al., 2024; Lim et al., 2025), but differs by learning the basis through a neural operator backbone rather than using a fixed or infinite-dimensional spectral basis. The unified velocity-matching objective (Eq. 4) is inherited directly from flow matching (Lipman et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2023), with the key architectural contribution being where and how the diffusion head is attached.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DLL provides a modular, drop-in probabilistic head that can be attached to any neural operator backbone, making uncertainty quantification accessible without redesigning the operator architecture. The theoretical connection to optimal rank-r reconstruction (Proposition 4.1) gives a principled justification for the coefficient space, and the Wasserstein stability result (Proposition 2.3) unifies stochastic and deterministic operator learning under a single training objective. However, the empirical gains over strong baselines (pixel-space diffusion, latent diffusion) are modest—DLL is competitive but not dominant, and on Kolmogorov flow the pixel-space U-Net still outperforms on pointwise accuracy. Open questions include principled calibration with coverage guarantees, extension to irregular geometries and inverse problems, and whether the coefficient-space bottleneck limits performance for highly complex dynamics.
