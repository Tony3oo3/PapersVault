## Problem

State-of-the-art dynamical systems reconstruction (DSR) models based on piecewise-linear recurrent neural networks (PLRNNs) are discrete-time maps that cannot naturally handle irregularly spaced observations or provide solutions at arbitrary time points. Neural ODEs address the continuous-time requirement but lack the mathematical tractability of PLRNNs and underperform in reconstruction quality. This paper closes the gap by developing continuous-time PLRNNs (cPLRNNs) that retain the PL structure's analytical advantages while operating natively in continuous time.

## Input / Output

The model consumes time series observations x_n ∈ R^N at potentially irregular timestamps t_n, coupled to an M-dimensional latent state z(t) via a simple identity observation model (first N latent dimensions serve as read-out). Beyond producing state trajectories at the observed times, the cPLRNN can inter- and extrapolate to arbitrary time points without retraining, and after training it yields semi-analytical topological objects: fixed points (via the SCYFI algorithm) and limit cycles (by solving a finite system of M−1+r equations). For the LIF and membrane potential benchmarks, the model also aligns its internal subregion-switching times with the physical spiking/reset events in the data.

## Method

The cPLRNN reformulates the discrete PLRNN map as the ODE ż(t) = Az(t) + WΦ*(z(t)) + h, where the ReLU nonlinearity partitions state space into 2^P linear subregions. Within each subregion the solution is an analytic sum of exponentials (Eq. 7), so the forward pass reduces to finding switching times—zero crossings of ReLU coordinates—using a custom interval Newton method with branch-and-prune that rigorously isolates the first root. Gradients flow through these switching times via the implicit function theorem (Eq. 11), with parameter derivatives of the eigen-decomposition computed by automatic differentiation. Training uses sparse teacher forcing with MSE loss and RAdam optimization. For state-space analysis, equilibria are found by solving W_Ω^k z* = −h in each region (real vs. virtual fixed points), and limit cycles are located by solving a coupled system of M−1+r nonlinear equations encoding the times-of-flight through a sequence of subregions, using a Trust Region solver with Variable Projection.

## Results

On the Lorenz-63 benchmark, cPLRNN achieves D_stsp = 0.37±0.14 and D_H = 0.116±0.012, statistically on par with the best Neural ODE solver (Tsit5: D_stsp = 0.30±0.11, D_H = 0.085±0.019; all p > 0.27) and slightly worse than the discrete-time standard PLRNN (D_stsp = 0.24±0.06, p < 0.043). On irregularly sampled LIF data, cPLRNN dramatically outperforms the standard PLRNN (D_stsp 6.6 vs 15.3; MAE 0.064 vs 0.17), while on membrane potential recordings it matches the standard PLRNN (D_stsp 4.0 vs 3.9, p > 0.23) and beats all Neural ODE variants. Training cPLRNN takes ~33 s per 2000 epochs (P=10) versus ~107 s for Neural ODE with Tsit5, a roughly 3× speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Neural ODEs (Euler, RK4, Tsit5), cPLRNN is statistically indistinguishable on Lorenz-63 (all p > 0.27) and outperforms them on membrane potential recordings (D_stsp 4.0 vs 5.4–6.5, all p < 0.06), while training ~3× faster than Tsit5. Against the discrete-time standard PLRNN, cPLRNN is slightly worse on regularly sampled data (Lorenz-63 D_stsp 0.37 vs 0.24, p < 0.043) but dramatically superior on irregularly sampled LIF data (D_stsp 6.6 vs 15.3; MAE 0.064 vs 0.17), where the discrete model essentially breaks down due to its inability to handle non-uniform time points. SINDy achieves the best Lorenz-63 scores (D_stsp 0.17) because its polynomial library matches the ground-truth equations, but it fails entirely on the LIF model (D_stsp = ∞) and membrane potential data (D_stsp 17.74). Neural CDE and Latent ODE are essentially unsuitable for DSR: they cannot perform autonomous long-term roll-outs, yielding D_stsp values of 12–18 across all benchmarks and near-zero prediction times.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the discrete-time PLRNN family (Durstewitz 2017; Brenner et al. 2022, 2024a; Hess et al. 2023) into continuous time by reformulating the ReLU-based map as a piecewise-linear ODE system, then exploits the PL structure to replace numerical ODE integration with semi-analytical solutions within each linear subregion. The switching-time computation draws on interval Newton methods (modeled after IntervalRootFinding.jl), while the topological analysis of trained models (equilibria via SCYFI, limit cycles via a trust-region solver) adapts tools from the PL continuous-time DS literature (Coombes et al. 2024; Eisenmann et al. 2023). The main departure from Neural ODEs (Chen et al. 2018) is the elimination of adaptive numerical solvers entirely, trading the generality of arbitrary vector fields for the analytical tractability that the PL structure provides.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The cPLRNN fills a practical gap: discrete PLRNNs cannot handle irregularly sampled observations without lossy binning, while Neural ODEs lack the mathematical tractability needed for mechanistic insight in scientific/medical contexts. The semi-analytical forward pass is ~3× faster than Neural ODE training at comparable accuracy, and the ability to extract equilibria and limit cycles from trained models makes DSR outputs genuinely interpretable rather than black-box. Open questions include numerical robustness for ill-conditioned eigen-decompositions at scale, the cost of restarting root-finding after every switching event, and whether the approach extends cleanly to deeper ReLU networks or stochastic settings.
