## Problem

Test-time scaling (TTS) strategies have proven effective for large language models but remain largely unexplored for next-token prediction (NTP) autoregressive image generation. Existing TTS methods for visual autoregressive models (e.g., TTS-VAR) rely on frequent partial decoding and external reward models, which are inefficient and ineffective for NTP-based generation due to the inherent instability of intermediate image token decodes. The paper identifies three compounding challenges: holism (truncated image token streams are unusable), objective ambiguity (no verifiable ground-truth target), and early signal scarcity (partial decodes are visually unreliable).

## Input / Output

ScalingAR takes a text prompt as input and produces a generated RGB image as output, operating as a plug-and-play inference-time framework on top of existing NTP-based AR image generators (LlamaGen at 512×512, AR-GRPO at 256×256, SimpleAR 1.5B, Janus-Pro 1B). It spawns N=8 candidate trajectories but adaptively prunes low-confidence ones mid-generation, reducing average visual token consumption to ~2,350 tokens per image (vs. 8,192 for Best-of-N). The method requires no external reward model, no partial decoding, and uses a single universal hyperparameter configuration across all base models without per-model tuning.

## Method

ScalingAR introduces a two-level test-time scaling framework that relies solely on intrinsic model signals. At the Profile Level, a Dual-Channel Confidence Profile constructs a unified confidence state: the intrinsic channel combines token-level confidence (EMA-smoothed normalized entropy and top-1/top-2 margin) with worst-block stability (monitoring the most unstable spatial blocks via dynamic normalization), while the conditional channel measures semantic drift via KL divergence between conditional and unconditional token distributions, normalized by a rolling z-score. At the Policy Level, two mechanisms act on this state: (1) Adaptive Termination uses a distribution-aware quantile threshold (updated via EMA) with a recovery hysteresis window to prune trajectories that fail to rebound from confidence dips, and (2) a Guidance Scheduler dynamically modulates the classifier-free guidance scale in a closed loop—strengthening it when semantic drift or intrinsic volatility is detected, and relaxing it when the trajectory shows a strong confidence rebound to preserve diversity. This eliminates the need for early decoding or external reward models while enabling active trajectory correction rather than passive selection.

## Results

ScalingAR improves LlamaGen (0.8B) by 12.5% on GenEval (0.32→0.36 overall) and 15.2% on TIIF-Bench (40.35→46.47 overall), while improving AR-GRPO (0.8B) by 58.1% on GenEval (0.31→0.49) and 62.5% on TIIF-Bench (16.22→26.35). ScalingAR reduces visual token consumption by 62.0% compared to Best-of-N (2,350 vs. 8,192 tokens/image) while achieving superior quality, and mitigates performance degradation by 26.0% under impossible-prompt robustness testing. On TIIF-Bench, ScalingAR achieves 57.36/44.13/42.54/46.47 (Basic/Advanced/Designer/Overall) on LlamaGen, outperforming both IS (54.81/40.34/39.93/42.44) and BoN (54.79/40.78/37.69/42.02).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two direct TTS baselines (Importance Sampling and Best-of-N), ScalingAR consistently wins on both GenEval and TIIF-Bench across both base models. On LlamaGen, ScalingAR achieves 0.36 GenEval overall vs. 0.14 (IS) and 0.15 (BoN), and 46.47 TIIF-Bench overall vs. 42.44 (IS) and 42.02 (BoN); on AR-GRPO, it reaches 0.49 vs. 0.44 (both IS and BoN) on GenEval and 26.35 vs. 19.84 (IS) and 21.08 (BoN) on TIIF-Bench. Notably, IS actually degrades LlamaGen's GenEval score (0.32→0.14), while ScalingAR improves it, highlighting that naive resampling is counterproductive for NTP image generation. ScalingAR also outperforms the base models themselves by 12.5% (LlamaGen GenEval) and 15.2% (LlamaGen TIIF-Bench), and by 58.1% and 62.5% respectively on AR-GRPO. In terms of efficiency, ScalingAR uses only 2,350 tokens/image versus 8,192 for BoN (a 71% reduction) while delivering higher quality, and achieves a 3.1× wall-clock speedup over BoN (69.56s vs. 218.44s). The paper also positions ScalingAR against larger AR models (Infinity 2B, Emu3 8.5B, Janus 1.5B) in Table 1, where ScalingAR-enhanced LlamaGen (0.8B) remains competitive on TIIF-Bench (46.47) though trailing on GenEval overall (0.36 vs. 0.73 for Infinity), reflecting the gap between 0.8B and 2B+ parameter scales.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the test-time scaling (TTS) paradigm from LLMs (Self-Consistency, Best-of-N, CoT) to next-token prediction autoregressive image generation, a setting where direct transfer is complicated by image holism, objective ambiguity, and early signal scarcity. It specifically departs from TTS-VAR (Chen et al., 2025b), which applied TTS to the next-scale prediction (NSP) paradigm by relying on frequent partial decoding and external reward models, by instead using intrinsic token entropy and conditional KL divergence as confidence signals without any external reward. The token entropy confidence estimation draws on recent NLP work (Kang et al., 2025; Fu et al., 2025) but is, per the authors, the first application of this signal to visual token generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ScalingAR provides the first TTS framework for NTP-based AR image generation that eliminates the need for external reward models and frequent partial decoding, reducing visual token consumption by 62% while outperforming Best-of-N with ImageReward. This is practically significant as AR image generation moves toward unified multimodal models where inference efficiency is critical. However, the approach relies on model calibration and entropy signals that may vary across architectures and training regimes, and the evaluation is limited to 0.8B–1.5B models. Open questions include whether finer-grained confidence measures or entropy-aware training could further improve scaling, and whether the method generalizes to larger-scale or multi-resolution AR generators.
