## Problem

In the Plan-then-Code paradigm, introducing explicit plans and verification reports substantially enlarges the context, causing small-scale LLMs (<8B) to suffer from Attention Allocation Imbalance: attention is either dispersed across irrelevant tokens (Attention Dispersion) or drifts toward recently generated code, marginalizing the plan (Attention Drift). Existing attention guidance and distillation methods address these issues only partially, lacking a unified mechanism to simultaneously mitigate both forms of imbalance.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a problem statement, a step-decomposed natural language plan, and a verification report as input, and produces code segments aligned to individual plan steps. During training, each sample is a tuple (x, p, v, y) where y contains both correct (positive) and first-failed (negative) code, with code segments explicitly aligned to plan steps via regex verification. The attention supervision signal is a binary target matrix (1 for verified key tokens, 0 otherwise) applied only to selected final layers and half the attention heads, while the sliding window restricts the alignment scope to the current plan step plus its two immediate predecessors.

## Method

PADA operates in three stages. First, it extracts top-k high-attention tokens from both teacher and student models, partitions them into Consensus and Disagreement sets, then verifies importance via mask-perturbation (measuring Perplexity Increment ΔPPL) to yield a final key token set K_pos and a negative set K_neg. Second, it constructs a Maximum-DID (Distillation Information Density) Attention Target Matrix, where DID = α·ln(ρL)/(ρL)·ā(ρ,t) captures the trade-off between information coverage and softmax attention dilution, with the optimal key-token density empirically identified at ~4–6%. Third, during training, a Progress-Aware Sliding Window restricts attention alignment to causally relevant plan steps, and a Difficulty-Aware Gating mechanism (driven by normalized AST depth/cyclomatic complexity and model perplexity) dynamically scales the attention loss weight to prevent over-interpretation on simple segments. The total loss combines standard LM loss with a Frobenius-norm attention alignment loss, modulated by the gate and a linearly decaying coefficient.

## Results

On the primary code generation benchmarks (Table 1), PADA improves average Pass@1 by up to 16.7% over the base model: Qwen3-4B reaches 77.8% (vs. 62.2% base), Qwen2.5-Coder-7B reaches 73.6% (vs. 56.9%), and Llama3.2-3B reaches 46.9% (vs. 33.3%). Against ultra-large-scale LLMs (Table 2), PADA-Qwen3 achieves a 77.8% average Pass@1, surpassing Gemini-2.5-pro (73.8%), GPT-5.2 (71.2%), and DeepSeek-v3.2 (68.7%). With oracle reference plans (Table 3), PADA-Coder attains 88.5% average, outperforming Gemini-2.5-pro (87.2%) and GPT-5.2 (85.7%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PADA outperforms all three method baselines (SPA, LPW, LeaF) on average Pass@1 across all three student models. Against SPA (attention guidance), PADA gains +11.8 pts on Qwen3-4B (77.8 vs. 66.0) and +8.6 pts on Llama3.2-3B (46.9 vs. 38.3), as SPA's rigid rule-based anchoring fails to address attention drift. Against LPW (Plan-Code-Reflect agent), PADA wins on Qwen3-4B (77.8 vs. 75.5) and Llama3.2-3B (46.9 vs. 35.5), and also on Qwen2.5-Coder-7B (73.6 vs. 70.4), while consuming 3.6× fewer tokens on APPS. Against LeaF (causal attention distillation), PADA leads by +9.2 pts on Qwen3-4B (77.8 vs. 68.6) and +6.1 pts on Llama3.2-3B (46.9 vs. 40.8), as LeaF still suffers from attention drift in later generation stages. Against ultra-large LLMs, PADA-Qwen3 (77.8% avg) surpasses Gemini-2.5-pro (73.8%), GPT-5.2 (71.2%), and DeepSeek-v3.2 (68.7%), with the largest margins on difficult benchmarks like APPS-C (+9.7 over Gemini) and LiveCodeBench (+3.1 over Gemini). On mathematical reasoning (Table 5), PADA improves over Plan-and-Solve prompting by +1.4 pts on GSM8K and +3.0 pts on MATH-500 for Qwen3-4B, and by +9.1 and +3.9 pts respectively for Llama3.2-3B.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the attention distillation lineage (MiniLM, A2D, LeaF) and the Plan-then-Code paradigm (Wang et al., 2023; Lei et al., 2025) by diagnosing a specific failure mode—Attention Allocation Imbalance (dispersion + drift)—that prior attention-guidance methods (SPA, Self-Anchor) and distillation methods (LeaF) each address only partially. It departs from standard attention distillation by introducing perturbation-verified token selection (avoiding 'Teacher Imitation Bias') and a principled Distillation Information Density metric to determine optimal key-token count, then couples this with a progress-aware sliding window and difficulty-aware gating to dynamically counter attention drift during training. The LPW framework provides the plan-construction backbone, while the causal verification methodology is adapted from LeaF.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for resource-constrained deployments: a 4B model trained with PADA reportedly matches or exceeds 70B+ closed-source LLMs on code generation benchmarks, suggesting that precise attention calibration can partially substitute for parameter scale. However, the method is tightly coupled to the Plan-then-Code paradigm and depends on plan quality, which small LLMs struggle to produce autonomously; the paper acknowledges this as a key limitation. Open questions include whether the DID framework generalizes to other reasoning tasks (math results are modest), whether the approach scales to longer contexts or multi-turn settings, and whether the binary attention target matrix could be replaced by softer, learned targets for further gains.
