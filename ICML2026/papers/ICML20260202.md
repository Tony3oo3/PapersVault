## Problem

scRNA-seq is destructive, yielding only population-level snapshots at discrete time points rather than individual cell trajectories, making it impossible to directly observe temporal dynamics. Existing approaches based on neural ODEs, flow matching, or SDEs either lack principled modeling of time-varying biological variability, are sensitive to overfitting, or cannot jointly handle temporal asynchrony and multi-lineage structure within a single coherent generative framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260202/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes population-level gene expression matrices X_t ∈ R^{n_t×G} at multiple time points (with optional cell type labels), and produces generated cell populations at any queried time point by sampling from a latent GP and decoding through a neural network. Notably, the model is decoder-only with no encoder: observed data enter exclusively through the OT objective, and per-cell embeddings can be recovered post-hoc via non-amortized inversion of the push-forward mapping. Evaluation uses the 2-Wasserstein distance between the full generated population (2000 samples) and the held-out ground truth population at each test time point.

## Method

LGP-OT places a multi-output heteroscedastic GP prior in an L-dimensional latent space, where both the signal f_l(t) and the log-noise standard deviation log(ζ_l(t)) are modeled as GPs with squared-exponential kernels, approximated via Hilbert space basis functions (eigendecomposition of the Laplacian) for O(NM) scalability. Training minimizes an entropic-regularized Wasserstein distance between generated and observed cell populations at each time point, plus a KL regularization toward the GP prior, optimized jointly over decoder parameters and variational parameters via the reparameterization trick. Temporal asynchrony is captured by per-cell latent times τ_n anchored to measurement times via an informative Gaussian prior, and multi-lineage structure is handled through a product kernel with Kronecker-product basis functions over categorical cell-type covariates. A gradient-based perturbation strategy extends the approach to the temporal domain by optimizing entire latent trajectories to match perturbed gene expression targets, enabling in silico perturbation analysis without recursive simulation.

## Results

LGP-OT is evaluated using the 2-Wasserstein distance (W₂, lower is better) between predicted and observed cell populations at held-out time points. On the DR dataset, LGP-OT achieves W₂ of 26.35, 28.22, and 30.74 on the easy (interpolation) task at t=4, 6, 8, and 32.15, 32.52, 36.51 on the medium (extrapolation) task at t=8, 9, 10. On the challenging SC hard task (combined interpolation + extrapolation), LGP-OT(w.time) achieves W₂ of 15.05 at t=17, substantially outperforming the best baseline scNODE (17.26) and PI-SDE (19.92). Heteroscedastic noise modeling provides consistent gains over the homoscedastic ablation across all datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LGP-OT outperforms all five baselines (VGFM, scNODE, MIOFlow, PI-SDE, PRESCIENT) on the DR dataset across all three difficulty levels, with the largest margins appearing in the hard (combined) task where MIOFlow degrades to W₂=41.43 at t=10 versus LGP-OT's 38.17. On the large-scale SC dataset (236K cells, 19 time points), the advantage is most pronounced in the hard task: LGP-OT(w.time) achieves W₂=15.05 at t=17, beating scNODE (17.26) by 2.21, PI-SDE (19.92) by 4.87, and MIOFlow (18.93) by 3.88. VGFM is the most competitive baseline overall but shows less consistency, particularly on extrapolation time points. PRESCIENT is consistently the weakest method, with W₂ values 3–13 points higher than LGP-OT across all datasets. The heteroscedastic GP prior provides a clear advantage over both fixed-noise (ζ=0.1) and optimizable-constant-noise (ζ=ζ) ablations, confirming that time-varying biological variability is essential for accurate temporal prediction.

## Relation to Prior Work

LGP-OT sits at the intersection of GP prior VAEs (Casale et al., 2018), Hilbert space GP approximation (Solin & Särkkä, 2020), and Wasserstein autoencoders (Tolstikhin et al., 2018), but departs from each in a key way: rather than modeling paired cell trajectories with neural ODEs/SDEs or assuming sample-to-sample correspondence across time, it models population-level dynamics with a heteroscedastic GP prior and matches generated to observed distributions via entropic OT. The decoder-only design (no encoder) is a deliberate break from VAE-based baselines like scNODE and MIOFlow, motivated by the destructive nature of scRNA-seq. The gradient-based perturbation strategy extends Bjerregaard et al. (2025) from pointwise latent shifts to full-trajectory optimization in the temporal domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, scalable generative framework for temporal scRNA-seq that handles the unpaired-snapshot problem natively through OT, and the heteroscedastic GP prior captures time-varying biological variability that deterministic ODE/SDE baselines cannot. The consistent SOTA across three datasets and three difficulty levels (especially the ~13–14% W2 gap over scNODE on the hardest SC extrapolation points) validates the architecture. Open questions remain: the model does not explicitly capture branching events, the SE kernel precludes oscillatory/cyclic dynamics, and extension to multi-modal or spatial single-cell data is unaddressed. The decoder-only design also means per-cell embeddings require non-amortized inversion, which may be costly at scale.
