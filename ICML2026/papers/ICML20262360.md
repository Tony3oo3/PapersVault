## Problem

Reflective Hamiltonian Monte Carlo (ReHMC) is a practical sampler for bounded and constrained supports, but it has no non-asymptotic mixing-time guarantees and existing formulations require the support to be a smooth convex body or polytope. This paper establishes the first total-variation mixing-time bounds for ReHMC and extends its applicability to general bounded supports, including nonconvex and nonsmooth domains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262360/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a target potential U defined on a bounded support K (which may be nonconvex or nonsmooth), a smooth convex container K̃ ⊇ K, and tuning parameters (step size η, penalty strength λ, boundary width b). For the Stiefel application, it additionally takes a target density on V_{r,u} and produces orthonormal r×u matrices via a polar reparameterization B → Γ(B) = B(BᵀB)^{-1/2}. Samples are generated on the convex container and thinned: only draws falling inside K are retained, yielding exact samples from the original target. In the PCA experiment, the input is an observed data matrix Y ∈ ℝ^{n×r} and the output is posterior samples of the orthogonal loading matrix Γ.

## Method

The paper contributes three interlocking components. First, it proves non-asymptotic TV mixing-time bounds for Metropolized ReHMC: under L-smoothness the bound scales as O(d²) in dimension but carries an exponential factor in smoothness parameters, while under an additional m-strong convexity assumption the exponential is eliminated, yielding k ≳ κd² log(d log(κγ) + log(1/ε)) log(1/ε). Second, it introduces a convex-container-plus-thinning construction that embeds an arbitrary bounded support K into a smooth convex container K̃, extends the potential via a penalty-based smooth surrogate (softplus boundary-violation function composed with a flat-endpoint penalty ψ), runs ReHMC on K̃, and recovers exact samples from the original target by retaining only draws inside K. Third, for Stiefel manifold sampling it proposes a modified polar expansion with a truncated Wishart auxiliary density that enforces a uniformly bounded condition number on BᵀB, avoiding the numerical instability of the unconstrained polar expansion near rank-deficient matrices. The mixing analysis combines local TV contraction of the reflective proposal (requiring new reflection-geometry estimates) with a blocking-conductance bound, following the framework of Lee et al. (2020) but with novel lemmas specific to specular boundary reflections.

## Results

On the Stiefel manifold (r=40), PR-HMC achieves comparable or superior effective sample size (ESS) across most (u, ω) configurations, with a 90.3% average ESS gain over the baseline at u=39 (e.g., 1162.82 vs. 459.30 at ω=100). Critically, the baseline fails to converge (numerical instability) at u=30 with ω∈{200, 500} and at all ambient dimensions under high anisotropy (ε=0.01) in the Bayesian PCA experiment, while PR-HMC maintains stable performance with ESS exceeding 921. PSRF (split-R̂) values remain near 1.0 (≤1.009) for PR-HMC across all successful configurations, indicating reliable convergence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the unconstrained polar-expansion HMC method of Jauch et al. (2021), which samples on the Stiefel manifold via a polar reparameterization without boundary constraints. PR-HMC wins decisively in the most challenging regimes: at u=39 (subspace dimension close to ambient r=40), PR-HMC achieves 90.3% higher average ESS (e.g., 1162.82 vs. 459.30 at ω=100, 1331.58 vs. 579.38 at ω=200), and the baseline's PSRF degrades to 1.011–1.013 while PR-HMC stays at 1.002. At u=30 with ω∈{200, 500}, the baseline suffers complete numerical failure (NA) while PR-HMC converges with ESS ≈ 1220–1246. In the Bayesian PCA experiment, under high anisotropy (ε=0.01), the baseline fails for all ambient dimensions r∈{10,20,30,40}, whereas PR-HMC maintains ESS between 921 and 1061. Under moderate settings (u=20, ε≥0.05), the two methods are essentially tied, with ESS differences within a few percent.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the reflective HMC framework of Mohasel Afshar & Domke (2015) and Chalkis et al. (2023) by establishing the first non-asymptotic mixing-time bounds for ReHMC, adapting the blocking-conductance architecture of Lee et al. (2020) to the reflective setting with new reflection-geometry estimates. It departs from prior work in two key ways: (i) a convex-container-plus-thinning construction that removes the convexity/polytope requirement on the support, and (ii) a modified polar expansion with a uniformly bounded condition number for Stiefel manifold sampling, addressing the numerical instability of the unconstrained polar-expansion HMC of Jauch et al. (2021). The O(d²) dimension dependence improves upon the O(d³) scaling of Langevin-type constrained samplers (Srinivasan et al., 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a notable theoretical gap—no rigorous mixing-time guarantees existed for ReHMC—and provides a practical framework (convex-container-plus-thinning) that extends HMC to nonconvex and nonsmooth bounded supports. The empirical finding that the unconstrained polar-expansion baseline fails entirely in high-anisotropy and near-full-rank settings while PR-HMC remains stable has direct implications for Bayesian inference on Stiefel manifolds. Key limitations include the exponential dependence on smoothness parameters in the general L-smooth bound and the requirement that the thinning retention probability α = π̃(K) be bounded away from zero; extending the framework to product spaces of bounded and unbounded parameters is identified as important future work.
