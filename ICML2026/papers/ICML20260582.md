## Problem

Standard cooperative MARL assumes additive joint rewards, but real collaborative tasks (multi-drone surveillance, multi-robot exploration) exhibit overlapping agent contributions with diminishing marginal returns. No formal framework or algorithms with provable guarantees existed for MARL with submodular rewards, and even the single-step case is NP-hard via reduction to submodular maximization under partition matroid constraints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260582/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each of K agents observes its local state s_i ∈ S and selects an action a_i ∈ A from a shared finite action space; the global reward is a monotone submodular set function f evaluated on the collection of all agents' state-action pairs. In the known-dynamics setting, the per-agent transition kernels P_{i,h} are given; in the unknown-dynamics setting, the algorithm learns them from T episodes of interaction. The output is a decomposable policy π = {π_{i,h}} where each agent's action depends only on its own local state, requiring only O(K|S||A|H) memory and enabling fully distributed execution.

## Method

The core mechanism is a marginal value decomposition: the submodular reward is telescoped into per-agent marginal gains Δr_i, and agents are optimized sequentially so that fixing policies π_{[i-1]} reduces agent i's problem to a single-agent MDP with a time-varying reward R_{i,h}(s,a|π_{[i-1]}). For known dynamics, Greedy Policy Optimization performs backward induction per agent, using Monte Carlo sampling of N trajectories to estimate marginal rewards (avoiding the exponential O(|S|^i|A|^i) cost of exact marginalization), and achieves a 1/2-approximation to the optimal joint policy. For unknown dynamics, UCB-GVI layers optimistic value iteration with Bernstein-based confidence bonuses on top of the same greedy sequential structure, yielding a 1/2-regret of Õ(H²KS√(AT)) over T episodes. The approximation proof introduces a two-group comparison (greedy-policy agents vs. optimal-policy agents) and exploits submodularity to bound each agent's marginal contribution, then telescopes over agents to recover the global 1/2 ratio.

## Results

The paper provides two main theoretical guarantees. For known transition dynamics, Greedy Policy Optimization (Theorem 1) achieves a 1/2-approximation: V₁^π(s̄₁) ≥ V₁^{π*}(s̄₁)/2 − εKH, with polynomial complexity O(K·H·|S||A|·max{|S|, ε⁻²log(K|S||A|H/δ)}). For unknown dynamics, UCB-GVI (Theorem 2) achieves a 1/2-regret bound of Õ(S²AH³K² + H²KS√(AT)) over T episodes. When K=1, the regret reduces to Õ(H²S√(AT)), recovering the single-agent setting, though with an additional √(HS) factor compared to the near-minimax single-agent bound of Õ(H√(SAHT)).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments, datasets, or algorithmic baselines compared. The paper positions its contributions relative to existing theoretical results: (1) the 1/2-approximation matches the classical greedy bound for monotone submodular maximization under matroid constraints (Wolsey, 1982), and (2) the regret bound for UCB-GVI is the first sublinear regret guarantee for MARLS. Compared to the near-minimax single-agent episodic RL bound of Õ(H√(SAHT)) (Azar et al., 2017; Domingues et al., 2021), the multi-agent submodular setting incurs an additional √(HS) factor and polynomial dependence on K, reflecting the challenges of marginal reward estimation and multi-agent coordination. The paper does not compare against other multi-agent RL algorithms (e.g., QMIX, MADDPG) empirically, as the focus is on establishing the first formal framework and provable guarantees for the submodular reward setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-studied areas: monotone submodular maximization under matroid constraints (where the greedy algorithm achieves a 1/2-approximation, Wolsey 1982) and model-based episodic RL with optimistic value iteration (UCBVI, Azar et al. 2017). It departs from standard cooperative MARL, which typically assumes additive or linearly decomposable rewards, by formalizing the first MARL setting where the joint reward is a monotone submodular set function over agent state-action pairs. It also extends static submodular maximization into a sequential MDP with stochastic transitions, introducing the additional challenge of estimating marginal rewards through sampling within a learning loop. The marginal value decomposition technique (Lemma 1) is the key conceptual bridge, reducing each agent's subproblem to a single-agent MDP with a time-varying reward induced by previously fixed agents.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first formal framework and first sublinear regret guarantee for MARL with submodular rewards, a setting that naturally arises in multi-drone surveillance, collaborative exploration, and multi-robot coordination where agent contributions overlap and exhibit diminishing returns. The result that decomposable policies (polynomial-size, distributed-executable) achieve a 1/2-approximation to the optimal non-decomposable joint policy is practically significant: it means the exponential curse of dimensionality can be overcome without sacrificing approximation quality beyond the inherent greedy bound. Key limitations include the restriction to tabular MDPs with independent agent transitions, the 1/2 approximation ratio (versus 1−1/e achievable by continuous-relaxation methods in the static case), and the absence of empirical validation. Open questions include whether the approximation ratio can be improved, whether the framework extends to correlated transitions or non-tabular function approximation, and how the algorithms perform in practice on the motivating drone/robot applications.
